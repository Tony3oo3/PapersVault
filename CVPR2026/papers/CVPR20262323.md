## Problem

Despite rapid progress in instruction-guided image editing models, the research community lacks large-scale, high-quality, and openly shareable editing datasets built from real photographs. Existing resources rely on synthetic generations from proprietary models or small human-curated subsets, and suffer from domain shifts, unbalanced edit-type distributions, and inconsistent quality control that hinder robust model development.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262323/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each example pairs a real photograph (sourced from OpenImages) with a natural-language editing instruction and produces a single edited image. Instructions are provided in two parallel variants per example: a long, detailed prompt generated by Gemini-2.5-Flash (optimized for rich supervision) and a short, user-style command rewritten by Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct using human annotations as in-context demonstrations. The dataset spans 35 edit types across 8 categories, with three specialized subsets: 258K single-turn SFT pairs, 72K multi-turn sequences (2–5 consecutive edits per image), and 56K preference triplets pairing successful and failed edits.

## Method

The construction pipeline chains three Gemini-family models: Gemini-2.5-Flash generates detailed editing instructions conditioned on each source image, Nano-Banana (Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image) executes the edit to produce the target image, and Gemini-2.5-Pro acts as an automated judge scoring four weighted criteria (Instruction Compliance 40%, Seamlessness 25%, Preservation Balance 20%, Technical Quality 15%) against a ~0.7 threshold. Failed edits are automatically retried up to three times; if all attempts fail the pair is discarded, while intermediate failures are retained as negative examples to form preference triplets for DPO-style alignment. For multi-turn sequences, 100K single-turn examples are expanded by randomly sampling 1–4 additional edit types, and Gemini-2.5-Pro writes referential instructions that link back to prior turns (e.g., 'change the color of it'), enforcing discourse continuity and coreference across the editing chain.

## Results

Pico-Banana-400K comprises ~400K editing examples: 258K single-turn SFT pairs, 56K preference triplets (success vs. failure), and 72K multi-turn sequences (2–5 turns each), spanning 35 edit types across 8 categories. Per-edit-type success rates (measured by Gemini-2.5-Pro judge, threshold ≈ 0.7) range from 0.9340 (strong artistic style transfer) and 0.9068 (film grain/vintage) at the high end, down to 0.5759 (change font/style), 0.5884 (caricature), and 0.5923 (relocate object) at the low end, revealing that global stylistic edits are reliable while fine spatial, layout, and typographic edits remain challenging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pico-Banana-400K is positioned between the smaller human-curated datasets (GIER, MagicBrush at 10⁴-scale) and the larger synthetic or mixed pipelines (UltraEdit, OmniEdit, GPT-Image-Edit-1.5M at 10⁶-scale). Compared to GIER and MagicBrush, it is roughly an order of magnitude larger and adds automated quality scoring; compared to HQ-Edit and Echo-4o-Image, it uses real source images rather than synthetic generations, avoiding domain shift. Against the 10⁶-scale datasets (UltraEdit, OmniEdit, GPT-Image-Edit-1.5M), Pico-Banana-400K is smaller in raw count but uniquely provides multi-turn editing sequences (72K) and preference triplets (56K) for alignment research, features absent from all seven baselines. The paper does not report head-to-head model benchmark comparisons, as its contribution is the dataset itself rather than a trained editing model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper follows the emerging trend of distilling frontier multimodal models to synthesize large-scale editing datasets, similar to Echo-4o-Image and GPT-Image-Edit-1.5M, but departs by grounding edits in real OpenImages photographs rather than synthetic inputs and by employing a dedicated 35-type editing taxonomy for systematic coverage. Unlike prior datasets that focus primarily on single-turn supervised pairs, Pico-Banana-400K introduces multi-turn editing sequences (72K) and preference triplets (56K) as first-class components, drawing on the DPO/alignment literature for its motivation. The dual-instruction design (Gemini-generated long prompts vs. Qwen-summarized short user-style commands) repurposes the instruction-rewriting paradigm from NLP to the image-editing domain, and the automated quality-judging pipeline with Gemini-2.5-Pro replaces the manual curation used in smaller datasets like MagicBrush and GIER.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a concrete gap: the absence of large-scale, openly licensed, quality-controlled editing data built from real images, which has constrained reproducible research on instruction-following editors. The per-edit-type success-rate analysis provides a useful empirical map of where current frontier models (Nano-Banana) succeed (global style transfer, ~0.93) and where they fail (object relocation ~0.59, text manipulation ~0.58), pointing to open problems in spatial conditioning, layout extrapolation, and typography that remain unsolved. The multi-turn and preference subsets open research directions on iterative editing planning and reward-model training that prior single-turn datasets cannot support. Limitations include reliance on a single editing model (Nano-Banana) for generation, which may introduce model-specific biases, and the absence of human-verified ground truth beyond automated scoring.
