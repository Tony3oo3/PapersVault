## Problem

Current video generation models produce visually realistic but physically inconsistent results, especially in complex or high-motion scenarios. Existing physics-aware approaches either internalize physical priors implicitly during training without explicit guidance at inference, or apply coarse video-level expert routing that fails to capture fine-grained, spatially localized physical phenomena when multiple laws coexist in different regions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt describing a physical scenario as input and produces a temporally coherent, physically plausible video as output. It operates on latent video diffusion backbones (Wan2.1-1.3B and CogVideoX-5B), where the text prompt is first parsed by a semantic router to identify relevant physical categories, and the denoising process operates on latent tokens of shape (F/r_f)×(H/r_s)×(W/r_s)×C. The physical branch is attached to the final transformer block of the backbone, with the number of Physical Blocks adapted to backbone depth and available GPU memory.

## Method

ProPhy introduces a two-stage Mixture-of-Physics-Experts (MoPE) mechanism: a Semantic Expert Block (SEB) that uses learnable physical basis maps and a text-conditioned router to produce video-level physical priors, followed by a Refinement Expert Block (REB) that applies token-level top-k expert routing to capture spatially anisotropic physical dynamics. A key innovation is a fine-grained physical alignment strategy that uses Qwen2.5-VL-32B to generate token-level attention-based supervision signals, aligning the REB's spatial predictions with VLM-inferred physical phenomenon locations. Training combines the standard diffusion loss with three auxiliary objectives: a cosine-similarity pairwise semantic alignment loss for the SEB router, a masked token-level alignment loss for the REB router (projected through an MLP to reduce training conflict), and a load-balancing loss to prevent expert collapse. The Physical Branch is initialized from the corresponding backbone transformer weights to preserve pretrained semantic understanding while progressively accumulating physics-specific information.

## Results

On the VideoPhy2 benchmark (ALL split), ProPhy applied to CogVideoX-5B achieves a Joint pass rate of 26.7%, PC of 72.5%, and SA of 32.2%, outperforming all compared baselines. On the harder HARD subset, ProPhy-CogVideoX reaches Joint 6.1% and PC 52.8%, while ProPhy-Wan2.1-1.3B achieves Joint 7.2% and SA 12.2%. On VBench, ProPhy boosts the Dynamic Degree score from 46.8 to 72.0 on CogVideoX and from 71.3 to 78.8 on Wan2.1-1.3B, and raises the overall Quality Score to 81.0 and 79.0 respectively, confirming that physical alignment improves dynamic realism without sacrificing visual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProPhy achieves the best or second-best Joint score across all VideoPhy2 settings. Against the strongest open-source T2V baselines, ProPhy-CogVideoX (Joint 26.7%) surpasses HunyuanVideo (24.7%), Cosmos (22.6%), Cosmos-Predict2.5 (23.1%), and Wan2.1-14B (24.7%) by 2.0–4.1 points. Compared to its own backbone CogVideoX-5B (Joint 22.3%), ProPhy yields a +4.4-point gain; on Wan2.1-1.3B (Joint 24.8%), the improvement is +1.7 points (a +19.7% relative gain as reported by the authors). Among physics-aware methods, ProPhy outperforms WISA (Joint 25.8% on CogVideoX) by 0.9 points and VideoREPA (Joint 22.0%) by 4.7 points, with the largest margin over VideoREPA coming from substantially higher SA (32.2% vs. 24.2%). On the HARD subset, ProPhy-Wan2.1-1.3B achieves the best Joint (7.2%) and SA (12.2%), while ProPhy-CogVideoX leads in PC (52.8%), indicating complementary strengths across backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProPhy extends WISA's Mixture-of-Physics-Experts (MoPE) structure from a single-level, video-level routing scheme into a two-stage progressive design: a Semantic Expert Block that infers physics categories from text, followed by a Refinement Expert Block that performs token-level expert routing. It departs from prior physics-aware methods (VideoREPA, PhysMaster, PISA, PhysHPO) that internalize physical priors implicitly during training by introducing explicit, learnable physical basis maps and a VLM-guided alignment loss that transfers fine-grained spatial localization from Qwen2.5-VL into the generative process. The progressive coarse-to-fine routing (semantic → token-level) is the key architectural departure from both the implicit-alignment line and WISA's global-level guidance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent gap between visual realism and physical plausibility in video generation, showing that explicit, spatially anisotropic physical conditioning can substantially improve dynamic consistency (e.g., +19.7% Joint on Wan2.1, Dynamic Degree 46.8→72.0 on CogVideoX). The expert-inversion experiment (flipping router logits to produce a rigid car door fluttering like cloth) demonstrates that distinct physical priors are genuinely encoded in separate experts, suggesting a path toward controllable physical attribute manipulation in world simulators. However, the authors acknowledge that region-level physical categorization remains coarse and that integrating governing differential equations for more principled physics injection is left as future work, leaving open the question of whether learned token-level priors can truly replace explicit physics simulation for complex multi-body interactions.
