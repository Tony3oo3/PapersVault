## Problem

Discovering governing physical laws from raw video is fundamentally difficult because the true low-dimensional physical structure is submerged in high-dimensional pixel data dominated by textures, lighting, and background motion. Prior approaches either require pre-extracted clean state variables, learn non-unique latent spaces that entangle visually salient but physically irrelevant factors, or rely on MLLMs' prior knowledge without a structured discovery workflow. A critical circular dependency remains unresolved: extracting good variables requires knowledge of the dynamics, while finding the dynamics requires a clean variable space.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw video sequences at three granularities—object-motion videos (e.g., celestial bodies), PDE field videos (e.g., reaction-diffusion patterns on pixel grids), and real-world scientific recordings (e.g., Kármán vortex streets, BZ reactions with sensor noise)—and produces a sparse symbolic governing equation of the form dz/dt = f(z(t)) together with the corresponding low-dimensional variable time series z(t). The output also includes long-term trajectory predictions generated by numerically integrating the discovered equation, evaluated via RMSE and valid prediction steps over unseen future frames. The framework is adaptable to any MLLM backbone (GPT-4o by default) and operates iteratively, refining both the variable space and the equation across multiple reasoning rounds.

## Method

Pixel2Phys is a collaborative multi-agent framework where a Plan Agent coordinates three specialized agents—Variable, Equation, and Experiment—in an iterative co-optimization loop that breaks the circular dependency between variable extraction and law discovery. The Variable Agent deploys multi-granularity tools (SAM-based object tracking for moving bodies, fixed convolutional kernels for spatial derivatives on PDE fields, and a physics-informed autoencoder for noisy real-world phenomena) to extract low-dimensional states z(t). The Equation Agent performs sparse symbolic regression via STLSQ over a candidate library of polynomial and transcendental terms, with the sparsity threshold dynamically set by the Plan Agent. The key mechanism is the feedback loop: when the Plan Agent diagnoses poor variable quality, it feeds the previously discovered equation f back into the autoencoder as a physics-consistency loss (L_eq = ||F(z) − f(z)||²), forcing the latent space to adhere to the governing dynamics and filter out irrelevant visual components. The Experiment Agent validates each (Z, f) pair through R² scoring, complexity measurement, phase-portrait visualization, and long-term extrapolation RMSE, feeding structured reports back to the Plan Agent for targeted refinement instructions.

## Results

On object-level dynamics (Table 1), Pixel2Phys achieves near-perfect long-term extrapolation with R²@1000 scores of 0.9913 (Linear), 0.9886 (Cubic), 1.0000 (Circular), 0.9954 (VDP), and 0.9995 (Glider), while implicit baselines (AE-SINDy, Latent-ODE) collapse to R² ≈ 0. On pixel-level PDE dynamics (Table 2), Pixel2Phys achieves the lowest RMSE across all four systems (0.03 on Lambda-Omega, 0.12 on Brusselator, 0.16 on FitzHugh-Nagumo, 0.18 on Swift-Hohenberg) with VPS@0.5 of 1000 steps (200 for SH), far exceeding all neural operator and symbolic regression baselines. Overall, the framework improves extrapolation accuracy (RMSE) by 45.35% over baselines across all three video categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pixel2Phys decisively outperforms all 10 baselines across the three evaluation categories. On object-level dynamics, implicit methods AE-SINDy [8] and Latent-ODE [10] suffer complete extrapolation collapse (R² ≈ 0.00–0.36), while Coord-Equ [34] achieves moderate R² (0.26–0.99) but introduces more false-positive terms; Pixel2Phys achieves R² ≥ 0.9886 with fewer or equal false positives. On pixel-level PDEs, neural operators (FNO [31], UNO [37], WNO [45]) accumulate severe error over 1000-step rollouts (RMSE 0.42–96.98, VPS < 800), whereas Pixel2Phys maintains RMSE ≤ 0.18 with VPS = 1000 (or 200 for SH). Among symbolic baselines, PDE-Find [38] is the closest competitor (RMSE 0.19 on SH) but still trails Pixel2Phys on all other systems; SGA-PDE [11] and LLM-PDE [13] frequently fail to converge (NaN) on multiple PDEs. On real-world scientific videos, the 14B-parameter video generation model Wan2.2 [48] produces visually realistic but dynamically inconsistent predictions (vortex drift), while Pixel2Phys achieves the lowest RMSE and vorticity error by distilling quantitative governing laws (e.g., eigenvalues λ₂,₃ ≈ ±2.136i) that enforce strict oscillation periods.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pixel2Phys builds directly on the SINDy/STLSQ sparse regression framework (Brunton et al., 2016) for law distillation, but departs fundamentally from prior visual equation discovery by replacing static pipelines with an iterative multi-agent loop coordinated by an MLLM. Prior approaches fall into two camps: pipeline methods (Coord-Equ) that extract trajectories then regress, and end-to-end latent methods (AE-SINDy, Latent-ODE) that suffer from a circular dependency between variable learning and law discovery. The key conceptual departure is using the discovered symbolic equation as a physics-informed loss to guide the autoencoder's variable extraction in subsequent iterations, effectively closing the loop that prior methods leave open. The multi-granularity tool design (SAM for objects, convolutional kernels for PDE fields, physics-informed autoencoders for complex phenomena) also distinguishes it from single-representation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The circular dependency between extracting meaningful variables and discovering the governing law has been a fundamental bottleneck in data-driven physics; demonstrating that an MLLM-orchestrated iterative loop can break this dependency is a meaningful step toward automated scientific discovery from raw observations. However, the framework's reliance on GPT-4o as the reasoning backbone raises questions about cost, reproducibility, and whether the gains come from genuine multi-step reasoning or from the model's parametric physics knowledge. The benchmarks, while diverse, are largely controlled simulations with known ground-truth equations; open questions include whether the approach scales to genuinely novel physics, multi-body interactions, or high-noise real-world settings where the candidate library may not contain the correct functional forms.
