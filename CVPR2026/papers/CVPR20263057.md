## Problem

Existing MLLM benchmarks focus on high-level semantic reasoning (captioning, VQA, mathematical reasoning) while overlooking fine-grained low-level visual perception, specifically the ability to detect subtle discrepancies among visually similar elements. This perceptual sensitivity is a prerequisite for robust spatial reasoning and grounding, yet no systematic, controllable benchmark exists to measure it, and it remains unclear how well current state-of-the-art models perform on such tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263057/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each input is a synthetic grid image (5–9 rows × 5–9 columns) of visually similar SVG icons rendered in 60–80 px blocks, where exactly one element differs from the rest by one or more controlled perceptual attributes (CIE-Lab color ΔE ∈ [5,20], size ratio 85–115%, rotation ±5°–25°, or position shift 5–12% of block size), accompanied by a text prompt instructing the model to identify the odd element. The expected output is the (row, column) grid coordinate of the anomalous icon. The benchmark spans 1,400 test samples across seven categories: four single-attribute types and three multi-attribute combinations (2-Type, 3-Type, 4-Type), with 200 samples per category.

## Method

OddGrid-GRPO is a reinforcement learning framework built on the GRPO algorithm (from DeepSeek-V3) that enhances fine-grained visual discrimination through two key mechanisms. First, curriculum-guided optimization partitions 30K training samples into Easy (15K), Medium (10K), and Hard (5K) subsets based on a composite difficulty score (grid size, attribute count, perturbation magnitude), and trains the model progressively across three stages to prevent premature convergence. Second, a distance-aware reward replaces the binary correctness signal with a continuous Gaussian-decay function r_d = max(exp(−d²/2σ²) − β, 0), where d is the Euclidean distance between predicted and ground-truth grid cells and σ scales with the grid diagonal (λ=0.25, β=0.3), providing richer spatial feedback for near-miss predictions. The overall reward blends this with a format reward (ω=0.2), and training is conducted on Qwen3-VL-2B under the EasyR1 framework for 100 steps on 4×A800 GPUs, achieving 82.64% overall accuracy versus 17.14% for the base model and 70.86% for standard GRPO.

## Results

On OddGridBench, the best-performing open-source model Qwen3-VL-32B achieves 68.07% total accuracy, while the best proprietary model Gemini-2.5-Pro reaches 49.29%, both far below the human accuracy of 87.47%. Several models (Phi-3.5-Vision, LLaVA-v1.6-34B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) perform at or near random chance (~2%), and all models show the weakest performance on Size (best: 39.5%) and Position (best: 39.0%) dimensions. After applying OddGrid-GRPO to Qwen3-VL-2B, total accuracy improves from 17.14% to 82.64%, surpassing both standard GRPO (70.86%) and GSPO (75.93%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 19 evaluated MLLMs, Qwen3-VL-32B leads all models at 68.07% total accuracy, outperforming the best proprietary system Gemini-2.5-Pro (49.29%) by 18.78 points and Qwen3-VL-4B (52.43%) by 15.64 points. Notably, smaller Qwen3-VL models (2B, 4B) outperform much larger models like InternVL3.5-38B (27.07%) and Molmo-72B (11.00%), suggesting perceptual sensitivity depends more on data alignment than parameter scale. Several models (Phi-3.5-Vision, LLaVA-v1.6-34B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) score at or below random chance (~2%), indicating a complete failure on fine-grained discrepancy detection. In the RL method comparison (Table 3), OddGrid-GRPO applied to Qwen3-VL-2B achieves 82.64% total accuracy, surpassing GSPO (75.93%) by 6.71 points and standard GRPO (70.86%) by 11.78 points, with the largest gains in Position (+23.0 over GRPO) and Rotation (+13.0 over GRPO). All models remain substantially below human performance (87.47%), with the gap most pronounced on Size and Position dimensions where even the best model reaches only ~39%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Odd-One-Out paradigm from cognitive psychology (Treisman & Gelade, 1980) and prior anomaly-detection work in vision into a systematic, parameterized evaluation framework for MLLMs, which prior benchmarks have largely overlooked in favor of high-level semantic reasoning. On the method side, it builds directly on GRPO (the core RL paradigm from DeepSeek-V3) and extends it with two targeted modifications: a curriculum-guided training schedule and a distance-aware reward that replaces the binary correctness signal with a continuous spatial-proximity-based feedback. It also positions itself against existing RL-based multimodal alignment work, which typically optimizes outcome-level semantic rewards rather than perceptual-level spatial accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a concrete and quantifiable perceptual bottleneck in current MLLMs — even proprietary frontier models (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro) achieve only ~49% total accuracy versus 87.47% for humans, with particularly severe deficits in rotation and position discrimination. The tolerance-accuracy analysis further reveals that models often localize near the correct cell but lack precise spatial calibration, suggesting a foundational limitation that likely propagates to higher-level spatial reasoning and grounding tasks. Open questions include whether the synthetic grid setting generalizes to natural scenes, whether the RL gains transfer across architectures and scales, and whether perceptual sensitivity can be improved through pre-training data curation rather than post-hoc RL.
