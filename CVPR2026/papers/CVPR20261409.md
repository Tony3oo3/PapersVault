## Problem

MLLMs remain fragile to viewpoint changes: pixel-wise warping amplifies small depth errors into severe geometric distortions, and even models fine-tuned with explicit 3D supervision or augmented with 3D-aware features struggle to reason about how a scene appears from a nearby viewpoint. The paper asks whether image tokens in ViT-based MLLMs—part-level perceptual units inspired by mental imagery theory—can serve as a more robust substrate for viewpoint transformation than individual pixels.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source RGB image, its depth map (ground truth or monocular estimate), source and target 4×4 camera pose matrices, the intrinsic matrix, and a natural-language question posed from the target viewpoint. It produces either a binary spatial-reasoning answer (left/right) or a free-form object description as it would appear from the target view. Crucially, the warping operates entirely on token embeddings at inference time with no fine-tuning, and the MLLM receives a dense, regularly spaced grid of tokens in the target view rather than a warped pixel image.

## Method

The core mechanism is backward token warping: a dense regular grid is defined on the target view, and for each grid point a corresponding source-view token is retrieved via ray casting on a lightweight 3D proxy mesh built from the source depth map. Two fetching strategies are explored—nearest fetching (assigning the closest precomputed source token) and adaptive fetching (re-patchifying the source image at the mapped coordinate to produce a new token centered exactly at that location). A key empirical finding is that tokens are highly robust to positional noise (accuracy remains stable even when patch centers are jittered by up to 20 pixels), explaining why token-level warping tolerates depth estimation errors that would catastrophically distort pixel-level warping. Backward warping consistently outperforms forward warping because it preserves the dense, regular token grid that MLLMs are trained on, and nearest fetching performs comparably to adaptive fetching despite requiring no additional re-patchification computation.

## Results

On ViewBench with ground-truth depth, Backward-Nearest token warping achieves 74.87% accuracy on ViewBench-Text (5–15% overlap) and 67.44% on ViewBench-Shape (5–15% overlap), outperforming the strongest generative baseline GenWarp (69.35% and 53.10%) by 5.5 and 14.3 percentage points respectively. On the hardest setting (5–15% overlap), Backward-Adaptive reaches 77.89% on Text and 75.72% on Shape at 25–35% overlap, approaching the oracle target-view performance of 100%. For the ViewBench-Object description task (1–10 scale), Backward-Adaptive scores 4.97 at 5–15% overlap, surpassing GenWarp (4.32) and pixel-wise backward warping (4.53). The method requires no fine-tuning and only minimal inference-time computation for the warping step.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Backward token warping (both Nearest and Adaptive variants) consistently outperforms all five baselines across every task and overlap level. Against the generative warping method GenWarp [76], the proposed approach gains +5.5 pp on ViewBench-Text and +14.3 pp on ViewBench-Shape at the hardest 5–15% overlap setting, and also surpasses it on the Object description task (4.80–4.97 vs. 4.32). Among specialist MLLMs, VLM-3R [20] is the strongest baseline (63.82% Text, 49.22% Shape at 5–15%), yet still trails Backward-Nearest by 11.05 and 18.22 pp respectively; the paper notes that VLM-3R's 3D-aware features from CUT3R are insufficient for mental viewpoint shifting. SpatialReasoner [52] (46.73% Text) and ViLaSR [91] (44.22% Text) lag further behind, as does the unmodified Qwen2.5-VL [2] base model (46.23% Text). Notably, forward token warping underperforms even pixel-wise backward warping, confirming that the dense regular grid produced by backward projection is critical for MLLM performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the ViT image token as a unit of geometric transformation, drawing on classical mental-imagery theory (Shepard, Minsky, Pylyshyn, Hinton) that posits part-level structural representations as the substrate for human perspective-taking. It departs from two existing lines: (1) pixel-wise warping and generative novel-view synthesis (e.g., GenWarp), which are sensitive to depth noise and can hallucinate content, and (2) object-level or 3D-feature-based spatial reasoning MLLMs (SpatialReasoner, VLM-3R, ViLaSR), which either lack fine-grained spatial detail or require expensive fine-tuning. The key conceptual move is treating the fixed-size patch token as the 'perceptual atom' at the right granularity between pixels (too fine) and objects (too coarse), and showing that backward warping onto a regular target grid preserves the dense, regularly-spaced token layout that ViT-based MLLMs expect.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that MLLM tokens tolerate positional perturbations of up to ~20 pixels (nearly a full patch size) without significant accuracy loss is practically important: it means imperfect monocular depth estimates (e.g., Depth Anything V2) are sufficient for viewpoint warping, removing a major practical barrier. The method is training-free and adds only minimal inference-time computation, making it a drop-in enhancement for any ViT-based MLLM. Limitations include restriction to nearby viewpoints with substantial overlap, dependence on a depth map, and evaluation primarily on indoor ScanNet scenes with a single base model (Qwen2.5-VL). Open questions include how the approach degrades for larger viewpoint changes, whether it generalizes to outdoor or dynamic scenes, and whether the regular-grid constraint of backward warping can be relaxed.
