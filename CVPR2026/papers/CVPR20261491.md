## Problem

Animating real-world 3DGS trees with structurally consistent, fine-grained branch and leaf-level motion remains unsolved. Existing 4D generation methods rely on 2D video diffusion priors that produce temporal and spatial inconsistencies, while physics-based simulators (e.g., MPM) are computationally expensive and use simplified constitutive models that suppress local deformation details. No prior work achieves fast, feed-forward, interactive animation of reconstructed real trees.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261491/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view images of a static tree, reconstructs a 3DGS model and mesh, then voxelizes the mesh into a sparse grid at 128³ resolution (from 512³ input) as the conditioning signal. It outputs a compact sparse voxel spectrum (K=16 frequency components, each a 6×n real-valued tensor encoding real and imaginary parts of x/y/z displacements), which is decoded via inverse FFT and devoxelization into dense mesh motion, then transferred to ~5 Gaussians per face to produce the full animated 3DGS sequence. For interaction, an external force vector drives a modal-analysis solver that returns a per-vertex deformation field in ~18 ms per frame.

## Method

DynamicTree uses a two-stage pipeline: a sparse voxel diffusion model generates mesh motion in the frequency domain, and a mesh-binding step (GaMeS) transfers that motion to 3DGS primitives. The key representation is the sparse voxel spectrum, where all vertices within a voxel share a single displacement and long-term quasi-periodic motion is captured by only the top 16 FFT frequency components, reducing the prediction target from dense per-vertex per-frame displacements to a compact 6×n×16 tensor. The diffusion backbone is a sparse-convolution U-Net (derived from XCube) conditioned on a voxel-grid latent and a frequency embedding, trained from scratch on the newly introduced 4DTree dataset (~8.5k synthetic animated trees, 100 frames each) with a two-stage loss schedule: 40k iterations of pure diffusion loss followed by 30k iterations adding a Local Spectrum Smoothness (LSS) regularizer that enforces spectral consistency among spatially neighboring voxels. For real-time interaction, the predicted spectrum doubles as a set of 3D modal bases; an external force is projected into modal space, decoupled second-order ODEs are integrated with explicit Euler, and the response is reconstructed as a linear combination of mode shapes, achieving ~18 ms per frame versus 1,800–15,600 ms for MPM-based baselines.

## Results

On 3D animation, DynamicTree achieves CLIP-I of 0.0052 and CLIP-T of 0.0021, substantially outperforming 4DGen (0.0103/0.0094) and SV4D 2.0 (0.0081/0.0057), and dominates the user study with 94.6% overall preference versus 1.7% and 3.7% for the two baselines. On interactive simulation, DynamicTree achieves CLIP-I of 0.0038 and CLIP-T of 0.0017 (best among all methods), with 47.9% overall user preference, while running at 18.22 ms/frame—roughly 100× faster than PhysGaussian (1,800 ms) and ~850× faster than PhysFlow (15,600 ms). The method also outperforms the traditional Weber & Penn tree animation baseline in a separate user study (57.86% vs. 42.14% overall).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 4D generation baselines (4DGen, SV4D 2.0), DynamicTree wins decisively on every metric: CLIP-I is roughly 2× lower, CLIP-T is 2–4× lower, and user-study overall preference jumps from 1.7–3.7% to 94.6%, indicating that VDM-based 4D methods produce severe structural artifacts on complex tree geometry. Against physics-based 3DGS simulators (PhysGaussian, PhysFlow), DynamicTree achieves the best CLIP-I (0.0038 vs. 0.0047/0.0061) and CLIP-T (0.0017 vs. 0.0025/0.0087), and wins the overall user study (47.9% vs. 31.9%/20.2%), though it trails PhysFlow slightly on 3D structural consistency (29.8% vs. 34.0%). The most striking advantage is speed: 18.22 ms/frame versus 1,800 ms (PhysGaussian) and 15,600 ms (PhysFlow), enabling real-time interaction. Against the traditional Weber & Penn tree animation method, DynamicTree wins the user study on motion complexity (62.86% vs. 37.14%), visual quality (65.71% vs. 34.29%), and overall (57.86% vs. 42.14%), while being comparable on motion authenticity (51.43% vs. 48.57%).

## Relation to Prior Work

DynamicTree sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) spectral motion representation from Generative-Dynamics [31] and ModalNeRF [37], which model quasi-periodic motion as a few FFT components; (2) sparse voxel generative architectures from XCube [43], repurposed here as a diffusion backbone for 3D motion rather than shape; and (3) mesh-to-Gaussian binding from GaMeS [55], which decouples motion prediction from the expensive per-Gaussian deformation. The key departure is from both VDM-based 4D generation (4DGen, SV4D), which inherits 2D temporal/spatial inconsistencies, and MPM-based physics simulation (PhysGaussian, PhysFlow), which is too slow for real-time use and relies on uniform material assumptions. By learning a 3D diffusion prior directly over sparse voxel spectra, the paper avoids per-scene optimization while retaining physically plausible, fine-grained branch-level dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100×+ speedup over MPM-based simulation (18 ms vs. 1,800–15,600 ms per frame) makes interactive 3DGS tree animation practical for VR and games, a setting where prior physics-based methods were infeasible. The 4DTree dataset (8.5k animated meshes with 100-frame sequences) is the first large-scale resource for learning 3D tree dynamics and could support future work beyond this specific architecture. However, the modal-analysis interaction is a global linear approximation that can produce synchronized motion across distant regions, and the current dataset covers primarily swaying rather than large deformations, leaving open the question of whether the spectral representation generalizes to more violent or asymmetric forces. The finding that 128³ voxel resolution outperforms 512³ due to synthetic-to-real noise smoothing is a useful practical insight for sparse 3D generative models more broadly.
