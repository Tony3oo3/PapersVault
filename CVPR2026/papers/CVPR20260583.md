## Problem

Reconstructing dynamic 4D scenes from monocular video remains difficult because moving objects corrupt camera pose estimation and existing feed-forward models are trained predominantly on static scenes. Optimization-based pipelines handle dynamics but are too slow for real-time or streaming use, while current streaming reconstruction methods lack explicit motion-static disentanglement and suffer from error accumulation over long sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260583/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a monocular video as a sequential frame stream, processing each new frame online via KV-cached grouped causal attention without requiring the full sequence upfront. It jointly outputs per-frame depth maps, 9-dimensional camera parameters, dynamic 3D point maps, and binary motion masks. Notably, motion masks are predicted as auxiliary outputs but are never fed back as explicit inputs at inference time; motion cues are instead implicitly encoded in the learned representations.

## Method

MoRe builds on the VGGT backbone and introduces three key mechanisms. First, an attention-forcing strategy uses ground-truth motion masks during training to supervise the camera token's attention distribution over image tokens, explicitly teaching the model to attend to static regions and suppress dynamic ones—this is entirely test-time-free. Second, a grouped causal attention scheme reformulates the standard upper-triangular causal mask into a frame-wise mask, preserving full bidirectional attention within each frame while enforcing temporal causality across frames, enabling efficient streaming via KV-caching. Third, a bundle-adjustment-like token aggregation step duplicates camera tokens after the full sequence is processed and performs an additional global attention pass over all cached key-value features to refine poses with long-range geometric consistency. Training combines confidence-weighted regression for depth and point maps, binary cross-entropy for motion masks, an attention-alignment loss, and relative pose supervision with selective gradient detachment to stabilize the streaming path.

## Results

On camera pose estimation, MoRe (Streaming) achieves ATE of 0.1474 on Sintel, 0.0211 on Bonn, 0.0260 on TUM-dynamics, and 0.0605 on ScanNet, outperforming all streaming baselines. The full-attention variant MoRe (FA) reaches ATE of 0.0877 on Sintel and 0.0115 on TUM-dynamics, surpassing VGGT (FA) by ~49% and ~46% respectively on these dynamic benchmarks. For video depth estimation, MoRe (Streaming) attains Abs Rel of 0.254 on Sintel and 0.072 on KITTI, while MoRe (FA) achieves 0.055 on Bonn and 0.066 on KITTI, matching or exceeding VGGT (FA) across all four depth benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoRe (Streaming) consistently outperforms all five streaming baselines on dynamic datasets: on Sintel ATE it achieves 0.1474 vs. Stream3R (0.2144), CUT3R (0.2163), WinT3R (0.2251), Spann3R (0.3313), and StreamVGGT (0.4159); on TUM-dynamics it reaches 0.0260 vs. CUT3R (0.0438) and WinT3R (0.0700), though Stream3R is slightly ahead at 0.0240. On the static ScanNet benchmark, Stream3R (0.0521) and WinT3R (0.0618) edge out MoRe (0.0605), indicating a small trade-off in purely static settings. The full-attention MoRe (FA) substantially surpasses VGGT (FA) on dynamic scenes—Sintel ATE 0.0877 vs. 0.1715 and TUM-dynamics ATE 0.0115 vs. 0.0109—while remaining competitive on Bonn (0.0138 vs. 0.0141). For depth estimation, MoRe (Streaming) leads all streaming methods on Sintel (Abs Rel 0.254 vs. Stream3R's 0.397) and KITTI (0.072 vs. Stream3R's 0.079), while MoRe (FA) matches VGGT (FA) on Bonn (0.055) and KITTI (0.066 vs. 0.073). Overall, the attention-forcing strategy yields the largest gains on dynamic benchmarks, confirming that explicit motion disentanglement during training is the key differentiator over both full-attention and streaming baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoRe builds directly on VGGT's transformer-based multi-view geometry backbone and Dust3R's point-map regression formulation, extending them to handle dynamic scenes and streaming input. It departs from prior streaming reconstruction methods (CUT3R, Stream3R, WinT3R) that adopt standard LLM-style causal attention by introducing a frame-wise grouped causal mask that preserves intra-frame spatial coherence while enforcing inter-frame temporal causality. The key conceptual departure is the attention-forcing training strategy: rather than relying on explicit motion priors (optical flow, segmentation) at inference as hybrid pipelines like MegaSaM or MonST3R do, MoRe uses ground-truth motion masks solely during training to shape the camera token's attention distribution, making inference test-time-free. The BA-like token aggregation step draws an analogy to classical bundle adjustment in SfM, repurposing a global optimization concept as a lightweight attention-based refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoRe addresses a practical gap in 4D reconstruction: producing accurate, temporally coherent geometry from monocular video in dynamic scenes at streaming speed without auxiliary motion modules. The attention-forcing mechanism is an elegant training-only trick that avoids the computational overhead of explicit motion segmentation at inference, making it attractive for real-time applications like AR and robotics. However, the approach still requires ground-truth motion masks during training, limiting its applicability to domains where such supervision is available or can be approximated. Open questions include how well the method generalizes to scenes dominated by dynamic content (where the 'static background' assumption weakens), whether the BA-like refinement can be made truly online rather than post-hoc, and how performance scales to very long sequences beyond the evaluated benchmarks.
