## Problem

Existing benchmarks for generative models each cover only a subset of evaluation dimensions: video-centric suites (VBench) lack 4D consistency and fine-grained physical realism, world-generation benchmarks (WorldScore) provide limited condition alignment and no physics assessment, and physics-specific benchmarks (PhyGenBench) ignore perceptual quality and multi-modal conditioning. No unified framework simultaneously measures perceptual quality, condition-4D alignment, physical realism, and 4D consistency across text, image, and video inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261899/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark accepts text prompts, single RGB images, or video clips as conditioning inputs for 3D/4D generation models spanning Image-to-3D/4D, Video-to-4D, and Text-to-3D/4D tasks. All non-text conditions are first converted into textual descriptions via Keye-VL 1.5 captioning, creating a unified textual evaluation space. The output is a structured score vector across four dimensions with sub-scores: perceptual quality (spatial, temporal, 3D texture), condition-4D alignment (event, scene, attribute, relationship, motion), physical realism (dynamics, optics, thermal), and 4D consistency (viewpoint, motion, style), each in [0,1].

## Method

The core contribution is an adaptive hybrid evaluation pipeline that maps all modality conditions into a common textual space, then uses an LLM (GPT-5) to identify which physical or alignment dimensions are semantically relevant to each specific condition and generate targeted diagnostic questions. A dual-track QA design separates concrete visual grounding (handled by MLLM-based judges like Qwen2.5-VL for surface-level video understanding) from abstract physical reasoning (handled by LLM-based judges for fluid dynamics, force interactions, and conservation laws). For physical realism, both the condition and generated video are captioned, the LLM generates physics-specific questions from the condition semantics, and binary correctness of predicted vs. expected answers yields a continuous score. 4D consistency combines DROID-SLAM reprojection error for 3D geometry, optical-flow similarity plus MLLM yes/no QA for motion rationality, and Gram-matrix feature comparison for style. Perceptual quality integrates CLIPIQA+ and CLIP-Aesthetic for spatial fidelity, FastVQA for temporal coherence, and mPLUG-Owl3 for 3D texture realism.

## Results

Across 4D generation models, DiffusionAsShader achieves the highest overall score (0.763) among Image-to-4D methods, ReCamMaster leads Video-to-4D (0.685), and 4Dfy tops Text-to-4D (0.535). For 3D generation, MotionCtrl scores best on Image-to-3D (0.770) and Text2NeRF on Text-to-3D (0.699). On the VideoPhy2-test validation set, the proposed evaluation method achieves Min(PC,SA) F1 of 0.439 and Joint F1 of 0.631, matching or exceeding the trained VideoPhy2 model (0.452/0.656) while substantially outperforming PhyGenBench (0.181/0.359) and VBench2 (0.123/0.187). Human correlation studies (Table 6) show the redesigned metrics improve PLCC by 0.15–0.33 over VBench2.0 and WorldScore baselines across alignment and consistency dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 4D generation models, DiffusionAsShader outperforms CamI2V by 0.066 overall (0.763 vs. 0.697), driven by stronger physical realism (Optics 0.850 vs. 0.738, Thermal 0.763 vs. 0.629) and 4D consistency (Viewpoint 0.908 vs. 0.701). In Video-to-4D, ReCamMaster leads with 0.685 overall, surpassing TrajectoryCrafter (0.670) and Vista (0.617), primarily through superior physical dynamics (0.680 vs. 0.671/0.607) and thermal realism (0.773 vs. 0.636/0.593). Text-to-4D models lag significantly behind image- and video-conditioned counterparts, with 4Dfy (0.535) and dreamin4D (0.439) showing the weakest physical realism scores (Dynamics 0.265/0.235, Thermal 0.200/0.150). For evaluation methodology validation on VideoPhy2-test, the proposed LLM-based QA approach matches the trained VideoPhy2 model on Min(PC,SA) F1 (0.439 vs. 0.452) while substantially outperforming PhyGenBench (0.181) and VBench2 (0.123) by 2.4× and 3.6× respectively. Human correlation analysis (Table 6) confirms the redesigned metrics improve PLCC over VBench2.0 by 0.15–0.33 (e.g., Attribute Ctrl. 0.483 vs. 0.167; Motion Ctrl. 0.546 vs. 0.250) and over WorldScore by 0.15–0.34 (e.g., Viewpoint 0.543 vs. 0.213), validating the adaptive hybrid evaluation design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies evaluation dimensions that prior benchmarks address in isolation: WorldScore covers 3D/4D scene generation and style/viewpoint consistency, VBench/VBench2.0 target video fidelity and controllability, and PhyGenBench/VideoPhy focus on physical commonsense. The key departure is the joint measurement of perceptual quality, condition-4D alignment, physical realism, and 4D consistency within a single multi-modal (text/image/video) framework, combined with an adaptive LLM/MLLM-as-judge QA methodology that maps all conditioning modalities into a unified textual space. The style and 3D consistency metrics are directly inherited from WorldScore, while physical prompts are adapted from PhyGenBench, positioning 4DWorldBench as a synthesis and extension rather than a fundamentally new evaluation paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As world generation models move from visual synthesis toward physically coherent 3D/4D simulation, a unified benchmark covering all four dimensions with multi-modal conditioning fills a clear gap for fair cross-model comparison. The finding that text-conditioned models consistently underperform image/video-conditioned models on physical realism highlights a fundamental limitation of language-only grounding for physics. However, the benchmark's practical impact is tempered by its relatively small scale (~126 text conditions, ~111 image conditions, ~96 video conditions), limited human studies (~15 participants, 100 videos), and the somewhat puzzling result that the hybrid LLM+MLLM judge performs worse than the pure LLM judge on physical realism, raising questions about the robustness of the evaluation pipeline. Open questions include scalability to longer-horizon 4D generation, broader real-world scenarios, and whether the LLM-based physical reasoning generalizes beyond the curated question set.
