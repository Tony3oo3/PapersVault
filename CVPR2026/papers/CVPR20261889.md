## Problem

Chain-of-thought reasoning is widely assumed to improve video understanding, yet the paper demonstrates that for RL-trained video models, direct answering often matches or even surpasses CoT inference on perception-oriented benchmarks while generating far fewer tokens. This raises the question of whether explicit step-by-step reasoning is always necessary for video tasks, and how to efficiently invoke it only when it provides tangible gains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261889/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip (up to 256 frames, 16K total video tokens) paired with a text question or grounding query. The output follows a strict template: an initial answer in a boxed block, a freeform rationale in a think block, and a reviewed answer in a second boxed block. For temporal grounding tasks, the answers are temporal boundary pairs. At inference, the model may terminate after the first boxed answer if its confidence exceeds a threshold, producing only a short direct answer of fewer than 10 tokens instead of the full reasoning trace.

## Method

VideoAuto-R1 introduces a 'Thinking Once, Answering Twice' training paradigm where the model always generates an initial answer, then a reasoning trace, then a reviewed answer, with both answers supervised via a dual-answer GRPO reward (w2 > w1 to prioritize the final answer, plus a fallback bonus for honest deferral on hard problems). This eliminates the need for per-sample think/no-think labels or mode-switch tokens during training. At inference, a confidence-based early-exit mechanism computes the length-normalized mean log probability of the first answer tokens; if it exceeds a fixed threshold (tau = 0.97), decoding terminates immediately as direct answering, otherwise the model continues to generate the full CoT and reviewed answer. The model is trained from Qwen2.5-VL-7B or Qwen3-VL-8B using GRPO on 83K curated samples spanning video QA, temporal grounding, and text/image reasoning, without a cold-start SFT stage.

## Results

VideoAuto-R1 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves 67.3% on VideoMME, 71.0% on MVBench, 69.7% on MMVU, 58.6% on VideoMMMU, and 39.4% pairwise accuracy on MVP, while generating only 44 tokens on average (vs. 386 for Video-R1). With the stronger Qwen3-VL-8B backbone, it reaches 71.7% on VideoMME, 71.1% on MMVU, 65.0% on VideoMMMU, and 43.0% on MVP. On temporal grounding, it improves Charades-STA mIoU from 52.9% (Qwen2.5-VL baseline) to 60.0% and ActivityNet mIoU from 26.9% to 47.6%, while NExT-GQA accuracy jumps from 53.3% to 80.6%. The think-mode activation rate is only 25% on perception-oriented MVBench but rises to 51% on reasoning-heavy VideoMMMU, confirming adaptive allocation of reasoning budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the non-reasoning Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline, VideoAuto-R1 gains +1.3 on VideoMME, +3.9 on VideoMMMU, and +2.9 on MVP while using only 44 tokens (vs. 3.0), showing that adaptive reasoning adds value without the cost of always-on CoT. Compared to Video-R1 (386-token responses), VideoAuto-R1 improves VideoMME by +5.5, VideoMMMU by +7.2, and MVP by +6.4 while reducing response length by ~8.8×. Against VideoChat-R1.5 (133 tokens), VideoAuto-R1 leads on VideoMME (+2.1), VideoMMMU (+9.0), and MVP (+0.8) with roughly 3× fewer tokens. On temporal grounding (Table 4), VideoAuto-R1 surpasses Time-R1 on Charades-STA mIoU (60.0 vs. 58.8) and ActivityNet mIoU (47.6 vs. 52.1 is a loss on ActivityNet, but NExT-GQA accuracy improves from – to 80.6), and beats VideoChat-R1.5 on Charades-STA mIoU (60.0 vs. 60.6 is a slight loss) while matching on ActivityNet. The Qwen3-VL variant further extends these margins, reaching 65.0% on VideoMMMU and 44.2% mIoU on NExT-GQA, setting new state-of-the-art across all evaluated benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeek-R1 and the video reasoning line (Video-R1, Time-R1, VideoChat-R1), all of which enforce an always-thinking paradigm for video understanding. It departs from prior auto-thinking methods (AdaptThink, R-4B) that learn a binary think/no-think switching policy during training, instead proposing a 'thinking once, answering twice' template where both answers are jointly optimized via dual-answer GRPO rewards, with the think/no-think decision deferred entirely to inference time via a confidence-based early-exit rule. The key conceptual departure is the empirical demonstration that CoT is often redundant or even harmful for perception-oriented video tasks, motivating the reason-when-necessary strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.3× reduction in average response length (149→44 tokens) while matching or exceeding accuracy of always-think models offers a practical efficiency win for deploying video reasoning systems at scale. The finding that token-level confidence in the initial answer reliably predicts whether further reasoning is needed—without any additional training signal or mode-switch head—suggests a broadly applicable principle for adaptive computation in multimodal LLMs. Open questions include whether the confidence-based routing generalizes to longer reasoning chains, more complex multi-step video tasks, or non-video modalities, and whether the approach degrades when the initial answer is systematically overconfident on adversarial inputs.
