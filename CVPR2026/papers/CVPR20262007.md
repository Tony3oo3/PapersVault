## Problem

Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (MCoT) models suffer from severe hallucinations, but prior work attributes them to the well-studied visual attention decay problem in LVLMs. The authors question whether MCoT models have unique causes of hallucination given their fundamentally different explicit reasoning process, and find that fabricated content is concentrated in associative reasoning steps (termed divergent thinking) where the model relies on internal reasoning rather than visual evidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262007/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text query as input to a pre-trained MCoT model (e.g., R1-Onevision-7B, GRIT-3B, PixelReasoner-7B). During the prefilling stage, visual token probabilities over the full vocabulary are pre-computed, so the per-step decoding overhead is negligible. The output is the model's standard text response structured as a reasoning chain (thinking) followed by an answer, but with the decoding distribution adaptively modified at detected divergent thinking steps to suppress hallucinated tokens.

## Method

The core contribution is a training-free, entropy-based decoding intervention. First, a visual entropy score is defined: visual tokens are passed through the model to obtain hidden states, mapped to vocabulary probabilities via the language head, and the entropy of the resulting visual activation distribution for the predictive token is computed (normalized to [0,1]). High visual entropy reliably identifies divergent thinking steps (McFadden's pseudo-R² > 0.9). Second, when the entropy exceeds a threshold γ, the decoding distribution is modified by multiplying with exp(−α·E(·,v)), penalizing tokens that would increase visual uncertainty and encouraging the model to ground its next token in visual evidence. The approach requires only two model-agnostic hyperparameters (γ=0.5, α=0.75), avoids double-inference overhead of contrastive decoding, and is composable with existing mitigation methods like DoLa and VCD.

## Results

On Object HalBench, the proposed method reduces sentence-level hallucination (CHAIR_S) by 30–47% relative to the backbone models: GRIT-3B drops from 23.8 to 16.0, PixelReasoner-7B from 22.0 to 15.4, and R1-Onevision-7B from 23.2 to 15.8. On POPE Random, accuracy improves to 81.0%, 87.3%, and 83.5% respectively, while on MMhalBench the hallucination rate (HalRate) decreases to 0.42, 0.26, and 0.50. The method also boosts general reasoning: MathVista accuracy rises by 1.1–1.9 points and VSR by 1.5–3.2 points across the three models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four main baselines (DoLa, VCD, MemVR, FlexAC) across every model and benchmark. The closest competitor is FlexAC, which achieves CHAIR_S of 19.2/18.8/18.4 on the three models, while the proposed method reaches 16.0/15.4/15.8 — a 3.2–3.4 point gap. DoLa and VCD, both contrastive-decoding approaches, show the weakest hallucination reduction (CHAIR_S 20.4–21.6) and can even degrade reasoning accuracy (e.g., VCD drops MathVista by 0.7 points on PixelReasoner-7B). MemVR offers moderate gains but still trails the proposed method by 3–4 CHAIR_S points. In the intervention-technique ablation (Table 3), the visual-entropy decoding strategy also beats PAI (CHAIR_S 19.6) and MemVR (19.1) when applied only to divergent thinking steps, confirming that the entropy-based modulation is the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the prevailing explanation of MCoT hallucinations as a consequence of global visual attention decay (Liu et al. [26], Tian et al. [51], Yang et al. [59]) by identifying a more localized and mechanistic cause: associative reasoning steps the authors term 'divergent thinking.' Methodologically, it sits within the training-free decoding-intervention family (DoLa, VCD, MemVR, FlexAC, PAI) but introduces a novel entropy-based diagnostic (visual entropy, building on Neo et al. [36]'s visual activation analysis) that enables targeted, step-level intervention rather than uniform decoding modification. The paper also draws on cognitive science's divergent/convergent thinking distinction [9] to structure its analysis of reasoning chains, a framing not previously applied to LVLM hallucination research.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic, interpretable account of where and why MCoT models hallucinate, which is valuable for both diagnosis and targeted intervention. The training-free, model-agnostic nature (fixed γ=0.5, α=0.75 across three architectures) and negligible inference overhead make it practically deployable, and its demonstrated compatibility with DoLa/VCD suggests it can serve as a plug-in layer for existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the divergent-thinking taxonomy generalizes to non-MCoT LVLMs or to longer/multi-turn reasoning, whether the entropy threshold remains stable as model scale increases beyond 7B, and whether the approach transfers to hallucination in video or 3D modalities.
