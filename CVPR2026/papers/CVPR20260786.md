## Problem

Existing 4D reconstruction methods based on Gaussian Splatting generate meshes independently per frame (e.g., via Poisson reconstruction), producing frame-wise topology variations that make downstream tasks like skeleton binding, animation, and keypoint tracking infeasible. No prior method maintains a fixed manifold topology across a dynamic Gaussian sequence while still allowing adaptive densification and pruning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260786/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB video with calibrated camera parameters (42 views at 1080×1080 on MIX-TAG; 30 of 59 views at 3000×4000 on TalkBody4D) plus an initial mesh from NeuS2 for the first frame. It outputs a sequence of meshes sharing identical vertex count and face connectivity across all frames, along with 3D keypoint trajectories parameterized by barycentric coordinates on the consistent topology. The underlying Gaussian representation is also retained for rendering.

## Method

The core contribution is a Gaussian topological structure that explicitly encodes manifold edges between neighboring Gaussians, enabling topology-aware densification (triangle subdivision into three faces when inserting a new Gaussian) and topology-preserving pruning (edge-collapse with a weighted geometric+attribute cost to merge pruned Gaussians into their 1-ring neighbor). Densification and pruning are restricted to the canonical (first-frame) space so the topology remains fixed for all subsequent frames. Temporal coherence is enforced through three 1-ring-neighborhood losses: edge-length consistency, a rigidity constraint aligning relative displacements via a per-Gaussian rotation delta, and a quaternion-based rotation consistency term. A differentiable mesh rasterizer (NvDiffRast) provides mesh-level color and mask supervision alongside standard Gaussian Splatting rendering losses, and a z-axis scale penalty plus Laplacian smoothness regularize the surface fit.

## Results

On the MIX-TAG dataset, TagSplat achieves the best rendering quality (PSNR 34.76/34.61/32.17 dB for Boxer/Dancer/Worker) and the lowest tracking MSE (0.000569/0.000101/0.000218) across all three subjects, outperforming all four baselines. For mesh reconstruction, TagSplat reports the lowest Chamfer Distance (0.32/0.24/0.39) and Earth Mover's Distance (0.010/0.088/0.10), substantially better than DG-Mesh (CD up to 10.31) and Dynamic2DGS (CD 0.45–0.75). On the real TalkBody4D dataset, TagSplat achieves PSNR 26.82 dB and SSIM 0.93 for mesh rendering, surpassing DG-Mesh (24.90 dB) and Dynamic-2DGS (23.58 dB). TagSplat is the only method that produces topology-consistent mesh sequences (T-C Mesh = √), a property no baseline achieves.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TagSplat outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric across all three MIX-TAG subjects. Against Dynamic3DGS [29], TagSplat gains 4–7 dB in PSNR and reduces tracking MSE by 17–76% (e.g., 0.000101 vs 0.000329 on Dancer). Against DG-Mesh [25], the advantage is most dramatic: TagSplat improves PSNR by 13–15 dB, reduces CD by 20–40×, and cuts tracking MSE by 20–220×, reflecting DG-Mesh's poor geometric fidelity. Against Deformable-GS [19], TagSplat edges ahead on PSNR (34.76 vs 34.38 on Boxer; 34.61 vs 19.08 on Dancer) and achieves 12–42× lower tracking MSE, while also being the only method to report mesh quality metrics. Against Dynamic2DGS [49], TagSplat wins on PSNR (by 0.3–7.2 dB), LPIPS, CD (0.32 vs 0.47 on Boxer), EMD (0.010 vs 0.13 on Boxer), and tracking MSE (0.000569 vs 0.009148 on Boxer). Critically, TagSplat is the sole method producing topology-consistent mesh sequences, a prerequisite for downstream animation tasks that none of the baselines can satisfy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting framework for dynamic reconstruction by introducing an explicit manifold topology over the Gaussian primitives, departing from prior methods (DG-Mesh, Dynamic 2DGS, Topo4D, GauSTAR) that reconstruct meshes independently per frame via Poisson or depth-based techniques. The topology-aware densification (triangle subdivision) and pruning (edge collapse inspired by Hoppe's quadric metric) are the key architectural departures, ensuring the Gaussian point cloud always forms a valid 2-manifold. Temporal coherence is enforced through 1-ring neighborhood regularization (edge-length, rigidity, rotation losses) rather than the per-frame independence used by Dynamic 3DGS or the template-mesh binding of Topo4D, which is restricted to head models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Topology-consistent dynamic meshes are a prerequisite for animation pipelines (skinning, retargeting, keyframe editing), and this work provides the first end-to-end Gaussian Splatting method that guarantees manifold topology throughout densification, pruning, and temporal deformation. The practical impact is significant for the animation and VFX industry, where per-frame Poisson meshes are currently unusable for downstream tasks. However, the method explicitly cannot handle drastic topological changes (clothing tears, object splits), and quantitative evaluation is limited to one synthetic dataset (MIX-TAG) with only four baselines; the real-data (TalkBody4D) results are qualitative only, leaving open questions about robustness to occlusion, extreme poses, and non-human subjects.
