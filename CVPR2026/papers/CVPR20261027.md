## Problem

3D point clouds inherently contain limited functional cues, so purely geometric 3D encoders struggle to distinguish parts that are geometrically similar but functionally distinct (e.g., a mug handle vs. its rim). Existing prompt-based affordance methods add textual or visual queries on top of these geometric encoders, but the bottleneck remains the encoder's lack of semantic structure rather than the prompting mechanism itself. The paper asks how to inject the rich, structured semantic knowledge already present in large-scale 2D Vision Foundation Models into 3D feature learning.

## Input / Output

At inference the model takes a 3D point cloud (uniformly sampled to 2048 points, grouped into 64 patches) together with a text prompt (e.g., 'Which part of the mug should be grasped') and/or a visual prompt (e.g., a hand-holding image), and outputs a dense per-point affordance segmentation map. The architecture is inherently multi-modal and can fuse both prompt types simultaneously, but current benchmarks support only one modality, so the unused channel receives a null input. During pretraining, each object is rendered into 12 RGB views at 224×224 from uniformly distributed camera poses to produce the lifted 2D supervision signal.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage pipeline centered on Cross-Modal Affinity Transfer (CMAT). In Stage 0, dense features from a frozen DINOv3 (ViT-L) are extracted from 12 rendered views and lifted onto 3D points via inverse projection and nearest-neighbor interpolation, yielding per-point 2D semantic descriptors. In Stage 1, a PointMAE-style transformer encoder (12 blocks, dim 384) is pretrained by aligning its patch-level cosine-similarity affinity matrix A^3D with the corresponding 2D affinity matrix A^2D through an MSE loss, supplemented by a 60%-masked geometric reconstruction loss and a KoLeo feature-diversity regularizer. In Stage 2, the CMAT-pretrained backbone is fine-tuned with a lightweight 6-block co-attentional segmentor that projects frozen RoBERTa-base text features and frozen DINOv3 (ViT-B) visual features into a shared embedding space, enabling bidirectional geometric–prompt interaction before upsampling to per-point segmentation logits.

## Results

On PIADv2 (the largest visual-prompt benchmark), the method achieves 44.88 aIoU and 0.783 SIM on the Seen split, surpassing the previous best (GREAT) by 6.85 aIoU points and 0.107 SIM. On the Unseen split, it reaches 27.40 aIoU and 0.593 SIM, a 7.24 aIoU improvement over GREAT. On the text-prompt LASO benchmark, it attains 21.7 aIoU (Seen) and 17.5 aIoU (Unseen), outperforming LASO [15] by 0.9 and 2.9 points respectively. The method also achieves the best SIM on PIAD Seen (0.725 vs. 0.590 for LASO), a 22.9% relative gain in structural fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and split. On PIADv2 Seen, it leads GREAT [37] (the strongest prior method using a 4B-parameter MLLM) by 6.85 aIoU (44.88 vs. 38.03) and 0.107 SIM, while using only 300M parameters and 4–8 GB VRAM versus GREAT's 16–30 GB. On PIADv2 Unseen, the gap widens to 7.24 aIoU (27.40 vs. 20.16), indicating stronger generalization to novel object categories. On PIAD Seen, it surpasses IAGNet [51] by 0.9 aIoU and LASO [15] by 1.7 aIoU, with a particularly large SIM margin (0.725 vs. 0.545 and 0.590). On the text-prompt LASO benchmark, it beats LASO [15] by 0.9 aIoU (Seen) and 2.9 aIoU (Unseen), and outperforms IAGNet [51] by 3.9 and 4.6 aIoU on the two splits respectively. The older baselines (MBDF, PMF, FRCNN, ILN, PFusion, XMF, ReferTrans, ReLA, 3D-SPS) trail by 8–14 aIoU points, confirming that the CMAT-pretrained backbone provides a fundamentally stronger semantic foundation than prior geometric or prompt-only approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the PointMAE masked autoencoder architecture for its 3D backbone and on multi-view feature lifting techniques (as in D3 Fields) to project 2D VFM features onto point clouds. It departs from prior 2D-to-3D knowledge transfer work, which typically aligns individual feature vectors or enforces broad consistency, by introducing affinity matrix alignment as the core pretraining objective—explicitly transferring relational structure among patches rather than pointwise semantics. The lightweight co-attention segmentor also contrasts with recent MLLM-based affordance pipelines (e.g., GREAT) by avoiding large language models entirely, instead injecting prompts directly into a semantically structured 3D feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete bottleneck in 3D affordance segmentation: the semantic poverty of sparse point clouds, which no amount of prompt engineering can fully overcome without a better feature space. The CMAT pretraining strategy is task-agnostic and could in principle improve other 3D understanding tasks (part segmentation, shape correspondence) that suffer from the same geometric ambiguity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to unstructured scenes (beyond single objects), whether the 12-view rendering requirement is a practical constraint in real-time robotics, and whether the affinity alignment mechanism generalizes to other VFM backbones or 3D architectures beyond PointMAE-style transformers.
