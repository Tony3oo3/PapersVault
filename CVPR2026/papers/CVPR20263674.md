## Problem

Existing identity-preserving video generation methods rely on static imitation learning with fixed reconstruction losses, which cannot actively navigate the complex, state-dependent trade-offs between identity fidelity, motion naturalness, and temporal coherence. This leads to a conservative equilibrium manifesting as the 'copy-paste' effect, where the reference identity is rigidly superimposed onto scenes with stiff, unnatural motion. The paper asks whether reformulating the task as a self-evolving reinforcement learning process can let the model dynamically prioritize competing quality dimensions throughout training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference identity image (e.g., a human face, encoded into VAE latent space) and a free-form text prompt describing the subject's actions, scene, and optionally camera motion, and produces a temporally coherent video clip in which the subject performs the described actions while maintaining perceptual identity. The base generator is a 14B-parameter Diffusion Transformer (VACE-14B) operating in the latent space of a pre-trained VAE, with inference using 20 Euler denoising steps and classifier-free guidance at scale 5.0. Training data consists of 291 curated face images each paired with at least 5 ChatGPT-generated scenario prompts, and evaluation is conducted on the 60-pair OpenS2V-Eval Human-Domain benchmark.

## Method

EvoID reformulates IP video generation as a sequential decision-making problem and optimizes a LoRA-adapted DiT (rank 32) via a reward-weighted velocity prediction loss derived from DiffusionNFT, where a stable anchor policy generates candidate videos and an evolving Student policy learns to shift its velocity predictions toward high-reward directions. A dual-path reward mechanism combines seven objective metrics (ArcFace/CurricularFace for identity, Gme for text adherence, Aesthetic/Q-Align/Naturalness/Optical Flow for quality) with an MLLM-based VQScore that holistically assesses five perceptual dimensions; an MLLM-Directed Dynamic Weighting scheme (using Qwen3-VL 30B) adaptively re-weights these rewards across three groups (Identity, Text, Quality) at each training iteration based on the model's current weaknesses. To prevent reward hacking and quality drift, a frozen T2V Teacher (Wan2.1-T2V-14B) provides an L2 alignment regularization, and a self-constraint loss anchors the Student to its initial IP policy, with the stable anchor updated via EMA of the Student parameters.

## Results

On the OpenS2V-Eval Human-Domain benchmark, EvoID achieves a Total Score of 0.704 and an EvoScore of 0.682, surpassing the strongest open-source model VACE-14B (0.658 / 0.544) and the leading closed-source model ViduQ2 (0.650 / 0.648). The enhanced variant EvoID†, which incorporates EvoScore as an additional training reward, pushes the EvoScore to a new record of 0.718 while maintaining a Total Score of 0.687 (still above all baselines). In blinded 2AFC human evaluation, EvoID wins 74% of comparisons against VACE-14B on Video Quality and 70% against ViduQ2 on Identity Consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvoID outperforms all 11 open-source baselines on both Total Score and EvoScore, with the largest margin over its own base model VACE-14B (+0.046 Total, +0.138 EvoScore), confirming the RL evolution paradigm's contribution beyond the underlying architecture. Against closed-source systems, EvoID beats Hailuo (0.704 vs. 0.653 Total) and ViduQ2 (0.704 vs. 0.650 Total) on the official metric, and surpasses ViduQ2 on EvoScore (0.682 vs. 0.648). On Face Similarity specifically, EvoID leads all methods at 0.745, exceeding VACE-14B (0.647) and Hailuo (0.577) by wide margins, while VideoMaker achieves a higher raw FaceSim (0.765) but at the cost of severely degraded GmeScore (0.453) and EvoScore (0.214), illustrating the identity-motion trade-off that EvoID's dual-path reward resolves. In human evaluation, EvoID wins 74% of Video Quality comparisons against VACE-14B but only 45% against ViduQ2, where commercial post-processing pipelines give ViduQ2 a slight edge in overall visual polish. The EvoID† variant trades a small Total Score drop (0.704→0.687) for a substantial EvoScore gain (0.682→0.718), demonstrating that the reward design can be steered toward different quality priorities without architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

EvoID builds directly on the VACE-14B identity-preserving DiT architecture and the DiffusionNFT policy-loss formulation, but departs fundamentally from the static imitation-learning paradigm (reconstruction loss) that dominates IP video generation. Where prior RL-for-diffusion work (PPO, DPO, GRPO, Identity-GRPO) relies on fixed or separately-trained reward models, EvoID introduces an MLLM-directed dynamic weighting mechanism that adaptively re-weights competing objective rewards at each training step, and anchors the evolving Student to a frozen T2V Teacher (Wan2.1-T2V-14B) to prevent quality drift. The self-regularization loss is adapted from GRPO's self-constraint, but the dual-path reward (objective metrics + MLLM holistic VQScore) and the Teacher-Student anchoring are the paper's distinctive architectural contributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm shift from passive reconstruction to active, reward-driven self-evolution offers a general recipe for steering diffusion-based generators toward human-preferred quality without architectural changes, and the MLLM-directed dynamic weighting is a practical technique transferable to other multi-objective generation tasks. However, the evaluation is limited to a single benchmark (OpenS2V-Eval Human-Domain, 60 face-prompt pairs) and a small training set (291 face images), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to non-human subjects, multi-subject scenes, or longer video durations. The observed trade-off—where adding EvoScore as a reward boosts the MLLM-based metric (0.682→0.718) but slightly reduces the OpenS2V Total Score (0.704→0.687)—highlights that no single reward perfectly captures all quality dimensions, and the optimal reward composition may be task- and domain-specific.
