## Problem

In Online Class Incremental Learning, existing methods represent each class with either a single adaptive centroid or fixed GMM centroids learned once via EM. Single centroids fail to capture the inherent multimodality of streaming data, while fixed GMM centroids become increasingly inaccurate as the backbone adapts to new data and the latent space shifts. Moreover, the EM algorithm required for GMM fitting is computationally expensive and ill-suited to the mini-batch, non-stationary nature of online learning.

## Input / Output

At each time step the model receives a mini-batch of new RGB images (from CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet, or MNIST) alongside a randomly sampled batch of old images drawn from a fixed-size replay memory buffer. During training it outputs updated class centroids and diagonal covariance matrices for every observed class; at inference it assigns an unseen image to the class whose GMM component yields the minimum Mahalanobis distance in the learned latent space, rather than using a linear classifier head.

## Method

The core module, MMOT, formulates class-distribution fitting as minimizing the ε-entropic Wasserstein distance between the empirical batch distribution and a K-component GMM, solved via the entropic dual form with a learned Kantorovich network and Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization of the mixing proportions. This replaces the multi-iteration EM algorithm with a few cheap gradient-descent steps per batch, enabling truly online, class-conditional updates of centroids and covariances. A complementary Dynamic Preservation loss then uses the learned centroids in a contrastive objective: same-class features are pulled toward all K centroids of their class while being pushed away from centroids and features of other classes, strengthening inter-class separability. Memory-buffer replenishment selects samples nearest to each centroid to maintain representativeness, and at test time classification is performed by minimum Mahalanobis distance to any Gaussian component of each class.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (M=1k), OTC achieves 76.1% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline BiC+AC (75.8%) and OnPro (74.7%). On CIFAR-100 (M=5k), OTC reaches 56.5%, surpassing MOSE (54.5%) and BiC+AC (54.2%) by 2–3%. On the most challenging Tiny-ImageNet (M=10k, 20 tasks), OTC attains 39.5%, exceeding MOSE (38.7%) by ~1% and BiC+AC (26.5%) by 13%. In terms of forgetting, OTC consistently ranks among the top two methods on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 (e.g., 9.8% forgetting at M=1k on CIFAR-10 vs. 12.8% for BiC+AC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OTC outperforms all 9 baselines (ER, ASER, CoPE, OCM, GSA, OnPro, MOSE, SBS, BiC+AC) on final average accuracy across every dataset and memory size tested. The closest competitor is BiC+AC, which OTC edges out by 0.3% on CIFAR-10 (M=1k: 76.1 vs 75.8) and by 2.3% on CIFAR-100 (M=5k: 56.5 vs 54.2). On Tiny-ImageNet, the gap widens dramatically: OTC leads MOSE (the second-best) by 0.8% at M=10k and beats BiC+AC by 13%. On the forgetting metric, OTC is consistently top-2 on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 (e.g., 9.8% vs BiC+AC's 12.8% at CIFAR-10 M=1k), though CoPE reports lower forgetting on Tiny-ImageNet (8.2% vs 16.5%)—however, the paper shows CoPE performs poorly from the start on that dataset, making its low forgetting metric misleading. The advantage of OTC is most pronounced at small memory budgets (M=0.2k–1k), which is the most resource-constrained and practically relevant OCIL setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on class representation in Online Class Incremental Learning, where prior methods either use a single adaptive centroid (CoPE, OnPro) or fixed GMM centroids learned once via EM (Li et al. 2024, Wang et al. 2023). The key departure is formulating GMM parameter learning as an Optimal Transport problem using the entropic dual form, which replaces the multi-iteration EM algorithm with a few gradient descent steps—making it naturally compatible with online mini-batch updates. The Gumbel-Softmax relaxation for the categorical mixing proportions and the Kantorovich network for the dual potential are borrowed from the entropic OT literature (Genevay et al. 2016, Jang et al. 2016) but applied here to the inverse GMM-learning problem in a non-stationary streaming setting, which the authors claim is unexplored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in providing a computationally cheaper alternative to EM for maintaining class distributions in online settings, and in showing that multiple adaptive centroids (rather than a single prototype) better capture the inherent multimodality of streaming data. However, the performance gains over strong baselines are modest (1–2% on CIFAR-10/100), and on Tiny-ImageNet the method shows more forgetting than CoPE, though CoPE's low initial accuracy inflates its forgetting metric. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones or higher-dimensional feature spaces, how the number of centroids K should be chosen adaptively, and whether the OT-based GMM learning generalizes beyond vision to other modalities or task-incremental settings.
