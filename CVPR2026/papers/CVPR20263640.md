## Problem

Audio-Visual Segmentation suffers from intra-modal noise (visual semantic overlap between target and background, audio frequency overlaps among multiple sources) and a persistent semantic gap between audio and visual representations, leading to blurred boundaries and incomplete masks. Existing remedies either depend on external priors (SAM-generated masks, offline prototype centers) or manually constructed positive-negative contrastive pairs, both of which add computational overhead and limit cross-domain generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263640/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single video frame (224×224) and a 1-second audio segment as input, encoded by a Swin-B visual backbone (producing four hierarchical feature levels) and an HTSAT audio encoder (producing a single D-dimensional vector), respectively. It outputs pixel-wise binary or semantic segmentation masks for sounding objects via a Deformable DETR pixel decoder, along with category-specific response scores derived from cross-modal attention. Training uses a batch size of only 4 on a single GPU, which motivates the use of Group Normalization in place of Batch Normalization within the contrastive projection heads.

## Method

BYOAVP comprises two adaptive modules. The Self-Supervised Audio Enhancement (SSAE) module treats audio and last-stage visual features as dual branches in a SimSiam-style contrastive framework: a cross-modal attention layer (audio as key/value, visual as query) performs early semantic alignment before independent projection heads, and a stop-gradient on the visual branch with a predictor on the audio branch enforces alignment via negative cosine similarity, denoising audio without explicit negative pairs. The Dynamic Prototype Constraint (DPC) module maintains a randomly initialized prototype bank updated per-batch via a momentum strategy with adaptive category weighting (scaled by pixel count per prototype); Gumbel-Softmax produces differentiable soft pixel assignments, and a cross-modal attention step identifies sounding categories whose attention scores are multiplied back onto the soft assignments to produce enhanced mask features. An Adaptive Prototype loss (amplified log-softmax weighted by soft assignments) regularizes the prototype bank, and the total objective combines segmentation (BCE+Dice+Focal), contrastive, and prototype losses.

## Results

On AVSBench, BYOAVP achieves J&F scores of 95.0 (S4), 80.5 (MS3), and 69.6 (AVSS), setting new state-of-the-art across all three sub-tasks. On the VPO benchmark, it reaches J&F of 75.5 (SS), 77.2 (MS), and 71.5 (MSMI), again outperforming all prior methods. The model uses a Swin-B visual encoder and CLAP/HTSAT audio encoder with 137.15M trainable parameters, trained with a batch size of 4.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BYOAVP outperforms all 26 baselines across all six sub-tasks on both AVSBench and VPO. On AVSBench-S4, it leads DDESEG (94.2) by 0.8 J&F and RAVS (93.5) by 1.5; on MS3, it surpasses AuralSAM2 (79.9) by 0.6 and DDESEG (77.9) by 2.6; on AVSS, it beats DDESEG (67.9) by 1.7 and RAVS (65.7) by 3.9. On VPO, the margins are 0.5 (SS vs. RAVS), 2.9 (MS vs. DDESEG), and 2.2 (MSMI vs. RAVS). Notably, the strongest baselines (DDESEG, RAVS, COMBO, VCT) all rely on offline-generated prototype centers or SAM-derived masks, whereas BYOAVP operates entirely without external priors. AuralSAM2 uses a substantially higher input resolution (1024×1024 vs. 224×224) yet is still outperformed on MS3 and AVSS. The largest gains appear on the multi-source and semantic tasks, indicating the prototype-based pixel classification and cross-modal attention are most beneficial when multiple sounding objects coexist.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised contrastive learning paradigm (SimSiam, BYOL) from single-modal to cross-modal audio-visual settings, using a stop-gradient predictor to align audio embeddings with visual semantics without explicit negative pairs. It departs from prior AVS methods that rely on external priors—SAM-generated masks (COMBO, VCT) or offline prototype centers (RAVS, DDESEG)—by replacing them with a fully adaptive, momentum-updated prototype bank and a self-supervised audio enhancement module. The Gumbel-Softmax mechanism from [24] is repurposed for differentiable pixel-to-prototype assignment, bridging discrete clustering with end-to-end gradient flow in the DPC module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating dependence on external priors (SAM masks, offline prototypes, manually constructed contrastive pairs), BYOAVP offers a more generalizable and computationally self-contained framework for AVS, which is important for deployment in in-the-wild scenarios where such priors are unavailable or unreliable. The demonstration that a fully adaptive prototype bank can outperform offline-generated ones challenges the assumption that pre-computed semantic anchors are necessary for fine-grained pixel classification. Open questions include whether the approach scales to substantially larger category spaces (beyond 71), whether the small-batch Group Normalization finding generalizes to other cross-modal tasks, and whether the framework transfers to related tasks like sound source localization or audio-visual event detection.
