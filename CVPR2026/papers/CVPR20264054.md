## Problem

Existing palmprint generation methods focus on style and texture diversity but largely ignore or oversimplify the geometric (non-rigid) deformations that naturally occur in real palms due to hand bending, finger articulation, and viewpoint variation. This gap limits the geometric diversity of synthetic training data and creates a performance bottleneck for downstream recognition models, as handcrafted perturbations like line oscillation or affine transforms cannot capture the complex, spatially varying deformations of real palms.

## Input / Output

The method takes a manual Bezier-curve crease map (256×256) representing the principal-line structure of a synthetic identity, a deformation field sampled from a pre-built library of RAFT-estimated optical flows between real same-identity palmprint pairs, and Gaussian noise. It produces a 256×256 synthetic palmprint image exhibiting realistic non-rigid geometric deformation while preserving the identity of the input crease map. The deformation library is constructed offline by filtering optical flow fields through smoothness (discontinuity ratio < 0.01) and identity-consistency (cosine similarity > 0.4 via a pretrained recognizer) checks.

## Method

FlowPalm constructs a deformation prior by estimating dense optical flow (RAFT-Large) between real same-identity palmprint pairs and filtering the resulting flow fields via a two-level quality assessment (smoothness validation and identity consistency check), forming a library of physically plausible non-rigid deformations. During generation, a three-stage progressive DDIM sampling strategy progressively incorporates the deformation: (1) the crease condition is warped by a sampled deformation field and denoised to t*=0.5T followed by a clean denoising step to obtain noise-free deformed principal lines; (2) a warped Gaussian noise (preserving the N(0,I) property via the ∫-Noise warping strategy) is injected at t* to synthesize textures that follow the deformed geometry while maintaining identity consistency; (3) the crease condition is dropped for the final 25% of diffusion steps (unconditional refinement) to correct over-constrained or unrealistic textures. The key distinction from prior work is the joint warping of both the structural condition and the noise input in a single step under deterministic DDIM sampling, rather than multi-iteration noise alignment, which ensures structural-textural deformation alignment while reducing computational overhead.

## Results

On six benchmark palmprint datasets, FlowPalm achieves the best average TAR@FAR=10⁻⁶ of 58.95% without augmentation and 85.20% with RandAugment, outperforming all five baselines. In the synthetic-only training setting, models trained exclusively on FlowPalm-generated data reach 85.20% average, surpassing the real-data-only baseline (73.59%) by 11.6 points. In the Syn.→Real pre-training-to-fine-tuning paradigm, FlowPalm attains 94.15% average, the highest among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowPalm outperforms all five baselines on the average TAR@FAR=10⁻⁶ metric in both evaluation settings. Without augmentation, FlowPalm (58.95%) leads UAA (51.35%) by 7.6 points, PFIG-Palm (37.01%) by 21.9 points, and Diff-Palm (9.84%) by 49.1 points, with the largest gap over RPG-Palm (0.66%). With RandAugment, FlowPalm (85.20%) extends its lead over Diff-Palm (79.02%) to 6.2 points and over UAA (74.70%) to 10.5 points, while PCE-Palm (40.98%) and PFIG-Palm (62.00%) trail by 44.2 and 23.2 points respectively. Notably, Diff-Palm, which ignores geometric deformation entirely, is the strongest baseline with augmentation but still falls short of FlowPalm's non-rigid deformation modeling. In the synthetic-only training paradigm, FlowPalm (85.20%) surpasses the real-data-only baseline (73.59%) by 11.6 points, indicating that its generated geometric diversity exceeds what real data provides. In the Syn.→Real fine-tuning setting, FlowPalm achieves 94.15% vs. PFIG-Palm's 92.03% and Diff-Palm's 90.00%, confirming consistent gains across training paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowPalm extends the diffusion-based palmprint generation line initiated by Diff-Palm (CVPR 2025) and the GAN-based predecessors (RPG-Palm, PCE-Palm, PFIG-Palm), all of which condition on Bezier-curve crease maps but treat geometric variation through handcrafted perturbations (line oscillation, affine transforms) or ignore it entirely. The key departure is the use of RAFT optical flow between real same-identity palmprint pairs to build a statistically grounded non-rigid deformation library, combined with the noise-warping strategy from Chang et al. (ICLR 2024) to propagate that deformation into the texture stage. The three-stage progressive pipeline—warped crease denoising, single-step warped-noise injection under DDIM, and unconditional refinement—repurposes the DDIM framework (Song et al., ICLR 2021) in a way that avoids the iterative noise alignment required by Diff-Palm's DDPM formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a recognition model trained exclusively on FlowPalm-generated samples (85.20% avg TAR) outperforms one trained on real data (73.59%) by over 11 points is practically significant for privacy-constrained biometric deployment, where collecting large real palmprint datasets is infeasible. The ablation showing that warping only the noise without the crease condition collapses performance (7.58% vs 58.95% w/o augmentation) underscores that structural-textural alignment is non-negotiable for identity-preserving deformation. Open questions include whether the static deformation library generalizes across acquisition devices and populations, whether the approach transfers to other biometric modalities (fingerprint, face) with analogous non-rigid variation, and whether the optical flow prior can be learned end-to-end rather than extracted offline.
