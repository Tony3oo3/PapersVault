## Problem

Existing multi-GPU parallelization methods for diffusion models (e.g., DistriFusion, AsyncDiff) require full activation exchange between devices after every denoising step, introducing communication latency that can offset the parallelism gains, especially under low-bandwidth interconnects like PCIe. These methods also suffer from incompatibility with fast samplers or specific architectures (U-Net vs. DiT), limiting their practical deployment. The paper tackles the gap of how to reduce inter-GPU communication while preserving generation fidelity and maintaining compatibility with accelerated sampling pipelines.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a standard text prompt and produces a generated RGB image (512×512 for SD 1.5, 1024×1024 for SDXL and SD3) using 50-step DDIM sampling with classifier-free guidance. It operates entirely in the latent space of pre-trained diffusion models without modifying the model weights or the input/output interface. The only operational constraint is a 4-step synchronous warm-up phase at the beginning of inference, after which the asynchronous sub-block transmission protocol takes over.

## Method

Otil (Only Transmit Informative Latents) exploits the observation that latent activations change only marginally between consecutive denoising steps and that these changes are spatially concentrated. It partitions the latent activations into N contiguous segments (one per GPU) and further decomposes each segment into K uniform square sub-blocks. After each denoising step, an information-guided module computes cosine similarity between paired sub-blocks across timesteps and transmits only the top-k most changed sub-blocks to other GPUs, reducing per-step communication by a factor of k/K relative to full exchange. A dynamic polling mechanism enforces a cyclic visitation schedule so that every sub-block is updated within a bounded number of steps, preventing persistent neglect of low-variation regions. The framework is plug-and-play: each GPU executes the same denoising computation as the original model over its assigned spatial region, preserving native inference semantics and ensuring compatibility with few-step samplers (DPM-Solver, UniPC) and architectural accelerations (LoRA) without retraining.

## Results

On COCO Captions 2014 with 50-step DDIM sampling, Otil achieves 1.74× speedup on 2 GPUs (SD 1.5, 512×512), 1.88× on 2 GPUs and 2.23× on 4 GPUs (SDXL, 1024×1024), and 2.26× on 4 GPUs (SD3/DiT, 1024×1024), consistently outperforming all baselines in latency. Image quality remains competitive: on SDXL 2 GPUs, Otil matches DistriFusion on LPIPS (0.014) and slightly exceeds it on CLIP Score (36.13 vs 36.10), while on SD3 4 GPUs it achieves the best speedup (2.26×) with CLIP Score 30.94. Otil reduces GPU-GPU communication by 87.5% (2 GPUs) and 93.75% (4 GPUs) relative to AsyncDiff, and by 75% relative to DistriFusion. When combined with few-step samplers (DPM-Solver, UniPC) or LoRA, speedups further increase to 2.46×–2.84× on 2 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Otil achieves the best speedup in every configuration tested, outperforming DistriFusion by 0.38× on SD 1.5 (1.74× vs 1.36×) and by 0.36–0.41× on SDXL (1.88× vs 1.50× on 2 GPUs; 2.23× vs 1.82× on 4 GPUs). Against AsyncDiff, Otil is faster on SDXL (1.88× vs 1.63× on 2 GPUs; 2.23× vs 1.81× on 4 GPUs) while also reducing communication volume by 87.5–93.75%. On the DiT-based SD3, Otil beats ParaStep (1.72× vs 1.66× on 2 GPUs; 2.26× vs 1.97× on 4 GPUs), PipeFusion (1.72× vs 1.12× on 2 GPUs; 2.26× vs 1.85× on 4 GPUs), and CompactFusion (2.26× vs 1.94× on 4 GPUs). In terms of quality, Otil is slightly behind DistriFusion on PSNR and FID for SDXL (e.g., PSNR 25.17 vs 26.60, FID 24.63 vs 24.05 on 2 GPUs) but matches or exceeds it on LPIPS and CLIP Score; against AsyncDiff, Otil substantially outperforms on all quality metrics (e.g., LPIPS 0.014 vs 0.251, CLIP 36.13 vs 32.21 on SDXL 2 GPUs). On SD3 4 GPUs, CompactFusion achieves the best LPIPS (0.259) and CLIP (31.64), but Otil still delivers the lowest latency (1.301 s) and highest speedup (2.26×).

## Relation to Prior Work

Otil builds directly on the spatial-partitioning parallelism paradigm established by DistriFusion and the stale-activation reuse strategy shared with AsyncDiff and ParaStep. Its key departure is the observation that inter-step latent changes are spatially concentrated, enabling selective sub-block transmission rather than full-activation broadcast. The dynamic polling mechanism is a novel scheduling idea that addresses a coverage gap inherent in any top-k selection strategy, and the framework's plug-and-play compatibility with few-step samplers (DPM-Solver, UniPC) and LoRA distinguishes it from pipeline-based methods like AsyncDiff that are architecturally incompatible with such accelerators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 87.5% communication reduction is practically significant for PCIe-connected GPU clusters, which remain the most common multi-GPU deployment topology outside data centers, where bandwidth is the dominant bottleneck. The plug-and-play design (no retraining, no architectural modification) lowers the adoption barrier considerably. However, the evaluation is limited to 2–4 GPUs, PCIe interconnects, and text-to-image generation at up to 1024×1024; open questions include whether the spatial-concentration property holds for video diffusion, whether the approach scales beyond 4 GPUs, and how it interacts with tensor-parallel or sequence-parallel strategies for very large DiT models.
