## Problem

Multilingual RAG and multimodal RAG have been studied in isolation, but their intersection—multilingual multimodal RAG—remains largely unexplored, despite real-world knowledge access being inherently both multilingual and multimodal. Cultural knowledge, which is long-tail and region-specific, is an ideal testbed but lacks a massive-scale benchmark that jointly evaluates retrieval and generation across languages, dialects, and modalities in a controlled, reproducible setting.

## Input / Output

The system takes a multiple-choice question in one of 42 languages (with 56 regional dialects/registers), an associated RGB image, and a multilingual Wikipedia corpus (223k articles for WORLDCUISINES, 307k for CVQA) as input, and produces a single VQA answer evaluated by macro-averaged accuracy. Retrieved passages are top-k=5 documents that may be in any language, reflecting real-world cross-lingual retrieval. The evaluation also includes adversarial prompts where retrieved context is intentionally misleading, and cross-lingual variants where prompts or oracle context are translated into target languages.

## Method

M4-RAG constructs a controlled retrieval environment from April 2025 Wikipedia snapshots, building multilingual query sets (question-only, answer-only, and culturally enriched) to retrieve top-25 articles independently in English and the target language, then parsing into section-level passages. Four evaluation configurations are compared: No-RAG baseline, Oracle Context (upper bound), Text-Based RAG using E5 dense retrieval with either oracle or VLM-generated caption queries, and Multimodal RAG using mmE5 (11B) or B3 (7B) from VLM2Vec to jointly encode question and image. Eleven VLMs across four families (Gemma3, Qwen2.5-VL, Qwen3-VL, Pangea) at multiple scales are evaluated end-to-end. The key empirical finding is a scale-retrieval mismatch: RAG consistently improves smaller models but degrades or fails to help larger ones, which exhibit stronger parametric priors and lower correction rates, while non-English retrieved context causes severe performance drops (up to −32%) even in multilingual-trained models.

## Results

On the CVQA and WORLDCUISINES benchmarks, Gemma3 27B achieves the best No-RAG baseline at 74.34% and 66.20% accuracy, respectively. Oracle context provides the upper bound across all models, while text-based RAG (E5) performs worse than the No-RAG baseline across all model sizes and datasets. Multimodal RAG with mmE5 consistently outperforms both text-based RAG and B3 (VLM2Vec), with smaller models (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B) gaining the most from retrieval, while larger models (72B, 27B) show diminished or negative RAG gains. In cross-lingual settings, performance drops reach −32.4% (Qwen2.5-VL 32B, CVQA) and −28.8% (Pangea, CVQA) for low-resource languages when oracle context is provided in the target language.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among retrieval strategies, mmE5 (11B) is the strongest RAG method, consistently outperforming B3 (7B, VLM2Vec) and E5 (text-only) across all model families and scales; E5-based text retrieval is the weakest, performing below the No-RAG baseline in most settings, indicating that naive image-to-text conversion introduces harmful noise. Among VLMs, Gemma3 27B leads on the No-RAG baseline (74.34% CVQA, 66.20% WORLDCUISINES), while Qwen2.5-VL 72B achieves the best oracle-context performance on WORLDCUISINES. Qwen3-VL (reasoning) models are more robust to RAG, maintaining retrieval gains at larger scales where non-reasoning models lose them. Pangea (7B), despite being explicitly trained on multilingual Wikipedia data, suffers among the largest cross-lingual drops (−28.8% on CVQA for low-resource languages), showing that multilingual pretraining does not confer robustness to non-English retrieved context at inference time. Overall, the paper demonstrates that no single VLM dominates all retrieval configurations: smaller models benefit most from RAG, while larger models increasingly rely on parametric knowledge and resist corrective evidence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of multilingual RAG (TyDiQA, MIRACL, MKQA, Mintaka) and multimodal RAG (MRAG-Bench, ViQuAE, Encyclopedic VQA), which prior work has treated as separate research threads. It repurposes two existing cultural VQA datasets (CVQA, WORLDCUISINES) as the evaluation backbone and constructs a controlled multilingual Wikipedia-based retrieval corpus, but the key departure is the joint evaluation of cross-lingual and cross-modal retrieval in a single end-to-end pipeline. The retrieval side draws on dense encoders (E5, mmE5, B3/VLM2Vec) without modifying them, positioning the contribution squarely in benchmarking and empirical analysis rather than new retrieval or generation architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that RAG reliably boosts smaller VLMs yet degrades larger ones exposes a fundamental scale-retrieval mismatch that challenges the assumption that more capable models should integrate external evidence more effectively. The severe multilingual performance gaps (up to −32% for low-resource languages) and the fact that Pangea—explicitly trained on multilingual data—remains among the most affected suggest that cross-lingual evidence integration is a deeper bottleneck than instruction following. Open questions include whether joint retriever-VLM post-training or test-time adaptation can close the integration gap, and whether the observed inertial priors in large models are an artifact of current retrieval quality or a structural limitation of parametric knowledge. The 80k-pair, 42-language benchmark itself provides a much-needed standardized resource for the multilingual multimodal RAG community.
