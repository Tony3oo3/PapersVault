## Problem

Existing 3D vehicle generation methods are trained on synthetic datasets, producing models with arbitrary poses and undefined scales that require manual alignment before integration into driving simulations. Moreover, they depend on camera pose annotations or 3D ground truth that are unavailable for real-world driving imagery, creating a fundamental barrier to generating simulation-ready assets at scale from real data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261885/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts one or more unposed RGB driving images of a vehicle (no camera intrinsics, extrinsics, or 3D labels required) and outputs a metric-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting vehicle model with predicted camera parameters and a harmonized appearance suitable for direct insertion into target driving scenes. The number of input views is flexible (1 to V), sampled probabilistically during training via an exponential decay schedule, and the output Gaussians are organized around N learnable structure tokens, each decoding m Gaussian primitives with position offsets, scaling, opacity, spherical harmonic color, and rotation.

## Method

The framework uses a two-stage training paradigm: first pre-training on ~1,000 posed 3DRealCar images with explicit camera and scale supervision, then fine-tuning on ~5 million unposed MAD-Cars images where a learnable camera token predicts pose parameters that drive differentiable 3DGS rendering for self-supervised photometric feedback. Visual features are extracted by DINOv2 and aggregated via Alternating Self-Attention (intra-frame for texture, inter-frame for geometry), with a shared camera token and attention pooling producing global cls and patch representations. A key insight is the progressive coordinate optimization—converting position prediction from Cartesian to spherical coordinates (radial distance first, then azimuth/elevation) to stabilize learning without 3D supervision—and a gradient filtering strategy that discards per-view gradients deviating beyond 3σ to mitigate the geometry-camera ambiguity problem during unposed fine-tuning. A lightweight harmonization module, supervised by a one-step 2D diffusion model, modulates only the spherical harmonic color via self-attention to adapt the asset's appearance to target scene lighting.

## Results

On 3DRealCar, Unposed-to-3D achieves SSIM 0.9172, PSNR 21.20 dB, LPIPS 0.0571, Chamfer Distance 0.578 (×10⁻³), and F-score 0.5341, outperforming all four baselines on every metric. On the zero-shot CFV dataset, it reaches SSIM 0.9183, PSNR 21.73 dB, LPIPS 0.0508, CD 0.744 (×10⁻³), and F-score 0.4252. Camera prediction accuracy (Table 3) yields SSIM 0.9263/0.9239 and PSNR 21.76/21.78 on 3DRealCar/CFV, while metric-scale reconstruction (Table 4) achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.016 m and F-score of 0.4886 at a 0.05 m threshold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Unposed-to-3D outperforms all four baselines on every metric across both 3DRealCar and CFV. The closest competitor is TRELLIS, which achieves F-score 0.4744 on 3DRealCar versus 0.5341 for the proposed method (+0.06), and 0.3355 on CFV versus 0.4252 (+0.09); however, TRELLIS still trails by 1.69 dB PSNR and 0.73 CD on 3DRealCar. The gap widens substantially against DGS (DreamGaussian), TGS, and LGM: on 3DRealCar, the proposed method reduces Chamfer Distance from 1.35 (DGS), 3.49 (TGS), and 7.12 (LGM) to 0.58, while improving F-score by 0.23, 0.33, and 0.33 respectively. On the zero-shot CFV dataset, LGM and TGS degrade most severely (CD 8.38 and 3.39 vs. 0.74 for Ours), indicating poor cross-dataset generalization. A critical practical advantage is that all baselines require manual pose alignment and scale normalization before evaluation, whereas Unposed-to-3D produces metrically grounded, pose-consistent assets directly without any post-processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction paradigm (building on 3DGS [17], VGGT [49], and DUSt3R [50]) to the specific problem of vehicle reconstruction from unposed driving images. It departs from prior vehicle reconstruction methods (ProtoCar [24], DreamCar [7], Car-Studio [27]) that all require camera pose annotations and/or 3D supervision, by introducing a two-stage curriculum that first pretrains on posed data and then removes pose supervision entirely, relying on self-supervised differentiable rendering with a learned camera prediction head. The Alternating Self-Attention aggregation and DINOv2 backbone are directly inherited from VGGT, while the harmonization module borrows from diffusion-based scene insertion work (R3D2 [28], [20]). The key conceptual departure is treating camera estimation and geometry reconstruction as a jointly optimized self-supervised problem rather than a two-step pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for 3D annotations and camera poses, this work opens a scalable path to generating simulation-ready vehicle assets from the vast pool of unannotated driving imagery, addressing a practical bottleneck in autonomous driving simulation. The metric-scale, pose-consistent output removes the manual alignment step that plagues existing 3D generation methods, and the downstream 3D detection validation (Table 5) provides concrete evidence of simulation utility. However, the method is validated only on vehicles, the gradient filtering strategy is heuristic and may not generalize to more ambiguous geometry-camera configurations, and the scale head is decoupled and fine-tuned separately, raising questions about robustness in the wild. Open questions include extension to other object categories, handling of heavy occlusion, and whether the self-supervised camera prediction remains stable under extreme domain shifts.
