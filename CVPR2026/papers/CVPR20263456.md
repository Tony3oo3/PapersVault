## Problem

State-of-the-art multimodal 3D human pose estimation pipelines rely on Transformers whose quadratic attention complexity makes real-time inference on long sequences impractical, while Mamba's linear-complexity state-space modeling lacks the spatial reasoning capacity needed for precise cross-modal fusion between visual and inertial signals. No prior work has combined Mamba's efficient temporal processing with an explicit cross-attention mechanism for visual-inertial 3D pose estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263456/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes J=17 normalized 2D keypoint coordinates (from MediaPipe, SimpleNet, or ground-truth detectors) and unit-quaternion readings from I=6 wearable IMUs (pelvis, sternum, limbs), with IMUs partitioned into G=5 anatomical groups (torso, left/right arm, left/right leg). It outputs root-relative 3D joint positions P ∈ R^{T×J×3} for the full sequence. A distinguishing property is native variable-length support: during training, sequence lengths are randomly sampled from {9, 18, …, 81} frames, eliminating the padding and masking overhead required by fixed-window Transformer baselines.

## Method

VIMCAN is a three-stage hybrid: (1) Spatio-Temporal Mamba (STMamba) feature extractors apply bidirectional spatio-temporal selective scans (BiSTSSM) in two passes—spatial first (skeleton-aware parent-child ordering for visual keypoints, part-aware SS2D four-directional scanning for each IMU group), then temporal—to encode intra-frame topology and inter-frame dynamics at O(L) cost; (2) a Multi-Head Cross-Attention fusion module uses visual features as queries and inertial features as keys/values within each body-part group, with a residual connection preserving skeletal structure, followed by a part-aware STMamba and dimension reduction to a global fused representation; (3) a stack of L_N=5 skeleton-aware STMamba layers performs global spatio-temporal modeling before a linear regression head. The key design insight is that Mamba's selective state-space dynamics efficiently compress temporal context while Cross-Attention explicitly models the fine-grained cross-modal spatial dependencies that pure Mamba cannot capture, as validated by the 7.1 mm P1 gap between the Cross-Attention and Cross-Mamba fusion variants. Training uses a composite loss (MPJPE + scale-normalized MPJPE + velocity + temporal-consistency terms) with variable-length sampling, and the model achieves 17.2 mm MPJPE on TotalCapture and 45.3 mm on 3DPW at >60 FPS on consumer hardware.

## Results

On TotalCapture with ground-truth 2D keypoints and 6 IMUs, VIMCAN achieves a mean per-joint position error (MPJPE/P1) of 17.2 mm and Procrustes-aligned MPJPE (P2) of 13.8 mm, outperforming the prior best GCN-Transformer baseline (Wang et al.) by 11.4 mm on P1. With SimpleNet 2D detection and 6 IMUs, VIMCAN reaches 31.2 mm P1 and 23.6 mm P2; with 8 IMUs, it further improves to 28.9 mm P1 and 21.3 mm P2. On the 3DPW dataset (MediaPipe 2D input), VIMCAN achieves 45.3 mm P1, surpassing all prior visual-inertial methods. The model also delivers real-time inference at 61.4 FPS with only 282.5 MB peak memory on consumer hardware (RTX 4060 Laptop).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIMCAN outperforms all five baselines across both datasets. On TotalCapture with 6 IMUs and MediaPipe, VIMCAN (33.2 mm P1) beats Pan's RNN-based method [22] (48.1 mm) by 14.9 mm and Wang's GCN-Transformer [29] (39.0 mm) by 5.8 mm. With SimpleNet and 8 IMUs, VIMCAN (28.9 mm) surpasses Bao's TCN-based method [2] (33.7 mm) by 4.8 mm and Wang's [29] (33.4 mm) by 4.5 mm, while also showing stronger generalization on unseen-subject Freestyle and Acting subsets. Under the upper-bound setting with ground-truth 2D keypoints, VIMCAN (17.2 mm) improves over Wang's [29] (28.6 mm) by a substantial 11.4 mm on P1 and 3.8 mm on P2. On 3DPW, VIMCAN (45.3 mm) outperforms Liu's CNN-Transformer [17] (60.3 mm) by 15.0 mm, Pan's [22] (55.0 mm) by 9.7 mm, and Wang's [29] (53.9 mm) by 8.6 mm, confirming consistent gains across datasets and 2D detector quality levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIMCAN sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: (1) Mamba-based state-space models for efficient sequence processing (Mamba, VMamba's SS2D scanning, PoseMamba's skeleton-aware scanning), and (2) Transformer-based multimodal fusion for visual-inertial 3D HPE (Wang's GCN-Transformer, Liu's CNN-Transformer). It departs from the Transformer paradigm by replacing quadratic-complexity self-attention in the temporal/spatial feature extraction stages with linear-complexity bidirectional state-space models, while retaining Cross-Attention specifically for the cross-modal fusion step where spatial reasoning is critical. The hybrid Mamba-Attention design is conceptually inspired by MambaVision but is the first application of this pattern to vision-inertial 3D pose estimation, and it introduces kinematic-prior-driven scanning (skeleton-aware reordering of joints) borrowed from PoseMamba.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that Mamba's linear complexity can replace Transformer attention in the computationally heavy feature-extraction stages of visual-inertial HPE without sacrificing accuracy, enabling real-time (>60 FPS) inference on consumer GPUs with a fraction of the memory footprint of prior GCN-Transformer baselines. The variable-length sequence support (no padding/masking) is a practical advantage for streaming deployment in motion capture and rehabilitation. However, the reliance on strict sensor calibration (T-pose alignment, extrinsic camera parameters) limits robustness in uncontrolled settings, and the ablation confirming that Cross-Attention still outperforms a pure Mamba fusion (17.2 vs 24.3 mm) suggests that Mamba's spatial-reasoning gap remains an open problem for multimodal tasks requiring fine-grained cross-modal alignment.
