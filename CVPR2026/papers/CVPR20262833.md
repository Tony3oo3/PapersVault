## Problem

Existing Transformer-based AU detectors rely on deterministic attention and lack explicit mechanisms to distinguish stable inter-AU dependencies from spurious correlations induced by confounders such as subject identity, lighting, and expression habits. No prior work has incorporated uncertainty estimation into Transformer-based AU detection, leaving models vulnerable to overfitting under the class imbalance, label noise, and limited annotation that characterize real-world AU datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262833/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB facial image, aligned and cropped to 224×224 via MTCNN, and produces binary activation labels for each of N AUs (12 on BP4D, 8 on DISFA) as a multi-label classification output. Internally, the backbone yields a global feature map that is decomposed into an AU-specific feature matrix V ∈ R^{N×C}; final predictions are obtained by averaging S Monte Carlo samples of the probabilistic Transformer, and the Causal Deconfounding module replaces a standard linear head with a cosine-similarity classifier against learnable AU prototypes.

## Method

UDCT integrates three components: (1) an AU-specific Feature Generation module with N parallel FC+GAP branches producing per-AU feature vectors; (2) an Uncertainty-Aware Transformer that parameterizes each attention score as a Gaussian N(μ_ij, σ_ij²) estimated by a lightweight predictor, uses the reparameterization trick for differentiable sampling, and aggregates S Monte Carlo attention samples to yield a predictive distribution; (3) an AU-specific Causal Deconfounding module that applies back-door adjustment (via NWGM reformulation) to shift the prediction from P(Y|X) to P(Y|do(X)), implemented as a linear model over AU features and a weighted aggregation of sample-level confounder prototypes, followed by a cosine-similarity classifier. Training uses a weighted asymmetric loss whose per-sample, per-AU weights are derived from mutual-information-based epistemic uncertainty, automatically down-weighting noisy or ambiguous samples to counteract class imbalance and label noise.

## Results

On DISFA, UDCT achieves the best average F1 score of 67.36%, surpassing the previous best methods AC²D (65.4%) and SACL (65.5%) by approximately 2%. On BP4D, UDCT achieves 62.59% average F1, remaining competitive with top methods like SACL (65.6%) and AC²D (64.6%) while showing gains on specific AUs (e.g., +1.47% on AU6 over AC²D). In cross-domain evaluation (BP4D→DISFA), UDCT attains 44.5% average F1, ranking second only to FG-Net (54.4%), which benefits from StyleGAN2 pretraining on FFHQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DISFA, UDCT (67.36% avg F1) outperforms all 12 baselines, with the largest margins over AC²D (+1.96%) and SACL (+1.86%), both of which incorporate causal or correlation-based strategies. On BP4D, UDCT (62.59%) trails the top methods SACL (65.6%) and AC²D (64.6%) by roughly 2–3%, though it still surpasses deterministic Transformer baselines like Jacob et al. (64.2%) and UGN-B (63.3%). Against uncertainty-based methods, UDCT outperforms UGN-B [36] on both datasets (67.36% vs. 60.0% on DISFA; 62.59% vs. 63.3% on BP4D), demonstrating that modeling uncertainty directly within Transformer attention is more effective than within a graph structure. In cross-domain transfer (BP4D→DISFA), UDCT (44.5%) ranks second to FG-Net (54.4%), which leverages StyleGAN2 pretraining on FFHQ for stronger generalization; UDCT still surpasses AUFormer (43.6%), IdenNet (39.7%), and Patch-MCD (37.4%). The ablation confirms that the causal deconfounding module contributes +2.95% and the uncertainty-aware attention contributes +1.44% over a deterministic Transformer baseline on DISFA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: Transformer-based AU detection (Jacob et al. [7], AUFormer [49]), causal deconfounding in facial analysis (CISNet [2], AC2D [35]), and uncertainty quantification in vision (Depeweg et al. [5], Kendall & Gal [10]). It departs from prior Transformer AU detectors by replacing deterministic softmax attention with Gaussian-distributed attention scores via the reparameterization trick, and from prior causal AU methods by embedding the back-door adjustment and NWGM-based deconfounding (borrowed from Wang et al. [43]) directly within the Transformer pipeline rather than as a post-hoc correction. The uncertainty-aware loss reweighting (using mutual information to estimate epistemic uncertainty) and the weighted asymmetric loss [22] are combined into a single training objective, a formulation not previously seen in AU detection literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap—no prior work had integrated probabilistic attention modeling into Transformer-based AU detection—and the ablation confirms each component (UAT, causal deconfounding) contributes meaningfully. However, the practical gains are modest: ~2% F1 improvement on DISFA, slightly below SOTA on BP4D, and second-best in cross-domain transfer behind a method leveraging StyleGAN2 pretraining. Open questions include whether the Monte Carlo sampling overhead (S forward passes per sample) scales to real-time deployment, whether the causal diagram assumptions (single confounder path C→X) hold for more complex multi-confounder scenarios, and whether the framework extends to dynamic (video) AU detection as the authors note for future work.
