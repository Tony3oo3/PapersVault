## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) underperform human experts on Industry Anomaly Detection (IAD) due to two gaps: insufficient integration of anomaly-detection domain knowledge during pre-training, and an inability to generate technically precise, context-aware language for anomaly reasoning. Existing fine-tuning approaches risk catastrophic forgetting and overfitting to narrow answer templates, while object-level prompts in zero-shot methods fail to capture type-level defect patterns (e.g., distinguishing 'scratch' from 'hole').
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263625/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a query image (RGB, grayscale, or X-ray) and a text instruction as input, and produces a multi-faceted output: an image-level anomaly detection score, pixel-level defect region localization, a defect type classification label, and free-form anomaly description and reasoning text. The query image is encoded by CLIP ViT-L/14@336px for feature extraction, while the VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL) handles instruction processing and complex reasoning. A key design choice is that the retrieved knowledge document from SEEK-M&V replaces the standard 1-shot normal-sample template used by other MLLMs, injecting domain-specific visual and textual context at inference time.

## Method

ADSeeker is a plug-and-play, training-efficient framework built on Qwen2.5-VL with a CLIP encoder, combining three modules: (1) Q2K RAG performs multimodal retrieval over the SEEK-M&V visual document knowledge base using a KDE-Sample strategy that applies Gaussian Mixture Modeling to sorted similarity scores, enabling non-uniform sampling across clusters to avoid redundant retrieval of highly similar defect types; (2) the AD Expert module computes anomaly localization via the ratio of cosine similarities between patch embeddings and positive/negative type-level textual embeddings, fusing this with visual embeddings into anomaly prior tokens fed to the VLM; (3) the Hierarchical Sparse Prompt (HSP) mechanism iteratively applies sparse optimization (combining Iterative Soft Thresholding with learnable prompt embeddings) to extract type-level defect features from query images, reducing hallucination from suspected anomaly regions. The framework requires no large-scale pre-training, adding only ≤27% memory overhead and ≤2s inference latency over the base model, and is evaluated in a zero-shot setting where it is fine-tuned on one dataset (MVTec AD) and tested on others.

## Results

On the primary ZSAD benchmark (Table 1), ADSeeker achieves an average image-level AUROC of 94.0% across 7 industrial and medical datasets, outperforming the strongest baseline Anomaly-OV (91.8%) by 2.2 points. The largest gains appear on BTAD (94.0 vs. 89.0 for Anomaly-OV) and MPDD (85.9 vs. 81.7), where type-level feature extraction via the Hierarchical Sparse Prompt mechanism is most impactful. On the MMAD anomaly reasoning benchmark (Table 2), ADSeeker (Qwen2.5-VL 7B under Seek-Setting) reaches 69.90% average accuracy, surpassing the best open-source MLLM baseline (LLaVA-OV 7B at 63.19%) by 6.7 points, with the largest improvement on defect classification (+3.96) and anomaly discrimination (+13.27). Against training-based SOTA methods (Table 3), ADSeeker's plug-and-play framework (LLaVA-OV*) achieves 73.54% average accuracy, exceeding Anomaly-R1 (71.93%) without any additional fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADSeeker outperforms all five ZSAD baselines in Table 1, with the most significant margins over CLIP (+31.4 avg) and WinCLIP (+13.2 avg), and a consistent 2.2-point lead over the closest competitor Anomaly-OV. The gap is largest on BTAD (+5.0 over Anomaly-OV) and MPDD (+4.2), suggesting the HSP mechanism's type-level features are particularly effective on complex multi-scale industrial defects. On the MMAD reasoning benchmark (Table 2), ADSeeker (Qwen2.5-VL 7B Seek-Setting) beats LLaVA-OV 7B by 6.7 points, LLaVA-NeXT 7B by 10.6 points, and InternVL2 8B by 6.8 points in average accuracy, with the Q2K RAG module contributing most to defect classification and localization subtasks. In the training-methods comparison (Table 3), ADSeeker's plug-and-play framework (LLaVA-OV* at 73.54%) surpasses both Anomaly-R1 (71.93%) and VLM-R1* (72.26%) without any fine-tuning, though Anomaly-R1 edges out ADSeeker on LOCO (70.55 vs. 70.31) and VLM-R1* leads on VisA (68.11 vs. 67.55). The ablation (Table 4) confirms that combining Q2K RAG and the AD Expert module yields the largest joint gain (+6.2 over baseline), while LoRA fine-tuning beyond 5 epochs causes catastrophic forgetting (dropping to 40.9% at 20 epochs).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) MLLM-based industrial anomaly detection (AnomalyGPT, Anomaly-OV, Myriad), which injects domain knowledge via fine-tuning, and (2) CLIP-based zero-shot anomaly detection (WinCLIP, AnomalyCLIP, AdaCLIP), which relies on object-level prompt learning. ADSeeker departs from both by avoiding fine-tuning altogether—instead using a retrieval-augmented generation pipeline (Q2K RAG) over a purpose-built visual document knowledge base (SEEK-M&V) combined with a Hierarchical Sparse Prompt mechanism that operates at the defect-type level rather than the object level. The KDE-Sample retrieval strategy, inspired by VisRAG, is adapted to handle the uneven similarity distributions inherent in category-organized industrial knowledge bases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design (no large-scale pre-training, ≤27% memory overhead) makes ADSeeker practically deployable in industrial settings where retraining MLLMs is infeasible, and the MulA dataset (11,226 images, 72 defect types) and SEEK-M&V knowledge base address the chronic data scarcity in IAD. However, the evaluation of the full framework is constrained: the Q2K RAG comparison in Table 3 uses only a subset of MMAD due to SEEK-M&V content limitations, and the HSP mechanism is validated primarily on a single CLIP backbone. Open questions include whether the KDE-Sample strategy generalizes to domains with non-Gaussian similarity distributions, whether type-level features transfer to novel defect categories unseen in MulA, and how the framework scales as the knowledge base grows beyond 26 categories.
