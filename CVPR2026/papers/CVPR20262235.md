## Problem

Monocular 3D object detection suffers from two intertwined failures: deterministic single-box predictors collapse to implausible mean estimates because the 2D-to-3D mapping is inherently multimodal, and absolute confidence scores regress to values that are highly sensitive to small depth or orientation errors, causing them to misalign with true localization quality. Existing methods address neither the multimodality of 3D geometry nor the instability of confidence regression in a principled way.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces, for each detected object, a 3D bounding box (2D center, depth, 3D size, yaw), a class label, and a confidence score. Internally, each RoI generates K=3 candidate 3D boxes; at inference the top-ranked candidate is selected as the final output, and detections with 2D scores below 0.2 are discarded without NMS. The model is trained on KITTI (3,712 images) and evaluated on KITTI test and nuScenes frontal validation splits.

## Method

RARE integrates two mechanisms within a deformable DETR-style architecture (DLA34 backbone, 3 encoder + 3 decoder layers, 256-dim hidden states). First, learning-to-rank replaces absolute confidence regression with a joint point-wise MSE loss (aligning predicted confidence to ground-truth 3D IoU) and a pair-wise logistic loss (enforcing correct relative ordering among detection pairs), yielding confidence scores that are robust to localization noise. Second, learning-to-retrieve constructs K content-dependent queries per RoI via an MLP, each predicting a distinct 3D box hypothesis; a multi-hypothesis loss softly aggregates box regression errors weighted by softmax selection probabilities and adds a uniformity regularization to prevent collapse onto a single mode. At inference, the highest-confidence hypothesis from each query set is retrieved as the final detection, explicitly modeling the multimodal 3D geometry.

## Results

On the KITTI test set (Car category), RARE achieves AP₃D|₄₀ of 28.83/19.57/17.38 and AP_BEV|₄₀ of 38.46/26.37/23.46 on Easy/Moderate/Hard, setting new state-of-the-art among all monocular methods. This represents relative gains of 9.4%/4.5%/8.8% in AP₃D over the previous best monocular method MonoDGP, while also outperforming the LiDAR-augmented MonoTAKD on AP₃D by 3.3%/0.7%/5.3%. On the Cyclist category, RARE achieves particularly large improvements (52.2%/39.3%/39.7% relative over the second-best monocular method). In cross-dataset depth evaluation, RARE achieves the lowest depth MAE (1.05 m overall) on nuScenes frontal validation, surpassing methods that explicitly use multiple depth cues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RARE outperforms all 22 compared baselines on the KITTI Car category (Table 1), with the largest margins over the previous best monocular method MonoDGP [43] (26.35/18.72/15.97 AP₃D) by +2.48/+0.85/+1.41 absolute points. It also surpasses the strongest LiDAR-augmented method MonoTAKD† [30] (27.91/19.43/16.51) on AP₃D while achieving comparable AP_BEV, demonstrating that the ranking-and-retrieval framework compensates for the absence of LiDAR supervision. On Pedestrian (Table 2), RARE leads all monocular methods on Moderate and Hard (10.79/9.04 vs. MonoUNI's 10.34/8.74), though MonoUNI retains a slight edge on Easy (15.78 vs. 14.85). The gains are most dramatic on Cyclist, where RARE (11.17/5.96/5.28) nearly doubles the performance of the best non-LiDAR monocular method MonoUNI (7.34/4.28/3.78). In cross-dataset depth evaluation (Table 3), RARE achieves the lowest overall depth MAE on both KITTI val (0.69 m) and nuScenes frontal val (1.05 m), outperforming MonoCD [61] which explicitly leverages complementary depth cues. In terms of efficiency (Table 7), RARE has the fewest parameters (32.9M) among compared DETR-based methods while achieving the highest Moderate AP₃D, with only a modest 6.4 ms runtime increase over MonoDETR.

## Relation to Prior Work

RARE builds on the Deformable DETR architecture for RoI-based query generation and the MonoDETR framework as its monocular 3D detection backbone, but departs from both in two fundamental ways. For confidence estimation, it replaces the standard absolute-score regression (depth uncertainty or IoU-based) with a joint point-wise and pair-wise ranking loss, drawing conceptually from 2D ranking losses (RankDetNet, Rank & Sort) but without the positive/negative partitioning that those methods require. For 3D localization, it introduces a multi-hypothesis query set per object with a retrieval-aware loss and diversity regularization, adapting ideas from mixture density networks and camera-multiplex to the monocular 3D detection setting where prior methods output a single deterministic box per detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RARE addresses two persistent weaknesses in monocular 3D detection—confidence scores that poorly reflect true localization quality and single-point predictions that collapse to implausible means—within a single end-to-end framework, achieving state-of-the-art results on KITTI without LiDAR supervision. The cross-dataset depth MAE results, where RARE outperforms MonoCD (which explicitly models multiple depth cues), suggest that learned multi-hypothesis retrieval may be a more effective strategy for handling depth ambiguity than dedicated depth components. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger hypothesis sets (K=3 is modest), whether it generalizes beyond the KITTI/nuScenes car-centric setting to more cluttered or aerial scenes, and whether the ranking formulation transfers to other 3D perception tasks like tracking or mapping.
