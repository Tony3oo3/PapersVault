## Problem

Test-time prompt tuning (TPT) for vision-language models relies on entropy minimization, which induces systematically overconfident predictions. While O-TPT addresses this by enforcing full orthogonality between text class prototypes, the paper shows that this quadratic repulsion aggressively pushes semantically related classes (e.g., 'annual crop land' and 'permanent crop land') apart, inflating confidence and degrading calibration precisely where semantic overlap is meaningful.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single unlabeled test image (encoded by a frozen ViT-L/14 or ViT-B/16 vision encoder) and a set of K text prompt templates (e.g., 'a photo of a [CLASS]') that are optimized at inference time. No labeled data is required; the prompts are updated via a single AdamW gradient step over 64 augmented views of the test image. The output is a class label with a calibrated softmax confidence score, where calibration is measured by ECE and selective classification accuracy.

## Method

SoC replaces O-TPT's full orthogonality constraint (‖S − I‖²) with a Huber-based regularizer applied to pairwise cosine similarities between text prototypes: quadratic for similarities below a margin δ, but linear (capped) above it, so that highly similar pairs experience bounded repulsion rather than unbounded quadratic penalty. The full objective is L_SoC = L_TPT + λ · (2/K(K−1)) Σ L_Huber(s_ij, δ), where L_TPT is the standard entropy-minimization loss. Theoretically, the paper derives a confidence floor bound (Proposition 1) showing that worst-case prototype similarity μ directly controls the minimum softmax confidence, and a first-order gradient analysis proving that O-TPT reduces μ by a factor of (1−4η) per step while the Huber regularizer caps the reduction at 2ηδ, yielding strictly lower confidence inflation. This mechanism preserves semantic proximity among related classes while still promoting inter-class separation, resulting in better-calibrated predictions across 15 benchmarks.

## Results

Across 11 fine-grained classification datasets with a ViT-L/14 backbone, SoC achieves an average accuracy of 72.3% and average ECE of 5.4, outperforming the state-of-the-art O-TPT (71.4% acc., 7.7 ECE) by +0.9 accuracy and −2.3 ECE. Notably, SoC's ECE (5.4) is nearly on par with zero-shot CLIP (5.1) while improving accuracy by +1.2 over zero-shot, demonstrating that calibration is preserved during adaptation. The largest ECE gains appear on semantically dense datasets: EuroSAT drops from 17.7 (O-TPT) to 3.2, and FGVC-Aircraft from 16.8 to 12.7. On the four ImageNet distribution-shift variants, SoC reduces average ECE by 1.5 over O-TPT (8.4 vs 9.9) while matching its accuracy (71.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SoC outperforms O-TPT (CVPR 2025), the primary state-of-the-art competitor, on both accuracy (+0.9 avg.) and ECE (−2.3 avg.) across 11 datasets, winning on ECE in 10 out of 11 datasets. Compared to C-TPT (ICLR 2024), SoC improves average ECE by 4.6 points (5.4 vs 10.0) while matching accuracy, and against vanilla TPT (NeurIPS 2022) it reduces ECE by 9.5 points (5.4 vs 14.9). Against zero-shot CLIP, SoC trades a small ECE increase (+0.3) for a meaningful accuracy gain (+1.2), confirming that adaptation is possible without the severe miscalibration seen in prior TPT variants. On the ImageNet distribution-shift benchmarks, SoC matches O-TPT's accuracy (71.3%) while reducing ECE by 1.5, and substantially outperforms TPT (−5.8 ECE) and C-TPT (−4.2 ECE). SoC is also complementary to the post-hoc SaLS calibration: when SaLS is applied, SoC remains the best-calibrated method, and the marginal improvement from SaLS is smallest for SoC, indicating its predictions are already well-calibrated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time prompt tuning (TPT) framework of Shu et al. (NeurIPS 2022) by replacing the entropy-only objective with a calibration-aware regularizer. It directly departs from O-TPT (CVPR 2025), which enforces full pairwise orthogonality between class prototypes, and C-TPT (ICLR 2024), which uses centroid-based dispersion. The key conceptual shift is showing that the quadratic repulsion in O-TPT is counterproductive for semantically similar classes: by capping the gradient via a Huber loss, SoC preserves the geometric proximity that reflects genuine semantic overlap while still promoting separation for dissimilar pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VLMs are increasingly deployed in safety-critical domains (healthcare, autonomous driving), reliable uncertainty estimates are as important as accuracy, yet TPT's entropy minimization systematically inflates confidence. The paper's theoretical bound (Proposition 1) and first-order analysis (Corollary 1) provide a clear geometric mechanism explaining why full orthogonality degrades calibration for high-similarity class pairs, offering a principled design criterion for future regularizers. Open questions include whether the fixed margin δ can be learned adaptively per class pair, whether the approach extends to multi-step or iterative TPT without degradation, and whether similar Huber-style capping applies to other VLM architectures beyond CLIP.
