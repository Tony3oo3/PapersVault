## Problem

Existing underwater instance segmentation methods are restricted to close-vocabulary prediction, and open-vocabulary (OV) segmentation has never been systematically explored for underwater scenes. Two compounding challenges make direct transfer of OV models to underwater imagery particularly difficult: severe visual degradation (color attenuation, scattering, occlusion) destabilizes appearance-based cues, and VLMs trained on terrestrial data lack fine-grained marine semantic priors, causing category ambiguity. Additionally, no large-scale fine-grained benchmark exists for evaluating OV underwater instance segmentation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single underwater RGB image and a set of free-form textual category descriptions (which may include categories unseen during training) as input. A frozen depth encoder (Depth Anything) is applied to the image to derive multi-scale geometric features, so depth is an internal byproduct rather than a required external input. The output is a set of pixel-accurate instance masks paired with class labels drawn from the provided text vocabulary, supervised by binary cross-entropy (classification) and Dice+BCE (mask) losses following the MaskFormer formulation.

## Method

The framework combines two complementary modules around a frozen CLIP visual encoder and ConvNeXt backbone. The Geometric Prior Enhancement Module (GPEM) extracts multi-scale depth features from a frozen Depth Anything encoder, adaptively fuses them with multi-scale CLIP visual features via a sigmoid-gated element-wise multiplication at each scale, and then refines the fused representation through a lightweight Q-Former to produce geometry-informed queries. The Semantic Alignment Injection Mechanism (SAIM) augments the CLIP text encoder with five underwater-specific prompt templates (environmental context, water medium, illumination, depth cues, scene interactions), adaptively selects the top-N most relevant templates per category based on visual-text similarity, and fuses the global depth token with aggregated mask features to sharpen category discrimination. Both modules operate on frozen pre-trained encoders, keeping trainable parameters around 22–23M, and are trained end-to-end with standard MaskFormer-style losses.

## Results

On the MARIS benchmark (in-domain, ConvNeXt-B backbone), the proposed method achieves 52.68 mAP on intersection classes, 39.77 mAP on open-vocabulary classes, and 44.37 mAP overall, surpassing the strongest baseline (MAFT+) by +4.53, +4.05, and +4.29 mAP respectively. With the larger ConvNeXt-L backbone, the method reaches 61.55 mAP (intersection) and 54.02 mAP (OV), a +6.23 mAP gain over the second-best. In the cross-domain setting (trained on COCO, evaluated on MARIS), the method achieves 32.62 mAP (ConvNeXt-B) and 46.18 mAP (ConvNeXt-L), outperforming the best baseline by +2.57 and +5.91 mAP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines across both in-domain and cross-domain settings. Against the strongest ConvNeXt-B baselines (MAFT+ and FCCLIP), the method gains +4.29 mAP overall and +4.05 mAP on OV classes in-domain, with the gap widening to +3.30 mAP overall under ConvNeXt-L. The ViT-B baselines (OVSeg, ODISE, SAN) trail significantly, with OVSeg achieving only 30.95 mAP overall versus 44.37 for the proposed method on ConvNeXt-B. EOVSeg (AAAI'25) performs notably worse than other baselines in both settings (31.22 mAP in-domain, 18.90 mAP cross-domain), suggesting its domain-specific cues fail to transfer. In the cross-domain setting, the method's advantage is most pronounced with ConvNeXt-L, where it surpasses MAFT+ by +5.91 mAP (46.18 vs. 40.27), indicating that geometric and semantic priors are particularly valuable when the training distribution (COCO) differs substantially from the evaluation domain (underwater).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the open-vocabulary instance segmentation (OVIS) paradigm—established on natural images by methods like OVSeg, FCCLIP, and MAFT+—to the underwater domain, which the authors identify as entirely unexplored. Methodologically, it repurposes components from vision-language models (Q-Former from BLIP-2, CLIP encoders) and monocular depth estimation (Depth Anything) into a unified framework, combining them with multi-scale deformable attention and MaskFormer-style query decoding. The key conceptual departure is the explicit treatment of two underwater-specific failure modes—visual degradation (color attenuation, scattering) and semantic misalignment (VLMs trained on terrestrial data lack marine taxonomic knowledge)—as distinct problems requiring complementary solutions (GPEM and SAIM), rather than a single generic domain adaptation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MARIS provides the first large-scale fine-grained benchmark (158 categories, 16K+ images) for underwater OV instance segmentation, filling a critical gap where prior datasets offered fewer than 20 coarse categories. The cross-domain evaluation protocol (COCO → MARIS) offers a rigorous stress test that will be useful for future underwater perception research. However, the authors acknowledge that extreme environments and rare species remain underrepresented, and the method's reliance on a frozen depth encoder introduces an additional inference dependency. Open questions include whether the geometric prior strategy generalizes to other degraded imaging domains (e.g., aerial, medical) and whether the prompt-based semantic alignment can be replaced by more scalable domain-adaptive pretraining.
