## Problem

Autoregressive point cloud generation has consistently lagged behind diffusion-based methods in quality because existing AR approaches impose an artificial 1D ordering on inherently unordered point sets, collapsing global shape generation into brittle local predictions that violate permutation invariance. This paper asks whether permutation-invariant autoregressive modeling is possible for 3D point clouds, and if so, whether it can match or exceed diffusion-based generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262065/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a multi-scale token sequence Q = ([start], q_1, …, q_{K-1}), where each q_k is a set of VQ-VAE tokens at resolution s_k (e.g., 128→256→512→1024→2048 points), constructed via Farthest Point Sampling to preserve permutation invariance. At each autoregressive step the model predicts the full set of tokens for the next scale (not a single point), and the final output is a complete 3D point cloud of N points (2048 or 8192) reconstructed by summing upsampled feature contributions across all scales and passing through an MLP decoder.

## Method

PointNSP is a two-stage coarse-to-fine framework. Stage 1 trains a multi-scale VQVAE that extracts residual latent features at K FPS-downsampled scales and quantizes each into a shared codebook, producing a hierarchical token sequence where each scale captures complementary geometric detail. Stage 2 trains a causal transformer over the token sequence using a block-diagonal mask that enforces bidirectional attention within each scale (treating tokens as a complete 3D shape) while maintaining strict causal ordering across scales. A key design choice is position-aware soft masking: the model decodes an intermediate 3D structure from ground-truth tokens up to the current scale, derives absolute positional encodings from those coordinates, and uses them to construct a symmetric soft mask that injects geometric proximity information without breaking permutation equivariance. Reconstruction uses PU-Net-style duplication-and-reshape upsampling, and the overall objective combines cross-entropy token prediction with Chamfer Distance, Earth Mover's Distance, and codebook commitment losses.

## Results

On the standard 2048-point ShapeNet random split, PointNSP-m achieves the lowest Mean CD (59.65) and Mean EMD (56.13), outperforming the strongest diffusion baseline TIGER (60.46 CD, 57.08 EMD) and the best prior autoregressive method PointGPT (63.44 CD, 62.24 EMD). On the more challenging LION split, PointNSP-m further improves to 58.04 Mean CD and 52.30 Mean EMD, surpassing TIGER (58.55, 52.74) and LION (58.17, 54.90). In the dense 8192-point setting, PointNSP-m extends its lead with 52.12/53.26 CD/EMD on Car, beating TIGER (53.78/56.34). PointNSP-m also requires only 32M parameters and 190 GPU-hours of training versus 55M/320 for TIGER and 60M/610 for LION, while sampling in 5.48 s versus 42.1 s and 59.5 s respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointNSP-m outperforms all ten baselines on both the random and LION splits of the 2048-point ShapeNet benchmark. Among diffusion-based methods, TIGER is the closest competitor (58.55 CD / 52.74 EMD on LION split), but PointNSP-m still leads by 0.51 CD and 0.44 EMD; LION trails more noticeably on EMD (54.90 vs. 52.30). Among autoregressive baselines, PointGPT is the strongest prior method (63.44 CD / 62.24 EMD on random split), and PointNSP-m improves CD by 3.79 and EMD by 6.11 over it. The older AR methods PointGrow (70.96 CD) and CanonicalVAE (68.72 CD) are substantially outperformed. On the dense 8192-point setting (Table 2), PointNSP-m extends its margins, e.g., 52.12 CD on Car vs. TIGER's 53.78. On downstream tasks (Table 5), PointNSP-m achieves the best completion CD/EMD across all three categories (e.g., 13.84/20.68 on Car vs. DPF-Net's 13.96/23.18) and the best upsampling scores (e.g., 55.85/46.74 on Car vs. LION's 57.14/47.56), confirming its versatility beyond pure generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointNSP directly adapts the next-scale prediction paradigm from VAR (Tian et al., 2024) to 3D point clouds, replacing the next-point prediction used by prior autoregressive methods (PointGrow, PointGPT, CanonicalVAE) that flatten unordered point sets into 1D sequences under fixed orderings. The multi-scale VQVAE tokenizer with residual feature extraction and PU-Net-style upsampling draws on point cloud upsampling literature, while the position-aware soft masking and intermediate-structure decoding are novel architectural contributions specific to the unordered nature of point sets. The method also inherits the block-wise causal masking strategy from VAR, enabling full bidirectional attention within each scale while maintaining causal dependencies across scales.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first autoregressive method to match or exceed strong diffusion-based baselines in point cloud generation quality, challenging the long-standing assumption that iterative denoising is necessary for high-fidelity 3D synthesis. The substantial efficiency gains (22–32M parameters vs. 45–60M, ~10× faster sampling) make dense point cloud generation (8192 points) practically feasible, which was previously prohibitive. However, evaluation is confined to ShapeNet with limited category coverage, and the quality margins over the best diffusion methods (TIGER, LION) are modest (~0.1–0.5 CD), leaving open whether the advantage persists on larger, more diverse 3D datasets or for conditional generation. The authors note future work toward foundation-scale models on large 3D datasets as a natural next step.
