## Problem

Video-LLMs produce self-contradictory temporal grounding responses when questions are rephrased or shifted, yet the internal mechanisms causing this inconsistency remained unexplored. This paper asks: which internal factors drive temporal logic inconsistency, and can they be directly targeted to improve consistency?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261655/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard Video-LLM inputs (video frames at 224×224 resolution—16 frames for Qwen2.5-VL, 96 for TimeChat, 8 for Video-LLaMA—plus a text query) and produces the same outputs as the base model: temporal grounding timestamps and verification answers. TCAS does not alter the input/output interface; instead, it adds a contrastive loss computed over the model's internal attention distributions during fine-tuning, requiring no ground-truth temporal labels for the loss itself. The method is architecture-agnostic, introducing no additional modules beyond the existing transformer.

## Method

The paper first identifies a small set of mid-layer cross-modal attention heads (via a Cross-Modal Score measuring average text-to-visual-token attention) and shows through correlation and causal intervention that their inability to discriminate video tokens across timestamps is the primary cause of temporal inconsistency. To fix this, TCAS (Temporally Conditioned Attention Sharpening) selects the top-t cross-modal heads and, for text query tokens whose attention to aggregated temporal bins exceeds a threshold, computes a margin-based contrastive loss: it pushes attention scores above the per-token temporal mean (positive set) away from scores below the mean (negative set) by at least a margin m. This forces heads to make explicit relevance judgments across timestamps without relying on ground-truth temporal annotations. The TCAS loss is combined with standard next-token prediction via a balancing weight w_ae, and the entire procedure is applied as an auxiliary objective during supervised fine-tuning on existing VTG datasets (TimeIT or VTune).

## Results

On Charades-CON, TimeChat fine-tuned with VTune + TCAS achieves 83.3 IoU on Grounding, 75.0 IoU on Rephrase-Grounding, 39.5 IoU on Shift-Grounding, 52.9% on Hard-Verify, and 50.8% on Common-Verify, outperforming the SFT baseline (76.2 / 69.2 / 36.2 / 44.8% / 42.4%) across all five metrics. On the standard VTG benchmarks (Table 4), TimeChat+TCAS reaches 60.2 R@1,0.5 and 37.6 R@1,0.7 on Charades-STA, and 41.2 / 24.9 on ActivityNet-Caption, surpassing the SFT variant (58.4 / 34.7 and 41.0 / 23.7). The largest relative consistency gains appear on the hardest sub-tasks: S-Grounding improves from 10.5 to 33.8 IoU (TimeIT data) and H-Verify from 16.7% to 37.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) applied to the same backbone models (Qwen2.5-VL, Video-LLaMA, TimeChat) with the same training data (VTune or TimeIT). TCAS wins on every metric across all three backbones and both consistency benchmarks. The most dramatic gains occur on TimeChat+TimeIT, where S-Grounding jumps from 10.5 to 33.8 IoU (+23.3) and H-Verify from 16.7% to 37.3% (+20.6 pp), indicating that TCAS is especially effective when the base model has the weakest temporal grounding. On the stronger TimeChat+VTune setting, gains are more moderate but still consistent: +7.1 IoU on Grounding, +8.1 pp on H-Verify, and +8.4 pp on C-Verify. On ActivityNet-CON, improvements are smaller (e.g., +1.4 IoU on Grounding for TimeChat+VTune), which the authors attribute to noisier, longer-sentence event descriptions in that dataset. On the standard VTG benchmarks (Table 4), TimeChat+TCAS (60.2 R@1,0.5 on Charades-STA) still trails the task-specific Mr.BLIP model (69.3) but surpasses TimeChat+SFT (58.4) and other Video-LLM baselines like HawkEye (58.3) and VTG-LLM (57.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant approach of enhancing Video-LLM temporal understanding through additional specialized temporal modules (e.g., time gating, timestamp encoding) by instead diagnosing the problem from within the model's attention mechanisms. It builds on the consistency benchmark of Jung et al. [8], which surfaced the inconsistency phenomenon, and on the MLLM interpretability literature (Nikankin [15], Venhoff [23], Yu & Ananiadou [26]) that has studied modality-specific circuits, but is the first to apply causal intervention and attention discriminability analysis specifically to temporal reasoning in Video-LLMs. The proposed TCAS loss repurposes contrastive learning principles to shape attention distributions over temporal tokens without introducing new architectural components, positioning it as a training-time regularizer rather than a structural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing a causal link between cross-modal attention discriminability and temporal logic consistency, the paper provides a mechanistic explanation for why Video-LLMs fail at rephrased and shifted grounding, moving the field beyond empirical benchmarking toward targeted intervention. The observation that TCAS also improves general VTG performance (not just consistency scores) suggests that temporal consistency is a fundamental bottleneck rather than a narrow evaluation artifact, which could reshape how the community designs temporal grounding methods. However, the authors acknowledge that logical inconsistency is only one facet of temporal understanding, and the analysis is primarily conducted on TimeChat-7B with limited cross-model validation (Qwen2.5-VL on a single EOJ task), leaving open whether the identified mechanism generalizes to all architectures and temporal reasoning sub-tasks.
