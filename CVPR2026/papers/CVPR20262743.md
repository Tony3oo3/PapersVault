## Problem

Learned image codecs can be directly optimized for human perception, yet no perceptual codec has achieved practical deployment: existing perceptual codecs are an order of magnitude slower than needed for on-device use and lack essential features like rate control and cross-platform robustness. This paper closes the gap by systematically studying which modeling choices maximize perceptual quality without incurring computational overhead, then searching over millions of backbone configurations to find models that meet on-device runtime targets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262743/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The codec takes an RGB image and a scalar quality level (signaled in the bitstream) as input, and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image. Images are partitioned into non-overlapping 504×504 tiles, each padded to 512×512 with a 4-pixel contextual margin from neighboring tiles to maintain feature continuity. The scale decoder is quantized to UINT8 and executed on CPU under IEEE FP compliance to guarantee bit-exact entropy decoding across heterogeneous hardware, while the remaining neural components run on the ML accelerator in a pipelined fashion.

## Method

PICO builds on a modified hyperprior architecture where the hyper-decoder is split into a bit-exact scale decoder and a context decoder, and the hyper-encoder is absorbed into the main encoder. Key architectural contributions include ConvScale311 blocks (inverted residuals with learned input/output elementwise scales that reparameterize at zero inference cost), an input-specific learned quantization width from the context decoder, a one-shot context model that decodes σ in one pass while applying iterative autoregressive prediction to μ and q entirely on the accelerator, and Conv+Haar wavelet resampling that adds multi-scale inductive bias at zero extra FLOPs. Training uses a two-stage recipe (MSE pre-training followed by perceptual fine-tuning) with a combined loss of MSE, LPIPS, MS-SSIM, a multi-resolution TilingArtifactLoss for cross-tile color consistency, a TextFidelityLoss that applies heavy L1 in text-salient regions while suppressing GAN losses there, and a boosted patch-wise GAN discriminator with warm-up scheduling. A multi-step neural architecture search filters 1.4M decoder candidates via kMACs/pixel bounds, on-device runtime benchmarking, partial-training PSNR screening, and full perceptual training to select the final 9.6M-parameter decoder.

## Results

PICO achieves a perceptual BD-rate of 0% (anchor) against all baselines, translating to 2.3–3× bitrate savings over traditional codecs (HEIC: 227%, AV1: 169%, AV2: 173%, VVC: 151%, ECM: 148%) and 20–40% savings over the strongest learned perceptual codecs (MRIC: 21%, C3-WD: 40%, HiFiC: 40%). On an iPhone 17 Pro Max, PICO encodes 12MP images in 230 ms and decodes in 150 ms, faster than most state-of-the-art learned codecs running on a V100 GPU. Subjective Elo scores from 74,925 pairwise comparisons by 610 reviewers confirm PICO's dominance across CLIC 2020, Kodak, and DIV2K test sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PICO outperforms all 13 baselines on perceptual quality as measured by subjective Elo scores. Against traditional codecs, PICO achieves 2.3–3× bitrate savings: HEIC (227% BD-rate), AV1 (169%), AV2 (173%), VVC/VTM (151%), ECM (148%), and BPG (>3×). Among learned non-perceptual codecs, PICO dominates MLIC++ (268%), TCM (220%), DCVC-RT (172%), JPEG-AI HOP (129%), and CDC (137%), while also being the only learned codec with both rate control and cross-device support. The closest competitors are the learned+perceptual codecs: MRIC (21% BD-rate), C3-WD (40%), and HiFiC (40%), all of which require 20–40% larger file sizes for equivalent quality and lack practical features like rate control or cross-platform robustness. On speed, PICO's 230 ms encode / 150 ms decode on iPhone 17 Pro Max is faster than HiFiC (680/3,200 ms on iPhone), DCVC-RT (710/980 ms on iPhone), and all GPU-based learned codecs (e.g., CDC at 2,200/490,000 ms on V100, C3-WD at 3.5e10 ms encode on V100). PICO is the only codec in the comparison that simultaneously achieves top perceptual quality, on-device speed, rate control, and cross-device compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

PICO builds directly on the hyperprior architecture (Minnen et al., 2018) and the quality-level conditioning recipe from Elf-VC (Rippel et al., 2021), but restructures the framework by splitting the hyper-decoder into a bit-exact scale decoder and a flexible context decoder, and absorbing the hyper-encoder into the main encoder. It departs from prior perceptual codecs (HiFiC, MRIC, C3-WD, CDC) that achieved strong perceptual quality only at GPU-scale runtimes, and from practical learned codecs (JPEG-AI, DCVC-RT, MLIC++) that optimized for PSNR/SSIM rather than human perception. The paper's core positioning is bridging this gap: it is the first work to jointly optimize a learned codec for perceptual quality and on-device runtime through systematic ablations and neural architecture search over millions of backbone configurations, introducing several novel components (ConvScale, one-shot context model, learned quantization width, Haar-based resampling, and artifact-specific losses) that individually add expressivity or mitigate failure modes without increasing FLOPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PICO demonstrates that a phone-class device (iPhone 17 Pro Max) can encode 12MP images in 230ms and decode in 150ms while achieving 2.3–3× bitrate savings over industry-standard codecs (AV1, AV2, VVC, ECM, JPEG-AI) and 20–40% savings over the strongest GPU-based perceptual codecs, based on a large-scale subjective study (74,925 pairwise comparisons, 610 reviewers). This challenges the prevailing assumption that high perceptual compression quality necessitates heavyweight GPU inference, and provides a concrete, deployable reference for the next generation of learned image standards. Open questions include whether the specific architectural choices and NAS-discovered configurations generalize beyond Apple's hardware ecosystem, whether the approach extends naturally to video compression, and whether the subjective-study-based BD-rate metric will be adopted as a standard evaluation protocol in the field. The systematic ablation tables (Tab. 1, Tab. 2) also serve as a practical design guide for future codec developers.
