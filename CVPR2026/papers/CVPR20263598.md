## Problem

Current Scene Graph Generation models encode relational knowledge implicitly through neural classification, producing relation triplets without explaining why they hold or composing knowledge across unseen contexts. This limits compositional generalization and interpretability: models treat each relation as an independent prediction, missing multi-hop logical structures (e.g., inferring travel-on from riding and on) that require explicit rule chaining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263598/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a scene graph containing entity nodes (with pixel-precise masks from Mask2Former), relation triplets, and—uniquely—interpretable first-order logic rules with confidence scores that form explicit reasoning chains. The output is structured as a directed graph where edges carry both a predicted predicate and the evidential chain of compositional rules (Horn clauses) that justified the prediction, enabling auditability beyond a single classification score.

## Method

NeuroRule integrates a Mask2Former (Swin-L backbone) for pixel-precise entity segmentation with a differentiable rule induction engine that learns interpretable Horn clauses end-to-end. A neural-symbolic bridge maps continuous entity features and spatial relation vectors to soft predicate truth values via sigmoid-weighted embeddings, while a rule template mechanism with attention-based soft predicate selection (softmax over a predicate set at each body position) discovers rule structures differentiably. Multi-hop compositional reasoning is achieved through forward chaining: intermediate variables in rule bodies are resolved by max-over-entities (differentiated via softmax), enabling inferences like travel-on(x,z) from riding(x,y) ∧ on(y,z). Training minimizes a combined loss of relation prediction cross-entropy and a rule complexity regularizer (L1 sparsity plus semantic body-head coherence), jointly optimizing predicate embeddings, rule weights, and rule structures in a single gradient-driven pipeline.

## Results

On the Visual Genome SGDet benchmark, NeuroRule achieves state-of-the-art results across all eight metrics, with AP50 of 42.3 (vs. 38.9 for the next-best ViStruct), R@50 of 63.2, mR@50 of 36.7, and F1 of 0.47. On the Panoptic Scene Graph benchmark, NeuroRule leads by approximately 10 points in Overall Spatial Accuracy (88.4% vs. 78.4% for ViStruct), with Cont@R of 85.2%, Supp@R of 88.7%, and Cont@ of 91.3%. On Open-PSG zero-shot evaluation, NeuroRule attains the best Open-Entity recall (42.7%), Open-Relations mR@20 (38.9%), Compositional Accuracy (45.2%), and Transfer Score (0.82), outperforming all baselines by 6–11 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroRule outperforms all 11 baselines on every reported metric across all three benchmarks. On VG, the closest competitor is ViStruct (CodeT5+BLIP-2), which NeuroRule surpasses by +3.4 AP50, +2.7 R@50, and +2.0 mR@50; the gap widens substantially against earlier methods like EGTR (+10.6 AP50) and RAHP (+12.5 AP50). On PSG, NeuroRule's mask-level spatial reasoning yields a decisive ~10-point margin in Overall Spatial Accuracy over ViStruct (88.4 vs. 78.4), with the largest gap in Containment (91.3 vs. 82.1). On Open-PSG zero-shot tasks, NeuroRule leads ViStruct by +7.5 points in Open-Entity recall, +6.1 in Open-Relations mR@20, and +0.11 in Transfer Score, while RAHP and EGTR trail by over 10 points on most metrics. The efficiency analysis (Fig. 4) further shows NeuroRule is ~3× faster than the symbolic P-SGG baseline while achieving +9.3% higher R@50 than the purely neural Motif-Net, confirming that the differentiable rule module adds interpretability without prohibitive computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuroRule carves new ground at the intersection of scene graph generation and differentiable rule learning, repurposing Horn-clause logic and fuzzy-logic conjunction (product t-norm) from the knowledge-graph reasoning literature (DRUM, RNNLogic) into a visual perception pipeline. Rather than building on a single prior SGG architecture, it grafts a differentiable rule-induction engine onto Mask2Former's pixel-precise segmentation, departing from the purely neural classifiers (EGTR, DECOLA, ViStruct, RAHP) that dominate current SGG by treating relations as independent predictions. The key conceptual departure is elevating rules from fixed, hand-engineered constraints to learnable, composable primitives whose structure and weights are jointly optimized end-to-end with the visual backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in SGG: current models can identify *what* relations exist but cannot explain *why* or compose them into multi-hop inferences, limiting interpretability and zero-shot generalization. By providing explicit, auditable reasoning chains alongside predictions, NeuroRule offers a path toward trustworthy visual intelligence that is particularly relevant for safety-critical and open-world applications. Open questions remain around the scalability of the rule space (up to 1,000 templates with L=4), whether learned rules truly transfer beyond the training distribution, and how the approach behaves under severe data scarcity or highly cluttered scenes with many interacting entities.
