## Problem

Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks for VLMs test only high-level relational concepts (e.g., 'left of', 'behind') and rely on approximate annotations from monocular depth estimation, leaving a critical gap in fine-grained object-interaction reasoning: precise 3D pose estimation, grasp affordance prediction, collision-aware motion planning, and multi-step object rearrangement in cluttered scenes. These capabilities are essential for embodied robotic manipulation but remain unaddressed by prior datasets that lack millimeter-level geometric grounding.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single RGB image from a cluttered tabletop scene paired with a natural-language question. Outputs are structured as pixel-level 2D keypoint lists projected from 3D geometry: 3D cuboid poses (8 corner points), 5-point grasp poses (center, left/right finger bases, left/right finger tips), ordered waypoint sequences for inter-object trajectories, a single object identifier for rearrangement, or binary yes/no for spatial and depth queries. Unlike prior spatial datasets that use multiple-choice or bounding-box outputs, BOP-Ask requires precise pixel-coordinate regression grounded in exact 6D pose ground truth from the BOP benchmark.

## Method

The pipeline automatically generates 33M QA pairs from BOP's 6D pose annotations through three stages: (1) world-frame reconstruction via RANSAC plane fitting on table surfaces (localized by a pointing VLM) with Rodrigues rotation alignment; (2) geometric prior computation using RRT planners for collision-free inter-object trajectories (simplified via Ramer-Douglas-Peucker) and M2T2 transformer-based parallel-gripper grasp prediction with dual global/object-centric sampling; (3) LLM-driven question generation from task-specific templates with 3D positional disambiguation attributes for duplicate object categories. Training is supervised fine-tuning of open-source VLMs (NVILA 2B/15B, Qwen-VL 2.5 3B) on the generated corpus, with ablations confirming that multi-skill co-training (including binary spatial/depth tasks) and multi-dataset diversity (HOPE, HANDAL, YCB-V, LineMOD) are both necessary for strong generalization.

## Results

On BOP-Ask-core, NVILA(15B)-SFT achieves the strongest overall performance: 73.5 3D IoU on pose estimation, 64.2% success rate on trajectory prediction (77.4 px distance error), 1.40 NCE on grasps, 95.8% spatial reasoning accuracy, 94.6% relative depth accuracy, and 57.7% recall on object rearrangement. NVILA(2B)-SFT attains the best pose IoU (77.4) and competitive scores across all other tasks. These SFT models substantially exceed the human baseline (54.2 IoU, 67.3% traj SR, 44.1% rearrangement recall) on pose, spatial reasoning, depth, and rearrangement, while remaining slightly below humans on trajectory success rate (64.2% vs. 67.3%) and grasp NCE (1.40 vs. 1.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

All eight baselines are decisively outperformed by the BOP-Ask–fine-tuned models across every task. Among proprietary models, Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5 is the strongest (24.4 Pose IoU, 43.0% trajectory SR, 48.9% rearrangement recall) yet still trails NVILA(15B)-SFT by ~49 IoU points on pose and ~21 points on trajectory SR; GPT-5 fails entirely on trajectory and grasp tasks (0% SR, inf distance error/NCE). Among open-source baselines, RoboRefer achieves the highest pose IoU (34.3) but produces no valid trajectory or grasp outputs; NVILA(15B) without fine-tuning scores only 27.2 IoU and 6.2% trajectory SR, a gap of 46.3 IoU and 58 points in trajectory SR after BOP-Ask training. Molmo (72B) and Intern-VL show moderate spatial/depth accuracy (68–76%) but near-zero trajectory success and no valid grasp predictions. On out-of-domain benchmarks (Table 4), BOP-Ask fine-tuning also yields consistent gains: NVILA improves from 63.4→69.1 on RoboSpatial-Home, 78.2→89.3 on CV-Bench, and 47.5→50.0 on SpatialBench, while Qwen-VL 2.5 improves from 78.1→81.3, 88.8→92.4, and 60.0→65.0 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

BOP-Ask extends the BOP 6D pose estimation benchmark into the VLM reasoning domain, inheriting precise geometric ground truth that prior spatial reasoning datasets (SpatialVLM, RoboSpatial, BLINK) lack, as they rely on approximate monocular depth or synthetic annotations. It also departs from the multiple-choice and yes/no evaluation paradigm of existing spatial benchmarks by requiring pixel-level outputs for 3D cuboids, 5-point grasp poses, and continuous motion trajectories. The grasp generation pipeline builds on M2T2's transformer-based parallel gripper model, while trajectory synthesis uses RRT with Ramer-Douglas-Peucker simplification, grounding the dataset in physically plausible manipulation geometry rather than abstract spatial relations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The striking failure of frontier proprietary models (GPT-5: 0% trajectory success, infinite grasp error) alongside strong performance of a 2B open-source model after BOP-Ask fine-tuning (64% trajectory SR, 1.40 NCE) reveals that general-purpose VLM scaling does not transfer to precise geometric manipulation reasoning—targeted geometric supervision is essential. The robot experiment (0/15 → 10/15 pick-and-place success) provides rare real-world validation, while the OOD gains on RoboSpatial-Home, CV-Bench, and SpatialBench suggest the learned spatial priors generalize beyond the training distribution. Object rearrangement remains the hardest task (~58% best), and the BOP-Ask-lab benchmark exposes persistent gaps in open-world generalization, leaving room for future work on more diverse scenes and multi-step manipulation planning.
