## Problem

Existing 3D change detection methods formulate the task as one-step point-wise segmentation, which breaks spatial consistency (causing scattered misclassifications within coherent regions) and is severely biased by the extreme class imbalance where unchanged points vastly outnumber changed ones, leading to incomplete interiors and blurred boundaries. The paper argues that this segmentation paradigm is inherently unsuitable because it cannot model the progressive, hierarchical evolution of stability from confident interior cores toward uncertain boundaries.

## Input / Output

The method takes two registered bi-temporal 3D point clouds (P₀ and P₁) in a common coordinate system, downsampled through a KPConv-FPN encoder into multi-scale feature levels. It produces a per-point change probability map at the finest resolution, thresholded at 0.5 to yield a binary label (changed/unchanged) for each point. The intermediate refined masks at each scale are also supervised during training, enabling the coarse-to-fine growth to be guided at every level.

## Method

SRGCD replaces the conventional decoder with a Stability-Driven Region Growth Framework that iteratively propagates stability from sparse, high-confidence unchanged seeds outward across multiple scales. At the deepest layer, a Mutual Geometric Consistency Prior (MGCP) selects seeds via mutual nearest-neighbor matching, distance thresholding, and surface-normal angle constraints, guaranteeing zero false-unchanged seeds. At each subsequent scale, a Stability-Guided Controlled Attention (SGCA) module performs one-way feature propagation from stable to unstable regions using masked keys/values, with two adaptive controls: Stability-Adaptive Propagation (SAP) modulates per-point update magnitude by change confidence, and Stability-Proportional Temperature (SPT) adjusts softmax temperature based on the fraction of stable points at that scale. The optimized features are fused with upsampled masks and difference features via an MLP to produce a refined mask that guides the next iteration, forming a closed-loop coarse-to-fine growth. Training uses layer-wise BCE loss plus a Contrastive Boundary Learning (CBL) loss that enforces inter-class feature separation at boundary points to prevent over-expansion.

## Results

On the synthetic Urb3DCD-V1 dataset, SRGCD achieves 94.11% mIoU and 96.95% mAcc, surpassing the strongest Transformer-based baseline PGN3DCD by +1.65% mIoU and the CNN-based SiameseKPConv by +4.22% mIoU. On the more challenging real-world HKCD dataset, SRGCD attains 78.79% mIoU and 87.93% mAcc, outperforming PGN3DCD by +1.84% mIoU and SiameseKPConv by +5.06% mIoU. The method also substantially outperforms traditional approaches (RF, DSM-FC-EF) by over 25% mIoU on Urb3DCD, demonstrating the benefit of end-to-end geometric feature learning over handcrafted DSM-based subtraction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRGCD outperforms all six baselines on both datasets in both mIoU and mAcc. Against the closest Transformer-based competitor PGN3DCD, SRGCD gains +1.65% mIoU on Urb3DCD (94.11 vs. 92.46) and +1.84% on HKCD (78.79 vs. 76.95), with a larger mAcc margin of +2.50% on Urb3DCD (96.95 vs. 94.45). Compared to PBFormer, the advantage is +2.37% mIoU on Urb3DCD and +3.67% on HKCD, with the gap widening on the noisier real-world data. Against the CNN-based SiameseKPConv (which also serves as the backbone-only ablation baseline), SRGCD improves mIoU by +4.22% on Urb3DCD and +5.06% on HKCD. The largest margins are over 3DCDNet (+9.90% mIoU on Urb3DCD, +17.59% on HKCD), which lacks cross-temporal feature interaction. Qualitatively, SRGCD produces fewer isolated noise points and sharper boundaries, while baselines like PBFormer and PGN3DCD exhibit block-shaped errors and 3DCDNet shows uniformly scattered misclassifications.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant one-step point-wise segmentation paradigm for 3D change detection (SiameseKPConv, PGN3DCD, PBFormer) by reformulating the task as a progressive, stability-driven region growth process. It repurposes classical geometric consistency checks (mutual nearest neighbors, distance and normal-angle thresholds) from traditional change detection into a learned seed-initialization module (MGCP), and introduces a novel one-way controlled attention mechanism (SGCA) that has no direct counterpart in prior 3DCD literature. The contrastive boundary learning loss is adapted from point cloud segmentation [26], and the KPConv-FPN backbone follows the SiameseKPConv architecture, so the method is best understood as a new decoder/propagation strategy grafted onto an established encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The region-growth reframing offers a principled way to tackle the severe class imbalance in change detection at the architectural level rather than through loss re-weighting, which could inspire similar coarse-to-fine propagation strategies in other imbalanced detection tasks. However, the method is currently limited to binary (changed/unchanged) detection on aligned bi-temporal point clouds, and the authors themselves flag multi-class extension as future work. Evaluation is restricted to two datasets (one synthetic, one real), and the moderate performance gains (+1.6–1.8% mIoU over the strongest baseline) leave open whether the paradigm shift yields larger advantages on more challenging or larger-scale benchmarks.
