## Problem

Existing embodied question answering benchmarks evaluate reasoning over small spatial extents in household settings, under-representing the multi-scale reasoning, long-range spatial understanding, and domain-specific rating criteria demanded by real-world infrastructure inspection. No prior benchmark provides expert-grounded supervision, standardized numerical ratings, or egocentric imagery spanning dozens of views per scene, leaving a gap in evaluating VLMs on tasks with measurable professional criteria.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language inspection question and an image-based scene graph (constructed purely visually from egocentric inspection photos, averaging 47.93 images per bridge scene, with no GPS or external spatial sensors required). The scene graph encodes nodes as individual images with semantic labels and directed edges capturing structural, spatial, and condition-similarity relationships. The output is a structured response containing a free-text answer, an explicit list of cited image filenames as visual evidence, and an integer NBI condition rating on the 0–9 scale, mirroring the documentation format of professional inspection reports.

## Method

EMVR (Embodied Memory Visual Reasoning) reformulates episodic-memory EQA as a Markov decision process over an image-based scene graph, where the agent starts with only the graph structure (node labels, descriptions, edges) and dynamically retrieves images via function calls—MOVE to navigate to a neighboring node, COMPARE to load multiple images for side-by-side analysis, REASON to self-question a single image, and RESPOND to emit the final answer with citations and rating. This selective retrieval mitigates the 'lost in the middle' positional bias that degrades Multi-Frame VLM performance on long image sequences, by promoting mid-sequence evidence to the end of the VLM's context window. The scene graph uses images as nodes rather than detected objects because bridge inspection lacks foundation models capable of densely detecting all structural components. A VLM (Gemini 2.5 Flash, Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite, or Grok 4 Fast) implements the policy, and a new Image Citation Relevance metric (VLM-as-a-judge, Spearman 0.817 with human annotators) evaluates semantic alignment between agent-cited and inspector-validated reference images.

## Results

On the BridgeEQA test set (1,100 QA pairs), EMVR VLM w/ Images+SG with Grok 4 Fast achieves 0.648 Answer Correctness and 0.889 Image Citation Relevance, the best scores across all five methods and three VLMs. The paper reports that EMVR improves condition rating accuracy within ±1 by 9.34 percentage points, Image Citation Relevance by 20.2 percentage points, and Answer Correctness by 7.2 percentage points over the Multi-Frame VLM baseline using Grok 4 Fast. With Gemini 2.5 Flash, EMVR VLM w/ SG Only reaches 0.609 Answer Correctness and 0.836 Image Citation Relevance, also outperforming all baselines on that model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMVR consistently outperforms both baselines across all three VLMs on both metrics. Against Multi-Frame VLM [28] (the strongest OpenEQA baseline), EMVR VLM w/ Images+SG with Grok 4 Fast gains +7.2 pp on Answer Correctness (0.648 vs. 0.576) and +20.2 pp on Image Citation Relevance (0.889 vs. 0.687); with Gemini 2.5 Flash, EMVR VLM w/ SG Only gains +12.5 pp on Answer Correctness (0.609 vs. 0.484) and +14.2 pp on ICR (0.836 vs. 0.694). Against Socratic LLM w/ SG [28,47], EMVR also wins on every configuration: with Grok 4 Fast, +3.4 pp on Answer Correctness (0.648 vs. 0.614) and +5.1 pp on ICR (0.889 vs. 0.838); with Gemini 2.5 Flash, +2.1 pp on Answer Correctness (0.609 vs. 0.588) and +6.9 pp on ICR (0.836 vs. 0.767). The largest gains appear in Image Citation Relevance, suggesting that the scene-graph navigation mechanism most strongly improves the agent's ability to identify and cite the correct supporting images rather than merely producing a plausible text answer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the OpenEQA framework (Majumdar et al., CVPR 2024) by reformulating its Multi-Frame VLM baseline into an active, navigational agent that traverses an image-based scene graph via a Markov decision process, directly addressing the 'lost in the middle' positional bias in long-context VLMs. It departs from prior EQA benchmarks (which focus on household scenes with simple spatial queries) by introducing the 'Inspection EQA' problem class, which demands multi-scale reasoning, domain-specific NBI condition ratings, and explicit image citation. The scene graph construction draws on 3D scene graph literature (Armeni et al., 2019; Rana et al., 2023) but substitutes image nodes for object nodes, a design choice motivated by the absence of foundation models capable of densely detecting all bridge structural components.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BridgeEQA provides the first large-scale, expert-grounded benchmark for embodied QA in infrastructure inspection, a domain with direct public-safety and economic impact given that nearly 40% of U.S. bridges have exceeded their design life. The Image Citation Relevance metric and the finding that poor citations proxy for hallucinations offer a practical diagnostic tool for VLM reliability in safety-critical settings. Key limitations include reliance on proprietary VLMs (open-source sub-30B models could not handle the structured-output and function-calling requirements) and evaluation on a single geographic region (Vermont), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to other infrastructure asset types (dams, tunnels, pipelines) and diverse imaging conditions.
