## Problem

The rapid proliferation of 3DGS optimization improvements has created a fragmented landscape where implementation-level speedups are tightly coupled with fundamental algorithmic changes, making it impossible to assess the upper bound of achievable training performance while preserving original reconstruction quality. No consolidated, rigorously optimized baseline exists that integrates all net-positive contributions from prior work into the standard 3DGS pipeline without altering quality or Gaussian count.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sparse point cloud (or random initialization), multi-view RGB training images with known camera poses and intrinsics, and produces a set of 3D Gaussian primitives (position, anisotropic covariance, opacity, and up to degree-3 spherical harmonics) that render novel views via differentiable tile-based rasterization. A key constraint is that training images are pre-loaded into VRAM before optimization begins, and the output must match the original 3DGS in both visual quality and total Gaussian count—no pruning, compression, or reduced-precision shortcuts are permitted. For dynamic scenes, the output extends to 4D Gaussians with temporal mean and scale parameters, rendered at a given timestep via conditional 3D Gaussian computation.

## Method

Faster-GS consolidates and evaluates the most effective prior optimizations—opacity-aware rectangular bounding boxes, load-balanced tile-based culling, two-stage sorting, per-Gaussian backward pass, fused activation functions, and separate SH buffers—then introduces three novel contributions: (1) periodic z-ordering of Gaussians during densification to improve memory coalescence and reduce warp divergence, (2) a custom fused Adam optimizer that eliminates PyTorch overhead via fused-multiply-adds and minimal instruction count, and (3) fusing the Adam parameter update directly into the rasterization backward pass to eliminate separate optimizer kernel launches. The key insight is that 3DGS training is overwhelmingly memory-bound rather than compute-bound, so the dominant speedups come from reducing memory access costs through coalesced reads, fewer duplicated Gaussians per tile, and eliminating redundant buffer loads/stores between pipeline stages. The framework achieves up to 5× faster training (163 s average on RTX 4090) with 30% less VRAM while maintaining identical PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS and Gaussian counts, and extends directly to 4D Gaussian optimization for dynamic scenes.

## Results

Faster-GS achieves up to 5.2× faster training than the original 3DGS while maintaining identical reconstruction quality (PSNR within 0.03 dB) and using 30% less VRAM. On Mip-NeRF360, training drops from 18m44s (3DGS) to 4m31s with 6.1 GiB peak VRAM vs. 8.8 GiB. On Deep Blending, the speedup reaches 5.2× (19m43s → 3m46s). For 4D dynamic scene reconstruction on D-NeRF, Faster-GS trains 2.8× faster than the reference 4DGS implementation (18m09s → 6m22s) with lower VRAM (1.2 vs. 1.9 GiB) and marginally higher PSNR (31.79 vs. 31.52).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Faster-GS outperforms all five baselines on training time and VRAM while matching or slightly exceeding their reconstruction quality. Against the original 3DGS [35], it achieves a 4.1× speedup on Mip-NeRF360 (18m44s → 4m31s) and 5.2× on Deep Blending (19m43s → 3m46s), with 30% less peak VRAM and PSNR within 0.03 dB. Compared to the strongest prior optimized baseline, Taming-3DGS [54] (10m49s on Mip-NeRF360), Faster-GS is 2.4× faster on Deep Blending and uses 31% less VRAM (6.1 vs. 8.9 GiB). StopThePop [63] and Speedy-Splat [24] provide only modest speedups over 3DGS (1.1–1.2×) and higher VRAM, while Faster-GS achieves 3.7–3.9× speedups over both. For 4D dynamic scenes, Faster-GS trains 2.8× faster than the reference 4DGS implementation [90] on D-NeRF (6m22s vs. 18m09s) with 37% less VRAM and 0.27 dB higher PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

Faster-GS consolidates and extends a fragmented body of 3DGS training optimizations that were previously scattered across inference-focused works (SplatShop [71], FlashGS [15]), quality-focused works (StopThePop [63], Speedy-Splat [24]), and resource-constrained training (Taming-3DGS [54]). Rather than proposing a new rendering formulation, the paper systematically evaluates which of these techniques are net-positive for training speed without altering reconstruction quality, then adds two genuinely novel contributions: z-ordering for memory coalescence during densification and a fused backward-pass/Adam implementation. The 4D extension builds directly on Yang et al. [90], demonstrating that the same GPU-level optimizations transfer to dynamic scene reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing a new training-speed baseline (4–5× faster, 30% less VRAM) that preserves the original 3DGS quality and Gaussian count, this work removes a practical bottleneck for the large 3DGS research community and lowers the hardware barrier for experimentation. The finding that load-balanced tile culling becomes counterproductive at high Gaussian counts due to warp divergence challenges a commonly adopted optimization and highlights the tension between load balancing and memory coalescence on GPUs. The remaining bottleneck—parameter updates consuming 40–60% of training time—points toward second-order optimizers or more compact appearance representations as the next frontier, while the 4D extension shows the framework generalizes to dynamic scenes.
