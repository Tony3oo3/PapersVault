## Problem

Existing VLM-based SVG generation methods formulate SVG output as pure text, providing no visual signal during autoregressive decoding. This causes minor coordinate errors to produce catastrophic rendering failures and limits generalization, since these unimodal models can only train on small SVG datasets rather than leveraging large-scale text-image corpora.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263664/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts arbitrary combinations of text prompts, raster images (resized to 384×384 and encoded into 576 discrete visual tokens via a VQ codebook of size 16,384), and SVG code as conditioning input. Output is a single autoregressive sequence structured as image tokens followed by SVG tokens (truncated to 12,000 text tokens with a restricted command vocabulary of {M, L, C, Q, A, Z, Ellipse, Circle, Polygon, Rect}), so the generated image tokens causally guide subsequent SVG token prediction.

## Method

DuetSVG builds on Janus-Pro-7B, using SigLIP as an understanding encoder and a VQ tokenizer as a generation encoder, each projected into the LLM space via separate MLP aligners. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) large-scale T2I pretraining on real and FLUX.1-synthesized data to instill visual priors for clean, geometric SVG-style images, and (2) multi-task SFT jointly on T2I, T2SVG, and I2SVG with SVG-specific augmentations (random perturbation of rotation, translation, scaling, color, and path dropout). At inference, a two-stage test-time scaling strategy first samples N image candidates with classifier-free guidance and selects the best via CLIP, then generates SVG tokens in small chunks, accepting each chunk only if its rendered LPIPS distance to the selected image does not increase—providing internal visual guidance at far lower cost than best-of-N over full SVG sequences.

## Results

On the SVG-Hub-5M test set, DuetSVG with test-time scaling (TTS) achieves FID 33.574 and CLIP score 26.106 for text-to-SVG, outperforming the best proprietary VLM (Gemini-3-Pro: FID 48.765, CLIP 25.146) by a wide margin. For image-to-SVG, DuetSVG (TTS) reaches DINO 0.962, SSIM 0.928, LPIPS 0.075, PSNR 23.590, SVG Code Similarity 0.964, and Path Semantics 2.724, surpassing all VLM baselines. On the SArena-Icon benchmark, DuetSVG (TTS) further leads with FID 11.712, FID-CLIP 3.928, and CLIP 25.734 for T2SVG, and DINO 0.972, LPIPS 0.060, PSNR 24.016 for I2SVG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DuetSVG (TTS) outperforms all proprietary VLMs (GPT-5-Thinking, Gemini-3-Pro, Gemini-2.5-Pro) on every T2SVG and I2SVG metric; for example, it reduces T2SVG FID from 48.765 (Gemini-3-Pro) to 33.574 and improves I2SVG DINO from 0.921 to 0.962. Among open-source VLMs fine-tuned on SVG-Hub-5M, DuetSVG beats Qwen3-VL-8B (FID 43.720→33.574, DINO 0.947→0.962), LLM4SVG-7B (FID 49.318→33.574), and OmniSVG-3B (FID 58.237→33.574) by substantial margins. The classical vectorizer VTracer slightly edges DuetSVG on raw I2SVG DINO (0.968 vs. 0.962) and PSNR (23.623 vs. 23.590), but DuetSVG dominates on SVG Code Similarity (0.964 vs. 0.788) and Path Semantics (2.724 vs. 0.982), indicating far cleaner and more semantically organized vector output. Optimization-based methods (VectorFusion, SVGDreamer, T2V-NPR) trail DuetSVG on all T2SVG metrics by 15–25 FID points and produce redundant paths. The ablation confirms that removing internal visual guidance degrades T2SVG FID from 33.574 to 51.482, and removing T2I pretraining raises FID to 36.953, validating both the multimodal design and the pretraining stage.

## Relation to Prior Work

DuetSVG builds directly on Janus-Pro, a unified autoregressive model that jointly generates image and text tokens, repurposing its architecture for SVG generation. It departs from the dominant paradigm of prior SVG generation work (StarVector, LLM4SVG, OmniSVG, and proprietary VLMs) that treats SVG purely as a text sequence, by co-generating discrete image tokens alongside SVG tokens so that visual predictions serve as internal guidance during SVG decoding. It also contrasts with optimization-based methods (VectorFusion, SVGDreamer, T2V-NPR) that iteratively refine SVG paths via differentiable rendering, offering an end-to-end autoregressive alternative. The paper further introduces a novel test-time scaling strategy—image-guided chunked SVG resampling—that replaces the expensive best-of-N reranking used in text-only models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling joint training on large-scale text-image datasets in addition to SVG data, DuetSVG addresses a key bottleneck: prior SVG-specific VLMs are constrained by the small size of available SVG corpora, limiting generalization to complex or out-of-distribution prompts. The unified multimodal design also simplifies downstream task extension (SVG completion, semantic editing) and makes verifier-based test-time scaling more efficient. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger unified models (the authors mention Emu3.5 as a future direction), how well it handles highly complex multi-layer SVGs beyond the 12K-token limit, and whether the reliance on a frozen VQ tokenizer for image tokens constrains the fidelity of the visual guidance signal.
