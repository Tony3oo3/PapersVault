## Problem

MeanFlow (MF) enables few-step image generation by learning long PF-ODE jumps, but its training is computationally expensive (600+ H100 GPU-days on ImageNet 256), prone to gradient explosion, and requires intricate classifier-free guidance hyperparameters for class-conditional generation. Additionally, the SD-VAE decoder used in standard latent MF accounts for ~73% of total generation cost, bottlenecking the few-step speed advantage. This paper addresses all three issues by reformulating MF in the latent space of a Representation Autoencoder (RAE) with a lightweight decoder and a guidance-free training pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260936/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label and a Gaussian noise vector in the RAE latent space (high-dimensional semantic tokens from a frozen DINOv2/SigLIP2 encoder) as input, and produces a synthetic RGB image at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution via a lightweight ViT-based decoder. Generation requires only 1 or 2 network evaluations (NFE) of the DiT^DH transformer, after which the RAE decoder (≈106 GFLOPS) maps the latent back to pixel space, compared to ≈310 GFLOPS for the SD-VAE decoder used in vanilla MF.

## Method

MF-RAE learns the MeanFlow map h_θ(z_t, t, s) in the RAE latent space using a DiT^DH backbone augmented with a time-difference (t−s) embedding. Training is decomposed into three stages: (1) flow matching pre-training to obtain a teacher, (2) Consistency Mid-Training (CMT) to produce a trajectory-aware MF initialization that prevents the gradient explosion observed with random or FM-teacher initialization, and (3) MeanFlow Distillation (MFD) that regresses h_θ against a stop-gradient target built from the teacher velocity, optionally followed by a short MeanFlow Training (MFT) bootstrapping stage using a one-point velocity estimator to reduce residual bias. A theoretical decomposition (Proposition 3.1) formalizes the bias-variance trade-off between MFD (low variance, teacher-dependent bias) and MFT (higher variance, lower bias), justifying the two-stage schedule. The Jacobian-vector product in the MF loss is replaced by a central finite-difference approximation (Δt = 0.005), eliminating the need for forward-mode autodiff and yielding ~1.5× faster wall-clock training with negligible FID degradation.

## Results

On ImageNet 256, MF-RAE achieves a 1-step FID of 2.03 and 2-step FID of 1.89, outperforming vanilla MeanFlow (3.43/2.20) and AlphaFlow (2.58/1.95) while using only ~100 H100 GPU-days of total training (vs. 600+ for vanilla MF). Total generation cost is reduced by 38% (263 vs. 424 GFLOPS for 1-step) thanks to the lightweight RAE decoder. On ImageNet 512, MF-RAE attains a competitive 1-step FID of 3.23 with the lowest sampling GFLOPS (1051) among all compared flow map models, at a total training cost of only 17 H100 GPU-days.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MF-RAE decisively outperforms all prior few-step flow map models on ImageNet 256: it improves 1-step FID from 3.43 (MeanFlow) to 2.03, from 2.58 (AlphaFlow) to 2.03, and from 3.34 (CMT w/ MF) to 2.03, while also beating iCT (34.24), IMM (7.77), and Shortcut (10.60) by wide margins. On ImageNet 512, MF-RAE (FID 3.23) beats CMT w/ECD (3.38), AYF (3.32), and sCT (4.29), but trails sCD (2.28) by ~1.0 FID; however, MF-RAE achieves this at 1051 GFLOPS versus sCD's 2344 GFLOPS, and with 17 H100 GPU-days of training versus sCD's 233 days. Compared to the multi-step RAE diffusion model (FID 1.51 at 50 NFE), MF-RAE trades a modest FID increase (2.03) for a 50× reduction in network evaluations. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that removing guidance from SD-VAE-based MF degrades FID from 3.15 to 5.94, whereas MF-RAE requires no guidance at all, highlighting the RAE latent space as the key enabler of guidance-free class-conditional generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three recent threads: MeanFlow (MF) [11], which learns long PF-ODE jumps for few-step generation; the Representation Autoencoder (RAE) [48], which replaces SD-VAE with a frozen pretrained encoder (e.g., DINOv2) plus a lightweight ViT decoder; and Consistency Mid-Training (CMT) [16], which provides trajectory-aware initialization for flow map models. The key departure from vanilla MF is the shift from SD-VAE latents to RAE latents, which the authors show fundamentally changes the optimization landscape (causing gradient explosion without CMT) while simultaneously eliminating the need for classifier-free guidance. The paper also contributes a theoretical bias-variance decomposition (Proposition 3.1) of the MFD-vs-MFT training trade-off, which had not been formally analyzed before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: an 83% reduction in training cost (100 vs. 600+ H100 GPU-days), a 38% reduction in sampling GFLOPS, and a better 1-step FID (2.03 vs. 3.43) on ImageNet 256, all while removing guidance hyperparameters that previously required extensive grid search. This makes few-step flow map generation significantly more accessible and easier to deploy. Open questions include whether the CMT initialization requirement generalizes to other representation spaces or modalities, whether the optional MFT bootstrapping stage becomes necessary as teacher quality degrades, and whether the approach extends to video or other high-dimensional generation tasks. The finding that RAE latents enable high-quality unguided class-conditional MF generation suggests the semantic richness of pretrained encoders may be a more important factor for few-step models than previously appreciated.
