## Problem

Current text-to-image generative models produce display-referred, tone-mapped images that discard the full dynamic range needed for professional post-processing. No prior work addresses direct generation of scene-referred linear images from text, and the high dynamic range and bit depth of linear images exceed the representational capacity of standard VAEs used in latent diffusion models, making naive adaptation infeasible.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260441/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces a scene-referred linear image (camera-independent RGB with full dynamic range) along with a scalar radiance scale that recovers true scene irradiance. The linear image is internally represented as four exposure brackets at EV = {-4, -2, 0, 2}, each clipped to [0,1], which are hierarchically fused at inference into a single high-dynamic-range output. Training data consists of ~25k RAW images from RAISE and self-collected captures, captioned with Qwen2.5-VL, and evaluated on the MIT-Adobe FiveK benchmark.

## Method

The core insight is to decompose a linear image into a sequence of exposure brackets (analogous to bracketing in photography), each fitting within the VAE's representable range, thereby circumventing the dynamic-range bottleneck of a single high-bit-depth frame. A DiT-based flow-matching architecture built on Flux generates all four bracket latents jointly, augmented with Exposure Modulation Self-Attention for cross-bracket structural consistency and 3D-RoPE positional encoding that disambiguates spatial location from bracket identity. A dedicated radiance-scale token (discretized into 20 log-space bins) is jointly denoised with image tokens under a restricted attention mask (attending only to text and the EV0 bracket), enabling explicit prediction of scene radiance. Training uses LoRA fine-tuning (rank 64) on the Flux backbone with a composite loss combining flow-matching velocity, radiance-scale velocity, and a pixel-space bracket-consistency term that enforces correct multiplicative radiance ratios across exposures. At inference, the four decoded brackets are fused hierarchically from brightest to darkest using per-channel non-saturation masks and brightness-alignment ratios to reconstruct the final linear image.

## Results

On the Adobe FiveK test set, the proposed method achieves the best FID (28.29), aesthetic score (5.700), NIQE (3.658), and linear score (LS 23.06) among all baselines. The closest competitor, CameraCtrl with fine-tuning, reaches FID 37.25 and LS 8.97, while direct Flux LoRA finetuning yields FID 32.12 but cannot produce a meaningful LS score. The method also attains the highest CLIP similarity (26.02) among methods that produce valid multi-exposure brackets, trailing only CameraCtrl (26.89) on that single metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on FID, aesthetic score, NIQE, and linear score (LS). Compared to direct Flux LoRA finetuning (FID 32.12, AS 4.712), the exposure-bracket approach reduces FID by ~3.8 points and improves AS by nearly 1.0, while also producing a valid LS of 23.06 where Flux cannot span the full dynamic range. Against Wan 2.1 T2V finetuning, the gap is most dramatic on LS (23.06 vs. 1.12), reflecting Wan's 4× temporal downsampling that entangles brackets into a single latent. CameraCtrl with fine-tuning is the strongest competitor, achieving a slightly higher CLIP similarity (26.89 vs. 26.02), but the proposed method still leads on FID (28.29 vs. 37.25), AS (5.700 vs. 5.230), NIQE (3.658 vs. 4.131), and LS (23.06 vs. 8.97). Generative Photography, both with and without fine-tuning, shows the weakest overall performance, with FID above 40 and LS below 7.5, confirming that simply inflating an image diffusion model with temporal modules is insufficient for linear image synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Flux DiT flow-matching backbone and LoRA fine-tuning, but departs fundamentally from both HDR generation (GlowGAN, Bracket Diffusion, LEDiff) and RAW reconstruction (UPI, CycleISP, RAWDiffusion) by formulating text-to-linear-image generation as a new task. The key conceptual departure is representing a linear image as a sequence of exposure brackets rather than a single high-dynamic-range frame, directly circumventing VAE representational limits. The radiance-scale token denoising mechanism and 3D-RoPE for bracket disentanglement are novel architectural contributions not present in prior diffusion or flow-matching work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to generate scene-referred linear images from text, bridging a gap between AI generation and professional photography workflows where exposure, white balance, and tone editing require full dynamic range. The exposure-bracketing decomposition is a practical, photography-inspired solution to the VAE dynamic-range bottleneck that could generalize to other high-bit-depth generation tasks. Limitations include reliance on scarce RAW training data (~25k images), a fixed set of 4 exposure brackets, and baselines that are adapted rather than purpose-built, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger datasets or more extreme dynamic ranges. Downstream applications (linear-space editing, ControlNet conditioning) demonstrate practical utility but remain early-stage.
