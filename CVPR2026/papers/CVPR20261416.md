## Problem

Existing HOI benchmarks such as HICO-DET rely on exact-match evaluation under incomplete annotations, penalizing valid but unlabeled interactions and making cross-paradigm comparison between general-purpose VLMs and HOI-specific methods unreliable. This raises the open question of whether standalone VLMs can perform HOI detection competitively against specialized methods, which cannot be answered with current evaluation protocols.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261416/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each question presents an RGB image with exactly four candidate HOI interaction options (randomized order) and asks the model to select all correct ones, allowing multiple positive answers per question. In Setting 1 the model must also produce person bounding boxes (IoU ≥ 0.5 with ground truth) before answering; in Setting 2 the target person's box is provided to isolate recognition; in Setting 3 the model identifies interactions for all persons in the scene. Evaluation uses set-based metrics (Instance-F1, Macro-F1, Micro-F1, Exact Match) comparing predicted label sets against ground-truth sets rather than single-label matching.

## Method

CrossHOI-Bench reformulates HOI detection as a multiple-answer, multiple-choice task where each question explicitly defines annotated positives and curated negatives, eliminating false negatives from incomplete annotations. The construction pipeline proceeds in three stages: (1) coarse screening via a sequential multi-VLM pipeline (GPT-4.1 for semantic consistency filtering, then Qwen2.5-VL-32B and GPT-4o in agreement) to generate negative candidates; (2) manual refinement that removes overly simple head-class scenes, adds hard negatives (surrounding-person interactions, fine-grained action distinctions), and permits multiple valid positives per question; (3) redistribution of the test set to reduce train-test distribution overlap (KL divergence increases from 0.088 to 0.629). The main benchmark contains 1,274 images from HICO-DET covering 600 HOI classes, supplemented by V-COCO-based (647 questions, multi-person) and SWiG-HOI-based (1,852 questions, human-human) sub-benchmarks. Three complementary evaluation settings decouple detection, recognition, and multi-person attribution, enabling targeted diagnosis of where each paradigm fails.

## Results

On the full HOI detection pipeline (Setting 1), Qwen2.5-VL-32B achieves the best Instance-F1 of 52.94% and Macro-F1 of 50.71%, surpassing the strongest HOI-specific method (CMD-SE: 44.66% Instance-F1, 47.49% Macro-F1) by +8.28% and +3.22% respectively. In the recognition-only setting (Setting 2, with person box provided), the gap widens: Qwen2.5-VL-32B reaches 69.52% Instance-F1 and 62.90% Macro-F1, outperforming the best HOI-specific method (CMD-SE: 57.25% Instance-F1) by +12.27%. In the multi-person image-level setting (Setting 3), Qwen2.5-VL-32B still leads with 67.19% Instance-F1 versus 57.25% for CMD-SE, though EM accuracy drops 5–10 points relative to Setting 2, revealing difficulty in disentangling concurrent interactions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among HOI-specific methods, CMD-SE leads on Macro-F1 (47.49%) and EM (20.33%) in Setting 1, while ADA-CM achieves the highest Instance-F1 (47.76%); all five remain below 50% on most metrics. Qwen2.5-VL-32B, the strongest VLM, outperforms every HOI-specific baseline on Instance-F1 (+5.18% over ADA-CM) and Macro-F1 (+3.22% over CMD-SE) in Setting 1, and the margin expands to +12.27% Instance-F1 in Setting 2 where detection is removed. InternVL3-38B is the second-best VLM (38.68% Instance-F1 in Setting 1, 67.41% in Setting 2), still trailing Qwen2.5-VL-32B by 14–21 points. Small 7B VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LLaVA-OV-7B) are roughly on par with HOI-specific methods in Setting 2 (57.25% vs. 57.25% Instance-F1) but collapse in Setting 1 due to poor detection recall, with LLaVA-OV-7B and Qwen2-VL-7B producing no valid boxes at all. A consistent pattern across all VLMs is high precision (75–100%) paired with substantially lower recall (17–67%), indicating they predict only the most salient action and miss co-occurring interactions, whereas HOI-specific methods show the opposite gap (precision ~73–78%, recall ~47–52%) driven by low-confidence predictions in complex scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the exact-match evaluation paradigm that has defined HOI detection benchmarks since HICO-DET (2018), V-COCO, and SWiG-HOI, arguing that incomplete annotations systematically penalize valid predictions—especially from generative VLMs that naturally produce multiple plausible descriptions. Rather than building on a specific detection architecture, it repurposes the multiple-choice question format (common in VQA and NLP evaluation) to create a controlled evaluation space with curated negatives, enabling unified comparison between HOI-specific methods (ADA-CM, CMMP, HOLa, CMD-SE, LAIN) and general-purpose VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3, LLaVA-OV) that have never been directly comparable under a single protocol. The dataset construction pipeline, which combines multi-VLM coarse screening with manual refinement, is a methodological contribution distinct from prior HOI dataset curation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that the apparent large gap between VLMs (~15% mAP) and HOI-specific methods (~50% mAP) on HICO-DET is substantially an artifact of evaluation methodology rather than a true capability gap, which reframes how the community should interpret prior results. The complementary failure patterns—VLMs missing co-occurring actions and misattributing interactions across nearby people, while HOI-specific models struggle with general HOI reasoning and out-of-distribution generalization—point toward hybrid architectures that combine VLM reasoning with structured spatial modeling. Open questions include whether the multiple-choice format fully captures open-ended HOI understanding at scale (thousands of interaction classes), whether cross-person attribution can be resolved through better attention mechanisms or explicit person grounding, and whether the benchmark's curated negatives generalize beyond the HICO-DET label space.
