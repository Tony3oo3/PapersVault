## Problem

Conventional graph-based representations cannot establish a unified formulation for the heterogeneous faces and edges in B-rep CAD models, which constrains generative models from handling the strong geometry-topology coupling, extreme sparsity of B-rep graphs, and continuous node feature reconstruction simultaneously. No prior variational graph autoencoder could holistically encode and decode complete B-rep structures from a single latent representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260766/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes B-rep models as sparse homogeneous graphs where both faces and edges are nodes (up to 30 faces/90 edges on DeepCAD, 50/150 on ABC), with per-node features derived from UV-sampled face point clouds and 1D curve-sampled edge point clouds, plus a one-dimensional type indicator. The decoder produces, from a single 256-dimensional latent vector, a sparse adjacency matrix (face-edge and edge-edge blocks), a node type classification, and continuous geometric feature vectors for all nodes, which are then assembled into a watertight 3D B-rep solid via boundary-loop traversal, spline fitting, and G2-continuous surface bridging.

## Method

BrepVGAE unifies heterogeneous B-rep primitives by representing both faces and edges as nodes in a single sparse isomorphic graph, then encodes it with a two-layer sparse GNN and multi-head attention pooling into one 256-dim latent vector via reparameterization. Decoding avoids autoregressive generation through a multi-slot learnable query mechanism: N_max queries interact with expanded key-value pairs from the latent via two stages of cross-attention and FFN, followed by type-conditioned projections to yield per-node features. Topology is reconstructed in one pass using symmetric bilinear scoring layers (with explicit symmetry enforcement for edge-edge connections and label smoothing), while a dedicated classification head infers node types. Separate high-fidelity point cloud autoencoders (conv-transposed for faces, 1D residual blocks with dilated convolutions for edges) handle geometric reconstruction, and a two-stage training curriculum first freezes geometry to learn topology, then linearly warms up geometric losses to couple both without early interference. Feature alignment uses Hungarian matching with a degree convergence penalty that enforces the physical prior that each edge connects to exactly two faces.

## Results

On the DeepCAD dataset, BrepVGAE (Hungarian matching variant) achieves 79.82% Coverage, 72.6% Validity, and 1.21 JSD, outperforming all baselines on the two most critical generation metrics. For geometric reconstruction, the face autoencoder reduces Chamfer Distance by 51.8% (0.619 vs. 1.285) and JSD by 53.8% (0.102 vs. 0.221) relative to BrepGen's surface VAE, while the edge autoencoder achieves 35.4% lower CD and 40.5% lower JSD. On the larger ABC dataset (up to 50 faces / 150 edges), BrepVGAE still leads BrepGen by 9.18 points in Coverage (73.51% vs. 64.33%) and 5.9 points in Validity (63.1% vs. 57.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against BrepGen, BrepVGAE (*H) wins decisively on Coverage (79.82% vs. 73.87%), JSD (1.21 vs. 1.28), and Validity (72.6% vs. 62.9%), but trails on MMD (1.13 vs. 1.04) and Novelty/Uniqueness (98.2/98.1% vs. 99.8/99.7%). Against SolidGen, the gap widens: BrepVGAE leads by 8.79 points in Coverage, 0.10 in JSD, and 12.3 points in Validity, while SolidGen retains a slight MMD edge (1.08 vs. 1.13). Against DeepCAD, BrepVGAE dominates every metric, most notably Validity (72.6% vs. 46.1%) and Coverage (79.82% vs. 65.46%). In the reconstruction sub-task, BrepVGAE's specialized face and edge autoencoders outperform BrepGen's VAEs on all three geometric metrics (CD, MMD, JSD) for both surfaces and edges, with the largest margin on surface JSD (0.102 vs. 0.221). On the ABC dataset, BrepVGAE maintains its Coverage and Validity lead over BrepGen (73.51% vs. 64.33%; 63.1% vs. 57.2%) despite the increased structural complexity, though BrepGen retains a marginal MMD advantage (1.53 vs. 1.62).

## Relation to Prior Work

BrepVGAE sits at the intersection of variational graph autoencoders (VGAE, ARVGAE, GraphVAE) and direct B-rep CAD generation (SolidGen, BrepGen, DTGBrepGen). It departs from prior CAD generators in two key ways: rather than autoregressively emitting vertices/edges/faces (SolidGen) or decoupling topology from geometry in separate phases (DTGBrepGen), it encodes both faces and edges as nodes in a single sparse isomorphic graph and decodes the entire structure—adjacency matrix plus continuous per-node features—from one global latent vector via a parallel set decoder with bilinear topology heads. The set-decoding paradigm borrows from object-detection-style query mechanisms, while the sparse GNN encoder and two-stage geometry-topology curriculum are tailored to the extreme sparsity and heterogeneous coupling of B-rep graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single 256-dim latent vector can drive joint reconstruction of B-rep topology and geometry with 72.6% validity (vs. 62.9% for BrepGen), the paper validates a unified-latent formulation for CAD generation that could simplify downstream tasks like part retrieval and feature recognition, as shown by the L2-distance retrieval experiment. However, the evaluation is constrained to relatively simple models (≤30 faces / ≤90 edges on DeepCAD, ≤50 / ≤150 on ABC) with NURBS surfaces excluded, leaving open whether the approach scales to industrially complex parts. The ~27% invalidity rate and the reliance on Hungarian matching for evaluation (which penalizes topologically valid but structurally different outputs) suggest that robustness to open-ended generation and handling of free-form surfaces remain significant open problems.
