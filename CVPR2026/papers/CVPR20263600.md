## Problem

Existing table structure recognition (TSR) models struggle with complex, long tables containing merged cells and nested structures, while most high-accuracy approaches are too large for on-device deployment. Additionally, standard TEDS metrics can mask per-table structural errors, and annotated training data remains scarce and limited in visual diversity. PIX-TAB targets the gap of achieving pixel-precise, language-agnostic TSR with a compact model fast enough for mobile inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263600/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single RGB table image (normalized to 480 px height) and produces a PAPP-OTSL token sequence in which row-start tokens <rYYY> and column-boundary tokens <cXXX> encode explicit pixel coordinates, allowing deterministic reconstruction of all cell bounding boxes without a separate detection head at inference. An external, swappable OCR model supplies cell text, and the final output is assembled as HTML. The architecture is language-agnostic: supporting a new script requires only replacing the OCR module.

## Method

PIX-TAB uses a ResNet-31-D encoder with Global Context Blocks feeding a two-layer shared transformer decoder that branches into a StructDecoder (predicting PAPP-OTSL tokens) and a training-only BboxDecoder (providing L1 box supervision for spatial grounding, removed at inference). The key architectural insight is embedding pixel coordinates directly into the token vocabulary (PAPP tokens), which makes the sequence highly regular across rows and enables a rule-based speculative decoding algorithm that proposes up to K=10 future tokens per step, verified in a single forward pass, cutting decoder steps by 1.5–3×. A parallel Region-Based Image Segmentation module (flood-fill with density and size filtering) handles large bordered tables, and a hybrid selection rule (γ=0.7 row/column count ratio) picks the better of the two outputs. Training uses cross-entropy on tokens plus normalized L1 box loss with the Ranger optimizer, and a synthetic data pipeline extending Wikipedia HTML tables with varied CSS styles generates over one million additional training images.

## Results

On FinTabNet, PIX-TAB achieves TEDS_struct of 98.72% (without RBIS) and 98.65% (with RBIS) at 480px input resolution, with inference speeds of 7.96 and 7.23 FPS respectively on an NVIDIA A100 40GB. Speculative decoding alone accelerates decoding by 1.5× on FinTabNet and up to 3× on PubTabNet with negligible accuracy loss. On a Samsung Fold 5 mobile device, the optimized model runs 3.0–3.7× faster than the full model (6.6 s vs. 19.9 s average) while retaining TEDS_struct of 96.64% vs. 97.26%, and outperforms NCGM on both accuracy (96.01% vs. 95.4% TEDS) and speed (6.6 s vs. 9.1 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PIX-TAB (98.72% TEDS_struct without RBIS) is competitive but trails the top accuracy leaders: Zhu et al. [43] (99.04%), UniTable [26] and BGTR [7] (both 98.89%), MuTabNet [9] (98.87%), and Ly & Takasu [20] (98.85%). It essentially ties TABLET [6] (98.71%) on accuracy while operating at a smaller input resolution (480 vs. 960 px), though TABLET is substantially faster at 18.01 FPS on an A100 80GB versus PIX-TAB's 7.96 FPS on an A100 40GB. PIX-TAB clearly outperforms VAST [8] (98.63%, 1.38 FPS), GridFormer [22] (98.63%), TableMaster [36] (98.32%), TSRFormer DQ-DETR [34] (98.40%, 4.17 FPS), SEM [38] (97.10%), RobusTabNet [23] (97.00%), TableFormer [24] (96.80%), and SPRINT [10] (98.03%) on both accuracy and/or speed. On the mobile benchmark (Table 3), the optimized PIX-TAB model surpasses NCGM [15] in both TEDS (96.01% vs. 95.4%) and average inference time (6.6 s vs. 9.1 s) on a Samsung Fold 5, validating its on-device deployment advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

PIX-TAB extends the MTL-TabNet multi-task architecture (Ly & Takasu, 2023) by replacing its separate bounding-box decoder with Position-Aware Pixel-Precise (PAPP) tokens that embed row/column coordinates directly into the OTSL sequence, eliminating the need for a dedicated box head at inference. The speculative decoding mechanism adapts the LLM inference technique of Leviathan et al. (2023) to table structure recognition, but replaces the learned draft model with a purely rule-based algorithm that exploits the high regularity of PAPP-OTSL row patterns. A region-based flood-fill segmentation module is introduced as a classical complement to the neural encoder-decoder, combined via a hybrid selection strategy—a design choice that departs from the purely learned pipelines dominant in recent transformer-based TSR work (TableFormer, TRUST, TABLET).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most state-of-the-art TSR models are too large for on-device deployment, and the proposed mobile-optimized variant achieves 3× speedup with only ~0.6% TEDS_struct loss, making real-time table parsing feasible on smartphones. The introduction of TEDS_100 (proportion of perfectly recognized tables) exposes that high average TEDS_struct scores (97–99%) mask a 10–20% failure rate on individual tables, a limitation of current evaluation practice. Open questions remain: speculative decoding is ineffective for tables with unique row patterns, RBIS degrades on irregular or borderless layouts, and overall accuracy is tightly coupled to the external OCR model's quality—none of which are resolved here.
