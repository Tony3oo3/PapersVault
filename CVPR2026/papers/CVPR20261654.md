## Problem

Neural video codecs achieve superior compression ratios over traditional codecs like H.266/VTM, but their prohibitive computational complexity—driven by frame-by-frame sequential processing, explicit motion vector estimation, and multi-step entropy coding—remains a critical barrier to real-world deployment. Existing NVCs that adopt hierarchical-B coding still rely on costly motion pipelines and rigid pre-defined coding orders, limiting the achievable rate-distortion-complexity trade-off.

## Input / Output

The method takes a non-overlapping chunk of N consecutive video frames (N=8 for high-throughput mode, N=1 for low-delay mode) along with a quantization parameter, and produces a single compact quantized latent that is arithmetically encoded into a bit-stream. On the decoding side, the bit-stream is parsed in one consolidated arithmetic decoding step, and N reconstructed frames are produced simultaneously by parallel frame-specific decoders. The chunk encoder is conditioned on a propagated temporal context from the previous chunk, enabling inter-chunk dependency without explicit motion vectors.

## Method

DCVC-UF replaces frame-by-frame hierarchical-B coding with a chunk-based paradigm: all N frames in a chunk are jointly encoded into one compact latent via cross-frame interaction modules that implicitly model spatial-temporal correlations, then decoded in parallel by N frame-specific decoders (analogous to a Mixture-of-Experts design where each decoder specializes in its temporal position). A streamlined entropy model decouples scale and mean estimation: scales for all four quadtree partitions are predicted in a single forward pass, consolidating arithmetic decoding into one bit-stream interaction step, while means retain four-step progressive estimation but execute entirely on GPU without bit-stream access. Feature propagation in the latent space across chunks captures long-term temporal correlations, and the compact single-latent-per-chunk design reduces training memory enough to fine-tune on 128-frame sequences, further improving distribution estimation. The architecture is built on CNN depth-wise convolution blocks with patch embedding to 1/8 spatial resolution, trained first on 7-frame Vimeo-90K clips and then fine-tuned on longer sequences.

## Results

DCVC-UF (HT-L) achieves an average BD-Rate saving of 42.2% over VTM (LD) across six test sequences (HEVC B–E, UVG, MCL-JCV), while delivering 371.1 encoding and 273.6 decoding FPS on a 4090 GPU for 1080p video. The smaller DCVC-UF (HT-S) variant saves 31.6% bitrate at 655.9/453.3 FPS, and the low-delay DCVC-UF (LD) saves 9.5% at 313.6/353.8 FPS. On the B200 GPU, DCVC-UF (HT-S) reaches a record 1415.1 encoding and 945.8 decoding FPS for 1080p, demonstrating strong hardware scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCVC-UF (HT-L) outperforms VTM (Hierarchical-B, GOP 32) by 8.4 percentage points in average BD-Rate (−42.2% vs −33.8%) while incurring only 7 frames of delay versus VTM's 31; if VTM is constrained to the same GOP 8, its saving drops to 23.7%, widening the gap to 18.5 points. Against the previous SOTA neural codec DCVC-RT (−21.0%), DCVC-UF (HT-L) more than doubles the bitrate saving while running 3.1× faster in encoding (371.1 vs 118.8 FPS) and 2.6× faster in decoding (273.6 vs 105.3 FPS). Compared to DCVC-FM (−21.3%), the speedup is over 100× in encoding (371.1 vs 3.7 FPS) with a 20.9-point BD-Rate improvement. DCVC-UF (HT-S) matches VTM (Hierarchical-B, GOP 32) in compression (−31.6% vs −33.8%) at 655.9 encoding FPS versus VTM's 0.01 FPS—a 65,000× speed advantage. In the low-delay setting, DCVC-UF (LD) sacrifices some compression (−9.5% vs DCVC-RT's −21.0%) but achieves 2.6× faster decoding (353.8 vs 105.3 FPS) and 2.6× faster encoding (313.6 vs 118.8 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DCVC series (DCVC-RT, DCVC-DC, DCVC-FM) by replacing the frame-by-frame hierarchical-B coding paradigm with a chunk-based framework that encodes N frames into a single compact latent and decodes them in parallel via frame-specific decoders. It amplifies the motion-vector-free design of DCVC-RT and builds on the spatial-temporal autoencoder concept from video generation, but adds cross-frame interaction modules and conditional coding across chunks for long-term temporal modeling. The streamlined entropy model departs from the quadtree partition approach of DCVC-DC by decoupling scale and mean estimation, consolidating bit-stream interactions into a single arithmetic decoding step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DCVC-UF achieves a practical breakthrough in neural video codec deployment: 371–656 FPS encoding and 274–453 FPS decoding at 1080p on a 4090 GPU while matching or exceeding VTM (Hierarchical-B) compression, a regime previously unreachable by NVCs. The scalability argument—that NVCs automatically benefit from future GPU generations without hardware-specific re-engineering, unlike traditional codecs requiring bespoke silicon—has significant implications for large-scale transcoding and cloud video workloads. Open questions include whether adaptive chunk sizing can handle content with varying temporal complexity, whether longer training sequences (beyond 128 frames) further close the gap with VTM at high quality (>40 dB), and whether cross-chunk pipeline parallelism can push throughput even higher.
