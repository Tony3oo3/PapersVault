## Problem

Conventional 3D Gaussian Splatting implicitly assumes consistent exposure and tone mapping across all views, but real driving data violates this due to heterogeneous ISP pipelines, per-camera exposure control, and varying outdoor illumination. This entangles camera-specific photometric distortions into the learned radiance field, producing illumination mismatches, color shifts, and seam artifacts in static backgrounds critical for autonomous-driving simulation. Prior 3DGS extensions focus on dynamic-object modeling or 2D appearance corrections but leave cross-view photometric inconsistency unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only standard LDR multi-view images (no HDR supervision or multi-exposure pairs required) along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics and an SfM point cloud for 3D initialization. It produces a view-invariant linear HDR radiance field encoded as 3D Gaussians with SH coefficients, plus per-view scalar exposure scales and per-view gamma tone-mapping parameters. At inference, exposure and tone parameters are averaged across all training views to stabilize temporal luminance, and the HDR radiance is converted to LDR through the estimated photometric pipeline for rendering.

## Method

P2GS reformulates 3DGS optimization in the linear HDR domain, where each Gaussian's SH coefficients represent physically meaningful radiance rather than LDR color. The key mechanism is the Principle of Invariant Radiance (PIR), which states that in the linear HDR domain the ratio of observed radiances across any two views equals the ratio of their exposure scales, providing a direct constraint that decouples scene radiance from camera photometry. A per-view exposure scalar and a learnable gamma tone-mapping function are jointly optimized with the radiance field under a unified loss combining photometric reconstruction (L1 + DSSIM in LDR space), a relative-exposure consistency term that enforces linear HDR radiance ratios across view pairs, and regularization terms that anchor global exposure scale, penalize inter-view exposure variance, and center gamma near the sRGB prior. This fully differentiable pipeline requires no HDR ground truth and preserves the real-time rasterization efficiency of standard 3DGS while yielding an exposure-invariant radiance field.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset, P2GS achieves the best SSIM (0.939), LPIPS (0.209), HIS (0.092), and Std-Luminance (0.034) among all methods, while running at 90 FPS. Compared to PVG, P2GS improves SSIM by 6.9%, reduces LPIPS by 37.7%, and cuts HIS by 74.7%. On the CARLA dataset under severe inter-camera exposure noise (ISO std4), P2GS degrades only 0.85 dB in PSNR for reconstruction and 0.95 dB for NVS, versus 2.20 dB and 3.79 dB for vanilla 3DGS, confirming its robustness to illumination variation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P2GS outperforms vanilla 3DGS on SSIM (0.939 vs. 0.928), LPIPS (0.209 vs. 0.230), HIS (0.092 vs. 0.102), and Std-Luminance (0.034 vs. 0.041), though 3DGS retains a PSNR advantage (33.62 vs. 31.02) because it reproduces pixel-level sensor noise. Against PVG, P2GS leads on every metric with particularly large gains in photometric consistency (HIS: 0.092 vs. 0.365, a 74.7% reduction). The affine-transform baseline (3DGS+AT) is the closest competitor, matching P2GS on Std-Luminance (0.035 vs. 0.034) and slightly ahead on PSNR (31.45 vs. 31.02), but P2GS still wins on SSIM, LPIPS, and HIS. Luminance-GS, a neural per-view correction method, degrades severely on large-scale driving scenes (PSNR 17.68, HIS 0.497), confirming that 2D-space corrections do not generalize to sparse outdoor viewpoints. On CARLA under ISO std4 noise, P2GS maintains SSIM 0.847 and PSNR 22.91 for reconstruction, while Luminance-GS collapses to SSIM 0.387 and PSNR 11.43, and 3DGS+AT drops 3.17 dB in PSNR versus P2GS's 0.85 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

P2GS extends standard 3D Gaussian Splatting by shifting the optimization from LDR pixel fitting into the linear HDR domain, where scene radiance can be disentangled from per-view exposure and tone-mapping. It departs from prior HDR-GS approaches (GaussHDR, SeHDR, LuminanceGS) that either require multi-exposure supervision, operate in 2D correction space, or lack a physically grounded formulation. In the driving-specific 3DGS lineage (StreetGS, DrivingGaussian, PVG), P2GS addresses a gap those methods leave open: they model dynamics and scale but implicitly assume photometric consistency across cameras, which real driving data violates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

P2GS provides a practical path to exposure-invariant 3D scene representations from only LDR inputs, which is essential for closed-loop driving simulators that must render consistent backgrounds under arbitrary camera settings. The Principle of Invariant Radiance gives a clean, differentiable constraint that avoids the instability of prior HDR-NeRF variants. Key limitations remain: the method does not separate intrinsic material properties from external illumination (no relighting), and its compatibility with dynamic Gaussian extensions is stated but not demonstrated. Whether the HDR-domain formulation generalizes to highly dynamic or weather-varying scenes is an open question.
