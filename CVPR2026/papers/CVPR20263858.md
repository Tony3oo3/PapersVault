## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods optimize global distributional proximity but do not explicitly model the hierarchical nature of object semantics, where structures at different scales (global layout, mid-level parts, fine textures) jointly support recognition. This paper asks whether amplifying class-relevant semantics at each generation scale of a coarse-to-fine autoregressive model can produce more effective distilled data without directly matching global proximity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263858/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained VAR model (depth 16, 256×256 resolution, VQ-VAE codebook of 4096) fine-tuned for 5 epochs with per-scale class tokens, along with class labels for the classification objective. It outputs synthetic images at the target IPC (1, 10, 50, or 100 per class) that constitute the distilled dataset, which is then used to train downstream classifiers (ResNet-18/101, MobileNet-V2, EfficientNet-B0) on CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-Woof, ImageNet-100, and ImageNet-1K.

## Method

HIERAMP injects a learnable class token at each of the 9 non-trivial VAR scales, constrained by a scale-restricted attention mask so it attends only to same-scale image tokens, and trains each token with a lightweight classifier. During autoregressive decoding, the class-token attention map at scale n serves as a semantic saliency map; the top-ρ_n% salient positions are identified and a positive logit bias β_n is added to their key columns for all queries, steering attention toward object-relevant regions. This amplification is applied with a stage-aware schedule across Coarse (scales 1–3), Mid (4–6), and Fine (7–9) stages. The key empirical finding is that coarse-stage amplification increases token entropy and coverage (more diverse layouts), while fine-stage amplification concentrates token usage (sharper details), and that coarse-stage amplification yields the largest accuracy gains, indicating that global structural richness most strongly influences downstream performance.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (IPC=10, ResNet-18), HIERAMP achieves 47.6% top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline CaO2 (46.1%) by 1.5% and D3HR/Minimax (44.3%) by 3.3%. At IPC=50, it reaches 60.8%, and at IPC=100, 62.7%, consistently leading all four baselines. On CIFAR-10 (IPC=10, ResNet-18), HIERAMP scores 44.3% versus D3HR's 41.3% and RDED's 37.1%. On ImageNet-Woof (IPC=50), it achieves 70.0%, surpassing CaO2 (68.9%) and RDED (68.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HIERAMP outperforms all four baselines (Minimax, D3HR, RDED, CaO2) on the majority of dataset–IPC configurations. The largest margins appear on CIFAR-10 (IPC=10: +3.0% over D3HR, +7.2% over RDED) and ImageNet-Woof (IPC=10: +7.3% over RDED, +5.7% over Minimax). On the most challenging ImageNet-1K benchmark, HIERAMP leads CaO2 by 1.5% at IPC=10 and by 0.8% at IPC=50, while the gap over RDED widens to 5.6% and 4.3% respectively. At IPC=100 on ImageNet-1K, HIERAMP edges out D3HR by 0.2% (62.7% vs. 62.5%). The only near-tie is ImageNet-Woof IPC=10, where HIERAMP (45.8%) barely surpasses CaO2 (45.6%) by 0.2%, suggesting that at very small IPC the advantage of hierarchical amplification is most pronounced when more samples per class are available.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Visual Autoregressive (VAR) model's coarse-to-fine generation paradigm and adapts DINO-style learnable class tokens to probe scale-specific semantics. It departs from the dominant dataset distillation literature—which has focused on global distributional proximity via gradient matching, trajectory matching, or diffusion-based synthesis—by arguing that hierarchical object semantics at different scales are underexplored and that amplifying attention toward semantically salient regions at each VAR scale yields better distilled data. Rather than introducing a new generative architecture, HIERAMP repurposes the existing VAR backbone with a lightweight attention-bias mechanism, positioning itself as a semantic complement to distributional matching rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic analysis of how token diversity (entropy) and utilization (coverage) shift across hierarchical scales when attention is amplified, offering interpretable diagnostics for dataset distillation that go beyond aggregate accuracy. The finding that coarse-scale amplification drives the largest accuracy gains—despite not directly encoding object-specific details—suggests that global structural richness is a more critical bottleneck than fine-grained texture in distilled datasets. Open questions include whether the class-token attention mechanism generalizes to non-VAR hierarchical generators (e.g., cascaded diffusion), whether the amplification schedule can be learned end-to-end rather than hand-tuned per stage, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D distillation where temporal/spatial hierarchies add further complexity.
