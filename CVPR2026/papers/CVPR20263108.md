## Problem

Roadside LiDAR sensors with fixed viewpoints suffer from long-term or severe occlusions that existing ego-vehicle or object-level completion methods cannot handle. Directly using noisy vision-based 3D detector outputs as supervision for occluded objects propagates substantial geometric errors into the completion model, and the resulting pose uncertainty has not been formally addressed as a probabilistic inference problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263108/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse roadside LiDAR point cloud and camera images processed by a pre-trained BEVHeight 3D object detector (yielding per-object 7-DoF poses with covariance and confidence scores) as input. It produces a completed full-scene point cloud via a diffusion sampler, along with corrected per-object poses and confidence-weighted pseudo-ground-truth point clouds derived from ShapeNet templates (1,854 vehicle templates, 1024 sampled points each). At inference, the confidence scores dynamically modulate classifier-free guidance strength (s ∈ [0, 3]) per object, allowing the sampler to trust well-refined poses more strongly than uncertain ones.

## Method

PDL decomposes the intractable joint posterior over scene and poses into two staged minimizations via ELBO factorization. Stage 1 (PPDM) refines each noisy detector pose through weighted Gauss-Newton iterations that jointly minimize a visual-prior term (governed by detector covariance Σ_det⁻¹) and a geometric-likelihood term (aligning ShapeNet template points to LiDAR observations, governed by sensor noise σ_ℓ²), producing pseudo-ground-truth and per-object confidence scores. Stage 2 (SDL) trains a DDPM with MinkUNet backbone conditioned on the sparse LiDAR and pseudo-ground-truth, augmented by two novel losses: a region discrepancy loss (confidence-weighted chamfer distance in occluded subdomains where the observation likelihood vanishes) and a global sampling discrepancy loss (importance-weighted by inverse local point density to counteract non-uniform LiDAR sampling). The full training objective combines the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss with both discrepancy proxies, and inference employs classifier-free guidance with confidence-scaled strength.

## Results

On V2X-Seq, PDL achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.29, a 17.1% reduction over the previous best diffusion-based method LiDPM (0.35), and the lowest 3D JSD of 0.48 (5.9% below LiDPM's 0.51). On the unseen TUMTraf-V2X dataset (zero-shot transfer), PDL attains CD 0.30 (11.8% below LiDPM's 0.34) and 3D JSD 0.47. Across both datasets, PDL also achieves the highest voxel IoU (55.9 / 43.7 at 0.5 m / 0.2 m on V2X-Seq; 55.4 / 44.1 on TUMTraf-V2X), yielding average reductions of 14.5% in CD and 6% in 3D JSD over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDL outperforms all five baselines on every metric across both V2X-Seq and TUMTraf-V2X. The closest competitor is LiDPM [22], the prior state-of-the-art diffusion-based method; PDL reduces CD by 17.1% (0.35→0.29) on V2X-Seq and 11.8% (0.34→0.30) on TUMTraf-V2X, while also improving 3D JSD by ~6% and voxel IoU by 3–5.6 points. LiDiff [23] is the second-strongest baseline (CD 0.37 on both datasets) but still trails PDL by 21–19% in CD. The non-diffusion baselines LODE [15] and PVD [40] are substantially weaker, with CD values of 0.83–0.96, roughly 3× worse than PDL. MID [28] occupies an intermediate position (CD 0.49–0.57), outperforming LODE and PVD but remaining 40–70% worse than PDL in Chamfer Distance. Notably, PDL achieves these gains while using only corrected visual detector outputs as supervision (rather than ground-truth labels), and generalizes zero-shot to the unseen TUMTraf-V2X dataset without fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based LiDAR scene completion line (LiDPM, LiDiff, PVD) from ego-vehicle and object-level settings to the roadside fixed-viewpoint regime, where long-term occlusions dominate. Its key departure is a two-stage variational-inference decomposition: a probabilistic pose discrepancy minimization (PPDM) that refines noisy BEVHeight detector outputs via weighted Gauss-Newton iterations against LiDAR geometry, followed by a DDPM-based scene discrepancy learning (SDL) conditioned on the corrected pseudo-ground-truth. Unlike prior methods that rely solely on ground-truth supervision or purely LiDAR-based priors, PDL explicitly incorporates external visual evidence and treats pose uncertainty as a probabilistic inference problem rather than a deterministic correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Roadside LiDAR is a critical sensing modality for intelligent transportation systems, yet fixed mounting positions create occlusion patterns that ego-vehicle-oriented completion methods cannot address. The practical relevance is high: the approach shows that a lightweight template-based Gauss-Newton step can substantially reduce pose errors from a SOTA visual detector, making the pipeline deployable without retraining the detector. Key limitations include dependence on ShapeNet templates (1,854 fixed vehicle shapes), camera-LiDAR extrinsic calibration accuracy, and the BEVHeight detector as an external dependency. The authors note that replacing fixed templates with learned category-level shape representations is the natural next step, and the cross-dataset generalization (TUMTraf-V2X without fine-tuning) suggests the learned completion prior is reasonably robust, though long-term scalability to diverse vehicle categories remains open.
