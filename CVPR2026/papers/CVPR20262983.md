## Problem

Current video datasets provide rich semantics but lack explicit 3D geometric ground truth, while spatial datasets offer accurate camera poses and depth but remain small, object-centric, or synthetic. This disconnect prevents models from learning physically grounded, spatiotemporally coherent world simulation at scale. The paper addresses this by introducing the first large-scale, real-world, dynamic video dataset that unifies dense geometric annotations with rich semantic and motion-aware textual descriptions.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests 21,789 hours of manually screened YouTube videos (33,443 clips) with diverse camera trajectories, segments them into 3–15 s clips, and standardizes all clips to 1280×720 H.265 MP4. Each of the resulting 2.7 million clips (7,089 hours) is annotated with per-frame camera extrinsics, metric depth maps, per-frame dynamic masks, a five-field structured caption (category tags, motion trends, scene description, camera description, shot summary), and a serialized sequence of cinematographic motion instructions (e.g., dolly in, pan left, truck right) derived from pose deltas. A balanced 1,111-hour subset (SpatialVID-HQ, 370K clips) is further curated by tightening quality thresholds and stratifying over motion type and scene category.

## Method

The curation pipeline has three stages. (1) Filtering applies four metrics—CLIP+MLP aesthetic scoring, VMAF-based motion intensity, PaddleOCR text-area ratio, and luminance bounds—to reduce 7M+ raw clips to motion-rich, visually clean candidates. (2) Annotation uses an enhanced MegaSaM pipeline (with UniDepth v2 and Depth Anything v2 replacing the original monocular depth module) for camera poses and depth; SAM2 prompted by adaptive-thresholding contour anchors extracts dynamic masks; an acceleration-based detector rejects non-physical trajectories; and three quantitative metrics (MoveDist, RotAngle, TrajTurns) characterize motion. Motion instructions are generated by temporally smoothing pose sequences, thresholding perceptible segments, and mapping them to a controlled cinematographic vocabulary. Structured captions are produced by a two-stage VLM+LLM pipeline: Gemini-2.0-Flash parses sampled frames for scene and motion descriptions, then Qwen3-30B-A3B refines the text using camera-pose priors to correct spatial directions and ensure coherence. (3) Sampling tightens quality thresholds and balances semantic tags and trajectory statistics to form SpatialVID-HQ. Validation experiments fine-tune Wan2.2 (with ReCamMaster camera-control injection) for camera-controlled video generation, GS-LRM for novel view synthesis, and CUT3R/VGGT for pose estimation, demonstrating consistent gains over models trained on RealEstate10K or Sekai-Real.

## Results

On the primary camera-controlled video generation task, models trained on SpatialVID-HQ achieve the best camera accuracy across all three benchmarks: TransErr of 7.42/6.04/4.33 and RotErr of 0.99/1.43/3.81 on RE10K, Sekai, and SpatialVID test sets respectively, outperforming models trained on RE10K and Sekai-Real. SpatialVID-HQ also yields the highest CLIP-T (text-video alignment) scores (30.54/35.19/30.26) and the best VBench Imaging Quality (75.68/73.13/70.38). For novel view synthesis, GS-LRM fine-tuned on SpatialVID-HQ improves PSNR by 0.79 dB on DL3DV (27.80 vs. 27.01) and 0.84 dB on SpatialVID (24.97 vs. 24.13) over the RE10K-trained model. Fine-tuning CUT3R and VGGT on SpatialVID reduces ATE on TUM-dynamics from 0.049→0.040 and 0.015→0.013 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For camera-controlled video generation, SpatialVID-HQ-trained models consistently outperform both RE10K-trained and Sekai-Real-trained models across all three test benchmarks, with the largest gains on the in-domain SpatialVID test set (TransErr 4.33 vs. 5.16 for RE10K and 5.63 for Sekai-Real; Imaging Quality 70.38 vs. 66.23 and 66.14). On the Sekai benchmark, SpatialVID-HQ also beats Sekai-Real (which is the in-domain training set for that benchmark) on TransErr (6.04 vs. 6.49) and CLIP-T (35.19 vs. 33.98), demonstrating cross-dataset generalization. For camera pose estimation, fine-tuning CUT3R on SpatialVID reduces TUM-dynamics ATE from 0.049 to 0.040 and RPE-rot from 0.449 to 0.395; VGGT similarly improves from ATE 0.015 to 0.013, though VGGT shows a slight ATE regression on Sintel (0.134→0.148). For novel view synthesis, GS-LRM trained on SpatialVID-HQ beats the RE10K-trained variant by 0.79 dB PSNR on DL3DV and 0.84 dB on SpatialVID, with corresponding SSIM and LPIPS improvements. In the quality comparison with Panda-70M (Fig. 5), over 80% of Panda-70M clips cannot be reconstructed by MegaSaM due to insufficient motion, whereas SpatialVID-HQ maintains a balanced distribution of trajectory turns and motion metrics, validating the manual curation pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpatialVID positions itself at the intersection of two previously disjoint dataset families: large-scale video corpora (Panda70M, MiraData) that offer rich semantics but no geometric ground truth, and spatial datasets (CO3D, RealEstate10K, TartanAir) that provide accurate camera parameters but are small, object-centric, or synthetic. For geometric annotation, the pipeline builds on MegaSaM, replacing its depth module with UniDepth v2 and Depth Anything v2, and uses SAM2 for dynamic mask extraction. For validation, the camera-controlled generation experiments adopt the ReCamMaster injection mechanism on a Wan2.2 backbone, while reconstruction and pose-estimation baselines draw from the DUSt3R/VGGT/CUT3R lineage. The key departure is the explicit unification of per-frame camera poses, depth maps, structured captions, and serialized motion instructions at a scale (2.7M clips, 7,089 hours) not previously achieved for real-world dynamic scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first large-scale, real-world, dynamic video dataset with both explicit 3D geometry and rich semantic annotations, SpatialVID directly addresses the data bottleneck for physically grounded video generation, world simulation, and feed-forward 3D reconstruction. The consistent (if moderate) gains across camera-controlled generation, novel view synthesis, and pose estimation when training on SpatialVID-HQ versus RE10K or Sekai-Real suggest that motion diversity and annotation quality matter as much as raw scale. Limitations remain: the annotation pipeline inherits MegaSaM's failure modes under object-dominated frames or severe distortion, predicted poses are non-metric in some scenarios, and dynamic masks degrade in complex scenes. Future work with improved pose estimators (e.g., ViPE) and larger-scale training will determine whether the dataset's current annotation ceiling can be raised.
