## Problem

Category-level 6D pose estimation and robotic grasping require large-scale, canonically aligned 3D asset collections, but existing datasets are bottlenecked by labor-intensive scanning (15–60 min per object), inconsistent mesh quality, and the absence of consistent cross-instance alignment. No prior framework could generate aligned 3D meshes at scale from scratch, forcing methods to rely on inter-category transfer rather than strong per-category learning.

## Input / Output

The pipeline accepts only a text category name (e.g., "mug") as input and produces 1,000 canonically aligned, textured 3D meshes per category in under 3 minutes each, along with rendered synthetic RGB-D datasets (up to 1.2M images) carrying full annotations: 6D poses, NOCS coordinate maps, instance and semantic masks, and depth. For grasping, the output additionally includes URDF files with V-HACD collision meshes and SAPIEN scene configurations (pile and packed layouts) ready for zero-shot sim-to-real evaluation.

## Method

The pipeline operates in four stages: (1) an LLM generates randomized, self-verified geometry prompts per category; (2) a diffusion model produces initial images, from which DepthFM extracts monocular depth maps; (3) ControlNet conditioned on those depth maps generates 10 texture-varied images per depth map (1,000 total per category), enforcing pose consistency that rises from 57% (text-only) to 96–97% across categories; (4) Hunyuan3D-v2.0 reconstructs each image into a textured mesh. The key insight is that depth maps serve as an ideal conditioning signal—they capture global object structure and spatial positioning without constraining local geometric variation or texture, unlike Canny edges which introduce artifacts. Generated meshes are then integrated into BlenderProc for two rendering strategies (full 3D simulation à la Omni6D and mixed-reality compositing with ray-traced shadows à la Omni6Dpose) and into SAPIEN for CenterGrasp/SDFG-based grasping simulation, enabling end-to-end dataset creation from a single category prompt.

## Results

On the NOCS REAL275 zero-shot Sim2Real benchmark, the mixed-reality pipeline with generated meshes (Mix_SAI^sh) achieves an average score of 34.75, outperforming the fully synthetic baseline (33.10) and approaching the supervised upper bound of 61.67 without using any real training data. For robotic grasping in SAPIEN simulation, the best model (Custom-CG-rdom-val-rdom) achieves 87.8% grasp success rate and 0.453 IoU shape completion, while the textured variant (Custom-CG-tex-val-tex) reaches 86.8% grasp success and 0.475 IoU. On real-robot zero-shot transfer, category-specialized models achieve up to 100% detection and 73% grasp success on Camera objects, substantially exceeding the general-purpose baseline (0% detection, 0% grasp on Laptop).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two grasping baselines: CenterGrasp [4] (trained on 300+ manually curated objects) and GIGA [17] (point-cloud-based grasp inference). On grasp success rate, the best Custom-CG variant (87.8%) outperforms CenterGrasp (82.7%) by 5.1 points and GIGA (63.8%) by 24 points. On shape completion IoU, the textured variant achieves 0.475, a 51% relative improvement over CenterGrasp (0.314) and a 3.3× improvement over GIGA (0.146). The bidirectional surface error is also lowest for the Custom-CG models (19.2–23.5) compared to CenterGrasp (27.0–28.0) and GIGA (55.2–63.9). In the 6D pose Sim2Real setting, the generated-data pipeline (Mix_SAI^sh, avg 34.75) surpasses the fully synthetic Replica baseline (33.10) and approaches the supervised NOCS upper bound (61.67) without any real training images, demonstrating that the generated meshes close most of the sim-to-real gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own GenVegeFruits3D pipeline [10]—which generated 3D assets for symmetric produce with 57% pose consistency—by introducing depth-conditioned image generation (via ControlNet + DepthFM) that lifts consistency to 96% across 153 arbitrary categories, and by integrating the full downstream stack: BlenderProc-based 3D simulation (extending Omni6D [36]) and mixed-reality rendering (open-sourcing Omni6Dpose [34]). For grasping, it builds on CenterGrasp [4] and the SAPIEN simulator [32], using V-HACD convex decomposition for physical properties. The key conceptual departure from prior dataset-creation work is the elimination of any dependency on pre-existing 3D scans or repositories, replacing them with a fully automated text-to-mesh pipeline that enforces canonical alignment at the image-generation stage rather than through post-hoc registration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of 153K canonically aligned meshes and 1.2M fully annotated 6D pose images represents the largest publicly available aligned 3D dataset to date, directly addressing the data scarcity that has constrained category-level 6D pose estimation and 3D foundation model development. The 5–20× speedup over manual scanning (under 3 minutes per object) makes task-specific dataset generation practically feasible for new categories. Open questions include whether the 96% consistency ceiling is sufficient for all downstream tasks (e.g., highly asymmetric or articulated objects), whether the pipeline generalizes beyond the 153 Omni6Dpose categories, and whether the zero-shot sim2real gap can be further closed as 3D generation models improve. The reliance on a single 3D reconstruction model (Hunyuan3D-v2.0) and a single pose estimator (DualPoseNet) for validation limits the breadth of evidence.
