## Problem

Post-training text-to-image models typically relies on external reward models (human preference scorers, OCR validators, aesthetic predictors) that require large-scale annotation, add pipeline complexity, and are vulnerable to reward hacking where optimizing a narrow critic degrades non-target capabilities. The paper asks whether the text-to-image generator's own internal signal—its self-confidence in recovering noise from its outputs—can serve as a meaningful, annotation-free reward for reinforcement-learning post-training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261256/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text prompt and produces a 512×512 RGB image via a rectified-flow (SD3.5) generator. A key design choice is that the self-confidence reward is computed entirely in latent space by re-noising and probing the generated latent, avoiding the decode-to-pixel step that external rewards require. Training uses LoRA (rank 32, α=64) on the flow-matching backbone, with prompts drawn from the Flow-GRPO text-rendering training set for longer, more informative conditioning.

## Method

SOLACE defines self-confidence as the model's ability to recover noise injected into its own generated latents: given a terminal latent z₀, it re-noises via the rectified-flow forward kernel z_t = (1−t)z₀ + tε at K antithetically-paired noise probes across selected timesteps, queries the velocity field to estimate the injected noise, and converts the per-timestep MSE into a scalar reward via a negative-log transform. This intrinsic reward drives GRPO post-training (adopting Flow-GRPO's SDE conversion of the flow-matching ODE), with group-relative advantage normalization over G=16 sampled trajectories. Two stabilization mechanisms prevent reward hacking: training is restricted to a suffix of the denoising schedule (ρ=0.6 of later steps) to avoid collapse into trivially-predictable latent regimes, and self-confidence is computed without classifier-free guidance so the reward measures the base conditional policy rather than the guided proxy. The signal is computed online with the model being trained, so it improves in reliability as the model improves.

## Results

Applying SOLACE to SD3.5-M (2.5B params) yields GenEval 0.71, OCR 0.67, and CLIPScore 0.288, nearly matching the much larger SD3.5-L (8.1B params) which scores 0.71, 0.68, and 0.289 on the same metrics. Human-preference metrics show modest but consistent gains (PickScore 22.34→22.41, ImageReward 0.84→0.87, UnifiedReward 3.08→3.11). When SOLACE is applied on top of Flow-GRPO external-reward post-training, it recovers non-target capabilities: e.g., after PickScore-optimized Flow-GRPO, adding SOLACE lifts OCR from 0.65 to 0.71 and CLIPScore from 0.293 to 0.294, while after ImageReward-optimized Flow-GRPO it recovers GenEval from 0.54 to 0.77.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SDXL, SOLACE-post-trained SD3.5-M dominates on GenEval (0.71 vs. 0.55) and OCR (0.67 vs. 0.14), while matching on CLIPScore (0.288 vs. 0.287) and slightly trailing on Aesthetic (5.39 vs. 5.60). Against the larger SD3.5-L, SOLACE on SD3.5-M matches GenEval exactly (0.71) and is within 0.01 on OCR (0.67 vs. 0.68) and CLIPScore (0.288 vs. 0.289), despite having less than one-third the parameters. Against Flow-GRPO with external rewards, SOLACE shows a complementary pattern: it sacrifices a small amount on the targeted external metric (e.g., PickScore 22.51→22.50) while substantially recovering non-target capabilities (OCR 0.65→0.71, GenEval 0.54→0.77 after ImageReward optimization), directly mitigating the reward hacking observed in pure external-reward post-training. A user study (~3,600 responses, 40 participants) confirms that SOLACE-post-trained SD3.5-M is preferred over the baseline 59.0% of the time on visual realism and 57.3% on text alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

SOLACE builds directly on Flow-GRPO [41], which established GRPO-based online RL for flow-matching text-to-image models, and repurposes the score-distillation-sampling idea [55, 66, 79]—where a pretrained T2I model critiques another generator's outputs—by letting the model critique its own generations. Conceptually, it transplants the intrinsic-reward paradigm from LLM post-training (self-certainty, self-consistency as RL signals) into the continuous denoising trajectory of flow-matching models, where likelihoods are implicit and no discrete token-level confidence exists. The key departure from prior T2I post-training work is the elimination of all external reward models: instead of PickScore, HPSv2, or OCR validators, the reward is the model's own noise-recovery error, computed entirely in latent space without decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SOLACE offers a practical, zero-annotation post-training recipe that improves compositionality, text rendering, and text-image alignment without the pipeline complexity and reward-hacking risk of external-reward RL. The complementarity result—applying SOLACE on top of externally post-trained models recovers non-target capabilities while only mildly degrading the targeted metric—gives practitioners a concrete tool to mitigate reward hacking. However, gains on human-preference metrics remain modest, and the method cannot target a specific alignment objective on its own, leaving open the question of how to calibrate intrinsic signals for task-specific reward shaping. Future extensions to 3D/4D generation and multi-view settings are identified but not yet explored.
