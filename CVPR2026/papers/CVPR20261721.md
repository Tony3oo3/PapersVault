## Problem

Existing diffusion-based I2I translation methods use a single coupled reverse process that simultaneously removes noise and residuals, which prematurely erodes the domain-harmonizing effect of injected Gaussian noise before the source-to-target mapping is complete. This makes unified I2I translation—where one model must handle multiple distinct degradation types or domains—particularly difficult, especially under limited paired data. The paper identifies that noise injection implicitly aligns feature distributions across domains (a previously under-explored property) but that coupled diffusion destroys this benefit.

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded RGB image (e.g., noisy, blurry, low-light, or hazy) as input and produces a restored clean image as output. At inference, the input image is first augmented with a fixed-level Gaussian noise (optimal intensity around 1.0–1.2) to form the starting point of the reverse process, rather than beginning from pure noise. The denoising network uses a U-Net with channel depth 64 and multipliers (1,2,4,8), while the residual removal network uses a deeper U-Net with channel depth 128 and multipliers (1,1,2,2,4,4); both use DDIM sampling with step size 2.

## Method

DRDD decouples the conventional single forward diffusion into two sequential, independent stages: a stochastic noise diffusion that injects Gaussian perturbations into the target image for domain harmonization and manifold lifting, followed by a deterministic residual diffusion that models the target-to-source mapping entirely within the fixed-noise domain. The reverse process is symmetrically decoupled: a residual-removal network first removes the source-target difference within the noise-carrying domain (preserving the harmonization effect), and a separate denoising network then removes the Gaussian noise to yield the clean target. The key theoretical insight (Proposition 3.1) proves that injecting Gaussian noise reduces KL divergence between domain distributions, formalizing noise as a 'domain harmonizer.' The denoising network is trained exclusively on unpaired target-domain images (or initialized from ImageNet-pretrained weights), while the residual network is trained on paired data, substantially improving data efficiency. The framework is shown to be compatible with DDPM, DDIM, and score-based SDE paradigms.

## Results

On the All-in-One-5 unified multi-task restoration benchmark, DRDD achieves the best average SSIM of 0.916, LPIPS of 0.073, and FID of 18.3, outperforming the strongest baseline DFPIR (0.912/0.081/24.9) across all three metrics. On the CDD-11 composite degradation benchmark, DRDD attains the highest average SSIM among six compared methods, with particularly large gains in challenging composite scenarios (e.g., L+H+S, L+H+R). On the self-constructed MNMD multi-domain denoising benchmark, DRDD achieves 0.9338 SSIM and 0.0553 LPIPS on average, surpassing VLUNET (0.9274/0.0784) and RDDM (0.8406/0.1702) across natural, medical, and remote sensing domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRDD outperforms all five baselines on the All-in-One-5 average across SSIM, LPIPS, and FID, with the closest competitor being DFPIR (0.912 SSIM, 0.081 LPIPS, 24.9 FID) — DRDD improves SSIM by 0.004, LPIPS by 0.008, and FID by 6.6 points. Against diffusion-based baselines, DRDD shows the largest margins: DiffuIR trails by 0.047 SSIM and 15.4 FID, while DA-CLIP lags by 0.040 SSIM and 1.7 FID. On the CDD-11 composite degradation benchmark, DRDD achieves the highest average SSIM and shows the smallest performance drop in multi-degradation scenarios (L+H+S, L+H+R) where PromptIR, WGWSNet, and MoCE-IR-S degrade noticeably. On the MNMD multi-domain denoising task, DRDD beats VLUNET by 0.0064 SSIM and 0.0231 LPIPS on average, and surpasses RDDM by a substantial 0.0932 SSIM margin, confirming the advantage of decoupled residual removal in the noise-carrying domain. When extended to the SDE framework (Table 3), the decoupled variant (De-IR-SDE) outperforms the coupled IR-SDE baseline on inpainting (LPIPS 0.0490 vs 0.0517) and deraining (PSNR 28.1 vs 27.2), while achieving comparable denoising quality with a better FID (31.87 vs 33.29).

## Relation to Prior Work

DRDD builds directly on the DDPM/DDIM framework and the residual denoising diffusion model (RDDM), which introduced the idea of starting the reverse process from a noise-carrying input rather than pure noise. The key departure is a structural decoupling: where prior coupled methods (RDDM, I2SB, IR-SDE) remove noise and residuals simultaneously in a single reverse process, DRDD splits this into a deterministic residual-removal stage (operating entirely within a fixed-noise domain) followed by a stochastic denoising stage. This is motivated by a newly formalized property—Proposition 3.1—that injecting Gaussian noise reduces KL divergence between domain distributions, effectively acting as a 'domain harmonizer' that prior coupled processes erode by progressively removing the noise before the mapping is complete.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling offers two practical benefits: it simplifies unified multi-task I2I translation by preserving domain harmonization throughout the core mapping, and it enables the denoising network to be trained exclusively on unpaired target-domain images, substantially improving data efficiency. The framework's demonstrated compatibility with DDPM, DDIM, and SDE-based paradigms suggests broad applicability. Open questions include how the optimal noise level (identified theoretically via an MMD-based objective) transfers across architectures and task complexities, whether the harmonization effect extends to non-Gaussian or structured noise, and whether the approach scales to video or 3D restoration where domain gaps are even more pronounced.
