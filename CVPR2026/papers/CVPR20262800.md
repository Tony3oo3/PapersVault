## Problem

Few-step diffusion models (2–4 NFEs) produce poor inpainting quality when using standard blended sampling with random Gaussian noise initialization, because the large per-step ODE updates cannot correct the semantic misalignment between the initial noise and the unmasked background context. Existing few-step inpainting solutions like TurboFill require complex multi-stage adversarial training with real-image supervision, and prior diffusion inversion methods are iterative and too slow for few-step inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262800/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a masked RGB image (with a binary mask indicating regions to inpaint) and a text prompt as input, and produces a fully inpainted image at 1024×1024 resolution. The masked image is VAE-encoded into a latent, which is fed through the one-step inversion network to produce a semantically aligned noise latent; this latent is then blended with fresh random Gaussian noise in the masked regions before entering the few-step (2–4 NFE) blended sampling pipeline. Training requires no curated image-mask-text triplets—the one-step generator synthesizes training pairs on the fly from random noise and sampled prompts.

## Method

InverFill introduces a one-step inversion network F_θ (sharing architecture and pretrained weights with the target one-step generator, e.g., SANA-Sprint DiT or SDXL-Turbo UNet) that maps a masked image latent to a semantically informed noise latent, replacing random Gaussian initialization. Two key training components address inpainting-specific challenges: (1) a Re-Blending operation that replaces the masked regions of the predicted noise latent with fresh random Gaussian noise during both training and inference, preventing structural information leakage from visible regions into the latent; and (2) a Gaussian regularization loss that moment-matches the first and second statistics of the blended latent to a standard Gaussian, correcting the distributional mismatch caused by the masked noise loss. An adversarial loss using a frozen multi-step teacher's intermediate features further improves visual fidelity. The entire training pipeline is image-free, requiring only text prompts and the pretrained generator, and adds only 0.04–0.06 s of inference overhead per image.

## Results

On BrushBench, SDXL Turbo + InverFill (4 NFEs) achieves IR 12.38, HPS 28.44, AS 6.08, and CLIP 27.67, improving over the vanilla SDXL Turbo baseline (IR 11.42, HPS 28.20, CLIP 27.26) by +0.96 IR and +0.41 CLIP. On MagicBrush, the same configuration reaches IR 3.75 and CLIP 26.08, up from 3.51 and 25.79 without InverFill. SANA-Sprint 0.6B + InverFill (2 NFEs) boosts IR from 11.02 to 11.65 and HPS from 26.21 to 27.93 on BrushBench. The added inference overhead is only 0.04–0.06 seconds per image on a single A100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla few-step baselines, InverFill consistently wins on all four metrics: SANA-Sprint 0.6B gains +0.63 IR and +1.72 HPS (2 NFEs), while SDXL Turbo gains +0.96 IR and +0.41 HPS (4 NFEs). Compared to the specialized few-step inpainting model BrushNet (SDXL Turbo + BrushNet, 4 NFEs), InverFill-equipped SDXL Turbo trades a small IR deficit (12.38 vs 12.56) for higher HPS (28.44 vs 28.26) and CLIP (27.67 vs 27.51), and notably does so without any real-image supervision. Against multi-step baselines, SDXL Turbo + InverFill (4 NFEs) surpasses HD-Painter (30 NFEs) on HPS (28.44 vs 28.17) and CLIP (27.67 vs 27.43) but trails on IR (12.38 vs 12.82) and AS (6.08 vs 6.30). It also outperforms SANA 0.6B (20 NFEs) on IR (12.38 vs 12.12) and HPS (28.44 vs 27.04) while using 5× fewer NFEs. The strongest multi-step baseline, SDXL-Inpainting (30 NFEs), still leads on IR (13.16) and HPS (28.92), but InverFill matches or exceeds it on CLIP (27.67 vs 27.15) at roughly 1/7 the inference cost. On MagicBrush, InverFill-equipped models lead all few-step baselines and approach multi-step quality, with SDXL Turbo + InverFill reaching IR 3.75 vs 3.91 for SDXL-Inpainting.

## Relation to Prior Work

InverFill extends SwiftEdit's one-step inversion framework from image editing to inpainting, addressing two specific failures of naive adaptation: information leakage from visible regions and distributional mismatch of the inverted latent. It departs from TurboFill, the only prior few-step specialized inpainting model, by eliminating the need for real-image supervision and a complex 3-step adversarial training scheme, instead using an image-free pipeline with Re-Blending and Gaussian moment-matching regularization. The method also builds on the Blended Latent Diffusion sampling strategy but identifies and corrects its fundamental breakdown under few-step inference, where large ODE updates prevent gradual semantic alignment that multi-step models rely on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InverFill provides a lightweight, plug-and-play mechanism (≤0.06 s overhead) that enables off-the-shelf few-step text-to-image models to perform inpainting at quality comparable to specialized multi-step inpainting systems, without any task-specific fine-tuning or curated image-mask-text triplets. This lowers the barrier to fast, high-quality inpainting deployment and suggests that the initialization strategy, rather than the architecture, is the key bottleneck in few-step inpainting. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video inpainting, higher-resolution settings, or architectures beyond DiT and UNet, and whether the blended sampling strategy itself can be further improved to reduce the residual gap with multi-step methods.
