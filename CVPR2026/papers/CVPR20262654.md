## Problem

Unified visual tokenizers that jointly support image generation and semantic understanding suffer from an inherent optimization conflict: pixel reconstruction and semantic alignment pull the shared encoder in opposing directions, with the pixel codebook converging far more slowly than the semantic codebook and becoming a training bottleneck. Existing unified tokenizers (VILA-U, TokenFlow, UniTok) rely on a limited set of proxy tasks—typically pixel reconstruction plus CLIP-style feature alignment—leaving architecture design, diverse supervision sources, and task interaction largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The tokenizer encodes 256×256 or 384×384 RGB images into two streams of discrete tokens: semantic tokens (codebook size 32768, dim 32) derived from deep encoder layers and pixel tokens (codebook size 65536 as two indices per group, dim 8) derived from shallow layers. In the unified MLLM setting, text prompts and/or images are consumed as input, and the model autoregressively produces either a text response (understanding) or a sequence of discrete image tokens that are decoded back into a generated image (generation). The tokenizer is trained on 100M image-text pairs from COYO for one epoch.

## Method

The paper proposes a single ViT-L encoder (initialized from CLIP-ViT-L-16) with a lightweight CNN aggregation neck that hierarchically decouples proxy tasks: layers 1–3 feed pixel reconstruction while layers 22–24 feed semantic alignment. Semantic supervision is enriched beyond CLIP by jointly aligning to DINOv2 and SAM features via separate MLP heads, capturing both structural semantics and fine-grained segmentation priors. To resolve the pixel-codebook bottleneck, an attention-prioritized mapping strategy computes token-wise semantic similarity from attention key values, groups semantically similar tokens via DFS with threshold τ=0.7, and merges their corresponding pixel codebook groups so that similar tokens search a smaller, semantically coherent pixel codebook subset. After the semantic branch converges (at 0.5 epochs), a coarse-to-fine reconstruction loss (L_F) uses the now-aligned DINO/SAM features to supervise fine-grained edge and texture details in the pixel decoder, yielding a staged training objective that transitions from L_S + L_R to L_S + L_R + L_F.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, the unified tokenizer achieves rFID 0.33, PSNR 25.17, SSIM 0.822, and 80.9% zero-shot classification accuracy, surpassing the best pixel-only reconstructor (VAR, rFID 1.00) and the best unified tokenizer (UniTok, rFID 0.38, 78.6% accuracy). At 384×384, performance further improves to rFID 0.17 and 81.4% zero-shot accuracy. The 7B unified MLLM built on this tokenizer achieves an average score of 63.23 across seven understanding benchmarks, exceeding TokenFlow-13B (60.18) by 3.1 points, and attains gFID 5.01 on MJHQ-30K and 0.86/0.74 on GenAI-Bench basic/advanced prompts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest unified-tokenizer baselines, the method dominates: it improves zero-shot accuracy over VILA-U by 7.6 points (80.9% vs. 73.3%) and over UniTok by 2.3 points (80.9% vs. 78.6%), while also achieving a substantially lower rFID (0.33 vs. 1.80 for VILA-U, 0.38 for UniTok, 1.37 for TokenFlow). In the MLLM setting, the 7B model outperforms the larger TokenFlow-13B by 3.1 points in average understanding score (63.23 vs. 60.18) and beats VILA-U by 9.3% on SEED-Img. On generation, the method surpasses UniTok by 7% on GenAI-Bench advanced prompts (0.74 vs. 0.67) and achieves a lower MJHQ-30K gFID (5.01 vs. 7.46 for UniTok, 5.47 for Liquid). The zero-shot accuracy of 80.9% is competitive with the continuous-embedding SigLIP (80.5%) but falls short of the semantic-only DFN (82.2%), which does not perform reconstruction. Overall, the method is the only unified tokenizer to simultaneously exceed all pixel-only reconstructors in rFID and all prior unified tokenizers in zero-shot accuracy at 256 resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified visual tokenizer line initiated by VQVAE and advanced by TokenFlow, VILA-U, and UniTok, which aim to serve both autoregressive generation and semantic understanding within a single discrete token space. Its key departure is a systematic diagnosis of the pixel-vs-semantic codebook convergence conflict, followed by a single-encoder hierarchical decoupling design (shallow layers for reconstruction, deep layers for semantics) that replaces TokenFlow's dual-encoder architecture. The attention-prioritized mapping strategy generalizes TokenFlow's rigid shared-index correspondence by allowing semantically similar patches to share a larger aggregated pixel codebook, and the multi-source supervision (CLIP + DINOv2 + SAM) departs from prior work that relies solely on CLIP-style contrastive features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Unified tokenizers are the critical bottleneck for autoregressive MLLMs that must simultaneously understand and generate images; this paper provides the most thorough analysis to date of why existing unified tokenizers underperform specialized ones and offers a concrete architectural recipe (hierarchical decoupling, diverse supervision, attention-prioritized mapping, coarse-to-fine reconstruction) that narrows the gap substantially. The finding that a 7B model with this tokenizer outperforms TokenFlow-13B suggests tokenizer quality can compensate for model scale, which is practically important for resource-constrained deployments. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video or higher resolutions (beyond 384), whether the attention-prioritized mapping remains effective with much larger codebooks, and whether the coarse-to-fine supervision stage can be made more stable or automated.
