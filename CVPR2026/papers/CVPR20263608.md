## Problem

Existing visual anomaly detection methods focus on pixel-level deviations (texture defects, reconstruction errors) or prompt-conditioned hallucination detection, largely overlooking object-level structural and contextual inconsistencies in natural scenes. This paper introduces scene layout anomaly detection as a new task, targeting diverse layout deviations such as implausible object attributes (e.g., a dog with five legs) and spatial relation mismatches (e.g., a car on a lake) that are invisible to pixel-level detectors but critical for downstream applications like text-to-image generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 640×640) as input, from which object instances are extracted via a pretrained segmentation model (SAM) to form the scene layout. It outputs an object-level anomaly score for each detected object, which can be projected onto the pixel space via segmentation masks to produce a pixel-level anomaly map. The framework is fully unsupervised, requiring no anomaly labels during training, and operates without any text prompt or reference image.

## Method

LayoutAD constructs two complementary graphs from the extracted object set: a semantic graph (CLIP appearance embeddings concatenated with category text representations) and a geometric graph (normalized spatial descriptors including centroid, shape, size, and aspect ratio), connected via a hybrid k-NN and distance-threshold edge strategy. A Misalignment Reasoning Module then performs GATv2 message passing within each graph followed by a cross-graph transformer with bidirectional attention (semantic tokens query geometric keys/values and vice versa) plus an edge-aware relational bias in the attention logits, enabling the model to detect semantic-geometric inconsistencies. The Anomaly Ranking Module models conditional likelihoods using K-component Gaussian mixture density networks: an object attribute score measures the negative log-likelihood of semantic given geometric (and vice versa) under a global scene context, while a relation score measures the negative log-likelihood of pairwise geometric relations given the semantic context of both objects. Training is unsupervised, minimizing the weighted sum of attribute-level and relation-level negative log-likelihoods, and the final per-object anomaly score is a learnable-weighted combination of attribute and relation scores.

## Results

On the COCOAD benchmark, LayoutAD achieves the best performance across all three metrics: I-AUROC 0.586, P-AUROC 0.871, and A-P-AUROC 0.883. The largest gains appear in pixel-level localization, where LayoutAD outperforms the strongest segmentation baseline SynBoost (P-AUROC 0.773, A-P-AUROC 0.777) by roughly 10 percentage points. On the StreetScene video anomaly detection task, LayoutAD achieves 25.4% RBDC without any temporal modeling, surpassing motion-based methods Flow (11.0%), FG (21.0%), and EVAL (24.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LayoutAD outperforms all 12 baselines on every reported metric. Among structural anomaly detectors, DualAnoDiff is the closest competitor on I-AUROC (0.573 vs. 0.586), but LayoutAD leads by a wide margin on pixel-level metrics (P-AUROC 0.871 vs. 0.572), indicating that appearance-based reconstruction methods fundamentally miss layout-level inconsistencies. Segmentation-based methods SynBoost and PixOOD are the strongest pixel-level baselines (P-AUROC 0.773 and 0.720 respectively), yet LayoutAD still surpasses them by 9.8 and 15.1 points on P-AUROC, showing that OOD-region detection is insufficient for relational anomalies. Logical anomaly methods SINBAD and WinCLIP perform worst overall (I-AUROC 0.449 and 0.455), confirming that prompt-conditioned or set-level semantic reasoning alone cannot capture fine-grained geometric misalignment. On the StreetScene video task, LayoutAD (25.4% RBDC) beats the best motion-based baseline EVAL (24.3%) by 1.1 points and substantially outperforms Flow (11.0%) and FG (21.0%), demonstrating that spatial-semantic reasoning alone can rival temporal approaches for contextual video anomalies.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new problem space between pixel-level structural anomaly detection (PatchCore, DRAEM, etc.), logical anomaly detection in industrial settings (SINBAD, WinCLIP, SALAD), and prompt-conditioned hallucination detection. Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes GATv2 message passing, cross-graph transformers, and mixture-density normality estimators into a unified object-level reasoning pipeline that jointly models semantic and geometric graphs. The key conceptual departure is shifting the unit of analysis from pixels to objects and from appearance deviation to semantic-geometric misalignment, while remaining unsupervised and prompt-free.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing scene layout anomaly detection and releasing COCOAD, the paper provides a much-needed benchmark for object-level plausibility reasoning that is orthogonal to both texture-based defect detection and text-prompt hallucination checking. The demonstrated applications—self-corrected T2I generation, video anomaly detection without temporal modeling, and robust image anomaly segmentation—suggest broad utility for generative AI quality control. However, the low I-AUROC (0.586) signals the task remains far from solved, and the heavy dependence on accurate segmentation masks (SAM) limits robustness in cluttered scenes. Open questions include whether the graph-based formulation scales to very dense scenes and whether the iterative resampling strategy for self-correction can be replaced by differentiable layout guidance.
