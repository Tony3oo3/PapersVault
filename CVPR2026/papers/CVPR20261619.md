## Problem

Self-supervised monocular depth estimation suffers from two underaddressed limitations: (1) frequency aliasing introduced by downsampling in encoder-decoder architectures folds high-frequency edge and texture information into low-frequency bands, producing blurred depth boundaries and lost thin structures; and (2) existing pose networks fuse adjacent frames too early in the network, limiting their ability to model fine-grained cross-frame geometric correspondences. Additionally, the complementary relationship between depth and pose estimation—where each can reinforce the other—remains largely unexploited in standard single-stage joint training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes consecutive monocular RGB video frames resized to 640×192 pixels, using view synthesis between source and target frames as the self-supervised signal. It outputs multi-scale disparity maps at 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, and full resolution (converted to depth), along with a 4×4 relative camera pose (rotation + translation) between adjacent frames. Predicted depth is relative and scaled to align with ground truth during evaluation, following standard self-supervised practice.

## Method

The framework comprises two networks trained via a progressive three-stage strategy. FGDepth embeds plug-and-play Frequency-Guided Sampling (FGS) blocks at each decoder upsampling stage; each FGS block computes an adaptive low-pass kernel via softmax over a 3×3 convolution, derives a complementary high-pass kernel, and fuses the two paths to reinject lost high-frequency details while suppressing aliasing artifacts. PQNet uses Channel-Aligned Attention (CAA) to query features from the previous frame against the current frame, with learnable relative positional encoding and 7×7 windowed computation for efficiency, followed by a Pose Trunk and Head for regression. The three-stage training decouples optimization: Stage 1 performs standard joint self-supervised training; Stage 2 freezes the depth network and trains PQNet with an additional SE(3) reciprocal pose constraint; Stage 3 freezes PQNet and fine-tunes only the FGS blocks in FGDepth. This schedule is theoretically grounded in Non-convex Inexact Block Coordinate Descent, avoiding the optimizer-state misalignment that plagues batch- or epoch-level alternating schemes.

## Results

On the KITTI Eigen split (640×192), FGDepth achieves the best results across all metrics: Abs Rel 0.096, Sq Rel 0.679, RMSE 4.303, RMSE log 0.174, and δ<1.25¹ 0.902. This represents a 4.1% reduction in Sq Rel over the strongest baseline MonoViT (0.708) and a 1.6% reduction in RMSE. On the Make3D zero-shot generalization test, FGDepth achieves Sq Rel 2.627 and RMSE 6.500, outperforming all self-supervised baselines by 4.75% in Sq Rel over MonoViT. For pose estimation on KITTI Odometry sequences 09–10, the full progressive pipeline achieves ATE of 0.016±0.008 (Seq.09) and 0.014±0.010 (Seq.10), the best among all ablation variants.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGDepth outperforms all 25+ compared baselines on every KITTI metric. The closest competitors are Gao et al. [8] (Sq Rel 0.692) and GeoDepth [31] (Sq Rel 0.694), both of which FGDepth surpasses by 1.9% and 2.2% in Sq Rel respectively; against the primary Transformer-based baseline MonoViT [39], the gap widens to 4.1% in Sq Rel (0.679 vs. 0.708) and 1.6% in RMSE (4.303 vs. 4.372). On Make3D zero-shot generalization, FGDepth (Sq Rel 2.627, RMSE 6.500) beats the next-best self-supervised method GeoDepth (2.750, 6.735) by 4.5% in Sq Rel, and also surpasses supervised baselines like Laina et al. [12] (Sq Rel 1.840) in Abs Rel (0.283 vs. 0.204) while remaining competitive overall. For pose estimation, the progressive three-stage training with PQNet achieves the lowest ATE (0.016/0.014 on Seq.09/10), improving over the joint-training variant (0.018/0.015) by roughly 11% on Seq.09. The ablation in Table 3 confirms that the progressive strategy alone (vs. joint training with the same architecture) yields a further 3.55% Sq Rel reduction, and that the 'Batch' alternating strategy degrades performance substantially (Sq Rel 0.819), validating the need for the staged decoupling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised monocular depth estimation paradigm established by Monodepth2 (Godard et al., ICCV 2019), which provides the photometric loss formulation, view synthesis pipeline, and evaluation protocol. It departs from prior frequency-aware methods (e.g., Chen et al., WACV 2023) that rely on computationally heavy global Fourier transforms by instead attributing boundary blurring to Nyquist-Shannon aliasing in encoder-decoder downsampling and addressing it with lightweight spatial-domain FGS blocks. The progressive three-stage training strategy is positioned against existing multi-stage approaches (uncertainty modeling, teacher-student, self-evolving refinement) by explicitly decoupling depth and pose optimization in a manner theoretically grounded in Block Coordinate Descent, rather than treating them as a single joint objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play FGS blocks offer a practical, architecture-agnostic tool for improving boundary sharpness in any encoder-decoder depth network, and the progressive training strategy provides a concrete recipe for stabilizing joint depth-pose optimization. However, the evaluation is concentrated on KITTI driving scenes, and the Make3D generalization test uses only 134 images, leaving open whether the frequency-domain framing and progressive strategy transfer to more diverse or dynamic environments. A key open question is whether the mutual reinforcement between depth and pose networks identified here extends to richer multi-task settings (e.g., adding optical flow or semantics) without the same decoupling overhead.
