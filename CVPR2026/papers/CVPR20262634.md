## Problem

Existing training-free diffusion acceleration methods (higher-order ODE solvers, Taylor-based predictors, token caching) all perform their updates with Euclidean vector operations, implicitly assuming flat feature spaces. However, intermediate features of diffusion transformers lie on low-dimensional curved manifolds, and ignoring this geometry causes progressive 'manifold drift' that degrades sample fidelity under aggressive step schedules, capping achievable speedups.

## Input / Output

GeoRK2 is a drop-in sampler replacement that consumes the same inputs as the underlying diffusion transformer: class labels (ImageNet-256, 256×256), text prompts (FLUX.1-dev, HunyuanVideo), and random noise latents. It intercepts intermediate activations at four bottleneck layers (6, 12, 18, 24) during each forward pass to estimate a local feature covariance metric, then outputs the next latent in the denoising sequence. For video generation, it produces 24-frame clips at 512×320 resolution, with the geometric correction applied per-frame to preserve temporal manifold structure.

## Method

GeoRK2 reformulates diffusion sampling as Riemannian gradient flow on a feature manifold whose metric is induced by activation covariances at bottleneck layers, truncated to the top-64 principal directions (capturing >99% spectral energy). The integrator has three components: (1) a manifold-aware RK2 predictor that projects the midpoint onto the dominant eigen-subspace (acting as a first-order retraction) to suppress drift into low-confidence directions; (2) a metric-preconditioned corrector that applies a curvature-compensated update Δh_geo = −λ·G̅⁻¹(h_pred − F(h_pred)) using the Woodbury identity for O(d·r²) inversion of the low-rank metric; and (3) an adaptive stabilization module that detects regime transitions via acceleration-variance spikes and falls back to conservative two-point extrapolation, with momentum mixing (ρ=0.85) to dampen oscillations. The metric is updated every 5 steps via truncated SVD and temporally smoothed with an exponential moving average (β=0.9), adding only 5.1% FLOPs and 3.8% wall-clock overhead per step.

## Results

On ImageNet-256 with DiT-XL/2, GeoRK2(N=2) achieves FID 2.41 at 1.95× speedup (beating the full DDIM-50 baseline of FID 2.51), while GeoRK2(N=8) maintains FID 3.32 at 4.92× speedup. On FLUX.1-dev (DrawBench), GeoRK2(N=5) attains ImageReward 0.989 and CLIP Score 34.96 at 3.52× acceleration, the highest reported values at that speed. On HunyuanVideo (VBench), GeoRK2(N=8) scores 80.73 at 4.66× speedup, a 2.36-point drop from the 323-second original, outperforming TaylorSeer (79.99) at comparable acceleration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoRK2 consistently outperforms all compared baselines at matched or near-matched speedup levels. At ~2.7× speedup on ImageNet-256, GeoRK2(N=3) achieves FID 2.67, beating TaylorSeer(N=3) (2.73), ToCa(N=3) (2.93), FORA(N=3) (5.31), and SmoothCache (4.43). In the aggressive 4.5–5× regime, GeoRK2(N=8) reaches FID 3.32 versus TaylorSeer(N=5) at 4.31 and FORA(N=5) at 9.87, a gap of 1.0 and 6.5 FID points respectively. On FLUX.1-dev, GeoRK2(N=5) achieves ImageReward 0.989 and CLIP 34.96, surpassing TaylorSeer(N=4) (0.986, 34.20) and FORA(N=3) (0.967, 34.17), while caching methods TeaCache (0.878) and DBCache (0.616) degrade substantially under text conditioning. On HunyuanVideo, GeoRK2(N=8) scores VBench 80.73 at 4.66× speedup, edging out TaylorSeer(N=7) (79.99) and ToCa(N=6) (78.97) while also achieving the lowest FLOPs (3106T vs. 3493T and 4983T). The only baseline that approaches GeoRK2 at low speedup is DPM++-20 (FID 3.24 at 2.62×), but GeoRK2(N=2) still wins on both FID (2.41) and SSIM (0.96 vs. 0.83) at a comparable 1.95× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoRK2 sits at the intersection of two established lines: numerical ODE solvers for diffusion (DDIM, DPM-Solver) that operate in Euclidean data space, and feature-space predict-and-correct frameworks (TaylorSeer, ToCa, FORA, TeaCache) that exploit temporal smoothness in intermediate activations. Its key departure is importing Riemannian geometric integration—projection-as-retraction, metric-preconditioned correction via the Woodbury identity, and low-rank covariance-induced metrics—from the optimization and geometric-numerical-analysis literature (Amari's natural gradients, Hairer et al.'s structure-preserving integrators, Munthe-Kaas's Lie-group Runge-Kutta methods) into the inference-time setting of pre-trained diffusion transformers. Unlike prior feature-space accelerators that apply Euclidean linear interpolation and L2 corrections, GeoRK2 explicitly models the denoising trajectory as Riemannian gradient flow on a low-dimensional feature manifold, using activation covariance spectra to define a local metric that guides both prediction and correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is immediate: a training-free, plug-and-play wrapper that delivers 4–5× speedup with ΔFID ≈ 0.81 across three architecturally distinct backbones (DiT-XL/2, FLUX.1-dev, HunyuanVideo), including text-to-video generation where temporal coherence is hardest to preserve. The 'manifold drift' framing gives the field a concrete geometric diagnostic for why aggressive step schedules degrade quality, potentially guiding future solver design beyond ad-hoc caching or Taylor extrapolation. Open questions include whether the slowly-varying-metric assumption holds for architectures with very different depth or attention patterns, whether hypernetwork-predicted metrics (mentioned as future work) can eliminate the 5-step amortization, and whether the approach extends to non-DiT diffusion backbones (e.g., U-Net-based LDMs) where the feature manifold structure may differ substantially.
