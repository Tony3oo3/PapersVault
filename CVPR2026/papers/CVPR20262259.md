## Problem

Existing dyadic gesture generation methods either treat the two speakers' audio as a single blended signal without disentangling individual contributions, or overlook social context such as relationship type and personality traits that shape how people move and respond in conversation. This leads to generic, socially incoherent gestures that fail to capture the moment-to-moment coordination and role-specific dynamics of real two-person interactions.

## Input / Output

The model takes dyadic audio from two speakers (encoded via Wav2Vec2), an optional 4-class relationship label (friends, strangers, family, dating), a 5-dimensional Big Five personality vector, and optionally the partner's gesture sequence as conditioning. It produces a 300-frame (10 s) upper-body gesture sequence in 6D rotation format (R^{T×J×6}), generated in a compact latent space (d=64, 4× temporal downsampling) by a residual VQ-VAE with four hierarchical codebooks, and decoded back to continuous poses at inference.

## Method

DyaDiT is a diffusion transformer (DiT) trained with standard ε-prediction loss under the DDPM framework, operating on quantized motion latents from a residual VQ-VAE tokenizer. Its key architectural contribution is the Orthogonalization Cross Attention (ORCA) module, which first projects the self-speaker audio onto the other-speaker's subspace via a lightweight MLP and subtracts the projection to remove redundant correlated components, then performs bidirectional cross-attention (self→other and other→self) fused by a learnable sigmoid gate, yielding a disentangled joint audio representation. Social context (relationship and personality) is injected through both FiLM-based modulation and cross-attention across all DiT blocks. A learnable Motion Dictionary of orthogonal gesture primitives modulates the partner audio via cross-attention to provide style-aware conditioning, and at inference classifier-free guidance can strengthen or drop this style signal. The model is trained on 3,000 clips (~182 hours) from the Seamless Interaction dataset with supervised ε-prediction, and the VQ-VAE encoder/decoder are frozen during DiT training and inference respectively.

## Results

On the Seamless Interaction Dataset, DyaDiT achieves the best Fréchet Distance on both static (6.40) and kinetic (1.37) metrics, substantially outperforming ConvoFusion (9.22 / 1.74) and Audio2PhotoReal (8.77 / 1.84). It also attains the highest motion diversity among all methods (Static: 27.46, Kinetic: 1.38), approaching the ground-truth static diversity of 28.42. In the user study, DyaDiT is preferred over ConvoFusion by 73.9% of participants on overall quality, 69.8% on relationship consistency, and 66.7% on personality consistency, and even slightly outperforms ground-truth gestures in perceived quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyaDiT outperforms both baselines across every reported metric. Against ConvoFusion [31], DyaDiT reduces static FD by 30.6% (6.40 vs. 9.22) and kinetic FD by 21.3% (1.37 vs. 1.74), while increasing static diversity by 49.8% (27.46 vs. 18.33) and kinetic diversity by 25.5% (1.38 vs. 1.10). Against Audio2PhotoReal [33], the gains are similar: static FD drops from 8.77 to 6.40 (27.0% improvement) and kinetic FD from 1.84 to 1.37 (25.5% improvement), with static diversity rising from 19.35 to 27.46 (41.9%). In the A/B user study, DyaDiT is preferred over ConvoFusion by a large margin (73.9% overall quality, 69.8% relationship consistency, 66.7% personality consistency), with statistical significance at p < 10⁻⁸. Notably, DyaDiT also edges out ground-truth gestures in perceived overall quality and relationship consistency, attributed to smoother diffusion-generated motion and more expressive social conditioning.

## Relation to Prior Work

DyaDiT extends the diffusion-transformer paradigm for gesture generation (building on DDPM and residual VQ-VAE tokenization) into the dyadic setting, where prior methods like ConvoFusion and Audio2PhotoReal either blend two audio streams into a single signal or lack explicit social-context conditioning. The key architectural departure is the ORCA module, which orthogonalizes the two speakers' Wav2Vec2 features before cross-attention fusion, and the motion dictionary (inspired by LIA) that injects style-aware motion priors. Unlike single-speaker co-speech gesture work, DyaDiT explicitly conditions on relationship type and Big-Five personality scores via FiLM modulation and cross-attention, addressing a gap the authors identify in both co-speech and dyadic gesture literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work is a step toward socially aware synthetic agents by showing that explicit social-context conditioning (relationship, personality) measurably improves both quantitative metrics and human-perceived social consistency in dyadic gesture generation. However, the authors acknowledge that personality cues may be implicitly encoded in audio, creating conditioning conflicts that limit controllability, and the model is restricted to upper-body motion on a single dataset (~182 hours). Open questions include whether audio neutralization can better disentangle social cues, whether the approach scales to full-body or multi-agent settings, and whether the modest quantitative gains over strong baselines (ConvoFusion, Audio2PhotoReal) translate to robustness across diverse conversational scenarios.
