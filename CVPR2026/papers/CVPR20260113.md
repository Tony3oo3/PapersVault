## Problem

Existing slot-attention methods for unsupervised object segmentation extract features exclusively from the final layer of a pre-trained ViT encoder, discarding the complementary semantic information encoded across intermediate layers. Prior work has shown that DINO ViT layers form a representational hierarchy where spatial, mid-level, and high-level semantics emerge at different depths, yet no method had integrated this multi-layer richness into the slot-attention bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260113/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and processes it through a frozen pre-trained DINO ViT-B/16 encoder, extracting patch-wise features (N tokens of dimension 768) from the last four consecutive layers by default. It produces K object slots (K=6 for VOC, K=7 for COCO, K=11 for MOVi-C) and segmentation masks derived from either the slot-attention attention maps or the autoregressive decoder's cross-attention maps, with the maximum across both reported. The framework is plug-and-play: it replaces the single-layer SA bottleneck in DINOSAUR or SPOT without altering the encoder, decoder, or training objective.

## Method

MUFASA runs independent slot-attention modules (each with its own trainable parameters) on M selected encoder layers, producing M sets of K slots and M corresponding attention masks. Slots are aligned across layers via Hungarian matching that maximizes mIoU between binarized attention masks, ensuring corresponding indices bind to the same object. A novel M-Fusion module then fuses aligned slots by pairwise-summing adjacent slot sets in a sliding-window fashion, concatenating the M−1 resulting pairs along the feature dimension, and projecting through a single-hidden-layer MLP (768 units, GELU) into a unified slot representation. Attention masks are fused analogously via pairwise summation followed by a weighted linear combination (uniform weights for DINOSAUR-M; learned softmax-normalized weights for SPOT-M). The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with only the base model's reconstruction loss, requiring no additional supervision or architectural changes beyond the multi-layer SA module and fusion head.

## Results

SPOT-M (SPOT + MUFASA) achieves a new state of the art in unsupervised object segmentation on PASCAL VOC (mBO^c = 59.8%, mBO^i = 51.3%, mIoU = 49.4%) and MOVi-C (mBO^i = 49.2%, mIoU = 48.2%, FG-ARI = 67.8%), surpassing all prior methods. On COCO, SPOT-M leads in mBO^c (45.5%) and mBO^i (34.8%) but trails SPOT slightly in mIoU (32.5% vs. 32.7%) and FG-ARI (35.6% vs. 37.8%). Notably, the simpler DINOSAUR-M also outperforms SPOT on VOC (mBO^c: 57.6% vs. 55.3%) and MOVi-C (mBO^i: 49.2% vs. 47.0%), while reducing training time by 90–94% on VOC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original slot-attention method (SA [41]), both SPOT-M and DINOSAUR-M improve mBO^c by over 30 points on VOC (59.8% and 57.6% vs. 24.9%) and by over 26 points on COCO (45.5% and 43.0% vs. 19.2%). Compared to SLATE [48], the gains are even more pronounced: SPOT-M exceeds SLATE by 18.3 points in mBO^c on VOC and by 15.8 points in mBO^i on MOVi-C. Against DINOSAUR [47], both MUFASA variants consistently win: SPOT-M improves mBO^c by 4.5 points on VOC and 5.8 points on MOVi-C (mBO^i), while DINOSAUR-M gains 6.4 points in mBO^c on VOC and 6.8 points in mBO^i on MOVi-C. The closest comparison is SPOT-M vs. SPOT [26]: SPOT-M wins on VOC across all four metrics (e.g., mBO^c 59.8% vs. 55.3%) and on MOVi-C (mBO^i 49.2% vs. 47.0%, FG-ARI 67.8% vs. 57.9%), but on COCO it edges out SPOT only in mBO^c (+1.2) and mBO^i (+0.1) while trailing in mIoU (−0.2) and FG-ARI (−2.2), making COCO the sole dataset where SPOT retains a narrow advantage on reconstruction-sensitive metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

MUFASA extends the slot-attention lineage (Locatello et al., 2020) through its two most influential real-world instantiations, DINOSAUR (Seitzer et al., ICLR 2022) and SPOT (Kakogeorgiou et al., CVPR 2024), both of which restrict slot attention to the final DINO-ViT layer. The key conceptual departure is treating the ViT's representational hierarchy as a resource rather than a nuisance: by running independent SA modules on the last four consecutive layers and fusing their outputs via Hungarian-matched pairwise summation and an MLP (M-Fusion), the paper converts a single bottleneck into a multi-layer one. The fusion design draws on multi-layer feature aggregation ideas from visual correspondence and object discovery, but is the first to apply such a strategy within the slot-attention bottleneck for UOS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and consistent SOTA gains across VOC, COCO, and MOVi-C make MUFASA a practical upgrade for any DINO-based SA pipeline, and the ~90% training-time reduction on VOC is a significant efficiency win for large-scale OCL experiments. However, the method inherits the well-known limitation of grouping same-class instances into a single slot, and the rigid one-to-one Hungarian matching across layers may constrain expressiveness in scenes with variable object counts. Open questions include whether soft or many-to-one matching schemes could alleviate this, whether the four-layer sweet spot generalizes to deeper ViTs (e.g., ViT-L), and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D settings where temporal or spatial consistency across layers could be further exploited.
