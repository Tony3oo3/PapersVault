## Problem

Existing anti-face-recognition methods suffer from two critical gaps: (1) protected images form dense clusters in the feature space, so a protected query can still retrieve protected database entries with high accuracy—a realistic threat since scraped DBs will contain protected images; and (2) they assume front-facing faces, making them visually incoherent and ineffective for videos where head pose naturally varies over time.

## Input / Output

At deployment, Protego takes a single RGB facial image and a pre-trained privacy protection texture (PPT), a 2D perturbation map defined in canonical UV space. A lightweight face detector (MediaPipe) localizes the face region, and a pretrained UV mapping network (SMIRK) estimates the per-pixel UV coordinates of the input. The PPT is then deformed via differentiable grid sampling into a spatially-aligned 3D mask of the same dimensions as the input, which is subtracted from the image (clipped to [0,1]) to produce the protected output. The PPT itself is learned offline from a small set (~100) of the user's facial images using PGD-style optimization with an L∞ bound of 0.063.

## Method

Protego's key insight is a hypersensitivity loss that maximizes the log-determinant of the Gram matrix of protected feature vectors across an ensemble of FR models, forcing protected images to exhibit diverse, near-orthonormal features so that even a protected query cannot match protected DB entries. This is combined with a standard term penalizing similarity between protected and unprotected features, and an SSIM-based perceptual constraint to limit visual distortion. For pose invariance, the PPT is learned in a canonical UV coordinate space where each location corresponds to a semantic facial region; at inference, a pretrained UV mapping network projects the input image's pixels into this space, and differentiable grid sampling deforms the PPT into a pose- and expression-aligned 3D mask. Training uses sign-gradient (PGD) updates over mini-batches with dynamic SSIM weighting, and the entire PPT generation is a one-time offline process (6–75 minutes depending on hardware) after which protection of any new image takes ~0.02 s on GPU.

## Results

On FaceScrub, Protego reduces FR retrieval recall from a baseline of 71.68% to 18.09% in the hard scenario (both query and DB entries protected), and to ≤1.05% in the easy scenario (only one side protected). On LFW, the corresponding reductions are from 70.09% to 20.00% (hard) and ≤2.22% (easy). Protego achieves 3.5× and 2.7× better recall reduction than Chameleon and OPOM respectively in the hard case. The hypersensitivity loss is critical: removing it causes recall to jump to 78.52%, worse than no protection at all (71.68%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Protego is compared against two state-of-the-art per-user perturbation methods: Chameleon (ECCV 2024) and OPOM (TPAMI 2022). In the easy scenario (only one side protected), all three methods perform well, reducing recall to near-zero levels, as this is the setting implicitly assumed by prior work. The critical differentiator is the hard scenario where both the query and DB entries are protected: Protego drops recall to 18.09% (FaceScrub) and 20.00% (LFW), while Chameleon and OPOM suffer only a minor drop because their protected images form dense clusters in feature space, allowing protected queries to still retrieve protected entries. Protego achieves 3.5× and 2.7× better recall reduction than Chameleon and OPOM respectively. In qualitative retrieval (Table 3), Protego's protected query retrieves entries of unrelated individuals, whereas Chameleon and OPOM still return the correct person's protected entries. Protego also outperforms both baselines on visual quality metrics (PSNR, normalized L0) and maintains consistent protection across users with minimal variance over five random seeds.

## Relation to Prior Work

Protego extends the per-user perturbation mask paradigm established by OPOM and Chameleon, which learn a single reusable mask per individual to enable rapid protection of any of their images. It departs from these methods in two key ways: (1) introducing a hypersensitivity loss based on the Gram matrix determinant that forces protected images to exhibit diverse features rather than forming a dense cluster, and (2) replacing the 2D front-facing mask with a pose-invariant privacy protection texture (PPT) defined in UV space and deformed via grid sampling to match arbitrary head poses and expressions. The lineage traces from per-image adversarial optimization (Fawkes, LowKey) through ensemble-diversity approaches (PMask) to the per-user mask paradigm that Protego refines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a critical and previously unaddressed vulnerability in all existing anti-FR methods: protected images cluster tightly in feature space, so a protected query can retrieve protected database entries with high accuracy—a realistic threat given that intruders may unknowingly use protected images as queries. The 3D UV-mapping design also opens the door to video protection, which is increasingly relevant as services like PimEyes expand to video search. Limitations include the need for a one-time training phase with diverse-angle selfies, the fact that protection does not fully disable other face-centric tasks (age, expression estimation), and reliance on a small ensemble of FR models for training. Open questions include whether the hypersensitivity principle generalizes to other biometric modalities and whether the approach scales to very large, heterogeneous databases.
