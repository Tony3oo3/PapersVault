## Problem

Existing sketch colourisation methods face a fundamental trade-off: text-guided diffusion approaches are fast but spatially ambiguous, producing colour bleeding and semantic errors, while reference-based or manual methods offer precision at the cost of heavy labour. No prior training-free framework allows artists to directly specify both *where* (via region masks) and *what colour* (via palettes) should appear, while preserving the rich generative priors of pretrained diffusion models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262780/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 line-art sketch, a set of binary region masks (512×512×1, e.g., drawn in Photoshop), and corresponding colour palettes expressed as hex codes. The hex codes are mapped to CSS3 colour names via a 3-D K-D Tree over 147 reference colours, and the sketch class is inferred zero-shot by BLIP-2 VQA. The output is a fully colourised 512×512 RGB image produced in 15–20 DPM-Solver++ inference steps on a single consumer GPU (RTX 4090).

## Method

SketchDeco is a training-free, two-stage latent-composition pipeline. In the global stage, BLIP-2 infers sketch semantics, a K-D Tree converts hex palettes to colour names, and a pretrained Scribble ControlNet generates n+1 globally colourised images (one per palette plus a colour-free background). In the local stage, the user's masks are applied in pixel space to composite region-specific colours onto the background, the result is inverted to noisy latents via DPM-Solver++ ODE inversion using an exceptional prompt (TF-ICON) for trajectory stability, Gaussian noise is injected only in mask-boundary transition zones to let generative priors smooth edges, and a self-attention map from the composited image is injected during the first τ fraction of denoising steps to anchor global structure while text guidance handles the remaining blending. A scaling factor τ (default 0.4) controls the trade-off between structural/colour fidelity and harmonious blending, and no model weights are updated at any point.

## Results

On local sketch colourisation, SketchDeco achieves the best FID and DCCW (colour fidelity) across all four datasets: FID of 123.87 / DCCW 11.85 on Place365-Indoor, 112.48 / 7.65 on Place365-Outdoor, 134.54 / 9.23 on Danbooru2023, and 95.64 / 8.89 on PascalVOC2012. On global colourisation, the method attains the lowest FID on AFHQ-cat (50.31), AFHQ-dog (89.70), Place365-Indoor (113.99), Place365-Outdoor (129.11), and PascalVOC2012 (75.27), while IDeepColor achieves lower DCCW only because it uses ground-truth colour hints. The full pipeline runs in 15–20 DPM-Solver++ steps on a single RTX 4090 Super GPU without any fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SketchDeco outperforms all four local-colourisation baselines (ColorizeDiffusion, ColorFlow, MangaNinja, Cobra) on FID and DCCW across every dataset, confirming superior global consistency and local colour fidelity. On Danbooru2023, MangaNinja and Cobra achieve lower LPIPS/PSNR/SSIM (reflecting their line-art domain specialisation), but SketchDeco still leads on FID (134.54 vs. 304.17 and 138.48) and DCCW (9.23 vs. 19.61 and 13.57), indicating more realistic and colour-accurate outputs. For global colourisation, SketchDeco beats DiffBlender and T2I-Adapter on FID across all six sub-datasets (e.g., 50.31 vs. 86.82 on AFHQ-cat; 75.27 vs. 82.15 on PascalVOC2012). The T2I-Adapter + IDeepColor baseline achieves the best DCCW on global tasks (e.g., 6.93 on AFHQ-cat) but only because it uses 100 ground-truth colour hints, and it exhibits visible colour bleeding and desaturation artefacts. On PascalVOC2012 local colourisation, ColorizeDiffusion scores better on LPIPS/PSNR/SSIM (0.517/11.42/0.257) than SketchDeco (0.481/8.97/0.298), likely due to its line-art-like appearance, yet SketchDeco's FID (95.64 vs. 110.80) and DCCW (8.89 vs. 24.37) reveal substantially better perceptual quality and colour accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of sketch-to-image generation via ControlNet/T2I-Adapter, user-guided image colorization, and training-free latent-space composition (TF-ICON, Prompt-to-Prompt). It departs from text-prompt-driven colorization (which the authors argue is spatially ambiguous) and from reference-based or manual color assignment (which is labor-intensive) by reformulating the problem as a two-stage latent composition: a global ControlNet-based colourisation followed by a local ODE-inversion-and-self-attention-injection step. The key conceptual departure is treating region masks and hex-code palettes as first-class inputs that are 'painted' into noisy latents via guided inversion, rather than being mediated through textual descriptions or pixel-space blending.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, 15–20-step pipeline on a single consumer GPU makes precise, region-controlled sketch colourisation accessible without fine-tuning, which is practically important for design and animation workflows. However, the method still requires users to manually create region masks (e.g., in Photoshop), and the K-D Tree colour-name mapping is restricted to 147 CSS3 colours, limiting chromatic expressiveness. Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully to many overlapping regions, whether the pixel-space-then-invert composition strategy generalises to video or 3D, and whether the self-attention injection can be replaced by lighter-weight spatial priors to reduce the two-pass ODE solve.
