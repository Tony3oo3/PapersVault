## Problem

Existing LVLM fingerprinting methods (IF, Proflingo, PLA) rely on semantically abnormal trigger queries or out-of-distribution responses that adversaries can easily detect and strip away. The paper formalizes this vulnerability through the Semantic Divergence Attack (SDA), which uses a lightweight reference model to flag and substitute fingerprint interactions, showing that prior fingerprints are not semantic-preserving and lack robustness under realistic model modifications such as quantization and fine-tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263883/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a perturbed RGB trigger image (initialized from ImageNet-2012 validation set with an L∞ perturbation budget of 16/255) paired with a generation-task text prompt from the AMBER benchmark, and feeds them to a frozen LVLM under standard decoding. The output is a semantically coherent text response that carries a statistically detectable unigram watermark signal (50% vocabulary token list with logit offset 4), verified via a per-query z-score threshold calibrated on unrelated models to guarantee zero false positives. A total of 200 image-text pairs serve as the fingerprint query set, and the trigger images remain visually indistinguishable from their originals.

## Method

SIF is a non-intrusive fingerprinting framework built on two components. Semantic-Aligned Fingerprint Distillation (SAFD) distills a decoding-phase text watermark into the input image by jointly optimizing a watermark alignment loss (pushing the student's top-K=50 truncated distribution onto the key-dependent watermark token list G_t) and a semantic alignment loss (cross-entropy against the teacher's watermarked response), so that standard decoding of the perturbed image naturally yields watermarked yet semantically coherent text. Robust-Fingerprint Optimization (RFO) then simulates worst-case representation drift by computing gradients of the base loss with respect to all intermediate activations, aggregating them into a single norm-bounded perturbation (ρ=0.5), injecting it into the next forward pass, and re-optimizing the trigger under this perturbed computation. The full pipeline uses PGD with 1000 iterations and step size 1/255, requires no parameter modification to the LVLM, and takes approximately 6 minutes per fingerprint query on a single H100 GPU.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B, SIF achieves FMR of 0.49 (4-bit) and 0.89 (8-bit) under quantization, and 0.31–0.67 under full fine-tuning, consistently outperforming all baselines. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, SIF reaches 0.88 (4-bit) and 0.98 (8-bit) under quantization and 0.43–0.89 under fine-tuning. Under the Semantic Divergence Attack (SDA), SIF retains FMR of 0.86 (LLaVA) and 0.85 (Qwen2.5-VL), while Proflingo and IF drop to 0 and PLA to 0.07–0.11. On unrelated models, SIF achieves zero FMR, ensuring no false positives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIF outperforms all six baselines across every evaluation setting. Against PLA [40], the strongest prior method, SIF gains 0.09 FMR under 4-bit quantization on LLaVA (0.49 vs. 0.40) and 0.12 under 4-bit on Qwen2.5-VL (0.88 vs. 0.76); the gap widens dramatically under fine-tuning, e.g., 0.49 vs. 0.37 on MathV and 0.67 vs. 0.49 on Paintingform for LLaVA. Proflingo [17] and IF [43] are completely neutralized by the Semantic Divergence Attack (FMR drops to 0), while SIF retains 0.85–0.86 FMR, demonstrating a fundamental stealthiness advantage. IF [43] is also inapplicable to already-released models (marked N/A for Hugging Face fine-tuned variants), whereas SIF requires no parameter modification. Ordinary PGD [29], DIM [42], and CroPA [28] all suffer near-total fingerprint loss under fine-tuning (FMR ≤ 0.08 on most LLaVA fine-tuning tasks), while SIF maintains 0.31–0.67. On reliability, SIF achieves zero FMR on all unrelated models, whereas PLA produces a small false-positive rate of 0.005 on LLaVA-1.5-13B.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIF repurposes decoding-phase text watermarking (Zhao et al. [50]), originally designed for content provenance tracing, as the signal source for model-level fingerprinting—a cross-domain transfer that prior LVLM fingerprinting work (PLA, Proflingo, IF) did not explore. It departs from the adversarial-optimization lineage of PLA and Proflingo by optimizing trigger images to produce semantically coherent, in-distribution responses rather than fixed out-of-distribution phrases, and from intrusive methods like IF that require retraining. The Robust-Fingerprint Optimization component draws on adversarial training principles (worst-case perturbation) but applies them to intermediate activations rather than inputs, targeting representation drift from quantization and fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SDA attack exposes a practical vulnerability: existing LVLM fingerprints are nearly completely neutralized (FMR drops to 0–0.11) by a lightweight reference-model filter, undermining the security guarantees of current IP protection schemes. SIF's non-intrusive, black-box verification is directly deployable for already-released open-source models, addressing an urgent need as LVLMs proliferate on Hugging Face and GitHub. Open questions include scalability to 70B+ models, robustness against adaptive watermark-removal attacks that exploit the limited number of fingerprint queries, and whether the approach generalizes to non-generative or encoder-only LVLM architectures.
