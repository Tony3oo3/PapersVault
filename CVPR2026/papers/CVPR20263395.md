## Problem

Existing diffusion-based super-resolution methods generate HR images from random noise through multiple denoising steps, which limits the model's ability to focus on fine-grained details and leads to texture loss. These approaches rely on global image features and lack explicit high-frequency prior guidance, resulting in blurred or distorted fine details in the reconstructed output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263395/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution RGB image (h×w×3) and produces a 4× super-resolved high-resolution image (H×W×3). Before diffusion, the LR image is upsampled by a lightweight RepRFN network to create a PreSR image, which then serves as a persistent high-frequency guidance signal throughout all denoising steps. Diffusion is performed on the residual map (HR − PreSR) rather than the full image, narrowing the dynamic range the model must learn.

## Method

HDW-SR replaces the standard U-Net backbone with HDW-Net, which uses Haar wavelet decomposition in place of CNN pooling for lossless multi-scale frequency separation. The network comprises two sub-networks: HE-Net performs multi-level wavelet decomposition on the PreSR image to extract high-frequency subbands at each scale, while HA-Net processes the noisy residual through wavelet downsampling and applies Dynamic Focused Attention (DFA) to compute sparse cross-attention between the PreSR's high-frequency subbands (as K and V) and the diffused image's low-frequency subbands (as Q). A Dynamic Thresholding Block (DTB) replaces the conventional Top-k selection by computing an Otsu-style optimal threshold from intra-class and inter-class variances of the similarity matrix, yielding a data-adaptive binary mask for more precise high-frequency element selection. During upsampling, the network switches from using PreSR's high-frequency components to the diffused image's own high-frequency components to avoid amplifying pseudo-details as the denoising progresses toward t=0.

## Results

On DIV2K (4× SR), HDW-SR achieves PSNR 24.52, LPIPS 0.2823, DISTS 0.1934, NIQE 4.43, CLIPIQA 0.6937, and MUSIQ 69.68, ranking first on all perceptual and no-reference metrics. On RealSR, it reaches PSNR 25.71, LPIPS 0.2672, DISTS 0.2044, and MUSIQ 70.10, again leading on perceptual quality. On DrealSR, HDW-SR obtains PSNR 28.43, NIQE 6.20, CLIPIQA 0.6970, and MUSIQ 66.36, maintaining top performance on no-reference and perceptual scores while remaining within 0.02 dB of the best PSNR (ResShift, 28.45).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HDW-SR consistently outperforms all seven diffusion-based baselines on perceptual (LPIPS, DISTS) and no-reference (NIQE, CLIPIQA, MUSIQ) metrics across all three datasets, with the largest margins over ResShift (e.g., LPIPS 0.2823 vs. 0.3374 on DIV2K; MUSIQ 69.68 vs. 60.92) and StableSR (e.g., MUSIQ 70.10 vs. 65.42 on RealSR). The closest competitor is DiT-SR, which edges out HDW-SR on CLIPIQA for RealSR (0.6961 vs. 0.6702) and on SSIM for DrealSR (0.7835 via OSEDiff), but HDW-SR still leads on LPIPS, DISTS, NIQE, and MUSIQ for those datasets. On PSNR, ResShift retains a slight advantage (24.69 vs. 24.52 on DIV2K; 26.31 vs. 25.71 on RealSR), while HDW-SR is within 0.02 dB on DrealSR (28.43 vs. 28.45). Against GAN-based methods (RealESRGAN, BSRGAN, LDL), HDW-SR dominates every metric on both DIV2K and RealSR, achieving the best PSNR (24.52 / 25.71), lowest LPIPS (0.2823 / 0.2672), and highest MUSIQ (69.68 / 70.10), confirming that the wavelet-guided diffusion approach surpasses GAN-based SR in both fidelity and perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

HDW-SR sits at the intersection of three prior threads: the residual diffusion strategy of ResShift (which motivates performing diffusion on a PreSR-to-HR residual rather than the full image), the progressive sparse attention mechanism of PFA (which is adapted into the DFA encoder), and classical Otsu thresholding (repurposed as the Dynamic Thresholding Block for adaptive sparse-attention masking). Its key departure from the diffusion-SISR lineage is the replacement of the standard U-Net backbone with a Haar-wavelet decomposition network (HDW-Net), where high-frequency subbands from a lightweight PreSR image serve as explicit cross-attention guidance at every scale. This contrasts with prior wavelet-diffusion work (e.g., DiWa, Lifting Wavelet-Guided Diffusion) that operates the diffusion process itself in the wavelet domain, whereas HDW-SR keeps diffusion in the image domain and uses wavelets purely as a structural decomposition tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and recurring weakness of diffusion-based SISR—loss of fine-grained high-frequency detail—by injecting multi-scale wavelet priors through sparse cross-attention, a design that is modular and could be transplanted into other diffusion restoration pipelines. However, the performance gains over strong 2024–2025 baselines (OSEDiff, DiT-SR, AdcSR) are modest and mixed across metrics (e.g., PSNR on RealSR is actually lower than ResShift), and the method is evaluated only at 4× magnification with a single Haar wavelet, leaving open whether the approach scales to higher factors or benefits from richer wavelet families. The extreme sensitivity to the β hyperparameter (PSNR collapsing from 24.52 to 10.30 between β=0.2 and β=0.5) also raises practical concerns about training stability. A broader open question is whether explicit high-frequency guidance from a separate PreSR network is necessary, or whether a single end-to-end model could learn equivalent frequency separation.
