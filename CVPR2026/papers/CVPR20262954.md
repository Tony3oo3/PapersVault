## Problem

Existing pedestrian attribute recognition (PAR) systems rely exclusively on RGB frame cameras, making them vulnerable to lighting variations, motion blur, and cluttered backgrounds, while also ignoring the emotional dimension of pedestrian behavior. No multi-modal RGB-Event PAR dataset or benchmark has existed to address these limitations, and no framework has effectively fused asynchronous event streams with RGB frames for attribute recognition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262954/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a temporally aligned pair of inputs: an RGB video sequence (T×H×W×C) and event streams from a DVS346 camera, where each asynchronous event point {x, y, t, p} is stacked into event frames synchronized with RGB exposure times. The output is a multi-label prediction vector over 50 fine-grained attributes spanning 12 groups (gender, age, clothing, accessories, posture, activity, and six emotions), trained with a weighted cross-entropy loss to handle the long-tail attribute distribution present in the 100K-sample EventPAR dataset.

## Method

The framework employs VisionRWKV as a shared visual encoder for both RGB and event modalities, where each block combines a Spatial-Mix module (Bi-WKV linear-complexity bidirectional attention with Q-Shift token operations) and a Channel-Mix module (gated SquaredReLU channel fusion). The key architectural contribution is the OTN-RWKV asymmetric fusion module: first, a KNP-filter uses a self-similarity matrix over event tokens to retain only the top-K most informative tokens, reducing redundancy from the asynchronous event stream; then, an asymmetric cross-attention mechanism applies Bi-WKV with RGB tokens as queries and filtered event tokens as keys/values, replacing the learnable bonus vector from LCR with the spatial-mix output V_s to focus attention on the current sample. This design preserves fine-grained multimodal detail while avoiding the information loss inherent in concatenation or addition-based fusion, and the linear-complexity attention keeps inference tractable for video-length sequences.

## Results

On the proposed EventPAR dataset, OTN-RWKV with RGB+Event input achieves mA=87.70, Acc=84.94, Prec=89.15, Recall=89.48, and F1=89.18, outperforming all 17 retrained baselines on four of five metrics (VTB leads only on mA at 88.41). Using RGB alone, the method still scores mA=79.32 and F1=83.22, while adding the event modality yields a substantial +8.38 mA and +5.96 F1 gain. On MARS-Attribute, the method reaches Acc=73.21, Prec=85.63, Recall=81.53, F1=83.22; on DukeMTMC-VID-Attribute it achieves Acc=73.15, Prec=84.45, Recall=82.16, F1=82.78, both setting new state-of-the-art on accuracy and precision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On EventPAR, OTN-RWKV (RGB+Event) achieves the best Acc (84.94), Precision (89.15), Recall (89.48), and F1 (89.18), surpassing the strongest baselines SequencePAR (F1=88.83) and VTB (F1=88.53) by 0.35 and 0.65 F1 points respectively; however, VTB retains a slight edge on mean Accuracy (88.41 vs. 87.70). On MARS-Attribute, the proposed method leads all six compared baselines in Accuracy (73.21 vs. VTB's 72.73) and Precision (85.63 vs. 82.79), though it trails VTB on Recall (81.53 vs. 83.52). On DukeMTMC-VID-Attribute, it similarly outperforms VTB in Accuracy (73.15 vs. 72.50) and Precision (84.45 vs. 83.60) while matching closely on Recall (82.16 vs. 82.24). The Mamba-based baselines (MambaPAR, MaHDFT) perform substantially worse across all datasets (F1 ≈ 55–59 on EventPAR), and the PLIP-pretrained variants (VTB-PLIP, Rethink-PLIP) also underperform their non-pretrained counterparts, suggesting that domain-specific training remains critical for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established pedestrian attribute recognition (PAR) task in two conceptual directions: adding an event-camera modality (DVS346) alongside RGB frames, and incorporating six emotion attributes absent from all prior PAR benchmarks. Architecturally, it builds directly on VisionRWKV [6] as the visual encoder and adapts the Bi-WKV linear-attention mechanism into a novel asymmetric OTN-RWKV fusion module, drawing inspiration from the LCR cross-attention design [51] but replacing its learnable bonus vector with the sample-specific V_s projection. The paper departs from the dominant Transformer-based PAR literature by demonstrating that linear-complexity RWKV blocks can match or exceed ViT and ResNet-50 backbones while reducing FLOPs, and it positions event-based vision (previously applied to super-resolution, deblurring, and segmentation) as a natural complement to RGB for attribute recognition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EventPAR is the first large-scale (100K paired samples) multi-modal RGB-Event PAR dataset and the first to include emotion attributes, providing a much-needed benchmark with 17 retrained baselines that the community can use to track progress in event-based attribute recognition. The finding that event-only input (5 frames) nearly matches the full RGB+Event combination, and that increasing RGB frame count beyond one actually degrades performance, suggests that event streams carry most of the discriminative temporal information for attributes—a counterintuitive result that could reshape how practitioners allocate sensor resources. Open questions include whether learned (rather than hand-crafted) event representations can further close the gap, whether the approach generalizes beyond the DVS346 hardware, and how the emotion attributes interact with appearance attributes in downstream tasks like person re-identification or retrieval.
