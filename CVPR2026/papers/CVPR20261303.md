## Problem

Slice-based volumetric modalities such as ultrasound and light-sheet microscopy produce 2D slices with a finite focal thickness governed by an anisotropic point spread function, yet no existing Gaussian splatting rendering model correctly accounts for this intermediate regime. Standard 3DGS, designed for surface appearance from multi-view images, discards internal volumetric structure and produces incoherent 3D reconstructions with severe floating artifacts when naively applied to slice data, while INR-based compression methods are too slow and lose high-frequency detail for practical use.

## Input / Output

The method takes a stack of 2D slices from a slice-based imaging system along with the axial PSF width σ_z (estimable from the Nyquist sampling interval δ_z when unavailable) and produces a sparse set of anisotropic 3D Gaussian primitives. These primitives support three downstream uses: real-time 2D slice rendering via additive rasterization, 3D volume reconstruction through a differentiable voxelizer operating on 8³ tiles at >100 FPS, and a compact compressed file via Morton-order sorting, adaptive per-attribute quantization (14-bit positions, 12-bit opacities/scales/quaternions), delta-encoding, and LZMA entropy coding achieving 16–26× reduction over raw voxel storage.

## Method

GaussianPile re-engineers the 3DGS forward pass with a physics-aware focus model in three stages: (1) axial re-parameterization injects the imaging system's axial resolution σ_z into the inverse covariance matrix (Σ_e⁻¹ = Σ_c⁻¹ + e₃e₃ᵀ/σ_z²), producing a 'Focus Gaussian' whose finite axial support matches the slice formation physics; (2) opacity modulation attenuates primitives far from the focal plane using the change in Mahalanobis distance, suppressing ghosting artifacts; (3) additive rasterization accumulates weighted 2D marginal Gaussian footprints without occlusion, appropriate for slice imaging where intensities superimpose. The representation discards spherical harmonics entirely since intensity is covariance-driven rather than view-dependent, reducing per-primitive parameters by ~40%. Training follows the standard 3DGS loop with one randomly sampled slice per iteration, L1 + D-SSIM loss, and a tile-wise densify-and-prune strategy, converging in ~3–13 minutes on an A800 GPU.

## Results

GaussianPile (30k iterations) achieves the best 2D PSNR across all five datasets: 33.07 dB on TDSC-ABUS, 34.57 dB on rDL-LSM, 42.08 dB on TNNI1, 32.59 dB on hiPSC, and 36.14 dB on Tribolium, with corresponding SSIM values of 0.825, 0.791, 0.948, 0.728, and 0.848. On 3D volumetric metrics (Table 3), it reaches 33.22/0.876 (ABUS), 34.47/0.814 (rDL-LSM), 40.17/0.952 (TNNI1), 33.73/0.752 (hiPSC), and 35.05/0.860 (Tribolium) PSNR/SSIM. Training converges in 5–13 minutes (avg. ~8 min), roughly 5× faster than the best INR baseline (INIF) and up to 11× faster than NeRF-based approaches. With Morton-order quantization and LZMA entropy coding, the method achieves 16–26× compression over raw voxel grids while preserving rendering fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the traditional HEVC codec, GaussianPile surpasses it on PSNR for ABUS (33.07 vs. 29.67), rDL-LSM (34.57 vs. 29.34), and hiPSC (32.59 vs. 19.73), while HEVC retains a slight edge on TNNI1 (35.76 vs. 42.08 — actually GaussianPile wins here too) and Tribolium (35.51 vs. 36.14 — GaussianPile also wins). Among INR baselines, INIF is the strongest on TNNI1 (30.89 PSNR) but is outperformed by GaussianPile on every dataset by 2–15 dB, while also requiring 27–84 min of training versus 5–13 min. CoordNet and NeurComp show mixed 2D results (e.g., CoordNet reaches 31.98 on rDL-LSM, NeurComp 39.25 on TNNI1) but are consistently 3–12 dB below GaussianPile and 4–12× slower. The most direct comparison is slice-adapted 3DGS [14], which GaussianPile outperforms on all five datasets by 4–12 dB in 2D PSNR and by 5–18 dB in 3D PSNR (Table 3), while also training 1.5–9× faster; 3DGS's 3D fidelity is particularly poor on hiPSC (15.20 vs. 33.73 PSNR) and Tribolium (21.46 vs. 35.05), confirming that without the focus-aware model, Gaussian primitives produce incoherent volumetric structures despite plausible 2D projections.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussianPile extends 3D Gaussian Splatting [14] to slice-based volumetric imaging by introducing a focus-aware forward model that encodes the finite-thickness point spread function of the acquisition system. Prior 3DGS adaptations addressed either all-in-focus (σ_z→∞, e.g., X-ray [4]) or zero-thickness (σ_z→0, e.g., MRI [20]) regimes, but the intermediate finite-σ_z case dominant in ultrasound and light-sheet microscopy remained unresolved. The method also draws on INR-based compression literature [8,12,22] for the compression angle, but departs from it by using a rasterizable sparse Gaussian representation with a tailored Morton-order quantization and entropy-coding scheme rather than a neural network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, deployable solution for the storage and interactive exploration of large slice-based volumetric datasets (ultrasound, LSM), achieving 16× compression over voxel grids and 3-minute convergence—capabilities that INR-based methods cannot match in speed. The key open question is whether the spatially invariant PSF assumption holds under real optical aberrations, and whether the approach generalizes to severely noisy or undersampled data without oversmoothing. Future directions include learnable spatially varying PSFs, 4D spatiotemporal extension for live-cell imaging, and feed-forward reconstruction to bypass iterative optimization entirely.
