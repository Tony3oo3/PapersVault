## Problem

Existing neuron interpretation methods assume every neuron encodes a well-defined, discriminative concept and that concepts inferred from high-activation samples are inherently correct. In practice, redundant or low-discrimination neurons produce misleading descriptions, and the observational hypothesis process is vulnerable to probe-dataset biases. The paper asks whether neuron-concept associations can be validated through intervention rather than accepted at face value.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261091/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a pretrained network (ResNet-18/50 or ViT-B/16), a probe dataset (ImageNet-1K val, Places365 test, or Eurosat), a predefined concept vocabulary (Broden 1.2k or Common Words 3k), and a target neuron index in the penultimate layer. It outputs a set of verified natural-language concept labels per neuron (up to K=2 per cluster) together with an Activation Rate score; concepts whose AR falls below the running mean are discarded, so the final output is a filtered, validated subset of hypotheses rather than a raw description list.

## Method

SIEVE is a three-stage pipeline. (1) Select: for each neuron, the 99th-percentile-to-median ratio of its activation distribution over the probe set is compared against a threshold β (default 10); neurons passing the threshold yield their top-20 high-activation samples. (2) Hypothesize: each sample is cropped by its Grad-CAM-style activation map, features are extracted, and agglomerative clustering (cluster count set by Silhouette score) partitions the patches into m groups; CLIP image-text cosine similarity then assigns the top-K=2 concepts from the predefined vocabulary to each cluster. (3) Verify: each hypothesized concept is used as a text prompt for Stable Diffusion (SDXL) to generate a set of images independent of the probe data; the Activation Rate is the fraction of generated images whose neuron activation exceeds the Top-1% threshold of the probe distribution, and any concept whose AR falls below the global mean is rejected. This constructive intervention—generating stimuli from the hypothesis and measuring the neuron's response—closes the loop that purely observational methods leave open.

## Results

On ResNet-50 (ImageNet-1K), SIEVE achieves a mean Activation Rate of 86.29% in the penultimate layer, approximately 1.5× the best baseline (CLIP-Dissect at 57.91%). In the final layer, SIEVE scores 0.7914 CLIP cosine similarity and 0.4547 MPNet cosine similarity with the Common Words (3k) concept set, matching or exceeding all baselines. The same pattern holds on ViT-B/16 (85.24% mean AR) and ResNet-18 on Places365 (84.40% mean AR), confirming generalizability across architectures and datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIEVE outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. The most dramatic gap is on mean Activation Rate: SIEVE reaches 86.29% versus CLIP-Dissect's 57.91% (the strongest baseline), a 28.4-point improvement that validates the verification step. On final-layer CLIP cosine similarity, SIEVE (0.7914) edges out CLIP-Dissect (0.7868) and WWW (0.7713), while on MPNet cosine it leads all methods (0.4547 vs. 0.4463 for WWW). Against DnD, which leverages GPT-3.5 Turbo for open-ended descriptions, SIEVE gains 34.8 points in mean AR (86.29 vs. 51.46), showing that unconstrained language-model descriptions introduce many mismatched concepts that verification filters out. FALCON and Network Dissection, which rely on more limited concept sets or pixel-level IoU matching, trail by 30–40 points in mean AR. The consistent ~1.5× mean AR advantage across ResNet-50, ViT-B/16, and ResNet-18 (Places365) indicates the Select-Hypothesize-Verify loop is architecture- and dataset-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIEVE extends the natural-language neuron interpretation lineage (Network Dissection → CLIP-Dissect → FALCON → DnD → WWW) by adding two missing stages: a selection mechanism that filters out low-discrimination neurons via activation-distribution analysis, and a verification stage that uses text-to-image generation to test whether hypothesized concepts actually activate the target neuron. The concept-matching algorithm is directly inherited from CLIP-Dissect, while the verification step draws on intervention-based methods (LIME, neuron ablation) but repurposes them constructively—generating stimuli rather than ablating them. The paper frames this as closing a loop that prior methods leave open: they observe activations and hypothesize concepts but never test whether the hypothesis is causally supported.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that SIEVE provides a principled way to discard incorrect neuron-concept assignments, which prior methods silently propagate as if all neurons encode meaningful features. The ~1.5× improvement in mean Activation Rate (86% vs. ~57% for the best baseline) across ResNet-18/50 and ViT-B/16, on both ImageNet and Places365, suggests the verification step catches a substantial fraction of spurious interpretations. Limitations include dependence on text-to-image generation quality (domain shift to Eurosat degrades all methods, though verification still helps), reliance on a predefined concept set, and evaluation restricted to penultimate-layer neurons in classification networks. Open questions include whether the framework scales to deeper or generative models, whether the activation-rate threshold is robust across architectures, and whether the selection criterion (99th-percentile-to-median ratio) is optimal for identifying truly discriminative neurons.
