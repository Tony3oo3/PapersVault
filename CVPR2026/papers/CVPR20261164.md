## Problem

Existing RGBT trackers assume perfect spatial alignment between RGB and thermal infrared modalities, which is unrealistic for multi-sensor systems where installation offsets and field-of-view discrepancies cause significant misalignment. The few methods that handle unaligned inputs estimate all cross-modal alignment parameters (shift and scale) simultaneously, making them inflexible to varying misalignment degrees during tracking and requiring complex models that incur heavy computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261164/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes spatially unaligned RGB and TIR video sequences with heterogeneous resolutions (e.g., 1600×1200 RGB paired with 640×480 TIR, up to 3840×2160/1280×1024) and no manual pre-alignment. It outputs a target bounding box per frame along with decomposed cross-modal alignment parameters at three granularities: a 2D center offset, a 4-parameter scale-plus-offset transformation, and a 4-parameter residual refinement, rather than a single monolithic homography matrix.

## Method

PMATrack decomposes cross-modal alignment into three progressive stages mapped to shallow, middle, and deep Transformer layers: center offset prediction from geometric cues, scale transformation estimation from combined geometric-semantic cues, and global residual refinement from high-level semantics. At each stage, a difficulty-aware router adaptively selects among three alignment experts—Target-Response (optimal transport on saliency maps), Feature-Matching (frequency-decomposed cross-modal correlation), and Detail-Perception (Tiny U-Net fine-grained features)—balancing accuracy against a cost-penalized selection loss. A Transformation-guided Cross-Modal Deformable Attention (TCMDA) module uses the predicted homography as a geometric prior for sampling, combined with learned local offsets, to perform alignment-aware feature fusion. During inference, a Template-Offset Contrastive Update (TOCU) mechanism maintains a dynamic homography matrix that is selectively updated based on IoU-based reliability checks, preventing drift-induced target loss. Training is two-stage: first the OSTrack backbone is trained with standard tracking loss, then only the alignment network and TCMDA are trained with smooth-L1 offset regression, BCE response supervision, and the cost-penalized expert selection loss.

## Results

On the LasHeR-Unaligned dataset, PMATrack achieves PR=64.4, NPR=58.7, and SR=50.6 at 28.0 FPS, outperforming the previous best AINet (PR=61.4, NPR=55.7, SR=48.3) by +3.0/+3.0/+2.3 points. On the newly introduced MUART244 dataset with larger cross-modal offsets, PMATrack reaches PR=62.7, NPR=55.9, and SR=45.8, surpassing AINet by +5.4/+5.5/+4.7 points and UnTrack by +8.6/+8.0/+5.9 points. The method also outperforms the dedicated unaligned tracker NAT (PR=58.1, NPR=52.3, SR=44.8) by +6.3/+6.4/+5.8 points on LasHeR-Unaligned, validating the progressive alignment strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMATrack outperforms all 21 compared baselines on both evaluation datasets. On LasHeR-Unaligned, it surpasses the strongest aligned-modality tracker AINet [27] (PR 61.4→64.4, +3.0) and the dedicated unaligned tracker NAT [21] (PR 58.1→64.4, +6.3), confirming that progressive parameter decoupling beats both simultaneous homography estimation and all-layer token fusion. On MUART244, where misalignments are substantially larger, the gap widens: PMATrack leads AINet by +5.4 PR and the unified tracker SUTrack [3] by +13.2 PR, while also exceeding UnTrack [37] by +8.6 PR. Among aligned-modality trackers, TBSI [13] (PR 60.3) and BAT [1] (PR 60.5) are the closest competitors on LasHeR-Unaligned, but both drop sharply on MUART244 (PR 53.1 and 44.5 respectively), highlighting their inability to handle dynamic misalignment. The difficulty-aware expert routing keeps PMATrack at 28 FPS, competitive with AINet (38.1 FPS) while delivering substantially higher accuracy, and well above NAT (19 FPS) and SDSTrack (20.9 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

PMATrack builds on OSTrack as its tracking backbone and extends the template-update strategy from TBSI-Ext into a dynamic offset-maintenance mechanism (TOCU). Conceptually, it departs from prior unaligned RGBT trackers (NAT, AMNet) that regress all cross-modal alignment parameters (shift, scale, rotation) in a single monolithic step; instead, PMATrack decomposes alignment into a coarse-to-fine pipeline (center offset → scale → residual refinement) and introduces a difficulty-aware router that selects among three cue-specific experts (target response, feature matching, detail perception) to balance accuracy and FLOPs. The Transformation-guided Cross-Modal Deformable Attention (TCMDA) module repurposes deformable attention with homography-derived geometric priors, bridging the gap between explicit geometric alignment and learned feature fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: real-world RGBT sensor rigs rarely achieve pixel-perfect alignment, yet most trackers assume it, forcing costly manual preprocessing. The MUART244 benchmark (multi-platform, multi-resolution, 26 categories, 22 challenges) fills a gap left by the single-resolution LasHeR-Unaligned dataset and will likely become the standard evaluation set for unaligned RGBT tracking. Open questions include whether the progressive decomposition generalizes to more than two modalities (e.g., RGB-T-IR), whether the difficulty-aware router can be distilled for edge deployment, and how the method performs under extreme viewpoint changes or partial sensor occlusion that go beyond the tested challenge types.
