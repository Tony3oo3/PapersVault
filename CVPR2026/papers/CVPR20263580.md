## Problem

Current VLM-based embodied question answering agents suffer from two coupled failures: reactive planning that selects the next action solely from the current view, producing incoherent trajectories under partial observability, and monolithic memory that stores all observations indiscriminately, burying sparse question-relevant evidence under irrelevant frames. The paper asks whether reframing EQA through predictive processing—where the agent forms testable hypotheses about where evidence will appear and corrects them through interaction—can yield coherent long-horizon exploration and efficient evidence accumulation.

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives 3 egocentric RGB-D views (120° field of view aligned with heading; 6 views at 360° at initialization) and a natural-language question, and outputs either a text answer or a navigation direction toward a selected frontier. Frontiers are extracted by clustering TSDF boundary voxels within a 1.7 m explored-radius, and episodes are capped at 50 steps. The system is entirely training-free: all reasoning is performed by a locally deployed VLM (Qwen2.5-VL 7B or Qwen3-VL 8B) invoked via vLLM with per-agent prompt contexts, requiring no fine-tuning or auxiliary detectors.

## Method

Pred-EQA implements a recurrent prediction-correction-update loop with two coupled mechanisms. On the planning side, a high-level VLM planner generates structured predictive exploration branches—each a semantic hypothesis (e.g., 'the kitchen is behind that doorway') with a status flag and a refinement annotation that can confirm, update, or archive prior predictions—while a low-level executor selects the frontier that maximally reduces uncertainty within the active branches, pruning branches when predictions are falsified. On the memory side, a dual-memory system separates a textual structural memory (symbolic step entries summarizing stable spatial-semantic priors, accumulated as a growing prior for the planner) from a visual evidence memory (a VLM-filtered set of at most a few question-relevant frames per step, discarding anything already captured textually or irrelevant to the question). This architecture is instantiated as a multi-agent pipeline (Answerer, Planner, Executor, Recorder, Manager) over a frontier-based navigation baseline, and is evaluated training-free on OpenEQA and Express-Bench.

## Results

On A-EQA (OpenEQA), Pred-EQA with Qwen3-VL 8B achieves 53.3 LLM-Match and 48.5 LLM-SPL, surpassing the best proprietary-VLM baseline (3D-Mem, GPT-4o) by +0.7% in accuracy and +6.5% in exploration efficiency. With the smaller Qwen2.5-VL 7B, Pred-EQA still reaches 46.2 LLM-Match, outperforming all open-source VLM agents by +10.7%. On Express-Bench, Pred-EQA (Qwen3-VL 8B) achieves 70.54 C* and 47.66 E_path, improving over the SOTA ToolEQA by +2.77% in scene-aware correctness and +20% in exploration efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pred-EQA outperforms all eight baselines on both benchmarks. On A-EQA, it beats the strongest proprietary-VLM agent 3D-Mem (GPT-4o, 52.6 LLM-Match / 42.0 LLM-SPL) by +0.7% accuracy and +6.5% SPL with Qwen3-VL 8B, and surpasses MTU3D (51.1 / 42.6) by +2.2% / +5.9%. Among open-source VLM agents, Pred-EQA (Qwen2.5-VL 7B) leads ToolEQA (35.5) by +10.7% LLM-Match and Explore-EQA* (27.4) by +18.8%. On Express-Bench, Pred-EQA (Qwen3-VL 8B) improves over ToolEQA (the prior SOTA) by +5.23% in general correctness C, +2.77% in scene-aware C*, and +21.84 in E_path (47.66 vs. 25.82), while ToolEQA retains a slightly smaller mean geodesic distance d_T (5.25 vs. 5.64) due to its object-detector-based physical approach. Notably, even the 7B open-source variant of Pred-EQA exceeds all proprietary-VLM baselines, indicating the gains stem from the predictive architecture rather than model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant reactive paradigm in VLM-based EQA agents (Explore-EQA, Fine-EQA, 3D-Mem, MemoryEQA), which select the next action based solely on the current observation and accumulate all frames in a monolithic memory. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, it repurposes the predictive-processing / active-inference framework from cognitive science (Friston's free-energy principle) as a design lens, instantiating it as a hierarchical prediction-correction loop (high-level semantic branch predictor + low-level uncertainty-reducing executor) paired with a functionally split dual memory (textual structural priors vs. sparse visual evidence). It also contrasts with LLM planner work from tool-use and robotic-control settings, arguing that those assume near-full observability and static environments, whereas EQA's partial observability demands continuous prediction revision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical takeaway is that architectural design (predictive planning + specialized memory) can compensate for model scale: a 7B open-source VLM with Pred-EQA outperforms GPT-4o-based proprietary agents, suggesting that embodied agents need not rely on ever-larger VLMs if the planning and memory structure is principled. The prediction-correction framing also offers a reusable template for other partially observable embodied tasks beyond EQA. Limitations include reliance on frontier-based exploration in indoor HM3D scenes, VLM-prompted (not learned) planning, and evaluation on only two benchmarks; whether the dual-memory separation and branch-pruning generalize to outdoor, multi-room, or temporally dynamic settings remains open.
