## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models frequently misidentify present objects or hallucinate absent ones when objects appear in unexpected contexts or are missing from expected ones, yet existing benchmarks (POPE, AMBER, HallusionBench) all preserve object-context consistency and never probe this high-uncertainty regime where weak local visual evidence opposes strong scene priors. This paper identifies contextual incongruity as a previously unexplored source of recognition failure and asks how severely it degrades state-of-the-art LVLMs and whether targeted training can mitigate the resulting uncertainty-driven errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261576/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample pairs an MSCOCO RGB image with a binary existence question ("Is there a [object] in the image?") where the queried object is deliberately incongruous with the surrounding scene. The image is implicitly decomposed into an ROI (the queried object region) and a context (all other elements); evaluation runs at temperature 0 with a 1,024-token limit, averaging over four prompt variants. In the Visual-RFT fine-tuning setting, the model additionally emits a structured reasoning trace enclosed in <REASONING> and <SOLUTION> tags before the final yes/no answer, which is used to compute verifiable format and correctness rewards.

## Method

ORIC constructs incongruous object-context pairs via two complementary strategies: (1) LLM-guided sampling for positive questions, where objects with bounding-box area below the 50th percentile are designated ROI, and GPT-5 is queried to flag which ROI objects are contextually unexpected given the non-ROI categories, yielding hard-to-recognize present objects; and (2) CLIP-guided sampling for negative questions, where the most visually similar image is retrieved via cosine distance in CLIP embedding space, and nonexistent objects with the highest CLIPScore against that similar image are selected as plausible-but-absent hallucination triggers. For uncertainty mitigation, the paper fine-tunes Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct using Visual-RFT (GRPO with verifiable binary rewards for answer correctness and format compliance) on 600 ORIC-style samples, replacing the PPO critic with group-relative advantage normalization over G=8 sampled responses per prompt, which enforces evidence-grounded reasoning without requiring large supervised datasets.

## Results

On ORIC-Bench (2,000 binary object-existence questions from 1,000 MSCOCO validation images), Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct achieves the highest macro F1 of 79.55, narrowly surpassing GPT-5 (78.61) and InternVL3-9B (76.87). Most evaluated models cluster between 60–77 F1, with vision-encoder-free models (best: Emu3-Chat at 64.78) and open-vocabulary detectors (Grounding DINO 1.5 Pro at 72.48, OWLv2 at 72.02) trailing the top LVLMs. After Visual-RFT fine-tuning on 600 ORIC-style samples, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct improves to 82.79 macro F1 on ORIC-Bench and gains 3.01 points on AMBER (87.48→90.49), while remaining stable on HallusionBench (69.37→69.81).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct (79.55 F1) outperforms GPT-5 (78.61) by 0.94 points and InternVL3-9B (76.87) by 2.68 points on ORIC-Bench, showing that open-source vision-encoder LVLMs can match or exceed proprietary systems under contextual incongruity. Vision-encoder-based models consistently dominate vision-encoder-free counterparts: the best encoder-free model (Emu3-Chat, 64.78) trails the best encoder-based model by 14.77 F1 points, attributed to the lack of structured ViT features for fine-grained perception. Open-vocabulary detectors (Grounding DINO 1.5 Pro at 72.48, OWLv2 at 72.02) lag the top LVLMs by ~7 points, as their region-text alignment lacks holistic reasoning about object absence. Llama-3.2-11B-Vision exhibits extreme class bias (YP=0%, F1=33.33), systematically predicting 'no' due to an overfitted identity-safety heuristic. After Visual-RFT fine-tuning, Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct improves from 79.55 to 82.79 F1 on ORIC-Bench and from 87.48 to 90.49 on AMBER, while HallusionBench performance remains stable (69.37→69.81), confirming generalization without overfitting to ORIC-style data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the existing LVLM hallucination and recognition benchmarks (POPE, AMBER, HallusionBench), which all maintain object-context consistency and thus never test the regime where scene priors oppose local visual evidence. For the training component, it builds directly on Visual-RFT and GRPO, repurposing verifiable-reward reinforcement learning from text reasoning to binary object-existence questions under incongruous contexts. The benchmark construction methodology is novel in combining LLM-guided sampling (GPT-5 identifying hard-to-recognize present objects) with CLIP-guided sampling (mining plausible-but-absent objects), a pipeline not seen in prior benchmark construction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ORIC-Bench provides the first systematic diagnostic for a specific, previously unmeasured failure mode in LVLMs, and the finding that even GPT-5 drops from near-perfect to ~60% F1 on a 50-question controlled swap underscores that contextual priors remain a dominant error source. The practical recipe of fine-tuning with only 600 ORIC-style samples via Visual-RFT is attractive for resource-constrained settings. However, the benchmark is limited to MSCOCO validation images and binary yes/no existence questions, leaving open whether the findings generalize to open-ended recognition, diverse domains, or compositional queries. Future work should explore whether the incongruity effect persists in video, 3D, or multi-object reasoning settings.
