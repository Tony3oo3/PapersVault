## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for dense 3D shape correspondence rely on handcrafted descriptors (e.g., wks, shot) or require pre-alignment of input shapes, fundamentally limiting robustness under non-isometric deformations, partiality, non-manifold structures, and arbitrary rotations. No prior method simultaneously achieves invariance to extrinsic orientation, effective exploitation of surface geometry, and robustness to noise and topological variations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260673/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

RINO takes raw xyz vertex coordinates of two shapes (meshes or point clouds) with no preprocessing, no handcrafted features, and no pre-alignment. It produces SO(3)-invariant per-point features of dimension 3c, from which three complementary map representations are derived: a soft pointwise map via softmax on feature similarity, a functional map C solved via a differentiable convex optimization, and a complex functional map Q based on feature gradients. At inference, dense correspondences are obtained in a single forward pass via Euclidean nearest-neighbor search in the learned feature space.

## Method

RINONet revises DiffusionNet to be SO(3)-equivariant end-to-end by replacing scalar hidden states with vector neuron (VN) representations of dimension n×c×3. The key architectural constraint is that the learned diffusion time must be shared across all three VN dimensions to preserve equivariance, and the gradient layer computes an invariant feature via summation along the VN dimension before multiplying back with the equivariant diffused feature to maintain expressivity. A VN-invariant layer at the end converts the equivariant features to invariant ones. Training is fully unsupervised, using a novel coupling loss that enforces consistency among three map representations (pointwise, FMap, CFMap); the CFMap term is critical because it can theoretically only encode orientation-preserving maps, thereby eliminating intrinsic symmetry flips without requiring extrinsic pre-alignment.

## Results

On the primary non-rigid matching benchmark (Table 2), RINO with raw xyz input achieves mGeoErr of 4.6 on SMAL, 5.3 on DT4D, and 2.5 on FAUSTSCAN, outperforming all six unsupervised baselines by large margins (e.g., 4.6 vs. 9.9 for SmpFM on SMAL/xyz). With wks input, RINO is competitive (2.7 on FSCAN, best overall) but trails DUOFM (6.7) on SMAL and HbrFM (5.4) on DT4D. The SO(3)-invariance test (Table 1) is the most striking: RINO maintains a constant mGeoErr of 4.6 across all rotation settings (I/I, I/SO(3), SO(3)/SO(3), Y/Y), while baselines degrade by 4–12× under unseen rotations (e.g., DUOFM: 11.7→41.4; SMS: 4.6→57.6). On partial matching (Table 4), RINO achieves 6.7 (CUTS) and 12.09 (HOLES), beating even the supervised EchoMatch (12.7/57.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RINO decisively outperforms all six unsupervised baselines when using raw xyz coordinates as input, achieving the best mGeoErr on all three datasets (SMAL 4.6, DT4D 5.3, FSCAN 2.5). The closest competitor with xyz is SmpFM (9.9 on SMAL, 5.5 on DT4D), which still trails by 2.3–21.8 mGeoErr; DUOFM and URSSM collapse under random rotations (25.2 and 24.8 on SMAL/xyz respectively). With wks input, RINO is competitive but not dominant: DUOFM leads on SMAL (6.7 vs. 28.7) and HbrFM on DT4D (5.4 vs. 9.8), while RINO wins on FSCAN (2.7 vs. 3.0 for URSSM). On the SO(3)-invariance stress test, RINO's constant 4.6 mGeoErr across all rotation configurations contrasts sharply with every baseline, which degrades 4–12× under unseen rotations. On symmetry (Table 3), RINO produces zero flipped correspondences on both FAUST and SCAPE, whereas all baselines exhibit 63–249 flips out of 400 test pairs. On partial matching (Table 4), RINO (6.7 CUTS, 12.09 HOLES) beats URSSM (42.1/29.8), Wormhole (48.2/26.9), and even the supervised EchoMatch (12.7/57.1), confirming that rotation invariance is critical for handling unaligned partial shapes.

## Relation to Prior Work

RINO re-architects DiffusionNet [54]—the de facto standard feature extractor for deep shape correspondence—by replacing its scalar hidden states with vector neurons [19] to enforce SO(3)-equivariance end-to-end, eliminating the need for handcrafted descriptors (wks, shot) or extrinsic pre-alignment. It further integrates Complex Functional Maps [21] (which encode only orientation-preserving maps) with the classic Functional Maps framework [43] in a novel triple-branch unsupervised loss that couples pointwise, functional, and complex-functional map representations. This departs from prior unsupervised methods (DUOFM, URSSM, SmpFM, etc.) that either rely on spectral descriptors sensitive to non-isometry, require aligned xyz inputs, or cannot theoretically resolve intrinsic symmetries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making rotation invariance an architectural guarantee rather than a data-augmentation strategy, RINO removes a major practical bottleneck in 3D shape correspondence and unifies rigid and non-rigid matching in a single end-to-end pipeline. The method's consistent performance across arbitrary poses, non-isometric deformations, partial shapes, and noise suggests a path toward genuinely data-driven shape understanding that does not depend on theoretical assumptions (isometry) or preprocessing. Open questions include whether the vector-valued features can be consumed directly without flattening (as the authors note as future work), how the approach scales to very large or highly non-manifold shapes, and whether the CFMaps-based symmetry resolution generalizes to shapes with complex topological symmetries beyond left-right flips.
