## Problem

Large video diffusion models require hundreds of iterative denoising steps, making them impractical for real-time interactive applications. Existing distillation methods either treat the diffusion backbone as a monolithic mapping or struggle to preserve both global motion coherence and fine-grained spatial details when compressing trajectories to a few steps in the high-dimensional video domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262148/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The distilled student takes a text prompt and generates an 81-frame 480×832 video (latent shape [21, 60, 104]) in as few as 1–4 effective forward passes. Unlike standard distillation where NFE is an integer number of full-network steps, TMD supports fractional effective NFE (e.g., 1.38 or 2.33) because the flow head uses only a subset of DiT blocks (H out of L total), giving fine-grained control over the speed–quality tradeoff.

## Method

TMD decouples the pretrained video DiT into a main backbone (early layers extracting semantic features per outer transition step) and a lightweight recurrent flow head (last few layers performing N inner refinement steps to predict the transition target y = x₁ − x). Training proceeds in two stages: (1) Transition Matching MeanFlow (TM-MF) pretraining, which converts the flow head into a conditional flow map using a MeanFlow objective conditioned on backbone features, and (2) DMD2-ν distribution matching distillation, which applies a VSD loss plus a Conv3D GAN discriminator to the unrolled student output, backpropagating gradients through all N inner flow steps to close the train–inference gap. DMD2-ν introduces three video-specific improvements over the original DMD2: Conv3D discriminator layers, KD warm-up restricted to one-step distillation only, and a timestep shifting function to prevent mode collapse.

## Results

On VBench for distilling Wan2.1 1.3B, TMD-N2H5 achieves an overall score of 84.68 at effective NFE=2.33, outperforming the strongest baseline rCM (84.43 at NFE=4) and the DMD2-ν baseline (84.60 at NFE=4). In the one-step regime (NFE≈1), TMD-N2H5 reaches 83.80 at NFE=1.17, surpassing DMD2-ν (83.24) and rCM (82.65). For the larger Wan2.1 14B model, TMD-N4H5 achieves 84.24 at NFE=1.38, improving over one-step rCM by +1.22 points while adding only minimal inference cost. The teacher Wan2.1 1.3B itself scores 84.26 at NFE=100, meaning TMD recovers nearly all quality at roughly 2.3% of the compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest trajectory-based baseline rCM, TMD wins at every comparable NFE: at NFE≈2, TMD-N2H5 (84.68) beats rCM (84.09) by +0.59 overall; at NFE≈1, TMD-N2H5 (83.80) beats rCM (82.65) by +1.15. Against the distribution-based DMD2-ν baseline (the paper's own improved DMD2), TMD-N2H5 at NFE=2.33 edges out DMD2-ν at NFE=4 (84.68 vs 84.60) while using fewer effective forward passes, and at NFE≈1 TMD (83.80) leads DMD2-ν (83.24) by +0.56. Older distilled methods DOLLAR (82.57) and T2V-Turbo-v2 (82.34) at NFE=4 trail TMD by over 2 points, and APT at NFE=1 (82.00) is outperformed by TMD by +1.80. For the 14B model (Table 2), TMD-N4H5 at NFE=1.38 (84.24) surpasses one-step rCM (83.02) by +1.22 and one-step DMD2-ν (83.69) by +0.55, while at NFE=2.75 it matches rCM (84.92 vs 85.05) at lower cost. The full teacher Wan2.1 1.3B scores 84.26 at NFE=100, so TMD at NFE=2.33 (84.68) actually exceeds the teacher's VBench score, suggesting the distillation process can even improve quality at a fraction of the inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

TMD sits at the intersection of three prior lines: Transition Matching (TM), which models large probabilistic jumps between widely separated noise levels via an inner flow; MeanFlow, which learns flow maps between arbitrary timesteps; and DMD2, which distills via distribution matching (GAN + VSD losses). The key departure is architectural: rather than treating the diffusion backbone as a monolithic mapping (as most prior distillation methods do), TMD decouples it into a semantic main backbone and a lightweight recurrent flow head, then backpropagates distillation gradients through the unrolled inner flow at each transition step. This design is inspired by decoupled-backbone generative models (DDT, TM) but is specifically adapted for distilling pretrained video diffusion models into few-step generators, a setting where prior trajectory-matching methods struggle with training instability at video scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TMD provides a practical path toward near-real-time video generation (effective NFE ≈ 1.4) while retaining competitive VBench scores, which is important for interactive applications like content editing and world modeling. The decoupled architecture offers fine-grained, fractional control over the speed-quality tradeoff that monolithic few-step models cannot match. Open questions include whether the two-stage training (TM-MF pretraining + DMD2-v distillation) can be unified into a single pipeline, and how TMD composes with system-level optimizations (feature caching, sparse attention) that are orthogonal but complementary. A limitation is that the method still requires a large pretrained teacher and a 500k-pair dataset for distillation, so it does not reduce the upfront training cost.
