## Problem

Dense Video Object Captioning (DVOC) requires jointly detecting, tracking, and describing all object trajectories in a video, but no existing dataset provides dense (mask, box, category, caption) annotations for every object, and prior methods resort to disjoint training on separate subtask datasets or multiple specialized models, preventing end-to-end optimization. This data scarcity and architectural fragmentation limit performance on all three subtasks simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a video as N_clip consecutive clips of T frames each (e.g., 4 uniformly sampled frames for the data-generation pipeline) and produces, for every tracked object trajectory, a per-frame segmentation mask, a bounding box, and a single natural-language caption summarizing the object's appearance and action across the full video. The captioning head operates at the video level after tracking, so one caption is generated per trajectory rather than per clip, and the model can additionally output segmentation masks beyond the box-based predictions of prior DVOC methods.

## Method

CaptionFormer builds on OVFormer's Mask2Former-based clip-level segmentation, extending it with a 4-layer MLP detection head for boxes and a BLIP-2-based captioning head (OPT-2.7B LLM) for text generation. A top-K enhanced query-matching module with Hungarian bipartite matching tracks object queries across clips, enabling re-identification of objects that disappear and reappear. The key architectural novelty is temporal aggregation: tracked queries from T_agg uniformly sampled clips are processed through the BLIP-2 query transformer and combined via a detection-score-weighted sum to form a single video-level query, which the LLM decoder converts into one caption per trajectory. For training data, the authors generate synthetic object-level captions by prompting Gemini 2.0 Flash with a multi-modal prompt (drawn bounding boxes as visual cue plus textual category labels, bbox coordinates, area, and few-shot examples), extending LVIS and LV-VIS into LVISCap and LV-VISCap. Training proceeds in two stages—segmentation/detection first, then the frozen backbone with a tuned captioning head—using a combination of segmentation, detection, classification, and language-modeling losses at both clip and video levels.

## Results

On the VidSTG benchmark, CaptionFormer with temporal aggregation and pretraining on COCO+LVISCap+LV-VISCap achieves a CHOTA of 64.0 (CapA 55.4, DetA 66.8, AssA 71.0), outperforming the previous best (DVOC-DS, CHOTA 56.9) by 7.1 points. On VLN, the best configuration reaches CHOTA 47.7 (CapA 23.4, DetA 50.1, AssA 92.7), surpassing DVOC-DS (41.3) by 6.4 points. On BenSMOT, CaptionFormer achieves DetA 91.6, AssA 87.5, and CIDEr 42.6, improving CIDEr by +17.2 over DVOC-DS (25.4). The method also extends DVOC to segmentation masks, a capability no prior baseline provides.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CaptionFormer outperforms all five baselines across all three benchmarks. Against the strongest prior DVOC method, DVOC-DS [81], CaptionFormer gains +7.1 CHOTA on VidSTG (64.0 vs 56.9), +6.4 CHOTA on VLN (47.7 vs 41.3), and +17.2 CIDEr on BenSMOT (42.6 vs 25.4), while also requiring far less training compute (208 vs 2032 GPU hours). Compared to OW-VISCaptor [18], which uses a similar BLIP-2-based captioning head, CaptionFormer improves CHOTA by +11.0 on VidSTG (64.0 vs 53.0), driven primarily by superior detection (DetA 66.8 vs 60.1) and tracking (AssA 71.0 vs 54.0). The OVFormer [21] baseline, which uses predicted category labels as captions, achieves only CHOTA 34.6, confirming that class names are insufficient for meaningful object descriptions. Gemini-2.5-flash [19] fails catastrophically (CHOTA 6.6) due to inability to produce correctly formatted localized outputs in 13.6% of videos. On BenSMOT, SMOTer [41] is the only additional baseline; CaptionFormer surpasses it by +10.8 DetA and +33.9 CIDEr, though AssA is slightly lower (87.5 vs 73.7 for SMOTer, but 89.6 for DVOC-DS).

## Relation to Prior Work

CaptionFormer composes three existing components into a unified end-to-end pipeline: OVFormer's clip-level open-vocabulary segmentation backbone, CLIP-VIS's top-K query-matching tracking module, and a BLIP-2-based captioning head adapted from OW-VISCapTor. The key departure from prior DVOC work (DVOC-DS, OW-VISCapTor) is the introduction of a VLM-based synthetic data generation pipeline that produces the first training sets with jointly annotated (mask, box, category, caption) tuples, enabling true end-to-end training rather than the disjoint or isolated training strategies used previously. The temporal aggregation of tracked queries across uniformly sampled clips before captioning is a novel architectural addition not present in any prior DVOC or OV-VIS method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first practical recipe for scaling DVOC training data via VLM-generated object-level captions, a pipeline that is directly reusable for other vision-language tasks suffering from annotation scarcity. The finding that the captioning loss improves segmentation mAP by 2.5 points suggests that language supervision acts as a useful regularizer for visual queries, hinting at broader benefits for open-vocabulary segmentation. Open questions include whether the synthetic caption quality (averaging 13.9 words, generated by a single VLM) becomes a ceiling for performance, how the approach scales to longer or more complex videos, and whether the evaluation bias from synthetic captions (shown to be marginal on a 100-video subset) persists at larger scale.
