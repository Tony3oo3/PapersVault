## Problem

Diffusion-based video inverse problem solvers face a fundamental trade-off: image diffusion priors produce temporally inconsistent results and remain slow, while native video diffusion priors offer strong temporal coherence but require iterative posterior sampling that is 100× too slow for real-time streaming. No existing method achieves both diffusion-level reconstruction quality and real-time throughput for video restoration tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes degraded video measurements (random masks, Gaussian blur, or 4× downsampled frames) at 832×480 resolution, optionally conditioned on a text prompt for semantic control. It produces a fully restored video in a single forward pass, processed causally in temporal blocks with KV caching to support streaming. Notably, training requires only the degraded measurements y and the frozen teacher model—no paired clean/noisy video data is needed, as degraded inputs are generated on-the-fly from clean videos via known forward operators.

## Method

InstantViR distills a bidirectional video diffusion model (Wan2.1-1.3B) into a causal autoregressive student via a ground-truth-free variational objective combining a data-fidelity loss (enforcing consistency with the measurement y) and a score-matching prior loss (aligning the student's output distribution with the teacher's diffusion score). The student uses block-causal attention—bidirectional within each T-frame block and causal across blocks with standard KV caching—enabling low-latency streaming inference. To overcome the VAE bottleneck, the authors introduce a teacher-space regularized distillation scheme that replaces the original video VAE with the lightweight LeanVAE: the likelihood term is evaluated in the new latent space, while the prior term maps latents back through the original encoder so the frozen teacher score remains applicable, bridging the induced latent distribution shift. This yields a one-step feed-forward solver achieving >35 FPS on A100 GPUs with up to 100× speedup over iterative baselines.

## Results

On 50% random inpainting, InstantViR† achieves the best PSNR (31.78 dB) and LPIPS (0.13), while on Gaussian deblurring it leads all metrics (PSNR 31.16, SSIM 0.97, LPIPS 0.15). For 4× super-resolution, VISION-XL retains a slight PSNR edge (35.69 vs. 34.91 for InstantViR), though InstantViR matches or exceeds it on LPIPS. The headline speed result is that InstantViR† runs at 35.56 FPS on a single A800 GPU, achieving over 100× speedup over SVI (0.29 FPS) while simultaneously attaining the lowest FVD on inpainting (132.59) and deblurring (103.45).

## Baselines & Comparisons

InstantViR and its LeanVAE-accelerated variant InstantViR† consistently outperform all four baselines on Gaussian deblurring, achieving the highest PSNR (31.85/31.16 vs. 30.03 for VISION-XL and 26.93 for SVI), SSIM (0.97), and LPIPS (0.17/0.15). On 50% random inpainting, InstantViR† leads in PSNR (31.78 vs. 30.83 for VISION-XL) and LPIPS (0.13 vs. 0.17 for SVI), while VISION-XL retains a marginal SSIM advantage (0.95 vs. 0.96 is actually InstantViR† winning). On 4× super-resolution, VISION-XL achieves the best PSNR (35.69) and SSIM (0.98), edging out InstantViR (34.91/0.96), and InstantViR† drops substantially in PSNR (27.04) due to the LeanVAE latent shift. The most striking comparison is speed: InstantViR† at 35.56 FPS is roughly 123× faster than SVI (0.29 FPS) and over 200× faster than VISION-XL (<0.17 FPS), while simultaneously achieving the lowest FVD on inpainting (132.59) and deblurring (103.45), confirming that the one-step amortized solver matches or exceeds iterative diffusion baselines in both quality and temporal coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the amortized variational inference framework for diffusion-based inverse problems—previously demonstrated in the image domain (Lee et al., ECCV 2024)—to the video setting, where the prior is a native bidirectional video diffusion model (Wan2.1) rather than an image model. It departs from the dominant paradigm of iterative posterior sampling (DPS, SVI, VISION-XL) by distilling the teacher's multi-step denoising trajectory into a single causal autoregressive forward pass, drawing architectural inspiration from the bidirectional-to-autoregressive distillation line (Yin et al., 2024). The teacher-space regularized distillation scheme for swapping in LeanVAE is a novel contribution that addresses latent distribution shift, a problem not previously tackled in the video inverse-problem literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InstantViR makes diffusion-quality video restoration practical for streaming, interactive, and AR/VR applications by achieving 35+ FPS with up to 100× speedup over iterative baselines while matching or exceeding their temporal coherence (FVD). The ground-truth-free training requirement (only degraded measurements + frozen teacher) removes the need for massive paired datasets, making the framework broadly applicable to new degradation types without retraining from scratch. Key open questions include whether the slight quality gap of the LeanVAE variant can be closed through joint VAE fine-tuning, whether the approach scales to longer sequences or higher resolutions, and whether the causal block architecture generalizes to tasks requiring global temporal reasoning beyond local coherence.
