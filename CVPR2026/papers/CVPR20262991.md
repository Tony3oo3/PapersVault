## Problem

In privacy-preserving prototype-based personalized federated learning, the standard defense of isotropic Gaussian noise (IGPP) over-perturbs the most discriminative feature dimensions, degrading class separability, while a fixed ℓ2 clipping threshold creates a dilemma: a large threshold inflates the required noise scale, and a small one irreversibly erases semantic content. No existing mechanism adaptively reallocates privacy budget across feature dimensions or stabilizes feature norms to mitigate clipping distortion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262991/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client encodes local RGB images through a frozen ViT-small backbone (512-dim hidden size) with trainable adapters, producing per-sample embeddings that are grouped by class to form prototypes. The uplink communication consists solely of privatized class prototypes (means or cluster centroids) with groupwise Gaussian noise, while the downlink delivers global prototypes for local contrastive or classification fine-tuning. The final output per client is a personalized classifier producing class labels, with no raw data or full model parameters ever leaving the client.

## Method

VPDR is a client-side plug-in with two modules. VPP computes ANOVA-normalized discriminative scores per dimension, privately selects a discriminative subspace via the oneshot Laplace Top-k mechanism (consuming a fraction r of the privacy budget), then applies groupwise ℓ2 clipping with dimension-proportional bounds and injects group-specific Gaussian noise calibrated so that discriminative coordinates receive strictly less perturbation than the isotropic baseline while preserving the same (ε,δ)-LDP guarantee. DCR appends a differentiable soft-clipping layer to the local encoder and enforces prediction consistency between pre- and post-clipped features via an EMA teacher-student KL divergence, driving feature norms to concentrate near the clipping threshold and breaking the norm-weight compensation shortcut. The two modules are complementary: VPP optimizes the noise allocation at release time, while DCR stabilizes the feature geometry during local training, and together they yield consistent accuracy gains across six ProtoPFL frameworks on three multi-domain benchmarks without increasing communication cost.

## Results

Across all six ProtoPFL frameworks and three multi-domain benchmarks (Digits, Office-Caltech, PACS), VPDR consistently improves average accuracy over the IGPP baseline. Gains are most pronounced on the heterogeneous PACS benchmark, where FedPCL jumps from 79.73% to 86.91% (+7.18 pp) and FedProto from 90.58% to 92.71% (+2.13 pp). On Office-Caltech, FedTGP improves from 92.31% to 95.04% (+2.73 pp) and MPFT from 92.23% to 94.71% (+2.48 pp). Standard deviation (cross-domain dispersion) also typically shrinks, e.g., FedPCL on PACS drops from 16.08 to 11.84, indicating more stable personalization across domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPDR is evaluated as a plug-in against the IGPP (isotropic Gaussian prototype perturbation) baseline within six ProtoPFL frameworks: FedProto, FedPCL, FPL, FedPLVM, FedTGP, and MPFT. VPDR wins on every framework–benchmark combination, with the largest absolute gains on the most heterogeneous PACS dataset (up to +7.18 pp for FedPCL) and Office-Caltech (up to +2.73 pp for FedTGP). On the relatively easier Digits benchmark, gains are smaller but still consistent (+1.0 to +1.6 pp). Cross-domain standard deviation decreases in most settings, confirming that variance-adaptive noise allocation reduces inter-domain variance. Under privacy attacks (Table 4), VPDR's defense metrics (FSH cosine similarity, MIA ROC-AUC) match IGPP within one standard deviation, confirming no privacy leakage from the adaptive mechanism. Under extreme label skew on CIFAR-10 (α=0.1), VPDR yields the largest relative gains (e.g., FPL: 28.42%→37.18%), while the advantage narrows as data becomes balanced (α=10).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototype-based Personalized Federated Learning (ProtoPFL) line of work (FedProto, FedPCL, FPL, FedPLVM, FedTGP, MPFT) by addressing a specific privacy gap: existing prototype-release defenses inject isotropic Gaussian noise without formal per-example clipping or full local DP accounting. It departs from the standard IGPP recipe (uniform l2 clipping + isotropic Gaussian noise) by introducing a dimension-wise adaptive mechanism grounded in ANOVA discriminative scores and the oneshot Laplace Top-k selection, combined with a distillation-guided soft-clipping regularizer. The theoretical framing builds on the Gaussian mechanism and sequential composition for LDP, while the EMA teacher-student distillation borrows from self-supervised learning to stabilize feature norms under clipping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VPDR offers a drop-in client-side plug-in that improves the privacy-utility trade-off across six ProtoPFL frameworks without changing communication cost or server-side logic, making it immediately adoptable in existing pipelines. The theoretical guarantee that groupwise reallocation is no weaker than isotropic perturbation under the same (ε,δ) budget is a clean and useful result for practitioners calibrating privacy. Open questions include whether the ANOVA-based discriminative proxy remains reliable under extreme class imbalance or very small local sample sizes, whether the fixed budget split ratio r=0.1 generalizes to higher-dimensional backbones (e.g., ViT-Base/Large), and whether the approach extends naturally to non-prototype personalization paradigms such as prompt-based or adapter-based PFL.
