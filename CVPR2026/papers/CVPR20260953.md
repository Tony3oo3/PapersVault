## Problem

Low-light image enhancement is an ill-posed one-to-many inverse problem: regression-based models produce overly-smooth 'averaged' outputs, while existing diffusion-based approaches rely on unidirectional formulations (noise-to-image or image-to-image) that struggle to preserve content fidelity. No prior work has exploited the symmetric relationship between enhancement and degradation as an explicit training constraint within a diffusion bridge framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260953/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model consumes paired (low-light, normal-light) RGB image pairs from the LOL dataset. At inference, a single low-light image is fed in with the normal-light counterpart designated as the bridge endpoint x_T, and the reverse SDE is solved iteratively to produce the enhanced output; conversely, setting the low-light image as x_T yields a degraded output. A single shared U-Net handles both directions, with the direction indicator m simply determining which image serves as the conditioning endpoint.

## Method

Bi-Bridge exploits the structural symmetry of DDBM's tractable Gaussian bridge mean with respect to its two endpoints: for each training pair, a binary variable m stochastically swaps which image is the startpoint x_0 and which is the endpoint x_T, forcing one shared U-Net predictor D_θ to learn both enhancement and degradation simultaneously. Training uses a pred-x parameterization where the network predicts the startpoint via MSE loss (implicitly performing score matching through the bridge score reparameterization), augmented by an auxiliary perceptual loss on deep features. At inference, a higher-order hybrid sampler (Euler-Maruyama stochastic step followed by Heun's deterministic corrector) solves the reverse SDE from t=T to t=0, with the score function dynamically derived from the network's startpoint prediction at each step. This bidirectional consistency acts as a powerful implicit regularizer, compelling the network to separate invariant content from variable illumination, yielding a +4.93 dB PSNR gain over the unidirectional DDBM baseline.

## Results

Bi-Bridge achieves SOTA performance across all three paired benchmarks: 31.019 dB PSNR / 0.025 LPIPS on LOL-v2-Synthetic, 31.287 dB PSNR / 0.040 LPIPS on LOL-v2-Real, and 27.879 dB PSNR / 0.052 LPIPS on LOL-v1. The most striking gain is over its direct unidirectional baseline DDBM, with +4.934 dB PSNR on LOL-v2-Real (31.287 vs. 26.353), attributed solely to the bidirectional consistency constraint. On zero-shot unpaired evaluation, Bi-Bridge attains the best NIQE on 4 of 5 datasets (e.g., 3.11 on MEF and NPE, 3.14 on LIME), outperforming the next-best ReDDiT by 0.13–0.13 NIQE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest regression-based method CIDNet [35], Bi-Bridge gains +2.894 dB PSNR on LOL-v2-Real (31.287 vs. 28.393) and +1.453 dB on LOL-v2-Synthetic, confirming the advantage of a generative formulation over one-to-one regression. Versus the best Noise-to-Image diffusion method ReDDiT [15], Bi-Bridge leads by +0.368 dB PSNR on LOL-v2-Real and +0.853 dB on LOL-v2-Synthetic, while matching LPIPS (0.040) on the real set. Within the Image-to-Image Bridge category, the gap is largest: Bi-Bridge surpasses the general-purpose bidirectional BDBM [14] by +6.185 dB PSNR on LOL-v2-Real and outperforms its direct unidirectional baseline DDBM [46] by +4.934 dB, a gain the authors attribute entirely to the symmetric training constraint rather than any architectural change. On zero-shot generalization (Table 2), Bi-Bridge achieves the best NIQE on DICM (3.35), LIME (3.14), MEF (3.11), and NPE (3.11), narrowly edging out ReDDiT [15] and CIDNet [35], while GSAD [9] slightly leads on VV (3.18 vs. 3.19). The performance-efficiency analysis (Figure 5) further shows that Bi-Bridge at 10 NFEs matches or exceeds DDBM and I²SB [18] at 80 NFEs, indicating roughly 4× sampling efficiency from the bidirectional objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

Bi-Bridge extends the Denoising Diffusion Bridge Model (DDBM, ICLR 2024) by exploiting the structural symmetry of its Gaussian bridge mean to enforce bidirectional consistency through stochastic endpoint swapping during training. Unlike the general-purpose BDBM framework (KDD 2025), which is built on the Chapman-Kolmogorov equation for arbitrary distributions, Bi-Bridge is a task-specific, simpler design that leverages the particular symmetry of the Gaussian bridge as an implicit regularizer. It also departs from invertible normalizing-flow approaches like LLFlow (AAAI 2022), where invertibility serves exact likelihood computation rather than explicitly modeling the physical degradation process. The pred-x parameterization and higher-order hybrid sampler are adapted from Karras et al. (NeurIPS 2022).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a simple stochastic endpoint-swap training objective yields a +4.93 dB PSNR gain over the unidirectional baseline—without any architectural change—suggests that bidirectional consistency is a powerful, underexploited regularizer for ill-posed inverse problems more broadly. The emergent capability where the bidirectionally trained model outperforms a unidirectional specialist for degradation hints at a more fundamental, transferable representation of illumination change. However, the iterative SDE-sampling nature remains a barrier to real-time deployment, and the paper acknowledges inference acceleration as a key open direction. Whether this symmetric-bridge regularizer transfers to other paired image translation tasks (e.g., deblurring, super-resolution) remains an open question.
