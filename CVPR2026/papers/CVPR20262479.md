## Problem

Diffusion Transformers suffer from low inference efficiency due to repeated timesteps and transformer blocks, and existing training-free acceleration methods (caching and pruning) rely on fixed or heuristic configurations that fail to capture fine-grained sensitivity variations across timesteps, layers, and modules. This leads to skipping highly sensitive computations and degrading generation fidelity, while also exhibiting poor cross-model generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262479/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SODA is a training-free acceleration wrapper applied to existing DiT models (DiT-XL/2, PixArt-α, OpenSora). It takes the same inputs as the base model—class labels for ImageNet generation or text prompts for MS-COCO and video generation—and produces the same outputs (images or videos) but at reduced latency. A one-time offline sensitivity modeling step (using 100 random samples for images, 10 for video) precomputes model-specific sensitivity error tables that are then loaded at inference with negligible memory overhead (~0.16–0.27 MB).

## Method

SODA unifies caching and pruning decisions through three components. First, Offline Fine-grained Sensitivity Modeling (OFS) measures cosine-distance errors between accelerated and ground-truth features across all timesteps, layers, and modules for both caching (varying cache intervals) and pruning (varying pruning ratios), stored as model-specific priors. Second, Dynamic Caching Scheduling Optimization (DCS) formulates cache-interval selection as a dynamic programming problem where sensitivity error serves as the cost function, yielding a globally optimal combination of cache intervals for a given acceleration budget with no runtime overhead. Third, Unified Adaptive Strategy Formulation (UAS) adaptively determines pruning timing by only applying pruning when its sensitivity error is lower than the caching error at the same node, and sets the pruning rate proportionally to the local caching sensitivity error, thereby skipping only insensitive tokens while preserving sensitive computations.

## Results

On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet, 50-step DDIM), SODA at 2.49× speedup achieves FID 2.75, sFID 4.56, and IS 235.65, outperforming ToCa (FID 3.05) and DuCa (FID 3.05) by 0.30 FID. At a lower 1.94× speedup, SODA reaches FID 2.39 with IS 240.75, surpassing the unaccelerated 50-step baseline (FID 2.25, IS 239.97) in IS. On PixArt-α (MS-COCO2017), SODA at 1.88× speedup achieves FID-30k 27.33 and CLIP 16.42, beating DuCa (FID 28.05) by 0.72. On OpenSora (VBench), SODA at 2.50× speedup scores 78.49%, exceeding ToCa (78.34%) and DuCa (78.39%) while matching the original 30-step quality (79.13%) at 1.42× speedup with zero degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SODA consistently outperforms all five baselines across all three generation tasks. On DiT-XL/2 (DDIM, ~2.5× speedup), SODA reduces FID by 0.30 over both ToCa and DuCa (2.75 vs. 3.05) and by 0.33 over FORA (2.80), while also improving sFID by 0.10–0.14 and IS by 2–8 points. On PixArt-α (~1.9× speedup), SODA achieves FID-30k 27.33 versus 28.38 (ToCa) and 28.05 (DuCa), a 0.72–1.05 FID advantage, and reaches the highest CLIP score of 16.44 at 2.21× speedup. On OpenSora (~2.5× speedup), SODA scores 78.49% VBench, beating ToCa (78.34%), DuCa (78.39%), PAB (76.95%), FORA (76.91%), and Δ-DiT (78.21%). Notably, at low acceleration (1.42× on video, 1.55× on image), SODA matches or even slightly exceeds the unaccelerated model's quality, whereas baselines show measurable degradation at comparable speedups. The advantage is most pronounced at moderate speedups (2–2.5×), where heuristic baselines like ToCa and DuCa skip highly sensitive computations, while SODA's sensitivity-aware scheduling preserves them.

## Relation to Prior Work

SODA extends the caching-pruning pipeline introduced by ToCa and DuCa, which combine feature caching across timesteps with token-level pruning to accelerate Diffusion Transformers. The key departure is replacing the fixed or heuristic scheduling of cache intervals and pruning rates with a sensitivity-aware, data-driven approach: an offline fine-grained sensitivity error model across timesteps, layers, and modules feeds into a dynamic programming solver for globally optimal cache-interval placement and an adaptive pruning decision that only prunes when its error is lower than pure caching. This unifies two previously independently configured operations under a single error-minimization objective, drawing on the dynamic programming formulation from prior sensitivity-based scheduling work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training-free acceleration of DiT models is critical for real-world deployment in latency-sensitive or resource-constrained settings, and SODA's sensitivity-driven approach offers a principled alternative to hand-tuned heuristics with demonstrated cross-model generalization (DiT-XL/2, PixArt-α, OpenSora) without per-model re-tuning. The offline modeling cost is negligible (under one hour, <0.3 MB storage), making the method practical. However, the authors acknowledge that SODA still lags behind training-based methods like distillation, and its integration with such techniques remains an open question. A broader open question is whether the sensitivity-error framework can be extended to other acceleration axes (quantization, architectural compression) or to models with fundamentally different inductive biases.
