## Problem

Euclidean Gramian volumes for multimodal alignment suffer from volume collapse: L2 normalization forces Gram matrices toward identity, yielding det(G)≈1.0 with negligible variance (std=0.005), which eliminates both cross-sample discrimination and within-pair semantic sensitivity. Moreover, Euclidean polynomial volume growth (V∝r³) cannot match the exponential expansion of interpretation spaces as text descriptions grow semantically richer. Pure hyperbolic geometry preserves variance but underperforms on cross-category discrimination, leaving a gap that neither geometry alone fills.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips, audio clips, and text descriptions, encoding them via EVA-CLIP ViT-g/14, BEATs, and BERT-base respectively into d-dimensional embeddings. Rather than computing pairwise cosine similarities, the method constructs a 3×3 Gram matrix from Lorentzian inner products of projected embeddings and extracts a scalar pseudo-volume √|det(G)| as the alignment score. These hybrid volumes serve directly as similarity logits in a contrastive retrieval objective, producing ranked video-text pairs for zero-shot evaluation.

## Method

HyperGRAM projects Euclidean embeddings onto the Lorentz hyperboloid via π(x)=[√(1+‖x‖²), x], computes a hyperbolic Gram matrix using Lorentzian inner products, and defines a pseudo-volume as √|det(G_Hyp)|. A learnable scalar α∈[0,1] (initialized at 0.5) blends this with the Euclidean volume: V_α=(1−α)V_Hyp+αV_Euc, enabling data-driven balancing of hyperbolic semantic variance with Euclidean discriminative stability. Training combines a volume-based contrastive loss (matched triplets learn smaller volumes) with a Data-Anchor Matching binary classifier for hard-negative mining, weighted by β=0.1. The key mechanism is that the position-dependent timelike component x⁰=√(1+‖x‖²) in the Lorentzian inner product preserves spatial norm variance through Gram entries, preventing the identity collapse that plagues L2-normalized Euclidean Gram matrices and yielding 20–25× higher volume variance (std=0.12 vs 0.005).

## Results

HyperGRAM achieves zero-shot T2V Recall@1 of 56.6% (MSR-VTT), 51.3% (DiDeMo), 58.2% (ActivityNet), and 79.9% (VATEX), with corresponding V2T R@1 of 53.6%, 49.5%, 51.8%, and 75.7%. These represent consistent improvements of +1.8% to +2.9% T2V R@1 and +1.0% to +2.2% V2T R@1 over the Euclidean GRAM baseline across all four benchmarks. The method introduces only one learnable scalar parameter (α) and <3% training overhead relative to Euclidean GRAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperGRAM outperforms all 16 baselines on every benchmark where they overlap. Against the direct Euclidean GRAM baseline [7], gains range from +1.5% (DiDeMo T2V) to +2.9% (VATEX T2V) in T2V R@1, and from +1.0% (DiDeMo V2T) to +2.2% (ActivityNet V2T) in V2T R@1. The closest non-GRAM competitor, PMRL [25], is surpassed by +2.1% T2V on MSR-VTT (56.6 vs 54.5) and +2.2% on ActivityNet (58.2 vs 56.0), though PMRL edges out HyperGRAM on VATEX T2V (80.5 vs 79.9). The VAST backbone [5] that HyperGRAM builds upon is improved by +5.9% T2V on MSR-VTT (56.6 vs 50.7). Notably, the pure hyperbolic variant (α=0) underperforms Euclidean GRAM on DiDeMo T2V (49.1 vs 49.8) and VATEX T2V (76.7 vs 77.0), confirming that neither geometry alone is sufficient and motivating the hybrid mixing that recovers and exceeds both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends GRAM (Cicchetti et al., ICLR 2025), which introduced Euclidean Gramian volumes as a higher-order multimodal alignment metric, by reformulating the Gram determinant in the Lorentz model of hyperbolic geometry. It also draws on the hyperbolic representation learning line (Nickel & Kiela's Poincaré/Lorentz embeddings, MERU's hyperbolic image-text representations, Ramasinghe et al.'s modality gap analysis) and mixed-curvature learning (product manifolds of Balazevic et al., Gu et al.), but departs from those by using a simpler convex combination of Euclidean and hyperbolic volumes rather than separate subspaces. The 'interpretation space' formalization is a new conceptual framing that motivates why exponential geometric capacity is needed, though it is presented as a hypothesis rather than a proven theorem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The volume collapse problem (std=0.005, det(G)≈1) in Euclidean Gramian volumes is a genuine limitation that undermines the discriminative utility of the metric, and the 20–25× variance recovery in hyperbolic space is a concrete, well-measured fix. However, the learned mixing parameter α converging to ≈0.5 across all four datasets is somewhat anticlimactic, suggesting the hybrid is closer to a fixed average than a truly adaptive geometry selection, and the interpretation space theory remains motivational rather than rigorously proven. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond three modalities, whether the Lorentz model's numerical advantages persist at larger embedding dimensions, and whether the cross-dataset correlation patterns (positive for coherent narratives, negative for fragmented captions) can be leveraged for downstream tasks like caption quality assessment.
