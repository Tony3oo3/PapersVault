## Problem

Spiking neural networks suffer from inherent temporal inconsistency: differences in membrane potential and input current across timesteps cause spike maps to vary widely, degrading representation quality especially at low latency. Prior work (e.g., MPS) addresses this indirectly via membrane potential smoothing and logit distillation, but requires modifying neuron dynamics, making it incompatible with fixed neuron models on neuromorphic chips. The paper tackles the open challenge of efficiently and universally promoting spike consistency without altering neurons or architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260499/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either neuromorphic event data (sparse binary events with dimensions [t, x, y, p] where p is polarity) or static RGB images, processed by an SNN backbone over T discrete timesteps (default T=4 for low-latency evaluation). Internally, the method operates on binary spike feature maps of shape T×B×C×H×W from the backbone layers, extracting a stable skeleton via bitwise AND and a noise-perturbed variant. The final output is a class label: the clean path averages predictions over all T timesteps, while the noise path produces a single prediction from the perturbed stable firing rate.

## Method

Stable Spike performs dual consistency optimization as a plug-and-play training-time loss with no changes to neuron models or architecture. First, it computes a stable spike skeleton by applying bitwise AND (&) to adjacent-timestep spike maps, retaining only 1-valued spikes that persist across timesteps, then aggregates these into a stable firing rate Φ̃; an MSE loss (L_spike) aligns the original spike firing rate to this skeleton, directly suppressing variable noise spikes. Second, it injects amplitude-aware binary spike noise—sampled from a Bernoulli distribution whose probability equals the local stable firing rate—into Φ̃, forward-propagates the perturbed features through the classifier, and enforces perturbation consistency via a temperature-scaled KL divergence (L_noise) between noisy and clean predictions. The total loss is L_CE + β·L_spike + γ·L_noise, and the AND-based extraction is hardware-friendly, requiring only a single bitwise operation per adjacent pair with negligible additional forward-pass cost beyond the classifier.

## Results

On DVS-Gesture, the method achieves 94.44% accuracy with VGG-9 at T=4 (vs. 87.15% vanilla), and 95.49% with QKFormer at T=4 (vs. 93.75% baseline). On CIFAR10-DVS, it reaches 77.1% (VGG-9, T=4) and 83.7% (VGGSNN, T=4), surpassing the prior best MPS at the same latency. On ImageNet, ResNet-34 with T=4 achieves 70.59% top-1 accuracy, outperforming all compared SNN methods. The largest single gain is +8.33% on DVS-Gesture at ultra-low latency (T=2), where vanilla SNN accuracy is 83.68% and the method reaches 92.01%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms all 26 compared baselines across every dataset and architecture tested. On DVS-Gesture, it beats the closest competitor MPS [4] by 1.21% (94.44% vs. 93.23%) while using one fewer timestep (T=4 vs. T=5), and surpasses QKFormer [48] by 1.74% at the same T=4 latency. On CIFAR10-DVS, it exceeds MPS [4] by 0.5% with VGGSNN at T=4 (83.7% vs. 83.2%) and by 0.33% with VGG-9 (77.1% vs. 76.77% at T=5). On N-Caltech101, it outperforms MPS [4] by 1.21% (VGG-9, T=4 vs. T=5) and by 0.57% (VGGSNN, T=10: 94.25% vs. 93.68%). On ImageNet, ResNet-34 at T=4 achieves 70.59%, narrowly beating Strong2Weak [5] (70.53%) and STAA-SNN [46] (70.40%). The method also synergizes with other techniques: combined with CLIF [15] on DVS-Gesture it reaches 95.83% (+6.25% over CLIF alone), and with TAB [16] it reaches 92.36% (+4.86%), confirming plug-and-play compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SNN temporal consistency line initiated by MPS [4], which promotes spike stability through membrane potential smoothing and logit distillation between adjacent timesteps. The key departure is replacing that indirect, neuron-modifying strategy with a hardware-friendly bitwise AND operation that directly extracts a 'stable spike skeleton' as a consistency anchor, requiring no changes to neuron dynamics or architecture. The amplitude-aware spike noise component repurposes the ANN feature-augmentation-for-generalization idea [9,19] but adapts it to the discrete, amplitude-sensitive nature of binary spikes, filling a gap where vanilla Gaussian noise causes training-inference mismatches. The method is positioned as a plug-and-play complement to existing SNN improvements (neuron models, BN layers, training algorithms) rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.33% accuracy gain at T=2 on DVS-Gesture is practically significant for neuromorphic deployment, where ultra-low latency is the primary advantage of SNNs over ANNs. The bitwise AND formulation is attractive for hardware implementation on neuromorphic chips where neuron models are often fixed, and the plug-and-play nature means it can be layered onto existing SNN pipelines without re-derivation. Open questions include whether the MSE consistency objective is truly optimal (Table 1 suggests KL divergence may be competitive), whether the approach generalizes to recurrent or attention-heavy SNN architectures beyond the tested VGG/ResNet/Transformer variants, and whether the gains persist at even lower latencies (T=1) or with more complex event-based tasks like segmentation.
