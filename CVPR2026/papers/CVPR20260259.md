## Problem

Existing generative image-goal navigation methods rely on pre-built topological maps or lack explicit trajectory evaluation mechanisms, leading to inefficient exploration under large semantic gaps. Diffusion- and flow-based policies also suffer from temporal inconsistency and high inference cost, producing kinematically valid but semantically suboptimal or temporally unstable motion. Additionally, the absence of a unified closed-loop benchmark with data-aligned evaluation makes fair, reproducible comparison across methods difficult.

## Input / Output

At each control step the agent receives a forward-facing RGB-D observation (90° horizontal FoV) and a goal image, plus a textual navigation prompt. The policy generates five candidate trajectories, each an 8-step sequence of continuous linear and angular velocities (v_t, ω_t) executed at 1-second intervals on a nonholonomic wheeled platform (r_b = 0.1 m, L = 0.1 m). The HRM selects one collision-free, semantically aligned trajectory for execution; an episode succeeds when the final pose is within 1 m and 25° of the target viewpoint.

## Method

GeniNav couples three modules: (1) a Latent Guidance Module (LGM) that feeds the current RGB image, goal image, and prompt into Qwen2.5-VL-7B, then refines the last-layer hidden states through a lightweight transformer encoder-decoder and MLP to produce a compact subgoal latent z_s, regularized by semantic alignment, discretized SE(2) pose classification, and contrastive losses; (2) a GeniPolicy built on Multi-Segment Consistency Flow Matching (MS-CFM), which partitions the flow time horizon [0,1] into K segments, each governed by an independent conditional vector field v_θ^(i), and enforces velocity alignment between adjacent segments via an EMA-based consistency loss to yield temporally smooth, goal-directed action sequences from Gaussian noise in just K inference steps; (3) a Hybrid Ranking Module (HRM) that projects five candidate trajectories onto the depth map for geometric collision filtering, scores semantic alignment via GPT-4V on an overlaid trajectory visualization, and computes a view-gain metric from ray-cast visibility, then selects the trajectory maximizing a weighted combination of semantic and visibility scores.

## Results

On the Gibson validation split, GeniNav achieves 68.7% SR, 59.4% SPL, and 9.8% CR, outperforming the strongest baseline MetricNet (54.5% SR, 43.3% SPL, 11.9% CR) by +14.2 SR and +16.1 SPL while reducing collisions by 2.1 points. On the unseen MP3D cross-domain split, GeniNav attains 55.2% SR and 45.7% SPL, surpassing the best baseline NaviDiffusor (40.9% SR, 28.6% SPL) by +14.3 SR and +17.1 SPL. The method also demonstrates sim-to-real transfer, deploying in real-time on an RTX 6000 Ada GPU with a RealSense D435i camera without model compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeniNav outperforms all nine baselines on both Gibson and MP3D across SR, SPL, and CR. Against the strongest in-domain baseline MetricNet (54.5% SR on Gibson), GeniNav gains +14.2 SR and +16.1 SPL while reducing CR from 11.9% to 9.8%. On cross-domain MP3D, the gap widens: GeniNav leads NaviDiffusor (40.9% SR) by +14.3 SR and NavDP (40.6% SR) by +14.6 SR, indicating that the latent subgoal guidance and hybrid ranking generalize better to unseen environments. Among diffusion-based methods, NoMaD (35.0% SR) and NaviDiffusor (48.0% SR) trail GeniNav by 33.7 and 20.7 SR points respectively on Gibson, highlighting the advantage of flow matching over iterative denoising. Flow-based FlowNav (44.7% SR) benefits from depth but lacks semantic guidance, yielding a 24.0 SR gap. The VLM-based VL-TGS (48.2% SR) shows that semantic evaluation alone is insufficient without geometric safety filtering, as its CR (14.2%) remains higher than GeniNav's (9.8%). Overall, GeniNav's combination of latent subgoal imagination, MS-CFM trajectory generation, and hybrid semantic-geometric ranking produces the largest margins over both diffusion-based and flow-based competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeniNav extends the flow-matching navigation line initiated by FlowNav, replacing its single global conditional flow field with Multi-Segment Consistency Flow Matching (MS-CFM) to enforce temporal coherence across trajectory segments. It departs from diffusion-based navigators (NoMaD, NaviDiffusor) on efficiency grounds and from explicit image-subgoal methods (ViNT, ImagineNav) by representing intermediate goals as VLM-derived latent features rather than pixel-space predictions. The Hybrid Ranking Module synthesizes ideas from critic-guided scoring (NavDP), cost-guided selection (NaviDiffusor), and VLM-based evaluation (VL-TGS) into a unified geometric-plus-semantic criterion, while GeniBench addresses the absence of a large-scale closed-loop benchmark for generative image-goal navigation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeniBench (176 scenes, 491.6 km of kinematically feasible trajectories with data-aligned evaluation) fills a concrete gap that has hampered fair comparison across generative navigation methods, and is likely to become a standard evaluation platform. The latent-subgoal formulation sidesteps the geometric inconsistencies and hallucinations of explicit image generation, but the reliance on a 7B VLM for subgoal inference and GPT-4V for trajectory scoring raises practical deployment questions about latency and cost on resource-constrained robots. Open questions include whether MS-CFM's segment count K and the HRM's weighted scoring generalize to outdoor or much longer-horizon settings, and whether the sim-to-real gap can be closed without the small real-world fine-tuning dataset the authors use.
