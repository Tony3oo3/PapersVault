## Problem

Prior generative brick assembly methods are restricted to discrete voxel grids with only a handful of part types, lacking the expressivity of real LEGO construction with thousands of part types and diverse connection semantics. When pieces are placed in arbitrary 3D poses, directly predicting coordinates causes rapid accumulation of precision errors, making build sequences invalid after only a few steps. The paper addresses both the data gap (no large-scale dataset of human-designed structures with diverse parts) and the representational challenge of autoregressively generating physically valid assemblies in this richer setting.

## Input / Output

For text-conditioned generation, the model takes a natural-language prompt describing a desired object and autoregressively emits a serialized build sequence of up to 100 parts (capped at 4,096 tokens). Each build step specifies a part type, color, and a typed connector edge (e.g., which stud mates with which hole, plus a yaw angle) that defines the local SE(3) transform relative to an already-placed part. Executing the full spanning-tree sequence recovers the complete 3D structure with 6-DoF poses for every piece, with rotational parameters rounded to the nearest degree and slide scalars to the nearest LDU.

## Method

The core contribution is a graph-backed parametrization that makes part connectivity first-class: each LDraw part is annotated with typed connectors across five families (stud, hinge, axle, ball, fixed), and the structure is represented as a graph whose edges encode the full local SE(3) transform between paired parts. This graph is serialized as a spanning tree of build steps, which an LLM learns to generate autoregressively via standard next-token prediction. The authors finetune Qwen 3 models (0.6b–14b) in two stages: unconditional pretraining on BrickNet-PT (320K samples, 40M placed bricks) followed by supervised fine-tuning on BrickNet-SFT (67K captioned samples) for text-conditioned generation. The graph representation dramatically improves connectivity validity over direct pose prediction (94+ valid steps vs. <50 under nucleus sampling), because spatial relationships are encoded structurally rather than requiring the model to maintain numerical precision across accumulated transforms.

## Results

On the 512-sample text-conditioned evaluation set, the 14b graph-backed model achieves VQAScore 0.602, PE 0.283, and SigLIP2 0.625, substantially outperforming BrickGPT (0.050, 0.157, 0.052) on all three perceptual metrics. However, the proposed method has a higher invalid-placement rate (P_inv = 0.231 for 14b) compared to BrickGPT (0.063). In unconditional generation, the graph-backed parametrization sustains 94–97 valid connectivity steps (nucleus sampling) versus only 31–50 for the direct-pose baseline, while collision-free validity is comparable at roughly 18–19 steps for both.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is BrickGPT [27] (Pun et al., ICCV 2025), which serializes block placements as voxel coordinates over a 20×20×20 grid with only 8 brick types. The proposed method decisively outperforms BrickGPT on all three perceptual similarity metrics: the 14b model scores 0.602 VQAScore vs. 0.050, 0.283 PE vs. 0.157, and 0.625 SigLIP2 vs. 0.052 — roughly a 12×, 1.8×, and 12× improvement respectively. The trade-off is physical validity: BrickGPT achieves a much lower invalid-placement rate (P_inv = 0.063) than any of the proposed models (0.231–0.260), likely because its restricted 8-part, grid-aligned domain makes collision avoidance far easier. Among the proposed model sizes, the 8b variant achieves the best SigLIP2 (0.647) and PE (0.284), while the 4b variant leads on VQAScore (0.615); the 14b model is best only at minimizing invalid placements (0.231), suggesting diminishing returns from scale on perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior generative brick assembly work (BrECS, BrickGPT, Thompson et al.) that was restricted to voxelized grids with a handful of part types, by targeting the full LDraw ecosystem with thousands of part types and diverse connection semantics. Conceptually, it borrows the graph/program-synthesis lineage from StructureNet and ShapeAssembly—representing 3D structure through typed connectivity edges rather than raw geometry—and applies it to an autoregressive LLM setting. The key architectural choice is serializing a spanning tree over a typed connector graph as the token sequence for a Qwen 3 language model, making connectivity first-class rather than predicting absolute 6-DoF poses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The BrickNet dataset (320K human-designed LDraw structures, 9,743 part types, 40M placed bricks) is the first large-scale resource for this domain and will likely enable follow-up work on reconstruction, editing, and constrained generation. The graph-backed parametrization is a clean, general idea that could transfer to other part-based assembly domains, though the paper notes it requires domain-specific connector knowledge that limits out-of-domain generalization. Key open questions include scaling beyond 100 parts, improving collision avoidance (where graph and pose representations perform comparably at ~20 valid steps), and whether inference-time decoding strategies can close the gap between structural validity and physical plausibility.
