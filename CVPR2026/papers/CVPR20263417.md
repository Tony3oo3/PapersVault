## Problem

Vision-language models decode exclusively as text, forcing them to verbalize visual reasoning and limiting performance on tasks requiring spatial manipulation or visual imagination. Prior attempts to enable interleaved image-text reasoning require heavy image-generation pretraining, which degrades the model's core reasoning ability. The paper asks whether VLMs can reason through compact latent visual representations—akin to human mental imagery—without ever producing pixel-level images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263417/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes one or more RGB images plus a textual query and produces an autoregressive trajectory that interleaves ordinary text tokens with k continuous latent visual vectors (default k=4), terminating in a boxed final answer. The latent tokens are not discrete vocabulary entries or image patches; they are continuous hidden-state vectors obtained by skipping the language projection head at designated positions, and they are compressed from full patch-level features via average pooling to retain only task-critical visual cues. Training data is synthesized by pairing each question with a task-specific helper image (e.g., a map annotated with arrows, a concatenated jigsaw composite) and prompting a large VLM to generate a reasoning chain that embeds that image.

## Method

Mirage augments VLM decoding with latent visual tokens: when the model emits a special trigger token, it reuses its own last-layer hidden state as a compact visual embedding appended to the context, bypassing the language projection. Training proceeds in two stages. Stage 1 jointly supervises text tokens (cross-entropy) and latent tokens (cosine similarity against average-pooled patch features of a helper image), grounding the latent vectors in the visual subspace. Stage 2 removes the cosine loss entirely and lets the model autoregressively generate its own latent tokens, which then serve as differentiable priors for subsequent text generation—gradients from the text loss propagate back through the latent vectors, allowing them to adapt to the task without matching any fixed embedding. A final GRPO reinforcement-learning stage optimizes the full trajectory with accuracy and format rewards. The key design choice is that only k=4–6 latent vectors are used, echoing the concise, sketch-like nature of human mental imagery and avoiding the overhead of full image generation.

## Results

On the VSP benchmark (Qwen2.5-VL 7B), Mirage with GRPO achieves 89% average accuracy on spatial reasoning and 60% on spatial planning, outperforming the best text-only baseline (CoT SFT + GRPO) by 4% and 9% respectively. On BLINK-Jigsaw and SAT (Qwen2.5-VL 3B), Mirage reaches 85% Jigsaw accuracy and 89% SAT synthetic accuracy, surpassing the strongest baselines (ViGoRL, MindJourney) by 18–29% on Jigsaw and 16–22% on SAT synthetic. On COMT-Geometry, Mirage attains 77% accuracy, exceeding MINT-CoT (72%) and R1-VL (69%) by 5 and 8 points. The two-stage training plus GRPO consistently yields the best results across all four benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mirage decisively outperforms all three unified image-generation baselines on VSP: Anole (52%/1% avg) and MVoT (61%/11%) are far behind Mirage Direct (86%/76%), while Aurora (71%/13%) is the closest but still trails by 15% on spatial reasoning and 63% on spatial planning. Against the spatial-reasoning-specific baselines on Jigsaw and SAT (Table 2), Mirage (85% Jigsaw, 89% SAT synthetic, 64% SAT real) beats ViGoRL (56%/67%/59%) by 29%/22%/5% and MindJourney (–/73%/57%) by 16%/7% on the respective metrics. On COMT-Geometry (Table 3), Mirage (77%) surpasses MINT-CoT (72%), which is explicitly trained on large-scale math geometry data, by 5 points, and R1-VL (69%) by 8 points. The paper notes that MVoT's reproduced numbers are lower than originally reported due to data differences, yet Mirage still exceeds the original MVoT results, reinforcing the advantage of latent tokens over explicit image generation. Overall, Mirage wins on every benchmark and every compared baseline, with the largest margins on spatial planning tasks where explicit image generation baselines collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mirage sits at the intersection of two research threads: latent reasoning in LLMs (Hao et al., 2024), which showed that continuous hidden-state tokens can replace explicit chain-of-thought text, and unified multimodal models (Anole, MVoT, Aurora) that interleave generated images with text during reasoning. The key departure is that Mirage replaces pixel-level image generation with a handful of compressed latent vectors (via average pooling over patch features), drawing an analogy to human mental imagery rather than photorealistic rendering. For the RL stage, it follows the GRPO + accuracy/format reward recipe from LMM-R1, but extends it to allow gradient flow through the latent tokens during policy optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a VLM can gain meaningful spatial-reasoning improvements by interleaving just four average-pooled latent vectors into its text stream, without the heavy image-generation pretraining that unified models require. This suggests a lightweight path to multimodal CoT that could be bolted onto existing VLMs. However, the evaluation is confined to spatial-reasoning benchmarks (VSP, Jigsaw, SAT, COMT-Geometry) and a single base-model family (Qwen2.5-VL), so generalization to other reasoning domains (e.g., temporal, causal, or abstract symbolic tasks) remains untested. An open question is whether the latent tokens genuinely encode task-relevant visual structure or merely act as a learned regularization that nudges attention, since the t-SNE analysis is suggestive but not conclusive.
