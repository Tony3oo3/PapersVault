## Problem

Existing zero-shot ObjectNav methods rely on scene-agnostic commonsense knowledge from LLMs/VLMs trained on web-scale text, rather than embodied 3D spatial experience, and discard episodic observations after each episode. This prevents the accumulation of lifelong navigation experience and limits the agent's ability to leverage how objects are typically arranged within spatial layouts across environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263001/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During navigation, the agent consumes egocentric RGB-D frames and its own pose to incrementally build an open-vocabulary semantic map (via GroundingDINO + MobileSAM), from which a topo-polar trajectory and candidate frontier paths are derived. The pre-built TrajRAG knowledge base (200k+ trajectories from HM3D-v1 and 150k+ from MP3D training splits) is queried with these candidate trajectories. The output is a selected frontier waypoint, which a local A* policy converts into a discrete action sequence; the agent stops within 1 m of the target within a 500-step budget.

## Method

TrajRAG introduces a topological-polar (topo-polar) trajectory representation: the navigable region is skeletonized into topological nodes, and each node is characterized by 12 polar sectors (30° each) recording the first-hit semantic label, obstacle, or unknown region within a 1.5 m radius. This representation enables loop pruning for compactness and serves as a geometric-semantic fingerprint for matching. A hierarchical chunking architecture organizes trajectories into groups with unified topo-polar summaries (coarse index) and individual trajectory embeddings (fine index), where the fine index uses a frozen DistilBERT for node encoding plus a DistilGPT2 for sequence encoding, trained via contrastive learning to cluster same-goal trajectories. During navigation, candidate frontier paths undergo coarse-to-fine retrieval—first matching against summary graphs via rotation-invariant semantic similarity and RANSAC geometric alignment, then retrieving top-K similar trajectories via embedding similarity—whose natural-language descriptions are fed to a Qwen3-32B planner to select the optimal exploration path.

## Results

TrajRAG achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot ObjectNav performance across all three benchmarks: 42.6% SR / 18.0% SPL on MP3D, 62.5% SR / 33.9% SPL on HM3Dv1, and 78.1% SR / 40.2% SPL on HM3Dv2. On HM3Dv2, it surpasses the previous best (ApexNAV, 76.2% SR / 38.0% SPL) by 1.9 SR and 2.2 SPL points. Cross-dataset evaluation shows that combining retrieval corpora from both HM3Dv1 and MP3D training splits yields the best results (62.5/33.9 on HM3Dv1, 42.6/18.0 on MP3D), confirming transferability of the geometric-semantic experience.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TrajRAG outperforms all 15 baselines on every benchmark where they overlap. On HM3Dv1, it leads BeliefMapNav (61.4/30.6) by 1.1 SR and 3.3 SPL, and ApexNAV (59.6/33.0) by 2.9 SR and 0.9 SPL. On MP3D, it surpasses UniGoal (41.0/16.4) by 1.6 SR and 1.6 SPL, and SG-Nav (40.2/16.0) by 2.4 SR and 2.0 SPL. On HM3Dv2, the margin is largest: TrajRAG (78.1/40.2) beats ApexNAV (76.2/38.0) by 1.9 SR and 2.2 SPL, and VLFM (63.6/32.5) by 14.5 SR and 7.7 SPL. Among non-open-vocabulary methods, SGM (60.2/30.8 on HM3Dv1) and GOAL (41.7/15.5 on MP3D) are the strongest, but both are outperformed by TrajRAG by 2.3+ SR and 3+ SPL. The cross-dataset ablation (Table 4) further shows that a corpus built solely on MP3D training data still achieves 59.8/31.6 on HM3Dv1 validation, confirming that the retrieved geometric-semantic experience generalizes across scene distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

TrajRAG extends the retrieval-augmented generation paradigm (Lewis et al., 2020) from text documents to embodied navigation by retrieving geometric-semantic trajectory experiences rather than textual passages. It departs from prior LLM/VLM-based zero-shot ObjectNav methods (VoroNav, SG-Nav, UniGoal, etc.) that rely solely on scene-agnostic web knowledge or discard episodic memory after each episode, by introducing a persistent, incrementally growing knowledge base of structured navigation trajectories. The topo-polar trajectory representation and hierarchical coarse-to-fine retrieval are novel architectural contributions that distinguish this work from both standard RAG and embodied-RAG approaches (EmbodiedRAG, NavRAG) that operate only within a single scene.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TrajRAG addresses a fundamental limitation in zero-shot ObjectNav: the disconnect between scene-agnostic LLM commonsense and scene-specific spatial experience. The cross-dataset transfer results (HM3Dv1 corpus aiding MP3D navigation and vice versa with only ~2% SR drop) suggest the topo-polar representation captures somewhat universal spatial-semantic navigation patterns, which is a promising signal for building general-purpose navigation memory. Open questions include scalability to truly open-world (non-simulated) environments, the computational cost of maintaining and querying the growing trajectory corpus over thousands of episodes, and whether the pre-built-then-frozen evaluation protocol understates the method's potential for genuine lifelong learning where the corpus evolves during deployment.
