## Problem

End-to-end document parsing with MLLMs still produces repetitive, hallucinated, and structurally inconsistent outputs on real-world captured documents, primarily due to the scarcity of large-scale full-page end-to-end supervision data and the absence of structure-aware training strategies. Cascaded modular pipelines, while competitive on clean digital pages, suffer from error propagation when layout segmentation fails under non-standard capture conditions (illumination variation, geometric distortion, wrinkles).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261645/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB document image as input—ranging from clean scanned/digital pages to casually captured photos with perspective shifts, moiré patterns, and uneven lighting—and autoregressively decodes a structured output of up to 8,192 tokens containing text with explicit reading order, table structures in HTML, and mathematical formulas in LaTeX. The 1B-parameter model (DocHumming, built on InternVL2-1B) processes the full page in one forward pass without external layout segmentation, and parsing a text-dense page takes approximately 3 seconds.

## Method

The paper proposes a data-training co-design framework. On the data side, Realistic Scene Synthesis (RSS) composes ~9M atomic elements (tables, formulas, paragraphs, figures) with 576K annotated layout templates under spatial and structural constraints to generate DocMix-3M (~3M synthetic documents), augmented with capture-aware distortions (perspective, illumination, background overlays) to bridge the synthetic-to-wild gap. On the training side, the Document-Aware Training Recipe combines a Progressive Training Paradigm—Stage 1 parses isolated elements with heterogeneous prompts to build type-specific capabilities, then Stage 2 transitions to unified full-document decoding with homogeneous prompts—and a Structure-Token Aware Optimization that assigns a higher loss weight (λ=4) to tokens enclosed within structural tags (e.g., <table>, </table>) to stabilize autoregressive decoding of structured content. Both stages fine-tune all parameters of InternVL2-1B with cosine LR decay, and the combined recipe reduces the repetition rate from 4.7% to 2.1% on OmniDocBench while achieving the highest overall score (93.75) among all compared systems.

## Results

On OmniDocBench, DocHumming (1B) achieves an Overall score of 93.75, surpassing the best modular pipeline (PaddleOCR-VL, 91.93) and the best end-to-end parser (dots.ocr, 88.41), while also outperforming much larger general MLLMs such as Qwen3-VL-235B (89.15) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (88.03). On the proposed Wild-OmniDocBench, DocHumming retains 87.03 Overall with only a −6.72 degradation from the clean setting, whereas modular pipelines degrade by 18–20 points (e.g., PaddleOCR-VL drops to 72.19) and other E2E models degrade by 10–13 points. On XFUND multilingual parsing, DocHumming leads across all six non-English/Chinese scripts, e.g., 87.99 on Japanese vs. 86.32 for Gemini-2.5-Pro and 85.15 on German vs. 83.90 for Mathpix.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DocHumming (1B) outperforms all 20 baselines on OmniDocBench Overall, beating the strongest modular pipeline PaddleOCR-VL (0.9B) by +1.82 (93.75 vs. 91.93) and the best prior E2E model dots.ocr (3B) by +5.34 (93.75 vs. 88.41). It also surpasses the largest general MLLM, Qwen3-VL-235B (235B), by +4.60, demonstrating that a 1B specialized model can exceed 235B general-purpose models on document parsing. On Wild-OmniDocBench, the advantage widens dramatically: DocHumming degrades only −6.72 from clean to wild (93.75→87.03), while modular pipelines degrade 18–20 points (PaddleOCR-VL: 91.93→72.19; MinerU2.5: 90.67→70.91) and other E2E models degrade 10–13 points (dots.ocr: 88.41→78.01; DeepSeek-OCR: 87.01→74.23). On XFUND multilingual parsing, DocHumming leads every language, e.g., +1.67 over Gemini-2.5-Pro on Japanese (87.99 vs. 86.32) and +1.25 over Mathpix on German (85.15 vs. 83.90). The only metric where a modular method edges ahead is Table TEDS on OmniDocBench (PaddleOCR-VL 91.01 vs. DocHumming 91.49 — actually DocHumming still leads), confirming a consistent win across all five reported metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the recent trend of reverting to cascaded/modular pipelines (MinerU, PaddleOCR-VL, MonkeyOCR) by arguing that truly end-to-end MLLM-based parsing has been underexplored due to data scarcity rather than fundamental model limitations. It draws on the end-to-end document parsing line (GOT, DeepSeek-OCR, dots.ocr) and the synthetic data generation tradition (SynthDog, DocLayoutYOLO, PDF-to-LaTeX pipelines), but departs from both by coupling a layout-template-based scene synthesis engine with a structure-aware training recipe (progressive curriculum + token-weighted loss) borrowed from LLM long-context training. The paper also extends the evaluation landscape by introducing Wild-OmniDocBench, which goes beyond the clean digital pages of OmniDocBench to model real-world capture artifacts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1B-parameter model outperforms 235B+ general MLLMs on standardized parsing, and the Wild-OmniDocBench + DocMix-3M pipeline gives the community tools to train and evaluate under realistic conditions. The finding that end-to-end models degrade far less than modular cascades under real-world capture (−6.7 vs. −19 to −20 overall) challenges the working assumption that layout decomposition aids robustness, with direct implications for deployment architecture choices. Open questions remain around highly irregular layouts (newspapers, posters), ultra-high-resolution pages requiring tiling, and whether the data synthesis can capture cross-element semantic coherence; the ~3 s/page latency also limits interactive use.
