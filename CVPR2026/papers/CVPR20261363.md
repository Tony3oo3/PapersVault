## Problem

Current fine-grained bird identification benchmarks evaluate models only on answerable, in-schema images, implicitly rewarding confident guesses over principled abstention. In real deployment, many bird images are genuinely unanswerable from a single photo (requiring vocalization, suffering from occlusion or low resolution), yet no existing benchmark measures whether a model can both classify correctly and abstain with an accurate, evidence-based rationale when it cannot.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261363/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a single RGB bird image (sourced from iNaturalist, no fixed resolution) and a text prompt (e.g., 'What is the species of this bird?') and expects either a species label from a 3,442-way list or an abstention decision paired with one of three concrete rationales: vocalization needed, low image quality, or view obstructed. For encoder models, the output is a probability vector over all species prototypes that is aggregated leaf-to-parent to yield genus- and class-level predictions; for MLLMs, a free-form text response is parsed via nlg2choice constrained decoding to extract both the species candidate and any stated abstention reason.

## Method

RealBirdID is constructed by mining 1.4M iNaturalist verifiable observations, filtering for bird presence (YOLOv3) and perceptual quality (MANIQA), then extracting community identification histories and applying handcrafted regex patterns to flag ambiguity signals (range, life-stage, molt, viewpoint, hybrid, taxonomic uncertainty, quality). Expert annotators verify a subset to refine the parsing, yielding 3,253 unanswerable examples paired with 31,885 answerable images sampled from the same genera. The paper proposes three complementary metrics: Abstention Tradeoff (UA/A, the AUC of the abstention-rate curve separating answerable from unanswerable sets), Classification Performance (IG, the DARTS accuracy-vs-information-gain curve across taxonomic levels), and Model Calibration (AUC of accuracy at varying abstention thresholds). Evaluation spans five encoder families (CLIP, MetaCLIP, WildCLIP, SigLIP, BioCLIP) using zero-shot prototype matching with hierarchical probability aggregation, and six MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, Llama-3.2V, InternVL3, Gemma-3, Gemini-2.5 Pro, GPT-5) using free-form prompting with nlg2choice answer extraction, revealing that classification accuracy and abstention ability are largely decoupled and that MLLMs systematically misattribute abstention reasons.

## Results

On RealBirdID, species-level identification is extremely challenging: the best encoder (BioCLIP) achieves only 17.0% species accuracy (AUC 30.1), while the strongest MLLMs (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5 Pro) reach just 10.4% and 12.7% species accuracy respectively. For abstention, no model exceeds 53.2 UA/A (SigLIP), and the best MLLM (Gemini-2.5 Pro) scores 46.2, trailing BioCLIP (49.6) by ~3.4 points. Genus-level accuracy is substantially higher (57.0% for BioCLIP, 52.8% for Gemini-2.5 Pro on the answerable set), but even here models struggle to separate answerable from unanswerable examples, with unanswerable-set genus AUCs ranging from 43.2 to 79.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BioCLIP dominates among encoders on classification (IG=68.9, species Acc=17.0%) but SigLIP achieves the best abstention tradeoff (UA/A=53.2), demonstrating that classification strength and abstention ability are decoupled (Pearson r=0.60, not significant). Among MLLMs, Gemini-2.5 Pro leads on both IG (57.7) and UA/A (46.2), yet still trails BioCLIP by 11.2 IG points and 3.4 UA/A points, confirming that specialized encoders outperform general-purpose MLLMs for fine-grained taxonomy-aware classification. GPT-5 matches Gemini-2.5 Pro on IG (57.7) but lags on genus accuracy (45.6% vs. 52.8%) and unanswerable-set genus AUC (76.5 vs. 71.8). Adding range-map information boosts average IG from 57.2 to 88.1 across all models, but only mildly improves abstention (45.8→47.7) and actually degrades MLLM abstention tradeoff, indicating that geographic priors help classification without teaching models to recognize visual unanswerability. Gemma-3-12B is a notable outlier, essentially never abstaining (1–2% rate) regardless of prompt wording, while InternVL3-8B shows extreme brittleness with abstention rates ranging from 3% to 23% under semantically equivalent instructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends fine-grained bird recognition benchmarks (CUB-200, NABirds, iNaturalist challenges) by introducing a paired answerable/unanswerable validation structure with labeled abstention rationales, and builds on the hierarchical classification metrics from DARTS/TreeGT [13] (Accuracy vs. Information Gain curves with leaf-to-parent probability aggregation) to handle the partially-labeled taxonomic tree. For MLLM evaluation, it repurposes nlg2choice [31] for constrained-decoding answer extraction and abstention-reason detection. The key conceptual departure from prior abstention work (AbstentionBench, SelfAware, RGQA) is that answerability here is determined solely by visual evidence—prompts are fixed and semantically equivalent—forcing the model to reason about image quality, occlusion, or missing vocalization cues rather than detecting textual ambiguity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even top proprietary MLLMs (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) achieve ≤13% species-level accuracy on 3,442-way classification, and that classification competence does not transfer to reliable abstention (no significant correlation; range-map information that boosts classification actually degrades MLLM abstention). The finding that MLLMs systematically default to 'image quality' as an abstention reason while virtually never citing missing vocalization exposes a visual-bias blind spot with implications beyond birds to any domain where non-visual cues matter. Open questions include how to design explicit abstention-aware training objectives for FGVR encoders, whether multi-frame or audio-visual inputs can resolve the unanswerable cases, and how to make MLLM abstention robust to prompt paraphrasing.
