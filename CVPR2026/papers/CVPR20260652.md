## Problem

Existing embodied learning pipelines treat data collection, simulation, representation learning, and deployment as loosely coupled or isolated stages, resulting in exogenous data dependence, static pre-rendered environments, fragmented task-specific architectures, and sparse deployment feedback. No prior system establishes a persistent, editable path from real-world deployment experience back into simulation assets and policy training, preventing sustained self-improvement across the robot's operational lifecycle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260652/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes synchronized multimodal streams (RGB-D, LiDAR, IMU, odometry, poses) captured during autonomous frontier-based exploration, along with natural-language task instructions, and produces both executable robot actions and updated simulation assets. Navigation outputs are expressed in a 7-primitive discrete control space generalizing across morphologies, while manipulation outputs are 7D actions decoded via an OpenVLA-style de-tokenizer. Unusually, the output also includes regenerated USD scene objects and refined policy weights, making the system bidirectional: deployment traces become training data and environment modifications rather than being discarded after evaluation.

## Method

Arcadia couples four stages into a non-decomposable closed loop: (1) a frontier-based exploration policy scores unexplored boundaries by expected entropy reduction to autonomously collect task-aligned multimodal data; (2) a Gaussian-splat-based reconstructor synthesizes simulator-compatible USD assets directly from multi-view observations, with SpatialLM parsing scenes into structured 3D graphs; (3) a shared Qwen2.5-VL backbone is jointly trained on VLN (A*-generated paths in 7-primitive space) and VLA (RRT-generated trajectories) with lightweight task-specific decoders, inducing a common embodied state representation across navigation and manipulation; (4) a Sim-from-Real feedback mechanism decomposes deployment rollouts into three structured channels—task-level (weighted reward, state transition, perceptual consistency, and goal-distance terms), scene-level (environment dynamics and perception quality), and robot-level (joint states, actuator load, constraint violations)—which are reintegrated into simulation to update assets, dynamics, and supervision targets, closing the real-to-sim-to-real loop.

## Results

On VLN benchmarks, Arcadia w/ feedback achieves 50.1% SR on VLN-CE-Isaac, 55.9% SR on R2R Val-Unseen, and 45.7% SR on RxR Val-Unseen, outperforming NaVILA by 5.0, 4.3, and 3.1 percentage points respectively. On manipulation, Arcadia reaches 87.2% success rate on LIBERO, surpassing the OpenVLA backbone (76.5%). In real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 robot, Arcadia completes 46% of navigation and 27% of manipulation trials, compared to 13% (NaVILA) and 9% (OpenVLA) for the respective baselines, with an average simulated improvement of 7.07% on navigation and 11.08% on manipulation over baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NaVILA, Arcadia w/ feedback wins on all four VLN benchmarks: +5.0 SR on VLN-CE-Isaac (50.1 vs 45.1), +4.3 SR on R2R (55.9 vs 51.6), +3.1 SR on RxR (45.7 vs 42.6), and +2.8 Meteor on ScanQA (19.1 vs 16.3). Even without real-world feedback, Arcadia w/o feedback already surpasses NaVILA by +3.6 SR on VLN-CE-Isaac and +2.6 SR on R2R, attributing gains to task-aligned autonomous data collection rather than dataset scale. Against OpenVLA on manipulation, Arcadia achieves 87.2% on LIBERO versus the 76.5% backbone baseline, with the largest gap on BridgeData V2 where feedback enhances object grounding and long-horizon stability. In real-world trials, Arcadia's 46% navigation success rate is 3.5× NaVILA's 13%, and its 27% manipulation rate is 3× OpenVLA's 9%; both baselines fail entirely on multi-destination and multi-object compositional tasks where Arcadia still achieves a 17% success rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

Arcadia builds directly on NaVILA's hierarchical architecture (high-level VLM + low-level robot controllers) and OpenVLA's 7D action de-tokenizer, but repositions them within a closed real-to-sim-to-real lifecycle rather than treating each stage in isolation. It departs from GRUtopia's simulation-centric expansion and NaVILA's execution-span extension by insisting that deployment feedback must flow back into simulation assets and policy training, making the four stages (exploration, generative reconstruction, shared representation, feedback-driven evolution) non-decomposable. The generative reconstruction pipeline draws on 3D Gaussian Splatting and SpatialLM for scene parsing, while the shared embodied backbone unifies VLN and VLA under a single Qwen2.5-VL encoder with task-specific decoders, a design choice that prior work (e.g., separate navigation and manipulation stacks) had not attempted at this level of integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that embodied learning is a lifecycle problem requiring tight coupling of data acquisition, simulation, representation, and deployment feedback—addresses a genuine fragmentation in the field where most pipelines optimize one link and treat deployment as a terminal evaluation step. However, the current implementation is confined to a single robot platform (Unitree G1), a single simulator (Isaac Sim), and a 7B-scale VLM, limiting the evidence for scalability. Real-world success rates (46% navigation, 27% manipulation) remain modest, and the ablation revealing that the shared backbone contributes minimally (49.8 vs. 50.1 SR) partially undercuts the architectural unification narrative. Open questions include whether the lifecycle coupling sustains improvement over extended deployment horizons, how the framework generalizes to morphologies beyond humanoids, and whether the generative reconstruction pipeline can handle dynamic or outdoor environments.
