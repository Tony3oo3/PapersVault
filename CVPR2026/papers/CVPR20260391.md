## Problem

Semi-dense feature matching methods scale quadratically with spatial resolution due to full correlation computations (O(W²H²) memory) and transformer-based long-range context modeling, making them prohibitively expensive for high-resolution, latency-sensitive, and resource-constrained applications. While recent efficient variants reduce some overhead, the fundamental bottleneck of redundant correlation over all spatial positions and the quadratic cost of attention-based cross-view interaction remain unresolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260391/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a grayscale image pair (I_A, I_B) ∈ R^(H×W×1) and produces semi-dense correspondences with subpixel precision. Training uses 1024×1024 inputs with edge padding, while evaluation spans resolutions from 480×640 (ScanNet) to 1200×1200 (MegaDepth). The L2-norm filtering threshold η=0.65 is applied at the 1/8 feature scale to select candidate positions, and the recurrent refinement operates on original-scale feature windows, yielding final matches with adaptive accuracy-speed control via iteration count (T=4 default).

## Method

SLiM combines three components: (1) a three-layer Conv-Mamba backbone interleaving ConvNeXt, SS2D (four-directional Mamba scanning), and InceptionNeXt blocks with a convolutional FPN, achieving linear-complexity cross-view and cross-scale context fusion; (2) a training-free L2-norm-based feature filtering mechanism that identifies geometrically salient positions to restrict coarse correlation to a sparse subset, followed by local window correlation at 1/2 scale for fine matching; (3) a lightweight Recurrent Refinement Unit (816K parameters) that iteratively predicts subpixel offsets via depthwise convolutions and MLPs over k×k feature windows, replacing expectation-based coordinate regression with an iterative optimization inspired by optical flow architectures. The network is trained end-to-end with negative log-likelihood matching losses at both scales plus an exponentially decayed L2 coordinate refinement loss.

## Results

On MegaDepth (1200×1200), SLiM achieves AUC@5° of 57.9, AUC@10° of 72.8, and AUC@20° of 83.5, outperforming Efficient LoFTR (56.4/72.2/83.5) by 1.5 points at the strictest threshold while using only 5.9M parameters (vs. 16.0M). On ScanNet (640×480), SLiM reaches 18.0/34.7/50.4 AUC at 5°/10°/20°. Inference time is 77.0 ms on MegaDepth and 32.9 ms on ScanNet (single RTX 4090, AMP), making it 1.8× faster than Efficient LoFTR and 2.4× faster than JamMa at the same resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLiM achieves the best MegaDepth AUC@5° (57.9) among all sparse and semi-dense matchers, surpassing Efficient LoFTR (56.4) by 1.5 points, ASpanFormer (55.3) by 2.6 points, and JamMa (55.4) by 2.5 points, while using fewer parameters than all of them. In terms of speed, SLiM (77.0 ms) is 1.8× faster than Efficient LoFTR (139.2 ms), 4.1× faster than LoFTR (315.4 ms), and 2.4× faster than JamMa (181.3 ms) at the same 1200×1200 resolution. On ScanNet, SLiM (18.0 AUC@5°) trails Efficient LoFTR (19.2) and ASpanFormer (19.6) by 1.2 and 1.6 points respectively, but still outperforms LoFTR (16.9) and JamMa (14.5). Compared to sparse methods, SLiM is slower than LightGlue (44.2 ms) and MambaGlue (46.3 ms) but delivers substantially higher accuracy (57.9 vs. 49.9/50.1 AUC@5° on MegaDepth). The single-iteration variant SLiM† (65.3 ms) remains faster than JamMa (181.3 ms) while maintaining 57.6 AUC@5°, confirming the efficiency-accuracy trade-off advantage across the full comparison set.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLiM inherits the coarse-to-fine dual-softmax matching paradigm from LoFTR and adopts Efficient LoFTR's depth-enhancement strategy, but replaces the transformer-based global context modeling with a hybrid Conv-Mamba backbone (ConvNeXt + SS2D blocks) that achieves linear complexity. Its most distinctive departure is the training-free L2-norm-based sparse correlation, which borrows the norm-growth observation from out-of-distribution detection literature and repurposes it as a saliency prior to prune redundant correlation computations. The recurrent refinement unit (RRU) draws from optical flow architectures (PWC-Net, RAFT) to replace the standard expectation-based subpixel regression used in LoFTR and MatchFormer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic memory and compute scaling of full correlation tensors has been a persistent bottleneck for high-resolution and resource-constrained matching; SLiM's sparse correlation and linear-complexity backbone directly address this, achieving 45% less memory and 2.4× faster inference than JamMa at 1200×1200 while matching or exceeding accuracy. The L2-norm filter's plug-and-play nature—demonstrated by inserting it into LoFTR, MatchFormer, and Efficient LoFTR without retraining—suggests a broadly applicable inference-time acceleration technique for the semi-dense matching family. Open questions include whether the norm-based saliency prior generalizes to color inputs or video matching, whether the recurrent refinement can be further parallelized, and whether the approach maintains its efficiency advantage at resolutions beyond 1200×1200 where dense matchers like RoMa v2 begin to close the accuracy gap.
