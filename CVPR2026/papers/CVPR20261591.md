## Problem

Reconstructing full-body 3D human motion from ground pressure signals is severely ill-posed: pressure maps are sparse, noisy, and lack a direct kinematic mapping to full-body pose, making simple regression infeasible. Prior pressure-based methods are limited to static or lying poses, while existing controllable motion synthesis approaches rely on clean kinematic abstractions (trajectories, keypoints) that cannot handle a physically grounded, indirect control modality. No prior work has combined pressure sensing with text guidance for dynamic, standing-pose motion capture.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method requires only a ground pressure mat (producing H×W 2D pressure maps per frame at 20 FPS over N frames) and a free-form text prompt; no cameras or wearable sensors are needed. The output is a full-body 3D motion sequence in the HumanML3D representation (pelvis velocity, local joint positions/velocities, joint rotations in pelvis space, and binary foot-contact flags), kept in global space to maintain alignment with the pressure signal. The MPL dataset provides five levels of text detail per sequence, enabling the model to handle varying prompt specificity.

## Method

Pressure2Motion uses a dual-level pressure feature extractor: a frozen ResNet+GRU module (from MotionPro) encodes the sequence into a Pressure-Inferred Movement Trajectory (T_traj), while a jointly-trained multiscale convolutional module processes raw pressure maps, their temporal differences, and grid positional encodings to produce fine-grained Pressure-Induced Posture Shifts (S_shift). These two signals are injected hierarchically into a pre-trained MDM text-to-motion diffusion backbone: a ControlNet (initialized from MDM with zero-initialized linear layers) receives T_traj via element-wise addition to the noisy motion, yielding global residual features, while parallel Adapter blocks (self-attention + cross-attention + FFN) fuse these residuals with S_shift and text embeddings for local refinement. The final denoised motion is the MDM output plus the Adapter-refined residuals. Training combines the standard DDPM diffusion loss with a pressure-motion consistency loss that penalizes L2 distance between reconstructed positions of five key joints (pelvis, both ankles, both feet) and the pressure-inferred trajectories, weighted by a binary contact mask.

## Results

On the MPL dataset, Pressure2Motion achieves the best FID (0.262), Foot Skating (0.0553), CoP Error (0.4260), LMPJPE (0.1273), MPJPE (0.1622), and R-precision Top-3 (0.545) among all compared methods. The closest competitors, OmniControl and MaskControl, trail by 17–37% on CoP Error and by 5–9% on MPJPE. Notably, the simple Regression baseline (single-step diffusion) collapses with FID 40.015 and CoP Error 1.4832, confirming the ill-posed nature of direct pressure-to-motion mapping. The only metric where Pressure2Motion is not best is Trajectory Error (>50 cm), where MaskControl (0.1009) and OmniControl (0.1035) slightly outperform it (0.1445).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pressure2Motion outperforms all four external baselines on FID, Foot Skating, CoP Error, LMPJPE, MPJPE, and R-precision. Against the strongest controllable-synthesis baselines, OmniControl (FID 0.315, CoP Error 0.5862) and MaskControl (FID 0.388, CoP Error 0.5644), Pressure2Motion reduces CoP Error by 27% and 25% respectively, and MPJPE by 5.4% and 4.4%, while also achieving the lowest FID (0.262 vs. 0.315/0.388). The text-only diffusion baselines MDM (FID 4.819) and MotionDiffuse (FID 3.812) are far behind, confirming that without pressure conditioning the model cannot recover physically grounded motion. The only metric where Pressure2Motion trails is Trajectory Error (>50 cm): MaskControl (0.1009) and OmniControl (0.1035) beat it (0.1445), suggesting the hierarchical pressure conditioning slightly sacrifices long-range trajectory precision for local contact fidelity. The internal Text-Only ablation (FID 0.872, CoP Error 1.0810) further validates that pressure is indispensable for physical realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the pretrained Motion Diffusion Model (MDM) as a reconstruction prior rather than a creative synthesis tool, adapting it with a ControlNet–Adapter hierarchy borrowed from the controllable motion synthesis line (OmniControl, MaskControl, Sketch2Anim). However, it departs fundamentally from those methods: where prior controllable synthesis conditions on clean kinematic abstractions (joint trajectories, keypoints) with direct geometric correspondence to pose, Pressure2Motion must interpret a sparse, noisy, physically-grounded signal (ground pressure) that has no simple kinematic mapping to full-body motion. On the pressure-estimation side, prior work (PIMesh, in-bed pose estimators) was restricted to quasi-static or lying poses; this paper extends the problem to dynamic, free-moving full-body motion by decomposing pressure into a high-level trajectory signal and a fine-grained posture-shift signal, each injected at a different architectural level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pressure2Motion establishes the first large-scale benchmark (MPL, 20,944 sequences with paired text, pressure, and motion) for a task that enables privacy-preserving, camera-free, wearable-free motion capture in residential, clinical, or public settings—a practical need that existing optical/inertial/visual MoCap systems cannot meet. The introduction of the CoP Error metric directly validates physical alignment with the input signal, filling a gap in evaluation that prior controllable-synthesis metrics (trajectory error, MPJPE) do not address. Key limitations remain: the dataset covers only flat-surface motions (no inclined or uneven terrain), inference is computationally demanding, and the real-world generalization is demonstrated in only one uncontrolled corridor scenario, leaving open whether the approach scales to complex multi-person or cluttered environments.
