## Problem

Large image editing models offer flexible stylization but suffer from high computational cost, structural hallucinations, and imprecise color/tone control, making them unsuitable for real-time, high-resolution photorealistic workflows. Classical color transfer methods avoid hallucinations but lack spatial and semantic awareness. The paper bridges this gap by distilling a large editing model into a lightweight network that is constrained to photorealistic, locally affine color transforms while retaining an interpretable, user-controllable style interface.

## Input / Output

The method takes a content RGB image and the per-channel marginal color histogram of a style image (not the full style image) as input. It outputs a stylized RGB image at the same resolution as the content, along with an intermediate bilateral grid of size (8,16,16) containing 12 affine coefficients and 1 uncertainty alpha per cell, plus a learned per-channel monotonic nonlinearity (LUT). Because the bilateral grid decouples transform resolution from image resolution via trilinear interpolation, the same 1.5M-parameter network scales from 256×256 to 4096×4096 without retraining, processing 16 MP images in ~0.1 s on a single GPU.

## Method

Hist2Style uses a dual-branch architecture: a ConvNeXt-style convolutional encoder downsamples the content image to 16×16 spatial features, while a 1D ConvNeXt CNN encodes the three per-channel style histograms into a global style token; the two are fused via cross-attention and passed through a small convolutional head that predicts the bilateral grid. The bilateral grid constrains all edits to locally affine transforms in a learned guidance (luminance) dimension, which structurally prevents the free-form distortions and hallucinations of generative models. Training is a selective knowledge distillation from Flux Kontext: an LLM generates diverse photorealistic style descriptions, Flux Kontext produces synthetic style variants of Unsplash images, and the student network regresses to these targets using MSE spatial loss plus a sorted-pixel Wasserstein-2 distribution loss, both computed in VGG perceptual space. The histogram-based style embedding (rather than a neural feature or text prompt) gives users direct, interpretable control over color and tone through interactive histogram manipulation and Y'CbCr sliders.

## Results

In a 3,000-trial user study with 31 photography experts, Hist2Style wins over 61% of comparisons against every baseline, with the strongest margin (82.57%) against SA-LUT and the narrowest (61.62%) against PhotoWCT². On the proposed SQA metric (N=7000), Hist2Style scores 50.00 versus 36.70 for PhotoWCT² and 33.90 for ReHistoGAN, confirming high alignment with human preference. At 256×256 resolution, inference takes 0.001 s and scales to 0.1 s at 4096×4096, an order of magnitude faster than PhotoWCT² (0.3 s → 1 s) and WCT² (OOM at 4096²). On FID, Hist2Style achieves 71.44, competitive with SA-LUT (71.72) and ReHistoGAN (66.84), though Xia et al. leads at 50.46.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hist2Style outperforms all six baselines in the user study, winning 61–83% of trials depending on the opponent. On the SQA metric, it leads by a wide margin over PhotoWCT² (50.00 vs. 36.70) and ReHistoGAN (33.90), with the remaining baselines scoring below 29. However, PhotoWCT² and WCT² achieve lower color-matching error (W₂² on Y': 224.86 and 308.57 vs. Hist2Style's 387.30), indicating stronger global color distribution transfer at the cost of perceptual quality. IDT attains the best cycle consistency (MSE 215.57 vs. 401.92), reflecting its global color-mapping nature, while Xia et al. achieves the lowest FID (50.46 vs. 71.44). In terms of speed, Hist2Style (0.001 s at 256²) is faster than PhotoWCT², SA-LUT, WCT², and ReHistoGAN at all resolutions, though Xia et al. scales slightly better at 4096² (0.03 s vs. 0.1 s), and D-LUT is fastest after its 100 s amortized initialization cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

Hist2Style sits at the intersection of three research threads: classical color transfer (Reinhard, Pitié/IDT) which matches global color statistics; bilateral-grid-based photorealistic stylization (Gharbi et al., Xia et al.) which constrains edits to locally affine transforms in a guidance space; and large image editing models (Flux Kontext) that offer expressive but uncontrolled generation. The paper's key departure is to use a large editing model purely as a synthetic-data teacher, then distill its spatially varying color edits into a 1.5M-parameter network whose output is hard-constrained to a bilateral grid, replacing the deep-feature or language-prompt style representations of prior neural methods with an interpretable per-channel histogram embedding. This combines the photorealism guarantee of bilateral grids with the semantic richness of foundation-model training data, while the histogram interface restores the direct, slider-like control that classical color-grading workflows offer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for real-time and high-resolution color-grading pipelines: a 1.5M-parameter model running in ~0.1 s at 4K with 1 GB memory is orders of magnitude lighter than diffusion-based editors, and the editable histogram gives artists an interpretable knob that neither text prompts nor deep-feature embeddings provide. The proposed SQA metric, validated against a 3,000-trial photographer study, offers a reproducible proxy for the expensive user studies that currently dominate style-transfer evaluation. Open questions include extending the approach to video (temporal consistency), multi-object spatial stylization, and whether the distillation recipe generalizes to other foundation-model teachers or to tasks beyond color/tone (e.g., relighting, grain).
