## Problem

Linear state-space models (SSMs) like Mamba2 and Gated DeltaNet maintain only a lossy, fixed-dimensional fading state of the past, causing them to underperform softmax Attention on recall-oriented and long-context tasks. The paper addresses this gap by designing a memory layer that conditions on the exact past (as Attention does) while preserving SSM-style constant memory and linear-time computation, and further tackles the numerical instability of the Kalman Filter recursion in low-precision (bfloat16) training and the difficulty of parallelizing it on modern GPUs.

## Input / Output

For language modeling, GKA consumes a text token sequence (up to 128K tokens) and produces next-token logits at each position; for vision, it takes image patches and outputs class labels. At each timestep the layer maintains two D×D state matrices (H_t for weighted key covariance, U_t for weighted key-value cross-covariance) that are updated via a low-rank-plus-identity recursion, and the output is computed by solving a regularized linear system (H_t + λ_t I)x = q_t via r iterations of Chebyshev Iteration, then left-multiplying by U_t. The entire forward and backward pass is executed in a chunk-wise fashion (chunk size C) to exploit GPU parallelism without materializing all intermediate states.

## Method

GKA replaces the myopic single-step regression of standard SSMs with a test-time ridge regression over the entire KV-cache, solved via the Kalman Filter recursion. Two key innovations ensure practical viability: (1) adaptive regularization λ_t = a·||H_t||_F, which upper-bounds the condition number of the Hessian to (a+1)/a regardless of sequence length, preventing numerical blowup in bfloat16; and (2) Chebyshev Iteration (CH) as the linear solver, which the paper proves (Lemma 2) yields identical gradients to exact implicit differentiation and is empirically more stable than conjugate gradient in multi-layer networks. Exponentially decaying gating weights η_{t,i} = ∏_{j=i+1}^{t} γ_j introduce input-dependent recency bias while preserving linear-time complexity. The backward pass uses implicit differentiation through the linear system, with a novel chunk-wise derivation for the non-rank-1 term B_i k_i that arises from differentiating through the Frobenius-norm regularization, implemented in custom Triton kernels.

## Results

On short-context language modeling (2.8B models, 10 tasks), GKA achieves an average score of 58.89, outperforming Gated DeltaNet (57.00), Mamba2 (55.94), DeltaNet (51.29), and Gated Linear Attention (47.55), though still trailing the Transformer baseline (63.92). On long-context tasks up to 128K tokens, GKA delivers over 10% relative improvement over all SSM baselines on real-world RAG and LongQA benchmarks. On ImageNet classification, GKAVision-T reaches 81.27% top-1 accuracy, surpassing MambaVision-T (81.18%) while approaching NextViT-S (81.99%) at 33% higher throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GKA outperforms all four SSM baselines (GDN, Mamba2, DeltaNet, GLA) on average across the 10 short-context tasks, with the largest margins on recall-intensive tasks: FDA (12.89 vs. 8.26 for GDN, +56% relative) and SWDE (50.95 vs. 44.28 for GDN, +15% relative). GKA also leads on commonsense reasoning tasks like COPA (85.00 vs. 82.00), SciQ (83.20 vs. 80.60), and Winogrande (64.17 vs. 62.35). However, GKA still trails the Transformer baseline (63.92 avg) by ~5 points, particularly on FDA (12.89 vs. 58.53) and SWDE (50.95 vs. 72.28), reflecting Attention's eidetic KV-cache advantage. On long-context RAG and LongQA (up to 128K tokens), GKA surpasses all SSM baselines by at least 10% relative improvement, though it underperforms on synthetic recall tasks beyond 4K context. On ImageNet, GKAVision-T (81.27%) narrowly beats MambaVision-T (81.18%) but remains below NextViT-S (81.99%), while offering 33% higher throughput than the Transformer.

## Relation to Prior Work

GKA draws its core state-update mechanism from the Kalman Filter, which computes the MAP-optimal state conditioned on the entire observed history, and from Chebyshev Iteration as a numerically stable, parallelizable solver for the resulting ridge regression. This departs fundamentally from prior linear SSMs (Mamba2, DeltaNet, Gated DeltaNet, GLA), whose state updates are derived from myopic, single-step regression objectives that only consider the previous lossy state and the current token. The paper also addresses a gap in prior test-time optimization work [63, 65], which overlooked the critical role of condition-number control in low-precision (bfloat16) training, by introducing adaptive regularization tied to the Frobenius norm of the data covariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GKA narrows the long-standing performance gap between efficient SSM layers and full Attention on recall-intensive and long-context tasks (up to 128K tokens) while preserving SSM-level compute and memory, making it a practical candidate for deployment where quadratic Attention is prohibitive. The finding that GKA excels on realistic RAG/LongQA tasks but diverges on synthetic recall at longer contexts reveals an important open question: how much of the layer's recall ability depends on pretrained semantic priors versus purely algorithmic memory management. Remaining limitations include a persistent gap with full Attention on verbatim recall (FDA, SWDE) and the fact that the Chebyshev iteration count r is a hyperparameter that trades accuracy for speed, with no automatic adaptation mechanism yet.
