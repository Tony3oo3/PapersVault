## Problem

Brain visual decoding via direct vision-brain contrastive alignment suffers from a fundamental information asymmetry: natural images contain task-irrelevant background regions while brain signals are contaminated by task-irrelevant neural noise, causing the model to form spurious correlations rather than focusing on the target object. Previous methods overlook this bidirectional redundancy, resulting in suboptimal zero-shot retrieval performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261824/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes EEG (17 occipital/parietal electrodes, 250 Hz, 0–1000 ms post-stimulus) or MEG (200 Hz, 0.1–100 Hz bandpass, 0–1000 ms) signals recorded under an RSVP paradigm, together with a short object-oriented text description (e.g., 'A photo of a [class concept]') that serves as a linguistic prior. The output is a ranked list of 200 candidate images, evaluated by Top-1 and Top-5 retrieval accuracy in a zero-shot setting where test concepts are excluded from training. The visual encoder is a frozen pretrained CLIP model (RN50, RN101, ViT-B/16, or ViT-B/32), while the brain encoder is trained from scratch.

## Method

The core mechanism is a linguistic-prior-guided visual decoupling pipeline that dynamically constructs image variants with a sharp central region and Gaussian-blurred periphery, where the spatial weight matrix is modulated by the cosine similarity between the brain signal and the text description. A confidence interval (derived from the batch-level normal distribution of brain-text similarity scores) assigns each sample a decoupling level I ∈ {−1, 0, 1}, controlling how aggressively the center is preserved versus blurred. A second dynamic alignment stage further adjusts the blur radius based on brain-to-decoupled-image similarity, and at inference a multimodal similarity modulation module amplifies visual similarity scores for brain signals deemed semantically reliable. Training uses a symmetric InfoNCE contrastive loss in a shared embedding space, with the CLIP image encoder frozen and the brain encoder trained end-to-end via AdamW.

## Results

On THINGS-EEG (200-way zero-shot retrieval), the proposed method achieves 58.2% Top-1 and 89.1% Top-5 accuracy (intra-subject, averaged over 10 subjects), surpassing the previous SOTA (UBP) by 7.3 and 9.4 percentage points respectively. In the more challenging inter-subject setting, it reaches 15.8% Top-1 and 39.4% Top-5, a 3.4 and 6.0 point gain over UBP. On THINGS-MEG, the method achieves 32.5% Top-1 / 62.9% Top-5 (intra-subject) and 5.4% Top-1 / 13.6% Top-5 (inter-subject), outperforming UBP by 5.8/7.7 and 3.2/3.2 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five prior methods on THINGS-EEG and THINGS-MEG. Against the closest competitor UBP [42] (uncertainty-aware blur prior, CVPR 2025), the proposed method wins on all four evaluation settings: +7.3/+9.4 points (intra) and +3.4/+6.0 points (inter) on EEG, and +5.8/+7.7 (intra) and +3.2/+3.2 (inter) on MEG. VE-SDN [2] (cross-modal disentanglement) is outperformed by 21.0/19.2 points in the intra-subject EEG setting, though it is not evaluated inter-subject. ATM-S [20] (guided diffusion reconstruction) trails by 29.7/28.7 points intra-subject on EEG. NICE and its variants [36] (contrastive brain-image alignment) are surpassed by 42.1/45.5 points intra-subject, and BraVL [9] (tri-modal mutual information) is outperformed by a wide margin of 52.4/71.6 points. The method wins every head-to-head comparison across both datasets and both evaluation protocols.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive-learning lineage for brain-visual decoding (NICE, BraVL, ATM-S, VE-SDN, UBP) by identifying a specific failure mode—bidirectional information asymmetry between full-scene images and noisy brain signals—and addressing it through linguistic-prior-guided visual decoupling. The closest prior work is UBP [42], which also applies Gaussian blur to images before alignment, but UBP uses a static uncertainty-aware prior whereas this paper makes the blur radius dynamically conditioned on brain-text semantic similarity via confidence intervals. The conceptual framing draws on dual-coding theory from cognitive neuroscience to justify why textual descriptions can serve as a bridge for symmetric alignment, a motivation absent from purely geometric or statistical prior approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 7.3% Top-1 gain over the previous SOTA (UBP) on THINGS-EEG intra-subject retrieval is substantial for this task, and the method generalizes across CNN and ViT CLIP backbones, suggesting the decoupling principle is architecture-agnostic. However, the approach is tightly coupled to the RSVP paradigm (attention directed to image center) and does not model the temporal dynamics of brain signals, limiting its applicability to more naturalistic viewing conditions. An open question is whether the linguistic prior can be replaced by learned semantic embeddings or whether explicit text descriptions remain necessary for the decoupling signal to be effective.
