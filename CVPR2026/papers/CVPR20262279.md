## Problem

Supervised image deblurring requires meticulously paired blur-sharp data that is expensive and often impractical to collect (e.g., for dashcams or bodycams), while reblurring-based unsupervised methods depend on identifying a suitable intermediate blur domain, introducing domain mismatch and multi-stage overhead. The paper asks whether the domain gap can be eliminated entirely by training a deblurring model directly within the target domain using only unpaired blurry and sharp images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262279/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, BluRef takes a single blurry RGB image and produces a deblurred image via one forward pass of the deblurring backbone (NAFNet or Restormer), with no references or extra modules required. During training, the method additionally consumes N unpaired sharp reference images (default N=6) that need not be geometrically aligned or temporally synchronized with the blurry input, only drawn from similar scenes. Training crops are 256×256 pixels, and the confidence mask (threshold 0.7) restricts supervision to high-certainty matched regions.

## Method

BluRef alternates between two steps across epochs: (1) a self-supervised Dense Matching model (PDC-Net+ with GLU-Net-GOCor, trained on synthetically warped and blur-augmented images) establishes pixel correspondences between the current deblurred estimate and unpaired sharp references, producing a pseudo-sharp image and a confidence mask; (2) the deblurring backbone is optimized against this pseudo-sharp target using a masked reconstruction loss. Three aggregation strategies (Weighted Average, Sequential Accumulation, Progressive Reference Averaging) combine matches from multiple references, with Progressive Averaging retaining previously matched sharp details while selectively enhancing unmatched regions. The iterative loop progressively refines the pseudo-ground truth as the deblurring network improves, and the generated pseudo-pairs can be reused to train lightweight models for mobile deployment.

## Results

On GoPro, NAFNet-BluRef with Progressive Averaging achieves 31.94 dB PSNR / 0.960 SSIM at Δ=1, approaching the supervised NAFNet upper bound of 33.32 dB / 0.962. On RB2V, the same configuration reaches 27.87 dB / 0.821 SSIM at Δ=1, exceeding the supervised Restormer upper bound of 27.43 dB / 0.849. On the unpaired real-world PhoneCraft dataset, BluRef+Blur2Blur achieves the best no-reference quality with 8.47 NIQE / 5.62 FID, outperforming BSRGAN (13.34/10.25) and standalone Blur2Blur (10.07/6.28). Performance degrades only marginally as temporal separation Δ increases from 1 to 20 frames, confirming robustness to significant content misalignment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BluRef decisively outperforms all three unsupervised baselines: on GoPro (Δ=1), NAFNet-BluRef(Prog.) reaches 31.94 dB PSNR versus UAUD's 24.25 dB, UID-GAN's 23.42 dB, and DualGAN's 22.23 dB — a gap of 7–10 dB. Against supervised upper bounds, BluRef narrows the gap to ~1.4 dB on GoPro (31.94 vs. 33.32 for NAFNet) and actually exceeds the supervised Restormer on RB2V (27.87 vs. 27.43 dB), demonstrating that unpaired reference-guided training can match or surpass paired supervision. On the unpaired PhoneCraft dataset, standalone BluRef (10.43 NIQE / 6.45 FID) slightly trails Blur2Blur with RSBlur as intermediate domain (10.07/6.28), but the combined BluRef+Blur2Blur pipeline achieves the best scores (8.47/5.62), surpassing both BSRGAN (13.34/10.25) and RSBlur-based baselines. The combined approach also outperforms the supervised NAFNet upper bound on RB2V (29.62 vs. 28.54 dB PSNR), showing that the unsupervised pipeline can exceed what is achievable with paired data in real-world blur conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

BluRef repurposes dense matching architectures (PDC-Net+, GLU-Net, GOCor)—originally designed for optical flow and geometric correspondence—into a cross-domain blur-to-sharp semantic matcher, a use the authors frame as novel. It departs from prior unsupervised deblurring methods (DualGAN, UID-GAN, UAUD) that rely on self-supervised cycles or synthetic degradation, and from reference-based deblurring methods that remain within the supervised paradigm requiring paired ground truth. The iterative pseudo-ground-truth generation loop is conceptually related to the authors' own Blur2Blur reblurring pipeline, but eliminates the need for an intermediate blur domain and a pre-trained deblurring network, operating entirely within the target domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for paired blur-sharp data, BluRef makes high-quality deblurring accessible in settings where paired collection is impractical (dashcams, bodycams, consumer phones), and the pseudo-pair byproduct enables training lightweight models for mobile deployment. A key limitation is the requirement for unpaired sharp references from the same scene, which constrains applicability to video or multi-frame capture scenarios rather than truly single-image settings. Open questions include how the method generalizes when reference images come from substantially different scenes or when blur is caused by out-of-focus defocus rather than motion, and whether the dense matching pretraining on synthetic warping can be further reduced or eliminated.
