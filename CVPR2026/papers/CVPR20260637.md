## Problem

Existing test-time adaptation methods for VLMs rely on unreliable pseudo-labels due to inadequate uncertainty estimation and treat all test samples homogeneously, overlooking class-specific diversity. This causes progressive performance degradation as noisy or distributionally-shifted samples accumulate during streaming inference. The paper asks how to perform universal prompt learning at test time that selectively leverages informative samples while suppressing detrimental OOD noise, without access to ground-truth labels.

## Input / Output

STAR consumes unlabeled test images (encoded via ViT-B/16 or ViT-B/32) and text prompts (ImageNet-1K class names) in a streaming fashion during inference, with no labeled data required. It produces a binary ID/OOD decision per sample, a class label for ID samples, and conjugate-optimized pseudo-label vectors that drive online prompt fine-tuning. A dynamic memory bank (capped at 64 samples per class) stores high-confidence image embeddings to maintain class-wise prototypes and quantile-based detection thresholds that are updated incrementally as new samples arrive.

## Method

STAR integrates three coupled components for test-time prompt adaptation. First, a separability-aware soft gating mechanism uses the Fisher score over entropy values to adaptively determine a threshold θ, then replaces the hard indicator with a sigmoid-based soft gate that smoothly weights cross-entropy (for confident/ID samples) against KL divergence (for uncertain/OOD samples). Second, the loss is reformulated via a Legendre-Fenchel conjugate perspective on the softmax parameterization, yielding closed-form pseudo-labels (Eq. 14) that replace ground-truth supervision and enable reliable adaptation without labels. Third, a calibrated prototypical retrieval module maintains a dynamic memory bank of high-confidence samples, constructs class-wise prototypes via momentum updates, and applies per-class quantile thresholds on prototype similarity to perform fine-grained OOD detection, with meta-cluster fallback for classes with insufficient samples.

## Results

On the ImageNet-1K ID benchmark with four OOD targets (iNaturalist, SUN, Texture, Places), STAR_L (built on LoCoOp) achieves an average FPR95 of 27.03% and AUROC of 93.64% under 1-shot tuning, improving over LoCoOp^G by 6.05% in FPR95 and 1.78% in AUROC. Under 16-shot tuning, STAR_L further reduces average FPR95 to 19.91% with AUROC of 95.36%. In terms of harmonic mean accuracy (ACC_H) combining ID classification and OOD detection, STAR_L reaches 46.04% (16-shot) and STAR_S reaches 45.88% (16-shot), substantially outperforming the base LoCoOp (39.85%) and SCT (41.18%) frameworks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR consistently outperforms all eight baselines across both 1-shot and 16-shot settings. Against the strongest zero-shot baseline CLIPN (avg FPR95 31.10, AUROC 93.10), STAR_L achieves a 4.07% FPR95 reduction and 0.54% AUROC gain at 1-shot, widening to 11.19% FPR95 reduction and 2.26% AUROC gain at 16-shot. Compared to the closest few-shot prompt-tuning baselines LoCoOp^G and SCT^G, STAR_L improves average FPR95 by 6.05% (1-shot) and 8.24% (16-shot), while STAR_S improves by 6.64% (1-shot) and 3.45% (16-shot). On the harmonic mean accuracy metric (ACC_H), STAR_L gains 4.51 points over LoCoOp and 5.86 points over SCT at 16-shot, confirming that test-time adaptation via conjugate optimization and prototypical retrieval translates into balanced improvements in both ID classification and OOD rejection. The largest absolute gains appear on iNaturalist (e.g., STAR_L FPR95 drops from 19.32 to 3.85 at 16-shot), while Texture remains the hardest OOD target where all methods show comparatively weaker AUROC (~86–90%).

## Relation to Prior Work

STAR builds directly on the few-shot prompt learning paradigm for OOD detection in VLMs, specifically extending LoCoOp and SCT from offline few-shot tuning to online test-time adaptation. The conjugate pseudo-label optimization draws on the Legendre-Fenchel reformulation introduced by Goyal et al. (NeurIPS 2022) for test-time adaptation, but repurposes it within a VLM prompt-tuning context with an entropy-based soft gating mechanism. The key departure from prior TTA methods is the explicit separation of ID and OOD samples via a Fisher-score-adaptive threshold, combined with a dynamic memory bank and prototypical retrieval for class-wise calibrated detection, rather than treating all test samples homogeneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STAR addresses a practically important gap: progressive test-time data arrival where accumulating noisy or distributionally-shifted samples degrade prompt-tuned VLMs. The Fisher-score adaptive threshold and conjugate pseudo-labels offer a principled, label-free mechanism for selective adaptation, which is relevant to any streaming deployment of VLMs. Limitations include evaluation restricted to ImageNet-1K as the sole ID benchmark, only ViT-B/16 and ViT-B/32 backbones, and notably weaker gains on the Texture OOD dataset. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger VLMs (ViT-L, ViT-H), multi-modal OOD settings, and whether the dynamic memory bank remains stable under severe distribution drift over long inference horizons.
