## Problem

Existing self-supervised vision methods are designed for static third-person images or short scripted clips and fail to maintain stable object identities in unconstrained egocentric video, where continuous ego-motion, severe occlusions, and dense clutter make it difficult to discover, recognize, and persistently track objects. The paper asks how an artificial system can learn identity-consistent, class-agnostic object representations from continuous, uncurated first-person video without any manual annotations.

## Input / Output

EgoViT consumes RGB egocentric video frames (a single 65-minute Walking Tours clip) together with a pseudo-depth channel generated by Depth Anything V2, since the training video is RGB-only; the depth is used exclusively during training and is not required at inference. The model outputs class-agnostic proto-object feature representations from a ViT-S/16 backbone, where each attention head in the final layer corresponds to one emergent proto-object, yielding per-object embeddings that transfer to segmentation, detection, tracking, and classification tasks.

## Method

EgoViT is a teacher-student ViT framework that jointly optimizes three synergistic mechanisms. (1) Proto-object Learning uses the teacher's attention heads to delineate proto-objects via prototype synthesis, cosine-similarity localization, and adaptive thresholding, then applies intra-frame knowledge distillation (cross-entropy at both global and compositional levels) to form discriminative representations. (2) Depth Regularization adds an auxiliary scale-invariant depth prediction task with a gradient-consistency term, grounding features in geometric structure without requiring precise depth. (3) Teacher-Filtered Temporal Consistency leverages the momentum-updated teacher to compute inter-frame proto-object similarity, prunes unreliable correspondences caused by occlusion or ego-motion via a confidence threshold, and applies a contrastive loss only on validated pairs to enforce temporal identity. The key insight is that depth provides stable geometric context, proto-objects supply initial grouping, and temporal learning tracks persistence; ablations show that combining proto-objects with temporal loss without depth degrades performance, confirming the necessity of geometric grounding for reliable temporal supervision under ego-motion.

## Results

EgoViT_Zurich, trained on a single 65-minute egocentric video, achieves 45.2% CorLoc on PASCAL VOC object discovery (+8.0% over DINO), 26.0% mIoU on ADE20K semantic segmentation (+4.8% over DINO), and 26.7% mAP on MS-COCO object detection (+4.7% over DINO). On DAVIS-2017 video object segmentation it reaches 54.3% (J&F)m, and on ImageNet-1k linear probing it attains 34.0% accuracy. Scaling to the full Walking Tours dataset (EgoViT_WT-all) further lifts CorLoc to 50.2% and mIoU to 30.6%, confirming graceful data scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoViT_Zurich outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric. The most striking margin is against DoRA [62], the closest prior work in the egocentric-video setting: EgoViT gains +21.1% CorLoc (45.2 vs 24.1) and +4.4% mIoU, indicating that DoRA's use of temporal correspondence purely for spatial augmentation is far less effective than EgoViT's direct proto-to-proto temporal alignment. Against DINO [11], the strongest general-purpose self-supervised baseline, EgoViT leads by +8.0% CorLoc, +4.8% mIoU, and +4.7% detection mAP, with the object-discovery gap being the largest. Compared to iBOT [75] and MAE [29], EgoViT gains +8.9% and +9.3% CorLoc respectively, while also surpassing them on segmentation and detection. Against SimCLR [14], AttMask [34], and MoCo-v3 [15], EgoViT consistently leads by 3–10 points across all tasks, with the advantage being most pronounced on object discovery and dense prediction. Notably, EgoViT also surpasses DINO on the LaSOT tracking benchmark (64.7 AUC vs 60.5), confirming that the temporal-consistency mechanism translates into superior long-horizon tracking under occlusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoViT extends the DINO teacher-student ViT framework by adding three synergistic mechanisms (proto-object distillation, depth regularization, and teacher-filtered temporal contrast) specifically designed for unconstrained egocentric video. It departs from DoRA, which also uses ViT attention for prototype discovery from a single long video, by making temporal identity the primary supervision axis rather than using temporal correspondence merely for spatial data augmentation. The work also positions itself against object-centric learning (Slot Attention, SAVi++), which assumes quasi-static scenes, and against multi-object tracking, which requires pre-defined category-specific detectors—neither of which handles the non-stationary, detector-free setting of first-person video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that persistent, class-agnostic object representations can emerge from a single 65-minute egocentric video without annotations, offering a data-efficient path toward world models for embodied AI. A key practical finding is that the framework is robust to depth quality and source (Gaussian-blurred maps, MiDaS, Depth-Pro), broadening its applicability where precise geometry is unavailable. Open questions include whether incorporating semantic cues from language models can resolve early-stage visual ambiguities, how the approach scales to multi-view or lifelong learning settings, and whether the depth-regularization requirement can be relaxed further for purely RGB deployments.
