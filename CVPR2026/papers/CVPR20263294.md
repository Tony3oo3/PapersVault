## Problem

Flattened vector icons lose their original semantic layer structure, merging all parts into a single compound path and making routine editing, restyling, and animation infeasible. No existing method addresses the full pipeline of semantic segmentation, amodal completion of occluded geometry, and occlusion-order inference for highly abstract, texture-free icons, and no large-scale annotated dataset exists to support supervised training or evaluation in this domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263294/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a flattened monochrome or duotone SVG icon, rasterized into a binary silhouette at 512×512 for segmentation and 256×256 for completion. It produces a fully editable layered vector representation: each semantic part is a complete amodal shape (including occluded regions) expressed as Bézier paths, stacked in an inferred occlusion order. A curve-reuse strategy preserves original high-quality Bézier segments from the input and only inserts new bridge curves for repaired regions, keeping the output compact and geometrically clean.

## Method

SemLayer is a three-stage generation-driven pipeline. First, semantic segmentation is reformulated as a controllable colorization problem: a diffusion transformer (EasyControl/FLUX-based) is trained with a flow-matching objective and conditional LoRA to produce a colorized rendering where each distinct color maps to one semantic part, bypassing the failure modes of prompt-based segmenters like SAM on texture-free icons. Second, amodal completion fine-tunes pix2gestalt (a latent-diffusion UNet conditioned on CLIP embeddings and VAE-encoded occluded patches) on a custom 50K-sample icon overlap dataset, explicitly training with fragmented visibility so that multiple disjoint fragments of the same object all converge to the same completed shape; an IoU-threshold merging step (τ=0.7) consolidates duplicate predictions. Third, layer ordering is cast as an integer linear program that maximizes occlusion consistency (extra pixels covered by upper layers) while penalizing visibility violations, subject to transitivity constraints over pairwise ordering variables, solved with λ=1 to balance the two objectives.

## Results

On the 48-icon test set, SemLayer achieves 84.3% mIoU and 76.1% PQ for semantic segmentation, and 85.2% mIoU with 46.6 Chamfer Distance for amodal completion. Compared to the strongest baseline (fine-tuned SAM2*), SemLayer improves segmentation by +5.0 mIoU and +16.7 PQ, and completion by +4.5 mIoU and −2.5 CD. The completion model (Table 2) further shows SemLayer reaching 85.2% mIoU and 46.6 CD, outperforming MP3D-finetuned (75.3% mIoU, 68.9 CD) by +9.9 mIoU and −22.3 CD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SemLayer is compared against three segmentation baselines (GPT-image-1, SAM2 zero-shot, and fine-tuned SAM2*) and two completion baselines (MP3D and MP3D-finetuned). Against GPT-image-1, SemLayer wins decisively on every metric: 84.3% vs. 25.4% mIoU and 76.1% vs. 6.2% PQ for segmentation, and 85.2% vs. 60.9% mIoU for completion. Against zero-shot SAM2, the gap is also large (84.3% vs. 51.1% mIoU, 76.1% vs. 26.2% PQ). The closest competitor is fine-tuned SAM2* (79.3% mIoU, 59.4% PQ), which SemLayer surpasses by +5.0 mIoU and +16.7 PQ on segmentation and by +4.5 mIoU / −2.5 CD on completion. For the completion component specifically (Table 2), SemLayer (85.2% mIoU, 46.6 CD) outperforms MP3D-finetuned (75.3%, 68.9) by +9.9 mIoU and −22.3 CD, and MP3D (70.5%, 79.4) by an even wider margin, confirming that domain-specific fine-tuning on icon amodal data is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of layer-wise image vectorization, semantic segmentation, and amodal completion, but departs from each line in a key way. Rather than the optimization-based or differentiable-rendering approaches used in prior layer-wise vectorization work, SemLayer leverages pretrained diffusion models (EasyControl/FLUX for segmentation, pix2gestalt for completion) as generative priors to handle the abstract, texture-free nature of icons where traditional segmentation (including SAM2) struggles. The conceptual reframe of semantic segmentation as a controllable colorization problem—where distinct colors encode semantic parts—is the paper's central methodological departure from both classical segmentation and prior generative vectorization work. Layer ordering is formulated as an ILP, a combinatorial approach distinct from the perceptual-cue or learned-depth strategies in earlier vectorization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of SemLayer-Segmentation (8,567 icons) and SemLayer-Completion (50,000 triplets) provides the first large-scale resources for supervised learning in SVG semantic segmentation and icon-specific amodal completion, a gap the authors identify as a major bottleneck in the field. Practically, the pipeline enables editing, animation, and restyling workflows for flattened vector assets that are ubiquitous in web design but currently require manual re-segmentation. Key limitations include restriction to monochrome line-drawing icons (no color or fill), a small 48-icon test set that limits statistical confidence, and acknowledged failure cases on highly tangled or heavily occluded icons. Extending the framework to multi-color, filled, and stylistically diverse vector graphics remains an open direction.
