## Problem

Existing illumination degradation image restoration (IDIR) methods either rely on paired supervision (limiting generalization to unseen scenes), use unpaired references (suffering from distribution mismatch), or operate zero-shot (restricted to specific degradation types). Additionally, diffusion models exhibit a low-frequency generative bias in exposure, making it difficult to jointly perform illumination correction and high-frequency detail reconstruction within a single generative framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261153/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single illumination-degraded RGB image (low-light, backlit, or over-exposed) and outputs a restored high-quality image, requiring no paired ground truth or external reference at either training or inference time. Training is performed on 10k unpaired degraded images using 256×256 patches, and the framework is unified across three degradation types (LLIE, BIE, MEC) without task-specific modifications.

## Method

ZeroIDIR decouples restoration into two stages: (1) an Adaptive Gamma Correction Module (AGCM) that performs Retinex decomposition and then predicts spatially varying gamma maps for under-exposed and over-exposed regions via two dedicated branches with learned weight maps, regularized by a histogram-guided KL-divergence loss toward a natural-scene illumination prior; and (2) a Perturbed Consistency Diffusion Model (PCDM) that reinterprets the illumination-corrected image as an intermediate noisy state x_{t*} on the diffusion trajectory, continuing forward diffusion from that state and training a U-Net denoiser with a standard noise-prediction loss plus a perturbed diffusion consistency loss that enforces VGG-16 feature-space agreement between the input corrected image and the re-noised version of the predicted clean output. This decoupling lets the diffusion model focus exclusively on detail reconstruction and noise suppression while the AGCM handles exposure, and the consistency loss prevents trajectory drift in the absence of supervision.

## Results

On the LLIE task, ZeroIDIR achieves PSNR 20.874 / SSIM 0.811 / LPIPS 0.167 on LOL, 18.823 / 0.563 / 0.301 on LSRW, and 20.327 / 0.806 / 0.151 on MIT5K, outperforming all unsupervised baselines on LSRW and MIT5K (LPIPS) while remaining competitive with supervised methods. On BIE (BAID), ZeroIDIR attains the best PSNR (21.753), SSIM (0.871), LPIPS (0.133), NIQE (3.070), and CLIPIQA (0.563) across all metrics. On MEC (SICE over-exposed), ZeroIDIR achieves PSNR 16.975 / SSIM 0.661 / LPIPS 0.259, surpassing all supervised and unsupervised competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On LOL, ZeroIDIR (PSNR 20.874) outperforms all unsupervised baselines except LightenDiff (20.190) on PSNR, but trails the supervised Reti-Diff (25.180) which was trained on LOL itself. On LSRW, ZeroIDIR (PSNR 18.823, SSIM 0.563, LPIPS 0.301) surpasses every method—both supervised (best: UHDFour at 17.300) and unsupervised (best: LightenDiff at 18.388)—demonstrating strong generalization to unseen scenes. On MIT5K, ZeroIDIR beats all supervised methods (best: PyDiff at 18.617 PSNR) but is slightly behind LightenDiff (21.248) on PSNR while leading on SSIM (0.806 vs 0.799) and LPIPS (0.151 vs 0.181). For BIE on BAID, ZeroIDIR achieves the best score on all five metrics, edging out CLIP-LIT (PSNR 21.705) and RAVE (PSNR 20.086). On the SICE over-exposed subset for MEC, ZeroIDIR (PSNR 16.975, SSIM 0.661, LPIPS 0.259) outperforms all supervised methods including CoTF (16.840, 0.638, 0.288) and SLOT (16.673, 0.622, 0.285), as well as unsupervised UEC (15.514, 0.603, 0.280), confirming superior cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on standard diffusion frameworks (DDPM/DDIM) and Retinex decomposition, but departs from prior diffusion-based IDIR methods (Reti-Diff, AnlightenDiff, LightenDiff, AGLLDiff) that either require paired supervision or suffer from distribution mismatch in unpaired/zero-shot settings. The key conceptual departure is the decoupling of illumination correction (via a spatially adaptive gamma module operating on the Retinex illumination map) from detail reconstruction (via a perturbed consistency diffusion model), where the illumination-corrected image is reinterpreted as an intermediate noisy state on the diffusion trajectory. The exposure control loss follows Zero-DCE, and the clean-image estimation in the consistency loss follows QuadPrior, but the perturbed diffusion consistency loss that enforces trajectory fidelity in VGG feature space is novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By training solely on degraded images without any paired or unpaired references, ZeroIDIR addresses the practical bottleneck of paired data availability in IDIR while achieving generalization to unseen scenes that supervised methods lack (outperforming them on LSRW, MIT5K, and SICE). The decoupling strategy—handling low-frequency exposure correction separately from high-frequency detail generation—offers a reusable architectural principle for other restoration tasks where diffusion models exhibit low-frequency bias. Open questions include whether the two-stage training can be unified, whether the static histogram prior can be made scene-adaptive, and whether the approach extends to video or higher-resolution settings without the current U-Net computational cost.
