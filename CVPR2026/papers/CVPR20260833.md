## Problem

High-quality facial albedo capture has historically required controllable lighting setups (Light Stage, smartphone flashlights), increasing cost and limiting usability. Model-based inverse rendering is ill-posed under complex in-the-wild lighting, while data-driven methods inevitably bake lighting artifacts (e.g., shadows) into their predictions. The paper tackles the gap of reconstructing production-quality diffuse albedo from an unconstrained smartphone video with no assumptions about scene lighting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260833/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a ~30-second smartphone video orbiting the subject (300 frames at 960×720, 16 selected for reflectance estimation) plus the subject's skin tone (manual or automatic). It produces a 4K diffuse albedo map, a specular albedo map, and a detailed normal map; the reflectance is solved at 1K resolution and then super-resolved to 4K via a residual channel attention network, making the pipeline significantly faster than directly sampling at 4K.

## Method

WildCap proposes a hybrid inverse rendering framework: first, SwitchLight (a data-driven relighting network) converts raw in-the-wild images into predicted diffuse albedo images under simpler, more constrained lighting; then, a model-based optimization in UV space explains the residual non-physical baking artifacts as lighting effects. The key architectural contribution is a texel grid lighting model that assigns a 2D grid of 2nd-order Spherical Harmonics parameters (27 coefficients) across the UV map, queried via bilinear interpolation and modulated by a binary shadow mask, giving each facial region its own local SH lighting to absorb shadow-baking artifacts. To resolve the scale ambiguity between local lights and albedo, the method jointly samples a patch-level diffusion prior (trained on 48 Light Stage scans at 64×64) and optimizes the texel grid via diffusion posterior sampling, steering the denoising process with photometric gradients. The final 1K reflectance maps are super-resolved to 4K, completing in ~8 minutes on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

On face reconstruction (averaged over 6 subjects, evaluated on cropped facial skin regions), WildCap achieves PSNR 28.79, SSIM 0.9520, and LPIPS 0.0610, outperforming FLARE* (PSNR 27.81, SSIM 0.9411, LPIPS 0.0929) and DeFace* (PSNR 22.20, SSIM 0.9279, LPIPS 0.1192). The method produces 4K-resolution diffuse albedo, specular albedo, and detailed normal maps in approximately 8 minutes on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090, compared to 508 minutes for DoRA. Qualitatively, WildCap matches DoRA's reconstruction quality while operating on in-the-wild smartphone video rather than controllable-lighting captures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WildCap outperforms both in-the-wild baselines on all three metrics. Against FLARE* (which uses split-sum lighting), WildCap gains +0.98 PSNR, +0.011 SSIM, and reduces LPIPS from 0.0929 to 0.0610, primarily because FLARE's split-sum model cannot represent the non-physical baking artifacts in network predictions. Against DeFace* (which segments the face into a limited number of SH-lit regions), WildCap achieves a much larger margin of +6.59 PSNR and +0.024 SSIM, as DeFace's fixed region count restricts its lighting expressiveness. Against DoRA (the state-of-the-art controllable-lighting method), WildCap achieves comparable visual quality on the same subject while using only an in-the-wild smartphone video instead of a co-located flashlight sequence, and runs 63× faster (8 min vs. 508 min) thanks to its sample-then-super-resolution design rather than direct 4K diffusion sampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of data-driven delighting (SwitchLight) and model-based inverse rendering, combining them in a hybrid pipeline where the data-driven step converts complex in-the-wild lighting into a more constrained intermediate representation before differentiable rendering refines it. The texel grid lighting model departs from both physics-based environment maps and DeFace's region-based SH approach by using a dense 2D grid of SH coefficients in UV space, providing per-texel expressiveness to explain non-physical baking artifacts that no physical light source could produce. The optimization strategy builds directly on DoRA's patch-level diffusion prior and diffusion posterior sampling, but introduces a sample-then-super-resolution design that reduces runtime from 508 to 8 minutes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 30-second smartphone video with no lighting assumptions yields 4K facial reflectance maps at quality comparable to controllable-lighting setups, dramatically lowering the barrier to digital human creation. Key limitations include dependence on SwitchLight (a closed-source API) and a slow automatic shadow-detection step, both of which the authors identify as targets for replacement with open-source alternatives trained on datasets like FaceOLAT. An open question is whether the texel grid lighting model generalizes beyond facial capture to other object categories where network-predicted textures exhibit similar non-physical artifacts.
