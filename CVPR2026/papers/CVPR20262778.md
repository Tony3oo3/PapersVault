## Problem

Segmenting subcellular objects in 3D cryo-ET tomograms is hampered by the absence of annotated data, the coexistence of objects spanning multiple scales (large membranes to small macromolecules), and high inter-domain variability across experiments. Existing supervised methods require extensive manual annotation, handle only one object scale at a time, and fail to generalize across imaging conditions, while standard unsupervised segmentation approaches for natural images do not transfer to the very different appearance of cryo-ET volumes.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a small set of 2D slab images (xy slices, typically 512×512 after quartering) selected from a single tomogram—only ~1.33% of all slices in the dataset—after contrast stretching and CLAHE preprocessing. It produces a multiscale binary segmentation mask per slab, which is decomposed via CellPose into a membrane mask and macromolecule coordinates; these pseudo-labels then train a supervised UNet and DeepETPicker that are deployed to infer membrane masks and particle coordinates across all remaining tomograms in the dataset.

## Method

The core mechanism extracts query–key attention embeddings from all 16 attention layers of the Stable Diffusion conditional UNet (no fine-tuning), computes per-layer affinity matrices, and solves a spectral-clustering eigenvalue problem (adapted from Zhang et al., CVPR 2024) via gradient-based optimization to obtain C eigenvectors per slab. A novel 'diversity score' heuristic—measuring the standard deviation of local patch standard deviations—selects the most informative eigenvector image as a base and iteratively merges additional eigenimages only when they increase diversity, maintain high similarity to the base, and do not introduce excessive contour noise. The aggregated 64×64 feature map is corrected along the z-axis using SSIM-based neighbor comparisons, upsampled, and binarized with Gaussian adaptive thresholding. The resulting multiscale mask is split by pretrained CellPose into membrane and macromolecule components, which serve as pseudo-ground truth for training a supervised UNet (membranes) and DeepETPicker (macromolecule coordinates) that generalize to the full dataset.

## Results

On the VPP S. Pombe cellular cryo-ET dataset, the unsupervised method achieves a Dice score of 0.309 for membrane segmentation, closely approaching the supervised UNet baseline (0.324, only ~4% lower) while dramatically outperforming SAM (0.048), FreeSOLO (0.003), and CutLer (0.003). For macromolecule localization, the method achieves an F1 score of 0.43 using only 88 pseudo-ground-truth coordinates, surpassing DeepETPicker trained with 100 real coordinates (0.35, +22.84%) and CrYOLO trained with 500 real coordinates (0.31, +38.71%), though falling short of DeepETPicker with 500 coordinates (0.57, −32.55%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For membrane segmentation, the unsupervised method (Dice 0.309) nearly matches the supervised UNet (0.324) while crushing all off-the-shelf and natural-image unsupervised baselines: SAM scores only 0.048, and both FreeSOLO and CutLer score near zero (0.003), confirming that general-purpose segmentation tools fail on cryo-ET imagery. For macromolecule localization, the method (F1 0.43) outperforms DeepETPicker trained with 100 ground-truth coordinates (0.35) by 22.84% and CrYOLO trained with 500 coordinates (0.31) by 38.71%, demonstrating that pseudo-labels from the unsupervised pipeline are more informative than a comparable number of real annotations. However, DeepETPicker trained with 500 real coordinates (F1 0.57) still outperforms the unsupervised method by 32.55%, suggesting that processing more slabs through the unsupervised pipeline could close this gap. The paper also notes that the supervised method produces fewer false positives, though true positives are considered more critical for the biological task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the spectral clustering eigenvector optimization framework from Zhang et al. (CVPR 2024), which decodes 'where' visual pathways from layer-distributed neural representations, and repurposes it for cryo-ET segmentation by extracting query-key attention features from all 16 layers of Stable Diffusion's conditional UNet rather than from a dedicated segmentation backbone. It departs from prior unsupervised segmentation methods (FreeSOLO, CutLer) that rely on ResNet/ViT final-layer features and iterative self-training, instead introducing a diversity-score-driven heuristic for aggregating eigenvector features across layers and a z-axis continuity correction for 3D volumetric consistency. On the cryo-ET side, it contrasts with supervised single-object-type models (Membrain, DeePiCt) by offering a unified, label-free pipeline that handles both membranes and macromolecules simultaneously, using pretrained CellPose and DeepETPicker only as downstream refinement tools rather than as the primary segmentation engine.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for manual annotations, this pipeline directly addresses the primary bottleneck in cellular cryo-ET analysis, where expert annotation of large 3D volumes is extremely time-consuming and introduces human bias. The ability to segment multi-scale objects (membranes and macromolecules) in a single pass, and to generalize qualitatively across organisms and cell lines without retraining, opens the door to automated discovery of previously uncharacterized subcellular structures. Key limitations remain: quantitative validation is restricted to a single S. Pombe dataset, the eigenvector optimization is computationally expensive (7–8 min/slab on a single GPU), and the method segments by intensity rather than identifying individual organelles. Open questions include whether processing more slabs closes the remaining gap to fully supervised methods, and whether the Stable Diffusion feature pathway generalizes to other microscopy modalities.
