## Problem

Small task-specific vision models in high-stakes domains (medical imaging, manufacturing) frequently learn spurious shortcuts rather than domain-relevant features, leading to brittle real-world performance. Existing correction methods either demand expensive per-instance human feedback in image space or target group-balanced accuracy without explicitly aligning the model to human domain knowledge. This paper asks whether a Large Vision Language Model can serve as a bidirectional translator that converts class-level human specifications into instance-level correction signals, eliminating the need for fine-grained expert annotation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (224×224), short natural-language class descriptions provided by domain experts, and per-pixel DeepLIFT-SHAP attribution maps from the target model. The alignment set is selected via a low-output-entropy sampling strategy (e.g., N=256–1024 samples) to focus LVLM calls on shortcut-prone examples. The pipeline produces per-cluster binary relevance verdicts, which are aggregated into a binary correction mask A (spurious regions marked 1) used in the RRR fine-tuning loss, ultimately yielding an aligned vision model with reduced reliance on spurious features.

## Method

LVLM-VA is a two-step alignment procedure. First, a novel segmentation method—PPEPS-WGM—fits a weighted Gaussian mixture over normalized spatial coordinates, weighted by positive Shapley attribution mass, to partition the explanation map into J clusters that isolate high-attribution regions (unlike SAM, which segments by image content and tends to merge spurious features with core structures). An LVLM Critic (GPT-4o) then receives the original image, the segmentation overlay, and class-level human descriptions, and uses a chain-of-thought prompt to judge each cluster's relevance; a separate LLM Judge converts the free-form Critic output into structured binary verdicts via few-shot exemplars. Second, these verdicts are assembled into a binary correction mask A and plugged into the Right-for-the-Right-Reasons (RRR) loss, which penalizes gradients in spurious regions while preserving cross-entropy accuracy. The original model is fine-tuned with alignment samples mixed into standard training batches to avoid catastrophic forgetting, requiring only class-level text specifications rather than per-image expert annotations.

## Results

On the knee radiograph dataset, LVLM-VA achieves a ΔWGA of +0.16 ± 0.06 while maintaining overall accuracy, making it the only method that improves worst-group accuracy without sacrificing average group accuracy. On the ISIC skin lesion dataset, LVLM-VA similarly improves WGA while preserving AGA, whereas SUBG improves WGA but significantly degrades overall accuracy. On DecoyMNIST, LVLM-VA with λ=10⁵ approaches the upper bound achieved by ground-truth instance-wise correction masks, with both alignment score and test accuracy rising monotonically with λ until the cross-entropy term becomes negligible. The LVLM Critic & Judge pair achieves 87% verdict accuracy on the knee dataset (GPT-4o), and a user study confirms 86–88% agreement between LVLM verdicts and human expert judgments.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LVLM-VA outperforms all three baselines on the key criterion of improving worst-group accuracy while maintaining overall accuracy. SUBG (data balancing) achieves the highest WGA gain on the knee dataset, even exceeding LVLM-VA, but at the cost of a significant drop in average group accuracy, rendering it impractical for deployment. DFR (last-layer retraining) is ineffective on both medical datasets, showing no meaningful improvement in either AGA or WGA. JTT (two-stage reweighting) yields only a slight WGA improvement on the skin dataset and fails to consistently help on the knee dataset. LVLM-VA is the sole method that simultaneously increases WGA and preserves AGA across both real-world medical benchmarks, and it does so without requiring per-image group labels or instance-level human annotations—only class-level natural-language descriptions from domain experts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RRR loss framework of Ross et al. [23] for constraining model explanations and the set-of-mark prompting paradigm of Yang et al. [32] for improving LVLM spatial grounding, but departs from both by replacing instance-wise human feedback with class-level specifications translated through an LVLM Critic–Judge pair. It also diverges from non-human-centric shortcut-mitigation methods (DFR, JTT, SUBG) that require per-image group labels, and from prior VLM-based explanation work (Gu et al., Stammer et al.) that lacks a bidirectional human-steering interface. The novel PPEPS-WGM segmentation, which clusters by positive attribution density rather than object boundaries, is a methodological contribution that steers the LVLM toward model-relevant regions specifically for spurious-feature detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in reducing the human feedback burden from tedious per-instance image-space corrections to concise class-level descriptions, making expert-in-the-loop alignment feasible for time-constrained domain experts (e.g., radiologists). The method's ability to improve worst-group accuracy while preserving overall accuracy—without requiring group labels—addresses a key limitation of prior shortcut-mitigation baselines. Open questions include whether class-level descriptions remain sufficient for tasks where core and spurious features are spatially entangled, how the approach scales to multi-class or open-vocabulary settings, and whether the reliance on a specific LVLM (GPT-4o/5) limits reproducibility as model capabilities shift.
