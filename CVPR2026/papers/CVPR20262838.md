## Problem

Existing noisy-label FER methods—sample selection, label refinement, and consistency regularization—primarily supervise target-class outputs and provide limited regulation over non-target class distributions, leaving spurious non-target activations induced by occlusion, illumination variation, or geometric transformation insufficiently constrained. The complementary information carried by non-target labels is rarely explicitly modeled, creating a gap in how robustly the model can suppress error-prone predictions under label noise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262838/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB facial image (aligned via three landmarks, cropped, and resized to 224×224) and produces a single expression class label from a fixed set (7 basic emotions + neutral on RAF-DB, 8 categories on FERPlus, 7 basic emotions on AffectNet). During training, each image is paired with an augmented view (random horizontal flip and random erasing) processed through a shared ResNet-18 backbone; at inference, only a single forward pass on the original image is performed with no additional cost from the exchange mechanism.

## Method

CLEX exploits complementary supervision by performing stochastic exchange of a random subset of non-target logits between the original and augmented views (keeping the target coordinate fixed), then applying L_p-norm normalization to remove magnitude artifacts so that regularization operates on geometrically meaningful directions. A complementary suppression loss selectively penalizes spurious activations over a randomly retained subset of non-target classes, avoiding uniform shrinkage that would compromise discriminative capacity. Two auxiliary terms stabilize training: an attention consistency loss aligning class-activation maps across augmented views (following EAC), and a standard cross-entropy on pre-exchange probabilities to preserve target supervision. The total objective is L_CSL + 0.4·L_CE + 2·L_AC, optimized with Adam over 60 epochs on a ResNet-18 backbone pre-trained on MS-Celeb-1M, with fixed hyperparameters (γ=0.5, p=3, d=1) across all datasets.

## Results

Under 30% symmetric label noise, CLEX achieves 87.11% on RAF-DB, 63.87% on AffectNet, and 87.12% on FERPlus, outperforming the strongest baseline NLA by 0.40%, 1.21%, and 0.15% respectively. Under 10% noise, CLEX reaches 88.91% (RAF-DB), 64.30% (AffectNet), and 88.24% (FERPlus). On the real-world noisy AffectNet_Auto benchmark, CLEX attains 58.76%, surpassing the best prior method EAC (57.68%) by 1.08%. On CIFAR-10/100 with 30% symmetric noise, CLEX achieves 85.10% and 53.42%, respectively, confirming generality beyond FER.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLEX consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and noise level. The closest competitor is NLA [12], which CLEX surpasses by 0.40% (RAF-DB), 1.21% (AffectNet), and 0.15% (FERPlus) at 30% noise; the gap widens to 1.21% on AffectNet at 10% noise. LA-Net [33] and EAC [38] trail CLEX by 1.78% and 2.69% on RAF-DB at 30% noise, respectively. RUL [37] and SCN [29] show substantially larger gaps (5.05% and 9.65% on RAF-DB at 30% noise), indicating that sample-selection and uncertainty-based strategies degrade more sharply under heavy corruption. On the real-world AffectNet_Auto benchmark, CLEX (58.76%) beats DMUE [26] by 1.78% and MTAC [19] by 1.38%, confirming that the advantage holds beyond synthetic noise. On CIFAR-10/100, CLEX edges out both EAC and NLA under both symmetric and asymmetric 30% noise, demonstrating that the complementary logit exchange mechanism generalizes to non-FER classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLEX sits at the intersection of noisy-label FER and complementary-label learning. It builds directly on the consistency-regularization line (EAC's attention alignment, cross-view prediction agreement) and the complementary-label idea from NL/NLNL, but departs from both in a key way: rather than aligning target-class outputs or assigning a single sampled non-target class, CLEX operates at the logit level to stochastically exchange subsets of non-target predictions across augmented views. This reframes the supervision problem from 'how to better trust the target label' to 'how to exploit the structured information in the non-target subspace,' a perspective the authors argue prior target-driven methods (SCN, RUL, NLA, LA-Net, DMUE) have largely ignored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: FER in the wild is inherently noisy, and CLEX offers a plug-and-play training objective that requires no clean subset, no noise-rate prior, and no extra inference cost. However, the margins over the strongest baseline (NLA) are modest (0.15–1.21% at 30% noise), and all experiments use a single ResNet-18 backbone with uniform synthetic noise, leaving open whether the gains persist with deeper architectures, structured or instance-dependent noise, or larger-scale FER benchmarks. The generalization to CIFAR-10/100 is encouraging but the improvements there are similarly small, suggesting the technique is a useful regularizer rather than a paradigm shift.
