## Problem

Vision Foundation Models (e.g., DINO, CLIP) produce features downsampled by 14×–16×, making them unsuitable for pixel-level tasks without a heavy decoder. Existing feature upsampling methods either require dataset-level retraining (limiting generalization across backbones and domains) or rely on expensive per-image implicit optimization (~49 s/image). The paper seeks a lightweight, training-free upsampling operator that generalizes seamlessly across architectures, resolutions, and signal types.

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution RGB image (used as guidance) and a low-resolution signal—foundation features, depth map, or probability map—producing the corresponding high-resolution output at the original image grid. The HR image is bilinearly downsampled to match the LR signal's spatial size before optimization, and the learned kernels are then applied to upsample the LR signal back to full resolution. The approach is resolution-free: it operates identically whether the LR grid is 4×4, 16×16, or 32×32, and the entire pipeline (optimization + rendering) completes in ≈0.419 s for a 224×224 image without any dataset-level training.

## Method

Upsample Anything formulates upsampling as a per-image test-time optimization of anisotropic Gaussian kernels {σx, σy, θ, σr} assigned to each LR pixel, unifying Joint Bilateral Upsampling (spatial-range weighting) with 2D Gaussian Splatting (continuous anisotropic covariance). The optimization minimizes an L1 reconstruction loss between the HR guidance image and its GSJBU-rendered version from the downsampled image, requiring only 50 Adam iterations. Once converged, the learned per-pixel kernels are directly transferred to upsample any LR signal via normalized weighted blending (no value synthesis), making the operator inherently model- and task-agnostic. The spatial-range constraint inherited from JBU prevents the over-smoothing and instability observed with unconstrained 2DGS, while the anisotropic Gaussian formulation provides the expressivity needed for edge-preserving, orientation-aware interpolation that fixed-kernel JBU cannot achieve.

## Results

On semantic segmentation, Upsample Anything achieves the best mIoU across all three benchmarks: 61.41 on COCO, 82.22 on PASCAL-VOC, and 42.95 on ADE20K, with a probability-map variant pushing these to 63.40, 84.57, and 44.29 respectively. On NYUv2 depth estimation, it attains RMSE 0.498 and δ₁ 0.829, outperforming the previous SOTA AnyUp (RMSE 0.513, δ₁ 0.817). The method processes a 224×224 image in ≈0.419 s via 50 TTO iterations, and scales to 1024×1024 resolution where AnyUp runs out of memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Upsample Anything outperforms all six baselines on the primary segmentation benchmarks (Table 1), with the largest margin over AnyUp (previous SOTA) on ADE20K (+0.93 mIoU) and PASCAL-VOC (+0.04 mIoU, +0.51 Acc). On NYUv2 depth estimation (Table 2), it beats AnyUp by 0.015 RMSE and 0.012 δ₁, and substantially outperforms LoftUp, which degrades below the bilinear baseline (RMSE 0.796 vs. 0.545), suggesting LoftUp suffers from domain gaps. Against the classical JBU baseline (Table 5), the method gains +0.57 mIoU on PASCAL-VOC and −0.033 RMSE on NYUv2 depth, confirming that per-pixel anisotropic optimization over fixed isotropic kernels is beneficial. On depth-map upsampling (Table 3), Upsample Anything wins on Middlebury (RMSE 0.209 vs. 0.231 bilinear, 0.491 GLU) but trails bilinear on NYUv2 (0.214 vs. 0.167), likely because NYUv2 ground-truth depth is inherently smooth. In terms of efficiency, the method is ~117× faster than FeatUp (Implicit) (0.419 s vs. 49 s per image) and remains memory-stable at 1024×1024 where AnyUp OOMs, though it uses more peak memory than AnyUp at smaller resolutions (e.g., 3969 MB vs. 531 MB at 224×224).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate ideas—Joint Bilateral Upsampling (JBU) and 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS)—by showing that JBU is a discrete, isotropic special case of guidance-modulated 2DGS, then generalizing it to per-pixel anisotropic Gaussian kernels optimized via test-time optimization. It departs from the dominant dataset-level training paradigm of FeatUp, LoftUp, JAFAR, and AnyUp by requiring no paired training data or retraining when the backbone or domain changes. The only prior TTO approach, FeatUp (Implicit), parameterizes HR features as an implicit MLP optimized per image; this paper replaces that heavy implicit function with a lightweight explicit Gaussian splatting kernel, reducing per-image optimization from ~49 s to ~0.42 s.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a training-free, plug-and-play upsampling operator that transfers across architectures (ViT, CNN), modalities (features, depth, probability maps, 3D representations), and resolutions without retraining, which is practically valuable for anyone deploying foundation-model features in dense prediction pipelines. An open question the paper raises is whether feature upsampling provides meaningful gains for semantic segmentation under high-capacity backbones (gains are modest) versus geometry-oriented tasks like depth estimation (gains are larger), suggesting the value of upsampling is task-dependent. The authors note limitations under severe occlusion or low-SNR guidance where optimization becomes unstable, and future work should address robustness in such regimes.
