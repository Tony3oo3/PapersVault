## Problem

While LoRA adapter merging has been extensively studied, no training-free method exists for combining multiplicative orthogonal adapters, which offer superior training stability and inherent preservation of spectral and Frobenius norms. The specific challenge of fusing a subject-driven concept adapter with an artistic style adapter into a single generator remains unresolved, limiting the ability to produce images that simultaneously preserve a user-defined concept and apply a user-defined style without joint retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260020/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two independently trained GS-orthogonal adapters (each a structured matrix of the form P_L · L · P · R · P_R with block-diagonal orthogonal blocks of size b×b and perfect-shuffle permutations) and produces a single merged GS-orthogonal adapter of the same structured class, controlled by a scalar fusion parameter t ∈ [0,1]. The merged adapter is then applied multiplicatively to the frozen diffusion model weights (W* = A(t)·W) to generate stylized concept images at the base model's native resolution (e.g., 1024×1024 for SDXL). The entire merging procedure completes in under one second on a single GPU.

## Method

OrthoFuse establishes that the set of GS-orthogonal matrices forms a smooth Riemannian manifold (Theorem 2), enabling the use of geodesic curves to interpolate between two adapters. The merging proceeds in two steps per block: (1) block-wise geodesic interpolation via B(t) = B_C · exp(−t·log(B_S^T B_C)), computed efficiently through eigendecomposition of each small b×b block, and (2) a spectra restoration transform that applies a Cayley transform with a time-varying multiplier η(t) = 1 + 4t(1−t) to counteract the empirical compression of eigenvalues toward 1 that naive geodesic interpolation causes. This second step is derived as a first-order approximation (Proposition 3) that avoids full diagonalization at every t, making the operation hardware-efficient. The approach is entirely training-free, requires no additional optimization, and preserves the GS-orthogonal structure of the output adapter by construction.

## Results

On 72 concept–style pairs (6 DreamBooth concepts × 12 styles from StyleDrop/K-LoRA, 10 images each, SDXL base), OrthoFuse achieves the highest style fidelity (CLIP style similarity 0.61) and the best geometric mean of style and concept (0.64 for Style–CLIP, 0.56 for Style–DINO), while completing the merge in under 1 second. Concept-preservation metrics (CLIP 0.68, DINO 0.51) are slightly lower than the training-based Joint baseline (CLIP 0.79, DINO 0.67), which is expected given the stronger stylization. In a 65-participant user study (1,460 pairwise comparisons), OrthoFuse is preferred 77–83% of the time for style transfer and 67–76% overall over K-LoRA and ZipLoRA respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against K-LoRA (r=8 and r=64), OrthoFuse substantially outperforms on style fidelity (0.61 vs. 0.46–0.49 CLIP style similarity, a 12–25% relative gain) and on the combined Style–CLIP geometric mean (0.64 vs. 0.59–0.61), while trading off concept metrics (CLIP 0.68 vs. 0.76, DINO 0.51 vs. 0.55–0.56). Against ZipLoRA (r=8 and r=64), OrthoFuse again leads on style similarity (0.61 vs. 0.49) and the Style–CLIP geometric mean (0.64 vs. 0.60–0.61), but lags on DINO concept preservation (0.51 vs. 0.55–0.64), particularly at r=64. The training-based Joint Orthogonal baseline achieves the highest concept scores (CLIP 0.79, DINO 0.67) but the lowest style similarity (0.48) and requires 1.5 hours of additional fine-tuning per pair, whereas OrthoFuse is entirely training-free and merges in under a second. In the user study, OrthoFuse is preferred over K-LoRA 77% of the time for style transfer and 67% overall, and over ZipLoRA 83% for style and 76% overall, confirming that the quantitative style advantage translates into perceptual preference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the orthogonal fine-tuning framework of Qiu et al. (NeurIPS 2023) and the Group-and-Shuffle structured parametrization of Gorbunov et al. (NeurIPS 2024) by showing that the set of GS-orthogonal matrices forms a Riemannian manifold, enabling geodesic-based interpolation between adapters. It departs from the dominant LoRA-merging literature (K-LoRA, ZipLoRA, MoLe) by operating in multiplicative orthogonal parameter space rather than additive low-rank space, eliminating the scale-mismatch problem inherent to LoRA fusion. The spectral restoration step via a Cayley-transform-based eigenvalue rotation is a novel contribution not present in prior adapter-merging or Riemannian optimization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OrthoFuse provides the first training-free merging method for orthogonal adapters, reducing fusion cost from hours (joint training) to under one second while achieving the best style-transfer scores among all compared methods. The manifold-structure theorem and geodesic approximation offer a principled geometric foundation that could generalize to other structured matrix classes beyond GS-orthogonal. Open questions include whether the approach extends to merging more than two adapters (Riemannian barycenters), whether the spectral restoration heuristic generalizes to adapters farther from identity, and whether the modest concept-preservation trade-off (CLIP 0.68 vs. 0.79 for joint training) can be closed without sacrificing style fidelity.
