## Problem

Existing backdoor defenses are fragmented: traditional purification methods (e.g., RNP, MNP) fail on conditional backdoors that remain dormant in the original model, while quantization-specific defenses (e.g., EFRAP, LACPDA) cannot handle traditional backdoor attacks. No single method provides universal protection across both threat families, leaving a gap for a unified defense that works with minimal clean data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263413/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a backdoored model (CNN or ViT) and a very small clean sample set as low as 0.02–1% of the original training set (e.g., 10–2500 images for CIFAR-10). It outputs a purified model with pruned channels in the linear classifier head, where pruned columns are zeroed and frozen. The clean data is used both for logit-margin optimization in Phase 1 and for the lightweight recovery fine-tuning in Phase 2; no trigger information or attack knowledge is required.

## Method

LMR first identifies the backdoor class via an anti-learning step that maximizes cross-entropy on a small clean batch, then computes the class with the highest mean log-posterior under the anti-learned parameters. Phase 1 (Logit-Geometric Purification) optimizes a composite loss on clean samples: Selective Cross-Entropy (SCE) disables the CE weight for backdoor-class samples to avoid reinforcing backdoor representations; Directed Suppression (DSC) enforces a positive margin m₁=3 between the backdoor-class logit and the maximum non-backdoor logit, geometrically contracting the backdoor decision region; and a Conditional Margin (CM) term stabilizes non-backdoor class boundaries by penalizing only ambiguous samples. Phase 2 (Delta-Prune) computes the ℓ₁ change in each head channel's backdoor-class weight between the start and end of Phase 1, prunes the top-k most-changed channels to sever feature-to-backdoor-class shortcuts, and performs a brief CE fine-tuning to restore benign accuracy on the backdoor class.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, 1% clean data), LMR reduces the average ASR from 96.80% (no defense) to 0.53% across nine traditional backdoor attacks, while retaining 95.03% clean accuracy (only 0.52% below the backdoored model's 95.55%). For conditional backdoor attacks (QCB, QCB-D, PCB), LMR achieves an average ASR of 0.72% with 89.03% ACC, matching the no-defense accuracy. On ImageNet (ResNet-34), LMR drops the average ASR from 94.18% to 0.68% while maintaining 82.26% ACC, the highest among all compared defenses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMR achieves the lowest average ASR (0.53%) across all nine traditional backdoor attacks on CIFAR-10, outperforming the next-best general-purpose method MNP (3.42%) by a factor of ~6.5×, and substantially beating RNP (13.56%), I-BAU (15.94%), IAP (16.61%), NAD (23.15%), and FP (24.64%). LMR also preserves the highest clean accuracy (95.03%) among all non-specialized defenses, losing only 0.52% relative to the backdoored model, whereas FP and IAP drop 6+ points. The QCB-specialized methods EFRAP and LACPDA are ineffective against traditional backdoors (avg. ASR 89.41% and 81.42% respectively), confirming the gap LMR fills as a universal defense. On conditional backdoor attacks, LMR (0.72% avg. ASR) surpasses MNP (2.14%) and dramatically outperforms RNP (29.53%), I-BAU (32.25%), and FP (58.51%), while EFRAP/LACPDA remain limited to QCB only. On ImageNet, LMR achieves 0.68% avg. ASR with 82.26% ACC, beating MNP (0.95% ASR, 80.23% ACC) and RNP (1.39% ASR, 79.57% ACC) on both metrics simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

LMR builds directly on the unlearning-based backdoor-class identification paradigm from RNP (Li et al., ICML 2023), repurposing the anti-learning step to localize the target class before purification. It departs from prior defenses by replacing architecture-specific pruning heuristics (FP, MNP) or QCB-specific alignment strategies (EFRAP, LACPDA) with a unified logit-space formulation: a directed suppression margin (DSC) that actively contracts the backdoor class's decision region on clean data, combined with selective cross-entropy and a conditional stability regularizer. The key conceptual departure is that LMR treats both traditional and conditional backdoors as instances of the same geometric problem—insufficient logit margin between the backdoor class and its competitors—rather than requiring separate detection or purification pipelines for each attack family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LMR is the first defense to claim simultaneous coverage of traditional, quantization-conditioned, and pruning-conditioned backdoors with a single algorithm, closing a gap explicitly identified in the literature where QCB-specific methods (EFRAP, LACPDA) fail on traditional attacks and vice versa. Its practical appeal is strong: only 0.1% (even 0.02%) clean data is needed, and the method is architecture-agnostic across CNNs and ViTs. Limitations include the pruning step being restricted to the linear classifier head (not intermediate layers), the assumption of logit access, and evaluation limited to image classification—open questions remain about applicability to LLMs, multi-modal models, and more adaptive conditional attacks that could learn to respect the imposed margin.
