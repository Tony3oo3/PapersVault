## Problem

Neural 3D reconstructions (NeRF, SDF, 3DGS) produce dense, unstructured meshes with irregular topology that are difficult to edit, animate, or reuse in graphics pipelines. Existing primitive-based methods that do yield compact, part-based geometry rely exclusively on additive composition, fundamentally limiting their ability to model topologically rich structures such as holes and concavities that are ubiquitous in real-world objects.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262062/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 26 multi-view RGB images at 256×256 resolution (24 viewpoints uniformly sampled on a unit sphere plus top and bottom views) as input. It outputs a compact 3D mesh (averaging ~1.5k vertices and ~0.8k faces) extracted via closed-form Boolean difference between the positive and negative superquadric components, along with the learned dual-primitive parameters (scale, shape, translation, rotation, transparency, sharpness, and color per primitive). Unlike SDF-grid-based methods that require Marching Cubes post-processing, the mesh is directly generated from analytic primitive wireframes, preserving clean edge loops and part boundaries.

## Method

DualPrim represents each shape element as a pair of superquadrics: a positive density superquadric (PSQ) that builds the base volume and a negative density superquadric (NSQ) that carves local regions. The subtraction is made differentiable through a probabilistic gating mechanism (Equation 4) that computes an effectiveness probability P_E via sigmoid functions of both ISF values, so the NSQ only removes density where it overlaps the PSQ. The merged implicit surface and normals are computed as weighted combinations (Equations 5–6), and volume density is estimated following the NeuS formulation. Training uses a volumetric differentiable renderer with six loss terms (photometric, mask, sparsity, entropy, max-value, and normal consistency) to jointly optimize all primitive parameters from multi-view supervision. An adaptive pruning strategy, inspired by 3DGS, iteratively removes primitives with transparency below 0.02 or scale below 0.01, evolving from 100 initial primitives to a compact final set.

## Results

On ShapeNet (12 categories, 180 objects, 26 views), DualPrim achieves a Chamfer Distance of 7.94, substantially outperforming the next-best method MeshAnything (10.12) and all other baselines. The method also produces the most compact meshes among high-fidelity approaches (1.54k vertices, 0.79k faces) while attaining the highest user-rated editability score (8.29/10), nearly 3 points above the runner-up DiffCSG (5.50). In terms of normal consistency loss, DualPrim (7.73) is competitive with DiffCSG (8.20) and CAPRI (11.55), though slightly behind PA (4.48) and MA (4.00).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualPrim wins decisively on Chamfer Distance (7.94) against all 13 baselines, with the closest competitor being MeshAnything (10.12, a 21.5% gap) followed by NVD (10.63) and RNS (10.71). On the user-study editability score, DualPrim (8.29) leads by a wide margin over DiffCSG (5.50), NVD (5.47), and MA (5.12), confirming that the dual-primitive structure is significantly more editable than both implicit-field and CSG-based alternatives. In terms of mesh compactness, DualPrim (1.54k vertices) is second only to MA (0.55k) but achieves far superior geometric fidelity (CD 7.94 vs. 10.12), whereas volume-rendering baselines like RNS (124.41k) and 2DGS+MC (151.71k) produce meshes 80–100× larger. On normal consistency loss, DualPrim (7.73) trails PA (4.48) and MA (4.00) but outperforms most other methods including CAPRI (11.55), D²CSG (13.21), and FC (16.11). The CSG-based methods (CAPRI, D²CSG, DiffCSG) and primitive-based methods (PA, MP, EMS) all rely on SDF inputs from RNS or 2DGS, introducing error propagation that DualPrim avoids by learning primitives end-to-end from images.

## Relation to Prior Work

DualPrim extends the primitive-based reconstruction lineage (Tulsiani et al., Paschalidou et al., MarchingPrimitives, EMS, PrimitiveAnything) by introducing a subtractive component: each positive superquadric is paired with a negative one that carves local volumes via a differentiable boolean difference. This departs from the purely additive composition used by all prior superquadric methods and from non-differentiable CSG parsers (CSGNet, D²CSG, CAPRI) that cannot learn directly from images. The rendering pipeline borrows the NeuS density-from-SDF formulation and the 3DGS-style adaptive pruning strategy, but the core novelty is the probabilistic PSQ/NSQ merging mechanism (Equations 4–6) that makes the subtraction differentiable and end-to-end trainable from multi-view supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a compact dual-primitive representation can simultaneously achieve state-of-the-art Chamfer Distance (7.94 vs. 10.12–16.64 for baselines), extreme mesh compactness (1.54k vertices vs. 124k for RNS), and substantially higher user-rated editability (8.29 vs. ≤5.50), addressing a persistent gap between dense neural reconstructions and artist-editable geometry. Key limitations acknowledged by the authors include difficulty with parts thinner than the voxel grid, residual primitive overlap, and reduced performance on irregular real-world objects beyond industrially designed shapes. Open questions include whether the dual-primitive paradigm scales to scenes with many interacting objects, whether the NSQ mechanism generalizes to non-superquadric primitives, and how the approach handles dynamic or deformable geometry.
