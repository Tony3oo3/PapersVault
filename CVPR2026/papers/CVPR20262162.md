## Problem

Whole-body mobile manipulation couples base mobility with arm dexterity, exponentially expanding the configuration space and demanding datasets orders of magnitude larger than fixed-base manipulation. Existing acquisition methods—teleoperation and CPU-based planning (e.g., AKR at 60 trajectories/hour)—are either labor-intensive or computationally prohibitive at scale, leaving a critical gap in large-scale, physically valid, coordinated trajectory data across diverse embodiments and environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262162/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a task specification triplet (scene, object, robot): scene geometry is converted to an ESDF restricted to an axis-aligned bounding box around the object's start/goal states; objects provide full URDF kinematic chains with joint limits (inverted and re-rooted at the grasp point for articulated objects); robots supply URDF definitions augmented with fitted-sphere collision approximations and self-collision masks. Each generated trajectory comprises 30 joint-space waypoints paired with synchronized RGB-D images (320×240) and 4,096-point point clouds rendered at 120 frames per trajectory from both egocentric and fixed cameras in Isaac Sim.

## Method

AutoMoMa constructs an Augmented Kinematic Representation (AKR) that merges the mobile base (modeled as two prismatic + one revolute virtual joints), the manipulator, and the target object into a single serial kinematic chain by inverting the object's kinematic tree and inserting a virtual joint at the grasp pose. Trajectory optimization minimizes a weighted sum of travel distance and acceleration smoothness (Eq. 7) subject to kinematic chain constraints, task-goal tolerances, joint limits, and velocity/acceleration bounds, with collision avoidance handled via GPU-batched sphere-based checks. The key acceleration comes from batching all trajectory optimization and collision queries on the GPU (leveraging Curobo), achieving 5,000 episodes per GPU-hour—80× faster than CPU baselines. For complex multi-step tasks (e.g., grasp switching in confined spaces), the planner samples an intermediate re-grasp state to connect two trajectory segments. Downstream, the 500k+ generated trajectories train IL policies (DP3, Diffusion Policy, ACT) on fused point-cloud and proprioceptive observations, empirically demonstrating that tens of thousands of demonstrations are required for SOTA methods to reach ~80% success on a single articulated-object task.

## Results

AutoMoMa generates 5,000 physically valid whole-body trajectories per GPU-hour, an 80× speedup over CPU-based AKR planning (60 trajectories/hour), yielding a dataset of over 500k trajectories across 330 scenes and three robot embodiments. In downstream policy learning, the fixed-base robot reaches 100% success with fewer than 800 trajectories, while the mobile-base policy saturates at ~70% even with 3,200 trajectories, highlighting the combinatorial complexity of 10-DoF base-arm coupling. Scaling to 30 scenes with higher per-scene trajectory density lifts DP3 to ~75% success on both seen and unseen environments, and per-object evaluation at 100k trajectories shows >50% success on seen configurations for 4 of 5 articulated objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoMoMa dominates all 10 compared datasets on every axis simultaneously: scale (500k vs. 73.5k max for RT-1), scene diversity (330 vs. 216 max for DobbE), and acquisition method (fully automatic GPU planning vs. teleoperation or scripted policies). The closest competitor in scale, RT-1 (73,499 episodes), covers only 10 scenes and uses end-effector poses rather than joint-space trajectories, while DobbE (5,208 episodes, 216 scenes) still requires tool-based teleoperation and provides end-effector poses. Mobile ALOHA (276 episodes) and BC-Z (39,350 episodes) offer whole-body coordination but are limited to 5 and 2–3 scenes respectively, with BC-Z using decoupled base-arm control. In policy learning, DP3 trained on AutoMoMa data outperforms DP and ACT at equivalent trajectory densities, confirming that the 3D point-cloud modality is critical, yet all three architectures show monotonic gains with data scale—evidence that prior datasets were too small to saturate any of these methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoMoMa extends the AKR (Augmented Kinematic Representation) framework introduced by Jiao et al. (IROS 2021, T-RO 2025), which unifies mobile base, manipulator, and object kinematics into a single serial chain for constraint-aware planning. The key departure is throughput: prior AKR implementations relied on CPU-based solvers generating only ~60 trajectories/hour, whereas AutoMoMa integrates Curobo's GPU-parallelized trajectory optimization to achieve 5,000 episodes per GPU-hour (80× speedup). In the data-collection landscape, the paper positions itself against teleoperation datasets (Mobile ALOHA, BC-Z) that are high-fidelity but limited to hundreds–thousands of episodes, and against scripted-policy datasets that lack whole-body base-arm coordination, offering a middle path of automated planning with full kinematic validity at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 500k-trajectory dataset across 330 scenes and three robot embodiments is an order of magnitude larger than any prior mobile manipulation dataset, and the empirical scaling experiments demonstrate that even a single articulated-object task requires tens of thousands of demonstrations for SOTA policies (DP3) to reach ~80% success—confirming data scarcity, not algorithmic design, as the binding constraint. Limitations include reliance on known scene geometries, absence of dynamic human-robot interaction or deformable objects, and sphere-based collision approximations that can introduce geometric inaccuracies. Open questions include whether learning-based generation can complement or replace the optimization pipeline, and whether the framework can be extended to unknown or partially observed environments.
