## Problem

Human vision robustly segments independently moving objects across radically different settings—sparse random dot kinematograms, camouflaged textured objects perceivable only through motion, and naturalistic scenes—yet no single computer vision system achieves this generality. The paper asks what computational principles underlie this broad perceptual capability and whether a unified probabilistic framework can replicate it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261522/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video preprocessed into per-pixel 3D position-velocity pairs via RAFT optical flow and VideoDepthAnything monocular depth (optionally augmented with DINO appearance features), operating on unordered point clouds without tracked correspondences. It outputs a hierarchical posterior over 3D Gaussian particles (local matter regions with spatial extent and velocity) and their cluster assignments (coherently moving entities), which can be projected to 2D tracks or segmentation masks. Inference produces 500 posterior samples per frame via MCMC, from which MAP estimates or probabilistic predictions are extracted.

## Method

GenMatter defines a two-level hierarchical generative model where clusters (parameterized by spatial Gaussians and SE(3) rigid-body transformations) generate particles (local Gaussians with velocity means drawn from the parent cluster's rigid motion plus isotropic slack), which in turn generate observed position-velocity data points. Inference proceeds via parallelized block Gibbs sampling on GPU (GenJAX), exploiting conjugate Normal-Inverse-Wishart and Dirichlet-Categorical updates at each hierarchical level, with rigid transforms sampled from discretized SE(3) via parallel likelihood enumeration. The key design choice is that particle velocity covariance provides probabilistic slack for intra-cluster deformation, replacing hand-crafted ARAP regularization with a principled small-variance asymptotic limit that recovers K-means/Procrustes objectives. Multi-frame tracking uses sequential MCMC where particle means are propagated by inferred velocities but cluster assignments and transformations are re-inferred at every frame to handle articulation, and the entire inference engine requires no task-specific training—only pretrained feature extractors for input construction.

## Results

On the Gestalt camouflaged rotating objects benchmark (140 videos), GenMatter achieves 0.94 per-pixel accuracy and 0.72 Jaccard index, outperforming FlowSAM (0.87 / 0.67) and SegAnyMo (0.33 / 0.26). On TAP-VidDAVIS, GenMatter matches the supervised CoTracker3 tracker with a matter-weighted Jaccard of 0.79 vs. 0.78, without any task-specific pretraining. On random dot kinematograms, GenMatter reproduces human object judgments with r²=0.86 correlation, while FlowSAM achieves only r²=0.04.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenMatter decisively outperforms SegAnyMo [28] on the Gestalt benchmark (0.94 vs. 0.33 accuracy; 0.72 vs. 0.26 Jaccard), where SegAnyMo frequently fails to detect the object at all. Against FlowSAM [63], GenMatter wins on both the Gestalt dataset (0.94 vs. 0.87 accuracy; 0.72 vs. 0.67 Jaccard, winning on 111/140 videos) and the RDK stimuli (r²=0.86 vs. 0.04 correlation with human judgments), demonstrating that probabilistic inference over a structured generative model generalizes far better than learned feedforward segmentation on motion-dominated grouping. On TAP-VidDAVIS, GenMatter matches the supervised CoTracker3 [31] tracker (0.79 vs. 0.78 matter-weighted Jaccard) without any task-specific pretraining, while additionally providing explicit 3D particle representations and cluster-level object groupings that CoTracker3 does not offer. Ablations confirm the hierarchical cluster structure is essential: removing it degrades RDK correlation from r²=0.86 to 0.35–0.41 and TAP-VidDAVIS Jaccard from 0.79 to 0.69.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenMatter carves new ground at the intersection of analysis-by-synthesis probabilistic programming (Bayes3D, 3DP3, PICTURE) and modern motion-based segmentation, but departs from both threads in important ways. Unlike prior probabilistic perception systems that impose restrictive geometric assumptions, GenMatter uses pretrained feature extractors (RAFT, VideoDepthAnything, DINO) as observation models while keeping the core inference engine training-free and domain-agnostic. It also explicitly contrasts with ARAP-style regularization by showing that hierarchical Bayesian inference over cluster assignments subsumes fixed-radius rigidity penalties, and with tracking-any-point systems by adaptively discovering particle representations rather than tracking user-specified points. The result is a unified generative model that operates across RDKs, camouflaged 3D stimuli, and naturalistic video—settings where no single prior CV system succeeds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured probabilistic inference with a few kilobytes of inference code can match supervised trackers on naturalistic video and outperform them on motion-dominated grouping, suggesting that inductive biases from hierarchical generative priors may be more important than task-specific training for motion-based perception. Key limitations include a fixed particle count (no dynamic allocation), absence of explicit physics-based dynamics (limiting occlusion handling), and reliance on 2D masks as a proxy for 3D tracking evaluation due to the lack of large-scale 3D deformable-object ground truth. Open questions include whether incorporating a physics engine would enable forward prediction through complete occlusion, and whether the framework scales to longer sequences or more complex multi-object interactions.
