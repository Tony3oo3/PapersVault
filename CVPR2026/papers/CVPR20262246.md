## Problem

Existing compound activity modeling methods are siloed: chemoproteomics-centric approaches capture molecular binding mechanisms but miss downstream phenotypic consequences, while phenotype-centric imaging methods capture systems-level effects without explicit molecular context. Although multimodal approaches have emerged, they rely on shallow fusion strategies, lack biological contextualization, and fail to exploit the interplay between molecular mechanisms and cellular responses, leaving substantial performance gains unrealized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262246/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes compound SMILES strings, protein amino acid sequences, 480×480 five-channel 16-bit HCI image patches (from Cell Painting at a fixed 20 μM dose), and a binarized protein-protein interaction adjacency matrix. It produces binary activity predictions for each compound–protein–concentration triplet, where the concentration set varies per protein across 123 assays spanning multiple technologies and species. The compound-to-task label matrix is extremely sparse (~3% fill rate), with 170 tasks across 65 proteins, making the output a highly multi-task, multi-label prediction problem.

## Method

BiGMINT performs a three-stage hierarchical integration: (1) a frozen PSICHIC graph neural network with a learned adapter encodes compound–protein molecular interactions into a chemoproteomics embedding; (2) a DINOv2/DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 extracts patch-level phenotypic features, which are then aggregated via task-specific cross-attention where the chemoproteomics embedding serves as the query, amplifying mechanism-relevant cellular patches while suppressing confounding signals; (3) a non-parametric outer-product fusion operator combines the molecular and phenotypic embeddings per task, capturing multiplicative cross-modal interactions without additional learned parameters. To address label sparsity, a protein-protein interaction prior is converted into a task-task interaction matrix (top-K correlated tasks), and cross-attention augmentation propagates information from well-characterized related proteins to poorly-measured tasks. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end with a multi-task weighted binary cross-entropy objective, using hard-parameter sharing for the HCI projector to regularize across tasks.

## Results

BiGMINT achieves 78.23% AUCROC on U2OS and 76.51% on iNeuron, outperforming the best HCI-only method (MIL→MTL: 71.17%/69.51%), the best chemoproteomics-only method (PSICHIC: 71.11%/72.62%), and the best multimodal baseline (MM-Union: 75.10%/74.99%). At the AUCROC ≥ 80% threshold, BiGMINT covers 67 and 59 tasks on U2OS and iNeuron respectively, exceeding the optimistic MM-Union upper-bound by 56% and 5%. Ablations confirm that chemoproteomics-guided HCI aggregation, outer-product fusion, and PPI-based task-task interaction priors each contribute incremental gains over simpler multimodal integration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiGMINT outperforms all 11 baselines on both datasets. Against HCI-only methods, it surpasses the best (MIL→MTL [46]: 71.17% U2OS, 69.51% iNeuron) by 7.06 and 7.00 AUCROC points respectively. Against chemoproteomics-only methods, it beats PSICHIC [34] (71.11%/72.62%) by 7.12/3.89 points, and DrugBAN [3] (68.99%/69.59%) by 9.24/6.92 points. Among multimodal baselines, BiGMINT exceeds the optimistic MM-Union upper-bound [42] (75.10%/74.99%) by 3.13/1.52 points, and concatenation-based methods [58] (73.65%/72.71% and 73.34%/73.19%) by 4.58/3.80 and 4.89/3.32 points. Alignment-based methods CLOOME [49] (63.49%/62.55%) and MolPhenix [19] (70.17%/68.26%) are substantially outperformed, confirming that integration strategies dominate alignment for this task. The one-hot logistic regression baseline [13] (49.16%/49.52%) confirms minimal dataset memorization bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiGMINT builds on PSICHIC (Koh et al., 2024) as a frozen chemoproteomics encoder and adopts the outer-product fusion operator from Pathomic Fusion (Chen et al., 2020) for cross-modal integration. It departs from prior multimodal drug-discovery work (CLOOME, MolPhenix, MM-Union, concatenation-based methods) by introducing a hierarchical, biologically-guided pipeline where the chemoproteomics embedding actively conditions HCI patch aggregation via cross-attention before fusion, rather than treating modalities as parallel inputs. The PPI-prior-based task embedding augmentation extends multi-task learning with structured biological knowledge, going beyond the label-correlation-based TTI used in prior HCI multi-task work (MoMIL).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that hierarchical, mechanism-guided integration of chemoproteomics and HCI yields substantially better compound activity prediction than either modality alone or simple multimodal fusion, with the largest gains on sparse, low-performing tasks. The mechanistic finding that HCI captures hub-protein effects while chemoproteomics handles less-connected proteins provides actionable insight for model design. Limitations include reliance on in-house datasets (99K/40K pairs) that are not publicly available, a fixed HCI treatment dose decoupled from task-specific concentrations, and the need for future validation on unseen proteins and novel compound-protein pairs.
