## Problem

Existing activation steering methods for bias mitigation rely on pre-defined heuristics (e.g., contrastive activation addition, inference-time intervention) that struggle to achieve equiprobable outcomes across demographic groups in vision-language models, and offer no principled way to balance bias reduction against model capability loss at inference time. Steering for bias in VLMs remains largely underexplored, with prior work focusing on hallucinations, jailbreaks, or toxicity rather than stereotypical decision-making.

## Input / Output

For VLMs, the method consumes a text prompt (e.g., 'Who is the doctor around me?') paired with an RGB image showing two individuals of opposite perceived gender, and produces a text decision identifying one candidate. For LLMs, it takes a text-only coreference prompt and produces a referent answer. The DSO procedure itself outputs sparse linear steering parameters (per-layer vectors a and b applied to LayerNorm activations) plus a scalar strength λ, which practitioners can adjust at inference time to trade off fairness against accuracy without retraining.

## Method

DSO formulates bias mitigation as a reinforcement learning problem over linear interventions on LayerNorm activations, using a per-sample fairness reward that penalizes responses matching the model's majority stereotype for each occupation and rewards the minority response. This reward-based formulation is proven equivalent to directly minimizing the aggregated Per-Occupation Bias metric (Theorem 1), enabling standard policy-gradient solvers like PPO. An L1 sparsity penalty restricts interventions to the most relevant neurons (less than 0.005% of total parameters), while a KL divergence constraint between the steered and base model provides a theoretical capability-preservation bound (Theorem 2). The scalar λ scales the learned intervention at inference time, giving practitioners a monotonic, interpretable knob to trade fairness against accuracy without re-optimization.

## Results

On the SocialCounterfactuals dataset (occupation identification task), DSO at λ=0.2 on Qwen-2.5-7B VL reduces Per-Occupation Bias from 25.8% to 15.4% (−10 p.p.) and Stereotype Gap from 18.0% to 6.6% (−12 p.p.), while retaining Unambiguous accuracy at 95.7% (vs. 96.5% base) and MMMU at 44.9% (vs. 46.0%). At maximum strength (λ=1.0), DSO drives Per-Occupation Bias to 8.7% and Stereotype Gap to 0.1% on the same model, at the cost of 16 p.p. Unambiguous accuracy drop. DSO Pareto-dominates the fairness–accuracy trade-off curve (Fig. 4) and achieves its bias reduction by intervening on fewer than 0.005% of model parameters (sparse LayerNorm neurons).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DSO consistently outperforms all three baselines (CAA, ITI, and Prompting) on Per-Occupation Bias across every VLM tested. CAA and Prompting are largely ineffective for VLM bias mitigation: on Qwen-2.5-7B VL, CAA actually *increases* Per-Occupation Bias from 25.8% to 28.5%, and Prompting barely moves it (24.5%), while DSO at λ=0.2 achieves 15.4%. ITI can reduce bias (e.g., 21.8% on Qwen-2.5-3B VL) but at a steep accuracy cost—MMMU drops from 41.3% to 30.5%—whereas DSO at comparable bias levels preserves MMMU within ~1.4 p.p. of base. DSO also provides monotonic, controllable bias reduction via λ, while ITI and CAA exhibit non-monotonic behavior as intervention strength increases. In LLMs (SynthBias coreference), DSO at λ=1 reduces Per-Occupation Bias from 60.4% to 5.9% on Qwen-2.5-3B, outperforming CAA (34.1%) and ITI (57.7%) while maintaining or improving Unambiguous accuracy (99.7% vs. 88.5% base).

## Relation to Prior Work

DSO extends the activation steering literature (CAA, ITI, AcT, LineAcT, LinEAS) by replacing predefined heuristics or proxy distribution-matching objectives with a reinforcement learning formulation that directly optimizes steering parameters for a fairness metric. It builds on Rodriguez et al.'s finding that LayerNorms are the most effective intervention points for linear transformations, and adopts Stickland et al.'s KL-divergence constraint to bound capability degradation. The paper positions itself as the first to apply RL-optimized steering specifically to bias mitigation in VLMs, a gap it identifies as 'largely underexplored' relative to the more mature LLM steering literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DSO offers practitioners a practical, inference-time knob (λ) to trade fairness against accuracy without retraining, which is valuable for deployed VLMs in consequential settings like hiring or visual assistance. The finding that standard steering methods (CAA, Prompting) fail to reduce Per-Occupation Bias in VLMs challenges the assumption that general-purpose steering transfers to fairness, motivating task-specific optimization. Key limitations include the restriction to binary gender-occupation bias (no race, age, or intersectional axes), the absence of comparison to fine-tuning baselines, and reliance on small counterfactual datasets; open questions include whether DSO's RL formulation generalizes to other behavioral controls (toxicity, style) and how it scales to larger, more diverse demographic settings.
