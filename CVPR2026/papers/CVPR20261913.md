## Problem

Point tracking and point forecasting are closely related motion estimation tasks, yet they have been addressed with fundamentally different architectures and training objectives—tracking relies on bespoke regression networks with iterative prediction, while forecasting uses generative latent diffusion models. This paper asks whether a single generative model can handle both tasks by simply toggling the availability of visual conditioning, eliminating the need for task-specific architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261913/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a conditioning video of T_C frames (resized to 768px shorter side) and N query point locations in the first frame, and produces full point trajectories (T×N×2 positions) plus per-point visibility indicators (T×N binary values) over a target span T ≥ T_C. The key design choice is that visual conditioning is frame-by-frame: when a frame's features are provided the model tracks, and when they are replaced by a learned null embedding the model forecasts, so the same weights handle both regimes without architectural changes.

## Method

The core architecture is a 6-block Diffusion Transformer (hidden dim 384) operating directly in point-space, where each point at each timestep is a token. Each block contains three attention modules: spatial self-attention among all points in the same frame, causal temporal self-attention along each point's history, and cross-attention to DINO-v3-S visual features (or a null embedding for forecasting). Training uses conditional flow matching with a logits-normal noise schedule for positions and binary cross-entropy for visibility, with per-point AdaLN conditioning that bilinearly samples visual features at each query location. To handle long sequences, the model generates autoregressively in sliding windows, applying diffusion forcing (independent per-frame noise levels during training and small context re-noising at inference) to prevent error accumulation. A factorization strategy—reshaping N_train points into a×b sub-batches at each training step—makes the model robust to variable query counts at test time.

## Results

On the Kubric MOVi-A forecasting benchmark, the unified model achieves Best of K = 98.6 and LRTL = 13.9, outperforming the prior best trajectory generator (Boduljak et al., Best of K = 127.0, LRTL = 14.1) and all video-generation baselines (e.g., WAN 1.3B†: Best of K = 162.8). On Physics101, the model achieves an overall MSE of 21.41, substantially beating Boduljak et al. (28.62) and WAN (30.08). On TAP-Vid tracking, the tracking-only variant reaches AJ = 50.5 (Kinetics) and AJ = 61.7 (DAVIS), which is competitive with but slightly below the best regression-based trackers (CoTracker3: 54.1/64.5; Track-On: 53.9/65.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Kubric forecasting benchmark, the unified model decisively beats the prior best trajectory generator (Boduljak et al.) on Best of K (98.6 vs. 127.0, a 22% reduction) and LRTL (13.9 vs. 14.1), while matching it on FVMD (18091 vs. 17838). All video-generation baselines (WAN, Stable Video Diffusion, LTX-Video, DynamicCrafter) are far worse, with Best of K scores ranging from 152 to 240. On Physics101, the model achieves 21.41 overall MSE versus 28.62 for Boduljak et al. and 30.08 for WAN, with particularly large gains on the Spring category (25.76 vs. 65.31 and 70.48). On TAP-Vid tracking, the method is competitive but not state-of-the-art: CoTracker3 (AJ 54.1/64.5) and Track-On (AJ 53.9/65.0) outperform the tracking-only variant (AJ 50.5/61.7) on Kinetics and DAVIS respectively, though the gap narrows on occlusion accuracy (OA 87.6–90.6 vs. 87.1–90.8). The unified model trades ~2 AJ points on tracking for the ability to forecast, and under synthetic occlusion it actually surpasses the tracking-only model by up to +14.3 in δ_avg^occ (DriveTrack), demonstrating that the learned motion prior aids robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant regression-based paradigm in point tracking (TAPIR, CoTracker, TAPTR, Track-On), which relies on bespoke architectures, cost volumes, and iterative prediction, by reframing tracking as a video-conditioned generative problem. It builds on the DiT architecture and flow matching objective, and adopts diffusion forcing to handle autoregressive error accumulation, but applies these to point-space generation rather than pixel or latent space. The closest prior work is Boduljak et al.'s latent diffusion forecaster, which uses a VAE to encode points into grid-based latents; this paper instead treats points directly as tokens, enabling a single model to toggle between tracking and forecasting simply by adding or removing visual conditioning. The very recent TAPNext work also uses a generative formulation for tracking but relies on autoregression and parallel point decoding, precluding joint point modeling and forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean unification of tracking and forecasting under a single generative model—controlled solely by the presence or absence of visual conditioning—offers a practical and extensible framework for motion estimation that could accept diverse conditioning signals (goals, actions, text) without architectural changes. The finding that a learned motion prior from forecasting training improves tracking under occlusion (up to +14.3 in δ_avg_occ on DriveTrack) suggests that generative models capture richer motion structure than pure regression. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer horizons and more complex 3D scenes, how to model uncertainty in tracking through the generative structure, and whether the slight tracking performance gap versus SOTA regression methods can be closed with more capacity or data.
