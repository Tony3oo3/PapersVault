## Problem

Existing autoregressive mesh generation methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: coordinate-level tokenization produces long sequences that prevent scaling to high-poly meshes (capped at ~22% compression), and the generative process relies on global shape embeddings with static vocabulary representations, offering no fine-grained local geometric guidance to preserve surface fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263268/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a point cloud with normals sampled from an input surface mesh, which is voxelized at 128³ resolution (7-bit quantization) and encoded by a sparse-voxel encoder. It autoregressively generates a 3D triangle mesh with up to 16K faces, using a two-level space partition (16, 8) and patch-based traversal to produce a compact 2D vertex-token sequence at 18% compression ratio.

## Method

MeshWeaver shifts the generation paradigm from next-coordinate to next-vertex prediction, where each decoding step directly predicts a complete vertex via coarse-to-fine multi-level voxel refinement within a single transformer pass. A hierarchical sparse-voxel encoder injects local geometric context through three complementary mechanisms: multi-level voxel features replace static vocabulary embeddings as vertex representations, cross-attention to level-specific voxel features guides each prediction head, and occupied-voxel masking at inference constrains predictions to the input surface. The backbone is a 24-layer LLaMA3-style transformer (1024 hidden, RoPE, 600M params) with 16+8 layer split across the two spatial levels, trained on 800K meshes with subvolume pruning for efficiency and a cross-attention KV cache that boosts inference throughput by ~14.5%.

## Results

On the Toys4K benchmark, MeshWeaver achieves the best Chamfer Distance (0.116×10⁻¹), Hausdorff Distance (0.087), and |NC| (0.914) among all compared autoregressive mesh generation methods, while matching Mesh-Silksong on Normal Consistency (0.732 vs. 0.734). In terms of tokenization efficiency, MeshWeaver's vertex-level scheme achieves a state-of-the-art compression ratio of 18%, substantially below the ~22% ceiling of prior coordinate-level methods. The model scales to meshes with up to 16K faces, whereas several baselines (e.g., MeshAnythingV2, EdgeRunner) are limited to fewer than 4K faces due to sequence-length constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeshWeaver outperforms all five generation baselines on CD and HD, with the largest margins over MeshAnythingV2 (CD 0.116 vs. 0.213, a 45% reduction) and TreeMeshGPT (CD 0.116 vs. 0.205, a 43% reduction). Against the closest competitor Mesh-Silksong, MeshWeaver improves CD by 17% (0.116 vs. 0.140) and HD by 18% (0.087 vs. 0.106), while NC is essentially tied (0.732 vs. 0.734). On |NC|, MeshWeaver leads all methods (0.914), surpassing BPT (0.909) and Mesh-Silksong (0.900). In the tokenization benchmark (Table 1), MeshWeaver's 18% compression ratio beats the next-best methods—TreeMeshGPT and Mesh-Silksong—at 22%, and is dramatically more compact than MeshAnythingV2 (46%) and EdgeRunner (47%). The paper notes that MeshAnythingV2 and EdgeRunner are further limited by their inability to handle meshes beyond ~4K faces, whereas MeshWeaver scales to 16K faces.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeshWeaver extends the autoregressive mesh generation lineage (PolyGen → MeshGPT/MeshXL → EdgeRunner/TreeMeshGPT → BPT/DeepMesh/Mesh-Silksong) by shifting the fundamental prediction unit from individual coordinates to complete vertices, and by introducing a hierarchical sparse-voxel encoder that injects local geometric context at three levels (vertex representation, cross-attention guidance, and inference-time scaffolding). Conceptually, it reframes the task from conditional shape generation to surface weaving (analogous to retopology under known geometry), departing from prior work that treats point-cloud conditioning as a global embedding. The patch-based traversal is inherited from BPT, while the multi-level voxel representation and sparse-attention encoder draw on the broader sparse-voxel 3D generation literature (X-Cube, SparseFlex, etc.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 18% compression ratio and ability to scale to 16K faces address two practical bottlenecks that have limited autoregressive mesh generation to low-poly outputs, making the approach more applicable to production-quality assets. The sparse-voxel scaffolding mechanism (masking empty voxels at inference) is a simple but effective way to prevent surface drift, and the three-way decomposition of geometric guidance (representation, attention, scaffold) provides a clear recipe for future work. Open questions include whether the compression can be pushed further (the authors note BOS tokens could be eliminated), whether the approach generalizes to text- or image-conditioned generation, and whether the 16K-face ceiling can be raised with longer sequences or more efficient attention.
