## Problem

Existing VLA models suffer from four coupled limitations: massive parameter counts (3–7B) that demand high GPU memory and slow inference, heavy reliance on costly large-scale robot data pretraining, low control frequency that hinders real-time deployment, and end-to-end training that degrades the vision-language backbone's perceptual representations, causing overfitting and poor generalization. Evo-1 addresses all four simultaneously by proposing a sub-billion-parameter architecture that achieves state-of-the-art manipulation performance without any robot data pretraining.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view RGB observations (resized to 448×448, then downsampled 4× via pixel-unshuffle to reduce visual tokens), a natural-language instruction, and the robot's proprioceptive state vector. It outputs a continuous action chunk of H future steps predicted via a flow-matching denoising process, where the interpolation weight τ is sampled from a Beta distribution clamped to [0.02, 0.98]. The entire pipeline runs at 16.4 Hz on a single RTX 4090d with only 2.3 GB GPU memory, enabling real-time closed-loop control on a 6-DoF xArm6.

## Method

Evo-1 (0.77B params) builds on the InternVL3-1B VLM backbone (InternViT-300M visual encoder + Qwen2.5-0.5B language decoder, retaining only the first 14 language layers for stronger cross-modal alignment) and couples it with a cross-modulated Diffusion Transformer action expert that uses exclusively stacked cross-attention layers (no self-attention), learning a flow-matching velocity field to denoise action trajectories. An integration module concatenates the 14th-layer VLM representation with the robot state to form key-value inputs for the DiT's cross-attention, preserving full information without projection. Training follows a two-stage paradigm: Stage 1 freezes the VLM and trains only the action expert and integration module to establish coherent alignment without back-propagating noisy gradients into pretrained weights; Stage 2 unfreezes the VLM for full fine-tuning. This staged approach empirically preserves the VLM's semantic attention patterns (verified via attention-map visualization), yielding superior generalization under distractors, background changes, and spatial perturbations compared to single-stage end-to-end training.

## Results

Evo-1 (0.77B params, no robot-data pretraining) achieves state-of-the-art average success rates on all three simulation benchmarks: 80.6% on Meta-World (vs. 68.2% for SmolVLA, 47.9% for π0), 94.8% on LIBERO (vs. 94.2% for π0, 93.9% for GR00T N1), and 37.8% on RoboTwin (vs. 30.9% for π0). In real-world evaluation on an xArm6, Evo-1 reaches 78% overall success rate while consuming only 2.3 GB GPU memory and running at 16.4 Hz inference frequency, compared to 17.9 GB and 11.5 Hz for the 3.5B π0 model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Evo-1 outperforms all baselines across every benchmark where they share evaluation. On Meta-World, Evo-1 (80.6%) surpasses the previous best SmolVLA (68.2%) by 12.4 points and π0 (47.9%) by 32.7 points, while using roughly 3× fewer parameters than SmolVLA and 4.5× fewer than π0. On LIBERO, Evo-1 (94.8%) edges out π0 (94.2%) and GR00T N1 (93.9%) by narrow margins, and substantially exceeds SmolVLA (88.8%) and OpenVLA (76.5%). On RoboTwin (dual-arm), Evo-1 (37.8%) beats π0 (30.9%) by 6.9 points and RDT (25.8%) by 12 points. In real-world tasks, Evo-1 (78%) outperforms π0 (73%), OpenVLA-OFT (55%), and SmolVLA (50%), while also achieving the highest inference frequency (16.4 Hz) and lowest memory footprint (2.3 GB) among all compared models. Notably, Evo-1 achieves these results without any large-scale robot-data pretraining, whereas π0, OpenVLA, GR00T N1, and RDT all rely on it.

## Relation to Prior Work

Evo-1 sits at the intersection of two VLA design lines: the large-scale pretraining paradigm (OpenVLA, π0, GR00T N1) that relies on multi-billion-parameter backbones and robot-data pretraining, and the emerging lightweight line (TinyVLA, SmolVLA) that reduces parameter count but still underperforms on complex tasks. It departs from both by adopting a natively multimodal VLM (InternVL3-1B) rather than a post-hoc-aligned LLM, replacing the alternating self/cross-attention DiT used by π0 and SmolVLA with a cross-attention-only diffusion transformer, and introducing a two-stage training schedule that freezes the VLM before full fine-tuning to preserve its semantic space. The integration module's choice to concatenate VLM features with robot state (rather than project into a shared space) is a deliberate information-preservation design that distinguishes it from prior cross-attention conditioning schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a 0.77B model running at 16.4 Hz on 2.3 GB of GPU memory achieves competitive or superior performance to 3.5–7B models requiring 15–18 GB, making VLA deployment on consumer-grade hardware genuinely feasible without the costly robot-data pretraining pipeline. The two-stage training paradigm offers a reusable recipe for adapting pretrained VLMs to control tasks without degrading their perceptual representations, a problem that plagues end-to-end VLA fine-tuning broadly. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dexterous multi-fingered manipulation, whether the 50–100 demonstration budget per task scales to long-horizon or open-world settings, and whether the modest RoboTwin scores (37.8%) reflect a fundamental limitation of the compact architecture on bimanual coordination or simply insufficient task-specific data.
