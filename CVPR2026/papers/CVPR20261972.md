## Problem

Most language-supervised vision models assume multi-positive text supervision, where multiple captions per image are semantically redundant paraphrases. In non-object-centric domains like medical imaging and remote sensing, images are instead paired with itemized text: multiple independent findings describing spatially disjoint regions with minimal semantic overlap. Existing CLIP-style and multi-positive objectives either pull all positive captions together (violating item independence) or concatenate items into a single caption (discarding compositional structure), failing to produce representations that are simultaneously item-differentiable and complete.

## Input / Output

The method takes a visual input (2D image or 3D volume with multiple sequences, e.g., a whole-study brain MRI) and a set of n_i semantically independent text items, where n_i varies per sample. The visual is encoded into a global CLS token and m patch-level visual tokens; each text item is encoded separately. At inference, zero-shot classification uses TCSim between a class text embedding and the visual tokens, while the cross-attention attention maps serve as item-level explanations, segmentation masks (top-K tokens), and region-based retrieval queries over a text corpus.

## Method

ItemizedCLIP augments a standard ViT + text encoder with a multi-headed cross-attention module that produces text-item-conditioned visual embeddings by using each item's encoding as query over the patch-level visual tokens. Training combines four SigLIP-based objectives: (1) Itemized Local Alignment (ILA), which adapts FLAIR's TCS loss with Upweighting Worst Positive (UWP) to enforce completeness and Bernoulli masking for robustness; (2) Inter-Item Separation (IIS), which treats text-conditioned visual representations of different items as negative pairs to enforce spatial independence; (3) Multi-positive SigLIP (MPS) for global CLS-token alignment; and (4) Key Token Alignment (KTA), which restricts TCS to only the top-K% highest-attention visual tokens to sharpen localization. The key design insight is that IIS (which failed under multi-positive supervision in FLAIR) becomes effective under itemized supervision because items are genuinely independent, while UWP directly targets the completeness criterion by upweighting the currently weakest positive item.

## Results

On the UM220K prospective brain MRI test set (52 diagnostic tasks), ItemizedCLIP achieves 90.5% mean AUC, outperforming the previous SOTA HLIP (83.7%) by +6.8% and Prima (75.7%) by +14.8%. On Pub-Brain-5, ItemizedCLIP reaches 83.6% 12-metric mean balanced accuracy versus 76.5% for HLIP (+7.1%). On HeadCT240K's prospective test set (83 tasks), ItemizedCLIP achieves 85.1% mean AUC versus 75.8% for HLIP (+9.3%), and on chest CT (CT-Rate, 16 tasks) it reaches 83.2% mean AUC versus 78.7% for HLIP-SA. On remote sensing (RSICD 30-way classification), ItemizedCLIP attains 46.2% Top-1 accuracy versus 41.8% for FLAIR, and on Flickr retrieval it achieves 19.2% T@1 versus 16.7% for FLAIR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ItemizedCLIP outperforms all 14 baselines across every evaluated domain. On brain MRI, it beats HLIP [40] by +6.8% mAUC (90.5 vs 83.7) on UM220K and +7.1% mean BAcc (83.6 vs 76.5) on Pub-Brain-5, and surpasses Prima [24] by +14.8% mAUC. On head CT, ItemizedCLIP's zero-shot performance (91.5% RSNA, 90.0% CQ500) exceeds the linear-probing results of three dedicated CT foundation models: FM-HeadCT [42] (91.3/80.0), Google-CT [34] (87.2/76.1), and Merlin [2] (73.6/55.1). On chest CT, it leads HLIP-SA [40] by +4.5% mAUC on CT-Rate (83.2 vs 78.7) and outperforms fVLM [31], CT-CLIP [14], BIUD [4], and Merlin [2] by 5–10% margins. On remote sensing and natural images, ItemizedCLIP beats FLAIR [32] (the closest multi-positive baseline) by +4.4% Top-1 on RSICD and +2.5% T@1 on Flickr, while also surpassing DreamLIP [41], Vanilla CLIP [27], and Vanilla SigLIP [38] by larger margins. Notably, ItemizedCLIP also outperforms BiomedCLIP [39] (83.6 vs 59.8) and ConceptCLIP [26] (83.6 vs 58.9) on Pub-Brain-5, demonstrating that itemized supervision yields substantially stronger medical representations than general-purpose or concept-enhanced CLIP variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

ItemizedCLIP builds directly on FLAIR's Text Conditioned SigLIP (TCS) and Multi-positive SigLIP (MPS) objectives, which were designed for multi-positive caption supervision where captions are semantically redundant. It also draws on the text-conditioned visual representation ideas from DreamLIP and Llip, which use cross-attention to localize subcaptions to visual tokens. The key departure is the formalization of itemized text supervision—where text items are semantically independent and refer to spatially disjoint regions—as a distinct training regime, and the introduction of tailored objectives (IIS, UWP, KTA, masked attention) that enforce item independence and representation completeness, properties that multi-positive objectives actively contradict.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a practically critical but underexplored supervision structure that naturally arises in medical imaging, remote sensing, and other non-object-centric domains, where missing even one finding (e.g., a tumor) can have serious consequences. The completeness and independence guarantees are especially valuable for trustworthy medical AI, and the item-level attention maps provide a direct interpretability mechanism absent in prior CLIP-style radiology models. Open questions include whether the approach scales to billion-parameter pretraining, how it interacts with initialization from powerful pretrained VLMs, and whether the itemized framing generalizes to longitudinal or temporal settings where findings evolve across studies.
