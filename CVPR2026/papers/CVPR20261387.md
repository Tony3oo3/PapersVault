## Problem

Existing LoRA merging methods either rely on gradient-free heuristics that collapse on dense prediction tasks (e.g., depth, normals) or use entropy-based surrogates that are undefined for regression and scale poorly with sequence length in LLMs and VLMs. There is no label-free, output-agnostic merging signal that generalizes uniformly across classification, regression, and sequence generation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes K independently fine-tuned LoRA adapters (each a pair of matrices B_k and A_k per layer), the frozen pretrained base weights, and a small unlabeled validation set per task. It outputs layer-wise merge coefficients and a single merged model. Crucially, for LLMs and VLMs the objective is evaluated only on input prompts (image tokens or text embeddings), so computational cost is independent of generated sequence length, unlike entropy-based methods that must score every output token.

## Method

NSC Merging exploits the observation that during LoRA fine-tuning the down-projection factor A in the low-rank update progressively compresses its null space, and the resulting null-space ratio (the fraction of input activation discarded by the A projection) is inversely correlated with task performance across both classification and regression. The method formulates a gradient-based objective that minimizes the mean null-space ratio over unlabeled samples across LoRA-equipped layers, using this ratio as a label-free surrogate loss to optimize per-task, per-layer merge coefficients. For efficiency, the null-space ratio is computed via a cached rank-by-rank Gram-inverse matrix rather than an explicit projection matrix, and the objective is restricted to the last quarter of transformer blocks. This makes NSC applicable to any task type without output logits, and its per-step cost matches single-token evaluation regardless of generation length.

## Results

On 20 heterogeneous vision tasks (NYUD-v2, PASCAL-Context, Taskonomy) with a ViT-B backbone, NSC achieves 92.0% average normalized performance, outperforming the next-best method RobustMerge (89.9%) and all gradient-free baselines (74.3–83.4%). On six NLI benchmarks with LLaMA-3-8B, NSC reaches 92.3% average normalized accuracy versus 91.2% for RobustMerge and 90.9% for Task Arithmetic. On six vision-language tasks with LLaVA-1.5-7B, NSC attains 82.7% average normalized score, matching AdaMerging (Full Token) at 82.4% while requiring only ~1/7th the optimization time since its cost is independent of generated sequence length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NSC outperforms all 11 baselines on the 20-task vision benchmark, with the largest margins over gradient-free methods: Task Arithmetic, TIES, and DARE-TIES collapse to 1–2% on NYUD-v2 semantic segmentation while NSC reaches 85.1%, and SVD/FR-Merging (83.0%/83.4% avg) still trail NSC by ~9 points. RobustMerge is the closest competitor at 89.9% average on vision and 91.2% on NLI, but NSC leads by 2.1 and 1.1 points respectively. On the six NLI benchmarks, NSC (92.3%) beats AdaMerging (89.6%) by 2.7 points, confirming that entropy-based surrogates are less effective for LLM merging than the structural null-space signal. On VLM tasks, NSC (82.7%) edges out AdaMerging Full Token (82.4%) while matching its Single Token variant (80.9%) in computational cost, since NSC's objective is input-oriented and does not scale with generated sequence length. LoRA-specific baselines (KnOTS, LoRA-LEGO, SVD, Linear) consistently underperform on dense-prediction tasks (depth, normals, segmentation) where they score below 21% on PASCAL-Context Parts, whereas NSC maintains 76.7% there.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-based model merging framework introduced by AdaMerging, which learns layer-wise merge coefficients by minimizing an output-entropy surrogate. The key departure is replacing this output-space signal with a purely structural, input-space signal: the null-space ratio of the LoRA down-projection factor A, which the authors show systematically decreases during fine-tuning and correlates with task performance. This positions the work at the intersection of LoRA-specific merging (KnOTS, LoRA-LEGO, SVD-based approaches) and adaptive coefficient optimization, but removes the dependency on task-specific output distributions that limits entropy-based methods to classification and makes them infeasible for long-sequence generation in LLMs and VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for the growing ecosystem of LoRA adapters on model hubs: NSC provides a label-free, task-agnostic merging signal that works uniformly across classification, regression, and sequence generation, removing the need for task-specific surrogate design. The observation that null-space compression in the down-projection matrix tracks performance is a clean structural property that could inform other PEFT-related analyses. Open questions include whether the null-space compression signal generalizes to other parameter-efficient methods (e.g., adapters, prefix tuning), whether it remains informative at very high LoRA ranks where the subspace covers a larger fraction of the feature space, and whether the approach extends to merging adapters trained with different base models or different PEFT configurations.
