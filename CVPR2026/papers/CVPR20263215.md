## Problem

While RLVR has proven effective for incentivizing reasoning in Large Multimodal Models, the underlying mechanism by which it modifies internal representations remains poorly understood. This paper identifies that RLVR unexpectedly shifts low-entropy input activations more than high-entropy ones, and that this shift is associated with degraded reasoning behavior. The key question is whether this insight can be exploited to improve reasoning at test time without expensive policy optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263215/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires both a base LMM and its paired RLVR post-trained LMM to be available at test time. Given a multimodal input (image/video frames plus text instruction), the base LMM is run once to extract low-entropy activation targets via logit lens projection. The RLVR LMM receives the same visual tokens augmented with per-input learned perturbations (zero-initialized vectors added element-wise to each visual token embedding), and the resulting modified context is used for standard autoregressive decoding (greedy for Pass@1, sampling for Pass@K).

## Method

Activation Replay is a training-free, per-input test-time optimization that introduces zero-initialized learnable tokens added to the visual token embeddings of the RLVR LMM. Using logit lens (normalization + vocabulary head projection), the method identifies low-entropy activations in the base LMM via a dynamic threshold (τ × max base entropy) and computes token-level KL divergence between base and RLVR projected distributions at those positions. The learnable tokens are then updated via a few gradient steps to minimize this masked KL divergence, effectively steering the RLVR LMM's low-entropy input activations back toward the base LMM's distributions while leaving high-entropy activations untouched. The optimized tokens are added to the visual input before any decoding, requiring no architectural changes or retraining of either model.

## Results

Activation Replay consistently improves RLVR post-trained LMMs across 8 math/knowledge benchmarks, with gains ranging from +0.2 to +3.9 points (e.g., MM-Eureka-7B: MathVerse 45.1→47.7, MMMU 58.7→62.0; VL-Rethinker-7B: LogicVista 46.1→49.7). On agentic reasoning (DeepEyes-7B), HRBench-4 improves by +2.6 and VisualProbe-Medium by +2.6. On video reasoning (Video-R1-7B), gains reach +4.0 on VideoMMMU and +4.4 on Video-Holmes. The method also boosts Pass@K, with up to +5.0 improvement at Pass@16, mitigating the narrower reasoning coverage of RLVR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Activation Replay is a training-free test-time method applied on top of existing RLVR models, so the primary comparison is each RLVR model with vs. without replay. Across all four RLVR models (MMR1-Math-7B, MM-Eureka-7B/32B, VL-Rethinker-7B), replay yields consistent gains of +0.2 to +3.9 points on math/knowledge benchmarks, with the largest improvements on LogicVista (+3.8 for VL-Rethinker) and MMMU (+3.9 for MM-Eureka-32B). On agentic reasoning, DeepEyes-7B improves by +2.6 on HRBench-4 and VisualProbe-Medium, outperforming the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (52.2/72.0) and approaching GPT-4o (48.0/–). On video reasoning, Video-R1-7B gains +4.0 on VideoMMMU and +4.4 on Video-Holmes, surpassing GPT-4o (61.2/42.0) on Video-Holmes. Against closed-source references, MM-Eureka-32B+replay (52.4 on MathVerse, 63.2 on MMMU) remains competitive with Claude-3.5 Sonnet (52.0, 51.5) and GPT-4o (40.6, 51.9), while the 7B models with replay close the gap to these larger systems. The one exception is DynaMath where MM-Eureka-32B slightly drops (62.1→61.8), but all other benchmarks show non-negative gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the logit lens interpretability framework (Nanda 2020; Neo et al. ICLR 2025) to analyze how RLVR post-training reshapes LMM input activations, extending its use from output-space diagnostics to input-context analysis. It complements the recent finding that high-entropy minority tokens drive RLVR reasoning gains [48] by revealing the previously underexplored role of low-entropy activations, which are shifted more dramatically by RLVR. Methodologically, it departs from the dominant 'train more' paradigm for multimodal reasoning by proposing a training-free, test-time intervention (Activation Replay) that manipulates visual tokens rather than updating model weights, positioning itself as a lightweight complement to RLVR post-training rather than a replacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic mechanistic account of how RLVR distorts LMM input activations, offering practitioners a diagnostic lens for understanding post-training side effects. Activation Replay is practically appealing as a zero-cost (no gradient updates, no architectural changes) inference-time boost, but its utility is constrained by the requirement to run both the base and RLVR model in parallel, and the typical gains of 1–4 points, while consistent, are modest relative to the added inference overhead. Open questions include whether the low-entropy regularization principle generalizes to non-RLVR post-training (e.g., SFT, DPO), whether it transfers to audio or 3D modalities, and whether the learnable-token formulation can be distilled into a fixed, model-agnostic perturbation.
