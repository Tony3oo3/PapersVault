## Problem

Current multimodal verification methods rely on passive holistic fusion, encoding entire images and texts into global vectors that are then blended via attention or concatenation. This causes 'feature dilution,' where subtle local semantic conflicts (e.g., a single word change like 'recovered' to 'failed') are averaged out by overwhelming global alignment, making sophisticated misinformation undetectable. The paper asks whether an active, bidirectional verification process—mimicking how humans cross-reference specific visual regions against specific textual claims—can explicitly pinpoint these fine-grained cross-modal inconsistencies.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (resized to 256×256 for DGM4) and a text caption (padded/truncated to 50 tokens for DGM4, 128 for MFND) as primary inputs, plus N mask-label pairs extracted from the image via either an Open Vocabulary Parser (OMG-LLaVA) or a Caption-Anchored Parser (GLIP + SAM2). Outputs are task-specific: a binary authenticity score, four manipulation-type labels (face swap, facial attribute, text swap, text attribute), a 4-element bounding box for image manipulation grounding, and per-token binary predictions for text manipulation grounding. The mask-label pairs serve as intermediate semantic anchors that bridge pixel regions to their textual descriptions, enabling local-level reasoning rather than global averaging.

## Method

MaLSF replaces passive fusion with a three-stage pipeline: (1) dual-path semantic extraction using Swin-B for multi-scale visual features (global [CLS] + mask-pooled local features) and a shared 6-layer BERT for caption and label text; (2) Bidirectional Cross-modal Verification (BCV), where two parallel transformer encoder streams perform gated cross-attention—Text-as-Query (caption queries image regions) and Image-as-Query (image queries text labels)—with sigmoid-gated relevance weights to automatically select informative local semantics; (3) Hierarchical Semantic Aggregation (HSA), which first decouples [CLS] and sequence tokens via cross-attention shallow fusion, then uses learnable query tokens to extract task-specific features (binary classification, manipulation type, image grounding, text grounding) in a deep fusion stage. The architecture is pre-trained with X-VLM weights and trained end-to-end with a weighted multi-task loss combining cross-entropy for classification/grounding and L1+GIoU for bounding box regression.

## Results

On the DGM4 benchmark, MaLSF achieves 95.56% AUC, 89.33% accuracy, 83.84% CF1, 82.47% IoUmean (image grounding), and 76.88% F1 (text grounding), yielding an average improvement of +4.87% over the strongest prior method (EMSF, +3.33%). On multimodal fake news detection, MaLSF reaches 95.5% accuracy on Weibo21 and 93.5% on Weibo17, surpassing the previous best (DAMMFND at 94.7% and MIMoE-FND at 92.8%, respectively). The Open Vocabulary Parser variant (MaLSF◇) marginally outperforms the Caption-Anchored variant on text grounding F1 (77.19% vs 76.88%) while the Caption-Anchored variant leads on AUC (95.56% vs 95.60% is actually the OV parser). MaLSF also has the fewest trainable parameters (319M) among compared methods while running at 22.9 FPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DGM4, MaLSF outperforms all eight baselines on 11 of 12 metrics. The closest competitor, EMSF [33], leads only on mAP (91.42% vs 90.76%), while MaLSF surpasses it by +0.45 AUC, +0.58 ACC, +1.64 IoUmean, and +3.44 text-grounding F1. Against HAMMER++ [27] and ASAP [44], the gains are larger: +2.23 AUC and +2.67 ACC over HAMMER++, and +1.18 AUC and +1.62 ACC over ASAP. On MFND, MaLSF★ achieves 95.5% accuracy on Weibo21, beating DAMMFND [19] (94.7%), FND-CLIP [45] (94.3%), and BMR [39] (92.9%); on Weibo17 it reaches 93.5%, exceeding MIMoE-FND [16] (92.8%), COOLANT [34] (92.3%), and BMR (91.8%). The two parser variants show complementary strengths: the Open Vocabulary Parser (MaLSF◇) yields slightly better text-grounding F1 (77.19% vs 76.88%) and AUC (95.60% vs 95.56%), while the Caption-Anchored Parser (MaLSF★) is marginally stronger on Weibo21 accuracy (95.5% vs 95.1%), suggesting the open-domain labels better capture diverse manipulation patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'passive holistic fusion' paradigm in multimodal verification (used by CAFE, COOLANT, MIMoE-FND for fake news detection, and HAMMER, UFAFormer, EMSF for manipulation grounding), where global image and text vectors are blended via attention or concatenation. Instead, it repurposes mask-label pairs—previously used in open-vocabulary segmentation (TransVG, Mask-CLIP, SAM2, GLIP, OMG-LLaVA)—as fine-grained semantic anchors that enable a bidirectional, query-driven verification mechanism. The architectural building blocks (Swin-B, BERT, cross-attention transformers) are standard, but their organization into parallel Text-as-Query and Image-as-Query streams with hierarchical aggregation is novel. The paper does not directly extend a single prior method but synthesizes ideas from open-vocabulary segmentation, cross-modal attention, and human cognitive verification into a unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'feature dilution' problem—where a single-word semantic conflict is averaged out by global alignment—is a real and under-addressed limitation in multimodal verification, and the mask-label pair approach provides a concrete, interpretable solution. The framework's dual applicability to both the multi-output DGM4 task (classification + grounding) and the binary MFND task suggests a generalizable verification architecture. However, reliance on external parsers (SAM2, GLIP, or OMG-LLaVA) for mask-label extraction adds pipeline complexity and potential error propagation, and the paper does not explore self-supervised or end-to-end mask-label generation. Open questions include extension to video-text verification, scalability to longer texts, and whether the bidirectional verification paradigm generalizes beyond the two tasks tested.
