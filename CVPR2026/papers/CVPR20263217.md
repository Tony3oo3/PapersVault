## Problem

Producing high-quality stereo video for XR devices requires expensive dual-camera rigs, and existing algorithmic approaches—whether 3D-reconstruction-based novel view synthesis or depth-warping-inpainting pipelines—suffer from geometric instability, temporal inconsistency, and broken pixel-level correspondence. Additionally, publicly available stereo datasets use baselines far exceeding human interpupillary distance (IPD), making them unsuitable for comfortable stereoscopic viewing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263217/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular left-view video as the sole input and directly generates the corresponding right-view video, producing a complete stereo pair. Training operates at 480p resolution with 81-frame clips (≈3 s at 24 FPS), while inference extends to higher resolutions and longer durations via spatial and temporal tiling. The stereo pairs are aligned to natural human IPD (55–75 mm baseline), unlike prior datasets that use baselines exceeding 10 cm.

## Method

StereoWorld adapts a pretrained DiT-based video diffusion model (Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B) via frame-dimension concatenation of left and right view latents, requiring no architectural modification—the existing 3D spatiotemporal self-attention naturally fuses cross-view information. A geometry-aware regularization combines two complementary signals: (1) disparity supervision, where a lightweight differentiable stereo projector estimates disparity between the input left view and generated right view, supervised by ground-truth disparity maps via a log-domain global loss plus pixel-wise L1 loss; and (2) depth supervision, where the last few DiT blocks are duplicated into dual branches that jointly learn velocity fields for both RGB and depth distributions, providing complete per-pixel geometric guidance in non-overlapping regions. The model is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 128) on the newly curated StereoWorld-11M dataset (11M IPD-aligned Blu-ray SBS frames), and spatio-temporal tiling enables efficient high-resolution, long-duration synthesis at inference.

## Results

On the StereoWorld-11M test set (1,000 clips), StereoWorld achieves PSNR 25.98, SSIM 0.796, LPIPS 0.095, IQ-Score 0.502, and TF-Score 0.970, outperforming all three baselines on every metric. On geometry accuracy, it reduces EPE to 17.45 (vs. 24.78 for StereoCrafter, the closest competitor) and D1-all to 0.421 (vs. 0.527), indicating substantially more accurate stereo correspondence. In human evaluation, StereoWorld scores 4.8–4.9/5 across Stereo Effect, Visual Quality, Binocular Consistency, and Temporal Consistency, versus 3.6–4.3 for the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StereoWorld outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest competitor, StereoCrafter [63] (a training-based depth-warping-inpainting pipeline), StereoWorld gains +2.94 dB PSNR, +0.14 SSIM, and reduces EPE by 7.33 pixels and D1-all by 0.106, reflecting markedly better geometric correspondence. The training-free SVG [11] baseline achieves the highest TF-Score (0.971 vs. 0.970, essentially tied) but lags significantly on reconstruction fidelity (PSNR 18.03 vs. 25.98) and geometry (EPE 33.25 vs. 17.45), consistent with its inability to properly inpaint occluded regions. The image-based GenStereo [37] performs worst overall (PSNR 19.45, EPE 35.00), confirming that frame-level methods fail to generalize to video due to temporal instability. In human evaluation, StereoWorld leads by 0.5–1.2 points on all four subjective dimensions (SE, VQ, BC, TC), with the largest gap on Binocular Consistency (4.9 vs. 4.1 for StereoCrafter).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant depth-warping-inpainting paradigm (SVG, StereoCrafter, StereoDiffusion) that decomposes stereo generation into separate depth estimation, warping, and inpainting stages, arguing that this decoupling breaks pixel-level correspondence and causes temporal inconsistency. Instead, it repurposes a pretrained video diffusion transformer (Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B) via simple frame-dimension latent concatenation for monocular conditioning (inspired by ReCamMaster) and introduces a geometry-aware regularization combining disparity and depth supervision. The approach also builds on the Rectified Flow / Conditional Flow Matching training framework and leverages LoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, while the StereoWorld-11M dataset addresses a practical gap: existing stereo datasets use baselines far exceeding human IPD, making them unsuitable for XR viewing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IPD-aligned StereoWorld-11M dataset (11M frames) is a practically important resource for the XR and stereoscopic content community, filling a gap where prior datasets were either not IPD-aligned or not publicly available. The end-to-end formulation eliminates the error accumulation inherent in multi-stage pipelines, and the ablation confirms that both disparity and depth supervision are necessary for geometric fidelity. However, the ~6-minute-per-clip inference speed and the lack of explicit stereo-baseline control limit immediate deployment, and the evaluation is constrained to a single self-curated dataset with only three baselines, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to diverse content types and resolutions beyond the tested setting.
