## Problem

State-of-the-art autoregressive video generation models (e.g., LongLive, Self-Forcing++) that use attention sink frames for temporal stability suffer from a failure mode called sink-collapse, where generated content abruptly reverts to the initial sink frames, producing cyclic motion and scene resets. The paper identifies the root cause as an inherent conflict between the periodic structure of Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE) and multi-head attention: at long horizons, phase re-alignment across all RoPE dimensions causes multiple attention heads to simultaneously over-weight sink frames, collapsing generation diversity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces real-time streaming video at 20 fps on a single NVIDIA H100, with demonstrated generation up to 12 hours in length. It operates on top of existing 1.3B-parameter autoregressive diffusion models (LongLive, Self-Forcing++) that use a local attention window of 12 latent frames and 3 sink frames, with a 3D causal VAE decoder enabling sliding-window decoding to keep memory bounded. All frame indices in the paper refer to latent frames, where latent index 132 corresponds to roughly 528 video frames (~33 seconds at 16 fps).

## Method

LoL is a training-free inference-time modification that introduces multi-head RoPE jitter: for each attention head h, the RoPE base frequency is perturbed as θ̂_h = θ_0(1 + σ_θ ε_h) where ε_h ~ U[-1,1] and σ_θ = 0.8, so each head uses a slightly different frequency schedule. This breaks the inter-head phase synchronization that causes all heads to simultaneously assign high attention weights to sink frames, thereby preventing the collective degeneracy that triggers sink-collapse. The paper shows through ablation that the collapse is not attributable to any single RoPE dimension (contradicting RIFLEx's single-frequency fix) but arises from the summed phase concentration across all dimensions, and that jittering all heads is necessary for full mitigation. Combined with dynamically sampled streaming RoPE positions and noise, plus the 3D causal VAE's sliding-window decoding, the approach extends generation from ~4 minutes to effectively infinite length with minimal quality degradation.

## Results

On LongLive (100s videos), LoL reduces Sink-Collapse Max from 73.06 (PE baseline) to 16.67 and Sink-Collapse Avg from 30.54 to 3.93, while maintaining Dynamic Degree at 35.27 (vs. 34.62 for PE). On Self-Forcing++, Sink-Collapse Max drops from 68.07 to 22.70 and Avg from 34.11 to 6.12, with Dynamic Degree preserved at 81.20 (vs. 83.32 for PE). In Table 2, Self-Forcing++(LoL) achieves the highest Dynamic Degree (81.20 at 100s) and Temporal Quality (92.91) among all ultra-long autoregressive models, while LongLive(LoL) matches or exceeds its unmodified counterpart on all VBench quality metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against naive position extrapolation (PE), LoL reduces Sink-Collapse Max by 77% on LongLive (73.06→16.67) and 67% on Self-Forcing++ (68.07→22.70) while preserving Dynamic Degree within 1–2 points. Compared to Position Interpolation (PI), which nearly eliminates sink-collapse (4.97) but catastrophically kills motion (Dynamic Degree 0.35 on LongLive, 1.95 on SF++), LoL trades a modest increase in collapse score for a 95× improvement in motion dynamics. Against NTK, LoL achieves substantially lower sink-collapse (16.67 vs. 41.11 on LongLive) and higher Dynamic Degree (35.27 vs. 28.72). Against YARN, LoL has slightly higher Sink-Collapse Max (16.67 vs. 11.17) but dramatically better Dynamic Degree (35.27 vs. 2.67 on LongLive; 81.20 vs. 36.71 on SF++). RIFLEx, designed for bidirectional models, fails to address autoregressive sink-collapse (70.95 on LongLive, nearly identical to PE), whereas LoL reduces it to 16.67 while matching RIFLEx's Dynamic Degree (35.27 vs. 35.11). In Table 2, Self-Forcing++(LoL) achieves the highest Dynamic Degree (81.20) and Temporal Quality (92.91) among all ultra-long autoregressive models at 100s, outperforming unmodified Self-Forcing++ (54.12, 90.87) and LongLive (34.62, 88.56).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the autoregressive video generation paradigm established by LongLive and Self-Forcing++, which extend bidirectional diffusion models to multi-minute generation via distribution matching distillation and attention sinks (originating from StreamingLLM). It departs from RIFLEx, which diagnosed repetition in bidirectional models as a single-dimension RoPE frequency issue, by showing that in autoregressive settings the failure mode (sink-collapse) arises from collective multi-head phase synchronization across all temporal dimensions simultaneously. The proposed multi-head RoPE jitter is a training-free modification to the RoPE mechanism itself, distinct from prior position-embedding extension methods (PI, NTK, YaRN) that rescale or interpolate frequencies globally rather than per-head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration of real-time, streaming, infinite-length video generation with minimal quality decay, pushing the practical limit from ~4 minutes to 12 hours using only 1.3B-parameter models and KV cache. The training-free nature of the fix means it can be immediately applied to existing autoregressive video models without retraining. Key open questions remain: long-term memory over multi-hour horizons is still challenging, the approach inherits quality limits from the underlying local-attention models, and whether alternative positional embedding schemes or sparse/linear attention could further improve scalability are left for future work.
