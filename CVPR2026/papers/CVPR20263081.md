## Problem

Feed-forward view synthesis models condition on Plücker ray maps, which encode camera geometry in absolute world coordinates and are highly sensitive to coordinate-gauge changes and simple camera transformations (zoom, roll, aspect-ratio changes). This brittleness causes inputs to stray from the training distribution, degrading 3D consistency and producing artifacts under even minor pose adjustments. The paper asks what input representation best conditions a model for robust, gauge-invariant view synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263081/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes context RGB images, per-pixel depth maps extracted by off-the-shelf perception models (e.g., MapAnything), and the target camera intrinsics/extrinsics. Rather than feeding raw camera parameters into the network, it deterministically unprojects context pixels into a unified 3D point cloud and rasterizes it into the target view, producing a point cloud projection image with holes in disoccluded regions. The output is a single novel-view RGB image at the target camera's resolution, generated in one forward pass by a decoder-only ViT.

## Method

PVSM replaces Plücker ray conditioning with a target-view point cloud projection image, reframing novel view synthesis as an image-to-image translation problem where the network completes disoccluded regions and synthesizes view-dependent effects. The architecture is a decoder-only ViT (12 or 24 layers, patch size 8, d_model 768) that processes concatenated tokens from context images, the projection image, and DINOv3 features, with RoPE applied to disambiguate empty patches. Training is two-stage: a self-supervised MAE-style pretraining on DL3DV where the model reconstructs a randomly masked target image from context views, followed by supervised fine-tuning on RealEstate10K with the projective cue. A quotient-space analysis shows the projective operator q = Rast ∘ UnProj is invariant to global SE(3) transformations by construction, so the network never needs to learn gauge invariance from data. The model achieves state-of-the-art PSNR on RealEstate10K and substantially outperforms ray-conditioned baselines on a new out-of-distribution consistency benchmark involving world-scale, FOV, anisotropic-pixel, and roll perturbations.

## Results

On RealEstate10K (NoPoSplat splits), the 24-layer PVSM achieves 26.90 PSNR / 0.851 SSIM / 0.133 LPIPS overall, surpassing the 24-layer LVSM baseline (25.74 / 0.830 / 0.150) by +1.16 dB PSNR. The advantage grows as viewpoint overlap decreases: at small overlap, PVSM reaches 24.98 PSNR vs. 22.71 for LVSM-24 (+2.27 dB). On the authors' new consistency benchmark, PVSM scores 25.43 PSNR under world-scale transformation versus only 14.56 for LVSM, demonstrating far greater robustness to coordinate-gauge changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PVSM outperforms all eight baselines on RealEstate10K across every overlap setting. Against the closest competitor, 24-layer LVSM, PVSM-24 gains +1.16 dB PSNR overall and +2.27 dB at small overlap, with the gap widening as context-target overlap shrinks, indicating stronger disocclusion handling. The 12-layer PVSM already surpasses 12-layer LVSM by +1.04 dB overall and +2.06 dB at small overlap, showing the projective conditioning benefit is architecture-independent. Among feed-forward 3D Gaussian methods, PVSM-24 leads NoPoSplat by +3.12 dB, MVSplat by +2.78 dB, and PixelSplat by +3.14 dB PSNR; AnySplat and WorldMirror trail by even larger margins (9.6 dB and 5.4 dB respectively). RayZer, the self-supervised ray-conditioned model, lags PVSM-24 by 4.05 dB PSNR, partly due to its latent-camera formulation struggling with precise viewpoint control. On the consistency benchmark, PVSM dominates under world-scale (25.43 vs. 14.56 PSNR for LVSM) and anisotropic-pixel transformations, while LVSM+augmentation shows only marginal recovery, confirming that Plücker-space distribution shift is not easily corrected by short fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LVSM's decoder-only ViT architecture for feed-forward view synthesis, but replaces the Plücker ray conditioning that LVSM and its successors (RayZer, Less3D) rely on with a target-view point-cloud projection image. Conceptually, it revives the projective/reprojection cue used in earlier systems like LookinGood and Infinite Nature, but reframes it within a deterministic single-pass ViT pipeline and provides a quotient-space invariance argument (Sec. 4.1) that the ray-based approach lacks. The MAE-style pretraining stage draws on CroCo and standard masked image modeling, adapted to exploit the 2D nature of the projection cue, while DINOv3 features and RoPE are borrowed from the broader ViT literature to improve structural consistency and handle empty patches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that Plücker ray conditioning—a representation now standard across the LVSM family—is fundamentally gauge-dependent and brittle under even simple camera transformations (world scaling, FOV change, roll), with the World Scale gap reaching ~11 dB. This challenges a working assumption in the feed-forward view synthesis community and suggests that the field's default camera encoding may be suboptimal. Remaining limitations include dependence on the quality of off-the-shelf depth estimation (MapAnything), sensitivity to dynamic scenes, and evaluation primarily on indoor RealEstate10K sequences. Open questions include whether projective conditioning scales to outdoor or large-scale scenes where depth errors compound, and whether the MAE pretraining strategy transfers to other 2D-cue-based synthesis formulations.
