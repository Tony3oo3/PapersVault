## Problem

Existing crack segmentation models based on CNNs, Transformers, or Mamba each capture only a subset of the spatial and structural information needed for thin, irregular crack patterns, and prior hybrid architectures (e.g., MambaVision, RestorMixer) simply stack these components in parallel or sequential fashion without exploiting their inherent interaction logic. The paper identifies that Mamba's selective state-space mechanism implicitly encodes an attention-like weighting over channels, yet no prior work has leveraged this hidden attention to naturally partition processing into complementary global and local pathways within a single encoder.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB crack image resized to 512×512 pixels and produces a binary pixel-level segmentation mask of the same spatial dimensions. All four benchmark datasets (DeepCrack, Crack500, CamCrack789, CrackMap) are split 7:1:2 into train/validation/test, and the model is trained with batch size 1 on a single A100 GPU, consuming only 1190 MiB of training memory and 2.05 GFLOPs at inference.

## Method

The core contribution is TransMixer, which runs a standard Mamba SSM and then sorts the per-channel step-size Δ_t to split output channels into a global subset (top γ·d channels) and a local subset; the global tokens are enhanced with self-attention to model long-range dependencies while the local tokens pass through a lightweight Local Refinement module (1×1 conv + max-pool gating) to sharpen fine textures. A Direction-guided Edge Gated Convolution (DEGConv) partitions each feature map into non-overlapping spatial blocks, computes Sobel-based orientation histograms per cell to build a direction embedding vector, and uses this embedding to gate horizontal/vertical strip convolutions, enhancing edge sensitivity under irregular crack geometries at negligible cost. A Spatial Refinement Multi-Level Fusion (SRF) decoder generates a spatial attention map from the highest-resolution feature and uses it to weight each upsampled lower-level feature before channel concatenation and a 1×1 conv + MLP head. Training uses a 1:5 BCE-to-Dice mixed loss with AdamW (lr 5e-4) for 50 epochs, and the full model contains only 2.54 M parameters.

## Results

MixerCSeg achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four crack segmentation benchmarks. On DeepCrack, it reaches 91.51% mIoU and 92.05% F1, outperforming the second-best SCSegamba by 1.43% mIoU and 1.04% F1. On CamCrack789, CrackMap, and Crack500, it similarly leads all baselines (e.g., 84.09% mIoU on CamCrack789, 81.23% mIoU on CrackMap, 78.24% mIoU on Crack500). The model achieves this with only 2.05 GFLOPs and 2.54M parameters, an 88.7% reduction in FLOPs over SCSegamba.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MixerCSeg outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor SCSegamba (a Mamba-based crack segmentation model), it gains +1.43% mIoU and +1.04% F1 on DeepCrack, and +1.41% mIoU on CamCrack789. Compared to the hybrid MambaVision backbone, MixerCSeg improves by +1.78% mIoU and +4.61% F1 on DeepCrack, and by +2.63% mIoU on Crack500 where MambaVision drops to 46.03% F1. Against the CNN-based U-Net, gains are substantial: +1.64% mIoU on DeepCrack and +7.19% mIoU on Crack500. The Transformer-based DTrCNet trails by +4.90% mIoU on DeepCrack. In the directional-embedding ablation (Table 6), MixerCSeg's Sobel-based direction prior outperforms ARConv's predicted-angle approach by +0.45% mIoU on DeepCrack, confirming the advantage of geometry-driven over learned angle supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mamba's selective state space model, but departs from prior hybrid architectures (MambaVision, RestorMixer, VAMBA) that simply stack Mamba, Transformer, and CNN blocks in parallel or sequential fashion. The key conceptual departure is the analysis of Mamba's 'hidden attention' via the Δ_t parameter, which motivates decoupling the channel dimension into global tokens (routed to self-attention) and local tokens (routed to convolutional refinement) within a single TransMixer block. This positions the work as a principled re-interpretation of Mamba's internal computation rather than a modular composition of separate architectures, and the DEGConv module extends directional-prior-based edge modeling from rotated object detection (ARConv) into a segmentation context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.05 GFLOPs / 2.54 M parameter footprint while achieving SOTA crack segmentation is a notable efficiency result, reducing FLOPs by ~89% relative to the closest lightweight competitor (SCSegamba) while improving accuracy. This suggests that the decoupling of Mamba's latent attention into specialized global/local pathways may be a general recipe for efficient hybrid encoders beyond crack segmentation. However, the accuracy gains over strong baselines are modest (1–2% mIoU), and the method has only been validated on four relatively small crack datasets; whether the TransMixer decoupling strategy and DEGConv directional embeddings transfer to other fine-structure segmentation tasks (e.g., wire, hair, or road marking segmentation) remains an open question.
