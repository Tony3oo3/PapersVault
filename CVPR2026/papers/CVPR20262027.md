## Problem

Existing 4D object representations fail to simultaneously satisfy three desiderata: descriptiveness (capturing both geometry and appearance), compressiveness (compact latent form), and accessibility (requiring only readily available inputs). Many methods are task-specific, omit appearance modeling, or depend on hard-to-obtain temporal correspondences, limiting their utility as a general-purpose 4D foundation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262027/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The encoder consumes an unstructured spatiotemporal color point cloud (3D positions, RGB colors, and timestamps per point) with no correspondence or mesh structure required, and compresses it into 8192×32 dynamic tokens. The two decoders then expand these tokens into a 4D surface point cloud (via flow-matching integration) and per-timestep 3D Gaussians at sparsely occupied voxels. Downstream, the same tokens accept monocular video (24 frames), a single image, or RGBD video plus query points as conditioning, producing generated 4D objects, 3D trajectories, or simulated cloth dynamics.

## Method

Velox uses a Perceiver IO encoder with windowed self-attention to process local 4D spatiotemporal patches (each query cross-attends only to its nearest neighbors in space-time), achieving >30× compression without correspondences. Training is jointly supervised by two complementary decoders: a flow-matching 4D surface decoder that models the conditional distribution p(x|τ, s) to recover precise geometry at any target time, and a 3D Gaussian decoder (also Perceiver IO) that maps tokens to Gaussians at sparsely occupied voxels for appearance. A KL-regularization term keeps tokens near a standard normal, and texture augmentation via on-the-fly UV retexturing (rendered at >500 fps with nvdiffrast) addresses the low appearance diversity of dynamic object datasets. For downstream generation tasks, a DiT conditioned on DINOv2 features directly generates dynamic tokens from video or image inputs, while 3D tracking uses a lightweight Perceiver IO that cross-attends query points to the tokens.

## Results

On the reconstruction benchmark (256 held-out Objaverse scenes), Velox with GT voxels achieves PSNR 35.39, SSIM 0.984, LPIPS 0.021, FVD 48.99, and Chamfer 36.36, outperforming GVF (PSNR 26.45, FVD 173.62) by ~9 dB in PSNR and 3.5× in FVD, and per-frame LiTo (PSNR 32.55, FVD 126.38) by ~3 dB in PSNR and 2.6× in FVD. For 3D tracking on dynamic Objaverse, Velox achieves L2 distance of 0.025 (all points) and APD of 0.835, surpassing CoTracker3+GT depth (L2 0.060, APD 0.755) and SpatialTrackerV2 (L2 0.068, APD 0.600). In video-to-4D generation on Objaverse, Velox attains PSNR 24.04 / LPIPS 0.056 on the input view and PSNR 20.62 / LPIPS 0.104 on novel views, beating L4GM (22.59 / 0.074 input; 18.30 / 0.146 novel) and GVF (18.21 / 0.117 input; 16.41 / 0.157 novel).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GVF [119], which models 4D deformations via a Gaussian variation field, Velox wins decisively on reconstruction (PSNR 35.39 vs. 26.45, FVD 48.99 vs. 173.62) and video-to-4D generation (PSNR 24.04 vs. 18.21 on input view; 20.62 vs. 16.41 on novel views), with the gap attributed to GVF's inability to handle appearing/disappearing geometry. Against LiTo [11] (per-frame 3D tokenization), Velox improves PSNR by ~3 dB and FVD by 2.6×, demonstrating that joint spatiotemporal encoding reduces temporal flickering and yields more faithful appearance. In 3D tracking, Velox outperforms both CoTracker3+GT depth (L2 0.025 vs. 0.060; APD 0.835 vs. 0.755) and SpatialTrackerV2 (L2 0.025 vs. 0.068; APD 0.835 vs. 0.600), with a notable advantage on textureless regions due to joint geometry-appearance modeling; however, Velox's occlusion accuracy (OA 0.871) is slightly lower than the baselines (0.889–0.897) because it does not directly predict visibility. In video-to-4D, Velox surpasses L4GM [73] on novel-view generation (PSNR 20.62 vs. 18.30, LPIPS 0.104 vs. 0.146) while L4GM retains a small FVD edge on the input view (184 vs. 245) due to its pixel-aligned Gaussian formulation and test-time alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

Velox builds on the Perceiver IO architecture for its encoder and on latent flow matching (Chang et al., 2024) for its 4D surface decoder, extending both to the spatiotemporal domain. It departs from prior 4D representation work (GVF, CASPR, Motion2VecSets) by eliminating the need for temporal correspondences as encoder input, and from per-time-step 3D tokenizers (LiTo) by jointly encoding geometry and appearance across time in a single compact latent. The combination of a flow-matching geometry decoder with a 3D Gaussian appearance decoder under a shared Perceiver-style encoder is the paper's core architectural contribution, unifying two previously separate supervision signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Velox provides a single 4D latent that transfers across generation, tracking, and physics simulation, suggesting a path toward general-purpose 4D foundation models. The finding that texture augmentation substantially improves geometry reconstruction (not just appearance) is a useful practical insight for 4D dataset curation. Key limitations remain: residual temporal flickering from per-frame decoding, texture quality degradation in generation, and high training compute due to the absence of large-scale 4D pretraining. Open questions include whether memory-efficient joint multi-timestamp decoding can eliminate flickering, and whether the representation scales to full scenes rather than single objects.
