## Problem

Existing 4D Gaussian Splatting methods face a fundamental tradeoff when modeling long-range dynamic videos: all-at-once training causes GPU memory explosion and limited representational capacity for disoccluded regions, while chunk-based training introduces temporal flickering at boundaries and cannot reconstruct newly revealed content. No prior approach simultaneously achieves bounded memory, global temporal consistency, disocclusion handling, and practical random temporal access for real-world multi-minute sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261339/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single sparse point cloud (from SfM) plus multi-view video frames with known camera poses, and produces a rendered novel-view image at any requested time index t. A key operational detail is that rendering uses on-demand loading: given time t, only the two adjacent Key-frame Anchors (determined by GOP spacing) and their deformation fields are loaded into GPU memory, keeping peak rendering memory around 126 MB regardless of total sequence length (tested up to 3500 frames).

## Method

MoRel introduces Anchor Relay-based Bidirectional Blending (ARBB), a four-stage training pipeline: (1) a Global Canonical Anchor (GCA) is trained on the full sequence for global consistency; (2) periodically placed Key-frame Anchors (KfAs) are initialized from the GCA and refined locally with Feature-variance-guided Hierarchical Densification (FHD), which assigns each anchor-point a frequency-complexity level via feature variance and modulates gradient-based densification weights over training progress; (3) Progressive Windowed Deformation (PWD) trains each KfA's bidirectional deformation field within a local sliding window to prevent inter-chunk interference (backward contamination); (4) Intermediate Frame Blending (IFB) learns per-anchor-point temporal opacity offsets and decay speeds to smoothly fuse adjacent KfAs, eliminating boundary flicker. The entire pipeline requires no external optical flow or motion cues, and the periodic KfA placement provides natural random-access points for streaming applications.

## Results

On the newly composed SelfCap_LR dataset (5 sequences, 3500+ frames), MoRel achieves the best average PSNR of 21.00 dB, SSIM of 0.664, and LPIPS of 0.355, outperforming all-at-once methods (4DGS: 18.95 dB, MoDec-GS: 19.61 dB, LocalDyGS: 20.64 dB) and chunk-based methods (GIFStream: 19.02 dB, 4DGS_chunk: 19.31 dB). MoRel also attains the lowest temporal-of-flow (tOF) score of 0.203, indicating superior temporal consistency, while using only ~6,000 MB training memory compared to 12,000–22,000 MB for all-at-once baselines. Rendering memory is 126 MB, competitive with LocalDyGS (122 MB) and well below all-at-once methods (143–154 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoRel outperforms all four baseline categories on both reconstruction quality and temporal consistency. Against all-at-once methods, MoRel gains +2.03 dB PSNR over 4DGS (21.00 vs. 18.95), +1.39 dB over MoDec-GS (21.00 vs. 19.61), and +0.36 dB over LocalDyGS (21.00 vs. 20.64), while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.355 vs. 0.371–0.402). Against chunk-based methods, MoRel improves PSNR by +1.98 dB over GIFStream and +1.69 dB over 4DGS_chunk, and dramatically reduces tOF from 0.539/0.680 to 0.203, confirming that bidirectional blending eliminates the boundary flickering inherent to independent chunk training. On memory, MoRel uses ~6,000 MB training memory—roughly 3× less than 4DGS (~18,000 MB) and 3.7× less than MoDec-GS (~22,000 MB)—while its 126 MB rendering memory is only slightly above LocalDyGS (122 MB) and well below all-at-once methods (143–154 MB). The only trade-off is that MoRel's rendering memory (126 MB) is higher than the most memory-efficient chunk-based baselines (4DGS_chunk: 65 MB, GIFStream: 93 MB), reflecting the cost of maintaining two KfAs simultaneously for blending.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoRel builds on the anchor-point-based representation of Scaffold-GS and the 4D Gaussian Splatting framework, but departs from both all-at-once deformation methods (DeformGS, MoDec-GS, LocalDyGS) and chunk-based streaming approaches (GIFStream, 3DGSStream) by introducing a hybrid Anchor Relay mechanism that periodically places Key-frame Anchors and learns bidirectional deformations between them. The bidirectional blending with learnable per-anchor temporal opacity extends the fixed exponential decay used in prior 4DGS works, while the Feature-variance-guided Hierarchical Densification is a novel scheduling strategy that modulates anchor growth by local frequency complexity rather than applying uniform gradient-based densification. The periodic KfA placement draws conceptual inspiration from video coding GOP structures (HEVC/VVC), repurposing a compression-domain idea for 4D scene representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoRel addresses a practical gap in 4DGS: modeling real-world dynamic videos lasting minutes with bounded GPU memory, temporal random access, and flicker-free rendering simultaneously—properties that existing methods cannot all satisfy. The newly introduced SelfCap_LR dataset, with larger motion magnitudes and wider spatial coverage than prior benchmarks, provides a more realistic evaluation ground for long-range 4D motion. Open questions include whether the GOP-based anchor spacing generalizes to highly non-periodic or stochastic motion, how the method scales to multi-camera or unbounded scene extents, and whether the four-stage training pipeline can be further compressed for near-real-time streaming deployment.
