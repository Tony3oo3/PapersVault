## Problem

Existing safety methods for text-to-image diffusion models rely on expensive fine-tuning or attention modulation designed for U-Net cross-attention, making them structurally incompatible with modern MMDiT architectures like FLUX.1 that use unified multi-modal self-attention. Moreover, these approaches depend on predefined labels and fail to capture implicit risks arising from complex multi-token compositions (jailbreak prompts), while parameter-modifying methods incur prohibitive costs on 10B+ parameter models and degrade general generation quality.

## Input / Output

SafeRoPE operates on FLUX.1 (a rectified-flow MMDiT) where a text prompt is encoded by T5 into token embeddings that share a zero positional ID, while image patches carry spatially structured RoPE position IDs. At inference, the method intercepts query and key vectors within identified safety-critical attention heads, applies LRS-modulated orthogonal rotations to their unsafe subspace components, and passes the modified vectors through the standard attention pipeline to produce a generated image. The rotation matrices are precomputed and frozen at inference, adding negligible overhead to the denoising loop.

## Method

SafeRoPE identifies safety-critical attention heads via a Head Discrimination Score (HDS) that measures how well each head's SVD-derived unsafe subspace separates unsafe from safe token projections. For each such head, a low-rank unsafe subspace U_{r,b,h} is constructed via SVD of collected unsafe query/key vectors, and a Latent Risk Score (LRS) is computed as the normalized projection energy of any input vector onto this subspace. The core intervention replaces RoPE's discrete position-ID rotation with a continuous, risk-aware orthogonal rotation: a trainable skew-symmetric matrix A_{b,h} (r×r, where r ≪ d) is exponentiated and applied only to the unsafe component of each vector, scaled by its LRS so that safe tokens (LRS→0) pass through unchanged while unsafe tokens (LRS→1) receive maximal rotation. Training uses a bi-level objective that maximizes velocity deviation on unsafe prompts (Unsafe-1K) while minimizing it on safe MS-COCO pairs, jointly optimizing all A_{b,h} across safety-critical heads with the base FLUX.1 parameters frozen.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev, SafeRoPE achieves the lowest Unsafe Rate (UR) across all four erasure concepts: Nude (15.4% on Unsafe-1K, 7.0% on I2P), Bloody (15.5%), VanGogh (19.2%), and Pikachu (13.3%), compared to the original model's rates of 38.8%, 10.3%, 68.1%, 76.7%, and 62.4% respectively. FID improves from 76.8 to 68.9 on Nude, indicating better generation quality post-erasure. Cross-model transfer of rotation matrices from FLUX.1-dev to FLUX.1-sch reduces Nude UR from 6.9% to 5.1% without retraining, demonstrating strong architectural generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SafeRoPE outperforms all five baselines (ESD, SLD, UCE, DES, EraseAnything) on Unsafe Rate across every erasure concept. On the most challenging Nude/Unsafe-1K benchmark, SafeRoPE achieves 15.4% UR versus the best baseline ESD at 18.6% and the random-rotation ablation at 35.2%, confirming that learned risk-aware rotations are essential. On Bloody, the gap is largest: SafeRoPE (15.5%) nearly halves the best baseline UCE (25.2%), while DES and SLD perform poorly (52.6% and 43.8%). On VanGogh, SafeRoPE (19.2%) beats the best baseline Rand (24.6%) and all fine-tuning methods (26.0–57.5%). On Pikachu, the margin is tighter—SafeRoPE (13.3%) edges out SLD (14.1%) and UCE (14.3%)—but it remains the only method that fully erases the concept qualitatively. In terms of generation quality, SafeRoPE achieves the best FID (68.9 vs. 75.6–76.9 for baselines) and a VQA score (88.7) that exceeds the original model (87.5), though it trails SLD's VQA (88.9) by a marginal 0.2 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

SafeRoPE departs from the dominant concept-erasure paradigm (ESD, UCE, DES, EraseAnything) that relies on fine-tuning or cross-attention modulation designed for U-Net denoisers, by instead exploiting the RoPE mechanism native to MMDiT-based rectified-flow transformers like FLUX.1. It builds directly on LieRE and ComRoPE, which demonstrated that per-head RoPE rotations can be learned via skew-symmetric matrix exponentials, repurposing this machinery from long-sequence modeling to safety alignment. The SVD-based identification of low-rank unsafe subspaces within individual attention heads extends prior head-level interpretability analyses (showing functional specialization in transformers) into a concrete, actionable safety intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first safety framework explicitly designed for the MMDiT/rectified-flow transformer architecture, filling a gap left by U-Net-specific erasure methods that are structurally incompatible with unified multi-modal self-attention. The lightweight design (training only small r×r rotation matrices per safety-critical head) and demonstrated cross-variant transferability (FLUX.1-dev → FLUX.1-sch) make it practically attractive for deploying safety on large T2I models without costly retraining. However, the notable FID degradation (76.8 → 68.9 on FLUX.1-dev) and the requirement to re-derive unsafe subspaces per target concept limit its generality; extending the approach to broader safety domains (bias, misinformation) and to non-FLUX MMDiT models remains open.
