## Problem

Existing affordance learning methods operate in a single modality (image or point cloud) or at the object level, leaving the problem of localizing spatial affordances in full 3D scenes from visual signifiers (RGB images of human-object interactions) largely unaddressed. The cross-modal gap between 2D interaction cues and 3D volumetric affordance regions is compounded by the absence of large-scale paired RGB–point-cloud datasets with interaction annotations, making end-to-end training and evaluation infeasible.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a high-resolution voxelized indoor scene point cloud (downsampled to 100,000 points with 0.05 m voxel size, organized in a 64³ grid with per-point RGB and surface-normal features) together with a 224×224 RGB image depicting a human-object interaction (the visual signifier, annotated with bounding boxes for human, object, and contact region). It produces a 3D volumetric affordance mask identifying the precise interactable region within the scene, evaluated zero-shot under standard mAP@IoU thresholds. The AffordBridge dataset provides 317,844 such paired 2D–3D samples across 689 scenes with 291,637 affordance annotations spanning 157 object categories and 61 actions.

## Method

AffordMatcher employs a dual-encoder design: a ViT-B/16 backbone (reasoning extractor φ) encodes the RGB visual signifier, while a PointNet++ backbone (affordance extractor ψ) encodes 3D candidate regions, both projected into a shared embedding space. Bidirectional cross-modal attention (Eq. 3) produces spatial keypoint features W^(M) guided by the image and reasoning feedback W^(R) from the 3D context; their cosine dissimilarity forms a matrix D that is flattened and processed through a FastFormer-style self-attention layer followed by a match-to-match attention mechanism (adapted from Transformatcher) to yield the final correspondence map. Training combines four losses—unit-hypersphere embedding regularization, S-CLIP-based alignment, bidirectional projection consistency, and ReLU-clipped dissimilarity—optimized jointly over 100 epochs. A soft-thresholding mask on D (allowing D_ij < 0.2 to propagate multiple times) handles one-to-many correspondences in cluttered scenes, and the model achieves 53.4 mAP with 20.7 M parameters and 112.5 ms inference.

## Results

On the AffordBridge benchmark, AffordMatcher achieves an overall mAP of 53.4, outperforming the second-best baseline (OpenMask3D-F at 45.6) by 7.8 points. It also leads on mAP@0.25 (69.7) and mAP@0.50 (59.5), demonstrating superior localization across both lenient and strict IoU thresholds. Notably, AffordMatcher is the fastest method at 112.5 ms/sample with 20.7M parameters, compared to 315.1 ms and 39.7M parameters for OpenMask3D-F.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AffordMatcher outperforms all six baseline groups on every reported metric. Against the SceneFun3D-based 3D instance segmentation methods (Mask3D-F, SoftGroup-F, OpenMask3D-F), it gains 7.8–12.2 mAP, with the largest margin over Mask3D-F (53.4 vs. 41.2). Compared to object-centric affordance methods, the advantage is even more pronounced: AffordMatcher surpasses PIAD by 27.3 mAP (53.4 vs. 26.1), AffordPose-DGCNN by 23.7 mAP, and 3DAffordanceNet by 19.2 mAP. Against the more recent scene-level baselines, it leads LASO by 15.9 mAP and Ego-SAG by 13.1 mAP. In addition to accuracy, AffordMatcher is the fastest method (112.5 ms/sample) and uses fewer parameters than OpenMask3D-F (20.7M vs. 39.7M) and SoftGroup-F (20.7M vs. 30.4M), indicating a favorable accuracy–efficiency trade-off for near real-time deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

AffordMatcher extends the affordance learning literature from object-level 3D segmentation (3DAffordanceNet, PIAD, LASO) and text-prompt-based scene understanding (SceneFun3D) to a new cross-modal formulation: grounding spatial affordances in full 3D scenes using RGB visual signifiers that depict human-object interactions. Architecturally, it repurposes the match-to-match attention mechanism from Transformatcher for semantic correspondence and FastFormer's additive attention for efficient dissimilarity quantification, adapting both to the 2D-3D affordance alignment problem. The AffordBridge dataset itself builds on PIAD's interaction images and SceneFun3D's raw 3D scans, but introduces the first large-scale pairing of RGB signifiers with volumetric 3D affordance masks at scene level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AffordBridge dataset (317K paired 2D-3D samples, 685 scenes, 157 categories, 61 affordances) fills a critical gap: prior affordance benchmarks lacked paired RGB images with annotated 3D interactable regions, precluding end-to-end cross-modal training. The ablation showing a 16.1 mAP drop when removing the 2D visual signifier branch underscores that purely geometric 3D methods are fundamentally limited for disambiguating interaction semantics (e.g., 'sit' vs. 'pull' on the same chair). Open questions include scalability to higher-resolution scenes (memory constraints noted), handling of overlapping or ambiguous affordances, and extension to temporal/interactive settings for real-time robotic deployment.
