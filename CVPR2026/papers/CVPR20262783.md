## Problem

Masked video modeling (MVM) methods encode temporal dynamics only implicitly through pixel or feature reconstruction, causing them to underperform on motion-centric benchmarks like Something-Something V2 and FineGym. Prior efforts either bias masking toward motion regions or use hand-crafted motion features, but none explicitly ask the model to predict how objects move as a first-hand reconstruction signal during pretraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262783/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB video clip (e.g., 16 frames at 224×224) that is patchified into space-time tubelets of size 2×16×16, yielding a grid of tokens. An off-the-shelf CoTracker3 point tracker is run on the clip to extract sparse 2D trajectory displacements on a 14×14 grid (one query point per patch), which are spatially upsampled (v=2) to denser motion targets matching the token grid. The frozen ViT encoder produces video representation features used for downstream linear probing or full fine-tuning on action recognition and generalization benchmarks.

## Method

TrackMAE augments standard masked video modeling with two components: (i) a lightweight motion decoder that predicts point-track displacement vectors extracted by CoTracker3, supervised only on masked positions with an L2 loss, and (ii) a motion-aware masking strategy that equally samples visible tokens from high-motion and low-motion bins (controlled by ρ_motion=50%) using the same trajectory displacements. The architecture uses a shared ViT encoder with two separate decoders—one for spatial reconstruction (pixels or CLIP features) and one for motion prediction—trained jointly with a weighted sum loss (λ=1 for pixels, λ=0.25 for CLIP). A key efficiency trick spatially interpolates the sparse 14×14 tracker output to 28×28, simulating denser trajectories at no additional tracking cost and yielding +1.7–1.9% gains over the sparse baseline. The motion target is complementary to both pixel and semantic feature targets, as trajectory prediction captures how things move while CLIP features capture what is present.

## Results

On linear probing (ViT-B, pretrained on K400), TrackMAE with CLIP feature reconstruction achieves 55.2% on K400, 53.1% on HMDB51, 27.3% on SSv2, and 31.8% on FineGym, setting new state-of-the-art on both motion-centric benchmarks. In full finetuning, TrackMAE (CLIP, ViT-B) reaches 72.8% Top-1 on SSv2 and 83.9% on K400, while the ViT-L variant achieves 75.7% and 86.7% respectively. On the SEVERE generalization benchmark, TrackMAE with CLIP+motion attains the best scores on 6 out of 8 evaluation settings, including 91.1% on Gym99 (domain shift) and 59.0% on FX-S1 (action granularity).

## Baselines & Comparisons

In pixel reconstruction, TrackMAE outperforms VideoMAE by ~5% across all four benchmarks and surpasses motion-aware baselines MGM (+1.9% on SSv2, +3.2% on FineGym) and MGMAE (+0.8% on K400, +2.9% on FineGym), confirming that explicit trajectory prediction is a stronger motion signal than optical-flow or motion-vector-based masking alone. In feature reconstruction, TrackMAE (CLIP) beats the prior SOTA SMILE on both motion-centric tasks (SSv2: 27.3 vs 23.7; FineGym: 31.8 vs 30.2) and in full finetuning (SSv2: 72.8 vs 72.1; K400: 83.9 vs 83.1), while trailing SMILE slightly on appearance-centric K400 (55.2 vs 56.2) and HMDB (53.1 vs 53.4). TrackMAE also outperforms SIGMA (DINO) by +6.5% on SSv2 and +1.7% on FineGym in linear probing, and dominates MME (HOG) by large margins on all motion-centric benchmarks. In full finetuning with ViT-L, TrackMAE (CLIP) improves over VideoMAE (Pixel) by +1.7% on SSv2 and +1.5% on K400, demonstrating favorable scaling. On the SEVERE benchmark, TrackMAE (CLIP+motion) achieves the best or near-best results in 6 of 8 settings, with the largest gains on action granularity (FX-S1: 59.0 vs 55.1 for SMILE) and domain shift (Gym99: 91.1 vs 90.8), while remaining competitive on task shift (UCF-RC: 0.170, tied with SMILE).

## Relation to Prior Work

TrackMAE extends the VideoMAE masked video modeling framework by adding an explicit motion prediction head that reconstructs sparse point trajectories extracted by CoTracker3, alongside the standard spatial reconstruction (pixel or CLIP features). Prior work improved MVM along two axes—masking strategy (MGM, MGMAE, AdaMAE) and reconstruction target (MME, SIGMA, SMILE)—but all supervised motion only implicitly. TrackMAE departs by treating temporal correspondence as a first-class prediction target and repurposing the same trajectories to construct a motion-aware masking distribution, unifying both axes under a single tracking signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent gains on motion-centric benchmarks (SSv2, FineGym) and the SEVERE generalization suite address a known weakness of MVM: under high masking ratios, pixel/feature reconstruction can be solved via spatial shortcuts without learning fine-grained dynamics. The 50% pretraining overhead from point tracking is a practical cost, and the upsampling trick (spatially interpolating sparse trajectories to simulate denser supervision) is a useful but somewhat heuristic mitigation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer videos or larger backbones, whether it generalizes beyond CoTracker3 to other trackers (TAPNext is mentioned only in supplementary), and whether the motion-aware masking benefit persists at different masking ratios or with learned (rather than tracker-derived) motion signals.
