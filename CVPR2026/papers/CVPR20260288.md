## Problem

Mainstream radiology report generation (RRG) pipelines rely on multi-stage supervised fine-tuning over large paired corpora (223K–1.27M samples) and oversized VLM backbones, making them highly data- and compute-intensive. When the more efficient GRPO reinforcement learning algorithm is applied to RRG, it suffers from two failures: (i) a large fraction of sampled groups yield all-zero rewards early in training, producing vanishing gradients and wasted rollouts, and (ii) existing reward proxies (BLEU, CIDEr, report-level metrics) under-penalize sentence-level factual errors and cross-sentence contradictions in long-form clinical narratives.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single chest X-ray image (DICOM format) and a generation prompt as input, and produces a free-text radiology report as output. The generated report is scored by extracting atomic clinical facts (e.g., 'no pleural effusion', 'linear atelectasis at the left base') and checking entailment against a fixed set of 14 CheXpert pathology labels, yielding a dense per-label F-β reward. Training uses only 1,000 image-report pairs per dataset with a balanced ground-truth label distribution, and inference runs in real time (~3.3 s/image) on a 3B-parameter model.

## Method

OraPO adaptively blends GRPO and DPO losses per prompt using a Zero-Reward Rate (ZRR) signal: when a GRPO group of K=8 completions yields all-zero rewards, the ZRR triggers a DPO update that directly prefers the ground-truth report over the failed rollouts, recycling them as free negative examples without extra sampling. An exponential moving average of ZRR is mapped through a bounded, monotonic mixing weight w_i^(t) that increases DPO's influence during exploration failure and tapers as GRPO regains informative advantages. The FactS reward (inspired by FactScore) prompts GPT-4.1 to extract atomic clinical facts from each generated report, runs LLM-based entailment against the 14 ground-truth labels, and computes a per-instance F-β score (β>1 to weight recall) as a dense, interpretable, label-wise reward. The entire pipeline is RL-only (no SFT or alignment stage), trained on Qwen2.5-VL-3B with 1K samples on 4× A10 GPUs, achieving SOTA F1 on CheXpert Plus (0.341) and MIMIC-CXR (0.357) with 2–3 orders of magnitude less training data than prior best baselines.

## Results

On CheXpert Plus, OraPO achieves a macro F1 of 0.341 and Recall of 0.832 using only 1K training samples (vs. 223K–1.27M for baselines), setting new SOTA in both F1 and Recall. On MIMIC-CXR, it reaches F1 = 0.357 and Recall = 0.891, surpassing the previous best (MambaXray-L, F1 = 0.340) by +5.0% in F1 and +153.8% in Recall. On the CheXpert human gold-label validation set, the method attains F1 = 0.288 and Recall = 0.641, outperforming MambaXray-L (F1 = 0.280) and GPT-4.1 (F1 = 0.253) while using 0.1% of MambaXray's training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OraPO outperforms the previous SOTA MambaXray-L (CVPR25) by +1.8% in F1 (0.341 vs. 0.335) and by +160.8% in Recall (0.832 vs. 0.319) on CheXpert Plus, while using 1,000 training samples versus MambaXray-L's 1.27M (a ~1,270× reduction). Against the most recent baseline VLCI (IEEE TIP25), OraPO improves F1 by +109.2% (0.341 vs. 0.163) and Recall by +375.4% (0.832 vs. 0.175). On MIMIC-CXR, OraPO achieves F1 = 0.357, surpassing MambaXray-L (0.340) by +5.0% and CXRMate (0.283) by +26.1%, with Recall of 0.891 exceeding the best baseline (CXRMate, 0.351) by +153.8%. On the CheXpert gold-label set, OraPO beats GPT-4.1 by +110.2% in Recall and +13.8% in F1, and outperforms GPT-5 Thinking by +52.3% in Recall (though F1 is 4.3% lower). The trade-off is lower Precision (0.237) compared to MambaXray-L (0.377) and CheXagent (0.373), which the authors frame as a deliberate recall-oriented choice aligned with clinical preference for high sensitivity over specificity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath), a critic-free RL objective that computes group-normalised advantages from multiple sampled completions, and on DPO, which trains a policy to prefer chosen over rejected responses. The key departure is coupling the two: when GRPO produces all-zero-reward groups (a common failure mode in domain-specific tasks like radiology report generation), OraPO recycles those failed rollouts as DPO negatives against the ground-truth report, creating a single-stage RL-only training loop without SFT or alignment. On the reward side, the paper moves away from the BLEU/CIDEr or report-level clinical metrics used in prior RLHF-style RRG work toward a FactScore-inspired atomic-fact entailment signal that provides dense, per-label supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2–3 orders-of-magnitude data reduction (1K vs. 1.27M samples) with a compact 3B model on 4×A10 GPUs makes high-quality RRG accessible to settings that cannot afford multi-stage pre-training on massive corpora, which is practically significant given radiologist workforce shortages. The recall-oriented trade-off (0.832 recall vs. 0.237 precision) is clinically motivated—missed abnormalities carry greater risk than false positives—but the substantial precision gap relative to MambaXray-L (0.377) and the fact that GPT-5 Thinking still edges ahead on F1 (0.301 vs. 0.288 on gold labels) leave open whether the approach can close the precision gap without sacrificing recall. Future directions flagged by the authors include extending OraPO to non-verifiable-reward tasks, scaling beyond the 3B/1K regime, and enriching FactS with fine-grained attribute entailment (laterality, severity, temporal change).
