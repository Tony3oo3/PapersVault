## Problem

Existing multimodal generative models lack robustness in complex real-world scenarios due to insufficient generalized instruction alignment. Open-source base models are either too specialized to handle tasks beyond their training scope or over-optimized toward narrow aesthetic preferences, losing the plasticity needed for versatile generation. The paper tackles both challenges: building a flexible, world-knowledge-grounded foundation model and then systematically aligning it across multiple generation tasks with appropriate reward signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263104/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts heterogeneous multimodal inputs—text prompts, single or multiple reference/context images, and source images paired with editing instructions—all processed through a shared VLM front-end. A learned <|img|> token triggers the transition from autoregressive understanding to diffusion-based image synthesis, with the VLM's variable-length hidden states (text tokens only) and Flux-VAE-encoded image features jointly conditioning the diffusion decoder. Outputs are RGB images at up to 1024×1024 resolution, covering text-to-image generation, instruction-based editing, and in-context generation with subject/scene consistency.

## Method

OmniGen2 employs a decoupled two-transformer architecture: a frozen Qwen2.5-VL-3B handles multimodal understanding and semantic encoding, while a randomly initialized diffusion transformer (Lumina-Image 2.0 backbone with shared cross-modal parameters) performs high-fidelity image synthesis, conditioned on the VLM's variable-length hidden states rather than fixed query tokens to avoid information bottlenecks. A novel Omni-RoPE positional encoding assigns each token a 3D identifier (instance_id, h, w), separating image identity from intra-image spatial coordinates to enable cross-image localization and edit consistency. Training proceeds in two phases: (1) a resolution-curriculum pre-training (256²→512²→1024²) with mixed T2I/editing/in-context data under a Rectified Flow objective, followed by SFT; and (2) a progressive three-stage RL alignment using Flow-GRPO, sequentially optimizing Edit (EditScore reward), T2I (GenEval verifiable reward), and In-Context (Qwen2.5-VL-72B reward) tasks to promote cross-task knowledge transfer while avoiding reward hacking and task interference.

## Results

On GenEval, OmniGen2 achieves 0.95 overall, surpassing all unified models (BAGEL 0.88, UniWorld-V1 0.84) and the T2I-specialized Qwen-Image (0.91). On the proposed OmniContext benchmark, OmniGen2 scores 7.95 overall, outperforming Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 (7.69) and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image (7.84), though trailing GPT-4o (8.80). On GEdit-Bench-EN, OmniGen2 achieves 7.21 overall (SC 7.58, PQ 7.94), beating Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image (7.10) and BAGEL (6.52) but falling short of Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 (7.54). On Emu-Edit, OmniGen2 records the best CLIP-Out (0.311) and DINO (0.876) scores among all compared models, indicating superior edit accuracy and source-image preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniGen2 (3B+4B) achieves the best GenEval score (0.95) among all compared models, beating the T2I-specialized Qwen-Image (0.91) and unified models BAGEL (0.88) and UniWorld-V1 (0.84), demonstrating that RL alignment substantially boosts compositional generation. On OneIG-Bench, OmniGen2 (0.47) trails Qwen-Image (0.54) and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image (0.55), indicating a gap in broader perceptual quality and text rendering. For image editing on GEdit-Bench-EN, OmniGen2 (7.21) outperforms Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image (7.10) and BAGEL (6.52) but is surpassed by Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 (7.54) and GPT-4o (7.53); on Emu-Edit it achieves the highest CLIP-Out (0.311) and DINO (0.876), confirming precise localized edits with strong source preservation. On the new OmniContext benchmark, OmniGen2 (7.95) surpasses Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 (7.69) and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image (7.84) across all three subtask groups (Single, Multiple, Scene), though GPT-4o remains the strongest closed-source model at 8.80. Compared to its predecessor OmniGen (3.8B), OmniGen2 improves GenEval from 0.68 to 0.95, GEdit from 5.06 to 7.21, and OmniContext from 4.34 to 7.95, validating the two-stage foundation-plus-RL-alignment pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniGen2 extends the unified multimodal generation line (OmniGen, BAGEL, MetaQuery, Show-o) by adopting a decoupled architecture: a frozen VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) provides variable-length semantic conditioning to a separately initialized diffusion transformer, avoiding the information bottleneck of fixed-length query tokens used in MetaQuery and the high training cost of jointly optimizing both pathways as in BAGEL. For alignment, the paper departs from single-task RL approaches (DDPO, DPOK, ReFL) and even recent multi-task GRPO variants by introducing a progressive, sequentially-scheduled curriculum (Edit → T2I → IC) with carefully matched reward signals, rather than joint or randomly interleaved training. The Omni-RoPE positional encoding extends RoPE from 1D text to a 3D (instance, height, width) scheme, building on Qwen2-VL's multi-image RoPE but with a cleaner decomposition that separates image identity from intra-image spatial coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive multi-task RL alignment pipeline offers a practical recipe for aligning unified generative models across heterogeneous tasks, with the ablation showing that both task selection (skill overlap matters) and ordering (editing before T2I) significantly affect final performance. The OmniContext benchmark addresses a real gap in in-context generation evaluation, providing a standardized tool where prior benchmarks (DreamBench, etc.) were too small or relied on uninformative metrics like CLIP-I. Open questions include whether the frozen-VLM design limits ceiling performance, whether the curriculum generalizes to new task types (e.g., video, 3D), and whether the reward-hacking vulnerability demonstrated with HPSv3 extends to other learned reward models at scale.
