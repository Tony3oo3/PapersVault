## Problem

Continual learning in large multimodal models (LMMs) suffers from catastrophic forgetting when sequentially acquiring new tasks, and existing mitigation strategies (knowledge distillation, LoRA adapters) fail to address fairness under imbalanced data distributions. The paper identifies that imbalanced multimodal data causes systematically biased gradient updates that favor majority semantic classes, exacerbating forgetting and degrading performance on tail categories across incremental tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262763/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each continual learning step, the model receives an RGB image (encoded by CLIP-ViT-L-14 at 336×336) paired with a textual instruction and produces a free-form text answer. Training additionally requires pairwise preference annotations (y⁺, y⁻) per instruction: y⁺ is the reference answer representing well-retained knowledge, while y⁻ is an LLM-hallucinated plausible-but-flawed alternative, both manually verified. The LMM backbone (LLaVA v1.5 with Vicuna-7B) is updated via LoRA (rank 32) rather than full fine-tuning.

## Method

The approach reformulates continual learning as Direct Preference Optimization: instead of an explicit reward model or KL-divergence distillation, the current policy π_t is trained against a frozen reference π_{t-1} using pairwise preference signals where y⁺ (well-retained) is preferred over y⁻ (forgotten), yielding a logistic loss on policy log-ratios that implicitly regularizes updates. To address data imbalance, the authors introduce a Fairness DPO (φ-DPO) loss that applies a focal-style focusing parameter γ to modulate group-wise gradients, provably eliminating gradient bias as γ grows (Lemma 3). Theoretical analysis (Lemmas 1–2) establishes two-sided bounds showing the DPO loss governs the KL divergence used in prior knowledge distillation, positioning DPO as a generalized, preference-driven form of distillation. The final objective combines standard supervised fine-tuning with the φ-DPO loss at each step, and the authors construct DPO preference labels for three existing continual learning benchmarks (CoIN, MLLM-CL Domain, MLLM-CL Ability) by pairing reference answers with LLM-generated hallucinated alternatives.

## Results

On the MLLM-CL Domain benchmark, φ-DPO achieves MFT 74.29%, MFN 74.00%, MAA 75.68%, and BWT −0.37%, outperforming the strongest prior method MR-LoRA (MFT 69.64%, MFN 69.63%, MAA 71.06%, BWT −0.01%) by 4.65 points in MFT and 4.37 points in MFN. On MLLM-CL Ability, φ-DPO reaches MFT 45.55% and MFN 45.31% versus MR-LoRA's 41.89% and 41.88%. On the CoIN benchmark (8 tasks), φ-DPO attains MFN 68.86% and MAA 74.94%, surpassing the next-best HiDe (MFN 62.82%, MAA 64.70%) by over 6 points in MFN and 10 points in MAA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

φ-DPO outperforms all 11 baselines across every benchmark and metric. Against the closest competitor MR-LoRA [115], φ-DPO gains +4.65 MFT, +4.37 MFN, +4.62 MAA, and achieves comparable BWT (−0.37 vs −0.01) on MLLM-CL Domain, and +3.66 MFT, +3.43 MFN on MLLM-CL Ability. LoRA-based baselines (LoRA-FT, O-LoRA, MoELoRA) suffer severe forgetting (BWT between −12 and −15) and 9–21 point MFN gaps, confirming that standard LoRA fine-tuning is inadequate for multimodal continual learning. Mixture-of-Experts approaches (CL-MoE, MoELoRA) improve over plain LoRA but still trail φ-DPO by 19+ points in MFN. Regularization-based methods from the CoIN benchmark (LwF, EWC, L2P) achieve MFN of only 48–53%, far below φ-DPO's 68.86%, indicating that explicit parameter regularization is insufficient for LMM-scale continual learning. The ablation in Table 4 further shows that vanilla DPO already surpasses knowledge distillation (KD) by 2.53 MFN and 2.53 MFN, while the fairness term (φ) adds another 1.49 MFN and 2.26 MFN, validating both components.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) from LLM alignment to continual learning in LMMs, framing catastrophic forgetting as a preference-optimization problem where well-retained outputs are preferred over forgotten ones. It further adapts the focal loss mechanism (Lin et al., ICCV 2017) to the DPO gradient to counteract group-wise bias from imbalanced multimodal data. Conceptually, this departs from the dominant LoRA-based and knowledge-distillation paradigms in LMM continual learning (CoIN, MLLM-CL) by replacing explicit KL-regularization with a pairwise contrastive objective, and extends the authors' prior unimodal fairness continual learning work to the multimodal LMM setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to explicitly address fairness under data imbalance in LMM continual learning, filling a gap that prior LMM-CL benchmarks (CoIN, MLLM-CL) left unaddressed. The theoretical bounds (Lemmas 1–2) providing a two-sided link between DPO loss and KL divergence offer a principled justification for why preference optimization can subsume knowledge distillation as a forgetting-mitigation mechanism. Key open questions include the sensitivity of the DPO data construction (relying on LLM-hallucinated negative examples) to domain shifts and class imbalance, the practical tuning burden of β and γ, and whether the fairness guarantee (Lemma 3, which holds only as γ→∞) translates to meaningful per-group accuracy gains in deployment.
