## Problem

State-of-the-art multi-view head avatars rely on a two-stage pipeline of mesh-based head tracking followed by per-subject Gaussian optimization, requiring 10+ hours of tracking and 27+ hours of optimization per avatar. This makes scaling to large numbers of subjects prohibitively expensive. The paper asks whether Gaussian splats in dense semantic correspondence can be predicted directly from multi-view images in a single feed-forward pass, eliminating both the tracking and optimization bottlenecks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262297/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

MATCH takes 12 calibrated multi-view head-centered images at 640×512 resolution with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics as input. It outputs a 1024×1024 UV texture encoding per-texel Gaussian splat attributes (RGB color, opacity, 3D location, anisotropic scale, and rotation quaternion), totaling 1M Gaussians renderable at 570 fps. The texture is in dense semantic correspondence: each texel consistently represents the same semantic region (e.g., nose tip) across all subjects and expressions, enabling direct interpolation, editing, and expression transfer between identities.

## Method

MATCH is a transformer that processes tokenized input images and UV texture patches through alternating registration-guided attention and grouped attention blocks. The key architectural contribution is the registration-guided attention: a coarse mesh (from TEMPEH's global stage) is rasterized to determine, for each UV token, which image tokens depict the corresponding head region, and attention is restricted to only the top-k highest-scoring image tokens via an IoU-based correspondence score. This localizes the reconstruction, improves generalization over dense attention, and keeps compute constant as the number of input views grows. Image tokens are formed by concatenating Plücker ray coordinates with Sapiens features through a 2D convolution; UV tokens combine barycentric-interpolated 3D locations and reprojected RGB with learnable positional embeddings. Training uses a three-stage curriculum (geometry-only → add photometric loss → joint Ava-256 + NeRSemble) with LPIPS, L1, SSIM photometric losses, L2 geometry loss, and scale/opacity regularization.

## Results

On Ava-256, MATCH achieves LPIPS 0.163, CSIM 0.928, PSNR 23.68 dB, and SSIM 0.848, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On NeRSemble, the jointly-trained model reaches LPIPS 0.152, CSIM 0.944, PSNR 25.51 dB, and SSIM 0.884. For subject-specific GEM avatars, MATCH reduces reconstruction time from 45.3 h (GEM) to 4.6 h (a 10× speedup) while improving self-reenactment LPIPS from 0.214 to 0.174 and PSNR from 21.76 to 24.12 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MATCH outperforms all five novel-view-synthesis baselines on every metric for both Ava-256 and NeRSemble. The closest competitor, FaceLift [40], trails by 0.045 LPIPS and 0.060 CSIM on Ava-256 (0.208 vs. 0.163; 0.868 vs. 0.928), while single-view methods LAM [20] and FastAvatar [59] lag substantially (LPIPS ≈ 0.27–0.29) and exhibit identity shift on extreme expressions. Avat3r [27] achieves competitive PSNR (22.72) but suffers a large CSIM gap (0.626 vs. 0.928), indicating identity loss especially in the mouth and eye regions. For geometry, MATCH beats TEMPEH [5] on full-head P2P (6.69 vs. 7.84 mm) and P2S (2.10 vs. 2.18 mm), though TEMPEH is slightly better in the mouth and eye sub-regions. For subject-specific avatars (Table 4), MATCH dominates GEM [66] on self-reenactment (LPIPS 0.174 vs. 0.214, PSNR 24.12 vs. 21.76) and matches it on cross-reenactment CSIM (0.813 vs. 0.800), while being 10× faster (4.6 h vs. 45.3 h). RGBAvatar [30] and GaussianAvatars [49] are both outperformed on all avatar metrics, with RGBAvatar showing the largest CSIM gap (0.657 vs. 0.813) and noticeable artifacts on long hair.

## Relation to Prior Work

MATCH extends the feed-forward Large Reconstruction Model (LRM) paradigm to head avatars by predicting Gaussian splat UV textures directly from multi-view images, building on the image tokenization of GS-LRM and the Sapiens feature fusion of Avat3r. It departs from prior LRM-based avatar methods (LAM, FastAvatar) by reconstructing in posed space rather than a canonical template, and from pixel-aligned methods (Avat3r, FaceLift) by enforcing dense semantic correspondence across frames and subjects via a novel registration-guided attention mechanism. The coarse mesh initialization borrows from TEMPEH, and the downstream avatar pipeline adapts GEM's PCA-based Gaussian Eigen Model formulation, but bypasses both the mesh tracking and CNN avatar training stages that dominate prior pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× reduction in avatar creation time (45 h → 4.6 h) makes large-scale personalized avatar generation practically feasible, and the learned dense correspondence unlocks editing, expression transfer, and interpolation applications that were previously intractable without per-subject optimization. Key limitations remain: training still requires geometry supervision (Ava-256 registrations), direct expression transfer can leak identity between dissimilar subjects, and the resulting GEM avatars are bounded by the training expression manifold. An open question is whether the geometry branch can be trained purely on synthetic data to eliminate the dependency on registered multi-view datasets, which would substantially broaden the method's applicability.
