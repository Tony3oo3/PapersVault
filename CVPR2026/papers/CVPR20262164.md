## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D indoor scene editing systems—whether generative, constraint-optimization, or image-lifting based—cannot simultaneously satisfy instruction fidelity, preservation of unchanged scene regions, and physical plausibility. These failures stem from treating editing as a single forward generative or optimization step rather than as a structured, goal-directed transformation, causing semantic drift, unintended global modifications, or geometrically invalid layouts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source 3D indoor scene (object geometry with pose, category labels, and front-face orientation) and a freeform natural-language instruction ranging from simple rearrangements to multi-step compositional edits. It produces an edited 3D scene along with an interpretable, ordered sequence of atomic symbolic actions in EditLang. Before planning, all EditLang predicates are evaluated on the source scene under a closed-world assumption to construct the initial symbolic state, and after each accepted action the predicates are recomputed from updated geometry to keep symbols and 3D aligned.

## Method

Edit-As-Act reframes scene editing as goal-regressive planning inspired by classical STRIPS: a natural-language instruction is mapped to symbolic goal predicates in EditLang, a PDDL-inspired action language with explicit preconditions and effects encoding support, contact, collision, and other geometric relations. Two LLM-driven modules operate in a backward loop—a planner proposes a single action satisfying at least one current goal, and a validator enforces goal-directedness, monotonicity (no undoing of already-satisfied goals), contextual consistency, and formal schema validity. A source-aware regression operator propagates only unsatisfied preconditions, avoiding redundant reconstruction of scene aspects already true in the source. The loop terminates when all goals regress to the source state, and the reversed action sequence is executed by a deterministic Python DSL runtime that recomputes predicates after each step, guaranteeing finite termination and physical coherence.

## Results

On E2A-Bench (63 editing tasks across 9 indoor environments), Edit-As-Act achieves average scores of 69.1 IF, 86.6 SC, and 91.7 PP, outperforming all baselines on all three metrics simultaneously. The largest margins are in Semantic Consistency, where Edit-As-Act leads the next-best method (AnyHome at 60.5) by 26.1 points, and in Instruction Fidelity, where it exceeds LayoutGPT-E (42.3) by 26.8 points. Against the strongest reasoning baseline SceneWeaver, Edit-As-Act gains 8.3 points in SC and 9.6 points in PP while using fewer model calls (5.9 vs. 7.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Edit-As-Act wins on all three metrics against every baseline. Against the three paradigm representatives: LayoutGPT-E (forward layout generation) trails by 26.8 IF, 37.8 SC, and 13.1 PP; AnyHome (constraint optimization) trails by 11.5 IF, 26.1 SC, and 7.2 PP; ArtiScene-E (image-to-3D lifting) trails by 20.8 IF and 35.4 SC, though it is close on PP (90.3 vs. 91.7) due to strong 2D rendering priors. Against direct single-pass LLM reasoning (GPT-5, Gemini-3-Pro, Claude-4.5-opus), Edit-As-Act leads by 18.5–26.0 IF, 34.1–43.1 SC, and 18.4–23.2 PP, showing that structured goal-regressive planning substantially outperforms one-shot generation. The closest competitor is SceneWeaver (iterative action-and-reflection with the same GPT-5 backbone): Edit-As-Act edges ahead on IF (69.1 vs. 68.7) and leads by 8.3 SC and 9.6 PP, while also requiring fewer API calls (5.9 vs. 7.3) at a comparable token budget (~19k vs. ~18.3k).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the three dominant paradigms for language-conditioned 3D scene editing—data-driven generation (LayoutGPT, EditRoom, EchoScene), constraint-based optimization (AnyHome, Holodeck, I-Design), and image-to-3D lifting (ArtiScene, Text2Room)—by reframing editing as a goal-regressive planning problem drawn from classical STRIPS/PDDL automated planning. Rather than building on any single prior editing system, it repurposes symbolic planning machinery (preconditions, effects, backward regression) into a new domain-specific action language (EditLang) and couples it with LLM-driven planner and validator modules. The closest conceptual neighbor is SceneWeaver's iterative action-and-reflection loop, but Edit-As-Act replaces open-ended forward search with structured backward goal regression and explicit geometric validation, a distinction the paper isolates in ablation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single framework can simultaneously satisfy instruction fidelity, semantic locality, and physical plausibility—three criteria that prior paradigms each fail on at least one—offering a principled alternative to the generate-then-repair or optimize-then-lift pipelines that dominate the field. However, the evaluation rests on a modest 63-task benchmark across 9 indoor scenes, all evaluated by a single-view LVLM judge, which limits confidence in the generality of the gains; the authors themselves note failure modes with ambiguous instructions and planning deadlocks under strict monotonicity. Open questions include whether the symbolic approach scales to larger or outdoor scenes, whether the GPT-5-dependent planner can be replaced by smaller or open-weight models, and whether the E2A-Bench protocol (single-view LVLM scoring) adequately captures full 3D physical validity.
