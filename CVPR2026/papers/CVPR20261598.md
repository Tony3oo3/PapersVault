## Problem

Existing self-reflection mechanisms for multimodal models rely on purely text-based rethinking, which performs well on image understanding but paradoxically degrades performance on long-form video tasks. This failure stems from two factors: long-form videos contain far richer and more dynamic visual content than static images, making text-only re-evaluation insufficient to correct reasoning errors, and text-based reflection inherently lacks cross-modal interaction to integrate visual cues during the reflection process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261598/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long-form video (sparse-uniformly sampled into N frames, capped at 8K video tokens) and a text question as input. It produces a two-stage output: Stage 1 yields a preliminary chain-of-thought reasoning trace plus a proposed temporal review segment S = [t_start, t_end]; Stage 2 yields a refined reasoning trace and final answer. A visual toolbox performs dense re-sampling (higher FPS) from the identified segment S, and the model is re-invoked in a reflection mode conditioned on the original question, the initial trace, and the newly sampled frames.

## Method

REVISOR is a two-stage tool-augmented multimodal reflection framework built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. In Stage 1, the MLLM generates an initial reasoning trace and proposes a temporal segment S warranting closer examination; a visual toolbox then densely re-samples frames from S. In Stage 2, the MLLM operates in reflection mode, integrating the initial trace with the densely sampled visual evidence to produce a refined answer. Training uses GRPO (following DAPO) with a Dual Attribution Decoupled Reward (DADR) that combines a Final Answer Verification Reward (λ₁=0.6) with a Causal Segment Sufficiency Reward (λ₂=0.3). The CSSR component tests whether the model can derive the correct answer using only the question and the frames from the proposed segment S, thereby enforcing causal alignment between the selected video evidence and the reasoning, and preventing the model from relying on irrelevant or overly broad segments.

## Results

On four long-form video understanding benchmarks with 8K video tokens, REVISOR (7B) achieves 65.7% on VideoMME Overall, 57.5% on LongVideoBench, 69.8% on MLVU, and 42.0% on LVBench, improving over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model by 1.4%, 1.0%, 2.5%, and 1.8% respectively. The gains are most pronounced on longer videos: +2.8% on VideoMME-Long and +2.5% on MLVU (videos up to 120 min). On temporal video grounding, REVISOR reaches 51.4% mIoU on Charades-STA and 33.2% mIoU on NExT-GQA, surpassing the prior SOTA iMOVE by 4.1% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REVISOR outperforms the text-based reflection method VL-Rethinker by 3.6% on VideoMME Overall (65.7 vs. 62.1) and 4.8% on LVBench (42.0 vs. 37.2), and beats the text-only reasoning approach Video-R1 by 4.3% on VideoMME (65.7 vs. 61.4), confirming that visual rethinking is essential for long-form video. Against the authors' own video-trained text-only reflection variant, REVISOR gains 2.3% on VideoMME, isolating the contribution of the visual segment re-sampling mechanism. On temporal grounding, REVISOR surpasses the RL-based TVG-R1 by 4.7% mIoU on Charades-STA (51.4 vs. 46.7) and 3.9% on NExT-GQA (33.2 vs. 29.3), and exceeds the SFT-based iMOVE by 4.1% mIoU. However, REVISOR trails larger proprietary models (Gemini-1.5-Pro at 75.0%, GPT-4o at 71.9% on VideoMME Overall) and also underperforms NVILA on LongVideoBench (57.5 vs. 70.1) and VideoChat-Flash on MLVU (69.8 vs. 74.7), indicating that the 7B open-source setting still lags behind both closed-source and some specialized 7–8B models on certain benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

REVISOR extends the self-reflection line of work (VL-Rethinker, Video-R1, SRPO) by replacing purely textual re-evaluation with a tool-augmented multimodal reflection that re-samples dense visual frames from model-identified segments. The training procedure builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeekMath) and follows the DAPO recipe, but introduces the Dual Attribution Decoupled Reward (DADR) mechanism—a novel reward decomposition that separates final-answer correctness from causal segment sufficiency—to address the credit-assignment problem inherent in multi-stage video reasoning. Conceptually, the paper departs from the prevailing assumption that text-based chain-of-thought reflection is sufficient for all multimodal tasks, arguing instead that long-form video's dynamic visual content necessitates a dedicated visual re-examination channel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first clear empirical evidence that text-based self-reflection, which improves image understanding, actively degrades long-form video performance—a finding that should caution practitioners against blindly porting reflection strategies across modalities. The DADR mechanism offers a practical, SFT-free recipe for teaching MLLMs to localize question-relevant video segments via RL, which could be adopted by other video reasoning pipelines. However, the ~2% average gain is modest, the evaluation is limited to a single 7B base model (Qwen2.5-VL), and the framework's reliance on a fixed 8K video-token budget and a simple dense-sampling tool leaves open questions about scalability to hour-scale videos, more complex visual tools (e.g., optical flow, object tracking), and whether the DADR reward generalizes beyond the specific benchmark distributions used in training.
