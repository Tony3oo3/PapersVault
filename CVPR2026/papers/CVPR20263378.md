## Problem

Existing style transfer methods modify visual appearance but ignore the emotional impact carried by styles, while affective image manipulation methods evoke emotions through color or content edits but produce realistic rather than artistic results. No prior approach can apply artistic styles to evoke a specific emotion using only an emotion word as prompt, without requiring reference style images or art-knowledge-based text descriptions. Additionally, no dataset provides content–emotion–stylized image triplets needed to learn emotion-aware stylization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a user-provided RGB content image and a single emotion word (one of eight categories from Mikels' wheel: amusement, awe, contentment, excitement, anger, disgust, fear, sadness) as input, and produces a single stylized image that evokes the target emotion while preserving semantic and structural content. The emotion is encoded as a 1×8 one-hot vector rather than free text, and the content image is processed through SigLIP for semantic features and Canny edge maps for structural guidance. The framework also extends to text-to-image generation by replacing the image encoder with a text encoder while keeping the same emotion-word conditioning.

## Method

EmoStyle comprises two modules built on a frozen MM-DiT backbone. The Emotion-Content Reasoner extracts content features via SigLIP, encodes the emotion as a one-hot vector, projects both into a shared embedding space, and processes them through a four-block multi-modal transformer (self- and cross-attention) to produce an emotion-aware, content-conditioned style query. The Style Quantizer, inspired by VQ-VAE, maintains eight discrete style dictionaries (one per emotion, each with K prototypes initialized via USO's style encoder and similarity clustering) and maps the continuous query to its nearest prototype, yielding an interpretable, discrete style representation. Training is two-stage: first, prototype style features are learned via a clustering loss on stylized images; second, the full pipeline is fine-tuned with a flow-matching loss plus an alignment loss that pulls the style query toward its assigned prototype, with all losses weighted by per-sample emotion scores from ArtEmis. The dataset EmoStyleSet (10,041 triplets) is constructed from ArtEmis by disentangling content from style using UnZipLoRA and regenerating realistic content images with ControlNet.

## Results

On the 405-image EmoEdit inference set (3,240 stylized results across 8 emotions), EmoStyle achieves the best Emotion Accuracy (33.36%), Sentiment Gap (1.976), DINO structural score (0.842), and Style Difference (7.59), while ranking second on CLIP semantic consistency (0.718 vs. CLVA's 0.727). The Emo-A margin over the runner-up (IP2P at 24.34%) is nearly 9 percentage points, and the DINO score exceeds the next best (OmniStyle at 0.813) by 0.029. In the user study, 89.70% of participants preferred EmoStyle for the balance of emotion and aesthetics, 79.92% for aesthetic perception, and 64.47% for emotion fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmoStyle outperforms all 10 baselines on four of five metrics (DINO, SG, Emo-A, SD) and is narrowly second only on CLIP (0.718 vs. CLVA's 0.727). Among style-transfer methods, InST achieves the second-best SG (2.016) but collapses on DINO (0.679) and CLIP (0.569), indicating poor content preservation; OmniStyle leads on DINO among baselines (0.813) yet scores only 12.80% Emo-A. Among image-editing methods, IP2P reaches the highest baseline Emo-A (24.34%) but still trails EmoStyle by 9 points and has a much higher SG (3.459). Among AIM methods, CLVA and AIF produce stylized outputs but with subtle cross-emotion style differences (SD 9.49 and 8.48 respectively), while EmoEdit and EmoEditor focus on realistic color/content edits and score poorly on both style (SD 28.83 and 13.65) and emotion (Emo-A 12.60% and 14.88%). In the user study, EmoStyle dominates all three preference dimensions, with the closest competitor (InST) receiving only 29.63% for emotion fidelity versus EmoStyle's 64.47%.

## Relation to Prior Work

EmoStyle sits at the intersection of style transfer and affective image manipulation, drawing the MM-DiT backbone and style encoder from USO [40] and the vector quantization mechanism from VQ-VAE [35]. It departs from prior style transfer (CLIPStyler, OmniStyle, InST) by replacing reference images or art-knowledge prompts with simple emotion words, and departs from AIM methods (EmoEdit, AIF, CLVA) by producing stylized rather than realistic outputs. The dataset construction pipeline repurposes UnZipLoRA for content-style disentanglement and ControlNet for content image generation, bridging the artwork emotion analysis literature (ArtEmis) with generative style transfer in a way no prior work has attempted.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing Affective Image Stylization as a distinct task and providing both a dataset and a framework, this paper opens a direction where emotion words serve as the sole style prompt, lowering the barrier for non-expert users. The interpretable, discrete style dictionary (one-to-many emotion-to-style mapping) is a practical design choice that enables user control and transfer to text-to-image generation. However, the many-to-many emotion-style relationship is simplified, content's contribution to emotional perception is underexplored, and the absolute emotion accuracy (33%) suggests substantial room for improvement. Establishing reliable evaluation for subjective emotional impact in stylized images remains an open challenge.
