## Problem

Existing 3D referring expression segmentation methods assume access to dense, high-quality point clouds from LIDAR or lengthy RGB-D SLAM pipelines, yet real-world embodied agents (robots, AR glasses, phones) perceive scenes through only a few sparse RGB views. Under such conditions, two-stage reconstruct-then-segment pipelines produce noisy, incomplete geometry that degrades segmentation, and purely 2D methods cannot enforce global 3D consistency. The paper formalizes Multi-view 3D Referring Expression Segmentation (MV-3DRES), requiring joint scene reconstruction and language-grounded segmentation directly from sparse multi-view images.

## Input / Output

The model takes N=8 sparsely sampled RGB frames (uniformly extracted from ScanNet video sequences) and a natural-language referring expression as input, with no ground-truth 3D geometry available at inference. It jointly outputs a reconstructed 3D point cloud S′ (K×3, typically 10⁶–10⁷ points) and a corresponding binary mask M (K,) marking the referred object. Evaluation uses both a global 3D mIoU on the reconstructed cloud and per-view 2D diagnostic metrics (mIoU_view, mIoU_pos, mIoU_neg) obtained by reprojecting the 3D mask back into each view, with difficulty splits defined by whether the target occupies <5% of pixels in all visible views.

## Method

MVGGT is a dual-branch transformer: a frozen Pi3 reconstruction backbone (36 alternating self/cross-view attention blocks) provides camera poses, depth, and a coarse point cloud, while a trainable 12-block multimodal branch fuses geometric and linguistic cues. Geometric features from the last L/3 blocks of the frozen branch are injected into each multimodal block via a zero-initialized 1×1 convolution, and language (RoBERTa embeddings) is injected through cross-attention, so text guides cross-view feature aggregation from early layers. The key training challenge is Foreground Gradient Dilution: in sparse reconstructions the target occupies <2% of points, making 3D Dice gradients drop to 10⁻⁹–10⁻¹¹ and stalling optimization. PVSO (Per-view No-target Suppression Optimization) resolves this by shifting supervision to 2D view space where the target occupies 10–15% of pixels, using positive-aware sampling to guarantee sufficient foreground views per batch, applying per-view 2D Dice loss, and down-weighting no-target views by 1/|V_n| to prevent trivial background collapse. The final objective combines a 3D BCE loss with the PVSO 2D loss (λ_p=1), and per-view 2D masks are back-projected and aggregated on the reconstructed cloud to yield the 3D output.

## Results

On the MVRefer benchmark, MVGGT achieves 39.9 mIoU_global and 69.3 mIoU_view overall, outperforming the two-stage baseline (18.5 / 20.3) by 21.4 and 49.0 points, and the 2D-Lift baseline (17.8 / 20.4) by 22.1 and 48.9 points. On the Hard subset (target <5% pixels in all views), MVGGT reaches 24.4 mIoU_global versus 8.1 (two-stage) and 6.4 (2D-Lift). Under the standard ScanRefer protocol (Table 2), MVGGT attains 39.9 mIoU overall using only sparse RGB, approaching the best traditional 3D-RES method RG-SAN (44.6 mIoU) which requires dense ground-truth point clouds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two MV-3DRES baselines, MVGGT dominates across every metric and difficulty split: on Overall mIoU_global it beats two-stage (Pi3 + LESS) by 21.4 points (39.9 vs. 18.5) and 2D-Lift (ReferDINO + Pi3) by 22.1 points (39.9 vs. 17.8), with the gap widening to ~18 points on the Hard subset. Under the standard ScanRefer protocol (Table 2), MVGGT's 39.9 mIoU overall is within 4.7 points of the strongest traditional 3D-RES method RG-SAN (44.6 mIoU) and 5.4 points of Reason3D (42.0 mIoU), despite operating without any ground-truth point cloud. Compared to other traditional 3D-RES methods—TGNN (28.8), 3D-STMN (39.5), SegPoint (41.7), 3D-LLaVA (43.3), and LESS (33.7)—MVGGT is competitive or superior on mIoU while using a fundamentally different (and more realistic) input modality. The two MV-3DRES baselines (two-stage and 2D-Lift) both suffer severe degradation relative to their component models run on dense point clouds, confirming the paper's central claim that sparse-view geometry is the bottleneck. Notably, MVGGT's mIoU_neg (79.9) far exceeds both baselines (16.9 and 12.1), indicating that PVSO's no-target suppression is critical for avoiding false positives in views where the target is absent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 3DRES paradigm, which assumes access to dense, high-quality point clouds from LIDAR or offline RGB-D SLAM, and instead grounds language in 3D directly from sparse multi-view RGB images. Architecturally, it builds on the VGGT/Pi3 family of feed-forward multi-view reconstruction transformers, using Pi3 as a frozen geometric scaffold whose per-block features are injected into a trainable multimodal branch via zero-initialized convolutions and cross-attention with RoBERTa text embeddings. The key conceptual departure is the identification of Foreground Gradient Dilution as a fundamental optimization barrier under sparse 3D supervision, addressed by the PVSO strategy that shifts early learning signals to the 2D image domain where foreground occupies a much larger pixel fraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing MV-3DRES and releasing MVRefer, the paper provides the first standardized evaluation protocol for language-grounded 3D perception under realistic sparse-view sensing, a setting directly relevant to robots, AR glasses, and mobile devices. The FGD analysis offers a reusable diagnostic for any task involving extreme foreground-background imbalance in 3D space, and the finding that 2D-projected supervision outperforms direct 3D loss by 1–3 orders of magnitude in gradient magnitude is a practical recipe for sparse-geometry learning. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 8 views and indoor ScanRefer scenes, whether the frozen-geometry assumption holds for highly dynamic or outdoor environments, and how to close the remaining ~5-point mIoU gap to full 3D-RES methods without sacrificing the single-pass latency advantage.
