## Problem

Client-wise federated unlearning requires erasing the entire data contribution of specific clients from a globally trained model, but existing methods face a fundamental obstacle: forget clients lack access to the retain dataset, making the standard unlearning objective infeasible. Retraining-based approaches are computationally and communication-prohibitive for real-world FL, while approximate methods only handle feature-level unlearning where both retain and forget data coexist locally. This paper addresses the gap of efficient, faithful client-wise unlearning that preserves retain-client knowledge while removing forget-client knowledge without on-server data access.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images with class labels from domain-generalization datasets (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, TerraIncognita) distributed across 20 federated clients (5 per domain), along with per-client gradients of the non-causal featurizer uploaded to the server during unlearning rounds. The unlearning request specifies which domain's clients constitute the forget set. The output is an updated global model in which only the non-causal featurizer parameters have been modified, producing class labels at inference; the causal featurizer remains frozen throughout unlearning, ensuring domain-invariant knowledge is preserved.

## Method

FOUL operates in two stages. In the Learning-to-Unlearn (L2U) stage, the feature extractor is disentangled into a causal featurizer (trained via prototypical networks to produce compact, domain-invariant class representations) and a non-causal featurizer (trained via a hinge loss on intra-class variance to capture domain-specific information), jointly optimized with a reconstruction loss and a ground-truth classification loss on the causal branch. In the on-server unlearning stage, only the non-causal featurizer is updated: the server aggregates client gradients using learnable weighting coefficients Γ (one per client, summing to 1) to construct a global gradient that maximizes cosine similarity with retain-client gradients while minimizing it with forget-client gradients. This reduces the optimization space from the full parameter dimension d to just U coefficients, and Theorem 1 provides a closed-form solution for the optimal unlearning gradient as a scaled combination of the standard FL gradient and the retain-minus-forget gradient difference, requiring no on-server data access.

## Results

On PACS, FOUL (full pipeline) achieves 92.33% Retain Accuracy (RA) and 70.97% Forget Accuracy (FA), closely matching the Retrain baseline (82.84% RA, 70.51% FA) while substantially exceeding all approximate unlearning methods in RA by 3–14 points. On TerraIncognita, FOUL reaches 42.13% RA and 29.92% FA, again near the Retrain reference (42.41% RA, 30.64% FA). FOUL also attains the best Test Accuracy among all methods on TerraIncognita (39.16% vs. 38.94% for Retrain) and the lowest communication cost (16.02 MB/round vs. 42.73 MB for most baselines) with 2.35×10¹⁶ FLOPs, roughly half the computation of retraining-based approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FOUL dramatically outperforms all eight approximate unlearning baselines on Retain Accuracy: on PACS it reaches 92.33% RA versus 76.97–80.91% for the best baselines (FATS 80.91%, FedOSD 80.15%), a gap of 3–14 points. On Forget Accuracy, FOUL (70.97% on PACS, 29.92% on TerraIncognita) is closest to the Retrain gold standard (70.51%, 30.64%), whereas all approximate methods show higher FA (i.e., worse forgetting) by 2–7 points. Test Accuracy is competitive: FOUL achieves 76.43% on PACS (vs. 77.45% Retrain) and 39.16% on TerraIncognita (vs. 38.94% Retrain), slightly exceeding every baseline. MIA scores confirm FOUL's privacy: 51.93% on PACS and 57.11% on TerraIncognita, far below FedRecovery (75.24%, 84.03%) and FedCDP (72.26%, 81.91%), which suffer severe membership leakage. In terms of efficiency, FOUL requires only 16.02 MB per-round communication and 2.35×10¹⁶ FLOPs, roughly 63% less communication and 60% less computation than retraining-based methods (42.73 MB, 5.81×10¹⁶ FLOPs), and also outperforms FUSED (0.98 MB, 2.81×10¹⁶ FLOPs) in accuracy despite slightly higher communication.

## Relation to Prior Work

FOUL repurposes two ideas from domain generalization—causal/non-causal representation disentanglement (Lv et al. 2022; Lu et al. 2021) and on-server gradient matching (Shi et al. 2022; Nguyen et al. 2025)—and transplants them into the federated unlearning setting, where they had not previously been combined. The learning-to-unlearn stage borrows the prototypical-network objective (Snell et al. 2017) and a VICReg-style hinge loss (Bardes et al. 2022) to train disentangled featurizers, while the unlearning stage inverts the invariant-gradient-direction assumption into a gradient-conflict objective. Unlike prior approximate FUL methods (FATS, FedDCP, FFMU, FUSED, MoDE) that operate on the full model or require client-side retain/forget data, FOUL restricts updates to the non-causal subnetwork and performs unlearning entirely on-server using only client gradients, avoiding the repeated subnetwork identification overhead of gradient-conflict-based pruning approaches (Khalil et al. 2025; Xu et al. 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper tackles a practically important gap: client-wise FUL where forget clients cannot access retain data, a setting most prior methods handle only implicitly. The time-to-forget metric and the domain-generalization-based evaluation protocol offer a more transparent benchmark for the field. However, the approach is validated exclusively on four domain-generalization datasets with IID client splits, leaving open whether the causal/non-causal disentanglement and gradient-conflict strategy generalize to truly heterogeneous, non-IID federated settings or to non-vision modalities. The observation that retain accuracy *improves* during unlearning (e.g., 82.84 → 92.33 on PACS) hints that forget clients may induce negative transfer, but this is not analyzed in depth.
