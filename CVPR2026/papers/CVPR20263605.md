## Problem

Recent generative models of neural network weights claim to synthesize novel, high-performing checkpoints from collections of trained models, yet it remains unclear whether they truly generalize beyond their training data or merely memorize it. This paper asks a critical but previously unexamined question: do the generated weights represent meaningfully distinct models, or are they essentially replicas and simple interpolations of the training checkpoints?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263605/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

As an empirical study of four methods (Hyper-Representations, G.pt, HyperDiffusion, P-diff), the evaluation framework takes each generated checkpoint and its nearest training checkpoint under L2 distance, then compares them across multiple axes: raw weight values (heatmaps of 64 random parameter indices), model behaviors (2D PCA-projected decision boundaries for classifiers, 3D mesh reconstructions via Marching Cubes for neural fields), and a novelty metric defined as the maximum IoU of incorrect test-set predictions (or minimum Chamfer Distance for 3D shapes). A Gaussian noise-addition baseline is constructed by perturbing training weights with amplitudes tuned so that the noise-added models match the generated models' novelty level, enabling a direct accuracy-novelty trade-off comparison.

## Method

The paper introduces a multi-faceted memorization evaluation protocol: (1) L2 distance distributions from generated checkpoints to their nearest training checkpoints are compared against training-to-training distances to quantify weight-space novelty; (2) model behaviors are visualized side-by-side (decision boundaries, 3D shapes) to confirm that weight-space proximity translates to behavioral similarity; (3) an accuracy-novelty trade-off is measured against a noise-addition baseline, showing that generative methods offer no advantage over simply adding Gaussian noise or averaging training weights. To diagnose causes, the authors scale G.pt's training data 10× (reducing memorization without accuracy loss), reinitialize layers of HyperDiffusion's training MLPs with random Kaiming weights (showing the model still memorizes, indicating it captures no meaningful structure), and apply permutation augmentations to HyperDiffusion (showing symmetry-based augmentation is insufficient and can even prevent convergence). The central finding is that all four methods produce checkpoints that are close replicas or narrow-range interpolations of training data, and that overparameterized models with limited data and underexploited structural priors (permutation and scaling symmetries) drive this memorization.

## Results

Across all four weight-generation methods, generated checkpoints are significantly closer to their nearest training checkpoint than training checkpoints are to each other: for HyperDiffusion, 94.4% of generated checkpoints have L2 distance < 10 to a training checkpoint, while any pair of training checkpoints has L2 distance > 50. For G.pt, the mean L2 distance from generated to nearest training checkpoint is 2.25, versus 3.64 between training checkpoints. Scaling G.pt's training data 10× (2.1M → 20.4M checkpoints) nearly eliminates this gap (2.78 vs. 2.70) while preserving accuracy (94.0% → 94.1%). HyperDiffusion memorizes even random weights: when all layers are reinitialized, generated checkpoints remain at mean L2 distance 0.8 from training, versus 16.0 between training checkpoints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates four generative weight-generation methods (Hyper-Representations, G.pt, HyperDiffusion, P-diff) against simple baselines: Gaussian noise addition to training weights, weight averaging, and Gaussian sampling. For G.pt and Hyper-Representations, noise-added models achieve comparable or higher accuracy than generated models at the same level of novelty (maximum error similarity to training models), meaning the generative methods offer no advantage over random perturbation. HyperDiffusion similarly shows no significant difference between generated and noise-added MLPs in Chamfer Distance to training or test shapes. P-diff is the sole exception, outperforming noise addition on the accuracy–novelty trade-off, but the paper shows this is because P-diff simply interpolates training checkpoints (its outputs match weight-averaging and Gaussian-fitting baselines in both accuracy and t-SNE weight-space geometry). Scaling G.pt's data 10× reduces memorization without degrading accuracy, while Hyper-Representations does not benefit from data scaling, and symmetry-based augmentations (permutations) actually cause HyperDiffusion to fail convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper critically re-examines four existing weight generation methods (Hyper-Representations, G.pt, HyperDiffusion, P-diff) that have been presented as capable of synthesizing novel neural network weights. Rather than proposing a new method, it departs from the prior work's evaluation paradigm—which focused solely on downstream task performance—by introducing memorization-aware metrics (nearest-neighbor L2 distance in weight space, model behavior similarity, accuracy-novelty trade-off against noise baselines). The analysis draws on the broader diffusion memorization literature and on the structural symmetries of weight space (permutation, scaling) to diagnose why these methods fail to generalize.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work challenges the foundational premise of an active research area: if current weight generation methods merely replicate or interpolate training checkpoints, the motivation of producing novel, high-performing weights without gradient-based optimization is substantially undermined. The identification of specific causes—limited data, overparameterization, and underuse of weight-space symmetries—points toward concrete remedies (data scaling, symmetry-aware architectures such as graph neural networks) but leaves open whether truly novel weight generation is achievable at scale. For practitioners, the finding that simple noise addition or weight averaging matches or exceeds sophisticated generative models in the accuracy-novelty trade-off is a practical warning against over-investing in current approaches.
