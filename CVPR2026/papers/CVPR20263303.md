## Problem

Current VLMs for video QA uniformly sample frames, causing two failures: (L1) information overload, where redundant frames overwhelm the model's reasoning, and (L2) insufficient key-information awareness, where the model misses semantically critical frames because it cannot identify which moments matter for a specific question. Both limitations stem from semantic sparsity—only a small subset of frames is relevant to any given query—yet no prior method combines question-aware iterative frame selection with a persistent, structured memory that accumulates evidence across rounds.

## Input / Output

REVISE takes a video (represented as L frames) and a multiple-choice question as input, but consumes frames iteratively rather than all at once: it begins with a small uniformly sampled set and then requests up to 3 specific frame indices per round (max 4 rounds), all under a global token budget K. The output is a structured response containing a <summary> block with five fixed fields (Previously seen, Observations, Hypothesis updates, Uncertainties, Reasons) plus either a <frames> request or a final <answer>. The summary-as-state is the sole information carried between rounds, replacing raw conversation history and previously seen frames.

## Method

REVISE is a multi-round agent that couples a structured summary-as-state (inspired by RNN hidden states) with adaptive frame selection: each round the VLM reads a few new frames, updates the five-field summary (P/O/H/U/R), and either requests more frames or answers, with the R field explicitly guiding the next selection. In plug-and-play mode, this wraps any frozen VLM via its API without parameter changes. For open-source models, REVISE is fine-tuned with GRPO using the EAGER reward, which has three annotation-free terms: (i) confidence gain rewarding the increase in log-odds margin between the correct and strongest alternative option after new frames are added, (ii) summary sufficiency rewarding whether the answer is recoverable from the last summary alone, and (iii) correct-and-early-stop rewarding correct answers within a small turn budget. This design concentrates evidence into single-digit frames while maintaining cross-turn coherence, achieving higher accuracy with fewer frames, rounds, and prompt tokens than uniform-sampling baselines.

## Results

On VideoEspresso, GPT-4o + REVISE achieves 48.9% average accuracy across 14 fine-grained reasoning categories using only 7.99 frames per video, a +22.5 point improvement over GPT-4o alone (26.4%). Open-source backbones also benefit: Qwen2-VL + REVISE reaches 37.8% (+9.3 over baseline) with 6.25 frames, and InternVL2 + REVISE reaches 32.1% (+3.4) with 2.87 frames. On EgoSchema, GPT-4o + REVISE scores 60.6% with 9.8 frames, and on NExT-QA it scores 63.8% with 8.4 frames. With reinforcement fine-tuning (EAGER + GRPO) on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, REVISE reaches 51.3% on NExT-QA and 27.8% on VideoEspresso using only 3.9–4.1 frames and ~1.3–1.4 rounds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On VideoEspresso, GPT-4o + REVISE (48.9%) outperforms all listed baselines including VideoEspresso's own core-frame pipeline (34.1%), mPLUG-Owl3 (28.0%), and Qwen-VL-Max (26.0%), while using far fewer frames than dense-sampling methods like LongVA-DPO (128 frames) and FPS-based approaches. On EgoSchema, GPT-4o + REVISE (60.6%, 9.8 frames) slightly edges out VideoAgent (60.2%, 8.4 frames) in accuracy while remaining in the same ultra-low-frame regime, but trails VideoTree (66.2%, 62.4 frames) and LVNet (68.2%, 12 frames) on accuracy—trading ~6–8 points for 6–18× fewer visual inputs. On NExT-QA, REVISE (63.8%, 8.4 frames) beats LVNet (61.1%, 12 frames) and marginally surpasses SeViLA (63.6%, 32 frames), but falls short of VideoTree (73.5%, 56 frames), VideoAgent (71.3%, 8.2 frames), and LLoVi (67.7%, 90 frames) on accuracy. The consistent pattern is that REVISE achieves competitive accuracy at 1/6 to 1/18 the frame budget of dense or caption-heavy pipelines, with the largest absolute gains observed when wrapping weaker open-source backbones (e.g., +9.3 for Qwen2-VL, +22.5 for GPT-4o on VideoEspresso).

## Relation to Prior Work

REVISE synthesizes two prior threads: the multi-round agent-based frame selection paradigm from VideoAgent [59] and the multi-turn RL training framework from RAGEN [62], while replacing VideoAgent's captioner-dependent pipeline with direct visual reasoning through modern VLMs. Its key conceptual departure is the 'summary-as-state' mechanism—borrowing the recurrent hidden-state idea from LSTMs—to maintain a compact, structured (P/O/H/U/R) persistent memory that is the sole information carried across rounds, distinguishing it from prior iterative agents that reprocess full conversation histories. The EAGER reward, which combines confidence gain, summary sufficiency, and early-stopping signals without requiring frame-level annotations, extends the RL fine-tuning literature for VLMs beyond outcome-only or chain-of-thought rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REVISE offers a practical, plug-and-play recipe for making any VLM (proprietary or open-source) perform sparse video reasoning with single-digit frames, which is directly deployable without retraining. The annotation-free EAGER reward lowers the barrier for RL fine-tuning of open-source VLMs on video tasks, since only answer labels are needed. However, limitations remain: performance is backbone-dependent, multi-round API calls add latency, and whole-frame selection (rather than spatial regions) caps efficiency on fine-grained localization tasks. Open questions include whether the summary-as-state formulation transfers to open-ended video generation, whether adaptive spatial cropping can further reduce token cost, and whether the approach scales to hour-long videos without degrading the persistent state.
