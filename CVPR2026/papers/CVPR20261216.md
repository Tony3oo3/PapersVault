## Problem

Current text-to-video diffusion models produce low-diversity outputs when generating multiple videos from a single prompt, collapsing to repetitive scenes, motions, and camera angles. While diversity has been studied in image generation, no prior work directly addresses diversity in the video domain, where outputs must vary along temporal and cinematic dimensions (object motion, camera movement, scene structure) in addition to visual appearance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261216/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single user text prompt and a target set size K, and produces K diverse video prompts via a Qwen2-7b-Instruct policy model. These prompts are then fed to any T2V generator (Wan2.1, CogVideoX, or black-box Veo3) to produce 81-frame video clips at 40 inference steps with guidance scale 6. The key design choice is optimizing a prompt-level policy rather than modifying the video model itself, making the approach plug-and-play across open-source and API-based generators without architectural changes or latent access.

## Method

DPP-GRPO formulates diverse video generation as set-level policy optimization, combining an L-ensemble Determinantal Point Process diversity reward with Group Relative Policy Optimization. The DPP component computes a marginal diversity gain Δ(p_i | R_q) = log det(L_φ(R_q ∪ {p_i})) − log det(L_φ(R_q)) using SBERT embeddings and a cosine-similarity kernel, enforcing diminishing returns so the first novel variant (e.g., a dolly shot) is rewarded while redundant copies yield near-zero gain. A relevance term R_rel enforces joint similarity to both the original query and curated reference variants, preventing semantic drift. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on 30K prompt-variant pairs (50 iterations), followed by GRPO reinforcement learning (~1,200 iterations) where G candidates per prompt receive group-normalized advantages from the composite reward λ_div·Δ + λ_rel·R_rel. At inference, the policy performs autoregressive rollout—each new prompt is generated conditioned on the partially constructed set—mirroring the training structure without requiring access to the curated reference sets.

## Results

On Wan2.1, DPP-GRPO achieves TCE 31.95, TIE 49.09, and VENDI 11.29, substantially outperforming the original prompts (19.76 / 39.7 / 9.2) and all baselines. On CogVideoX, the method reaches TCE 27.59, TIE 45.46, and VENDI 10.30 versus 22.21 / 40.33 / 8.10 for the base model. Diversity gains come without sacrificing quality: VideoScore VQ improves from 2.83 to 3.37 (Wan2.1) and 3.59 to 4.08 (CogVideoX), and CLIP alignment rises from 0.28 to 0.311 (Wan2.1). Inference overhead is minimal at +0.58 s (+0.67%) per video.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPP-GRPO outperforms all four baselines on every diversity metric (TCE, TIE, VENDI) across both Wan2.1 and CogVideoX. Against Promptist, the gap is largest on TCE (31.95 vs. 19.79 on Wan2.1; 27.59 vs. 23.45 on CogVideoX) and TIE (49.09 vs. 37.58), while both methods have near-identical inference overhead (0.58 s vs. 0.51 s). Against GPT-5, DPP-GRPO more than doubles TCE (31.95 vs. 16.71) and TIE (49.09 vs. 29.27) on Wan2.1, and reduces inference time from 22.42 s to 0.58 s per video. Against Prompt-A-Video, the diversity advantage is even more pronounced (TCE 31.95 vs. 15.72; TIE 49.09 vs. 24.10), and DPP-GRPO is ~19× faster at inference (0.58 s vs. 11.02 s). SPARKE, an image-domain diversity method, is compared only on computational cost: it adds 12.4% overhead per image versus 0.67% for DPP-GRPO. In the user study, DPP-GRPO scores 4.07/5 on perceived diversity versus 3.02–3.10 for the strongest baselines, and also leads on alignment (4.28 vs. 3.80–3.97).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two established tools—L-ensemble Determinantal Point Processes (for modeling set diversity via log-determinant volume) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (for value-free, groupwise RL updates)—into a novel prompt-level policy that generates diverse video prompt sets. It departs from prior diversity work in image generation (entropy-based sampling, dataset-coverage objectives, noise-injection schemes) by operating at the prompt level rather than the latent or pixel level, making it model-agnostic and applicable to black-box APIs. It also distinguishes itself from RL-based video alignment methods (VideoDPO, Flow-DPO, DanceGRPO) that optimize quality or temporal coherence, by explicitly targeting set-level diversity across cinematic dimensions (camera motion, scene layout, subject variation) as the primary objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing diverse video set generation as a first-class task and releasing a 30K-prompt benchmark, this paper provides the field with both a problem definition and evaluation infrastructure that were previously absent. The plug-and-play, prompt-level design (0.67% inference overhead) makes the approach immediately deployable across open-source and proprietary T2V systems without architectural modification. Key open questions include whether prompt-level optimization can match model-level diversity interventions for more complex temporal dynamics, how the method scales to longer or multi-shot videos, and whether the curated reference sets used during training can be replaced by self-generated references at inference time without quality loss.
