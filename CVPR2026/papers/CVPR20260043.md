## Problem

Vision foundation models such as DINOv2 produce feature maps at patch-level resolution, lacking pixel-accurate representations that embed both geometric and semantic properties of dynamic scenes. Existing dense representation methods either rely on image-based pretext tasks that ignore temporal dynamics, or on video-based action-level objectives that do not scale to dense per-pixel prediction. The paper addresses this gap by learning pixel-level features from unlabelled videos that are simultaneously geometrically grounded, semantically rich, and temporally consistent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260043/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, LILA accepts a single RGB image and outputs a dense feature map at the same spatial resolution, with per-pixel feature vectors of dimensionality 128, 192, or 256 depending on backbone size (ViT-S14, B14, L14). During training, the network consumes aspect-preserving crops of adjacent video frame pairs, while the frozen DINOv2 encoder and a trainable DPT decoder (with skip connections to four intermediate encoder blocks) produce the pixel-level outputs. The depth and optical flow networks used to generate training cues are discarded entirely at test time.

## Method

LILA's core mechanism is linear in-context learning: given a context frame, an optimal linear projection W* is solved via closed-form ridge regression to map the context feature grid to concatenated cue maps (PAMR-refined encoder features, monocular depth, and optical flow). The network is then trained so that applying the same W* to the query frame's features reconstructs the query cue maps, enforcing that the representation remains consistent under a fixed linear transformation across the temporal window. The total loss combines an L1 reconstruction term with a gradient-matching edge loss that encourages alignment of discontinuities with scene structure. Training uses noisy off-the-shelf cues from SEA-RAFT (flow) and DepthAnythingV2 (depth) on uncurated video datasets (YouTube-VOS, Kinetics-700), and the cross-frame formulation suppresses frame-specific noise that would otherwise be absorbed by standard distillation.

## Results

On DAVIS-2017 video object segmentation, LILA (DINO2-B14) achieves 70.4 JF with linear probing and 74.2 JF with local k-NN, outperforming the strongest prior method (FlowFeat) by 4.7 and 5.2 JF respectively. On NYUv2 surface normal estimation, LILA (DINO2-L14 trained on Kinetics) reduces RMSE to 24.04° (vs. 24.70° baseline). On COCO-Stuff semantic segmentation, LILA (DINO2-L14, Kinetics) reaches 63.3 mIoU and 81.4 pAcc, a +4.6 mIoU gain over the DINOv2 baseline. LILA also improves zero-shot segmentation on unseen COCO-Stuff categories (22.7 mIoU unseen vs. 19.0 baseline for B14).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LILA consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every metric and probing methodology. Against the strongest prior pixel-dense method, FlowFeat [3], LILA gains +2.8 JF (linear probing) and +6.3 JF (k-NN) on DINO2-S14, with the gap widening to +4.7/+5.2 JF on DINO2-B14. Compared to the upsampling approaches FeatUp [16] and LoftUp [21], LILA improves by +8.1 and +5.6 JF respectively under linear probing (S14), notably without requiring mask supervision (LoftUp uses SAM annotations). Against video-based pre-training methods V-JEPA [7] and VideoMAE [38], LILA leads by +19.6 and +25.3 JF (linear probing, S14), demonstrating that the in-context learning strategy with geometric cues is far more effective than contrastive or masked-reconstruction video objectives for dense pixel-level tasks. On surface normal estimation (NYUv2), LILA reduces RMSE by 0.85° over FlowFeat (B14) and by 0.66° over the DINOv2 baseline (L14+Kinetics). On semantic segmentation (COCO-Stuff), LILA improves mIoU by +2.0 over FlowFeat (B14) and +4.6 over the raw DINOv2 baseline (L14+Kinetics), with gains increasing with model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

LILA extends the DINOv2 + DPT encoder-decoder paradigm by introducing a novel training objective—linear in-context learning—that replaces the contrastive, reconstruction, or direct-distillation signals used in prior video pre-training (FlowE, PooDLe, VITO) and feature-upsampling methods (FeatUp, LoftUp). It builds directly on the authors' own FlowFeat work and adapts PAMR (Araslanov & Roth, CVPR 2020) for self-distillation cue construction, but departs from both by framing supervision as a cross-frame linear consistency constraint rather than a per-frame prediction task. The key conceptual departure is that the optimal linear projection learned on a context frame must also explain a query frame's cues, which implicitly filters temporally unstable noise from the off-the-shelf depth and flow estimates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LILA offers a practical recipe for upgrading frozen foundation-model encoders into pixel-level feature producers with temporal consistency, using only uncurated video and noisy off-the-shelf cues—no mask or geometry annotations required. The consistent 3–10% gains across VOS, surface normals, and semantic segmentation (and across S/B/L model sizes) suggest the in-context formulation is a broadly applicable training signal rather than a task-specific trick. Open questions include whether the approach generalises beyond the domains where SEA-RAFT and DepthAnythingV2 produce plausible cues (the authors note aerial imagery is confounded by shadows), whether the frozen-encoder constraint is truly necessary, and whether the linear projection can be replaced by richer mappings without losing the noise-suppression benefit.
