## Problem

Existing multimodal 3D segmentation methods rely on dual-branch architectures that inflate training costs and sacrifice image resolution, while strict cross-modal alignment is infeasible due to the inherent field-of-view mismatch between 360° LiDARs and narrow camera FOVs. Moreover, most fusion approaches require camera inputs at inference time, preventing deployment on LiDAR-only systems such as legacy vehicles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260818/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes LiDAR point clouds, RGB images (1–6 views depending on dataset), and metric depth maps (from pre-trained estimators like Depth Anything V2/UniDepth or online completion via IP-BASIC). At inference, only the LiDAR point cloud is required, producing per-point semantic class labels. The back-projected image features are injected as additional 3D points into the segmentation network's input, so the model's forward pass at test time is architecturally identical to a standard LiDAR-only pipeline.

## Method

IPFP back-projects aggregated image features into the physical 3D space so they coexist with LiDAR points within a single-branch segmentation network (e.g., PTv3 or SPVCNN). Image features are first extracted by lightweight conv+linear layers, then aggregated to depth-constrained cluster centers (sampled within the LiDAR-measured depth percentile range) using cosine-similarity-weighted neighborhood pooling. The aggregated features are lifted to 3D via the estimated depth map and camera intrinsics/extrinsics, merged with the point cloud features, and jointly processed by the backbone's spatial aggregation layers without any explicit fusion module. A stochastic discard operator (Bernoulli masking with parameters γ and δ) randomly removes subsets of back-projected features during training, forcing the network to remain robust when image information is absent at inference. This design eliminates modality-specific subnetworks, avoids BEV-space distortions, and resolves FOV misalignment by operating entirely in unified 3D space.

## Results

IPFP(PTv3) achieves 81.4±0.2 mIoU on nuScenes, 71.1±0.3 mIoU on SemanticKITTI, and 72.4±0.1 mIoU on Waymo, improving over the LiDAR-only PTv3 baseline by +1.1, +2.5, and +1.2 mIoU respectively, while still inferring with LiDAR data only. IPFP(SPVCNN) improves KITTI mIoU from 63.8 to 65.1 (+1.3). In terms of efficiency, IPFP adds only 0.01 s/iteration of training overhead (0.213 s vs. 0.196 s for the baseline) and 1.3 GiB of GPU memory, compared to dual-branch methods like PointPainting that nearly double training time (0.397 s) and memory (12.0 GiB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPFP(PTv3) outperforms all LiDAR-only baselines across every dataset: it beats PTv3* by +1.1 mIoU on nuScenes, +2.5 on KITTI, and +1.2 on Waymo, and surpasses SphereFormer (78.4/67.8/69.9) and PTv2 (80.2/70.3/70.6) by a wider margin. Against multimodal fusion methods that require camera input at inference (MSeg3D, EPMF, PMF), IPFP(PTv3) achieves higher mIoU on all three benchmarks while remaining deployable on LiDAR-only systems—for example, 81.4 vs. 80.0 (MSeg3D) on nuScenes and 72.4 vs. 69.6 (MSeg3D) on Waymo. Among LiDAR-only-inference methods (2DPASS, CMDFusion, UniDSeg), IPFP(PTv3) leads by +1.9 mIoU over 2DPASS on nuScenes, +1.8 over 2DPASS on KITTI, and +2.5 over UniDSeg on KITTI. The SPVCNN variant also improves over its LiDAR-only baseline (65.1 vs. 63.8 on KITTI), confirming the method's generality across backbone architectures. In efficiency, IPFP adds negligible overhead (0.01 s/iter, 1.3 GiB) compared to dual-branch approaches like PointPainting (0.397 s, 12.0 GiB) and 2DPASS (0.381 s, 11.7 GiB), which nearly double training time and memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

IPFP builds on the feature affinity-guided neighborhood aggregation mechanism from DVLO [30] and Image as Set of Points [37], repurposing it to generate cluster-center features from images that are then lifted into 3D via depth back-projection. It departs from the dominant dual-branch paradigm in multimodal 3D segmentation (PointPainting, PMF, MSeg3D) and from knowledge-distillation approaches (2DPASS, CMDFusion) that still require separate image and point-cloud subnetworks during training. By operating in a unified 3D physical space rather than intermediate representations like BEV or spherical projections, IPFP avoids the FOV-mismatch problem that constrains pixel-to-point alignment methods, while the stochastic masking strategy during training explicitly prepares the model for LiDAR-only inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-branch design reduces training cost (nearly identical per-iteration time to the LiDAR-only baseline, versus ~2× for dual-branch fusion) while still delivering consistent mIoU gains across three driving benchmarks, making multimodal pre-training practical for resource-constrained settings. The robustness to depth-map quality (IP-BASIC vs. Depth Anything V2 differ by <0.3 mIoU) lowers the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether the approach extends beyond driving scenes, whether unlabeled back-projected features can be exploited via unsupervised constraints as the authors suggest, and whether the 5% back-projected-to-original point ratio generalizes to denser or sparser LiDAR configurations.
