## Problem

Existing self-supervised contrastive learning methods for skeleton-based action recognition rely on binary positive/negative objectives that enforce rigid class separability, fragmenting intra-class clusters and disrupting the smooth topology of the motion manifold. This binary paradigm overlooks the intrinsic continuity of human motion—where actions evolve gradually and share sub-motions—resulting in uncalibrated confidence scores and poor generalization on ambiguous or transitional behaviors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260600/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D skeleton sequences (joint trajectories over time) and decomposes them into five body parts (left-arm, right-arm, left-leg, right-leg, trunk) for local spatio-temporal manipulation. During self-supervised pretraining, no labels are required; the encoder (ST-GCN with 16 hidden channels) is trained purely on geometric alignment losses. Downstream, a frozen encoder feeds a linear probe that produces both action class labels and calibrated confidence scores, with calibration quality (ECE/AECE) reported as a first-class evaluation metric alongside accuracy.

## Method

TranCLR replaces binary contrast with a continuous geometric calibration framework built on MoCo v2. The Action Transitional Anchor Construction (ATAC) module synthesizes intermediate motion states between action pairs via either global trajectory interpolation (convex combination) or local spatio-temporal substitution (replacing body-part sub-sequences with flexible temporal scaling), dynamically selecting between the two per pair. The Multi-Level Geometric Manifold Calibration (MGMC) mechanism then enforces topological consistency at three levels: intra-sample (aligning anchor embeddings with interpolated parent embeddings to preserve local continuity), inter-sample (bridging distinct action classes as continuous semantic distances), and cross-anchor (imposing geometric constraints among anchors sharing parent samples to refine global manifold structure). All alignment losses use a soft KL-divergence objective with asymmetric temperatures over top-K neighbors from a memory queue, rather than hard InfoNCE, to avoid training instability from conflicting calibration signals.

## Results

On NTU RGB+D (60), 3s-TranCLR achieves 88.5% average linear evaluation accuracy (86.3% X-Sub, 90.7% X-View), surpassing the strongest baseline 3s-ActCLR (86.6%) by 1.9%. On the more challenging NTU RGB+D 120, 3s-TranCLR reaches 78.9% average (78.8% X-Sub, 79.0% X-Set), a 3.9-point gain over 3s-ActCLR (75.0%). On PKU-MMD, 3s-TranCLR sets new state-of-the-art at 92.8% (Part I) and 59.9% (Part II). In transfer learning to PKU-MMD Part II, TranCLR pretrained on NTU-60 achieves 65.6%, and calibration error (ECE) drops to 0.65% on NTU-120 X-Set versus 5.63% for ActCLR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TranCLR consistently outperforms all 26 baselines across every evaluation protocol. Against the strongest contrastive baseline 3s-ActCLR [25], TranCLR gains +1.9% average on NTU-60 (88.5% vs 86.6%) and +3.9% on NTU-120 (78.9% vs 75.0%), with the largest single-metric gap of +5.3% on NTU-120 X-Sub (74.3% vs 69.0% for single-stream). On PKU-MMD Part II, 3s-TranCLR (59.9%) surpasses 3s-ActCLR (55.8%) by 4.1% and 3s-Skeleton-logoCLR [20] (57.7%) by 2.2%. In transfer learning, TranCLR (65.6%) beats Heter-Skeleton [48] (64.3%) and 3s-ActCLR+ [26] (62.1%) on PKU-MMD Part II. For action retrieval, TranCLR leads on NTU-60 X-Sub (74.6% vs 70.6% for CMD [34]) and NTU-120 X-Set (62.5% vs 60.9% for CMD), though it trails Heter-Skeleton on NTU-60 X-View (83.9% vs 87.1%). Calibration is the most dramatic win: ECE drops from 5.63% (ActCLR) to 0.65% on NTU-120 X-Set, an ~88% relative reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

TranCLR builds directly on the MoCo v2 momentum-contrastive framework and the ST-GCN encoder, but departs from the binary positive/negative contrastive objective that defines prior skeleton-based contrastive methods (SkeletonCLR, AimCLR, ActCLR). Its core conceptual move—introducing interpolated 'transitional anchors' as manifold regularizers, inspired by Mixup, and calibrating their geometric relationships across three levels (intra-sample, inter-sample, cross-anchor)—repurposes data-augmentation ideas as structural constraints on the embedding space rather than merely as additional training pairs. The soft alignment via top-K KL divergence with asymmetric temperatures borrows from knowledge distillation to stabilize the multi-level calibration, distinguishing it from the hard InfoNCE losses used throughout prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 88% relative reduction in Expected Calibration Error (5.63% → 0.65% on NTU-120 X-Set) is the most practically significant finding, as reliable uncertainty estimation is critical for deploying skeleton action models in safety-sensitive settings like medical rehabilitation or human-robot interaction, yet calibration has been almost entirely unexplored in this subfield. The paper also honestly acknowledges a trade-off: softening class boundaries for manifold continuity costs some peak retrieval precision, suggesting that the continuous-manifold paradigm may not be universally superior to rigid separation. Open questions include whether the transitional-anchor and multi-level calibration principles transfer to other modalities (video, point clouds) or to larger action taxonomies where the combinatorics of anchor pairs become prohibitive, and whether the gains persist under deeper encoders or larger-scale pretraining.
