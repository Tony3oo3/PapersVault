## Problem

Existing no-reference image quality assessment (NR-IQA) models fail to simultaneously satisfy four practical requirements: operating at arbitrary resolutions, preserving original-resolution quality cues, maintaining budget-adaptive computational cost, and being jointly trainable on multiple heterogeneous IQA datasets with mismatched MOS scales. Most SOTA methods either discard fine-grained quality cues via aggressive resizing, scale poorly to ultra-high-resolution inputs, or are locked to a single dataset's perceptual scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260692/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an RGB image at any resolution and produces a single scalar quality score. Internally, it samples fixed-size patches (matching CLIP's pretraining resolution) from the original image and two resized variants (short dimensions 512 and 224), then at test time encodes only the top-k most important patches per scale, where k is set by the available compute budget. This makes inference cost directly tunable: for a 3840×2560 UHD image, only 48 of 748 original-resolution candidate patches need encoding to reach peak performance.

## Method

ReLIQS is a CLIP-based multiscale patch-driven architecture with three core components. A Perceptual Importance Estimator (PIE), built on TinyCLIP ViT-8M with a shallow convolutional decoder, predicts a dense IQA-specific saliency map from the lowest-resolution variant, which is upsampled to all scales to yield per-patch importance weights. A Latent Quality Axis Module (LQAM) maintains A=4 learnable quality direction pairs and, via learned axis queries with cosine-similarity attention, assigns each scale a distribution over latent quality axes and computes axis-specific scores in [0,1], which are then adaptively combined into the final prediction. Training jointly optimizes a margin-ranking loss and a PLCC loss across multiple datasets using uncertainty-based loss weighting, enabling the model to learn from heterogeneous MOS scales without requiring cross-dataset calibration.

## Results

In the single-dataset setting (trained on KonIQ-10K), ReLIQS achieves the best or near-best PLCC/SRCC across 7 of 8 benchmarks, reaching 0.958/0.949 on KonIQ-10K, 0.891/0.894 on SPAQ, 0.842/0.818 on CSIQ, and 0.879/0.894 on LIVE. On the UHD high-resolution benchmark, ReLIQS achieves 0.837/0.865 PLCC/SRCC at 543 GMACs, outperforming the best dedicated UHD model (SJTU, 0.799/0.846) and MLLM baselines (DeQA 0.654/0.701, Q-Align 0.627/0.683) by a large margin. A compute-adaptive variant (ReLIQS*) reduces cost to 47 GMACs while still achieving 0.824/0.847 on UHD. The only benchmark where ReLIQS trails is AGIQA-3K (0.768/0.705 vs. DeQA's 0.809/0.729), attributed to AIGC-specific distribution shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReLIQS outperforms all CNN-based baselines (NIQE, BRISQUE, MANIQA, DBCNN, NIMA, HyperIQA, CLIP-IQA+) by a wide margin across every benchmark, with the largest gaps on FLIVE (0.654 vs. 0.561 SRCC for NIMA) and CSIQ (0.842 vs. 0.772 PLCC for CLIP-IQA+). Against the transformer-based MUSIQ, ReLIQS wins on all 8 benchmarks, most notably on KADID (0.701 vs. 0.575 PLCC) and LIVE (0.879 vs. 0.722 PLCC). Versus the strongest MLLM baselines, ReLIQS beats Q-Align on 7 of 8 benchmarks (e.g., KonIQ-10K 0.958 vs. 0.941 PLCC; FLIVE 0.654 vs. 0.554 SRCC) and beats DeQA on 7 of 8 (e.g., KonIQ-10K 0.958 vs. 0.953 PLCC; LIVE 0.879 vs. 0.809 PLCC), while losing only on AGIQA-3K (0.768 vs. 0.809 PLCC for DeQA). On the UHD high-resolution benchmark, ReLIQS (0.837/0.865) surpasses all baselines including dedicated UHD models SJTU (0.799/0.846) and GS-PIQA (0.793/0.830), while MLLM methods degrade severely (DeQA 0.654/0.701, Q-Align 0.627/0.683) due to aggressive resizing. In the multi-dataset setting (Table 2), ReLIQS matches or exceeds C2Score, Q-Align, and DeQA on most benchmarks and clearly outperforms UNIQUE and LIQE, though the margins are smaller than in the single-dataset setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReLIQS synthesizes ideas from three distinct threads in IQA: (1) CLIP-based encoders (CLIP-IQA+, LIQE) repurposed here as fixed-size patch encoders rather than full-image encoders, sidestepping the resolution-mismatch problem; (2) multi-scale IQA (MUSIQ) reimagined with independent patch encoding to avoid quadratic transformer complexity at high resolutions; and (3) saliency-guided pooling, but with a key departure—instead of using visual saliency maps, the paper learns an IQA-specific importance field end-to-end via MOS supervision, primarily to control compute rather than to improve accuracy. The multi-dataset training objective (margin-ranking + PLCC with uncertainty weighting) departs from the standard L1/L2 regression losses that tie models to a single subjective study's MOS scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper articulates four practical requirements (resolution-agnosticism, low-level cue preservation, budget-adaptive compute, multi-dataset trainability) that individually are natural but collectively unmet by any prior SOTA method, and demonstrates a single architecture satisfying all four. The UHD results are particularly impactful: MLLM-based SOTA methods (Q-Align, DeQA) drop sharply on ultra-high-resolution content due to aggressive resizing, while ReLIQS's patch-based design preserves fine-grained cues and sets a new SOTA. A notable limitation is underperformance on AGIQA-3K, suggesting CLIP's pretraining distribution may not cover AIGC-specific artifacts well. Open questions include whether the latent quality axes develop interpretable semantics, whether the approach extends to video or 3D content, and whether the saliency module can be further compressed for edge deployment.
