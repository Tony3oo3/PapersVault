## Problem

Existing parsing-based gait recognition methods rely on CNNs or standard GCNs, which model only pairwise relationships between body parts or frames and thus fail to capture the complex, non-linear, higher-order correlations inherent in gait parsing sequences. This underutilization of the rich part-level and temporal information in parsing limits recognition accuracy in challenging real-world scenarios such as occlusion and extreme viewing angles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263905/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a fixed-length sequence of 30 gait parsing frames (each H×W with pixel-level body-part labels from 5 coarse or 11 fine categories) as its sole input, processed through a ResNet9 backbone (channels [64,128,256,512]) to yield frame-level feature maps of size c×h×w. The output is a single gait feature vector produced by concatenating three branch features (global, spatial hypergraph, temporal hypergraph) and passing them through fully connected layers with batch normalization, used for sequence-to-sequence matching via cosine similarity in both training and inference.

## Method

HyperGait employs a three-branch architecture over shared backbone features: a Global Head (temporal pooling + horizontal pyramid pooling from GaitSet), a Spatial Hypergraph Convolution Module (SHCM), and a Temporal Hypergraph Convolution Module (THCM). SHCM extracts part-level feature vectors via parsing-mask pooling, computes pairwise Euclidean distances, and constructs a spatial hypergraph whose incidence matrix connects parts below an adaptive threshold τ = min(τ₀, 1.5·mean(D)); hypergraph convolution then propagates information vertex→hyperedge→vertex using learnable weight matrices and degree-normalized incidence matrices. THCM divides the sequence into K=10 segments, selects the middle frame per segment, and builds a temporal hypergraph via a k-NN (k=3) strategy rather than a distance threshold, then applies the same hypergraph convolution to capture higher-order temporal dependencies. The key architectural insight is that hyperedges connecting an arbitrary number of vertices naturally encode multi-way spatial and temporal interactions that pairwise GCN edges cannot represent, and the combination of spatial and temporal hypergraph convolutions yields a 4.8% Rank-1 gain over the baseline, outperforming the analogous GCN combination by 2.4%.

## Results

On the Gait3D dataset, HyperGait achieves 80.5% Rank-1, 91.4% Rank-5, 72.7% mAP, and 54.3% mINP, representing the best performance among single-representation gait recognition methods. On SUSTech1K, HyperGait reaches 79.9% Rank-1 and 93.0% Rank-5, outperforming the strongest parsing-based competitor MultiGait^P by 2.6% in Rank-1. The method uses only gait parsing sequence as input, with a ResNet9 backbone and two hypergraph convolution modules (spatial and temporal) operating in parallel with a Global Head.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HyperGait outperforms all 15 compared baselines on both datasets. On Gait3D, it surpasses the closest parsing-based competitors—MultiGait^P (76.4%) and PSGait (76.2%)—by 4.1% and 4.3% in Rank-1, respectively, and leads ParsingGait (76.2%) by 4.3%. The gap over silhouette-based methods is even larger: GaitSet (36.7%) and GaitPart (28.2%) trail by over 40 and 52 Rank-1 points. On SUSTech1K, HyperGait (79.9% Rank-1) beats MultiGait^P (77.3%) by 2.6% and GaitHeat (76.7%) by 3.2%, while also showing the strongest per-condition performance in clothing change (39.6% vs. 29.4% for MultiGait^P) and nighttime (53.2% vs. 43.3%). Notably, HyperGait achieves these gains using only parsing as input, whereas some competitors (e.g., MultiGait, PSGait) leverage multiple gait modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

HyperGait builds directly on the parsing-based gait recognition paradigm established by ParsingGait (which introduced gait parsing sequences and GCN-based part modeling) and on GaitSet's Horizontal Pyramid Pooling for global feature aggregation. Its key conceptual departure is replacing pairwise graph edges with multi-way hyperedges, motivated by the observation that body parts and temporal frames exhibit higher-order (non-pairwise) correlations that standard GCNs cannot capture. This is the first application of hypergraph convolution to gait recognition, though the hypergraph machinery itself is well-established in other vision tasks (object detection, action recognition, multi-view learning).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that higher-order spatial and temporal correlations in gait parsing sequences carry meaningful discriminative signal beyond what pairwise GCN edges can extract, providing a principled architectural upgrade for parsing-based gait recognition. However, the hypergraph construction remains relatively simple (fixed threshold for spatial, kNN for temporal), and the approach has only been validated on two datasets with a fixed 30-frame input. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned hypergraph construction could yield further gains, whether the technique transfers to other gait representations (skeleton, optical flow), and whether the modest GCN-to-HGCN gap (0.5–1.1%) would widen under more challenging conditions or larger-scale training.
