## Problem

Existing 4D human-scene capture systems rely on expensive multi-camera rigs, wearable motion suits, or LiDAR scanners confined to controlled studio environments, making large-scale collection of scene-conditioned human motion data in the wild impractical. This bottleneck limits the training of embodied AI agents that require physically grounded, scene-aware human behavior data from diverse real-world settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262868/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes two synchronized moving iPhone RGB-D videos of a performer plus a single iPhone RGB-D video of the static scene (with IMU data) as input. Depth maps are truncated at 3.5 m (indoor) or 5 m (outdoor) based on iPhone LiDAR effective range. The output is a unified metric Z-up world frame containing a dense scene mesh, per-frame SMPL parameters (shape β∈ℝ¹⁰, pose θ∈ℝ⁷², root translation γ∈ℝ³), calibrated camera trajectories, and triangulated 3D human keypoints — all scene-consistent and gravity-aligned.

## Method

The pipeline proceeds in four stages: (1) a single iPhone RGB-D sequence is processed by SpectacularAI SDK for metric camera poses, PromptDA for depth refinement, and TSDF fusion to produce a dense scene mesh plus a COLMAP sparse structure; (2) dual-view sequences are processed with SAI for per-frame cameras and off-the-shelf models (YOLO, ViTPose, SAM2, PromptDA, VIMO) for human priors, synchronized via a laser-pointer cue; (3) dual-view trajectories are aligned to the scene frame by first initializing rigid offsets via COLMAP registration, then jointly optimizing per-view yaw and translation under a composite loss of VGGT-based dual-view point tracking, Chamfer distance to the global mesh, and bundle adjustment reprojection; (4) dual-view 2D keypoints are triangulated into 3D via weighted least squares, and SMPL parameters are refined in world coordinates through a two-stage SMPLify optimizing 3D keypoint, smoothness, prior, and reprojection losses. The key insight is that dual-view pixel-wise dense correspondence resolves the depth ambiguity inherent in single-view capture, enabling automatic metric-scale scene localization of human trajectories without markers or static cameras.

## Results

On the optical mocap studio benchmark (9,420 frames, 5 sequences), the proposed dual-view method achieves 56.61 mm WA-MPJPE and 72.86 mm W-MPJPE at chunk=100, with RTE of 1.13 m, substantially outperforming the GVHMR monocular baseline (66.56 / 123.44 mm, RTE 1.85 m) and single-view optimization (108–125 / 212–218 mm, RTE 2.7–3.7 m). The advantage grows with sequence length: at chunk=1000, dual-view reaches 119.45 / 169.11 mm versus 179.47 / 593.79 mm for GVHMR. Downstream, fine-tuning π³ and VIMO on the EmbodMocap dataset improves EMDB W-MPJPE from 229.04 to 220.65 mm and RTE from 1.78% to 1.71%, while physics-based skill training on EmbodMocap data matches or exceeds optical mocap on most tasks and dramatically outperforms monocular estimation on the high-difficulty Support skill (66.0% vs. 20.6% success rate).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is GVHMR [53], a state-of-the-art monocular human motion recovery model. The proposed dual-view method outperforms GVHMR on every metric and chunk length: at chunk=100, dual-view achieves 56.61 mm WA-MPJPE vs. GVHMR's 66.56 mm (15% reduction) and 72.86 mm W-MPJPE vs. 123.44 mm (41% reduction); at chunk=1000 the gap widens dramatically to 119.45 vs. 179.47 mm (WA) and 169.11 vs. 593.79 mm (W), with RTE of 1.13 m vs. 1.85 m. The paper also ablates against its own single-view variants (V1, V2), which perform worse than even GVHMR at longer chunks, confirming that the dual-view geometric constraint is essential for resolving depth ambiguity. In downstream physics-based skill training (Table 4), EmbodMocap data matches optical mocap on most skills (e.g., 99.8% vs. 99.9% success for Follow) and significantly outperforms monocular GVHMR-estimated motions, particularly on the high-difficulty Support task where monocular drops to 20.6% success versus 66.0% for EmbodMocap. For monocular reconstruction fine-tuning (Table 3), the improvements over the unfrozen baseline are modest (W-MPJPE 229.04→220.65 mm), suggesting the dataset's value is more pronounced in physics-based and robot control applications than in narrow feed-forward reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the line of 4D human-scene capture datasets (PROX, RICH, EgoBody, EMDB, SPLOPER4D, Nymeria) that rely on expensive multi-camera rigs, mocap suits, or LiDAR scanners, and departs from them by demonstrating that two moving iPhones with joint geometric calibration can achieve comparable metric-scale, scene-consistent reconstruction. On the reconstruction side, it builds on feedforward models like VIMO and π³ as baselines and fine-tunes them on its paired data, following the trend toward joint human-scene recovery (Human3R, JOSH, Crisp). For downstream applications, it leverages established physics-based RL frameworks (PPO, MimicKit, BeyondMimic) and sim-to-real pipelines, treating its contribution as a data-quality enabler rather than a new control algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing the hardware cost from $20K–$60K+ to roughly $1K and eliminating the need for static cameras, markers, or studio environments, EmbodMocap opens the door to large-scale, diverse, in-the-wild 4D human-scene data collection that was previously infeasible. The dual-view design's ability to resolve depth ambiguity (achieving ~5 cm scene alignment vs. >30 cm for single-view) is a practical enabler for training scene-aware embodied agents. Open questions include scalability to multi-person scenes, very long outdoor sequences, and whether the slight quality gap versus optical mocap (visible in Table 2) becomes problematic for high-precision tasks like the Support skill, where success rates remain modest (~66%).
