## Problem

VideoLLMs frequently generate descriptions that are temporally inconsistent or causally implausible (temporal hallucination), yet this issue remains underexplored compared to spatial hallucination. Existing training-free mitigation methods such as DINO-HEAL and TCD rely on spatial saliency or frame-dropping, which fail to capture event causality and temporal ordering, leaving a gap for a method that can adaptively suppress both temporal and spatial hallucinations without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a video sampled as 8 frames plus a free-form text query and produces a hallucination-mitigated text response at inference time with no parameter updates. Internally, the vision encoder processes the original video alongside two constructed negatives (a spatial negative via Gaussian noise and a temporal negative via layer-wise feature homogenization), and the text decoder's multi-head attention at layers 20–23 is queried per token to compute frame-level attention distributions that drive the adaptive decoding weights.

## Method

SEASON is a training-free contrastive decoding framework with two core mechanisms. First, Temporal Homogenization constructs a temporally-hard negative by computing a layer-wise mean of frame features in the vision encoder and progressively re-injecting this global context into each frame's representation (controlled by β), yielding a video that is spatially consistent but temporally incoherent—ensuring the contrastive signal isolates temporal spurious correlations rather than trivial spatial corruption. Second, a Self-Diagnostic Mechanism extracts the frame-level attention distribution of the preceding token under the original, spatial, and temporal negatives, computes Jensen-Shannon divergences to estimate each token's spatial vs. temporal hallucination tendency, and derives adaptive weights (w_S, w_T). The final logit distribution is then computed as a weighted contrastive combination: (1+α)·logits(v^O) − α·[w_S·logits(v^S) + w_T·logits(v^T)], dynamically penalizing the hallucination type most relevant to each generated token.

## Results

SEASON achieves the best training-free performance across all three hallucination examination benchmarks (VidHalluc, VideoHallucer, EventHallusion) for every backbone tested. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, SEASON improves the overall VidHalluc score by +5.3% over the base model and +5.6% over the training-based ArrowRL baseline, with the largest gains on the temporal hallucination subtask (TSH: +18.7% over base). Crucially, these hallucination reductions come without sacrificing general video understanding: SEASON improves TempCompass by +1.4% and TVBench by +1.2% on LLaVA-Video-7B while maintaining VideoMME and MVBench scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEASON outperforms both training-free baselines (TCD and DINO-HEAL) on all three hallucination benchmarks across every backbone, with the largest margin on VidHalluc (e.g., +5.1% over TCD and +5.2% over DINO-HEAL on Qwen2.5-VL-7B). Against training-based methods, SEASON surpasses ArrowRL on VidHalluc (+5.5% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and RRPO on EventHallusion (+1.0% on LLaVA-Video-7B), while matching or exceeding TPO on all hallucination metrics. On temporal understanding (TempCompass, TVBench), SEASON consistently improves over the base model and all baselines, indicating that hallucination suppression does not degrade temporal reasoning. On conventional video understanding (VideoMME, MVBench), SEASON maintains or slightly improves scores, confirming that the contrastive penalties do not over-suppress correct tokens. The only case where SEASON trails a training-based method is EventHallusion on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (66.0 vs. ArrowRL's 69.0), which the authors attribute to the Yes/No VQA format's compliance bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEASON extends the contrastive decoding paradigm established for image hallucination mitigation (VCD, OPERA, MARINE) to the video domain, where prior training-free methods like DINO-HEAL and TCD struggle with temporal causality. Its key departure is the construction of a 'temporally-hard' negative via layer-wise temporal homogenization in the vision encoder, which preserves spatial structure while destroying temporal coherence—unlike Gaussian noise (spatial negative) or frame shuffling/reversal, which corrupt both dimensions simultaneously. The self-diagnostic mechanism, which uses Jensen-Shannon divergence of frame-level attention to adaptively allocate spatial vs. temporal contrastive penalties per token, is a novel integration that no prior video hallucination method employs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By being training-free and model-agnostic, SEASON offers a practical drop-in solution for deploying VideoLLMs in reliability-critical settings (healthcare, autonomous driving) without retraining costs or preference data. The finding that a carefully constructed temporal negative can match or exceed training-based methods (ArrowRL, RRPO) on temporal hallucination subtasks suggests that inference-time intervention may be sufficient for temporal faithfulness, reducing the field's dependence on expensive RL or preference optimization pipelines. Open questions include scalability to longer videos beyond 8 frames, whether the self-diagnostic attention-divergence signal remains informative in very long sequences, and whether the approach generalizes to audio-visual or 3D video understanding.
