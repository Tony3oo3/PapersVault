## Problem

State-of-the-art video generative models frequently produce physically implausible content, and while prior work attributes this to insufficient physics learning during pre-training, this paper identifies that suboptimal inference strategies also contribute to the shortfall. Crucially, no prior work has explored using off-the-shelf latent world models as reward signals to improve physics plausibility at inference time, leaving a gap between the strong physics understanding demonstrated by models like VJEPA-2 and their potential utility in steering video generation.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-trained video generative models (MAGI-1 24B, Sora2, or a 5B vLDM) without any retraining, taking as input a text prompt plus either a conditioning image (I2V) or a three-second context video (V2V), and producing five-second video continuations. The latent world model VJEPA-2 is consumed as a frozen reward oracle: a sliding window of C+M frames is applied across the generated video, where context frames are encoded and future frames are predicted in latent space. The output is a single selected or guided video sample from a reward-tilted distribution, with search budgets of 8–16 particles depending on the setting.

## Method

WMReward repurposes VJEPA-2's prediction surprise as a differentiable physics plausibility reward: at each sliding-window position, the model encodes context frames, uses VJEPA-2's predictor to forecast future-frame embeddings, and computes the mean cosine distance between predicted and actual future embeddings as the reward signal. This reward is then integrated into sampling from a tilted distribution p*(x) ∝ w(x)p(x) via three schemes: (i) gradient-based guidance that adds λ∇r(x_{0|t}) to the denoising score using Tweedie's formula, (ii) Best-of-N selection over N independent samples, and (iii) a combined ∇+BoN strategy that first applies guidance to generate N candidates and then selects the highest-reward sample. The key insight is that latent world models, by predicting in compressed representation space rather than pixel space, naturally encode physical dynamics (motion, object permanence, trajectory continuity) while ignoring superficial appearance, making their prediction surprise a more effective physics proxy than pixel reconstruction (VideoMAE) or VLM judgment (Qwen-VL). The ∇+BoN combination achieves the strongest scaling with particle count, sharpening the score distribution toward high-physics-plausibility regions without requiring large guidance temperatures that would degrade the score approximation.

## Results

On the PhysicsIQ benchmark, WMReward(∇+BoN) applied to MAGI-1 achieves a V2V score of 62.0%, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art (MAGI-1 at 55.22%) by 6.78%. For I2V generation, WMReward(BoN) on Sora2 reaches 46.43%, outperforming Sora2's baseline of 42.30% by 4.13%. On VideoPhy (T2V), WMReward(∇+BoN) improves MAGI-1's physics consistency from 25.0 to 33.1 (+8.1%) and vLDM's from 28.0 to 34.9 (+6.9%). A human-preference study confirms an 11.4% win-rate improvement over baselines in physics plausibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WMReward consistently outperforms all 15 baselines on PhysicsIQ. On V2V, the headline result is MAGI-1 + WMReward(∇+BoN) at 62.0%, beating MAGI-1 alone (55.22%) by 6.78% and the prior best (Lumiere at 23.00, VideoPoet at 29.50) by a wide margin. On I2V, WMReward(BoN) lifts Sora2 from 42.30 to 46.43 (+4.13%), Wan2.2 from 38.26 to 44.39 (+6.13%), and MAGI-1 from 29.77 to 36.56 (+6.79%), all surpassing the previous SOTA. Among alternative reward signals, WMReward dominates VideoMAE (e.g., +5.14 vs +1.66 on vLDM I2V), Qwen2.5-VL (which actually degrades performance, e.g., −4.78 on MAGI-1), and Qwen3-VL (marginal +0.44 on MAGI-1), confirming that latent-space predictive surprise is a stronger physics proxy than pixel reconstruction or VLM judgment. On VideoPhy (T2V), WMReward(∇+BoN) improves MAGI-1's physics consistency from 25.0 to 33.1, surpassing all prior models including LaVIE (31.5) and VideoCrafter2 (29.7), though at a small cost to semantic adherence (54.4→44.8). The VLIPP baseline (34.6 on I2V), which uses VLM-based motion planning, is outperformed by MAGI-1 + WMReward(BoN) at 36.56.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the inference-time alignment framework for diffusion models (tilted distribution sampling via guidance and Best-of-N, as formalized by Singhal et al.) to the specific problem of physics plausibility in video generation, where prior work had focused almost exclusively on pre-training or post-training interventions to inject physics. The key conceptual departure is using a latent world model's (VJEPA-2) prediction surprise as a differentiable reward signal, rather than the VLM-based prompt rewriting or motion planning approaches that had previously explored inference-time physics improvement. The method also draws on the observation from Garrido et al. that self-supervised video JEPA models develop intuitive physics understanding, but translates this into a practical reward function for steering generative sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that physics plausibility in video generation can be substantially improved at inference time without any retraining, with a simple and broadly applicable recipe (any latent world model + any video generator + BoN/guidance search). However, the reward is text-agnostic, causing a measurable drop in semantic adherence for T2V, and VJEPA-2's physics coverage is limited (e.g., material properties like weight and friction). Open questions include developing text-conditioned or compositional physics rewards, improving search efficiency (early denoising steps produce unreliable reward estimates), and whether stronger latent world models trained on broader physics data can close the remaining gap. The practical compute overhead (~5× for guidance, linear in N for BoN) is a real constraint for deployment.
