## Problem

Existing 3D generative models rely on iso-surface fields (SDF, Flexicubes) that inherently cannot represent open, non-manifold, or fully-enclosed surfaces, and most focus on shape while neglecting the appearance and material information that is fundamentally correlated with geometry. The field lacks a representation that can both faithfully capture the full spectrum of arbitrary 3D assets and be efficiently compressed into compact latents suitable for large-scale neural generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263166/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For image-to-3D generation, the method takes a single RGB image (up to 1024×1024) and produces a fully textured 3D mesh with PBR materials (base color, metallic, roughness, opacity) at resolutions up to 1536³ in roughly 60 seconds on an H100. For shape-conditioned texture generation, it accepts an existing 3D mesh plus a reference image and synthesizes PBR texture maps natively in 3D space. The internal representation is a sparse voxel structure (O-Voxel) on a regular N×N×N grid, where a 1024³ asset is encoded into only ~9.6K latent tokens via 16× spatial downsampling.

## Method

The core contribution is O-Voxel, an omni-voxel representation that uses a Flexible Dual Grid (inspired by Dual Contouring but field-free) to encode arbitrary-topology geometry via per-voxel dual vertices, edge intersection flags, and splitting weights, while storing volumetric PBR attributes (color, metallic, roughness, opacity) aligned to surface geometry. A Sparse Compression VAE (SC-VAE) with a fully sparse-convolutional U-shaped architecture learns a compact latent space, employing non-parametric residual autoencoding shortcuts for high-ratio downsampling, an early-pruning upsampler, and ConvNeXt-style optimized residual blocks to achieve 16× spatial compression. Generation proceeds in three sequential stages—sparse structure, geometry, and material—each modeled by a ~1.3B-parameter DiT trained with flow matching, conditioned on DINOv3-L image features with AdaLN and RoPE, totaling ~4B parameters. The key distinction from prior structured-latent methods is that latents are learned directly from native 3D data rather than multi-view 2D renderings, enabling end-to-end asset generation without view-dependent post-processing.

## Results

On image-to-3D generation (Table 2), the method achieves the highest alignment scores across all metrics: CLIP 0.894, CLIP-N 0.758, ULIP-2 0.477, and Uni3D 0.436, and dominates the user study with 66.5% preference (69.0% on normal maps) versus the next-best Hunyuan3D 2.1 at 13.3%. On shape reconstruction at 1024³ (Table 1), the method uses only 9.6K tokens with 16× spatial downsampling, achieving MD 0.0042×10⁻⁶, F1 0.971, PSNR 43.11 dB, and LPIPS 0.005 on Toys4K—substantially outperforming SparseFlex 1024 (225K tokens, MD 0.3132, PSNR 37.34) and TRELLIS (9.6K tokens, MD 85.07, PSNR 30.29). Inference runs in ~3 s (512³), ~17 s (1024³), and ~60 s (1536³) on an H100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method wins decisively against all eight baselines. In shape reconstruction (Table 1), it outperforms Dora [3] by orders of magnitude on MD (0.0042 vs. 360.8 at 4.1K tokens) and PSNR (43.11 vs. 27.32), and beats TRELLIS [58] by ~20,000× on MD (0.0042 vs. 85.07) despite using the same 9.6K token budget, thanks to its 16× vs. 4× spatial compression. SparseFlex [17] achieves the closest reconstruction quality (PSNR 37.34 at 1024³) but requires 225K tokens—23× more—and 10× longer decode time. Direct3D-S2 [57] lags on both MD and PSNR at matched resolutions. In image-to-3D generation (Table 2), the method surpasses Hunyuan3D 2.1 [19] by +0.025 CLIP, +0.003 ULIP-2, and +0.009 Uni3D, and wins the user study by a 5:1 margin (66.5% vs. 13.3% preference). Step1X-3D [28] and Direct3D-S2 [57] trail by 0.019 and 0.057 on ULIP-2 respectively. Hi3DGen [63] is the weakest generation baseline (ULIP-2 0.395, Uni3D 0.373). For PBR texture generation (Section 4.3), the method qualitatively outperforms both Hunyuan3D-Paint [19] (multi-view, suffers from ghosting and view inconsistencies) and TEXGen [64] (UV-based, suffers from seam artifacts), producing sharper textures with consistent shape-material alignment including internal surfaces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the structured 3D latent paradigm introduced by TRELLIS [58] by replacing its multi-view 2D-image-derived latents with a native 3D representation (O-Voxel) learned directly from mesh and PBR data. The O-Voxel geometry encoding adapts the Dual Contouring [21] dual-grid and QEF formulation into a field-free, optimization-free structure, while the Sparse Compression VAE borrows the residual autoencoding principle from DC-AE [1] and the flexible quad-splitting rule from Flexicubes [48]. The key departure is from iso-surface field representations (SDF, Flexicubes) that underpin most prior sparse-voxel methods, which inherently cannot represent open, non-manifold, or fully-enclosed surfaces without lossy preprocessing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16× spatial compression (≈9.6K tokens for a 1024³ fully-textured asset) combined with substantially better reconstruction and generation quality than prior methods at comparable or lower token counts shifts the efficiency–fidelity frontier for 3D generative modeling. By handling arbitrary topology and PBR materials natively in 3D space, the approach eliminates the multi-view baking and texture-alignment pipelines that plague current systems, enabling end-to-end asset generation in under a minute at 1536³. Open questions include whether the representation generalizes to full scenes (not just single assets), how it scales to even higher resolutions or more complex material models (e.g., subsurface scattering), and whether the 4B-parameter flow-matching models can be distilled for on-device deployment.
