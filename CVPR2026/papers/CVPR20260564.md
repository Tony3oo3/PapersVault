## Problem

GRPO-based reinforcement learning for visual text generation is unstable when optimizing multiple conflicting rewards (text accuracy, text-image alignment, aesthetics) via naive weighted-sum scalarization, which produces contradictory optimization signals and suboptimal convergence. Additionally, prompt selection for RL training remains unsolved: large datasets are computationally expensive while small ones underperform, and mixing diverse prompts for multi-reward optimization creates a complex landscape that slows learning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a descriptive text prompt (generated by Gemini 2.5 from source images) specifying the scene and a target text string to render, along with glyph and positional conditions inherited from the AnyText base model. It produces a single RGB image with the specified text rendered within the described scene. During training, each prompt is sampled 16 times with 50 denoising steps (no classifier-free guidance), and three reward models (CLIP ViT-B/32, EasyOCR with NED, HPS-v2.1) score each generated image to form a K-dimensional reward vector used for Pareto sorting.

## Method

POCA replaces the standard GRPO weighted-sum advantage with a bi-directional Pareto sorting mechanism: within each group of G sampled outputs, it identifies the non-dominated set (positive samples) and the fully dominated set (negative samples, obtained by applying non-dominated sorting to negated rewards), then assigns zero advantage to all other samples so only Pareto-relevant signals drive the policy gradient. For data selection, POCA introduces an adaptive curriculum planner that uses the OCR reward as a difficulty measurer, computes the ECDF of mean OCR scores across the full dataset to rank prompts, and partitions them into easy/medium/hard bins at fixed percentile thresholds (0.7 and 0.3). A training scheduler then assigns stage-specific mixture ratios (e.g., 0.6/0.3/0.1 → 0.4/0.5/0.1 → 0.1/0.6/0.3 for easy/medium/hard) across three stages, re-evaluating difficulty at each stage boundary with the current policy to keep the curriculum coupled to model growth. The entire pipeline is applied on top of AnyText via LoRA (rank 128) for 300 training steps on the 20k-image POCA-20k dataset.

## Results

On the AnyText-benchmark (English), POCA achieves 0.7651 sentence accuracy, 0.8983 NED, 0.8985 CLIP score, and 0.2694 HPS score, surpassing the GRPO baseline (0.7246 Sen.ACC, 0.8970 CLIP) and the base AnyText model (0.7041 Sen.ACC, 0.8827 CLIP) across all metrics. For Chinese prompts, POCA reaches 0.6942 Sen.ACC, 0.8696 NED, 0.8170 CLIP, and 0.2653 HPS. Notably, POCA outperforms the SOTA AnyText2 on CLIP (0.8985 vs 0.8941) and HPS (0.2694 vs 0.2497) for English, demonstrating superior text-image alignment and aesthetic quality, though it trails AnyText2 on raw text accuracy (0.7651 vs 0.8122 Sen.ACC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

POCA outperforms the GRPO baseline (weighted-sum scalarization) on English Sen.ACC (0.7651 vs 0.7246) and CLIP (0.8985 vs 0.8970), confirming that Pareto-guided selection resolves conflicting reward signals that destabilize standard GRPO. Against the SOTA AnyText2†, POCA wins on CLIP (0.8985 vs 0.8941) and HPS (0.2694 vs 0.2497) for English, and on NED (0.8696 vs 0.8488), CLIP (0.8170 vs 0.8102), and HPS (0.2653 vs 0.2486) for Chinese, but trails on raw text accuracy (Sen.ACC 0.7651 vs 0.8122 English; 0.6942 vs 0.7171 Chinese). POCA substantially surpasses the base AnyText model on all four English metrics (e.g., Sen.ACC +6.1%, CLIP +1.6%) and all four Chinese metrics. Compared to earlier methods (ControlNet, TextDiffuser, GlyphControl), POCA leads by a wide margin on every reported metric, with the largest gaps in Chinese Sen.ACC (0.6942 vs 0.0454 for GlyphControl) and English HPS (0.2694 vs 0.2257 for ControlNet). The ablation in Table 2 further shows that POCA-1k (trained on only 1k prompts) nearly matches the Pareto-guided method trained on 10k prompts, highlighting strong data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based reinforcement learning framework for diffusion models (as in DanceGRPO [44] and DeepSeek-R1 [8]) by replacing the standard weighted-sum reward aggregation with a bi-directional Pareto-optimal set selection, building directly on PARROT [16] but adding a negative (fully dominated) set for symmetric credit assignment. It further introduces an adaptive curriculum planning strategy—using ECDF-based difficulty ranking of prompts via OCR reward—into the multi-reward RL training loop, a component absent from prior GRPO-based visual generation work. The key conceptual departure from prior multi-reward RL methods is treating conflicting reward signals as a multi-objective optimization problem rather than a scalarization problem, thereby avoiding the need to manually balance reward weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

POCA addresses a practical pain point in multi-reward RL for visual text generation: the instability and suboptimal convergence caused by naive reward scalarization, offering a principled alternative that eliminates weight-tuning. The curriculum strategy also tackles the open question of prompt selection for RL training, showing that a small, difficulty-ordered subset (1k prompts) can approach the performance of a 10k static dataset, which has direct implications for training cost. Limitations include evaluation on a single base model family (AnyText/SDXL), a fixed set of three reward models, and only 300 training steps; whether the Pareto-guided approach scales to more than three objectives or to flow-matching architectures remains open.
