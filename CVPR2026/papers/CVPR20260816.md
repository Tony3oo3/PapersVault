## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) in text-to-image diffusion models relies on a semantically vacuous null prompt as the negative condition, creating a large semantic gap that produces geometrically entangled guidance signals. This entanglement conflates content correction with style and structure, leading to well-documented failures in compositional tasks such as text rendering, attribute binding, and spatial relationships. The paper asks how to construct a negative condition that adaptively degrades the core semantics of the positive prompt in a principled, training-free manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image, operating entirely within the existing diffusion model inference pipeline. No external models, additional training, or auxiliary inputs are required; the only modification is a one-time mask computation on the text encoder's token embeddings at the first denoising step. The method is plug-and-play across architectures (SD3, SD3.5, FLUX.1-dev, Qwen-Image) and introduces only ~3.6% computational overhead at the default setting, where the mask reduces to a simple 'replace all content tokens' operation.

## Method

CDG replaces CFG's null condition with a semantically degraded condition c_deg constructed via stratified degradation. The key structural observation is that transformer text encoder tokens split into content tokens (fine-grained object semantics) and context-aggregating tokens (coarse-grained global context); Weighted PageRank applied to the self-attention graph quantifies this importance dichotomy. A unified Degradation Ratio R_deg ∈ [0,2] controls the degradation: R_deg ∈ [0,1] removes content tokens first, and R_deg ∈ (1,2] then removes context-aggregating tokens, with R_deg = 1.0 as the default boundary. The degraded condition is formed by masked interpolation between the original embedding and the null embedding, reframing guidance from 'good vs. null' to 'good vs. almost good.' Geometric analysis shows this enables common-mode rejection: the semantically close pair cancels shared normal-space components, yielding guidance signals with near-perfect orthogonality to the denoising manifold and minimal interference energy.

## Results

On MS-COCO 2017, CDG consistently outperforms CFG across all four backbones: on SD3 it reduces FID from 35.69 to 34.05 and raises VQA Score from 91.44 to 92.40; on SD3.5 it lowers FID from 34.56 to 33.07 and lifts VQA from 91.94 to 92.61; on FLUX.1 it cuts FID from 38.55 to 37.11; and on Qwen-Image it drops FID from 42.45 to 39.02. On GenAI-Bench (SD3.5), CDG achieves the strongest gains on compositional tasks requiring subtle semantic contrasts: Differentiation improves by +3.64 (75.10→78.74) and Comparison by +2.36 (73.70→76.06) over CFG. CDG also adds only +3.6% inference overhead (5.655 s vs. 5.456 s for CFG) with a one-time mask computation strategy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDG outperforms all seven baselines on every model tested. Against the closest competitor DNP (VLM-generated negatives), CDG achieves lower FID on SD3 (34.05 vs. 34.68) and substantially higher VQA (92.40 vs. 89.65), confirming that semantically structured degradation beats externally generated negatives. CADS (Gaussian noise on conditions) and ICG (random prompts) remain near CFG levels or slightly worse, validating that unstructured perturbations fail to isolate semantic corrections. Structure-level methods PAG and SEG perform worst across all models (e.g., PAG FID 50.60 on SD3 vs. CDG's 34.05), demonstrating that manipulating attention or energy curvature without semantic awareness is far less effective than input-level condition degradation. On GenAI-Bench, CDG's advantage is most pronounced on Differentiation (+3.64 over CFG on SD3.5) and Comparison (+2.36), tasks requiring fine-grained semantic contrast where the 'good vs. almost good' paradigm excels. Improvements are smaller on FLUX.1 (FID 37.11 vs. 38.55) because its Guidance Distillation training reduces reliance on inference-time guidance, yet CDG still wins on all four metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) by replacing the semantically vacuous null prompt with a strategically degraded condition, positioning itself against two existing camps: process-rectification methods (APG, TCFG) that keep the c-vs-∅ contrast but apply geometric post-hoc corrections, and negative-reframing methods (Autoguidance, PAG, SEG, ICG, CADS, SFG, DNP) that explore alternative negatives. The key departure is the 'stratified degradation' principle: exploiting a functional dichotomy between content tokens and context-aggregating tokens in transformer text encoders to construct a semantically close negative, rather than using random prompts, Gaussian noise, or external VLM-generated negatives. Weighted PageRank serves as an analytical tool to identify the content/context boundary, though ablations show the stratification structure matters more than the specific ranking algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CDG provides a training-free, plug-and-play module with negligible overhead that consistently improves compositional accuracy across diverse architectures (SD3, SD3.5, FLUX, Qwen-Image), and the geometric 'common-mode rejection' analysis offers a principled explanation for why semantically close negatives outperform the null prompt. However, improvements are modest in absolute terms (1–3 FID points, 1–2 VQA points), and the gains shrink on FLUX due to its guidance-distillation training paradigm, raising questions about how much headroom remains once models are trained to be less guidance-dependent. Open questions include whether the content/context-aggregating dichotomy generalizes to non-transformer encoders or multimodal conditioning, and whether the geometric orthogonality argument fully accounts for the observed quality gains or is a post-hoc rationalization.
