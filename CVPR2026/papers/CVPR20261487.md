## Problem

Rigid registration must simultaneously handle large initial misalignments and achieve sub-centimeter accuracy under noise and partial overlap, yet existing methods force a trade-off: feature-based filtering (e.g., MAC, MAC++) is accurate but slow, while fast RANSAC variants (e.g., TCF) sacrifice alignment quality. No prior method achieves both real-time CPU speed and state-of-the-art accuracy across indoor and outdoor benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261487/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a source point cloud with per-point normals, a target point cloud with normals, and a set of raw feature-based correspondences (e.g., from FPFH or FCGF descriptors). It outputs a single rigid transformation (3×3 rotation matrix and 3D translation vector) that aligns source to target. The pipeline is entirely CPU-based (C++/PCL/Eigen), requiring no GPU, and operates on point clouds of varying density and overlap (10%–90%), with the normal vectors being essential for the tangential-distance consistency check in the filtering stage.

## Method

DualReg proposes a three-stage pipeline. First, a lightweight 1-point RANSAC reduces the candidate correspondence space from O(n³) to O(n) by defining a consensus set via length consistency, point-to-plane tangential distance, and a symmetry-disambiguation check, then iteratively scores correspondences by their frequency in high-quality consensus sets. Second, a probability-weighted 3-point RANSAC (inspired by BANSAC's dynamic Bayesian network) refines the filtered set to produce a high-inlier-ratio correspondence set C_II. Third, a dual-space optimization jointly minimizes a weighted objective over both the filtered feature-based anchor correspondences and geometry-based nearest-neighbor correspondences established on local proxy neighborhoods extracted around each anchor; Gaussian robust weights (σ derived from the 3-sigma rule on a high-confidence subset) downweight residual outliers, and the rigid transform is solved in closed form via SVD within an ICP-style iterative loop that alternates correspondence updates and transform estimation.

## Results

On 3DMatch with FPFH features, DualReg achieves 84.41% registration recall (RR) with 4.55 cm RMSE, 1.75° rotation error, and 6.19 cm translation error in 0.14 s per registration. On KITTI (outdoor), it reaches 98.20% RR with 12.26 cm RMSE in 0.12 s, a 32× CPU-time speedup over MAC (3.91 s) while also improving RMSE (12.26 vs. 15.57 cm). On the challenging low-overlap 3DLoMatch dataset, DualReg attains 41.66% RR (FPFH) and 60.86% RR (FCGF), outperforming all CPU-based baselines in both recall and accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DualReg achieves the best RMSE, RE, and TE across all three datasets while maintaining competitive RR. Against MAC, it matches or exceeds RR (84.41% vs. 83.92% on 3DMatch) while running 15× faster (0.14 s vs. 2.10 s); on KITTI the speedup reaches 32× (0.12 s vs. 3.91 s) with a 21% RMSE reduction (12.26 vs. 15.57 cm). Compared to MAC++, DualReg is 30× faster on 3DMatch (0.14 s vs. 4.28 s) and 79× faster on KITTI (0.12 s vs. 9.49 s), while also halving KITTI RMSE (12.26 vs. 25.25 cm). Versus TCF, the fastest CPU baseline, DualReg is slightly slower (0.14 s vs. 0.08 s on 3DMatch) but gains 6.3% RR (84.41 vs. 78.13) and 14% lower RMSE (4.55 vs. 5.32 cm). Against the GPU-based learning methods PointDSC and VBReg, DualReg runs on CPU yet achieves higher RR on 3DMatch (84.41% vs. 78.00%/81.39%) and substantially lower RMSE on KITTI (12.26 vs. 17.36/17.01 cm). On the low-overlap 3DLoMatch benchmark, DualReg leads all CPU methods in both RR and RMSE, with a 1.2% RR and 13% RMSE advantage over the next-best CPU method (MAC++).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-studied lines in point cloud registration: RANSAC-based correspondence filtering (building on TCF's 1-point RANSAC [38] and BANSAC's dynamic Bayesian sampling [30]) and ICP-style local geometric alignment [4]. Its key conceptual departure is the 'dual-space' paradigm that jointly optimizes over filtered feature-space correspondences (global, transformation-invariant) and dynamically established geometric-space correspondences (fine-grained, pose-dependent), rather than treating them as sequential coarse-to-fine stages. The 1-point RANSAC consensus set definition, symmetry disambiguation module, and the geometric proxy construction are novel contributions that differentiate it from TCF and MAC-family methods, which rely on maximal clique search or simpler pairwise constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 32× CPU-time speedup over MAC on KITTI with comparable accuracy is practically significant for real-time SLAM and robotics applications where GPU access is unavailable. The method's strong performance on 3DLoMatch (low-overlap) scenarios addresses a persistent weakness of ICP-based approaches. However, the authors acknowledge that RANSAC-based filtering inherently fails when inlier ratios are extremely low, and the method still depends on the quality of initial feature-based correspondences. An open question is whether the dual-space framework can be extended to non-rigid or similarity registration, and whether learned correspondence quality can further reduce the filtering burden.
