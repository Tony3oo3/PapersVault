## Problem

Existing multimodal RAG systems face two critical gaps: (1) current multimodal knowledge graphs (MMKGs) are largely single-hop and image-text-centric, lacking the multi-hop temporal and causal connectivity needed for audio-visual reasoning; and (2) retrieval in shared multimodal embedding spaces suffers from the modality gap, retrieving off-topic or redundant knowledge that fails to support the answer. This paper addresses both by constructing multi-hop, modality-aware MMKGs and introducing a grounded, selective pruning mechanism that retains only query-relevant, answer-useful evidence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262228/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the system accepts a text question paired with an audio clip, a video clip, or both, and produces a free-form text answer. The knowledge graph is pre-built from aligned multimodal corpora (text captions, audio, and visual data), and at query time the retrieval is modality-matched: video queries are embedded with InternVL2 and audio queries with CLAP, then searched via FAISS L2 distance against same-modality items in the graph. The final answer is generated by an MLLM conditioned on the pruned subgraph, where each triplet is rendered as a relation with refined entity-description pairs.

## Method

M³KG-RAG has two core components. First, a lightweight multi-agent pipeline (rewriter, extractor, normalizer, searcher, selector, refiner, inspector) constructs a multi-hop MMKG in three steps: Context-Enriched Triplet Extraction (rewriting generic captions into knowledge-intensive text and extracting entity-relation triplets), Knowledge Grounding (normalizing entities and retrieving descriptions from open knowledge bases), and Context-Aware Description Refinement (selecting and adapting the most context-appropriate description per entity), all guarded by a Self-Reflection Loop. Second, GRASP (Grounded Retrieval And Selective Pruning) performs modality-wise retrieval to obtain a query-aligned initial subgraph, then scores entity presence using GroundingDINO (visual, max over four sampled frames) and a Text-to-Audio Grounding model (audio, triplet-level sentence scoring), pruning triplets below modality-specific thresholds. A final lightweight LLM applies a conservative keep-or-drop mask to remove triplets that do not contribute to answering the question, yielding a compact, answer-supporting subgraph fed to the MLLM.

## Results

Using Model-as-Judge (M.J.) scores, M³KG-RAG achieves the best performance across all three benchmarks for both MLLMs. With Qwen2.5-Omni, it reaches 60.77 on AudioCaps-QA, 44.35 on VCGPT, and 44.67 on VALOR, surpassing the strongest baseline VAT-KG (51.30 / 43.50 / 35.44) by 9.47, 0.85, and 9.23 points respectively. With VideoLLaMA2, the gains are 8.63 (AudioCaps-QA), 0.50 (VCGPT), and 0.95 (VALOR) over VAT-KG. Notably, several baselines (Wikidata, VTKG, M²ConceptBase) occasionally degrade below the no-retrieval baseline, while M³KG-RAG consistently improves over the unassisted MLLM on every benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M³KG-RAG outperforms all four baselines on every benchmark for both MLLMs. Against Wikidata (text KG + naive shared-embedding RAG), the gains are largest on AudioCaps-QA (+9.65 for Qwen2.5-Omni) and VALOR (+14.39), while Wikidata actually degrades VCGPT performance below the no-retrieval baseline (40.82 vs. 42.21). VTKG (image-text MMKG) and M²ConceptBase (concept-centric MMKG) show only marginal or negative effects, with VTKG dropping below the no-retrieval score on AudioCaps-QA for Qwen2.5-Omni (48.95 vs. 49.00). The closest competitor, VAT-KG (audio-visual MMKG with single-hop retrieval), is consistently beaten: the gap widens to 9.23 points on VALOR with Qwen2.5-Omni (44.67 vs. 35.44), confirming that multi-hop connectivity plus GRASP pruning provides substantially more answer-supporting context than single-hop, similarity-only retrieval.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal knowledge graph RAG line initiated by VAT-KG (same first author) and M²ConceptBase, which organized image-text or audio-visual corpora into concept-level, largely single-hop graphs retrieved via similarity search in shared embedding spaces. It departs from that lineage in two key ways: (1) constructing a multi-hop MMKG via a multi-agent LLM pipeline that enriches triplets with grounded, context-aware entity descriptions, and (2) replacing shared-space similarity retrieval with modality-wise retrieval (searching within the query's own modality) followed by GRASP, which uses off-the-shelf grounding models (GroundingDINO, TAG) and a lightweight LLM to prune triplets that are absent from the query or unhelpful for answering. The multi-agent construction pipeline draws on recent self-reflection and collaborative LLM agent work, while the retrieval design is motivated by the well-documented modality gap in unified multimodal embedding spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that multi-hop, modality-aligned retrieval with grounded pruning can substantially improve audio-visual QA over both no-retrieval baselines and existing MMKG-RAG methods, including on a strong commercial model (GPT-4o), suggesting the approach is not merely compensating for weak model knowledge. However, the evaluation is limited to three relatively small benchmarks and three MLLMs, and the strongest baseline (VAT-KG) is from the same research group, making it difficult to assess how the method compares against other recent multimodal RAG systems. Open questions include scalability of the multi-agent KG construction pipeline to much larger corpora, the sensitivity of GRASP's grounding thresholds to entity granularity, and whether the modality-wise retrieval strategy generalizes to settings where the query modality does not cleanly correspond to a single index (e.g., mixed or text-only queries over audio-visual knowledge).
