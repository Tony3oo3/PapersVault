## Problem

Existing contrastive learning methods for medical imaging learn representations from visual appearance features (textures, intensities, color patterns) while neglecting topological characteristics—connectivity patterns, boundary configurations, and cavity formations—that are diagnostically critical for distinguishing lesion types. Standard CL augmentations provide no guarantee of preserving or controllably perturbing these structures, and no prior method explicitly encodes topological patterns during contrastive self-supervised pretraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 2D medical images (pathology, retinal OCT, abdominal CT, dermoscopy, endoscopy) and produces 256-dimensional learned representations for downstream linear-probe classification. Unusually, each input image is consumed in two parallel streams: the raw image passes through a ResNet-50 visual encoder, while its cubical persistence diagram (top-48 H₀ and top-96 H₁ features) computed on SAM-extracted regions of interest passes through a separate topology encoder. At inference, topology features can be precomputed offline, reducing the online cost to the MoE fusion module.

## Method

TopoCL augments any base contrastive learning method with three components. First, topology-aware augmentations use relative bottleneck distance between persistence diagrams (on SAM-extracted ROIs) to calibrate weak (d_B^rel 5–15%) and strong (15–25%) topological perturbations, ensuring positive pairs preserve diagnostically relevant structure while providing sufficient diversity. Second, a Hierarchical Topology Encoder processes persistence diagrams via a PointNet-like PH Encoder, then applies self-attention within each homology dimension (H₀, H₁) and bidirectional cross-attention between them to capture geometric dependencies such as hole containment within components. Third, an adaptive Mixture-of-Experts module with five fusion experts (visual-only, topology-only, concatenation, gated blending, cross-attention) uses learned per-sample softmax gating to dynamically weight fusion strategies. Training follows a pretrain-then-fuse strategy: both encoders are independently pretrained with the same contrastive objective, then jointly fine-tuned through the MoE module.

## Results

TopoCL achieves an average linear-probe accuracy gain of +3.26% and AUC gain of +0.90% over the corresponding CL baselines across five medical image datasets (PathMNIST, OCTMNIST, OrganSMNIST, ISIC2019, Kvasir). Per-method ACC improvements range from +2.46% (SimCLR) to +4.60% (DINO), with AUC gains from +0.45% to +1.80%. Statistical significance is strong: 86% (43/50) of individual dataset-metric comparisons reach p<0.05, and 80% of dataset-averaged metrics reach p<0.001. The only notable exception is MoCo-v3 on OrganSMNIST, where AUC drops slightly (99.57%→98.75%) despite a +2.56% ACC gain, likely due to near-saturated baseline performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoCL is integrated with five CL baselines (SimCLR, MoCo-v3, BYOL, DINO, Barlow Twins) and consistently outperforms each on average. DINO+TopoCL shows the largest gains (+4.60% ACC, +1.80% AUC) with statistical significance across all ten dataset-metric combinations, suggesting DINO's self-distillation objective benefits most from explicit topological supervision. SimCLR+TopoCL and Barlow Twins+TopoCL achieve moderate but consistent improvements (+2.46% and +3.26% ACC respectively), while MoCo-v3+TopoCL and BYOL+TopoCL show intermediate gains. The one exception is MoCo-v3 on OrganSMNIST, where AUC decreases from 99.57% to 98.75% (p=0.023) despite a +2.56% ACC gain, attributed to near-saturated baseline AUC (>99.5%). Dataset-wise, Kvasir (GI endoscopy) and OCTMNIST (retinal OCT) show the largest average ACC gains (+4.29% and +3.02%), indicating that topological features are particularly informative for modalities where structural patterns (e.g., polyp boundaries, retinal layers) are diagnostically critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of contrastive self-supervised learning and topological data analysis for medical imaging. Prior TDA work in medical imaging (PersLay, PHG-Net, topological loss functions) used persistent homology as auxiliary supervision or in labeled settings, while standard CL methods (SimCLR, MoCo-v3, BYOL, DINO, Barlow Twins) learned purely visual representations with augmentations designed for appearance preservation. TopoCL departs from both lines by integrating cubical persistent homology directly into the contrastive pretraining loop—introducing topology-aware augmentations calibrated via relative bottleneck distance, a PointNet-based hierarchical encoder for persistence diagrams, and an MoE fusion module that adaptively combines visual and topological representations. The MoE approach to visual-topological fusion is, to the authors' knowledge, the first such application.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TopoCL provides a practical, drop-in enhancement for any existing CL pipeline in medical imaging, with a modest 6–18% training overhead and the option to precompute topological features offline for deployment. The dataset-specific gating patterns (e.g., ISIC2019 strongly favoring topology-only features, PathMNIST favoring cross-attention) suggest that the relative value of topology vs. appearance is modality-dependent, an insight that could guide future architecture design. Open questions include extension to 3D volumetric data, higher homology dimensions (H2+), whether topology-aware CL benefits natural image domains, and whether the SAM-based ROI extraction introduces a dependency that limits applicability to unlabeled or poorly segmented data.
