## Problem

Asymmetric quantization (AsymQ) is widely adopted in post-training quantization for vision models to handle skewed weight distributions, but its zero-point offsets increase multiplier bit-width by one bit, inflate circuit area by ~1.3×, and leave many quantization levels unused between the dense inlier region and sparse outlier tails. The paper asks whether zero points are truly necessary, revealing that the apparent asymmetry in pretrained weights is caused almost entirely by a small number of sparse outliers, and that the remaining core distribution is nearly symmetric.

## Input / Output

The method operates on pretrained vision model weights (ResNet, MobileNet-V2, RegNetX, ViT, DeiT, Swin, Mask R-CNN) using only a small calibration set for step-size optimization, with no retraining. Each weight matrix is decomposed into a dense component (k_D-bit SymQ) and a sparse component (k_S-bit SymQ at 98% sparsity), with the step-size ratio constrained to a power of two (Δ_S/Δ_D ≈ 2^u) so that the sparse MatMul reduces to bit-shifts. The quantized model produces the same outputs as the original: class labels on ImageNet, bounding boxes and segmentation masks on COCO.

## Method

DASQ decomposes each weight matrix W ≈ Ŵ_D + Ŵ_S, where the dense matrix captures the near-symmetric core distribution using low-bit SymQ and the sparse matrix isolates the top (1−p) outliers using higher-bit SymQ. An alternating optimization (Alg. 1) iteratively refines the dense and sparse step sizes by minimizing per-channel MSE while maintaining the sparsity constraint, with a power-of-two projection applied to the sparse step-size ratio to enable hardware-efficient bit-shift operations. At inference, the dense MatMul (W_Dq^T x_q) and sparse MatMul (W_Sq^T x_q) execute in parallel within a single compute core; because the sparse component is 98% zero, its MAC operations complete before the dense ones, so the sparse path adds no latency. The zero-point term from activation AsymQ is absorbed into a precomputed bias, and the sparse MatMul replaces the costly zero-point addition of AsymQ with lightweight shift-and-add logic, reducing BOPs from (k_D+1)·k_A to (k_D + (1−p)·k_S)·k_A.

## Results

On ImageNet with ViT-B at W3/A4, DASQ+RepQ-ViT achieves 75.24% top-1 accuracy (vs. 26.98% for RepQ-ViT alone) while reducing BOPs from 267.71G to 206.14G; DASQ+ERQ reaches 78.44% (vs. 72.37% for ERQ). At W4/A4, DASQ+ERQ attains 80.04% top-1 with 273.07G BOPs, surpassing ERQ's 78.67% at 334.64G BOPs. On ResNet-50 at W2/A4, DASQ+QDrop achieves 72.63% top-1 with 39.76G BOPs, outperforming QDrop (70.08%), MRECG (70.53%), and CL-Calib (70.92%) at 57.35G BOPs. On COCO with Mask R-CNN (Swin-T, W4/A4), DASQ+RepQ-ViT reaches 41.5 AP^box and 39.1 AP^mask, exceeding ERQ (39.4/37.5) and RepQ-ViT (36.1/36.0) while using fewer BOPs (2219.62G vs. 2719.52G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DASQ consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every task and bit-width tested. On ViT-B at W3/A4, DASQ+RepQ-ViT improves top-1 accuracy by 48.26 points over RepQ-ViT (75.24% vs. 26.98%) and by 6.07 points over ERQ (78.44% vs. 72.37%), while reducing BOPs by ~23% in both cases. On ResNet-50 at W2/A4, DASQ+QDrop beats QDrop by 2.55 points, MRECG by 2.10 points, and CL-Calib by 1.71 points, all at 31% fewer BOPs (39.76G vs. 57.35G). On COCO with Swin-T, DASQ+RepQ-ViT surpasses ERQ by 2.1 AP^box and 1.6 AP^mask, and RepQ-ViT by 5.4 AP^box and 3.1 AP^mask, while using 18% fewer BOPs. The SymQ-based baselines (PTQ4ViT, APQ-ViT) suffer severe accuracy degradation (e.g., 6.9 AP^box for PTQ4ViT on Swin-T), confirming that plain SymQ without outlier handling is insufficient. On FPGA, DASQ also achieves higher top-1 accuracy (up to +6.7%) and lower power consumption than AsymQ at comparable throughput, validating the hardware efficiency advantage of eliminating zero-point multipliers.

## Relation to Prior Work

DASQ departs from the dominant PTQ paradigm that relies on per-channel asymmetric quantization (AsymQ) to handle skewed weight distributions, as adopted by Adaround, BRECQ, MRECG, QDrop, RepQ-ViT, and ERQ. Conceptually, it repurposes the dense-sparse decomposition idea popularized in LLM quantization (SpQR, SqueezeLLM) for vision models, but with a distinct motivation: eliminating the zero-point to reduce multiplier bit-width and circuit area in MAC arrays. Rather than proposing a new reconstruction objective, DASQ is a drop-in replacement for the weight quantization step within existing PTQ pipelines, preserving the surrounding calibration and optimization procedures unchanged.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: DASQ simultaneously improves accuracy and reduces hardware cost (multiplier width, LUT usage, power) compared to AsymQ, validated on FPGA. The hidden-symmetry observation provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding why low-bit PTQ degrades and where the error actually comes from. Limitations include the continued use of AsymQ for depthwise convolutions (too few weights per channel for decomposition), the added complexity of maintaining two weight matrices with alternating optimization, and the power-of-two step-size ratio constraint that may not be optimal in all regimes. An open question is whether this dense-sparse framing transfers to memory-bound LLM inference, where the computational bottleneck differs fundamentally.
