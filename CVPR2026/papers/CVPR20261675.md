## Problem

Existing dynamic 3D reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: Frame-Stream approaches (e.g., 3DGStream, Dynamic3DGS) scale to long sequences but accumulate inter-frame jitter and reconstruction errors, while Clip approaches (e.g., 4DGS, SpaceTimeGS) achieve local temporal consistency but incur prohibitive memory costs and cannot extend beyond a few hundred frames. Neither paradigm robustly handles long multi-view sequences containing large-scale or fast motions, which are common in real-world dynamic scenes like sports.

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view video sequences (e.g., 36 cameras at 4K for Long 360, 21 cameras at 2704×2028 for N3DV, 34 cameras at 1920×1080 for VRU) along with COLMAP-estimated camera poses and point clouds. The video is partitioned into clips of M frames each; the first clip serves as the Reference Clip and all subsequent clips as Source Clips. The output is a Temporal Gaussian representation—comprising inherited frozen anchors with static features, clip-specific residual anchors, per-clip spatio-temporal fields, and a shared decoder—that renders novel views at real-time rates via differentiable rasterization with α-blending.

## Method

ClipGStream introduces a Clip-Stream framework that unifies clip-level temporal coherence with stream-level scalability through two complementary strategies. Intra-clip, each Source Clip trains an independent spatio-temporal field (4D hash grid + MLP) to model local motion, while a geometry-aware residual anchor compensation module (using SDF-based deduplication against the Reference Clip's spherical coverage field) injects only newly emerged or displaced anchors to handle large motions without redundant growth. Inter-clip, the Reference Clip's anchors, static features, and decoder are inherited and frozen across all Source Clips, ensuring that static geometry and appearance decoding remain temporally consistent while only dynamic features and residual anchors are optimized per clip. Training uses a standard photometric loss (L1 + SSIM) plus a volume regularization term, with the Adam optimizer's learning-rate schedule reinitialized at each clip boundary to prevent under-training.

## Results

On the Long 360 dataset (1,400 frames, 36 cameras), ClipGStream achieves PSNR 24.54, DSSIM₁ 0.079, DSSIM₂ 0.036, and LPIPS 0.146, outperforming the best static method (3DGS, PSNR 24.13), the best Frame-Stream method (iFVC, PSNR 22.35), and the best Clip method (LocalDyGS, PSNR 23.11) across all metrics. On VRU GZ (250 frames), it reaches PSNR 30.67 and LPIPS 0.137, surpassing LocalDyGS (PSNR 30.58, LPIPS 0.173). On the 1,200-frame flame salmon sequence from N3DV, ClipGStream attains PSNR 29.40 and LPIPS 0.144, exceeding 4DGaussian (PSNR 28.89) and LocalDyGS (PSNR 28.15).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ClipGStream outperforms all 22 baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. Against static methods (2DGS, 3DGS, ScaffoldGS, GOF), it achieves higher PSNR on Long 360 (24.54 vs. 24.13 for 3DGS) while also handling full temporal dynamics, whereas static methods only render a single frame. Against Frame-Stream methods (3DGStream, iFVC, StreamRF, HiCoM, 4DGC), the advantage is substantial: ClipGStream gains 2.2 dB PSNR over iFVC (24.54 vs. 22.35) and 2.6 dB over 3DGStream (24.54 vs. 21.94) on Long 360, reflecting the elimination of inter-frame jitter and accumulated errors. Against Clip methods (4DGaussian, Swift4D, LocalDyGS, SpaceTimeGS, K-Planes, HexPlane, RealTimeGS), ClipGStream leads by 1.43 dB PSNR over LocalDyGS (24.54 vs. 23.11) and 2.53 dB over 4DGaussian (24.54 vs. 22.01) on Long 360, and by 0.09 dB PSNR over LocalDyGS (30.67 vs. 30.58) on VRU GZ. On the 1,200-frame flame salmon sequence, ClipGStream surpasses 4DGaussian by 0.51 dB PSNR (29.40 vs. 28.89) and LocalDyGS by 1.25 dB (29.40 vs. 28.15), while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.144 vs. 0.153). The consistent margin across all three datasets and both motion scales (large basketball motion on Long 360/VRU, fine-scale motion on N3DV) demonstrates that the Clip-Stream framework generalizes robustly without sacrificing temporal coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

ClipGStream builds directly on the ScaffoldGS anchor-decoder architecture and the static/dynamic feature decoupling introduced by LocalDyGS, but reorganizes the training paradigm from either per-frame (Frame-Stream: 3DGStream, Dynamic3DGS) or whole-sequence (Clip: 4DGaussian, SpaceTimeGS) optimization into a clip-level stream. The key conceptual departure is treating the first clip as a frozen 'Reference' whose anchors, static features, and decoder are inherited by all subsequent Source Clips, while each Source Clip adds only residual anchors and an independent spatio-temporal field to capture local motion. This hybrid design explicitly targets the gap the authors identify: Frame-Stream methods accumulate inter-frame jitter over long sequences, while Clip methods cannot scale beyond a few hundred frames due to memory and optimization constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Clip-Stream framework offers a practical path toward production-quality free-viewpoint video for VR/MR/XR, where sequences of thousands of frames with large athlete-scale motion are the norm. The newly released Long 360 dataset (1,400 frames, 36 cameras, 4K) fills a notable gap in public benchmarks for long-sequence, high-motion multi-view reconstruction. A key open limitation is the dependence on COLMAP-estimated poses, which degrades in textureless or low-overlap regions; integrating learned or robust pose estimation remains an important next step. Whether the frozen-inheritance strategy generalizes to scenes with truly non-repeating content (e.g., a person entering and leaving the frame across many clips) is not yet explored.
