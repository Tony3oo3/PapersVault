## Problem

Multifunctional metasurface imagers are fundamentally limited by severe chromatic aberrations that confine their operational bandwidth to 10–100 nm, making them unsuitable for hyperspectral imaging across the visible spectrum. No prior metasurface-based hyperspectral imager has demonstrated operation over the full visible range, and existing designs that rely on a single metasurface for both beam splitting and image formation suffer from high F-numbers and bulky form factors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261829/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system captures a single snapshot on a shared 7.1 mm × 7.1 mm global-shutter RGB sensor, producing four spatially separated sub-images: two achromatic (for HDR or polarization) and two chromatic with orthogonal dispersions (for spectral encoding). From this single exposure, the pipeline reconstructs a hyperspectral datacube over 450–700 nm plus either a fused HDR image (via Debevec-Malik fusion of the two achromatic channels with a ~4× exposure ratio) or two orthogonal linear polarization channels (0° and 90°). The optical system operates at f/6 with a 12° field of view and 17 mm total track length.

## Method

MetaSpectra+ decouples beam splitting from image formation using a two-layer metasurface-refractive hybrid: a first metasurface (M₀) randomly interleaves four linear-phase profiles to split and deflect the collimated beam into four channels at ~33°, while a second layer of metasurfaces (M₁–M₄) either introduces controlled chromatic dispersion (for spectral encoding) or cancels the deflection (αᵢ + βᵢ = 0) to produce achromatic sub-images. This separation enables a 250 nm operational bandwidth and a lower F-number than single-metasurface designs. Post-processing employs two architectures: a DWDN-based pipeline (Wiener deconvolution in feature space followed by multi-scale CNN refinement) and a DDPM-based pipeline that denoises a hyperspectral datacube per patch while enforcing spatial consistency via a learned normalization factor. Both are trained on synthetic measurements rendered with the D-Flat simulator from Harvard and ICVL datasets, then fine-tuned on real front-parallel scenes to bridge the simulation-to-reality gap.

## Results

On the KAUST dataset, MetaSpectra+ (DWDN) achieves PSNR 32.92 dB, SSIM 0.94, and SAM 0.17°, while the DDPM variant reaches PSNR 33.31 dB, SSIM 0.92, and SAM 0.23°—the best values across all compared snapshot and multi-shot hyperspectral systems. The prototype also achieves an 11 dB increase in dynamic range for HDR imaging over a standard CMOS capture, and the system's total track length of 17 mm is the shortest among all compared designs. In real-world experiments, the system reconstructs high-fidelity hyperspectral datacubes from a single snapshot over the 450–700 nm band.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaSpectra+ outperforms all seven compared baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, SAM) on the KAUST dataset. The closest competitor is 2-in-1Cam [52] (PSNR 31.14, SSIM 0.86, SAM 0.24), which MetaSpectra+ surpasses by 1.78 dB in PSNR and 0.08 in SSIM while also halving the total track length (17 mm vs. 50 mm). Array-HSI [53] (PSNR 27.44) and SfD [3] (PSNR 27.54) trail by roughly 5.5 dB, while the single-sub-image DOE-based systems SCCD [2] and Baek et al. [5] lag by 6+ dB. The multi-shot Foley et al. [19] (PSNR 20.40) and the RGB-to-spectrum MST++ [9] (PSNR 21.85) are the weakest baselines, underscoring the advantage of the four-channel CTIS configuration. Notably, MetaSpectra+ achieves the smallest total track length (17 mm) among all systems, a direct benefit of the metasurface-based hybrid optical design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multifunctional metasurface imaging line initiated by Brookshire et al. (MetaHDR) and the authors' own MetaH2, which demonstrated snapshot HDR + hyperspectral capture but were confined to narrow spectral bands (10–100 nm) due to inherent metasurface chromatic dispersion. The key architectural departure is a two-layer hybrid design: a beamsplitting metasurface (following Brookshire's random interleaving) paired with a dispersion-control metasurface that can either cancel or introduce controlled chromatic shift per channel, decoupling beam splitting from image formation and enabling a 250 nm operating band. For post-processing, the paper adapts two existing reconstruction backbones—DWDN (Dong et al.) and a DDPM-based pipeline (Hazineh et al.)—rather than proposing a new network architecture, positioning the contribution squarely in optical system design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By breaking the narrow-band limitation that has constrained all prior multifunctional metasurface imagers, this work opens the door to compact, single-snapshot, multi-modal sensing (hyperspectral + HDR or polarization) across the full visible spectrum, which is critical for applications like agricultural phenotyping and forensics where cross-modal registration matters. The 17 mm total track length and 11 dB demonstrated dynamic-range gain are practically compelling for embedded or mobile sensing. However, the prototype is lab-scale with a limited depth of field (0.2–0.7 m), requires per-unit spectral calibration, and the reconstruction gains over the strongest baseline (2-in-1Cam) are moderate (~1.8 dB PSNR), so the broader impact hinges on whether the hybrid design scales to larger apertures, wider FoVs, and real-time processing.
