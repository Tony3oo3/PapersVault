## Problem

Modern video diffusion models produce visually realistic but physically inconsistent videos—objects drift, collisions lack realistic rebound, and material responses ignore underlying properties. Prior approaches either require explicit physics simulators and 3D geometry reconstruction at inference (limiting scalability) or embed only coarse kinematic cues without continuous control over diverse physical attributes. PhyCo addresses this gap by enabling continuous, interpretable control over friction, restitution, deformation, and applied force purely through generative modeling, without any simulator at test time.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt, a single initial RGB frame, and spatially aligned physical property maps as input. The property maps are compact circular-blob representations (not full geometry) encoding normalized values in [-1,1] for friction/restitution, Neo-Hookean deformation parameters (d_μ, d_λ, d_γ), and force magnitude with direction (cos φ, sin φ), grouped into three semantic channels each. The output is a physically consistent video; the model is trained on 57-frame sequences at 24 FPS but extrapolates to 120 frames at test time, and generalizes to real-world scenes beyond the synthetic training domain.

## Method

PhyCo introduces a two-stage training pipeline on a pretrained Cosmos-Predict2-2B DiT backbone. Stage 1 (Physics-Supervised Fine-Tuning) attaches a multi-branch ControlNet that injects tokenized physical property maps—each semantic group (friction+restitution, deformation, force) processed by a separate branch for compositionality—while keeping the base model frozen, trained via standard diffusion score-matching on 100K+ photorealistic Kubric/PyBullet/Blender simulation videos. Stage 2 (VLM-Guided Reward Optimization) performs N-step denoising rollouts, decodes the latent to video, and queries a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-3B with targeted binary physics questions (e.g., 'Does the object move in the intended force direction?'), computing a differentiable reward from the logit difference between correct and incorrect answer tokens. Only the ControlNet layers are updated in both stages, and the VLM loss is backpropagated end-to-end through the VLM, tokenizer, and DiT backbone, yielding more stable and physically faithful generations than joint score-matching training.

## Results

On the Physics-IQ benchmark, PhyCo (ControlNet + VLM Loss) achieves an overall IQ Score of 43.6 under train-time conditions (57 frames + last-frame repetition), outperforming the strongest baseline VLIPP (34.6) by 9.0 points and the base Cosmos-Predict2-2B (27.7) by 15.9 points. Even under test-time extrapolation to 120 frames (beyond the 57-frame training length), PhyCo scores 36.3, still exceeding VLIPP (34.6). In a 2AFC user study, PhyCo is preferred over CogVideoX-I2V-5B on 82–100% of video pairs across friction, restitution, deformation, and force axes, and achieves a mean force-direction error of 15.2° versus 40.5° for Force-Prompting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhyCo outperforms all eight baselines on the Physics-IQ benchmark. The closest competitor, VLIPP (34.6 IQ), is surpassed by 9.0 points under train-time conditions (43.6) and by 1.7 points even when extrapolating to 120 frames at test time (36.3 vs 34.6). The base model Cosmos-Predict2-2B (27.7) is improved by 15.9 points, while SVD-XT (19.1), LTX-Video-I2V (26.8), CogVideo-I2V-5B (27.1), and SG-I2V (29.7) trail by 14–25 points. In the user study, PhyCo is preferred over CogVideoX-I2V-5B on 82–100% of pairs and over Cosmos-Predict2-2B on 86–100% of pairs across all four physical attributes. On force-direction adherence, PhyCo achieves a mean angular error of 15.2° compared to Force-Prompting's 40.5°, and is preferred over Force-Prompting on 71.7% of force-conditioned pairs. The ablation (Table 4) confirms that the VLM reward loss provides the largest single gain, reducing force-magnitude error from 0.33 to 0.28 and angular deviation from 38.05° to 22.53° relative to ControlNet alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhyCo extends the implicit physical control line (PhysCtrl, VLIPP, Force Prompting) by replacing trajectory- or language-based guidance with spatially aligned, multi-attribute physical property maps injected via a ControlNet-style architecture on a pretrained DiT backbone (Cosmos-Predict2). It departs from explicit-simulator approaches (PhysGen, WonderPlay, PhysDreamer) by requiring no geometry reconstruction or physics engine at inference, while going beyond Force Prompting's single-attribute (force-only) setup to continuous control over friction, restitution, deformation, and force simultaneously. The VLM-guided reward optimization stage adapts the differentiable-reward paradigm from image editing (Kumari et al., Luo et al.) to physics-aware video generation, using a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL as a targeted physics critic rather than a general aesthetic or semantic evaluator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100K-video dataset with disentangled continuous physical annotations and the two-stage training recipe (ControlNet fine-tuning + VLM reward) offer a practical, simulator-free recipe for injecting physical priors into large video diffusion models, with demonstrated generalization from simple synthetic scenes to real-world objects and interactions. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object interactions, fluid dynamics, or longer-horizon sequences beyond the 57-frame training window, and whether the four-attribute formulation can be extended to richer material models. The train-test frame mismatch (57 vs. 120 frames on Physics-IQ) and the still-moderate absolute IQ scores (~44) suggest headroom for improvement as video models grow in temporal capacity.
