## Problem

Existing cross-view geo-localization (CVGL) methods for UAV navigation rely on a matching-to-tile (M2T) paradigm that either repeatedly encodes satellite tiles at inference (high compute/storage) or pre-encodes them into discrete feature vectors (accuracy capped by grid density). These approaches also largely ignore heading estimation and are sensitive to the substantial viewpoint-induced parallax, misalignment, and varying overlap between oblique UAV views and orthorectified satellite tiles, limiting generalization to real-world GNSS-denied scenarios.

## Input / Output

The model takes one UAV-view patch (UVP, an oblique 3D-mode image) and four adjacent orthorectified satellite tiles (RSTs, 0.25 m/px, cropped from 4096×4096 source images into a 2×2 remote-sensing block) along with their 2D relative coordinates C = {(-1,1), (-1,-1), (1,1), (1,-1)}. It outputs a bounded, dimensionless relative position (2D offset within the RSB, from which absolute geo-coordinates are recovered deterministically via the RSB index) and a heading direction vector (cos θ̂, sin θ̂) rather than a raw angle, avoiding periodicity ambiguity in regression.

## Method

Bearing-UAV replaces tile matching with a single-stage regression network built from four key submodules: (1) GLUF, which extracts a unified feature vector by passing input through VGG-16, a non-local block, and SGMNet-style soft clustering over K learnable centers to jointly encode global context and position-aware local segments; (2) RCE, a lightweight MLP that embeds the 2D relative coordinates of each RST into the same feature dimension; (3) PSG, which computes softmax-weighted cosine similarities between the UVP and each RST to produce a similarity-guided 2D coordinate prior; and (4) CA, a cross-attention layer where the UVP acts as query and the four position-injected RST features serve as keys/values to capture overlap-aware cross-view correspondences. These outputs are concatenated into a fused vector fed to two parallel MLP heads that regress position and heading simultaneously. Training uses a weighted Smooth L1 loss (0.8 position + 0.2 heading) with Adam at lr=5e-5 for 100 epochs, and the heading is parameterized as a unit vector to provide a continuous, well-behaved regression target.

## Results

On the UAV-view (cross-view) setting of Bearing-UAV-90K, Bearing-UAV achieves a mean localization error (MLE) of 8.61 m and mean heading error (MHE) of 12.90°, compared to 28–33 m MLE for all four matching/retrieval baselines. The localization success rate within 15 m (LSR@15) reaches 89.36%, far exceeding the 15–28% of baselines, and Recall@1 is 83.17% vs. 60–73%. For navigation, Bearing-UAV is the only method that completes any route, achieving a 50% success rate (SR@20) and 29.82% SPL on the UAV-view setting, while all baselines score 0%. Weather-augmented training further reduces MLE to 7.48 m and MHE to 9.63°.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four baselines (University-1652, SUES-200, DenseUAV, GTA-UAV) follow the matching-to-tile (M2T) retrieval paradigm and are evaluated under the same Bearing-UAV-90K protocol. Bearing-UAV outperforms every baseline on all localization metrics: MLE is reduced from 28–33 m to 8.61 m (a 3–4× improvement), and LSR@15 jumps from 15–28% to 89.36%. The largest relative gap appears on LSR@15, where the best baseline (GTA-UAV at 27.96%) is still more than 3× worse than Bearing-UAV. None of the baselines provide heading estimation or can execute navigation routes (all score 0% SR@20), whereas Bearing-UAV achieves 50% SR@20 and 29.82% SPL, making it the only method capable of end-to-end vision-only navigation. The weather-augmented variant further narrows MLE to 7.48 m and MHE to 9.63°, though its navigation SR drops to 25% due to a few highly deviated trajectories, while NE improves to 248.77 m.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant matching-to-tile (M2T) paradigm in cross-view geo-localization, where UAV views are matched or retrieved against discrete satellite tiles, and instead proposes a direct regression formulation that jointly predicts absolute position and heading from four adjacent remote-sensing tiles. The feature extraction pipeline builds on SGMNet's clustering scheme and non-local blocks to construct the GLUF vector, while the fusion stage introduces cross-attention and patch-similarity-guided coordinate weighting to handle cross-view misalignment. Unlike prior two-stage approaches (localize-then-orient) or single-corridor heading methods like AngleRobust, Bearing-UAV treats position and heading as parallel regression targets within a single network, explicitly encoding relative spatial layout via a lightweight coordinate encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in GNSS-denied UAV navigation: existing CVGL methods provide only grid-resolution localization and no heading, making end-to-end autonomous navigation infeasible. The Bearing-UAV-90K benchmark is the first multi-city, unaligned, heading-annotated U-S cross-view dataset, filling a clear evaluation gap. However, all experiments are conducted solely on the authors' own dataset, the navigation success rate remains moderate (~50% SR@20), and the reliance on VGG-16 as the sole primary backbone limits confidence in scalability. Open questions include whether the regression paradigm generalizes to truly unseen cities, whether the 4-tile context window is optimal, and how the method performs under dynamic (non-static) satellite references.
