## Problem

Existing RGB-D semantic scene completion methods fuse multi-modal features in the spatial domain, where misalignment arises both from imperfect raw data (inaccurate 2D segmentation projections and depth sensor noise) and from the smoothing/detail loss introduced by deep convolution and downsampling. This creates a dilemma: complex spatial operations improve semantic alignment but degrade geometric fidelity, while simpler operations preserve geometry at the cost of semantics. The paper addresses this by moving alignment and fusion into the frequency domain to decouple global semantics from local details.

## Input / Output

The network takes an RGB-D image pair: the RGB image is processed by a pretrained 2D segmentation network and projected into a 3D semantic volume S ∈ R^{H×W×D} (one-hot encoded for training stability), while the depth image is encoded via TSDF into a geometric volume G ∈ R^{H×W×D}. The output is a full-resolution 3D semantic occupancy map R̂ ∈ R^{C'×H×W×D} where C' includes the 'free' class. Notably, the dual-encoder uses only 1/2 downsampling (versus 1/4 or smaller in prior work like SISNet and AMMNet), keeping the network lightweight at 10.10M parameters.

## Method

MFDNet is a lightweight dual-encoder CNN built from DDR blocks with limited convolution and downsampling layers, equipped with two frequency-domain modules. Multi-modal Adaptive Frequency Fusion (MAFF) transforms encoded semantic and geometric features into the frequency domain via FFT, decomposes them into k bands using Gaussian low-pass filters, learns per-band weights from the overall features (an 'overall-to-band' strategy), and fuses the weighted bands before inverse-transforming back to spatial domain; auxiliary semantic and geometric losses supervise the calibrated per-modality features to enforce alignment. Frequency Detail Compensation (FDC) extracts high-frequency components from shallow (pre-downsampling) features, learns adaptive weights from the coarse decoder output R_I, and adds the weighted high-frequency cues to restore lost local details. Together, MAFF and FDC form a global-to-local alignment paradigm: MAFF aligns modalities across all frequency bands from a global perspective, while FDC refines fine-grained details locally, achieving SOTA mIoU (57.0% on NYUv2, 69.7% on NYUCAD) with a 54.4% parameter reduction over prior methods.

## Results

On the NYUv2 test set, MFDNet achieves 77.1% IoU and 57.0% mIoU, outperforming the best spatial-domain baseline AMMNet (76.3% IoU, 56.1% mIoU) by 0.8% and 0.9% respectively. On NYUCAD, MFDNet reaches 87.6% IoU and 69.7% mIoU, surpassing the strongest baseline SG-SSC (86.6% IoU, 69.2% mIoU) by 1.0% and 0.5%. The method also reduces parameters by 54.4% (10.10M vs. 22.17M for AMMNet) while maintaining state-of-the-art accuracy, and shows particularly large gains on fine-grained categories such as window (+3.4% over AMMNet on NYUv2) and tvs (+2.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MFDNet outperforms all seven baselines on both NYUv2 and NYUCAD. On NYUv2, it beats the strongest spatial baseline AMMNet [27] by 0.8% IoU and 0.9% mIoU, and the prior frequency-domain method FFNet [30] by a large margin of 5.3% IoU and 12.6% mIoU, indicating that FFNet's separate high/low-frequency fusion and neglect of depth inaccuracies are significant limitations. On NYUCAD, MFDNet surpasses SG-SSC [21] (the best spatial method) by 1.0% IoU and 0.5% mIoU, and FFNet by 2.1% IoU and 12.3% mIoU. Compared to the other spatial baselines (SISNet, CVSformer, PCANet, MFFNet), MFDNet leads by 4.6–14.3% mIoU on NYUv2. In a controlled fusion-strategy comparison (Table 7), MFDNet without FDC already outperforms AMMNet without its discriminator by 0.8% IoU and 0.5% mIoU while using only 4.79M vs. 20.85M parameters (77% fewer), confirming that the frequency-domain MAFF fusion is inherently more efficient than AMMNet's spatial modulation approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

MFDNet builds on the dual-encoder SSC architecture inherited from SISNet [1] and CVSformer [8], reusing their feature extractors and DDR blocks [14] as the backbone, but departs fundamentally in how multi-modal features are fused. While prior spatial-domain methods (AMMNet, SG-SSC) and the earlier frequency-based FFNet [30] either ignore cross-modal misalignment or fuse frequency bands independently (disrupting intra-modal dependencies), MFDNet's MAFF jointly models intra-modal multi-band dependencies and inter-modal relationships within a unified frequency representation. The FDC module further distinguishes this work by repurposing high-frequency components from shallow, pre-downsampling features as a detail-compensation signal—a strategy not present in prior SSC literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 54.4% parameter reduction while matching or exceeding SOTA on both NYUv2 and NYUCAD makes MFDNet a practical option for resource-constrained 3D scene understanding pipelines (robotics, AR/VR). The global-to-local alignment paradigm (MAFF for global frequency-band calibration, FDC for local high-frequency compensation) offers a structured recipe that could transfer to other multi-modal 3D tasks. However, evaluation is limited to two indoor datasets with modest scene diversity; generalization to outdoor, large-scale, or dynamic scenes remains untested, and the frequency-domain approach's interaction with more recent transformer-based or diffusion-based SSC methods is unexplored.
