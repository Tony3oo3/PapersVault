## Problem

Existing zero-shot 3D anomaly detection methods render point clouds into multi-view 2D images and rely on pre-trained Vision-Language Models, inevitably discarding intrinsic 3D geometric cues and introducing view-selection bias. This paper asks whether pre-trained Point-Language Models (PLMs), which natively encode 3D geometry, can be directly leveraged for zero-shot 3D anomaly detection without any 3D-to-2D projection.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud uniformly downsampled to 2,048 points via farthest point sampling, along with a hybrid learnable text prompt (4 context tokens flanked by fixed phrases like 'normal object' or 'defective object'). It produces two levels of output: an object-level anomaly score computed from the global CLS embedding's cosine similarity to text embeddings, and per-point anomaly scores derived from patch-level fused embeddings compared against the same text embeddings, which are thresholded to yield a point-level localization mask.

## Method

BTP builds on ULIP2 (a PointBERT-based 3D encoder aligned with a text encoder) and introduces two key modules. The Multi-Granularity Feature Embedding Module (MGFEM) fuses intermediate patch features from encoder layers 2, 5, 8, and 11, learnable geometric descriptors from a PointNet-based Geometric Feature Creation Module (GFCM), and the global CLS token into a single patch-level embedding aligned with the text space. Training uses a joint loss combining focal+dice loss for point-level localization, binary cross-entropy for object-level classification, and a contrastive loss that aligns the learned GFCM descriptors with handcrafted FPFH descriptors to inject explicit geometric priors. Anomaly scoring is performed by measuring cosine similarity between the fused point-cloud embeddings and the hybrid-prompt text embeddings, enabling both global and fine-grained localization entirely in 3D space.

## Results

On Real3D-AD, BTP achieves a mean point-level AUROC of 84.5% and P-PRO of 81.9%, surpassing the second-best supervised method CPMF (75.9% P-AUROC) by +8.6 points and the strongest zero-shot baseline PointAD (73.5%) by +11.0 points. At the object level, BTP reports 61.4% O-AUROC, trailing PointAD (74.8%) and R3D-AD (73.4%). On Anomaly-ShapeNet, BTP extends its point-level lead to 87.3% P-AUROC and 82.2% P-PRO, while maintaining a competitive 65.2% O-AUROC. BTP ranks first in 8 of 12 Real3D-AD categories at the point level and second in 2 others.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BTP decisively outperforms all baselines on point-level localization: on Real3D-AD it leads CPMF (75.9%) by +8.6 P-AUROC, PointAD (73.5%) by +11.0, and PatchCoreBERT (61.4%) by +23.1; on Anomaly-ShapeNet it surpasses M3DMMAE (65.1%) by +22.2 P-AUROC. Among zero-shot methods specifically (PointCLIPV2, AnomalyCLIP, PointAD), BTP ranks first on every reported point-level metric on both datasets. However, BTP trails the strongest supervised/unsupervised methods on object-level detection: PointAD achieves 74.8% O-AUROC vs. BTP's 61.4% on Real3D-AD, and R3D-AD reaches 73.4%. The gap at the object level is attributed to BTP's design emphasis on fine-grained point-level scoring rather than global aggregation. On Anomaly-ShapeNet, BTP's 65.2% O-AUROC is competitive with IMR (65.9%) and well above M3DM variants (~55%), while its 87.3% P-AUROC is the best reported across all methods. Cross-category transfer (Table 2) shows BTP maintains >80% mean P-AUROC for most training categories, with gemstone yielding the highest (86.7%) and toffees the lowest (73.5%), confirming robust generalization despite structural diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes pre-trained Point-Language Models (specifically ULIP/ULIP-2) for zero-shot 3D anomaly detection, departing from the dominant VLM-based pipeline (PointAD, MVP) that renders point clouds into multi-view 2D images and back-projects results. The key architectural departure is the Multi-Granularity Feature Embedding Module (MGFEM), which fuses multi-layer PointBERT patch features, a learnable geometric descriptor (GFCM replacing handcrafted FPFH), and the CLS token into a unified text-aligned space, combined with a three-term joint loss (local focal+dice, global BCE, geometric contrastive) that has no direct analogue in prior 3D anomaly detection work. The hybrid learnable prompt design follows the broader prompt-learning trend in zero-shot anomaly detection (AdaCLIP, AnomalyCLIP) but adapts it to the point-language modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By operating natively in 3D space, BTP eliminates the view-selection bias and projection-back-projection overhead inherent in VLM-based pipelines, making it more suitable for industrial deployment where rendering angles are uncontrolled. However, the notable gap between point-level (84.5% P-AUROC) and object-level (61.4% O-AUROC) performance—where PointAD leads at 74.8%—reveals an open question about how to effectively aggregate fine-grained point scores into reliable global anomaly judgments. The strong cross-category generalization (most training settings >80% P-AUROC) suggests PLMs may be a more transferable backbone than VLMs for 3D inspection, but the evaluation is limited to two relatively small datasets (12 and 40 categories), leaving scalability to larger, more diverse industrial settings untested.
