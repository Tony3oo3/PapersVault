## Problem

Frozen visual encoders (CLIP, DINOv2/v3, SSCD) deployed for non-biometric tasks like retrieval and copy detection on face-containing data may leak identity, yet this leakage has never been measured at the open-set, low-FAR operating points that real deployments target. Prior privacy audits either focus on dedicated face-recognition models or study CLIP in isolation, leaving no calibrated, attacker-aware benchmark for DINOv2/v3 and SSCD and no deployable mitigation that preserves embedding utility.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes L2-normalized 512-dimensional embeddings produced by a frozen encoder (CLIP, DINOv2, DINOv3, or SSCD) from RGB face images. ISP is a fixed d×d orthogonal projector applied as a single matrix multiplication at inference, yielding a re-normalized sanitized embedding with submillisecond added latency. For the evaluation suite, the method also outputs open-set verification scores (cosine similarity of projected query-support pairs), template-inversion reconstructions judged by cross-model FR verification, and face-context attribution indices (FII, CPI, B*) computed under equal-area, FCR-normalized perturbations.

## Method

ISP is a one-shot, moment-based linear projector that removes an estimated identity subspace without retraining the encoder. Given labeled embeddings, it computes per-identity means, forms a centered mean matrix M ∈ R^{d×m}, takes a thin SVD, and retains the top-r left singular vectors U_r; the sanitized embedding is then Pz/‖Pz‖₂ where P = I − U_r U_r^T. The construction depends only on class means (no covariance inversion), runs in O(dm²), and produces a fixed projector exportable to any retrieval pipeline. Rank r is selected on held-out identities to meet a privacy target (e.g., TAR@FAR=10⁻⁴ ≤ 5%), and the paper shows the resulting projector transfers across datasets with minimal degradation, indicating the identity subspace is compact and dataset-agnostic. The approach is positioned as a means-only variant of the Fisher/Mahalanobis solution, trading the theoretical optimality of whitening for numerical robustness and auditability.

## Results

On CelebA-20 at k=1, raw open-set TAR@FAR=10⁻⁴ ranges from 4.5% (DINOv2, DINOv3) to 16.4% (CLIP) for non-FR encoders, versus 93.7% for ArcFace and 93.6% for AdaFace. After ISP, non-FR TAR drops to 2.1–11.9% on CelebA-20 and 0.0–2.7% on VGGFace2-20, approaching chance-level overlap. Utility is largely preserved: ImageNet k-NN top-1 accuracy remains at 85.4–100.1% of raw baseline across all four encoders (Table 2), with DINOv2 showing essentially zero loss (100.1%). Cross-dataset transfer of the ISP projector (trained on one dataset, applied to the other) maintains comparable protection with only minor degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to dedicated FR encoders (ArcFace, AdaFace), all non-FR encoders exhibit dramatically lower identity leakage: CLIP peaks at 16.4% TAR@FAR=10⁻⁴ on CelebA-20 (k=1) versus 93.7% for ArcFace, a gap of over 77 percentage points. Among non-FR encoders, CLIP shows the highest raw leakage on CelebA-20 (16.4–19.8% across k), while DINOv2, DINOv3, and SSCD remain below 10%, suggesting self-supervised and copy-detection encoders are inherently less identity-informative than vision-language encoders. ISP reduces CLIP's CelebA-20 TAR from 16.4% to 11.9% (k=1) and from 19.8% to 13.0% (k=16), a relative reduction of ~27–34%; for DINOv3 on VGGFace2-20, the drop is from 9.6% to 0.4% (k=1), a 96% relative reduction. On utility, ISP matches or slightly outperforms LEACE on ImageNet k-NN (e.g., DINOv2: 100.1% vs. 100.0%; CLIP: 98.3% vs. 98.5%), with differences within experimental noise. Cross-dataset transfer (ISP-X) is generally effective but shows one notable exception: CLIP's VGGFace2-20 TAR at k=1 rises to 13.0% under ISP-X (trained on CelebA-20), suggesting CLIP's identity subspace is less transferable than DINOv2/v3 or SSCD's.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept-erasure lineage (INLP, LEACE, SAL) from low-cardinality binary attributes to high-cardinality open-set facial identity, replacing iterative null-space fitting with a single SVD on class means. It also repurposes template-inversion attacks (DiffMI, Bob, Vec2Face, ALSUV) and face-context attribution tools (saliency, perturbation) that were previously calibrated only for dedicated FR models, adapting them to frozen non-FR encoders at biometric operating points (FAR ≈ 10⁻⁴). Prior privacy audits of visual encoders (Caldarella et al., Hintersdorf et al.) focused on CLIP in isolation or reported closed-set accuracy; this work generalizes the audit to DINOv2/v3 and SSCD and grounds all metrics in open-set, identity-disjoint verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first deployable, auditable mitigation (a single d×d matrix multiply) that suppresses open-set linear identity access to near-chance while retaining 97–100% of ImageNet utility, which is directly actionable for teams deploying frozen encoders under GDPR/CCPA constraints. The cross-dataset transferability of the identity subspace (principal angles > 0.99) suggests operators can fit one projector on a privacy-approved corpus and ship it broadly without retraining. Key open questions remain: ISP certifies only against linear probes, higher-order or non-linear identity cues may persist in the complementary space, and the method has not been stress-tested against black-box or gradient-free adversaries. Extending the framework to training-time integration and to sensitive attributes beyond identity are natural next steps.
