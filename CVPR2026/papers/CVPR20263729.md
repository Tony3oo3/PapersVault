## Problem

In VLM-based source-free cross-domain few-shot learning, the standard cross-entropy fine-tuning loss inherently contains a visual learning component that acts as a shortcut, reducing classification loss without improving the critical cross-modal (visual-text) alignment. This 'discriminability trap' means that enhancing visual feature discriminability—traditionally beneficial—actually suppresses VLM performance in cross-domain settings, a phenomenon previously unexplored in the SF-CDFSL literature.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263729/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates in a source-free N-way K-shot episode setting, taking a small support set of target-domain images (medical, satellite, agricultural) and per-class text prompts (e.g., 'a photo of a cat') as input, producing class labels via cosine similarity between ViT-Base/16 visual embeddings and text embeddings. It is plug-and-play and applicable to text-branch-only (CoOp), visual-branch-only (CLIP-LoRA-Vision), or all-branch (Maple) fine-tuning configurations, requiring only a few lines of code and incurring nearly zero additional computational overhead.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-phase fine-tuning strategy with two modules. First, the Suppressing Visual Learning (SVL) module introduces an anti-visual loss (L_ad) that replaces the true classifier weights with randomly sampled visual features, creating perturbations that make visual features indistinguishable and thereby suppress the shortcut visual learning during early epochs. Second, the Relationship Alignment (RA) module constructs a fused similarity matrix that linearly interpolates between the visual feature similarity matrix and the text feature similarity matrix over training epochs, then applies a KL-divergence loss to gradually guide visual feature relationships toward text feature relationships. During initial epochs (first 3/5 of training), the total loss is L_vlm + β·L_ra + λ·L_ad with small hyperparameters; in later epochs, only L_vlm is used, allowing visual learning to resume once cross-modal alignment is established.

## Results

On the 5-way 1-shot CDFSL benchmark, the method applied to PE-Core-LoRA achieves 62.14% average accuracy across four cross-domain datasets (ISIC, EuroSAT, CropDisease, ChestX), outperforming the unmodified PE-Core-LoRA (59.78%) by 2.36 points. On the 5-way 5-shot setting, PE-Core-LoRa + OURS reaches 70.29% average, surpassing the strongest prior baseline (CLIP-LoRA-Vision at 66.50%) by 3.79 points. The method also improves CLIP-LoRA-Vision from 56.07% to 58.26% (1-shot) and from 66.50% to 68.38% (5-shot), and SigLIP2-LoRA from 54.14% to 57.01% (1-shot), demonstrating consistent gains across all three VLM backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is evaluated as a plug-and-play extension on top of three representative fine-tuning strategies: CoOp (text-branch only), CLIP-LoRA-Vision (visual-branch only), and Maple (all branches), as well as SigLIP2-LoRA and PE-Core-LoRA. It consistently outperforms every baseline: on 5-way 1-shot, PE-Core-LoRA + OURS (62.14% avg) beats the next-best CLIP-LoRA-Vision (56.07%) by 6.07 points and StepSTP (52.68%) by 9.46 points. On 5-way 5-shot, the same configuration (70.29%) surpasses CLIP-LoRA-Vision (66.50%) by 3.79 points and StepSTP (65.97%) by 4.32 points. The gains are largest on the most challenging dataset ISIC (medical skin lesions), where PE-Core-LoRA + OURS improves from 40.89% to 45.01% (1-shot) and from 58.81% to 61.41% (5-shot), reflecting the method's effectiveness in high-domain-gap scenarios. Even when applied to the text-branch-only CoOp, the method yields modest but consistent improvements (e.g., 51.77% → 52.36% on 1-shot avg), confirming generality across different fine-tuning paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new conceptual ground at the intersection of VLM fine-tuning and shortcut learning theory. While prior work has identified the modality gap in VLMs [28, 38] and its disruption in cross-domain scenarios [18, 21, 34, 41], and while SF-CDFSL methods (LoRA, CoOp, Maple) have been developed to address it, no prior work has decomposed the standard cross-entropy fine-tuning loss into its visual-learning and cross-modal-learning components. The paper repurposes the shortcut learning framework [9, 35] from traditional vision to explain why the visual-learning component acts as a resource-diverting shortcut that undermines the cross-modal alignment that existing methods implicitly target. The proposed SVL and RA modules are new loss designs, but they draw on standard tools (random classifier perturbation, KL divergence) applied in a novel combination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The counterintuitive finding that enhancing visual discriminability degrades VLM performance in cross-domain few-shot settings challenges a long-held assumption in visual learning and provides a concrete, actionable explanation for why existing fine-tuning strategies underperform in large-domain-gap scenarios. The plug-and-play nature of the method (a few lines of code, applicable to LoRA, CoOp, and Maple across CLIP/SigLIP/PE-Core) makes it immediately adoptable. Open questions include whether the two-phase suppression schedule generalizes to other VLM architectures or multi-modal tasks beyond classification, and whether the 'dual-valve' decomposition holds for other loss functions (e.g., contrastive or InfoNCE-based objectives). The theoretical proof (Theorem 4.1) relies partly on empirical observation for the cross-class case, leaving room for a tighter analytical treatment.
