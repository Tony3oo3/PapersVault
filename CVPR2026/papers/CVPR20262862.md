## Problem

Recurrent video generators (e.g., GSPN/GSTPN) achieve linear complexity but must partition sequences into fixed-size chunks for GPU parallelization, creating a 'chunk isolation' problem where each chunk loses access to hidden states from prior chunks, causing temporal discontinuities and identity loss. Additionally, for longer videos spanning multiple segments, previously generated segments are cleared from memory, leaving no mechanism for cross-segment consistency without expensive camera annotations or frame storage.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt and generates 81-frame videos at 480×832 resolution (~5 s at 16 fps) using 50 denoising steps. After VAE encoding, latents have shape [C_z=16, T=21, H'=60, W'=104], and each GSTPN orientation processes the 4D tensor as a 2D sequence of length 200 (chunk size L=200). The LCaM memory bank stores up to M historical segment latents in BFloat16, achieving >60× storage compression over raw frames.

## Method

The paper introduces a dual-granularity memory framework on top of GSTPN (a multi-orientation recurrent architecture scanning ST, WTH, and HTW projections). For intra-chunk consistency, Context Memory designates the first N_sink=3 columns as learnable sink anchors that persist across all chunks via input-independent gating matrices, and retains N_buf=2 boundary positions from the preceding chunk for local continuity, adding only ~150K parameters (<0.1% overhead). For inter-segment consistency, LCaM maintains a FIFO bank of historical VAE latents, retrieves the top-K=3 most relevant segments via cosine similarity on spatiotemporal average-pooled descriptors (threshold τ=0.3), and fuses them through multi-head cross-attention with a learnable sigmoid gate initialized near zero. The entire system is trained via DMD knowledge distillation from WanVideo-1.3B, with an auxiliary memory consistency loss (Frobenius norm between conditioned and unconditioned student latents) that activates only when the memory bank is non-empty.

## Results

On WanVideo-1.3B (81 frames × 480×832, 33K tokens), the dual-memory GSTPN achieves the fastest inference at 67s, a 1.54× speedup over the full-attention baseline (103s). It leads all efficient methods on imaging quality (IQ: 62.3, surpassing even full attention's 62.1), aesthetic quality (AQ: 55.9), aesthetic video quality (VA: 75.5), technical video quality (VT: 81.0), and vision reward (VR: 0.040). Subject consistency (SC: 92.8) is competitive with full attention (93.0), while overall consistency (OC: 21.6) trails the full-attention baseline (23.3) and most efficient baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six efficient attention baselines (DiTFastAttn, SVG, SageAttn, SpargeAttn, MoBA, VMoBA) on imaging quality (62.3 vs. 60.0–61.0), aesthetic quality (55.9 vs. 54.8–55.7), aesthetic video quality (75.5 vs. 72.8–75.1), technical video quality (81.0 vs. 78.2–80.8), and vision reward (0.040 vs. 0.021–0.035), while also achieving the lowest latency (67s vs. 77–126s). Against the full-attention reference, the method matches or exceeds it on IQ (62.3 vs. 62.1) and is within 0.2–1.9 points on AQ, VA, VT, and SC, but lags on overall consistency (21.6 vs. 23.3) and vision reward (0.040 vs. 0.059), which the authors attribute to architectural heterogeneity between the recurrent GSTPN pathway and LCaM's cross-attention conditioning. MoBA achieves the highest OC among efficient methods (23.6) but at the cost of the slowest latency (126s), while SpargeAttn offers the best speed among attention-based baselines (77s) with moderate quality. The 1.54× speedup over full attention comes from replacing quadratic-complexity self-attention with linear-complexity recurrent propagation augmented by only ~200K additional parameters for the memory mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GSPN (CVPR 2025) from 2D spatial propagation to a 4D spatiotemporal variant (GSTPN) and identifies a previously unaddressed limitation—chunk isolation—that arises when recurrent architectures are parallelized via fixed-size chunks. The Context Memory mechanism adapts the attention-sink principle from StreamingLLM (ICLR 2024) to recurrent causality, replacing passive KV-cache retention with learnable sink columns that actively participate in propagation. LCaM departs from the frame-based Context-as-Memory approach [61] by operating entirely in VAE latent space, eliminating camera-annotation requirements and achieving ~60× storage compression. The distillation pipeline builds on the DMD framework, but the paper's core contribution is the dual-granularity memory design rather than the distillation objective itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.54× inference speedup with competitive (and in IQ, marginally superior) quality demonstrates that recurrent architectures can practically replace attention in video diffusion transformers, which is significant given the quadratic cost of full self-attention at high resolution and long sequences. However, the Overall Consistency (21.6 vs 23.3) and Vision Reward (0.040 vs 0.059) gaps relative to full attention suggest that the architectural heterogeneity between recurrent propagation and cross-attention conditioning introduces a consistency tax that remains unresolved. The approach is validated only on a single 1.3B model at 480p/81 frames, leaving open whether the dual-memory framework scales to larger models, higher resolutions, or substantially longer videos where inter-segment consistency becomes more critical. A key open question is whether the OC gap can be closed by deeper integration of LCaM into the recurrent pathway rather than treating it as a separate cross-attention module.
