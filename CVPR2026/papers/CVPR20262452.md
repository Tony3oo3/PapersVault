## Problem

Propagation-based video editing requires large-scale paired (source, edited) video datasets that are expensive and complex to acquire. Existing work either synthesizes pairs only for local edits via object masks, relies on auxiliary signals (depth, optical flow) that propagate errors, or uses computationally expensive iterative diffusion sampling to generate paired data, limiting the diversity and scalability of training supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262452/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a source video, a single edited first frame (synthesized by an external image editor such as Gemini 2.5 Flash), and a text prompt, and outputs a fully edited video at 480×832 resolution. The edited first frame and the entire source video are concatenated along the temporal axis and fed as condition features to the adapter, while denoising is performed with 25 UniPC steps (~120 s for the 14B model, ~30 s for the 1.3B model).

## Method

PropFly generates training pairs on-the-fly from a frozen Wan2.1 flow-matching backbone by performing a one-step clean latent estimation (x̂₀|t = x_t − t·v_θ) at two different CFG scales: a low scale (ω_L=1) yields the structurally faithful 'source' latent and a high scale (ω_H=7) yields the semantically edited 'target' latent, both derived from the same noised latent x_t to guarantee perfect structural alignment. A trainable VACE adapter (with N_B/S_in DiT blocks injected at stride S_in into the frozen backbone) is conditioned on the full source latent (structure) and the first frame of the target latent (style), and is trained with a Guidance-Modulated Flow Matching (GMFM) loss that regresses the adapter's velocity prediction onto the stop-gradient high-CFG velocity of the frozen VDM. Random Style Prompt Fusion (RSPF) appends randomly sampled style descriptors to the video caption to diversify the content–style combinations seen during training, improving generalization to unseen edits.

## Results

On EditVerseBench-Appearance, PropFly-14B achieves SOTA across all five metrics: Pick 20.42 (video quality), Frame CLIP 28.71 and Video CLIP 26.05 (text alignment), and CLIP 99.21 / DINO 99.05 (temporal consistency). On the TGVE benchmark, PropFly-14B leads all four metrics with Pick 21.19, CLIP 0.978, ViCLIP_dir 0.228, and ViCLIP_out 0.278. The smaller PropFly-1.3B model also outperforms all baselines on most metrics, confirming the training pipeline's effectiveness independent of model scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PropFly-14B outperforms all 14 baselines on every metric in both Table 1 (EditVerseBench-Appearance) and Table 2 (TGVE). Against the strongest text-guided method, Runway Aleph (Pick 20.19, Video CLIP 24.96), PropFly-14B gains +0.23 Pick and +1.09 Video CLIP, while also leading on temporal consistency (CLIP 99.21 vs. 98.82). Among propagation-based baselines, PropFly-14B surpasses AnyV2V (Pick 19.78, CLIP 95.97) by +0.64 Pick and +3.24 CLIP, and Señorita-2M (Pick 19.69, CLIP 98.04) by +0.73 Pick and +1.17 CLIP. On TGVE, PropFly-14B leads the best propagation baseline Señorita (CLIP 0.961, ViCLIP_dir 0.220) by +0.017 CLIP and +0.008 ViCLIP_dir, and the best text-guided baseline EVE (Pick 20.76, ViCLIP_out 0.262) by +0.43 Pick and +0.016 ViCLIP_out. Notably, PropFly-1.3B (1.3B params) already beats the 14B VACE text-guided model on Pick (20.35 vs. 20.04) and CLIP (99.03 vs. 98.96), demonstrating that the on-the-fly training pipeline is effective even at smaller scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

PropFly builds on the flow-matching training framework (Lipman et al.) and the VACE adapter architecture (Jiang et al., ICCV 2025), but its core conceptual departure is repurposing Classifier-Free Guidance (Ho & Salimans) from a quality-enhancement mechanism into a data-generation tool: varying CFG scales on a frozen VDM produces structurally aligned yet semantically distinct latent pairs without any paired dataset or auxiliary signals (optical flow, depth maps). This contrasts with prior propagation-based methods—AnyV2V (inversion-based, zero-shot), Señorita-2M (large-scale precomputed paired data), CCEdit/Go-with-the-Flow (auxiliary guidance), and GenProp (mask-based, local edits only)—all of which either require expensive paired data, per-video optimization, or are limited to specific edit types. The GMFM loss further departs from standard flow matching by training the adapter to match the high-CFG velocity rather than reconstructing the original video, directly encoding the desired transformation as the supervision target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for large-scale paired video datasets or auxiliary guidance signals, PropFly offers a practical and scalable training recipe for propagation-based video editing that can generate infinite diverse training pairs from a limited set of real videos. The finding that one-step clean latent estimation outperforms full iterative sampling (due to path divergence) is practically important for training efficiency. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond appearance-based edits (e.g., camera motion, structural changes), whether it transfers to non-flow-matching diffusion backbones, and how the method scales to longer videos or higher resolutions. The reliance on a frozen VDM also means the quality ceiling is bounded by the backbone's generative capability.
