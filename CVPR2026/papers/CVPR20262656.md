## Problem

Existing image editing methods struggle with Portrait Collection Generation (PCG), which requires simultaneously executing complex multi-attribute modifications (pose, camera viewpoint, spatial layout) while preserving fine-grained details like makeup, clothing, and accessories. ControlNet-style methods constrain spatial diversity, instruction-following models handle only single-dimensional edits, and zero-shot personalization methods (IP-Adapter, InstantID) rely on high-level semantic embeddings that lose pixel-level detail under complex transformations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262656/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single high-resolution reference portrait image (832×1216 pixels) and a natural language modification text as input, producing a generated portrait image that satisfies the described transformation while preserving reference details. Modification texts range from single-dimensional changes (e.g., a pose shift) to complex combinations spanning camera distance, viewpoint, spatial layout, and subject-level variations, reflecting real-world portrait collection diversity.

## Method

SCheese builds on an SDXL denoising backbone and introduces two complementary conditioning modules. The Fusion IP-Adapter concatenates reference image features (via an image encoder) with modification text features (via a text encoder), projects them jointly, and is trained with a KL-divergence alignment loss toward target image features plus teacher forcing that probabilistically substitutes fused features with ground-truth target features. The ConsistencyNet is a separate SDXL-inpainting UNet (mask layers removed) that extracts multi-scale low-level features from the reference image and injects them at every U-Net block via a Decoupled-Attention mechanism: parallel self-attention (spatial dependencies in the generated image) and cross-attention (alignment to reference features) are averaged, then fused with text and Fusion IP-Adapter features through an additional decoupled cross-attention layer. The full model is trained via supervised fine-tuning on the CHEESE dataset (24K collections, ~576K triplets) for 50K iterations on 8×H800 GPUs.

## Results

On the CHEESE test set (~2K triplets), SCheese achieves the highest scores on CLIP-T (0.436), Qwen-DP (0.855), and Qwen-PF (0.793), demonstrating superior instruction following and detail preservation. While FLUX.1 Kontext (CLIP-I 0.857, DINO-I 0.791) and Kolors IP-Adapter (CLIP-I 0.853, DINO-I 0.824) achieve marginally higher reference-similarity scores, they exhibit substantially lower prompt-following scores (0.679 and 0.428 respectively), reflecting a 'copy-pasting' tendency. In the user study, SCheese scores 0.778 on DP, 0.803 on PF, and 0.688 on Collection Coherence, compared to 0.653/0.670/0.467 for Kontext and 0.410/0.397/0.293 for Kolors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCheese outperforms all nine baselines on the three most task-relevant metrics: CLIP-T (instruction alignment), Qwen-DP (detail preservation), and Qwen-PF (prompt following). Against the strongest zero-shot competitors, SCheese beats FLUX.1 Kontext by +0.060 on Qwen-PF (0.793 vs 0.679) and +0.063 on Qwen-DP (0.855 vs 0.792), while Kontext leads on CLIP-I by only +0.018 — a gap the authors attribute to Kontext's tendency to replicate the reference rather than follow the edit. Kolors IP-Adapter shows an even more extreme copy-paste pattern, scoring 0.853 on CLIP-I but only 0.428 on Qwen-PF, a 0.365 gap that SCheese avoids (0.839 CLIP-I, 0.793 Qwen-PF). Per-subject fine-tuning methods (DreamBooth, DB LoRA) trail significantly across all metrics, with DreamBooth scoring just 0.305 on Qwen-DP and 0.443 on Qwen-PF, confirming that test-time adaptation is impractical for PCG. Among zero-shot methods, IP-Adapter and IP-Adapter+ achieve moderate reference similarity (0.764–0.794 CLIP-I) but fail to follow complex multi-attribute instructions (Qwen-PF ≤ 0.549), while Emu2, despite strong semantic understanding, scores only 0.352 on Qwen-PF due to its lack of dedicated personalization mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCheese builds on the IP-Adapter family of image-conditioned diffusion models and extends them with a text-image fusion mechanism inspired by Composed Image Retrieval, where modification text and reference image features are jointly projected to approximate target-image conditioning. The ConsistencyNet module repurposes a pretrained inpainting UNet as a low-level feature extractor, injecting pixel-level detail through a Decoupled-Attention mechanism that separates spatial self-attention from reference cross-attention. The paper departs from prior personalization work (DreamBooth, LoRA, InstantID) by eliminating per-subject training, and from text-guided editing methods (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush) by targeting complex multi-attribute modifications (simultaneous pose, camera, and layout changes) rather than single-dimensional edits. The CHEESE dataset itself is constructed via an LVLM-based annotation pipeline with inversion-based CLIP validation, a technique borrowed from composed image retrieval literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CHEESE provides the first large-scale benchmark (24K collections, ~576K triplets) for a practically important task—generating coherent portrait collections from a single reference with natural language instructions—filling a gap where prior datasets either lacked complex multi-attribute annotations or fine-grained detail consistency. The paper's identification of the 'copy-pasting' trade-off (high reference similarity but poor instruction following) in methods like FLUX.1 Kontext and Emu2 is a useful diagnostic for the field. However, the remaining gap between SCheese and target images in the user study (0.778 vs. 0.936 DP) suggests that pixel-level detail preservation under large geometric transformations remains an open challenge, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the portrait domain to other subject types is unexplored.
