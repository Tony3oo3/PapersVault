## Problem

Existing label-free proxy metrics for evaluating model generalization under distribution shift—such as confidence, accuracy-on-the-line, and sharpness—rely solely on model outputs and are unreliable due to underspecification and overconfidence. This paper asks whether a model's internal computational mechanisms (circuits) provide a stronger, label-free signal of generalization performance, addressing two practical needs: selecting the best model before deployment and monitoring silent performance degradation after deployment.

## Input / Output

The method consumes ViT model weights and unlabeled target images, extracting circuits at sub-layer granularity (each attention head and MLP block as a node) via EAP-IG with mean-ablation. For DDB, only the target-data circuit is needed; for CSS, an ID baseline circuit (typically provided by the model provider) is additionally required. Outputs are scalar scores—DDB as a log-ratio of deep-to-shallow dependency, and CSS as a distance between circuit representations—that directly yield model rankings or binary alarm decisions without any target labels.

## Method

The paper defines circuits as continuous edge-weight mappings where each edge's weight is the expected KL divergence between the full model and the model with that edge mean-ablated, then aggregates weights into an inter-layer dependency matrix (IDM). For pre-deployment selection, CCA over IDMs across a model zoo reveals a Universal Generalization Motif: deep-layer edges correlate positively with OOD performance while shallow-layer edges correlate negatively, motivating Dependency Depth Bias (DDB), the log-ratio of total deep-to-shallow dependency for a chosen set of target layers. For post-deployment monitoring, Circuit Shift Score (CSS) measures the distance between the test-data circuit and the ID baseline circuit; the best variant (CSS with SRCC on edge-weight vectors) captures relative rewiring patterns rather than absolute magnitudes. A threshold calibration strategy uses corrupted ID validation sets (CIFAR-10-C corruptions and stylizations) to set the alarm threshold without target labels.

## Results

For pre-deployment model selection, DDB_out achieves the highest average correlation (0.766 ± 0.029) with true OOD performance across PACS, Camelyon17, and Terra Incognita, outperforming the best baseline (ID Accuracy, 0.632 ± 0.047) by 13.4%. For post-deployment monitoring, CSS(v,SRCC) achieves an average correlation of 0.811 ± 0.041 across PACS, FMoW, Camelyon17, and ImageNet, exceeding the strongest baseline (ATC, 0.470 ± 0.095) by 34.1%. On the Camelyon17 alarm-raising task, CSS(v,SRCC) achieves a ~45% improvement in detection F1 over the best baseline within the clinically acceptable performance range (0.8–0.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDB_out (0.766) outperforms all eight baselines in pre-deployment model selection, with the largest margins over Sharpness (0.217, +54.9%) and RANKME (0.273, +49.3%), and a 13.4% improvement over the strongest baseline ID Accuracy (0.632). In post-deployment monitoring, CSS(v,SRCC) (0.811) dominates all baselines, with the most dramatic gains over AC (0.391, +42.0%) and ANE (0.418, +39.3%), and a 34.1% improvement over the best baseline ATC (0.470). Notably, ATC is the only baseline that achieves competitive correlation on ImageNet (0.942 R²) but collapses on PACS (0.645) and Camelyon17 (0.028), whereas CSS(v,SRCC) maintains strong correlation (≥0.519 R²) across all four post-deployment datasets. Vector-based CSS variants consistently outperform graph-based ones (e.g., CSS(g,NetLSD) averages only 0.129), indicating that fine-grained edge-weight patterns are more informative than coarse topological similarity. The paper also shows that DDB_out's correlation is stable across the hyperparameter τ ∈ {0.1, 0.3, 0.5} (R² ranges 0.744–0.801), confirming robustness to the depth-split ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: (1) label-free generalization evaluation, where prior work relies on output-level proxies (confidence, entropy, accuracy-on-the-line) or feature-level proxies (RANKME, α-ReQ, MDE), and (2) mechanistic interpretability, where circuit discovery methods (ACDC, EAP, EAP-IG) have been used to explain model behavior (induction heads, factual recall, logical reasoning) but never as predictive metrics. The key departure is repurposing circuit discovery—specifically EAP-IG with mean ablation—as a quantitative generalization signal, moving the evaluation lens from model outputs to internal causal pathways. The paper also builds on the underspecification observation (multiple models with identical ID accuracy but different OOD accuracy) and the overconfidence problem of probability-based proxies, positioning circuit metrics as a structural alternative that avoids both failure modes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two high-stakes deployment challenges (pre-deployment model selection and post-deployment silent-failure monitoring) with a principled, label-free approach that outperforms all existing proxies by 13.4% and 34.1% in correlation with true OOD performance. The finding that inter-layer circuit topology is a consistent generalization signal pre-deployment but becomes contradictory post-deployment (requiring a shift to fine-grained rewiring metrics) is a useful conceptual distinction for practitioners. The main limitation is the computational cost of circuit discovery, which is acceptable for one-time model selection but hinders real-time monitoring; the authors note that more efficient circuit discovery algorithms remain an active research direction. A promising future direction is directly optimizing circuit metrics during training to encourage more generalizable internal mechanisms, which could close the loop between interpretability and model design.
