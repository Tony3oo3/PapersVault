## Problem

State-of-the-art multimodal person Re-ID methods, trained on full-sentence captions, suffer up to 50% Recall@1 degradation when queried with short, keyword-based attribute phrases due to ambiguity, training-data bias toward complete descriptions, and under-represented rare attributes. No prior benchmark or method explicitly requires the retrieved image to satisfy both identity and a short composite attribute constraint simultaneously, leaving a gap between natural user search behavior and what existing systems can handle.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 224×224 reference image, a short attribute text query (ranging from a single keyword like 'straw hat' to a multi-attribute phrase like 'red jacket and white sneakers'), and a gallery of images, producing cosine-similarity-ranked gallery results where a match must satisfy both identity and the stated attributes. At inference, no VLM or external annotation is required—only the frozen CLIP encoders, a pose estimator for spatial patch grouping, and the learned FiLM fusion module. For standard CC-ReID (image-only retrieval), the text query is simply omitted and raw image PARs are compared directly.

## Method

The architecture builds Part-Aware Representations (PAR) by running a ViT image encoder (EVA02-CLIP-L/14) and a frozen CLIP text encoder, then using an HRNet-W32 pose estimator to assign ViT patch tokens to five semantic body regions (head, top, bottom, feet, other) via mean-pooling and per-part MLP projections; the identity slot concatenates the global token with the head-region feature to remain clothing-invariant. Text is projected into matching part slots through learned linear maps, with a parsing gate zeroing out unmentioned parts so that 'red jacket' activates only the top slot. Image and text PARs are fused slot-wise via a lightweight FiLM residual module that modulates appearance regions while leaving the identity slot untouched. Training combines a standard identity triplet loss with a two-part Dense Disentangling Loss: L_DDL1 enforces identity-attribute separation through a multi-triplet objective over four relational sample types (full match, attribute-only, identity-only, no match), and L_DDL2 penalizes inter-part cosine similarity to prevent feature leakage across body regions.

## Results

On the proposed CA-ReID benchmark, the method achieves 58.6% R@1 and 20.4 mAP on Celeb-ReID-Light (Hard), outperforming Instruct-ReID by +17.0% R@1 and +5.7% mAP. On COCAS+Real2 (Hard), it reaches 50.4% R@1 and 21.3 mAP, a +6.4% R@1 gain over Instruct-ReID. For Easy queries (full descriptions), performance is on par with Instruct-ReID (83.1 vs 81.8 R@1 on Celeb-ReID-L), confirming the method does not sacrifice standard retrieval quality. On standard CC-ReID benchmarks (LTCC, PRCC), the method is competitive but trails the best image-only baselines (DIFFER: 68.5 Top-1 on LTCC) by a few points, as expected given its attribute-conditioned design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is against Instruct-ReID [21], the strongest multi-modal baseline that pairs a reference image with full-sentence descriptions. CA-ReID wins across all difficulty levels on both datasets, with the largest gap on Hard queries (+17.0% R@1 on Celeb-ReID-L, +6.4% R@1 on COCAS+Real2), where short keyword queries expose Instruct-ReID's weakness in handling ambiguous, under-represented attributes. DIFFER [38], a strong image-only CC-ReID method, performs poorly in the CA-ReID setting (23.6 R@1 Easy on Celeb-ReID-L) because it ignores the text conditioning entirely. On standard CC-ReID benchmarks (Table 5), CA-ReID trails DIFFER (68.5 vs 63.8 Top-1 on LTCC) and Instruct-ReID (66.7 vs 63.8), but outperforms older baselines like CAL (55.2), AIM (57.9), CCFA (45.3), and CLIP3D (42.1) on LTCC, and surpasses Instruct-ReID on PRCC mAP (43.4 vs 52.3 is actually lower, but CA-ReID beats Instruct-ReID on PRCC Top-1: 55.2 vs 54.2). The ablation (Table 4) confirms both DDL components contribute: adding L_DDL2 alone gives +0.3 R@1 over identity-only, and adding L_DDL1 further adds +1.1 R@1 on Celeb-ReID-L Hard.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new task at the intersection of Composed Image Retrieval (CIR) and person Re-ID: given a reference image and short composite attribute keywords, retrieve gallery images satisfying both identity and attribute constraints. It departs from Instruct-ReID (LI-ReID), which pairs a reference image with full-sentence descriptions but evaluates identity-only, by enforcing attribute satisfaction and supporting ambiguous short queries. The Part-Aware Representations build on pose-guided feature disentanglement (PFD, DIFFER) and part-based Re-ID, while the FiLM-based fusion and CLIP backbone follow the CIR and VLM-ReID literature. The Dense Disentangling Loss extends standard triplet-based identity losses with an explicit inter-part orthogonality regularizer and multi-relational triplet mining over identity-attribute combinations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that SOTA VLM-based Re-ID methods lose roughly half their Recall@1 when switching from full sentences to short keyword queries is practically significant, as short queries are the natural user interaction pattern. The CA-ReID benchmark with its three ambiguity levels provides a structured way to evaluate this gap, and the pose-guided part-aware approach offers a principled mechanism for grounding text attributes to body regions. Limitations include reliance on pose estimation quality (degraded under occlusion), the diffuse 'other' category that resists spatial grounding, and the observation that rare/fine-grained attributes still underperform. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly open-vocabulary attributes without the predefined five-region taxonomy, and how it handles multi-person or heavily occluded scenes.
