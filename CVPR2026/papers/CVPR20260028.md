## Problem

Pre-trained diffusion-based generative models for scene recovery follow a 'train on synthetic, test on real' paradigm that suffers from two critical limitations: the autoencoding pathway is never calibrated to real degraded distributions, producing non-faithful textures, and the structure-recovery capacity is over-fitted to a single parameter space, yielding poor adaptation and weak generalization to unseen degradations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260028/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image (low-light, hazy, or underwater) and produces a single restored RGB image at the same resolution. No paired clean reference is required at inference time; the real-domain loss is a CLIP-based contrastive objective comparing the recovery against learned positive/negative text prompts. Training uses only 500 synthetic image pairs and 50 real degraded images per scenario, with 500 held-out real images for testing.

## Method

BiProLoRA builds on SD-Turbo and introduces two stages. First, a self-supervised Distribution-Fidelity Learning (DFL) stage inserts lightweight zero-convolution adapters into the VAE encoder-decoder pathway and trains them with L1 reconstruction on real degraded data, calibrating the latent space to task-irrelevant real distributions before any denoising occurs. Second, a bilevel joint modeling stage treats prompt embeddings as a degradation-aware hyperparameter (upper level, optimized on real data via a CLIP contrastive loss) that steers LoRA's structure-recovery capacity (lower level, optimized on synthetic paired data via L2+LPIPS). The bilevel problem is solved with a penalty-based scheme: a mirror copy of LoRA is quickly optimized on synthetic data to approximate the lower-level value function, and the prompt gradient includes a penalty term that prevents the prompt from degrading LoRA's synthetic-learned structural integrity, thereby decoupling structure preservation from real-degradation adaptation.

## Results

On the seen DARKFACE dataset, BiProLoRA achieves the best score across all five no-reference metrics: NIQE 2.971, LIQE 2.585, DE 7.201, MUSIQ 61.33, and ARNIQ 0.652. Generalization to unseen datasets is strong: on ExDark it leads all metrics (NIQE 3.806, MUSIQ 54.30, ARNIQ 0.584), and on NOD it wins four of five metrics (NIQE 3.119, LIQE 1.258, DE 7.160, ARNIQ 0.559), with ReDDiT slightly ahead on MUSIQ (48.11 vs 46.01). For hazy and underwater scenes, the method also achieves the best scores on all metrics (e.g., MUSIQ 61.79 on hazy, NIQE 3.514 on underwater). On the downstream nighttime object detection task (ExDark), BiProLoRA boosts mAP from 37.5 (baseline) to 40.9, outperforming Di-Retinex (36.2), LightenDiff (36.8), and ReDDiT (36.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiProLoRA outperforms all 12 low-light baselines on every metric for the seen DARKFACE dataset, with the closest competitor being Zero-IG on NIQE (3.030 vs 2.971) and ReDDiT on DE (7.163 vs 7.201). On the unseen ExDark dataset, the method leads all five metrics, with the largest margins over QuadPrior on MUSIQ (54.30 vs 49.61) and ARNIQ (0.584 vs 0.548). On unseen NOD, the method wins four of five metrics; ReDDiT edges ahead on MUSIQ (48.11 vs 46.01) and LIQE (1.242 vs 1.258), but BiProLoRA still leads on NIQE (3.119 vs 3.244), DE (7.160 vs 7.096), and ARNIQ (0.559 vs 0.548). For hazy scenes, BiProLoRA surpasses C2PNet, UME, PHAT, and WeaDiff on all metrics (e.g., MUSIQ 61.79 vs 57.43 for C2PNet). For underwater scenes, it outperforms SUIR, HUPE, SMDR, and WF-Diff across the board (e.g., NIQE 3.514 vs 3.735 for WF-Diff). On the downstream detection task, BiProLoRA achieves mAP 40.9, exceeding the Cascade R-CNN baseline (37.5) by 3.4 points and the best prior method ReDDiT (36.9) by 4.0 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of diffusion-based image restoration and parameter-efficient adaptation, building on SD-Turbo as the generative backbone, LoRA for structure recovery capacity, and prompt embeddings for degradation-aware modulation. It departs from prior 'train on synthetic, test on real' approaches by introducing a self-supervised distribution-fidelity learning stage that calibrates the VAE autoencoding pathway directly on real degraded data (rather than clean or synthetic images), and by framing the LoRA–prompt interaction as a bilevel hyperparameter optimization problem rather than a naive joint training objective. The bilevel formulation draws on penalty-based bilevel optimization [Shen & Chen, ICML 2023] and the broader hyperparameter-optimization literature [Franceschi et al., ICML 2018], repurposing these tools for the specific challenge of synthetic-to-real scene recovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled decomposition of the synthetic-to-real adaptation problem—separating reusable structure recovery (LoRA) from degradation-specific modulation (prompts)—which could inform future work on adapting any pretrained generative model to new real-world domains. Its practical appeal is reinforced by the modest real-data requirement (50 real images per scenario) and consistent gains across low-light, hazy, and underwater settings. However, the authors acknowledge that compounded degradations (e.g., device-induced motion blur) remain challenging, and the bilevel optimization adds training complexity (three optimizers, inner/outer loops) that may limit adoption. Open questions include whether the DFL calibration generalizes to video or multi-modal restoration, and whether the bilevel framework can be simplified without sacrificing the structure-preservation guarantee.
