## Problem

SAM, when applied to medical images, frequently over-segments due to ambiguous low-contrast anatomical boundaries, and existing SAM-based few-shot segmentation methods (e.g., ProtoSAM) only generate foreground prompts, overlooking the critical role of accurate background prompts in constraining this over-segmentation. The paper reformulates SAM-based FSMIS as a background-centric prompt localization problem to address this gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260108/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 1-shot support pair (image + binary mask) and a query image, all reshaped to 256×256, and produces two sets of point prompts: N_f foreground points sampled from high-confidence correlation regions and N_p background points localized near the object boundary. These prompts are fed to a frozen SAM (ViT-H) at inference to yield the final segmentation mask. FoB is trained independently of SAM and operates as a plug-and-play prompt generator, decoupling prompt generation from the segmentation backend.

## Method

FoB consists of three collaboratively designed modules. BPPC samples background points from a differential region between two dilated support masks, constructs Gaussian heatmaps, and extracts background prompt prototypes via Masked Average Pooling on ResNet-101 features. BCM suppresses foreground features using a cosine-similarity mask, generates coarse prompt proposals via cross-attention between prototypes and query features, then refines them through a masked attention transformer where the proposals serve as attention bias, followed by a detection head that outputs coarse background prompt coordinates. SPR encodes structural priors via a graph combining an adaptive similarity matrix and a static ring topology, propagates them to query prototypes through cross-instance GCN, and then applies iterative deformable refinement (inspired by Deformable DETR) to progressively correct prompt coordinates. Training combines a Region-aware Contrastive loss (InfoNCE-based) to prevent background prompts from entering the foreground, heatmap and coordinate regression losses, and a foreground classification loss, all optimized in a meta-learning 1-shot episode framework.

## Results

On Abd-CT, FoB+SAM achieves 86.21% and 84.80% average Dice under Settings I and II respectively, outperforming the previous best conventional FSMIS method GMRD by 7.69% and 7.48%. On Abd-MRI, FoB+SAM reaches 84.46% (Setting I) and 80.46% (Setting II), surpassing the second-best by 1.16% on average. On Skin-DS, FoB+S-2D achieves 84.80% (Setting I) and 83.80% (Setting II), while FoB+SAM alone reaches 76.62% and 75.56%, still exceeding all conventional baselines. In the cross-domain setting (CT→MRI), FoB+SAM achieves 73.30% average Dice, outperforming RobustEMD (62.61%) and FAMNet (65.79%) by 10.69% and 7.51% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoB+SAM decisively outperforms all conventional FSMIS baselines: ALPNet (73.35%→86.21% on Abd-CT Setting I, +12.86%), RPT (77.83%→86.21%, +8.38%), GMRD (78.52%→86.21%, +7.69%), and PGRNet (77.17%→86.21%, +9.04%). Among SAM-based methods, FoB+SAM surpasses ProtoSAM (75.50%→86.21% on Abd-CT Setting I, +10.71%) by a wide margin, attributed to ProtoSAM's reliance on foreground-only prompts that fail to constrain over-segmentation. The concurrent AM-SAM achieves a near-tie on Abd-CT (86.19% vs. 86.21%) but is significantly inferior on Abd-MRI (81.40% vs. 84.46% Setting I; 75.56% vs. 80.46% Setting II), where blurred MRI boundaries exacerbate over-segmentation. In the cross-domain evaluation (Table 2), FoB+SAM outperforms both RobustEMD (62.61%→73.30% CT→MRI, +10.69%) and FAMNet (65.79%→73.30%, +7.51%), demonstrating that background-centric prompt localization is more domain-invariant than feature-matching approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SAM as a segmentation backend and the prototypical-network paradigm for few-shot matching, but departs sharply from prior SAM-based FSMIS methods (ProtoSAM, AM-SAM) by arguing that background prompts, not foreground prompts, are the critical constraint for preventing over-segmentation. Conceptually, it borrows the localization machinery from few-shot keypoint detection (Gaussian heatmaps, deformable offset prediction from Deformable DETR) and repurposes it for prompt generation, while introducing a graph-based structural prior (ring topology) specific to medical anatomy. The method is positioned as a plug-and-play module that trains independently of SAM, contrasting with AM-SAM's joint fine-tuning approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that precise background prompts are the binding constraint on SAM's medical-image performance (rather than foreground prompts) reframes how practitioners should design prompting strategies for foundation models in low-contrast domains. The plug-and-play design and strong cross-domain generalization (CT↔MRI) suggest the approach could be adopted broadly without retraining SAM, reducing the barrier to deploying foundation models in clinical settings. Open questions include whether the ring-structure prior generalizes to 3D volumetric segmentation or to anatomical structures with highly irregular boundaries, and whether the approach transfers to other foundation models beyond SAM.
