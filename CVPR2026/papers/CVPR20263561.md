## Problem

Existing scene-level point cloud self-supervised learning methods rely on sample-independent modeling, causing learned semantic representations to be inconsistent across scenes and preventing the construction of a unified, transferable semantic space. This semantic fragmentation limits cross-scene generalization and hinders the establishment of coherent global semantic topology in the latent space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263561/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes scene-level point clouds as input; during pretraining on ScanNetV2, each scene is split into 2 global and 8 local subregions (each reduced to 1024 points via voxel sampling or FPS), and the entire batch is serialized into a single continuous sequence for SSM processing. During finetuning and inference, the model operates on standard single-scene inputs (one scene per forward pass) using only the student network, producing per-point feature embeddings that drive semantic segmentation, object classification, or part segmentation outputs.

## Method

PointCSP introduces two complementary mechanisms. Cross-Sample Semantic Propagation (CSP) serializes a batch of B point cloud samples into a unified sequence of length B×L and processes it through a Gated DeltaNet (enhanced Mamba2) state-space model that recursively propagates hidden states along the serialized dimension, explicitly modeling inter-sample semantic dependencies and establishing global semantic alignment. Pretraining follows a DINO-inspired self-distillation framework with multi-region augmentation (global and local subregions), where the teacher (EMA-updated) sees only global views and the student sees both, optimized with a semantic consistency cross-entropy loss plus a multi-level geometric reconstruction loss. Semantic Preservation Distillation (SPD) addresses the batch-dependency mismatch at finetuning: the teacher continues processing serialized sequences to preserve the global semantic topology, while the student operates on standard single-scene inputs and is aligned to the teacher via an MSE feature-matching loss, enabling stable semantic transfer without requiring batch-level context at inference.

## Results

On S3DIS, PointCSP achieves 88.2% mIoU on Area5 and 93.1% mIoU under 6-fold cross-validation, surpassing the previous SOTA CamPoint (83.3%) by 4.9 points. On 3DSES Silver, it reaches 96.41% OA with pseudo-labels and 95.78% OA with real labels. For instance-level tasks, PointCSP attains 92.8% OA on ScanObjectNN (PB_T50_RS), 93.9% OA on ModelNet40, and 86.8% instance mIoU on ShapeNetPart, all setting new SOTA results.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointCSP outperforms all 20 compared baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On S3DIS Area5, it leads the closest competitor CamPoint [58] by 4.9 mIoU (88.2 vs. 83.3) and Sonata [43] by 12.2 mIoU (88.2 vs. 76.0); the gap widens to 10.8 points over Sonata on the 6-fold protocol (93.1 vs. 82.3). On 3DSES Silver, PointCSP surpasses Swin3D [49] by 2.95 OA (96.41 vs. 93.46) under pseudo-labels and by 5.31 OA (95.78 vs. 90.47) under real labels. For object classification, PointCSP edges out CamPoint [58] on ScanObjectNN (92.8 vs. 92.1 OA) and PointSD [7] on ModelNet40 (93.9 vs. 93.7 OA), with margins of 0.7 and 0.2 points respectively. On ShapeNetPart, the improvement over the strongest baselines (Point-MoDE [57] at 86.5, PointGPT-S [6] and PointMamba [26] at 86.2) is modest at 0.3–0.6 instance mIoU, suggesting the scene-level pretraining advantage is most pronounced on large-scale scene segmentation rather than fine-grained part-level tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DINO self-distillation paradigm (teacher-student with EMA) to point cloud pretraining, but departs fundamentally from prior PC-SSL methods (PointContrast, SSPL, MSP) that model each sample independently. The key conceptual shift is using a state-space model (Gated DeltaNet, an enhanced Mamba2) not for intra-sample feature extraction—as in PointMamba, Mamba3D, or PCM—but for inter-sample semantic propagation by serializing an entire batch into a continuous sequence. The SPD mechanism then addresses a practical gap left open by this serialization: how to transfer batch-level pretrained semantics to single-scene inference without structural degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-sample serialization strategy offers a principled way to enforce global semantic consistency across scenes without requiring larger or more diverse training data, which is a meaningful advantage given the cost of scene-level point cloud collection. However, the fundamental tension between batch-dependent pretraining and single-scene inference remains an open question: while SPD mitigates the gap, it introduces an additional teacher network and MSE constraint that add training overhead, and it is unclear how well the approach scales to very large batches or heterogeneous scene compositions. The 3DSES evaluation on TLS data is encouraging but limited to 30 scenes, leaving cross-sensor generalization underexplored.
