## Problem

Existing 3D point tracking methods rely on RGB cameras at ~30fps, which suffer from motion blur and large inter-frame gaps that miss crucial dynamics in high-speed motion (e.g., insect wingbeats, spinning rotators). Simply increasing RGB frame rate incurs prohibitive storage, bandwidth, and illumination costs. This paper introduces the first systematic task of high-speed 3D point tracking by fusing the texture fidelity of RGB with the microsecond temporal acuity of event cameras.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260194/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 4 synchronized RGB views at 30fps (1080×720) and 2 event camera streams (EVK4, microsecond resolution, 1080×720) from a custom cuboid rig, along with calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics, depth maps (from VGGT for RGB; unprojected-projected from RGB for Event), and initial 3D query points (also from VGGT). Event streams are segmented at 150fps intervals and voxelized into 5 non-overlapping bins before processing. The output is a dense 3D point trajectory at 150fps, a 5× frame-rate uplift over the native RGB capture rate.

## Method

MER-Tracker operates in three stages: (1) a dual-modal 2D feature extractor computes multi-scale CNN features on RGB frames and a CNN-LSTM (3-layer conv + LSTM, hidden dim 256) on event voxel grids to capture temporally dependent motion-change cues; (2) a heterogeneous 3D fusion lifts both modalities into 3D via depth unprojection, applies linear interpolation to upsample RGB 3D features from 30fps to 150fps, then merges and rebalances the combined point cloud using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) anchor sampling to resolve temporal non-smoothness and spatial imbalance; (3) a temporal correlation Transformer associates each query point with K-nearest neighbors from adjacent frames (t−1, t, t+1) to exploit the 'slow-motion' coherence at high frame rates, with LoRA adapters fine-tuned on 70 synthetic FMV-Kubric scenes from MVTracker-pretrained weights (20k steps, 2×A6000, 40h).

## Results

On the FMV-Kubric synthetic dataset, MER-Tracker achieves AJ=72.3, δ_avg=82.4, OA=91.5, and MTE=1.2, outperforming the strongest baseline (MVTracker+Frame.Inter+E2V) by +8.8 in AJ and reducing MTE from 2.0 to 1.2 (−40%). On FMV-Panoptic, it reaches AJ=76.3 and MTE=6.5, improving over the best baseline by +11.1 in AJ. On the real-world object dataset, MER-Tracker achieves RMSE=0.228, a 25.7% reduction over the best baseline (0.307), demonstrating consistent gains across all three evaluation settings at 150fps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two SOTA multi-view 3D tracking methods—Tri-SpaTracker (SpatialTracker) and MVTracker—each tested in four high-frame-rate adaptation configurations (trajectory interpolation, frame repetition, RIFE frame interpolation, and frame interpolation + E2V event-to-video conversion). MER-Tracker wins on every metric across all three datasets. On FMV-Kubric, it surpasses the best MVTracker variant (Frame.Inter+E2V, AJ=63.5) by +8.8 in AJ and cuts MTE from 2.0 to 1.2; Tri-SpaTracker trails further behind at AJ=56.1. On FMV-Panoptic, the gap widens: MER-Tracker reaches AJ=76.3 versus 65.2 for the best baseline, a +11.1 improvement, while also achieving the highest OA (91.5 vs. 82.6). On the real-world dataset, MER-Tracker reduces RMSE from 0.307 to 0.228 (−25.7%), confirming that the event-RGB fusion provides a substantial advantage over RGB-only or event-to-video conversion approaches for high-speed motion tracking.

## Relation to Prior Work

MER-Tracker builds directly on MVTracker's multi-view 3D point tracking framework (Transformer-based trajectory prediction with relationship tokens) and CoTracker3's iterative token construction, adapting them via LoRA fine-tuning for a new high-speed regime. It departs from prior event-based 3D work (E2NeRF, EventNeRF, E-4DGS), which focused on scene reconstruction from event streams, by repurposing event data as a temporal-resolution complement to RGB for point-level trajectory estimation. The heterogeneous 3D fusion strategy—linear interpolation for discrete RGB features combined with anchor sampling (FPS) to balance the merged point cloud—is a novel architectural choice not present in either the event-reconstruction or standard multi-view tracking literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic formulation of high-speed 3D point tracking with multi-view Event-RGB fusion, providing both a physical capture rig and two synthetic benchmarks (FMV-Kubric, FMV-Panoptic) with ground-truth trajectories at 150fps, which should lower the barrier for future work in this niche. Key limitations include the small-scale, controlled-object setting (shaking cubes, spinning planes), the absence of a depth sensor in the real rig (forcing surrogate RMSE evaluation), and baselines that are adapted rather than purpose-built for high-speed tracking. Open questions include scalability to human-scale or outdoor scenes, whether the linear-interpolation assumption for RGB features holds under non-rigid deformation, and how the method generalizes when event camera count or scene complexity increases.
