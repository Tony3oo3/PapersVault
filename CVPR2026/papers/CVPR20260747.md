## Problem

Existing modality reconstruction methods for incomplete multimodal learning suffer from a critical learning imbalance under high missing ratios: noisy reconstructed samples dominate the optimization process, biasing model updates and weakening representations learned from complete samples. This imbalance has been largely overlooked, as prior work focuses on reconstruction quality or prompt design rather than the optimization dynamics between complete and reconstructed subsets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260747/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts image-text pairs where one or both modalities may be absent, using CLIP encoders to embed the available modality for retrieval from a pre-built memory bank of unimodal representations. Missing modalities are reconstructed via mean pooling over Top-K retrieved instances (K ∈ {1,3,5,7,9}). The output is a class label: binary (hateful/not) for HateMemes, multi-label genre for MM-IMDb, or one-of-101 food category for Food-101, produced by a classifier head on the ViLT joint embedding.

## Method

ANGA combines three components: (1) a Multimodal Instance Retriever that reconstructs missing modalities by retrieving Top-K semantically similar instances from a memory bank; (2) an anchor-guided gradient alignment mechanism that decouples per-batch gradients for complete and reconstructed samples, builds a dynamic optimization anchor from complete samples plus progressively incorporated reliable reconstructions (selected by prediction entropy via a linear curriculum), and then projects the remaining reconstructed gradient toward the anchor using a cone-based rule—preserving gradients inside the cone, shrinking orthogonal components for outside-but-correlated gradients, and zeroing out conflicting gradients; (3) a Semantic-Enhanced Adapter that generates dynamic, instance-specific prompts via cross-attention between the target sequence and its retrieved counterparts, injected into selected MSA layers of the frozen ViLT backbone. The key insight is that rebalancing optimization at the gradient level, rather than merely improving reconstruction fidelity, is necessary to prevent semantic noise from reconstructed samples from degrading complete-sample representations.

## Results

Under a 70% missing rate, ANGA achieves 68.54% AUROC on HateMemes (text-missing), 57.28% F1-Micro on MM-IMDb (text-missing), and 77.23% ACC on Food-101 (text-missing), consistently outperforming all 13 baselines across every dataset and missing-modality scenario. The largest gain is on HateMemes text-missing, where ANGA surpasses the strongest baseline RAGPT by 4.44% AUROC. On Food-101, ANGA leads RAGPT by 1.70% ACC (text-missing) and 1.53% ACC (both-missing).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANGA wins against all 13 baselines on every dataset and every missing-modality configuration. The closest competitor is RAGPT [15], the retrieval-based reconstruction method that ANGA builds upon; ANGA improves over RAGPT by 4.44% AUROC on HateMemes text-missing, 2.12% F1-Micro on MM-IMDb text-missing, and 1.70% ACC on Food-101 text-missing. Among VLP-based methods, IPer [22] is the strongest (62.09% AUROC on HateMemes text-missing), still trailing ANGA by 6.45%. Modality-invariant methods (IF-MMIN, ShaSpec, DrFuse, CorrKD, MoMKE) and generative reconstruction methods (AcMAE, DiCMoR, MMDS) all lag substantially, with the largest gaps exceeding 10% on HateMemes. The paper attributes ANGA's consistent advantage to its gradient alignment mechanism, which suppresses optimization drift from noisy reconstructed samples that plagues all reconstruction-based baselines under high missing ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the retrieval-augmented reconstruction paradigm of RAGPT, adopting its multimodal instance retriever (MIR) to reconstruct missing modalities from a memory bank. It departs from prior incomplete MML work by identifying a previously overlooked optimization-level problem—learning imbalance where noisy reconstructed samples dominate gradient updates under high missing ratios—and addresses it through an anchor-guided gradient alignment mechanism combined with an entropy-driven curriculum. The semantic-enhanced adapter extends the static, input-agnostic prompt tuning used in VLP-based methods (MAPs, MSPs) to dynamic, retrieval-conditioned prompts via cross-attention, bridging retrieval-augmented generation and prompt learning in a way not previously explored for incomplete MML.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of learning imbalance as a distinct failure mode in incomplete MML provides a useful diagnostic lens for the community, and the gradient alignment technique is modular enough to be plugged into other reconstruction-based frameworks. However, the evaluation is confined to two modalities (text and image) and three datasets, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more complex multimodal settings (e.g., audio, video, 3D) or to generative downstream tasks. The method introduces several hyperparameters (cone threshold τ, curriculum bounds, retrieval cardinality K) whose interactions are only partially explored, and the reliance on a frozen CLIP encoder for retrieval limits adaptability to domain shifts. A key open question is whether the gradient alignment principle can be formalized more broadly as a general tool for balancing heterogeneous data sources during training.
