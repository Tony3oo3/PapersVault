## Problem

Existing cryo-EM reconstruction methods cannot resolve extreme compositional heterogeneity—mixtures of tens to hundreds of distinct molecular species. 3D classification is limited to fewer than ~10 classes due to EM scalability, while neural methods with shared decoders force morphologically distinct structures through a single set of weights, capping resolution for any individual structure. No prior architecture scales to reconstructing hundreds or thousands of unknown structures simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260734/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D cryo-EM projection images (128×128 pixels at 3.0 Å/pix) with known particle poses (rotation in SO(3) and in-plane translation) as input, and produces 3D density volumes representing the electron scattering potential. Reconstruction is performed entirely in Fourier space: the INR maps Fourier coordinates (kx, ky, kz) to the Fourier-transformed density, yielding a clean CTF-free prediction that is then multiplied by the CTF before computing MSE loss against ground truth projections. The fixed-pose assumption isolates the architectural contribution from pose estimation.

## Method

CryoHype is a transformer-based hypernetwork in which a ViT encoder tokenizes the input projection and a set of learnable weight tokens, processes them jointly through transformer layers, and outputs weight tokens that are partitioned per INR layer. Each group of weight tokens passes through a learnable linear head and is elementwise-multiplied with the corresponding base INR parameters, dynamically generating structure-specific weights for every layer of a ReLU MLP with residual connections. The key insight is that conditioning by concatenation (used by cryoDRGN) is equivalent to a linear hypernetwork producing only first-layer biases, whereas a full hypernetwork modulating all layers is strictly more expressive and reduces parameter sharing across structures. The entire system is trained end-to-end with MSE loss, and the latent space is defined by the high-dimensional weight tokens (q×d dimensions), which can be projected via PCA and UMAP for interpretability. Ablations confirm that both the hypernetwork conditioning (vs. concatenation) and the ViT encoder (vs. U-Net or MLP) are critical for performance at scale.

## Results

On Tomotwin-100 (100 distinct structures), CryoHype achieves a mean FSC_AUC of 0.346, outperforming the best baseline CryoDRGN (0.316) and all other methods by a wide margin. On the new Sim2Struct-1000 dataset, CryoHype reconstructs 1,000 structures with a mean FSC_AUC of 0.232, compared to CryoDRGN's 0.139, and the performance gap widens as compositional heterogeneity increases (e.g., at 500 structures: 0.305 vs 0.216 FSC_AUC). CryoHype also achieves the lowest mean Chamfer distance (2.18 Å on Tomotwin-100; 3.02 Å on Sim2Struct-1000-1000) and competitive volumetric IoU (0.61 on Tomotwin-100), approaching the backprojection upper bound of 0.71.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CryoHype outperforms all seven baselines on Tomotwin-100 in FSC_AUC, with the closest competitor being CryoDRGN (0.346 vs 0.316, ~9.5% relative gain) and the next-best RECOVAR trailing by 25% (0.258). On Chamfer distance, CryoHype (2.18 Å) is comparable to CryoDRGN (2.26 Å) but dramatically better than all other methods (18–33 Å), while on volumetric IoU CryoDRGN edges out slightly (0.63 vs 0.61). On Sim2Struct-1000, CryoHype consistently beats CryoDRGN across all heterogeneity levels, with the advantage growing from ~7% FSC_AUC gain at 10 structures to ~67% at 1,000 structures (0.232 vs 0.139), confirming that the hypernetwork's reduced parameter sharing becomes critical as compositional diversity increases. The ablation (Table 3) further shows that replacing the hypernetwork with simple concatenation conditioning drops FSC_AUC from 0.346 to 0.255, and swapping the ViT encoder for a U-Net or MLP encoder degrades performance to 0.208 and 0.234 respectively, validating both architectural choices.

## Relation to Prior Work

CryoHype extends the transformer-as-hypernetwork paradigm from Chen & Wang (ECCV 2022), which used ViTs to generate INR weights for general 3D shape reconstruction, to the cryo-EM domain. It departs from the dominant cryo-EM reconstruction paradigm (cryoDRGN, SFBP, Opus-DSD) where a single shared decoder is conditioned by concatenation or linear combination, arguing that this excessive parameter sharing becomes a bottleneck at scale. The paper also extends the compositional heterogeneity benchmark from 100 structures (Tomotwin-100 in CryoBench) to 1,000 structures via the new Sim2Struct-1000 dataset, and introduces real-space shape metrics (Chamfer distance, volumetric IoU) to complement the standard FSC evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to reconstruct 1,000 distinct structures simultaneously opens the door to high-throughput structural discovery from unpurified or cellular lysate samples, a scenario increasingly relevant in structural proteomics. However, the fixed-pose assumption is a significant limitation: real cryo-EM data requires joint pose estimation, and the authors acknowledge this as the critical next step. The finding that FSC can be insensitive to shape differences in heterogeneous settings, and that cryoDRGN's latent space degrades beyond ~500 structures while CryoHype's remains well-clustered, provides useful diagnostic tools for the field. Open questions include whether the hypernetwork approach transfers to joint pose estimation, conformational-plus-compositional heterogeneity, and cryo-ET tilt series.
