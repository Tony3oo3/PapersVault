## Problem

Standard Mixture-of-Experts architectures suffer from router–expert misalignment, where learned gating logits route tokens to suboptimal experts, causing unstable routing, representation collapse, and load imbalance. The conventional remedy—auxiliary load-balancing losses—introduces interference gradients that over-uniform expert assignments and weaken the very specialization MoE is designed to exploit, creating an accuracy–stability trade-off that has no clean resolution.

## Input / Output

For 2D vision, the model consumes RGB images (32×32 CIFAR, 64×64 Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 ImageNet) tokenized by a ViT-B/16 backbone and outputs class labels or, in the CLIP-ERMoE retrieval variant, image–text relevance rankings against a frozen CLIP text tower. For 3D neuroimaging, T1-weighted MRI volumes (H×W×D×C) are partitioned into non-overlapping 16³ cubes with 3D positional encodings, and the model outputs a predicted brain age via a classification-then-expectation head over discretized age bins rather than direct regression. Routing operates with a fixed confidence threshold T=0.5 and top-k=2 expert selection per token, so only two of eight experts (or 3 region + 5 free experts in the 3D variant) are activated per token.

## Method

ERMoE reparameterizes each expert's weight matrix as W = U diag(s) Vᵀ, where U and V are orthonormal eigenbases (maintained via a light Frobenius-norm penalty, λ=5×10⁻⁵) and s are trainable scale coefficients. Instead of learned gating logits, the router computes an Eigenbasis Score for each expert: the cosine similarity between the token's projection and its attention-weighted context's projection into that expert's eigenbasis, making routing a geometric alignment measure tied to each expert's representational subspace. A confidence threshold T filters low-alignment experts before top-k selection, and mixture weights are the normalized positive scores of the selected experts. This design eliminates all auxiliary load-balancing losses—balance emerges from the geometry of near-orthogonal expert bases—while a 3D variant (ERMoE-ba) adds anatomically pre-trained region experts (WM, GM, CSF) alongside free experts for brain-age prediction.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, ERMoE achieves 88.03% Top-1 and 98.97% Top-5 accuracy, surpassing V-MoE (87.41%/97.94%) without any auxiliary load-balancing loss. In few-shot linear probes, ERMoE leads by up to ~4% on CIFAR-100 5-shot (93.47% vs. 89.49%) and ~2% on Tiny-ImageNet 5-shot (83.17% vs. 81.12%). For brain-age prediction on ADNI, ERMoE-ba attains 2.31 years MAE, improving over 3D Swin Transformer (2.83), 3D CNN/SFCN (3.13), and 3D ViT (3.52) by 18–34%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERMoE outperforms V-MoE [32] by +0.62% Top-1 on ImageNet and by up to +4% on CIFAR-100 5-shot, while achieving a flatter expert-usage distribution (Fig. 4) without the auxiliary load-balancing loss that V-MoE requires. Against Single-gated MoE [33] and DeepMoE [42], ERMoE leads by 15–25% Top-1 on ImageNet and by 10–25% on few-shot probes, indicating that their routing strategies suffer from severe collapse. In image-text retrieval, CLIP-ERMoE beats CLIP-MoE [46] and Fix-CLIP [40] on I2T R@1 (65.4% vs. 65.0%/62.3% on COCO; 63.4% vs. 60.5%/60.5% on Flickr30K), but trails Fix-CLIP on COCO T2I R@10 (81.6% vs. 82.4%), which the authors attribute to the frozen CLIP text tower. For brain-age prediction, ERMoE-ba surpasses 3D Swin Transformer [17] (2.31 vs. 2.83 MAE), SFCN-style 3D CNN [27] (2.31 vs. 3.13), and 3D ViT [48] (2.31 vs. 3.52) on the ADNI test set. In expert-balancing analysis, ERMoE approaches the near-uniform distribution of BASE layers [23] and is flatter than both V-MoE and SoftMoE's top-1 projection [30], confirming that eigenbasis-aligned routing naturally prevents the rich-get-richer skew without balance-by-construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

ERMoE extends the V-MoE (Riquelme et al., NeurIPS 2021) paradigm of inserting sparse experts into ViT FFN layers, but fundamentally reparameterizes the routing mechanism: instead of learned gating logits with an auxiliary load-balancing loss, each expert's weight matrix is decomposed into an orthonormal eigenbasis and tokens are routed by cosine similarity between their feature projections and the expert's basis. This departs from the broader line of routing-modification work (SoftMoE, optimal-transport routers, BASE layers) that adjusts the assignment procedure while leaving expert parameterization untouched; ERMoE instead ties the router geometrically to the experts' internal representational subspaces. The 3D brain-age variant (ERMoE-ba) extends the same eigenbasis routing to volumetric T1 MRI, building on SFCN-style 3D CNNs and 3D ViT/Swin baselines for brain-age estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that MoE practitioners can potentially drop auxiliary load-balancing losses and their associated hyperparameter tuning (the LBL coefficient) while retaining or improving both accuracy and expert utilization, simplifying training pipelines. The anatomically interpretable expert specializations in the brain-age setting (WM/GM/CSF experts) offer a template for interpretable MoE in clinical imaging, though this is demonstrated on a single cohort (ADNI) and a single task. Open questions include whether the eigenbasis constraint and threshold T generalize to much larger expert counts or non-ViT backbones, whether the orthogonality penalty is truly load-bearing or merely stabilizing, and whether the T2I retrieval gap (attributed to the frozen text encoder) closes with joint training. The modest ImageNet gain (~1% over V-MoE) suggests the routing improvement is real but not transformative at scale.
