## Problem

Diffusion models suffer from accumulated gradient error along the sampling trajectory due to the misalignment between the simulation-free training objective and the iterative reverse process, causing poor generalization especially in complex conditional tasks. While guidance methods like CFG and AutoGuidance mitigate this, their effective operational regimes remain ambiguous—practitioners lack a principled understanding of when each type of guidance is appropriate, leading to suboptimal method selection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263578/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, SGG operates on pre-trained flow-matching transformers (SD3-Medium, SD3.5-Medium) with a text prompt, applying segmented guidance across 28 sampling steps with a single extra forward call (NFE/s=2). At training, the method modifies the velocity-matching objective of a SiT-B/2 transformer on ImageNet 256×256, where the weak signal is constructed either via an unconditional forward pass (CFG), a separate smaller network (AG), or a novel branching from an intermediate transformer layer (BR, requiring no extra forward call). The output in both settings is a synthetic RGB image, with the training-time variant aiming to produce strong unguided samples (NFE/s=1) that match or exceed guided baselines.

## Method

The paper unifies prevalent guidance methods under a weak-to-strong (W2S) extrapolation framework, categorizing them into condition-dependent guidance (CDG, e.g., CFG, which manipulates the condition) and condition-agnostic guidance (CAG, e.g., AG, which manipulates the model). Through synthetic recursive-Gaussian experiments and ImageNet velocity-error analysis, it demonstrates that CDG corrects inter-class/semantic errors at high noise levels while CAG refines intra-class perceptual details at low noise levels. SGG exploits this temporal separation by switching the guidance direction from CDG (t > τ) to CAG (t ≤ τ) within a single sampling trajectory. The W2S principle is further migrated into training by augmenting the standard velocity-matching target with a stop-gradient guidance term u_w2s = u + w·sg[g(x_t,t,c)], where g follows the SGG segmentation, enabling the unguided model to learn extrapolative capacity directly. A novel Branching (BR) weak-signal construction taps an auxiliary output from an intermediate transformer layer, providing CAG at only 2% training overhead without any extra forward pass.

## Results

On SD3.5-Medium/MS-COCO-1K, SGG achieves the best HPSv2.1 score (29.736) and best Aesthetic score (5.717) among all guidance methods, outperforming CFG (29.199 / 5.279) and SLG (27.295 / 5.714) while requiring only 2 NFE/s (same as CFG). On SD3-Medium/MS-COCO-1K, SGG similarly leads with HPSv2.1 = 29.541 and Aesthetic = 5.614. In training-time integration on ImageNet 256×256 (SiT-B/2, conditional), SGG achieves FID 4.58 with NFE/s=1, surpassing the baseline model's CFG-guided output (FID 6.02, NFE/s=2) and the MG training variant (FID 5.88).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGG consistently outperforms all inference-time baselines on both HPSv2.1 (prompt adherence) and Aesthetic score across SD3 and SD3.5. Against CFG [15], SGG gains +0.54 HPSv2.1 and +0.44 Aesthetic on SD3.5/MS-COCO-1K while using the same 2 NFE/s. Against SLG [17], SGG recovers the prompt-adherence gap (29.736 vs. 27.295 HPSv2.1) while matching its aesthetic strength (5.717 vs. 5.714). SGG also surpasses S²-Guidance [4] (29.736 vs. 29.614 HPSv2.1) and Rectified-CFG++ [40] (29.736 vs. 28.540) with fewer NFE/s (2 vs. 3). In training-time integration, SGG (FID 4.58, NFE/s=1) beats MG [48] (FID 5.88) and the baseline+CFG inference (FID 6.02, NFE/s=2), and when combined with REPA [56], achieves FID 3.07 versus REPA alone at 20.46. The AG [21] training variant shows degraded performance (FID 13.96), confirming that CAG alone is insufficient without the CDG component at high noise levels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of diffusion guidance—Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) and AutoGuidance (AG)—under a single weak-to-strong (W2S) extrapolation framework, showing they differ only in how the weak signal is constructed (condition manipulation vs. model degradation). It departs from prior work by providing a systematic empirical analysis of their distinct operational regimes (CDG effective at high noise for inter-class separation, CAG effective at low noise for intra-class refinement) and by proposing SGG, a simple timestep-segmented hybrid. The training-time integration extends MG [48] and AG [21] by incorporating the segmented guidance principle directly into the velocity-matching objective, going beyond prior work that treated guidance as purely an inference-time mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SGG offers a practical, training-free guidance strategy that resolves the well-known trade-off between prompt adherence and aesthetic quality in text-to-image models, with the added benefit of requiring only 2 NFE/s (same as CFG). The training-time migration is particularly relevant for reducing inference cost: the SGG-trained unguided model (NFE/s=1, FID 4.58) outperforms the CFG-guided baseline (NFE/s=2, FID 6.02), suggesting a path toward guidance-free generation without sacrificing quality. However, training experiments are limited to SiT-B/2 on ImageNet 256×256, and the synthetic analysis is restricted to 2D toy distributions, leaving open whether the temporal segmentation principle generalizes to discrete diffusion, video, or other modalities as the authors note.
