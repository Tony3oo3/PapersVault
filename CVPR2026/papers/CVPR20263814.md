## Problem

Existing monocular 3D object detectors assume fixed camera intrinsics during training and inference, causing significant performance degradation when evaluated under unseen focal lengths. The paper identifies that the core issue is not merely data diversity but how intrinsics are represented: treating them as raw numeric values provides weak inductive bias because intrinsic variation is fundamentally a perceptual transformation that alters apparent scale, perspective, and spatial geometry rather than a simple scalar difference.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and its camera focal length as input. The focal length is not fed as a raw number into the network; instead, it is first converted into a language-grounded semantic embedding (via LLM-generated perceptual descriptions encoded by CLIP) and then injected into the detector. At inference, for unseen focal lengths, the embedding is synthesized via a hybrid interpolation strategy that either reuses the nearest seen embedding (gap ≤ 32 px) or linearly interpolates between two nearest embeddings. The output consists of 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, orientation), class labels, and per-object depth maps, predicted via a DETR-style transformer decoder with Hungarian matching.

## Method

MonoIA is built on MonoCoP and introduces three components. (1) An Intrinsic Simulation Module generates training images across focal lengths 700–1300 px by FoV-based resizing, exposing the detector to diverse camera configurations without 3D re-rendering. (2) An Intrinsic Encoder uses ChatGPT-4o to generate N concise textual descriptions of each focal length's perceptual and geometric effects (e.g., perspective compression, depth magnification), which are then encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-H/14 Text Encoder and averaged to form a single intrinsic embedding per focal length. (3) An Intrinsic Adaptation Module bridges the frozen semantic embedding space to the learnable visual space via a two-layer MLP Connector with GELU, followed by hierarchical fusion that adds the adapted embedding to both multi-scale backbone feature maps (feature-level) and object queries in the transformer decoder (query-level). During training the Intrinsic Encoder remains frozen to preserve its pretrained semantic structure, while the Connector and detector are trained jointly with standard 2D box, 3D attribute, and depth-map losses.

## Results

On the KITTI Test leaderboard (IoU≥0.7, Moderate), MonoIA achieves 20.29% AP₃D and 26.59% AP_BEV, surpassing the previous best image-only method MonoCoP by +1.18% and +1.02% respectively. On nuScenes Val, MonoIA reaches 12.33% AP₃D (Easy) and 10.74% (Moderate), outperforming MonoCoP by +1.48% and +1.03%. Under multi-dataset training (KITTI+nuScenes+Waymo), MonoIA achieves 28.91% AP₃D on KITTI and 11.48% on nuScenes, demonstrating that intrinsic-aware modeling enables beneficial cross-dataset transfer rather than the degradation seen in baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MonoIA outperforms all ten baselines across every reported benchmark. Against the primary baseline MonoCoP [89] (on which MonoIA is built), MonoIA gains +1.18% AP₃D on KITTI Test Moderate, +0.42% on KITTI Val Moderate, and +1.48% on nuScenes Val Easy AP₃D. It also surpasses MonoDGP [56] by +1.57% (KITTI Test Mod. AP₃D) and MonoDETR [84] by +3.82% on the same metric. Notably, MonoIA exceeds methods that use additional sensor inputs: it beats MonoTAKD [40] (LiDAR-supervised) by +0.86% on KITTI Test Mod. AP₃D and OccupancyM3D [54] (LiDAR) by +3.27%. On nuScenes, MonoIA leads DEVIANT [31] by +2.64% AP₃D (Easy) and MonoCD [79] by +5.80% (Easy). In the multi-dataset setting, MonoCoP degrades sharply when trained jointly on KITTI+nuScenes (23.98→17.26% AP₃D), whereas MonoIA improves (24.40→26.54%), confirming that intrinsic-aware modeling is the key differentiator for cross-dataset generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MonoIA builds directly on MonoCoP as its detection backbone, inheriting its DETR-style architecture, Hungarian matching, and depth-map supervision. The key conceptual departure is in how camera intrinsics are represented: prior Mono3D methods (MonoDETR, MonoDGP, MonoCoP) either fix intrinsics during training or feed raw focal-length values as numeric conditioning, whereas MonoIA encodes intrinsics through LLM-generated perceptual descriptions processed by a CLIP text encoder, producing semantically structured embeddings. This positions the work at the intersection of the growing trend of leveraging foundation models (LLMs, vision-language models) for 3D perception tasks and the long-standing practical problem of cross-camera generalization in monocular detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real deployment bottleneck: monocular 3D detectors trained on one camera configuration degrade sharply on unseen intrinsics, and the finding that simply augmenting training data with diverse focal lengths actually *hurts* performance (−1.93% AP3D) underscores that the problem is representational, not data-driven. The language-grounded intrinsic encoding offers a principled path toward camera-agnostic 3D perception, and the multi-dataset training results (+4.46% on KITTI Val) suggest practical value for systems that must unify data from heterogeneous sensors. Open questions include whether the approach extends to full intrinsic variation (principal point, skew, distortion) rather than focal length alone, how sensitive the pipeline is to the quality of LLM-generated descriptions, and whether the 32 px interpolation threshold generalizes across architectures with different spatial downsampling.
