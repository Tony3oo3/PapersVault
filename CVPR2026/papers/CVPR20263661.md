## Problem

Neural reconstruction methods (NeRF, 3DGS) produce visually compelling scenes but exhibit novel-view artifacts and fail to realistically integrate inserted dynamic objects (missing shadows, lighting mismatches, tone discrepancies). Existing generative post-processing solutions are either too slow for online simulation (video diffusion models) or lack temporal consistency (image-based models), leaving no practical method that can run in real-time on a single GPU while preserving scene structure and temporal coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263661/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The enhancer takes a single degraded rendered frame at 1024×576 resolution along with up to K=4 previously *enhanced* (not original) frames as temporal context, and produces one enhanced frame in 212 ms on a single H100 GPU. The VAE encoder/decoder are frozen; only the 0.6B-parameter diffusion backbone is fine-tuned. The model operates deterministically in a single forward pass—no iterative denoising—making it suitable for streaming frame-by-frame enhancement in an online simulator.

## Method

DiffusionHarmonizer converts a pretrained multi-step image diffusion model (Cosmos 0.6B) into a single-step, temporally conditioned enhancer by feeding clean latents directly (no noise injection) with fixed null timestep and text tokens, and interleaving temporal attention layers with spatial attention to condition on K=4 prior enhanced frames. A key training challenge is the noise-trajectory mismatch between multi-step pretraining and single-step inference, which causes high-frequency checkerboard artifacts; this is addressed with a multi-scale perceptual loss computed on randomly sampled square patches of varying sizes (128–512 px) that perturbs patch boundaries relative to the receptive field. A temporal warping loss using RAFT optical flow further enforces frame-to-frame consistency, and mixed temporal/non-temporal batch training prevents over-reliance on context. Training is supervised by a five-component data curation pipeline (DIFIX3D+ artifact degradation, randomized ISP compositing, generative relighting, PBR shadow rendering, and shadow-free asset re-insertion) that jointly provides paired supervision for harmonization, artifact correction, and physically plausible shadow synthesis.

## Results

On the out-of-domain object insertion benchmark (Waymo), DiffusionHarmonizer achieves FID 101.27, FVD 453.17, DINO-Struct-Dist 0.9096, and temporal consistency 0.9670 at 212 ms per frame on a single H100 GPU. On ground-truth holdout sets, it reaches PSNR 28.10 / SSIM 0.9974 / LPIPS 0.0020 on ISP modification, PSNR 26.31 / SSIM 0.9966 / LPIPS 0.0028 on PBR shadow, and PSNR 23.93 / SSIM 0.9938 / LPIPS 0.0042 on relighting. In the user study, 84.28–90.11% of human evaluators prefer DiffusionHarmonizer over each editing baseline, and the VLM evaluator agrees at 75–89% preference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three general-purpose editing baselines, DiffusionHarmonizer wins on every perceptual and structural metric: it improves FID by 9.7–52.7 points and FVD by 210–283 points over SDEdit(SD3) and InstructPix2Pix, while also achieving the highest DINO-Struct-Dist (0.9215 in-domain, 0.9096 out-of-domain) indicating the best structural preservation. Compared to the video-editing baseline Wan-VideoV2V, it matches temporal consistency within 0.0005 (0.9827 vs. 0.9828 in-domain) while being 13× faster (212 ms vs. 2827 ms) and achieving lower FID/FVD. Against the two specialized harmonization baselines (VHTT and Ke et al.), DiffusionHarmonizer substantially outperforms on pixel-level fidelity (PSNR 28.58 vs. 20.96 and 25.98; LPIPS 0.0021 vs. 0.0049 and 0.0037) and generative quality (FID 42.03 vs. 46.23 and 61.51), though it trails slightly on the VBench temporal flickering score (0.9805 vs. 0.9838 for VHTT and 0.9872 for Ke et al.). In the user study, human evaluators prefer DiffusionHarmonizer 84.28% of the time over SDEdit, 90.10% over InstructPix2Pix, and 90.11% over Wan-VideoV2V, confirming the quantitative advantages in perceived realism.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of neural reconstruction enhancement and image harmonization, building directly on the Cosmos 0.6B diffusion model as its backbone and adopting DIFIX3D+'s four degradation modes for artifact-correction supervision. It departs from prior harmonization work (Ke et al., VHTT) by jointly addressing reconstruction artifacts, appearance mismatches, and shadow synthesis in a single model, and from video editing models (VACE/WAN V2V) by converting a multi-step image diffusion model into a deterministic single-step enhancer with lightweight temporal attention. The multi-scale perceptual loss is a targeted training technique to resolve the noise-trajectory mismatch inherent in repurposing a multi-step denoiser as a one-step predictor, a problem not previously addressed in the diffusion fine-tuning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for autonomous driving simulation: a 212 ms per-frame enhancer on a single H100 GPU that matches video-diffusion temporal coherence while correcting artifacts, harmonizing appearance, and synthesizing shadows in one pass removes a major bottleneck for real-time neural simulators. The scalable data curation pipeline (combining DIFIX3D+ degradations, ISP randomization, generative relighting, PBR shadows, and asset re-insertion) provides a reusable recipe for constructing paired supervision where real-world ground truth is unavailable. Open questions include whether the single-step conversion and temporal conditioning generalize beyond driving scenes to other 3D reconstruction domains, and whether the 0.6B backbone is sufficient for more complex multi-object interactions or long-horizon temporal consistency beyond the 4-frame context window.
