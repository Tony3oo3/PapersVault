## Problem

Existing 2D-to-3D detection pipelines over-rely on current-frame 2D cues to initialize 3D queries while underutilizing historical 3D detections, causing spatial discrepancies (2D features lack explicit depth, scale, and orientation) and temporal inconsistencies (object omission under occlusion due to insufficient cross-frame interaction). This paper addresses the gap by establishing explicit spatio-temporal alignment between 2D and 3D representations rather than treating 2D detection as a one-shot prior.

## Input / Output

The method consumes six surrounding-view RGB images (360° coverage) at resolutions ranging from 704×256 to 640×1600, along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics and, during training, LiDAR-projected sparse depth maps for supervision. It also ingests high-confidence 2D and 3D detections from the previous frame as temporal priors. Outputs include 3D bounding boxes (center, dimensions, yaw, velocity), intermediate 2D bounding boxes (top-128 retained), and per-pixel depth maps; at inference the system operates in vision-only mode without LiDAR.

## Method

STUR3D introduces three complementary modules that unify 2D and 3D spatio-temporal representations. STOPP reprojects historical 2D and 3D detections onto the current image plane via ego-motion transformation and camera projection, encoding category and RoI features into spatial prior maps that suppress background noise and maintain attention on occluded targets. STGE fuses these prior maps with current image features through a depth-aware geometric attention mechanism (inspired by Deformerv2) that generates image-plane and depth-distance geometric priors, combined with an Adaptive Threshold Gate to emphasize high-confidence foreground regions. OQG lifts refined 2D detections into 3D queries by sampling depth beneath box centers to form pseudo-3D points, then fusing coarse and depth-guided positional encodings via a linear offset to produce geometry-grounded 3D query initialization. The full pipeline is trained end-to-end with supervised losses on 3D detection and LiDAR-supervised depth regression, using ResNet50/101 or V2-99 backbones with FPN necks.

## Results

On the nuScenes test set (V2-99 backbone, 640×1600 input), STUR3D achieves 57.9% mAP and 64.6% NDS, setting a new state-of-the-art without CBGS, TTA, or future frames. On the nuScenes val set, STUR3D reaches 53.1% mAP and 61.3% NDS with ResNet101 (1408×512), surpassing the previous best Sparse4Dv2 by 1.0% mAP. With the lighter ResNet50 backbone (704×256), STUR3D attains 48.6% mAP and 57.9% NDS, outperforming the StreamPETR baseline by 3.7% mAP and 2.9% NDS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STUR3D outperforms all 11 compared baselines on both the nuScenes val and test sets. On the test set, it leads DVPE [25] by 0.7% mAP (57.9 vs 57.2) and RayDN [15] by 1.4% mAP (57.9 vs 56.5), while also achieving the best mATE (0.449) indicating superior localization. On the val set with ResNet101, STUR3D surpasses Sparse4Dv2 [14] by 1.0% mAP (53.1 vs 52.1) and OPEN [5] by 1.2% mAP (53.1 vs 51.9). Against the 2D-to-3D pipeline methods, STUR3D gains 6.0% mAP over MV2D-T [28] (53.1 vs 47.1) and 1.0% mAP over QAF2D [7] (53.0 vs 52.1 with V2-99). Notably, STUR3D runs at 7.8 FPS, roughly 2× faster than QAF2D (3.9 FPS) while being more accurate, and matches OPEN [5] on NDS (61.2%) while exceeding it in mAP (53.0 vs 52.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

STUR3D sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) query-based temporal 3D detection (DETR3D → PETR → StreamPETR), which propagates 3D decoder embeddings across frames, and (2) 2D-to-3D lifting pipelines (MV2D, QAF2D, Far3D, DVPE), which use a 2D detector to initialize or refine 3D queries. The paper's key departure is that prior 2D-to-3D methods treat 2D cues as the dominant signal and 3D history as a secondary prior, creating a spatio-temporal asymmetry. STUR3D inverts this by explicitly projecting historical 3D detections back onto the 2D image plane (STOPP) and injecting depth-aware geometric attention (STGE) so that the 2D detector is guided by both 2D and 3D temporal evidence before lifting to 3D via object-center queries (OQG).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a concrete failure mode—object omission under occlusion and spatial mislocalization after 2D-to-3D lifting—that has been acknowledged but not systematically resolved in prior 2D-to-3D pipelines. The plug-and-play STOPP module (validated on OPEN and DVPE) offers a reusable component for the broader community, and the depth-aware geometric attention provides a principled mechanism for cross-modal alignment. Open questions include whether the geometric attention scales efficiently to higher resolutions or longer temporal windows, and whether the LiDAR-supervised depth training requirement can be fully eliminated without performance loss. The paper notes future work on efficiency and scalability of the geometric attention mechanism.
