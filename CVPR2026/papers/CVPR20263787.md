## Problem

Existing LSS-based BEV detection methods focus on improving BEV feature representations but directly adopt the 2D center-based detection head without task-specific optimization. This leads to three inherent limitations: a geometric mismatch between the Gaussian classification kernel and the small, rotated BEV objects, severe performance degradation under end-to-end inference without NMS, and extremely sparse regression supervision signals that are inadequate for the multi-attribute BEV regression task.

## Input / Output

The method consumes six synchronized RGB camera images (processed to 256×704) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, which are lifted into a BEV feature map (128×128 or 256×256) by an upstream LSS-based backbone. The detection head outputs per-pixel keypoint heatmaps for 10 object classes and regression targets including center offsets, height-above-ground, yaw rotation (sin/cos), 3D dimensions (w, l, h), and 2D velocity. During end-to-end inference, the top-150 predictions by classification score are retained with a 0.1 score threshold, eliminating the need for NMS.

## Method

Spe-BEVHead is a dual-branch detection head that replaces the standard center-based head with three BEV-specific adaptations. First, a Rotated Box Kernel (RBK) replaces the Gaussian kernel by computing an elliptical distance in each object's local rotated coordinate frame, producing geometry-aligned classification weights that respect the object's yaw and aspect ratio. Second, a Local Response Refinement Module (LRRM) applies an Adaptive Mean Attenuation (AMA) operator—preserving local maxima while subtracting the neighborhood mean from all other pixels—to suppress non-peak responses and improve end-to-end detection without NMS. Third, the dual-branch design pairs a main branch (strict one-to-one matching with LRRM) for inference with an auxiliary branch (nine-sample regression around the center, RBK-weighted focal loss) that provides denser supervisory signals during training but is discarded at test time. The head is plug-and-play: it can be swapped into any LSS-based BEV backbone without modifying other components.

## Results

On the nuScenes val set, Spe-BEVHead delivers consistent gains across all LSS-based baselines when simply replacing the original center-based head. The largest improvement is on FastBEV: +1.7% NDS (38.4→40.1), +1.1% mAP (29.5→30.6), and −5.2% mATE (74.9→69.7). Combined with the strongest baseline GeoBEV4D, Spe-BEVHead achieves a new state-of-the-art of 54.6 NDS and 42.7 mAP. Under the end-to-end setting (no NMS), Spe-BEVHead recovers +3.4% NDS and +4.3% mAP over the center-based head on FastBEV, substantially narrowing the gap to the NMS-based result.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Spe-BEVHead is evaluated as a drop-in replacement for the center-based head across seven LSS-based baselines (FastBEV, BEVDet, FastBEV4D, BEVDet4D, BEVDepth4D, BEVStereo4D, GeoBEV4D). It wins on NDS for six of seven baselines, with the most notable gains on FastBEV (+1.7% NDS, −5.2% mATE) and BEVDet4D (+0.9% NDS, +1.1% mAP). The only NDS regression is on FastBEV4D (−0.2%), though mAP still improves by +0.5%. On the strongest baseline GeoBEV4D, Spe-BEVHead adds +0.6% NDS and substantially improves orientation error (mAOE 0.424→0.371), establishing a new state-of-the-art. In the end-to-end (no-NMS) setting, Spe-BEVHead outperforms the center-based head by +3.4% NDS and +4.3% mAP on FastBEV, and by +2.4% NDS and +4.1% mAP on BEVDet4D, while the baseline suffers a 7.6% mAP drop when NMS is removed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the CenterNet (Objects as Points) center-based detection head, which has been the default prediction module for LSS-based BEV detectors (BEVDet, BEVDet4D, BEVDepth, BEVStereo, FastBEV, GeoBEV). The key departure is the argument that this head, designed for 2D image-plane detection, suffers from three structural mismatches in the BEV domain: an oversized Gaussian kernel relative to small BEV objects, inability to produce sharp enough responses for NMS-free inference, and overly sparse regression supervision. Rather than improving the BEV feature backbone (the focus of most prior LSS work), Spe-BEVHead redesigns the head itself with a Rotated Box Kernel, a Local Response Refinement Module, and a dual-branch architecture, positioning itself as a plug-and-drop upgrade orthogonal to backbone choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper highlights an underappreciated component of the BEV detection pipeline: the detection head has been treated as a fixed, interchangeable module while the field focused on feature extraction. By demonstrating consistent gains across six different LSS backbones without modifying any other component, it provides a practical, low-cost improvement path for existing systems. The end-to-end results (+7.6% mAP without NMS on FastBEV) are particularly relevant for deployment where NMS is GPU-unfriendly. However, the method still lags behind NMS-based inference, and the open question of achieving fully NMS-free detection within purely convolutional architectures remains unresolved.
