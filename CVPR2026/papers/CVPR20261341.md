## Problem

Existing story visualization methods rely on text prompts alone to position multiple characters, lacking the pixel-level precision needed for spatial layout control, and struggle to simultaneously preserve subject identity and artistic style (especially non-photorealistic styles like cartoons). No prior DiT-based method supports both multi-subject identity customization and explicit layout-driven positioning in a single generative model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261341/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a text script (one description per panel), multiple reference subject images, and layout bounding boxes (panel-level D_i ∈ R⁴ and per-character BOX_{i,j} ∈ R⁴) as input, and produces comic-style story images at 1280×720 resolution. The layout generator (Qwen2.5-VL 7B) accepts only the text script and outputs structured bounding boxes for panels and characters, while the image customization model (Dream-Illustrator) consumes the reference images, text, and layout boxes jointly to render a single target frame in ~17 seconds. The comic-centric representation treats each panel as a tuple (T_i, D_i, {BOX_{i,1},...,BOX_{i,n}}), enabling both page-level panel arrangement and within-panel character positioning.

## Method

DreamingComics repurposes a pretrained video DiT (HunyuanVideo-I2V with FramePack) as a single-frame image customization model by treating reference images as the first frame and generating a temporally distant target frame (t=3), leveraging the video model's spatiotemporal priors for identity and style consistency. For spatial control, RegionalRoPE deterministically re-indexes each reference latent's 3D Rotary Position Embedding coordinates to align with its target layout bounding box, preserving the latent aspect ratio via a scaling factor and centering within the region. A masked condition loss (ReLU-based, applied to cross-attention maps at DiT layer 2) penalizes attention leakage beyond the assigned layout region, complementing RegionalRoPE with explicit spatial supervision during training. An LLM-based layout generator (Qwen2.5-VL 7B, LoRA rank 8) is fine-tuned on 25K annotated comic layouts to predict panel and character bounding boxes from text, and the image model is trained with LoRA (rank 32) on 55K single-subject and 20K multi-subject paired samples derived from curated video sources, using a combined flow-matching and masked-condition objective.

## Results

On ViStoryBench, DreamingComics achieves the highest CIDS-cross score of 66.6 (a 29.2% improvement over the next-best DreamO at 51.6) and the highest CSD-cross of 53.6, along with the best OCCM (86.7) and layout precision (61.6). On DreamBench++, it attains the top DINO (60.50) and CLIP-I (79.50) scores among all compared methods. The LLM-based layout generator outperforms GPT-4 on every layout quality metric, scoring 100% panel count vs. 87.67% and 79.14 coverage ratio vs. 29.25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DreamingComics decisively outperforms all image-customization baselines (DreamO, UNO, Eligen, DRA-Ctrl, RealGeneral) on character identity (CIDS-cross: 66.6 vs. 36–52) and style similarity (CSD-cross: 53.6 vs. 29–39), with the largest gap over DreamO (+15.0 CIDS, +15.3 CSD). It also leads on layout precision (61.6 vs. 42.0 for DiffSensei and 39.7 for Eligen) and OCCM (86.7 vs. 85.7 for DreamO), confirming superior spatial control. However, it trails on Inception Score (6.81 vs. 8.77 for DRA-Ctrl) and Aesthetic Score (4.54 vs. 5.81 for Eligen), which the authors attribute to the video model's lower photorealistic sharpness and the aesthetic metric's bias toward realistic rendering. On DreamBench++, it beats DreamBooth, UNO, and DreamO on DINO and CLIP-I while remaining competitive on CLIP-T. The layout generator surpasses GPT-4 on all five structural metrics (panel count, coverage, ordering, valid characters, character count), with the most dramatic gain in coverage ratio (79.14 vs. 29.25) and valid character boxes (100% vs. 57.67%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes a pretrained video DiT (HunyuanVideo-I2V with FramePack) for image-level subject customization, following the broader trend of leveraging video model spatiotemporal priors for image tasks (RealGeneral, DRA-Ctrl, Frame2Frame). It departs from prior DiT customization methods (UNO, DreamO, Eligen) that rely on implicitly learned spatial cues by introducing RegionalRoPE for explicit per-subject spatial grounding and a masked condition loss to enforce layout boundaries. The LLM-based layout generator extends the plan-and-render paradigm of TheaterGen and MovieAgent by producing multi-panel comic layouts (not just single-image layouts) and integrating them as first-class conditioning signals within a single generative model rather than a multi-agent pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DreamingComics is the first framework to simultaneously support multi-subject identity preservation, layout control, DiT architecture, and LLM-based layout generation (Table 1), filling a gap where prior methods supported at most two of these capabilities. The 29.2% gain in character consistency and 36.2% in style similarity over prior methods, combined with the insight that video model spatiotemporal priors improve stylistic coherence across diverse art styles (pencil, Disney, cel animation, live-action), suggests a broader principle: temporal consistency mechanisms can substitute for explicit style-anchoring in image generation. Open questions include robustness to erroneous layout inputs, handling conflicts between text/image/layout modalities, and whether the approach scales to longer narratives or more complex multi-character interactions.
