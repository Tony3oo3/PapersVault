## Problem

Traditional training-free feature caching methods inevitably lose semantic and fine-grained detail under high compression, and this degradation becomes far more severe when applied to step-distilled models where sparse sampling steps create large inter-step feature differences that simple reuse or Taylor-series extrapolation cannot capture. Meanwhile, MeanFlow-based step distillation, while effective for image generation, suffers catastrophic training divergence and generative artifacts on large-scale video DiTs due to numerical errors in Jacobian-vector product operations and its inherently aggressive one-step design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264053/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt as input and produces a 704×704, 129-frame, 5-second video via the HunyuanVideo DiT backbone. At inference, a single full forward pass through the large DiT initializes a single cached feature tensor; subsequent N−1 steps are computed by a lightweight predictor (<4% of the DiT size) that consumes this cache along with the current timestep pair (t′, r′) and conditional embedding, eliminating the need for per-layer multi-order derivative caches used by prior methods like TaylorSeer.

## Method

DisCa combines two complementary innovations. First, Restricted MeanFlow constrains the mean-velocity integration interval to I = (t−r) ∈ [0, R] with R < 1, pruning the overly aggressive long-range compression cases that cause numerical instability in large video DiTs, yielding a more stable few-step distilled model. Second, a lightweight learnable predictor—built from a small stack of DiT blocks—is trained to forecast the full model's velocity output at subsequent timesteps from a single cached tensor, replacing handcrafted Taylor-series or interpolation heuristics. The predictor is trained with an MSE regression loss against the frozen DiT's ground-truth outputs plus a multi-scale GAN adversarial loss (spectral-normalized discriminator with hinge loss) to recover high-frequency details and semantic structure that pure L2 optimization misses. This data-driven predictor captures the high-dimensional feature evolution that becomes non-trivially nonlinear after step distillation, enabling up to 11.8× acceleration with only ~1.4% total VBench score drop.

## Results

On HunyuanVideo (704×704, 129 frames, 5 s) evaluated with VBench, DisCa (R=0.2, N=4) achieves 11.8× speedup (97.7 s vs. 1155.3 s for the original 50-step model) while retaining 69.3% Semantic, 81.1% Quality, and 78.8% Total scores — a drop of only 5.7%, 0.5%, and 1.4% respectively. At a more moderate 8.84× speedup (N=3), the degradation shrinks to just 4.4% Semantic and 0.5% Total, and at 7.56× (N=2) the Total score is within 0.3% of the 50-step baseline. Peak VRAM remains at 97.64 GB, comparable to the original model, unlike TaylorSeer and FORA which exceed 120 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the high-acceleration regime (~10 steps), DisCa (N=4, 11.8×) outperforms every training-free caching baseline by a wide margin: Δ-DiT (N=8) collapses to 42.7% Semantic (−41.9%) and PAB (N=8) drops to 56.3% (−23.4%), while FORA (N=6) and TeaCache (l=0.4) lose 21.8% and 15.5% Semantic respectively; DisCa's 5.7% Semantic drop is the smallest among all methods at comparable or higher speed. TaylorSeer (N=6, O=1), the strongest training-free 'cache-then-forecast' method, still trails DisCa by 5.6% in Semantic (63.7% vs. 69.3%) and 2.3% in Total (76.7% vs. 78.8%) at a lower 6.96× speed. Against the step-distillation baseline, original MeanFlow at 10 steps loses 19.7% Semantic, whereas Restricted MeanFlow at 9 steps (10.7×) recovers to 67.8% (−7.8%), and DisCa (N=4) further improves to 69.3% (−5.7%) at 11.8×. At the moderate ~5× region, DisCa (N=2, 7.56×) already surpasses all caching baselines (TeaCache, FORA, TaylorSeer, Δ-DiT, PAB) in both Semantic and Total scores while also exceeding their speedups. Notably, DisCa also uses less peak VRAM (97.64 GB) than TaylorSeer (130.7 GB) and FORA (124.6 GB), making it more practical for high-resolution long-video generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two acceleration paradigms for diffusion models: step distillation (specifically MeanFlow, which models mean velocity instead of instantaneous velocity) and training-free feature caching (specifically TaylorSeer's 'cache-then-forecast' paradigm). The key conceptual departure is identifying that step distillation fundamentally breaks traditional caching because the sparsified sampling trajectory makes handcrafted Taylor-series or linear interpolation predictions inadequate. The authors replace the handcrafted predictor with a lightweight learnable neural network (<4% of the DiT size) trained with adversarial objectives borrowed from Adversarial Diffusion Distillation, and simultaneously modify MeanFlow into 'Restricted MeanFlow' by pruning overly aggressive compression intervals to stabilize training on large video models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 11.8× acceleration with only 1.4% total VBench degradation on HunyuanVideo is a practically significant result for deploying large-scale video generation, where inference cost is the primary bottleneck. The finding that MeanFlow's one-step-oriented design causes catastrophic quality loss on video models—and that simply restricting the compression ratio (R=0.2) recovers 12% semantic score at 10 steps—provides useful guidance for practitioners attempting step distillation on complex video DiTs. Limitations include evaluation on a single model (HunyuanVideo) at one resolution and duration, the additional training overhead for the predictor and GAN, and unclear generalizability to other architectures or modalities. Open questions include whether the learnable predictor can be further compressed or made architecture-agnostic, and whether the Restricted MeanFlow principle extends to other distillation objectives beyond mean velocity.
