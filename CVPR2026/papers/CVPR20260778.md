## Problem

RLHF has become standard for text-to-image generation but remains largely unexplored for image editing, primarily due to the lack of a robust, general-purpose reward model. Existing edit reward models act as holistic scorers that output a single score without decomposing instruction requirements, leading to biased or hallucinated feedback that fails to balance instruction fidelity, preservation of unedited regions, and visual quality simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260778/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The editing model takes a reference RGB image and a text edit instruction, producing an edited image via flow-matching diffusion. The RRM (built on Qwen-VL-2.5 at 3B or 7B) consumes a quadruple — reference image, edited image, instruction, and a pre-decomposed principle set — and outputs a structured Chain-of-Thought verification trace (principles checked in JSON format) followed by a scalar score enclosed in <score> tags. The principle set is decomposed per-sample into three categories: Keep (elements to preserve), Follow (required modifications), and Quality (generic visual integrity).

## Method

The framework trains a Verifier-based Reasoning Reward Model (RRM) in two stages. Stage 1 (cold-start SFT) constructs ~2M quadruples from 200K ImageEdit samples, uses multiple VLMs to generate diverse CoT reasoning traces with principle-by-principle verification, and selects the highest-accuracy trace via an external VLM judge to form the SFT dataset. Stage 2 introduces Group Contrastive Preference Optimization (GCPO), a novel RL algorithm that generates N reasoning traces per image in a human preference pair, computes win/loss ratio rewards via exhaustive cross-group score comparisons, then computes advantages independently within each group and optimizes with a clipped surrogate loss — effectively aligning a pointwise reasoning model with pairwise preference data. Downstream, the trained non-differentiable RRM serves as the reward signal in a GRPO loop (Flow-GRPO, G=24, β=0.04) to optimize editing models like FLUX.Kontext and Qwen-Image-Edit, where the RRM's structured principle verification provides fine-grained, interpretable feedback that standard holistic scorers cannot.

## Results

On the internal 5K-sample benchmark, the 7B RL-RRM (SFT + GCPO) achieves 82.2% pairwise preference accuracy, surpassing the Seed-1.5-VL API baseline (79.3%) and Seed-1.6-VL (77.2%). On the public EditRewardBench, the RL-RRM reaches 78.2% accuracy versus EditScore-7B's 65.9% (72.7% with inference scaling). For downstream editing on GEdit-Bench-EN, applying RL-RRM (7B) to FLUX.Kontext improves the Overall score from 5.77 to 6.24 and Semantic Consistency from 6.27 to 6.86, outperforming RL with SeedVLM-1.5 (O 6.03, SC 6.74).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the internal reward-model benchmark, the 7B RL-RRM (82.2%) outperforms the Seed-1.5-VL API (79.3%) by ~3 points and the VIESCORE-prompted Qwen-7B baseline (68.3%) by ~14 points. On the public EditRewardBench, the RL-RRM (78.2%) beats EditScore-7B (65.9%) by 12.3 points and even EditScore-7B with inference scaling (72.7%) by 5.5 points. For downstream editing on FLUX.Kontext, RL with the RL-RRM (7B) achieves O 6.24 / SC 6.86, surpassing RL with SeedVLM-1.5 (O 6.03 / SC 6.74) and the SFT-only 7B RRM (O 6.20 / SC 6.81), confirming that GCPO refines the reward signal into a stricter evaluator. On Qwen-Edit, the gains are more modest (O 7.45 → 7.50) because the base model already saturates Best-of-N selection, though a notable 15.2% relative improvement appears in the Motion Change category (SC 4.01 → 4.62). The 3B RL-RRM (O 6.10) trails the 7B version (O 6.24), demonstrating a clear scaling trend with model size.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF-for-visual-generation line (Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO) to image editing by introducing a non-differentiable, reasoning-based reward model into the GRPO loop, where prior editing RL work (OneReward, ByteEdit) relied on differentiable holistic scorers or DPO. It departs from the dominant 'single-score' reward paradigm (EditScore, Skywork-EditReward) by adopting a verifier architecture that decomposes instructions into Keep/Follow/Quality principles and performs per-principle CoT verification, drawing conceptually from DeepSeek-GRM's principle-based reasoning but specializing it to visual edits. The novel GCPO algorithm bridges a gap left by standard DPO/GRPO: aligning a pointwise reasoning model with pairwise human preferences, a problem the authors identify as unsolved in prior RLHF literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck—RLHF for image editing has lagged behind T2I generation largely due to the absence of a robust, interpretable reward model—and provides a concrete, two-stage recipe (SFT cold-start + GCPO) that practitioners can adopt. The finding that GCPO-refined RRMs yield lower training rewards but higher evaluation rewards (i.e., they become stricter evaluators) offers a useful diagnostic for reward-model quality that may generalize beyond editing. Open questions include whether the principle-decomposition approach scales to open-ended or multi-turn editing, the cost of requiring an external VLM as a quality-control judge during data curation, and whether the 10k-preference-pair GCPO stage can be further reduced or replaced with synthetic preferences.
