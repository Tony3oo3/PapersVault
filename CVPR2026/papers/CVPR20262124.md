## Problem

Most image restoration methods, including diffusion-based approaches like DDRM, assume i.i.d. Gaussian noise, but real rolling-shutter sensors produce spatially correlated noise that violates this assumption and degrades restoration quality. Additionally, no publicly available dataset explicitly contains real-world correlated sensor noise, leaving a critical gap in experimental evaluation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262124/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CARD takes a noisy or degraded RGB image (256×256 in experiments), an estimate of the spatial noise covariance matrix Σ (obtained from dark-frame calibration or estimated blindly from flat patches in the noisy image), and a pretrained unconditional diffusion model. It outputs a restored clean image. The covariance matrix is the distinguishing input: it encodes inter-pixel spatial correlations and is applied via a Cholesky-based whitening transform before any diffusion sampling occurs.

## Method

CARD is a two-step, training-free extension of DDRM that handles correlated Gaussian noise. Step 1 (whitening): the measurement equation y = Hx₀ + n with n ~ N(0, σ²Σ) is preconditioned by left-multiplying with Σ^(−1/2), converting the correlated noise into i.i.d. Gaussian noise and yielding a modified forward operator H̃ = Σ^(−1/2)H. Step 2 (noise-whitened updates): DDRM's closed-form posterior sampling is applied in the whitened measurement space using the SVD of H̃, so the interpolation weights depend on the singular values of H̃ rather than H. This preserves DDRM's closed-form efficiency and requires no retraining of the diffusion model. For high-resolution images, whitening is applied patchwise over non-overlapping tiles, exploiting the short-range nature of sensor noise correlations.

## Results

On ImageNet denoising with correlated noise, CARD achieves 34.0 dB PSNR / 0.07 LPIPS at σ₀=0.1, 29.1 / 0.15 at σ₀=0.5, and 26.7 / 0.22 at σ₀=0.9, outperforming the closest diffusion baseline DDRM by 3.0–4.0 dB PSNR across noise levels. On LSUN (Bed+Cat average), CARD reaches 34.7 / 0.06, 29.6 / 0.14, and 27.2 / 0.19 at the same noise levels. On the real-sensor CIN-D dataset at high noise, CARD achieves 31.5 dB PSNR / 0.34 LPIPS, surpassing DDRM (30.8 / 0.39) and all learning-based correlated-noise denoisers (≤17.7 dB). CARD also leads on Gaussian deblurring (26.6 dB on ImageNet at σ₀=0.2 vs. DDRM's 24.3) and 4× super-resolution (25.0 dB vs. DDRM's 22.9 at σ₀=0.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARD outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset, task, and noise level tested. The closest competitor is DDRM [9], the i.i.d.-noise diffusion restoration model that CARD directly extends; CARD beats DDRM by 3.0 dB PSNR on ImageNet denoising at σ₀=0.1 (34.0 vs. 31.0), 4.3 dB at σ₀=0.5 (29.1 vs. 24.8), and 4.0 dB at σ₀=0.9 (26.7 vs. 22.7), with analogous margins on LSUN and CIN-D. Among learning-based i.i.d. denoisers, Restormer [6] is the strongest at low noise (31.3 dB) but degrades sharply to 18.5 dB at σ₀=0.9, while Noise2Info [8] and PCST [42] remain below 26 dB across all levels. The correlated-noise learning baselines (APRRD-BSN/NBSN [45], AP-BSN [43], LG-BPN [44]) all trail CARD by 4–16 dB, with LG-BPN [44] being the best at 27.5 dB (σ₀=0.1) yet still 6.5 dB below CARD. On deblurring, CARD surpasses DDRM by 2.3 dB (26.6 vs. 24.3 on ImageNet at σ₀=0.2) and DiffIR [30] by 6.5 dB; on 4× super-resolution, CARD leads DDRM by 2.1 dB (25.0 vs. 22.9) and DiffIR by 1.2 dB. On the real-sensor CIN-D dataset, CARD (38.1 dB low / 31.5 dB high) exceeds DDRM (35.3 / 30.8) and PnP-ADMM [49] (33.5 / 25.8), while correlated-noise denoisers APRRD-BSN/NBSN [45] collapse to ~17 dB, indicating poor generalization beyond their training statistics.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARD is a direct extension of DDRM (Kawar et al., NeurIPS 2022), which derives closed-form posterior updates for diffusion-based restoration under the i.i.d. Gaussian noise assumption. The key conceptual departure is a Cholesky-based whitening step that converts spatially correlated sensor noise into an i.i.d. form before applying DDRM's spectral-domain conditioning, making the method a drop-in replacement for any linear inverse problem. This contrasts with learning-based correlated-noise denoisers (AP-BSN, LG-BPN, APRRD) that require task-specific retraining and approximate correlations heuristically, and with other diffusion restoration methods (DDNM, DPS, DiffIR) that inherit the same i.i.d. limitation. The CIN-D dataset addresses a gap the authors identify: no prior public benchmark explicitly contains spatially correlated sensor noise for evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Because rolling-shutter CMOS sensors are ubiquitous in consumer and machine-vision cameras, the i.i.d. noise assumption underlying nearly all restoration methods is practically violated in a large fraction of real-world imaging, making CARD's training-free correction broadly relevant. The introduction of CIN-D provides the first public evaluation bed for correlated-noise restoration, which should accelerate future work in this direction. Open questions include extending CARD to non-Gaussian (e.g., Poisson-Gaussian) sensor noise, improving blind covariance estimation without dark frames, and handling non-linear degradation operators where the whitening-then-DDRM decomposition no longer applies directly.
