## Problem

Direct 6-DoF pose regression methods rely on globally pooled first-order features and discontinuous pose representations (quaternions, Euler angles), making them less accurate than indirect PnP-based approaches. The paper identifies that spatial second-order statistics (covariance) of backbone features are strongly correlated with pose distance, yet have never been exploited for end-to-end pose regression, and that continuous rotation parametrizations remain underutilized in direct regressors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262181/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces a 6-DoF pose as a continuous 6D rotation representation (two 3D vectors mapped to SO(3) via differentiable Gram-Schmidt) coupled with a 3D translation vector. Internally, the EfficientNetB6 backbone outputs feature maps at 17×17 spatial resolution, which are converted into a 289×289 spatial covariance matrix; the final pose is decoded from a compact 4×4 SPD matrix via Cholesky factorization, where diagonal entries encode translation (exponentiated) and off-diagonal entries encode the rotation vectors.

## Method

Cov2Pose replaces standard global pooling with spatial covariance pooling, encoding pairwise co-activations across the 17×17 feature grid as a 289×289 SPD matrix. This high-dimensional SPD descriptor is compressed to 4×4 through four alternating BiMap (Stiefel-constrained congruence projection) and ReEig (eigenvalue floor) layers that preserve the SPD manifold geometry at each step. A differentiable Cholesky decomposition then maps the compact SPD matrix to a lower-triangular factor whose entries directly parameterize the 6D rotation and 3D translation, yielding an injective, continuous, and differentiable pose decoding. Training is fully end-to-end with a mixed-geometry optimizer: Riemannian gradient steps with QR retraction for the Stiefel-constrained BiMap weights and Adam for the Euclidean backbone, supervised by geodesic distance on SO(3), L2 translation loss, and orthonormality/unit-norm regularizers on the rotation vectors.

## Results

On LineMod, Cov2Pose achieves 97.2% ADD(-S) average, surpassing all end-to-end baselines (best prior: EPro-PnP at 95.80%) and PnP-based methods (PVNet at 86.27%). On LineMod Occlusion, it reaches 76.8% ADD(-S), outperforming the best end-to-end method (GDR-Net at 62.2%) by 14.6 points and nearly matching the top PnP-based method ZebraPose (76.9%). On YCB-Video, Cov2Pose attains 69.7% ADD(-S) (best among end-to-end) and 90.0% AUC(ADD-S), trailing GDR-Net by only 1.6 AUC points. Inference time is 46.9 ms, competitive with other end-to-end methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among end-to-end methods, Cov2Pose dominates across all three benchmarks: on LM it beats EPro-PnP (95.80) and BPnP (93.27) by 1.4 and 3.9 ADD(-S) points respectively; on LM-O it surpasses GDR-Net (62.2) by 14.6 points and Self6D++ (59.8) by 17.0 points; on YCB-V it leads all end-to-end baselines in ADD(-S) (69.7 vs. GDR-Net's 60.1). Against PnP-based methods, Cov2Pose slightly trails on LM-O (76.8 vs. ZebraPose's 76.9, a 0.1-point gap) and YCB-V (69.7 vs. VAPO's 84.9 in ADD(-S)), but narrows the AUC gap to the best PnP method to just 2.3 points. The ablation confirms that the 6D continuous rotation encoding contributes +5.9 ADD(-S) over Euler angles on LM-O, and replacing the SPD head with a Euclidean MLP causes a catastrophic drop to 31.0 ADD(-S), underscoring the necessity of manifold-aware processing.

## Relation to Prior Work

Cov2Pose sits at the intersection of two established research threads: SPD-manifold deep learning (BiMap/ReEig layers from Huang & Van Gool, AAAI 2017, previously applied to classification tasks like face and action recognition) and continuous rotation representations (6D + Gram-Schmidt from Zhou et al., CVPR 2019). The paper's conceptual departure is repurposing spatial covariance pooling—long used in classification and detection—as a feature extractor for pose regression, and then decoding the resulting SPD matrix into a pose via a differentiable Cholesky factorization, a combination not previously explored. It explicitly positions itself against prior direct regressors (PoseCNN, DeepIM, GDR-Net, Self6D++) that rely on globally pooled first-order features and non-continuous rotation encodings (quaternions, Euler angles), and against PnP-based indirect methods that incur iterative optimization costs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that second-order spatial statistics carry pose-relevant information that first-order pooling discards, the paper opens a new design axis for direct pose regressors and narrows the accuracy gap to correspondence-based PnP methods to within ~1–2 ADD(-S) points on LineMOD. The ablation showing a 45-point collapse when the SPD head is replaced by a Euclidean MLP underscores that the Riemannian geometry is not merely a formal nicety but functionally critical. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-object or category-level pose estimation, how it handles object symmetries (acknowledged as a current limitation), and whether the 4×4 SPD bottleneck is the optimal dimensionality or whether larger compact SPD codes could further close the gap to PnP methods.
