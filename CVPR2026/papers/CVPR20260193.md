## Problem

Capturing scenes with both high dynamic range and high-speed motion remains unsolved: conventional HDR video methods rely on frame-based cameras whose limited frame rates and alternating-exposure strategies introduce temporal resolution loss, while existing spike-camera approaches either require long accumulation windows (causing motion blur) or hardware modifications to the sensor. No prior method achieves HDR reconstruction at high frame rates (≥1000 FPS) from a single sensor without sacrificing spatial or temporal fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260193/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-attenuated spike frames generated by a rapidly rotating SpokeND filter (three attenuation levels—92%, 75%, 0%—repeated four times per revolution at 1800 RPM) placed in front of a spike camera. ReGain takes a local temporal window of 2K+1 TFI-reconstructed baseline frames centered at time t with fixed interval Δg; ReFine concatenates the current ReGain output with a previous frame at interval Δf to produce the refined output. The final output is an HDR video at an arbitrary target frame rate up to 2000 FPS, with each frame reconstructed in a coarse-to-fine manner.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage supervised pipeline, ReST-Net, that exploits the ultra-high temporal resolution of a spike camera to sample multi-attenuated light through a custom-designed rotating SpokeND filter, enabling both spatial and temporal modulation with a single sensor. The ReGain module (U-Net with self-attention blocks) learns to recover the spatially consistent gain—i.e., remove the spatially varying attenuation—by processing 2K+1 temporally adjacent multi-attenuated frames, leveraging the fact that rapid rotation ensures at least one attenuation level avoids saturation or zero-spike conditions in any temporal neighborhood. The ReFine module (another U-Net with self-attention) then suppresses inter-frame flickering by conditioning on the current and a previous ReGain output, producing temporally consistent HDR frames. Training is supervised on 235 synthetic groups (spikes simulated from HDR videos by Chang et al. and Su et al.) using L1+L2 loss for ReGain and L1+L2+temporal-consistency loss for ReFine, with the method generalizing to 100 real-world groups without retraining despite lens defocus.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset, ReST-Net achieves PSNR of 34.27 dB, SSIM of 0.916, HDR-VDP3 of 7.501, and HDR-VQM of 0.152, outperforming all spike-based baselines across every metric. The closest competitor, TFW-200 (long temporal window accumulation), reaches 29.05 PSNR and 0.742 SSIM but introduces significant motion blur in high-speed scenes. The method reconstructs HDR video at up to 2000 FPS using a single spike camera with a rapidly rotating SpokeND filter operating at 1800 RPM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four spike-based reconstruction baselines from Huang et al. [14]: TFW-10, TFW-70, TFW-200 (temporal window accumulation with different window sizes), and TFI (temporal frame interpolation based on inter-spike intervals). ReST-Net wins decisively on all four metrics against every baseline, with the largest margin over TFW-10 (PSNR 34.27 vs. 13.47) and a more moderate but still clear advantage over the strongest baseline TFW-200 (PSNR 34.27 vs. 29.05, SSIM 0.916 vs. 0.742). The authors note the comparison is not entirely fair since the baselines are designed for non-attenuated spikes, yet they still serve as strong references. Ablation results confirm both modules contribute: removing ReGain drops PSNR to 21.89 (spatial inconsistency), while removing ReFine drops it to 31.48 (reduced temporal refinement). On real-world data, qualitative evaluation shows the method successfully reconstructs HDR details in both indoor and outdoor scenes where baselines exhibit noise, spatial inconsistency, or motion blur.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the integrate-and-trigger spike camera model and TFI/TFW reconstruction baselines established by Huang et al. [14], extending them to handle optically modulated (multi-attenuated) spike streams. It departs from prior spike-HDR approaches that modify sensor hardware (quantization-level modulation in [2]) or require hybrid multi-sensor systems [1], instead introducing an external mechanical modulation (rotating SpokeND filter) that exploits the spike camera's ultra-high temporal resolution to sample multiple attenuation levels per pixel. The two-stage ReST-Net (ReGain + ReFine) is architecturally a U-Net with self-attention, positioned as a coarse-to-fine pipeline analogous to prior HDR video fusion networks [3, 16] but adapted to the nonlinear, spatially-varying attenuation introduced by the rotating filter.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to reconstruct HDR video at up to 2000 FPS from a single spike sensor, addressing a gap where conventional alternating-exposure methods are fundamentally limited by frame rate. The practical hardware design (a simple rotating resin filter at 1800 RPM) is accessible and avoids the complexity of multi-sensor synchronization or sensor-level modifications. Key limitations remain: performance degrades predictably with increasing motion speed (PSNR drops from 34.27 to 26.35 at 3× speed), the filter's external placement introduces defocus that the network must absorb without retraining, and the approach is currently monochrome. Open questions include extending to color reconstruction, placing the filter at the sensor plane to eliminate defocus, and whether the two-stage design can be unified or made more efficient for real-time deployment.
