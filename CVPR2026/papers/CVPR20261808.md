## Problem

Existing learning-based keypoint detectors are trained on image pairs, optimizing for instantaneous matchability within isolated pairs. This objective is misaligned with sequential applications like SLAM and SfM, where keypoints must remain trackable over long trajectories under viewpoint and illumination changes. Even prior RL-based methods (DISK, RIPE) compute rewards from a single image pair, leaving long-term trackability unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference RGB image (480px during training, up to 1600px at inference) and produces a pixel-wise keypoint probability map (logit map L ∈ R^{H×W}) from which N keypoints are extracted via 5×5 NMS, alongside a dense 256-d descriptor map at 1/4 resolution from a frozen DINOv3-ConvNeXt + deformable transformer branch. During RL training, the reference image is paired with a 5-frame sequence whose relative poses and depth maps are known, enabling geometric projection of sampled keypoints into all target frames for reward computation.

## Method

TraqPoint reframes keypoint detection as a sequential decision-making problem: a 4-layer CNN policy network outputs a spatial probability distribution over the reference image, and a hybrid sampling strategy (global sampling from P_θ plus per-cell grid sampling) selects N=256 candidate keypoints balancing exploitation and spatial coverage. The core innovation is a track-aware composite reward: each sampled keypoint is projected into all frames of a 5-image sequence, and its per-frame reward combines a Rank Reward (saliency percentile within a local K×K patch, with a 0.2 cutoff) and a Distinctiveness Reward (Lowe's ratio test against all N projected descriptors using the frozen descriptor branch, threshold 0.85). The final track reward is the mean over all visible frames, and the policy is optimized via policy gradient with spatial entropy regularization and a FAST-detector warm-up loss that anneals out over the first 10% of training. The descriptor branch is pretrained on MegaDepth with focal loss and kept frozen, decoupling policy learning from descriptor training.

## Results

On relative pose estimation (Table 1), TraqPoint achieves AUC@5° of 55.8 on MegaDepth and 16.6 on ScanNet, outperforming the strongest MNN-based baseline RDD (51.9 / 13.7) by 3.9 and 2.9 points respectively. On visual odometry (KITTI Seq-01), TraqPoint reduces Average Trajectory Error to 29.9 m (vs. 35.3 m for RDD) and nearly doubles the Average Keypoint Tracking Length to 7.3 frames (vs. 4.6 for RDD). On ETH 3D reconstruction (Madrid Metropolis), TraqPoint registers 693 images and produces 254k sparse points with a mean track length of 11.14, surpassing all baselines on density and track persistence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TraqPoint outperforms all 14 baselines on the primary relative pose estimation task (Table 1), with the largest margins over RL-based methods DISK (+17.3 AUC@5° on MegaDepth) and RIPE (+10.4), and a 3.9-point gain over the strongest supervised baseline RDD. On visual localization (Aachen Day-Night), TraqPoint matches or exceeds all methods, achieving 100.0% at the 1.0 m / 10° threshold at night (tied with DISK and ALIKE) and the best daytime score of 87.9% at 0.25 m / 2°. On visual odometry (KITTI), TraqPoint dominates on all three sequences: it cuts ATE by 15–78% relative to RDD and RIPE, and more than doubles AKTL (e.g., 7.3 vs. 4.6 on Seq-01), confirming the trackability advantage. On ETH 3D reconstruction, TraqPoint leads in registered images, sparse point count, and track length across all three scenes, while incurring only a marginal increase in reprojection error (e.g., 1.03 px vs. 0.60 px for RootSIFT on Madrid Metropolis) due to retaining harder keypoints. The only baseline that approaches TraqPoint on ScanNet pose estimation is SP+SuperGlue (16.1 vs. 16.6 AUC@5°), but that method requires a separate learned matcher, whereas TraqPoint uses only MNN matching and was never trained on indoor data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the dual-branch architecture of RDD (deformable transformer descriptor + lightweight CNN keypoint branch from ALIKED), replacing the ResNet-50 backbone with DINOv3-ConvNeXt. Its key conceptual departure is from the pairwise RL paradigm established by DISK, RIPE, and RFP, which compute rewards from a single image pair's matchability. TraqPoint instead treats the entire image sequence as the RL environment, computing a composite trackability reward (rank consistency + Lowe's-ratio distinctiveness) across multiple views, thereby shifting the optimization objective from instantaneous matchability to long-term track persistence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine misalignment in the field: most learned keypoint detectors are trained on pairs yet deployed in sequential systems (SLAM, SfM, VO) where long-term track stability is the actual requirement. The consistent gains across pairwise (AUC@5), localization, odometry (AKTL), and reconstruction (track length, sparse points) benchmarks suggest the sequence-aware reward captures a real signal beyond what pairwise objectives provide. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences, the dependency on known relative poses and depth for reward computation (limiting fully unsupervised use), and whether the approach transfers to video without ground-truth geometry. The fixed 5-frame training window and 256-keypoint budget also raise questions about robustness in highly dynamic or long-trajectory scenarios.
