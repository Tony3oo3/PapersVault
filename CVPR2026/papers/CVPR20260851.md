## Problem

Existing multi-modal test-time adaptation (MM-TTA) methods apply modality-agnostic updates that cause negative transfer in well-aligned (unbiased) modalities and catastrophic forgetting in corrupted (biased) modalities. No prior approach can selectively prioritize stability or plasticity per modality, leaving the fundamental stability-plasticity dilemma unresolved in multi-modal settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260851/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired audio and video clips processed by ViT-based encoders (CAV-MAE) and produces a single class label via a shared classification head. Adaptation operates on unlabeled mini-batches of 64 target-domain samples at test time; the redundancy score is computed on batch-level feature matrices after projection into the shared fusion latent space, making cross-modality comparison possible without source-domain statistics.

## Method

DASP is a diagnose-then-mitigate framework. For diagnosis, it computes an interdimensional redundancy score R(Z) on batch feature matrices in the shared latent space; the modality whose redundancy exceeds a threshold δ relative to the minimum is flagged as biased. For mitigation, each modality-specific adapter is decoupled into a low-rank stable component and a high-rank plastic component. For the biased modality, only the plastic adapter is updated (stable adapter frozen) to capture domain-specific shifts; for the unbiased modality, the plastic adapter is bypassed and the stable adapter is updated under KL regularization to prevent negative transfer. The overall unsupervised objective combines entropy minimization, batch-level diversity regularization, and the KL penalty, with the asymmetric routing ensuring domain-specific parameters are externalized while domain-agnostic knowledge is preserved.

## Results

On Kinetics50-C with video corruptions (episodic adaptation), DASP achieves 65.2% average accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline TSA (63.8%) by 1.4% and the source model (59.9%) by 5.3%. On VGGSound-C with audio corruptions, DASP reaches 39.7% average accuracy, a 5.0% improvement over TSA (34.7%) and a 14.1% gain over the source model (25.6%). Under continual adaptation with interleaved modality corruption, DASP achieves 67.3% on Kinetics50-C and 52.5% on VGGSound-C, surpassing the best baselines by 3.4% and 1.5% respectively, while uni-modal baselines like Tent collapse to 47.4% and 34.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DASP outperforms all five baselines across every evaluation setting. Against the two multi-modal TTA methods, DASP gains 1.4% over TSA and 2.7% over READ on Kinetics50-C video corruptions (episodic), and the gap widens to 5.0% and 7.3% respectively on VGGSound-C audio corruptions where the source model is weakest (25.6%). The three uni-modal baselines (Tent, EATA, SAR) perform poorly on the audio-corrupted VGGSound-C, with Tent collapsing to 4.5% (episodic) and 2.3% (continual) due to negative transfer on the dominant audio modality, while DASP maintains 39.7% and 39.6%. Under the most challenging interleaved continual setting on VGGSound-C, TSA degrades catastrophically to 12.8% (vs. 52.5% for DASP), highlighting that soft routing mechanisms fail under sustained multi-modal shifts. The largest absolute gains for DASP appear in continual and interleaved scenarios, where its decoupled stable/plastic adapter design prevents the catastrophic forgetting that plagues all baselines (e.g., Tent drops from 59.9% to 41.8% on Kinetics50-C video continual, while DASP improves to 65.3%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-modal test-time adaptation (MM-TTA) line initiated by READ (ICLR'24) and TSA (ICML'25), which address reliability bias through self-adaptive fusion and selective routing, respectively. It departs from these modality-agnostic or soft-routing approaches by introducing a hard, non-parametric diagnostic (the interdimensional redundancy score) to identify the biased modality, then applying an asymmetric stable/plastic adapter decomposition inspired by LoRA-style parameter-efficient fine-tuning. The entropy-minimization and KL-regularization objectives are standard in TTA (Tent, EATA, SAR), but their asymmetric application across modalities—freezing the stable adapter for biased modalities while bypassing the plastic adapter for unbiased ones—is the paper's core architectural departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The redundancy score offers a simple, source-free diagnostic for modality bias that avoids the pitfalls of entropy/confidence comparison across modalities with inherent information asymmetry. The asymmetric adapter design provides a clean mechanism to externalize domain-specific parameters, directly addressing the stability-plasticity dilemma that plagues continual TTA. However, the evaluation is limited to two modalities (audio and video) on two benchmarks, and the fixed threshold δ = 0.05 raises questions about generalizability to more than two modalities, other task types (e.g., detection, segmentation), or scenarios where both modalities are simultaneously corrupted. Whether the redundancy-based diagnosis remains reliable under compound or subtle shifts remains an open question.
