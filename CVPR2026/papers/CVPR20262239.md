## Problem

Modern diffusion models still produce plausible structural artifacts (duplication, omission, distortion, fusion) that existing VLMs cannot reliably detect, localize, or explain. Prior artifact-aware methods depend on costly human-annotated datasets and target only simple distortions (Gaussian noise, blur) prevalent in early models, leaving a gap for scalable, annotator-free synthesis of the diverse structural artifacts produced by current DiT-based generators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262239/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes real RGB images from four source datasets (COCO, Caltech-101, 11K Hands, Celeba HQ) and produces paired clean/artifact-injected images at the native resolution of the source, along with structured annotations: a binary artifact label, bounding boxes for each artifact region, a short local explanation per region, and a global image-level explanation. These pairs are converted into VQA training samples to fine-tune VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL3.5-8B), which then output detection decisions, localized boxes, and free-text explanations at inference time. The inversion-injection operates at the patch level inside DiT self-attention, so the artifact granularity is bounded by the patch size of the underlying diffusion transformer.

## Method

ArtiAgent is a three-agent pipeline. The perception agent decomposes each image into an entity–subentity hierarchy via an off-the-shelf VLM and grounds them with Grounded-SAM, performing containment analysis to link subentities to parent entities. The synthesis agent selects one of four tools (add, remove, distort, fuse) to build a target-reference patch mapping, then applies a novel inversion-injection module: during an inversion pass it caches per-layer value embeddings, and during the subsequent denoising pass it replaces both the RoPE positional embedding and the value embedding of target patches with those of their reference patches while leaving background patches untouched, effectively transplanting spatial semantics from one region to another. Injections are restricted to early-to-middle transformer layers and disabled in the final denoising steps to avoid edge-discontinuity shortcuts. The curation agent filters results using LPIPS thresholds for distortion artifacts or VLM-based triplet verification for the other three types, then generates local and global textual explanations via GPT-4o, yielding 100K annotated training pairs without any human labeling.

## Results

On the new ArtiBench benchmark, Qwen2.5-VL-7B fine-tuned with ArtiAgent achieves 62.7% accuracy and 62.7% F1 for binary artifact detection, surpassing GPT-5 (59.9% Acc), GPT-4o (61.9% Acc), and Gemini-2.5-Pro (58.2% Acc). For localization on ArtiBench, the same model reaches mIoU 0.111 and F1 0.168, nearly doubling GPT-5's mIoU of 0.061. For explanation, ArtiAgent-trained Qwen2.5-VL-7B attains ROUGE 0.233 and CSS 0.643 on ArtiBench, substantially outperforming GPT-5 (ROUGE 0.145, CSS 0.434). InternVL3.5-8B + ArtiAgent achieves comparable or slightly better detection (63.0% Acc) and localization (mIoU 0.119) on ArtiBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArtiAgent-trained open-source VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL3.5-8B) consistently outperform all proprietary VLMs (GPT-5, GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro) across all three tasks on the new ArtiBench: detection accuracy improves from 59.9% (GPT-5) to 62.7–63.0%, localization mIoU nearly doubles (0.061 → 0.111–0.119), and explanation CSS jumps from 0.434 (GPT-5) to 0.625–0.643. Against prior artifact-specific methods, LEGION (trained on SynthScars) achieves the strongest prior localization on SynthScars (mIoU 0.106) but is surpassed by ArtiAgent-trained models (mIoU 0.137–0.140); DiffDoctor leads on LOKI localization (mIoU 0.175) but fails to generalize to ArtiBench (no reported score) or SynthScars (mIoU 0.081 vs. 0.137). PAL is the weakest baseline, achieving mIoU ≤ 0.079 across all localization benchmarks. Compared to their own vanilla counterparts, ArtiAgent fine-tuning yields the largest gains: Qwen2.5-VL-7B detection accuracy rises from 50.1% to 62.7% (+12.6 pp), and InternVL3.5-8B from 49.8% to 63.0% (+13.2 pp). On the 1K-sample comparison (Table 4), ArtiAgent synthetic supervision matches human-annotated SynthScars on detection (55.5% Acc) and outperforms it on explanation (ROUGE 0.222 vs. 0.156, CSS 0.606 vs. 0.521), while SynthScars retains a slight localization edge (mIoU 0.094 vs. 0.074), attributed to patch-level granularity of ArtiAgent labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inversion-restoration paradigm (FireFlow, SDEdit, Null-text inversion) by introducing a novel patch-wise positional embedding (RoPE) manipulation within DiT self-attention layers to synthesize structural artifacts, going beyond prior value-injection-only editing methods. It departs from existing artifact datasets (PAL, SynthScars, DiffDoctor) that rely on costly human annotation and target naive distortions from early diffusion models, instead proposing a fully automated agentic pipeline that generates plausible structural artifacts at scale. The VLM fine-tuning approach builds on standard supervised fine-tuning of open-source models (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3.5) but demonstrates that synthetic artifact supervision can match or exceed human-annotated data, positioning the work at the intersection of data synthesis, VLM training, and diffusion model quality control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: modern DiT-based diffusion models produce subtle structural artifacts (duplication, omission, distortion, fusion) that even SOTA VLMs like GPT-5 detect near random chance, yet no scalable annotation pipeline exists for these failure modes. The agentic data synthesis approach offers a cost-effective, annotator-free alternative to human labeling, and the ArtiBench benchmark provides the first evaluation set reflecting modern generative model artifacts. Key open questions include whether synthetically injected artifacts fully capture the diversity of naturally occurring diffusion failures, whether the patch-level granularity limitation in localization generalizes to finer-grained tasks, and whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation where structural artifacts are even more consequential.
