## Problem

Most efficient super-resolution networks apply spatially uniform computation, allocating equal resources to all image regions regardless of reconstruction difficulty. However, reconstruction errors are disproportionately concentrated in texture-rich and edge-heavy areas that carry the most information, making uniform allocation wasteful under tight computational budgets. The paper addresses the lack of a principled, information-theoretic mechanism for identifying and prioritizing these high-information-density regions during SR.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263258/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single low-resolution RGB image as input and produces a high-resolution RGB image at ×2, ×3, or ×4 scale. During training, 64×64 LR patches are extracted with random flips and rotations; at inference, FLOPs are reported for a 1280×720 HR output. A key intermediate output is the pixel-wise Information Density Map (IDM), an unsupervised spatial map that quantifies local encoding complexity and guides both branches of the feature enhancement pipeline.

## Method

IAFMNet first estimates a pixel-wise Information Density Map (IDM) via an Information Density Estimator that models quantized shallow features with a factorized Gaussian density (using Generalized Divisive Normalization) and minimizes an unsupervised Information Entropy Loss, yielding a spatially varying scale map θ that highlights high-encoding-cost regions. The IDM then drives a dual-branch Information-guided Feature Enhancement Module: (1) an Information-Guided Resource Allocation branch that generates a binary top-k% mask from θ and applies Submanifold Sparse Convolution to restrict computation to informative sites, followed by IDM-guided self-attention refinement; and (2) an Affine Recalibration Module that concatenates θ with downsampled features to produce information-aware channel-wise scale parameters for soft feature modulation. The network is trained with a composite loss L₁ + λ·L_IE (λ=0.0001), jointly optimizing reconstruction fidelity and density-model accuracy, and the final HR image is reconstructed via a lightweight convolution plus PixelShuffle.

## Results

IAFMNet (198K params, 42G FLOPs) achieves 32.52 dB PSNR / 0.9312 SSIM on Urban100 and 39.32 dB / 0.9792 on Manga109 for ×2 SR, outperforming all lightweight CNN baselines while using fewer FLOPs than SeemoRe-T (45G) and SMFANet (41G). At ×4, IAFMNet (220K, 11G) reaches 26.39 dB on Urban100 and 30.97 dB on Manga109, surpassing SLVR-S (158K, 8.5G) and SMFANet (197K, 11G). The larger IAFMNet-L variant (519K, 28G) achieves 26.73 dB on Urban100 and 31.39 dB on Manga109 at ×4, beating SRFormer-light (873K, 63G) and CATANet (535K, 41G) with substantially lower computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IAFMNet outperforms all 10 lightweight CNN baselines (FSRCNN, EDSR-baseline, CARN-M, IMDN, ShuffleMixer, LAPAR-A, SMFANet, SeemoRe-T, SAFMN, SLVR-S) across all five datasets and all three upscaling factors. The margin is most pronounced on Urban100, where IAFMNet gains 0.30 dB over SeemoRe-T at ×2 and 0.21 dB over SMFANet at ×4, confirming that the IDM-guided allocation is most beneficial for texture-rich content. Against the larger ViT-based methods in Table 2, IAFMNet-L (519K params, 28G FLOPs) surpasses SRFormer-light (873K, 63G) by 0.08 dB on Urban100 and 0.22 dB on Manga109 while using 40% fewer FLOPs, and edges out CATANet (535K, 41G) on Manga109 (31.39 vs. 31.31 dB) with 32% fewer FLOPs. IAFMNet-L trails CATANet by 0.14 dB on Urban100 (26.73 vs. 26.87 dB), the only loss among the top-tier comparisons. Among the CNN baselines, SMFANet and SeemoRe-T are the closest competitors, with IAFMNet winning by 0.10–0.32 dB depending on dataset and scale, while older methods like FSRCNN and CARN-M lag by 1–2 dB. The consistent advantage on Manga109 (anime/manga content with dense line art) across all scales further validates the information-density-driven strategy for structured, high-contrast textures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'spatially uniform' paradigm in efficient SR, where both CNN and ViT methods allocate equal computation across all regions. It repurposes tools from learned image compression—specifically Generalized Divisive Normalization (Ballé et al., 2015) for density modeling and Submanifold Sparse Convolution (Graham & Van der Maaten, 2017) for restricted computation—into a super-resolution context, framing reconstruction difficulty through Shannon entropy rather than empirical proxies like gradients or PSNR gaps. The dual-branch design (hard sparse allocation via IGRA + soft affine modulation via ARM) is a novel combination, though each component individually draws from established literature on sparse networks and feature recalibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, information-theoretic justification for adaptive computation in SR, replacing ad-hoc heuristics with an interpretable Information Density Map that highlights where reconstruction is genuinely hard. This could influence how future efficient restoration methods allocate resources, particularly on edge devices where FLOPs budgets are tight. However, the performance gains over strong baselines are modest (0.05–0.30 dB), and the evaluation is limited to bicubic degradation on standard benchmarks; whether the IDM generalizes to real-world degradations (noise, blur, sensor artifacts) or to video SR remains an open question. The unsupervised nature of the IDM estimation is appealing but its robustness across diverse content types beyond the tested datasets is not thoroughly stress-tested.
