## Problem

While prior medical VLM work has focused on architectural changes, new RL objectives, or single-modality pipelines, the composition of the SFT training mixture—how question sources, modalities, and reasoning traces are structured, balanced, and scaled—remains largely unexplored. This paper asks what data recipes for structuring and balancing multimodal medical reasoning data most effectively improve generalization to out-of-distribution clinical tasks.

## Input / Output

The model accepts medical images (preprocessed to a maximum of 512×512 pixels, 262,144 total) alongside text questions (clinical vignettes or multiple-choice prompts averaging 71 tokens) and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace (averaging 840 tokens) followed by a final answer. The training corpus spans 8.1M examples split roughly 45% vision-language and 55% text-only, covering imaging modalities including fundus, pathology, MRI, and radiology, as well as text-only USMLE-style reasoning questions.

## Method

OctoMed distills reasoning traces from two teacher models—DeepSeek-R1 for text-only data and GPT-4o for multimodal data—using rejection sampling that retains only traces whose final answer matches ground truth. The key data recipe contributions are: (1) mixing three knowledge-source categories (text-only, multimodal reasoning, multimodal classification) yields complementary gains without interference; (2) generating 16 rejection samples per question acts as regularization, raising peak MedQA accuracy by ~10% over a single trace; (3) CoT prompting is adopted over direct prompting for its broad applicability across reasoning and classification tasks. The student (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) is fully fine-tuned for 3 epochs on the resulting 8M-example, 6.8B-token dataset, and the mixture of varying trace lengths from both teachers induces emergent task-aware reasoning calibration, where the model self-adjusts reasoning depth to task difficulty without explicit supervision.

## Results

OctoMed (7B, SFT-only) achieves overall accuracy of 67.83 on text-only medical benchmarks, 50.36 on multimodal reasoning, and 67.29 on multimodal classification. It outperforms MedGemma-27B (4× larger) on all three categories (67.83 vs 66.56, 50.36 vs 44.25, 67.29 vs 50.97) and surpasses GPT-4o on multimodal classification (67.29 vs 53.96). On text-only tasks, OctoMed reaches 90.81 on MedQA (10-sample majority vote) and 89.56 on HeadQA, trailing only DeepSeek-R1 (93.16, 90.93). On multimodal reasoning, GPT-4o (58.07) still leads OctoMed (50.36), but OctoMed exceeds all open-source baselines by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OctoMed (7B, SFT-only) outperforms all open-source baselines across every benchmark category: it leads the next-best OSS model (GLM-4.1V, 9B SFT+RL) by 6.0 points on text-only (67.83 vs 61.84), 2.8 points on multimodal reasoning (50.36 vs 47.54), and 12.6 points on multimodal classification (67.29 vs 54.66). Against the 4×-larger MedGemma-27B, OctoMed wins on all three categories (67.83 vs 66.56, 50.36 vs 44.25, 67.29 vs 50.97). On multimodal classification, OctoMed also surpasses GPT-4o (67.29 vs 53.96), a model that served as its own multimodal teacher. The only baselines that beat OctoMed are the proprietary DeepSeek-R1 on text-only (76.05 vs 67.83 overall) and GPT-4o on multimodal reasoning (58.07 vs 50.36), both of which are substantially larger or use different training paradigms. Among same-size (7B) open-source models, OctoMed's advantage is most pronounced on multimodal classification, where it exceeds QoQ-Med (RL-only, 56.24) by 11.0 points and LingShu (SFT+RL, 54.66) by 12.6 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a data-centric niche in the medical multimodal reasoning literature, where prior work has focused on new architectures, RL objectives (MedVLThinker, LingShu, MedGemma), or multi-stage distillation pipelines (MedCoT, Chiron-O1). It repurposes the knowledge-distillation-from-teacher paradigm (DeepSeek-R1 for text, GPT-4o for multimodal) and rejection sampling, but shifts the research question from 'what training objective to use' to 'what data mixture, formatting, and scaling strategy to use during SFT.' Conceptually, it extends the data-recipe line of work from general vision-language reasoning (Honeybee, FineVision, OpenThoughts) into the medical domain, where modality diversity and safety-critical fidelity impose additional curation constraints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8M-example, 6.8B-token dataset and the systematic ablations (question sourcing, CoT vs. direct prompting, filtering, multiple traces, teacher choice) provide a practical blueprint for training medical VLMs via SFT alone, reducing dependence on expensive RL pipelines. The emergent task-aware reasoning—where the model self-calibrates trace length to task difficulty without explicit supervision—is a useful signal for future post-training and data-filtering pipelines. Open questions include whether these data-recipe findings hold at larger model scales, whether RL post-training on top of this SFT foundation yields further gains, and whether the Qwen2.5 data-leakage concerns fully invalidate some of the reported numbers.
