## Problem

Existing physics-based human-object interaction controllers either depend on explicit full-body reference planners that become brittle under dynamic instability, or rely on adversarial distributional matching that is difficult to scale. Distilling imitation experts into goal-conditioned policies captures broad skills but remains brittle in the vast configuration space of loco-manipulation, where even a few object degrees of freedom induce combinatorial explosion of contact modes. The paper addresses how to consolidate reconstructed latent skills into a robust, generalizable interaction manifold that handles unseen goals, failure recovery, and novel objects.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a human-object state vector (positions, orientations, velocities, signed distances to object surfaces, and binary contact flags) in a root-centric local heading frame, plus a set of masked residual goal encodings at multiple horizons (short-horizon preview offsets and a long-horizon snapshot), where the mask specifies which components—end-effector pose, object pose, contacts, or combinations—are provided. Output is a joint position target vector (51×3 for SMPL, 29 for G1) in exponential map form, converted to torques via PD control at 30 Hz. During inference, only the prior Transformer is active, sampling a bounded latent from recent history and sparse goals to produce multi-modal action distributions.

## Method

InterPrior follows a three-stage paradigm: (1) train an InterMimic+ expert via PPO with expanded reference scope (random pose initialization, sparse velocity impulses, object shape/physics augmentation) and a reference-free hand reward that guides grasping from current simulation state rather than rigid trajectory following; (2) distill the expert into a masked conditional variational policy using DAgger-style online distillation, where a Transformer prior encodes history and sparse goals, an MLP encoder sees full references, and a decoder maps the bounded latent (projected onto a hypersphere) to actions, optimized with ELBO plus latent-shaping and temporal-consistency losses; (3) RL finetune via in-betweening—tracking from randomized initial configurations toward single-frame goals drawn from the dataset—with failure-state resets to acquire recovery behaviors, prior preservation through a multi-objective schedule that concurrently optimizes the original distillation objective in a subset of environments, and optional learnable tokens for out-of-distribution skills like getting up.

## Results

On goal-conditioned tasks (Table 1), the full InterPrior achieves 90.0% success rate on snapshot goals, 94.6% on trajectory goals, 90.7% on contact goals, 68.8% on multi-goal chaining, and 88.6% on random initialization, substantially outperforming the MaskedMimic baseline (64.2%, 88.0%, 52.2%, 29.1%, 31.7% respectively). On full-reference tracking with thin objects and perturbations (Table 2), InterPrior reaches 83.2% success rate on OMOMO versus 63.9% for InterMimic. For zero-shot generalization to unseen datasets, InterPrior+finetuning achieves 52.0% SR on BEHAVE and 72.4% on HODome, compared to 10.7% and 27.8% for InterMimic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MaskedMimic [59] (adapted to the same task setup), InterPrior wins decisively on every task formulation: +25.8 pp on snapshot SR (90.0 vs 64.2), +6.6 pp on trajectory SR (94.6 vs 88.0), +38.5 pp on contact SR (90.7 vs 52.2), +39.7 pp on multi-goal chaining SR (68.8 vs 29.1), and +56.9 pp on random initialization SR (88.6 vs 31.7). The largest gains appear on long-horizon and out-of-distribution settings, where the RL finetuning step is most impactful. Against InterMimic [88] on full-reference tracking with thin objects and initialization noise, InterPrior improves SR from 63.9% to 83.2% on OMOMO, though at a slight cost in human position error (8.9 vs 7.1 cm) due to intentional deviations for contact re-alignment. On novel-dataset generalization (BEHAVE, HODome), InterPrior+finetuning substantially outperforms InterMimic (52.0% vs 10.7% and 72.4% vs 27.8% SR), demonstrating that the variational prior transfers more effectively to unseen objects and interaction styles than the full-reference imitator.

## Relation to Prior Work

InterPrior sits at the intersection of two lines of physics-based character control: adversarial imitation (AMP, ASE) that learns generative motor priors but struggles to scale, and distillation-based approaches (MaskedMimic, ControlVAE) that absorb large-scale data but remain brittle outside the demonstration distribution. The paper builds directly on InterMimic for full-reference HOI tracking and on MaskedMimic/Dexplore for the variational distillation architecture, adding two key departures: (1) an improved imitation expert (InterMimic+) with reference-free hand rewards and physical perturbations to reduce over-reliance on references, and (2) an RL post-training stage that uses in-betweening (single-frame goals from randomized initializations) to consolidate latent skills into a robust interaction manifold. The DAgger-style online distillation framework provides the training mechanism, but the critical conceptual contribution is positioning RL not as a standalone trainer but as a local optimizer anchored to a pretrained prior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, three-stage recipe for scaling physics-based HOI control to diverse objects and interaction styles, with demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen objects (BEHAVE, HODome) and embodiment transfer (SMPL → Unitree G1). The finding that a relatively simple RL finetuning step—combined with latent bounding—can dramatically improve robustness on stress tests (random initialization success jumps from ~32% to ~89%) while preserving in-distribution precision offers a reusable paradigm for other loco-manipulation settings. Open questions include whether the approach extends to soft-body interactions, whether the in-betweening formulation scales to even larger configuration spaces (multi-object, deformable), and how well the sim-trained prior transfers to real-world deployment with perception and language conditioning. The paper acknowledges remaining failure modes on extremely thin objects and multi-goal canonicalization errors, suggesting the configuration space is still not fully covered.
