## Problem

Applying reinforcement learning policy optimization to masked generative models (MGMs) is challenging because the iterative non-autoregressive sampling process requires step-wise probability estimation, making full-trajectory optimization computationally prohibitive. Existing approaches either optimize over all steps (high cost) or use static step selection based on mask ratio, which underexplores the varying contributions of individual steps to the final image and yields suboptimal results.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image via the Meissonic masked generative transformer. During RL training, each prompt generates a group of G=8 candidate images, and the model operates on VQ-VAE-quantized discrete visual tokens over 64 iterative unmasking steps. The reward signal comes from CLIP/GenEval scores for compositional tasks or HPS for preference alignment, with classifier-free guidance set to 5.

## Method

MaskFocus introduces two core mechanisms for efficient RL post-training of MGMs. First, Critical Step Selection (CSS) computes the cosine similarity between VQ-VAE image embeddings at each intermediate sampling step and the final generated image, then selects the top-K=6 steps with the highest information gain (absolute similarity difference between consecutive steps) for policy optimization, reducing the optimization budget from 64 to 6 steps. Second, Dynamic Routing Sampling (DR-Sampling) splits each group by entropy: high-entropy samples follow standard confidence-based sampling (exploitation), while low-entropy samples receive entropy-based dynamic temperature modulation to encourage exploration of alternative unmasking strategies. The RL objective uses GRPO with a clipped surrogate and KL penalty, where probability estimation at each critical step incorporates all masked tokens rather than only the selected subset.

## Results

On the GenEval benchmark, MaskFocus applied to Meissonic achieves an overall score of 0.76, surpassing MaskGRPO (0.73) and Mask-GRPO (0.73), and outperforming strong diffusion baselines like FLUX.1-dev (0.66) and DALL-E 3 (0.67). On human preference metrics (DrawBench), MaskFocus reaches HPS 35.52, DEQA 4.39, PickScore 22.39, and ImageReward 1.09, exceeding FLUX.1-dev (HPS 31.13) and SD3.5-M (HPS 30.17). The method also shows notable gains on compositional sub-tasks: Counting (0.85 vs. 0.83 for MaskGRPO) and Position (0.42 vs. 0.39).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskFocus (0.76 GenEval Overall) outperforms the closest masked-generative RL baselines MaskGRPO (0.73) and Mask-GRPO (0.73) by 0.03, with the largest margin on Two Obj. (0.91 vs. 0.90) and Position (0.42 vs. 0.39). Against the strongest diffusion baselines, MaskFocus surpasses FLUX.1-dev (0.66) and DALL-E 3 (0.67) by 0.09–0.10 on GenEval Overall, and exceeds FLUX.1-dev on HPS (35.52 vs. 31.13) and DEQA (4.39 vs. 4.37). On human preference metrics, MaskFocus narrowly edges out MaskGRPO (HPS 35.52 vs. 35.48; ImageReward 1.09 vs. 1.06) while both substantially improve over the untrained Meissonic base (HPS 28.89). The method also outperforms AR-based models like Janus-Pro-1B (0.73) and Emu3 (0.66) on GenEval, and beats Show-o (0.68) and Show-o+PARM (0.69) on the masked-generative track. The only metric where a non-MGM baseline leads is Position, where Janus-Pro-1B (0.65) and DALL-E 3 (0.43) exceed MaskFocus (0.42), though the gap is small.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL post-training paradigm for visual generation—previously applied to autoregressive models (T2I-R1, ReasonGen-R1) and diffusion/flow-matching models (FlowGRPO, DanceGRPO)—to masked generative models (MGMs). It builds directly on Mask-GRPO, which first formulated iterative unmasking as a multi-step MDP amenable to GRPO, and on MaskGRPO, which introduced static step selection by mask ratio. The key departure is replacing full-trajectory or static step selection with a dynamic, similarity-based critical-step selection (CSS) and an entropy-routed sampling strategy (DR-Sampling) borrowed from entropy-aware AR generation [30], thereby reducing computational cost while targeting the steps that most influence final image quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying that only a small subset of sampling steps (K=6 out of 64) carries most of the information gain, MaskFocus makes RL post-training for MGMs substantially more computationally tractable without sacrificing performance, which is important as MGMs gain traction as a faster alternative to diffusion. However, the method is validated only on Meissonic at 1024×1024, and the authors acknowledge that the off-policy masking strategy (re-masking with generated masks vs. regenerating masks) introduces probability estimation errors that remain unresolved. Open questions include whether the critical-step criterion generalizes across different MGM architectures and resolutions, and whether the entropy-based routing can be made adaptive per-prompt rather than per-group.
