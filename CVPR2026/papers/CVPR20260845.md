## Problem

Existing detectors for fully generated videos are either image-based (scoring frames independently and missing temporal artifacts) or supervised (requiring labeled synthetic data and generalizing poorly to unseen generators). The first zero-shot video detector, D3, relies solely on second-order temporal differences without per-frame spatial analysis and lacks principled theoretical foundations. A mathematically grounded, training-free detector that jointly models spatial realism and temporal motion consistency in a unified probabilistic framework was missing.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a video clip standardized to 8 or 16 frames at 8 FPS (2-second duration) and encodes each frame with DINOv3 into a d-dimensional embedding. A pre-computed calibration set of ~33k real videos (from VATEX, disjoint from all evaluation data) provides fixed whitening matrices and percentile reference distributions; no generated samples are ever used. The output is a single scalar score in [0,1] representing the percentile-normalized average of spatial and temporal likelihoods, where higher values indicate greater consistency with real-video statistics.

## Method

STALL computes two closed-form Gaussian log-likelihoods: a spatial score over per-frame DINOv3 embeddings (whitened via PCA-whitening from the calibration set, then scored by their squared norm under an isotropic Gaussian) and a temporal score over inter-frame embedding differences. The key insight for the temporal branch is that raw transition vectors are non-Gaussian, but normalizing them to the unit sphere induces approximate Gaussianity per the Maxwell-Poincaré lemma, enabling the same whitening-and-likelihood procedure. Spatial likelihoods are aggregated by maximum (most realistic frame) and temporal likelihoods by minimum (most unnatural transition), chosen to minimize cross-domain correlation. Both scores are converted to percentiles against the calibration distributions and averaged into a final video-level score, making the detector scale-free, training-free, and robust to OOD values.

## Results

STALL achieves an average AUC of 0.83 (AP 0.85) on VideoFeedback, 0.80 (AP 0.80) on GenVideo, and 0.85 (AP 0.86) on the newly introduced ComGenVid benchmark, yielding an overall average AUC of 0.82 across all three benchmarks. It is the only method that maintains AUC > 0.5 for every individual generator, whereas all baselines exhibit inverted decision boundaries (AUC < 0.5) on at least one model. Inference latency is 0.49 s per 16-frame video, placing STALL among the fastest methods tested (comparable to D3 at 0.5 s and RIGID at 0.6 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STALL outperforms all six baselines on average across every benchmark. On VideoFeedback, STALL (0.83 AUC) surpasses the best image-based baseline RIGID (0.63) by 20 points and the best video-based baseline D3-cosine (0.55) by 28 points. On GenVideo, the gap narrows somewhat: STALL (0.80) beats D3-L2 (0.72) by 8 points, while on ComGenVid the advantage widens again to 12 points over D3-L2 (0.73). A critical distinction is consistency: STALL is the only method that never drops below AUC 0.5 for any individual generator, whereas D3, ZED, and AEROBLADE all exhibit inverted decision boundaries (AUC < 0.5) on multiple models. STALL also outperforms the two supervised video detectors (T2VE and AIGVdet) shown in Figure 6b, despite being entirely training-free. In terms of efficiency, STALL (0.49 s/video) is on par with D3 (0.5 s) and RIGID (0.6 s), and roughly 2× faster than ZED (0.92 s) and T2VE (0.97 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

STALL extends the zero-shot image-detection line of work based on whitened-embedding Gaussian likelihoods (Betser et al.) from the spatial (per-frame) domain to the temporal (inter-frame transition) domain, and unifies both in a single probabilistic score. It departs from D3, the first zero-shot video detector, which relies solely on second-order temporal differences without spatial content or a formal likelihood model, and from supervised video detectors that require labeled synthetic data and generalize poorly to unseen generators. The theoretical grounding draws on the Maxwell-Poincaré lemma to justify Gaussian approximations of normalized high-dimensional transition vectors, providing a principled basis absent in prior empirical approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As frontier generators like Sora and Veo3 produce increasingly realistic videos, a training-free, model-agnostic detector that requires no synthetic training data is practically valuable for deployment in screening pipelines where new models appear faster than retraining cycles. The release of ComGenVid, a benchmark targeting these latest commercial models, fills a gap in evaluation infrastructure. Open questions include whether the Gaussian-likelihood assumption holds for future encoders or more diverse real-video distributions, how the method scales to very long videos or variable frame rates beyond the tested ranges, and whether the calibration set requirement (33k real videos) can be further reduced without performance loss.
