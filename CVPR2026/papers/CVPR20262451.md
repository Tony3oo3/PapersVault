## Problem

Existing VLM-based HOI detection methods apply scene-level contextual cues uniformly across all instances, failing to capture the nuanced, instance-specific semantic information needed for accurate interaction reasoning. The diverse contextual cues—visual cues within the target instance, relationships with surrounding instances, and broader scene context—are not effectively mined and integrated at the instance level, limiting the model's ability to interpret complex human-object relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262451/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and processes it through two parallel frozen backbones: a DETR detector with ResNet-50 for instance detection and a CLIP visual encoder (ViT-B or ViT-L) for semantic features. It outputs human-object pair bounding boxes with interaction class labels, evaluated under standard IoU ≥ 0.5 matching. Both backbones remain frozen during training; only the context mining and interaction reasoning modules are learned, with 3 context-mining layers (L=3) and 2 interaction decoder layers.

## Method

InCoM-Net introduces Instance-centric Context Mining, comprising two submodules. The Instance-centric Context Refinement (ICR) module applies masked self-attention to CLIP features using scene-adaptive binary masks derived from the detector: an instance mask isolates intra-instance context, a surrounding mask (union of all other instance masks) captures inter-instance context, and an all-ones mask yields global context, each passed through an FFN. The Progressive Context Aggregation (ProCA) module then iteratively fuses these three context streams with detector query features via separate cross-attention heads over L layers, progressively enriching each instance representation. A Masked Feature Training (MFT) strategy randomly drops either VLM or detector features during training (full, detector-only, or VLM-only configurations), preventing the model from over-relying on one source and encouraging balanced exploitation of both heterogeneous feature streams.

## Results

On HICO-DET with ViT-L, InCoM-Net achieves 43.96 mAP (Full), 45.61 mAP (Rare), and 43.46 mAP (Non-rare), surpassing the previous best NMSR by 1.03 mAP on Full. With ViT-B, it reaches 39.53 mAP (Full), outperforming HORP by 0.92 mAP. On V-COCO with ViT-L, InCoM-Net attains 73.6 AP_role^S1 and 75.4 AP_role^S2, exceeding NMSR by 3.8 on S1 and SPLHOI by 2.5 on S2. In zero-shot HICO-DET (ViT-L), it achieves 37.69 mAP on RF-UC Unseen and 39.45 mAP on NF-UC Unseen, both state-of-the-art.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InCoM-Net achieves state-of-the-art results across all evaluated settings, winning every head-to-head comparison. On HICO-DET with ViT-B, it surpasses the strongest prior method HORP [7] by 0.92 mAP (39.53 vs. 38.61 Full) and leads on Rare categories by 2.73 mAP (38.87 vs. 36.14). With ViT-L, it outperforms NMSR [36] by 1.03 mAP on Full (43.96 vs. 42.93) and by 3.20 mAP on Rare (45.61 vs. 42.41). On V-COCO, the gains are even more pronounced: with ViT-B, InCoM-Net beats SCTC [14] by 5.1 AP_role^S1 (72.2 vs. 67.1) and 2.5 AP_role^S2 (74.2 vs. 71.7); with ViT-L, it exceeds NMSR [36] by 3.8 on S1 (73.6 vs. 69.8) and SPLHOI [28] by 2.5 on S2 (75.4 vs. 72.9). In zero-shot HICO-DET (ViT-L), InCoM-Net leads VDRP [37] by 0.97 mAP on RF-UC Unseen (37.69 vs. 36.72) and LAIN [18] by 1.93 mAP on NF-UC Unseen (39.45 vs. 37.52), confirming strong generalization to unseen interaction categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VLM-integrated HOI detection line (HOICLIP, UniHOI, CLIP4HOI, BCOM) by departing from both scene-level context injection and RoI-based instance adaptation. Its key conceptual departure is the instance-centric multi-context decomposition—using scene-adaptive masks to separately extract intra-instance, inter-instance, and global features from VLM representations—rather than applying a single uniform context or cropping to bounding boxes. The progressive cross-attention aggregation (ProCA) and masked feature training (MFT) are novel architectural choices that address the underexplored problem of balancing heterogeneous feature sources (VLM semantics vs. detector spatial features) during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The instance-centric context mining framework provides a principled, reusable recipe for extracting multi-level semantic context from frozen VLMs, which could transfer to other detection or scene-understanding tasks beyond HOI. The MFT finding—that models trained on both VLM and detector features develop a strong bias toward VLM features, causing a ~19 mAP collapse when detector features are removed—highlights a practical training instability that other dual-branch VLM methods likely share but have not explicitly addressed. Open questions include whether the three-way context decomposition is strictly necessary (vs. a single learned attention over the full VLM feature map), how the approach scales to denser scenes with many instances, and whether the frozen-CLIP assumption limits ceiling performance.
