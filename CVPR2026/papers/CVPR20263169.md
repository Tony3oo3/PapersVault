## Problem

Current concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models operate under a text-centric paradigm, equating erasure with severing the text-to-image mapping while leaving the underlying visual knowledge intact in model parameters. All existing adversarial attacks similarly remain tethered to the textual conditioning pathway, creating a co-evolutionary arms race that gives a false sense of security. This paper asks whether a deterministic, text-free generative trajectory for an erased concept still exists within the sanitized model.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image of the erased concept (VAE-encoded to latent z_0), the sanitized diffusion model ε_θ, and a null-text condition c_null as inputs. It produces an optimized initial noise vector z_T* in the latent space, which when fed into the same erased model via deterministic DDIM sampling (T=50 steps) under the null-text condition, regenerates the forbidden concept. Target images are generated from the original pre-erasure Stable Diffusion v1.4 checkpoint using prompts from standard benchmarks (I2P for nudity, ESD setup for Van Gogh style, GPT-4-generated prompts for the tench object class).

## Method

TINA reframes DDIM inversion as an optimization-based fixed-point problem to overcome the accumulating approximation errors that plague standard inversion under null-text conditions. At each timestep t, instead of accepting the approximate z_t from the standard formula (which uses ε_θ(z_{t-1}, t-1, c) to estimate the next latent), TINA minimizes the self-consistency loss L_t = ||C_1(t)·z_{t-1} + C_2(t)·ε_θ(z_t, t, c_null) − z_t||² via K=25 gradient descent iterations (AdamW, lr=0.001) on z_t, enforcing that each latent satisfies the exact DDIM sampling relation. This inner optimization loop is applied sequentially for t=1,…,T, tracing a highly accurate path from z_0 back to the true initial noise z_T* that is completely decoupled from any textual influence. The resulting z_T* then serves as the starting point for a standard deterministic DDIM sampling pass under c_null, regenerating the erased concept without any textual guidance.

## Results

TINA achieves the highest Attack Success Rate (ASR) across all eight unlearning defenses on the nudity erasure task, reaching 92.96% against MACE and 82.39% against ESD. Most critically, against adversarially robust defenses (AdvUnlearn, SalUn, STEREO), TINA maintains ASRs of 78.87%, 71.13%, and 80.99% respectively, while the best text-centric baselines drop to 23–25%. On style erasure, TINA scores 70–80% ASR across all methods, and on object erasure it reaches 68–78%, where text-based attacks collapse to single digits against modern defenses like Scissorhands and STEREO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TINA outperforms all five text-centric baselines (MMA, P4D, UDA, RAB, CCE) on the nudity erasure task across all eight defenses, with the largest margins against adversarially robust methods: TINA scores 80.99% on STEREO vs. 25.35% (UDA, the best baseline), 71.13% on SalUn vs. 15.49% (P4D), and 78.87% on AdvUnlearn vs. 76.76% (CCE). On style erasure, TINA leads on 7 of 8 defenses (e.g., 70% vs. 32% UDA on ESD; 70% vs. 2% UDA on AdvUnlearn), though UDA slightly edges TINA on SPM (88% vs. 80%). On object erasure, the gap is most dramatic: against Scissorhands and STEREO, all text-based attacks score 0–6% while TINA achieves 78% and 72% respectively. CCE (embedding-space attack) is the strongest text-centric baseline on robust defenses but still trails TINA by 2–14 points on nudity and by 26–34 points on object erasure. MMA and RAB (prompt-level attacks) are consistently the weakest baselines, scoring below 25% on most robust defenses.

## Relation to Prior Work

TINA builds on DDIM inversion (Song et al., 2020) but fundamentally repurposes it: rather than using text-guided inversion for image editing, it operates under a null-text condition and replaces the standard approximative closed-form update with a per-step fixed-point optimization to correct compounding errors. Conceptually, it departs from the entire lineage of adversarial attacks on concept erasure (MMA, P4D, UDA, RAB, CCE), all of which presuppose access to the text-conditioning pathway, by identifying a completely text-free attack surface. It also positions itself against the dominant erasure paradigm (ESD, AC, FMN, UCE, AdvUnlearn, STEREO), arguing that these methods sever text-image mappings without removing the underlying visual knowledge from the parameter space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a foundational assumption in the AI safety community: that severing text-to-image links constitutes meaningful erasure. The consistent high ASR (71–93%) against even adversarially robust defenses like AdvUnlearn and STEREO, where text-based attacks drop to 0–25%, suggests that current SOTA unlearning provides a false sense of security. Open questions include whether true visual-knowledge erasure is achievable in diffusion models, whether the attack generalizes beyond Stable Diffusion v1.4 (the only architecture tested), and whether defenses can be redesigned to operate on internal visual representations rather than text-image mappings. The white-box requirement and need for a target image limit immediate practical exploitability but do not diminish the conceptual threat.
