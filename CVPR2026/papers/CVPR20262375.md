## Problem

Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving rely on massive datasets with dense Chain-of-Thought reasoning annotations, incurring prohibitive data, annotation, and inference costs. When reasoning and data are removed, standard GRPO post-training yields negligible improvement (+0.67%) on the weaker SFT policy, creating the false impression that reasoning is necessary. The paper identifies this failure as an instance of difficulty bias in GRPO and asks whether a data-efficient, reasoning-free VLA can be trained to competitive performance by fixing the optimization rather than the policy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262375/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

NORD consumes RGB images from three cameras (front, front-left, front-right), the past ego-trajectory, current speed, and acceleration, and directly outputs future ego-trajectory tokens at 10 Hz without any intermediate reasoning text. Trajectories are discretized via k-disc tokenization: 0.5-second segments are clustered into a 2048-entry codebook by contour distance, and the resulting tokens are appended to the Qwen vocabulary, keeping the output sequence short and eliminating the token overhead of CoT generation.

## Method

NORD is built on Qwen-2.5VL-3B-Instruct and trained in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on a reduced dataset (80K NAVSIM / 12K WaymoE2E samples, no reasoning annotations) followed by RL post-training. The key technical contribution is replacing standard GRPO with Dr. GRPO, which removes the within-group standard deviation from the group-relative advantage (A = r_i − mean(r)), thereby eliminating the difficulty bias that causes GRPO to ignore high-variance, intermediate-difficulty scenarios where a weak SFT policy most needs learning signal. The RL objective further employs DAPO-style asymmetric clipping to prevent entropy collapse and omits KL-divergence regularization. This drop-in algorithmic change yields an 11.68% PDM improvement over the SFT base (vs. 0.67% with GRPO), enabling NORD to reach competitive scores on NAVSIM and WaymoE2E with ≥60% less data and no reasoning tokens.

## Results

On NAVSIM (navtest), NORD achieves a PDM Score of 85.6 using only 3 RGB camera frames, no LiDAR, and no reasoning annotations, with fewer than 90,000 training samples. In the best-of-6 (BoN) setting, NORD-BoN reaches 92.4 PDMS, surpassing the reasoning-based AutoVLA-BoN (92.1). On WaymoE2E, NORD attains an RFS of 7.709 (3rd among all VLAs) and the best ADE@3 of 1.2504, using only 12,000 SFT samples and 450 RLFT samples—over 6× less data than competing methods. Dr. GRPO post-training yields an 11.68% PDMS improvement over NORD-BASE, compared to just 0.67% with standard GRPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NAVSIM, NORD (85.6 PDMS) trails the strongest reasoning-based VLAs—AutoVLA (89.1) and RecogDrive (89.6)—by 3.5 and 4.0 points respectively, but outperforms BEV-based methods UniAD (83.4) and Transfuser (84.0) while using only 3 RGB frames and no LiDAR. In the BoN setting, NORD-BoN (92.4) edges out AutoVLA-BoN (92.1) by 0.3 points, demonstrating that the data-efficient, reasoning-free policy can match or exceed reasoning-based models when given multiple sampling opportunities. On WaymoE2E, NORD (RFS 7.709) beats AutoVLA (7.556) and UniPlan (7.692) but falls short of Poutine (7.986) and HMVLM (7.736), which both use reasoning and/or ensembling. However, NORD achieves the best ADE@3 (1.2504) among all compared methods, indicating superior trajectory accuracy despite using ≥6× less training data. The critical distinction is that NORD is the only top-performing model that requires no reasoning traces, no ensembling, and no LiDAR, operating in the high-efficiency regime on both Pareto fronts.

## Relation to Prior Work

NORD sits at the intersection of two VLA lines: reasoning-centric models (AutoVLA, ORION) that achieve SOTA on challenging benchmarks but require massive annotated datasets and CoT tokens, and reasoning-free models (EMMA, SimLingo) that are efficient but have not been competitive on long-tail benchmarks. The paper's key methodological departure is repurposing Dr. GRPO (Liu et al., COLM 2025), originally designed to mitigate difficulty bias in LLM mathematical reasoning, as a drop-in RL post-training algorithm for driving policies. It also aligns with the 'Reasoning-Planning Decoupling Hypothesis' literature questioning whether explicit textual reasoning causally improves planning, providing an empirical test in the driving domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: competitive driving VLA performance can be achieved with 60%+ less data, zero reasoning annotations, and 3× fewer tokens, reducing both training cost and inference latency for real-world deployment. The identification of difficulty bias as the root cause of GRPO's failure on weak SFT policies provides a diagnostic tool applicable beyond driving to any RL post-training setting with sparse, multi-metric rewards. However, NORD's absolute scores (85.6 PDMS on NAVSIM, 7.709 RFS on WaymoE2E) remain below reasoning-based SOTA (89.1–92.1, 7.986), and the authors acknowledge Dr. GRPO 'remains imperfect,' leaving open whether further algorithmic refinements or hybrid approaches can close the remaining gap without reintroducing reasoning overhead.
