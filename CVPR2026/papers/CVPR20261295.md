## Problem

Existing text-to-video diffusion models generate only short clips offline, making them unsuitable for interactive and real-time applications. Training-free streaming approaches like FIFO-Diffusion lack a training solution and sampling distillation, while autoregressive methods sacrifice quality by replacing bidirectional attention with causal masking. The paper addresses the gap of a trainable, high-quality streaming video generation pipeline that also achieves real-time inference speed.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (or an input video plus a new prompt for video-to-video editing) and produces a continuous stream of video frames at 512p resolution. Internally, a buffer of 16 latent frames (64×64 spatial, 8 channels) slides along the temporal axis: fully denoised frames are popped out as output while new noise frames are pushed in, yielding 2 latent frames (8 video frames) per denoising step. After distillation, the pipeline runs 8 sampling steps without CFG per chunk, reaching 16 FPS on a single H100 GPU.

## Method

StreamDiT modifies flow matching by introducing a moving buffer where frames carry different noise levels, governed by a unified partitioning scheme with N chunks of c frames and s micro denoising steps per chunk; mixed training across multiple partitioning schemes (spanning uniform noise to diagonal noise) improves consistency and prevents overfitting. The architecture is a 4B-parameter adaLN DiT with a key modification: the scalar time embedding is replaced by a per-frame varying time embedding (sequence τ) applied to scale-and-shift modulations, and full self-attention is replaced by shifted window attention for efficiency. For real-time inference, a multistep distillation splits the flow-matching trajectory into N segments and performs simultaneous step and guidance distillation within each segment, collapsing the micro step s to 1 so that the total NFE equals the number of chunks (8) with no classifier-free guidance.

## Results

On the Movie Gen evaluation dataset (50 prompts, 8-second 512p videos), StreamDiT achieves the highest VBench Quality Score of 0.8185, outperforming ReuseDiffuse (0.8019) and FIFO-Diffusion (0.7981). The distilled model (Ours-distill) retains a Quality Score of 0.8163 while achieving the best Dynamic Degree of 0.7040, compared to 0.5240 for the teacher and 0.2900/0.3094 for baselines. In human evaluation, StreamDiT shows higher win rates across all four axes (overall quality, frame consistency, motion completeness, motion naturalness) compared to both baselines. The distilled model reaches real-time 16 FPS on a single H100 GPU with 8 sampling steps and no CFG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StreamDiT outperforms both ReuseDiffuse and FIFO-Diffusion on overall Quality Score (0.8185 vs. 0.8019 and 0.7981) and by a large margin on Dynamic Degree (0.5240 vs. 0.2900 and 0.3094), indicating substantially more motion and less static content. However, both baselines achieve higher Temporal Flickering scores (0.9838/0.9796 vs. 0.9671) and Motion Smoothness (0.9912/0.9889 vs. 0.9861), which the authors attribute to the baselines generating more static, less dynamic videos. The distilled model (Ours-distill) sacrifices a small amount of Quality Score (0.8163 vs. 0.8185) but achieves the highest Dynamic Degree of 0.7040, nearly doubling the motion content of the teacher while enabling real-time 16 FPS inference. In human evaluation, StreamDiT wins across all four axes (overall quality, frame consistency, motion completeness, motion naturalness) against both baselines, confirming the quantitative findings.

## Relation to Prior Work

StreamDiT extends Flow Matching (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) by introducing a moving buffer with per-frame noise levels, unifying previously separate partitioning schemes (uniform noise for bidirectional T2V, diagonal noise for FIFO-Diffusion) under a single chunked-denoising framework. Architecturally, it modifies the adaLN DiT (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) with frame-varying time embeddings and Swin-style window attention, and is initialized from the MovieGen 4B T2V model (Polyak et al., 2024). Unlike training-free streaming methods (FIFO-Diffusion, StreamDiffusion) that sacrifice quality, or autoregressive video models that sacrifice bidirectional attention, StreamDiT provides a fully trainable pipeline that preserves bidirectional attention within the buffer while enabling sequential frame output. The multistep distillation adapts guided distillation (Meng et al., CVPR 2023) to the streaming setting by distilling micro-steps within each chunk segment independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 16 FPS real-time streaming video generation at 512p on a single H100 GPU is a practical milestone that opens the door to interactive video applications (game engines, live video-to-video editing, infinite streaming) that were previously infeasible. The unified partitioning scheme and mixed training strategy offer a principled way to balance temporal consistency against streaming latency, and the approach generalizes to larger models (30B) with improved quality. Key limitations remain: the 4B model exhibits artifacts, and the short context window causes objects to change appearance when they re-enter the frame, pointing to the need for extended context with KV caching. An open question is whether the distillation recipe and partitioning design transfer cleanly to substantially larger or higher-resolution models without re-tuning.
