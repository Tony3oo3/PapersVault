## Problem

Pre-trained video DiT models are trained on short clips, and directly extending them to longer sequences causes severe quality degradation due to two out-of-distribution issues: frame-level relative position O.O.D (RoPE extrapolation beyond the trained range) and context-length O.O.D (attention diffusion over expanded token sequences). Existing training-free methods either rely on sliding windows that break long-range coherence or apply uniform positional/attention modifications across all layers, ignoring the heterogeneous sensitivities of individual transformer layers.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and an off-the-shelf pre-trained video DiT model (Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B or HunyuanVideo) and produces a longer video at 480p (832×480) with 2× or 4× temporal extension (e.g., 161 or 321 frames from an 81-frame base). No additional training, fine-tuning, or auxiliary data is required; all corrections are applied at inference time by modifying RoPE encodings and attention masks within the existing model weights.

## Method

FreeLOC is a training-free, layer-adaptive framework built on two core mechanisms. First, Video-based Relative Position Re-encoding (VRPR) remaps out-of-range frame-level relative positions back into the pre-trained RoPE domain using a three-level multi-granularity scheme: fine-grained (preserving exact positions within a local window), medium-grained (quantizing mid-range positions with group size G₁), and coarse-grained (aggressively compressing distant positions with group size G₂), motivated by the empirical observation that attention magnitude decays with temporal distance. Second, Tiered Sparse Attention (TSA) constructs a 4D attention mask that applies dense local window attention for nearby frames, striped spatially-aligned attention for mid-range frames (shrinking the effective token count by a factor α while expanding temporal receptive field), and an attention sink on the first frame for long-range global coherence. Crucially, an offline layer-wise probing procedure quantifies each self-attention layer's sensitivity to the two O.O.D sources (via Vision Reward degradation and Attention Logits Difference for positional O.O.D, and attention entropy change for context-length O.O.D), enabling FreeLOC to selectively apply VRPR alone or VRPR+TSA to the most responsive layers rather than uniformly across the network.

## Results

On Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B at 4× extension (321 frames), FreeLOC achieves the best scores across all six VBench metrics: Subject Consistency 98.44, Background Consistency 97.78, Motion Smoothness 98.97, Imaging Quality 67.44, Aesthetic Quality 61.21, and Dynamic Degree 36.27. The most dramatic gains appear in Imaging Quality (+8.2 over Direct Sampling) and Dynamic Degree (+32.0 over Direct Sampling), indicating that naive extrapolation severely collapses motion and visual detail. On HunyuanVideo at 4× (509 frames), FreeLOC similarly leads with IQ 67.92, AQ 61.09, and DD 39.28, outperforming the next-best method (FreeNoise) by 4.0 in IQ and 5.1 in AQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeLOC outperforms all three training-free baselines on both models at 4× extension. Against RIFLEx [39], the gap is most severe in Dynamic Degree (36.27 vs 4.45 on Wan; 39.28 vs 8.45 on HunyuanVideo) and Imaging Quality (67.44 vs 59.92; 67.92 vs 56.56), confirming that RIFLEx's uniform positional modification fails to preserve motion and detail at longer lengths. Against FreeLong [22], FreeLOC leads by 4.3–5.1 in IQ and 6.5–6.7 in AQ, with a 15–18 point advantage in DD, showing that spectral-blend attention alone is insufficient for long-range coherence. Against FreeNoise [25], the closest competitor, FreeLOC gains 1.1–4.0 in IQ and 5.1–5.2 in AQ, while matching or slightly exceeding FreeNoise in DD (36.27 vs 35.11 on Wan; 39.28 vs 35.11 on HunyuanVideo). Notably, Sliding Window achieves competitive DD but sacrifices Subject and Background Consistency (e.g., 96.15 vs 98.44 SC on Wan 4×), illustrating the local-detail-vs-global-coherence trade-off that FreeLOC's tiered design resolves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free long video generation line by reframing quality degradation as two distinct out-of-distribution problems (frame-level relative position O.O.D and context-length O.O.D) rather than a monolithic extrapolation failure. It repurposes RoPE extrapolation ideas from LLMs (clipping, grouping, LM-Infinite) into a multi-granularity video-specific scheme (VRPR) that respects the hierarchical temporal structure of video, and introduces a hierarchical sparse attention mechanism (TSA) that goes beyond fixed sliding windows. Crucially, it departs from prior methods (RIFLEx, LongDiff, FreeLong) by introducing a layer-adaptive probing mechanism that selectively applies corrections, and by targeting DiT-based architectures (Wan, HunyuanVideo) where most prior training-free methods were built on UNet backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The layer-adaptive insight—that different transformer layers exhibit markedly different sensitivities to positional vs. contextual O.O.D, and that uniform application is suboptimal—provides a principled framework for future inference-time adaptations of pre-trained video DiTs. The method's practical value is high since it requires no retraining and plugs into SOTA models, but it is currently validated only up to 4× extension on two models (Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B and HunyuanVideo), leaving open whether the probing-based layer assignment generalizes to larger models, longer extensions (8×+), or other DiT variants. The offline probing cost, while modest, adds a per-model calibration step that limits immediate drop-in deployment.
