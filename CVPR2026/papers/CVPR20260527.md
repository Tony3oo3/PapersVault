## Problem

Existing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) provide overly positive aesthetic feedback, failing to identify compositional flaws or offer actionable shooting guidance during photo capture. Without this aesthetic guidance (AG) capability, they also cannot effectively identify distracting regions or optimize compositional balance for aesthetic cropping after capture. The paper formalizes AG as an underexplored task and shows that no dedicated dataset, benchmark, or method exists to address it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260527/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB photo and a text instruction as input. In Stage 1, it produces a structured two-part text response: an aesthetic analysis (interpreting the photo from an aesthetic perspective) followed by aesthetic guidance (identifying specific issues and suggesting actionable shooting adjustments). In Stage 2, it outputs a cropping box (spatial coordinates) jointly with a chain-of-thought aesthetic rationale explaining the compositional logic behind the crop. Evaluation of Stage 1 outputs uses GPT-assisted scoring on Completeness, Preciseness, and Relevance (each 0–2), validated against expert assessments; Stage 2 is evaluated by IoU, boundary displacement, and recall of high-quality crops (IoU ≥ 75%) on the FLMS benchmark.

## Method

Venus is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (AG capability building) applies supervised instruction fine-tuning on the newly introduced AesGuide dataset (10,748 photos with expert-annotated scores, analyses, and guidance), using progressively complex aesthetic questions that evolve from overall impressions to detailed analyses to actionable refinements, mirroring human aesthetic reasoning; the visual encoder and modality connector are frozen while only the LLM is updated via LoRA. Stage 2 (cropping power activation) performs full-parameter fine-tuning on public cropping datasets (CPC, GAIC, FCDB) where the model jointly predicts a cropping box and a CoT-based aesthetic rationale; both 'good' and 'bad' crops are included with rationales generated by GPT-4o and validated by Qwen2.5-VL-72B to ensure alignment with visual content. The key insight is that embedding AG capability within the MLLM is a prerequisite for effective aesthetic cropping, as ablations show significant performance drops when Stage 1 is removed.

## Results

On the FLMS aesthetic cropping benchmark, Venus-Q achieves 87.01% IoU, 0.0292 boundary displacement, and 92.0% recall (IoU ≥ 75%), surpassing the previous SOTA specialized model SAC-Net (85.51% IoU) by +1.50 IoU and outperforming GPT-4o (71.61% IoU) by +15.4 IoU. On the newly introduced AesGuide AG benchmark, the best Venus variant (Venus-L-13B) attains a mean GPT-assisted score of 1.49/2.0 and an expert score of 1.53/2.0, ranking first among all 15 evaluated models, while GPT-4o scores only 0.98/2.0 (mean) and 1.15/2.0 (expert). Venus-Q also uniquely provides interpretability and interactivity (natural-language rationales and user-feedback adaptation), which no specialized cropping model supports.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Venus-Q decisively outperforms all 15 baselines on FLMS. Against the strongest specialized cropping model SAC-Net (85.51% IoU, 0.0333 Disp), Venus-Q gains +1.50 IoU and −0.0041 Disp while additionally providing interpretability and interactivity that SAC-Net lacks. Against proprietary MLLMs, the gap is dramatic: GPT-4o achieves only 71.61% IoU and 43.2% recall, Gemini-2.0-Pro 68.15% IoU, and Qwen-VL-Max 58.09% IoU, all far below Venus-Q's 87.01% IoU and 92.0% recall. Aesthetic-oriented MLLMs perform worst on cropping (AesExpert 37.86% IoU, UNIAA 21.03% IoU), confirming that aesthetic perception training alone does not transfer to spatial cropping. Among open-source general MLLMs, Qwen-VL-Chat (73.84% IoU) and InternVL 2.5 (71.53% IoU) are the closest competitors, yet Venus-Q still leads by 13+ IoU points. On the AesGuide AG benchmark, Venus-L-13B (mean 1.49, expert 1.53) and Venus-I (mean 1.47, expert 1.50) rank 1st and 2nd respectively, surpassing Gemini-2.0-Pro (1.19/1.16) and GPT-4o (0.98/1.15), with the largest gains in Relevance (+1.42 over LLaVA-1.5-13B, +0.79 over InternVL 2.5), indicating that AG fine-tuning specifically improves the model's ability to focus on actionable guidance rather than generic praise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of aesthetic assessment and image cropping by formally defining 'aesthetic guidance' (AG) as a distinct task that prior aesthetic datasets (UNIAA, AesMMIT, PhotoCritique) and MLLMs (AesExpert, PhotoEye) do not address. It departs from specialized cropping models (anchor-based and regression-based) by repurposing MLLMs as interpretable, interactive cropping agents, and from general MLLMs by showing they produce 'flattery' rather than actionable critique. The two-stage design—progressive SFT for AG followed by CoT-rationale fine-tuning for cropping—builds on standard LLaVA-style instruction tuning but introduces the conceptual claim that AG capability is a prerequisite for effective cropping, a dependency not explored in prior cropping or aesthetic MLLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AesGuide is the first dedicated AG dataset and benchmark, filling a gap that the paper's 1,069-participant survey (91% user preference) confirms is practically urgent. The finding that AG pretraining is necessary for strong cropping (ablation: −2.1 IoU without it) suggests a broader principle that compositional reasoning benefits from explicit aesthetic critique before spatial decision-making. Open questions include whether the GPT-assisted evaluation truly captures subjective aesthetic quality at scale, whether the approach generalizes to video or multi-shot composition, and whether the expert-annotation pipeline (20 experts, 20 sessions) is reproducible outside well-resourced labs. The interpretability and interactivity of Venus-Q also raise the question of how to handle genuinely conflicting user preferences in a single model.
