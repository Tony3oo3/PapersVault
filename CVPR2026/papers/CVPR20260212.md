## Problem

Long-horizon robotic manipulation suffers from two coupled failures: temporal error accumulation in subgoal planning (where small early deviations compound over time) and a planning-execution gap where abstract subgoals cannot be reliably grounded in the noisy perception-action space. Prior backward-planning methods like LBP partially address temporal consistency but remain unidirectional and offer no mechanism for spatial grounding, leaving the policy unable to resolve where in the visual scene an abstract action should be executed.

## Input / Output

The policy π_θ takes a current RGB observation I_t, a context signal c (language instruction, goal image, or latent subgoal), and the robot's proprioceptive state p_t, and outputs a low-level action a_t via a DDPM decoder. The bidirectional planner produces a sequence of K latent subgoals Z_plan in the DecisionNCE RN50-CLIP embedding space, and the affordance grounding module yields an implicit attention map (reshaped cross-attention weights) over the Swin Transformer feature grid that highlights task-relevant regions without requiring explicit affordance annotations.

## Method

AGiLe uses a two-stage training strategy. In stage 1, a Backward Planner (initial predictor + recursive Transformer decoder) autoregressively generates subgoals from the goal, while a Forward Critic (MLP) predicts the final goal from any candidate subgoal; they are jointly optimized via backward imitation loss plus forward consistency and forward prediction losses, distilling reachability constraints into the planner so the critic is discarded at inference. In stage 2, the frozen planner's output is fused with the initial-state embedding to form a task Query, which cross-attends against the Swin Transformer visual feature map (Key/Value) to produce a purified, task-relevant feature vector serving as an information bottleneck. This grounded feature, concatenated with proprioception, conditions a DDPM policy decoder trained end-to-end with a single noise-prediction MSE loss, so gradients simultaneously refine both the action decoder and the affordance attention weights.

## Results

On the LIBERO-LONG benchmark (10 multi-stage manipulation tasks, 50 demos each), AGiLe achieves a 97.1% average success rate, an 8.5% absolute improvement over the strongest baseline LBP (88.6%). AGiLe reaches 100% success on 7 of 10 tasks, compared to only 3 for LBP and 1 for Seer. In real-world validation on four long-horizon tasks (4- and 6-stage), AGiLe maintains stable performance across all stages while LBP's success collapses to 5% and 2% in the final stages of the 6-stage tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGiLe outperforms all seven baselines on the LIBERO-LONG benchmark. The closest competitor, LBP (88.6%), is surpassed by 8.5% in average success; AGiLe also wins on 8 of 10 individual tasks, with the largest gaps on 'Put both pots on stove' (+18.6%) and 'Put mugs on left and right plates' (+23.4%). Seer (87.7%), a dense video-prediction method, is outperformed by 9.4% on average, with AGiLe achieving 100% on tasks where Seer scores 61.7–71.7%. MPI (77.3%) and SuSIE (76.3%) trail by roughly 20 points, while large-scale pre-trained models OpenVLA (54.0%) and MVP (68.2%) and the multitask imitation baseline MTACT (41.0%) are substantially outperformed. In real-world experiments, AGiLe consistently exceeds LBP at every stage, with the gap widening dramatically in 6-stage tasks where LBP drops to 2–5% success in final stages while AGiLe remains stable.

## Relation to Prior Work

AGiLe extends Latent Backward Planning (LBP, Liu et al., ICML 2025) by adding a forward critic that validates generated subgoals for dynamic consistency, transforming unidirectional backward reasoning into a bidirectional training loop where the planner internalizes reachability constraints. It operates within the DecisionNCE (Li et al., ICML 2024) latent space for multimodal encoding and departs from forward-predictive planners (Seer, SuSIE) that suffer from autoregressive error accumulation. The affordance grounding mechanism is positioned as a structural guidance mechanism rather than a standalone affordance prediction task, using the latent plan as a cross-attention query to filter visual features—an information bottleneck approach distinct from supervised affordance map prediction in prior robotics literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two underappreciated failure modes in long-horizon manipulation—temporal drift in subgoal sequences and the planning-execution gap—within a single unified framework, and validates both in simulation and on a physical xArm6 robot. The real-world results, where LBP collapses to 2–5% success in the final stages of 6-step tasks while AGiLe maintains stable performance, highlight that backward planning alone is insufficient under real-world uncertainty. Key limitations include the two-stage training (planner frozen before policy learning), preventing online adaptation, and evaluation restricted to seen objects and a single simulation benchmark. Open questions include whether end-to-end or online planner refinement can close the remaining gap, and whether the affordance-as-query mechanism generalizes to open-world settings with novel objects.
