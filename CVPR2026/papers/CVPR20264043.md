## Problem

Existing story visualization benchmarks are narrow in scope—limited to short prompts, single-image generation, or lacking character references—failing to capture the multi-shot, multi-character, multi-style complexity of real-world narrative generation. There is no unified evaluation standard that jointly assesses character consistency, style coherence, prompt alignment, and generation artifacts, obscuring true model performance and hindering systematic progress.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264043/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 80 multi-shot stories (1,317 shots total) across 10 visual styles, each with 344 characters and 509 manually curated reference images. Each shot prompt is structured into five components: setting description, plot correspondence, onstage character list, static shot description (actions/positions), and cinematographic design (shot size, type, angle). Models must produce a sequence of 1080p images (one per shot) that faithfully render all five prompt components while maintaining character identity and style consistency across the full sequence. A Lite subset (20 stories, ~1/4 scale) is provided for cost-efficient evaluation of commercial platforms.

## Method

ViStoryBench constructs its dataset via structured prompt engineering with 5 strategies that convert an LLM into a controllable visual narrative script generator, with all outputs human-verified for coherence; character references are manually collected to ensure style consistency within each story. The evaluation framework comprises 12 automated metrics organized into five dimensions: (1) Character Identification Similarity (CIDS) using a 4-stage pipeline of Grounding DINO detection, face/CLIP feature extraction, bipartite matching, and cosine similarity scoring; (2) Style Similarity (CSD) via a style-trained CLIP encoder; (3) Prompt Alignment (Scene, Shot, Character Interaction, Individual Action) scored by Gemini-3-Pro on a 0–4 Likert scale; (4) Onstage Character Count Matching (OCCM) using an exponential decay formula penalizing count mismatches; and (5) Copy-Paste Detection via a geometrically-normalized metric comparing generated character features to both the specific input reference and an alternative reference of the same character. Aesthetic quality is assessed with Inception Score and Aesthetic Predictor V2.5. The hybrid expert-model + VLM pipeline is validated through human studies showing strong Pearson correlations (up to 0.80) between automated metrics and human ratings.

## Results

On the full ViStoryBench (80 stories, 1,317 shots), among open-source story image methods, QwenImageEdit-2509 leads in prompt alignment (Scene 3.21, CI 2.39, IA 2.13) while UNO achieves the best character count matching (CM 74.2) and OmniGen2 scores highest on CIDS Cross (0.548) and CIDS Self (0.647). On ViStoryBench-Lite, Gemini-3.0 Pro attains the strongest prompt alignment (Scene 3.94, CI 3.71, IA 3.25), Sora2 leads in CIDS Cross (0.766) and Inception Score (81.42), and Doubao tops character identification consistency in user studies (92.6%). The Copy-Paste Baseline achieves near-perfect CIDS (0.929/0.984) but scores poorly on prompt alignment (Scene 0.34) and copy-paste rate (0.474), confirming the consistency–diversity trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among open-source story image methods on the full ViStoryBench, QwenImageEdit-2509 leads in prompt alignment (CI 2.39, IA 2.13) over UNO (CI 1.92, IA 1.78) and OmniGen2 (CI 2.14, IA 1.98), while UNO achieves the best character count matching (CM 74.2 vs. 70.2 for OmniGen2 and 59.8 for QwenImageEdit). OmniGen2 attains the highest character identification similarity (CIDS Cross 0.548, Self 0.647) among non-trivial methods, outperforming UNO (0.485/0.620) and QwenImageEdit (0.475/0.574). StoryDiffusion (text-only) achieves the best aesthetic score (5.76) and lowest copy-paste rate (0.181) among open-source methods, while CharaConsist records the absolute lowest CP (0.172) but at the cost of the weakest CIDS Cross (0.315). On ViStoryBench-Lite, Gemini-3.0 Pro dominates prompt alignment (Scene 3.94, CI 3.71, IA 3.25) over GPT-4o (3.82/3.58/3.12) and Doubao (3.88/3.23/2.65), while Sora2 achieves the strongest CIDS Cross (0.766) and Inception Score (81.42) but trails in aesthetics (4.72) compared to MOKI (5.79) and Doubao (5.61). Story video methods (Vlogger, AnimDirector, MMStoryAgent, MovieAgent) generally score lower on prompt alignment and aesthetics than their image-generation counterparts, with AnimDirector (SD3) and MovieAgent (SD3) being the strongest video baselines (PA CI 2.48 and 2.53 respectively). The Copy-Paste Baseline confirms the expected trade-off: it achieves the highest CIDS (0.929/0.984) and CSD Cross (0.728) but the worst prompt alignment (Scene 0.34) and a high CP rate (0.474), validating the benchmark's ability to distinguish genuine generation from trivial reference copying.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViStoryBench carves new ground in the evaluation landscape for story visualization by combining character-reference-based multi-image generation with a 12-metric automated suite, departing from prior benchmarks (DreamBench++, OmniContext, VinaBench) that either lack character references, cover fewer styles, or evaluate fewer methods. Conceptually, it repurposes established components—Grounding DINO for detection, ArcFace/CLIP for feature extraction, and VLMs (Gemini-3-Pro) for semantic scoring—into a unified pipeline specifically designed for narrative consistency, rather than introducing new generative architectures. The benchmark extends the problem formulation from prior single-image or short-sequence evaluations to multi-shot scripts with explicit camera design, character interaction, and plot correspondence, drawing on the structured script format from prior story visualization systems like StoryDiffusion and TheaterGen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This benchmark is likely to become the standard reference for story visualization evaluation, given its breadth (80 stories, 10 styles, 25+ methods, 12 metrics) and the explicit commitment to a public leaderboard and open-sourced code. Key open questions remain: the absence of background-reference evaluation, the lack of audio-visual coherence assessment, and the acknowledged trade-off between expert-model stability and VLM semantic richness in the hybrid scoring pipeline. The finding that no current method simultaneously achieves high character consistency, strong prompt alignment, and low copy-paste behavior underscores that the field still lacks a unified solution, pointing toward future work on multi-shot training data and holistic multi-modal architectures.
