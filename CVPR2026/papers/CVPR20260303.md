## Problem

Large Vision-Language Models suffer from Language-Prior Hallucination, where accumulated textual context during autoregressive generation gradually fades visual grounding, causing the model to output grammatically coherent but visually ungrounded content. Existing training-free mitigation methods either require multiple forward passes (2× or more inference time), modify internal model components (attention, FFNs, layers), or depend on external encoders, limiting their efficiency and generalizability. The paper identifies that hallucinated tokens erroneously emerge in the logit distribution at intermediate decoding steps and progressively surpass genuine tokens, motivating a decoding-stage intervention that leverages this temporal signal.

## Input / Output

ResDec operates as a drop-in replacement for the standard decoding step of any LVLM, consuming an RGB image and a text query as input and producing a generated text response. Crucially, it reuses the logit distributions already computed during the autoregressive forward pass—no additional model calls, contrastive encoders, or architectural modifications are needed. The method maintains a sliding historical window of previously computed logits (typically 64–512 tokens) and applies a head-truncation threshold (β) to restrict the output vocabulary at each step, incurring only ~0.02× additional latency over greedy decoding.

## Method

ResDec exploits the implicit multi-token prediction capability of LVLMs by analyzing how candidate token distributions evolve over a sliding historical window. It computes Jensen-Shannon Divergence between adjacent time steps to identify a U-shaped pattern, decomposing the window into Pre-Semantic Clarity, Semantic Anchoring, and Expressive Divergence phases; logits from the post-convergence region (near and right of the JSD valley) are selected as they reflect stable, semantically anchored predictions less influenced by language priors. These selected logits are aggregated using confidence weights derived from negative log-likelihood within the candidate token pool, producing a residual guidance signal. This residual is fused with the current decoding logits via a convex combination controlled by α (default 0.5), and a head-truncation constraint (β) filters out low-probability tokens to prevent the augmented distribution from promoting implausible outputs. The entire procedure requires a single forward pass with no contrastive model, external encoder, or training, making it plug-and-play across LVLMs.

## Results

On the POPE hallucination benchmark, ResDec achieves average accuracy of 87.23% (LLaVA-1.5), 83.58% (InstructBLIP), and 90.16% (Qwen2.5-VL), yielding average improvements of 7.84% in accuracy and 8.01% in F1 over regular decoding across three LVLMs. On HallusionBench, ResDec reaches 47.1 fACC on Qwen2.5-VL (vs. 43.4 for regular decoding), and on CHAIR it reduces CHAIR_S from 30.6 to 25.8 and CHAIR_I from 8.4 to 6.8 on Qwen2.5-VL. On comprehensive benchmarks (Table 3), ResDec leads on all eight datasets, e.g., 2348.40 on MME, 82.64 on MMBench, and 63.33 on MMVP for Qwen2.5-VL. Efficiency-wise, ResDec adds only 0.57 ms/token latency over greedy decoding (29.11 vs. 28.54 ms/token), far below OPERA (104.46 ms) or VCD (62.79 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResDec outperforms all eight baselines (VCD, ICD, DoLa, OPERA, ONLY, AGLA, MemVR, VISTA) on every POPE subset and all three LVLMs. The closest competitors are VISTA and MemVR on LLaVA-1.5 (86.15/86.29 and 86.13/86.04 avg Acc/F1 vs. ResDec's 87.23/86.93), and ONLY and VISTA on Qwen2.5-VL (89.16/88.49 and 88.83/88.99 vs. ResDec's 90.16/89.56). On HallusionBench, ResDec leads by a wider margin: 47.1 fACC on Qwen2.5-VL vs. 43.4 (Regular) and 41.5 (OPERA), and 18.2 fACC on LLaVA-1.5 vs. 17.9 (Regular) and 17.9 (MemVR). On CHAIR, ResDec achieves the lowest hallucination rates (e.g., CHAIR_S 25.8 and CHAIR_I 6.8 on Qwen2.5-VL vs. 27.9/7.8 for VCD). In terms of efficiency, ResDec (29.11 ms/token) is nearly as fast as greedy decoding (28.54 ms/token), while OPERA is 3.7× slower (104.46 ms) and VCD is 2.2× slower (62.79 ms), giving ResDec a clear practical advantage alongside its accuracy gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

ResDec builds on the implicit multi-token prediction literature (Pal et al., Samragh et al.) and the probabilistic language-prior framework of Lin et al. [39], but departs from prior hallucination-mitigation work by operating purely at the decoding stage without contrastive models, architectural interventions, or additional forward passes. It borrows the confidence-weighted aggregation mechanism from Fu et al. [18] and repurposes it for logit-level residual guidance. Unlike contrastive decoding methods (VCD, ICD) that require 2×+ inference, or architecture-level methods (OPERA, ONLY, MemVR) that modify internal components, ResDec treats the logit evolution across a historical window as a self-contained signal, using JSD to identify semantically stable phases.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive hallucination reduction at nearly zero additional inference cost (0.02× slower than greedy vs. 2–4× for contrastive baselines), making it a drop-in replacement for production LVLM pipelines. The discovery of the U-shaped JSD pattern and the three-phase decomposition (PSAP/SAP/EDP) provides a mechanistic account of when language priors most strongly corrupt decoding, which could inform future training or alignment strategies. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to larger models (tested only at 7B), multi-turn or video settings, and whether the fixed hyperparameters (α=0.5, β=0.1) transfer across domains without re-tuning.
