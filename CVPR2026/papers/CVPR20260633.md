## Problem

Directly merging VLA experts fine-tuned on different manipulation tasks yields near-zero success rates, unlike in language or vision domains where model merging works well. The paper identifies two root causes of this failure—divergent LoRA updates in the VLM backbone and interblock self-attention dependencies in action experts—and asks how to design a VLA architecture that remains mergeable by construction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260633/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the model consumes a third-person RGB image, a wrist-mounted RGB image, and a natural-language task instruction, producing a continuous action chunk via conditional flow matching in the action expert. In the mixed-task evaluation setting, the task identity is unknown at inference time; the model must infer which skill to activate from the initial observation alone, and the selected task mask and expert head are then fixed for the remainder of the episode.

## Method

MergeVLA introduces three architectural and algorithmic changes to make VLA experts mergeable. First, a task-specific binary mask (derived from a parameter-level consistency test between each task's LoRA update and the merged update) sparsely activates only the LoRA parameters aligned with the target task, suppressing cross-task interference in the VLM backbone. Second, the action expert is redesigned to remove all self-attention layers (retaining only cross-attention on VLM hidden states) and to replace the tanh gate with a sigmoid gate, which localizes task specialization to the final block (the 'expert head') and allows all earlier blocks to be merged via simple weight averaging. Third, a training-free test-time task router performs SVD on the value projection matrices of the merged action expert, projects each candidate task's VLM hidden states onto the resulting principal subspaces, and selects the task with the highest combined activation score—requiring no additional supervision or retraining.

## Results

On LIBERO (4 task suites), MergeVLA with TIES+S merging achieves 90.2% average success rate, only 6.5% below its own single-task finetuned performance (96.7%), while all direct merging attempts on OpenVLA and VLA-Adapter yield 0% success. On LIBERO-Plus (7 perturbation types), the merged MergeVLA (TIES) reaches 62.5% average, surpassing OpenVLA (16.3%), π0 (56.3%), and VLA-Adapter (59.0%) in their single-task settings. On RoboTwin 2.0 cross-embodiment evaluation, the merged model achieves 88.7% (Setting A: same task, 3 robots) and 70.7% (Setting B: different tasks, 3 robots), and real-world SO101 experiments report 90.0% success.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OpenVLA [27], MergeVLA dominates on every metric: single-task finetuned MergeVLA scores 96.7% vs. 76.5% on LIBERO and 72.4% vs. 16.3% on LIBERO-Plus, and the merged MergeVLA (TIES, 62.5% on LIBERO-Plus) still exceeds OpenVLA's single-task performance by 46 points. Against VLA-Adapter [47], MergeVLA's single-task finetuned model is slightly lower on LIBERO (96.7% vs. 98.5%) but substantially higher on LIBERO-Plus (72.4% vs. 59.0%), and critically, while VLA-Adapter's merged variants collapse to 0–23.1%, MergeVLA's merged model retains 90.2% on LIBERO. Against π0 [3], MergeVLA's merged model (62.5% on LIBERO-Plus) outperforms π0's single-task finetuned result (56.3%) by 6.2 points, demonstrating that the merge-friendly architecture preserves and even transfers robustness across tasks. On RoboTwin cross-embodiment (Setting A), the TIES-merged MergeVLA (88.7%) marginally exceeds the average of individually finetuned policies (88.0%), confirming that merging does not sacrifice per-embodiment performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VLA-Adapter's dual-system architecture (VLM + from-scratch action expert) but departs from it by removing self-attention layers and replacing the tanh gate with a sigmoid gate to restore mergeability. The task-mask formulation for LoRA merging adapts the parameter-level consistency test from task-vector compression (Wang et al., ICML 2024), while the overall merging pipeline is compatible with mainstream data-free methods (TA, TIES, WUDI, KnOTS, TSV). Conceptually, the paper positions itself against the broader VLA literature (OpenVLA, π0, π0.5) where multi-skill capability requires joint training, and against the model-merging literature where VLAs have been largely unexplored—showing that standard merging techniques that succeed for LLMs and VLMs catastrophically fail for VLAs due to two structural causes: divergent LoRA directions and self-attention-induced interblock coupling in action experts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By identifying the specific architectural mechanisms (self-attention feedback in action experts, extreme LoRA parameter selfishness) that prevent VLA merging, the paper provides a diagnostic framework that generalizes beyond this particular model family. The practical implication is a scalable path to generalist embodied agents: independently trained task experts can be consolidated without joint retraining or dataset revisiting, with the merged model matching or exceeding individual experts on LIBERO (90.2% vs. 96.7% finetuned) and showing 13.4% better OOD robustness. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger VLM backbones, whether pretraining on diverse robot data reduces the need for per-task expert heads, and whether the training-free router generalizes to open-ended task distributions beyond a fixed set of M skills.
