## Problem

Existing multimodal sentiment analysis methods rely on coarse shared-private disentanglement or insufficient cross-modal regularization, lacking explicit isolation of sentiment-irrelevant noise factors and sample-adaptive fusion mechanisms. This limits their ability to preserve fine-grained affective cues under modality heterogeneity, redundancy, and noise contamination, particularly when signal-to-noise ratios are low or modalities are partially corrupted.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263380/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes word-aligned feature sequences from three modalities: contextualized text embeddings (RoBERTa last-layer, masked mean-pooled), COVAREP acoustic descriptors (pitch, energy, voice quality), and FACET facial action units, all projected to 128 dimensions and encoded by a one-layer BiGRU. It outputs a continuous sentiment intensity score (range [-3,3] for MOSI/MOSEI, [-1,1] for SIMSv2) and ordinal sentiment labels under multiple discretization protocols (binary, 3-way, 5-way, 7-way).

## Method

FUSE-Net decomposes each modality representation into three complementary subspaces—shared (modality-invariant sentiment semantics), specific (modality-exclusive affective cues), and noise (sentiment-irrelevant variations)—via a Hierarchical Modality Factorization module regularized by contrastive separation, an information-gain constraint that pushes sentiment-discriminative content into shared/specific branches while suppressing it in the noise branch, and a dual consistency regularizer enforcing mutual recoverability. A Modality Reconstruction Channel then applies a Variational Information Bottleneck objective over the concatenated factors, using a diagonal-Gaussian latent posterior and reparameterization trick to preserve task-relevant semantics while filtering residual redundancy. Finally, a Multi-factor Dynamic Fusion module adaptively aggregates the three factor types across modalities using a product of a sample-conditioned scalar modulation score, a learnable factor-type coefficient, and a branch-wise scalar attention score, with an additional element-wise sigmoid gate applied to the noise branch before concatenation and a two-layer MLP prediction head.

## Results

FUSE-Net achieves the best MAE on all three benchmarks: 0.688 on CMU-MOSI, 0.527 on CMU-MOSEI, and 0.297 on SIMSv2. It also attains the highest Acc7 on both English datasets (49.27 on MOSI, 54.32 on MOSEI) and the best Corr on SIMSv2 (0.712). Relative to the strongest disentanglement baseline DTN, FUSE-Net yields average relative gains of 2.88% (MOSI), 4.21% (MOSEI), and 1.96% (SIMSv2), with the largest single-metric improvement being a 7.87% MAE reduction on MOSEI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUSE-Net outperforms all 14 baselines on MAE across all three datasets, with the most notable margins over DTN (0.688 vs. 0.716 on MOSI; 0.527 vs. 0.572 on MOSEI) and Self-MM (0.688 vs. 0.708 on MOSI; 0.527 vs. 0.531 on MOSEI). On Acc7, FUSE-Net leads by a clear margin over DTN (49.27 vs. 47.5 on MOSI; 54.32 vs. 52.3 on MOSEI) and PriSA (54.32 vs. 53.95 on MOSEI). On SIMSv2, FUSE-Net achieves the best MAE (0.297), Corr (0.712), Acc3 (73.69), and Acc5 (55.51), though CENet edges it out on F1 (79.63 vs. 79.53) and ALMT ties on Acc2 (79.59). Fusion-oriented baselines (TFN, LMF, MulT, ICCN) are consistently outperformed by 0.1–0.3 in MAE, confirming that explicit factorization and noise isolation provide substantial gains over direct cross-modal attention. The paper highlights that the 7.87% MAE reduction over DTN on MOSEI is the largest single-metric improvement, attributed to the explicit noise subspace preventing nuisance leakage into sentiment-bearing representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUSE-Net extends the shared-private disentanglement line (MISA, DeCUR, DMRNet) by introducing a third, explicit noise subspace, moving from a two-way to a three-way factorization that isolates sentiment-irrelevant nuisance variations. It incorporates the Variational Information Bottleneck (Alemi et al., 2016) as a reconstruction regularizer to prevent over-suppression of weak affective cues during decomposition, a mechanism absent from prior disentanglement-based MSA methods. The multi-factor dynamic fusion module builds on sample-adaptive attention (MulT, MSG) but decomposes fusion control into three orthogonal factors (modulation score, factor-type coefficient, branch-wise attention) operating at different scopes, which is a more structured alternative to single-level gating.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit noise subspace addresses a practical failure mode in MSA where nuisance variations (background noise, recording artifacts, exaggerated paralinguistic cues) contaminate sentiment-bearing representations, and the VIB reconstruction provides a principled safeguard against over-purification. However, the relative gains over the strongest baseline (DTN) are modest (2–4% on key metrics), and the framework's scalability to more than three modalities or to genuinely missing-modality scenarios remains untested. Open questions include whether the tri-factor decomposition generalizes to higher-dimensional or streaming settings, and whether the information-gain constraint and duality regularizer remain stable under distribution shift.
