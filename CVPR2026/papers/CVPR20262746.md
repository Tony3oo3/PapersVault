## Problem

GRPO, the dominant RL algorithm for training VLM reasoning, suffers from diversity collapse: models prematurely converge to a narrow set of reasoning strategies within the first ~20 training steps, discarding the majority of alternative solution paths. This leads to local optima and poor test-time scaling, as RL models achieve higher single-attempt accuracy but fail to solve a broader range of problems when multiple sampling attempts are permitted, unlike their base counterparts which exhibit divergent thinking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262746/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image paired with a text question and produces a reasoning trajectory (chain-of-thought) followed by a final answer. During training, N=15 candidate trajectories are sampled per example at temperature 1.0, then partitioned into K groups via constrained clustering in embedding space. Evaluation uses both greedy decoding (acc@1) and 4-sample majority voting (acc@4) to assess depth and breadth of reasoning respectively.

## Method

MUPO (Multi-Group Policy Optimization) is a drop-in replacement for GRPO that partitions sampled responses into K groups using constrained clustering on reasoning embeddings, then computes advantages locally within each group rather than globally. A diversity reward is introduced, defined as the average cosine distance between a trajectory's embedding and all trajectories in other groups, gated by an accuracy condition (applied only when R_acc=1) to prevent reward hacking. The diversity reward weight λ is annealed via a cosine schedule from λ_max=0.4 to λ_min=0.1, encouraging broad exploration early and exploitation later. A load-balance weight w_k=(N/(K|G_k|))^β prevents larger groups from dominating the composite objective, which is essentially a weighted sum of per-group GRPO objectives.

## Results

MUPO-Thinker-7B achieves an average acc@1 of 51.6% on mathematical benchmarks (MathVerse, MathVista, MathVision, LogicVista, WeMath, Geometry3K) and 65.6% on general-purpose benchmarks (MMStar, HallusionBench, MMVet), improving over the previous best open-source baseline Vision-R1-7B by 2.5% and 2.3% respectively. On acc@4 (test-time scaling), MUPO-Thinker-7B reaches 58.8% on math and 72.4% on general benchmarks, surpassing the strongest baselines by 6.0% and 6.2%. At 3B scale, MUPO-Thinker-3B achieves 43.5% acc@1 on math and 57.8% on general, outperforming LMM-R1 (4B) by 2.0% and 2.4% while using fewer parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUPO-Thinker-7B outperforms all open-source 7B baselines on both acc@1 and acc@4 across mathematical and general benchmarks. On math acc@1, it beats Vision-R1-7B (49.1→51.6, +2.5%), VLAA-Thinker-7B (47.2→51.6, +4.4%), R1-OneVision-7B (46.1→51.6, +5.5%), and InternVL2.5-8B (41.7→51.6, +9.9%). The acc@4 gains are substantially larger: MUPO-Thinker-7B reaches 58.8% vs. Vision-R1's 52.8% (+6.0%) and VLAA's 51.5% (+7.3%), and even surpasses its own base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B (56.5%) by 2.3 points. On general benchmarks (Table 2), MUPO-Thinker-7B leads all 7B baselines with 65.6% acc@1 and 72.4% acc@4, exceeding Vision-R1 (63.3/66.1) and VLAA (63.6/66.2). At 3B scale, MUPO-Thinker-3B (43.5% math acc@1) surpasses both LMM-R1-4B (41.5%) and VLM-R1-4B (37.8%) despite having fewer parameters, and its acc@4 of 51.5% on math exceeds LMM-R1's 45.5% by 6 points. Against proprietary models (GPT-5-Thinking at 76.1%, Gemini-2.5-Pro at 76.0%), MUPO-Thinker-7B remains substantially behind, as expected for a 7B open-source model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a direct modification of GRPO (from DeepSeekMath [27]), reframing the standard global advantage computation into a multi-group, locally-normalized objective with an explicit diversity reward. It departs from prior RL-for-reasoning work in VLMs (Vision-R1, R1-OneVision, VLAA-Thinker) by diagnosing a specific failure mode—diversity collapse in the first ~20 training steps—that prior methods implicitly accept. The empirical comparison between RL and base models on acc@k and embedding-space diversity is a new analytical lens not present in the existing GRPO literature, and the method draws on constrained clustering [2] for response grouping, a technique repurposed from classical ML into policy optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic finding that GRPO prematurely collapses reasoning diversity—causing RL models to solve *fewer* problems than their base counterparts under multi-sample evaluation—challenges the prevailing assumption that RL strictly expands a model's reasoning repertoire. MUPO's simple grouping-plus-diversity-reward mechanism is a practical drop-in that practitioners can adopt without architectural changes, and the acc@4 gains (6%+) suggest meaningful test-time scaling benefits. Open questions include whether the diversity-collapse phenomenon generalizes beyond Qwen2.5-VL and ViRL39K, whether the optimal number of groups K is task-dependent in a principled way, and whether similar grouping strategies can be applied to other RL algorithms (PPO, DPO) or to LLM-only settings.
