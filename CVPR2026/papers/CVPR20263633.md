## Problem

Large-scale image-to-video diffusion models possess strong world-simulation and temporal reasoning priors, yet their potential as zero-shot, tuning-free image editors remains largely unexplored. Existing video-based editing approaches either require costly fine-tuning to shorten generation trajectories or depend on heavy VLM-based post-selection to pick the best frame, and no systematic study has characterized where video priors help or hurt across diverse editing tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263633/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a free-form text editing instruction as input. The image is concatenated with (F−1) zero-frame placeholders to form a pseudo-video, encoded by a 3D VAE into a conditioning latent, and the instruction is first rewritten by a VLM into a temporally grounded prompt. The output is a single edited image (not a video), obtained by selecting the sharpest frame from a 32-frame generation and refining it via a brief still-video pass; each full editing run completes in ~12 seconds on one H100 GPU using Wan 2.2 with Lightning-LoRA and 8 denoising steps.

## Method

IF-Edit repurposes a pre-trained image-to-video diffusion model (Wan 2.2, a 27B MoE DiT with separate high-noise and low-noise experts) for zero-shot image editing through three lightweight, training-free components. First, a Chain-of-Thought Prompt Enhancement module uses Qwen3-VL-A3B to jointly interpret the input image and instruction, producing a temporally grounded reasoning prompt that describes how the scene evolves frame-by-frame, aligning the static edit with the video model's world-simulation prior. Second, Temporal Latent Dropout (TLD) performs a one-shot temporal subsampling (stride K=3) on the latent tensor immediately after the expert-switch point, reducing the active temporal dimension from F to F/K and cutting denoising cost from O(F) to O(F/K) while preserving the global layout established by the high-noise expert. Third, Self-Consistent Post-Refinement (SCPR) deterministically selects the sharpest decoded frame via a Laplacian-based blur score, then re-injects that frame into the same video model with a 'still-video' prompt to generate a short refinement clip, from which the final output is again chosen by sharpness—yielding clearer textures without external deblurring models or VLM scoring.

## Results

On TEdBench, IF-Edit achieves the best scores across all three metrics: LPIPS 0.19 (vs. 0.22 for the next best F2F/FlowEdit), CLIP-I 0.96 (vs. 0.89), and CLIP-T 0.65 (vs. 0.63). On ByteMorph, IF-Edit leads in Camera Zoom (67.89), Human Motion (67.04), and Interaction (69.05), while remaining competitive on Camera Move (59.80) and Object Motion (72.02 vs. Step1X-Edit's 72.78). On RISEBench, IF-Edit attains 11.1% overall reasoning accuracy—best among open-source editors but well below commercial systems Nano-Banana (32.8%) and GPT-Image (28.9%). On ImgEdit, IF-Edit scores 2.19 overall, trailing all major baselines (best: GPT-Image at 4.20), confirming its limitation on general-purpose attribute and style edits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On TEdBench, IF-Edit outperforms all six baselines on every metric, with the largest gap in CLIP-I (0.96 vs. 0.89 for F2F and FlowEdit) and a clear LPIPS advantage (0.19 vs. 0.22). On ByteMorph, IF-Edit beats Step1X-Edit on Camera Zoom (67.89 vs. 59.34), Human Motion (67.04 vs. 65.39), and Interaction (69.05 vs. 65.99), but slightly trails it on Object Motion (72.02 vs. 72.78) and UltraEdit on Camera Move (59.80 vs. 59.01). On RISEBench, IF-Edit (11.1% overall) surpasses all open-source editors—Qwen-Image-Edit (8.9%), BAGEL (6.1%), Kontext-Dev (5.8%), Ovis-U1 (2.8%), Step1X-Edit (1.9%), OmniGen (0.8%), and HiDream-Edit (0.0%)—yet remains far behind the commercial Nano-Banana (32.8%) and GPT-Image (28.9%), particularly on Temporal (5.8% vs. 25.9%) and Causal (21.1% vs. 47.8%) reasoning. On ImgEdit, IF-Edit (2.19 overall) is outperformed by every baseline, including MagicBrush (1.83), Instruct-P2P (1.88), AnyEdit (2.45), UltraEdit (2.70), ICEdit (3.05), Step1X-Edit (3.06), UniWorld-V1 (3.26), BAGEL (3.42), OmniGen2 (3.44), Kontext-Dev (3.71), and GPT-Image (4.20), with its relative strength confined to the Action category (3.10 vs. 2.52 for Step1X-Edit). Overall, the video prior gives IF-Edit a decisive edge on motion- and reasoning-centric edits but a clear deficit on localized, attribute-level, and style-transfer tasks where specialized image editors dominate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Wan 2.2 image-to-video diffusion model (a Mixture-of-Experts DiT with high-noise and low-noise denoising experts) as a zero-shot image editor, positioning itself between two existing lines of work: training-free single-frame editors (SDEdit, LEDITS++, FlowEdit) that lack temporal/causal priors, and video-based editing approaches like F2F (which relies on costly VLM frame selection) and ChronoEdit (which requires fine-tuning). The key conceptual departure is treating image editing as a short, controlled traversal through the video model's world-simulation state space, guided by chain-of-thought prompts that reformulate static instructions into temporally grounded reasoning. The three proposed components—CoT Prompt Enhancement, Temporal Latent Dropout, and Self-Consistent Post-Refinement—are lightweight, training-free modifications to the standard video generation pipeline rather than architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic evaluation of off-the-shelf image-to-video models across multiple image-editing benchmarks, providing a clear map of where video priors help (non-rigid deformation, temporal/causal reasoning) and where they fall short (localized attribute/style edits, region-specific modifications). The Temporal Latent Dropout strategy is a practical efficiency contribution that generalizes beyond this specific task to any video diffusion inference setting. Open questions include whether video-model temporal reasoning can be distilled into compact image-only models, whether region-aware controls can close the general-editing gap, and whether the >40 GB memory footprint can be reduced without quality loss—limitations the authors acknowledge but do not resolve.
