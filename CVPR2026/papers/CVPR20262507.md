## Problem

Existing LNL methods follow a clean-sample reliance paradigm (sample selection, label correction, re-weighting) that inherently cannot prevent overfitting to even a small fraction of noisy labels, creating a persistent performance bottleneck. The paper identifies two distinct noise-fitting stages—early generalized learning where noisy knowledge is unavoidably assimilated, and later-stage overfitting where the model increasingly memorizes corrupted annotations—and argues that merely selecting clean samples is insufficient without mechanisms to actively forget already-internalized noise and suppress further noise assimilation.

## Input / Output

FINE operates as a plug-and-play regularizer on top of any existing sample-selection-based LNL method: it receives the standard noisy labeled RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR100N/CIFAR80N, variable resolution for Web-Aircraft/Bird/Car) along with the clean/noisy partition produced by the base method's selection strategy. The AFMU and NSNL losses are applied exclusively to the noisy subset D_N, while the clean subset D_C is trained with the base method's original loss L_base. The output is a robust multi-class classifier whose predictions are evaluated on clean test sets, with the framework adding no architectural changes—only two lightweight loss terms with hyperparameters β=0.001 and γ=0.1.

## Method

FINE unifies two complementary mechanisms targeting the two identified noise-fitting stages. The AFMU module applies a negative cross-entropy loss (l_MU = +1/C Σ y_i^j log p_i^j) on noisy samples, reversing the standard CE optimization direction to actively erase memorized noisy knowledge accumulated during early training. The NSNL module constructs a random complementary label ỹ_i (one non-given class retained, all others zeroed) and applies a negative learning loss (l_NL = −1/C Σ ỹ_i^j log(1−p_i^j)) that drives the model's confidence on that complementary class toward zero, thereby discouraging further fitting to corrupted supervision in later stages. The total objective L_total = L_base + β·L_MU + γ·L_NL combines the base method's clean-sample loss with these two noisy-sample regularizers, making FINE a drop-in addition to frameworks like SED or ACT without modifying their architectures or selection logic.

## Results

On synthetic benchmarks (CIFAR100N and CIFAR80N), SED+FINE achieves the highest average test accuracy of 59.79%, surpassing the strongest standalone baseline CA2C (ICCV 2025) by 1.22% and its own base SED by 3.88%. Under the most challenging CIFAR100N-Sym-80% setting, SED+FINE reaches 45.54%, exceeding CA2C by 4.57%. On real-world noisy datasets (Web-Aircraft, Web-Bird, Web-Car), SED+FINE attains 86.60% average accuracy with ResNet50, outperforming CA2C (86.43%) and ACT (85.58%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FINE is designed as a plug-and-play regularizer and is evaluated by augmenting two SOTA baselines: SED (ECCV 2024) and ACT (MM 2024). SED+FINE consistently outperforms its base SED across all six synthetic noise settings, with the largest gains under high-noise conditions (e.g., +7.39% on CIFAR100N-Sym-80%, +7.64% on CIFAR100N-Asym-40%). Similarly, ACT+FINE improves over ACT by 0.36–1.63% per setting. Against the strongest standalone competitor CA2C (ICCV 2025, avg 58.57%), SED+FINE (59.79%) leads by 1.22% on average, with a particularly large 4.57% margin on CIFAR100N-Sym-80%. On real-world benchmarks, SED+FINE (86.60%) edges out CA2C (86.43%) and ACT+FINE (85.97%), confirming that the gains transfer to unknown noise patterns. Earlier methods such as DivideMix (41.10%), Jo-SRC (44.85%), and NPN (49.85%) trail substantially, highlighting the cumulative progress of the clean-sample-reliance paradigm that FINE extends.

## Relation to Prior Work

FINE departs from the dominant clean-sample reliance paradigm in LNL (sample selection, label correction, re-weighting) by introducing a complementary 'forget and suppress' perspective. It repurposes machine unlearning—previously studied for privacy and model adaptability—into a negative cross-entropy loss that actively erases already-memorized noisy knowledge, and integrates negative learning (previously explored in NLNL and JNPL as standalone strategies) as a complementary-label-based regularizer to prevent further noise fitting. Both modules are designed as plug-and-play add-ons to existing sample-selection baselines (SED, ACT), building directly on the memorization-effect literature and the two-stage noise-fitting dynamics observed in prior empirical studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design lowers the barrier for practitioners to improve robustness without redesigning their LNL pipeline, and the two-stage diagnostic (early generalized learning vs. later overfitting) offers a useful lens for understanding why clean-sample selection alone plateaus. However, the numerical gains are modest (+1.05% average on synthetic, +0.77% on real-world), and the framework's dependence on the quality of upstream sample selection for identifying noisy samples remains an open vulnerability. Scaling to very large models, instance-dependent noise, or settings where the noise rate is unknown and high remains unexplored, and whether the forgetting mechanism interacts adversely with long training schedules or larger architectures is unclear.
