## Problem

Existing VLM debiasing methods (fine-tuning, prompt tuning, weight pruning) alleviate surface-level bias symptoms without modifying the internal representations through which bias propagates, making it impossible to identify and target the specific components responsible for encoding social biases. This lack of interpretability often degrades general model capability, as individual neurons encode polysemantic concepts that simultaneously affect bias and task performance.

## Input / Output

For T2I retrieval, the method takes text prompts (adjectives, occupations, activities, stereotypes) and retrieves from a pool of 10,954 cropped facial images; for VQA, it takes an image paired with a text question and produces a debiased answer. The SAE is trained on facial image or caption datasets (FairFace, CelebA, Cocogender, Bias in Bios) without any social attribute labels, and the intervention operates at the latent activation level of the frozen VLM encoder's last layer, producing a weighted blend (α=0.6) of the SAE-reconstructed feature and the original hidden state that replaces the encoder output for downstream computation.

## Method

DEBIASLENS attaches a Matryoshka SAE (expansion factor 8) to the last layer of a frozen VLM's vision or text encoder and trains it via self-supervised reconstruction on facial datasets without demographic labels. Social neurons are identified by probing: a neuron is selected for group g if its activation is non-zero for at least τ=0.9 of samples in g but absent from all other groups, then ranked by mean within-group activation. During inference, the top-1 selected social neuron per group has its activation zeroed in the SAE latent vector, the modified vector is decoded, and the result is blended with the original feature via a weighted sum. This yields interpretable, neuron-level debiasing that achieves 9–16% Max Skew reduction for CLIP and 40–50% gender disproportion decrease for InternVL2 while preserving general VLM performance, without any retraining or weight modification of the base model.

## Results

On CLIP ViT-B/16 T2I retrieval (FairFace, Max Skew@1000, lower is better), DEBIASLENS(T) achieves the best overall bias mitigation with scores of 7.1 (adjectives), 16.2 (occupations), 14.2 (activities), and 8.1 (stereotypes), outperforming all non-interpretable baselines on adjective and stereotype prompts. For CLIP ViT-L/14@336, DEBIASLENS(I) reduces Max Skew to 12.0/20.4/17.2/11.2 across the four prompt types, substantially beating MMNeuron (17.3/23.5/26.0/20.3). On LVLMs, DEBIASLENS-Intern (α=0.6) achieves a 40–50% reduction in gender disproportion rate on VLAGenderBias while incurring only a 4–10 point drop in general performance (MME, ImageNette), yielding the best bias-vs-utility tradeoff among all tested debiasing strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Prompt [8] and Projection [14], DEBIASLENS(T) dominates on all four prompt categories for CLIP ViT-B/16 (e.g., 7.1 vs. 12.3 on adjectives; 8.1 vs. 52.0 on stereotypes for Projection), and notably does so without requiring social-attribute labels during SAE training. Against Bend-VLM [19] and SANER [28], the comparison is mixed: DEBIASLENS(T) achieves lower Max Skew on adjective (7.1 vs. 10.8 / 8.9) and stereotype (8.1 vs. 9.1 / –) prompts, but Bend-VLM and SANER remain stronger on occupation (10.2 / 14.5 vs. 16.2) and activity (9.8 / 7.7 vs. 14.2) prompts, suggesting that test-time per-input adaptation still has an edge for certain prompt types. For the larger CLIP ViT-L/14@336, DEBIASLENS(I) outperforms the interpretable baseline MMNeuron [33] by 4–9 points across all four categories (e.g., 12.0 vs. 17.3 on adjectives). On LVLMs, Figure 4 shows DEBIASLENS-Intern achieving the best bias-mitigation-to-general-performance tradeoff compared to Full Fine-Tuning, LoRA, Pruning, Prompt Tuning, and Prompt Engineering from Girrbach et al. [21]: it reduces gender disproportion by 40–50% while losing only 4–10 points on MME/ImageNette, whereas Pruning achieves comparable bias reduction but with a much larger general-performance drop. On SBBench (Table 3), DEBIASLENS with FairFace-trained SAE and α=1.0 lifts InternVL2-8B gender accuracy from 83.83% to 87.87% (rule-based) and age accuracy from 43.11% to 48.51%, confirming gains across both social attributes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: post-hoc VLM debiasing (SANER, Bend-VLM, projection methods, LoRA fine-tuning) and mechanistic interpretability via sparse autoencoders (SAEs) for feature disentanglement. Prior debiasing methods operate on surface-level embeddings or require retraining, while prior SAE-based interpretability work has been applied to LLMs and T2I diffusion models but not to encoder-based VLMs for bias mitigation. DEBIASLENS departs from both by attaching a Matryoshka SAE to frozen VLM encoder outputs, identifying 'social neurons' through unsupervised activation consistency across demographic groups, and selectively deactivating them at inference—requiring no model weight modification or retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By localizing bias to a small set of interpretable neurons, DEBIASLENS transforms debiasing from a black-box correction into a transparent, auditable intervention, which is critical for deploying VLMs in high-stakes settings. The finding that deactivating only the top-1 social neuron per group achieves performance comparable to deactivating all effective neurons suggests minimal intervention suffices, reducing the risk of semantic degradation. However, the framework currently handles only a single top neuron per attribute group, and the observed intersectional leakage (age neurons partially mitigating gender bias) indicates that more complex, multi-attribute interactions remain unresolved. Open questions include whether this approach generalizes to non-facial social attributes, to more than two demographic groups simultaneously, and to LVLMs with deeper transformer stacks where bias may be distributed across layers rather than concentrated in the final encoder output.
