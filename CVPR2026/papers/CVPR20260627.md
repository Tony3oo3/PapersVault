## Problem

Existing LoRA-based multi-task PEFT methods apply a single fixed rank across all tasks and backbone layers, ignoring that different tasks and depths require different adaptation strengths. Additionally, these methods lack spatial inductive bias and fail to exploit cross-task frequency-domain relationships that are critical for dense visual prediction tasks like segmentation and depth estimation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260627/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and produces all dense task outputs (semantic segmentation, human part segmentation, saliency, surface normals, and depth maps) in one forward pass through a frozen Swin-Tiny backbone with per-task HRNet decoders. The backbone is pre-trained on ImageNet-1k, and the model is evaluated on PASCAL-Context (4 tasks) and NYUDv2 (3 tasks) at their native resolutions. The key architectural constraint is that only the last transformer block per stage is task-specific; all earlier blocks share DoRA adapters across tasks.

## Method

FAAR combines three mechanisms: (1) Performance-Driven Rank Shrinking (PDRS) dynamically reduces the LoRA rank per task and per layer during training by first applying random prefix rank-masking to push important adaptation into lower rank dimensions, then computing EMA-smoothed directional-derivative importance scores for each rank-1 component and permanently pruning ranks that fall below a coverage threshold ρ each epoch; (2) DoRA adapters (magnitude-direction weight decomposition) replace standard LoRA for stability at the very low ranks that PDRS converges to; (3) a Task-Spectral Pyramidal Decoder (TS-PD) applies per-channel, per-task, per-resolution FFT-based spectral filters (CW-SP) to select task-relevant frequency bands, and a Cross-Task Consensus Alignment module (XT-Cons) that aligns each task's representation toward the average frequency spectrum of auxiliary tasks on both low- and high-frequency bands to enforce geometric consistency across tasks.

## Results

On PASCAL-Context (4 tasks), FAAR achieves a mean relative improvement of +5.28% over single-task baselines with only 3.38M trainable parameters, outperforming the best MTL PEFT baseline TADFormer (+4.24%, 7.38M params) and MTL full fine-tuning (+2.23%, 30.06M params). On NYUDv2 (3 tasks), FAAR achieves −7.88% relative degradation with 2.85M params, the best among all MTL methods, compared to MTL full fine-tuning (−8.49%, 28.10M) and TADFormer (−10.42%, 8.90M). FAAR reduces trainable parameters by up to 9× relative to traditional MTL fine-tuning while improving overall accuracy on both benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAAR outperforms all 11 baselines on PASCAL-Context across every individual task metric and the aggregate Δm score. Against the closest MTL PEFT competitors, FAAR beats TADFormer [4] by +1.04% Δm (5.28 vs 4.24) while using 54% fewer parameters (3.38M vs 7.38M), and beats MTLoRA [2] by +2.73% Δm (5.28 vs 2.55) with 59% fewer parameters. DiTASK [33] is the most parameter-efficient baseline (3.55M) but trails FAAR by +2.06% Δm. On NYUDv2, where all MTL methods underperform single-task due to the small dataset (795 training images), FAAR still achieves the best Δm (−7.88%) with the fewest parameters (2.85M), outperforming MTL full fine-tuning (−8.49%, 28.10M) and TADFormer (−10.42%, 8.90M). Single-task PEFT methods (Adapter, BitFit, VPT, Compacter, LoRA) all show negative Δm on PASCAL-Context, confirming that multi-task adaptation is necessary for dense visual tasks, and none of them support single-inference multi-task output.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAAR builds directly on the DoRA weight-decomposition framework and the LoRA low-rank adaptation paradigm, extending both into a multi-task setting where prior LoRA-based MTL methods (MTLoRA, TADFormer) apply a single fixed rank across all layers and tasks. The Performance-Driven Rank Shrinking mechanism draws conceptual inspiration from single-task automatic rank selection methods (DyLoRA, AdaLoRA, AutoLoRA) but adapts them to the MTL objective by using a global loss signal and per-task, per-layer coverage criteria. The Task-Spectral Pyramidal Decoder repurposes FFT-based frequency filtering—previously explored in single-task domain adaptation and night-time segmentation (FADA, NightAdapter, Adapt-ICMH)—for the first time in dense visual multi-task learning, adding a cross-task frequency alignment module (XT-Cons) that has no direct precedent in the MTL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FAAR offers a practical path to scaling MTL to more tasks by reducing backbone adaptation cost by up to 9× while simultaneously improving accuracy, which is valuable as MTL models grow in task count. The dynamic per-task, per-layer rank allocation is a useful inductive bias that acknowledges heterogeneous task difficulty and layer importance, and the frequency-domain cross-task alignment provides a cheap mechanism for geometric consistency. However, evaluation is limited to two small-scale datasets (PASCAL-Context, NYUDv2) with a single backbone (Swin-Tiny), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger backbones, more tasks, or non-dense prediction settings. The modest accuracy gains over the strongest PEFT baselines (TADFormer, DiTASK) suggest the efficiency savings are the primary contribution rather than a fundamental accuracy breakthrough.
