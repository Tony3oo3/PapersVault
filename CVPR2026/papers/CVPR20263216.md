## Problem

Existing human motion synthesis methods that interact with dynamic scenes rely on simple contact or penetration priors that fail to model true physical forces, producing artifacts like floating and foot sliding. Physics-based approaches using simulators or reinforcement learning are non-differentiable and cannot integrate into end-to-end generative pipelines. The closest prior work, PhysPT, uses a continuous spring-based contact model but is restricted to static planar surfaces with fixed upward normals and decoupled normal/tangential forces, preventing generalization to arbitrary 3D geometries or dynamic objects.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D object motion sequence O ∈ R^{T×B} (translation plus BPS representation) and a static scene S ∈ {0,1}^{Nx×Ny×Nz} in voxel format as input, and produces a full-body 3D human motion Q ∈ R^{T×75} parameterized by SMPL pose, shape, global orientation, and translation. As an intermediate output, the first stage also predicts hand trajectories H ∈ R^{T×6} and per-contact-point force model coefficients (stiffness, damping, friction parameters) that condition the second-stage motion generation.

## Method

The core contribution is a differentiable continuous contact force model that decomposes interaction forces into a normal component (damped spring aligned with the local surface normal at the nearest scene/object point) and a tangential component (static friction proportional to lateral acceleration plus kinetic friction proportional to the normal force), enabling physically grounded interactions on arbitrary 3D surfaces. Human and object dynamics are both formulated via Euler-Lagrange equations and coupled through Newton's third law, so the reaction force from the object on the hand is explicitly constrained by the object's own motion. The synthesis pipeline is a two-stage transformer-based diffusion model: stage 1 predicts joint torques, contact coefficients, and hand trajectories from scene and object-motion tokens; stage 2 generates full-body SMPL motion conditioned on those physics parameters, trained with an L1 reconstruction loss plus a dynamic consistency loss that penalizes residuals of the Euler-Lagrange equations to enforce force and torque balance.

## Results

On the OMOMO dataset, InterPhys achieves the best performance on all metrics except foot sliding (FS=0.41 vs. 0.38 for OMOMO), with HandJPE of 20.09 cm, MPJPE of 10.02 cm, and contact F1 of 0.80, representing at least 12% improvement over the second-best method (OMOMO) on motion metrics. On TRUMANS, the method reduces scene penetration from 33.48 cm to 21.03 cm (a 37% reduction) while improving contact F1 from 0.59 to 0.69. The method also achieves the lowest collision rate (0.19% on OMOMO, 0.23% on TRUMANS) and the highest contact precision (0.87 on OMOMO, 0.75 on TRUMANS) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InterPhys outperforms all four OMOMO baselines on motion quality metrics (HandJPE, MPJPE, MPVPE, T_root, O_root) and interaction metrics (collision, contact precision/recall/F1). Against the closest competitor OMOMO [21], the improvement is 16.3% on HandJPE (20.09 vs. 24.01 cm), 19.3% on MPJPE (10.02 vs. 12.42 cm), and 11.1% on contact F1 (0.80 vs. 0.72). InterDiff [51] and CHIOS [22] are substantially weaker, with HandJPE errors 37% and 42% higher respectively, and contact F1 scores of only 0.63 and 0.67. InterAct [53] is the strongest baseline on contact metrics (F1=0.73) but still trails InterPhys by 9.6% on F1 and 18.5% on HandJPE. On TRUMANS, InterPhys beats the Trumans [18] baseline on every metric, most notably reducing scene penetration by 37% (21.03 vs. 33.48 cm) and improving contact F1 by 17% (0.69 vs. 0.59). The only metric where InterPhys does not lead is foot sliding (FS), which the authors attribute to the metric's known limitation: a very low FS can result from feet floating above the ground rather than genuine contact.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous contact modeling line initiated by Brubaker et al. [3] and PhysPT [58], which used damped spring systems to approximate contact forces, by generalizing from planar ground planes to arbitrary 3D surfaces via local surface normals and by explicitly coupling normal and tangential (friction) forces. It departs from prior diffusion-based HOI methods (InterDiff, CHIOS, InterAct) that rely on binary contact labels or penetration constraints, replacing them with a differentiable physics-based force model embedded in a two-stage transformer-diffusion pipeline inherited from OMOMO [21]. The explicit formulation of object dynamics via Euler-Lagrange equations with Newton's third law coupling is a key conceptual departure from prior work that treats the object as a fixed kinematic input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: prior generative HOI methods produce physically implausible artifacts (floating, sliding) because they lack explicit force modeling, while physics-based RL approaches are non-differentiable and hard to generalize. The proposed continuous contact model with surface-normal-conditioned forces and coupled friction is a reusable component that could benefit other differentiable physics pipelines. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets with a single dynamic object, a slightly worse foot-sliding score, and reliance on ground-truth physics parameters obtained via offline optimization for training supervision. Open questions include scalability to multiple interacting objects, articulated tools, and multi-human scenarios, which the authors acknowledge as future work.
