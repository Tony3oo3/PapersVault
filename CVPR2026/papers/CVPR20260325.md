## Problem

Vision-language models struggle with 3D spatial reasoning from limited multi-view images because they lack an intrinsic mechanism to form 3D geometric representations during reasoning. Existing approaches either rely on pure text or 2D visual cues with limited representational capacity, require densely annotated cognitive maps or BEV labels, or depend on external priors (depth estimators, point clouds, camera parameters) that restrict real-world applicability. The paper asks whether a VLM can learn to 'think with 3D'—generating geometric mental representations from images alone—without any 3D-annotated training data or auxiliary models at inference.

## Input / Output

The model receives a set of multi-view RGB images (ego-centric or arbitrary limited views) and a text question, and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trajectory that interleaves natural language with a block of 3D special tokens (e.g., <|latent_start|><|latent_pad|>...<|latent_end|>) placed at the beginning of the  segment, followed by a final text answer. The 3D latent tokens (default size 12) are the last-layer hidden states of the VLM at those positions; via a learned projector they can be mapped into VGGT's feature space and further decoded into point clouds through VGGT's DPT head, providing an interpretable visualization of the model's 'mental 3D scene.'

## Method

3DThinker introduces 3D latent tokens into the VLM's reasoning trajectory and trains them in two stages. In Stage 1 (supervised), a high-level LLM (GPT-4.1) synthesizes CoT data with 3D placeholders; the VLM is then fine-tuned so that the hidden states at those placeholder positions, after passing through a small MLP projector, align with VGGT's patch-level geometry features via a Frobenius-norm loss, while a standard cross-entropy loss preserves textual coherence. In Stage 2 (reinforcement learning), the projector is frozen and the full trajectory is optimized with GRPO using three composite rewards: a 3D-alignment reward (cosine similarity between projected latents and VGGT features), a format-compliance reward, and a binary answer-correctness reward distributed uniformly across all tokens. This outcome-driven RL refines the 3D mentaling without any intermediate-step annotation, and the key architectural insight is that the 3D tokens act as a compact geometric 'mental image' that the VLM generates intrinsically—no external depth model, point cloud, or camera parameter is needed at inference.

## Results

On MindCube-Tiny, 3DThinker-S1+S2 (InternVL3-78B) achieves 78.9% overall accuracy, surpassing the strongest closed-source model o3 (56.6%) by 22.3 points. On Ego3D-Bench, the same model reaches 73.3% average accuracy, slightly exceeding o3 (73.0%). Across all base VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL 3B–72B, InternVL3 8B–78B), 3DThinker yields consistent gains of 51.8%–108.8% on MindCube-Tiny and 18.1%–36.9% on Ego3D-Bench over the unmodified base models. On the broader spatial benchmark suite (VSI-Bench, SPBench, CV-Bench, SPAR-Bench, ViewSpatial-Bench, MMSI-Bench), 3DThinker-S1+S2 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves 64.7% average, outperforming the prior SOTA VILASR-7B (48.4%) by 16.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DThinker consistently outperforms all compared baselines. Against the base VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL and InternVL3), 3DThinker-S1+S2 improves MindCube-Tiny overall accuracy by 24.6–45.5 points and Ego3D-Bench by 10.9–13.4 points depending on model size. Among dedicated spatial-reasoning models, 3DThinker-S1+S2 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves 64.7% average across six spatial benchmarks, beating VILASR-7B (48.4%), SpaceR-7B (47.5%), Spatial-MLLM-4B (46.6%), SpatialLadder-3B (49.6%), and Video-R1 (40.5%). Compared to Ego3D-VLM, which relies on external GroundingDINO and DepthAnything modules, 3DThinker achieves superior Ego3D-Bench accuracy at every parameter scale (e.g., 50.8 vs. 44.4 on 3B, 73.3 vs. 71.8 on 78B) without any extrinsic priors at inference. Against the cognitive-map-based training strategies from MindCube (Yin et al.), 3DThinker-S1+S2 reaches 75.2% on MindCube-Tiny versus 70.7% for Plain-CGMap-FFR-Out-SFT+RL, a 4.5-point gain, while also avoiding the need for BEV annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

3DThinker extends the latent-reasoning paradigm introduced by Mirage and LVR (which use 2D visual latent tokens) to the 3D spatial domain, replacing pixel-level image generation with compact 3D geometric latents distilled from VGGT. It departs from cognitive-map-based approaches (MindCube, Ego3D-VLM) that require BEV annotations or external depth/grounding models by making 3D representation an intrinsic, annotation-free capability of the VLM itself. The two-stage training (feature-alignment SFT followed by GRPO-based RL) follows the broader RL-for-reasoning trend (DeepSeek-R1, Video-R1) but is the first to apply outcome-based RL specifically to refine 3D spatial latents within a reasoning trajectory.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling VLMs to form 3D mental representations natively during reasoning—without external priors, dense annotations, or auxiliary models at inference—3DThinker offers a practical path toward spatial intelligence in embodied and autonomous systems where only monocular multi-view images are available. The interpretability bonus (recovering point clouds from 3D latents via a projector) addresses a common criticism of latent reasoning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to dynamic/long-horizon 3D scenes, whether the VGGT distillation bottleneck limits ceiling performance, and whether the Travel Time weakness (requiring real-world scale alignment) can be resolved without explicit metric supervision.
