## Problem

MeanFlow enables stable one-step generation from scratch, but its performance degrades sharply when the proportion of non-boundary trajectory samples (r≠t) exceeds a small threshold. The root cause is a temporal scale imbalance: gradient variance grows as O(1/Δ) near the boundary (r≈t) and as O(Δ²) for long intervals (r≪t), so a fixed sampling strategy cannot simultaneously enforce boundary stability and exploit long-range trajectory information.

## Input / Output

The model receives a Gaussian noise vector x₁ ~ N(0,I) and an optional class label, and outputs a single generated image in one network forward pass (1-NFE). For ImageNet-256×256, generation occurs in the 32×32×4 latent space of a pretrained VAE; for CIFAR-10, it operates directly in 32×32 pixel space. With classifier-free guidance, the one-step output is x₀ = x₁ − u_θ(x₁, 0, 1), and a 2-NFE variant (one conditional + one unconditional pass) further improves quality without changing the architecture.

## Method

TEMF augments the MeanFlow training objective with two complementary mechanisms. First, a temporal equilibrium weighting function w(Δ) = 1/((1+λ₁Δ^β₁)·√(1+λ₂Δ²)) multiplies the per-sample loss, where Δ = t−r; the first factor suppresses the O(1/Δ) boundary-constraint variance at small intervals while the second counteracts O(Δ²) Jacobian-vector-product amplification at large intervals, yielding uniformly bounded gradient variance (Theorem 2). Second, a dynamic boundary scheduler drives the r≠t sampling ratio p(τ) from 0.1 to 0.9 via a sigmoid over normalized training progress τ, implementing a two-stage curriculum: early training emphasizes boundary stabilization (r≈t), while later training prioritizes trajectory accuracy (r≪t). Classifier-free guidance is integrated by training on a guided velocity target with a decaying velocity-mixing ratio m(τ) to reduce target variance. No architectural changes are required; the method is a drop-in modification of the MeanFlow loss and sampling schedule.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, TEMF-XL+ achieves a FID-50K of 2.62 with a single network forward pass (1-NFE), the best among all diffusion- and flow-based one-step methods. With 2-NFE sampling (one step with classifier-free guidance), FID drops further to 2.30, matching or surpassing multi-step models like DiT-XL (2.27) and SiT-XL (2.06) that require 500 NFEs. On CIFAR-10 (32×32, unconditional), TEMF achieves FID 2.81 with 1-NFE, outperforming all prior one-step methods including iCT (2.83), MeanFlow (2.92), and SoFlow (2.92).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEMF-XL+ (FID 2.62, 1-NFE) substantially outperforms all prior one-step flow/consistency methods: it beats MeanFlow-XL (3.43) by 0.81 FID, SoFlow-XL (3.35) by 0.73 FID, IMM-XL (7.77) by 5.15 FID, Shortcut-XL (10.60) by 7.98 FID, and iCT-XL (20.30) by 17.68 FID. With 2-NFE sampling, TEMF reaches FID 2.30, nearly matching DiT-XL (2.27) and approaching SiT-XL (2.06) while using 250× fewer network evaluations. On CIFAR-10, TEMF (2.81) achieves the best 1-NFE FID, edging out iCT (2.83) and clearly surpassing MeanFlow (2.92), SoFlow (2.92), sCT (2.97), IMM (3.20), and ECT (3.60). The paper also notes that multi-step autoregressive models (MAR 1.55, VAR 1.80) and GANs (StyleGAN-XL 2.30) remain competitive or superior in absolute quality, but require different architectural paradigms or more NFEs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MeanFlow [10], which introduced average velocity fields to enable stable one-step training from scratch without distillation. The key departure is a diagnosis of MeanFlow's core limitation—temporal scale imbalance in gradient contributions across different time intervals—and a two-part remedy (scale-aware loss weighting and a dynamic boundary scheduler) that modifies only the training procedure, not the architecture. It sits alongside other one-step flow/consistency methods (iCT, Shortcut, IMM, SoFlow) but distinguishes itself by providing a formal variance analysis (Theorem 1) that explains *why* the original fixed sampling strategy fails at high r≠t ratios, rather than merely proposing an empirical fix.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1-NFE FID of 2.62 (and 2.30 at 2-NFE) on ImageNet-256×256 narrows the gap between one-step and multi-step generation to near-parity with DiT-XL and SiT-XL, making single-forward-pass generation practically competitive for latency-sensitive deployment. The temporal equilibrium weighting and dynamic boundary scheduling are lightweight, architecture-agnostic modifications that could be adopted by other flow-matching or consistency-model training pipelines. Open questions include whether the hyperparameters (λ₁, λ₂, β₁, scheduling parameters) transfer cleanly across resolutions and modalities, and whether the exponential boundary decay trajectory (Theorem 3) holds under different data distributions or larger model scales.
