## Problem

Multi-frame visual trackers are vulnerable to model drift: when a tracker makes an inaccurate prediction (e.g., during occlusion or near a distractor), that error is naively aggregated into the temporal model, triggering a cascade of compounding failures. Existing temporal modeling strategies—whether online template updating or direct multi-frame feature fusion—treat all historical frames as equally reliable, providing no mechanism to distinguish high-quality predictions from noisy, corrupted ones.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 5-frame sequence per forward pass: an initial static template (z₀), three historical dynamic reference frames (z₁–z₃) selected from past predictions, and the current search region (x₀). Template and reference frames are processed at lower resolution (112×112 for the Base model, 196×196 for the Large model) while the search region uses higher resolution (224×224 or 378×378) to capture fine details. The output is a target bounding box predicted by anchor-free MLP heads, and the DTPTrack module itself is designed as a plug-and-play component that can be inserted into any existing tracker with minimal architectural changes.

## Method

DTPTrack is a lightweight, two-stage module that gates and synthesizes historical information to suppress drift. The Temporal Reliability Calibrator (TRC) first summarizes each historical frame's patch embeddings via masked average pooling, then a lightweight MLP with sigmoid predicts a per-frame reliability score (c₀ is fixed to 1.0 for the ground-truth template), producing calibrated summary vectors. The Temporal Guidance Synthesizer (TGS) converts these calibrated summaries into a compact set of dynamic prior tokens by modulating learnable base tokens with an MLP-generated signal; these tokens are prepended to the visual feature sequence so that, through self-attention, they provide high-level predictive guidance without directly contaminating raw visual features. The module is integrated into an extended LoRATv2 backbone that uses Frame-Wise Causal Attention (imposing causal masking across frames for efficiency) and Stream-Specific LoRA Adapters (per-frame low-rank adapters on a frozen DINOv2 ViT). During training, only the DTPTrack parameters, LoRA adapters, and prediction heads are learned while the backbone remains frozen, and the module adds less than 1G MACs and 1–3M parameters when plugged into existing trackers.

## Results

DTPTrack-L378 achieves a new state-of-the-art 77.5% AUC on LaSOT, 47.2% AUC on VastTrack, 80.3% AO on GOT-10k, and 86.9% AUC on TrackingNet. The lighter DTPTrack-B224 variant reaches 74.3% AUC on LaSOT and 76.5% AO on GOT-10k. On additional benchmarks, DTPTrack-L378 leads with 72.3% AUC on UAV123, 74.7% AUC on OTB2015, and 63.7% AUC on TNL2K. The module adds less than 1G MACs and 1–3M parameters when plugged into existing trackers while delivering consistent gains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its direct backbone LoRATv2-L378, DTPTrack-L378 improves LaSOT AUC by +1.4 (77.5 vs 76.1), VastTrack AUC by +3.0 (47.2 vs 44.2), and GOT-10k AO by +2.1 (80.3 vs 78.2). It surpasses the strongest recent competitor SPMTrack-L on LaSOT (77.5 vs 76.8) and GOT-10k (80.3 vs 80.0), while matching it on TrackingNet (86.9 vs 86.9). Compared to LoRAT-g378, the gains are +1.3 on LaSOT and +1.4 on GOT-10k. On VastTrack, DTPTrack-L378 leads all prior methods by a clear margin (47.2 vs 46.0 for LoRAT-g378). The plug-and-play generalizability study confirms consistent improvements when the module is added to OSTrack (+1.0 LaSOT AUC), ODTrack (+0.5), and LoRAT (+0.8), with negligible overhead (<1G MACs, 1–3M params).

## Relation to Prior Work

DTPTrack builds directly on the LoRATv2 backbone (NeurIPS 2025), inheriting its Frame-Wise Causal Attention and Stream-Specific LoRA Adapters, and adapts the dynamic reference-frame selection strategy from SPMTrack (CVPR 2025). Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm in multi-frame tracking—where all historical frames are fused or updated with equal trust—by introducing an explicit reliability-gating stage (TRC) and a decoupled prior-token synthesis stage (TGS) that sit between the raw visual features and the backbone's attention layers. The paper positions DTPTrack not as a new tracker but as an orthogonal, plug-and-play module, distinguishing it from prior work that proposes monolithic architectures (OSTrack, ODTrack, SeqTrack) or online template-updating schemes (TATrack, DiMP) that are vulnerable to irreversible corruption.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design with minimal overhead (<1G MACs, 1–3M parameters) makes DTPTrack a practical drop-in for a wide range of existing trackers, and the consistent gains across three architecturally diverse hosts (OSTrack, ODTrack, LoRAT) validate that the reliability-gating principle is orthogonal to the host's native temporal strategy. However, the SOTA margins over the strongest baselines (SPMTrack-L) are modest—0.7 AUC on LaSOT, 0.3 AO on GOT-10k—suggesting the drift-suppression benefit, while real, is incremental rather than transformative. Open questions include whether the learned reliability scores generalize to very long sequences beyond the five-frame window, whether the approach extends naturally to multi-object or language-guided tracking, and whether the frozen-backbone constraint limits the ceiling of what the module can learn.
