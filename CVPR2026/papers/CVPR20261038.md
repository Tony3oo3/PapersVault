## Problem

No single active learning strategy dominates across datasets, models, or AL cycles, as different strategies capture fundamentally different notions of data value (e.g., uncertainty vs. representativeness). Committing to one strategy risks suboptimal performance, and existing ensemble methods either require learning to switch strategies or produce inconsistent results. The paper asks how to robustly combine multiple complementary AL strategies without prior knowledge of which will perform best in a given setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large unlabeled image pool and a growing labeled pool, with features extracted by a frozen foundation model (DINOv2-ViT-S/14, DINOv3-ViT-S/16, or CLIP-ViT-B/16) and a fine-tuned classification head. It produces two outputs per AL cycle: a refined candidate pool C_R (a smaller, higher-quality subset of the unlabeled pool) and a final batch of b instances selected for annotation. The batch size b varies per dataset (10–200), and the method operates in a pool-based batch setting with 20 AL cycles per experiment.

## Method

REFINE operates in two stages per AL cycle. Stage 1 (progressive filtering) iteratively refines the unlabeled pool over R=5 rounds: in each round, all M ensemble strategies select J=10 batches from a random subsample (ratio α=0.4) of the current pool, and the union of all selected instances forms the next round's input, progressively discarding instances no strategy finds valuable. Stage 2 (coverage-based selection) then picks the final batch from C_R by greedily maximizing kernel coverage over C_R when combined with the labeled pool, implemented via UHerding to balance exploration and exploitation. The union-over-interaction design preserves instances uniquely valuable to specific strategies, while iterative refinement exponentially reduces uninformative instances (formally bounded in Theorems 1–3). The method is training-free, requires no hyperparameter tuning per dataset, and is trivially extensible with new AL strategies.

## Results

REFINE achieves the highest pairwise win rates across all 12 baselines averaged over 3 foundation models (DINOv2, DINOv3, CLIP), 5 datasets, and 10 trials. Against the strongest individual strategies, REFINE wins 85% of trials vs. BAIT and 80% vs. UHerding, while achieving 100% win rates against ensemble methods SelectAL and TAILOR, 98% vs. TCM, and 97% vs. AutoAL. As a standalone preprocessing step, progressive filtering improves AULC of any individual strategy by +0.7% to +5.4% on CIFAR-100, with weaker strategies (e.g., BAIT) benefiting most (+4.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REFINE outperforms all 8 individual AL strategies (Margin, BADGE, BAIT, TypiClust, AlfaMix, DropQuery, MaxHerding, UHerding) and all 4 ensemble methods (SelectAL, TCM, TAILOR, AutoAL) in pairwise AULC comparisons. The closest individual competitor is UHerding, which REFINE beats in 80% of trials; BAIT is second at 85%. Against ensemble baselines, REFINE dominates: 100% win rate vs. SelectAL and TAILOR, 98% vs. TCM, and 97% vs. AutoAL. A notable limitation is observed on Dopanim with CLIP, where REFINE's performance degrades around 350 labels as three ensemble strategies perform at or below random sampling. Progressive filtering as a standalone preprocessing step improves every individual strategy when applied to the refined pool, with gains of +0.7% (UHerding) to +5.4% (BAIT) AULC on CIFAR-100, confirming that the pool-cleaning mechanism is beneficial independent of the final selection strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

REFINE departs from prior ensemble AL methods (SelectAL, TAILOR, TCM, AutoAL) that attempt to learn or plan when to switch between strategies, instead proposing a training-free progressive filtering mechanism that applies all strategies simultaneously across multiple rounds. The filtering concept is generalized from single-step heuristics in DropQuery and AlfaMix to iterative, multi-strategy pool refinement, while the final selection stage repurposes coverage-based methods (UHerding, MaxHerding) on the already-refined candidate pool. The theoretical analysis (value preservation, exponential reduction of uninformative instances, monotonic concentration) is novel for this type of ensemble filtering and provides formal guarantees absent in prior ensemble AL work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that progressive filtering alone—without any ensemble selection—improves the performance of every individual AL strategy applied to the filtered pool makes it a broadly applicable preprocessing step, not just a component of REFINE. This is practically significant for practitioners who can adopt filtering as a drop-in improvement without changing their existing strategy. Key limitations include sensitivity to poorly performing strategies in the ensemble (degradation observed on Dopanim with CLIP) and the assumption that strategies are complementary rather than correlated. Open questions include whether weighting ensemble components could mitigate the degradation issue, and whether the approach extends naturally to non-classification tasks or settings where the strategy set is very large.
