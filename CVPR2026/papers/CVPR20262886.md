## Problem

Existing dehazing methods operate on RGB frames whose limited dynamic range (~60 dB) is further compressed by haze, making the restoration problem ill-posed and prone to structural loss under heavy fog. Event cameras offer 120 dB HDR and microsecond temporal resolution, yet no prior work has exploited them for dehazing, and real-world paired hazy/clean supervision is unavailable. The paper asks how to inject event-derived HDR structural cues into a dehazing model without requiring aligned real-world pairs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a hazy RGB frame and a synchronized event stream as input. The event stream is first converted into a Temporal Pyramid Representation (TPR) with L=3 hierarchical levels and M=2 time bins per level, yielding a 4D voxel tensor that is then compressed by a lightweight convolutional encoder into a low-dimensional feature map. The output is a single dehazed RGB image produced after only 15 DDIM denoising steps in the frozen VQ-VAE latent space, balancing speed and quality.

## Method

EvDehaze reformulates dehazing as conditional image generation within a latent diffusion framework. A frozen VQ-VAE encodes the hazy frame into a latent, to which Gaussian noise is added; a DDIM-based U-Net then iteratively denoises the latent back to a clean image. The key novelty is an Event-Guided Diffusion Module that injects event features (extracted via TPR + conv encoder) into selected U-Net layers through cross-attention at every denoising step, using event features as queries and U-Net features as keys/values. This allows the generative trajectory to be steered by physically grounded edge and contrast cues from events without explicitly translating events into RGB. Training is supervised with a combined L1 pixel loss and VGG-based perceptual loss (λ_pix=1.0, λ_perc=0.2) on synthetic RESIDE-ITS pairs augmented with Vid2E-simulated events, and the architecture follows the ResShift pipeline with the event-guidance module added on top.

## Results

On the SOTS benchmark, EvDehaze achieves 34.12 PSNR, 0.986 SSIM, and 0.012 LPIPS, outperforming the other diffusion-based methods IR-SDE (33.82 PSNR) and ResShift (29.06 PSNR). On NH-HAZE, EvDehaze reaches 18.43 PSNR, 0.637 SSIM, and 0.313 LPIPS, surpassing ResShift (16.26 PSNR) and IR-SDE (12.59 PSNR) while using only 122.68M parameters versus IR-SDE's 537.21M. The paper also demonstrates that adding the Event-Guided Diffusion Module to a Restormer backbone (Restormer+EGDM) improves PSNR from 38.43 to 39.12 on SOTS, confirming the value of event cues beyond the diffusion setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among diffusion-based methods, EvDehaze clearly outperforms both IR-SDE and ResShift on both benchmarks: on SOTS it gains +0.30 PSNR over IR-SDE and +5.06 PSNR over ResShift, while on NH-HAZE it gains +5.84 PSNR over IR-SDE and +2.17 PSNR over ResShift, all with substantially fewer parameters than IR-SDE (122.68M vs. 537.21M). Against supervised CNN/Transformer baselines (FFA-Net, Restormer, Dehamer), EvDehaze reports lower PSNR/SSIM, which the authors attribute to the inherent trade-off of diffusion models favoring perceptual realism over pixel-wise fidelity; the paper explicitly states its goal is not to surpass RGB-only restorers on PSNR. The Restormer+EGDM variant (39.12 PSNR on SOTS) beats the original Restormer (38.43) and FFA-Net (36.39), demonstrating that event guidance provides measurable gains even when injected into a non-diffusion transformer backbone. On NH-HAZE, Dehamer still leads in PSNR (20.66) and LPIPS (0.230), but EvDehaze achieves the best LPIPS among diffusion methods (0.313) and competitive SSIM, suggesting better perceptual quality in the generative setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the DDIM latent diffusion pipeline (Song et al., 2020) and the ResShift restoration architecture (Li et al., 2024), repurposing them for a new conditioning modality. It departs from the long line of RGB-only dehazing methods (DCP, DehazeNet, FFA-Net, DehazeFormer) that operate within the limited dynamic range of active pixel sensors, and from prior event-based enhancement work (low-light, deraining, deblurring) that relies on large-scale paired supervision. The key conceptual departure is reformulating dehazing as a conditional generation problem where sparse, high-dynamic-range event features are injected into the denoising trajectory via cross-attention, rather than being decoded into an intermediate RGB estimate or concatenated as a second input channel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By introducing the first real-world RGB-event dehazing dataset captured under severe pollution (AQI ≈ 341) and the first event-guided diffusion framework for this task, the paper opens a new modality for a problem that has been stuck on the fundamental information bottleneck of APS sensors. The ablation showing that event cues improve even a strong supervised transformer (Restormer+EGDM) suggests the HDR structural prior from events is broadly transferable across restoration architectures. Open questions include whether the frozen VQ-VAE bottleneck limits performance in highly dynamic scenes, whether the approach generalizes to other degradations (rain, snow, motion blur), and whether the perceptual-quality gains claimed over supervised methods can be validated with human studies rather than LPIPS alone.
