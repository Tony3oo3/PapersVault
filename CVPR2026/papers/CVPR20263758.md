## Problem

Existing VLM-based progress estimation methods (e.g., GVL, ROVER) rely primarily on video understanding and large context windows while neglecting the reasoning capabilities of VLMs, limiting their accuracy on complex long-horizon tasks with multi-step temporal dependencies. Additionally, processing full video trajectories through VLMs incurs prohibitive computational cost and latency, making such approaches impractical for real-time embodied deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263758/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each temporal iteration, the model receives a short video snippet (4 uniformly sampled frames from a 4-second ALFRED or 2-second Ego4D clip), a natural-language task description, and the Chain of Thought generated in the previous iteration. It autoregressively produces an updated CoT that explicitly records the task decomposition, which key subtasks have been observed, and their completion status, followed by a scalar progress estimate in [0, 100]. The historical CoT serves as a compressed memory of all prior visual evidence, so the model never reprocesses earlier frames.

## Method

R²VLM (built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) introduces a recurrent reasoning loop: at iteration t, the model takes (task description, current snippet v_t, historical CoT c_{t-1}) and outputs an updated CoT c_t and progress p_t, where the CoT can dynamically merge, split, or reorder subtask steps as new visual evidence arrives. Training is two-stage: first, SFT on ~250K automatically generated (snippet, CoT, progress) tuples from ALFRED and Ego4D expert trajectories (with distractor task descriptions to prevent shortcut learning); second, multi-turn RL using PPO (chosen over GRPO because the multi-turn input c_{t-1} differs across trajectories, violating GRPO's identical-input assumption). The RL reward is a composite of format, bin (coarse step-level correctness), MAE (fine-grained accuracy), improvement (error reduction relative to the previous turn, normalized to [-1, 0.8] to penalize regression), and finish (correct 100% detection) components, with the bin and MAE terms multiplied to ensure fine-grained reward only when the coarse prediction is correct.

## Results

On ALFRED, R²VLM (7B) achieves a progress MAE of 2.19, step-level bin accuracy of 0.917, and task-completion accuracy of 0.988, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline GVL-SFT (p_mae=6.21, bin=0.830, acc=0.930) and all API-based models (GPT-5: p_mae=18.35; Gemini-2.5-Pro: p_mae=16.27). On the more challenging real-world Ego4D benchmark, R²VLM attains p_mae=19.25, Δp_mae=3.88, bin=0.318, and acc=0.761, again leading all 12 baselines. The recurrent reasoning design also yields constant inference time regardless of video length, unlike full-video approaches whose latency grows linearly.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R²VLM (7B) achieves state-of-the-art results on all four metrics across both ALFRED and Ego4D, outperforming every one of the 12 baselines. On ALFRED, the closest competitor is GVL-SFT (p_mae=6.21), which R²VLM surpasses by 2.8× in progress MAE (2.19 vs. 6.21) and by 8.7 points in task-completion accuracy (0.988 vs. 0.930); API-based models like GPT-5 (p_mae=18.35) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (16.27) are 8–9× worse in progress MAE. On the harder Ego4D benchmark, R²VLM reduces p_mae from 25.04 (GPT-5) to 19.25 and from 26.80 (GVL-SFT) to 19.25, a 23–28% improvement, while also leading in Δp_mae (3.88 vs. 6.48 for ROVER) and bin accuracy (0.318 vs. 0.259 for GPT-5). Notably, R²VLM's 7B model outperforms the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (p_mae 24.88 on ALFRED, 26.88 on Ego4D) and InternVL3-78B (30.54 / 28.16), demonstrating that the recurrent reasoning framework and task-specific RL training are more effective than simply scaling model size. The only baseline competitive on a single metric is ROVER on Ego4D Δp_mae (6.48 vs. R²VLM's 3.88), where R²VLM still wins by 40%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior VLM-based progress estimators (GVL, ROVER) that rely on large context windows and in-context learning to process full video trajectories, instead introducing a recurrent reasoning architecture where an evolving Chain of Thought serves as compressed historical memory while only local video snippets are processed at each step. It also extends the reward-modeling literature beyond IRL and hierarchical decomposition by learning task decomposition and step-level reasoning end-to-end through PPO with multi-component rewards (bin, MAE, improvement, finish). The streaming video input scheme draws from recent online video LLM work, but the key novelty is coupling it with iterative CoT updates and RL-based training specifically for progress estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The recurrent CoT design offers a practical solution to the computational bottleneck of processing long egocentric videos with VLMs, enabling near real-time progress tracking with constant inference cost regardless of task duration. The validated downstream applications (dense reward modeling for RL, progress-enhanced policy learning, proactive assistance) position the model as a general-purpose progress oracle for embodied agents. However, the significant performance gap between ALFRED (p_mae 2.19) and Ego4D (p_mae 19.25) highlights that robustness to real-world noise, varied execution paths, and distractors remains an open challenge. Future work could explore whether the recurrent CoT mechanism generalizes to even longer horizons, multi-agent settings, or tasks with highly variable subtask structures.
