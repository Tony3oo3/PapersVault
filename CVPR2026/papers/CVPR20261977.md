## Problem

Existing automatic color grading methods rely on patch-wise CNN recoloring or fixed filter banks, which struggle to generalize across diverse creative intents and cannot unify text-guided and reference-guided grading in a single framework. Moreover, the adversarial training paradigm commonly used for perceptual quality is unstable, sensitive to hyperparameters, and difficult to scale for consistent improvement.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261977/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a query image plus either a reference image (for photorealistic style transfer) or a free-form text instruction (for instruction-guided grading) and produces a 3×32×32×32 3D-LUT as a sequence of 64 discrete tokens drawn from a 256-entry codebook. The LUT is decoded into a continuous volumetric tensor and applied non-destructively to the query image, yielding the graded output. Inference runs in ~1.2 s per request (under 300 ms with vLLM), making it suitable for real-time editing pipelines.

## Method

AceTone formulates color grading as autoregressive LUT-token generation by a vision-language model (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) rather than adversarial image regression. A 3D VQ-VAE tokenizer with cascaded convolutional encoders/decoders compresses a 3×32×32×32 LUT into a 4×4×4 latent grid of 64 discrete tokens (codebook size 256), achieving ΔE < 2 reconstruction fidelity with only ~4M parameters. Training proceeds in three phases: (1) generative pretraining on ~800K image-LUT triplets to learn the conditional LUT-token distribution, (2) supervised fine-tuning on paired style-transfer and instruction-graded data, and (3) GRPO reinforcement learning that samples G LUT hypotheses per prompt and optimizes a composite reward of CIEDE2000-based color similarity and DeQA aesthetic score, with KL regularization against the SFT policy to prevent drift.

## Results

On photorealistic style transfer, AceTone achieves LPIPS of 0.09 on PST-50 and 0.11 on AceTone-Bench[Transfer], improving LPIPS by up to 50% over the strongest baseline SA-LUT (0.16 and 0.22 respectively). It also leads on PSNR (24.26 vs. 21.64 on PST-50; 22.49 vs. 18.14 on Transfer), ΔE (7.26 vs. 9.01; 8.98 vs. 13.10), and aesthetic score (3.29 vs. 3.07; 3.57 vs. 3.33). On instruction-guided grading, AceTone attains LPIPS 0.22 and aesthetic score 3.54, outperforming the best diffusion-based baseline Qwen-Image-Edit (LPIPS 0.33, Aes. 3.19) while running ~100× faster (1.2 s vs. 2 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AceTone outperforms all four PST baselines across every metric on both PST-50 and AceTone-Bench[Transfer]. The closest competitor, SA-LUT (ICCV-25), is surpassed by AceTone by 0.22 in PSNR, 0.07 in LPIPS (44% lower), 1.75 in ΔE, and 0.22 in aesthetic score on PST-50; the gap widens on the larger Transfer benchmark where LPIPS drops from 0.22 to 0.11 (50% improvement) and ΔE from 13.10 to 8.98. Older recoloring methods (WCT², ModFlow) lag further behind, with LPIPS roughly 2–3× higher. On instruction-guided grading, AceTone beats the diffusion-based Qwen-Image-Edit (LPIPS 0.33→0.22, Aes. 3.19→3.54) and the agent-based JarvisArt (LPIPS 0.31→0.22) while being ~33× faster than Qwen-Image-Edit and ~33× faster than JarvisArt. In the user study, AceTone receives the highest preference rate (41.4% for PST, 46.9% for IGG) and best average rank (1.75 and 1.69), confirming quantitative gains translate to perceived quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

AceTone departs from the dominant LUT-based grading paradigm (Neural Preset, SA-LUT, NILUT) that treats color transformation as a regression or weighted-combination problem over fixed or learnable filter banks, and from patch-wise recoloring methods (WCT, ModFlow) that operate directly on image pixels. Instead, it reframes grading as autoregressive discrete token generation over a VQ-VAE-compressed 3D-LUT, leveraging a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) as the generative backbone. The RL alignment stage draws on the GRPO/Flow-GRPO lineage for preference optimization, but applies it to a novel output space (LUT tokens) with composite color-similarity and aesthetic rewards rather than image-level scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing interpretable, non-destructive 3D-LUTs rather than pixel-level edits, AceTone integrates directly into professional post-production pipelines and enables a unified workflow for both reference-based and text-guided grading—something prior methods handle in separate, incompatible systems. The AceTone-800K dataset and dual benchmarks (Transfer/Instruct) fill a notable gap in reproducible evaluation for conditional color grading. Open questions remain around local (spatially-varying) color shifts that global LUTs cannot capture, VLM color-perception limitations that degrade instruction annotation quality, and extension to video or interactive grading.
