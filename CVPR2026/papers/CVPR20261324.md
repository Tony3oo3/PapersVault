## Problem

Existing MLLM-based 3D object arrangement methods are limited to single-step edits, lacking the ability to decompose complex instructions into coherent multi-step sequences with physically valid intermediate states. Additionally, these approaches struggle with weak visual grounding (linking language-based edits to precise 3D outcomes) and have no mechanism to recover from errors that compound across iterative steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261324/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single rendered image of a 3D scene (from a fixed camera in Blender) and a free-form text instruction that can range from abstract goals (e.g., 'Create a dining space') to detailed per-step commands. It outputs a sequence of single-object 3D poses (translation and rotation) executed iteratively, where each intermediate state must be collision-free and floating-free. The Planner additionally outputs a normalized 2D pixel coordinate as spatial grounding for each step, and the entire pipeline operates within Blender via MCP-based Python APIs.

## Method

VULCAN employs a collaborative multi-agent architecture with three specialized MLLM agents (Gemini-2.5-pro) operating in a Plan-Execute-Evaluate loop: the Planner uses global visual history to generate a concrete action with a 2D target coordinate, the Executor grounds this into 3D via MCP-based visual probing tools (LISTOBJECTSINAREA, RAYPROBE, RENDERWITHHIGHLIGHT), formulates geometric constraints (CLOSETOPIX, CONTACT, NOOVERHANG, DISTANCE, FACETO, ROTATE), and solves for a collision-free pose using an AdamW-based optimizer with stochastic pixel perturbation; the Evaluator performs consensus-based visual quality scoring across multiple agents. An adaptive backtracking search algorithm maintains an anchor step that resets to half the current depth on failure, enabling recovery from dead ends in the exponentially branching multi-step search space. Visual annotations (pixel coordinate grids and action arrows) are overlaid on rendered images to improve spatial grounding for all agents.

## Results

On a benchmark of 25 scenes (111 unit tasks) sourced from BlenderKit, InfiniGen, and BlenderGym, VULCAN achieves 0% collision rate and 0% floating rate, compared to 45.9–63.1% collision and 22.5–77.4% floating for the three baselines. It also attains the highest plausibility score (3.796/4) and instruction-consistency score (3.592/4), surpassing the best baseline (FirePlace: 3.515 and 3.135 respectively). In a 30-subject human study, VULCAN is preferred over all baselines with win rates of 54.4–70.0% across physical plausibility, visual plausibility, and instruction-following criteria.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VULCAN decisively outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against BlenderAlchemy (script-based executor-evaluator loop), VULCAN eliminates all collisions (0% vs. 63.1%) and floating artifacts (0% vs. 67.6%) while improving plausibility by 0.43 points and consistency by 0.82 points. Against FirePlace (constraint-based single-step solver), VULCAN reduces collision rate from 51.3% to 0% and floating rate from 22.5% to 0%, with gains of 0.28 in plausibility and 0.46 in consistency. Against the simplest baseline Blender-MCP (raw tool calls without specialized agents or solver), VULCAN cuts collision rate by 45.9 points and floating rate by 77.4 points. The human study confirms these margins: VULCAN wins 70.0% of pairwise comparisons against BlenderAlchemy on physical plausibility and 58.0% against FirePlace, with no baseline achieving a majority preference over VULCAN on any criterion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of MLLM-based 3D scene editing (BlenderAlchemy, FirePlace, ScanEdit, SceneCraft) from single-step edits to iterative, multi-step object arrangement. It departs from prior approaches that either feed raw 3D data or simplified scene descriptions to the MLLM by instead equipping agents with MCP-based visual probing APIs (ray casting, object listing, highlighted rendering) and a constraint-based collision-free solver, shifting the interaction from brittle code generation to function-level tool calls. The multi-agent Planner–Executor–Evaluator decomposition and adaptive backtracking search are novel architectural choices not present in prior single-step pipelines, drawing on classic search algorithms and the emerging MCP tool-calling paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that combining tool-augmented MLLMs with constraint solvers and multi-agent coordination can achieve zero collision and zero floating artifacts on multi-step 3D arrangement tasks, a capability absent in all prior single-step methods. This positions MCP-based visual tooling as a practical bridge between language-level reasoning and precise 3D manipulation, a pattern likely to transfer to other interactive 3D editing and robotics planning settings. Key limitations include reliance on a single fixed camera view (preventing occluded placements), dependence on a specific frontier MLLM (Gemini-2.5-pro), and a small 25-scene benchmark, leaving open questions about scalability to larger scenes, multi-view reasoning, and generalization across MLLM backbones.
