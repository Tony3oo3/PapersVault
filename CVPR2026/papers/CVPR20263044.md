## Problem

Existing video evaluation benchmarks are fragmented across single tasks (understanding, generation, or editing), restricted to single-shot clips, and often rely on copyrighted web videos that risk data contamination. No prior benchmark systematically evaluates cinematic quality dimensions across all video tasks simultaneously, and evaluation metrics are task-specific and incompatible, preventing fair cross-task comparison of unified video foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263044/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 200 human-validated multi-shot videos (100 single-shot, 100 multi-shot averaging 3.72 shots) at 720×480 resolution, each paired with dimension-complete captions, multi-format editing instructions, and 864 reference images spanning subject, style, and scene categories. UniV-Eval accepts any flexible combination of source video, reference image, and text instruction as input, and outputs a structured per-shot weakness checklist decomposed into 9 major categories (21 sub-dimensions) plus aggregated scores along 6 evaluation dimensions, rather than a single scalar metric.

## Method

UniV-Eval is a dynamically adaptive agentic evaluation system that first mechanically segments long videos into clips, then uses PySceneDetect to decompose each clip into shot-level units. A shot_classification agent aligns reference images and user instructions with their corresponding shots, producing shot-level input tuples (video, image, text). A shot_evaluation agent then performs per-category comparisons between the model output and the input tuple across 21 fine-grained sub-dimensions, generating a structured weakness checklist. An evaluation_score agent aggregates these diagnostic signals into final quantitative scores. The system also introduces a novel V2V reconstruction task where the model must first caption a video and then regenerate it from its own captions, enabling direct diagnosis of perception-generation coupling by comparing the reconstructed output against the original.

## Results

On UniVBench, no single model dominates all six tasks, confirming the 'unification gap.' Gemini 2.5 Pro leads video understanding (V2T) at 54.1% average across eight cinematic dimensions, while Seedance-1.0-Pro tops text-to-video generation (T2V) at 77.9%. For the novel video reconstruction (V2V) task, Wan2.1-VACE-14B achieves the best score of 62.7%, substantially outperforming CogVideoX-1.5-5B (20.7%) and Omni-Video (47.9%). The 'Action' dimension is consistently the weakest across all tasks and models, with understanding scores as low as 6.4% (Qwen3-VL-30B), while generative models excel on stylistic dimensions like Color (up to 84.8%) and Relative Position (up to 91.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 10+ models across six tasks, revealing clear task-specific specializations rather than a single dominant system. In video understanding (V2T), Gemini 2.5 Pro (54.1%) and Seed 1.6 (42.8%) substantially outperform open-source unified models like Showo-2 (16.3%) and Qwen3-VL-30B (17.6%), with a gap of over 30 points. For T2V generation, Seedance-1.0-Pro (77.9%) and Wan2.2-14B (74.9%) lead, while CoDi-2 (40.1%) trails by over 35 points, indicating that in-context generation architectures struggle with multi-shot cinematic fidelity. In the novel V2V reconstruction task, Wan2.1-VACE-14B (62.7%) significantly outperforms Omni-Video (47.9%) and CogVideoX-1.5-5B (20.7%), suggesting that editing-capable models retain more visual information through the understand-then-generate pipeline. Notably, CogVideoX achieves a surprisingly high T2V score (65.7%) driven by strong Color (82.0%) and Lighting (83.2) scores, yet collapses to 20.7% on V2V, exposing a severe perception-generation coupling failure. The consistent weakness on the Action dimension across all models (ranging from 6.4% to 74.3%) highlights that temporal dynamics remain the hardest aspect of unified video modeling, regardless of architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation paradigm (Li et al., EMNLP 2025) from single-scalar scoring into a decomposed, shot-level, multi-dimensional agentic system (UniV-Eval) that operates consistently across six video tasks. It departs from the fragmented benchmark landscape—where understanding, generation, and editing benchmarks each use disjoint metrics, single-shot clips, and often copyrighted web videos—by unifying all three under a common 8-dimension/21-sub-dimension cinematic taxonomy and a shared multi-shot, copyright-free dataset. The V2V reconstruction task is a novel diagnostic that no prior benchmark provides, explicitly coupling perception and generation into a single measurable pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniVBench is the first framework that can attribute a unified video model's failure to either its perception or generation component, which is essential for guiding training of truly integrated architectures. The quantitative 'unification gap'—no model currently excels across all six tasks, and V2V scores are consistently lower than T2V scores—provides a concrete target for future work. Key limitations include the modest 200-video scale, reliance on LLM-as-a-judge (which carries known self-preference biases), and the fact that all videos are AI-generated rather than natural footage, potentially limiting ecological validity. An open question is whether the 85% human-agreement rate holds as the benchmark scales and as evaluation criteria evolve with model capabilities.
