## Problem

End-to-end 3D reconstruction methods designed for a single camera and trajectory degrade in multi-agent settings, suffering from unstable tracking, excessive memory consumption, scale drift, and frequent loop-closure failures. Existing multi-agent approaches either maintain independent maps with severe inconsistencies or centralize computation on a server that saturates GPU/CPU resources while still relying on local geometric heuristics that do not enforce global topological consistency across agents.

## Input / Output

Each agent streams its own RGB image sequence, which is tokenized and processed incrementally via a causal attention front-end with a per-agent KV-cache storing previously generated point clouds and map tokens. The system outputs a globally aligned dense 3D point cloud, per-agent camera poses (in Sim(3)), and a scene-level topology skeleton; agents communicate only compact topological summaries (edge residual descriptors and node-level aggregates) rather than raw observations, keeping per-agent memory bounded even as the sequence grows.

## Method

TOPOMA builds a scene-level topology skeleton (a maximum spanning tree with candidate back edges derived from persistent-homology-based topological distances) that guides all three core modules. The front-end uses causal incremental attention to produce local point clouds and map tokens, regularized by pairwise topological distances; the back-end applies global multi-head attention over a shared token pool with topological similarity as an attention bias (Eq. 6), regressing pose corrections and fusion weights. Decentralized loop closure gates candidate loop edges on both an appearance score and a geodesic distance on the skeleton, then refines poses via a multi-modal residual energy (depth, color, pointmap, topology) unrolled as a few gradient steps inside the attention block. Residual transport compresses per-sample residuals into permutation-invariant edge descriptors, aggregates them into node descriptors, and propagates them along the skeleton toward a designated anchor agent using geodesic-distance-weighted message passing, concentrating global corrections on one agent while others retain only local summaries. The total objective combines the pose-refinement energy with a residual-transport consistency loss, all optimized end-to-end in a fully distributed architecture where no central server is required.

## Results

On KITTI tracking (Table 1), TOPOMA achieves an average RMSE of 22.51 m and Mean ATE of 18.32 m, outperforming the best single-agent baseline VGGT-Long (24.36 / 19.68) and dramatically surpassing VGGT-SLAM (94.23 / 77.26, with tracking lost on 3 of 5 sequences). On ScanNet reconstruction (Table 2), TOPOMA attains the lowest average Depth L1 of 12.19 cm and Accuracy of 11.10 cm, beating VGGT-SLAM (13.80 / 12.60) and MASt3R-SLAM (15.71 / 12.58). On Replica multi-agent tracking (Table 3), TOPOMA achieves 0.53 cm average RMSE, roughly 2× better than the best multi-agent baseline MAGiC-SLAM (1.06 cm) and 2.2× better than CP-SLAM (1.39 cm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TOPOMA outperforms all five single-agent baselines across every dataset and metric. On KITTI, the gap is most dramatic against VGGT-SLAM, which loses tracking on 3 of 5 sequences (RMSE 94.23 m vs. TOPOMA's 22.51 m), and against MASt3R-SLAM (84.48 m), while the closest competitor VGGT-Long (24.36 m) is still ~8% worse in average RMSE. On ScanNet reconstruction, TOPOMA reduces average Depth L1 from 13.80 cm (VGGT-SLAM) and 15.71 cm (MASt3R-SLAM) to 12.19 cm, and Accuracy from 12.60 / 12.58 cm to 11.10 cm. Among multi-agent baselines on Replica, TOPOMA (0.53 cm avg RMSE) nearly halves the error of MAGiC-SLAM (1.06 cm) and more than doubles the margin over CP-SLAM (1.39 cm), while also beating the best single-agent method VGGT-SLAM (1.17 cm). In the residual-transport ablation, MNE-SLAM [10] achieves 16.71 cm ATE versus TOPOMA's 10.48 cm, confirming that topology-guided distributed transport substantially outperforms prior multi-agent neural SLAM in global consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

TOPOMA sits at the intersection of two lines of work: learning-based pointmap SLAM (MASt3R-SLAM, SLAM3R, TTT3R, VGGT-Long, VGGT-SLAM), which achieves strong single-agent dense reconstruction but degrades under multi-agent conditions, and multi-agent collaborative reconstruction (MAGiC-SLAM, CP-SLAM, MNE-SLAM), which addresses collaboration but relies on centralized fusion or heavy communication. Rather than parallelizing a single-agent pipeline or centralizing on a server, TOPOMA repurposes persistent-homology-based topological descriptors as a structural backbone that simultaneously guides transformer attention, gates loop-closure acceptance, and defines the message-passing graph for residual transport. This topology-skeleton-as-attention-bias and topology-skeleton-as-communication-graph design is the paper's core architectural departure from both the pointmap-SLAM and multi-agent-SLAM literatures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fully distributed, topology-guided architecture is practically relevant for large-scale VR/AR, robot swarms, and digital-twin deployments where a central server is infeasible and bandwidth is constrained. The paper demonstrates that lightweight topological summaries suffice for inter-agent consistency, reducing memory and communication overhead relative to centralized baselines. However, the framework is explicitly limited to static or near-static scenes, and the authors acknowledge that strong dynamic-object interference degrades performance; incorporating explicit dynamic modeling into the topology skeleton and residual transport is left as future work. Open questions include scalability beyond four agents, robustness in highly dynamic environments, and whether the persistent-homology descriptors generalize to scenes with very different topological complexity.
