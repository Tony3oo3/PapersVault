## Problem

C-Flat, a CL-friendly optimizer that jointly optimizes zeroth-order sharpness and first-order flatness to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, requires three additional backward passes per iteration, making it roughly 3–4× slower than vanilla SGD. This overhead becomes prohibitive in long task sequences and large-scale pre-trained-model-based continual learning, creating a tension between pursuing flatter minima and maintaining practical training efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261430/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

C-Flat Turbo is a plug-and-play optimizer replacement that operates on standard RGB image classification data (CIFAR-100 at 32×32, CUB-200, ImageNet-R, ObjectNet) and produces class labels, with no changes to model architecture or data pipeline. It can be dropped into any CL method (iCaRL, MEMO, L2P, Ranpac, EASE) by swapping the per-iteration gradient computation, and is evaluated on both from-scratch training (ResNet-18/34) and frozen-backbone fine-tuning (ViT-B/16-IN1K) settings.

## Method

C-Flat Turbo exploits the observation that the component of the first-order flatness gradient orthogonal to the proxy-model gradient (g_vf) varies far more slowly than the empirical or sharpness gradients, enabling the optimizer to cache g_vf and reuse it for k−1 surrogate steps, approximating the flatness update as g_f ≈ g_0 + β·‖g_0‖/‖g_vf‖·g_vf without recomputing the second perturbed backward pass. A stage-wise linear scheduler (k_t = k_0 + 10·t/N) progressively increases the shortcut interval across tasks, reflecting the empirical trend that sharpness and flatness gradients stabilize as learning proceeds. An adaptive trigger based on an exponential moving average of ‖g_0‖² activates the flatness regularization only when the proxy gradient norm exceeds a dynamic threshold (μ_f + σ_f), falling back to vanilla SGD otherwise. Together, these mechanisms reduce the per-iteration cost from three extra backward passes to a fraction of that, yielding 1.0×–1.25× speedup over C-Flat while matching or improving accuracy.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 B0_Inc10 with ViT-B/16-IN1K, C-Flat Turbo improves average accuracy over C-Flat across all five CL methods (e.g., iCaRL: 79.82 vs. 79.72; MEMO: 83.02 vs. 82.61; EASE: 92.36 vs. 92.05) while roughly doubling training throughput (e.g., EASE: 102.74 vs. 44.25 Img/s, i.e., 61.6% vs. 26.5% of vanilla speed). On the harder domain-shift benchmarks (CUB-200, ImageNet-R, ObjectNet), C-Flat Turbo consistently matches or exceeds C-Flat, with the largest gains on ObjectNet (iCaRL: 50.49 vs. 49.59 Avg; MEMO: 57.52 vs. 56.50 Avg). When trained from scratch with ResNet-18/34, C-Flat Turbo boosts iCaRL last accuracy by 1.61% (R18) and MEMO last accuracy by 3.05% (R18) over C-Flat.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-Flat Turbo is applied as a drop-in optimizer on top of five CL methods (iCaRL, MEMO, L2P, Ranpac, EASE) and consistently outperforms the original C-Flat optimizer on both accuracy and speed across all four benchmarks. Compared to C-Flat, C-Flat Turbo gains 0.1–1.0% average accuracy (e.g., +0.31 on iCaRL/CIFAR-100, +0.69 on MEMO/IN-R, +0.31 on EASE/CIFAR-100) while roughly doubling throughput (e.g., 45.89 vs. 19.72 Img/s for iCaRL; 102.74 vs. 44.25 for EASE). Against the vanilla optimizer (no sharpness/flatness regularization), C-Flat Turbo still provides meaningful accuracy gains (e.g., +1.91 Avg for iCaRL on CIFAR-100, +0.45 Avg for EASE) at only ~40% speed cost, whereas C-Flat costs ~70–75% speed. In the optimizer comparison (Table 3), C-Flat Turbo surpasses both SAM and LookSAM in accuracy on L2P (89.57 vs. 88.18/88.35 Avg) and EASE (91.75 vs. 91.36/90.89 Avg), while also being faster than SAM (65.50 vs. 56.60 Img/s on L2P). LookSAM, which reuses only the zeroth-order sharpness direction, degrades EASE accuracy (90.89 vs. 91.16 vanilla), whereas C-Flat Turbo's combined sharpness+flatness shortcut preserves or improves accuracy. The one case where C-Flat Turbo does not strictly beat C-Flat is L2P on CUB-200 (74.12 vs. 74.36 Avg), a minor 0.24% drop likely due to the large domain gap and limited prompt capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends C-Flat (Bian et al., NeurIPS 2024), a CL-tailored optimizer that jointly optimizes zeroth-order sharpness (SAM) and first-order flatness (GAM) to find uniformly low-loss regions. The key conceptual bridge is LookSAM (Liu et al., CVPR 2022), which showed that the component of SAM gradients orthogonal to the empirical gradient (g_vs) varies slowly and can be cached. C-Flat Turbo generalizes this decomposition to the first-order flatness term, identifying an analogous direction-invariant component (g_vf) that is even more stable than g_vs, enabling more aggressive reuse of flatness gradients. The adaptive triggering mechanism also draws on AE-SAM's EMA-based thresholding, extended here to the flatness norm rather than sharpness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners using C-Flat in long task sequences or large PTM-based CL pipelines, the ~2× speedup over C-Flat (while matching or slightly exceeding accuracy) makes flatness-aware optimization substantially more practical. The plug-and-play design means the speedup transfers across memory-based, expansion-based, and PTM-based CL methods without architectural changes. However, C-Flat Turbo remains roughly 2× slower than vanilla SGD, and the evaluation is confined to class-incremental image benchmarks; extending to vision-language models, reinforcement learning, or scenarios where the flatness computation itself can be further approximated (e.g., lower-rank Hessian estimates) are natural next steps.
