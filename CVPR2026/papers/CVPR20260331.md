## Problem

Unsupervised image deraining suffers from under-constrained optimization due to the absence of paired supervision, making it difficult for networks to converge reliably across diverse rain patterns. While high-quality deraining results occasionally emerge during training, existing methods do not exploit these intermediate outputs to regularize learning or compact the optimization space. No prior work has leveraged VLM-based perceptual quality scores as dynamic rewards to guide unsupervised deraining optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unpaired sets of rainy and clean RGB images (randomly cropped to 256×256 patches) without requiring any corresponding pairs, and produces a single derained RGB image per rainy input. The VLM-based DACLIP-IQA scorer is invoked only during training to evaluate intermediate derained outputs and update the reward pool; at inference, only the NAFNet derainer is needed, yielding the lowest FLOPs (16.3G) and fastest inference time (0.11s) among compared methods.

## Method

RGSUD is a two-stage unpaired deraining framework built on a GAN-based cycle (clean→rainy→clean) with NAFNet as the derainer, a U-Net-based Degradation Estimation Module (DEM) for rain extraction, a ResNet generator, and a PatchGAN discriminator. In Stage 1 (Reward Recycling), the network trains on unpaired data while a VLM-based DACLIP-IQA scorer dynamically evaluates each derained output and replaces the stored reward for that sample if the new score is higher; rewards are detached from the computation graph. In Stage 2 (Self-Reinforcement), the accumulated rewards are injected into the gradient flow in two ways: (i) they serve as clean feature representations inside the DEM, bypassing U-Net extraction to yield more accurate rain residuals for synthesizing higher-quality pseudo-paired data, and (ii) they form a self-reinforcement loss L_re = ||B_rw − B_r||²_F that supplies the missing data-consistency term in the MAP formulation, constraining the optimization space toward the natural-image distribution. The total loss combines adversarial losses, PSNR/SSIM reconstruction, and the reward-driven L_re with tunable weights (λ₁=1.0, λ₂=0.8), and the SR strategy is shown to be plug-and-play, improving other unsupervised derainers (DCD-GAN, CSUD, DerainCycleGAN) by 0.4–0.7 dB PSNR when applied as a post-training refinement.

## Results

On the synthetic paired Rain100L dataset, RGSUD achieves 34.41 dB PSNR and 0.967 SSIM, outperforming the best unsupervised baseline CSUD (33.28 dB / 0.954) by 1.13 dB. On the real-world paired RealRain1K-L dataset, RGSUD reaches 32.88 dB PSNR and 0.955 SSIM, surpassing CSUD (32.71 / 0.959) in PSNR. On unpaired real datasets (SIRR and Real3000), RGSUD achieves the best no-reference IQA scores across MUSIQ, DACLIP-IQA, and NIMA, e.g., 61.64 MUSIQ and 0.193 NIMA on Real3000, compared to 60.92 and 0.170 for CSUD. The method also achieves the lowest FLOPs (16.3G) and fastest inference time (0.11s) among compared unsupervised methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RGSUD outperforms all unsupervised deraining baselines on every evaluated dataset. Against the strongest unsupervised competitor CSUD [15], RGSUD gains +1.13 dB PSNR on Rain100L, +0.72 dB on SPA-Data, and +0.64 dB on Night-Rain, while also achieving higher SSIM across all datasets. Compared to DCD-GAN [7], the gains are larger: +2.59 dB on Rain100L and +2.39 dB on RealRain1K-L. The retrained unpaired restoration methods (Fr-Diff [56], TP-Diff [57], DehazeSB [40]) are consistently outperformed by 1.3–1.8 dB PSNR on synthetic datasets. Against supervised methods, RGSUD remains 3–8 dB below NeRD-Rain-S [9] and DRSformer [8] on paired benchmarks (e.g., 34.41 vs. 42.00 dB on Rain100L), which is expected given the absence of paired supervision. However, on unpaired real datasets (SIRR, Real3000), RGSUD surpasses all unsupervised methods in no-reference IQA metrics (MUSIQ, DACLIP-IQA, NIMA), and the paper notes that supervised methods like NeRD-Rain-S and DRSformer degrade significantly when applied directly to real-world rainy data without retraining. The SR strategy is also shown to be plug-and-play: adding it to DCD-GAN yields +0.41 dB and to CSUD yields +0.68 dB on Rain100L, confirming broad applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GAN-based unpaired deraining paradigm established by CycleGAN and its deraining-specific variants (DerainCycleGAN, DCD-GAN, NLCL, CSUD) by introducing a VLM-based reward recycling mechanism that selects high-quality intermediate outputs during training as implicit supervision. The key conceptual departure is borrowing the reward-guided optimization idea from reinforcement learning and instantiating it with DACLIP-IQA perceptual scores, which serve both to refine pseudo-paired data synthesis (via the Degradation Estimation Module) and to construct a self-reinforcement loss that compensates for the missing data-consistency term in unsupervised learning. Unlike prior unsupervised methods that rely solely on adversarial or contrastive regularization, RGSUD explicitly injects a quality-anchored reconstruction signal, compacting the optimization space toward perceptually faithful outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that VLM-based perceptual quality scores can function as effective training-time rewards in unsupervised image restoration, opening a path for leveraging large pretrained models to guide optimization without paired data. The plug-in compatibility of the SR strategy across multiple derainer architectures (NAFNet, Restormer, DRSformer, NeRD-Rain) and unsupervised baselines (DCD-GAN, CSUD) suggests broad applicability beyond a single framework. However, the approach depends on the initial derainer producing sufficiently good intermediate results (NLCL shows only marginal gains), DACLIP-IQA adds training-time computational overhead, and the two-stage protocol introduces scheduling complexity. Open questions include whether the reward recycling idea transfers to video deraining, night-time scenarios, or other coupled degradations, and whether lighter-weight IQA models can match DACLIP-IQA's reward quality.
