## Problem

Unified multimodal models have advanced in visual understanding and grounding but remain largely unable to generate images that satisfy explicit spatial layout constraints, especially for multi-instance composition with identity preservation. Existing layout-controlled generation methods rely on task-specific fusion modules or region-aware U-Net modifications that are architecturally incompatible with Transformer-based unified frameworks, and no large-scale dataset jointly provides instance-level layouts, subject correspondences, and diverse multi-instance configurations for training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes natural-language prompts in which each subject phrase is immediately followed by a normalized bounding box token (e.g., `<bbox>[0.109, 0.297, 0.607, 0.87]</bbox>`), optionally interleaved with reference images for identity-conditioned composition. Coordinates are discretized to three decimal places in [0,1] and treated as ordinary text tokens within the same sequence as semantic words and visual tokens. The output is a single generated image in which all specified instances appear at their designated spatial positions while preserving reference identity when provided.

## Method

ConsistCompose builds on Bagel's Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture with separate understanding and generation experts sharing unified self-attention over a concatenated token sequence of text, ViT features, and VAE latents. The core LELG paradigm encodes layout as Instance Coordinate Binding Prompts (ICBP), inserting normalized bounding boxes directly into the linguistic stream so that spatial grounding is learned implicitly through self-attention without any layout-specific encoder or geometric loss. A Coordinate-CFG mechanism at inference time amplifies the velocity difference between coordinate-conditioned and coordinate-unconditioned predictions, with a normalization step to stabilize guidance magnitude. Training combines Flow Matching for image generation and autoregressive next-token prediction for understanding in a two-stage schedule (alignment followed by hybrid SFT), supervised entirely by the 3.4M-sample ConsistCompose3M dataset that pairs ICBP-formatted captions with layout annotations and reference images.

## Results

On COCO-Position, ConsistCompose achieves an average Instance Success Ratio of 92.6% and Image Success Ratio of 76.1%, surpassing the second-best baseline (InstanceDiffusion) by 4.8% and 10.6% respectively. Position Accuracy reaches mIoU 85.3% and AP 70.9%, a 7.2% mIoU gain and 13.7% AP improvement over InstanceDiffusion. On MS-Bench, the method attains DINO 0.660, mIoU 0.889, and AP 0.789, leading all baselines on spatial and identity metrics simultaneously. General multimodal understanding (MMBench 81.4, GenEval 0.88) remains on par with the Bagel backbone, confirming no capability degradation from layout training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConsistCompose outperforms all six COCO-Position baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, InstanceDiffusion [43], trails by 4.8% in Instance Success Ratio and 13.7% in AP; GLIGEN [18] and PlanGen [11] lag further behind (AP 40.5% and 31.9% respectively), while CreatiLayout [57] is weakest overall (mIoU 64.9%). On MS-Bench, ConsistCompose dominates on DINO (0.660 vs. MUSE's 0.619 and GLIGEN's 0.454) and mIoU (0.889 vs. GLIGEN's 0.868), while MS-Diffusion [45] achieves the highest CLIP-T (0.336) but collapses on spatial metrics (mIoU 0.466, AP 0.108). MUSE [29] offers the best identity preservation among baselines (DINO 0.619) but sacrifices spatial accuracy (mIoU 0.698). On DreamBench, ConsistCompose edges out OmniGen2 [50] and UNO [51] on DINO in both single- and multi-object settings (0.677/0.506 vs. 0.671/0.459 and 0.661/0.491), confirming that layout-aware training transfers to subject-driven generation without task-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConsistCompose builds directly on the Bagel MoT architecture (two Transformer experts for understanding and generation with shared self-attention) and extends its deep-fusion mechanism for spatially controllable generation. It departs fundamentally from the layout-controlled generation lineage (GLIGEN, InstanceDiffusion, MIGC, CreatiLayout, PlanGen), which relies on dedicated layout encoders, gated cross-attention layers, or region-aware U-Net modifications, by instead encoding bounding boxes as plain text tokens within the linguistic stream (the LELG paradigm). This positions the work at the intersection of two previously separate threads: unified multimodal generation (Janus, Show-O, OmniGen2, Bagel) and layout-conditioned synthesis, arguing that spatial constraints need not be a separate modality but can be absorbed into the language interface without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LELG formulation offers a clean, architecture-agnostic recipe for injecting spatial control into any unified multimodal Transformer, potentially making layout controllability a drop-in capability rather than a bespoke pipeline. The 3.4M-sample ConsistCompose3M dataset with instance-level layout and identity annotations is a substantial resource that addresses a noted gap in large-scale multi-instance supervision. Key limitations include restriction to axis-aligned bounding boxes (no part-level, pose, or 3D spatial structure), dependence on the Bagel backbone's quality, and the need for per-task CFG tuning. Open questions include whether the text-embedded coordinate approach scales to video generation, interactive editing, or more complex spatial relationships (occlusion, depth ordering, relative positioning).
