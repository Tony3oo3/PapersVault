## Problem

Existing DPO methods for video diffusion models require generating multiple candidate videos per prompt and ranking them with human annotations or critic models, incurring high inference and labeling costs. Moreover, global preference scores aggregate multiple quality dimensions, producing ambiguous or conflicting supervision signals, and ignore region-specific artifacts (e.g., localized flickering, detail collapse) that disproportionately affect human perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260997/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a text prompt and produces a temporally coherent video via 50 DDIM steps with classifier-free guidance (scale 6.0) on DiT-based VDMs (CogVideoX-2B/5B, Wan2.1-1.3B). During training, the pipeline consumes 63K curated high-quality real video clips (from Pexels, annotated with Qwen2.5-VL) as positive samples; negative samples are synthesized by locally corrupting masked regions of these same clips, so each preference pair requires only a single model inference rather than multi-sample generation and ranking.

## Method

LocalDPO constructs preference pairs by randomly generating closed 3D spatio-temporal masks via Bezier curves, then performing a masked progressive denoising: the original video latent is noised to a random level α∈[0.75, 0.95], and during each denoising step a latent fusion (z_{t-1} = M⊙ẑ_{t-1} + (1−M)⊙z_{t-1}^{orig}) restricts corruption to the masked region while preserving original content elsewhere. The key training innovation is a region-aware DPO loss (L_RA-DPO) that applies the DPO reconstruction-error difference only within the mask M, weighted by a noise-level normalization η(α), so the model explicitly learns to prefer the real video over the corrupted version in the affected spatio-temporal region. A hybrid objective combines L_RA-DPO with standard full-latent DPO and SFT losses (weights 1.0, 1.0, 0.1) to prevent overfitting to local regions while preserving global video structure. Fine-tuning uses LoRA (rank 64) on DiT attention layers only, trained for 540 iterations with batch size 128.

## Results

On VBench (aesthetic and imaging quality prompts), LocalDPO achieves the best Overall VideoAlign score across all three models: 7.8568 on CogVideoX-2B, 10.2930 on CogVideoX-5B, and 7.9588 on Wan2.1-1.3B. The most striking gain is on CogVideoX-5B, where Imaging Quality jumps from 0.6631 (baseline) to 0.7107 (+7.2%) and Overall from 9.7191 to 10.2930 (+5.9%). LocalDPO also leads on HPS-v2 (0.2782) and Image Reward (0.6297) for CogVideoX-5B, and on Aesthetic Quality for all three models. On the VideoJAM benchmark, similar patterns hold with CogVideoX-5B Overall improving from 7.5012 to 7.6424.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LocalDPO consistently outperforms all four baselines (pretrained baseline, SFT, Vanilla DPO, and DenseDPO) on the majority of metrics across all three model scales. Against the pretrained baseline, the largest gains appear on CogVideoX-5B: Overall VideoAlign improves by 0.57 points (9.7191→10.2930) and Imaging Quality by 0.048 (0.6631→0.7107). Compared to Vanilla DPO [31], LocalDPO wins on Aesthetic Quality, Imaging Quality, HPS-v2, and Image Reward for CogVideoX-5B (e.g., HPS-v2 0.2782 vs. 0.2658), while Vanilla DPO retains a slight edge on Motion Quality (1.5498 vs. 1.6682 is actually LocalDPO winning; the one metric where Vanilla DPO leads is PickScore on CogVideoX-2B: 21.41 vs. 21.46 — actually LocalDPO also wins there). Against DenseDPO [62], LocalDPO achieves higher Overall scores on all three models (7.8568 vs. 7.8157, 10.2930 vs. 9.5726, 7.9588 vs. 7.8373) and notably surpasses it on Imaging Quality for CogVideoX-5B (0.7107 vs. 0.6962). SFT is the weakest post-training baseline, with LocalDPO exceeding it on every metric for CogVideoX-5B and Wan2.1-1.3B, confirming that preference-based alignment with localized corruption is more effective than simple fine-tuning on high-quality videos.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO-for-diffusion line of work (originally for LLMs, then image diffusion, then video diffusion) by fundamentally changing how preference pairs are constructed. Rather than the standard pipeline of generating multiple candidate videos per prompt and ranking them with a critic model or human annotators, LocalDPO takes high-quality real videos as positives and synthesizes negatives by locally corrupting masked spatio-temporal regions using the model's own denoising process. The key methodological departure is the region-aware DPO loss, which restricts the preference signal to the corrupted regions only, complementing a global DPO loss and SFT regularization to prevent overfitting to local patches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LocalDPO offers a substantially cheaper and more targeted alternative to existing video DPO pipelines: a single forward pass per prompt replaces multi-sample generation plus critic-model scoring, and the region-level supervision directly addresses the localized artifacts (flickering, detail collapse) that global scores miss. A useful negative result emerges from the ablation: pairing real videos with model-generated videos as DPO positives/negatives fails to improve over vanilla DPO due to distribution mismatch, underscoring that preference pairs must be 'homologous' in global content. Open questions include whether the Bezier-curve corruption strategy adequately covers the full diversity of real-world failure modes, how the approach scales to larger models (tested up to 5B), and whether the region-aware loss generalizes to other alignment objectives beyond DPO.
