## Problem

SFT-based RVOS methods require expensive large-scale fine-tuning that cannot keep pace with rapid MLLM evolution, while existing zero-shot approaches rely on straightforward single-pass workflows that accumulate errors and suffer from MLLM hallucinations, leaving them significantly behind SFT-based performance. The paper addresses how to design an effective training-free collaboration mechanism among MLLMs, grounding models, and segmentation models that includes self-correction to mitigate hallucination and error propagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261145/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form textual query (ranging from simple descriptions to complex reasoning instructions) and a video of T frames at H x W resolution, and outputs a list of k per-frame binary masklets (each T x H x W) covering all referred objects. Internally, the video is first reduced to N=10 coarsely sampled frames and then K=5 fine-selected frames; the keyframe receives a larger, higher-resolution visual block while context frames are compressed into smaller slots, and SAM2 propagates the initial bounding-box prompt into full video masks.

## Method

Refer-Agent decomposes RVOS into a four-stage reasoning pipeline (Coarse-to-Fine frame selection via CLIP+MLLM scoring, Intent Analysis with a Dynamic Focus Layout that allocates a large high-res block to the keyframe and small low-res slots to context frames, Object Grounding via MLLM bounding-box prediction, and Mask Generation via SAM2) and wraps it in a Two-stage Chain-of-Reflection (CoR) mechanism. CoR employs a Questioner-Responder pair in each stage: Existence Reflection checks whether the keyframe clearly shows all targets and whether a better frame exists, while Consistency Reflection decomposes the query into attributes and generates multi-choice questions to verify predicted objects against the original intent. Failed verifications produce a reflection chain that is fed back to re-run the relevant pipeline stage, and this reasoning-reflection loop iterates up to MAX_TURN=4 rounds. The entire system is training-free, using Ovis2.5-9B as the MLLM and SAM2 as the segmentation backbone, requiring no fine-tuning to integrate new MLLMs.

## Results

Refer-Agent (using Ovis2.5-9B, no fine-tuning) achieves 71.3 J&F on Ref-YouTube-VOS, 54.7 J&F on MeViS, 69.8 J&F on ReasonVOS, 33.4 J&F on GroundMoRe, and 61.3 J&F overall on ReVOS. On the most challenging reasoning benchmarks, it outperforms the best SFT-based method (RGA3) by 16.2% J&F on ReasonVOS and 1.3% on ReVOS, and surpasses the best zero-shot method (CoT-RVS with GPT-4o) by 4.3% and 5.4% respectively. On motion-heavy benchmarks, it leads by 2.5% J&F on MeViS and 6.2% on GroundMoRe over all prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Refer-Agent outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset where they overlap. Against SFT-based methods, the largest margins are on reasoning-heavy benchmarks: +16.2% J&F over RGA3 on ReasonVOS (69.8 vs 53.6), +19.9% over VideoLISA (69.8 vs 47.5), and +19.9% over GLUS (69.8 vs 49.9). On Ref-YouTube-VOS, it edges out the strongest SFT competitor VRS-HQ by 0.3% (71.3 vs 71.0) and Sa2VA by 0.6% (71.3 vs 70.7). On ReVOS (Table 2), Refer-Agent achieves 61.3 J&F overall, surpassing VRS-HQ (60.0), RGA3 (58.0), HyperSeg (55.7), InstructSeg (54.5), and ViLLa (57.0). Against zero-shot baselines, the advantage is most pronounced on ReasonVOS where it beats CoT-RVS with GPT-4o by 4.3% (69.8 vs 65.5) and AL-Ref-SAM2 (GPT-4) by 1.9% on Ref-YouTube-VOS (71.3 vs 67.9). On GroundMoRe, Refer-Agent leads MoRa by 6.2% J&F (33.4 vs 27.2), the largest single-dataset gap in the paper.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant SFT-based paradigm for RVOS (VISA, GLUS, RGA3, VRS-HQ), which requires large-scale fine-tuning of MLLMs jointly with segmentation models, and also moves beyond prior zero-shot pipelines (AL-Ref-SAM2, CoT-RVS) that chain MLLM reasoning, grounding, and SAM2 in a single straightforward pass. Conceptually, it repurposes the multi-agent collaboration paradigm from long-video understanding (LVAgent) and instruction-based editing (EDIVAL-AGENT) into the RVOS setting, introducing a two-stage Chain-of-Reflection (Questioner–Responder) self-correction loop that has no direct analogue in prior RVOS work. The Coarse-to-Fine frame selection (CLIP + MLLM scoring) and Dynamic Focus Layout are incremental refinements over single-pass sampling, but the alternating reasoning–reflection architecture is the core architectural departure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a fully training-free 9B open-source MLLM pipeline outperforms SFT-based methods (by up to 16.2% J&F on ReasonVOS) and even GPT-4o-based zero-shot baselines challenges the prevailing assumption that supervised fine-tuning is necessary for competitive RVOS, and offers a practical path to rapidly adopt newly released MLLMs without retraining. Limitations include the computational overhead of multiple reflection rounds (~195 s/sample at MAX_TURN=4), dependence on the underlying MLLM's grounding quality, and the fact that the reflection mechanism adds architectural complexity that may not transfer trivially to other segmentation tasks. Open questions include whether the Chain-of-Reflection mechanism generalizes to audio-referenced or long-video RVOS, and whether the performance gap narrows as base MLLMs improve their native video reasoning.
