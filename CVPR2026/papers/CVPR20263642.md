## Problem

CLIP-based Compositional Zero-Shot Learning methods inherit a fundamental limitation: the modality gap, a geometric separation between image and text embeddings caused by information imbalance in training pairs (images encode richer detail than captions). In fine-grained CZSL, the standard CLS token aggregates all visual information including redundant context, contaminating the alignment signal for attribute-object recognition. Prior approaches exploit CLIP's cross-modal alignment but leave this imbalance unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image processed by a CLIP ViT-L/14 visual encoder (yielding L patch tokens plus a CLS token) and learnable soft prompts for attributes, objects, and compositions fed through the CLIP text encoder. It outputs a probability distribution over all compositions (seen and unseen), with the final prediction integrating three sub-models: sparse alignment scores, VAC condensed representations, and memory-bank prototype retrieval. The system operates in both closed-world (known compositions only) and open-world (full A×O Cartesian product) evaluation settings.

## Method

SAM is a three-stage framework that targets the information imbalance at its source. Stage I (Sparse Alignment) replaces the CLS token with a max-operation over patch-to-text similarity scores, selecting only the most semantically relevant patch per composition to create an information-balanced training signal. Stage II (Visual Adaptive Condensation) uses a learnable query with cross-attention blocks to adaptively aggregate all patch features into a unified representation, regularized by KL-divergence distillation from Stage I's sparse predictions to recover contextually relevant cues without reintroducing redundancy. Stage III (Dynamically Updated Memory Bank) stores high-confidence visual prototypes per composition and continuously updates them with reliable test samples during inference, enabling a visual-only classification path that inherently bypasses the modality gap. The visual encoder is fine-tuned with LoRA, and the final prediction is a weighted combination of all three modules' outputs.

## Results

Under the closed-world setting, SAM-CZSL achieves the best AUC of 50.0, 24.0, and 16.2 on UT-Zappos, MIT-States, and C-GQA respectively, along with the best Harmonic Mean of 62.0, 40.8, and 34.8. It also attains the highest Seen accuracy (73.3, 53.2, 45.8) and near-best Unseen accuracy (76.8, 53.0, 39.5) across all three benchmarks. Under the more challenging open-world setting, SAM-CZSL still leads with AUC of 42.3, 9.4, and 4.6 and HM of 54.8, 23.1, and 15.3, outperforming the previous best (ClusPro) by 2.8, 0.1, and 1.6 AUC points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM-CZSL outperforms all nine baselines on AUC and HM across all three datasets in the closed-world setting. Against the strongest prior method ClusPro [34], SAM gains +3.4 AUC on UT-Zappos (50.0 vs 46.6), +0.2 AUC on MIT-States (24.0 vs 23.8), and +1.3 AUC on C-GQA (16.2 vs 14.9), while also improving HM by +3.5, +0.1, and +2.0 respectively. Compared to LogiCzsl [47], the gains are +4.2 AUC (UT-Zappos), +0.6 AUC (MIT-States), and +0.9 AUC (C-GQA). The improvement over Troika [9] is particularly large on UT-Zappos (+8.3 AUC) and C-GQA (+3.8 AUC), reflecting the benefit of sparse alignment in fine-grained attribute recognition. On MIT-States, SAM's Unseen accuracy (53.0) is slightly below ClusPro (54.0) and LogiCzsl (53.9), but its Seen accuracy (53.2) and AUC (24.0) are the best, indicating better overall balance. In the open-world setting, SAM leads on AUC for all three datasets (42.3, 9.4, 4.6) and on HM for UT-Zappos (54.8) and C-GQA (15.3), though its Unseen accuracy on MIT-States (21.2) trails ClusPro (22.1) by 0.9 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CLIP-based CZSL lineage (CoOp, CSP, DFSP, Troika, LogiCzsl, ClusPro) but departs from the standard practice of using the [CLS] token for cross-modal alignment. Its theoretical grounding comes from the information-imbalance analysis of Schrodi et al. [37], which reframes the modality gap as a consequence of visual representations encoding more information than their textual counterparts. The sparse alignment mechanism borrows the patch-token-for-local-prediction idea from CLIP-based segmentation [5, 22], while the dynamically updated memory bank extends retrieval-augmented and test-time adaptation ideas [46, 49, 59] into a CZSL-specific, training-free inference pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of Sparse Alignment (demonstrated by its integration into Troika without additional parameters) makes it a broadly applicable training paradigm for any CLIP-based fine-grained recognition task, not just CZSL. The memory bank's visual-only classification path offers a clean way to sidestep the modality gap at inference, though its effectiveness depends on sequential test-sample access and may degrade under adversarial or shuffled ordering. Open questions include whether the information-imbalance perspective generalizes to non-CLIP backbones, whether the ~25% patch sparsity is dataset-specific, and how the approach scales to open-world settings with very large composition spaces where the memory bank must cover all A×O combinations.
