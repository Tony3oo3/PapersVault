## Problem

Binary real/fake detectors are insufficient as generative video models multiply and evolve, creating an urgent need to identify *which* specific model produced a synthetic video. Prior work on source attribution is limited to static images or, in the single video case, only 4 closed-source generators at a single granularity level, with no data-efficient training strategy or interpretability mechanism. SAGA addresses this by providing the first large-scale, multi-granular (five levels), data-efficient, and interpretable framework for AI-generated video source attribution.

## Input / Output

Input is a video clip whose frames are resized to a fixed resolution and individually encoded by a frozen vision foundation model (ViT) into tokenized embeddings of shape l_t × d_t per frame; these are stacked in temporal order to form the video-level representation fed to the trainable transformer. Output is a multi-granular attribution label spanning five levels (BIN-L, TASK-L, SD-L, TEAM-L, GEN-L) plus Temporal Attention Signatures (T-Sigs), which are averaged frame-to-frame attention heatmaps providing a visual fingerprint of the generator's temporal artifacts.

## Method

SAGA uses a two-stage training protocol: Stage 1 pretrains a video transformer (a spatial encoder that applies one transformer block per frame followed by average pooling, and a temporal encoder of D stacked MHSA blocks with sinusoidal positional encoding) for binary real/fake classification with cross-entropy loss on abundant unlabeled-by-source data. Stage 2 adapts this pretrained backbone to multi-class source attribution using only 0.5% of source-labeled examples, combining CE loss with a Hard Negative Mining (HNM) contrastive objective that selects the nearest cross-class negative in the batch to enforce geometric separation in embedding space where CE alone leaves overlapping clusters. T-Sigs are extracted by averaging the attention scores from the penultimate temporal encoder block across multiple videos per class, yielding stable, class-specific temporal fingerprints that explain *why* generators are distinguishable. The frozen foundation-model front-end provides domain-agnostic visual features critical for in-the-wild robustness, while the trainable transformer captures the spatio-temporal inconsistencies unique to each generator.

## Results

On the DeMamba dataset (in-domain), SAGA achieves 99.94% accuracy with 100.00% precision and 99.89% recall for binary real/fake detection, substantially outperforming the best baseline FTCN-CLIP-B (89.67% accuracy). On the DVF dataset (cross-domain, trained only on DeMamba), SAGA reaches 95.39% accuracy, surpassing the in-domain DVF baseline (92.00%). For the most challenging GEN-L source attribution task, SAGA's two-stage training with Hard Negative Mining achieves 94.99% mean accuracy using only 0.5% of source-labeled data, approaching the 97.41% achieved with 100% data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGA decisively outperforms all 14 baselines on both datasets. On DeMamba, SAGA (99.94% accuracy) exceeds the strongest baseline FTCN-CLIP-B (89.67%) by over 10 percentage points, and MINTIME-CLIP-B (89.98%) by nearly 10 points. On DVF, where SAGA is evaluated in a cross-domain setting (trained only on DeMamba), it still surpasses the in-domain DVF baseline (92.00%) by 3.39 points and HifiNet (84.30%) by over 11 points. The margin is even more pronounced against older methods like CNNDet (78.20%), DIRE (62.10%), and TALL (69.50%), where SAGA leads by 17–33 points. For the fine-grained GEN-L attribution task (Table 7), SAGA's two-stage HNM approach (94.99%) nearly matches full-data training (97.41%) while using only 0.5% of labeled examples, whereas single-stage CE-only training with the same 0.5% data collapses to 24.55%, highlighting the critical role of the contrastive objective.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGA extends the binary real/fake video detection literature (DeMamba, UNITE, TALL) into a multi-granular source attribution setting, drawing on image source attribution methods (Vahdati et al., POSE, Wang et al.) that have not previously addressed video-specific temporal dynamics. Architecturally, it repurposes a frozen vision foundation model (ViT) as a feature extractor feeding a custom multi-head attention video transformer, while the training strategy adapts contrastive learning with hard negative mining from face recognition (FaceNet) to the attribution problem. The only prior video source attribution work (Vahdati et al., 4 generators, single granularity) is explicitly positioned as a narrow precursor that SAGA generalizes to 19 generators across five attribution levels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAGA establishes the first large-scale, multi-granular benchmark for AI video provenance, which is practically critical for digital forensics, IP enforcement, and regulatory compliance as generative video models proliferate. The 0.5% data-efficiency result and cross-dataset generalization (DeMamba→DVF) suggest the framework could be deployed in settings where source-labeled data is scarce. Open questions include scalability to truly open-set generators (e.g., future Sora versions), robustness to aggressive video compression and re-encoding, and whether T-Sigs remain discriminative as generator architectures converge. The limited evaluation on only 19 generators and reliance on a single training dataset (DeMamba) leaves generalization to a broader ecosystem untested.
