## Problem

Existing developmentally inspired vision-language pretraining efforts (e.g., BabyVLM-V1) suffer from limited data coverage, lack of instruction tuning, benchmarks not grounded in established psychological assessments, and near-zero open-set performance. This paper asks whether a compact model pretrained from scratch on a longitudinal, infant-centric audiovisual corpus can achieve competitive cognitive performance, and whether a benchmark can be rigorously grounded in a standardized neurodevelopmental tool (NIH Baby Toolbox) to enable principled evaluation of developmentally plausible vision foundation models.

## Input / Output

The model accepts heterogeneous visual inputs—single frames, multi-frame sequences, short video clips (padded to ≥0.5 s), and multi-turn interleaved image-text dialogues—paired with naturalistic caregiver utterances or task-specific text prompts, and produces free-form text responses (e.g., a word label, a count, a spatial direction, or a multiple-choice answer). Pretraining data spans three formats: 181k video-utterance pairs, 768k image-utterance pairs (sampled at 1 FPS with CLIP similarity > 0.2), and 63k interleaved sequences (window size 4–8), all drawn from 478 hours of egocentric SAYCam recordings of three infants aged 6–32 months. The DevCV Toolbox benchmark tasks require the model to process 1–29 images per example and, for the Memory task, engage in multi-turn dialogue with corrective feedback.

## Method

The framework combines three components: (1) a minimally curated pretraining corpus from SAYCam that preserves the children's raw sensory intake, segmented into video-utterance, image-utterance, and interleaved multi-turn pairs using X-CLIP/CLIP similarity thresholds and Azure Speech transcription; (2) a compact VLM (ViT-L-16 visual encoder + LLaMA-1.1B language model + MLP connector) pretrained from scratch via a three-stage pipeline (visual-linguistic alignment, video-language pretraining, interleaved sequence pretraining) and then instruction-tuned on 113k task-specific examples; and (3) the DevCV Toolbox, a ten-task benchmark adapted from all vision-related NIH Baby Toolbox measures, covering Language (Looking While Listening, Picture Vocabulary, Localization), Executive Function/Memory (Left/Right, Spatial Details, Visual Delayed Response, Memory), and Math (Who Has More, Subitizing, Object Counting), with in-domain (SAYCam) and out-of-domain (Ego4D) splits. The key design principle is developmental fidelity: data curation is kept minimal to mirror infant sensory experience, and benchmark tasks are grounded in a clinically validated, normed neurodevelopmental assessment rather than ad-hoc cognitive tests.

## Results

On the DevCV Toolbox (in-domain), the compact 'baby model' (ViT-L-16 + LLaMA-1.1B, ~1.4B params) achieves 63.9% overall accuracy, matching Qwen2.5-VL-7B (63.7%) and substantially outperforming LLaVA-OneVision-7B (53.4%) and all smaller open-source models. Notably, the baby model outperforms GPT-4o on Object Counting (47.3% vs. 39.0%) and Who Has More (synthetic) (99.7% vs. 87.9%), and matches or exceeds all proprietary models on Spatial Details (92.8%) and Left/Right (96.4%). Human performance reaches 93.0% overall, while random guess sits at 31.8%, confirming substantial headroom for model improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against proprietary models, the baby model (63.9% overall) trails GPT-5 (87.6%), Gemini-2.5-pro (82.5%), GPT-4o (74.6%), and Gemini-2.5-flash (72.7%) on overall accuracy, but it outperforms GPT-4o on Object Counting (47.3% vs. 39.0%) and Who Has More synthetic (99.7% vs. 87.9%), and matches or exceeds all four on Spatial Details (92.8% vs. 92.6–94.5%) and Left/Right (96.4% vs. 34.9–96.0%). Among open-source models of similar size, the baby model ties Qwen2.5-VL-7B (63.9% vs. 63.7%) and clearly beats LLaVA-OneVision-7B (53.4%), Qwen2.5-VL-3B (47.0%), InternVL3.5-1B (37.2%), and LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B (33.2%). Against the larger Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B (78.0%), the baby model loses overall but remains competitive on Spatial Details (92.8% vs. 90.4%) and Who Has More synthetic (99.7% vs. 98.0%). The paper also reports that replacing natural caregiver transcripts with GPT-4o synthetic captions during pretraining yields only a modest 1.5-point overall gain (65.4% vs. 63.9%), confirming the quality of the minimally curated data. Out-of-domain evaluation on Ego4D drops the baby model to 41.1% (vs. 55.2% in-domain), and two held-out tasks (Looking While Listening, Subitizing) yield near-random performance, indicating limited zero-shot generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct and substantial extension of BabyVLM-V1 (Wang et al., ICCV 2025), which first demonstrated sample-efficient VLM pretraining on the SAYCam infant corpus. The key departures are threefold: (1) the pretraining data expands from image-utterance pairs to include video-utterance and interleaved multi-turn sequences, (2) the benchmark (DevCV Toolbox) is grounded on the NIH Baby Toolbox, a standardized and normed clinical assessment released in February 2025, rather than the ad-hoc developmental tasks in BabyVLM-V1 or other prior developmental benchmarks (DevBench, KIVA, Zorro, InfLevel, etc.), and (3) the model supports instruction tuning and multi-modal multi-turn inputs. The model architecture (ViT-L-16 + LLaMA-1.1B) is unchanged from BabyVLM-V1, keeping the focus on data and evaluation rather than architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DevCV Toolbox is the first developmental benchmark for vision FMs grounded on a standardized, normed clinical tool (NIH Baby Toolbox), providing a principled and reproducible evaluation that prior ad-hoc developmental benchmarks lacked. The finding that a ~1.4B-parameter model pretrained from scratch on ~1,000 hours of infant egocentric data can match or exceed GPT-4o on specific math tasks (Object Counting, Who Has More) suggests that developmentally grounded pretraining may be a viable alternative to massive-scale pretraining for certain cognitive capabilities. Key limitations include near-random performance on tasks excluded from instruction tuning (Looking While Listening, Subitizing), modest OOD generalization (41.1% on Ego4D vs. 55.2% in-domain), and the absence of actual children's performance data on DevCV Toolbox (a multi-year survey is in progress). Open questions include whether the framework scales to larger models, how to improve zero-shot generalization to unseen task formats, and whether the developmental plausibility constraint is ultimately a limitation or an advantage for sample efficiency.
