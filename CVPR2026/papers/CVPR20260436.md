## Problem

Dynamic-static decomposition methods for 3D Gaussian Splatting rely on mask priors and tag representations that are either multi-view inconsistent, temporally invariant, or continuous with threshold-based post-processing. These inaccuracies cause misassignment of static and dynamic Gaussians, leading to boundary artifacts, loss of fine-grained motion details (e.g., fingers), and overfitting that degrades side-view synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260436/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes synchronized multi-view video sequences of a dynamic scene and produces a dynamic 3D Gaussian field where each primitive carries a binary dynamic-static label (d^s ∈ {0,1}) alongside standard geometric attributes. At inference, deformed dynamic Gaussians are combined with static ones and rendered via alpha-blending in real time. Notably, the authors introduce a side-view evaluation protocol where test views are placed at the periphery of the training view range, exposing overfitting that standard center-view splits mask.

## Method

The framework introduces two key modules. First, a spatio-temporally fine-grained 4D mask field generates viewpoint- and timestep-specific 2D masks by combining residual-based coarse filtering, spatial box-filter diffusion to exploit structural smoothness of dynamic regions, and temporal standard-deviation masks, then trains a 4D network to predict these masks for arbitrary (viewpoint, timestep) queries. Second, a discontinuous dynamic-static tagging field replaces continuous probability tags with an integrate-and-fire spiking neuron that directly outputs binary labels; the non-differentiable Heaviside step is handled via an arctangent surrogate gradient for stable backpropagation. Training alternates between optimizing the binary tag d^s (with geometric attributes frozen) and optimizing geometric attributes (with d^s frozen), supervised by a binary cross-entropy loss between the rendered dynamic map and the 4D mask field target, followed by standard rendering loss for reconstruction.

## Results

Under the proposed side-view evaluation setting, the method achieves PSNR 26.30 / LPIPS 0.0615 on N3DV and PSNR 26.64 / LPIPS 0.0626 on MeetRoom, outperforming all four baselines on both quality metrics. On the VRU dataset with complex motions, it reaches PSNR 29.43 and LPIPS 0.170, surpassing Swift4D (29.03 / 0.187) and 4DGS (27.87 / 0.191). Rendering speed is competitive at 137 FPS (N3DV), 154 FPS (MeetRoom), and 77 FPS (VRU), with the FPS advantage over 4DGS (37/43/12 FPS) being particularly large.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method wins on both PSNR and LPIPS against all four baselines across all three datasets. Against 4DGS [49], it gains +0.11 dB PSNR on N3DV and +0.37 dB on MeetRoom while rendering 3.7× faster (137 vs 37 FPS). Against Swift4D [50], the closest competitor, it improves PSNR by +0.50 dB on N3DV and +1.48 dB on MeetRoom, with LPIPS reduced from 0.0619→0.0615 and 0.0676→0.0626 respectively, and matches or exceeds FPS (137 vs 124, 154 vs 101, 77 vs 77). Against Ex4DGS [17], it gains +0.20 dB PSNR on N3DV and +1.54 dB on MeetRoom with comparable speed. Against 3DGStream [46], it achieves +0.75 dB PSNR on N3DV and +0.96 dB on MeetRoom, though 3DGStream remains faster (230 vs 137 FPS on N3DV). On VRU, the gap over Swift4D narrows to +0.40 dB PSNR but LPIPS improves by 0.017, and both methods tie at 77 FPS.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic-static decomposition paradigm in 3D Gaussian Splatting (building on Swift4D's 4D hash encoder and deformation field) by addressing two specific weaknesses in how existing methods assign static vs. dynamic Gaussians. It departs from prior work in two ways: (1) replacing discrete 2D mask priors (from external segmentation models or temporal invariance heuristics) with a learned 4D spatio-temporal mask field, and (2) replacing continuous probabilistic tags with threshold-based post-processing by directly optimizing a binary tag via an integrate-and-fire spiking neuron with surrogate gradients, an idea adapted from Spiking NeRF. The paper also introduces a side-view evaluation protocol that places test views outside the training view range, a methodological departure from the standard center-view splits used in N3DV and MeetRoom benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The side-view evaluation setup is a practically useful contribution that exposes overfitting artifacts hidden by standard evaluation protocols, and could be adopted by the broader dynamic NVS community. The spiking-neuron-based binary tagging offers a clean, hyperparameter-free alternative to threshold-based discretization, though the performance gains over well-tuned continuous baselines are modest. A key limitation is the reliance on photometrically consistent input views; color discrepancies across views degrade the mask field and overall quality. Open questions include whether the 4D mask field generalizes to scenes with occlusion-heavy or highly non-rigid motion, and whether the spiking neuron approach extends naturally to multi-class (beyond binary) decomposition.
