## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting methods for X-ray CT reconstruction rely on a local affine approximation of the projective transformation, introducing projection errors that cause artifacts, cross-view inconsistencies, and convergence failures during optimization. While ray tracing methods compute the exact ray-Gaussian integral, they are computationally prohibitive for both forward rendering and backpropagation. The paper seeks a splatting-based formulation that is mathematically exact (equivalent to ray tracing) yet fast enough for GPU rasterization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view 2D X-ray projections from a cone-beam CT geometry (source-to-detector distance D, source-to-rotation-center distance R, pixel coordinates) along with camera extrinsics. It outputs a 3D volume parameterized as a set of M Gaussian kernels, each with a learnable 3D mean position, a 3×3 covariance matrix (decomposed into rotation and scale), and a scalar central density representing the linear attenuation coefficient. Novel view projections are generated by evaluating the closed-form projection formula at arbitrary pixel positions, and the 3D volume is extracted via voxelization for quantitative evaluation.

## Method

The key insight is to define a per-pixel coordinate system whose z-axis aligns with the ray direction for that pixel, so that the ray integral reduces to a simple marginalization (dropping the z-component) of the 3D Gaussian in that rotated frame. A Rodrigues rotation matrix T(u,v,D), depending only on pixel position and geometry, transforms each Gaussian from camera space into pixel space, yielding a closed-form projection value p_i = ρ_i(2π)^{1/2}|Σ_{i,p}|^{1/2}/|Σ̂_{i,p}|^{1/2} · exp(−½ μ̂^T Σ̂^{-1} μ̂) that is mathematically identical to the ray tracing integral without any approximation. For efficient GPU rendering, a CTAccuTile algorithm (adapted from Speedy-Splat) computes precise Gaussian-tile intersections using the density and integration bias as the threshold, and the entire forward/backward pass is implemented as a tiled CUDA kernel in PyTorch. The differentiable rasterizer supports end-to-end optimization with a compound L2 + SSIM loss, and a 2D positional gradient in the image plane drives adaptive density control.

## Results

On the synthetic dataset with 75 views, Exact-GS achieves PSNR-2D of 73.49 dB and PSNR-3D of 59.17 dB, outperforming R2-GS (70.44 / 57.17 dB) by ~3 dB in projection quality and ~2 dB in volume reconstruction. The projection error comparison (Table 1) shows Exact-GS reaches 149.64 dB PSNR-2D versus 55.56 dB for R2-GS and 50.43 dB for Optimal-GS, a 94 dB improvement over R2-GS. On the real-world FIPS dataset (75 views), Exact-GS achieves PSNR-2D 39.11 dB and PSNR-3D 44.11 dB, marginally surpassing R2-GS (38.99 / 44.02 dB) while using fewer Gaussians (50,010 vs. 50,186). Runtime is approximately 2× that of R2-GS (28 min 26 s vs. 13 min 35 s) but 2× faster than ray tracing methods in the forward pass.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against R2-GS [38], the primary 3D-GS baseline, Exact-GS wins on every metric across all view counts: +3.05 dB PSNR-2D and +2.0 dB PSNR-3D on 75-view synthetic data, with the gap widening to +1.9 dB PSNR-3D at 25 views. Against Optimal-GS [15], Exact-GS achieves a 99 dB PSNR-2D improvement in single-Gaussian projection error (149.64 vs. 50.43 dB), confirming that the tangent-plane approximation is fundamentally limited. Traditional iterative methods (FDK, SART, ASD-POCS) are outperformed by 20–40 dB in PSNR-3D, as expected given their voxel-grid formulation. NeRF-based methods (NAF, TensoRF) achieve competitive 2D metrics on the real-world FIPS dataset (NAF: 39.17 dB PSNR-2D vs. Exact-GS 39.11 dB) but produce visibly blurred high-frequency structures and lower 3D fidelity (44.14 vs. 44.11 dB PSNR-3D for NAF at 75 views). SAX-NeRF [7] is compared only in runtime (7 h 22 min vs. 28 min 26 s for Exact-GS), confirming the efficiency advantage of the Gaussian-splatting formulation. Overall, Exact-GS consistently leads or ties on all quantitative metrics while being ~2× faster than ray-tracing-based exact methods in the forward pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting line of work for CBCT reconstruction, building directly on R2-GS (which corrected integration bias) and Optimal-GS (which minimized projection error via tangent planes). It departs from both by eliminating the local affine approximation entirely: instead of projecting each 3D Gaussian onto a single image plane or tangent plane, Exact-GS orthogonally projects onto a per-pixel coordinate system and exploits the marginalization property of multivariate Gaussians to obtain a closed-form expression identical to the exact ray integral. This positions the method as a bridge between the speed of splatting-based rasterization and the mathematical rigor of ray tracing, while also introducing a CTAccuTile algorithm (based on Speedy-Splat) for precise Gaussian-tile intersection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of the affine approximation error addresses a fundamental source of projection inconsistency and convergence instability in all prior 3D-GS-based CT methods, with the convergence study (R2-GS oscillating while Exact-GS converges cleanly) providing clear mechanistic evidence. However, the practical reconstruction gains remain modest (≈2 dB on synthetic, <0.5 dB on real data) because the ADC-based densification strategy still introduces stripe artifacts in smooth regions, capping the achievable quality. The per-pixel marginalization trick is conceptually generalizable to other 3DGS applications (e.g., transmittance integration in natural-light rendering), but the authors leave hardware-accelerated ray tracing platforms (OptiX, VKGS) and second-order optimization as future work, so the full efficiency potential is unrealized.
