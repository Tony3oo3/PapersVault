## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting rendering on mobile GPUs (e.g., Nvidia AGX Orin, with only ~3.4% of A100 resources) achieves barely 20 FPS, far below the 90 FPS required for VR. The paper identifies three compounding bottlenecks: high per-pixel computational intensity from processing thousands of Gaussians, uniform rasterization that wastes cycles on Gaussians contributing negligibly to final pixels, and unsustainable peak GPU memory when all Gaussians must reside on-device simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264027/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a pre-trained 3DGS model (Gaussian centroids, 3D covariance matrices, opacities, and spherical harmonic coefficients) along with a camera pose and view direction at runtime. It produces a rendered RGB image frame on a tile-by-tile basis. A key operational detail is that the view-dependent scene representation means only a subset of Gaussian clusters (the nearest M clusters plus always-resident shared Gaussians) is loaded into GPU memory per frame, with future clusters asynchronously prefetched via a dedicated GPU stream to avoid frame stuttering.

## Method

SEELE introduces two orthogonal GPU-oriented techniques. Hybrid Preprocessing (HP) performs offline clustering of Gaussians into shared (used across all viewpoints) and exclusive (viewpoint-group-specific) sets based on top-k contribution to cumulative transmittance, then at runtime selects only the nearest M clusters and applies an opacity-aware online filtering equation that tightens the 3σ intersection test to eliminate false-positive Gaussian-tile overlaps. Contribution-Aware Rasterization (CR) groups pixels into 2×2 blocks where a single leader pixel computes each Gaussian's transparency; if it falls below 1/255, all pixels in the group skip that Gaussian's color blending, which simultaneously reduces total computation and eliminates warp divergence within a GPU warp. A brief 1000-step fine-tuning with a FLIP-based view consistency loss (γ=0.1) restores rendering quality after the pipeline modifications. Together these yield up to 6.3× speedup and 39.1% runtime model reduction while improving PSNR/SSIM over baselines.

## Results

On Mip-NeRF360, SEELE+3DGS achieves 59.67 FPS (vs. 20.79 for vanilla 3DGS) with PSNR 27.72 dB, SSIM 0.814, and LPIPS 0.217, while reducing GPU memory from 710.6 MB to 380.9 MB. Across all four baselines and three datasets, SEELE delivers an average 2.4× speedup and 39.1% runtime model reduction while simultaneously improving PSNR by 0.28 dB and SSIM by 0.004. The largest speedup (3.2×) is observed on the dense 3DGS pipeline, while even the already-sparse MiniSplatting benefits from a 1.8× speedup and 23.2% memory savings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEELE consistently outperforms all four baselines on every quality and efficiency metric across all three datasets. Against vanilla 3DGS, SEELE achieves a 2.9× FPS improvement (20.79→59.67 on Mip-NeRF360) while simultaneously improving PSNR by 0.26 dB and cutting GPU memory by 46%. Against MiniSplatting (the fastest baseline at 71.31 FPS), SEELE still nearly doubles throughput to 131.62 FPS and improves PSNR by 0.47 dB, the largest quality gain among all comparisons. Against LightGaussian, SEELE achieves a 2.5× speedup (30.89→76.36 FPS) with modest quality gains (+0.12 dB PSNR). Against AdR-Gaussian, SEELE delivers a 1.5× speedup (65.95→96.25 FPS) and a 49% memory reduction (288.6→147.6 MB). Notably, SEELE also improves view consistency (FLIP scores) over all baselines, e.g., reducing FLIP₇ from 0.0466 to 0.0292 on 3DGS, confirming that the acceleration does not degrade temporal coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) pipeline of Kerbl et al. [25], modifying its preprocessing and rasterization stages rather than introducing a new rendering primitive. It departs from prior acceleration work in two key ways: (1) instead of uniform model pruning or compression (CompactGS, MiniSplatting, LightGaussian), it introduces a view-dependent scene representation that clusters Gaussians by viewpoint relevance, and (2) instead of treating all Gaussians identically during rasterization (as in FlashGS, Balanced3DGS, Speedy-Splat), it dynamically allocates computation based on per-Gaussian contribution. The authors explicitly position their techniques as orthogonal to existing optimizations, demonstrating integration with four distinct 3DGS variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical deployment gap: 3DGS on mobile/edge hardware (e.g., AGX Orin at 3.4% of A100 resources) currently falls far short of the 90 FPS needed for VR, and the 6.3× speedup with 39.1% model reduction while *improving* quality is practically significant for AR/VR and autonomous driving. The contribution-aware rasterization insight—that 1.5% of Gaussians drive 99% of pixel values and that low-contribution Gaussians correlate with low-frequency content—provides a principled basis for future GPU architecture design for splatting. Open questions include scalability to dynamic or very large scenes, whether the offline clustering generalizes to streaming/interactive content, and whether the 2×2 pixel group size remains optimal at higher resolutions.
