## Problem

Monocular 3D object detection methods assume dense 3D annotations for all visible objects, but real-world datasets suffer from sparse and inconsistent labels because 3D annotation is 3–16× more expensive than 2D. Existing sparsely annotated object detection (SAOD) methods for 2D or point-cloud settings do not transfer to monocular 3D detection, since they rely on classification confidence scores that fail to capture 3D geometric errors or on LiDAR depth cues absent in single-camera scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261181/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image paired with sparse 3D annotations (30–70% of objects labeled with center, dimensions, and orientation) and camera extrinsics. During training it additionally requires SAM-generated object segmentation masks and road region masks for augmentation, and the detector outputs 3D bounding boxes (x, y, z, h, w, l, r_y) with class labels and per-prediction depth uncertainty σ. The depth uncertainty is not a final output but an internal signal used to gate pseudo-label acceptance.

## Method

The framework uses a teacher-student architecture with two complementary modules. Road-Aware Patch Augmentation (RAPA) segments clean object patches from source images via SAM, transforms their 3D centers through camera extrinsics, applies horizontal translation with observation-angle-preserving rotation updates, and validates placement against road masks and existing object IoU before pasting the resized patch onto the target image. Prototype-Based Filtering (PBF) maintains a bank of class-specific RoI feature prototypes initialized from sparse ground truths; at each step it scores teacher predictions on geometric reliability (exp(−σ) from Laplacian aleatoric depth uncertainty) and semantic consistency (max cosine similarity to prototypes), accepting only predictions that exceed both thresholds. Accepted pseudo-labels are fed back to refine prototypes via weighted cumulative updates and stored in a growing GT Bank that augments supervision in subsequent epochs, while the student network is trained jointly on sparse ground truths and filtered pseudo-labels.

## Results

On the KITTI validation set under 30% sparse annotation, the proposed method achieves AP_3D of 21.28 (Easy), 15.60 (Moderate), and 12.79 (Hard), outperforming the best existing SAOD method (Calibrated Teacher: 17.14/12.96/10.58) by 4.14/2.64/2.21 points respectively. On the KITTI test set at 30% annotation, the method reaches 17.47/11.36/8.83 AP_3D, more than doubling the best baseline (SparseDet: 10.76/7.42/6.16). Under foggy conditions (Foggy KITTI, fog density 0.1, 30% annotation), the method achieves 19.11/13.72/10.35 AP_3D, surpassing the strongest baseline (Calibrated Teacher: 11.97/8.65/7.26) by over 7 points on Easy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed MonoSAOD outperforms all six baselines across every sparsity level and difficulty setting. Against the MonoDETR baseline (no SAOD strategy), the method gains +10.11/+6.87/+5.23 AP_3D at 30% annotation, demonstrating the substantial cost of ignoring sparse-annotation challenges. Among existing SAOD methods, Calibrated Teacher is the strongest competitor at 70% annotation (25.39 Easy) but is surpassed by MonoSAOD (26.67 Easy); at 30% annotation the gap widens to 4.14 points on Easy. SparseDet and Co-mining, which rely on confidence-based pseudo-label selection, trail MonoSAOD by 4.33 and 5.27 points respectively on Easy at 30%, confirming that 2D confidence alone is insufficient for 3D pseudo-label quality. Co-student shows the weakest performance among SAOD methods at 30% (15.99 Easy), suggesting its consistency-based strategy is particularly ill-suited to 3D geometry. Finally, when applied as a plug-in to MonoDGP (CVPR'25), the framework lifts Easy AP_3D from 15.56 to 22.62, demonstrating architecture-agnostic gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of monocular 3D object detection (M3OD) and sparsely annotated object detection (SAOD), two well-studied but previously disjoint areas. It departs from existing SAOD methods (Co-mining, SparseDet, Calibrated Teacher, Co-student) that select pseudo-labels via classification confidence, arguing this is fundamentally inadequate when predictions must encode 3D geometric attributes like depth and orientation. The RAPA module repurposes SAM-based segmentation and copy-paste augmentation with 3D geometric consistency constraints, while the PBF module introduces a dual criterion (prototype similarity + aleatoric depth uncertainty) for pseudo-label validation that has no direct analogue in prior 2D SAOD or point-cloud SAOD work. The framework is designed as a plug-in on top of existing M3OD backbones (MonoDETR, MonoDGP) rather than proposing a new detector architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formulation of sparsely annotated monocular 3D object detection, addressing a practically critical gap: 3D annotation costs 3–16× more than 2D, making dense labeling infeasible for large-scale deployment. The ablation showing that confidence-based pseudo-labeling yields only marginal gains (+1.2 AP) while geometric+semantic filtering yields substantial gains (+4.1 AP) validates the core thesis that 3D reliability cannot be inferred from 2D confidence. However, evaluation is limited to the KITTI car category (with foggy KITTI as a secondary test), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to multiple object classes, diverse driving scenes, or non-automotive domains. The plug-in design and GT Bank accumulation strategy suggest the framework could be adopted by other M3OD pipelines, but the single-dataset, single-class scope limits broader conclusions.
