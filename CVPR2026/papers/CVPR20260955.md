## Problem

Event-based motion segmentation has relied on assumption-heavy geometric pipelines (flow clustering, contrast maximization) or deep models that fail to generalize across sensors and datasets. The paper argues the missing ingredient is moving border ownership: not merely detecting motion boundaries but assigning which side of each boundary belongs to the foreground object, a cue that resolves occlusions by design and mirrors early biological vision computations in retinal ganglion cells.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260955/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single-channel time surface (10 ms window) where each pixel encodes the normalized recency of the last event, avoiding multi-frame stacking. It produces two dense outputs at full resolution: a signed-distance border-ownership field (0 at owned borders, clamped to −10 deep inside foreground objects) and a binary motion mask; these are averaged 50/50 and thresholded to yield the final segmentation mask for mIoU evaluation. The streaming ConvLSTM hidden state carries temporal context across windows and resets at scene boundaries.

## Method

The architecture pairs a MobileNetV3-Large encoder (GroupNorm for batch-size-1 streaming) with a ConvLSTM temporal bottleneck and a U-Net-style decoder that feeds dual prediction heads plus auxiliary heads at intermediate scales. Training is fully supervised on a 1.6 TB synthetic dataset rendered via BlenderProc (Replica rooms + ShapeNet objects on Lissajous trajectories, V2E event simulation at 1200 FPS), where perfect instance masks and depth maps yield exact border-ownership ground truth unavailable in real data. The loss combines a spatially-weighted MSE on the ownership field (10× weight within 10 px of borders) with binary cross-entropy on the motion mask, both applied at multiple decoder scales with α = 0.5 auxiliary weighting. An ablation shows ownership supervision adds 5.55 mIoU over a binary-mask-only variant, and synthetic-only training (81.55 mIoU) outperforms real-only training (74.52) on EVIMO2. Optional ConvLoRA adapters (1.3% extra parameters) in the decoder enable few-shot adaptation, lifting EVIMO2 mIoU to 85.12 while keeping the backbone frozen.

## Results

Trained exclusively on synthetic events, the model achieves 81.55 mIoU zero-shot on EVIMO2 (event-only), surpassing multi-modal baselines like SemanticAided (79.82, semantic+depth) and UDA (63.4, RGB+event), and approaching the best model-based method VCM (84). With few-shot ConvLoRA adaptation, mIoU rises to 85.12, exceeding VCM. On EVIMO1, the zero-shot event-only model reaches 69.6 average mIoU, competitive with the full EVIMO pipeline (70.6, event+flow+depth) while outperforming all prior event-only learners (SpikeMS 53.8, GConv 57, EVDodgeNet 66). On EED, the model achieves a 90% average detection rate with perfect scores on Occlusions and Background scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On EVIMO2, the zero-shot event-only model (81.55 mIoU) outperforms all learning-based baselines including SemanticAided (79.82, which uses semantic+depth) and UDA (63.4, which uses RGB+event), as well as model-based methods MSEE (77.4) and EMSGC (64.38). It trails only VCM (84.0) in the zero-shot setting, but surpasses VCM after ConvLoRA fine-tuning (85.12 vs. 84.0). On EVIMO1, the model (69.6 mIoU) is within 1 point of the full EVIMO pipeline (70.6) that leverages event+flow+depth, while decisively beating all event-only competitors: EVDodgeNet (66), GConv (57), SpikeMS (53.8), and Un-EVIMO (49.6). On EED, the 90% average detection rate is competitive with 0-MMS (94.2) and EMSMC (92.31), with perfect 100% on Occlusions and Background but a notable drop to 72.7% under Lighting Variation, revealing a sensitivity to illumination-driven event-statistics shifts. The ablation confirms that border-ownership supervision contributes a 5.55 mIoU gain over binary-mask-only training (81.55 vs. 76.00), and synthetic training outperforms real-only training on EVIMO2 (81.55 vs. 74.52) despite the latter having access to ground-truth labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the border-ownership concept—long studied in biological vision (retinal ganglion cells, V2/V4 cortex) and in image-based figure-ground reasoning—for event-based motion segmentation, where prior learning pipelines (EV-IMO, GConv, SpikeMS) produce only binary motion masks without explicit ownership. It departs from the classical model-based event segmentation line (EMSMC, EMSGC, contrast maximization) by replacing assumption-heavy motion clustering with a learned ownership-signed distance field, and from prior learning-based approaches by training exclusively on a large synthetic BlenderProc dataset with perfect ownership labels unavailable in real event data. The architecture (MobileNetV3 encoder + ConvLSTM temporal bottleneck + U-Net decoder) is standard; the novelty lies in the task formulation and the synthetic supervision strategy, with ConvLoRA borrowed from domain-adaptation literature for optional few-shot fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot transfer from synthetic to four real event datasets (EVIMO1/2, EED, EMSMC) without any fine-tuning is a practically significant result, suggesting that ownership-aware supervision on clean synthetic data can substitute for dataset-specific training and auxiliary modalities (flow, depth, RGB). The ablation showing a 5.55 mIoU gain from ownership supervision over binary-mask-only training provides evidence that the ownership signal is not merely a regularization trick but carries genuine spatial information. Key limitations remain: performance degrades under slow motion, high sensor noise, and illumination shifts; ConvLoRA adaptation is validated only on EVIMO2; and the formulation targets moving objects specifically rather than general static figure-ground boundaries under pure ego-motion. Extending ownership-aware supervision to optical flow, tracking, and structure-from-motion, and broadening adaptation across sensor families, are natural next steps.
