## Problem

High-resolution video snapshot compressive imaging (SCI) reconstruction is prohibitively memory-intensive, with most computation wasted on uninformative background regions. Existing object detectors fail on SCI measurements because coded exposure fuses temporally varying pixels into single spatial locations, creating severe spatial-temporal aliasing that acts as a temporal low-pass filter suppressing the high-frequency contours and boundaries critical for localization. No prior framework enables object-guided, selective ROI reconstruction directly from coded SCI measurements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261115/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D SCI measurement (720×1280) produced by a CACTI system with compression ratio 8, along with the corresponding set of coded aperture masks used for element-wise normalization. It outputs object bounding boxes with class labels (person detection in sports scenes), which then drive selective ROI reconstruction via EfficientSCI rather than full-frame recovery. The measurement encodes 8 consecutive 720p video frames into one image, and annotations are constructed by temporally unioning bounding boxes across those 8 frames per person ID.

## Method

DetectSCI builds on RT-DETR's encoder-decoder architecture and introduces two key modules. The encoder uses weight-sharing Mamba-Implicit Modules (MIM), each combining a Multi-Scale Spatial Mamba block (multi-scale dilated convolutions at rates 7/13/19 followed by SS2D bidirectional scanning and a depth-wise feed-forward network with channel gating) and an Implicit Neural Representation block (Fourier positional encoding of 2D coordinates fed through a lightweight MLP to learn a continuous coordinate-to-feature mapping that reintroduces sub-pixel detail lost to aliasing). The Frequency Mamba (FM) module performs DCT decomposition into low/mid/high frequency bands, aggregates them via a Tri-Pooling Unit (global avg/max/min pooling), applies channel-wise reweighting to counteract SCI's low-pass bias, then uses SS2D for inter-channel global context and a Mix Attention block (Spatial Attention + Frequency-Gated Attention) to produce discriminative features for DETR-style query selection. The weight-sharing design across MIM scales reduces parameters from 61.2M to 53.1M while retaining most accuracy, and the full system achieves 80.9 AP on SportsMOT-derived SCI data, surpassing the best CNN and Transformer baselines by 2.8 and 4.1 AP respectively.

## Results

On the SportsMOT-based SCI dataset (compression ratio 8, CACTI system), DetectSCI achieves 80.9 AP, 98.5 AP50, and 93.1 AP75, surpassing the best CNN-based detector (YOLOv10-X, 78.1 AP) by +2.8 AP and the best Transformer-based detector (RT-DETR, 76.8 AP) by +4.1 AP. The model uses 268.1 GFLOPs and 53.1M parameters, yielding 1.52 AP per 10M parameters—higher than all YOLO variants tested. At stricter localization thresholds, the gap widens: AP75 reaches 93.1 vs. 87.5 for YOLOv10-X (+5.6) and 86.9 for RT-DETR (+6.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DetectSCI outperforms all 15 baselines on AP. Against the strongest CNN detector, YOLOv10-X (78.1 AP), it gains +2.8 AP while using 36% more FLOPs (268.1 vs. 196.4) but fewer parameters (53.1M vs. 51.7M). Against the strongest Transformer baseline RT-DETR (76.8 AP), the gain is +4.1 AP with only 0.7% higher FLOPs and 4.2M fewer parameters. Among other Transformer variants, the margins are larger: +8.3 AP over DINO (72.6), +15.9 over DN-DETR (65.1), and +19.2 over DAB-DETR (61.8). Compared to MS-DETR (75.8 AP), the closest Transformer competitor, DetectSCI improves AP by +5.1 while reducing FLOPs by 16.7% (268.1 vs. 321.8). The original DETR (21.4 AP) and Deformable-DETR (66.3 AP) show that standard Transformer detection architectures struggle severely under SCI's temporal-spatial aliasing, underscoring the need for the proposed MIM and FM modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of snapshot compressive imaging (SCI) and modern object detection, building on the RT-DETR architecture as its detection backbone while introducing two novel modules—Mamba-Implicit Modules (MIM) and Frequency Mamba (FM)—specifically designed to counteract the spatial-temporal aliasing inherent in coded-aperture measurements. Conceptually, it departs from the standard detection paradigm (which assumes clean, discrete pixel grids) by treating SCI measurements as a fundamentally degraded input where temporal modulation fuses semantically distinct pixels, and it draws on Implicit Neural Representations (from NeRF and video super-resolution) and state-space models (Mamba/SS2D) to address this degradation. The frequency-domain analysis connects to prior work on frequency disentanglement in detection, but applies it in the novel context of recovering high-frequency details suppressed by the temporal low-pass effect of coded exposure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DetectSCI addresses a practical bottleneck in high-resolution SCI: full-frame reconstruction becomes infeasible at high resolutions due to memory constraints, and the paper demonstrates that object-guided ROI reconstruction can dramatically reduce computational cost while preserving task-relevant information. The frequency-biased degradation analysis (showing that SCI acts as a temporal low-pass filter suppressing motion-related high-frequency structures) provides a clear mechanistic explanation for why standard detectors fail on coded measurements, and the FM module's targeted countermeasure is well-motivated. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (SportsMOT) at a single compression ratio (8×), leaving open questions about generalization to other scene types, higher compression ratios, and real-time deployment. The framework also raises the broader question of whether detection-guided partial reconstruction can be extended to other compressed sensing modalities beyond CACTI.
