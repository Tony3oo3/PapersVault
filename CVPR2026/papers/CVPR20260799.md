## Problem

Egocentric walking tour videos are a rich, widely available source of diverse urban environment imagery, but the significant presence of humans—both crowds and close-proximity individuals—occludes large fractions of each frame, rendering them unusable for static environment modeling. Existing video inpainting and object-removal methods (e.g., Casper/GenOmnimatte) produce unreasonable artifacts when confronted with large human-masked regions and complex outdoor backgrounds, largely due to a domain shift between their training data and walking tour footage.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 7-second (197-frame at 16 fps) egocentric video clip at 720×1080 resolution, a corresponding per-frame human mask video (masks exclude shadow regions, forcing the model to learn shadow removal implicitly), and a fixed text prompt ('A video of a beautiful empty, human-free scene.'). It outputs a spatially and temporally consistent human-free background video clip. The underlying Casper/CogVideoX backbone processes fixed-length clips of 85 frames, which limits long-range temporal coherence for sustained occlusions.

## Method

The core contribution is EgoCrowds, a semi-synthetic dataset of 1,000 paired 7-second video clips constructed by compositing real human foregrounds (sourced from 10 cities, uniformly distributed across five Crowd% ranges of 0–50%) onto real background clips (sourced from 50 cities, filtered for low human presence via Grounded-SAM-2 with P=5, τ=10%). Per-human shadows are simulated via pivot-point estimation, random rotation, horizontal shear (s_x~U(0.15,0.35)), vertical scaling (s_y~U(0.8,0.95)), Gaussian softening, and random shadow strength α∈[0.2,0.8], with a single consistent shadow direction per clip. The model fine-tunes Casper (a video diffusion model built on CogVideoX) on these composite-to-background pairs, freezing the VAE and text encoder while training the 3D transformer for 100 epochs on 4 H200 GPUs (~15 hours). Training uses a composite loss combining the standard diffusion denoising loss with a temporal motion sub-loss that penalizes discrepancies in frame-to-frame noise residuals (weighted at α=0.25), encouraging smooth temporal dynamics in the inpainted regions.

## Results

On the EgoCrowds test set (35 videos across 7 cities and 5 Crowd% ranges), CrowdEraser achieves the best average PSNR (26.74 dB) and DreamSim (0.022) among all methods, with LPIPS of 0.118. The advantage grows substantially at higher occlusion: at 40–50% Crowd%, CrowdEraser reaches 23.31 dB PSNR versus 22.27 (DiffuEraser), 20.93 (ProPainter), and 19.24 (Casper). At low Crowd% (0–10%), all methods are more comparable, with CrowdEraser leading by only ~0.5 dB PSNR over DiffuEraser.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrowdEraser outperforms all three baselines on PSNR and DreamSim across every Crowd% range. Against ProPainter, the gap is largest at high occlusion (40–50%): 23.31 vs. 20.93 dB PSNR and 0.031 vs. 0.134 DreamSim, reflecting ProPainter's difficulty with sharp cast shadows and large masked regions. Against DiffuEraser, CrowdEraser leads by 0.79 dB average PSNR and 0.015 DreamSim, with the most pronounced advantage at 40–50% Crowd% (23.31 vs. 22.27 dB). Against the unfinetuned Casper backbone, CrowdEraser gains 2.10 dB average PSNR and reduces DreamSim from 0.029 to 0.022; the improvement is most dramatic at 40–50% Crowd% (23.31 vs. 19.24 dB), where Casper hallucinates objects inside large masked regions. On LPIPS, CrowdEraser achieves the best average (0.118) but the margin over DiffuEraser (0.120) is narrow, suggesting perceptual similarity is more competitive than pixel-level reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper fine-tunes Casper (the Generative Omnimatte video diffusion model, CVPR 2025) specifically for human and shadow removal in egocentric walking tour videos, addressing a domain gap where Casper degrades under significant human presence. The core methodological contribution is the EgoCrowds semi-synthetic dataset, which composites real foreground humans (with rule-based shadow simulation) onto real background clips sourced from 50 cities worldwide—a strategy analogous to semi-synthetic training data used in optical flow and segmentation. Among competing video inpainting methods (ProPainter, DiffuEraser), the paper positions itself as a diffusion-based approach that leverages Casper's learned object-effect associations while closing the distribution gap through targeted fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Walking tour videos represent a vast, geographically diverse, and largely untapped source of urban environment imagery, but their heavy human occlusion has prevented direct use in 3D/4D scene reconstruction. By demonstrating that CrowdEraser's outputs enable coherent SpatialTrackerV2 reconstructions where raw videos fail, the paper establishes a practical pipeline for converting widely available egocentric footage into usable environment models. Key open questions include temporal consistency over long sustained occlusions (limited by the 85-frame clip window), recovery of fine-grained high-frequency details at extreme Crowd% levels, and generalization beyond the urban training distribution—addressing these would require temporal memory mechanisms or 3D geometric constraints.
