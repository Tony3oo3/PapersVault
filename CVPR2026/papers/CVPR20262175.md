## Problem

Existing crowdsourced evaluations of automated neuron explanations only assess top-activating inputs, effectively measuring Recall while ignoring Precision, and are prohibitively expensive when extended to full-distribution correlation-based evaluation. The paper tackles how to reliably estimate the Pearson correlation between neuron activations and concept presence labels using a small, strategically chosen subset of human-labeled inputs, reducing evaluation cost from ~$90,000 to ~$2,160.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262175/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a neuron's activation vector over a probing dataset (e.g., 50k ImageNet validation images), a text explanation, and SigLIP concept-presence predictions as the model-guided signal. Human raters on AMT provide binary concept-present/absent judgments on a small subset (180 inputs × 3 raters per neuron), and the output is an unbiased Pearson correlation coefficient estimating explanation quality. The SigLIP model serves a dual role: it defines the importance sampling distribution for input selection and provides the prior in the Bayesian rating aggregation, making the final estimate a hybrid of human and model knowledge.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary techniques. First, Model-Guided Importance Sampling (MG-IS) constructs a proposal distribution proportional to |normalized_activation × normalized_SigLIP_concept|, mixed 80/20 with uniform sampling to ensure unbiasedness, then applies importance-weighted corrections to estimate the correlation, concept mean, and variance from the labeled subset. Second, Bayes Rating Aggregation (BRAgg) models each rater as making independent errors at a fixed rate η (estimated at 23%), and computes the posterior probability of concept presence via Bayes' rule, using either a uniform prior (β=0.05) or a SigLIP-derived prior clipped to [0.001, 0.999]. Together, MG-IS reduces the number of inputs needed by ~13× and BRAgg reduces ratings per input by ~3×, yielding a ~40× total cost reduction while maintaining correlation estimation accuracy within 20–28% relative error.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative contribution is a ~40× reduction in crowdsourced evaluation cost (from ~$90,000 to ~$2,160) by combining Model-Guided Importance Sampling (~13× fewer inputs) with Bayes Rating Aggregation (~3× fewer ratings per input). In the large-scale crowdsourced study evaluating 100 neurons per model across ResNet-50 (Layer 4) and ViT-B-16 (Layer 11 MLP), Linear Explanations with SigLIP (LE(SigLIP)) achieves the highest Pearson correlation scores: 0.2413 on ResNet-50 and 0.2968 on ViT-B-16, outperforming all other methods. The best overall correlation scores remain modest (~0.2–0.3), highlighting that current automated interpretability methods still have significant room for improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LE(SigLIP) [23] clearly outperforms all other methods on both models, achieving 0.2413 correlation on ResNet-50 (vs. 0.1904 for CLIP-Dissect [22] and 0.1867 for INVERT [7]) and 0.2968 on ViT-B-16 (vs. 0.1849 for CLIP-Dissect and 0.1343 for INVERT). The LLM-based methods DnD [1] and MAIA [29] underperform simpler CLIP-based baselines, with DnD scoring 0.1534/0.1049 and MAIA scoring 0.1396/0.1497, attributed to their focus on top-activating inputs only and higher variance in explanation quality. Broden-based methods (ND [3], MILAN [15]) perform worst overall, likely because their concept sets lack higher-level concepts needed for later-layer neurons. The crowdsourced study (Figure 5) confirms the automated ranking, with LE(SigLIP) maintaining its lead, while CLIP-Dissect and INVERT trade the second-best position depending on the model. Overall, all methods achieve relatively low correlations (~0.1–0.3), indicating that no current method provides highly reliable neuron explanations across the full activation distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the evaluation framework proposed by NeuronEval [24], which identified Pearson correlation as the principled metric for neuron explanations but did not conduct actual human studies using it. Prior crowdsourced evaluations (Network Dissection, DnD, CLIP-Dissect) only measured whether explanations matched top-activating inputs (effectively Recall), ignoring precision and lower-activation behavior. The paper's two core techniques—Model-Guided Importance Sampling and Bayes Rating Aggregation—draw on classical statistical estimation (importance sampling theory) and Bayesian rater modeling, respectively, but are the first to combine them specifically for full-distribution correlation estimation of neuron explanations at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40× cost reduction makes systematic, full-distribution evaluation of interpretability methods feasible for the first time, lowering the barrier from ~$90k to ~$2k per study. The finding that simple Linear Explanations outperform recent LLM-based methods (MAIA, DnD) challenges the assumption that more sophisticated generative pipelines yield better neuron descriptions, suggesting the field may be over-investing in LLM complexity at the expense of coverage across the full activation range. However, the overall correlation scores remain quite low (~0.2 even for the best method), indicating that current explanation methods are still far from adequately capturing neuron function, and the techniques are validated only on ImageNet-trained vision models, leaving open whether they generalize to language models or other modalities.
