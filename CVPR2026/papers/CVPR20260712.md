## Problem

Multi-view image editing from sparse input views (e.g., a few casually captured photos) remains unsolved: per-scene neural fields (NeRF, 3DGS) lack a data-driven 3D prior in their weights and overfit with limited views, while temporal-attention methods fail under significant viewpoint differences. The paper tackles how to produce instruction-faithful edits that remain geometrically and appearance-consistent across all views when only a handful of source images are available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260712/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N unordered sparse RGB images (N=4 in main experiments) of a static scene along with their 4×4 camera pose matrices and a free-form text editing instruction. It outputs N edited RGB images that are mutually multi-view consistent, i.e., they correspond to renderings of a single underlying edited 3D scene. The student model (SEVA) is an 'M-in, N-out' architecture requiring at least one clean reference latent in addition to the N noisy latents, so one input frame is pre-edited by the teacher and encoded as a conditioning signal throughout distillation.

## Method

I-Mix2Mix replaces the conventional neural-field consolidator in Score Distillation Sampling with a pretrained multi-view diffusion model (SEVA) whose weights encode a data-driven 3D prior, then distills editing knowledge from a frozen monocular teacher (InstructPix2Pix) into this student. Four key SDS adaptations make this work: (1) incremental single-step distillation at each student timestep τ via Tweedie estimates rather than full sampling trajectories; (2) lightweight bilinear-interpolation alignment between student and teacher latent spaces instead of costly decode-encode; (3) a stochastic TruncNorm teacher noise schedule that prevents collapse to near-identity reconstructions; and (4) Random Cross-View Attention (RCVAttn), where at each iteration a randomly selected key frame's tokens are attended to by all other frames in the teacher U-Net, enforcing cross-view coherence at zero additional computational cost. The student is personalized iteratively from τ=T down to τ=0, with k optimization steps per timestep, yielding final edited views that are both instruction-faithful and multi-view consistent.

## Results

On the sparse-view editing benchmark (N=4 views, 20 edits across 3 I-N2N test scenes), I-Mix2Mix achieves the highest CLIP Directional Consistency (0.342) among all methods, indicating superior multi-view coherence. It also attains the best CLIP Similarity (0.258) and competitive CLIP Directional Similarity (0.173). In a human study against the strongest baseline DGE, I-Mix2Mix produces fewer inconsistencies on average (1.34 vs. 2.02) and wins 75% of scene-level comparisons, with 65% of scenes rated fully consistent versus only 34% for DGE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

I-Mix2Mix outperforms all four baselines on the primary multi-view consistency metric (CLIP Directional Consistency), with the largest margin over I-N2N (0.342 vs. 0.034), which fails entirely in the sparse-view regime due to NeRF fitting artifacts. Against the 3DGS-based methods I-GS2GS (0.314) and DGE (0.287), the consistency gains are 0.028 and 0.055 respectively, reflecting the overfitting of 3DGS to limited views. On per-frame edit quality, I-Mix2Mix leads in CLIP Similarity (0.258) over all baselines, but DGE edges it out on CLIP Directional Similarity (0.182 vs. 0.173), suggesting DGE's extended-attention mechanism produces slightly stronger per-frame semantic alignment at the cost of cross-view coherence. The human study confirms the quantitative advantage: I-Mix2Mix achieves a 75% scene win rate over DGE and reduces the proportion of highly inconsistent scenes (≥3 inconsistencies) from 31% to 13%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) framework from DreamFusion by replacing the conventional per-scene neural field student (NeRF or 3DGS) with a pretrained multi-view diffusion model (SEVA), leveraging its data-driven 3D prior for cross-view consistency. It departs from prior sparse-view editing methods that rely on extended self-attention (T2VZ, DGE) or 3DGS consolidation, which the authors argue overfit or fail to enforce fine-grained consistency under limited viewpoints. The key methodological contributions—incremental per-timestep distillation, a stochastic teacher noise schedule, and zero-cost Random Cross-View Attention—are novel adaptations required to make SDS work with a diffusion-model student rather than a differentiable renderer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: editing scenes from a handful of casually captured photos, where dense-view 3D reconstruction methods (NeRF, 3DGS) break down. The modular SDS-based framework can in principle swap in stronger future backbones (better editors, better multi-view models), and the RCVAttn mechanism is a lightweight technique potentially reusable in other multi-view diffusion settings. However, the method is more than twice as slow as DGE, inherits backbone limitations (InstructPix2Pix and SEVA quality ceilings), and extends poorly to non-editing tasks like conditional generation where SDS blurriness persists. Reducing the per-timestep distillation overhead and generalizing beyond instruction-based editing remain open.
