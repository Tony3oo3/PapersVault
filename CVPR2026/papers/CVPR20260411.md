## Problem

State-of-the-art MLLMs fail on knowledge-intensive VQA queries where relevant information is underrepresented in pre-training data. Existing retrieval-augmented approaches for KB-VQA suffer from low retrieval precision that injects noisy passages into the generator context, and they lack explicit reasoning capabilities to critically evaluate and leverage retrieved evidence before producing an answer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260411/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query RGB image, a free-form text question, and a set of retrieved Wikipedia passages (filtered by a critic model) as input. The output is a structured generation containing an explicit natural-language reasoning trace followed by a final answer, with the two components delimited by special tokens added to the vocabulary. The reasoning trace is unconstrained in format (no predefined pattern), while the answer must conform to dataset-specific formats (e.g., single/multi-answer, numerical/textual).

## Method

ReAG combines a multi-level retrieval pipeline (coarse-grained full-image retrieval plus fine-grained retrieval using a GroundingDINO-cropped region, both via EVA-CLIP-8B) with a critic MLLM that binary-classifies each retrieved passage as relevant or irrelevant, reducing noise by ~16–18% compared to prior filtering. The generator (Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B) is trained in two stages: a supervised fine-tuning cold-start on reasoning traces linking passages to answers, followed by reinforcement learning using a GRPO-inspired objective with DAPO modifications (no KL penalty, token-level loss) and rule-based rewards for answer accuracy and output format. Unlike prior multimodal RAG methods that rely on implicit reflection tokens, ReAG trains the model to produce full natural-language reasoning traces, making the inference process interpretable and grounded in retrieved evidence.

## Results

On Encyclopedic-VQA (E-VQA), ReAG with Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves 47.0 BEM on the full test set and 44.9 on single-hop, outperforming the strongest prior method (VLM-PRF with InternVL3-8B at 39.2/40.1) by +7.8 points. On InfoSeek, ReAG (7B) reaches 47.2 overall accuracy, surpassing ReflectiVA (43.9) by +3.3 and VLM-PRF (42.8) by +4.4. The 3B variant also sets new state-of-the-art, gaining +7.7 over ReflectiVA on E-VQA and +4.3 on InfoSeek overall. Under oracle document access (Table 3), ReAG further extends its lead, reaching 81.5 on E-VQA single-hop and 59.7 on InfoSeek overall with the 7B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReAG consistently outperforms all retrieval-augmented baselines on both benchmarks. On E-VQA with the 3B generator, ReAG (42.9 All) beats ReflectiVA (35.2) by +7.7 and VLM-PRF (32.4) by +10.5; with the 7B generator, it surpasses VLM-PRF with InternVL3-8B (39.2) by +7.8. On InfoSeek, ReAG (7B) achieves 47.2 overall, exceeding ReflectiVA (43.9) by +3.3, VLM-PRF (42.8) by +4.4, and mKG-RAG (40.5) by +6.7. Zero-shot MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL at 18.3–23.7) are far below all retrieval-augmented methods, confirming the necessity of external knowledge. The gains are consistent across both model scales and both datasets, with the largest absolute improvements on E-VQA where the knowledge base is larger (2M pages) and retrieval noise is more severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReAG extends the multimodal RAG pipeline for KB-VQA by combining three previously separate ideas: multi-level retrieval (coarse image-level plus fine-grained region-level), a learned critic for passage filtering, and reinforcement learning for reasoning over retrieved evidence. The RL stage directly builds on GRPO (DeepSeekMath) and DAPO, adopting their clipped surrogate objective while dropping the KL penalty and computing loss at the token level; the multi-stage SFT-then-RL training protocol follows DeepSeek-R1. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior multimodal RAG methods like ReflectiVA (which uses implicit control tokens) and VLM-PRF (which uses external tools for filtering) by replacing these with an explicit natural-language reasoning trace and a dedicated autoregressive critic model, making the filtering step agnostic to the retrieval backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that retrieval noise is a first-order problem in KB-VQA: the ablation shows that naively feeding all top-20 passages actually degrades performance below zero-shot, and that the critic model alone recovers most of the gap. This reframes the bottleneck from 'better retrieval' to 'better filtering plus better reasoning over what is retrieved,' a practical insight for anyone building RAG systems on top of MLLMs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Wikipedia-scale knowledge bases to more heterogeneous or rapidly updating corpora, how the critic model scales when the document pool grows to millions, and whether the RL stage's gains persist when the generator backbone is much larger than 7B.
