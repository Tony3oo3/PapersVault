## Problem

Existing video scene segmentation methods are encoder-based classifiers that exhibit visual-centric biases (ignoring dialogue and character cues), classify each shot in isolation without leveraging causal dependencies across consecutive decisions, and provide no natural-language explanation for their boundary predictions. No prior work has applied a vision-language model to this task, leaving a gap in multimodal narrative reasoning and explainability for scene boundary detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260093/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a context window of 20 consecutive shots, each represented by 3 sampled frames (with a small shot-ID marker overlaid for temporal anchoring), synchronized subtitles extracted via Whisper, and optional actor-identity metadata. It sequentially emits a Yes/No boundary verdict for each of the 10 central shots in the focus window in the format 'Shot i: Yes/No', from which a confidence score is derived as P(Yes)/(P(Yes)+P(No)) over the token-level logits. An optional aligned variant additionally appends a short natural-language rationale after each boundary decision.

## Method

Scene-VLM fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-7B via next-token prediction on structured multimodal shot sequences, replacing the standard point-wise classification paradigm with sequential generation where each shot's boundary decision is causally conditioned on all preceding predictions. A context-focus window design (20-shot context, 10-shot focus) ensures every evaluated shot has sufficient temporal padding from both sides, preventing the edge-position degradation observed without margins. Confidence scores are extracted by probing the softmax probabilities of the 'Yes' and 'No' tokens at each shot's verdict position, enabling controllable precision-recall trade-offs typically reserved for encoder-based classifiers. For explainability, a minimal additional fine-tuning stage on just 35 human-annotated rationale examples aligns the model to produce coherent, grounded natural-language explanations for its boundary decisions, eliminating the parsing failures and hallucinations observed with naive prompting.

## Results

On MovieNet-318, Scene-VLM achieves 62.1 F1 and 66.8 AP, setting a new state of the art with gains of +6.8 F1 and +8.2 AP over the previous best F1 method (MEGA) and +13.7 F1 and +6.0 AP over the previous best AP method (TranS4mer). Zero-shot on BBC Planet Earth, Scene-VLM reaches 45.8 AP, outperforming TranS4mer by +2.2 AP. On the video chaptering task (VidChapters-7M), under a matched Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, Scene-VLM achieves 32.2 F1 and 63.9 tIoU, surpassing the adapted Chapter-LLaMA baseline (28.4 F1, 59.5 tIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scene-VLM outperforms all seven baselines on the primary MovieNet-318 benchmark. Against the strongest encoder-based methods, it gains +6.8 F1 / +8.2 AP over MEGA (55.3 / 58.6) and +13.7 F1 / +6.0 AP over TranS4mer (48.4 / 60.8), the two prior leaders on F1 and AP respectively. On the zero-shot BBC Planet Earth benchmark, Scene-VLM (45.8 AP) beats TranS4mer (43.6), TimeSformer (42.2), and BaSSL (40.0). On video chaptering, Scene-VLM with a Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone (32.2 F1, 63.9 tIoU) outperforms Chapter-LLaMA adapted to the same backbone (28.4 F1, 59.5 tIoU), though the original Chapter-LLaMA with its native LLaMA-3.1-8B backbone (42.6 F1) remains stronger in absolute terms. The authors attribute the chaptering gap to Chapter-LLaMA's text-only pipeline (caption-then-reason) versus Scene-VLM's direct end-to-end multimodal processing of raw frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established encoder-based paradigm for video scene segmentation (BaSSL, TranS4mer, MEGA, ShotCoL), which relies on point-wise classification within local windows and visual-centric features, by repurposing a generative VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) as a sequential multimodal reasoner over shot-level inputs. The closest conceptual predecessor is Chapter-LLaMA, which applies LLMs to video chaptering, but that method is text-only (caption-based) and targets web videos rather than cinematic content. The key architectural novelty is the context-focus window design combined with confidence extraction from token-level logits, which bridges the gap between generative VLMs and the discriminative scoring interface that downstream applications expect.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that VLMs can match or exceed specialized encoder architectures on scene segmentation while simultaneously providing confidence scores and natural-language rationales—capabilities that were previously mutually exclusive. The finding that only 35 annotated examples suffice to align the model toward reliable explanation generation suggests a broader principle about minimal supervision for VLM alignment in structured prediction tasks. Open questions include whether the sequential prediction paradigm scales to very long videos (beyond the 20-shot context window), whether the structured Yes/No format can be relaxed without sacrificing confidence extraction, and whether the approach generalizes to non-cinematic domains without retraining. The chaptering results, while positive under matched backbones, still trail the specialized Chapter-LLaMA, indicating the paradigm shift has not yet fully closed the gap on all related tasks.
