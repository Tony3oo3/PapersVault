## Problem

Precisely editing both camera and object motion in a video remains unsolved: camera-controlled V2V methods preserve object motion by design, object-centric methods lack camera control, and track-conditioned I2V models lose full scene context by conditioning only on the first frame. No existing framework jointly manipulates camera viewpoint and fine-grained 3D object motion while preserving the spatiotemporal coherence of the original video.

## Input / Output

The method takes an 81-frame source video (672×384), paired source and target 3D point tracks (N≈500–1000 tracks, projected to 2D screen coordinates with normalized disparity z∈[0,1]), and corresponding camera poses, all estimated via off-the-shelf tools (SAM2, CoTracker3, SpatialTrackerV2). It outputs an 81-frame edited video at the same resolution with the specified camera and/or object motion changes. Notably, the model supports partial track input (e.g., specifying only body motion while omitting legs) and processes a full video in ~4.5 minutes on an A100 with 50 denoising steps.

## Method

Edit-by-Track fine-tunes the Wan-2.1 T2V DiT (1.3B) with LoRA (rank=64) using a novel 3D track conditioner that adaptively encodes paired 3D tracks into screen-aligned tokens via coordinate-based cross-attention: source track coordinate embeddings query source video tokens to sample visual context, Transformer blocks aggregate temporal information per track, and a second cross-attention splats the sampled tokens into both source and target frame spaces, with depth (z) positional embeddings injected for 3D-aware occlusion reasoning. Source and noisy target video tokens are concatenated along the spatial dimension, and the resulting track token pairs are element-wise added before feeding into DiT blocks. Training uses a two-stage strategy: Stage 1 bootstraps motion control on synthetic Blender scenes (Mixamo humans + Kubric backgrounds) with ground-truth 3D tracks, then Stage 2 fine-tunes on 24K real monocular videos where pairs are formed by sampling non-contiguous clips to simulate joint camera/object motion changes. The key design choice is omitting visibility labels and attention bias terms, allowing the model to implicitly resolve occlusions from the 3D depth cues in the tracks.

## Results

On the DyCheck dataset (joint camera and object motion editing, 12 scenes), Edit-by-Track achieves PSNR 14.80, SSIM 0.424, and LPIPS 0.406 (full-frame), and mPSNR 15.99, mSSIM 0.747, mLPIPS 0.247 (masked), outperforming all baselines by a clear margin. On 100 in-the-wild MiraData videos, the 1.3B model reaches PSNR 19.55, SSIM 0.657, LPIPS 0.236, and the best track-control EPE of 6.12, surpassing the 14B ATI model on all metrics except FVD (306.44 vs. 268.80). The method requires no ground-truth first frame or optical flow, unlike several competing approaches that rely on privileged information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Edit-by-Track outperforms all seven baselines on every metric in the DyCheck joint-motion evaluation, with the largest gaps over I2V-based methods (TrajAttn, DaS, PaC, ATI) that lose full scene context by conditioning only on the first frame. Among V2V inpainting baselines, GEN3C and TrajCrafter struggle to synthesize plausible object motion even when given warped inputs, and their performance degrades further under large viewpoint changes where optical-flow estimation fails. The strongest baseline, TrajAttn extended with NVS-Solver, still trails Edit-by-Track by ~0.86 dB PSNR and 0.102 LPIPS on DyCheck, and by ~2.4 dB PSNR and 5.3 EPE on in-the-wild MiraData videos. On MiraData, the 1.3B Edit-by-Track model beats the 14B ATI model on PSNR (+0.48), SSIM (+0.022), LPIPS (−0.008), and EPE (6.12 vs. 11.44), though ATI achieves a lower FVD (268.80 vs. 306.44), suggesting slightly better temporal diversity at the cost of track adherence. Notably, Edit-by-Track requires no privileged ground-truth information (no GT first frame, no GT optical flow), whereas several baselines marked with * depend on such information to reach their reported scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two main lines of work: track-conditioned I2V models (TrajAttn, DaS, PaC, ATI) that generate motion from a single frame and lose full scene context, and camera-controlled V2V methods (GEN3C, TrajCrafter) that handle only viewpoint changes via inpainting. The key architectural departure is the 3D track conditioner, which adapts Tracktention's coordinate-based cross-attention into a learnable sampling-and-splatting mechanism that projects visual context from source video tokens into target frame space with explicit depth cues. The two-stage training (synthetic Mixamo/Kubric scenes → real monocular video pairs) is a practical solution to the absence of large-scale annotated video pairs with controlled motion variations, building on the LoRA fine-tuning paradigm applied to the Wan-2.1 DiT backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes the first framework for joint camera and object motion editing in a single V2V pass, a capability that prior methods could only address in isolation. The 3D track conditioner's cross-attention sampling/splatting mechanism and the finding that omitting visibility labels (letting the model implicitly reason about occlusion) works well are reusable design choices for future track-conditioned generation. Limitations remain: the method struggles with densely clustered tracks on small objects and cannot synthesize complex physical consequences of edited motions (e.g., correct splash patterns), reflecting the limited physical grounding of current diffusion priors. Scaling the real-data fine-tuning pipeline to larger, more diverse video collections is a natural next step that could close these gaps.
