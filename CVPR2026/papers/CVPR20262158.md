## Problem

Existing 3D human avatar reconstruction methods built on 3D Gaussian Splatting and SMPL require sharp, high-quality multi-view video, yet real-world captures inevitably contain motion-induced blur that distorts both the learned 3D geometry and the estimated SMPL parameters. Two-stage pipelines that first apply 2D deblurring and then train the avatar model fail because 2D deblurring cannot enforce multi-view consistency, leaving residual artifacts and misaligned reconstructions. This paper is the first to directly reconstruct sharp, animatable 3DGS avatars from blurry video by jointly modeling the 3D blur formation process and sub-frame human motion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes blurry multi-view RGB frames (4 training views at 512×512 or 612×512 resolution), coarse SMPL pose/shape/LBS parameters estimated by EasyMocap from the blurry video, and human masks from SAM. It produces a canonical 3D Gaussian Splatting avatar with optimized SMPL parameters (pose, shape, LBS weights) that can be animated to novel poses. The real-world benchmark uses a 360-degree hybrid-exposure rig of 12 synchronized Blackfly cameras: 4 record at 50 ms exposure (blurry) and 8 at 3.125 ms (sharp ground truth for novel-view evaluation), with a 1/16 ND filter on the blurry cameras to match pixel intensity.

## Method

The key insight is reformulating the 2D motion-blur integral as a 3D-aware process: the observed blurry image is modeled as the average of T virtual sharp frames, each rendered by rasterizing the canonical 3DGS after warping it with an intermediate SMPL pose. Sub-frame motion within each exposure is parameterized by B-spline interpolation (P=4 control knots) over the 24 SMPL joint rotations, augmented by a small CNN that predicts per-joint, per-timestep pose displacements to capture high-frequency non-rigid variations. A geodesic-distance regularization between the last pose of one exposure and the first pose of the next resolves the directional ambiguity inherent in motion blur. The canonical 3DGS, SMPL shape, and LBS weight offsets (predicted by a CNN from Gaussian coordinates) are jointly optimized with an L1 photometric loss between the synthesized and observed blurry frames, requiring only coarse SMPL initialization and no sharp reference images.

## Results

On the synthetic ZJU-MoCap-based dataset, the proposed method achieves 25.55 dB PSNR, 0.8290 SSIM, and 0.1476 LPIPS, outperforming the best two-stage baseline (BSST+GauHuman) by 2.47 dB in PSNR and 0.059 in SSIM. On the real 360-degree hybrid-exposure dataset, it reaches 27.01 dB PSNR, 0.8271 SSIM, and 0.1668 LPIPS, surpassing the strongest baseline by 1.44 dB in PSNR and 0.020 in SSIM. The method also generalizes across blur magnitudes (K_blur = 7–11) and view counts (N_view = 2–4), maintaining consistent advantages over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines: direct GauHuman training on blurry frames, and four two-stage pipelines that first apply a 2D video deblurring model (ShiftNet, RVRT, VRT, or BSST) before training GauHuman. The proposed method decisively outperforms all baselines on every metric across both datasets. The two-stage baselines (ShiftNet/RVRT/VRT/BSST + GauHuman) cluster tightly together, achieving only ~0.003 dB PSNR improvement over direct GauHuman, confirming that 2D deblurring fails to enforce multi-view consistency and thus provides negligible benefit to the 3DGS reconstruction. The gap between the proposed method and the best baseline is largest on the synthetic dataset (~2.5 dB PSNR) and still substantial on the real dataset (~1.4 dB PSNR, ~0.02 SSIM, ~0.07 LPIPS). The authors attribute the baseline weakness to per-view 2D deblurring inconsistencies that corrupt the 3DGS optimization, whereas their 3D-aware blur model jointly resolves motion ambiguity and learns a coherent canonical avatar.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) human avatar line (GauHuman, GaussianAvatar, 3DGS-Avatar) by addressing a practical failure mode: motion blur in input video. Rather than the natural two-stage pipeline of 2D video deblurring followed by 3DGS training, it formulates a 3D-aware blur formation model that jointly optimizes sub-frame SMPL motion (via B-spline interpolation and a CNN-based pose deformation network) and the canonical 3DGS representation. The approach draws on the physics-based blur integration model from multi-view deblurring work (Deblur-NeRF, BAD-Gaussians) but re-derives it in the 3D avatar parameterization space, and on the SMPL/LBS framework for structured human motion. It also builds on the ZJU-MoCap/EasyMocap calibration pipeline and introduces a novel 360-degree hybrid-exposure camera system for real-world benchmarking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to reconstruct sharp, animatable 3D human avatars directly from motion-blurred video, and it establishes the first dedicated benchmarks (synthetic + real hybrid-exposure captures) for this task, which will be useful for the broader 3D avatar and deblurring communities. Key limitations remain: the SMPL-based motion model cannot represent handheld objects or loose garments, the sRGB averaging approximation may introduce physical inaccuracies in high-contrast regions, and geometry recovery (normals, BRDF) is still constrained by the 3DGS representation. Open questions include whether the 3D-aware blur formulation generalizes beyond SMPL-parameterized humans (e.g., to full-body with accessories) and whether the approach can handle rolling-shutter or global-shutter blur simultaneously.
