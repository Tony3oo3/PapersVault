## Problem

Existing zero-shot and few-shot AI-generated image detectors frequently misclassify synthetic images that have undergone physical remapping (e.g., print+scan) as real, because they lack a principled criterion for distinguishing real from synthetic images. The paper argues that the fundamental distinction lies in whether an image has a prior physical-world origin—specifically, whether it was captured as RAW sensor data and converted through an ISP pipeline—rather than in digital-space artifacts alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263143/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At test time, the method consumes a single RGB image (no RAW data required) and produces a fixed-dimensional forensic feature vector (the alignment trace) in a learned latent space. In zero-shot mode, this trace is used for unsupervised clustering (evaluated via NMI, ARI, SC) or pairwise similarity scoring (cosine similarity, AUC) to separate real from synthetic images. In few-shot mode, the trace space is fine-tuned with a small set of known GenAI samples (e.g., ProGAN) to yield a real/synthetic classifier with cross-domain generalization to unseen generators.

## Method

The alignment trace is a trainable latent feature designed to be content-invariant yet pipeline-discriminative, learned entirely from ~10^9 synthetic RAW-to-RGB pipelines generated from 5,000 real DNG images (FiveK). Three branches jointly optimize the shared space: (1) an RGB-Vision branch distills features from a frozen ViT backbone using Stratified Entropy Attention (SEA), which bins spatial tokens by physical entropy (gradient + luminance-chrominance), performs stratified sampling for balanced coverage, and applies entropy-weighted attention to emphasize physics-related signals over semantic content; (2) a RAW-Graph branch encodes each ISP pipeline as a directed topological graph (typed nodes for demosaicing, white balance, tone mapping, post-processing; weighted edges for sequential dependencies and normalized parameters) and maps it to the feature space via a GraphConv GNN, aligned to the RGB trace through a CLIP-style symmetric contrastive loss; (3) a RAW-Vision branch aligns the latent Gaussian distributions of RAW and RGB images (from a pre-trained VAE encoder) via KL divergence, with the alignment trace modulating the RAW mean through cross-attention. At inference, a query image is flagged as synthetic if its trace deviates (by KL divergence) beyond a threshold from the learned manifold of known pipelines.

## Results

In the zero-shot clustering evaluation (Table 1), the proposed method achieves an average NMI of 0.964, ARI of 0.978, and SC of 0.829 across 10 GAN/DM synthesis types, a 22.2% NMI improvement over the second-best method MIB (0.742). In the zero-shot similarity evaluation (Table 2), it reaches an average AUC of 0.925 versus 0.734 for MIB. In the few-shot cross-domain detection on ForenSynths (Table 3), fine-tuning with only ProGAN priors yields an average AP of 0.987, outperforming the supervised SOTA Effort (0.953) by 3.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 14 baselines across every evaluation setting. In the few-shot ForenSynths benchmark (Table 3), it achieves 0.987 avg AP, beating the closest competitor Effort [68] (0.953) by 3.4% and CNNSpot [55] (0.919) by 6.8%; the gap is most pronounced on the hardest categories—SAN (0.957 vs. Effort's 0.815 and CNNSpot's 0.705) and Facial Deepfake (0.940 vs. Effort's 0.876). In zero-shot clustering (Table 1), the method's 0.964 avg NMI surpasses MIB [9] (0.742) by 22.2%, FSD [39] (0.596) by 38.1%, and ZED [16] (0.544) by 42.3%, with particularly dramatic gains on diffusion-model outputs (e.g., MidJourney: 0.990 vs. MIB's 0.730). In zero-shot similarity (Table 2), the 0.925 avg AUC exceeds MIB (0.734) by 26.0% and FSD (0.683) by 35.4%. RAW-reconstruction baselines R2LCM [57] and ReRAW [8], not designed for detection, show the weakest performance (avg NMI 0.147 and 0.132), confirming that raw reconstruction error alone is insufficient without the proposed alignment framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of RAW image forensics and zero-shot synthetic detection. Rather than building on a single prior method, it repurposes ideas from RAW-to-RGB reconstruction (ReRAW, RawDiff), CLIP-style contrastive alignment, GNN-based pipeline encoding, and VAE latent-space supervision into a unified RAW-RGB alignment framework. Its key conceptual departure from prior zero-shot detectors (MIB, ZED, FSD, ZeroFake) is grounding the real/synthetic distinction in physical-world origin rather than digital-space statistical traces, and operationalizing this through a jointly optimized feature space supervised by RGB pixels, ISP pipeline topologies, and RAW visual features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The redefinition of 'synthetic' as lacking prior physical-world existence resolves a practical ambiguity (e.g., a printed-and-scanned AI image becomes 'real') that has confounded evaluation in prior work. The method's strong zero-shot performance (NMI 0.964, AUC 0.925) and few-shot cross-domain results (AP 0.987) suggest RAW-RGB alignment is a robust forensic signal, but its dependence on authentic RAW-RGB training pairs (FiveK) limits immediate deployment where RAW data is unavailable. Open questions include robustness to digital post-processing after physical capture, generalization across camera models beyond Canon/NIKON, and whether the physical-origin definition holds for increasingly realistic synthetic pipelines that simulate sensor characteristics.
