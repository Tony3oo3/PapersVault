## Problem

Reinforcement fine-tuning of LLMs relies on coarse-grained rewards (outcome or step-level process rewards) that leave hundreds of tokens within a single reasoning step without differentiated supervision, causing credit-assignment ambiguity and training instability. Existing token-level reward approaches either require training dedicated reward models on noisy pseudo-labels at substantial cost, or use heuristic proxies like token entropy that lack semantic alignment with actual reasoning quality. The paper asks how to obtain principled, fine-grained token-level rewards from an existing differentiable reward model without any additional training.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a generated token sequence (reasoning trajectory) and a pre-trained differentiable Process Reward Model (PRM) that scores the sequence at the step level. It produces a scalar token-level reward for every token in the sequence, structured so that the sum of all token rewards exactly equals the original coarse process reward. The null token used for attribution is the [PAD] token from the model's existing vocabulary, chosen because Transformer attention mechanisms already mask it as semantically inert, requiring no new parameters or embeddings.

## Method

P2T defines each token's attribution score as the change in the PRM's process reward when that token's embedding is replaced by a null (padding) token embedding. Computing this naively requires N separate forward passes, but the paper shows a first-order Taylor approximation reduces it to a single forward and backward pass: the score is the inner product of the PRM's gradient with respect to the token embedding and the difference between the token embedding and the null embedding. These raw attribution scores are then passed through a softmax normalization and scaled by a hyperparameter ω, and added to the original process reward as a baseline to yield the final per-token reward. The resulting token rewards are integrated into GRPO's advantage function as an additive term weighted by α, enabling precise credit assignment during policy optimization without co-training any auxiliary network.

## Results

On math reasoning benchmarks (Table 1), P2T token reward consistently outperforms outcome reward across all tested models: +6.3% average on Qwen2.5-Math-7B (51.9 vs 45.6), +10.0% on LLaMA3.2-3B-Instruct (33.9 vs 23.9), and +8.0% on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (54.5 vs 46.5). The largest individual gains are +11.5% on AIME24 (Qwen2.5-Math-7B) and +14.2% on MinervaMath (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B). For multimodal models, P2T achieves +4.9% on MathVista for Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and +5.6% for LLaVA-CoT-11B. P2T also accelerates GRPO convergence by approximately 4× compared to outcome reward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares P2T against two existing token-level reward methods (PRIME and SPRO) on the Eurus-2-7B-SFT model using Eurus-2-RL-Data (Table 2). P2T achieves 40.7% average across six benchmarks, outperforming SPRO (38.4%) by +2.3% and PRIME (36.0%) by +4.7%. P2T wins on every individual benchmark: AMC (33.8 vs 31.9 SPRO, 31.2 PRIME), MATH (55.4 vs 53.6, 52.7), OlympiadBench (30.6 vs 28.2, 25.4), K12 (56.1 vs 55.0, 54.6), CodeForces (32.3 vs 29.4, 26.4), and CodeContests (35.8 vs 32.1, 25.9). A key practical advantage is that P2T is training-free during RL (no co-optimized auxiliary network like PRIME), while SPRO relies on a heuristic log-probability proxy that lacks explicit semantic alignment with reasoning quality. Additionally, P2T can serve as a secondary fine-tuning step on already-converged outcome-reward models, yielding further gains of +12.1% on MinervaMath and +6.8% on AIME24 for DeepScaleR-1.5B-Preview.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of process reward modeling and token-level credit assignment in RL fine-tuning. It builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeekMath) as the policy optimization backbone, modifying its advantage function to incorporate token-level rewards. Conceptually, it departs from two existing families of token-reward methods: (1) training-based approaches like PRIME, OREAL, and TVM that require co-optimizing or separately training a token-level reward model, and (2) heuristic proxies like SPRO or token entropy that lack semantic grounding. The key departure is reframing token attribution as a first-order Taylor approximation of the influence function around a null token, reducing the problem to a single forward-backward pass through an existing PRM without any additional training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in making fine-grained credit assignment accessible to any lab with an existing PRM, eliminating the need for expensive token-level annotation or auxiliary model training. The finding that P2T can serve as a secondary optimization step—further improving models already converged under outcome reward (e.g., +12.1% on MinervaMath for DeepScaleR-1.5B)—suggests a new two-stage training paradigm that could become standard practice. Open questions include whether the first-order approximation degrades for very long sequences where higher-order token interactions matter, whether the method extends to non-differentiable or LLM-as-judge reward models, and whether the PAD token's assumed semantic neutrality holds across all architectures. The 4× convergence speedup, if reproducible at scale, would substantially reduce RL training costs.
