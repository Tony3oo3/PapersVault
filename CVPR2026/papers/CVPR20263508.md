## Problem

State-of-the-art single image reflection removal methods (e.g., DSRNet, DSIT, RDNet) rely on large pre-trained CNN or Transformer backbones, incurring 150–350M parameters and 140–360 GFLOPs that preclude deployment on mobile or edge devices. The paper identifies that reflections are predominantly concentrated in the low-frequency (LL) wavelet component while high-frequency details are nearly identical between the mixed and transmission layers, making uniform full-frequency processing both suboptimal and computationally wasteful.

## Input / Output

The network takes a single 224×224 RGB image containing reflection contamination (M = T + R) and outputs the estimated transmission layer T. At inference, the model is tested on resolutions up to 1024×1024, where its efficiency advantage over SOTA methods widens dramatically. The architecture is a U-shaped encoder-decoder with 9.50M parameters and 9.62 GFLOPs, requiring no pre-trained backbone at test time.

## Method

LightRR's core is the Asymmetric Frequency Mamba Block (AFM), which applies DWT to decompose features into LL/LH/HL/HH sub-bands, then routes the artifact-rich LL component through a 2D Mamba-based state space model for long-range context modeling while processing the three HF components with a lightweight depthwise convolution. The U-Net's downsampling (WDD) and upsampling (WSU) modules exploit the same wavelet decomposition to skip HF details via direct connections, ensuring all deep AFM blocks operate only on 1/4-resolution low-frequency features. A knowledge distillation strategy trains the lightweight encoder against a frozen VGG19 teacher using a feature-matching L1 loss, so the teacher is discarded entirely at inference. Training uses a two-stage schedule (40 epochs with distillation loss, then 40 epochs fine-tuning without it) on synthetic PASCAL VOC pairs plus real data, with a combined reconstruction, perceptual, and distillation loss.

## Results

On the four real-world benchmarks (Real20, Objects, PostCard, Wild) with the full training set (w/ Nature), LightRR achieves an average PSNR of 26.39 dB and SSIM of 0.915, trailing the best SOTA methods RDNet (26.65 dB) and DSIT (26.49 dB) by only 0.26 dB and 0.10 dB respectively. The critical advantage is efficiency: LightRR uses just 9.50 M parameters and 9.62 GFLOPs, corresponding to 3.01% of RDNet's parameters and 5.22% of its FLOPs. In the ablation against scaled-down SOTA counterparts (Table 3), LightRR outperforms RDNet-light (25.14 dB) and DSIT-light (25.16 dB) by over 1.2 dB while matching or beating their FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LightRR achieves near-SOTA accuracy at a fraction of the cost: it trails RDNet by 0.26 dB average PSNR (26.39 vs 26.65) and DSIT by 0.10 dB (26.39 vs 26.49), while using only 3.01% of RDNet's parameters (9.50 M vs 315.71 M) and 5.22% of its FLOPs (9.62 vs 184.21 GFLOPs). Against the unfolding-based DExNet, LightRR wins by 0.43 dB PSNR (26.39 vs 25.96) with comparable FLOPs. LightRR clearly outperforms DSRNet (+0.64 dB), Zhu et al. (+1.05 dB), and DURRNet (+2.39 dB) in average PSNR. In the fair same-parameter comparison (Table 3), LightRR beats RDNet-light (25.14 dB) and DSIT-light (25.16 dB) by 1.23–1.25 dB while using fewer FLOPs (9.62 vs 10.39 and 48.04 GFLOPs), demonstrating that the wavelet-Mamba architecture is intrinsically more parameter-efficient than simply shrinking existing SOTA backbones. LightRR also achieves the best Objects PSNR (27.31 dB) among all methods in the w/ Nature setting, surpassing DSIT (27.27) and RDNet (26.78).

## Relation to Prior Work

LightRR extends the Mamba/state-space-model line of work (Mamba, MambaIR) into the single-image reflection removal domain for the first time, adapting the 2D-SSM into an asymmetric frequency-selective variant. It departs from the dominant SIRR paradigm of coupling a lightweight task-specific network with a heavy pre-trained backbone (VGG19, Swin, FocalNet) by replacing that backbone at inference with a knowledge-distilled lightweight encoder. The wavelet-based frequency decomposition draws on prior observations that reflection artifacts concentrate in low-frequency sub-bands, but is the first to exploit this asymmetry architecturally via differential Mamba vs. depthwise-convolution processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic efficiency gain (3.01% parameters, 5.22% FLOPs of RDNet) with only a 0.26 dB PSNR drop makes real-time reflection removal viable on mobile and edge devices, a practical gap that prior SOTA methods could not fill. The frequency-domain insight—that reflections live in LL while critical details reside in HF—provides a principled basis for future lightweight restoration architectures beyond SIRR. Open questions include whether the Mamba-wavelet framework generalizes to other low-level tasks (deraining, dehazing, as the authors note), whether the VGG19 teacher can be replaced by more modern backbones without distillation overhead, and whether the small absolute PSNR gap to RDNet/DSIT narrows on harder, more diverse real-world scenes.
