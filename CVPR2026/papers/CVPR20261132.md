## Problem

Current video post-training methods suffer from two critical limitations: existing datasets often lack temporal-centricity (answers inferable from isolated keyframes rather than requiring holistic temporal integration), and training data generated by proprietary models like GPT-4V or Gemini-2.5-Pro contains systematic errors in fundamental temporal perception such as confusing motion directions or misjudging speeds. This raises the question of how to obtain high-quality, temporal-centric training data with guaranteed correctness without relying on flawed proprietary model annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261132/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips (synthetic videos rendered at 30 FPS via Matplotlib and encoded as H.264 MP4, or real-world videos at standard resolutions) paired with text questions, and produces structured chain-of-thought reasoning chains that explicitly reference frame-level timestamps (e.g., 'at 00:00', 'at 00:02') before arriving at a final text answer. During the RL stage, the output is scored by a verifiable accuracy reward computed against ground-truth answers derived directly from simulation logs, enabling perfectly correct supervision signals.

## Method

SynRL decomposes temporal understanding into short-term perceptual primitives (direction, speed, acceleration, collision counting across 12 video types) and long-term cognitive primitives (state tracking, retrodictive inference across 6 scenario types), all generated programmatically via Python code that simulates physics and records frame-level metadata. A four-stage CoT pipeline (Generation → Verification → Reflection → Polishing) uses the procedural metadata to produce temporally grounded reasoning chains, with an LLM evaluator filtering inconsistent outputs over up to 5 iterations. Training follows a two-stage strategy: SFT on 7.7K mixed CoT samples (synthetic + 15% LLaVA-Video without CoT to prevent distribution shift), followed by GRPO reinforcement learning on 7K synthetic video-QA pairs using only verifiable accuracy rewards. The key insight is that abstract temporal primitives learned from simple geometric shapes and state-transition sequences transfer effectively to complex real-world videos, with 7.7K synthetic samples outperforming Video-R1's 165K model-generated samples by 21× in data efficiency.

## Results

On temporal grounding, Qwen3-VL-4B+SynRL achieves R@0.3 of 38.8 on RexTime (+12.6 over base) and 38.8 on NExTGQA (+5.9), with mIoU gains of +8.0 and +4.6 respectively. On complex reasoning, SynRL improves TOMATO by +4.6 (4B) / +4.9 (8B) and Video-TT by +1.8 / +0.9. Across 15 benchmarks, the 7.7K synthetic CoT samples consistently outperform Video-R1's 165K model-generated CoT samples, representing a 21× data efficiency improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Video-R1-7B (the closest RL-based competitor), SynRL (Qwen3-VL-4B) wins on every reported benchmark: TOMATO 36.7 vs 25.1, MVBench 67.1 vs 63.9, VideoMME 62.0 vs 59.3, and TemporalBench 68.2/27.9 vs 63.7/21.2. The most striking comparison is in Table 6, where SynRL's 7.7K synthetic CoT samples outperform Video-R1's 165K model-generated CoT samples on all seven benchmarks (e.g., TOMATO 34.2 vs 28.2, TemporalBench Multi-Binary 27.5 vs 22.9), demonstrating that Video-R1's large-scale data actually degrades performance relative to the base model. Against Video-Jigsaw-7B, SynRL leads by a wide margin on TOMATO (36.7 vs 25.3) and MVBench (67.1 vs 53.1). Compared to the strongest pretrained baseline VideoLLaMA3-7B, SynRL wins on TOMATO (36.7 vs 30.1) and TemporalBench (68.2/27.9 vs 69.1/29.4, roughly tied) but trails on MVBench (67.1 vs 69.7) and VideoMME (62.0 vs 66.2), indicating that the 8B variant is needed to match or exceed the best general video understanding scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based video post-training paradigm established by Video-R1 and Video-Jigsaw, which rely on proprietary models (GPT-4V, Gemini) to generate chain-of-thought annotations for training. The key departure is replacing model-generated data with programmatically synthesized videos whose ground-truth annotations are guaranteed by construction, eliminating the systematic temporal perception errors the authors document in proprietary models. The GRPO algorithm from DeepSeekMath is adopted as the RL backbone, but the contribution lies in the data generation pipeline and the decomposition of temporal understanding into short-term perceptual and long-term cognitive primitives, rather than in the optimization method itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 7.7K guaranteed-correct synthetic samples outperform 165K model-generated samples—and that the latter actually degrades performance below the base model—challenges the prevailing assumption that scaling model-generated annotations is the path to better video reasoning. This opens a cost-efficient scaling path for video post-training that does not depend on proprietary model access. However, the transfer evidence is limited to the Qwen model family and relatively simple synthetic scenarios (geometric shapes, card games), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to more complex temporal reasoning (e.g., multi-agent interactions, long-horizon causal chains) and to architectures beyond Qwen. The practical recipe (Matplotlib-based video generation + SFT + GRPO) is immediately reproducible, which increases its adoption potential.
