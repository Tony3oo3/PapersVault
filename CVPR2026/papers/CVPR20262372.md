## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary detectors are trained and evaluated exclusively on RGB imagery, and their performance degrades sharply when transferred to thermal images due to low texture, emissivity variations, and the fundamental modality gap. Current thermal detectors remain closed-set, restricted to a handful of pre-defined categories (pedestrians, vehicles), and cannot generalize to unseen object classes or interpret free-form text queries. This paper addresses the absence of any annotation-free, language-driven open-vocabulary detector for the thermal domain.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single thermal image and a free-form text prompt (e.g., "a person", "the center front car") and outputs bounding boxes with class labels and confidence scores; the LLM used during training is discarded. Text prompts support both category-level queries and spatially referential expressions, a capability that RGB-trained open-vocabulary detectors handle unreliably and adaptation-based thermal pipelines cannot interpret at all. During training, the system additionally consumes paired RGB-thermal frames (e.g., from M³FD) for distillation and synthetic thermal images with grounding texts and scene captions from the converted GroundingCap-1M corpus.

## Method

Thermal-Det fully fine-tunes a Grounding DINO-style detector on a synthetic thermal dataset (GroundingCap-1M converted via F-ViTA with RGB-specific caption terms filtered out), then applies three complementary training signals: (1) a Thermal-Text Alignment Head (TTAH) that augments frozen CLIP text embeddings with learnable radiometric attribute vectors (e.g., hot, silhouette, reflective) via a two-layer MLP, expanding each class into M thermal sublabels and selecting the best-matching variant by confidence-gated similarity; (2) cross-modal knowledge distillation from a frozen RGB teacher using paired RGB-thermal data, with GIoU box alignment, InfoNCE semantic feature alignment, and KL-divergence confidence calibration; and (3) LLM-supervised caption generation (scene-level and object-level) where a thermal-adapted LLM with LoRA-style adapters and a Modality-Fused Cross-Attention module grounds language in thermal features, with the LLM discarded at inference. All objectives are optimized jointly in a single end-to-end pipeline, and the detector is fully fine-tuned rather than adapted via lightweight adapters, allowing both convolutional and attention layers to internalize thermal contrast patterns while preserving CLIP text-space alignment.

## Results

On FLIR-Aligned, Thermal-Det achieves 0.372 AP / 0.664 AP50 / 0.359 AP75, outperforming the strongest RGB-based baseline LLMDet (0.359 AP) and G-DINO (0.337 AP). On the more challenging FLIR-V2 benchmark, it reaches 0.096 AP / 0.173 AP50, surpassing G-DINO (0.081 AP) and LLMDet (0.048 AP). On CAMEL, the method attains the top AP of 0.511 (vs. G-DINO 0.482 and LLMDet 0.383), and on LLVIP it delivers 0.566 AP / 0.856 AP50. All results are zero-shot: the model is trained only on synthetic thermal data and unlabeled paired RGB-thermal frames, with no thermal annotations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Thermal-Det outperforms all four RGB-based open-vocabulary baselines across every evaluated thermal benchmark. Against GLIP, the gains are largest: +12.1 AP on FLIR-Aligned (0.372 vs. 0.251) and +32.5 AP on CAMEL (0.511 vs. 0.186), reflecting GLIP's limited grounding capability under thermal domain shift. YOLO-World shows a similar pattern, trailing by +10.6 AP on FLIR-Aligned and +31.4 AP on CAMEL. The closest competitor is G-DINO, which Thermal-Det surpasses by +3.5 AP on FLIR-Aligned, +1.5 AP on FLIR-V2, and +2.9 AP on CAMEL; notably, G-DINO's AP75 on CAMEL (0.543) actually exceeds Thermal-Det's (0.525), suggesting slightly better localization precision at high IoU thresholds for that specific setting. LLMDet, the most recent LLM-supervised detector, is beaten by +1.3 AP on FLIR-Aligned, +4.8 AP on FLIR-V2, and +12.8 AP on CAMEL, with the largest gap on CAMEL indicating that LLM caption supervision alone is insufficient without explicit thermal-text alignment and cross-modal distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the open-vocabulary detection paradigm (GLIP, Grounding DINO, LLMDet) from RGB to thermal imagery, a modality where prior detectors have been strictly closed-set and supervised on small annotated datasets (CAMEL, FLIR, LLVIP, KAIST). It departs from prior thermal adaptation work (parameter-efficient fine-tuning, adapter-based transfer) by fully fine-tuning the detector and introducing three new components: a synthetic thermal dataset via F-ViTA conversion of GroundingCap-1M, a Thermal-Text Alignment Head that recalibrates CLIP text embeddings with radiometric sublabels, and a three-level (box/semantic/confidence) RGB-to-thermal distillation loss. The LLM captioning supervision with LoRA-style thermal adapters and Modality-Fused Cross-Attention draws on recent LLM-for-detection work (LLMDet) but repurposes it for cross-modal thermal grounding rather than RGB refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in thermal perception: the near-absence of large-scale, open-vocabulary thermal annotations. The synthetic data pipeline (F-ViTA + GroundingCap-1M with caption filtering) is a reusable recipe that could benefit other low-resource modalities. However, performance still degrades sharply on small, thermally ambiguous objects (motorcycle, traffic light at 0.006 AP on FLIR-V2), and the distillation component requires spatially aligned RGB-thermal pairs, limiting applicability to thermal-only deployments. Open questions include whether the synthetic-to-real gap can be further closed without paired data, whether TTAH's radiometric sublabel mechanism generalizes beyond the tested attribute bank, and whether the framework extends to video or multi-sensor fusion settings.
