## Problem

Existing text-driven 3D scene generation methods suffer from three key limitations: LLM-only approaches produce physically implausible layouts with collisions and unreasonable placements; VLM-based methods that incorporate spatial constraints sacrifice generative diversity; and virtually all prior work focuses on generating scenes from scratch while neglecting the practical need for interactive scene editing (moving, adding, or removing objects). Additionally, gradient-based optimization used by recent VLM methods is computationally expensive, precluding real-time interaction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form natural language text prompt (or an editing instruction such as 'move a stool to the upper left corner') as input. Internally, the VLM also consumes a top-down 2D view of the current scene annotated with grid lines, coordinates, and visual markers. The output is a hierarchical 3D scene layout where each object has XY-plane coordinates, a yaw rotation, and a parent-child support relationship; the Z-coordinate is not predicted directly but is automatically derived from the parent object's surface and the object's dimensions. Objects are instantiated by retrieving 3D assets from 3D-FUTURE and Objaverse using a combined SBERT + CLIP + size scoring function.

## Method

HOG-Layout is a four-stage pipeline: (1) an LLM with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG over a FAISS-indexed template rule library) produces a structured scene plan with object lists, room dimensions, and functional groupings; (2) a VLM integrates the plan with a top-down view to predict a hierarchical layout where objects are organized by support relationships (floor, wall, ceiling, or other objects as parents), requiring the VLM to predict only XY coordinates and yaw; (3) a hierarchical force-directed optimizer decomposes all physical and semantic constraints into planar (XY), vertical (Z), and rotational forces, updating objects simultaneously across hierarchy levels with explicit deadlock detection and evasion; (4) an editing module routes user text to plan/add/delete/move commands via the LLM, then reuses the VLM and optimizer to produce the modified scene. The key architectural insight is that the support-based hierarchy both simplifies the VLM's prediction space (only 2D + rotation per object) and enables the optimizer to decompose forces into independent directional accumulators, achieving convergence in ~70 seconds versus ~320 seconds for gradient-based baselines.

## Results

On the SceneEval-100 benchmark, HOG-Layout achieves the best overall performance across fidelity and plausibility metrics: OOR 43.09%, OAR 75.74%, COLob 5.28%, COLsc 16.00%, SUP 81.17%, ACC 94.74%, OOB 2.45%, SP 69.69, and CLIPsim 18.61. It also generates scenes in 70.44 s on average, roughly 4.5× faster than LayoutVLM (321.47 s) and 3.9× faster than Holodeck (271.97 s). In human evaluation (7-point scale), HOG-Layout scores 5.33 on plausibility and 5.75 on semantic alignment, substantially outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LayoutGPT, HOG-Layout dominates on virtually every metric: OOR improves from 6.11% to 43.09%, OAR from 11.48% to 75.74%, COLob drops from 35.67% to 5.28%, and SP rises from 35.14 to 69.69; the only trade-off is generation time (70.44 s vs. 37.41 s). Against Holodeck, HOG-Layout wins on fidelity (OOR 43.09 vs. 18.99, OAR 75.74 vs. 38.27), most plausibility metrics (COLob 5.28 vs. 12.24, COLsc 16.00 vs. 63.00, SUP 81.17 vs. 34.72, ACC 94.74 vs. 91.21), SP (69.69 vs. 55.45), and is 3.9× faster (70.44 s vs. 271.97 s); Holodeck retains an edge only on OOB (0.34 vs. 2.45) and NAV (99.29 vs. 97.15). Against the closest competitor LayoutVLM, HOG-Layout achieves substantially lower collisions (COLob 5.28 vs. 29.44, COLsc 16.00 vs. 55.00), higher OAR (75.74 vs. 61.99), higher ACC (94.74 vs. 87.67), lower OOB (2.45 vs. 8.42), and a 4.6× speed advantage (70.44 s vs. 321.47 s), while LayoutVLM edges ahead only on ATR (61.79 vs. 60.01). The ablation further confirms that the hierarchical force-directed optimizer is the key differentiator: removing it (w/o HierOpt) degrades COLob to 36.46% and COLsc to 65.00%, while replacing it with LayoutVLM's gradient-based optimizer (w/ GradOpt) increases time to 147.93 s with worse collision scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the convergence of LLM-based scene planning (LayoutGPT, FLAIR-GPT), VLM-based layout generation (LayoutVLM, Holodeck), and classical force-directed layout optimization. It departs from prior VLM-based methods by introducing a hierarchical parent-child representation grounded in physical support relationships, which decomposes the optimization into same-level and cross-level interactions. Unlike LayoutVLM's gradient-based optimizer or Holodeck's DFS/MILP solvers, HOG-Layout uses a custom iterative force-directed scheme with deadlock detection, achieving comparable or better physical plausibility at roughly 4–5× lower latency. The RAG-augmented planning module and the unified generation-plus-editing pipeline are novel combinations, though each individual component (FAISS retrieval, SBERT/CLIP scoring, visual markers) is drawn from existing toolkits.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in unifying scene generation and low-latency editing (most operations under 20 s) within a single framework, which is directly relevant to interactive VR and embodied-AI applications where users expect to modify scenes rather than regenerate them. Limitations include heavy dependence on proprietary LLM/VLM APIs (GPT-4o, GPT-5), evaluation restricted to SceneEval-100 (100 scenes, 10 room types), and retrieval-based object acquisition that constrains the asset vocabulary. Open questions include whether the hierarchical force decomposition scales to outdoor or multi-room scenes with hundreds of objects, whether the approach generalizes to non-rectangular or irregular room geometries, and whether the RAG template library can be replaced by learned priors to reduce manual curation.
