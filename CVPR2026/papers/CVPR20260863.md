## Problem

Existing data pruning methods evaluate sample importance in the full parameter space or require multi-epoch training dynamics, both of which are misaligned with LoRA-based fine-tuning where only a low-rank adapter subspace is updated and convergence occurs within a few epochs. This leads to overestimation of sample importance (counting gradient energy in frozen backbone directions) and pruning overhead that can exceed the training savings it aims to provide.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a training set of RGB images with class labels (or detection annotations) and a pretrained vision model (ViT, DeiT, Swin, or DETR) instrumented with LoRA adapters (rank r=8, α=16). During a single forward/backward pass, it collects per-layer input activations x_t and backpropagated error signals δ_t to compute TSA scores, then outputs a binary selection vector m that retains the top-scoring fraction of samples for subsequent fine-tuning. No additional warm-up epochs or full-parameter-space gradient computations are required.

## Method

LoPrune introduces a Trainable Subspace Alignment (TSA) Score that projects the influence function onto the LoRA trainable subspace via a Jacobian mapping J, so that only gradient components along updatable directions contribute to the importance estimate. The exact TSA score s(z) = g_φ(z)ᵀ H_φ⁻¹ g_φ(z) is then approximated efficiently using K-FAC, which factorizes the LoRA-subspace Hessian as a Kronecker product of input covariance Σ_x and output covariance Σ_δ per layer. For each LoRA factor pair (A, B), the score decomposes into a Mahalanobis-distance term on the input side (projected through A) and a Mahalanobis-distance term on the output side (projected through B), both computable from standard forward/backward activations. Aggregating these per-layer scores across all T trainable blocks yields the final per-sample importance, enabling single-epoch pruning with approximately linear-time overhead.

## Results

At a 0.5 pruning rate, LoPrune achieves 89.59% Top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-100 (ViT-Small) and 84.78% on Tiny-ImageNet, within 0.27% and 0.13% of the no-pruning baselines (90.05% and 84.91%), while reducing total training time by 33.8% and 37.4% respectively. On COCO-2017 object detection, LoPrune attains 66.70 AP₅₀ with Swin-T and 59.92 AP₅₀ with DETR, saving 33.0% and 33.5% of total training time over no-pruning. Compared to the best baseline (MoSo), LoPrune further reduces training overhead by up to 72.9% (3.69× speedup) while improving accuracy by up to 3.50% at high pruning ratios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LoPrune outperforms all five baselines on both accuracy and total training time across every model–dataset combination. Against MoSo (the strongest baseline), LoPrune gains 0.34% Top-1 on CIFAR-100 (ViT-Small) while cutting total time from 893.3 s to 371.5 s (58.4% reduction), and gains 0.30 AP₅₀ on COCO with DETR while reducing time from 11160 s to 4804 s (57.0% reduction). EL2N and GraNd, which rely on multi-epoch warm-up to track learning difficulty, suffer the largest accuracy drops (up to 4.3% on CIFAR-100) and incur the highest overheads (e.g., GraNd takes 1339.8 s vs. LoPrune's 371.5 s on CIFAR-100), because their scoring is not aligned with the LoRA trainable subspace. The optimization-based method (Opt) performs worst overall, dropping 3.2% in accuracy on CIFAR-100 and 3.9% on COCO (DETR) while requiring the longest training times (up to 14760 s), as it computes influence in the full parameter space. Forgetting-based pruning is competitive in accuracy at low pruning ratios but its multi-epoch tracking adds 2–3× the training time of LoPrune. TFDP, a training-free heuristic, is the second-fastest method but still lags LoPrune by 0.6–1.1% in accuracy and by 30–50% in total time.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoPrune sits at the intersection of three established research threads: parameter-efficient fine-tuning via LoRA (Hu et al., 2021), influence-function-based sample importance estimation (Koh & Liang, 2017), and Kronecker-factored curvature approximation (Martens & Grosse, 2015). It departs from prior data pruning methods (EL2N, GraNd, Forgetting, optimization-based coreset selection, MoSo) by recognizing that their importance scores, computed in the full parameter space or via multi-epoch learning dynamics, are fundamentally misaligned with the low-rank adapter subspace that LoRA actually updates. The key conceptual move is projecting the influence function onto the LoRA trainable subspace via a Jacobian mapping, yielding a Trainable Subspace Alignment (TSA) score that is then computed efficiently through K-FAC, enabling single-epoch pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For resource-constrained IoT and edge devices that must continually fine-tune vision models on-device, the demonstrated 72.9% reduction in total training overhead (pruning + training) with a 3.69× speedup and simultaneous accuracy gain is a practically significant efficiency win. The finding that four of seven standard data pruning baselines perform worse than random selection at high pruning ratios under LoRA fine-tuning highlights a previously unaddressed mismatch between full-space importance estimation and low-rank adaptation, motivating subspace-aware pruning as a distinct sub-problem. Open questions include whether the TSA score generalizes to other PEFT methods (e.g., adapters, prompt tuning), whether it scales to much larger models (e.g., ViT-Large or LLMs), and how it interacts with other efficiency techniques like quantization or dynamic rank allocation.
