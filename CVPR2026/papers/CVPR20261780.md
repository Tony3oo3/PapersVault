## Problem

Vision-language models suffer substantial performance degradation when transferring from ground-view to aerial-view detection due to systematic geometric differences in object scale, density, and spatial layout. Fixed VLM fusion mechanisms apply uniform processing regardless of scene complexity, and existing cross-view methods fail to exploit the semantic consistency inherent in synchronized ground-aerial image pairs, leaving a persistent ground-aerial performance gap and high training variance unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261780/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (ground or aerial) paired with a fixed text detection prompt (the Florence-2 `<OD>` token) and produces COCO-style bounding boxes with normalized coordinates scaled to [0, 1000] along with object category labels. Notably, Florence-2 does not output per-detection confidence scores, so all predictions are assigned equal weight during COCO evaluation, and NMS with a 0.5 IoU threshold is applied post-hoc to remove duplicates.

## Method

CrossVL augments Florence-2-base with two training-only components. Complexity-Aware Pathway Aggregation (CPA) estimates a 3-dimensional scene complexity vector from multimodal feature statistics (mean, variance, max of visual tokens and mean, variance of text tokens) via a small MLP, then routes visual features through three specialized pathways—sparse (attention-based token selection), medium (hierarchical region pooling with cross-attention), and dense (full self-attention with global pooling)—and fuses them via a complexity-conditioned softmax gate, adding only 2.5% parameters with zero inference cost. Paired Curriculum Learning (PCL) schedules the sampling probability of synchronized ground-aerial pairs from 1.0 to 0.0 linearly over training, providing stable scene-level semantic supervision early and promoting viewpoint generalization late. The key finding is a mutual regularization effect: CPA's stable complexity-aware representations prevent the catastrophic seed failures observed with curriculum learning alone (reducing variance by 3.3×), while the paired schedule enhances CPA's representational richness, yielding super-additive gains in aerial mAP and a narrowed ground-aerial performance gap.

## Results

On MAVREC aerial test set, CrossVL (CPA + PCL) achieves 61.03% mAP, improving over the Florence-2-base random-mixing baseline (58.66%) by +2.37pp, and over the strongest vision-only baseline YOLOv7 (31.9%) by nearly 30pp. On the aerial validation set, the combined method reaches 65.35% mAP (+1.62pp over baseline). The method also narrows the ground-aerial performance gap from 8.63pp to 6.65pp on the test set and reduces cross-seed variance by 3.3× (±1.50 vs. ±4.97 std) compared to curriculum learning alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Florence-2-base random-mixing baseline, CrossVL improves aerial test mAP from 58.66% to 61.03% (+2.37pp) and validation mAP from 63.73% to 65.35% (+1.62pp), with the combined gain exceeding the sum of individual component contributions (super-additive synergy). Against the strongest vision-only baseline (YOLOv7, 31.9% test mAP from the MAVREC protocol), CrossVL outperforms by approximately 29pp, underscoring the advantage of multimodal semantic priors for cross-view geometric variation. Notably, curriculum learning alone actually degrades test mAP to 56.53% (−2.13pp) with high variance (±4.97 std), while CPA alone provides a stable +2.00pp gain; only their combination yields both the highest accuracy and the lowest variance. The combined method also achieves the smallest ground-aerial gap (6.65pp test, 3.73pp val) compared to all other configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Florence-2, a unified VLM for detection, by adding a training-only complexity-aware pathway aggregation module (CPA) that routes visual features through sparse/medium/dense pathways based on estimated scene complexity, and a paired curriculum learning schedule (PCL) that exploits semantic consistency in synchronized ground-aerial pairs. Conceptually, CPA draws on adaptive fusion and mixture-of-experts routing but repurposes it for the specific geometric disparity between ground and aerial views rather than content-based computation allocation. The paper departs from prior cross-view work (matching, retrieval, viewpoint-invariant recognition) by targeting instance-level detection under non-overlapping fields of view, and from standard VLM detection by explicitly modeling the geometry-induced complexity imbalance rather than treating it as a domain shift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-view VLM detection is practically important for autonomous navigation, urban monitoring, and disaster response where aerial imagery is essential but training data is predominantly ground-level. The mutual regularization between CPA and PCL—where each component stabilizes the other's failure modes—is a useful design principle, though it is demonstrated on a single dataset (MAVREC) and a single backbone (Florence-2-base), limiting generalizability claims. Open questions include whether the complexity estimation (based on simple feature statistics) transfers to scenarios where variance is driven by non-geometric factors (extreme lighting, sensor noise), and whether the approach extends to other cross-view pairs such as satellite-street or indoor-outdoor viewpoints.
