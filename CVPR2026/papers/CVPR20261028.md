## Problem

Dominant gait recognition methods (e.g., GaitSet) use order-invariant pooling that captures global motion context but fundamentally cannot model the sequential, part-level dynamics of walking. Kinematic alternatives relying on optical flow are noisy, computationally expensive, and limited to short-term frame-to-frame modeling. Additionally, more powerful motion representations risk overfitting to nuisance factors (clothing, viewpoint), degrading out-of-domain generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video sequences of 30 consecutive frames (training) or up to 120 frames (testing), each resized to 448×224 via padding, processed by a frozen DINOv3 backbone. The output is a gait identity embedding R ∈ R^{n×d} formed by concatenating a semantic representation (n₁×d, order-invariant) and a kinematic representation (n₂×d, part-level temporal). During training, natural language nuisance descriptions from GCcaption are additionally encoded via OpenCLIP to supervise the decorrelation objective.

## Method

GaitMax is a dual-branch framework: a semantic branch applies temporal mean pooling and horizontal pyramid pooling over DINOv3 features (order-invariant, global context), while a kinematic branch uses DETR-inspired learnable queries with temperature-enhanced cross-attention to track n₂ body parts across the full sequence, followed by per-part temporal modeling and inter-part union perception. The key positional encoding, GauPE, parameterizes each part's attention map as a Gaussian covariance ellipse via moment matching, injecting centroid coordinates via RoPE-style rotation and shape parameters (variances, covariance) via concatenation, enabling long-range, geometry-aware trajectory modeling without optical flow. To prevent the enhanced model from shortcutting on nuisance factors, the Conditional Decorrelation Loss (CDLoss) minimizes second-order statistical correlation between gait embeddings and OpenCLIP text embeddings of nuisance attributes (attire, viewpoint, carrying), explicitly disentangling identity from confounders. This loss is enabled by GCcaption, a new annotation resource providing natural language descriptions of seven subject- and environment-related attributes across multiple gait datasets, generated via Gemini-2-Flash-Lite with embedding-space aggregation for sequence-level consistency.

## Results

On CCPG (in-domain), GaitMax achieves a mean Rank-1 accuracy of 89.6%, surpassing the strongest multi-modality baseline MultiGait++ (87.6%) and the RGB-only BigGait (87.2%). On CCGR MINI, GaitMax reaches 83.6% Rank-1, 74.2% mAP, and 62.2% mINP, improving over BigGait by +2.9% R1, +7.0% mAP, and +2.4% mINP. In the out-of-domain setting (trained on CCPG, tested on CASIA-B and SUSTech1K), GaitMax achieves 72.9% mean Rank-1 on CASIA-B and 59.7% on SUSTech1K, outperforming BigGait by +12.1% and +5.0% respectively, with the largest gains under clothing-change conditions (+12.6% on CASIA-B CL, +11.3% on SUSTech1K CL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaitMax outperforms all 12 baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest silhouette-only method DeepGaitv2 [9], GaitMax gains +6.3% mean Rank-1 on CCPG and +44.2% R1 on CCGR MINI, demonstrating the advantage of RGB input over binary silhouettes. The closest in-domain competitor is DenoisingGait [23] (89.5% CCPG mean), which GaitMax edges out by +0.1% while using only RGB input (no silhouette or parsing). Among RGB-only methods, GaitMax surpasses BigGait [74] by +2.4% CCPG mean and +7.0% mAP on CCGR, with the gap widening to +12.1% mean on CASIA-B and +5.0% on SUSTech1K in the out-of-domain setting. Multi-modality methods like MultiGait++ [22] (requiring silhouette + parsing + optical flow) are outperformed by +2.0% CCPG mean, and skeleton-based methods (BiFusion [41], SkeletonGait++ [11]) trail by 24–25% on CCPG. The largest absolute gains appear on CCGR MINI, where GaitMax's mINP of 62.2 exceeds the previous best (BigGait, 59.8) by +2.4 and GaitEdge [30] by +28.4, reflecting superior robustness under 53 covariates and 33 viewpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate paradigms in gait recognition: the dominant semantic (order-invariant) line from GaitSet through BigGait, and the kinematic (order-dependent) line from GFI through DenoisingGait. It departs from prior kinematic methods by replacing dense optical flow with a DETR-inspired part-localization mechanism and a novel Gaussian Positional Embedding (GauPE) that encodes both centroid position and shape/orientation of body regions. The Conditional Decorrelation Loss extends the standard identity-discrimination objective (CE + triplet) by introducing a vision-language-guided disentanglement term, and the GCcaption resource moves beyond the coarse categorical protocol flags (e.g., 'U0D0') that have constrained prior gait datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of semantic and kinematic representations in a single frozen-LVM framework, combined with explicit nuisance disentanglement via CDLoss, offers a principled path toward robust gait recognition that generalizes across clothing, carrying, and viewpoint changes without requiring multi-modal inputs. The ablation revealing that simple parallel fusion can actually degrade performance on the CL protocol (−2.2% vs. kinematic-only) highlights an open question about adaptive fusion that selectively trusts the most robust branch. GCcaption, while limited to static attributes due to computational constraints, establishes a foundation for context-aware gait analysis that prior coarse labels could not support. The Diving48 experiment suggests the semantic-kinematic representation may generalize beyond gait to broader periodic human action analysis.
