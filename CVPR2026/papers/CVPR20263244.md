## Problem

Existing Incomplete Multi-View Classification (IMVC) methods rely exclusively on internal semantic information from partially observed views to impute missing data, which imposes an inherent performance ceiling. While external textual knowledge (e.g., WordNet) has been explored for unsupervised tasks, no prior work leverages external vision knowledge as supervision signals to guide missing-view completion in supervised multi-view classification, leaving a gap in how to reconcile potentially conflicting external and internal semantics.

## Input / Output

The method takes incomplete multi-view feature matrices (N instances × V views, with a binary missing-view indicator matrix W marking unavailable views as zeros) and an external vision knowledge library constructed from a diverse unlabeled image collection (e.g., 50,000 ImageNet images across 1,000 categories). Each image is processed through BLIP to generate textual descriptions, then BERT to produce text embeddings, which are further encoded into knowledge representations. The output is a predicted class label per instance, produced by a cross-entropy classifier over the fused representation that combines available view encodings with purified external knowledge for missing views.

## Method

EXOTIC employs a dual-channel decoupled architecture where Channel 1 (encoders E_c) is dedicated to filtering and purifying external knowledge, while Channel 2 (encoders E_p) handles representation learning and classification, preventing task interference. Knowledge Filtering computes cosine similarity between each sample's fused internal representation and all external knowledge embeddings, selecting the top-K most relevant and averaging them into a sample-specific knowledge vector. Knowledge Purification then applies two alignment losses: a category alignment loss (L_vc) that enforces task-label relevance of the filtered knowledge, and a knowledge alignment loss (L_kc) that maximizes consistency between the external knowledge and view-specific internal representations to mitigate semantic conflict. In External Completion, missing views in Channel 2 are directly replaced by the purified knowledge embedding, and the resulting fused representation is classified via cross-entropy, with the total objective L_all = L_c + α·L_kc + β·L_vc balancing classification, knowledge alignment, and category alignment.

## Results

EXOTIC achieves the highest classification accuracy across all seven datasets (Caltech101, Scene15, LandUse21, HW, Fashion, NUSWIDE, CUB) at every missing rate from 0.1 to 0.9. At the most challenging setting (MR=0.9), EXOTIC reaches 88.16% on Caltech101, 94.05% on HW, 92.55% on Fashion, and 81.00% on CUB, outperforming the strongest baselines (LMVCAT, DIMC, AIMNET) by 2–6 percentage points. The performance gap widens as the missing rate increases, with EXOTIC showing the smallest accuracy drop (e.g., only ~6% drop on Caltech101 from MR=0.1 to MR=0.9) compared to baselines that degrade by 10–40%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EXOTIC outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset at every missing rate, with the margin growing as incompleteness increases. At MR=0.1, the closest competitor is LMVCAT (91.41% on Caltech101 vs. EXOTIC's 94.33%, a +2.92% gap), while at MR=0.9 the gap widens to +6.28% (88.16% vs. 81.88%). On HW, EXOTIC leads DIMC (91.80%) and DICNET (92.20%) by 1.85–2.25% at MR=0.9, and on NUSWIDE it surpasses AIMNET (43.63%) by 1.28%. The weaker baselines (CPM-NET, MTD, SIP, RANK) are consistently 10–40% below EXOTIC, with MTD and SIP particularly struggling on Caltech101 and NUSWIDE. Notably, EXOTIC's advantage is most pronounced on LandUse21 and NUSWIDE at high missing rates, where internal-only methods degrade sharply (e.g., UIMC drops from 52.19% to 20.67% on LandUse21 from MR=0.1 to 0.9, while EXOTIC only drops from 80.00% to 55.24%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the incomplete multi-view classification (IMVC) literature, which has been dominated by methods that mine internal semantic structure from partially observed views (e.g., DCP, AIMNet, LMVCAT) or learn from available views without imputation (e.g., MTD, RANK). It departs from this internal-only paradigm by borrowing the external-knowledge-guided idea from unsupervised clustering work (SIC, TAC), but repurposes it for supervised classification and replaces textual knowledge (WordNet) with vision-language-model-generated representations from unlabeled images. The dual-channel decoupling architecture is conceptually related to MTD's two-channel design but is restructured here to separate knowledge filtering from representation learning rather than to handle missing views directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that easily obtainable external vision knowledge (unlabeled images processed through BLIP+BERT) can break the semantic ceiling imposed by internal-only supervision in IMVC, with consistent gains across seven datasets that are most pronounced at high missing rates (≥0.5). A practical limitation is the dependency on pre-trained VLM quality and the added pipeline overhead of constructing and filtering a knowledge library; the approach also assumes the external image pool is semantically overlapping with the target domain. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to non-visual views (e.g., audio, sensor data), how sensitive performance is to the quality and domain alignment of the knowledge library, and whether the filtering/purification modules can be replaced by simpler retrieval strategies.
