## Problem

Existing MLLM-based image aesthetics assessment methods suffer from modality bias—producing either scores or text but not both—and lack fine-grained attribute decomposition, making them unsuitable for professional aesthetic evaluation. Additionally, continuous score prediction in MLLMs is inherently difficult because these models are designed for discrete token generation, and prior discretization approaches (e.g., Q-Align's 5-level ratings) incur significant information loss. Available IAA datasets are also small, coarse in granularity, and annotated by non-experts, limiting the ability of models to learn nuanced aesthetic reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image at fixed resolution (modified from InternVL-3-8B's dynamic resolution strategy) and produces two simultaneous outputs: a continuous aesthetic score in [0, 100] and a structured textual analysis covering 8 fine-grained aesthetic attributes (Composition & Design, Visual Elements & Structure, Technical Execution, Originality & Creativity, Theme & Communication, Emotion & Viewer Response, Overall Gestalt, Comprehensive Evaluation). The score is not a single predicted token but is derived by computing the expectation over the probability distribution of 101 designated score tokens, yielding a sub-integer continuous value.

## Method

ArtiMuse is built on InternVL-3-8B and trained in two stages: (1) text pretraining with LoRA on ~360K images paired with 8-attribute aesthetic analysis captions to establish structural aesthetic understanding, and (2) score fine-tuning with LoRA to learn continuous score prediction without degrading text capability. The key technical contribution is the Token As Score strategy, which designates 101 existing tokens from the native LLM vocabulary (specifically twin-letter combinations carrying ordinal semantics) as [Aes_Score_Token_0] through [Aes_Score_Token_100], each mapping to an integer score. During inference, the final score is computed as S = Σ i × p_i over all 101 score token probabilities, avoiding both vocabulary expansion and the quantization loss of coarse discrete levels. This approach outperforms both Text As Score (which suffers hallucination) and Level As Score (Q-Align's 5-level scheme, which lacks granularity) while preserving the model's linguistic analysis ability through LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning.

## Results

On the five IAA benchmarks, ArtiMuse achieves SRCC/PLCC of 0.827/0.826 (AVA), 0.936/0.958 (PARA), 0.510/0.543 (TAD66K), 0.814/0.837 (FLICKR-AES), and 0.614/0.627 (ArtiMuse-10K). It outperforms the strongest MLLM baseline Q-Align by over 0.05 PLCC on PARA (0.958 vs 0.888) and ArtiMuse-10K (0.627 vs 0.573). In the generalization setting (trained only on AVA, tested zero-shot on other datasets), ArtiMuse* surpasses Q-Align* on all four out-of-distribution benchmarks, e.g., 0.725 vs 0.711 PLCC on PARA and 0.376 vs 0.320 on ArtiMuse-10K. For structural aesthetic analysis, ArtiMuse is selected as best by a judging MLLM in 71.1% of cases on average across 8 attributes, versus 14.3% (Qwen-2.5-VL) and 14.5% (InternVL3-8B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest MLLM-based IAA baseline Q-Align [27], ArtiMuse wins on PARA (PLCC 0.958 vs 0.888, +0.07), FLICKR-AES (0.837 vs 0.818, +0.02), and its own held-out ArtiMuse-10K (0.627 vs 0.573, +0.05), while matching closely on AVA (0.826 vs 0.817) and TAD66K (0.543 vs 0.531). On AVA, the only model slightly ahead is Next Token Is Enough [16] (SRCC 0.828 vs 0.827), a negligible gap. In the zero-shot generalization protocol (both models trained only on AVA), ArtiMuse* beats Q-Align* on every out-of-distribution dataset, with the largest margin on ArtiMuse-10K (PLCC 0.376 vs 0.320, +0.056). Traditional regression models (MUSIQ, TANet, AesMamba) and general-purpose MLLMs (Qwen-2.5-VL, InternVL3-8B, mPLUG-Owl2, ShareGPT4V) are substantially outperformed across all benchmarks; for instance, Qwen-2.5-VL-7B scores only 0.179 PLCC on ArtiMuse-10K versus ArtiMuse's 0.627. For the structural analysis task (Table 2), ArtiMuse is preferred by a Gemini-2.0-flash judge in 71.1% of attribute-level comparisons on average, dwarfing Qwen-2.5-VL (14.3%), InternVL3-8B (14.5%), and AesExpert (0.1%), and is also the top human-rated model at 67.8%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based image aesthetics assessment line initiated by Q-Align (discrete level scoring), AesExpert (expert-style text without scores), and UNIAA (unified text+score), by unifying both capabilities in a single model with 8 fine-grained attribute-level analysis. Its scoring mechanism, Token As Score, departs from Q-Align's 5-level discretization by mapping 101 existing vocabulary tokens to integer scores 0–100, avoiding both the hallucination of free-text score generation and the granularity loss of coarse level words. The dataset contribution builds on and supersedes prior IAA benchmarks (AVA, APDDv2, Impressions, TAD66K) by adding professional expert annotations, multi-attribute decomposition, and coverage of AIGC and design content largely absent from earlier photographic-only datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ArtiMuse-10K fills a notable gap by providing the first expert-curated, multi-attribute aesthetic dataset spanning AIGC, design, and traditional art, which should benefit the broader IAA community. The Token As Score strategy is a lightweight, drop-in technique for continuous prediction in MLLMs that avoids vocabulary expansion and could generalize beyond aesthetics to other scoring tasks. However, the model currently cannot generate aesthetic improvement recommendations, and the 10K scale, while expert-annotated, remains modest compared to crowd-sourced datasets like AVA (255K), leaving open whether the fine-grained attribute framework scales and generalizes to truly diverse, in-the-wild image distributions.
