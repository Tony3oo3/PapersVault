## Problem

Existing hyperbolic MLR and FC layers either rely on flat-space approximations (tangent or ambient Minkowski) that distort intrinsic geometry, are restricted to a single model (Poincaré or Lorentz), or suffer from over-parameterization and batch inefficiency. While the Busemann function provides a natural intrinsic generalization of the inner product, the only prior Busemann-based MLR (Nguyen et al.) yields a pseudo point-to-hyperplane distance, retains manifold-valued parameters, and is not batch efficient. The paper asks whether one can construct MLR and FC layers that are simultaneously intrinsic, model-agnostic, compactly parameterized, and computationally efficient.

## Input / Output

Both BMLR and BFC accept a point x in either the Poincaré ball P_K^n or the Lorentz model L_K^n (with curvature K<0) and produce, respectively, a C-dimensional probability vector via softmax or an m-dimensional hyperbolic output point. BMLR parameters per class are a unit direction v_k ∈ S^{n-1}, a positive scale α_k, and a real bias b_k — no manifold-valued point is needed, unlike prior hyperbolic MLRs. BFC solves m coupled equations of the form signed-distance(y, H_{e_k,e}) = u_k(x) to recover the output y in closed form, with the activation function φ optionally inserted into the logit.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces the Euclidean inner product ⟨a_k, x⟩ in MLR and FC logits with the Busemann function B^{v_k}(x), yielding logits u_k(x) = −α_k B^{v_k}(x) + b_k. This gives BMLR a genuine point-to-horosphere distance interpretation (Theorem 3.3 in Hadamard spaces), compact per-class parameters, and batch-efficient computation, while recovering Euclidean MLR as K→0⁻. BFC generalizes the FC layer by expressing each output coordinate as a signed point-to-horosphere distance from y to a coordinate hyperplane, then solving the resulting system in closed form on both models (Theorems 4.1–4.2). An activation φ can be composed into the logit to jointly model FC+activation, and an optional gyroaddition bias extends the output. Both layers maintain O(nm) FLOPs and are model-agnostic, unlike prior Möbius, Poincaré FC, or Lorentz FC formulations.

## Results

On image classification with a ResNet-18 backbone, BMLR-P achieves 95.32% top-1 accuracy on CIFAR-10, 78.10% on CIFAR-100, 66.16% on Tiny-ImageNet, and 73.36% on ImageNet-1k, consistently outperforming all prior hyperbolic MLRs. The accuracy margin over the best prior hyperbolic baseline grows with the number of classes, from +0.28 pp on CIFAR-10 to +1.59 pp on ImageNet-1k. BMLR-L is the fastest hyperbolic MLR (e.g., 2276.53 s/epoch on ImageNet-1k vs. 3907.12 s for PBMLR-P), and BFC layers achieve the best link prediction AUC on all four graph datasets, with the largest gain on the most hyperbolic graph (Disease: 80.45 vs. 76.35 for Möbius FC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BMLR-P outperforms the original Poincaré MLR of Ganea et al. [23] (via the reparameterized PMLR of Shimizu et al. [51]) by 0.28–1.59 pp across all four image benchmarks, with the gap widening as class count increases. Against the Pseudo-Busemann MLR of Nguyen et al. [45], BMLR-P wins on every dataset while using roughly half the head parameters (5.14K vs. 10.24K on CIFAR-10) and is ~1.7× faster on ImageNet-1k (2300.77 vs. 3907.12 s/epoch). In the Lorentz model, BMLR-L beats the Lorentz MLR of Bdeir et al. [3] by 0.27–0.78 pp on image classification and is the fastest hyperbolic MLR overall. For FC layers, BFC-P surpasses both the Möbius FC [23] and Poincaré FC [51] on all four link prediction datasets (e.g., 80.45 vs. 76.35 AUC on Disease), and BFC-L outperforms the Lorentz FC of Chen et al. [11] by 5.58 pp on Disease and 2.38 pp on Airport, confirming that Busemann-based constructions better capture intrinsic hyperbolic geometry than tangent-space or ambient-Minkowski approximations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper lifts the Busemann function—a classical tool from metric geometry—into the design of neural network layers, building on closed-form Busemann expressions for the Poincaré and Lorentz models (Bonet et al.) and the point-to-hyperplane reformulation of Euclidean MLR (Lebanon & Lafferty). It departs from prior hyperbolic MLRs (Ganea, Shimizu, Bdeir, Nguyen) that define decision boundaries via geodesic hyperplanes or ambient Minkowski hyperplanes, and from prior hyperbolic FC layers (Möbius, Poincaré FC, Lorentz FC) that operate in tangent or ambient flat spaces. The key conceptual shift is treating horospheres (Busemann level sets) as the intrinsic hyperbolic analogue of hyperplanes, yielding a single formulation valid on both Poincaré and Lorentz models without model-specific reparameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a unified, model-agnostic construction for the two most fundamental classification and transformation layers in hyperbolic networks, this work reduces the fragmentation of the hyperbolic deep learning toolkit and offers practitioners a drop-in replacement with fewer parameters and lower FLOPs. The observation that BMLR's advantage grows with the number of classes and that it remains robust across a wide range of graph hyperbolicity (δ) suggests Busemann geometry captures intrinsic structure that tangent/ambient approximations miss in high-complexity settings. Open questions include whether the Busemann-based recipe extends naturally to other hyperbolic components (attention, batch normalization, graph convolution) and whether it generalizes to variable-curvature or non-constant-curvature manifolds.
