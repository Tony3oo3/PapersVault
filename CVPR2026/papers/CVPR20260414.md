## Problem

Existing underwater image enhancement methods produce a single fixed output, but underwater imaging is inherently ill-posed and human preferences for enhancement level vary substantially. Prior controllable approaches (UIESS, PWAE, CECF) either lack explicit numerical control over enhancement degree or cannot accept natural-language instructions, leaving a gap for precise, user-preferred enhancement tuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260414/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SDUIE-Quant takes a real-world underwater RGB image and a continuous fusion factor α ∈ [0,1] (where 0 = pure hue preservation, 1 = full enhancement) and outputs a single enhanced image. SDUIE-Text takes the same underwater image plus a text prompt following the template 'Enhance this image by level X' (X ∈ {one, …, ten}) and produces the corresponding enhancement. Training uses 1001 synthetic underwater–terrestrial pairs generated via a physics-based color-distortion model, plus unlabeled real-world underwater and terrestrial images; evaluation is conducted at 256×256 on UCCS, EUVP, U45, and Challenge-60.

## Method

SDUIE fine-tunes a pretrained latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) with LoRA in a dual-branch architecture sharing a single UNet in latent space: an enhancement branch (encoder E_ir → UNet → decoder D_ie) trained supervised on synthetic underwater-to-terrestrial pairs, and a hue-preservation branch (E_ir → UNet → D_ir) trained self-supervised to reconstruct real underwater and terrestrial images. Both branches use pixel L1 losses plus adversarial losses with separate discriminators, and the shared UNet aligns synthetic and real representations in latent space. For inference, SDUIE-Quant merges the two decoder LoRA weights via spherical interpolation parameterized by α, yielding a continuous enhancement spectrum. SDUIE-Text then initializes its encoder, diffusion model, and decoder from SDUIE-Quant and fine-tunes them on image–prompt pairs (enhanced images at various α levels paired with 'Enhance this image by level X' prompts), enabling direct text-semantic control of enhancement intensity without requiring a numeric parameter at test time.

## Results

SDUIE-Quant achieves the best UIQM scores across all four benchmark datasets: 5.010 (Challenge-60), 5.501 (U45), 5.351 (UCCS), and 4.966 (EUVP), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline HLRP (4.392, 4.908, 4.760, 4.491 respectively). SDUIE-Quant also leads on URANKER in all four datasets (1.850, 2.478, 2.481, 2.292), while SDUIE-Text achieves the best UCIQE on U45 (0.620) and EUVP (0.609). The two variants perform closely, confirming that text-guided control does not sacrifice quantitative quality relative to the numerical fusion-factor control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDUIE-Quant dominates on UIQM across all four datasets, exceeding the best baseline HLRP by 0.62 (Challenge-60), 0.59 (U45), 0.59 (UCCS), and 0.48 (EUVP). It also leads on URANKER in every dataset, with the largest margin over HLRP on U45 (2.478 vs. 1.364, +1.11) and UCCS (2.481 vs. 1.299, +1.18). HLRP retains the best UCIQE on Challenge-60 (0.644), UCCS (0.646), and EUVP (0.655), reflecting its stronger colorfulness preservation, while SDUIE-Text edges out on UCIQE for U45 (0.620 vs. HLRP's 0.601). Among the deep-learning baselines, Semi-UIR and UIE-DM are the closest competitors on URANKER but trail SDUIE-Quant by 0.4–0.5 points. Non-deep methods (MIP, IBLA, ULAP) are consistently the weakest, with several producing negative URANKER scores, confirming the advantage of learned enhancement. Overall, SDUIE trades a small UCIQE gap (≈0.04–0.09 below HLRP on three datasets) for large gains in perceptual quality (UIQM) and ranking-based quality (URANKER), and uniquely offers continuous level control that none of the baselines provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDUIE builds on latent diffusion models (Rombach et al., CVPR 2022) as its generative backbone and repurposes LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) weight merging—previously used for multi-task composition in text-to-image generation (Zhong et al., 2024)—as a continuous control knob for enhancement intensity via spherical interpolation. It departs from prior controllable enhancement methods (CECF, PWAE, UIESS) that rely on style latent spaces or guidance images for diversity, instead introducing explicit numerical and textual level control. The semi-supervised training strategy, combining physics-based synthetic underwater images with real-world self-reconstruction through a shared UNet, extends the synthetic-to-real adaptation paradigm from prior underwater enhancement work (e.g., HCLR-Net, Semi-UIR) into the diffusion setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: most underwater enhancement methods produce a single fixed output, yet human preferences for blue-green tone retention vary substantially, making level-aware controllability practically important for real-world deployment. The dual-modality design (continuous α slider + natural language prompts) offers a user-friendly interface that could generalize to other image enhancement or restoration tasks where 'how much' matters as much as 'what.' Limitations include a small subjective study (4 volunteers), evaluation restricted to 256×256 for some baselines, and reliance on adversarial distillation for single-step inference, which may constrain fidelity at higher resolutions. Open questions include whether the LoRA-merging control mechanism transfers to video enhancement or other degradation types, and whether more diverse prompt templates or LLM-generated instructions further improve text-guided control.
