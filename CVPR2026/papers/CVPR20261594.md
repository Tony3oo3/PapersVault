## Problem

Existing fine-grained alignment methods for text-based person retrieval either enforce rigid one-to-one word-region matching or use pointwise attention that normalizes weights without explicit constraints on the total matching capacity each unit can carry. Under attribute overlap and background noise, this causes matching weights to collapse onto a few high-frequency cues or leak to irrelevant regions, degrading discriminative power. The paper asks how to simultaneously model many-to-many semantic correspondences while explicitly controlling per-unit matching capacity, without requiring fine-grained annotations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a natural-language description (up to 64–77 tokens depending on the text encoder) and a gallery of person images (patchified into N_i local regions by the visual encoder). During training, QC-Align operates on the local features—N_i × d patch embeddings and M_i × d token embeddings—producing a transport matrix Π ∈ R^{N_i × M_i} that encodes many-to-many word-region correspondences. At inference, only the global representations (v^g, t^g) are used for cosine-similarity ranking, so the transport machinery adds zero latency; the method is purely a training-time regularizer.

## Method

QC-Align decouples capacity estimation from transport allocation into two parameter-free components. First, the Context-Aware Marginal Estimator (CAME) computes bidirectional cross-modal attention to build context representations for each unit, scores each unit's discriminability via cosine similarity to its context, softmax-normalizes these scores into importance weights, and back-projects them through the attention matrices to yield non-uniform marginal distributions (μ, ν) that serve as per-unit matching quotas. Second, the Quota-Calibrated Transport (QCT) objective solves an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem via Sinkhorn iterations under these marginal constraints, minimizing cross-modal transport cost while the marginals prevent over-concentration on a few words or irrelevant regions. To avoid trivial feature collapse, QCT adopts a Sinkhorn divergence formulation that subtracts intra-modal self-transport costs (computed under uniform marginals), retaining only the cost specific to cross-modal alignment. The total training loss combines a standard InfoNCE contrastive term on global representations with λ·L_QCT on local features, enabling end-to-end optimization of the shared encoders without any fine-grained annotations or additional inference cost.

## Results

On CUHK-PEDES, CADA+QC-Align achieves 79.31% Rank-1 (up from 78.37% for CADA alone), surpassing the previous best MARS (77.62%) by 1.69%. On the smaller RSTPReid dataset, QC-Align yields the largest absolute gain of +4.60% Rank-1 over the non-CLIP baseline (38.75% → 43.35%). In cross-dataset transfer (CUHK→ICFG), CADA+QC-Align reaches 53.68% Rank-1, outperforming CADA alone (51.37%) by 2.31%. The method is parameter-free and adds zero inference overhead, serving purely as a training regularizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QC-Align is applied as a plug-and-play regularizer on top of existing backbones rather than competing as a standalone architecture. On CUHK-PEDES, CADA+QC-Align (79.31% R@1) surpasses MARS (77.62%) and BiLMA (74.03%), establishing a new state of the art; IRRA+QC-Align (74.62%) edges out TBPS (73.54%) and BiLMA (74.03%). On RSTPReid, CADA+QC-Align (69.60% baseline → improved) clearly leads MARS (67.55%) and TBPS (61.95%). In the ablation-style comparison (Table 2), QC-Align outperforms standard OT, UOT, and SCAN on the CLIP architecture, where those methods actually degrade performance (e.g., SCAN drops 0.92% on CUHK-PEDES-CLIP), while QC-Align gains +2.18% over the CLIP baseline. In cross-dataset transfer (Table 3), CADA+QC-Align improves over CADA by 2.31% (C→I) and 1.62% (I→C), and IRRA+QC-Align gains 2.11% (C→I) and 3.91% (I→C) over IRRA alone, confirming that the quota-calibrated transport reduces dataset-specific shortcut bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimal transport framework for cross-modal retrieval (Cuturi 2013; Feydy et al. 2019) by replacing the standard uniform marginals with context-aware, non-uniform marginals estimated parameter-free from cross-modal attention (CAME), and by adopting Sinkhorn divergence to prevent the representation collapse that plagues naive OT on Transformer backbones. It departs from prior fine-grained alignment methods (attention-based like SCAN, or explicit segmentation-based) by explicitly constraining the total matching capacity each word or region can carry, rather than relying solely on row/column-normalized similarity. The work also builds on the dual-encoder contrastive learning paradigm dominant in TPR, positioning QC-Align as an additive training regularizer rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-free, plug-and-play nature of QC-Align (zero inference overhead, no fine-grained annotations) makes it immediately applicable to existing TPR pipelines, and the consistent gains across CNN and CLIP-based backbones on three benchmarks demonstrate broad practical utility. The finding that standard OT and SCAN actively degrade Transformer-based models while helping CNN-based ones reveals an important architecture-dependent failure mode in local alignment that the community has not previously characterized. Open questions include whether the marginal estimation strategy generalizes to other cross-modal tasks (video-text, audio-visual), whether the modest 1–2% improvements scale to larger or noisier datasets, and whether the Sinkhorn divergence component can be replaced by lighter alternatives without sacrificing the anti-collapse guarantee.
