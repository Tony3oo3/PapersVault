## Problem

Integrating vision understanding into a conversational speech LLM faces three compounding challenges: paired image-speech datasets are orders of magnitude scarcer than image-text data, real-time full-duplex dialogue imposes strict compute and memory budgets at inference, and the model must preserve prosodic features (tone, emotion) that are lost in text-only pipelines. No open-source solution existed that could naturally talk about an image while seamlessly switching to unrelated conversation topics under these constraints.

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image (448×448, encoded to 1024 tokens by PaliGemma's Stage-2 vision encoder), the user's live audio stream, and the full conversation history as summed speech tokens (temporally aligned text + audio codebooks). It produces the assistant's audio (decoded from a hierarchy of residual audio codebooks via a small depth transformer) and text tokens simultaneously at 12.5 Hz, with only ~7 ms additional latency per step over the base Moshi model on an L4 GPU, staying well within the 80 ms real-time threshold.

## Method

MoshiVis augments the frozen 7B Moshi speech backbone with lightweight gated cross-attention modules inserted between the self-attention and FFN in every transformer block, using image embeddings as keys and values. A 2-layer MLP gate (hidden size 1/8) modulates each cross-attention output, allowing the model to suppress image information and exactly recover base-model behavior when the conversation topic is irrelevant. Training is a single-stage, parameter-efficient fine-tune of only 206M cross-attention parameters, using a mixture of 'speechless' image-text samples (leveraging large VLM datasets) and a small proportion of image-speech samples, exploiting Moshi's joint text-audio token prediction to transfer visual knowledge into the speech modality. QKV projections are shared across all layers so image KV pairs are computed once and cached, and a synthetic dialogue pipeline (two Mistral-Nemo models role-playing user/assistant over shared image captions) generates realistic multi-turn spoken visual conversations for final training.

## Results

At the recommended p_audio = 25% mixed-data ratio, MoshiVis achieves ~68% accuracy on OCR-VQA, ~78% on VQAv2, and ~127 CIDEr on COCO when prompted in text, with comparable scores in audio evaluation. As a generalist conversational model, MoshiVis scores 78.1 CIDEr on COCO captioning in audio, substantially outperforming other speech-capable omni-models (AnyGPT: 43.9, MiniOmni2: 27.9). When fine-tuned specifically for captioning on MS-COCO, MoshiVis reaches 127.6 CIDEr in audio, surpassing AnyGPT-base (110.0) trained on 600M LAION-COCO samples. The model adds only ~7 ms per inference step over the base Moshi backbone on an L4 GPU, staying well within the 80 ms real-time threshold.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoshiVis substantially outperforms both speech-capable omni-models on COCO audio captioning: it scores 78.1 CIDEr versus AnyGPT's 43.9 and MiniOmni2's 27.9 as generalist chat models, a gap of 34 and 50 points respectively. When fine-tuned for captioning, MoshiVis (127.6) also beats AnyGPT-base (110.0) despite being trained on 550k MS-COCO samples versus AnyGPT's 600M LAION-COCO samples. Against the text-only Pixtral 12B (90.3 CIDEr), MoshiVis trails by ~12 points, but Pixtral does not produce speech output and thus is not a direct conversational competitor. On the three downstream vision benchmarks (OCR-VQA, VQAv2, COCO), MoshiVis reaches scores comparable to PaliGemma stage 3 [6], which further fine-tunes both the vision encoder and LLM, whereas MoshiVis keeps both backbones frozen and trains only 206M adaptation parameters. The paper notes that the lower absolute CIDEr scores of all dialogue models (including Pixtral) reflect the inherent tension between natural conversational verbosity and the rigid formatting expected by CIDEr.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of three research threads: (1) cross-attention-based VLMs (Flamingo, ePALM, MAPL) that inject visual information via lightweight adaptation modules rather than token insertion, (2) the Moshi speech LLM for real-time full-duplex dialogue, and (3) visual dialogue datasets that extend beyond single-turn Q&A. It deliberately departs from the dominant VLM paradigm of inserting image tokens into the sequence (which disrupts pretrained LLMs, bloats KV cache, and requires multi-stage training) in favor of frozen-backbone cross-attention with shared QKV projections. It also departs from cascaded ASR+VLM+TTS pipelines and from joint multi-modal pretraining approaches (AnyGPT, MiniOmni2) by leveraging an already-conversational speech backbone and a single-stage parameter-efficient fine-tuning recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe of mixing 'speechless' image-text data with a small fraction of speech samples (as low as 1–25%) to train visual understanding in a speech model is broadly applicable and significantly lowers the data barrier for building vision-speech systems. The 7 ms per-step latency overhead and 206 M trainable parameters make this approach accessible for real-time deployment, which is critical for conversational AI. Open questions remain around evaluating conversational models (the paper itself notes that dialogue-trained models underperform on rigid benchmarks like CIDEr), scaling to video inputs, and whether the synthetic dialogue pipeline generalizes to more diverse or adversarial conversation patterns. The gating mechanism's learned behavior during context switches is intriguing but only qualitatively characterized.
