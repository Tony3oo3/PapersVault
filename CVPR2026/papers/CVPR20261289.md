## Problem

Existing controllable video generation methods either treat camera and object motion separately or rely on restrictive interfaces (2D sketches, depth maps, optical flow) that make it difficult to specify complex, choreographed scenes where object dynamics and camera paths are inherently entangled. No prior framework generates both object and camera trajectories jointly from natural language in a shared 3D space, leaving a gap between high-level directorial intent and machine-executable motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261289/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language prompt describing object and camera motion (e.g., 'the drone orbits clockwise around the runner while the camera tail-tracks from behind') and optionally a first-frame image. It produces a pair of symbolic DSL motion programs (one for the object, one for the camera), each an ordered list of up to 4 motion tags spanning T=21 frames in 4 temporal segments. These programs are deterministically converted to 3D bounding-box trajectories, rendered into a frame-aligned control video (2D box overlays + projected global cube edges), and fed to a pretrained diffusion generator (VACE) to synthesize the final video.

## Method

LAMP reframes motion planning as language-to-program synthesis: a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL LLM generates structured motion programs in a cinematography-inspired DSL with four primitives (free-form, orbit track, tail track, rotation track) parameterized by translational, rotational, and stylistic modifiers (e.g., speed_fast, ease_in, jitter_low). The generation is factorized as p(s_obj|t_obj)·p(s_cam|s_obj, t_cam), reflecting the hierarchical cinematographic relationship where the subject's motion anchors the scene and the camera adjusts relative to it. A deterministic DSL-to-trajectory converter integrates per-frame parameters into physically coherent 3D paths, with tracking primitives computed relative to the object before transforming to world coordinates. The LLM is trained on a 400K-sample procedural corpus of text-DSL-trajectory triplets, where motion primitives are sampled and composed algorithmically and an auxiliary LLM paraphrases descriptions for linguistic diversity. Ablations confirm that the symbolic DSL representation outperforms direct per-frame coordinate regression (discretized into bins) in both directional accuracy and rotation error, validating that LLMs reason more effectively over structured programs than continuous values.

## Results

On the ET benchmark, LAMP achieves an F1 score of 0.976 and CLaTr score of 35.10 on the Pure split, and 0.769 F1 / 36.59 CLaTr on the harder Mixed split, substantially outperforming all baselines (best prior: ET-DirB at 0.86 F1 / 23.10 CLaTr on Pure). On DataDoP, LAMP (pretrained, no dataset-specific training) reaches a Revised F1 of 0.763 and CLaTr of 36.29, surpassing GenDoP (F1 0.400, CLaTr 36.18) which was explicitly trained on that dataset. On the procedural object-motion test set, LAMP attains 0.9983 coarse translation F1, 0.9293 fine translation F1, and 0.975 fine rotation F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAMP decisively outperforms all four ET-benchmark baselines on both splits: on the Mixed split, LAMP's F1 of 0.769 exceeds ET-DirC (0.48) by ~60% relative and ET-DirB (0.39) by ~97% relative, while CLaTr improves from ~21–22 to 36.6. On DataDoP, LAMP (pretrained without access to the training set) beats GenDoP (trained on DataDoP) on Revised F1 (0.763 vs. 0.400) and matches its CLaTr (36.29 vs. 36.18), though GenDoP achieves a lower FID (22.7 vs. 66.9), suggesting LAMP's trajectories are slightly less diverse in distribution. Against CCD and Director3D, LAMP wins by wide margins on every metric (e.g., F1 0.763 vs. 0.297 for CCD). In the qualitative video user study, LAMP is preferred over the GPT-DSL in-context baseline 52% of the time (with 25% 'both'), and is consistently preferred over the text-only Wan 2.1 baseline and the GPT-Traj trajectory-regression baseline on camera adherence, object adherence, and overall quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

LAMP builds directly on the CameraBench taxonomy [21] for its motion primitive vocabulary, but departs fundamentally from prior camera-control methods (CineMaster, CameraCtrl II, ReCamMaster) that treat objects as static and from LLM-assisted approaches (Modular-Cam, VideoDirectorGPT, ET, GenDoP) that use language models only for captioning or selecting from fixed operator libraries. The key conceptual departure is casting motion control as language-to-program synthesis: rather than regressing per-frame 3D coordinates (as CCD, ET, Director3D, and GenDoP do), LAMP fine-tunes an LLM to emit structured DSL programs that are deterministically compiled into trajectories. This unifies object and camera motion planning in a shared 3D space, a joint formulation absent from all cited baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By decoupling motion planning from video synthesis, LAMP enables iterative, text-based refinement of trajectories before committing to expensive diffusion-based rendering—a workflow that mirrors how human directors actually block shots. The 400K-sample procedural dataset and the DSL specification provide reusable infrastructure for future language-driven motion research. However, the DSL is currently limited to four primitives, lacks semantic object categories, fine-grained orientation, and multi-object interaction, and the object-trajectory evaluation is conducted on the authors' own procedural dataset, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to in-the-wild, physically diverse scenes. A natural next step is extending the DSL to handle multiple interacting objects and appearance-level specifications.
