## Problem

Conventional latent diffusion models train the VAE and diffusion model in separate stages, freezing the VAE before diffusion training begins, which prevents the diffusion objective from shaping the latent space for generation. Prior attempts at end-to-end joint training fail catastrophically because unrestricted backpropagation of the diffusion loss causes the latent space to collapse into a degenerate, low-variance representation that discards semantic content. The paper identifies this latent collapse as the root cause and asks: what mechanism can stabilize end-to-end training so the VAE benefits from direct diffusion gradient feedback?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263788/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an ImageNet class label as conditioning input and generates RGB images at 256×256 or 512×512 resolution. The VAE compresses images by 16× spatially (f16d32) for 256×256 generation or 32× (f32d32) for 512×512, producing a 32-channel latent that the diffusion model denoises. Sampling uses the Euler-Maruyama SDE solver with 250 NFEs, and guidance (when applied) uses AutoGuidance rather than Classifier-Free Guidance.

## Method

SpeeDiff jointly trains a ViT-VAE and a Refined-DiT diffusion model from scratch in a single end-to-end pass without stop-gradient. The key technical contribution is the Tweedie Pixel Reconstruction (TPR) loss, which estimates the clean latent from an intermediate noisy state via Tweedie's formula (ẑ₀ = z_t − t·V_θ(z_t, t)), decodes it back to pixel space, and penalizes the MSE against the ground-truth image—this pixel-level anchor prevents the latent collapse that plagues vanilla end-to-end training. On top of this stable foundation, the architecture replaces the CNN-VAE with a fully transformer-based ViT-VAE and modernizes the diffusion backbone with RMSNorm, SwiGLU, 2D RoPE, and shared global modulation. Finally, REPA++ introduces dual representation alignment: Latent-REPA aligns the clean latent to DINOv3 patch features via a learned MLP, while Diff-REPA aligns intermediate diffusion features to the same VFM representations, both optimized end-to-end with the VAE and diffusion model.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, SpeeDiff-XL (w/ REPA++) achieves a gFID of 1.50 without guidance after 200 epochs, setting a new state-of-the-art among all compared methods. Without any VFM alignment (w/o REPA++), SpeeDiff-XL still reaches gFID 2.42, outperforming all non-alignment baselines. On ImageNet 512×512 with a 32× compressed VAE, SpeeDiff-XL (w/ REPA++) attains gFID 1.53 without guidance. In terms of convergence speed, SpeeDiff-XL reaches gFID 7.36 within just 10 epochs, surpassing SiT's 1400-epoch result, representing a >140× speedup over Vanilla SiT and 61× over REPA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpeeDiff-XL (w/ REPA++) achieves the best gFID without guidance (1.50) among all compared methods on ImageNet 256×256, outperforming REPA-E (1.83, 800 epochs), REG (2.20, 480 epochs), and REPA (5.90, 800 epochs) by a wide margin. Among non-alignment methods, SpeeDiff-XL (w/o REPA++) at gFID 2.42 surpasses MDTv2 (1.58 w/ guidance only), MDT (6.23), FasterDiT (7.91), SiT (8.61), DiT (9.62), and MaskDiT (5.69). On ImageNet 512×512, SpeeDiff-XL (w/ REPA++) reaches gFID 1.53 without guidance, beating EDM2-XXL (1.91) and all other 512×512 baselines including U-ViT (4.05 w/ guidance) and SiT (2.62 w/ guidance). In convergence speed, SpeeDiff-XL reaches gFID 7.36 in 10 epochs, already surpassing SiT's 1400-epoch result (8.61), while REPA requires 800 epochs to reach 5.90. Notably, SpeeDiff uses a fully transformer-based pipeline (ViT-VAE + Refined-DiT) versus the CNN-based SD-VAE used by most baselines, and jointly scales both the VAE and diffusion model rather than freezing the tokenizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpeeDiff extends the latent diffusion paradigm by replacing the conventional two-stage pipeline (pretrain VAE, then train diffusion on frozen latents) with a single-stage end-to-end joint training of VAE and diffusion from scratch. The idea of joint training traces back to LSGM [54] and was revisited in REPA-E [33], but both suffered from latent space collapse; SpeeDiff diagnoses this failure mode (suppressed per-channel variances, large biases, non-Gaussian peaked distributions) and resolves it with a simple Tweedie Pixel Reconstruction loss that anchors the VAE in pixel space. Architecturally, it replaces the CNN-VAE with a ViT-VAE and modernizes DiT with RMSNorm, SwiGLU, and 2D RoPE (Refined-DiT), while extending REPA [60] into REPA++ by adding a Latent-REPA component that aligns the clean latent itself (not just intermediate diffusion features) with VFM representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 140× convergence speedup over SiT and 61× over REPA, combined with SOTA FID (1.50 without guidance at 256×256, 1.53 at 512×512), makes end-to-end LDM training practically viable and substantially reduces the compute barrier for high-quality image generation. The finding that the jointly-trained VAE produces a 'generation-friendly' latent space (Table 4: a new diffusion model on the frozen VAE converges at nearly the same rate as full SpeeDiff) suggests SpeeDiff can serve as a latent pretraining stage, decoupling tokenizer quality from downstream diffusion training. Open questions include whether the end-to-end paradigm and TPR loss transfer to text-to-image, video generation, or larger-scale settings, and whether the 32× compressed VAE at 512×512 can be pushed further without quality loss.
