## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on BEV 3D object detectors are either invasive (requiring physical modification of target vehicles) or lack true 3D scene consistency, relying on flat 2D patches or NeRF-based renders that fail to maintain correct perspective and occlusion across multiple camera views. This paper addresses the gap by proposing the first framework for generating universal, non-invasive, and 3D-consistent adversarial objects that can be placed in the environment without contacting target vehicles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261609/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes calibrated multi-camera images (6 cameras, 256×704 or 736×1280 resolution), 3D bounding box annotations for all scene objects, and an initial 3D mesh (default: cylinder with radius 0.3 m and height 2.0 m) as input. It produces an optimized adversarial mesh (vertices and texture map) and the corresponding rendered adversarial multi-view images. The mesh is positioned 0.1 m from the target vehicle's right-rear-bottom corner using the vehicle's 3D bounding box, and occlusion masks are generated via SAM2 segmentation of overlapping scene objects.

## Method

The pipeline has three core components: (1) a differentiable renderer (PyTorch3D) that projects the 3D adversarial mesh onto all N camera views using calibrated intrinsics/extrinsics, ensuring geometric consistency across views and frames; (2) a Realistic Occlusion Processing Module that applies a two-stage filter—2D bounding-box overlap followed by a BEV-plane visibility cone check—to identify true occluders, then iteratively updates the mesh's transparency mask using SAM2 segmentation masks and composites multiple meshes via a Painter's Algorithm; (3) a BEV Spatial Feature-Guided Optimization that jointly minimizes three losses: target suppression (reducing confidence in the target region), localization disruption (maximizing L1 error between predicted and ground-truth 3D boxes), and scene confusion (minimizing cosine similarity between BEV features of adversarial and clean images). The mesh vertices and texture are optimized end-to-end, and the static mesh placement in world coordinates ensures temporal consistency across frames without re-optimization.

## Results

On nuScenes, the proposed non-invasive adversarial mesh achieves ASR of 0.613/0.657/0.720 (IoU 0.3/0.5/0.7) on BEVDet, 0.530/0.590/0.670 on BEVDet4D, and 0.405/0.472/0.621 on BEVFormer (without Real Occlusion). In terms of scene-level degradation, the attack reduces NDS by 38–47% and mAP by 50–58% across the three detectors (e.g., BEVDet NDS drops from 0.3942 to 0.2097, mAP from 0.3086 to 0.1298). Enabling the Realistic Occlusion Processing Module reduces ASR (e.g., BEVDet ASR0.3 drops from 0.613 to 0.454) but still causes substantial performance degradation, confirming the attack's robustness under physically realistic occlusions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Adv3D [15] (the closest non-invasive baseline using NeRF-based rendering), the proposed method achieves a 41.4% NDS drop and 55.6% mAP drop, substantially exceeding Adv3D's 19.3% NDS and 44.0% mAP drops. The paper also notes that Adv3D's evaluation setting introduces severe self-occlusion (randomly rendering two cars per view), which artificially inflates its initial performance drop and makes the comparison less favorable to Adv3D. Against UAP [35] (an invasive 2D patch-based method), the proposed method outperforms at lower and medium IoU thresholds (ASR@0.1: 0.568 vs 0.405; ASR@0.3: 0.613 vs 0.514; ASR@0.5: 0.657 vs 0.613), indicating superior ability to cause critical detection failures. However, UAP achieves a slightly higher ASR@0.7 (0.759 vs 0.720), which the authors attribute to UAP's invasive setup placing patches directly on the nearest vehicle with minimal occlusion, making it a less realistic threat model. Overall, the proposed method demonstrates stronger scene-level disruption and greater practical deployability as a non-invasive attack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new threat model at the intersection of adversarial 3D detection attacks and differentiable rendering. It departs from the dominant 'invasive' paradigm (modifying target vehicles with patches or textures) and from 2D patch-based attacks that lack multi-view consistency, instead placing a full 3D adversarial mesh into the scene via PyTorch3D with an occlusion-aware module. The closest prior work, Adv3D, uses NeRF to synthesize adversarial vehicles but renders them from a fixed viewpoint and pastes them onto 2D images, failing to achieve true scene-level geometric integration. The BEV spatial feature-guided loss is a novel optimization component that targets the detector's internal representations rather than only final bounding-box predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a concrete, physically deployable vulnerability in mainstream BEV detectors: a small non-invasive object placed near a vehicle can cause 40–55% drops in NDS/mAP, revealing that these models over-rely on learned environmental co-occurrence priors rather than robust object-level reasoning. The physical proof-of-concept and the multi-model, multi-distance, multi-shape ablations make this a practical robustness-evaluation tool for AD developers. Open questions include whether the vulnerability is dataset-specific (the pedestrian-like textures hint at annotation biases), how to defend against scene-level contextual manipulation, and whether the attack generalizes to LiDAR-fusion or end-to-end driving stacks.
