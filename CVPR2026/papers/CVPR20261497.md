## Problem

Strand-level hair reconstruction from monocular video faces a severe accuracy–efficiency trade-off: implicit neural methods capture global shape but lose fine strand detail, while explicit optimization approaches (e.g., MonoHair) achieve high fidelity at 4–9 hours per hairstyle, far slower than the 30–60 minute budgets of typical volumetric avatar pipelines. The paper targets closing this gap without sacrificing strand-level geometric detail, especially for challenging curly and complex hairstyles.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261497/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video as input, from which COLMAP estimates camera poses and Instant-NGP reconstructs a coarse scene. Gabor filters extract 2D orientation and confidence maps per frame, and a View-Aware Transformer infers internal (occluded) strand orientations from rendered unidirectional strand maps. The output is a set of individual 3D hair strands (polylines) with scalp attachment, represented as an occupancy-orientation volume, directly importable into rendering (Blender) and physics simulation (Houdini) pipelines.

## Method

EfficientMonoHair introduces two key modules under a hybrid implicit-explicit framework. First, FPMVO (Fusion-Patch-based Multi-View Optimization) replaces MonoHair's sequential per-view patch search with a three-step pipeline: multi-view direction sampling with depth uncertainty, medoid-based direction fusion that selects the most geometrically consistent candidate direction (rather than averaging, which causes orientation blurring), and patch-based consistency scoring in the image domain—accelerating outer direction optimization by ~28×. Second, PHG (Parallel Hair Growing) reformulates strand tracing as a fully GPU-parallelized volumetric pipeline: tens of thousands of scalp seeds are traced simultaneously with relaxed voxel occupancy constraints (postponing collision checks to batch boundaries), and short segments are linked via batched KD-tree nearest-neighbor queries enforcing both spatial proximity and tangent-direction alignment. This parallel growing is 2–3× faster than MonoHair's sequential CPU integration and substantially more robust to noisy orientation fields, yielding the most accurate strand reconstructions when combined with MonoHair's original PMVO.

## Results

On Hair20K, EfficientMonoHair (full) achieves 5.9× speed-up over MonoHair (P=5) (23 min vs. 136 min) while matching or exceeding its reconstruction quality: occupation F1 of 62.9% at 4 mm voxel size (vs. 60.6% for MonoHair) and orientation F1 of 30.3% at 4/40 (vs. 34.7% for MonoHair, ~88% of baseline). The PHG-only ablation achieves the highest accuracy across all metrics (occupation F1 65.4%, orientation F1 36.5%) at 1.9× speed. DiffLocks is 818× faster (10 s) but substantially less accurate, with occupation F1 of only 43.2% and orientation F1 of 24.0% at the 4 mm/4/40 setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MonoHair (P=5), the primary explicit-optimization baseline, EfficientMonoHair (full) is 5.9× faster (23 min vs. 136 min) and slightly outperforms it on occupation F1 at all voxel sizes (e.g., 62.9% vs. 60.6% at 4 mm), while achieving ~88% of its orientation F1 (30.3% vs. 34.7% at 4/40). The PHG-only variant actually surpasses MonoHair on both occupation and orientation F1 at every setting, confirming that parallel growing improves robustness to direction-field noise. Against MonoHair's fast variant (P=1), which degrades sharply in quality for only a 1.2× speed-up, EfficientMonoHair is ~5× faster while maintaining higher accuracy on both metrics. Against DiffLocks, the diffusion-based implicit method, EfficientMonoHair is dramatically more accurate (e.g., 62.9% vs. 43.2% occupation F1 at 4 mm; 30.3% vs. 24.0% orientation F1 at 4/40), though DiffLocks is orders of magnitude faster (10 s vs. 23 min). Visually, GaussianHaircut [41] fails on several challenging curly hairstyles where EfficientMonoHair succeeds, highlighting the robustness advantage of the proposed approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct efficiency-focused extension of MonoHair (CVPR 2024), which established the hybrid implicit-explicit pipeline for strand-level hair reconstruction from monocular video. The two core departures are algorithmic: FPMVO replaces MonoHair's sequential per-view patch optimization with a simultaneous multi-view medoid-based direction fusion, and PHG replaces the serial CPU-based strand growing with a GPU-parallelized volumetric tracing that relaxes voxel occupancy constraints. The broader lineage traces from explicit multi-view geometric optimization (Paris, Luo et al.) through implicit neural representations (Neural Strands, NeuralHDHair) to recent hybrid Gaussian/diffusion-based methods (Neural Haircut, GaussianHaircut), positioning this work as an efficiency-driven refinement within the hybrid paradigm rather than a new representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing strand-level reconstruction from 4–9 hours to ~23 minutes brings it into the same time budget as volumetric avatar pipelines (30–60 min), making strand-level geometry feasible in production digital-human workflows. The ablation revealing that PHG alone (with MonoHair's original direction optimization) yields the highest accuracy is a useful insight: robustness of the growing strategy to noisy direction fields matters more than initial direction precision. Open limitations include failure on highly entangled hairstyles (braids, buns) and the still-sequential scalp attachment step, leaving room for future work on inter-strand dependency modeling and fully parallel endpoint matching.
