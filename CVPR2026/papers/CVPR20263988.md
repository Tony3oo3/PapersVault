## Problem

Diffusion Transformers are limited by the quadratic O(N²) cost of full self-attention, preventing scaling to high-resolution pixel-space sequences. Existing Top-K sparse attention methods reduce the attention stage but still incur quadratic cost in the selection stage and require ever-larger K values as sequences grow, because their single-level coarse representation cannot capture global structure. The paper identifies this single-level design as the root cause and asks whether a hierarchical, multi-level selection can achieve true log-linear scaling.

## Input / Output

The method operates directly on pixel-space images without patchification or VAE encoding, processing up to 256×256 (65,536) pixel tokens. For 2D spatial data, tokens are reordered so that spatially adjacent pixels become neighbors in the flattened 1D sequence before hierarchical pooling. A noise-rescaling factor s = n/64 is applied to the flow-matching scheduler for images larger than 64×64 to maintain consistent SNR, and high-resolution models are initialized from low-resolution pretrained weights.

## Method

LLSA compresses Q, K, V into O(log N) hierarchy levels via recursive mean pooling, then performs coarse-to-fine Top-K selection where each level only searches among KB candidates identified by the coarser level, reducing selection cost from O(N²) to O(NK). A Hierarchical KV Enrichment mechanism appends sparse tokens from multiple coarse levels into each query's key/value set, preserving global context so that a small constant K (e.g., K=8) suffices regardless of sequence length. Coarse tokens are reweighted by B^l during attention to reflect their larger effective receptive field. The GPU implementation replaces dense binary masks with sparse CSC indices obtained via a parallel transpose kernel, enabling both forward and backward passes to run in O(N log N) without quadratic memory overhead.

## Results

On FFHQ-128, LLSA (K=8) achieves FID 24.37 and training throughput of 436.40 ×10³ tokens/s, outperforming Full Attention (FID 24.91, 188.88) and both sparse baselines VSA (FID 26.91) and SLA (FID 25.73). On FFHQ-256, LLSA reaches FID 39.29 with 375.34 ×10³ tokens/s throughput, again beating Full Attention (FID 38.77, 61.64) in quality while being 6.09× faster. On ImageNet-256 via PixelFlow, LLSA achieves FID 20.41 and Inception Score 73.21, surpassing VSA (FID 23.59, IS 64.07) and SLA (FID 22.58, IS 65.31) with the highest throughput (34.16 img/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLSA is compared against two trainable Top-K sparse attention methods: VSA (which adds a full-attention branch on coarse tokens) and SLA (which adds a linear-attention branch for unselected tokens). LLSA wins on both FID and throughput across all four evaluation settings (FFHQ-128, FFHQ-256, ImageNet-256). Notably, the comparison is conservative: LLSA uses K=8 while VSA and SLA are given K=20 (128×128) or K=32 (256×256) to match the effective number of attended blocks, yet LLSA still achieves lower FID (e.g., 24.37 vs. 26.91 for VSA on FFHQ-128) and higher throughput (436.40 vs. 421.02). On ImageNet-256, the FID gap widens to 3.18 over VSA and 2.17 over SLA, with a 14.5% Inception Score improvement over VSA. The efficiency advantage stems from LLSA's log-linear selection (vs. quadratic for baselines) and its sparse-index-based backward kernel that avoids dense mask construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLSA extends the Top-K block sparse attention paradigm (MoBA, Native Sparse Attention) from a single-level selection to a multi-level hierarchical coarse-to-fine scheme, building directly on FlashAttention's tiling infrastructure. The most relevant prior work is Multi-resolution Attention [37], which also performs hierarchical Top-K selection but remains theoretical without a GPU implementation; LLSA provides the high-performance Triton kernels and validates the approach on long-sequence DiT training. The paper also departs from prior trainable sparse attention for DiTs (VSA, SLA, VMoBA) by replacing their single-level block selection with logarithmic hierarchy compression and introducing Hierarchical KV Enrichment to preserve global context without inflating K.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing both selection and attention costs to O(N log N), LLSA makes pixel-space DiT training at 256×256 (65,536 tokens) feasible on a single GPU, removing the need for VAE encoding and patchification that have been standard workarounds. The finding that K=8 with hierarchical enrichment outperforms K=32 without it challenges the prevailing assumption that sparsity must increase with sequence length to maintain quality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video-length sequences (tens of thousands of tokens), whether the hierarchical structure generalizes to non-visual modalities, and whether the KV reweighting heuristic (weight = block size) is optimal or could be learned.
