## Problem

Vision-language models like CLIP exhibit systematic, non-random misalignment between visually and semantically similar categories (e.g., terrier consistently confused with bulldog), revealing an intrinsic fine-grained discrimination gap that existing prompt tuning methods overlook. These fixed confusion patterns persist across models and datasets, yet prior approaches treat all classes uniformly without explicitly modeling inter-class confusion relationships.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262473/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and outputs a class label via cosine similarity between the image embedding and learned text prompt embeddings, operating on a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone. During training, the method additionally consumes a Confusion Bank of misclassified samples (indexed by the category each sample was misaligned into) and retrieves up to five confusion sample pairs per iteration with a batch size of 4. Evaluation spans 11 standard few-shot benchmarks (1/2/4/8/16-shot) plus zero-shot cross-dataset and cross-domain transfer settings.

## Method

CAPT constructs a Confusion Bank from the base model's misclassifications, then mines confusion at two complementary levels: a Semantic Confusion Miner (SEM) identifies stable inter-class confusion pairs using pseudo-GT (highest-confidence prediction) combined with global confusion statistics, and generates LLM-derived difference/commonality prompts; a Sample Confusion Miner (SAM) retrieves the most representative misclassified instance per confusion pair via cosine similarity and processes it through a Diff-Manner Adapter that fuses ViT self-attention (global context) with 2D depthwise convolution (local detail) under a dynamic weight α. A Multi-Granularity Discrepancy Expert (MGDE) module, structured as a Mixture-of-Experts with separate semantic-level and sample-level experts, adaptively routes and fuses the two confusion signals, while clustering-based prompt optimization compresses low-discriminative tokens. Training optimizes an InfoNCE contrastive loss over the confusion sample pairs with the backbone frozen, requiring only the adapter and expert parameters to be learned.

## Results

On the 16-shot base-to-novel generalization benchmark across 11 datasets, CAPT achieves 87.41% base accuracy, 80.90% novel accuracy, and 83.90% harmonic mean (HM), outperforming the strongest baseline PromptKD (86.96/80.73/83.73). In the cross-dataset transfer setting (trained on ImageNet, zero-shot evaluation), CAPT attains 71.95% average accuracy, surpassing PromptKD (71.33%) and LwEIB (68.81%). For cross-domain generalization, CAPT reaches 69.71% on ImageNet-V2, 50.46% on ImageNet-Sketch, 57.73% on ImageNet-A, and 83.61% on ImageNet-R, consistently leading all compared methods. The method also resolves 50.72% of confusable sample pairs stored in the Confusion Bank.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAPT outperforms all eight baselines on the average HM metric (83.90%), with the closest competitor being PromptKD at 83.73% (+0.17% gap). The advantage is more pronounced on fine-grained and texture-heavy datasets: CAPT leads PromptKD by +1.57% HM on DTD (79.51 vs 77.94), +0.55% on UCF101 (86.65 vs 86.10), and +0.71% on StanfordCars (83.84 vs 83.13). On ImageNet, CAPT achieves a higher novel-class accuracy (75.13% vs 74.66%) and HM (77.73% vs 77.62%) than PromptKD, though it trails by 0.32% on base accuracy. CAPT also dominates in cross-dataset transfer (71.95% avg vs 71.33% for PromptKD) and cross-domain generalization (e.g., 57.73% on ImageNet-A vs 51.73% for TAC, a +6.0% margin). The older CoOp baseline lags substantially, with a 12.24% HM gap on average, highlighting the cumulative gains from confusion-aware modeling over vanilla conditional prompt learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

CAPT extends the CLIP prompt-tuning lineage (CoOp, MaPLe, PromptKD, TAC, LwEIB) by reframing the optimization objective around the model's own systematic misclassification patterns rather than global image-text alignment alone. Its Confusion Bank and dual-granularity miners (SEM for inter-class semantic confusion, SAM for instance-level confusion) draw on ideas from contrastive triplet losses (DPC, TripletCLIP), LLM-generated chain-of-thought prompts, and Mixture-of-Experts routing, but the explicit indexing of misaligned samples by their confusion target and the Diff-Manner Adapter (ViT attention + 2D depthwise convolution) are new architectural contributions. The key conceptual departure from prior prompt-tuning work is treating the pretrained model's fixed confusion structure as a learnable signal rather than an irreducible error floor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The observation that confusion patterns are stable and class-specific (e.g., terrier→bulldog on OxfordPets) provides a concrete, actionable diagnostic for fine-grained vision-language models, and the 50.72% correction rate of confusable pairs demonstrates that a substantial fraction of residual errors are addressable through targeted confusion-aware training. However, the approach inherits the base model's confusion structure (built via PromptKD), raising open questions about whether the Confusion Bank remains valid when the backbone changes or when domain shift alters confusion topology. The authors note future work on task-adaptive confusion mining for dynamic ambiguity, suggesting the current static-bank formulation may not transfer cleanly to open-vocabulary or continually evolving settings.
