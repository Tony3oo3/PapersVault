## Problem

Face forgery detectors show a persistent gap between offline benchmark performance and real-world efficacy, which the authors attribute to the ecological invalidity of existing training data. Current datasets consist of static, curated examples that fail to capture the diverse human intents, iterative creation processes, and adversarial text-image interactions that characterize forgeries in social media environments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261347/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes real face images (cropped to 224×224 via RetinaFace) and optional text descriptions as input, and produces a large-scale multimodal dataset where each sample carries two labels: a binary image authenticity label (real/forged) and a text-image consistency label indicating whether the accompanying text accurately reflects the image's true status. The consistency label is the key novelty—forged images can be paired with either accurate or deliberately misleading descriptions, creating hard negative samples that go beyond simple binary classification.

## Method

Agent4FaceForgery is a two-phase multi-agent LLM framework. In Phase 1, agents equipped with a Profile module (quantifiable traits like forgery frequency, methodological diversity, and target conformity, plus a stylistic description, all initialized from FF++ creator statistics), a Memory module (factual and evaluative logs with LLM-driven reflection), and an Action module (sequential visual edit operators from a toolbox including face-swap, GAN-based attribute editing, and style synthesis, plus text generation) iteratively produce forged image-text blueprints. An Adaptive Rejection Sampling gate scores each candidate via a fused metric of agent self-assessment and an external detector, with a threshold that tightens over time to select progressively harder examples. In Phase 2, a multi-role social simulation (Watcher, Explorer, Critic, Chatter, Poster, and a Gemini Auditor that generates adversarial claims) produces realistic comment streams and hard negative samples where text contradicts image authenticity, enabling training on nuanced text-image consistency rather than mere image-level labels.

## Results

Trained on FF++ (HQ), Agent4FaceForgery achieves the best or near-best cross-dataset performance across all benchmarks: 99.50% AUC / 2.97% EER on FF++, 93.25% AUC / 13.04% EER on DFD, 88.10% AUC / 19.19% EER on DFDC-P, 86.50% AUC / 21.87% EER on WildDeepfake, and 87.10% AUC / 20.12% EER on Celeb-DF. On the DF40 robustness protocol (six unseen forgery techniques), the method reaches 93.9% average AUC, surpassing the previous best (SG-Det, 87.8%) by over 6 points. The LLaVA backbone fine-tuned with Agent4FaceForgery data improves from 83.2% to 92.2% AUC and from 24.8% to 16.8% EER on Celeb-DF.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Agent4FaceForgery outperforms all 20 baselines on 4 of 5 cross-dataset benchmarks (DFDC-P, WildDeepfake, Celeb-DF, and FF++ intra-domain) in both AUC and EER. The closest competitor, FFTG [43], leads on DFD AUC (94.79% vs. 93.25%) but trails on DFD EER (15.31% vs. 13.04%) and is surpassed by 3.36–4.83 AUC points on the remaining four datasets. On the DF40 robustness protocol, the method achieves 93.9% average AUC across six unseen forgery techniques, exceeding the prior best SG-Det [31] (87.8%) by 6.1 points and ProgressiveDet [7] (78.7%) by 15.2 points. Among earlier CNN-based detectors (Xception, EN-B4, F3-Net, MAT, GFF, LTW, LRL, DCL), the gap is even larger: the best prior method (DCL [42]) reaches only 82.30% AUC on Celeb-DF versus 87.10% for the proposed method. The method also dominates reconstruction-based (RECCE [3], SBI [40]) and frequency-domain approaches (F3-Net [36], GFF [30]) by 10–20 AUC points on the hardest cross-domain targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of work on improving face forgery detection algorithms (frequency-domain methods, reconstruction-based approaches, multimodal LLM detectors) by instead targeting the data side of the problem. It repurposes the multi-agent LLM simulation paradigm—previously applied to social simulation (AI Town), task automation (AutoGen), and collaborative programming (MetaGPT)—for the novel purpose of generating ecologically valid forgery training data with realistic social context. The detection task itself is extended from binary image authenticity to a richer formulation incorporating text-image consistency labels (δ) and adversarial social discourse, building conceptually on multimodal forgery analysis work like SIAF but shifting the contribution from detection architecture to data generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a widely acknowledged but under-addressed bottleneck: the ecological validity gap between curated benchmark datasets and real-world forgery ecosystems. By demonstrating that simulation-generated data yields consistent 8–12 point AUC gains across diverse backbones (Xception, ViT-B, LLaVA, Phi-3.5), it validates a scalable data-augmentation paradigm that could be extended to other media types and forgery modalities. Open questions include whether the simulated social dynamics truly capture the adversarial co-evolution seen in production environments, the computational cost of multi-agent simulation at scale, and whether the gains persist as forgery generation techniques advance beyond the toolbox used here.
