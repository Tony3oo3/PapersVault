## Problem

Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for autonomous driving suffer from a persistent misalignment between language instructions and generated actions (e.g., the model reasons 'change lane left' yet outputs a lane-keep trajectory), and from the inherent inefficiency of autoregressive action generation requiring T sequential forward passes for T waypoints. Prior remedies—data curation, post-hoc RL, or implicit latent-space matching—either sidestep the architectural root cause or lack direct, verifiable supervision for cross-modal consistency.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view camera images (encoded by InternViT-300M at 448px), a natural-language instruction, and a navigation target (either a GPS point or a text command). It produces a chain-of-thought textual rationale followed by 20 geometric path waypoints and 10 temporal speed waypoints per frame, all represented as discrete tokens from a unified 5,656-cell BEV grid spanning x∈[0,50] m and y∈[−30,30] m. At inference, the coarse-to-fine decoder emits the full trajectory in just two forward passes rather than 30 sequential steps.

## Method

LinkVLA unifies language and action tokens into a single shared discrete codebook processed by one VLM backbone (InternViT-300M + Qwen2-0.5B), eliminating the modality gap by design. Action tokenization uses a signed-log coordinate transformation to concentrate resolution near the ego-vehicle and spatial soft-labeling (a 2D Gaussian over neighboring grid cells) to preserve spatial topology in the supervision signal. A bidirectional training objective—action generation p(A|L,V) plus a novel action-understanding objective p(L|A,V) that forces the model to caption trajectories—enforces verifiable semantic consistency in the shared embedding space. For efficient inference, a coarse-to-fine (C2F) scheme replaces T-step autoregressive decoding with two parallel stages: a single forward pass predicts the trajectory endpoint, which is linearly interpolated into coarse waypoints, and a second pass refines all T waypoints in parallel conditioned on the vision-language context, reducing latency from 361 ms to 48 ms (86% reduction) while improving driving score.

## Results

On the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, LinkVLA achieves a Driving Score of 91.01 and Success Rate of 74.55%, surpassing the previous best (SimLingo: 85.07 DS, 67.27% SR) by 5.94 and 7.28 points respectively. The coarse-to-fine (C2F) inference reduces per-step latency from 361 ms (autoregressive) to 48 ms while simultaneously improving the Driving Score to 91.01. On the Action Dreaming instruction-following benchmark, the full model reaches 87.16% mean success rate across six instruction classes, and on DriveLM-hard it attains SPICE 73.0 / BLEU 74.7 / ROUGE-L 77.0 for VQA and SPICE 57.4 / BLEU 65.7 / ROUGE-L 70.8 for commentary generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LinkVLA outperforms all nine baselines on the primary Driving Score metric. Against the closest competitor SimLingo (CVPR 2025), LinkVLA gains +5.94 DS and +7.28% SR, with the largest multi-ability improvements in Overtake (+11.11) and Brake (+11.66); however, SimLingo retains a slight edge in Efficiency (259.23 vs 255.84) and lower latency (34 ms vs 48 ms). Against Orion (ICCV 2025), LinkVLA improves DS by +13.27 while also reducing latency from 65 ms to 48 ms. Against AutoVLA (NeurIPS 2025), LinkVLA gains +12.17 DS and +16.82% SR. The earlier end-to-end baselines (UniAD, VAD, DriveTransformer, DriveAdapter, ThinkTwice, TCP) trail by 27–49 DS points, reflecting the substantial advantage of the VLA paradigm with explicit language-action alignment. On instruction following (Action Dreaming), the full LinkVLA configuration reaches 87.16% mean success, exceeding the no-tokenization baseline by 17.05 points, with the alignment objective contributing the final +5.29 points over the tokenization+C2F-only variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

LinkVLA builds directly on the SimLingo [37] pipeline (data collection, LoRA adaptation, CARLA evaluation protocol) and the broader VLA-for-driving line (ORION, AutoVLA, DriveVLM, EMMA), but departs from them on two axes. Structurally, it replaces the modality gap between language and action with a shared discrete codebook, whereas prior methods either keep separate action heads (SimLingo's MLP, ORION's VAE) or align modalities only implicitly in latent space. Semantically, it introduces an explicit inverse task—generating language captions from trajectories—as a training objective, drawing an analogy to the image-captioning / text-to-image duality in unified multimodal models (Show-O, Transfusion, MetaMorph), which had not been applied to driving VLA before.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 86% inference-latency reduction (361 ms → 48 ms) via coarse-to-fine decoding is the most practically impactful contribution, making autoregressive VLA driving feasible at real-time rates without sacrificing driving score. The bidirectional training objective offers a lightweight, data-free mechanism for cross-modal alignment that could transfer to other VLA domains (robotics, manipulation). Key limitations include evaluation exclusively in the CARLA simulator with a 1B-parameter model, exclusion of variable-length CoT latency from timing comparisons, and reliance on a single expert policy (PDM-lite) for data collection, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to real-world sensor noise, larger backbones, or multi-agent interactions.
