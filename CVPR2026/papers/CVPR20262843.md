## Problem

Multi-period scene reconstruction—where the same spatial region is captured months or years apart with discontinuous geometric and appearance changes—remains largely unexplored. Static methods enforce a single time-invariant geometry, while dynamic methods assume smooth continuous motion; neither assumption holds when captures span seasons or years with abrupt changes like construction, vegetation growth, or seasonal shifts.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-period RGB images with calibrated camera poses (COLMAP format) registered into a common world coordinate frame, along with merged sparse point clouds from all periods used to initialize the anchor scaffold via voxelization into a uniform spatial grid. It produces a single unified 3D Gaussian representation (anchor scaffold with per-anchor and global features) that can render novel views at any discrete period or at continuous interpolated period indices, enabling both period-specific reconstruction and plausible intermediate-state synthesis.

## Method

ChronoGS represents the scene as a unified anchor scaffold (following Scaffold-GS) covering the union geometry across all periods, where each anchor stores a period-invariant base feature, a local period-varying feature pool (one vector per period), and explicit period-invariant Gaussian center offsets. A global period-varying feature captures scene-level factors like illumination and seasonal appearance. Temporal modulation uses a one-hot encoding for observed periods with linear interpolation for inter-period queries, which gates both local and global period-varying features before a lightweight MLP decodes them into K=10 Gaussians per anchor (shape, opacity, color). A geometry activation mechanism excludes Gaussians with negative decoded opacity from both compositing and gradient backpropagation, allowing the scaffold to adaptively deactivate anchors corresponding to geometry absent in a given period. The entire scaffold is jointly optimized across all periods with a hybrid L1+SSIM photometric loss, with periodic pruning of low-gradient anchors and spawning of new anchors where view-space positional gradients exceed a threshold.

## Results

On the ChronoScene benchmark, ChronoGS achieves 22.16 PSNR / 0.6533 SSIM / 0.3390 LPIPS (avg.) on real scenes and 28.80 PSNR / 0.8562 SSIM / 0.2509 LPIPS on synthetic scenes, outperforming all seven baselines across every scene. Memory footprint is 0.52 GB (real) and 0.65 GB (synthetic), comparable to or smaller than most baselines while delivering the best reconstruction quality. The method also requires only 40k training iterations for joint multi-period optimization, versus 120k for per-period static training (Table 2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChronoGS outperforms all seven baselines on every scene in both real and synthetic splits. Against the strongest static baseline GS-W, it gains +1.8 dB PSNR on real scenes (22.16 vs. 20.33) and +3.7 dB on synthetic (28.80 vs. 25.11). Against the strongest dynamic baseline Realtime4DGS, it gains +1.3 dB PSNR on real and +6.5 dB on synthetic while using roughly 10× less memory (0.52 GB vs. 5.53 GB real; 0.65 GB vs. 7.41 GB synthetic). The gap is largest against NeuSC (+7.8 dB real, +12.9 dB synthetic) and CLNeRF (+6.4 dB real, +8.3 dB synthetic), which assume fixed geometry or gradual changes and fail under the large discontinuous temporal gaps in ChronoScene. On synthetic scenes, ChronoGS also achieves the best SSIM (0.8562) and LPIPS (0.2509), confirming superior structural and perceptual fidelity beyond PSNR alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChronoGS extends the anchor-based scaffold paradigm of Scaffold-GS and the Gaussian splatting rendering pipeline of 3DGS into a temporally modulated setting. It departs from static methods (3DGS, Scaffold-GS) that enforce a single geometry, from dynamic methods (4DGS, D-NeRF, K-Planes) that assume smooth continuous motion, and from in-the-wild or continual-learning approaches (NeRF-W, GS-W, CLNeRF) that handle appearance drift but assume fixed geometry. The key conceptual departure is treating multi-period scenes as discrete in time yet shared in major structure, enabling a unified scaffold with per-anchor and global period-varying features plus an opacity-based geometry activation mechanism rather than deformation fields or per-period models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ChronoScene benchmark fills a genuine gap: no prior dataset systematically captures long-term, discontinuous geometric and appearance evolution in aligned multi-period captures, making it a valuable testbed for future temporal 3D modeling work. The cross-period benefit finding—that joint multi-period training improves reconstruction of static regions over per-period training while halving storage and iterations—suggests a practical path for efficient long-term scene monitoring. Open questions include scalability to many more than two or three periods, handling of very large spatial extents (city-scale), and whether the discrete one-hot temporal encoding generalizes to irregularly spaced or open-ended time intervals.
