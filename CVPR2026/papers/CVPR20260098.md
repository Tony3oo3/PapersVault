## Problem

Semantic segmentation in unstructured environments (off-road terrain, uneven landscapes, ambiguous boundaries) remains underexplored both in standardized benchmarking and architectural design, unlike structured urban scenes. Prior work on datasets like RELLIS-3D and RUGD is fragmented with inconsistent evaluation protocols, and existing models either lack accuracy or are computationally heavy for resource-constrained deployment in contested or field settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260098/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 512×512 RGB images and produces per-pixel class probability maps (segmentation masks) over 18 classes (RELLIS-3D), 6 coarse classes (RELLIS-3DC), or 22 classes (RUGD). The RMAE encoder outputs four non-contiguous feature maps all at H/16 × W/16 spatial resolution, which the F2P neck rescales into a four-level pyramid spanning H/4 through H/32, preserving a consistent channel dimension C throughout the pipeline.

## Method

RMAE-ProGRess combines a depth-reduced ViT-MAE encoder (RMAE, 8/6/4 layers instead of 12) that extracts features at non-contiguous layer indices, a Feature-to-Pyramid neck that rescales these into a multi-scale pyramid, and a novel ProGRess decoder. The decoder's Progressive Leapwise Fusion (PLF) performs recursive top-down fusion of the non-contiguous feature maps with a self-fusion step at the deepest level, ensuring information from all pyramid levels propagates to the output (formally proven via induction). A Lightweight Channel Attention with Residuals (LCAR) module applies pooling-free 1×1-convolution gating per pixel and channel, with residual connections selectively applied only at the deepest level. Finally, a Bottleneck Feature Fusion (BFF) block upsamples all refined features to H/4 × W/4 and aggregates them via a single 1×1 convolution before a classification head, keeping the decoder modular and parameter-efficient (4.86M params with ViT-B16 vs. 32.27M for UPerNet).

## Results

On RELLIS-3D, RMAE-ProGRess with ViTMAE-Base achieves 57.41% mIoU and 69.21% mAcc, outperforming the strongest baseline Swin-UPerNet (53.86% mIoU) by 3.55 points. On RUGD, the same model reaches 45.63% mIoU and 57.80% mAcc, surpassing SegFormer (43.69% mIoU) by 1.94 points. On the coarse-grained RELLIS-3DC dataset, ProGRess with RMAE-8L achieves 78.95% mIoU, exceeding the previous best GA-Navr8 (74.44%) by 4.51 points. The lightweight RMAE-4L variant (46.47M params, 128.1 GFLOPs) still attains 53.23% mIoU on RELLIS-3D, competitive with much heavier models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On RELLIS-3D, ProGRess (ViTMAE-Base) at 57.41% mIoU outperforms all 16 benchmarked baselines, with the largest margin over Swin-UPerNet (53.86%, +3.55 pts), SegFormer (51.51%, +5.90 pts), and DPT (47.95%, +9.46 pts). On RUGD, ProGRess (ViTMAE-Base) at 45.63% mIoU beats SegFormer (43.69%, +1.94 pts) and Swin-UPerNet (41.76%, +3.87 pts), while CNN-based baselines (DeepLabV3+ at 29.97%, OCRNet at 27.16%) trail by 15+ points. On RELLIS-3DC, ProGRess (RMAE-8L) at 78.95% mIoU surpasses the previous best GA-Navr8 (74.44%) by 4.51 points and FastSCNN (69.27%) by 9.68 points. In zero-shot domain transfer (RELLIS-3D → RUG-REL-Common), ProGRess achieves 21.05% mIoU, edging out Swin-UPerNet (19.51%) and Segmenter (18.57%). The lightweight RMAE-4L variant (46.47M params) matches or exceeds several heavier baselines on RELLIS-3D (e.g., beats Swin-UPerNet by 0.37 pts in mIoU with ~61% fewer parameters), demonstrating strong parameter efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ViT-MAE pretrained weights, departing from prior lightweight-ViT strategies (MobileViT, ViT-Tiny) that reduce embedding dimensions or model width by instead pruning transformer depth (12→8/6/4 layers) while preserving the original channel dimension. The ProGRess decoder draws on FPN-style top-down fusion and SE-Net-inspired channel attention but restructures them for non-contiguous feature maps: PLF performs recursive leapwise fusion rather than standard FPN lateral connections, and LCAR replaces global-pooling-based attention with a pooling-free 1×1 convolution. The paper also positions itself against the limited prior work on RELLIS-3D and RUGD (memory modules, active learning, boundary enhancement) by providing the first comprehensive CNN-and-transformer benchmark on these unstructured-environment datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The comprehensive benchmarking of 16 segmentation architectures on RELLIS-3D, RUGD, and RELLIS-3DC fills a genuine gap in reproducible baselines for off-road segmentation, a domain critical to defense robotics, search-and-rescue, and planetary exploration. The RMAE depth-reduction strategy offers a simple, reusable recipe for lightweight ViT encoders, and the ProGRess decoder's modular design (validated across ResNet, Swin, MiT, and ViT backbones) makes it a practical building block. However, the model remains heavier than real-time alternatives (PIDNet, BiseNetV1, DDRNet), and the zero-shot domain generalization results (21% mIoU) are modest, leaving open whether the architecture's gains persist under stricter compute budgets or more diverse unstructured domains.
