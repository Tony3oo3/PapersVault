## Problem

Existing remote sensing object counting benchmarks assume objects remain visible in at least one modality, leaving untested whether models can infer object counts when clouds, fog, shadows, or low light obscure visual evidence. Although earth observation inherently provides stable geo-modalities (land use, map, DSM) that persist under adverse conditions, no prior dataset or benchmark has been designed to evaluate geo-guided amodal reasoning for counting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262976/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each sample is a 1024×1024 patch at 0.25 m GSD containing four spectral bands (RGB + NIR, with NIR stored as a pseudocolor channel) plus three pixel-aligned geo-modalities: a categorical land-use raster, a rasterized map layout layer, and a single-band digital surface model. The agent consumes these modalities in a purely prompted, zero-shot setting (no fine-tuning on GROC) and produces per-class integer counts for four categories (cars, trucks, boats, airplanes) together with a structured reasoning trace that explains how visual cues, geo-priors, and tool outputs were combined.

## Method

The GROC Agent is a lightweight agentic baseline built on an MLLM backbone (GPT-5) that follows a reasoning–action–observation loop: it first assesses the degradation level from visual signals, then retrieves aligned geo-modalities as stable structural and semantic context, and finally adaptively selects among expert counting tools (BL, PSGCNet, FIDTM, DINO-X) rather than naively aggregating their outputs. A consistency-aware reasoning step suppresses unreliable tool predictions under specific degradation types and refines the final estimate using geospatial priors. The supporting data engine pairs a controllable degradation generator (multi-scale Perlin turbulence for cloud/shadow synthesis, gamma darkening plus signal-dependent noise for low light, applied identically to RGB and NIR) with a DINO-X-assisted interactive annotator that accelerates dense point labeling through detector-in-the-loop refinement, yielding 1.2M human-verified annotations across 14K images.

## Results

On the GROC test set, the proposed GROC Agent achieves the best overall MAE of 3.940 on clear scenes and 14.476 on adverse (combined weather + illumination) scenes, outperforming the strongest specialized counting baseline PSGCNet (4.105 clear / 18.947 adverse) and the best density-based method BL (4.968 clear / 19.322 adverse). Under adverse weather alone, GROC Agent reaches MAE 15.378 vs. FIDTM's 16.464; under adverse illumination, it reaches 13.518 vs. DINO-X's 13.924. The MLLM baseline Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct degrades minimally from clear to adverse (MAE 40.302 → 40.841) but remains far worse than all other methods, indicating that general-purpose multimodal reasoning alone is insufficient for accurate counting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GROC Agent outperforms all 10 baselines across every evaluation condition. On clear scenes, it achieves MAE 3.940, edging out PSGCNet (4.105) and BL (4.968), the two strongest specialized counting models. Under adverse conditions, the gap widens: GROC Agent's MAE of 14.476 beats the next-best BL (19.322) by 4.8 MAE points and PSGCNet (18.947) by 4.5 points. On the weather-only subset, FIDTM is the closest competitor (16.464 vs. 15.378), while on illumination-only, DINO-X is nearest (13.924 vs. 13.518). Detection-based open-vocabulary models (GroundingDINO Edge/Pro, DINO-X) suffer large errors on dense categories like boats and cars, with GroundingDINO Edge reaching MAE 42.992 under adverse conditions. The MLLM baseline Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct is dramatically worse than all other methods (MAE ≈ 40–41 across all conditions), showing that general-purpose multimodal reasoning without task-specific tools cannot compensate for visual degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends remote sensing object counting (RSOC) and multimodal object counting (MMOC) by introducing geo-modalities (land use, map, DSM) as stable structural priors for reasoning about objects that are not visible in any visual channel. Prior RSOC benchmarks (RSOC, NWPU-MOC, CARPK) assume full visibility, and prior MMOC benchmarks (DroneRGBT, RGBT-CC, SARDet-100k) require objects to remain perceptible in at least one modality. The closest prior work, CAPTURE, evaluated amodal counting in generic scenes but lacked geospatial cues entirely. GROC carves new ground by pairing controllable degradation synthesis with aligned geo-modalities and framing the task as 'geo-guided amodal reasoning,' a formulation absent from prior literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GROC provides the first large-scale testbed (1.2M points, 14K images) that explicitly separates perception from reasoning: can a model count objects it cannot see? The finding that all existing counting models, open-vocabulary detectors, and even a general-purpose MLLM degrade substantially (often 3–4× in MAE) under adverse conditions, while geo-modalities offer only partial relief, underscores that current architectures lack genuine amodal reasoning capability. Open questions include whether the synthetic degradations (Perlin-noise clouds, gamma darkening) faithfully capture real atmospheric effects, whether the four mobile-object categories generalize to static infrastructure, and whether the GPT-5-based agent's gains over expert-tool ensembles reflect true geo-reasoning or merely adaptive aggregation. The benchmark and its data engine are likely to become a standard reference for robust RS counting research.
