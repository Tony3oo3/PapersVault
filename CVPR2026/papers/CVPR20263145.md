## Problem

Vision-language models frequently fail at systematic visual reasoning, especially in inductive tasks where they must discover rules distinguishing positive from negative image sets. Existing neuro-symbolic approaches either require explicit task queries (limiting them to query-driven settings) or depend on domain-specific object detectors, preventing generalization across diverse visual domains. This paper addresses both limitations by decoupling VLM perception from symbolic reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263145/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes small sets of labeled RGB image examples (6–20 positive/negative pairs per task) and produces an executable symbolic program in a domain-specific language, not merely a label. At inference time, the synthesized program is executed directly on a new test image: VLM functions are called to extract structured object-property and action representations, which are then processed by symbolic operators to yield a binary prediction. The program itself is the primary output, providing a human-interpretable explanation of the learned visual rule.

## Method

VLP operates in three stages: (1) symbol grounding, where a pretrained VLM is queried to discover task-relevant objects, properties, and actions from the labeled examples; (2) grammar construction, where a Probabilistic Context-Free Grammar is derived from a fixed domain-specific language that composes VLM-based perception functions (e.g., get_objects, get_actions), symbolic reasoning functions (e.g., exists_object, count), and logical operators (AND, OR, NOT, comparisons); (3) program synthesis via heap search over the grammar, ranking candidates by accuracy on training examples with a generative prior weighted by each symbol's discriminative power (frequency × precision in positives). The key architectural insight is that the VLM serves solely as a callable perception function within the program rather than as the reasoning engine, enabling training-free, interpretable, and logically consistent rule induction without domain-specific detectors.

## Results

Across five VLMs and five datasets, VLP yields average balanced-accuracy improvements of +5.5% to +13.5% over direct prompting, with the largest gains on compositionally complex CLEVR-Hans3 (up to +40.0% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B, from 46.1% to 86.1%). On COCOLogic, VLP-enhanced models reach 70.1–81.0% balanced accuracy versus 73.2–78.4% for the base VLMs. In the reasoning-model comparison (Table 2), VLP with non-thinking models matches or exceeds dedicated thinking models (e.g., Kimi w/ VLP: 83.3% vs. 30.0% on CLEVR-Hans3; GPT-5 w/ VLP: 81.8% vs. 78.5% on COCOLogic) while using 2–7× fewer tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLP consistently outperforms all five base VLMs on at least four of five datasets. Against InternVL3-8B/14B (Chen et al., CVPR 2024), VLP gains +13.5% and +6.8% average accuracy respectively, with the largest single-dataset jump being +26.1% on CLEVR-Hans3 for the 8B model. Against Kimi-VL-A3B-Instruct (Kimi Team, 2025), VLP improves average accuracy by +7.0%, and in the dedicated reasoning comparison (Table 2) Kimi w/ VLP (83.3% CLEVR-Hans3, 68.3% COCOLogic) substantially surpasses Kimi w/ Think (30.0%, 52.2%) while using ~2.4× fewer tokens. Against Qwen2.5-VL-7B (Qwen Team, 2025), VLP yields +9.4% average, including a +40.0% gain on CLEVR-Hans3 (46.1%→86.1%). Against Qwen3-VL-30B (Qwen Team, 2025), VLP gains +5.5% average but slightly underperforms on Bongard-OpenWorld (66.3% vs. 68.5%) due to annotation errors in that dataset. Against GPT-5 (OpenAI, 2025), VLP with low-reasoning effort matches or exceeds GPT-5 Thinking on both logical-reasoning benchmarks (81.8% vs. 78.5% COCOLogic; 68.3% vs. 65.0% CLEVR-Hans3) with 6–12× fewer tokens, and combining Thinking+VLP further pushes COCOLogic to 84.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLP extends the neuro-symbolic program synthesis line (Wüst et al. 2024, Pix2Code) by replacing domain-specific object detectors with VLMs as the perceptual interface, enabling application to natural images without task-specific pretraining. It also departs from query-driven visual programming frameworks (VisProg, ViperGPT, CodeVQA) by focusing on inductive rule discovery from labeled examples rather than generating programs conditioned on natural-language questions. The probabilistic context-free grammar derived from a fixed DSL, combined with heap search over the program space, draws on classical program synthesis (DreamCoder, Gulwani et al.) but applies it to a compositional vision-language setting where VLM functions serve as callable perceptual primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, training-free path to interpretable visual reasoning: the induced programs are executable, inspectable, and editable, enabling targeted shortcut mitigation and human-in-the-loop debugging that opaque VLMs cannot support. Key limitations include the absence of explicit spatial/object representations (restricting applications to spatial reasoning), reliance on VLM perception quality (formatting errors and omitted properties cap performance), and the Bongard-OpenWorld failure mode where logically consistent programs overfit to annotation noise. Open questions include automated DSL expansion, whether the approach scales to richer spatial and relational reasoning, and how to handle the perception bottleneck without substantially increasing prompt overhead.
