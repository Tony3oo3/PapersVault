## Problem

Existing virtual try-on and pose-driven human animation are handled as separate two-stage pipelines, causing identity drift, garment distortion, and front-back inconsistency when the stages are chained. No public dataset provides the required triplet structure—a human image in a *different* outfit, target garment images, and a video of the same person wearing the target garment—making unified single-stage training infeasible with current data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260182/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single human image showing the person in an arbitrary outfit (G′ ≠ G), one or more target garment images (upper, lower, accessories), a pose guidance video (2D keypoint sequence from DWPose), and a text prompt describing actions and background. It outputs an F-frame temporally coherent video of the same person wearing the target garment(s) while following the pose trajectory. The human image must depict a *different* garment than the target; using a frame from the target video as the human image causes the model to learn motion reenactment rather than garment transfer.

## Method

Vanast builds on the frozen Wan T2V DiT backbone and introduces a Dual Module architecture: a Human Animation Module (HAM) that encodes the human image and pose keypoint latents via frame-wise temporal concatenation, and a Garment Transfer Module (GTM) that encodes garment latents with zero-padding for temporal alignment. At every other transformer block, the HAM and GTM outputs are added to the backbone with fixed weights (α=β=0.5), enabling progressive multi-level conditioning while preserving pretrained generative quality. Training uses a large-scale synthetic triplet dataset constructed by (1) FLUX inpainting to generate identity-preserving human images in alternative outfits, (2) VLM-guided frame selection and LLM-based garment prompting for diversity, and (3) automatic garment extraction from in-the-wild videos to eliminate the need for catalog images. The modular GTM design further enables zero-shot garment interpolation by computing a γ-weighted sum of GTM block outputs for two input garments.

## Results

On the Internet dataset, Vanast achieves L1=0.0719, PSNR=17.95, LPIPS=0.2370, FID=91.05, and VFID_ResNeXt=0.39, outperforming the best two-stage VTON+animation baseline (Any2AnyTryon+DisPose: L1=0.0950, PSNR=15.53, LPIPS=0.3160, FID=116.39, VFID_ResNeXt=0.78) across all metrics except SSIM (0.7550 vs. 0.7778). On the ViViD dataset, Vanast achieves L1=0.1077, PSNR=14.67, LPIPS=0.3649, FID=105.89, and VFID_ResNeXt=1.30, substantially surpassing the best baseline (OmniTry+DisPose: L1=0.2027, PSNR=10.75, LPIPS=0.5867, FID=149.84, VFID_ResNeXt=8.45). The single-stage unified framework also outperforms VACE in both 1-stage and 2-stage configurations on all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vanast outperforms all four image VTON + animation baselines (OOTDiffusion, CatVTON, OmniTry, Any2AnyTryon, each paired with DisPose) on L1, PSNR, LPIPS, FID, VFID_I3D, and VFID_ResNeXt on both the Internet and ViViD datasets, with the largest margins on perceptual metrics (e.g., FID 91.05 vs. 111.08–172.94 on Internet; VFID_ResNeXt 0.39 vs. 0.78–1.68). The only metric where Vanast trails is SSIM on the Internet dataset (0.7550 vs. 0.7779 for CatVTON+DisPose), which the authors note is comparable. Against subject-to-image + animation baselines (VisualCloze, MOSAIC, UNO), Vanast achieves substantially lower L1 (0.0719 vs. 0.1714–0.2590) and FID (91.05 vs. 141.89–210.37) on the Internet dataset. VACE, evaluated in both 1-stage and 2-stage settings, is outperformed by Vanast on all metrics (e.g., L1 0.0719 vs. 0.1453 for VACE 1-stage; FID 91.05 vs. 115.40). The gap is particularly pronounced on the ViViD dataset, where Vanast's L1 (0.1077) is roughly half that of the best VTON+animation baseline (0.2027), indicating the unified single-stage approach avoids the compounding errors of two-stage pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—image-based virtual try-on (OOTDiffusion, CatVTON, OmniTry, Any2AnyTryon) and pose-driven human image animation (StableAnimator, DisPose, Animate Anyone)—into a single video diffusion transformer framework. Architecturally, it builds on the Wan video DiT backbone and draws design inspiration from VACE's auxiliary-module approach, but departs by splitting conditioning into two specialized modules (HAM for pose/identity, GTM for garment) rather than a single fused context module. The synthetic triplet data pipeline repurposes FLUX inpainting, Qwen2.5-VL, and SegFormer to construct training pairs that no public dataset provides, addressing a fundamental data gap that prior video VTON work (ViViD, Fw-GAN) sidestepped by assuming the person already wears the target garment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the two-stage decomposition, Vanast removes a major source of compounding artifacts (identity drift, garment distortion) that has limited practical deployment of virtual try-on in video. The Dual Module architecture is a reusable pattern for multi-condition video generation where a single context module fails to balance competing signals. Open questions include whether the approach extends to full-body 3D garments, longer sequences beyond 10 seconds, and whether the synthetic triplet pipeline generalizes to accessories and multi-person scenes. The reliance on a pose video as input also constrains real-world applicability where only text or audio guidance is available.
