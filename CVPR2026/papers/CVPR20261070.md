## Problem

Large vision-language models frequently hallucinate by overrelying on linguistic priors rather than visual evidence, yet no existing method provides a quantitative measure of how much a model actually depends on visual inputs during reasoning. Current expert-coordination frameworks either invoke tools based on the LLM's own textual confidence (inheriting its biases) or require costly joint optimization across experts, leaving no principled, measurable criterion for deciding when and which visual expert to call.

## Input / Output

The framework takes an RGB image and a free-form text question as input. The question is first decomposed by a 70B LLM into a set of concrete visual queries (e.g., object names, attributes), which are then grounded spatially via a CLIP-based segmentation model. The output is a refined text answer accompanied by a scalar utilization score U_q that quantifies the model's dependence on question-relevant visual regions. Expert outputs (bounding boxes, segmentation masks, depth maps) are not passed as structured data but are rendered as visual overlays (gray, blur, or highlight) directly onto the image so the LVLM can consume them through its existing vision encoder without architectural changes.

## Method

DnR introduces a question-conditioned utilization metric computed via relevance-based probabilistic masking: a query-conditioned relevance map localizes question-specific evidence, Gumbel-k sampling generates stochastic Top-k (relevant) and Bottom-k (irrelevant) masks, and semantic deviations between original and masked predictions—measured by a SentenceTransformer encoder—are aggregated with an adaptive weight α derived from the relevance map's entropy and contrast. This baseline utilization U_q^base is then compared against utilization scores recomputed after each of K visual experts (GroundingDINO, SAM, DepthAnything, mDETR) renders its output onto the image; the expert yielding the largest positive gain in U_q is selected, and if no expert improves upon the baseline the draft is kept unchanged. A lightweight three-layer MLP selector is subsequently trained to predict the optimal expert directly from the state (image, query set, draft, relevance map), reducing the exhaustive K-expert evaluation to a single forward pass with ~70% cost reduction and negligible accuracy loss.

## Results

DnR improves accuracy across all 7 LVLM backbones on 16 multimodal benchmarks, with gains ranging from +0.3 to +13.4 points depending on the model's baseline visual grounding. Weaker-grounded models benefit most: IDEFICS gains +10.05 on VQAv2 (37.8→47.85) and +13.4 on GQA (12.1→25.5), while InstructBLIP improves +8.37 on GQA (31.4→39.77). Stronger models show smaller but consistent gains, e.g., Qwen2.5-VL +1.5 on VQAv2 (83.95→85.45) and LLaVA 1.6 +2.7 on GQA (61.5→64.2). On hallucination benchmarks, DnR reduces hallucination rates by 8–35% (e.g., InstructBLIP MMHal-Bench H: 35.42→28.12; LLaVA 1.6 MMHal-Bench H: 8.33→4.17) while increasing grounded responses by up to +6.23 pp (Qwen2.5-VL on COCO Caption).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare DnR against competing refinement or expert-coordination methods; instead, the baseline is each LVLM's own unrefined 'Draft' prediction, making this a self-improvement study. DnR wins on the majority of benchmark–model pairs: across 7 models and 16 benchmarks, improvements appear in most cells, with the largest gains concentrated in weaker-grounded models (IDEFICS, MiniGPT-v2, InstructBLIP). Notable exceptions include CogVLM on VCR (67.7→60.5, a −7.2 drop) and Qwen2.5-VL on VCR (57.3→55.9, −1.4), where the rendering strategy appears to suppress fine-grained spatial cues. The learned policy-driven selector (Table 3) matches exhaustive expert selection within 0.07–20.35 points (depending on benchmark) while reducing computational cost by ~70% on average, confirming that the utilization-guided selection generalizes without exhaustive search. Hallucination results (Table 2) show DnR consistently reduces the Hallucination category and increases Grounded/Correct categories across all 7 models on 4 benchmarks, with the strongest proportional reduction in LLaVA 1.6 (−29.1% on MMHal-Bench) and Qwen2.5-VL (−35.0% on MMHal-Bench).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of language-driven expert invocation in agentic LVLMs (HuggingGPT, MM-React, Toolformer), where tool selection relies on chain-of-thought confidence or textual heuristics, by introducing a quantitative visual utilization metric (U_q) computed via relevance-based perturbation as the decision criterion. It also distinguishes itself from hallucination-mitigation approaches that apply global feature amplification or post-hoc corrections by conditioning on query-specific relevance maps, enabling selective rather than uniform refinement. The rendering mechanism that converts structured expert outputs (boxes, masks, depth) into visual cues processable by an unmodified LVLM vision encoder repurposes the perturbation-analysis literature (Gumbel-k sampling, semantic deviation) in a novel agent-coordination context without requiring joint training or architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DnR provides a principled, training-free criterion for deciding when and which visual expert to invoke, addressing a gap where prior agentic systems lacked measurable justification for tool calls. The consistent but modest gains across 16 benchmarks and 7 LVLM backbones, combined with 8–29% hallucination reduction, suggest the framework is most valuable as a stabilizing layer for weaker or less visually grounded models rather than a transformative improvement for state-of-the-art systems. Key limitations include dataset- and model-specific tuning of rendering parameters, negligible benefit on knowledge-intensive VQA where missing facts cannot be recovered by visual cues, and the computational overhead of multiple forward passes (partially mitigated by the learned selector achieving ~70% cost reduction). Open questions include whether adaptive normalization of U_q across domains can reduce manual tuning, and whether the utilization metric generalizes to video or multi-image reasoning settings.
