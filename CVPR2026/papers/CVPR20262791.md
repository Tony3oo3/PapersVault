## Problem

Existing world models for video generation and scene understanding operate on global prompts (text, actions, scene embeddings) and lack a mechanism for flexible conditional inference over local scene variables—e.g., how motion at one location causally influences motion elsewhere, or how forces propagate through an object's structure. This limits their ability to support physically grounded queries such as isolating movable objects, discovering articulated subparts, or inferring support relationships between objects in a scene.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a single RGB frame (or short video clip) tokenized via shallow convolutional VQ quantizers into spatial patch tokens, optionally augmented with a point prompt, a sparse flow poke vector, a 6DOF camera/object transform, or a camera-stop token. Outputs are produced by querying the model at arbitrary spatiotemporal locations in either sequential mode (each prediction conditions on all previously generated patches, capturing causal dependencies) or parallel mode (independent evaluations for speed), yielding motion probability heatmaps, expected displacement fields, diverse future flow/RGB completions, segmentation masks, and pairwise physical-relationship graphs.

## Method

PhyWM formulates the visual world as a probabilistic graphical model over local spatiotemporal variables, then realizes it as a GPT-style autoregressive sequence model by serializing the world state as interleaved pointer tokens (encoding spatiotemporal location) and content tokens (encoding RGB appearance, optical flow, or camera motion). The key insight is that training a next-token prediction objective across arbitrary spatiotemporal orderings with random flow masking (0–1 ratio) amortizes learning of the full joint distribution, so the model can answer any conditional query by simply appending a pointer token and reading off the predicted content distribution. The 7B-parameter transformer is trained on ~1.4 trillion tokens from 3 million real-world video clips with cross-entropy loss on content tokens only. At inference, flow tokens serve dually as prediction targets (generating plausible dynamics from sparse pokes) or conditioning signals (rendering future appearance given dynamics), and motion-correlation statistics computed over multiple sequential flow samples enable zero-shot object and articulated-part segmentation without any task-specific architecture.

## Results

On SpelkeBench point-prompted movable object segmentation, PhyWM achieves state-of-the-art mIoU of 0.681 and AR of 0.541, outperforming the strongest baselines SAM2 (mIoU 0.623, AR 0.482) and FPT (mIoU 0.566, AR 0.368). On DragAMove articulated part segmentation, PhyWM reaches mIoU 0.410, substantially exceeding FPT (0.287) and MotionI2V (0.073). On 3DEditBench 3D object manipulation, PhyWM with its own segments achieves the best EA of 0.776 and lowest LPIPS of 0.161, while also improving EA by 0.143 over SAM2 segments when used with the same editing pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhyWM outperforms all 10 baselines on SpelkeBench point-prompted segmentation, with the largest margins over appearance-based methods (SAM2: +0.058 mIoU; CutLER: +0.258 mIoU) and motion-based world models (FPT: +0.115 mIoU; CWM: +0.200 mIoU). On unprompted segmentation (Table 1b), PhyWM achieves the best F1-score (0.38) by balancing precision and recall, whereas SAM2 over-segments (high AR 0.62 but low AP 0.11, F1 0.17) and ProMerge under-segments (high AP 0.42 but low AR 0.34). For articulated part discovery on DragAMove, PhyWM (mIoU 0.410) more than doubles FPT (0.287) and is 5.6× better than MotionI2V (0.073). In 3D manipulation on 3DEditBench, PhyWM's own segments consistently improve EA over SAM2 segments across all three editing backends (PasC: 0.679 vs 0.536; DH: 0.576 vs 0.495; DasS: 0.640 vs 0.503), and PhyWM-as-editor achieves the best overall EA (0.776) and LPIPS (0.161). The paper notes that SAM2 tends to split objects by texture while CutLER/ProMerge merge same-category instances, whereas PhyWM's motion-correlation segments align with physically coherent units.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhyWM repurposes the GPT-style autoregressive sequence modeling paradigm for visual world modeling by introducing a pointer-content tokenization of spatiotemporal visual data, effectively implementing a Probabilistic Graphical Model over local scene variables. It departs from classical action-conditioned world models (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018) by treating optical flow and camera tokens as cheap proxies for agent actions, and from specialized object-understanding systems (SAM2, CutLER, DragAMove methods) by unifying segmentation, articulated-part discovery, 3D manipulation, and physical-relationship reasoning under a single probabilistic architecture. The pointer-content serialization is closely related to the authors' prior work on local random-access sequence modeling (Lee et al., 2025), but here it is elevated to a full PGM interpretation that enables arbitrary conditional queries over visual variables.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single 7B autoregressive model, trained purely on next-token prediction over video, can zero-shot perform a wide range of physical scene-understanding tasks without task-specific heads or architectures, which is a practical and conceptually clean unification. The 'poor man's world model' framing—using flow and camera tokens as action surrogates—makes physically grounded reasoning accessible without expensive action-labeled data. Open questions include whether the PGM interpretation captures genuine causal structure or merely correlational patterns, how the approach scales to larger scenes or longer temporal horizons, and whether the heuristic physical-relationship scoring (motion influence graphs) generalizes beyond simple stacking configurations. The authors note potential extensions to domains like medical imaging and materials science where human intuition about objecthood is weaker.
