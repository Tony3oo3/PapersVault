## Problem

Existing VLN agents suffer from two coupled deficits: limited long-horizon planning that causes them to lose track of complex instructions in unseen environments, and a fundamentally reactive nature that prevents them from anticipating future environmental states. Prior work has addressed these on separate fronts—VLM-based planners and world-model predictors—but the disconnection between them means plans can hallucinate away from visual reality while predictions drift from the instructional goal. NavForesee unifies both capabilities in a single VLM so that planning guides prediction and imagined futures inform action.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261834/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language instruction, a sequence of H past panoramic RGB frames (90° FOV on R2R-CE, 79° on RxR-CE), and its own pose-orientation history. It outputs K=5 continuous waypoints (x, y, sin θ, cos θ, stop-flag) per step, a hierarchical text plan (completed sub-instruction, current sub-instruction, next sub-instruction), and predicted depth maps plus DINOv2/SAM semantic features at both a fixed short-term horizon (k steps) and an adaptive long-term horizon (M_t steps to the next milestone). The long-term horizon is not preset but scales with the agent's remaining progress toward the current sub-goal.

## Method

NavForesee extends Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct with learnable dream queries (short-term Q_S and long-term Q_L, each split into depth and semantic sub-queries) and an action query Q_a, all processed through a structured attention mask that enforces causal ordering (long-term conditioned on short-term), blocks cross-modal leakage between depth and semantics, and lets the action query attend to all context. A position encoder injects the agent's relative pose into every frame embedding, and lightweight convolutional decoders map the dream embeddings to predicted depth (trained with SiLogLoss) and DINOv2/SAM features (trained with MSE), while an MLP head performs inverse-dynamics action prediction conditioned on both predictive latents. Hierarchical planning is trained autoregressively on ~1.5M samples generated by Gemini 2.5 Pro from R2R-CE/RxR-CE episodes, which decompose instructions into milestone-anchored sub-instructions with waypoint-level reasoning labels. The two objectives are interleaved in a single training loop so that planning-aware hidden states implicitly encode sub-goal intent that the world model leverages for adaptive long-horizon prediction, closing a perception–planning/prediction–action loop.

## Results

On R2R-CE Val-Unseen, NavForesee achieves a Success Rate (SR) of 66.2%, Oracle Success Rate (OSR) of 78.4%, Navigation Error (NE) of 3.94 m, and SPL of 59.7, setting new SOTA on SR, OSR, and NE. On RxR-CE Val-Unseen, it reaches SR 66.3%, NE 4.20 m, and SPL 53.2, remaining competitive but slightly behind the best method (CorrectNav). The ablation study confirms that removing hierarchical VLM planning drops SR from 66.2% to 48.8%, and removing long-term prediction drops SR to 58.6%, validating the synergy of all three modules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On R2R-CE Val-Unseen, NavForesee outperforms the strongest baseline CorrectNav [48] on NE (3.94 vs. 4.24 m), OSR (78.4 vs. 67.5), and SR (66.2 vs. 65.1), but trails on SPL (59.7 vs. 62.3), suggesting slightly less path-efficient trajectories. Against HNR* [43], the prior SOTA world-model-based method, NavForesee improves SR by 5.2 points (66.2 vs. 61.0) and reduces NE by 0.48 m. On RxR-CE Val-Unseen, NavForesee is outperformed by CorrectNav on all three reported metrics (NE 4.20 vs. 4.09, SR 66.3 vs. 69.3, SPL 53.2 vs. 63.3), which the authors attribute to CorrectNav's use of additional large-scale training data beyond R2R-CE/RxR-CE. Despite this, NavForesee still achieves the highest OSR across both datasets, indicating that its world-model foresight helps the agent reach the correct goal region even when the final path is suboptimal. Compared to video-generation-based methods (NA Vid [50], Uni-NA Vid [49]), NavForesee substantially outperforms on R2R-CE (SR 66.2 vs. 37.0/47.0) while using only panoramic RGB input without depth or odometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

NavForesee carves new ground by unifying two previously separate research threads—VLM-based hierarchical planning (as in dual-system 'fast-and-slow' architectures like StreamVLN and CorrectNav) and latent-space world model prediction (as in NavMorph, HNR, and DreamVLA)—into a single Qwen2.5-VL backbone. Rather than treating the planner and predictor as distinct modules with separate training, the paper interleaves their objectives and uses structured attention masks to let long-horizon predictions condition on short-horizon ones while preventing cross-modal leakage. The hierarchical planning dataset is generated by prompting Gemini 2.5 Pro over R2R-CE/RxR-CE episodes, and the predicted features (depth, DINOv2, SAM) follow the compact-representation philosophy of DreamVLA rather than pixel-level video generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that a 3B VLM trained exclusively on public R2R-CE/RxR-CE data can match or exceed methods that leverage large-scale external datasets, suggesting that the planning-prediction feedback loop itself is a strong inductive bias for long-horizon navigation. However, the 1.1% SR gain over SOTA on R2R-CE coexists with a 10.9% OSR gain, hinting that the agent reaches the vicinity of goals more reliably but still struggles with final-approach precision; on RxR-CE it trails the best methods, indicating limited generalization to more complex, multilingual trajectories. Open questions include whether the dual-horizon prediction mechanism scales to truly open-world settings beyond Matterport3D, whether the coarse depth predictions (acknowledged by the authors) become a bottleneck in cluttered or outdoor scenes, and whether the Gemini-generated planning labels introduce systematic biases that cap performance.
