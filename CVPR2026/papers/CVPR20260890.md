## Problem

Earth observation foundation models show strong research performance but adoption in non-profit and humanitarian settings lags due to training complexity and deployment cost. When replicating existing latent masked image modeling approaches for satellite data, the authors encountered training instability and representation collapse, indicating that standard latent MIM formulations are unreliable in this domain. The paper addresses both gaps by proposing a stable training recipe and deploying the resulting models in an open platform for partner organizations.

## Input / Output

The model ingests multimodal satellite image time series (Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, Landsat-8) and six derived maps (WorldCereal, WorldCover, SRTM, Canopy Height Map, OpenStreetMap, Cropland Data Layer), all resampled to 10 m/pixel over 2.56 km × 2.56 km tiles with up to 12 monthly timesteps per year. A FlexiViT-style projection converts pixels to tokens at a variable patch size (1–8), producing one token per patch × timestep × bandset, with 2D sincos positional, sinusoidal temporal, and learnable modality embeddings added. During pretraining the decoder predicts frozen random-projection latents for masked tokens; downstream, the encoder outputs token embeddings that feed linear probes, kNN classifiers, or task-specific decoders for classification, segmentation, and object detection.

## Method

OlmoEarth introduces Latent MIM Lite, which replaces the learned target encoder of standard Latent MIM with a frozen, randomly initialized copy of the embedding layer, eliminating representation collapse while preserving the representational power of latent-space prediction. A modality-aware masking strategy assigns each bandset to one of four roles (not selected, encode-only, decode-only, encode-and-decode), reframing the task from reconstructing masked patches to reconstructing missing bandsets from partial views of others; derived maps are always decode-only so the encoder never processes them. The loss combines a modality-aware patch discrimination term (cosine-similarity contrastive loss restricted to same-modality targets to avoid trivial cross-modality negatives) with a SimCLR-style instance contrastive loss that pools encoder tokens from two independently masked views of the same input, encouraging a shared representation space across modalities. The architecture is a ViT encoder (4–24 layers, 1.4M–300M parameters) with a shallow 4-layer cross-attention decoder, trained with AdamW, random patch sizes, random spatial crops, and ~100 billion tokens over 667k steps.

## Results

OlmoEarth ViT Base achieves the best performance on 15 of 24 kNN/linear-probing (embedding) tasks and 19 of 29 full fine-tuning tasks, compared against 12 other Earth observation foundation models. On key research benchmarks it leads on m-so2sat (93.3% Acc), m-SA-crop-type (70.8% μF1), and m-bigearthnet μF1 (67.7%), while Panopticon narrowly edges it on m-eurosat (95.2% vs. 94.7% Acc) and m-bigearthnet Acc (64.9% vs. 62.4%). The model family shows consistent Nano→Tiny→Base scaling, but the Base→Large transition exhibits tradeoffs (e.g., OlmoEarth Large improves m-eurosat to 96.3% but drops m-SA-crop-type from 70.8% to 56.6%), a pattern the authors note is shared across all competing model families.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OlmoEarth Base wins on m-so2sat (93.3% vs. Panopticon 92.9%, CROMA 92.0%), m-SA-crop-type (70.8% vs. Galileo 60.9%, TerraMind 57.0%), and m-bigearthnet μF1 (67.7% vs. Galileo 55.7%, the next best), but loses to Panopticon on m-eurosat (94.7% vs. 95.2%) and m-bigearthnet Acc (62.4% vs. 64.9%), and to Galileo on PASTIS (73.4% vs. 81.9%) and m-cashewplant (64.0% vs. 69.7%). DINOv3 Sat, while strong on purely visual tasks, drops sharply on multi-temporal benchmarks (e.g., 31.3% on m-cashewplant) and lacks radar support. TerraMind and CROMA are competitive on fine-tuning tasks but trail OlmoEarth on embedding-based evaluations, particularly on multi-modal (S1+S2) partner tasks. Galileo shows the strongest agriculture-specific performance (m-cashewplant 69.7%, PASTIS 81.9%) but underperforms on broader land-cover and crop-type tasks. Overall, OlmoEarth's modality-aware masking and latent MIM Lite training give it the most consistent cross-task performance, winning the majority of both embedding and fine-tuning evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

OlmoEarth sits at the intersection of masked autoencoding (MAE) and latent-space prediction (I-JEPA, Latent MIM), adopting the encoder-decoder architecture of MAE and the latent-space reconstruction objective of I-JEPA while replacing the learned target encoder with a frozen, randomly initialized linear projection (Latent MIM Lite). The flexible patch embedding follows FlexiViT and Galileo, and the instance-level contrastive loss draws on SimCLR-style batch contrast. The key conceptual departure is that maps (WorldCover, SRTM, etc.) are used exclusively as decode targets rather than encoder inputs, a design choice shared with AlphaEarth but motivated here by the need to avoid the encoder learning to shortcut predictions from supervised maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's most practically significant contribution is the OlmoEarth Platform, which packages frontier Earth observation models into an end-to-end tool for nonprofits and NGOs working on conservation, food security, and ecosystem mapping—addressing a real adoption gap in the humanitarian sector. A notable open question is the Base→Large scaling bottleneck observed across all model families (OlmoEarth, CROMA, TerraMind, DINOv3), suggesting fundamental challenges in scaling Earth observation transformers that remain unresolved. The finding that encoding maps through the online encoder degrades performance (vs. decode-only) is a useful negative result that may inform future multimodal pretraining designs beyond Earth observation.
