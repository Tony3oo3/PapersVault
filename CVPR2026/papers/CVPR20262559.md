## Problem

Photorealistic relighting of dynamic full-body humans from sparse-view images remains unsolved: prior methods either require prohibitively expensive OLAT captures limited to static scenes, focus on rigid body parts like faces and hands, or rely on oversimplified BRDF models that produce implausible decompositions. No existing approach can take a handful of RGB views of a moving person and produce relightable, view-consistent renderings of unseen motions in a single feed-forward pass.

## Input / Output

At inference the method consumes only four sparse RGB views captured under neutral illumination, a skeletal motion sequence, a target HDR environment map, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It produces photorealistic relit renderings from arbitrary novel viewpoints, internally represented as per-texel 3D Gaussian splats (position, scale, rotation, opacity, color) attached to a UV-parameterized coarse mesh that are posed into world space and rasterized. The model is person-specific, trained per identity on a multi-view lightstage dataset, and generalizes to unseen motions, viewpoints, and lighting conditions at test time.

## Method

The pipeline first deforms a person-specific template mesh via a DDC-style Character Animation Module (embedded-graph deformation plus vertex refinement driven by skeletal pose), yielding a temporally consistent mesh with stable UV parameterization. Physics-informed features—coarse normal stacks, high-frequency image normals unprojected to UV space, a position map, an AlbedoNet-refined albedo, preintegrated diffuse shading, and a per-texel camera view encoding—are extracted in this UV space. RelightNet, a 2D convolutional network with self-attention in UV space and multi-head cross-attention against linearly projected environment-map tokens (with sinusoidal positional encoding), then regresses per-texel 3D Gaussian parameters in a single feed-forward pass. The cross-attention mechanism is directly inspired by the rendering equation: each UV texel queries the environment map to aggregate directional light contributions, implicitly learning the full light transport integral without explicit BRDF evaluation. Training is supervised on a novel lightstage capture scheme that interleaves frames lit by random HDR environment maps with uniformly lit tracking frames, providing dense geometry supervision alongside diverse illumination and dynamics coverage.

## Results

On the RHC Multi-view Lightstage Dataset (5 subjects, 3 test views, 8 novel HDR environment maps), RHC achieves an average PSNR of 31.49, LPIPS of 6.70, and SSIM of 89.56 across all subjects, outperforming all baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, HPC+NG (sparse-view rendering + per-identity Neural Gaffer relighting), trails by ~0.9 PSNR and ~1.5 LPIPS on average. RHC also surpasses the strongest relighting baselines (R4D+GT Env, MA+GT Env) by 1.5–2.5 PSNR, demonstrating that end-to-end learning from natural lightstage captures with cross-attention on environment maps yields superior photorealistic relighting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RHC outperforms all five baselines across every subject and metric. Against the strongest relighting baseline, R4D+GT Env (trained with ground-truth environment maps), RHC gains 1.5–2.5 PSNR and reduces LPIPS by 2–4 points, showing that explicit BRDF-based inverse rendering cannot match end-to-end learned relighting. IntrinsicAvatar+GT Env (IA) is the weakest relighting baseline, lagging RHC by 3–5 PSNR, largely due to SMPL-based tracking errors and simplified BRDF assumptions. MeshAvatar+GT Env (MA) improves over IA with a hybrid mesh+implicit representation but still trails RHC by ~1.5–2.5 PSNR, as its pose-only input cannot capture input-view-consistent wrinkles. The closest competitor is HPC+NG, which combines sparse-view photorealistic rendering with per-identity Neural Gaffer diffusion relighting; RHC beats it by ~0.5–2.5 PSNR and ~1–3 LPIPS, with the largest gaps on SSIM (up to 8 points on S2), indicating that image-space diffusion relighting introduces view-inconsistent shading that the 3D Gaussian-based RHC avoids.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Deep Dynamic Characters (DDC) for mesh-based character animation and on Holoported Characters (HPC) for sparse-view UV-space feature extraction and albedo refinement. It departs from the dominant inverse-rendering paradigm (R4D, IntrinsicAvatar, MeshAvatar) that explicitly decomposes albedo and BRDF from uncalibrated lighting, and from OLAT-based capture (Relightables, Relightable Gaussian Codec Avatars) that requires static scenes and linear composition. The key conceptual shift is replacing explicit material/lighting decomposition with a single feed-forward cross-attention between physics-informed UV features and an HDR environment map, implicitly learning the rendering equation integration rather than evaluating it analytically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RHC is the first method to achieve photorealistic relighting of unseen full-body dynamic performances from only four sparse RGB views in a single inference pass, making scalable telepresence and virtual avatar insertion practically feasible without expensive multi-light capture rigs. The finding that natural environment-map capture dramatically outperforms OLAT (5.6 dB PSNR gap) challenges the long-standing assumption that OLAT is the gold standard for relighting data, potentially reshaping how future lightstage datasets are designed. Key open questions include cross-identity generalization (currently per-subject), real-time performance (~2 FPS), and handling topology changes, translucent materials, and accessories.
