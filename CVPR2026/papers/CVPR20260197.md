## Problem

Gaussian Splatting reconstruction assumes multi-view photometric consistency, which breaks down in dark scenes where a camera-mounted active light source moves with the camera, causing severe, spatially localized shading and shadow changes across views. Existing appearance-variation methods (WildGaussians, GS-W, VAST) model only global tone shifts and fail on these drastic local changes, while DarkGS handles moving lights but requires pre-calibrated light-source parameters that must be redone for every hardware setup.

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGB images captured in a dark environment with a co-moving active illuminant (e.g., robot-mounted LED or phone flash), along with COLMAP-estimated camera poses and intrinsics. It produces a 2D Gaussian Splatting scene representation whose per-surfel color is a view-independent albedo (replacing spherical harmonics), plus a learned lighting MLP and virtual light position that together enable novel-view rendering, unlit albedo extraction, and per-view depth/normal maps. No explicit light-source calibration is required; the light geometry is learned end-to-end.

## Method

SunFaded replaces the spherical-harmonic color attributes of 2D Gaussian surfels with view-independent albedo features and trains the representation in three stages. Stage 1 optimizes the Gaussians under an illumination-weighted photometric loss (inspired by Retinex) that down-weights bright, light-dominated pixels via a large-kernel Gaussian filter in log-space, driving the scene toward its unlit appearance while using Depth-Pro priors for geometric supervision. Stage 2 introduces a Lighting Module: an MLP conditioned on per-tile (16×16 px) light direction and distance that predicts RGB intensity, which is then applied as a simplified diffuse shading term directly on the rendered albedo image rather than modifying individual Gaussian colors, explicitly disentangling illumination from intrinsic appearance at low computational cost. Stage 3 refines geometry by computing depth and normal pseudo-labels from the recovered illumination-invariant albedo (avoiding lighting-induced artifacts in the prior), freezing all Gaussian attributes except opacity initially, then jointly fine-tuning appearance and geometry.

## Results

On the DarkRobotic dataset (4 scenes), SunFaded achieves an average PSNR of 41.02, SSIM of 0.9739, and LPIPS of 0.0286, outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. The closest competitor, GS-W, reaches only 35.62 PSNR and 0.1577 LPIPS, while the most directly comparable active-lighting method DarkGS achieves 35.50 PSNR and 0.0778 LPIPS. On the self-captured DarkPhone dataset (4 scenes), SunFaded again leads with PSNRs of 36.73–41.01, whereas DarkGS fails to produce reasonable reconstructions due to its reliance on pre-calibrated lighting. In terms of efficiency, SunFaded trains in ~22 minutes and renders at 193 FPS, the fastest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SunFaded outperforms all six baselines on every scene and every metric in both datasets. Against the standard Gaussian Splatting baselines (2DGS, 3DGS), the gains are substantial: 41.02 vs. 30.30/28.91 average PSNR, confirming that photometric-consistency assumptions break down under moving illumination. Among in-the-wild appearance methods, GS-W (35.62 PSNR) and VAST (34.15 PSNR) are the strongest but still trail SunFaded by 5.4 and 6.9 dB respectively; VAST also shows highly inconsistent SSIM (0.7299 average), indicating unstable reconstructions. WildGaussians performs worst overall (25.14 PSNR), suggesting its per-Gaussian appearance embeddings overfit to the severe local lighting changes. The most relevant comparison is DarkGS, which also targets dark scenes with moving lights but requires pre-calibrated light-source parameters; SunFaded surpasses it by 5.5 dB in PSNR and 0.049 in LPIPS on DarkRobotic, and DarkGS fails entirely on the DarkPhone dataset where the hardware setup differs from its calibration. Even without the geometry-prior stage (Ours*), SunFaded already exceeds all baselines, indicating that the illumination-weighted loss and tile-based lighting module are the primary drivers of the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) as its geometric backbone, replacing spherical harmonic color coefficients with view-independent albedo attributes, and on the 3DGS tile-based rasterization pipeline for efficient rendering. It departs from prior appearance-variation methods (WildGaussians, GS-W, VAST) that attach per-Gaussian view-dependent features, arguing these entangle illumination with intrinsic appearance and overfit under drastic local lighting changes. It also moves beyond DarkGS, which requires pre-calibrated light-source parameters and per-Gaussian lighting, by introducing a tile-based image-space shading module and a Retinex-inspired illumination-weighted loss that operates without any hardware calibration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically important gap: reconstructing scenes from robots or phones that must carry their own light in dark environments, a setting where standard photometric-consistency assumptions break down. The tile-based shading scheme is a useful architectural choice that decouples illumination from geometry at low computational cost, and the three-stage training strategy (unlit recovery → lighting fitting → geometry refinement) provides a practical recipe for ill-posed joint optimization. However, the lighting model is intentionally minimal (no cast shadows, coarse specular handling), and the approach has only been validated on two small datasets with limited scene diversity, leaving open whether it generalizes to more complex multi-light or dynamic-lighting scenarios.
