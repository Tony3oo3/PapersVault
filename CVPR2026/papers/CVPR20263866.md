## Problem

Fast Adversarial Training (FAT) suffers from catastrophic overfitting, where robustness collapses after only a few epochs, and from a robustness-accuracy trade-off that worsens as the perturbation budget grows. Prior work has not identified which samples drive these failures or how to address both simultaneously. This paper shows that low-confidence, predominantly misclassified samples are the primary contributors to both phenomena, and that applying uniform perturbation and supervision across all samples is inherently suboptimal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and outputs a class label over L classes. During training, each sample in a batch of 128 is assigned a per-sample perturbation budget (ranging from 4/255 to 12/255) based on its confidence ranking, and a modified soft supervision label that depends on whether the adversarially perturbed sample is correctly or incorrectly classified. The final model is evaluated under ℓ∞ perturbations of 8/255 using single-step (FGSM) and multi-step (PGD, C&W, APGD, AutoAttack) attacks.

## Method

DDG (Distribution-aware Dynamic Guidance) jointly adapts three training signals per sample. First, a tanh-based budget allocation (Eq. 5) assigns larger perturbation magnitudes to high-confidence samples and smaller ones to low-confidence samples, guiding all inputs toward consistent decision boundaries while preventing low-confidence samples from amplifying spurious correlations. Second, the supervision label is conditionally modified: correctly predicted samples receive standard label relaxation, while misclassified samples receive additional positive-class reinforcement (scaled by 1 − batch accuracy) and negative-class suppression (scaled by 1/L) to avoid over-emphasizing erroneous targets (Eq. 7). Third, a weighted ℓ2 smoothing regularization (Eq. 9) balances the regularization strength across the batch and adds an extra penalty for misclassified examples, stabilizing gradients that would otherwise become nonsmooth under the dynamic guidance. The full procedure uses single-step FGSM-style attacks with a ResNet-18 backbone, requiring roughly one-tenth the compute of multi-step baselines.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DDG achieves 60.44% PGD-10 robustness and 82.67% clean accuracy (best checkpoint), outperforming the strongest FAT baseline TDAT (56.36% PGD-10, 82.46% clean) by over 4 points in robustness while maintaining comparable clean accuracy. On CIFAR-100, DDG reaches 34.42% PGD-10 and 57.98% clean accuracy, surpassing TDAT (33.56%/57.32%) and FGSM-PGK (32.78%/57.36%). On Tiny-ImageNet, DDG attains 24.35% PGD-10 robustness with 43.44% clean accuracy, exceeding TDAT (23.98%/42.51%). Notably, DDG uses only single-step FGSM updates yet surpasses multi-step methods like MART (54.83% PGD-10) and LAS-AWP (56.37% PGD-10) at roughly one-tenth the computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DDG outperforms all 11 baselines on PGD-10, PGD-50, FGSM, and APGD across all three datasets. Against the strongest FAT competitors, DDG gains +4.08% PGD-10 over TDAT (60.44 vs. 56.36) and +4.30% over FGSM-PGK (60.44 vs. 56.14) on CIFAR-10, with similar margins on CIFAR-100 (+0.86% over TDAT) and Tiny-ImageNet (+0.37% over TDAT). DDG also surpasses the multi-step baselines MART (54.83%) and LAS-AWP (56.37%) on PGD-10 despite using only a single FGSM step, at roughly one-tenth the training cost. On clean accuracy, DDG (82.67%) is competitive with FGSM-RS (83.69%) and FGSM-SDI (83.55%) while delivering substantially higher robustness. The only metric where DDG does not lead is C&W, where FGSM-PGK (50.90%) and FGSM-PGI (50.88%) slightly edge out DDG (49.86%), a gap the authors attribute to C&W's any-to-one attack objective favoring sharper decision boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the FGSM-RS single-step adversarial training paradigm [41] and the confidence-grouped label relaxation framework of TDAT [38], but departs from both by rejecting the uniform-guidance assumption that underlies all prior FAT methods. Where FGSM-RS applies a fixed perturbation budget to every sample and TDAT applies a fixed relaxation strength per confidence group, DDG makes both the perturbation magnitude and the supervision signal sample-adaptive within each batch, using a smooth tanh-based ranking to allocate budgets and a prediction-state-dependent formula to modulate labels. The weighted regularization term (Eq. 9) is a new stabilization mechanism specifically designed to counteract gradient nonsmoothness introduced by the dynamic budget assignment, which prior FAT methods do not need to address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful diagnostic: low-confidence (mostly misclassified) samples are the primary drivers of both catastrophic overfitting and the robustness-accuracy trade-off, and alleviating excessive error reinforcement on these samples improves both metrics simultaneously. This reframing suggests that future FAT methods should treat samples heterogeneously rather than uniformly. However, all experiments use only ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet, leaving open whether the dynamic-guidance strategy generalizes to deeper networks, Vision Transformers, or larger-scale benchmarks like ImageNet. The interaction between dynamic perturbation budgets and multi-step attacks, and the behavior under different perturbation norms (ℓ₂, ℓ₁), also remain unexplored.
