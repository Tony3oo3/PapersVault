## Problem

Current football imitation research optimizes reward-based objectives such as goals scored or win-rate proxies rather than faithfully replicating a team's spatio-temporal tactical organization. Full-team 11-v-11 trajectories from real broadcast footage remain difficult to obtain due to commercial data restrictions, multi-camera switching, and occlusions, while existing evaluation frameworks assess individual-level errors instead of the spatial and temporal consistency of collective team actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263054/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives the first-frame positions of all 22 players and the ball, projected onto a normalized pitch coordinate system (x ∈ [−1, 1], y ∈ [−0.42, 0.42]) matching Google Research Football field dimensions, and must generate the subsequent 25-frame multi-agent trajectory sequence. Evaluation is conducted at 3.0 s, 5.0 s, and 10.0 s prediction horizons on an adaptive spatial grid whose cell size scales inversely with the average displacement magnitude within the temporal window. Only the ball trajectory is scored in the final benchmark metric, since individual player trajectories introduce high-variance matching noise from role changes, occlusions, and identity ambiguities.

## Method

The dataset pipeline reconstructs full-team trajectories from monocular 1080p/25 FPS broadcast footage via YOLOv11 detection, DeepSORT tracking, and TVCalib-based homography that maps image coordinates to a standardized bird's-eye pitch space. Off-camera gaps are filled by a conditional VAE with a Bi-directional RNN encoder-decoder (trained on the Alfheim dataset) using a demonstrator–learner imitation structure in which a masked decoder reconstructs missing motion under supervision from the demonstrator's decoded representations. The benchmark defines an adaptive grid discretization that scales cell size with local motion intensity, then computes two complementary metrics—Jaccard-index spatial occupancy similarity and a cosine-like movement-vector similarity—combined via harmonic mean into a single tactical-fidelity score. Five imitation-learning baselines (BC, CMIL, IRL, CoDAIL, DRAIL) are evaluated under a unified multi-window training protocol (context lengths 1/10/25/50) with short-term closed-loop inference to mitigate exposure bias.

## Results

On the TacSIm benchmark, the best-performing baseline CoDAIL achieves a composite Score of 50.89% (S_t = 48.56%, S_v = 53.22%) at the 150-cell grid (15×10) with a 3.0 s prediction window, the configuration the authors identify as optimal. Performance degrades sharply with longer horizons: at 10.0 s the same CoDAIL score drops to 28.37%, and all methods fall below 30%. Medium-resolution grids (150 and 240 cells) consistently outperform both coarser (60) and finer (600, 1768) grids, with the 1768-cell grid reducing CoDAIL's 3.0 s score to just 27.10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoDAIL [17] is the strongest baseline across nearly all grid and time configurations, leading by a clear margin at the 150-cell grid (50.89% vs. 42.98% for CMIL at 3.0 s) and maintaining the top Score at 5.0 s (39.39%) and 10.0 s (28.37%). CMIL [16] is the closest competitor at short horizons and at the 240-cell grid, where it actually edges out CoDAIL at 3.0 s (47.87% vs. 43.22%), suggesting its shared-latent coordination mechanism is well-suited to finer spatial discretization. DRAIL [15] shows relative robustness at the 10.0 s horizon, achieving the highest S_t (26.49%) among all methods at the 150-cell grid, consistent with the authors' observation that diffusion-based denoising aids long-horizon spatial occupancy. BC [26] and IRL [29] are consistently the weakest baselines, with BC's 10.0 s Score collapsing to 8.63% at the 150-cell grid, confirming that pure supervised mapping and reward-inference approaches struggle to capture coordinated multi-agent tactical dynamics. Overall, no single method dominates every setting, which the authors frame as evidence that TacSIm meaningfully differentiates imitation strategies along both temporal and spatial axes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the SoccerNet-GSR pipeline for broadcast-to-pitch coordinate reconstruction and on conditional VAE-based trajectory imputation for handling occlusions, but departs from prior football datasets (SoccerNet, StatsBomb, Metrica) by adding explicit tactical phase annotations (attack/defense/transition) and a real-to-virtual simulation evaluation loop. In the imitation learning lineage, it extends multi-agent approaches (CMIL, CoDAIL, DRAIL) from synthetic or proprietary tracking data to trajectories extracted from monocular broadcast footage, introducing a task-specific evaluation protocol (spatial occupancy + movement vector similarity) that prior sports analytics work has not formalized for team-level tactical reproduction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TacSIm is the first dataset to couple real broadcast-derived full-team trajectories with tactical semantics and a virtual simulation evaluation, addressing a gap where prior work relied on synthetic environments (GRF) or commercially restricted tracking data. The benchmark reveals that all evaluated methods degrade sharply beyond 3-second horizons, suggesting that current imitation learning architectures capture local motion extrapolation but not genuine tactical intent reasoning. Open questions include whether richer semantic inputs (player roles, opponent positioning) or hierarchical planning could close the long-horizon gap, and whether the single-broadcast-view, EPL-only scope limits generalizability to other leagues or camera configurations.
