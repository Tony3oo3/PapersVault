## Problem

Existing weakly supervised 3D occupancy prediction methods assume rigid-body motion via simple frame-to-frame offsets, making them unable to capture the fine-grained nonrigid deformations characteristic of human-centric agents. Additionally, uniform Gaussian primitive allocation wastes modeling capacity on large static backgrounds, leaving insufficient resolution for small yet safety-critical human instances, resulting in degraded human-related mIoU and poor temporal consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260658/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single frame of 6 synchronized RGB camera images (nuScenes format) and produces a 200×200×16 voxel occupancy grid at 0.4 m resolution with per-voxel semantic class probabilities. During training, a short temporal sequence of frames is sampled as augmentation to drive the deformation module, but this adds no inference cost. Weak supervision comes from pseudo segmentation labels (GroundedSAM) and pseudo metric depth (Metric3D) rendered per camera view, rather than dense 3D voxel annotations.

## Method

DeGO introduces two coupled modules over a feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting backbone. First, Decoupled Gaussian Deformation (DGD) assigns each Gaussian a learnable rigid-body mask m_i ∈ [0,1] that gates a combined update: rigid instances receive only positional offsets, while nonrigid instances additionally undergo learned rotation, scale, and opacity deformation, with a binary regularization loss pushing the mask toward discrete assignments. Second, Factorized Feature Distillation (FFD) uses VGGT as a frozen teacher, exploiting its interleaved cross-camera (spatial) and cross-frame (temporal) attention to produce factorized 4D teacher features; these are projected and aligned with rendered student Gaussian features via a cosine-similarity loss, injecting multi-view and temporal reasoning into the Gaussian field. The full training objective jointly optimizes pixel-wise segmentation, depth regression, distillation alignment, and deformation smoothness losses, while inference remains a single-frame feed-forward pass that voxelizes the predicted Gaussians.

## Results

On the Occ3D-NuScenes benchmark, DeGO achieves 18.05 mIoU and 45.38 IoU, surpassing the previous best (GaussianFlowOcc) by 10.9% in mIoU and 12.4% in IoU. The method also reports 11.04 Human-centric mIoU (HCM), 10.34 Instance mIoU (InsM), and 33.46 Scene mIoU (ScnM), representing 13.5%, 7.8%, and 13.0% relative gains over GaussianFlowOcc respectively. DeGO leads on every per-class metric in the table, with particularly strong gains on pedestrian (18.05 vs. 16.27) and driving surface (66.19 vs. 59.17).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeGO outperforms all eight baselines on mIoU, with the largest gap over GaussianFlowOcc (18.05 vs. 16.27, +10.9% relative) and GaussTR (18.05 vs. 13.26, +36.1% relative). On IoU, DeGO (45.38) narrowly edges out GaussTR (45.19) and SelfOcc (45.01), while maintaining a clear 12.4% advantage over GaussianFlowOcc (40.39). The Human-centric mIoU (HCM) metric, which isolates safety-critical nonrigid classes, shows DeGO's most pronounced advantage: 11.04 vs. 9.73 for GaussianFlowOcc (+13.5%) and 11.04 vs. 5.81 for GaussTR (+90%). On Scene mIoU, DeGO (33.46) leads GaussianFlowOcc (29.62) by 13.0%, indicating stronger static-structure reconstruction alongside dynamic agents. Notably, older NeRF-based methods (DistillNeRF, OccNeRF) and early Gaussian-based methods (GaussianOcc, LangOcc) trail significantly on both mIoU and HCM, confirming that the combination of decoupled deformation and 4D foundation distillation is the key differentiator over prior weakly supervised approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeGO extends the GaussianFlowOcc line of work, which represents dynamic scenes as rigid Gaussian primitives updated via frame-to-frame offsets, by introducing a soft rigidity mask that lets each Gaussian adaptively choose between pure offset, nonrigid deformation, or a blend of both. The factorized distillation module departs from prior 2D per-frame distillation (DINOv2, CLIP) by exploiting VGGT's interleaved cross-camera and cross-frame attention to transfer 4D spatiotemporal knowledge into the Gaussian field. Conceptually, the paper bridges two previously separate threads—deformable Gaussian Splatting for dynamic rendering and foundation-model distillation for occupancy—by showing they are mutually reinforcing when the deformation is decoupled by rigidity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 13.5% gain on the proposed human-centric mIoU addresses a concrete safety gap in camera-based autonomous driving where nonrigid agents (pedestrians, cyclists) are under-modeled by rigid-offset assumptions. The decoupled deformation formulation is a practical, low-overhead mechanism that could be adopted by other Gaussian-based occupancy or reconstruction pipelines. Open questions remain around scaling to longer temporal horizons (performance degrades sharply beyond 8 frames), generalization beyond Occ3D-NuScenes, and whether the VGGT teacher can be replaced by lighter or more diverse 4D foundation models to reduce training cost.
