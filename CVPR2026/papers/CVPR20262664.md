## Problem

Unsupervised optical flow methods lack reliable per-pixel uncertainty estimation, limiting their robustness and interpretability in downstream tasks. Two core challenges remain: (1) no ground-truth variance or likelihood signal exists to supervise uncertainty in a self-supervised setting, and (2) even when uncertainty is estimated, it is unclear how to integrate it back into the training loop to improve flow accuracy rather than treating it as a mere byproduct.

## Input / Output

The method takes two consecutive RGB frames resized to 256×832 (KITTI) or 448×1024 (Sintel) and produces a dense optical flow field (H×W×2) along with a per-pixel variance map (H×W) at each of K=12 recurrent iterations. The uncertainty head outputs log-variance for numerical stability, and the final fused flow (with the optional bidirectional fusion module) is the primary output used for evaluation. Instance normalization replaces batch normalization throughout to stabilize training under small-batch unsupervised conditions.

## Method

U²Flow builds on the RAFT recurrent architecture and introduces a decoupled uncertainty learning strategy: the supervisory signal for the uncertainty head is derived from the model's own predictive inconsistency under strong appearance and spatial augmentations, formulated as a Laplace-based maximum likelihood objective where the flow residual is detached to prevent gradient leakage into the main flow branch. The predicted uncertainty is fed back into the recurrent update block as a sigmoid-weighted modulation of flow features (with stop-gradient), enabling adaptive refinement that suppresses unreliable regions. An uncertainty-guided bidirectional flow fusion module then corrects forward flow in regions where forward uncertainty is high but backward uncertainty is low, replacing heuristic occlusion masks with learned per-pixel confidence. Additionally, an uncertainty-enhanced homography smoothness loss (applied only to KITTI) uses the predicted uncertainty to select reliable correspondences for RANSAC-based regional flow refinement.

## Results

On KITTI 2015, U²Flow achieves Fl-all = 6.13% and EPE = 1.83, outperforming the previous best unsupervised two-frame method UPFlow (Fl-all = 9.38%) and multi-frame methods SMURF (6.83%) and M2Flow (7.37%). On Sintel Final test, U²Flow reaches EPE = 4.16, surpassing SMURF (4.18) and M2Flow (5.01). The enhanced variant U²Flow (+FF) further improves to Fl-all = 6.00% on KITTI 2015 and EPE = 4.10 on Sintel Final. For uncertainty estimation, U²Flow achieves AUSE = 0.11 and CC = 0.66 on Sintel, and AUSE = 0.12 and CC = 0.64 on KITTI, outperforming all baselines including the supervised PDC-Net+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

U²Flow outperforms all unsupervised baselines on both KITTI 2015 and Sintel. On KITTI 2015, it reduces Fl-all from 9.38% (UPFlow, the prior best two-frame method) to 6.13%, and also beats multi-frame SMURF (6.83%) and M2Flow (7.37%) as well as semantics-guided SemARFlow (8.38%) and UnSAMFlow (7.83%). On Sintel Final test, U²Flow achieves EPE 4.16, narrowly beating SMURF (4.18) and substantially outperforming M2Flow (5.01) and UnSAMFlow (5.20). The bidirectional flow fusion variant (+FF) further narrows the gap to supervised methods, reaching Fl-all 6.00% on KITTI 2015 (vs. 4.68% for supervised FlowFormer). For uncertainty estimation (Table 2), U²Flow achieves AUSE 0.11 / CC 0.66 on Sintel and AUSE 0.12 / CC 0.64 on KITTI, surpassing the supervised PDC-Net+ (AUSE 0.18/0.16, CC 0.45/0.50) and heuristic baselines (FB Check: AUSE 0.19/0.21, CC 0.57/0.53). Notably, the ablation in Table 5 shows that using a conventional occlusion mask for fusion actually degrades performance (Fl-all 8.17%) compared to no fusion (6.59%), while the uncertainty-guided mask improves it to 6.30%, validating the learned uncertainty as a superior fusion signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

U²Flow builds directly on the RAFT recurrent architecture and borrows its instance normalization and bidirectional fusion structure from SMURF, while adopting ARFlow's augmentation-based supervision and UnSAMFlow's homography smoothness. The key conceptual departure is the decoupled uncertainty learning strategy: prior uncertainty-estimation methods for optical flow (Ilg et al., PDC-Net+) rely on supervised ground-truth variance or likelihood, which is unavailable in the self-supervised setting. By deriving uncertainty supervision from the model's own predictive inconsistency under data augmentation and explicitly detaching the residual from the MLE objective, the paper separates the flow and uncertainty learning objectives in a way that prior coupled formulations do not.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that per-pixel uncertainty in unsupervised optical flow is not merely a diagnostic byproduct but a functionally useful signal that can guide adaptive refinement, modulate smoothness constraints, and drive bidirectional fusion—closing a gap that has limited the robustness and interpretability of self-supervised flow methods. The ablation showing that naive occlusion-mask-based fusion degrades performance below the no-fusion baseline challenges a widely adopted heuristic and suggests that binary geometric consistency checks are insufficient proxies for estimation reliability. A key limitation is that the uncertainty supervision is bounded by the predefined augmentation space, which may not capture all real-world error sources (e.g., severe motion blur, atmospheric distortion), leaving open the question of whether generative or learned augmentation strategies could broaden the uncertainty signal's coverage.
