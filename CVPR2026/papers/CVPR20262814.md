## Problem

Streaming VideoLLMs face severe latency bottlenecks from redundant ViT encoding of temporally similar frames and inflated visual token sequences during LLM prefilling. Existing token compression methods are incompatible with streaming because they require global video access or future user instructions, leaving a gap for causal, query-agnostic compression that simultaneously reduces both ViT encoding cost and LLM prefill length.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262814/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a continuous video stream at 0.5 fps, processed in discrete chunks of L frames, along with a text query that arrives after frames are encoded. Internally, it produces a compressed visual token sequence (reduced to 25–30% of original length) fed to the LLM, which then generates a text response. A key operational constraint is that all compression decisions must be made causally—without access to future frames or the user query—making the method plug-and-play for existing streaming VideoLLMs without retraining.

## Method

STC introduces two orthogonal, training-free modules. STC-Cacher performs cache-aware selective computation inside ViT: for every Nth reference frame it runs a full forward pass and caches intermediate K, V, attention, and MLP representations; for subsequent frames it computes cosine similarity between current Key projections and cached reference Keys, selects the top-k most dissimilar (dynamic) tokens, computes only their Query and Value, and scatter-updates them into the cached matrices, reducing ViT FLOPs by the reuse ratio R_Cacher. STC-Pruner then compresses the post-encoder token sequence before LLM prefill by scoring each token's novelty as a weighted sum of cosine distances to a Temporal Context Anchor (mean of a sliding history buffer) and a Spatial Context Anchor (mean of the current frame's tokens), retaining only the top-k highest-scoring tokens per the pruning ratio R_Pruner. Both modules are query- and future-agnostic, and their combination yields 24.5% ViT encoding and 45.3% LLM prefill latency reduction on ReKV while retaining 99% accuracy.

## Results

On OVO-Bench (streaming video understanding), STC-Cacher & Pruner applied to ReKV achieves an overall score of 52.0, retaining 99% of the baseline ReKV accuracy (52.6) while reducing ViT encoding latency by 24.5% (103.7s → 78.3s) and LLM prefilling latency by 45.3% (482.4s → 263.7s). On StreamingBench, the combined STC achieves 65.2 overall versus 69.1 for the ReKV baseline. On offline long-video benchmarks (EgoSchema, MLVU-dev, VideoMME), STC-Pruner alone achieves 61.8 average, slightly exceeding the ReKV baseline of 61.3, while the full STC pipeline scores 60.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ToMe (ICLR 2023), STC-Cacher & Pruner achieves 52.0 overall on OVO-Bench versus ToMe's 46.4, a 5.6-point accuracy advantage, though ToMe is slightly faster on ViT encoding (70.5s vs. 78.3s); on StreamingBench the gap widens to 65.2 vs. 59.4. Against VisionZip (CVPR 2025), STC gains 4.5 points on OVO-Bench (52.0 vs. 47.5) and additionally reduces ViT latency (78.3s vs. 103.7s), whereas VisionZip only accelerates LLM prefilling. Against VidCom² (EMNLP 2025), the closest competitor, STC improves OVO-Bench overall by 1.6 points (52.0 vs. 50.4) and StreamingBench by 1.6 points (65.2 vs. 63.6), while also cutting ViT encoding time by 24.5%—something VidCom² does not address. For end-to-end online models (Dispider, LiveCC, StreamForest), STC-Cacher alone reduces ViT encoding latency by 28–35% with only 1–2 points of accuracy loss, confirming plug-and-play compatibility. On offline long-video benchmarks, STC-Pruner alone slightly outperforms the ReKV baseline (61.8 vs. 61.3 average), while the full STC pipeline trades 0.5 points for the latency reductions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two largely separate lines of work: token compression for VideoLLMs (ToMe, VisionZip, VidCom2) and streaming video understanding frameworks (ReKV, Dispider, LiveCC, StreamForest). Prior compression methods are designed for offline settings requiring global video access or query awareness, making them incompatible with causal streaming constraints, while streaming frameworks address temporal coherence but leave ViT encoding as an unoptimized bottleneck. STC departs from both by introducing a two-stage, streaming-native design: STC-Cacher repurposes the feature-caching idea (analogous to KV caching in NLP) for selective ViT recomputation, and STC-Pruner introduces a query-agnostic dual-anchor scoring mechanism that replaces the instruction-aware or global-context token selection used in offline methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free nature of STC makes it immediately deployable on existing streaming VideoLLMs, addressing a practical bottleneck (ViT encoding latency) that has been largely overlooked in favor of LLM-side optimizations. The 24.5% ViT and 45.3% LLM prefill latency reductions while retaining ~99% accuracy on ReKV represent a meaningful step toward real-time deployment in latency-sensitive applications like AR glasses and live commentary. Open questions include how the method scales to higher frame rates or more dynamic scenes where temporal redundancy diminishes, whether the fixed hyperparameters (cache interval, pruning ratio) can be made adaptive, and whether the approach extends to audio-visual streaming settings.
