## Problem

Existing real-to-sim object reconstruction pipelines decompose the task into sequential modules (detection, segmentation, shape reconstruction, pose estimation), causing error propagation, computational inefficiency, and inconsistent shape proportions. Monocular generative 3D models cannot resolve scale ambiguity, and category-level methods are limited to fewer than six categories due to the difficulty of defining canonical spaces. No prior work achieves end-to-end, multi-object, metric-accurate reconstruction from a single stereo pair in one forward pass.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single calibrated stereo image pair (left and right views) as the sole input, requiring no 2D bounding boxes, segmentation masks, or pose annotations. In a single forward pass, it simultaneously detects all objects in the scene and outputs each object's 3D center position (x, y, z), physical scale, and a pose-aware 3D shape represented as occupancy values in a spherical voxel space. Category labels are assigned post-hoc via CLIP on the projected 2D bounding box rather than through a learned classification head.

## Method

UniPR combines three key components: (1) a Pose-Aware Shape Representation (PASR) that encodes object geometry in a spherical voxel space to eliminate rotation-dependent scale ambiguity and remove the need for per-category canonical definitions; (2) a Triplane-View (TPV) encoder that lifts DINOv2-extracted stereo features into a global UVD coordinate system via stereo cross-attention, providing the geometric constraints needed to resolve monocular scale ambiguity; and (3) a DETR-style transformer decoder with learned object queries that processes all scene objects in parallel, regressing position, scale, and a KL-regularized Gaussian shape embedding. A separately trained pose-aware shape VAE (2048 surface points → 64-dim latent → occupancy decoder) is frozen and integrated as the shape decoder. Training uses Hungarian matching with L1 loss for position/scale and KL divergence for the shape distribution, while the VAE is trained with binary cross-entropy on occupancy.

## Results

On the LVS6D reconstruction benchmark (50 objects), UniPR achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.0083, F-Score of 0.883, and Shape Proportion Error of 0.109, substantially outperforming Trellis (CD 0.1096, F-Score 0.334, SPE 0.475) and HunYuan3D 2.1 (CD 0.0644, F-Score 0.553, SPE 0.320) which require perfect 2D masks and poses as input. Inference time for a full 5-object scene is 0.63 s end-to-end, compared to 43.08 s (Trellis) and 370.78 s (HunYuan3D 2.1), yielding up to 100× speedup. On the LVS6D detection-and-reconstruction task, UniPR reaches AP of 0.702–0.764 and ACD of 0.413–1.224 across Easy/Medium/Hard subsets, consistently surpassing Coders (AP 0.070–0.112, ACD 1.816–10.146).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two SOTA image-to-3D generative models, UniPR dominates on every metric: CD is 13× lower than Trellis (0.0083 vs 0.1096) and 8× lower than HunYuan3D 2.1 (0.0083 vs 0.0644), while SPE is 4.4× and 2.9× lower respectively, confirming that stereo geometric constraints resolve the scale ambiguity inherent in monocular generative approaches. The end-to-end design also eliminates the need for upstream detection/segmentation, cutting full-scene inference from 370.78 s (HunYuan3D 2.1) to 0.63 s. On the LVS6D detection-and-reconstruction benchmark, UniPR outperforms Coders [39] across all three difficulty subsets, with the largest gap on the Hard subset (AP 0.752 vs 0.070; ACD 1.224 vs 10.146), where predefined canonical spaces cause Coders to fail on geometrically diverse objects. On the public SS3D dataset, UniPR substantially exceeds Coders (3D50: 89.3 vs 61.7; 3D75: 55.4 vs 21.5), but on the TOD transparent-object dataset Coders retains a slight edge (3D50: 99.8 vs 97.5; 3D75: 62.1 vs 52.5), suggesting stereo-based methods still face challenges with highly transparent surfaces. Overall, UniPR wins or ties on 5 of 7 head-to-head comparisons, with the sole losses being narrow margins on TOD's 3D IoU thresholds.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniPR unifies three previously decoupled sub-tasks—object detection, 6D pose estimation, and shape reconstruction—into a single end-to-end stereo pipeline, departing from the modular DETR-style detection followed by per-object reconstruction pipelines (e.g., Coders, OnePoseViaGen). Architecturally, it builds on the triplane-view representation for spatial feature aggregation, the DETR transformer decoder with object queries for parallel multi-object processing, and the occupancy-network shape VAE paradigm, while replacing the canonical-space (NOCS) formulation with a pose-aware spherical-voxel representation that removes the need for per-category canonical definitions. The stereo-based design explicitly contrasts with monocular image-to-3D generative models (Trellis, Hunyuan3D) that inherit scale ambiguity, positioning geometric constraints as the key to metric-accurate reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For robotics and real-to-sim transfer, the 100× speedup from parallel multi-object processing and the elimination of error-prone intermediate modules (segmentation, per-object reconstruction) make physically accurate scene reconstruction practical in deployment settings. The LVS6D dataset (192 categories, 6,300+ objects) and the PASR representation lower the barrier to large-vocabulary reconstruction by removing the need for hand-defined canonical spaces, which has been a bottleneck limiting prior work to fewer than six categories. The principal open question is whether the significant performance drop in monocular settings (AP drops from 0.70 to 0.17 on Easy) can be mitigated with learned depth modules, which would broaden applicability to single-camera setups common in field robotics.
