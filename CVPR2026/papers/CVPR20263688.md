## Problem

Generating 3D Gaussians remains challenging without reliable geometric priors: existing methods either predict point maps whose inaccuracies propagate into poor Gaussian quality, or depend on 3D meshes that require extensive manual modeling and UV unwrapping. Meanwhile, easily accessible 3D point clouds (from LiDAR, depth cameras, or retrieval) have not been leveraged as geometric references for Gaussian appearance generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263688/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (synthetic 100K-point samples or noisy real-scanned data with varying densities) and a text prompt as input, producing a complete 3D Gaussian Splatting representation using 2D oriented disks rather than ellipsoids. No mesh, UV map, or watertight surface is required; all geometric information (depth, normals, positions) is derived from the point cloud via a learned unsigned distance field. For text-to-3D, the point cloud can be retrieved via Uni3D or generated as a scaffold by LGM.

## Method

GaussianGrow initializes 2D Gaussian disks at point cloud positions with orientations from a CAP-UDF-learned unsigned distance field, then grows their appearances through a two-stage process. Stage 1 uses a multi-view diffusion model (Hunyuan3D-Paint) conditioned on depth/normal/position maps to synthesize 10 consistent views (6 cardinal + 4 optimized for overlap regions), with view-specific optimization that only updates front-facing Gaussians per viewpoint to avoid disturbing already-optimized regions. Stage 2 iteratively identifies the largest un-grown region via a differentiable occlusion loss that optimizes camera pose, renders an occluded view, inpaints it with a depth-aware 2D diffusion model, and grows Gaussians in the previously invisible region until complete. A spatial inpainting post-processing step propagates appearance from optimized neighbors to any remaining hard-to-observe Gaussians.

## Results

On the Objaverse text-guided visual synthesis benchmark (410 models, 225 categories), GaussianGrow achieves FID 36.07, KID 3.04, and CLIP score 27.30, outperforming the best baseline GAP (FID 40.39, KID 5.28) by 4.32 FID and 2.24 KID. In user studies, GaussianGrow scores 4.67/5 for overall quality and 4.72/5 for text fidelity, surpassing the next-best SyncMVD (4.13/4.34). On the T3Bench text-to-3D benchmark, the retrieve-based variant (Ours+Uni3D) achieves 31.55% CLIP similarity, 82.00% CLIP R-Precision, and ImageReward −0.316, leading all competitors including DiffSplat (30.95%, 81.00%, −0.491) and LGM (29.96%, 78.00%, −0.720).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianGrow outperforms all five text-guided texturing baselines (TexTure, Text2Tex, SyncMVD, Paint3D, GAP) on FID and KID, with the largest margin over TexTure (FID 42.63 vs. 36.07) and the closest competition from GAP (FID 40.39 vs. 36.07). When those baselines are forced to operate on reconstructed meshes (via BPA or CAP-UDF + xatlas UV unwrapping), their performance degrades severely (FID 52–65), highlighting the advantage of GaussianGrow's direct point-cloud-to-Gaussian pipeline that bypasses UV parameterization entirely. On T3Bench text-to-3D, Ours+Uni3D leads all seven competitors on CLIP similarity (31.55% vs. 30.95% for DiffSplat) and ImageReward (−0.316 vs. −0.491 for DiffSplat, −0.720 for LGM), while Ours+LGM matches DiffSplat on CLIP R-Precision (81.00%) but still wins on ImageReward. In the point-to-Gaussian visual comparison, GaussianGrow produces noticeably better geometric fidelity and color accuracy than both DreamGaussian (which suffers from SDS oversaturation) and TriplaneGaussian (constrained by triplane resolution). The ablation confirms that both the overlap-region camera pose optimization and the iterative inpainting contribute roughly 4.4 FID improvement each over the full model.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussianGrow departs from two dominant paradigms in 3D content generation: (1) mesh-based texturing methods (TEXTure, Text2Tex, Paint3D, SyncMVD) that require UV unwrapping and manual modeling, and (2) joint geometry-and-appearance Gaussian generation methods (DiffSplat, GVGEN, GaussianCube) that are vulnerable to geometric prediction errors. Instead, it positions readily available point clouds (from LiDAR, depth cameras, or retrieval via Uni3D) as the geometric prior, building on CAP-UDF for unsigned distance field estimation, 2D Gaussian Splatting for the primitive representation, and Hunyuan3D-Paint for multi-view appearance synthesis. The key conceptual shift is reframing Gaussian generation as a 'growing' process from fixed point positions rather than a joint optimization of structure and appearance, with the novel overlap-refinement and iterative inpainting mechanisms addressing view-consistency and coverage gaps specific to this formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in decoupling geometry acquisition (trivial via scanning or retrieval) from appearance generation (handled by diffusion models), making high-quality 3D Gaussian creation accessible without 3D modeling expertise or mesh reconstruction. The iterative inpainting and overlap-refinement strategies are general-purpose techniques that could transfer to other view-consistent generation settings. Limitations include per-instance optimization cost, dependence on the quality of pretrained multi-view diffusion models (Hunyuan3D-Paint), and sensitivity to point cloud noise/density (mitigated but not eliminated by spatial inpainting). Open questions include whether the approach scales to large scenes, whether the multi-view diffusion dependency can be replaced by a feed-forward model for real-time generation, and how well it handles highly non-convex or thin-structure geometries where point cloud coverage is inherently sparse.
