## Problem

Existing HOI synthesis methods (e.g., CHOIS, HOIFHLI) require users to manually specify intermediate waypoints and assign all optimization objectives—human pose, object pose, and contact—onto a single denoising network. This design narrows generative autonomy, increases optimization complexity, and produces unsynchronized artifacts such as object hovering and hand-object penetration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261180/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text instruction, object geometry encoded as a 1024-point Basis Point Set (BPS), the current human pose (global joint coordinates plus per-joint 6D rotations over T frames), the current object pose (3D position and 9-parameter rotation per frame), and a goal point. It outputs a full 3D human pose sequence (T×D_h), a 3D object pose sequence (T×12), and an SMPL-X human mesh. Notably, no intermediate waypoints are required at inference, and the object geometry is consumed through a lightweight MLP encoder that produces a fixed-dimensional embedding concatenated to each frame's conditioning vector.

## Method

DecHOI decouples generation into two sequential conditional denoising diffusion models with transformer backbones: a Trajectory Generator (TG) that plans continuous 3D paths for both human and object, and an Action Generator (AG) that synthesizes full joint poses conditioned on the TG's clean trajectories as dense positional priors. Text conditioning is applied via cross-attention rather than sequence-axis concatenation, yielding stronger semantic alignment. A compact adversarial discriminator, trained on distal-joint kinematics (hands and feet via forward kinematics) and per-frame object surface points, regularizes the AG to enforce complete contact and reduce interpenetration. A forward-kinematics loss further stabilizes distal joint reconstruction, and inference-time gradient guidance penalizes hand-object distance and foot-ground deviation at each denoising step. For dynamic scenes, a DynaPlan module performs A*-based re-planning with a pre-trained trajectory predictor to handle moving counterparts, choosing between detour and wait behaviors.

## Results

On FullBodyManipulation, DecHOI achieves the best scores across nearly all metrics: FID of 0.33 (vs. 1.58 for CHOIS and 2.06 for HOIFHLI), R-precision of 0.72, contact F1 of 0.67, and hand penetration of 0.53 cm. Trajectory accuracy is also strongest, with start and end position errors of 1.59 cm and 6.91 cm respectively, and object translation error drops to 22.96 cm (vs. 51.86 cm for CHOIS). On the unseen-object 3D-FUTURE benchmark, DecHOI maintains its lead with FID 1.01, R-precision 0.69, and hand penetration 0.15 cm. On the long-sequence DynaPlan benchmark, DecHOI reduces all four penetration metrics (hand, body, object-to-scene, human-to-scene) relative to CHOIS while also lowering trajectory errors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DecHOI outperforms all three baselines on the vast majority of metrics. Against CHOIS [24], the closest competitor, DecHOI reduces FID from 1.58 to 0.33, improves R-precision from 0.68 to 0.72, lowers hand penetration from 0.66 to 0.53 cm, and cuts object translation error roughly in half (51.86 → 22.96 cm). Against HOIFHLI [43], DecHOI wins on FID (0.33 vs. 2.06), R-precision (0.72 vs. 0.62), contact F1 (0.67 vs. 0.64), and both penetration metrics (0.53/0.54 vs. 0.58/0.61 cm). Against the OMOMO [23] variants, which condition on per-frame object poses, DecHOI achieves substantially lower FID (0.33 vs. 13.01 for Pred-OMOMO) and better trajectory accuracy, despite using far less object conditioning. The only metric where DecHOI does not lead is diversity (DIV = 8.86 vs. CHOIS's 8.31), though the real-data reference is 9.02, making DecHOI's value closer to the target. On the unseen-object 3D-FUTURE test, DecHOI extends its lead over CHOIS with FID 1.01 vs. 2.04 and hand penetration 0.15 vs. 0.18 cm, confirming generalization to novel geometries.

## Relation to Prior Work

DecHOI builds directly on the diffusion-based HOI synthesis line established by CHOIS and HOIFHLI, which condition a single denoising network on sparse object waypoints and CLIP text embeddings. The key architectural departure is splitting that monolithic network into a trajectory generator and an action generator, each with a simpler optimization objective, and replacing sequence-axis text concatenation with cross-attention. A compact adversarial discriminator focused on distal-joint kinematics and object geometry is added to regularize contact, and the static-scene formulation is extended to dynamic multi-agent planning via A*-based re-planning (DynaPlan).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the need for manually specified intermediate waypoints and reducing per-network optimization complexity makes HOI synthesis more practical and autonomous, while the distal-joint adversarial training addresses the persistent penetration and hovering artifacts that plague prior methods. The DynaPlan extension toward responsive, long-horizon multi-agent interaction is a step toward embodied applications, though it relies on a pre-trained trajectory predictor and A* search that may not scale to highly cluttered or stochastic environments. Open questions include whether the decoupled formulation generalizes to articulated objects, whether the adversarial component can be replaced by differentiable contact constraints, and how inference speed (still diffusion-based) compares to autoregressive or flow-matching alternatives.
