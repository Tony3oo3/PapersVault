## Problem

Diffusion-based image editing systems rely on monolithic text prompts or single reference images, which cannot disentangle a user's distinct intentions for what to create, where to place it, how it should be shaped, and with what colors to paint it. This 'intention gap' forces users to sacrifice the spatial precision of traditional graphics software (e.g., Photoshop layers) when working with generative models, as intertwined commands in a single prompt lead to imprecise, uncontrollable edits.

## Input / Output

The system takes a context image, a natural language instruction, and a stack of independently composable visual cues: one or more foreground reference images (content layer), a binary spatial mask (spatial layer), a sketched edge map (structural layer), and painted color strokes (color map). All control cues are resized to a fixed low resolution for computational efficiency while preserving spatial alignment via scaled positional encodings. The output is a single edited image that jointly respects all active layers, with per-cue guidance strength (σ) allowing users to tune how strictly each layer constrains the generation.

## Method

Built on FLUX Kontext's MMDiT backbone, MagicQuill V2 introduces three key components. First, a data generation pipeline synthesizes 5,000 object-scene interaction images, extracts foregrounds via Grounding SAM, restores occluded objects with a dedicated completion LoRA, and applies photometric (ICLight), perspective, and resolution augmentations before compositing augmented objects back into masked backgrounds to train context-aware content integration. Second, a unified control module processes all control cues (mask, edge, color) through LoRA-adapted conditional branches in the MMDiT, with a Causal Modulated Attention mechanism that adds a structured bias matrix to attention logits—enabling per-cue guidance scaling via log(σ_k) while setting inter-cue attention to −∞ to prevent interference. Third, the spatial layer is explicitly fine-tuned on self-distilled local editing pairs and an object removal dataset, enabling precise regional edits and content-aware removal rather than simple inpainting.

## Results

On the content layer task (200 curated samples), MagicQuill V2 achieves L1=0.061, L2=0.019, CLIP-I=0.962, DINO=0.930, and LPIPS=0.202, substantially outperforming the best baseline (Nano Banana: L1=0.105, LPIPS=0.321). On the Pico-Banana-400K benchmark for control layers (1,000 samples), the combined Edge+Color configuration reaches L1=0.080, DINO=0.930, and LPIPS=0.283, versus Qwen-Image-Edge's L1=0.131 and LPIPS=0.387. For object removal on RORD (5,000 samples), MagicQuill V2 attains the best scores across all six metrics, including PSNR=24.45 and SSIM=0.840, surpassing OmniEraser(CN) (PSNR=22.96, SSIM=0.817).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On content layer composition, MagicQuill V2 dominates all five baselines (Insert Anything, Nano Banana, Qwen-Image, FLUX Kontext, and the community LoRA 'Put it Here') on every metric except CLIP-T, where it ties with Nano Banana and Put it Here at 0.335; the LPIPS gap is particularly large (0.202 vs. 0.321–0.438), reflecting superior perceptual fidelity in handling occlusion, lighting, and perspective. On the Pico-Banana-400K control-layer benchmark, the combined Edge+Color model outperforms Qwen-Image-Edit (with its native edge module) by 39% in L1 (0.080 vs. 0.131) and 27% in LPIPS (0.283 vs. 0.387), while also surpassing the base FLUX Kontext by 47% in L1. For object removal on RORD, MagicQuill V2 beats SmartEraser and both OmniEraser variants on all six metrics (L1, L2, LPIPS, SSIM, PSNR, FID), with the largest margin over OmniEraser(CN) in FID (16.42 vs. 25.92). The paper also qualitatively shows that inpainting-based baselines (FLUX Fill, MagicQuill V1) fail at content-aware regional edits because they ignore masked-region content, whereas MagicQuill V2's spatial layer preserves identity while applying style or color changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FLUX Kontext (a diffusion-transformer editing backbone) and MagicQuill V1 (an interactive editing interface), extending both into a unified layered composition framework. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of monolithic text-prompt or single-reference-image editing by decomposing user intent into four independent visual layers (content, spatial, structural, color), drawing on the layered workflow of professional graphics software. The unified control module with Causal Modulated Attention is a novel architectural contribution that generalizes prior single-condition control approaches (ControlNet, OminiControl, EasyControl) to simultaneously manage multiple heterogeneous cues with per-cue strength tuning and inter-cue isolation. In the layered synthesis literature, it differs from methods like LayerDiff or LayerFusion by treating the 'foreground' as a generative cue rather than a fixed compositional element, and from image composition methods (AnyDoor, Insert Anything) by providing explicit geometric and color control beyond layout masks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The layered composition paradigm offers a practical bridge between the semantic flexibility of diffusion models and the spatial precision of traditional graphics tools, addressing a real usability gap for creators who need fine-grained control. The data pipeline (object completion LoRA, photometric/perspective/resolution augmentations) is a reusable recipe for training context-aware content integration, and the finding that structural/color control trained on generation generalizes to editing is a useful practical insight. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video editing or 3D scenes, how it handles truly novel compositions (rather than modifications of existing scenes), and whether the FLUX Kontext dependency limits broader adoption. The system's reliance on a 5,000-image base dataset and self-distillation for spatial layer training also raises questions about robustness to out-of-distribution content.
