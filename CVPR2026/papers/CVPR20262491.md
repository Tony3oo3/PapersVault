## Problem

Existing cross-architecture knowledge transfer methods treat Small-to-Large (S2L) and Large-to-Small (L2S) scaling as fundamentally separate problems: S2L relies on parameter synthesis via layer duplication or trainable mapping functions, while L2S depends on heuristic parameter selection. This fragmentation has produced specialized, incompatible tools with no unified, size-agnostic mechanism that handles both transfer directions within a single principled framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262491/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained source model's weights, which are first consolidated into 3D weight modules by stacking corresponding 2D matrices (e.g., all query/key/value projections) across layers into tensors of shape L_src × d_in × d_out. The wavelet decomposition level (downsampling factor f) is chosen to precisely match the target model's layer count and hidden dimensions, making the framework size-agnostic. The output is a fully initialized target model weight tensor of the target architecture's dimensions, ready for continued pre-training or direct fine-tuning without any additional learnable transfer parameters.

## Method

BoT treats model weights as continuous signals where models of different sizes represent distinct discretizations of the same underlying knowledge signal, with the low-frequency spectrum encoding the transferable 'learngene.' For L2S transfer, it applies a 3D Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) to each consolidated weight module, extracting the low-frequency approximation sub-band (cA) as a compact learngene that directly initializes the smaller target. For S2L transfer, it sets the small model's weights as the approximation coefficients and zeros all seven high-frequency detail bands, then applies the 3D Inverse DWT (IDWT) to synthesize the larger target architecture. The recursive, multi-level nature of the wavelet transform makes the decomposition level a natural dynamic scaling factor, and the entire process is parameter-free and training-free, requiring only a single forward wavelet operation per weight module.

## Results

BoT achieves up to 67.1% FLOPs savings for S2L (BERT-S→BERT-B) and 52.8% for L2S (BERT-B→BERT-S) pre-training acceleration, with analogous gains on GPT-2 (58.3%/31.0%) and DeiT (22.0%/39.0%). On direct fine-tuning, BoT-initialized BERT-S reaches 70.29% GLUE average and 48.25% SQuAD average, while BERT-B reaches 73.24% GLUE and 56.51% SQuAD. On DeiT downstream tasks, BoT attains 75.1% average accuracy (L2S, DeiT-S) and 84.1% (S2L, DeiT-B), leading on fine-grained benchmarks like CUB-200-2011 and Stanford Cars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against WS (Weight Selection, L2S-only, training-free), BoT wins decisively: on BERT-S it gains +9.39% GLUE average (70.29 vs 60.90) and more than doubles SQuAD average (48.25 vs 21.28), while also saving 19.8% more FLOPs during pre-training. Against bert2BERT (S2L, naive stacking), BoT achieves +14.26% GLUE average (73.24 vs 58.98) and +36.20% SQuAD average (56.51 vs 20.31) on BERT-B, showing that simple parameter copying severely limits downstream transfer. Against the trainable S2L methods LiGO and Mango, BoT edges ahead by +0.43% GLUE and +0.82% SQuAD on BERT-B, and by +11.4% and +5.3% FLOPs savings respectively on DeiT-B, while being entirely parameter-free. On DeiT-S (L2S), BoT outperforms WS by +1.2% average accuracy with particularly large margins on fine-grained tasks (+3.6% CUB, +2.5% Cars). Knowledge Distillation (KD) is mentioned qualitatively as an L2S alternative but is not included in the numerical tables; the paper notes BoT surpasses KD on DeiT-S pre-training FLOPs savings (39.0% vs KD's lower figure) without incurring per-step teacher inference costs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the fragmented treatment of cross-architecture initialization, where Large-to-Small (L2S) methods like Weight Selection [51] rely on heuristic parameter sub-sampling and Small-to-Large (S2L) methods like LiGO [46] and Mango [25] employ trainable projection operators. BoT reframes both directions as a single signal-processing problem by treating model weights as continuous signals and applying 3D Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) for downsampling (L2S) and Inverse DWT with zeroed high-frequency bands for upsampling (S2L). It also extends the frequency-domain analysis literature in deep learning—which has been confined to input features or intra-architecture compression via DCT [41,42]—by repurposing wavelet transforms as a cross-architecture, bidirectional initialization mechanism. The conceptual grounding draws on the 'learngene' paradigm from the same group [6,7,8], which posits that pre-trained models contain a size-agnostic core of transferable knowledge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BoT offers a parameter-free, training-free initialization that achieves substantial FLOPs savings (up to 67.1% for BERT S2L, 52.8% for BERT L2S) while matching or exceeding trainable methods on downstream benchmarks, making it a practical tool for flexible model scaling without additional training overhead. However, the experiments are limited to factor-of-2 scaling (6→12 layers) and Transformer architectures only, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger size gaps, non-integer scaling, or non-Transformer backbones (e.g., CNNs, Mamba). The architecture- and direction-dependent wavelet choice (Haar for BERT L2S, bior6.8 for BERT S2L, coif3 for GPT L2S, db2 for GPT S2L) suggests the method is not yet truly 'size-agnostic' in the strongest sense, and a principled wavelet selection criterion remains an open question.
