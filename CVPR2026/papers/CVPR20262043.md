## Problem

Automated PET report generation is severely underexplored: existing medical VLMs are trained predominantly on 2D chest X-rays or 3D CT, and none are designed to jointly process dual-modality PET/CT volumes with lesion-level spatial grounding. PET reports demand fine-grained, lesion-specific descriptions of small (often <0.1% of volume), spatially dispersed lesions, yet no large-scale public dataset provides 3D lesion segmentations aligned with free-text radiological findings, and standard global feature extraction in 3D VLMs erases these fine-grained details.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 3D PET volume, a co-registered 3D CT volume (both resampled to 192×192×352 at 3 mm), a binary 3D lesion mask, and a text query as input. The mask is a sparse binary volume (typically 0.01%–2% of the scan) that localizes a single lesion; from it, a cubic focal subvolume is extracted with random center and size perturbations (±20%) to provide a high-resolution local view. The output is a free-text lesion-level finding describing anatomical site, morphology, and metabolic activity (e.g., SUVmax), structured to match clinical radiology report language.

## Method

PETAR-4B uses a shared 3D Vision Transformer to encode PET and CT patch embeddings, with the lesion mask injected via element-wise additive conditioning on the PET tokens (Z_P + Z_M) before transformer processing, so the encoder attends to mask-relevant regions. A 3D focal prompt—a randomly perturbed cubic crop centered on the mask—provides a complementary high-resolution local view; global and focal features are combined by element-wise addition, spatially pooled, and linearly projected into the Phi3-4B language model's embedding space for autoregressive text generation. Training follows a three-stage curriculum (projection-head alignment → mask-embedding alignment → full end-to-end finetuning), first pretrained on ~100K TotalSegmentator anatomical segmentations to establish PET–CT structural correspondence before fine-tuning on PETARSeg-11K. The architecture is built on M3D's 3D ViT encoder and spatial pooling, extended with the mask-aware conditioning channel and the focal-prompt mechanism adapted from Describe Anything to 3D.

## Results

On the PETARSeg-11K test set (1,175 samples), PETAR-4B achieves BLEU 0.535, ROUGE-L 0.524, METEOR 0.560, CIDEr 0.457, BERTScore 0.795, RaTEScore 0.713, and GREEN 0.257, outperforming all 2D and 3D baselines on every metric. The largest margins are on clinically grounded metrics: GREEN is 3.6× higher than the best finetuned 3D baseline (M3D-RAD: 0.071) and 3.0× higher than the best finetuned 2D baseline (MedGemma: 0.086). In a five-physician human study, PETAR-4B scores 3.7–3.9/5 on anatomical accuracy, interpretation, and utility (vs. 4.3–4.4 for human reports), and is preferred or tied in ~60% of 116 cases. On the external autoPET dataset, PETAR-4B maintains comparable scores (3.8–4.0/5), confirming out-of-distribution generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PETAR-4B outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric. Against the strongest finetuned 2D baseline (MedGemma-4B), it gains +0.040 BLEU, +0.070 ROUGE-L, +0.050 METEOR, +0.338 CIDEr, +0.041 BERTScore, +0.100 RaTEScore, and +0.171 GREEN. Against the strongest finetuned 3D baseline (M3D-RAD), gains are +0.050 BLEU, +0.078 ROUGE-L, +0.059 METEOR, +0.325 CIDEr, +0.045 BERTScore, +0.086 RaTEScore, and +0.186 GREEN. The mask-aware 3D baseline Reg2RG (finetuned) trails PETAR-4B by +0.057 BLEU and +0.226 GREEN, confirming that dual-modality PET/CT processing plus focal prompting provides advantages beyond mask conditioning alone. Zero-shot 3D models (Merlin, CT-CHAT, Med3DVLM) are essentially non-functional on PET, with GREEN scores of 0.001–0.003 and BERTScores below 0.52, highlighting the domain gap between CT-trained and PET-specific models. In the human evaluation, PETAR-4B is preferred or tied in 69/116 cases (~60%), with anatomical, interpretation, and utility scores of 3.9, 3.9, and 3.7 respectively versus 4.4, 4.4, and 4.3 for human reports.

## Relation to Prior Work

PETAR-4B extends the M3D 3D vision-language architecture by adding mask-aware conditioning and a 3D focal prompt mechanism adapted from the 2D Describe Anything Model, while jointly encoding dual-modality PET/CT volumes rather than the single-modality CT volumes used by M3D, Merlin, and CT2Rep. The mask-guided conditioning generalizes Reg2RG's organ-level CT region prompting to the finer-grained, lesion-level PET/CT setting, where lesions occupy less than 0.1% of the volume. The PETARSeg-11K dataset fills a gap identified in prior work: existing PET/CT resources (ViMed-PET, Pet2Rep) provide text but lack grounded masks, while segmentation datasets (SegAnyPet, autoPET) provide masks but lack aligned clinical findings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The first public dataset linking 3D PET/CT lesion masks to free-text findings, combined with the first human evaluation study for automated PET reporting, establishes a foundational benchmark for a modality that is severely underrepresented in medical VLM research. The severe domain shift from CT to PET (3D VLMs like Merlin scoring near-zero on PET) highlights that simply scaling CT-trained models is insufficient, and the architectural contribution (focal prompt + mask conditioning) provides a ~3.6× GREEN improvement over the best finetuned 3D baseline. Key open questions include building a fully automated pipeline (current method requires mask inputs from physicians or segmentation models) and extending from individual lesion findings to complete templated PET reports.
