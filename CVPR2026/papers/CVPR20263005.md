## Problem

Unified vision-language models that handle both understanding and generation in a single autoregressive framework require hundreds to thousands of visual tokens per image, creating substantial compute and memory overhead. Naive token compression (uniform downsampling or pruning) preserves understanding accuracy but degrades generation quality by over 15%, because image synthesis is sensitive to the loss of fine-grained spatially consistent tokens. No existing plug-in compression method jointly supports both tasks under a single autoregressive interface without requiring full LLM retraining.

## Input / Output

For understanding, a 512×512 RGB image is encoded into a compressed visual sequence of 64 local tokens plus 4 global meta tokens (a 4× reduction from the original 256-token grid), which is projected into the LLM embedding space and consumed alongside text to produce captions or VQA answers. For generation, the LLM autoregressively predicts discrete indices for both the global and local token streams, which are de-quantized via a shared VQ codebook and expanded by the autoregressive decompressor back to a dense 16×16 token grid before the image decoder reconstructs the final image. The compressed sequence is delimited by special tokens [IMG_BOS], [IMG_SEP], and [IMG_EOS], and the LLM interface remains a standard autoregressive sequence, making the compression transparent to the backbone.

## Method

UniCompress inserts three lightweight modules around an off-the-shelf discrete tokenizer: (1) a global token extractor that uses one-way cross-attention with N_g=4 learnable meta query tokens to summarize scene-level semantics from the full H×W token field; (2) a fixed-size average-pooling compressor that aggregates non-overlapping 2×2 spatial patches to reduce the local token grid by 4×; and (3) an autoregressive Transformer decompressor that expands the compact representation back to the original dense resolution, using masked self-attention over the generated prefix and cross-attention to both the global and local compressed tokens at every layer. The key insight is that global meta tokens act as semantic anchors during decompression, providing long-range structural constraints that allow the autoregressive decoder to restore fine-grained textures and boundaries that uniform pooling alone would destroy. Training is two-stage: first, the tokenizer plus all three modules are trained end-to-end with a reconstruction loss (token-level regression plus codebook consistency) while the LLM is frozen; second, the compressed tokenizer is frozen and the LLM is lightly fine-tuned on the shorter compressed sequences for both understanding and generation. This plug-in design requires no architectural modification to the language model and can be applied to diverse unified backbones (UniTok, VILA-U, VARGPT, UniFork, OpenUni, Bagel) without retraining from scratch.

## Results

UniCompress reduces visual tokens by 4× (256→64) across six unified model backbones while keeping understanding performance within ≤3 points on most benchmarks (e.g., UNITOK GQA: 55.71→53.07; VARGPT GQA: 58.12→55.90). On generation, FID increases range from 0.19 (UNITOK: 16.14→16.33) to 7.84 (OPENUNI: 16.45→24.29), with CLIP alignment drops of 1–5 points. Inference latency for generation drops by over 40% (e.g., UNITOK: 32.25→18.96 min; VILA-U: 30.15→17.55 min), and training time decreases by 10–15%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates UniCompress as a plug-in against the uncompressed version of each of six unified model backbones (UNITOK, VILA-U, VARGPT, UNIFORK, OPENUNI, BAGEL) rather than comparing across different architectures. On understanding, the compressed variants consistently stay within 1–4 points of their full-token counterparts; notably, UNITOK-COMPRESSED actually improves on MM-Bench (40.34→42.14) and VARGPT-COMPRESSED on MMMU (27.13→27.40), while the largest understanding drop is UNIFORK on GQA (54.20→50.10, −4.1 pts). On generation, UNITOK and VARGPT show the smallest FID degradation (+0.19 and +0.25 respectively), whereas OPENUNI suffers the largest drop (FID 16.45→24.29, +7.84) and BAGEL shows a moderate increase (12.73→17.22, +4.49). In terms of efficiency, all compressed variants reduce generation inference time by 35–42% (e.g., VILA-U: 30.15→17.55 min; VARGPT: 38.74→22.71 min) and training time by 10–15%, confirming that the 4× token reduction translates directly into wall-clock savings without architectural changes to the LLM backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniCompress builds on the meta-token compression idea from Harvill et al. (2025) and Jiang et al. (2024), adapting learnable global meta tokens to the visual domain, and applies it within the unified autoregressive vision-language model paradigm established by UniTok, VILA-U, VARGPT, UniFork, OpenUni, and Bagel. It departs from prior token-compression work (DivPrune, LVPruning, Dynamic-VLM), which targets understanding-only pruning via attention scores, and from generation-efficiency methods (MaskGIT, next-scale prediction), which are task-specific and incompatible with a shared tokenizer. The key conceptual departure is treating compression as a joint understanding-and-generation problem: the paper shows that naive downsampling degrades generation by >15% while barely affecting understanding, motivating the global-guided autoregressive decompressor as a necessary component absent from prior compression approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in, 4× token reduction with ≤3 pt understanding drop and ≤5 pt FID increase, combined with >40% generation inference speedup, offers a practical path to deploying unified multimodal models on resource-constrained hardware (embodied AI, edge devices) without full retraining. The modular design—compress once, reuse for both tasks—means the same mechanism can be grafted onto diverse backbones (pure autoregressive, diffusion-head, Y-shaped), broadening its applicability. Open questions include whether compression ratios beyond 4× remain viable (the ablation shows sharp generation degradation at 1/16), whether the approach extends to video token sequences, and whether the sensitivity gap observed in diffusion-based models (OpenUNI) can be closed with architecture-specific adaptations.
