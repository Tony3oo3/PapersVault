## Problem

Video harmonization—adjusting a foreground's lighting to match a new background—lacks paired training data because identical motions cannot be re-recorded under different illumination. Applying image harmonization frame-by-frame introduces severe temporal flickering, and existing video methods either fail to condition explicitly on the background, alter foreground/background identity, or are fragile to imperfect masks. No prior framework simultaneously achieves temporal coherence, identity preservation, mask robustness, and expressive relighting.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a foreground video with per-frame masks (extracted via Grounded-SAM-2), a target background video, and optionally a pseudo-alpha mask for boundary conditioning. It outputs a temporally coherent harmonized video in which the foreground's shadows, color tone, and illumination intensity are adapted to the background while preserving both foreground and background content. For sequences exceeding 85 frames, temporal MultiDiffusion extends the generation window; all processing occurs in the 3D-VAE latent space of a CogVideoX-based architecture.

## Method

HarmoVid employs a two-stage data pipeline followed by dual-path training of a 3D latent diffusion transformer (CogVideoX-based DiT). Stage 1 applies the image harmonization model HarmoIm frame-by-frame to composite foreground-background pairs, then a dedicated lighting deflickering network (same 3D DiT architecture) is trained to suppress both global color flicker and local shadow/highlight jitter that per-frame processing introduces. Stage 2 trains the final harmonization model on both real and deflickered synthetic videos via two complementary paths: Real→Synthetic (real foreground + synthetic background → synthetic target) preserves expressive relighting, while Synthetic→Real (synthetic foreground + real background → real target) enforces temporal coherence and physically consistent shading. A key design choice is asymmetric mask conditioning: binary masks are used on the synthetic path, while pseudo-alpha maps with smoothly decaying boundaries are used on the real path, enabling clean foreground-background transitions robust to imperfect segmentation.

## Results

HarmoVid achieves the best performance across all quantitative metrics in Table 1: PSNR of 17.91 dB (vs. 15.89 for the best baseline, Relightful Harmonization), SSIM of 0.9306, LPIPS of 0.0554, and RMSE of 0.1325 for spatial harmonization quality. For temporal consistency, it attains a CLIP Score of 0.9963 and Motion Preservation of 0.5264, outperforming all baselines. In the user study (33 participants, 30 samples), HarmoVid is preferred for temporal consistency (82%), identity preservation (78%), and overall harmonization (72%), substantially exceeding the next-best methods (56%, 58%, and 51% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HarmoVid outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest spatial-quality baseline, Relightful Harmonization [35] (PSNR 15.89, RMSE 0.1643), HarmoVid improves PSNR by ~2 dB and reduces RMSE by ~19%, while also achieving lower LPIPS (0.0554 vs. 0.0581). Among video-based methods, Light-A-Video [56] is the closest competitor on temporal metrics (Motion Preservation 0.5775 vs. 0.5264; CLIP Score 0.9955 vs. 0.9963), but HarmoVid still leads on all spatial metrics and by a wide margin in the user study (72% vs. 51% for harmonization preference). RelightVid [9] shows the best CLIP Score among baselines (0.9946) but lags significantly on spatial fidelity (PSNR 15.70, RMSE 0.1711) and user-study temporal consistency (35%). IC-Light [50], a frame-wise method, performs worst overall (PSNR 14.77, Motion Preservation 1.2928), confirming the temporal degradation expected from independent per-frame processing. The user-study gaps are particularly large: HarmoVid's 82% temporal-consistency preference exceeds the next-best 56% by 26 percentage points, and its 78% identity-preservation score exceeds the next-best 58% by 20 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CogVideoX 3D latent diffusion transformer as its architectural backbone and on the HarmoIm image harmonization model (CVPR 2024) as the per-frame relighting engine in its data pipeline. It departs from prior video harmonization methods (RelightVid, Light-A-Video, IC-Light) by introducing a dedicated lighting deflickering network as an intermediate training stage and by employing a dual-path (Real→Synthetic, Synthetic→Real) training strategy with asymmetric mask conditioning (binary for synthetic, pseudo-alpha for real). The key conceptual departure is treating temporal flickering in pseudo-paired data as a first-class problem to solve before harmonization learning, rather than relying on the diffusion model to implicitly handle noisy supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental data bottleneck in video harmonization—impossibility of capturing identical motions under varied lighting—by constructing a high-quality pseudo-paired dataset through a two-stage refinement pipeline, making the task tractable without real paired recordings. The finding that skipping the deflickering stage causes harmonization to be 'almost entirely absent' highlights how sensitive 3D diffusion transformers are to temporal noise in training data, a practical insight for anyone building video generation models on imperfect supervision. Limitations include training exclusively on portrait foregrounds (though generalization to non-portrait objects is demonstrated), reliance on a specific image harmonization model for Stage 1, and evaluation on relatively short sequences (100 frames). Open questions include whether the deflickering insight generalizes to other video editing tasks with noisy paired data, and whether the dual-path strategy can be extended to multi-object or scene-level harmonization.
