## Problem

Existing VLM safety defenses either rely on computationally expensive adversarial training and input preprocessing, or use heuristic single-modality activation steering that distorts general representations without principled localization of unsafe components. There is no training-free framework that precisely identifies which internal channels causally drive unsafe behavior and repairs them while preserving the model's multimodal reasoning capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260630/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images paired with text prompts fed into VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, LLaVA-OneVision-8B). During inference, it intercepts FFN layer activations at specific mid-network layers (layers 16–18 for LLaVA, layers 12–14 for Qwen), applies a learned projection operator, and writes the corrected activations back into the residual stream before the model continues generating its text response. The entire intervention is a single matrix projection per targeted layer, adding negligible latency and requiring no gradient computation or weight updates.

## Method

CARE comprises two phases. First, a causal diagnosis phase uses layer-wise blocking and component-wise ablation (FFN vs. MHSA) combined with clustering metrics (Silhouette, Class Separation, Mahalanobis distance) to pinpoint that FFN outputs in mid-layers are the primary causal mediators of unsafe behavior. Second, a dual-modal repair phase constructs safety subspaces: RBF kernels compute cross-modal (visual–textual) and self-modal (textual) token attribution scores to select the most safety-relevant activations, then a generalized eigen-decomposition between benign and malicious covariance matrices yields the top-k harmful directions. At inference, FFN activations are projected onto the orthogonal complement of these harmful directions with a benign-activation regularization term, and an adaptive fusion weight dynamically balances the visual and textual corrections before reinserting the fused activation into the model's forward pass.

## Results

On JailBreakVBench, CARE reduces the attack success rate (ASR) from 45.71% (original LLaVA-OneVision) to 7.03%, and from 20.00% (original Qwen2.5-VL) to 6.55%. On MM-SafetyBench, ASR drops to 9.13% (LLaVA) and 8.72% (Qwen). Under the strongest PGD adversarial perturbation (κ=64/255), CARE achieves ASR of 12.78% (PGD-Toxic) and 8.46% (PGD-Jailbreak) on LLaVA, and 4.60% and 10.68% on Qwen, compared to 60–73% for the unprotected models. General capability is largely preserved, with only 2–8% drops on MMBench, MM-Vet, and SQA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CARE outperforms all four baselines on the majority of evaluation settings. Against SPO-VLM (activation steering + preference optimization), CARE achieves a 32.2% relative ASR reduction on JailBreakV for LLaVA (7.03 vs. 10.37) and a 44.0% reduction on MM-Safety for Qwen (8.72 vs. 15.57 for ASTRA). Against ASTRA (adaptive steering), CARE is superior on nearly all benchmarks, with the sole exception being Qwen PGD-Toxic at κ=64/255 where ASTRA reaches 3.38% versus CARE's 4.60%. JailGuard (prompt-level detection) and Refusal Pairs (contrastive activation addition) are consistently the weakest baselines, with ASR values 2–5× higher than CARE across both model backbones. The gap is most pronounced under strong PGD perturbations, where CARE maintains ASR below 15% while detection-based methods (JailGuard, Refusal Pairs) remain above 30–50%.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARE extends the causal tracing and activation patching methodology from Meng et al. (2022) to the VLM safety domain, repurposing neuron-level causal mediation analysis to localize safety-critical components without retraining. It departs from recent activation-steering defenses (ASTRA, SPO-VLM) that apply single-modality, heuristic linear steering vectors by instead constructing a dual-modal safety subspace via generalized eigen-decomposition and projecting onto its orthogonal complement. The use of RBF kernels for cross-modal token attribution and the FFN-vs-MHSA functional dissection represent methodological departures from prior work that treated VLM safety as a purely textual or single-pathway problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, inference-time nature of CARE makes it practically deployable without the compute cost of adversarial training or the latency of multi-pass response detection, and the causal localization provides interpretability that black-box steering methods lack. However, the defense is static (fixed subspace per model) and has only been validated on 7B–8B parameter VLMs, leaving open whether the identified mid-layer FFN safety subspaces persist in larger or architecturally different models. The paper also notes the trade-off between safety projection and fine-grained cross-modal reasoning (visible in MM-Vet drops), and future work on adaptive, input-dependent safety control and dynamic safety reasoning is explicitly flagged as needed.
