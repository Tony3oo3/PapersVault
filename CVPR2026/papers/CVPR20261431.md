## Problem

Existing active 3D Gaussian reconstruction methods suffer from two coupled failures: NBV selection metrics that quantify geometric coverage without distinguishing photorealistic from poorly rendered viewpoints, and sparse view configurations that induce floater artifacts—spurious Gaussians exploiting depth ambiguities—which severely degrade novel-view synthesis. No prior work jointly addresses rendering-aware viewpoint optimization and principled floater suppression within a unified active reconstruction pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method consumes posed RGB-D observations (60° vertical, 90° horizontal FOV) from a Habitat simulator agent, processing them incrementally at each planning iteration to maintain a global 3D Gaussian map and a dynamic candidate keyframe database with spatial pruning. It outputs a refined 3D Gaussian scene representation, the selected next-best-view camera pose at each step, and novel-view renderings evaluated at 2000 frames per sequence. The pipeline alternates between exploration (NBV selection via GL-Graph) and refinement (revisiting high-discrepancy keyframes) phases within a fixed frame budget.

## Method

ActivePolicy formulates NBV selection as graph stability optimization: a GL-Graph with a virtual global node and candidate view nodes encodes geometric uncertainty (via Riemannian depth manifold fusion of α-Depth and C-Depth using geodesic interpolation with Fisher precision weighting), rendering quality (via spatial-aware spectral analysis partitioning rendered images into adaptive blocks scored by high-frequency energy, directional anisotropy, and boundary importance), and inter-view redundancy (via depth reprojection overlap modulated by normalized cross-correlation and spatial proximity). The NBV is selected as the node with minimum Fiedler vector magnitude in the normalized Laplacian eigendecomposition, yielding globally-aware selection that balances all three objectives through a single spectral criterion. For floater suppression, 4D-Reg computes pairwise geodesic divergence across three depth variants in Riemannian space to identify spurious Gaussians, then applies confidence-weighted stochastic dropout with opacity compensation for retained floaters and gradient enhancement for stable surface Gaussians, enabling the optimizer to naturally redistribute photometric responsibility without the rendering collapse caused by direct removal.

## Results

On the Replica dataset, ActivePolicy achieves 1.03 cm accuracy, 1.28 cm completion, 98.04% coverage ratio, 31.89 dB PSNR, 0.97 SSIM, and 0.12 LPIPS — the best values across all metrics among all compared methods. On Matterport3D, it reaches 1.42 cm accuracy, 1.82 cm completion, 96.42% coverage, and 26.86 dB PSNR, outperforming the strongest active baseline ActiveGAMER by 12.9% in accuracy and 5.7% in PSNR. The method simultaneously surpasses dense passive baselines (MonoGS, SplaTAM) in rendering quality while achieving substantially higher coverage, demonstrating that rendering-aware NBV selection with 4D-Reg mitigates sparse-view overfitting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActivePolicy outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest active Gaussian method ActiveGAMER [4], it improves accuracy from 1.28 to 1.03 cm (19.5% reduction), completion from 1.74 to 1.28 cm, coverage from 96.59% to 98.04%, and PSNR from 30.99 to 31.89 dB. Versus the passive SLAM baselines SplaTAM [24] and MonoGS [34], ActivePolicy achieves comparable or better PSNR (31.89 vs 27.33 and 29.28 dB) while dramatically improving coverage (98.04% vs 85.17% and 73.20%) and completion (1.28 vs 5.38 and 6.98 cm), showing that active exploration with rendering-aware selection resolves the coverage–fidelity trade-off. Against NARUTO [10] and ActiveSplat [33], which focus on geometric coverage, ActivePolicy achieves higher PSNR (31.89 vs ~26 and 24.72 dB) and lower LPIPS (0.12 vs 0.28 and 0.17), confirming that spatial-aware spectral analysis and 4D-Reg effectively suppress floater-induced rendering degradation. On the more challenging Matterport3D dataset, the same pattern holds: ActivePolicy leads ActiveGAMER by 12.9% in accuracy and 28.3% in completion while maintaining 96.42% coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the scene representation and SplaTAM's Gaussian initialization for incremental mapping, but departs fundamentally from prior active reconstruction methods (ActiveGAMER, NARUTO, ActiveSplat, GS-Planner) in two ways. First, it reframes NBV selection from greedy per-view information gain maximization to a graph spectral stability problem (GL-Graph with Fiedler vector analysis), unifying geometric uncertainty, rendering quality, and inter-view redundancy into a single structural criterion. Second, it introduces Riemannian manifold geometry for depth fusion and floater detection, treating depth as a positive-real manifold with hyperbolic metric rather than a scalar quantity, which is a conceptual departure from the variance-based or single-estimator approaches in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine tension in active 3D reconstruction: methods that maximize geometric coverage tend to sacrifice rendering quality, and sparse-view configurations are vulnerable to floater-induced overfitting. The graph-spectral formulation offers an elegant unification that could be adopted beyond this specific pipeline, and the 4D-Reg stochastic dropout provides a practical mechanism for floater suppression without the rendering collapse that direct removal causes. However, the evaluation is limited to two indoor datasets (Replica, MP3D) with relatively small scene counts, and the ablation showing complete exploration failure without GL-Graph raises questions about robustness in more complex or larger-scale environments. Open questions include whether the Riemannian depth fusion provides meaningful gains over simpler multi-depth heuristics at scale, and whether the spectral NBV formulation generalizes to outdoor or large-scale scenes where the candidate graph becomes much denser.
