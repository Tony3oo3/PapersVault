## Problem

Existing AIGC video detection datasets are built primarily on open-source generative models whose outputs are visually distinguishable from real footage, causing detectors to overfit low-quality artifacts and fail on high-fidelity commercial AIGC videos. Even datasets containing a few commercial samples often retain visible watermarks, further compromising authenticity. This gap leaves the community without a reliable benchmark for evaluating detection under realistic, watermark-free commercial generation conditions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes ~5-second RGB video clips (16 frames at 24–30 FPS) along with their associated text descriptions; the CoCoVideo dataset provides semantically aligned real-fake pairs that share an identical first frame and prompt. The base network outputs a scalar confidence score in [0,1] (higher = more likely real). For samples falling in the uncertainty band (1−τ < p < τ, with τ=0.9), the MLLM additionally produces a structured JSON containing a binary prediction, a semantic confidence score, and a natural-language reasoning explanation, which are then fused with the base score to yield the final decision.

## Method

CoCoDetect uses an R3D-18 spatio-temporal backbone feeding two parallel heads: a 3-layer MLP confidence head (sigmoid output for real/fake classification) and a 2-layer MLP projection head (L2-normalized 128-dim embeddings). Training combines binary cross-entropy with a paired contrastive loss that enforces separation only between semantically aligned real-fake pairs (same first frame and text prompt) rather than imposing global intra-class compactness, avoiding counterproductive clustering across diverse scenes. At inference, a confidence-gated mechanism routes uncertain samples to LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B, which reasons about physical plausibility and scene coherence from sampled key frames. The MLLM's confidence is mapped into the base model's semantic space and fused via an adaptive weight u = √(2|p̃−0.5|), giving greater influence to the MLLM when its own confidence is high while preserving robustness when it is uncertain.

## Results

On the CoCoVideo-26K test set, CoCoDetect achieves 90.69% accuracy, 90.62% F1, 89.95% recall, and 95.93% AUC, outperforming the second-best method DeMamba (82.59% Acc, 91.03% AUC) by over 8 points in accuracy. In cross-dataset generalization, CoCoDetect leads on four of five public benchmarks (GVD 71.49%, GVF 74.56%, GenVideo 57.04%, GenVidBench 64.78%), though it trails slightly on GenBuster (64.80% vs. VideoMAE's 68.20%). The ablation confirms each component contributes: removing the MLLM drops accuracy from 90.69% to 88.92%, and removing the projection head drops it further to 81.05%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoCoDetect outperforms all five baselines on the CoCoVideo benchmark across all four metrics, with the largest margins over D3 (training-free, ~42 points lower accuracy) and TALL (~20 points lower). Against the strongest baseline DeMamba, CoCoDetect gains +8.10 Acc, +7.13 F1, and +4.90 AUC, while DeMamba retains a slight edge in recall (88.05% vs. 89.95% — actually CoCoDetect wins here too at 89.95%). In cross-dataset evaluation, CoCoDetect dominates on GVD (+47.38 Acc over DeMamba), GVF (+47.65), GenVideo (+8.87), and GenVidBench (+32.16), but underperforms VideoMAE on GenBuster (64.80% vs. 68.20% Acc), which the authors attribute to GenBuster using earlier model versions where generation-specific artifacts are more exploitable. The D3 training-free method performs near-random on CoCoVideo (48.95% Acc), confirming that high-fidelity commercial AIGC videos cannot be detected without learned representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the AIGC video detection line in two complementary directions. On the data side, it departs from prior benchmarks (GenBuster, GenVidBench, GVD, GenVideo) that rely predominantly on open-source generators by constructing a contrastive dataset from 13 commercial models with semantically aligned real-fake pairs, addressing the quality gap that limits generalization. On the method side, it repurposes the standard dual-head contrastive learning paradigm (backbone + confidence head + projection head) for paired video discrimination and introduces a confidence-gated routing mechanism that escalates uncertain predictions to an MLLM (LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B) for physical-plausibility reasoning, positioning the MLLM as a fallback rather than the primary detector as in prior MLLM-based detection work (BusterX, FakeGPT, SIDA).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CoCoVideo-26K benchmark fills a practical gap: existing AIGC detection datasets are dominated by open-source model outputs that are visually distinguishable from real videos, causing detectors to overfit to low-quality artifacts and fail on high-fidelity commercial content. The confidence-gated MLLM design is a pragmatic architectural choice that limits expensive reasoning to uncertain cases, though the ablation shows the MLLM contributes only ~1.8% accuracy over the dual-head baseline, suggesting the semantic-reasoning pathway is currently a modest rather than transformative addition. Open questions include whether the approach scales to audio-visual AIGC, whether performance degrades further as commercial models improve, and whether the paired-contrastive objective is genuinely superior to standard supervised contrastive learning for this task.
