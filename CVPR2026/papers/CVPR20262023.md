## Problem

Visual encoding (image → fMRI) and decoding (fMRI → image) have been treated as separate tasks requiring distinct models and training procedures, and prior attempts to link them still rely on two independent networks without a shared latent space. This separation is inefficient and fails to enforce the consistency that synthetic neural signals should be transformable back into coherent images. The paper asks how both directions can be unified within a single model that guarantees encoding-decoding consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262023/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images (COCO, 9,000 train / 1,000 test per subject) and 7T fMRI BOLD signals (NSD, 4 subjects, ~40 hours of scanning, 3 trials per test image to capture neural variability). In encoding mode it produces synthetic fMRI voxel signals; in decoding mode it produces reconstructed RGB images via a frozen UnCLIP decoder. The two directions are distinguished solely by the sign of the temporal step in the ODE solver, so a single trained model handles both without architectural switching.

## Method

NeuroFlow integrates two components: (1) NeuroVAE, a variational fMRI encoder-decoder that models neural variability via a KL-regularized posterior, aligns the neural latent space to CLIP visual semantics through SoftCLIP contrastive loss, and enforces cycle-consistency so synthetic fMRI preserves semantic content rather than overfitting voxel noise; a compact latent z_c decouples the encoding pathway from decoding. (2) Cross-Modal Flow Matching (XFM), which learns a time-dependent vector field (parameterized by a SiT transformer) that transports samples directly between the empirical visual and neural latent distributions via cosine interpolation, bypassing the standard noise-to-data diffusion paradigm. Encoding corresponds to integrating the ODE forward (z_v → z_n) and decoding to integrating backward (z_n → z_v), so reversibility and consistency are enforced by the uniqueness of ODE solutions rather than by separate training objectives. The visual backbone (CLIP encoder + UnCLIP decoder) is kept frozen, and the entire trainable model comprises ~660M parameters trained in under 5 hours on a single A100 GPU.

## Results

On the NSD dataset, NeuroFlow achieves the best decoding performance with 95.6% Inception Score and 94.2% CLIP similarity, surpassing MindEye2 (95.4%, 93.0%) and MindEye (94.6%, 93.3%). For encoding-to-decoding consistency, NeuroFlow reaches 98.6% Incep and 98.7% CLIP, significantly outperforming the best encoding-only baselines SynBrain (95.7%, 94.3%) and MindSimulator (93.1%, 91.2%). NeuroFlow also achieves the best synthetic retrieval accuracy (97.0%) and the lowest encoding Eff distance (0.590) and SwAV distance (0.341), while using only 660M parameters—approximately 25% of MindEye2's 2.60B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroFlow outperforms all five baselines on the unified encoding-to-decoding task, achieving 98.6% Incep and 98.7% CLIP versus SynBrain's 95.7%/94.3% and MindSimulator's 93.1%/91.2%, with the largest gap (≈3–5 points) over MindSimulator. On pure decoding, NeuroFlow edges out MindEye2 by 0.2% Incep and 1.2% CLIP, and surpasses MindEye by 1.0% Incep and 0.9% CLIP, though it trails MindEye2 on Eff (0.674 vs. 0.618) and SwAV (0.364 vs. 0.344) distances. For raw fMRI retrieval, NeuroFlow (80.6%) underperforms MindEye2 (98.8%) and MindEye (90.0%), but its synthetic-signal retrieval (97.0%) exceeds both, indicating that the model distills more semantically coherent neural representations. In terms of parameter efficiency, NeuroFlow (660M, no pretraining) uses roughly 25% of MindEye2's parameters (2.60B, pretrained) and 66% of MindEye's (1.00B), while matching or exceeding their decoding performance. Overall, NeuroFlow is the only method evaluated on both encoding and decoding simultaneously, and it leads on every metric where both directions are reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeuroFlow extends SynBrain's variational fMRI synthesis backbone (NeuroVAE) from encoding-only to bidirectional modeling by introducing a compact latent bottleneck and cycle-consistency loss, while replacing the standard noise-to-data diffusion paradigm with Cross-Modal Flow Matching (XFM) that learns a direct, reversible vector field between visual and neural latent distributions. This departs from prior unified encoding-decoding attempts (Beliy et al., Gaziv et al., Qian et al.) that relied on two separate networks or independent linear regressions, and from diffusion-based cross-modal methods (MindEye, MindEye2) that condition Gaussian noise on one modality. The flow matching formulation, grounded in Lipman et al. (ICLR 2023), is repurposed here to transport between two empirical distributions rather than from noise to data, making encoding and decoding literal reversals of the same ODE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NeuroFlow provides the first principled, single-model formulation where encoding-decoding consistency is enforced by construction (ODE reversibility) rather than by separate training objectives, and it does so with roughly 25% of the parameters of the strongest decoding baseline. The brain functional analysis showing suppression of early visual cortex and enhancement of category-selective regions (FFA, EBA, PPA) lends biological interpretability to the learned representations. Open questions include generalization beyond fMRI to higher-temporal-resolution modalities (EEG, MEG), cross-subject transfer, and whether the reversible flow formulation scales to video or multi-region whole-brain encoding. The evaluation is limited to four NSD subjects and a single GPU, so robustness across subjects and datasets remains to be established.
