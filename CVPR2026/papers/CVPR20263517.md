## Problem

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) remain vulnerable to jailbreak attacks that exploit adversarial perturbations in image, text, or both modalities, yet existing defenses—external inference-time detectors and safety fine-tuning—fail under white-box adversarial scenarios. No prior work has applied adversarial training directly to MLLMs, and straightforward extensions of LLM-based adversarial training are impractical due to the large number of image tokens (e.g., 576 in LLaVA-1.5) and the open-ended generation nature of the task.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image paired with a text query and produces a free-form text response. During adversarial training, perturbations are not applied to pixel values or individual text tokens but are injected as two compact token embedding matrices (P_0^h placed before the query to simulate an adversarial image, and P_0^t placed after the query to simulate an adversarial suffix), each with only K=8 tokens of dimension C, drastically reducing the perturbation parameter count compared to 576 image tokens. The visual encoder parameters remain frozen throughout training; only the cross-modal adapter and LLM decoder are updated via LoRA.

## Method

SAFEMLLM is an iterative adversarial training framework with two alternating steps. In Step I (CoE-Attack), the model parameters are frozen while two token embedding matrices are optimized via gradient descent for M steps to maximize the likelihood of generating an affirmative response to a toxic query and simultaneously minimize the likelihood of a safety refusal through a contrastive sigmoid loss, yielding the worst-case adversarial embeddings {P_M^h, P_M^t}. In Step II, the model's LoRA parameters are updated to maximize the probability of the safety response under the fixed adversarial embeddings (defense loss) while a utility loss on benign image-question pairs prevents over-refusal on normal inputs. The key design insight is that token-embedding-level perturbations unify image and text attack modalities into a single compact optimization problem, achieving comparable or better attack strength than pixel-level or latent-representation perturbations at roughly 3–7× lower runtime and memory cost.

## Results

SAFEMLLM achieves near-zero average ASR across all six jailbreak attacks: 0.00% on GCG, 1.33% on AutoDAN, 1.25% on VAA, 1.00% on FigStep, 2.27% on MM-SafetyBench, and 5.17% on ImgJP, compared to 33.83%, 66.67%, 32.92%, 38.00%, 26.62%, and 51.33% for the original (unprotected) models respectively. On the strongest text-based attack (AutoDAN), SAFEMLLM reduces ASR from 66.67% (original) to 1.33% on average. Utility on MM-Vet is preserved, with the ablation showing that removing the utility loss drops LLaVA-1.5-13B scores from 37.8 to 21.6, confirming that SAFEMLLM maintains benign interaction quality while defending against attacks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAFEMLLM outperforms both LLM-based adversarial training baselines (R2D2 and CAT) on all six attacks. The gap is most dramatic on AutoDAN, where SAFEMLLM achieves 1.33% average ASR versus 32.33% (R2D2) and 22.33% (CAT), and on GCG where SAFEMLLM reaches 0.00% versus 15.83% and 4.83% respectively. Against the safety-fine-tuning baseline VLGuard, SAFEMLLM dominates on all four white-box attacks (e.g., ImgJP: 6%/0% vs. 88%/36% on LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B; GCG: 0%/0% vs. 79%/26%), while both methods achieve 0% on the two black-box attacks (FigStep, MM-SafetyBench). In the efficiency comparison, SAFEMLLM's token-embedding perturbation approach runs in 20.73 s/iteration on LLaVA-1.5-7B versus 55.74 s for LAT and 84.42 s for direct image perturbation, while achieving comparable or better ASR (6% vs. 10% and 5% respectively), validating the design choice of optimizing only 8 perturbation tokens instead of 576 image tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAFEMLLM extends the adversarial training paradigm from LLMs (R2D2, CAT, LAT) to the multimodal setting, but departs from prior work in two key ways: it replaces modality-specific perturbations (pixel-level image noise or text suffixes) with a unified 8-token embedding perturbation that simultaneously simulates both image and text attacks, and it introduces a contrastive loss that suppresses refusal responses relative to affirmative ones rather than simply maximizing target likelihood. Conceptually, it also contrasts with safety fine-tuning approaches like VLGuard, which the paper shows are vulnerable to white-box adversarial perturbations, positioning adversarial training as a fundamentally more robust defense mechanism. The use of LoRA for parameter-efficient updates and the iterative attack-then-defend loop follow standard adversarial training practice, but the contrastive embedding attack (CoE-Attack) and the dual perturbation placement (before and after the query) are novel design choices specific to the MLLM architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first adversarial training framework for MLLMs, filling a clear gap between LLM adversarial training and the multimodal safety literature. The practical efficiency argument—using 8 token embeddings instead of 576 image tokens—makes the approach significantly more deployable than naive image-perturbation alternatives, and the near-zero ASR against white-box attacks (GCG, AutoDAN) demonstrates a level of robustness that safety fine-tuning alone cannot achieve. Open questions include whether the defense generalizes to unseen attack families (e.g., multi-turn or video-based jailbreaks), whether the white-box assumption (attacker has gradient access) is realistic in deployment, and whether the approach scales to larger or video-capable MLLMs without prohibitive compute costs.
