## Problem

Diffusion-based text-to-image models still fail to accurately follow complex compositional prompts involving attribute bindings, spatial relationships, and object counting. While Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has shown promise for aligning generative models with human preferences, its application to compositional T2I generation remains unexplored, and existing preference alignment methods for diffusion models focus on overall image quality rather than fine-grained compositional fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261778/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a standard text prompt and produces a single generated image, with no additional inputs (no layouts, scene graphs, or external tools) required. The base model is Stable Diffusion XL (or SD3-Medium for MMDiT experiments), fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 8) for only 200 steps with an effective batch size of 2048 on 4×H100 GPUs (~13 hours). Training consumes preference pairs consisting of image-caption pairs with minimal visual differences, drawn from the constructed BiCOMP dataset (53k samples) combined with 12k real-world samples from VisMin.

## Method

BiDPO extends Diffusion DPO to a bimodal formulation by introducing TextDPO, which keeps the preferred image fixed and contrasts it against two different captions (preferred vs. less preferred), thereby learning text-side preference. BiDPO then combines two such TextDPO losses over a preference pair (x_w, y_w) and (x_l, y_l), implicitly achieving image-to-image contrastive learning through two explicit text-to-text contrastive processes. A region-level guidance mechanism multiplies the loss element-wise with a mask derived from bounding boxes of edited objects, assigning higher weight (1.0) to relevant regions and lower weight (0.5) to external regions to focus fine-grained alignment. The training data is constructed via a fully automated pipeline that generates images with Flux.1-dev, regenerates captions using Grounding DINO + SAM2 + Qwen2.5-VL, edits captions and images with Qwen-Image-Edit, and applies VQA-based filtering, yielding the BiCOMP dataset covering six compositional dimensions.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench, SDXL-BiDPO achieves 79.35 (Color), 60.47 (Shape), and 71.36 (Texture) for attribute binding, representing a ~17% average improvement over the SDXL baseline (58.90/46.90/53.13), and 23.41/32.29 for spatial/non-spatial relationships. On GenEval, the overall score rises from 0.53 to 0.62, surpassing DALL-E 3 (0.67) and FLUX (0.66) in single-object and color sub-tasks. On DPG-Bench, the overall score improves from 73.38 to 78.84, and on GenEval 2, Soft-TIFA-AM increases from 50.1 to 56.7. Visual aesthetic quality (HPSv2 on DrawBench) also improves by 2.65% (30.22 → 32.87), confirming that compositional gains do not come at the cost of image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDXL-BiDPO outperforms the SDXL baseline by large margins on T2I-CompBench attribute binding (e.g., +20.4 on Color, +13.6 on Shape, +18.2 on Texture) and modestly on spatial/non-spatial relationships (+2.2/+1.1). It surpasses layout-conditioned methods (GLIGEN, LMD+, InstanceDiffusion) and attention-guidance methods (Attn-Exct v2) across all five categories while requiring only text prompts. Against stronger baselines like GenTron (76.74 Color) and EVOGEN (72.34 Texture), BiDPO leads in Color (79.35) and Shape (60.47) but trails slightly in Texture (71.36 vs. 72.34). On GenEval, BiDPO (0.62 overall) surpasses DALL-E 3 and FLUX in single-object (1.00 vs. 0.96/0.98) and colors (0.88 vs. 0.83/0.79) sub-tasks, though it remains behind in position (0.19 vs. 0.43/0.22). On DPG-Bench, BiDPO (78.84) narrows the gap to DALL-E 3 (83.50) and SD3-medium (84.08) but does not close it. When extended to the MMDiT architecture, SD3-Medium-BiDPO outperforms Flux on GenEval 2 compositionality at higher atomicity levels (e.g., 27.6 vs. 26.0 at 6 objects, 14.2 vs. 4.0 at 7 objects), demonstrating model-agnostic generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Diffusion DPO (Wallace et al., CVPR 2024) from image-only contrastive preference learning to a bimodal formulation that jointly optimizes over both image and text modalities. The key conceptual departure is the TextDPO loss, where the 'less preferred' sample is constructed as the preferred image paired with the less preferred caption, so that two such text-preference losses implicitly encode image-to-image contrastive learning. The paper also introduces a region-level masking mechanism during loss computation, which has no direct analogue in prior diffusion DPO work, and builds an automated data pipeline (BiCOMP) that leverages VLMs, detection, and segmentation to produce preference pairs at scale—departing from the smaller, manually curated datasets used in earlier preference-alignment studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BiDPO demonstrates that a lightweight post-training procedure (LoRA fine-tuning for 200 steps on 4×H100) can substantially improve compositional fidelity without requiring external structural inputs like layouts or scene graphs, making it broadly applicable to any pre-trained diffusion model. The ablation revealing that TextDPO alone catastrophically degrades generation quality (13.48 vs. 43.57 on T2I-CompBench) is a useful cautionary result for the preference-optimization community. However, gains in spatial relationship understanding remain modest (+2.2%), and the method's reliance on a carefully constructed, VLM-dependent data pipeline raises questions about scalability to more complex or open-world compositions. The authors note future work on extending the approach to autoregressive T2I models, leaving open whether the bimodal DPO formulation transfers beyond diffusion architectures.
