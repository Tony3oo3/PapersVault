## Problem

Existing 3D open-vocabulary segmentation methods handle explicit queries (e.g., 'segment the cup') but fail on implicit, reasoning-based instructions that require spatial relationships, commonsense, or attribute inference (e.g., 'segment the object between the lamp and the book'). Meanwhile, MLLMs excel at such 2D visual reasoning but lack intrinsic 3D spatial grounding, and feeding a single rendered view to an MLLM makes results highly sensitive to viewpoint selection.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-reconstructed 3DGS scene (with training camera poses) and a free-form text prompt—either explicit or implicit/reasoning-based—and produces a per-Gaussian 3D segmentation mask identifying the target instance. Downstream, the segmented mask enables 3D editing operations (object removal, replacement, style transfer) that modify only the target while preserving occlusion relationships with surrounding geometry. The pipeline renders photorealistic novel views from the 3DGS at arbitrary viewpoints, so no additional 2D annotations are needed at inference time.

## Method

REALM constructs a 3D feature field that assigns a learnable identity feature to each Gaussian primitive, supervised by SAM-derived 2D masks propagated across views via a temporal tracking model, with a lightweight classifier mapping rendered features to instance IDs. An MLLM-based Instance Segmenter (LMSeg) takes one rendered view and a text query, prompting Qwen2.5-VL to output a 2D bounding box, object category, and rationale; the box is fed to SAM for a binary mask, which is intersected with the 3D feature field's predicted instance map to recover the target Gaussian identity. To overcome viewpoint sensitivity, the Global-to-Local Spatial Grounding (GLSpaG) strategy first clusters training cameras via K-means, selects the top-K views observing the most distinct instances, runs LMSeg in parallel on each, and aggregates predictions by majority voting to obtain a coarse 3D mask. A local refinement stage then selects views where the target is visible, generates fine-grained 2D masks via LMSeg, and optimizes the 3D mask through a differentiable rasterization loss over ~50 iterations, yielding boundary-accurate segmentation without overfitting.

## Results

On implicit (reasoning-based) queries, REALM achieves mIoU of 92.88% on LERF, 93.68% on 3D-OVS, and 82.30% on the newly introduced REALM3D benchmark, substantially outperforming all baselines. The corresponding mBIoU scores are 90.12%, 86.02%, and 70.37% respectively. The largest margin is on LERF, where REALM exceeds the best baseline (Gaga, 44.82% mIoU) by nearly 48 percentage points. On REALM3D, REALM still leads the strongest baseline (GS-Group, 65.55% mIoU) by 16.75 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REALM decisively outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and metric. On LERF, REALM (92.88% mIoU) surpasses Gaga (44.82%) by 48.06 points and GS-Group (42.43%) by 50.45 points; GAGS performs worst on this dataset (17.84%). On 3D-OVS, REALM (93.68%) leads GAGS (58.46%) by 35.22 points and GS-Group (41.79%) by 51.89 points. On the new REALM3D benchmark, the gap narrows somewhat but remains substantial: REALM (82.30%) beats GS-Group (65.55%) by 16.75 points and Gaga (58.56%) by 23.74 points. The paper attributes the baselines' failures to their inability to perform reasoning over implicit instructions—they rely on CLIP or Grounded-SAM for explicit keyword matching and cannot resolve spatial-relationship or commonsense queries. Notably, GAGS is the strongest baseline on 3D-OVS but the weakest on LERF, indicating inconsistent cross-dataset generalization among existing methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

REALM sits at the intersection of two research threads: 3DGS-based open-vocabulary segmentation (Gaussian Grouping, GAGA, GAGS, LangSplat), which handles explicit queries via CLIP or Grounded-SAM but cannot interpret implicit, reasoning-based instructions, and MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, GPT-4V, LISA), which excel at 2D visual reasoning but lack 3D spatial grounding. The paper departs from prior 3D segmentation work by replacing CLIP-based language understanding with an MLLM agent that reasons over rendered 3DGS views, and from prior MLLM grounding work (ScanReason, ReasonGrounder) by producing fine-grained 3D masks rather than bounding boxes and by avoiding reliance on a single top-down viewpoint. The Global-to-Local Spatial Grounding strategy is a novel aggregation mechanism specifically designed to mitigate the viewpoint sensitivity that arises when feeding rendered views to an MLLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REALM demonstrates that off-the-shelf MLLM reasoning can be effectively lifted into the 3D domain without 3D-specific post-training, offering a practical pathway for language-driven 3D scene understanding and editing in robotics and human-AI interaction. The introduction of REALM3D (100+ scenes, 1444 implicit prompt-mask pairs) addresses a critical evaluation gap, as prior benchmarks only test explicit queries. Open questions include scalability to large-scale or dynamic scenes, the cost of repeated MLLM calls in latency-sensitive settings, and whether the framework generalizes to object categories or spatial relationships not seen during MLLM pre-training. The reliance on a pre-optimized 3DGS and a trained feature field classifier also limits applicability to scenes where such representations are already available.
