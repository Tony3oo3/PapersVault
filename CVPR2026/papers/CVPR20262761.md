## Problem

Existing 3DGS-SLAM systems apply uniform photometric weighting during pose estimation, leaving tracking and global alignment vulnerable to drift in low-texture regions, transparent surfaces, and areas with complex reflectance. While prior work has modeled geometric depth uncertainty or relied on pretrained predictors, appearance uncertainty that directly reflects instability in 3DGS rendering has not been treated as a first-class, learned quantity in online dense SLAM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262761/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a streaming RGB-D image sequence and operates in a submap-based pipeline where each submap covers a limited spatial region. Beyond standard camera poses and a 3DGS map, VarSplat produces a differentiable per-pixel uncertainty map V rendered in the same single-pass rasterization as color and depth, plus per-splat appearance variance σ² stored as an additional parameter on each Gaussian. The final global map is merged via TSDF fusion and refined for appearance, yielding a 3D mesh for reconstruction evaluation.

## Method

VarSplat augments each 3D Gaussian with a per-splat appearance variance σ² (distinct from spatial covariance and SH mean color) and derives a closed-form per-pixel uncertainty V by applying the law of total variance through alpha compositing: V = Σ wᵢ(σᵢ² + cᵢ²) − (Σ wᵢcᵢ)², computed in the same single-pass rasterizer as color and depth. During mapping, σ² is trained end-to-end with a Gaussian negative-log-likelihood loss (MSE residual divided by V plus log V) that jointly supervises from both color and depth residuals, so variance adapts to measurement reliability without pretrained predictors. The rendered V is then used as a confidence weight in tracking (reweighting photometric loss) and submap registration, while per-splat σ² modulates loop-detection similarity via an opacity-ratio score, giving uncertainty-aware robustness at local, mid-range, and long-range scales.

## Results

On ScanNet (6 scenes), VarSplat achieves the best average ATE RMSE of 6.5 cm in tracking, outperforming the best NeRF baseline GO-SLAM (6.8 cm) and the best 3DGS baseline LoopSplat (7.7 cm). On ScanNet++, VarSplat improves ATE RMSE by ~18% over the second-best method (1.69 cm vs. LoopSplat's 2.05 cm) and avoids the catastrophic failure of SplaTAM (443.10 cm on scene d). For rendering on ScanNet, VarSplat matches LoopSplat on PSNR (24.92 dB) while slightly improving SSIM (0.848 vs. 0.845) and LPIPS (0.422 vs. 0.425), and achieves the best novel view synthesis PSNR on ScanNet++ (21.33 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VarSplat outperforms all 14 baselines on average tracking ATE RMSE across all four datasets. Among 3DGS methods, it beats LoopSplat (its closest competitor) by 0.13 cm on Replica (0.23 vs. 0.26), 0.36 cm on ScanNet++ (1.69 vs. 2.05), 0.13 cm on TUM-RGBD (3.20 vs. 3.33), and 1.2 cm on ScanNet (6.5 vs. 7.7). Against the other uncertainty-aware 3DGS method CG-SLAM, VarSplat improves by 0.04 cm on Replica, 0.8 cm on TUM-RGBD, and 1.6 cm on ScanNet. Among NeRF baselines, VarSplat surpasses the best (GO-SLAM at 6.8 cm on ScanNet) by 0.3 cm, and dramatically outperforms Point-SLAM (12.3 cm) and NICE-SLAM (10.7 cm). On rendering, VarSplat ties LoopSplat on ScanNet PSNR (24.92 dB) and slightly edges it on SSIM and LPIPS, while on ScanNet++ novel view synthesis it achieves the best PSNR (21.33 vs. LoopSplat's 21.30). The ablation (Table 8) confirms that all three uncertainty applications (tracking, loop detection, registration) contribute: removing any one degrades ATE RMSE from 6.53 to 7.49–7.63 cm.

## Relation to Prior Work

VarSplat extends the submap-based 3DGS-SLAM pipeline established by Gaussian-SLAM and LoopSplat by adding a per-splat appearance variance parameter that is jointly optimized with standard Gaussian attributes. The variance rendering mechanism is derived from the law of total variance applied through alpha compositing, a formulation that departs from prior uncertainty work in SLAM (which typically models depth variance or termination-probability fields) and from NeRF-based uncertainty methods that rely on Bayesian reparameterization or pretrained predictors. The Gaussian negative log-likelihood loss for training variance is borrowed from ActNeRF but adapted to operate directly within the 3DGS rasterizer without any neural network, preserving single-pass rendering efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first 3DGS-SLAM system to treat appearance uncertainty as a first-class, learned quantity propagated through the rasterizer, offering a principled confidence signal for tracking, registration, and loop detection without sacrificing real-time performance. The consistent improvements across four datasets (especially the ~18% ATE RMSE gain on ScanNet++ and robustness where SplaTAM fails entirely) suggest that explicit appearance uncertainty is a meaningful missing component in current 3DGS-SLAM pipelines. Open questions include whether the Gaussian NLL assumption on residuals holds in highly dynamic or outdoor scenes, how the approach extends to monocular or stereo settings without depth, and whether the learned variance can be repurposed for downstream tasks like semantic segmentation or active view selection.
