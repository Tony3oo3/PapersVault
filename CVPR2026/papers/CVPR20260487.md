## Problem

Radiance field methods for novel view synthesis typically model view-dependent appearance with low-order Spherical Harmonics, which are band-limited and cannot represent high-frequency signals like specular highlights without introducing Gibbs ringing artifacts. While explicit alternatives such as Spherical Gaussians and Spherical Betas can capture localized directional variation, their optimization is unstable and sensitive to initialization due to the locality of their kernels. The paper asks whether a differentiable, adaptive partition of the sphere can provide both the expressivity of explicit representations and the optimization stability of global bases.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images with known camera poses and produces novel-view renderings. In the view-direction setting, each 3D Gaussian stores 8 Spherical Voronoi sites (48 learnable parameters total), matching the degrees of freedom of degree-3 SH. In the reflection-based setting built on 2DGS, the scene is augmented with 128 (synthetic) or 1024 (real) spatially distributed Voronoi light probes, each with 2048 directional sites, plus a 256×256×6×3 learnable cubemap for far-field illumination. The reflection pipeline additionally outputs per-pixel diffuse color, specular color, roughness, and surface normals as a full material decomposition.

## Method

Spherical Voronoi represents a directional function as a weighted sum over K directional sites, where weights are computed via a softmax over dot products between site directions and the query direction, with per-site temperature parameters controlling partition sharpness. This yields a soft, non-overlapping decomposition of the sphere that avoids the kernel-competition instability of Spherical Gaussians/Betas while remaining differentiable everywhere. For specular reflections, the paper introduces learnable Voronoi light probes: spatially distributed probes each encoding a local SV function queried along the reflected view direction, interpolated via inverse-distance weighting. A deferred rendering pipeline rasterizes 2DGS primitives into geometry/material buffers, then computes specular shading as a blend of near-field probe contributions and a far-field cubemap, with surface roughness modulating the SV temperature to control reflection sharpness. The entire formulation is explicit and differentiable, requiring no neural decoder.

## Results

On the reflection-based parameterization, Spherical Voronoi with Voronoi Light Probes achieves state-of-the-art PSNR of 36.09 on Ref-NeRF and 31.30 on GlossySynthetic, surpassing the previous best (Ref-GS) by 0.52 and 0.03 dB respectively, while also obtaining competitive results on Ref-Real (23.91 PSNR). For view-direction parameterization, SV consistently outperforms all spherical bases (SH, SG, SB) across four benchmarks, e.g., 28.57 PSNR on Mip-NeRF360 vs. 28.09 for Zip-NeRF with SH, and 34.53 on NeRF-Synthetic vs. 34.15. The method runs at 0.45× the rendering speed of 3DGS with 2.43× training time, faster than Ref-GS (0.37× / 2.63×).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest competitor Ref-GS [34] (same 2DGS backbone but using an MLP decoder for near-field reflections), SV wins on Ref-NeRF (36.09 vs. 35.57 PSNR) and GlossySynthetic (31.30 vs. 31.27), while slightly trailing on Ref-Real LPIPS (0.244 vs. 0.238) though matching PSNR (23.91 vs. 23.81). Against 3DGS-DR [31], the previous deferred-shading SOTA, the gap is larger: +1.96 dB on Ref-NeRF and +0.94 dB on GlossySynthetic. On the view-direction parameterization (Table 1), SV beats Zip-NeRF with SH by 0.48 dB on Mip-NeRF360 and 0.38 dB on NeRF-Synthetic, and outperforms Beta-Splatting's SG/SB variants by 0.39–0.81 dB across all four radiance benchmarks. The ablation (Table 3) confirms that replacing SV with SG, SB, or a cubemap for light probes drops PSNR by 1.6–2.0 dB on Ref-NeRF, validating the Voronoi partition as the key design choice. Overall, SV is the only fully explicit method to reach SOTA on both synthetic and real reflective benchmarks without any neural decoder.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper introduces Spherical Voronoi (SV) as a new explicit basis for spherical functions, departing from the standard Spherical Harmonics used in 3D Gaussian Splatting and from localized alternatives like Spherical Gaussians and Betas that suffer from unstable optimization. Conceptually, it draws on differentiable Voronoi diagrams and the Ref-NeRF principle of querying radiance in the reflected direction, but applies both within an explicit splatting framework (2DGS backbone) rather than a neural field. The Voronoi light probes extend the idea of spatially varying illumination from neural radiance fields to a fully explicit, probe-based formulation, bridging the gap between direct appearance memorization and physically-motivated reflection modeling in 3DGS.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a simple, differentiable, and stable representation that replaces SH in 3DGS pipelines with minimal overhead (48 parameters per Gaussian, one dot-product + softmax), making it easy to adopt. The finding that a fully explicit Voronoi-based model matches or slightly exceeds a neural-decoder-based method (Ref-GS) on reflective benchmarks challenges the assumption that complex view-dependent effects require learned implicit decoders. Limitations include the deferred rendering overhead (0.45× 3DGS speed), the fixed probe count that may not scale to very large scenes, and the fact that gains over the strongest baseline are modest. Open questions include whether SV can handle true inter-reflections and whether the probe-based near-field model generalizes to dynamic or unbounded scenes.
