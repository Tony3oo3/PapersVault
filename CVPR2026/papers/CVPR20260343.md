## Problem

Deploying Vision-Language Models on μNPUs—low-power processors with integrated NPUs offering GOPS-level compute but only tens of MB of memory and a limited operator set (no softmax, no multi-head attention)—remains unsolved. Existing lightweight VLMs target smartphones with one to two orders of magnitude more resources, and no VLM architecture has been designed from the ground up to satisfy the stringent memory (<32 MB) and operator constraints of μNPU platforms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260343/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image (224×224) as input and autoregressively generates a natural language caption, decoded via beam search (beam size 3) over a pruned vocabulary (words with count ≥ 5). The entire pipeline—encoder (21.4 MB) plus decoder (8.2 MB)—must fit within the 4.2 MB on-chip SRAM of the target STM32N657 μNPU, with unsupported operators (SSM scan, normalization) falling back to hand-optimized CPU routines.

## Method

μVLM comprises an OverMod encoder and an AttSSM decoder co-designed around lightweight dynamic attention. OverMod follows a biomimetic 'overview-first, look-closely-next' dual-branch structure: OverNet rapidly produces a global context prior, which ModNet uses via Global Spatial Modulation (GSM)—an additive bias map generated by a 1×1-convolution signal generator—to recalibrate spatial features without any NPU-unsupported operators. The AttSSM decoder replaces standard cross-attention with Global Context Dynamic Modulation (GCDM), combining spatial additive modulation and Squeeze-and-Excitation channel modulation guided by the SSM hidden state, achieving Bahdanau-competitive alignment at O(H²)+O(H·N) cost. A Coordinated Full-Parameter Quantization strategy eliminates precision mismatch at the encoder-decoder boundary by quantizing the encoder first and then calibrating the decoder on the encoder's actual output distribution. Training proceeds in four stages: ImageNet pre-training of the encoder, supervised decoder training on COCO Karpathy, end-to-end fine-tuning, and SCST reinforcement learning with CIDEr as reward.

## Results

On the MS COCO Karpathy test split, μVLM-Base achieves a CIDEr score of 117.8 with a total model size of 29.6 MB, making it the first VLM to fit within the <32 MB memory budget of μNPU platforms while remaining competitive with much larger baselines. The smaller variants μVLM-Small (21.2 MB, CIDEr 109.1) and μVLM-Tiny (13.8 MB, CIDEr 96.4) further demonstrate the scalability of the design. On ImageNet-1K, the OverMod-Base encoder reaches 82.4% Top-1 accuracy with 18.1M parameters, matching or exceeding larger CNNs like ConvNeXT V2 (78.5%) and PVTV2-B1 (78.7%). On-device inference on the STM32N657 achieves a Time to First Token of 208 ms and Time Between Tokens of 21 ms at <300 mW power.

## Baselines & Comparisons

μVLM-Base (29.6 MB, CIDEr 117.8) outperforms all NPU-compatible baselines: it beats NIC (~80 MB) by 25.8 CIDEr and SCST:Att2in (~200 MB) by 6.4 CIDEr, while being 2.7× and 7× smaller respectively. Against larger non-NPU-compatible models, μVLM-Base trails SmallCap (872 MB, CIDEr 121.8) by 4.0, RFNet (~500 MB, CIDEr 121.9) by 4.1, and I-Tuning (~1000 MB, CIDEr 119.4) by 1.6, but is 29×, 17×, and 34× smaller in memory footprint. Notably, μVLM-Base surpasses ClipCap (~1000 MB) by 4.7 CIDEr despite being 34× smaller, and outperforms Up-down (~400 MB) by a narrow margin on BLEU-1 (78.3 vs 79.8) while trailing by 2.3 CIDEr. The encoder OverMod-Base (18.1M params) achieves 82.4% ImageNet-1K Top-1, exceeding ConvNeXT V2 (78.5%), PVTV2-B1 (78.7%), and MambaOut-Femto (78.9%) while using fewer FLOPs than most competitors. The paper's central claim is not raw CIDEr superiority but rather achieving near-competitive captioning quality at a memory footprint 5–34× smaller than all prior VLMs, which is the first such result for μNPU-constrained deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

The encoder design directly extends OverLock's [29] overview-first, look-closely-next dual-branch principle, but replaces its full dynamic convolution (which generates N×C×k² kernel parameters) with the proposed Global Spatial Modulation (GSM), which generates only a C×H×W additive bias map — a 250× parameter reduction that makes the mechanism compatible with μNPU operator sets. The decoder builds on the Selective SSM (Mamba) [11] for sequential modeling but augments it with a lightweight Global Context Dynamic Modulation (GCDM) attention that substitutes for standard Bahdanau/KQV cross-attention using only NPU-supported convolutions and element-wise operations. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant Transformer/LLM-based VLM paradigm and even from recent lightweight VLM efforts (SmallCap, I-Tuning, LightCap) that target smartphones, instead returning to a CNN-decoder architecture specifically constrained by μNPU memory (<32 MB) and operator limitations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a competitive image captioning model (CIDEr 117.8, within ~4 points of 872+ MB baselines) can run in real time on a μNPU with tens of MB of SRAM, opening the door to privacy-preserving, offline generative AI on wearables and small robots. The GSM mechanism — using unbounded additive bias as a spatial attention proxy without softmax — is a reusable design pattern for any NPU-constrained architecture that needs input-dependent feature modulation. Key limitations include evaluation on a single benchmark (COCO Karpathy), a single hardware platform (STM32N657), no zero-shot or open-vocabulary capability, and the SSM decoder still requiring CPU fallback rather than native NPU acceleration. Open questions include whether the coordinated quantization strategy generalizes to multi-stage pipelines, whether GSM scales to higher-resolution or multi-image inputs, and whether the approach extends to other generative tasks (VQA, dialogue) on the same hardware.
