## Problem

Existing diffusion-based motion generation methods fuse semantic, stylistic, and temporal conditioning into a single pathway, causing signal entanglement that limits fine-grained controllability and style fidelity. Autoregressive variants that append history as static context still lack explicit control over how past motion influences each denoising step, leading to error accumulation and drift over long sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262583/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing the desired action, a style reference motion sequence (encoded via MotionCLIP into a style embedding), and the last k=10 frames of the previously generated chunk as historical context. Output is a long-sequence stylized 3D human motion produced autoregressively in overlapping chunks with 60-frame transition segments, where each new chunk is conditioned on the text, style embedding, and the tail of the prior chunk to ensure smooth inter-segment continuity.

## Method

MoCoDiff introduces Injection Modulation Controllers (IMCs), lightweight cross-attention modules that inject text (SIMC), style (STIMC), and history (TIMC) signals through independent key-value pathways into a frozen U-Net diffusion backbone, avoiding the entanglement of fused conditioning. The key distinction is that TIMC does not merely append history as an input feature but modifies the step-wise diffusion transition itself, converting the denoising process from a memoryless Markov chain into a controlled Markov process with finite history, actively suppressing drift across segments. Training uses a progressive rollout curriculum that gradually replaces ground-truth history with EMA-smoothed model predictions, bridging the gap between teacher-forced supervision and autoregressive inference. All IMCs are plug-and-play linear modules trained on top of the frozen backbone, with pre-projection LayerNorm and dimension-specific Q/K normalization ensuring stable multi-condition attention.

## Results

On the long-sequence stylized motion benchmark (HumanML3D + 100Style), MoCoDiff achieves the highest Style Recognition Accuracy (SRA 26.37%) and best content preservation (R-Top-3 0.564), outperforming the strongest baseline AutoMLD+SMooDi (SRA 19.28%, R-Top-3 0.537). For transition smoothness, MoCoDiff attains the lowest Peak Jerk (0.27) and Area Under the Jerk (1.58), indicating the most temporally coherent long-horizon sequences. In single-motion generation (Table 2), MoCoDiff reaches SRA 27.21% versus 20.65% for SMooDi, while in efficiency (Table 3) it runs at 136.89 FPS—4.8× faster than the next-best baseline (AutoMLD+MCM_LDM at 28.57 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoCoDiff clearly wins on style fidelity and temporal smoothness: it surpasses AutoMLD+SMooDi (the strongest style baseline) by 7.1 points in SRA (26.37 vs. 19.28) and by 0.027 in R-Top-3 (0.564 vs. 0.537), while also achieving the lowest PJ (0.27 vs. 0.33) and AUJ (1.58 vs. 2.04). Against AutoMDM+PersonaBooth, MoCoDiff leads by 12.0 SRA points and halves the AUJ (1.58 vs. 2.71). Compared to ControlNet+FlowMDM, MoCoDiff gains 17.4 SRA points and reduces PJ from 0.45 to 0.27. The one metric where MoCoDiff trails is FID: AutoMLD+SMooDi achieves 2.24 versus MoCoDiff's 5.95, suggesting SMooDi produces slightly more realistic single-frame distributions at the cost of weaker style control and transition quality. On efficiency, MoCoDiff (136.89 FPS, 7.31 ms/frame) is 4.8× faster than AutoMLD+MCM_LDM (28.57 FPS) and 48× faster than ControlNet+FlowMDM (2.84 FPS), making it the only method suitable for near-real-time deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoCoDiff departs from the dominant fused-conditioning paradigm in diffusion-based motion generation (MDM, InterGen, MotionGPT), where text, style, and temporal cues are concatenated into a single conditioning vector, by introducing modality-specific Injection Modulation Controllers (IMCs) that inject each signal through independent cross-attention pathways into a frozen U-Net backbone. For long-horizon synthesis, it extends the autoregressive diffusion line (DART, MotionStreamer, AAMDM) by replacing static history conditioning with a Temporal IMC (TIMC) that modifies the step-wise denoising transition itself, converting the process into a controlled Markov chain with finite history. The progressive rollout curriculum is adapted from DART, and the style encoder leverages MotionCLIP, but the core architectural contribution—disentangled, plug-and-play linear modulation with pre-projection normalization—is novel relative to both ControlNet-style duplication and FiLM/AdaLN-based conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular IMC design offers a practical recipe for adding interpretable, inference-time-adjustable multi-condition control to any frozen diffusion backbone without retraining, which could transfer beyond motion to other sequential generation tasks. The 4.8× speedup over the strongest baseline (136.89 FPS vs. 28.57) makes real-time stylized motion synthesis feasible, broadening applicability to interactive animation and embodied AI. However, the fixed 10-frame history window limits global dependency modeling, the frozen backbone constrains expressiveness under rare conditions, and error accumulation over very long rollouts remains an open problem. The paper itself flags future directions including physical priors for contact stability, hierarchical planning for longer sequences, and extending IMCs to nonlinear or adaptive forms.
