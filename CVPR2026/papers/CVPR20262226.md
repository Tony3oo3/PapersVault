## Problem

Existing sound source localization methods treat the task as a feature-matching problem, aligning audio and visual embeddings without verifying whether the matched region truly corresponds to the sound source or performing any causal/semantic reasoning. While MLLMs have been explored as auxiliary encoders for SSL, their full structured reasoning capabilities—multi-step verification, open-set interpretation, and self-correction—remain unexploited for this task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262226/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single center frame (resized to 224×224) from a 3-second video and a 16 kHz audio signal processed as log-scale mel-spectrograms. It produces a refined bounding box (x1, y1, x2, y2 in pixel coordinates), an open-vocabulary audio class label with a self-reported confidence score, and a natural-language visual description of the predicted region. All intermediate and final outputs are structured JSON generated via prompt engineering on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B, with an adaptive gating mechanism that can skip the refinement stage entirely when the initial prediction is deemed reliable.

## Method

The GAR framework reformulates SSL as a three-stage cognitive reasoning pipeline executed entirely through prompt engineering on an MLLM (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B), requiring no task-specific training. Stage 1 (Generation) independently produces an initial bounding box via cross-modal grounding and an open-vocabulary audio classification with confidence. Stage 2 (Analysis) performs fine-grained verification using open-set role tagging (discovering up to 4 sound-producing components with a visibility constraint), anchor voting (up to 5 semantic evidence anchors with confidence scores), an audio-visual consistency score, and an adaptive gating rule that decides whether refinement is needed; this stage is repeated N times (N=5) with multi-trial consensus aggregation. Stage 3 (Refinement) applies targeted geometric operations—delta shift, expand/shrink, or recenter—guided by anchor positions and role tags, but only executes when the gating flag indicates the initial box is unreliable, preventing performance degradation from unnecessary adjustments.

## Results

On the multi-source MUSIC-Duet benchmark, the proposed GAR framework (N=5) achieves 56.7% CAP, 82.7% CIoU@0.3, and 53.2% AUC, outperforming the strongest vision-based baseline OA-SSL by 36.8 percentage points in CIoU@0.3 and 17.1 points in AUC. On VGGSound-Duet, it reaches 47.2% CAP, 77.6% CIoU@0.3, and 45.8% AUC. For single-source localization, the method attains 60.5% AP and 60.2% IoU@0.5 on VGGSound-Single, and 80.6% AP and 98.5% IoU@0.5 on MUSIC-Solo, surpassing all prior methods. All results are obtained in a fully training-free, zero-shot setting using Qwen2.5-Omni-7B with prompt engineering only.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The GAR framework outperforms all 14 baselines across every metric on both multi-source and single-source benchmarks. Against the strongest vision-based method OA-SSL (CVPR'25), the largest gap appears on MUSIC-Duet CIoU@0.3 (82.7% vs. 45.9%, +36.8 pp) and AUC (53.2% vs. 36.1%, +17.1 pp), while on VGGSound-Duet the CIoU@0.3 advantage is +22.4 pp (77.6% vs. 55.2%). Compared to the off-the-shelf MLLM baseline Qwen2.5-Omni used without structured reasoning, the GAR pipeline gains +35.0 pp in CIoU@0.3 on VGGSound-Duet (77.6% vs. 42.6%) and +32.1 pp on MUSIC-Duet (82.7% vs. 50.6%), demonstrating that the Generation-Analysis-Refinement structure is critical rather than raw MLLM capability alone. On single-source VGGSound-Single, Ours (N=5) leads OA-SSL by 8.8 pp in AP (60.5% vs. 51.7%) and 12.9 pp in IoU@0.5 (60.2% vs. 47.3%); on MUSIC-Solo the AP margin is smaller (80.6% vs. 79.8%) but IoU@0.5 jumps to 98.5% versus 71.1%. The weaker MLLM baselines (MiniCPM-o, InteractiveOmni) are substantially behind on all metrics, confirming that model scale and structured reasoning jointly drive performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant contrastive-learning paradigm in sound source localization (LVS, Mix-and-Localize, AVGN, NoPrior, OA-SSL) by reframing SSL as a structured cognitive reasoning problem solvable by a frozen MLLM. Rather than training audio-visual encoders to align embeddings, it repurposes the intrinsic cross-modal reasoning of Qwen2.5-Omni through a three-stage Generation-Analysis-Refinement pipeline, drawing on chain-of-thought and in-context learning techniques from the broader MLLM literature. The open-set role tagging and anchor voting mechanisms are novel contributions that go beyond prior MLLM-assisted SSL work (e.g., OA-SSL), which used MLLMs only as auxiliary encoders without leveraging their full reasoning capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a training-free MLLM pipeline can match or surpass task-specific trained methods on SSL challenges the assumption that contrastive learning is necessary for audio-visual grounding, potentially opening a broader path for MLLM-based perception without dataset-specific training. However, the approach is constrained by inference cost (~4 s/sample with a 7B model), dependence on a single backbone, and evaluation on only two datasets with small test splits (MUSIC-Duet has 17 samples). Open questions include whether temporal reasoning over video sequences, broader real-world acoustic scenes, and smaller/faster MLLMs can preserve the gains, and whether the structured reasoning overhead is justified over simpler prompt-based approaches at scale.
