## Problem

Text-to-video diffusion models frequently fail to generate the exact number of objects specified in a prompt, a limitation the authors trace to two root causes: numeral tokens produce diffuse, low-contrast cross-attention (semantic weakness) and the heavily downsampled spatiotemporal latent space limits instance separability. Existing remedies—retraining with count-annotated data or exhaustive seed search—are either computationally prohibitive or yield only marginal gains, leaving a practical gap for count-accurate generation.

## Input / Output

NUMINA takes a standard text prompt containing explicit numerals (e.g., 'three cats') and a random noise seed, operating within the existing Wan T2V pipeline (81 frames at 832×480 to 1280×720, 50 denoising steps). It performs a truncated pre-generation pass at timestep t*=20 to extract attention maps, then re-runs the full denoising with layout-guided cross-attention modulation, producing a single video whose object counts match the prompt. No auxiliary input video, spatial mask, or trained network is required.

## Method

NUMINA follows a training-free identify-then-guide paradigm. In the identification phase, it dynamically selects the most instance-discriminative self-attention head (scored via PCA-projected foreground-background separation, block-level structural variance, and Sobel edge clarity) and the most text-concentrated cross-attention head per noun token at an intermediate layer; fusing these maps through Mean Shift and DBSCAN clustering yields a countable per-frame layout whose foreground region count is directly comparable to the prompted numeral. In the guidance phase, the layout is conservatively refined—removing the smallest region for overcounts and inserting a template (copied from an existing instance or a default circle) placed by minimizing a heuristic cost combining overlap, center proximity, and temporal stability. The refined layout then steers regeneration by modulating cross-attention: a large negative bias suppresses attention in removed regions, while a positive bias or direct pre-softmax score overwrite boosts attention in added regions, all scaled by a monotonically decreasing intensity function δ(t) that applies stronger guidance early in denoising and weaker guidance later to preserve fine details.

## Results

On CountBench, NUMINA improves counting accuracy (CountAcc) by 7.4% on Wan2.1-1.3B (42.3% → 49.7%), by 4.9% on Wan2.2-5B (47.8% → 52.7%), and by 5.5% on Wan2.1-14B (53.6% → 59.1%), all with a single generation trial. CLIP score also increases consistently (e.g., 33.9 → 35.6 on 1.3B), and Temporal Consistency is maintained or improved (81.2% → 83.4% on 1.3B; 83.3% → 84.0% on 14B). Notably, the 1.3B model with NUMINA (49.7%) surpasses the unmodified 5B model (47.8%) in counting accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NUMINA is compared against the unmodified Wan base models, seed search (generating 5 videos and selecting the best by count), and LLM-based prompt enhancement. NUMINA wins on CountAcc across all three model scales, outperforming seed search by 4.2–5.5% and prompt enhancement by 2.5–5.5%. On CLIP score, NUMINA is the only method that consistently improves text-video alignment (positive deltas on all three scales), whereas prompt enhancement slightly degrades CLIP on 1.3B and 14B. Temporal Consistency is also best or tied-best with NUMINA, confirming that the layout-guided regeneration does not introduce flickering. In the ablation (Table 2), the attention-derived layout outperforms a GroundingDINO-derived layout by 2.2% CountAcc, showing that native DiT attention captures nascent instance structure more effectively than an external detector.

## Relation to Prior Work

NUMINA builds on the DiT-based T2V paradigm (Wan2.1/2.2) and repurposes the model's own self- and cross-attention maps as a free layout signal, departing from prior video-editing methods (VideoGrain, OmnimatteZero) that require external segmentation masks and operate in a video-to-video setting. It contrasts with CountGen, which addresses counting in T2I but relies on SDXL-specific observations, a trained layout-completion network, and explicit masks. The paper positions its identify-then-guide pipeline as complementary to—and more effective than—practical training-free strategies like seed search and LLM-based prompt enhancement, while remaining compatible with inference accelerators such as EasyCache.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Numerical misalignment is a practical reliability gap for precision-sensitive applications (instructional visualization, compositional scenes), and NUMINA offers a broadly applicable, training-free fix that requires no retraining data or auxiliary networks. The finding that a 1.3B model augmented with NUMINA outperforms a 5B model without it suggests structural guidance can partially substitute for model scale on counting tasks. Key open questions remain: performance on very dense scenes (tens to hundreds of instances), generalization beyond the Wan family to other DiT architectures, and whether the heuristic layout-refinement costs can be learned or made adaptive per scene complexity.
