## Problem

Model inversion attacks on unimodal DNNs are well-studied, but the vulnerability of vision-language models (VLMs) to such attacks remains largely unexplored. VLMs differ fundamentally from unimodal networks in that they produce token sequences rather than class labels, often freeze their vision encoders during training, and encode private visual information primarily in the language model and projector parameters. This paper asks whether and how VLMs leak private visual training data through their textual outputs.

## Input / Output

The attacker operates in a white-box setting with full access to a fine-tuned 7–8B VLM (LLaVA-v1.6, Qwen2.5-VL, MiniGPT-v2, or InternVL2.5), a text prompt (e.g., 'Who is the person in the image?'), and the target text answer (e.g., a person's name or dog breed). Optimization is performed in the latent space of a StyleGAN2 generator trained on a public dataset (FFHQ for faces, AFHQ-Dogs for dogs), so the reconstructed image is always a GAN-generated face or dog rather than an arbitrary pixel array. The output is a single reconstructed image intended to resemble the private training sample associated with the given prompt-answer pair.

## Method

The paper introduces four MI strategies tailored to VLMs' token-generative nature: Token-based MI (TMI), Convergent Token-based MI (TMI-C), Sequence-based MI (SMI), and the proposed SMI-AW. The key insight behind SMI-AW is that output tokens vary substantially in their visual grounding—some are strongly driven by image content while others are determined by linguistic context—so their gradients carry unequal information for image reconstruction. SMI-AW computes a per-token weight β_i proportional to the token's total cross-attention magnitude over the image, then forms a weighted sum of per-token inversion losses (using logit-maximization loss) and updates the StyleGAN2 latent code via gradient descent. Crucially, the weights are recomputed at every inversion step because a token's visual dependence shifts as the reconstructed image becomes more consistent with the target output. This adaptive reweighting concentrates optimization on visually informative tokens and suppresses noise from linguistically driven ones, yielding the highest attack accuracy (61.21% human-evaluated on CelebA) across all tested VLMs and datasets.

## Results

On FaceScrub with LLaVA-v1.6-7B, SMI-AW with the logit-maximization loss (L_LOM) achieves the highest MLLM-based attack accuracy of 61.01%, outperforming SMI (59.17%), TMI (44.34%), and TMI-C (31.16%). On CelebA and StanfordDogs, SMI-AW reaches 67.05% and 78.13% MLLM-based attack accuracy respectively. Human evaluation confirms the severity of the attack, with 61.21% of reconstructed CelebA images correctly identified as matching the private training identity by annotators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper introduces four MI strategies and compares them as internal baselines: TMI (single update per token per iteration), TMI-C (K updates per token before advancing), SMI (averaged loss over all tokens), and the proposed SMI-AW (adaptive token weighting via cross-attention). SMI-AW consistently outperforms all other strategies across every metric and every inversion loss. On FaceScrub with L_LOM, SMI-AW achieves 61.01% MLLM-based attack accuracy versus 59.17% for SMI, 44.34% for TMI, and 31.16% for TMI-C — a 1.8-point gain over SMI and a 16.7-point gain over TMI. The gap is even more pronounced on CelebA (67.05% vs. 64.93% for SMI) and StanfordDogs (78.13% vs. 75.94% for SMI). TMI-C performs worst overall, with match rates below 30% between reconstructed images and target text, indicating that forcing convergence per token before advancing disrupts the generative dependency structure. The authors attribute SMI-AW's advantage to its dynamic reweighting: by amplifying gradients from visually grounded tokens and suppressing those from linguistically driven tokens, the optimization focuses on the most informative signals for image reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative model inversion paradigm established by Zhang et al. (CVPR 2020) and the rethinking framework of Nguyen et al. (CVPR 2023) from unimodal classification DNNs to vision-language models. The core departure is that VLMs output token sequences rather than single class labels, necessitating new inversion formulations (token-based, sequence-based) and the key insight that output tokens differ in visual grounding, motivating the adaptive token weighting in SMI-AW. The evaluation protocol follows the plug-and-play MI framework of Struppek et al. (ICML 2022), adapted with MLLM-based and human evaluation to better reflect identity reconstruction quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With VLMs increasingly deployed in sensitive domains (healthcare, finance), demonstrating that publicly released models like LLaVA-v1.6-7B leak identifiable training data at ~61% human-verified accuracy raises urgent privacy concerns. The finding that the vision encoder is typically frozen during VLM training means private visual information is embedded in the language model and projector, making standard defenses (e.g., protecting the vision encoder) insufficient. Open questions include whether the white-box assumption is realistic for deployed models, whether defenses exist that preserve VLM utility while preventing inversion, and whether the vulnerability extends to other modalities (audio, video) or to black-box API access.
