## Problem

Existing large vision-language models (LVLMs) often ignore the contents of intermediate rationales generated during multi-modal chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning. The paper's preliminary experiments show that rationale tokens contribute far less to output predictions than image tokens, and that swapping rationales with irrelevant ones leaves performance nearly unchanged, indicating LVLMs do not effectively ground final answers on rationale semantics. This undermines both the accuracy and faithfulness that CoT is expected to provide.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263331/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an input RGB image and a text query, first generates a rationale (either a natural-language description via CoT or a JSON-formatted scene graph via CCoT), then decodes a text response. During answer generation, RED requires two parallel forward passes per token—one conditioned on (image, query) and one on (rationale, query)—which are batched together to minimize latency. The output is a standard free-form text answer, and the approach is fully training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no architecture modification to the underlying LVLM.

## Method

RED reformulates multi-modal CoT decoding as a KL-constrained reward maximization where the reward is the rationale-conditional log-likelihood log p(y_i | y_<i, r, q) and the reference policy is the image-conditional distribution p(y_i | y_<i, x, q). The paper proves (Theorem 4.1) that the optimal policy is the product of these two distributions, with the rationale-conditional term raised to a tunable power λ, making RED a power-of-experts combination. Practically, this is implemented as a weighted sum of the log-softmax logits from the two separate forward passes, which are computed in batch-parallel to avoid sequential latency. The approach is training-free, requires no auxiliary reward models, and is complementary to existing contrastive decoding methods like VCD since it operates on a different conditional distribution.

## Results

RED (applied to both CoT and CCoT) consistently outperforms all baselines across 7 benchmarks and 4 LVLMs. On GQA, CCoT+RED achieves 45.87% (Gemma-3-4B, +5.87 over baseline), 47.50% (Gemma-3-12B, +2.16), 61.92% (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, +1.04), and 65.91% (Llama3-LLaVA-Next-8B, +0.69). The largest gains appear on MME Cognition (up to +275.35 for CoT+RED on Gemma-3-4B) and LLaVA-Bench (up to +10.90 for CCoT+RED on Llama3-LLaVA-Next-8B), while standard CoT/CCoT alone sometimes degrades performance (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B drops from 60.88 to 46.70 on GQA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RED (both CoT+RED and CCoT+RED) outperforms all five baselines—Baseline, VCD, VCD+ICD, CoT, and CCoT—on nearly every benchmark–model pair. Against standard CoT and CCoT, RED is most critical where CoT alone degrades performance: on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, CoT drops GQA from 60.88 to 46.70 (−14.18), while CCoT+RED recovers to 61.92 (+1.04 over baseline); similarly, CCoT on Llama3-LLaVA-Next-8B drops GQA to 60.43 (−4.79), whereas CCoT+RED reaches 65.91 (+0.69). Against the contrastive decoding baselines VCD and VCD+ICD, RED consistently wins on GQA, LLaVA-Bench, and MME Cognition (e.g., CCoT+RED scores 76.90 vs. VCD's 75.70 on LLaVA-Bench for Gemma-3-4B), while VCD/ICD occasionally match or slightly exceed RED on MME Perception for smaller models. The intervention analysis (Table 4) further confirms that RED's gains are rationale-dependent: GPT-4-generated rationales boost CCoT+RED by an additional +3.06 over self-generated rationales on Gemma-3-4B GQA, while irrelevant random rationales degrade it by −6.51, demonstrating that RED genuinely grounds on rationale content rather than exploiting spurious signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two lines of work: multi-modal CoT prompting (MM-CoT, CCoT, UnifiedQA), which focuses on improving rationale *generation* quality, and plug-and-play contrastive decoding (VCD, LCD, ICD), which modulates image-conditional probability to mitigate hallucination. The key departure is the empirical demonstration that the standard joint conditional p(y|x,r,q) used by all prior CoT methods fails to ground outputs on rationale content, making both lines of work partially ineffective. RED is framed as orthogonal to both: it does not change how rationales are generated (unlike CCoT) and does not contrast against corrupted images (unlike VCD), but instead decouples image and rationale conditioning at the logit level, grounded in a KL-constrained reward maximization formulation borrowed from the RLHF/DPO literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that off-the-shelf LVLMs largely ignore rationale semantics in CoT (irrelevant rationales barely change outputs) challenges a core assumption underpinning the multi-modal CoT literature and has implications for the broader faithfulness-of-reasoning question. RED's plug-and-play nature (simple weighted logit sum, no training, no architecture change) makes it immediately deployable, and its compatibility with existing contrastive decoding methods suggests composability. Open questions include the root cause of rationale neglect (position bias, attention sinks, visual instruction tuning overfitting), whether the dual-forward-pass overhead can be further reduced, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-image settings. The scalability result—that larger LVLMs do not consistently improve with CoT but do with RED—suggests a potential bottleneck in how current architectures integrate intermediate text into visual reasoning.
