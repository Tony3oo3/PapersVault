## Problem

Sinusoidal neural networks (SIRENs) suffer from a fundamental frequency localization failure: their neurons are globally coupled, so high-frequency components generated by the network leak into smooth regions, producing noisy artifacts, while low-frequency initialization underfits detailed areas. This sensitivity to the input frequency parameter ω₀ makes SIRENs unstable to train and impractical for signals with mixed frequency content, and existing global INRs lack any mechanism to spatially restrict which frequencies are active where.

## Input / Output

SASNet takes 2D or 3D spatial coordinates as input and outputs a signal value (RGB triplet, scalar density, or SDF value) at each coordinate. The coordinates are consumed twice: once through a frozen sinusoidal frequency-embedding layer with fixed ω and phase, and once through a multi-scale hash-grid encoder that feeds a small ReLU MLP producing per-layer spatial masks. For SDF reconstruction, the learned field is converted to a mesh via Marching Cubes on a 512³ grid; for image fitting, the network is evaluated at pixel coordinates or at sub-pixel offsets for super-resolution.

## Method

SASNet couples a frozen frequency embedding layer with learned spatially-adaptive masks to achieve localized frequency control in SIRENs. The frequency embedding layer fixes the input frequencies ω so the network's full spectrum (integer linear combinations ⟨k,ω⟩ per Theorem 1) is determined at initialization, and only the Bessel-function amplitudes α_k are learned during training, stabilizing optimization. A lightweight multi-scale hash-grid MLP generates per-layer masks M^i(x)∈[0,1]^{n_i} that are applied via Hadamard product to each sinusoidal layer, making the frequency amplitudes spatially dependent and confining high-frequency neuron activity to regions that need it. Neurons are grouped by frequency magnitude (input layer) or evenly (hidden layers), with one mask per group to limit parameter overhead. Training jointly optimizes both networks with an L1 sparsity penalty on the masks, encouraging selective activation of high-frequency components only where the signal demands them.

## Results

On DIV2K image fitting, SASNet achieves PSNR 39.82 dB, SSIM 0.979, edge-region PSNR 36.81 dB, and noisiness 2.105, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On ScalarFlow volumetric reconstruction, SASNet reaches PSNR 55.92 and SSIM 0.9973, surpassing the next-best WIRE (53.06 / 0.9963) by a clear margin. On SDF reconstruction, SASNet attains the lowest average Chamfer distance (2.587e-06) and highest average IoU (0.9792) across four shapes, while converging to FINER's final loss within ~35K iterations versus 200K for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SASNet outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric across the three tasks. On image fitting, the closest competitor is FINER (PSNR 37.52, noisiness 4.617), which SASNet surpasses by +2.3 dB PSNR and nearly halves the noisiness score; GaussianImage (37.38) and NeuRBF (36.33) trail by 2.4 and 3.5 dB respectively. SIREN, despite being the architectural backbone, lags by 5.2 dB PSNR and 5.4 dB edge PSNR due to global frequency leakage. On volumetric reconstruction, SASNet leads WIRE (53.06) by 2.9 dB and FINER (49.77) by 6.2 dB. On SDF fitting, SASNet achieves the lowest Chamfer distance on all four shapes (e.g., 1.964e-06 on Dragon vs. WIRE's 2.172e-06) and the highest IoU (0.9792 avg vs. WIRE's 0.9746), while converging roughly 6× faster than FINER. The ablation confirms the frequency embedding layer contributes ~4 dB and spatial masks add a further ~2 dB on top.

## Relation to Prior Work

SASNet sits at the intersection of three INR research threads: SIREN's sinusoidal activations for high-frequency signal capture, Novello et al.'s frozen frequency embedding layer that fixes the network's spectral support via Bessel-function expansion, and InstantNGP's multi-scale hash-grid encoding for spatial feature lookup. The paper positions itself as a hybrid INR that departs from global SIREN variants (SIREN, FINER) by adding learned spatial masks to localize frequency contributions, and from local INRs (WIRE, GaussianImage) by retaining explicit sinusoidal frequency control. It is most closely related to SAPE and Walker et al.'s spatially-adaptive hash encodings, but differs in using a SIREN backbone with group-level Hadamard masking rather than ReLU-based networks with residual-dependent gating.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency leakage problem—where high-frequency SIREN neurons contaminate smooth regions—is a practical bottleneck for INR-based image and geometry fitting, and SASNet's clean decomposition of spectral control (frozen embedding) from spatial control (learned masks) offers a reusable architectural pattern. The convergence speedup (reaching baseline final loss in ~35K vs. 200K iterations for SDF) has direct practical value for iterative geometry pipelines. Limitations include the hash-grid memory/compute overhead for mask generation, diminishing gains under heavy over-parameterization, and the current reliance on a fixed number of frequency groups; reducing mask-generation cost and exploring alternative initialization strategies are identified as open directions.
