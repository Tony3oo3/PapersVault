## Problem

State-of-the-art VLMs, including GPT-5, achieve near-zero accuracy on monocular 3D visual grounding, failing to localize objects in 3D space from a single image and text description. The paper identifies three root causes: imprecise 2D grounding, deficient understanding of 3D geometry (depth, relative size, spatial relationships), and an inability to exploit camera projection/unprojection matrices. Specialized vision-only models handle this task well but lack the rich semantic and language understanding that VLMs provide.

## Input / Output

The model receives a single RGB image, a natural-language query describing the target object, and camera intrinsics (focal lengths f_x, f_y and principal point c_x, c_y), which are assumed available at both training and inference time. The output is a 7-parameter 3D bounding box in the camera coordinate system: center (x, y, z), dimensions (l, w, h) in meters, and yaw angle θ around the vertical axis. The paper explicitly rejects the alternative 8-vertex parameterization, finding the higher-dimensional prediction space detrimental to performance.

## Method

MonoVLM applies a three-stage GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) curriculum to a standard VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B or MiMo-VL-7B) without any architectural modifications. Stage 1 trains 2D visual grounding using 2D IoU as the reward, establishing precise pixel-level localization. Stage 2 optimizes 3D center prediction via an exponential distance reward, forcing the model to learn the 2D-to-3D unprojection mapping. Stage 3 refines the full bounding box with a composite reward combining 3D IoU, center distance, normalized L1 size error, and cosine-similarity rotation error. GRPO eliminates the need for a separate critic by normalizing rewards across a group of sampled responses, and the progressive curriculum exploits the geometric duality between 2D projection and 3D unprojection to build 3D understanding incrementally.

## Results

On the Mono3DRefer benchmark, MonoVLM-MiMo achieves an overall Acc@0.25 of 69.41% and Acc@0.5 of 42.96%, surpassing the SOTA vision-only baseline Mono3DVG-TGE (68.44% / 51.21%) on the primary Acc@0.25 metric. The overall mIoU reaches 38.11, a more than 5× improvement over the best VLM baseline GPT-5 (7.53). In the challenging 'Multiple' object scenario, MonoVLM-MiMo attains 71.23% Acc@0.25, exceeding Mono3DVG-TGE (69.83%), demonstrating superior language-guided disambiguation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against all six VLM baselines (MiMo-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Gemini-2.5-Flash, GPT-5, GPT-o3, Gemini-2.5-Pro), MonoVLM-MiMo wins by an order of magnitude: 69.41% vs. at most 5.98% Acc@0.25 (GPT-5), and 38.11 vs. at most 7.53 mIoU. Against the vision-only Mono3DVG, MonoVLM-MiMo wins on Overall Acc@0.25 (69.41 vs. 64.36) but trails on Acc@0.5 (42.96 vs. 44.25). Against the stronger Mono3DVG-TGE, MonoVLM-MiMo edges out on Overall Acc@0.25 (69.41 vs. 68.44) and clearly wins in the 'Multiple' disambiguation scenario (71.23 vs. 69.83), but loses on Overall Acc@0.5 (42.96 vs. 51.21), indicating the specialized model still produces tighter boxes at higher IoU thresholds. The two-stage Cube R-CNN+Best baseline is decisively outperformed on both metrics (55.77 / 29.92). Overall, MonoVLM-MiMo is the first VLM to match or exceed SOTA vision-only methods on this task, though a gap remains at the stricter Acc@0.5 threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: specialized monocular 3D grounding models (Mono3DVG, Mono3DVG-TGE) that achieve strong accuracy but lack semantic flexibility, and general-purpose VLMs that excel at 2D understanding but fail at 3D spatial reasoning. The key methodological departure is repurposing GRPO (originally developed for mathematical reasoning in LLMs) as the training signal for a staged 3D grounding curriculum, rather than designing a task-specific architecture. The three-stage decomposition (2D localization → 3D center → full 3D box) is motivated by a diagnostic pilot study showing that naive end-to-end 3D IoU optimization produces accurate depth but poor 2D grounding, revealing a specific failure mode that prior VLM-for-3D work had not isolated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is one of the first demonstrations that a general-purpose 7B VLM, trained with a simple staged RL curriculum, can match or exceed specialized vision-only 3D grounding models, suggesting VLMs may serve as unified backbones for both 2D and 3D perception tasks. However, the evaluation is confined to the Mono3DRefer (KITTI-based) benchmark with camera intrinsics provided, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to unconstrained scenes, novel object categories, or other 3D tasks like detection without language queries. The reliance on GRPO also raises practical questions about training stability and compute cost at scale, and whether the staged curriculum is truly necessary or whether a single well-designed reward could suffice with more training.
