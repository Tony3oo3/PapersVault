## Problem

Training diffusion models for pose-guided human video generation demands extreme computational resources (up to 100 GB VRAM for a DiT at 512×512, 20 frames), yet conventional fine-tuning updates all parameters uniformly regardless of their individual contribution to the target conditional task. This static, undifferentiated training wastes compute on low-impact blocks and prevents scaling to higher resolutions, longer videos, or richer control signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261498/cropped_page2_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a reference image (for subject identity) and a pose sequence (estimated via Multi-HMR from source video) as conditioning signals, and generates temporally coherent human videos. Training uses 10-frame sequences at 512×512 (video phase) or single frames at 768×768 (image phases), with a three-phase curriculum: subject-driven image generation (50k steps, batch 32), pose-guided image generation (200k steps, batch 8), and temporal video generation (200k steps, batch 4). Inference employs the IDDPM sampler with 100 denoising steps and classifier-free guidance scale 4.0.

## Method

Ent-Prog combines two mechanisms for efficient progressive training of a pretrained DiT. First, Conditional Entropy Inflation (CEI) scores each residual block by measuring the increase in output noise uncertainty (Gaussian log-variance ratio) when that block is skipped, yielding a task-aware priority ranking that determines the unfreezing order. Second, an adaptive progressive schedule uses a Nested Diffusion Supernet: at the start of each stage, a one-shot supernet epoch trains all candidate unfreezing sizes in a shared weight space, and the number of top-priority blocks to unfreeze is selected by maximizing convergence efficiency (average loss decrease per wall-clock time) on a fixed hold-out set. The selected sub-network then inherits supernet parameters and trains for the remainder of the stage, progressively expanding capacity only where it yields the best return per unit time. This yields up to 2.2× training speedup and 2.4× GPU memory reduction across three datasets without degrading FVD, FID-VID, or pose adherence.

## Results

On the Bilibili dataset (200k steps), Ent-Prog achieves a 2.17× training speedup (6 vs. 13 days) and reduces GPU memory from 72 GB to 53 GB (2.4× reduction), while simultaneously outperforming full training on all quality metrics: FVD drops from 132.06 to 119.77, FID-VID from 15.01 to 14.90, and LPIPS from 0.128 to 0.121. On TikTok, Ent-Prog yields a 1.52× speedup with a 46.1% relative FVD improvement (385.64 → 264.03). On UBC-Fashion, it achieves 1.69× speedup with memory reduced to 63% of full training, while matching or exceeding full training on all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is standard full training (all blocks unfrozen from the start), which serves as the conventional approach for fine-tuning a pretrained DiT-XL/2 diffusion model. Ent-Prog consistently outperforms full training across all three datasets and both tasks (video and image generation), which is notable because the method trains fewer parameters at each stage yet achieves better quality. On Bilibili video generation, Ent-Prog improves FVD by 9.3% (132.06→119.77) and FID-VID by 0.7% while training 2.17× faster. On TikTok video generation, the FVD improvement is more dramatic at 46.1% (385.64→264.03), suggesting the prioritized unfreezing strategy is particularly beneficial when the domain gap is larger. On UBC-Fashion, Ent-Prog matches full training on SSIM (0.906) and slightly improves FVD (79.94→79.76) while using 37% less memory. The ablation (Table 5) confirms both components are necessary: removing CEI priority degrades FID-VID from 32.15 to 37.43, and removing the adaptive schedule (replacing with linear) degrades FVD from 264.03 to 382.41.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the progressive learning lineage from Bengio et al. (2006) through Auto-Prog (Li et al., CVPR 2022) and the authors' own prior work on progressive unfreezing for diffusion models (Li et al., TPAMI 2026), adding two key departures: a task-aware block prioritization signal (Conditional Entropy Inflation) inspired by network pruning literature, and an adaptive stage-length schedule driven by a Nested Diffusion Supernet borrowed from one-shot NAS. Unlike prior progressive learning that grows uniformly from scratch, Ent-Prog operates on a pretrained DiT and selectively unfreezes blocks ranked by their contribution to conditional uncertainty reduction, while the supernet scores convergence efficiency to determine how many top-priority blocks to activate per stage. The application domain—pose-guided human video generation with DiT backbones—follows the Animate Anyone / Champ / Human4DiT line but reframes the training procedure rather than the architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.2× training speedup and 2.4× GPU memory reduction are practically significant for a task where a single DiT-XL/2 run at 512×512×20 frames can exceed 100 GB VRAM, making the method relevant to practitioners constrained by hardware. The CEI metric offers a principled, task-aware alternative to heuristic or uniform unfreezing schedules, and the supernet-based adaptive scheduling could generalize to other diffusion fine-tuning scenarios beyond human video. Open questions include whether the entropy-based prioritization transfers to non-DiT architectures (e.g., U-Net), whether the quality gains over full training persist at larger scales or with different conditioning signals, and how sensitive the method is to the number of sampled (τ, x, c) pairs used to estimate π(b).
