## Problem

Vision-language models remain weak at dynamic spatial reasoning (DSR)—reasoning about how object geometry and spatial relationships evolve in 3D over time—largely due to the scarcity of scalable 4D-aware training data and benchmarks. Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks are confined to static scenes, short-horizon motion, or domain-specific settings (e.g., autonomous driving), and naively injecting 3D reconstruction features into VLMs degrades general video understanding performance.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip (20–120 s, sampled at 32 frames for training or 1 FPS for evaluation) and a text question describing a dynamic spatial query (e.g., direction change between two tracked objects over a time sub-interval from a specified viewpoint) as input, and produces a multiple-choice answer. Questions are grounded in extracted 3D trajectories and support both egocentric (agent-following) and allocentric (camera-fixed) viewpoints, with answers structured as fine-grained procedural sequences (e.g., 'Left; Behind') rather than single-frame snapshots.

## Method

The paper introduces DSR Suite, comprising an automated data pipeline and a lightweight Geometry Selection Module (GSM). The pipeline curates in-the-wild videos from Koala-36M, extracts geometric cues (camera poses and point clouds via π³, object masks via Grounded SAM2, agent orientations via Orient Anything), and synthesizes 50K multiple-choice QAs (DSR-Train) plus a human-refined 1,484-question benchmark (DSR-Bench) covering six question types across absolute and relative viewpoints. GSM uses two stacked Q-Formers: the first condenses question semantics into N=32 learnable query embeddings, and the second selectively retrieves question-relevant geometry from π³ 3D tokens into a compact set of geometry tokens that are concatenated with vision and text tokens before the LLM head. This targeted extraction avoids overwhelming the VLM with irrelevant geometric noise, preserving general video understanding while boosting DSR accuracy. The full system (Qwen2.5-VL-7B + GSM) is trained for 1 epoch at lr 2×10⁻⁷ with the vision encoder frozen, achieving 58.9 average on DSR-Bench versus 38.4 for the best prior spatial reasoning model.

## Results

On DSR-Bench, the proposed method (Qwen2.5-VL-7B + GSM trained on DSR-Train) achieves an average accuracy of 58.9%, substantially outperforming all baselines. The closest spatial-reasoning competitor, VG-LLM, reaches only 38.4%, while the strongest proprietary model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) scores 31.7%. The method also maintains competitive general video understanding (59.9 on Video-MME) and improves on other spatial benchmarks (48.3 on VLM4D, 35.2 on STI-Bench) compared to the unmodified baseline (43.1, 33.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method wins decisively against all 11 baseline groups on DSR-Bench average accuracy. Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Pro (31.7%) is the strongest, yet the proposed method exceeds it by 27.2 points; GPT-5 (30.8%) and GPT-4o (26.4%) trail further behind. General-purpose VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 23.5%, Qwen3-VL-30B at 31.1%, InternVL3.5-38B at 26.7%) and video-understanding models (VideoRefer at 26.9%, LongVILA-R1 at 25.3%) perform only marginally above random, confirming their limited dynamic spatial reasoning capacity. The closest spatial-reasoning competitor, VG-LLM (38.4%), is outperformed by 20.5 points, while VLM-3R (31.4%) lags by 27.5 points. Notably, even the largest general-purpose models (32B/38B parameters) do not close the gap, indicating that scale alone is insufficient without dedicated 4D training data and geometric priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatial-reasoning-in-VLMs line of work (VLM-3R, VG-LLM, LLaVA-3D) by shifting from static 3D scenes to dynamic 4D environments with moving objects and evolving spatial relationships. It builds directly on π³ for relative-scale 3D reconstruction, Grounded SAM2 for object tracking, and Orient Anything for orientation estimation, but departs from prior fusion strategies (naive cross-attention or direct addition of 3D tokens) by proposing the Geometry Selection Module, which uses two stacked Q-Formers to selectively retrieve question-relevant geometric knowledge. Unlike prior benchmarks (VLM4D, STI-Bench, DynSuperCLEVR) that are domain-constrained or single-object, DSR-Bench targets in-the-wild multi-object scenes with viewpoint transformations and fine-grained procedural answers, and is paired with a large-scale training corpus (DSR-Train) that no prior work provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DSR Suite is the first unified resource (training data + benchmark + model) for dynamic spatial reasoning, filling a gap where prior work offered only evaluation benchmarks without scalable supervision. The GSM finding—that naive geometric fusion degrades general video understanding (Video-MME drops from 60.2 to 48.6) while selective retrieval preserves it (59.9)—provides a practical recipe for integrating 3D priors into VLMs without the typical generality tax. Open questions include whether the approach extends to metric-scale reasoning, longer temporal horizons beyond 120s, and embodied/interactive settings where the agent must act on spatial predictions rather than merely describe them.
