## Problem

Viewing graph solvability — whether pairwise two-view relationships uniquely determine all cameras — has been thoroughly studied for calibrated and uncalibrated cameras, but remains completely unexplored for the affine camera model. The affine camera is a practical approximation in scenarios with small depth variation (aerial imagery, turntable acquisitions), yet no theoretical or algorithmic results existed to determine when a graph of affine fundamental matrices yields a unique reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262249/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a connected viewing graph whose edges are affine fundamental matrices (3×3 matrices with a fixed zero top-left 2×2 block, 4 DOF up to scale) and whose nodes are affine cameras (2×4 matrices with last row [0 0 0 1], 8 DOF). It outputs a binary verdict: affine solvable (unique camera configuration up to a global 4×4 affine transformation) or not. A key structural detail is that 12 parameters must be fixed across two distinct cameras to eliminate the global affine ambiguity, since a single camera (8 DOF) is insufficient to constrain the 12-DOF affine group.

## Method

The core contribution is a linear-system characterization of affine solvability, in contrast to the polynomial system required for uncalibrated cameras. Starting from the skew-symmetric condition QᵀFP (Proposition 1 from Hartley & Zisserman), the authors substitute the special affine camera and affine fundamental matrix forms, yielding exactly 4 linear equations per edge. Collecting these over all m edges and appending 12 gauge-fixing equations produces a system Ax = b with 4m+12 equations and 8n unknowns; the graph is affine solvable if and only if A is full rank. The paper further derives necessary conditions (biconnectivity and m ≥ 2n−3 edges) and conjectures — supported by extensive synthetic and real-data experiments — that affine solvability is equivalent to 2D parallel rigidity, which would yield a combinatorial characterization currently lacking for the uncalibrated case.

## Results

On synthetic graphs with 15% edge density (1000 samples per size), affine solvability (the proposed method) always yields the exact same count as 2D parallel rigidity, supporting the conjecture that the two notions are equivalent. Affine solvability is consistently more restrictive than uncalibrated solvability (e.g., 266 vs. 327 at n=10; 447 vs. 457 at n=25), which in turn is more restrictive than calibrated solvability (374 at n=10). On real SfM datasets (Table 3), affine and uncalibrated solvability produce identical outcomes for all 22 sequences, with 19 of 22 graphs passing all tests.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares affine solvability (the proposed linear-system method) against three established notions: 2D parallel rigidity [2], uncalibrated (finite) solvability [6], and calibrated solvability / 3D parallel rigidity [30]. Affine solvability matches 2D parallel rigidity exactly in every experimental setting (Tables 1, 2, and 3), with identical pass counts at every graph size and edge density, strongly supporting the conjecture that the two are equivalent. Against uncalibrated solvability [6], affine solvability is strictly more restrictive at small graph sizes (e.g., 266 vs. 327 at n=10, a gap of 61 graphs) but converges to the same count as graphs grow denser (identical at n≥50 in Table 1). Against calibrated solvability [30], the gap is even larger at small n (266 vs. 374 at n=10) and narrows with increasing size. On real SfM data, affine and uncalibrated solvability produce identical binary outcomes for all 22 sequences, suggesting the distinction between the two models is negligible in typical turntable-like or small-depth-variation scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the viewing graph solvability framework—previously studied only for calibrated cameras (reducible to 3D parallel rigidity) and uncalibrated cameras (requiring intractable polynomial systems)—to the affine camera model. The linear formulation is derived by specializing Hartley and Zisserman's fundamental matrix characterization (Q^T F P skew-symmetric) to the constrained affine camera and affine fundamental matrix forms, yielding a rank-testable linear system. The necessary conditions (biconnectivity, edge-count lower bound) mirror those established for calibrated and uncalibrated solvability, while the conjectured equivalence to 2D parallel rigidity draws on the bearing-based localization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The affine camera model is practically relevant for turntable and aerial imagery where depth variation is small, yet its solvability properties were entirely unexplored. The reduction to a linear system provides a practical, polynomial-time solvability test—unlike the uncalibrated case where no efficient method exists. The central open question is whether affine solvability is provably equivalent to 2D parallel rigidity; if confirmed, this would yield a combinatorial characterization of affine solvability (analogous to the calibrated case) and potentially unify solvability theory across camera models. The conjecture remains unproven, and the empirical evidence, while consistent across synthetic and real graphs, does not constitute a formal guarantee.
