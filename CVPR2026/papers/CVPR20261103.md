## Problem

In privacy-sensitive identity recognition (person re-identification, face recognition), regulatory and copyright constraints severely limit real data collection, yet existing synthetic data methods still depend on that same scarce source distribution to train generators, creating a self-reinforcing data-scarcity loop. The paper identifies that prior approaches merely mimic the limited source distribution rather than surpassing it in task utility, and asks how to leverage rich general-domain generative priors to produce diverse, identity-preserving synthetic samples under minimal supervision.

## Input / Output

The generator is conditioned solely on an identity class label (no textual prompt), with the class embedding layer of a pretrained DiT-XL/2 replaced by a task-specific head. A reference feature memory bank stores normalized embeddings for each identity, which are consumed at reward-computation time to evaluate semantic consistency, distributional coverage, and covariance spread. The output is synthetic RGB images at 256×256 for person re-identification and 128×128 for face recognition, intended to augment the small target training set before downstream classifier training.

## Method

The framework proceeds in three stages. First, a cold-start step fine-tunes only the class-embedding head of a pretrained DiT-XL/2 (backbone frozen) on limited target samples to establish semantic alignment. Second, a DPOK-style policy-gradient RL stage optimizes the denoising trajectory against a multi-objective reward computed in feature space: (i) semantic consistency via cosine similarity between the generated feature and the identity prototype, (ii) distributional coverage via an RBF-kernel alignment term minus a redundancy penalty among generated samples, and (iii) expressive diversity via a squared-deviation penalty on the trace of the generated feature covariance relative to the reference. Each component is batch-standardized and combined with tanh-bounded weighting. Third, during downstream classifier training, a dynamic sample selection mechanism performs a one-step virtual gradient update on a mixed real/synthetic batch and retains only synthetic samples whose identity loss decreases most, adaptively emphasizing high-utility samples and mitigating residual distributional bias.

## Results

On person re-identification, the method achieves 88.6% mAP and 94.9% Rank-1 on Market-1501 (a 3.2% mAP gain over the ResNet-50 baseline) and 76.6% mAP / 79.3% Rank-1 on CUHK03-NP (a 2.5% mAP gain). On face recognition trained from a small CASIA-WebFace subset, it reaches 79.07% average verification accuracy across LFW, AgeDB, CFP-FP, CA-LFW, and CP-LFW, surpassing the real-data baseline (78.47%) and the strongest diffusion-based competitor NegFaceDiff (78.13%). On the RFW demographic bias benchmark, the method attains 69.78% average accuracy, the highest among all compared approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against real-image augmentation methods (R-Erasing, CIDAM, CaAug), the proposed method achieves the highest mAP on both Market-1501 (88.6% vs. 87.6% for R-Erasing) and CUHK03 (76.6% vs. 76.7% for R-Erasing, a near-tie), while also matching or exceeding their Rank-1 scores. Simulation-based approaches (FineGPR, InfinitePerson, VIPerson) suffer from a large domain gap and trail the proposed method by 2–31 mAP points depending on the dataset. Among generation-based competitors, GIF-SD degrades below the baseline (74.9% mAP vs. 85.4%), while IDiff achieves 85.4% mAP on Market-1501; the proposed method outperforms IDiff by 3.2 mAP on Market-1501 and 3.5 mAP on CUHK03. For face recognition, the method surpasses the strongest diffusion-based baseline NegFaceDiff by 0.94% average accuracy (79.07% vs. 78.13%) and the real-data CASIA-WebFace baseline by 0.60%, with the largest per-dataset gain on LFW (+1.90%) and CA-LFW (+3.01%). On the RFW racial bias benchmark, the method leads all competitors by 2.5% average over NegFaceDiff and shows the most balanced per-ethnicity scores, particularly improving Indian (+2.07%) and African (+2.54%) subsets over the real-data baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on DiT (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) as its generative backbone and DPOK (Fan et al., NeurIPS 2023) as its RL fine-tuning mechanism, but departs from both in purpose: rather than text-to-image alignment or general image quality, the RL signal is a task-specific multi-objective reward (semantic consistency, RBF-based distributional coverage, covariance expansion) tailored to identity preservation. Conceptually, it shifts the synthetic-data-generation paradigm from distribution-matching on scarce domain data (as in SFace, IDiff-Face, NegFaceDiff) to adapting a general-domain pretrained prior via reward-guided policy optimization, breaking what the authors frame as a self-reinforcing data-scarcity loop. The dynamic sample selection stage further distinguishes it from prior RL-diffusion work by extending the utility signal into downstream training rather than stopping at generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical recipe for privacy-sensitive identity tasks where large-scale data collection is legally or ethically infeasible, and the modest but consistent gains across person ReID and face recognition benchmarks (plus reduced demographic bias on RFW) suggest the RL-guided adaptation of general priors is a viable alternative to domain-specific generator training. However, the approach still depends on the quality of the pretrained backbone and requires task-specific reward tuning, limiting its plug-and-play applicability. Open questions include whether the method extends to video or event-based modalities, how it behaves in domains with poor prior alignment (e.g., medical imaging with very different visual statistics), and whether the multi-objective reward can be learned automatically rather than hand-designed.
