## Problem

Test-time computation scaling (e.g., Best-of-N) has proven effective for LLMs on complex reasoning tasks, but its adaptation to visual autoregressive image generation remains unexplored and faces unique obstacles: raster-scan decoding lacks a global blueprint of the full canvas, and the autoregressive nature makes early errors irrecoverable, causing Best-of-N to waste full-length computation on trajectories that are already misaligned with the prompt.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262234/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a compositional text prompt and produces a single high-resolution RGB image; for image editing, it takes a source image plus an edit instruction and produces the edited image. Internally, the image is represented as a grid of discrete VQ-VAE codebook tokens (h×w), and the canvas is partitioned into contiguous horizontal row segments (e.g., four quarters) that are generated and verified independently. A vision-language model (GPT-4.1) serves as a zero-shot verifier over partial grid images, and a reformulated prompt with explicit spatial layout is injected mid-generation to guide the remaining decoding.

## Method

GridAR partitions the image canvas into R₁=4 row-wise tiles and generates N independent partial candidates for the upper quarter in parallel, then uses a VLM verifier to reject infeasible candidates (e.g., wrong object count or color) while retaining all others to preserve diversity. Viable candidates are fixed as anchors and propagated into R₂=2 row grids for continued autoregressive generation, yielding four half-images that undergo a second verification round before final completion into four full images selected by an outcome reward model. To address the blueprint deficiency of raster-scan decoding, the verifier simultaneously performs a layout-specified prompt reformulation (e.g., 'eight bears' → 'three on top and five on the bottom'), which is then applied either via a three-way classifier-free guidance that orthogonalizes the reformulated-prompt logit direction against the original-prompt direction, or via a simpler prompt-replacement strategy. The entire pipeline requires no additional training and matches the token budget of standard Best-of-N with the same N, while redirecting computation away from erroneous trajectories.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench++, GridAR (N=4) with Janus-Pro achieves an average score of 0.5654, outperforming Best-of-N (N=8) by 14.4% while reducing wall-clock cost by 25.6%. On GenEval, Janus-Pro+GridAR (N=8) reaches 0.88 overall (0.79 Counting, 0.92 Position, 0.73 Color Attribution), surpassing Janus-Pro+BoN (N=8) at 0.86 overall. On PIE-Bench for image editing, GridAR achieves a 14.5% gain in semantic preservation (structure-aware distance 36.632 vs. 42.873) over Best-of-N (N=8), while matching its CLIP similarity for instruction following (25.532 vs. 25.628).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GridAR (N=4) with Janus-Pro decisively outperforms Best-of-N (N=8) on T2I-CompBench++, gaining 14.4% in average score (e.g., Color: 0.8050 vs. 0.7235; Shape: 0.6014 vs. 0.4177; Numeracy: 0.5684 vs. 0.5068) while using half the candidates and 25.6% less wall-clock time. Against diffusion baselines, Janus-Pro+GridAR (N=4) surpasses DALL·E 3 on Color (0.8050 vs. 0.7785), Shape (0.6014 vs. 0.6205 is a loss), Texture (0.7268 vs. 0.7036), and Numeracy (0.5684 vs. 0.5926 is a loss), but trails SD3 on Color (0.8050 vs. 0.8132) and 3D Spatial (0.3503 vs. 0.4084). On GenEval, Janus-Pro+GridAR (N=8) leads all AR baselines (Show-o, Emu3, Infinity, LlamaGen) and matches or exceeds SD3 (0.88 vs. 0.74 overall), with the largest gap on Position (0.92 vs. 0.33 for SD3). For image editing on PIE-Bench, GridAR (N=4) reduces structure-aware distance by 10.54% and MSE by 26.07% over Best-of-N (N=4), and outperforms Best-of-N (N=8) on source preservation (distance 36.632 vs. 42.873) while matching its CLIP similarity. The paper also compares against TTS-VAR [7] and step-by-step verification [38] from other AR families in the appendix, confirming GridAR's advantage in cost-performance trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends test-time scaling strategies from LLMs (Best-of-N, chain-of-thought) to visual autoregressive image generation, a setting where prior work has only begun to explore computation scaling (e.g., TTS-VAR, T2I-R1, step-by-step verification). It departs from naive Best-of-N by identifying two structural limitations of raster-scan AR decoding—absence of a global layout blueprint and irreversibility of early token errors—and addresses them through grid-partitioned progressive generation (inspired by tree search in LLMs) and layout-specified prompt reformulation. The framework builds on standard classifier-free guidance for AR models, extending it to a three-way formulation that orthogonalizes a reformulated layout prompt against the original, and is applied across multiple AR backbones (Janus-Pro, LlamaGen, EditAR) without any retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GridAR provides a training-free, model-agnostic recipe for improving visual AR generation quality at inference time, and the finding that N=4 GridAR outperforms N=8 Best-of-N while cutting wall-clock cost by ~25% offers a practically attractive cost-quality trade-off for practitioners deploying AR image models. The blueprint-deficiency diagnosis and its remedy via mid-generation prompt reformulation open a direction for addressing the fundamental sequential-planning weakness of raster-scan AR decoding. Open questions include how the approach scales to higher resolutions or non-raster-scan AR paradigms (masked, next-scale), whether the verifier quality bottleneck (currently GPT-4.1) can be closed with smaller open-source models, and whether the grid partitioning strategy generalizes to video or 3D generation.
