## Problem

Unrolled networks that embed the forward operator A into the architecture achieve state-of-the-art reconstruction quality, but training them on large-scale 3D problems (e.g., 501³ voxels) is infeasible on a single GPU because the network step at full resolution consumes prohibitive memory. Standard patch-based training does not apply because the forward operator in modalities like cone-beam CT couples all voxels globally, preventing a trivial block-diagonal decomposition.

## Input / Output

The method takes noisy linear measurements y ∈ R^m produced by a known forward operator A ∈ R^{m×n} and outputs a full 3D volume reconstruction x̂ ∈ R^n. For CBCT, inputs are sparse-view projections (30, 50, or 100 out of 1200 views) with 501³ voxel volumes; for MC-MRI, inputs are undersampled k-space data (acceleration rates 5 and 10) with 256×218×170 per coil across 12 coils. At test time, a two-step procedure is used: an initial full-volume unrolling followed by a domain-partitioned refinement pass, with the prior step evaluated patch-by-patch to stay within memory limits.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary techniques to scale unrolled PGD networks to arbitrarily large 3D problems. First, domain partitioning decomposes R^n into a patch subspace R^p and a context subspace R^q, reformulating the inverse problem as ỹ = Ã x_patch where Ã = AS^T and ỹ = y − AS_⊥^T x_context; this allows training on small patches (e.g., 8×384²) while the forward operator still acts globally, with the context provided by ground truth at train time and by an initial estimate at test time. Second, the normal operator A^T A is approximated by a diagonal-circulant factorization H = diag(m) F^{-1} diag(λ) F, whose parameters (m, λ) are fitted by gradient descent on random Gaussian vectors without any problem-specific data; on partitioned domains the convolution kernel is restricted to size k = 2p, avoiding full-volume FFTs. The backbone is a DRUNet (36.2M/96.5M params for 2D/3D) unrolled for K = 3 (CBCT) or K = 5 (MC-MRI) PGD iterations, trained end-to-end with a batch size of 1 on a single H100 GPU.

## Results

On the Walnut-CBCT dataset (501³ voxels, sparse-view reconstruction), the proposed Unrolled[3D]-ours achieves PSNR of 31.17 / 34.21 / 37.07 dB and SSIM of 0.877 / 0.926 / 0.947 at 30 / 50 / 100 views, outperforming all baselines while using only 44.70 GB VRAM on a single H100 GPU. The standard unrolled 3D network is infeasible (OOM) on this problem. On Calgary-Campinas MC-MRI, the method achieves PSNR of 37.36 / 34.25 dB at acceleration rates 5 and 10, within 0.4 dB of the standard unrolled 3D network (37.74 / 34.72 dB) while halving peak memory from 75.93 GB to 37.02 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method decisively outperforms all non-learned and learned baselines on Walnut-CBCT: FDK [15] (PSNR 24.74) and TV [18] (31.63) are far behind, while INR/instantNGP [40] (33.74), PnP-αPGD [25] (33.97 2D / 33.72 3D), and DPIR/RAM [60] (31.17) all trail by 3+ dB. The strongest learned baseline, 3D DRUNet post-processing [72] (35.22 dB), is surpassed by 1.85 dB in PSNR and 0.016 in SSIM. Critically, the standard unrolled network [53] is infeasible on CBCT (OOM on a single 80 GB GPU), whereas the proposed method trains and runs within 44.70 GB. On Calgary-Campinas MC-MRI, the standard Unrolled[3D] [53] retains a slight edge (37.74 vs. 37.36 dB PSNR at R=5) but at 2× the memory cost (75.93 vs. 37.02 GB); the proposed method matches or exceeds all other baselines (DRUNet [72]: 35.02 dB, PnP-αPGD [25]: 27.27 dB) while halving peak VRAM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unrolled network paradigm (Adler & Öktem, Sriram et al.) to large-scale 3D problems by combining two ideas: (1) a domain partitioning strategy that generalizes patch-based training (Zhang et al. 2017) to settings where the forward operator globally couples all voxels (e.g., cone-beam CT), and (2) a normal operator approximation built on the diagonal-circulant matrix factorization of Schmid et al. (2000), enabling FFT-based data-consistency updates. It departs from prior unrolled methods that require full-volume training and from deep equilibrium approaches that still need a full network evaluation per iteration, by making both the network step and the data-fidelity step independently scalable to arbitrary patch sizes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: training and inference of unrolled 3D networks on 501³ volumes becomes feasible on a single 80 GB GPU, a regime that was previously infeasible (OOM for standard unrolling). The normal operator approximation can be fitted with random vectors alone, requiring no problem-specific data, which is valuable when training data is scarce. Limitations include the focus on Gaussian noise (Poisson models for low-dose CT are not directly addressed), the approximation's limited benefit for MC-MRI where FFT-based data steps are already cheap, and the modest performance gap over strong post-processing baselines like 3D DRUNet. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-Cartesian MRI, whether additional diagonal-circulant factors help for more complex operators, and how it interacts with diffusion-based priors.
