## Problem

Existing DiT-based video virtual try-on methods fail to capture fine-grained garment dynamics (wrinkles, backside textures, motion-induced variations) and preserve background integrity across frames, while simultaneously incurring high computational costs from additional interaction modules bolted onto the DiT backbone. Public datasets remain limited in scale and resolution, further restricting generalization. The paper asks whether keyframes—which inherently encode both multi-view garment dynamics and background structure—can serve as a lightweight, architecture-preserving source of detail injection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260879/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes five inputs: a reference garment image, the source video V_in, a garment-agnostic video V_agn (produced by inpainting clothed regions per frame), binary agnostic masks, and OpenPose skeletal-keypoint pose sequences. All video processing operates at 810×1080 resolution with 81-frame clips. The output is a temporally consistent try-on video in which the character's clothing is replaced by the target garment while motion, background, and garment dynamics are preserved; the same pipeline also supports static image try-on as a secondary task.

## Method

KeyTailor builds on the Wan2.1-I2V-14B-720P DiT backbone and introduces a keyframe-driven details injection strategy with three components. First, an instruction-guided keyframe sampling (IKS) module uses QWen to parse view-action instructions, generates anchor pose frames via HumanParsing, and scores each frame by a motion-difference and garment-area-ratio composite, applying a dual-threshold selection to ensure temporal uniformity. Second, a Garment Dynamic Details Enhancement (GDDE) module performs single-image try-on on the first agnostic frame using FiTDiT with LoRA, encodes the result into a VAE latent, then distills multi-view garment variation details from keyframe garment-region latents through a two-layer 1×1 convolution + LayerNorm component. Third, a Collaborative Background Details Optimization (CBDO) module combines a coarse 4-layer 3D-convolutional encoding of the agnostic video with a fine-grained keyframe background latent (selected by highest background completeness score), blended at α=0.3. The enriched garment and background latents are fused with pose and mask tokens via a three-step projection-and-addition pipeline and injected into standard DiT blocks without any architectural modification; only LoRA adapters on self- and cross-attention are trained, adding just 2.10% parameters over the base model.

## Results

On the proposed ViT-HD dataset (paired setting), KeyTailor achieves VFID_I^p = 7.53, VFID_R^p = 0.163, SSIM = 0.907, and LPIPS = 0.040, roughly halving the VFID_I^p of the strongest baseline MagicTryOn (14.06) and reducing LPIPS by more than 50% (0.040 vs. 0.083). On the public ViViD benchmark, KeyTailor (fine-tuned on ViT-HD) reaches VFID_I^p = 8.22 and SSIM = 0.902, outperforming DreamVVT (11.02 / 0.874) and MagicTryOn (12.20 / 0.884). On image-based try-on (VITON-HD), KeyTailor attains FID^p = 5.29 and KID^p = 0.72, surpassing the best image baseline CatVTON (6.14 / 0.96). In terms of efficiency, KeyTailor adds only 2.10% parameters over the Wan2.1 backbone (0.206B trainable) versus 15.11% for MagicTryOn and 157.10% for ViViD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KeyTailor outperforms all 13 baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. Among video-centered DiT-based methods, it beats MagicTryOn [16] by ~47% on VFID_I^p (7.53 vs. 14.06 on ViT-HD) and by ~14% on ViViD (8.22 vs. 12.20), while also achieving the highest SSIM (0.907 vs. 0.862) and lowest LPIPS (0.040 vs. 0.083). Against CatV2TON [7], the margin is even larger: VFID_I^p drops from 15.87 to 7.53 on ViT-HD. On the ViViD benchmark, KeyTailor§ (fine-tuned on ViT-HD) surpasses DreamVVT§ [31] by 25% on VFID_I^p (8.22 vs. 11.02) and by 3.5% on SSIM (0.902 vs. 0.874). For image-based try-on on VITON-HD, KeyTailor leads CatVTON [6] (FID^p 5.29 vs. 6.14) and IDM-VTON [5] (5.29 vs. 6.34), and on DressCode it achieves the best FID^p (2.75) and KID^p (0.52). Older GAN-based video methods (FW-GAN [8], MV-TON [30], ClothFormer [13]) are substantially outperformed on the VVT dataset, where KeyTailor reaches VFID_I^p = 1.23 versus ClothFormer's 3.97. The user study (80 participants) confirms these quantitative gains, with KeyTailor receiving the highest preference rates in visual quality, semantic consistency, and overall quality, particularly over CatV2TON and ViViD.

## Relation to Prior Work

KeyTailor builds on the Wan2.1 video DiT backbone and FiTDiT for initial garment appearance injection, but deliberately departs from the dominant paradigm in DiT-based VVT (CatV2TON, MagicTryOn, DreamVVT) of inserting additional cross-attention or interaction modules into the transformer. Instead, it treats keyframes as a free source of multi-view garment dynamics and background structure, distilling them through two lightweight modules and injecting the resulting latents into unmodified DiT blocks via LoRA fine-tuning. The companion ViT-HD dataset addresses a data-scarcity bottleneck that prior methods (trained on VVT or ViViD) have not resolved, providing 15,070 HD samples at 810×1080 with diverse garments and clean subject integrity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.10% parameter overhead versus 15–157% for competing DiT-based methods makes high-fidelity video try-on substantially more accessible for deployment, and the ViT-HD dataset fills a clear gap in the field by offering the first large-scale, high-resolution VVT benchmark. Open questions include whether the keyframe-driven strategy generalizes to longer videos (beyond 81 frames), more occluded or multi-person scenes, and whether the instruction-guided sampling can be replaced by learned selection to reduce reliance on a VLM. The approach also raises the broader question of whether 'inject more structured information' can consistently outperform 'add more parameters' across other video generation tasks.
