## Problem

Large-scale image datasets contain faces, documents, and other identity signals that pose privacy risks when models memorize and leak them, yet existing anonymization methods rely on closed-set detectors that miss open-domain privacy cues and introduce artifacts that degrade downstream utility. The core challenge is that anonymization is not merely a removal problem: sensitive content must be rewritten so identity cues are suppressed without destroying the semantic structure needed for learning, and no prior pipeline combines open-domain privacy reasoning with controlled, context-aware image editing.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes an RGB image and a set of predefined privacy criteria (derived from VISPR) as input. A VLM first inspects the image and, if flagged as unsafe, produces a private caption (full scene description) and a public caption (same scene minus sensitive details); an LLM then generates a structured edit instruction specifying identity-neutral substitutions. The diffusion editor consumes the public caption and edit instruction as dual textual conditions to produce a single anonymized image, with no segmentation masks or attribute labels required.

## Method

Unsafe2Safe is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 uses a VLM (InternVL2.5) for privacy inspection and paired caption generation, then an LLM (Qwen3-4B-Instruct) to merge the public caption with plausible pseudo-private details into a compact edit instruction. Stage 2 applies a text-guided diffusion editor (InstructPix2Pix or FlowEdit) conditioned on both the public caption (semantic anchor preserving layout and non-private objects) and the edit instruction (directing targeted rewriting of sensitive regions). The key architectural contribution is Safe Cross Attention, an auxiliary module that concatenates the token embeddings of the public caption and edit instruction into a unified sequence projected into shared Key/Value tensors, giving the denoising process simultaneous access to preservation and transformation signals. Fine-tuning InstructPix2Pix on automatically generated (private caption, public caption, edit instruction) triplets further improves both privacy protection and semantic fidelity, with the Safe Attention variant achieving the strongest demographic neutrality and lowest face similarity.

## Results

On Caltech101, Unsafe2Safe (U2S) with FlowEdit and the LLM-composed text prior achieves 92.884% top-1 accuracy (vs. 94.277% raw), while reducing FaceSim from 1.0000 to 0.3428 and TextSim from 1.0000 to 0.4881. On MIT Indoor67, the best U2S variant reaches 80.746% accuracy (vs. 83.881% raw) with FaceSim dropping to 0.2294. On OK-VQA, U2S with FlowEdit and the LLM prior actually exceeds raw-data accuracy (70.93% vs. 60.64%) while achieving the strongest privacy scores (FaceSim 0.1951, TextSim 0.1196). Fine-tuning InstructPix2Pix on the generated triplets further pushes Caltech101 accuracy to 95.116% with improved privacy metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Unsafe2Safe substantially outperforms both DeepPrivacy2 and FaceAnon on all four privacy metrics: FaceSim drops to 0.34–0.37 (vs. 0.39–0.46 for baselines) on Caltech101 and 0.21–0.23 (vs. 0.35–0.50) on Indoor67; TextSim falls to 0.49–0.52 (vs. 0.94–0.96) on Caltech101 and 0.21–0.24 (vs. 0.77–0.87) on Indoor67; and Race Entropy rises to 0.76–0.88 (vs. 0.61–0.75). On Caltech101 classification accuracy, U2S matches or slightly exceeds both baselines (94.79% with c_edit vs. 94.60% DP2 and 94.85% FaceAnon). However, on Indoor67, both baselines retain higher accuracy (84.03%) compared to U2S's best of 80.75%, a gap of roughly 3–7 points attributed to the more aggressive scene-level edits. On OK-VQA, U2S with FlowEdit achieves 70.93% VQA accuracy, surpassing both raw data (60.64%) and FaceAnon (66.32%), while simultaneously delivering the lowest FaceSim (0.1951) and TextSim (0.1196) among all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: instruction-driven diffusion editing (InstructPix2Pix, FlowEdit, FreePrompt), visual privacy detection (VISPR), and face-centric anonymization (DeepPrivacy2, FaceAnon). It departs from prior anonymization pipelines that rely on closed-set face/person detectors followed by blurring or inpainting by replacing the detection stage with open-domain VLM-based privacy inspection and the editing stage with text-guided diffusion. The Safe Cross Attention module is a targeted architectural addition to the UNet that jointly conditions denoising on both a public caption (semantic anchor) and an edit instruction (transformation directive), addressing the over-editing problem inherent in instruction-following diffusion models. The dual-caption (private/public) plus LLM-generated edit instruction conditioning scheme is the paper's core conceptual contribution, unifying what prior work treated as separate detection and generation steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone constructing large-scale training datasets: Unsafe2Safe offers a fully automated, annotation-free pipeline that scales to open-domain imagery without requiring segmentation masks or predefined privacy taxonomies. The unified four-axis evaluation framework (Quality, Cheating, Privacy, Utility) addresses a real gap in how anonymization methods are assessed, moving beyond face-similarity-only metrics. Key open questions include whether VLM-based privacy detection generalizes to adversarially crafted or culturally specific privacy cues, how the pipeline performs on video (temporal consistency), and whether the VLM-judged privacy scores (VLMScore) truly correlate with human-perceived privacy risk. The authors themselves frame the system as 'a configurable dataset construction tool, not an autonomous arbiter of privacy,' leaving the definition of acceptable privacy criteria to practitioners.
