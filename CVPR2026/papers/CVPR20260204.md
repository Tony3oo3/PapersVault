## Problem

Activation outliers in large-scale transformer models create excessively large dynamic ranges that cause severe accuracy drops during quantization, yet their root cause and a practical mitigation for already-pretrained models remain unclear. This paper establishes that outlier severity escalates with pre-training scale (CLIP → SigLIP → SigLIP2) and is directly caused by uncontrolled growth of dominant singular values in weight matrices, an artifact of AdamW's anisotropic optimization. The key gap addressed is how to surgically correct this spectral pathology in existing pretrained checkpoints without expensive retraining from scratch.

## Input / Output

S²D operates on pretrained ViT-based vision encoders (SigLIP2-Base-384, CLIP, SigLIP) and vision-language models (LLaVA1.5 with SigLIP2 + Qwen2.5-0.5B), applied as a regularizer during downstream fine-tuning or as a standalone post-processing step on weight matrices. For vision tasks, inputs are standard RGB images at 384×384 or 512×512 resolution producing class labels, bounding boxes, or segmentation masks; for VLM tasks, inputs are RGB image + text prompt pairs producing free-form text responses. The method does not alter the input/output interface of the model—it conditions the internal weight geometry so that subsequent quantization (W3A4 through W8A8) preserves accuracy.

## Method

S²D defines a spectral regularizer L_S²D = (λ/(n+1)) Σᵢ σᵢ^(n+1) with n>1, which disproportionately penalizes the largest singular values of weight matrices while leaving smaller ones nearly untouched, in contrast to standard L2 decay that shrinks all singular values uniformly. To make this selective and efficient, the method uses the Principal Component Dominance Ratio (PCDR_k) to identify which layers have pathological spectral concentration (PCDR_k ≥ τ) and targets only their top-k singular components. SVD is computed amortized every m=100 training steps, with cached singular vectors reused for the S²D gradient (∂L/∂W_ij = λ Σ_k U_ik σ_k^n V_jk) in between. The approach is applied on top of standard AdamW fine-tuning (or combined with QAT using STEs), requiring no architectural changes and preserving full-precision accuracy while producing well-conditioned, quantization-ready weight matrices.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k with SigLIP2-Base-384, S²D fine-tuned models achieve up to 7.4-point gains over AdamW under ERQ W4A4 PTQ (73.0% vs. 65.6%) and up to 32-point gains under RepQ-ViT W5A5 (78.0% vs. 46.0%). Under QAT, S²D adds 2.5% (W3A4) and 3.9% (W4A4) absolute accuracy over vanilla QAT. On MS-COCO object detection, S²D yields a 29.9-point AP50 improvement at W5A5 ERQ (40.7 vs. 10.8), and on VLM benchmarks (LLaVA1.5), S²D improves GQA W4A4 from 35.3 to 40.1 and TextVQA W4A4 from 6.8 to 9.8, while also slightly improving full-precision accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is standard AdamW fine-tuning (Kingma & Ba, 2017), which S²D outperforms across every PTQ method (ERQ, PTQ4ViT, RepQ-ViT), every bit setting (W4A4 through W8A8), and both resolutions (384 and 512). The most dramatic gaps appear at low bit-widths: under RepQ-ViT W5A5, S²D jumps from 46.0% to 78.0% (+32 pts), and under PTQ4ViT W5A5 from 44.4% to 62.0% (+17.6 pts). At W8A8, where outliers matter less, gains shrink to 0.1–0.7 points, confirming S²D's benefit is concentrated in the low-bit regime. The second baseline, Muon (Jordan, 2024), is an orthogonal optimizer designed for training from scratch; Figure 1 shows that applying Muon to an AdamW-pretrained SigLIP2 model does not meaningfully reduce activation outliers, whereas S²D substantially suppresses them. Full-precision accuracy is preserved (or slightly improved) in all comparisons, indicating S²D acts as a pure conditioning step rather than a capacity trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of activation outlier research and model quantization. Prior work (Dettmers et al., Xiao et al., Wei et al.) identified outliers as a quantization barrier, and subsequent methods (SmoothQuant, SpinQuant, OmniQuant) worked around them via scaling or rotation, while orthogonal optimizers like Muon addressed the root cause but required training from scratch. S²D departs from both lines by proposing a targeted spectral regularizer that can be applied during fine-tuning of existing pre-trained models, and by introducing PCDR as a mechanistic diagnostic linking outliers to specific dominant singular components rather than treating them as a monolithic phenomenon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: enabling robust W4A4 quantization of heavily pre-trained vision-language models (SigLIP2) that were previously nearly impossible to deploy at low precision, with a simple drop-in regularizer requiring no retraining from scratch. The conceptual reframe—that outliers are optimization artifacts of AdamW's anisotropic updates rather than functionally necessary features—opens the door to suppressing them at the source during pre-training. Open questions include whether S²D interacts beneficially with alternative optimizers, whether applying it during large-scale pre-training (not just fine-tuning) prevents outlier formation entirely, and whether the spectral mechanism generalizes beyond ViT architectures to other model families.
