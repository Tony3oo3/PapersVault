## Problem

Existing video action detection methods rely on visual foundation models that capture semantics but struggle with fine-grained, compositional actions because they ignore explicit motion dynamics. When skeleton motion is added as a second modality, two issues arise: label-driven skeleton encoders produce features too coarse to describe primitive-level motion, and naive fusion (concatenation, late fusion) causes interference between heterogeneous motion and visual feature spaces rather than structured complementarity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video segments (processed by a frozen encoder such as I3D or ViCLIP into per-frame features F_v ∈ R^{C_v×T}) alongside 2D/3D skeleton sequences extracted by a pose estimator, represented as spatio-temporal tensors m ∈ R^{C_in×T×M×J} where M is the number of detected persons and J the number of joints. Output is a frame-wise multi-label score matrix P ∈ R^{Cls×T} produced by a per-frame classifier, optimized with binary cross-entropy. Long untrimmed videos are handled via a sliding window of size W_s, and multi-person scenes are aggregated through confidence-ranked pooling over the person dimension.

## Method

MoVie introduces a two-stage motion-visual alignment pipeline. First, a Structural Motion Projection (SMP) module projects spatio-temporal skeleton features onto a frozen, pretrained motion dictionary D_m ∈ R^{K×C_m} (from ViA) to obtain per-primitive activation magnitudes α = ‖D_m F‖₂, yielding viewpoint-invariant, physically interpretable motion descriptors. Second, a Motion-Guided Feature Regularization (MGFR) module aligns these primitives with visual features via a learnable orthogonal matrix Q ∈ R^{K×C_v}, producing F_mv = ε(F_v) + λ(Q^T α̂), where Q is re-orthogonalized each iteration via Gram-Schmidt to keep motion directions independent and prevent channel mixing. A temporal consistency loss L_align further enforces that motion-induced feature changes match natural visual variation. The resulting features are fed to an MS-TCT temporal encoder (alternating Transformer and TCN layers) with cached history features concatenated along the channel dimension, and the whole system is trained end-to-end with BCE detection loss plus the alignment term while keeping the motion dictionary frozen.

## Results

With ViCLIP features, MoVie achieves 50.1% frame-level mAP on TSU-CS, 30.1% on TSU-CV, 33.5% on Charades, and 48.3% on Multi-THUMOS, setting new state-of-the-art across all four benchmarks. With I3D features, MoVie reaches 49.6% TSU-CS, 28.6% TSU-CV, 29.2% Charades, and 46.8% Multi-THUMOS. On PKU-MMD (event-level, IoU 0.1), MoVie scores 92.8% mAP, surpassing the previous best multi-modal baseline (Augmented-RGB) by +6.5%. The largest single-dataset gain is +15.9% over the prior visual-only SoTA (MS-TCT) on TSU-CS with I3D features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoVie (I3D) outperforms the previous visual-only SoTA MS-TCT [12] by +15.9% on TSU-CS (49.6 vs. 33.7), +3.8% on Charades (29.2 vs. 25.4), and +3.7% on Multi-THUMOS (46.8 vs. 43.1). Against the strongest prior multi-modal baseline MMFF [62] (ViCLIP), MoVie gains +8.5% on TSU-CS (50.1 vs. 41.6), +4.4% on TSU-CV (30.1 vs. 25.7), +4.3% on Charades (33.5 vs. 29.2), and +2.0% on Multi-THUMOS (48.3 vs. 46.3). Compared to the motion-only LAC [57], MoVie (I3D) improves by +12.8% on TSU-CS and +23.4% on Multi-THUMOS, confirming that structured motion is most effective as a guide for visual features rather than a standalone signal. MoVie also surpasses the text-visual AAN [14] by +8.8% on TSU-CS (50.1 vs. 41.3) and +1.5% on Charades (33.5 vs. 32.0) with ViCLIP, indicating that geometric motion priors complement visual semantics more effectively than language cues for fine-grained temporal action detection. On the event-level PKU-MMD benchmark, MoVie (92.8%) exceeds Augmented-RGB [9] (86.3%) by +6.5% and MMFF [62] (79.6%) by +13.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoVie builds directly on three prior works: ViA [59] provides the pretrained motion dictionary that underpins the Structural Motion Projection module, MS-TCT [12] supplies the alternating Transformer-TCN temporal backbone, and UNIK [56] informs the spatio-temporal skeleton encoder. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior multi-modal fusion approaches (e.g., MMFF [62], Augmented-RGB [9]) that treat skeleton features as an auxiliary input combined via attention or concatenation. Instead, MoVie reframes motion as a structural regulator: it decomposes skeleton sequences into viewpoint-invariant motion primitives and uses an orthogonal projection to modulate visual features along physically meaningful directions, rather than merging two heterogeneous feature spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured, primitive-level motion provides substantially more useful guidance for visual representations than raw or label-driven skeleton features, yielding large gains (+15.9% on TSU-CS) that persist across datasets and backbones (I3D, ViCLIP). However, the approach still depends on reliable pose estimation, and fine-grained actions with weak motion cues (e.g., 'Drink from bottle', 'Stir coffee') show minor regressions, suggesting that hand-object interaction modeling and motion uncertainty estimation remain open. The orthogonal regularization mechanism is a clean, interpretable design choice that could inform other multi-modal alignment problems, but its generality beyond skeleton-based human motion is not yet tested.
