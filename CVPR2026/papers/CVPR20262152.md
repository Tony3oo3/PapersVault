## Problem

Existing Aerial VLN methods rely on aligning long sequences of landmark descriptions with UAV observations (lookahead paradigm), which is computationally expensive and fails to disambiguate common urban landmarks like 'tree' or 'bridge.' Crucially, these methods overlook directional cues naturally embedded in instructions (e.g., 'turn left,' 'go past the building on your right'), which encode rich egocentric spatial context that humans use for navigation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262152/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language navigation instruction, a 6-view panoramic RGB observation (front, left, right, back, top, bottom), and depth images for 3D landmark localization. It outputs one of six discrete actions (Move Forward, Turn Left, Turn Right, Ascend, Descend, Stop) along with an execution count, where each step corresponds to fixed magnitudes (15° rotation, 2 m vertical, 5 m forward). The system is entirely zero-shot, requiring no task-specific training; the SLKB is populated by sampling 50 trajectories per seen scene or pre-rendering images for unseen scenes.

## Method

LookasideVLN comprises three components: (1) a Spatial Landmark Knowledge Base (SLKB) that stores lightweight (textual description, 3D location) pairs retrieved via word-embedding cosine similarity, (2) an Egocentric Lookaside Graph (ELG) that hierarchically organizes candidate landmark locations into layers, with inter-layer edges annotated by egocentric directional relationships (horizontal deflection angle, elevation gain, horizontal distance) computed relative to the agent's estimated orientation at each waypoint, and (3) a Lookaside MLLM Navigation Agent (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) that performs chain-of-thought reasoning over four stages—observation description, navigation progress tracking, direction-aware path selection from instruction-like ELG path descriptions, and action reasoning. The key insight is that converting ELG paths into natural-language descriptions (e.g., 'turn left 30 degrees, move forward 10 meters and descend 4 meters to reach the intersection') allows the MLLM to perform semantic alignment with the user instruction, effectively using directional cues to disambiguate landmarks with only a 2-step lookahead rather than full-sequence graph search.

## Results

On the AerialVLN-S benchmark, LookasideVLN (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) achieves 14.7% SR and 5.4 SDTW on the Seen split, and 12.6% SR on the Unseen split, outperforming the prior state-of-the-art CityNavAgent (GPT-4V) which scores 13.9% SR / 5.1 SDTW (Seen) and 11.7% SR / 5.0 SDTW (Unseen). On the full-scale AerialVLN benchmark, LookasideVLN reaches 6.4% SR on the Unseen split, nearly doubling the best learning-based baseline Zhao'25 (3.2% SR Unseen). Notably, LookasideVLN uses only a 2-level lookahead (N_ahead=2) while CityNavAgent performs full landmark-sequence lookahead over the entire urban scene graph.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the state-of-the-art CityNavAgent (GPT-4V), LookasideVLN wins on SR (14.7 vs 13.9 Seen; 12.6 vs 11.7 Unseen), OSR (31.2 vs 30.2 Seen; 36.0 vs 35.2 Unseen), and NE on the Seen split (77.1 vs 80.8), but trails on SDTW and NE for the Unseen split (3.6 vs 5.0 SDTW; 100.9 vs 60.2 NE). Compared to STMR (GPT-4o), LookasideVLN achieves higher SR on both splits (14.7 vs 12.6 Seen; 12.6 vs 10.8 Unseen) and lower NE (77.1 vs 96.3 Seen; 100.9 vs 119.5 Unseen), though STMR slightly edges out on OSR Seen (31.6 vs 31.2). All learning-based baselines (Seq2Seq, CMA, LAG, Zhao'25) are substantially outperformed, with LookasideVLN achieving 2–7× higher SR. Zero-shot indoor VLN methods (NavGPT, MapGPT) perform near-randomly on the aerial task, confirming the domain gap. On the full-scale AerialVLN benchmark, LookasideVLN's 6.4% Unseen SR nearly doubles Zhao'25's 3.2%, demonstrating stronger generalization without any task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'lookahead' paradigm in Aerial VLN (exemplified by CityNavAgent and LM-Nav), which constructs large-scale global scene graphs and performs path planning by aligning long landmark sequences with visual observations. Instead, LookasideVLN repurposes directional cues already present in natural language instructions ('turn left,' 'go past the building on your right') as the primary spatial signal, encoding them in a compact Egocentric Lookaside Graph rather than a full scene graph. The Spatial Landmark Knowledge Base draws on retrieval-augmented generation ideas from embodied planning (EmbodiedRAG, RAG-Driver) but stores only lightweight (description, location) pairs rather than rich visual features, justified by a Liebig's-Law argument that the linguistic bottleneck makes detailed visual features unnecessary. The Lookaside MLLM Agent follows the chain-of-thought reasoning pattern established by NavGPT and CityNavAgent but operates over direction-aware path descriptions rather than raw landmark sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that egocentric directional cues can substitute for expensive full-sequence landmark alignment—offers a simpler, more interpretable path to Aerial VLN that could reduce the computational overhead of maintaining city-scale scene graphs. However, the empirical gains are modest and mixed: while SR and SDTW improve slightly over CityNavAgent on AerialVLN-S, the Navigation Error on the Unseen split is substantially worse (100.9 m vs. 60.2 m), suggesting the method may trade trajectory fidelity for endpoint accuracy. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even larger urban environments, how it performs with real (non-simulated) sensor data, and whether the reliance on a 72B-parameter MLLM API is practical for on-board UAV deployment. The method's zero-shot, training-free nature is appealing, but the absolute success rates (~15%) remain far from deployment-ready.
