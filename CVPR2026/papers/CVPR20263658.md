## Problem

Existing image forgery localization methods learn discriminative patterns of known forgeries, making them brittle to novel manipulation types as generative editing techniques rapidly evolve. The persistent tension between architectural specialization and cross-dataset generalization remains a central bottleneck, as detectors tightly coupled to specific training distributions fail on unseen diffusion models or traditional tampering operations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263658/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB image as input and produces a pixel-level binary probability mask at the same spatial resolution, indicating forged regions. Internally, the input is consumed twice: first by a frozen MAE to generate a reconstruction residual map, and then by the shared segmentation network which jointly processes the original image and the (amplified) residual as dual-stream inputs. The final output is a sigmoid-activated dense mask supervised by ground-truth forgery annotations.

## Method

IFA-Net implements a two-stage 'Detect-Guide-Amplify' closed loop grounded in a frozen MAE (ViT-Large, ImageNet-pretrained) as a universal realness prior. In Stage 1, the frozen MAE performs unconditional reconstruction to expose initial manifold deviations; a Dual-Stream Segmentation Network (DSSN, built on MiT-B2 with cross-attention fusion between a content stream and an artifact stream) fuses the original image with the residual to predict a coarse mask. In Stage 2, a Task-Adaptive Prior Injection (TAPI) module encodes the coarse mask into prompt tokens that modulate the frozen MAE encoder features via FiLM, steering a trainable decoder to fail more strongly on suspicious regions and produce an amplified residual. The shared DSSN then refines the mask from this amplified signal. Training jointly optimizes both stages with BCE + Dice losses (α=0.5), keeping the MAE encoder frozen throughout to preserve the realness prior while only the decoder and segmentation network are learned.

## Results

On the four diffusion-based GIT benchmarks (OpenSDID, GIT10K, CocoGlide, Inpaint32K), IFA-Net achieves an average IoU of 0.778 and F1 of 0.855, surpassing the second-best method (DcDsDiff: IoU 0.713, F1 0.774) by 6.5% IoU and 8.1% F1. On the three traditional tampering TT benchmarks (IMD2020, NIST16, CASIA), IFA-Net reaches an average F1 of 0.708, with a standout result on NIST16 (IoU 0.949, F1 0.974), though DcDsDiff retains a slight edge on the TT average (IoU 0.625, F1 0.732). The ablation confirms the TAPI module contributes the largest single gain (+6.1% IoU on GIT-AVG) over the dual-stream baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On all four GIT benchmarks, IFA-Net achieves the best IoU and F1, with the closest competitor being DcDsDiff (e.g., GIT10K: 0.928 vs 0.801 IoU; CocoGlide: 0.887 vs 0.878 IoU) and MaskCLIP on OpenSDID (0.487 vs 0.427 IoU). On traditional tampering, IFA-Net dominates NIST16 (IoU 0.949 vs DcDsDiff's 0.843) but trails DcDsDiff on CASIA (0.416 vs 0.637 IoU) and is marginally behind on IMD2020 (0.392 vs 0.396 IoU), resulting in DcDsDiff holding a slight TT-average advantage (F1 0.732 vs 0.708). Classical baselines (TruFor, PSCC-Net, MVSS-Net, SPAN) are consistently outperformed by 10–50+ points in IoU across both GIT and TT settings. IML-ViT is competitive on GIT10K (IoU 0.882) and CASIA (F1 0.806) but degrades sharply on other datasets, highlighting its limited generalization. Overall, IFA-Net's realness-prior approach yields the strongest and most consistent performance on diffusion-based forgeries while remaining competitive on traditional tampering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reconstruction-error-based forgery detection line (AEROBLADE, FIRE, DcDsDiff, LARE²) by replacing static generative priors with a frozen MAE and, crucially, introducing a task-adaptive feedback loop (TAPI) where coarse segmentation predictions are converted into FiLM prompts that steer the MAE decoder to amplify reconstruction failures in suspicious regions. It departs from the dominant discriminative paradigm (TruFor, MVSS-Net, SPAN, IML-ViT) that learns manipulation-specific artifact patterns, instead modeling a universal 'realness' prior. The dual-stream segmentation backbone (DSSN) draws on SegFormer and multi-branch contrastive designs (CFL-Net, MFINet), while the prompt-injection mechanism connects to visual prompting literature for segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The realness-driven, closed-loop formulation offers a more principled path to generalization across unseen generators and manipulation types than memorizing forgery signatures, which is a persistent bottleneck in the field. However, the robustness evaluation reveals that under severe JPEG compression (quality ≤ 50) or strong Gaussian blur (level ≥ 15), DcDsDiff's global diffusion-prior reconstruction remains more stable, suggesting that manifold-deviation amplification is sensitive to texture-destroying distortions. Open questions include whether the frozen MAE prior truly generalizes to future, architecturally different generators (e.g., autoregressive or flow-based models), and whether the two-stage loop can be extended to video or multimodal forensics as the authors note.
