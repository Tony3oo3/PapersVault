## Problem

Existing visual personalization methods focus on identity replication—reproducing a subject's appearance across scenes—while ignoring the broader visual language that makes content feel personally authored: aesthetic preferences, cultural context, and stylistic familiarity. No benchmark or evaluation protocol exists to measure whether a generative model's output is indistinguishable from what a given person might plausibly create or share, and real-world user data is inaccessible due to privacy constraints, fundamentally limiting research at scale.

## Input / Output

The system takes a short text query (e.g., 'A preferred outdoor spot') together with a structured persona profile: demographics, an element library of atomic visual terms (lighting, materials, pose, foreground, background), and 30 captioned assets represented as 'deferred renderings'—attribute-rich text intermediates rather than raw pixels—enabling privacy-safe operation. VPRAG produces a personalized prompt (constrained to a 3-phrase or 150-word budget) that is fed to an external T2I or I2I model (Qwen-Image, Nano-Banana, etc.) to yield a generated or edited image, and a text-only VPTT score (0–1) that serves as a scalable proxy for human perceptual judgment.

## Method

VPRAG is a zero-shot, inference-time retrieval-augmented generation engine that personalizes prompts without any per-user retraining. It performs hierarchical two-level retrieval: at the post level, captions are embedded (text-embedding-3-small) and weighted via temperature-controlled Boltzmann softmax, with entropy of the resulting distribution (n_eff = exp(H)) adaptively capping how many posts are retrieved; at the element level, a lightweight MiniLM encoder ranks atomic visual cues within semantic categories (foreground, background, lighting, pose) by relevance to the query. A proportional-fair quota allocation distributes the retrieval budget across categories so high-weight posts contribute more while low-weight ones preserve diversity. Selected elements are composed with a persona summary into a final prompt under a token-length budget, optionally refined by an LLM. The VPTT Score, a convex combination of Persona Alignment (cosine similarity to persona text), GS Reconstruction (projection onto the Gram-Schmidt orthonormal basis of caption embeddings), Cluster Proximity (distance to thematic centroids), and Novelty (trigram overlap penalty), provides a differentiable, text-only metric validated to correlate strongly (Spearman ρ ≈ 0.68) with human and VLM judgments.

## Results

On the 6,000-annotation human study, VPRAG achieves a VPTT score-c of 0.464, a VLM score of 4.32/5, and a human perceptual score of 3.34/5, with a 62.0% human winning accuracy. The text-only VPTT score-c correlates with human judgments at Spearman ρ=0.68 (generation: 0.78) and with VLM scores at ρ=0.68 (generation: 0.78), validating it as a reliable perceptual proxy. At scale (10,000 personas, 120,000 evaluations), VPRAG achieves a VPTT score of 0.631 (Qwen), 0.640 (GPT-4o-mini), and 0.625 (Gemini) for generation, consistently outperforming BRAG (0.581–0.628) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPRAG outperforms the BRAG baseline (Tailored Visions [7]) across all three evaluation modalities: VPTT score-c (0.464 vs. 0.420), VLM score (4.32 vs. 3.52), and human perceptual score (3.34 vs. 2.69), with a particularly large gap in winning accuracy (62.0% vs. 21.3% human, 73.3% vs. 19.3% text). The Persona Only baseline (demographics without asset history) sits between Baseline and BRAG, confirming that structured visual memory provides substantial additional signal beyond demographic context. At the 10,000-persona scale (Table 3), VPRAG maintains its lead over BRAG by 0.04–0.05 VPTT score across all three LLM backbones (Qwen, GPT-4o-mini, Gemini), with Cohen's d values of 0.1–1.1 indicating that the gap is most pronounced for the smaller Qwen model. The BRAG baseline tends to overfit to captions (copy-pasting), earning high alignment but low originality, whereas VPRAG's hierarchical retrieval and entropy-guided selection preserve the alignment–novelty balance. The combined BRAG+VPRAG variant achieves marginally higher VLM scores (4.30 vs. 4.08 for generation) but does not consistently surpass VPRAG alone on the text-level VPTT score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant identity-replication paradigm in visual personalization (DreamBooth, LoRA, IP-Adapter) by reframing the goal as contextual authenticity—capturing a person's aesthetic preferences, cultural context, and visual familiarity rather than their appearance. Methodologically, VPRAG builds on the personalized RAG concept from Tailored Visions [7] but replaces its black-box LLM prompt rewriting with a hierarchical, interpretable retrieval architecture (Boltzmann-weighted post selection, entropy-guided quota allocation, category-level element ranking). The evaluation side introduces a Turing-test-style indistinguishability criterion that goes beyond the preference-scoring approaches of ViPer, PPD, and ImageReward, positioning personalization as a holistic perceptual judgment rather than a pairwise preference optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VPTT framework provides the first scalable, privacy-safe benchmark (10k synthetic personas) and a validated text-only proxy metric (VPTT score, ρ≈0.68 with humans) for evaluating contextual personalization at scale, filling a gap the authors identify as increasingly critical as foundation models approach photorealism. Key limitations include the entirely synthetic persona construction (no real user data), the proof-of-concept-only feedback loop, and the restriction to image generation/editing. Open questions include whether the deferred-rendering representation transfers to video or 3D, whether the VPTT score remains reliable as generative models improve, and how the framework adapts when real opt-in user data replaces synthetic personas.
