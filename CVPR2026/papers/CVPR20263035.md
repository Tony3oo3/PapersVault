## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary segmentation methods in remote sensing rely solely on clear-sky optical RGB imagery and degrade severely under cloud or haze contamination. Two fundamental obstacles prevent straightforward SAR integration: vision foundation models (CLIP, DINO) are trained on RGB and face a large domain gap with SAR backscattering patterns, and VLMs' image-level supervision limits their dense prediction capability for pixel-wise segmentation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a co-registered pair of optical RGB (H×W×3) and single-channel SAR (H×W×1) images at 0.5–3 m GSD, cropped to 256×256 or 1024×1024 pixels depending on the dataset, together with a text prompt template ('a photo of {CLASSLIST}') for each candidate category. At inference, the model accepts an extended category set that includes unseen novel classes not present during training, and outputs a pixel-wise semantic map Y ∈ {1,…,N_c}^{H×W} assigning each pixel to the most relevant textual label via cosine similarity in the CLIP embedding space.

## Method

MM-OVSeg employs a two-stage training pipeline. In the Cross-Modal Unification (CMU) stage, a SAR-specific DINO encoder is trained via InfoNCE contrastive loss against frozen RGB DINO features using 25,087 unlabeled co-registered RGB-SAR pairs, establishing a shared dense representation space without requiring a large-scale SAR corpus. In the full training stage, the Dual-Encoder Fusion (DEF) module extracts multi-scale features (from the 4th, 8th, and 12th ViT-B/16 transformer blocks) from both the frozen RGB DINO and CMU-aligned SAR DINO encoders, fuses them via element-wise addition after block-specific convolutional projection, and then aligns the resulting multimodal dense features with CLIP text embeddings. A key design choice is the residual fusion of dense visual-text similarity maps with global CLIP visual-text similarity maps (concatenated through a 7×7 convolution plus sigmoid, then added back to the global map), which preserves CLIP's pretrained semantic structure while incorporating fine-grained multimodal cues. An FPN-style decoder upsamples and concatenates the fused features across scales, and a linear classifier produces the final segmentation, trained with standard cross-entropy loss.

## Results

MM-OVSeg achieves the highest mean mIoU of 51.7% across six evaluation settings, outperforming the second-best method GSNet (45.6%) by 6.1 points. The largest gains appear under cloudy conditions: on DDHR-SK→DDHR-SK (setting ②), MM-OVSeg reaches 73.1% mIoU versus 57.0% for GSNet, and on the cross-domain DDHR-SK→DDHR-CH setting (⑥), it scores 42.6% versus 40.6% for FGAseg. Even on the clear-sky benchmark (PIE-clean→PIE-clean, setting ⑤), where SAR is less critical, MM-OVSeg still leads with 59.7% mIoU over GSNet's 57.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MM-OVSeg outperforms all five baselines on every one of the six evaluation settings. The closest competitor is GSNet (AAAI'25), which combines CLIP with a DINO encoder for RS-specific local cues; MM-OVSeg surpasses it by 6.1 points in mean mIoU (51.7 vs. 45.6), with the largest single-setting margin of 18.1 points on DDHR-SK→DDHR-SK (73.1 vs. 55.0). FGAseg (arXiv'25) is the strongest baseline on the cross-domain setting ⑥ (40.6 vs. 42.6 for MM-OVSeg), but trails by 9.2 points on mean. CAT-Seg (CVPR'24) and EBSeg (CVPR'24), both designed for natural images, degrade substantially under cloudy conditions (e.g., EBSeg drops to 25.6% on OEM-thin). SegEarth-OV (CVPR'25), a training-free method, performs worst overall (31.0% mean) and collapses to 17.6% on DDHR-SK, highlighting the limitations of relying solely on CLIP's inherent localization without domain-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two distinct research threads: (1) open-vocabulary segmentation (OVS) in remote sensing, where prior methods (SegEarth-OV, GSNet, CAT-Seg, EBSeg) operate exclusively on single-modality RGB imagery under clear-sky assumptions, and (2) optical-SAR fusion for land-cover mapping, where existing approaches (MCANet, CroFuseNet, multimodal fusion transformers) are confined to closed-set classification with fixed annotated classes. By combining CLIP's global visual-text alignment with DINO's dense local features in a Dual-Encoder Fusion module, and introducing a Cross-Modal Unification stage inspired by ImageBind to bridge the SAR-RGB domain gap via contrastive learning on unlabeled pairs, the paper is the first to unify multimodal fusion with open-vocabulary generalization in the RS setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practically critical gap: real-world RS observations are frequently degraded by clouds and haze, rendering existing OVS methods unreliable for time-sensitive applications like disaster response and long-term monitoring. The CMU strategy of aligning SAR features to a frozen RGB encoder using only unlabeled paired data (25K pairs) is a practical recipe that avoids the infeasible task of collecting a DINO-scale SAR corpus. However, most experiments rely on synthetic clouds (SatelliteCloudGenerator, GIMP), and the seen-vs-unseen class gap remains substantial (e.g., Road at 21.2% IoU vs. Forest at 87.3%), leaving open whether the framework generalizes to genuinely novel semantic categories or to additional modalities (e.g., multispectral, LiDAR) in operational settings.
