## Problem

Existing no-reference 3D point cloud quality assessment (NR-PCQA) methods treat 2D projections and 3D geometry as independent modalities fused naively, ignoring the coupled perceptual mechanism by which the human visual system processes 3D stimuli. They also lack viewpoint-dependent processing, selective attention to quality-salient regions, and principled multiview integration, making it difficult to model how distortions in 3D space manifest in 2D projections and vice versa.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 3D point cloud (with geometric and optional photometric attributes) as input and produces a scalar no-reference quality score aligned with human MOS. Internally, the point cloud is projected from 20 icosahedral viewpoints into 2D RGB images, and geometric-aware reprojection establishes 9 pixel-to-subcloud correspondences per view (each subcloud containing 8192 points via KNN), so the model consumes both the full 2D projection and a policy-selected 3D subcloud per viewpoint rather than the entire point cloud.

## Method

R3-PCQA establishes precise 2D-3D spatial correspondence via a geometric-aware reprojection pipeline: 20 icosahedral cameras project the point cloud, K-means on valid-depth pixels yields 9 candidate locations per view, and back-projected rays intersect the point cloud to seed KNN-based subcloud extraction. A Quality-Salient Subcloud Selector (QSS) formulates subcloud choice as a contextual bandit problem, where a self-attention policy over 11×11 intermediate feature-map patches selects the single most quality-relevant subcloud from the 9 candidates, trained via REINFORCE with a reward derived from global prediction accuracy and distributed by global-view attention weights for credit assignment. A local view encoder fuses 2D texture features (capturing fine-grained distortions) and 3D geometric features (capturing coarse distortions) via an MLP, and a global view attention module adaptively aggregates local view tokens across all 20 viewpoints through multi-head attention. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: a warm-up phase with random subcloud selection and supervised losses (global + local MSE), followed by joint training that activates the RL policy while fine-tuning the full network.

## Results

R3-PCQA achieves state-of-the-art NR-PCQA performance across all three benchmarks: on SJTU-PCQA it reaches SRCC 0.9401 / PLCC 0.9606 / RMSE 0.7230, on WPC it reaches SRCC 0.9017 / PLCC 0.8882 / RMSE 10.9584, and on WPC 2.0 it reaches SRCC 0.8693 / PLCC 0.8650 / RMSE 12.0970. These results surpass the strongest prior NR methods (GMS-3DQA and MM-PCQA) by 2.9–6.7 SRCC points across datasets. In cross-dataset evaluation, R3-PCQA also leads in 3 of 4 transfer scenarios, with the largest margin (PLCC 0.920 vs. 0.861) when transferring from WPC to WPC 2.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R3-PCQA outperforms all 14 compared methods (7 FR, 7 NR) on every dataset and every metric. Among NR baselines, the closest competitors are GMS-3DQA [48] and MM-PCQA [47]: on SJTU-PCQA, R3-PCQA leads GMS-3DQA by 2.93 SRCC points (0.9401 vs. 0.9108) and MM-PCQA by 2.98 points (0.9401 vs. 0.9103); on WPC the margins widen to 7.09 and 6.03 SRCC points respectively; on WPC 2.0 the gaps are 4.21 and 6.70 SRCC points. The older NR methods (IT-PCQA, PQA-Net, 3D-NSS, ResSCNN) trail by 10–35 SRCC points, reflecting their reliance on single-modality or naive fusion strategies. All FR baselines (MSE/HD-p2po, MSE/HD-p2pl, PCQM, GraphSIM, PointSSIM) are substantially outperformed, with the best FR method (GraphSIM) reaching only SRCC 0.8783 on SJTU versus R3-PCQA's 0.9401. In cross-dataset transfer, R3-PCQA also leads in 3 of 4 scenarios, most notably achieving PLCC 0.920 on WPC→WPC 2.0 versus 0.861 for MM-PCQA, confirming superior generalization across distortion types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prevailing multimodal NR-PCQA methods (MM-PCQA, MFT-PCQA) that fuse 2D and 3D features without enforcing geometric correspondence, instead introducing a ray-based reprojection pipeline that establishes precise pixel-to-subcloud mapping from icosahedral viewpoints. It repurposes the icosahedron multiview setup from 3D shape recognition [31] and PointNet++-style hierarchical encoding for 3D features, but carves new ground by formulating subcloud selection as a contextual bandit problem solved via REINFORCE — the first reinforcement learning framework applied to point cloud quality assessment. The curriculum learning strategy (warm-up with random subclouds, then joint RL training) addresses the instability of simultaneously training a policy and a quality encoder from scratch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly encoding HVS mechanisms (viewpoint-dependent perception, selective attention, multiview integration) into the architecture, R3-PCQA provides a principled alternative to ad-hoc fusion strategies and achieves SOTA on all three major benchmarks with strong cross-dataset generalization (0.920 PLCC on WPC→WPC2.0). However, the ablation reveals that the RL policy contributes a modest ~1.4 SRCC point over the warm-up baseline, suggesting the geometric reprojection and multimodal fusion are the primary drivers of performance. Open questions include whether the RL overhead is justified at scale, how the method generalizes to much larger or unstructured point clouds beyond the small benchmark datasets, and whether the icosahedral viewpoint assumption holds for non-compact or open scenes.
