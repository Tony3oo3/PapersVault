## Problem

Physics-based 4D synthesis currently requires slow per-scene optimization: reconstructing 3DGS from dense multi-view images, then either manually specifying or SDS-optimizing physical properties through a differentiable simulator. This pipeline is computationally prohibitive, lacks scalability, and the common practice of naively concatenating a prebuilt 3DGS with a separate physics module ignores physical cues embedded in appearance, yielding suboptimal dynamics.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single posed RGB image (or up to 4 views) at 512×512 resolution along with camera intrinsics/extrinsics; during single-image inference, MVAdapter synthesizes three auxiliary views (rear, left, right) to provide multi-view context. In one forward pass it outputs per-pixel 3DGS parameters (position, rotation, scale, opacity, SH coefficients) aggregated across views, plus a probabilistic distribution over three physical attributes—material class, Young's modulus, and Poisson's ratio—sampled at inference to drive an MPM simulation that produces the final 50-frame 4D animation in under one minute.

## Method

PhysGM is a 24-layer transformer that jointly regresses 3DGS geometry and physical properties from DINOv3 image tokens concatenated with Plücker-coordinate camera tokens, using a DPT head for per-pixel Gaussian parameters and a separate physics head (classification + Gaussian-distribution regression) fed by three learnable global tokens. Training is two-stage: first, supervised pre-training on the 50K+ PhysAssets dataset jointly optimizes both heads under MSE/alpha/LPIPS rendering losses and physics regression losses, establishing a physical prior; second, DPO fine-tuning generates preference pairs by sampling K parameter sets, running MPM simulations, rendering videos, and ranking them against ground-truth videos via SAM-2 segmentation and CoTracker-3 point-tracking distance, then optimizing the DPO objective to shift the physics distribution toward perceptually superior simulations without requiring a differentiable simulator. The MPM simulation is tightly coupled to the 3DGS representation: each material point maps one-to-one to a Gaussian, and the deformation gradient F_p is polar-decomposed to set the Gaussian's rotation and anisotropic scale, ensuring rendered geometry mirrors simulated deformation.

## Results

On the PhysAssets test set, PhysGM (with DPO) achieves an average CLIPsim of 0.2748 and a User Preference Rate (UPR) of 42.8% across five material types (metal, jelly, plasticine, snow, sand), substantially outperforming OmniPhysGS (CLIPsim 0.2091, UPR 10%) and DreamPhysics (CLIPsim 0.2291, UPR 17.2%). Inference completes in under one minute per scene, compared to >12 hours for OmniPhysGS and >0.5 hours for DreamPhysics. On the GSO multi-view synthesis benchmark, PhysGM achieves PSNR 28.95, SSIM 0.953, and LPIPS 0.039 at 512 resolution, matching or exceeding GS-LRM on SSIM and LPIPS while using only 10% of its training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysGM decisively outperforms both SDS-based baselines on all metrics. Against OmniPhysGS, PhysGM improves average CLIPsim by 0.066 (0.2748 vs. 0.2091) and UPR by 32.8 percentage points (42.8% vs. 10%), while reducing inference time from >12 hours to under 1 minute. Against DreamPhysics, the gains are CLIPsim +0.046 and UPR +25.6 points, with inference time dropping from >0.5 hours to <1 minute. The DPO fine-tuning stage contributes an additional CLIPsim gain of 0.0055 and UPR gain of 12.8 points over the supervised-only variant, confirming its role in aligning the model with perceptual quality. On the secondary GSO multi-view synthesis task, PhysGM slightly trails GS-LRM in PSNR (28.95 vs. 30.52) but surpasses it in SSIM (0.953 vs. 0.952) and LPIPS (0.039 vs. 0.050) at 512 resolution, while using only 10% of GS-LRM's training data; it also substantially outperforms LGM at 256 resolution (PSNR 25.47 vs. 21.44).

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysGM sits at the intersection of three research threads: feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction (LGM, GS-LRM), physics-coupled Gaussian simulation (PhysGaussian, which first linked 3DGS to MPM), and preference-based optimization (DPO from NLP). It departs from the dominant per-scene SDS paradigm (OmniPhysGS, DreamPhysics) by replacing iterative gradient-based optimization with a single transformer forward pass, and from static feed-forward 3D models by jointly predicting physical properties alongside geometry. The two-stage training—supervised pre-training to establish a physical prior, then DPO to align with non-differentiable simulation outputs—is a novel combination that avoids both the cost of SDS and the instability of backpropagating through a physics engine.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sub-minute inference time (vs. hours for SDS-based methods) makes physics-based 4D content generation practically viable for interactive applications like embodied AI and autonomous driving, and the 50K+ PhysAssets dataset fills a critical gap in training data for this task. However, the sim-to-real gap remains a fundamental obstacle: simplified constitutive models and synthetic training data limit real-world generalization, and MPM's computational cost still precludes real-time deployment for complex phenomena like fluids or fracture. Open questions include whether the DPO refinement strategy generalizes beyond the five material classes tested, and whether the feed-forward formulation can scale to multi-object or scene-level physics.
