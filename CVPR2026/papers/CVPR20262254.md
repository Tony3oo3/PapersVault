## Problem

While language modalities have been incorporated to guide single-view X-ray prohibited item detection, the dual-view imagery that human inspectors routinely rely on to resolve occlusions and structural ambiguities has been largely overlooked. No existing resource simultaneously bridges visual, geometric (cross-view), and linguistic (cross-modal) modalities, leaving Vision-Language Models unable to exploit the inherent dual-view consistency of X-ray scans. The paper asks whether a second-view image can function as a 'language-like modality' whose geometric correspondences, jointly learned with textual semantics, provide additional constraints for detection.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a pair of X-ray images (top-view and side-view) along with a free-form text query. The vision encoder processes both views through a shared-weight ViT-L/14 backbone with 3D convolutional patch embedding and rotary positional encoding, producing dense token sequences. Output is structured reasoning text following a Chain-of-Thought template with explicit <top>, <side>, and <conclusion> segments, yielding prohibited item identification, spatial relationship descriptions, occlusion inferences, and cross-view consistency judgments across eight diagnostic sub-tasks (counting, recognition, spatial relation/distance, occluded area, contact-occlusion, placement, and spatial attributes).

## Method

GSR (Geometric-Semantic Reasoner) is built on the Qwen3-VL-MoE-8B foundation, with the key architectural innovation being hierarchical reasoning tokens (<top>, <side>, <conclusion>) that explicitly tag visual evidence by viewpoint and signal aggregation, enabling the model to maintain spatial awareness and disambiguate occluded structures across orthogonal views. A two-layer MLP with GeLU activation aligns the vision encoder's geometric feature space into the LLM's linguistic embedding space, after which the MoE text decoder jointly processes projected visual tokens and the textual query to generate structured reasoning. Training uses the GSXray dataset (44,019 dual-view QA samples with structured CoT sequences) via full fine-tuning of both the visual encoder and alignment module (bfloat16, AdamW lr=1e-6, cosine decay, batch 256, 2 epochs on 8×H200 GPUs). The core insight is that the side-view image, when properly aligned through structured CoT supervision, acts as a geometric 'language' that constrains and complements the top-view, converting an underutilized modality into a structured signal that reinforces cross-view reasoning.

## Results

On DualXrayBench, GSR-8B achieves 65.4% overall accuracy, 70.6% F1, and 52.3% mIoU, outperforming all 22+ baselines by a wide margin. The closest competitor, Qwen3-VL-235B, reaches only 58.8% Acc and 26.0% mIoU, while proprietary models like GPT-4o (47.0% Acc) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (58.6% Acc) trail by 7–18 points. GSR-8B leads on every individual sub-task, with particularly large gaps in Spatial Distance (61.0 vs. 61.5 for Gemini-2.5-Pro, but 61.0 vs. 47.0 for Qwen3-VL-8B) and Occluded Area Recognition (64.5 vs. 52.9 for Gemini-2.5-Pro). On the STING-BEE VQA benchmark, GSR-8B scores 55.3 overall (vs. 52.8 for STING-BEE), and on PID cross-category detection it reaches 26.3 AP50, surpassing OVXD (21.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GSR-8B (65.4% Acc, 52.3% mIoU) decisively outperforms every baseline on DualXrayBench. Against the strongest proprietary model, Gemini-2.5-Pro (58.6% Acc, 28.7% mIoU), GSR-8B gains +6.8 Acc and +23.6 mIoU; against GPT-4o (47.0% Acc), the gap widens to +18.4 Acc. Among open-source VLMs, the largest model Qwen3-VL-235B (58.8% Acc, 26.0% mIoU) is surpassed by +6.6 Acc and +26.3 mIoU, while the same-size Qwen3-VL-8B (53.5% Acc) improves by +11.9 Acc after GSR fine-tuning. The domain-specific single-view X-ray model STING-BEE performs worst (23.8% Acc), confirming that single-view architectures cannot handle dual-view reasoning. On the STING-BEE VQA benchmark, GSR-8B (55.3 overall) edges out STING-BEE (52.8) with the largest gains in Instance Location (+3.3) and Complex Reasoning (+3.8). On PID cross-category detection, GSR-8B (26.3 AP50) surpasses OVXD (21.0) and all vision-only detectors, though BARON reports a higher AP50 (37.8) while GSR-8B leads on every individual category (e.g., Wrench 50.5 vs. 18.1, Scissors 24.2 vs. 1.5).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: single-view X-ray prohibited item detection (SIXray, PIDray, HiXray), dual-view X-ray detection (DvXray, Dualray, LDXray), and vision-language models for security inspection (STCray, OVXD, STING-BEE). It builds directly on Qwen3-VL-MoE-8B as its backbone and on the LDXray dual-view dataset as its data foundation. The key conceptual departure is reframing the second-view image as a 'language-like modality' whose geometric correspondences are jointly learned with textual semantics via structured Chain-of-Thought tokens (<top>, <side>, <conclusion>), rather than treating the auxiliary view as mere additional imagery to be feature-fused as in prior dual-view detectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DualXrayBench is the first benchmark to systematically evaluate cross-view reasoning (spatial relations, occlusion inference, depth-axis distance) in X-ray inspection, filling a gap where prior dual-view datasets were vision-only and prior VLM benchmarks were single-view. The finding that untrained VLMs are actually *harmful* when given a second view (Table 6) before fine-tuning, but benefit substantially after GSXray training, suggests that cross-view geometric alignment requires explicit supervision rather than emergent capability. Open questions include whether the 'language-like' framing generalizes beyond 12 categories and whether the structured CoT tokens transfer to other multi-view domains (e.g., medical CT, industrial inspection), and whether the 8B model's advantage over 235B baselines reflects genuine architectural insight or domain-specific overfitting to the narrow category set.
