## Problem

Existing channel-adaptive vision models for immunofluorescence microscopy can train across multiple channel configurations but cannot evaluate on datasets with unseen channel arrangements without retraining, limiting cross-dataset generalization. The paper identifies a biologically grounded 'context-concept' separation of IF channels—where context channels provide stable structural reference and concept channels carry experiment-specific semantic variation—and asks whether this principle can be exploited architecturally and in pre-training to enable OOD evaluation on novel channel configurations without any retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262044/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-channel IF image and partitions its channels into a context group (e.g., Nucleus, Microtubules, ER in HPA) and a concept group (e.g., the protein-of-interest channel), with group assignments being dataset-specific and manually specified at inference for unseen channels. The encoder produces a single cell-level embedding of dimension D (typically 2d, where d is the per-branch width) that can be used for protein localization, compound retrieval, or MoA prediction. A key design property is that computational complexity scales linearly with channel count rather than quadratically, since individual channels within each group are processed independently through shared group-specific encoders before being combined.

## Method

C3R combines two components built around the context-concept principle. The Context-Concept Encoder (CCE) uses separate group-specific convolutional stems and lightweight ViT branches (f_c1, f_c2) to encode context and concept channels independently up to a certain depth, then merges them via a combiner function g and passes the result through joint encoder layers f_s to capture inter-group dependencies; at inference, unobserved channels are simply assigned to a group and processed without retraining. The Masked Context Distillation (MCD) strategy modifies the iBOT student-teacher pipeline by randomly sampling a subset of context channels for the student while the teacher retains the full context set, computing a KL-divergence loss between the masked-context student and full-context teacher representations; this encourages concept channels to contribute meaningfully to the global representation even under limited structural reference. The full training objective combines MCD with iBOT masked image modelling and the SubCell weakly-supervised antibody loss, all trained on HPA (~800k images) with ViT-S or ViT-B backbones.

## Results

On HPA-Loc (ID, ViT-B), C3R achieves mAP-31 = 0.548 and mAP-19 = 0.737, outperforming all baselines including SubCell (0.519/0.695) and DINO4Cells (0.508/0.683). On JUMP-Ret (OOD by channels, ViT-B), C3R reaches mAP = 0.363 and kNN = 0.530 without any retraining, surpassing SubCell (0.350/0.514) and DINO4Cells (0.339/0.509), and nearly matching Base-CP (0.355/0.513) which was specifically retrained to match JUMP-CP's channel configuration. On the CHAMMI-FE frozen-encoder benchmark (ViT-S), C3R achieves a Performance Score of 0.543, substantially outperforming ChannelViT (0.472), DiChaViT (0.459), and Base-SC (0.474).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C3R outperforms all four external baselines across both ID and OOD evaluations. Against ChannelViT [7] and DiChaViT [8] (channel-token architectures), C3R gains +0.098 mAP-31 and +0.120 mAP-19 on HPA-Loc (ViT-S), and +0.009 mAP / +0.015 kNN on JUMP-Ret, while also achieving a 0.071 higher CHAMMI-FE CPS (0.543 vs 0.472 for ChannelViT). Against DINO4Cells [3] and SubCell [4] (stronger SSL baselines evaluated with ViT-B), C3R leads by +0.029/+0.040 mAP-31/mAP-19 on HPA-Loc and +0.013/+0.016 mAP/kNN on JUMP-Ret. Notably, C3R achieves these gains without any retraining on the target channel configuration, whereas SubCell and DINO4Cells require retraining (marked with *) to handle JUMP-CP's 5-channel setup. The only method that slightly exceeds C3R on JUMP-Ret is Base-CP (0.355 vs 0.354 mAP, ViT-S), but Base-CP was specifically pre-trained on a 3-channel HPA subset matched to JUMP-CP, making it a target-specific baseline rather than a general-purpose one.

## Relation to Prior Work

C3R builds directly on the iBOT pre-training pipeline (masked patch prediction plus student-teacher distillation) and the SubCell weakly supervised antibody loss, replacing the standard KL-divergence distillation with a context-channel-masking variant (MCD). Architecturally, it departs from channel-tokenization methods like ChannelViT and DiChaViT, which assign per-channel parameters and are locked to the channel set seen during training, by instead grouping channels into biologically motivated context and concept branches with shared intra-group encoders. The key conceptual departure is the 'context-concept principle'—a dataset-dependent but structurally consistent partition of IF channels into stable structural references and task-specific readouts—which prior channel-adaptive methods do not exploit and which enables OOD evaluation on unseen channel configurations without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

C3R provides a practical pathway for deploying IF-image foundation models across laboratories and staining protocols without the costly retraining that current methods (SubCell, DINO4Cells) require for each new channel configuration, which is a significant barrier for clinical and drug-discovery pipelines. The CHAMMI-FE frozen-encoder benchmark is a useful addition that isolates representation quality from encoder fine-tuning. A key limitation is that the context-concept partition must be identifiable in the target dataset, restricting applicability to IF datasets designed around a clear structural-reference-plus-readout protocol. An open question the authors flag is whether MCD's benefits extend to purely zero-shot tasks without any learnable adaptation, since the distillation gains did not transfer to JUMP-Ret retrieval.
