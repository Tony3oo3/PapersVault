## Problem

Existing PTM-based class-incremental learning methods optimize the feature extractor but largely overlook the classification head, which remains a closed-set softmax or cosine-similarity layer incapable of rejecting out-of-distribution inputs. This forces multi-task routing to rely on a fragile Task-ID predictor that itself suffers from forgetting. The paper asks: if each task head could natively distinguish IND from OOD samples, could CIL emerge without any explicit task-routing module?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261100/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image, encodes it through a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone augmented with LoRA adapters, and produces a global logit vector formed by concatenating per-task cosine-similarity scores against fixed ETF anchors. The highest-scoring task head determines the predicted class, while the feature-to-anchor distance simultaneously serves as an OOD confidence score—low similarity indicates the input belongs to no known task. Inference supports two modes: a single-pass coarse mode using a shared EMA adapter, and a refined mode that re-encodes only the top-k candidate tasks for higher accuracy.

## Method

GOD replaces the learnable classifier with a fixed set of Equiangular Tight Frame (ETF) vectors as class anchors, so that the cosine similarity between a normalized feature and its anchor is both the classification logit and the OOD uncertainty signal. Training enforces two geometric properties via a composite loss: a temperature-scaled cross-entropy against the ETF anchors (inter-class separation) and an ArcFace angular-margin loss (intra-class compactness), while a shared frozen random projection lifts features into a higher-dimensional space for improved separability. For parameter efficiency, the LoRA adapters are decomposed into a shared shallow stack (trained once on task 1, then frozen) and lightweight task-specific deep stacks, reducing per-task parameter overhead. At inference, a universal EMA adapter provides a single-pass coarse prediction, and a refined mode sparsely activates only the top-k task-specific LoRA blocks for re-encoding, yielding a tunable speed-accuracy trade-off.

## Results

GOD(Refined) achieves state-of-the-art average incremental accuracy (Ā) across all four benchmarks: 92.87% on CIFAR-100 (T=10), 73.39% on ImageNet-A (T=5), 86.90% on ImageNet-R (T=5), and 85.23% on Stanford Cars (T=5). The largest gains appear on fine-grained datasets, where GOD(Refined) outperforms the runner-up SD-LoRA by +3.22% on ImageNet-R and +5.64% on Stanford Cars (both T=5). In the efficient Coarse mode (single forward pass), GOD already reaches 85.31% on ImageNet-R (T=5), surpassing most SOTA methods without any sparse re-encoding.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOD(Refined) outperforms all 12 baselines on every dataset and task setting. The margins are most pronounced on fine-grained benchmarks: on Stanford Cars (T=5), GOD(Refined) reaches 85.23% Ā, beating SD-LoRA (79.59%) by +5.64% and NC-CIPM (72.98%) by +12.25%; on ImageNet-R (T=5), it achieves 86.90% vs. SD-LoRA's 83.68% (+3.22%). On the relatively easier CIFAR-100, the gap narrows to ~0.5–1% over LORA-DRS and EASE, which the authors attribute to the PTM's strong inherent generalization. Notably, even the single-pass GOD(Coarse) variant already surpasses LORA-DRS and SD-LoRA on ImageNet-R (85.31% vs. 82.56% and 83.68% at T=5), demonstrating that the ETF-based OOD-aware head provides substantial gains without any sparse re-encoding. The performance gap between GOD and competitors widens as the number of tasks increases (Fig. 4), indicating superior long-sequence stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant PTM-based CIL paradigm (prompt-based, representation-based, model-mixture-based) that optimizes the feature extractor while treating the classifier as a secondary concern, instead arguing that the classifier's geometry is the critical design axis. It operationalizes Neural Collapse theory (Papyan et al., 2020) by hard-coding the ETF structure as fixed class anchors rather than hoping it emerges during training, and borrows ArcFace's angular margin mechanism to enforce intra-class compactness around those anchors. The parameter-efficient hybrid architecture extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) with a depth-wise shared/task-specific decomposition motivated by an empirical observation about where task-specific variation concentrates in transformer blocks, and the EMA-based two-mode inference strategy draws on momentum averaging to avoid O(T) forward passes at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean conceptual reframing—that a classifier with reliable OOD rejection naturally provides task routing without a fragile Task-ID predictor—offers a principled alternative to both single-expanding-classifier and multi-head-with-router designs that have dominated PTM-based CIL. The parameter efficiency (2.64M trainable vs. ~4.73M for competitors) and the coarse/refined inference trade-off make the approach practically attractive for long task sequences. Open questions include whether the fixed ETF geometry remains optimal when class distributions within a task are highly imbalanced or when the number of classes per task varies, and whether the depth-wise LoRA sharing heuristic generalizes beyond ViT-B/16 to other backbone architectures or modalities.
