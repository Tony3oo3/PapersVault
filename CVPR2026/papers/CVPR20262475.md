## Problem

Large video generation models (e.g., HunyuanVideo, WanX) require 13B+ parameters and 50-step iterative sampling, making inference prohibitively slow (~20 min per 5-second video on an H100). Prior acceleration work has addressed either step distillation (LCM, DMD2, MagicDistillation) or model pruning (ICMD, F3-Pruning) in isolation, leaving the synergistic joint optimization of both dimensions unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262475/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt and a reference image (and optionally audio for the HunyuanVideo-ATI2V variant) to generate a 5-second video. Internally, a VAE compresses the video from T×H×W to ⌊T/4⌋×⌊H/8⌋×⌊W/8⌋ latent space, and the compressed generator operates with only 4 sampling steps and 70% of the original DiT blocks retained, yielding a theoretical ~35.7× speedup over the 50-step, full-parameter baseline with CFG.

## Method

FastLightGen is a three-stage co-distillation pipeline. Stage I identifies unimportant DiT blocks by computing the ELBO drop (via Tweedie's formula) when each block is skipped, revealing a U-shaped importance pattern where initial and final layers are most critical. Stage II trains a robust model via dynamic probabilistic pruning, stochastically skipping identified blocks with Bernoulli(p=0.5) and supervising the pruned sub-model purely through distillation from the unpruned counterpart (α=1, no ground-truth loss). Stage III performs fine-grained distribution matching (DMD2-style KL divergence) where the 'real DiT' is a novel hybrid of the pruned and unpruned models, guided by a 'well-guided teacher guidance' mechanism that independently controls text-conditional strength (inter-CFG β₁) and unpruned-model influence (intra-CFG β₂) via uniform sampling, avoiding both under-supervision and over-complex supervision signals.

## Results

On VBench-I2V (WanX-TI2V), FastLightGen achieves an average score of 0.794 with 4-step sampling and 70% parameter retention, completing inference in 28.3 s—approximately 31× faster than the 50-step Euler baseline (885.3 s, avg 0.790). It surpasses its own teacher model (WanX-TI2V) on the overall average while matching or exceeding it on dynamic degree (0.500 vs 0.461), aesthetic quality (0.656 vs 0.653), and imaging quality (0.717 vs 0.711). Against the strongest step-distillation competitor, MagicDistillation, FastLightGen trails by only 0.004 in average score (0.794 vs 0.798) while reducing inference time by 7.1 s (28.3 s vs 35.4 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FastLightGen outperforms all step-distillation baselines except MagicDistillation: it beats DMD2 by 0.078 in average VBench score (0.794 vs 0.716) and LCM by 0.110 (0.794 vs 0.684), while also being faster than both (28.3 s vs 35.4 s). Against MagicDistillation, FastLightGen trails by a negligible 0.004 in average score but is 7.1 s faster per video. Among model-pruning baselines, FastLightGen dominates: it exceeds ICMD by 0.025 in average (0.794 vs 0.769) and F3-Pruning by 0.043 (0.794 vs 0.751), while reducing inference time from 396.2 s to 28.3 s (≈14× faster). Notably, FastLightGen also surpasses its own 50-step teacher (WanX-TI2V, avg 0.790) on the overall average while being ~31× faster, and it leads all open-source I2V models in Table 1 (CogVideoX-I2V-SAT 0.759, I2Vgen-XL 0.753, SEINE 0.751, VideoCrafter-I2V 0.736, SVD-XT-1.0 0.789, Step-Video-TI2V 0.788). The only metric where a competitor edges ahead is dynamic degree, where MagicDistillation reaches 0.561 vs FastLightGen's 0.500, though the paper attributes MagicDistillation's higher value to less physically plausible motion upon visual inspection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work on accelerating video diffusion models: step distillation (DMD2, LCM, MagicDistillation) and structured model pruning (F3-Pruning, ICMD). Prior methods address either reducing NFEs or reducing parameter count in isolation; FastLightGen is the first to jointly optimize both dimensions through a three-stage pipeline. The final-stage distribution matching directly extends DMD2's KL-divergence score-matching framework, while the novel 'well-guided teacher guidance' mechanism (Eq. 9) introduces a dual-CFG formulation that blends pruned and unpruned model outputs to calibrate teacher strength for the student.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated 35× speedup while matching or exceeding teacher quality on VBench-I2V makes large-scale video generation practically deployable on consumer hardware, addressing a critical bottleneck for real-world applications. The U-shaped layer-importance pattern and the finding that pure distillation (α=1, no ground-truth supervision) outperforms mixed supervision provide actionable design principles for future compression pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to text-to-video and video-to-video tasks, to non-DiT architectures, and whether the dynamic Bernoulli pruning strategy scales to more aggressive compression ratios beyond 70% retention.
