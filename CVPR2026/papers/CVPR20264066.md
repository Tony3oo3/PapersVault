## Problem

Camera-based perception degrades severely at night, and existing approaches either passively rely on ambient lighting or require costly additional sensors (LiDAR, radar, thermal). Domain adaptation and generalization methods mitigate distribution shift but still fail in severely under-exposed scenes. No prior work has dynamically controlled high-definition headlights in a closed loop to actively maximize downstream perception performance without retraining the perception models.

## Input / Output

The LiDAS network takes three inputs: the current RGB camera frame, the previous frame's illumination map M_{t-1} (to distinguish self-induced light from ambient), and a CoordConv-inspired 2-channel spatial coordinate map providing coarse geometric context. It outputs a residual illumination update ΔM_t ∈ [-1,1]^{H×W}, which is added to M_{t-1}, clamped to [0,1], and normalized to a target energy budget η. At deployment, the resulting light field is warped to the 320×80 px HD headlight pixel grid via a one-time camera-headlight calibration, and the full pipeline runs in 6.8 ms on an RTX 4090.

## Method

LiDAS learns an illumination policy via an encoder-decoder (54M params, four downsampling + two upsampling stages with skip connections) that predicts residual light-field corrections rather than absolute intensities. Training is end-to-end through a differentiable relighting operator that linearly interpolates between a fully-illuminated render and a no-headlight render, with gradients flowing from frozen downstream task losses (three COCO-pretrained YOLO detectors and a Cityscapes-pretrained Mask2Former segmenter) back through the relit image to the illumination map. To mirror closed-loop deployment, the model is unrolled for N=40 refinement steps on the same training image, with gradients backpropagated only at K=5 sampled steps to encourage stability under its own illumination. An energy-budget schedule linearly ramps the target power from 10% to the final value over training epochs, forcing early prioritization of critical regions followed by later refinement. Trained entirely on synthetic nighttime scenes from the Applied Intuition simulator, the policy transfers zero-shot to a real car-mounted HD headlight prototype in closed-loop driving.

## Results

In the synthetic evaluation (Table 1), LiDAS at equal power to Low Beam (LiDAS[1]) achieves 47.3 mAP50 for detection and 72.8 mIoU for segmentation, improving over Low Beam[1] by +10.4% mAP50 and +6.8% mIoU. Notably, LiDAS[0.6] (40% power reduction) outperforms all baselines including High Beam[1.8], which uses 1.8× more energy. In the real-world closed-loop car-mounted evaluation (Table 4), LiDAS[1] delivers +18.7% mAP50 and +5.0% mIoU over Low Beam[1] on unlit roads, with LiDAS[0.6] still surpassing High Beam[1.8] in recall.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiDAS consistently outperforms all passive illumination baselines. At equal power (×1), LiDAS beats Low Beam by +10.4% mAP50 and +6.8% mIoU, and surpasses High Beam (×1.8 power) by +4.3% mAP50 while using 44% less energy. The energy-saving LiDAS[0.6] configuration outperforms every baseline at every power level, including Uniform[4] which consumes 4× the power of Low Beam. The Static baseline (averaged LiDAS predictions, non-adaptive) trails dynamic LiDAS[1] by 4.7% mAP50 and 2.9% mIoU, confirming that scene-adaptive allocation is critical. In the real-world closed-loop test, LiDAS[1] achieves 34.5 mAP50 vs. 15.8 for Low Beam and 16.0 for High Beam on unlit roads, a +18.7% absolute gain. Under rain (unseen condition), LiDAS[1.8] leads High Beam[1.8] by +7.0% mAP50, demonstrating robustness to novel reflective phenomena.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiDAS departs from prior headlight-for-perception work, which was either heuristic (reactive adaptive beams based on hand-crafted saliency [8, 34]), purely theoretical (minimum illumination analysis in a single scene [40]), or fixed-pattern (structured LED for depth [9]). It also positions itself as complementary to domain adaptation/generalization methods [14, 22, 51], which improve model robustness but cannot overcome sensor limits in severely under-exposed scenes. The key conceptual departure is framing illumination as a learnable, task-driven control variable optimized end-to-end through a differentiable relighting surrogate, closing the perceive-illuminate-perceive loop rather than applying a static or rule-based light pattern.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiDAS offers a practical, bolt-on solution to nighttime perception that requires no additional sensors, no retraining of downstream models, and leverages HD headlights already present on modern vehicles, making it deployable at scale in low-to-mid-cost cars. The 40% energy reduction while matching or exceeding high-beam performance addresses a real efficiency concern for autonomous systems. Open questions include robustness in dense multi-vehicle traffic with competing headlights, generalization to more complex urban scenes beyond the controlled test track, and whether the simplified linear-interpolation relighting operator remains adequate for materials with strong specular or subsurface scattering. The paper also notes that depth-oriented illumination conflicts with detection/segmentation-oriented lighting, leaving the multi-task trade-off unresolved.
