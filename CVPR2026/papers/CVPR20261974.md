## Problem

Pretrained monocular geometry foundation models (e.g., MoGe) achieve strong single-image accuracy but exhibit severe temporal inconsistency on continuous video streams, dominated by scale-shift drift that causes non-rigid warping and positional jitter in 3D reconstructions. The paper identifies the root cause as fluctuations in latent feature statistics (mean and variance) rather than geometric degradation, and shows that existing fixes—full-network finetuning with temporal attention or recurrent memory—are computationally expensive and degrade per-frame accuracy and zero-shot generalization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes streaming RGB frames one at a time in a strictly causal, online fashion (no future frames or fixed-length clips required at inference), maintaining a compact recurrent hidden state across arbitrary sequence lengths. Each frame is encoded by a shared frozen ViT-based MGE encoder into latent features, which are then modulated by DyFN and decoded into a per-frame 3D point map. These point maps can be fused with known or estimated camera poses to produce a coherent global 3D reconstruction, and the method supports real-time inference at 2K resolution.

## Method

DyFN is a lightweight ConvGRU-based module inserted between a frozen ViT encoder and decoder that dynamically modulates latent feature statistics over time. At each frame, incoming features are normalized (Eq. 1), fed into a ConvGRU that maintains a hidden state aggregating all prior observations, and two 1×1 convolutional heads predict spatially-varying mean and standard deviation parameters to re-modulate the normalized features (Eq. 6), replacing unstable per-frame statistics with temporally-aware ones. Only DyFN is trained (~2% of total parameters) while the backbone remains frozen, preserving single-frame accuracy. Training uses a composite loss: the base MoGe loss plus a global alignment loss that enforces a single scale-and-shift across the entire sequence, and a multi-scale inter-frame temporal loss (window sizes k∈{1,2,4}) that matches predicted inter-frame point deltas to ground-truth deltas in a common metric space.

## Results

On the video depth estimation protocol (single global scale-shift alignment per sequence), DyFN achieves state-of-the-art results across all four benchmarks: AbsRel of 0.180 / 0.073 / 0.062 / 0.044 and δ<1.25 of 73.0 / 96.6 / 97.3 / 98.4 on Sintel, ScanNet, KITTI, and Bonn respectively. The most striking gain is on ScanNet where δ<1.25 improves from 84.7 (MoGe v1 backbone) to 96.6, an 11.9-point increase, and on KITTI where AbsRel drops from 0.183 (MoGe v2) to 0.062. In the single-frame evaluation (Table 2), DyFN perfectly matches MoGe v1's per-frame accuracy on all datasets, confirming that freezing the backbone preserves geometric fidelity while the 2%-parameter DyFN module adds temporal stability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DyFN outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and metric in the video evaluation protocol. Against the closest streaming competitor FlashDepth, DyFN gains 8.8 points on Sintel δ<1.25 (73.0 vs 64.2) and 6.3 points on ScanNet (96.6 vs 90.3), while also halving the AbsRel gap on KITTI (0.062 vs 0.103). Against offline video methods, DyFN surpasses VDA by 9.7 points on Sintel δ<1.25 (73.0 vs 63.3) and DepthCrafter by 11.0 points on ScanNet (96.6 vs 85.6), despite using a far simpler causal recurrent architecture rather than bidirectional attention. Against multi-frame geometry methods, DyFN dominates on dynamic scenes (Sintel AbsRel 0.180 vs VGGT's 0.287) and still wins on static Bonn (0.044 vs 0.055), whereas VGGT's strong ScanNet result (0.031) is attributed to in-domain training. Metric depth models like MoGe v2 show severe instability under sequence-level alignment (KITTI AbsRel 0.183), confirming that metric training alone does not solve temporal drift. Crucially, in the single-frame evaluation DyFN exactly matches its MoGe v1 backbone (e.g., 0.124 AbsRel / 83.7 δ<1.25 on Sintel), whereas FlashDepth degrades from 95.3 to 92.6 on KITTI δ<1.25 after fine-tuning, demonstrating that DyFN's parameter-efficient design avoids the accuracy–consistency tradeoff.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MoGe, a monocular geometry estimation foundation model, as a frozen encoder-decoder backbone, and departs from the dominant paradigm of prior streaming depth methods (FlashDepth, CUT3R, TTT3R) that inject temporal attention or recurrent memory into the full network requiring extensive finetuning. The key conceptual departure is an empirical diagnosis that temporal inconsistency in pretrained MGE models is not primarily a geometric error but a scale-shift drift driven by fluctuations in latent feature mean and variance. Rather than adding heavy temporal modules, DyFN addresses this root cause with a lightweight ConvGRU-based normalization head (2% of total parameters) that modulates only these statistics, preserving the backbone's single-frame accuracy exactly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-efficient strategy (freezing the backbone, training only a 2% stabilizer) offers a practical path for adapting any pretrained monocular geometry model to streaming video without the data and compute costs of full finetuning, and without the accuracy degradation that plagues prior approaches. The empirical finding that feature statistics are tightly coupled to predicted scale/shift but largely decoupled from relative geometric accuracy provides a useful diagnostic lens for the broader community. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other backbone architectures beyond MoGe's ViT-based encoder, how it behaves under domain shifts not covered by the ~1M training frames, and whether the ConvGRU hidden state suffices for sequences far beyond the 500-frame evaluation horizon.
