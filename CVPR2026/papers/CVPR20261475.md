## Problem

Existing visually grounded reasoning methods for LVLMs rely on a top-down, coarse-to-fine paradigm that requires one-shot localization of complete evidence regions from the full image, making them highly sensitive to noisy context (attention sink) and semantically similar distractors (attention drift). This leads to mislocalization and incorrect answers in fine-grained visual understanding tasks where targets occupy less than 0.05% of the image area.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261475/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution RGB image (average ~2246×1582 on V* Bench) and a text question as input. The image is dynamically partitioned into patches of 576×576 (single-object) or 768×768 (multi-object) for local cue exploration, while the full image is used for point-prompt segmentation. The output is a grounded multiple-choice answer, accompanied by intermediate evidence artifacts: segmentation masks, bounding boxes, and a hybrid multi-image prompt (fine-grained evidence crops plus a coarse-grained recalibrated view) that is fed back to the LVLM for final reasoning.

## Method

DeepScan is a training-free, three-stage framework that inverts the conventional top-down grounding paradigm into a bottom-up one. (1) Hierarchical Scanning performs patch-wise Local Cue Exploration using GradCAM attention maps thresholded by Otsu's method, extracting connected components as cues and selecting point-based proxies via a joint semantic-topological scoring (attention peak × distance-to-boundary); these proxies are then lifted to image coordinates for Multi-Scale Evidence Extraction via LangSAM point-prompt segmentation, followed by morphological closing and dilation to seal interior holes and extend context. (2) Refocusing runs a depth-2 collaborative search between the LVLM and a detection expert over four candidate views (original, zoom-in, zoom-out, zoom-in-after-zoom-out), selecting the smallest view that the LVLM confirms contains sufficient evidence, using a size-regularized reward. (3) Evidence-Enhanced Reasoning assembles a Hybrid Evidence Memory of fine-grained evidence crops and the best coarse-grained view into an ordered multi-image prompt, enabling the LVLM to resolve attributes from local detail and infer spatial relations from global context simultaneously.

## Results

On V* Bench, DeepScan (built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves 90.6% overall accuracy, a +16.3% improvement over the vanilla model (74.3%), with 93.0% on Attribute and 86.8% on Spatial subsets. On HR-Bench, it reaches 75.0% (4K) and 72.4% (8K) average accuracy, outperforming all training-free baselines by 2.6–6.3%. On TreeBench, DeepScan attains 42.5% overall mIoU (+5.5% over baseline), surpassing all RL-based and training-free grounded-reasoning methods while remaining competitive with much larger proprietary models (GPT-o3: 54.8%, Gemini-2.5-Pro: 54.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepScan outperforms all training-free grounded-reasoning baselines: it beats Dyfo by 6.3% on V* Bench (90.6 vs. 84.3), ZoomRefine by 8.4%, and TreeVGR by 4.7%. Against RL-based methods, DeepScan matches DeepEyes (90.6 vs. 90.0 on V* Bench) while exceeding PixelReasoner (80.6) and Thyme-VL (82.2) by 10+ points, all without any fine-tuning. On HR-Bench, DeepScan leads all training-free methods by 2.6–3.7% and is within 0.1% of DeepEyes on the 4K split. On TreeBench, DeepScan (42.5% mIoU) surpasses DeepEyes (37.5%), Pixel-Reasoner (39.0%), TreeVGR (41.0%), and Dyfo (39.3%), though it trails much larger proprietary models like GPT-o3 (54.8%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (54.1%). Notably, DeepScan's 90.6% on V* Bench exceeds the 72B Qwen2.5-VL (84.8%) by 5.8 points, demonstrating that the training-free grounding pipeline compensates for model scale on fine-grained perception tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepScan departs from the dominant top-down (coarse-to-fine) grounding paradigm used by both RL-based methods (DeepEyes, PixelReasoner, TreeVGR) and training-free methods (Dyfo, ZoomRefine), which all perform one-shot image-level localization before refinement. Instead, it introduces a bottom-up paradigm that first explores local cues in patches via a search expert (BLIP-ITM + GradCAM) and then recovers image-level evidence through point-prompt segmentation (LangSAM), explicitly motivated by human visual search behavior. The framework repurposes standard components—Otsu thresholding, morphological operations, and a pruned depth-2 search space—into a cohesive pipeline without requiring any LVLM fine-tuning, positioning itself as a plug-and-play complement to existing LVLM architectures rather than a modification of them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, architecture-agnostic design makes DeepScan immediately applicable to any LVLM without adaptation cost, which is practically valuable given the rapid turnover of LVLM releases. The paper's observation that RL-based grounding methods primarily bias LVLMs toward perception rather than fundamentally strengthening visual reasoning, and that excessive zoom-in degrades reasoning performance, offers useful guidance for the field. However, the ~24.5s per-sample latency and dependence on two external experts (BLIP-ITM, LangSAM) limit deployment in latency-sensitive settings, and the search-space design (depth-2, 4 states) may not generalize to scenarios with many spatially distributed evidence regions. An open question is whether the bottom-up paradigm can be internalized into the LVLM itself through training, eliminating the need for external experts.
