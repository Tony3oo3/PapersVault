## Problem

Existing video benchmarks predominantly assess passive, retrospective understanding through offline analysis of complete clips or isolated perception tasks, failing to capture the interactive, adaptive, and real-time nature of human-AI collaboration. There is no benchmark that evaluates whether MLLMs can function as embedded, proactive assistants who track task progress, diagnose errors, and provide actionable guidance from a first-person perspective in continuous daily-life scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260665/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each query presents the model with only the 30-second egocentric video segment relevant to the question (streaming-style, not the full clip) plus a text question; models without native video support receive 8 uniformly sampled frames from that window. Outputs are either a multiple-choice selection (scored by exact match) or a free-text response (scored 0–1 in 0.25 increments by GPT-5 as judge against a reference answer). The benchmark comprises 4,075 QA pairs over 591 clips from 100 daily-life scenarios totaling 44.19 hours, with each question grounded to a specific timestamp and accompanied by a reasoning chain.

## Method

LifeEval introduces a six-dimensional capability taxonomy (Static Environment Perception, Dynamic Task Reasoning, Contextual Knowledge Retrieval, Goal-oriented Planning, Safety and Feasibility Assessment, Multi-turn Interactive Collaboration) that shifts evaluation from passive perception to active task assistance. The annotation pipeline directly feeds raw video to Gemini 2.5 Pro (bypassing intermediate textual narrations) to generate dimension-specific MCQs with reasoning chains and plausible distractors, followed by two-stage quality filtering (automated self-review + human verification), a controlled difficulty-enhancement pass, and finally reformulation into open-ended questions. Evaluation uses a streaming protocol where models see only the relevant temporal window in a zero-shot setting, with MCQs scored by exact match and OEQs scored via LLM-as-a-Judge (GPT-5, graded 0–1 in 0.25 steps). The benchmark is validated by evaluating 26 state-of-the-art MLLMs (proprietary, open-source, and video-specialized), revealing that performance gaps stem from reasoning and interaction challenges rather than scene variation, and that moderate frame sampling (8 frames) near-saturates performance.

## Results

On LifeEval, Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the highest multiple-choice accuracy at 85.61% overall, while GPT-5 leads on open-ended questions with a 72.39% LLM-judged score. The best open-source MLLM (Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B) reaches 70.01% on MCQ and 48.61% on OEQ, trailing the top proprietary models by 13.4 and 23.8 points respectively. All 20 open-source models (both general MLLMs and video-specialized LMs) fall below 50% on open-ended questions, and performance is weakest on Dynamic Task Reasoning and Goal-oriented Planning across all model families.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-2.5-Pro outperforms the best open-source MLLM (LLaVA-OneVision-72B) by 13.42 points on MCQ (85.61 vs. 72.19) and the best video-specialized LM (LLaVA-Video-7B) by 27.19 points. On open-ended questions, GPT-5 leads at 72.39%, exceeding the best open-source model (Qwen3-VL-30B at 48.61%) by 23.78 points and the best video-specialized model (InternVideo2.5 at 28.91%) by 43.48 points. A critical finding is that video-specialized architectures (LLaVA-Video, VideoLLaMA3, LongVA, InternVideo2.5) do not meaningfully outperform general MLLMs of comparable parameter size, indicating that the bottleneck is model capacity rather than architectural specialization. Scaling within open-source families is inconsistent: InternVL3.5-38B scores lower than InternVL3.5-8B on MCQ (59.69 vs. 57.98 is actually higher, but the 14B drops to 57.38), and LLaVA-NeXT-110B underperforms its 72B counterpart (65.45 vs. 66.26 on MCQ), suggesting over-parameterization can introduce hallucinations in egocentric collaboration. All models show their strongest performance on Contextual Knowledge Retrieval (up to 91.54% for Gemini-2.5-Pro) and weakest on Dynamic Task Reasoning and Goal-oriented Planning, highlighting a persistent gap in procedural and adaptive reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

LifeEval carves new ground at the intersection of egocentric video understanding and interactive task assistance, departing from both general video QA benchmarks (MVBench, Video-MME) that evaluate offline, retrospective comprehension, and existing egocentric benchmarks (EgoSchema, EgoPlan-Bench, EgoLifeQA) that remain passive and goal-agnostic. The key conceptual shift is from 'what can the model perceive' to 'what can the model do to help in real time,' introducing a streaming evaluation protocol and a six-dimensional capability taxonomy (SEP, DTR, CKR, GP, SFA, MIC) that no prior benchmark jointly covers. The annotation pipeline repurposes Gemini 2.5 Pro as both generator and reviewer over Ego4D clips, a practical adaptation of LLM-assisted data curation to the video domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LifeEval provides the first structured, large-scale evaluation of MLLMs as embedded, real-time assistants, exposing that even top proprietary models (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro) drop substantially on open-ended, task-oriented questions and that open-source models fall below 50% on free-form responses—highlighting a critical gap between passive video understanding and actionable collaboration. The finding that parameter scaling is non-monotonic (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-110B underperforms 72B; InternVL3.5-38B shows no gain over 8B) and that 8 frames nearly saturate performance challenges the assumption that more parameters or denser temporal sampling will close the assistive-interaction gap. Open questions include whether architectural changes (e.g., explicit state-tracking modules, action-planning heads) rather than scale are needed, and whether the benchmark's reliance on Gemini 2.5 Pro for annotation introduces generator-specific biases that could inflate scores for models sharing similar training data.
