## Problem

Generative attacks offer fast one-step inference and strong transferability, but they fundamentally depend on abundant prior information (large labeled datasets or pre-trained surrogates) that is unavailable in the Practical No-box Scenario (PNS), where only a few unlabeled images are accessible. Existing PNS attacks rely on iterative optimization, suffering from low inference speed and limited transferability. No prior work has demonstrated an effective generative attack under the PNS, leaving open how much potential generative attacks can offer in this information-scarce setting.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes only 4,000 arbitrary unlabeled RGB images (sourced from ImageNet or MS COCO) to learn both the surrogate and the generator from scratch. At inference, a single clean image is passed through the generator in one forward pass, producing an adversarial example whose perturbation is bounded by an ℓ∞ norm of ε (default 16/255). The image is internally partitioned into K=4 non-overlapping regions, each resized back to full resolution for stable regional feature extraction, and the final output is clipped to the valid [0,1] pixel range.

## Method

PGA decomposes the pipeline into two stages. For surrogate learning, Curriculum-Guided Micro-Robust Optimization (CMRO) first trains a ResNet-18 via SimSiam-style self-supervised learning on global-global and global-local view pairs, then progressively introduces FGSM-style perturbations to one global view starting at epoch T=200 with τ ramping linearly to 0.01, preventing the representation collapse that plagues direct robust training under limited data. For generator training, Region-Aware Consistent Perturbation Learning (RCPL) jointly minimizes cosine similarity of surrogate intermediate features at the global level and at K=4 regional levels, while a cross-region consistency loss computed via pairwise Frobenius distances of Gram matrices enforces spatial coherence across regions. This regional decomposition guides the generator toward fine-grained, spatially coherent perturbations that avoid the fragmented, low-coverage failure mode typical of generative attacks under insufficient supervision, yielding transferable adversarial examples in a single inference step.

## Results

On ImageNet (ε=16, 4,000 unlabeled samples), PGA achieves an average ASR of 71.39% on CNNs (AVG_c) and 47.50% on ViTs/MLPs (AVG_v), outperforming the strongest generative baseline GAPF (56.41% / 32.79%) by ~15% and ~15% respectively. On MS COCO, the gains are 68.51% / 43.67% vs. GAPF's 54.13% / 31.60%. In cross-domain evaluation across seven datasets (CIFAR-10/100, SVHN, STL-10, CUB, SFC, AIR), PGA leads on every domain, e.g., 62.13% on CIFAR-100 vs. 44.64% for GAPF. Under stricter ε=10, PGA still improves AVG_c by ~15% over the best baseline, and under seven defense mechanisms it achieves an average ASR of 72.16% vs. 56.88% for FACL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGA outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluation setting. Among PNS iterative attacks, AGS [55] is the strongest (AVG_c=50.26, AVG_v=31.39 on ImageNet), yet PGA still leads by +21.1 and +16.1 points respectively; PNAA [22], ETF [45], and CDTA [25] trail even further (AVG_c ≤ 39.6). Among generative attacks, GAPF [41] is the closest competitor (AVG_c=56.41, AVG_v=32.79), with PGA improving by +15.0 and +14.7; BIA [61] and its variants (BIA+DA, BIA+RN) cluster around AVG_c≈54 and AVG_v≈32–33, while FACL [58] is marginally better than BIA on AVG_v (33.08 vs. 32.95) but still 14+ points below PGA. CDA [32] is the weakest generative baseline (AVG_c=44.20). Notably, the paper observes that all four PNS iterative methods underperform the generative baselines equipped with a vanilla SimSiam surrogate, underscoring the advantage of the generative paradigm even under the no-box constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: self-supervised surrogate training (building directly on SimSiam's encoder–projector–predictor architecture and symmetrized similarity loss) and generative adversarial attacks (building on BIA's feature-matching generator training). The key departure is that all prior generative attacks (GAP, CDA, LTAP, BIA, FACL) assume access to pre-trained surrogates and large-scale datasets, while all prior PNS attacks (PNAA, ETF, CDTA, AGS) rely on iterative optimization. PGA is the first to combine a generative attack pipeline with the PNS constraint, introducing CMRO (a curriculum that progressively adds FGSM-style micro-perturbations to SimSiam training) and RCPL (regional feature separation with Gram-matrix consistency) to address the data-scarcity challenges that previously made generative attacks infeasible in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a one-step generative attack can outperform iterative PNS methods in both transferability and inference speed, this work reframes the PNS evaluation landscape and provides a more practical, faster benchmark for assessing model robustness under realistic information constraints. The finding that iterative PNS attacks do not surpass generative attacks equipped with even a vanilla SimSiam surrogate challenges the implicit assumption that iterative optimization is necessary under data scarcity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even fewer samples (e.g., hundreds rather than thousands), whether the regional partitioning strategy generalizes to non-grid spatial structures, and how defenses specifically designed against generative attacks might differ from those targeting iterative methods.
