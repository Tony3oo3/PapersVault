## Problem

Current VLM-based autonomous driving planners face a fundamental tension: text-based chain-of-thought reasoning introduces modality mismatch with continuous trajectory control and incurs high latency from autoregressive token-by-token decoding, while end-to-end action planners lack the structured, interpretable reasoning that VLMs provide. No existing framework simultaneously preserves VLM-level semantic reasoning, produces causally consistent multi-scale trajectories, and meets real-time inference constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262255/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes six-camera multi-view images (processed by an EVA-02-L backbone and Q-Former into visual tokens), LiDAR/radar features, a fixed driving text prompt, and a scalar ego-state token encoding velocity, heading, and curvature. It outputs a K-step continuous trajectory as nested coarse-to-fine waypoint sets (e.g., endpoint-only → partial → full trajectory) decoded in a single parallel pass, along with a discrete meta-action label (e.g., straight cruise, unprotected left turn, hard braking) selected from a bank of C learnable queries. The hierarchical output structure means the same forward pass yields trajectories at S temporal resolutions simultaneously, with finer scales conditioned on coarser ones via a causality-preserving mask.

## Method

ColaVLA replaces autoregressive text CoT with a two-pass Cognitive Latent Reasoner: the first VLM forward pass fuses prompt, visual tokens, and ego state into unified representations; an ego-adaptive FiLM router then scores and selects the top-K safety-critical visual tokens (K=256 out of 1200); the second pass feeds these pruned tokens plus C learnable meta-queries through the same VLM to produce compact meta-action embeddings. A Hierarchical Parallel Planner then expands the chosen meta-action with temporal embeddings into S nested trajectory scales, concatenates them with the pruned context, and decodes all scales in one forward pass under a causality-preserving hybrid attention mask that permits bidirectional attention within each scale, global context access, and strictly coarse-to-fine inter-scale flow. The entire system is built on LLaVA v1.5 (LLaMA-7B) with LoRA-adapted weights, trained in two stages: QA pretraining on OmniDrive-nuScenes followed by joint fine-tuning with the action planner, using focal loss for meta-action classification and confidence-guided one-hot supervision for multi-mode trajectory regression.

## Results

On the nuScenes open-loop benchmark, ColaVLA achieves the best average L2 error of 0.30 m and the lowest average collision rate of 0.23% among all action-based driving models, outperforming the strongest prior action-based method SOLVE-E2E (0.31 m L2, 0.30% collision) by 3% in L2 and 23% in collision rate. On the NeuroNCAP closed-loop benchmark, ColaVLA sets a new state of the art with a NeuroNCAP score of 3.48 and an average collision rate of 36.8%, surpassing the previous best (ImpromptuVLA, 2.06 score, 65.1% collision) by +1.42 in score and reducing collisions by 44%. Inference latency is 727 ms per frame on a single H20 GPU, over 5× faster than text-based VLM planners (OmniDrive: 3727 ms; SOLVE-VLM: 3719 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among action-based baselines, ColaVLA outperforms SOLVE-E2E (the strongest prior action-based method) by 3% in average L2 (0.30 vs 0.31 m) and 23% in average collision rate (0.23% vs 0.30%), and beats VAD-Base, BEV-Planner++, and UniAD by wider margins. In closed-loop NeuroNCAP evaluation, ColaVLA (3.48 score, 36.8% collision) surpasses ImpromptuVLA (2.06, 65.1%) by +1.42 in score and 28.3 points in collision reduction, and also outperforms BridgeAD-B with trajectory post-processing (3.06, 44.3%) by +0.42 in score. Notably, ColaVLA matches or exceeds the best text-based VLM planners (EMMA+ at 0.29 m L2, SOLVE-VLM at 0.28 m L2) on open-loop L2 while achieving substantially lower collision rates (0.23% vs 0.20–0.30%), and does so with over 5× lower inference latency (727 ms vs ~3700 ms) by eliminating autoregressive text decoding. The paper also reports that ColaVLA achieves these results without additional safety-critical training data, unlike ImpromptuVLA which uses curated extra data.

## Relation to Prior Work

ColaVLA builds on the LLaVA v1.5 VLM backbone, the SQ-Former visual reasoning architecture from SOLVE, and StreamPETR detection/lane decoders, but its core conceptual departure is from the dominant text-based chain-of-thought paradigm in driving VLMs (DriveVLM, EMMA, OmniDrive, SOLVE-VLM). Rather than autoregressively generating intermediate textual reasoning steps, the paper relocates the entire cognitive process—scene comprehension, critical entity selection, strategic rethinking, and decision synthesis—into a shared latent space using only two VLM forward passes, drawing inspiration from latent-space reasoning in LLMs. The Hierarchical Parallel Planner further departs from both deterministic MLP planners and iterative diffusion samplers by decoding multi-scale trajectories in a single forward pass under a causality-preserving hybrid attention mask, a design not previously explored in the E2E driving literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5× inference latency reduction (727 ms vs. ~3700 ms) while matching or exceeding text-based VLM planners on both open-loop and closed-loop metrics makes VLM-informed driving planning practically deployable, addressing a key barrier to real-time autonomous driving. The substantial closed-loop gap on NeuroNCAP (3.48 vs. 2.06 for the strongest text-based baseline) suggests that latent-space reasoning may capture decision-relevant structure more faithfully than discrete text tokens, though this remains to be validated beyond nuScenes. Open questions include whether the fixed-size meta-action bank generalizes to novel driving maneuvers, whether the approach scales to smaller or larger VLMs, and whether the single-dataset evaluation (nuScenes/NeuroNCAP) reflects broader robustness. The reliance on a 7B-parameter VLM also raises questions about edge-deployment feasibility.
