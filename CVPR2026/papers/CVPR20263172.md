## Problem

Generating 3D sketches from natural language remains largely unexplored: existing 3D shape generation methods (diffusion SDS, neural implicit) require explicit geometry supervision and extensive retraining, while language-driven sketch agents like SketchAgent are confined to the 2D image plane and cannot reason about depth or spatial consistency. The paper tackles how to equip a frozen LLM with 3D spatial drawing ability without any parameter updates or ground-truth sketches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263172/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt (and optionally a reference image) and produces a set of N 3D cubic Bézier curves, each defined by four 3D control points in ℝ³. The LLM emits these as a structured Python list wrapped in <curves>...</curves> delimiters, which a lightweight parser validates and converts into parametric form for rendering. Rendering uses pydiffvg with 16 fixed camera viewpoints projected onto a 512×512 canvas via perspective projection, enabling multi-view CLIP scoring and visualization.

## Method

3DrawAgent is a training-free, three-stage pipeline built on a frozen LLM (Gemini-2.5Pro or DeepSeek-V3.2). First, the LLM acts as a spatial planner, generating 3D Bézier curve sequences via in-context prompting with role instructions, format constraints, coordinate specifications, and ground-truth examples. Second, a Contrastive Knowledge Extraction (CKE) module samples K=5 candidate sketches per prompt, scores them with multi-view CLIP similarity, and constructs pairwise (better, worse) contrastive pairs; an LLM judge then articulates in natural language why one sketch is superior in terms of structural integrity, spatial continuity, or geometric plausibility. Third, these textual advantages are accumulated into an experience library via discrete Add/Delete/Modify/Keep operations, which is injected as an additional prompt segment to guide subsequent generation. The key distinction from training-free GRPO is the shift from group-wise numerical advantage estimation to pairwise relative comparison, eliminating the need for ground-truth references, gradient updates, or structured group rollouts while still enabling iterative self-improvement of the LLM's 3D drawing behavior.

## Results

On the Text-to-3D (Category) benchmark, 3DrawAgent (Gemini-2.5Pro) achieves CLIP-S_T of 0.649 and AES of 4.161, matching or exceeding trained baselines like Diff3DS (0.648 / 3.791) and approaching Dream3DVG (0.660 / 4.150) — all without any parameter updates. On Text-to-3D (Fine-Grained), 3DrawAgent (Gemini) reaches CLIP-S_T 0.669 and AES 4.175, essentially tying Dream3DVG (0.670 / 4.174). For Image-to-3D, 3DrawAgent (Gemini) attains the best CLIP-S_I of 0.873 and AES of 4.255, slightly outperforming 3Doodle (0.869 / 4.264) and Diff3DS (0.865 / 3.828). A user study further confirms 3DrawAgent is preferred 46.66% of the time over Dream3DVG (36.67%) and Diff3DS (16.67%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diff3DS, 3DrawAgent (Gemini) wins decisively on aesthetic quality across all settings (AES 4.161 vs 3.791 on Category, 4.175 vs 3.770 on Fine-Grained, 4.255 vs 3.828 on Image-to-3D) while matching or slightly trailing on CLIP-S_T (0.649 vs 0.648 Category; 0.669 vs 0.650 Fine-Grained). Against Dream3DVG, 3DrawAgent (Gemini) is essentially tied on Fine-Grained CLIP-S_T (0.669 vs 0.670) and AES (4.175 vs 4.174), but trails slightly on Category CLIP-S_T (0.649 vs 0.660) while edging ahead on Category AES (4.161 vs 4.150). Against 3Doodle (Image-to-3D only), 3DrawAgent (Gemini) achieves a higher CLIP-S_I (0.873 vs 0.869) with a marginally lower AES (4.255 vs 4.264). The critical distinction is that all three baselines require substantial training (diffusion SDS, optimization, or 3DGS-guided optimization), whereas 3DrawAgent is entirely training-free, relying solely on in-context prompting and contrastive experience accumulation over a frozen LLM.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends SketchAgent's 2D language-driven sequential sketch generation to 3D by adding depth and spatial continuity constraints to the LLM's action space, and adapts the training-free GRPO paradigm from group-based relative evaluation to a pairwise contrastive experience setting. It departs from the dominant diffusion/SDS-based 3D generation pipeline (DreamFusion, Diff3DS, Dream3DVG) by eliminating the need for a pretrained 3D prior or any gradient-based optimization, instead relying on a frozen LLM as spatial planner and CLIP-based self-evaluation as a non-parametric reward signal. The contrastive knowledge extraction loop—where the LLM critiques its own outputs and accumulates natural-language geometric principles into an experience library—repurposes the GRPO advantage-estimation idea for open-ended creative tasks where scalar rewards or ground-truth references are impractical.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 3D geometric priors can be acquired by a frozen LLM purely through self-produced contrastive feedback, opening a training-free pathway to 3D spatial reasoning that requires only API calls and a single GPU. However, the approach plateaus after roughly two CKE epochs (attributed to LLM over-reasoning), and semantic alignment (CLIP-S_T) remains slightly below trained baselines, suggesting the experience library has limited capacity for encoding complex geometric knowledge. Open questions include whether the accumulated experience generalizes across object categories and scene complexity, whether interactive or iterative sketching is feasible, and whether the pairwise contrastive formulation can be extended to multi-object or scene-level 3D generation.
