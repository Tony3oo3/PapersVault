## Problem

Standard generative model evaluation metrics like FID operate on continuous recognition features that are explicitly trained to be invariant to appearance variations (texture, sharpness, local coherence), causing them to discard cues critical for perceptual quality and to rely on Gaussian distributional assumptions that fail on multimodal or non-photorealistic data. This paper argues that the evaluation space itself—continuous recognition features—is the root cause, and proposes shifting to discrete visual token statistics as a more faithful and interpretable basis for both distribution matching and single-image quality assessment.

## Input / Output

CHD takes two sets of 256×256 RGB images (real and generated), tokenizes each with a retrained TiTok encoder into 128 discrete tokens from a 4096-entry codebook, and outputs a single Hellinger-based distance in [0,1] requiring no training. CMMS takes a single 256×256 RGB image, tokenizes it identically, and outputs a scalar no-reference quality score in [0,1] via a lightweight Transformer-MLP regressor. Both metrics are computed purely from token sequences, with CHD adding negligible overhead beyond encoding and CMMS processing over 1,000 images per second on a single A100.

## Method

The core contribution is a discrete-token evaluation paradigm built on a 1D TiTok tokenizer (128 tokens, 4096 codebook) retrained on 100M DataComp images to capture diverse visual domains. CHD computes two complementary statistics: a unigram histogram over token frequencies (CHD-1D) measuring global vocabulary usage, and a symmetrized 2D spatial co-occurrence histogram over adjacent token pairs (CHD-2D) measuring local structural grammar; the final score is the arithmetic mean of their Hellinger distances. CMMS is a 2-layer Transformer encoder (8 heads, 512-dim embeddings with sinusoidal positions) followed by global average pooling and a 2-layer MLP, trained entirely on synthetic degradations of ImageNet-1K token sequences—uniform token injection at rate p, semantic fragment swapping, and pixel-space distortions (blur, JPEG, noise, occlusion, photometric shifts)—with target quality mapped as q(p)=exp(−20p) to model non-linear human sensitivity. No human preference labels are used in training, and the model is applied zero-shot to all downstream benchmarks.

## Results

On AGIQA, CHD achieves a Spearman correlation of 0.829 with human ratings (N-MSE 0.112), outperforming all distribution-based baselines (FID: 0.771, DINO-FID: 0.657, CMMD: 0.657). CMMS reaches a Spearman of 0.943 and N-MSE of 0.050 on AGIQA, and 0.872 on HPDv3, surpassing the strongest quality baselines (DEQA: 0.886, QUALI: 0.771). For pairwise preference prediction, CMMS attains 71.5% accuracy on AGIQA, 74.9% on HPDv2, 61.3% on HPDv3, and 66.7% on VisForm, leading all compared methods on every benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHD outperforms all six distribution-based baselines on AGIQA: it improves Spearman over FID (0.829 vs. 0.771), CLIP-FID (0.829 vs. 0.714), DINO-FID (0.829 vs. 0.657), CMMD (0.829 vs. 0.657), IS (0.829 vs. 0.543), and KID (0.829 vs. 0.486), with the largest gap over KID and IS. On HPDv3, CHD extends this lead to 0.867 Spearman versus DINO-FID's 0.782 and FID's 0.648. For single-image quality, CMMS dominates all four IQA baselines on AGIQA (Spearman 0.943 vs. DEQA 0.886, QUALI 0.771, MUSIQ 0.486, CLIP-IQA −0.086) and on HPDv3 (0.872 vs. DEQA 0.836). In pairwise preference prediction (Table 3), CMMS leads every baseline on all four benchmarks, beating the closest competitor DEQA by 2.8 points on AGIQA (71.5% vs. 68.7%), 4.3 points on HPDv2 (74.9% vs. 70.6%), 8.6 points on HPDv3 (61.3% vs. 52.7%), and 3.6 points on VisForm (66.7% vs. 63.1%). CLIP-Score and Q-Align trail CMMS by 7–8 points on AGIQA preference accuracy, confirming that token-space representations capture perceptual quality more faithfully than CLIP-based or VLM-based scorers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of evaluating generative models in continuous recognition-feature space (FID, CLIP-FID, DINO-FID, CMMD) by shifting to discrete token space, where a 1D tokenizer (TiTok) maps images to codebook indices that preserve both semantic and perceptual information. It also contrasts with learned no-reference quality metrics (MUSIQ, CLIP-IQA, DEQA, Q-Align) that require large-scale human preference annotations, by instead training CMMS on synthetic degradations of token sequences. The conceptual lineage draws on codebook histograms from compression and VQ-VAE-style tokenization, but repurposes them as a first-class evaluation domain rather than an internal representation for generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The VisForm benchmark (210K images, 62 visual forms, 12 generative models, 14 expert-annotated perceptual dimensions) fills a notable gap in cross-domain evaluation, and the training-free CHD metric offers a lightweight, interpretable alternative to FID that stabilizes with far fewer samples (~1K vs. ~10K+). Open questions include whether the retrained tokenizer on DataComp is essential or whether off-the-shelf TiTok suffices, how higher-order token statistics (beyond bigrams) would affect spatial modeling, and whether the approach extends naturally to video and 3D generation. A practical limitation is that CHD-2D's sparse co-occurrence representation may struggle with very small sample sets where many token pairs are unobserved.
