## Problem

Surgical robotic manipulation demands millimeter-level precision on delicate structures, yet existing approaches either rely on multi-stage 3D scene reconstruction (causing error accumulation and preventing end-to-end optimization) or add wrist-mounted cameras to patient-side manipulators (impractical in clinical settings due to trocar spatial constraints). Additionally, no large-scale public surgical dataset with 3D annotations exists, making it difficult to train geometric models in-domain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262505/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes stereo endoscopic image pairs (1920×1080) from the camera manipulator along with proprioceptive state, operating in a highly confined environment where organ-to-camera distances are often below 10 cm. It outputs 7-dimensional relative actions per arm (3D translation, 3D rotation as Euler angles, 1D jaw angle) expressed in the endoscope coordinate frame, predicted as chunks of 100 future actions with exponential temporal weighting. The geometry transformer also produces 3D latent embeddings and dense point maps in the endoscope frame as intermediate representations.

## Method

SST is an end-to-end visuomotor policy built on three components: (1) a MASt3R-based geometry transformer finetuned on the newly constructed Surgical3D dataset (30K photorealistic stereo pairs with 3D ground truth generated in NVIDIA Omniverse) to extract robust 3D latent embeddings via DPT heads with confidence-aware point-map regression; (2) a Multi-Level Spatial Feature Connector (MSFC) that aggregates embeddings from four specific decoder layers (those feeding the DPT heads), projects them to a lower dimension, concatenates along the feature axis, and aligns them to the action space via a lightweight MLP; and (3) a 12-layer endoscope-centric policy decoder using the ACT framework to predict action chunks, trained end-to-end with MSE loss. The endoscope-centric action space is adopted because surgical robots lack accurate forward kinematics (Set-Up Joints use only potentiometers), and all 3D embeddings are naturally defined in the endoscope frame, ensuring perception-action alignment.

## Results

On three real-world surgical tasks (peg pickup, knot tying, ex-vivo gallbladder dissection), SST achieves 10/10 and 8/10 success on peg pickup (Test1/Test2), 10/10 grasp and 7/10 loop (7/10 whole task) on knot tying, and 10/10 grasp and 6/10 dissection (6/10 whole task) on gallbladder dissection. SST is the only method without wrist cameras to achieve non-zero success on the ex-vivo gallbladder dissection task, where both ACT and Diffusion Policy score 0/10. On spatial generalization (Test2 with expanded pickup range and depth variation), SST achieves 8/10 versus 6/10 for SRT (with wrist cameras) and 1–2/10 for ACT and DP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SST outperforms all three baselines across every task and subtask. Against SRT (which uses additional wrist cameras), SST matches the 10/10 grasp rate on knot tying but substantially exceeds it on the loop subtask (7/10 vs. 3/10) and whole task (7/10 vs. 2/10), while also achieving better spatial generalization on peg pickup Test2 (8/10 vs. 6/10). Against ACT, SST improves peg pickup Test2 from 2/10 to 8/10, knot tying whole task from 0/10 to 7/10, and gallbladder whole task from 0/10 to 6/10. Against Diffusion Policy, SST similarly dominates: knot tying whole task goes from 1/10 to 7/10, and gallbladder whole task from 0/10 to 6/10. Notably, SST achieves these results using only endoscopic images, whereas SRT requires wrist-mounted cameras that are impractical in real surgical settings due to trocar spatial constraints.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on MASt3R as a feed-forward geometry transformer backbone and the Action Chunk Transformer (ACT) framework for action prediction, but departs from both the multi-stage reconstruction pipelines (SfM, NeRF-based) and the wrist-camera-augmented approaches (SRT) that dominate prior surgical manipulation work. The key conceptual departure is treating 3D spatial priors as a learned, end-to-end component: rather than explicitly reconstructing the scene or adding hardware sensors, SST finetunes a geometry transformer on a purpose-built synthetic dataset (Surgical3D) and bridges its multi-level latent embeddings to the action space via a lightweight connector. This positions the work at the intersection of feed-forward 3D reconstruction and visuomotor policy learning, addressing a gap where neither paradigm alone suffices for the precision and sensing constraints of surgical robotics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of Surgical3D (30K stereo pairs with 3D ground truth) addresses a critical data bottleneck in surgical 3D perception, and the demonstration that a lightweight finetuned geometry transformer can replace impractical wrist cameras while matching or exceeding their performance is practically significant for clinical deployment. However, the evaluation is limited to three tasks on a single robot platform with only 10 trials each, and the ex-vivo gallbladder dissection remains the weakest result (6/10), suggesting that in-vivo generalization and longer-horizon tasks remain open. The synthetic-to-real domain gap, mitigated here with pseudo-labels from VGGT, is acknowledged but not fully resolved, and whether the MSFC design generalizes beyond the specific MASt3R architecture or to other surgical platforms is untested.
