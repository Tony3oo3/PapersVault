## Problem

Existing zero-watermarking methods rely on absolute feature descriptors or global statistics that are fundamentally destroyed by diffusion-based generative editing, while embedding watermarking sacrifices image fidelity to survive such edits. No prior paradigm simultaneously preserves perfect visual quality and achieves robustness against modern AI editing operations like style transfer, inpainting, and deterministic regeneration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260301/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 RGB image, partitions it into 196 non-overlapping 16×16 patches, and extracts per-patch features via a frozen ViT-B/16. The output is a set of K=50 patch-pair indices (the zero-watermark), which is stored externally (hashed or encrypted) without any modification to the image pixels. Verification of a suspect image computes its own top-K predicted pairs and measures the Jaccard-style overlap ratio against the stored reference set.

## Method

The core insight is that while individual patch RGB values change drastically under generative editing, the L2 distance between patch-pair features exhibits near-affine invariance (fitted slope ≈1, R²>0.95), making relational geometry a stable fingerprint. For training, a pre-trained Stable Diffusion VAE simulates generative edits; ViT features of the original and VAE-reconstructed images are compared to identify the top-K most distance-stable patch pairs as ground-truth labels. A lightweight MLP edge predictor takes the concatenation of two patch features plus their L2 distance and outputs a sigmoid stability score, trained with binary cross-entropy to recover the ground-truth pair set. At inference, only the ViT encoder and MLP predictor are needed: all 19,128 pair scores are computed and the top-50 indices form the zero-watermark, requiring no adversarial training against specific editing models.

## Results

Rel-Zero achieves TPR@(0.1%FPR) of 85.13% on deterministic regeneration, 89.65% on InstructPix2Pix, 95.63% on MagicBrush, 96.55% on UltraEdit, and 97.43% on ControlNet-Inpainting, while maintaining perfect image fidelity (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS unchanged). On common distortions, it scores 95.12–98.57% across Gaussian noise, scaling, contrast, brightness, and cropout. These results substantially outperform existing zero-watermarking baselines (ConZWNet and FGPCET), which drop to 0.01–7.25% TPR under generative edits, and approach the robustness of embedding methods (VINE, Robust-Wide) without any image modification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two zero-watermarking baselines, Rel-Zero dominates overwhelmingly: ConZWNet achieves only 0.01–5.13% TPR under generative edits (e.g., 0.01% on MagicBrush, 2.41% on ControlNet-Inpainting), and FGPCET reaches at most 7.25% (UltraEdit), whereas Rel-Zero scores 85–97% across the same attacks. Against the embedding baselines, Rel-Zero trades a small robustness gap for perfect fidelity: VINE leads on deterministic regeneration (99.98% vs. 85.13%) and UltraEdit (99.96% vs. 96.55%), while Robust-Wide is slightly ahead on InstructPix2Pix (97.23% vs. 89.65%); however, Rel-Zero matches or exceeds both on ControlNet-Inpainting (97.43% vs. 93.04% and 82.11%) and MagicBrush (95.63% vs. 94.58% and 81.97%). On common distortions, all methods perform well, but Rel-Zero (95.12–98.57%) is competitive with VINE and Robust-Wide while introducing zero pixel modification. The DWT-DCT baseline is essentially broken under all generative edits (<1% TPR), confirming that traditional frequency-domain embedding cannot survive diffusion-based reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both embedding watermarking (DWT-DCT, VINE, Robust-Wide), which sacrifices fidelity to survive generative edits, and prior zero-watermarking (ConZWNet, FGPCET), which relies on fragile absolute feature descriptors that generative models destroy. Rather than building on a specific prior architecture, it repurposes ViT patch embeddings and Stable Diffusion's VAE in a novel relational-geometry formulation: the watermark is not a feature vector or hash but a set of patch-pair indices whose inter-patch distances are empirically invariant under editing. The VAE-as-edit-simulator trick for generating training targets is borrowed conceptually from EditGuard [43], but applied here to define relational stability rather than tamper localization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a concrete gap: no prior zero-watermarking method survived modern generative edits (TPR near 0%), while embedding methods that do survive degrade image quality intolerably for high-stakes domains (medical imaging, autonomous driving). The empirical discovery of near-affine invariance in patch-pair distance space (R² > 0.95) provides a principled, interpretable foundation rather than a black-box learned fingerprint. Open questions include whether the invariance holds under adversarially targeted edits designed to break relational structure, whether it extends to video or 3D content, and whether the VAE-based training proxy faithfully captures the full distribution of real generative edits at scale.
