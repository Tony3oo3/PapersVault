## Problem

Multimodal large reasoning models (MLRMs) remain highly prone to hallucinations, and the paper identifies that transition words (e.g., because, however, wait) marking causal, contrastive, and reflective reasoning steps consistently exhibit high token-level entropy and are strongly correlated with hallucinated content. Existing training-free decoding strategies lack analysis of these reasoning-specific behavioral patterns, and standard discrete token sampling collapses the full predictive distribution at each step, discarding distributional information that could guide the model through uncertain reasoning states.

## Input / Output

LEAD operates as a plug-and-play decoding wrapper around any pretrained MLRM, consuming the same RGB image and text query as the base model. During generation, the model first produces an explicit reasoning chain (delimited by think tags) and then a final answer; the key difference is that at high-entropy steps, the next-step input is a continuous probability-weighted embedding over the full vocabulary rather than a single sampled token embedding, while the final output tokens are still sampled discretely. No additional training, fine-tuning, or architectural modification is required.

## Method

LEAD introduces an entropy-aware reasoning mode switching mechanism that uses token-level entropy as a dynamic confidence signal: when entropy exceeds a tracked reference threshold, the model enters latent mode and feeds a probability-weighted mixture of all token embeddings (E_{v~p_t}[e(v)]) as the next-step input, preserving multiple candidate reasoning hypotheses; when entropy drops below the threshold, it reverts to discrete one-hot token embeddings for deterministic convergence. A persistence window prevents rapid oscillation between modes, and a global switch counter (default C_max=5) halts further reasoning to avoid overthinking. Additionally, at the onset of each high-entropy phase, LEAD injects a visual anchor vector (the averaged embedding of pretrained vision special tokens) into the latent embedding with a tunable strength λ, re-orienting the model toward visual evidence during uncertain reasoning. The entire procedure is training-free and requires only access to the model's logits and embedding matrix at inference time.

## Results

On R1-Onevision-7B, LEAD achieves an average +3.6% improvement on general reasoning/understanding benchmarks (e.g., VStar 71.2, MMEval-Pro 73.9) and significant hallucination-mitigation gains of +4.7% on MMHalu (3.80/6) and +3.8% on Bingo (3.84/5). Across five MLRMs, LEAD consistently improves hallucination scores by 2.8–4.7% (MMHalu) and 2.8–4.2% (Bingo). On domain-specific tasks, LEAD yields +2.0% average accuracy on mathematics benchmarks and +3.2% on scientific benchmarks (Physics, Chemistry, Biology subsets of MMK12).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LEAD outperforms all three baselines (VCD, MemVR, SID) across every benchmark for R1-Onevision-7B. On the hallucination metric MMHalu, LEAD scores 3.80 versus SID 3.70, MemVR 3.69, and VCD 3.55, representing a 1.0–2.7% absolute gain over the strongest baseline. On Bingo, LEAD (3.84) leads MemVR (3.68) by 1.6% and VCD (3.61) by 2.3%. On general reasoning, LEAD's VStar score of 71.2 exceeds SID (70.2) and MemVR (69.6), while its MMEval-Pro score of 73.9 surpasses MemVR (71.3) by 2.6 points. The gains are consistent across all five evaluated MLRMs (R1-Onevision, Vision-R1, VL-Rethinker, VL-Cogito, OpenVLThinker), confirming LEAD's model-agnostic effectiveness as a plug-and-play decoding strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

LEAD builds directly on the superposed-representation line of work (Swireasoning, Soft Thinking, Mixture of Inputs), which showed that replacing discrete one-hot token embeddings with probability-weighted continuous embeddings can preserve latent reasoning hypotheses. However, those prior methods apply latent decoding uniformly or with fixed schedules, whereas LEAD introduces an entropy-driven, adaptive switching mechanism that dynamically toggles between discrete and latent modes based on token-level uncertainty. The paper also departs from the dominant hallucination-mitigation strategies in MLRMs—visual reward shaping, data augmentation, and contrastive decoding—by operating purely at the decoding level with no retraining, and adds a visual anchor injection component that has no direct analogue in the superposed-representation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MLRMs become the default architecture for complex visual reasoning, hallucination mitigation that is training-free and model-agnostic is practically valuable; LEAD's plug-and-play design means it can be retrofitted onto any existing MLRM without fine-tuning. The empirical link between transition words, high token entropy, and hallucination-prone content provides a mechanistic explanation that could inform future training objectives or reward designs. Open questions include whether the fixed entropy threshold and persistence window generalize to larger models or longer reasoning chains, whether the visual anchor injection remains effective for video or multi-image inputs, and whether the modest 2–5% gains compound when combined with other decoding strategies.
