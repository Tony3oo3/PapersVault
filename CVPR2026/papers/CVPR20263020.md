## Problem

Current MLLM benchmarks for medical diagnosis are predominantly single-modality and single-turn, failing to evaluate the progressive multi-step reasoning and cross-modal evidence integration that real clinical workflows demand. In ophthalmology specifically, accurate diagnosis (e.g., diabetic macular edema) requires correlating findings across complementary imaging modalities in a logically-sequenced chain, a capability that no existing benchmark systematically assesses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263020/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs one or more ophthalmic images (from CFP, OCT, FFA, ICGA, EP, or RetCam modalities) with a multiple-choice VQA prompt, where distractors are sampled from same-attribute clinical nouns to ensure semantic relevance. The model must produce a structured six-stage reasoning chain (IQA→AL→LC→DD→SG→CD) with a selected answer and self-reported confidence score (normalized to [0,1]) at each stage. Questions are stratified into three difficulty tiers (Resident, Attending, Specialist) with per-question clinical importance scores, and cross-modal tasks require temporally aligned same-eye image sets across up to four modalities.

## Method

X-PCR formalizes ophthalmic diagnosis as a six-stage progressive reasoning chain with enforced causal dependencies, where each stage conditions on validated outputs from its predecessor (e.g., lesion characterization requires correct anatomical localization, severity grading requires correct diagnosis). Cross-modal reasoning is operationalized through three task families—Correspondence Identification, Diagnostic Integration, and Modality Selection—built on a disease-centric alignment framework that standardizes each condition via a Disease-Anatomy-Lesion-Grading-Management schema and temporally aligns same-eye multi-modal images. The benchmark comprises 26,415 images and 177,868 expert-verified VQA pairs from 51 public datasets plus 58 hospital-collected multi-modal cases, with VQAs generated semi-automatically (GPT-5 drafting, Gemini-2.5-pro auditing, ophthalmologist validation with κ≥0.8 adjudication). Evaluation employs a difficulty- and uncertainty-aware scheme combining Stage-Wise Accuracy, Chain Completion Rate, Expertise-Stratified Accuracy, an Uncertainty-Aware Score (UAS) that penalizes overconfident errors, Expected Calibration Error (ECE), and a Modality Contribution Score (MCS) for ablation-based cross-modal integration analysis.

## Results

On X-PCR, GPT-5 achieves the highest average stage-wise accuracy (76.24%) and Chain Completion Rate (24.47%), followed by Gemini-2.5-Pro (69.61% avg, 0.77% CCR) and InternVL-32B (70.14% avg, 0.92% CCR). Stage-wise accuracy declines sharply across the six-stage reasoning chain for all models (e.g., GPT-5 drops from 98.90% on IQA to 54.71% on CD), and no model surpasses attending physicians (79.91% avg accuracy, 41.24% CCR) or specialists (82.85% avg, 62.48% CCR). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B achieves the best Uncertainty-Aware Score (66.37%), while medical-specialized models (MedGemma-27B-IT at 65.21%, Lingshu-7B at 61.66%) trail general-purpose models by 5–11 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 leads all 21 evaluated MLLMs with 76.24% average stage-wise accuracy and 24.47% CCR, outperforming the next-best commercial model Gemini-2.5-Pro (69.61% avg) by 6.6 points and the top open-source model InternVL-32B (70.14% avg) by 6.1 points. However, GPT-5 still falls short of attending physicians (79.91% avg, 41.24% CCR) and specialists (82.85% avg, 62.48% CCR), with the CCR gap to specialists reaching 38.52 percentage points. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (69.15% avg, 13.77% CCR) and InternVL-32B (70.14% avg) are the strongest, while older 7B models like LLaVA-v1.5-7B (48.04% avg) and ShareGPT4V-7B (49.71% avg) lag by over 20 points. Medical-specialized models underperform general-purpose counterparts: MedGemma-27B-IT (65.21%) trails GPT-5 by 11 points, and LLaVA-Med-7B (39.60%) is the weakest model overall. On uncertainty calibration, GPT-5 achieves the lowest ECE (0.062) and highest UAS (74.32), while smaller open-source models (LLaVA, ShareGPT4V) show ECE >0.10, indicating poor confidence calibration that would be unsafe in clinical deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

X-PCR extends the medical VQA evaluation paradigm (PathVQA, EndoBench, LOMD) by introducing two orthogonal axes absent from prior benchmarks: a six-stage progressive reasoning chain with enforced causal dependencies (extending the multi-image aggregation of MedFrameQA and Rjua-Meddqa into a domain-specific diagnostic workflow), and explicit cross-modal integration across six ophthalmic imaging modalities with ground-truth correspondences. It departs from prior ophthalmic benchmarks (LOMD, EyePCR) that evaluate modalities in isolation, and from general progressive-reasoning benchmarks that lack modality-specific alignment. The uncertainty-aware and expertise-graded evaluation scheme is novel relative to the accuracy-only metrics used in all compared benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest MLLM (GPT-5) completes only 24.5% of full six-stage reasoning chains versus 62.5% for human specialists, and that adding more imaging modalities often *degrades* performance (negative Modality Contribution Scores), suggesting current models cannot effectively integrate cross-modal evidence rather than simply lacking knowledge. This has direct implications for clinical deployment: models that appear competent on single-modality tasks may be unreliable in real multi-modal diagnostic workflows. Open questions include whether the cross-modal integration gap is architectural (attention over heterogeneous image types) or data-driven (lack of aligned multi-modal training pairs), and whether uncertainty calibration can be improved to make model abstention clinically safe.
