## Problem

Existing vision-language-action models for robotic manipulation rely on fixed camera viewpoints and shared visual encoders, which cause occlusion-induced perception failures in cluttered scenes and task interference when handling visually diverse manipulation tasks. This limits both the completeness of scene understanding and the scalability of multi-task generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260065/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a language instruction, 128×128 RGB-D images from four fixed cameras (frontal, left/right shoulder, wrist-mounted), and the current gripper state. The RGB-D inputs are first converted to a global point cloud in world coordinates, which serves as the shared 3D representation for both viewpoint exploration and action prediction. The model outputs a 7-DoF end-effector pose (Cartesian position + quaternion rotation), binary gripper and collision states, and K=3 virtual camera poses each encoded as a 5-dimensional spherical-coordinate vector (azimuth, elevation, radius, up-vector azimuth, up-vector elevation) under a look-at constraint toward the origin.

## Method

TVVE integrates two core modules: (1) TaskMoE, a Mixture-of-Experts visual encoder that routes features through N_G=8 gates (fewer than N_J tasks) to a shared pool of N_E=16 experts, where routing is guided by cross-attention between instruction and scene features fused with the task ID via FiLM, and top-2 experts are activated per input; and (2) MVEP, a reinforcement-learning policy that takes the global point cloud concatenated with RGB features and predicts K camera poses as diagonal Gaussians in spherical coordinates, sampled via the reparameterization trick and constrained by sigmoid to valid ranges. MVEP is trained with PPO in a pseudo-environment where the reward combines the task-loss improvement over a frozen fixed-view reference model, negative grounding-heatmap entropy for confidence, and pairwise cosine distance for viewpoint diversity, all adaptively normalized via the Welford algorithm. Training proceeds in three stages: supervised fixed-view pretraining, PPO-based MVEP optimization (only MVEP trainable), and final end-to-end fine-tuning with MVEP frozen.

## Results

On RLBench (18 tasks, multi-view setup), TVVE achieves an average success rate of 86.6% with an average rank of 2.17, surpassing the previous state-of-the-art ARP (81.6%, rank 2.83) by ~5 percentage points. Notable per-task gains include Insert Peg (98.0% vs. 53.2% for ARP) and Sort Shape (62.0% vs. 35.2%). On the out-of-distribution RLBench-OG benchmark, TVVE attains 67.0% average success rate (rank 1.4), outperforming RVT-2 (64.5%) and ARP (63.7%) under occlusion, lighting, texture, and camera-pose perturbations. Real-robot experiments on Dobot and Franka confirm gains of +20% and +6% over Diffusion Policy and ARP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TVVE outperforms all 11 baselines on the primary RLBench multi-view benchmark (18 tasks), achieving 86.6% average success rate versus 81.6% for ARP, 81.4% for RVT-2, and 81.3% for 3D Diffuser Actor. The largest absolute gains are on occlusion-sensitive tasks: Insert Peg improves from 53.2% (ARP) / 40.0% (RVT-2) to 98.0%, and Sort Shape from 35.2% (ARP) / 35.0% (RVT-2) to 62.0%, directly attributable to dynamic viewpoint exploration. On the single-view RLBench setup (10 tasks), TVVE leads with 83.2% vs. 82.7% (RVT-2) and 78.4% (3D Diffuser Actor). On RLBench-OG, TVVE achieves the best average rank (1.4) and outperforms ARP by +3.3 points and RVT-2 by +2.5 points, with consistent advantages across all seven perturbation types (occlusion, lighting, table color/texture, distractor, background texture, camera pose). On real-robot tasks, TVVE beats Diffusion Policy by +20% on Dobot (88.0% vs. 68.0%) and by +6% over ARP on Franka (78.0% vs. 72.0%), confirming cross-platform generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

TVVE builds directly on the RVT-2 pipeline (coarse-to-fine grounding, multi-view transformer) and the ARP autoregressive action policy, upgrading both with a TaskMoE module that replaces the shared visual encoder with task-routed experts guided by cross-attention and FiLM-fused instruction/scene cues. It departs from the fixed-viewpoint paradigm common to RVT, RVT-2, and most VLA models by introducing MVEP, an RL-trained policy (PPO in a pseudo-environment) that dynamically selects and re-renders K virtual camera poses from a reconstructed point cloud. The decoupled gating design (N_G < N_J) distinguishes TaskMoE from prior MoE applications in robotics (e.g., SDP), which typically use one gate per task, by encouraging parameter sharing among semantically similar tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks in multi-task VLA manipulation—occlusion from fixed cameras and cross-task feature interference—with a combined solution validated on simulation (RLBench, RLBench-OG) and two real-robot platforms. The RLBench-OG benchmark, covering occlusions, lighting, texture, distractor, and camera-pose perturbations, fills a gap in OOD evaluation for manipulation. However, the framework's reliance on accurate global point clouds limits applicability to reflective or transparent objects, and multi-view re-rendering adds inference latency; whether the RL-based view exploration generalizes beyond tabletop settings with sparse depth remains an open question.
