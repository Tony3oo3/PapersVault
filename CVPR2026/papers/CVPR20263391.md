## Problem

Generating long-duration character videos with consistent multi-view appearance and expressive identity remains challenging: existing methods either provide insufficient context (a single reference image or text prompt) or inject non-character-centric frames as ad-hoc 'memory,' both of which fail to preserve stable identity cues across viewpoints and expressions as sequences grow longer. The paper reframes character video generation as an 'outside-looking-in' scenario where a character's visual attributes can be compactly and persistently represented by a structured set of anchor frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263391/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt, a first-frame image (which doubles as the global anchor capturing the overall scene), optional audio for lip-sync and expression driving, and optional structured anchor sets: up to 4 viewpoint frames (front, back, left, right) and up to 8 expression frames (surprise, angry, disgust, fear, contempt, sad, neutral, happy). Output is a long-duration character video exceeding 10 minutes, generated via chunk-wise autoregressive inference where each 5-second chunk uses the last four latent frames of the previous chunk as prefix tokens with linear blending in overlap regions, while all anchors remain fixedly attached throughout the entire sequence as persistent references.

## Method

Gloria builds on the Wan-I2V DiT architecture (17B parameters, 40 blocks) and introduces a content-anchor injection mechanism where anchor frames are encoded through a shared 3D VAE, patchified, and concatenated with the noised video latent tokens so they participate directly in self-attention. Two key mechanisms prevent failure modes: (1) Superset Content Anchoring samples anchors from the full-length source video rather than only the 5-second training clip, forcing the model to learn semantic correspondences instead of copy-pasting pixels; (2) RoPE as Weak Condition assigns distinct temporal offsets (o_v=200, o_e=400, o_l=600 plus sub-offsets 0–7) to different anchor types and subcategories in 3D RoPE, letting the model disentangle overlapping visual content across anchors during attention. Training proceeds in three stages—audio-visual alignment (2M clips), global anchor injection with probabilistic prefix sampling (10M clips), and viewpoint/expression anchor fine-tuning (500K clips)—using flow matching with classifier-free guidance, and a scalable data pipeline extracts anchors via GVHMR pose estimation for viewpoints and EmotiEffLib + Gemini verification for expressions.

## Results

On long-term consistency (5–10 min videos), Gloria achieves 0.960 subject consistency, 0.951 background consistency, and 0.787 ArcFace score, surpassing the strongest baseline InfiniteTalk (0.952/0.942/0.695). For multi-view appearance and expressive identity, Gloria scores 0.821 DINO-I, 0.858 CLIP-I, and 0.717 expression consistency, outperforming all six multi-reference baselines including Humo (0.810/0.816/0.672) and Kling (0.698/0.797/0.599). On fundamental capability, Gloria attains the best IQA (4.65) and Sync-C (5.12) while maintaining competitive AES (3.63) and the lowest Sync-D (8.83) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gloria outperforms all baselines across every evaluated dimension. In long-term consistency, it beats InfiniteTalk (the strongest long-duration baseline) by +0.008 subject consistency and a substantial +0.092 ArcFace score, indicating far better identity preservation over 10-minute sequences; HunyuanAvatar and WanS2V trail further behind. For multi-view appearance and expressive identity, Gloria edges out Humo (the closest competitor) by +0.011 DINO-I and +0.045 expression consistency, while leading Kling by +0.123 DINO-I and +0.119 CLIP-I, and Phantom by +0.224 DINO-I. On fundamental capability, Gloria achieves the best lip-sync quality (Sync-C 5.12 vs. 4.73 for HunyuanAvatar) and highest image quality (IQA 4.65 vs. 4.62 for SekoTalk), though it is marginally behind SekoTalk on aesthetic score (3.63 vs. 3.65). User studies with 60 domain experts confirm consistent human preference for Gloria over all compared methods in both reference-based and fundamental capability evaluations.

## Relation to Prior Work

Gloria builds directly on the Wan-I2V DiT architecture and extends it with a structured content-anchor mechanism for persistent character reference. It departs from prior long-duration approaches (InfiniteTalk's frame re-insertion, WanS2V's FramePack) that use non-character-centric or ad-hoc frames as memory, instead introducing a compact, semantically organized set of anchors (global scene, multi-view, multi-expression) that are explicitly character-centric. The RoPE-as-Weak-Condition mechanism repurposes rotary positional embeddings from RoFormer as a lightweight disambiguation signal for multiple simultaneous anchors, and the Superset Content Anchoring strategy addresses a known failure mode (copy-paste) in reference-based generation by mixing intra- and extra-clip anchors during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to generate 10+ minute character videos with stable multi-view appearance and expressive identity consistency is practically significant for film, gaming, and virtual production pipelines where manual re-anchoring is infeasible. The automated data pipeline (GVHMR for viewpoints, EmotiEffLib + Gemini for expressions) lowers the barrier to constructing anchor sets at scale, making the approach more accessible. Open questions include whether the single-subject focus generalizes to multi-character scenes, how well the model handles novel viewpoints or expressions not covered by the provided anchors, and whether the 17B/512-GPU training cost is prohibitive for broader adoption. The chunk-wise autoregressive inference, while effective, still relies on fixed anchor attachment and may struggle with scene-level changes (e.g., costume changes) that invalidate the anchor set.
