## Problem

General-purpose vision-language models pretrained on large-scale RGB datasets suffer severe performance degradation on SAR imagery due to fundamental modality mismatch, lack of geospatial prior knowledge, and the extreme information sparsity inherent to coherent microwave imaging. No existing VLM systematically incorporates multi-source geospatial priors to compensate for sparse SAR representations, leaving a critical gap in open-world SAR interpretation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263678/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a SAR image, a set of 64-dimensional AlphaEarth Foundation (AEF) embedding vectors sampled on an N_lon × N_lat grid over the image's geographic bounding box (with imaging year), and a natural-language instruction. AEF coordinates are linearly mapped to the SAR image's pixel coordinate system to establish strict spatial alignment. Outputs include target counts, 3×3 grid spatial locations, coarse- and fine-grained class labels, and bounding boxes (pre-aligned to Qwen2.5-VL's unified absolute coordinate resolution), all generated as free-form text.

## Method

FUSAR-GPT builds on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and introduces two core mechanisms. First, a Token-wise Linear Modulation (TLM) module treats sparse AEF geospatial vectors as conditioning signals: a 2-layer MLP with SiLU activation projects each AEF vector into per-channel (γ, β) modulation parameters, which are then spatially interpolated onto the dense visual token grid via distance-based Gaussian kernel weights, and applied as channel-wise affine transformations x′ = x ⊙ (1+γ) + β without modifying the visual backbone. Second, a two-stage decoupled SFT strategy separates knowledge injection from task execution: Stage 1 trains only the AEF-embedding MLP (θ_ae) to align multi-source geospatial features with descriptive text while freezing the visual encoder and LLM; Stage 2 trains only rank-8 LoRA adapters (θ_lora) for downstream task reasoning while freezing both the visual encoder and the Stage-1 AEF MLP, preventing conflicting optimization of cross-modal fusion and instruction following.

## Results

On the FUSAR-GPT evaluation set, FUSAR-GPT achieves 52.53% accuracy on target counting, surpassing the best baseline (Qwen3-VL-4B at 45.45%) by over 7%. For spatial localization, it reaches 52.02% Acc@100, 79.29% Acc@50, and 91.41% Top1, improving upon the best baselines by 8–12%. On target detection at IoU=0.25, the overall F1 score jumps from 47.1% (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) to 74.8%, a near-28% absolute gain. In coarse-grained target classification, FUSAR-GPT exceeds Qwen2.5-VL-7B by more than 12% on both plane (76.85% vs. 64.66%) and ship (67.42% vs. 55.19%) categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUSAR-GPT outperforms all six baseline families across every evaluated task. On target counting, it leads Qwen3-VL-4B (the strongest baseline) by 7.08 percentage points (52.53% vs. 45.45%), and notably the larger Qwen3-VL-8B actually underperforms its 4B counterpart (41.41%), confirming that scaling general-purpose VLMs does not resolve SAR-specific challenges. For spatial localization, FUSAR-GPT gains 8.08 points over Qwen3-VL-4B on Acc@100 (52.02% vs. 43.94%) and 8.10 points on Acc@50 (79.29% vs. 71.21%), while its Top1 score of 91.41% exceeds the best baseline (85.86%, shared by Qwen3-VL-8B and LLaVA-NeXT) by 5.55 points. In target detection at IoU=0.25, FUSAR-GPT nearly doubles the F1 of Qwen2.5-VL-7B (74.8% vs. 47.1%), with particularly large gains on the ship category (57.1% vs. 38.5%), indicating superior robustness to low-contrast targets. For coarse-grained classification, FUSAR-GPT surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 12.19 points on plane (76.85% vs. 64.66%) and 12.23 points on ship (67.42% vs. 55.19%), with even larger margins on several fine-grained sub-classes (e.g., P-3: 100% vs. 66.67%, P-12: 71.43% vs. 0.00%). The consistent 10%+ advantage across tasks and baselines underscores that the spatiotemporal feature embedding and two-stage decoupled SFT strategy address fundamental SAR modality gaps that no amount of parameter scaling in general VLMs can overcome.

## Relation to Prior Work

FUSAR-GPT builds directly on Qwen2.5-VL-7B as its visual-language backbone, extending it with a geospatial prior (AlphaEarth Foundation embeddings) injected via a Token-wise Linear Modulation (TLM) module inspired by conditional normalization (FiLM-style affine modulation). It departs from prior SAR VLM efforts (SARCLIP, SARLANG-1M), which achieved only basic contrastive image-text alignment, by introducing a third modality (geospatial features) and a two-stage decoupled SFT strategy that separates cross-modal knowledge injection from task-specific reasoning. The broader remote sensing VLM lineage (EarthGPT, GeoChat, EarthDial) has focused almost exclusively on optical or multispectral imagery; FUSAR-GPT is positioned as the first VLM explicitly designed for SAR's coherent-imaging characteristics and information sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that injecting stable geospatial priors as conditioning signals can substantially close the modality gap between optical-pretrained VLMs and SAR imagery, offering a practical recipe (TLM + two-stage SFT) that other SAR or low-signal remote sensing modalities could adopt. However, the evaluation is constrained to only 10k training and 2k test images with just two target classes (plane, ship), and no SAR-adapted baselines are included, making it difficult to isolate how much of the gain comes from the geospatial prior versus simply fine-tuning on domain data. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more diverse SAR targets and scenes, whether explicit temporal modeling (beyond the single-year AEF snapshot) would further improve performance, and whether the TLM mechanism scales to higher-resolution or multi-polarization SAR inputs.
