## Problem

Embedding-based unsupervised anomaly detection methods typically assume a single-prototype (e.g., one hypersphere) to enclose all normal features, which produces an overly loose decision boundary when normal data exhibits high intra-class variance from illumination, pose, or texture changes. Existing feature-space anomaly synthesis strategies (e.g., SimpleNet's fixed-scale Gaussian noise) further compound this by generating pseudo-anomalies blindly, often placing them inside normal clusters rather than at the true decision boundary.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261700/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 256×256, extracts multi-scale features from a frozen WideResNet50 (layers 2 and 3, concatenated to 1536 channels), and projects them through a trainable residual MLP into a d-dimensional embedding space. At inference, the trained discriminator produces a per-pixel normality probability that is inverted to form a pixel-level anomaly map (upsampled via bilinear interpolation), while the image-level score is the maximum pixel score across the map.

## Method

PGBL combines three synergistic modules. MPCC structures the normal feature space using a dual-level prototype system (one global anchor plus M=10 cluster prototypes updated via EMA) and a composite contrastive loss that simultaneously enforces global cohesion, local cluster compactness, and inter-prototype diversity. BAAS identifies topological boundary points within each cluster via k-NN sparsity scores, synthesizes pseudo-anomalies by adding adaptive Gaussian noise at those points, and applies a prototype-aware quality filter that rejects candidates falling closer to any prototype than their origin. DBR is a 4-layer MLP discriminator trained with binary cross-entropy to separate normal features from the synthesized boundary anomalies, with gradients backpropagated to both the discriminator and the projection head to jointly tighten the decision surface. The entire framework is trained end-to-end with L_total = L_MPCC + γ·L_DBR while the backbone remains frozen.

## Results

On MVTec-AD, PGBL achieves 99.8% I-AUROC, 99.9% I-AP, 98.1% P-AUROC, 65.1% P-AP, and 95.0% P-AUPRO, setting new state-of-the-art on four of five metrics. On VisA, it reaches 98.0% I-AUROC, 98.2% I-AP, 98.6% P-AUROC, 50.4% P-AP, and 92.6% P-AUPRO. On the large-scale Real-IAD benchmark, PGBL attains 91.5% I-AUROC and 92.3% P-AUPRO, outperforming the previous best (MVAD) by 1.3 points on image-level detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGBL outperforms all seven baselines on MVTec-AD for I-AUROC (99.8 vs. 99.6 for SimpleNet, the closest competitor) and I-AP (99.9 vs. 99.8), and leads on P-AP (65.1 vs. 60.5 for NoCoAD) and P-AUPRO (95.0 vs. 94.1 for NoCoAD), while tying at 98.1% P-AUROC with SimpleNet, DeSTSeg, and PatchCore. On VisA, the gains are more pronounced: PGBL surpasses the runner-up SimpleNet by 2.6 points on I-AUROC (98.0 vs. 95.4) and by 4.6 points on P-AP (50.4 vs. 36.8), indicating stronger localization of small defects. On Real-IAD, PGBL achieves the best I-AUROC of 91.5%, beating MVAD (90.2%) and PatchCore (89.4%), while matching MVAD at 92.3% P-AUPRO. The largest absolute margin over any single baseline appears on VisA P-AP, where PGBL exceeds DeSTSeg (41.1) by 9.3 points, suggesting the multi-prototype boundary mechanism is particularly effective for fine-grained pixel-level discrimination on challenging industrial textures.

## Relation to Prior Work

PGBL departs from the dominant single-prototype embedding paradigm (CFA, PatchSVDD, NoCoAD) that encloses all normal features in one hypersphere, arguing that intra-class variance forces an overly loose boundary. It also extends the feature-space anomaly synthesis line initiated by SimpleNet (fixed-scale Gaussian noise) and refined by progressive boundary-guided synthesis [3] and gradient-ascent synthesis [4], by making synthesis explicitly multi-prototype-aware: boundary points are identified per-cluster via k-NN sparsity, and candidates are filtered against all prototypes rather than a single center. The discriminator-based boundary refinement draws on the GAN-inspired calibration in [3], but is coupled to the multi-prototype structure so that the decision surface is shaped around multiple compact sub-clusters rather than one global manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical tension in industrial UAD—capturing diverse normal states without sacrificing sensitivity to subtle defects—and shows that structured multi-prototype representation plus targeted boundary synthesis yields consistent gains across MVTec-AD, VisA, and Real-IAD. However, the method remains confined to the single-class setting, relies on a frozen WideResNet50 backbone, and the k-NN boundary identification adds non-trivial training overhead (21 ms vs 7.8 ms for SimpleNet). Open questions include whether the prototype count M and boundary ratio P can be adapted per-category or learned end-to-end, whether the approach extends to multi-class or few-shot regimes, and whether online-updatable prototypes could handle distribution drift in long-running production deployments.
