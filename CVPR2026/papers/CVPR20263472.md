## Problem

Multimodal Large Language Models show promise for general anomaly detection (GAD), but their AD capability is severely limited by two gaps: (1) MLLMs are pretrained on web-sourced data that lacks industrial AD scenarios and AD-specific image-text pairs, and (2) existing AD datasets are image-only and unsuitable for MLLM post-training. The field lacks a large-scale, reasoning-based multimodal AD dataset to both train and evaluate MLLM-based general AD models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263472/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a spatially-aligned (reference, input) image pair where the reference is a normal sample of the same product, along with a structured text instruction that optionally includes category-specific domain knowledge listing known anomaly types. The output follows a strict think-then-answer format: a  block containing step-by-step visual comparison reasoning, followed by an <answer></answer> block with a Yes/No verdict and, if anomalous, a list of objects each containing 'bbox_2d': [x1,y1,x2,y2] and 'label': '<anomaly type>'. Localization is evaluated at IoU ≥ 0.1, and the precise coordinate format (rather than coarse spatial words like 'top-left') is a key distinction from prior multimodal AD datasets.

## Method

The paper contributes two main components. First, MMR-AD is constructed by sampling from 14 public AD datasets (127K images, 188 product categories, 395 anomaly types), manually verifying and annotating bounding boxes, then auto-generating detailed Chain-of-Thought reasoning texts via Qwen2.5-VL-72B prompted with red-bbox visual hints and coordinate/text hints, with a verification step that discards inconsistent outputs. Second, Anomaly-R1 fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-7B via LoRA in two stages: (a) SFT cold-start on MMR-AD CoT data to teach format-following and basic AD reasoning, then (b) GRPO reinforcement learning with a composite reward combining a binary result reward (correct Yes/No) and a consistency penalty (−0.2 per undetected ground-truth bbox with IoU > 0.5), augmented by contrastive sampling that injects guaranteed-correct or deliberately-wrong responses to prevent zero-advantage groups, and by injecting per-category anomaly type lists as domain knowledge in the prompt.

## Results

On MVTecAD, Anomaly-R1-7B achieves 88.8% detection accuracy and 66.3% localization accuracy (at IoU≥0.1), a dramatic improvement over its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B (75.0%/8.9%). The best commercial MLLM, GPT-5, reaches only 78.7%/41.8% on the same benchmark, while Gemini-2.5-pro scores 79.4%/34.4%. Full-shot specialist models still lead: PatchCore achieves 93.8%/73.2% and HGAD reaches 96.2%/73.2% on MVTecAD, confirming a remaining gap for general AD. Across all four evaluation datasets, precise anomaly localization remains the weakest capability of all evaluated generalist MLLMs, with most scoring below 20% localization accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Anomaly-R1-7B decisively outperforms all generalist MLLMs on localization: on MVTecAD it reaches 66.3% vs. GPT-5's 41.8%, Gemini-2.5-pro's 34.4%, and Qwen2.5-VL-72B's 36.4%, while also surpassing them on detection accuracy (88.8% vs. 78.7% for GPT-5). Among open-source models, the gap is even starker—InternVL3.5-38B achieves only 7.1% localization on MVTecAD compared to Anomaly-R1-7B's 66.3%. AnomalyGPT-7B, the closest prior MLLM-based AD method, reports 85.7% detection accuracy on MVTecAD but provides no localization metrics, and Anomaly-R1-7B surpasses it on detection (88.8%) while adding precise bounding-box localization. However, full-shot specialist models (PatchCore, HGAD, INP-Former) still outperform Anomaly-R1-7B on both detection and localization across all four datasets (e.g., PatchCore: 93.8%/73.2% vs. 88.8%/66.3% on MVTecAD), confirming that zero-shot general AD has not yet closed the gap with class-specific training. The domain-knowledge variant (Anomaly-R1-7B†) narrows this gap further, reaching 91.0%/67.7% on MVTecAD and 80.2%/48.4% on MVTec3D, approaching full-shot levels on detection while remaining competitive on localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of industrial anomaly detection (IAD) and multimodal large language models (MLLMs). Prior MLLM-based AD work (AnomalyGPT, Myriad, FabGPT) relied on simple answer-only text during training, while earlier multimodal AD datasets (MMAD, Anomaly-Instruct-125K) provided only coarse-grained descriptions or multiple-choice formats without reasoning chains. MMR-AD departs from both by constructing a large-scale dataset with detailed chain-of-thought reasoning texts, precise bounding-box annotations, and a reference-image comparison paradigm, then training a baseline (Anomaly-R1) via SFT cold-start followed by GRPO-based reinforcement learning—a training recipe not previously applied to AD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset (127K samples, 188 product categories, 395 anomaly types) is the largest multimodal reasoning-based IAD resource to date and is designed to be improvable as stronger MLLMs emerge, making it a likely standard benchmark for the field. The empirical finding that even frontier models like GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-pro achieve localization accuracy below 50% (often under 10% at low IoU) quantifies a substantial gap between general-purpose MLLMs and industrial AD requirements. A key open question is whether the remaining performance gap to full-shot AD models (e.g., PatchCore) can be closed through further scaling of multimodal AD data and reasoning, or whether architectural changes beyond prompt-based MLLMs are needed.
