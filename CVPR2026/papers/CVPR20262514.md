## Problem

State-of-the-art diffusion-based portrait animation methods, such as X-NeMo, encode head pose and facial expression into a single entangled latent code, making it impossible to independently control or edit one without affecting the other. This entanglement prevents practical applications like expression-only or pose-only editing and limits precise control over head rotation, translation, and scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262514/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 source portrait image and a driving video sequence, from which it extracts separate pose (RTS transformation) and expression (512-dim latent) signals. Because pose and expression are fully disentangled, the driving pose and expression can be sourced from two different videos, enabling mixed-signal animation, expression-only editing (keeping source pose), or pose-only editing (keeping source expression). The output is a temporally coherent animated portrait video sequence at 512×512 resolution.

## Method

DeX-Portrait uses a two-stage pipeline. First, a GAN-based motion trainer learns three encoders—a 3D appearance encoder, an explicit pose encoder (ConvNeXt outputting a 3×4 RTS matrix), and a latent expression encoder (FAN producing a 512-dim code)—with augmentation strategies (eye/mouth masking for pose input, random rotation and central cropping for expression input) to enforce disentanglement. Second, a latent diffusion model (LDM) with a reference UNet receives pose through a dual-branch mechanism: a Plücker-style ray map concatenated with the noisy latent, plus 3D-warped source features element-wise added to the denoising UNet, while the expression latent is injected via cross-attention. A progressive hybrid classifier-free guidance scheme delays the expression condition for the first 5 of 35 DDIM steps and ramps it in over the next 5, improving identity consistency during large pose changes.

## Results

On self-reenactment, DeX-Portrait achieves the best PSNR (28.59 dB) and LPIPS (0.088), with SSIM (0.862) marginally below Wan-Animate (0.865). On cross-reenactment, it attains the highest identity similarity (CSIM 0.623), lowest expression distance (AED 0.0515), and lowest pose distance (APD 0.145), outperforming all diffusion-based baselines. On disentangled-reenactment, DeX-Portrait is the only method achieving CSIM 0.631, AED 0.0546, and APD 0.100, substantially surpassing the best GAN-based baseline LivePortrait (CSIM 0.458, AED 0.0695, APD 0.195).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeX-Portrait outperforms all six baselines on cross-reenactment identity (CSIM 0.623 vs. 0.551 for Wan-Animate, 0.492 for X-NeMo) and pose accuracy (APD 0.145 vs. 0.180 for Wan-Animate, 0.551 for X-NeMo), with the gap over X-NeMo being particularly large on pose. On self-reenactment, it leads in PSNR (28.59 vs. 27.97 for Wan-Animate) and LPIPS (0.088 vs. 0.098), while trailing Wan-Animate by only 0.003 in SSIM. Among the three methods supporting disentangled reenactment (LivePortrait, EMO Portraits, HelloMeme), DeX-Portrait dominates on all three metrics: CSIM 0.631 vs. 0.458 (LivePortrait), AED 0.0546 vs. 0.0695, and APD 0.100 vs. 0.195. GAN-based methods (LivePortrait, EMO Portraits) suffer from blurriness and motion distortion, while diffusion-based methods without disentangled controls (HunyuanPortrait, X-NeMo, Wan-Animate) cannot perform pose-only or expression-only editing at all. X-NeMo, the closest diffusion-based competitor, achieves comparable expression distance (AED 0.0518 vs. 0.0515) but fails on pose control (APD 0.551 vs. 0.145) due to its entangled latent representation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: GAN-based portrait animation (LivePortrait, EMO-Portraits) that achieves disentangled pose/expression control but limited fidelity, and diffusion-based portrait animation (X-NeMo, WanAnimate, HunyuanPortrait) that achieves high fidelity but entangles pose and expression in a single latent code. DeX-Portrait departs from both by using a hybrid representation—explicit RTS transformation for pose and a 512-dim latent code for expression—trained via a GAN-based motion trainer, then injected into a latent diffusion model through a novel dual-branch mechanism (ray map + 3D reference warping) and cross-attention. The reference UNet architecture follows Animate Anyone, and the progressive hybrid CFG draws inspiration from FLOAT's staged conditioning, but the specific combination of explicit pose injection via ray maps and warped reference features is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work is the first diffusion-based portrait animation method to achieve truly disentangled pose-only and expression-only editing with precise control over rotation, translation, and scale, filling a practical gap that limits applications like targeted expression transfer or pose retargeting. The large APD improvement over X-NeMo (0.145 vs 0.551) demonstrates that explicit pose representations are substantially more controllable than entangled latents for geometric precision. However, the method is limited to single real-human portraits and struggles with occlusions and non-photorealistic styles, leaving open questions about generalization to cartoons, multi-person scenes, and whether the GAN-based motion trainer can be replaced by a purely diffusion-based alternative.
