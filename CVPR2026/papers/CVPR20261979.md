## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting depends on SfM preprocessing for camera poses and 3D initialization, which degrades severely under sparse-view conditions. Recent SfM-free methods (e.g., InstantSplat, CF-3DGS) bypass COLMAP using pretrained image-matching models but treat both primitive initialization and iterative position updates deterministically, ignoring the probabilistic uncertainty that accumulates from noisy feed-forward predictions and insufficient image constraints. This unmodeled uncertainty leads to redundant, floating primitives and unstable geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261979/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3–18 spatially distributed RGB views (downsampled resolution) as input, with no COLMAP or other SfM preprocessing. Initialization is provided by a pretrained feed-forward model (MASt3R or VGGT) that predicts per-pixel 3D points and camera poses. The output is a pruned set of 3D Gaussian primitives (position, color, opacity, scale, rotation) suitable for real-time novel view synthesis; the Bayesian filtering step actively removes noisy and redundant primitives, reducing the total primitive count and rendering time relative to baselines.

## Method

BA-GS introduces a two-level Bayesian framework. At the global initialization level, a Variational Bayesian Gaussian Mixture Model (VB-GMM) clusters primitives in a 2D density–gradient feature space (density from a KD-Tree over the MASt3R point cloud; gradient from multi-view fused color and depth gradients), enabling region-aware pruning and re-sampling before training. At the local refinement level, an Adaptive Kalman Filter treats each primitive's per-iteration position update as a noisy observation of a latent state, recursively fusing the predicted position with the gradient-based observation while scaling the observation noise covariance R_t inversely with local gradient magnitude (high-gradient regions receive smaller R_t, i.e., higher trust). A Prior-Guided Adaptive Density Control module replaces the fixed global densification threshold with a per-primitive threshold conditioned on the same gradient and density priors, allowing the system to adaptively balance detail preservation in textured regions against coverage in flat regions.

## Results

On Tanks and Temples (12 views), MASt3R+BA-GS achieves 31.61 PSNR, 0.9367 SSIM, and 0.0673 LPIPS, outperforming InstantSplat (30.09 PSNR, 0.9220 SSIM, 0.0844 LPIPS) by +3.15% on average. On LLFF (12 views), MASt3R+BA-GS reaches 23.32 PSNR and 0.1887 LPIPS, while VGGT+BA-GS shows the most dramatic gain over VGGT+InstantSplat with +18.24% PSNR and +47.91% LPIPS improvement, demonstrating strong robustness to noisy feed-forward priors. On MVImgNet (12 views), BA-GS achieves 23.81 PSNR and 0.2157 LPIPS versus InstantSplat's 23.38 PSNR and 0.2471 LPIPS (+9.89% LPIPS improvement).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BA-GS consistently outperforms InstantSplat [8] across all three datasets: on Tanks and Temples it gains +3.15% average (PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS), on MVImgNet +2.23% PSNR and +9.89% LPIPS, and on LLFF +3.54% PSNR and +12.30% LPIPS. Against MASt3R+DropGaussian [25], BA-GS wins on PSNR and LPIPS in most settings (e.g., 31.61 vs 30.19 PSNR on T&T 12-view) but trails slightly on SSIM (0.9367 vs 0.9180). Compared to MASt3R+FS-GS [41], BA-GS dominates across all metrics and view counts (e.g., 31.61 vs 28.04 PSNR on T&T 12-view). Against NeRFmm [33], BA-GS achieves substantially higher quality (31.61 vs 19.97 PSNR on T&T 12-view) with faster rendering. The most striking comparison is VGGT+BA-GS vs VGGT+InstantSplat on LLFF, where BA-GS gains +18.24% PSNR and +47.91% LPIPS, showing that the Bayesian filtering is critical when the initialization prior is noisy and globally misaligned.

## Relation to Prior Work

BA-GS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting and the emerging line of SfM-free initialization methods (InstantSplat, CF-3DGS) that leverage pretrained image-matching networks like DUSt3R and MASt3R to generate Gaussian primitives without COLMAP. Its key departure is treating both the initialization noise from these feed-forward models and the iterative gradient updates during training as probabilistic observations rather than deterministic signals, introducing a two-level Bayesian framework: a Variational Bayesian GMM for global primitive distribution modeling and an Adaptive Kalman Filter for per-primitive position denoising. This connects conceptually to Bayesian NeRF work (Bayesian NeRF, Bayes' Rays, KfD-NeRF) but applies uncertainty-aware inference to the explicit, per-primitive structure of 3DGS rather than implicit radiance fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in SfM-free 3DGS: pretrained initialization models produce redundant and noisy primitives under sparse views, and deterministic gradient descent amplifies this noise during training. The two-level Bayesian formulation offers a principled alternative to heuristic loss weighting and fixed-threshold densification, and the VGGT+BA-GS results (up to ~48% LPIPS improvement over VGGT+InstantSplat on LLFF) demonstrate meaningful robustness gains when the feed-forward prior is particularly noisy. Limitations include the Gaussian noise assumption, reliance on density/gradient priors that may fail in textureless or heavily occluded regions, and additional training-time computation from the adaptive filtering. Open questions include extending the Bayesian treatment to color and opacity estimation, and reducing the computational overhead via model compression.
