## Problem

Existing parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods for point clouds rely on static network parameters during inference, which limits their ability to adapt to the diverse local geometries, imbalanced category distributions, and heterogeneous spatial layouts inherent in scene-level point clouds. The paper identifies that inference-time parameter dynamism—generating input-aware weights at test time—is the missing mechanism needed to bridge the gap between pretrained foundation models and complex scene-level understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261844/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unordered scene-level point clouds (N points with C-dimensional features) and produces per-point semantic segmentation labels. A key processing detail is that the raw unordered points are first serialized via space-filling curves (Hilbert or Z-order) into spatially coherent local patches of up to n points each, with zero-padding for uniformity. The dynamic parameter projector operates on these grouped patches, generating patch-specific projection weights that are then applied before deserializing back to the original point ordering for the final segmentation head.

## Method

PointTPA introduces two lightweight modules—Serialization-based Neighborhood Grouping (SNG) and Dynamic Parameter Projector (DPP)—inserted into the last block of each PTv3 encoder stage via a mixed-insertion strategy, while static adapters handle the remaining blocks. SNG reorders unordered points using space-filling curves to form locally coherent patches, preserving geometric locality. DPP maintains a parameter base set of K learnable weight matrices; a lightweight MLP router computes softmax-normalized coefficients per patch, and the dynamic projection weight is a convex combination of the bases. During training the backbone is frozen and only the DPP and adapters are updated; during inference the DPP generates input-dependent weights for each patch, enabling the frozen backbone to adapt its behavior to scene-specific geometric variations. The entire adaptation introduces fewer than 2% of the backbone's parameters (~1.18M vs. 124.8M), yet achieves 78.4% mIoU on ScanNet, surpassing prior PEFT methods and approaching full fine-tuning.

## Results

PointTPA achieves 78.4% mIoU on ScanNet validation, 74.9% mIoU on S3DIS Area5, and 40.9% mIoU on ScanNet++ validation, using only 1.18M trainable parameters (1.09% of the PTv3 backbone). On ScanNet, this surpasses the prior SOTA PEFT method PointGST by 0.7% mIoU and DAPT by 0.7% mIoU, while operating approximately 4× faster in both training and inference. On S3DIS, PointTPA exceeds the full fine-tuning (FFT) reference (Sonata w/o dec.) by 0.4% mIoU, and on ScanNet++ it outperforms PointGST by 0.9% mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest point-cloud-specific PEFT baseline PointGST [27], PointTPA wins on ScanNet (+0.7% mIoU: 78.4 vs 77.7) and ScanNet++ (+0.9% mIoU: 40.9 vs 40.0) but trails by 0.1% mIoU on S3DIS (74.9 vs 75.0). Compared to DAPT [68], PointTPA leads on all three benchmarks: +0.7% mIoU on ScanNet, +0.3% on S3DIS, and +1.3% on ScanNet++. Against the FFT reference Sonata (w/o dec.) [49], PointTPA is within 0.5% mIoU on ScanNet (78.4 vs 78.9) but actually surpasses it on S3DIS (+0.4% mIoU) while using only 1.18M vs 108.5M trainable parameters. All general PEFT methods (Adapter, LoRA, VeRA, Prefix Tuning, BitFit, RandLoRA) are clearly outperformed, with the best general method (Adapter) trailing PointTPA by 1.6% mIoU on ScanNet. PointTPA also operates approximately 4× faster than PointGST in both training and inference per sample, making it the most efficient method among those achieving competitive accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointTPA extends the PEFT lineage for point cloud analysis (IDPT, DAPT, PointGST) by shifting the locus of adaptation from training-time static modules to inference-time dynamic parameter generation. It repurposes the space-filling curve serialization already present in PTv3's attention mechanism as a grouping primitive (SNG) and introduces a routing-over-bases mechanism (DPP) that is conceptually analogous to mixture-of-experts gating but applied to synthesize projection weights rather than select experts. The method is built on the Sonata pre-trained PTv3 backbone and positioned as a scene-level generalization of object-level PEFT, arguing that the geometric diversity and category imbalance of scenes demand input-conditioned weight dynamics that static adapters cannot provide.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that test-time parameter dynamism—generating input-specific projection weights at inference—can close most of the gap to full fine-tuning with under 1.2M trainable parameters and a 4× speedup over the prior SOTA PEFT method (PointGST), making it practically attractive for deployment on large-scale scene data. The ablation showing that an overabundance of parameter bases degrades performance (training instability) is a useful practical warning against simply scaling up the dynamic component. Open questions include whether the dynamic-parameter approach generalizes beyond the PTv3/Sonata backbone to other 3D architectures, and whether the scene-level framing (vs. object-level) is the true driver of the gains or whether the mechanism would also help on object-level benchmarks.
