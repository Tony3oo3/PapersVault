## Problem

Existing point cloud restoration methods rely on global bottleneck features that discard fine-grained geometry and degrade under varying input quality (noise, outliers, occlusion, sparsity). Moreover, completion, denoising, and deformation are typically handled by separate dedicated networks rather than a single unified model, leaving a gap for a robust, point-only backbone that adapts to diverse and combined degradation scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263105/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded point cloud (incomplete, noisy, with outliers and irregular density) as input with no fixed ordering, and produces a restored point cloud at progressively higher resolutions through a coarse-to-fine pipeline. Internally, the input is downsampled via farthest-point sampling into coarse-level point proxies that serve as source queries, while the final output is generated at multiple upsampled levels, each supervised independently. The model is point-only, requiring no auxiliary 2D images, meshes, or text prompts.

## Method

PQDT decomposes restoration into two cooperative transformer stages: an observation-guided Stage I that decodes pseudo-queries from encoded inputs using a DETR-like decoder initialized with spherical point priors, and a prior-guided Stage II that refines these pseudo-queries under stronger influence of learned shape priors. A key architectural component is the Geometric-Embedding (GE) self-attention, which encodes transformation-invariant spatial relationships via sparse kNN-limited distance and angular embeddings injected as additional keys and values, replacing raw coordinate-based positional encoding. A Dynamic Query Selection (DQS) module using Perturb-and-Top-k with annealed Gumbel noise adaptively filters the most representative candidate points at each stage, balancing input fidelity against learned priors. The final point proxies are expanded to full resolution through hierarchical upsample transformer layers, and the network is trained end-to-end with multi-level L1 Chamfer Distance losses plus a dedicated pseudo-query supervision term.

## Results

On the ShapeNet-55 completion benchmark, PQDT achieves an average CD_ℓ2 of 0.68 and F1-Score of 0.570 across all difficulty levels, outperforming the previous best method AnchorFormer (CD_ℓ2=0.76, F1=0.558) with a notable 0.13 improvement on the hardest setting (CD-H: 1.13 vs 1.26). On the newly introduced PFS dataset, PQDT reduces CD_ℓ2 by 76.8% compared to the second-best method (0.16 vs 0.69) while improving F1 by 27.1% and F0.5 by 40.1%. On ShapeNetCar-Occ, PQDT attains the best CD_ℓ2 of 0.82 and F1 of 0.261, and on ShapeNet-Deform it achieves CD_ℓ2=1.01 and F1=0.290, both leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PQDT outperforms all ten baselines on every metric across all four datasets. The closest competitor on ShapeNet-55 is AnchorFormer [4], which PQDT surpasses by 0.08 in CD_ℓ2 (0.68 vs 0.76) and 0.012 in F1 (0.570 vs 0.558), with the largest gap on the hardest setting (CD-H: 1.13 vs 1.26). Among transformer-based methods, AdaPoinTr [62] and T-CorresNet [8] are the strongest baselines (CD_ℓ2 ≈ 0.80–0.81), still trailing PQDT by ~0.12. On the deformation benchmark (ShapeNet-Deform), PQDT leads AdaPoinTr by 0.02 in CD_ℓ2 (1.01 vs 1.03) and by 0.002 in F1. On ShapeNetCar-Occ, the margin narrows to 0.03 CD_ℓ2 (0.82 vs 0.85) over AdaPoinTr, but PQDT still wins on F1 (0.261 vs 0.251). The most dramatic advantage appears on the PFS dataset, where PQDT's CD_ℓ2 of 0.16 is 76.8% lower than the runner-up AdaPoinTr (0.69), and its F0.5-Score (0.311) exceeds the next best by 40.1%, highlighting the benefit of the pseudo-query mechanism for local geometric fidelity in industrial freeform-surface restoration.

## Relation to Prior Work

PQDT extends the sequence-to-sequence point proxy paradigm introduced by PoinTr [61] and the hierarchical upsample transformer of SeedFormer [69], while using DGCNN's EdgeConv [47] for multi-scale feature extraction. The key departure is the introduction of pseudo-queries as auxiliary intermediate entities in a dual-stage transformer: Stage I generates observation-guided queries from distorted inputs, and Stage II refines them under stronger influence of learned shape priors, with a Dynamic Query Selection module filtering non-representative tokens at each stage. This contrasts with prior anchor/seed-based methods (AnchorFormer, PoinTr) that decode directly from a single global or seed feature, and with global-bottleneck approaches (PCN, FoldingNet) that lose fine-grained geometry through pooling. The geometric-embedding self-attention, combining distance and angular encodings in a sparse kNN formulation, replaces the coordinate-based positional encodings used in most prior point transformer works.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified treatment of completion, denoising, and deformation in a single point-only backbone addresses a practical gap: real-world 3D observations are rarely degraded by a single artifact type, yet prior methods handle each task separately or only pair completion with noise (AdaPoinTr) or upsampling (SuperPC). The three new datasets—particularly PFS, derived from automotive Body-in-White components—provide the first standardized benchmark for local freeform-surface restoration with guidance, a setting relevant to industrial reverse engineering. Limitations include the small PFS test set (80 samples), the exclusion of multi-modal (2D image) baselines, and evaluation confined to ShapeNet-scale geometry; whether the pseudo-query mechanism scales to large-scale outdoor scenes (e.g., autonomous driving point clouds with millions of points) remains open.
