## Problem

Learning-based edge detectors trained with standard cross-entropy loss produce thick, ambiguous edge regions that deviate from the crisp, single-pixel contours provided by human annotators. Prior work has addressed this by designing specialized sparsity losses, refining supervision labels, or recasting edge detection as a generative task, yet crispness on multi-annotator benchmarks like BSDS and Multicue often remains below 50%. The paper argues that a carefully designed training and inference strategy alone—without architectural changes or auxiliary losses—is sufficient to achieve human-like edge quality.

## Input / Output

MEMO takes an RGB image (256×256 or 384×384 during training, full resolution at evaluation) and produces a binary edge map of the same spatial dimensions. During iterative inference, the model also consumes a partially-revealed masked edge map and a scalar mask ratio at each step, progressively finalizing pixels. A user-defined granularity scale s (typically 1.0–2.0) controls edge density at inference time, enabling multi-granularity output without retraining. The final edge map requires no post-processing such as NMS or edge thinning.

## Method

MEMO consists of a frozen DINOv2-b image encoder, a masked edge encoder, and a shared decoder, trained with only binary cross-entropy loss on randomly masked ground-truth edges at varying ratios. The key training insight is that the model learns to treat already-finalized (unmasked) pixels as fixed context and suppress redundant activations in their neighborhoods, yielding sharper localization over iterations. At inference, a LocMax strategy finalizes a pixel only if its confidence is the maximum within its 3×3 neighborhood, preventing spatially correlated high-confidence pixels from being finalized simultaneously and producing thick contours. Multi-granularity prediction is achieved by adapting classifier-free guidance: the model is also trained in an unconditioned setting (zero image input), and at inference the conditioned and unconditioned predictions are extrapolated with a scale parameter s. Pre-training on 400K synthetic edge pairs (generated via SAM segmentation of LAION images) followed by LoRA-based fine-tuning (1.2% additional parameters) improves generalization while keeping per-task costs minimal.

## Results

On BSDS, MEMO achieves a CEval ODS of 0.836 and OIS of 0.841 (at s=1.4), substantially outperforming the previous best DiffEdge (0.749/0.754) by +0.087/+0.087. Under standard SEval, MEMO reaches ODS 0.854 and OIS 0.861, also setting a new state of the art. For crispness, MEMO attains an Average Crispness (AC) of 0.663 (C*) and 0.705 (AC*), far exceeding DiffEdge's 0.476 and all other baselines. On BIPED and Multicue, MEMO similarly leads on CEval (0.883 and 0.849 ODS respectively) while remaining competitive on SEval.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEMO decisively outperforms all 19 baselines on the crispness-aware evaluation (CEval) across all three datasets. On BSDS, MEMO's CEval ODS of 0.836 exceeds the prior best DiffEdge (0.749) by +0.087 and SAUGE (0.687) by +0.149; the gap is even larger on AC, where MEMO scores 0.663 versus DiffEdge's 0.476 and SAUGE's 0.197. On BIPED, MEMO (CEval ODS 0.883) edges out DiffEdge (0.856) by +0.027, though DiffEdge retains a slight advantage on SEval ODS (0.899 vs. 0.888) and AC (0.849 vs. 0.841). On Multicue, MEMO leads DiffEdge on CEval ODS by +0.065 (0.849 vs. 0.784) and on AC by +0.164 (0.626 vs. 0.462), while DiffEdge holds a marginal SEval ODS edge (0.904 vs. 0.894). Among multi-granularity methods, MEMO dominates Table 4 on BSDS, achieving CEval ODS 0.855 (M=11) versus MuGE's 0.745 and SAUGE's 0.706, confirming that its inference-time granularity control via classifier-free guidance requires no additional paired annotations. The consistent pattern is that MEMO's masked training and LocMax inference produce natively crisp edges, so it does not rely on post-processing (NMS/thinning) to inflate SEval scores, unlike methods such as TopK-unmasking variants that degrade sharply under CEval.

## Relation to Prior Work

MEMO departs from the dominant paradigm of achieving crisp edges through specialized loss functions (sparsity-promoting losses, refined labels) or architectural changes (diffusion backbones in DiffEdge), arguing instead that a masked-prediction training scheme combined with a confidence-ordered inference strategy is sufficient. Conceptually, it repurposes two ideas from other domains: BERT-style masked prediction applied to edge maps, and classifier-free guidance from diffusion models [11] repurposed as an inference-time granularity knob for multi-granularity edge detection. The practical pipeline also draws on LoRA [13] for parameter-efficient fine-tuning and SAM [15] for generating a large-scale synthetic pre-training dataset, positioning MEMO at the intersection of these recent techniques rather than introducing a fundamentally new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that training and inference strategy alone can match or exceed diffusion-based and loss-engineered methods for crispness—challenges the field's assumption that architectural or supervision complexity is necessary for human-like edge quality. The LocMax unmasking strategy and masked edge training are simple, modular techniques that could transfer to other dense prediction tasks where single-pixel localization matters. Limitations include the iterative inference cost (10 steps ≈ 1.3 s on a 3090), evaluation on relatively small benchmarks (BSDS, BIPED, Multicue), and the observation that SEval scores slightly degrade with more steps due to annotation incompleteness rather than model error. Open questions include whether the confidence-ordered inference can be parallelized or distilled into a single-pass model, and whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse datasets.
