## Problem

MLLMs exhibit severe confidence miscalibration: their self-reported confidence remains nearly flat even as visual perception degrades sharply (e.g., under progressive noise), meaning models do not know when they do not know. Prior calibration work targets token-level uncertainty in text-only LLMs, but visual perception in MLLMs is holistic across the entire response, creating a granularity mismatch that existing methods fail to address.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text question as input. During training, each sample is augmented into an original-noise image pair, where noise is applied selectively to key visual regions identified by CLIP attention maps. The model outputs a chain-of-thought reasoning trajectory, a final answer, and a calibrated confidence score computed as the arithmetic mean of top-k negative log-probabilities across all generated tokens. At test time, the CA-TTS framework aggregates outputs from three decoupled modules into a single final answer via a shared weighted voting dictionary.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, CDRL trains the model via GRPO using original-noise image pairs with a novel Confidence-based Calibration Reward that combines a Perception Term (α·tanh(β·ΔC), rewarding large confidence drops between original and noised outputs) and a Calibration Term ((2·R_Output−1)·C_norm, rewarding high confidence when correct and penalizing it when wrong), alongside standard accuracy and formatting rewards. Second, CA-TTS performs adaptive test-time scaling by coordinating three decoupled modules—Self-Consistency (confidence-weighted voting augmented by an Expert Voter), Self-Reflection (Expert Critic generates a critique that the base model uses to revise its answer), and Self-Check (Visual Contrastive Decoding between original and noised images)—all scheduled by an Expert Model acting as Planner. The key insight is that CDRL's calibrated confidence serves as a free lunch for test-time scaling, enabling the model to route computational effort toward uncertain predictions and achieving a 2.2–3.1× steeper scaling slope than baselines.

## Results

On four visual reasoning benchmarks, the proposed CDRL + CA-TTS framework (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves ALL scores of 79.5% on Math-Vista, 42.4% on Math-Vision, 71.3% on MMStar, and 66.3% on MMMU, setting new state-of-the-art on all four. The paper reports a consistent 8.8% average gain over the strongest competing method. Ablation on Math-Vision shows CDRL alone lifts ALL from 23.0% to 26.4%, CA-TTS alone from 23.0% to 38.0%, and the combination reaches 42.4%, confirming synergistic contribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines on every benchmark. Against training-free baselines, it surpasses Majority Voting by +9.7 (Math-Vista), +12.3 (Math-Vision), +2.3 (MMStar), and +8.8 (MMMU), and beats Deepconf by +8.8, +12.8, +10.2, and +10.2 respectively. Among training-based competitors, the closest rival is VL-Rethinker (74.1 on Math-Vista), which the method exceeds by +5.4; on Math-Vision the margin is largest at +11.7 (42.4 vs. 30.7). DreamPRM (InternVL-2.5-8B) leads on MMMU among baselines at 61.4 but is still surpassed by +4.9. R1-Onevision and We-Think trail the proposed method by 15.4 and 13.0 points on Math-Vision respectively, indicating that confidence-calibrated RL plus adaptive test-time scaling yields substantially larger gains than prior RL-based visual reasoning approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: MLLM visual perception (building on CLIP, LLaVA, Qwen-VL), LLM confidence calibration (uncertainty and logit-based methods), and test-time scaling (Self-Consistency, Self-Refine, ToT, DeepConf). Its key conceptual departure is arguing that token-level LLM calibration fundamentally misaligns with MLLM visual perception due to a 'granularity mismatch'—visual understanding is holistic across the response, not per-token—motivating a response-level confidence metric and a novel confidence-based calibration reward within GRPO. The test-time scaling framework repurposes Visual Contrastive Decoding and confidence-weighted voting but adds an adaptive multi-module architecture scheduled by an external expert model, distinguishing it from fixed-pipeline TTS methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'perceptual bluntness' finding—that MLLM confidence remains nearly flat while accuracy collapses under visual noise—highlights a blind spot in current MLLM training that goes beyond hallucination mitigation. The practical limitation is the heavy dependency on a powerful external expert model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) for planning, voting, and critique, which undermines the 'free lunch' narrative and raises cost concerns for deployment. Open questions include whether the CLIP-attention-based noise generation captures the right perturbation space, whether the approach transfers to larger or differently-architected MLLMs, and whether the expert model can be replaced by the base model itself without significant performance loss (Table 3 suggests partial success but with a notable gap).
