## Problem

Existing longitudinal radiology report generation (LRRG) methods conflate static pathology recognition and temporal progression modeling within a single representation space, causing feature entanglement that obscures disease-specific semantics and limits temporal reasoning. Additionally, they treat report generation as a single-pass procedure, ignoring the mutual dependencies between prior and current reports that could enable error correction across visits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260510/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a pair of chest X-ray images (prior and current) from the same patient along with the ground-truth prior radiology report, which serves as a textual reference for temporal reasoning. The output is a free-text current radiology report describing both static findings and disease progression relative to the prior study. At test time, the prior report is available as input, but the current report is generated entirely by the model through iterative refinement.

## Method

TIM is a two-stage framework. Stage I performs temporal-decoupled representation learning with two independent branches: a static pathological recognition branch (Swin Transformer encoder contrastively aligned with CheXbert-annotated disease descriptions) and a dynamic progression modeling branch (X-CLIP video encoder processing prior image, two learnable perception frames, and current image, contrastively aligned with progression text). A dual-timepoint generation mechanism uses a frozen LLaMA2-7B to produce both prior and current reports simultaneously, encouraging bidirectional temporal reasoning. Stage II introduces mutual report refinement: errors in the generated prior report (identified via CheXbert label comparison against ground truth) are encoded as error triplets, combined with a semantic aggregator of the current report, and fed to the LLM for corrective regeneration. At inference, Stage I and Stage II alternate in a closed loop for three iterations, progressively suppressing diagnostic errors and improving temporal coherence.

## Results

On the Longitudinal-MIMIC test set (2,058 samples), TIM (Stage II) achieves state-of-the-art performance across all NLG and CE metrics: BLEU-1 of 0.430, BLEU-4 of 0.124, ROUGE-L of 0.287, METEOR of 0.185, and F1-score of 0.511. Stage I alone already surpasses all prior longitudinal baselines (e.g., BLEU-1 0.421, F1 0.483), and Stage II's mutual refinement further lifts F1 by +0.028 and ROUGE-L by +0.012. The model is evaluated using CheXbert-mapped 14-disease labels for clinical efficacy and standard NLG metrics for linguistic quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIM outperforms all 14 baselines across every reported metric. Against the strongest single-image method RADAR [9], TIM gains +0.018 on BLEU-4 (0.124 vs. 0.114), +0.030 on ROUGE-L (0.287 vs. 0.257), and +0.051 on F1-score (0.511 vs. 0.417). Against the strongest longitudinal baseline Diff-RRG [44], TIM improves BLEU-1 by +0.025 (0.430 vs. 0.405), ROUGE-L by +0.011 (0.287 vs. 0.276), and F1 by +0.037 (0.511 vs. 0.474). Among longitudinal methods, HERGen [35] achieves the highest ROUGE-L (0.282) but has the lowest F1 (0.295) due to poor recall (0.289), while HC-LLM [22] and LLM-RG4 [38] trail TIM by 0.026 and 0.075 in F1 respectively. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the temporal-decoupled design (DPM + DRG) contributes most of the gain over the single-image baseline, with the contrastive alignment losses (L_spr, L_dpm) adding further F1 improvements of +0.014 and +0.005 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

TIM carves new ground at the intersection of longitudinal radiology report generation and self-refinement, departing from prior LRRG methods (Prefilling, HERGen, HC-LLM, Diff-RRG) that conflate static pathology and temporal progression within a single representation space. Conceptually, it repurposes video-encoder architectures (X-CLIP) with learnable perception frames for temporal modeling and applies contrastive alignment (CLIP-style) to both spatial and temporal branches, while the mutual refinement stage extends self-correction ideas from NLP into a bidirectional, cross-timepoint diagnostic feedback loop. The framework is built on standard components (Swin Transformer, LLaMA2-7B, CheXbert) but their two-stage decoupled composition is the novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The temporal-decoupling insight—treating spatial pathology recognition and progression modeling as fundamentally different subtasks—offers a clean conceptual framework that could generalize beyond chest X-rays to other longitudinal imaging modalities. The mutual refinement mechanism, which exploits the observation that ~70% of prior-report errors propagate to the current report, provides a practical error-correction strategy for clinical deployment where report consistency across visits is critical. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (Longitudinal-MIMIC) and a fixed two-timepoint setting; open questions include scalability to longer patient histories, other body regions, and whether the iterative refinement loop introduces unacceptable latency in real clinical workflows.
