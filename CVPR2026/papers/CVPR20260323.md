## Problem

Training-free non-rigid image editing with diffusion models suffers from 'attention collapse': existing attention-sharing mechanisms either let positional embeddings dominate (producing source-duplicate images) or let semantic features dominate (producing prompt-driven images that lose source fidelity). No prior method dynamically balances these two signals across denoising steps, making faithful complex edits like pose or layout changes unreliable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260323/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image, a source text prompt describing it, and a target text prompt specifying the desired edit. Both source and target branches are initialized with the same random noise and denoised jointly through FLUX.1-dev (50 steps, guidance scale 3.5), with attention sharing applied only in position-insensitive blocks. The output is a single edited image that follows the target prompt while preserving the source image's appearance and identity.

## Method

SynPS introduces a stepwise editing measurement M_t, defined as the ratio of cosine similarity between source/target image tokens to that between source/target text tokens, averaged over all transformer blocks at each denoising step. This measurement drives a scaling weight w ∈ [0,1] applied to RoPE position IDs in the attention-sharing queries and keys: when M_t exceeds a threshold (under-editing), w=0 discards positional encoding to allow semantic change; when M_t falls below a threshold (over-editing), w=1 enforces full positional guidance to prevent drift; in between, w is linearly interpolated. The modulation is implemented by scaling the 2D position indices fed into RoPE, which proportionally scales the rotation angles and thus the effective relative distance between tokens. Attention sharing is restricted to position-insensitive blocks identified by FreeFlux, and the entire pipeline is training-free, requiring no fine-tuning or additional parameters beyond two scalar thresholds (M_min=0.9, M_max=1.0).

## Results

On PIE-Bench Change Pose, SynPS achieves the best scores across all three MLLM judges: GPT-4o 6.99, GPT-5 5.82, and Gemini-2.5-Pro 4.17, outperforming the strongest baseline CharaConsist (6.32 / 4.84 / 3.49) by 10.6%, 20.1%, and 19.3% respectively. On the curated Non-Rigid Editing Benchmark (200 prompt pairs), SynPS leads with GPT-4o 7.87, GPT-5 6.66, and Gemini-2.5-Pro 5.43, improving over FreeFlux by 6.2%, 11.5%, and 28.3% on the three MLLM scores. SynPS also attains the highest CLIP_txt (0.2683 / 0.2344) on both benchmarks, while its CLIP_img (0.9415 / 0.9051) is slightly lower than FreeFlux or FlowEdit, which the authors attribute to those methods' tendency to duplicate the source image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynPS outperforms all five baselines on every MLLM judge (GPT-4o, GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro) and CLIP_txt across both benchmarks. The largest margin is against FreeFlux on Gemini-2.5-Pro: 4.17 vs. 3.24 on PIE-Bench (+28.6%) and 5.43 vs. 4.23 on the Non-Rigid Benchmark (+28.3%), confirming that FreeFlux's fixed position-insensitive attention sharing suffers from under-editing. Against CharaConsist, the second-best method on PIE-Bench, SynPS gains 19.3% on Gemini-2.5-Pro and 20.1% on GPT-5, indicating that CharaConsist's semantic-correspondence-based position adjustment still produces over-editing artifacts. On CLIP_img, FreeFlux (0.958 / 0.918) and FlowEdit (0.973 / 0.957) score higher than SynPS (0.942 / 0.905), but the authors argue this reflects source-image duplication rather than genuine fidelity, as those methods fail to execute the intended semantic edit. StableFlow and RF-Solver-Edit trail significantly on MLLM scores (e.g., StableFlow Gemini 2.85 vs. SynPS 4.17 on PIE-Bench), confirming that general-purpose editing methods are ill-suited for complex non-rigid transformations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FreeFlux's identification of position-insensitive blocks in RoPE-based MM-DiT architectures for attention sharing, and on CharaConsist's semantic-correspondence-based position embedding adaptation. It departs from both by diagnosing a shared failure mode—attention collapse, where either positional or semantic signals dominate retrieval—and replacing their fixed strategies (static block selection or fixed correspondence mapping) with a dynamic, measurement-driven modulation of RoPE position IDs. The editing measurement, inspired by DeltaEdit's text-similarity computation, is repurposed as a per-step control signal rather than a diagnostic, which is the key conceptual shift from prior attention-sharing methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The attention collapse diagnosis provides a useful unified lens for understanding why existing training-free non-rigid editing methods fail, and the stepwise editing measurement offers a lightweight, interpretable diagnostic that practitioners can monitor during generation. However, the approach is tightly coupled to FLUX.1-dev's RoPE-based MM-DiT architecture and the specific position-insensitive block set from FreeFlux, leaving open whether the synergy principle generalizes to other diffusion backbones (e.g., SD3 with input-only positional embeddings) or to video editing. The method also assumes access to a pre-generated target image for correspondence, and the thresholds (M_min, M_max) are set empirically, raising questions about robustness across diverse editing types without manual tuning.
