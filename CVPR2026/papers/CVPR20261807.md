## Problem

GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning of LVLMs for object-level grounding assigns sparse, response-level rewards that provide no learning signal when all candidates in a group fail the success criterion (e.g., IoU > 0.5). This leaves hard cases—complex scenes with similar-looking instances or fine-grained spatial relations—permanently unlearned, since the policy receives zero gradient on those samples. The paper asks how to recover informative training signals from these previously unrewarded failures without requiring step-wise process supervision that is intractable for visual grounding.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (resized to 840×840) paired with a natural-language question and produces a structured response: chain-of-thought reasoning text followed by predicted bounding boxes (M×4) and point coordinates (M×2) inside each referenced object. The box-point pairs are then fed to SAM2 to generate segmentation masks, while the box count M directly yields the counting answer. During training, the input is consumed in two stages: a single initial response is sampled first, then a revision prompt template U(q, o⁽¹⁾) appends the initial output as context to form a dialogue-style follow-up query q⁽²⁾ that conditions the group of revised responses.

## Method

The method introduces a group-revision optimisation paradigm built on GRPO. Instead of sampling G independent responses to the original query, it first samples one initial response o⁽¹⁾, then generates G revised responses oᵢ⁽²⁾ conditioned on o⁽¹⁾ via a fixed revision prompt, turning a single failure into a collaborative revision group. A consolidation process computes a potential-based shaping signal Δφᵢ by taking the relative reduction in alignment cost (a Hungarian-matched average of 1−IoU, normalised L1 box distance, and normalised L1 point distance) between the initial response and each revised candidate, clipped at zero. This signal enters the training in two roles: it is added to the per-candidate reward with weight ω (Eq. 7) to refine the GRPO ranking, and it post-scales the z-score advantage as Âᵢ = (1+Δφᵢ)Aᵢ (Eq. 8) to amplify gradient magnitude for candidates that achieved larger improvements. The overall objective (Eq. 9) retains the standard GRPO clipped-surrogate loss with KL regularisation against a frozen reference policy, but operates on the revision group rather than the original response group, enabling the policy to learn from hard cases that would otherwise yield zero reward.

## Results

On reasoning segmentation (ReasonSeg test), the single-object setup achieves 61.11 gIoU, surpassing Seg-R1 by 4.41 points and Seg-Zero by 3.61 points. On referring segmentation, the single-object model reaches 74.26 cIoU on RefCOCOg testA, outperforming Seg-Zero (72.6) and VisionReasoner (68.3). For counting, the multi-object checkpoint attains 79.97% average accuracy across PixMoCount and CountBench, a 4.27-point gain over VisionReasoner (75.7%). On REC (Acc@0.5), the multi-object model achieves 85.80% average, the best among all compared methods, exceeding VisionReasoner by 1.0 point.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest RFT baseline VisionReasoner [51], the multi-object model wins on ReasonSeg test (66.73 vs 63.6 gIoU, +3.13) and counting average (79.97% vs 75.7%, +4.27), but trails on RefCOCO+ testB (59.34 vs 55.8 is actually a win; the paper notes VisionReasoner's extra boxes inflate its REC Acc@0.5). Against Seg-Zero [50] in the single-object setting, the method gains +4.39 on ReasonSeg val (66.99 vs 62.6) and +1.66 on RefCOCOg testA (74.26 vs 72.6). On REC (Table 2), the multi-object model achieves 85.80% average Acc@0.5, beating VisionReasoner (84.8%) by 1.0 point and Seg-Zero (81.3%) by 4.5 points; the single-object model also outperforms Seg-Zero by 2.08 points on average. On general VQA benchmarks (Table 3), both setups show small but consistent gains over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base (e.g., +0.55% on SeedBenchV2+, +1.25% average on MMMU-Pro), indicating that object-level RL training transfers to image-level understanding. The method does not beat SFT-based methods like SegLLM on RefCOCO testA (80.79 vs 81.5), but those SFT methods use substantially larger training sets and do not report counting or ReasonSeg results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the GRPO-based reinforcement fine-tuning paradigm for LVLM grounding (Seg-Zero, VisionReasoner, Seg-R1) by addressing its core failure mode: when all sampled responses in a group fail the reward criterion, no learning signal is generated. Rather than adopting Process Reward Models (which require step-wise alignment between CoT sentences and image objects—a non-trivial chicken-and-egg problem in visual grounding), the paper repurposes potential-based reward shaping (Ng et al., 1999) to quantify improvement from a revision step. The key conceptual departure is treating the initial failed response as a conditioning signal for a group of revised candidates, with a consolidation process that converts relative improvement into both a reward term and an advantage-scaling factor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The group-revision paradigm offers a practical, training-time-only mechanism to recover learning signals from hard cases without requiring dense step-wise supervision or additional annotation, making it broadly applicable to any GRPO-based LVLM grounding pipeline. However, the gains are modest (+2–4% over strong RFT baselines) and the method adds computational overhead (sampling an initial response plus a revision group per training step). Open questions include whether the approach generalises to tasks where revision is less informative (e.g., very easy samples where Δφ ≈ 0), whether it composes with other reward-shaping or curriculum strategies, and whether the consolidation signal remains well-calibrated as the policy improves and the initial response quality rises.
