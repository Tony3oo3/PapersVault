## Problem

Existing personalized text-to-image generation methods rely on multimodal LLMs to extract user preferences, but the resulting prompts or latent codes rarely reflect preferences faithfully due to representation-space mismatches, weak instruction-following on nuanced descriptions, and token dilution when concatenating conditional tokens in MM-DiT. This paper asks whether learnable embeddings trained directly via diffusion loss backpropagation can serve as more effective, fine-grained preference representations that avoid these bottlenecks.

## Input / Output

The method requires only user-provided preference images (no textual preference descriptions) and a standard text prompt as input. During training, each user's embedding is a 30×1024 tensor learned from 40–80 preference examples; for new users with limited history (as few as 2 images), the embedding is constructed as a linear combination of training-set user embeddings. The output is a single generated image from FLUX.1-dev that simultaneously aligns with the user's visual style and the semantic content of the prompt.

## Method

Premier represents each user's preference as a learnable embedding and introduces two cross-attention-based preference adapters (block-shared and block-distinct) that take text tokens as queries and the user embedding as keys/values to produce token-specific modulation directions, which are added to the AdaLN modulation vector in MM-DiT rather than concatenated as tokens—avoiding the token dilution problem. A dispersion loss (InfoNCE-based) is applied to the adapter outputs to enforce separation between different users' modulation directions, preventing the adapters from overfitting to text tokens and producing similar images across users. Training proceeds in two stages: first, adapters and training-set user embeddings are jointly optimized with flow matching plus dispersion losses; second, new-user embeddings are obtained by optimizing only the linear combination coefficients over frozen training-set embeddings, providing stable cold-start representations. The entire approach operates within the FLUX.1-dev flow-matching framework, with the modulation mechanism applied prior to image generation so that preference conditioning is decoupled from the denoising process.

## Results

On PrefBench, Premier achieves the best scores across all four evaluation metrics: ViPer Score 0.6889, ViPer Rate 0.876, CLIP T2I 0.3183, and LPIPS 0.5986 (lower is better). The closest competitor, InstantStyle, trails by 0.061 in ViPer Score and 0.10 in ViPer Rate. In a human A/B study with 40 experts, Premier is preferred over ViPer (86.6%), InstantStyle (78.4%), DrUM (66.7%), Bagel (64.1%), and Qwen-Image-Edit (63.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Premier outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest competitor InstantStyle (single reference image), Premier gains +0.061 ViPer Score, +0.10 ViPer Rate, +0.0195 CLIP T2I, and −0.0655 LPIPS. Against ViPer (which uses multi-dimensional preference extraction from preferred/non-preferred images and comments), Premier leads by +0.173 ViPer Score and +0.20 ViPer Rate, indicating that learnable embeddings with modulation capture preferences more faithfully than LLM-extracted textual descriptions. Against DrUM (condition-level modeling with historical text), Premier improves ViPer Score by +0.210 and LPIPS by −0.0555. Against Bagel and Qwen-Image-Edit (which use only 1–3 reference images), Premier's advantage is even larger in ViPer Score (+0.181 and +0.220 respectively), reflecting the benefit of leveraging 8 historical preference images through a dedicated learnable embedding rather than in-context conditioning. In the human study, Premier is preferred over every baseline by a wide margin (63–87%), confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

Premier builds on the MM-DiT modulation mechanism (Peebles & Xie; TokenVerse; XVerse), which injects conditioning via additive offsets to AdaLN modulation vectors, but repurposes it for user-preference conditioning rather than reference-image or multi-subject control. It departs from the dominant paradigm of extracting preferences through multimodal LLMs (ViPer, DrUM, PMG) by instead learning user embeddings end-to-end via diffusion loss backpropagation, and from token-concatenation approaches (OmniGen, OminiControl) by operating at the modulation level to avoid token dilution. The dispersion loss is adapted from Wang & He's representation-regularization framework, and the cold-start linear-combination strategy draws on the intuition that well-trained embeddings form a useful basis for interpolating new users.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, training-free-at-inference pipeline for personalized generation that does not require users to articulate preferences in text, addressing a real usability gap. The cold-start linear-combination strategy is particularly relevant for deployment where new users provide only a handful of images. Limitations include evaluation on a single base model (FLUX.1-dev) and a single dataset (PrefBench, 1,000 training users), and reliance on the ViPer proxy metric which may not fully capture human perceptual nuance. Open questions include whether the modulation-based preference injection generalizes to other DiT architectures, how preference embeddings behave under long-term preference drift, and whether the approach scales to millions of users without per-user storage overhead.
