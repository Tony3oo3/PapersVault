## Problem

While recent zero-shot stereo matching methods leverage monocular depth foundation models for feature extraction and cost volume construction, the iterative refinement stage remains underexplored. Conventional GRU-based recurrent units are independent of vision foundation models, constrain hidden states to narrow ranges, and distort state information through direct convolutions, limiting their representation capacity and scalability for zero-shot generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262947/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a rectified stereo image pair (left and right, 3×H×W) and produces a dense pixel-wise disparity map at full resolution. The left image is processed by a frozen Depth Anything V2 to extract relative depth and monocular depth features, while both images pass through a stereo feature encoder (DINOv2-based) for multi-level features. At inference, 32 iterative refinement steps are applied, with the final disparity regressed from the highest-resolution hidden state.

## Method

PromptStereo replaces the GRU in MonSter with a Prompt Recurrent Unit (PRU) built on the DPT decoder of Depth Anything V2, which naturally forms a multi-resolution recurrent architecture and inherits pre-trained monocular depth priors. Two prompt mechanisms inject additional cues via residual additions: a Structure Prompt encodes the affine-invariant difference between current disparity and monocular relative depth, and a Motion Prompt encodes local cost volume and current disparity. An Affine-Invariant Fusion module normalizes the initial disparity and monocular depth under a shared scale/shift before blending them with a learned confidence map. The update strategy removes GRU's reset gate and range constraints on hidden states, using only a single update gate computed from higher-resolution states, which reduces computational cost while enabling more flexible iterative refinement.

## Results

Under the Scene Flow training setting, PromptStereo achieves the best EPE (Noc) on 4 out of 5 basic benchmark datasets: 0.73 on KITTI 2012, 0.60 on Midd-T(H), 0.75 on Midd-2021, and 0.20 on ETH3D, with Bad 2.0 (Noc) of 3.76 on Midd-T(H) and 4.84 on Midd-2021. Under the unlimited training setting, PromptStereo further improves to 0.44 EPE (Noc) on Midd-T(H) and 0.61 on Midd-2021, surpassing FoundationStereo (0.38 and 0.73 respectively) on Midd-T(H) while matching or exceeding it on other datasets. On the advanced Booster benchmark (unlimited training), PromptStereo achieves 0.67 EPE and 2.35 Bad 2.0, outperforming the second-best MG-Stereo (1.24 EPE, 5.97 Bad 2.0) by over 50%. Inference time is 0.36 s per pair on Scene Flow, comparable to or faster than all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptStereo outperforms all 9 compared methods on the majority of metrics across both basic and advanced benchmarks. Against the primary baseline MonSter [14], PromptStereo reduces Midd-2021 EPE (Noc) from 1.21 to 0.75 (Scene Flow) and from 1.50 to 0.61 (unlimited), a ~50–60% improvement, while also cutting inference time from 0.64 s to 0.36 s. Compared to the strongest non-foundation-model baselines (RAFT-Stereo, IGEV-Stereo, GREAT-IGEV), PromptStereo achieves 15–30% lower EPE on Midd-T(H) and Midd-2021. Against other monocular-prior methods (DEFOM-Stereo, BridgeDepth, MG-Stereo, SMoE-Stereo), PromptStereo consistently leads on Midd-T(H) and ETH3D, with the largest margin over SMoE-Stereo on Midd-2021 (0.75 vs. 3.50 EPE Noc). Under unlimited training, PromptStereo surpasses FoundationStereo [45] on Midd-T(H) (0.44 vs. 0.38 EPE Noc) and Booster (0.67 vs. 1.23 EPE), while FoundationStereo retains a slight edge on KITTI 2012 (0.63 vs. 0.65 EPE Noc) and ETH3D (0.49 vs. 0.79 Bad 1.0 Noc). On the challenging Booster benchmark with reflective/transparent surfaces, PromptStereo (unlimited) achieves 0.67 EPE and 2.35 Bad 2.0, more than doubling the performance of MG-Stereo (1.24 EPE, 5.97 Bad 2.0) which was fine-tuned on TransScene.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MonSter (CVPR 2025) for feature extraction and cost volume construction, and on IGEV-Stereo for the geometry encoding volume and loss formulation. Its key architectural departure is replacing the GRU-based iterative refinement (standard in RAFT-Stereo, IGEV-Stereo, and most recent foundation-model-based methods) with a Prompt Recurrent Unit (PRU) built on the DPT decoder from Depth Anything V2, thereby allowing the refinement stage to inherit monocular depth priors rather than being trained from scratch. The paper positions itself as the first to make iterative refinement prompt-guided by monocular structure and stereo motion cues, addressing the identified limitation that GRU's constrained hidden states and convolutional fusion distort guidance signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRU is demonstrated to be a pluggable recurrent unit that improves multiple existing architectures (RAFT-Stereo, IGEV-Stereo) with minimal overhead, suggesting it could become a standard component in iterative stereo matching pipelines. The convergence speed finding—near-optimal performance at 16 iterations versus MonSter still improving at 32—has practical implications for real-time deployment. The authors note that performance in extreme adverse weather remains constrained, and the broader question of how far prompt-guided refinement can push zero-shot stereo matching toward in-domain accuracy on the hardest scenarios (e.g., transparent/reflective surfaces) remains open.
