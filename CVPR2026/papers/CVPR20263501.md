## Problem

Reinforcement learning-based preference optimization (DPO, GRPO) has shown promise for multi-step generative image super-resolution but remains unexplored for one-step generative ISR, whose deterministic mapping from LR to HR prevents the diverse sample generation that RL requires. Additionally, DPO relies on a limited set of offline preference pairs, while GRPO computes only full-image likelihood, overlooking the local texture details critical for super-resolution quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263501/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 128×128 low-resolution image and a composite text prompt (extracted via DAPE and CLIP) as input, and produces a 512×512 super-resolved image in a single forward pass. A key design choice is that controllable Gaussian noise is injected into the VAE-encoded latent before denoising, with the noise-addition timestep (t_add) deliberately set larger than the diffusion denoising timestep (t_diff) to expand sampling diversity without sacrificing reconstruction fidelity.

## Method

GDPO-SR builds on a Noise-Aware One-Step Diffusion (NAOSD) base model that injects controllable noise into the latent space using an unequal-timestep strategy (t_add ≠ t_diff), enabling diverse outputs from the same LR input while preserving fidelity. The GDPO training procedure operates in two stages: (1) an attribute-aware reward function (ARF) scores each of G online-generated samples by dynamically weighting full-reference (PSNR) and no-reference (MANIQA, MUSIQ) metrics according to the proportion of smooth versus detailed regions in the image, then converts these scores into group-relative advantages via z-normalization; (2) the policy optimization reformulates the Diffusion-DPO loss to weight each sample's pixel-level denoising error by its group-relative advantage, so high-reward samples receive stronger gradient signal. This design inherits DPO's implicit pixel-level likelihood (capturing local details) while adopting GRPO's online group-based advantage estimation (improving sample efficiency over DPO's single offline pair).

## Results

On DRealSR, GDPO-SR achieves the best PSNR (28.18 dB), LPIPS (0.2851), FID (138.87), DISTS (0.2112), MUSIQ (65.63), and CLIPIQA (0.7020) among all compared methods, while also attaining the lowest AFINE (18.72). On RealSR, GDPO-SR leads in PSNR (25.48 dB), LPIPS (0.2675), FID (112.13), and DISTS (0.1980), with competitive NR scores (MUSIQ 69.42, CLIPIQA 0.6760). On the synthetic DIV2K-val set, GDPO-SR achieves the best PSNR (23.92 dB), LPIPS (0.2897), and DISTS (0.1965), though it trails slightly on FID (26.44 vs. 24.44 for StableSR). Inference time is 0.11 s per 512×512 image with 2.27 T FLOPs, matching OSEDiff and confirming no additional computational overhead from the GDPO training paradigm.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against multi-step SD-based methods (StableSR, DiffBIR, SeeSR, PASD), GDPO-SR consistently wins on fidelity metrics (PSNR, LPIPS, DISTS) across all three datasets while also leading on most NR metrics; the closest competitor is SeeSR, which edges out GDPO-SR on MUSIQ (69.82 vs. 69.42) and AFINE (24.07 vs. 17.73) on RealSR but trails by 0.33 dB in PSNR and 13.3 in FID. Among one-step methods, GDPO-SR outperforms OSEDiff on PSNR (+0.33 dB on DRealSR, +0.33 dB on RealSR), LPIPS, and FID, while OSEDiff is slightly ahead on MUSIQ (69.09 vs. 69.42 on RealSR is actually GDPO winning; OSEDiff leads on AFINE at 20.92 vs. 17.73 on RealSR). InvSR achieves the lowest AFINE (16.58 on RealSR) but sacrifices fidelity (PSNR 24.30 vs. 25.48) and FID (129.52 vs. 112.13). Compared to the RL-based multi-step method DP²O-SR, GDPO-SR substantially outperforms on all FR metrics (e.g., PSNR 28.18 vs. 23.95 on DRealSR, FID 138.87 vs. 157.76) while DP²O-SR leads on NR metrics (MUSIQ 71.09 vs. 65.63, AFINE 11.39 vs. 18.72), indicating DP²O-SR trades fidelity for perceptual richness. In ablation, GDPO surpasses Diffusion-DPO on both FR and NR metrics and avoids the fidelity degradation seen with DanceGRPO (PSNR drops from 25.25 to 25.10), confirming that pixel-level DPO-style constraints better preserve local detail than GRPO's global likelihood estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: (1) one-step diffusion-based Real-ISR methods (OSEDiff, InvSR) that sacrifice generative diversity for speed, and (2) preference-optimization RL for diffusion models (Diffusion-DPO, Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO) that have been applied to multi-step generation but not to deterministic one-step models. GDPO conceptually unifies Diffusion-DPO's pixel-level denoising constraint with GRPO's online group-relative advantage, while the NAOSD base model introduces a noise-injection mechanism with an unequal-timestep strategy to make one-step models stochastic enough for RL. The closest prior work, DP²O-SR, applies Diffusion-DPO to multi-step Real-ISR; GDPO departs by operating entirely online with group samples and by targeting the one-step regime where stochasticity must be explicitly engineered.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that RL-based preference optimization can improve one-step generative Real-ISR, a regime previously considered too deterministic for DPO/GRPO-style training. The attribute-aware reward function, which adaptively weights fidelity and perception metrics based on spatial content (smooth vs. textured regions), offers a practical template for content-adaptive reward design in generative restoration. Key limitations remain: the training overhead of generating G samples per input, the manually designed heuristic reward, and the modest (though consistent) metric gains over the base model. An open question the authors flag is how to design rewards that better align with human visual perception rather than relying on fixed metric combinations.
