## Problem

Visual autoregressive (VAR) models suffer from quartic attention complexity growth with resolution, making high-resolution generation (e.g., 1024×1024) extremely slow. Existing acceleration methods like FastVAR and SkipVAR skip the last high-resolution scales to reduce computation, but this discards the fine-grained texture and spatial sharpness that those scales are responsible for, causing visible quality degradation. The paper asks whether effective acceleration is possible without skipping any scales.

## Input / Output

SparVAR is a training-free inference-time module applied to pretrained VAR models (Infinity-2B, Infinity-8B, HART). It takes a text prompt as input, which is embedded and projected to the first 1×1 scale, and produces a full-resolution 1024×1024 RGB image through K=13 sequential next-scale prediction steps. The method modifies only the attention computation at later scales (k > S, where S is a mid-scale decision point), leaving the model weights, tokenization, and generation pipeline entirely unchanged.

## Method

SparVAR exploits three empirically observed properties of VAR attention—strong attention sinks, cross-scale activation similarity, and pronounced spatial locality—to design two plug-and-play sparse attention modules. The first, Cross-Scale Self-Similar Sparse Attention (CS⁴A), computes full dense attention at a sparse decision scale (e.g., scale 10), extracts top-k key indices via a column-sum tensor over query blocks, and maps those indices to later high-resolution scales through a simple proportional index mapping (⌊N_k/N_S × inds^(S)⌋), optionally augmented with a sink region for global anchors. The second, Cross-Scale Local Sparse Attention (CSLA), restricts each query token to attend only to a local spatial neighborhood across all historical scales plus a small set of early-scale sink tokens, then converts the resulting token-wise mask into a block-wise sparse mask (128×128 blocks) aligned with GPU tiling. CSLA is implemented on top of FlexAttention and achieves 5.61× forward speedup over FlashAttention at the last scale. Together, these modules reduce 8B-model 1024×1024 generation to ~1 second (1.57× speedup) without skipping any scales, and up to 2.28× when combined with scale-skipping strategies.

## Results

On Infinity-8B generating 1024×1024 images without skipping any scales, SparVAR achieves a 1.57× speedup (1.05s vs. 1.65s baseline) while maintaining a GenEval score of 0.796 (vs. 0.798 baseline) and a PSNR of 29.48 dB, substantially outperforming FastVAR (17.40 dB) and ScaleKV (23.42 dB) in fidelity. On Infinity-2B, SparVAR achieves 1.38× speedup with GenEval 0.738 (vs. 0.736 baseline) and PSNR 28.88. When combined with scale-skipping strategies, SparVAR reaches up to 2.28× acceleration on Infinity-8B (0.72s) while still preserving a GenEval of 0.800 and PSNR of 17.10, compared to FastVAR's 15.27 PSNR under the same setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparVAR consistently outperforms all baselines on both speed and fidelity. Against FastVAR (which prunes tokens and skips scales), SparVAR achieves higher speedups (1.57× vs. 1.14× on 8B w/o skip; 2.28× vs. 1.79× with skip) and dramatically better PSNR (29.48 vs. 17.40), indicating FastVAR's token pruning causes severe texture loss. Against ScaleKV (KV cache compression), SparVAR is faster (1.57× vs. 0.67×—ScaleKV is actually slower than the baseline due to expensive key-value selection) and achieves higher PSNR (29.48 vs. 23.42). Against SkipVAR (adaptive scale skipping), SparVAR with dynamic skipping achieves higher speedup (2.05× vs. 1.71×) and better PSNR (19.61 vs. 17.98) while matching GenEval (0.796 vs. 0.789). On human preference (HPSv2.1), SparVAR without skipping matches or slightly exceeds the Infinity-8B baseline (31.01 vs. 31.00), while all competing methods score lower (FastVAR 30.62, ScaleKV 30.82).

## Relation to Prior Work

SparVAR builds directly on the VAR next-scale prediction paradigm (Tian et al., NeurIPS 2024) and adapts the column-sum tensor sparsity extraction from Chipmunk (Silveria et al., 2025) to the multi-scale attention structure of VAR. It departs conceptually from prior VAR acceleration methods (FastVAR, ScaleKV, SkipVAR), which achieve speedup by skipping high-resolution scales or compressing KV caches at the cost of high-frequency detail loss. Instead, SparVAR exploits three empirically observed attention properties—sinks, cross-scale similarity, and spatial locality—to perform sparse attention computation across all scales, using FlexAttention (Dong et al., 2024) as the kernel substrate for a block-wise sparse implementation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that VAR attention exhibits strong cross-scale activation similarity is a useful empirical insight that opens a principled direction for training-free acceleration of multi-scale autoregressive models without the quality degradation inherent in scale-skipping. The practical impact is moderate: a 1.57× speedup (reaching ~1s for 1024×1024 on 8B) with near-lossless fidelity is valuable for deployment, but the absolute speedup is limited compared to what aggressive scale-skipping achieves. Open questions include whether the cross-scale sparsity patterns generalize to video VAR models or other next-scale architectures, whether the approach composes well with quantization or speculative decoding, and whether the dense decision-scale pass can itself be made cheaper.
