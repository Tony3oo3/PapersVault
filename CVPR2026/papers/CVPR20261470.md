## Problem

In class-incremental learning with pre-trained models, parameter-efficient fine-tuning (e.g., LoRA) remains vulnerable to catastrophic forgetting as task-specific subspaces drift from the pre-trained manifold. Existing gradient projection methods impose rigid orthogonality constraints that confine updates to a narrow subspace, severely limiting plasticity and the model's ability to acquire new knowledge. This paper addresses the stability-plasticity dilemma by replacing hard orthogonal constraints with a softer gradient fusion strategy and adding representation-level alignment mechanisms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261470/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images as input, processed by a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone (pre-trained on ImageNet-1K or ImageNet-21K) augmented with task-specific LoRA adapters of rank 32. During training, each image is fed simultaneously through the frozen pre-trained model and the LoRA-augmented model to compute both the task loss and the patch-level alignment loss. The output is a class label predicted by a unified classifier that is recalibrated after each incremental task by sampling from Gaussian distributions fitted to semantically-shifted class prototypes, rather than by simple concatenation of per-task sub-classifiers.

## Method

The DGS framework introduces three complementary mechanisms. First, the Dual Gradient (DG) update constructs a pre-trained subspace P_t^l at each layer via SVD of the input covariance matrix, then fuses the raw task gradient with its projection onto this subspace through an interpolated combination controlled by hyperparameter α, yielding an update direction that is both expressive and stable without hard orthogonality. Second, a Patch-level Alignment (PA) loss weights the L2 distance between current and previous patch tokens by the inverse cosine similarity to the class token, granting task-relevant patches greater freedom to adapt while anchoring less relevant ones. Third, a Semantic Shift Estimation module computes a weighted shift vector from current-task class tokens to update stored prototypes of old classes, and a Classifier Alignment (CA) step retrains the unified classifier by sampling features from Gaussian distributions fitted to these updated prototypes, ensuring decision boundaries remain consistent with the drifted feature space.

## Results

Using ViT-B/16-IN21K as the backbone, DGS achieves the best Aavg and ALast across all six CIL benchmarks. Headline numbers include 93.82/90.44 (Aavg/ALast) on CIFAR-100 B0 Inc5, 86.21/81.72 on ImageNet-R B0 Inc20, and 73.04/63.53 on ImageNet-A B0 Inc20. The largest margins over the runner-up appear on the domain-shift datasets: +2.14% ALast on ImageNet-R (vs. SSIAT at 62.15) and +1.38% ALast on ImageNet-A (vs. SSIAT at 62.15). On the easier CIFAR-100 and CUB settings, DGS leads by narrower margins of 0.28% and 0.47% in Aavg, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGS outperforms all 13 baselines on Aavg across every dataset and on ALast on five of six. The most decisive wins are on the domain-shift benchmarks: on ImageNet-R B0 Inc20, DGS reaches 81.72% ALast, surpassing the second-best SSIAT (62.15%) by 19.57 points and infLoRA (59.88%) by 21.84 points; on ImageNet-A B0 Inc20, DGS (63.53%) leads SSIAT (62.15%) by 1.38 points. On the in-distribution CIFAR-100 B0 Inc5, DGS (90.44%) edges out infLoRA (90.16%) and RanPAC (89.65%) by 0.28 and 0.79 points respectively. The sole exception is CUB B0 Inc10 ALast, where MOS (89.44%) narrowly beats DGS (89.27%) by 0.17 points, though DGS still leads on Aavg (93.29 vs. 93.01). Prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODAPrompt) and adapter-based methods (EASE, MoAL) trail DGS by 5–15 points on ALast across most datasets, while the closest LoRA-based competitors (infLoRA, LoRA-DRS, SSIAT) are consistently outperformed by 1–2 points on in-distribution data and by much larger margins under domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of PEFT-based continual learning and gradient-constrained optimization. It builds directly on LoRA as the parameter-efficient adaptation backbone, on SVD-based feature covariance subspaces (Wang et al., CVPR 2021) for defining the pre-trained learning space, on Semantic Drift Compensation (Yu et al., CVPR 2020) for estimating prototype shifts, and on prototype-augmented classifier retraining (Zhu et al., CVPR 2021). The key departure from prior gradient-projection methods (e.g., InfLoRA, GPM) is replacing hard orthogonality constraints with a soft interpolated fusion between the raw task gradient and its projection onto the pre-trained subspace, complemented by a novel patch-token alignment loss that has no direct analogue in the CIL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a simple interpolation parameter (α) can resolve the stability-plasticity dilemma more effectively than rigid subspace constraints, offering a practical and interpretable knob for practitioners tuning PEFT-based CIL systems. The multi-level protection strategy (gradient-level DG, token-level PA, classifier-level CA) provides a template for combining complementary anti-forgetting mechanisms. Limitations include reliance on ViT backbones, linear growth of stored LoRA modules with task count, and the SVD computation over full task data before each training phase, which may be costly for very large models or long task sequences. Whether the dual-gradient idea transfers to non-Transformer architectures or to other continual learning paradigms (domain, task) remains open.
