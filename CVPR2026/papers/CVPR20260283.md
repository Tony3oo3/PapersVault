## Problem

Existing SEM 3D reconstruction methods face a fundamental trade-off: multi-view photogrammetry fails on textureless microstructures, while single-view photometric stereo requires reference-sample calibration and is corrupted by geometric shadowing. Learning-based 3D reconstruction methods trained on macroscopic RGB imagery do not generalize to the SEM domain due to the severe scale and appearance gap and the absence of SEM-specific imaging physics. No prior framework simultaneously achieves self-calibrated, shadow-robust, and geometry-complete 3D reconstruction of complex microstructures from multi-detector SEM signals.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two input streams per viewpoint: SE images (used only for the Agisoft Metashape coarse reconstruction that yields per-view depth maps and surface confidence maps) and four 4Q-BSE quadrant images (1024×768 each, 37 views from ±45° stage tilts in 5° increments). The output is a 3D mesh extracted via Marching Cubes from the optimized SDF, together with 16 learned BSE forward-model parameters (per-quadrant c, d, e plus four polynomial coefficients) and per-view binary shadow masks. The entire per-sample optimization takes approximately 2 minutes on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

NFH-SEM represents geometry as an SDF neural field (MLP with multi-resolution hash encoding) and, instead of directly supervising surface gradients, embeds a learnable BSE forward model F that maps predicted normals to 4Q-BSE intensities. The forward model replaces the conventional sec(θ) emission term with a fourth-order polynomial and assigns independent parameters to each quadrant (16 total), enabling self-calibration without reference samples. An iterative shadow-separation loop updates binary masks by thresholding the BSE residual at a level proportional to the learned sensitivity parameter d, creating a self-reinforcing feedback that progressively cleans supervision. Training proceeds in three stages—coarse depth initialization, joint BSE-model and geometry optimization, and shadow-masked refinement—using a composite loss of weighted depth error, SDF unit-norm regularization, masked BSE MAE, and inter-quadrant parameter variance regularization.

## Results

On the simulated TPL dataset, NFH-SEM achieves an average Chamfer distance of 17.48 nm and average normal angle error of 3.70°, substantially outperforming the multi-view coarse baseline (25.11 nm CD, 7.85° NAE) and the single-view photometric stereo baseline (512.22 nm CD, 12.99° NAE). The learned BSE forward model reaches a mean pixel-intensity error of 0.27, confirming accurate self-calibration of detector parameters. On real-world samples, NFH-SEM recovers 478 nm layered features in TPL structures, 782 nm pollen surface textures, and 1.559 μm fracture steps on SiC particles—details missed entirely by both conventional baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the multi-view photogrammetry baseline (Agisoft Metashape), NFH-SEM recovers fine surface details—hair strands, layered steps, pollen textures—that the multi-view method over-smooths or misses entirely in textureless regions; quantitatively, the coarse multi-view initialization yields 25.11 nm average Chamfer distance versus 17.48 nm for NFH-SEM. Against the single-view photometric stereo method [53], NFH-SEM is dramatically superior: 17.48 nm vs. 512.22 nm average Chamfer distance (≈29× improvement) and 3.70° vs. 12.99° normal angle error, as the single-view method suffers from shadow-induced gradient errors and requires manual calibration. Against learning-based methods (NeuS, 2DGS, PGSR, DN-Splatter), NFH-SEM produces faithful geometry while these methods, which treat SEM signals as standard RGB radiance fields, generate severe surface distortions and incomplete meshes. Against feed-forward models (VGGT, MapAnything), NFH-SEM avoids the domain-gap failure mode: these pretrained networks produce fragmented surfaces and unstable pose estimates on SEM data, whereas NFH-SEM's per-scene optimization with an embedded SEM physics model generalizes across TPL, pollen, and SiC specimens without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

NFH-SEM sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: conventional SEM 3D reconstruction (multi-view SfM/MVS for coarse geometry, single-view photometric stereo from 4Q-BSE signals for fine gradients) and modern neural implicit surface reconstruction (NeuS-style SDF networks, 3D Gaussian Splatting). It departs from prior hybrid SEM methods [72] by replacing their 2D height-map fusion with a continuous SDF neural field, and from learning-based RGB reconstruction methods (NeuS, 2DGS, VGGT, MapAnything) by embedding a learnable BSE forward model—derived from the signal equation in Slówko [53] but with a flexible polynomial emission term and per-quadrant parameters—directly into the volume rendering pipeline. This design eliminates the need for reference-sample calibration and introduces an iterative shadow-separation mechanism absent from all prior SEM reconstruction approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first multi-view, multi-detector real-world SEM dataset (TPL microstructures, peach pollen, SiC fracture surfaces) with a systematic acquisition protocol, lowering the barrier for the SEM metrology community to adopt learning-based 3D reconstruction. The learnable-forward-model-in-neural-field paradigm is a natural but previously unexplored way to inject domain-specific imaging physics into per-scene optimization, potentially transferable to other calibrated multi-sensor imaging modalities. Open questions remain: charging effects on low-conductivity samples, material-dependent emission variability confounding geometry with composition, and fully occluded regions (e.g., microporous materials) where all four BSE quadrants are shadowed leave no recoverable signal. The method's ~2-minute training time on a single GPU makes it practically deployable, but its per-scene optimization limits throughput for large-scale screening applications.
