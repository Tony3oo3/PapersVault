## Problem

Existing 3DGS watermarking methods treat watermark embedding and model quantization as independent processes, causing watermark extraction accuracy to collapse under aggressive quantization (e.g., 3D-GSW drops from 98% at 32-bit to 61% at 4-bit) while simultaneously degrading rendering quality. No prior work has designed watermarking schemes that are explicitly aware of the quantization noise introduced when compressing 3DGS models for deployment on resource-limited devices.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained 3DGS scene (N Gaussians, each with 14 parameters: 3D position, RGB color, opacity, 3D scale, and 4D quaternion), rendered images at given camera poses, and an M-bit binary watermark message (default 48 bits). It produces a watermarked 3DGS model whose Gaussian positions are perturbed in the mid-frequency DCT band (k ∈ [⌊N/8⌋, ⌊3N/8⌋]) and watermarked rendered images with mid-frequency FFT annular modifications (r ∈ [min(H,W)/8, 3·min(H,W)/8]). At extraction time, dual decoders (a HiDDeN-based 2D image decoder and a PointNet-based 3D Gaussian decoder) recover the M-bit message from either the quantized rendered image or the quantized 14-dimensional Gaussian attribute vectors.

## Method

Robust3DGSW is a two-stage quantization-aware watermarking pipeline. In the embedding stage, watermarks are injected into mid-frequency bands of both 3D Gaussian positions (via 1D DCT with Gaussian noise at σ=0.001) and 2D rendered images (via 2D FFT with Gaussian noise at σ=0.01), exploiting the fact that mid-frequency coefficients lie below the human JND threshold yet resist the rounding errors that preferentially destroy high-frequency information at 4-bit precision. In the decoder training stage, a dual-decoder architecture (HiDDeN for 2D, PointNet for 3D) is trained under a multi-scale adversarial perturbation schedule that simultaneously applies frequency-domain perturbations, b-bit uniform quantization, and rendering-level distortions (Gaussian noise, JPEG, blur, crop), while a gradual quantization continuation method progressively reduces bit-width from 8 to 4 bits via b(p)=⌈8−4p⌉ to avoid the non-convex loss landscape of direct 4-bit training. A cross-decoder consistency loss (squared L2 between sigmoid outputs) enforces that both decoders converge to the same watermark, and auxiliary balance and batch-consistency losses stabilize training under small batches.

## Results

On the Blender, LLFF, and MipNeRF-360 datasets, Robust3DGSW achieves 99.78% bit accuracy without quantization and maintains 87.51% at 4-bit quantization, compared to ~60% for 3D-GSW at the same level. Under 4-bit quantization with distortions, Robust3DGSW retains 81.4–88.8% accuracy across noise, blur, rotation, and cropping, while baselines drop substantially. Rendering quality at 4-bit reaches 20.46 dB PSNR (vs. 10 dB for 3D-GSW), and model storage is reduced to 83.49 MB (from 840.10 MB unquantized).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Robust3DGSW outperforms all four baselines across every metric and quantization level. Against the strongest 3DGS-specific baselines, GaussianMarker [9] and 3D-GSW [11], Robust3DGSW gains up to 2.37% bit accuracy without quantization and a much larger margin under 4-bit quantization (87.51% vs. ~60% for 3D-GSW). The rendering-quality gap is especially pronounced: at 4-bit, Robust3DGSW achieves 20.46 dB PSNR while 3D-GSW drops to 10 dB, a 10.46 dB improvement attributable to mid-frequency embedding that avoids quantization-sensitive high-frequency bands. Against the image-level baselines StegaNeRF+3DGS [13] and HiDDeN+3DGS [26], Robust3DGSW also leads in both bit accuracy and PSNR, as these methods embed watermarks before or independently of 3DGS optimization and thus suffer greater degradation under quantization. Under distortion attacks at 4-bit (Figure 5), all baselines show significant accuracy drops, whereas Robust3DGSW's multi-scale adversarial perturbation training keeps accuracy above 81% for all four distortion types.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3DGS watermarking line (GaussianMarker, 3D-GSW, GSHider, StegaNeRF+3DGS) by being the first to explicitly integrate model quantization into the watermarking pipeline, rather than treating embedding and compression as independent stages. Conceptually, it repurposes classical frequency-domain watermarking ideas (mid-band DCT/FFT embedding) to the 3D Gaussian parameter space and pairs them with a dual-decoder architecture (HiDDeN for 2D renders, PointNet for 3D attributes) trained under gradual quantization-aware training. The departure from prior work is thus both in the embedding strategy (mid-frequency vs. spatial or high-frequency) and in the training protocol (progressive 8→4-bit schedule with multi-scale adversarial perturbations), rather than in the underlying 3DGS or decoder architectures themselves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS assets are increasingly deployed on mobile and edge devices where sub-8-bit quantization is necessary, the finding that standard watermarking methods collapse to ~60% accuracy at 4-bit while Robust3DGSW maintains >80% addresses a practical gap that prior 3DGS watermarking work has ignored. The mid-frequency embedding insight provides a principled design guideline (avoid both low-frequency perceptual damage and high-frequency quantization fragility) that could inform future 3D content protection schemes. However, the method currently handles only common distortions (blur, noise, rotation, crop) and does not address sophisticated adversarial attacks, and the dual-decoder training adds ~40% overhead, leaving open questions about scalability to more aggressive compression regimes and real-time extraction on constrained hardware.
