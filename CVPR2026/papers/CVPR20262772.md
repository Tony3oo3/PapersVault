## Problem

Dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting methods that use neural deformation fields (e.g., DeformableGS, 4DGS) achieve the highest reconstruction quality but require per-Gaussian neural inference at every frame, making them several times slower than analytic or non-neural motion representations. This efficiency-fidelity gap limits real-time deployment of high-quality dynamic scene reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes monocular RGB video sequences with estimated camera poses and timestamps, initialized via SfM point clouds, and produces a compact dynamic scene representation consisting of pruned 3D Gaussian primitives (reduced by ~10×) and J=2048 shared SE(3) rigid transformations that replace per-Gaussian neural deformation. At inference time, novel views are rendered by applying the grouped rigid transforms to canonical Gaussians and rasterizing via differentiable alpha blending, achieving 13.71× faster rendering than the DeformableGS baseline.

## Method

SpeeDe3DGS integrates three complementary modules into DeformableGS. Temporal Sensitivity Pruning (TSP) computes temporally aggregated second-order sensitivity scores (sum of squared image-space gradients across all training poses and timesteps) to identify and remove low-impact Gaussians, reducing primitives by over 12×. Temporal Sensitivity Sampling (TSS) injects a linearly annealed Gaussian perturbation (β=0.1, τ=20000) into the deformation function's timestamp input during sensitivity estimation, exposing temporally unstable floaters that appear stable on training frames but drift at unseen timesteps. GroupFlow then distills the learned neural deformation field into grouped rigid motion: it selects J control points via farthest point sampling, assigns each Gaussian to the nearest control point by trajectory similarity (weighted mean and std of positional residuals), and estimates per-group SE(3) transforms via Umeyama alignment, reducing motion inference from N per-Gaussian predictions to J per-group transformations.

## Results

On MonoDyGauBench (50 scenes), DeformableGS + Pruning (TSP+TSS) achieves 137.01 FPS (6.78× speedup) with PSNR 23.86 and SSIM 0.694, while adding GroupFlow further accelerates rendering to 276.91 FPS (13.71×) and reduces training time to 2461 s (2.53×), with SSIM 0.709 and MS-SSIM 0.771 surpassing the original DeformableGS baseline. On NeRF-DS, the full pipeline (TSP+TSS+GroupFlow) reaches 505.60 FPS (10.68×) with 11.91× fewer Gaussians and 2.44× shorter training. On HyperNeRF, the combined method achieves 29.21× faster rendering, 12.18× Gaussian reduction, and 3.74× shorter training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the five MonoDyGauBench baselines (EffGS, STG, STG-decoder, RTGS, 4DGS), the full SpeeDe3DGS pipeline (DeformableGS + Pruning + GroupFlow) achieves the highest FPS (276.91) and shortest training time (2461 s), beating the fastest baseline (STG at 181.70 FPS) by ~52% and the fastest training (EffGS at 3758 s) by 34%. On image quality, it surpasses all non-neural baselines in PSNR (23.52 vs. best 21.84 EffGS), SSIM (0.709 vs. best 0.678 STG-decoder), MS-SSIM (0.771 vs. best 0.742 STG-decoder), and LPIPS (0.313 vs. best 0.347 EffGS). Compared to the neural baselines it extends, DeformableGS + Pruning + GF retains 97.7% of DeformableGS's PSNR (23.52 vs. 24.07) and 98.2% of 4DGS's PSNR (23.52 vs. 23.55) while delivering 13.71× and 4.40× faster rendering respectively. Notably, SSIM and MS-SSIM of the full pipeline (0.709, 0.771) actually exceed the original DeformableGS (0.694, 0.755), suggesting a mild regularizing effect from GroupFlow. The 4DGS + Pruning variant reaches 179.64 FPS (2.85×) with PSNR 22.44, making it competitive with the fastest non-neural methods while retaining higher fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gradient-based pruning lineage of Speedy-Splat and PUP 3D-GS from static to dynamic 3DGS by aggregating second-order sensitivities over both spatial views and timesteps, and introduces temporal perturbation (TSS) to expose floaters invisible at training frames. For motion compression, it departs from group-based methods like SC-GS, SPGaussian, and MoSca—which use control points with linear blend skinning or dual quaternion blending—by distilling the learned neural deformation field into shared SE(3) rigid transformations per cluster, a simpler and more parameter-efficient formulation. The evaluation is grounded in MonoDyGauBench, which standardizes comparison across five motion parameterizations, positioning SpeeDe3DGS as a drop-in acceleration layer on top of DeformableGS and 4DGS rather than a new motion model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 13.71× rendering speedup with preserved (and in some metrics improved) image quality makes neural-motion dynamic 3DGS practically deployable at real-time frame rates, closing a gap that MonoDyGauBench identified as the central tradeoff in the field. The modular design—TSP/TSS applicable to any dynamic 3DGS framework, GroupFlow to any with an explicit motion model—suggests broad adoptability beyond DeformableGS. Open questions include whether the locally-rigid assumption of GroupFlow can be relaxed for highly non-rigid deformations, whether the observed regularizing effect (GroupFlow improving SSIM over the unpruned baseline) can be exploited as a principled motion prior, and how these techniques interact with learned dynamics or physics-based motion models.
