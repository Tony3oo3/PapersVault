## Problem

Autoregressive mesh generation models decode tokens strictly sequentially, making inference prohibitively slow for interactive and large-scale applications. While speculative decoding has accelerated LLM inference, adapting it to hierarchical mesh structures (faces, vertices, coordinates) within Hourglass Transformer architectures is non-trivial due to strong geometric and topological dependencies that flat-sequence methods ignore.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262496/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a generation condition (e.g., class label) as input and produces a 3D mesh as a hierarchical token sequence organized into face tokens, point tokens, and coordinate tokens, following the autoregressive ordering convention (z-y-x sorting). Each face token corresponds to nine coordinate tokens and each point token to three, so the output is a structured, multi-level sequence rather than a flat token stream. Meshes with more than 10,000 faces are excluded from training, and evaluation uses 5,000 uniformly sampled surface points for Chamfer and Hausdorff distance computation.

## Method

FlashMesh introduces a predict-correct-verify paradigm built on the Hourglass Transformer. In the predict stage, lightweight SP-Blocks (parameter-independent transformer blocks) generate multiple draft tokens in parallel from current-layer hidden states, while HF-Blocks fuse these speculative high-level features with cached key-value states from the current layer to produce refined low-level predictions across face, point, and coordinate levels. In the correct stage, a label head classifies each generated point as historical, new, or intra-batch, and a correction algorithm enforces vertex-sharing consistency by duplicating misaligned vertices and re-sorting along z-y-x axes. In the verify stage, the backbone re-evaluates all draft tokens in a single forward pass with causal masking, accepting the longest consistent prefix and discarding the rest. Training uses a combined cross-entropy loss over coordinate predictions and point-label classification, with a balancing weight γ=0.3.

## Results

On the combined ShapeNetV2 + gObjaverse test set, FlashMesh (Meshtron-2B) achieves the best Chamfer Distance of 0.089 and Hausdorff Distance of 0.198, while also delivering the highest throughput of 136.6 tokens/sec among the 2B+ class models — a 2.03× speedup over the original Meshtron-2B (67.3 TPS). The 1B variant (FlashMesh Meshtron-1B) reaches 180.4 TPS (1.83× speedup) with CD 0.120 and BBox-IoU 0.905, matching or slightly improving upon the base Meshtron-1B on all quality metrics. Across all configurations, FlashMesh simultaneously improves geometric fidelity (lower CD/HD, higher BBox-IoU) and inference speed relative to the underlying autoregressive backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to Meshtron-2B (the strongest baseline at 2.3B params), FlashMesh (Meshtron-2B) improves CD from 0.092 to 0.089, HD from 0.206 to 0.198, and BBox-IoU from 0.942 to 0.949, while nearly doubling throughput from 67.3 to 136.6 TPS (×2.03 speedup). Against Meshtron-1B, the 1B FlashMesh variant matches CD (0.120 vs 0.121), improves HD (0.267 vs 0.269) and BBox-IoU (0.905 vs 0.901), and more than doubles TPS from 98.6 to 180.4 (×1.83). Versus Mesh-RFT, FlashMesh (Mesh-RFT) ties on CD (0.114) but edges out on HD (0.252 vs 0.254) and BBox-IoU (0.913 vs 0.912) while nearly doubling speed (179.2 vs 95.5 TPS, ×1.87). Against the smaller BPT and DeepMesh baselines, FlashMesh dominates on all quality metrics (e.g., CD 0.089–0.120 vs 0.128–0.139) and is 3–6× faster in TPS, though the authors note these baselines use incompatible token-compression schemes. Overall, FlashMesh wins on every metric in every head-to-head comparison, achieving both faster inference and higher geometric fidelity simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashMesh builds directly on Meshtron's Hourglass Transformer architecture for autoregressive mesh generation and adapts the speculative decoding paradigm from LLMs (Gloeckle et al., Chen et al.) to the hierarchical face-vertex-coordinate structure of meshes. It departs from prior mesh acceleration efforts like XSpecMesh (which uses LoRA-tuned draft models) and Iflame (interleaved decoding) by introducing lightweight SP-Block and HF-Block modules that exploit the hourglass hierarchy for parallel multi-token prediction, combined with a structure-aware correction mechanism that enforces vertex-sharing consistency. The predict-correct-verify framing positions FlashMesh as a principled middle ground between pure speed-oriented speculative decoding and fidelity-preserving autoregressive generation, addressing the paper's observation that prior methods 'primarily focus on speed and often sacrifice geometric fidelity or structural consistency.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 2× speedup with simultaneous quality improvement is practically significant for interactive 3D content creation, gaming, and large-scale mesh pipelines where autoregressive decoding has been a deployment bottleneck. The predict-correct-verify paradigm, particularly the structure-aware correction step, offers a reusable template for accelerating other hierarchical autoregressive generation tasks beyond meshes. However, the framework inherits the inherent sensitivity of autoregressive models to early prediction errors, is validated only on hourglass architectures, and the quality gains over baselines are modest in absolute terms (e.g., CD 0.121→0.120 at 1B scale). Open questions include whether hybrid decoding strategies or more explicit geometric priors can further close the gap to non-autoregressive methods while preserving topological control.
