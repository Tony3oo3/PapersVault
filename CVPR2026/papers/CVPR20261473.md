## Problem

RL post-training for VLMs in data-scarce specialized domains (chemistry, earth science, multimodal mathematics) is hampered by limited synthetic data distributions and reward hacking, where models exploit high-reward shortcuts, collapse policy entropy, and fail to generalize. Existing question-answering supervision paradigms overlook the rich contextual information embedded in problem stems, making it difficult to elicit genuine reasoning rather than pattern-matching on narrow synthetic distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261473/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The Thinker receives a question-masked multimodal context (image plus retained scenario text, with the explicit question and answer cues removed) and produces a free-form textual analysis. The Solver receives the full original context, the question, and the Thinker's analysis as a prompt, then generates a structured response in the format ⟨think⟩ reasoning chain ⟨/think⟩ final answer, with a maximum response length of 4096 tokens. In Stage 2, the Thinker itself produces this full reasoning-plus-answer output on the original (unmasked) problem.

## Method

DoGe (Decouple to Generalize) splits the policy into a learnable Thinker and a parameter-frozen Solver, both initialized from the same base VLM each iteration. In Stage 1 (Learning from Context), the Thinker freely explores the question-masked context; the frozen Solver attempts to solve the original problem conditioned on the Thinker's analysis, and its pass rate serves as the quantitative reward for the Thinker, creating a self-supervised feedback loop that measures the usefulness of contextual understanding. In Stage 2 (Learning from Application), the updated Thinker is trained with standard GRPO on the original problems using correctness-based rewards, internalizing the contextual knowledge into direct reasoning capability. Both stages use GRPO (with DAPO-style decoupled clipping), and the entire process is wrapped in an iterative curriculum that maintains an evolving seed problem pool (selecting problems with 0.1–0.3 pass rate) and a multimodal knowledge pool to sustain data diversity across self-evolution rounds.

## Results

DoGe achieves an average improvement of +5.7% (3B) and +2.3% (7B) over the Qwen2.5-VL base models across seven benchmarks after three iterations of self-evolving training. The 3B model reaches 50.2 on MMMU, 57.0 on MathVista, and 47.3 on MSEarthMCQ, while the 7B model reaches 53.6 on MMMU, 68.3 on MathVista, and 68.0 on HallusionBench. Notably, DoGe improves HallusionBench by +1.9% (3B) and +2.0% (7B) over the base, indicating reduced visual hallucination alongside stronger domain reasoning. The method also outperforms prior RL-based VLMs (Visionary-3B, Vision-R1-7B) on most benchmarks, with the largest gains in domain-specific tasks (MSEarthMCQ +7.5% for 3B, +3.2% for 7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen2.5VL-3B base, DoGe-3B (Iter 3) wins on all seven benchmarks, with the largest margins on MathVista (+8.2), MMMU (+9.2), and MSEarthMCQ (+6.5). Compared to Visionary-3B (a prior RL-based VLM), DoGe-3B leads on every benchmark, achieving +9.5 on MMMU, +12.3 on MathVision, and +9.1 on MSEarthMCQ. At the 7B scale, DoGe-7B (Iter 3) outperforms the Qwen2.5VL-7B base on six of seven benchmarks (losing only 0.1 on ChemBench: 48.5 vs. 48.6) and surpasses Vision-R1-7B on MMMU (+6.7), MMStar (+2.2), HallusionBench (+1.3), ChemBench (+2.5), and MSEarthMCQ (+1.7), though Vision-R1 retains a notable edge on MathVision (29.0 vs. 25.2) and a marginal lead on MathVista (68.5 vs. 68.3). InternVL2.5 models serve as reference points but are not directly comparable since they lack the RL post-training pipeline; DoGe-3B exceeds InternVL2.5-2B on MMMU (+6.6) and MathVision (+10.7), while DoGe-7B surpasses InternVL2.5-8B on MMMU (+4.7) and HallusionBench (+17.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

DoGe builds directly on the GRPO-based RL post-training paradigm for VLMs established by Vision-R1 and OpenVLThinker, and on the broader self-evolving VLM literature (SEAgent, VL-Rethinker, Training-Free GRPO). Its key departure is a dual-decoupling of the policy into a learnable Thinker (context analysis on question-masked inputs) and a frozen Solver (problem solving conditioned on the Thinker's output), which reframes the RL reward signal from answer-correctness to context-usefulness in stage one. The iterative curriculum data pipeline with a seed problem pool extends prior synthetic-data and self-play approaches [18] by coupling them with the two-stage training loop, creating a self-bootstrapping cycle that prior single-stage GRPO methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DoGe offers a practical pathway for RL post-training of VLMs in specialized, data-scarce domains (chemistry, earth science, multimodal math) where high-quality annotated reasoning data is prohibitively expensive to produce. The policy-entropy analysis provides a mechanistic explanation for why naive GRPO suffers reward hacking and entropy collapse, and how context-first learning mitigates this—insight that could inform RL training design beyond this specific architecture. Limitations include only three iterations tested, modest gains on the 7B model (+2.3% avg), small ablation margins over naive GRPO (≈0.3–0.8 points), and an ad-hoc masking function whose sensitivity is not thoroughly ablated. Open questions include whether the Thinker-Solver decomposition scales to larger models, how robust it is to imperfect context masking, and whether the approach transfers to non-visual or multi-turn reasoning settings.
