## Problem

Standard deep learning segmentation models treat pixels independently and cannot guarantee topology accuracy, producing broken tubular structures and spurious isolated regions. Existing topology loss functions are either computationally prohibitive (persistence homology-based, increasing training from hours to days) or restricted to tubular morphologies (skeletonization-based), leaving no plug-and-play, CPU/GPU-efficient method that generalizes across structure shapes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262782/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SCNP operates on the unnormalized logits (Z ∈ R^{B×C×H×W}) and one-hot-encoded ground truth (Y) produced by any segmentation network, replacing each logit with a neighborhood-propagated value before the loss is computed. It is applied to 2D and 3D images spanning medical (MRI, CT, fundus, electron microscopy), satellite, and histological modalities, with the final output remaining a standard per-pixel segmentation mask. The method introduces a single hyperparameter—neighborhood window size w (default 3)—and requires only a few extra GPU MiBs and milliseconds per iteration.

## Method

SCNP (Same Class Neighbor Penalization) replaces each foreground logit with the minimum value among its same-class foreground neighbors and each background logit with the maximum among its same-class background neighbors, implemented via masked Min/Max Pooling with a configurable window. The key insight is that pixels at structure breakages and isolated false positives are necessarily the worst-classified neighbors of their neighbors, so propagating the poorest neighbor's value forces the model to fix those bad neighbors before improving the center pixel. The resulting gradients penalize the poorest misclassifications as many times as they are propagated and couple predictions across neighboring pixels and classes, effectively optimizing an upper bound of the standard loss. SCNP is architecture-agnostic and loss-agnostic: it is inserted as three lines of code between the network's logits and any loss function (Cross Entropy, Dice, Tversky, clDice, Focal, etc.), and can be combined with the standard loss to mitigate the slight convergence slowdown from gradient inhibition.

## Results

On FIVES (tubular medical imaging), SCNP reduces the Betti error (β₀ₑ) from 10.82±2.71 (CEDice baseline) to 6.94±1.47 (CEDice+SCNP), a ~36% reduction, while maintaining Dice at 0.92. Across 13 datasets spanning tubular, non-tubular, and rounded structures, SCNP achieves the lowest β₀ₑ on 5 of 6 tubular datasets and 2 of 3 rounded-structure datasets. In the ablation on FIVES, SCNP improves every tested loss function: β₀ₑ drops from 16.08 (Focal) to 7.75, from 15.99 (RWLoss) to 8.64, from 36.55 (clDice) to 5.44, and from 12.45 (SkelRecall) to 5.07, without degrading Dice or clDice scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCNP+CEDice (β₀ₑ=6.94) outperforms all non-SCNP baselines on FIVES: clDice (36.55), Focal (16.08), RWLoss (15.99), Tversky (12.22), SkelRecall (12.45), and TopoLoss (8.09). When SCNP is applied to these same loss functions, it dramatically improves them—clDice drops from 36.55 to 5.44, SkelRecall from 12.45 to 5.07, and Focal from 16.08 to 7.75—making SCNP-enhanced topology losses competitive with or superior to plain SCNP+CEDice. On tubular datasets (Groups 1–2), SCNP consistently achieves the lowest β₀ₑ without degrading Dice or clDice, whereas on non-tubular medical datasets (Group 3) results are mixed: beneficial on CirrMRI600 (halving β₀ₑ) but detrimental on MSLesSeg. On rounded-structure cell datasets (Group 4), SCNP achieves the lowest β₀ₑ on NuInsSeg and IHC_TMA, and also improves roundness across all three datasets. The key advantage over PH-based losses (TopoLoss) and skeletonization-based losses (clDice, SkelRecall) is computational efficiency: SCNP adds only milliseconds per iteration and a few MB of GPU memory, versus seconds for PH-based methods, while also working on non-tubular structures where skeletonization-based methods are inapplicable.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCNP departs from the two dominant families of topology loss functions—persistence-homology-based (TopoLoss, Betti Matching) and skeletonization-based (clDice, SkelRecall)—by avoiding explicit topological computation entirely. Instead, it repurposes the simple idea of neighbor-aware penalization (related to Max Pooling loss and NeighborLoss) as a logit-space transformation applied before any loss function, making it orthogonal to and composable with existing losses. The method is conceptually closest to neighbor-aware methods but differs in that it selectively propagates only the worst-classified same-class neighbor rather than amplifying all misclassifications or modeling pairwise affinities, and it requires no additional network branches or post-processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCNP's practical appeal is strong: three lines of code, negligible compute overhead, and compatibility with any loss function and architecture make it a low-friction addition to existing segmentation pipelines. However, its effectiveness is inconsistent on non-tubular, small-structure datasets (detrimental on MSLesSeg), and the optimal neighborhood size must be tuned to structure thickness, limiting its plug-and-play nature. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to 3D volumetric data with complex topology (holes, cavities beyond β₀), whether the small-structure limitation can be addressed, and whether the logit-propagation principle can be extended to other spatially-structured prediction tasks beyond segmentation.
