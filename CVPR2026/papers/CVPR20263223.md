## Problem

Symmetric, order-invariant fusion in audio-visual deepfake localization propagates cross-modal noise under single-sided or asynchronous forgeries, degrading strict-IoU boundary precision. Existing methods lack a principled scheduler for multimodal asynchrony and a calibrated, temporally local measure of cross-modal discrepancy to steer interaction depth and compute allocation, leaving high-precision localization unreliable precisely where it matters most.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263223/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired video and audio clips (video at 128×128 or 216×120 resolution; audio at 640 Hz or 540 Hz embedding rate), encoded by frozen ViT-S (VideoMAE-initialized) and WavLM backbones into per-token feature sequences. It outputs per-modality temporal intervals—normalized start time, duration, and confidence score—mapped to absolute seconds, plus a cross-modal consistency score derived from the Schrödinger Bridge control energy. The interval-level output provides auditable, time-stamped evidence rather than a single video-level label.

## Method

IaMSB is a three-stage cascaded Schrödinger Bridge (SB) decoder: a Coarse SB (CSB) runs 2 cross-attention steps per modality to propose candidate intervals; a Witness SB (WSB) computes a static entropy-regularized optimal-transport coupling (single Sinkhorn pass) between the two modalities' coarse events, yielding cross-modal statistics (weighted residual, unmatched rate, coupling entropy, interval inconsistency) that drive asymmetric allocation of refinement steps and event tokens via a softmax-gated budget splitter; a Refinement SB (RSB) then iterates step-tunable updates with injected cross-modal witnesses (top-k sparse coupling entries) and shared-KV cross-attention to polish boundaries. The SB formulation replaces explicit noise injection/denoising with a stochastic-control transport objective, so the terminal control energy and minimum step count S*(ε) serve as a calibrated, O(1) consistency score that couples detection confidence with compute budgeting. Training uses a supervised localization loss (EIoU + Huber + BCE + coverage), a margin-based ranking loss on directional uncertainty scores, and a step-value non-increase regularizer that penalizes cross-modal budget imbalance.

## Results

On LAV-DF, IaMSB achieves AP@0.95 of 55.92, surpassing the next-best MMMS-BA (39.02) by 16.9 points and UMMAFormer (37.61) by 18.3 points, while also leading on AP@0.5 (99.33) and all AR metrics (AR@100 = 95.52). On TVIL (visual-only forgery), IaMSB reaches AP@0.95 of 65.62, outperforming UMMAFormer (62.43) and MMMS-BA (28.43). On AV-Deepfake1M, IaMSB attains AP@0.95 of 23.01, more than doubling RegQAV (12.57) and DiMoDif (5.43), while maintaining the highest AR across all proposal budgets (AR@50 = 90.02).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IaMSB outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all three datasets, with the largest margins at strict IoU (AP@0.95). On LAV-DF, the 16.9-point AP@0.95 gap over MMMS-BA (55.92 vs. 39.02) and 18.3-point gap over UMMAFormer (55.92 vs. 37.61) demonstrate that asymmetric, budget-allocated fusion substantially improves boundary precision over symmetric cross-modal attention. On TVIL (visual-only forgery), IaMSB edges out UMMAFormer by 3.2 points at AP@0.95 (65.62 vs. 62.43) and leads all AR metrics, while essentially tying MMMS-BA at AP@0.5 (96.89 vs. 96.87) but pulling ahead sharply at stricter thresholds. On AV-Deepfake1M, IaMSB nearly doubles RegQAV's AP@0.95 (23.01 vs. 12.57) and improves AP@0.90 by 3.2 points (45.15 vs. 41.98), while also achieving the best AR at every proposal budget (e.g., AR@50 = 90.02 vs. 88.14 for RegQAV). Proposal-refine methods (BA-TFD, BA-TFD+) and anchor-free ActionFormer trail significantly at strict IoU, confirming that uniform refinement without cross-modal inconsistency awareness cannot match adaptive step allocation. The consistent pattern—competitive or leading relaxed-IoU scores but decisive wins at AP@0.90/0.95—indicates that IaMSB's step-tuned refinement specifically targets boundary polishing rather than coarse detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Schrödinger Bridge (SB) formulation from De Bortoli et al. (NeurIPS 2021) — originally developed for score-based generative modeling — as a consistency estimator and iterative refinement scheduler for audio-visual deepfake temporal localization. It departs from the dominant symmetric-fusion paradigm (UMMAFormer, MMMS-BA, RegQAV) that applies equal-depth cross-modal attention to both streams, instead using entropy-regularized optimal transport as a lightweight bottleneck to select cross-modal witnesses and allocate refinement steps asymmetrically. The cascaded CSB→WSB→RSB architecture positions SB as a unifying framework for proposal generation, evidence filtering, and boundary polishing, rather than as a generative model requiring explicit noise injection and denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first application of diffusion/SB-style iterative refinement to audio-visual deepfake localization, the paper offers a principled mechanism for handling single-sided and asynchronous forgeries where symmetric fusion degrades. The budget-allocation view (coupling control energy with step count) provides a practical knob for precision-throughput trade-offs, and the O(T) fusion complexity is attractive for long-form video. However, the method's complexity (three cascaded bridges, OT coupling, learned step allocation, witness selection) is substantial relative to the 3–10% AP@0.95 gains, and the SB framing's theoretical depth beyond justifying iterative refinement remains somewhat loose. Open questions include whether per-instance adaptive budgets (flagged as future work) can simplify the architecture, and whether the OT bottleneck generalizes beyond the specific cost function used here.
