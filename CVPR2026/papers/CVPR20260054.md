## Problem

Existing controllable diffusion-based ISR methods adjust the fidelity-realism trade-off in the latent space, requiring a full forward pass through the network (VAE decoder, SD UNet) for every parameter adjustment. This makes interactive exploration of the trade-off computationally prohibitive in practice, as the number of inferences grows linearly with each user adjustment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality RGB image (e.g., 128×128 bicubically upsampled to 512×512) and produces a super-resolved high-quality image. A single scalar parameter γ ∈ [0,1] controls the fidelity-realism trade-off: γ=0 yields maximum fidelity (highest PSNR/SSIM), γ=1 yields maximum realism (highest CLIPIQA/NIQE). Crucially, after one initial inference that produces both the fidelity-specific and realism-specific outputs, any intermediate γ value is obtained by a pixel-level linear blend with zero additional network evaluation.

## Method

IFCSR introduces two specialized one-step diffusion networks (each a pre-trained SD UNet with a rank-4 LoRA adapter) that are linearly combined in the image space: x̂_H = (1−γ)·f_θ(x_L) + γ·f_φ(x_L). A two-stage training scheme first trains the fidelity-specific network (γ=0) with a depth-decaying weighted LPIPS loss (emphasizing shallow VGG features for structural fidelity), then trains the realism-specific network (γ sampled uniformly) with a depth-increasing weighted LPIPS loss (emphasizing deep VGG features for semantic realism) while freezing the fidelity network. The depth-dependent weighting (w_l = k^{L−l} for fidelity, w_l = k^{l−1} for realism, with k=7) is the key loss design that widens the controllable spectrum by forcing each network to specialize in its respective objective. This image-space formulation is what enables inference-free control: the expensive diffusion inference is performed exactly once per specialized network, and all subsequent γ adjustments are free pixel arithmetic.

## Results

On RealSR, IFCSR at γ=0.0 achieves the highest PSNR (28.00 dB) and SSIM (0.7975) among all compared methods, while at γ=1.0 it attains the best NIQE (4.90) and CLIPIQA (0.7011), demonstrating effective fidelity-realism control. On DRealSR, the same pattern holds: γ=0.0 yields the top PSNR (30.86 dB) and SSIM (0.8472), and γ=1.0 achieves the best NIQE (5.70) and CLIPIQA (0.7446). At the balanced setting γ=0.5, IFCSR produces competitive scores across all metrics (e.g., FID 110.60 on RealSR, MUSIQ 69.68), matching or exceeding most one-step diffusion baselines. The method requires only 2 inferences total (one per specialized network) regardless of how many γ values the user explores, versus linearly growing inference counts for latent-space controllable methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary controllable baseline PiSA-SR, IFCSR wins decisively on fidelity at γ=0.0 (PSNR 28.00 vs. 25.50, SSIM 0.7975 vs. 0.7418 on RealSR) and on realism at γ=1.0 (NIQE 4.90 vs. 5.50, CLIPIQA 0.7011 vs. 0.6701), while also offering inference-free control versus PiSA-SR's latent-space interpolation that requires a full UNet forward pass per adjustment. Compared to the strongest one-step fidelity-oriented methods (TVT, OSEDiff), IFCSR at γ=0.0 achieves substantially higher PSNR (28.00 vs. 25.81 for TVT, 25.15 for OSEDiff on RealSR), though TVT retains a slight FID edge at the balanced setting (109.87 vs. 110.60). On the realism side, TSD-SR achieves a marginally higher CLIPIQA (0.7158 vs. 0.7011 on RealSR) but IFCSR surpasses it on NIQE (4.90 vs. 5.13) and matches or exceeds it on MUSIQ (70.29 vs. 71.18). At the balanced γ=0.5 setting, IFCSR is competitive across the board, with FID (110.60) close to TVT's best (109.87) and MUSIQ (69.68) within 1.5 points of TSD-SR (71.18). The key practical distinction is that all γ values are obtained from a single pair of inferences, whereas PiSA-SR, CTSR, and OFTSR require a new network forward pass for each parameter change.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the one-step diffusion ISR architecture of OSEDiff and the dual-LoRA controllable framework of PiSA-SR, both of which use a pre-trained Stable Diffusion UNet with LoRA adapters for LQ-to-HQ transformation. The key departure is moving the fidelity-realism control from the latent space (where prior controllable methods like PiSA-SR, CTSR, and OFTSR require repeated forward passes through the VAE decoder and UNet) to the image space, where a simple linear blend of two specialized outputs suffices. The depth-dependent weighting of LPIPS layers, inspired by the known shallow-to-deep feature hierarchy in VGG, is a targeted extension of the standard LPIPS loss to enforce specialization in each training stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The inference-free control mechanism offers a clear practical advantage: once two one-step inferences are complete, users can sweep the fidelity-realism spectrum at zero additional compute, which is particularly valuable for interactive or deployment settings where latency matters. However, the method is slightly slower than competing one-step methods (0.35 s vs. 0.08–0.20 s) because it must run two networks, and linear blending in image space can produce semi-transparent artifacts when the two outputs differ structurally. Open questions include whether score-distillation losses (e.g., OSEDiff-style VSD) can be more effectively integrated to push realism further, and whether the non-monotonic LPIPS behavior across γ can be resolved without sacrificing the controllable spectrum width.
