## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary object detectors are trained with multimodal supervision but perform inference as a single-shot text-to-region matching routine, creating a mismatch that limits reasoning under visual ambiguity and detection of rare categories. While textual-space optimization (prompt learning, LLM-generated descriptors) has shown promise, current approaches are either static single-step adjustments or rely on heavy LLM-based agents that contradict the efficiency and deployability of object detectors. The paper addresses the need for a lightweight, context-dependent, iterative mechanism that continuously refines textual representations throughout inference without large-model dependencies.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a fixed set of category name prompts as input, but unlike standard OVOD, the text prompt is not consumed in a single forward pass. Instead, the agent iteratively modifies the visual-linguistic context (image region + text) through up to 7 explicit visual operations (color extraction, texture analysis, geometric reasoning, etc.), each triggering an additional detector forward pass. The final output is standard OVOD format—bounding boxes with per-category confidence scores—plus the full reasoning trajectory, with total added latency of 90–155 ms depending on the base detector and trajectory length.

## Method

OVOD-Agent models visual-semantic transitions as a Weakly Markovian Decision Process (w-MDP) over eight compact states, where state and action are unified into a single 'weak Markov unit' z_t = g(c_t, a_t), eliminating the need to enumerate explicit state-action pairs. Seven interpretable visual operators (dictionary lookup, color, texture, background, geometry, lighting, spatial) form the action space, each performing a lightweight deterministic transformation on the image-text context. A UCB-based contextual Bandit drives exploration under limited supervision, collecting diverse trajectories while Dirichlet priors regularize transition estimates. These trajectories and empirical Markov transition matrices jointly train a compact dual-head Reward-Policy Model (20 MB MLP) via a three-term loss combining trajectory distillation, reward reconstruction, and KL-based Markov regularization. At inference, the RM replaces Bandit exploration entirely, selecting the next weak Markov unit via policy-driven, reward-driven, or hybrid decision rules, forming a closed self-evolving loop from exploration to learned policy.

## Results

On LVIS val, OVOD-Agent improves rare-category AP (APr) by +2.7, +2.4, +1.4, and +1.2 for GroundingDINO, YOLO-World, GroundingDINO 1.5, and DINO-X Pro respectively, while overall AP (APall) gains range from +0.5 to +1.2. On LVIS minival, APr improvements are +1.6 to +1.8, and on COCO 2017 val, mAP increases by +0.6 to +1.3. The added inference latency is modest, ranging from +90 ms (YOLO-World) to +155 ms (DINO-X Pro) per image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OVOD-Agent is evaluated as a plug-in module on four representative OVOD backbones: GroundingDINO, YOLO-World, GroundingDINO 1.5, and DINO-X Pro. It consistently outperforms every base detector on the rare-category metric APr, with the largest gain (+2.7) on GroundingDINO (LVIS val) and the smallest (+1.2) on the strongest baseline DINO-X Pro. Overall AP (APall) also improves in all cases (+0.5 to +1.2), confirming that the agent does not sacrifice common-category performance. On COCO 2017 val, where categories are more balanced, mAP gains are smaller (+0.6 to +1.3), indicating the benefit is most pronounced for long-tail recognition. The latency overhead scales with trajectory length, ranging from +90 ms (YOLO-World) to +155 ms (DINO-X Pro), which the authors note is acceptable given the consistent accuracy gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the growing trend of LLM-centric OVOD agents (CoT-PL, LLM-generated descriptors) by proposing a fully LLM-free alternative grounded in a Weakly Markovian Decision Process over eight discrete visual states. It repurposes ideas from contextual bandit exploration (UCB) and Markov transition modeling—previously applied in control and NLP—into a closed-loop mechanism where Bandit trajectories train a lightweight Reward-Policy Model that replaces online exploration at inference. Conceptually, it extends the Visual-CoT paradigm from continuous, LLM-driven reasoning to a discrete, interpretable sequence of seven primitive visual operators (color, texture, geometry, etc.), positioning itself between static prompt-matching detectors and heavyweight multimodal agents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment gap: OVOD detectors trained with multimodal supervision still perform unimodal inference, and prior fixes (LLM agents, multi-round feedback) are too heavy for real-time detection. OVOD-Agent's <100 ms overhead and <20 MB memory footprint make it a drop-in module for existing backbones, and its consistent rare-category gains (+1.2 to +2.7 APr) on both COCO and LVIS validate the approach. However, the authors acknowledge failure on non-canonical object states (e.g., dried apricot) and tiny occluded objects, leaving open whether the eight-state w-MDP is expressive enough for truly open-world reasoning or whether richer visual priors are needed.
