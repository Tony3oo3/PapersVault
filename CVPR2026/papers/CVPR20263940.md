## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based SLAM systems assume strict photometric consistency across views, but real-world cameras use auto-exposure (AE) to adjust brightness per frame, breaking this assumption. Prior approaches either use per-image appearance embeddings (which cannot model view-independent exposure effects) or per-Gaussian radiance-to-color CRF mapping (which couples exposure with rendering, degrading quality and increasing cost). Additionally, handcrafted feature-based localization (e.g., ORB-SLAM3) is fragile under illumination variations, further degrading both pose estimation and mapping accuracy.

## Input / Output

The system consumes stereo RGB image sequences (captured with a ZED 2i stereo camera or brightness-augmented EuRoC MAV frames) and produces a camera pose trajectory, a 3D Gaussian splatting map, per-frame exposure time estimates (in milliseconds), and both RGB and scene radiance map renderings for novel views. A distinguishing output is the recovered camera response function (CRF) curve, which enables post-hoc exposure-controlled re-rendering of the same scene at arbitrary exposure times without re-optimizing the Gaussian map.

## Method

AERGS-SLAM adopts a decoupled two-thread architecture: a localization thread using AirSLAM's learning-based illumination-robust features for bundle adjustment, and a mapping thread performing 3DGS optimization. The key architectural contribution is the Camera Exposure Network (CEN), which replaces HDR-GS's per-Gaussian CRF mapping with three independent MLPs that operate on the fully rendered per-image radiance map, decoupling exposure from rendering so that computational cost depends on image resolution rather than Gaussian count. The CEN models the CRF in the log-radiance domain following Debevec-Malik calibration, enabling a single forward pass to convert any rendered radiance map to an exposure-controlled RGB image. A time-aware sliding window coarse-to-fine strategy assigns older keyframes smaller downsampling scales (high-frequency supervision) and newer keyframes larger scales (low-frequency supervision), dynamically adapting the optimization to the temporal maturity of each frame within the window.

## Results

On the processed EuRoC MAV dataset (with simulated AE-induced brightness variations), AERGS-SLAM achieves the best localization RMSE among all 3DGS-based SLAM methods, with values of 0.021–0.215 m across six sequences, outperforming Photo-SLAM (0.029–1.001 m) and SEGS-SLAM (0.037–0.288 m) on most sequences. For photorealistic mapping, AERGS-SLAM achieves the highest PSNR (19.92–23.55 dB on EuRoC; 18.23–21.60 dB on self-collected) and lowest LPIPS (0.204–0.352 on EuRoC), surpassing MonoGS, Photo-SLAM, SEGS-SLAM, and its own HDR-GS variant. On the self-collected stereo dataset, AERGS-SLAM achieves RMSE of 0.132–0.523 m, significantly outperforming all baselines (e.g., 0.132 m vs. 2.473–3.633 m for SEGS-SLAM on sequences S4–S6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the coupled 3DGS-SLAM baseline MonoGS [26], AERGS-SLAM wins on every sequence where both methods run, with RMSE reductions of up to 25× (e.g., 0.132 m vs. 16.975 m on S4) and PSNR gains of 4–9 dB in mapping. Against the decoupled baselines Photo-SLAM [14] and SEGS-SLAM [37], AERGS-SLAM achieves lower RMSE on all sequences where both run (e.g., 0.021 vs. 0.029 m on MH01; 0.132 vs. 2.473 m on S4) and higher PSNR (up to 23.55 vs. 11.34 dB on V102), demonstrating that learning-based illumination-robust features and the CEN jointly outperform handcrafted ORB features and appearance embeddings. Against the classic ORB-SLAM3 [3], AERGS-SLAM is more robust (no running failures) and achieves lower RMSE on all comparable sequences. Against DROID-SLAM [34], AERGS-SLAM is slightly less accurate on EuRoC (e.g., 0.021 vs. 0.012 m on MH01) but DROID-SLAM does not produce photorealistic maps and has no results on the self-collected dataset; AERGS-SLAM's advantage is the joint localization + exposure-controlled mapping. Against AirSLAM [38], AERGS-SLAM achieves comparable or slightly better RMSE (e.g., 0.021 vs. 0.022 m on MH01) while additionally providing high-quality 3DGS-based photorealistic mapping, which AirSLAM does not offer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decoupled 3DGS-based SLAM framework of Photo-SLAM by replacing its ORB-SLAM3 localization with AirSLAM's learning-based illumination-robust features, and by introducing a Camera Exposure Network (CEN) that decouples the camera's CRF-based exposure mapping from the Gaussian splatting rendering process. The CEN is a direct architectural departure from HDR-GS, which maps per-Gaussian radiance to color (coupling exposure with rendering), whereas AERGS-SLAM maps the entire per-image radiance map to RGB via a lightweight MLP, inheriting the logarithmic-domain CRF formulation from Debevec & Malik and the unit-exposure loss from HDR-NeRF. The paper positions itself as the first decoupled 3DGS-SLAM to jointly address both illumination-robust localization and exposure-controlled photorealistic mapping, filling a gap left by MonoGS (simple brightness parameters) and SEGS-SLAM (view-dependent appearance embeddings that cannot capture view-independent AE effects).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Auto-exposure is ubiquitous in real-world camera systems, yet most 3DGS-based SLAM methods assume photometric consistency, making them unreliable outside controlled environments. The CEN's decoupling of exposure from rendering yields a ~10× speedup over HDR-GS for exposure-controlled synthesis (3700 vs. 416 FPS), making exposure-aware Gaussian splatting practical for real-time applications. However, evaluation is limited to brightness-scaled EuRoC sequences and six short self-collected stereo sequences, leaving open questions about robustness to more complex AE behaviors (white-balance shifts, gain staging, HDR bracketing) and scalability to large-scale or dynamic scenes. The reliance on DROID-SLAM trajectories as ground truth for the self-collected dataset also introduces a circularity concern in localization evaluation.
