## Problem

RL-based fine-tuning of diffusion models suffers from poor generalization across models, limited composability, and susceptibility to reward hacking. Existing prompt-refinement alternatives avoid weight modification but operate in a feed-forward manner, applying a single refined prompt uniformly across all denoising timesteps and thus failing to exploit the sequential, state-dependent structure of the diffusion sampling process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261402/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The MLLM policy receives as input the current denoised latent estimate (converted from the noisy state x_t via the diffusion model's noise prediction), the user's original query, the previously refined prompt, and the timestep index. It outputs a refined text prompt that is injected into the next denoising step of the frozen diffusion model. At inference, the policy can generate all timestep-specific refined prompts a priori in a single pass, eliminating the need for intermediate visual feedback and keeping the generation loop unmodified.

## Method

PromptLoop formulates prompt refinement as a T-step MDP structurally analogous to diffusion RL: the state is (x_t, c_t, q, t), the action is the refined prompt c_{t-1}, the transition is a frozen diffusion model step, and the terminal reward is r(x_0, q). A multimodal LLM serves as the policy, trained with GRPO (group-normalized advantage estimation) to maximize the reward without any gradient flow through the diffusion model or reward model. A sparse refinement strategy applies the policy at only N_R selected timesteps rather than every step, reducing inference overhead to ~23% relative increase. A key design choice is feeding denoised latent estimates (closer to the data manifold) rather than raw noisy states to the policy, and the paper shows that once trained, the policy generalizes to unseen diffusion backbones and composes orthogonally with existing alignment methods like DDPO, ReFL, and Diffusion-DPO without retraining.

## Results

On SDXL with ImageReward as the training signal, PromptLoop achieves an ImageReward score of 1.0948, outperforming the vanilla SDXL baseline (0.7244) and all prompt-refinement baselines including RePrompt (1.0148), ReFL (1.0119), GPT-5 (0.6299), and Qwen2.5-VL-3B (0.5114). On SD1.5, PromptLoop reaches 0.6320 ImageReward versus 0.0816 for the unmodified model, surpassing DDPO (0.6051) and ReFL (0.6248). The method also improves secondary metrics: Aesthetics rises from 6.073 to 6.583 on SDXL and from 5.458 to 5.853 on SD1.5, while MLLM Score increases from 0.735 to 0.764 (SDXL) and 0.675 to 0.725 (SD1.5). Inference overhead is minimal, adding only 5–23% runtime (15.0 s → 18.4 s for 5 refinement steps on SDXL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PromptLoop outperforms all direct prompt-refinement baselines on the target ImageReward metric: it beats RePrompt (1.0948 vs. 1.0148 on SDXL; 0.6320 vs. 0.4344 on SD1.5), ReFL (1.0948 vs. 1.0119; 0.6320 vs. 0.6248), GPT-5 (1.0948 vs. 0.6299; 0.6320 vs. −0.0950), and Qwen2.5-VL-3B (1.0948 vs. 0.5114; 0.6320 vs. −0.1720), with the largest margins over the LLM-based prompt refiners. Against DDPO on SD1.5, PromptLoop wins on ImageReward (0.6320 vs. 0.6051), Aesthetics (5.853 vs. 5.562), and MLLM Score (0.725 vs. 0.693), though DDPO retains a slight edge on HPSv2 (0.2726 vs. 0.2701). The key differentiator is orthogonality: when stacked on top of Diffusion-DPO, DDPO, DanceGRPO, ReFL, or NPNet, PromptLoop consistently adds 0.2–0.5 ImageReward points (e.g., SDXL+Diffusion-DPO+PromptLoop reaches 1.2898 vs. 0.9921 for Diffusion-DPO alone; SD1.5+DDPO+PromptLoop reaches 0.9842 vs. 0.6051). Generalizability is demonstrated by applying a policy trained on SDXL to SD1.5 (0.4546 vs. 0.0816 baseline) and vice versa (1.0859 vs. 0.7244), confirming that no retraining is needed for unseen backbones. On the composite-reward task (Table 2, SDXL-turbo), PromptLoop also leads RePrompt on ImageReward (0.8516 vs. 0.7876) and HPSv2 (0.2938 vs. 0.2912) while maintaining competitive GenEval scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

PromptLoop sits at the intersection of two research threads: RL-based fine-tuning of diffusion models (DDPO, ReFL, Diffusion-DPO, DanceGRPO) and prompt refinement for text-to-image generation (RePrompt, PromptEnhancer, test-time prompt optimization). It departs from the first by treating the diffusion model as a frozen environment rather than an optimization target, and from the second by replacing feed-forward or post-hoc prompt updates with a closed-loop, timestep-aware policy that conditions on intermediate latent states. The MDP formulation explicitly mirrors diffusion RL (state = latent + prompt + timestep, action = refined prompt, reward = final image score), but the policy is an MLLM trained with GRPO rather than the diffusion network itself, preserving plug-and-play modularity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, weight-agnostic design means a single trained policy can be applied across different diffusion backbones and stacked orthogonally on top of existing alignment methods without retraining, addressing the generalization and composability limitations of direct RL fine-tuning. The finding that intermediate visual feedback is unnecessary at inference time (the policy can pre-generate all refined prompts) makes integration into existing pipelines trivial with only ~5–23% overhead. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video diffusion or multi-reward settings with conflicting objectives, whether the MLLM policy can be distilled into a lighter model, and how robust the method is to adversarially designed reward functions beyond the tested set.
