## Problem

Existing video agentic models rely on dense frame parsing (0.2–2 FPS) and expensive preprocessing to build video databases or multi-granular descriptions, a cost that scales poorly with video length. The paper observes that over 80% of LVBench questions can be answered by inspecting less than 5% of the original video, indicating that exhaustive parsing is largely unnecessary. VideoSeek addresses this by leveraging the video's inherent temporal and causal logic flow to actively seek only answer-critical evidence, rather than greedily parsing the full video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261744/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a video, a natural-language query, and optionally a subtitle stream, and produces a text answer (multiple-choice or free-form). The video is never loaded in full; instead, it is accessed on demand through three tools at different temporal granularities, with the total frame budget typically ranging from ~16 to ~112 frames depending on the benchmark and subtitle availability. GPT-5 serves as both the reasoning LLM and the visual interpreter for sampled frames, and the maximum reasoning trajectory is capped at 20 think-act-observe turns.

## Method

VideoSeek operates in a ReAct-style think-act-observe loop where a thinking LLM reasons over the full conversation history (query, past thoughts, actions, and observations) to plan the next tool call, rather than relying on a prebuilt video database or a fixed memory buffer. The toolkit comprises three multi-granular tools: <overview> uniformly samples 16α frames across the entire timeline to build a coarse storyline; <skim> samples 4α frames from a candidate segment to check for query-relevant evidence at low cost; and <focus> inspects a short clip (≤4α seconds) at 1 FPS for fine-grained, answer-critical details. The agent adaptively selects and sequences these tools based on accumulated observations, progressively narrowing the search space from the full video to specific answer-critical intervals. This logic-flow-guided navigation allows VideoSeek to achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on four benchmarks while processing 76–96% fewer frames than its GPT-5 base model and 99%+ fewer frames than prior video agents like DVD.

## Results

On LVBench (w/ subtitles), VideoSeek achieves 76.7% accuracy using only 27.2 frames on average, a +10.2 point improvement over its base model GPT-5 (66.5% with 384 frames) while using 93% fewer frames. On Video-MME (long, w/ sub), it reaches 81.2% with 15.9 frames, and on LongVideoBench (long) it scores 73.5% with 29.6 frames. On the complex reasoning benchmark Video-Holmes, VideoSeek attains 47.3% overall accuracy with 42.7 frames, surpassing Gemini 2.5 Pro (45.0% with 185.1 frames) and GPT-5 (44.1% with 384 frames).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoSeek outperforms all 16 baselines across every benchmark it is evaluated on. Against its base model GPT-5 (384 frames), VideoSeek gains +8.3 pts on LVBench (w/o sub), +10.2 pts on LVBench (w/ sub), +2.2 pts on Video-MME (w/o sub), +3.1 pts on Video-MME (w/ sub), +9.0 pts on LongVideoBench, and +3.2 pts on Video-Holmes, while using 76–96% fewer frames. Among video agents, the closest competitor is DVD (8,074 frames on LVBench), which scores 74.2% (w/o sub) and 76.0% (w/ sub); VideoSeek matches or exceeds DVD (76.7% w/ sub) while consuming only 0.3% of DVD's frame budget. On Video-MME (w/ sub), VideoSeek (81.2%, 15.9 frames) surpasses DrVideo (71.7%, 493.2 frames) by nearly 10 points with 97% fewer frames. On Video-Holmes, VideoSeek (47.3%, 42.7 frames) beats Gemini 2.5 Pro (45.0%, 185.1 frames) by 2.3 points with 77% fewer frames, and outperforms all RL-trained video models (Video-R1 36.5%, VideoChat-R1 33.0%, SEED-Bench-R1 33.5%) by 11–14 points. The only setting where VideoSeek does not achieve the absolute top score is LVBench (w/o sub), where DVD leads by 5.8 points (74.2% vs 68.4%), but DVD requires 87× more frames.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoSeek extends the ReAct think-act-observe paradigm (Yao et al., ICLR 2022) to the video domain, introducing a multi-granular toolkit (overview/skim/focus) that mirrors human viewing strategies. It departs sharply from prior video agents (DVD, DrVideo, MR.Video, VideoAgent, VideoTree) that either densely parse videos at 0.2–2 FPS or build pre-structured video databases before answering; instead, VideoSeek reasons over accumulating observations in a single conversation history without any prebuilt index. The conceptual departure is framing video QA as a long-horizon evidence-seeking problem guided by 'video logic flow' (temporal/causal structure) rather than a single-pass or retrieval-over-database task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic efficiency gain—93% fewer frames while improving accuracy by 10+ points on LVBench—challenges the prevailing assumption that dense frame coverage is necessary for strong video understanding, and the model-agnostic design makes the framework broadly applicable to any capable LMM. A key open question is generalizability to tasks where decisive evidence is genuinely unpredictable (anomaly detection, rare events), which the authors acknowledge as a limitation of logic-driven navigation. Future work could explore adaptive strategies that blend logic-guided seeking with stochastic or attention-based scanning for unexpected content, and investigate whether the approach transfers to streaming or real-time video settings.
