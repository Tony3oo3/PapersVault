## Problem

Existing progressive point cloud denoising methods enforce fixed iterative pipelines that process all spatial regions uniformly, causing redundant computation and over-smoothing in low-noise areas while under-refining heavily corrupted regions. No prior framework adapts the denoising path per local patch based on its actual noise characteristics, leaving a gap between denoising quality and computational efficiency for point clouds with non-uniform noise distributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260357/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy point cloud (tested at 10K and 50K points with 1%–3% Gaussian noise) and decomposes it into overlapping local patches of 1000 points each via Farthest Point Sampling and KNN search. Each patch independently traverses a variable-length subsequence of the L=4 denoising modules, so different patches in the same cloud may undergo different numbers of refinement steps. The final output is a globally assembled denoised point cloud approximating the clean ground truth.

## Method

DSNet introduces a normal-similarity-based noise discriminator (built on EdgeConv layers) that computes a per-patch similarity factor ρ from the quartic angular deviation between clean and noisy surface normals, then maps ρ through a reverse-monotonic logarithmic decision function λ(ρ) to a discrete module index, routing noisier patches to earlier aggressive stages and cleaner patches to later fine-tuning stages. A path-selective iteration mechanism re-evaluates ρ after every denoising step and re-plans the next module, enabling non-sequential cross-stage skipping (e.g., Net₂→Net₅→Net₇→Net_L) rather than a rigid cascade. Each denoising module is a U-Net with Neighborhood Attention Aggregation in the encoder and multi-head cross-attention in the decoder for multi-scale feature fusion. Training uses progressively noise-reduced intermediate ground truths (σᵢ = σᵢ₋₁/γ) with a composite loss combining Chamfer distance and a displacement-coherence term at every stage, and the discriminator is pretrained independently before joint optimization.

## Results

On the PU-Net dataset, DSNet achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance (CD) and Point-to-Mesh (P2M) errors across all tested configurations (10K/50K points, 1%–2.5% Gaussian noise). At the most challenging setting (50K points, 2.5% noise), DSNet attains CD 0.762 and P2M 0.514 (×10⁻⁴), outperforming the next-best method ASDN (CD 0.850, P2M 0.575) by 10.4% and 10.8% respectively. On the real-scanned Kinect dataset, DSNet is competitive (CD 1.040, P2M 0.885) though slightly behind P2P-Bridge (CD 0.974, P2M 0.854), indicating room for cross-domain improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the synthetic PU-Net benchmark, DSNet outperforms all six baselines (Score, PD-Flow, IterativePFN, P2P-Bridge, ASDN, 3DMambaIPF) on both CD and P2M across every resolution and noise level. The margin is most pronounced at higher noise: at 50K/2.5%, DSNet's CD of 0.762 beats ASDN (0.850) by 10.4% and 3DMambaIPF (0.928) by 17.9%. Among progressive methods, IterativePFN (the closest architectural predecessor) is surpassed by DSNet by 25.3% CD at 50K/2.5% (0.762 vs 1.015), demonstrating the benefit of dynamic path selection over fixed sequential refinement. On the real-world Kinect dataset, DSNet is slightly behind P2P-Bridge (CD 1.040 vs 0.974; P2M 0.885 vs 0.854) and IterativePFN (CD 1.040 vs 0.993), though it remains within ~7% of the best, suggesting the synthetic-to-real gap is a shared limitation rather than a method-specific weakness. Single-stage methods (Score, PD-Flow) degrade substantially at higher noise and resolution, confirming that progressive refinement is necessary but that adaptive routing further improves upon fixed-path progressive designs.

## Relation to Prior Work

DSNet builds directly on IterativePFN's progressive denoising framework, which introduced intermediate noise-reduced ground truths for each refinement stage, but departs from its fixed sequential pipeline by introducing per-patch dynamic routing. The noise discriminator leverages DGCNN's EdgeConv layers for local geometric feature extraction, while the U-Net denoising modules employ Neighborhood Attention Aggregation and multi-head cross-attention for multi-scale refinement. Conceptually, the work extends the progressive denoising paradigm from a uniform 'all patches, all stages' schedule to an adaptive, on-demand allocation of computation, drawing on the intuition that surface normal deviations serve as a reliable proxy for local noise intensity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamic routing mechanism addresses a practical inefficiency in progressive denoising—redundant computation on already-clean regions and over-smoothing of fine geometric details—making it relevant for real-time 3D perception pipelines where latency matters. However, the gains on real-world scanned data (Kinect, Paris-Rue-Madame) are marginal or even slightly negative compared to some baselines, raising open questions about cross-domain generalization of the normal-similarity metric under non-Gaussian, sensor-specific noise. Future work could explore whether the routing strategy transfers to other progressive tasks (e.g., completion, upsampling) or whether learned (rather than hand-designed) decision functions could improve robustness.
