## Problem

State-of-the-art text-to-image models suffer from an 'identity crisis' when generating multi-human scenes: they produce duplicate faces, merge distinct identities, and miscount individuals, even when overall image quality is high. No prior method explicitly optimizes for human identity diversity both within a single image and across groups of generated samples for the same prompt, leaving a critical gap for applications like group photo personalization, consistent character generation, and social simulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260119/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing a multi-human scene (2–7 people, optionally with per-person attributes like clothing or hairstyle) and produces a single RGB image containing the specified number of visually distinct individuals. Training requires no real data—only 30,000 GPT-5-generated captions of group scenes—and the fine-tuned flow-matching model (FLUX-Dev or Krea-Dev) is evaluated by generating 8–16 samples per prompt to assess both intra-image and cross-sample identity diversity. The approach is model-agnostic, applicable to any flow-matching backbone.

## Method

DISCO fine-tunes flow-matching text-to-image models via Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a four-component compositional reward: (i) intra-image diversity penalizing maximum pairwise ArcFace cosine similarity among detected faces, (ii) a novel group-wise counterfactual 'remove-one' reward that measures each image's contribution to overall group identity spread and discourages cross-sample identity repetition, (iii) a binary count-accuracy term enforcing the exact prompted person count, and (iv) a normalized HPSv3 score preserving perceptual quality and prompt alignment. The key insight is that optimizing intra-image diversity alone causes global diversity collapse—duplicate identities simply migrate across samples rather than being eliminated—necessitating the group-relative term. A single-stage curriculum anneals the prompt distribution from simple (2–4 people) to uniform over the full range, which is critical for specialist models. The framework also regularizes against reward hacking (face undercounting, grid artifacts) through the count and quality terms, and generalizes beyond faces to clothing and body-type diversity as a byproduct.

## Results

On the DiverseHumans benchmark (2–7 people), DISCO(Flux) achieves 98.6% Unique Face Accuracy, 98.3% Global Identity Spread, and 92.4% Count Accuracy, surpassing GPT-Image-1 (85.1% UFA, 89.8% GIS, 90.5% Count Acc.) and all open-source baselines by wide margins. On MultiHuman-TestBench (1–5 people), DISCO(Flux) reaches 94.3% UFA and 88.7% GIS versus 83.7% and 81.0% for GPT-Image-1. HPSv2 quality scores remain competitive (33.4 for DISCO(Flux) vs. 33.4 for GPT-Image-1), confirming that identity-diversity gains do not come at the cost of perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DISCO(Flux) decisively outperforms all open-source baselines on identity-diversity metrics: its 98.6% UFA on DiverseHumans exceeds the best open-source model (SD3.5 at 69.1%) by nearly 30 points, and its 98.3% GIS dwarfs the next-best (SD3.5 at 72.5%). Against proprietary systems, DISCO(Flux) beats GPT-Image-1 on UFA (98.6% vs. 85.1%) and GIS (98.3% vs. 89.8%) while matching its HPS score (33.4 vs. 33.4), and it surpasses Gemini-Nanobanana on every metric (e.g., 98.6% vs. 57.2% UFA). On MultiHuman-TestBench, the same pattern holds: DISCO(Flux) leads with 94.3% UFA and 88.7% GIS versus GPT-Image-1's 83.7% and 81.0%. The specialist variant DISCO(Krea) also substantially improves over its Krea-Dev baseline (45.8% → 89.7% UFA on DiverseHumans) and remains competitive with GPT-Image-1 on GIS (90.6% vs. 89.8%). Notably, HPS quality and MLLM Action scores are maintained or slightly improved over the respective Flux/Krea baselines, indicating that identity-diversity optimization does not degrade general generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DISCO builds directly on Flow-GRPO [19], adopting its MDP formulation of the denoising process, SDE-based exploration, and group-relative advantage computation as the RL backbone. It departs from prior RL-for-image-generation work (which targeted prompt faithfulness, aesthetics, or human preference scores) by introducing identity diversity as an explicit, compositional optimization objective at both the intra-image and cross-sample levels. The group-relative counterfactual reward for cross-sample diversity is a novel mechanism not present in earlier GRPO applications, motivated by the paper's key observation that intra-image diversity alone causes duplicate identities to silently migrate across samples rather than being eliminated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a persistent and visually obvious failure mode in SOTA text-to-image models, and the finding that intra-image diversity optimization paradoxically worsens global identity spread is a useful cautionary result for anyone applying RL to diversity objectives. The characterization of reward hacking (face undercounting, grid artifacts) and the explicit regularization terms to counter them provide practical guidance for the broader RL-for-generation community. Open questions include whether the approach extends to non-human subjects, video generation, and personalized/consistent character generation, all of which the authors flag as future directions. A limitation is the dependence on face-specific tooling (RetinaFace, ArcFace), which constrains generality beyond human identity.
