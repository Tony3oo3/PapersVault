## Problem

Caching-based acceleration methods that exploit redundant intermediate computations work well for 2D image and video diffusion, but directly applying them to 3D diffusion models causes severe geometric inconsistencies because small numerical errors in cached latent features accumulate into structural artifacts and topological failures. No existing caching strategy accounts for the unique voxel stabilization dynamics inherent to 3D geometry synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263381/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (processed through DINOv2) as conditioning input and accelerates the sparse structure generation stage of TRELLIS/DSO, producing a set of active voxel coordinates that define the 3D object's geometry. The latent feature grid S_t ∈ R^{B×C×D×H×W} is decoded at each step into a binary occupancy grid to track voxel-level changes, and the final output is a 3D mesh normalized into a unit cube for evaluation via Chamfer Distance and F-Score.

## Method

Fast3Dcache is a training-free, three-phase caching pipeline built on two synergistic modules. The Predictive Caching Scheduler Constraint (PCSC) models the empirically observed log-linear decay of dynamic voxels during denoising, calibrating a one-time anchor at the end of Phase 1 to predict a time-varying cache quota c_t that adapts to geometric stability. The Spatiotemporal Stability Criterion (SSC) then selects which specific tokens to cache by computing a weighted score combining normalized velocity magnitude (update intensity) and acceleration magnitude (instantaneous caching error), ranking tokens so that only the least stable subset is recomputed through the Flow Transformer's self-attention. Phase 2 enforces periodic full-refresh steps every τ timesteps to eliminate accumulated approximation error, while Phase 3 (after CFG is disabled) switches to a fixed aggressive caching ratio ξ with a periodic full-correction cycle every f_corr steps.

## Results

On the Toys4K dataset (852 image prompts), Fast3Dcache (τ=8) achieves a 27.12% throughput increase (0.5055→0.6426 iters/s) and 54.83% FLOPs reduction (244.2T→110.3T) on TRELLIS, with only a 2.48% increase in Chamfer Distance (0.0686→0.0703) and 1.95% drop in F-Score (54.82→53.75). On DSO, the same configuration yields 16.45% speedup and 52.74% FLOPs reduction with 2.47% CD increase and 2.35% F-Score drop. When combined with TeaCache, the joint method reaches 3.41× speedup (1.74 iters/s) while actually improving F-Score over TeaCache alone (53.94 vs. 53.56).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla TRELLIS and DSO, Fast3Dcache (τ=8) delivers 27.12% and 16.45% throughput gains respectively, with FLOPs reduced by over 50%, while geometric quality degrades by less than 2.5% in both CD and F-Score. Compared to RAS (a 2D region-adaptive caching method adapted to 3D), Fast3Dcache is the clear winner on quality: RAS at 25% sampling ratio causes a catastrophic 26.53% F-Score drop on TRELLIS (54.82→40.28) and 15.20% on DSO, whereas Fast3Dcache maintains F-Score within 2% of the unaccelerated baseline at comparable or higher speed. On the DSO framework specifically, Fast3Dcache (τ=8) achieves 16.45% speedup versus RAS's 24.17%, but with a 12.85-point F-Score advantage (53.55 vs. 46.50), demonstrating that geometry-aware caching is essential for 3D. In the complementarity experiment (Table 2), Fast3Dcache combined with TeaCache reaches 3.41× speedup (vs. 2.84× for TeaCache alone) and improves F-Score from 53.56 to 53.94, confirming that the two methods address orthogonal sources of redundancy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of training-free feature caching for diffusion model acceleration (DeepCache, TeaCache, RAS, BlockDance) from 2D images and video to 3D geometry synthesis, explicitly arguing that pixel-level redundancy exploitation does not transfer to voxel-level geometric generation. It builds directly on the TRELLIS sparse-voxel Flow Transformer architecture and its variant DSO, targeting the structure generation stage. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of geometry-aware scheduling (PCSC) and token selection (SSC) grounded in the observed three-phase voxel stabilization pattern, rather than the perceptual similarity metrics used in 2D/video caching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, training-free speedup (up to 27% throughput, 55% FLOPs reduction) for 3D diffusion inference with minimal geometric quality loss, which is valuable given the high computational cost of current 3D generation pipelines. The quantified failure of 2D caching methods on 3D (26.5% F-Score drop) serves as an important cautionary result for the community. Limitations include evaluation on a single framework family (TRELLIS/DSO), a single dataset (Toys4K, 71 objects), and only the structure generation stage; whether the stabilization pattern and caching strategy generalize to other 3D architectures (e.g., Gaussian splatting, mesh-based) or to the texture generation stage remains open.
