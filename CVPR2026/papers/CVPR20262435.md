## Problem

Flow-GRPO applies GRPO to flow-matching models but assigns a single uniform advantage—computed from the final image reward—to every denoising step in a trajectory. This ignores the temporal hierarchy of diffusion generation (early steps determine composition and layout, late steps resolve textures) and can inadvertently reinforce suboptimal intermediate steps whose errors are later corrected, as evidenced by non-monotonic intermediate reward trajectories that frequently dip and recover.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image via SD3.5-Medium using 10 denoising steps (cfg=1.0 for training, cfg=4.5 for final evaluation). During training, each prompt generates N=16 trajectories that share the same initial noise, and intermediate clean-image estimates are computed at every denoising step using T'=5 ODE substeps from each noisy state. Reward models (PickScore, ImageReward, UnifiedReward-7b) score both the final images and the intermediate Tweedie estimates, with all T reward estimates per trajectory computed in parallel.

## Method

Stepwise-Flow-GRPO replaces the uniform final-reward advantage with per-step gain-based advantages: at each denoising step t, it estimates the clean image x̂₀(t) via Tweedie's formula (or T'=5 ODE substeps for higher quality), computes a reward r_t = R(x̂₀(t), prompt), and defines the stepwise gain g_t = r_{t-1} − r_t measuring that step's marginal reward improvement. These gains telescope (Σg_t = r_0 − r_T), so maximizing them is equivalent to maximizing final reward improvement, while joint group-relative normalization across all steps and trajectories preserves the larger magnitudes of early compositional gains. The policy is updated with a clipped GRPO objective using these stepwise advantages plus a KL penalty against the reference policy. Additionally, the paper introduces a DDIM-inspired SDE that interpolates between deterministic flow ODE and stochastic sampling with a noise schedule σ_t = η(t−Δt)√(1−t), producing visually cleaner samples for the reward model while preserving the stochasticity needed for policy gradients and matching the flow-ODE Fokker-Planck marginal in the Taylor limit.

## Results

On GenEval (the primary compositional benchmark), Stepwise-Flow-GRPO achieves an overall score of 0.71 at cfg=4.5, outperforming Flow-GRPO (0.68) and the base SD3.5-M model (0.63). The gains are most pronounced in counting (0.70 vs. 0.64) and spatial positioning (0.29 vs. 0.24). In terms of sample efficiency, Stepwise-Flow-GRPO reaches higher final-image rewards per training step than Flow-GRPO across all four reward-function settings (PickScore, ImageReward, UnifiedReward on GenEval; PickScore on its own dataset), achieving visibly superior performance in 3 of 4 settings while also converging faster in wall-clock time despite the extra cost of intermediate denoising.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is Flow-GRPO [Liu et al., 2025], the prior state-of-the-art RL fine-tuning method for flow-matching models. At cfg=1.0, Stepwise-Flow-GRPO matches Flow-GRPO on overall GenEval score (0.60 vs. 0.60) with a slight edge on two-object composition (0.75 vs. 0.73) but a marginal loss on attribute binding (0.34 vs. 0.35). At cfg=4.5, Stepwise-Flow-GRPO clearly outperforms Flow-GRPO: overall 0.71 vs. 0.68, with notable gains in counting (0.70 vs. 0.64, a 9.4% relative improvement) and spatial positioning (0.29 vs. 0.24, a 20.8% relative improvement), while tying on single-object, colors, and attribute-binding sub-tasks. In sample-efficiency curves (Figs. 4–6), Stepwise-Flow-GRPO reaches higher rewards at every training step across all four reward-function configurations and converges faster in wall-clock time in 3 of 4 settings, even after accounting for the additional compute of intermediate Tweedie denoising. The advantage persists when both methods use the improved DDIM-inspired SDE, confirming that the credit-assignment and sampling improvements are complementary rather than redundant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Flow-GRPO [26], which first applied GRPO-style policy gradients to flow-matching models by converting the deterministic ODE into a marginal-matching SDE, by replacing its uniform credit assignment (one advantage per trajectory) with per-step gain-based advantages computed via Tweedie's formula. The theoretical grounding draws on Kveton et al. [23]'s adaptive submodular policy optimization, generalized here to the off-policy, group-relative setting of GRPO. The DDIM-inspired SDE [43] is adapted to the rectified-flow parameterization to produce cleaner intermediate samples for reward evaluation while preserving the stochasticity needed for policy gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stepwise credit assignment addresses a fundamental inefficiency in RL fine-tuning of diffusion/flow models: uniform advantages conflate early compositional decisions with late detail refinement, wasting gradient signal on steps that merely correct earlier mistakes. The consistent sample-efficiency gains across four reward/dataset configurations and the complementary DDIM-SDE improvement make this a practical recipe for faster RL training of flow models. Open questions include whether the submodularity-like structure (suggested by diminishing gains) can be formally verified for common reward models, whether the approach extends to longer denoising schedules or larger models, and how per-step gain variance might drive curriculum learning or self-correcting diffusion.
