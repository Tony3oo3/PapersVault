## Problem

Existing activation steering methods for VLM jailbreak defense inject a fixed refusal direction into all hidden states, which shifts benign activations alongside harmful ones and causes over-refusal on safe queries. Moreover, these methods lack a theoretical mechanism to guarantee that steering affects only adversarial representations, limiting both robustness and interpretability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264023/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, NullSteer takes a standard RGB image and text prompt, encodes them into the VLM's shared embedding space, and applies a per-layer linear transformation to the LLM decoder's hidden states (layer 20 for 13B models, layer 14 for 7B models) before autoregressive decoding. Constructing the steering transformation requires a small calibration set of benign image-text pairs (N_b ≈ 8 suffices) and malicious adversarial examples (N_m ≈ 100) to estimate the null-space projection and refusal target, but no gradient-based training of the VLM itself is needed.

## Method

NullSteer collects benign hidden activations into a matrix H_b and computes a projection matrix P onto the null space of H_b via SVD of H_b H_b^T, ensuring P·H_b = 0. The steering transformation is then factorized as Δ = Δ̃P, so that any update applied to a benign activation vanishes identically while harmful components outside the benign subspace are redirected. The unconstrained matrix Δ̃ is solved in closed form by minimizing a composite objective that (i) aligns steered malicious activations to a refusal target R, (ii) regularizes transformation magnitude, and (iii) suppresses residual alignment with attribution-based harmful directions V extracted via visual token masking. At inference, each hidden state is updated as h' = h + λΔ̃*Ph, where λ controls steering intensity, yielding a training-free, per-token overhead of less than 1 ms.

## Results

Under the strongest unconstrained perturbation attack on MiniGPT-4, NullSteer achieves 2.89% Toxicity Score and 7.32% Jailbreak ASR, outperforming the closest steering-based baseline ASTRA (4.48% / 9.09%) and traditional defenses like ECSO (42.86% / 37.27%). On Qwen2-VL at ε=32/255, NullSteer records 3.51% Toxicity and 4.55% ASR versus ASTRA's 5.45% and 5.00%. Utility is preserved: on MiniGPT-4, MM-Vet improves from 19.40 (Vanilla) to 21.05 (NullSteer) and MMBench from 35.90 to 36.25, while XSTest over-refusal remains at 87.80%. Inference overhead is negligible at 173.57 ms/token on MiniGPT-4 versus 173.19 ms/token without defense.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NullSteer consistently outperforms all six baselines across all three VLMs on both Toxicity Score and Jailbreak ASR. Against the closest competitor ASTRA [52], NullSteer reduces Toxicity by 1.6–2.8 percentage points and ASR by 1.2–2.2 points under unconstrained attacks (e.g., 2.89% vs 4.48% Toxicity on MiniGPT-4). Traditional VLM defenses—Self-Reminder [60], JailGuard [63], and ECSO [19]—remain highly vulnerable, with Toxicity scores above 20% and ASR above 15% even on MiniGPT-4, confirming that input/output-level adjustments are insufficient against perturbation-based attacks. Text-only steering baselines (Refusal Pairs [44] and Jailbreak Templates [3]) perform inconsistently: Refusal Pairs achieves moderate ASR reduction (16.36% on MiniGPT-4 unconstrained) but high Toxicity (35.71%), while Jailbreak Templates [3] actually worsens ASR on Qwen2-VL (74.55% vs 76.36% undefended) due to lack of multimodal coupling. Under adaptive white-box attacks, NullSteer still achieves the lowest Jailbreak rate (19.3% at ε=64/255 vs 49.1% undefended), whereas ASTRA's advantage narrows, further validating the null-space constraint's robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

NullSteer extends the activation-steering paradigm for VLM safety (introduced by CAAC and operationalized for multimodal jailbreaks by ASTRA) by imposing a null-space projection constraint borrowed from continual learning and knowledge editing (AlphaEdit, Wang et al. 2021). Unlike prior steering methods that inject a fixed refusal direction into all activations—causing over-refusal on benign inputs—NullSteer restricts the steering transformation to the orthogonal complement of a benign activation subspace, providing a theoretical invariance guarantee. The paper also builds on AlphaSteer's null-space formulation for refusal steering but generalizes it to the multimodal setting with a closed-form solution that jointly aligns harmful activations toward refusal semantics and suppresses attribution-based jailbreak directions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The null-space constraint offers a principled, training-free mechanism to decouple safety from utility in VLMs, addressing a core tension that prior steering methods handle only heuristically. The practical finding that only ~8 benign and ~100 malicious samples suffice to construct a well-conditioned projection makes the approach deployable without large calibration datasets. Open questions include OOD generalization beyond ImageNet-derived perturbations, scalability to larger or differently-architected VLMs, and whether the null-space formulation can be integrated into fine-tuning pipelines for adaptive safety. The modest but consistent gains over ASTRA (e.g., 2.89% vs. 4.48% toxicity on MiniGPT-4) suggest the theoretical guarantee translates to real robustness, though the margin is not dramatic.
