## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based thermal infrared (TIR) reconstruction methods retrofit visible-light assumptions by treating TIR radiance as color channels, ignoring the physical imaging chain of self-emission, environmental reflection, and atmospheric transmittance, which produces floating artifacts and blurred thermal edges. Additionally, the low texture and radiometric inconsistency inherent to TIR imagery degrade keypoint-based SfM initialization, destabilizing downstream 3DGS optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260670/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view passband-integrated TIR images (480×720, 7.5–13.5 μm) with optional co-registered RGB views, and produces 3D Gaussian primitives annotated with per-primitive temperature, emissivity, and a TIR-specific opacity, from which novel thermal views are synthesized via volume rendering. VGGT-IR additionally outputs camera poses (unit-norm quaternion, translation, focal length), dense depth maps, and 3D point maps in a single forward pass, bypassing keypoint detection and matching entirely. The rendered output passes through a monotonic radiometric response module that maps physical radiance to sensor gray values.

## Method

PhysIR-Splat assigns physical quantities to each Gaussian: temperature (via softplus + T_min), in-band emissivity (via sigmoid), and a TIR-specific opacity derived from geometric normalization with a scene-constant equivalent extinction coefficient. Self-emission is computed from a precomputed passband-integrated Planck radiance LUT; environmental reflection is approximated by convolving a Lambertian cosine kernel with low-order (l≤2) spherical harmonics; and atmospheric attenuation is modeled as exp(−βd_i) with a learnable extinction coefficient β. A three-layer Softplus-constrained MLP enforces a strictly monotonic radiance-to-gray sensor response, unifying the full imaging chain. VGGT-IR, a 12-layer alternating-attention Transformer fronted by DINOv3 ViT-L/16 encoders (with IR-adapted patch embedding), additively fuses IR and optional RGB features and directly regresses camera parameters and dense geometry in one pass, followed by lightweight bundle adjustment. Training combines L1, SSIM, total-variation smoothness, and an emissivity-sparsity (Huber) loss, with VGGT-IR trained end-to-end using RGB augmentation at probability 0.2.

## Results

On the TI-NSD dataset (IR-only), PhysIR-Splat with VGGT-IR achieves an average PSNR of 37.33 dB, SSIM of 0.962, and LPIPS of 0.150, outperforming the strongest baseline Veta-GS (35.97 dB / 0.958 / 0.169) by +1.36 dB in PSNR. On the RGBT-Scenes dataset (IR+RGB), the full system reaches 29.09 dB PSNR and 0.902 SSIM on the thermal modality and 27.44 dB / 0.885 on RGB, surpassing ThermalGaussian (25.73 / 0.878 thermal) by +3.36 dB. For camera pose estimation on the Multi-Spectral Dataset, VGGT-IR reduces APE-Trans to 0.0823 m on desk1-xyz-dim2 versus 0.3107 m for COLMAP and 0.2133 m for VGGT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysIR-Splat+VGGT-IR outperforms all eight baselines across every evaluated metric and dataset. Against the closest IR-only competitor Veta-GS [35], it gains +1.36 dB PSNR and reduces LPIPS from 0.169 to 0.150 on TI-NSD, with the largest margin in outdoor scenes (+1.79 dB). Thermal3D-GS [7] trails by +2.29 dB PSNR on average, while vanilla 3DGS [25] and InstantNGP [34] are substantially weaker (32.01 and 24.91 dB respectively). On the multimodal RGBT-Scenes benchmark, the method beats ThermalGaussian [30] by +3.36 dB thermal PSNR and +3.31 dB RGB PSNR, and dominates ThermoNeRF [18] by +6.67 dB thermal PSNR, which also suffers the worst RGB reconstruction (17.86 dB). For pose estimation, VGGT-IR reduces APE-Trans by 73% over COLMAP [41] (0.0823 m vs 0.3107 m on desk1-xyz-dim2) and by 62% over VGGT [46] (0.0823 m vs 0.2133 m), confirming its advantage in low-texture TIR sequences where keypoint-based SfM degrades.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) to thermal infrared by explicitly assigning physical quantities—temperature, emissivity, and environmental irradiance—to each Gaussian primitive, rather than treating the color channels as thermal radiance as prior thermal-3DGS methods (Thermal3D-GS, Veta-GS, ThermalGaussian) do. The radiative transfer formulation builds directly on the TeV decomposition from PID (Mao et al., 2026), embedding passband-integrated Planck emission, SH-based environmental reflection, and atmospheric transmittance into the volume-rendering pipeline. For initialization, it adapts VGGT (Wang et al., 2025) into VGGT-IR, replacing keypoint-based SfM with a feed-forward Transformer that regresses poses and geometry directly from TIR (with optional RGB), addressing the well-known degradation of COLMAP on low-texture thermal sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding the 3DGS renderer in the actual infrared imaging chain, the paper offers a principled path toward physically consistent thermal reconstruction that could transfer to other spectral bands or sensor configurations. The VGGT-IR component is practically valuable for any TIR pipeline that currently suffers from unreliable SfM initialization. However, the diffuse-reflection assumption limits applicability to specular materials (glass, polished steel), as confirmed by the emissivity correlation analysis, and the approach has not been tested on outdoor long-range scenes with significant atmospheric path emission or on mid-wave infrared bands where scattering is less negligible. Open questions include whether the per-primitive emissivity can be regularized with material priors to handle the remaining specular cases, and whether the framework extends to time-varying thermal fields.
