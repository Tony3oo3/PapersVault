## Problem

Establishing object-level visual correspondence between egocentric and exocentric video views is severely challenged by drastic appearance variation, occlusions, and disjoint spatial references that break traditional appearance-based or tracking-based methods. Prior cross-view approaches either rely on auxiliary modules, external mask proposals, or large-scale paired data, and none exploit self-supervised signals that could also enable adaptation at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263341/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source image with a binary object mask (the query) and a target image from a different viewpoint as input. The source mask is normalized to sum to one and used as spatial weights to compute a single compact feature vector from the source backbone's feature map, which is then projected into a conditioning token. The output is a binary segmentation mask for the corresponding object in the target view, plus a binary visibility prediction from a separate CLS head indicating whether the queried object is visible in the target frame.

## Method

The architecture uses a ConvNeXt-based DINOv3-L as source feature extractor and a ViT-based DINOv3-L as target encoder, bridged by a single Conditioning Token (CDT) that carries the mask-weighted source feature into the transformer via cross-token attention, requiring minimal architectural modification. Training combines three losses: a standard mask loss (BCE + Dice) on the target prediction, a deep-supervision auxiliary loss on intermediate encoder layers, and a cycle-consistency loss that projects the predicted target mask back to the source view and penalizes deviation from the original query mask via BCE. Because the cycle-consistency loss requires no target ground truth, it doubles as a self-supervised objective for test-time training, where the last K transformer layers are fine-tuned for a few gradient steps per test pair. A two-stage training protocol (frozen-backbone linear probing followed by full fine-tuning) and data augmentation strategies—unifying Ego2Exo/Exo2Ego directions, same-view exemplar synthesis, and relaxed temporal alignment—further improve robustness.

## Results

On the Ego-Exo4D correspondence benchmark, the proposed method achieves a mean IoU (mIoU) of 44.57%, surpassing the previous SOTA O-MaMa (43.32%) by +2.9% relative improvement. In the Exo Query setting, it reaches 47.18% IoU (+7.0% relative over O-MaMa's 44.08%), while in the Ego Query setting it achieves 41.95% IoU, slightly below O-MaMa's 42.57%. On HANDAL-X, the method attains 78.8% IoU without any HANDAL-X training data, outperforming ObjectRelator (42.8%) by +84.1% relative, and reaches 85.0% IoU after fine-tuning on HANDAL-X.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against 7 baselines spanning tracking-based (XSegTx, XMem, XView-XMem), general-purpose segmentation (SEEM, PSALM, CMX), and cross-view-specific methods (ObjectRelator, O-MaMa). The proposed method achieves the highest mIoU (44.57%) across all baselines, with the closest competitor being O-MaMa (43.32%), which uses a mask-matching strategy with FastSAM proposals. In the Exo Query setting, the gap is largest: 47.18% vs. 44.08% IoU for O-MaMa, and 47.18% vs. 40.31% for ObjectRelator. The method also dramatically outperforms ObjectRelator on HANDAL-X zero-shot (78.8% vs. 42.8% IoU), demonstrating superior cross-view generalization. Notably, the proposed method achieves near-perfect visibility accuracy (98.92%/99.86%) compared to O-MaMa's 50.00%/50.05%, indicating its CLS head provides far more reliable visibility predictions. General-purpose segmentation models (SEEM, PSALM, CMX) perform poorly (mIoU < 10%), confirming that cross-view-specific training is essential for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a simpler, more end-to-end path for cross-view object correspondence by repurposing DINOv3 vision foundation models with a single conditioning token (CDT) rather than the auxiliary alignment modules used by ObjectRelator or the FastSAM-based mask-matching pipeline of O-MaMa. The cycle-consistency loss draws conceptually from Baade et al.'s predictive cycle consistency for cross-view segmentation, but here it is applied to a learned segmentation network rather than to pseudo-labeling, and it doubles as the self-supervised signal for test-time training. The approach also builds on the Ego-Exo4D benchmark's formulation of the task (XSegTx, XView-XMem baselines) while deliberately avoiding their reliance on temporal context or multi-frame memory, instead operating on single image pairs with a compact transformer encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a minimal architectural intervention (one extra token in a pretrained ViT) combined with a self-supervised cycle-consistency objective can match or exceed more complex cross-view pipelines, suggesting that the bottleneck in ego-exo correspondence may be training signal design rather than model capacity. The first application of test-time training to this task opens a practical avenue for adapting to domain shifts at inference without retraining. However, performance on small objects (below 0.1% of target-view area) remains weak, and the Ego2Exo direction consistently lags Exo2Ego, indicating that the object-size distribution imbalance is an unresolved challenge. Whether the TTT strategy extends to video-level correspondence or to other cross-view settings beyond ego-exo remains an open question.
