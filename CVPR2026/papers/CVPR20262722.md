## Problem

End-to-end AI severe weather forecasting is hindered by three gaps: (1) no large-scale dataset pairs raw multi-year meteorological fields with textual warnings at fine temporal resolution, (2) existing multimodal LLMs cannot natively ingest 4D gridded meteorological data (time × pressure level × longitude × latitude) and instead rely on lossy compression into RGB images or daily averages, and (3) no architecture captures the coupled spatiotemporal and vertical dependencies inherent in atmospheric fields.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262722/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model ingests a 12-hour window [t, t+11] of ERA5 reanalysis data at 0.25° resolution, spanning 37 vertical pressure levels across 5 variables (temperature, humidity, precipitation, wind speed, pressure), yielding 185 spatiotemporal channels, together with a free-form text query specifying a geographic region and question type. Outputs are structured natural-language responses in four formats: multiple-choice classification (7 weather types × 2–4 severity sub-levels + normal), true/false detection, regional weather identification, and a nationwide structured listing of all active severe weather events with location, type, and severity.

## Method

MMLM directly ingests the full 185-channel 4D meteorological tensor without compression into 2D images, and applies three parallel plug-and-play modules that each target one axis of the data: DTGF computes L2-norm differences between adjacent time steps to generate per-location temporal gating weights that highlight abrupt atmospheric changes; TGS extracts geographic coordinates from the text query, maps them to grid indices, and applies a 2D Gaussian spatial mask to focus attention on the queried region; TGCA projects text embeddings into the channel dimension and computes softmax attention against channel-wise spatiotemporal means to re-weight the 185 channels by their relevance to the query. The three module outputs are concatenated and fused by a learnable 3D convolution before an MLP maps the result to the LLM's input dimensionality. The entire system is fine-tuned with LoRA on top of existing MLLM backbones (Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-NeXT-Video, Video-LLaVA, InternVL3), with the three modules and fusion layer as additional learnable components, trained on MP-Bench (421,363 pairs) using 2023 data for training and 2024 for testing.

## Results

On MP-Bench, the best MMLM variant (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct + DTGF/TGS/TGCA) achieves 72.37% MC-main accuracy and 50.88% Macro-F1, 58.19% MC-sub accuracy and 29.31% Macro-F1, 87.13% T/F accuracy, 71.23% RSW accuracy, and a 2.1/5 NSW score. The closed-source GPT-4o (without fine-tuning) performs near-randomly, scoring only 11.92% MC-main accuracy and 0.19% T/F accuracy. Cross-regional generalization on the U.S. NOAA Storm Events Dataset yields 64.20% MC-main accuracy and 35.18% Macro-F1, confirming transferability beyond the training geography.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMLM (Qwen2.5-VL) outperforms all four open-source baselines on every metric. Against its own backbone baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, 3-channel), MMLM gains +16.11 points in MC-main accuracy (72.37 vs 56.26) and +24.00 points in Macro-F1 (50.88 vs 26.88), with T/F accuracy jumping from 68.33% to 87.13%. The largest absolute gains appear over InternVL3-8B (+29.79 MC-main Acc, +35.41 Macro-F1) and LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B (+24.38 MC-main Acc, +30.18 Macro-F1), indicating that the 185-channel input plus the three plug-and-play modules are especially beneficial for weaker backbones. Video-LLaVA-7B is the strongest baseline (59.77% MC-main Acc), yet MMLM still surpasses it by +12.60 Acc and +28.43 Macro-F1. GPT-4o without fine-tuning is effectively non-functional on this task (0.19% T/F accuracy, below random), confirming that raw meteorological grid data cannot be interpreted by general-purpose MLLMs without domain-specific adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in MLLM-based weather forecasting (CLLMate, WeatherQA, ClimateIQA, OmniEarth-Bench), which compresses high-dimensional meteorological fields into 3-channel RGB images or daily averages to fit standard vision encoders. Instead, MMLM directly ingests 185-channel 4D ERA5 data (5 variables × 37 pressure levels × 12 hours) and introduces three plug-and-play modules (DTGF, TGS, TGCA) to handle temporal, spatial, and vertical dimensions natively. The dataset contribution (MP-Bench, 421K pairs) also extends prior severe-weather benchmarks (SEVIR, GridRad-Severe, HR-Extreme) by pairing raw gridded fields with textual warnings at hourly temporal alignment rather than relying on remote-sensing imagery or NWP outputs alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MP-Bench provides the first large-scale, nationwide, multi-year multimodal resource for severe weather prediction with precise 12-hour temporal alignment, filling a critical data gap that has limited generalization in prior work. The demonstration that a fine-tuned open-source MLLM can interpret raw 4D meteorological data and produce structured textual warnings (while GPT-4o fails catastrophically) validates a path toward fully automated AI-driven warning pipelines. However, the NSW task score remains low (2.1/5), misclassified samples involve complex multi-scale patterns, and the authors acknowledge that integrating physical constraints and multi-source data (radar, satellite) will be necessary for operational deployment—leaving the gap between research prototype and real-time forecasting system still substantial.
