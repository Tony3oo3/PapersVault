## Problem

Offline vectorized map construction from a single ego trajectory suffers from viewpoint insufficiency: occluded and long-range regions remain invisible, and memory-augmented methods (temporal stacking, rasterized map history) merely extend observation time without introducing the spatial diversity needed to recover those regions. Naively fusing all nearby non-ego vehicles is infeasible due to large candidate pools, near-collinear redundancy, and pose/occlusion noise, yet no prior work systematically addresses how to select and integrate complementary multi-vehicle views for offline mapping.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the ego vehicle's 6 surround-view RGB images with per-pixel semantic segmentations, plus the same modalities from K (typically 2–5) OVS-selected non-ego vehicles within a 60 m radius (≥30 m minimum separation, ≥30 min time gap to avoid near-collinear views), along with camera calibration parameters for BEV warping. Output is a set of vectorized map elements—each an ordered 2D point sequence of variable length in the ego-centric BEV frame—tagged with one of three semantic classes (pedestrian crossing, lane divider, road boundary). The pipeline is plug-and-play at the BEV feature level: it requires no modification to the downstream DETR-style decoder and operates on asynchronous cross-trajectory observations aligned to a common BEV coordinate system.

## Method

OptiMVMap reformulates multi-vehicle mapping as a select-then-fuse problem. The Optimal Vehicle Selection (OVS) module first computes per-vehicle BEV uncertainty maps from single-vehicle predictions, spatially partitions the ego BEV into regions, and scores each candidate non-ego vehicle via cross-attention between its position embedding and an uncertainty-integrated feature, retaining only the top-K (K=2–5) most complementary helpers. Selected views are then aligned by Cross-Vehicle Attention (CVA), a two-layer deformable-attention mechanism that learns feature-conditioned sampling offsets to compensate for inter-vehicle calibration errors and temporal asynchrony. A Semantic-aware Noise Filter (SNF) applies a softmax-gated, content-aware weighting over ego and non-ego BEV features to suppress occlusion- and dynamics-induced artifacts before element-wise fusion. A key architectural choice is Hard-LSS, which decouples depth and semantic channels during PV-to-BEV lifting by retaining only the highest-confidence semantic feature per BEV cell, avoiding the semantic blurring of standard soft LSS pooling. Training is two-stage: the fusion backbone (CVA+SNF+decoder) is pretrained with random non-ego sampling, then OVS is trained with a sigmoid BCE loss against ground-truth helper sets determined by exhaustive mAP evaluation, keeping the overall pipeline lightweight and detector-agnostic.

## Results

On nuScenes, MapTRv2+OptiMVMap+QI (K=2) achieves 72.0 mAP, improving over the MapTRv2 baseline by +10.5 mAP; with K=5 helpers it reaches 73.9 mAP, and at 110 epochs with K=7 it reaches 78.1 mAP, surpassing MapExpert (76.5) by +1.6 mAP. On Argoverse2, MapTRv2+OptiMVMap (K=2) achieves 73.6 mAP, a +9.3 mAP gain over the MapTRv2 baseline (64.3) and +0.8 mAP over the prior best (MapExpert, 72.8). The method also outperforms memory-augmented single-vehicle baselines MVMap (+6.2 mAP) and HRMapNet (+3.8 mAP) on nuScenes with only 2 helper vehicles.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OptiMVMap consistently outperforms all 15 compared baselines on both datasets. On nuScenes (24-epoch setting), it beats the strongest single-vehicle methods InteractionMap (71.8) and Mask2Map (71.6) by +0.2 and +0.4 mAP respectively with K=2+QI, and by a wider margin (+2.1, +2.3) with K=5. Against memory-augmented single-vehicle methods, it surpasses MVMap (48.9) by +6.2 mAP and HRMapNet (67.2) by +3.8 mAP, demonstrating that complementary multi-vehicle views are more effective than temporal stacking or rasterized map history. On Argoverse2, it exceeds InteractionMap (72.1) by +1.5 mAP and MapExpert (72.8) by +0.8 mAP with only K=2 helpers. At the 110-epoch setting on nuScenes, it surpasses MapExpert (76.5) by +1.6 mAP and MapTracker (74.7) by +3.4 mAP. The plug-and-play nature is confirmed by consistent gains across diverse backbones: +14.2 mAP on VectorMapNet, +6.1 mAP on MGMap, and +9.5 mAP on MapTRv2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DETR-style vectorized map construction lineage (MapTRv2, VectorMapNet, MGMap) by introducing a multi-vehicle offline setting, departing fundamentally from memory-augmented single-vehicle methods (MVMap, HRMapNet, StreamMapNet) that aggregate temporally adjacent ego frames or coarse map histories. The Cross-Vehicle Attention mechanism adapts deformable attention from object detection to cross-trajectory BEV alignment, while the Hard-LSS variant modifies LSS's soft depth-weighted pooling for semantic features. The Optimal Vehicle Selection module is the key conceptual departure: rather than fusing all available views or stacking ego frames, it formulates helper selection as an uncertainty-reduction decision problem, a framing absent from prior offline mapping work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small set (2–5) of uncertainty-guided, geometrically complementary helper vehicles outperforms both indiscriminate multi-view fusion and longer temporal stacks, with the selection step contributing more accuracy (+4.8 mAP) than alignment and denoising combined. This challenges the implicit assumption in memory-augmented methods that more ego frames or simply closer vehicles are always beneficial. Open questions include scalability to denser urban traffic with hundreds of candidates, applicability to online/real-time settings, and whether the plug-and-play module transfers to LiDAR-based or multi-sensor mapping pipelines. The extended nuScenes-MV and AV2-MV datasets provide a useful benchmark for future multi-vehicle mapping research.
