## Problem

Existing text-based image editing methods can adjust appearance or style but struggle to perform precise object-level geometric transformations (translation, rotation, resizing) because they rely on pixel-level MSE losses that cannot disentangle objects from backgrounds, require expensive paired supervision data, or depend on manual 2D/3D control primitives. No prior work formulates this spatial manipulation task within a reinforcement learning framework that learns directly from verifiable geometric rewards.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262690/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a scene image and a natural-language instruction following standardized templates (e.g., 'move the mug to the left', 'rotate the man 90° counterclockwise', 'resize the car to 2×') and produces an edited image where only the target object undergoes the specified geometric change while the background and other objects remain consistent. Instructions are constrained to predefined parameter spaces: four cardinal directions with spatial references for translation, three axes with four angles for rotation, and discrete scaling ratios (1.25×–4×) for resizing.

## Method

TALK2MOVE builds on the QwenImageEdit flow-matching backbone and trains via a two-stage pipeline: a LoRA-based SFT cold start on small paired data (synthesized via video generation and image editing models), followed by GRPO-style RL where stochastic perturbations are injected into the deterministic ODE to form diverse SDE rollouts. A spatially grounded reward model uses specialist tools (Segment Anything, Grounding DINO, Orient-Anything) to explicitly localize the target object and compute task-specific geometric rewards (displacement, orientation alignment, size ratio) rather than holistic image-level scores. To improve efficiency, an off-policy step evaluation identifies the denoising step with maximum reward variance, and active step sampling then takes ODE shortcuts from that exit step to the final step, reducing total computation by ~49% while simultaneously improving accuracy and convergence speed.

## Results

On the synthetic benchmark (100 samples per task), TALK2MOVE achieves 76.67% translation accuracy (vs. 64.29% for GPT-Image-1), 29.55% rotation accuracy (vs. 13.64% for Bagel), and 9.17% resize accuracy (vs. 7.50% for QwenImageEdit). It also attains the lowest rotation error (0.2861) and the highest human win rates across all three tasks (57.50%, 68.75%, 63.89%). On real OpenImages-V6 images, TALK2MOVE reaches 53.85% translation accuracy and 31.25% rotation accuracy, outperforming all baselines on both metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TALK2MOVE outperforms all four baselines on translation accuracy (76.67% vs. 64.29% GPT-Image-1, 32.86% QwenImageEdit, 14.49% Bagel, 4.41% Flux-Kontext) and rotation accuracy (29.55% vs. 13.64% Bagel, 9.30% QwenImageEdit, 6.82% Flux-Kontext, 2.33% GPT-Image-1). On resize accuracy, TALK2MOVE (9.17%) narrowly beats QwenImageEdit (7.50%) and GPT-Image-1 (5.08%), though GPT-Image-1 achieves a slightly lower scaling error (0.3501 vs. 0.3894). In the human evaluation, TALK2MOVE dominates with win rates of 57.50%, 68.75%, and 63.89% across translation, rotation, and resize respectively, far exceeding the next-best method. On real OpenImages-V6 images, TALK2MOVE leads on translation accuracy (53.85% vs. 42.31% QwenImageEdit) and rotation accuracy (31.25% vs. 25.00% GPT-Image-1 and QwenImageEdit), confirming generalization beyond synthetic data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow-based GRPO paradigm introduced by FlowGRPO [24] and DanceGRPO [46] from general generation quality to the specific problem of object-level geometric transformation in scene editing. It departs from prior RL-for-diffusion work (DDPO, FlowGRPO, DanceGRPO) that relies on holistic image-level rewards (aesthetic scores, CLIP alignment) by introducing spatially grounded, object-centric rewards built on specialist models (SAM, Grounding DINO, Orient-Anything). The early-exit active step sampling mechanism builds on sliding-window and tree-based trajectory reorganization [17, 20] but goes further by identifying task-specific informative steps through off-policy reward variance analysis, drawing an analogy to high-entropy token selection in LLM reasoning [42].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that RL with verifiable spatial rewards can achieve precise geometric object manipulation without the costly paired supervision that SFT-based editing methods require, and that the RL stage is remarkably data-efficient (1/10 data suffices where SFT fails). The early-exit finding—that fewer, more informative denoising steps yield both faster training and higher accuracy—challenges the default assumption that all diffusion steps contribute equally and could generalize to other flow-matching RL applications. Key limitations include the small dataset (800 unique images), the still-modest rotation accuracy (~30%), and the restriction to three basic transformations; scaling to richer spatial operations, more complex scenes, and other generative backbones (GANs, autoregressive models) remains open.
