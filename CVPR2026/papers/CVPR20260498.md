## Problem

High-resolution video generation is severely bottlenecked by slow inference (e.g., ~50 min for a 5s 720p video with Wan 2.1), and existing acceleration methods—step distillation, attention sparsity, cascade generation—inevitably compromise the distinctive signatures (layout, semantic alignment, motion dynamics) of the original pretrained model. The paper asks whether it is possible to achieve large speedups while maximally preserving these model-specific generative characteristics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260498/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a high-resolution video (720p or 1080p, 5 seconds at 16–24 fps). The prompt is consumed by a powerful pretrained video diffusion model (e.g., Wan 2.1 14B or HunyuanVideo) operating at its native training resolution during the early denoising steps, after which the latent is dynamically downsampled. The final output is a full-resolution video whose layout, semantics, and motion closely match those of the base model, and the framework can also be applied as a plug-in on top of other acceleration methods (sparse attention, step distillation) for additional speedup.

## Method

SURF splits generation into two stages. In the Preview stage, a training-free technique called noise reshifting starts denoising at the pretrained model's optimal resolution for the first k steps (where global structure is established), then estimates the clean latent, downsamples it spatially, reinjects noise at the corresponding timestep, and continues denoising at the lower resolution for the remaining steps—preserving signatures while cutting token count. In the Refine stage, a lightweight 1B-parameter transformer Refiner learns a flow-matching mapping from the upsampled low-resolution preview latent to the high-resolution target latent, requiring only 10 NFE. The Refiner employs a cyclic shift-window 3D self-attention mechanism (alternating unshifted and half-window-shifted layers with attention masks at boundaries) combined with 3D RoPE to achieve full temporal connectivity at reduced memory cost. Training uses paired LR-HR latents with both pixel-level blur and latent-level degradation to prevent the task from degenerating into trivial super-resolution, with a progressive curriculum from 21 to 81 frames.

## Results

On Wan 2.1 720p (5s, 16fps), SURF achieves a 12.58× speedup (278s vs. 3497s) while matching the baseline's quality score (QS 83.26 vs. 83.31) and semantic adherence (SA 41.82 vs. 41.82), with only a modest drop in physics commonsense (PC 38.18 vs. 45.45). As a plug-in on HunyuanVideo, SURF delivers an 8.7× speedup (356s vs. 3081s) while substantially improving SA (43.64 vs. 29.09) and PC (45.45 vs. 27.27). For 1080p generation, SURF outperforms all video SR baselines on DINO (93.75), LAION aesthetic (63.50), and DOVER (81.20) while running ~12× faster than the next-best method (76.5s vs. 912.7s for STAR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the full Wan 2.1 baseline, SURF matches quality (QS 83.26 vs. 83.31) and semantic adherence (SA 41.82) exactly, while achieving 12.58× speedup; the only notable gap is physics commonsense (PC 38.18 vs. 45.45). Compared to SVG (sparse attention), SURF achieves a 12.58× vs. 1.29× speedup and substantially higher SA (41.82 vs. 25.45) and PC (38.18 vs. 20.00), indicating SVG's token sparsity degrades model signatures. Against DMD (distillation), SURF matches speed (12.58× vs. 12.40×) but wins decisively on dynamic degree (DD 72.22 vs. 52.78), SA (41.82 vs. 34.55), and PC (38.18 vs. 30.91), confirming that distillation sacrifices motion and semantic fidelity. As a plug-in on HunyuanVideo, SURF (8.7×) vastly outperforms sparse attention (1.1×) in both speed and quality (SA 43.64 vs. 30.91, PC 45.45 vs. 23.64). On AccVideo (step-distilled), SURF adds a further 1.3× speedup while improving SA (36.36 vs. 32.73) and PC (38.18 vs. 23.64), demonstrating compatibility with existing acceleration pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SURF builds on the flow-matching formulation (Esser et al., ICML 2024) and operates atop pretrained video DiTs like Wan 2.1, but departs from prior acceleration strategies (step distillation, sparse attention, cascade multi-scale) by introducing a two-stage preview-then-refine pipeline with a training-free noise-reshifting mechanism that switches resolution mid-denoising. Unlike distillation-based methods (DMD, AccVideo) that sacrifice the base model's signatures, or sparse-attention methods (SVG, Jenga) that offer limited speedup, SURF preserves the original model's layout, semantics, and motion by performing early high-noise denoising at the model's optimal resolution before downsampling. The lightweight Refiner with cyclic shift-window 3D attention and flow-mapping from low-res to high-res latents draws on Swin-style windowed attention adapted for video, but the key conceptual contribution is the resolution-dynamic denoising trajectory rather than any single architectural component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 12.5× speedup for 720p video generation while maintaining near-identical quality to the full 50-step baseline is a practically significant efficiency win that could make high-resolution video generation accessible in production settings, and the plug-in design broadens applicability across architectures (Wan 2.1, HunyuanVideo, AccVideo). Open questions include whether the 1B Refiner trained on synthetic degradation data generalizes to substantially longer videos or resolutions beyond 1080p, and whether the noise-reshifting turning point (k) can be made adaptive per prompt rather than fixed. The ablation showing shift-window attention is largely unnecessary for the refinement stage suggests the Refiner's generative capacity, not its receptive field, is the dominant factor — a finding that could simplify future upscaling architectures.
