## Problem

Global foundation models for PM forecasting (e.g., Aurora) underperform in East Asia due to regional data imbalance and multi-day reanalysis update latencies, making them unsuitable for real-time operational warning systems. Additionally, standard MSE-based training treats all forecast errors symmetrically, causing models to over-predict and produce high False Alarm Rates that erode public trust, while the asymmetric operational costs of false alarms versus missed severe pollution events remain unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261203/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes sequential 6-hour gridded input fields at 27 km resolution over Korea and China, combining CMAQ reanalysis (PM2.5, PM10, O3) with spatially interpolated ground observations from 1,822+ monitoring stations and meteorological data. Forecasts are produced via autoregressive 6-hour rollouts extending to 120-hour lead times, yielding both continuous PM concentration fields and discrete AQI class labels (Good, Moderate, Bad, VeryBad) for operational alerting.

## Method

FAKER-Air is a two-stage framework built on an Aurora-based 3D encoder-decoder. Stage 1 (SFT) introduces a temporal accumulation loss that supervises multi-step autoregressive trajectories (up to 4 steps) with linearly increasing step weights, directly mitigating exposure bias by exposing the model to its own rollout errors during training. Stage 2 applies Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a binary class-wise AQI reward (1 for correct AQI class, 0 otherwise), where G sampled rollouts per input are ranked via softmax-normalized relative advantages to update the policy without a critic. A curriculum rollout schedule progressively extends the optimization horizon from 1-step to 4-steps across epochs, reducing gradient variance and stabilizing long-horizon credit assignment. This is, to the authors' knowledge, the first application of policy optimization to spatio-temporal atmospheric forecasting.

## Results

On the CMAQ-OBS test set (2023, 120h horizon), FAKER-Air GRPO achieves a binary F1-score of 56.72 for PM2.5, a 3.5× improvement over Aurora (16.06), while reducing the False Alarm Rate by 47.3% relative to the SFT baseline (32.86 → 17.32). Macro CSI rises from 14.28 (Aurora) to 26.79 (GRPO) for PM2.5 and from 12.41 to 28.07 for PM10. The Bias metric moves from 1.52 (SFT) to 0.96 (GRPO), approaching the ideal value of 1.0, indicating well-calibrated event frequency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is Aurora [Bodnar et al., Nature 2025], the only open-source foundation model with explicit PM forecasting capability. FAKER-Air GRPO dramatically outperforms Aurora on all detection-oriented metrics: F1-score improves from 16.06 to 56.72 (PM2.5) and from 4.73 to 57.32 (PM10), while Macro CSI more than doubles (14.28 → 26.79 for PM2.5; 12.41 → 28.07 for PM10). However, Aurora's extremely low FAR (2.24) and Bias (0.13) reveal that it essentially predicts 'no pollution,' making it a degenerate baseline that avoids false alarms by missing nearly all events. The SFT intermediate stage, while achieving high CSI, suffers from severe over-prediction (FAR 32.86, Bias 1.52), confirming the decision-cost mismatch of MSE. GRPO resolves this by cutting FAR by 47.3% (PM2.5) and 41% (PM10) while preserving or improving per-class CSI, particularly for the Good and Bad categories, yielding a more operationally trustworthy operating point than either Aurora or SFT alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Aurora (Bodnar et al., Nature 2025), the only open-source foundation model that explicitly includes PM forecasting, as its architectural backbone, and on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) from the LLM reasoning literature, repurposing group-relative advantage estimation for a spatiotemporal forecasting setting. Conceptually, it departs from the standard supervised fine-tuning paradigm for weather/air-quality models by introducing a two-stage pipeline: a temporal accumulation loss to mitigate exposure bias during autoregressive rollouts, followed by policy optimization with class-wise AQI rewards to encode asymmetric operational costs. The CMAQ-OBS dataset positions the work against global reanalysis products (CAMS, ERA5) that the authors argue systematically underfit East Asian dynamics due to data imbalance and multi-day update latencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 47.3% FAR reduction is operationally significant for public-health alerting, where false alarms erode public trust while missed severe events endanger populations. The released CMAQ-OBS dataset (8+ years, 1,822+ stations, 27 km resolution) fills a concrete gap for the East Asian air-quality community that previously lacked a validated, real-time-capable regional benchmark. However, the evaluation is confined to a single region, a single base architecture (Aurora), and two pollutants; whether GRPO with curriculum rollout generalizes to other spatiotemporal forecasting tasks (e.g., precipitation, temperature) or to different backbone architectures remains open. The binary AQI reward (correct/incorrect class) is also a simplification—finer-grained or continuous cost-sensitive rewards could further refine the decision boundary.
