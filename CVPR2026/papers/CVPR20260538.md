## Problem

Pretrained vision encoders inherit spurious correlations that disproportionately harm underrepresented groups, yet existing debiasing methods either require explicit demographic annotations (Group-DRO, LfF) or gradient access to the backbone (JTT, adversarial re-weighting), making them infeasible for proprietary frozen models. This paper addresses how to achieve worst-group robustness in a fully black-box setting where neither backbone parameters nor bias labels are available.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260538/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images passed through a frozen backbone (ResNet-18, CLIP RN-50, or ViT-B/16) to produce fixed-dimensional embeddings, which are then processed by a trainable two-layer MLP adapter (d→128→128, ReLU) and a weight-normalized linear classifier to yield a class label. No bias or demographic labels are provided at any stage; the only supervision signal is the task label. The adapter and classifier are the sole trainable components, keeping the backbone entirely frozen and opaque.

## Method

PLD-Debias is a three-stage pipeline: (1) Rank-Regularized Amplification trains the adapter with a loss that penalizes off-diagonal entries of the batch feature correlation matrix, collapsing representations into low effective rank and exaggerating the dominant spurious bias direction; (2) Unsupervised Pseudo-Bias Induction applies k-means within each class on the amplified adapter features to infer high-fidelity pseudo-bias cluster assignments (>90% alignment with ground truth) without any demographic annotation; (3) Bias-Guided Refinement jointly optimizes a supervised contrastive loss that pulls together same-label/different-pseudo-bias pairs (erasing the spurious direction) and a cluster-aware adaptive angular margin loss that assigns larger decision margins to rarer pseudo-bias clusters, equalizing worst-group risk. The key insight is that rank regularization makes the bias axis dominant enough for near-perfect clustering, and the resulting pseudo-labels then drive both the contrastive alignment and margin correction, yielding oracle-level robustness without demographic supervision.

## Results

On CelebA (ResNet-18), PLD-Debias achieves a worst-group accuracy (WG-Acc) of 83.10%, improving by 44.2 points over ERM (27.20%) and 2.31 points over the strongest baseline CM (80.79%), while reducing the accuracy gap to 2.32%. On Waterbirds with CLIP ResNet-50, the method reaches 84.92% WG-Acc with a gap of only 2.15%, outperforming Co-Adapt (81.95%) and CM (79.28%). On CMNIST-0.9 with CLIP, bias-conflicting accuracy reaches 95.61% with a gap of just 1.01%, and on the extreme CMNIST-0.995 setting it achieves 87.27% bias-conflicting accuracy versus 80.65% for CM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLD-Debias consistently outperforms both baselines on worst-group accuracy across all datasets and backbones. Against Co-Adapt [34], the gains are substantial: +4.73 points on CelebA WG-Acc (83.10 vs 78.37), +5.44 on Waterbirds/ResNet-18 (83.01 vs 67.57), and +2.97 on Waterbirds/CLIP (84.92 vs 81.95). Against CM [4], the improvements are more modest but consistent: +2.31 on CelebA (83.10 vs 80.79), +2.72 on Waterbirds/ResNet-18 (83.01 vs 80.29), and +5.64 on Waterbirds/CLIP (84.92 vs 79.28). On CMNIST-0.9 with CLIP, the method achieves 95.61% bias-conflicting accuracy versus 94.07% (CM) and 83.81% (Co-Adapt), narrowing the gap to 1.01%. The only setting where a baseline edges ahead is average-group accuracy on Waterbirds/CLIP, where Co-Adapt reaches 87.35% versus 87.07% for PLD-Debias, a negligible 0.28-point difference. Overall, PLD-Debias wins on the primary fairness metric (WG-Acc) in every configuration tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate debiasing techniques—rank regularization (Park et al., CVPR 2024) for amplifying spurious directions, cluster-based adaptive margins (Basu et al., NeurIPS 2024) for protecting minority groups, and supervised contrastive alignment (Zhang & Ré, 2022) for representation refinement—into a single two-stage pipeline operating entirely on frozen backbone embeddings. The key departure from prior work is the use of rank-regularized adapter features as the substrate for k-means pseudo-bias labeling, which bridges the gap between bias amplification and downstream debiasing without requiring demographic annotations. Unlike Group-DRO or Learning-from-Failure, which need explicit group labels, and unlike adversarial reweighting, which requires white-box gradient access, the proposed method is fully self-supervised and compatible with proprietary black-box encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is significant: as foundation models (CLIP, DINOv2, SAM) become standard frozen feature extractors, a plug-and-play adapter that closes worst-group gaps without retraining or demographic labels addresses a real deployment bottleneck. The finding that pseudo-bias labels achieve >90% fidelity with ground-truth annotations (98%+ on CMNIST) suggests that rank-regularized clustering is a surprisingly reliable proxy for demographic supervision, potentially reducing the annotation burden for fairness auditing. However, the method assumes separable spurious features and targets only classification, leaving entangled biases, dense prediction, and regression open. Privacy concerns also remain: the pipeline explicitly recovers sensitive group structure, which could be a liability in regulated settings.
