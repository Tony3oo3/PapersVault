## Problem

Existing part-level 3D generation methods either perform implicit latent grouping with limited granularity control or rely on external segmenters trained on large part-annotated datasets, creating a dependency that limits flexibility and scalability. The paper identifies that part-aware structure already emerges implicitly in whole-object geometry generators (evidenced by attention correlation maps in Hunyuan3D-2.1) but has not been harnessed for explicit, controllable part decomposition and high-fidelity part geometry synthesis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260883/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces a decomposable 3D object mesh composed of N individual part meshes, where N is determined automatically by the object's complexity. Each part is generated at full resolution in a normalized [0,1]³ canonical space and then transformed into global coordinates using scale and position recovered from a parallel global-coordinate-space latent, enabling seamless composition without decode-encode geometry degradation.

## Method

UniPart introduces Geom-Seg VecSet, a unified latent representation that extends the standard VecSet VAE with a SAM2-style promptable segmentation decoder, jointly encoding geometry and part labels in the same latent vectors without increasing latent dimensionality. A two-stage latent diffusion pipeline then operates on this space: a whole-level DiT (rectified flow) generates the global geometry-segmentation latent, from which part masks are extracted via the frozen segmentation decoder with FPS prompting and NMS post-processing to yield explicit part-latent segments. A part-level DiT then performs dual-space generation, simultaneously predicting each part's latent in both a global coordinate space (encoding scale, position, and compositional context) and a normalized canonical space (encoding high-fidelity part geometry), using a global-local attention mechanism and learnable space embeddings to couple the two predictions. This avoids the geometry loss of decode-encode cycles used by prior generate-then-segment pipelines and provides domain-aligned latent conditioning for part diffusion.

## Results

On the part-level 3D generation benchmark (300K-object dataset), UniPart achieves the best Chamfer Distance (CD = 0.72 ×10⁻²) and the highest F1@0.1 score (92.21 ×10⁻²), outperforming the strongest competitor X-Part (CD = 0.82, F1@0.1 = 88.90). However, UniPart trails X-Part (IoU = 31.95) and OmniPart (IoU = 27.94) on the IoU metric, where UniPart scores 21.99. The Geom-Seg VAE reconstruction quality (Table 2) is on par with the base Hunyuan3D-2.1 VAE (CD = 1.30 vs 1.29 ×10⁻⁴; F1@0.01 = 80.89 vs 80.85), confirming that adding segmentation information does not degrade geometric fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniPart is compared against five part-level 3D generation baselines: HoloPart, PartPacker, PartCrafter, OmniPart, and X-Part. UniPart achieves the lowest CD (0.72) and highest F1@0.1 (92.21), beating the closest competitor X-Part by 12% on CD and 3.7 points on F1@0.1. The margin over PartCrafter is particularly large (CD 1.03→0.72, F1 46.08→92.21), reflecting the advantage of explicit latent segmentation over implicit grouping. However, UniPart underperforms X-Part (31.95) and OmniPart (27.94) on IoU, where UniPart scores only 21.99, suggesting that the dual-space composition strategy may introduce slight volumetric misalignment despite superior surface fidelity. For VAE reconstruction (Table 2), UniPart's Geom-Seg VAE matches Hunyuan3D-2.1 (CD 1.30 vs 1.29 ×10⁻⁴) and slightly edges out TRELLIS (1.32) and Dora (1.45), confirming that the unified geometry-segmentation latent does not sacrifice reconstruction quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VecSet-based VAE-LDM paradigm (CLAY, Hunyuan3D-2.1) by augmenting the purely geometric latent space with geometry-aligned segmentation features, creating a unified Geom-Seg VecSet that jointly encodes shape and part structure. It departs from the dominant generate-then-segment pipeline (X-Part, HoloPart, OmniPart) by performing part segmentation directly in latent space during the first diffusion stage, and from implicit-segmentation methods (PartCrafter, PartPacker) by producing explicit part-latent masks with controllable granularity. The dual-space part diffusion (global + canonical coordinates) and latent-based conditioning represent a novel architectural choice relative to prior part-level generators that condition on decoded explicit representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified latent-space approach eliminates the decode-encode geometry degradation that plagues generate-then-segment pipelines and removes the dependency on large-scale part-annotated segmenters, making part-level generation more self-contained and scalable. However, the IoU metric (21.99) actually trails X-Part (31.95) despite superior CD and F-Score, suggesting the composed parts may not align as tightly in volume, and failure cases on structurally complex objects indicate the joint geometry-segmentation learning is still fragile. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to open-ended part counts, whether the 300K curated dataset is sufficient for diverse real-world categories, and whether the dual-space mechanism can be extended to textured or articulated objects.
