## Problem

Existing video world models lack a unified, precise control interface for both camera motion and multi-object dynamics in a shared 3D world coordinate frame. Prior approaches rely on 2D cues (bounding boxes, optical flow, masks) that are view-dependent and fragile under large viewpoint changes, or on 3D representations (SMPL-X, 3D bounding boxes, sparse trajectories) that are category-specific, rigid, or noisy—none providing a compact, editable, category-agnostic 4D geometric scene state for realistic multi-object video generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference RGB image, a text prompt, a user-specified camera trajectory, and per-object 3D Gaussian trajectories (mean and full covariance per frame) as input. The reference image is processed to extract monocular depth (MoGe-2), camera intrinsics, and object masks (Grounded SAM2), from which a static background point cloud and initial 3D Gaussians are derived. The output is an 81-frame 720P dynamic video clip; the first frame is preserved exactly (its control mask is zeroed and its RGB is replaced by the input image), while subsequent frames are generated conditioned on the rendered 4D control maps.

## Method

VerseCrafter introduces a 4D Geometric Control representation that encodes the scene as a static background point cloud plus per-object 3D Gaussian trajectories in a shared world coordinate frame, where each Gaussian's mean traces the motion path and its covariance captures spatial extent and orientation over time. These are rendered into multi-channel 4D control maps (background RGB/depth, trajectory RGB/depth, and a soft merged mask) that condition a frozen Wan2.1-14B video diffusion backbone through a lightweight GeoAdapter—a DiT-style branch interleaved every 5th backbone block with residual modulation. The decoupled background and foreground channels enable disentangled control over camera motion versus object motion while preserving geometric consistency. Training is supervised on the newly constructed VerseControl4D dataset (35K real-world samples with auto-derived prompts and rendered 4D control maps), updating only the GeoAdapter in a two-stage 480P→720P schedule.

## Results

On VerseControl4D for joint camera and object motion control, VerseCrafter achieves the best VBench-I2V Overall Score of 88.10 (vs. 85.47 for Yume, 83.66 for Perception-as-Control, and 83.55 for Uni3C), with substantially lower 3D control errors: RotErr 0.890, TransErr 3.103, and ObjMC 2.507. On the static-scene camera-only subset, VerseCrafter scores 86.80 Overall with RotErr 0.650 and TransErr 2.587, outperforming FlashWorld (85.33, 1.792, 3.257), ViewCrafter (84.04, 2.101, 9.868), and Voyager (78.12, 3.557, 3.880). The method also leads on fine-grained VBench sub-metrics including Imaging Quality (72.70), Aesthetic Quality (57.49), and Dynamic Degree (86.26).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VerseCrafter outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric. Against the joint-control baselines (Table 1), it reduces RotErr by 5.6× vs. Yume (0.890 vs. 7.560) and 5.6× vs. Perception-as-Control (0.890 vs. 5.006), and cuts ObjMC by 3.2× vs. Yume (2.507 vs. 7.959) and 2.6× vs. Perception-as-Control (2.507 vs. 6.556); Uni3C is the closest competitor on RotErr (1.361) but still trails VerseCrafter by 35% and is limited to single-person SMPL-X control. On the camera-only static-scene subset (Table 2), VerseCrafter achieves the lowest RotErr (0.650) and TransErr (2.587), beating FlashWorld by 2.7× on rotation error and ViewCrafter by 3.2× on translation error; Voyager lags most significantly with an Overall Score of 78.12 and I2V Subject of 85.03. Qualitatively, Perception-as-Control and Uni3C exhibit noticeable human deformation, Yume lacks precise camera control, and Uni3C cannot handle non-human objects, whereas VerseCrafter maintains sharp appearance and geometrically consistent backgrounds across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of video world models, camera-controlled video generation (CameraCtrl, ViewCrafter, Voyager), and object-motion control (Boximator, Uni3C, Perception-as-Control), but departs from all of these by introducing a unified 4D geometric scene state rather than fragmented 2D or category-specific 3D cues. The key conceptual move is repurposing 3D Gaussian Splatting—originally a rendering primitive—as a soft, category-agnostic control signal for object dynamics, replacing rigid bounding boxes or parametric body models like SMPL-X. Architecturally, it follows the ControlNet-style adapter paradigm (GeoAdapter on a frozen Wan2.1-14B backbone), but the conditioning signal is a rendered 4D control map derived from an explicit geometric world state rather than 2D feature maps or pose embeddings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified 4D Geometric Control representation offers a clean, editable interface for specifying both camera and multi-object motion in a shared world coordinate frame, which prior methods handle in a fragmented or category-limited way. The VerseControl4D dataset (35K real-world samples with automatic annotation) addresses a practical data bottleneck for training geometry-conditioned video models. Open questions include: how well the pipeline scales to longer sequences beyond 81 frames, how sensitive results are to errors in monocular depth (MoGe-2) and segmentation (Grounded SAM2) that propagate into the control maps, and whether the approach generalizes to scenes with more than 6 objects or highly deformable entities. The 1152-second inference time for a single 81-frame clip also limits near-term practical deployment.
