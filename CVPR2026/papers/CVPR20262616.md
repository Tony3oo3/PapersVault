## Problem

Existing EM-NLQ methods treat natural language queries as fixed, one-shot inputs and cannot handle the inherently iterative nature of real-world episodic memory search, where users refine ambiguous queries or correct wrong predictions through natural language feedback. Large vision-language models, which seem natural candidates for interactive refinement, actually degrade in feedback-handling ability after video fine-tuning and are too resource-intensive for on-device use.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262616/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an egocentric video segmented into non-overlapping 16-frame clips (encoded by ViT-1B from EgoVideo), a natural language query and user feedback text (encoded by gte-Qwen2-7B-instruct), and a reference span represented as the concatenation of its start, end, and mean clip embeddings. It produces a refined temporal span [t_s, t_e] and a per-clip alignment vector P ∈ [0,1]^m that reweights video features to shift the underlying EM-NLQ model's attention toward clips matching the user's feedback intent.

## Method

The core contribution is FALM, a plug-and-play Feedback ALignment Module that predicts per-clip alignment scores by encoding video, query, feedback, and reference span features through two-layer Transformer encoders and cross-attention decoder blocks, then reweighting the host EM-NLQ model's clip features via a lightweight EM Adapter (learned scalars α, β). FALM is supervised with pseudo-labels derived from three LLM-extracted feedback cues—contains, not-contains, and temporal—combined via logical AND, and trained with a multi-task loss of binary cross-entropy and L2 regression. A synthetic feedback generation recipe converts existing EM-NLQ datasets into EM-QnF training data by sampling reference spans (model failures, visually similar, or random), generating captions and explanations with an LVLM, and prompting a reasoning LLM to produce diverse feedback without revealing the answer. The module is integrated into existing models (GroundNLQ, OSGNet) as ReFocus, preserving their efficiency while enabling interactive refinement, and extends to multi-turn feedback via late fusion of per-feedback cross-attention features.

## Results

ReFocus(GroundNLQ) achieves the best performance across all three QnF benchmarks. On Ego4D-QnF (IoU=0.3), it reaches R1=33.1 and R5=59.7, with feedback improving R1 by +3.3 over query-only performance. On GoalStep-QnF, it achieves R1=26.8 (+4.9) and R5=56.2 (+5.4), and on HD-EPIC-QnF, R1=15.1 (+3.0) and R5=39.6 (+5.4). Critically, LVLM baselines (TimeChat, UniTime) show *negative* deltas with feedback (e.g., UniTime drops from 25.1 to 19.9 R1 on Ego4D-QnF), while ReFocus consistently benefits, demonstrating that existing LVLMs fail to leverage user feedback for temporal localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReFocus(GroundNLQ) outperforms all baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the LVLM baselines, the gap is dramatic: TimeChat (ZS) achieves only R1=1.6 on Ego4D-QnF (IoU=0.3) versus 33.1 for ReFocus(GroundNLQ), and UniTime (FT) reaches 21.7 versus 33.1. More importantly, both LVLMs show *negative* feedback deltas (e.g., UniTime drops 5.2 points on Ego4D-QnF), meaning they perform worse when given user feedback, whereas ReFocus gains +3.3 to +4.9 R1. Against the task-expert baselines, ReFocus(OSGNet) improves over OSGNet by +2.9 R1 on Ego4D-QnF (32.5 vs. 29.6) and +3.5 R1 on GoalStep-QnF (31.9 vs. 30.2), while ReFocus(GroundNLQ) improves over GroundNLQ by +3.5 R1 on Ego4D-QnF (33.1 vs. 29.6) and +3.5 R1 on GoalStep-QnF (26.8 vs. 23.3). The consistent positive deltas for ReFocus versus near-zero or negative deltas for all baselines confirm that the FALM module is the key differentiator in leveraging feedback.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the established EM-NLQ task (one-shot moment localization in egocentric video) by introducing an interactive feedback loop, positioning itself as the first work to formalize and address query ambiguity through user corrections. Methodologically, it repurposes existing EM-NLQ architectures (GroundNLQ, OSGNet) as backbones and adds a lightweight plug-and-play Feedback Alignment Module (FALM) that reweights clip features based on feedback, rather than proposing a new end-to-end model. It deliberately departs from the LVLM-based temporal grounding line (TimeChat, UniTime), showing that these models' dialogue capabilities do not transfer to feedback-driven video refinement, and instead relies on frozen pretrained encoders (EgoVideo ViT-1B, gte-Qwen2-7B-instruct) with a small trainable transformer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that even commercial LVLMs (Gemini-2.5-Flash) degrade in performance when given user feedback challenges the assumption that LLM dialogue training equips models for interactive video search, suggesting a fundamental gap in how current architectures handle corrective signals over long temporal contexts. The plug-and-play FALM design means the approach can be retrofitted onto any existing EM-NLQ model without retraining the backbone, lowering the barrier for adoption. Open questions include closing the remaining gap between synthetic and human feedback quality, developing principled multi-turn feedback modeling beyond the current late-fusion averaging, and whether the feedback alignment paradigm generalizes to other interactive video tasks beyond egocentric moment localization.
