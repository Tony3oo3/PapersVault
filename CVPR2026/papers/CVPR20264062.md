## Problem

Discriminative dark optical flow estimators lack explicit noise modeling and are fragile to the heavy noise and weakened motion patterns in low-light scenes, while diffusion-based approaches introduce flow discontinuities and incur high runtime costs from iterative denoising. Flow matching offers a more efficient generative alternative, but its applicability to conditional dark optical flow estimation—where the target vector field is highly variable and structurally constrained by physical motion—remains entirely unexplored.

## Input / Output

FlowFM takes a pair of consecutive dark RGB frames (F1, F2) at 368×496 resolution and outputs a 2D optical flow field of shape 2×H×W encoding per-pixel X/Y displacements. The initial flow f0 is constructed from a coordinate grid at 1/8 input resolution combined with cost volume sampling, and Gaussian noise is injected into f0 to form the noisy flow that the decoder must denoise. Unlike recurrent decoders that perform 12 iterative residual updates, FlowFM produces the final flow in a single decoding step.

## Method

FlowFM replaces the conventional flow matching objective (regressing the variable target vector field u^t) with explicit flow field regression: the network v_Θ directly predicts the ground truth f^gt from the noisy map x^t using an L1 loss, which the authors prove satisfies the marginal conditions of flow matching in expectation, enabling one-step inference without time-step guidance during decoding. The noisy flow is synthesized by linearly interpolating Gaussian noise with ground truth and adding the result to the initial flow f0. The core decoder, the Implicit Fourier Denoising Decoder (IFDD), embeds a Fourier Motion Refactor (FMR) module inside GRU units: FMR applies FFT to extract amplitude and phase, enhances amplitude via a 1×1 convolution with LeakyReLU to recover noise-degraded motion intensity, preserves the original phase as a spatial anchor, and reconstructs the spatial-domain signal via IFFT. A gated attention encoder (GAE) and MLP-based context encoder (MCE) extract features from the dark frames, and a 4D cost volume built from cosine similarity provides inter-frame correspondence cues that condition the denoising process.

## Results

On the FCDN benchmark (trained on FCDN), FlowFM achieves an EPE of 0.87, outperforming the previous best CEDFlow++ (1.01) by 13.9% and CEDFlow (1.08) by 19.4%. On the VBOF benchmark, FlowFM achieves 13.28 EPE, surpassing the second-best CEDFlow++ (20.56) by 35.4%. When trained on the more complex Mix dataset, FlowFM still leads with 1.06 EPE on FCDN and 5.24 on VBOF, beating CEDFlow++ (1.14 / 6.25) by 7.0% and 16.2% respectively. FlowFM also achieves the lowest inference time (28 ms) and memory (1.3 GB) among all compared methods, thanks to its one-step denoising design.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FlowFM decisively outperforms all 10 baselines on both FCDN and VBOF under both training settings. The closest competitor is CEDFlow++ (IJCV-25), which achieves 1.01 EPE on FCDN and 20.56 on VBOF; FlowFM improves these to 0.87 and 13.28 respectively, a 13.9% and 35.4% reduction. Among diffusion-based methods, FlowDiffuser (CVPR-24) records 1.09 / 20.77, still trailing FlowFM by 20.7% and 36.1%. Classical discriminative baselines such as RAFT (1.23 / 21.84), GMA (1.18 / 21.77), and GAFlow (1.10 / 20.84) show substantially larger gaps, with FlowFM reducing VBOF EPE by roughly 40% over RAFT. The most recent DOFE-specific methods CEDFlow (1.08 / 20.89) and DPFlow (1.17 / 20.92) are also clearly surpassed. Notably, FlowFM achieves these gains while running in 28 ms with 1.3 GB memory—faster and lighter than every baseline (next fastest: CEDFlow++ at 57 ms / 1.9 GB)—thanks to its one-step denoising instead of 12 recurrent iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlowFM builds on RAFT's dual-branch encoder and cost volume architecture while replacing the recurrent GRU decoder with a flow matching framework that performs one-step denoising. It departs from the diffusion-model-based FlowDiffuser by avoiding iterative denoising (which breaks flow continuity) and from discriminative DOFE methods (CEDFlow, CEDFlow++) by introducing a generative paradigm with explicit flow field regression. The Implicit Fourier Denoising Decoder (IFDD) is a novel contribution that repurposes Fourier-domain amplitude/phase decomposition—previously used in low-light image enhancement—into the optical flow decoding pipeline, operating within a GRU-based recurrent unit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FlowFM demonstrates that flow matching can replace both discriminative regression and diffusion-based generation for dark optical flow, achieving one-step inference (28 ms) that outperforms 12-iteration recurrent methods by large margins (35%+ on VBOF). The Fourier-domain insight that noise primarily corrupts amplitude while preserving phase offers a principled, task-specific denoising strategy that could transfer to other frequency-sensitive vision tasks. However, quantitative evaluation is limited to two synthetic benchmarks (FCDN, VBOF); real-world results are qualitative only, and the theoretical justification for the explicit L1 constraint (Eq. 6) relies on expectation-level marginal conditions that may not hold tightly per-sample. Open questions include whether the one-step paradigm generalizes to other generative estimation tasks and whether the Fourier decoder's benefits persist under more diverse degradation types.
