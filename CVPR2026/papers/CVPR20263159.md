## Problem

Compositional 3D scene reconstruction from real-world videos remains challenging because existing methods either produce holistic representations unsuitable for simulation, rely on heuristic view selection that fails under heavy occlusion, or yield visually plausible but physically implausible scenes. The paper identifies two critical stage-transition bottlenecks: converting unstructured 3D geometry into effective image conditions for object generation, and ensuring physically stable assembly of generated assets in a simulator.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263159/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes only raw RGB video as input (no depth, normals, or semantic annotations) and produces a simulation-ready compositional 3D scene where each object is a complete mesh with PBR textures, 6-DoF pose, and physical attributes (mass, center of mass, material). The final output is an assembled scene in a physics simulator (Blender/Isaac Sim) guided by a scene graph encoding support and attachment relationships, rather than a purely visual rendering representation.

## Method

SimRecon implements a Perception-Generation-Simulation pipeline (2DGS + SceneSplat for semantic reconstruction, Rodin/CLAY for single-object generation, Blender/Isaac Sim for assembly) bridged by two key modules. Active Viewpoint Optimization formulates view selection as information-gain maximization, using 3DGS alpha-blending accumulated opacity as a differentiable proxy; it iteratively finds K views via gradient-based camera optimization with depth regularization and multiplicative opacity decay to progressively cover unobserved regions. Scene Graph Synthesizer partitions objects into DBSCAN regions, uses a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) to infer local support/attachment subgraphs from optimally rendered region images, then performs BFS-based online merging with conflict resolution (re-querying the VLM from adjudication viewpoints) to produce a globally consistent graph. This graph drives hierarchical physics-based assembly: support relations trigger brief active-rigid-body settling under gravity, while attachment relations apply fixed constraints, ensuring physical plausibility from the outset.

## Results

On 20 ScanNet scenes, SimRecon achieves the best reconstruction fidelity across all geometric and rendering metrics: CD of 4.34 (vs. 6.90 for the closest baseline InstaScene), F-Score of 62.65 (vs. 49.69), and NC of 87.37 (vs. 82.55). For novel-view rendering, it attains PSNR 24.43, SSIM 0.924, LPIPS 0.153, and MUSIQ 73.56, outperforming all baselines. Inference time is 21 minutes per scene, substantially faster than DPRecon (10 h 42 min) while remaining competitive with InstaScene (29 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SimRecon outperforms all four reconstruction baselines on every geometric and rendering metric. Against the closest scene-level competitor InstaScene, it reduces CD by 37% (4.34 vs. 6.90), improves F-Score by 13 points (62.65 vs. 49.69), and lowers LPIPS by nearly half (0.153 vs. 0.302), while also being 8 minutes faster. Against DPRecon, the gains are even larger in geometry (CD 4.34 vs. 9.26) and the method is roughly 30× faster (21 min vs. 10 h 42 min). Single-view methods Gen3DSR and SceneGen lag substantially on all metrics, confirming their limited generalization to real cluttered scenes. For physical plausibility, the paper qualitatively compares against MetaScenes, showing that SimRecon's scene-graph-guided hierarchical assembly avoids the floating objects and penetrations that MetaScenes' post-hoc MCMC collision resolution produces.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compositional 3D reconstruction line (DPRecon, InstaScene, Gen3DSR, SceneGen) by explicitly targeting simulation-ready output rather than visual fidelity alone. It builds on 2DGS/3DGS for the perception backbone, SceneSplat for instance segmentation, and Rodin (CLAY) for single-object generation, but departs from prior work by introducing two bridging modules: an information-theoretic Active Viewpoint Optimization that replaces heuristic view selection, and a progressive Scene Graph Synthesizer that replaces post-hoc collision resolution (as in MetaScenes) with a constructive, physics-informed hierarchical assembly. The modular 'Perception-Generation-Simulation' framing also deliberately decouples each stage from specific implementations, contrasting with tightly coupled pipelines like DPRecon's SDF+SDS approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a persistent 'real-to-sim' gap by making physical plausibility a first-class objective rather than a post-processing step, which is practically important for embodied AI and robotics simulation. The modular bridging design (AVO and SGS) is extensible to new perception or generation backends, which could accelerate adoption as those components improve. Limitations include evaluation on only 20 ScanNet scenes, dependence on a specific VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) for scene graph inference whose robustness to novel object categories is unclear, and the assumption that supportive/attached relations suffice for all physical interactions (articulated objects, deformables, etc. are not addressed). Open questions include scalability to large outdoor or dynamic scenes and whether the progressive graph merging generalizes beyond the DBSCAN spatial partitioning used here.
