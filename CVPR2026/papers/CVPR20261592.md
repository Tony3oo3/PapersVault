## Problem

Multi-label image classification models exploit dataset-specific spurious correlations rather than learning true causal mechanisms, causing performance collapse under distribution shift. Existing causal de-biasing methods either require instance-level bounding box annotations that are unavailable in large-scale industrial settings, or rely on static, pre-defined confounder dictionaries that cannot adapt to evolving feature spaces during training. No prior framework performs principled causal intervention using only image-level supervision while dynamically modeling confounders.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resolutions range from 224×224 on Sewer-ML to 576×576 on MS-COCO depending on backbone) and produces K multi-label class logits via a causal branch, alongside standard observational logits from a parallel classification head. Critically, the framework requires only image-level labels during training—no bounding boxes or instance annotations—making it applicable to industrial datasets where only whole-image labels exist. The backbone's intermediate feature maps and class activation maps are consumed internally for confounder extraction, but these are derived from the standard forward pass without additional supervision.

## Method

ProCI approximates Pearl's backdoor adjustment P(Y|do(X)) = Σ_z P(Y|X,z)P(z) through two synergistic modules. The Dynamic Confounder Memory (DCM) maintains a learnable prototype matrix M ∈ R^{K×D} where each row encodes the class-wise contextual bias; prototypes are updated via a two-fold strategy—within-batch attention-weighted aggregation (selectivity) followed by exponential moving average across batches (stability)—using CAM-guided feature extraction as the sole supervision signal. The Adaptive Causal Intervention (ACI) module disentangles the image representation into a global semantic feature (GAP) and a class-specific query (CAM features), then computes a sample-specific confounder estimate by attending over the memory prototypes conditioned on the query. The final causal logits are produced by an MLP that modulates the global feature with the estimated confounder, encouraging the classifier to rely on the causal path while accounting for the spurious path. Training jointly optimizes an asymmetric classification loss on the causal logits, a contrastive alignment loss anchoring extracted features to their prototypes, and a standard classification loss for stability.

## Results

On MS-COCO, ProCI sets a new state-of-the-art of 91.4% mAP with Swin-L(21k) backbone, surpassing the previous best Q2L-SwinL (90.5%) by +0.9 points, and achieves 90.7% mAP with the smaller Swin-Base(1k) backbone. On Pascal VOC 2007, ProCI reaches 96.0% mAP, improving over the prior best OPRL (95.1%) by +0.9 points. On the highly-confounded Sewer-ML industrial dataset, ProCI achieves 68.82% F2CIW and 92.65% F1Normal, outperforming the previous SOTA SPM by +5.44 points on F2CIW. Under co-occurrence shifts on COCO-Stuff, ProCI gains +8.3 mAP (exclusive) and +5.6 mAP (co-occur) over the prior best causal method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MS-COCO, ProCI consistently outperforms all 30+ baselines with matching backbones: with TResNet-L it surpasses CMLL (89.2→90.1 mAP) and PAT-T (88.7→90.1), and with Swin-L(21k) it beats Q2L-SwinL (90.5→91.4) while using a weaker pretraining source for Swin-Base. On Pascal VOC 2007, ProCI (96.0% mAP) edges out OPRL (95.1%), MambaML (95.0%), and C-TMS (94.8%), achieving best or tied-best AP on 14 of 20 classes. On Sewer-ML, ProCI (68.82% F2CIW) decisively beats the previous SOTA SPM (63.38%) by +5.44 points and the strongest end-to-end non-causal baseline TResNet-L (54.63%) by +14.19 points, while also surpassing all two-stage approaches including KSSNet (55.37%). Under COCO-Stuff co-occurrence shifts, ProCI (39.3/75.9/64.5 mAP) substantially outperforms the prior causal method Chen et al. (31.0/70.3/61.4) and feature-split (27.9/64.9/56.1), confirming superior robustness to distribution shift in label co-occurrence patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProCI sits at the intersection of causal inference for visual de-biasing and prototype learning, repurposing the latter from its traditional role in few-shot classification (representing discriminative class semantics) to explicitly encoding class-wise contextual confounders. It departs from prior causal MLC methods (CMLL, IDA, PAT-T) that either require instance-level bounding boxes or rely on static, pre-computed confounder dictionaries, by introducing a dynamically updated confounder memory (DCM) trained solely from CAM-guided features under image-level supervision. The backdoor adjustment framework from Pearl provides the theoretical target, while the alignment loss borrows the contrastive structure from momentum contrast, and the CAM-based weighted pooling follows Zhou et al. and Chen et al. as the operational signal for extracting contextual evidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in eliminating the bounding-box dependency that has limited the deployment of causal de-biasing methods to datasets with instance-level annotations, making causal intervention feasible for the large-scale industrial and web-scale image classification settings where only image-level labels exist. The +5.44-point margin on Sewer-ML and +8.3-point gain under COCO-Stuff exclusive shifts demonstrate that dynamic confounder modeling outperforms static dictionaries under severe, real-world bias. Open questions remain around scalability to very large vocabularies with diffuse co-occurrence patterns, and the paper acknowledges that abstract categories (e.g., 'sunset', 'street') without salient visual anchors challenge the CAM-based extraction mechanism, suggesting that more expressive confounder representations are needed.
