## Problem

Monocular depth estimation from foundation models produces relative (affine-invariant) depth that lacks metric scale, while single-image metric models suffer from poor temporal consistency and limited zero-shot generalization. Existing approaches to recover metric scale either require additional depth sensors (LiDAR) or depend on pre-trained metric depth models whose accuracy degrades out-of-distribution. The paper addresses how to recover stable, temporally consistent metric depth using only a monocular camera and a lightweight odometry signal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes consecutive RGB or long-wave infrared (LWIR) image frames together with a scalar metric baseline from wheel odometry or GPS (not full 6-DoF pose). It produces a per-pixel metric depth map and the relative camera pose (rotation Ω and translation T) between consecutive frames. No additional depth sensor or pre-trained metric depth model is required; the relative depth structure is supplied by a frozen Depth Anything V2 model, and the only external metric signal is the odometry-derived baseline distance.

## Method

The pipeline first estimates optical flow (DIS Flow for RGB, FieldScale-preprocessed for thermal) and recovers camera pose via the Longuet-Higgins-Prazdny motion field, using RANSAC with grid-cell-balanced sampling to reject dynamic-object outliers and the odometry baseline to fix translation scale. Sparse metric depth is then obtained by triangulating flow correspondences under the estimated pose, with per-pixel Sampson residuals serving as geometric reliability scores. Rather than fusing depth maps directly, the method tracks a latent scale field S (where Z = S · d^rel) through a recursive Bayesian update: the prior is the warped previous-frame posterior, the observation is the triangulated scale, and a chi-square-gated Kalman gain with a consistency-capped upper bound prevents aggressive updates. Finally, Felzenszwalb superpixel segmentation consolidates per-pixel scales into per-segment medians, absorbing the shift component of the affine-invariant model output that a single global scale cannot capture, yielding the final metric depth as S_seg · d^rel.

## Results

On out-of-distribution datasets, PTC-Depth achieves the best metric depth accuracy: AbsRel of 0.427 (δ<1.25 = 0.688) on TartanAir, 0.309 (δ<1.25 = 0.725) on the custom Roadside dataset, and 0.480 (δ<1.25 = 0.520) on Forest, substantially outperforming UniDepth (e.g., 0.503/0.176 on TartanAir) and VDA (e.g., 2.198/0.010 on Roadside). On KITTI, where UniDepth's training distribution matches, UniDepth retains the edge (AbsRel 0.047 vs. 0.137), but PTC-Depth remains competitive. Temporal consistency (TAE) is maintained at 5.35 on KITTI and 5.42 on TartanAir, comparable to single-image baselines while providing metric scale. On the custom wheel-odometry dataset, the method achieves ~3× higher δ₁ accuracy than the MADPose triangulation baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against UniDepth (CVPR 2024), PTC-Depth loses on KITTI (AbsRel 0.137 vs. 0.047) where UniDepth's training distribution aligns, but wins decisively on all out-of-distribution datasets: TartanAir (0.427 vs. 0.503 AbsRel, δ<1.25 0.688 vs. 0.176), Roadside (0.309 vs. 0.465, δ 0.725 vs. 0.201), and Forest (0.480 vs. 0.444 AbsRel but δ 0.520 vs. 0.088). Against DA v2 metric, PTC-Depth achieves lower AbsRel on TartanAir (0.427 vs. 0.513) and Roadside (0.309 vs. 0.494) with substantially higher δ<1.25 scores. Against VDA (CVPR 2025), PTC-Depth dominates on metric accuracy across all datasets (e.g., Roadside AbsRel 0.309 vs. 2.198) while maintaining competitive TAE (5.27 vs. 1.98), though VDA's artificially low TAE reflects a systematically biased (underestimated) depth that is temporally consistent but metrically wrong. In the triangulation evaluation (Table 1), PTC-Depth using only wheel odometry matches or exceeds MADPose (which requires a pretrained metric depth model) on all out-of-distribution datasets, achieving ~3× higher δ₁ on the custom dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Depth Anything V2 as a pre-trained relative depth foundation model, but departs fundamentally from both video-based depth estimation (e.g., VDA) and depth completion approaches. Rather than learning temporal consistency through multi-frame training or requiring LiDAR-based sparse depth, it recovers metric scale geometrically via optical flow triangulation constrained by wheel odometry, then applies recursive Bayesian filtering on a latent per-pixel scale field. The conceptual lineage traces to classical structure-from-motion (Longuet-Higgins-Prazdny motion field, Sampson residual) repurposed as an observation model for a Kalman-style update, with Felzenszwalb superpixel segmentation to handle the shift component of affine-invariant depth outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, sensor-light solution for mobile robots that have only a camera and wheel odometry, achieving metric depth with temporal consistency and zero-shot generalization to thermal and forest environments without retraining. Key limitations include failure under rotation-dominant motion (degenerate epipolar geometry), large dynamic-object coverage (>50% of frame), and diminished accuracy in the far range (20–80 m) where parallax is insufficient for reliable triangulation. Open questions include whether learned geometric reasoning can handle rotation-heavy segments while preserving zero-shot generalizability, and whether the Bayesian scale framework extends to multi-camera or event-camera settings.
