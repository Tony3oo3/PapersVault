## Problem

Existing fairness-enhanced deepfake detectors either fail to generalize across domains or degrade detection accuracy when reducing demographic bias. Prior strategies—resampling, loss reweighting, and feature disentanglement—address fairness in isolation without simultaneously preserving the discriminative forgery cues needed for accurate detection, leaving a gap in jointly optimizing inter-group and intra-group fairness with overall AUC.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260492/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB facial images annotated with sensitive attributes (gender, race, and their intersectional combinations such as Male-White, Female-Asian) and outputs a binary real/fake classification score. Training uses the FaceForensics++ dataset with batch size 64, and evaluation spans four benchmarks (FF++, DFDC, DFD, Celeb-DF) where test sets are partitioned by demographic subgroups to compute group-specific FPR, demographic parity, and es-AUC alongside overall AUC.

## Method

The framework operates in two synergistic stages. First, Structural Fairness Decoupling (SFD) computes a per-channel fairness index via a Soft Nearest Neighbor Loss that measures how strongly each convolutional channel's feature map clusters samples by sensitive attribute; channels in the bottom percentage (e.g., 2%) of this index are decoupled to sever the model's reliance on demographic-correlated features. Second, Global Distribution Alignment (GDA) formulates an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem (solved via Sinkhorn-Knopp) that minimizes the transport cost between each sensitive subgroup's predicted real/fake distributions and the corresponding global distributions, with a mutual information penalty enforcing statistical independence between the sensitive attribute and the prediction. The final training objective is the sum of cross-entropy classification loss and the averaged transport-based fairness loss across all subgroups, weighted by a balancing hyperparameter λ.

## Results

On the intra-domain FF++ test set, the proposed method achieves 97.71% AUC (vs. 97.66% for PG-FDD and 97.46% for DAW-FDD) while attaining the best gender F_FPR (0.53%), gender F_DP (3.61%), and gender es-AUC (96.45%). In cross-domain evaluation, the method achieves the highest overall AUC on all three held-out datasets: 61.86% on DFDC, 76.75% on Celeb-DF, and 81.46% on DFD. On DFD, it also records the best es-AUC across all three attribute settings (78.98% gender, 77.44% race, 69.73% intersection), demonstrating strong fairness generalization without sacrificing detection accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DAG-FDD and DAW-FDD [20], the proposed method consistently outperforms on both fairness and detection: on FF++ gender, F_FPR drops from 1.82/0.78 to 0.53 and F_DP from 4.65/9.52 to 3.61, while AUC improves from 96.72/97.46 to 97.71. Against PG-FDD [24], the method wins on gender F_FPR (0.53 vs. 0.62), gender F_DP (3.61 vs. 4.74), and AUC (97.71 vs. 97.66) on FF++, and achieves the best es-AUC on DFD across all attribute settings (78.98/77.44/69.73 vs. 76.37/74.81/66.32), though PG-FDD retains a slight edge on intersection F_FPR for FF++ (9.19 vs. 20.18). Against RSEF-FDD [18], the method dominates on FF++ gender (F_FPR 0.53 vs. 0.57, F_DP 3.61 vs. 8.55, es-AUC 96.45 vs. 94.91) and achieves higher AUC on all cross-domain datasets (e.g., 81.46 vs. 80.54 on DFD). Fairadapter [10], designed for AI-generated image detection with a ViT-L/14 backbone, performs substantially worse across all metrics (e.g., 71.50% AUC on FF++ vs. 97.71%), confirming that its fairness strategy does not transfer to the deepfake detection setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two previously separate fairness strategies in deepfake detection: channel-level structural decoupling (related to but distinct from the feature-space disentanglement of Lin et al. [24]) and distributional alignment via entropy-regularized optimal transport (a mechanism not previously applied to this task). Prior work has addressed fairness through data resampling [29,43], loss reweighting [20], or disentanglement [24], each with the trade-off of either poor generalization or degraded detection accuracy. The key conceptual departure is the two-stage synergy: first identifying and removing attribute-sensitive channels via a Soft Nearest Neighbor Loss-based fairness index, then aligning group-level prediction distributions to the global distribution using Sinkhorn-Knopp optimal transport, which the authors argue allows the model to extract 'common sense' from the decoupled features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fairness in deepfake detection is critical for equitable deployment in judicial, financial, and media contexts where biased error rates across demographic groups can cause real harm. The paper's main open question is whether the channel decoupling strategy generalizes beyond the specific convolutional layer it targets and to more complex intersectional attribute combinations beyond gender×race. Limitations include evaluation restricted to facial images (not video), a fixed decoupling ratio (2%) determined empirically, and the optimal transport computation that, while reduced to O(n²), still adds per-batch overhead. The approach could be extended to video deepfake detection and to additional sensitive attributes such as age or disability.
