## Problem

Existing diffusion bridge models such as I2SB inherit the standard score-matching training scheme, which creates a fundamental noise level mismatch at the target endpoint (t→0): the network input becomes nearly deterministic while the regression target becomes pure noise. This ill-conditioned mapping causes a dual failure—variance collapse and directional error—that caps the achievable restoration quality during the final refinement stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260676/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For restoration tasks, the method takes a single degraded RGB image (JPEG-compressed, blurred, or 4× downsampled) at 256×256 resolution and produces a restored high-quality image. For image-to-image translation, it takes a 64×64 edge map and generates the corresponding object image. The degraded input is first passed through a frozen Mean Network to obtain a cleaner intermediate endpoint before the stochastic bridge is constructed, so the restoration network never directly sees the raw degraded image as a bridge endpoint.

## Method

NADB introduces two coupled innovations to resolve the endpoint underfitting. First, a Magnitude-Aligned Stochastic Interpolant redefines the bridge path as X_t = (1−t^α)X_0 + t^α X̂_0 + kt(1−t)Z with a corresponding target Y_t = (X̂_0−X_0) + kt^{1−α}(1−t)Z, ensuring both the input and target noise coefficients vanish at t=0 and t=1 (Proposition 1), eliminating the variance mismatch. Second, a Mean Network M(X_1;φ) trained with MSE approximates the posterior mean E[X_0|X_1], providing a cleaner bridge endpoint X̂_0 that is provably closer to the target in Wasserstein-2 distance (Theorem 2), thereby correcting the directional error. Both networks use identical U-Net architectures initialized from the ADM checkpoint, trained with α=0.4 and k=0.75. The reverse sampling process splits at a threshold d≈(1−α)/(2−α) to maintain non-negative variance near the target endpoint.

## Results

On the primary I2SB comparison (Table 1, NFE=100), NADB achieves FID 4.3 on JPEG-5 restoration (vs. 4.7 for I2SB), FID 3.7 on 4× super-resolution with Pool filter (vs. 4.1), and FID 3.1 on Gaussian deblurring (vs. 3.9), with the largest PSNR gain in Gaussian deblurring (30.34 vs. 23.98 dB). Against conditional diffusion models at NFE=100 (Table 2), NADB achieves FID 4.3 on JPEG-5, 1.0 on Bicubic 4× SR, and 3.1 on Gaussian deblurring, outperforming DDRM, IIGDM, DDNM, and Palette across all three tasks. On image translation (Table 3), NADB reaches FID 111.3 on edges→handbags and 117.8 on edges→shoes, beating both I2SB and DDBM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NADB consistently outperforms I2SB, its direct baseline, across all six restoration sub-tasks (JPEG-5/10, Pool/Bicubic SR, Uniform/Gaussian deblurring) on both FID and distortion metrics, with the most dramatic gains in deblurring (e.g., Gaussian: FID 3.9→3.1, PSNR 23.98→30.34 dB, SSIM 0.66→0.88). Against conditional diffusion models at NFE=100, NADB achieves substantially lower FID than DDRM (4.3 vs. 28.2 on JPEG-5), IIGDM (4.3 vs. 8.6), Palette (4.3 vs. 8.3), and DDNM (1.0 vs. 13.6 on Bicubic SR), confirming the advantage of the bridge formulation over the destroy-then-reconstruct paradigm. On image translation, NADB beats DDBM (FID 111.3 vs. 114.3 on edges→handbags; 117.8 vs. 120.1 on edges→shoes) and I2SB (111.3 vs. 116.0; 117.8 vs. 119.5), while also maintaining quality at low NFE where DDBM degrades significantly. The ablation confirms both components are necessary: magnitude alignment alone fixes variance collapse but not direction, and the mean network alone does not resolve the ill-conditioned mapping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on I2SB (Image-to-Image Schrödinger Bridge) and the stochastic interpolant framework, but departs from the standard score-matching training objective that I2SB and related diffusion bridge methods inherit from vanilla diffusion models. The key conceptual departure is identifying that this mimicry creates a fundamental noise-level mismatch at the target endpoint (t→0), where the network input becomes nearly deterministic while the regression target becomes pure noise. Rather than patching the existing formulation, NADB redesigns the interpolant path (magnitude-aligned stochastic interpolant) and introduces a mean network to reduce the distributional gap, positioning the work as a correction to the bridge paradigm itself rather than an incremental sampler or architecture improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of endpoint underfitting as a structural flaw in the I2SB-style formulation is a useful diagnostic for the broader diffusion bridge community, as it suggests that any method inheriting the same input-target noise mismatch may suffer similar degradation at the boundary. The ablation showing that neither the mean network nor the magnitude-aligned interpolant alone suffices reinforces that the two failures (direction and magnitude) are genuinely independent. Open questions include whether the specific interpolant form (α=0.4, k=0.75) is optimal, whether the approach extends to higher resolutions or video, and whether the mean network's over-smoothing introduces a ceiling on achievable fidelity that a learned, task-specific endpoint could surpass.
