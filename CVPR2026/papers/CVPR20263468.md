## Problem

Momentum-based optimizers use a fixed coefficient β to blend past and current gradients, locking the estimator into a rigid bias-variance trade-off: as β→1, classical momentum diverges in both bias and variance, while EMA suffers unbounded bias from outdated gradients. No existing method dynamically adapts the blending weight to the observed noise level, leaving a gap between the theoretical optimum (minimum mean-squared-error estimation) and what fixed-momentum schemes actually achieve during training.

## Input / Output

SGDF consumes per-element stochastic gradients g_t (computed from mini-batches of RGB images at standard resolutions: 32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet, 384×384 for ViT fine-tuning) and the current parameter vector θ_{t-1}. It maintains two running statistics per element — a bias-corrected first moment m̂_t and a variance estimate ŝ_t — and outputs a single filtered gradient ĝ_t = m̂_t + K_t^γ(g_t − m̂_t) that replaces the raw gradient in the parameter update θ_t = θ_{t−1} − α_t ĝ_t. The gain K_t is computed element-wise and time-varyingly, so the method is a drop-in gradient refinement module compatible with any downstream optimizer (SGD, Adam, sign-based, Muon).

## Method

SGDF reformulates the momentum update as an online optimal linear filtering problem: the bias-corrected momentum m̂_t serves as the prediction, the current gradient g_t as the observation, and the fused estimate is a variance-weighted interpolation ĝ_t = (1−K_t)m̂_t + K_t g_t. The gain K_t = ŝ_t / (ŝ_t + (g_t − m̂_t)² + ε) is derived by minimizing the MSE of the fused estimate, where ŝ_t is a bias-corrected exponential moving average of (g_t − m_t)² following AdaBelief's variance estimation. A power-scaling exponent γ_t = γ_0/√t (with γ_0 = 1/2) modulates the gain to ensure O(√T) regret in convex settings and O(log T / √T) convergence in non-convex stochastic optimization. The construction is equivalent to fusing two independent Gaussians (the momentum prediction and the noisy observation) via Bayesian posterior mean, and it requires no additional parameters or curvature estimates beyond the two running moments already present in Adam-type optimizers.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet18, SGDF achieves 70.51% Top-1 and 89.69% Top-5 accuracy, outperforming SGD (70.23%), AdaBelief (70.08%), and Adam (63.79%). On PASCAL VOC object detection (Faster-RCNN + FPN, ResNet50 backbone), SGDF reaches 83.81 mAP, surpassing the next-best AdaBelief (81.02) by 2.79 points. Across CIFAR-10/100 with VGG11, ResNet34, and DenseNet121, SGDF consistently achieves the highest test accuracy among all compared optimizers. In ViT post-training across six datasets, SGDF improves over SGD by 0.1–6.75 points (e.g., 92.68% vs 89.71% on Oxford-IIIT-Pets with ViT-B/32).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGDF outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. Against the closest competitor AdaBelief [79], SGDF leads by 0.43 points on ImageNet Top-1 (70.51 vs 70.08) and by 2.79 mAP on PASCAL VOC (83.81 vs 81.02). The margin over Adam [38] is substantial: 6.72 points on ImageNet Top-1 and 5.14 mAP on VOC. RAdam [44] and AdamW [45] trail by 2.89 and 2.58 points respectively on ImageNet, while MSVAG [1] and AdaBound [47] lag by 4.52 and 2.38 points. On the ViT post-training benchmark, SGDF beats SGD [50] on all six datasets, with the largest gain of 6.75 points on Oxford-IIIT-Pets (ViT-B/32). In the diffusion-model setting, Muon+SGDF [33] achieves a FID of 64.24 versus Adam's 68.32, and Sign-SGDF converges faster than Adam on DiT/SiT-Base. The only comparison where SGDF does not strictly dominate is Filter-Adam vs Vanilla-Adam on DenseNet121 (both at 74.89%), where the residual/dense connections already stabilize gradients enough to neutralize the filter's advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

SGDF reinterprets momentum-based gradient estimation through the lens of Optimal Linear Filtering (Kalman, 1960), deriving a time-varying gain K_t via the MMSE principle to replace the fixed momentum coefficient. It borrows the second-moment variance estimation mechanism from AdaBelief [79] and builds its SDE-based bias-variance analysis on prior continuous-time formulations of SGD [62]. The paper explicitly departs from static-interpolation approaches like QHM and heuristic frequency-scaling methods like Grokfast, and distinguishes itself from heavier Kalman-based optimizers (e.g., Koala, Kalman Gradient Descent) by requiring no curvature estimation or Fisher information, keeping computational overhead at the level of standard Adam.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SDE analysis revealing that stochastic noise σ itself amplifies parameter-shift bias in momentum estimators fills a gap left by prior work that assumed stationary parameters, and the resulting O(√T) / O(log T/√T) convergence guarantees with constant β coefficients (unlike Adam's decaying β) are a clean theoretical contribution. The plug-and-play extensibility to Adam, sign-based optimizers, and Muon suggests the filter module could become a reusable component in future optimizer design. However, the paper is limited to vision benchmarks (CIFAR, ImageNet, VOC, ViT transfer), the memory footprint matches Adam's, and the empirical gains over strong baselines are modest (typically 0.2–1.5%), leaving open whether the advantage persists at LLM scale or in non-vision domains.
