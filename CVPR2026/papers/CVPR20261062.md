## Problem

Existing multimodal document QA research predominantly generates text-only answers, underutilizing the rich visual elements (tables, charts, figures, diagrams) embedded in real-world documents. While a few recent works have begun interleaving visual elements into answers, they either operate on narrow-domain collections or provide limited treatment of how to detect and cite visual regions directly from raw document page images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261062/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a user query and a context of top-K relevant document page images retrieved via ColPali over a multi-domain corpus (academic papers, web pages, textbooks, guidebooks, research reports, financial reports, slides). The output is a structured long-form answer with a header and body in which each cited visual element (chart, table, or figure) is inserted as an image immediately before the sentence that references it, with the citation made via a unique identifier token. Under Page Encoding the model receives full-page images plus bounding-box coordinates as text tokens; under Modality Encoding it receives OCR-extracted text tokens and separately cropped visual-element images.

## Method

The core contribution is VinQA, a 42,700-example training set (plus 1,605 test) built by collecting raw documents from six public QA datasets, textualizing pages with OCR and DocLayout bounding-box detection, generating questions via ColPali embedding clustering, retrieving top-K pages, and prompting an LLM to produce grounded answers with interleaved visual citations. Two encoding strategies feed raw page images into an MLLM: Page Encoding directly encodes full-page images with DocLayout-detected bounding boxes as citable regions, while Modality Encoding parses each page into OCR text and cropped visual elements encoded as separate modality-specific tokens. The paper fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-7B on VinQA for 3 epochs (16×A100) and evaluates with M-GroSE (a multimodal extension of GroUSE scoring relevance, completeness, faithfulness, and unanswerability), Visual Source F1 for citation accuracy, and Visual G-Eval (a GPT-5-based judge scoring effectiveness, position, and faithfulness of each cited image). A key finding is that Modality Encoding is initially more robust on complex documents, but after VinQA fine-tuning Page Encoding reaches comparable performance without explicit parsing.

## Results

On the VinQA test split, the best proprietary model (GPT-4.1 with Modality Encoding) achieves an M-GroSE Avg of 3.63 and Visual Source F1 of 0.72, while the best open-source model before fine-tuning (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) scores only 2.08 (M-GroSE Avg) and 0.40 (Visual Source F1) under Modality Encoding. Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on the VinQA training split substantially closes this gap, reaching 3.34 M-GroSE Avg and 0.55 Visual Source F1 under Page Encoding, and 3.33 and 0.58 under Modality Encoding. Modality Encoding generally outperforms Page Encoding before fine-tuning (positive deltas in Table 3), but after VinQA training the two encoding strategies reach comparable performance levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B against its own pre-fine-tuning version and against InternVL3-8B under both Page and Modality Encoding. Under Page Encoding, fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B (VinQA) improves M-GroSE Avg from 1.99 to 3.34 (+1.35) and Visual Source F1 from 0.31 to 0.55 (+0.24), substantially outperforming InternVL3-8B (2.05 / 0.23). Under Modality Encoding, the same fine-tuned model improves from 2.08 to 3.33 (+1.25) on M-GroSE Avg and from 0.40 to 0.58 (+0.18) on Visual Source F1, again far exceeding InternVL3-8B (2.24 / 0.32). After fine-tuning, the gap between Page and Modality Encoding nearly vanishes (−0.02 M-GroSE Avg, +0.02 Visual Source F1), whereas before fine-tuning Modality Encoding consistently leads by 0.09–0.19 on M-GroSE Avg and 0.09 on Visual Source F1. Despite these gains, the fine-tuned open model still trails the best proprietary models (GPT-4.1 at 3.63 / 0.72; Claude 3.5 Sonnet at 3.62 / 0.70) by roughly 0.3 on M-GroSE Avg and 0.12–0.14 on Visual Source F1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal document QA line of work (VisRAG, VDocRAG, M-LongDoc) by introducing a visual-element citation mechanism into two existing encoding strategies—Page Encoding (from VisRAG) and Modality Encoding (from M-LongDoc)—so that generated answers interleave cited visual elements with supporting text rather than remaining text-only. For evaluation, it adapts GroUSE into M-GroSE for multimodal grounded answers and adopts Visual Source F1 from MCiteBench, while adding Visual G-Eval to directly assess visual citation quality. The key departure from prior citation-generation work (MCiteBench, VISA, MDocSum) is operating on raw document page images across seven real-world domains rather than pre-extracted text snippets or cropped figures from a single domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VinQA provides the first large-scale dataset (42.7K training, 1.6K test) specifically for visual-element-interleaved long-form answer generation over diverse real-world documents, filling a gap where prior benchmarks either produced text-only answers or operated on narrow, pre-processed contexts. The finding that Page Encoding—despite requiring the model to read text from pixels and interpret bounding boxes—matches Modality Encoding after fine-tuning suggests that explicit parsing may be less critical than previously assumed, potentially simplifying deployment pipelines. Open questions include whether these gains hold for test-time reasoning models, how performance scales to even longer contexts (degradation observed beyond 7.5k tokens), and whether the single-model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) fine-tuning results generalize across architectures.
