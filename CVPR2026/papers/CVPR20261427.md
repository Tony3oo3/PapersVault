## Problem

State-of-the-art temporal online HD mapping methods exhibit a severe 'backward-looking' bias: their performance gains concentrate in rearward (already-traversed) regions while providing minimal improvement in the forward, unseen road ahead. This is a critical safety mismatch because downstream trajectory prediction degrades sharply with forward perception errors but is largely insensitive to rearward errors, as demonstrated by the paper's masking experiments. The paper introduces A-mAP and R-mAP metrics to quantify this forward-backward asymmetry and proposes a solution that enhances forward perception without adding inference cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261427/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the student model consumes only the current frame of multi-view RGB images (6 cameras on nuScenes, 7 on Argoverse 2) and outputs instance-level vectorized map elements—pedestrian crossings, lane dividers, and road boundaries—represented as learned queries with associated geometry and class predictions. No historical or future frames are processed during deployment, preserving single-frame latency (20.1 FPS with R50, 31.5 FPS with R18). The teacher model, which accesses 4 future cached BEV features and a future vector memory buffer during training, is entirely discarded at inference time.

## Method

AMap employs a 'distill-from-future' paradigm: a MapTracker-based teacher (R50) with future temporal context is pre-trained, then its representations are transferred to a lightweight current-frame student (R18) with all temporal modules removed. Two key distillation components address the asymmetric teacher-student setting: (1) Multi-Level BEV Distillation applies a ground-truth segmentation mask to suppress background noise and distills both basic and refined BEV features via masked L2 loss; (2) Asymmetric Query Distillation uses Hungarian matching to establish one-to-one correspondences between the student's 100 static queries and the teacher's 116 dynamically tracked queries, then minimizes KL divergence on matched logit pairs. This combination transfers forward-looking spatial-semantic knowledge into the student's static queries, yielding a 1.65-point A-mAP gain over the baseline student while matching or exceeding temporal models in forward-region accuracy at single-frame inference cost.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set with a ResNet-50 backbone, AMap achieves 69.26 mAP, 70.19 A-mAP, and 69.61 R-mAP at 20.1 FPS, outperforming the strongest non-temporal baseline MapTracker (68.30 mAP, 69.30 A-mAP) by +0.96 mAP and +0.89 A-mAP. With a lightweight ResNet-18 backbone, AMap reaches 64.49 mAP and 66.28 A-mAP at 31.5 FPS, surpassing MapTracker (R18) by +1.68 mAP and +1.65 A-mAP. Notably, AMap's A-mAP of 70.19 (R50) exceeds that of the temporal MapTracker (70.03) while running at single-frame inference cost, and on Argoverse 2 it gains +2.52 A-mAP over the R18 baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMap (R50) outperforms all non-temporal baselines on every metric: it beats MapTR by +25.16 mAP, MapTRv2 by +7.88 mAP, MapQR by +2.91 mAP, and MapTracker (non-temporal) by +0.96 mAP, with the largest A-mAP gains over MapTRv2 (+5.98) and MapQR (+1.81). Against temporal models, AMap's A-mAP of 70.19 marginally exceeds temporal MapTracker (70.03) and substantially surpasses HRMapNet (62.34) and StreamMapNet (54.94), while temporal MapTracker retains a higher R-mAP (78.92 vs. 69.61) due to its rearward bias. In the KD comparison (Table 4), AMap's Future→Static distillation yields +1.68 mAP over the R18 student baseline, whereas BEVDistill and MapDistill degrade performance by −4.84 and −5.61 mAP respectively, confirming that standard distillation methods fail under the asymmetric temporal setting. On Argoverse 2, AMap (R18) improves over the MapTracker baseline by +1.56 mAP and +2.52 A-mAP, while the future-prior teacher achieves 80.79 A-mAP versus 73.00 for the past-prior teacher, validating the forward-aware advantage of future priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MapTracker (ECCV 2024) as the architectural backbone for both teacher and student, and extends the BEV-level distillation paradigm from BEVDistill and MapDistill by adding ground-truth-guided spatial masking and multi-level (basic + refined) feature supervision. At the query level, it adapts the Hungarian-matching strategy from DETRDistill to handle the asymmetric setting where the teacher uses dynamically tracked queries while the student uses static learnable queries. The key conceptual departure from prior temporal mapping work (MapTracker, StreamMapNet, HRMapNet) is the shift from historical-frame fusion to a 'distill-from-future' paradigm, where future temporal context serves as privileged training-time supervision rather than inference-time input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of a systematic 'backward-looking' bias in state-of-the-art temporal mappers, coupled with the downstream evidence that forward perception errors are far more consequential for planning than rearward errors, reframes how the community should evaluate and design online mapping systems. The A-mAP/R-mAP metrics provide a practical tool to expose this asymmetry in future work. Open questions include whether the distill-from-future strategy generalizes to dynamic element prediction (e.g., moving agents), whether the teacher's lack of physical meaning at inference limits real-world deployment confidence, and whether the modest absolute gains (+1.68 mAP) scale to more complex or longer-horizon driving scenarios.
