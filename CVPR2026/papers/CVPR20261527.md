## Problem

Existing VLM-based open-vocabulary part segmentation methods suffer from two complementary failures: object over-segmentation, where CLIP's cross-modal similarity maps produce overly broad activations that spill into structurally irrelevant regions, and part under-segmentation, where weak fine-grained perception causes small parts to be incompletely covered or entirely missed. Prior work addresses these issues in isolation, but no method simultaneously suppresses false object-level activations while expanding incomplete part-level responses without pixel-level supervision.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and text prompts specifying part category names (e.g., "cat head", "dog tail"), where part categories are tightly coupled to their parent object class so that identically named parts from different objects are distinct labels. It produces a hierarchical output: first an object-level binary segmentation mask per object class, then part-level segmentation masks restricted to the predicted object region. The object mask is used to crop and resize the image before part segmentation, effectively enlarging the object area to enhance fine-grained perception at the patch resolution used by CLIP and DINO.

## Method

HOPS is a two-stage hierarchical framework built on CATSeg's cost aggregation pipeline (Swin Transformer spatial aggregation + transformer class aggregation), augmented with two parameter-free modules that exploit the complementarity between CLIP's semantic attention and DINO's structural attention. In the object stage, the Attention-Aware Filtering Module (AFM) performs bidirectional attention fusion: DINO's softmax-normalized attention is element-wise multiplied with CLIP's attention to suppress semantically-related-but-structurally-irrelevant patch pairs, while CLIP's mean attention serves as a threshold to filter DINO's attention for semantic relevance; the fused semantic-structural attention is further gated by a cross-layer reliable association mask and a per-class confidence mask to refine the object cost map. In the part stage, the Affinity-Guided Enhancement Module (AEM) reuses the same bidirectional fusion to build an N×N affinity matrix, then iteratively propagates part responses within bounding-box constraints around initial activation regions, with an adaptive stopping criterion based on per-iteration gain and a one-shot small-part compensation mechanism to prevent premature termination for weakly activated parts.

## Results

On Pascal-Part-116, HOPS achieves 54.77% mIoU on seen parts and 44.81% on unseen parts, yielding a harmonic mean (h-IoU) of 49.29%, surpassing the previous SOTA PartCATSeg by 2.15% and 4.30% respectively. On PartImageNet, HOPS reaches 60.81% (seen) and 57.46% (unseen) mIoU with h-IoU of 59.08%. On the more challenging ADE20K-Part-234, HOPS attains 40.11% (seen) and 20.84% (unseen) mIoU with h-IoU of 27.42%. HOPS also reduces the Over-Segmentation Index (mOSI) and Under-Segmentation Index (mUSI) by 6.11% and 6.17% over PartCATSeg on Pascal-Part-116, confirming improved mask precision and completeness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HOPS outperforms all seven baselines across all three zero-shot benchmarks. Against the closest competitor PartCATSeg (CVPR 2025), HOPS gains +2.15% seen and +4.30% unseen mIoU on Pascal-Part-116, +3.48% and +4.39% on PartImageNet, and +1.24% and +3.28% on ADE20K-Part-234, while also achieving the lowest mOSI and mUSI in every setting. Compared to the hierarchical method PBAPS (NeurIPS 2024), HOPS improves h-IoU by 5.31 points on Pascal-Part-116 and 16.02 points on PartImageNet, demonstrating that the AFM+AEM combination substantially narrows the gap between seen and unseen performance. Against the one-stage baselines (CLIPSeg, CAT-Seg, PartCLIPSeg), HOPS leads by 11–27 mIoU points on unseen categories, confirming that the hierarchical object-then-part pipeline with attention-aware filtering is critical for open-vocabulary generalization. In cross-dataset transfer (PartImageNet OOD and PartImageNet→Pascal-Part-116), HOPS achieves 45.08% unseen mIoU on PartImageNet OOD versus 40.17% for PartCATSeg, and 21.17% on the cross-dataset transfer versus 18.21%, further validating its robustness under distribution shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

HOPS builds directly on CATSeg's cost aggregation framework and extends PartCATSeg, which first introduced DINO features as structural guidance via simple concatenation. The key conceptual departure is the bidirectional semantic-structural attention fusion: rather than treating DINO as a passive structural prior, HOPS lets DINO's attention actively filter CLIP's semantically-broad activations (suppressing co-occurrence artifacts) while CLIP's attention in turn constrains DINO to semantically relevant regions. The hierarchical object-then-part pipeline follows the paradigm established by PartGLEE and PBAPS, but HOPS adds targeted modules (AFM for object refinement, AEM for part response propagation) that operate without any additional trainable parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By isolating and addressing two specific failure modes—object over-segmentation from CLIP's coarse spatial activations and part under-segmentation from weak fine-grained responses—HOPS provides a clean diagnostic framework that other OVPS methods can adopt. The parameter-free nature of both AFM and AEM makes the approach easy to plug into existing pipelines, and the bidirectional fusion principle (each model constrains the other's blind spots) is a reusable design pattern beyond part segmentation. Open questions include whether the iterative propagation in AEM scales to very small or highly occluded parts, whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D part segmentation, and whether the reliance on both CLIP and DINO self-attention matrices (rather than just features) limits applicability to architectures without explicit attention layers.
