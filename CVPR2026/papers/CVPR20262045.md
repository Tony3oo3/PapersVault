## Problem

Wildfire risk prediction requires integrating visual, climatic, and geographic factors through causal reasoning to infer continuous risk maps, yet existing methods either rely on coarse meteorological indices (e.g., FWI) or learn direct image-to-risk correlations without reasoning about underlying drivers. No unified benchmark or framework exists that couples multi-source data with reasoning-intensive risk estimation and enables systematic cross-continental generalization testing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262045/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes Sentinel-2 Level 2A cloud-free summer mosaics at 10 m resolution (1024×1024 px per ~100 km² tile) and a 60-dimensional monthly climatology vector (temperature, precipitation, humidity, wind speed/direction) from NASA POWER at 50 km resolution. The Oracle outputs a scalar ordinal risk score (0–9) accompanied by a free-text CoT trace; the Encoder-Decoder then produces a 341×341 continuous risk raster at 30 m/pixel, quantile-normalized to [0,1], derived from the Wildfire Risk to Communities 'Risk to Potential Structures' product.

## Method

FireScope is a two-stage reasoning-to-generation pipeline. In stage 1, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct is fine-tuned as an 'Oracle' via GRPO (a critic-free RL algorithm) with a reward of 0.9·accuracy + 0.1·format, encouraging it to produce explicit CoT reasoning before emitting a scalar risk estimate. In stage 2, a lightweight vision Encoder-Decoder (SegFormer, AlphaEarth, or U-Net) regresses the 341×341 risk raster, conditioned on the Oracle's scalar through FiLM layers inserted before each trainable block, and trained with a composite loss of Smooth-L1 reconstruction, SSIM structure, and first-order edge matching. The key design choice is the scalar bottleneck: the Oracle must distill all multimodal reasoning into a single interpretable number, which the vision decoder then uses as a spatially uniform contextual prior via FiLM rather than as concatenated metadata. Ablations show that CoT-trained Oracles yield substantially better OOD generalization than supervised-fine-tuned Oracles, and that attaching a perceiver decoder directly to the VLM underperforms the two-stage design, confirming that the gains come from structured reasoning rather than raw VLM capacity.

## Results

On the OOD European wildfire event discrimination task, FireScope (CoT Oracle + U-Net) achieves the best Brier score of 0.191, ROC AUC of 0.750, and ECE of 0.068, outperforming all image-only and climate-conditioned baselines. At the pixel level, CoT Oracle + SegFormer attains the highest ROC AUC (0.658) and IoU@0.5 (0.184). In-distribution, FireScope remains competitive with climate-conditioned models (e.g., AlphaEarth + Climate: MSE 0.025, MAE 0.110), confirming that reasoning conditioning does not sacrifice ID accuracy. At the Oracle level, CoT Qwen achieves QWK 0.766 in-distribution and ROC AUC 0.748 out-of-distribution, substantially exceeding GPT-5 (QWK 0.316, ROC AUC 0.636) and FWI (ROC AUC 0.551).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FireScope's CoT Oracle substantially outperforms both external baselines on OOD wildfire event discrimination: it achieves ROC AUC 0.748 and Brier 0.196, compared to GPT-5's 0.636 and 0.281, and FWI's 0.551 and 0.321. The gap is most pronounced in calibration (ECE 0.077 vs. 0.229 for GPT-5 and 0.255 for FWI), indicating that chain-of-thought reasoning produces better-calibrated risk estimates. In-distribution, GPT-5 and FWI are not directly comparable on raster metrics, but the Climate MLP (a simple tabular baseline) matches CoT Qwen on QWK (0.766 vs. 0.766) while failing to transfer OOD (ROC AUC 0.524 vs. 0.748), highlighting that FireScope's gains stem from reasoning-driven generalization rather than memorization. At the full-framework level, CoT Oracle conditioning improves every encoder over its image-only counterpart by 3–10 points in ROC AUC on OOD events, and the gains persist even when the U-Net is trained on 40× more data without reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three threads: chain-of-thought reasoning in VLMs (Wei et al., Kojima et al.), FiLM-based conditioning for visual generation (Perez et al.), and geospatial raster prediction (SegFormer, U-Net, AlphaEarth). It departs from prior wildfire risk modeling (FWI, hybrid climate-ML approaches) by replacing hand-crafted meteorological indices with learned multimodal reasoning, and from prior raster generation by introducing a language-based reasoning signal as the conditioning mechanism rather than direct pixel-to-pixel mapping. The GRPO fine-tuning of the Oracle draws on recent RL-for-reasoning work (DeepSeek-R1, Shao et al.), but applies it to a novel ordinal risk-estimation task rather than mathematical or QA reasoning. No single prior method is directly extended; the contribution is the architectural coupling of a CoT-trained VLM scalar to a spatial decoder via FiLM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that explicit language-based reasoning can serve as a generalization mechanism for structured spatial prediction, a finding that could transfer to other geospatial tasks (flood risk, land-use change, crop yield). The FireScope-Bench benchmark (5.7M km², cross-continental design) fills a genuine gap in standardized evaluation for multimodal fire risk models. A key limitation is the scalar bottleneck: the Oracle conveys only one number to the decoder, constraining spatial granularity of the reasoning signal. Open questions include whether token-level or region-aware reasoning conditioning can unlock finer-grained improvements, whether the OOD gains persist at the full 40K-sample scale, and whether the interpretability metrics (fidelity, consistency) generalize beyond wildfire to other physically grounded raster tasks.
