## Problem

While BioCLIP aligns biological images with taxonomic text and CLAP aligns animal sounds with text, no existing framework unifies all three modalities (image, text, audio) into a shared representation space for species-level understanding. Current multimodal biological datasets cover only pairwise modalities, leaving visual-textual-acoustic (VITA) alignment unexplored and preventing comprehensive cross-modal species retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262547/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes audio clips (averaging 24.6 s at 44.1 kHz, randomly cropped to 10-s segments), RGB images (119×119 to 2048×2048 px), and text prompts generated from species labels via randomized taxonomic description templates. Each modality is encoded into a 768-dimensional L2-normalized embedding; retrieval is evaluated as 100-way ranking (one positive among 99 distractors) across six directional pairs at Species, Genus, and Family taxonomic levels, and the same embeddings support linear-probe ecological trait classification over 34 trait categories.

## Method

BioVITA Model uses three transformer-based encoders—HTS-AT (four SwinT groups) for mel-spectrogram audio, ViT-L/14 for images, and a 12-layer Transformer for text—all projecting to a shared 768-d space. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 (30 epochs, AdamW, lr 1e-4, batch 64) aligns only audio and text via a symmetric contrastive loss (ATC), grounding audio features in the pre-trained BioCLIP 2 text space before visual cues are introduced. Stage 2 (10 epochs, halved lr) activates audio-image (AIC) and image-text (ITC) contrastive losses with a linearly scheduled weight λ (0→0.1 over 2 epochs) to prevent destabilizing the ATC alignment, while making both audio and text encoders trainable. This staged strategy avoids the difficulty of simultaneously learning fine-grained visual and acoustic distinctions and leverages BioCLIP 2's hierarchical taxonomic structure to enrich all three modalities.

## Results

On the species-level seen benchmark (BioVITA Bench), BioVITA (Stage 2) achieves an average Top-1 accuracy of 71.7% and Top-5 of 89.2% across six cross-modal retrieval directions (A2T, T2A, A2I, I2A, I2T, T2I). On the unseen subset of 325 held-out species, it retains 51.9% Top-1 and 73.0% Top-5, demonstrating strong generalization. At the genus level, BioVITA reaches 66.0% Top-1 average, and at the family level 36.4% Top-1, both substantially ahead of the next-best baseline (TaxaBind at 21.3% and 11.2% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BioVITA (Stage 2) outperforms all five baselines across every retrieval direction it supports. Against the strongest tri-modal baseline, ImageBind, the gap is most dramatic in audio-involved directions: BioVITA scores 63.7% Top-1 on A2T versus ImageBind's 2.0%, and 50.3% on A2I versus 1.8%, indicating ImageBind's audio encoder is essentially non-functional for fine-grained species retrieval. Compared to the ecological tri-modal model TaxaBind, BioVITA leads by 54.1 points on A2T (63.7% vs 9.6%) and by 34.1 points on I2T (86.3% vs 56.9%). Against the bi-modal baselines, BioVITA surpasses BioCLIP 2 on image-text retrieval (86.3% I2T and 91.2% T2I vs 65.1% and 84.8%), showing that VITA alignment enriches even the pre-trained image-text representations. CLAP, the audio-text baseline, scores near random (0.7% A2T Top-1), highlighting that generic audio-language pretraining is insufficient for species-level ecological retrieval. On the unseen-species subset, BioVITA's 51.9% average Top-1 still exceeds ImageBind (16.4%) and TaxaBind by a wide margin, confirming that the learned unified space generalizes to novel taxa.

## Relation to Prior Work

BioVITA extends the biological image-text alignment paradigm established by BioCLIP and BioCLIP 2 by incorporating the audio modality, following the contrastive pretraining recipe of CLAP for audio-text grounding. The closest prior work, TaxaBind, also pursues multi-modal ecological alignment but only bridges the image modality and trains on a much smaller audio set (75k samples). The key conceptual departure is the two-stage training strategy: first aligning audio to the pre-trained BioCLIP 2 text space (Stage 1), then jointly optimizing all three modalities (Stage 2), which avoids the instability of training all encoders from scratch. The dataset construction unifies previously siloed pairwise resources (iNaturalist images, Xeno-Canto/ASA audio, ToL-200M) under a single taxonomic and ecological-trait annotation scheme.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BioVITA provides the first large-scale tri-modal biological dataset (1.3M audio clips, 2.3M images, 14k species, 34 ecological traits) and a 36-scenario retrieval benchmark that enables systematic analysis across modality, taxonomic level, and generalization axes—resources that did not previously exist in a unified form. The 34 ecological trait annotations open a path toward trait-aware species understanding beyond mere identification. A notable limitation is the large performance gap between seen (91.2% Top-1) and unseen (51.9% Top-1) species, suggesting the model still struggles with rare or acoustically atypical taxa. Open questions include whether the two-stage curriculum is optimal versus end-to-end training, how performance scales with further data, and whether the framework extends to non-vocal species or finer-grained behavioral tasks.
