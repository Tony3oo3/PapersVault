## Problem

Prevailing multimodal contrastive frameworks (e.g., CLIP, ImageBind) align modalities only in pairwise fashion, missing higher-order synergistic dependencies that emerge when three or more modalities interact. Existing higher-order methods either require all modalities to be present at inference (incompatible with standard 1→1 retrieval) or neglect pairwise alignment, degrading single-modality performance. The paper asks whether a single contrastive objective can simultaneously capture both pairwise and higher-order multimodal structure.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes three modalities per sample (e.g., an RGB image, a 10-second audio clip, and a text caption), each processed by a modality-specific encoder followed by a projector into a shared latent space Z. A lightweight fusion MLP then combines pairs of encoded features to produce fused representations z_ij that live in the same space Z as the individual modality embeddings. This unified space supports both 1→1 retrieval (query one modality, retrieve another) and 2→1 retrieval (query a fused pair, retrieve the third modality) without architectural changes, and the model gracefully degrades when only a subset of modalities is available at test time.

## Method

ConFu extends the standard CLIP-style InfoNCE objective with an additional fused-modality contrastive term. For each of the three modality pairs, a shallow MLP fusion network g_ψij maps the pair of encoded features into the shared embedding space, and the resulting fused vector z_ij is aligned with the remaining modality's embedding via a separate InfoNCE loss. The total training loss is a weighted sum L = (1−λ)L_pair + λL_fused, where L_pair aggregates InfoNCE over all ordered modality pairs and L_fused aggregates InfoNCE over all (modality, fused-pair) triplets. Theoretically, this objective maximizes a contrastive lower bound on the total correlation TC(X₁,X₂,X₃), decomposed via the chain rule of mutual information into pairwise MI terms I(X_i;X_j) and higher-order terms I(X_k;X_i,X_j). The formulation is architecture-agnostic, adds only lightweight MLPs on top of frozen or trainable encoders, and preserves full 1→1 retrieval capability while enabling 2→1 compositional retrieval within the same embedding space.

## Results

On the SSW60 bird classification benchmark, ConFu achieves 71.4% zero-shot A+V accuracy, outperforming TRIANGLE (64.1%), Symile (60.2%), and GRAM (56.9%). On VB100, where audio is largely uninformative (~4% unimodal accuracy), ConFu's fused model (18.1%) stays close to the best visual-only baseline (19.3%), while GRAM, Symile, and TRIANGLE drop 5–10% below. On AV-MNIST, ConFu reaches 71.2% A+V accuracy versus Symile's 70.9% and GRAM's 64.4%. Under Gaussian noise (SNR ~20 dB) applied to the vision modality at test time, ConFu retains 45.4% accuracy, substantially ahead of Symile (40.9%), TRIANGLE (26.5%), and GRAM (25.9%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConFu outperforms all three higher-order baselines on every A+V fusion metric across both bird datasets: on SSW60 it leads Symile by 11.2 points (71.4 vs 60.2), TRIANGLE by 7.3 points (71.4 vs 64.1), and GRAM by 14.5 points (71.4 vs 56.9); on VB100 the margins are 4.7, 6.0, and 10.1 points respectively. A critical structural advantage is that Symile and TRIANGLE cannot perform 1→1 retrieval at all (they require all modalities at inference), whereas ConFu supports both 1→1 and 2→1 retrieval within a single model; GRAM supports both but degrades severely on 1→1 (e.g., 6.1% mean on MOSI vs ConFu's 19.5%). On the synthetic AV-MNIST benchmark, ConFu edges out Symile by 0.3 points (71.2 vs 70.9) while beating GRAM (64.4) and TRIANGLE (64.9) by roughly 6–7 points. Under noise-induced distribution shift, ConFu's 45.4% accuracy exceeds Symile by 4.5 points and GRAM/TRIANGLE by ~19 points, confirming that its explicit pairwise-plus-fused decomposition provides greater stability when one modality is corrupted.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CLIP-style pairwise contrastive alignment to a unified framework that simultaneously optimizes pairwise (1→1) and higher-order (2→1) multimodal dependencies. It departs from pivot-based methods (ImageBind, LanguageBind) that mediate all interactions through a single anchor modality, and from geometric higher-order approaches (GRAM, TRIANGLE) that use a single joint similarity measure requiring all modalities at inference. The key conceptual departure from Symile, which also targets total correlation, is the explicit factorization of the TC lower bound into interpretable pairwise and fused-modality InfoNCE terms, enabling independent control and reliable performance with any subset of modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified 1→1 and 2→1 retrieval capability within a single contrastive objective addresses a practical gap: prior higher-order methods (Symile, TRIANGLE) cannot perform standard single-modality retrieval, while pairwise methods miss synergistic structure. The Bird-MML dataset fills a notable void in publicly available audio-visual-text triplets for systematic evaluation. Open questions include handling partially missing modalities during training, scaling beyond three modalities without combinatorial loss-term explosion, and whether the lightweight MLP fusion can be replaced by more expressive cross-attention without losing the architectural simplicity that makes the framework broadly applicable.
