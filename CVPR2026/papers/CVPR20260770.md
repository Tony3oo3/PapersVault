## Problem

Prompt tuning of pre-trained vision-language models (e.g., CLIP) struggles to maintain tail-class discriminability on class-imbalanced data, as head classes dominate optimization and rare classes remain poorly separated. Existing neural-collapse-based prompt tuning methods enforce a global Equiangular Tight Frame (ETF) on all class prototypes, which flattens the hierarchical semantic geometry learned during pre-training, compresses the effective rank of the similarity matrix, and introduces high seed-to-seed training instability. The paper addresses the gap of achieving strong local tail-class separability while preserving the global semantic structure needed for cross-dataset and OOD generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (processed by a frozen ViT-B/16 image encoder with learnable continuous prompts) and class label text (processed by a frozen text encoder with learnable prompts) as input, producing a predicted class label via cosine similarity between image and text features. Only the prompt tokens are updated during training; all backbone parameters remain frozen. Class imbalance is simulated by downsampling each class to follow an exponential decay distribution with imbalance ratio τ ∈ {1, 0.25, 0.06}, where the maximum class size is fixed at 16 samples.

## Method

CPT introduces a cluster-invariant space by running K-means once on the frozen pre-trained textual features to partition classes into M semantic clusters, then freezing this partition throughout training to preserve the VLM's hierarchical semantic structure. Within each cluster, three neural-collapse-inspired losses are applied: (1) a Textual ETF Separation Loss that enforces uniform pairwise angles among intra-cluster textual prototypes to improve local inter-class separation, (2) a Class-wise Convergence Loss that pulls visual features toward their corresponding textual prototype to reduce intra-class angular spread, and (3) a Rotation Stabilization Loss that anchors each learnable textual prototype to its frozen pre-trained counterpart via L1 distance, eliminating the unconstrained global-rotation degree of freedom that causes seed-to-seed variance. The total objective combines the standard CLIP contrastive loss with weighted sums of these three losses, and the key design choice is restricting ETF constraints to local cluster neighborhoods rather than the full label space, which gains tail-class discriminability without globally flattening the semantic hierarchy.

## Results

On the base-to-new harmonic mean across 11 datasets, CPT achieves 80.28% under balanced data (τ=1), 73.58% at τ=0.25, and 72.47% at τ=0.06, consistently outperforming all baselines. In cross-dataset transfer (trained on ImageNet-1K, evaluated on 10 OOD datasets), CPT reaches 67.78% average at τ=1 and 65.97% at τ=0.06. For domain generalization on four ImageNet variants, CPT attains 60.50% average at τ=1 and 59.12% at τ=0.06, the best among all compared methods across every imbalance level.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPT outperforms all three primary baselines across every evaluation setting and imbalance ratio. Against NPT (KDD'24), the closest competitor in the neural-collapse prompt-tuning family, CPT gains +0.60% at τ=0.25 (73.58 vs 72.98) and +0.71% at τ=0.06 (72.47 vs 71.76) on base-to-new, with similar margins on cross-dataset (+0.66% at τ=0.06) and domain generalization (+0.84% at τ=0.06). Against DeKg (ICLR'25), CPT leads by +0.16% at τ=1 (80.28 vs 80.12) but pulls ahead more substantially under imbalance: +0.59% at τ=0.25 and +0.97% at τ=0.06, indicating the cluster-aware constraint becomes more valuable as the tail shrinks. Against DPC (CVPR'25), CPT achieves +0.66% at τ=1, +0.66% at τ=0.25, and +0.72% at τ=0.06 on base-to-new. Notably, CPT also shows lower seed-to-seed variance (std ≈ 0.35–0.52) compared to global-ETF baselines, attributed to the rotation stabilization loss that anchors prototypes to their frozen pre-trained positions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the neural-collapse-based prompt tuning line initiated by NPT (KDD'24), which enforces a global Equiangular Tight Frame (ETF) constraint on all class textual prototypes to improve tail-class separation. CPT departs from this by restricting the ETF constraint to within semantic clusters mined via K-means on frozen pre-trained textual features, thereby preserving the hierarchical inter-cluster geometry that global ETF flattens. It also introduces a rotation stabilization loss (L1 anchoring to frozen prototypes) that addresses the uncontrolled global-rotation degree of freedom left by ETF constraints alone, a practical gap not addressed in prior NC-prompting work. The method sits alongside logit-adjustment and reweighting approaches for long-tailed recognition but operates at the representation-geometry level rather than the classifier-score level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cluster-aware formulation offers a principled middle ground between unconstrained prompt tuning (which underfits tail classes) and global ETF (which compresses semantic hierarchy and hurts OOD transfer), and the consistent gains across 11 datasets under three imbalance ratios make it a practical option for long-tailed VLM adaptation. However, the improvements over strong baselines like NPT and DPC are modest (roughly 0.5–1% harmonic mean), and the method is validated only with ViT-B/16 and a fixed 16 samples per class, leaving open how it scales to larger backbones, more extreme imbalance, or open-vocabulary settings. An open question is whether the static cluster partition remains optimal when the downstream label space differs substantially from the pre-training distribution, and whether the three-loss combination can be simplified without sacrificing the stability gains from the rotation term.
