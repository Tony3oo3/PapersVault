## Problem

Existing 3D scene reconstruction captures only static geometry, failing to recover how objects move and articulate under human interaction. Prior articulated-reconstruction methods rely on controlled multi-view captures, manual multi-state alignment, or CAD priors, and none provides an end-to-end pipeline for scene-scale, physically grounded digital twins directly from in-the-wild egocentric interaction videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260460/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single egocentric RGB-D interaction video (e.g., 1920×1080 at 15 FPS from a head-mounted Azure Kinect DK) with known camera intrinsics and produces a simulation-compatible 3D scene: a static background mesh, per-part canonical meshes, per-frame SE(3) part poses, articulation parameters (joint axis, pivot point, and per-timestep joint states), dense part segmentation masks, and exportable URDF/USD files with inferred physical properties. The pipeline is entirely training-free and optimization-based, requiring no learned model weights at inference time.

## Method

FunREC is a training-free, optimization-based pipeline that decomposes the input video into static and dynamic fragments via a video-language model, then processes each dynamic fragment through a multi-stage pipeline: camera poses are estimated from RoMA dense matches filtered by hand/object masks and refined with SuperANSAC pose-graph optimization; sparse 3D trajectories are obtained with TAPIP3D and clustered into articulated components by fitting per-track 3D lines (prismatic) or circles (revolute) and grouping via HDBSCAN on joint-parameter similarity; dense part masks are produced by combining SAM over-segmentation with a motion-ratio test on projected tracks, then propagated temporally with SAM2. Part poses and articulation parameters are jointly refined through a pose-graph objective optimized with Ceres Solver under manifold constraints, and geometry is reconstructed via separate TSDF fusions for the static scene (world frame) and each articulated part (canonical frame). Finally, all fragment submaps are globally aligned using PREDATOR registration and fused into a unified scene.

## Results

On OmniFun4D, FunREC estimates articulation axis direction within 5.3° (revolute) / 5.4° (prismatic) and axis position within 0.03 m, with a joint state error of 5.0°/0.02 m and only 1.7% failure rate. On RealFun4D, it achieves 5.6°/5.5° axis error, 0.05 m position error, and 8.4°/0.03 m state error with 0% failures. For moving-part segmentation, FunREC reaches 77.9 / 76.4 / 74.8 mIoU on OmniFun4D / HOI4D / RealFun4D, a ~50-point improvement over the best baseline (MonST3R at 23.6–26.8 mIoU). For 6D part pose, it attains 79.0% ADD-S and 3.2 cm Chamfer Distance on OmniFun4D, more than 3× better than the next-best method (BundleSDF: 22.8% ADD-S, 17.1 cm CD).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FunREC outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest baseline, BundleSDF (given ground-truth masks and camera poses), FunREC reduces axis direction error from 38.2° to 5.3° (~7×) and axis position error from 0.95 m to 0.03 m (~32×) on OmniFun4D, while cutting the failure rate from 55% to 1.7%. The 4D reconstruction pipelines (MonST3R, SpatialTrackerV2) produce axis errors of 47–70° and position errors of 0.5–1.2 m, roughly 10× worse than FunREC, and SpatialTrackerV2 additionally fails on 20–30% of sequences. ArtGS, which requires two pre-captured static states, fails on 67–95% of sequences and cannot track motion in video, making it unsuitable for the egocentric setting. On segmentation, FunREC's 74.8–77.9 mIoU exceeds MonST3R (23.6–26.8) by ~50 points and SpatialTrackerV2 (5.8–13.4) by ~60 points, confirming that the joint optimization of articulation parameters with part poses yields far more accurate part delineation than feedforward 4D reconstruction or tracking-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

FunREC carves new ground by unifying scene-scale 3D reconstruction with articulated motion recovery from a single egocentric video, a combination absent from prior work. It departs from multi-state capture pipelines (MultiScan), static-scan annotation efforts (SceneFun3D, Articulate3D), and CAD-proxy 'digital cousin' approaches by grounding articulation directly in observed human interaction. At the object level, it extends the line of articulated reconstruction from controlled multi-view or single-view setups (DITTO, PARIS, ArtGS) to in-the-wild egocentric video, while at the scene level it goes beyond 4D reconstruction pipelines (MonST3R, SpatialTrackerV2) that recover geometry but not kinematic structure. The method is training-free, repurposing foundation-model priors (VLM for interaction detection, SAM2 for segmentation, TAPIP3D for tracking) within a geometric optimization framework (TSDF fusion, pose-graph and manifold optimization).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end system producing simulation-ready, physically articulated 3D digital twins from casual egocentric video, with two new datasets (RealFun4D, OmniFun4D) that fill a critical benchmarking gap for functional scene understanding. The demonstrated applications—URDF/USD export, robot-scene interaction from human demonstration, and hand-guided affordance mapping—bridge a practical gap between perception and embodied AI. Open questions include generalization beyond prismatic/revolute joints, handling multiple simultaneous articulations within a single fragment, and whether the training-free optimization can be replaced or augmented by learned components for longer, more complex sequences. The reliance on foundation models (VLM, SAM2, TAPIP3D) also raises questions about robustness when those components degrade on out-of-distribution inputs.
