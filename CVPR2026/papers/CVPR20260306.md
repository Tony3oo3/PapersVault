## Problem

Existing image-to-3D reconstruction models are trained on isolated synthetic objects and fail on natural images where occlusion, clutter, and scene context dominate. The core bottleneck is the absence of large-scale paired data: natural images with corresponding 3D shape, texture, and pose ground truth, since generalist annotators cannot directly create meshes for arbitrary objects in photos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260306/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB image with a binary object mask, encoded via DINOv2 at both cropped-object and full-image resolution to capture local detail and global recognition cues; optionally a coarse point map (from LiDAR or monocular depth) conditions layout estimation. Output is a full 3D shape (not just the visible 2.5D surface), PBR texture, and camera-frame layout (6D rotation, 3D translation, 3D scale), decoded to either a mesh or 3D Gaussian splats through shared-encoder VAE decoders.

## Method

SAM 3D uses a two-stage latent flow-matching architecture: a 1.2B-parameter Mixture-of-Transformers Geometry model jointly predicts a 64³ coarse voxel shape and object layout, followed by a 600M-parameter sparse latent flow transformer that refines geometric detail and synthesizes texture. Training follows an LLM-style multi-stage recipe—synthetic pretraining on 2.7M rendered objects (2.5T tokens), semi-synthetic mid-training on 61M render-paste samples with occlusion and layout supervision (2.7T tokens), then real-world post-training via supervised fine-tuning, DPO preference alignment, and shortcut distillation (25→4 NFE). The critical enabler is a model-in-the-loop data engine where annotators select the best of 8 ensemble candidates and pose them in a 2.5D scene, yielding ~1M annotated images with 3.14M meshes at unprecedented scale, with iterative re-collection as the model improves.

## Results

On the real-world SA-3DAO benchmark, SAM 3D achieves F1@0.01 of 0.2344 and vIoU of 0.2311, substantially outperforming the best prior method Hi3DGen (0.1629 / 0.1531) by ~44% and ~51% respectively. On the isolated-object ISO3D set, SAM 3D matches or slightly exceeds prior SOTA in perceptual similarity (Uni3D 0.3707 vs. TripoSG 0.3687). For 3D layout on SA-3DAO, SAM 3D's joint generation achieves ADD-S@0.1 of 0.7232 versus 0.5396 for the best pipeline (HY3D-2.0+FoundationPose), and on Aria Digital Twin reaches 0.7673 vs. 0.5992. In human preference studies, SAM 3D achieves a 5:1 win rate on object reconstruction and 6:1 on scene reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAM 3D decisively outperforms all shape-generation baselines on the real-world SA-3DAO benchmark: it achieves F1@0.01 of 0.2344 vs. 0.1629 (Hi3DGen), 0.1574 (HY3D-2.0), 0.1533 (TripoSG), 0.1513 (Direct3D-S2), 0.1475 (Trellis), and 0.1399 (HY3D-2.1), with Chamfer distance roughly halved (0.0400 vs. 0.0844–0.1126). On the isolated-object ISO3D set, SAM 3D is competitive but not clearly dominant: TripoSG edges it on ULIP (0.1529 vs. 0.1488) due to higher mesh resolution, while SAM 3D leads on Uni3D (0.3707 vs. 0.3687). For layout, SAM 3D's joint generation surpasses all pipeline combinations (Trellis+Megapose, HY3D-2.0+Megapose, HY3D-2.0+FoundationPose, HY3D-2.1+FoundationPose) on both SA-3DAO and Aria Digital Twin, with ADD-S@0.1 of 0.7232 and 0.7673 respectively versus at most 0.5992 for the best pipeline. Against the joint scene model MIDI, SAM 3D achieves 3D IoU of 0.4970 vs. 0.0336 on Aria Digital Twin, a dramatic margin. In head-to-head human preference, SAM 3D is preferred over all competitors at 5:1 (objects) and 6:1 (scenes), confirming the quantitative gaps are perceptually meaningful.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAM 3D extends the two-stage latent flow matching architecture of Trellis (Xiang et al., CVPR 2025) from isolated-object reconstruction to full multi-object scene generation with layout (rotation, translation, scale), using a Mixture-of-Transformers design to jointly model geometry and pose. The multi-stage training recipe (synthetic pretraining → semi-synthetic mid-training → real-world post-training) directly adapts the LLM pretraining/posttraining playbook, while the render-paste data construction draws on Dosovitskiy et al.'s compositing ideas. The key conceptual departure is the model-in-the-loop data engine: rather than requiring humans to author 3D meshes, annotators select and align from model-generated candidates, with professional artists handling only the hardest cases, thereby scaling 3D supervision to ~1M images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5:1 and 6:1 human-preference margins over prior SOTA on real-world objects and scenes, combined with the release of code, weights, and the SA-3DAO benchmark, make this a practical reference point for in-the-wild 3D reconstruction. The MITL data pipeline offers a reusable recipe for overcoming the 3D data bottleneck that has constrained the field, and the near-linear Elo scaling with data-engine iterations suggests the approach can continue to improve. Open questions include whether the data flywheel plateaus, how well the model generalizes to object categories underrepresented in the annotation taxonomy, and whether the reliance on point-map conditioning for layout limits deployment in RGB-only settings.
