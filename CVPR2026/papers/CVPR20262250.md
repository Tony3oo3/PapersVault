## Problem

Existing surgical image restoration datasets are either synthetically generated (limiting clinical generalization) or address only a single degradation type such as desmoking, leaving no comprehensive real-world benchmark for multi-type endoscopic degradation. It also remains unclear how well general-purpose image restoration methods transfer to the unique structural and semantic characteristics of surgical scene degradation compared to natural scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262250/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes degraded endoscopic images at 1280×720 resolution as input and produces restored images as output, covering three degradation types: desmoking (2,127 images), defogging (849), and desplashing (137). A critical practical detail is that paired reference labels are inherently unaligned—derived from the last clean frame preceding the noisy frame in surgical video—so all models must use PWC-Net optical flow warping to create spatially aligned training pairs. Degradation severity is annotated at four levels for smoke/fog and four substance subtypes (blood, fat, bile, tissue fluid) for splashing, enabling fine-grained evaluation.

## Method

The core contribution is the SurgClean dataset (3,113 images from 414 patients across two medical sites, ~43,640 minutes of laparoscopic and thoracoscopic video) and a standardized evaluation protocol. To handle unaligned references, the protocol estimates optical flow between the predicted restoration and the unaligned reference using pre-trained PWC-Net, warps the reference into alignment, and applies a masked L1 reconstruction loss to prevent trivial solutions in regions with imperfect flow. The benchmark evaluates 22 methods—12 generic (CNN-based ConvIR/FocalNet, Transformer-based Restormer/X-Restormer/AST/RAMiT/Histoformer, Mamba-based MambaIR/MambaIRv2, Fourier-based Fourmer/SFHformer) and 10 task-specific (desmoking: MS-CNN, Desmoke-LAP, IIT-EDC, DCP, SelfSVD; defogging: C²PNet, RIDCP, CORUN, DEA, CoA)—all trained on 128×128 patches for 200k iterations. Beyond pixel-level metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, NIQE, PI), the paper probes domain-specific differences via depth estimation and SAM/MedSAM-based scene parsing, revealing that surgical fog exhibits localized abrupt depth transitions unlike gradual natural haze, and that high restoration scores do not reliably translate to better downstream scene understanding.

## Results

On the SurgClean benchmark, no method achieves clinically acceptable restoration quality. For desmoking, ConvIR attains the best PSNR (19.432) and LPIPS (0.336), while FocalNet leads defogging with PSNR 19.069 and SSIM 0.628. For desplashing, X-Restormer achieves the highest PSNR (22.317) and SSIM (0.735). Notably, task-specific methods (e.g., MS-CNN at PSNR 19.02 for desmoking) do not surpass the best generic restorers, and all 22 evaluated methods show substantial gaps relative to the clarity required for safe surgical decision-making.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark evaluates 12 generic and 10 task-specific restoration methods, revealing that no single architecture dominates all three surgical degradation tasks. ConvIR (TPAMI'24) leads desmoking with PSNR 19.432 and LPIPS 0.336, narrowly beating MambaIRv2 (19.391) and Fourmer (19.367); FocalNet (ICCV'23) edges out Restormer (CVPR'22) for defogging (PSNR 19.069 vs. 19.036). For desplashing, X-Restormer (ECCV'24) achieves the best PSNR (22.317) and SSIM (0.735), while AST (CVPR'24) attains the lowest LPIPS (0.288). Critically, task-specific desmoking methods such as MS-CNN (PSNR 19.02) and Desmoke-LAP underperform the best generic restorers, and task-specific defogging methods (C2PNet, RIDCP, CORUN, DEA, CoA) also trail generic models, indicating that surgical degradation complexity exceeds what domain-specific architectures currently handle. Lightweight models like RAMiT (0.30M params) and MambaIRv2 (0.77M) achieve competitive scores, suggesting potential for edge deployment, yet all methods remain far below the clarity threshold needed for reliable intraoperative anatomical identification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying three previously separate surgical degradation tasks (desmoking, defogging, desplashing) into a single real-world benchmark, whereas prior datasets (Cyclic-DesmokeGAN, Desmoke-LAP, DesmokeData, LSVD) each addressed only one degradation type, often synthetically. It repurposes the PS-frame pairing scheme from SelfSVD and PWC-Net optical flow alignment to handle the inherent misalignment in real surgical video pairs, adapting a technique from RAW-to-sRGB mapping to the surgical domain. The 'beyond pixel-level' analysis draws on depth estimation (Depth Anything V2) and foundation-model segmentation (SAM, MedSAM) to probe structural and semantic differences between surgical and natural degradations, a perspective absent from prior surgical restoration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that both generic and task-specific restoration methods fall well short of clinical requirements, and notably that task-specific desmoking/defogging models do not outperform generic restorers on surgical data—a finding that challenges the assumption that domain-specialized architectures transfer advantageously. The disconnect between pixel-level restoration quality and downstream task performance (depth estimation, semantic segmentation) suggests that clinically useful restoration must be semantic-aware rather than purely photometric. Open questions include handling simultaneous multi-type degradation, developing lightweight models for real-time intraoperative deployment, and whether synthetic degradation simulation can close the data gap.
