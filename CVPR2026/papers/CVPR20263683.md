## Problem

Existing head avatar reconstruction methods that rely on per-view photometric supervision suffer from early over-sharpening (pseudo high-frequency artifacts) and cross-view drifting, because each camera independently overfits localized appearance without explicit geometric correspondence. Prior frequency-regularization approaches (e.g., BARF, FreGS) impose global spectral constraints that treat all spatial regions uniformly, ignoring the position-sensitive spectral characteristics of facial surfaces versus hair and boundaries, and failing to enforce cross-view consistency.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view head video frames (downsampled to 802×550) from 16 calibrated cameras, along with per-frame facial parsing masks and depth maps from Metashape, to fit a hybrid parametric mesh (FLAME-based with learnable UV displacement) plus an anisotropic 3D Gaussian hair field. The output is a fully animatable head avatar model that renders photorealistic novel-view images and supports self-reenactment and cross-identity reenactment by driving the mesh with arbitrary pose and expression parameters.

## Method

Fresco introduces a dual-domain optimization framework that operates without modifying the underlying representation. In the image domain, a Laplacian-pyramid frequency curriculum progressively schedules supervision from low to high spectral bands using differentiable Gaussian low-pass and Difference-of-Gaussian band-pass filters, with a cosine-annealing schedule that activates each frequency band at a threshold iteration; an edge-aware mask and gradient-difference loss prevent over-sharpening during high-frequency refinement. In the UV domain, a differentiable UV-baking operator projects multi-view renderings onto a shared mesh atlas, enforcing per-texel cross-view alignment within a mutual-visibility region weighted by surface-normal confidence, while a seam regularization term (paired-texel L1 plus total-variation smoothing) enforces color continuity across chart boundaries. The three components are activated in a staged three-phase schedule—low-frequency stabilization, UV consistency, then high-frequency refinement plus seam regularization—forming a self-balanced coarse-to-fine trajectory that suppresses pseudo-detail accumulation and cross-view inconsistency simultaneously.

## Results

On the NeRSemble dataset (8 subjects), Fresco achieves the best PSNR and LPIPS across both evaluation tasks. For novel-view synthesis, Fresco reaches 31.80 PSNR, 0.938 SSIM, and 0.058 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline GaussianAvatars (31.50 / 0.935 / 0.060). For self-reenactment, Fresco attains 30.30 PSNR and 0.064 LPIPS, surpassing GaussianAvatars (29.75 / 0.066) by 0.55 dB in PSNR, though SSIM is marginally lower (0.929 vs. 0.930). The gap over the next-best method is most pronounced in self-reenactment, where Fresco leads by over 0.5 dB in PSNR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fresco is compared against four state-of-the-art head avatar methods on NeRSemble: Gaussian Head Avatar [34], EMavatar [40], GaussianAvatars [23], and MeGA [27]. Fresco wins on PSNR and LPIPS for both novel-view synthesis and self-reenactment, with the largest margin over GaussianAvatars in self-reenactment PSNR (+0.55 dB). On novel-view SSIM, Fresco edges out GaussianAvatars by 0.003 (0.938 vs. 0.935), but on self-reenactment SSIM it trails GaussianAvatars by a negligible 0.001 (0.929 vs. 0.930). The two older baselines (Gaussian Head Avatar and EMavatar) are substantially outperformed, with Fresco gaining roughly 1.6–1.9 dB in PSNR for novel-view and 7.7–7.9 dB for self-reenactment. MeGA, the most recent mesh-Gaussian hybrid, is consistently outperformed by Fresco on all six metrics, suggesting that the frequency curriculum and UV-space consistency provide meaningful gains beyond the representation design alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fresco builds directly on the hybrid mesh-plus-Gaussian representation introduced by GaussianAvatars and MeGA, adopting the same FLAME-based facial mesh with anisotropic 3D Gaussians for hair, but its contribution is entirely in the optimization dynamics rather than the representation. It departs from prior frequency-domain stabilization methods (BARF, FreNeRF, FreGS) that impose global Fourier or amplitude-spectrum constraints, instead using spatially localized Laplacian-pyramid and DoG filters in the image domain to respect region-specific spectral characteristics of faces versus hair. The UV-space texel-alignment loss with seam regularization is a new addition to the head-avatar optimization literature, complementing the frequency curriculum with explicit geometric correspondence in a shared parameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a carefully staged, dual-domain optimization schedule (low-frequency stabilization → UV cross-view alignment → high-frequency refinement) can meaningfully reduce pseudo high-frequency artifacts and cross-view drifting without altering the underlying representation, offering a practical recipe transferable to other mesh-Gaussian head avatar pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to eight NeRSemble subjects and a single FLAME-based representation, leaving open whether the strategy generalizes to purely implicit or purely Gaussian formulations, to larger-scale datasets, or to real-time training budgets. The reliance on a parametric mesh for UV baking also constrains applicability to subjects well-modeled by FLAME, and the staged schedule introduces additional hyperparameters (T1, T2, cosine annealing thresholds) that may require per-dataset tuning.
