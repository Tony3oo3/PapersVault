## Problem

Directly applying pre-trained video diffusion models to video super-resolution faces two critical gaps: (1) the VAE's temporal scaling operators lack sufficient spatial modeling capacity to handle complex camera motions (shaking, zooming), causing structural distortions during reconstruction, and (2) parameter-efficient LoRA fine-tuning's inherent low-rank constraint limits the model's ability to capture the complex feature interactions needed to preserve LQ structural fidelity while generating accurate details.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260308/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded low-quality video (with realistic degradations synthesized via RealBasicVSR and Real-ESRGAN pipelines, augmented with camera shake and scaling effects) and a text prompt as input, producing a temporally coherent high-quality video. Most evaluations use a ×4 upscaling factor, while RealVSR and SportsLQ are evaluated at original scale (×1). During training, video patches are cropped to 480×480 spatial resolution with 27–33 temporal frames, and the maximum clip length is limited to 9 frames to alleviate motion misalignment.

## Method

STCDiT builds on Wan 2.1 I2V-14B with LoRA (rank 128) fine-tuning and introduces two key mechanisms. First, a motion-aware VAE reconstruction method uses Shi-Tomasi corner detection and Lucas-Kanade optical flow to estimate inter-frame affine transformations, segments the video into clips with uniform motion characteristics, and encodes/decodes each clip independently through the VAE to avoid temporal compression artifacts under complex motion. Second, an anchor-frame guidance approach exploits the observation that the first-frame latent in each clip (unaffected by temporal compression) retains richer spatial structure; these anchor-frame latents are refined, concatenated with video tokens for self-attention interaction (with RoPE indices shifted to prevent overlap), and further processed through an anchor-corresponding feature modulation (ACFM) module that uses depth-wise convolutions with GELU gating to discriminatively select and fuse local spatial features from anchor frames into their corresponding video frame features. The full approach adds only 7% of the LoRA parameter count while achieving state-of-the-art structural fidelity and temporal consistency.

## Results

On REDS30, STCDiT (14B) achieves the best LPIPS (0.2866), MUSIQ (61.65), and DOVER (42.94), outperforming the next-best method (Wan 14B LoRA) by 2.25 points on DOVER. On UDM10, it leads in MUSIQ (66.46), CLIPIQA+ (0.5234), MANIQA (0.3763), and DOVER (64.10). On the real-world RealVSR benchmark, STCDiT attains the best LPIPS (0.1553) and DOVER (61.57), and on the newly introduced SportsLQ dataset it achieves the highest DOVER (48.98) and FasterVQA (0.7574) among all methods. The smaller STCDiT-tiny (1.3B) also remains competitive, e.g., achieving DOVER 40.09 on REDS30 versus 39.69 for the 14B Wan LoRA baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STCDiT outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of no-reference quality metrics (MUSIQ, CLIPIQA+, MANIQA, FasterVQA, DOVER) across every evaluated dataset. Against the closest competitor, Wan 14B with LoRA fine-tuning, STCDiT gains +3.25 DOVER on REDS30 (42.94 vs. 39.69) and +5.20 DOVER on RealVSR (61.57 vs. 56.37), confirming that anchor-frame guidance adds substantial value over plain LoRA. Compared to SeedVR (7B, full fine-tuning), STCDiT achieves higher DOVER on REDS30 (42.94 vs. 36.36) and UDM10 (64.10 vs. 60.93) while using only 7% of the LoRA-128 training parameters. Against DOVE (5B, one-step diffusion), STCDiT leads by +6.31 DOVER on REDS30 and +7.41 on RealVSR. On the challenging SportsLQ dataset with intense human motion, STCDiT achieves the best DOVER (48.98) and FasterVQA (0.7574), surpassing the next-best STCDiT-tiny by 2.56 and 0.0128 respectively. The only metric where STCDiT does not consistently lead is E*warp (warping error), where methods like UAV and STAR sometimes score lower; the authors note this metric tends to penalize results with rich fine details.

## Relation to Prior Work

STCDiT builds directly on the Wan 2.1 video diffusion model (DiT-based) and departs from SeedVR's full fine-tuning strategy by combining LoRA with a novel anchor-frame guidance mechanism that adds only ~7% of LoRA-rank-128 parameters. The motion-aware VAE reconstruction repurposes classical motion estimation (Shi-Tomasi + Lucas-Kanade) to segment video before VAE encoding, sidestepping the need to redesign temporal scaling operators. The anchor-corresponding feature modulation (ACFM) adapts the gated feature injection concept from DiT4SR (image SR) to the video setting with discriminative local spatial selection, while the anchor-frame insight—that first-frame latents escape temporal compression and retain richer structure—is a new observation specific to video VAEs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-efficient formulation makes it practical to adapt large (14B) video diffusion models for VSR without full fine-tuning, lowering the barrier for the community. The motion-aware segmentation addresses a real-world failure mode (camera shake/zoom) that prior diffusion-based VSR methods handle poorly, and the new SportsLQ benchmark fills a gap for high-motion evaluation. However, the warping-error results show the temporal consistency advantage is less pronounced than the perceptual-quality gains, and the reliance on empirical thresholds for motion segmentation raises questions about robustness to highly irregular or non-rigid motions. Open questions include whether the anchor-frame strategy generalizes to longer sequences or higher upscaling factors, and whether the 10-step inference budget is sufficient for the most degraded inputs.
