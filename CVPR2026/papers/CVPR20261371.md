## Problem

Diffusion-based text-to-image models systematically drift toward high-density (frequent) concepts when generating rare or compositional prompts, because the learned score function is under-constrained in low-density regions of the training distribution. Prior methods such as R2F mitigate this by alternating between target and frequent anchor prompts, but rely on fixed heuristic schedules that cannot adapt per diffusion timestep, leaving a gap between anchor stability and target fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261371/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For rare concept generation, the method takes a user text prompt, decomposes it into rare target concepts, and pairs each with an LLM-generated frequent anchor (e.g., 'hairy frog' → 'hairy object'), reconstructing two complete prompts before feeding them into a pre-trained diffusion model (SD3.0, 50 steps, CFG scale 7.0). For image editing, it takes a source image, its original prompt, and an edited target prompt, running through the FlowEdit inversion-free ODE pipeline (CFG scale 20.5). Both settings are entirely training-free at inference time; the only additional computation is evaluating three score functions (unconditional, target-conditioned, anchor-conditioned) per timestep to compute the adaptive coefficient.

## Method

AAPB generalizes classifier-free guidance by replacing the single conditional score with a linear blend of target and auxiliary anchor scores, governed by a per-timestep coefficient γ_t. The key insight is that Tweedie's formula makes posterior-mean alignment in image space equivalent to score-space alignment, so minimizing the squared distance between the blended score and the target score yields a closed-form optimal γ*_t computed as a projection: the inner product of (target − unconditional) and (anchor − target) scores divided by the squared norm of (anchor − target), scaled by (1−w)/w. This coefficient is re-evaluated at every denoising step, dynamically shifting anchor influence based on local score geometry rather than a fixed schedule. A supporting proposition shows that under log-concave target distributions, this stepwise projection achieves a tighter squared 2-Wasserstein bound than any fixed interpolation, and a controlled 2D Gaussian toy example empirically confirms the advantage.

## Results

On RareBench (GPT-4o text-to-image alignment), AAPB built on SD3.0 achieves an average score of 84.1, outperforming the strongest prior training-free baseline R2F (75.7) by 8.4 points and the base SD3.0 model (61.5) by 22.6 points. On the FlowEdit editing benchmark, AAPB attains the best structure-preservation scores—CLIP-I 0.905, DINO 0.814, LPIPS 0.155, DreamSim 0.155—while maintaining near-parity text alignment (CLIP-T 0.341 vs. FlowEdit's 0.344). The method is entirely training-free, requiring only a per-timestep closed-form coefficient computed at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On RareBench, AAPB (SD3) wins on every single category against all 10 baselines, with the largest margins over the base SD3.0 model (+22.6 avg) and the closest competitor R2F (+8.4 avg); gains are especially pronounced on shape (+10.0 over SD3.0) and multi-object relation (+6.8 over R2F). Among LLM-grounded methods, ELLA (51.5) and RPG (36.4) trail substantially, while SynGen (44.1) and LMD (30.9) show the weakest rare-concept handling. On FlowEdit, AAPB surpasses FlowEdit on all four structure-preservation metrics (CLIP-I +0.033, DINO +0.095, LPIPS −0.026, DreamSim −0.104) while matching its text alignment within 0.003 (CLIP-T 0.341 vs. 0.344). Against SDEdit, ODE Inversion, and iRFDS, AAPB leads on every metric, with the largest structural gains over iRFDS (DINO +0.280, LPIPS −0.221). The method also outperforms R2F under all four anchor-generation strategies tested (human, arbitrary, 'objects', LLaMA3, GPT-4o), confirming robustness to anchor quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes classifier-free guidance (CFG) by replacing its single conditional score with a per-timestep linear blend between a target prompt score and an auxiliary anchor score, then derives a closed-form optimal blending coefficient via Tweedie's posterior-mean alignment. It departs from R2F's heuristic prompt-level alternation by operating continuously in score space, enabling per-step adaptivity without manual schedule design. For image editing, it builds directly on FlowEdit's inversion-free ODE formulation, injecting the adaptive blending mechanism into the denoising trajectory to preserve structure while enforcing target semantics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AAPB offers a principled, training-free mechanism to stabilize diffusion generation in low-density regions, unifying rare-concept synthesis and structure-preserving editing under one optimization principle. The theoretical guarantee (tighter W₂ bound under log-concavity) is clean but relies on an idealized assumption unlikely to hold for real image distributions, leaving open whether the Wasserstein advantage persists in practice beyond the toy setting. The finding that even arbitrary or minimally-informative anchors yield consistent gains over R2F suggests the adaptive coefficient itself is the key ingredient, potentially reducing dependence on high-quality LLM-generated anchors. A practical limitation is the need for an LLM call to produce anchor prompts, and the method's behavior under very strong guidance scales or multi-step editing chains remains unexplored.
