## Problem

Dexterous hands maintain high-dimensional actuation spaces (22+ DoF), making holistic latent representations from VAE or diffusion models unable to capture the structured, compositional coordination among fingers. Existing condition-driven grasp generators rely solely on object geometry for guidance, producing grasps that lack semantic alignment with task intent, while limited textual diversity in prior datasets leads to ambiguous conditioning and reduced expressiveness of robotic grasping behaviors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264063/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an object point cloud (sampled as 2000×3 points), a free-form text description (encoded via a pre-trained CLIP model), and a discrete task ID (mapped through a learnable embedding) as conditioning signals. The output is a full Shadow Hand grasp configuration—22 joint angles, an SO(3) orientation, and a 3D translation—defined in the object's canonical frame, produced by decoding 6 discrete token indices (one per anatomical part: palm, thumb, index, middle, ring, and little finger) from per-part codebooks of 256 entries with 512-dimensional embeddings.

## Method

MaskDexGrasp introduces a part-aware grasp tokenizer that partitions hand surface points into six anatomical subcomponents, each encoded by a dedicated PointNet encoder and quantized through a separate VQ-VAE codebook, transforming the continuous grasp manifold into a short sequence of six discrete tokens that preserves inter-part structural consistency. A Bidirectional Masked Grasp Transformer (BMGT), a 9-layer transformer with 16 heads and 512-dim embeddings, then learns the conditional distribution over these tokens using a bidirectional masked modeling objective—randomly masking a subset of tokens and predicting them jointly from both preceding and succeeding context, conditioned on a concatenated vector of task, text, and object embeddings. During inference, an iterative masked sampling scheme with a cosine masking schedule progressively resolves tokens over T iterations, augmented by classifier-free guidance (10% unconditional dropout) to balance fidelity and diversity. The discrete, part-indexed representation natively supports finger-level editing: specific anatomical tokens can be re-masked and re-sampled under a modified condition without retraining, enabling interpretable, localized grasp modification.

## Results

On the TDG dataset, MaskDexGrasp achieves a 75.16% success rate on Subset 2 (vs. 58.90% for the strongest baseline DexGraspAnything) and 44.68% on Subset 1, while maintaining the lowest maximal penetration depth (0.340 cm on Subset 1, 0.413 cm on Subset 2) and the highest Q1 stability score (0.048 / 0.126). Inference time is 0.033 s per grasp, roughly 10× faster than DGTR (0.284 s) and over 100× faster than DexGraspAnything (4.417 s). The method also achieves the best Hmean diversity on Subset 2 (3.922) and competitive diversity on Subset 1 (3.876).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DGTR [67] and DexGYS [62], MaskDexGrasp dominates on every metric for both subsets, with success rates roughly 1.5–2.6× higher. Compared to SceneDiffuser [23] and UGG [37], the method improves success rate by 9–32 percentage points and reduces penetration depth by 0.07–0.13 cm, while matching or exceeding diversity scores. The closest competitor is DexGraspAnything [83]: on Subset 1 it edges out MaskDexGrasp in success rate (46.54 vs. 44.68) and Hmean diversity (4.207 vs. 3.876), but MaskDexGrasp achieves lower penetration (0.340 vs. 0.376 cm) and higher Q1 stability (0.048 vs. 0.042). On Subset 2, MaskDexGrasp clearly surpasses DexGraspAnything in success rate (75.16 vs. 58.90), penetration (0.413 vs. 0.477 cm), and Hmean (3.922 vs. 3.662), though DexGraspAnything retains a lower Hstd (0.253 vs. 0.406), indicating more uniform diversity across samples. Inference speed is a decisive advantage: MaskDexGrasp generates a grasp in 0.033 s, compared to 0.202–4.417 s for all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the discrete-token generative paradigm from human motion synthesis (MoMask [16], VQ-VAE [54]) to dexterous grasp generation, introducing a key architectural departure: decomposing the hand into six anatomical parts (palm + five fingers) with separate codebooks rather than encoding the entire hand into a single holistic latent space. The bidirectional masked modeling strategy and cosine masking schedule are directly inherited from MoMask, while classifier-free guidance [20] is repurposed for logit-level conditioning on object geometry and text. The paper also departs from prior text-conditioned grasp methods (DexGYS, DextoG) by addressing their limited textual diversity through a VLM-assisted annotation pipeline that produces 260K descriptions across 11 task categories, and from part-based articulated shape work (NASA, CoAP) by being the first to apply part-level VQ decomposition specifically to robotic hand grasping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The part-aware discrete representation enables a practically important capability—finger-level grasp editing without retraining—that holistic latent-space methods cannot offer, opening a path toward interactive and task-adaptive robotic manipulation. The TDG dataset (65K grasps, 260K text annotations, 2,296 objects) fills a notable gap in the field by providing semantically rich, task-aligned dexterous grasp data at scale. However, the framework is limited to static, single-step grasps with a fixed discrete codebook, and the authors acknowledge that extending to dynamic, multi-step manipulation and continuous control remains open. The ~130× inference speed advantage over diffusion-based baselines (0.033s vs 4.4s) makes the approach attractive for real-time deployment, though the success rate on Subset 1 (44.68%) trails the best baseline (DexGraspAnything, 46.54%), suggesting the discrete representation still sacrifices some generation quality.
