## Problem

Multi-view learning systems deployed in the wild suffer from Test-time Noisy Correspondence (TNC), where asynchronous sampling or transient network congestion causes cross-view misalignment at inference. Existing uncertainty-based fusion methods (e.g., Evidential Deep Learning, Fuzzy Set Theory) estimate view reliability from clean, well-aligned training data without explicit supervision, creating a train-test task gap that yields overconfident or miscalibrated weights when views are actually misaligned.

## Input / Output

Each sample consists of M views: for the ten feature-vector benchmarks these are pre-extracted feature vectors, while for the newly constructed SUN R-D-T benchmark the views are an RGB image, a depth image (both resized to 256×256 and cropped to 224×224), and a single-sentence text description generated by Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct. The method outputs a fused class label via weighted late fusion of per-view logits, along with per-view reliability weights α_i^(m) ∈ (0,1) produced by a lightweight MLP that ingests the concatenation of the view feature, an inter-view Jeffreys-divergence scalar, and an intra-view entropy scalar (dimension d+2).

## Method

BML performs in-place TNC bootstrapping: each epoch, a fraction ρ of training samples is selected and a random subset of views (at least 1, at most ⌊M/2⌋) is corrupted by shuffling inputs within the selected pool, with the corruption mask s_i recorded as explicit supervision. A lightweight per-view MLP estimator E(·;ψ_m) is trained under a reveal-supervised binary cross-entropy loss that aligns predicted reliability α_i^(m) with the known clean/corrupted indicator, jointly with the classification cross-entropy (L = L_cls + λL_w). The estimator input is refined with two prediction-derived signals: an inter-view discrepancy J_i^(m) computed as the averaged Jeffreys divergence between view m's predictive distribution and all other views, and an intra-view uncertainty Q_i^(m) derived from normalized Shannon entropy of the logits. At test time, the learned reliability weights directly modulate late fusion of per-view logits, suppressing misaligned views without requiring any explicit noise indicator.

## Results

Across 10 feature-vector benchmarks (Tables 1–2), BML achieves average accuracy of 92.45% (0% noise), 89.02% (50% TNC), and 85.39% (100% TNC), consistently outperforming the strongest baselines FUML (89.38/83.49/78.11%) and RML (88.85/79.13/69.01%). On the raw-data SUN R-D-T scene recognition benchmark (Table 4), BML reaches 68.15% (clean), 64.54% (50% TNC), and 60.97% (100% TNC), surpassing the best baseline FUML by 4.38, 4.86, and 5.42 points respectively. The gap widens substantially under TNC: at 100% noise, BML leads FUML by 7.28 points on average across feature-vector datasets and by 5.42 points on SUN R-D-T.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BML outperforms all nine baselines (TMC, UIMC, ECML, CCML, MAMC, ETF, TMCEK, FUML, RML) on every dataset and noise level tested. The closest competitors are FUML (fuzzy-set-based uncertainty) and RML (perturbation-simulated contrastive alignment): at 0% noise, BML leads FUML by 3.07 points in average accuracy (92.45 vs. 89.38) and RML by 3.60 points; at 100% TNC noise, the margins expand to 7.28 points over FUML and 16.38 points over RML. Evidential-based methods (TMC, ECML, TMCEK) and fuzzy-based CCML degrade most severely under TNC, dropping 20–30 points from clean to 100% noise, whereas BML loses only ~7 points, confirming that the reveal-supervised bootstrapping paradigm effectively closes the train–test task gap. On the raw-data SUN R-D-T benchmark, BML similarly dominates, beating the strongest baseline FUML by 4.4–5.4 points across noise levels, while deterministic methods like RML and MAMC collapse more sharply under TNC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of trustworthy multi-view learning (TMC, ECML, FUML, TMCEK) that estimates view reliability via unsupervised uncertainty modeling (Evidential Deep Learning or Fuzzy Set Theory) trained exclusively on clean, well-aligned data. It also extends the Noisy Correspondence (NC) literature, which has treated imperfect cross-view pairings as a training-time phenomenon (robust objectives, sample reweighting, correspondence rectification), by shifting the focus to test-time misalignment. BML repurposes the data-augmentation idea of bootstrapping and the standard tools of Jeffreys divergence and Shannon entropy into a reveal-supervised framework where the reliability estimator receives explicit binary supervision from known corruption masks, rather than inferring reliability indirectly from task loss alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing TNC and demonstrating that a simple in-place bootstrapping + supervised reliability estimator consistently outperforms purpose-built uncertainty methods across 11 benchmarks, the paper challenges the field's default assumption that unsupervised uncertainty modeling is the right mechanism for view weighting under distribution shift. The plug-and-play design (no external data, no architectural changes to encoders) makes it immediately deployable in multi-sensor pipelines. The authors identify a natural next step—jointly handling TNC and test-time view missingness—that remains open, and the bootstrapping strategy (shuffling within a batch) may not capture all real-world misalignment patterns such as temporal drift or partial occlusion.
