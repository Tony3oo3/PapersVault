## Problem

Existing multimodal retrieval benchmarks either focus on coarse-grained global alignment (e.g., MS-COCO, Flickr30K) or introduce fine-grained edits anchored by reference images (e.g., FashionIQ, CIRR), but none simultaneously enforce fine-grained attribute reasoning, multi-condition queries, and cross-modal evidence where different conditions must be grounded in both images and text. This gap leaves untested whether current retrievers can jointly satisfy multiple heterogeneous constraints that span complementary visual and textual metadata.

## Input / Output

The query is a text-only natural-language sentence (avg. ~36 tokens, max 57) that combines multiple compositional constraints drawn from both image-only attributes (color, texture, shape) and text-only metadata (material, fit, brand). Each candidate is a product instance consisting of an RGB product image paired with long-form structured metadata (avg. ~191 tokens, max 269) across five domains (upper/bottom clothing, jewelry, shoes, furniture). A candidate is relevant only if all query conditions are simultaneously satisfied; the system outputs a ranked list scored by Recall@K, nDCG@K, and MRR@10, while the reranking stage produces a per-pair binary relevance judgment via the normalized logit of the 'true' token.

## Method

MCMR is constructed via a cooperative multi-stage pipeline: Qwen2.5-VL-32B extracts structured image-only attributes (color, texture, structural details) from product images, while Qwen3-32B generates text-only metadata profiles and first-person multi-condition queries conditioned on both evidence layers. A complementarity constraint ensures each product carries at least one attribute inferable only from the image and one only from the text, preventing single-modality shortcuts. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B serves as an independent verifier for cross-modal coverage and numeric consistency, with a 100-sample human study confirming generated queries match human-authored ones (4.33 vs. 4.41 on a 5-point scale). The evaluation protocol tests five retrievers under three candidate-visibility regimes (fused, image-only, text-only) plus query-side modality ablation and constraint-count variation (1T+1I through 5T+5I), and further applies seven MLLM-based pointwise rerankers on the top-50 pool to isolate fine-grained condition-verification ability from global embedding similarity.

## Results

On MCMR with fused image+text candidates, the best first-stage retriever (CORAL, 3B) achieves only 26.57% Recall@1 and 53.34% Recall@10, while LLaVE (7B) leads at longer cutoffs with 72.01% Recall@50 and 78.64% Recall@100. VLM2Vec (4B) performs dramatically worse than all other retrievers, reaching just 1.83% Recall@1. MLLM-based pointwise rerankers applied to the LLaVE top-50 pool dramatically improve early-rank ordering: lychee-reranker-mm (8B) reaches 82.51% NDCG@1, compared to the best first-stage retriever's 26.57% NDCG@1, exposing a large fine-grained reasoning gap in current embedding-based retrievers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CORAL (3B) achieves the best early-rank performance among first-stage retrievers, leading on R@1 (26.57%), R@10 (53.34%), and MRR@10 (34.94%), while LLaVE (7B) surpasses it at longer cutoffs (R@50: 72.01% vs. 70.90%; R@100: 78.64% vs. 77.73%), indicating CORAL ranks the correct item higher but LLaVE retrieves it more reliably within the top-100. VLM2Vec (4B) is an extreme outlier, scoring 1.83% R@1 versus 17–27% for all other retrievers, suggesting its embedding space is poorly aligned with MCMR's compositional query structure. Under modality ablation, GME-Qwen2VL is the most visually robust (image-only R@10 = 51.10%, even exceeding its fused score of 45.74%), whereas LLaVE nearly collapses without text (R@1 drops from 24.99% to 0.90%), revealing strong textual-prior dependence. Among MLLM rerankers on the fixed LLaVE top-50 pool, lychee-reranker-mm (8B) dominates at every cutoff (NDCG@1 = 82.51, NDCG@50 = 87.26), followed by InternVL-8B (80.28 / 86.61) and Qwen3-VL-Reranker-8B (78.69 / 85.57); the smaller Qwen3-VL-4B lags at 69.92 NDCG@1, showing that parameter count alone does not determine reranking quality. The ~56-point gap between the best retriever's NDCG@1 (26.57) and the best reranker's NDCG@1 (82.51) is the paper's central empirical finding, demonstrating that current dense retrievers cannot verify multi-condition cross-modal consistency in a single embedding comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three previously separated benchmark threads: coarse-grained image-text alignment (MS-COCO, Flickr30K), fine-grained single-condition retrieval with reference images (FashionIQ, CIRR), and text-only multi-condition retrieval (MultiConIR). Rather than building on a specific method, MCMR identifies that no existing benchmark simultaneously enforces fine-grained attributes, multi-condition queries, and cross-modal evidence where different conditions must be grounded in different modalities. The dual-evidence design (each item requires at least one image-only and one text-only attribute) departs from MERIT's reference-image formulation by making conditions independently verifiable across modalities without exemplar images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~56-point NDCG@1 gap between the best first-stage retriever (26.57%) and the best MLLM reranker (82.51%) exposes a fundamental limitation: current dense retrieval models capture holistic semantic similarity but cannot verify whether all multi-condition constraints are simultaneously satisfied. The modality asymmetry findings (visual cues dominate early-rank precision; textual metadata stabilizes long-tail ordering) provide actionable diagnostic signals for model developers. The central open question is how to integrate fine-grained, condition-aware compositional reasoning into scalable retrieval architectures without incurring the prohibitive cost of pointwise MLLM reranking over large corpora.
