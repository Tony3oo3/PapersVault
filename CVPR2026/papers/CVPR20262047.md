## Problem

Pretrained 3D point-cloud encoders are evaluated almost exclusively on geometric or semantic tasks, leaving it unclear whether their learned representations capture topological structure. No prior work quantifies how much topology these encoders retain, nor provides a feed-forward mechanism to extract persistence diagrams directly from point clouds without classical TDA pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262047/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D point clouds sampled at 1024 points, encoded by a frozen pretrained backbone (Point-BERT, Point-MAE, PointGPT, or PCP-MAE) into patch features with 3D positional encodings. It outputs an unordered set of H1 persistence pairs (birth, death) from the α-filtration, each accompanied by an existence probability indicating whether the pair is a genuine topological feature or a no-pair slot. Diagrams are scaled dataset-wise to [0,1] and truncated to the top 10% most persistent pairs to discard noise.

## Method

FILTR adapts DETR's set-prediction architecture to persistence diagram generation: a frozen 3D encoder produces patch features that condition a trainable transformer decoder via cross-attention, where N learned queries (N > max diagram size) interact with encoder features. Each query feeds two MLP heads—one regressing the persistence pair (birth via sigmoid, death via birth + softplus to enforce d > b) and one producing an existence logit. Training uses a composite loss combining Hungarian-matched MSE reconstruction, binary cross-entropy on existence, and a diagonal regularizer that pushes unmatched predictions toward the diagonal, making inference-time thresholding optional. The key insight is that although probing reveals encoders do not linearly expose global topology, their features preserve local geometric structure that a non-linear decoder can exploit to recover accurate multiscale topological summaries, enabling generalization to unseen distributions (ModelNet40, ABC) without retraining the encoder.

## Results

On DONUT, FILTR with frozen pretrained encoders achieves a best W₂ of 16.02×10⁻² (Point-MAE_C), bottleneck distance of 9.838×10⁻³, and PIE of 1.214, matching or surpassing the strongest end-to-end baseline DGCNN (W₂=16.62×10⁻², dB=10.15×10⁻³, PIE=1.213). On out-of-distribution ModelNet40, the best pretrained-encoder variant (Point-BERT_C) reaches W₂=41.92×10⁻² and PIE=9.900, outperforming DGCNN (W₂=43.27×10⁻², PIE=9.773) on W₂. On ABC, DGCNN slightly edges out pretrained encoders on bottleneck distance (31.70×10⁻³ vs. 31.87×10⁻³) and PIE (3.760 vs. 3.780), while pretrained encoders remain competitive on W₂. Probing experiments (Table 1) show that pretrained encoders capture only limited global topology, with Point-BERT(CLS) achieving just 57.2% accuracy on connected components and 25.9% on genus, far below end-to-end RepSurf (83.3% and 57.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FILTR with frozen pretrained encoders outperforms all end-to-end baselines on DONUT W₂: the best pretrained variant (Point-MAE_C, 16.02×10⁻²) beats DGCNN (16.62×10⁻²), PointNet (24.85×10⁻²), and the pathological PointNet++ (39.37×10⁻²). On ModelNet40, Point-BERT_C achieves the lowest W₂ (41.92×10⁻²) among all methods, surpassing DGCNN (43.27×10⁻²) and PointNet (51.32×10⁻²), while on ABC, DGCNN narrowly leads on bottleneck distance (31.70×10⁻³ vs. 31.87×10⁻³ for Point-BERT_L) and PIE (3.760 vs. 3.780 for Point-BERT_C). Among the four pretrained encoders, Point-MAE and Point-BERT are the strongest on DONUT, while PointGPT and Point-BERT generalize best to out-of-distribution data (ModelNet40/ABC), whereas Point-MAE and PCP-MAE degrade more sharply under distribution shift. The end-to-end DGCNN baseline is the closest competitor, slightly outperforming pretrained encoders on ModelNet40 and ABC for bottleneck distance and PIE, but requiring substantially more trainable parameters. RepSurf, the strongest end-to-end model on the probing task (83.3% on #components), is discussed in the appendix and relies on a PointNet++ backbone, which is the pathological case in Table 3.

## Relation to Prior Work

FILTR adapts the DETR set-prediction paradigm (originally for 2D object detection) to the problem of predicting persistence diagrams, replacing bounding boxes with birth-death pairs and using Hungarian matching with a diagonal regularizer. It departs from prior persistence-diagram approximation work (RipsNet, Yan et al.) that hard-codes algorithmic structure mirroring persistent homology computation, instead relying on topological signals implicitly captured by pretrained 3D encoders. The paper also inverts the common TDA-for-ML pipeline: rather than vectorizing diagrams as inputs to downstream models (PersLay, ATOL, sliced Wasserstein kernels), it treats diagrams as the output target, enabling direct measurement of how much topology resides in encoder features. The DONUT benchmark complements existing topologically-annotated datasets (ABC, Thingi10K) by providing clean, manifold, balanced topological labels specifically designed for probing encoder representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic quantification of topological competence in pretrained 3D point-cloud encoders, revealing that current self-supervised pretraining strategies (masked reconstruction, autoregressive generation) do not strongly encode global topology, yet local geometric structure suffices for a non-linear decoder to recover approximate persistence diagrams. The practical implication is a feed-forward, differentiable alternative to classical TDA pipelines that can be plugged into learning-based architectures. Key limitations include restricted evaluation to H1 with alpha-filtration, training on a synthetic dataset, and noticeable degradation under distribution shift (ModelNet40, ABC). Open questions include whether multimodal foundation models encode topology differently, and whether these findings transfer to domains like graph learning where topology is central but strong general-purpose encoders remain lacking.
