## Problem

Real-world infrared image super-resolution (IISR) remains largely unaddressed: existing IISR methods are trained on synthetic degradations from downsampled fusion datasets that fail to capture the coupled optical-sensor degradations (spatially varying blur, thermal drift, radiometric nonlinearity) of real infrared imaging. Moreover, no prior super-resolution framework incorporates infrared-specific physical constraints, where thermal intensity frequently misaligns with structural edges, causing boundary distortion and thermal peak drift in reconstructions.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 128×128 low-resolution infrared image (4× downsampled from a 1024×768 FLIR T1050sc capture) with real-world defocus or motion blur as input, and produces a 512×512 high-resolution infrared reconstruction. Beyond the raw LR image, the pipeline derives auxiliary heat maps and edge maps from the input to provide thermal-structural guidance, and the autoregressive decoder generates the HR image scale-by-scale via next-scale token prediction rather than a single-step regression.

## Method

Real-IISR builds on the VAR (next-scale prediction) autoregressive backbone with a VQ-VAE tokenizer, introducing three infrared-specific components. First, a Thermal-Structural Guidance (TSG) module encodes a heat map and an edge map via DINOv3-based encoders, fuses them with a learnable attention gate, and injects the fused layout prior into LR features through cross-attention to align thermal radiation with object boundaries. Second, a Condition-Adaptive Codebook (CAC) replaces static VQ-VAE table lookup with low-rank perturbations (rank r=8) conditioned on degradation-aware TSG features, so the same discrete index decodes to different embedding vectors under different blur/noise conditions, mitigating quantization bias. Third, a Thermal Order Consistency Loss (L_TOC) enforces monotonic brightness-temperature ordering across adjacent patch pairs via a ReLU penalty on inverted orderings, remaining robust to sub-pixel LR-HR misalignment. Training combines token-level cross-entropy, pixel-level MSE, and L_TOC (λ₁=0.2, λ₂=0.8), fine-tuning from pre-trained VAR/VQVAE weights for 10k iterations on 4 A800 GPUs.

## Results

On the proposed FLIR-IISR dataset, Real-IISR achieves the best no-reference scores (MUSIQ 59.90 / MANIQA 0.3776 on Set5; MUSIQ 57.06 / MANIQA 0.3403 on Set15) and the best reference-based scores (PSNR 28.51 / SSIM 0.8278 / LPIPS 0.1615 on Set5; PSNR 29.51 / SSIM 0.8895 / LPIPS 0.1340 on Set15). On the M³FD benchmark, Real-IISR attains the highest MUSIQ (41.58 / 49.05) and best LPIPS (0.1997 / 0.1361) across both subsets, though it trails BI-DiffSR and DifIISR on PSNR (32.32 vs. 35.92 on Set5) and SinSR/DifIISR on MANIQA. The model also achieves the fastest inference among all compared methods at 2.45 FPS on a single A800 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the FLIR-IISR dataset (both motion-blur Set5 and optical-blur Set15), Real-IISR outperforms all nine baselines on every metric, with the largest margins over IISR methods (CoRPLE, InfraFFN) and visible-domain ISR methods (HAT, BI-DiffSR, PFT-SR, RealSR). The closest competitors are SinSR (MUSIQ 54.16 vs. 59.90 on Set5) and DifIISR (MUSIQ 54.79 vs. 59.90), both of which lack infrared-specific degradation modeling. On M³FD, Real-IISR wins on MUSIQ and LPIPS across both subsets but trails BI-DiffSR (PSNR 35.92 vs. 32.32) and DifIISR (PSNR 35.84 vs. 32.32) on PSNR, and loses on MANIQA to SinSR (0.3348 vs. 0.3074 on Set15) and DifIISR (0.3248 vs. 0.3074). The autoregressive baseline VARSR is consistently outperformed by Real-IISR on all four dataset subsets, confirming that the thermal-structural guidance and condition-adaptive codebook provide meaningful gains over a generic VAR-SR backbone. Overall, Real-IISR demonstrates the strongest perceptual quality (MUSIQ) and structural fidelity (SSIM, LPIPS) while trading some pixel-level PSNR on the M³FD benchmark, where its training distribution (FLIR-IISR) differs from the evaluation data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Visual Autoregressive (VAR) framework for super-resolution (VARSR [35]) into the infrared domain, adapting it with three infrared-specific components: a Thermal-Structural Guidance module that uses DINOv3-based encoders to align heat maps with edge maps, a Condition-Adaptive Codebook that modulates VQ-VAE embeddings via low-rank perturbations conditioned on degradation priors, and a Thermal Order Consistency Loss enforcing monotonic temperature-intensity ordering. It departs from prior IISR methods (CoRPLE, DifIISR, CRG) that rely on synthetic degradations, and from real-world visible SR methods (RealSR, SinSR, Real-ESRGAN) that ignore infrared-specific physics, by constructing the first paired real-world infrared SR dataset (FLIR-IISR) with actual defocus and motion blur captured via a FLIR T1050sc camera.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FLIR-IISR dataset (1,457 paired LR-HR images across 6 cities, 3 seasons, 12 scene categories) fills a critical gap: prior IISR methods were trained on downsampled IVIF datasets that cannot capture coupled optical-sensor degradations, leading to poor real-world generalization. The physically-motivated TOC loss and the finding that VAR outperforms diffusion (ResShift) for infrared SR provide useful design guidance for thermal imaging restoration. However, the dataset is relatively small, limited to a single sensor and 4× magnification, and the model's PSNR on the synthetic M³FD benchmark is notably lower than diffusion-based baselines (32.3 vs 35.9 on Set5), suggesting a perceptual-fidelity trade-off that may limit applicability where radiometric accuracy is paramount. Open questions include scalability to higher magnifications, multi-sensor generalization, and whether the 1.1B parameter count is necessary or if smaller models with the same guidance mechanisms could match performance.
