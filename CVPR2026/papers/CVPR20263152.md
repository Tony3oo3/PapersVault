## Problem

Black-box copyright tracking for MLLMs requires trigger images that reliably activate only in fine-tuned derivatives of a base model while remaining inert in unrelated models. Existing trigger-based methods (e.g., PLA) overfit to the base model's specific response patterns, causing degraded tracking performance when the derivative undergoes domain-specific fine-tuning, and they lack a mechanism to exploit structural invariances shared across MLLM variants.

## Input / Output

The method takes 200 clean RGB images (sampled from ImageNet-1K validation) and 5 rare trigger question–answer pairs as input, producing 1000 trigger images (200 per question) via adversarial perturbation under an L∞ budget of 16/255. The trigger question is deliberately chosen to be rare so that standard downstream fine-tuning datasets do not inadvertently teach the trigger pattern. At verification time, the trigger image is paired with its trigger question and submitted as a black-box query to the suspicious model; a match between the model's generated answer and the target text (via substring inclusion) constitutes a positive copyright signal.

## Method

AGDI formulates trigger image generation as a min-max adversarial optimization with a dual-injection loss. The first injection (response-level) minimizes the cross-entropy between an auxiliary MLLM's token-level output and the predefined target text, while the second injection (semantic-level) minimizes the cosine distance between the CLIP-like image encoder's features of the trigger image and the text encoder's features of the target text, exploiting the observation that this cross-modal alignment module remains stable across fine-tuned variants. The auxiliary model is simultaneously updated in the opposite direction (maximizing the same loss) to simulate the resistance that downstream fine-tuning would impose, forcing the trigger to discover more transferable perturbations. The trigger image is updated via PGD sign-gradient steps while model parameters use clipped gradient descent; after each trigger image is optimized, the auxiliary model is reset to its reference copy to prevent cumulative drift. This design ensures the trigger carries ownership-relevant semantics at both the end-to-end textual behavior level and the shared CLIP-like embedding level, confining activation to derivative models while avoiding false positives on non-derivative architectures.

## Results

AGDI is evaluated using Attack Success Rate (ASR) on downstream fine-tuned variants of Qwen2-VL and LLaVA-1.5 across five domain-specific datasets (V7W, ST-VQA, TextVQA, PaintingForm, MathV). On Qwen2-VL LoRA fine-tuning, AGDI achieves ASR of 53–81% (mean ~64%), outperforming PLA (48–76%, mean ~57%) and RNA (36–40%, mean ~35%). On LLaVA-1.5 LoRA fine-tuning, AGDI reaches 30–79% ASR versus PLA's 18–55% and IF's 8–30%. AGDI also achieves 0% ASR on non-derivative models (MiniGPT-4, Qwen2-VL, LLaVA-1.6, Llama3-V), confirming no false positives.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGDI consistently outperforms all baselines across every fine-tuned variant and dataset. Against PLA (Wang et al., ICLR 2025), the closest competitor, AGDI gains 5–13 percentage points in ASR on Qwen2-VL LoRA fine-tuning (e.g., 81% vs. 76% on PaintingForm, 68% vs. 60% on MathV) and 13–24 points on LLaVA-1.5 (e.g., 79% vs. 55% on PaintingForm, 64% vs. 51% on V7W). RNA (also from Wang et al.) performs near-randomly (28–40% ASR), confirming that stochastic parameter perturbation without semantic guidance is insufficient. IF (Xu et al., NAACL 2024), an LLM instruction-fingerprinting method, is weakest on LLaVA-1.5 (8–30% ASR), as it was not designed for multimodal trigger images. AGDI also maintains 0% ASR on four non-derivative MLLMs, matching PLA and RNA in avoiding false positives while achieving substantially higher true-positive tracking rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM copyright tracking line initiated by PLA [32], which introduced adversarial trigger images with response-level alignment to embed ownership signals. AGDI departs from PLA in two key ways: it adds a semantic-level injection that exploits the stability of the CLIP-like cross-modal alignment module shared across fine-tuned variants, and it introduces adversarial training of an auxiliary model to simulate downstream fine-tuning resistance. The work also positions itself against white-box fingerprinting and watermarking methods [12, 14, 34] that require internal model access, arguing for a purely black-box, post-deployment verification paradigm that is practical for proprietary or closed-source derivative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As open-source MLLMs (LLaVA, Qwen-VL, etc.) proliferate and are fine-tuned for commercial applications, a practical black-box copyright tracking mechanism is increasingly urgent for model publishers. The observation that the CLIP-like alignment module remains stable across fine-tuning variants provides a principled basis for transferable triggers, but the approach is inherently architecture-specific (0% ASR on non-derivative models), limiting its applicability to a single model family. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to fundamentally different MLLM architectures, how it performs under iterative or multi-round fine-tuning, and whether the trigger images can be made more robust against deliberate removal attacks by well-resourced adversaries.
