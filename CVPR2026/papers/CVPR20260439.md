## Problem

Existing articulated object reconstruction methods either require fixed multi-view cameras with non-trivial axis alignment between articulation states, or assume a static base part during casual monocular video capture—both of which limit real-world scalability. No prior work or benchmark addresses the free-moving scenario where both the object pose and joint state vary arbitrarily relative to the camera, leaving a gap in producing full-coverage, simulation-ready articulated assets from unconstrained capture.

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB-D video (640×360, ~100 frames) of a two-part articulated object along with foreground masks from SAM-2, and produces canonical 3D Gaussians for each rigid part, the joint type (revolute or prismatic), joint axis and pivot, and per-frame joint angles or displacements. The output is a fully articulated 3DGS asset that can be rendered at novel views and joint states. The method assumes exactly two rigid parts and requires depth for 3D lifting of tracked points.

## Method

FreeArtGS operates in three stages. First, a sliding-window part segmentation module lifts AllTracker 2D trajectories to 3D using depth, then jointly optimizes two rigid transforms and per-point soft part weights (initialized by DINOv3 feature clustering) with a Huber reconstruction loss regularized by entropy, feature-space smoothness, and initialization consistency. Second, a joint estimation module uses off-the-shelf pose estimators (BundleSDF) to obtain robust part-to-camera transforms, calibrates both parts to a canonical frame, and solves the joint type (via rotation span and translation linearity) and axis (closed-form for revolute, PCA for prismatic) from pairwise relative transforms with 2σ outlier filtering for noise resistance. Third, an end-to-end 3DGS optimization applies blended rendering—soft part weights interpolate between identity and joint-transformed Gaussians—and jointly refines Gaussian parameters, camera poses, and articulation variables under RGB (L1+SSIM), depth, and mask supervision.

## Results

On the new FreeArt-21 benchmark, FreeArtGS achieves a mean joint axis error of 1.04° (revolute) and 1.85° (prismatic), with geometry errors below 0.5 cm Chamfer distance on the whole object, and a PSNR of 24.02 dB (revolute) / 22.92 dB (prismatic). On Video2Articulation-S, the method further reduces axis error to 1.77° (revolute) and 0.77° (prismatic) with 1.00 cm state error, outperforming all baselines by a large margin. On six real-world objects, the average axis error is 2.73° and average PSNR is 22.40 dB, confirming robustness to hand occlusion and unconstrained capture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreeArtGS substantially outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric. Against Articulate-Anything [16] (a vision-language foundation model predicting URDF from a single image), FreeArtGS reduces revolute axis error from 42.00° to 1.04° (≈40× improvement) and prismatic axis error from 45.00° to 1.85°; Articulate-Anything also fails to produce geometry or rendering outputs. Against Video2Articulation [24] (feed-forward point-map dynamics under a static-base assumption), FreeArtGS cuts revolute axis error from 20.00° to 1.04° and state error from 27.37° to 1.43°, while also achieving 24.02 dB PSNR where Video2Articulation reports none. On Video2Articulation-S, RSRD [15] performs worst (67.06° axis error, 203 cm position error) due to its assumption of unique per-part motion patterns, while FreeArtGS achieves 1.77° axis and 1.31 cm position. The paper attributes the gap to FreeArtGS's optimization-based refinement of off-the-shelf priors (point tracking, pose estimation) rather than relying on them as final predictions, which is especially critical under the free-moving setting where baselines' static-base or single-view assumptions break down.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeArtGS departs from three existing lines of articulated object reconstruction: foundation-model single-view methods (Articulate-Anything), multi-view two-state methods requiring axis alignment (SPLART), and monocular video methods assuming a static base part (Video2Articulation, MonoMobility, RSRD). It builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting for the rendering/reconstruction backbone and AllTracker for dense point-tracking priors, but the key conceptual departure is an optimization-based part segmentation that identifies rigid parts purely from relative motion without any static-reference assumption. The joint estimation module then recovers kinematic parameters from the relative part transforms, and a blended-rendering end-to-end 3DGS stage jointly refines geometry, appearance, and articulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The free-moving setting addresses a practical gap: real-world capture of objects like scissors or pliers inevitably involves moving the whole object, violating the static-base assumption of prior video-based methods. The new FreeArt-21 benchmark (21 objects, 7 categories, VR-teleoperated in Sapien) provides the first evaluation protocol for this setting. Key limitations remain: the method is restricted to two-part objects, relies on a chain of off-the-shelf models (SAM-2, AllTracker, DINOv3, pose estimators) that can accumulate cascading errors, and requires RGB-D input rather than RGB-only. Extending to multi-part structures, unifying the pipeline into a single feed-forward model, and removing the depth requirement are the natural next steps.
