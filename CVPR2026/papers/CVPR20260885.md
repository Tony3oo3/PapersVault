## Problem

Existing RLVR datasets for video MLLMs suffer from a bimodal zero-variance problem: powerful models like Qwen3-VL either solve nearly all questions perfectly or fail all of them, providing ineffective learning signals for GRPO training. Additionally, multi-agent annotation pipelines introduce systemic biases that create spurious reward signals, while manual annotation of new high-quality video data remains prohibitively expensive. The paper asks whether the intrinsic information within videos themselves can be harnessed to self-generate high-quality, verifiable training data without any human or MLLM annotations.

## Input / Output

The method takes raw video clips that are algorithmically modified before presentation: segments are perturbed (rotated, color-swapped, frame-shuffled), procedural geometric shapes are overlaid onto selected frames, or the video is partitioned into shuffled segments. During training, videos are processed at 48 frames with 256×256 pixel resolution; evaluation uses 32/48/64 frames at 512×512 pixels with FPS=2. Outputs are structured verifiable answers: (start, end) timestamp pairs for anomaly localization, a vector of per-shape-type counts for object counting, and an inverse-permutation sequence for temporal jigsaw restoration.

## Method

VideoSSR constructs the VideoSSR-30K dataset entirely from three self-supervised pretext tasks—Anomaly Grounding, Object Counting, and Temporal Jigsaw—each generating verifiable Q&A pairs directly from raw videos with parametrically scalable difficulty (e.g., number of shapes, number of segments, perturbation type). Training employs GRPO on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct for one epoch, but the key technical contribution is a set of task-specific smooth reward functions that replace strict binary matching: IoU-based reward for temporal grounding, average relative-error reward for counting, and normalized cumulative-displacement reward for jigsaw ordering. These smooth rewards address the sparse-signal problem inherent in difficult pretext tasks, where strict matching would yield near-zero advantages in GRPO and cause training instability. The framework demonstrates that task diversity (mixing all three pretext tasks) outperforms scaling a single task, and that self-supervised data surpasses annotated datasets of comparable scale (LongVideoReason, ReWatch) while avoiding annotation-induced degradation.

## Results

VideoSSR-8B (built on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) achieves an average improvement of 5.1% across all 17 benchmarks at 48 input frames. The most dramatic gains appear on temporal grounding (QVHighlights mIoU: 61.1 vs. 46.4, +14.7) and complex reasoning (VCRBench accuracy: 15.3 vs. 7.4, +7.9), while general video QA shows consistent but smaller gains (e.g., MVBench 68.8 vs. 67.6, VinoGround 55.6 vs. 45.0). On the newly introduced VIUBench, VideoSSR-8B scores 51.9 overall, surpassing all open-source models (Qwen3-VL-8B: 19.5, GLM-4.5V: 34.7) though still trailing GPT-5 (58.7). Training takes ~16 hours on 8 H200 GPUs with GRPO for one epoch on 30K samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, VideoSSR-8B wins on all 16 external benchmarks with no regressions, with the largest margins on VinoGround (+10.6), QVHighlights (+14.7), and VCRBench (+7.9). Against closed-source GPT-4o, VideoSSR-8B surpasses it on MVBench (68.8 vs. 64.6), TempCompass (75.8 vs. 73.8), and VinoGround (55.6 vs. 54.0), but trails on AoTBench (61.7 vs. 63.4) and all long-video benchmarks. Against Gemini-1.5-Pro, VideoSSR-8B dominates on general video QA (e.g., VinoGround 55.6 vs. 35.8) but remains behind on long-video tasks (Video-MME 66.7 vs. 75.0). On VIUBench, VideoSSR-8B (51.9) outperforms all open-source models including Qwen3-VL-235B (30.5) and GLM-4.5V (34.7), yet still falls short of GPT-5 (58.7) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (56.7). In the training-data ablation (Table 5), VideoSSR-30K outperforms both LongVideoReason (32k) and ReWatch (27k) on all reported metrics, while fine-tuning on LongVideoReason actually degrades Video-MME performance (63.6 vs. 64.1 baseline), confirming the bias issue with multi-agent-annotated data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: RLVR for MLLMs (Video-R1, Time-R1, SpaceR, ReWatch-R1) and self-supervised video representation learning (jigsaw puzzles, counting, odd-one-out). It departs from prior RLVR work by eliminating the dependency on external annotations—whether multi-agent or manual—for generating verifiable training data, instead deriving questions directly from intrinsic video properties. It also departs from prior self-supervised video work, which typically targets a single pretext task for representation learning, by combining three diverse pretext tasks with parametrically scalable difficulty within a GRPO-based RLVR pipeline for MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck: as MLLMs grow more capable, existing annotated video datasets become too easy (bimodal zero-variance scores) and expensive to refresh, while the finding that training a powerful model on data annotated by a less-capable model can degrade performance (Table 5) challenges the assumption that more annotated data is always beneficial. The self-supervised paradigm offers a scalable, bias-free alternative, and the VIUBench provides a useful diagnostic for intrinsic video understanding. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer videos (the paper acknowledges a gap on long-video benchmarks), whether it scales beyond 8B models, and whether additional pretext tasks can further close the remaining performance gap with closed-source systems.
