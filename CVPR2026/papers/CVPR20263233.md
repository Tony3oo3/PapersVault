## Problem

Existing MLLM-based video QA methods suffer from quadratic memory and time costs as frame counts grow, and simply increasing sampling density can degrade accuracy by introducing redundant, distracting frames. Prior agent-based approaches are slow (e.g., VideoAgent exceeds ten minutes per hour-long video), rely on a single MLLM without effective multi-agent collaboration, and struggle to locate key frames for long or complex questions. The paper addresses how to achieve high-quality long-video reasoning under tight frame budgets through coordinated multi-agent perception and action.

## Input / Output

A4VL consumes a video (partitioned into event-based blocks), a text question with multiple-choice options, and optionally subtitles. Internally it operates on two tiers of sampled frames: N1=4 randomly drawn perception frames for clue generation, and N2=16 CLIP-guided action frames for answer reasoning. The output is a single selected answer choice accompanied by a concise explanatory reason synthesized by a summarizer that reads the full multi-round deliberation history.

## Method

A4VL is a training-free multi-agent framework that iterates a perception-action loop across up to three rounds. In perception exploration, each agent samples 4 random frames to produce a query-specific perception clue; the video is then partitioned into event-based blocks (via DINOv2 embeddings, KTS/PELT/SSM novelty detection, and NMS), and CLIP similarity between each clue and each block drives adaptive frame allocation to select 16 action frames. In action exploration, agents generate answers with reasons; if full consensus is not reached, agents cross-evaluate each other (1–10 ratings), the lowest-scoring agent is pruned, and surviving agents refine their perception clues to target the next round. An unsupervised agent-teaming step pre-selects the top-3 agents from a pool of 8 heterogeneous MLLMs (e.g., InternVL3-78B, QwenVL-2.5-72B, LLaVA-Video-72B) based on agreement frequency over unlabeled task samples, ensuring task-specific collaboration without ground-truth labels.

## Results

A4VL achieves the best accuracy across all five benchmarks: 85.1% on NeXT-QA, 82.2% on EgoSchema, 72.2% on LongVideoBench, 58.0% on MLVU, and 77.2% (without subtitles) on Video-MME. The largest absolute gain is on LongVideoBench, where A4VL (built solely from open-source models) surpasses GPT-4o by 5.5 points (72.2% vs. 66.7%). On Video-MME, A4VL exceeds the strongest closed-source model Gemini 1.5 Pro by 2.2 points (77.2% vs. 75.0% without subtitles). In terms of latency, A4VL answers a single MLVU question in 74 s, compared to 127 s for GPT-4o, 204 s for InternVL3-78B, and 450 s for TraveLER.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A4VL outperforms all 23 baselines on every benchmark. Against the strongest single-model baselines, A4VL gains +1.1 pp over InternVL3-78B on NeXT-QA (85.1 vs. 84.0), +3.2 pp on EgoSchema (82.2 vs. 76.8), and +2.2 pp over Gemini 1.5 Pro on Video-MME without subtitles (77.2 vs. 75.0). The most striking margin is on LongVideoBench, where A4VL (72.2%) beats GPT-4o (66.7%) by 5.5 pp and VideoRAG-72B (65.4%) by 6.8 pp, despite using only open-source models. Among agent-based systems, A4VL surpasses LVAgent (the closest agent baseline) by 1.8 pp on EgoSchema, 5.3 pp on LongVideoBench, and 8.0 pp on MLVU, while also reducing inference latency from 175 s to 74 s on MLVU. Compared to TraveLER, A4VL improves accuracy by 26.8 pp on MLVU (58.0 vs. 36.3) while cutting latency by roughly 6× (74 s vs. 450 s). On the hardest benchmark MLVU, A4VL leads the next-best method (InternVL3.5-38B at 56.1%) by 1.9 pp, confirming gains even under complex multi-task long-video conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

A4VL carves new ground at the intersection of multi-agent LLM collaboration and long-video understanding, departing from prior agent-based systems (VideoAgent, TraveLER, LVAgent) that rely on a single MLLM for all decision-making and lack structured multi-agent coordination. It repurposes standard change-point detection tools (KTS, PELT, SSM novelty) and CLIP similarity scoring into a perception-action loop, combining them with an unsupervised agent-teaming selection mechanism that has no direct precedent in the video QA literature. The authors explicitly position their approach as orthogonal to token-merging (DYTO, BOLT) and architecture-level (AuroraLong, DynFocus) optimizations, framing multi-agent perception-action alliance as a complementary axis of improvement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A4VL demonstrates that a training-free, multi-agent coordination framework can match or exceed the accuracy of much larger single-model systems (e.g., GPT-4o, InternVL3-78B) while cutting inference latency by roughly 40–60% on long videos, making long-video QA more practical for real-world deployment. The ablations confirm that each component—event-based partitioning, agent pruning, and full-consensus stopping—contributes meaningfully, but the system still requires six H200 GPUs and a pool of 8 candidate MLLMs, limiting accessibility. Open questions include whether the CLIP-based block similarity can be replaced by richer task-conditioned functions, whether the framework extends to audio-visual inputs, and whether the 3-agent cap is truly optimal or merely a latency trade-off.
