## Problem

Reconstructing the full geometry and motion of dynamic scenes from video remains challenging because existing approaches fragment the task into separate, task-specific decoders or rely on expensive test-time optimization to fuse disparate signals. No prior method provides a unified, single-stage feedforward formulation that simultaneously handles depth, spatiotemporal correspondence, and camera parameters for both static and dynamic content while remaining computationally efficient.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB video clip (48 frames at 256×256 during training) and produces a fixed latent Global Scene Representation that can be queried on demand. Rather than emitting a fixed set of outputs, the decoder accepts arbitrary queries of the form (u, v, t_src, t_tgt, t_cam) and returns the 3D position of the corresponding point, enabling flexible extraction of point tracks, full point clouds, per-frame depth maps, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics through simple variations of the query parameters. Camera extrinsics are recovered via Umeyama's algorithm on decoded 3D point sets, and intrinsics via a pinhole-model back-projection with median aggregation over a coarse grid.

## Method

D4RT uses a ViT-g encoder (1B parameters, 40 layers, 2×16×16 spatio-temporal patches) to produce a Global Scene Representation F, followed by a lightweight 8-layer cross-attention decoder (144M parameters) that independently decodes each query. Each query token is constructed from Fourier feature embeddings of the 2D coordinates, learned discrete timestep embeddings for t_src/t_tgt/t_cam, and a local 9×9 RGB patch embedding that provides critical low-level appearance context. The key architectural insight is that queries are processed fully independently (no inter-query self-attention), which minimizes training memory (only 2048 sampled queries per video), enables trivial parallelization at inference, and forces the encoder to encode global scene consistency into F. An occupancy-grid algorithm (Alg. 1) exploits spatio-temporal redundancy to track all pixels in a video with 5–15× speedup over naive enumeration, and the model is trained end-to-end with L1 3D position loss plus auxiliary losses on 2D position, surface normals, visibility, displacement, and confidence.

## Results

On TAPVid-3D (with GT intrinsics), D4RT achieves APD₃D of 0.410 on DriveTrack, 0.408 on ADT, and 0.498 on PStudio for camera-coordinate 3D tracking, and APD₃D of 0.470 (L1=0.017) on DriveTrack for world-coordinate tracking, outperforming all baselines. For point cloud reconstruction, D4RT achieves L1 of 0.768 on Sintel and 0.028 on ScanNet, the best across all methods. On camera pose estimation, D4RT attains ATE of 0.065 on Sintel and 0.014 on ScanNet, and Pose AUC of 83.5 on Re10K. In terms of efficiency, D4RT produces 550 full-video 3D tracks at 60 FPS on a single A100, which is 18–300× faster than prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D4RT outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated task. Against the strongest tracking competitor SpatialTrackerV2, D4RT improves APD₃D by 49% on DriveTrack (0.410 vs 0.275) and by 134% on world-coordinate DriveTrack (0.470 vs 0.201), while also reducing L1 error from 0.117 to 0.017. The CoTracker3+VGGT pipeline, the best multi-model baseline, is surpassed by D4RT on all camera-coordinate metrics (e.g., APD₃D 0.410 vs 0.342 on DriveTrack) and by a wide margin on world-coordinate tracking (APD₃D 0.470 vs 0.292). For pure reconstruction, D4RT beats π³ (the best prior method) on Sintel point cloud L1 (0.768 vs 1.139) and Sintel depth AbsRel(S) (0.171 vs 0.241), and on ScanNet pose ATE (0.014 vs 0.015). MegaSaM and VGGT, which lack dynamic correspondence, are outperformed on depth and pose but cannot be directly compared on tracking. In throughput, D4RT produces 550 tracks at 60 FPS versus 0 for DELTA and 29 for SpatialTrackerV2, a 18–300× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

D4RT builds directly on the Scene Representation Transformer (SRT) paradigm of encoding a scene into a global latent representation and then independently querying it, but extends this from static novel-view synthesis to full 4D dynamic reconstruction. It departs from the VGGT/DUSt3R lineage, which relies on separate task-specific decoders (depth head, pose head, point-cloud head) or multi-model pipelines, by replacing all of them with a single lightweight cross-attention decoder that handles every output modality through a unified query interface. It also contrasts with iterative-refinement trackers like SpatialTrackerV2 and pairwise methods like St4RTrack by processing the entire video holistically in one forward pass with no test-time optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified query-based interface eliminates the architectural fragmentation that has plagued 4D reconstruction, making it straightforward to add new output modalities without new decoder heads. The 18–300× speedup over prior tracking methods and the 9× advantage over VGGT on pose estimation make dense, all-pixel 4D reconstruction practically feasible on a single GPU, opening the door to real-time applications. Open questions include how the 48-frame, 256×256 training regime generalizes to longer or higher-resolution videos, whether the VideoMAEv2 pretraining dependency can be removed, and whether the occupancy-grid dense-tracking algorithm scales to very long sequences without memory blowup.
