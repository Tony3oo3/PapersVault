## Problem

LVLMs suffer from hallucination because visual information is strongly expressed only in early decoder layers and rapidly diminishes, while system prompts and textual priors dominate mid-to-late layers, weakening the causal pathway from visual tokens to output generation. Existing inference-time mitigations (contrastive decoding, multi-pass feedback) require multiple model queries, inflating latency and memory, which limits their use in real-time settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261084/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard RGB image and a text prompt (system prompt plus user query) in the usual LVLM format, and produces autoregressively generated text (captions, VQA answers) as output. The intervention is applied entirely within a single forward pass on the decoder's mid-layer hidden states (layers 10–20), requiring no additional model queries, no perturbed inputs, and no architectural modification—only a modest increase in per-step computation for the head decomposition and residual correction.

## Method

CausalLens decomposes each multi-head attention layer into three pathway-specific contributions (system prompt, textual, visual) by slicing the attention matrix along contiguous token segments. A visual sensitivity score (variance-to-mean ratio of a head's attention over image tokens) identifies heads with concentrated, object-level visual focus; only these high-sensitivity heads receive amplification. A hybrid causal intervention in mid-layers shifts each selected head's representation along a visual-minus-system contrast direction, scaled by the normalized sensitivity and gated by an adaptive coefficient γ that balances the intervention against the original head output based on the relative energy of system versus visual pathways. After multi-head fusion, a projection-aligned residual correction re-injects the visual-minus-system difference through the output projection matrix W^O, ensuring the intervention survives the linear mixing into the layer's semantic basis. The entire procedure is training-free, single-pass, and adds negligible latency (~1.04× baseline) and memory overhead.

## Results

On the POPE benchmark (averaged over MS-COCO, A-OKVQA, and GQA), CausalLens achieves the best accuracy and F1 across all three models and all three split types: e.g., on LLaVA-v1.5-7B it reaches 90.6% accuracy / 90.4 F1 (Random), 86.5% / 86.8 (Popular), and 81.6% / 82.8 (Adversarial), outperforming the strongest baseline VAF by 1.0–1.5 points. On CHAIR (LLaVA-v1.5-7B, max token 64), CausalLens attains the lowest hallucination rates of CHAIR_S = 18.7 and CHAIR_I = 6.2, compared to 19.1/6.3 for DeGF and 23.1/7.7 for VCD. In terms of efficiency, CausalLens adds only 0.012 s latency (×1.04) and 213 MB peak memory (×1.01) over the regular baseline, remaining on par with VAF and substantially faster than VCD (×2.01) and DeGF (×4.07).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CausalLens outperforms all three baselines—VCD, DeGF, and VAF—on every POPE category and model, with the largest margins on the Adversarial split (e.g., +1.5 acc over VAF on LLaVA-7B, +1.3 over DeGF on LLaVA-13B). On CHAIR, CausalLens achieves the best sentence-level rate (18.7 vs. 19.1 for DeGF, 20.7 for VAF, 23.1 for VCD) and the best individual-object rate (6.2 vs. 6.3 for DeGF), confirming consistent gains in captioning fidelity. In terms of efficiency, CausalLens is essentially tied with VAF in latency (0.293 s vs. 0.292 s) and memory (×1.01 vs. ×1.00), while being roughly 1.6× faster than VCD and 3.9× faster than DeGF, which require multiple forward passes or iterative feedback. The only setting where a baseline is marginally competitive is Qwen2-VL-7B Adversarial accuracy, where VAF reaches 84.9 vs. CausalLens's 84.7, though CausalLens still leads on F1 (85.2 vs. 84.8). Overall, CausalLens dominates the accuracy–efficiency frontier, matching the speed of single-pass methods while surpassing them in hallucination suppression.

## Relation to Prior Work

CausalLens departs from the dominant contrastive-decoding paradigm (VCD, DeGF, VAF) that requires multiple forward passes by operating as a single-pass, training-free intervention directly on decoder hidden states. Conceptually, it extends the line of attention-head-level interventions [11, 34, 37, 47] that identify visually relevant heads, but shifts the intervention target from attention weights to the post-attention hidden-state contributions, introducing a sensitivity-based selection criterion and a projection-aligned residual correction to preserve modifications through multi-head fusion. The causal-decomposition framing (visual vs. textual vs. system-prompt pathways) repurposes the additive structure of multi-head attention as a natural intervention surface, a perspective not explicitly exploited in prior hallucination-mitigation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive hallucination reduction with near-zero latency overhead (0.293 s vs. 0.281 s baseline), making it deployable in real-time settings where contrastive decoding's 2–4× latency is prohibitive. The structural finding that visual attention collapses to early layers while system-prompt attention dominates mid-to-late layers provides a mechanistic explanation for hallucination that could inform future architectural designs. Open questions include whether the fixed mid-layer window (L10–L20) and sensitivity metric generalize to architectures with different depth, tokenization, or visual-encoder designs, and whether the method degrades on tasks requiring fine-grained spatial reasoning beyond object presence.
