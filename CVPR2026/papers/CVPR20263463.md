## Problem

Existing defenses against adversarial patch attacks on object detection models suffer from two critical limitations: they degrade performance on benign images and require processing times (often >4000 ms) incompatible with real-time OD applications that demand at least 10 FPS. Since only a small fraction of OD deployments face adversarial threats, a defense must preserve benign accuracy while remaining fast enough for real-time use, a balance that prior purification-based and in-training defenses have not achieved.

## Input / Output

AntiStyler takes a standard RGB image as input, first pads it with a 10-pixel border of random values drawn from a uniform [0,1) distribution to guarantee the presence of a 'random style' for both benign and adversarial images. It outputs a binary mask identifying adversarial patch regions and a defended image (the original with the negative mask applied, zeroing out patch pixels) that is forwarded to any downstream OD model. The full pipeline processes each image in approximately 80–90 ms on an RTX 3090, yielding 10–12 FPS suitable for real-time deployment.

## Method

AntiStyler's core insight is that adversarial patches exhibit a 'random' visual style that can be detected and removed by inverting the style transfer objective: instead of minimizing the style loss (Gram-matrix MSE) to blend styles, it maximizes it to push the output image's style away from a random-style reference, while preserving content via the standard content loss. The pipeline consists of four phases: (1) a VGG-19-based AntiStyle model performs style removal on the padded image, producing an AntiStyled image; (2) a filter phase computes pixel-wise absolute differences between the padded input and AntiStyled image, thresholding at a dynamic τ-percentile of the maximum change to yield a raw mask; (3) an enhancement phase applies a sequence of spatial filters (dilation, erosion, smoothing, thresholding, and a final dilation) to merge nearby masked pixels, remove isolated noise, and close holes; (4) the negative of the final mask is applied to the original input to produce the defended image. The method is fully agnostic (model-, patch-, and attack-agnostic) and requires no training, operating as a zero-shot defense with a single optimization step per image.

## Results

On the COCO dataset, AntiStyler improves adversarial mAP by approximately 8–15% over undefended models while fully preserving benign performance (e.g., 51.6% benign mAP for Faster R-CNN, 53.0% for DETR). Against the M-PGD attack on Faster R-CNN, AntiStyler achieves 38.0% adversarial mAP versus 22.3% undefended, outperforming all five baselines. Processing time is 85–93 ms per image (10–12 FPS), making it the only post-training defense alongside NutNet (~40–45 ms) that meets the 10 FPS real-time threshold, compared to 4,000–55,000 ms for ObjectSeeker, PAD, and DIFFender.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AntiStyler outperforms all five baselines on adversarial mAP for Faster R-CNN across all three attacks (Google, M-PGD, DPatch), with the largest gap over DIFFender (e.g., 38.0% vs. 26.3% on M-PGD) and a consistent 4–6 mAP advantage over the closest competitor KDAT. On DETR, AntiStyler leads on the Google attack (41.7% vs. 37.8% KDAT) and DPatch (44.4% vs. 45.5% KDAT is a narrow loss), while KDAT edges ahead on M-PGD (37.9% vs. 39.6%—actually AntiStyler wins here too at 39.6%). The critical differentiator is speed: AntiStyler processes images in 85–93 ms, versus 4,072–4,192 ms for ObjectSeeker, 54,721–55,099 ms for PAD, and 7,603–7,606 ms for DIFFender, making it the only post-training defense (alongside NutNet at ~43 ms) viable for real-time OD at ≥10 FPS. NutNet is the closest speed competitor but trails AntiStyler by 5–10 mAP on adversarial performance for Faster R-CNN. KDAT matches or slightly exceeds AntiStyler on DETR benign mAP but requires costly per-dataset retraining, whereas AntiStyler is fully zero-shot and model-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

AntiStyler repurposes the Gatys et al. style transfer framework by inverting the style loss (maximizing rather than minimizing Gram-matrix MSE), grounded in the MMD/domain-adaptation interpretation from Li et al. that matching Gram matrices adapts one image's domain to another's. This places it in the purification-based defense lineage (DIFFender, NutNet) that exploits the distributional gap between adversarial and benign images, but departs from those methods by using style removal instead of autoencoder or diffusion reconstruction, thereby avoiding the object-position distortions that harm OD localization. The random-padding trick and the multi-stage spatial filtering pipeline (dilation–erosion–smoothing–thresholding) are novel engineering contributions that make the approach fully zero-shot and agnostic to model, patch, and attack type.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving 8–15 mAP% adversarial robustness at 80–90 ms per frame (10–12 FPS) while preserving benign performance, a combination that no prior post-training defense achieves simultaneously; most competitors either sacrifice benign accuracy or require 4000+ ms per image. The zero-shot, model-agnostic nature means it can be dropped in front of any OD architecture without retraining, lowering the adoption barrier considerably. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond patch attacks to full-image adversarial perturbations, how it behaves under partial-occlusion scenarios where the mask covers legitimate object regions, and whether the VGG-19 backbone's reliance on local texture features limits scalability to higher-resolution or video-based detection settings.
