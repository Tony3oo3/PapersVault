## Problem

Existing dynamic Gaussian Splatting methods from monocular videos either implicitly model deformation via canonical-to-observation space transformations or explicitly model only continuous position trajectories (e.g., cubic Hermite splines), but none guarantee continuous orientation deformation of dynamic Gaussians. This results in non-smooth pose variations and significant rendering artifacts, particularly in regions with complex motions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263713/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular video along with 2D tracklets from a pretrained tracking model (Bootstrap), per-frame depth maps for Gaussian initialization, and (potentially inaccurate) camera extrinsics. It produces a dynamic 3D Gaussian scene parameterized by learnable SE(3) B-spline motion bases that can render novel views at 480×360 resolution at approximately 45 FPS on a single RTX 4090 GPU, with the scene decomposed into static Gaussians (no deformation) and dynamic Gaussians (deformed via nearest motion bases).

## Method

The core contribution is an explicit continuous SE(3) B-spline motion base representation that simultaneously models position and orientation trajectories of dynamic Gaussians using SE(3) Cumulative B-splines with a compact set of learnable control points, initialized from 3D tracklet poses. An adaptive control mechanism periodically prunes redundant control points (when reconstruction error stays below a threshold) and densifies motion bases in complex-motion regions identified by rendering-error masks, balancing expressiveness against overfitting. Dynamic Gaussians are deformed by fusing relative pose transformations from K nearest motion bases via Dual Quaternion Blending (DQB), and a soft segment reconstruction strategy modulates Gaussian opacity by the temporal distance between reference and observation timestamps to suppress long-interval deformation interference. A multi-view diffusion model (Zero-1-to-3) provides scene-level multi-view cues through an SDS loss to regularize invisible regions, while a camera smoothness loss enforces temporal consistency of learnable camera extrinsics to compensate for inaccurate monocular video poses.

## Results

On the iPhone dataset, the method achieves mPSNR 20.17, mSSIM 0.729, and mLPIPS 0.274, outperforming the closest competitor MoSca (mPSNR 19.33, mSSIM 0.718) by 0.84 dB in mPSNR. On the NVIDIA dataset, it reaches PSNR 27.81, SSIM 0.871, and LPIPS 0.049, surpassing SplineGS (PSNR 27.12) by 0.69 dB while matching its SSIM (0.872). The method trains in 30 minutes on a single RTX 4090 and renders at 45.1 FPS at 480×360 resolution, with 134.7M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all six baselines on the iPhone dataset across mPSNR and mSSIM, with the largest margin over MoDec-GS (+5.52 dB mPSNR) and MarbleGS (+4.09 dB mPSNR). The closest competitor is MoSca, which the method surpasses by 0.84 dB in mPSNR and 0.011 in mSSIM while tying on mLPIPS (0.274). On the NVIDIA dataset, the method achieves the best PSNR (27.81) and LPIPS (0.049), edging out SplineGS by 0.69 dB in PSNR, though SplineGS retains a marginal SSIM advantage (0.872 vs. 0.871). SplineGS is notably faster at inference (387.5 FPS vs. 45.1 FPS) but requires 2 hours of training versus 30 minutes. The method also achieves the best correspondence tracking accuracy (PCK-T 0.833) on the iPhone dataset, surpassing MoSca (0.821) and HiMoR (0.809).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the explicit trajectory-based line of dynamic 3DGS (SplineGS, Shape-of-Motion) by unifying continuous position and orientation deformation into a single SE(3) Cumulative B-spline representation, whereas prior methods either model only position (SplineGS) or use discrete per-timestamp affine transforms (SoM, MoSca). It inherits the 3D tracklet initialization and DQB fusion strategy from MoSca, and the SE(3) motion bases concept from Shape-of-Motion, but replaces their discrete or position-only formulations with a mathematically continuous SE(3) B-spline. The adaptive control mechanism (pruning/densifying control points) and the soft segment reconstruction strategy are novel additions not present in any of the cited baselines, and the multi-view diffusion prior (Zero-1-to-3) is repurposed from object-centric generation to scene-level monocular NVS regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit continuous SE(3) B-spline formulation offers a clean, interpretable motion representation that naturally handles both translation and rotation, which is practically important for monocular dynamic NVS where orientation discontinuities cause visible artifacts. The demonstrated robustness to 2D tracking prior errors (Table 5) suggests the continuous representation acts as a built-in smoother, reducing sensitivity to noisy off-the-shelf trackers. However, the paper acknowledges failure on large non-rigid deformations and motion-blurred inputs, leaving open the question of whether B-spline-based rigid-body motion bases can be extended to capture truly non-rigid dynamics without losing the continuity guarantee. The method's 45 FPS rendering at 480×360 is competitive but not dramatically faster than SplineGS (387 FPS), so the efficiency gain over MoSca is modest.
