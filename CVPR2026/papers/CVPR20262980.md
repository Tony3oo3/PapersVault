## Problem

Skeleton sequences lack the contextual cues (objects, environments) that are visually present in video, creating an inherent semantic gap between motion representations and textual descriptions. This gap makes it difficult for existing zero-shot skeleton action recognition methods to distinguish visually similar actions such as 'typing on a keyboard' versus 'writing on paper,' since both produce nearly identical joint trajectories without the interacting object to disambiguate them.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262980/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes skeleton sequences of 25 joints sampled to 64 frames (from NTU RGB+D 60/120 or PKU-MMD) as primary input, along with LLM-generated structured contextual descriptions (10 per action class, specifying environment, used object, and target object) that serve as training supervision only. At inference, the model outputs an action class prediction (under ZSL or GZSL protocol), reconstructed contextual semantics inferred purely from motion, and a per-frame joint importance map highlighting motion-critical body parts.

## Method

SkeletonContext enriches the skeleton encoder itself with language-driven contextual prompts rather than enhancing the text side. A Cross-Modal Context Prompt Module uses BERT-base to reconstruct masked contextual slots (environment, used object, target) from a structured prompt, with a Differential Joint Encoder capturing inter-joint pose differences and a Progressive Partial Masking curriculum that gradually increases masking difficulty from partial to full context reconstruction. A Key-Part Decoupling Module predicts joint-importance maps calibrated by LLM-derived body-part priors to handle context-independent actions. Training jointly optimizes a contrastive alignment loss between context-augmented skeleton features and CLIP semantic embeddings, a masked-language-model reconstruction loss, and a KPD calibration loss, all using only seen-class data.

## Results

On the GZSL harmonic mean (H), SkeletonContext achieves 77.1% on NTU-60 (55/5), 61.1% on NTU-60 (48/12), 63.1% on NTU-120 (110/10), and 56.1% on NTU-120 (96/24), outperforming the strongest prior methods (Neuron, FS-VAE) on 3 of 4 splits. On ZSL accuracy, it reaches 89.6% on NTU-60 (55/5) and 64.4% on NTU-60 (48/12), surpassing all baselines by 2.7–7.2 points. Under random-split evaluation (Table 3), it attains 85.7% ZSL and 75.5% H on NTU-60, and 73.5% ZSL and 71.4% H on PKU-MMD, setting new state-of-the-art on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SkeletonContext outperforms the two strongest recent baselines (Neuron [6] and FS-VAE [40]) on GZSL harmonic mean for NTU-60 (55/5: 77.1 vs. 75.7/71.4) and NTU-60 (48/12: 61.1 vs. 59.1/52.1), with margins of 1.4–5.7 points. On NTU-120 (110/10) GZSL, it is essentially tied with FS-VAE and Neuron (63.1 vs. 63.3), while on NTU-120 (96/24) it leads by 1.4 points over FS-VAE (56.1 vs. 54.7). For ZSL, SkeletonContext achieves the best result on NTU-60 (55/5: 89.6 vs. 86.9 for Neuron/FS-VAE) and NTU-60 (48/12: 64.4 vs. 62.7 for Neuron), but trails TDSM (65.1) and FS-VAE (62.5) on NTU-120 (96/24) ZSL by 5.0 and 2.4 points respectively. On PKU-MMD, it substantially outperforms SCoPLe [50] (71.4% H vs. 54.9%) and Neuron [6] (71.4% vs. 69.2%), confirming strong cross-dataset generalization. Overall, the method wins or ties on 7 of 8 primary evaluation splits, with the only clear losses on the two NTU-120 ZSL splits where TDSM and FS-VAE retain a small edge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant ZSSAR paradigm of aligning skeleton and text embeddings in a shared latent space (SynSE, SCoPLe, Neuron) by instead enriching the skeleton encoder itself with language-driven contextual semantics. It draws on cross-modal masked reconstruction (VL-BEiT, OSCAR) but repurposes it for a setting where the 'missing' information (objects, environments) is inherently absent from the input modality rather than merely occluded. The framework builds directly on Neuron's action-aware semantic embeddings and GZSL calibration scheme, while introducing a Differential Joint Encoder and Progressive Partial Masking strategy that have no direct counterpart in prior prompt-based ZSSAR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental representational gap in skeleton-based recognition: the absence of interaction objects and environmental context that are trivially visible in video but entirely missing from joint coordinates. By demonstrating that a frozen language model can reconstruct plausible contextual semantics from motion alone, it opens a path toward context-aware zero-shot recognition without requiring visual input. Limitations include reliance on LLM-generated descriptions (quality and hallucination risk), a fixed structured prompt format, and evaluation limited to three curated benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to few-shot or open-vocabulary settings, and whether the structured 'environment + object + target' template generalizes to actions with no clear object interaction.
