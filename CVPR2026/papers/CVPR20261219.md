## Problem

Existing reasoning segmentation benchmarks and models are built on ground-view or nadir-view imagery with moderate resolution, limited scale variation, and sizable objects—assumptions that break down for UAV imagery characterized by oblique viewpoints, extreme scale variation with dense small objects (58% of instances occupy <2% of image area), and ultra-high-resolution scenes. No dedicated benchmark or baseline exists for instruction-driven pixel-level segmentation under these aerial conditions, leaving the community unable to systematically measure progress in UAV reasoning segmentation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a high-resolution UAV image (captured at 30 m, 60 m, or 100 m altitude under varied illumination) and a free-form, category-agnostic natural language instruction as input. Unlike standard referring segmentation, instructions describe relational, contextual, or intent-driven semantics (e.g., 'the safest open area for landing', 'the vehicle behind the fire truck') rather than naming target categories, requiring the model to infer what to segment from scene context. The output is a single binary pixel-level mask (H×W) for the region satisfying the instruction, evaluated with gIoU and cIoU.

## Method

PixDLM employs a Dual-Path Vision Encoder that separately extracts global semantics via CLIP (Global-Scope Path) and high-resolution structural details via SAM (Fine-Scope Path), then fuses them through MultiPath Alignment: fine-scope features are injected into three intermediate CLIP stages via channel projection, spatial resampling, and gated residual fusion, followed by an explicit output-level summation. A LLaMA-2-13B backbone processes text tokens, the fused visual tokens, and a learnable Mask Token, whose hidden state encodes integrated multimodal reasoning. A three-layer Hierarchical Reasoning Decoder progressively refines the mask by modulating multi-scale SAM features with higher-level predicted masks through sigmoid gating, then fusing all intermediate masks with learnable layer-wise coefficients. The model is trained with supervised fine-tuning on DRSeg using a combined cross-entropy, binary cross-entropy, and Dice loss, with Chain-of-Thought reasoning supervision providing the largest performance gain over mask-only training.

## Results

On the DRSeg benchmark with supervised fine-tuning, PixDLM achieves 62.80% gIoU / 62.84% cIoU on Attribute Reasoning, 61.75% / 64.03% on Scene Reasoning, and 62.51% / 62.80% on Spatial Reasoning, outperforming all baselines across all three dimensions. In the zero-shot setting, the strongest existing model (LISA-13B-llama2-v1) reaches only 52.65% gIoU on Attribute Reasoning, highlighting the difficulty of UAV reasoning segmentation for current multimodal models. On standard referring segmentation benchmarks (RefCOCOs, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg), PixDLM also demonstrates competitive performance over LISA-7B and PixelLM, confirming cross-domain generalization of the Dual-Path architecture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the SFT setting on DRSeg, PixDLM consistently outperforms both LISA-7B-v1 and PixelLM-7B across all three reasoning dimensions. Compared to LISA-7B-v1, PixDLM gains +3.58 gIoU on Attribute (62.80 vs. 59.22), +7.30 on Scene (61.75 vs. 54.45), and +5.18 on Spatial (62.51 vs. 57.33). Against PixelLM-7B, the margins are +4.41 gIoU on Attribute, +6.56 on Scene, and +7.13 on Spatial, with the largest advantage on Scene-level reasoning where PixDLM's global semantic pathway provides a clear benefit. In the zero-shot setting, no UAV-specific model is competitive: LISA-13B-llama2-v1 leads at 52.65% gIoU (Attribute) while SegEarth-R1, a geospatial reasoning model, drops to 20.58%, indicating that neither generic MLLMs nor remote-sensing models transfer well to oblique UAV viewpoints. The paper also reports that PixDLM achieves strong results on RefCOCOs, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg (details in supplementary), demonstrating that the Dual-Path design generalizes beyond UAV imagery to standard referring segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixDLM extends the reasoning segmentation paradigm established by LISA (Mask Token + LLM for instruction-conditioned pixel prediction) and PixelLM (integrated pixel decoder without external segmentation modules) to the UAV domain, where oblique viewpoints, extreme scale variation, and ultra-high resolution break the assumptions of ground-view models. Architecturally, it follows the mixture-of-resolution philosophy of LLaVA-HR by injecting high-resolution structural features (from SAM) into a low-resolution semantic pathway (CLIP) through a MultiPath Alignment module, rather than processing full-resolution imagery directly. The key departure is the formalization of three reasoning dimensions (spatial, attribute, scene-level) specific to aerial scenes, and the use of GPT-5-guided Chain-of-Thought annotation to create reasoning-aware supervision that prior UAV datasets lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DRSeg fills a clear gap: no prior benchmark systematically evaluates instruction-driven segmentation under UAV-specific visual statistics (oblique views, 58% small-object instances, multi-altitude capture). The zero-shot results confirm that existing reasoning segmentation models (LISA, PixelLM) do not transfer to aerial imagery, validating the need for domain-specific evaluation. Limitations include single-instance-per-image annotation (limiting multi-object reasoning assessment), reliance on GPT-5 for CoT generation (introducing potential annotation bias), and evaluation on only one primary benchmark. Open questions include whether the dual-path design scales to multi-object UAV scenes, whether the CoT supervision generalizes beyond the GPT-5 annotation style, and whether real-time inference is feasible for on-board drone deployment.
