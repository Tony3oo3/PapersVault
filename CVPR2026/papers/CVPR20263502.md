## Problem

Existing single-view compositional 3D scene generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: feed-forward approaches are efficient but generalize poorly to complex scenes due to limited training data, while per-instance divide-and-conquer methods improve generalization but rely on error-prone and time-consuming pose optimization to align generated assets with the scene. The paper asks whether layout priors from geometry estimation can be exploited directly without explicit alignment, by completing each instance in-place from its visible geometry.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263502/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and internally extracts per-instance masks (via SAM 2/Grounded SAM) and fragmented point clouds (via MoGe v2 or VGGT) as geometric conditions. The fragmented point cloud serves as a spatial anchor that preserves the original object location, eliminating the need for separate pose estimation or registration. Output is a complete 3D scene composed of textured meshes with accurate 6DoF poses, generated in approximately 30 seconds on an RTX 5090 GPU.

## Method

3D-Fixer adapts the TRELLIS object-generation prior (SLAT representation, flow-matching DiT) into a dual-stream architecture where a trainable scene-conditioning branch (encoding fragmented point clouds via depth-ratio-embedded self-attention and 2D features via Geometry-Aware Feature Projection) interacts with a frozen object-generation branch. A coarse-to-fine scheme decouples boundary prediction from detail generation: the model first hallucinates topology within a 4× expanded AABB to resolve occlusion-induced boundary ambiguity, then refines high-fidelity geometry within the predicted tight boundary. Training is stabilized by an Occlusion-Robust Feature Alignment (ORFA) loss that distills layer-wise representations from a frozen clean-input TRELLIS teacher, mitigating the one-to-many mapping ambiguity caused by occlusion. The framework is trained on ARSG-110K, a procedurally generated dataset of 110K+ scenes with 3M+ annotated views and object-level 3D ground truth.

## Results

On the MIDI testset (3D-FRONT), 3D-Fixer achieves a scene-level Chamfer Distance of 0.069 and F-Score of 78.67, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline MIDI (CD_S=0.080, FS_S=50.19) and Gen3DSR (CD_S=0.123, FS_S=40.07). At the object level, 3D-Fixer reaches CD_O=0.032 and FS_O=94.39, nearly doubling MIDI's object F-Score (53.58). Inference time is 30 seconds per scene on an RTX 5090, faster than MIDI (40s) and dramatically faster than Gen3DSR (9 min). On the ScanNet real-world subset, 3D-Fixer achieves CD_S=0.130 and FS_S=61.58, outperforming Gen3DSR (0.167/50.19) and MIDI (0.163/37.10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-Fixer decisively outperforms the strongest feed-forward diffusion baseline MIDI on all geometric metrics: scene-level CD drops from 0.080 to 0.069, scene-level F-Score jumps from 50.19 to 78.67, and object-level F-Score nearly doubles from 53.58 to 94.39. The only metric where MIDI leads is bounding-box IoU (0.518 vs. 0.492), suggesting 3D-Fixer's generated assets are slightly smaller in extent but far more geometrically accurate. Against the per-instance optimization method Gen3DSR, 3D-Fixer wins on every metric while being 18× faster (30s vs. 9 min), confirming that in-place completion avoids the costly and error-prone pose alignment step. Compared to retrieval-based DiffCAD and per-instance optimization REPARO, 3D-Fixer achieves lower CD and higher F-Scores across both scene and object levels. On the real-world ScanNet subset, 3D-Fixer (CD_S=0.130, FS_S=61.58) surpasses both Gen3DSR (0.167/50.19) and MIDI (0.163/37.10), demonstrating that the in-place completion paradigm generalizes to uncontrolled environments where feed-forward methods degrade sharply.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both dominant paradigms in single-view compositional 3D scene generation: feed-forward methods (MIDI, Gen3DSR) that predict all assets simultaneously but generalize poorly, and divide-and-conquer methods (Gen3DSR, REPARO, DiffCAD) that generate or retrieve individual objects then align them via optimization. The key conceptual departure is the 'in-place completion' paradigm, which repurposes fragmented geometry from estimation models (MoGe v2, VGGT) not as an alignment target but as a spatial anchor and partial condition, letting a pre-trained object generative prior (TRELLIS/SLAT) complete unseen parts at the original location. Technically, it builds on ControlNet-style dual-branch conditioning, flow-matching diffusion, and knowledge distillation (ORFA), but the combination with geometry-estimation priors as in-place spatial constraints is the novel architectural contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The in-place completion paradigm eliminates the error-prone and time-consuming pose alignment step that plagues divide-and-conquer methods, while retaining the generalization of pre-trained object priors — a practical win for real-world deployment. ARSG-110K (110K scenes, 3M views, 180K+ assets) addresses a critical data bottleneck and is positioned as the largest open-source benchmark for this task, which should accelerate future research. Open questions include scalability to very large outdoor scenes with dozens of interacting objects, the sensitivity to geometry-estimation errors in highly occluded or reflective environments, and whether the coarse-to-fine boundary strategy generalizes to non-rigid or deformable assets.
