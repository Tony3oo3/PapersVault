## Problem

Existing 3D head avatar methods either couple face and hair into a single representation—neglecting the strand-level geometric structure that defines realistic hair—or require dense multi-view studio captures and lengthy per-identity optimization, making them impractical for casual mobile photography. No prior feed-forward approach simultaneously handles arbitrary input counts, explicitly disentangles face from hair at the Gaussian level, and completes reconstruction in minutes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262633/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts an arbitrary, unordered set of 1–6+ RGB head images (512×512) together with a canonical FLAME template mesh, with no fixed camera configuration required. It outputs a composable 3D Gaussian head avatar in which face Gaussians are planar primitives bound one-per-pixel to FLAME UV-space triangles, and hair Gaussians are connected strand segments rooted at scalp vertices; the two sets can be independently manipulated for hairstyle transfer or texture editing. Animation is driven by FLAME blendshapes at up to ~250 FPS via a decoupled coordinate-update pipeline.

## Method

FHAvatar uses a three-stage encoder–decoder pipeline. A feature tokenizer produces three token streams: image tokens from a frozen DINOv2 backbone refined by a trainable DPTHead, head geometric tokens from positional-encoded FLAME UV coordinates passed through an MLP, and hair tokens obtained by cross-attending DiffLocks monocular hair features against the image tokens plus scalp-region geometric tokens. An aggregated transformer backbone (4 layers, 1024-dim) then fuses these via cross-attention from head/hair queries to image keys, with frame-wise self-attention on image tokens (VGGT-style) and half of the self-attention layers shared between the head and hair branches to promote structural coherence. The dual-branch decoder independently predicts face Gaussians (one planar Gaussian per UV pixel with offset, covariance, rotation, opacity, color) and hair Gaussians (256 direction vectors per scalp pixel decoded by frozen modulated-SIREN layers from DiffLocks, accumulated into connected strand segments with adaptive density-based downsampling). Training is supervised on NeRSemble with randomly sampled 1–6 input frames, using a hair-region separation loss, photometric loss (L1+SSIM+LPIPS), and thresholded L2 regularization on Gaussian offsets and scales; an optional 100-epoch refinement fine-tunes only the decoders and aggregated tokens for subject-specific detail.

## Results

On NeRSemble, FHAvatar (full refinement) achieves the best reconstruction quality across all input settings: PSNR 22.80 (1 frame), 23.40 (3 frames), 23.71 (6 frames), and 24.22 (16 frames), with SSIM up to 0.837 and LPIPS as low as 0.271. The method also excels at expression reenactment, achieving AKD as low as 2.72 (16 frames) and 3.66 (1 frame), far below the next-best GaussianAvatars (5.54 at 16 frames). Modeling time is 49–130 s (vs. 1–5 h for optimization-based baselines), and the output avatars render at ~250 FPS in real time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FHAvatar outperforms all six baselines on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, AKD, and CSIM across every input setting. The closest competitor is GaussianAvatars [50] (optimization-based), which reaches PSNR 23.44 at 6 frames and 23.50 at 16 frames versus FHAvatar's 23.71 and 24.22; however, GaussianAvatars requires ~1.5–4.2 h of per-identity optimization and renders at only ~33–48 FPS. Among feed-forward methods, LAM [22] and GAGAvatar [10] are faster (sub-second modeling) but lag significantly in reconstruction quality (PSNR 16.41 and 14.19 at 1 frame) and expression control (AKD 48.64 and 84.10). DiffusionRig [15] produces the worst SSIM values (0.25–0.28) and highest AKD (~56), reflecting its 2D-diffusion-based approach's inability to maintain 3D consistency. FlashAvatar [70] and MeGA [63] (both optimization-based) fail to form complete 3D heads under sparse inputs, with FlashAvatar's CSIM dropping to 0.055 at 16 frames. FHAvatar's single-pass mode (3.13 s, no refinement) already surpasses all baselines, and the full pipeline adds only ~46 s while improving PSNR by ~0.3 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

FHAvatar sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) 3DGS-based head avatars (GaussianAvatars, FlashAvatar, MeGA), from which it departs by explicitly decoupling face and hair into separate Gaussian sets rather than treating hair as a scalp deformation; (2) feed-forward reconstruction models (LAM, GAGAvatar, Avat3r), which it generalizes from fixed or single-view inputs to an arbitrary, unordered number of casual captures via an aggregated transformer backbone inspired by VGGT; and (3) strand-level hair modeling (DiffLocks, Perm), from which it borrows the frozen SIREN-based strand generator and monocular hair prior but embeds them within a multi-view, UV-space pipeline. The key architectural departure is the dual-branch decoder that predicts planar Gaussians for the face and connected strand Gaussians for hair in a shared FLAME UV space, unifying both under a single transformer backbone with shared self-attention layers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reducing 3D head avatar creation from hours of per-identity optimization to a minute-level feed-forward pass on a consumer GPU, FHAvatar makes high-fidelity, animatable avatars accessible from casual phone captures—a significant practical step toward consumer-level digital avatar tools. The composable face-hair design opens a straightforward path to hairstyle transfer and texture-based stylized editing without re-optimization, capabilities that were previously tightly coupled to per-subject training. Open questions include whether the strand-based hair representation generalizes to extreme styles (very long, braided, or voluminous hair) beyond the NeRSemble distribution, how the method extends to full-body avatars, and whether the optional refinement stage can be further compressed or eliminated for truly one-shot deployment.
