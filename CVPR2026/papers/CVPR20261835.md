## Problem

End-to-end deep learning video stabilizers require large paired stable/unstable datasets, offer limited interpretability and controllability, and are typically offline, making them impractical for real-time deployment on resource-constrained hardware. Existing benchmarks also focus almost exclusively on handheld visible-light footage, leaving domains such as UAV nighttime infrared remote sensing unaddressed. This paper tackles all three gaps with an unsupervised, strictly causal (no look-ahead) stabilization pipeline that integrates classical geometric priors with lightweight neural modules.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only consecutive frame pairs (I_{t-1}, I_t) in a strictly causal manner—no future frames are ever accessed—operating on 720p or 1080p video at 25–30 FPS in both visible and infrared modalities. Internally it produces a regular grid motion field Δg_t (per-vertex displacements) and a compensation displacement field M_t, which are bilinearly interpolated to pixel level for backward-warping. The final output is a stabilized frame rendered with ProPainter outpainting at boundaries to minimize black borders, delivered through a three-thread asynchronous pipeline that decouples estimation, propagation, and compensation for low-latency streaming.

## Method

The pipeline has three causal stages. (1) Motion Estimation fuses keypoints from heterogeneous detectors (traditional SIFT/ORB and deep detectors) via weighted NMS, enforces spatial uniformity with Spatially Selective Clustering, and estimates a causal dense backward flow with MemFlow that is reweighted by a keypoint-neighborhood mask to suppress dynamic-object sensitivity. (2) Motion Propagation (EfficientMotionPro) anchors displacements to a regular grid using a multi-homography prior (K-means clustering of keypoint displacements followed by per-cluster RANSAC homographies with soft fusion), then a lightweight GhostNet+ECA backbone with causal convolutions predicts only the non-rigid/parallax residuals; training is fully self-supervised via a confidence-weighted Charbonnier keypoint-consistency loss. (3) Motion Compensation (OnlineSmoother) applies a learnable 3-tap causal kernel (effective window L=7) to smooth grid trajectories, regularized by a time-adaptive second-order penalty and a DFT-based frequency prior that suppresses high-frequency oscillations within each causal window; the resulting displacement field drives backward grid warping. The entire system is trained without any paired stable/unstable data and runs online with a multi-threaded buffering architecture.

## Results

On the NUS benchmark, the proposed method achieves the best online performance with Cropping Ratio C=0.95, Distortion Value D=0.98, and Stability Score S=0.90, matching the geometric fidelity of NNDVS while surpassing it in stability and cropping. On the new UAV-Test dataset (visible and infrared aerial videos), it sets a new online state of the art with C=0.94, D=0.90, S=0.89, outperforming NNDVS (C=0.89, D=0.87, S=0.84) and Liu et al. (C=0.82, D=0.89, S=0.85). On GyRo, it reaches C=0.99, D=0.96, S=0.93, competitive with top offline methods like Gavs (C=1.00, D=0.99, S=0.93) while operating strictly causally without future frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among online methods, the proposed approach consistently outperforms NNDVS [53] on Cropping Ratio and Stability Score across all five datasets (e.g., NUS: C 0.95 vs 0.92, S 0.90 vs 0.87; UAV-Test: C 0.94 vs 0.89, S 0.89 vs 0.84), while matching its Distortion Value on NUS (both 0.98) and DeepStab (both 0.91). It also surpasses Liu et al. [26] on all three metrics for NUS, Selfie, and UAV-Test, and on D and S for GyRo (D 0.96 vs 0.94, S 0.93 vs 0.89). Against the strongest offline methods, the gap narrows substantially: on GyRo, the method achieves S=0.93 matching Gavs [49] and D=0.96 vs Gavs' 0.99, while on NUS it reaches D=0.98 vs RStab's 0.99. The method's only consistent loss is in Cropping Ratio versus offline methods that can use full-frame inpainting (e.g., RStab and Gavs achieve C=1.00 on most datasets), reflecting the inherent trade-off of causal, online processing. On the new UAV-Test dataset, the advantage over all online baselines is most pronounced, with 5–12 point gains in C over NNDVS and Liu et al., demonstrating stronger generalization to multimodal aerial scenarios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper re-instantiates the classical three-stage stabilization pipeline (motion estimation → smoothing → compensation) with targeted neural augmentations, departing from both purely geometric methods (Grundmann, Bundle) and end-to-end deep learning frameworks (StabNet, PW-StableNet, DUT, NNDVS) that require paired stable/unstable training data. It builds directly on MemFlow for causal dense optical flow and on lightweight CNN backbones (GhostNet + ECA) for residual prediction, while introducing multi-detector keypoint collaboration with spatially selective clustering and a multi-homography prior to anchor grid motion. The key conceptual departure from prior online methods (MeshFlow, NNDVS) is the fully unsupervised training objective combined with a learnable causal smoothing kernel, eliminating the need for ground-truth trajectories or paired datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the dependency on paired stable/unstable video data and future-frame access, this framework lowers the practical barrier to deploying stabilization on resource-constrained or safety-critical platforms (UAVs, guided munitions) where labeled data is unavailable and latency is critical. The UAV-Test dataset fills a notable gap in benchmarks by covering aerial, multi-modal (visible + infrared), and diverse-terrain scenarios absent from existing handheld-centric benchmarks. Open questions include whether the 2D grid-warping formulation can extend to 3D parallax-heavy scenes, whether the multi-detector collaboration strategy generalizes to extreme low-texture or high-occlusion conditions beyond the tested UAV scenarios, and whether the lightweight residual network can be further compressed for embedded deployment without quality loss.
