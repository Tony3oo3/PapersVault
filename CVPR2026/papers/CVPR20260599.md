## Problem

Panoptic occupancy prediction requires jointly inferring voxel-wise semantics and instance identities in 3D, but existing benchmarks lack instance-level annotations, provide only low and fixed voxel resolutions, and suffer from incomplete geometry due to LiDAR-based ground truth pipelines that cannot recover occluded or unobserved structures. Additionally, no unified, semantically structured 3D mesh library tailored for autonomous driving exists to support high-fidelity, physically consistent dataset construction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260599/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes six surround-view RGB/depth/semantic images at 1408×376 pixels, a 32-channel LiDAR point cloud (80 m range, 250K points/s), and sensor poses, all synchronized at 20 Hz. Outputs are panoptic occupancy grids at voxel resolutions as fine as 0.05 m (four times finer than SemanticKITTI) over a 76.8×51.2×13 m volume, with each occupied voxel carrying both a semantic label (30 categories) and an instance ID. The dataset also provides rectified depth maps, semantic maps, surface normal maps, and per-instance metadata including 3D bounding boxes, velocities, and pedestrian gait states.

## Method

The paper introduces ADMesh, a unified 3D mesh library of 15K+ models aggregated from CARLA, BuildingNet, MeshFleet, and ShapeNetCore under a 30-category semantic taxonomy, built via an automated export toolchain that resolves component hierarchies, attachment topologies, and runtime semantic labels. CarlaOcc is then generated by reconstructing each simulation frame directly from these meshes (rather than LiDAR scans) using a topology-aware mesh permutation strategy that sorts instances by world-space elevation and integrates them bottom-up to produce non-overlapping, physically consistent panoptic occupancy labels. A Skeletal Motion Analyzer handles non-rigid pedestrians by matching their runtime gait descriptors to a canonical walking cycle via geodesic distance, and an instance-guided ray-casting pipeline corrects transparency-induced depth and semantic artifacts in the rendered sensor data. Two novel quality metrics—Spatial Continuity Score (fraction of non-isolated occupied voxels per class) and Temporal Consistency Score (inter-frame semantic IoU under ego-motion warping)—are proposed to quantitatively assess dataset coherence, and representative panoptic and semantic occupancy models are benchmarked under a unified 0.2 m voxel protocol.

## Results

On the CarlaOcc benchmark, Panoptic-FlashOcc achieves the best panoptic occupancy prediction with PQ 13.5, SQ 49.1, RQ 27.5, and mIoU 18.4, outperforming PanoOcc (PQ 11.9) and SparseOcc (PQ 10.3). For semantic occupancy prediction, surround-view methods lead with GaussianFormer-2 reaching IoU 38.3 and mIoU 20.7, while the best single-view method CGFormer achieves IoU 35.2 and mIoU 17.8. In dataset quality evaluation, CarlaOcc attains a Spatial Continuity Score of 0.996 and Temporal Consistency Score of 0.873, surpassing the next-best dataset CarlaSC (0.887 / 0.775) by approximately 12% and 13% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In panoptic occupancy prediction, Panoptic-FlashOcc [46] leads all baselines, exceeding PanoOcc [41] by 1.6 PQ points and 1.1 mIoU points, while SparseOcc [28] trails by 3.2 PQ due to weaker instance discrimination on small or occluded objects. For surround-view semantic occupancy, GaussianFormer-2 [20] achieves the highest mIoU (20.7), narrowly beating OPUS [39] (19.3) and GaussianFormer [19] (19.2). Among single-view methods, CGFormer [45] leads with mIoU 17.8, outperforming Symphonies [21] (15.9), COTR [29] (15.4), OccFormer [48] (14.4), and VoxFormer [25] (13.9). In dataset quality metrics, CarlaOcc dominates all existing benchmarks: it improves spatial continuity over CarlaSC [43] from 0.887 to 0.996 and temporal consistency from 0.775 to 0.873, while real-world datasets like SemanticKITTI [1] (s_sc 0.353, s_tc 0.023) and SurroundOcc [42] (s_sc 0.878, s_tc 0.589) show substantially lower coherence due to LiDAR sparsity and surface-reconstruction artifacts. The authors note that overall method performance on CarlaOcc is slightly lower than reported results on prior benchmarks (e.g., SSCBench, SemanticKITTI), attributed to the higher-fidelity ground truth with precise geometry and faithful occlusions that increases learning difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant LiDAR-voxelization pipeline used by prior occupancy datasets (SemanticKITTI, Occ3D, SurroundOcc) by generating ground truth directly from 3D meshes, yielding physically complete geometry rather than sensor-visible surfaces only. It builds on the CARLA simulator and Unreal Engine 5 for scene generation, but introduces a novel Mesh Exportation Toolchain and a unified taxonomy (ADMesh) that consolidates assets from CARLA, BuildingNet, MeshFleet, and ShapeNetCore into a single driving-oriented library. The panoptic occupancy prediction task itself was recently formalized by SparseOcc, PanoOcc, and Panoptic-FlashOcc, but those works relied on pseudo-instance labels derived from bounding boxes or clustering; this paper provides the first dataset with true instance-level occupancy annotations, closing that gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The absence of instance-level annotations and physically consistent geometry has been the primary bottleneck preventing meaningful evaluation of panoptic occupancy prediction; CarlaOcc addresses both by offering 100K+ frames at 0.05 m resolution with genuine instance IDs, enabling the field to move beyond heuristic pseudo-labels. The proposed spatial and temporal continuity metrics give the community a standardized way to quantify dataset quality, which was previously ad hoc. Key open questions include whether models trained on this synthetic benchmark transfer to real-world driving, how the 4× finer resolution changes architectural design choices, and whether the very low PQ scores (≤13.5) reflect a fundamental difficulty in instance discrimination or simply insufficient model capacity for the task.
