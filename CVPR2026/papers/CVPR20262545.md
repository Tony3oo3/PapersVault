## Problem

Existing RAG chunkers for long, multi-page industrial documents rely on text-centric or autoregressive hierarchy generation, which misses cross-page parent-child relations, figure/table-caption bindings, and visual boundary cues. This produces fragmented or redundant chunks whose boundaries do not follow the document's true structure, degrading both retrieval precision and downstream QA accuracy. The paper tackles the gap of recovering a globally consistent, multi-modal document dependency tree before constructing retrieval units.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes multi-page document images (scanned pages or rendered PDFs) and first converts them via a frozen DP+OCR layer (SharedDet) into a unified set of global document blocks, each carrying a normalized bounding box, layout type, OCR text, and page index. The final output is a single-rooted, acyclic document dependency tree over all blocks, from which deterministic structure-aware chunks are emitted; each chunk is annotated with a root-to-section path, a page span, and its constituent block list, making chunks distinguishable in a corpus-level retrieval index.

## Method

M3DocDEP is a four-stage parse-then-chunk pipeline. A frozen LVLM (Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA-OneVision, or InternVL-3.5) produces page-level multi-modal tokens, which a SoftROI embedder pools into per-block embeddings using boundary-aware weights proportional to (u(1−u))^α(v(1−v))^α over the token grid. A biaffine scoring head, augmented with pairwise geometric features (relative offsets, size ratios, page distance), assigns scores to candidate parent-child edges filtered by header-centric priors and a top-k vertical-distance constraint. An MST-based decoder then selects the highest-scoring globally compatible tree satisfying single-root, single-parent, and acyclicity constraints, replacing fragile autoregressive hierarchy generation. Finally, a deterministic section-root DFS over the decoded tree emits chunks that merge cross-page descendants, force figure/table-caption pairs into the same chunk, and attach section-path and page-span metadata for retrieval-time disambiguation.

## Results

On DHP benchmarks (shared GT layout blocks), M3DOCDEP achieves 82.87/76.52 F1/STEDS on HRDS, 77.75/71.65 on HRDH, and 76.01/70.83 on DocHieNet, outperforming the strongest tree-aware baseline DSHP-LLM (MultiDocFusion) by +31.97/+47.00 STEDS on HRDS and +11.72/+17.34 on DocHieNet. On corpus-level retrieval, M3DOCDEP attains the best nDCG across all four VQA corpora (27.81 on DUDE, 24.52 on MP-DocVQA, 89.12 on CUAD, 75.54 on MOAMOB), improving over MultiDocFusion by +2.76 to +10.00 nDCG points. Downstream QA ANLS reaches 21.43 (DUDE), 18.17 (MP-DocVQA), 29.25 (CUAD), and 27.14 (MOAMOB), a +2.84 to +4.18 gain over the next-best chunker.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M3DOCDEP dominates all 12 baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the closest tree-aware competitor DSHP-LLM/MultiDocFusion, it gains +31.97 STEDS on HRDS (76.52 vs. 29.52), +20.31 on HRDH (71.65 vs. 51.34), and +17.34 on DocHieNet (70.83 vs. 53.49), with F1 gaps of +37.97, +16.46, and +11.72 respectively. General-purpose LVLMs (GPT-5, LLaVA-OneVision-1.5, InternVL-3.5, Qwen2.5-VL) score 22–35 F1 and 8–26 STEDS, confirming that zero-shot or SFT-based sequence generation cannot recover global document trees. Among prior DHP methods, DSPS leads on HRDS (59.57 STEDS) but collapses on DocHieNet (23.81), while DocParser and DSG remain below 33 STEDS everywhere; M3DOCDEP exceeds all of them by 17–72 STEDS points. On retrieval and QA, M3DOCDEP beats MultiDocFusion by +2.76 to +10.00 nDCG and +2.84 to +4.18 ANLS across DUDE, MP-DocVQA, CUAD, and MOAMOB, with the largest margins on the noisier, more cross-page-heavy corpora (DUDE, MOAMOB). A no-metadata fairness control (removing section-path and page-range fields from both methods) still leaves M3DOCDEP +2.3% nDCG ahead, confirming the gains come from boundary quality rather than metadata alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

M3DocDEP repurposes the biaffine dependency-scoring formulation from NLP (Dozat & Manning, 2017) to score parent-child edges between document blocks, and adapts RoIAlign-style continuous sampling (Mask R-CNN, 2017) into a boundary-aware SoftROI pooler over LVLM token grids. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant SFT-based LVLM paradigm for Document Hierarchical Parsing—which autoregressively generates a tree as a token sequence—by replacing sequence generation with edge-wise scoring followed by MST-constrained graph decoding, thereby enforcing single-root, single-parent, and acyclicity constraints by construction. The pipeline also extends the authors' own prior MultiDocFusion work by substituting LLM-inferred hierarchies with a learned dependency head over frozen multi-modal block embeddings, and by introducing a shared-block evaluation protocol that isolates chunk quality from parsing differences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, modular recipe (frozen LVLM → SoftROI embeddings → biaffine scorer → MST decoder → deterministic chunking) that practitioners can adopt without fine-tuning a large model end-to-end, and the shared-protocol evaluation design makes cross-method comparisons more trustworthy than in prior DHP/chunking literature. The no-metadata fairness control (retaining a 2.3% nDCG edge after stripping section-path and page-range fields) strengthens the claim that gains come from boundary quality rather than metadata. Open questions include whether the frozen-LVLM + small-head design scales to very long documents (hundreds of pages), whether joint training of the LVLM and dependency head closes the remaining gap to oracle layouts, and how the pipeline behaves under severe OCR degradation or non-Latin scripts where the current SharedDet layer may be weakest.
