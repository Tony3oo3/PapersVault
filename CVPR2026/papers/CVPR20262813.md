## Problem

Cryo-ET tomograms are degraded by both extremely low SNR (from dose limits) and missing-wedge artifacts (from restricted tilt angles), yet existing deep learning methods address these two problems in isolation and rely on self-supervised learning because clean ground truth is experimentally unobtainable. This isolated, unsupervised paradigm prevents the model from disentangling structured missing-wedge artifacts from true biological signal, limiting restoration quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262813/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy cryo-ET tomogram reconstructed via Weighted Back Projection from a limited tilt series (typically ±60°, 2–3° increments) as input, with no preprocessing or deconvolution required. It outputs a restored 3D volume with both noise suppressed and missing-wedge information filled in. The U-Net operates on 96³ patches with 32-pixel overlap, and the model trained entirely on synthetic data is applied directly to real tomograms without fine-tuning.

## Method

cryoDeRec is a fully supervised multi-task framework built on a U-Net (Noise2Noise architecture) trained with a dual-objective loss: L_fidelity (L2 between network output on the noisy tomogram and a full-angle 90° clean reconstruction) and L_recon (L2 between network output on a randomly rotated, Fourier-masked noisy tomogram and the correspondingly rotated clean target). The key enabling component is a generative physical simulation pipeline that produces high-fidelity training pairs: a WGAN-GP noise synthesizer (trained on noise maps extracted from motion-corrected tilt series) generates realistic projection noise, structural densities from EMDB are distributed via SAWLC statistics, and CTF modulation is applied before WBP reconstruction. At test time, the model fills the unmeasured Fourier wedge using structural priors learned from the full-angle training targets rather than direct observation. The simulation-to-real generalization is validated by training exclusively on synthetic data and evaluating directly on four EMPIAR datasets and two simulated datasets, consistently outperforming Topaz-Denoise, SC-Net, and IsoNet.

## Results

On four realistic cryo-ET datasets, cryoDeRec achieves the highest CNR and ENL across all benchmarks. For example, on EMPIAR-10643 (HIV-1), it reaches CNR 2.434 / ENL 193.694, compared to IsoNet (1.874/152.665), SC-Net (1.249/51.984), and Topaz-Denoise (0.458/143.658). On the simulated datasets (Tomo-5lzf, Tomo-1qvr), cryoDeRec attains PSNR ≈ 10.8 dB and SSIM ≈ 0.84–0.85, substantially outperforming IsoNet (PSNR ≈ 7.1–7.6, SSIM ≈ 0.38–0.55) and Topaz-Denoise (PSNR ≈ 6.2–7.0, SSIM ≈ 0.36–0.46) across both SNR=0.1 and SNR=0.5 settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

cryoDeRec outperforms all three baselines on every dataset and every metric. Against Topaz-Denoise [2] (a general-purpose denoiser), cryoDeRec gains roughly 2–4× in CNR (e.g., 2.434 vs. 0.458 on EMPIAR-10643) and 1.3–6.8× in ENL, reflecting its ability to recover missing-wedge information that a denoising-only model cannot address. Against SC-Net [45] (self-supervised denoising with sparsity), cryoDeRec leads by a wide margin on ENL (193.69 vs. 51.98 on EMPIAR-10643) and by ~1.9× on CNR, confirming that supervised multi-task training yields substantially better structural recovery. Against IsoNet [23] (self-supervised missing-wedge reconstruction), cryoDeRec still wins on all four realistic datasets in both CNR and ENL (e.g., 0.506 vs. 0.361 CNR on EMPIAR-10045; 1.637 vs. 0.336 on EMPIAR-10499), with the largest gap on the low-contrast EMPIAR-10499 dataset where IsoNet produces blurry reconstructions. On the simulated datasets, cryoDeRec's PSNR advantage over IsoNet is approximately 3.2–3.7 dB and over Topaz-Denoise approximately 3.8–4.2 dB, with SSIM improvements of 0.3–0.46 absolute points, demonstrating consistent superiority in both denoising and missing-wedge completion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant self-supervised paradigm in cryo-ET restoration (Noise2Noise, Topaz-Denoise, SC-Net for denoising; IsoNet for missing wedge) by introducing a fully supervised multi-task framework. The key enabler is a generative physical simulation pipeline that synthesizes training tomograms with known clean ground truth, circumventing the 'missing GT' dilemma that has forced the field toward SSL. Architecturally it repurposes the U-Net from Noise2Noise but fundamentally changes the training signal from self-supervised noise-to-noise to supervised noisy-to-clean, while adding a Fourier-domain masking loss for missing wedge recovery that no prior supervised method has combined with denoising in a single network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim—that a model trained exclusively on three EMDB structures (HIV, ribosome, nucleosome) generalizes directly to four diverse real cryo-ET datasets without fine-tuning—would, if robust, fundamentally change how cryo-ET restoration is approached by making supervised learning viable where it was previously thought impossible. However, the training set's structural diversity is limited, and the 'ground truth' is itself a 90° WBP reconstruction rather than a truly clean volume, leaving open whether the approach scales to the full complexity of intact cellular environments. The paper acknowledges the need for more diverse biological structures in future work, and the generalizability of the generative noise model across different microscope configurations remains untested.
