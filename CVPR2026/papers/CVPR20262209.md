## Problem

High-quality 4D dynamic scene reconstruction typically requires dense arrays of 20+ synchronized cameras, making it impractical for most settings. Existing sparse-view approaches either rely on geometric regularization (e.g., MonoFusion) that degrades rapidly under viewpoint shifts, or naively incorporate diffusion-generated views whose inherent spatio-temporal inconsistencies cause severe blurring and artifacts. The paper tackles the open problem of achieving photorealistic, spatio-temporally consistent 4D reconstruction from as few as 2–3 uncalibrated cameras.

## Input / Output

The method takes 2–3 sparse, uncalibrated camera video sequences (N sequences × L frames) as real input, supplemented by M generated video sequences produced by a camera-controlled diffusion model (ViewCrafter, 25 frames per sequence). Camera poses for both real and generated views are initially estimated via COLMAP and then jointly refined during training. The output is a canonical 4D Gaussian Splatting model; the Spatio-Temporal Distortion Field is discarded after training, so novel-view rendering incurs zero additional computational overhead beyond standard 4DGS splatting.

## Method

The core contribution is the Spatio-Temporal Distortion Field (STDF), a 5D Ennea-plane representation that factorizes the (x, y, z, t, s) volume into nine 2D multi-resolution feature planes (omitting the t–s plane since it encodes no distortion), decoded by a lightweight multi-head MLP to produce per-Gaussian deformations in position, rotation, and scaling. During training, the canonical 4DGS renders real input views while the distorted 4DGS (canonical + STDF offset) renders diffusion-generated views, allowing the model to disentangle generative inconsistencies from true scene geometry. Camera extrinsics are treated as learnable variables and jointly optimized with the 4DGS and STDF, regularized toward COLMAP initializations. The total loss combines photometric terms (L1 + D-SSIM for real views; L1 + LPIPS for generated views), a pose-anchoring penalty, grid-based TV loss on spatial planes, and a second-derivative smoothness loss along the pose axis. After convergence, the STDF is discarded, leaving a clean canonical 4DGS that renders spatio-temporally consistent novel views at real-time rates.

## Results

On the three standard multi-camera dynamic scene benchmarks, SparseCam4D achieves PSNR of 23.15 / 21.91 / 24.81, SSIM of 0.728 / 0.789 / 0.794, and LPIPS of 0.299 / 0.258 / 0.150 on Technicolor, Neural 3D Video, and Nvidia Dynamic Scenes respectively, using only 2–3 input cameras. These results substantially outperform the strongest baseline MonoFusion* (PSNR 17.97 / 18.43 / 20.22) by 3.5–5.2 dB in PSNR across all datasets. The method also surpasses general 4DGS approaches (4DGaussians, RealTime4DGS, 4D-Rotor) by 5–8 dB in PSNR, demonstrating that generative priors with explicit distortion modeling are far more effective than geometric regularization alone under sparse-view conditions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SparseCam4D wins on every metric across all three datasets against all five baselines. The closest competitor is MonoFusion* (reproduced), which leverages monocular depth, 3D tracking, and DINOv2 features as geometric priors; SparseCam4D still leads by +5.18 dB PSNR on Technicolor, +3.48 dB on Neural 3D Video, and +4.59 dB on Nvidia Dynamic Scenes. General 4DGS methods (4DGaussians, RealTime4DGS, 4D-Rotor) degrade severely under sparse inputs, with PSNR dropping to 14.85–19.38, producing broken geometry and missing details in dynamic regions. HyperReel, designed for dense multi-view settings, performs worst overall (PSNR 14.14–19.88), confirming that dense-view methods do not transfer to the sparse-camera regime. The paper notes that baselines are given ground-truth poses (a mild advantage), while SparseCam4D must jointly optimize poses from uncalibrated inputs, making the margin even more significant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) by introducing a Spatio-Temporal Distortion Field (STDF) that factorizes a 5D volume (x,y,z,t,pose) into nine 2D planes (Ennea-planes), a direct generalization of the K-planes/hex-planes representation from 4D to 5D. It departs from prior sparse-view 4D reconstruction (MonoFusion, Shape-of-Motion), which relies on geometric priors like monocular depth and tracking, by instead leveraging camera-controlled video diffusion models (ViewCrafter, ReCamMaster) as a source of auxiliary observations and explicitly modeling their spatio-temporal inconsistencies. The key conceptual departure is treating generative artifacts not as noise to be suppressed but as a structured, learnable distortion that can be disentangled and then discarded, leaving a clean canonical 4D representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to achieve sparse-camera 4D reconstruction evaluated across all views on standard multi-camera dynamic benchmarks, addressing the practical barrier that high-quality dynamic capture currently requires 12–21 synchronized cameras. The STDF's zero-inference-cost design (discarded after training) makes the approach deployable, and the generality across different diffusion backbones (ViewCrafter, ReCamMaster) suggests the framework could adapt as generative models improve. Open questions include scalability to longer sequences and more complex outdoor dynamics, sensitivity to the quality of the underlying diffusion model, and whether the 5D plane representation remains tractable for higher-resolution or longer-duration scenes.
