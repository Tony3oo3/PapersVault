## Problem

In MoE-based VLMs, how modality-specific signals (vision vs. text) should guide expert routing remains under-explored. Existing strategies are either hand-crafted hard/hybrid routing that imposes rigid, layer-agnostic modality boundaries, or modality-agnostic soft routing that provides no specialization guidance, both misaligned with the dynamic, layer-dependent fusion patterns observed in practice. This misalignment degrades both task accuracy and expert-parallel deployment efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method operates on token-level features within MoE layers of a VLM, where each token carries a soft modality score M ∈ [0,1] (summing to 1 across text and vision) computed per layer. Vision tokens (from CLIP ViT-L/14) and text tokens are processed jointly through the MoE backbone, with the routing mechanism producing expert assignments that respect modality-aware binning. The output is a standard text response, but the routing decisions are structured so that modality-aligned expert bins can be co-located on the same GPU for expert-parallel deployment.

## Method

SMoES introduces three coupled components: (1) dynamic soft modality scores estimated via either attention-accumulated propagation (aggregating attended tokens' scores weighted by attention matrices, updated with residual-norm weighting) or Gaussian-statistics scoring (maintaining per-modality diagonal-covariance Gaussians via EMA and computing temperature-scaled softmax of log-likelihoods); (2) momentum-adaptive expert binning that partitions experts into N_bins groups sorted by their EMA-tracked text-bias score, serving as natural device-placement units for expert parallelism; and (3) an inter-bin mutual information objective that maximizes I(M;B) between soft modality scores and bin assignments, encouraging each bin to develop a coherent modality preference. The full training loss combines task loss, bin-level load balancing, and the MI term, with the bin-level granularity directly aligning specialization with EP communication reduction.

## Results

Across four MoE-VLM backbones (DeepSeekMoE, OLMoE, Moonlight-MoE, Qwen3-MoE) and 16 benchmarks, SMoES achieves a 0.9% average gain on multimodal tasks and 4.2% average gain on language-only tasks over the soft-routing baseline. On DeepSeekMoE specifically, the attention-soft variant yields +1.8% multimodal and +6.2% language improvements, while the Gaussian-soft variant yields +1.3% and +4.2% respectively. Under expert-parallel deployment on two NVIDIA Orin GPUs, SMoES reduces cross-GPU communication by up to 86.6% for vision tokens and improves TTFT by up to 22.0% at batch size 8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMoES outperforms all four baselines on both multimodal and language-only tasks across all tested backbones. Hard Routing [39] enforces strict modality-expert separation (MSI=1.0) but incurs severe degradation: −3.9% to −6.1% multimodal and −14.5% to −34.1% language, confirming that rigid specialization misaligns with layer-dependent fusion dynamics. MoIIE [53] (hybrid routing with hand-crafted text/vision/shared partitions) also underperforms the soft-routing baseline (−1.5% to −3.0% multimodal, −9.6% to −20.7% language), showing that manual partitioning fails to adapt to evolving modality distributions. SMAR [61] (KL-divergence regularization) is the closest competitor, achieving +0.6% multimodal on DeepSeekMoE but suffering −15.3% to −25.0% language degradation due to incompatibility between KL regularization and load-balancing constraints. SMoES is the only method that simultaneously improves both multimodal and language performance while maintaining load balance, with the attention-soft variant achieving the best overall gains (+3.5% on DeepSeekMoE, +2.9% on OLMoE).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against three lines of prior work on MoE-VLM routing: (1) hard/hybrid modality assignment (Mono-InternVL, MoIIE, BEiT-3), which pre-assigns experts to modalities with hand-crafted, layer-agnostic partitions; (2) soft modality-aware routing via KL divergence (SMAR), which regularizes routing distributions toward modality-specific patterns but suffers incompatibility with load balancing; and (3) MI-based expert-task or expert-token alignment (Mod-Squad, ModuleFormer, CoMoE), which the authors repurpose for modality-expert alignment rather than task-expert alignment. SMoES departs from all three by introducing layer-adaptive soft modality scores (attention-accumulated and Gaussian-statistics estimators) that capture the continuous, depth-dependent fusion dynamics the authors document via JS divergence analysis, and by coupling an inter-bin MI objective with a momentum-adaptive binning mechanism that doubles as a device-placement unit for expert-parallel deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the unification of accuracy and deployment efficiency: modality-aligned expert binning simultaneously improves task performance (0.9%/4.2% on multimodal/language-only) and cuts EP communication overhead by 56.1% with 12.3% throughput gains on edge hardware (NVIDIA Orin), a setting directly relevant to autonomous-vehicle deployment. The acknowledged limitation that the Gaussian-soft estimator uses a simple unimodal diagonal-covariance model (with GMM only explored preliminarily) leaves open the question of whether richer density models or multi-modal extensions (beyond vision+text) would further sharpen specialization. More broadly, the finding that rigid modality partitioning (hard routing) incurs severe language-only degradation (−22.2%) while naive soft routing under-specializes provides a useful design principle: specialization must be learned, layer-adaptive, and compatible with load balancing to be viable at scale.
