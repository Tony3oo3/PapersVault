## Problem

Existing 4D Gaussian-based language field methods suffer from two fundamental limitations: (1) assuming a static object identity per Gaussian causes semantic inconsistency when deformation fields warp Gaussians across object boundaries over time (Gaussian ID oscillation), and (2) modeling dynamic semantics as a discrete set of learned state prototypes introduces an action boundary bias that blurs fine-grained temporal transitions and limits temporal continuity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263261/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences (from HyperNeRF or Neu3D) along with text prompts for open-vocabulary queries. Data preparation uses DEVA for temporally consistent hierarchical masks, SAM2 for cross-view instance matching via pseudo-video propagation, and MLLM-generated temporal descriptions encoded by LLMs into sentence embeddings for supervision. The output is a continuous 4D language field that supports both time-agnostic queries (segmenting objects at arbitrary timestamps) and time-sensitive queries (identifying the precise time segment where a described state or action occurs), evaluated via mIoU, vIoU, and accuracy.

## Method

LangField4D introduces a two-stage pipeline on top of 4D-GS. First, Identity-Adaptive Gaussian Grouping (IdaGG) augments the multi-head deformation decoder with a lightweight identity adaptation head that queries HexPlane spatio-temporal features to predict a dynamic offset Δe, so each Gaussian's identity encoding e′ = e + φ_id(f_d) adapts to its current instance affiliation, suppressing ID oscillation. Second, a Continuous Spatio-Temporal Semantic Learning module factorizes the coupled 5D space (x, y, z, t, l) into four multi-resolution 2D planes (TetraPlane): three space-semantic planes encoding time-invariant object-level semantics and one time-semantic plane capturing smooth state-dependent variations. Features from all planes are fused via Hadamard product across resolution levels and decoded by separate static and dynamic MLP heads, trained with an alternating strategy using L1 language loss, spatial TV regularization, and temporal Laplacian smoothness to enforce continuous, identity-aware semantic encoding at arbitrary time points.

## Results

On time-agnostic open-vocabulary querying, LangField4D achieves 83.09% mIoU on HyperNeRF and 71.62% mIoU on Neu3D, outperforming the previous SOTA 4DLangSplat (80.93% and 55.18% respectively). On time-sensitive querying over HyperNeRF, the method attains 64.31% vIoU and 84.79% accuracy overall, compared to 47.04% vIoU and 67.65% accuracy for 4DLangSplat. The largest single-scene gain is on the split-cookie scene, where vIoU jumps from 33.36% to 79.95% and accuracy from 47.17% to 92.45%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LangField4D outperforms all four baselines on both datasets for time-agnostic querying. Against the closest competitor 4DLangSplat [21], the gains are +2.16 mIoU on HyperNeRF (83.09 vs 80.93) and a substantial +16.44 mIoU on Neu3D (71.62 vs 55.18), indicating that the identity-adaptive grouping is particularly effective in more complex dynamic scenes. Against LangSplat [39], the improvement is +8.17 mIoU on HyperNeRF and +10.13 on Neu3D. The 3D-only baselines (Gaussian Grouping [61] and Feature-3DGS [65]) trail by 30+ mIoU points, confirming the necessity of explicit 4D modeling. For time-sensitive querying, LangField4D surpasses 4DLangSplat by +17.27 vIoU and +17.14 accuracy on the overall HyperNeRF benchmark, with the most dramatic improvement on the split-cookie scene (+46.59 vIoU), directly validating the continuous TetraPlane's ability to delineate fine-grained temporal action boundaries.

## Relation to Prior Work

LangField4D extends the 4D language field paradigm introduced by 4DLangSplat [21], which itself builds on 4D-GS [57] for dynamic scene rendering and LangSplat [39] for 3D open-vocabulary semantics. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing the static per-Gaussian identity encoding inherited from Gaussian Grouping [61] with a dynamic Identity-Adaptive Gaussian Grouping (IdaGG) module that leverages HexPlane deformation features to track instance affiliation over time, and (2) replacing 4DLangSplat's discrete state-prototype interpolation with a continuous TetraPlane factorization that uses the learned semantic coordinate as an explicit queryable dimension. Conceptually, the paper reframes the 4D semantic problem as a 5D (x,y,z,t,l) factorization, borrowing the plane-decomposition strategy from HexPlane and K-Planes [2,9] but applying it to the semantic domain rather than geometry or appearance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete failure modes (Gaussian ID oscillation and action boundary bias) that limit the practical utility of 4D language fields for real-world dynamic scene understanding, and the proposed solutions are modular enough to be adopted in other 4D-GS pipelines. However, the method's reliance on DEVA for upstream instance tracking and on MLLM-generated temporal descriptions for supervision introduces a dependency chain that limits robustness in scenes with imperfect segmentation or ambiguous action states. Open questions include whether the TetraPlane factorization generalizes to scenes with topological changes or large numbers of interacting objects, and whether the identity adaptation mechanism can operate without per-instance supervision from multi-view mask propagation.
