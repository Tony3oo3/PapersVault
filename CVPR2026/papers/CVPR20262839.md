## Problem

Existing image editing benchmarks focus on common operations (object addition, replacement, deletion, color adjustment) and rely on opaque MLLM holistic scoring that lacks interpretability and may overlook critical evaluation dimensions. There is no systematic, human-aligned framework for assessing model performance on complex, free-style creative editing tasks where instructions are semantically rich and open-ended.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262839/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes triplets of [source image, edited image, evaluation query] and produces per-question YES/NO answers that are aggregated into three metric scores (IF, VC, VQ), combined via a fixed weighted formula S = 0.4·S_IF + 0.4·S_VC + 0.2·S_VQ. Each image-instruction pair carries at least 15 structured QA pairs (≥5 per metric), and the VC metric additionally assigns importance weights w∈{1,2,3} to preserved visual elements so that loss of iconic features (e.g., the pearl earring in 'Girl with a Pearl Earring') penalizes the score more heavily than loss of minor details.

## Method

CREval replaces direct MLLM scoring with a VQA-based pipeline: for each image-instruction pair, a generator MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B) uses Chain-of-Thought reasoning to decompose the instruction and produce targeted evaluation questions with explicit YES/NO reference answers across three dimensions—Instruction Following (does the output fully realize the instruction?), Visual Consistency (are key identity elements preserved, weighted by importance?), and Visual Quality (are there artifacts or structural distortions?). A separate evaluator MLLM (e.g., GPT-4o) then answers each question given the source image, edited image, instruction, and question; a point is awarded only if the answer matches the reference. This decomposition yields transparent, per-question scoring rationales that expose exactly where a model succeeds or fails, in contrast to a single opaque score. The accompanying CREval-Bench spans 3 categories (Customization, Contextualization, Stylization) and 9 creative dimensions with 874 image-instruction pairs and 13K+ evaluation queries, constructed via GPT-4o few-shot instruction generation over curated high-quality source images.

## Results

On CREval-Bench (874 samples, 13K evaluation queries), Seedream 4.0 achieves the highest overall score of 83.43 (IF: 89.12, VC: 73.44, VQ: 92.01), followed by Gemini 2.5 Flash Image (81.34) and Qwen-Image-Edit (79.78, best open-source). GPT-Image-1 scores 78.97 overall, dragged down by the lowest visual consistency among closed-source models (VC: 63.46) despite a strong IF of 88.34. Visual Consistency is the weakest metric across all models, with even the best performer (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image) reaching only 74.79, indicating that preserving key visual elements remains a major challenge for creative editing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source models, Seedream 4.0 leads across nearly all creative dimensions with an overall score of 83.43, outperforming Gemini 2.5 Flash Image (81.34) by ~2 points and GPT-Image-1 (78.97) by ~4.5 points. Notably, Qwen-Image-Edit (best open-source, 79.78) surpasses GPT-Image-1 in overall score, primarily because GPT-Image-1 suffers from the lowest visual consistency (63.46) among all closed-source models, despite achieving a near-top IF of 88.34. Among open-source models, FLUX.1 Kontext [dev] (74.81) and Step1X-Edit v1-p2 (69.46) trail Qwen-Image-Edit by 5 and 10 points respectively, while ICEdit (53.78) and UniWorld-V1 (65.37) perform weakest; UniWorld-V1's relatively high VC (79.74) is misleading, as it largely stems from failing to execute edits rather than true consistency. The 'think' variants of Bagel and Step1X-Edit do not improve over their base versions in IF, suggesting the additional reasoning module does not help instruction alignment. Human preference validation (Table 3) confirms that CREval scores using both GPT-4o and Qwen3-VL as evaluators correlate closely with human judgments, outperforming VIEScore and EditScore baselines in ranking fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior instruction-based image editing benchmarks (ImgEdit-Bench, KRIS-Bench, RISE-Bench, GEdit-Bench) in two key ways: it targets creative/free-style editing rather than common object-level operations, and it replaces opaque MLLM holistic scoring with a structured VQA-based evaluation pipeline. The VQA-based evaluation idea draws on text-to-image faithfulness work (TIFA, VIEScore) and the broader trend of using MLLMs as evaluators, but CREval's specific decomposition into Instruction Following, Visual Consistency, and Visual Quality with importance-weighted element preservation is novel to the creative editing setting. The benchmark construction leverages GPT-4o for instruction generation across nine predefined creative dimensions, extending the few-shot instruction synthesis approach used in prior editing datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CREval-Bench fills a clear gap by providing the first systematic evaluation of creative/free-style image editing, a scenario increasingly relevant to real users but previously unmeasured. The finding that all evaluated models—especially GPT-Image-1—struggle significantly with visual consistency (preserving key identity elements) highlights a fundamental limitation that current architectures have not solved. Open questions include whether the 9-dimension taxonomy captures the full space of creative editing, whether the MLLM-based VQA evaluation generalizes to more subtle perceptual failures, and whether the gap between open- and closed-source models will narrow as open models improve. The moderate size (874 samples) and reliance on a single evaluator family (GPT-4o/Qwen3-VL) limit the robustness of absolute scores, though relative rankings appear stable.
