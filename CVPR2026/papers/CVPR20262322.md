## Problem

Federated learning suffers from a severe communication bottleneck: each client must upload multi-million-parameter gradient updates every round, and existing compression strategies (sparsification, quantization) introduce information loss that degrades convergence, while increasing local computation causes model divergence on non-IID data. No prior method can faithfully reconstruct the full local training trajectory from an extremely compact representation without lossy gradient-space compression.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262322/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client downloads the current global model, performs K local training epochs on its private data, and transmits a single compact snapshot per round — a grid of M×M synthetic image patches (each bilinearly upscaled to full input resolution, e.g., 224×224×3 for CV or a sequence of learnable embeddings for NLP) paired with M² learnable label embeddings. The server receives all C client snapshots, performs a single forward-and-backward pass over the concatenated batch to recover the aggregate gradient, and updates the global model in one step. The snapshot size is constrained by an L0 communication budget B, and the entire per-client payload is on the order of 0.03–13.7 MB regardless of model size.

## Method

OS-FED replaces direct gradient compression with learning a compact multi-grid snapshot whose synthetic gradient approximates the accumulated update over an entire local training trajectory. The snapshot is parameterized as a unified tensor that a differentiable multi-grid function F(·) unfolds into M² distinct synthetic samples (each patch upscaled and paired with a label embedding), decoupling conflicting gradient directions into separate spatial regions. Snapshot parameters are optimized end-to-end via L-BFGS to minimize a normalized L2 distance between the synthetic gradient and the target trajectory (current accumulated update plus an inherited error vector). An error compensation mechanism carries the per-round compression residual forward, preventing information loss from accumulating across rounds. On the server side, aggregation reduces to a single O(1) forward-and-backward pass over the batch of all client snapshots, and a theoretical proposition shows the multi-grid parameterization achieves a strictly better optimum than direct snapshot optimization under the gradient L2 distance measure.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, OS-FED (4×4) achieves 30.3% top-1 accuracy with only 0.12 MB per round, surpassing the strongest gradient-compression baseline LoRA-FAIR (26.3%) by 4.0 percentage points while using 30× less communication. On ImageNet-10 with RegNetX-4.0GF, OS-FED reaches 64% accuracy at 57 MB total traffic versus 65% for uncompressed FedAvg at 8,040 MB (>100× reduction). On the NLP benchmark AGNews with GPT-2-mini, OS-FED (4×4) attains 81.6% top-1 accuracy at 7.66 MB, outperforming LoRA-FAIR (75.5%) by 6.1 points with 3.3× less communication, and its server-side decompression completes in 0.68 s versus 8+ minutes for dataset-distillation baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OS-FED consistently outperforms all gradient-compression baselines: on CIFAR-100 it beats LoRA-FAIR by 4.0 points (30.3% vs 26.3%) and FedMPQ by 10.1 points (30.3% vs 20.2%) while transmitting 30× and 117× less data, respectively. Against the uncompressed FedAvg reference, OS-FED (4×4) achieves 30.3% vs 27.4% on CIFAR-100 with 3,760× less communication, and on ImageNet-10 it matches FedAvg's 65% within 1 point at 141× lower traffic. On NLP (AGNews), OS-FED (4×4) surpasses LoRA-FAIR by 6.1 points (81.6% vs 75.5%) and the augmented dataset-distillation baseline FedAF+ by 1.6 points, while reducing client-side compression time from 56 minutes to 5.46 seconds and server-side decompression from 13 minutes to 0.82 seconds. Compared to the privacy baseline DP-SGD on ImageNet-1K, OS-FED with noise injection achieves comparable cosine similarity (0.05 vs 0.046) at 33.5% accuracy versus 33.4%, but with 2.2× less memory and 2.7× less wall-time per client. The only setting where OS-FED trails is CIFAR-10, where FedAvg still reaches 47.6% vs OS-FED's 44.9%, though this gap is at the cost of 3,727× more communication.

## Relation to Prior Work

OS-FED departs from both dominant paradigms for reducing FL communication: direct gradient compression (sparsification, quantization, low-rank methods like FedMPQ, RandTopk, LoRA-FAIR) and local data distillation (FedAF, FedSD2C). Rather than lossily compressing the gradient vector or synthesizing a dataset for server-side retraining, it repurposes the dataset-distillation idea of learning synthetic samples to match gradients, but uses the resulting snapshot purely as a one-shot gradient proxy whose forward/backward pass regenerates the accumulated update. The multi-grid parameterization and error-compensation mechanism are novel contributions not present in prior snapshot or distillation methods, and the theoretical analysis (Proposition 1) provides a formal justification for why the multi-grid design dominates a monolithic snapshot under a gradient L2 distance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 140× communication reduction with ~1% accuracy loss on ImageNet-10, combined with plug-and-play compatibility with LoRA and quantization, makes OS-FED a practical candidate for bandwidth-constrained FL deployments, especially as LLMs make the communication bottleneck more acute. The first demonstration on a GPT-style transformer (GPT-2-mini) opens the door to communication-efficient federated fine-tuning of language models. Open questions include how the L-BFGS client-side optimization scales to truly large LLMs on resource-constrained devices, whether the approach degrades under extreme non-IID settings beyond Dirichlet(0.5), and whether the privacy guarantees (currently empirical cosine-similarity analysis) can be made formally rigorous without the overhead of full DP-SGD.
