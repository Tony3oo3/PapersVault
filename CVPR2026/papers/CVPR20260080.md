## Problem

Existing video self-supervised learning methods (e.g., VideoMAE, MAE-ST) rely on space-time patch prediction objectives that do not strongly enforce pixel-level temporal correspondence, resulting in weak representations for point tracking. The paper asks how to design a self-supervised pretraining task in which long-range correspondence naturally emerges in the learned representations without any tracking annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260080/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes k=16 RGB video frames, patchified into 16×16 tokens per frame with a 95% masking ratio, and produces 256 3D Gaussian primitives per frame (each a 14-dim vector of position, scale, rotation quaternion, color, and opacity). For zeroshot tracking, the projected 2D Gaussian mean-deltas are splatted into a dense flow field that advects arbitrary query points; for fine-tuned tracking, a cross-attention readout with 64-dim Fourier positional queries maps encoder latents to per-frame 2D point coordinates and occlusion logits.

## Method

Video-GMAE replaces the standard MAE pixel-patch decoder with a differentiable Gaussian splatting renderer: a ViT encoder processes masked video patches, and a lightweight decoder predicts 256 explicit Gaussians for the first frame plus per-frame delta Gaussians (Δμ, Δr) for all subsequent frames, so each Gaussian retains a fixed identity across the sequence. The deltas are integrated cumulatively (G_t = ΔG_t + G_{t−1}) and rendered via volumetric alpha compositing, with reconstruction loss backpropagated end-to-end through the splatting pipeline. This correspondence-aware objective forces the encoder latents to encode long-term motion structure rather than merely local patch statistics. Zeroshot tracking is extracted by projecting Gaussian centers to pixel space, encoding per-Gaussian 2D displacements as pseudo-RGB values, and splatting them into a dense flow field; a top-k anchor mechanism with visibility thresholds handles occlusions. For supervised evaluation, a cross-attention readout with learnable temporal embeddings and a residual MLP is trained on the (optionally frozen) encoder features to predict tracked points and occlusion states.

## Results

On the TAP-Vid protocol (stride 5), Video-GMAE-large finetune achieves 75.1 AJ / 81.6 δ_avg / 97.9 OA on Kinetics, 57.9 AJ / 67.7 δ_avg / 93.5 OA on Davis, and 74.0 AJ / 82.4 δ_avg / 97.6 OA on Kubric. This represents a 34.6% AJ improvement over the best supervised tracker (CoTracker3, 55.8 AJ) on Kinetics and a 13.1% improvement over the best supervised tracker (TAP-Net, 65.4 AJ) on Kubric. Even in the zeroshot setting (no tracking labels), Video-GMAE reaches 60.1 AJ on Kinetics, surpassing all prior self-supervised methods (best: GMRW-C at 31.9 AJ). In the frozen-encoder comparison against other video SSL pretraining (Table 1), Video-GMAE outperforms MAE-ST and VideoMAE by 18–21 AJ points across all three datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against other video SSL pretraining methods (Table 1), Video-GMAE dominates: on Kinetics it reaches 65.1 AJ vs. 46.9 (VideoMAE) and 42.3 (MAE-ST); on Kubric 62.4 vs. 44.8 and 41.5; on Davis 46.7 vs. 31.8 and 28.3. Among self-supervised tracking methods (Table 2), Video-GMAE-zeroshot already matches or exceeds GMRW-C (the strongest SSL tracker) on all three datasets, with a particularly large gap on Kinetics (60.1 vs. 31.9 AJ). After fine-tuning, Video-GMAE-large surpasses all supervised trackers on Kubric (74.0 vs. 65.4 for TAP-Net) and Kinetics (75.1 vs. 55.8 for CoTracker3), but trails CoTracker3 (63.8) and LocoTrack (62.9) on Davis (57.9 AJ). The frozen-encoder setting shows a clear scaling benefit: the large model outperforms the base model by 1.6–3.4 AJ across datasets, and fine-tuning adds a further 12–14 AJ over the frozen variant. The paper notes that Video-GMAE's zeroshot tracker is more temporally stable and conservative around occlusions compared to GMRW-C, but struggles with small detailed regions undergoing large displacements due to the 256-Gaussian spatial resolution limit.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the image-level Gaussian Masked Autoencoder (GMAE) [38] to video by adding a temporal axis and, crucially, predicting delta-Gaussians (position and color changes) rather than absolute Gaussians for subsequent frames. The reconstruction target shifts from pixel patches (as in VideoMAE [48] and MAE-ST [14]) to a set of 3D Gaussian primitives rendered via differentiable splatting [26], which imposes a 3D-geometric inductive bias that standard spatio-temporal patch prediction does not. The delta formulation is the key architectural departure: by forcing the decoder to predict only changes relative to the previous frame, each Gaussian retains a persistent identity across the sequence, explicitly encoding temporal correspondence into the latent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that structured, correspondence-aware generative objectives (3D Gaussians evolving over time) are a viable and effective alternative to standard patch-based video pretraining, yielding substantially stronger frozen-encoder representations for tracking than VideoMAE or MAE-ST. The emergent zeroshot tracking capability—where a simple flow-field extraction from Gaussian trajectories rivals dedicated SSL tracking methods like GMRW-C—is a compelling signal that the representation genuinely encodes correspondence rather than merely reconstructing pixels. Key limitations remain: the static-camera assumption precludes metric 3D recovery, the 256-Gaussian budget constrains spatial fidelity for fine details under large motion, and the correspondence regularization degrades at longer frame lengths (24+), leaving open whether the approach scales to longer-horizon or camera-moving video.
