## Problem

Driving world models have achieved striking visual realism, but the field lacks a unified protocol to assess whether generated worlds preserve 3D/4D geometry, obey physical causality, and support reliable closed-loop control. Existing metrics emphasize frame-level aesthetics (LPIPS, FVD) and reveal little about geometric consistency, behavioral fidelity, or downstream task utility, leaving progress fragmented and results incomparable across models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261694/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes generated driving video sequences (typically multi-camera, dash-cam style) from various world models, paired with their semantic segmentation masks, monocular depth estimates, and 3D bounding boxes. It produces a 24-dimensional scorecard spanning five aspects, plus concrete artifacts: 4D Gaussian Splatting reconstructions rendered at novel camera poses, downstream perception metrics (mIoU, NDS, AMOTA, RayIoU) from pretrained real-data models, and closed-loop driving scores (PDMS, ADS) from a generative simulator. The WorldLens-Agent additionally accepts a video clip and outputs a 1–10 score with a natural-language rationale per dimension.

## Method

WorldLens decomposes evaluation into five complementary aspects with 24 interpretable metrics: (1) Generation uses 8 sub-metrics leveraging DINO/CLIP embeddings, SegFormer segmentation, Depth Anything V2, I3D FVD, and LoFTR cross-view matching; (2) Reconstruction lifts each video into a 4D Gaussian Field and re-renders under original and novel trajectories to expose geometric floaters; (3) Action-Following runs a pretrained planner (PDM/VAD) in a closed-loop generative simulator, measuring displacement error, PDMS, route completion, and Arena Driving Score; (4) Downstream Task applies real-data-pretrained BEVFusion, BEV-Former, ADA-Track, and SparseOcc to synthetic frames to quantify transferability; (5) Human Preference uses 10 annotators scoring 4 synchronized views (video, mask, depth, 3D boxes) on a 1–10 scale. To enable scalable auto-evaluation, the authors curate WorldLens-26K (26,808 records with numerical scores and textual rationales) and distill it into WorldLens-Agent via LoRA-based SFT on Qwen3-VL-8B, yielding a vision-language critic that predicts scores and generates human-consistent explanations zero-shot on unseen models.

## Results

Across 24 evaluation dimensions, no single driving world model dominates: DiST-4D achieves the best Reconstruction scores (Photometric Error 0.066, Geometric Discrepancy 0.080, Novel-View Quality 43.09%) and leads Downstream Tasks by 30–40% over the next best model (e.g., 3D Detection NDS 33.22% vs. 29.89% for X-Scene). OpenDWM provides the most balanced Generation performance (Subject Fidelity 36.30, Perceptual Discrepancy 90.42) but underperforms in 3D detection (21.96%) and tracking (6.90%). In Action-Following, all models collapse under closed-loop conditions, with Route Completion ranging from only 6.89% (MagicDrive) to 13.51% (RLGF), and human preference scores remain modest at 2–3 out of 10 across all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiST-4D [27] achieves the best Reconstruction scores (lowest Photometric Error 0.066, Geometric Discrepancy 0.080, and highest Novel-View Quality 43.09%), reducing photometric and geometric errors by ~55% over MagicDrive [24], and leads all Downstream Tasks by 30–40% (e.g., Map Segmentation 35.55% vs. 27.63% for OpenDWM). OpenDWM [83] provides the most balanced Generation performance (best Subject Fidelity 36.30, best Perceptual Discrepancy 90.42) and the lowest Reconstruction Photometric Error (0.065), yet underperforms DiST-4D in 3D detection (21.96% vs. 33.22%) and tracking (6.90% vs. 15.30%), suggesting multi-domain training hinders task-specific adaptation. DriveDreamer-2 [125] achieves the best Depth Discrepancy (17.73) and Cross-View Consistency (302.83) among non-conditioned models, while MagicDrive [24] ranks lowest across nearly all Generation and Reconstruction metrics. In Action-Following (Table 2), all models perform poorly under closed-loop control: Route Completion ranges from 6.89% (MagicDrive) to 13.51% (RLGF [107]), with Closed-Loop Adherence (ADS) never exceeding 10.59%, indicating that photometric realism alone is insufficient for functional driving. For Human Preference, DiST-4D achieves the most balanced scores (Physical Plausibility 2.58, Behavioral Safety 2.59), while OpenDWM attains the highest World Realism (2.76) but slightly lower physical consistency, and MagicDrive ranks lowest overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior video-generation benchmarks (VBench, EvalCrafter, T2V-CompBench) and early world-model evaluations (WorldScore, VideoWorld) that remain confined to 2D appearance metrics, and from driving-agent benchmarks (NAVSIM, DriveArena, nuPlan) that evaluate the controller rather than the world it inhabits. It repurposes existing tools—3D Gaussian Splatting for 4D reconstruction, pretrained planners (PDM, VAD) for action-following, and BEV perception models (BEVFusion, SparseOcc) for downstream tasks—into a single unified protocol that jointly measures spatial fidelity, behavioral consistency, and physical realism. The WorldLens-Agent component extends the VLM-as-judge paradigm (Qwen3-VL-8B with LoRA SFT) from generic video quality assessment to domain-specific, explainable world-model scoring grounded in a 26K human-annotated preference dataset.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that no existing driving world model dominates across all five aspects, and that the gap between open-loop realism and closed-loop functionality is severe (Route Completion collapses to 6–14% despite 71–79% PDMS), underscoring that photometric fidelity alone is insufficient for practical deployment. The finding that perceptually strong models (OpenDWM) can underperform geometry-aware ones (DiST-4D) by 30% on downstream detection challenges the field's implicit assumption that visual quality proxies for utility. Open questions include how to jointly optimize appearance and geometry without one degrading the other, how to achieve autoregressive resilience for long-horizon closed-loop generation, and whether the WorldLens-Agent generalizes beyond the driving domain to other embodied world-model settings.
