## Problem

While structured output generation from text-only inputs has received growing attention, no benchmark systematically evaluates whether MLLMs can produce schema-conformant structured outputs grounded in visual evidence. Existing visual-structured works either cover narrow domains, rely on simple flat KIE templates, or omit the diverse, nested JSON schemas that real-world agentic applications require, leaving a gap in quantifying how well MLLMs handle complex schema adherence over images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260616/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a triplet: an RGB image (from UI screens, natural images, documents, or charts), a pre-defined JSON schema (nested dictionaries up to 22 levels deep with over 2K fields, specifying keys, types, and hierarchy), and a free-form user instruction that ranges from precise to ambiguous or dialectal. The output is a structured JSON that must be both syntactically valid against the schema and semantically faithful to the image content. Evaluation uses AST-based recursive comparison with three matching strategies per primitive field (exact, fuzzy via edit distance/relative error, or ignore), decomposing performance into schema validation accuracy, field matching accuracy, and full structure matching accuracy.

## Method

SO-BENCH is constructed via a multi-stage auto-labeling pipeline: (1) CLIP-based multimodal embedding search associates images with schemas from a 6.5K repository (real-world + synthetic), with a frontier LLM selecting the best match from top-k candidates; (2) multi-image grouping (m=3 nearest neighbors) generates unified nested schemas capturing shared structure across image clusters; (3) 60K persona-based user profiles drive diverse instruction generation; and (4) a critic-refiner loop (Gemini-2.5-Pro for initial outputs, GPT-5 for refinement, up to 3 iterations) with human expert verification at each stage produces ground-truth responses. For training experiments, the authors fine-tune a 3B ViTDet-L-based MLLM via SFT on 114K pipeline-generated examples and via RLVR using MDPO with a reward function R(O,G) = α·FMA(O,G)² (penalizing invalid JSON at −0.1, with α=1.0 for schema-valid and 0.8 for schema-invalid outputs), showing SFT yields up to 20% gains in schema validation and 13% in field matching over the baseline.

## Results

On SO-BENCH, Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the strongest performance across all metrics, reaching 97.74% schema validation accuracy, 73.14% field match (fuzzy), and 18.91% full structure match (fuzzy). GPT-5 follows with 96.38% schema validation and 62.74% field match (fuzzy), while GPT-5-mini attains the highest schema validation at 98.70%. No model exceeds 20% on full structure matching (fuzzy), and small open-source models (3B–8B) show substantial gaps, with schema validation ranging from 16% to 72% and full match accuracy below 10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary frontier models, Gemini-2.5-Pro leads on all five metrics, outperforming GPT-5 by 7.7 points on field match (fuzzy) (73.14% vs. 62.74%) and by 7.3 points on full match (fuzzy) (18.91% vs. 11.60%), while GPT-5-mini edges out GPT-5 on schema validation (98.70% vs. 96.38%). Claude-4.5-Sonnet trails Gemini-2.5-Pro by 10.5 points on field match (fuzzy) (62.67% vs. 73.14%) and by 10.2 points on full match (fuzzy). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL (72B) achieves the best schema validation at 87.39% but still lags behind the weakest proprietary model (GPT-4o at 76.94%) on field match (fuzzy) by only 2 points, while its full match (fuzzy) of 9.25% is far below Gemini-2.5-Pro's 18.91%. Small open-source models (≤8B) show the largest gaps: Qwen3-VL (2B) scores only 16.28% schema validation and 6.36% full match (fuzzy), and Phi-4-Vision (5.6B) achieves just 22.04% schema validation. Notably, Qwen3-VL (32B) shows an anomalous drop in schema validation (47.71%) compared to Qwen2.5-VL (32B) at 87.16%, suggesting architectural or training differences affect schema compliance. Overall, the benchmark reveals a clear hierarchy: frontier proprietary models > large open-source (32B–72B) > mid-size open-source (12B–27B) > small open-source (≤8B), with the largest performance gap appearing on full structure matching rather than schema validation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three existing research threads: text-only structured output benchmarks (JSONSchemaBench, StructEval), visual structured understanding (Pix2Struct, Image2Struct), and key information extraction (KIE) in document parsing. Rather than building on a single prior formulation, it repurposes the schema-constrained generation paradigm from the text domain and the multi-domain visual coverage from OCR/document benchmarks, but deliberately departs from KIE's simple one-layer templates by introducing nested, application-driven JSON schemas paired with diverse user instructions. The agentic tool-use literature (BFCL, TauBench) informs the evaluation philosophy—treating model outputs as machine-consumed artifacts—but SO-Bench extends this to the multimodal setting with large-scale schema diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a critical and practically important gap: frontier models achieve >95% schema validation accuracy yet only ~17–19% full structure matching accuracy, meaning models can produce syntactically valid JSON while filling in wrong or missing values. This has direct implications for anyone deploying MLLMs in agentic pipelines (web automation, data extraction, tool use) where downstream systems require both structural and semantic correctness. Open questions include whether semantic (rather than exact/fuzzy) matching would close the gap, whether the RLVR approach scales beyond 3B models, and whether the distribution shift between synthetic and real-world schemas identified in the training experiments can be resolved with better generation pipelines.
