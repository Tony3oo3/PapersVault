## Problem

Current VLMs exhibit fundamental deficiencies in spatio-temporal consistency: they confuse camera rotation with object motion, misinterpret gripper states, and reverse movement directions across multi-frame inputs. Existing remedies—architectural modifications or data augmentation—address isolated aspects without a unified framework spanning data construction, training optimization, and evaluation for fine-grained motion discrimination.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multiple RGB video frames (from navigation, robotic manipulation, indoor exploration, or game simulation) paired with a text question probing inter-frame motion attributes (e.g., camera rotation direction, gripper openness, object displacement). Output is structured as a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer enclosed in <answer> tags; during SFT, loss is computed exclusively on the answer tokens, while during GRPO the full response is scored by a composite reward. The training data is organized as motion-contrast triplets (anchor, positive, negative) where the positive and negative are visually similar but encode opposing motion properties.

## Method

ReMoT integrates a rule-based multi-expert data pipeline with GRPO-based reinforcement learning. The pipeline orchestrates three specialized components operating on structured video meta-annotations (SE(3) pose matrices, robot action logs): a motion estimation expert extracts compositional motion properties, a triplet construction expert selects salient positives via magnitude thresholds and synthesizes hard negatives through property-conditioned geometric transformations or visual-similarity retrieval, and a VQA formulation expert generates multi-perspective reasoning chains in diverse formats (multiple-choice, true/false, fill-in-the-blank). For training, the paper systematically compares SFT, pure GRPO, sequential (SFT→GRPO), and alternating (SFT↔GRPO) schedules, with GRPO employing a composite reward that combines task accuracy, a logic-consistency reward penalizing transitivity violations across interdependent sub-questions, and a length penalty to suppress verbose reasoning. The alternating schedule, where SFT and GRPO steps interleave every few updates, achieves the best performance (38.0% overall accuracy, a 25.1% relative gain over the base Qwen3-VL-4B-Thinking model) by jointly evolving linguistic alignment and reward optimization.

## Results

On the new ReMoT-16k-Test benchmark, the best variant (SFT↔GRPO) achieves 38.0% overall accuracy and 64.0% partial accuracy, representing a +18.7% and +25.1% relative improvement over the Qwen3-VL-4B-CoT base model (20.7%/38.9%). On standard spatial-temporal benchmarks, ReMoT-4B-CoT reaches 70.0% on VLM2, 58.8% on VSI, and 30.8% on MMSI, outperforming the 7.5× larger Qwen3-VL-30B-CoT by +1.8%, +2.7%, and +2.3% respectively. On general multimodal benchmarks (BLINK, MUIR, MMStar, MMMU), ReMoT maintains competitive performance within 1–5% of the 30B base, confirming no catastrophic forgetting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary ReMoT-16k-Test benchmark, all trained variants dramatically outperform every open-source baseline: SFT↔GRPO reaches 38.0% overall accuracy vs. 20.7% for Qwen3-VL-CoT-4B, 5.1% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 14.9% for InternVL3-2B, and 9.7% for LLaVA-One-Vision. On standard spatial-temporal benchmarks (Table 4), ReMoT-4B-CoT surpasses the 7.5× larger Qwen3-VL-30B-CoT on all three spatial-temporal suites (VLM2: 70.0 vs. 68.2; VSI: 58.8 vs. 56.1; MMSI: 30.8 vs. 28.5) and also beats GPT-4o on VLM2 (+9.6) and VSI (+16.3). However, ReMoT-4B-CoT trails GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Pro on general multimodal benchmarks (e.g., MMMU: 71.4 vs. 81.8 for GPT-5; MUIR: 72.8 vs. 77.5 for GPT-5), and it also lags Qwen3-VL-30B-CoT on general tasks (MMStar: 70.4 vs. 75.5; MMMU: 71.4 vs. 76.0), indicating that the spatial-temporal gains come at a modest cost to broad multimodal capability. Among the paper's own training variants, the alternating SFT↔GRPO schedule yields the best overall accuracy (38.0%) and partial accuracy (64.0%), while pure GRPO achieves the highest Camera Navigation score (27.0% Ov.) but lower composite manipulation (79.2% vs. 86.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeekMath, repurposing it for vision-language model training with a novel composite reward (task accuracy + logic consistency + CoT length penalty). It departs from prior VLM spatio-temporal work—which has focused on architectural modifications (3D reconstruction, disentangled attention) or coarse-grained video-text data—by introducing a rule-based multi-expert pipeline that constructs fine-grained motion-contrast triplets directly from structured video metadata (SE(3) poses, robot telemetry). The systematic comparison of SFT, pure GRPO, and hybrid (sequential/alternating) schedules extends prior hybrid training work (e.g., LLaVA-RLHF) by introducing the alternating SFT↔GRPO schedule and showing that pure GRPO on contrastive data nearly matches hybrid approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a well-documented and practically critical failure mode in VLMs (confusing camera motion with object motion, misjudging gripper states) that blocks deployment in robotics, navigation, and autonomous driving. The multi-expert pipeline offers a scalable, low-cost alternative to manual annotation or VLM-based generation (which suffers 55% format errors), making fine-grained motion supervision accessible. However, evaluation is limited to a single 4B base model, the primary benchmark is self-constructed, and the rule-based approach requires structured metadata that may not exist in all domains—open questions include whether the gains transfer to larger models, whether the alternating schedule advantage holds at scale, and whether the triplet formulation generalizes to domains lacking explicit pose/action annotations.
