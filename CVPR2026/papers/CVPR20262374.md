## Problem

Industrial anomaly detection suffers from scarce anomalous samples, and existing few-shot anomaly synthesis methods either require expensive training (model fine-tuning or embedding learning) that still fails to faithfully capture real anomaly distributions, or are training-free but lack precise spatial control and produce unrealistic anomaly appearances. The paper addresses the gap of generating mask-localized, background-faithful, text-consistent anomalies in a fully training-free, one-shot setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262374/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a single reference anomaly image with its binary mask, one normal image, a target anomaly mask specifying where the defect should appear, and a text prompt following the template 'A photo of a [cls] with a [anomaly_type]', the method synthesizes new anomaly images at 512×512 resolution using 50 DDIM denoising steps. The output is a synthesized image where the anomaly is confined to the target mask region while the background outside the mask faithfully preserves the normal image's appearance, enabling direct use for training downstream U-Net segmentation and ResNet-34 classification models.

## Method

O2MAG coordinates three parallel Stable Diffusion v1.5 branches (reference-anomaly, normal-image, target-anomaly) via Tri-branch Attention Grafting (TriAG): within the target mask, the target branch's self-attention queries attend to keys/values from the reference-anomaly branch (masked to foreground), while outside the mask they attend to the normal branch (masked to background), decoupling defect appearance from background without any weight updates. Anomaly-Guided Optimization (AGO) performs 500 steps of Adam optimization on the CLIP text embedding using a reconstruction loss against the DDIM-inverted reference anomaly, aligning prompt semantics with real defect appearance while keeping the diffusion backbone frozen. Dual-Attention Enhancement (DAE) is injected at selected timesteps to upweight self-attention (additive logit bias γ plus temperature scaling τ_fg on masked pixels) and cross-attention (scaling the anomaly-type token's attention by C=100) within the mask, ensuring the synthesized defect fully occupies the target region. The entire pipeline requires no model fine-tuning and operates in a one-shot regime with a single reference anomaly image.

## Results

On MVTec-AD, O2MAG achieves the best pixel-level AP of 86.3% and F1-P of 80.8% for anomaly detection/localization, outperforming the strongest training-based baseline DualAnoDiff (84.5% AP-P, 78.8% F1-P) by +1.8% and +2.0% respectively. For anomaly classification with a ResNet-34, O2MAG reaches 82.35% average accuracy, surpassing AnomalyDiffusion (70.25%) by +12.1% and the training-free TF² (61.88%) by +20.47%. In generation quality, O2MAG attains the lowest average KID of 45.55 (vs. 62.85 for DFMGAN and 103.87 for DualAnoDiff), indicating the closest match to the real anomaly distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

O2MAG outperforms all seven baselines across every reported metric. Against the strongest training-based method DualAnoDiff [20], O2MAG gains +1.8% AP-P (86.3 vs 84.5) and +2.0% F1-P (80.8 vs 78.8) on average, with the largest per-category margins on screw (+10.1 AP-P) and grid (+21.4 AP-P). Against AnomalyDiffusion [18], the gap widens to +4.9% AP-P and +4.5% F1-P, and the classification accuracy advantage reaches +12.1% (82.35% vs 70.25%). The GAN-based DFMGAN [9] trails substantially, with average AP-P of only 62.7% and classification accuracy of 49.61%. Among training-free methods, O2MAG beats AnomalyAny [36] by +1.8% AUC-P and +15.5% F1-P (Table 3), and surpasses TF² [41] by +20.47% classification accuracy (82.35% vs 61.88%). In the zero-shot cross-class setting (wood-hole → hazelnut-hole), O2MAG-zero achieves 86.8% AP-P, outperforming SeaS-zero (78.6%) and DRAEM (74.8%), and nearly matching its own in-domain performance (90.4% AP-P).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts MasaCtrl's tuning-free self-attention editing to the industrial anomaly generation domain, extending it from single-reference image editing to a tri-branch (reference-anomaly, normal, target) architecture with mask-guided foreground/background decoupling. It departs from the dominant training-based diffusion paradigm (DreamBooth finetuning in DualAnoDiff, textual inversion in AnomalyDiffusion, separation-and-sharing in SeaS) by operating entirely at inference time, and from prior training-free methods (AnomalyAny, TF2) by performing fine-grained spatial attention grafting rather than coarse latent-level feature transfer. The Anomaly-Guided Optimization module borrows the textual-inversion idea of optimizing a text embedding but applies it as a one-shot, reference-image-conditioned reconstruction rather than a full per-concept training procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating training entirely, O2MAG removes the compute and storage overhead that has limited adoption of diffusion-based anomaly generators in industrial settings where retraining per product line is impractical. The zero-shot cross-class transfer (e.g., wood-hole guiding hazelnut-hole synthesis) suggests the approach could reduce the need for per-category anomaly references, though this remains underexplored. Key limitations include evaluation on a single dataset (MVTec-AD, 15 categories), dependence on at least one reference anomaly image per anomaly type, and the per-anomaly-type text embedding optimization that may not scale to open-vocabulary defect taxonomies. Whether the tri-branch attention grafting generalizes to multi-anomaly scenes or 3D industrial inspection remains open.
