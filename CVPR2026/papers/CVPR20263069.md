## Problem

Existing image-to-video generation models frequently exhibit appearance drift and geometric distortion when animating rigid objects under changing viewpoints, causing identity shifts, shape warps, and texture changes across frames. The authors attribute this to two systemic limitations: sparse single-view 2D observations that under-constrain object geometry, and weak cross-modal alignment where text and image inputs are encoded separately and fused only late in the network. Additionally, no large-scale object-centric dataset or benchmark specifically targets identity preservation across viewpoints and time.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a reference image (first frame), two unposed auxiliary images of the same object (no camera pose required), and a free-form text instruction describing desired motion. It outputs an 81-frame video clip at 832×480 resolution, generated via 50 diffusion sampling steps with classifier-free guidance scale 5. The auxiliary views are uncalibrated, meaning the model must infer geometric relationships without explicit pose annotations, making the approach practical for real-world product photos where multi-view images are available but poses are not.

## Method

ConsID-Gen augments the single reference frame with unposed auxiliary views and processes them through a dual-visual encoder: a CLIP-style 2D encoder extracts semantic appearance tokens from the first frame, while a VGGT geometry encoder produces dense structural tokens from all three views via alternating frame-wise and global self-attention. A Multi-Modal Visual-Geometric Module (MVGM) then fuses appearance and geometry tokens through dual-stream attention, with geometric features from auxiliary views injected via cross-attention to reinforce spatial consistency. A Multi-Modal Text-Visual Module (MTVM) further aligns the fused visual-geometric representation with textual tokens in a second dual-stream attention stage, producing unified conditioning tokens for the Wan2.1 DiT backbone. The key architectural insight is performing fine-grained cross-modal pre-alignment before projection into the diffusion network, rather than the standard late-fusion concatenation used by prevailing I2V pipelines. Training uses AdamW at 1e-4 learning rate with gradient accumulation over 4 steps for 33K steps on A100 GPUs, fine-tuning from Wan2.1-Fun-1.3B-InP.

## Results

On the proprietary subset of ConsIDVid-Bench, ConsID-Gen achieves the best scores across most metrics: 95.30 Subject Consistency (vs. 91.99 for Wan2.2-5B), 99.52 Motion Smoothness, and a substantially lower MEt3R of 0.0978 (a 30.2% improvement over the next-best Wan2.1-1.3B at 0.1401). On the public subset, ConsID-Gen attains the lowest Chamfer Distance (0.1277, a 7.26% improvement over Wan2.1-14B at 0.1322) and the highest Video Similarity (84.95). The model also leads in Object Similarity (69.2 proprietary, 71.8 public) and Temporal Flickering (99.24 proprietary), confirming superior identity fidelity and temporal coherence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConsID-Gen outperforms all six baselines on the proprietary subset in Subject Consistency (95.30 vs. 91.99 for the strongest competitor Wan2.2-5B, a 3.6-point margin) and MEt3R (0.0978 vs. 0.1401 for Wan2.1-1.3B, a 30.2% reduction). Against Wan2.2-5B, the closest overall competitor, ConsID-Gen leads on Subject Consistency, Background Consistency, Motion Smoothness, Temporal Flickering, Object Similarity, and MEt3R, while Wan2.2 edges ahead by 0.04 on Video Similarity (88.69 vs. 88.65). On the public subset, ConsID-Gen achieves the best Chamfer Distance (0.1277 vs. 0.1322 for Wan2.1-14B) and Video Similarity (84.95 vs. 84.81 for Wan2.2-5B), but trails Wan2.2-5B on I2V Subject (98.14 vs. 98.47) and I2V Background (98.49 vs. 98.64), which the authors attribute to degradation on inputs with distracting structures. Compared to HunyuanVideo, ConsID-Gen shows a dramatic MEt3R advantage (0.0978 vs. 0.2270 on proprietary), indicating far superior multi-view geometric consistency. ConsistI2V and CogVideoX1.5-5B are clearly outperformed across all metrics, with ConsID-Gen gaining 4+ points in Subject Consistency and 30–50% lower MEt3R. SkyReelsv2 is the weakest baseline, trailing ConsID-Gen by over 9 points in Subject Consistency and 55% in MEt3R on the proprietary set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Wan2.1 DiT-based video generation backbone and extends the MMDiT dual-stream attention paradigm from the text-visual domain to a visual-geometric domain, coupling a CLIP-style 2D semantic encoder with a VGGT geometry encoder. It departs from prior I2V conditioning strategies (channel-wise latent concatenation in SVD/CogVideoX, mask-guided cross-attention in Wan2.1, spatial-temporal attention in ConsistI2V) by introducing unposed multi-view auxiliary images as explicit geometric anchors and a dedicated text-visual connector that pre-aligns all modalities before the diffusion backbone. The paper also carves out a new data and evaluation axis: the ConsIDVid dataset and ConsIDVid-Bench reframe I2V evaluation as a multi-view consistency problem, filling a gap left by scene-level benchmarks like VBench that do not capture fine-grained object identity drift.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Identity-preserving I2V is a critical bottleneck for e-commerce product videos, advertising, and any application where a single catalog image must be animated without appearance degradation; this paper provides both a practical method and a standardized benchmark for measuring that specific failure mode. The observation that T2V models outperform I2V models on identity consistency (Table 1) challenges the assumption that visual conditioning is always beneficial and points to cross-modal alignment as a first-order architectural concern. Limitations include degradation on inputs with distracting structures (e.g., grid paper), ablations conducted on only 50% of training data, and evaluation restricted to rigid objects, leaving open whether the multi-view geometric anchoring generalizes to deformable or non-rigid subjects.
