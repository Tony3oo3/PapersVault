## Problem

Feed-forward multiview transformers (MVTs) such as VGGT and π³ are natively designed to accept only RGB images, yet real-world 3D reconstruction scenarios often provide additional priors like camera poses, intrinsics, or depth maps. Existing prior-aware methods (Pow3R, MapAnything) require architectural modifications and full retraining to incorporate these priors, making them inflexible and computationally expensive whenever the backbone or available priors change.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N RGB images at resolution H×W along with optional priors (camera poses, intrinsics, or depth maps) that are used as constraints rather than network inputs. The MVT outputs per-view depth maps, confidence maps (both at H×W), camera poses (parameterized as quaternion+translation or rotation+translation), and intrinsic matrices (focal lengths under a centered pinhole model). Point maps in camera or world coordinates are derived post-hoc by composing these three predictions. The priors are never encoded or fed into the network; they only appear as penalty terms in the test-time optimization loss.

## Method

TCO reformulates prior-aware 3D reconstruction as a test-time constrained optimization: priors become differentiable penalty terms on the MVT's predictions, while a self-supervised compatibility objective (photometric or geometric L1 loss) enforces multi-view consistency by rendering source views into target views via a 2DGS differentiable renderer whose parameters are heuristically inferred from the MVT's depth, pose, and confidence predictions. To exploit cross-task synergy, all task-specific prediction heads are frozen and only the shared decoder is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 4) using Adam for 5–80 iterations at inference. For point-map estimation, the loss combines photometric compatibility with rotation (angular distance), translation (scale-normalized L1), and focal-length penalties; for camera pose estimation, it combines geometric compatibility with a scale-and-shift-aligned depth penalty. This plug-and-play design requires no architectural changes or retraining of the pre-trained MVT.

## Results

On ETH3D, TCO-π³ reduces mean point-map accuracy error from 0.194 (π³ base) to 0.071, and TCO-VGGT reduces it from 0.280 (VGGT base) to 0.114, both surpassing prior-aware retrained methods MapAnything (0.167) and Pow3R (0.230). On 7-Scenes and NRGBD, TCO-VGGT achieves mean Acc. of 0.020 and 0.023 respectively, halving the base VGGT errors (0.046 and 0.051) and outperforming MapAnything (0.026 and 0.040). For camera pose estimation on ScanNet (stride 1), TCO-VGGT achieves ATE of 0.0072, improving over VGGT (0.0094) and Pow3R (0.0172). The method requires only 5–80 test-time LoRA iterations on the shared decoder, with no architectural changes or retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCO consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every benchmark. Against the base image-only MVTs, TCO-π³ reduces ETH3D mean Acc. from 0.194 to 0.071 (63% reduction) and TCO-VGGT from 0.280 to 0.114 (59% reduction); similar >50% reductions appear on 7-Scenes (0.046→0.020) and NRGBD (0.051→0.023). Against the prior-aware retrained methods, TCO-VGGT (0.114) beats both MapAnything (0.167) and Pow3R (0.230) on ETH3D, and TCO-π³ (0.071) beats both by a wider margin. On DTU, where the compatibility objective is critical, TCO-VGGT with all priors achieves Acc. 0.976, outperforming Pow3R (1.234) and MapAnything (2.731). For camera pose estimation on ScanNet, TCO-VGGT achieves ATE 0.0072 at stride 1, beating VGGT (0.0094) and Pow3R (0.0172), and maintains the advantage at strides 4 and 16. The image-only baselines Fast3R, CUT3R, and FLARE are substantially weaker on ETH3D (Acc. 0.46–0.83) but competitive on NRGBD, where FLARE (0.053) is close to VGGT (0.051) before TCO's improvement to 0.023.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of prior-aware feed-forward 3D reconstruction (Pow3R, MapAnything), which modifies the MVT architecture to encode priors as additional input modalities and requires full retraining. Instead, TCO reframes priors as differentiable constraints on the network's outputs and solves a test-time constrained optimization problem, drawing on the test-time training lineage (Test3R, TTT3R) and energy-based model ideas. The cross-view compatibility objective is implemented via 2DGS-based differentiable rendering, repurposing the 2D Gaussian Splatting framework as a projection tool rather than a scene representation to be optimized. The method is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated on both VGGT and π³, and uses LoRA to fine-tune only the shared decoder while freezing all task-specific heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of TCO means any pre-trained MVT can immediately benefit from available camera or depth priors without retraining, which is practically valuable in deployment settings where the backbone or available sensor modalities change. A key open question is the test-time compute cost (5–80 optimization iterations per scene), which trades latency for accuracy and may limit real-time applications. The finding that the self-supervised compatibility objective is essential—without it, priors can actively degrade reconstruction on DTU—highlights a non-trivial interaction between prior injection and multi-view consistency that future work should explore more deeply. Extending the framework to additional prior types (e.g., sparse 3D points, semantic maps) and to dynamic scenes remains unaddressed.
