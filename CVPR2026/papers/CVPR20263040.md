## Problem

Multi-stage progressive guessing frameworks for human motion prediction (e.g., PGBIG, EqMotion) stack refinement stages with separate parameters, incurring linearly growing memory, lacking a principled stopping criterion, and introducing stage-wise inconsistency through manually designed intermediate supervision targets. This paper asks whether the progressive guessing process can be reformulated as a single implicit fixed-point problem that achieves infinite-level refinement at constant memory cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263040/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a past sequence of T_p frames of 3D joint positions (J joints × 3 coordinates per frame) and predicts future poses over T_f timestamps. The initial guess is constructed by replicating the last observed frame across the prediction horizon, while geometric and pattern features extracted from the full observed sequence are injected as external conditions at every DEQ iteration. In streaming mode, the previous round's converged fixed point serves as a warm initial guess, and a deviation signal (difference between the prior prediction and the newly available ground truth) is fed through a lightweight equivariant correction adapter.

## Method

MotionDEQ replaces the stacked, stage-specific refinement layers of EqMotion with a single shared-parameter transformation f_θ whose output is defined as the fixed point z* = f_θ(z*, x|θ), solved via Anderson mixing. To preserve the equivariant/invariant structure of the underlying motion model, the state is decomposed into equivariant geometric features and invariant pattern features, with dedicated injection layers (F_EGI, F_IPI) that incorporate observed conditions while maintaining these symmetries at every iteration. Training uses implicit differentiation for an O(1)-memory backward pass, a 2-step Neumann-series phantom gradient to approximate the inverse Jacobian, and sparse fixed-point supervision that directly aligns sampled intermediate solver states with ground truth. For streaming prediction, the converged fixed point from round r is reused as the warm initial guess for round r+1, and an equivariant correction adapter (one-layer EGNN + MLP) absorbs deviation feedback from the previous round's prediction error.

## Results

On Human3.6M (short-term, up to 400 ms), MotionDEQ achieves the lowest average MPJPE across all horizons: 8.9 mm (80 ms), 20.1 mm (160 ms), 44.1 mm (320 ms), and 55.3 mm (400 ms), using only 298 K parameters. On the more challenging 3DPW dataset (long-term, up to 1000 ms), it attains an average MPJPE of 65.95 mm, outperforming EqMotion (67.07 mm) by 3.3% at 400 ms. Training memory is reduced by over 2.3× compared to EqMotion and 4.1× compared to an 8-stage shared RNN variant, while inference time remains comparable to feed-forward baselines thanks to warm-start reuse of the previous fixed point.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionDEQ outperforms all ten baselines on every reported metric. Against the closest multi-stage refinement competitors, it reduces average MPJPE by 5.8% over PGBIG (55.3 vs 58.5 mm at 400 ms) and by 1.0% over EqMotion (55.3 vs 55.9 mm), with larger relative gains at shorter horizons (3.4% over EqMotion at 80 ms). Among recent feed-forward baselines, it beats LuKAN (57.2 mm), SiMLPe (57.3 mm), and ALIEN (58.3 mm) at 400 ms, and surpasses the classic GCN/RNN methods (MSR-GCN 62.9 mm, LTD 63.5 mm, Res-sup 115.5 mm) by wide margins. On 3DPW, the advantage over EqMotion widens to 4.6% at 200 ms and 3.3% at 400 mm, while SPGSN [30] is outperformed on both CMU-MoCap and 3DPW. In the streaming setting, the equivariant correction adapter (ECA) yields the largest gains on high-amplitude motions (e.g., walking) compared to DeFeeNet [42], confirming that deviation feedback is most informative when prediction errors are large.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reformulates the multi-stage progressive guessing paradigm (PGBIG, EqMotion) as a fixed-point problem within a Deep Equilibrium Model, replacing stage-specific parameters with a single shared transformation solved via black-box solvers (Anderson mixing). It inherits the equivariant/invariant architectural design from EqMotion and the streaming deviation-feedback concept from DeFeeNet, but departs from both by eliminating explicit stage stacking and hand-crafted intermediate supervision in favor of implicit infinite-depth refinement with sparse fixed-point correction. The work is the first to apply DEQs to human motion prediction, bridging the implicit deep learning literature (Bai et al., Geng et al.) with equivariant skeletal motion modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(1) training memory and ~300K parameter footprint make high-accuracy motion prediction feasible on resource-constrained deployment targets, and the warm-initial-guess mechanism offers a practical path to real-time streaming prediction with reduced inference latency. However, the performance gains over EqMotion are modest (1–3.4% MPJPE reduction), and the paper does not address convergence guarantees for the fixed-point solver in all motion regimes or validate the approach on multi-person or long-horizon (>1s) settings beyond 1000ms. Open questions include whether the infinite-depth formulation provides genuine representational benefits over a well-tuned finite depth, and how the method scales to more complex tasks like motion generation or multi-agent interaction.
