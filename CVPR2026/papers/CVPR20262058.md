## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on LVLMs face a fundamental trade-off: white-box attacks overfit to specific tasks and require full model access, while black-box attacks rely on expensive transfer strategies and large perturbations. Gray-box attacks targeting the shared vision encoder offer a promising middle ground, but prior methods (e.g., VEAttack, VT-Attack) maximize feature discrepancy without directional guidance, causing overfitting to a few visual attributes and poor generalization across diverse LVLM tasks. Additionally, these methods treat all patch tokens uniformly, wasting limited perturbation budget on redundant tokens.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single clean RGB image (224×224, matching CLIP's input resolution) and a pre-computed set of K=20 prototypes derived from a 3000-image guidance dataset via PCA (w=1024) and K-Means clustering. It produces a single adversarial image constrained to an ℓ∞-norm ball of ε=2/255 or 4/255, generated via 150 PGD-style steps (50 in stage one, 100 in stage two) with step size α=1/255. Crucially, the same adversarial image transfers across multiple LVLM architectures (LLaVA-1.5-7B/13B, OpenFlamingo-9B) and task types (captioning, VQA, hallucination detection) without task-specific re-optimization.

## Method

PA-Attack introduces two mechanisms on top of a standard vision-encoder cosine-similarity attack loss. First, prototype-anchored guidance selects the farthest prototype (minimum cosine similarity) from K pre-computed cluster centroids as a stable attack direction, preventing the optimization from collapsing onto a few dominant tokens and encouraging coverage of diverse visual attributes. Second, a two-stage attention enhancement mechanism weights each patch token by the softmax-normalized class-token attention from the middle encoder layer (temperature T=1/20), concentrating the perturbation budget on critical tokens; after the first 50-step stage, attention weights are recalculated from the partially-optimized adversarial image to track the shifting attention landscape. The combined loss is L = -(1/N)Σⱼ wⱼ·[−cos(vⱼ, v′ⱼ) + λ·cos(v′ⱼ, pⱼ^{k*})], optimized with sign-gradient PGD under the ℓ∞ constraint, achieving 75.1% average score reduction rate across five benchmarks and three LVLMs.

## Results

PA-Attack achieves an average Score Reduction Rate (SRR) of 77.1% (ε=2/255) and 79.0% (ε=4/255) on LLaVA1.5-7B, 63.4% and 66.4% on OpenFlamingo-9B, and 77.3% and 79.8% on LLaVA1.5-13B, across five downstream tasks (COCO, Flickr30k, TextVQA, VQAv2, POPE). Under the small perturbation budget ε=2/255, PA-Attack drives COCO CIDEr from 115.5 to 6.1 and TextVQA accuracy from 37.1 to 8.3 on LLaVA1.5-7B. The overall average SRR across all models and tasks is 75.1%, surpassing the strongest gray-box baseline VEAttack by 11.1 percentage points on LLaVA1.5-7B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PA-Attack outperforms all four gray-box baselines on every model and perturbation budget. Against the strongest baseline VEAttack [26], PA-Attack achieves +11.1% SRR on LLaVA1.5-7B (77.1% vs. 65.2% at ε=2/255) and +6.7% on LLaVA1.5-13B (77.3% vs. 67.1%), with the gap narrowing on OF-9B (63.4% vs. 52.3%). Against AttackVLM-ii [42] (a black-box-style baseline targeting the vision encoder), PA-Attack leads by +27.7% SRR on LLaVA1.5-7B and +17.3% on OF-9B at ε=2/255. VT-Attack [35] shows occasional advantages on VQAv2 under ε=4/255 (e.g., 28.3 vs. 33.9 on LLaVA-7B) but degrades sharply at ε=2/255 and fails to generalize across tasks. MIX.Attack [32] is consistently the weakest baseline, achieving only 25–32% SRR, confirming that aligning with irrelevant textual concepts in CLIP space is far less effective than the prototype-anchored and attention-enhanced strategy proposed here.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the gray-box vision-encoder attack paradigm established by VEAttack, which first showed that targeting the shared CLIP backbone with cosine-similarity minimization over patch tokens is both efficient and transferable across LVLMs. It departs from prior gray-box methods (VT-Attack, AttackVLM-ii, VEAttack) by addressing two identified failure modes: overfitting to a few visual attributes (solved via prototype-anchored guidance using K-Means on PCA-reduced features) and uniform token treatment (solved via a two-stage attention-weighted optimization). Conceptually, it repurposes clustering and prototype selection from representation learning as a mechanism to stabilize and generalize adversarial optimization direction, rather than merely maximizing feature displacement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a 2/255 perturbation targeting only the vision encoder can reduce LVLM performance by over 75% across captioning, VQA, and hallucination tasks underscores that shared vision backbones are a critical single point of failure for the entire LVLM ecosystem. The two-stage attention refinement reveals that attention patterns shift meaningfully during the attack, a dynamic that prior static-weight methods ignore. Limitations include evaluation on only three LVLM architectures, a guidance dataset drawn from the same COCO distribution as evaluation, and no defense experiments; open questions include whether prototype-based guidance transfers to vision encoders with different tokenization (e.g., dynamic-resolution models) and how to build defenses that are robust to such targeted, attribute-general perturbations.
