## Problem

Object detection in High-Resolution Wide (HRW) shots faces extreme foreground sparsity (<15% of image area), gigapixel resolutions, and highly variable target counts per scene. Existing sparse backbones like SparseFormer and SaccadeDet rely on fixed sparsity rates, wasting computation on background in sparse scenes and under-allocating in dense ones, making them unable to adapt to the wide range of target densities encountered in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261502/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes uniformly cropped 1280×800 windows from gigapixel HRW images (e.g., 25,000×16,000 on PANDA) and produces axis-aligned bounding boxes with category labels. A key operational detail is that the number of image patches processed at each backbone stage is not fixed but determined at inference time by the predicted foreground proportion, so the effective input size to local attention varies per image and per stage. In the WSOD setting, bounding-box annotations are absent and the method relies on pseudo-labels generated by the detection head itself.

## Method

ElasticFormer is a sparse Vision Transformer backbone in which each stage's local attention operates on a dynamically selected subset of windows rather than all of them. A variance-based scoring mechanism (residual between the feature map and its window-averaged interpolation) ranks windows by likely foreground content, and a lightweight two-layer MLP (ElasticSelector) predicts the overall foreground proportion ρ̂ from adaptively pooled stage features; the top ⌈ρ̂·N_w⌉ windows are retained for local attention. ElasticSelector is trained with a novel loss L_select = (ρ − ρ̂)² + γ·ρ̂ that penalizes both prediction error and over-allocation, and supports supervision from either ground-truth or model-generated pseudo bounding boxes. A 3-phase training strategy first freezes the selector to let the backbone converge on all tokens, then trains the selector in isolation, and finally enables joint end-to-end optimization with the predicted ρ̂ gating token selection. The design is backbone-agnostic: replacing self-attention with convolutions yields ElasticNet, which achieves 87% FLOPs reduction over ResNet-50 while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Results

On the PANDA gigapixel benchmark, DINO+ElasticFormer achieves 80.6% AP50 with only 13.13 GFLOPs overall backbone computation, outperforming DINO+SparseFormer (78.0% AP50 at 75.71 GFLOPs) by 2.6% AP50 while reducing FLOPs by 82.7%. Compared to the full-resolution DINO+Swin-T baseline (60.6% AP50 at 132.84 GFLOPs), ElasticFormer delivers a 20% AP50 gain at 90.2% lower computational cost. On PASCAL VOC 2007 for weakly supervised detection, Modified-DINO+ElasticFormer reaches 50.9% mAP and 63.9% CorLoc at 10.71 GFLOPs, surpassing Modified-DINO+SparseFormer (50.3% mAP at 22.12 GFLOPs) and Modified-DINO+Swin-T (49.9% mAP at 38.29 GFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ElasticFormer with DINO (80.6% AP50) outperforms SparseFormer with DINO (78.0% AP50) by 2.6% while using only 13.13 vs. 75.71 GFLOPs (82.7% reduction), and beats DINO+Swin-T (60.6% AP50) by 20% at 90.2% lower FLOPs. Against traditional CNN-based detectors on PANDA—Cascade R-CNN (310.78 GFLOPs), ClusDet (207.07 GFLOPs, 71.8% AP50), GigaDet (92.20 GFLOPs, 68.4% AP50), and PAN (207.07 GFLOPs, 71.5% AP50)—ElasticFormer achieves substantially higher AP50 at a fraction of the cost. The backbone-agnostic variant ElasticNet with DINO (75.4% AP50 at 16.54 GFLOPs) also surpasses DINO+ResNet-50 (54.2% AP50 at 124.24 GFLOPs) by 21.2% AP50 with 87% fewer FLOPs. In the WSOD setting on PASCAL VOC 2007, ElasticFormer (50.9% mAP, 63.9% CorLoc at 10.71 GFLOPs) edges out SparseFormer (50.3% mAP at 22.12 GFLOPs) and Swin-T (49.9% mAP at 38.29 GFLOPs), while all WSOD2 variants (37.9–48.9% mAP) remain below ElasticFormer. Dynamic-Head+ElasticFormer (78.2% AP50 at 13.49 GFLOPs) also beats Dynamic-Head+SparseFormer (77.1% AP50 at 64.64 GFLOPs) by 1.1% AP50 with 79% fewer FLOPs, confirming the advantage is head-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is a direct successor to SparseFormer (same first author, ACM MM 2024), which introduced fixed-ratio token sparsification in a Vision Transformer backbone for HRW detection. The key conceptual departure is replacing the static sparsity ratio with a per-stage, content-adaptive foreground proportion predicted by the lightweight ElasticSelector module, drawing on the broader dynamic-computation literature (DynamicViT, DGE, DynamicDet) but applying it specifically to window-level selection in hierarchical ViTs. The backbone-agnostic extension to CNNs (ElasticNet) and the WSOD-compatible pseudo-label supervision with a 3-phase training strategy are novel additions not present in any prior sparse-detection work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80–90% backbone FLOPs reduction while simultaneously improving AP50 over the fixed-ratio baseline is practically significant for real-time gigapixel detection in UAV inspection, satellite imagery, and medical imaging where compute is constrained. The backbone-agnostic design broadens applicability beyond Transformers, and the WSOD extension opens the method to settings where bounding-box annotations are unavailable. Open questions include how the variance-based scoring generalizes to scenes with low-contrast or texture-poor foreground, whether the 3-phase training protocol transfers cleanly to video (PANDA is a video dataset but evaluation is frame-level), and how the method behaves under extreme intra-image scale variation (e.g., a single tiny object alongside large ones).
