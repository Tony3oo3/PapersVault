## Problem

Existing In-Context-Learning based multi-subject generation methods (e.g., UNO, OmniGen) rely on a single coupled reconstruction loss to simultaneously optimize subject fidelity and multi-dimensional human preferences, which forces the model into a sub-optimal compromise. This coupled paradigm causes 'attention bleeding'—spatial entanglement of subject-specific attention fields in DiT double blocks—leading to attribute leakage and degraded identity preservation, while the indirect reconstruction signal cannot directly align aesthetic quality or text-image consistency.

## Input / Output

The method takes N reference subject images (320×320) and a free-form text prompt as input, producing a single 512×512 generated image containing all referenced subjects. Reference images are encoded into latent features and injected into the DiT attention mechanism during both training and inference; no additional spatial layout annotations or user-provided bounding boxes are required at inference time, distinguishing it from layout-guided methods like MS-Diffusion.

## Method

MultiCrafter decouples multi-subject generation into two training stages. In pre-training, Identity-Disentangled Attention Regularization computes a dice loss between the mean attention map (aggregated across all double blocks) of each reference subject and its pre-annotated spatial mask, explicitly forcing spatial disentanglement of attention regions; a Mixture-of-Experts LoRA (4 experts, top-1 routing) in the FFN layers provides the capacity to handle diverse prompt-dependent spatial layouts. In post-training, Identity-Preserving Preference Optimization (IPPO) performs online reinforcement learning using the GSPO objective with a sequence-level policy ratio over a sliding SDE window, optimizing a composite reward that combines CLIP text alignment, HPSv2 aesthetics, and a Multi-ID Alignment Reward that applies the Hungarian matching algorithm to maximize one-to-one face/object similarity between references and the generated image, preventing reward hacking via attribute leakage.

## Results

MultiCrafter achieves the highest overall average score of 0.5592 across both multi-human and multi-object generation benchmarks, outperforming all seven baselines. In multi-human generation, it leads in subject fidelity with a Face-Sim of 0.5284 (a 28.3% relative improvement over the next-best XVerse at 0.4117), DINO-I of 0.8294, and CLIP-I of 0.8524. In multi-object generation, it achieves the top CLIP-T (0.3380), DINO-I (0.7824), and CLIP-I (0.8608), while ranking second in aesthetics (AES 0.2748 vs. OmniGen2's 0.2872).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MultiCrafter outperforms all seven baselines on the overall average score (0.5592 vs. 0.5153 for the closest competitor XVerse). The most striking margin is in multi-human Face-Sim, where MultiCrafter (0.5284) surpasses XVerse (0.4117) by 28.3% relatively, and DreamO (0.3345) by 58% relatively, demonstrating the effectiveness of the Identity-Disentangled Attention Regularization in preventing attribute leakage. In multi-object generation, MultiCrafter leads in CLIP-T (0.3380 vs. OmniGen2's 0.3310), DINO-I (0.7824 vs. OmniGen2's 0.7538), and CLIP-I (0.8608 vs. OmniGen2's 0.8470), but trails OmniGen2 in aesthetics (0.2748 vs. 0.2872), which the authors attribute to training data quality. UNO, the ICL-based baseline also used for ablation, shows the weakest subject fidelity (Face-Sim 0.1474), confirming the attention bleeding problem that motivates the paper's decoupled approach. ICL-based methods (UNO, OmniGen, OmniGen2) consistently underperform in subject fidelity compared to fine-tuning-based methods (DreamO, XVerse), while MultiCrafter's two-stage decoupling achieves the best of both worlds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ICL-based multi-subject generation line (UNO, OmniGen) by diagnosing their core failure mode—attention bleeding in DiT double blocks—and proposing a decoupled two-stage training framework. The pre-training stage introduces explicit positional supervision via dice loss on attention maps, a technique borrowed from segmentation, combined with MoE-LoRA for capacity. The post-training stage adapts MixGRPO's sliding-window SDE formulation and replaces token-level GRPO ratios with GSPO's sequence-level ratios for stability under MoE routing, while introducing a Hungarian-matching-based Multi-ID Alignment Reward that is, to the authors' knowledge, the first online RL reward specifically designed for multi-subject fidelity scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling principle—treating subject fidelity and human preference alignment as separate optimization stages rather than competing objectives under a single reconstruction loss—offers a reusable architectural pattern for other multi-constraint generation tasks. The 28.3% relative Face-Sim gain over the next-best method is practically significant for identity-sensitive applications. However, the framework is validated only at 512×512 resolution on a single base model (Flux), the aesthetic score actually drops in multi-human generation (attributed to data quality), and it remains open whether the MoE-LoRA capacity requirement generalizes or is an artifact of the specific attention-regularization loss. Scaling to video or higher resolutions, and whether the decoupling gap widens or narrows with stronger base models, are natural next questions.
