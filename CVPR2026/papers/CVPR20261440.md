## Problem

Self-supervised articulated object reconstruction methods based on differentiable rendering (e.g., 3DGS) are critically dependent on the initial part segmentation: heuristic clustering or pre-trained segmentation models frequently produce incorrect splits for complex multi-part objects, causing optimization to converge to local minima with wrong kinematic structures. This brittleness is especially severe for objects with multiple adjacent movable parts, where small segmentation errors lead to large motion estimation failures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261440/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view RGBD images of an articulated object in two states (start t=0 and end t=1) as input; for real-world captures, depth is estimated via Depth Anything V2 and object masks are extracted with SAM2 from 100 images per state. The output is a 3DGS representation where each Gaussian carries a part-assignment probability field (one static + M movable parts), along with per-part kinematic parameters (joint axis, pivot center, rotation angle for revolute joints or translation vector for prismatic joints), enabling realistic motion simulation of the reconstructed digital twin.

## Method

ArtPro reframes part segmentation as a dynamic 'propose-verify-merge' process rather than a fixed initialization. It begins with over-segmented mobility proposals generated by growing regions around farthest-point-sampled seeds using PartField features, then initializes each proposal's motion via a mixed-variable optimization over OBB principal axes (joint axis, angle, translation) minimizing one-sided Chamfer distance to the end-state point cloud. During per-instance 3DGS optimization, two self-correcting mechanisms operate: (1) collision-aware motion pruning every 100 iterations detects inter-part OBB overlap growth along motion trajectories and projects out the colliding translation component or suppresses near-identity rotations; (2) adaptive proposal integration every 5K iterations merges spatially adjacent (k=8 NN) proposals whose rendered depth L1 scores change by less than a threshold when their motion parameters are exchanged, progressively consolidating over-segmented proposals into coherent movable parts. A final post-processing stage refines geometry and appearance with a collision loss between movable and static parts.

## Results

On the 8-object multi-part dataset (3–11 parts), ArtPro achieves a mean Axis Ang of 0.07° and Axis Pos of 0.00 m, compared to 22.42°/4.96 m for DTA and 8.70°/0.32 m for ArtGS (both with joint-type prediction errors marked 'F'). For geometry, ArtPro attains CD-m of 3.86 and CD-w of 0.65, dramatically outperforming ArtGS (345.31 and 2.02) and DTA (38.17 and 1.11). On the ArtGS-Multi dataset (5 objects, 4–7 parts), ArtPro again leads with Axis Ang 0.05°, CD-m 0.89, and CD-w 0.29, versus ArtGS's 0.28°, 2.14, and 0.90.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArtPro decisively outperforms both baselines across all six metrics on the multi-part dataset. Against DTA [44], ArtPro reduces Axis Ang from 22.42° to 0.07° (320× improvement) and CD-m from 38.17 to 3.86, while DTA frequently fails to identify the correct number of movable parts (marked 'F' on 4 of 8 objects). Against ArtGS [32], ArtPro achieves a 125× reduction in CD-m (3.86 vs. 345.31) and eliminates all joint-type prediction errors that plague ArtGS on 3 of 8 objects; ArtGS's clustering-based initialization causes it to converge to incorrect kinematic structures on adjacent multi-part objects. On the ArtGS-Multi dataset, ArtPro similarly dominates: CD-m of 0.89 vs. ArtGS's 2.14 and DTA's 283.49, with Axis Ang of 0.05° vs. 0.28° (ArtGS) and 26.61° (DTA). PARIS [23] and ArticulatedGS [11] are compared only in the supplementary material for two-part objects, where 3DGS-based methods (including ArtPro) produce competitive reconstruction results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ArtGS [32] and the broader 3DGS-based per-instance optimization paradigm for articulated object reconstruction (DTA [44], ArticulatedGS [11]), departing from their reliance on a fixed initial part segmentation (typically from heuristic clustering or pre-trained models). The key conceptual shift is reframing part segmentation as a dynamic 'propose-verify-merge' process rather than a one-shot initialization: over-segmented mobility proposals are generated using PartField [28] features and motion priors, then progressively merged during 3DGS optimization via motion-consistency scoring and collision-aware pruning. For real-world data, the pipeline leverages Depth Anything V2 [51] and SAM2 [39] for depth estimation and masking, positioning the method within the self-supervised, annotation-free reconstruction family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical practical bottleneck in 3DGS-based articulated reconstruction—catastrophic sensitivity to initial part segmentation—that has limited the real-world applicability of otherwise high-fidelity methods. The over-segmentation-plus-adaptive-merging strategy is a general, modular principle that could transfer to other 3DGS reconstruction tasks involving part-level structure. However, the method remains constrained to two object states, may struggle with perfectly symmetric or very subtle motions, and reconstruction quality at part boundaries is sensitive to input sensor accuracy. Open questions include whether multi-state tracking, stronger semantic priors, or geometry-aware constraints could further improve robustness and extend the approach to more complex or open-vocabulary scenarios.
