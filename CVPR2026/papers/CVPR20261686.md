## Problem

Existing portrait animation methods, whether diffusion-based or GAN-based, struggle to fully disentangle facial expression from head pose rotation, making it impossible to edit only the expression of a source video while preserving head pose, camera motion, and background. This limits their utility in film and animation production where artists need to recast a specific actor's performance without altering the rest of the shot.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261686/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source portrait video (or static image for the animation task) and a driving video at 512×512 resolution, producing an edited video where only the facial expression is modified. Two inference modes are supported: replacement mode swaps the source expression with the driving expression frame-by-frame, while enhancement mode adds the driving expression delta on top of the source expression. For portrait animation, a single source image is animated using both expression and head pose from the driving video, generating output at ~6 fps on consumer hardware.

## Method

PerformRecast builds on LivePortrait's warping-based GAN architecture but modifies the keypoints transformation formula from s·(x_c·R + δ) + t to s·((x_c + δ)·R) + t, aligning the order of operations with FLAME's forward process so that expression deformation is applied before head pose rotation, preventing information leakage between the two. The motion extractor is supervised with a FLAME loss using 49 explicit 3D keypoints extracted from the FLAME mesh via Pixel3DMM tracking, replacing LivePortrait's implicit keypoints and their associated equivariance/prior losses. A Boundary Alignment Module pre-trains a teacher model for facial expression synthesis, then trains the student with separate perceptual and L1 losses on facial regions (against the teacher output) and non-facial regions (against the ground truth source), eliminating boundary misalignment. The appearance feature extractor uses a DINOv2 backbone, and the improved disentanglement eliminates the need for LivePortrait's second-stage stitching and retargeting modules.

## Results

On the MetaHuman Expression Editing Benchmark (replacement mode), PerformRecast achieves PSNR 29.27, SSIM 0.914, LPIPS 0.047, CSIM 0.761, MAE 9.12°, AED 0.499, APD 0.012, FID 12.0, and FVD 103.0, outperforming all 18 baselines on every metric. In enhancement mode, it further improves to PSNR 30.27, SSIM 0.922, LPIPS 0.039, and FVD 90.2. On the portrait animation task (self-reenactment on VFHQ), PerformRecast achieves the best CSIM (0.843), MAE (5.0°), AED (0.261), APD (0.009), FID (20.2), and FVD (164.2), trailing only LivePortrait on PSNR (22.71 vs. 22.88).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PerformRecast outperforms all 18 baselines on every reported metric in both replacement and enhancement modes of expression editing. The closest competitor is LivePortrait (the base architecture), which trails by 1.54 dB in PSNR, 0.015 in SSIM, 1.35° in MAE, and 62.1 in FVD on replacement mode; the key gap is in expression fidelity (AED 0.610 vs. 0.499) and pose preservation (APD 0.016 vs. 0.012). Diffusion-based methods (SkyReels-A1, Hunyuan-Portrait, FantasyPortrait, Wan-Animate) are substantially worse, with FVD values 6–19× higher than PerformRecast, confirming their inability to perform expression-only editing without head pose leakage. The commercial product Runway Act-Two also underperforms, with the worst APD (0.072) and MAE (15.96°), indicating poor head pose preservation. On the portrait animation task (Table 2), PerformRecast achieves the best CSIM (0.843), MAE (5.0°), AED (0.261), and FVD (164.2) in self-reenactment, and the best AED (0.703) and APD (0.030) in cross-reenactment, while EMOPortrait reports slightly lower MAE (10.39° vs. 10.96°) and APD (0.0245 vs. 0.0303) but suffers severe background distortion. Overall, the FLAME-consistent keypoints transformation and boundary alignment module yield consistent gains across all evaluation settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

PerformRecast is built directly on LivePortrait's warping-based GAN architecture but makes a targeted conceptual departure: it reorders the keypoints transformation so that expression deformation is added to canonical keypoints *before* head-pose rotation (matching FLAME's forward model), rather than after, as in LivePortrait. This is paired with explicit 3D FLAME-mesh keypoints (tracked via Pixel3DMM) to supervise the motion extractor, replacing the implicit, physically-undefined keypoints used in prior warping-based methods (LivePortrait, Face Vid2vid, EMOPortrait). The paper also introduces a teacher-student boundary alignment module absent from all prior portrait animation work, addressing a practical failure mode (facial/non-facial misalignment) that becomes critical when only expression is edited while head pose is held fixed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper carves out a practically important sub-task—expression-only video editing for film and animation post-production—that prior portrait animation methods cannot handle because they entangle expression with head pose. The MetaHuman-based benchmark (20 digital humans × 19 rendered videos with controlled head pose) is a useful and reproducible evaluation resource that the community lacked. Limitations include the 512×512 resolution cap inherent to the GAN/SPADE decoder, dependence on FLAME tracking quality for extreme poses or occlusions, and a synthetic benchmark that may not fully capture real-world lighting and texture diversity. Open questions include whether the FLAME-consistent transformation and explicit keypoint supervision transfer to higher-resolution or diffusion-based backbones, and whether the boundary alignment module generalizes to multi-person or heavily occluded scenes.
