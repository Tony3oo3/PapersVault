## Problem

Computer-using agents require large-scale, high-quality UI interaction trajectories for training, but manual annotation is prohibitively expensive (AgentNet cost $32K for 22K tasks over six months) and existing automatic video-to-trajectory pipelines (MONDAY, TongUI) suffer from cascading errors in multi-stage heuristic processing or brittle MLLM-based action grounding. The paper asks whether web-scale human demonstration videos can be converted into accurate, executable trajectories without handcrafted parsing or manual labeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262592/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The IDM takes two consecutive raw screen screenshots (vision-only, no HTML or accessibility trees) and predicts a single atomic action from a six-primitive space (Click, Release, Scroll, Type, Wait, Move). Coordinates are discretized into 1000 integer bins per axis (0-999) rather than regressed in continuous space, and typed text is generated autoregressively via a GPT-2 decoder conditioned on the visual transition. The full pipeline consumes YouTube tutorial videos (sampled at 1 fps) and task descriptions, producing 53,125 structured trajectories that serve either as few-shot in-context exemplars (augmented with Gemini-generated reasoning traces) or as (state, action) sequences for supervised fine-tuning.

## Method

The key insight is to recast trajectory annotation as an inverse dynamics problem: given two consecutive observations (O_t, O_{t+1}), predict the intermediate action a_t, which is a one-step prediction far easier to learn than generating entire trajectories. The IDM uses a SigLIP-2 vision encoder followed by four Transformer layers with three specialized heads (categorical action classifier, 1000-bin coordinate classifier, and GPT-2 text decoder), trained with a multi-task cross-entropy objective on 600K+ automatically collected web state transitions. A task-aware retrieval module (using Gemini 2.5 Flash for query refinement and video filtering) locates relevant YouTube tutorials, and the trained IDM labels every consecutive frame pair to assemble executable trajectories. These trajectories are then deployed in two complementary modes: as in-context exemplars that provide planning and grounding priors at inference time, and as supervised training data for fine-tuning open-source CUAs (UI-TARS-1.5-7B, Qwen 2.5-VL 7B).

## Results

On OSWorld-Verified (50-step limit), W&L trajectories yield consistent gains across all model categories: in-context use improves general-purpose models by +1.6 to +3.0 points (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Flash 19.0→22.0, o3 21.8→24.3, Claude 4 Sonnet 43.9→45.5) and the Jedi agentic framework by +2.2 points (50.6→52.8). Supervised fine-tuning on the 53K trajectories produces larger boosts for open-source CUAs: Qwen 2.5-VL 7B jumps from 1.9% to 13.0% (+11.1) and UI-TARS-1.5-7B from 27.3% to 31.1% (+3.8). On WindowsAgentArena (15-step limit), fine-tuning UI-TARS-1.5-7B with W&L trajectories achieves 24.0% success rate, the highest among 7B-scale models, surpassing UltraCUA-7B (21.7%) and OpenCUA-7B (13.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TongUI (the primary automatic-labeling baseline), W&L wins on every comparison: on OSWorld ICL, W&L exemplars improve o3 by +2.5 while TongUI exemplars slightly degrade it (−0.7), and W&L improves Claude 4 Sonnet by +1.6 while TongUI drops it by −0.5. On OSWorld SFT, the gap widens dramatically—W&L lifts Qwen 2.5-VL by +11.1 versus TongUI's +3.5, and UI-TARS-1.5-7B by +3.8 versus TongUI's −3.5. On WindowsAgentArena, W&L-finetuned UI-TARS-1.5-7B reaches 24.0% while TongUI-finetuned drops to 12.9% (−5.2 from zero-shot), and W&L also outperforms the specialized CUA baselines UltraCUA-7B (21.7%) and OpenCUA-7B (13.5%). As a labeling model, the W&L IDM achieves 95.8% ActionType accuracy and 91.7% Action accuracy on the held-out test set, substantially outperforming both Gemini 2.5 Flash (81.5%/70.5%) and TongUI's UI-TARS-based labeling (84.3%/72.3%), confirming that the accuracy advantage in downstream tasks stems from superior action annotation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the inverse dynamics formulation from robotics (VPT, DreamGen) to the computer-use domain, where prior trajectory synthesis methods (MONDAY, TongUI, Explorer, OS-Genesis) relied on multi-stage heuristic pipelines or MLLM-based action labeling that suffer from cascading errors. The key departure is casting UI action recovery as a one-step prediction problem from consecutive screen states, trained on a 600K-transition web interaction corpus, rather than prompting a general-purpose MLLM to parse transitions. It also positions itself within the in-context learning literature for agents, showing that video-derived trajectories serve dual roles as both SFT data and inference-time exemplars, complementing prior data-centric frameworks that generate synthetic data through exploration or self-play.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core scalability bottleneck in CUA research: manual annotation costs ~$1.45 per task, making million-scale datasets prohibitive. By demonstrating that web-scale tutorial videos can be automatically converted into high-quality executable trajectories via a learned IDM, it provides a practical, near-zero-annotation-cost path to large-scale supervision. Open questions include whether the IDM generalizes to applications not represented in the 69-app training distribution, how the approach scales to long-horizon multi-application workflows, and whether the trajectories can serve as effective demonstrations for reinforcement learning. The finding that noisy MLLM-based labels (TongUI) actively degrade performance on Windows tasks underscores that label quality is a critical, underappreciated factor in CUA training.
