## Problem

Existing depth-based category-level pose estimation methods rely almost exclusively on geometric features of point clouds, neglecting topological structure (e.g., loops, voids shared within a category) and SE(3)-equivariance (the property that rotating/translating the input should rotate/translate the predicted pose identically). This paper is the first to simultaneously incorporate geometric, topological, and SE(3)-equivariant information into category-level pose estimation, addressing the gap that no prior method leverages all three properties together.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a depth image per frame; the RGB image is used solely for instance segmentation via Mask-RCNN, while the depth image is cropped by the mask to yield a 1024-point object point cloud that drives all downstream processing. The network outputs a 6D pose (rotation R∈SO(3) decomposed into two vectors, translation t∈R³, and 3D size s∈R³) together with a reconstructed point cloud in NOCS space, which is then aligned to the category NOCS template for pose refinement.

## Method

SEGPose has three coupled components. First, a geometry-topology-guided reconstruction module uses the HS-encoder backbone plus a transformer-based graph module (adapted from CatFormer) to predict 1D and 2D persistence images, constrained by both per-object topological labels (computed via alpha-complex persistent homology with regularized Gaussian kernels and persistence-weighted patch integration) and category-level topological priors, then feeds the resulting features into an MLP reconstructor. Second, an SE(3)-equivariance encoding module built from stacked VNN-DGCNN layers extracts equivariant vector features, which are fused with the geometric features through element-wise products and shape-weighted point cloud adjustment. Third, an SE(3)-equivariance-guided pose head uses the equivariant features to generate Sigmoid-gated feature weights that modulate the point cloud features before an MLP predicts rotation, translation, and scale. Training is supervised with a composite loss combining pose/symmetry terms, point-cloud and category topology losses (with an exponential balancing factor), NOCS alignment via Density Chamfer Distance, feature-consistency and reconstruction losses for data augmentation, and an SDF-based bounding-box-pose consistency term.

## Results

On CAMERA25, SEGPose achieves IoU50 of 93.7%, 5°5cm of 82.9%, and 10°5cm of 91.2%, outperforming all depth-based methods (best prior: HRC-Pose at 93.6% IoU50). On REAL275, SEGPose reaches IoU50 of 83.1%, 5°5cm of 61.1%, and 10°5cm of 87.1%, surpassing the previous depth-based SOTA (HRC-Pose: 83.4% IoU50, 58.6% 5°5cm, 85.4% 10°5cm). On the Wild6D generalization benchmark, SEGPose attains 72.2% IoU50 and 48.1% 10°5cm mAP, exceeding the best depth-based method HS-Pose (58.1% IoU50, 41.6% 10°5cm) by a wide margin. In real-robot grasping, SEGPose achieves 88.9% success on single-object grasping and 84.4% under external disturbances, outperforming HS-Pose (75.6% and 75.6% respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among depth-based methods, SEGPose outperforms all 10 compared baselines on both CAMERA25 and REAL275. Against the closest depth-based competitor HRC-Pose [18], SEGPose gains +0.1 IoU50 on CAMERA25 (93.7 vs 93.6) and +2.5 points on 5°5cm on REAL275 (61.1 vs 58.6), while matching on 10°5cm (91.2). Against TG-Pose [43], which also uses topological information, SEGPose improves 5°5cm by +3.9 on CAMERA25 (82.9 vs 81.9) and +2.1 on REAL275 (61.1 vs 59.0), demonstrating that combining topology with SE(3)-equivariance yields gains beyond topology alone. Against HS-Pose [46] (the HS-encoder backbone source), SEGPose improves IoU50 by +0.4 on CAMERA25 and +1.0 on REAL275, with a larger gap on rotation-sensitive metrics (5°5cm: +2.4 on C25, +5.9 on R275). Among RGB-D methods, SEGPose trails only SpotPose [31] (94.3 vs 93.7 IoU50 on C25) and AG-Pose [23] (93.8 vs 93.7), but runs at 45.8 FPS versus SpotPose's undisclosed speed and AG-Pose's 26.8 FPS. On Wild6D, SEGPose leads all depth-based methods by a large margin (72.2% IoU50 vs 58.1% for HS-Pose), and in robotic grasping it surpasses HS-Pose by 13.3 points on single-object grasping (88.9% vs 75.6%) and 8.8 points under external disturbances (84.4% vs 75.6%).

## Relation to Prior Work

SEGPose builds directly on the HS-encoder from HS-Pose [46] as its geometric feature extraction backbone, adapts the transformer-based graph module from CatFormer [41] for topological feature analysis, and employs Vector Neuron Networks (VNN-DGCNN) [9] for SE(3)-equivariant feature encoding. It departs from prior depth-based category-level pose estimation methods (GPV-Pose, SAR-Net, HS-Pose) that rely predominantly on geometric features, and from TG-Pose [43] which introduced topology but ignored equivariance, by simultaneously integrating geometric constraints, topological constraints (via persistence images from alpha complexes), and SE(3)-equivariance into a unified pipeline. The paper positions itself as the first to combine all three properties in category-level pose estimation, though each individual component is drawn from established subfields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation—replacing RGB-dependent methods with depth-only approaches for low-light and dynamic robotic scenarios—is well-motivated, and the SE(3)-equivariance framing addresses a genuine gap in how prior methods handle viewpoint changes during grasping. However, the improvements over the strongest depth-based baselines (HRC-Pose, TG-Pose) on CAMERA25 and REAL275 are modest (1–3 points on rotation/translation metrics), and the method still trails the SOTA RGB-D method (SpotPose) by a meaningful margin. Open questions include whether the topological label computation (alpha complex + persistence images) scales to more complex or larger point clouds, whether the approach generalizes beyond the six object categories tested, and whether the equivariance benefit persists when the input point cloud is heavily occluded or incomplete.
