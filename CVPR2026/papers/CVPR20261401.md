## Problem

Existing shadow removal methods achieve strong results on single-light direct shadow benchmarks but fail under multi-source ambient lighting with diffuse indirect illumination and chromatic shifts. The root cause is prior misalignment: geometric features (depth, normals) respond to local shading variations while semantic features (object identity) remain stable across lighting, and without proper alignment these conflicting signals degrade boundary localization and color recovery in complex scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261401/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single shadowed RGB image (256×256 crops during training, 640×480 at inference) and produces a shadow-free RGB image of the same resolution. No shadow masks are required; instead, frozen pretrained models (DepthAnything-v2 for depth and surface normals, DINO-v2 for semantic embeddings) provide auxiliary geometric and semantic priors computed on-the-fly from the input. The pipeline is fully mask-free, making it applicable to real-world deployment where automatic shadow detection is unreliable.

## Method

PhaSR performs dual-level physically aligned prior alignment. At the global level, Physically Aligned Normalization (PAN) applies a parameter-free closed-form pipeline: Gray-world color normalization to remove chromatic bias, log-domain Retinex decomposition (log I = log R + log S) to separate reflectance from illumination via spatial averaging in log-space, and dynamic range recombination to produce an illumination-consistent input. At the local level, Geometric-Semantic Rectification Attention (GSRA) extends differential attention to cross-modal alignment: depth-derived geometric features and DINO-v2 semantic embeddings are injected into separate key-value streams, and a shared query computes two attention maps whose difference (A_rect = A_sem − λ·A_geo) rectifies semantic over-smoothing at illumination boundaries while preserving geometric precision. The backbone is a hierarchical Transformer encoder-decoder (base channel 32, 2 layers per block across 7 stages) trained end-to-end with Charbonnier and SSIM losses under supervised learning, achieving 55.63 GFLOPs and 18.95 M parameters.

## Results

PhaSR achieves the best PSNR on ISTD (30.73 dB), WSRD+ (28.44 dB), and Ambient6K (23.32 dB), with the largest margin on WSRD+ where it outperforms the next-best method (DenseSR, 26.28 dB) by 2.16 dB. On ISTD+, PhaSR reaches 34.48 dB PSNR, trailing only StableShadowDiffusion (35.19 dB), while on INS it scores 30.38 dB, slightly behind DenseSR (30.64 dB). On the challenging Ambient6K benchmark with multi-source indoor lighting, PhaSR surpasses dedicated ambient-light-normalization methods (IFBlend 21.44, RLN²-Lf 21.71, ReHiT 19.98) by 1.6–3.3 dB. The model also maintains the lowest FLOPs (55.63 G) and second-smallest parameter count (18.95 M) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhaSR achieves the highest PSNR on three of five benchmarks: ISTD (30.73 vs. DenseSR 30.64), WSRD+ (28.44 vs. DenseSR 26.28, a 2.16 dB gap), and Ambient6K (23.32 vs. OmniSR 23.01). On WSRD+, the margin over all prior methods is substantial—exceeding the next-best DenseSR by over 2 dB and ReHiT by 2.29 dB—indicating that the physically aligned prior alignment is particularly effective under real-world indoor single-source lighting. On Ambient6K, PhaSR outperforms dedicated ambient-light-normalization methods (IFBlend 21.44, RLN²-Lf 21.71, ReHiT 19.98) by 1.6–3.3 dB, demonstrating generalization beyond single-light shadow removal. However, PhaSR trails StableShadowDiffusion on ISTD+ (34.48 vs. 35.19 dB) and DenseSR on INS (30.38 vs. 30.64 dB), suggesting that diffusion-based refinement and dense-prediction formulations retain an edge on synthetic or single-light outdoor benchmarks. Among transformer-based non-diffusion methods, PhaSR leads or matches the best on all five datasets, and its efficiency (55.63 G FLOPs, 18.95 M params, 87.9 ms/inference) is the best among all compared architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhaSR extends the Differential Transformer's subtraction-based attention mechanism from intra-modal noise reduction to cross-modal rectification between depth-derived geometry (DepthAnything-v2) and semantic embeddings (DINO-v2), a departure from prior geometric-semantic fusion strategies in OmniSR and DenseSR that rely on adaptive or learned fusion. The Physically Aligned Normalization module follows the Retinex-guided shadow removal line (ReHiT, RLN², IFBlend) but replaces learned illumination decomposition with closed-form Gray-world normalization and log-domain operations, positioning itself as a parameter-free preprocessing step rather than a learned component. The paper's central conceptual framing—that geometric and semantic priors encode conflicting signals requiring explicit alignment rather than simple concatenation—distinguishes it from prior work that treats these priors as complementary but independent inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: most shadow removal methods are validated on single-light outdoor benchmarks, yet real-world deployment increasingly involves indoor multi-source ambient lighting where prior methods degrade. The demonstration that a parameter-free Retinex-based preprocessing (PAN) yields consistent 0.15–0.34 dB gains when plugged into existing architectures (OmniSR, DenseSR) makes it a low-cost drop-in improvement for practitioners. However, the gains over the strongest baselines (DenseSR, OmniSR) remain modest on standard benchmarks (ISTD+, INS), with the most notable margin appearing on WSRD+ (~2 dB), and the generalization to ALN, while promising, is evaluated on a single dataset (Ambient6K). Open questions include whether the cross-modal differential rectification scales to video or 3D reconstruction settings, and whether the frozen DINO-v2/DepthAnything-v2 priors remain effective under domain shifts beyond the tested indoor/outdoor split.
