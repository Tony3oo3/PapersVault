## Problem

Nearly all MLLM-based segmentation methods depend on specialist mask decoders (e.g., SAM, Mask2Former) or multiple [SEG] tokens to produce masks, adding external parameters and complexity. The fundamental bottleneck for decoder-free segmentation is that pixel-shuffle downsampling in modern MLLMs substantially reduces spatial resolution, losing the fine-grained detail needed for accurate pixel-level masks. This paper asks whether a single [SEG] token, with no specialist decoder, can achieve competitive segmentation by better exploiting the MLLM's own high-resolution features.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a natural language referring expression as input and produces a binary segmentation mask at the original image resolution. The mask is generated by a dot-product between high-resolution image features (at the uncompressed N₀-pixel resolution from the image encoder, not the compressed N₁ resolution used by the LLM) and a single post-processed [SEG] embedding. For open-vocabulary segmentation, each category is queried individually and pixels are assigned to the highest-scoring category.

## Method

SELF1E builds on InternVL3-2B/8B and introduces three key mechanisms. First, Residual Features Refilling (RFR) replicates each image-encoder pixel feature α times and applies the same compression MLP to recover uncompressed-resolution features F_V1^HQ, then adds upsampled LLM residual features (F_IMG − F_V1) to inject semantic granularity. Second, the Residual Features Amplifier (RFA) applies separate MLP-with-Pixel-Unshuffle operations to the compressed features and the LLM-processed features, taking their difference to produce amplified residuals at even higher resolution, which are fused with F_V1^HQ via pixel-unshuffle. Third, a segmentation-specific attention mask enables bidirectional interaction among [IMG] tokens and [IMG]→[SEG] visibility, allowing the single [SEG] token to gather spatial information from all image tokens while preserving causal inference for VQA. The model is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 128 for 2B, 64 for 8B) using a combined cross-entropy + BCE + DICE loss on segmentation and VQA data.

## Results

On Referring Expression Segmentation, SELF1E-SEG-8B achieves 86.2% cIoU on RefCOCO testA, 84.2% on RefCOCO+ testA, and 82.8% on RefCOCOg test, outperforming the previous best decoder-free method UFO-8B(ft) by 3.6%, 3.8%, and 5.5% respectively. The 2B variant (SELF1E-SEG-2B) already reaches 85.4% on RefCOCO testA, surpassing most 7B/8B models that rely on specialist mask decoders. On gRefCOCO (zero-shot), SELF1E-8B achieves 44.4% cIoU on val, setting a new SOTA among zero-shot methods. On Reasoning Segmentation, SELF1E-8B establishes new SOTA with 65.9% gIoU and 69.7% cIoU on val, improving over HiMTok-8B by 5.2% gIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SELF1E-SEG-8B outperforms the strongest decoder-free baseline UFO-8B(ft) by 3.6–5.5% cIoU across all RefCOCO/+/g splits, and also surpasses specialist-decoder methods HiMTok-8B(ft) on RefCOCO+ testA (84.2 vs. 82.7) and RefCOCOg test (82.8 vs. 80.6), though HiMTok-8B(ft) retains a marginal 0.3% edge on RefCOCO val (85.0 vs. 84.7). The 2B variant SELF1E-SEG-2B (85.4% RefCOCO testA) already exceeds all 7B specialist-decoder baselines such as LISA-7B(ft) (78.9%), GSVA-7B(ft) (78.9%), and GLaMM (83.2%), demonstrating that the resolution-preserving design compensates for model scale. On gRefCOCO (zero-shot), SELF1E-8B (44.4% cIoU val) beats the previous best zero-shot method PSALM (42.0%) and LIRA-8B (40.9%). On Reasoning Segmentation, SELF1E-8B (65.9% gIoU / 69.7% cIoU val) surpasses HiMTok-8B (60.7% / 67.0%) by 5.2% gIoU, and even SELF1E-2B (59.4% / 68.8%) matches or exceeds HiMTok-8B on cIoU. On open-vocabulary segmentation, SELF1E-2B achieves 50.2 mIoU on ADE20k-150, nearly doubling HiMTok-8B (25.0) and PSALM (18.2), though HyperSeg leads on Pascal Context-59 (64.6 tied) and Pascal VOC-20 (92.1 vs. 89.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-based segmentation lineage initiated by LISA (which introduced the [SEG] token paradigm) and UFO (which first removed the specialist mask decoder but required 16 [SEG] tokens). It departs from both by identifying the pixel-shuffle resolution compression in MLLMs—not the single-token constraint—as the fundamental bottleneck for decoder-free segmentation, and addresses it through Residual Features Refilling (RFR) and Residual Features Amplifier (RFA) that fuse high-resolution encoder features with LLM-processed residual features. The base architecture is InternVL3, and the mask prediction mechanism (dot-product between image and [SEG] embeddings) is inherited directly from UFO, but the feature engineering pipeline is substantially redesigned.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating both the specialist mask decoder and the need for multiple [SEG] tokens, SELF1E simplifies the MLLM segmentation pipeline and removes dependency on external foundation models like SAM or Mask2Former, which is practically significant for deployment and parameter efficiency. The conceptual reframing—that spatial resolution loss from pixel-shuffle, not token count, is the core limitation—opens a clear direction for future MLLM segmentation work. Open questions include whether the RFR/RFA resolution-recovery strategy generalizes to other MLLM backbones with different compression schemes, whether it extends to video or 3D segmentation, and whether the remaining gap to the strongest specialist-decoder methods (e.g., HiMTok-8B(ft)) can be closed without reintroducing external components.
