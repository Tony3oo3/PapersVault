## Problem

Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR) methods, while memory-efficient, consistently underperform feature matching approaches in large-scale scenes due to two unaddressed issues: (1) implicit triangulation supervision is weak or absent for the majority of single-view points in sparsely observed environments, and (2) the standard concatenation of global retrieval features with local matching features introduces an inherent objective misalignment that degrades discriminative power.

## Input / Output

The method takes only RGB training images with known camera poses and intrinsics (no explicit 3D model or ground-truth 3D coordinates required) and produces a compact neural network (~47 MB) that regresses per-pixel 3D scene coordinates from a query image. The final 6-DoF pose is recovered via PnP-and-RANSAC from the predicted 2D-3D correspondences. At test time, a single forward pass through the network yields the full scene coordinate map, avoiding any retrieval or matching step.

## Method

CoLoR introduces a two-stage training framework that explicitly partitions scene points into multi-view and single-view sets via efficient top-K co-visibility matching. In stage one, multi-view points are trained with a strong multi-view reprojection loss that couples lateral and depth coordinates across views; in stage two, the stage-one model generates sparse high-confidence depth maps that are densified by a depth completion model to produce pseudo-depth supervision for single-view points, extending strong supervision to the entire scene. Additionally, the paper re-frames the local feature encoder as a pixel-level retrieval feature (rather than a matching feature) and trains it with a MoCo-style contrastive objective against negatives from different scenes, resolving the objective inconsistency with the global image-level retrieval feature and yielding a unified multi-granularity retrieval representation.

## Results

On Aachen Day-Night, CoLoR achieves 78.6% / 89.4% / 96.8% at (0.25 m, 2°) / (0.5 m, 5°) / (5 m, 10°) during day and 67.3% / 88.8% / 96.9% at night, using only a 47 MB map. This outperforms the direct SCR baseline R-SCoRe by +3.8% (day) and +3.0% (night) at the strictest threshold, while the best FM method (HLoc+SPSG) reaches 89.6% / 86.7% but requires a 7.82 GB map. On Hyundai Department Store 4F, CoLoR achieves 80.9% at (0.1 m, 1°), a 20.7-point improvement over R-SCoRe (60.2%) and approaching HLoc+D2-Net (84.2%) with a 50 MB map versus 183 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoLoR outperforms its direct SCR baseline R-SCoRe on every metric across both datasets: +3.8% at (0.25 m, 2°) on Aachen Day and +20.7% at (0.1 m, 1°) on Hyundai 4F, while maintaining the identical 47 MB / 50 MB map size. Against the strongest prior SCR method NeuMap, CoLoR surpasses it by +2.2% (day) and +19.3% (night) on Aachen with a 27× smaller map (47 MB vs. 1.26 GB). Against the best FM method HLoc+SPSG, CoLoR still trails by 11.0% (day) and 19.4% (night) on Aachen, but closes the gap from ~15% under R-SCoRe and does so with a 166× smaller map (47 MB vs. 7.82 GB). On Hyundai 1F, CoLoR (75.2%) nearly matches HLoc+D2-Net (78.0%) at (0.1 m, 1°) with a 3,054× smaller map (127 MB vs. 398 GB), while on 4F it reaches 80.9% versus HLoc+D2-Net's 84.2%. All other SCR baselines (HSCNet, HSCNet++, GLACE, ACE, ESAC) are substantially outperformed, with GLACE and ACE showing particularly large gaps (e.g., CoLoR 78.6% vs. GLACE 8.6% on Aachen Day), confirming that the two-stage training and retrieval feature design are critical for large-scale performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoLoR builds directly on R-SCoRe's architecture and training configuration but fundamentally reworks the training paradigm inherited from ACE's Gradient Decorrelation Training. Where ACE and its successors (GLACE, R-SCoRe) rely on implicit triangulation via single-view reprojection loss, CoLoR replaces this with explicit multi-view reprojection supervision for co-visible points and a depth-completion-bootstrapped pseudo-depth loss for single-view points. The pixel-level retrieval encoder repurposes DeDoDe's architecture under a MoCo-style contrastive objective, reframing local features from a matching-oriented to a retrieval-oriented perspective to resolve an identified inconsistency with global image descriptors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear diagnosis of why SCR has consistently underperformed feature matching at scale—sparse co-visibility renders implicit triangulation weak or absent for the majority of scene points—and offers a principled two-stage solution that narrows the accuracy gap to near-FM levels while retaining a 47 MB map (vs. 7.82 GB for HLoc+SPSG). The feature-inconsistency finding (global AP 0.924 degrading to 0.816 when concatenated with matching-oriented local features) is a useful diagnostic for the broader SCR community. Open questions include whether the depth-completion dependency introduces systematic bias in textureless or highly dynamic regions, and whether the approach extends to truly unbounded or continuously changing environments without periodic retraining.
