## Problem

In Transformer-based 3D instance segmentation, multiple learnable queries frequently target the same instance, producing fragmented masks and slow convergence. The authors formalize this as inter-query competition, driven by spatial overlap and the absence of a clear dominance hierarchy, and show that existing implicit attention-based modeling is insufficient to resolve these conflicts explicitly.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262845/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw 3D point cloud (P x 9: xyz coordinates, RGB color, surface normals) processed by a Sparse UNet backbone into point-wise features, which are then aggregated into M scene primitives (superpoints or voxels). A fixed set of N learnable queries is refined across a multi-layer decoder to produce per-instance masks and classification scores. Unusually, the decoder leverages its own previous-layer predictions (pairwise mask IoU and confidence scores) as explicit supervisory signals for the current layer's competitive structure.

## Method

CompetitorFormer inserts three synergistic modules into a standard Transformer decoder. The Query Competition Layer (QCL), placed before each decoder stage, identifies each query's strongest spatial competitor via IoU and assigns a dominance label from confidence scores, then fuses learnable dominant/subordinate embeddings into the query feature via an MLP. The Relative Relationship Encoding (RRE) multiplies the rank and IoU matrices, quantizes the result into 70 discrete bins (step 0.02), and retrieves semantic-aware bias vectors from lookup tables to modulate self-attention scores. The Rank Cross-Attention (RCA) applies column-wise min-max normalization to the query-superpoint similarity matrix so that queries compete for each superpoint, then element-wise multiplies the normalized scores back into the original similarity before row-wise softmax, enforcing mutual exclusivity in feature aggregation.

## Results

CompetitorFormer achieves state-of-the-art mAP on all four benchmarks: 63.4 mAP (81.6 mAP50) on ScanNetV2 validation, 34.1 mAP (44.1 mAP50) on ScanNet200, 73.8 mAP50 on S3DIS Area 5 (77.7 mAP50 under 6-fold CV), and 34.1 mAP (48.5 mAP50) on ScanNet++V2 validation. On the ScanNetV2 hidden test set it reports 62.9 mAP, and on ScanNet++V2 test 33.5 mAP. The method also converges faster, reaching 62.0 mAP at epoch 300 versus epoch 450 for the Relation3D baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CompetitorFormer outperforms all 17 compared baselines on every dataset where results are reported. Against the strongest recent transformer baselines, it gains +0.9 mAP over Relation3D [22] on ScanNetV2 Val (63.4 vs 62.5), +0.5 mAP over IKNE [26] (63.4 vs 62.9), and +2.2 mAP over DCD [34] (63.4 vs 61.2). The margins are largest on the more challenging long-tail and dense datasets: +2.5 mAP over Relation3D on ScanNet200 (34.1 vs 31.6) and +5.6 mAP over DCD on ScanNet++V2 Val (34.1 vs 28.5). On S3DIS, it leads by +1.3 mAP50 over Relation3D on Area 5 (73.8 vs 72.5) and +0.8 mAP50 over IKNE (73.8 vs 73.0). Older grouping-based methods (PointGroup, SoftGroup, HAIS) trail by 15–25 mAP, underscoring the advantage of the query-based transformer paradigm that CompetitorFormer builds upon.

## Relation to Prior Work

CompetitorFormer sits within the transformer-based 3D instance segmentation lineage (SPFormer, Mask3D, QueryFormer) and specifically addresses the inter-query competition problem that prior relational methods (Relation3D, IKNE, EASE-DETR, DCD) have attempted to mitigate through implicit attention biases or post-hoc feature aggregation. Its key departure is making competition explicit and structured: rather than learning an all-to-all relation graph or correcting fragmented masks after the fact, it constructs a per-query 'competitive landscape' from IoU overlaps and confidence rankings, then injects this awareness at three distinct stages (feature-level via QCL, self-attention via RRE, cross-attention via RCA). The design repurposes the competitive-suppression idea from EASE-DETR but elevates it from a single learnable bias to a multi-stage, interpretable hierarchy grounded in spatial and confidence signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit competition modeling offers a reusable inductive bias for any fixed-query transformer decoder, and the demonstrated convergence speedup (reaching 62.0 mAP at epoch 300 vs. 450 for the baseline) is practically valuable for training budgets. The +5.6 mAP gain on ScanNet++V2 suggests the approach scales well to denser, more cluttered scenes where query conflicts are most severe. However, the method is tied to a fixed-query architecture and depends on previous-layer mask predictions to bootstrap the competitive landscape, leaving open questions about cold-start behavior at the first decoder layer, applicability to dynamic-query or 2D detection settings, and whether the gains persist under different backbones or point densities.
