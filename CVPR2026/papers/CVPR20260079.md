## Problem

Existing VLMs process chart and table images through a single visual encoder that implicitly entangles structural cues (axes, grids, layouts) with fine-grained details (values, legends, colors), lacking an explicit inductive bias for chart-table reading. Additionally, current models remain insufficiently sensitive to numerical tokens, producing unreliable values, and existing benchmarks cover too few chart types and task formats to fully evaluate model capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260079/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes an RGB chart or table image together with a free-form text question. Internally, the image is duplicated: the raw image and a Canny edge-detected structural version are both fed through a shared image encoder. Outputs span three formats—natural-language answers (short ≤256, medium ≤1024, long ≤4096 tokens), LaTeX table code, and Python chart-reconstruction code—across 17 chart types and 11 task types in the TwintVQA benchmark.

## Method

Twin-T is trained in two stages on Ovis2-1B / Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbones. Stage 1 introduces a dual-head image encoder: one head encodes the raw image (E_Img) and the other encodes a Canny structural image (E_Stru); a structure-aware soft gate (sigmoid of embedding norm minus a threshold) adaptively weights each token, and a Schur-style projection subtracts the structural direction from E_Img to yield a purified detail embedding E_Det, which is then fused with E_Stru via gated summation before being passed to the text decoder. Stage 2 applies MINT preference learning on chosen/rejected response pairs with three loss components: (i) a weighted hinge contrastive loss that up-weights numeric and comparator-keyword tokens, (ii) a low-entropy regularizer that sharpens logits at numeric positions, and (iii) a text-vision evidence loss that maximizes cosine similarity between evidence-span text tokens and visual tokens for the chosen response relative to the rejected one.

## Results

Twin-T-7B achieves an overall score of 719.94 across 10 chart-table benchmarks, surpassing GPT-4o (714.89) and GLM-4.5V-106B (667.08), while remaining within 4.3 points of Gemini-2.5-Pro (724.26). Among open-source models, Twin-T-7B leads by a wide margin over the next-best general VLM Ovis2-8B (637.22) and the best chart-table expert ChartAst-13B (600.98). The smaller Twin-T-1B (576.20) also outperforms all other ≤4B models except Qwen2.5-VL-3B (583.81), and notably scores 70.20 on the authors' TwintVQA benchmark, the highest open-source result on that benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Twin-T-7B (719.94 overall) outperforms GPT-4o (714.89) by 5.05 points and GLM-4.5V-106B (667.08) by 52.86 points, while trailing Gemini-2.5-Pro (724.26) by only 4.32 points. Against all open-source general-purpose VLMs, Twin-T-7B leads the strongest baseline Ovis2-8B (637.22) by 82.72 points, and against chart-table expert models it surpasses ChartAst-13B (600.98) by 118.96 points. On the authors' TwintVQA benchmark specifically, Twin-T-7B scores 70.20, beating GPT-4o (67.06) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (63.58), while Twin-T-1B (58.79) edges out Ovis2-1B (37.17) and ChartCap-4B (28.14) but falls slightly short of Qwen2.5-VL-3B (50.31) on that single benchmark. The 7B model's largest individual-benchmark wins are on AI2D (90.77 vs. 89.80 for Gemini-2.5-Pro) and TableVQA (80.27 vs. 80.08 for GLM-4.5V-106B), while its weakest relative performance is on ChartMimic (34.37 vs. 64.22 for Gemini-2.5-Pro), a code-reconstruction task where the authors attribute the gap to smaller model capacity and less code-aligned training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

Twin-T builds on the standard VLM recipe (visual encoder → connector → text decoder) established by LLaVA, Qwen-VL, and Ovis, using Ovis2-1B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B as base models. It departs from prior chart-table expert models (ChartLLaMA, ChartVLM, ChartAst) that rely on generic visual pathways or auxiliary tokens by introducing an explicit dual-head encoder that separates structural cues (via Canny edge maps) from fine details, fused through a Schur-style projection. The MINT preference learning method extends standard DPO-style contrastive training with token-level numeric/keyword weighting, low-entropy regularization on number tokens, and a text-vision evidence alignment loss, none of which appear in prior chart-table VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The TwintVQA benchmark (17 chart types, 11 task types, 3 data formats, 3 length settings) fills a notable gap: existing chart-table benchmarks cover too few types and emphasize short answers, limiting evaluation of long-form reasoning and code/LaTeX generation. The finding that a 7B model with a simple Canny-based structure-detail separation can outperform a 106B general-purpose model suggests that domain-specific inductive bias can partially substitute for scale in structured-visual tasks. Open questions include whether the dual-head approach generalizes to other structured visual domains (e.g., scientific figures, UI screenshots), whether the Canny preprocessing step is optimal versus learned edge detectors, and whether the MINT preference loss transfers to non-chart numerical reasoning tasks.
