## Problem

Autoregressive image generation suffers from high inference latency due to sequential token-by-token decoding, with sequence lengths growing quadratically with resolution. While speculative decoding has been adapted to the visual domain (e.g., LANTERN), existing methods operate purely at the token level and ignore the spatial structure and multi-scale hierarchy inherent in images, leaving a gap between text-oriented acceleration techniques and the structural properties of visual data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262278/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image at 512p or 1024p resolution, operating entirely at inference time on pretrained autoregressive models (Tar-1.5B/7B, LlamaGen-XL) without retraining. The text prompt is consumed by a unified multimodal LLM that autoregressively emits discrete VQ-VAE tokens; the acceleration pipeline works on these token sequences, with the final output decoded to pixels via the VQ decoder. No architectural modification to the target model is required—only a lower-resolution checkpoint of the same model family serves as the drafter.

## Method

MULO-SD combines three mechanisms: (1) multi-scale drafting, where a low-resolution AR model (e.g., 256p) sequentially generates tokens that are upsampled 2× or 4× via a learnable latent-space conv net or a training-free pixel-space super-resolution model to produce high-resolution candidate tokens for parallel verification by the target model; (2) local verification, which replaces raster-scan rejection with a relaxed acceptance rule (pooled probability over k nearest VQ codebook neighbors exceeding threshold τ) and resamples only within a small spatial neighborhood (radius l) around each rejected token, exploiting the strong spatial locality of visual AR attention; (3) parallel decoding integration, where ZipAR is applied both to draft the low-resolution image in one parallel pass and to fill 'rejection islands' (8-connected components of rejected positions) concurrently, reducing sequential target-model steps. The cycle repeats—downsampling verified tokens to form the next low-resolution prefix—until the full target-length sequence is produced, achieving up to 5× speedup over sequential decoding while preserving semantic alignment and perceptual quality.

## Results

On the MS-COCO 5k validation split, MULO-SD achieves up to 5.33× speedup (Tar-7B at 1024p) while maintaining GenEval of 85.4 (vs. 85.2 baseline) and HPSv2 of 29.5 (vs. 30.5 baseline). At 512p, MULO-SD(2×) reaches 1.94–2.03× speedup on Tar-1.5B/7B, outperforming LANTERN (1.08–1.20×) and EAGLE-2 (0.72–0.76×, which actually slows down generation). At 1024p, MULO-SD(4×) achieves 3.90× (Tar-1.5B) and 5.33× (Tar-7B) speedups, surpassing ZipAR (3.65×) and LANTERN (1.42–1.45×) while keeping GenEval within 0.3 points of the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MULO-SD consistently outperforms LANTERN on speedup across all configurations: 1.94× vs. 1.08× (Tar-1.5B 512p), 2.03× vs. 1.20× (Tar-7B 512p), 3.90× vs. 1.42× (Tar-1.5B 1024p), and 5.33× vs. 1.45× (Tar-7B 1024p), while maintaining comparable or better GenEval scores. Against ZipAR, MULO-SD wins at 1024p (3.90× vs. 3.65× for Tar-1.5B; 5.33× vs. 3.65× for Tar-7B) but trails at 512p on LlamaGen (1.40× vs. 1.88×), though it matches or exceeds ZipAR on the Tar models at 512p (1.94× and 2.03× vs. 1.88×). EAGLE-2, a text-oriented speculative decoding method, consistently fails on image generation, producing sub-1× speedups (0.72–0.96×) across all settings due to low acceptance rates from token ambiguity in the VQ latent space. On quality metrics, MULO-SD stays within ~1 point of the base model on GenEval and DPG-Bench, with FID and HPSv2 deltas comparable to or smaller than LANTERN's, confirming that the speedup is achieved without meaningful quality degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

MULO-SD sits at the intersection of three research threads: speculative decoding for LLMs (Leviathan et al., EAGLE-2), its adaptation to visual AR models via relaxed acceptance criteria (LANTERN), and spatial-locality-based parallel decoding (ZipAR, LPD). The key conceptual departure is injecting a multi-scale inductive bias into the drafting stage—using a lower-resolution model paired with an upsampler to propose candidate tokens—combined with a local neighborhood-based rejection rule that replaces raster-scan resampling. This makes MULO-SD the first speculative decoding method to exploit the coarse-to-fine hierarchy of image resolutions, while remaining compatible with standard next-token prediction objectives used in unified MLLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5× speedup at 1024p with minimal quality degradation is practically significant for deploying AR image generators in latency-sensitive settings, and the method integrates cleanly with existing unified MLLM pipelines without retraining. Key limitations include the need for two separate checkpoints (doubling memory for weights and KV-caches) and sensitivity to distributional alignment between drafter and verifier, which the authors acknowledge as a barrier for heterogeneous model pairs. Open questions include whether self-speculative decoding (using internal layers of a single model) can eliminate the dual-checkpoint overhead, and whether the framework extends to video or other sequential multimodal generation tasks.
