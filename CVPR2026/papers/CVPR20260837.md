## Problem

Cross-degradation generalization in RGB-IR multimodal object detection remains difficult when training data covers only limited degradation types. Existing fusion methods implicitly optimize to match the training data distribution, which may encode degradation-specific patterns and fail on unseen scenarios. The paper identifies that the alignment target for fused features is a critical but underexplored design choice: aligning to the pretrained distribution where the frozen detector performs optimally substantially outperforms aligning to training degradations or end-to-end adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method takes paired RGB and IR images (224×224) that are independently encoded by a frozen ViT-Base into 197-token sequences (196 patch tokens + 1 [CLS] token, 768-dim each). The fusion module operates on these token-level features rather than raw pixels, producing rectified features F_rect ∈ R^{197×768} that are fed to a frozen DETR decoder to output object bounding boxes with class labels. The [CLS] token of the fused features is additionally used for the GMM-based alignment loss, providing a global distribution-level supervision signal alongside the per-object detection loss.

## Method

The framework has two decoupled stages: offline, a GMM (K=8, diagonal covariance) is fitted to [CLS] token features extracted from normal validation images to model the pretrained distribution P_normal; online, the entire 86M-parameter ViT+DETR detector is frozen and only a 13M-parameter fusion module is trained. The fusion module comprises 8-head bidirectional cross-modal attention for complementary information exchange, adaptive token gating (channel-wise sigmoid gating over concatenated enhanced features) for dynamic modality weighting, and a fusion MLP projecting back to 768 dimensions. Training optimizes a combined loss: −log p_normal(F_rect^cls) for explicit distribution alignment plus λ·L_det for task performance (λ=0.5). The key insight is that aligning to P_normal — the feature region where the frozen detector's decision boundaries are calibrated — provides a stable, degradation-agnostic target that generalizes to unseen degradations, whereas aligning to P_degraded overfits to training-specific patterns.

## Results

On three RGB-IR benchmarks, the method achieves mAP@0.5 of 98.1 on LLVIP, 79.5 on FLIR, and 57.8 on DroneVehicle, setting new state-of-the-art across all datasets. Under challenging scenarios (overexposure/underexposure on LLVIP), it reaches 47.3/51.8 mAP, outperforming the best baseline (M2FNet) by 3.2/3.3 points. Training completes in 3.5 hours with only 13M trainable parameters (86M detector frozen), compared to 14–17 hours for end-to-end baselines, yielding a ~4× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 10 baselines on every dataset. Against the strongest advanced-fusion baselines, margins are modest but consistent: +0.3/+0.7/+0.7 mAP over RSDet [36], +0.5/+1.0/+0.9 over LRAFNet [7], and +0.7/+1.2/+1.2 over CrossFormer [15]. Against SOTA end-to-end methods, gains are larger: +0.6/+0.9/+0.9 over M2FNet [19], +0.4/+0.6/+0.6 over CFMW [16], and +0.8/+1.3/+1.3 over C2Former [32]. Simpler attention-based baselines (CBAM [29], SENet [10], MMTM [12]) trail by 2.3–5.5 mAP, confirming that explicit distribution alignment provides substantially stronger guidance than implicit task-loss supervision. Notably, the method achieves these gains while training 4× faster (3.5h vs. 14–17h) by freezing the 86M-parameter detector and optimizing only 13M fusion parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the RGB-IR multimodal fusion literature (CBAM, ICAFusion, C2Former, M2FNet, CFMW, CrossFormer, RSDet) but departs from the dominant end-to-end training paradigm by freezing the pretrained ViT-Base + DETR detector and training only a 13M-parameter fusion module. The key conceptual departure is the explicit distribution alignment loss: rather than relying on task loss alone to implicitly guide feature learning (as in prior lightweight fusion methods like LRAF-Net and CrossFormer), the paper models the pretrained feature distribution P_normal via GMM and directly optimizes fused features toward it. This repurposes domain adaptation principles (Ben-David et al.; Yosinski et al.) in an inverted direction—instead of adapting the model to a target domain, it pulls features back toward the source distribution where the frozen detector is calibrated.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the alignment target (P_normal vs. P_degraded) determines cross-degradation generalization offers a practical design principle for multimodal fusion: when training data covers limited degradation types, anchoring to the pretrained distribution generalizes better than fitting to observed degradations. The 4× training speedup (3.5h vs. 14–17h) makes the approach attractive for iterative development. However, the key alignment-target comparison is validated only on LLVIP, and the method's reliance on a GMM approximation of the feature distribution and on at least one modality remaining informative limits its applicability when both RGB and IR are simultaneously degraded. Open questions include whether more expressive distribution models (e.g., normalizing flows) or learned alignment targets could further improve generalization, and whether the frozen-detector strategy transfers to other modality pairs or detection backbones.
