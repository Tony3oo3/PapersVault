## Problem

Fine-tuning Vision-Language Models on real-world datasets with label noise causes severe accuracy drops (e.g., >25% on CIFAR-100 with 40% noise), yet existing LNL methods rely on self-referential loops where the model's own noisy predictions generate supervisory signals, creating confirmation bias. While recent VLM-specific approaches use cross-modal information for noise detection, none leverage the text modality as a fully independent, immutable source of ground truth to guide the entire optimization process.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input and produces class labels via a parameter-free Text-Anchored Classifier that computes a weighted consensus over pre-computed semantic anchors (K=40 text prompts per class, encoded by the frozen CLIP text encoder). The visual encoder (CLIP ViT-B/16 by default) is fine-tuned, while the text encoder and all anchor vectors remain frozen throughout training. Training supervision comes from noisy labels, but the anchors provide clean, label-correct-by-definition reference points that anchor both classification and sample refinement.

## Method

TANGO introduces two core mechanisms built on Stable Cross-Modal Semantic Anchors—frozen feature vectors from diverse class-descriptive text prompts. First, a parameter-free Text-Anchored Classifier (TAC) replaces the conventional linear head: for each image feature, it computes cosine-similarity affinities to all anchors and synthesizes logits as a weighted sum of the anchors' clean one-hot labels, grounding every prediction in text-based ground truth. Second, Anchor-Guided Refinement performs epoch-level sample selection and label correction by fusing visual k-NN consensus with a semantic signal derived from a binary visual-to-semantic graph (polling which anchors consider each image a neighbor) and a semantic-to-visual graph (propagating anchor labels to their nearest images). The training alternates between this refinement step and standard batch-level optimization with a dual loss: cross-entropy on clean-identified samples plus a Mixup-regularized cross-entropy against corrected soft pseudo-labels on all samples.

## Results

On the real-world CIFAR-100N benchmark (40% noise), TANGO achieves 83.83% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the strongest VLM-specific baseline DeFT (79.04%) by 4.79% and the best general LNL method LSL (81.00%) by 2.83%. On synthetic noise, TANGO leads on all five CIFAR-100 noise configurations (e.g., 89.83% under 40% symmetric noise vs. 88.17% for DeFT) and on Tiny-ImageNet (83.59% average vs. 82.42% for LSL). Across five real-world datasets, TANGO sets new state-of-the-art on four of five, with the sole exception being WebVision validation where LSL edges ahead by 0.20% (87.64% vs. 87.44%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TANGO outperforms all eight baselines on CIFAR-100N (83.83% vs. LSL 81.00%, SSR 80.15%, DeFT 79.04%) and Animal10N (93.62% vs. LSL 92.56%), with the largest margin over DeFT (+4.79%) reflecting the advantage of integrating semantic anchors into the training loop rather than using them only for pre-filtering. On synthetic noise (CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet), TANGO consistently leads across all noise types and ratios, beating LSL by 1.2–2.4% and DeFT by 1.7–6.0% depending on the setting. The one notable exception is WebVision validation, where LSL (87.64%) narrowly surpasses TANGO (87.44%) by 0.20%, though TANGO still wins on the ILSVRC2012 evaluation of the same model (86.44% vs. 85.40% for SSR). Among classic LNL methods, DivideMix and ELR are consistently the weakest (e.g., 75.50% and 63.93% on CIFAR-100N), confirming that noise-robust loss functions alone are insufficient for VLM fine-tuning. The VLM-specific baselines CLIPCleaner and DeFT, which use CLIP's zero-shot capability as an offline cleaning step, are outperformed by TANGO's end-to-end anchor-guided optimization on every dataset where they are reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

TANGO builds on the standard paradigm of fine-tuning CLIP's visual encoder with a linear classifier, but replaces that classifier with a parameter-free retrieval mechanism over text-encoded semantic anchors. It departs from prior LNL methods (SSR, LSL, DivideMix) that operate in a self-referential loop—using the model's own noisy predictions for sample selection and label correction—by introducing an external, immutable ground truth derived from the frozen text encoder. It also differs from recent VLM-specific noise-robust approaches (DeFT, CLIPCleaner) that treat the text modality as a multi-stage pre-processing oracle; TANGO integrates cross-modal alignment directly into the end-to-end training objective at every iteration. The anchor concept is related to ARF and ClusterAdapter, but those use data-dependent anchors as regularizers, whereas TANGO's anchors are pure text and serve as an independent reference for active noise correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Label noise is pervasive in real-world datasets, and the 25%+ accuracy drop from 40% noise on CIFAR-100 underscores a critical bottleneck for practical VLM deployment. TANGO's parameter-free classifier and anchor-guided refinement offer a principled, drop-in alternative that avoids the confirmation-bias risk of self-referential correction, and the stress test showing graceful degradation (still outperforming LSL even with 90% anchor corruption) provides a useful safety guarantee. Open questions include whether the approach extends to domains where text descriptions are inherently ambiguous or noisy (e.g., fine-grained medical imaging), and whether adaptive prompt curation can reduce the current reliance on curated or LLM-generated descriptions.
