## Problem

Existing diffusion-based avatar reconstruction methods rely primarily on 2D appearance priors, producing results that degrade under novel views due to weak 3D geometric consistency. Moreover, the diffusion generation stage and the 3D reconstruction stage are loosely coupled—avatars are supervised only by generated RGB images—failing to exploit the diffusion model's capacity for geometry-aware knowledge distillation. The paper addresses how to learn strong 3D geometry priors within a diffusion framework while simultaneously preserving identity and capturing subtle facial expressions.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single reference portrait image (encoded via a pretrained VAE into hierarchical identity latents), a driving expression video (compressed into low-dimensional expression latents by the pose-free encoder), and explicit head pose maps (rasterized vertex normals from a FLAME mesh with expression parameters zeroed to prevent identity leakage). The video generation model outputs a sequence of 512×512 portrait frames paired with corresponding surface normal maps across 12 views and ~200 frames; the reconstruction stage then produces an animatable 4D avatar as 3D Gaussians attached to FLAME mesh triangles, renderable in real time.

## Method

GeoDiff4D comprises three tightly coupled components. First, a pose-free expression encoder is trained with a cross-view pairing strategy—sampling frames of the same identity and timestep from different viewpoints so that driving and target frames share identical expressions but differ in pose—enabling view-invariant expression latents without auxiliary losses. Second, a UNet-based latent diffusion model jointly denoises RGB and surface-normal latents by concatenating them along a domain dimension, replacing vanilla 2D self-attention with 3D Domain-Spatial attention that merges domain and batch dimensions for convolutions but concatenates domains along width for attention, allowing controlled cross-domain information exchange; head pose maps are concatenated with noisy latents for explicit pose control. Third, 3D Gaussian Splatting primitives on a FLAME mesh are optimized under dual supervision from generated images and normals, with a hierarchical refinement pipeline (learnable FLAME parameter residuals, a U-Net predicting per-vertex deformations conditioned on expression latents via cross-attention, and an MLP for per-primitive Gaussian attribute residuals) and an L1 surface-normal regularization loss that extends the Gaussian representation with dedicated normal channels following GaussianShader.

## Results

On the NeRSemblev2 self-reenactment benchmark, GeoDiff4D achieves PSNR 19.951, SSIM 0.822, LPIPS 0.195, CSIM 0.721, and JOD 6.720, outperforming all four baselines on every metric. The video generation model (VGM) alone achieves the best self-reenactment scores (PSNR 21.586, SSIM 0.831, LPIPS 0.174, CSIM 0.754, JOD 7.127), confirming that the geometry-aware diffusion stage is the primary driver of quality. On cross-reenactment, GeoDiff4D attains the best JOD (5.178) and competitive CSIM (0.656), surpassing CAP4D (JOD 5.064) and GAGAvatar (JOD 5.081) in temporal coherence while matching or exceeding them in identity preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoDiff4D outperforms all four baselines on every self-reenactment metric. Against the strongest baseline CAP4D, GeoDiff4D gains +0.656 PSNR (19.951 vs. 19.295), +0.011 SSIM, and +0.159 JOD, while matching LPIPS (0.195) and edging out CSIM (0.721 vs. 0.719). Against GAGAvatar, the margin is larger: +2.401 PSNR and +0.476 JOD. Portrait4D-v2 and LAM are clearly outperformed across the board, with LAM showing the weakest identity preservation (CSIM 0.608). On cross-reenactment, GeoDiff4D achieves the best JOD (5.178) over CAP4D (5.064) and GAGAvatar (5.081), while its CSIM (0.656) is essentially tied with CAP4D (0.655) and well above GAGAvatar (0.588) and LAM (0.516). The VGM component alone surpasses all baselines on self-reenactment, indicating that the geometry-aware diffusion stage contributes the largest quality gain, with the 3DGS reconstruction stage adding further 3D consistency at a modest photometric cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoDiff4D extends the two-stage diffusion-to-3DGS pipeline established by CAP4D and MVP4D, but departs from them in two key ways: (1) the diffusion model jointly denoises RGB and surface-normal latents using a Domain-Spatial attention mechanism, rather than generating only appearance; and (2) a pose-free expression encoder trained with cross-view pairing replaces 3DMM-parameter-based motion control. The 3D reconstruction stage builds directly on GaussianAvatars' FLAME-attached 3DGS formulation, adding hierarchical mesh refinement and normal-channel regularization. The joint appearance-geometry generation draws conceptual inspiration from VideoJAM and Wonder3D, which pair color with motion or normal maps, but is the first to do so for temporally coherent portrait video with explicit head-pose conditioning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that injecting 3D geometric cues (surface normals) into the diffusion prior meaningfully improves both the generated video's multi-view consistency and the downstream 3DGS reconstruction, offering a principled bridge between 2D generative models and 3D avatar pipelines. However, the approach remains computationally heavy (1-hour data synthesis + 3-hour per-identity optimization), and the reliance on monocular 3DMM tracking for pose estimation is a known weak point. Open questions include whether the joint RGB-normal formulation generalizes beyond heads to full-body avatars, whether the cross-view pairing strategy can be replaced by cheaper alternatives, and how to reduce the diffusion sampling cost to enable near-real-time deployment.
