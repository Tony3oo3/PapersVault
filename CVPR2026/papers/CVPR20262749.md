## Problem

While the π-series demonstrated that large-scale real-robot data enables strong VLA generalization, it remained unproven whether synthetic data alone could match this pre-training effectiveness at scale. Existing simulated datasets cover narrow skill sets (primarily pick-and-place), focus on rigid objects, require substantial human teleoperation, and have not been validated for large-scale VLA pre-training, leaving open whether high-fidelity simulation can substitute for real data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262749/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The π0 model consumes multi-view RGB images (primary and wrist cameras, perturbed ±5° rotation and ±5 cm translation during synthesis), robot proprioceptive states (base and end-effector 6D poses), and a natural-language task instruction, producing a dense joint-space action sequence via flow-matching. The dataset stores 401.4M frames across 630k episodes in LeRobot format, with each episode containing object metadata, camera parameters, and action control labels; optional depth maps, grounding annotations, and bounding boxes can be recorded via configuration.

## Method

InternData-A1 is generated through a fully decoupled, compositional simulation pipeline with four stages: environment construction (retrieving verified USD embodiments, GRUtopia scenes, and annotated rigid/articulated/deformable/fluid objects), task composition (invoking modular scripted skill policies that map robot and object states to waypoint sequences, then sequencing or parallelizing skills for long-horizon tasks), domain randomization (174 environment maps, randomized object poses, grasp poses sampled from top-40 AnyGrasp candidates, and contact-region perturbation), and trajectory generation (CuRobo interpolates dense joint actions between waypoints, physics simulation validates feasibility without rendering, and only successful trajectories are rendered). The resulting 630k trajectories span 4 embodiments, 18 skills, 70 tasks, and 227 scenes, produced at 209.7 hours/day on 8 RTX 4090 GPUs at under $0.003 per episode. A π0 model (Paligemma VLM + flow-matching action expert) is pre-trained exclusively on this synthetic data and matches the official π0 trained on the closed-source π-dataset across 49 simulation, 5 real-world, and 4 long-horizon dexterous tasks, with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer exceeding 50% on ten tasks.

## Results

On 49 bimanual simulation tasks (RoboTwin 2.0), the π₀ model pre-trained exclusively on InternData-A1 achieves 60.0% success rate in Easy mode and 26.5% in Hard mode, outperforming the official π₀ (trained on the closed-source π-dataset) by 5.0 and 6.5 percentage points respectively. On two real-world tasks, InternData-A1 reaches 90.0% (Sort Rubbish) and 60.0% (Pass Bottle), surpassing all open-source baselines. Ten simulated tasks achieve direct zero-shot sim-to-real transfer with average success rates exceeding 50%, and fewer than 1,600 simulated episodes can match the performance of 200 real episodes on representative tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the official π₀ trained on the closed-source π-dataset, InternData-A1 wins on all 49 simulation tasks (60.0% vs 55.0% Easy; 26.5% vs 20.0% Hard) and achieves comparable performance on 9 real-world tasks including 4 dexterous long-horizon tasks, establishing the first evidence that synthetic-only data can match the strongest real-robot pre-training data. Against OXE (real-world, 32.5%/11.0% sim), InternData-A1 leads by 27.5 and 15.5 points in Easy/Hard simulation and by 50 and 23.3 points on the two real tasks. Against Agibot World (real-world, 52.5%/12.0% sim), InternData-A1 leads by 7.5 and 14.5 points in simulation and by 36.7 and 3.3 points on Sort Rubbish and Pass Bottle respectively. Against RoboCasa (simulation, 50.0%/11.0% sim), InternData-A1 leads by 10 and 15.5 points in simulation and by a substantial 57.7% average margin on real-world tasks, which the authors attribute to InternData-A1's photorealistic rendering and larger data volume.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the π_0 architecture (Paligemma VLM + flow-matching action expert) and the CuRobo motion planner, but departs fundamentally from prior simulation datasets (MimicGen, RoboCasa, RoboTwin 2.0, GraspVLA) in both scope and autonomy. Where existing synthetic datasets cover narrow skill sets (primarily pick-and-place on rigid objects) and often require teleoperation or RL, InternData-A1 introduces a fully decoupled, compositional pipeline that autonomously generates trajectories across fluid, deformable, articulated, and rigid objects with 18 distinct skills. The key conceptual departure is the empirical claim that synthetic data alone, at sufficient scale and fidelity, can match the pre-training effectiveness of the strongest closed-source real-robot dataset (π-dataset), a question no prior work has addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that large-scale simulation-only data can match the leading real-robot dataset for VLA pre-training, potentially removing the primary bottleneck (expensive teleoperation) that limits most research groups. The open-sourcing of both the 630k-trajectory dataset and the generation pipeline (costing <$0.003/episode) significantly lowers the barrier to scalable robotic data creation. However, limitations remain: physics simulators still cannot handle highly dexterous tasks (tying shoelaces, threading needles), and the comparison to π-dataset is inherently constrained by its closed-source nature. Open questions include whether trajectory diversity (rather than raw volume) is the true driver of effective pre-training, and whether the sim-to-real gap narrows further with improved physics engines.
