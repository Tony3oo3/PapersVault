key_contribution: Proposes TLMA, a weakly supervised video anomaly detection framework that explicitly separates Weakly Labeled Information (WLI) from true anomalies via a dynamically constructed triplet loss (using high-scoring normal-video segments as anchors) and a lightweight motion-aware feature enhancement module (Sobel inter-frame edge differences aligned to ViT tokens), achieving state-of-the-art results on UCF-Crime (89.47% AUC), XD-Violence (86.78% AP), and MSAD (93.68% AUC).
limitations: The anchor selection hyperparameter k_a is dataset-specific (3, 11, and 1 for UCF-Crime, XD-Violence, and MSAD respectively), indicating the method lacks a fully adaptive mechanism for WLI proxy selection. The triplet construction relies on the model's own predicted scores, creating a potential circular dependency, and the evaluation is limited to three WSVAD benchmarks without testing on other weakly-supervised video tasks or real-world deployment scenarios.

surprise_factor: The 5% AUC gap on MSAD (93.68% vs. 88.68% for PI-VAD) is notably larger than typical WSVAD improvements, though MSAD is a newer dataset with fewer strong baselines, so the gap may partly reflect dataset novelty rather than a fundamental breakthrough. ## Problem

Weakly supervised video anomaly detection relies on coarse video-level labels that inevitably introduce Weakly Labeled Information (WLI)—normal content within abnormal videos and atypical content within normal videos—that misleads the model and causes misclassifications. Existing MIL-based methods lack an explicit mechanism to separate WLI from true anomalies in the feature space, resulting in persistent false positives on both normal and abnormal videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263299/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes video clips as input, divided into non-overlapping 16-frame segments with frames resized to 224×224, and processes them through a VideoMAEv2 (ViT-G/14) backbone that tokenizes each segment into 2×14×14 spatiotemporal cubes. It produces per-segment anomaly scores in [0,1] and a video-level score via top-k pooling (k = ⌊L/16⌋+1). The motion-aware module additionally computes inter-frame Sobel edge differences at the 2-frame temporal resolution matching the ViT tokenization, yielding motion importance scores per spatiotemporal token.

## Method

TLMA combines two components under a joint loss L_MIL + 0.1·L_Triplet. The triplet learning strategy dynamically constructs triplets from model predictions: anchors are the top-k_a highest-scoring segments from normal videos (a practical WLI proxy), positives are the lowest-scoring segments from abnormal videos (likely WLI), and negatives are the highest-scoring segments from abnormal videos (true anomalies); a margin-based triplet loss (m=1) pulls anchor and positive together while pushing them away from the negative, explicitly separating WLI from true anomalies in feature space. The motion-aware feature enhancement module applies Sobel filters to each frame, computes inter-frame edge magnitude differences to produce motion maps, aligns these to the ViT token grid, and computes a weighted sum of token features that emphasizes motion-salient foreground regions while suppressing static background. The backbone is a pre-trained VideoMAEv2 (ViT-G/14) with a GL-MHSA module for long-range temporal modeling, and the entire framework is trained end-to-end under weak supervision with only video-level labels.

## Results

On UCF-Crime, TLMA achieves 89.47% AUC and 76.16% AUC_A, surpassing the previous best (VadCLIP: 88.02% AUC, 70.23% AUC_A) by 1.45% and 5.93% respectively. On XD-Violence, TLMA reaches 86.78% AP and 86.23% AP_A, exceeding the prior best AP (PEL4VAD: 85.59%) by 1.19% and the highest AP_A (UR-DMU: 83.94%) by 2.29%. On MSAD, TLMA attains 93.68% AUC and 81.3% AP, outperforming the strongest competitor PI-VAD (88.68% AUC, 71.26% AP) by 5.00% and 10.04% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TLMA outperforms all 17 compared baselines across all three benchmarks. On UCF-Crime, it leads VadCLIP (88.02% AUC) by 1.45% and TPWNG (87.79%) by 1.68%, with a particularly large margin on AUC_A (76.16% vs. 70.23% for VadCLIP, a 5.93% gap) indicating substantially better anomaly localization within abnormal videos. On XD-Violence, TLMA's 86.78% AP surpasses PEL4VAD (85.59%) by 1.19% and VadCLIP (84.51%) by 2.27%, while its AP_A of 86.23% exceeds UR-DMU's 83.94% by 2.29%. On MSAD, the advantage is most pronounced: TLMA's 93.68% AUC beats PI-VAD (88.68%) by 5.00% and GS-MOE (87.72%) by 5.96%, and its AP of 81.3% exceeds PI-VAD's 71.26% by over 10 points. Notably, TLMA uses a VideoMAEv2 (ViT-G/14) backbone, whereas most competitors rely on I3D or CLIP, suggesting the combination of a stronger spatiotemporal encoder with the proposed triplet and motion-aware components drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MIL-based WSVAD framework established by Sultani et al. (CVPR 2018) and the top-k pooling strategy from Wu et al. (CVPR 2022), while incorporating the GL-MHSA temporal modeling module from UR-DMU (AAAI 2023). Its key conceptual departure is formalizing 'Weakly Labeled Information' (WLI) as an explicit source of misclassification and designing a triplet loss specifically to separate WLI segments from true anomalies in feature space, whereas prior triplet-based methods (NG-MIL, UR-DMU) used triplets for normality prototypes or dual-memory modeling rather than WLI isolation. The motion-aware enhancement module is a lightweight, filter-based addition (Sobel inter-frame edge differences) on top of a VideoMAEv2 backbone, contrasting with heavier attention or auxiliary-network approaches in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The WLI framing provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding why WSVAD methods produce false positives in both normal and abnormal videos, and the proposed triplet strategy offers a practical, annotation-free remedy that practitioners can adopt without additional labeling cost. The motion-aware module's simplicity (no extra network, just Sobel filters) makes it easy to integrate into existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the WLI concept generalizes beyond WSVAD to other weakly-supervised video tasks, whether the dataset-specific anchor-k selection can be made adaptive, and whether the triplet construction's reliance on the model's own predictions creates a circular dependency that limits gains as the model improves.
