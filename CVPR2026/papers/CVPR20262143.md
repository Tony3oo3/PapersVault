## Problem

Existing industrial defect detection systems operate as task-specific black boxes with limited scale, lack multimodal (image-text) annotations, and cannot generalize across diverse manufacturing contexts. While VLMs like CLIP have revolutionized natural image understanding, they lack the domain-specific terminology and pixel-level precision required for subtle, localized industrial defects. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing IMDD-1M, the first million-scale industrial defect dataset with aligned image-text pairs, and a unified diffusion-based foundation model for open-vocabulary defect understanding.

## Input / Output

The model accepts 512×512 RGB industrial defect images paired with optional text captions (averaging 42 words) or category names for open-vocabulary classification. When captions are unavailable, an implicit captioner synthesizes pseudo text embeddings directly from CLIP image features, enabling the diffusion backbone to operate without explicit text. Outputs include binary segmentation masks, defect class labels (via dot-product matching against CLIP text embeddings), bounding boxes derived from masks, and text-conditioned generated defect images for synthetic augmentation.

## Method

The core architecture is a text-conditioned Stable Diffusion v1.5 U-Net (860M parameters) trained from random initialization on IMDD-1M, augmented with a lightweight implicit captioner (0.3M-parameter two-layer MLP projecting 512-d CLIP image embeddings into 768-d text space). During Stage 1 pretraining (100 epochs), stochastic conditioning randomly selects between ground-truth CLIP text embeddings and implicit embeddings (50/50), with an auxiliary cosine similarity loss enforcing alignment. After pretraining, the diffusion model is frozen and multi-scale features are extracted via a single forward pass at timestep t=50 in VAE latent space, then decoded to pixel-aligned representations. A Mask2Former generator (45M parameters) with FPN pixel decoder and 100-query transformer decoder predicts masks and embeddings, supervised by binary cross-entropy for segmentation, cross-entropy against CLIP text embeddings for open-vocabulary classification, and a bidirectional contrastive grounding loss for caption-based pseudo-label supervision.

## Results

On MVTec AD anomaly detection, the proposed method achieves 96.1% P-AUC-ROC and 90.2% AUC-PRO using only 200 samples per class, approximately 2% below full-data supervised baselines (98.1–98.2% P-AUC-ROC). For object detection on MVTec AD, the mask-based approach reaches 74.6% mAP@0.5 and 58.9% mAP@0.75, compared to YOLOv8's 78.3% and 62.1%. Classification accuracy averages 96.7% across four industrial datasets (MVTec AD, VisA, Magnetic Tile, Steel Surface), and pixel-level segmentation yields 52.9% average IoU. The generative model attains 100.29 Inception Score and 5.5–13.6 FID on the Magnetic Tile dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method trails all six full-data anomaly detection baselines on MVTec AD by 1.2–2.1 points in P-AUC-ROC and 2.8–3.8 points in AUC-PRO, with FAIR [12] being the strongest baseline (98.2% / 94.0%) and PromptAD [10] the closest (96.5% / 90.5%). However, this gap is achieved with only 200 samples per class versus the full training sets (~4,000 samples after augmentation) used by all baselines, representing a >95% reduction in annotation requirements. In object detection, the mask-based approach (74.6% mAP@0.5) falls 3.7 points short of YOLOv8-m (78.3%) but does so without any explicit bounding-box annotations, deriving boxes directly from segmentation masks. The data-efficiency ablation (Figure 11) confirms that performance saturates beyond 200 samples per class, indicating the IMDD-1M pretraining has already captured generalizable defect representations. Overall, the paper's central claim is not state-of-the-art absolute performance but competitive accuracy at a fraction of the supervision cost, positioning the method as a data-efficient foundation model rather than a peak-performance detector.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the industrial anomaly detection line (DAGM → MVTec AD → VisA → Real-IAD) by introducing the first million-scale multimodal dataset with aligned image-text pairs, addressing the fundamental gap that all prior industrial benchmarks lack textual annotations. Architecturally, it repurposes the Stable Diffusion U-Net as a feature extractor (trained from scratch rather than fine-tuned) and couples it with Mask2Former for mask prediction, while the implicit captioner module is a novel bridge that enables text-conditioned feature extraction on downstream datasets lacking captions. The approach departs from prior VLMs (CLIP, ALIGN, Flamingo) that are trained on natural images and lack domain-specific industrial terminology, positioning IMDD-1M as the necessary domain-specific pretraining corpus.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1.24M image-text pair dataset spanning 63 domains and 421 defect types is a substantial resource that will likely become a standard benchmark for industrial multimodal learning, filling a gap that no prior dataset addressed. The <5% data-efficiency claim is practically significant for manufacturing settings where expert annotation is expensive, though the absolute performance gaps (~2% P-AUC-ROC, ~52.9% IoU) indicate the foundation model is competitive rather than superior to full-supervision methods. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to temporal/video-based defect tracking, 3D reasoning, and truly unseen manufacturing sectors, and whether the diffusion-based feature extraction is genuinely necessary versus a simpler pretrained backbone.
