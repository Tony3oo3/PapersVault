## Problem

Existing infrared-visible image fusion (IVIF) methods fundamentally require paired visible and infrared images to be simultaneously available, yet in most real-world deployments only visible sensors are present because thermal infrared sensors are prohibitively expensive. This paper introduces the concept of Single Image Fusion (SIF), asking whether the benefits of cross-spectral fusion can be preserved from a single degraded visible image alone by shifting from data-level to knowledge-level fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263719/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded visible image (e.g., low-light, hazy, or noised) as its sole input—no infrared image is required at inference time. It produces a 'Magic Image' (MagImg), a fused cross-spectral image that simultaneously exhibits enhanced visible-spectrum details and learned infrared thermal radiation patterns, along with a pixel-wise semantic segmentation map. The input is encoded into a shared latent space via a lightweight autoencoder with InstanceNorm, and the fused latent is decoded back to image space; the segmentation head operates on attention features extracted from the fusion network at each diffusion timestep.

## Method

MagicFuse comprises three branches built on latent diffusion models: an Intra-Spectral Knowledge Reinforcement (IKR) branch that restores degraded visible details, a Cross-Spectral Knowledge Generation (CKG) branch that translates visible images to infrared via learned thermal radiation patterns, and a Multi-Domain Knowledge Fusion (MKF) branch that fuses the probabilistic noise estimates from both diffusion streams at every timestep using a learned, timestep-dependent weighting coefficient. The key insight is that the noise predicted by each denoiser at a given timestep encodes task-specific knowledge, making it a natural medium for knowledge-level fusion without requiring actual infrared pixels. A segmentation head embedded in the MKF branch enforces visual-semantic coupling and produces a radiation category map that modulates the fusion weights to prevent training collapse toward the visible-only stream. Training follows a two-phase scheme: the two diffusion models are first trained independently on paired degraded-clean and visible-infrared datasets, then frozen while the fusion network and segmentation head are trained end-to-end using DDIM-accelerated sampling with 25 inference steps.

## Results

On the MFNet test set (392 degraded visible images), MagicFuse achieves the best EN (7.29), MI (4.13), and PSNR (63.49) among all 10 methods, while using only a single degraded visible image versus paired infrared-visible inputs for all competitors. On semantic segmentation (mIoU), MagicFuse scores 62.19, second only to SegMiF (62.28) and well above the degraded visible baseline (54.09). On the FMB generalization set, MagicFuse retains competitive visual scores (EN=6.93, MI=4.09, PSNR=62.51) and semantic mIoU of 55.41, outperforming the degraded and IKR-enhanced visible baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MagicFuse outperforms all nine multi-modal baselines on EN (7.29 vs. best 7.26 DAFusion), MI (4.13 vs. best 3.38 ControlFusion), and PSNR (63.49 vs. best 63.38 Text-IF), despite using only a single degraded visible image. It trails Text-IF on SSIM (0.45 vs. 0.52) and Qabf (0.50 vs. 0.59), indicating slightly lower structural similarity and information fidelity to the visible input. On semantic mIoU (Table 3), MagicFuse (62.19) is marginally behind SegMiF (62.28) but ahead of EMMA (61.98), Text-IF (60.65), and all other baselines, while substantially surpassing the degraded visible baseline (54.09). On the FMB generalization set, MagicFuse achieves second-best MI (4.09 vs. ControlFusion 4.23) and competitive PSNR (62.51), confirming that the learned knowledge fusion transfers to unseen data. Overall, the key finding is that knowledge-level fusion from a single visible image matches or exceeds data-level fusion from paired infrared-visible inputs across both visual and semantic metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established infrared-visible image fusion (IVIF) paradigm, which strictly requires paired visible and infrared inputs, by introducing Single Image Fusion (SIF) that operates on a single degraded visible image alone. Methodologically, it builds on latent diffusion models (LDMs) with U-Net backbones and DDIM sampling, repurposing the denoising noise estimates from two separate diffusion streams (one for visible restoration, one for visible-to-infrared translation) as a medium for knowledge-level fusion rather than data-level fusion. The multi-domain knowledge fusion branch and the visual-semantic coupling via an embedded segmentation head are novel architectural contributions that bridge the gap between generative modeling and task-oriented perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: in most real-world deployments (autonomous driving, surveillance), only visible sensors are available due to the high cost of thermal infrared hardware, rendering all existing IVIF methods inapplicable. MagicFuse demonstrates that cross-spectral scene representations can be recovered from a single degraded visible image, potentially eliminating the need for expensive multi-sensor rigs. However, the method still requires paired multi-modal data during training (for the CKG branch), and generalization to unseen datasets (FMB) shows measurable degradation in both visual and semantic metrics. Open questions include whether the approach extends to other modality pairs (e.g., RGB-D, RGB-LiDAR), how it handles truly novel degradation types absent from training, and whether the computational cost (~518M parameters for Φ) is acceptable for real-time deployment.
