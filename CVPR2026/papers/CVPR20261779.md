## Problem

Existing methods for incomplete multi-view multi-label learning (iM2L) address view imbalance through gradient modulation or alternating optimization, but they assume weaker views are merely under-optimized. In reality, some views suffer from an inherent representation ceiling due to limited information capacity or high noise, so forcing the model to learn from them degrades overall performance. No prior method both actively transfers knowledge from strong to weak views and filters out irredeemably low-quality representations before fusion.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-sample multi-view features of heterogeneous dimensions (e.g., GIST, HSV, DenseHue, DenseSIFT, RGB, LAB for most datasets; RGB, LAB, VGG, CLIP, Wavelet for ODIR) along with a binary view mask matrix indicating which views are observed, and a binary label mask matrix indicating which label entries are annotated. View-specific encoders project each view into a shared latent space, and the model outputs a probability vector over all labels via a shared multi-label classifier. Missing views are handled natively through the mask rather than imputation, and the model must produce predictions even when only a subset of views is available for a given sample.

## Method

CDAM introduces two complementary modules operating on the shared latent representations. First, the Cross-view Distillation Module (CDM) identifies a per-sample teacher view as the one with the highest learned fusion weight among observed views, then applies a stop-gradient L2 alignment loss to pull all other student view representations toward the teacher, enabling unidirectional knowledge transfer without collapse. Second, the Adaptive View Masking Module (AMM) uses a per-view MLP scorer whose evidence is converted into a differentiable binary selection via Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization with a Straight-Through Estimator, dynamically excluding views whose post-distillation representations remain unreliable. The final objective combines an autoencoder reconstruction loss, the distillation loss, and an improvement loss that penalizes the model only when the filtered representation yields worse classification than the unfiltered one, plus a masking penalty to prevent trivial all-select solutions. A label-frequency-aware weighting (smoothed inverse frequency) is applied to the BCE classification loss to counteract long-tail label imbalance.

## Results

Under the standard 50% missing-view and 50% missing-label setting, CDAM achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six benchmark datasets (Corel5k, Pascal07, Espgame, Iaprtc12, Mirflickr, ODIR) on all six evaluation metrics (AP, HL, RL, AUC, OE, Cov). In the 30% dual-missing-rate comparison (Table 2), CDAM reaches AP 0.488 / AUC 0.935 on Corel5k, AP 0.637 / AUC 0.895 on Pascal07, and AP 0.664 / AUC 0.887 on Mirflickr, outperforming the strongest baselines by 1.5–2.7 AP points. Ablation studies confirm that both the cross-view distillation loss and the adaptive masking loss contribute independently, with the full framework yielding the highest scores (e.g., AP 0.4281 on Corel5k vs. 0.3730 for the backbone alone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDAM outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and metric reported. The closest competitors are SIP (Corel5k AP 0.473 vs. 0.488, a 1.5-point gap) and VCMN (Pascal07 AP 0.610 vs. 0.637, a 2.7-point gap), both of which rely on static view-level weighting or channel mixing that cannot adapt to instance-level quality variation. MSLPP and MTD are competitive on Mirflickr (AP 0.651 and 0.643 respectively) but still trail CDAM by 1.3 and 2.1 points. DICNet, despite its instance-level contrastive learning, underperforms on Pascal07 (AP 0.570) because its contrastive objective does not account for inherent information-quality disparity across views. DIMC and AIMNet, which use imputation or attention-based embedding strategies, show the largest gaps (up to 4–5 AP points), suggesting that generated or imputed features introduce noise that degrades downstream classification. The paper also notes that CDAM degrades gracefully as missing rates increase and is more sensitive to missing views than missing labels, a pattern consistent across all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in multi-view imbalance mitigation (gradient modulation, alternating optimization, and pre-trained teacher guidance) by arguing that weaker views may possess an inherent representation ceiling that self-optimization cannot overcome. It repurposes knowledge distillation—previously applied to single-view or cross-domain settings—into a cross-view mechanism where the highest-weighted view per sample acts as a teacher for other views. The adaptive masking component draws on Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization and straight-through estimation from discrete decision literature, applying them to instance-level view selection in a multi-view fusion context. The autoencoder-style reconstruction loss and label-frequency-aware gradient modulation are standard components borrowed from prior iM2L and long-tail learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage design (distill first, then mask) offers a practical recipe for handling heterogeneous multi-view data where some views are fundamentally less informative, a scenario common in sensor-fusion and medical imaging applications. However, the global view preference vector used for teacher selection limits the method's adaptability to highly instance-dependent view quality, and the approach has only been validated on six relatively small-scale datasets with 5–6 views. Open questions include whether the distillation-masking pipeline generalizes to truly heterogeneous modalities (e.g., text + image + audio), scales to dozens of views, or remains effective when the 'teacher' view itself is corrupted by noise.
