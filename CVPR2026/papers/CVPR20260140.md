## Problem

Existing spectral compressive imaging (SCI) reconstruction methods operate frame-by-frame, suffering from two fundamental issues: mask-based encoding occludes spatial-spectral information making single-frame reconstruction inherently uncertain, and independent per-frame processing produces temporally inconsistent results (flickering). Additionally, no high-quality dynamic hyperspectral video dataset exists to support video-level reconstruction research, as existing datasets are either static images or low-fidelity sequences designed for downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short sequence of T=3 compressed 2D measurements (each H×W′) captured by a fixed-pattern DD-CASSI system covering 30 spectral channels (500–650 nm) and reconstructs the full hyperspectral video X ∈ R^{T×H×W×C}. In simulation, spatial resolution is 256×256 with 30 channels; in real experiments, 1024×1024 spatial resolution is used. The key constraint is that all frames share the same optical encoding pattern, so complementary information across frames is the sole source for recovering masked spectral content.

## Method

PG-SVRT is a U-Net-based architecture built from three components: (1) Mask-Guided Degradation Perception (MGDP) explicitly models the compression degradation by comparing the full mask with its spatially-projected version, producing a learned weight map that modulates measurement features before they enter the main network; (2) Cross-Domain Propagated Attention (CDPA) performs spatial-then-temporal attention where a shared value tensor propagates features across domains, and a bridged token (pooled from Q) enables indirect Q-K-V interaction within spatial windows, reducing complexity to O(4THWC² + 4THWN_B·C + 2T²HWC); (3) Multi-Domain Feed-Forward Network (MDFFN) splits spectral features into heads for independent spatial and temporal self-attention followed by cross-domain fusion. The spatial-then-temporal ordering with value-sharing ensures QK interactions are computed before any domain-specific processing, preventing crosstalk while promoting inter-domain feature fusion.

## Results

PG-SVRT achieves 41.23 dB PSNR and 0.9882 SSIM on KAIST, and 41.82 dB PSNR and 0.9904 SSIM on DynaSpec, setting new state-of-the-art across all four evaluation metrics (PSNR, SSIM, SAM, ST-RRED). It also attains the best spectral fidelity (SAM 3.805 on KAIST, 4.0118 on DynaSpec) and temporal consistency (ST-RRED 19.35 on KAIST, 27.14 on DynaSpec). Notably, PG-SVRT maintains only 2.48M parameters and 28.18 GFLOPs per frame—lower than several image-based baselines—while the strongest temporal competitor DPU* requires 77.36 GFLOPs and 15.14M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PG-SVRT outperforms all 11 image-based SOTA methods and the temporal variant DPU* on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, SAM, ST-RRED) across both KAIST and DynaSpec. The closest competitor, DPU* (DPU with temporal frames concatenated), achieves 40.50/41.36 dB PSNR but at 2.7× the FLOPs (77.36 vs. 28.18) and 6× the parameters (15.14M vs. 2.48M), while still trailing PG-SVRT by 0.73/0.46 dB in PSNR and 7.36/4.06 in ST-RRED. Among pure image-based methods, DPU is the strongest baseline (40.02/41.01 dB PSNR), yet PG-SVRT leads by 1.21/0.81 dB. On spectral fidelity (SAM), PG-SVRT achieves 3.805 on KAIST—nearly matching MST-L (3.8248) which is the best image-based method—while all other baselines score 4.13 or worse. On temporal consistency (ST-RRED), PG-SVRT's 19.35 on KAIST is substantially lower (better) than the next-best DPU* (26.71) and DPU (25.90), confirming that the spatial-then-temporal propagation design effectively enforces inter-frame coherence without inflating computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the well-established image-level spectral compressive imaging (SCI) reconstruction paradigm—dominated by mask-guided transformers (MST-L, DPU) and deep unfolding networks—to the video level, a setting the authors explicitly identify as 'largely unexplored.' Architecturally, it repurposes linear attention concepts (bridged tokens) and spatial-then-temporal decomposition from video understanding (G-MSA/F-MSA/W-MSA) into a spectral reconstruction context, while retaining the U-Net backbone and mask-guided degradation perception inherited from prior SCI methods. The key conceptual departure is treating a fixed encoding pattern across a temporal sequence as a source of complementary information that resolves the inherent uncertainty of single-frame masked reconstruction, rather than merely enforcing temporal smoothness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The DynaSpec dataset and the systematic four-architecture SCI comparison (concluding DD-CASSI's superiority for video-level tasks) provide the field with its first controlled benchmark for video-level spectral reconstruction, addressing a genuine data and evaluation gap. The DD-CASSI hardware prototype adds practical credibility beyond simulation. However, the dataset's controlled indoor acquisition, limited 30 sequences, and 3-frame temporal window constrain generalization claims; the authors themselves acknowledge that unscripted natural scenes remain untested. Open questions include whether the temporal complementarity insight scales to longer sequences, higher spectral resolutions, or different motion regimes, and whether the bridged-token efficiency strategy transfers to other high-dimensional video reconstruction tasks.
