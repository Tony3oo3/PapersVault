## Problem

Decoder-only autoregressive image generation with 2D VQ-VAE tokenization suffers from quadratic token growth with resolution (e.g., 256 tokens at 256×256 vs. 4096 at 1024×1024), making attention computationally prohibitive at higher resolutions. Existing remedies—1D tokenizers sacrifice spatial structure needed for editing, while fixed-factor token merging indiscriminately combines high-information regions and degrades quality. The paper asks whether tokens can be dynamically merged based on their information content while preserving 2D spatial structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262595/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (or text prompt) as conditioning signal and generates a 256×256 or 384×384 RGB image via sequential token sampling. Images are tokenized with a LlamaGen VQ-VAE (codebook size 16,384, 16× downsampling), yielding 256 or 576 tokens respectively. At inference, the entropy threshold can be adjusted independently from training, allowing the model to produce coarser patches (up to ~2.16 average patch length vs. 1.81 at training) for additional speedup with only marginal FID degradation.

## Method

DPAR introduces a four-component pipeline: (1) a lightweight unconditional GPT entropy model that computes next-token prediction entropy for each VQ-VAE token, (2) a patch encoder that aggregates tokens within each patch via causal self-attention with 2D RoPE followed by cross-attention (patches as queries, tokens as keys/values), (3) a LLaMA-based decoder-only transformer operating on the reduced patch sequence with a Dynamic RoPE mechanism for variable-length patches, and (4) a patch decoder that expands patches back to token-level predictions via copy-norm-linear plus causal self-attention. Patchification is governed by three rules: a token joins the current patch if its entropy is below a threshold H_Th, patches are capped at P_max tokens, and new patches always start at row boundaries. The key insight is that low next-token prediction entropy reliably identifies homogeneous image regions (sky, walls) where merging is safe, while high-entropy regions retain token-level granularity—allocating compute where it matters. Training uses standard cross-entropy loss on token predictions with a packed xformers implementation to handle variable-length patch sequences without padding, and the entropy model is pre-trained unsupervised on the same dataset.

## Results

On the ImageNet-256 class-conditional benchmark, DPAR outperforms LlamaGen at every model size and resolution: DPAR-B achieves FID 3.98 vs. LlamaGen-B's 5.46 (27.1% improvement), DPAR-L achieves 2.93 vs. 3.80 (22.9%), and DPAR-XL achieves 2.67 vs. 3.39 (21.2%). At 384×384 resolution, the gains are largest for smaller models (DPAR-384-B: FID 4.29 vs. 6.09, a 29.6% improvement) and DPAR-384-XXL reaches FID 2.30 with 1.4B parameters. DPAR also reduces training FLOPs by up to 40.4% (DPAR-XL vs. LlamaGen-XL at 384px) while requiring fewer sampling steps (142 vs. 256 at 256px; 280 vs. 576 at 384px) due to the reduced patch count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline LlamaGen (same raster-order paradigm, same VQ tokenizer), DPAR wins at every model size and both resolutions: FID improves by 27.1% (B), 22.9% (L), and 21.2% (XL) at 256px, and by 29.6% (B), 9.1% (L), 0.8% (XL), and 1.7% (XXL) at 384px, while also using roughly 45% fewer sampling steps. Against other raster-order AR models with different tokenizers, DPAR-384-XXL (FID 2.30) beats Open-MAGVIT2-XL (2.33) and RQTran.-re (3.80), and DPAR-XL (2.67) beats VQGAN-re (5.20). Against non-raster-order methods (RAR-XL FID 1.48, VAR-2.0B FID 1.92, RandAR-XL FID 2.22), DPAR-XL (2.67) trails, but the authors note these use fundamentally different token orderings or training paradigms, making direct comparison unfair; DPAR's advantage is efficiency (40.4% fewer training FLOPs) within the same raster-order setting. DPAR also demonstrates faster convergence than LlamaGen throughout training (Figure 5) and more favorable quality-efficiency scaling across resolutions 128–512 (Figure 4), using up to 60% fewer tokens than LlamaGen at higher resolutions.

## Relation to Prior Work

DPAR builds directly on LlamaGen's architecture (VQ-VAE tokenizer, LLaMA decoder backbone, raster-order NTP training) and adapts the entropy-guided byte-merging strategy from BLT (Byte Latent Transformer) to the image domain. It departs from prior token-reduction approaches—1D tokenizers (CAT, TiTok) that sacrifice 2D spatial structure, and fixed-factor merging methods (Token-Shuffle, MSViT) that indiscriminately combine high- and low-information regions—by using next-token prediction entropy from a lightweight unsupervised AR model to selectively merge only low-information tokens while preserving 2D layout. The paper positions itself as orthogonal to modified-training-paradigm methods (VAR, RAR, RandAR, SAR), which address generation fidelity rather than the quadratic token-scaling bottleneck.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The quadratic growth of token count with resolution is a fundamental bottleneck for AR image generation at high resolutions (e.g., 4096 tokens at 1024×1024), and DPAR's 40.4% FLOPs reduction with simultaneous FID improvement offers a practical path toward scaling AR models to higher resolutions. The finding that dynamic patchification yields representations robust to patch-boundary changes at inference (graceful degradation vs. catastrophic failure of static baselines) suggests a broader principle: training with variable-length units can produce more generalizable spatial representations. However, the work is limited to raster-order, class-conditional ImageNet generation with a single VQ tokenizer; extending to text-conditional, random-order, or multi-scale settings remains open, as does developing KV-cache strategies for the encoder-decoder patch architecture to accelerate inference.
