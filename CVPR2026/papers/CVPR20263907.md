## Problem

Existing jailbreak attacks on text-to-image models face a fundamental trade-off: prompt-level methods optimize proxy losses that fail to capture the true end-to-end 'bypass + harmfulness' objective, while generator-level methods that do optimize the real objective require large-scale RL-trained LLMs with tens of billions of parameters. This leaves a gap for a lightweight, LLM-free approach that directly optimizes the end-to-end jailbreak reward under black-box access.

## Input / Output

JANUS takes a single target NSFW text prompt and operates under a strict black-box setting where the attacker only receives generated images or rejection messages (no gradients or parameter access), optionally augmented by an open-source NSFW scorer. The output is a discrete adversarial prompt obtained by projecting sampled continuous token vectors back to the vocabulary via per-position argmax or Gumbel-Softmax, which when fed to the T2I model yields an image that bypasses both the text-level and image-level safety filters while remaining semantically aligned with the original target.

## Method

JANUS reframes jailbreak as optimizing a low-dimensional prompt distribution rather than searching for a single adversarial string. Stage 1 constructs two semantically anchored diagonal Gaussians by relaxing the one-hot token selection vectors of the target prompt and its NSFW-word-removed 'clean' variant into continuous random vectors (a Dirac-inspired relaxation), then combines them as a convex mixture p_α = αN_t + (1−α)N_c, where the shared core semantics constructively interfere to preserve meaning. Stage 2 treats the scalar mixing ratio α as a policy parameter and optimizes it via policy gradient, using a reward R(p) = C(p, M(p))·S(M(p)) that jointly captures filter bypass and NSFW harmfulness from the black-box T2I system. The gradient ∇_α log p_α(p) = (N_t(p) − N_c(p)) / p_α(p) is estimated with Monte Carlo sampling over a batch of K prompts, and α is updated by projected gradient ascent, requiring no backpropagation through the T2I pipeline and no large generator model.

## Results

On SD3.5LT, JANUS achieves a text attack success rate (TASR) of 94.25% and an overall attack success rate (ASR-8) of 43.15%, substantially outperforming the best baseline QFA (25.30% ASR-8). It also attains the highest CLIP score (0.37) and NSFW score (0.33), confirming both semantic fidelity and genuine harmfulness of generated images. On the more defensively robust DALL-E3, JANUS still leads with TASR 12.98%, ASR-8 3.39%, CLIP 0.24, and NSFW 0.08, exceeding the next-best method (PGJ) by roughly 60% in ASR-8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JANUS dominates all five baselines on every reported metric for both SD3.5LT and DALL-E3. On SD3.5LT, the closest competitor is QFA (ASR-8 25.30%), which JANUS surpasses by 17.85 percentage points; PGJ achieves a higher IASR-8 (41.21% vs. 46.65%) but a much lower ASR-8 (17.15%) due to its weaker text-filter bypass (TASR 32.75% vs. 94.25%). SneakyPrompt and MMA trail further behind, with ASR-8 of 11.24% and 4.00% respectively, while MMP completely fails on SD3.5LT (all metrics at 0). On DALL-E3, where absolute success rates are much lower due to stronger safety mechanisms, JANUS still leads: it outperforms PGJ (the next-best) by 1.26 points in ASR-8 (3.39% vs. 2.13%) and by 0.06 in CLIP score, while MMA and MMP—despite comparable TASR (~11–12%)—achieve ASR-8 below 0.6%, indicating their prompts rarely survive the image-level filter.

## Relation to Prior Work

JANUS departs from both established paradigms for T2I jailbreak: prompt-level optimization (soft/hard) that relies on proxy losses, and generator-level optimization that trains large RL-based LLMs. Rather than building on either, it reframes the problem through an energy-based model lens, parameterizing a dual-Gaussian mixture over two semantically anchored prompt distributions and optimizing a single scalar mixing policy via lightweight policy gradient. The Dirac-inspired relaxation from discrete one-hot token selection to continuous Gaussian rows, combined with the wave-interference intuition for constructive semantic overlap, is a novel synthesis not present in prior jailbreak literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results expose a structural vulnerability: current T2I safety filters, even on commercial systems like DALL-E3, can be bypassed by a method requiring no LLM and only a single learned scalar, suggesting that distribution-aware defenses are needed rather than point-wise prompt screening. The modest DALL-E3 results (ASR-8 of 3.39%) indicate that stronger multi-layered safety pipelines still provide meaningful protection, but the 94.25% TASR on SD3.5LT highlights how fragile open-source filter stacks remain. Open questions include whether the dual-distribution approach generalizes to video generation or multimodal models, and whether defenders can detect the characteristic 'interference pattern' in adversarial prompts.
