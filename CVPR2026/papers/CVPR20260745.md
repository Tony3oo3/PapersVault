## Problem

Existing open-source mathematical expression recognition (MER) models struggle with diverse real-world formulas—particularly complex long expressions, screen-captured images with noise, and handwritten content—while global-attention Transformers incur prohibitive quadratic computational costs. No single solution simultaneously delivers high recognition accuracy, low computational overhead, and robust generalization across all four major formula scenarios (simple printed, complex printed, screen-captured, and handwritten).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260745/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts RGB mathematical expression images at a baseline resolution of 192×672 (scalable to 384×1344 for complex formulas) and autoregressively generates LaTeX or Markdown markup strings. Tokenization uses a BPE vocabulary augmented with LaTeX-specific tokens, with a maximum sequence length of 1536. Input images span a wide aspect-ratio range (average 1:3.5) and may contain inconsistent fonts, background noise, geometric distortions, or freehand handwriting.

## Method

UniMERNet's core contribution is the Raster-Scan (R-S) Attention mechanism, which decomposes 2D self-attention into a sequential horizontal (row-wise) pass followed by a vertical (column-wise) pass, reducing complexity from O(NH²W²D) to O(NHWD(H+W)) while preserving long-range dependencies. This design is motivated by the observation that Transformer decoder cross-attention for MER exhibits a distinctive left-to-right, top-to-bottom scanning pattern that mirrors human reading, making full global attention computationally redundant. The encoder is a four-stage hierarchical Transformer backbone ({2,2,14,2} MER blocks) that alternates Window Attention and R-S Attention within each block, supplemented by 3×3 depth-wise ConvEnhance modules for local detail; the decoder is an 8-layer mBART architecture with self- and cross-attention. Trained end-to-end on the 1.06M-sample UniMER-1M dataset with stratified sampling over formula length and complexity, the model achieves SOTA CDM scores across all four test subsets while using only 37% of the memory and running 5× faster than a global-attention baseline.

## Results

On UniMER-Test, UniMERNet (313M params) achieves CDM scores of 99.1%, 95.5%, 93.9%, and 94.1% on the SPE, CPE, SCE, and HWE subsets respectively, with an inference speed of 10.48 FPS at batch size 64. At the higher 384×1344 input resolution (UniMERNet†), CDM improves to 99.5%, 97.2%, 94.0%, and 95.4% across the four subsets. On the handwritten MNE-N3 benchmark (most complex multi-line expressions), UniMERNet reaches 73.98% ExpRate, nearly doubling the previous best of 34.08% (PosFormer). The Raster-Scan Attention mechanism reduces memory to 37% of global attention while delivering 5× faster inference with a 1.2% CDM improvement on CPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest specialist baseline Texify (same Swin Transformer + mBART architecture), UniMERNet gains 1.0%, 26.6%, 14.1%, and 42.0% CDM on SPE, CPE, SCE, and HWE respectively, demonstrating that the Raster-Scan Attention and UniMER-1M dataset are the primary drivers of improvement. UniMERNet also surpasses the commercial Mathpix API on all four subsets (e.g., 95.5% vs 84.2% CDM on CPE) while running on a single GPU at 10.48 FPS. Among generalist multimodal LLMs, UniMERNet outperforms GPT-4o by 17.2% CDM on CPE and 10.5% on HWE, and beats Qwen2.5-VL by 59.0% on CPE; the only near-tie is SCE where Qwen2.5-VL edges ahead by 0.1% (0.940 vs 0.939). On handwritten benchmarks, UniMERNet leads all baselines on CROHME 2014/2016/2019 (69.5/67.8/66.3% ExpRate vs 62.6/62.5/65.3% for SSAN) and achieves a dramatic 73.98% vs 34.08% ExpRate on the hardest MNE-N3 subset, though it trails PosFormer by 1.0% on HME100K (68.5% vs 69.5%).

## Relation to Prior Work

UniMERNet sits at the intersection of Transformer-based document understanding (Donut, Nougat, GOT) and specialized formula recognition (Pix2tex, Texify), but departs from both by introducing a purpose-built attention mechanism grounded in an empirical observation about formula reading order. The Raster-Scan Attention factorizes 2D self-attention into sequential horizontal and vertical passes, conceptually related to axial attention (Axial-DeepLab) and criss-cross attention (CC-Net), but explicitly motivated by the raster-scan pattern observed in decoder cross-attention maps for MER. On the data side, the paper extends the modest Pix2tex and CROHME/HME100K corpora into a million-scale, stratified-sampled dataset covering four distinct real-world formula types, addressing a data-diversity gap that prior MER work has not tackled at this scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× inference memory reduction and 5× speedup over global attention make high-resolution (384×1344) formula recognition practically deployable, which is critical for large-scale scientific literature parsing pipelines feeding LLM training. The UniMER-1M dataset and UniMER-Test benchmark fill a significant gap: prior MER datasets were small, homogeneous, and skewed toward simple printed formulas, limiting generalization research. Open questions remain for highly non-linear layouts (commutative diagrams, multi-column proofs) that violate the left-to-right, top-to-bottom assumption, and the authors note the raster-scan paradigm could extend to broader document understanding tasks like tables and complex multi-element layouts.
