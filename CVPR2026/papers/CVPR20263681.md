## Problem

Self-supervised video pre-training methods developed for natural videos exhibit a 'motion bias' when applied to endoscopic videos: they overemphasize non-semantic dynamics like camera jitter and tissue displacement while underutilizing the static, localized visual cues (lesion morphology, color, texture) that are critical for clinical diagnosis. No existing paradigm explicitly models the structured, lesion-centric static semantics and their temporal evolution in a way that matches how experienced endoscopists actually examine video.

## Input / Output

The method consumes endoscopic video clips (30 fps, ~5 s duration) by sampling three sparse views (past, current, future) of 2 frames each from a random temporal window, with frames resized to 224×224 and patchified at size 16. It produces a learned video representation (token-level features from the EndoMamba-S student encoder) that is transferred to downstream tasks—classification, segmentation, detection, and surgical phase recognition—via standard fine-tuning. A frozen VideoMamba-S teacher provides saliency priors and alignment targets during pre-training but is not part of the inference pipeline.

## Method

FPRL implements a two-stage hierarchical semantic modeling mechanism inspired by the clinical 'focus before perception' workflow. In the Static Semantic Focus stage, Teacher-Prior Adaptive Masking (TPAM) fuses an l2-normalized teacher saliency map with a lightweight multi-head attention head to produce a learnable binary mask that retains only lesion-salient patches, suppressing background and motion noise; the masked current view is encoded by an EndoMamba student (bidirectional intra-frame + unidirectional inter-frame Mamba blocks) and reconstructed at the pixel level. In the Contextual Semantic Perception stage, Cross-View Masked Feature Completion (CVMFC) performs cross-attention between the masked current tokens and past/future view tokens in latent space to recover masked semantics, while Attention-Guided Temporal Prediction (AGTP) pools each adjacent view using its cross-attention map with the current view and applies InfoNCE contrastive learning at the view level with EMA-updated target heads. The three losses—pixel MSE reconstruction, cosine feature alignment to the frozen teacher, and temporal contrastive loss—are jointly optimized with fixed weights (λ₁=1.0, λ₂=0.8, λ₃=1.0, λ_pf=20) over 400 epochs.

## Results

On the three primary downstream tasks, FPRL achieves 95.2% F1 on PolypDiag (classification), 86.1% Dice on CVC-12k (segmentation), and 89.8% F1 on KUMC (detection), outperforming the strongest baseline EndoMamba by +0.7%, +1.6%, and +1.0% respectively. FPRL also reduces pre-training time to 18.2 hours, a 52% reduction compared to EndoMamba's 38.2 hours, while using the same EndoMamba-S backbone. The method is evaluated on 11 endoscopic video datasets (33,311 videos, ~6M frames) with pre-training on 7 datasets and evaluation on 4 downstream tasks including surgical phase recognition on Cholec80 (reported in supplementary material).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FPRL outperforms all 12 baselines across all three primary downstream tasks. Against the closest competitor EndoMamba [57] (same backbone, MICCAI 2025), FPRL gains +0.7% F1 on PolypDiag, +1.6% Dice on CVC-12k, and +1.0% F1 on KUMC, while cutting pre-training time from 38.2h to 18.2h (−52%). The next-strongest endoscopic-specific methods, M²CRL [25] (NeurIPS 2024) and EndoFM-LV [65] (JBHI 2025), trail FPRL by 1.0–2.8% across tasks; notably EndoFM-LV drops to 72.6% F1 on KUMC detection, well below FPRL's 89.8%. General video SSL methods (VideoMAE [59], VideoMAE V2 [62], VideoMamba [35]) lag by 3.8–7.0% on classification and 3.3–5.2% on segmentation, confirming the motion-bias gap the paper identifies. Among contrastive baselines (FAME [12], ProViCo [47], VCL [50]), FPRL leads by 9.8–10.4% F1 on classification, underscoring the advantage of the hierarchical static-then-contextual design over purely motion-driven objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

FPRL builds directly on EndoMamba as its student encoder backbone and VideoMamba as a frozen teacher, inheriting the hybrid bidirectional/unidirectional Mamba topology for spatio-temporal decoupling. Its adaptive masking mechanism derives from AdaMAE's learnable probability-based masking, while the overall self-supervised video pre-training paradigm follows the VideoMAE/M2CRL/EndoFM lineage. The key conceptual departure is from the motion-centric assumption shared by most prior video SSL methods: FPRL explicitly argues that endoscopic diagnostic semantics are primarily static and localized, and therefore designs a hierarchical 'focus-then-perceive' pipeline that separates lesion-centric static semantics (TPAM + sparse sampling) from cross-view contextual semantics (CVMFC + AGTP), rather than modeling both jointly as prior work does.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'motion bias' framing provides a useful diagnostic lens for why dense spatio-temporal pre-training underperforms on endoscopic videos, and the 52% reduction in pre-training time (18.2 h vs. 38.2 h for EndoMamba) while improving downstream metrics makes the approach practically attractive for clinical deployment where compute budgets are constrained. However, the individual components (adaptive masking, cross-attention completion, InfoNCE contrastive loss, EMA target updates) are all well-established techniques recombined in a new configuration, so the methodological novelty is moderate. Open questions include whether the hierarchical static/contextual split generalizes beyond endoscopy to other domains with predominantly static diagnostic cues (e.g., histopathology video), and whether the single-frame pre-training variant's sensitivity to endoscopic artifacts can be addressed through quality-aware sampling as the authors suggest.
