## Problem

Existing 4D Gaussian Splatting methods for monocular video reconstruction suffer from two fundamental bottlenecks: underconstrained geometry, where view-dependent appearance signals fail to enforce strict 3D structural consistency for dynamic objects, and accumulated temporal drift, where 2D tracking priors lack 3D awareness and cause geometric collapse over long sequences. These limitations prevent current approaches from scaling to large-scale scenes and extended video durations while maintaining both accurate geometry and coherent motion.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular video as input and leverages pre-trained 2D foundation models (π³ for monocular depth and camera poses, SAM2 for foreground masks, CoTracker3 for dense 2D point tracks) to extract per-frame priors. It produces a 4D Gaussian Splatting representation decomposed into static background Gaussians and dynamic foreground Gaussians governed by a cluster-based motion field, from which novel view renderings, 3D point trajectories, and refined camera poses can be extracted. The scene is initialized on a short temporal window and progressively extended to cover the full sequence.

## Method

MotionScale introduces a cluster-centric motion field where dynamic Gaussians are partitioned into K clusters, each parameterized by a global SE(3) transformation and B local refinement bases that are blended per-Gaussian via learnable coefficients. An adaptive control mechanism (inspired by 3DGS densification) splits clusters exhibiting motion inconsistency—detected via HDBSCAN and Agglomerative Clustering on 3D trajectories—and culls undersized clusters, allowing the representation to scale with scene complexity. Optimization proceeds progressively in two decoupled stages: a background extension stage that populates newly visible regions, refines camera poses via end-to-end photometric gradients, and models transient shadows with dedicated shadow Gaussians; and a foreground propagation stage that enforces motion consistency through a three-stage refinement (one-directional alignment, bi-directional short-term tracking, and global long-term refinement with ARAP regularization). The tracking supervision accumulates 3D positions via alpha-blending and compares projected 2D tracks and depth against the 2D priors, while the photometric loss and ARAP term are introduced only in the final long-term stage.

## Results

On the DyCheck NVS benchmark, MotionScale achieves 17.98 PSNR, 0.70 SSIM, and 0.40 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest baseline 4D-Fly (17.03 PSNR, 0.60 SSIM, 0.37 LPIPS) and Shape of Motion (16.72 PSNR, 0.63 SSIM, 0.45 LPIPS). On NVIDIA Dynamic Scenes, MotionScale reaches 26.75 PSNR and 0.78 SSIM, surpassing Shape of Motion (23.37 PSNR, 0.75 SSIM) by over 3 dB. For 3D point tracking on DyCheck, MotionScale attains the lowest EPE (0.070 m) and highest accuracy at 5 cm (47.0%) and 10 cm (76.4%) thresholds, beating the next-best Shape of Motion (0.082 m EPE, 43.0%, 73.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MotionScale outperforms all 14 baselines on the primary NVS metrics (PSNR, SSIM) across both DyCheck and NVIDIA. The closest NVS competitor is 4D-Fly [48], which achieves a marginally lower LPIPS (0.37 vs. 0.40) on DyCheck but trails by 0.95 dB in PSNR and 0.10 in SSIM; on NVIDIA, MotionScale leads 4D-Fly by 4.23 dB in PSNR. Shape of Motion [43] is the strongest baseline on NVIDIA (23.37 PSNR) yet still falls 3.38 dB short of MotionScale. Among NeRF-based methods, T-NeRF [10] and HyperNeRF [30] lag by 2–4 dB in PSNR, while 4D Gaussians [49] and Deformable-GS [53] show the largest gaps (4.9–6.1 dB), reflecting the efficiency and fidelity advantage of the Gaussian Splatting formulation. For 3D tracking, MotionScale beats the dedicated tracker SpatialTracker [50] (EPE 0.125 m) and TAPIR+DA [5] (0.114 m) by 44% and 39% in EPE respectively, and surpasses the closest reconstruction-based competitor Shape of Motion [43] by 15% in EPE and 4 points at the 5 cm threshold. GFlow [46] is compared only qualitatively (Fig. 3), where MotionScale avoids the 'cloud-like' artifacts and geometric drift observed in GFlow's renderings.

## Relation to Prior Work

MotionScale extends the 4D Gaussian Splatting line of work (4D Gaussians, Dynamic 3D Gaussians, 4D-Fly, Shape of Motion) by replacing their global or fixed-capacity deformation fields with a cluster-centric motion representation that adaptively splits and prunes clusters to match scene complexity. It inherits the 3DGS rendering pipeline and densification philosophy but departs from prior methods' reliance on monolithic MLP-based or spline-based motion models, instead allocating motion capacity locally per cluster. The progressive optimization strategy with decoupled background extension and three-stage foreground propagation is a practical departure from the global joint optimization used by most prior 4DGS methods, and the dedicated shadow Gaussians introduce a lightweight mechanism for transient lighting that prior works handle implicitly or ignore.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cluster-based adaptive motion field offers a principled way to scale 4D reconstruction to longer sequences and more complex scenes without the quadratic cost of global deformation fields, making it a practical architecture for real-world monocular video. The ablation showing that shadow Gaussians contribute a substantial PSNR gain (17.98 → 16.26) highlights that transient lighting is a non-trivial component of dynamic scene fidelity that prior methods under-address. Open questions include how the method handles multi-object interactions with occlusion, whether the 2D tracking priors (CoTracker3) remain reliable under extreme viewpoint changes, and whether the progressive strategy can be parallelized for near-real-time applications.
