## Problem

Existing MLLMs learn visual representations primarily under sparse textual supervision, and when visual supervision is introduced (e.g., via VQ token prediction or feature reconstruction), it is typically applied at the post-training stage where perceptual features are already largely fixed. The paper identifies three structural failures of the standard AR+VQ approach—input discretization loss, gradient orthogonality between low-dimensional image and high-dimensional text losses, and reliance on external visual decoders—and asks whether visual supervision can instead be integrated into pre-training as a primary force for shaping fine-grained perceptual representations.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images (targeting 512×512 or higher resolution) and text prompts as input. Visual features are extracted by SigLIP2 as continuous embeddings (no quantization), projected into the LLM's embedding space, and concatenated with text token embeddings. The autoregressive decoder jointly generates text tokens and pixel-level image tokens; each image token indexes a 32×32×3 pixel patch (3072-dim vector) from a 200K-entry codebook, enabling lossless image reconstruction without any external decoder. During SFT, image-token loss is dropped so the model focuses on instruction-following text generation.

## Method

UVU unifies vision and language generation in a single raw high-dimensional space by exploiting the inherent input symmetry between pixel-level image patches and textual tokens, thereby eliminating VQ discretization at the input and external decoders at the output. A 200K pixel-level visual codebook is constructed via a large-scale iterative hierarchical clustering algorithm over 2 billion 32×32×3 patches: sinusoidal positional encoding is added to 3072-dim patch vectors, duplicate distances are removed, long-tail clusters are filtered and cached, and hierarchical sampling ensures uniform training distribution across iterations until convergence (implemented with Faiss for distributed streaming). The unified loss is 0.5·L_image + L_text, where both image and text tokens are predicted autoregressively in the same high-dimensional embedding space, which the authors show keeps the angle between image and text loss gradients below 90° (vs. consistently ≥90° for AR+VQ). To counteract the long-tail frequency distribution of image tokens, inverse-frequency sampling equalizes learning probabilities across codewords, and a batch-level dynamic threshold balances image-to-text token ratios during pre-training. The full system (SigLIP2 encoder + Qwen2.5-3B LLM + 200K codebook) is trained on 1.04 trillion tokens.

## Results

UVU (3B, Qwen2.5 backbone) achieves 91.8 on RefCOCO, 71.7 on LISA-Grounding, 52.8 on BLINK, and 2201.9 on MME, substantially outperforming same-size Qwen2.5-VL (84.1, 57.4, 46.9, 2141.7 respectively). On perception-heavy benchmarks, UVU gains are most pronounced: +7.7 on RefCOCO, +14.3 on LISA, and +5.9 on BLINK over Qwen2.5-VL. UVU also surpasses larger models (7B LLaVA-OV, 9B GLM-4v, 13B LLaVA-v1.5) on most benchmarks despite having only 3B parameters, with the largest margins on visual grounding and spatial reasoning tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the same-size Qwen2.5-VL (3B, same backbone), UVU wins on all 12 benchmarks, with the largest gains on RefCOCO (+7.7), LISA (+14.3), BLINK (+5.9), and ScienceQA (+12.1), confirming that pixel-level visual supervision during pre-training directly boosts fine-grained perception. Compared to the larger 7B LLaVA-OV, UVU (3B) surpasses it on 10 of 12 available benchmarks, including CVBench-2D (+10.7), CVBench-3D (+13.2), RefCOCO (+13.7), LISA (+24.3), and HallusionBench (+19.1), though it trails slightly on SEEDBench (74.1 vs 75.4) and MMStar (55.0 vs 56.7). Against the 9B GLM-4v, UVU leads on all comparable benchmarks (SEEDB +3.0, MMStar +0.2, MME +183.1, CVB2D +9.8, CVB3D +13.2, HallusionB +21.6) but falls short on ScienceQA (91.3 vs 96.7). Versus the 13B LLaVA-v1.5, UVU dominates across the board, with particularly large margins on MMStar (+20.7), MME (+421.1), RefCOCO (+18.3), LISA (+31.3), and HallusionBench (+42.1). The ablation row UVU* (without visual supervision) shows that removing visual supervision degrades performance on every benchmark, with the steepest drops on LISA (−12.1), RefCOCO (−6.2), and HallusionBench (−7.4), isolating the contribution of the pixel-level visual codebook supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant MLLM paradigm (Qwen-VL, InternVL, LLaVA) that relies on text-centric supervision with frozen or lightly-tuned visual encoders, and from the emerging line of work adding visual supervision at post-training (VISTA, DiT-based feature prediction, latent-space MIM). Its key conceptual departure is from AR+VQ unified autoregressive frameworks: rather than discretizing visual inputs via VQVAE and reconstructing through an external decoder, UVU uses continuous SigLIP2 features at the input and a 200K pixel-level codebook (built by iterative hierarchical clustering of raw 32×32×3 patches) at the output, arguing that the shared high-dimensional input space between image patches and text tokens resolves the gradient orthogonality that plagues VQ-based designs. The paper thus repurposes clustering (via Faiss) as a vocabulary-construction tool for autoregressive generation, a role previously filled by learned VQ codebooks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard AR+VQ visual supervision actively degrades multimodal understanding (Table 1) is a useful caution for the field, and the gradient-angle diagnostic (consistently >90° for VQ vs. <90° for UVU) offers a concrete mechanistic explanation. However, the evidence is limited to a single 3B Qwen2.5 backbone with one visual encoder, and the SFT phase simply drops image-token supervision, raising questions about whether the pre-training gains persist in more complex downstream settings. Open questions include scaling to 7B+ models, whether the 32×32 patch granularity generalizes to higher resolutions, and how the static codebook interacts with truly open-ended image generation rather than reconstruction.
