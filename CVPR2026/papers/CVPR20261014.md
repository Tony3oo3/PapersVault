## Problem

Although VMamba's state-space formulation offers linear-time complexity, it still processes a dense H×W token grid with sequential SS2D scans, incurring substantial redundant computation that limits real-world deployment. Existing token pruning and merging strategies were designed for ViTs with global attention and, when transferred to SSMs, disrupt the directional and recurrent state-update dynamics of selective scanning, potentially destroying identity-bearing structures along the scan path.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261014/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes 224×224 RGB person images, divided into 4×4 patches, and produces a fixed-length identity-discriminative embedding for gallery retrieval. A key operational detail is that the adaptive pruning and merging policy is applied symmetrically to both query and gallery images at inference time, preserving geometric and semantic alignment between the two sides while reducing FLOPs by over 70% relative to the unpruned backbone.

## Method

TE-VMamba derives token importance directly from SS2D state-update magnitudes: each token's step size |Δ_k| (computed via a softplus gating function over the input and previous hidden state) quantifies its contribution to the recurrent scan, and tokens below a layer-adaptive threshold θ = μ − ασ are pruned in middle blocks (5–10). In deeper blocks (12–18), surviving tokens are merged in even-odd pairs whose normalized dot-product similarity exceeds a learned threshold predicted by a lightweight two-layer MLP gating head, consolidating semantically redundant regions. The strict Prune→Merge ordering after each SS2D block preserves the recurrence relation h_t = Āh_{t−1} + B̄x_t and avoids the instability of simultaneous or reversed application. A depth-wise schedule—no reduction in early layers, pruning in the middle, merging in late layers—aligns token reduction with the observed rise in noisy state updates (blocks 5–10) and increasing semantic redundancy (blocks 12–18). The model is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy (label smoothing) plus batch-hard triplet loss, with softplus-parameterized thresholds regularized toward a target sparsity for stable convergence.

## Results

On Market-1501, TE-VMamba-Base achieves 96.7% Rank-1 and 87.1% mAP, outperforming all CNN and Transformer baselines while reducing FLOPs by over 75% (15.46G → 3.82G). On CUHK03-NP, TE-VMamba-Base sets new state-of-the-art results with 97.3% Rank-1 / 94.0% mAP (Labeled) and 95.4% Rank-1 / 91.7% mAP (Detected), substantially exceeding the next-best method (DFLN at 86.8/84.0 Labeled). On MSMT17, TE-VMamba-Base reaches 83.3% Rank-1 and 62.7% mAP, remaining competitive with strong Transformer baselines. On Occluded-ReID, TE-VMamba attains the best Rank-1 (95.5%) among all compared methods, though its mAP (85.3%) trails FLaN-Net (89.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TE-VMamba-Base wins on Market-1501 Rank-1 (96.7%) over all CNN baselines (best: RGA-SC 96.1%, DFLN 95.9%) and Transformer baselines (best: PHA 96.1%, DC-Former 96.0%), though its mAP (87.1%) trails DC-Former (90.6%) and PHA (90.2%). On CUHK03-NP, TE-VMamba-Base dominates every baseline by a wide margin—e.g., 97.3% Rank-1 vs. DFLN's 86.8% (Labeled) and 95.4% vs. DFLN's 84.8% (Detected)—suggesting the SSM backbone with token adaptation is particularly effective for this dataset's protocol. On MSMT17, TE-VMamba-Base (83.3/62.7) is outperformed by DC-Former (86.9/70.7), AAFormer (84.4/65.6), and PHA (86.1/68.9), indicating that the larger-scale, multi-camera setting still favors Transformer-based global attention. On Occluded-ReID, TE-VMamba achieves the highest Rank-1 (95.5% vs. FLaN-Net's 92.6%) but a lower mAP (85.3% vs. 89.5%), suggesting it excels at top-1 retrieval but is less robust in ranking the full gallery under heavy occlusion. Notably, even the smallest variant (TE-VMamba-Tiny) surpasses all CNN and Transformer baselines on CUHK03-NP, highlighting the efficiency-accuracy trade-off enabled by the SSM backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends VMamba's SS2D selective-scanning backbone by inserting SSM-aware token pruning and merging modules, departing from the ViT-oriented token-reduction literature (DynamicViT, ToMe, EViT) that relies on attention-based importance scores. The key conceptual shift is deriving token saliency directly from SS2D state-update magnitudes (Δ values) and directional coherence rather than external heuristics, aligning token decisions with the recurrent scanning dynamics inherent to Mamba. The depth-wise schedule—pruning in mid-layers where state updates are noisy, merging in deep layers where semantic redundancy accumulates—is motivated by empirical block-wise analysis of Δ statistics and token similarity, a design choice not present in prior token-efficiency work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60%+ FLOPs reduction with competitive accuracy on standard Re-ID benchmarks makes SSM-based person Re-ID more viable for edge and real-time deployment, addressing a practical gap that linear-time complexity alone does not solve. However, the ablation reveals a non-trivial accuracy cost: pruning alone drops Rank-1 by 1.5–4.6 points depending on model size, and the combined approach still trails full VMamba by 1.5 points (Base) to 4.6 points (Tiny), suggesting the efficiency-accuracy trade-off is less favorable for smaller models. Open questions include whether the Δ-guided pruning criterion generalizes to other Mamba-based vision tasks beyond Re-ID, how the approach behaves under extreme occlusion or very high-resolution inputs, and whether the learned thresholds remain stable across domain shifts. The authors note future extension to multimodal Re-ID as a natural next step.
