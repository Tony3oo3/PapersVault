## Problem

Image captioning models trained on human-annotated references inherit annotator bias, producing captions that are often incomplete (missing salient visual facts) or incorrect (containing hallucinations). Existing RL-based captioning methods like CapRL optimize only completeness via query-answering rewards, which inadvertently encourages the model to generate more text and hallucinate off-query details, causing correctness to degrade as training progresses. No prior framework jointly enforces both completeness and correctness as explicit, symmetric optimization objectives.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and generates a free-form text caption as output. During training, each image is paired with a set of ~10 diverse visual queries (generated by a multi-MLLM pipeline) that serve as the completeness supervision signal, while the generated caption itself is decomposed into up to 5 sub-caption queries for correctness verification. The training corpus CCcaption-44k contains 44,077 image-query samples collected from 16 public datasets after deduplication and quality filtering.

## Method

CCCaption is a GRPO-based reinforcement learning framework that combines two symmetric rewards: a completeness reward that measures the fraction of multi-MLLM-generated visual queries the caption correctly answers (evaluated by a frozen judge model), and a correctness reward that decomposes the generated caption into atomic sub-caption queries and scores each against the original image to penalize hallucinations. A key innovation is the multi-MLLM query generation pipeline, which iteratively samples queries from diverse MLLMs (LLaVa, Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL, Qwen3-VL) and filters them using an embedding-based diversity metric to ensure comprehensive visual coverage. A dynamic query sampling strategy, inspired by DAPO, adjusts per-query sampling probability based on their contribution to group advantage, preventing uninformative queries from diluting gradients in later training stages. The two rewards are combined as R = α·R_comp + (1−α)·R_corr with α=0.05, and the model (Qwen3-VL-2B) is trained for 20 epochs using the VeRL framework with 5 rollouts per step.

## Results

CCCaption-2B achieves a Prism average of 52.80 across 9 diverse benchmarks (ChartQA, CharXiv, InfoVQA, MathVerse, MMBench, MMMUPro, OCRBench, RefCOCO, WeMath2.0-Pro), surpassing the much larger Qwen3-VL-32B (52.26) and CapRL-3B (51.07). On the CapArena pairwise evaluation, CCCaption-2B scores 46.39 average, dramatically outperforming CapRL-3B (17.56) and even Claude-3.5-Sonnet-2024 (−22.08). On the Hallucinations evaluation, CCCaption-2B reaches 70.57 average correctness, exceeding Qwen3-VL-32B (69.87) and its own base model Qwen3-VL-2B (70.19), while CapRL-3B actually degrades below its base model (66.91 vs 67.77).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCCaption-2B (2B params) outperforms all five baselines on the Prism completeness evaluation. Against CapRL-3B, the closest RL-based competitor, CCCaption-2B gains +1.73 average (52.80 vs 51.07), with the largest margin on OCRBench (+5.18) and RefCOCO (+1.17), while CapRL actually underperforms its own base model on Hallucinations (66.91 vs 67.77). Against the 16× larger Qwen3-VL-32B, CCCaption-2B edges ahead by +0.54 on Prism and +0.70 on Hallucinations (70.57 vs 69.87), demonstrating that the dual-reward RL training compensates for the parameter gap. Against InternVL3.5-38B, CCCaption-2B leads by +1.46 on Prism, with a notable +4.53 advantage on OCRBench. On CapArena, CCCaption-2B (46.39) dramatically surpasses CapRL-3B (17.56) by +28.83 and even beats Claude-3.5-Sonnet-2024 (−22.08) by +68.47, indicating strong human-preference alignment. The only baseline where CCCaption-2B does not clearly dominate is Qwen3-VL-32B on individual Prism sub-benchmarks (e.g., MMBench 79.28 vs 81.34, MathVerse 35.11 vs 35.82), though the overall average still favors CCCaption-2B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based captioning line initiated by CapRL, which rewards captions for answering more visual queries to improve completeness. CCCaption departs from CapRL in two key ways: it replaces single-MLLM query generation with a multi-MLLM pipeline (with a diversity metric) to produce more comprehensive query sets, and it introduces a symmetric correctness reward that penalizes hallucinated sub-caption facts. The correctness mechanism builds on prior work by Lee et al. and Ye et al. on decomposing captions into atomic facts for factuality evaluation, while the dynamic query sampling strategy draws inspiration from DAPO's adaptive sample selection for RL training efficiency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically confirms a critical failure mode in RL-based captioning: optimizing completeness alone (as in CapRL) causes reward to increase while correctness degrades, because the model learns to hallucinate off-query details to inflate query-answering scores. The dual-reward formulation provides a principled remedy, and the resulting 2B model outperforming a 32B model suggests that targeted RL training can compensate for parameter scale in captioning. Open questions include whether the MLLM-based judge models introduce their own biases into both rewards, whether the approach generalizes beyond the Qwen3-VL family, and how to refine the completeness/correctness metrics beyond binary query-answering and soft hallucination scores.
