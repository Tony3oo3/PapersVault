## Problem

Existing open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding methods are predominantly offline, requiring pre-collected multi-view data, which prevents robots from simultaneously exploring and understanding unknown environments in real time. Moreover, current online mapping approaches lack instance-level panoptic understanding, making them unable to distinguish individual 3D objects by text query. OnlinePG addresses both gaps by unifying online geometric reconstruction with open-vocabulary panoptic perception.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263617/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a posed RGB-D video stream, sampling a keyframe every 20 frames and maintaining a sliding window of 12 keyframes. For each keyframe, 2D instance masks are extracted via EntitySeg and 512-dimensional language features via LSeg/CLIP, which are lifted into 3D using depth. The output is a 3D Gaussian Splatting scene augmented with sparse voxel grids (3 cm resolution) storing per-voxel language features, confidence, panoptic instance labels, and instance weights, enabling both open-vocabulary text queries and instance-level segmentation at 10–18 FPS.

## Method

OnlinePG employs a local-to-global paradigm. Locally, 3D Gaussian segments initialized from 2D masks are merged into consistent instances via a multi-cue clustering graph that combines geometric overlap (computed efficiently via voxel counting), semantic cosine similarity of pooled language features, and view consensus rate; two segments are merged if their combined geometry-semantic score exceeds a threshold or their view consensus exceeds a second threshold. The local map is then voxelized into explicit spatial attribute grids storing language features, confidence, instance IDs, and observation weights. Globally, local and global instances are matched via bidirectional bipartite matching (Hungarian algorithm applied to both forward and backward correspondence matrices, with mutual agreement required), and the global map is updated by confidence-weighted feature averaging plus discrete instance label/weight adjustments for matched, unmatched, and newly discovered instances.

## Results

On ScanNetV2, OnlinePG achieves 48.48 mIoU and 66.01 mAcc for 3D semantic segmentation, and 37.97 PRQ(T) / 41.81 PRQ(S) for panoptic segmentation. On Replica, it reaches 47.93 mIoU, 54.94 mAcc, 41.02 PRQ(T), and 12.83 PRQ(S). Among online methods, OnlinePG is the best on all four metrics across both datasets. The system runs at 18 FPS on simple scenes and 10 FPS on complex scenes (excluding VLM inference).

## Baselines & Comparisons

OnlinePG outperforms both online baselines by a large margin: on ScanNetV2 it gains +17.2 mIoU over O2V-Mapping (48.48 vs 33.74) and +17.2 mIoU over OnlineAnySeg (48.48 vs 31.28), with corresponding mAcc gains of +10.5 and +13.8. In panoptic metrics, OnlinePG surpasses OnlineAnySeg by +2.0 PRQ(T) and +15.5 PRQ(S) on ScanNetV2, and by +6.8 PRQ(T) and +3.7 PRQ(S) on Replica. Against the strongest offline panoptic method PanoGS, OnlinePG actually exceeds it on ScanNetV2 panoptic metrics (37.97 vs 33.84 PRQ(T); 41.81 vs 36.22 PRQ(S)) but trails on mIoU (48.48 vs 50.72) and mAcc (66.01 vs 70.20). On Replica, PanoGS still leads on mIoU (54.98 vs 47.93) and PRQ(S) (30.60 vs 12.83), though OnlinePG narrows the gap substantially. The paper attributes the remaining gap to offline methods using ground-truth point clouds and global multi-view information, whereas OnlinePG operates purely from streaming RGB-D input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: 3D Gaussian Splatting-based scene reconstruction (building on 3DGS [11] and Gaussian Splatting SLAM [23]), open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding via lifted 2D VLM features (building on LERF [12], OpenScene [31], LangSplat [33], PanoGS [58]), and online 3D semantic/panoptic mapping (building on PanopticFusion [28], O2V-Mapping [42], OnlineAnySeg [41]). The key departure is that prior open-vocabulary 3DGS methods are offline and lack instance-level reasoning, while prior online methods lack panoptic instance discrimination. OnlinePG bridges this gap by introducing a local-to-global paradigm where noisy 2D VLM priors are first consolidated into consistent 3D instances via multi-cue graph clustering within a sliding window, then fused into a global map through bidirectional bipartite matching with explicit voxel-based spatial attribute grids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical bottleneck for embodied AI: robots need to simultaneously explore, reconstruct, and semantically understand environments in real time with open-vocabulary queries, a capability no prior system fully provides. The local-to-global sliding-window design with multi-cue clustering and bidirectional matching offers a principled way to handle the inherent noise and inconsistency of 2D VLM priors in streaming settings, achieving near-offline panoptic quality (within ~2 mIoU of PanoGS) at 10–18 FPS. Key limitations remain: the system cannot handle dynamic objects and still requires depth and pose inputs, leaving fully feed-forward, pose-free, depth-free online open-vocabulary panoptic mapping as an open problem.
