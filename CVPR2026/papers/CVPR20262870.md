## Problem

Energy-based test-time adaptation methods rely on iterative stochastic sampling (e.g., SGLD) to approximate energy gradients, incurring prohibitive computational overhead that limits real-world deployment. Recent sampling-free alternatives require maintaining a replay buffer of source training data at test time, introducing privacy vulnerabilities. The paper asks: how can one achieve effective energy alignment during TTA without costly sampling procedures and without accessing source domain data during inference?

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled RGB test images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet-200, 224×224 for PACS) and produces standard class label predictions. During a pre-testing phase, the pre-trained model's energy outputs on training data are used to train a small MLP that maps scalar energy values to complex-valued wave representations; at test time, only the frozen MLP and unlabeled test images are needed. Adaptation is performed by fine-tuning normalization layer parameters (BatchNorm or GroupNorm) of the backbone network, with no gradient updates to other layers.

## Method

APT reformulates scalar energy as a complex-valued wave ψ(x,t) whose amplitude encodes energy uncertainty and whose phase encodes adaptation direction, governed by a second-order wave equation from distributed-parameter control system theory. Before test time, an MLP is trained with a physics-informed loss that enforces the wave equation, continuity at a critical energy threshold (set to the mean training energy), and vanishing boundary conditions, producing a differentiable mapping from energy to wave. At test time, the method minimizes the wave probability density |Ψ(E)|² in high-energy regions (above the threshold) while simultaneously enforcing the probability current conservation law ∂ρ/∂t + ∇·j = 0, which directs probability mass from error-prone high-energy regions toward accurate low-energy regions. This transforms TTA into a constrained control-system evolution: the penalization loss specifies the destination (low-density regions) while the wave-equation loss ensures valid evolutionary pathways, achieving adaptive energy redistribution in a single forward-backward pass per test batch without any stochastic sampling.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-C (severity 5), APT achieves 86.93% accuracy with mCE 31.82, outperforming the best baseline CRKD (84.02%, mCE 42.52) and TEA (83.34%, mCE 43.69). On CIFAR-100-C, APT reaches 67.55% accuracy (mCE 51.81), surpassing TEA (65.10%, mCE 56.18) by 2.45 points. On TinyImageNet-200-C, APT attains 33.29% accuracy (mCE 85.98), exceeding TEA (31.67%, mCE 87.99). Across all three corruption benchmarks with WRN-28-10, APT improves over the best baseline by an average of 2.21% in accuracy while requiring only 1/3 to 1/7 of the adaptation time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

APT outperforms all 10 baselines on both accuracy and mCE across all three corruption benchmarks (CIFAR-10-C, CIFAR-100-C, TinyImageNet-200-C) at severity 5. The closest competitor is CRKD on CIFAR-10-C (84.02% vs. 86.93%, a 2.91-point gap), while on CIFAR-100-C the nearest rival is TEA (65.10% vs. 67.55%, a 2.45-point gap). On TinyImageNet-200-C, APT leads TEA by 1.62 points (33.29% vs. 31.67%). Entropy-based methods (TENT, EATA, SAR) and pseudo-label methods (PL, SHOT) consistently underperform energy-based approaches, with TENT achieving only 81.41% on CIFAR-10-C. On the PACS domain generalization task (ResNet-18), APT improves average accuracy over TEA by 1.49–4.05 points depending on source domain, with the largest gain on Cartoon source (35.01% vs. 30.12%). A critical practical advantage is speed: APT requires only 1/3 to 1/7 of the adaptation time of the top-3 baselines (TEA, CRKD, DISTA), making it suitable for real-time deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the energy-based TTA line initiated by TEA [37] and ETA/EATA [28], which rely on SGLD or MCMC sampling to approximate energy gradients at test time. It departs from both the sampling-based energy methods and the recent sampling-free approach of Han et al. [13] (which requires a source-data replay buffer, creating privacy vulnerabilities) by reformulating the energy landscape as a complex-valued wave governed by a control-system wave equation. The wave formulation draws on physics-informed neural network ideas and the state-space/control-system perspective popularized by Mamba [6,10], but applies it to a fundamentally different problem: guiding probability current redistribution across an energy landscape rather than modeling sequential dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: eliminating SGLD/MCMC sampling reduces adaptation time to 1/3–1/7 of top baselines while removing the need for source data at inference, making energy-based TTA viable in privacy-sensitive, high-throughput deployments. The probability-current-conservation constraint provides a principled mechanism to avoid the mode collapse and energy-landscape degradation that plague direct energy minimization, as visualized in Fig.1(a). Open questions include whether the bounded geodesic-distance assumption (d(D_train, D_test) ≤ ε) holds for severe distribution shifts, whether the wave formulation generalizes beyond image classification to other modalities, and whether the MLP-based wave encoder scales to larger architectures or higher-dimensional energy spaces.
