## Problem

Existing VLM-based deepfake detectors (e.g., CLIP adapters) focus exclusively on refining the visual encoder while ignoring the rich vision-language alignment semantics that constitute the VLM's most distinctive strength. Moreover, CLIP's visual encoder, trained for semantic object recognition, distributes attention to task-irrelevant regions rather than subtle forgery artifacts, limiting its discriminability for authenticity reasoning.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB face video frames at spatial resolution h×w along with identity-aware text prompts of the form 'This is a real/fake photo of <id> person,' where the <id> placeholder token is replaced at inference by the visual class token embedding from the final ViT block, effectively injecting a per-identity prior into the text modality. The primary output is a scalar authenticity score (higher = fake) obtained by a weighted combination of a global-level score (from enriched class tokens) and a local-level score (from fused spatial maps). Auxiliary outputs include a pixel-level forgery localization map and a patch-level VLA attention map that highlight manipulated regions.

## Method

VLAForge introduces two complementary components on top of a frozen CLIP backbone. First, ForgePerceiver is a lightweight ViT with learnable query tokens that produces (i) head-specific forgery-aware masks, injected as attention biases into CLIP's self-attention to enrich the replicated class tokens with artifact-specific semantics, and (ii) a coarse forgery localization map via query-visual similarity, supervised by MSE against ground-truth masks; an orthogonality loss enforces diversity across query-wise masks. Second, Identity-Aware VLA Scoring replaces the <id> token in real/fake text prompts with the visual class token embedding, feeds the refined prompts through CLIP's text encoder, and computes a VLA attention map by softmax-normalizing the similarity between ID-aware text features and patch-token embeddings (projected via a learnable adapter), supervised by Dice loss against forgery masks. The VLA attention map is element-wise multiplied with the forgery localization map and passed through a fusion network to yield the local-level score. Training jointly optimizes four losses (global CE, local CE, localization MSE, VLA Dice) plus the orthogonality regularizer, and inference combines the two branch scores with a tunable weight α.

## Results

On frame-level cross-dataset evaluation, VLAForge achieves 93.9% AUROC on CelebDF-v1, 91.2% on CelebDF-v2, 87.0% on DFDC, and 93.6% on DeepfakeDetection, surpassing the strongest baseline ForAda by up to 2.7% AUROC (on DFDC). At the video level, VLAForge reaches 96.8% (CDF-v2), 89.6% (DFDC), and 97.2% (DFD), improving over the best competitor by up to 2.4% AUROC. On full-face generation forgeries (Table 2), VLAForge achieves 98.4% frame-level AUROC on VQGAN and 99.7% video-level on both VQGAN and StyleGAN-XL, substantially outperforming ForAda (e.g., 77.4% vs. 69.0% on SiT frame-level).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLAForge outperforms all 25 compared baselines across every dataset and evaluation level. Among VLM-based methods, ForAda (CVPR'25) is the strongest competitor, achieving 91.4/90.0/84.3/93.3 frame-level AUROC; VLAForge improves upon it by 2.5/1.2/2.7/0.3 points respectively, with the largest gap on DFDC. RepDFD (AAAI'25), another VLM-based approach, trails VLAForge by 10.9 points on CDF-v1 and 9.7 on DFDC frame-level, confirming that merely reprogramming CLIP with global cosine similarity is insufficient. Non-VLM methods such as LSDA (CVPR'24, 86.7/83.0/73.6/88.0) and CDFA (ECCV'24, 89.9/78.7) are consistently outperformed by 5–13 points, highlighting the advantage of leveraging cross-modal semantics. On the more challenging full-face generation benchmarks (Table 2), VLAForge's advantage widens: it beats ForAda by 4.5 points on VQGAN, 5.5 on StyleGAN-XL, 8.4 on SiT, and 8.7 on DiT at the frame level, demonstrating superior generalization to GAN- and diffusion-based forgeries that lack visible blending artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP as its VLM backbone and departs from prior VLM-based deepfake detection methods (ForAda, UDD, FCG, Yan et al.) that focus exclusively on adapting the visual encoder via adapters, bias mitigation, or spatiotemporal modeling. It also distinguishes itself from RepDFD, which reprograms VLMs for DFD but relies solely on global image-level cosine similarity between visual and textual features, and from FFTG, which enriches datasets with synthesized text pairs rather than adapting the alignment mechanism itself. The key conceptual departure is exploiting cross-modal vision-language semantics at patch-level granularity, conditioned on identity priors injected into text prompts, to produce fine-grained authenticity cues that purely visual approaches cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identity-aware VLA scoring mechanism offers a principled way to leverage VLMs' pretrained cross-modal alignment for fine-grained forgery localization, potentially transferable to other detection tasks where identity-conditioned semantics matter. However, the method still requires pixel-level forgery masks for supervising both the localization loss and the VLA attention loss, limiting its applicability to settings where such annotations are available or can be approximated. The notably weaker performance on diffusion-based generators (SiT, DiT) compared to GAN-based ones suggests that the learned forgery cues may not fully capture the more coherent, artifact-free traces of modern diffusion models. Open questions include whether the identity prior mechanism generalizes to non-face forgeries or to settings without explicit identity embeddings.
