## Problem

Despite rapid advances in multimodal large language models, it remains unclear whether they can perform embodied 3D proximity reasoning—the spatial relations between the body and surrounding objects—from a first-person perspective. Existing egocentric VQA benchmarks focus on causality, planning, or grounding but leave 3D proximity reasoning during daily activities entirely unexplored, leaving a critical gap in assessing the perception-action coupling that underpins human spatial intelligence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261501/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a temporally ordered egocentric video clip (frames from a first-person fisheye camera, sampled so the answer is inferable from the last observable frame x_T) paired with a natural language question specifying a goal or manipulation context. For Intention, Exploration, and Exploitation tasks, the output is a multiple-choice selection over discretized proximity measurements (angular rotations, translational displacements, or spatial predicates like left-right/front-back). For Chain of Actions, the output is an ordered sequence of 3–5 action steps drawn from a 10-candidate set, with each consecutive pair annotated by a relative spatial relation on the image plane of the final frame.

## Method

The paper introduces an agent-based data engine built on Gemini-2.5-Pro that orchestrates a suite of specialized 3D tools—Salient Clip Sampler, Occupancy Map Generator, Exploration Path Generator (8-connected A* search), Spatial Calculator (distance and direction), Gaze Parser, Affordance Detector, Keystep Extractor, and Chain Constructor—to automatically synthesize high-quality VQA pairs across four task categories organized along a cognitive hierarchy (Intention → Exploration → Exploitation → Chain of Actions). The benchmark is constructed from EgoExo4D and Aria Digital Twin, leveraging their complementary annotations (upper-body pose and atomic actions vs. dense 3D object labels). For evaluation, all MLLMs are tested zero-shot with a unified chain-of-thought prompt; for additional analysis, LoRA fine-tuning on as few as 800 task-specific or 1,200 dataset-specific samples reveals large cross-domain gains, demonstrating that current MLLMs contain latent spatial knowledge that structured supervision can unlock without introducing new parameters.

## Results

On the EgoProx benchmark, all evaluated MLLMs fall substantially short of human performance across all four task categories. The best proprietary model, Gemini-2.5-Pro, achieves 42.75% on Intention (Approximate) and 50.24% on Exploitation (Approximate), compared to human levels of 62.50% and 82.02% respectively. The Chain-of-Actions task is the most challenging: even Gemini-2.5-Pro reaches only 25.14% Action Accuracy versus 80.23% for humans. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B leads with 46.21% on Exploitation (Approximate), but scaling from 7B to 72B yields only marginal gains, confirming that model size alone does not close the spatial reasoning gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-5 are the strongest proprietary baselines, with Gemini leading on Intention Approximate (42.75 vs. 33.16) and Exploitation Approximate (50.24 vs. 46.45), while GPT-5 edges ahead on Exploration Approximate (41.18 vs. 36.90) and Relational Accuracy-Strict (20.83 vs. 17.03) for Chain-of-Actions. Both proprietary models substantially outperform all open-source models, particularly on Exploration tasks where GPT-5 reaches 41.18% versus a best open-source score of 30.48% (Qwen2.5-VL-32B). Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B achieves the highest Exploitation Approximate score (46.21%) and the best Chain-of-Actions Action Accuracy (13.04%), but scaling from 7B to 72B yields only ~7% absolute gain on Exploitation, indicating diminishing returns. The instruction-tuning experiments (Tables 3–4) further show that fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on just 800 Intention samples boosts its Exploitation Approximate score from 38.63% to 64.93%, surpassing GPT-5's zero-shot 46.45%, demonstrating that latent spatial knowledge exists but requires structured supervision to unlock.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of egocentric video understanding and 3D spatial reasoning, two threads that have developed largely in parallel. Prior egocentric VQA benchmarks (EgoSchema, EgoPlan, EgoGazeVQA) focus on narrative causality, goal-oriented planning, or gaze-informed reasoning but do not explicitly evaluate 3D proximity relations between the observer's body and surrounding objects. Conversely, spatial intelligence benchmarks (ScanQA, MMSI-Bench, VSI-Bench) operate in exocentric, scene-centric settings and ignore the embodied, first-person perspective. The paper also departs from prior VQA construction methodologies by replacing MLLM-with-human-in-the-loop or pure human annotation with an agent-based pipeline that orchestrates specialized 3D analysis tools (occupancy maps, A* pathfinding, gaze parsing, affordance detection) to generate ground truth at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoProx fills a clear gap by being the first benchmark to assess 3D perception-action coupling from a first-person viewpoint, which is directly relevant to applications in smart glasses, AR, and robotics. The cross-domain instruction-tuning finding—that a small amount of task-specific data (especially Intention) unlocks spatial knowledge already present in pretrained MLLMs—suggests the bottleneck is retrieval and alignment rather than knowledge acquisition, which has implications for how future embodied MLLMs should be trained. Limitations include the modest benchmark size (2,405 samples), reliance on only two source datasets with complementary but incomplete annotations, and tuning experiments restricted to a single model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), leaving open whether the latent-knowledge hypothesis generalizes across architectures and scales.
