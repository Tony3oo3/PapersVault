## Problem

Combining independently trained LoRA modules for content and style in personalized image generation suffers from three persistent issues: entangled representations that prevent clean content-style separation, a lack of fine-grained semantic control over which subject attributes to preserve, and unstable weight fusion that typically requires additional optimization. Existing methods either collapse the rich hierarchy of visual attributes into a single coarse token or modify LoRA parameters during fusion, risking loss of identity or stylistic fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt annotated with explicit content and style markers (e.g., "A photo of a person <c> smiling in a watercolor style <s>"), a single content reference image, and a single style reference image. At inference, users can additionally tune continuous scalars γ_c and γ_s in [0,1] to adjust the relative strength of each branch, enabling compositions like "preserve content but replace style" without retraining. The output is a standard diffusion-generated image at SDXL resolution that integrates both the specified subject identity and artistic rendering.

## Method

CRAFT-LoRA integrates three complementary components. First, a rank-constrained backbone fine-tuning stage learns separate orthonormal subspaces for content and style via QR decomposition of learnable basis matrices, projecting out backbone components along those subspaces with a hierarchical rank schedule (r_max=128 to r_min=4) that allocates more capacity to earlier layers where content and style are more entangled; this is trained on 100 contrastive pairs constructed via Gaussian frequency-domain decomposition (σ=0.35). Second, an expert encoder with specialized branches routes prompt semantics to content and style LoRA adapters operating on disjoint layer subsets (lower/middle layers for content, higher layers for style), enabling selective aggregation and fine-grained control. Third, a training-free Asymmetric Classifier-Free Guidance (ACFG) scheme applies time-dependent activation schedules—content LoRA active in early-to-mid timesteps [1,35] and style LoRA in mid-to-late timesteps [15,50]—on the conditional path while anchoring the unconditional path to the rank-limited backbone, so the guidance signal isolates adapter effects without conflating them with the unconditional baseline.

## Results

CRAFT-LoRA achieves the highest scores across all three automatic metrics: Content Similarity (CLIP-I) of 0.79, Style Similarity (CLIP-I) of 0.80, and GPT-4o Combination Score of 0.83, evaluated over 10 content-style pairs with 10 generated images per pair. In a user study with 30 participants rating 50 samples on a 1–5 scale, the method scores 4.1 (content fidelity), 4.3 (style fidelity), and 4.4 (coherence), the highest among all compared methods. The ablation confirms that rank-constrained fine-tuning (Rank-FT) is the single most important component, contributing +0.08 Content Sim. and +0.10 Style Sim. over the no-component baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRAFT-LoRA outperforms all five baselines on every metric. Against BLoRA (the strongest baseline at 0.74 Content Sim. and 0.77 Comb. Score), CRAFT-LoRA gains +0.05 Content Sim., +0.10 Style Sim., and +0.06 Comb. Score. StyleDrop achieves the highest Style Sim. among baselines (0.76) but lags significantly on Content Sim. (0.68) and Comb. Score (0.70), reflecting its style-biased design. ZipLoRA (0.70/0.69/0.73) and KLoRA (0.71/0.72/0.76) show more balanced but lower overall performance, with CRAFT-LoRA leading by 0.09–0.10 in Content Sim. and 0.07–0.10 in Comb. Score. LoRA.rar (0.73/0.71/0.76) is competitive on Content Sim. but still trails CRAFT-LoRA by 0.06 on Comb. Score. In the user study, CRAFT-LoRA leads BLoRA by 0.6 in content fidelity, 0.9 in style fidelity, and 0.8 in coherence, confirming that the automatic metric advantages translate to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRAFT-LoRA extends the LoRA-based personalization pipeline by intervening at the backbone level rather than solely at the fusion stage. It draws directly on PaRa's parameter rank reduction (learnable basis matrices with QR decomposition) and MAML-inspired initialization to create a rank-constrained U-Net that structurally separates content and style subspaces before any LoRA is trained. This departs from prior composition methods (ZipLoRA, BLoRA, KLoRA, LoRA.rar) that treat the backbone as fixed and attempt to reconcile entangled LoRA weights post-hoc. The asymmetric CFG mechanism further distinguishes the work by anchoring the unconditional diffusion path to the modified backbone, a design not present in standard CFG or in prior training-free fusion approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-time rank-constrained backbone fine-tuning (5000 steps on 2×4090) provides a reusable initialization that improves all downstream LoRA training, and the training-free ACFG can be applied to existing SDXL LoRAs without retraining, offering a practical drop-in improvement for practitioners. However, the method's reliance on frequency-domain decomposition for content-style separation introduces assumptions that break down for flat-color or abstract styles, and the two-branch architecture limits multi-concept or multi-style mixing. Open questions include whether automated layer assignment can replace the manual content/style layer partitioning, whether the approach generalizes to non-U-Net architectures (e.g., KOALA, SANA), and whether the contrastive-pair training strategy scales beyond 100 curated pairs without diminishing returns.
