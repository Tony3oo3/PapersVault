## Problem

Sparse attention for video diffusion models faces a trade-off: static patterns lack content adaptivity, while dynamic methods incur persistent per-step mask recalculation overhead. The paper challenges the assumption that attention patterns are volatile, showing empirically that they exhibit strong temporal stability and that required computational density progressively decays across denoising steps, making continuous re-computation both redundant and suboptimal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261741/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

RAPID operates as a drop-in replacement for the attention module in existing DiT-based video diffusion models (Wan2.1-14B at 768p/81 frames, HunyuanVideo at 768p/129 frames), taking a text prompt as input and producing a generated video. No architectural modification or retraining is required; the method intercepts the standard self-attention computation and substitutes a block-sparse variant using a cached mask, with all latency measured end-to-end on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU.

## Method

RAPID performs a single high-fidelity importance estimation at an early denoising step (after a short dense warm-up), computing per-block attention mass scores and generating a binary sparse mask via a hybrid Top-K + Top-p selection strategy that guarantees minimum connectivity while adapting to content complexity. Both the score matrix and the resulting mask are cached and reused for all subsequent steps, eliminating per-step recalculation. A Turbo mode further exploits the observed density decay by re-thresholding the cached scores at a later step (e.g., 25% of total steps) to activate a more aggressive sparsity level, achieving up to 2.01× speedup without additional computation beyond a simple re-thresholding operation.

## Results

On Wan2.1-14B (768P, 81 frames), RAPID's default mode achieves 26.11 PSNR, 0.871 SSIM, and 0.096 LPIPS at ~42% attention density, outperforming the best baseline (Radial Attention: 22.92 PSNR) by 3.2 PSNR points while delivering a 1.53× speedup. On HunyuanVideo (768P, 129 frames), RAPID reaches 31.49 PSNR, 0.948 SSIM, and 0.073 LPIPS at ~43% density, surpassing Radial Attention (27.50 PSNR) by 4.0 PSNR points with a 1.73× speedup. RAPID-Turbo pushes acceleration to 1.79× (Wan2.1-14B) and 2.01× (HunyuanVideo) at lower densities (27.2% and 31.5% respectively) while maintaining quality on par with the strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAPID's default mode decisively outperforms all three baselines on every quality metric at comparable attention density (~42–44%). On Wan2.1-14B, it beats Radial Attention (the strongest baseline) by 3.2 PSNR (26.11 vs 22.92), 0.076 SSIM, and 0.101 LPIPS; on HunyuanVideo, the margin widens to 4.0 PSNR (31.49 vs 27.50). X-Attention is the weakest baseline on quality in both models (e.g., 21.37 PSNR on Wan2.1-14B), while SVG sits in the middle. In terms of speed at matched density, Radial Attention is the fastest baseline (1.56× on Wan2.1-14B, 1.79× on HunyuanVideo), and RAPID's default mode is slightly slower (1.53×, 1.73×) but delivers substantially higher fidelity. RAPID-Turbo trades some quality for maximum speed (1.79× and 2.01×), landing at a quality level comparable to SVG and Radial Attention while achieving the highest speedup of any method tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAPID carves new ground by challenging the core assumption of dynamic sparse attention methods (X-Attention, Draft Attention) that attention patterns are volatile and must be re-evaluated at every denoising step. It repurposes the caching philosophy from feature-reuse methods (DeepCache, Learning-to-Cache) but applies it to attention block importance scores rather than intermediate activations, and borrows the hybrid top-k/top-p selection strategy from long-context LLM inference (MInference, FlexPrefill) into the video diffusion setting. The result is a third paradigm—neither static (SVG, Radial Attention) nor fully dynamic—that performs a single high-fidelity scoring pass and reuses the cached mask and scores for the remainder of inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1.5–2× speedup on 14B-parameter video diffusion models is directly useful for production pipelines, and the 4.0 PSNR quality gain over the best baseline at matched density suggests that per-step mask recomputation was not merely redundant but actively harmful to fidelity. The empirical finding of strong temporal stability in attention patterns (high recall/precision of an early mask across all later steps) is the key conceptual contribution that could inform sparsity strategies beyond video diffusion. Open questions include whether the one-shot paradigm generalizes to longer sequences, different architectures, or conditional generation settings, and whether learned or scheduled sparsity thresholds (as the authors suggest) could push the quality-speed frontier further.
