## Problem

Existing language-distilled Gaussian and radiance field methods operate at a single semantic granularity and treat objects in isolation, failing on queries that involve spatial relations ("the cup next to the laptop") or object parts ("the keyboard of the laptop"). Prior relational approaches such as RelationField require hours of per-scene optimization and render below 10 fps, making them impractical for scalable deployment. The paper seeks a unified, training-free framework that simultaneously captures multi-level semantic hierarchy and explicit inter-object relationships within a single 3D Gaussian scene.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-reconstructed 3D Gaussian field (standard 3DGS output) as its sole geometric input, along with natural-language text queries at inference time; no per-scene training data or additional sensor modalities are required. For the LLM-based relation lifting variant, per-view Gaussian ID maps are rendered and fed to a multimodal LLM (GPT-4V) with Set-of-Mark prompting. Outputs include a nested hierarchical Gaussian scene (sub-part → part → object clusters, each with a CLIP-aligned embedding), an explicit open-vocabulary 3D scene graph whose edges carry Jina-embedding relation vectors, and 3D segmentation masks returned for relation-guided triplet queries ⟨s, p, o⟩.

## Method

ReLaGS is a two-stage, training-free pipeline. Stage 1 builds a multi-hierarchy Gaussian scene by extending THGS's superpoint-based clustering with two novel refinements: Maximum Weight Pruning removes Gaussians whose peak rendering contribution across all views falls below a threshold, eliminating floaters that corrupt boundaries; Robust Outlier-Aware Feature Aggregation computes per-view CLIP feature consistency via mean cosine similarity, applies Z-score normalization, and discards features below a threshold before averaging, yielding stable object-level language embeddings. Stage 2 constructs an explicit 3D scene graph at the object level (inter-object) and at lower levels (intra-object part graphs), with edges encoded as Jina-embedding relation vectors. Relations are obtained either by lifting SoM-prompted LLM annotations from rendered ID maps into 3D (high-quality but sparse) or by a lightweight residual GNN pretrained on 3RScan with contrastive learning, which predicts open-vocabulary relation embeddings directly from fused language-geometry node features and transfers to the Gaussian domain without fine-tuning. The hierarchical structure enables a tree-search querying algorithm that adaptively matches query granularity (whole object vs. part) and a triplet-based relational search that ranks candidate subject-object pairs by subject-text, object-text, and predicate-relation alignment.

## Results

On the 3DSSG (RIO10) benchmark for 3D scene graph prediction, ReLaGS (GNN-predicted) achieves Object R@5 of 0.68 and Predicate R@5 of 0.87, outperforming RelationField (0.69 / 0.82) on predicate recall by +0.05 while matching on object recall, and surpassing the scene-agnostic Open3DSG (0.56 / 0.65) by a large margin. On relationship-guided 3D instance segmentation (ScanNet++), ReLaGS attains mIoU 0.56, exceeding the best training-based competitor RelationField (0.53) without any per-scene training. On open-vocabulary segmentation (LeRF-OVS), ReLaGS achieves mean mIoU 64.4%, the best among both training-based and training-free methods, and on ScanNet semantic segmentation it reaches 47.17 mIoU / 66.08 mAcc on the 10-class subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RelationField [19], the closest training-based competitor, ReLaGS (GNN) wins on predicate recall (R@3: 0.79 vs 0.76, R@5: 0.87 vs 0.82) while matching on object recall (R@5: 0.68 vs 0.69), and does so 4.7× faster (12.6 min vs 60 min) with 7.6× less GPU memory (7.5 GB vs 32 GB). Against the scene-agnostic Open3DSG [18], ReLaGS improves object R@5 by +0.12 and predicate R@5 by +0.22, demonstrating that the Gaussian-based representation with hierarchical structure substantially outperforms point-cloud-only scene graphs. On relationship-guided segmentation (ScanNet++), ReLaGS (mIoU 0.56) surpasses all training-based methods including RelationField (0.53), LangSplat (0.49), and OpenNeRF (0.45), as well as the training-free THGS (0.29). On LeRF-OVS open-vocabulary segmentation, ReLaGS (64.4% mean mIoU) edges out LAGA (64.0%), VALA (61.7%), and Occam's LGS (61.3%), with the largest gains in cluttered scenes like Figurines (+6.6 over THGS) and Teatime (+12.7 over THGS). The VLM-based variant (SoM-LLM lifting) matches object recall but performs poorly on predicate prediction (R@5: 0.35), confirming that the GNN-based relation prediction is the more effective route for scalable scene graph construction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on THGS [7] for its training-free hierarchical Gaussian clustering (superpoints → parts → objects via SAM-guided merging) and extends it with two novel refinements: Maximum Weight Pruning to remove geometrically irrelevant Gaussians and Robust Outlier-Aware Feature Aggregation to filter inconsistent multi-view CLIP features. For relational reasoning, it departs from RelationField [19], which learns relation fields via per-scene optimization (hours of training, <10 fps), by instead using a lightweight residual GNN pretrained on 3RScan point-cloud data or lifting 2D LLM annotations via Set-of-Mark prompting. The work also positions itself against Open3DSG [18] and ConceptGraphs [10] for open-vocabulary 3D scene graph generation, but is the first to embed both hierarchical part-object structure and explicit relational edges within a single Gaussian splatting representation without scene-specific training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying multi-level semantic hierarchy and explicit relational edges in one training-free Gaussian field, ReLaGS provides a practical foundation for structured 3D understanding (compositional + relational reasoning) at a fraction of the cost of prior relational methods (12.6 min / 7.5 GB vs. 60 min / 32 GB for RelationField). The finding that a GNN pretrained on point-cloud-image features transfers directly to Gaussian splatting without fine-tuning suggests the relational latent space is modality-robust, potentially enabling broader reuse of pretrained graph models. Open questions include scalability to large outdoor scenes, whether the GNN's cross-dataset generalization holds beyond indoor environments, and how the framework handles dynamic or partially observed scenes where the hierarchical clustering assumptions may break down.
