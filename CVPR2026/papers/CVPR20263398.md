## Problem

Existing MLLMs for fine-grained visual understanding treat region-level captioning and pixel-level grounding as independent tasks, and even unified approaches merely expand task-specific data without exploring the intrinsic relationship between their representation tokens. The paper asks: what is the semantic connection between the visual prompt embedding <VP> (input to captioning) and the segmentation token [SEG] (output of grounding) that both refer to the same region, and can enforcing consistency between them mutually benefit both tasks?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263398/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image, a text prompt, and optionally one or more region masks as visual prompts for captioning. For grounding, it outputs [SEG] tokens that are decoded by SAM into pixel-level masks; for captioning, it generates free-form textual descriptions conditioned on the visual prompt embeddings. The visual prompt embedding itself is a composite of a semantic mask token (<mask>) and a spatial position token (<pos>), both produced by the hybrid region extractor. The novel DL-RES task inverts the grounding direction, taking highly detailed localized descriptions (reversed from Describe Anything captions) as the referring expression to localize and segment the target.

## Method

FCLM, built on Qwen2.5-VL, introduces three coupled designs. First, a hybrid region extractor progressively refines visual prompt embeddings through local aggregation (Focal Cropping + SAM encoder + mask pooling), pixel enhancement (cross-attention with high-resolution SAM features), and semantic guidance (deformable attention sampling global visual-encoder features at the region center), while a separate position branch encodes mask geometry and center coordinates. Second, a consistency learning loss enforces alignment between <pos> and [SEG]: Stage 1 uses a self-reconstruction loss that decodes <pos> through the SAM mask decoder and compares the result to the original visual prompt mask, while Stage 2 replaces this with a cosine-similarity latent loss that directly supervises [SEG] using <pos> as a pseudo-label. Third, a two-stage training pipeline first trains all modules jointly on captioning, grounding, and GCG tasks, then freezes the extractor and decoder to fine-tune only the LLM backbone on the DL-RES task, leveraging the already-aligned representation tokens for stable pseudo-label supervision.

## Results

On RefCOCO/+/g, FCLM-7B achieves 82.6/83.9/79.9 (val/testA/testB) cIoU on RefCOCO, 77.4/80.5/71.3 on RefCOCO+, and 79.4/80.5 on RefCOCOg, outperforming the strongest prior method PixelSAIL (80.9/82.6/79.0) and UniPixel-7B (80.8/83.0/77.4). On the proposed DL-RES task, FCLM-7B reaches 68.0 gIoU / 82.1 cIoU, surpassing UniPixel-7B (58.5/72.2) by a substantial margin of ~10 points. On ReasonSeg, FCLM-7B attains the best gIoU (64.1) but trails READ (67.6) on cIoU. On MUSE multi-target segmentation, FCLM-7B achieves 51.5 gIoU / 57.4 cIoU (test), outperforming PixelLM-13B (44.8/54.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FCLM-7B outperforms the strongest unified baseline UniPixel-7B on all RefCOCO/+/g splits (e.g., +1.8 cIoU on RefCOCO val, +3.5 on RefCOCO+ val, +3.0 on RefCOCOg val) and on DL-RES by a large margin (+9.5 gIoU, +9.9 cIoU). Against the pixel-grounding specialist PixelSAIL, FCLM-7B leads on every RefCOCO/+/g metric (e.g., +1.7 on RefCOCO val, +1.2 on RefCOCO+ val, +0.7 on RefCOCOg val). On ReasonSeg, FCLM-7B achieves the best gIoU (64.1 vs. UniPixel-7B's 60.5) but trails READ (67.6 cIoU) on cIoU, indicating that reasoning-based segmentation with explicit chain-of-thought still holds an edge in precise mask quality for that specific task. The 3B variant of FCLM already surpasses UniPixel-7B on DL-RES (66.1 vs. 58.5 gIoU) and matches or exceeds PixelSAIL on most RefCOCO splits, demonstrating strong parameter efficiency. On MUSE multi-target segmentation, FCLM-7B (51.5 gIoU) outperforms the larger PixelLM-13B (44.8 gIoU) by 6.7 points, and on GCG it surpasses GLaMM-4B across all metrics (e.g., 56.2 vs. 47.2 CIDEr, 67.6 vs. 66.3 mIoU on validation). Overall, FCLM achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA on 6 of 7 evaluated tasks, with the sole exception being ReasonSeg cIoU where READ retains a narrow advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

FCLM builds directly on the Osprey visual prompt embedding framework and the LISA-style [SEG] token paradigm for MLLM-based segmentation, unifying them within a Qwen2.5-VL backbone. It departs from prior unified approaches (UniPixel, UFO, GLaMM) that handle captioning and grounding through expanded data or pipeline design by explicitly enforcing semantic consistency between the two tasks' representation tokens via a self-reconstruction loss and a cosine-similarity latent loss. The hybrid region extractor extends Osprey's single-branch design with a progressive three-step semantic pipeline (focal cropping, pixel enhancement via cross-attention, and deformable-attention semantic guidance) borrowed from Describe Anything's Focal Cropping, addressing the known weakness of CLIP-based encoders for small-object detail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistency-learning formulation provides a principled mechanism for mutual reinforcement between region captioning and pixel grounding, and the introduced DL-RES benchmark fills a gap by testing whether models can localize targets from highly detailed (rather than concise) referring expressions—a setting more aligned with real-world usage. The ablation showing that the position token alone outperforms the combined mask+position token for self-reconstruction is a useful practical insight for visual prompt design. Open questions include whether the consistency signal generalizes beyond SAM-based decoding, whether it transfers to video or 3D spatial representations (boxes, points), and whether the gains persist when the base LLM is swapped for a different architecture.
