## Problem

Pretrained diffusion models for fabric material generation struggle to produce yarn-level microstructures that conform to weaving rules, often yielding artificial stripes, physically implausible patterns, or entirely omitted microstructural details. Existing procedural fabric models neglect natural irregularities such as yarn sliding and flyaway fibers, and still require expert artists to design weaving drafts and parameters manually.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262714/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a natural language text prompt (required) and an optional fabric image (flat or wrinkled) as input. It produces a tileable, microstructure-free albedo map from the diffusion branch, and from the LLM branch a binary weaving draft (constrained to at most 16×16, physically realizable on a 16-shaft loom) plus scalar fabric parameters (ply count, roughness, twist angle, etc.). The procedural model then synthesizes spatially-varying normal, tangent/orientation, and height fields on demand via analytical UV-space queries rather than precomputing fixed-resolution texture maps, enabling arbitrarily fine close-up rendering.

## Method

FabricGen decouples fabric generation into a macro-scale texture branch and a micro-scale geometry branch. The macro branch fine-tunes FLUX.1-dev with LyCORIS on 600 collected microstructure-free fabric textures (with noise rolling for tileability) to produce clean albedo maps, optionally conditioned on a VAE-encoded input image. The micro branch uses WeavingLLM—Qwen2.5-14Bit fine-tuned via QLoRA on 1,142 annotated weaving drafts from Handweaving.net and further prompt-tuned with domain-specific fabric expertise—to output a formatted weaving draft and parameter set from text. A novel curved-helix procedural model maps ply-level geometry analytically from UV coordinates (supporting multi-ply yarns), augmented with Perlin-noise-driven yarn sliding and a stochastic flyaway-fiber orientation field. The two branches are fused at render time using the SpongeCake layered shading model, combining the albedo with the procedural microstructure for photorealistic output.

## Results

On text-conditioned fabric generation, FabricGen achieves a CLIP Score of 0.317, surpassing DressCode (0.307) and MatFuse (0.240), and receives 82.55% user preference in a 12-case study with 64 participants, compared to 16.02% for DressCode and 1.43% for MatFuse. Under text-and-image conditioning, FabricGen attains a CLIP-I Score of 0.827 versus MatFuse's 0.722, demonstrating stronger consistency with the reference image. All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DressCode [4], the fabric-specific diffusion baseline, FabricGen wins on both CLIP Score (0.317 vs. 0.307) and user preference (82.55% vs. 16.02%), with the gap in user study being particularly large, reflecting DressCode's tendency to produce blurred microstructures and structural artifacts under close-up inspection. Against MatFuse [23], the general-purpose material diffusion model, FabricGen achieves a substantially higher CLIP Score (0.317 vs. 0.240) and a dominant user preference margin (82.55% vs. 1.43%), as MatFuse struggles to capture yarn-level details and often generates physically implausible patterns. Under image conditioning, FabricGen also outperforms MatFuse on CLIP-I Score (0.827 vs. 0.722), indicating better preservation of the input image's pattern style while adding procedural microstructure. In the ablation comparing WeavingLLM against GPT-5 [13] and the base Qwen2.5 model, the domain-fine-tuned LLM produces weaving drafts with higher Fourier-spectrum cosine similarity to reference drafts and stronger user preference, confirming that general-purpose LLMs lack the weaving-rule priors needed for realistic pattern generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the surface-based procedural fabric model of Jin et al. (SIGGRAPH Asia 2022) by introducing a multi-ply curved helix yarn representation and global irregularity effects (yarn sliding, flyaway fibers) that prior UV-space models neglected. It departs from prior text-to-fabric-material methods (DressCode, FabricDiffusion) that attempt to generate both macro texture and microstructure jointly via diffusion, instead decomposing the problem: a fine-tuned FLUX.1-dev model (via LyCORIS) produces microstructure-free albedo, while a QLoRA-fine-tuned Qwen2.5 LLM (WeavingLLM) drives a procedural geometric model for yarn-level detail. The LLM-driven procedural generation paradigm is, to the authors' knowledge, novel in the fabric domain, though the broader idea of using LLMs to parameterize procedural content has appeared in other creative domains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The macro/micro decomposition offers a practical recipe for any material domain where diffusion models struggle with fine structural constraints: let the generative model handle what it does well (color, pattern) and let a rule-based procedural model handle what it cannot (geometric microstructure). The LLM-as-designer approach lowers the barrier for non-expert users, and the on-demand procedural queries (no precomputed high-res maps) enable arbitrarily fine rendering. However, the method is limited to woven fabrics with repeat units up to 16×16, excluding knitted, jacquard, and embroidered textiles, and the 600-image fine-tuning dataset is small, raising questions about generalization to diverse fabric styles. Extending the LLM-driven procedural paradigm to other structured materials (e.g., wood grain, tile patterns) is a natural open direction.
