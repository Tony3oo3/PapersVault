## Problem

Conventional MLLM benchmarks compress complex model behavior into sparse aggregate scores, obscuring heterogeneous capability gaps and making it impossible to answer 'what changes and why' when models are retrained or fine-tuned. Closed-set evaluations are bounded by fixed knowledge scope, and the most significant weaknesses are often entangled in the long tail that static benchmarks miss. The paper asks whether an automated, interpretable auditing paradigm can systematically discover and rectify these hidden failure modes without human annotation.

## Input / Output

The auditor (a fine-tuned Gemma3-4B MLLM) takes an input RGB image (448×448 px for PaliGemma2) plus three instruction prompts and produces three coupled outputs: a probing question Q*, a diffusion caption C (fed to FLUX.1-dev) or an editing command E (fed to FLUX.1-Kontext-dev), and the resulting counterfactual image I*. These are combined into question-image pairs (Q*, I*), (Q*, I), or (Q, I*) that maximize response divergence between the target MLLM and a reference ensemble. At inference, a single forward pass on any image yields a tailored failure-inducing pair, which is then converted into weakness-targeted training samples with pseudo-labels from the ensemble consensus.

## Method

AuditDM fine-tunes an MLLM auditor via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), where the reward signal is a binary semantic-agreement judge measuring whether the target model's answer differs in meaning from the reference ensemble's consensus on the generated question-image pair. The auditor jointly learns three generation policies—probing question synthesis, diffusion caption writing for full image regeneration, and targeted image-editing commands—so that the resulting (Q*, I*) pairs expose capability gaps in a single inference pass. GRPO's group-relative normalization (Eq. 2) converts per-sample disagreement scores into advantages within each batch, steering the auditor toward inputs that reliably flip the target model's prediction while the ensemble remains consistent. For rectification, the trained auditor synthesizes large-scale, annotation-free training data via two strategies: augmenting existing labeled datasets with auditor-generated examples, or bootstrapping unlabeled images using multiple auditor checkpoints at different training steps to produce pseudo-labeled samples iteratively. The key distinction from adversarial attacks or generic data synthesis is that the auditor is explicitly optimized to find interpretable, recurring failure patterns (not one-off perturbations) and to produce data that directly closes the identified capability gaps.

## Results

On PaliGemma2-3B, AuditDM fine-tuning yields gains of +1.9 to +9.8 points across 8 per-task benchmarks, with the largest improvements on AI2D (+9.3, 76.0→85.3) and ChartQA (+9.8, 54.0→63.8), enabling the 3B model to surpass the 28B variant on 6 of 8 tasks. On Gemma3-4B, AuditDM delivers +5.6 to +74.3 point improvements across 8 general multimodal benchmarks, with the 4B model surpassing the 12B counterpart on MMBench (75.0 vs 73.8), Seed-Bench-IMG (72.9 vs 70.6), MMMU (45.2 vs 44.8), and RealWorldQA (61.4 vs 58.3). The auditor also achieves a 91.1% search success rate for failure discovery versus 21.4% for the prompt-engineering baseline over 20K attempts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PaliGemma2-28B (the official 28B fine-tuned on original data), the AuditDM-enhanced 3B model wins on 6 of 8 per-task benchmarks: VQAv2 (86.7 vs 85.8), GQA (71.1 vs 68.3), AI2D (85.3 vs 84.6), DocVQA (77.5 vs 76.1), ChartQA (63.8 vs 61.3), and RefCOCO (77.8 vs 77.3), while trailing slightly on OK-VQA (69.2 vs 70.6) and COCOCap (145.1 vs 145.2). Against Gemma3-12B, the AuditDM-enhanced 4B model surpasses it on 5 of 8 general benchmarks (MMBench, MMTBench, Seed-Bench-IMG, MMMU, RealWorldQA) but remains behind on MME (1450.3 vs 1517.3), MMStar (52.4 vs 55.7), and POPE (85.5 vs 86.0). The 4B+AuditDM model does not surpass the 27B variant on any benchmark, though it narrows the gap substantially (e.g., MMBench 75.0 vs 78.3). The paper also compares against a prompt-engineering baseline (no RL fine-tuning of the auditor), where AuditDM achieves a 91.1% failure-discovery success rate versus 21.4% for the baseline, a 4.3× improvement in search efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

AuditDM sits at the intersection of three prior threads: RL-based training (GRPO from DeepSeekMath), adversarial probing of MLLMs (jailbreaks, visual adversarial attacks), and synthetic data generation for vision-language models (ShareGPT4V, SynthVLM, concept perturbation). It departs from adversarial work by targeting inherent capability gaps rather than safety vulnerabilities, and from generic synthetic-data pipelines by using cross-model divergence as an explicit optimization signal. The closest conceptual ancestor is human-adversarial VQA (Sheng et al.), which AuditDM automates and extends to the visual modality via diffusion-based counterfactual generation, while the self-evolution/weak-to-strong learning literature provides the iterative improvement loop that AuditDM instantiates with a model-specific auditor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As public benchmarks saturate and data scaling yields diminishing returns, AuditDM offers a concrete, annotation-free mechanism for diagnosing and closing specific capability gaps, making it a practical tool for continual model improvement in production settings. The finding that a 3B model can surpass its 28B counterpart on targeted tasks (counting, color recognition, hallucination avoidance) challenges the assumption that scale uniformly improves all capabilities and suggests that targeted rectification can be more effective than brute-force scaling. Key open questions include the high computational cost (≈5 days on 8×H100 for one model's data synthesis), the difficulty of generating reliable pseudo-labels for dense prediction and diagram tasks, and whether the cross-model divergence signal generalizes across architecturally diverse model families beyond the PaliGemma2/Gemma3 pairs tested.
