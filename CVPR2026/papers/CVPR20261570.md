## Problem

Vision RWKV offers linear-complexity global modeling as an alternative to quadratic-attention Transformers for high-resolution pan-sharpening, but its rigid bidirectional raster scanning is semantic-agnostic and introduces positional bias that degrades fusion quality. Additionally, both Transformer and RWKV architectures achieve higher-order feature interactions only by stacking multiple blocks with full WKV recalculation at each layer, incurring substantial computational overhead, while RWKV's linear attention inherently acts as a low-pass filter that discards high-frequency spatial details critical for pan-sharpening.

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution panchromatic image (H×W×1) and a low-resolution multispectral image (h×w×C) as dual inputs, which are processed through separate modality-specific encoders incorporating half-instance normalization; the MS image is upsampled to match PAN resolution before encoding. The output is a full-resolution fused image (H×W×C) reconstructed by a decoder that adds the upsampled MS image as a residual to the aggregated high-order features, preserving spectral fidelity while injecting spatial detail from the PAN channel.

## Method

The core architecture replaces RWKV's fixed raster scan with a semantic-driven scanning strategy: locality-sensitive hashing clusters the value matrix into semantically coherent groups, tokens are reordered so related regions are processed contiguously, and a tri-token prompt (spatially-averaged global token, cluster-mean prototype tokens, and a learnable register token) is appended to the sequence before Bi-WKV processing; after the scan, hierarchical broadcasting propagates global and prototype features back to individual tokens while the register token absorbs and discards noisy artifacts. To achieve higher-order interactions without expensive block stacking, the method introduces WKV-sharing (reusing WKV computations across consecutive layers within a group) and a WKV-moment mechanism (momentum-based feature propagation across groups with a learnable α coefficient), reducing redundant computation. For high-frequency preservation, a central difference convolution is applied on the value pathway to counteract RWKV's low-pass behavior, and a multi-scale Q-shift operation (equivalent to depth-wise 3×3 convolution) is embedded within an invertible neural network to enable lossless, parameter-efficient spatial feature transformation.

## Results

On WorldView-II, the proposed method achieves PSNR 42.38 dB, SSIM 0.9737, SAM 0.0208, and ERGAS 0.8816, outperforming the strongest baseline PanFlowNet (41.85 dB) by 0.52 dB in PSNR. On GaoFen-2, it reaches PSNR 47.89 dB and SSIM 0.9901, surpassing SFINet (47.47 dB) by 0.42 dB. On WorldView-III, the method attains PSNR 31.31 dB and SSIM 0.9319, exceeding SFINet (30.60 dB) by 0.71 dB. The method consistently achieves the lowest SAM and ERGAS values across all three datasets, indicating superior spectral fidelity and spatial detail preservation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 13 baselines across every metric on all three datasets. Among traditional methods (SFIM, GS, Brovey, IHS, GFPCA), the gap is substantial—over 6 dB in PSNR on WorldView-II—confirming the well-known limitations of component-substitution approaches. Among deep learning baselines, the closest competitors are SFINet (ECCV 2022), PanFlowNet (ICCV 2023), and MutNet (CVPR 2022), which cluster within 0.1–0.5 dB of each other. The proposed method leads SFINet by 0.52 dB (WV-II), 0.71 dB (WV-III), and 0.42 dB (GF2) in PSNR, while also achieving the best SSIM, SAM, and ERGAS on every dataset. Notably, on GaoFen-2 the method ties SFINet on SSIM (0.9901) but clearly wins on SAM (0.0097 vs. 0.0102) and ERGAS (0.5115 vs. 0.5462), indicating better spectral preservation. The ablation study (Table 2) further confirms that all four components—Momentum, Average Token, Learn Token, and Prototype—contribute additively, with the full configuration yielding the best results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Vision-RWKV (Duan et al., 2025), a linear-complexity recurrent architecture for visual perception, and adapts it to the pan-sharpening domain for the first time. It departs from standard RWKV in three conceptual ways: replacing the rigid bidirectional raster scan with an LSH-clustering-driven semantic scanning order, augmenting the sequence with a tri-token prompt (global, prototype, register) inspired by prompt-learning ideas, and introducing a WKV-sharing/moment mechanism to achieve higher-order interactions without the cost of stacking additional blocks. In the broader pan-sharpening literature, it positions itself beyond the dominant CNN-based (PNN, PANNet, MSDCNN) and Transformer-based (SFINet, PanFlowNet) paradigms, arguing that linear-complexity recurrent models are underexplored for this task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical value is demonstrating that a linear-complexity RWKV backbone can match or exceed quadratic-complexity Transformer methods on pan-sharpening, which could matter for very high-resolution satellite imagery where memory and compute are binding constraints. However, the evidence is limited to three standard benchmarks with modest margins (0.5–0.8 dB PSNR over the best baseline), and the LSH-based semantic clustering is a heuristic that has not been validated on more diverse or larger-scale remote sensing scenes. Open questions include whether the semantic scanning strategy generalizes to other fusion tasks (e.g., hyperspectral-SAR), whether the WKV-sharing/moment mechanism transfers to other linear-attention architectures, and whether the gains persist at substantially higher resolutions where the quadratic-vs-linear complexity gap would be most pronounced.
