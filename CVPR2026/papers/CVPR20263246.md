## Problem

Existing few-shot Pretrain-and-Adapt methods for 3D talking head synthesis primarily target neutral speech and lack explicit emotion-aware motion modeling, leading to audio-emotion mismatch and geometric instability under expressive facial motion. The paper asks whether few-shot 3D talking head synthesis can move beyond neutral speech to support emotion-aware animation while maintaining structural stability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263246/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw driving audio waveform, a set of pose & expression frames (processed by a FLAME Tracker for head pose and an Expression Network for upper-face Action Unit cues), and a 5-second identity video for adaptation. It outputs a real-time 3D talking head video at 512×512 and 25 FPS rendered from 60K rigged 3D Gaussians, with the underlying motion represented as FLAME expression parameters and jaw pose offsets plus intra-oral Gaussian deformation offsets. Adaptation requires only 11 minutes of fine-tuning on a single GPU, and inference runs at 76.4 FPS.

## Method

EmoTaG reformulates motion prediction in the structured FLAME parameter space rather than directly deforming 3D Gaussians, introducing explicit geometric priors for stability. The core Gated Residual Motion Network (GRMN) comprises an Identity-Conditioned Encoder that fuses Wav2Vec 2.0 audio features, OpenFace Action Unit expression cues, and an AdaFace identity embedding via AdaIN-based modulation, followed by an Expert Motion Decoder with three cooperative branches: a Base branch for phonetic articulation, a Residual branch for emotion-driven deviations, and a Gate branch that adaptively fuses them with a learned scalar. Semantic Emotion Guidance distills a categorical emotion distribution and scalar intensity score from a pretrained DeepFace recognizer into the residual and gate branches, enabling emotion-aware learning without manual annotations. The predicted FLAME parameters drive a rigged FLAME-Gaussian model where each Gaussian is bound to a parent mesh triangle, with additional learned offsets applied to intra-oral Gaussians for fine-grained teeth and tongue articulation. During adaptation, the pretrained GRMN is frozen and only the AdaIN modulation parameters are fine-tuned from the 5-second video, enabling efficient identity-specific personalization.

## Results

On the 5-second self-reconstruction setting, EmoTaG achieves the best PSNR (30.02 neutral / 29.95 emotional), lowest LPIPS (0.019 / 0.022), lowest LMD (2.221 / 2.456), and lowest AUE (0.685 / 0.702) across all baselines on both neutral and emotional test sets. It also attains the lowest Sync-E (0.210 / 0.236), indicating the tightest audio-visual temporal alignment. In the OOD audio-driven evaluation, EmoTaG achieves the best Sync-C (5.814 cross-identity, 5.432 cross-language) and lowest Sync-E (9.133 / 9.662). Adaptation takes only 11 minutes and inference runs at 76.4 FPS on a single RTX A6000.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmoTaG outperforms all six baselines on PSNR, LPIPS, LMD, AUE, and Sync-E across both neutral and emotional sets, with the largest margins over the few-shot competitor InsTaG (e.g., LMD 2.221 vs. 3.145 neutral, 2.456 vs. 3.428 emotional; AUE 0.685 vs. 0.921 neutral). Against the train-from-scratch methods ER-NeRF and TalkingGaussian, EmoTaG improves PSNR by ~1.6–1.8 dB and reduces LMD by ~1.3–2.0 while requiring only 11 minutes of adaptation versus 27 minutes to 2 hours of per-identity training. On Sync-C (lip-sync confidence), EmoTaG (6.212 neutral / 6.147 emotional) trails Real3DPortrait (6.719 / 6.583) and MimicTalk (6.341 / 6.113) by 0.1–0.5 points, which the authors attribute to those methods' large-scale pretraining; however, EmoTaG substantially leads them on all geometric and appearance metrics. In the OOD cross-identity and cross-language settings (Table 3), EmoTaG achieves the best Sync-C (5.814 / 5.432) and lowest Sync-E (9.133 / 9.662), surpassing InsTaG by ~1.1 Sync-C points and ER-NeRF by ~3.0 points. The user study (Table 4) confirms EmoTaG's advantage, scoring 4.50 on emotional expressiveness vs. 3.80 for InsTaG and 3.50 for MimicTalk.

## Relation to Prior Work

EmoTaG extends the Pretrain-and-Adapt paradigm established by InsTaG [26] and FIAG [32] from neutral speech to emotion-aware synthesis, while grounding the 3D representation in the rigged FLAME-Gaussian formulation from GaussianAvatars [35]. Rather than directly deforming 3D Gaussians (as in InsTaG), it predicts FLAME expression and jaw parameters, introducing an explicit geometric prior that constrains motion to a structurally valid space. The Gated Residual Motion Network's three-branch decomposition (base phonetic, residual emotional, adaptive gate) departs from prior single-path motion predictors, and the Semantic Emotion Guidance replaces the manual emotion annotations required by EMOTE [9] and EmoVOCA [33] with knowledge distillation from a pretrained DeepFace recognizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a practical gap: few-shot 3D talking head avatars that are both rapidly deployable (5-second adaptation, 11-minute training) and emotionally expressive, which prior methods could not achieve simultaneously. The FLAME-parameter-space formulation offers a reusable recipe for stabilizing 3DGS-based facial animation under exaggerated expressions, potentially transferable to other rigged-Gaussian applications. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the five MEAD emotion categories, how it performs under large head rotations or occlusions, and whether the 76 FPS rendering speed is sufficient for interactive VR deployment on consumer hardware. The paper does not address multi-speaker or conversational settings, which remain important for real digital-human applications.
