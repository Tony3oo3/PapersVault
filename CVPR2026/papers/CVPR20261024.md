## Problem

Existing feed-forward physics-from-vision methods produce a single deterministic point estimate of material properties, fundamentally failing to capture the inherent ambiguity that a visually identical object can possess a continuous spectrum of plausible stiffness. Additionally, all prior approaches are tightly coupled to a single simulation paradigm (typically MPM), making predicted parameters non-portable across fundamentally different physics solvers such as reduced-order LBS or anchor-based Spring-Mass systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261024/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view renderings of a 3D object (from a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation), extracts dense CLIP feature maps, and voxelizes them into a sparse 64³ grid with 768-dimensional features. A scalar control parameter α ∈ [0,1] (softest to stiffest) conditions the generation. Outputs are solver-specific: for MPM, per-voxel Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, density, and material class over K foreground voxels; for LBS, volumetric E/ν fields plus object-specific skinning weights from a HyperNetwork; for Spring-Mass, a stiffness vector over N_a anchors and a global softness scalar.

## Method

UNIPIXIE uses a Perceiver-IO-style Grid Encoder that first downsamples the 64³ feature grid to 16³ via 3D convolutions, then compresses it into L latent tokens through cascaded cross-attention and self-attention blocks, yielding a solver-agnostic physics-aware representation. Three specialized Flow Matching Transformer decoders (shared architecture, separate parameters) then generate solver-specific parameters from these tokens, with the control parameter α injected via adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN) to modulate each transformer block. Training uses a Conditional Flow Matching objective where the target is a linear interpolation between annotated softest (y_min) and stiffest (y_max) property fields, so the model learns a vector field mapping noise to the target conditioned on α. The PIXIEVERSE dataset is re-annotated with continuous property ranges via an Actor-Critic VLM pipeline (GPT-4o proposes ranges, Gemini-2.5-Flash verifies geometric coherence) followed by human boundary-value simulation checks, and cross-solver ground truths are derived by fitting slow test-time methods to boundary simulations at α=0 and α=1.

## Results

On the PIXIEMULTIVERSE benchmark, UNIPIXIE achieves a log Young's Modulus MSE of 0.0091, reducing prediction error by over 50% compared to the strongest deterministic baseline PIXIE (0.0205). It also leads on log density MSE (0.0194 vs. 0.0244) and Poisson's ratio MSE (0.0240 vs. 0.0557), while trailing PIXIE slightly on discrete material classification accuracy (93.9% vs. 97.3%). In the multi-solver evaluation (Table 2), the unified model outperforms specialized baselines on PSNR for LBS (33.83 vs. 28.30 at soft) and Spring-Mass (33.88 vs. 30.53 at soft), while running ~24× faster than Vid2Sim and ~200× faster than Spring-Gaus.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the VLM-based baselines, UNIPIXIE dominates: NeRF2Physics [25] and PUGS [18] produce log E MSEs of 0.5236 and 1.0591 respectively, roughly 57× and 116× worse than UNIPIXIE's 0.0091, and both suffer from simulation collapse or unnatural rigidity in qualitative comparisons. Against the closest deterministic feed-forward baseline PIXIE [9], UNIPIXIE more than halves the Young's Modulus MSE (0.0091 vs. 0.0205) and improves Poisson's ratio MSE by 57% (0.0240 vs. 0.0557), at the cost of a 3.4-point drop in material classification accuracy (93.9% vs. 97.3%). In the LBS solver comparison, UNIPIXIE outperforms Vid2Sim (full) [2] on PSNR at the soft (33.83 vs. 28.30) and stiff (41.63 vs. 40.13) extremes while roughly matching it at the midpoint (36.81 vs. 36.94), and is 24× faster (21.6 s vs. 521 s). For Spring-Mass, UNIPIXIE surpasses both Spring-Gaus [28] and its tuned variant on PSNR at soft (33.88 vs. 30.53) and mid (38.79 vs. 37.60) stiffness levels, while being approximately 200× faster (21.6 s vs. 4375 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

UniPixie directly extends PIXIE [9], which established the feed-forward paradigm for physics-from-vision using a U-Net to predict a single deterministic material property set. The key conceptual departure is reframing the output from a point estimate to a controllable continuous distribution (soft-to-stiff spectrum) via conditional Flow Matching, and replacing the U-Net with a Perceiver-IO-style encoder plus Transformer decoders. The multi-solver decoding heads adapt parameterization strategies from Vid2Sim (LBS via HyperNetwork) and Spring-Gaus (anchor-based stiffness), but unify them under a single shared latent representation — a portability guarantee no prior physics-prediction method provides.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified multi-solver architecture is practically significant: a single 21-second inference replaces hours of per-solver test-time optimization (521s for Vid2Sim, 4375s for Spring-Gaus) while matching or exceeding specialized-model quality, making interactive physics generation feasible in real-time pipelines. The PIXIEMULTIVERSE dataset with property-range annotations opens a new research direction in generative physics modeling. Open questions include whether the single-axis soft-to-stiff continuum adequately captures the full material manifold (e.g., anisotropy, viscoelasticity), how the approach handles occluded or unseen regions, and whether the VLM-assisted annotation pipeline scales beyond 10 semantic classes.
