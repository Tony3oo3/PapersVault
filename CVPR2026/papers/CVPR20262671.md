## Problem

Existing discrete video VAEs quantize at a single spatial-temporal scale with small codebooks (4K–8K tokens) and inject language supervision only at one site, resulting in poor cross-modal alignment, semantic drift across time, and weak zero-shot transfer to downstream tasks like segmentation and action localization. No prior tokenizer simultaneously exploits the hierarchical encoder structure, scales to a large vocabulary, and enforces both local and global text-video consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262671/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a masked video clip (C×T×H×W, scaling to 4K/8K) and a natural-language text prompt as input. It produces a multi-scale sequence of discrete tokens drawn from a shared ~48K binary codebook, organized hierarchically with separator tokens between pyramid levels and a start-of-image token after the text prefix. These tokens serve dual purposes: they are decoded back into a reconstructed video, and they can be directly consumed by a VLM decoder for zero-shot segmentation, temporal action localization, video classification, or fed into diffusion/autoregressive backbones for text-to-video generation.

## Method

PyraTok builds on a frozen pretrained video VAE (Wan 2.2) with LoRA adapters and introduces Language-aligned Pyramidal Quantization (LaPQ), which inserts a Quantization Block at each of L encoder stages, receiving the current feature map, the previous stage's quantized output, and the text embedding via multi-head cross-attention. Each block discretizes features using Lookup-Free Quantization with a shared binary codebook of ~48K entries, enabling up to 95% utilization without high-dimensional embedding lookups. A dual semantic alignment strategy couples local text-conditioned quantization at every pyramid level with a global autoregressive objective that predicts the full token hierarchy conditioned on the text, enforced through a hierarchical semantic codebook loss (vision commitment, entropy, stage-wise KL consistency, text-conditioned and text-codebook alignment terms) plus a drift-regularization KL term anchoring LoRA-adapted features to the frozen encoder.

## Results

On frame reconstruction, PyraTok achieves 35.72 PSNR / 0.879 SSIM / 0.066 LPIPS on WebVid-10M and 36.05 / 0.885 / 0.071 on COCO-Val, outperforming the best prior VAE (LARP) by +2.69 PSNR and reducing LPIPS by 27.5%. On zero-shot video instance segmentation, PyraTok reaches 24.54 mAP on YouTube-VIS 2021 and 8.9 mAP on OVIS, surpassing the previous zero-shot SOTA (OmniTokenizer) by +10.0 and +6.1 mAP respectively. For temporal action localization, PyraTok attains 33.17 mAP on THUMOS14 and 29.11 on ActivityNet, exceeding LARP by +5.75 and +3.58 mAP. On video understanding, PyraTok scores 86.03% overall on MVBench and 78.43% on Kinetics-400, beating LARP by +2.82 and +9.16 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PyraTok outperforms all 26 baselines across every evaluated benchmark. On frame reconstruction, it beats the strongest non-semantic VAE (LARP, 33.03 PSNR) by +2.69 PSNR and the best semantic VAE (SweetTok, 32.32 PSNR) by +3.40 PSNR on WebVid-10M, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.066 vs. 0.091 for LARP). On zero-shot video segmentation, PyraTok surpasses OmniTokenizer (the prior zero-shot SOTA) by +10.0 mAP on YouTube-VIS 2021 and +6.1 mAP on OVIS, and outperforms unsupervised methods (VideoCutLER, UVIS) by +7.4 and +7.0 mAP respectively. For temporal action localization, PyraTok exceeds LARP by +5.75 mAP on THUMOS14 and +3.58 on ActivityNet, and even surpasses supervised methods like STOV-TAL (48.8 mAP) on THUMOS14 in the zero-shot setting. On video understanding, PyraTok achieves 86.03% on MVBench, beating LARP (83.21%) by +2.82 points and non-VAE foundation models like InternVL3-78B (79.2%) by +6.83 points. On Kinetics-400, PyraTok reaches 78.43%, exceeding LARP (69.27%) by +9.16 points and PEcoreG (76.9%) by +1.53 points. In T2V generation, substituting PyraTok into MotionAura, MAGVITv2, and OmniGenV2 reduces FVD by 9–22 points and increases temporal consistency by 20–27 points over their native VAEs.

## Relation to Prior Work

PyraTok builds directly on Lookup-Free Quantization (LFQ) from MAGVITv2, which replaces learned codebooks with compact binary codewords to enable large vocabularies at low memory cost. It departs from prior text-guided VAEs (TokLIP, LG-VQ, SweetTok, VideoVAE+) that inject language supervision at a single encoder depth with small codebooks (4K–8K), by introducing multi-scale pyramidal quantization (LaPQ) with lateral connections at every encoder stage and a shared ~48K binary codebook. The dual alignment strategy—local text-conditioned quantization at each pyramid level plus a global autoregressive objective over the concatenated token hierarchy—extends the single-site contrastive or distillation losses used in prior work into a hierarchical, sequence-level formulation. LoRA adapters on a frozen pretrained video VAE (Wan 2.2) provide the efficient adaptation mechanism, keeping the reconstruction backbone intact while learning semantic structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PyraTok is the first discrete video VAE to demonstrate zero-shot text-guided video instance segmentation, a capability previously limited to dedicated segmentation models or large VLMs, suggesting that sufficiently language-aligned discrete latents can serve as a unified representation for both generation and understanding without task-specific heads. The consistent SOTA gains across 10 benchmarks (reconstruction, T2V, segmentation, action localization, video QA, classification) indicate that the pyramidal + dual-alignment design is a broadly applicable recipe rather than a task-specific trick. Open questions include whether the zero-shot segmentation advantage persists under more diverse or adversarial prompts, how the method scales to long-horizon multi-actor video reasoning, and whether the autoregressive alignment objective introduces sycophancy or alignment failures at scale—concerns the authors explicitly flag for future work.
