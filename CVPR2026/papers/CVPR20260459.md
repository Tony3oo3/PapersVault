## Problem

Vision-Language Models uniformly sample every pixel of an image, allocating equal resolution to semantically irrelevant background and critical foreground details alike. Under tight pixel budgets (1–5% of full resolution), this uniform downsampling discards task-relevant information and severely degrades VQA accuracy. The paper asks whether bio-inspired foveated sampling—mimicking cortical magnification in human vision—can reallocate the pixel budget adaptively to improve reasoning without any architectural changes or training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a full-resolution RGB image and a set of text questions, and produces an adaptively sampled image at a user-specified pixel budget B (e.g., 1%, 3%, 5% of original pixels) that is reconstructed to the original spatial dimensions via bilinear interpolation before being fed to a frozen VLM. The VLM then generates a text answer. A key constraint is that the method is entirely training-free and black-box compatible: it requires no access to internal model parameters, attention maps, or gradients, making it applicable to proprietary VLM APIs as well as open-source models.

## Method

LLMind's core module, BASS, applies a Möbius transformation (parameterized by four real scalars predicted by a lightweight MLP) to warp the image so that a foveal region is magnified while the periphery is smoothly compressed; uniform sampling is then performed at the target pixel budget on the warped image, and an inverse Möbius warp restores global spatial structure. At inference time, the Möbius parameters are optimized via a composite perceptual loss (VSI saliency + DISTS structural similarity + MSE) combined with a semantic loss computed as the cosine distance between the VLM's predicted answer and the ground-truth answer (embedded via MiniLM). Because the VLM is frozen and potentially black-box, gradients of the semantic loss are estimated using Simultaneous Perturbation Stochastic Approximation (SPSA), requiring only two forward passes per update. An adaptive question-selection mechanism exponentially upweights incorrectly answered questions to focus SPSA updates on the most challenging examples, and the final gradient direction balances perceptual and semantic terms by their relative norms.

## Results

Under tight pixel budgets (1–5%), LLMind achieves average accuracy gains of +20% on VQAv2, +38% on Seed-Bench, and +37% on A-OKVQA over uniform sampling. At peak efficiency, LLMind retains up to 82%, 92%, and 97% of full-resolution performance using only 1%, 3%, and 5% of pixels, respectively. In region-guided VQA on LVIS, LLMind surpasses full-resolution accuracy with just 3% pixels (e.g., 62.90% vs. 50.60% for Qwen3-VL), demonstrating that adaptive sampling can reduce background distraction and improve reasoning fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLMind consistently outperforms all four baselines across every dataset, model, and pixel budget tested. Against Static Foveated (Gizdov et al. [19,20]), LLMind gains +15.24 pp on VQAv2 (73.54% vs. 58.30% at 5%, Qwen2.5-VL) and +27.72 pp on A-OKVQA with SmolVLM at 1% (64.81% vs. 42.09%). Against Sunflower Inspired (Killick et al. [28]), the margin is even larger: +20.69 pp on VQAv2 at 5% and +12.32 pp on Seed-Bench at 5%. Against Radial Sampling (Robert-Inacio et al. [40]), LLMind leads by +23.42 pp on VQAv2 at 5% and +24.09 pp on A-OKVQA with SmolVLM at 1%. Notably, the static baselines (Sunflower, Radial) often perform *worse* than uniform sampling at higher budgets (e.g., Radial drops to 50.12% vs. 59.94% uniform on VQAv2 at 5%), whereas LLMind's dynamic, semantically-guided sampling maintains consistent gains across all budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously separate threads—bio-inspired foveated sampling (Gizdov et al., Killick et al., Robert-Inacio et al.), Möbius/conformal geometry for spatial warping, and gradient-free optimization via SPSA—into a unified training-free framework for VLMs. It departs from prior foveated approaches, which are static, single-fixation, and task-specific (often requiring new architectures or training), by making the sampling dynamic and inference-time adaptive. It also distinguishes itself from training-free VLM adaptation methods like ControlMLLM, which optimize over internal attention maps in a white-box setting, by optimizing the input sampling transformation itself in a fully black-box-compatible manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that adaptive, bio-inspired sampling can exceed full-resolution accuracy in region-guided VQA (using only 3% of pixels) challenges the implicit assumption that more visual information always improves reasoning, suggesting that suppressing background clutter reduces hallucination in small VLMs. The practical impact is significant for edge deployment and API-based VLMs where pixel budgets are constrained, since the method is plug-and-play and requires no architectural changes. Open questions include whether the test-time optimization latency (~4.75 s/iter with SPSA) is acceptable for real-time applications, whether gains hold for larger (>4B) models, and how the approach extends to video, 3D, or generative tasks as the authors themselves note.
