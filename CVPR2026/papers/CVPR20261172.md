## Problem

Standard rigid-body simulators fail to capture the nonsmooth, high-dimensional, and state-dependent dynamics of physical contacts, making sim-trained policies unreliable for contact-rich manipulation. Explicit system identification (tuning a few friction or mass parameters) is insufficient for these complex interactions, and existing neural dynamics models are largely contact-agnostic, treating discontinuities as noise or relying solely on kinematic and proprioceptive signals while under-utilizing tactile contact information.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a fixed-length history window (K+1 steps) of object SE(3) poses, robot joint configurations, actions, and binary hand-level contact flags, together with a static object point cloud (N×3, sampled once from the mesh and transformed by pose). The contact signal is deliberately reduced to a single binary value per timestep (any fingertip in contact), derived from collision detection in MuJoCo or a 0.3 N force threshold on XHand tactile sensors, ensuring a consistent representation across sim and real. The output is a two-stage prediction: an H-step binary contact probability sequence, followed by H-step 6D pose increments (3D translation delta + 3D rotation via exponential map) that are applied cumulatively to the current state to reconstruct future object trajectories.

## Method

The architecture couples a Contact Predictor (MLP with sigmoid output) and a Diffusion Pose Predictor (1D U-Net with FiLM-modulated conditioning) in a two-stage pipeline. A multimodal encoder fuses temporal history (poses, actions, joints, contact sequence) and a PointNet geometry embedding into a shared 512-d latent z_t; Stage I predicts H-step contact probabilities and projects them into a 64-d contact feature f_c, which is concatenated with z_t to form the conditioning vector h_t for Stage II. The diffusion model learns to denoise pose increments conditioned on h_t, with training loss combining binary cross-entropy for contact and MSE for noise prediction. For sim-to-real alignment, the model is first pretrained on large-scale MuJoCo rollouts (8K single-object, 15K multi-object trajectories with domain randomization), then fine-tuned with a small amount of real-world trajectories augmented with tactile readings, implicitly aligning simulated and physical contact dynamics in a shared representation without explicit parameter tuning.

## Results

On the single-object task with real-data fine-tuning, the proposed Diffusion-UNet w/Contact achieves 0.0082 MSE and 88.23% ADD-S, substantially outperforming the particle-grid baseline [52] (0.0194 MSE, 68.72% ADD-S on real data). On the multi-object task with real-data fine-tuning, it reaches 0.0058 MSE and 79.12% ADD-S, again the best across all methods. In the downstream task success rate evaluation, the Sim+Real co-trained model achieves 73.7% (single-object) and 64.7% (multi-object) success, compared to 52.6% and 47.1% for the real-only model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external baseline is the particle-grid neural dynamics model of Zhang et al. (RSS 2025) [52]. The proposed Diffusion-UNet w/Contact outperforms this baseline on MSE in every comparable setting (e.g., 0.0094 vs. 0.0194 on single-object real data; 0.0075 vs. 0.0161 on multi-object real data) and on ADD-S in all settings except single-object simulation data, where the baseline edges ahead (71.01% vs. 68.12%). The baseline does not report real-finetune results, so the largest gap appears in the co-training regime, where the proposed method reaches 88.23% ADD-S on single-object tasks. Internally, adding the contact conditioning to the Diffusion-UNet backbone yields consistent gains over the contact-free Diffusion-UNet (e.g., 88.23% vs. 82.45% ADD-S on single-object finetune; 79.12% vs. 75.82% on multi-object finetune), confirming that the binary contact signal is the key differentiator. Simpler architectures (MLP, UNet) are uniformly outperformed, with the MLP showing the weakest sim-to-real transfer (77.43% vs. 88.23% ADD-S on single-object finetune).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of neural dynamics models (neural simulators like [44, 51]) and sim-to-real alignment, but departs from both lines in a key way: rather than treating contact discontinuities as noise to be absorbed by a residual network or compensated by domain randomization, it explicitly conditions a diffusion-based pose predictor on binary tactile contact signals. Prior neural dynamics approaches are largely contact-agnostic, and explicit system identification assumes a low-dimensional parametric correction suffices; this work instead learns a high-dimensional, state-dependent contact-aware dynamics model via co-training on simulation and real data. The diffusion architecture with FiLM conditioning on a contact feature vector is a repurposing of generative modeling tools for the specific problem of modeling non-smooth contact transitions in SE(3) pose prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, scalable recipe for closing the sim-to-real gap in contact-rich manipulation without hand-tuning friction, mass, or geometry parameters—something that is increasingly important as dexterous manipulation policies (e.g., Dex1B) are trained at scale in simulation. Key limitations remain: the binary contact signal discards force magnitude, slip direction, and contact area; reliance on FoundationPose for ground-truth states introduces drift under occlusion; and long-horizon rollouts still accumulate error. Open questions include whether richer tactile representations (e.g., per-finger force vectors or contact patches) yield further gains, whether the approach generalizes beyond YCB-scale objects to deformable or multi-contact scenarios, and how the method scales to longer prediction horizons needed for planning.
