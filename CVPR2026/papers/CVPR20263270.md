## Problem

Diffusion-based dataset distillation suffers from two under-addressed distribution shifts: (1) the diffusion model learns the full complexity of the real data distribution rather than the simplified, task-aware manifold that small synthetic sets can actually encode, and (2) sequential i.i.d. sampling of a small number of samples (N ≪ N_real) causes diversity collapse and empirical distribution drift that no single-sample guidance can correct. Prior methods treat the diffusion model as an off-the-shelf generator or apply greedy per-sample optimization, overlooking both shifts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real RGB image dataset with class labels (256×256 resolution) and produces a synthetic dataset of N images per class (IPC = 10, 50, or 100) with the same labels. The real images are first encoded into VAE latents, and the final synthetic images are decoded from the denoised latents. The pre-trained DiT backbone is fine-tuned on the real latents, and all experiments run on a single RTX 4090 GPU with 50 denoising steps.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage framework targeting both distribution shifts. Stage 1 (Restricted Score Matching) adds an L1 sparsity penalty on the predicted clean latent x̂₀ to the standard diffusion fine-tuning loss, forcing the model to learn a compact, semantically sparse manifold that preserves only transferable features. Stage 2 (Collaborative Guided Sampling) replaces sequential i.i.d. generation with synchronous denoising of all N samples as a coupled system, applying two per-step guidance gradients: a Determinantal Point Process loss (negative log-determinant of a cosine-similarity kernel) that enforces mutual dissimilarity to prevent diversity collapse, and a distribution-matching loss (MSE between the synthetic ensemble mean and the analytically derived real-data class mean at each noise level) that anchors the set's center of mass to correct distribution drift. The guidance is applied as a simple gradient subtraction on the DDPM/DDIM prediction at every denoising step, adding negligible computational overhead.

## Results

On ImageNette with ConvNet-6 at IPC=10, RSM+CGS achieves 65.5% top-1 accuracy, surpassing Minimax (58.2%) by 7.3% and vanilla DiT (56.2%) by 9.3%. At IPC=50, RSM+CGS reaches 82.7%, edging out Minimax-IGD (82.3%). On the full ImageNet-1K with ResNet-18, the method achieves 46.7% at IPC=10 and 61.0% at IPC=50, outperforming IGD (46.2% / 60.3%) and D³HR (44.3% / 59.4%) while using only 25% of IGD's computation time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Minimax [9], the RSM regularizer alone delivers 2–4% gains across all architectures and IPCs (e.g., 62.4% vs 58.2% on ImageNette/ConvNet-6/IPC=10), and RSM+CGS adds a further 1–2% to reach SOTA. Compared to vanilla DiT fine-tuning [29], RSM yields over 5% improvement, confirming that the L1 sparsity constraint on the predicted clean latent is more effective than Minimax's bilevel objective. Against IGD [4], RSM+CGS achieves comparable or slightly higher accuracy (e.g., 82.7% vs 82.3% on ImageNette/ConvNet-6/IPC=50; 46.7% vs 46.2% on ImageNet-1K/IPC=10) while requiring only 25% of the computation time (2.7 s/sample vs 8.2 s/sample). On ImageNet-1K, the method outperforms D³HR [51] by 1.6–2.4% and RDED [36] by 4.7–4.5% at IPC=10/50, respectively. MGD³ [3] is outperformed by RSM+CGS in most settings (e.g., 65.5% vs 59.6% on ImageNette/ConvNet-6/IPC=10). SRe2L [44] and G-VBSM are significantly outperformed on ImageNet-1K, with gaps of 25.4% and 15.3% at IPC=10, though these are non-diffusion baselines from a different method family.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the diffusion-based dataset distillation line initiated by Minimax [9] and the DiT architecture [29], adopting their fine-tuning pipeline (Difffit) as the base. It departs from prior diffusion-DD methods in two conceptual ways: at training time, it replaces complex bilevel objectives (Minimax) or influence-based guidance (IGD [4]) with a simple L1 sparsity penalty on the predicted clean latent, arguing the generative prior should learn a *simplified* manifold rather than replicating the full data distribution; at sampling time, it abandons the sequential greedy paradigm used by IGD, MGD³, and CaO² in favor of synchronous denoising of all N samples with collaborative DPP and distribution-matching guidance. The DPP component draws from the submodular set-selection literature, while the distribution-matching loss follows the MMD-style moment alignment tradition in DD [48].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's main contribution is a clean conceptual decomposition of 'distribution shift' in generative DD into a training-time complexity mismatch and a sampling-time capacity-induced drift, each addressed by a targeted mechanism. The finding that a trivial L1 regularizer outperforms the more elaborate Minimax bilevel objective suggests that prior methods may have been over-parameterizing the fine-tuning objective. Limitations include evaluation restricted to ImageNet-family datasets and a small set of architectures (ConvNet-6, ResNetAP-10, ResNet-18), and the ImageNet-1K gains (~1%) are modest, leaving open whether the approach scales to truly large-scale or multi-modal settings. An open question is whether the L1 sparsity in pixel/latent space is the right complexity proxy, or whether feature-space or information-theoretic regularizers would be more principled.
