## Problem

Existing faithfulness metrics for vision-language generation (e.g., TIFA, VALOR-Eval, CLIPScore) are tailored to photorealistic natural images and produce single composite scores without explanation, making them ill-suited for scientific images that demand precise numerical details, correct object attributes, and accurate spatial relationships. General-purpose LMMs used as evaluators show poor correlation with human judgments on scientific content (Kendall < 0.3), while expert human annotation is prohibitively expensive (~$3,000 for 3,000 images). No unified, automatic, reference-free, and explainable metric exists for evaluating both scientific text-to-image generation and image captioning.

## Input / Output

The system takes a scientific RGB image paired with either a conditioning text prompt (for Sci-T2I evaluation) or a generated caption (for Sci-IC evaluation) and produces three outputs: a relevance score and an accuracy score, both bounded in [0,1], plus a free-form textual rationale that explicitly identifies unfaithful elements (e.g., wrong object counts, incorrect attributes, or spatial mismatches). The text input is unified across both tasks—prompts and captions are processed identically by the text encoder—while the rationale is unconstrained natural language rather than a structured label.

## Method

SCIEval decomposes faithfulness into three modules: SCIEval-R and SCIEval-A (CLIP-based dual encoders producing relevance and accuracy scores via cosine similarity) and SCIEval-E (a fine-tuned mPLUG-owl3 8B LMM generating rationales). Training uses a two-phase contrastive strategy: first, relevance encoders are trained with hard negatives constructed via adversarial filtering (nearest-neighbor caption retrieval through Faiss), then accuracy encoders are initialized from the relevance weights and further trained with negatives created by targeted text edits (altering object counts, attributes, spatial arrangements) paired with SEED-X image editing. The contrastive objective combines an InfoNCE-style cross-modal loss (pulling aligned pairs together, pushing unaligned pairs apart) with intra-modal margin losses that separate positive and negative representations within each modality. The Explainability module is fine-tuned on supervised rationale signals extracted as structured diffs between original and modified captions, enabling it to pinpoint specific unfaithful elements rather than producing generic commentary.

## Results

On SCIEval-Bench, SCIEval achieves the highest Spearman/Pearson correlation with human judgments across all four task-domain splits. For Sci-T2I (CS), it reaches 74.1/73.2 (Relevance) and 69.4/68.2 (Accuracy); for Sci-IC (CS), it reaches 75.9/75.6 (Relevance) and 69.9/69.1 (Accuracy). The closest competitor, GPT-4o, achieves 71.3/70.5 (Sci-T2I CS Relevance) and 72.8/72.4 (Sci-IC CS Relevance), trailing SCIEval by 2.8–3.1 points. In rationale quality assessment (5-point scale), SCIEval scores 4.7 (correctness) and 4.2 (completeness) under human judgment, outperforming GPT-4V (4.5/3.5) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (4.4/3.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCIEval outperforms all 24 competing methods across every task-domain split. Against the strongest closed-source LMM (GPT-4o), SCIEval leads by 2.8–3.1 Spearman points on Relevance and 2.2–2.7 points on Accuracy (e.g., 74.1 vs. 71.3 on Sci-T2I CS Relevance). The best open-source LMM (InstructBLIP-Vicuna-13b) trails SCIEval by over 30 points, and even the worst closed-source model (Claude 3 Haiku, ~50) surpasses the best open-source model (~41), highlighting a large closed-vs-open gap. Among task-specific metrics, TIFA (45.2 on Sci-T2I CS Relevance) and VALOR-EVAL (47.9 on Sci-IC CS Relevance) outperform generic LMMs but remain 20+ points below SCIEval. In rationale quality (Table 2), SCIEval's human-judged completeness score of 4.2 exceeds GPT-4V (3.5) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (3.3), while its correctness (4.7) marginally leads GPT-4V (4.5). The ablation confirms that the cross-modal contrastive loss (L_CM) contributes the largest single gain (~20 points), and even 25% of training data (8K pairs) yields scores competitive with the weakest closed-source LMMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the faithfulness evaluation paradigm (established by TIFA, VALOR-EVAL, and CLIPScore for natural images) into the scientific domain, where precise numerical details and object attributes matter far more than photorealistic quality. Architecturally, it builds on CLIP's dual-encoder design (Radford et al., 2021) for its scoring modules and on mPLUG-owl3 (Ye et al., 2025) for its rationale generation, but departs from prior work by decomposing faithfulness into three distinct dimensions (relevance, accuracy, explainability) rather than producing a single composite score. The key conceptual departure from LMM-as-judge approaches (GPT-4o, Gemini) is the use of a lightweight, task-specific CLIP-based evaluator trained via intra- and cross-modal contrastive learning on scientific data, rather than relying on expensive general-purpose multimodal models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCIEval-Bench (6,000 human-annotated samples) fills a genuine gap: prior scientific image benchmarks like ScImage required ~$3,000 and an 11-scientist panel for evaluation, while SCIEval-Bench was annotated for ~$1,200 by three annotators. The finding that a small CLIP-based model trained for 2.5 hours on 4 GPUs outperforms GPT-4o on scientific faithfulness scoring suggests that task-specific lightweight evaluators can be more reliable than frontier LMMs for narrow domains. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-panel figures (explicitly deferred by the authors), whether the three-dimension decomposition holds across all scientific disciplines, and whether the scores can effectively serve as RL rewards for generative models as the authors envision.
