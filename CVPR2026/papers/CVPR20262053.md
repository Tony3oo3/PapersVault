## Problem

Existing dynamic scene reconstruction methods only produce geometry at the time it was observed, discarding previously seen information about moving objects and thus lacking persistence. This means a complete, replayable 4D reconstruction of a scene across all timestamps is not achievable with current monocular approaches, even though retaining all observed geometry is the fundamental goal of mapping.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262053/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a casual monocular RGB video (e.g., iPhone footage) and internally consumes the per-keyframe point maps produced by a feed-forward reconstruction model (π³) along with dense 2D correspondences from AllTracker and segmentations propagated via SAMv2. The output is a collection of 3D point maps warped to every observed timestamp (i.e., a full 4D reconstruction), plus a motion segmentation as a byproduct. Long sequences are processed in chunks of 150 consecutive frames, and evaluation warps all observations to the final timestamp for comparison against multi-view ground truth.

## Method

4DPM decomposes each keyframe into non-overlapping 3D primitives (following SuperPrimitive), matches them across time into object groups, and assigns each primitive a single SE(3) pose relative to its object's last-observed segment. Dense 2D correspondences between adjacent keyframes (from AllTracker) provide the constraints, and a joint Gauss-Newton optimisation with Huber loss estimates all object poses simultaneously, reducing the all-to-all temporal mapping problem to one 6-DoF parameter per primitive. Time remapping then warps any primitive to any timestamp via relative pose composition, yielding complete reconstructions at all frames. A motion-segmentation module uses oriented bounding box contact and gauge-invariant velocity clustering to link occluded objects to visible parent objects, enabling object permanence for temporarily invisible scene elements.

## Results

On the HO3D object-scanning benchmark, 4DPM achieves an average F-score of 0.757 (precision 0.763, recall 0.777 at 1 cm threshold), outperforming the strongest baseline TraceAnything (0.637) by 12 F-score points. On the authors' Multi-Object dataset, 4DPM reaches a mean F-score of 0.795 (precision 0.720, recall 0.900), leading the next-best method POMATO (0.586) by over 20 points. The method consistently achieves the best precision–recall balance across both datasets, with the largest gains in recall over the π³ last-view baseline (0.202 → 0.777 on HO3D), confirming that temporal glueing recovers geometry that single-frame methods discard.

## Baselines & Comparisons

4DPM outperforms all three external baselines on both datasets. Against St4Track (CVPR 2025), 4DPM gains +21.8 F-score points on HO3D (0.757 vs 0.539) and +33.2 on Multi-Object (0.795 vs 0.463), with the gap driven primarily by much higher precision (0.763 vs 0.455 on HO3D). Against POMATO (ICCV 2025), 4DPM leads by +23.6 F-score points on HO3D and +20.8 on Multi-Object; POMATO achieves the highest recall among baselines (0.765 on HO3D) but suffers from low precision (0.407), indicating misaligned geometry. Against TraceAnything (arXiv 2025), the closest competitor, 4DPM still wins by +12.0 F-score points on HO3D and +31.3 on Multi-Object, with TraceAnything showing the most variance across sequences (e.g., 0.319 on MultiObj1 vs 0.738 on ShSu1). The two internal baselines (π³ last-view and π³) confirm that the gains come from temporal glueing rather than the underlying feed-forward model: π³ last-view has the highest precision (0.926) but the lowest recall (0.202), while unwarped π³ improves recall but loses precision due to mispositioned geometry.

## Relation to Prior Work

4DPM extends the SuperPrimitive framework by endowing each 2.5D primitive with a full SE(3) rigid motion parameter, reducing the dynamic reconstruction problem to per-primitive pose estimation rather than per-pixel motion fields. It leverages off-the-shelf components (π³ for feed-forward geometry, AllTracker for dense 2D correspondences, SAMv2 for segmentation propagation) but departs conceptually from DUSt3R-based temporal warping methods (St4Track, POMATO) by performing explicit 3D optimisation over a compact primitive representation rather than learned pairwise point-map alignment. The key positioning is that prior methods reconstruct geometry only at its observation timestamp, whereas 4DPM 'glues' primitives across time to produce complete scene reconstructions at every timestamp.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The object permanence capability—inferring the continued motion of occluded objects through transitive motion grouping—is, to the authors' knowledge, the first demonstration of spatial memory in a monocular reconstruction system, opening possibilities for persistent scene understanding in robotics and AR. The rigid-body assumption, while limiting, covers a surprisingly wide range of practical scenes and dramatically reduces optimisation dimensionality. Open questions include extending to non-rigid deformations, supporting incremental mapping over long sequences, and validating on larger-scale or more diverse dynamic scenes beyond the small evaluation sets used here.
