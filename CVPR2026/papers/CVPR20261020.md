## Problem

The paper addresses a gap in pan sharpening where the standard diffusion-model approach fails to produce a clean neural-operator output when the spectral residual is informed. The unexplored question is how the spatial-spectral residuals interact under the new architecture, as existing methods only handle the standard RGB images without considering the informed residuals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261020/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The methods under comparison take standard RGB images as input and produce class labels as output. The unusual constraint is that the input is consumed as a multispectral-image under the new architecture, but the output is structured as a HRMS image rather than the expected panchromatic-image, which requires the resolution constraint to be revisited.

## Method

The core technical contribution is the new architecture of a spatial-spectral residuals informed diffusion neural operator, which is trained under identical protocols with matched network widths and depths. This approach differs from previous work by introducing a supervised-learning transfer-learning paradigm that replaces the standard diffusion-model approach, and the key insight is that the empirical study of this new architecture reveals the mechanism by which the informed residuals operate within the system.

## Results

On the WorldView-3 reduced-resolution benchmark, SRINO achieves the best performance across all four reference-based metrics: PSNR 39.31 dB, SAM 2.87°, ERGAS 2.11, and Q2n 0.922, outperforming the strongest transformer-based competitor ADWM (39.17 dB, 2.91°, 2.15, 0.919) and the diffusion-based PanDiff (37.86 dB, 3.32°, 2.49, 0.906). On full-resolution WV3, SRINO attains the lowest spectral distortion (D_λ = 0.019) and highest HQNR (0.950). On GaoFen-2, SRINO reaches 44.23 dB PSNR and 0.646° SAM, marginally surpassing LFormer (44.20 dB, 0.648°), while on QuickBird it achieves 38.86 dB PSNR and 3.54 ERGAS, leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRINO outperforms all 11 baselines on every reference-based metric (PSNR, SAM, ERGAS, Q2n) across all three datasets (WV3, GF2, QB). The closest competitors are the transformer-based LFormer and ADWM: on WV3, SRINO leads ADWM by 0.14 dB in PSNR and 0.04° in SAM, and leads LFormer by 0.23 dB in PSNR. Against the diffusion-based PanDiff, SRINO gains 1.45 dB in PSNR and 0.45° in SAM on WV3, demonstrating that the neural-operator backbone plus residual guidance substantially improves over standard attention-based diffusion. On full-resolution WV3, SRINO achieves the best D_λ (0.019 vs. 0.024 for ADWM) and HQNR (0.950 vs. 0.948), though ADWM has a slightly lower D_s (0.029 vs. 0.032). On GF2, SRINO edges out LFormer by 0.03 dB in PSNR and 0.002° in SAM, while on QB it leads LFormer by 0.19 dB in PSNR and 0.07 in ERGAS. Classical methods (BDSD-PC, BT-H, LRTCFPan) trail all deep learning approaches by 5–6 dB in PSNR, confirming the dominance of learned fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent trends: neural operators for image super-resolution and diffusion models for pan-sharpening. It departs from prior NO+diffusion work (DiffFNO, Neurop-Diff) by placing the entire diffusion process within the operator learning space rather than using the NO as a peripheral up-sampling or refinement module. The denoising backbone replaces standard self-attention with Galerkin-type linear attention (Cao 2021), reducing complexity from O(N²) to O(Nd²). The triple guidance adaptation strategy also diverges from gradient-guided diffusion (DIFISR, AGLLDiff) by injecting pixel-wise spatial-spectral residuals directly as network inputs rather than backpropagating through unsupervised loss terms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The primary practical contribution is making diffusion-based pan-sharpening feasible on hardware-constrained satellite platforms by substantially reducing FLOPs and memory (Figure 1 shows attention-based counterparts hitting OOM at larger scales). The residual-informed guidance mechanism eliminates the need for gradient computation and weight tuning during inference, which is a meaningful simplification over prior guided-diffusion approaches. However, the performance gains over strong non-diffusion baselines (ADWM, LFormer) are modest (e.g., ~0.1–0.3 dB PSNR), and the two-stage training pipeline adds complexity. Open questions include whether the function-space formulation scales to very large scenes or hyperspectral data with many bands, and whether the efficiency advantage persists as model depth increases.
