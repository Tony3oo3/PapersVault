## Problem

Current self-supervised video learning methods are procedurally agnostic: they produce nearly identical features regardless of whether frames are presented in forward or reverse temporal order, revealing that their representations capture static content but ignore the sequential workflow structure inherent in procedural activities like surgery and cooking. Existing SSL objectives (contrastive, masked reconstruction) are centered on local instance discrimination or pixel-level reconstruction, offering no explicit signal for learning global temporal progression.

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled video sequences, from which it samples sparse clips of k=8 frames (random start, random uniform step) for the temporal ranking branch and frame triplets (past, current, future at ±1.5–2.5 s offsets) for the image branch. The shared ViT-B/16 encoder tokenizes each frame into 16×16 patches plus a [CLS] token, producing (N+1)×D context-aware embeddings. Downstream evaluation uses these frozen frame-level embeddings with linear probing or k-NN (k=20) for phase recognition and action segmentation.

## Method

PL-Stitch jointly trains a shared ViT-B/16 backbone with three objectives unified under a Plackett-Luce (PL) probabilistic ranking framework. The video branch feeds [CLS] embeddings of k sampled frames through a Transformer encoder head that outputs PL score parameters, then minimizes the negative log-likelihood of the ground-truth chronological permutation, providing a globally consistent listwise signal rather than O(k²) pairwise comparisons or brittle permutation classification. The image branch introduces a spatio-temporal jigsaw task where masked patch embeddings of the current frame serve as queries, while concatenated patch embeddings from adjacent past and future frames (without positional encodings) serve as keys and values in a cross-attention head that predicts the original spatial patch order via the same PL objective. A standard iBOT masked image modeling loss is added in parallel for robust semantic features. The total loss is a weighted sum (λ₁=1, λ₂=1, λ₃=0.4) of the three terms, and the entire procedure is fully self-supervised, requiring no manual annotations.

## Results

On the Cholec80 surgical phase recognition task, PL-Stitch achieves 81.7% k-NN accuracy and 80.4% linear probing accuracy, surpassing the strongest generalist baseline iBOT (70.3% k-NN, 74.6% linear) by +11.4 pp and +5.8 pp respectively. On AutoLaparo and M2CAI16, PL-Stitch similarly leads with 82.5% and 77.1% k-NN accuracy. On the Breakfast cooking action segmentation benchmark, PL-Stitch reaches 21.6% linear probing accuracy, a +5.7 pp gain over the second-best method (DINO at 15.9%), and 10.9% k-NN accuracy versus 7.5% for iBOT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PL-Stitch outperforms all eight baselines across all three surgical datasets under both linear probing and k-NN evaluation. The largest margin is against iBOT on Cholec80 k-NN (+11.4 pp: 81.7 vs. 70.3), confirming that the PL ranking objective adds substantial procedural structure beyond what masked modeling alone provides. Against DINO, PL-Stitch gains +7.2 pp k-NN on AutoLaparo (82.5 vs. 75.3) and +9.1 pp on M2CAI16 (77.1 vs. 68.0). The surgical-specific foundation model LemonFM [6], which uses a ConvNeXt backbone, is outperformed by PL-Stitch on all three datasets (e.g., 80.4 vs. 73.9 linear on Cholec80), showing that a generalist ViT with procedural-aware pretraining can surpass domain-specific models. On cooking, PL-Stitch beats DINO by +5.7 pp linear accuracy on Breakfast (21.6 vs. 15.9) and by +2.4 pp k-NN on GTEA (62.4 vs. 60.0), while T-CoRe and VideoMAEv2 trail significantly, indicating that temporal correspondence alone (T-CoRe) or symmetric masked video modeling (VideoMAEv2) is insufficient for procedural understanding.

## Relation to Prior Work

PL-Stitch builds on the iBOT masked image modeling objective for local feature learning and borrows the temporal context sampling strategy from T-CoRe for its spatio-temporal jigsaw task, but departs fundamentally from both by introducing the Plackett-Luce distribution as a listwise ranking loss for self-supervised learning. It positions itself against the broader family of contrastive (DINO, MoCo) and reconstruction-based (VideoMAEv2, MAE) SSL methods, arguing that their local, frame-level objectives are inherently blind to procedural temporal order. The key conceptual departure is from prior temporal ordering pretext tasks (pairwise sorting, permutation classification), which the authors show to be suboptimal formulations of the same underlying problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically validates a genuine gap in SSL—procedural agnosticism—where models produce nearly identical features for forward and reversed sequences, undermining their utility for sequential tasks like surgical phase recognition and action segmentation. The clean ablation showing PL listwise ranking outperforms both pairwise and permutation-classification alternatives provides a principled justification for the formulation choice. Open questions include whether the gains generalize beyond the surgical and cooking domains tested, whether the approach scales to longer videos or larger backbones, and how it interacts with emerging generative or multimodal video models. The authors note future directions toward action anticipation and text-aligned procedural understanding.
