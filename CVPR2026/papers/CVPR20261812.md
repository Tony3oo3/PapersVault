## Problem

Existing 3DGS language comprehension methods face a fundamental trade-off: vocabulary-bounded approaches (e.g., SceneSplat) generalize across scenes but cannot handle free-form language queries, while free-form methods (e.g., ReferSplat, LangSplat) require per-scene optimization and thus overfit to individual scenes with weak vision-language alignment. The paper asks whether a single model can achieve robust, generalizable language comprehension and spatial reasoning in 3DGS without test-time per-scene optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261812/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N multi-view RGB images (from which a 3D Gaussian field is reconstructed) and a free-form text query as input. The Geometry-Aware Frame Selector adaptively selects N*=32 informative views from the full set to feed the MLLM, avoiding the cost of processing all views. For referring segmentation, the output is a per-Gaussian mask that is differentially rendered into multi-view-consistent 2D masks; for VQA, the MLLM generates free-form textual answer tokens. No test-time per-scene optimization is required beyond the initial 3DGS reconstruction.

## Method

GenSplat introduces a Progressive Language Grounding Curriculum that trains in three stages: (1) semantic-level alignment where a SparseConvUNet Gaussian Encoder maps each Gaussian primitive to a feature vector supervised by 2D SAM+CLIP features via L1 loss, followed by clustering-based pooling; (2) instance-level alignment where a Transformer Instance Decoder with learnable object queries produces instance embeddings supervised by 2D mask (BCE+dice) and classification (CE) losses; (3) free-form language alignment where an MLLM (LLaVA-Video fine-tuned with LoRA) predicts a special <SEG> token encoding semantic and spatial priors, which conditions a Referring Decoder to produce the final 3D referring mask. A Geometry-Aware Frame Selector (GAFS) fuses 2D visual embeddings with visible 3D Gaussian semantic features via cross-attention to score view relevance, then applies iterative redundancy suppression based on camera pose distances to ensure view diversity. The multi-task training jointly optimizes text generation loss and mask prediction loss, enabling the model to handle both segmentation and VQA within a unified framework.

## Results

On 3D referring segmentation, GenSplat achieves 43.6 mIoU and 61.3 A@0.25 on ScanRefer, and 46.2 mIoU on Multi3DRefer, outperforming the strongest 3D MLLM baseline 3D-LLaVA (43.3 mIoU, 42.7 mIoU) by +0.3 and +3.5 mIoU respectively. On 3D VQA, GenSplat reaches 95.1 CIDEr and 44.9 ROUGE-L on ScanQA, and 56.0 EM / 58.9 EM-R on SQA3D, surpassing 3D-LLaVA (92.6 CIDEr, 54.5 EM, 56.6 EM-R) and the expert model SplatTalk (77.5 CIDEr, 47.6 EM, 49.4 EM-R). On the five challenging per-scene-optimization scenes from ReferSplat, GenSplat achieves 28.2 mIoU without any test-time optimization, compared to 16.5 for ReferSplat and 12.4 for Grounded-SAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenSplat outperforms all 18 baselines across both referring segmentation and VQA benchmarks. On ScanRefer, it surpasses the strongest 3D MLLM (3D-LLaVA, 43.3 mIoU) by +0.3 mIoU and the best expert model (3D-STMN, 39.5 mIoU) by +4.1 mIoU, while the 2D MLLM baseline LISA-7B trails by 22.4 mIoU. On Multi3DRefer, the margin over 3D-LLaVA widens to +3.5 mIoU (46.2 vs. 42.7), and over SegPoint to +10.1 mIoU. For VQA, GenSplat leads 3D-LLaVA by +2.5 CIDEr on ScanQA (95.1 vs. 92.6) and by +1.5 EM / +2.3 EM-R on SQA3D (56.0/58.9 vs. 54.5/56.6), while the expert model SplatTalk lags by +17.6 CIDEr on ScanQA and +8.4 EM-R on SQA3D. Notably, on the five difficult per-scene-optimization scenes (Table 2), GenSplat achieves 28.2 mIoU without any test-time optimization, beating ReferSplat (16.5), Grounded-SAM (12.4), and LangSplat (11.2) by 11.7–16.8 mIoU, demonstrating its generalization advantage over scene-specific methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) 3DGS-based language understanding (LangSplat, SceneSplat, ReferSplat), which either overfits to a fixed vocabulary or requires per-scene optimization, and (2) MLLM-based 3D scene understanding (3D-LLM, 3D-LLaVA, SegPoint, Reason3D), which typically feeds 3D point-cloud features directly into the language model. GenSplat departs from the first thread by introducing a progressive curriculum (semantic → instance → free-form) that avoids both vocabulary overfitting and scene-specific optimization, and from the second by using 2D rendered views as the MLLM's visual interface rather than raw 3D features, with a Geometry-Aware Frame Selector to choose informative views. The <SEG> token mechanism is adapted from LISA [38] for the 3DGS referring-segmentation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GenSplat is the first 3DGS framework that handles free-form language queries with cross-scene generalization without per-scene optimization, which is a practical prerequisite for deploying language-guided 3D understanding in AR/VR, embodied AI, and video editing. The progressive curriculum design offers a reusable recipe for grounding hierarchical linguistic concepts in any 3D representation. A noted limitation is ambiguous localization when scenes contain many semantically similar objects (e.g., rows of chairs), suggesting that fine-grained geometric priors during training remain an open direction. Broader open questions include whether the 2D-view interface to MLLMs scales to large outdoor scenes and whether the curriculum can be extended to video or 4D Gaussian representations.
