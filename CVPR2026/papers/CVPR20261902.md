## Problem

Current MLLMs struggle with fine-grained counting in long videos, and existing benchmarks are limited by short clips (<1 min), closed-set queries, absence of clue annotations, and single-modality (visual-only) evaluation. This makes it impossible to determine whether models genuinely reason about counts or exploit superficial correlations, and prevents fair assessment of audio-visual fusion for quantitative tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261902/cropped_page2_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 497 long-form videos (all >10 minutes) with 1,027 counting queries spanning five reference modalities: visual-only, audio-only, visual-referenced audio-query, audio-referenced visual-query, and joint audiovisual. For black-box evaluation, the model receives either the full video (Long Acc) or a trimmed reference segment (Ref Acc) and outputs a count; for white-box evaluation, it must additionally produce per-instance temporal segments (events), bounding boxes at first appearance (objects), or grouped attribute clusters, all in a strict JSON format verified by regex and bracket-matching parsers.

## Method

AV-Reasoner is built on Ola-Omni-7B and trained via a three-stage pipeline: cold-start SFT on grounding/counting tasks, curriculum-based RL (CB-RL) using GRPO organized by increasing difficulty (QA → temporal/spatial grounding → counting), and full-task RL (FT-RL) for balanced consolidation. The key insight is capability transfer: rather than training directly on scarce counting data, the model progressively acquires transferable localization skills that generalize to counting. A Stage Review Mechanism (SRM) mixes 20% of previously seen samples into each curriculum stage to mitigate catastrophic forgetting, and an offline data filtering pipeline discards trivially solved samples before each epoch. Verifiable rewards combine format compliance (regex for think/answer tags, JSON parsing for grounding), task-specific accuracy (exact match, IoU via greedy alignment, relative MAE), and are applied through GRPO's group-relative advantage estimation.

## Results

AV-Reasoner-Thinking achieves 85.01 accuracy on MusicAVQA (surpassing PAVE's 82.30), 64.70/62.00 on LLP segment/event-level grounding, 65.18 mAP on UnAV, and 46.73 cIoU on ARIG, all setting new SOTA. On DVD-Counting, AV-Reasoner reaches 44.00 accuracy, a 9.5-point improvement over Video-R1 (34.50). On the new CG-AV-Counting benchmark, AV-Reasoner attains 22.30 Long Acc and 35.83 Ref Acc, while the best closed-source model (Gemini 2.5 Pro) reaches 40.80/47.42, and human performance is 85.00/91.53, confirming counting remains far from solved. On comprehensive benchmarks, AV-Reasoner (without forced reasoning) scores 45.84 on WorldSense and 36.38 on AV-Odyssey, outperforming GPT-4o on the latter.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AV-Reasoner-Thinking outperforms all prior audio-visual models on MusicAVQA (85.01 vs. PAVE 82.30, VALOR 78.90, MEERKAT 79.15), LLP grounding (64.70/62.00 vs. Crab 59.00/54.44, MEERKAT 54.96), UnAV (65.18 vs. Crab 55.45), and ARIG spatial grounding (46.73 cIoU vs. GroundingGPT 44.02, Crab 41.78). On DVD-Counting, AV-Reasoner achieves 44.00 accuracy, beating Video-R1 by 9.5 points. However, on the new CG-AV-Counting benchmark, AV-Reasoner (22.30 Long Acc) trails Qwen3-Omni (30.77) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (40.80), and all models remain far below human performance (85.00). On WorldSense, AV-Reasoner without explicit reasoning (45.84) slightly trails Gemini 1.5 Pro (48.00), while on AV-Odyssey it surpasses GPT-4o (36.38 vs. 34.50). Notably, forcing explicit reasoning output (AV-Reasoner-Thinking) degrades performance on out-of-domain and hallucination-sensitive benchmarks (e.g., WorldSense Hallucination drops from 45.56 to 35.56), suggesting a trade-off between interpretability and robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two main threads: the omni-modal LLM architecture of Ola-Omni (which provides the base model) and the GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm popularized by DeepSeek-R1 (which provides the training algorithm). On the benchmark side, it departs from prior video counting datasets (DVD-Counting, RepCount, OVR, WorldSense) by extending them along three axes simultaneously: video length (from short clips to >10-minute videos), query modality (from visual-only to joint audio-visual), and evaluation depth (from black-box accuracy to white-box clue-grounded scoring). The training strategy also diverges from prior GRPO-based MLLMs (Video-R1, Visual-RFT) by introducing a curriculum that transfers grounding and QA capabilities into counting rather than training on counting data directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest closed-source models (Gemini 2.5 Pro at ~41% accuracy) remain far below human performance (85%), and that audio-visual fusion often *hurts* counting relative to vision-only baselines—exposing a fundamental weakness in cross-modal alignment for quantitative reasoning. The finding that explicitly outputting reasoning traces can degrade accuracy on out-of-domain and hallucination-sensitive benchmarks (e.g., WorldSense Hallucination drops from 45.56 to 35.56) challenges the prevailing assumption that more visible reasoning always helps. Open questions include whether more robust cross-domain reasoning mechanisms (beyond language-space CoT) can close the gap, and whether the curriculum-transfer approach generalizes beyond the specific task set studied here.
