## Problem

Automatic recognition of Markush structures—multimodal chemical representations combining a visual backbone with textual substituent definitions—lags behind in precision and cannot support large-scale document processing. Existing methods either handle only the image component, require pre-annotated OCR cells as input, or lack the ability to jointly interpret variable groups, positional/frequency variation indicators, and their textual definitions. Additionally, no large-scale real-world training dataset or comprehensive benchmark existed for this task.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single raw image crop of a molecule or Markush structure (no pre-annotated OCR or layout required at inference). It autoregressively generates a text sequence comprising two components: a CXSMILES (or standard SMILES for plain molecules) string encoding the molecular backbone with Markush features such as R-groups, positional and frequency variation indicators, and a structured substituent table listing all possible groups that replace variable regions. Stereochemistry is ignored during evaluation for cross-dataset consistency.

## Method

MarkushGrapher-2 employs a dual-encoder architecture: a frozen Swin-B ViT vision encoder (from MolScribe) extracts OCSR visual features, while a T5-based Vision-Text-Layout encoder processes OCR-extracted text, bounding boxes, and image patches using the UDOP spatial-fusion paradigm. A dedicated ChemicalOCR module (a 256M-parameter SmolDocling-based VLM fine-tuned on chemical images) provides the text and layout modalities end-to-end. The two encoder outputs are projected, concatenated, and fed to a T5 text decoder that autoregressively generates the CXSMILES and substituent table. Training follows a two-phase strategy: Phase 1 (Adaptation) trains only the projector and decoder on 243K OCSR samples with the vision encoder frozen, aligning the decoder to pretrained visual features; Phase 2 (Fusion) introduces the VTL encoder and trains it jointly with the decoder on 380K mixed synthetic and real-world Markush samples while keeping the vision encoder and projector frozen, preserving the OCSR feature space. The full model comprises 831M parameters (744M trainable) and is trained on a new USPTO-MOL-M dataset of 54K real-world Markush samples derived from USPTO MOL files.

## Results

On the primary Markush structure recognition task, MarkushGrapher-2 achieves 56% CXSMILES accuracy on M2S, 55% on USPTO-M, 48.0% on WildMol-M, and 53.7% on IP5-M, substantially outperforming all baselines. On the new IP5-M benchmark (1,000 real-world structures), it surpasses MolParser-Base (47.7%) by 6 points and MolScribe (22.3%) by over 31 points. For standard OCSR, MarkushGrapher-2 remains competitive, reaching 96.6% on UOB and 89.8% on USPTO, though MolParser-Base leads on WildMol (76.9% vs. 68.4%) and JPO (78.9% vs. 71.0%). The dedicated ChemicalOCR module achieves 87.2% F1 on M2S and 69.5% image-level accuracy on IP5-M, dramatically outperforming PaddleOCR v5 (1.9% F1) and EasyOCR (18.4% F1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MarkushGrapher-2 decisively outperforms all image-only baselines on Markush structure recognition: it beats MolParser-Base by 17 points on M2S (56 vs. 39) and 28 points on USPTO-M (55 vs. 30), and surpasses MolScribe by 35 points on M2S (56 vs. 21). Against the multimodal baselines, the advantage is even more pronounced—GPT-5 achieves only 3% CXSMILES accuracy on M2S and DeepSeek-OCR scores 0%, while MarkushGrapher-2 reaches 56%. Compared to its direct predecessor MarkushGrapher-1, MarkushGrapher-2 improves M2S CXSMILES accuracy from 38% to 56% and USPTO-M from 32% to 55%, while matching the Table F1 score of 65. On standard OCSR (Table 3), MarkushGrapher-2 is competitive but not dominant: MolParser-Base leads on WildMol (76.9% vs. 68.4%) and JPO (78.9% vs. 71.0%), while MarkushGrapher-2 achieves the best UOB score (96.6%) and is within 3 points of MolScribe on USPTO (89.8% vs. 93.1%). For OCR, ChemicalOCR dominates both PaddleOCR v5 and EasyOCR by margins exceeding 70 F1 points on all three benchmarks, confirming that general-purpose OCR models are inadequate for chemical structure images.

## Relation to Prior Work

MarkushGrapher-2 directly extends MarkushGrapher (CVPR 2025), the only prior vision-language model capable of jointly interpreting textual and visual components of Markush definitions, by removing its dependency on pre-annotated OCR cells and unifying molecular and Markush structure recognition in a single model. The architecture draws on the UDOP fusion paradigm for aligning spatially coincident visual and textual tokens, the MolScribe Swin-B vision encoder for OCSR features, and SmolDocling as the backbone for the new ChemicalOCR module. The two-phase training strategy departs from prior single-stage fine-tuning by first adapting the decoder to the frozen OCSR feature space before introducing the VTL encoder, preserving pretrained visual representations while learning Markush-specific features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Markush structures underpin patent prior-art searches and freedom-to-operate analyses, yet remain indexed only in proprietary databases (MARPAT, DWPIM); this work provides the first end-to-end, publicly available pipeline for large-scale automated extraction, along with the IP5-M benchmark (1,000 manually annotated structures spanning five patent offices, 1980–2025) and the USPTO-MOL-M training dataset. However, absolute accuracy remains modest (53.7% CXSMILES on IP5-M), reflecting the inherent difficulty of non-standardized drawing conventions and recursive textual definitions. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to even more complex Markush features (e.g., nested variable groups), whether the two-phase strategy transfers to other multimodal document tasks, and how performance scales with additional real-world training data beyond the current ~380k samples.
