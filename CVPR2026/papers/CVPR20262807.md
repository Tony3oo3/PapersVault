## Problem

State-of-the-art diffusion-based virtual try-on systems achieve high visual fidelity but require cloud GPUs, forcing users to upload personal photos and raising privacy concerns. Deploying such models on mobile devices is blocked by three barriers: excessive parameter counts for mobile NPUs, garment semantic drift across diffusion timesteps, and heavy reliance on large-scale pretraining that precludes lightweight standalone architectures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262807/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes exactly two inputs — a single person image and a single garment image — with no segmentation masks, pose maps, or additional user data required. It produces a full virtual try-on image at 1024×768 resolution, synthesizing the entire scene (body, clothing, background) in a mask-free manner. The entire pipeline runs offline on commodity mobile hardware with only 2.84 GB peak memory, compared to 5–18 GB for server-based baselines.

## Method

MOBILE-VTON introduces a modular TeacherNet–GarmentNet–TryonNet (TGT) architecture built on 415M-parameter Light-UNets adapted from SnapGen. A frozen Stable Diffusion 3.5 Large teacher provides score-based supervision via a Feature-Guided Adversarial (FGA) Distillation strategy that combines DMD2-style score matching with a lightweight GAN discriminator for photorealism. GarmentNet is trained with a trajectory-consistency loss that enforces garment feature invariance across all diffusion timesteps, preventing semantic drift. TryonNet performs garment-to-person alignment through height-wise latent concatenation of person and garment (inspired by CatVTON) plus dual-branch cross-attention using DINOv2-based visual features (Light-Adapter) and CLIP text encoders, all trained from scratch without large-scale pretraining.

## Results

On VITON-HD, MOBILE-VTON achieves the best LPIPS (0.088) and SSIM (0.893) among all compared methods, while on DressCode Upper-body it attains the best SSIM (0.935) and second-best LPIPS (0.053). In the unpaired setting, it records FID of 10.211 and KID of 2.023 on VITON-HD, and FID of 12.775 and KID of 1.917 on DressCode, remaining competitive with server-based baselines that use 2–7× more parameters. Crucially, MOBILE-VTON requires only 2.84 GB of memory and is the sole method capable of running entirely on commodity mobile devices, whereas all baselines need 5.8–18.5 GB and server-class GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOBILE-VTON wins on paired fidelity metrics: it achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.088) and highest SSIM (0.893) on VITON-HD, beating IDM-VTON (0.102/0.868) and BooW-VTON (0.107/0.864), and the highest SSIM (0.935) on DressCode, surpassing BooW-VTON (0.928) and IDM-VTON (0.920). On unpaired realism metrics (FID, KID), BooW-VTON remains the strongest baseline (FID 9.036/KID 1.011 on VITON-HD; FID 11.361/KID 1.043 on DressCode), with MOBILE-VTON trailing by roughly 1.2 FID and 1.0 KID on VITON-HD and 1.4 FID / 0.9 KID on DressCode. IDM-VTON leads on CLIP-I for both datasets (0.875 on VITON-HD, 0.870 on DressCode), where MOBILE-VTON scores 0.833 and 0.845 respectively. CatVTON and LaDI-VTON are consistently outperformed across nearly all metrics. The critical practical distinction is that MOBILE-VTON is mask-free and runs on-device at 2.84 GB, while all six baselines require server GPUs (5.8–18.5 GB) and most also depend on segmentation masks at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOBILE-VTON sits at the intersection of diffusion-based virtual try-on and mobile-efficient generation. It adapts the SnapGen Light-UNet architecture for on-device inference, borrows the score-matching distillation framework from DMD2, and reuses the decoupled cross-attention mechanism of IP-Adapter (with DINOv2 replacing CLIP-ViT for compactness). Its most direct methodological ancestors are CatVTON (latent concatenation for garment injection) and IDM-VTON (multi-level garment feature fusion), but it departs from both by training from scratch on VTON data rather than fine-tuning a large pretrained text-to-image model, and by adding a trajectory-consistency constraint on garment features that neither prior work employs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a diffusion-based VTON pipeline can run entirely on a commodity phone while matching server-based quality, which has immediate implications for privacy-sensitive fashion e-commerce and regulated markets. The practical limitation is that 2.84 GB of inference memory still restricts deployment to mid-to-high-end devices, and the training pipeline itself requires 8×A100 GPUs, so the 'on-device' claim applies only to inference. Open questions include whether the FGA distillation recipe generalizes to other on-device generative tasks (e.g., video try-on, full-body, multi-garment), and whether the trajectory-consistency loss can be extended to temporal settings without prohibitive compute.
