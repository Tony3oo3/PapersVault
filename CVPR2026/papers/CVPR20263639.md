## Problem

Existing zero-shot object counting methods suffer from a lack of fine-grained quantity awareness, treating counting as coarse category retrieval without understanding how object numbers visually differ. Additionally, adapting pretrained vision-language models to counting tasks causes feature space distortion and spatial insensitivity, forcing a trade-off between encoder adaptation and zero-shot generalization to unseen categories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263639/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (384×384 for ViT-B/16 or 392×392 for ViT-L/14) and a free-form text description specifying the target object category, and produces a pixel-wise density map whose integral equals the total object count. During training, K=5 quantity-oriented text prompts (one factual count plus four counterfactual values via adaptive interval binning) are processed in parallel through shared encoder parameters, while at inference only a category-only prompt is used, ensuring zero-shot transferability without numerical supervision.

## Method

QICA introduces three coupled components. First, the Synergistic Prompting Strategy (SPS) injects learnable prompt tokens into the first 9 layers of both frozen CLIP text and vision encoders, conditioned on discrete quantity embeddings and linked via a coupling function Φ that projects language prompts to vision prompts, enabling bidirectional gradient flow for joint quantity-aware adaptation. Second, the Cost Aggregation Decoder (CAD) computes cosine similarity maps between dense visual features and category-only text embeddings, then refines these maps through Swin Transformer spatial aggregation and progressive multi-scale upsampling with similarity-modulated skip connections, operating on the similarity map rather than intermediate features to preserve the pretrained embedding space. Third, the Multi-level Quantity Alignment Loss (L_MQA) enforces numerical consistency at both encoder level (ranking constraints ensuring the factual quantity prompt achieves highest alignment score and closer counts rank higher) and decoder level (auxiliary L2 losses tying each predicted count to its corresponding quantity hypothesis), all combined with standard density map regression.

## Results

On FSC-147 (the primary benchmark), QICA with ViT-L/14 achieves 12.41 MAE and 97.28 RMSE on the test set (12.70/76.82 average), while the ViT-B/16 variant reaches 13.05 MAE and 104.17 RMSE. Cross-domain evaluation without fine-tuning yields 6.07 MAE on CARPK and 140.7 MAE on ShanghaiTech-A (ViT-L/14), establishing competitive or state-of-the-art performance among open-set text-prompted counting methods. The ablation confirms a cumulative 23.2% MAE reduction from baseline CLIP fine-tuning (16.15) to the full QICA pipeline (12.41) on FSC-147 test.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QICA (ViT-L/14) outperforms all CLIP-based zero-shot text-prompted baselines on FSC-147: it reduces average MAE by 27.2% over VLCounter (17.56→12.70), 30.2% over CLIP-Count (18.29→12.70), and 25.7% over CounTX (17.23→12.70). Against the strongest text-prompted competitor CountGD (13.45 avg MAE), QICA achieves a 5.6% improvement while using a lighter CLIP backbone rather than GDINO-Swin-B. On CARPK, QICA† (6.07 MAE) slightly trails CountGD's text mode (5.98 MAE) but surpasses VLCounter (6.46), T2ICount (8.61), and CLIP-Count (11.96). On ShanghaiTech-A, QICA† achieves 140.7 MAE, marginally beating CountGD (141.9 MAE) and substantially outperforming CLIP-Count (192.6) and CounTX (219.8). The ViT-B/16 variant (13.05 test MAE) surpasses CountGD's test MAE (14.76) despite the lighter backbone, and shows notably smaller validation-to-test degradation (0.77 MAE gap) compared to CountGD's 2.62 MAE gap, indicating more stable generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

QICA extends the CLIP-based zero-shot counting paradigm (CLIP-Count, VLCounter, CounTX) by addressing two identified gaps: the absence of fine-grained quantity awareness in text prompts and spatial insensitivity caused by feature-space distortion during encoder adaptation. It repurposes the cost-aggregation decoder concept from open-vocabulary segmentation (CAT-Seg) to operate directly on vision-text similarity maps rather than intermediate features, and introduces numerically conditioned synergistic prompts with bidirectional gradient coupling between vision and language branches. The multi-level quantity alignment loss with ranking constraints draws on ordinal regression ideas but applies them jointly across encoder and decoder stages, a combination not previously explored in the counting literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical parameter-efficient path (≈19M trainable parameters with frozen CLIP encoders) to zero-shot counting with competitive accuracy and demonstrated cross-dataset generalization without fine-tuning, which is valuable for deployment in open-world surveillance and inventory scenarios. However, the improvements over strong baselines like T2ICount and CountGD are modest (sub-1 MAE on FSC-147 test), and the method is limited to single-category counting with discrete quantity hypotheses. Open questions include whether continuous quantity representations could replace the K-hypothesis scheme, whether the approach extends to multi-category simultaneous counting, and whether the cost-aggregation paradigm generalizes beyond CLIP-based backbones.
