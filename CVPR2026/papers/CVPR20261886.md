## Problem

Long-context video inference in both VLMs and DiTs is bottlenecked by the quadratic cost of self-attention, and existing sparse attention methods rely on coarse-grained line or block patterns that retain substantial redundant computation. The paper identifies that video attention maps instead exhibit a fine-grained vertical-vector sparsity structure that coarse patterns fail to capture, leaving a large gap to the oracle sparse pattern in both recall and effective sparsity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261886/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For video understanding, the method operates inside existing VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, InternVL-3.5-8B) on inputs of 64 video frames plus a text query, producing a text response at context lengths of ~17K–26K tokens. For video generation, it is applied within DiTs (Wan2.1-T2V-14B, HunyuanVideo-T2V-13B) to produce 720P videos from text prompts at much longer context lengths of 76K–119K tokens. The method is inference-time only and does not alter the model's input/output interface; it replaces the full attention computation with a vector-sparse variant controlled by a per-head filtering ratio α.

## Method

VecAttention exploits the observation that the most informative entries in video attention maps are organized as fine-grained vertical vectors (columns) rather than continuous lines or rectangular blocks. It operates in two stages: (1) Important-Vector Selection, where queries are pooled into groups of size Pq=64 to estimate per-column importance, and a sorting-free minS threshold filter (selecting entries where score ≥ row-max − α) identifies informative columns; this selection is fused into the tiled QK GEMM via TilingSelect so that intermediate attention tiles are never written back to HBM, cutting memory traffic from Θ(N²Pq⁻¹) to Θ(N²Pq⁻¹(1−ρ)). (2) Vector-Sparse Computation, where only the selected key-value vectors are dynamically loaded into on-chip memory to compute the sparse attention output. A dynamic-programming procedure offline searches for per-head filter ratios to maximize accuracy under a global sparsity budget, and a cross-tile row-max accumulator (controlled by group size Gk) improves filtering consistency across K-tiles.

## Results

On video understanding, VecAttention achieves 51.1% average accuracy at 78.6% sparsity on InternVL-3.5-8B (vs. 52.7% for full attention) and 49.9% at 78.5% sparsity on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (matching full attention's 49.9%), while all baselines drop 2.5–5.6 points below full attention. On video generation, VecAttention attains PSNR 22.8 / SSIM 0.779 on HunyuanVideo at 62.1% sparsity, outperforming XAttention (21.2 / 0.734) and Sparse VideoGen (21.8 / 0.769). Overall, VecAttention delivers a 2.65× speedup over full attention and a 1.83× speedup over the best sparse-attention baseline, with up to 93% sparsity achievable without substantial accuracy loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VecAttention outperforms all four baselines on video understanding. Against FlexPrefill (line/block hybrid), VecAttention gains 4.0 points average accuracy on InternVL (51.1 vs. 47.1) and 2.8 points on Qwen2.5-VL (49.9 vs. 47.1) at comparable or higher sparsity. Against XAttention (block-sparse with antidiagonal scoring), VecAttention leads by 1.6 points on InternVL and 2.7 points on Qwen2.5-VL, with the gap widening on VCRBench (33.8 vs. 32.5 and 25.4 vs. 20.0). Against AnchorAttention (stripe-granularity), VecAttention wins by 1.7 points on InternVL and 0.5 points on Qwen2.5-VL, while also achieving higher sparsity (78.6% vs. 78.6% and 78.5% vs. 74.6%). On video generation, VecAttention surpasses XAttention on both PSNR and SSIM for Wan2.1 (19.7/0.668 vs. 19.7/0.658) and HunyuanVideo (22.8/0.779 vs. 21.2/0.734), and beats Sparse VideoGen on PSNR and SSIM for both models, though SVG retains a marginal LPIPS edge on HunyuanVideo (0.326 vs. 0.330).

## Relation to Prior Work

VecAttention builds on FlashAttention-2's tiled GEMM and sharding strategy for GPU-efficient attention, but departs fundamentally from the coarse-grained sparse patterns (line and block) that dominate prior work such as MInference, FlexPrefill, XAttention, and AnchorAttention. The key conceptual shift is identifying the vertical vector (a column of P_q tokens) as the natural granularity for video attention sparsity, rather than full-length lines or rectangular blocks. The paper also addresses a system-level gap left by prior fine-grained methods (FG-Attention, AnchorAttention): the overhead of important-region selection at fine granularity, solved via TilingSelect (fusing selection into the QK GEMM) and a sorting-free minS filter that avoids materializing the full estimated attention map.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.65× speedup over full attention with comparable accuracy is practically significant for deploying long-context video models, where inference latency currently reaches minute-to-hour scales. The consistent observation that vertical-vector sparsity outperforms both horizontal-vector and coarse line/block patterns across four models and four benchmarks suggests this is a structural property of video attention rather than a model-specific artifact, potentially guiding future sparse-attention design for video. Open questions include whether the per-head dynamic-programming calibration generalizes to unseen video distributions, whether the pattern persists at even longer contexts (beyond 119K tokens tested), and whether the approach transfers to other modalities with similar temporal structure (e.g., audio, 3D point clouds).
