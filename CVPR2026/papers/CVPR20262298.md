## Problem

Existing motion forecasting models are trained and evaluated on fixed-size historical windows, yet in real-world driving the available observation length for each agent varies continuously as agents enter and leave the field of view. Prior streaming methods (e.g., RealMotion, DeMo) propagate context across inference steps but are optimized for a fixed number of streaming passes, causing significant performance degradation when the number of passes or the total context length deviates from the training configuration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262298/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes short observation windows (e.g., 1 s of agent positions/headings and vectorized lane segments) at each streaming step, augmented by the previous step's encoded scene context and prior trajectory predictions for relay. It outputs K multi-modal future trajectories (xy-coordinates over the prediction horizon) with calibrated probability scores, and can be extended to produce joint multi-agent world predictions by fusing per-agent mode queries through an interaction block. The dual training objective means the model is simultaneously trained to produce valid predictions from a single short chunk and from the accumulated streaming history.

## Method

SHARP builds on a compact transformer backbone (self-attention agent encoder, PointNet lane encoder, cross-attention scene encoder, DETR-like decoder) and introduces three key mechanisms for robust streaming. First, an instance-aware context streamer uses a learned attention mask bias to explicitly align agent tokens across streaming steps, replacing the purely positional correspondence used by prior streaming methods and enabling recovery from tracking discontinuities. Second, target-centric features are extracted by aggregating scene tokens around the K trajectory endpoints from the previous prediction, providing goal-oriented context at low latency. Third, a dual training loss jointly optimizes predictions conditioned on the full streamed context (L_stream) and on the current short chunk alone (L_chunk), forcing the encoder to learn representations that generalize across observation lengths. The model processes short 1 s windows rather than the 3 s windows of prior streaming methods, enabling more frequent gradient updates and reducing latency for newly detected agents.

## Results

On the Argoverse 2 multi-agent test set, SHARP achieves a new state-of-the-art with avgBrierMinFDE₆ = 1.80, outperforming DeMo (1.93) and RealMotion (2.01), while also leading on avgMinFDE₆ (1.15 vs. 1.24 and 1.32) and actorMR₆ (0.15 vs. 0.16 and 0.18). On the single-agent AV2 test set, SHARP is within a few centimeters of the best snapshot methods (mFDE₆ = 1.19 vs. DeMo's 1.17) while achieving a better Brier score (1.83 vs. 1.84). Across varying streaming context lengths (1 s to 8 s) on AV2 validation, SHARP consistently outperforms both RealMotion and DeMo, with the largest gaps at short contexts (e.g., mFDE₆ = 1.18 at T_cl = 3 s vs. 1.26 and 1.35) and long contexts (mFDE₆ = 0.48 at T_cl = 8 s vs. 0.78 and 0.70). Inference latency is 33 ms for 16 agents on an RTX 3090, faster than DeMo (35 ms) and RealMotion (44 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SHARP outperforms both prior streaming methods (RealMotion [32] and DeMo [46]) across all multi-agent AV2 metrics, with the largest margin on avgBrierMinFDE₆ (1.80 vs. 1.93 and 2.01) and avgMinFDE₆ (1.15 vs. 1.24 and 1.32). On the single-agent AV2 test set, SHARP is essentially tied with DeMo (mFDE₆ 1.19 vs. 1.17; b-mFDE₆ 1.83 vs. 1.84) and slightly trails on mADE₆ (0.64 vs. 0.61), confirming that the streaming design does not sacrifice snapshot accuracy. Against varying-length baselines (FLN [42], POP [38], TSD [52]), SHARP wins on every dataset and context length tested: on AV1 with 2 s history it achieves mFDE₆ = 0.91 vs. TSD's 0.94 and POP's 1.04; on AV2 with 5 s history it reaches mFDE₆ = 1.20 vs. POP's 1.45; and on nuScenes with 2 s history it obtains mFDE₅ = 1.92 vs. FLN's 3.78. The streaming robustness evaluation (Table 1) shows that RealMotion and DeMo degrade substantially when the number of streaming passes deviates from their fixed 3-pass training configuration, whereas SHARP maintains consistent performance from 1 s to 8 s context, with gaps widening at both short (e.g., mFDE₆ 1.18 vs. 1.35 at T_cl = 3 s) and long (0.48 vs. 0.78 at T_cl = 8 s) contexts.

## Relation to Prior Work

SHARP builds directly on the streaming motion forecasting framework introduced by RealMotion [32] and extended by DeMo [46], adopting their cross-attention scene context streamer and trajectory relay mechanism. It departs from these methods in three key ways: (1) replacing positional-only context streaming with an instance-aware attention-mask design that explicitly models agent correspondences, (2) reducing the observation window from 3 s to 1 s to enable more frequent updates and shorter latency for newly detected agents, and (3) introducing a dual training objective that jointly optimizes streamed and single-chunk predictions. The architecture itself draws on standard components from the trajectory prediction literature—self-attention agent encoders, PointNet-based lane encoding, and DETR-style multimodal decoding—without introducing new backbone designs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap that the community has under-addressed: existing streaming methods are trained and evaluated at a fixed number of inference passes, yet deployed AVs encounter agents with arbitrarily short or long observation histories. The evolving-scenes evaluation protocol introduced here (varying context lengths, prediction time steps, and horizons) is a useful contribution that should become standard. Open questions include whether the short-window streaming design generalizes to higher-frequency sensors or longer prediction horizons beyond 6 s, and whether the instance-aware attention mask remains effective under severe tracking discontinuities. The approach's reliance on a fixed-radius scene sampling also limits its applicability to very dense urban scenarios.
