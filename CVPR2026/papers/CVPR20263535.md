## Problem

Sentinel-2 provides global multispectral coverage but with only 12 bands and non-unified spatial resolutions (60/20/10 m), while NASA's AVIRIS-NG delivers 186+ bands at 5 m GSD but covers only the American region. The prior state-of-the-art (COS2A) achieved 12-to-172 spectral super-resolution at 10 m GSD by exploiting high-resolution Sentinel-2 bands as spatial guidance, but this strategy is inapplicable at 5 m GSD where no such guidance exists. This paper tackles the substantially harder joint 12-to-186 spectral super-resolution and 10 m-to-5 m spatial super-resolution without high-resolution spatial priors.

## Input / Output

The input is a multiresolution Sentinel-2 MSI with 12 bands at mixed GSDs (60 m, 20 m, 10 m), where 60 m and 20 m bands are upsampled by factors of 36 and 4 respectively to a unified 10 m spatial grid before processing. The output is an AVIRIS-level HSI with 186 contiguous spectral bands at 5 m GSD, requiring simultaneous 2× spatial upscaling and a 15.5× spectral expansion. The spatial super-resolution component is notably harder than typical SR because the 5 m target eliminates the mixed-pixel phenomenon, demanding physically reliable spectral signatures rather than merely visually plausible reconstructions.

## Method

UALNet is constructed by unfolding a quasi-Split Bregman (Qusai-SB) optimization with three terms: a data-fitting term modeling the spectral response function and spatial blurring, a discriminator-maximization regularization (DMR) that embeds adversarial learning directly into the optimization criterion, and a data-driven spectrum regularization constraining the estimated HSI's cross-band correlation to match a spectral prior matrix (SPM) learned by a lightweight PriorNet. The PriorNet (0.05 M params) performs preliminary 5 m spatial unification and approximates the 186×186 SPM, decoupling the spatial and spectral challenges. Each Qusai-SB iteration's A-update is decomposed into three gradient components (data-fitting, spectrum regularization, discriminator guidance) implemented as share-weighted convolutional modules with channel attention, unfolded over 2 iterations. The discriminator is trained jointly for 250 epochs then frozen, yet continues to guide inference through the DMR term—enabling adversarial supervision at test time without an explicit adversarial loss, a concept the authors term unfolding adversarial learning (UAL).

## Results

On the AVIRIS-NG evaluation set (20 test pairs, 252×252 spatial size), UALNet achieves the best performance across all four metrics: PSNR 32.60 dB, SAM 2.49°, SSIM 0.9214, and RMSE 0.0145. In terms of efficiency, UALNet requires only 1.76 M parameters and 120.1 G MACs, corresponding to roughly 3% of the parameters and 15% of the MACs of the next-best Transformer (MST++: 56.7 M params, 804 G MACs). The closest competitor COS2A trails by 0.30 dB in PSNR and 0.83° in SAM while using 2.6× more parameters and 1.9× more MACs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UALNet outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric (PSNR, SAM, SSIM, RMSE). Against the closest competitor COS2A (the only other method targeting Sentinel-2→AVIRIS conversion), UALNet gains +0.30 dB PSNR, −0.83° SAM, +0.032 SSIM, and −0.0033 RMSE, while using 62% fewer parameters and 48% fewer MACs. Compared to the strongest Transformer baseline MST++, UALNet improves PSNR by +0.92 dB and SAM by −0.26°, yet requires only 3.1% of the parameters (1.76 M vs. 56.70 M) and 14.9% of the MACs (120 G vs. 804 G). SPECAT is the second-closest Transformer in SAM (2.70°) and RMSE (0.0149), but still trails UALNet by 0.21° in SAM and 0.0004 in RMSE. The older CNN baselines (HSCNN+, HRNet, Restormer, MST) all fall 1.7–3.8 dB below UALNet in PSNR, and SSRNet performs worst overall (25.09 dB PSNR, 13.99° SAM), exhibiting severe spectral distortion. MSFN, despite the highest MACs (1680 G), achieves only 29.30 dB PSNR, confirming that architectural complexity alone does not guarantee superior spectral-spatial reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the deep unfolding paradigm (Zhang et al., CVPR 2020) and Split Bregman optimization (Goldstein et al., 2009) by replacing the conventional handcrafted or implicit deep priors with a lightweight data-driven PriorNet that provides both a spatial prior image and a spectral prior matrix. It departs from standard GAN usage by embedding the discriminator's maximization term directly into the unfolded optimization criterion, so the discriminator continuously guides inference rather than being discarded after training. The closest prior work, COS2A (Lin et al., 2025), achieves 12-to-172 spectral super-resolution at 10 m GSD via a spectral-spatial duality data-fusion strategy; this paper extends the target to 186 bands at 5 m GSD, where that duality-based fusion is inapplicable because 5 m Sentinel-2 bands do not physically exist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 5 m GSD is practically significant because it substantially mitigates the mixed pixel phenomenon that limits material identification at 10 m, and the 186-band output approaches AVIRIS-NG fidelity for global coverage. The efficiency result (1.75 M params, 120 G MACs vs. MST++'s 56.7 M / 804 G) is notable for deployment in resource-constrained RS pipelines, and the discriminator-guided inference concept could transfer to other unfolding-based inverse problems. However, the evaluation relies entirely on simulated data pairs (20 test images) derived from AVIRIS-NG, and the authors acknowledge that real Sentinel-2/AVIRIS-NG co-registered pairs remain scarce; generalization to other sensors, spectral ranges, or geographic regions is untested. An open question is whether the quasi-SB framework with a frozen discriminator remains stable under distribution shift in operational satellite data.
