## Problem

Most prototype-based interpretable classifiers learn part prototypes using only unimodal visual supervision, causing prototypes to capture superficial statistical correlations rather than semantically meaningful, human-understandable concepts. Additionally, existing orthonormality constraints operate solely in embedding space and fail to prevent multiple prototypes from responding to the same image regions, limiting both discriminative capacity and interpretability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263557/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image as primary input and the image's category name as a textual description for semantic conditioning. It produces a class prediction, per-prototype spatial activation maps (H_S × W_S heatmaps), and scalar similarity scores per prototype. The text input is not free-form but restricted to the ground-truth category label, and the attribution map is generated internally via CLIP's pre-trained encoders rather than requiring external annotations.

## Method

PRISM extends the ProtoPNet architecture with class-specific 1×1 prototypes (dimension 160) and introduces two key mechanisms. First, an information-theoretic attribution module inserts an IB layer into CLIP's image encoder, learning a pixel-wise attribution matrix A that maximizes mutual information between the compressed image representation and the text embedding while minimizing information retained from the original image; this yields a language-conditioned spatial map of semantically relevant regions. Second, an alignment loss (L_align) enforces the aggregated prototype activations to match this attribution map, while a spatial compactness loss (L_comp) minimizes the entropy of the softmax-normalized prototype responses at each spatial location, encouraging exclusive and non-overlapping prototype localization. The total objective combines cross-entropy, orthonormality, compactness, and alignment losses with tuned weight coefficients.

## Results

On CUB-200-2011, PRISM achieves 87.8% Top-1 accuracy with ViT-B/16 (10 prototypes per class), outperforming the strongest interpretable baseline ST-ProtoPNet (87.4%) and the non-interpretable ViT-B/16 baseline (87.0%). On Stanford Dogs, PRISM reaches 86.1% with ViT-B/16, again surpassing ST-ProtoPNet (85.9%) and the ViT baseline (85.5%). In interpretability, PRISM achieves a consistency score of 75.75% on ViT-B/16 (vs. 19.85% for ProtoPNet) and the best Deletion AUC (7.84%), Insertion AUC (77.67%), IoU (46.32%), and OIRR (26.41%) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRISM outperforms ProtoPNet [5] by +2.3% to +4.6% on CUB and +1.9% to +3.4% on Dogs across all shared backbones, and also dominates on interpretability metrics (e.g., 75.75% vs. 19.85% consistency on ViT-B/16). Against the strongest prototype-based baseline ST-ProtoPNet [49], PRISM gains a modest but consistent +0.4% to +0.7% in accuracy and substantially improves consistency (75.75% vs. 47.9% for T-ProtoPNet on ViT-B/16). TesNet [50] and EvalProtoPNet [14] are outperformed by 1.5–2.5% on CUB and 1.5–2.0% on Dogs. ProtoViT [28] is the closest competitor on ViT backbones (87.1% vs. 87.8% on CUB ViT-B/16), with PRISM winning by 0.7%. Against image-text concept-based methods, PRISM (87.8%) substantially outperforms Label-Free CBM [34] (76.4%) and FVLC [21] (77.9%) on ViT-B/16, confirming that implicit cross-modal alignment via information-theoretic attribution is more effective than explicit concept bottleneck supervision. The only metric where PRISM trails a baseline is stability on ViT-B/16 (69.71% vs. ProtoPNet's 70.34%), attributed to ProtoPNet's reliance on localized feature similarity being inherently robust to small perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRISM extends the Prototypical Part Network (ProtoPNet) architecture by introducing cross-modal semantic guidance from CLIP to anchor visual prototypes to language-conditioned regions, departing from the unimodal visual-only paradigm that characterizes most prior prototype-based methods (ProtoPNet, TesNet, ST-ProtoPNet, ProtoViT). It adapts the Information Bottleneck Attribution mechanism from Schulz et al. to a multimodal setting, using it not for post-hoc explanation but as a training signal to align prototype activations with text-conditioned relevance maps. Unlike concept-based approaches (ProtoCBM, Align2Concept) that require explicit concept annotations or external LLMs, PRISM's alignment is implicit and self-contained, relying only on category names as textual descriptions. The spatial compactness loss further distinguishes PRISM by operating on activation maps in input space rather than solely on prototype vectors in embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PRISM addresses a recognized gap in interpretable vision: prototypes learned purely from visual similarity tend to capture superficial statistical correlations rather than semantically meaningful parts, and standard nearest-patch visualization can produce ambiguous activations. The dramatic consistency improvement (≈20% → ≈76% on ViT-B/16) suggests that language-conditioned attribution is a practical and effective signal for grounding prototypes in human-understandable semantics without expensive concept annotation. However, the approach is limited to two fine-grained datasets, depends on CLIP's pre-trained alignment quality, and the stability score is marginally lower than ProtoPNet's, indicating a potential trade-off between semantic grounding and perturbation robustness. Open questions include whether the IB attribution mechanism scales to open-vocabulary or zero-shot settings, and whether the compactness-alignment trade-off can be resolved more gracefully.
