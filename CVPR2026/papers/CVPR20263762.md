## Problem

Standard residual connections, while essential for gradient propagation in deep networks, inject an echo of shallow, high-frequency representations into deeper layers, potentially reducing the capacity for abstract semantic feature learning. This tension between trainability and representation quality has not been carefully reconsidered in the context of generative self-supervised learning frameworks (masked autoencoders, diffusion models), where bottleneck features are expected to encode abstract semantics but the identity shortcut directly bypasses this abstraction.

## Input / Output

For MAE, the model takes 224×224 RGB images with a random subset of pixels masked out and produces a feature embedding at the encoder bottleneck, while the decoder reconstructs the masked pixels. For diffusion models, input is a 256×256 RGB image (or its 32×32×4 VAE latent) plus an optional class label, and output is a generated image. A key architectural detail is that encoder-decoder skip connections are added (absent in the original MAE) so that shallow-layer detail can flow directly to the decoder for reconstruction, freeing the bottleneck to learn abstract features.

## Method

The paper replaces each identity shortcut x_{l+1} = x_l + f_θ(x_l) with a decayed identity shortcut x_{l+1} = α_l · x_l + f_θ(x_l), where α_l = 1 − ((1−α_min)/L)·l decreases linearly from 1 at the first layer to α_min at the last, governed by a single fixed hyperparameter. This creates a smooth transition from residual to feed-forward behavior with depth, progressively suppressing the flow of fine-grained input information toward the bottleneck and encouraging low-rank, abstract representations. In MAE the decay is applied only within the encoder (with added encoder-to-decoder skip connections for reconstruction), while in diffusion models it extends through the full encoder-decoder stack. Residual zero initialization of each block's final output layer stabilizes training when α_min ≤ 0.7. The approach introduces no learnable parameters and is validated to improve linear probing (67.8%→72.7%), KNN (27.4%→63.9%), and diffusion FID (17.2→11.8) on ImageNet-1K.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with a ViT-B/16 backbone, MAE with decayed identity shortcuts (α_min=0.6) achieves 72.7% linear probing accuracy and 63.9% KNN accuracy, substantially improving over the standard MAE baseline (67.8% LP, 27.4% KNN). For class-conditional ImageNet-1K 256×256 generation with SiT-XL/2, the method reduces FID from 17.2 (baseline, 400k steps) to 11.8 (α_min=0.8, 400k steps) and improves Inception Score to 91.8. On COCO validation, the learned representations yield 10.4 mIoU on unsupervised semantic segmentation versus 4.1 mIoU for baseline MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct MAE baseline, decayed identity shortcuts yield a +4.9 pp gain in linear probing (72.7% vs. 67.8%) and a dramatic +36.5 pp gain in KNN accuracy (63.9% vs. 27.4%), while fine-tuning drops marginally (82.9% vs. 83.6%). Among generative baselines, the method outperforms Data2Vec (LP 72.7 vs. 68.0; KNN 63.9 vs. 33.2), CAE (LP 72.7 vs. 70.4; KNN 63.9 vs. 51.4), and Latent MIM (LP 72.7 vs. 72.0; KNN 63.9 vs. 50.1), and is essentially tied with I-JEPA on LP (72.7 vs. 72.9). Against contrastive methods, the approach still trails DINO (LP 72.7 vs. 78.2; KNN 63.9 vs. 76.1) and MoCo-v3 (LP 72.7 vs. 76.7; KNN 63.9 vs. 66.6), but narrows the gap considerably compared to standard MAE. For diffusion model generation (SiT-XL/2, ImageNet-1K 256×256), α_min=0.8 achieves FID 11.8 at 400k steps versus 17.2 for the baseline and 16.5 for α_min=1.0 with UNet skip connections, confirming the improvement stems from the decay scheme itself rather than the added skip connections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MAE (He et al., CVPR 2022) and SiT-XL/2 (Ma et al., ECCV 2024) as its primary generative representation learning and diffusion model baselines, modifying their residual connection structure. Conceptually, it departs from the standard identity shortcut of ResNets (He et al., 2016) and connects to highway networks (Srivastava et al., 2015), which introduced learned gating but empirically collapsed to near-identity. The key theoretical anchor is Huh et al. (2021), who showed that feed-forward stacks exhibit a low-rank simplicity bias absent in residual networks; this paper operationalizes that insight by linearly decaying the shortcut weight with depth, bridging residual and feed-forward behavior without learned parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a near-universal architectural choice (identity residual connections) actively degrades representation quality in generative frameworks challenges a foundational assumption in deep learning and opens the door to rethinking shortcut design across modalities and architectures. The dramatic KNN improvement (27.4% → 63.9%) and FID reduction (17.2 → 11.8) are backed by consistent results across ViT-B/L, U-ViT, and SiT-XL/2 on multiple datasets, lending strong credibility. However, the authors note the method does not transfer to contrastive learning (where high-rank features are explicitly encouraged), and the connection between low effective rank and representation quality remains correlational rather than causal. Open questions include whether the decay schedule generalizes to non-ViT backbones, other modalities, and whether the optimal α_min can be predicted from architecture alone rather than tuned per depth.
