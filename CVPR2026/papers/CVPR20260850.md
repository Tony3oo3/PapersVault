## Problem

Existing open-set 3D object retrieval methods built on CLIP suffer from limited fine-grained discrimination due to CLIP's global image-text alignment strategy, while naively adapting the more fine-grained self-supervised DINO encoder leads to severe overfitting on seen-class view patterns. The paper addresses how to harness DINO's fine-grained local detail capture while retaining CLIP's broad semantic generalization to unseen categories, without requiring text at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260850/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 24 multi-view images at 256×256 resolution per 3D object (projected following the HGM²R scheme) and produces a single compact d-dimensional descriptor embedding for retrieval. Unlike CLIP-based competitors that require both image and text modalities at inference, DEC operates solely on visual features, making it more efficient. During training only, CLIP's text encoder processes prompts of the form 'a photo of [class]' from an ImageNet-derived lexicon to synthesize virtual features for unseen categories.

## Method

DEC comprises two key modules. The Chunking and Adapting Module (CAM) divides the M view feature sequence into K non-overlapping chunks, applies shared CBR blocks (1D convolution + batch norm + ReLU) to aggregate local inter-view patterns within each chunk, then uses non-parametric 1D pooling to progressively integrate cross-chunk relations at longer ranges, ultimately yielding one global representation fused with the original DINO GAP feature via a learnable weight λ. The Virtual Feature Synthesis (VFS) module synthesizes virtual unseen-class features by taking a seen-class CLIP visual embedding, shifting it along the semantic direction (unseen text embedding minus seen text embedding) in CLIP's aligned space, and projecting the result into DINO's embedding space via a lightweight MLP; this is grounded theoretically by showing CLIP's contrastive alignment preserves semantic relations isometrically between text and visual spaces. Training uses multi-similarity metric learning end-to-end, with VFS active only during training to regularize CAM against overfitting, incurring zero additional inference cost.

## Results

On the open-set 3DOR benchmarks, DEC with DINOv3 ViT-L/16 achieves mAP of 62.75% (OS-ESB-core), 67.75% (OS-NTU-core), 72.15% (OS-MN40-core), and 70.96% (OS-ABO-core), setting new state-of-the-art across all four datasets. Notably, even the zero-shot DINO baseline (simple mean-pooling, no adaptation) with DINOv3 ViT-L/16 reaches 61.44% mAP on OS-ESB-core, surpassing all previously trained open-set methods. The full DEC framework with DINOv2 ViT-B/14 improves mAP over the prior SOTA DAC (CLIP ViT-B/32) by +3.12% on OS-ESB-core, +2.35% on OS-NTU-core, and +5.22% on OS-MN40-core, using only multi-view images without text at inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEC outperforms all 14 compared baselines across the four open-set 3DOR benchmarks. Against the prior SOTA DAC (CLIP ViT-L/14) [42], DEC (DINOv3 ViT-L/16) gains +4.95% mAP on OS-ESB-core (62.75 vs 57.80), +1.92% on OS-NTU-core (67.75 vs 65.83), +3.17% on OS-MN40-core (72.15 vs 68.98), and +0.22% on OS-ABO-core (70.96 vs 70.74), while using only visual features (no text/MLLM at inference). Against CLIP-AdaM (ViT-L/14) [16], improvements are +2.85% mAP on OS-ESB-core and +8.61% on OS-MN40-core. The multi-modal HGM²R [9] is surpassed by a wide margin (+11.01% mAP on OS-ESB-core, +7.95% on OS-MN40-core). All traditional 3DOR methods (TCL, SDML, CMCL, MMSAE, MCWSA, PROSER, InfoNCE) trail by 10–24% mAP. Zero-shot point-cloud models (OpenShape [23], ULIP-2 [48], Uni3D [54]) are also substantially outperformed; notably, even the unadapted DINO zero-shot baseline exceeds the best trained open-set method on OS-ESB-core. In the adapter comparison (Table 3), CAM outperforms CLIP-Adapter [10] and CLIP-AdaM [16] by +2.08% and +1.97% mAP respectively on OS-MN40-core.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CLIP-based open-set 3DOR line (CLIP-AdaM, DAC) by replacing the contrastive vision-language encoder with the self-supervised DINO backbone as the primary view feature extractor, while retaining CLIP solely as a semantic bridge for virtual feature synthesis. The Chunking and Adapting Module (CAM) extends the simple mean-pooling strategy common in prior multi-view 3DOR work by introducing a divide-and-conquer aggregation of local inter-view relations. The Virtual Feature Synthesis module draws on the OOD-detection literature (VOS) but adapts it to the open-set setting by synthesizing class-specific (rather than monolithic 'unknown') virtual features, grounded in a theoretical argument that CLIP's text-visual alignment preserves semantic geometry isometrically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a frozen DINO with simple mean-pooling already surpasses all CLIP-based and point-cloud-based zero-shot baselines challenges the prevailing assumption that text-aligned representations are necessary for open-set 3D generalization, suggesting self-supervised fine-grained features may be the more critical ingredient. The VFS regularization strategy—transferring textual semantic relations into the DINO visual space—is a lightweight, training-only mechanism that could be adapted to other open-set recognition tasks beyond 3D. Limitations include persistent confusion on symmetric objects (cups vs. vases) and dependence on an ImageNet-derived label lexicon for unseen-class synthesis, leaving open whether the approach scales to truly open-vocabulary settings without a fixed concept pool.
