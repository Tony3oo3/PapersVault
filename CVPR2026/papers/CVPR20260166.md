## Problem

Universal Multimodal Embeddings (UMEs) powered by MLLMs have achieved strong general retrieval performance, yet they largely neglect visual identity discrimination (VisID)—the ability to identify images sharing the same visual identity as a query under a natural language instruction. This gap is critical for instance retrieval, person re-identification, face recognition, and identity-preserving AI-generated content, but existing benchmarks like MMEB include only a single image-to-image subset, leaving VisID unmeasured and unoptimized.

## Input / Output

The model consumes image-text pairs where the image (processed at up to 200,704 pixels / 256 visual tokens) serves as the identity reference and the text (max 768 tokens) specifies the retrieval objective via a task instruction, caption, or both. The output is a single D-dimensional embedding vector used for cosine-similarity matching. For dual-encoder baselines evaluated under the same protocol, Identity Editing tasks require averaging visual and text embeddings since text-only inputs are unavailable.

## Method

VisME builds on Qwen2-VL/Qwen2.5-VL fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 16) and GradCache, trained jointly on MMEB (general multimodal) and the newly introduced MVEB (28 identity-centric datasets, 522K samples) using a unified contrastive loss with learnable temperature. The key training innovation is an identity-aware offline sampling scheme that pre-generates all mini-batches so that each identity appears at most once per batch (preventing false negatives) and is sampled with probability proportional to its instance count, combined with structured hard-negative mining that uses an auxiliary embedding model to select semantically similar but identity-inconsistent pairs from AIGC data. Each mini-batch aggregates M samples from each of K sub-datasets, preserving inter-source diversity while enabling intra-source hard negatives, and the same InfoNCE-style loss governs both semantic and identity tasks with task specificity emerging solely from triplet construction.

## Results

On the MMEB benchmark (general multimodal retrieval), VisME (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves an average Precision@1 of 72.2, marginally outperforming the best baseline B3 (72.0). On the proposed MVEB benchmark (identity-centric tasks), the same model reaches 78.8 average P@1, surpassing the strongest baseline B3-7B (52.7) by approximately 26 points. Even the 2B variant (VisME Qwen2-VL-2B) scores 69.1 on MVEB, exceeding the next-best 2B model VLM2Vec-V2.0 (52.3) by over 16 points. The largest gains appear on Identity Grounding (76.7 vs. 44.9 for LLaVE-7B) and Re-Identification (70.6 vs. 42.0 for LLaVE-7B), confirming that prior UMEs severely underperform on fine-grained identity discrimination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisME dominates all 11 baselines on the MVEB benchmark by a wide margin: the 7B model (78.8) outperforms the best UME baseline B3-7B (52.7) by 26.1 points and the best dual-encoder SigLIP2 (57.1) by 21.7 points. On MMEB, VisME-7B (72.2) edges out B3-7B (72.0) by only 0.2 points, indicating that the identity-aware training does not sacrifice general multimodal retrieval. Among 2B-scale models, VisME (69.1 MVEB) surpasses VLM2Vec-V2.0 (52.3) and B3 (40.1) by 16.8 and 29.0 points respectively, while maintaining competitive MMEB scores (67.6 vs. 68.1 for B3). Notably, dual-encoder models (CLIP, SigLIP2, LLM2CLIP) score 37–47 on MMEB but are relatively competitive on MVEB (48–57), suggesting that the dual-encoder architecture is not inherently unsuited to identity tasks but lacks the instruction-conditioned flexibility of MLLM-based UMEs. The largest single-task gap is on Identity Grounding, where VisME-7B reaches 76.7 versus 44.9 for the best baseline (LLaVE-7B), a 31.8-point margin that highlights a capability entirely absent in prior UMEs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Universal Multimodal Embeddings (UME) paradigm—exemplified by VLM2Vec, GME, UniME, and B3—which fine-tunes MLLMs with contrastive objectives for general semantic alignment. It departs from this line by explicitly introducing Visual Identity Discrimination (VisID) as a first-class capability, unifying previously siloed tasks (instance retrieval, person re-ID, face recognition, identity grounding, identity editing) under a single instruction-conditioned formulation. The training framework builds on standard in-batch contrastive learning but adds an identity-aware offline sampling scheme and structured hard-negative mining, drawing conceptually from domain-specific metric learning (e.g., ArcFace, Lifted Structured Embedding) while operating within the MLLM embedding space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a substantial and previously unquantified gap: all SOTA UMEs that score 65–72 on MMEB collapse to 36–55 on identity-centric tasks, suggesting that general multimodal alignment does not subsume fine-grained identity discrimination. MVEB (28 datasets, 522K samples) provides the first systematic benchmark for this capability and is likely to become a standard evaluation suite for identity-aware retrieval and AIGC identity-preservation research. Open questions include whether the identity-aware sampling strategy transfers to video or 3D modalities, whether the approach can serve as a principled evaluation metric for ID-preserving generative models, and whether the gap persists at larger model scales or with more diverse identity categories.
