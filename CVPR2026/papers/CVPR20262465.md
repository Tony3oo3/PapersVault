## Problem

Existing image-conditioned PBR texture generation methods require strict topological and geometric consistency between the input image and the target 3D mesh, causing generation quality to degrade drastically under non-isometric deformations or garment topology changes (e.g., transferring a skirt texture onto a long-pants mesh). This paper addresses the specific challenge of non-isometric image-based garment texture generation, where the image prompt and target geometry may differ in pose, topology, and geometric shape.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262465/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image prompt (H×W×3, sourced from industrial renders or wild images like DeepFashion2 masked by SAM2) and a target garment mesh as input. The mesh is rendered from 10 viewpoints to produce normal and position maps that serve as geometric constraints for the diffusion model. The output consists of three PBR texture maps in the mesh's UV space: albedo (N×N×3), roughness (N×N×1), and metallic (N×N×1), which are integrated via iterative multi-view baking into a single seamless texture set on the mesh.

## Method

NI-Tex employs a feedforward dual-branch diffusion architecture (guidance + generation branches) built on Stable Diffusion 2.1, connected via Multi-Channel Aligned Attention (MCAA) to spatially align albedo and MR channels. The key training innovation is a 3D Garment Videos dataset constructed from BEDLAM's physically simulated motion sequences, where two randomly sampled frames form condition-supervision pairs to enable cross-pose learning, combined with Nano Banana image editing to create cross-topology training pairs that distill texture identity consistency across garment topology changes. At inference, a ResNet-based Uncertainty Quantification model predicts per-pixel baking artifacts, driving an iterative view-selection and reweighting algorithm that fuses multi-view predictions into complete, artifact-free PBR textures. A switchable multi-channel U-Net allows the MR channel to be deactivated during albedo-only training stages, improving efficiency when image-edited prompts lack consistent material properties.

## Results

On the main quantitative benchmark (10 image prompts × 10 textured objects, 42 random seeds), NI-Tex achieves the best KID of 0.0364 and FID of 237.59, outperforming all four baselines on both metrics. The closest competitor, Meshy 6 Preview, records KID 0.0383 and FID 246.39, while the weakest baseline Paint3D (albedo-only) scores KID 0.0695 and FID 293.45. Qualitative evaluation on 4D-Dress further confirms that NI-Tex maintains consistent garment topology across poses and produces full mesh coverage via its uncertainty-guided iterative baking, whereas coverage-based baking leaves small regions untextured.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NI-Tex outperforms all four baselines on both KID and FID. Against Meshy 6 Preview (the strongest baseline), NI-Tex improves KID by 0.0019 (0.0364 vs. 0.0383) and FID by 8.8 points (237.59 vs. 246.39). Against Hunyuan3D — which serves as the pretrained backbone — NI-Tex reduces KID from 0.0528 to 0.0364 (a 31% relative improvement) and FID from 272.34 to 237.59, demonstrating that the non-isometric training data and iterative baking contribute substantial gains over the base architecture. Hyper3D OmniCraft sits in the middle (KID 0.0471, FID 285.45), while Paint3D, which outputs only albedo without MR maps, is the weakest at KID 0.0695 and FID 293.45. Qualitatively, the paper highlights that existing methods (Hunyuan3D, Meshy) produce distorted or inconsistent textures under topological mismatch, whereas NI-Tex maintains faithful texture transfer even when the image prompt and target mesh differ in pose and garment topology.

## Relation to Prior Work

NI-Tex builds directly on the feedforward dual-branch PBR texture generation architecture of Hunyuan3D 2.1 and the Multi-Channel Aligned Attention module from MaterialMVP, extending them to handle non-isometric image-garment pairs where topological and geometric discrepancies exist. It departs from prior image-based garment texture methods (Cloth2Tex, Garment3DGen, FabricDiffusion) that require canonical-pose alignment or mesh deformation, and from SDS-based PBR approaches (Fantasia3D, DreamMat) that are slow and less controllable. The iterative baking strategy draws on the Active View Selector framework for uncertainty-guided view selection, but applies it specifically to multi-view PBR texture integration on garment meshes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D garment production: existing texture generation tools degrade sharply when the input image and target mesh differ in pose or topology, which is common in real workflows. The publicly released 3D Garment Videos dataset and the use of image editing (Nano Banana) as a data augmentation strategy offer a reusable recipe for building non-isometric training pairs. Limitations include reliance on a specific image editing model, UQ training on simulated errors that may not capture all real-world baking artifacts, and evaluation on only 10 objects with 42 seeds, leaving open questions about scalability to diverse garment categories and whether the iterative baking loop converges reliably at scale.
