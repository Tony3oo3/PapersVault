## Problem

Medical image anomalies are subtle, heterogeneous, and structurally distributed across anatomical hierarchies, unlike the texture-driven, spatially localized defects common in industrial settings. Grad-CAM analysis reveals that discriminative activation maps—which succeed on industrial data—become diffuse and anatomically inconsistent on medical images, exposing the inadequacy of single-stream feature extractors for learning a complete normal manifold. Existing knowledge-distillation anomaly detectors rely on single-teacher, homogeneous feature spaces that cannot capture the complementary global-contextual and local-structural priors needed for reliable medical anomaly separation.

## Input / Output

The method takes 256×256 medical images (chest X-ray, brain MRI, liver CT, retinal OCT) and is trained exclusively on normal samples in an unsupervised manner. At inference, it produces both an image-level anomaly score and a pixel-level localization map; the localization map is computed by aggregating per-pixel cosine similarities between each frozen teacher and its corresponding student (cos(t₁,s₁)+cos(t₂,s₂)), while the image-level score derives from reconstruction errors and feature discrepancies across the dual-student outputs.

## Method

PDD employs a dual-teacher, dual-student reverse distillation architecture. Two frozen encoders—VMamba-Tiny (state-space, global contextual priors) and wide-ResNet50 (convolutional, local structural priors)—process the input in parallel; their intermediate features are fused layer-wise by the InA module (bilinear upsampling + addition), while their deep tail features are geometrically aligned into a shared manifold by the MMU module (1×1 dilated conv → 3×3 conv with GeLU and residual). The unified manifold is distilled into two structurally identical students: Student 1 performs layer-wise MSE distillation from InA features for local consistency, while Student 2 receives skip-projected prior representations through the MPA module (learnable affine MLP) to capture cross-layer dependencies. A diversity loss applies inverted cosine-similarity constraints—penalizing high similarity at low-dimensional layers to encourage representational diversity while enforcing similarity at high-dimensional layers for consistency—preventing both students from collapsing into a single mode. Training optimizes the combined objective L_kr + L_prp + L_div on normal samples only, with the diversity thresholds τ_low and τ_high controlling the low/high layer split.

## Results

On the four single-dataset benchmarks (Table 1), PDD achieves state-of-the-art AUROC of 97.5% on HeadCT (+11.8 pp over Skip-TS), 94.0% on ZhangLab (+2.9 pp over SIMSID), and 96.7% on BrainMRI (+8.5 pp over Skip-TS), while remaining competitive on CheXpert (79.1% vs. SIMSID's 79.7%). On the multi-class Uni-Medical benchmark (Table 2), PDD attains the highest F1_max across all three anatomical categories—96.7% (brain), 70.5% (liver), and 88.9% (retinal)—and the best AP of 92.7% on retinal, surpassing the strongest competitor MambaAD by 3.4 percentage points on mean F1_max (85.4% vs. 82.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDD decisively outperforms all six Table-1 baselines on three of four datasets: it beats Skip-TS (the strongest prior method) by 11.8 pp on HeadCT and 8.5 pp on BrainMRI, and surpasses SIMSID by 2.9 pp on ZhangLab. On CheXpert, PDD (79.1%) trails SIMSID (79.7%) by a narrow 0.6 pp, making it the only dataset where PDD does not claim the top spot. On the Uni-Medical multi-class benchmark, PDD wins F1_max against every competitor in all three categories (brain, liver, retinal), with the largest margin over MambaAD on liver (70.5% vs. 64.7%) and retinal (88.9% vs. 86.6%). However, PDD does not achieve the best AUROC on Uni-Medical: MambaAD leads on brain (94.2% vs. 90.9%) and DeSTSeg leads on liver (69.2% vs. 59.7%), indicating that PDD's advantage is concentrated in localization quality (F1_max, AP) rather than pure ranking (AUROC) on the multi-class setting. Against RD4AD, the method PDD builds upon, the gains are substantial—e.g., 97.5% vs. 74.3% AUROC on HeadCT—confirming that the dual-teacher manifold unification and dual-student diversity strategy contribute most of the improvement over vanilla reverse distillation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PDD extends the reverse knowledge distillation paradigm introduced by RD4AD (Deng & Li, CVPR 2022) from a single-teacher to a dual-teacher setting, replacing the homogeneous teacher-student architecture with heterogeneous backbones (frozen VMamba-Tiny for global sequential priors and frozen Wide-ResNet50 for local convolutional priors). The key conceptual departure is the manifold-matching perspective: the paper argues, supported by Grad-CAM visualizations, that industrial anomaly detection methods relying on localized discriminative activations fail on medical images because anomalies are structural deviations distributed across anatomical hierarchies rather than texture-driven local defects. This motivates the MMU module to geometrically align two heterogeneous feature manifolds into a shared space, and the dual-student design with a diversity loss to prevent representation collapse—neither of which appears in prior single-teacher distillation methods like Skip-TS or CFRG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Grad-CAM diagnostic (clean localized heatmaps on MVTec vs. diffuse anatomically inconsistent maps on BrainMRI/HeadCT) provides a useful, if informal, explanation for why industrial anomaly detection methods systematically underperform on medical data, and the manifold-level framing offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc multi-branch fusion. The 11.8% AUROC gain on HeadCT and consistent improvements across four datasets suggest that complementary backbone priors are not merely helpful but necessary for capturing the full normal anatomy manifold in medical imaging. However, the approach remains limited to 2D cross-sectional images, and the authors acknowledge false positives on clinically irrelevant artifacts (device markers, metallic implants), pointing to the open question of integrating clinical context or artifact-aware priors. Whether the specific backbone pairing (Mamba + ResNet) is optimal or whether other heterogeneous combinations (e.g., ViT + CNN) would yield similar gains remains unexplored.
