## Problem

Monocular 4D mesh reconstruction requires recovering the complete 3D shape and motion of a dynamic object, including invisible surfaces and dense vertex correspondences over time. Prior feed-forward methods only reconstruct visible geometry per frame or between frame pairs, while optimization-based approaches are slow and produce noisy results. The paper asks whether latent 3D generative models, extended with skeletal priors and holistic temporal modeling, can recover accurate complete 4D meshes in a single feed-forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261135/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular RGB video of T frames as input and produces a complete animated 3D mesh: a canonical mesh (N_v vertices, N_f triangular faces) reconstructed from the first frame, plus a dense deformation field that displaces every point on the mesh surface across all T frames, yielding per-frame meshes with consistent vertex identities for tracking. A PBR texture is generated from the first frame and carried through the animation. The deformation field is defined over the entire mesh surface (not just vertices), and the canonical mesh is obtained via an off-the-shelf image-to-3D model (Hunyuan3D 2.1) before the deformation is predicted.

## Method

Mesh4D is a three-stage feed-forward pipeline: (1) an off-the-shelf latent 3D model (Hunyuan3D 2.1) reconstructs the canonical mesh from the first frame; (2) a novel Deformation VAE encodes the full animation sequence into a compact latent code using a transformer with alternating spatial, temporal, and global attention layers, where skeleton information (skinning weights via masked self-attention and bone positions via cross-attention) is injected as privileged training-time supervision to shape the latent space; (3) a flow-matching diffusion model, conditioned on DINOv2 video features and the canonical mesh's spatial embeddings and shape latent, predicts the deformation latent in one shot. Farthest Point Sampling compresses the point cloud before the spatio-temporal attention blocks to keep computation tractable, and 1D RoPE embeddings are applied along the temporal axis. The key design choice is encoding the entire animation holistically rather than per-frame or pairwise, which the ablations show reduces jitter and improves rigid-motion recovery.

## Results

On the proposed Objaverse benchmark, Mesh4D achieves state-of-the-art geometry reconstruction with IoU 0.3731, P2S 0.0287, and Chamfer 0.0273, outperforming all baselines. For tracking (ℓ₂-Corr), Mesh4D scores 0.0384, beating GVFD (0.0514) by 25%. On novel view synthesis, Mesh4D attains PSNR 19.67, SSIM 0.9018, LPIPS 0.1087, and FVD 601.9, leading all methods on per-frame quality and temporal consistency. With aligned canonical shape input, results improve further to IoU 0.3949, Chamfer 0.0243, and FVD 572.7.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mesh4D outperforms all three baselines on every reported geometry and tracking metric. Against HY3D 2.1 (frame-wise inference with shared noise), Mesh4D improves IoU by 21% (0.3731 vs. 0.3071), reduces Chamfer distance by 26% (0.0273 vs. 0.0370), and lowers FVD by 13% (601.9 vs. 692.2), though HY3D edges out on CLIP similarity (0.9174 vs. 0.9141) due to per-frame texture generation. Against L4GM, Mesh4D achieves substantially better geometry (Chamfer 0.0273 vs. 0.0505, a 46% reduction) and NVS quality (PSNR 19.67 vs. 18.07, FVD 601.9 vs. 747.3); L4GM does not support tracking evaluation. Against GVFD, the closest competitor, Mesh4D wins on P2S (0.0287 vs. 0.0345), Chamfer (0.0273 vs. 0.0378), tracking (0.0384 vs. 0.0514, a 25% improvement), and all NVS metrics including FVD (601.9 vs. 905.0, a 33% reduction), confirming that explicit mesh-based deformation with spatio-temporal attention yields more accurate geometry and motion than 3D-Gaussian-based approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mesh4D builds directly on Hunyuan3D 2.1 as a pre-trained static 3D latent reconstruction backbone, extending it from single-image to full-sequence 4D mesh reconstruction. It departs from prior feed-forward 4D methods (MonST3R, Cut3R, Geo4D) that handle only pairwise frames or visible geometry, by encoding the entire deformation sequence into a single compact latent via a spatio-temporal transformer VAE. It also differentiates from concurrent latent-space 4D approaches (GVFD, L4GM, ShapeGen4D) by explicitly modeling dense vertex correspondences and using skeleton information as privileged training signal, rather than focusing primarily on novel-view synthesis quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mesh4D is among the first feed-forward methods to produce a complete animated 3D mesh with dense vertex tracking from a single monocular video, addressing the 'invisible geometry' problem that optimization-based and 3D-GS methods handle poorly. The skeleton-guided VAE design offers a reusable pattern for incorporating structural priors into latent 3D/4D models without requiring them at inference. Key limitations remain: the method cannot represent topological changes, struggles with highly non-rigid objects, and depends on a high-quality canonical mesh from the first frame. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences, objects without skeletal structure, and whether the 50-sequence benchmark is sufficient for robust evaluation.
