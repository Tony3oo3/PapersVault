## Problem

Existing RL-based temporal understanding methods for MLLMs are largely confined to a single task (typically temporal grounding), preventing them from capturing the hierarchical and compositional nature of temporal dependencies across diverse scenarios. This narrow scope limits generalization to broader tasks such as dense grounding, action localization, and time-sensitive question answering, where the correspondence between predicted intervals and ground-truth instances differs fundamentally.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263129/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video frames sampled at 2 FPS (capped at 448 frames) with spatial resolution dynamically scaled to keep visual tokens under 3,584, alongside a task-specific text prompt. Outputs are structured temporal intervals parsed via regular expressions: a single interval for TG, multiple sequential intervals for DTG/TAL, multiple highlight segments for VHD, and a text answer paired with evidence intervals for GVQA. The format reward enforces strict template compliance to ensure machine-parsable outputs across all five tasks.

## Method

TempR1 applies GRPO-based reinforcement learning to Qwen2.5-VL-7B on a 60K-sample multi-task corpus spanning five temporal tasks, with the key innovation being a taxonomy of three interval-instance correspondence types that drives tailored localization rewards. For one-to-one tasks (TG/DTG), the reward is mean IoU across corresponding pairs; for many-to-one tasks (VHD/GVQA), it is IoU between the union of predicted and ground-truth intervals; for many-to-many tasks (TAL), it combines an exponential instance-count penalty with a DP-based optimal matching reward computed as F1 over summed IoU. A format reward from regex matching and an additional classification reward for GVQA complete the composite reward signal. Joint optimization across all five tasks under a single GRPO objective produces synergistic gains, improving both multi-task generalization and individual task performance over single-task RL baselines.

## Results

On Charades-STA (TG), TempR1 achieves 61.4 mIoU and 72.7 R1@0.5, surpassing the best RL baseline VideoChat-R1 (60.8 mIoU) and MUSEG (59.7 mIoU). On QVHighlights (VHD), TempR1 reaches 71.1 mIoU and 79.4 R1@0.5, outperforming the second-best TAR-TVG by 5.2 mIoU. On ActivityNet-v1.3 (TAL), TempR1 achieves 71.0 mF1, exceeding MUSEG by 13.0 points. On ActivityNet DTG, TempR1 attains 59.8 mIoU, matching the best VLP expert HSCNet (59.7) while far surpassing RL-based MLLMs (e.g., VideoChat-R1 at 34.3 mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TempR1 outperforms all RL-based MLLM baselines on QVHighlights VHD by a large margin (71.1 mIoU vs. TAR-TVG 65.9, VideoChat-R1 50.1, MUSEG 33.5), and on ActivityNet DTG it matches the best VLP expert HSCNet (59.8 vs. 59.7 mIoU) while exceeding all RL MLLMs by 25+ mIoU over VideoChat-R1 (34.3). On ActivityNet-v1.3 TAL, TempR1 achieves 71.0 mF1, surpassing MUSEG (58.0) by 13.0 points and VideoChat-R1 (55.6) by 15.4 points. On Charades-STA TG, TempR1 edges out VideoChat-R1 (61.4 vs. 60.8 mIoU) and MUSEG (59.7), though TAR-TVG* with dataset-specific fine-tuning is slightly ahead (61.1). TempR1 does not match Time-R1* on ActivityNet TG (32.2 vs. 55.6 R1@0.5), where the single-task RL method retains an advantage. On NExTGQA GVQA, TempR1 achieves 70.1% answer accuracy (slightly below VideoChat-R1's 70.6%) but improves evidence localization mIoU by 3.1 points (39.2 vs. 36.1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-based temporal understanding line (Time-R1, VideoChat-R1, MUSEG, TAR-TVG) from single-task training—predominantly temporal grounding—to a unified multi-task framework covering five complementary tasks. It builds directly on GRPO from DeepSeekMath for stable group-relative policy optimization, replacing the critic network with intra-group advantage normalization. The key departure from prior RL methods is the structured reward taxonomy: three interval-instance correspondence types (one-to-one, many-to-one, many-to-many) with tailored localization rewards, including a dynamic-programming matching strategy for the many-to-many TAL case that no prior RL-based temporal method employed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated synergy—where joint multi-task RL training improves individual task performance beyond single-task training—provides empirical evidence that diverse temporal structures share transferable reasoning primitives, suggesting a path toward a single MLLM handling the full spectrum of temporal video tasks. Open questions include whether the reward taxonomy generalizes to novel temporal correspondence patterns (e.g., hierarchical or nested events), whether the approach scales beyond 60K samples or to larger base models, and whether the DP matching strategy remains tractable for very long videos with many instances. The reliance on a single 7B base model and one-epoch training limits conclusions about compute scaling.
