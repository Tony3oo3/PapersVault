## Problem

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting talking-head methods prioritize photorealistic rendering and lip-sync fidelity but lack fine-grained, expansive emotional expression editing driven by multi-modal inputs. The core difficulty is the ambiguous audio-to-emotion and emotion-to-expression mapping, which makes it hard to perform accurate, anatomically-grounded emotional manipulation within the dynamic 3D talking-head space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261797/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a subject's multi-view images (resized to 512×512) and a facial template to build a canonical 3DGS rig, then accepts arbitrary speech audio (encoded via 768-dim HuBERT features) to drive animation and a free-form text emotion prompt (e.g., 'the person is smiling') to modulate expressions. The output is a set of dynamic 3D Gaussian Splatting primitives with per-frame position, rotation, opacity, and triplane-fetched color, enabling free-viewpoint rendering of an emotionally expressive talking head.

## Method

The key insight is to mediate between speech/text inputs and Gaussian dynamics through an explicit Action Unit (AU) code space, providing interpretable, anatomically-grounded emotional control. The pipeline has three stages: (1) a canonical Gaussian rig reconstruction using triplane-based color prediction instead of spherical harmonics; (2) an AU-prompt Gaussian Diffusion that first encodes HuBERT audio features into AU codes via a multi-layer Transformer, then uses a diffusion Transformer to predict per-Gaussian position offsets conditioned on those AU codes, followed by separate RotNet and OPCNet decoders for rotation and opacity; (3) a Text-to-AU Emotion Controller that maps a text prompt to a binary AU activation vector and applies an enhancement-suppression transform to amplify or attenuate selected AU dimensions before feeding them back into the diffusion. Training proceeds in four sequential stages (encoder, diffusion, appearance decoders, emotion controller) with a unified geometric loss combining vertex reconstruction, temporal coherence, deformation regularization, and lip fidelity.

## Results

On EmoTalk3D, EmoDiffTalk achieves 25.78 dB PSNR, 0.86 SSIM, 0.12 LPIPS, 3.56 LMD, and 0.36 CPBD, surpassing the closest 3D baseline EmoTalk3D by +4.56 dB PSNR and the strongest 2D baseline Hallo3 by +7.48 dB PSNR. On RenderMe-360, it reaches 21.41 dB PSNR and 6.59 LMD, improving over Hallo3 by +1.28 dB PSNR and −29.4% LMD. In user studies, EmoDiffTalk scores highest in Video Fidelity (4.75/5) and Image Quality (4.50/5) on EmoTalk3D, and in Emotion Control (3.73/5) on RenderMe-360, confirming superior perceptual quality and emotional expressiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EmoDiffTalk outperforms all six baselines on PSNR and SSIM across both datasets. Against the closest 3DGS competitor EmoTalk3D [19], it gains +4.56 dB PSNR on EmoTalk3D and +2.97 dB on RenderMe-360, while also reducing LMD from 3.62 to 3.56 and 9.98 to 6.59 respectively. Versus the strongest 2D video-diffusion baseline Hallo3 [10], EmoDiffTalk leads by +7.48 dB PSNR and −80.5% LMD on EmoTalk3D, and by +1.28 dB PSNR and −29.4% LMD on RenderMe-360, though Hallo3 retains a slight edge in LPIPS (0.14 vs 0.15) and CPBD (0.30 vs 0.26) on RenderMe-360. The 2D methods EAMM [24], SadTalker [68], and EchoMimic [6] trail substantially (PSNR 9.8–13.8 on EmoTalk3D), confirming the advantage of explicit 3D representations for multi-view consistency. In user studies, EmoDiffTalk edges out Hallo3 in Video Fidelity (+0.24) and Image Quality (+0.20) on EmoTalk3D, and in Emotion Control (+0.08) on RenderMe-360, while Hallo3 scores marginally higher in S-V Sync on EmoTalk3D (4.70 vs 4.65) and Image Quality on RenderMe-360 (4.55 vs 4.35). Real3D-Portrait [64] and SadTalker [68] are clearly outperformed on every metric, with PSNR gaps exceeding 12 dB on EmoTalk3D.

## Relation to Prior Work

EmoDiffTalk builds directly on the canonical 3DGS rig reconstruction from EmoTalk3D and the diffusion-based facial animation paradigm from DiffPoseTalk, but departs from both by introducing Action Unit (AU) codes from the FACS system as an explicit, interpretable intermediate representation between multi-modal inputs and the Gaussian diffusion process. Prior emotion-editing approaches either operate in implicit latent spaces (EAMM), use 3DMM coefficients (EmoTalk), or apply text prompts directly to video diffusion (Hallo3); none use AUs as a structured bridge for 3DGS-based avatars. The triplane-based color prediction replaces the spherical harmonics used in EmoTalk3D, and the text-to-AU controller draws on the multimodal control idea from CafeTalk but grounds it in anatomically meaningful AU dimensions rather than holistic style vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes a principled pathway for anatomically-grounded, text-driven emotional editing in 3D Gaussian talking heads, addressing a gap where prior methods lacked fine-grained, interpretable control over expression dynamics. The AU-based formulation enables smooth interpolation and combinatorial blending of expressions that holistic latent-space methods cannot easily support. Key limitations remain: the multi-stage pipeline (HuBERT encoder, diffusion, appearance decoders, text controller) incurs substantial computational overhead, and the activation-suppression text-to-AU controller fails on very exaggerated expressions. Open questions include whether AU-based control can generalize to full-body or multi-person scenarios, and whether the four-stage training can be unified or distilled for real-time deployment.
