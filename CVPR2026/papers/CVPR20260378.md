## Problem

Existing retrieval-augmented Dense Video Captioning methods segment videos using heuristic strategies (uniform sampling, similarity-based clustering) that produce boundaries misaligned with true event transitions, causing the retrieval module to fetch irrelevant captions and the decoder to generate contextually poor descriptions. No prior approach learns frame-level saliency in a supervised manner directly from ground truth event annotations to simultaneously guide both segmentation and caption generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260378/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an untrimmed video (frames sampled at 1 FPS, truncated or zero-padded to a fixed length of T=100) and a transcribed text sequence as input. Spatial embeddings are extracted with a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14, while a temporal transformer encoder produces contextualized frame features. The output is a set of L events, each consisting of temporal boundaries (start, end frame indices) and a corresponding natural language caption, generated by a T5-based decoder that also receives top-p retrieved captions per segment from an external datastore.

## Method

STaRC learns frame-level saliency scores through a highlight detection module supervised by binary labels derived directly from DVC event annotations (frames inside annotated boundaries are labeled 1, others 0), requiring no additional annotation. A parameter-free Sliding-Window Self-Attention module (windows of 8, 32, 64) refines frame features before saliency prediction. The learned saliency is then used in two complementary ways: (1) Saliency-Guided Segmentation and Retrieval (SGSR) performs unbalanced Optimal Transport clustering where saliency acts as both a KL-divergence soft constraint on frame marginals and a bias term in the cost matrix, producing event-aligned segments for caption retrieval; (2) Saliency Prompts (SaliP) project saliency scores through a linear layer and inject them as explicit temporal grounding tokens into the decoder's input sequence. Training combines token-level cross-entropy for captioning with a listwise-softmax saliency loss (scaled by λ), built on Vid2Seq pretrained on 1.8M video-text pairs.

## Results

On YouCook2, STaRC achieves a CIDEr of 80.53, METEOR of 13.86, SODA_c of 10.73, and BLEU_4 of 6.75, outperforming the strongest prior method Sali4Vid (CIDEr 75.80) by +4.73 CIDEr. On ViTT, STaRC reaches CIDEr 56.04 and METEOR 10.49, surpassing Sali4Vid (53.87, 10.05) by +2.17 CIDEr. For event localization, STaRC attains the best F1 of 34.34 and Precision of 37.70 on YouCook2, and the highest Precision of 49.72 on ViTT, though it trails Sali4Vid on ViTT F1 (44.34 vs. 46.58) and Recall (40.02 vs. 44.31).

## Baselines & Comparisons

STaRC outperforms the closest retrieval-augmented competitor Sali4Vid [10] on YouCook2 across CIDEr (+4.73), METEOR (+0.32), and BLEU_4 (+0.40), and on ViTT across CIDEr (+2.17) and METEOR (+0.44), though it slightly trails on ViTT SODA_c (15.01 vs. 15.08) and BLEU_4 (0.89 vs. 0.91). Against HiCM² [13], STaRC leads by +8.69 CIDEr on YouCook2 and +4.75 CIDEr on ViTT. Over the non-retrieval baselines (PDVC, CM², E²DVC, CACMI), STaRC gains over 45 CIDEr points on YouCook2, reflecting the substantial benefit of retrieval augmentation combined with supervised saliency. For localization (Table 2), STaRC achieves the best YouCook2 F1 (34.34) and Precision (37.70), but loses to Sali4Vid on ViTT F1 (44.34 vs. 46.58) and Recall (40.02 vs. 44.31), while still holding the highest ViTT Precision (49.72 vs. 49.10). The ablation (Table 3) confirms both SGSR and SaliP contribute independently (+10.65 and +12.45 CIDEr over the Vid2Seq baseline respectively), with their combination yielding the full +14.24 CIDEr gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

STaRC builds directly on the retrieval-augmented DVC pipeline established by Vid2Seq and Sali4Vid, but departs from their heuristic segmentation strategies (uniform sampling in HiCM2, similarity-based clustering in Sali4Vid) by learning frame-level saliency in a supervised manner from DVC event annotations. The highlight detection module borrows from the moment-retrieval/highlight-detection literature (Um et al., Xiao et al.), while the segmentation mechanism adapts unbalanced Optimal Transport from ASOT for action segmentation. The key conceptual shift is treating saliency as a single supervised signal that simultaneously drives retrieval segmentation (SGSR) and decoder attention (SaliP), rather than the implicit, timestamp-derived saliency used in Sali4Vid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The correlation analysis (Figure 2) providing direct empirical evidence that segment-quality metrics (Recall@0.5, Mean IoU) strongly predict downstream caption quality is a useful diagnostic for the DVC community, suggesting that future improvements should prioritize temporal alignment over purely semantic retrieval. The zero-extra-annotation strategy for deriving highlight labels from existing DVC boundaries is practical and could be adopted by other retrieval-augmented video tasks. However, evaluation is limited to two instructional-video benchmarks (YouCook2, ViTT), leaving open whether supervised saliency generalizes to more diverse domains (sports, surveillance, open-world video) where event boundaries are less regular. The reliance on a frozen CLIP encoder and T5 decoder also constrains how much the framework benefits from future foundation-model advances.
