## Problem

One-stream Transformer-based trackers achieve strong visual object tracking performance but incur heavy computational cost due to quadratic attention over large token sequences, hindering real-time deployment. Existing token pruning methods reduce tokens in the search region, dynamic template, or static template in isolation, overlooking critical inter-component dependencies and yielding suboptimal pruning decisions that degrade spatial consistency and semantic completeness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263132/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The tracker receives a static template (2× expanded target crop from the first frame), a periodically updated dynamic template, and a search region (4× expanded crop from the current frame), all as RGB patches embedded into token sequences. For unified tracking, depth/thermal/event channels are concatenated to form a six-channel input projected into a shared embedding space, and a text description is encoded into a single CLIP-L token; missing modalities are handled by channel duplication or a fixed dummy sentence. The output is a 4-element target bounding box predicted by a tracking head after zero-padding pruned token slots back to their original spatial positions.

## Method

UTPTrack inserts a lightweight Candidate or Template Elimination Module (CTEM) into selected encoder layers of a one-stream Transformer to jointly prune tokens from the search region, dynamic template, and static template. Token importance is computed by reusing the encoder's own attention weights: SR and DT tokens are ranked by their attention similarity to the static template's center token, while ST pruning additionally applies a token type-aware bonus derived from a binary bounding-box mask (patch-level foreground scores) to protect foreground tokens from erroneous removal. For unified tracking, the same attention-based mechanism operates in the shared multimodal embedding space, and a text-guided variant combines the ST center-token attention with the CLIP-L text-token attention to jointly guide pruning, with ablations showing the dynamic template benefits most from language cues. Training uses standard supervised losses (weighted focal + L1 + GIoU, plus task-recognition cross-entropy for unified tracking) without any auxiliary pruning predictor, making the approach architecture-agnostic and requiring no structural modifications to the base tracker.

## Results

On RGB-based tracking (OSTrack₃₈₄), UTPTrack prunes 65.4% of vision tokens and reduces MACs by 31.3% while retaining 99.7% of baseline performance. On unified tracking (SUTrack₃₈₄), it prunes 67.5% of tokens with 28.4% MACs reduction while actually *exceeding* baseline accuracy by 0.5% (100.5%). At the most aggressive controlled budget (35.4% tokens retained, ~104 tokens), UTPTrack-S maintains 99.3% average performance across 10 benchmarks, compared to 98.3% (CE), 92.5% (ToMe), 96.7% (EViT), and 14.7% (DynamicViT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UTPTrack outperforms all four baselines at every compression ratio tested. Against the closest competitor, CE (OSTrack's built-in candidate elimination) [45], UTPTrack leads by 0.5–1.0% average performance at the 64.6% pruning level (99.3% vs. 98.3%), with the gap widening as compression increases. Against ToMe [1], the advantage is substantial: 6.8% at 64.6% pruning (99.3% vs. 92.5%), reflecting ToMe's inability to model cross-component dependencies. Against EViT [24], UTPTrack leads by 2.6% at the same budget (99.3% vs. 96.7%). DynamicViT [34] collapses catastrophically under aggressive pruning, dropping to 14.7% average performance (an 84.6% gap), indicating its learned saliency scores are poorly suited to the structured multi-component layout of tracking. Notably, at moderate pruning (25.6% reduction), UTPTrack-S already matches or exceeds the unpruned baseline on several individual benchmarks (e.g., VOT-RGBD22: 76.5 vs. 75.5; RGBT234: 70.2 vs. 70.0), suggesting that selective token removal acts as a mild regularizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

UTPTrack builds directly on the one-stream tracking paradigm established by OSTrack (Ye et al., ECCV 2022) and the unified multimodal tracking framework of SUTrack (Chen et al., AAAI 2025), both of which process search region, static template, and dynamic template tokens jointly in a single Transformer. It departs from prior token-pruning work in tracking (OSTrack's candidate elimination, ProContEXT) by being the first to jointly prune all three components rather than treating them in isolation, and it extends the pruning mechanism to multimodal and language-guided settings via a text-guided strategy. The attention-guided, token-type-aware design draws on broader ViT token-compression literature (ToMe, EViT, DynamicViT) but repurposes the model's own attention maps as the pruning signal, avoiding auxiliary prediction heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that 65–67% of vision tokens can be removed while preserving (or marginally exceeding) baseline accuracy, the paper provides a practical path toward real-time deployment of one-stream and unified trackers on resource-constrained hardware. The finding that selective pruning acts as a mild regularizer—sharpening attention to salient regions and yielding a small accuracy gain—is a useful insight for the broader token-compression community. Open questions include whether the center-token similarity heuristic generalizes to trackers without a well-defined static template, how the approach behaves under extreme occlusion or rapid appearance change where the ST itself becomes unreliable, and whether the text-guided pruning strategy transfers to richer language interfaces beyond single CLIP tokens.
