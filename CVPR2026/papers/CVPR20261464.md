## Problem

Existing contrastive decoding methods for LVLM hallucination mitigation suffer from two overlooked limitations: they cannot disentangle genuine visual grounding from spurious inter-modality correlations learned from web-scale co-occurrence statistics, and they ignore a systematic positional bias in cross-modal attention (caused by RoPE) that causes hallucinations to concentrate in the head portion of image tokens.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261464/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard LVLM inference: an RGB image (encoded into n patch tokens) and a text prompt (system prompt + query) are fed into models like LLaVA-1.5, InstructBLIP, or Qwen-VL (all with Vicuna-7B). Rather than modifying the input image or text, CMAC intervenes at the self-attention level during autoregressive decoding, producing a calibrated token probability distribution that suppresses hallucinated tokens. The output is a generated text response (yes/no VQA answer or detailed image caption) with no additional parameters or fine-tuning required.

## Method

CMAC comprises two training-free modules applied during inference. The Inter-Modality Decoding (IMD) module identifies the cross-modal segment of the attention logit matrix (text-query rows attending to image-token columns), computes a binary mask selecting attention weights above their mean, and replaces the corresponding value vectors with their dimension-wise mean before computing the attention output. This surgical distortion creates a 'hallucinatory' distribution that captures both spurious inter-modality correlations and unimodal overreliance, which is then subtracted from the original logits via the standard contrastive formula (1+α)l_t − αl̃_t. The Cross-Modal Position Calibration (CMPC) module scales the RoPE position indices of all image tokens by a factor γ=2, shrinking the angular gap between consecutive image tokens in the rotary matrices and thereby reducing the disproportionate attention to later image tokens. CMPC is applied only to the cross-modal segment of attention logits, preserving global positional relations while equalizing attention across the visual sequence.

## Results

On the POPE Popular benchmark (the standard evaluation setting), CMAC achieves 86.0% Accuracy and 85.70 F1 for LLaVA-1.5 under nucleus sampling, outperforming the strongest baseline PAI (83.45% Acc / 84.21 F1) by approximately 2.5 points. On the CHAIR metric for image captioning, CMAC reduces CHAIR_i by 9.6% (55.6→47.0) and CHAIR_s by 5.1% (17.8→12.7) for LLaVA-1.5. On the MME hallucination subset, CMAC improves the count hallucination score by 17.50 points (110.00→135.41) for LLaVA-1.5, demonstrating generalization beyond object-existence hallucination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMAC consistently outperforms all four baselines (VCD, ICD, PAI, OPERA) across all three LVLMs and both decoding strategies on POPE. The strongest competitor is PAI, which CMAC surpasses by 2.55 Acc / 1.49 F1 on LLaVA-1.5 (Popular, sampling) and by 0.94 Acc / 0.83 F1 on Qwen-VL (Popular, greedy). VCD, the most widely cited contrastive decoding method, is outperformed by a larger margin (3.35 Acc on LLaVA-1.5 Popular sampling), confirming that attention-level value masking is more effective than global visual distortion. ICD (instruction contrastive decoding) shows the weakest performance among the four baselines, particularly on InstructBLIP where it underperforms even the baseline under greedy decoding. OPERA, which only reports greedy results, is outperformed by CMAC on all models, with the largest gap on Qwen-VL (5.52 Acc on Popular greedy). On the CHAIR captioning benchmark, CMAC also leads all baselines, reducing CHAIR_i from 55.6 to 47.0 for LLaVA-1.5 (vs. 54.2 for VCD and 54.0 for ICD), and on MME hallucination it achieves the highest total score (612.49 for LLaVA-1.5 vs. 582.50 for ICD).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrastive decoding framework (Li et al. 2022) and its LVLM-specific instantiation VCD (Leng et al. 2024) by shifting the distortion from the input level (visual noise, text masking) to the attention level, specifically masking value vectors associated with high cross-modal attention weights. It departs from prior CD methods by identifying two previously overlooked failure modes—spurious inter-modality correlations and RoPE-induced positional bias in cross-modal attention—and addressing both within a unified training-free framework. The CMPC module, which linearly rescales image token position indices, has no direct precedent in the hallucination-mitigation literature and represents a novel intervention on the positional encoding mechanism itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of CMAC makes it immediately applicable to any LVLM without retraining, and the identification of RoPE-induced positional bias as a hallucination driver provides a useful diagnostic that may inform future architectural designs. However, the method requires two forward passes per token (original + distorted), doubling inference cost, and the position calibration is a heuristic linear scaling whose optimal γ may vary across architectures. Open questions include whether the position bias finding generalizes to non-RoPE positional encodings, whether the approach scales to larger models (all experiments use 7B backbones), and whether the value-masking strategy remains effective as cross-modal attention patterns become more refined in next-generation LVLMs.
