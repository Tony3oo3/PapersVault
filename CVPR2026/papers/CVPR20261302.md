## Problem

Existing visual concept composition methods rely on adapters like LoRA or explicit masks to select concepts, which fail to decouple complex concepts with occlusions, temporal alterations, or non-object attributes such as styles. Moreover, prior works are limited to animating a designated image subject with video motion, without supporting flexible combination of arbitrary attributes (styles, lighting, multiple concepts) from both images and videos into a single coherent output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261302/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes M concept images or videos (each with a corresponding textual prompt) and a designated target prompt describing the desired composition. It performs one-shot training per concept source (2400 iterations per stage on a single RTX 4090), then at inference composes the target prompt by routing concept-related tokens through their respective binders. The output is an 81-frame video generated by the Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B base model conditioned on the composed prompt tokens.

## Method

BiCo attaches a hierarchical binder structure (a global binder plus per-block binders, each a zero-initialized residual MLP) to the cross-attention layers of a DiT-based T2V model, encoding visual concepts into corresponding prompt tokens via one-shot training. A Diversify-and-Absorb Mechanism uses a VLM to generate multiple diverse prompts retaining key concepts, and introduces a learnable absorbent token that captures concept-irrelevant details during training and is discarded at inference. A Temporal Disentanglement Strategy decouples video training into two stages: first training binders on individual frames (spatial concepts only, matching image training), then on full videos with a dual-branch binder (spatial + temporal MLPs fused by a zero-initialized gating module) to inherit spatial knowledge while learning temporal concepts. A two-stage inverted training strategy prioritizes high-noise levels for the global binder before full training, stabilizing optimization.

## Results

On 40 test cases (images and videos from DAVIS and the Internet), BiCo achieves the best scores across all automatic and human-evaluation metrics: CLIP-T 32.66, DINO-I 38.04, and human-rated Overall Quality 4.64/5. Compared to the strongest prior method DualReal, BiCo improves Overall Quality by +54.67% (4.64 vs. 3.00), Concept Preservation by +51.9% (4.71 vs. 3.10), and Motion Quality by +60.4% (4.46 vs. 2.78). BiCo also uniquely supports non-object concept extraction, multiple concepts from a single input, and flexible prompt-controlled composition, capabilities absent in all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiCo outperforms all four baselines on every metric. Against the closest competitor DualReal, BiCo gains +1.06 on CLIP-T, +5.26 on DINO-I, and +1.64 on Overall human rating (4.64 vs. 3.00, a 54.67% relative improvement). DB-LoRA follows the prompt reasonably well but suffers significant visual concept drift from the original inputs, yielding lower DINO-I (27.74) and Concept scores (2.76). DreamVideo and Text-Inv fail to properly combine visual concepts from both image and video sources, producing near-static or incoherent outputs with the lowest Overall scores (1.79 and 2.42, respectively). Beyond quantitative gains, BiCo uniquely handles non-object concepts (e.g., style, lighting), extracts multiple concepts from a single input, and allows arbitrary image/video input combinations via prompt manipulation—functionalities that none of the four baselines support.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiCo builds on the Wan2.1 DiT-based T2V model and its cross-attention conditioning mechanism, but departs fundamentally from the dominant LoRA/adapter paradigm for video personalization (DreamVideo, DualReal, Grid-LoRA) by encoding visual concepts directly into prompt tokens via a lightweight hierarchical binder rather than learning separate low-rank adapters per concept. It also extends image-domain concept composition work (BreakA-Scene, TokenVerse) to the video setting, while addressing the temporal heterogeneity between image and video concepts through a two-stage disentanglement strategy that has no direct analogue in prior personalization methods. The use of a VLM to diversify prompts and an absorbent token to suppress irrelevant details is a novel training-time mechanism not seen in earlier concept-binding approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BiCo offers a practical one-shot pipeline for creators to compose arbitrary visual concepts (including non-object attributes like style and lighting) from mixed image and video sources, a flexibility that prior methods restricted to subject-plus-motion pairing could not provide. The paper acknowledges that all prompt tokens are treated equally during composition, ignoring the uneven importance of subject/motion tokens versus function words, which limits fine-grained control. Generalization beyond the single Wan2.1-1.3B base model and to longer or higher-resolution videos remains untested, and the evaluation on 40 test cases with a single human-evaluation cohort, while thorough in design, leaves open questions about robustness at scale.
