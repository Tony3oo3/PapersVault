## Problem

Deterministic top-K routing in Mixture-of-Experts architectures restricts exploration of diverse expert combinations, causing the model to overfit to a small subset of experts. While heuristic perturbations (Gaussian noise, multinomial sampling) add stochasticity, none explicitly optimize the expert selection policy itself, leaving the problem of learning an optimal routing strategy largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model accepts up to 8 images or 8 video frames paired with a natural-language question and produces a text answer (e.g., a multiple-choice letter). During training, stochasticity is introduced exclusively in the expert selection process via multinomial sampling over gating scores, while language generation uses greedy decoding; at inference, expert selection reverts to deterministic top-K. The MoE variant of InternVL3.5-1B activates K=2 experts from N=8 per layer (1.3B of 2.9B parameters), and the CLIP-MoE variant activates 2 of 4 experts (0.7B of 1.2B).

## Method

MoE-GRPO formulates expert selection as a sequential decision-making problem where each action is choosing K experts for a token at a specific layer, and optimizes the routing policy using Group Relative Policy Optimization with G=8 stochastic rollouts per input. The training objective combines two sub-losses: Token-GRPO, which reinforces high-reward token generations, and Gate-GRPO, which provides dense layer-wise supervision to the gating networks by computing importance ratios over expert-selection probabilities. A modality-aware router guidance mechanism computes vision- and text-awareness scores for each expert based on top-K activation frequency and deactivates the bottom 25% of experts per modality, constraining exploration to modality-relevant experts. The reward is binary accuracy (1/0), and no KL regularization or reference policy is used since gating networks are trained from scratch without a supervised pre-training stage.

## Results

On multi-modal understanding benchmarks (Table 1), MoE-GRPO (1.3B activated / 2.9B total params) achieves 56.0% average accuracy across 9 image and video benchmarks, outperforming deterministic fine-tuning (Det-FT, 54.0%), stochastic multinomial sampling (Stoch-FT-Multi, 53.7%), and Gaussian-noise perturbation (Stoch-FT-Noise, 54.3%) by 2.0%, 2.3%, and 1.7% respectively. On cross-dataset evaluation with CLIP-MoE (Table 2), MoE-GRPO reaches 71.9% average over 10 target datasets, surpassing Det-FT (68.8%) by 3.1%. On domain generalization (Table 3), MoE-GRPO achieves 67.5% average across four out-of-domain ImageNet variants, exceeding both CLIP-MoE (63.4%) and CLIP-MoE+Det-FT (66.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three MoE fine-tuning baselines, MoE-GRPO wins on 7 of 9 benchmarks: it outperforms Det-FT by up to 4.5 points (MLVU: 53.1 vs 48.6) and 3.0 points (MMT-Bench: 54.8 vs 51.8), Stoch-FT-Multi by up to 3.8 points (MLVU: 53.1 vs 50.3), and Stoch-FT-Noise by up to 2.0 points (MMT-Bench: 54.8 vs 52.0). On cross-dataset evaluation (Table 2), MoE-GRPO beats Det-FT on 9 of 10 target datasets (e.g., StanfordCars 75.5 vs 66.1, Flowers102 75.9 vs 68.6) and also surpasses the un-fine-tuned CLIP-MoE baseline (71.9 vs 70.8 avg), whereas Det-FT degrades below CLIP-MoE (68.8 vs 70.8). On domain generalization (Table 3), MoE-GRPO consistently outperforms both CLIP-MoE and Det-FT across all four out-of-domain splits (e.g., ImageNetV2: 76.9 vs 72.6 vs 75.0). Against alternative routing strategies (Table 7), MoE-GRPO exceeds Expert Choice routing by 1.0% and Optimal Transport (Sinkhorn) routing by 1.3% in average accuracy, and is complementary to the Switch Transformer load-balancing loss, yielding a further 0.9% gain when combined. Compared to dense VLMs of similar scale (e.g., InternVL2.5-1B), MoE-GRPO trades some absolute scores (e.g., MMBench 77.5 vs 70.7) but activates only 1.3B of 2.9B parameters, and notably surpasses the dense model on MMBench, MMT-Bench, and MVBench.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts GRPO (originally designed for LLM reasoning via chain-of-thought optimization) to the fundamentally different problem of optimizing expert routing in MoE-based VLMs, formulating sequential expert selection as a policy optimization task. It departs from the standard deterministic top-K routing paradigm and its stochastic variants (Gaussian noise in V-MoE, multinomial sampling) by introducing explicit reward-driven policy learning over expert combinations. The modality-aware router guidance mechanism is a novel contribution that conditions the RL exploration space on modality-specific expert activation statistics, a form of structured prior not present in prior MoE routing work (Expert Choice, Optimal Transport/Sinkhorn).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that RL-based expert routing optimization consistently outperforms both deterministic and heuristic stochastic routing, with the clearest gains in cross-dataset and domain generalization settings (3–4% over Det-FT), suggesting that diverse expert utilization mitigates overfitting to a small expert subset. A notable open question is scalability: all experiments use 1B–3B parameter models with 8 experts per layer, and it remains unclear whether the RL training cost (25K steps, ~1 day on 4 GPUs) and the modality-aware guidance remain effective at the 70B+ scale where MoE is most impactful. The finding that Gate-GRPO alone substantially degrades performance while Token-GRPO alone is nearly as good raises the question of whether the gate-level objective is truly necessary or merely a useful regularizer at this scale.
