## Problem

Existing CLIP-based zero-shot 3D anomaly detection methods project point clouds into 2D images but suffer from two limitations: the 3D-to-2D projection is lossy, causing the model to learn visual proxies rather than true geometric anomaly structure, and reliance on a single 2D modality (rendered or depth) provides an incomplete view of the anomaly. This paper addresses both gaps by injecting explicit 3D geometric priors into text prompts and synergistically fusing complementary rendered and depth representations.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (n×3 XYZ coordinates) as input, which is projected into 9 multi-view rendered images and corresponding depth maps at 336×336 resolution using Open3D. It produces an object-level anomaly probability (mean of per-view softmax scores) and a point-wise anomaly score map obtained by back-projecting 2D score maps with occlusion masks. An optional multimodal variant additionally fuses RGB images for surface-level defects like scratches and discolorations.

## Method

GS-CLIP employs a two-stage training strategy. In Stage 1, a frozen ViT vision encoder is paired with a trainable text prompt generator: a pre-trained PointNet++ extracts global shape features projected into a shape prompt, while a Geometric Defect Distillation Module (GDDM) computes per-point outlier scores against a learnable normal prototype memory bank, selects top-k outlier features, and aggregates them via self-attention into a defect prompt that is asymmetrically appended to the anomaly text prompt. In Stage 2, a dual-stream visual encoder processes rendered images through the frozen ViT and depth maps through a LoRA-adapted ViT branch (LoRA applied only to MLP layers), and a Synergistic Refinement Module fuses global and local features from both streams using bidirectional multiplicative attention followed by an MLP. Training uses BCE classification loss, combined Dice+Focal segmentation loss on both 2D and 3D score maps, and a cross-view consistency loss that penalizes deviation of per-view global features from their mean.

## Results

Under the one-vs-rest setting, GS-CLIP achieves the best performance across all four datasets: 83.6% O-AUROC and 96.5% O-AP on MVTec3D-AD, 71.5% O-AUROC on Eyecandies, 76.4% O-AUROC on Real3D-AD, and 84.1% O-AUROC on Anomaly-ShapeNet. At the point level, it reaches 96.3% P-AUROC and 86.4% P-PRO on MVTec3D-AD. In the more challenging cross-dataset setting (trained on a single MVTec3D-AD category, tested on others), GS-CLIP still leads all baselines, e.g., 70.3% O-AUROC and 92.9% P-AUROC on Eyecandies, with only a small performance drop relative to the one-vs-rest setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GS-CLIP outperforms all five baselines on every dataset and every metric under both one-vs-rest and cross-dataset settings. Against the closest competitor PointAD (NeurIPS'24, rendered-image-only), GS-CLIP gains an average of +1.8% O-AUROC, +1.6% O-AP, and +2.5% P-PRO, with the largest margin on MVTec3D-AD (83.6 vs. 82.0 O-AUROC; 86.4 vs. 84.4 P-PRO). Against MVP-PCLIP (depth-map-only), the advantage is even larger on Eyecandies (+2.2% O-AUROC, +6.0% P-PRO), confirming that neither single modality suffices. The 2D SOTA method AA-CLIP, when naively adapted via multi-view projection, lags GS-CLIP by 9.4% O-AUROC on MVTec3D-AD and 10.8% P-PRO, highlighting the gap between 2D and 3D anomaly semantics. The non-CLIP method 3DzAL (WACV'25) trails by a wide margin (e.g., 53.1 vs. 83.6 O-AUROC on MVTec3D-AD), suggesting that its pseudo-anomaly training signal is less effective than CLIP's learned visual-language alignment. Vanilla CLIP, without any 3D-specific adaptation, performs worst among the CLIP-based methods, underscoring the necessity of both geometry-aware prompts and synergistic view fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based multi-view projection paradigm for zero-shot 3D anomaly detection established by PointAD (NeurIPS 2024) and MVP-PCLIP (arXiv 2024), which both project 3D point clouds into 2D images and back-project anomaly scores. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) it injects 3D geometric priors (global shape context and local defect descriptions via a Geometric Defect Distillation Module) into the text prompt, rather than relying on generic or purely learnable prompts; and (2) it processes rendered and depth images in parallel through a dual-stream architecture with LoRA-adapted depth encoding and a Synergistic Refinement Module, instead of committing to a single 2D modality as PointAD (rendered) or MVP-PCLIP (depth) do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Zero-shot 3D anomaly detection addresses a practical industrial need where target-category data is unavailable due to privacy or cost constraints, and this paper provides a consistent (if modest) improvement over the current SOTA across four datasets, including cross-dataset generalization. The geometry-aware prompt mechanism offers a principled way to bridge the 3D-to-2D information gap that plagues all projection-based methods. However, the approach still fundamentally relies on 2D projection rather than native 3D representations, and the authors themselves identify exploring direct 3D-native encoders and richer multimodal fusion as important open directions. The inference cost (0.51 s/image, ~5.9 GB memory) is notably higher than baselines, which may limit deployment in latency-sensitive industrial pipelines.
