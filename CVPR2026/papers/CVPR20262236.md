## Problem

Existing video retake generation methods assume fixed-length inputs with minimal camera motion, causing rapid degradation on variable-length videos with dynamic camera trajectories. The core challenge is seamlessly integrating camera control signals with positional encodings so that multi-view geometry is preserved across both the input and target videos at arbitrary lengths, without relying on explicit per-frame warping that propagates geometric estimation errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262236/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary-length input video (tested from a few dozen to ~241 frames at 480×832 resolution), its camera poses (from the training dataset or estimated at inference via ViPE), and a target camera trajectory as Plücker ray tokens. It produces a video retake that follows the target trajectory while preserving the input scene content. Unlike prior methods that require fixed frame counts (e.g., 14, 49, or 81 frames), ReDirector handles any input length through shared 3D RoPE, and the output length matches the target trajectory length rather than being constrained to the input length.

## Method

ReDirector fine-tunes the Wan-I2V-CamCtrl diffusion transformer into a video-to-video model by updating only self-attention layers under a rectified flow / conditional flow matching objective. The first key correction is applying a shared 3D RoPE to both input and target video tokens, aligning their spatiotemporal positions and preserving length-agnostic relative encoding. The second contribution, Rotary Camera Encoding (RoCE), injects camera pose information (from Plücker rays) as a learnable SO(2) phase shift into the spatial axes of RoPE, initialized at zero and gradually learned during fine-tuning, so that attention becomes sensitive to relative camera poses between views. A third component, geometry-aware attention, applies the inverse RoCE phase shift to values before attention weighting and the forward shift after aggregation, enabling the model to disentangle dynamic objects from static backgrounds. Training uses identity-retake pairs and time-reversed video augmentation to strengthen multi-view alignment and viewpoint diversity.

## Results

On the DAVIS dataset (500 test cases, variable-length inputs), ReDirector achieves the best geometric consistency (Dyn-MEt3R 0.8477, MEt3R 0.3073) and camera accuracy (TransErr 0.0165, RotErr 1.666), outperforming the strongest baseline ReCamMaster by 7.9% on Dyn-MEt3R, 6.1% on MEt3R, 43.5% on TransErr, and 29% on RotErr. On the iPhone dataset for out-of-distribution generalization, ReDirector achieves PSNR 11.85 / LPIPS 0.611 at 241 frames, surpassing the best baseline (CogNVS: PSNR 10.81 / LPIPS 0.720) by over 1 dB in PSNR and 0.11 in LPIPS, with consistent gains as video length increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReDirector outperforms all four baselines on every geometric consistency and camera accuracy metric. Against the strongest implicit baseline ReCamMaster [4], ReDirector improves Dyn-MEt3R from 0.7857 to 0.8477 (+7.9%), MEt3R from 0.3472 to 0.3073 (−11.5%), TransErr from 0.0292 to 0.0165 (−43.5%), and RotErr from 2.347 to 1.666 (−29%). Against the explicit warping-based methods TrajectoryCrafter [72] and CogNVS [7], the gains are even larger: Dyn-MEt3R improves by 15.5% and 23.9% respectively, while RotErr drops from 9.115 and 10.878 to 1.666. On visual quality, ReDirector leads in Aesthetic Quality (0.5149) and Imaging Quality (0.6668) but trails ReCamMaster slightly on Background Consistency (0.9171 vs 0.9212), Temporal Flickering (0.9548 vs 0.9673), and Motion Smoothness (0.9867 vs 0.9881), which the authors attribute to ReDirector exploring larger scene scales under the same camera motion. On the iPhone dataset for out-of-distribution generalization, ReDirector is the only method that improves with increasing video length (PSNR rises from 10.82 at 81f to 11.85 at 241f), while all baselines plateau or degrade.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReDirector extends the implicit video retake generation line (ReCamMaster, GCD) by correcting what the authors identify as a fundamental design flaw: prior methods apply absolute or partial positional encodings to the input video, forcing fixed-length assumptions. The paper builds on the Wan-I2V-CamCtrl backbone and ReCamMaster's training recipe (self-attention-only fine-tuning with rectified flow), but introduces two key departures: a shared 3D RoPE across input and target videos for length-agnostic alignment, and RoCE, a camera-conditioned RoPE phase shift derived from Plücker ray tokens. The geometry-aware attention component adapts the GTA mechanism from Miyato et al. to the fine-tuning setting, replacing explicit geometric transforms in attention with SO(2) phase shifts on the value path.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to retake arbitrarily long videos with dynamic camera motion addresses a practical gap in production workflows where fixed-length, static-camera assumptions break down. The RoCE mechanism—injecting camera pose as a learnable phase shift into RoPE rather than as additive conditioning—is a general design pattern that could transfer to other camera-controlled generation tasks beyond retakes. Open questions include whether the approach scales to even longer sequences (beyond ~241 frames), whether it handles multi-object dynamic scenes with complex occlusions, and whether the reliance on estimated camera poses (ViPE) at inference introduces a bottleneck for real-time applications.
