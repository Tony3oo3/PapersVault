## Problem

Existing 3D shape anomaly detection methods rely primarily on local point representations or reconstruction errors, making them inadequate for large-scale structural anomalies such as planar displacements and angular misalignments common in industrial manufacturing. They also struggle to generalize across diverse anomaly types and scales, and are sensitive to noisy or incomplete local points during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261198/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud (10,000 uniformly sampled points, normalized to canonical space and voxelized into 256³ grids) and produces per-point anomaly scores in [0,1] along with 3D anomaly offset vectors. During training, both a normal shape S and a pseudo-anomalous shape S̃ (generated via negative augmentation) are consumed; during inference, only the test shape is provided while normal patch references are retrieved from a pre-cached, position-invariant codebook. Multi-scale patches are extracted at three granularities (e.g., 8, 32, 64 points per patch) to capture anomalies at varying spatial scales.

## Method

The core contribution is a hierarchical point-patch fusion architecture built on a pre-trained Minkowski 3D U-Net backbone. An adaptive multi-scale patchification module (FPS-based) extracts patches at three scales, whose translation-invariant features (computed from relative point distributions) are stored in a lightweight patch codebook with thresholded similarity merging to suppress redundancy. At inference, test patch features query the codebook via cosine similarity, and the best-matching scale is selected by argmax over summed diagonal similarities. A RoPE-enhanced multi-head cross-attention then fuses retrieved normal patch embeddings with local point features, followed by a gated patch-score modulation that adaptively scales and shifts point features based on the normal-anomalous patch discrepancy. Training uses negative augmentation (Gaussian displacements, sine-modulated normals, random holes, planar cut-offs) and a composite loss combining L1 offset regression, cosine direction alignment, and binary cross-entropy on a deviation sign mask.

## Results

On Real3D-AD, the proposed method achieves 84.2% object-level AUC-ROC, outperforming the second-best ISMP (76.7%) by 7.5 percentage points and PO3AD (76.5%) by 7.7 points. On Anomaly-ShapeNet (40 classes), it reaches 87.6% average object-level AUC-ROC versus 83.9% for PO3AD and 74.9% for R3D-AD. On the newly introduced Industry dataset with planar and angular displacement defects, the method achieves 75.6% point-level AUC-ROC, exceeding PO3AD (18.9%) by over 50 points and ISMP (51.8%) by 23.8 points, while R3D-AD fails on most samples (20.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all seven baselines on average across all three datasets. On Real3D-AD, it leads ISMP (76.7%) and PO3AD (76.5%) by 7.5 and 7.7 points respectively, though it trails ISMP on Diamond (90.1 vs 94.8) and Fish (92.4 vs 94.5), and Group3AD on Fish (92.4 vs 97.6). On Anomaly-ShapeNet, it surpasses PO3AD (83.9%) by 3.7 points on average, with notable gains on classes like tap0 (92.4 vs 68.1) and vase0 (89.5 vs 85.8), but slightly underperforms PO3AD on eraser0 (94.0 vs 99.5) and bottle0 (87.9 vs 90.0). On the Industry dataset, the advantage is most dramatic: the method achieves 75.6% point-level AUC-ROC versus 51.8% for ISMP, 20.0% for R3D-AD, and 18.9% for PO3AD, with R3D-AD failing entirely on 6 of 8 components. The gap over reconstruction-based methods (R3D-AD, IMRNet) is particularly large on structural anomalies like planar displacements and angular misalignments, where local point representations are insufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the point-offset regression paradigm of PO3AD by introducing a hierarchical patch-level reference mechanism: instead of relying solely on local point geometry, it caches multi-scale patch features from normal shapes into a position-invariant codebook and retrieves them at inference to guide anomaly scoring. The negative augmentation strategy (Gaussian-kernel displacements along local normals) is inherited from Real3D-AD, while the RoPE-enhanced cross-attention for fusing patch and point features draws on rotary position embeddings from the NLP literature. Conceptually, the work bridges memory-bank-based anomaly detection (storing normal patterns for OOD retrieval) with multi-scale geometric decomposition, departing from both reconstruction-based methods (R3D-AD, DRAEM-A) that are noise-sensitive and point-only methods (PO3AD, 3DKeyAD) that miss large structural displacements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in 3D anomaly detection: large-scale structural defects (planar shifts, gear-angle misalignments) that are common in manufacturing but underrepresented in public benchmarks. The new industrial test set, while small (8 components, one instance per class), provides a much-needed evaluation axis for global geometric errors. Key limitations include sensitivity to sampling randomness, the very small industrial test set limiting statistical confidence, and the fact that only PO3AD could be evaluated on the new dataset due to pre-trained weight availability. Open questions include whether the patch codebook approach scales to open-vocabulary industrial catalogs and whether topological or semantic part cues can replace the current FPS-based patchification for more consistent decomposition.
