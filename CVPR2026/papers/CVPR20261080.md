## Problem

Existing deep image watermarking methods achieve robustness against diffusion-based image editing but degrade significantly when a watermarked image is fed into an image-to-video (I2V) generator, because video synthesis weakens the watermark signal across frequencies and introduces temporally varying subpixel misalignments that make per-frame recovery intrinsically difficult. No prior method explicitly trains for robustness under the I2V transformation while preserving both first-frame and late-frame bit accuracy.

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a 512×512 RGB image and a k-bit binary message (k=100 in main experiments, reshaped into a √k × √k grid) and produces a watermarked image of the same resolution. The decoder accepts any single frame (original watermarked image, edited image, or a flow-warped video frame) and outputs k per-bit probabilities via sigmoid, from which the watermark is recovered by thresholding at 0.5. Evaluation is performed on 500 real-world images from UltraEdit, with videos generated by SVD-XT (U-Net) and CogVideoX (DiT) at 512×512.

## Method

WaTeRFlow couples three mechanisms to train the encoder-decoder for I2V robustness. First, the Flow-guided Unified Synthesis Engine (FUSE) is inserted between encoder and decoder during training: it applies InstructPix2Pix instruction-driven edits and AnimateLCM fast video diffusion (2–4 steps, no CFG) to the watermarked image, then warps each generated frame back to the first frame using RAFT optical flow, exposing the network to realistic I2V distortions at low compute cost. Second, a Temporal Consistency Loss (TCL) penalizes L2 differences between decoder logit vectors on adjacent warped frames, stabilizing per-frame predictions against residual subpixel drift. Third, a CLIP-based semantic preservation loss keeps the watermarked image's embedding close to the original, ensuring the conditioning signal (CLIP image embedding used as attention keys/values in SVD) remains intact so the first generated frame stays close to the watermarked input. The encoder is a message-conditioned U-Net with a JND-derived scale map and PatchGAN adversarial training for imperceptibility; the decoder is a stride-downsampling residual CNN. All FUSE components remain frozen during training, and the full system is optimized end-to-end for 20,000 steps on a single A100.

## Results

On SVD-XT (U-Net-based I2V), WaTeRFlow achieves 84.96% average per-frame bit accuracy and 96.93% first-frame bit accuracy with 100-bit capacity, outperforming the strongest baseline VINE (73.92% avg, 99.99% clean) by over 11 points on average. Under pre-I2V distortions such as image editing, H.264 compression, and Gaussian blur, WaTeRFlow maintains 80.20–85.78% bit accuracy, consistently leading all baselines by 8–20 points. On the DiT-based CogVideoX model, WaTeRFlow also attains the highest average bit accuracy across frames. In terms of imperceptibility, WaTeRFlow achieves PSNR 38.83 dB, SSIM 0.9902, and LPIPS 0.0291 on watermarked images, competitive with or better than all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WaTeRFlow outperforms all four baselines across every distortion scenario in Table 1. Against VINE (the strongest baseline at 73.92% avg I2V accuracy), WaTeRFlow gains +11.04 points on clean I2V, +13.64 points under image editing, and +8.12 points under Gaussian blur. Against TrustMark (73.76% avg), the gap is similar (+11.20 avg), with a particularly large margin under regeneration (81.98% vs 53.48%, a +28.5-point gap). Robust-Wide (63.40%) and WAM (63.87%, limited to 32 bits) trail by roughly 21 points on average I2V accuracy. Notably, WaTeRFlow also achieves the best watermarked-image quality (PSNR 38.83, LPIPS 0.0291) among methods with 100-bit capacity, while TrustMark achieves slightly higher PSNR (40.33) but lower bit accuracy (73.76%), indicating WaTeRFlow offers a superior robustness–imperceptibility trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

WaTeRFlow extends the encoder-decoder watermarking paradigm established by HiDDeN and its successors by inserting a unified synthesis engine (FUSE) between encoder and decoder that simultaneously exposes the system to instruction-driven image edits (following Robust-Wide's use of InstructPix2Pix) and fast video diffusion (via AnimateLCM), a combination no prior method has attempted. The key architectural departure from VINE and other I2V-aware baselines is the use of optical-flow warping (RAFT) to align generated frames to a reference frame, coupled with a Temporal Consistency Loss that regularizes adjacent-frame decoder outputs—addressing the temporally varying subpixel misalignments that I2V introduces. A CLIP-based semantic preservation loss further distinguishes the method by explicitly protecting the conditioning signal that diffusion-based I2V models consume, a mechanism not exploited by prior watermarking work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As I2V models move from short clips to multisecond coherent scenes used in content creation, world-modeling, and simulation, the ability to verify provenance from generated video frames becomes a practical necessity that current image watermarking methods cannot meet. WaTeRFlow demonstrates that robustness transfers across architectures (U-Net SVD and DiT CogVideoX) and survives pre-I2V distortions (editing, JPEG, noise) and post-I2V compression (H.264), making it a credible tool for real-world copyright verification. Open questions remain: the method is only validated on two I2V models, adversarial watermark removal is explicitly out of scope, and generalization to generators that substantially alter the conditioning frame or use fundamentally different synthesis principles is untested.
