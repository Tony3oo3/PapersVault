## Problem

Existing multimedia manipulation detection methods rely on rule-based text edits and semantically misaligned image-text pairs, making them easily detectable and failing to capture the emerging threat of MLLM-generated narratives that are fluent, contextually coherent, and semantically aligned with manipulated images. This paper addresses both the underestimation of MLLM-driven deception risk and the unrealistic misalignment artifacts in current benchmarks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263804/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB image (potentially face-swapped or face-attribute-edited) together with its paired news article text, processed as a concatenated sequence of image embeddings, learnable artifact tokens, and text embeddings. Output is generated as a unified autoregressive text string that simultaneously encodes the fake/authentic binary label, the manipulation type (Face Swap, Face Attribute, or Text Fabrication), and the bounding box coordinates of the tampered region, all in a single coherent textual response rather than separate classification heads.

## Method

AMD is built on Florence-2 and introduces a learnable Artifact Token sequence inserted between image and text embeddings to serve as a forensic memory. During training, a frozen pre-perception encoder processes the full input, and the artifact token is optimized via a binary detection loss (APE) to accumulate manipulation traces while original image/text embeddings are restored to preserve the MLLM's pretrained knowledge. A Manipulation-Oriented Reasoning module then performs grounding by cross-attending the artifact token to visual features (VAA) and a Dual-Branch Manipulation guidance that cross-attends image+artifact features against text features for binary classification. A Token Redundancy Penalty enforces orthogonality and uniform information distribution across artifact token dimensions via Gram-matrix off-diagonal minimization and KL-divergence modulation. The total loss combines APE, DBM, grounding (L1+GIoU), TRP, and language modeling terms, with all auxiliary heads discarded at inference.

## Results

On the MDSM dataset (trained on Guardian, tested cross-domain), AMD achieves 88.18 ACC, 60.25 mAP, and 61.02 mIoU on average across five news domains, outperforming the strongest baseline FKA-Owl (84.12 ACC, 58.13 mAP, 52.20 mIoU) by 4.06 ACC and 8.82 mIoU. On DGM4 (trained on Guardian), AMD reaches 74.47 ACC, 52.91 mAP, and 51.87 mIoU, surpassing HAMMER++ (65.61 ACC, 47.36 mAP, 46.19 mIoU) by nearly 9 ACC points. All zero-shot MLLM baselines (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0, Qwen3-VL-235B, etc.) score below 39 ACC and below 2 mIoU, highlighting the difficulty of the semantically aligned manipulation task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AMD outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric in both the MDSM and DGM4 evaluations. Among trained methods, FKA-Owl is the closest competitor, achieving 84.12 ACC and 52.20 mIoU on MDSM (Guardian-trained), but AMD leads by 4.06 ACC and 8.82 mIoU, with the gap widening on grounding (61.02 vs. 52.20 mIoU). The HAMMER series (HAMMER and HAMMER++) trails AMD by 13–14 ACC and 12–17 mIoU, while ViLT lags further behind at 76.61 ACC and 35.67 mIoU. On DGM4, AMD extends its lead: it beats FKA-Owl by 2.51 ACC and 7.72 mIoU, and HAMMER++ by 8.86 ACC and 5.68 mIoU. All zero-shot MLLM baselines (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0, Qwen3-VL-235B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Yi-VL-34B, DeepSeek-VL2-27B, LLaVA-v1.6-13B) score below 39 ACC and below 2 mIoU on MDSM, confirming that off-the-shelf MLLMs cannot detect semantically aligned manipulations without task-specific training. Notably, AMD achieves this with only 276M parameters versus FKA-Owl's 6,771M, yielding 5.55 p/s training throughput vs. 1.25 p/s.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multimodal manipulation detection line initiated by DGM4/HAMMER, which treated image and text manipulation as independent, rule-based operations producing semantically misaligned pairs. It departs from this paradigm by using MLLMs (Qwen2-VL) to generate contextually coherent deceptive narratives conditioned on manipulated images, closing the gap that MMFakeBench only partially addressed (30% aligned samples, 11k scale). Architecturally, AMD builds on Florence-2's sequence-to-sequence framework rather than the multi-head detection/grounding architectures of HAMMER, introducing an Artifact Token mechanism and auxiliary grounding/classification losses to inject forensic awareness into a unified MLLM pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MDSM dataset (441k samples across five media domains and five manipulation types) is the largest semantically-aligned multimodal manipulation benchmark to date, providing a much-needed resource for training and evaluating detection models against realistic MLLM-crafted disinformation. The finding that MLLM-generated text fabrication is notably harder to detect than image-level manipulations (TF cross-domain AP 57.53 vs. FA 71.37) underscores a growing blind spot in current detection pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond face-centric manipulations to broader editing types (object insertion, scene modification), whether it remains robust as MLLM capabilities improve, and whether the Florence-2-based architecture can scale to video or longer-form content.
