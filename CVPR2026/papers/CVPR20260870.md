## Problem

Zero-shot perspective depth models like Depth Anything generalize well on narrow-FoV images but degrade sharply on panoramas due to equirectangular projection distortions and the absence of spherical geometric awareness in standard ViT operators. Collecting large-scale panoramic RGB-D data is prohibitively expensive, and existing panoramic zero-shot methods either rely on costly real-data supervision or lack explicit rotation-equivariant modeling, limiting their data efficiency and geometric consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260870/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes each panoramic image in two simultaneous representations: the standard equirectangular projection (H×W×3) fed to the frozen ViT backbone via 14×14 patch embedding, and a spherical signal sampled on the Driscoll-Healy grid at bandwidth B=⌊H/8⌋ (2B×2B×3) processed by the spherical CNN. The output is a dense panoramic depth map decoded by a DPT head from a multi-scale feature pyramid that merges upsampled ViT features with SO(3)-projected equivariant features along the channel dimension.

## Method

The SO(3)-Equivariant ViT-Adapter wraps a frozen Depth Anything V2 backbone with trainable modules that inject rotation-equivariant geometric priors without altering the ViT architecture. An Equivariant Spherical Prior Module (ESPM) based on spherical CNNs extracts multi-scale SO(3)-equivariant features via S2Conv and SO3Conv in the spectral domain. The key mechanism is the SO(3) Deformable Cross-Attention Module (SO(3)-DCAM), which lifts deformable attention from 2D Euclidean sampling to the SO(3) rotation group: offset rotations are composed with transport rotations to define sampling locations on the equivariant feature tensor, and a Spherical Contextual Relative Positional Encoding (SCRPE) encodes relative positions via log-map to the Lie algebra with quantized direction and magnitude embeddings. A bidirectional S²–SO(3) feature interaction loop alternates between injecting equivariant cues into ViT features (via SO(3)-DCAM) and feeding depth-aware ViT features back to enrich the equivariant stream (via standard deformable cross-attention and S2Conv lifting). Trained for 30 epochs on only 6,548 synthetic PNVS-hard panoramas with BerHu loss, the adapter achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot sim-to-real performance on Matterport3D and Stanford2D3D without any real panoramic training data.

## Results

On Matterport3D (zero-shot sim-to-real), the proposed SO(3)-Equivariant ViT-Adapter with ViT-L backbone achieves Abs Rel 0.078, RMSE 0.388, and δ₁ 0.906, outperforming the strongest baseline PanDA-L (Abs Rel 0.091, RMSE 0.421, δ₁ 0.881) by 14.3% in Abs Rel and 7.8% in RMSE. On Stanford2D3D, the ViT-L variant reaches Abs Rel 0.060, RMSE 0.295, and δ₁ 0.953, surpassing PanDA-L (Abs Rel 0.073, RMSE 0.335, δ₁ 0.937). Notably, the proposed method trains exclusively on 6.5K synthetic PNVS panoramas, whereas PanDA and DepthAnywhere additionally require 18K unlabeled Structured3D panoramas with pseudo-labels, demonstrating superior data efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all five baselines across both Matterport3D and Stanford2D3D on every reported metric. Against the strongest panoramic baseline PanDA (which additionally uses 18K unlabeled Structured3D panoramas with pseudo-labels), the ViT-L variant improves Abs Rel by 14.3% (0.078 vs. 0.091) and RMSE by 7.8% (0.388 vs. 0.421) on Matterport3D, and by 17.8% in Abs Rel (0.060 vs. 0.073) and 11.9% in RMSE (0.295 vs. 0.335) on Stanford2D3D. Against perspective-only baselines (Marigold, DepthAnything V2), the improvement is even more dramatic: Abs Rel drops from 0.177–0.187 to 0.078, and δ₁ rises from 0.619–0.638 to 0.906. The 360MonoDepth baseline, which fuses multi-perspective MiDaS predictions, shows the worst RMSE (0.749) due to stitching artifacts, while DepthAnywhere, despite using semi-supervised pseudo-labels, still lags behind the proposed method by 49.4% in Abs Rel (0.155 vs. 0.078). The key differentiator is that the proposed method achieves these gains while training on strictly fewer data (only 6.5K synthetic PNVS panoramas, no real or pseudo-labeled panoramas), attributing its advantage to the SO(3)-equivariant inductive bias rather than data volume.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ViT-Adapter framework [7] to panoramic depth estimation by injecting SO(3)-equivariant features from a Spherical CNN-based module [9] into a frozen perspective ViT backbone (Depth Anything V2). It departs from prior panoramic zero-shot methods (360MonoDepth, DepthAnywhere, PanDA) that rely on multi-view projection, semi-supervised distillation with real unlabeled panoramas, or pseudo-labeling, by instead introducing operator-level rotation equivariance through a novel SO(3) deformable cross-attention mechanism that generalizes deformable attention [57] to the spherical manifold. The Spherical Contextual Relative Positional Encoding draws on LieTransformer [21] and EQ-Net [51] for encoding geometric relationships on the sphere, but applies them in a cross-attention setting between equivariant and non-equivariant feature spaces rather than within a single equivariant network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that 6.5K synthetic panoramas suffice to transfer zero-shot depth capability to real indoor scenes, eliminating the need for expensive real panoramic RGB-D collection or large-scale unlabeled real panoramas. The SO(3) equivariance provides inherent rotation robustness (reducing reliance on augmentation), which is valuable for applications like robotics and AR/VR where camera orientation varies. However, evaluation is limited to two indoor benchmarks with only vertical rotations tested; open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor panoramas, larger angular deviations, and whether the equivariance benefit persists at larger model scales or with more diverse scene content.
