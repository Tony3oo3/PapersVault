## Problem

Existing machine unlearning methods apply uniform treatment across all samples and classes, ignoring that shallowly memorized samples are easy to forget while deeply memorized ones are not, and that approximate retained classes suffer disproportionate accuracy drops. Additionally, the inherent conflict between forgetting and retention objectives produces dynamic gradient direction conflicts and single-task domination that fixed trade-off hyperparameters cannot resolve, leading to suboptimal convergence and degraded performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261914/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained classifier (ResNet-18, ResNet-50, or ViT-B/16) along with a forget set (randomly sampled instances or entire classes) and a retain set, and iteratively updates model parameters via gradient steps to approximate a model retrained without the forget set. For random sample unlearning, the forgetting objective is label-free, relying on predictive entropy of the forget samples; for class unlearning, the retain objective is modulated by a learnable class-weighting policy. The output is the updated parameter vector θ_u, evaluated against a retraining gold standard on forget accuracy, retain accuracy, test accuracy, and membership inference attack susceptibility.

## Method

The method combines two mechanisms. First, Adaptive Gradient Reweighting: for random sample unlearning, a bi-level optimization assigns learnable per-sample weights w_i = σ(a_i) that prioritize harder-to-forget instances via predictive entropy; for class unlearning, a min-max adversarial game between model parameters (defender) and a Gumbel-Softmax-parameterized class-weighting policy (attacker) forces the model to concentrate protection on the most vulnerable retain classes. Second, a three-stage multi-stage objective optimization handles the retention-forgetting gradient conflict: (1) Direction Rectification projects each gradient onto the orthogonal complement of the other to eliminate destructive interference; (2) Temporal Stabilization applies an exponential moving average to the rectified gradients to filter high-frequency directional noise; (3) Adaptive Objective Combination dynamically selects a balance factor λ_t at each step by minimizing a cosine-similarity-based conflict metric between the composite update and both task gradients, preventing either objective from dominating the update.

## Results

On 10% random unlearning, the method achieves an Avg Gap of 0.19 (ViT/CIFAR-10) and 6.03 (ResNet-18/CIFAR-100), substantially outperforming the best baseline LoTUS (0.85 and 11.83 respectively). For single-class unlearning on CIFAR-100, it attains an H-Mean of 80.47 versus 78.45 for DELETE, while achieving perfect forgetting (Acc_f = 0.00). At a 50% forget ratio, the method maintains an Avg Gap of 3.07 (ResNet-18/CIFAR-10) and 0.08 (ViT/CIFAR-10), compared to 6.50 and 0.50 for the next-best method LoTUS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 11 baselines across every evaluated setting. The closest competitor for random unlearning is LoTUS [50], which the method surpasses by 4.5× on ViT/CIFAR-10 (0.19 vs 0.85 Avg Gap) and by ~2× on ResNet-18/CIFAR-100 (6.03 vs 11.83). For class unlearning, DELETE [61] is the strongest baseline, but the method achieves a higher H-Mean (80.47 vs 78.45 on CIFAR-100) while matching its perfect forgetting (Acc_f = 0.00). Gradient-based baselines (NegGrad+, SCRUB, SSD) and distillation-based methods (BadT, UNSIR) show substantially larger gaps, particularly on ResNet-18 where their Avg Gap values range from 5.3 to 22.4. Boundary unlearning variants (BShrink, BExpand) and L2Un [7] degrade sharply as the number of forgotten classes increases in multi-class settings, whereas the method's retention accuracy actually improves slightly. In the 50% forget-ratio stress test, most baselines approach failure on ResNet-18 (Avg Gap > 20), while the method maintains 3.07, confirming robustness under extreme forgetting conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the machine unlearning literature by addressing two limitations common to prior methods: uniform treatment of all forget/retain samples and fixed hyperparameter-based gradient balancing. It draws on multi-task learning gradient surgery (PCGrad-style projection) and multi-objective optimization (adaptive loss balancing) to construct a three-stage optimization pipeline, while introducing an adversarial min-max game for class-level retention weighting that has no direct precedent in prior MU work. The instance-level attribution mechanism is conceptually related to influence-function-based unlearning but operates through a learnable per-sample weight rather than explicit influence estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, tunable-free framework for balancing the inherently conflicting forgetting and retention objectives, which is a core practical barrier to deploying MU in privacy-regulated settings (GDPR, etc.). The finding that retention accuracy can *improve* as more classes are forgotten (Table 3) challenges the assumption that unlearning is monotonically destructive. Limitations include evaluation primarily on small-scale datasets (CIFAR-10/100, TinyImageNet) and small forget sets in the medical/face applications; scalability to large-scale models (e.g., LLMs, large ViTs) and the computational overhead of the adversarial class policy remain open questions the authors acknowledge.
