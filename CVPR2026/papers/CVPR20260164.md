## Problem

Existing gaze target estimation methods rely on brittle multi-stage pipelines that require explicit intermediate inputs such as head bounding boxes and pose keypoints, causing detection errors to cascade and fail in unconstrained in-the-wild settings. Additionally, prior approaches lack the flexibility to specify which person to analyze via natural language or visual prompts, limiting their usability and scalability for diverse gaze analysis tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260164/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (H×W) paired with either a free-form text prompt structured around four categories (appearance, location, pose, action) or a visual prompt as a 2D spatial coordinate. It simultaneously outputs a 64×64 gaze target heatmap (probability of the subject gazing at each spatial location), a normalized 4-element head bounding box [x, y, w, h], and a binary in/out-of-frame presence logit. Unlike prior methods, no auxiliary depth maps, pose keypoints, or pre-computed head boxes are required at inference time.

## Method

GazeAnywhere employs frozen DINOv3 (or CLIP) and dino.txt encoders whose outputs are projected via trainable linear layers into a shared embedding dimension, then fused by a trainable transformer detector built from DINOv3 transformer blocks. Two specialized learnable tokens—a head token (initialized from the text [EOS] embedding) and a presence token (initialized from the visual [CLS] embedding plus a learned offset)—are concatenated with the projected patch and text tokens to form the detector input, with 1D and 2D sinusoidal positional encodings. Three dedicated decoders read specific output tokens: a two-layer transposed-convolutional network upsamples visual patch tokens to the 64×64 gaze heatmap, a 3-layer FFN regresses the head box, and a 2-layer FFN produces the presence logit. Training is end-to-end with a joint multi-task loss combining pixel-wise BCE on a Gaussian-smoothed gaze target, focal loss for the binary presence label, and L1 + GIoU loss for head box regression (λ_l1=5, λ_iou=2 following DETR/OWLv2 conventions).

## Results

GazeAnywhere-DINOv3-L achieves state-of-the-art PGE performance across all four benchmarks: AUC 0.958 / AvgL2 0.099 / MinL2 0.050 on GazeFollow-Concept, L2 0.123 / AP 0.879 on VAT-Concept, L2 0.090 / AP 0.902 on ChildPlay-Concept, and AP 0.902 on the out-of-domain Child-SC dataset. The model runs at 96 ms per sample (batch size 1), roughly 12× faster than the best two-stage pipeline (RexSeek-based, 1160–1183 ms). On the OOD Child-SC set, GazeAnywhere outperforms the strongest two-stage baseline (Gaze-LLE + RexSeek, AP 0.846) by 5.6 AP points, demonstrating robust generalization to unseen clinical data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GazeAnywhere-DINOv3-L outperforms all three two-stage gaze models (ViTGaze, Sharingan, Gaze-LLE) when paired with any OVD (GroundingDINO-B, LLMDet-L, OWLv2-L, RexSeek). Against the strongest two-stage baseline (Gaze-LLE + RexSeek), GazeAnywhere wins on GazeFollow-Concept AUC (0.958 vs 0.954), VAT-Concept AP (0.879 vs 0.861), ChildPlay-Concept L2 (0.090 vs 0.172), and Child-SC AP (0.902 vs 0.846), while being marginally worse on VAT-Concept L2 (0.123 vs 0.121). The OVD components themselves (GroundingDINO-B, LLMDet-L, OWLv2-L, RexSeek) all introduce significant error cascades: for example, Sharingan degrades from AUC 0.944 (GT head) to 0.888 (RexSeek) on GazeFollow-Concept, and its VAT-Concept AP drops from 0.852 to 0.601. In the VLM comparison (Table 2), GazeAnywhere-DINOv3-L (870M params) substantially outperforms both Qwen3-VL-8B (AvgL2 0.201, AP 0.651) and Gemini 2.5 Flash (AvgL2 0.216, AP 0.661) in 0-shot gaze prediction, confirming the benefit of a task-specific architecture over general-purpose VLMs. Notably, GazeAnywhere achieves these gains at 96 ms inference time versus 1160–1183 ms for RexSeek-based pipelines, a ~12× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant multi-stage gaze estimation pipeline (person detection → head localization → gaze prediction) by repurposing the promptable open-vocabulary detection paradigm (exemplified by Grounding DINO, OWLv2, SAM) into an end-to-end, concept-conditioned gaze task. Architecturally, it builds directly on DINOv3's transformer blocks for the detector and leverages frozen vision-language encoders (DINOv3, CLIP, dino.txt) as feature extractors, following the trend of using vision foundation models as backbones for human-centric tasks (as in ViTGaze and Gaze-LLE). The key conceptual shift is eliminating the sequential dependency on intermediate bounding boxes or pose keypoints, instead conditioning a single transformer detector on natural-language or coordinate prompts to jointly solve subject identification, presence, and gaze target estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying subject localization and gaze estimation into a single promptable model, this work removes the cascading failure mode of brittle multi-stage pipelines, which is particularly critical in clinical settings (e.g., autism assessment) where detection of children's faces is error-prone. The 120K-sample Gaze-Co dataset and PGE benchmark provide the first standardized evaluation for promptable gaze analysis, filling a gap that no prior gaze dataset addressed. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video sequences with temporal reasoning, how it handles ambiguous or adversarial prompts, and whether the frozen-encoder design limits adaptability to truly novel visual domains beyond the tested OOD Child-SC set.
