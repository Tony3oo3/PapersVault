## Problem

Existing ARC-AGI approaches treat the task as purely textual, representing grids as nested lists and ignoring the visual abstraction that humans naturally employ. A counterintuitive finding is that naively rendering grids as images actually degrades performance, revealing that vision and language have complementary but stage-specific strengths: vision excels at global pattern recognition while text excels at precise element-wise manipulation. The paper asks how to strategically combine these modalities across reasoning stages rather than forcing a single representation throughout.

## Input / Output

The method takes K example input-output matrix pairs (each matrix up to 30×30 with integer cell values 0–9) and a test input matrix, converting them between textual nested-list form and color-coded 2D grid images depending on the reasoning phase. The output is a natural-language transformation rule (e.g., 'rotate each connected component 90° clockwise') followed by the predicted output matrix in textual form. Both modality transformations are invertible, enabling seamless switching between representations within the pipeline.

## Method

The paper proposes two training-free strategies built on modality-aware task decomposition. Vision-Language Synergy Reasoning (VLSR) splits ARC-AGI into two subtasks: rule summarization is performed in the visual modality (color-coded grids enable holistic 2D pattern recognition), while rule application switches to the textual modality (nested lists enable precise element-wise manipulation). Modality-Switch Self-Correction (MSSC) addresses the failure of same-modality self-correction by visualizing the predicted output and using a visual consistency check against the example images; if inconsistency is detected, the model receives targeted feedback and performs another textual inference pass (up to 3 iterations). The approach is extended to fine-tuning by training a vision-language model (Qwen3-VL-8B) for visual rule summarization and a text-only model (Qwen3-8B) for textual rule application on 200k synthetic ARC-Heavy tasks, achieving a 3.5% improvement over text-only fine-tuning.

## Results

On the official ARC-AGI 400-task evaluation set, combining VLSR and MSSC improves text-only baselines by up to 7.25% (Gemini-2.5-Pro: 35.0→42.25), 6.25% (GPT-4o: 8.25→14.5), and 4.5% (o4-mini: 42.25→46.75). Across all four models and three benchmarks (ARC-AGI, BARC-100, Re-ARC), the average improvement over text-only reasoning is 4.33%. In the fine-tuning setting, VL synergy fine-tuning of Qwen3-8B achieves 13.25% on ARC-AGI, surpassing text-only fine-tuning (9.75%) by 3.5% and the closed-source GPT-4o baseline (8.25%) by 5.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against training-free inference-time baselines (Table 3, o4-mini base), VLSR+MSSC achieves 46.75% on ARC-AGI, outperforming ArcMemo-PS (45.25%) by 1.5% and Dynamic Cheatsheet (38.5%) by 8.25%; on Re-ARC it ties ArcMemo-PS at 39.0% while beating Cheatsheet (34.0%) by 5.0%. In the fine-tuning comparison (Table 5), the VL synergy fine-tuned Qwen3-8B (13.25% on ARC-AGI) surpasses the text-only fine-tuned Qwen3-8B (9.75%) by 3.5% and the closed-source GPT-4o (8.25%) by 5.0%, though it remains well below Gemini-2.5-Pro (35.75%) and o4-mini (42.25%). The paper also shows that text-only self-correction (TOSC) stagnates or degrades (e.g., GPT-4o drops from 8.25 to 8.0 in Round 2), whereas MSSC yields consistent monotonic gains across all three models, confirming that modality switching is essential for effective intrinsic self-correction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant ARC-AGI paradigm that treats grid matrices as purely textual nested lists during both training and inference, arguing this contradicts human visual intuition. It builds conceptually on visual-reasoning aids like Visual Sketchpad and ViLaSR, which render intermediate spatial hypotheses as images, but applies this idea specifically to the rule-summarization vs. rule-application decomposition unique to ARC-AGI. The modality-switch self-correction mechanism extends the self-correction literature (Huang et al., Zhang et al.) by showing that switching modalities between generation and verification breaks the confirmation bias that causes text-only self-correction to stagnate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that vision and text are not interchangeable in abstract reasoning but have stage-specific strengths, offering a principled recipe (use vision for global pattern extraction, text for precise execution) that could generalize to other structured-reasoning tasks beyond ARC-AGI. The extension to fine-tuning—where a 3.5% gain lets Qwen3-8B surpass GPT-4o—suggests the modality-aware decomposition is a useful training strategy for small open-source models. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to tasks with continuous or non-grid spatial structure, whether the 3-iteration MSSC loop is optimal, and whether the gains persist as base models improve their native visual grounding.
