## Problem

Existing humanoid control systems either rely on human teleoperation or decouple language understanding from physical execution via modular text-to-motion pipelines, which produce motions that are not physically feasible on real robots. No prior work achieves a fully end-to-end mapping from natural language directly to low-level whole-body motor commands without intermediate motion representations or latent abstractions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262869/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a natural language command (encoded via CLIP) and a multi-scale proprioceptive state history combining 10 timesteps at 50 Hz (short-term) with 10 seconds of states at 4 Hz (long-term), yielding a 29-DoF joint position/velocity, root velocity, angular velocity, and gravity vector per state. It outputs an action chunk of H=50 future joint-position targets (linearly projected via a scale and nominal offset), of which only K=5 are executed before re-querying in a receding-horizon fashion, plus a binary done-prediction token that triggers active command termination. For navigation, RGB-D images are processed by FoundationPose to estimate a waypoint, which is inserted into a language template and fed through the same text pathway.

## Method

SENTINEL is trained in three stages. First, a Mixture-of-Expert whole-body controller (PPO in IsaacLab) tracks retargeted human motions from AMASS under domain randomization, producing ~200K robot trajectories (~100M state-action pairs) paired with text annotations. Second, a Transformer language-state encoder jointly processes CLIP text tokens and the multi-scale state history, exposing key-value caches to a flow-matching action expert (a reduced-dimension Transformer decoder with interleaved cross- and self-attention) that predicts action chunks via velocity-field regression with Beta(1.5,1.0) interpolation; the expert is augmented with dynamics-aware state prediction (future root velocity, angular velocity, and joint positions) and a done-prediction MLP head. Third, a lightweight residual action head is trained with PPO under domain randomization to correct open-loop drift from action chunking, with rewards penalizing residual magnitude and action rate while encouraging tracking of the base model's predicted joint positions. The key architectural insight is that training on physically grounded robot trajectories rather than human motion, combined with end-to-end flow matching, eliminates the need for intermediate motion representations while preserving both semantic alignment and physical feasibility.

## Results

On the HumanML3D test set, SENTINEL achieves the best performance across all metrics: MM-Dist of 0.487, R@1 of 0.582, R@3 of 0.766, Diversity of 0.967, MMD of 3.438, and a 99.45% physical execution success rate. The closest baseline, T2M-GPT+Retarget, trails by 0.095 in MM-Dist, 0.101 in R@1, and 10.12 percentage points in success rate. SENTINEL also achieves near-ground-truth diversity (0.967 vs. 0.977) while maintaining the highest execution reliability among all methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SENTINEL outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the two-stage text-to-motion pipelines (MDM+Retarget and T2M-GPT+Retarget), SENTINEL improves R@1 by 0.244 and 0.101 respectively, and boosts success rate by 4.51 and 10.12 percentage points, demonstrating that end-to-end training on physically grounded robot trajectories yields both better semantic alignment and more stable execution than decoupled generation-then-tracking. Against UH-1, which generates robot poses directly from text without retargeting, SENTINEL still leads by 0.188 in R@1 and 12.50 points in success rate, indicating that collecting trajectories through actual simulated interaction (rather than open-loop pose generation) is critical. Against LangWBC, the MLP-based end-to-end controller, SENTINEL achieves substantially higher R@1 (0.582 vs. 0.435), MMD (3.438 vs. 8.642), and success rate (99.45% vs. 81.78%), confirming that the transformer architecture with flow-matching action expert provides superior semantic understanding and physical robustness over the CVAE+MLP design.

## Relation to Prior Work

SENTINEL departs from the dominant modular paradigm for language-conditioned humanoid control, which chains a text-to-motion generator (e.g., MDM, T2M-GPT) with a downstream whole-body controller, or uses intermediate latent representations (CVAE in LangWBC, robot poses in UH-1). Instead, it adopts the flow-matching action expert architecture popularized in VLA models (SmolVLA, π₀) and applies it directly to humanoid whole-body control, with a CLIP-based language encoder and a transformer language-state encoder for contextual representation. The post-training stage borrows the residual RL formulation from Johannink et al. to correct open-loop drift from action chunking, and the data pipeline leverages a pretrained MoE WBC policy trained via PPO in IsaacLab to generate physically grounded trajectories from AMASS motions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating intermediate motion representations, SENTINEL enables gradient flow from execution feedback back to language understanding, which the authors argue is necessary for robust, generalizable humanoid control. The successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Unitree G1 and the multi-modal extension (converting visual waypoints into language commands) demonstrate practical viability beyond simulation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-step task compositions, whether the 600M parameter requirement is fundamental or an artifact of the current training recipe, and how the method scales to environments with richer physical interactions (e.g., manipulation, terrain variation) beyond the AMASS-derived motion distribution.
