## Problem

In black-box adversarial attacks on Vision-Language Pre-training (VLP) models, the transferability of crafted adversarial examples across different architectures remains limited. Existing methods (SGA, LSSA, DRA) rely on a fixed, narrow set of input transformations and their optimization remains strongly coupled to source-model-specific gradients, causing overfitting to the surrogate model and poor cross-architecture generalization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image and its paired text caption as input. The image is first expanded into Q_S=5 scale variants, each of which undergoes learnable block-level transformations to produce Q_T=6 variants, yielding Q_O=30 auxiliary images per iteration (35 total including the original). The text is perturbed by substituting one word (budget ξ_t=1) selected from a candidate vocabulary of W=10, using GloVe embedding-space search and MLM fallback. The output is a jointly perturbed image (L∞ budget 8/255, 10 iterations, step 2/255) and caption designed to maximize the cosine similarity between the image embedding and a target text embedding in the VLP model.

## Method

TTA introduces two coupled mechanisms. First, a learnable block-level transformation maintains probability distributions over M blocking strategies and N transformation operations; for each image, a blocking strategy partitions it into K blocks and L operations are composed per block, with the distributions updated via gradient ascent in a bilevel optimization (inner loop optimizes the adversarial perturbation, outer loop optimizes the transformation probabilities). Second, Boosted Integrated Gradients (BIG) replaces the standard single-path IG integration with multi-path sampling: instead of drawing many points along one interpolation path (which yields near-identical gradients), it draws one point per transformed variant path, expanding the integration to Q_O diverse paths. This leverages IG's implementation invariance property to reduce dependence on the source model while preserving fine-grained cross-modal alignment. For the text modality, the method builds on BERT-Attack with embedding-level perturbations in GloVe space to generate semantically coherent word substitutions.

## Results

On the image-text retrieval task (Flickr30K), TTA achieves the highest attack success rate (ASR) across all source-to-target model pairs. In the most challenging cross-architecture setting (ALBEF→CLIP_ViT), TTA reaches 92.27% TR R@1 and 92.82% IR R@1, compared to 53.25%/60.89% for the strongest prior method LSSA. Similarly, ALBEF→CLIP_CNN yields 93.36%/93.58% for TTA versus 56.45%/64.43% for LSSA. In cross-task transferability (image captioning on MSCOCO, visual grounding on RefCOCO+), TTA consistently produces the lowest metric scores (e.g., BLEU-4 drops from 39.7 baseline to 12.1, CIDEr from 133.3 to 28.5), confirming superior adversarial transferability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTA consistently outperforms all five baselines (PGD, Co-attack, SGA, DRA, LSSA) in black-box transfer settings, with the largest gains in cross-architecture transfers. Against the primary baseline LSSA, TTA improves TR R@1 by 39.02% (ALBEF→CLIP_ViT: 92.27 vs 53.25), 36.91% (ALBEF→CLIP_CNN: 93.36 vs 56.45), 35.03% (CLIP_ViT→ALBEF: 81.02 vs 45.99), and 23.77% (CLIP_CNN→ALBEF: 55.16 vs 31.39). DRA and SGA show progressively weaker transferability, with SGA reaching only 36.69% TR R@1 on CLIP_ViT when sourced from ALBEF, while PGD and Co-attack remain far below (10.67% and 29.82% respectively). In same-architecture transfers (e.g., ALBEF→TCL), all methods approach saturation (>96%), and TTA's advantage narrows to 1–4% over LSSA. In cross-task evaluation (image captioning, visual grounding), TTA also achieves the lowest scores across all metrics (BLEU-4, METEOR, CIDEr, SPICE, grounding accuracy), confirming its superiority beyond the primary retrieval task.

## Relation to Prior Work

TTA sits at the intersection of two threads in multimodal adversarial attack research: input-transformation-based transferability enhancement (SGA's scale augmentation, LSSA's local shuffle) and optimization-trajectory diversification (DRA's clean-image interpolation, integrated gradients). The key departure is replacing the fixed, hand-designed transformation sets of SGA and LSSA with a learnable block-level transformation policy that adaptively selects optimal combinations per image, and augmenting integrated gradients by expanding the single integration path into multiple paths via these learned transformations. For the text modality, the paper follows the BERT-Attack + GloVe embedding perturbation pipeline established by prior VLP attack work, making the text side largely inherited rather than novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in black-box VLP security: adversarial examples crafted on one architecture (e.g., ALBEF) transfer poorly to structurally different targets (e.g., CLIP with a CNN backbone), and the 35–39% absolute ASR gains in cross-architecture settings are substantial. The learnable transformation framework is a generalizable idea that could extend beyond VLP to other multimodal or unimodal transferability settings. However, the evaluation is limited to three downstream tasks (retrieval, captioning, grounding) and four VLP architectures; whether the approach transfers to generative VLP models (e.g., LLaVA, Flamingo) or to more complex text perturbations (multi-word, sentence-level) remains open. The text attack remains a single-word substitution, which is a simplification relative to the image-side sophistication.
