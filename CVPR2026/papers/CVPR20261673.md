## Problem

Vision-Language-Action models with diffusion-based action heads rely on a fixed action chunk size at inference time, which is suboptimal because the ideal chunk size varies across tasks and even across phases within a single task. A large chunk reduces reactivity to new observations, while a small chunk introduces mode-jumping and jerky behavior. Existing adaptive methods either require task-specific reward signals, handcrafted value functions, or still fix the chunk size for the entire episode, leaving the problem of per-timestep adaptive chunk selection unsolved.

## Input / Output

At each inference timestep, the method consumes N parallel candidate action chunks (each of length H, e.g., H=16) generated by the flow-matching action head, conditioned on the current observation (wrist and side RGB images, language instruction, and proprioceptive state). It outputs a single scalar optimal chunk size h* (bounded below by a minimum ξ), which determines how many of the predicted actions are executed before the next replanning step. The N samples are drawn in parallel via batched computation, adding roughly 20 ms of inference latency on an A800 GPU when N=20.

## Method

AAC exploits action entropy as an uncertainty cue to select the chunk size purely at inference time, without any additional training or architectural modification. For each timestep within the predicted chunk, it computes Gaussian differential entropy over the continuous translation and rotation components (from the covariance matrix of N parallel samples) and Shannon entropy over the discrete gripper control (from the empirical close/open ratio). It then computes the average action entropy for every candidate chunk size h from 1 to H, and selects h* as the maximum differential point of this average-entropy curve (i.e., where the difference between consecutive average entropies is largest), clamped to a lower bound ξ to guarantee minimum action magnitude. The intuition is that high-entropy (uncertain) predictions trigger smaller chunks for frequent replanning and reactivity, while low-entropy (confident) predictions permit longer chunks for temporal consistency and computational efficiency. The approach generalizes across robot embodiments and VLA backbones (GR00T N1.5, π0.5) because the entropy formulation naturally handles both continuous and discrete action spaces.

## Results

On RoboCasa (24 kitchen tasks), AAC improves the average success rate from 59.7% (GR00T, fixed h=16) to 62.0%, with the largest gain on Rotation tasks (+3.8 pp, 57.6%→61.4%). On LIBERO (40 tasks), AAC raises the average from 94.1% to 95.0%, with a notable +4.0 pp improvement on the most challenging LIBERO-Long suite (88.8%→92.8%). On real-world tasks, AAC achieves a 15 pp average gain (67.0%→82.0%). The method also generalizes to the π0.5 backbone, improving LIBERO average from 97.0% to 97.9% and LIBERO-Pro OOD average from 30.9% to 34.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is GR00T N1.5 (Bjorck et al., 2025) with a fixed inference chunk size of h=16. AAC consistently outperforms this baseline on both RoboCasa (+2.3 pp average) and LIBERO (+0.9 pp average), and decisively on real-world tasks (+15 pp average, 67.0%→82.0%). Against the secondary backbone π0.5 (Black et al., 2025), AAC also yields gains: +0.9 pp on LIBERO (97.0%→97.9%) and +3.9 pp on LIBERO-Pro OOD (30.9%→34.8%). Notably, AAC surpasses *all* fixed-chunk-size variants of GR00T (h=2,4,8,12) on average across both benchmarks, confirming that no single fixed chunk size is optimal. The largest individual gains appear on fine-grained manipulation (Rotation: +3.8 pp) and long-horizon tasks (LIBERO-Long: +4.0 pp), where adaptive replanning at high-entropy moments is most beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the action chunking paradigm established by ACT and Diffusion Policy, which are now standard in diffusion/flow-matching-based VLA models like GR00T N1.5, π0, and SmolVLA. It departs from the dominant practice of using empirically fixed chunk sizes at inference-time and from prior adaptive methods (BID, TVBID, SGAC) that still commit to a fixed chunk length per episode. The key conceptual departure is using action entropy computed from parallel stochastic samples as a training-free, architecture-agnostic signal for chunk-size selection, avoiding the task-specific reward signals or handcrafted value functions required by RL-based approaches (Gou et al., Chen et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AAC offers a drop-in inference-time strategy that eliminates the need for per-task chunk-size tuning, a practical pain point acknowledged across the VLA literature. The 15% real-world success-rate gain (67%→82%) is the most compelling result, suggesting the method's value grows with task complexity and embodiment variability. However, the simulation improvements are modest (2.3% on RoboCasa, 0.9% on LIBERO), and the method requires 20 parallel forward passes per step (~20 ms overhead), which may be non-trivial for latency-critical deployments. Open questions include whether the entropy-based heuristic generalizes to non-diffusion action heads, multi-embodiment settings with heterogeneous action spaces, and whether the fixed lower bound ξ can itself be made adaptive.
