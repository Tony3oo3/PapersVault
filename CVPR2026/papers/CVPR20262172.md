## Problem

Unified MLLMs for text-to-image generation still fail at fine-grained object-level alignment, producing hallucinated, omitted, or distorted objects. Existing self-improving preference optimization methods (e.g., SILMM, SUDER) rely on Best-of-N sampling that generates preference-ambiguous pairs and treat images holistically, lacking explicit object-centric supervision across data construction and optimization stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262172/cropped_page6_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes compositional text prompts (covering attribute, layout, non-spatial relationship, and complex composition categories) and outputs sequences of discrete visual tokens from a VQ tokenizer, which decode to RGB images. All training data—prompts, perturbed variants, candidate images, and object masks—are generated autonomously by the MLLM itself via in-context learning, requiring no external datasets, human annotations, or auxiliary reward models. The model is trained on 20,000 self-generated samples after filtering.

## Method

OSPO is a five-stage self-improving pipeline: (1) the MLLM generates base prompts, (2) each prompt is perturbed via Replace/Swap/Drop strategies and jointly densified with its perturbed counterpart to create pairs sharing global context but differing in fine-grained object details, (3) one preferred and one non-preferred image are generated per pair, with binary object masks extracted from attention weights of object-representing text tokens over visual tokens in intermediate layers (binarized via Otsu thresholding), (4) decompositional VQA questions are used to compute per-image alignment scores, filtering out preference-ambiguous and preference-false pairs before selecting the highest-scoring pair, and (5) the model is fine-tuned with a combined Object-weighted SimPO loss (which applies spatial weights w_t = 1 + α·m_t to emphasize object-relevant visual tokens in the token-level reward) plus an SFT loss on the preferred image for structural coherence. This replaces the noisy Best-of-N sampling with targeted, object-level preference signals and introduces spatially grounded gradient emphasis during optimization.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench++, Janus-Pro-7B + OSPO achieves the best scores among all unified MLLMs across most categories: Color 0.8567, Shape 0.6386, Texture 0.7727, Spatial-2D 0.3562, and Complex 0.4147, surpassing SUDER (0.7824/0.5786/0.7292) and SILMM (0.7394/0.4325/0.5796) in the Attribute category. On DPGBench, the 7B OSPO model attains 85.61 overall score (second-highest among unified MLLMs), while on GenEval it reaches 0.83 overall (second-best, trailing SUDER's 0.84 primarily due to the Count category). The 1B model also shows consistent gains, reaching 0.8349 on Color and 84.46 on DPGBench overall, demonstrating robustness across model scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OSPO outperforms both self-improving baselines (SILMM and SUDER) on T2I-CompBench++ across nearly all categories for both 1B and 7B scales, with the largest margins in Attribute (e.g., Color: 0.8567 vs. 0.7824 for SUDER and 0.7394 for SILMM on 7B) and Layout (Spatial-2D: 0.3562 vs. 0.2524 for SUDER). On GenEval, SUDER edges out OSPO by 0.01 overall (0.84 vs. 0.83), a gap driven almost entirely by the Count category (0.70 vs. 0.63) where SUDER leverages external COCO image-text pairs for counting supervision. On DPGBench, OSPO achieves the highest overall score (85.61) among Janus-Pro-based self-improving methods, surpassing SILMM (84.56), though SUDER's DPGBench results are not reported. Against diffusion-based T2I models (SD-XL, DALL-E 3, FLUX.1), the 7B OSPO model surpasses them on Color, Shape, Texture, and Spatial-2D categories of T2I-CompBench++, while remaining competitive on Non-Spatial and Complex categories where the CLIP-T metric has limited discriminative range. The Janus-Pro baseline without any self-improving method is substantially weaker (e.g., Color 0.5215 vs. 0.8567), confirming that the gains come from the OSPO framework rather than the base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

OSPO extends the self-improving preference optimization line initiated by SILMM and SUDER, which use Best-of-N sampling to construct preference pairs for T2I generation. The key departure is replacing Best-of-N with a prompt-perturbation pipeline (Replace, Swap, Drop) that creates controlled, object-level semantic differences between paired prompts, thereby avoiding the preference-ambiguous pairs that plague Best-of-N. On the optimization side, OSPO modifies the SimPO loss by introducing spatial weights derived from attention-based object masks, shifting from holistic token-level reward aggregation to object-specific gradient emphasis. The framework is positioned as a fully self-contained loop within unified MLLMs, requiring no external reward models, human annotations, or auxiliary segmentation networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The object-centric data construction and weighted loss offer a principled mechanism for reducing object hallucination in autoregressive T2I generation without the computational overhead of multi-model RL (PPO/GRPO) or the data curation cost of DPO. The finding that a self-improving MLLM can match or exceed dedicated diffusion models (SD-XL, DALL-E 3, FLUX.1) on compositional benchmarks reinforces the viability of unified architectures for fine-grained generation. Limitations include evaluation restricted to Janus-Pro backbones, persistent weakness on counting (GenEval Count), and reliance on attention-based masks whose quality may degrade in highly cluttered scenes. Open questions include whether the object-weighted loss generalizes to continuous-token or diffusion-based unified models, and whether iterative self-improvement cycles compound gains or saturate.
