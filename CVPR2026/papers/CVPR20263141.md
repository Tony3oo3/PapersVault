## Problem

Existing Embodied Question Answering (EQA) benchmarks assume every query warrants an explicit response, yet real-world human-robot interaction frequently involves ambiguous or underspecified instructions where the correct behavior is to withhold an answer. The paper quantifies this gap through a 50-participant survey showing 32.4% of natural human queries lack sufficient contextual evidence, and identifies that no prior benchmark systematically evaluates whether embodied agents can recognize when to abstain rather than hallucinate.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural language question q and a sequence of egocentric video frames τ (from HM3D or ScanNet scenes). In the episodic-memory setting (EM-EQA), τ is a fixed sequence of T pre-recorded frames; in the active setting (A-EQA), the agent navigates the 3D scene and collects frames up to a maximum step budget T_max. The output is a two-part autoregressive sequence: a decision token (answer or abstain) followed by either the question answer or a structured abstention reason explaining which category of missing evidence applies. Frame-level causal annotations link each answer to its supporting visual evidence or each abstention to the specific frame revealing the evidence gap.

## Method

AbstainEQA is constructed by having human annotators transform well-posed OpenEQA questions into ambiguous variants across five categories derived from Norman's cognitive error model: Actionability Limitation, Referential Underspecification, Information Unavailability, False Presupposition, and Preference Dependence. The resulting 1,636 abstention cases are paired with 1,636 original OpenEQA answerable instances, then paraphrased into five LLM-generated variants for 16,360 total QA pairs, with frame-level annotations marking the causal evidence for each case. Evaluation uses GPT-4o as an LLM-as-a-Judge (validated at 0.88 Pearson correlation with human raters) to score both response correctness via semantic matching and abstention recognition via recall/precision/F1, supplemented by navigation metrics (Success Rate, Total Frames, Total Snapshots, Path Length) in the active setting. The empirical study then probes model behavior across scales (2B–32B Qwen3-VL, GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Pro), prompting strategies (coarse vs. fine-grained abstention cues), explicit reasoning chains, and supervised fine-tuning, revealing that all approaches rely on surface textual cues rather than visual grounding.

## Results

On AbstainEQA, the best frontier model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) achieves only 42.79% overall abstention recall, while humans reach 91.17%, revealing a massive gap. Most evaluated VLMs fall below 33% recall (GPT-4o: 32.09%, Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 28.61%, Qwen3-VL-4B: 27.32%, GPT-5: 22.19%, Claude-Sonnet-4.5: 1.04%). Models perform best on Information Unavailability (up to 86.30% for Gemini-2.5-Pro) but struggle severely with Referential Underspecification (0–10.62%) and Preference Dependence (0.29–15.12%). In A-EQA navigation, ambiguous queries reduce success rate from 77.17% to 61.41% and increase total frames from 35.86 to 51.41.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All evaluated VLMs perform far below human abstention recall (91.17%), with Gemini-2.5-Pro leading at 42.79% overall, followed by GPT-4o (32.09%), Qwen2.5-VL-7B (28.61%), Qwen3-VL-4B (27.32%), GPT-5 (22.19%), and Claude-Sonnet-4.5 (1.04%). The 3D-MEM agent, used as the A-EQA navigation baseline, shows that ambiguous queries degrade success rate by 15.76 percentage points (77.17% → 61.41%) and increase exploration cost (total frames 35.86 → 51.41). HM-EQA (Explore Until Confident) achieves 27.17% recall and 36.76% F1 in EM-EQA, comparable to Qwen2.5-VL-7B (28.61% recall, 42.42% F1), confirming that existing exploration-based agents do not inherently solve abstention. Notably, no model exceeds 15.12% on Preference Dependence or 10.62% on Referential Underspecification, indicating these categories remain essentially unsolved. The paper further shows that prompting, reasoning (thinking mode), and SFT yield only marginal or illusory gains, with SFT models overfitting to textual cues rather than visual evidence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Embodied Question Answering (EQA) paradigm established by OpenEQA and 3D-MEM by introducing abstention as a first-class evaluation dimension. While prior EQA benchmarks (EQA-v1, IQUAD, MP3D-EQA, HM-EQA, OpenEQA) assume every query warrants an explicit answer, and while abstention has been studied in text QA (AbstentionBench, AmbigQA) and visual QA (VizWiz), no prior work has formalized the requirement to withhold answers in embodied 3D navigation settings. The abstention taxonomy draws on Norman's cognitive error framework to categorize five types of missing evidence, and the paper positions abstention as a prerequisite for the broader clarification literature in human-robot interaction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 32.4% of natural human queries to embodied agents require abstention, combined with the ~50-point gap between the best model (42.79%) and humans (91.17%), exposes a fundamental safety gap in current embodied systems where hallucinated answers can cause physical harm. The negative results—that SFT models overfit to lexical cues (TF-IDF+LR matches multimodal SFT), that scaling does not generalize across architectures, and that explicit reasoning degrades recall—redirect the field away from straightforward fine-tuning or prompting toward genuinely uncertainty-aware multimodal mechanisms. Open questions include how to design training objectives that ground abstention in visual evidence rather than text patterns, and how abstention decisions should interact with active navigation policies to avoid the observed oscillation between premature termination and over-exploration.
