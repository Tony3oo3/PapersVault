## Problem

Autoregressive MLLMs tokenize input images in ways that lose fine-grained 2D spatial information, limiting their ability to accurately ground objects. Existing token-based grounding approaches—text coordinates, fixed-size visual patches, and 1D bin indices—either entangle texture with geometry, fail to preserve spatial topology, or produce action spaces where small index changes cause large reward fluctuations, making them poorly suited for reinforcement learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262384/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a text referring expression (or a spatial reference such as a point, box, or mask) and produces structured token sequences: grid tokens within <seg> tags for masks, <box> tags for bounding boxes, and offset tokens for sub-grid refinement. The image plane is discretized into a 32×32 grid by default, with a 64×64 effective resolution achieved through 10 offset tokens rather than 3072 additional grid tokens. Outputs are unordered sequences of grid tokens per mask instance, enabling multi-instance and multi-granularity referencing in a single autoregressive pass.

## Method

GETok augments the MLLM vocabulary with two learnable token families: grid tokens that partition the image into an n×n uniform lattice of spatial anchors, and offset tokens (discrete ±1 displacement vectors plus a <DELETE> token) that refine anchor positions at minimal vocabulary cost. A greedy mask-to-token conversion algorithm (using SAM prompts at all grid points) automatically transforms dense ground-truth masks into minimal grid-token sequences for SFT, while an offset-aware dataset construction procedure categorizes grid points into Inside/Ring/Far/Hard-Delete regions to generate high-value refinement training pairs. For reinforcement learning, a two-stage GRPO framework first trains grid-token generation with format, mask-IoU, box-IoU, and semantic-critical-point rewards, then introduces multi-turn offset refinement where the model iteratively proposes, critiques, and corrects its spatial predictions using offset and DELETE tokens, creating a geometry-aware low-entropy action space that stabilizes policy optimization.

## Results

On Referring Expression Segmentation (RES), GETok-SFT achieves 68.2 average gIoU across ReasonSeg and RefCOCO variants, outperforming the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline (65.7) by +2.5 points. Under reinforcement learning, GETok-R1 reaches 72.7 average gIoU, surpassing the best RL baseline VisionReasoner (70.7) by +2.0 points. On Referring Expression Comprehension (REC), GETok-R1 achieves 90.3% Acc@0.5 and 76.1% Acc@0.8 on RefCOCO variants, exceeding VisionReasoner (88.7% / 73.7%) and all SFT baselines. The offset token mechanism contributes a consistent +1.0% (SFT) and +1.5% (RL) gain over grid-only configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the SFT setting, GETok-SFT (68.2 avg gIoU) outperforms all specialized segmentation baselines: LISA (64.2), PixelLM, CRIS, ReLA, and LAVT, as well as the general-purpose Qwen2.5-VL-7B (65.7). In the RL setting, GETok-R1 (72.7) surpasses VisionReasoner (70.7), SAM-R1 (70.2), and Seg-Zero (69.8) by 2.0, 2.5, and 2.9 points respectively, with the largest margin on RefCOCO+ (77.4 vs. 76.2 for Seg-Zero). For REC (Table 2), GETok-SFT (88.2 Acc@0.5) beats UNINEXT-L (87.0), ClawMachineX (86.8), Qwen2.5-VL (86.6), Groma (86.5), Ferret (83.9), and Shikra (82.9); under the stricter Acc@0.8 metric, GETok-SFT (72.8) improves over Qwen2.5-VL (70.9) by +1.9 points. GETok-R1 further extends the lead in RL, reaching 90.3 Acc@0.5 and 76.1 Acc@0.8, exceeding VisionReasoner (88.7 / 73.7) by +1.6 and +2.4 points. The offset token mechanism consistently adds +1.0% (SFT) and +1.5% (RL) over grid-only variants, confirming its value for precise spatial refinement across all compared baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

GETok departs from three dominant grounding paradigms in MLLMs: text-based coordinate descriptions (Shikra, Qwen-VL) that lose spatial topology, patch-based visual tokens (Groma, ClawMachine) that entangle texture with geometry, and 1D bin methods (Pix2Seq, VisionLLM) that fail to capture 2D structural relationships. The closest conceptual ancestor is Kosmos-2, which envisioned 2D spatial tokens but was restricted to bounding-box grounding; GETok generalizes this to masks, polylines, and iterative refinement via offset tokens. For the RL component, the paper builds directly on GRPO (DeepSeekMath) but introduces a geometry-aware, two-stage reward structure that exploits the grid-offset hierarchy for self-correcting spatial reasoning, a departure from prior RL-for-segmentation work (Seg-Zero, VisionReasoner) that treats localization as a single-shot prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GETok offers a practical, architecture-preserving recipe for giving autoregressive MLLMs native 2D spatial reasoning, which is a persistent weakness of current vision-language models. The offset-token mechanism enabling iterative self-correction and the demonstration that a structured 2D token space yields more stable RL optimization (low-entropy action space) are conceptually useful insights for the broader MLLM community. However, the approach still depends on an external SAM decoder for mask generation, the grid resolution trade-off limits scalability to very fine-grained tasks, and all experiments are conducted on a single 7B base model, leaving open whether the gains persist at larger scales or in video/3D settings.
