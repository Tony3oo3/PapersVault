## Problem

Existing contrastive vision-language pre-training methods operate under single-region or masked-region objectives, limiting their ability to capture compositional semantics that span multiple image regions. Local alignment approaches (e.g., SPARC, FILIP) match individual tokens to patches, while global approaches (e.g., A-CLIP, C-PGS) apply a single mask, neither of which exhaustively explores combinatorial correspondences between groups of visual regions and structured textual phrases.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 RGB images encoded by a ViT-B/16 into N patch embeddings and text descriptions encoded by a 12-layer Transformer into L token embeddings, both in a shared D=512 dimensional space. For each image, M=10 region masks are generated (randomly or via SAM2) on the patch grid, and for each text, a constituency parse tree is extracted to define phrase-level token masks. The output is a pair of aligned image-text embeddings in a shared semantic space, usable for zero-shot classification, bidirectional retrieval, and compositional evaluation.

## Method

PowerCLIP defines a contrastive objective over the powerset of image region masks (all 2^M subsets) and the nodes of a textual parse tree, computing region embeddings as sums of L2-normalized masked patch features and phrase embeddings as sums of L2-normalized masked token features. A bidirectional triplet margin loss is minimized: R2T aggregation finds the best-matching phrase for each region subset, while T2R aggregation finds the best-matching region subset for each phrase. To avoid the exponential O(2^M) cost of naive powerset enumeration, the paper introduces Non-Linear Aggregators (NLAs) that reduce complexity to O(M) through three layers of summation-then-activation; NLA-T1 (softplus activation) approximates T2R and NLA-T2 (tanh activation with a residual antiderivative) approximates R2T, both provably converging to the exact loss with arbitrary precision as the temperature τ→0. The total training objective combines the standard CLIP contrastive loss with the NLA-approximated triplet loss (λ=0.2), trained from scratch on CC12M for 32 epochs.

## Results

PowerCLIP-S achieves 42.2% average top-1 accuracy across 17 zero-shot classification datasets, outperforming the best global-alignment baseline C-PGS (39.5%) by +2.7 points and the best local-alignment baseline SPARC (37.8%) by +4.4 points. On zero-shot image-text retrieval, PowerCLIP-S reaches 47.0% average Recall@1, surpassing C-PGS (45.1%) and CLIP (42.7%). The method attains state-of-the-art results on 22 out of 28 evaluated benchmarks, with particularly large gains on fine-grained datasets (Cars +6.5%, Food101 +8.9% over CLIP) and robustness benchmarks (ImageNet-R +5.9%, ImageNet-Sketch +4.0% over C-PGS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PowerCLIP-S outperforms all seven baselines on average across the 17 classification datasets, with the largest margins over CLIP/FLIP (+9.2 pts) and FILIP (+7.8 pts), and more competitive gaps over the strongest baselines C-PGS (+2.7) and SPARC (+4.4). On retrieval, PowerCLIP-S leads C-PGS by +1.9 R@1 (47.0 vs 45.1) and CLIP by +4.3 R@1, winning across all six retrieval settings (COCO/Flickr8k/Flickr30k × I2T/T2I). For robustness, PowerCLIP-S achieves 35.1% overall ImageNet accuracy vs 32.9% for C-PGS and 31.0% for CLIP, with the largest single-dataset gain on ImageNet-R (+5.4 over C-PGS). On compositionality, PowerCLIP-S leads on Winoground image accuracy (16.0 vs 13.5 for FILIP, the next best) but trails C-PGS slightly on SugarCrepe attribute (70.4 vs 70.8) and relation (67.1 vs 67.9) subsets, while winning on the object subset (76.1 vs 75.5). Notably, FILIP, despite strong token-level alignment, underperforms on most classification and robustness benchmarks (34.4% avg classification, 23.4% overall ImageNet), suggesting that fine-grained token-to-patch matching alone is insufficient without the combinatorial region-set structure that PowerCLIP introduces.

## Relation to Prior Work

PowerCLIP extends the CLIP contrastive pre-training paradigm by replacing the single global alignment objective with an exhaustive local-to-global alignment over all combinations of image region masks and textual parse-tree phrases. It departs from both local-alignment methods (SPARC, FILIP, FineLIP), which match individual tokens to individual patches, and global-masking methods (A-CLIP, E-CLIP, C-PGS, FLIP), which apply a single mask per image, by systematically exploring the full powerset of region subsets. The key technical departure is the Non-Linear Aggregator (NLA), a theoretically grounded approximation that reduces the exponential powerset computation to linear complexity while provably recovering the exact loss to arbitrary precision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PowerCLIP addresses a persistent weakness of CLIP-style models—capturing compositional semantics spanning multiple visual entities—without requiring fine-tuning or external supervision, and the NLA provides a clean theoretical bridge between an intractable combinatorial objective and a practical training procedure. The consistent gains across 22 of 28 benchmarks (classification, retrieval, robustness, compositionality) suggest the powerset alignment principle is broadly applicable rather than tuned to a specific evaluation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger encoders or longer texts where parse trees become deeper, whether the random-mask variant (PowerCLIP-R) can match SAM-based masks at higher M, and how the method extends to 3D or video settings where region combinations grow even more rapidly.
