## Problem

Diffusion-based HMR methods generate diverse 3D mesh hypotheses but often produce physically implausible or image-inconsistent predictions, especially under occlusion. Existing DPO-based alignment (e.g., ADHMR) relies on pairwise comparisons and an image-driven scorer that is easily misled by cluttered backgrounds or incorrect contacts, providing unreliable guidance. The paper addresses how to obtain stable, group-level preference signals for diffusion-based HMR without requiring 3D ground truth, enabling effective fine-tuning on noisy in-the-wild datasets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262503/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input and produces G diverse 3D human mesh predictions parameterized by SMPL pose (24×3 joint swing/twist) and shape (10-dim) parameters. The critique agent consumes rendered 2D mesh overlays composited on the input image and outputs per-mesh quality scores in [0,100] with one-sentence textual critiques. Notably, the fine-tuning stage requires no 3D annotations—only the input images and the VLM-generated preference scores—making it applicable to in-the-wild datasets with unreliable pseudo-labels.

## Method

The framework has two components. First, a VLM-guided critique agent (Qwen3-VL-32B) equipped with a dual-memory mechanism—rule memory storing assessment rules with UCB-based selection and prototype memory storing CLIP-embedded visual precedents—plus a self-reflective exploration phase that mines new rules by comparing its scores against GT metrics via Spearman correlation. Second, a group preference alignment loss that adapts GRPO to diffusion in an offline, ODE-compatible setting: for each image, G mesh hypotheses are scored by the frozen critique agent, normalized into group-relative advantages, and used to weight the log-likelihood ratio between the trainable denoiser and a frozen reference denoiser, expressed through the standard diffusion noise-prediction loss at a randomly sampled timestep. This avoids SDE sampling and trajectory-level reinforcement while retaining the stability of group-level optimization.

## Results

On the in-the-wild 3DPW benchmark (M=100), the proposed method achieves PVE=60.9, MPJPE=52.5, and PA-MPJPE=31.5, outperforming the previous SOTA ADHMR (PVE=65.4, MPJPE=57.2, PA-MPJPE=33.5) by 8.2% in MPJPE. On Human3.6M (M=100), it reaches PVE=43.8, MPJPE=35.0, PA-MPJPE=23.9, again surpassing ADHMR (PVE=45.9, MPJPE=36.9, PA-MPJPE=24.8). The Ours† variant, fine-tuned on InstaVariety using only preference signals without 3D labels, further improves to MPJPE=49.9 on 3DPW and MPJPE=34.3 on Human3.6M, demonstrating strong in-the-wild generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all probabilistic baselines on both 3DPW and Human3.6M. Against the closest competitor ADHMR (M=100), it reduces MPJPE from 57.2 to 52.5 (−8.2%) and PA-MPJPE from 33.5 to 31.5 on 3DPW, and from 36.9 to 35.0 and 24.8 to 23.9 on Human3.6M. Compared to ScoreHypo (M=100), the gains are larger: MPJPE drops from 63.0 to 52.5 on 3DPW and from 38.4 to 35.0 on Human3.6M. Against deterministic methods, the improvement is most dramatic over HMR (MPJPE 130.0→52.5 on 3DPW) and CameraHMR (MPJPE 56.0→52.5), while the gap over the strongest deterministic baseline Zolly (MPJPE 65.0) is 12.5 mm. The Ours† variant, fine-tuned solely on InstaVariety preference signals without 3D labels, further narrows the gap to 49.9 MPJPE on 3DPW, surpassing all prior methods including ADHMR by 7.3 mm in MPJPE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based HMR line (ScoreHypo, ADHMR) by replacing pairwise DPO alignment with a group-wise GRPO objective adapted to ODE-based diffusion sampling, avoiding the computational overhead of SDE rollouts. It departs from learned 2D-based scorers (HMR-Scorer, ScoreNet) by introducing a VLM critique agent with dual-memory (rule + prototype) and self-reflection to produce semantically grounded, stable quality scores. The GRPO formulation is borrowed from LLM alignment (DeepSeekMath, DeepSeek-R1) and the diffusion log-likelihood surrogate follows Diffusion-DPO, but the combination of offline group-wise advantage weighting with a VLM-generated preference dataset is novel in the HMR context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to fine-tune a diffusion HMR model on in-the-wild data (InstaVariety) using only preference signals—without any 3D ground truth—addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying HMR systems to unconstrained scenes. The VLM critique agent's dual-memory and self-reflection mechanism offers a reusable pattern for stabilizing VLM-based evaluation in other 3D reconstruction or generation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-person or video HMR, the sensitivity to VLM choice and prompt design, and whether the group size G=20 is optimal or whether larger groups yield diminishing returns.
