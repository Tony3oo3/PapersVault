## Problem

Existing VQ-based image compression methods lack a principled mechanism for joint rate-distortion optimization because the discrete nearest-neighbor codebook assignment blocks gradient flow from the rate objective to the encoder, creating a fundamental decoupling between representation learning and entropy modeling. Prior work relies on indirect heuristics (codebook size adjustment, selective transmission, uniform coding) rather than a differentiable connection that lets the entropy loss directly shape the encoder-induced latent distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261118/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

RDVQ takes full-resolution RGB images (768×512 on Kodak, 2K on CLIC2020 and DIV2K-val) and produces compressed bitstreams at extremely low bitrates (down to 0.01 bpp) along with perceptually reconstructed images. The encoder extracts multi-scale latent features that are flattened into a unified token sequence for vector quantization and autoregressive entropy modeling. The same entropy model also supports test-time rate control by transmitting only a prefix of indices and autoregressively completing the remainder, enabling flexible bitrate adjustment without retraining.

## Method

RDVQ introduces a differentiable soft relaxation of the VQ codebook assignment—defined as a temperature-scaled softmax over negative squared distances—that is used exclusively in the rate-estimation branch during training, while standard hard VQ is retained for reconstruction and entropy coding. This relaxation makes the cross-entropy rate loss differentiable with respect to encoder outputs, allowing the rate objective to directly shape the encoder-induced latent distribution and transform the traditionally uniform VQ prior into a learnable, entropy-aware prior. A masked-Transformer autoregressive entropy model captures intra-scale spatial and inter-scale hierarchical dependencies over the relaxed distribution, providing accurate entropy estimation for both rate optimization and entropy coding. The autoencoder is adapted from LlamaGen's VQ-VAE with a multi-scale feature decomposition, trained from scratch using GAN and perceptual losses (LPIPS) in a three-stage schedule (autoencoder pretraining, entropy model pretraining, joint fine-tuning), achieving competitive perceptual quality with fewer than 20% of the parameters of prior methods.

## Results

RDVQ achieves state-of-the-art DISTS and CLIPIQA scores across Kodak, DIV2K-val, and CLIC2020-test at ultra-low bitrates (down to 0.01 bpp on Kodak), while delivering highly competitive LPIPS and FID results against methods built on large pretrained diffusion or ViT backbones. On DIV2K-val, RDVQ reduces bitrate by up to 75.71% on DISTS and 37.63% on LPIPS compared to RDEIC. The model attains the best BD-DISTS on DIV2K-val while using less than 20% of the parameters of most baselines (251.9M total), and processes a 2K image in 1.3 s on an RTX 4090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the conventional VVC codec, RDVQ preserves fine structural edges and repetitive textures with minimal artifacts at ultra-low bitrates, whereas VVC suffers from over-smoothing and detail loss. Among VQ-based GIC methods, RDVQ outperforms DDCM (which hallucinates textures inconsistent with ground truth) and OSCAR (which shows structural distortion), and matches or exceeds ResULIC on LPIPS and FID despite being trained from scratch without large pretrained priors. The most direct comparison is with RDEIC (hybrid SQ-VQ + diffusion): RDVQ achieves the same perceptual quality at up to 75.71% lower bitrate on DISTS and 37.63% lower on LPIPS (DIV2K-val). Against the hybrid SQ-VQ method DLF, RDVQ better preserves local structures where DLF exhibits incomplete detail recovery. In the model-efficiency comparison (Fig. 6), RDVQ achieves the best BD-DISTS with fewer than 20% of the parameters of foundation-model-based baselines such as StableCodec and DLF, confirming that the lightweight architecture is not a trade-off for quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

RDVQ builds on the VQ-VAE autoencoder architecture from LlamaGen [59], adapting it for compression by removing attention layers and extending to multi-scale features. It departs from existing VQ-based compression methods (DDCM, OSCAR, DLF, RDEIC) that treat the codebook index distribution as an implicit or uniform prior, leaving the encoder's latent distribution unconstrained by the rate objective. The key conceptual departure is introducing a distance-aware softmax relaxation over codebook entries solely in the rate-estimation branch, creating a differentiable gradient path from the entropy loss back to the encoder—something that scalar quantization achieves via STE but VQ has lacked. The autoregressive masked-transformer entropy model extends the AR modeling paradigm from image synthesis (VQ-VAE, LlamaGen) into the compression setting, where it simultaneously serves as entropy coder and generative predictor for test-time rate control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine structural gap in VQ-based compression: without a differentiable rate-to-encoder path, the latent prior remains fixed and the entropy model can only passively fit it, preventing true end-to-end RD optimization. The proposed relaxation is clean in that it changes nothing about the deployment pipeline (hard VQ for reconstruction and coding), making it a drop-in improvement for existing VQ tokenizers. The paper also suggests broader applicability—existing VQ-based image tokenizers trained without entropy constraints could potentially be adapted into compression models under this framework. Open questions include whether the limited-range test-time rate control (0.02–0.32 bpp) can be extended, whether the relaxation generalizes to product quantization or multi-codebook settings, and whether the approach scales to video or higher-resolution compression without the current multi-stage training schedule.
