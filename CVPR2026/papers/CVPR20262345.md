## Problem

Existing single-image generative models (SinDDM, SinFusion, SinDiffusion) require hours of neural network training to learn the patch distribution of a single image, despite the training data being finite and low-dimensional. This paper asks whether the score function for a finite patch dataset can be computed analytically, eliminating training entirely while retaining the flexibility of diffusion-based sampling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262345/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB reference image (typically ~250×250 pixels, though gigapixel inputs are supported) and extracts overlapping 15×15 patches at S=4 scales with unit stride. For text-guided stylization, a CLIP text prompt is additionally provided; for structural analogies, a second 'structure' image supplies spatial layout via DDIM inversion. Outputs are new images at the same or retargeted resolution that match the reference's internal patch statistics, with optional constraints (symmetry, tileability) enforced during sampling.

## Method

The core insight is that because the patch dataset from a single image is finite, the optimal denoiser for a noisy patch reduces to a closed-form weighted average of all clean patches, weighted by Gaussian likelihood (Eq. 2), which is mathematically identical to non-local means at the patch level. This denoiser is embedded in a coarse-to-fine reverse diffusion process: at each scale and timestep, noisy patches are extracted, denoised in closed form, reassembled into an image via Gaussian-weighted blending (ρ=0.2), and then blended with the upsampled coarser-scale output using a two-scale Laplacian pyramid operation. The noise schedule follows flow matching (α(t)=1−t/T, σ(t)=t/T) with T=10 deterministic (DDIM) steps. Three acceleration techniques—FlashAttention reformulation of the patch denoiser, latent-space operation via a FLUX VAE, and approximate nearest-neighbor search reducing complexity from O(N²) to O(N^{3/2})—enable megapixel generation in ~1 second and gigapixel generation in minutes.

## Results

On unconditional single-image generation (186×248 images, 15 test images, 50 samples each), the proposed method with T=40, η=1 achieves a SIFID of 0.21±0.29, outperforming all trained diffusion baselines (SinDDM: 0.48, SinFusion: 0.51, SinDiffusion: 0.31) while requiring zero training time. It also achieves the highest generation diversity among all methods (LPIPS diversity 0.49, pixel diversity 0.15) and competitive no-reference quality scores (MUSIQ 55.81, NIQE 8.18). With approximate nearest neighbors (k=5), inference on an A6000 GPU drops to 0.88 s per image—faster than SinDDM (1.25 s) and SinFusion (1.99 s)—while incurring only a 0.09 SIFID increase.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the trained single-image diffusion models, the proposed method clearly wins on SIFID: 0.21 (T=40) or 0.29 (T=10) versus SinDDM's 0.48, SinFusion's 0.51, and SinDiffusion's 0.31, while also matching or exceeding their no-reference quality scores (MUSIQ 55.4–55.8 vs. 49.3–51.4) and dominating on diversity (LPIPS 0.49 vs. 0.36–0.41; pixel diversity 0.15 vs. 0.10–0.11). Against the non-parametric patch methods GPNN and GPDM, the proposed method loses on SIFID (0.21–0.29 vs. 0.06 and 0.015) but the authors note that GPNN/GPDM frequently produce near-duplicates of the input, making their low SIFID misleading; on diversity the proposed method is substantially better (LPIPS 0.49 vs. 0.29/0.31). Against SinGAN (GAN-based), the proposed method achieves comparable SIFID (0.29 vs. 0.13) but far higher diversity and avoids GAN training instability. The most striking practical advantage is zero training time (vs. 1.5–10 hrs for diffusion baselines) and, with ANN acceleration, the fastest inference (0.88 s on A6000) among all compared methods. For structural analogies, the proposed method preserves both the style-image patch distribution and the structure-image layout, whereas Nano Banana Pro (Gemini 3 Pro Image) fails on both.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lineages: classical non-parametric patch-based restoration (non-local means, GMM patch priors) and modern denoising diffusion models. The key insight is that for a finite dataset (the patches of a single image), the optimal denoiser in a diffusion model has a closed-form solution—essentially a weighted average over all patches—that is mathematically identical to a non-local-means filter. This eliminates the need for the hours-long neural network training required by prior single-image diffusion models (SinDDM, SinFusion, SinDiffusion) and GANs (SinGAN), while retaining the probabilistic framework that enables guidance (CLIP text prompts, symmetry constraints) and coarse-to-fine sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the training step entirely, single-image generative modeling becomes orders of magnitude more practical: the method achieves competitive SIFID and superior diversity to trained baselines with zero optimization, and acceleration techniques (fused attention, latent-space diffusion, ANN) enable gigapixel generation in minutes. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-image priors, real-time interactive generation, and solving inverse problems as an off-the-shelf single-image prior. A limitation is that the method is inherently tied to the patch statistics of one image, so it cannot generate content outside the input's structural vocabulary.
