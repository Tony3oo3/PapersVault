## Problem

Supervised denoising methods require known noise levels to construct noisy-clean training pairs, while existing unsupervised approaches (Noise2Void, Noise2Self, Neighbor2Neighbor) incur irreversible information loss through masking operations. Noisier2Noise, which avoids masking, still demands a known noise level for its additional synthetic noise. The paper addresses the gap of performing high-quality denoising from a single noisy image when the AWGN level is completely unknown and no clean reference is available.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single noisy image (RGB or grayscale) corrupted with zero-mean AWGN of unknown variance as its sole input, requiring no clean reference or prior noise statistics. It first outputs an estimated noise level σ (calibrated via a linear correction), which is then used to synthesize noisy-clean training pairs for a second-stage supervised denoising network that produces the final denoised image. The network architecture (DnCNN-based) explicitly conditions on the synthetic noise variance σ_M² as an input parameter, enabling a single model to handle varying noise levels during the estimation phase.

## Method

The framework has two stages. In stage 1, the authors extend Noisier2Noise by deriving a closed-form expression for the variance of the denoised estimate: Var(X̂) = Var(X) + ((σ_N² − σ_M²)²)/(σ_N² + σ_M²) · σ_N², which is convex in the ratio k = σ_M²/σ_N² and minimized exactly when σ_M² = σ_N². A network f_θ(Z′, σ_M²) is trained to predict Y from Z′ = Y + M using Tweedie's formula to handle the distribution mismatch between original and synthetic noise, and ternary search over σ_M² locates the variance minimum to recover σ_N. A linear calibration σ_N′ = (σ_N − 1.2591)/0.9622 corrects for systematic bias from stochastic variance estimation. In stage 2, the calibrated σ_N is used to generate noisy-clean pairs for standard supervised training of the denoising network, making the final denoising step theoretically equivalent to a known-noise-level supervised setup.

## Results

On the overall RGB evaluation (σ=25/255), the proposed method achieves 31.21 dB PSNR / 0.886 SSIM on CBSD68, 32.11 / 0.879 on Kodak24, 32.26 / 0.887 on McMaster, and 31.41 / 0.912 on Urban100, matching or marginally exceeding the noise-level-known DnCNN-S baseline (e.g., 32.26 vs. 32.25 PSNR on McMaster). For noise-level estimation, the method achieves a mean percentage error (%E) of 0.35% at σ=25/255 on SET12 and 0.90% on BSD68, substantially outperforming Foi et al. (3.83%/3.36%), Pimpalkhute et al. (2.46%/2.37%), and IVHC (6.06%/4.27%). When compared against known-noise-level denoisers (Table 4), the maximum gap is only 0.05 dB PSNR and 0.003 SSIM across all settings, and the method matches Restormer exactly at σ=25/255 on both SET12 and BSD68.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four noise-level-unknown denoising baselines: it gains +0.08 dB PSNR over DnCNN-B (31.21 vs. 31.13 on CBSD68), +0.61 dB over Noise2Void (31.21 vs. 30.60), +1.12 dB over Noise2Self, and +1.07 dB over Neighbor2Neighbor, with consistent SSIM improvements of 0.003–0.034. Against the noise-level-known DnCNN-S, the method ties on CBSD68 (31.21/0.886) and Urban100 (31.41/0.912) while slightly exceeding it on Kodak24 (0.879 vs. 0.878 SSIM) and McMaster (32.26 vs. 32.25 PSNR). For noise-level estimation, the method achieves 0.35% error at σ=25/255 on SET12, beating Foi et al. (3.83%), Pimpalkhute et al. (2.46%), and IVHC (6.06%) by 3–6×. In the known-noise-level comparison (Table 4), the method matches BM3D within 0.01 dB, Noisier2Noise within 0.04 dB, DnCNN-S within 0.05 dB, and Restormer exactly (0.00 dB difference at σ=25/255), confirming that the estimated noise level is sufficient to replicate supervised training performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Noisier2Noise by removing its critical assumption that the noise level is known, deriving a closed-form relationship between the additional synthetic noise variance and the variance of the denoised output that is minimized precisely when the two noise levels match. This theoretical insight enables a ternary-search-based noise estimation strategy that feeds into standard supervised denoising, positioning the work between blind-spot self-supervised methods (Noise2Void, Noise2Self, Neighbor2Neighbor) that sacrifice information through masking, and traditional statistical estimators (Foi, Pimpalkhute, IVHC) that lack precision. The framework departs from universal multi-noise-level models (DnCNN-B, Restormer, MambaIR) by instead recovering the specific noise level to enable noise-level-specific training, which the authors show consistently outperforms the generalist approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that this framework closes the performance gap between noise-level-unknown and noise-level-known denoising (≤0.05 dB PSNR, ≤0.003 SSIM), making supervised-quality denoising accessible in real-world settings where noise characteristics are unavailable. However, the method is constrained to AWGN with zero mean and pixel-wise independence, and the noise estimation stage incurs substantial time complexity due to the ternary search over multiple network evaluations. The heuristic calibration step (σ′ = (σ − 1.2591)/0.9622) raises questions about robustness across diverse imaging pipelines, and extending the framework to non-Gaussian, spatially-correlated, or sensor-specific noise remains an open challenge that the authors acknowledge as future work.
