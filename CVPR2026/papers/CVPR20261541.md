## Problem

Existing unified motion-language models fine-tune LLMs on limited motion-text pairs, causing catastrophic forgetting of language capabilities, and rely on discrete motion tokenization that introduces jitter artifacts or fixed-length generation constraints. The paper asks whether pretrained LLMs can be extended for both motion understanding and autoregressive motion generation of arbitrary length while fully preserving native text-only performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261541/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the model takes a free-form text prompt and produces a streaming 3D human motion sequence (272-dim per frame: root velocity, 6D root rotation, joint positions/velocities/rotations) at ≥30 FPS with no predetermined duration, terminated by a learned exit signal. For understanding, it takes a motion sequence and outputs a natural-language caption. Motion is encoded into a 32-dim continuous latent at 4× temporal downsampling via a causal VAE, and the interleaved input format is [BOS] {Text} [BOM] {Motion} [EOM] {Text} … [EOS].

## Method

LLaMo builds on a Llama decoder-only backbone with a modality-specific Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) design: text and motion tokens pass through separate RMSNorm, QKV, output-projection, and FFN parameters, but share self-attention for cross-modal interaction; all text-side parameters are frozen to preserve language capability. Human motion is encoded by a causal CNN VAE (4× downsampling, 32-dim latent) that uses uniform variance sampling (C_σ=0.01) instead of learned variance to prevent posterior collapse and stabilize autoregressive generation. A lightweight flow-matching head (rectified-flow interpolation) predicts the velocity field for each next motion latent conditioned on the autoregressive hidden state via classifier-free guidance, replacing discrete token sampling with continuous generation. A binary exit head (inspired by TransformerTTS) determines when to stop generation, enabling flexible-length output. Training follows a three-stage recipe—feature alignment, joint AR+flow-matching learning with distinct LR schedules, and motion-head annealing—on 3M+ motion sequences (3,076 hours) combining mocap and HMR-estimated data.

## Results

On HumanML3D text-to-motion generation, LLaMo-3B achieves FID 22.49, R@3 0.839, and diversity 27.58, matching or exceeding MotionMillion-7B (FID 23.58, R@3 0.819) despite HumanML3D comprising <1% of its training data. On motion-to-text captioning, LLaMo-3B achieves a CIDEr score of 100.8, substantially outperforming the best specialist baseline MotionGPT3 (28.7), while maintaining competitive BERTScore (34.8 vs 35.2). The ablation in Table 5 confirms that the MoT design preserves language performance (MMLU 63.4, IFEval 77.4 for LLaMo-3B, identical to the base Llama3.2-3B-Instruct), whereas full fine-tuning without MoT collapses MMLU to 24.9 and IFEval to 22.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the large-scale MotionMillion baselines [12], LLaMo-3B wins on FID (22.49 vs 23.76/23.58), R@2 (0.766 vs 0.749/0.752), R@3 (0.839 vs 0.817/0.819), and diversity (27.58 vs 26.63/26.83), while slightly trailing on MM-D (17.06 vs 16.94/17.00) and R@1 vs the 7B model (0.606 vs 0.616). Against specialist models trained exclusively on HumanML3D, LLaMo-3B is competitive on R-precision (R@3 0.839 vs MotionStreamer's 0.859) but shows a higher FID (22.49 vs 11.79); the authors argue FID is unreliable here due to the semantic distribution gap between HumanML3D and large-scale corpora. On motion-to-text captioning, LLaMo-3B dramatically outperforms MotionGPT3 [94] on CIDEr (100.8 vs 28.7) and BERTScore is comparable (34.8 vs 35.2), though it trails on BLEU@1 (38.3 vs 59.1) and ROUGE (39.9 vs 46.2), which the authors attribute to more diverse phrasing from large-scale training. The MoT ablation (Table 5) shows that without modality-specific parameters, language performance collapses (MMLU drops from 63.4 to 24.9) and motion generation degrades (FID worsens from 19.89 to 46.17), confirming the architectural choice is critical for both tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

LLaMo synthesizes three previously separate research threads: the Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture from LM-Fusion [55] for modality-disentangled parameter separation, the causal VAE motion codec from MotionStreamer [73] for streaming-friendly temporal compression, and the flow-matching head design from continuous-token autoregressive generation [32, 59] for sampling from a dense latent space. Prior unified motion-language models (MotionGPT [23], MotionGPT-2 [68], MotionGPT3 [94]) either discretize motion via vector quantization or fine-tune the full LLM, both of which introduce jitter artifacts or catastrophic forgetting of language. LLaMo's key departure is combining all three components—frozen text MoT branches, a continuous causal VAE, and a flow-matching decoder—within a single decoder-only Llama backbone, enabling the first unified motion-language model that preserves native text performance while supporting real-time streaming generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a pretrained LLM can be extended with high-fidelity motion generation and understanding without degrading its language capabilities, LLaMo establishes a practical template for adding new continuous modalities to foundation LLMs via MoT + flow-matching, a recipe likely to transfer to other modalities (audio, video). The 3M+ sequence / 3076-hour in-house dataset and the zero-shot generalization results (including emergent non-English motion generation) position this as a strong baseline for the emerging motion-language model space. Open questions remain: the doubled parameter count and elevated training cost of MoT, the sensitivity of flow-matching training dynamics to hyperparameters, and the observed asymmetry where scaling improves generation but not understanding all suggest the framework is not yet at its performance ceiling. Future work on motion editing, motion QA, and reinforcement-learning-based post-training could unlock broader downstream utility.
