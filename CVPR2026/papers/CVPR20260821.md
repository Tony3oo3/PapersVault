## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion models produce only a single RGB image, while intrinsic image decomposition methods require a given input image and cannot synthesize new scenes from language. The central challenge for text-to-intrinsic generation is maintaining cross-map structural consistency when each intrinsic map (albedo, irradiance, normal, depth, color) is generated from its own noise sample with only a shared text embedding as coupling—without an image anchor, structures drift and objects may appear in one map but vanish in another.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260821/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text prompt (BLIP-2 captions) as conditioning and jointly outputs five pixel-aligned 512×512 intrinsic maps: RGB color, albedo, irradiance, depth, and surface normal. Although trained exclusively with text conditioning, it supports image-conditioned intrinsic decomposition at inference by keeping one property (e.g., the color image) clean while denoising the remaining maps, effectively switching between generation and decomposition modes within the same forward pass. Each property is assigned an independent diffusion timestep (disentangled timestep sampling), acting as a soft mask that enables flexible partial conditioning.

## Method

LumiX fine-tunes the FLUX.1-dev DiT backbone via flow matching on ~3K Hypersim images, introducing two key mechanisms. First, Query-Broadcast Attention shares the color model's query matrix Q^(c) across all five intrinsic maps in every self-attention block, so all maps attend to the same spatial content while retaining independent per-map key and value projections that encode property-specific appearance. Second, Tensor LoRA represents the joint LoRA update matrix Δ^(M) ∈ R^(Md×Md) as a 4th-order tensor and factorizes it into three low-rank cores (A, B, C) with shared ranks R₁=R₂=8, capturing cross-map interactions at near-linear parameter cost in the number of maps rather than the quadratic cost of a fully fused update. The query projection W_Q is frozen (not fine-tuned) to preserve the diffusion prior, and only K/V projections receive the tensor-structured low-rank updates, yielding ~133M trainable parameters trained for 10K steps with the Prodigy optimizer on four A100 GPUs.

## Results

On the Hypersim test set, LumiX (Query-Broadcast Attention + Tensor LoRA) achieves a cross-map alignment score of 8.30 and an average ImageReward of 0.19 / PickScore of 20.52, outperforming the official IntrinsiX baseline (alignment 6.73, ImageReward −0.41) by 23% in alignment and a 0.60-point margin in preference. On zero-shot albedo decomposition over ARAP, LumiX attains RMSE 0.165 and SSIM 0.753, competitive with dedicated decomposition models while being the only method that also supports text-to-intrinsic generation. On 50 in-the-wild photographs, LumiX scores ImageReward 0.14 and PickScore 20.16, surpassing both RGB↔X and Colorful Shading despite training on only 3K images versus their 900K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary text-to-intrinsic baseline IntrinsiX [23], LumiX wins decisively: alignment improves from 6.73 to 8.30 (+23%) and average ImageReward from −0.41 to +0.19, while also reducing per-block FLOPs from 724.7 G to 145.1 G by replacing Cross-Intrinsic Attention with Query-Broadcast Attention. On ARAP albedo decomposition, LumiX (RMSE 0.165, SSIM 0.753) trails the dedicated Colorful Shading [7] model (0.149 / 0.796) and NIID-Net [34] (0.129 / 0.788), but clearly outperforms RGB↔X [51] (0.238 / 0.564), IntrinsicAnything [11] (0.171 / 0.692), IID [22] (0.160 / 0.738, near-tie on RMSE), Ordinal Shading [6] (0.162 / 0.751, near-tie), Lettry et al. [28] (0.193 / 0.732), and Zhu et al. [59] (0.184 / 0.729). On the in-the-wild evaluation, LumiX (ImageReward 0.14, PickScore 20.16) beats both Colorful Shading (0.06 / 20.03) and RGB↔X (−0.20 / 20.01), confirming that the diffusion-prior-based approach generalizes well despite training on only 3K images. Overall, LumiX is the only method in the comparison that simultaneously supports text-to-intrinsic generation and image-conditioned decomposition, and it leads on all text-conditioned generation metrics while remaining competitive on decomposition quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LoRA framework (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) from a matrix-based to a tensor-based formulation, drawing on tensor-train decomposition ideas to model cross-map relations in a parameter-efficient way. Architecturally, it builds on the content/style separation insight from shared-attention literature (Hertz et al., Chung et al.), repurposing it to broadcast queries across intrinsic maps while keeping keys and values property-specific. It positions itself against IntrinsiX (Kocsis et al., 2025), the first text-to-PBR model, by replacing its expensive Cross-Intrinsic Attention (which concatenates all K/V) with a lighter Query-Broadcast mechanism, and by adding irradiance, depth, and full-color outputs that IntrinsiX does not produce.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LumiX demonstrates that a single diffusion backbone can unify text-to-intrinsic generation and image-conditioned decomposition without any ControlNet-style conditioning, which simplifies the pipeline and opens the door to flexible multi-attribute scene generation. The practical limitation is scope: training on only ~3K Hypersim images and five fixed intrinsic maps means generalization to outdoor scenes, additional PBR channels (roughness, metallic), or 3D-consistent outputs remains untested. An open question is whether the Query-Broadcast mechanism scales gracefully beyond five maps or to higher resolutions without the structural drift the paper identifies as the core challenge.
