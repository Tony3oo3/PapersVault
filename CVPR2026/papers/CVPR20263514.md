## Problem

Text-to-image generative models produce physically implausible refractions and reflections for transparent objects, typically hallucinating content or applying arbitrary small-scale warping rather than following the laws of optics. Refraction is especially challenging because refracted pixel rays intersect surfaces that are also directly visible elsewhere in the image, creating a hard color constraint that generative models have not learned to satisfy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263514/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt describing a scene with a transparent object, a 3D mesh of that object (e.g., from a text-to-3D generator), its material properties (refractive index, absorption), and its pose (placed on a horizontal surface near the optical axis via heuristic). It concurrently generates a perspective image at 720×1280 and an equirectangular panorama at 1024×2048, both conditioned on the prompt, using FLUX-dev flow matching with 20 denoising steps. The method assumes a single non-scattering object with uniform refractive index, and requires a depth map (estimated via MoGe-2) from an initial object-free generation to build the 3D scene geometry for ray tracing.

## Method

Snellcaster is a training-free method that enforces optical physics at every denoising step by synchronizing pixel correspondences across three views: the perspective image, an object-free reference image, and a concurrently generated panorama centered at the transparent object. Pre-computed ray-tracing warping functions (derived from Snell's Law and the scene geometry) map each pixel inside the transparent object to its corresponding background location, and cross-view warping functions link the perspective and panoramic views. At each timestep, Euler estimates of the clean images are decoded to pixel space, warped via Laplacian pyramid warping, and blended using occlusion-masked value-weighted averaging to produce physically consistent refracted and reflected appearances. Refractive and reflective color contributions are combined via Fresnel equations in linear color space before encoding back to latent space for the next denoising step. A post-processing relighting step (FLUX Kontext) adds shadows and specularities to harmonize the inserted object with the scene lighting.

## Results

Across 300 scene-object combinations (10 prompts × 5 variations × 6 objects), Snellcaster achieves a masked PSNR of 16.51 and masked LPIPS of 0.24, substantially outperforming all five baselines on refraction fidelity (next best PSNR is 12.68 from FLUX-dev; next best LPIPS is 0.47 from FLUX Inpaint). On text-alignment and aesthetic metrics, Snellcaster scores 32.85 CLIP and −0.32 ImageReward, which are competitive with but not always the highest among baselines (e.g., Stable Diffusion 3.5 reaches 34.56 CLIP; FLUX.2 reaches 0.18 ImageReward). The authors note that CLIP and ImageReward are not trained to prefer physically correct refractions, so these metrics primarily confirm that the synchronization procedure does not degrade overall image quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Snellcaster decisively wins on both refraction-fidelity metrics: its masked PSNR of 16.51 exceeds the best baseline (FLUX-dev at 12.68) by nearly 4 dB, and its masked LPIPS of 0.24 is roughly half that of every baseline (0.47–0.53). On CLIP score, Snellcaster (32.85) trails Stable Diffusion 3.5 (34.56) and FLUX.2 (33.37) by 1.7–1.7 points, and on ImageReward it is below FLUX.2 (0.18) and SD 3.5 (0.08), though the authors argue these metrics do not capture physical correctness. The FLUX Inpaint baseline, which takes the clean object-free image plus a foreground mask, performs similarly to the other text-to-image models on refraction fidelity (PSNR 12.66, LPIPS 0.47), confirming that standard inpainting does not enforce optical constraints. Overall, the paper's method trades a small amount of text-alignment/aesthetic score for a large gain in physically accurate refraction, which is the central claim of the work.

## Relation to Prior Work

Snellcaster builds directly on the synchronized generation paradigm (SyncTweedies, SyncDiffusion, Visual Anagrams) by averaging Euler estimates across coupled diffusion/flow-matching paths, and on LookingGlass's Laplacian pyramid warping for geometrically consistent view blending. The key departure is replacing the purely geometric warping of anamorphic/mirror tasks with physically-grounded ray tracing governed by Snell's Law and Fresnel equations, and introducing a concurrent panoramic view to resolve occluded or out-of-frame surfaces that a single perspective camera cannot observe. Unlike prior relighting or shadow-generation work, the method is entirely training-free, enforcing optical constraints at every denoising step rather than learning them from data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Transparent objects remain a conspicuous failure mode of state-of-the-art text-to-image models, and this paper demonstrates that a training-free, physics-enforced pipeline can close a large gap (≈4 dB PSNR, halved LPIPS) without degrading text alignment. The panorama-synchronization trick for unseen regions is a generalizable idea that could extend to other view-consistency problems (e.g., video, multi-object scenes). Key limitations—single object, no scattering, uniform refractive index, no absorption—leave substantial room for extension, and the authors identify video generation with optically-accurate refraction as a major open problem where current models fail most visibly.
