## Problem

Existing hierarchical classification methods assume every training image is annotated at all taxonomy levels, but real-world supervision is mixed-granularity: a distant bird may only be labeled 'Bird' while a close-up yields 'Bald eagle.' This paper introduces free-grained hierarchical recognition, where labels can appear at any level of a fixed taxonomy, and shows that state-of-the-art hierarchical classifiers degrade by up to 40% in full-path accuracy under this realistic but previously unexplored setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262225/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image as input and produces a path of class labels through a multi-level taxonomy (e.g., 3 levels for ImageNet-3L, 8 levels for iNat21-mini). At training time, each image carries labels only up to its available depth (coarser labels are always present, finer ones may be missing). At inference, the model performs free-grained prediction by adaptively stopping at the deepest level where its prediction is both confident and taxonomy-consistent, rather than always outputting a full path.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary methods that share a modified hierarchical loss applying supervision only at levels with available labels. Text-Attr uses a frozen VLM (Llama-3.2-11B) to generate free-form image descriptions, encodes them with CLIP's text encoder, and aligns image features to text embeddings via a contrastive loss, inducing 'pseudo-attribute' representations that capture label-independent visual traits shared across hierarchy levels. Taxon-SSL treats missing-level labels as unlabeled data, generates pseudo-labels at every taxonomy level, and enforces cross-level consistency by building an affinity graph where two samples are positive only if their pseudo-labels agree at all levels simultaneously; a contrastive loss then pulls hierarchically consistent pairs together in feature space. The two methods are model-agnostic and can be combined, with Text-Attr excelling under sparse fine-grained labels and Taxon-SSL benefiting from richer per-class supervision.

## Results

On ImageNet-F, Text-Attr (H-CAST) achieves the best Full-Path Accuracy of 63.2%, outperforming the SOTA hierarchical baseline H-CAST (57.6%) by 5.6 points and HRN (37.8%) by 25.4 points. On iNat21-mini-F, Taxon-SSL leads with 31.7% FPA, surpassing H-CAST (25.6%) by 6.1 points and HRN (17.0%) by 14.7 points. The paper also reports that H-CAST's FPA drops from 84.9% to 45.1% on CUB-F and from 64.9% to 25.6% on iNat21-mini-F when transitioning from full to free-grain supervision, highlighting the difficulty of the task. Taxon-SSL reduces TICE (inconsistency error) to 37.3% on iNat21-mini-F versus 47.2% for H-CAST, while Text-Attr (H-CAST) achieves the lowest TICE of 18.6% on ImageNet-F.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two hierarchical baselines: HRN [7] (ResNet-50 backbone) and H-CAST [26] (SOTA, ViT-based). On ImageNet-F, Text-Attr (H-CAST) wins decisively, achieving 63.2% FPA versus H-CAST's 57.6% (+5.6 pts) and HRN's 37.8% (+25.4 pts), while also attaining the lowest TICE (18.6% vs. 21.8% and 46.7%). On iNat21-mini-F, the advantage shifts: Taxon-SSL achieves the best FPA at 31.7%, beating H-CAST by 6.1 points and HRN by 14.7 points, and substantially reducing TICE (37.3% vs. 47.2% and 53.8%). Text-Attr (H-CAST) still outperforms both baselines on iNat21-mini-F (29.7% FPA) but trails Taxon-SSL, which the authors attribute to Taxon-SSL's ability to propagate structural consistency across levels when per-class supervision is limited. The paper notes that the two proposed methods are complementary: Text-Attr excels under sparse fine-grained labels (leveraging textual semantics), while Taxon-SSL benefits from richer per-class data (exploiting visual consistency), and their combination yields consistent but modest additional gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semi-supervised hierarchical learning line initiated by CHMatch (Wu et al., CVPR 2023), which handled only a two-level coarse-fine setting, to arbitrary multi-level taxonomies via taxonomy-aligned contrastive learning (Taxon-SSL). It also builds on H-CAST (Park et al., ICLR 2025) by adapting its visually consistent grouping to the free-grain setting and layering text-guided pseudo-attribute learning (Text-Attr) on top. Conceptually, the paper departs from all prior hierarchical classifiers—which assume complete supervision at every level—and from single-granularity weakly/semi-supervised methods by jointly addressing mixed-granularity labels, class and level imbalance, and hierarchical consistency within one unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a significant practical gap: SOTA hierarchical methods lose up to 40% full-path accuracy when supervision becomes mixed-granularity, a condition that mirrors real-world annotation pipelines. The introduced benchmarks (especially ImageNet-3L and ImageNet-F) provide a much-needed large-scale, diverse testbed beyond the small CUB/Aircraft datasets, and the complementary behavior of Text-Attr (better under label scarcity) versus Taxon-SSL (better with more data) offers practitioners a clear decision heuristic. Open questions remain around explicitly modeling class- and level-wise imbalance, improving the CLIP-based pruning proxy for true visual ambiguity, and scaling the free-grained inference strategies to deeper or irregular taxonomies.
