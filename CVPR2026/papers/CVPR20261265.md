## Problem

Existing geometric obfuscation methods for privacy-preserving visual localization (e.g., random lines, coordinate permutation) are vulnerable to a recent neighborhood-based geometry-recovery attack that exploits the spatial distribution of obfuscated lines surrounding each original keypoint. The fundamental limitation is that uniformly distributed obfuscated lines allow an attacker to recover the true keypoint location by minimizing distance to neighboring obfuscations, subsequently enabling full image inversion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261265/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The client extracts keypoints (SuperPoint or SIFT) from a query image and replaces each 2D keypoint with a line connecting it to one of two fixed anchors, transmitting only these obfuscated lines plus descriptors to the server. The server matches descriptors against a pre-built 3D point map (triangulated via HLoc) and estimates the 6-DoF camera pose using the 16P minimal solver within a LORANSAC loop, followed by Levenberg-Marquardt refinement. The obfuscation is entirely client-side with negligible computational overhead, and the server never receives raw keypoint positions.

## Method

Dual Convergent Lines (DCL) partitions the image into two vertical regions separated by a central midline and places two fixed anchors at the top and bottom of that midline. Each keypoint is lifted to a line connecting it to the anchor in its assigned region, so that neighboring lines either all converge to the same anchor (yielding a trivial collapsed solution at the anchor) or become near-parallel across the partition boundary (yielding an ill-posed, high-variance intersection). The paper proves via Proposition 1 that the recovered point parameter is a weighted average of individual intersection offsets with weights proportional to sin²(θ) between lines, which becomes numerically unstable as lines approach parallelism. For pose estimation, DCL reuses the 16P solver with a simple degeneracy check that ensures the minimal set includes lines from both anchors, preventing rank-deficient normal matrices during RANSAC sampling.

## Results

On privacy robustness (Table 2), DCL achieves a mean keypoint recovery error of 330.4 px on 7Scenes (vs. 6.14 px for Random Lines and 10.56 px for Coordinate Permutation), with reconstructed image PSNR of 7.04 dB and LPIPS of 0.754, indicating near-complete failure of the inversion attack. On localization (Table 5, 7Scenes), DCL achieves median position errors of 1.0–26.3 cm and recall at 5 cm/5° of 13–97% across scenes, with real-time inference (~4 ms). On Aachen Day-Night (Table 4), DCL achieves 41.0% recall at 0.25 m/2° in Day and 13.3% in Night, outperforming learning-based methods ACE (6.9%/0.0%) and GLACE (8.6%/1.0%) in the challenging Night setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Random Lines [47], DCL sacrifices localization accuracy (e.g., 1.0 cm vs. 0.5 cm median position error on Chess, 97% vs. 100% recall) but is the only method resilient to the geometry-recovery attack, achieving 330.4 px recovery error vs. 6.14 px. Against Coordinate Permutation [30], DCL provides substantially stronger privacy (PSNR 7.04 vs. 13.36 dB, LPIPS 0.754 vs. 0.643 on 7Scenes). Against descriptor-free methods GoMatch [53] and DGC-GNN [50], DCL achieves 3–16× lower position errors (e.g., 1.0 cm vs. 4 cm on Chess) while remaining real-time. Against segmentation-based GSFF Privacy [32], DCL matches or slightly exceeds accuracy (e.g., 1.0 vs. 0.8 cm on Chess) while running in ~4 ms versus up to 45 s. Against structure-based methods (HLoc [40], DSAC* [2,3], ACE [4], GLACE [49]), DCL has 2–3× higher position errors but is the only approach that preserves privacy against all three attack classes [6, 33, 36]. On Aachen Night, DCL (13.3% recall at 0.25 m/2°) outperforms ACE (0.0%) and GLACE (1.0%), demonstrating robustness under appearance change.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 2D random line obfuscation and 16P minimal solver framework from Speciale et al. [47], but departs from it by identifying a fundamental geometric vulnerability: the uniform spatial distribution of random lines around each keypoint enables neighborhood-based recovery attacks [6]. The dual-anchor construction is conceptually related to RayCloud [25], which also uses dual-anchor line lifting, but DCL replaces learned K-means anchor placement with strategically fixed anchors at the image midline, specifically to induce ill-posed optimization in the attacker's recovery problem. The paper positions DCL as the only geometric obfuscation method resilient to the geometry-recovery attack while remaining compatible with traditional RANSAC-based localization pipelines, in contrast to segmentation-based approaches that sacrifice speed and pipeline compatibility.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a recently demonstrated vulnerability in geometry-based privacy-preserving localization, which was previously considered a practical and efficient solution for cloud-based visual localization. The theoretical analysis (Proposition 1, Corollary 1.1) providing a clear geometric explanation for why the attack fails adds rigor beyond empirical validation. However, DCL incurs a notable localization accuracy cost compared to Random Lines (e.g., roughly 2× median position error on 7Scenes), and a rare degenerate case arises when all keypoints fall in a single partition region. Open questions include whether adaptive or structure-aware partitions can mitigate this degeneracy, and whether the dual-anchor principle generalizes to other obfuscation geometries or higher-dimensional lifting schemes.
