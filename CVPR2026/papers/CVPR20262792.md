## Problem

Most document QA approaches treat the task as pure text generation and ignore where answers are grounded on the page, weakening reliability in domains like healthcare, law, and finance. Existing grounding methods produce coarse bounding boxes that fail to capture curved or irregular text and often include irrelevant background, resulting in ambiguous evidence localization. No prior work supports multi-span grounding (evidence from multiple spatially distinct regions) or multi-granular grounding (phrase, line, block levels) with pixel-level precision.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single-page document image and a free-form text question as input. It autoregressively generates an answer string in which each grounded span is immediately followed by a [GROUND] token; each such token triggers the segmentation head to emit three pixel-level binary masks at phrase, line, and block granularities. An answer may contain multiple [GROUND] tokens, enabling multi-span grounding where different parts of the answer are linked to spatially distinct evidence regions. For chart documents, only a single granularity level is produced since charts lack the phrase/line/block hierarchy of text documents.

## Method

M3Grounder decouples language modeling from spatial grounding by coupling a VLM backbone (InternVL3.5-8B or Qwen3-VL-8B) with a SAM promptable segmentation module. The VLM generates the answer interleaved with [GROUND] tokens; each token's final hidden state is projected through three granularity-specific MLP heads to produce phrase, line, and block prompt embeddings, which are decoded by SAM's mask decoder together with shared dense image features to yield hierarchical masks. Training jointly optimizes four loss terms: standard cross-entropy for text generation, Dice + BCE for mask accuracy, a bleed-suppression loss that penalizes predicted mask pixels falling outside the union of all text regions, and a hierarchical enclosure loss that enforces nested containment (phrase ⊂ line ⊂ block) across granularities. The granularity-specific MLP design and the two auxiliary losses are validated by ablations showing consistent gains over a shared-MLP baseline and over training without the auxiliary terms.

## Results

M3Grounder-Q (8B) achieves state-of-the-art grounding F1 of 81.4 on BoundingDocs-Test, 73.3 on DOGR-Bench, and 79.0 on GroundingDocQA-Bench, with answer quality (G-Eval) of 88.8, 80.8, and 80.1 respectively. On MMDocBench, it reaches 68.2 IoU for region alignment and 73.3 EM for answer correctness. The curved/skewed-text (CS) split of GroundingDocQA-Bench shows the largest margin: M3Grounder-Q scores 85.3 F1 versus 4.8 for GPT-5 and 32.1 for Gemini-2.5-Pro, demonstrating the advantage of mask-based over box-based localization for irregular geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

M3Grounder-Q outperforms all commercial VLMs by large margins: on GroundingDocQA-Bench it achieves 79.0 F1g versus 43.4 for Gemini-2.5-Pro (+35.6) and 4.5 for GPT-5 (+74.5), and on BoundingDocs-Test it reaches 81.4 F1g versus 70.0 for Gemini-2.5-Pro (+11.4). Against the dedicated grounding model DOGR (same 8B scale), M3Grounder-Q improves DOGR-Bench F1g from 66.4 to 73.3 (+6.9). Compared to fine-tuned versions of its own backbones (Qwen3-VL ft. and InternVL3.5 ft.), M3Grounder-Q gains +18.4 F1g on GroundingDocQA-Bench and +37.5 on DOGR-Bench over Qwen3-VL ft., confirming that the dedicated segmentation head and hierarchical mask supervision provide substantial benefits beyond what box-level fine-tuning achieves. Among open-source VLMs evaluated zero-shot, M3Grounder-Q dominates across all four benchmarks, with the largest gaps on curved-text grounding (85.3 CS F1 vs. 11.6 for Qwen3-VL). On general document QA (Table 3), M3Grounder-Q remains competitive with Qwen3-VL (92.1 vs. 96.1 DocVQA, 79.6 vs. 83.1 InfographicVQA), showing that the grounding objective does not substantially degrade standard QA ability.

## Relation to Prior Work

M3Grounder departs from the dominant box-based grounding paradigm in document QA (DOGR, BoundingDocs) by reformulating evidence localization as pixel-level segmentation, building on SAM's promptable architecture for the spatial head. It couples this with a VLM backbone (InternVL3.5 or Qwen3-VL) that autoregressively generates answers interleaved with [GROUND] tokens, decoupling language modeling from spatial prediction—a separation not present in prior joint box-and-text models. The hierarchical enclosure loss and bleed-suppression loss are novel training objectives that enforce multi-granular spatial consistency (phrase ⊂ line ⊂ block) and prevent mask spillover, respectively. The data engine's use of REPLICA to produce layout-aware Fid-HTML for traceable, multi-granular mask generation represents a methodological departure from OCR-span-matching pipelines used by DOGR and BoundingDocs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By shifting from coarse bounding boxes to hierarchical, pixel-level masks, M3Grounder addresses a concrete failure mode (curved and skewed text) that box-based methods cannot resolve, and the 200K-document / 2M-QA-pair GroundingDocQA dataset plus the human-verified GroundingDocQA-Bench provide the first large-scale supervision and evaluation infrastructure for multi-granular, mask-based document grounding. The consistent performance gains across four benchmarks and the ablation evidence for each loss component make the approach practically adoptable. Key open questions remain: the formulation is single-page only, mask prediction increases inference cost relative to box regression, and extending hierarchical grounding to multi-page or long-context documents is left as future work.
