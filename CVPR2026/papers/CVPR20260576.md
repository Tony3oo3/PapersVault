## Problem

Existing histology-conditioned generative models for spatial transcriptomics (e.g., Stem, STFlow) omit explicit modeling of gene-gene dependencies such as regulatory and co-expression patterns, undermining biological coherence of predictions. Single-cell foundation models (sc-FMs) like CellFM capture these dependencies through large-scale masked autoencoding pre-training, but they lack a visual pathway and were trained with an objective fundamentally different from diffusion-style denoising, making direct transfer to histology-conditioned expression generation nontrivial.

## Input / Output

The method takes an H&E histology image patch per spatial spot as input, encoded by a frozen pathology foundation model (UNI, CONCH, or their concatenation) into a global condition vector. It produces a gene expression vector over the Highly Mean-High Variance Gene (HMHVG) set (top 300 genes by mean and variance, intersected with CellFM's 24,078-gene vocabulary), with all values in log-scale. Because HINGE is a conditional generator, repeated sampling from the reverse process yields diverse yet histology-consistent expression profiles rather than a single deterministic prediction.

## Method

HINGE retrofits the frozen CellFM backbone into a conditional denoising network by inserting identity-initialized SoftAdaLN modulators before each attention and feed-forward sub-layer, which inject a shared histology+timestep condition embedding via SoftNorm (interpolated normalization) followed by learned scale/shift and a gated residual merge. To align the generative objective with CellFM's masked autoencoding pre-training, HINGE replaces standard DDPM Gaussian corruption with a stochastic masking process: genes are progressively set to zero following a power-schedule visibility curve, and the loss is computed only over masked entries, so the network always sees partially observed inputs and predicts only the hidden components. A warm-start curriculum further stabilizes early training by sampling only low-mask timesteps (matching CellFM's ~20% mask ratio) before expanding to the full timestep range. During fine-tuning, only the SoftAdaLN modulation parameters and the condition projection are updated; all CellFM weights and the image encoder remain frozen, preserving learned gene-gene relationships while gradually incorporating histology context.

## Results

HINGE achieves the best PCC-50 and PCC-200 across all three ST datasets: 0.705 / 0.613 on cSCC, 0.566 / 0.446 on Her2ST, and 0.428 / 0.309 on Kidney. On cSCC it improves PCC-50 by ~4% over the strongest generative baseline STFlow (0.678) and by ~3.2% over the strongest discriminative baseline TRIPLEX (0.683). On Kidney, HINGE's MSE (1.459) is slightly higher than STFlow's (1.402), but its correlation metrics remain the best, indicating the advantage is concentrated in gene-level correlation fidelity rather than absolute error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HINGE outperforms all six baselines on PCC-50 and PCC-200 across all three datasets. Against the strongest discriminative baseline TRIPLEX, HINGE gains ~3.2–4.4% in PCC-50 and ~3.3–4.3% in PCC-200; against the strongest generative baselines (STFlow on cSCC/Kidney, Stem on Her2ST), gains are ~1.3–4.4% in PCC-50 and ~3.0–6.0% in PCC-200. On cSCC, HINGE also achieves the lowest MSE (0.887) and MAE (0.703), beating even TRIPLEX (MSE 0.904). On Kidney, HINGE's MSE (1.459) and MAE (0.964) are slightly worse than TRIPLEX (1.315 / 0.915) and STFlow (1.402 / 0.929), so its advantage there is confined to correlation metrics. Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests confirm statistically significant improvements (p < 0.05) over multiple baselines, particularly on cSCC, and HINGE surpasses all baselines on the majority of individual slices rather than relying on a few outliers.

## Relation to Prior Work

HINGE sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: histology-conditioned spatial expression generation (Stem, STFlow) and single-cell foundation model transfer (scGPT-spatial, Nicheformer, SToFM). It departs from prior generative ST methods by explicitly leveraging gene-gene dependencies encoded in a pre-trained sc-FM (CellFM) rather than learning them from limited ST data, and departs from prior sc-FM transfer work by introducing a visual conditioning pathway (SoftAdaLN) and a masked diffusion objective that aligns with the sc-FM's masked autoencoding pre-training. The key conceptual move is treating the sc-FM backbone as a frozen, gene-relationship-preserving prior and injecting histology context through lightweight, identity-initialized modulators rather than fine-tuning the backbone itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides a practical, architecture-agnostic recipe for adapting any masked-autoencoding sc-FM to histology-conditioned generation, which could accelerate the use of increasingly large single-cell pre-training corpora for spatial biology. The identity-initialized SoftAdaLN design is a reusable pattern for cross-modal conditioning of frozen backbones beyond ST. Limitations include evaluation on only three datasets with modest gene sets (~300 genes), reliance on a single sc-FM (CellFM), and mixed results on the Kidney dataset where MSE/MAE do not clearly beat discriminative baselines. Open questions include whether the frozen-backbone strategy remains optimal as ST datasets grow, whether the approach extends to larger gene vocabularies or multi-tissue settings, and whether the masked diffusion process is truly necessary versus simpler corruption schemes at scale.
