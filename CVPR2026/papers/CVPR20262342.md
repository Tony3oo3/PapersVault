## Problem

Vision-language models like CLIP provide strong global semantic understanding but suffer from coarse spatial localization and semantic ambiguity when applied to dense prediction over large vocabularies. Existing methods address either spatial refinement or category disambiguation in isolation, leaving the interdependence between coarse localization and overlapping activations among similar categories unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262342/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes an RGB image (resized to 336px shorter side for VOC/Context, 448px for ADE20K/Cityscapes/COCO-Stuff) and a vocabulary of C text category descriptions (80 prompt templates per category) as input, and produces a dense pixel-level segmentation map assigning each pixel to one of the C categories. It is training-free and plugs into existing baselines (SCLIP, ProxyCLIP, CorrCLIP) as a post-processing module, requiring only the baseline's feature extraction pipeline plus CLIP and CLIPSeg as auxiliary models.

## Method

S2C2Seg is a two-stage training-free framework. Stage 1 (Category Subset Selection) filters the full vocabulary to a compact subset of 6–20 categories via multi-aspect scoring that jointly considers CLIP global semantic similarity, CLIPSeg local spatial activation, and cross-view consistency measured by conditional entropy, using a union-based recall-maximizing selection followed by min-max normalized reranking. Stage 2 (Consistent Semantic Guidance) fuses CLIP patch-text similarity maps (upsampled to full resolution) with CLIPSeg pixel-level predictions through category-specific confidence weights derived from temperature-scaled softmax of spatially-averaged global scores, applying the fusion as S_fused = S_glob + λ·w·S_spat so that categories with stronger global semantic evidence receive higher local feature integration while weak categories are down-weighted to suppress noise.

## Results

S2C2Seg, a training-free framework, achieves a new state-of-the-art average mIoU of 51.2% when integrated with CorrCLIP, surpassing the previous best (Trident at 45.8%) by 5.4 points. When integrated with SCLIP and ProxyCLIP, it yields improvements of +9.7 and +6.8 percentage points respectively, reaching 47.9% and 49.1% average mIoU. The largest per-dataset gain is +11.5 mIoU on PASCAL VOC21 (75.7% vs. 71.9% for CorrCLIP), where CSG corrects background interference. Results are consistent across all eight benchmarks (VOC20/21, Context59/60, COCO-Obj, COCO-Stuff, ADE20K, Cityscapes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2C2Seg is a plug-and-play, training-free module that integrates with existing OVSS baselines rather than replacing them. Integrated with SCLIP (attention refinement), it improves average mIoU from 38.2% to 47.9% (+9.7 pp); with ProxyCLIP (self-supervised proxy attention), from 42.3% to 49.1% (+6.8 pp); and with CorrCLIP (current SOTA), from 47.8% to 51.2% (+3.4 pp). The improvement inversely correlates with baseline sophistication, confirming that CSS and CSG address residual weaknesses in each method. Against all other training-free and training-based methods in Table 1 (Trident 45.8%, CASS 44.4%, SCCLIP 43.9%, ResCLIP 41.4%), S2C2Seg+CorrCLIP leads by at least 5.4 points. On the most challenging benchmarks—Context59 (50.8% vs. 46.3% for CorrCLIP) and Cityscapes (47.0% vs. 42.0%)—the gains are largest, indicating that category disambiguation and global-local fusion are most beneficial when vocabulary size or scene complexity is high.

## Relation to Prior Work

S2C2Seg positions itself at the intersection of two established but previously separate research threads in open-vocabulary segmentation: spatial refinement (attention surgery, feature denoising, DINO/diffusion integration) and semantic disambiguation (similarity-based category ranking as in CaR, FLOSS, CDAM). Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes two pretrained models—CLIP for global semantics and CLIPSeg for local spatial predictions—and introduces a training-free orchestration layer (CSS + CSG) that jointly addresses the spatial and semantic challenges that prior methods tackle in isolation. The key conceptual departure is the argument that coarse localization and category overlap are interdependent, so treating them independently (as all prior methods do) leaves systematic error uncorrected.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, training-free design makes S2C2Seg immediately applicable to any existing OVSS pipeline, and the consistent gains across three architecturally distinct baselines (SCLIP, ProxyCLIP, CorrCLIP) demonstrate broad utility. However, the oracle gap (51.2% vs. 61.9% mIoU) reveals that category selection remains a substantial bottleneck, and the diminishing returns on stronger baselines (+9.7 → +3.4) suggest the framework's ceiling is constrained by the quality of the underlying dense predictor. Open questions include whether the two-stage filtering can be made more principled (currently heuristic thresholds), whether the approach transfers to non-CLIP VLMs, and whether the fixed K_min/K_max bounds generalize to extremely large or extremely small vocabularies.
