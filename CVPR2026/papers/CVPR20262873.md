## Problem

Autoregressive mesh generation flattens meshes into long vertex-coordinate sequences, incurring O(N²) self-attention cost that makes high-fidelity generation computationally prohibitive. Prior compression strategies (graph traversal, block indexing) reduce sequence length but introduce brittleness or vocabulary explosion without addressing the root cause: operating at the vertex level rather than the face level, which is the natural semantic unit of a mesh.

## Input / Output

For reconstruction, the model takes a point cloud of 8192 surface points with normals (65,536 for the large model) and outputs a triangle mesh with vertex coordinates quantized to integers in [0,127] (base) or [0,1023] (large), with meshes constrained to fewer than 4,000 faces. For image-to-mesh generation, a single RGB image is encoded by DINOv3 to condition a diffusion model that produces a latent VecSet, which the frozen FACE decoder then translates into a mesh. Faces are emitted in a deterministic ZYX lexicographical order of their minimum-coordinate vertex, and each face is a 9-element quantized coordinate vector.

## Method

FACE is an Autoregressive Autoencoder (ARAE) whose core innovation is a 'one-face-one-token' strategy: each triangle face (a 9D coordinate vector) is projected by a lightweight MLP into a single d_model-dimensional token, reducing the Transformer sequence length by 9× and yielding a state-of-the-art compression ratio of 0.11. The encoder uses the 3DShape2VecSet architecture (FPS queries + cross-attention + Transformer stack) to produce a 2048-token VecSet latent, while the 24-layer decoder performs causal self-attention over face tokens and cross-attention to the VecSet at every layer. A CausalMLP head introduces a second level of autoregression within each face, predicting the 9 quantized coordinates sequentially rather than in parallel. The entire system is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy loss on quantized coordinates. For image-to-mesh, a 350M-parameter DiT trained with flow matching generates VecSet latents conditioned on DINOv3 image features, which are decoded by the frozen FACE decoder without fine-tuning.

## Results

FACE achieves state-of-the-art mesh reconstruction across all three evaluation datasets, with the largest gains on the challenging Famous benchmark (HD 0.077, CD 0.049) where it outperforms the best baseline BPT by 46% in Hausdorff Distance. On Toys4K, FACE reaches HD 0.067 and CD 0.033, improving over BPT (HD 0.091, CD 0.037) by 26% and 11% respectively. In addition to reconstruction quality, FACE attains a compression ratio of 0.11 (sequence length relative to one-token-per-coordinate), halving the previous best of 0.22 and yielding a theoretical 81× reduction in self-attention FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FACE outperforms all 11 compared baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest reconstruction baseline BPT [44], FACE reduces Hausdorff Distance by 26–46% and Chamfer Distance by 5–20% across Objaverse, Toys4K, and Famous. TreeMeshGPT [21] and Mesh Silksong [36] share the previous best compression ratio of 0.22, but FACE halves this to 0.11 while simultaneously achieving the best reconstruction quality (e.g., HD 0.067 vs. 0.133 on Toys4K). MeshAnything [6] and MeshAnything V2 [7] use the uncompressed one-token-per-coordinate scheme (ratio 1.00/0.46) and are the weakest on reconstruction, with Famous HD of 0.399 and 0.488 respectively versus FACE's 0.077. MeshGPT [34] and PivotMesh [43] (ratio 0.67) and EdgeRunner [38] (ratio 0.47) are intermediate in efficiency but are not directly compared on reconstruction quality in the main table. For image-conditioned generation, FACE qualitatively surpasses EdgeRunner [38] in detail fidelity and topological completeness, successfully reconstructing fine structures (e.g., a Lego figure's hand, a bird's eye) where EdgeRunner produces disconnected fragments. DeepMesh [53] (ratio 0.28) and Nautilus [40] (ratio 0.27) are competitive in compression but still trail FACE by 2× in sequence length and are not included in the reconstruction quality comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

FACE builds directly on the Autoregressive Autoencoder (ARAE) paradigm from EdgeRunner and the VecSet encoder from 3DShape2VecSet, while adopting the CausalMLP coordinate-decoding head from TreeMeshGPT. Its key departure is elevating the autoregressive generation unit from individual vertex coordinates (9 tokens per face, as in MeshGPT, MeshXL, MeshAnything, and all prior AR mesh methods) to a single face-level token. This 'one-face-one-token' idea had been explored in diffusion-based settings (PolyDiff, MeshCraft) but, as the authors note, had not been successfully realized within an autoregressive framework, which naturally handles variable-length sequences and sequential dependencies. The paper positions this as addressing the root cause (wrong semantic level) rather than the symptom (long sequences) that prior compression strategies—traversal-based, block-indexing, or architectural—attempted to mitigate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2× compression improvement (0.11 vs. 0.22) translates to an 81× theoretical reduction in self-attention compute, substantially lowering the barrier to generating high-resolution meshes with thousands of faces. The demonstration that the learned latent space supports high-fidelity image-to-mesh generation via a DiT without decoder fine-tuning validates the representation's semantic richness and suggests FACE could serve as a foundational component for multi-modal 3D generation pipelines. Open questions include whether the discrete quantization (128 or 1024 levels) becomes a hard ceiling for very fine geometric detail, and whether the point-cloud input bottleneck (inadequate sampling of thin structures like bicycle spokes) can be addressed without abandoning the current encoder design.
