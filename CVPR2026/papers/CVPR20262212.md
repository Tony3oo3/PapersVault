## Problem

Existing image editing benchmarks rely on a single image paired with an explicit instruction that directly specifies concrete object modifications, requiring minimal cognitive or creative ability. They fail to cover the full depth of the editing pipeline (awareness, interpretation, imagination) and lack assessment of metacognitive knowledge, leaving the community without a holistic measure of cognition- and creativity-informed image editing capabilities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262212/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each test case provides one or more RGB input images (26% are multi-image) alongside a free-form text editing instruction in both English and Chinese. Multi-image cases use ordinal terms (e.g., 'the first image') to reference specific inputs, where images serve distinct roles as either editing targets or attribute/object references that the model must infer from the instruction. The output is a single edited image, evaluated on a 1–10 scale across five metrics.

## Method

WiseEdit decomposes image editing into three cascaded cognitive steps—Awareness (locating the modification target without explicit spatial clues), Interpretation (parsing implicit instructions into executable visual changes via world knowledge), and Imagination (executing creative, subject-driven generation)—plus a WiseEdit-Complex task where none of the three steps can be trivially completed. The 1,220 test cases are structured around three knowledge types (Declarative, Procedural, Metacognitive) spanning natural science, cultural common sense, and spatio-temporal-logic domains. Evaluation employs GPT-4o as an automated judge with five metrics: Instruction Following, Detail Preserving, Visual Quality, Knowledge Fidelity (verifying correct knowledge application via provided hints), and Creative Fusion (quantifying imaginative depth), with the latter two applied selectively per task type. The benchmark evaluates 21 models (17 open-source, 4 closed-source) across both language versions, revealing that closed-source models outperform all open-source models by ~15 points and that knowledge-based reasoning and compositional creativity remain key bottlenecks.

## Results

On WiseEdit (English), the best closed-source model Nano Banana Pro achieves an overall score of 82.4, followed by GPT-image-1 (77.6) and Seedream 4.0 (75.2). Among open-source models, DreamOmni2 leads with 60.6 overall, closely followed by Qwen-Image-Edit (60.2) and Bagel (60.0), while the best diffusion-based model FLUX.2 Dev reaches 61.8. On the harder WiseEdit-Complex subset, Nano Banana Pro scores 77.1 versus 54.8 for the best open-source model (Uni-CoT), and all models show a notable performance drop compared to the three individual tasks, with Creative Fusion (CF) remaining below 57 for every model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All four closed-source models (Nano Banana Pro 82.4, GPT-image-1 77.6, Seedream 4.0 75.2, Nano Banana 75.0) outperform every open-source model by at least 15 points on the overall metric, with Nano Banana Pro leading the best open-source DreamOmni2 (60.6) by 21.8 points. Among open-source models, unified comprehension-generation architectures (Bagel 60.0, Qwen-Image-Edit 60.2, Uni-CoT 60.1, DreamOmni2 60.6) consistently surpass purely diffusion-based models, with FLUX.2 Dev (61.8) being the notable exception that rivals the top unified models. On Knowledge Fidelity, Nano Banana Pro reaches 91.4 (Awareness) and 75.8 (Interpretation), while most open-source models stay below 55, indicating a persistent gap in applying world knowledge. On Creative Fusion, all models score below 57, with Nano Banana Pro at 51.5 and open-source models ranging from 22–43, confirming that compositional creativity remains unsolved across the board. On WiseEdit-Complex, the hierarchy persists: Nano Banana Pro (77.1) > GPT-image-1 (71.2) ≈ Seedream 4.0 (71.4) > Nano Banana (68.1) > best open-source Uni-CoT (54.8), and all models show a 10–20 point drop relative to their individual-task averages, revealing that combining knowledge reasoning with creative execution is the current bottleneck.

## Relation to Prior Work

WiseEdit extends the instruction-based image editing benchmarking line (MagicBrush, InstructPix2Pix, UltraEdit) and the more recent knowledge-informed editing benchmarks (KrisBench, RiseBench, IntelligentBench) by introducing a three-step cognitive decomposition—Awareness, Interpretation, Imagination—drawn from cognitive science (Krathwohl's taxonomy, Sweller's creativity framework). Unlike prior benchmarks that focus predominantly on the interpretation phase with single-image inputs, WiseEdit adds task depth across all three steps, incorporates metacognitive knowledge assessment, supports multi-image inputs, and provides bilingual (English/Chinese) evaluation. The paper positions itself as the first benchmark to holistically evaluate both knowledge-based cognitive reasoning and compositional creative generation in a unified framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WiseEdit reveals that even the strongest closed-source models (Nano Banana Pro, GPT-image-1) score below 85 on the overall metric, and all models fail on logic-requiring tasks, establishing a clear capability boundary for the field. The finding that models achieve high Knowledge Fidelity and Creative Fusion on complex tasks yet suffer significant Instruction Following drops ('powerless in execution') suggests a critical disconnect between reasoning and generation that current architectures do not resolve. Multi-image inputs cause substantial performance degradation, and metacognitive knowledge cases remain notably weaker than declarative and procedural ones, pointing to concrete directions for future research. As a 1,220-case bilingual benchmark evaluating 21 models with novel metrics (Knowledge Fidelity, Creative Fusion), WiseEdit is likely to become a standard reference for assessing next-generation image editing systems.
