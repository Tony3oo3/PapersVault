## Problem

Multi-stage 3D generative pipelines (e.g., Trellis) contain non-differentiable operations between stages, which severs the gradient path and prevents preference signals applied to the final output from reaching earlier stages that determine geometry and structure. Applying DPO only to the final stage leaves critical early-stage generation unaligned, while independently aligning each stage introduces texture-geometry inconsistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261457/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces a 3D asset decodable into Gaussian splats, meshes, or radiance fields via Trellis's two-stage pipeline: a sparse voxel structure (Stage 1) followed by structured latent variables (Stage 2). Preference supervision is drawn from the DreamReward dataset (1,000 prompts × 9 human-ranked 3D assets), and evaluation uses a 100-prompt test set combining 3DRewardDB prompts with Gemini-generated novel prompts, scored by ImageReward, Hpsv2, Reward3D, and CLIP Score.

## Method

Circular-DPO constructs a preference feedback loop that bridges the non-differentiable gap between Trellis's two Rectified Flow Transformer stages. First, DPO is applied to the final structured-latent stage (G_L) using human preference pairs. Second, the optimized and original models each generate assets per prompt, and the resulting (optimized, unoptimized) pairs are re-encoded through voxelization and the VAE encoder into the sparse domain. Third, these newly formed sparse preference pairs are used to train the preceding sparse-structure stage (G_S) via the same Flow-DPO objective, effectively forwarding the preference signal that gradients cannot carry. A quality-aware weighting mechanism further improves robustness: w₁ computes a reward-model-based quality gap score to discard unreliable pairs, and w₂ applies a normalized exponentiated log-likelihood weight to down-weight pairs with low preference confidence.

## Results

On a 100-prompt test set, Circular-DPO applied to Trellis (OURS-Trellis) achieves ImageReward of −0.3146, Hpsv2 of 0.2024, Reward3D of −0.5607, and CLIP Score of 0.3290, improving over the unaligned Trellis baseline by 35.15% on ImageReward and 21.44% on Reward3D. Applied to MVDream (OURS-MVDream), the method reaches ImageReward −0.3747 and Reward3D −0.1047. The method outperforms all 3D-native baselines (Ln3diff, GaussianCube, Hunyuan3D, LGM, Latent-NeRF) across all four metrics, though it does not surpass score-distillation-based approaches like DreamReward (IR −0.1930, R3D 0.2733) or DreamDPO (R3D −0.1767) on the strongest preference-alignment metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the direct Trellis baseline, Circular-DPO improves ImageReward from −0.4596 to −0.3146 (+35.15%) and Reward3D from −0.7137 to −0.5607 (+21.44%), while also gaining on Hpsv2 (0.1991→0.2024) and CLIP (0.3183→0.3290). Among 3D-native methods, OURS-Trellis dominates all four metrics over Ln3diff, GaussianCube, Hunyuan3D, LGM, and Latent-NeRF, with the largest gaps on Reward3D (e.g., −0.5607 vs. −2.9517 for Latent-NeRF). Against the 2D-supervised MVDream baseline, OURS-MVDream wins on ImageReward (−0.3747 vs. −0.3948) and Hpsv2 (0.2030 vs. 0.2013) but loses on Reward3D (−0.1047 vs. −0.4292). The method trails the score-distillation-based DreamReward on both ImageReward (−0.3146 vs. −0.1930) and Reward3D (−0.5607 vs. 0.2733), and trails DreamDPO on Reward3D (−0.5607 vs. −0.1767), though it edges out DreamDPO on ImageReward (−0.3146 vs. −0.3774) and CLIP (0.3290 vs. 0.3234). The authors acknowledge this gap to score-distillation methods but note the trade-off in training efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO-based preference alignment paradigm (Rafailov et al., 2023; Jiao et al., 2024) from single-stage or final-output-only settings to multi-stage 3D generative pipelines, specifically Trellis's two-stage sparse-structure-then-detail architecture. Prior 3D preference alignment work (DreamReward, DreamDPO) optimizes only the final output, leaving early geometric stages unguided; this paper introduces a 'preference feedback loop' that constructs new preference pairs from the optimized final stage's outputs and uses them to train the preceding stage, thereby bridging the non-differentiable gap that blocks gradient backpropagation. The quality-aware weighting mechanism (w1, w2) draws on ranked-preference filtering and distributionally robust DPO ideas from the text-to-image literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The non-differentiable gap between stages is a structural limitation shared by many multi-stage generative pipelines (not just Trellis), so the data-loop strategy of forwarding preference signals as constructed pairs rather than gradients offers a generalizable recipe for holistic alignment. However, the paper acknowledges that Circular-DPO still trails score-distillation-based methods on quantitative metrics, and the feedback loop is demonstrated only on a single two-stage architecture with a fixed (off-policy) dataset, leaving open whether it scales to deeper pipelines or online/multi-round settings. The quality-aware weighting is a practical but somewhat standard robustness addition; its ablation shows meaningful but not dramatic gains.
