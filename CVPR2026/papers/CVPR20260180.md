## Problem

Generating crisp, one-pixel-wide edge maps remains a fundamental challenge because all existing methods rely on non-differentiable post-processing (NMS and skeleton-based thinning) to achieve satisfactory results, which prevents end-to-end optimization. Even dedicated crisp edge detection approaches still benefit from such post-processing, and prior attempts at direct crisp prediction lag behind the detect-then-post-process pipeline. The paper asks whether a differentiable, learnable module can replace hand-crafted post-processing entirely while matching or surpassing its quality.

## Input / Output

MATCHED is a plug-and-play module that takes the raw edge map (a W×H confidence map) produced by any existing edge detector and outputs a refined crisp edge map of the same spatial dimensions, with one-pixel-wide edges. It is appended to the final stage of the base model, so the overall pipeline still consumes a standard RGB image and produces a single-channel edge map. The module adds only ~21K parameters and introduces four hyperparameters (τ_c, τ_d, α, β), comparable in count to standard NMS.

## Method

MATCHED is a lightweight five-block CNN (~21K parameters) appended to any edge detector, trained end-to-end with a novel matching-based supervision signal. At each training iteration, it computes a confidence-weighted cost matrix between predicted edge pixels and ground-truth edge pixels, where the cost combines Manhattan distance and the prediction's confidence score, restricted by a distance threshold τ_d and a confidence threshold τ_c. An optimal one-to-one bipartite assignment (via linear sum assignment) then produces a matching-derived label that perfectly aligns each predicted response with a unique ground-truth edge pixel; unmatched ground-truth pixels are directly assigned to keep them active for future iterations. The module is trained with binary cross-entropy against this matching-derived label, while the base model is trained with its original loss, and the two are jointly optimized with a weighted total loss. This ensures training and testing protocols are consistent, since the same distance threshold used in evaluation governs the matching during training.

## Results

On BSDS under the crispness-emphasized evaluation (CEval), SAUGE+MATCHED achieves the best ODS of 0.854, OIS of 0.870, and AP of 0.912, while PiDiNet+MATCHED attains the highest Average Crispness (AC) of 0.919. Under standard evaluation (SEval with NMS+thinning), SAUGE+MATCHED reaches 0.846 ODS and 0.864 OIS, matching or exceeding all SOTA post-processed baselines. On BIPED-v2, PiDiNet+MATCHED (evaluated without post-processing) achieves 0.900 ODS, 0.905 OIS, and 0.971 AP, outperforming all SOTA methods that rely on post-processing. MATCHED adds only ~21K parameters and runs in 32.98 s on the full NYUD test set, compared to 1875.57 s for NMS+100×thinning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Under CEval on BSDS, SAUGE+MATCHED (0.854 ODS, 0.870 OIS, 0.912 AP) outperforms the previous best CEval method DiffEdge (0.749 ODS, 0.754 OIS) by +0.105 ODS and +0.116 OIS, and surpasses RankED (0.631 ODS) by +0.223 ODS. PiDiNet+MATCHED achieves an AC of 0.919, a 4.5× improvement over PiDiNet's raw AC of 0.202 and a 1.9× improvement over DiffEdge's AC of 0.476. Under SEval, SAUGE+MATCHED (0.846 ODS, 0.864 OIS) matches the best post-processed baseline SAUGE (0.847 ODS, 0.868 OIS) within 0.001–0.004, while PiDiNet+MATCHED (0.798 ODS) slightly trails PiDiNet's post-processed result (0.789 ODS) by a negligible margin. On BIPED-v2, PiDiNet+MATCHED (0.900 ODS, 0.905 OIS, 0.971 AP, no post-processing) beats all SOTA methods including DexiNed (0.895 ODS) and BDCN (0.890 ODS) that use NMS+thinning. On NYUD-v2, RankED+MATCHED (0.775 ODS, 0.784 OIS, 0.806 AP under CEval) outperforms the previous best CEval method DiffEdge (0.732 ODS, 0.738 OIS) by +0.043 ODS, and its SEval results (0.774 ODS, 0.784 OIS) are competitive with RankED's post-processed SEval (0.780 ODS, 0.793 OIS). The key finding is that MATCHED is the first method to match or surpass conventional NMS+thinning post-processing without any hand-crafted post-processing step.

## Relation to Prior Work

MATCHED repurposes the bipartite matching / linear sum assignment framework from object detection (DETR, [4], [36]) and applies it to the dense pixel-level problem of aligning predicted and ground-truth edge maps, a conceptual transfer not previously explored in edge detection. It departs from prior crisp-edge methods (LPCB, CATS, GLR, DiffusionEdge, CPD) that either modify loss functions, refine labels with fixed Canny guidance, or introduce architecture-specific modules, all of which still rely on NMS and skeleton-based thinning at inference. The key positioning is that MATCHED is the first post-processing-free, architecture-agnostic module that learns crispness end-to-end rather than correcting it after the fact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the non-differentiable NMS + thinning pipeline, MATCHED enables true end-to-end optimization of edge detectors and removes a long-standing bottleneck that has constrained joint training with downstream tasks (segmentation, inpainting, depth estimation). The practical plug-and-play nature (~21K parameters, <3% overhead on PiDiNet) makes it immediately adoptable across the diverse edge detection literature. Limitations include the need to retrain when tuning hyperparameters (unlike NMS, which is adjustable at inference) and significant GPU memory overhead at higher resolutions, though the authors mitigate this with patch-wise processing. An open question is whether the matching-based supervision generalizes beyond edge detection to other dense prediction tasks where one-to-one spatial alignment matters.
