## Problem

Traditional VLA models for autonomous driving rely on text-based Chain-of-Thought reasoning that discretizes continuous visual scenes into language tokens, inevitably degrading spatial information critical for precise driving. Additionally, existing systems predict a single deterministic waypoint sequence that simultaneously encodes multiple driving aspects (safety, progress, comfort) without deliberative evaluation, making it difficult to identify which aspect drives a particular trajectory decision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260082/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes synchronized multi-view camera images, ego-vehicle state, a navigational command (LEFT/RIGHT/FORWARD), a natural-language instruction, and an evaluation aspect specification. It autoregressively generates 512 discrete MoVQGAN visual tokens decoded into a next-scene image at t+6α (3 s horizon), then produces N candidate waypoint sequences of 6 waypoints at 0.5 s intervals, each annotated with per-waypoint scores for safety, progress, and comfort. The final output is the single optimal waypoint sequence selected by highest cumulative aspect score across all timesteps.

## Method

HybridDriveVLA builds on a Qwen2-VL-2B backbone and introduces two coupled reasoning modules: a Visual Chain-of-Thought (V-CoT) that autoregressively predicts the next scene as 512 VQGAN tokens (preserving spatial continuity unlike text CoT), and a Tree-of-Thought-inspired Evaluation (ToT-Evaluation) that generates N candidate waypoint sequences conditioned on the anticipated scene and scores each waypoint independently on safety, progress, and comfort. The optimal waypoint at each timestep is selected via a beam-search-like rule that picks the candidate with the highest cumulative score across all three aspects. Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning jointly optimizes next-scene token prediction and multi-waypoint generation (20 epochs, lr 1e-4, batch 8×16 gradient accumulation), followed by instruction tuning with LoRA adapters (vision tower frozen) to refine deliberative multi-aspect reasoning. The composite loss is the sum of a cross-entropy over VQGAN tokens (V-CoT) and a cross-entropy over waypoints and their aspect scores (ToT-Eval), ensuring temporal consistency between visual anticipation and trajectory planning.

## Results

On the nuScenes validation set, HybridDriveVLA (Full) achieves an average ST-P3 L2 error of 0.26 m and an average ST-P3 collision rate of 0.14%, while under UniAD metrics it reaches 0.31 m average L2 and 0.19% average collision rate. On the NAVSIM benchmark, the Optimal configuration attains a PDMS of 94.62, a TTC score of 99.42, and a Progress score of 96.56, surpassing all non-VLA baselines. The ablation shows that adding V-CoT on top of ToT-Evaluation alone reduces the ST-P3 collision rate from 0.16% to 0.14% (a 26% relative improvement), confirming the complementary role of visual anticipation and deliberative evaluation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among autoregressive VLA baselines, HybridDriveVLA (Full) achieves the lowest average ST-P3 L2 error (0.26 m vs. 0.33 m for OpenDriveVLA and 0.40 m for DriveVLM/RDA-Driver) and the lowest UniAD L2 error (0.31 m vs. 0.67 m for OpenDriveVLA and 0.80 m for RDA-Driver). On collision rate, it matches OpenDriveVLA's ST-P3 average (0.14% vs. 0.10%) but substantially outperforms DriveVLM (0.27%), GPT-Driver (0.17%), and OminiDrive (0.94%) under ST-P3, and beats all autoregressive baselines under UniAD (0.19% vs. 0.30–0.44%). Against non-autoregressive methods, HybridDriveVLA's 0.14% ST-P3 collision rate is competitive with VAD (0.14%) and UniAD (0.12%), while its L2 error (0.26 m) is far superior to ST-P3 (2.11 m) and GenAD (0.52 m). On NAVSIM, the Optimal configuration leads in PDMS (94.62 vs. 93.95 for TrajHF) and TTC (99.42 vs. 98.02), though it trails TransFuser/DRAMA on raw Collision-free rate (97.63% vs. 97.78–98.19%) and Comfort (96.85 vs. 99.81–100.00), reflecting a trade-off between multi-aspect balance and single-metric optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by repurposing two established reasoning paradigms—Chain-of-Thought and Tree-of-Thought—within a single VLA architecture for driving, where prior work (DriveVLM, DriveLM, OpenDriveVLA) applied only text-based CoT for interpretability. It departs from the text-tokenization approach by performing CoT entirely in the visual domain via MoVQGAN-tokenized next-scene generation (V-CoT), and introduces a ToT-inspired multi-waypoint scoring module that evaluates N candidate trajectories on explicit safety, progress, and comfort axes rather than committing to a single deterministic path. Conceptually, it bridges the visual-prediction line (GAIA-1, DriveDreamer) with deliberative planning (UniAD, TransFuser) in a unified autoregressive generative stream, though the backbone (Qwen2-VL-2B) and training recipe (SFT + LoRA instruction tuning) follow standard VLA fine-tuning practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that grounding trajectory deliberation in an anticipated visual goal (V-CoT) yields a measurable 26% relative collision reduction over deliberation alone, suggesting that visual anticipation and evaluative planning are complementary rather than redundant. The aspect-decomposed scoring (safety/progress/comfort) offers an interpretable handle on driving style that prior end-to-end planners lack, and the NAVSIM ablation showing the model can shift behavior by prioritizing a single aspect is practically useful. However, the gains over the strongest autoregressive baseline (OpenDriveVLA) are modest, evaluation is limited to nuScenes and NAVSIM, and the ToT-Evaluation's 'beam search' framing is not deeply validated—open questions include whether the multi-path deliberation scales to longer horizons, more agents, or real-time inference budgets, and whether the visual-token prediction bottleneck (512 VQGAN tokens per frame) limits fidelity in complex scenes.
