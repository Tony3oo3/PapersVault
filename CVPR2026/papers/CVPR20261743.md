## Problem

Conventional Mixture-of-Experts designs for video understanding suffer from expert homogenization: all experts receive the same undifferentiated input stream, forcing them into redundant generalist roles that fail to capture the inherently different types of information in video (static scene content vs. temporal evolution). This paper asks whether a group of functionally heterogeneous experts, each bound to a distinct temporal scale, can replace identical generalist experts to achieve more efficient and effective video representation learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261743/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip of 8 or 32 frames, encoded by a frozen visual backbone (CLIP ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14, or VideoMAEv2) into frame-level feature sequences. During training, 4 spatial crops × 3 temporal clips are used; at inference, a single crop is evaluated. The frame features are split into N parallel streams at different temporal sampling rates (e.g., rates 2/4/8 for 8-frame input, or 2/4/8/16 for 32-frame input), and the final output is a single action class label produced by a classifier applied to a fused video-level representation.

## Method

VidPrism introduces three coupled modules. First, a Rate-guided Spatio-Temporal Aggregation (RgSTA) module splits frame features into N streams at different temporal rates, using a hybrid importance score (learnable ScoreHead + L2-norm) to select keyframes and fuse information from discarded frames into kept frames via cosine-similarity-weighted attention, preserving information while compacting sequences. Second, a Dynamic Bidirectional Interaction (DBI) module enables gated information exchange between pathways: a slow-to-fast path upsamples and applies 1×1 convolution, while a fast-to-slow path uses strided temporal convolution, with a sigmoid-gated MLP determining the strength of each exchange. Third, a Heterogeneous Mixture-of-Experts (HMoE) module assigns each stream to a dedicated Transformer expert trained exclusively at that temporal rate, then aggregates all expert outputs via a learnable global query vector performing multi-head cross-attention over the concatenated sequences. Training combines cross-entropy classification loss with auxiliary ranking, diversity, and gate-balancing losses to encourage expert specialization and balanced utilization.

## Results

On Kinetics-400 with 8 frames and ViT-B/16, VidPrism achieves 84.0% Top-1 accuracy at only 162 GFLOPs, outperforming the MoTE baseline (83.0%) by 1.0% while using fewer FLOPs than FocusVideo (204 GFLOPs). Scaling to 32 frames, VidPrism reaches 85.1% (B/16) and 88.6% (L/14), surpassing all prior tuning-based methods. In few-shot settings, VidPrism-M (VideoMAEv2 backbone) sets new records on UCF-101 (96.6% at K=16) and SSv2 (18.3% at K=16), while VidPrism-C (CLIP backbone) achieves 74.1% on HMDB-51 at K=16.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VidPrism outperforms the closest MoE-based baseline MoTE by 1.0% (B/16, 8 frames: 84.0 vs 83.0) and 0.6% (L/14, 8 frames: 87.4 vs 86.8) on K400, while also reducing GFLOPs from 649 to 632 at the L/14 scale. Against the strongest adapting method FocusVideo, VidPrism-B/16 is marginally lower in accuracy (84.0 vs 84.1) but uses 21% fewer FLOPs (162 vs 204); at 32 frames with L/14, VidPrism clearly wins (88.6 vs 88.0). On few-shot UCF-101, VidPrism-M (VideoMAEv2 backbone) surpasses all baselines across every K-shot setting, reaching 96.6% at K=16 versus 94.8% for ETL and 93.6% for MoTE. On SSv2, VidPrism-M sets a new record of 18.3% at K=16, exceeding ETL (17.5%) and ViCLIP (15.4%). On HMDB-51, VidPrism-C achieves 74.1% at K=16, edging out ETL (70.4%) and MoTE (68.2%), though it trails ETL at K=2 (55.0 vs 61.2), suggesting the heterogeneous expert design benefits more from additional support samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

VidPrism directly extends MoTE (Zhu et al., NeurIPS 2024), which first introduced Mixture-of-Experts as a temporal module for image-to-video transfer, by replacing its homogeneous expert ensemble with functionally specialized experts bound to distinct temporal rates. Conceptually, the multi-rate, multi-pathway design draws on the two-stream hypothesis from neuroscience and the SlowFast network architecture, but generalizes the fixed dual-pathway structure into a flexible N-pathway framework with content-aware input routing. Within the broader I2V transfer learning lineage (ST-Adapter, X-CLIP, FocusVideo), VidPrism occupies the 'tuning' category but differentiates itself through its heterogeneous expert specialization and bidirectional cross-rate fusion rather than simple temporal adapters or learnable queries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine structural weakness in MoE-based video models—expert homogenization—and provides a principled architectural remedy with clear interpretability (visualized expert specialization). However, the practical gains are modest (≈1% over MoTE on K400), and the framework is validated only on short-clip action recognition benchmarks with 8–32 frames, leaving open whether the multi-rate heterogeneous design scales to long-form video understanding or video LLMs. An open question is whether the content-aware multi-rate sampling and bidirectional fusion generalize beyond the CLIP/VideoMAE backbones tested, and whether the four-expert configuration remains optimal as video length or action complexity increases.
