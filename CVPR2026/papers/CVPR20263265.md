## Problem

Existing raw point cloud gait recognition methods suffer from two critical gaps: spatial features at different scales are extracted in isolation without explicit cross-region semantic interaction (e.g., synchronized arm-leg coordination), and temporal sequences are compressed via simple max/average pooling that cannot handle gait's inherent heterogeneity from varying cadence and capture rates. These limitations cause significant performance degradation under non-gait nuisances such as carried objects, bulky clothing, and occlusions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a raw LiDAR point cloud sequence P_o ∈ R^{B×T×N×3} (batch × frames × points × 3D coordinates) directly, without projection to 2D depth images. It is designed to handle variable point densities arising from different LiDAR ranges (12 m in SUSTech1K vs. 25 m in FreeGait) and variable sequence lengths due to differing walking speeds. The output is a fixed-length gait identity embedding obtained by temporally pooling Transformer-encoded key-frame features, used for metric-based identity matching.

## Method

MS²Gait comprises two core modules. The Hierarchical Spatial Feature Extractor (HSFE) replaces PointNet++'s rigid MLP+MaxPooling with adaptive MLP+Softmax weighting and introduces four complementary mixing strategies—Intra-Set, Inter-Set, Intra-Layer, and Inter-Layer—that propagate features across spatially distant yet biomechanically related body parts (e.g., foot-to-hip chains, contralateral limbs, cross-scale dependencies). A Geometry-Aware Multi-Resolution Feature Fusion (GMFF) module then aligns all hierarchical features to a common point distribution via inverse-distance-weighted 3-NN interpolation before concatenation and UAP pooling. The Similarity-based Temporal Enhancement Transformer (STET) first performs parameter-free max-min diversity sampling in L2-normalized feature space to select K key frames adaptively (handling cadence/sampling variation without phase annotations), then applies multi-scale cosine-similarity aggregation at local (k=3) and mid-range (k=5) temporal neighborhoods to dynamically weight coherent frames and suppress noise, followed by a 4-layer Transformer encoder with positional encoding to capture long-range periodicity. Training uses Triplet Loss plus Cross-Entropy Loss with SGD and cosine annealing.

## Results

On SUSTech1K, MS²Gait achieves 93.5% OA@R1 and 98.3% OA@R5, outperforming the previous best point-cloud method LidarGait++ (92.7% / 98.2%) by 0.8% in Rank-1. On FreeGait, it reaches 83.1% Rank-1, 93.8% Rank-5, and 87.7% mAP, surpassing LidarGait++ (82.0% / 93.6% / 87.2%). Under challenging non-gait nuisance conditions, MS²Gait shows particularly large margins: 94.7% on Bag (vs. 78.7% for the PointNet++ baseline) and 92.5% on Umbrella (vs. 74.0%), demonstrating strong robustness to carried objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MS²Gait outperforms all 13 compared baselines on both SUSTech1K and FreeGait. The closest competitor is LidarGait++ [27], the prior state-of-the-art point-cloud method, which MS²Gait surpasses by 0.8% OA@R1 on SUSTech1K (93.5% vs. 92.7%) and by 1.1% Rank-1 on FreeGait (83.1% vs. 82.0%); the gap widens substantially on nuisance subsets (e.g., +1.0% on Bag, +1.0% on Umbrella). GaitCloud [45], the second-best point-cloud method, trails by 4.3% OA@R1 on SUSTech1K. The PointNet++ baseline [22] is outperformed by 16.4% OA@R1 on SUSTech1K and 23.8% Rank-1 on FreeGait, highlighting the gains from the proposed multi-scale interaction and temporal modules. 2D-based methods (GaitSet [4], GaitBase [9], LidarGait [25]) are consistently 7–28 points behind, confirming the advantage of direct 3D point-cloud processing. The hybrid DIs+PCs method HMRNet [12] achieves 90.2% OA@R1 on SUSTech1K, still 3.3% below MS²Gait, indicating that fusing depth images does not compensate for the lack of explicit cross-region spatial interaction.

## Relation to Prior Work

MS²Gait builds directly on the PointNet++ hierarchical sampling architecture (SGM blocks) used as its spatial backbone, but departs from prior LiDAR gait methods (LidarGait, LidarGait++, GaitCloud) in two key ways: it replaces isolated per-scale feature extraction with four complementary cross-region interaction strategies (intra-set, inter-set, intra-layer, inter-layer mixing) inspired by PointMixer's attention-based aggregation, and it replaces simple max/average temporal pooling with a Transformer-based module that performs diversity-driven key frame selection followed by multi-scale cosine similarity aggregation. The paper positions these as addressing two specific gaps in the point cloud gait literature: the absence of explicit cross-region semantic interaction in spatial modeling and the lack of gait-specific temporal designs that handle cadence/sampling heterogeneity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method sets a new SOTA on both major LiDAR gait benchmarks and, more importantly, demonstrates substantially improved robustness under non-gait nuisance conditions (bag, umbrella, carrying) where prior methods degrade significantly, suggesting the cross-region interaction strategies effectively isolate identity-discriminative body geometry from carried objects. The temporal heterogeneity robustness (maintaining performance under 50% frame dropout) is practically relevant for real-world LiDAR deployments with variable capture rates. Open questions include whether the cross-domain generalization gap (SUSTech1K→FreeGait) can be further closed, whether the four mixing strategies generalize beyond gait to other point cloud sequence tasks, and whether the computational overhead of the additional mixing operations becomes prohibitive at higher point densities or longer sequences.
