## Problem

Existing CoSOD models trained with supervised learning on limited datasets overfit to both the semantic categories present in training data and domain-specific style patterns (texture, color, tone), leading to degraded performance on unseen object categories and out-of-distribution visual domains. No prior CoSOD method systematically addresses both content-level and style-level distribution shifts in a unified framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260775/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a group of N collaborative RGB images (all sharing the same co-object category) resized to 224×224 pixels as input. During training, images are processed in two-category batches; during inference, a single-category image set is fed in. The output is a per-pixel co-salient object segmentation mask produced by an FPN decoder, with the MSM module bypassed at inference time.

## Method

CoMCS combines a VGG-16 encoder with a frozen CLIP-ViT-B/32 encoder in a dual-branch architecture. The Mixed Content Modulator (MCM) extracts domain-invariant scene structure priors by computing cross-attention between co-salient object prototypes (GAP of deep features) and CLIP multi-class semantic embeddings, then modulates deep features via element-wise multiplication with the resulting context-rich prototype. The Mixed Style Modulator (MSM), active only during training, synthesizes novel style statistics by perturbing per-channel mean and standard deviation of shallow features using batch-level variance as a dispersion index, interpolating with Beta-distributed mixing coefficients, and applying AdaIN to produce style-augmented features. The Collaborative Semantic Contrast Module (SCM) computes inter-image correlation maps to extract primary prototypes, applies adaptive sigmoid-gated adjustment, and uses the adjusted prototypes as 1×1 convolutional kernels to enhance co-salient features. Training is supervised by a composite loss combining a hyperspherical uniformity loss (pushing prototypes apart on the unit sphere), BCE, and IoU loss over 200 epochs with Adam (lr=1e-4).

## Results

On the three standard CoSOD benchmarks, CoMCS achieves the best or tied-best results across all four metrics (Sα, Fβmax, Eφmax, MAE). On the most challenging CoCA dataset, CoMCS reaches Sα=0.747, Fβmax=0.649, Eφmax=0.821, MAE=0.084, outperforming the second-best IPPO (Sα=0.747, Fβmax=0.644) and GCoNet+ (Sα=0.738, Fβmax=0.637). On the proposed unseen-domain dataset (UND), CoMCS achieves Sα=0.856, Fβmax=0.830, Eφmax=0.908, MAE=0.051, surpassing the next-best MCCL by 2.2% in Sα and 4.1% in Fβmax, demonstrating substantially stronger generalization to unseen categories.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoMCS outperforms all 17 compared methods on at least one metric per dataset, and achieves the best score on the majority of metrics across all three benchmarks. On CoCA, CoMCS edges out IPPO (the closest competitor) by 0.5% in Fβmax (0.649 vs 0.644) and 1.1% in Eφmax (0.821 vs 0.810), while tying on Sα (0.747) and MAE (0.084); it surpasses GCoNet+ by 0.9% in Sα and 1.2% in Fβmax. On CoSOD3k, CoMCS leads MCCL (the prior best) by 0.1% in Sα (0.857 vs 0.856) and 0.6% in Fβmax (0.843 vs 0.837), and achieves the lowest MAE (0.058 vs 0.061). On CoSal2015, CoMCS ties MCCL and ICSM on Sα (0.890) but leads in Fβmax (0.896 vs 0.893) and Eφmax (0.929 vs 0.927). The most striking gap appears on the unseen-domain dataset (UND), where CoMCS surpasses MCCL by 2.2% in Sα (0.856 vs 0.834) and 4.1% in Fβmax (0.830 vs 0.789), and GCoNet+ by 3.0% in Sα and 2.7% in Fβmax, confirming the generalization advantage of the mixed content-style modulation design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of co-salient object detection (CoSOD) and domain generalization (DG), addressing the observation that existing CoSOD methods (GCoNet, DCFM, MCCL, etc.) overfit to both semantic content and style patterns in their training distributions. It departs from prior DG work (MixStyle, feature decoupling) by explicitly separating content and style modulation: the MCM leverages frozen CLIP embeddings via cross-attention to inject domain-invariant scene structure priors, while the MSM extends AdaIN-style feature statistics manipulation with batch-variance-scaled noise for intra-domain style augmentation. The SCM with a hyperspherical uniformity loss draws on contrastive representation learning (Wang & Isola) to disambiguate co-features, building on the contrastive learning trend in recent CoSOD methods (MCCL, DCFM).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored gap: CoSOD models trained on fixed benchmarks degrade significantly on unseen categories and domains, yet no prior CoSOD method explicitly targets this. The introduction of the UND benchmark, while small (50 groups), provides a much-needed evaluation protocol for generalization in CoSOD. Limitations include reliance on a frozen CLIP encoder (which constrains the semantic knowledge to CLIP's training distribution), a relatively dated VGG-16 backbone, and the modest size of the unseen-domain evaluation set. Open questions include whether the CLIP-based content modulation transfers to other group-based segmentation tasks (co-segmentation, video co-saliency) and whether the approach scales to larger, more diverse domain shifts than those captured by UND.
