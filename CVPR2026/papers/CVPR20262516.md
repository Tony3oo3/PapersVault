## Problem

Existing diffusion-based image codecs rely on U-Net architectures that perform denoising in shallow latent spaces (typically 8× downscaled), incurring prohibitive sampling overhead and memory usage. This creates a fundamental mismatch with modern learned codecs that operate in much deeper latent domains (16×–64×), raising the question of whether diffusion can function effectively in such compact spaces without degrading reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262516/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, encodes it via an ELIC-based encoder into a 64×-downscaled latent (f64d320), and performs single-step diffusion reconstruction in a 32×-downscaled latent space (f32d32). Unlike generative diffusion that starts from pure Gaussian noise, the diffusion input here is a structured, entropy-constrained quantized latent already near the data manifold. The output is a reconstructed image decoded from the denoised latent, with the entire pipeline requiring no text input at inference time and capable of reconstructing 2048×2048 images on a 16 GB laptop GPU.

## Method

DiT-IC adapts a pretrained SANA text-to-image DiT into a one-step compression reconstruction model through three alignment mechanisms. First, a variance-guided reconstruction flow maps spatially varying latent uncertainty (σ) to pixel-wise pseudo-timesteps, collapsing iterative denoising into a single adaptive transformation where smooth regions receive weak denoising and textured regions receive strong denoising. Second, a self-distillation alignment loss enforces cosine similarity between the DiT's denoised output and the frozen encoder's latent representation, distilling multi-step diffusion behavior into one forward pass without external teacher supervision. Third, latent-conditioned guidance replaces text prompts with a lightweight projection of the compressed latent, co-aligned with text embeddings via a CLIP-style contrastive objective during training so that the model retains semantic priors while operating text-free at inference. The model is trained with LoRA adapters (rank 32/64) under a two-stage implicit bitrate pruning schedule with adversarial perceptual losses.

## Results

DiT-IC achieves average BD-rate of −83.65% (LPIPS) and −87.88% (DISTS) across Kodak, CLIC 2020, and DIV2K, outperforming the strongest prior diffusion codec StableCodec (−79.19% LPIPS, −83.95% DISTS) by roughly 4–4 percentage points. Decoding latency is 0.15 s per 1024×1024 image in FP16, approximately 2× faster than StableCodec (0.34 s) and OSCAR (0.32 s), and up to 30× faster than multi-step codecs like DiffEIC (12.4 s). The method operates entirely in a 32× downsampled latent space with a single diffusion step, enabling 2048×2048 reconstruction on a 16 GB laptop GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiT-IC outperforms all 11 baselines on both LPIPS and DISTS BD-rate across all three test sets. Against the closest competitor StableCodec (ICCV'25), DiT-IC improves average LPIPS BD-rate from −79.19% to −83.65% and DISTS from −83.95% to −87.88%, while also halving decoding latency (0.15 s vs. 0.34 s). The gap is most dramatic versus OSCAR (NeurIPS'25), where DiT-IC achieves −83.65% vs. −19.04% on LPIPS and −87.88% vs. −58.38% on DISTS, reflecting OSCAR's reliance on a U-Net backbone in a shallow 8× latent space. Compared to multi-step codecs (DiffEIC at 50 steps, PerCo at 20 steps, RDEIC at 5 steps), DiT-IC delivers substantially better perceptual quality at 1/10th to 1/80th the decoding time. Among VAE-based methods, GLC (TCSVT'25) is the strongest non-diffusion baseline (−75.50% LPIPS, −78.38% DISTS), but DiT-IC still leads by 8 and 9.5 percentage points respectively. The ablation baseline (DiT-IC without alignment mechanisms) scores −65.00% LPIPS and −67.19% DISTS, confirming that the three alignment components contribute an additional ~18–20 percentage points of BD-rate improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: diffusion-based image compression (StableCodec, OSCAR, ResULIC, DiffEIC) and Diffusion Transformer architectures (SANA, PixArt-α, SD3). Prior diffusion codecs universally employ U-Net backbones operating in shallow 8× latent spaces, while conventional learned codecs (ELIC, QARV) operate in much deeper 16×–64× domains. DiT-IC's core architectural insight is that the DiT's constant-resolution transformer blocks are naturally compatible with deeply compressed latents, eliminating the hierarchical downsampling that makes U-Nets poorly suited to this regime. The three alignment mechanisms—variance-guided flow matching, self-distillation against the frozen encoder, and latent-conditioned guidance replacing text prompts—are novel adaptations that bridge the generative-to-reconstructive gap, building on flow matching theory and contrastive alignment (CLIP-style) but applied in a compression-specific context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: 30× faster decoding than prior diffusion codecs and the ability to reconstruct 2048×2048 images on a 16 GB laptop GPU make diffusion-based perceptual compression viable outside datacenter settings. The finding that single-step reconstruction is achievable in a 32× latent space challenges the assumption that iterative denoising is necessary when the initial state already lies near the data manifold. Open questions remain at extremely low bitrates (<0.01 bpp) where latent conditioning may lack sufficient semantic information and auxiliary text priors could help, and whether adversarial distillation techniques can further close the perceptual gap. The approach also raises the broader question of whether other generative architectures (autoregressive, flow-based) can similarly benefit from deep-latent operation for compression.
