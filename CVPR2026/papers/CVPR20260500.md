## Problem

Existing visible-infrared image translation methods are confined to unidirectional, one-to-one mappings evaluated on closed-set benchmarks, limiting their scalability and diversity. They struggle to generalize to unseen scenes and cannot capture the inherent diversity of possible cross-modal correspondences (e.g., a single visible scene may map to multiple thermal appearances depending on object state). This paper addresses these gaps by formulating translation as an open-world, bidirectional, language-vision-guided process.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single infrared or visible image along with a free-form text prompt specifying the translation direction and target attributes. Foreground masks and object labels are automatically extracted by a pre-trained Visual Grounding Pipeline (RAM → Grounding DINO → SAM) without manual annotation, and are encoded as conditional embeddings alongside the text. The output is a translated image in the opposite modality, generated via a 30-step DDIM sampling process, with the same spatial resolution as the input.

## Method

SynthRGB-T builds on Stable Diffusion v1.5 and introduces three key mechanisms. A Visual Grounding Pipeline (VGP) automatically derives foreground masks and semantic labels from the input, which are fused with text embeddings via a three-branch Q-Former (initialized from BLIP-Diffusion) to produce foreground query tokens. A Decoupled Injection Mechanism (DIM) routes different guidance signals to different U-Net cross-attention layers: foreground prompt embeddings at low-resolution layers (4–8) for structural layout, image encoder features at higher layers for texture fidelity, and text embeddings globally for semantic consistency. A Dual-Conditional Cross-Attention (DCCA) module replaces parallel per-modality cross-attentions with a unified attention that concatenates projected keys and values from both image and text features, enabling collaborative representation learning. Training proceeds in two stages: first aligning VGP-derived query tokens with CLIP text embeddings via MSE and cosine losses, then jointly optimizing diffusion, Sobel-based geometric, and VGG-based perceptual losses while freezing the image encoder, text encoder, and VGP.

## Results

On the I2V task, SynthRGB-T achieves FID of 40.3 and SSIM of 0.753 on M3FD, and FID of 34.2 and SSIM of 0.885 on LLVIP, outperforming the strongest baseline CM-Diff (FID 43.9 / SSIM 0.665 on M3FD; FID 39.8 / SSIM 0.718 on LLVIP). On the V2I task, it reaches FID 37.5 / SSIM 0.783 on M3FD and FID 31.8 / SSIM 0.922 on LLVIP, again surpassing CM-Diff (FID 40.8 / SSIM 0.626; FID 40.0 / SSIM 0.751). On unpaired benchmarks, SynthRGB-T attains FID 36.3 on CTIR (I2V) and FID 33.8 on VisDrone (V2I), demonstrating strong open-world generalization beyond its training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynthRGB-T outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all four I2V and four V2I benchmarks. The strongest competitor is CM-Diff, the only other bidirectional diffusion method; SynthRGB-T improves FID by 3.6 points (40.3 vs. 43.9) and SSIM by 0.088 (0.753 vs. 0.665) on M3FD I2V, and by 5.6 FID points (34.2 vs. 39.8) and 0.167 SSIM on LLVIP I2V. Among GAN-based baselines, I2V-GAN achieves the best NIQE but suffers from an extremely high FID (208.1 on M3FD), indicating poor distributional fidelity; CycleGAN, UNIT, and CUT all score FID above 100, confirming their limited cross-modal consistency. LG-Diff and DiffV2IR, the other diffusion baselines, are competitive on LPIPS but trail SynthRGB-T by 8–15 FID points on paired benchmarks and show weaker SSIM, reflecting less accurate structural preservation. On unpaired benchmarks (CTIR for I2V, VisDrone for V2I), the gap widens further, with SynthRGB-T achieving FID 36.3 and 33.8 respectively versus 42.7 and 39.7 for CM-Diff, underscoring its superior open-world generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

SynthRGB-T builds directly on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion architecture and BLIP-Diffusion's Q-Former for subject-conditioned generation, repurposing both for cross-modal (infrared↔visible) translation rather than text-to-image synthesis. It departs from prior GAN-based translators (CycleGAN, I2V-GAN) and earlier diffusion-based approaches (LG-Diff, DiffV2IR, CM-Diff) by introducing a Visual Grounding Pipeline that chains RAM, Grounding DINO, and SAM to automatically derive foreground masks and labels, eliminating manual annotation. The Decoupled Injection Mechanism and Dual-Conditional Cross-Attention module represent a novel architectural contribution that addresses multi-condition interference in the U-Net's cross-attention layers, going beyond the single-condition or parallel-branch injection used in prior diffusion translators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified bidirectional framework with language-vision guidance addresses a practical bottleneck in multimodal research: the cost and limited diversity of paired infrared-visible datasets. By introducing RGBTSynth-86K and synthesizing 50K additional paired samples from unimodal inputs, the paper provides a reusable data resource for autonomous driving and surveillance communities. Open questions remain about whether the foundation-model grounding (RAM + Grounding DINO + SAM) generalizes to other modality pairs (e.g., medical imaging, which the authors mention as future work) and whether the diversity gains hold under stricter evaluation protocols with more diverse prompt distributions. The reliance on a frozen VGP pipeline also raises questions about error propagation when object detection or segmentation fails on atypical scenes.
