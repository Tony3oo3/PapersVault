## Problem

No existing dataset provides dense, synchronized multiview video captured from an egocentric perspective of real, dynamic scenes. Novel view synthesis datasets are typically exocentric or monocular and focus on static scenes, while egocentric datasets prioritize activity recognition with only 1–3 views, leaving a critical gap for benchmarking egocentric 4D reconstruction under the combined challenges of ego motion, near-range hand-object interaction, large disparities, and frequent occlusions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261380/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The research dataset supplies 12 dewarped rig camera views organized as 6 rectified stereo pairs at 1280×1280 resolution and 130° horizontal FOV, captured at 60 Hz over 8–10 second recordings (450–550 frames per camera, 956 recordings total). For NVS evaluation, two target views (stereo pair 3–4) are held out while the remaining 10 views serve as training inputs. The method outputs per-frame 3D Gaussian splatting representations and rendered novel views of the held-out cameras; the stereo-guided variant additionally produces fused TSDF depth surfaces that serve as the geometric initialization.

## Method

The core pipeline is a stereo-guided 3DGS applied independently per frame to yield a dense 4D reconstruction. For each frame, Foundation Stereo is run bidirectionally on all 6 rectified stereo pairs, and the resulting depth maps are fused via TSDF integration to produce watertight, metrically accurate geometry. Surface points with normals and colors are sampled from the fused TSDF to initialize 3D Gaussians, which are then fine-tuned for a small number of iterations minimizing a photometric loss (L1 + D-SSIM, λ=0.1). The key insight is that high-quality fused stereo geometry already encodes sufficient structure that minimal photometric refinement suffices, dramatically outperforming both unguided per-frame 3DGS (+7.9 dB PSNR) and dynamic NVS baselines like K-Planes and Spacetime Gaussians that are ill-equipped for the combination of ego motion, near-range hand dynamics, and large inter-view disparities.

## Results

On the Ego-1K dataset (96 recordings, 10% subset), the proposed stereo-guided 3DGS achieves PSNR 29.12, SSIM 0.830, and LPIPS 0.115 on held-out target views, substantially outperforming all baselines. The closest competitor, Spacetime Gaussians, reaches only PSNR 24.76 (a 4.4 dB gap), while per-frame 3DGS and K-Planes trail by 7.9 and 12.7 dB respectively. For stereo consistency, Foundation Stereo yields the lowest median absolute deviation (1.6 mm) and highest sub-millimeter agreement (74.0%), making it the chosen depth prior for the 4D reconstruction pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's stereo-guided 3DGS outperforms all three NVS baselines by wide margins: +7.9 dB PSNR over per-frame 3DGS [19], +12.7 dB over K-Planes [12], and +4.4 dB over Spacetime Gaussians [28], with LPIPS dropping from 0.260/0.443/0.270 to 0.115. Among the four stereo methods evaluated for depth prior quality, Foundation Stereo [47] achieves the best pairwise consistency (MAD 1.6 mm, 74.0% of pixels <1 mm), while Selective-Stereo [46] performs worst (MAD 8.0 mm, 0% <1 mm); BiDAStereo [18] has the lowest standard deviation (8.3 mm) indicating fewer extreme outliers but higher median error (2.2 mm), and StereoAnywhere [3] is competitive on MAD (1.7 mm) but lags on sub-millimeter agreement (29.5%). The paper demonstrates that existing dynamic NVS methods (K-Planes, Spacetime Gaussians) are fundamentally ill-suited to egocentric scenes with near-range hand motion and large disparities, as they assume object-centric or fixed-pose capture. The key finding is that geometry guidance via TSDF-fused stereo depth is essential—without it, even per-frame 3DGS fails to resolve the ill-posedness introduced by ego-motion and occlusions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new intersection between three established lines of work: neural 3D video synthesis datasets (which are exocentric or monocular), egocentric vision datasets (which are monocular/stereo and focused on activity recognition), and multiview egocentric datasets (which offer only 2–3 synchronized egocentric views). The method contribution builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023), replacing its standard sparse-point initialization with a dense surface sampled from TSDF-fused stereo depth maps (Foundation Stereo), then fine-tuning for a small number of photometric-loss iterations. This initialization strategy is conceptually related to geometry-prior 3DGS methods like DN-Splatter and EDGS, but differs in using metrically accurate stereo fusion rather than learned depth or sparse correspondences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Ego-1K fills a genuine gap: no prior dataset simultaneously provides egocentric perspective, 12+4 synchronized cameras, near-1K dynamic recordings, and hand-object interaction content, making it the first resource for benchmarking egocentric 4D novel view synthesis. The empirical finding that purpose-built dynamic NVS methods (K-Planes, Spacetime Gaussians) fail while a simple per-frame 3DGS with stereo initialization succeeds highlights that current dynamic reconstruction architectures are not designed for the combination of ego motion, near-range hands, and large disparities. Open questions include whether multiview/multi-baseline stereo can yield pseudo ground-truth depth for evaluating monocular methods, and whether the stereo-guided baseline can be extended to a truly temporal (4D) model that exploits inter-frame coherence.
