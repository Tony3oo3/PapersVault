## Problem

Diffusion models achieve high visual quality in image editing but their global denoising dynamics inherently conflate local editing targets with full-image context, causing unintended modifications to leak into non-target regions. Existing remedies—large-scale data, manual masks with inpainting, or inversion techniques—either cannot explicitly guarantee preservation, lack flexibility, or suffer from slow inference. The paper asks whether an alternative generative paradigm can fundamentally resolve this leakage problem.

## Input / Output

The model takes an original RGB image (short edge ≥1024 px) and a free-form text editing instruction as input, producing a single edited image at the same resolution. The original image is not fed as a separate conditioning channel but is encoded into the same VQ-GAN token space as the target and injected via a learned attention bias, so the architecture introduces zero additional parameters over the base text-to-image MGT. Inference completes in ~2 seconds on 1024×1024 images with a 13.8 GB memory footprint.

## Method

EditMGT builds on the Meissonic masked generative transformer, which synthesizes images by iteratively flipping masked VQ-GAN tokens in parallel rather than performing holistic denoising. The original image is incorporated as fixed-timestep conditioning tokens C_V that share parameters with the generation tokens C_I, with a tunable log(γ) bias matrix controlling cross-attention strength between them. At inference, a multi-layer attention consolidation aggregates text-to-image cross-attention maps from blocks 28–36 and applies adaptive filtering to produce a clean spatial localization of edit-relevant regions. A region-hold sampling step then reverts any token whose consolidated attention falls below a threshold λ back to its original counterpart, explicitly freezing non-target areas while the MGT continues to refine only the high-attention editing region. Training proceeds in three stages—base model with Gemma2-2B text encoder, full fine-tuning on 4M high-resolution editing pairs (including the newly constructed CrispEdit-2M), and a short high-quality alignment pass—without adding any parameters for the editing capability.

## Results

On EMU Edit, EditMGT (960M) achieves the best CLIPim score of 0.878 and DINO of 0.832, outperforming larger models like OminiGen2-7B (0.876/0.822) and VAREdit-8B (0.876/0.825). On MagicBrush, it reaches SOTA CLIPim of 0.911 and DINO of 0.881. On GEdit-EN-full, EditMGT scores 5.87 average, surpassing VAREdit-8B (5.73), OminiGen2-7B (5.57), and GoT-6B (3.95), while achieving a 17.6% improvement in style transfer and 9.8% in color alteration over the 12B FluxKontext.dev. The model edits 1024×1024 images in ~2 seconds (6× faster than comparable baselines) with only 13.8 GB memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EditMGT (960M) outperforms all same-size and larger baselines on image similarity: it beats OminiGen2-7B on EMU Edit CLIPim (0.878 vs 0.876) and DINO (0.832 vs 0.822), and surpasses VAREdit-8B on both benchmarks (MagicBrush CLIPim 0.911 vs 0.901, DINO 0.881 vs 0.844). On GEdit-EN-full, EditMGT (5.87 avg) exceeds VAREdit-8B (5.73), OminiGen2-7B (5.57), and GoT-6B (3.95), and wins over the 12B FluxKontext.dev on background change (7.69 vs 7.06), color alteration (7.71 vs 7.03), portrait editing (5.13 vs 4.68), and style transfer (6.53 vs 5.55, a 17.6% gain). However, FluxKontext.dev retains the overall GEdit average lead (6.26 vs 5.87), primarily due to stronger motion (5.62 vs 3.84) and text editing (6.10 vs 4.53) scores. On AnyBench, EditMGT achieves SOTA on style change (+3.6% over second-best) and implicit instruction (+1.7%) tasks. In terms of efficiency, EditMGT is 6× faster than comparable-performance models (2 s per 1024×1024 edit) while using only 13.8 GB of memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant diffusion-model paradigm for instruction-based image editing by repurposing Masked Generative Transformers (MGTs), building directly on Meissonic's high-resolution MGT architecture and OminiControl's attention-bias conditioning mechanism. While prior editing work (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, UltraEdit, VAREdit) has addressed the 'editing leakage' problem through data scaling, manual masks, or inversion techniques, EditMGT exploits MGT's inherent parallel token-flipping to explicitly preserve non-target regions without any of these workarounds. The multi-layer attention consolidation and region-hold sampling are novel inference-time mechanisms that leverage cross-attention maps as localization signals, a direction not previously explored in MGT-based generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 960M-parameter MGT can match or exceed 8B–12B diffusion models on editing quality while running 6× faster, this work challenges the assumption that iterative denoising is necessary for high-fidelity image editing and opens a path toward real-time, low-memory editing applications. The CrispEdit-2M dataset (2M high-resolution samples across 7 categories) addresses a critical data scarcity bottleneck in the field. Open questions include whether the attention-based localization generalizes to complex multi-object edits or video editing, whether the approach scales beyond 1024×1024, and whether the Gemma2-2B text encoder's language understanding is sufficient for increasingly complex compositional instructions.
