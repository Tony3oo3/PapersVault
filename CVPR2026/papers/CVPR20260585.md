## Problem

Diffusion transformers incur substantial computational cost during multi-step denoising, and existing token-reduction acceleration methods (caching and merging) degrade generation quality by producing denoising directions that are either non-updated (reusing stale features) or non-self (reusing merged features from other tokens). No prior method simultaneously preserves both the self-consistency and the temporal update of each token's denoising trajectory while reducing computation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260585/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

ResCa is a training-free, plug-and-play module that operates on the internal token features of a pre-trained diffusion transformer. It accepts the same inputs as the base model (text prompts for FLUX and HunyuanVideo, class labels for DiT-XL/2) and produces the same outputs (256×256 images for ImageNet, higher-resolution images for FLUX, or short videos for HunyuanVideo). The method alternates between dense timesteps (all tokens processed and cached) and sparse timesteps (only one proxy token per cluster is fully denoised), with the cache interval N and cluster count K as the main hyperparameters.

## Method

ResCa introduces a 'proxy denoising' framework with two components. First, Temporal-Enhanced Trajectory Clustering (TETC) groups tokens by their historical denoising trajectories using a temporally weighted cosine similarity (exponential moving average over per-timestep similarity matrices) followed by K-medoids clustering, ensuring that tokens with genuinely similar residual dynamics are grouped together. Second, Proxy-Driven Denoising Simulation (PDDS) performs true denoising on a single proxy token per cluster, computes its multi-order finite-difference residuals, and then estimates the next-step residuals of all driven tokens by blending their own cached residuals with the proxy's residuals, weighted by a per-order confidence score (cosine alignment of residuals at the current step). The driven tokens are then advanced via an implicit ODE update (implicit Euler, BDF2, or multi-order Taylor), which yields a denoising direction that is both self (anchored to the token's own cached feature) and updated (corrected by the proxy's freshly computed residuals), avoiding the quality degradation of pure caching or merging.

## Results

On FLUX.1-dev (text-to-image, DrawBench prompts), ResCa-IE with N=5, K=16 achieves an ImageReward of 0.9958 and CLIP Score of 19.537 at 4.14× FLOPs speedup, matching or exceeding the full 50-step baseline (0.9898 / 19.761). At higher acceleration, ResCa-IB with N=7 reaches 5.51× FLOPs speedup with ImageReward 0.9889, and ResCa-IT with N=8, O=2, K=32 pushes to 6.15× FLOPs speedup while retaining ImageReward 0.9468. On HunyuanVideo (text-to-video, VBench), ResCa-IE (N=6, K=32) attains the best VBench score of 79.98 at 5.53× FLOPs speedup, and on DiT-XL/2 (class-conditional, ImageNet), ResCa-IE (N=3, K=16) achieves FID 2.37 and sFID 4.63 at 2.58× speedup, outperforming all caching baselines at comparable or higher acceleration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResCa consistently outperforms all caching and merging baselines on FLUX. At the 34%-step setting (N=5), ResCa-IE achieves ImageReward 0.9958, beating ClusCa (0.9825), TaylorSeer (0.9857), ToCa (0.9802), and FORA (0.9776) while matching DuCa (0.9955) and slightly exceeding Chipmunk (0.9936). At the more aggressive 22%-step setting (N=7), ResCa-IB (BDF2) reaches ImageReward 0.9889, substantially outperforming TaylorSeer (0.9128), ClusCa (0.9364), and TeaCache (0.7394) at comparable FLOPs speedups (~5.5×). On HunyuanVideo, ResCa-IE (N=6, K=32) attains VBench 79.98, edging out TaylorSeer (79.78) and ClusCa (79.60) at similar 5.5–6.2× FLOPs speedups. On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet), ResCa-IE (N=3, K=16) achieves FID 2.37, improving over DuCa (2.85), ToCa (2.87), and FORA (2.66) at a higher 2.58× speedup. The only baseline that approaches ResCa's quality at N=5 is DuCa (0.9955 vs. 0.9958 ImageReward), but ResCa achieves this at a higher FLOPs speedup (4.14× vs. 3.80×).

## Relation to Prior Work

ResCa positions itself within the feature-based token-reduction family for diffusion transformers, departing from both caching methods (ToCa, DuCa, TokenCache) that produce non-updated denoising directions and merging methods (ToMeSD, ToMA, SDTM) that produce non-self directions. Its key conceptual departure is the 'proxy denoising' perspective: rather than reusing or blending features, one token per trajectory-based cluster undergoes true denoising, and its multi-order residuals correct the simulated updates of all other tokens via an implicit ODE (Euler, BDF2, or Taylor). The Temporal-Enhanced Trajectory Clustering (TETC) module further distinguishes the work by clustering on historical denoising trajectories with recency-weighted similarity rather than on instantaneous feature similarity, which prior methods (including ClusCa) rely on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play framework validated across DiT-XL/2, FLUX, and HunyuanVideo, ResCa offers practitioners a practical acceleration option that achieves up to 5.5× FLOPs reduction with near-lossless quality, particularly at aggressive caching intervals (N=7–8) where prior methods degrade more noticeably. The 'proxy denoising' paradigm—decoupling a small set of true computations from the bulk of simulated updates guided by residual corrections—could inspire further work on adaptive token budgets and higher-order ODE solvers for diffusion sampling. Open questions include whether the K-medoids clustering overhead becomes prohibitive at very large token counts (e.g., high-resolution video), how the method interacts with progressive distillation or few-step samplers, and whether the trajectory-based clustering advantage persists under non-stationary or multi-modal denoising dynamics.
