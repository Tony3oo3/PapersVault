## Problem

Existing vision-language-action (VLA) models for end-to-end autonomous driving are emotion-agnostic: they map multi-view images and commands to trajectories without interpreting the passenger's emotional tone or urgency, creating an 'emotion gap' that undermines comfort and public acceptance. Additionally, current VLAs operate largely in 2D without explicit allocentric (map-based) spatial reasoning, limiting their ability to ground referents and plan in cluttered, partially observed scenes. The paper defines the Open-Domain End-to-End AD (OD-E2E) task, requiring a single policy to jointly interpret free-form language, infer emotion, ground spatial referents, and produce physically feasible trajectories.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a tuple of an egocentric camera image, an allocentric bird's-eye-view (BEV) image, and a free-form natural-language command. It produces a single autoregressive token sequence containing a continuous 3-dimensional VAD emotion vector (valence, arousal, dominance), a grounded target bounding box in image coordinates, and T future 2D waypoints (T×2 spatial coordinates), followed by a free-text verbal feedback response whose tone and length adapt to the inferred emotion state. A lightweight action decoder post-processes the VLA's coarse trajectory into the final physically-realizable waypoint sequence.

## Method

E3AD is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with LoRA adapters and integrates two cognitively inspired modules: (1) an Emotion Modeling component that maps commands into continuous VAD space using GoEmotions sentence-level scores combined with word-level lexical VAD, augmented by Qwen-generated paraphrases that preserve driving intent while varying emotional tone; and (2) a dual-pathway Spatial Reasoning module that fuses an egocentric pathway (predicting 3D direction, distance, and image-space location) with an allocentric pathway (predicting BEV target location and coarse trajectory from a cognitive-map perspective). Training proceeds in three stages: modality pretraining (separate SFT for emotion and spatial skills), joint fine-tuning (autoregressive prediction of the full (e, b, τ) sequence in one forward pass), and Emotion-Action Alignment via DPO, where pseudo-preference pairs are constructed by generating a 'negative' trajectory from the most VAD-deviant emotion-augmented command variant, thereby teaching the model to suppress emotionally inconsistent plans while preserving intent-consistent ones.

## Results

On the end-to-end trajectory planning benchmark (Talk2Car-Trajectory), E3AD achieves ADE 3.88, FDE 6.64, and Fréchet 7.23, reducing these metrics by 17.01%, 20.00%, and 18.26% respectively over the strongest prior method PTPC. For visual grounding, E3AD reaches 80.12 IoU on Talk2Car and 82.56 on DrivePilot, surpassing the best dedicated grounding model CAVG by +6.86% and +6.79% absolute. On continuous emotion estimation (VAD), E3AD attains Spearman ρ of 0.95/0.94/0.94 for valence/arousal/dominance, far exceeding the best text-embedding baseline (Qwen3-Emb.-4B+Ridge at ρ=0.83/0.79/0.82). In spatial reasoning, E3AD achieves Location MAE of 0.47 and Depth MAE of 4.25, compared to 3.49–10.1 and 18.89–22.92 for the strongest VLM baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E3AD outperforms all 23 baselines across every evaluated metric. Against the strongest trajectory-planning baseline PTPC [17], E3AD reduces ADE by 17.01% (3.88 vs. 4.54), FDE by 20.00% (6.64 vs. 7.75), and improves PA₄ by 18.10% (55.62 vs. 45.55). Among dedicated visual grounding models, E3AD surpasses CAVG [31] (the best prior method) by +6.86% IoU on Talk2Car (80.12 vs. 74.62) and by +8.26% on the challenging Vision-Constraint corner-case split (76.62 vs. 68.39). General-purpose VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Qwen3-VL-8B) perform dramatically worse on all tasks—e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B achieves only 56.17 IoU on Talk2Car grounding and 12.51 ADE on trajectory planning—confirming that task-aligned architecture matters more than raw model scale. For emotion estimation, E3AD (ρ=0.95 valence) substantially exceeds the best text-embedding baseline Qwen3-Emb.-4B+Ridge (ρ=0.83) and classical NLP models like DistilBERT+Ridge (ρ=0.82), while raw Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct shows near-random correlation (ρ=0.11). The VLA baseline FSDrive-Finetuned [67] trails E3AD by 29.1% in FDE (10.45 vs. 6.64) and 52.7% in PA₂ (17.10 vs. 36.21), highlighting the benefit of explicit emotion modeling and dual-pathway spatial reasoning over purely visual-spatial VLA pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

E3AD extends the third VLA paradigm for autonomous driving (VLM-based perception coupled with dedicated planning, as in Simlingo, AutoVLA, and FSDrive) by injecting two cognitively motivated components that prior VLAs lack: a continuous Valence-Arousal-Dominance emotion model and a dual-pathway (egocentric + allocentric) spatial reasoning module. It departs from prior emotion-in-AD work, which typically performs passive discrete-label detection decoupled from control, by grounding emotion in a continuous VAD space and using it as a conditioning signal for trajectory generation. The training strategy repurposes DPO for driving by constructing pseudo-preference pairs from emotion-augmented command variants, a technique not previously applied to trajectory planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in autonomous driving: the disconnect between a vehicle's computational reasoning and the passenger's emotional state, which surveys and behavioral studies identify as central to public trust and acceptance. The practical implication is that emotion-aware modulation of driving behavior (e.g., straighter paths under high arousal, more cautious approaches under low arousal) could make AVs feel more responsive and trustworthy without sacrificing safety. Open questions include whether text-only VAD estimation generalizes to richer multimodal emotion signals (voice prosody, physiological cues), whether the synthetic DPO preference pairs capture genuine behavioral preferences, and whether the approach scales to real-time, closed-loop driving in high-fidelity simulation or on-road deployment.
