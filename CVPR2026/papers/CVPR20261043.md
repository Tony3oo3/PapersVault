## Problem

Existing Large Vision-Language Models lack explicit spatial reasoning and depth awareness, producing hallucinated object descriptions and unreliable distance estimates that are unsafe for pedestrian navigation. Grounded LVLMs add 2D segmentation masks but still lack the depth information needed to reason about relative distances and spatial hierarchy. No prior system unifies conversational reasoning, pixel-level grounding, and depth estimation into a single architecture for accessibility-aware pedestrian guidance without requiring user-provided visual cues.

## Input / Output

WalkGPT takes a single pedestrian-view RGB image and a free-form navigation query as input, and produces a structured conversational response containing four token types: <assessment> for an overall accessibility judgment, <p> for grounding referenced objects in dialogue, <SEG> for prompting the SAM pixel decoder to emit segmentation masks, and <distance> for object-level depth expressed as natural-language distance values. The model operates entirely without user-supplied anchor points or visual cues, and depth is estimated implicitly through language prediction rather than a dedicated regression head.

## Method

WalkGPT uses a shared SAM ViT-H pixel encoder whose embeddings are processed by two novel modules. The Multi-Scale Query Projector (MSQP) reshapes encoder features into a grid, average-pools at four spatial scales (native, ×2, ×4, global-mean), applies segmentation-aware gating, and refines each scale with learnable queries via two cross-attention layers, concatenating outputs into 36 compact image tokens for the LLM. The Calibrated Text Projector (CTP) maps <SEG> token hidden states from the 4096-dim LLM space into the 256-dim visual space via a linear projection followed by a bias-augmented MLP that expands each token into K_bank complementary sub-embeddings, which serve as text prompts for the SAM pixel decoder. A proposed Region Alignment Loss (InfoNCE) enforces contrastive consistency between each <SEG> token's projected embedding and the top-K cross-attended visual region, preserving semantic fidelity across the dimensionality reduction. Training is two-stage: MSQP is pretrained on ADE20K and RefCOCO, then all components (MSQP, CTP, pixel decoder, LoRA-adapted LLM) are jointly fine-tuned on the new PAVE dataset (41k pedestrian-view VQA triplets) with a combined objective of cross-entropy for conversation, Dice+CE for segmentation, and the contrastive alignment loss.

## Results

On the PAVE benchmark, WalkGPT (13B) achieves 41.17 CIDEr, 43.01 METEOR, 20.16 mIoU, 32.71 Recall, and 48.95% Depth Accuracy, outperforming the strongest fine-tuned baseline (Sa2VA-FT) by 2.35 CIDEr, 3.35 METEOR, 4.06 mIoU, and 8.41 Depth Accuracy points. The 7B variant achieves 41.97 CIDEr and 67.88 AbsRel, the best text-generation and depth-precision scores among all models. On RefCOCO-val, WalkGPT reaches 76.2 mIoU for referring expression segmentation, surpassing PixelLM (73.0) and LISA (74.1) by 3.2 and 2.1 points respectively. For hallucination, WalkGPT reduces CHAIR_i to 18.49 while boosting object coverage to 83.66, compared to 22–27 CHAIR_i and 31–39 coverage for non-grounded LVLMs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the six fine-tuned grounded LVLM baselines on PAVE, WalkGPT (13B) wins on CIDEr (41.17 vs. 38.82 Sa2VA-FT), METEOR (43.01 vs. 39.66), mIoU (20.16 vs. 18.23 GLAMM-FT), Recall (32.71 vs. 29.71 GSVA-FT), Depth Accuracy (48.95 vs. 40.54 Sa2VA-FT), and AbsRel (70.66 vs. 73.82 Sa2VA-FT), but trails Sa2VA-FT on AP50 (17.26 vs. 18.72). Zero-shot baselines are uniformly poor (CIDEr < 1.3, mIoU < 3.2) and cannot produce any depth estimates, underscoring the difficulty of the task without task-specific training. On RefCOCO/+/g RES benchmarks, WalkGPT achieves 76.2/70.0/72.6 mIoU on val splits, outperforming PixelLM (73.0/66.3/69.3) and LISA (74.1/62.4/66.4) by 2–4 points, despite not being trained on RES data. Compared to vision-only segmentation models (U-Net, nnU-Net, Swin-UNETR) on PAVE, WalkGPT matches the best mIoU (20.16 vs. 20.60 Swin-UNETR) while achieving higher Recall (32.71 vs. 30.65), demonstrating that grounded language supervision compensates for the lack of dedicated segmentation training. For hallucination, WalkGPT reduces CHAIR_i from 22–27 (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA 1.6 Mistral, Qwen-VL-Chat) to 18.49 and nearly doubles object coverage (83.66 vs. 31–39), confirming that pixel-level grounding via segmentation masks substantially mitigates unsupported object mentions.

## Relation to Prior Work

WalkGPT extends the grounded LVLM lineage (GLAMM, PixelLM, LISA) by adding depth-aware reasoning and accessibility-specific guidance on top of a shared SAM ViT-H pixel encoder. It departs from spatial-reasoning LVLMs (SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT, DepthLM) that require user-provided visual cues or anchor points, instead operating autonomously from a single pedestrian-view image. The two novel modules—MSQP for multi-scale visual token aggregation and CTP with a Region Alignment Loss for calibrated text-to-segmentation mapping—are architectural contributions layered onto the SAM+LLM paradigm, while the PAVE dataset fills a gap the authors explicitly identify: no prior dataset jointly provides pedestrian-view images with accessibility-aware VQA and depth-grounded segmentation annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WalkGPT establishes the first benchmark (PAVE, 41k images) and task formulation for grounded, depth-aware pedestrian accessibility navigation, a domain with direct practical impact for individuals with mobility challenges. The finding that depth estimation emerges purely through autoregressive language prediction of <distance> tokens—without a dedicated depth head or dense supervision—is a useful architectural insight for multimodal models. Key limitations remain: single-view reflections and motion blur cause significant segmentation failures, the dataset is built from only 91 video sessions (limiting scene diversity), and cross-domain generalization beyond pedestrian navigation is untested. Open questions include whether the depth-via-language approach scales to metric-accurate estimation and whether the framework transfers to other first-person navigation domains.
