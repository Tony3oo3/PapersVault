## Problem

Existing multi-task dense prediction (MDP) methods assume high-quality inputs and fail under real-world degradations such as noise, haze, rain, and blur. The dominant workaround—a two-stage pipeline that first restores then predicts—artificially severs information flow between restoration and prediction, preventing cross-task feature complementarity and increasing inference latency. This paper presents the first unified framework for multi-task dense prediction directly on degraded images, eliminating the restoration-prediction separation.

## Input / Output

The network takes a single degraded RGB image (corrupted with Gaussian noise, rain streaks, haze, or motion blur) and simultaneously produces a restored image plus up to six dense prediction maps: semantic segmentation, depth, surface normals, edge detection, saliency, and human parts segmentation. All outputs are at the original input resolution, and the architecture is evaluated on NYUD-v2 (indoor scenes, 795 train / 654 test) and PASCAL-Context (4,998 train / 5,105 test) with degradations synthesized following published corruption models.

## Method

ALLNet is a U-Net-like architecture built on an InternImage backbone with dual decoders (restoration and prediction) that interact at every scale through two key modules. The Mixture of Adaptive Experts (MaE) module implements degradation-adaptive restoration via a hierarchical set of experts with decreasing channel width but increasing window size; each expert contains three parallel branches (windowed self-attention, dynamic depth-wise convolution, and windowed MLP) fused by a Mutual Branch Interaction mechanism, and an image-level router with learned prior, temperature scaling, and a complexity-aware auxiliary loss selects the top-k experts per input. The Task-aware Collaborative Refinement (TCR) module establishes bidirectional cross-task collaboration: a Cross-Task Interaction Unit generates per-task global semantic tokens via GAP+MLP, performs cross-task attention to let each task perceive others' semantics, and an Adaptive Fusion Unit uses the refined task tokens as queries against the prediction features as key-value pairs to inject global collaborative information back into spatial features while preserving local structure. The two decoders jointly optimize through Selective Kernel Feature Fusion, enabling the restoration stream to guide prediction and the prediction stream to inform restoration in a single forward pass.

## Results

On degraded NYUD-v2, ALLNet achieves 55.41 mIoU for semantic segmentation, 0.4992 RMSE for depth estimation, 18.85 mErr for surface normals, and 79.13 odsF for edge detection, outperforming the best baseline (MLoRE) by 4.1 mIoU, 0.068 RMSE, 0.86 mErr, and 4.66 odsF respectively. On degraded PASCAL-Context, ALLNet reaches 80.23 mIoU (segmentation), 69.40 mIoU (parsing), 85.78 maxF (saliency), 13.92 mErr (normals), and 73.05 odsF (edges), surpassing MLoRE by 2.92, 3.77, 4.14, 1.67, and 4.39 points respectively. In the two-stage comparison (Table 2), ALLNet's one-piece design also outperforms the best cascade pipeline (MLoRE + AdaIR) by 2.98 mIoU on NYUD-v2 segmentation and 1.08 mIoU on PASCAL-Context segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ALLNet outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric across both degraded NYUD-v2 and PASCAL-Context. The closest competitor is MLoRE [63], which ALLNet surpasses by 4.1 mIoU on NYUD-v2 segmentation (55.41 vs. 51.31) and by 2.92 mIoU on PASCAL-Context segmentation (80.23 vs. 77.31); the gap widens to 4.14 maxF on PASCAL-Context saliency and 4.39 odsF on PASCAL-Context edges. TaskPrompter [61] and BridgeNet [64] form the second tier, trailing ALLNet by roughly 4–5 mIoU on segmentation. Older or less degradation-aware methods (MTI-Net, ATRC, MQTtransformer, DeMT, MDTP-Lib) lag substantially, with MDTP-Lib [62] scoring only 30.56 mIoU on NYUD-v2 segmentation. In the two-stage comparison (Table 2), ALLNet's unified one-piece design beats the best cascade (MLoRE + AdaIR [65]) by 2.98 mIoU on NYUD-v2 and 1.08 mIoU on PASCAL-Context, confirming that decoupling restoration from prediction incurs a measurable performance penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multi-task dense prediction (MDP) and all-in-one image restoration, two well-developed but previously separate research threads. It departs from the dominant two-stage 'divide-and-conquer' paradigm (restore-then-predict) by unifying both objectives in a single U-Net-like architecture with bidirectional feature interaction, drawing on MoE routing from vision scaling literature and cross-attention mechanisms from transformer-based MDP methods (InvPT, TaskPrompter, MLoRE). The Mixture of Adaptive Experts module repurposes the MoE paradigm from general capacity scaling to degradation-adaptive expert selection, while the Task-aware Collaborative Refinement module introduces explicit cross-task attention between restoration and prediction feature streams—a mechanism absent from prior MDP frameworks that assume clean inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing MDP under degradation as a first-class problem and providing a unified architecture that avoids the information bottleneck of cascaded pipelines, this work opens a practical path toward robust scene understanding in adverse conditions (autonomous driving, surveillance). However, the evaluation is limited to synthetic degradations on two datasets, the reliance on the relatively heavy InternImage backbone raises questions about deployment efficiency, and no real-world degradation testing is conducted. Open questions include whether the dual-stream design generalizes to unseen degradation types, whether the MoE routing remains effective at scale with many more experts, and whether the framework can be made lightweight enough for edge deployment.
