## Problem

Multimodal depression recognition is fundamentally limited by the scarcity of clinically annotated data, which is especially detrimental to the visual pathway since deep visual encoders are data-hungry. Existing generative augmentation pipelines are decoupled from the downstream task: a generator is trained independently for realism, and its outputs are treated as a static dataset, leaving no mechanism for the recognizer's performance to inform and steer the synthesis process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263364/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes three modalities per interview session: video frames (yielding 17 AU intensities, 6-DoF head pose, and eye gaze via OpenFace 2.0), audio at 100 Hz (yielding F0, voice quality, MFCC, and prosodic features via COVAREP), and word-level transcripts (yielding contextualized embeddings via BERT-base-uncased). All streams are temporally aligned to word-level granularity via average pooling over each word's timestamp interval. The output is a continuous depression severity score (regression, evaluated with CCC/RMSE on E-DAIC) or a binary depression class label (classification, evaluated with F1 on DAIC-WOZ).

## Method

CMG-VS couples a Conditional VAE-based visual synthesizer with a Hierarchical Fusion Transformer recognizer in an end-to-end joint optimization loop. The CVAE uses Transformer encoder-decoder blocks with cross-attention (visual features as Query, concatenated audio+text as Key/Value) to learn P(f_v | f_a, f_t), generating diverse visual feature sequences via stochastic latent sampling. The key innovation is task-guided feedback: the augmented-data recognition loss (L_aug) is back-propagated through the synthesizer, so the generator is steered not merely toward reconstruction fidelity but toward producing features that are maximally discriminative for depression recognition. The recognizer processes both real and synthesized visual streams through shared intra-modal encoders and an iterative [CLS]-token cross-attention fusion stack, trained with a composite loss L_total = L_real + λ_aug·L_aug + λ_cvae·(L_consis + β·L_KL) that balances recognition accuracy, generative consistency, and latent-space regularization.

## Results

On the DAIC-WOZ classification task, CMG-VS achieves an F1-Score of 0.860 with Precision 0.846 and Recall 0.875, outperforming the strongest baseline BiLSTM+BiGRU (F1 0.850) and MDSD-FGPL (F1 0.828). On the E-DAIC regression task, CMG-VS attains the best CCC of 0.69, lowest RMSE of 4.35, and lowest MAE of 3.78, surpassing the next-best MMFF (CCC 0.68, RMSE 4.91, MAE 3.98). The method's notable advantage is its significantly higher Precision (0.846 vs. 0.790–0.820 for most competitors), indicating fewer false positives in depression classification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CMG-VS outperforms all 14 baselines across both benchmarks. On DAIC-WOZ, it edges out BiLSTM+BiGRU (F1 0.850) by 0.010 in F1 while achieving substantially higher Precision (0.846 vs. 0.790), and surpasses MDSD-FGPL (F1 0.828) by 0.032. On E-DAIC, CMG-VS beats MMFF (the strongest regression baseline) by 0.01 in CCC and 0.56 in RMSE, and outperforms CubeMLP (CCC 0.58) and FPT-Former (RMSE 4.8) by wider margins. The method's consistent top performance across both classification and regression settings, and across two distinct datasets, indicates that the task-guided visual synthesis generalizes well beyond a single evaluation protocol. Notably, CMG-VS achieves the best Precision among all methods on DAIC-WOZ, reducing false positives from 5 (Recognizer-Only) to 2, which is critical for clinical deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of generative data augmentation and multimodal depression recognition, departing from the prevailing two-stage paradigm where a generator is trained independently and its outputs are frozen as a static training set. It repurposes the CVAE architecture—commonly used for pixel-level image synthesis—into a feature-level conditional synthesizer whose outputs are fed back into the recognizer's training loop via a task-guided loss. Conceptually, it also reframes cross-modal synthesis (previously oriented toward perceptual realism in speech-driven facial animation or text-to-image generation) as a mechanism for producing discriminative, task-relevant features rather than visually plausible content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The task-guided joint optimization loop offers a general recipe for data-scarce multimodal settings where one modality (here, visual) is underrepresented: instead of merely augmenting with realistic samples, the generator is steered by the downstream task's gradient. However, the current instantiation is limited to feature-level synthesis on two small clinical benchmarks (DAIC-WOZ, E-DAIC), and the paper does not explore whether the paradigm transfers to pixel-level generation, other clinical domains, or larger-scale datasets. An open question is whether the modest but consistent gains (≈1–3% F1) would hold under stricter evaluation protocols or with more diverse baselines, and whether the closed-loop training introduces instability at scale.
