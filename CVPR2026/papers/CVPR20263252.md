## Problem

RLVR methods like GRPO for MLLMs rely on high-quality labeled data to compute verifiable rewards, but real-world datasets often contain substantial annotation noise. Under noisy supervision, standard GRPO can overfit to incorrect labels, and existing entropy-based regularizers apply a static direction (always maximize or always minimize), which either prevents convergence or suppresses the response diversity that GRPO's group-wise advantage estimation requires.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263252/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (GUI screenshots at varying resolutions, pet photos, or COCO object images) paired with a text instruction specifying the target element or class. For GUI grounding and object detection, the model outputs a bounding box (coordinates or box parameters); for fine-grained classification, it outputs a class label string. Training sets are small (500 ScreenSpot samples, 4-shot Pets37, 975 COCO annotations), and noise is simulated by randomly displacing ground-truth boxes or swapping correct labels with incorrect ones at controlled rates from 0% to 100%.

## Method

The paper proposes a two-stage token-level entropy schedule added to the standard GRPO objective as L_total = L_GRPO + λ(τ)·L_entropy. In Stage 1 (exploration, τ ≤ τ_switch), λ is positive so the model maximizes per-token entropy, encouraging diverse rollouts that resist overfitting to noisy labels and preserve the intra-group reward variance needed for informative GRPO advantage estimation. At a fixed switch point (80% of total training steps by default), λ flips to a negative value, entering Stage 2 (exploitation) where entropy minimization drives the policy toward confident, deterministic outputs that consolidate the knowledge acquired during exploration. The token-level entropy is computed as the average of per-token cross-entropies over the vocabulary at each generation step, averaged across all tokens in the response and across K sampled rollouts per input.

## Results

On the ScreenSpot GUI grounding task with Qwen2.5-VL-3B, the proposed two-stage entropy-guided GRPO achieves 80.2% accuracy at 50% label noise (vs. 76.2% for standard GRPO, a +4.0% gain) and 75.8% at 100% noise (vs. 71.0%, +4.8%). On fine-grained classification (Pets37, 4-shot), the method reaches 69.0% at 50% noise and 70.0% at 0% noise, consistently outperforming standard GRPO. On the open-vocabulary object detection task (COCO), the two-stage schedule improves mAP@0.5 from 15.94 (GRPO) to 19.47 at 50% noise, a +3.53 absolute gain. The method also delivers the best out-of-distribution performance, reaching 20.7% on ScreenSpot-Pro and 60.6% on MMBench-GUI L2 with clean training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed two-stage entropy-guided GRPO outperforms standard GRPO [12] across all noise levels on both ScreenSpot and Pets37, with the largest gap of +4.8% at 100% noise on ScreenSpot (75.8% vs. 71.0%). Against one-shot RL [9] (Gao et al.), which uses a single entropy-minimization step, the two-stage method achieves consistently higher accuracy across all noise settings shown in Figure 1. Compared to spurious-reward baselines [31] (Shao et al.), which use format or random rewards without any labeled data, the proposed method substantially outperforms them, particularly at moderate-to-high noise levels where spurious rewards provide no useful signal. Against EMPO [49] (Zhang et al.), which applies pure entropy minimization throughout training, the two-stage schedule wins at most noise levels; pure entropy minimization (GRPO w. Min.) actually degrades below standard GRPO at 0% noise on ScreenSpot (79.0% vs. 82.2%), while the two-stage method reaches 83.6%. The key finding is that neither pure maximization nor pure minimization is optimal: maximization excels at 0% noise but struggles at high noise, while minimization helps at 100% noise but hurts clean-data performance—only the staged schedule balances both regimes effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (the RLVR algorithm from DeepSeek-R1) by adding a two-stage token-level entropy scheduling term to the policy loss. It departs from prior entropy-based RLVR methods—EMPO (pure entropy minimization), one-shot RL (entropy as a standalone reward), and CLIP-Cov (entropy collapse prevention)—by arguing that the direction of entropy optimization should not be static but dynamically scheduled: maximize early for exploration, minimize later for exploitation. The work also positions itself against external-signal methods (LLM-as-a-Judge, TTRL) and internal-signal methods (spurious/format rewards) that attempt to handle noisy supervision, showing that a simple entropy schedule on top of standard GRPO is more robust and task-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage entropy schedule is a lightweight, task-agnostic regularization that practitioners can drop into any GRPO-based RLVR pipeline without architectural changes, making it broadly applicable to MLLM training on imperfect data. However, the noise simulation (randomly displaced bounding boxes, random label swaps) is relatively artificial compared to real annotation errors, and the fixed 80% switching point has not been validated on longer training runs or more complex reasoning tasks. Open questions include whether the schedule generalizes to multi-step reasoning with structured (correlated) noise, whether the switching point can be made adaptive (e.g., based on reward variance), and whether the approach transfers to non-GRPO RLVR variants like PPO or DPO.
