## Problem

Mamba-based medical image segmentation serializes 2D images into 1D token streams, causing geometric-semantic mismatch where structurally unrelated but spatially adjacent regions are propagated together, blurring discriminative evidence. Additionally, existing variants treat spatial interaction and channel discrimination largely independently, so low-amplitude but diagnostically informative spatial-channel couplings are suppressed during aggregation. The unresolved question is whether a single-scan state-space model can simultaneously enforce geometry-conditioned semantic propagation and selective spatial-channel interaction without resorting to multi-directional scanning or dense pixel-wise attention.

## Input / Output

GeoSemba takes 2D medical images resized to 512×512 pixels across six modalities (dermoscopy, radiology, ultrasound, microscopy, colonoscopy, fundus) and produces pixel-level binary segmentation masks. The encoder-decoder architecture serializes each feature map into N=HW tokens for the state-space scan, with the Cross-Paradigm Module operating at multiple encoder/decoder levels. Training uses 120 epochs with Adam (lr=1e-4, batch size 8) on a single RTX 4090, and inference runs at ~17 ms/image with 41.11M parameters.

## Method

GeoSemba reformulates the selective state-space update into a Cross-Paradigm Module (CPM) following the MetaFormer paradigm, where the token mixer is a single-scan SSM whose parameters are jointly shaped by two components. The Semantic-guided State Refiner (SSR) groups N tokens into M=7 semantic prototypes via cosine-similarity assignment, constructs prototype nodes from concatenated mean features and spatial centroids, and applies Geometric-Semantic Nexus Attention (GSNA) that fuses a global context path with a Manhattan-distance-based spatial relation path to produce geometry-conditioned inter-region dependencies; these modulate the token-wise Δ, B, C scan parameters so state evolution follows structural relatedness rather than scan order alone. The Cross-dimensional Affinity Refiner (CAR) follows a coarse-to-fine pipeline: macro-perception (depthwise convolutions) establishes a global spatial prior, channel attention derives a region-consistent prior, and a Token Aggregation module builds multi-group (G=12) affinity matrices between position-sensitive queries and pooled keys, applies Top-K (K=0.5) sparsification to suppress weak correlations, and uses a learnable projection W^d to synthesize complementary token relations before adaptively aggregating a compact R×R feature grid. The final CPM update is h_i = Ā_i^SSR·h_{i-1} + B̄_i^SSR·CAR(x)_i, y_i = C_i^SSR·h_i + D·x_i, coupling structurally consistent semantic propagation with selective spatial-channel interaction within a single linear-complexity scan.

## Results

GeoSemba achieves the best DSC and IoU across all six medical imaging datasets, with average DSC of 88.6% and average IoU of 82.2%. Its strongest gains appear on DSB2018 (93.2% DSC) and CVC-ClinicDB (94.8% DSC), while the most challenging dataset (COVID19-1) still reaches 83.8% DSC. The model uses 44.11M parameters and runs at 0.018 s/image, compared to 54.86M parameters and 0.023 s/image for VM-UNet, confirming that single-scan state-space modeling preserves efficiency. Paired t-tests confirm statistically significant improvements (p < 0.05) over most competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoSemba outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset and metric. Against the strongest Mamba-based competitors, it gains 1.25% average DSC and 1.75% average IoU over Spatial-Mamba (structure-aware state fusion) and 0.78% DSC / 1.13% IoU over SCSegamba (multi-directional scanning), indicating that geometry-conditioned semantic propagation and selective spatial-channel interaction are more effective than scan-path redesign alone. Compared to the best CNN/Transformer baselines (PVT-EMCAD, MADGNet), GeoSemba leads by roughly 1.5–2.5% DSC on most datasets, with the largest margin on COVID19-1 (+0.2% DSC over PVT-EMCAD) and DSB2018 (+0.5% DSC). On CVC-ClinicDB, PVT-EMCAD (95.2% DSC) slightly edges out GeoSemba (94.8%), which the authors attribute to the dataset's relatively regular polyp shapes reducing the need for region-conditioned refinement. In terms of efficiency, GeoSemba (44.11M params, 17 ms/image) is lighter and faster than VM-UNet (54.86M, 23 ms) and SCSegamba, while matching or exceeding their accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoSemba extends the Mamba-based medical image segmentation lineage (Mamba, Vision Mamba, VM-UNet) by reformulating the selective state-space update to jointly encode geometry-conditioned semantic propagation and spatial-channel interaction within a single scan. It departs from structure-preserving variants (LocalMamba, SpatialMamba, DefMamba) that focus on maintaining spatial continuity during serialization, and from spatially enhanced or multi-directional scanning methods (MambaIRv2, SCSegamba, 2DMamba) that strengthen spatial context or enlarge coverage but treat channel discrimination largely independently. The MetaFormer paradigm provides the architectural template, with SSR parameterizing state evolution and CAR supplying interaction-refined input to the token mixer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By coupling prototype-level geometric-semantic context with coarse-to-fine spatial-channel refinement in a single linear-complexity scan, GeoSemba offers a practical path toward more discriminative Mamba-based segmentation without the overhead of multi-directional scanning or dense attention. The paper acknowledges that gains diminish on datasets with regular, well-bounded targets (e.g., CVC-ClinicDB), suggesting the approach's value is most pronounced for ambiguous boundaries and low-contrast regions. Open questions include whether the Manhattan-distance geometric prior and fixed prototype count generalize to 3D volumetric imaging or highly heterogeneous multi-organ tasks, and whether the Top-K sparsification ratio transfers across modalities without re-tuning.
