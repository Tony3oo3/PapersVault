## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on multi-object tracking target Tracking-by-Detection (TBD) components such as Kalman filters, NMS thresholds, and separate Re-ID banks, making them architecturally incompatible with emerging Tracking-by-Propagation (TBP) trackers. TBP trackers introduce novel structural vulnerabilities—a fixed zero-sum query budget, recurrent hidden-state propagation, and built-in temporal memory mechanisms—that have remained entirely unstudied from an adversarial perspective.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video sequences from MOT17 (~21 objects/frame) and MOT20 (~150 objects/frame) benchmarks and produces either pixel-wise digital perturbations (L∞-norm bounded at ε=8/255, applied for 1 frame) or optimized physical sensor-spoofing parameters (AAI: oscillation amplitudes x, y, phase φ, blur steps D=10; EAI: stripe tensor M∈R^{H×2}, stripe count N=20, applied for 3 frames). The physical parameters are constrained to ranges calibrated on real camera sensors (Samsung S20, iPhone 7, 360 M320 Dashcam, Google Pixel One) to ensure physical plausibility.

## Method

FADE formulates two complementary attacks targeting distinct TBP architectural vulnerabilities. Temporal Query Flooding (TQF) exhausts the fixed query budget by generating spurious high-confidence tracks, combined with cost mimicry (minimizing bipartite matching cost to ground-truth objects) and identity siphoning (maximizing cosine similarity between adversarial hidden states and previous legitimate track states) to ensure temporal persistence. Temporal Memory Corruption (TMC) directly destroys existing tracks by minimizing frame-to-frame cosine similarity of query hidden states (severing temporal links) and minimizing the L2-norm of matched query embeddings (erasing feature identity). Both attacks are optimized via a unified PGD loop (α=1/255 digital, α=8/255 physical; 50–100 iterations) that backpropagates through the full TBP tracker and, for physical attacks, through differentiable simulations of acoustic motion-blur and electromagnetic color-stripe sensor spoofing.

## Results

On MOT17, FADE_TQF achieves the largest HOTA degradation against CO-MOT (58.16 → 37.26, a 20.9-point drop) with IDSW increasing from 1.83 to 9.50 (5.2×). On MOT20, FADE_TMC causes the most severe collapse against MeMOTR (69.61 → 37.70 HOTA, a 31.5-point drop) with IDSW spiking from 0.46 to 4.90 (over 10×). FADE_TQF against MOTRv2 on MOT20 produces a 30-point HOTA drop (59.56 → 29.64). Across all five TBP trackers and both benchmarks, FADE consistently outperforms the three TBD-designed baselines (Daedalus, Hijacking, F&F) by 5–30 HOTA points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All three baselines (Daedalus, Hijacking, F&F) were designed for TBD trackers and are architecturally mismatched with TBP models. Hijacking and F&F, which manipulate Kalman filter motion predictions, are nearly ineffective against MOTRv2 on MOT20 (HOTA drops of only 1.3 and 0.7 points vs. FADE_TQF's 30-point drop), confirming that learned query propagation is immune to hand-crafted motion-model attacks. Daedalus, a detection-evasion attack, shows moderate effect on MOTR but is outperformed by FADE on memory-augmented trackers: on MeMOTR (MOT20), Daedalus drops HOTA by only 7.1 points while FADE_TMC achieves a 31.5-point drop, demonstrating that TBP's temporal smoothing filters non-persistent noise but cannot resist targeted memory corruption. On CO-MOT (MOT17), Daedalus produces the highest IDSW (14.59) but FADE_TQF achieves a lower HOTA (37.26 vs. 40.01) with more controlled IDSW (9.50), indicating FADE causes deeper structural damage rather than just identity confusion. Overall, FADE wins on HOTA degradation against all three baselines on 4 out of 5 trackers on both datasets, with the largest margins (15–30 HOTA points) on memory-augmented trackers (MeMOTR, Samba, CO-MOT).

## Relation to Prior Work

FADE departs from the established line of adversarial attacks on MOT (Daedalus, Hijack, F&F, BankTweak), which target TBD-specific components like Kalman filters, NMS thresholds, and separate Re-ID banks that do not exist in end-to-end query-propagation trackers. It repurposes differentiable physical sensor-spoofing simulations from single-frame detection work (Poltergeist/TPatch for acoustic, GlitchHiker/MagShadow for electromagnetic) and extends them to the temporal multi-object tracking setting. The two proposed attack strategies—Temporal Query Flooding and Temporal Memory Corruption—are novel loss formulations that exploit TBP-unique structural constraints (fixed query budget, recurrent hidden-state propagation, built-in memory banks/SSMs) rather than reusing TBD attack logic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As TBP trackers (MOTR, MeMOTR, Samba, CO-MOT) become the dominant paradigm for MOT in safety-critical deployments, this is the first systematic vulnerability analysis showing that their core architectural features—designed for robustness—introduce new attack surfaces that prior TBD defenses do not address. The finding that a single-frame perturbation induces persistent 15+ frame tracking failure (complete system reset) highlights a fragility that is qualitatively different from TBD's per-frame association errors. Key open questions include: closed-loop validation on real hardware (the paper only uses calibrated simulations), cross-architecture black-box transferability, and whether the identified vulnerabilities can be mitigated without sacrificing TBP's end-to-end advantages.
