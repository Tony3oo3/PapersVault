## Problem

Real-world design tasks require simultaneous integration of multiple heterogeneous visual conditions (depth, edges, subject references, backgrounds), yet existing multi-conditional diffusion frameworks either handle only one condition or naively stack all of them with uniform fusion. This one-size-fits-all approach causes condition interference, redundant computation, and degraded generation quality because geometric, semantic, and appearance cues have fundamentally different roles at different denoising stages and network depths.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260581/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a text prompt plus up to four visual conditions (depth map, canny edge map, subject reference image, background image), all projected into the same latent space via a frozen VAE. Uniquely, the number of active conditions is not fixed: the Condition Adaptation Module dynamically selects a subset per MMDiT block and per diffusion timestep, so the effective input dimensionality varies throughout generation. The output is a single generated image at the base model's native resolution (FLUX.1), with no test-time tuning or iterative refinement required.

## Method

DynFusion introduces a lightweight Condition Adaptation Module (CAM, <1% of total parameters) that takes noise tokens as input and predicts a binary mask over condition tokens via decoupled global-local aggregation (average pooling along token and embedding dimensions, fused through separate MLPs). The mask determines which conditions are activated at each MMDiT block and timestep, with Gumbel-Softmax enabling differentiable training and an attention-mask trick preserving batch parallelism. To prevent cross-condition information leakage, Decoupled Multi-Modal Attention (DMMA) restricts conditional tokens to self-attend only within their own modality while noise/text tokens attend to all active conditions. A Fusion-LoRA module in the denoising branch rectifies the noise latent distribution to accommodate the dynamically varying condition combinations, and the training objective combines flow-matching loss with a sparsity loss that penalizes FLOPs relative to a uniform-activation baseline, balancing quality against computational overhead.

## Results

DynFusion achieves the best FID across all four tasks on FLUX.1: 6.52 (Multi-Spatial), 4.53 (Subject-Insertion), 6.21 (Subject-Depth), and 5.72 (Subject-Canny), outperforming the closest competitor UniCombine by 0.77–1.03 FID points. It also attains the highest SSIM (0.66, 0.80, 0.56, 0.64) and subject-consistency scores (CLIP-I up to 97.21, DINO up to 93.14). Crucially, DynFusion halves the inference FLOPs (7.76–8.27T vs. 16.21–16.92T for UniCombine/OminiControl) while running ~40% faster (2.02–2.09 it/s vs. 1.42–1.46 it/s), demonstrating simultaneous gains in quality and efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DynFusion consistently outperforms all seven baselines on every quality metric across all four tasks. Against the strongest FLUX-based competitor UniCombine, DynFusion reduces FID by 0.77 (Multi-Spatial), 0.61 (Subject-Insertion), 0.71 (Subject-Depth), and 0.69 (Subject-Canny), while also improving SSIM by 0.05–0.07 and CLIP-I by 0.26–0.77 points. OminiControl, the other FLUX-based baseline, lags substantially behind on FID (gaps of 4.2–6.7) and subject consistency (CLIP-I gaps up to 11.8), confirming that uniform multi-condition stacking degrades generation. Older SD-based methods (UniControl, UniControlNet, Ctrl-X, ControlNet+IP-Adapter) are far behind on all metrics, with FID values 2–8× higher. Notably, DynFusion achieves these quality gains while consuming roughly half the FLOPs of both UniCombine and OminiControl and running ~40% faster, a direct benefit of its adaptive condition gating that skips inactive conditions at each MMDiT block.

## Relation to Prior Work

DynFusion extends the multi-conditional diffusion transformer lineage initiated by OminiControl (which concatenates condition tokens with noise embeddings and uses task-specific LoRA) and UniCombine (which unifies multi-conditional combination via cross-attention). The key departure is from the uniform fusion strategy adopted by these and earlier works (Cocktail, UniControlNet), which treat all condition modalities identically regardless of timestep or network depth. DynFusion introduces a data-driven Condition Adaptation Module that learns *which* conditions to activate *when* and *where*, replacing static stacking with a differentiable gating mechanism trained via Gumbel-Softmax, and pairs it with decoupled multi-modal attention to prevent cross-condition information leakage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in design-oriented generation where multiple heterogeneous cues (depth, edges, subject references) must coexist, and demonstrates that naive uniform fusion actively degrades quality through condition interference—a finding confirmed by the ablation where activating all conditions yields worse FID than selective activation. The ~2× FLOPs reduction (8.27T vs. 16.92T) with simultaneous quality gains makes multi-conditional generation more deployable in production pipelines. Open questions include whether the CAM generalizes to unseen condition types at inference, how the approach scales to video or 3D generation, and whether the learned activation dynamics transfer across base models beyond FLUX.
