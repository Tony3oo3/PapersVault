## Problem

Standard zero-shot VLM classifiers rely on textual prototypes derived from frozen text encoders and handcrafted prompts, which often yield correlated or weakly separated embeddings that limit discriminability—especially for fine-grained or semantically similar classes. While prompt tuning and visual adaptation have been extensively studied, the geometry of the text encoder's embedding space remains largely unexplored as a lever for improving downstream task performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260186/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes only class names (no images or captions) expanded into a small set of prompt templates (e.g., three templates per class), which are encoded by the text encoder to produce K×d embedding matrices. The output is a set of orthonormalized text prototypes that serve as drop-in replacements for standard CLIP/MetaCLIP textual classifiers, usable across zero-shot, few-shot (CoOp, CLAP), and test-time adaptation (MTA, TPT, StatA) pipelines without modifying the vision backbone or downstream adaptation logic.

## Method

ORION fine-tunes the text encoder via LoRA (rank 8, <5% trainable parameters) by minimizing a composite loss: a Frobenius-norm penalty ‖XXᵀ − I‖² that softly enforces pairwise orthogonality among class embeddings, plus a deviation penalty ‖X − V‖² anchoring the refined prototypes to the original averaged zero-shot embeddings. The orthogonality weight λ is scheduled to increase by 1.15× per epoch over 20 training steps. The paper provides a probabilistic interpretation via Huygens' theorem, showing the orthogonality penalty implicitly increases the in-between cluster scatter in a Gaussian K-means likelihood, thereby reducing overlap between class-conditional densities without explicit access to image features. A key design choice is the soft penalty over a hard SVD-based orthogonal Procrustes solution, which preserves semantic relationships among fine-grained classes (e.g., different land types) while still promoting angular separation.

## Results

On zero-shot classification across 11 datasets, ORION improves average Top-1 accuracy from 63.70% to 66.46% (+2.76) for CLIP ViT-B/16, from 71.34% to 72.85% (+1.51) for CLIP ViT-L/14, and from 69.07% to 69.80% (+0.73) for MetaCLIP. In the few-shot setting, ORION yields the largest gains at 1-shot (CoOp: 59.31→61.82, +2.51; CLAP: 60.75→62.75, +2.00) and sustains improvements up to 16-shot (CLAP: 70.04→74.99, +4.95). For test-time adaptation, ORION boosts MTA from 65.87% to 67.53% (+1.66) and TPT from 65.09% to 66.45% (+1.36) on average, with the strongest per-dataset gains on fine-grained benchmarks such as EuroSAT (+10.0% zero-shot) and DTD (+13.6% CLAP 4-shot).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORION is evaluated as a plug-and-play text-encoder replacement against seven baselines spanning three adaptation regimes. In zero-shot, it outperforms CLIP ViT-B/16 by +2.76% average (66.46 vs. 63.70), CLIP ViT-L/14 by +1.51% (72.85 vs. 71.34), and MetaCLIP by +0.73% (69.80 vs. 69.07), with the largest per-dataset gain on EuroSAT (+10.0% for ViT-B/16). In few-shot, ORION consistently beats CoOp at every shot level (e.g., +2.51% at 1-shot, +2.20% at 16-shot) and CLAP (e.g., +2.00% at 1-shot, +4.95% at 16-shot), with the most dramatic improvements on texture-rich datasets like DTD (+13.6% CLAP 4-shot) and EuroSAT (+16.3% CLAP 16-shot). For test-time adaptation, ORION improves MTA by +1.66% average (67.53 vs. 65.87) and TPT by +1.36% (66.45 vs. 65.09), and delivers consistent gains under both StatA batch-realistic (+0.86 to +1.23 across effective-class regimes) and online-realistic protocols (+1.09 to +1.61 across correlation levels). The only notable drops are minor (≤0.5%) on a handful of already-saturated datasets (e.g., SUN with ViT-L/14, Flowers 4-shot CoOp), confirming that ORION's orthogonal regularization is broadly beneficial without introducing regressions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VLM adaptation paradigm—prompt tuning (CoOp, CoCoOp), adapter-based fine-tuning (CLAP, TIP-Adapter), and visual encoder adaptation (TPT, MTA)—by instead fine-tuning the text encoder itself, which prior work has largely kept frozen. It builds on CLIP/MetaCLIP as the base model and on LoRA for parameter-efficient adaptation, but introduces a novel composite loss combining a Frobenius-norm orthogonality penalty with a prototype-deviation term, a formulation not previously applied to VLM textual prototypes. The theoretical grounding via Huygens' theorem and the K-means/MLE connection is a new analytical lens on why orthogonal text embeddings should improve zero-shot and adaptation performance, extending earlier orthogonal-regularization ideas from visual feature decorrelation (Bansal et al., Huang et al.) to the text-embedding manifold of VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a text-only, image-free fine-tuning step (using only class names) can serve as a universal plug-and-play upgrade across zero-shot, few-shot, and test-time adaptation settings, which lowers the barrier to improving deployed VLMs without access to labeled images. The SVD ablation (Table 6) revealing that hard orthogonality actually degrades performance while the soft penalty preserves fine-grained semantic structure is a useful caution against over-constraining embedding geometry. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very large class sets (K >> d), whether it extends to open-set or compositional recognition, and whether the per-task LoRA fine-tuning can be replaced by a single task-agnostic orthogonalization step.
