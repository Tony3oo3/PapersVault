## Problem

Masked Image Modeling (MIM) objectives cause ViT representations to encode substantial non-semantic positional noise, degrading zero-shot downstream performance. While positional collapse is a known issue, the extent of this noise across state-of-the-art MIM models has not been systematically quantified, and existing mitigations (e.g., RASA) require additional training. This paper asks: how much of a MIM model's representation is semantically invariant noise, and can it be removed without retraining?

## Input / Output

SOAP operates on the D-dimensional patch embedding space of any pretrained ViT backbone (e.g., 768-d for ViT-B/16). The PCA covariance is estimated over ImageNet-1k train using Welford's online algorithm, and the Semantic Invariance Score is computed by contrasting binary activation patterns on ImageNet-1k validation against 50,000 synthetic non-semantic images (mixture of pink noise, modulated white noise, and random low-frequency gradients). The output is a single D×D linear projection matrix P_φ that transforms raw patch embeddings z into denoised embeddings ẑ = P_φ z, applicable as a frozen head before any downstream evaluation (TokenCut, kNN segmentation, kNN classification).

## Method

The method proceeds in three steps. First, PCA decomposes the patch embedding space into orthogonal principal components, revealing that leading components in MIM models encode strong left/right and top/bottom positional bias. Second, a Semantic Invariance Score (SI-score) quantifies how invariant each component is to semantic content by comparing Bernoulli activation distributions on real vs. synthetic images, using a Dice-Sørensen-like formulation that penalizes uncertainty (scores near 0.5). Third, SOAP constructs a Gram-Schmidt projection P_φ = I − VWV^⊤ that subtracts the contribution of the most semantically invariant components, where the diagonal weight matrix W is shaped by a Fermi-window scaling function over the rank of SI-scores, providing smooth control over how many components are suppressed. The entire pipeline is training-free, model-agnostic, and reduces to a single linear head attached to any pretrained backbone.

## Results

SOAP consistently improves zero-shot performance across all MIM-based models. On salient segmentation (TokenCut), CAPI improves from 72.46 to 85.22 max Fβ on ECSSD (+12.76), and MAE from 79.95 to 82.09 max Fβ (+2.14). On kNN segmentation (ADE20K), DINOv2 gains +0.56 IoU (40.25→40.81) and MAE gains +0.77 IoU (11.88→12.65). On kNN classification (ImageNet), MAE improves from 59.30 to 60.62 Acc@1 (+1.32), while DINOv2 gains +0.27 Acc@1 (82.32→82.59). Non-MIM models (DINO) show no change, confirming the effect is specific to MIM-trained representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only direct external baseline comparison is against RASA [36], a trained post-hoc module that suppresses positional cues by learning to predict patch positions. On Franca ViT-B/14, SOAP substantially outperforms RASA on ECSSD salient segmentation (84.18 vs. 68.22 max Fβ, a +15.96 gap) and matches or slightly exceeds it on ImageNet kNN classification (65.08 vs. 64.89 Acc@1). The authors attribute this gap to SOAP directly identifying and removing non-semantic components via PCA and the SI-score, whereas RASA must first learn positional cues through 9 trained linear layers. Internally, SOAP improves over uncorrected embeddings for every MIM model tested (DINOv2, DINOv3, iBOT, CAPI, MAE, I-JEPA) across all three evaluation tasks, while leaving non-MIM models (DINO) unchanged—confirming the positional noise is a MIM-specific artifact. The scaling function (Fermi window) is necessary: without it, over-suppression degrades performance (e.g., DINOv2 kNN Acc@1 drops from 77.10 to 77.07, CAPI from 56.44 to 55.72).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the growing body of work identifying artifacts in MIM-based representations (register tokens, stochastic positional embeddings, RASA, SINDER) by providing a unified, model-agnostic diagnostic—the Semantic Invariance Score—that quantifies non-semantic noise across all MIM variants simultaneously. It departs from RASA, the closest prior method, by requiring no training and by targeting all semantically invariant components rather than only learned positional cues. The PCA-based decomposition and Gram-Schmidt projection draw on classical linear algebra tools repurposed for the SSL representation analysis setting, while the Fermi-window scaling borrows from signal processing to control suppression sparsity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic evidence that positional noise is pervasive across all MIM architectures—including RoPE-based DINOv3 and latent-space models—clarifies a key reason MIM models underperform contrastive models on zero-shot dense tasks, and SOAP offers a zero-training, plug-and-play remedy that practitioners can immediately adopt. However, the linear mixture-of-sources assumption is a simplification, evaluation is restricted to raw patch embeddings without learnable heads, and the modest gains (typically 1–5 points) leave open whether the noise is truly causal for the performance gap or merely correlated. Future work should examine SOAP's interaction with fine-tuning pipelines and whether the phenomenon extends to video or multimodal MIM settings.
