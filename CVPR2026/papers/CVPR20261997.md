## Problem

Defocus blur in spike cameras—caused by shallow depth of field or lens refocusing delays—remains a critical yet unaddressed degradation in spike-based image reconstruction. Unlike motion blur, which spike sensors naturally mitigate through high temporal sampling, defocus fundamentally alters the spatial distribution of photons at the sensor level, distorting spike emission patterns in ways that conventional temporal-domain techniques cannot recover. Existing spike-camera autofocus methods are purely preventive and cannot restore already-defocused spike streams for downstream reconstruction or perception tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a variable-length sequence of binary spike planes (e.g., 200 time windows) captured by a neuromorphic spike camera operating at up to 40,000 Hz, where each plane encodes which pixels fired at a given timestep. The output is a single sharp intensity image reconstructed from the defocused spike stream. Training uses 350 synthetic defocus pairs from the DPDD dataset, and evaluation includes 32 synthetic test pairs plus 75 real-world spike sequences captured with a physical spike camera.

## Method

DeSpike is an end-to-end framework that first models defocus formation via a thin-lens approximation, showing how optical blur reduces per-pixel photon density and delays spike emission in the integrate-and-fire circuit. The architecture employs five non-spiking IF neurons with differentiable membrane integration over temporal windows of 64–192 steps, each incorporating a pixel-specific FPN correction factor to compensate for hardware noise. A bank of learnable discrete Gaussian PSF kernels (initialized to approximate physical defocus spreads) is convolved with each temporal feature to produce deblurred candidates at multiple blur levels. A Transformer-based attention mechanism adaptively fuses these multi-scale deblurring results, and a three-stage coarse-to-fine iterative refinement progressively recovers sharp structures. Training uses a hierarchical multi-scale loss combining pixel-wise MSE across all temporal-spatial branches with FFT and LPIPS terms at the final output.

## Results

On simulated defocused spike data (32 test pairs from DPDD), DeSpike achieves PSNR 18.94 dB, SSIM 0.57, and LPIPS 0.25, outperforming the best baseline (RSIR-NRKNet at 17.77 dB PSNR) by over 1.1 dB. On real-world defocused spike sequences (75 samples), DeSpike attains the best RankIQA score of 4.74 (lower is better) and the highest Contrast of 0.15, surpassing the strongest baseline (TFI-GKMNet at 4.82 RankIQA, Spk2ImgNet-GKMNet at 0.12 Contrast). The method also demonstrates robustness across varying focal distances and temporal window sizes, maintaining effective deblurring even with shorter spike streams.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeSpike is compared against five spike reconstruction methods (TFI, TFP, RSIR, Spk2ImgNet) each paired with two frame-based deblurring networks (GKMNet, NRKNet), forming 16 two-stage baselines plus 8 retrained variants. On simulated data, DeSpike achieves the best PSNR (18.94 dB) and LPIPS (0.25), beating the strongest two-stage baseline RSIR-NRKNet (17.77 dB, 0.36 LPIPS) by 1.17 dB and 0.11 LPIPS respectively; however, TFP-GKMNet marginally edges out DeSpike on SSIM (0.58 vs. 0.57). On real-world data, DeSpike dominates all metrics: it achieves the lowest RankIQA (4.74 vs. 4.82 for TFI-GKMNet) and the highest Contrast (0.15 vs. 0.12 for Spk2ImgNet-GKMNet), with a particularly large gap over the retrained variants (e.g., 4.74 vs. 8.78 RankIQA for RSIR-GKMNet*). The retrained two-stage baselines degrade substantially on real data, confirming that the reconstruct-then-deblur pipeline loses information irrecoverably, whereas DeSpike's end-to-end design preserves defocus-aware spike structure throughout.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected research threads: spike-camera image reconstruction (TFP, TFI, Spk2ImgNet, RSIR) and frame-based defocus deblurring (GKMNet, NRKNet, DPDNet). It departs from prior spike reconstruction work, which has focused exclusively on motion blur and sensor noise, by introducing a physics-based thin-lens model that explicitly characterizes how optical defocus alters the integrate-and-fire spike emission process. The learnable discrete PSF prior module is conceptually inspired by GKMNet's Gaussian kernel mixture, but is re-embedded within a multi-temporal-scale spiking architecture with FPN compensation, making it the first architecture to jointly handle the temporal and spatial distortions unique to defocused spike streams.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Defocus is a practical degradation in autonomous driving and robotics where lens refocusing lags behind scene dynamics, and this work provides the first end-to-end solution for recovering sharp images from already-defocused spike data—something autofocus methods cannot do post-hoc. Key limitations include a small real-world dataset (75 sequences), synthetic data generated from the dual-pixel DPDD benchmark (which may not fully capture single-lens defocus characteristics), and a modest training set of 350 pairs. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to combined motion-plus-defocus scenarios, whether it operates in real time on embedded hardware, and how it scales to higher-resolution spike sensors.
