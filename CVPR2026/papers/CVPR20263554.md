## Problem

Autoregressive visual generators trained with strictly causal next-token prediction receive only local feedback at each step, which weakens global spatial coherence and slows convergence. The paper investigates whether injecting foresight—training signals derived from future tokens—can strengthen causal modeling in visual AR without altering the inference procedure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263554/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an ImageNet class label as conditioning and generates a 256×256 RGB image by autoregressively predicting discrete VQ-GAN tokens in raster order. During training, a 10-crop augmentation (four corners, center, and their horizontal flips) is applied, with one view randomly selected per epoch. At inference, all foresight-related components (projection heads, EMA encoder, bidirectional encoder) are discarded, so decoding is strictly token-by-token and computationally identical to the baseline LlamaGen model.

## Method

Mirai augments the standard next-token prediction loss with a foresight alignment loss that supervises the AR model's intermediate hidden states (at layer l, typically 8/12) to match future-aware representations on a 2D spatial grid, using cosine similarity through lightweight MLP projection heads. Two variants instantiate the foresight source: Mirai-E uses the model's own EMA as a unidirectional encoder to provide explicit, position-indexed foresight at K nearby 2D spatial neighbors, while Mirai-I uses a frozen bidirectional encoder (DINOv2) to provide implicit, globally contextualized foresight at the co-located position. A key empirical finding is that 2D grid alignment consistently outperforms 1D raster-scan alignment, and internal-representation-level alignment outperforms output-level multi-token prediction, which introduces harmful gradient interference. The total loss is L_NTP + λ·L_Foresight, with λ scheduled via a step decay (e.g., 2→1 at midpoint) for Mirai-E, and the entire foresight machinery is training-only with zero inference overhead.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, Mirai-I reduces FID from 5.34 to 4.34 on LlamaGen-B (111M, 300 epochs) and from 3.16 to 2.59 on LlamaGen-XL (775M), outperforming all AR-based methods. Mirai-E achieves FID 4.49 (B) and 2.76 (XL) with comparable gains. In terms of training efficiency, 40 epochs of Mirai-I matches the FID of 400 epochs of vanilla LlamaGen-B (~10× speedup), and 80 epochs of Mirai-E achieves a similar result (~5× speedup).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the vanilla LlamaGen baselines, both Mirai variants consistently improve FID across all three model scales (B: 5.34→4.34/4.49; L: 3.73→3.07/3.29; XL: 3.16→2.59/2.76), with Mirai-I delivering the largest FID gains. Mirai-I also improves Inception Score and Recall at every scale, though it slightly reduces Precision (e.g., 0.87→0.84 on B). Compared to parallelized AR (VAR-d20, FID 2.95) and GAN baselines (GigaGAN, FID 3.45), LlamaGen-XL + Mirai-I (FID 2.59) achieves the best FID among all AR and GAN methods. However, it still trails the strongest diffusion models—DiT-XL (FID 2.27) and SiT-XL (FID 2.15)—by roughly 0.3–0.5 FID points, though it matches or exceeds them on Recall (0.56 vs. 0.57/0.60). Among AR methods specifically, Mirai-I on XL surpasses VAR-d20 by 0.36 FID and VAR-d16 by 0.96 FID, establishing it as the top strictly-autoregressive approach on this benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LlamaGen's GPT-style autoregressive image generation framework, introducing a foresight alignment loss that supervises intermediate transformer representations with future-aware features. It departs from Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) in language modeling by demonstrating that output-level multi-token prediction actually degrades visual AR, and instead aligns internal hidden states to foresight from either the model's own EMA (explicit) or a frozen bidirectional encoder like DINOv2 (implicit). Conceptually, it is related to REPA's representation alignment for diffusion transformers, but reframes the signal as causality-compatible foresight on a 2D spatial grid rather than current-position semantic distillation. The systematic ablation across injection level, spatial layout (1D vs. 2D), and foresight source positions it as an empirical study that carves out a design space prior work had not explored for visual AR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× convergence speedup (40 epochs matching 400-epoch baseline) with zero inference overhead makes AR image generation substantially more practical, potentially closing the training-cost gap with diffusion models. The finding that foresight strengthens rather than breaks causality challenges the assumption that strictly local supervision is necessary for causal decoding, opening the door to richer training signals in other sequential generation tasks. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to text-to-image generation, higher resolutions, or other AR paradigms (e.g., VAR's next-scale prediction), and whether the gains persist when the base model is already well-trained. The negative result that output-level MTP harms visual AR is a useful caution for practitioners attempting to port language-modeling tricks to vision.
