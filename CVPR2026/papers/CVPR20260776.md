## Problem

Existing light field denoising methods either require large-scale noisy-clean pairs that are difficult to acquire and fail to generalize to unseen noise, or extend monocular blind-spot strategies that rely on local spatial continuity and struggle with high-frequency details. The paper identifies that the inherent multiview photometric consistency of LF images provides a far more reliable supervisory signal than spatial context, but extending the blind-spot principle to the view level introduces a non-trivial challenge: designing view masks that preserve geometric invariance so a single network can denoise all views without training multiple models.

## Input / Output

The method takes a noisy 4D light field tensor (U×V×H×W) and internally processes 7×7 view crops at 48×48 spatial resolution. It produces a complete denoised LF by fusing outputs from four rotated branches, a view-invariant latent representation volume (4C×H×W) encoding scene geometry, and a per-pixel depth probability distribution over D predefined depth planes. For light field microscopy, the latent volume is rendered through PSFs rather than the learned decoder to recover the explicit 3D representation.

## Method

LF-BVN extends blind-spot denoising to the view level via a Geometric Invariance Mask (GIM) that masks 25% of views in a fixed angular pattern, exploiting the property that disparity relationships remain invariant under concurrent spatial-angular rotation of the entire LF. A four-branch architecture with shared parameters processes the LF rotated by 0°, 90°, 180°, and 270°, each branch feeding the masked input through a UNet-based denoising module that constructs a plane sweep volume and outputs a latent representation volume. A refocusing-based depth estimation module predicts pixel-wise depth probabilities from the PSV, supervised by the noisy center view (which is masked and thus cannot be learned as identity). A reconstruction consistency loss (ℓ_rc) enforces that the inverse-rotated latent volumes from all branches agree, while the total training objective combines the blind-view reconstruction loss, ℓ_rc, and ℓ_depth with fixed weights (α=0.3, β=0.1).

## Results

On the HCI dataset with Gaussian noise (σ=20), LF-BVN achieves 37.86 dB PSNR and 0.960 SSIM, outperforming all self-supervised baselines and matching or exceeding supervised methods on SSIM. On the zero-shot HCIold and DLFD datasets, LF-BVN achieves 37.72/0.953 and 36.04/0.937 respectively, surpassing the best supervised method (SRDNet) by 0.94 dB PSNR and 0.016 SSIM on HCIold. Under out-of-distribution noise (Poisson λ=30, Uniform [-50,50]), LF-BVN maintains 36.09/0.932 and 36.46/0.943 on HCI, while supervised methods drop by 3–8 dB. For depth estimation from denoised LFs, LF-BVN achieves the lowest MAE (0.122) and MSE (0.056) on HCI, indicating superior cross-view geometric consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LF-BVN outperforms all self-supervised baselines by a wide margin: over V2V3D (the closest self-supervised LF method) by 1.65 dB PSNR and 0.051 SSIM on HCI (σ=20), and over monocular blind-spot methods B2U and ZSBlind by 4+ dB. Against supervised LF methods, LF-BVN achieves the highest SSIM across all three datasets (0.960 vs. 0.928 for MSPNet on HCI), while SRDNet edges it out by 0.31 dB PSNR on HCI alone (38.17 vs. 37.86) but LF-BVN reverses this on HCIold (+0.94 dB) and DLFD (+0.60 dB), demonstrating better generalization. Under unseen noise types (Poisson, Uniform), supervised methods degrade by 3–8 dB while LF-BVN retains 36+ dB PSNR, confirming its noise-agnostic advantage. For downstream depth estimation, LF-BVN's denoised LFs yield the best MAE (0.122) and MSE (0.056) on HCI, beating SRDNet (0.158/0.097) and V2V3D (0.187/0.124), validating superior cross-view photometric consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the blind-spot self-supervised denoising principle (Noise2Void, Krull et al. 2019) from pixel-level masking in single images to view-level masking in light fields, coining the 'blind-view' formulation. It departs from prior self-supervised LF work (V2V3D) that required multiple independent networks per view subset, instead exploiting the rotational geometric invariance of light fields to design a single Geometric Invariance Mask with weight sharing across four rotated branches. The latent representation volume concept is borrowed from Tanay & Maggioni (2024) for cross-view consistency enforcement, and the refocusing-based depth module adapts standard plane-sweep refocusing as a self-supervised signal without requiring clean depth ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is the strong generalization to unseen noise types (Poisson, Uniform), where supervised LF denoisers degrade by 3–5 dB while LF-BVN maintains or even improves performance, suggesting the self-supervised geometric signal is more robust than noise-specific supervision. The GIM design is a reusable technique for any LF task requiring angular-domain masking with geometric consistency. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger view arrays (beyond 7×7), whether the four-branch overhead can be reduced, and whether the assumption of non-coherent cross-view noise holds for structured sensor artifacts in real hardware.
