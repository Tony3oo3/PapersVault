## Problem

Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) and Model Compression (MC) methods are typically developed and evaluated in isolation, yet real-world edge deployments require both simultaneously. Naively combining them is inefficient: for example, applying DoRA PEFT after 50% compression of a ViT-S/16 increases the model size by 19%, erasing a large fraction of the compression benefit. The paper asks whether a single parameter-recombination framework can natively support both tasks without the overhead of stacking separate methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262741/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CRISP takes a pretrained model's weight matrices (ViT-S/16, ViT-B/16, or LLaMA-3.2-1B) and produces a factorized representation consisting of shared basis matrices B and per-layer mixing matrices A, which together reconstruct the full weight tensor. The retrofitting phase requires no dataset samples (completing in under a minute for ViTs or 30 minutes for LLaMA), while the optional compression stage uses only 2% of ImageNet. At inference, the factorized weights are materialized into standard weight matrices, so the model accepts the same RGB images or text prompts as the original and produces class labels or text answers unchanged.

## Method

CRISP reparameterizes each layer's weight as W_i = reshape(B_i' (σ(A_i') ⊙ A_i'), (d_out, d_in)), where B_i' is a shared basis matrix of shape (u×r) and A_i' is a small per-layer mixing matrix of shape (r×s). The element-wise SiLU gating σ(A)⊙A acts as a built-in regularizer on the mixing coefficients without introducing extra hyperparameters, and the new hyperparameter s independently controls the mixing matrix width, decoupling PEFT capacity from MC compression. For compression, basis matrices are shared across consecutive layer groups and r is reduced; for PEFT, the basis is frozen and only the tiny A matrices (fewer than 200 parameters per task in some settings) are updated. Aggressive compression uses a two-stage teacher-student procedure: a full-parameter teacher is first created via Neural Mimicry loss, then a compressed student is initialized from the teacher's top-r eigenvectors and trained with KL divergence, MSE, and per-layer feature matching on 2% of ImageNet.

## Results

On VTAB-1K (19 tasks), CRISP achieves 59.2% overall accuracy, outperforming the prior best PEFT method SSF (57.8%) by 1.4% while using 28% fewer trainable parameters (5×10⁻³% vs. 7×10⁻³% of the base ViT-S/16 model). For model compression at 50% parameter reduction on ViT-B/16, CRISP reaches 83.3% average accuracy across six fine-grained benchmarks, surpassing DGMR (81.9%) by 1.4% while using only 2% of ImageNet for distillation. In the combined PEFT+MC setting (~45M total parameters), CRISP achieves 88.8% average, beating the best prior combination (DGMR+SSF at 87.9%) by ~1% and RECAST's unified approach (83.7%) by 5.1%. On LLaMA-3.2-1B at 30% compression, CRISP attains 38.0% average across seven commonsense reasoning benchmarks, a 5% gain over the best baseline (Basis-Sharing at 33.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CRISP outperforms all 18 baselines across every evaluation setting. In PEFT on VTAB-1K, CRISP (59.2%) beats the closest competitors SSF (57.8%) and RoAD (57.5%) by 1.4% and 1.7% respectively, with the largest margin on Structured tasks (36.4% vs. 34.4% for RoAD) where linear-update methods like LoRA (32.1%) and RECAST (32.0%) plateau. In MC at 50% reduction, CRISP (83.3%) surpasses DGMR (81.9%), RECAST (79.3%), and Isomorphic Pruning (78.8%), while using only 2% of ImageNet versus the full dataset required by DGMR and RDHP. In the combined PEFT+MC setting, CRISP (88.8%) edges out the best prior combination DGMR+SSF (87.9%) by ~1%, and decisively beats RECAST's unified approach (83.7%) by 5.1%; WeCoLoRA's self-combination collapses to 10.7%, confirming its decomposition is unsuitable for direct PEFT. On LLMs (LLaMA-3.2-1B, 30% compression), CRISP (38.0%) leads Basis-Sharing (33.1%), PruneNet (32.4%), and DFJR (32.4%) by 5–6%, demonstrating cross-architecture generalization that prior MC methods lack. In efficiency, CRISP matches LoRA/DoRA training throughput (163 vs. 165/178 samp/s) and inference speed (657 samp/s, 1.52 ms), while RECAST is ~50× slower in training (3.3 samp/s) and ~94× slower in inference (7 samp/s) due to its K-fold template averaging.

## Relation to Prior Work

CRISP directly extends RECAST (same first author), which restricted mixing coefficients to a vector averaged over K templates, by generalizing to a full matrix A^{'rs} with SiLU element-wise gating, enabling benefits across a wider range of parameter budgets. It also builds on Basis Sharing's cross-layer basis-sharing mechanism for compression, but extends it from LLMs to ViTs and adds the PEFT capability that Basis Sharing lacks. The paper positions itself against the broader PEFT (LoRA, DoRA, SSF, RoAD) and MC (Isomorphic Pruning, DGMR, RDHP) literatures, arguing that treating these tasks in isolation leads to inefficient compositions, and that a single factorized reparameterization can serve both roles without redundant adapters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified PEFT+MC framework is practically relevant for edge deployment where models must be both small and adaptable, and the paper demonstrates that a single hyperparameter s can trade off compression vs. adaptability without additional tuning. However, LLM results remain weak (38% average on commonsense reasoning at 30% compression vs. 51.4% original), and only a 1B model is tested, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger language models. Future directions include learned hyperparameter selection, combining CRISP with quantization and pruning, and extending to larger LLMs where compression and adaptation demands are more acute.
