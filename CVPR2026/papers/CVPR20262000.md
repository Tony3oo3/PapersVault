## Problem

Self-consistency improves LLM outputs by sampling diverse responses and selecting the most consistent one, but this paradigm relies on identity matching to check consistency, which is trivial for discrete text outputs but inapplicable to continuous visual outputs like motion trajectories. The paper asks how to define and operationalize a notion of consistency over geometric shapes so that self-consistency can be extended to LLM-based visual generation and verification without supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262000/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For generation, the method takes a text prompt describing a trajectory shape (e.g., 'Move the circle in a logarithmic spiral path') and produces a single SVG motion trajectory by selecting the most self-consistent sample from N=19 LLM-generated programs (executed via Greensock in a 400×400 px canvas, resampled to 100 arc-length points). For verification, it additionally takes a query trajectory and outputs a binary match/mismatch label by testing membership in the recovered shape family. The LLM is prompted with a diversity-encouraging strategy to cover the tails of the trajectory distribution.

## Method

The key insight is to model the set of trajectories matching a prompt as a shape family F(o, W) = {w(o) | w ∈ W}, where o is a prototype trajectory and W is a Lie transformation group (rigid, similarity, affine, anisotropic similarity, etc.) from a partially ordered hierarchy. Two trajectories are deemed consistent if their warp-invariant distance d_W (computed via generalized ICP) falls below a threshold τ, enabling DBSCAN clustering under the appropriate metric. Since the correct W is unknown a priori, the paper proposes two unsupervised decision criteria: Majority-Consensus (traverse the hierarchy from most to least restrictive, selecting the first group yielding a >50% dominant cluster) and Hierarchical-Consistency (traverse from least to most restrictive, selecting the most restrictive group where the largest cluster remains intact). The centroid of the largest cluster becomes the prototype, and verification reduces to checking whether a query trajectory's distance to this prototype is below τ.

## Results

On the Motion Trajectory Benchmark (224 prompts), the self-consistency approach improves generation accuracy by 4–6 percentage points over single-sample LLM generation: from 62.1% to 68.0% (GPT-4.1) and from 79.1% to 83.3% (GPT-5) under the Majority-Consensus criterion, with the oracle setting reaching 68.5% and 83.5% respectively. For verification (2,240 query trajectories), the Hierarchical-Consistency criterion achieves the best unsupervised F1 of 84.6, surpassing GPT-5's F1 of 79.0 and GPT-4.1's F1 of 75.6, while the oracle setting reaches F1 85.6 with 87.9% precision. Performance stabilizes with as few as N=10 sampled trajectories, and the method is robust to the clustering threshold τ (only 7.2 F1 drop across a 32× sweep).

## Baselines & Comparisons

For generation, the only baseline is LLM-Direct (a single trajectory sampled per prompt). The self-consistency method consistently outperforms it: Majority-Consensus gains +5.9 pp (GPT-4.1) and +4.2 pp (GPT-5), while Hierarchical-Consistency gains +4.6 pp and +3.5 pp respectively. For verification, the baselines are GPT-4.1 and GPT-5 used as VLM judges (given a prompt and a rendered trajectory, asked to label match/mismatch). GPT-4.1 is poorly calibrated (90% predicted-positive rate vs. 50% base rate), yielding high recall (96.9) but low precision (62.0) and F1 75.6. GPT-5 is more balanced (precision 74.0, recall 84.7, F1 79.0). The proposed Hierarchical-Consistency verification achieves F1 84.6 (+5.6 over GPT-5) with 80.5% precision and 89.0% recall, while Majority-Consensus trades recall (66.1) for the highest unsupervised precision (85.8, +11.8 over GPT-5). The oracle setting (ground-truth transformation group) reaches F1 85.6, confirming the gap is primarily due to W-estimation rather than the clustering mechanism itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-consistency paradigm (Wang et al., 2022) from natural language reasoning—where consistency is checked via identity matching of discrete outputs—to the visual domain of motion graphics trajectories, where no two samples will pixel-match. The key conceptual departure is using a hierarchy of Lie transformation groups (rigid, similarity, affine, anisotropic similarity) to define warp-invariant distance metrics, replacing the trivial identity-matching step with geometric clustering (DBSCAN under ICP-based distances). It also positions itself against MoVer [20], which verifies motion graphics via first-order logic predicates over low-level motion primitives, arguing that geometric shape families better capture how people actually describe trajectories. The diversity-sampling prompting strategy [31] and program-synthesis framing for LLM generation follow established patterns in visual program synthesis work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a purely geometric, training-free approach can outperform VLM-based verification in precision (85.8% vs 74.0% for GPT-5), suggesting that for tasks with well-defined geometric structure, principled mathematical machinery may be more reliable than general-purpose learned models. However, the scope is narrow: the method only handles single-prototype shape families, cannot resolve ambiguous prompts (e.g., 'curved path'), and the 4–6% generation improvement is modest. The broader open question the authors raise—whether the Lie-group-based consistency framework generalizes to other spatial/shape-based visual domains beyond motion graphics—remains unexplored, and the multi-prototype case (e.g., heptagram {7/2} vs {7/3}) still requires ad-hoc modifications.
