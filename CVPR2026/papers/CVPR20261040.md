## Problem

Hessian-based block reconstruction is a key technique for low-bit PTQ of ViTs, but prior methods (APHQ-ViT, FIMA-Q) approximate the Hessian by averaging per-sample estimates under a sample-independence assumption that silently drops the covariance term. This neglected covariance induces a non-negligible approximation error that grows as bit-width drops below 4-bit, and the iterative multi-backpropagation estimation also incurs substantial computational overhead.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261040/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained ViT (ViT, DeiT, or Swin variants) and a small unlabeled calibration set (1024 images for ImageNet classification, 256 for COCO detection/segmentation) to produce a fully quantized model using standard uniform affine quantizers—channel-wise for weights and layer-wise for activations, including post-softmax and post-GELU tensors. Quantization is evaluated at W4/A4, W3/A3, W2/A4, and W2/A3 bit-widths, with outputs being class labels (ImageNet), bounding boxes, and segmentation masks (COCO).

## Method

LS-ViT formulates Hessian estimation as a least-squares regression: instead of averaging per-sample Hessians, it finds a single matrix H that minimizes the expected squared residual ‖H^(z)Δz − HΔz‖² across all calibration samples, thereby explicitly capturing the covariance term that independence-based averaging discards. Two complementary approximations are solved in closed form—a diagonal Hessian (Ĥ_ii = E[g_iΔz_i]/E[Δz_i²]) capturing per-parameter sensitivity, and a rank-1 Hessian (H = ûûᵀ) capturing dominant off-diagonal interactions—and their reconstruction losses are summed. The resulting loss is optimized per block via QDrop-style forward passes with AdaRound weight-rounding updates, requiring only a single full-model backpropagation per block rather than the 15 used by FIMA-Q, yielding 1.8×–2.7× speedup while achieving higher accuracy, particularly at W2/A3 where the covariance-induced error is largest.

## Results

On ImageNet, LS-ViT achieves state-of-the-art top-1 accuracy across seven ViT/DeiT/Swin models at ultra-low bit-widths. At W3/A3, it reaches 64.10% on ViT-S and 79.40% on Swin-B, outperforming the strongest prior method FIMA-Q by +0.17%p on average. The advantage grows sharply at lower bits: at W2/A4, LS-ViT gains +1.46%p over FIMA-Q on average (e.g., 74.95% on Swin-B vs. 72.36%), and at W2/A3 the average gain reaches +2.89%p (e.g., 67.73% on Swin-B vs. 63.15%). On COCO at W4/A4, LS-ViT also leads in object detection and instance segmentation, achieving 50.6 AP^b / 43.9 AP^m with Cascade Mask R-CNN + Swin-S.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LS-ViT consistently outperforms all 12 compared baselines across every model and bit-width setting. Against the strongest prior method FIMA-Q (which uses Fisher Information Matrix approximation with diagonal + rank-1 Hessian), LS-ViT gains +0.17%p on average at W3/A3, +1.46%p at W2/A4, and +2.89%p at W2/A3, with the largest individual gains on Swin-S (+6.20%p at W2/A3) and Swin-B (+4.58%p at W2/A3). APHQ-ViT (average perturbation Hessian) is surpassed by 1.0–3.2%p depending on bit-width and architecture. Earlier reconstruction-based methods (QDrop, BRECQ, PD-Quant) trail by 5–20+ points at ultra-low bits, confirming that the Hessian approximation quality is the dominant factor. On COCO W4/A4, LS-ViT edges out FIMA-Q by +0.1 to +0.2 AP in both detection and segmentation across all four backbone/detector combinations. Notably, LS-ViT achieves these gains while requiring only 1 full-model forward pass per block (vs. 15 for FIMA-Q), making it 1.8×–2.7× faster in wall-clock training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

LS-ViT sits within the Hessian-based block reconstruction lineage for ViT quantization, building directly on the QDrop pipeline, AdaRound's stochastic rounding updates, and BRECQ's chain-rule reformulation of the reconstruction loss. Its most immediate predecessors are APHQ-ViT and FIMA-Q, which approximate the Hessian by averaging per-sample estimates under an implicit sample-independence assumption. The key conceptual departure is reframing Hessian estimation as a least-squares regression problem over all calibration samples, which naturally recovers a covariance term that simple averaging discards, rather than treating each sample's Hessian as an independent draw.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the missing covariance term provides a clean theoretical explanation for why prior averaging-based methods degrade at sub-4-bit widths, and the least-squares formulation offers a principled fix that is also more computationally efficient (single backpropagation vs. multiple). This makes low-bit ViT quantization more practical for deployment on edge hardware. Open questions include whether the local-constancy assumption on the Hessian remains valid at extreme bit-widths (e.g., 1–2 bit), whether the approach extends to higher-rank approximations beyond rank-1, and whether the same covariance-aware formulation transfers to other architecture families (e.g., LLMs) where Hessian-based PTQ is also active.
