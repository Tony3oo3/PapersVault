## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based video super-resolution methods over-prioritize perceptual synthesis while neglecting fidelity gains from accurate inter-frame alignment and sufficient information compensation. The paper identifies two underexplored aspects: the latent feature domain provides significantly stronger spatial and temporal correlations than the pixel domain for guidance, and warping at an upscaled resolution preserves high-frequency details better than direct latent-space warping, though this benefit is not monotonically increasing with scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263285/cropped_page6_idx4.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of N low-resolution RGB video frames (h×w×3) and reconstructs the corresponding high-resolution sequence at a rescaling factor s (evaluated at 4×, producing sh×sw×3 frames). All diffusion denoising operates in the latent space of a pre-trained VAE, with optical flow computed between s×-upscaled adjacent frames using nearest-neighbor interpolation. The final latent features are decoded by a frozen VAE decoder to yield the HR output.

## Method

DGAF-VSR integrates two modules into each diffusion step of a latent diffusion pipeline: (1) an Optical Guided Warping Module (OGWM) that upscales the previous frame's noise-free latent approximation by s=4 via nearest-neighbor interpolation, warps it with RAFT-estimated bidirectional optical flow, and downscales it back, thereby preserving high-frequency details during alignment; and (2) a Feature-wise Temporal Condition Module (FTCM) that uses the full U-Net architecture (not just the encoder) as a guiding network to inject dense, pixel-to-pixel constraints from the aligned adjacent feature into the current denoising step. The diffusion steps are organized into T/2 forward-backward pairs for bidirectional temporal propagation, and the guiding U-Net is trained with a zero-initialized convolutional output to avoid disrupting the frozen denoising U-Net early in training.

## Results

On REDS4 (4× VSR), DGAF-VSR achieves the best perceptual scores across all five metrics: LPIPS 0.095, DISTS 0.043, MUSIQ 67.90, CLIP-IQA 0.429, and NIQE 2.66, while also attaining the highest PSNR (28.17 dB) and SSIM (0.804) among all DM-based methods. On Vid4, it similarly leads with LPIPS 0.175, DISTS 0.113, MUSIQ 67.95, CLIP-IQA 0.470, NIQE 3.10, PSNR 24.75 dB, and SSIM 0.714. On the real-world VideoLQ dataset, DGAF-VSR outperforms the next-best method (STAR) by 4.46 in MUSIQ and 59.11% in CLIP-IQA. The paper reports a 35.82% DISTS reduction, 0.20 dB PSNR gain over StableVSR, and 30.37% tLPIPS reduction in temporal consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGAF-VSR outperforms all six DM-based baselines (StableVSR, MGLD-VSR, UAV, STAR, DOVE, SeedVR2) on every perceptual and fidelity metric on both REDS4 and Vid4. Against the strongest DM-based competitor StableVSR, it gains 0.20 dB PSNR and 0.009 SSIM on REDS4 (28.17 vs. 27.97) while also improving LPIPS from 0.098 to 0.095 and DISTS from 0.045 to 0.043. Among non-DM-based methods, RVRT and BasicVSR++ still achieve substantially higher PSNR (32.75 and 32.39 dB vs. 28.17 on REDS4) and SSIM (0.911 and 0.907 vs. 0.804), reflecting the known fidelity gap of diffusion-based approaches; however, DGAF-VSR surpasses them on all five perceptual metrics (e.g., LPIPS 0.095 vs. 0.128 for RVRT, a 25.78% reduction). On the real-world VideoLQ dataset, DGAF-VSR leads STAR by 4.46 MUSIQ and 59.11% CLIP-IQA, and outperforms MGLD-VSR by 3.06 MUSIQ. In temporal consistency, DGAF-VSR reduces tLPIPS by 30.37% over StableVSR and by 56.30% over RVRT, confirming that its dense feature guidance and rescaling-based warping yield smoother inter-frame transitions than both DM-based and non-DM-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DM-based VSR line (StableVSR, MGLD-VSR, Upscale-A-Video) by identifying two empirically grounded design principles that prior methods overlooked: (a) the latent feature domain provides substantially stronger spatial-temporal correlations than the pixel domain for guidance, and (b) rescaling-based warping (upscale → warp → downscale) preserves high-frequency content better than direct latent-space warping, but only up to an optimal rescaling factor. Architecturally, it departs from prior DM-based VSR methods that use only the U-Net encoder as a guiding network by adopting the full U-Net (inspired by BrushNet) for dense pixel-to-pixel constraints, and replaces direct latent-space motion compensation (which introduces artifacts in StableVSR) with a simple nearest-neighbor rescaling strategy guided by RAFT optical flow computed at the upscaled resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that perceptual quality, fidelity, and temporal consistency in DM-based VSR need not be traded off against each other, achieving simultaneous SOTA on all three axes—a balance that prior DM-based methods (which typically sacrifice fidelity for perceptual gains) have struggled to achieve. The two observations are validated with extensive quantitative analysis (80,000+ feature groups, multiple high-frequency metrics), giving practitioners concrete design guidance for future DM-based video restoration pipelines. Open questions include scalability to longer video sequences, generalization to other restoration tasks (deblurring, denoising), and whether the optimal rescaling factor transfers across architectures and degradation types. The reliance on a pre-trained VAE decoder and frozen denoising U-Net also raises questions about how much of the gain comes from the generative prior versus the proposed alignment/guidance modules.
