## Problem

Existing multimodal face generation methods either retrofit frozen text-to-image backbones with auxiliary control adapters (e.g., ControlNet) or compose separate uni-modal generators at inference time, both of which prevent deep bidirectional fusion between spatial and semantic conditioning. These ad hoc designs suffer from modal dominance, rigid architectural constraints (e.g., matched latent dimensions), and failure under conflicting inputs, while the scarcity of semantically rich face annotations further limits progress.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt (up to 77 tokens from VLM-generated captions), a spatial condition (either a SegFormer segmentation mask or a U2Net sketch), and a discrete modality flag indicating which spatial type is active. All inputs are encoded into a shared VAE latent space (FLUX VAE, 16 channels), where the noisy image latent and condition latent are concatenated channel-wise into a 32-channel tensor. The output is a photorealistic face image at 512×512 resolution, decoded from the denoised latent via the VAE decoder.

## Method

MMFace-DiT is a 1.345B-parameter diffusion transformer (28 blocks, hidden dim 1152, 16 heads) whose core innovation is a dual-stream block that processes image and text tokens in parallel and fuses them at every layer through a single shared multi-head attention with hybrid RoPE: image tokens receive 2D axial positional encoding while text tokens receive 1D sequential encoding, enabling dense bidirectional cross-modal interaction. A lightweight Modality Embedder maps a discrete flag (mask vs. sketch) to a dense vector injected into the global conditioning vector (alongside timestep and CLIP pooled caption embeddings), allowing one set of weights to handle different spatial conditions without retraining. Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) derived from this global vector provides layer-specific modulation, while gated residual connections dynamically balance information flow to prevent a strong spatial prior from overwhelming subtle textual cues. Training uses either DDPM with Min-SNR weighting (λ=5.0) or Rectified Flow Matching, with progressive resolution (256² for 300 epochs, then 512² for 50 epochs) on just two RTX 5000 Ada GPUs using bfloat16, 8-bit AdamW, and gradient checkpointing.

## Results

On text + mask-conditioned face generation (Table 1), MMFace-DiT (flow-matching variant, Ours(F)) achieves an FID of 16.63, a 40.5% relative reduction over the strongest baseline UAC (48.88), along with the best LPIPS (0.34), SSIM (0.53), mIoU (50.12), CLIP Score (31.34), and LLM Score (0.6372). The DDPM variant (Ours(D)) also leads all baselines with FID 27.95, ACC 93.95%, and CLIP 31.69. On text + sketch conditioning (Table 2), the flow-matching variant further reduces FID to 9.14 (a 66.9% improvement over its own DDPM counterpart) and achieves LPIPS 0.20 and LLM Score 0.72, substantially outperforming the best sketch baseline MM2Latent (FID 40.91, LPIPS 0.58).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMFace-DiT outperforms all six baselines on every reported metric in both mask- and sketch-conditioned settings. Against the GAN-based TediGAN [32], the largest gap is in FID (16.63 vs. 62.55 for Ours(F)), while TediGAN retains the highest baseline LLM Score (0.4061) but still trails Ours(F) by 57%. ControlNet [38] achieves the best baseline mIoU (43.95) but suffers from the worst LPIPS (0.57) and a 24.8% lower CLIP Score than Ours(D). UAC [21] has the best baseline FID (48.88) yet the weakest CLIP alignment (23.75), highlighting its poor text adherence. CD [10] and DDGI [12] show competitive ACC (85.69 and 86.00) but the lowest mIoU values (38.85 and 36.02), indicating structural incoherence. MM2Latent [20] leads baselines in CLIP (26.78) and CLIP Distance (0.73) but still lags Ours(D) by ~18% in CLIP Score and 24.4% in LPIPS. Overall, the dual-stream architecture's advantage is most pronounced in semantic alignment metrics (CLIP, LLM Score) where the gap over the best baseline reaches 18–57%, confirming that prior methods sacrifice text fidelity to preserve spatial structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of retrofitting spatial control onto frozen text-to-image backbones (ControlNet) or composing separate uni-modal generators at inference time (UaC, Collaborative Diffusion) by proposing a natively unified dual-stream DiT that processes spatial and semantic tokens as co-equals through shared RoPE attention at every block. It builds directly on the DiT backbone [23], latent diffusion frameworks (Stable Diffusion, FLUX), and Rectified Flow Matching, while introducing a Modality Embedder that eliminates the need for separate models per spatial condition—a constraint inherent in prior adapter and compositional approaches. The VLM-based annotation pipeline (InternVL3 + Qwen3) addresses a data bottleneck that has limited multimodal face generation research, extending FFHQ and CelebA-HQ with semantically rich captions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified dual-stream design offers a principled alternative to the brittle, parameter-duplicating compositional pipelines that dominate controllable generation, and the released 1M-caption dataset lowers the barrier for future multimodal face research. However, the evaluation is confined to face generation at 512×512 with only two spatial modalities (mask, sketch), leaving open whether the dual-stream + shared RoPE fusion generalizes to broader image domains or additional conditioning types (e.g., depth, pose). The dramatic gap between the flow-matching and DDPM variants (FID 9.14 vs. 27.67 for sketches) suggests training objective choice may matter more than architectural novelty in this regime, a finding worth investigating more broadly.
