## Problem

Existing jailbreak research on Multimodal Large Reasoning Models (MLRMs) targets either the visual interface or forcibly hijacks the reasoning chain, but largely overlooks cognitive-level vulnerabilities embedded in the reasoning process itself. The anchoring effect—a well-established bias where initial information disproportionately shapes subsequent judgments—has been validated in LLMs for general tasks like negotiation but remains unexplored for safety judgments in MLRMs. This paper asks whether MLRMs exhibit this bias in safety decisions and how it can be systematically exploited to induce rationalization of harmful intent.

## Input / Output

The attack input is a paired sample: a programmatically generated mind map image (constrained to at most 3 hierarchical levels and 10 nodes, thematically related to the harmful intent but containing only safe content) and a text prompt assembled from a template with three placeholders—[role] (a professional persona), [topic] (the mind map subject), and [phrased harmful intent] (the original command rephrased as a nominalized phrase for grammatical fluency). The model's output is a harmful text response in which the model first rationalizes the harmful intent as a logical extension of the safe reasoning chain before executing it; over 95% of successful attacks exhibit this rationalization pattern.

## Method

RA-Attack exploits the anchoring effect by presenting a structured cross-modal safe anchor before the harmful intent, establishing a safely-biased cognitive base that the model's sequential reasoning then follows. The visual anchor is a mind map whose hierarchical structure mirrors the model's progressive reasoning process, providing a pre-defined reasoning path that the model naturally adopts when tasked with interpreting it. The text anchor constructs an educational scenario with a professional role, and the harmful intent is rephrased as a nominalized phrase so it appears as a coherent extension of the established safe reasoning rather than an abrupt malicious command. An automated pipeline (driven by Gemini-2.5-Pro) clusters harmful instructions into K categories, generates matching roles and mind map topics per category, and assembles samples at scale. The key differentiator from prior reasoning-chain attacks is that the safe anchor does not obfuscate or hijack the reasoning path but instead biases the model's initial cognitive frame, causing it to voluntarily rationalize the harmful content as a legitimate continuation.

## Results

RA-Attack achieves state-of-the-art Attack Success Rate (ASR) across all seven evaluated MLRMs on both AdvBench and Hades. On AdvBench, RA-Attack reaches 92% ASR on Gemini-2.5-Pro, 82% on GPT-4o, and 96% on LLaVA-CoT, with an average of 84.86% across models—surpassing the strongest baseline VisCRA (69.14% avg) by 15.7 points. On the more complex Hades dataset, RA-Attack maintains a 76.29% average ASR versus VisCRA's 62.91%. Notably, on the most safety-robust model (o4-mini), where all baselines score ≤12%, RA-Attack still achieves 44% (AdvBench) and 39.07% (Hades), more than tripling the best baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RA-Attack outperforms all six baselines on every model and both datasets. Against the strongest baseline VisCRA [28] (a reasoning-chain hijacking attack), RA-Attack gains +22 points on GPT-4o (82% vs 60%), +12 on Gemini-2.5-Pro (92% vs 80%), and +32 on o4-mini (44% vs 12%) on AdvBench. FC-Attack (Ensemble) [44] is the closest competitor on open-source models (92% on MM-E-InternVL, 90% on MM-E-Qwen), but RA-Attack still leads by 2–4 points and dominates on closed-source models (e.g., 82% vs 30% on GPT-4o). CS-DJ [38] and SI-Attack [45] achieve moderate ASR (46–86% and 2–60% respectively) but are consistently outperformed by 10–50+ points. The weakest baselines—Hades [15] and VA [26]—score below 34% on most models, highlighting that purely visual or adversarial-perturbation approaches are far less effective than RA-Attack's cognitive-bias-based reasoning manipulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms in multimodal jailbreaking—obfuscating harmful intent (ciphers, narratives, visual adversarial examples) and forcibly hijacking the reasoning chain (H-CoT, VisCRA, EmoAgent)—by instead exploiting an inherent cognitive bias (the anchoring effect) to make the model voluntarily rationalize harmful content. The anchoring effect has been validated in LLMs for general tasks like negotiation, but its role in multimodal reasoning models' safety judgments was unexplored. The structured mind map as a cross-modal visual anchor is a novel mechanism that aligns with MLRMs' sequential reasoning structure, distinguishing it from prior visual jailbreaks that use adversarial perturbations or semantic inconsistencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a cognitive-bias-based attack is most effective against the most safety-aligned model (o4-mini, where baselines cap at 12% but RA-Attack reaches 44%) suggests that stronger alignment may paradoxically increase susceptibility to reasoning-level manipulation, a counterintuitive result with broad implications for how safety is engineered. The Anchor Debiasing Prompt's ability to reduce ASR from 82% to 8% on GPT-4o while preserving utility points toward cognitive debiasing as a lightweight, generalizable defense direction. Open questions include whether other cognitive biases (framing, availability, confirmation bias) are similarly exploitable in MLRMs, whether the effect persists as models scale or undergo further alignment, and whether ADP-style defenses generalize to multi-turn or agentic settings.
