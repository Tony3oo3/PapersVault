## Problem

Onboard sensors in autonomous driving are inherently limited by perception horizon, line-of-sight constraints, and vulnerability to adverse conditions such as darkness, rain, and occlusion, causing significant performance degradation in mapping, occupancy prediction, and planning. Unlike human drivers who can recall previously seen road structure, current AD systems lack any mechanism to leverage offline geographic context, leaving them without a stable background prior when live sensing is unreliable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view camera images, ego-vehicle GPS pose, and retrieved geographic street-view images (fetched from Google Maps APIs and stored as equirectangular panoramas, then reprojected via virtual cameras matched to onboard intrinsics/extrinsics). A Reliability Estimation Gate computes a scalar weight from ZNCC feature similarity and GPS distance to down-weight missing or misaligned retrievals. Outputs are task-specific: 3D bounding boxes, vectorized road lines, 3D occupancy grids, ego trajectories, or generated future video frames, all produced by the original downstream heads without architectural modification.

## Method

The core contribution is a plug-and-play Spatial Retrieval Adapter that injects geographic context into existing BEV-based AD pipelines via cross-attention: BEV features serve as query, while geographic image features (encoded by the same backbone) plus PETR 3D positional encodings serve as key and value, with the residual update modulated by a learned reliability score. For generative world models, an analogous geographic cross-attention layer is inserted into DiT blocks, using noised latents as query and geographic features from start/end frame positions as key/value to maintain spatial consistency across long-horizon rollouts. The Reliability Estimation Gate outputs a sigmoid-weighted scalar from an MLP over ZNCC similarity and GPS distance, trained with binary labels (1800 manually identified misaligned cases) to gracefully handle missing or outdated geographic data. The authors also construct nuScenes-Geography, extending nuScenes with coordinate-aligned street-view panoramas stored in equirectangular format to reduce storage by over 70% while enabling per-frame virtual camera reprojection.

## Results

The spatial retrieval paradigm delivers its largest gains on online mapping, where MapTRv2+Geo improves mAP from 68.7 to 78.2 (+9.5) at 110 epochs and MapTR+Geo from 59.3 to 72.7 (+13.4). For generative world modeling, UVG+Geo reduces FVD from 36.10 to 29.97 (−6.13) and FID from 5.82 to 5.60. Occupancy prediction shows consistent but smaller mIoU gains (FBOcc: +0.63, FlashOCC:M1: +0.43), while object detection (BEVDet, BEVFormer) and end-to-end planning (VAD) see only marginal changes, with VAD+Geo reducing night-scene collision rate from 0.55% to 0.48%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's geographic prior adapter is evaluated as a plug-and-play addition to nine existing baselines across five tasks. The most dramatic wins are on online mapping: MapTRv2+Geo surpasses MapTRv2 by +9.5 to +11.9 mAP, and MapTR+Geo by +10.9 to +13.4 mAP, with the largest per-class gain on lane dividers (APdiv +14.1 to +15.8). For generative world modeling, UVG+Geo reduces FVD by 6.13 points (36.10→29.97) and MDD+Geo by 2.91 points (84.43→81.52), confirming improved spatial consistency. Occupancy prediction yields modest but consistent mIoU gains (FBOcc +0.63, FlashOCC:M1 +0.43), concentrated in static terrain categories. Object detection (BEVDet, BEVFormer) shows essentially no change (±0.1 NDS), as expected since geographic data primarily encodes background. For end-to-end planning, VAD+Geo matches VAD on L2 trajectory error (0.64 m avg) while reducing the night-scene collision rate from 0.55% to 0.48%, indicating a safety benefit without sacrificing accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions spatial retrieval as a general-purpose, task-agnostic input modality for autonomous driving, deliberately distinguishing it from HD maps (which provide centimeter-level geometric precision but are expensive and limited to pre-defined semantic types) and from prior task-specific retrieval uses in AD (visual place recognition, localization, trajectory sampling). Methodologically, it builds on PETR for 3D positional encoding of geographic image patches and on the DiT architecture for the generative world model branch, but the core contribution is the paradigm itself: a plug-and-play cross-attention adapter that injects offline geographic context into existing BEV-based pipelines without modifying downstream heads. The equirectangular panorama storage scheme and the Reliability Estimation Gate (using ZNCC + GPS distance) are practical engineering contributions that make the paradigm robust to real-world retrieval failures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The nuScenes-Geography dataset and the model-agnostic adapter lower the barrier for the community to explore offline geographic priors, and the paradigm addresses a fundamental limitation of onboard sensing (limited range, line-of-sight vulnerability) in a cost-effective, sensor-free way. Key open questions include whether gains transfer beyond nuScenes (Singapore/Boston) to regions with sparser street-view coverage, whether more sophisticated retrieval (multi-neighborhood, temporal) yields further improvements, and how to handle map staleness as road layouts change. The negligible detection gains and modest planning improvements suggest the paradigm's value is concentrated in structure-heavy tasks (mapping, occupancy, world modeling) rather than foreground-centric ones, which may limit its appeal for perception-focused groups.
