## Problem

Layered image generation and editing—enabling layer-wise reuse, editing, and composition of visual content analogous to word-level editing in NLP—remains significantly underdeveloped compared to flat text-to-image generation. This gap stems from the absence of large-scale, high-quality multi-layer datasets and limited exploitation of prior knowledge from state-of-the-art open-source text-to-image models, hindering systematic exploration of critical research directions in layered image synthesis at scale.

## Input / Output

The method accepts text prompts (multilingual, bilingual), raster images with spatial layout annotations (bounding boxes with Z-order), or existing layer sets combined with text prompts or user-provided reference images. It produces a full multi-layer RGBA transparent design comprising a fully transparent canvas layer, a semi-transparent background layer, and K foreground layers, all defined on a full-size canvas that extends beyond the visible background region to preserve overflow elements. The model operates at 512×512 and 1024×1024 resolutions across diverse aspect ratios, with the canvas layer accommodating elements that extend past background boundaries for complete editability.

## Method

MRT builds a 20B-parameter masked region diffusion transformer on Qwen-Image, performing full-parameter fine-tuning (not LoRA) with FSDP2 on 10M+ multilingual design samples. The core mechanism is an adaptive masking scheme: for each of three unified tasks (text-to-layers, image-to-layers, layers-to-layers), specific token subsets are designated as clean masked conditions while noise is applied only to target tokens, with full attention between masked and noise tokens enabling the model to learn cross-layer relationships. A key architectural innovation is the overflow-aware canvas layer, which treats the background as a special transparent foreground layer and encapsulates elements extending beyond visible boundaries—critical since over 60% of training samples contain such overflow layers. The model uses WAN-2.1-VAE to extract regional cropped representations for each layer and the composed image, then applies flow matching with full joint attention across all regional tokens. DMD2 distillation compresses the 50-step teacher into an 8-step student for near-realtime multi-layer generation with minimal quality degradation.

## Results

On the image-to-layers task, MRT achieves PSNR_merged of 27.34 / 25.91 / 25.72 and SSIM_merged of 0.903 / 0.876 / 0.849 for 4–8, 8–16, and 16–32 layers respectively, consistently outperforming Qwen-Image-Layered (25.81 / 23.06 / 22.18 PSNR; 0.871 / 0.832 / 0.807 SSIM). In user studies, MRT is preferred over ART on text-to-layers (instruction following, aesthetics, layer quality) and over LayerD, Qwen-Image-Layered, RoboNeo, and Lovart on image-to-layers (quality, integrity, granularity). The distilled 8-step model achieves a 6× speedup over the 50-step baseline with only a modest FID increase (16.02 → 18.58), and MRT runs 10–100× faster with 50–90% less GPU memory than Qwen-Image-Layered on high-layer-count images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ART (CVPR 2025), MRT wins the text-to-layers user study on all three dimensions—instruction following, overall aesthetics, and layer quality—and is the only method that natively generates overflow layers extending beyond the background boundary, whereas ART truncates pixels to the visible canvas. Against Qwen-Image-Layered, MRT achieves 1.5–3.5 dB higher PSNR_merged and 0.03–0.04 higher SSIM_merged across all layer-count bins (Table 1), is preferred in blind user studies on quality, integrity, and granularity, and runs 10–100× faster with 50–90% less activation GPU memory on a single H100 for 1K images with ~20 layers. Against LayerD (ICCV 2025), MRT is preferred in the image-to-layers user study; LayerD and Qwen-Image-Layered both produce overly grouped layers, while MRT yields sharper, more semantically correct RGBA boundaries. For the layers-to-layers editing task (no prior work exists), MRT outperforms a GPT-Image-1 iterative baseline on both layer addition (better cross-layer consistency in a single pass) and layer restylization (better geometry preservation and style harmonization without sequential error propagation).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ART (Anonymous Region Transformer) [38], which first introduced regional diffusion for multi-layer transparent image generation, and on Qwen-Image [55] as the 20B-parameter base architecture. It departs from prior work in three key ways: (1) unifying text-to-layers, image-to-layers, and layers-to-layers editing within a single masked-region framework via adaptive token masking, (2) introducing an overflow-aware canvas layer that previous systems (ART, Qwen-Image-Layered) lack, and (3) scaling training to 10M+ samples with full-parameter fine-tuning rather than the LoRA-only fine-tuning used by earlier methods due to resource constraints. The distribution matching distillation component follows the DMD2 line of work [60, 61] applied here for the first time to multi-layer generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of multi-layer transparent image generation at the scale of modern text-to-image models (20B parameters, 10M+ training samples), establishing that both model capacity and dataset scale are essential for high-quality layered synthesis. The overflow layer support addresses a practical gap that affects over 60% of real designs, making layers truly reusable in editing workflows rather than truncated at canvas boundaries. Open questions include whether the layers-to-layers editing task can be improved (it currently slightly degrades overall performance due to dataset quality issues), whether the approach generalizes beyond graphic design to photographic or 3D content, and whether the in-house 10M dataset can be partially released to enable broader community research.
