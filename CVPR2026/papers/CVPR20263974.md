## Problem

Existing EDA and graph-ML benchmarks entangle graph representation with model architecture and task labels, so the independent effect of circuit graph view on GNN performance has never been isolated. Prior datasets fix a single logical or spatial representation per stage, making it impossible to determine whether a model's accuracy reflects its architecture or its choice of graph encoding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263974/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes DEF files produced by the OpenROAD RTL-to-GDSII flow (post-placement and post-routing) together with technology library files, extracting gate coordinates, net connectivity, pin types, and routing geometry in DBU units. It produces five typed, attributed circuit graph views (all-elements-as-nodes, pins-as-edges, nets-as-edges, net-gate incidence, net edges without pin nodes) over 30 IP cores spanning up to 10⁶ nodes/edges, with the same feature set attached at different locations to ensure information parity. Supervision is resolution-matched: HPWL labels attach to net nodes (views b/c) or net edges (views d–f) for placement, while exact routed wire_length and via_count serve as routing labels at the corresponding entity.

## Method

R2G provides an end-to-end DEF-to-graph conversion pipeline that standardizes five complementary views with strict information parity—identical attribute sets differing only in where features attach (node vs. edge vs. aggregated)—enabling controlled cross-view ablations. Three homogeneous GNNs (GINE, GAT, ResGatedGCN) serve as reproducible baselines under unified train/validation/test splits, with systematic ablations over GNN message-passing depth (3–6 layers) and decoder-head depth (1–4 layers). The key empirical finding is that view choice dominates model choice (Test R² varies by >0.3 across views for a fixed GNN, and model rankings flip), node-centric views generalize best across both placement and routing, and decoder-head depth (3–4 layers) is the primary accuracy driver, lifting R² from −0.17 to >0.99 for placement and resolving NaN divergence for routing.

## Results

On the placement task (Table 3), node-centric view (b) yields the best Test R² across all three GNNs: GINE 0.8878, ResGatedGCN 0.8803, GAT 0.8049, while incidence-edge view (e) collapses to negative R² (−0.0107 for GINE, −0.0610 for ResGatedGCN). On routing (Table 4), view (b) again leads with ResGatedGCN R²=0.8763 and GINE R²=0.8596, whereas view (f) drops to R²≈0.00–0.32. Increasing decoder-head depth from 1 to 4 layers lifts placement R² from 0.85 (GINE) / −0.17 (ResGatedGCN) to 0.9962 / 0.9891, and routing R² from NaN to 0.9965 / 0.9841 (Tables 7–8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks three homogeneous GNNs—GINE [34], GAT [33], and ResGatedGCN [4]—across five circuit-graph views for placement and routing. No single GNN dominates: GINE leads on view (b) for placement (R²=0.8878 vs. 0.8803 ResGatedGCN), ResGatedGCN leads on view (c) (0.8519 vs. 0.8003 GINE) and on view (b) for routing (0.8763 vs. 0.8596 GINE), while GAT is best only on the incidence-edge view (e) for both tasks (placement R²=0.0472, routing R²=0.8389) but lags by 0.2–0.7 R² on all other views. The critical finding is that view choice overwhelms model choice: for a fixed GNN, Test R² swings by more than 0.3 across views (e.g., GINE placement ranges from 0.8878 on view (b) to −0.0107 on view (e)), and model rankings flip depending on the representation. ResGatedGCN's residual gating provides the most stable performance across node-centric views, whereas GAT's attention mechanism is highly view-sensitive and unreliable outside view (e).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of graph ML benchmarking (OGB, TU-Dataset) and EDA datasets (CircuitNet, OpenABC-D, ForgeEDA, EDA-schema) by identifying that prior EDA resources co-determine graph representation and task labels, making representation effects indiscernible. Rather than building on a specific prior method, it inherits graph-ML best practices (unified splits, scalable loaders, reproducible baselines) while introducing the key conceptual departure of information parity: five typed views of the same circuit that encode identical attributes but differ only in where features attach. This decouples representation choice from model choice, a confound that no prior EDA or graph-ML benchmark has controlled.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that view choice dominates model choice (Test R² varying by >0.3 for a fixed GNN) and that decoder-head depth (3–4 layers) is the primary accuracy driver—turning divergent training into R² > 0.99—provides actionable guidance that prior benchmarks, which emphasize message-passing stacks, have not surfaced. Limitations include coverage of only late physical-design stages, limited technology nodes, and evaluation restricted to classic homogeneous GNNs; future work on timing/congestion-aware tasks, graph transformers, and broader design coverage would strengthen the benchmark's scope. The near-perfect R² achieved with sufficient head capacity also raises the question of whether the current task difficulty is adequate to discriminate among more advanced architectures.
