## Problem

Existing RL-based fine-tuning for vision-language models relies on labeled data and curated training splits, misaligned with real-world scenarios where train-test boundaries do not naturally exist. This paper asks whether reinforcement learning can be performed directly at test time from unlabeled data, extracting reward signals from the model's own outputs rather than from external supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262560/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unlabeled image paired with a text prompt (e.g., a classification question or VQA query) and produces a class label or free-form text answer. Crucially, for each test sample the model generates N candidate responses from its current policy, and the reward is computed from the empirical distribution over these N outputs rather than from any ground-truth label. Only 20 randomly sampled test instances per dataset are needed for adaptation, and the method operates on decoder-based VLMs that produce autoregressive token sequences rather than fixed class-probability vectors.

## Method

TTRV bootstraps an off-the-shelf VLM with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using two fully unsupervised rewards derived from the model's own sampled outputs at inference time. The first is a frequency-based reward that assigns each response a score proportional to its empirical probability among N rollouts, providing soft probabilistic supervision rather than the hard majority-voting signal used by prior test-time RL work. The second is a diversity-control reward defined as the negative Shannon entropy of the empirical output distribution, which penalizes excessive dispersion and encourages the model to consolidate probability mass toward stable answers. The combined reward R(ŷ_j) = r₁(ŷ_j) + α·r₂ feeds into GRPO's relative advantage computation, updating the policy via clipped importance-weighted objectives with KL regularization against the reference model. This entire loop runs at test time on unlabeled data, requiring no labeled splits, and the paper shows it recovers latent recognition and reasoning capabilities attenuated during instruction tuning rather than merely adapting to the test distribution.

## Results

On image classification (8 benchmarks), TTRV applied to InternVL3-8B achieves a mean top-1 accuracy of 95.71%, surpassing GPT-4o (93.37%) by 2.34% on average and the best dual-encoder model SigLIP (70.97%) by 24.74%. The largest single-dataset gain is +52.49% on DTD (InternVL3-2B: 37.24% → 89.73%). On VQA (8 benchmarks), TTRV on InternVL3-8B improves mean accuracy from 38.05% to 55.56% (+17.50%), with the largest gain of +29.75% on MME, while TTRV on InternVL2.5-4B reaches 69.40% mean, remaining within ~2.6% of GPT-4o (71.97%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TTRV on InternVL3-8B (95.71% mean) outperforms GPT-4o (93.37%) by 2.34% on average across 8 image classification benchmarks, and also beats the strongest open-source decoder-based VLMs: LLaVA-1.5-7b (92.92%) and Phi-3.5-vision (92.58%). Against dual-encoder VLMs, TTRV dominates by a wide margin — the best dual-encoder SigLIP achieves only 70.97% mean, a 24.74% gap. On VQA, TTRV on InternVL2.5-4B (69.40% mean) narrows the gap to GPT-4o (71.97%) to within 2.6%, while TTRV on InternVL3-8B (55.56%) still trails GPT-4o by 16.4% but surpasses all other open-source baselines (LLaVA-1.5-7b: 46.02%, Phi-3.5-vision: 48.58%). Compared to the TTRL majority-voting reward [74], TTRV's combined frequency+diversity reward consistently yields higher accuracy (e.g., +9.54% on AI2D vs. −4.03% for majority voting on InternVL2.5-4B). TTRV also generalizes beyond the InternVL family, showing consistent gains on Qwen2.5-VL-3B (+2.63% to +4.13% across 4 datasets).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the test-time reinforcement learning paradigm introduced by TTRL (Zuo et al., 2025) from LLMs to vision-language models, while replacing the naive majority-voting reward with a two-component signal: a frequency-based reward that softly credits predictions proportional to their empirical occurrence, and an entropy-based diversity control that regularizes the output distribution. It builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as the optimization backbone but departs from all prior RL-based VLM fine-tuning work (VLM-R1, Perception-R1, CLS-RL, R1-VL) by eliminating the need for labeled data or curated training splits entirely. Unlike test-time training methods (TENT, TPT, Norm) that impose architectural constraints or operate on class-level distributions, TTRV is designed specifically for decoder-based VLMs that produce autoregressive token sequences, making it applicable to the current generation of open-source multimodal models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that test-time RL with as few as 20 unlabeled samples (or even a single sample) can recover substantial pre-training capabilities—evidenced by cross-dataset generalization and the failure of random rewards to produce similar gains—suggests that instruction tuning may attenuate visual recognition skills that remain latent in the weights. This reframes test-time adaptation not as distribution matching but as capability reactivation, with implications for how we think about the relationship between pre-training, instruction tuning, and downstream deployment. Key open questions include: why does this work mechanistically (no theoretical account is provided), whether the gains persist under more adversarial or truly novel distributions, and whether the computational cost of multiple rollouts per sample limits practical deployment. The method's applicability to any open-source VLM makes it a potentially widely adopted technique.
