## Problem

Current video diffusion models produce photorealistic clips but fail to maintain a single stable 3D scene over time, exhibiting view-time drift, geometric flicker, and entangled camera/object motion. Prior 4D generation approaches either optimize explicit scene representations (computationally expensive, single-object) or post-hoc lift 2D videos into 3D (inheriting the geometric inconsistency of the 2D prior). No existing method natively integrates a true 4D structure into a generative video prior that generalizes to in-the-wild dynamics.

## Input / Output

At inference, WorldReel takes a text prompt and a single 480×720 RGB image and produces a 49-frame video at the same resolution, requiring no additional geometric or motion inputs. Simultaneously, it outputs a unified 4D scene representation at a downsampled 13-frame cadence: per-frame depth maps, 3D point clouds, calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics (all in first-frame canonical coordinates), forward scene flow, and binary dynamic foreground masks. The geo-motion latent (depth + optical flow) is an internal training-time augmentation, not an inference-time input.

## Method

WorldReel augments the CogVideoX-5B DiT latent space by channel-concatenating a geo-motion latent (normalized depth and optical flow encoded by the same 3D VAE), with only input/output projection layers modified and new parameters zero-initialized for stable transfer. A shared lightweight temporal DPT decoder with multi-task heads predicts pointmaps, camera poses, scene flow, and dynamic masks from the augmented latent, enforcing geometric consistency through correlated feature learning. Training proceeds in two stages: first, the DiT is finetuned on the augmented latent and DPT heads are pretrained separately; then the full model is trained end-to-end with targeted regularizers that decouple static structure (inter-frame depth consistency via camera-projection) from dynamic regions (spatial smoothness of scene flow on foreground masks). The appearance-agnostic geo-motion latent reduces the synthetic-to-real domain gap, enabling effective use of precise 4D supervision from synthetic datasets (PointOdyssey, BEDLAM, Dynamic Replica, Omniworld-Game) alongside pseudo-annotated real videos from Panda-70M.

## Results

On the SpatialVid benchmark, WorldReel achieves a dynamic degree of 0.73 (general) and 1.00 (complex motion), substantially exceeding all baselines (next best: GeoVideo at 0.54/0.79). It also attains the best FVD (336.1 general, 394.2 complex) and FID (36.58 general, 44.95 complex), indicating superior perceptual quality alongside stronger motion. For 4D geometry, WorldReel reduces depth log-RMSE from 0.353 (GeoVideo) to 0.287 and achieves the lowest camera pose errors (ATE 0.005, RTE 0.007, RRE 0.317) across all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WorldReel outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric in both the general and complex motion splits. Against GeoVideo [3] (the closest competitor, trained on the same data), WorldReel improves dynamic degree from 0.54→0.73 (general) and 0.79→1.00 (complex), reduces FVD by 9.5% (371.3→336.1) and FID by 21.8% (46.78→36.58) on general motion. Against 4DNeX [10], WorldReel dramatically surpasses it in dynamic degree (0.73 vs. 0.03 general; 1.00 vs. 0.19 complex) and FVD (336.1 vs. 712.5), revealing that 4DNeX collapses toward near-static generation despite high frame-wise consistency scores. Against DimensionX [52], WorldReel wins on dynamic degree (0.73 vs. 0.47 general; 1.00 vs. 0.93 complex), FVD (336.1 vs. 470.7), and FID (36.58 vs. 42.28). Against the base CogVideoX-I2V [72], WorldReel improves dynamic degree (0.73 vs. 0.37) and FVD (336.1 vs. 617.4) while maintaining comparable motion smoothness, confirming that the geo-motion latent and joint 4D supervision add meaningful geometric and motion structure without sacrificing temporal coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

WorldReel builds on the CogVideoX DiT-based video diffusion backbone and the DPT decoder architecture, extending both with a geometry-motion augmented latent (depth + optical flow encoded through the existing 3D VAE) and a multi-task temporal DPT head that jointly predicts pointmaps, camera poses, scene flow, and dynamic masks. It departs from prior geometry-aware video generators (GeoVideo, OmniVDiff) that model only static depth, and from motion-aware generators (VideoJam, Track4Gen) that use 2D flow, by unifying explicit 3D geometry and 3D scene flow in a single augmented latent. Unlike optimization-based 4D methods (4D-Fy, DreamGaussian4D) or post-hoc reconstruction pipelines (DimensionX, GenXD), WorldReel natively integrates 4D structure into the generative prior via a feed-forward architecture, and unlike 4DNeX which produces near-static output, it explicitly regularizes to decouple camera motion from object dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WorldReel demonstrates that a single feed-forward model can produce photorealistic video alongside calibrated cameras, dense point clouds, and 3D scene flow with strong spatiotemporal consistency, moving the field closer to editable, agent-ready 4D world models. Key limitations include the reliance on 4D supervision (synthetic data or pseudo-labels from foundation models) during training, residual domain gaps for unusual dynamics, and failure under significant topology changes or heavy occlusions within the finite temporal window. Open questions include whether weak or self-supervised 4D signals can replace explicit supervision, whether streaming/causal diffusion can extend the temporal context, and whether controllable scene decomposition can enable long-horizon interactive generation.
