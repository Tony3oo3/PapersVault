## Problem

Independent object-centric 3D reconstruction discards strong repetition signals present in real scenes, where the same object model appears multiple times spatially or across temporal rescans. Existing approaches that exploit repetition rely on explicit matching and rigid alignment between observations, making them brittle to association errors and unable to handle non-rigid sub-object changes such as articulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263090/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For each object in a group, JRM consumes a segmented partial point cloud, sparse RGB views, and VLM-derived text descriptions, with camera poses and instance segmentations assumed available from upstream pipelines. The critical distinction is that these observations remain unaligned across objects—no explicit registration or pose matching is performed. The model outputs a set of jointly reconstructed 3D object meshes (decoded from latent tokens via signed-distance prediction), and although trained only on pairs, it generalizes to reconstructing up to 9 objects simultaneously at inference.

## Method

JRM extends the ShapeR flow-matching DiT by introducing a coupled fusion block that replaces every other single-stream transformer block. In these blocks, latent tokens from all K objects in a group are concatenated and processed through a shared attention layer, allowing shape latents to attend to each other while each object's observation-condition tokens remain separate. Training uses a pair-wise strategy on 80k objects, sampling 10% negative (dissimilar) pairs to teach the model to adaptively aggregate only relevant information. The flow-matching objective is applied per-object with an affine OT path, and the coupled attention operates solely on shape latents—following the intuition that the shared subject should be consistent while each instance respects its own observations. This implicit latent-space aggregation eliminates the need for explicit alignment and naturally extends to articulated objects in different states.

## Results

On real-world ScanNet++ scenes, JRM achieves a Chamfer Distance of 2.69 cm, Normal Consistency of 79.41, and F-Score of 85.53, substantially outperforming FM (CD 4.20, F1 72.96) and DPRecon (CD 5.53, F1 65.98). On Replica, JRM reaches CD 3.21 and F1 81.78, again leading both baselines. In the synthetic temporal repetition setting with 3 rescans and full multimodal input, JRM achieves F1 94.98 versus FM's 81.17, demonstrating that implicit aggregation scales with additional observations while explicit alignment degrades.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JRM outperforms all three baselines across the evaluated settings. Against FM (ShapeR), JRM consistently wins on Chamfer Distance and F-Score in multi-observation scenarios: with 3 rescans on synthetic data, JRM reaches F1 94.98 vs. FM's 81.17, and on ScanNet++ the gap widens to F1 85.53 vs. 72.96. FM's explicit rigid alignment makes it sensitive to matching and alignment errors, causing degradation as more (potentially misaligned) observations are added, whereas JRM's implicit latent-space coupling remains robust. Against DPRecon on real-world scenes, JRM improves F1 by 12.5 points on ScanNet++ (85.53 vs. 65.98) and 9.8 points on Replica (81.78 vs. 71.95), while also producing sharper geometry. Against MORE² (LivingScenes) in the temporal repetition setting, JRM achieves F1 93.56 with 3 rescans versus MORE²'s 34.17, a dramatic margin attributable to both the generative architecture and the implicit aggregation strategy. The one setting where JRM does not clearly win is target-only reconstruction (no support objects), where FM and JRM perform comparably, confirming that the benefit of joint reconstruction requires at least one additional observation.

## Relation to Prior Work

JRM extends ShapeR, a conditional flow-matching 3D generative model for individual object reconstruction, by introducing a coupled fusion block that enables inter-object attention in the diffusion latent space. Conceptually, it departs from the explicit alignment-and-registration pipeline of LivingScenes and Splat-and-Replace, which sequentially solve object association, point cloud registration, and reconstruction, by instead letting the generative model implicitly aggregate unaligned observations. The design draws on personalized image generation (JeDi) and multi-instance diffusion (MIDI-3D, SceneGen), but applies the coupling to reconstruction rather than generation, and uses a pair-wise training strategy that avoids the need for full-scene supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the explicit alignment step, JRM addresses a major practical bottleneck in multi-scan and multi-instance reconstruction, where errors in matching and registration compound and are particularly problematic for non-rigid changes like articulation. The pair-wise training strategy is a notable practical advantage, scaling with individual assets rather than scarce full-scene datasets while still generalizing to arbitrary group sizes at inference. Limitations include dependence on upstream pipelines for camera poses, depths, and instance segmentations, and evaluation on real-world scenes is limited to 13 scenes. Open questions include whether the implicit coupling generalizes to larger object groups, more complex scene-level interactions beyond repetition, and whether the approach can be extended to texture and material reconstruction.
