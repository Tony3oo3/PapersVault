## Problem

Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models face two compounding efficiency bottlenecks: the number of tokens grows quadratically with scale (yielding O(n⁴) attention cost), and the inherently sequential next-scale prediction prevents parallel decoding across scales. Existing acceleration methods like FastVAR rely on frequency-domain token pruning but do not exploit the model's own update dynamics and offer no mechanism for parallelizing the sequential scale steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262036/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LazyVAR is a training-free, plug-and-play acceleration layer applied to pre-trained VAR text-to-image models (Infinity-2B, HART-0.7B). It takes a text prompt (encoded by a pretrained language model into conditioning embeddings) and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image. The method modifies only the inference-time token selection and grouping schedule; no architectural changes or retraining are required, and it remains compatible with FlashAttention and the original KV-cache mechanism.

## Method

LazyVAR combines two complementary strategies. First, Scale-wise Update Index Guided Token Pruning (UIGTP) computes the token-wise cosine similarity between adjacent aggregated latent features (Update_Index = 1 − cos⟨f̂_{k−1}, f̂_k⟩) and uses the previous scale's Update Index to decide which tokens to retain at the current scale, since tokens with small updates at one scale tend to have small updates at the next. Second, Parallel Group Decoding (PGD) groups p successive large-scale steps and decodes them simultaneously by approximating each scale's interpolated input with the same downsampled aggregated latent feature from the group's first scale, which is valid because adjacent features are highly similar at large scales. Smaller scales are kept intact to preserve semantic structure, and within a group, tokens are assigned to scales by descending Update Index to avoid redundant updates at the same spatial position.

## Results

LazyVAR achieves a 2.94× speedup on the Infinity-2B model (1.38s → 0.47s) and a 1.67× speedup on HART-0.7B (0.80s → 0.48s) on a single RTX 4090, while reducing FLOPs from 101.58T to 34.32T (Infinity) and 45.59T to 32.09T (HART). Generation quality is largely preserved: Infinity+LazyVAR actually improves GenEval from 0.685 to 0.686 and keeps FID at 9.83 (vs. 9.80 baseline), while HART+LazyVAR shows a minor GenEval drop (0.476 → 0.468) and FID increase (10.98 → 11.04). The method also reduces peak GPU memory (16.1GB → 11.2GB for Infinity) and achieves a lower FID* (1.46 vs. 2.03 for Infinity; 3.77 vs. 8.06 for HART) than FastVAR, indicating better visual consistency with the unpruned model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary competing VAR acceleration method FastVAR, LazyVAR wins on every axis: it is faster (2.94× vs. 2.12× on Infinity; 1.67× vs. 1.38× on HART), uses fewer FLOPs (34.32T vs. 44.15T) and less memory (11.2GB vs. 11.9GB), and produces images closer to the unpruned model (FID* 1.46 vs. 2.03 on Infinity; 3.77 vs. 8.06 on HART). On GenEval, LazyVAR slightly outperforms FastVAR for Infinity (0.686 vs. 0.683) and HART (0.468 vs. 0.458). Compared to the unpruned Infinity baseline, LazyVAR matches or marginally improves GenEval (0.686 vs. 0.685) and CLIP (0.277 vs. 0.277) with a negligible FID increase (9.83 vs. 9.80); for HART the drop is small but measurable (GenEval 0.468 vs. 0.476, FID 11.04 vs. 10.98). Against diffusion baselines, LazyVAR is dramatically faster (0.47s vs. 3.36s for PixArt-Σ and 8.44s for SD3.5-M) but SD3.5-M still leads on GenEval (0.706 vs. 0.686) and FID (8.48 vs. 9.83). Versus token-level AR models (LlamaGen, Show-O), LazyVAR is 80× and 35× faster respectively while achieving substantially higher GenEval scores (0.686 vs. 0.325 and 0.528).

## Relation to Prior Work

LazyVAR builds directly on the VAR framework's next-scale prediction architecture and its residual aggregated latent feature formulation, but departs from prior VAR acceleration work (notably FastVAR's norm-based Pivotal Token Selection and SparseVAR's frequency-region heuristics) by using the model's own inter-scale update dynamics—specifically, the cosine similarity of adjacent aggregated latent features—as the pruning signal. The Parallel Group Decoding component draws a conceptual parallel to speculative decoding in LLMs but is tailored to VAR's hierarchical scale structure, where the high similarity at large scales justifies approximating multiple scale inputs from a single downsampled feature. The method is explicitly positioned as exploiting VAR-specific inductive biases rather than applying general-purpose acceleration techniques (quantization, distillation, KV-cache compression) that target diffusion or standard autoregressive models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.94× speedup with negligible (and in some metrics slightly positive) quality change makes high-resolution VAR-based text-to-image generation practically deployable on consumer hardware (1024×1024 in 0.5 s on a single RTX 4090), which is a meaningful step toward closing the latency gap with diffusion models. The training-free, plug-and-play nature means it can be immediately applied to existing VAR checkpoints without retraining. Limitations include evaluation on only two VAR variants (Infinity and HART), a more modest 1.67× speedup for HART, and the method's dependence on VAR's specific coarse-to-fine residual structure, leaving open whether the same update-similarity principle transfers to other hierarchical or non-hierarchical autoregressive architectures.
