## Problem

All-in-one image restoration (AiOIR) models are trained on fixed degradation distributions and suffer significant performance drops when test-time degradations deviate from the training set. Existing test-time adaptation methods for low-level vision are limited, and extending them to AiOIR faces two key challenges: generating reliable pseudo-supervision for pixel-level regression without ground truth, and preventing online parameter updates from destroying pretrained restoration capability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262707/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image as input and produces a restored clean image of the same resolution. Adaptation is performed as a one-time update per target test domain (e.g., ~32 seconds on Rain100H), after which inference runs in standard feed-forward mode with no additional overhead. The framework is backbone-agnostic and source-free: it requires no access to source-domain training data or ground-truth labels, and can be plugged into any pre-trained AiOIR model (AirNet, PromptIR, DFPIR).

## Method

DCTTA operates on a teacher-student architecture with three components. First, test-time redegradation generation (TRG) produces pseudo-labels via 8-way geometric augmentation ensemble, then trains a residual denoising diffusion model (RDDM) to model the degradation residual between the pseudo-label and the degraded input, synthesizing redegraded images that match the target distribution. Second, degradation-guided image restoration (DIR) enforces a self-supervised consistency loss (pixel-wise L1 + VGG perceptual) between the student's outputs on the original and redegraded images, plus an adaptation consistency loss aligning the student output with the teacher's pseudo-label; the teacher is updated via EMA of the student. Third, test-time important parameter selection (TIPS) computes a diagonal Fisher Information Matrix approximation from an augmentation consistency loss to identify the top 40% most important parameters, freezing them during adaptation to prevent catastrophic forgetting of pretrained knowledge.

## Results

On the three-task setting (denoising, dehazing, deraining), PromptIR+DCTTA achieves the best average of 18.61 dB PSNR and 0.654 SSIM, outperforming the strongest standalone baseline (AirNet* at 17.12 dB / 0.613 SSIM) by +1.49 dB. The largest gain appears in deraining on Rain100H, where PromptIR+DCTTA reaches 20.21 dB PSNR versus 15.64 dB for vanilla PromptIR (+4.57 dB). In the five-task setting, DFPIR+DCTTA attains the highest overall average of 23.61 dB PSNR and 0.802 SSIM, with a +3.86 dB improvement on Rain100H deraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCTTA is applied as a plug-in module on three AiOIR backbones (AirNet, PromptIR, DFPIR) and consistently improves each over its vanilla counterpart across all tasks. The most dramatic gains occur in deraining: PromptIR+DCTTA surpasses PromptIR by +4.57 dB PSNR on Rain100H, and DFPIR+DCTTA exceeds DFPIR by +4.33 dB on the same dataset. Against standalone baselines, PromptIR+DCTTA (18.61 dB avg) outperforms AdaIR (17.35 dB), VLU-Net (17.25 dB), and DACLIP (16.93 dB) by 1.26–1.68 dB in average PSNR. In the five-task setting, DFPIR+DCTTA (23.61 dB) also beats DiffUIR (18.94 dB) by a wide margin of +4.67 dB, though DiffUIR is a diffusion-based method with a fundamentally different inference paradigm. Gains in denoising and dehazing are more modest (+0.1–1.6 dB), reflecting the more spatially uniform nature of those degradations compared to structured rain streaks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends test-time adaptation (TTA), well-studied in classification and segmentation, to the all-in-one image restoration (AiOIR) setting, where a single model must handle multiple degradation types simultaneously. It repurposes the Residual Denoising Diffusion Model (RDDM) not as a restoration engine (as in TAO) but as a degradation generator that synthesizes pseudo degraded-clean pairs for self-supervised consistency training. The test-time important parameter selection (TIPS) module directly adapts the Fisher-information-based parameter freezing strategy from Niu et al. (ICML 2022) to the multi-degradation restoration context, and the overall teacher-student EMA architecture follows the CoTTA lineage. The key conceptual departure from prior TTA-for-restoration work is the 'degradation consistency' principle: multiple degraded views of the same scene should map to a single clean output, enabling supervision without ground truth.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DCTTA provides the first source-free, backbone-agnostic TTA framework for AiOIR, meaning practitioners can take any pre-trained AiOIR model (AirNet, PromptIR, DFPIR) and adapt it to a new degradation distribution at test time without retraining or source data access. The substantial gains on structured degradations (up to +4.57 dB on Rain100H) suggest that distribution shift is a more critical bottleneck for AiOIR than previously acknowledged. Open questions include: how the method behaves on truly novel degradation types with no structural analogy to training degradations, whether the 23.5 GB GPU memory requirement (driven by the diffusion generator) limits deployment, and whether the one-time 32-second adaptation cost is acceptable in streaming or edge settings. The modest improvements on spatially uniform degradations (denoising, dehazing) hint that the consistency signal is most informative when degradations have strong spatial structure.
