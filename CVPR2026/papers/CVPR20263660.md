## Problem

While diffusion models can generate anatomically realistic medical images, it remains unclear which properties of synthetic data actually drive downstream segmentation generalization, and how to efficiently synthesize such data. Existing approaches either rely on expensive DM retraining, unstable post-hoc filtering, or inject off-manifold adversarial perturbations that degrade rather than improve generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263660/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a conditioning segmentation mask (obtained from ground-truth or augmentations), a frozen diffusion model (e.g., FairDiff, SiameseDiff, DiffBoost, SegDiff), and a frozen downstream segmentation model (nnU-Net or SwinUNet). It produces 256×256 synthetic medical images paired with their conditioning masks for downstream training. The adversariality miner operates solely at the seed-selection stage, modifying only the initial Gaussian noise before standard DDIM sampling, so the DM architecture and sampling trajectory remain unchanged.

## Method

The paper first establishes a geometric decomposition of generalization gain into generation quality (gradient alignment angle) and synthetic adversariality (gradient norm), showing that only on-manifold 'native adversariality' from naturally hard DM samples yields consistent gains, while off-manifold 'artificial adversariality' from adversarial guidance methods is detrimental. To amplify native adversariality, the Adversariality Miner is a lightweight plug-in module that conditions on the frozen DM's initial denoising score S_φ^Init (via stop-gradient) and predicts shift statistics (Δμ, ΔΣ) to resample initial noise from a shifted distribution N^r(Δμ, I+ΔΣ). The miner is trained with a clipped adversariality objective (saturating at κ_up to prevent over-optimization) plus a KL regularization term anchoring the shifted noise to the diffusion prior, using a temporal stop-gradient heuristic to collapse the multi-step DDIM Jacobian into a closed-form constant for memory-efficient backpropagation. Only the miner parameters are updated; both the diffusion model and downstream segmentation model remain frozen throughout, yielding a ~1.2× inference overhead per image.

## Results

On the Polyps benchmark (SiameseDiff backbone, nnU-Net downstream), the proposed method achieves a ΔDSC of 10.25 and ΔASD of 3.66, outperforming the strongest baseline GAL (6.99 / 2.53) by over 3 DSC points. On Synapse (DiffBoost backbone, nnU-Net), it reaches ΔDSC 9.12 and ΔASD 7.99, again leading GAL (7.07 / 5.44) and CIG (6.38 / 3.80). The method consistently wins on all eight metric–dataset–backbone combinations in the main comparison, with the largest margins over diversity-oriented baselines (CIG, Da-fusion) and the closest competition from the post-hoc filtering method GAL. Notably, DiffAug produces negative ΔDSC (−3.22) on Polyps, confirming that classifier-oriented perturbations are counterproductive for dense prediction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric across both Synapse and Polyps. The closest competitor is GAL (post-hoc filtering), which is beaten by 2.05 DSC points on Synapse (nnU-Net) and 3.26 on Polyps (nnU-Net); GAL also leads on ASD for Polyps SwinUNet (1.67 vs. 3.20) but trails on DSC. Diversity-oriented methods CIG and Da-fusion achieve mid-range DSC gains (5.5–7.6) but fall 2–4 points short of the proposed method. Uncertainty-guided UGDM and classifier-oriented DiffAug are the weakest: UGDM yields only 1.7–3.3 ΔDSC and even negative ΔASD on SwinUNet, while DiffAug produces negative ΔDSC (−3.22) on Polyps, indicating that stochastic perturbations disrupt spatial consistency needed for segmentation. Adversarial Guidance methods (AdvDiffuser, P2P, Diff-PGD) are shown in §3.2 to reduce generalization below the base-synthesis baseline, confirming that off-manifold adversariality is harmful rather than beneficial.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the noise-reselection paradigm from Noise Hypernetworks [12], which amortizes test-time optimization by training a small network to predict input-noise adjustments, and adopts the temporal stop-gradient heuristic from [39] to enable efficient backpropagation through DDIM trajectories. It departs sharply from the adversarial-guidance line of work (AdvDiffuser, P2P, Diff-PGD), which the paper demonstrates produces off-manifold perturbations that harm rather than help downstream generalization. Conceptually, the paper reframes adversariality not as an attack mechanism but as a generative preference: it introduces the distinction between 'native' (on-manifold, DM-intrinsic) and 'artificial' (off-manifold, attack-induced) adversariality, positioning the adversariality miner as a lightweight seed-selection module rather than a trajectory-modifying guidance term.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean geometric decomposition of generalization gain into generation quality (angle) and synthetic adversariality (norm), offering a principled criterion for what to synthesize beyond visual fidelity. The finding that only ~7.6% of diffusion-generated samples drive the majority of generalization gain, and that adversarial-guidance methods actively degrade performance, challenges the common assumption that 'harder' synthetic data is always better for training. Limitations include the method operating only in image space (not mask space), the assumption that the DM produces on-manifold samples, and evaluation restricted to 2D slices. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D volumes, whether mask-space adversariality mining is feasible, and how the method behaves when the underlying DM has poor generative quality.
