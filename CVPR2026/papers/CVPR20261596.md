## Problem

Existing RGBT trackers model targets solely from initial-frame visual information, causing drift under severe appearance variations, while also suffering from redundant search regions and heterogeneous modality gaps that introduce background distraction. No RGBT tracking benchmark includes textual descriptions, leaving a gap in language-guided target modeling for multi-modal tracking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261596/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each frame, the method consumes paired RGB and TIR search images (256×256), paired RGB and TIR template images (128×128), and a natural-language target description. The text descriptions are initially generated by Qwen2.5-VL-3B from the first frame and dynamically refreshed during inference via the same MLLM. The prediction head outputs a bounding box constructed from classification scores, spatial offsets, and normalized sizes at the highest-scoring location, with a feature dimension of C=512 throughout.

## Method

RAGTrack integrates three components within a HiViT-B backbone: (1) a Multi-modal Transformer Encoder (MTE) that concatenates reasoning tokens, CLIP-encoded text tokens, template tokens, and search tokens into a unified sequence processed by parameter-shared multi-head self-attention layers; (2) an Adaptive Token Fusion (ATF) module deployed at layers 6, 12, 18, and 24 that aggregates four attention-score channels (search-to-reasoning, search-to-text, search-to-template, search-to-search) to retain only the top 85% target-relevant search tokens, then performs adaptive channel exchange between RGB and TIR features based on learned cross-modal relevance; and (3) a Context-aware Reasoning Module (CRM) implementing a four-stage RAG cycle—construction of a dynamic knowledge base (n=4 entries with cosine-similarity deduplication), retrieval of top-k=2 relevant historical text features, augmentation via reasoning-token propagation with temporal cross-attention, and generation of updated target descriptions by the MLLM—enabling continuous linguistic reasoning across frames.

## Results

RAGTrack achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four RGBT tracking benchmarks: 95.1% MPR / 79.3% MSR on GTOT, 93.2% PR / 67.1% SR on RGBT210, 93.8% MPR / 69.5% MSR on RGBT234, and 76.8% PR / 73.0% NPR / 61.1% SR on LasHeR. On the largest benchmark (LasHeR, 1,224 sequences), it surpasses the previous best (TVTracker) by +4.2% PR and (XTrack) by +5.4% SR. Attribute-specific analysis on LasHeR shows particularly strong gains under Total Occlusion (+10.7% PR) and Out-of-View (+5.5% SR) conditions, highlighting the benefit of language-guided temporal reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAGTrack outperforms all 28 compared baselines on every benchmark and metric. On GTOT, it leads MoETrack by +1.5% MPR and MambaVT by +4.0% MSR, confirming advantages under modality misalignment. On RGBT210, it exceeds AETrack by +2.8% PR and AINet by +2.3% SR, demonstrating robustness across diverse environmental conditions. On RGBT234, the margin over SMSTracker reaches +6.9% MPR and over STTrack +2.8% MSR, while on LasHeR it surpasses TVTracker by +4.2% PR and XTrack by +5.4% SR. The largest relative gains appear under challenging attributes (Total Occlusion, Out-of-View), where the CRM's dynamic knowledge base and MLLM-generated descriptions provide the most benefit over purely visual baselines. No baseline matches RAGTrack on any metric across the four benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of RGBT tracking and RGB-Language (RGBL) tracking, two established but previously separate lines of work. It departs from prior RGBT trackers (e.g., TBSI, STTrack, AINet) that model targets solely from initial-frame visual templates, and from prior RGBL trackers (e.g., SNLT, CiteTracker, ReasoningTrack) that face visual-language misalignment over time. The key conceptual departure is repurposing Retrieval-Augmented Generation—a technique originating in NLP—into a temporal reasoning mechanism for tracking, where a dynamic knowledge base of historical text features is retrieved and used to refine search features frame-by-frame. The MTE unifies visual and text tokens in a single transformer (building on HiViT-B and CLIP), while the ATF module addresses modality gaps through attention-guided token selection and channel exchange, a design distinct from prior fusion paradigms like template-bridged interaction or Mamba-based fusion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By extending four standard RGBT benchmarks with MLLM-generated textual annotations, the paper creates a new evaluation setting that the community can adopt, and the Qwen2.5-VL-3B-based annotation pipeline (514K descriptions for LasHeR training) is a practical resource. The finding that even the text-free CRM variant improves over baseline suggests the temporal propagation mechanism itself carries value independent of language content, raising the open question of how much of the gain is attributable to linguistic semantics versus structured temporal memory. A key limitation is the reliance on an external MLLM at inference for description generation, which introduces latency and computational cost that may limit real-time deployment. Whether the language-aware formulation generalizes beyond the four tested benchmarks to other multi-modal tracking settings (e.g., RGB-D, RGB-event) remains unexplored.
