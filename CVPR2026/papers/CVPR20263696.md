## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is the dominant inference-time technique for improving diffusion model output quality, but its reliance on external text conditions makes it unusable for unconditional generation. Existing condition-free alternatives (SAG, PAG, SEG) perturb inputs or attention maps in a global, indiscriminate manner, producing poor details at low guidance scales and noise, oversaturation, or oversimplification at high scales, with a narrow usable range of guidance strengths.

## Input / Output

SSG is a training-free plug-in applied during inference of latent diffusion models (SD1.5, SDXL). For conditional generation it takes a text prompt; for unconditional generation it takes no external condition. Internally, it operates on the model's intermediate token latents (shape B×T×D) at each transformer block and timestep, requiring no architectural modification. The two user-controllable hyperparameters are the guidance scale ω and the swap ratio r (fraction of tokens/channels to exchange). Output is a standard RGB image at the model's native resolution.

## Method

SSG constructs a degraded prediction by selectively swapping pairs of the most semantically dissimilar token latents, rather than injecting global noise. For spatial swap, token vectors are L2-normalised, pairwise cosine similarities across spatial positions are computed, and the N pairs with the lowest similarity (N determined by swap ratio r) are exchanged; channel swap is applied analogously along the channel dimension. Both operations are performed at the beginning of each transformer block and before residual shortcuts across all layers and timesteps. A two-branch forward pass maintains the clean prediction ε_ori and the swapped prediction ε_pert, combined via the standard guidance formula ε̃ = ε_ori + ω(ε_ori − ε_pert). The adversarial selection of dissimilar pairs (inspired by adversarial token analysis in ViTs) is critical: ablations show it outperforms both random and similar-pair swapping. Because SSG perturbs token space while CFG perturbs condition space, the two are orthogonal and can be stacked for additional gains in fidelity and prompt alignment.

## Results

On conditional generation with SDXL on MS-COCO 2014, SSG achieves FID 21.73, CLIP Score 0.313, IS 34.63, AES 5.902, PickScore 22.17, and ImageReward 0.276, outperforming all baselines on every metric. On unconditional generation with SDXL on MS-COCO 2014, SSG reduces FID from 119.04 (no guidance) to 70.91 and raises IS from 9.08 to 16.44. On SD1.5/ImageNet unconditional generation, SSG achieves the best FID of 63.05 and IS of 18.84. SSG also maintains stable quality across a wider range of guidance scales compared to baselines, which suffer from oversaturation or noise at high scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSG outperforms all three condition-free guidance baselines (SAG, SEG, PAG) on every metric across all four main evaluation tables. On the primary conditional generation benchmark (SDXL, MS-COCO 2014), SSG achieves a 4.8-point FID improvement over the closest competitor PAG (21.73 vs. 26.55) and a 6.8-point gap over SEG (28.55), while the largest margin is over SAG (34.14). The most striking difference is on ImageReward: SSG reaches +0.276 whereas all baselines remain at or below zero (PAG: −0.003, SEG: −0.016, SAG: −0.487), indicating substantially better human-preference alignment. On unconditional generation (SDXL, MS-COCO 2014), SSG's FID of 70.91 beats SEG (89.29), PAG (103.72), and SAG (113.33) by wide margins. The paper also notes that SSG maintains stable visual quality across a broader range of guidance scales, whereas SAG, PAG, and SEG exhibit oversaturation, noise, or oversimplified details at higher scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

SSG extends the two-branch guidance structure of Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) [15] to a condition-free setting, replacing the unconditional text branch with a self-generated perturbed branch. It departs from prior condition-free guidance methods (SAG, PAG, SEG, TSG) that inject global noise into inputs or attention maps, instead operating at token granularity by selectively swapping the most semantically dissimilar token latents in spatial or channel dimensions. The adversarial selection of dissimilar pairs draws on insights from adversarial analysis of vision transformers, but the core perturbation mechanism—structured token exchange rather than additive noise—is a novel departure from the noise-injection paradigm that dominates the field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSG unlocks CFG-like quality gains for unconditional generation (e.g., inverse problems, class-agnostic synthesis) where CFG is inapplicable, and does so as a training-free plug-in compatible with existing SD pipelines and stackable on top of CFG. Its fine-grained perturbation yields stable quality across a wider range of guidance scales than prior methods, reducing the need for careful hyperparameter tuning. Limitations include evaluation restricted to two SD variants on image generation only, with no theoretical analysis of why token swap is effective or evidence for video/3D modalities. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-transformer U-Net architectures and whether the swap ratio–guidance scale interaction admits a principled schedule.
