## Problem

Feed-forward Reconstruction Models (FFRMs) achieve impressive 3D reconstruction but depend on large-scale multi-view geometric annotations (dense point maps, camera poses) produced by expensive and brittle SfM/MVS pipelines, making them difficult to scale. The paper asks whether FFRMs can be trained from scratch by learning multi-view geometric principles directly from the images themselves, without any such annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260302/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Reliev3R takes a group of N RGB images (8 contiguous views in experiments) with known camera intrinsics as input and produces view-wise dense depth maps plus camera poses in a single forward pass; together these yield registered 3D point maps in world coordinate. Pseudo-labels for supervision come from zero-shot pretrained models (Depth Pro for relative depth, CoTracker for sparse 2D correspondences), neither of which is trained on the same dataset, simulating real-world scaling to unseen data.

## Method

Built on the π³ architecture (450M parameters) with the point-map head replaced by a depth head, Reliev3R is trained with two complementary losses that together enforce multi-view geometric consistency without ground-truth 3D annotations. First, an ambiguity-aware scale-invariant depth loss regularizes each view's depth shape against pseudo monocular relative depth, using a learned confidence map W_i (bounded in (0,2)) and weighted median absolute deviation to automatically downweight unreliable regions like sky or reflective surfaces. Second, a trigonometry-based reprojection loss minimizes the angle between vectors from a camera center to back-projected 3D points across view pairs, avoiding the trivial L2 collapse that occurs with naive 3D distance minimization and producing correct gradients for both camera pose and depth scale registration. The total objective is L = L_d + 0.5·L_rgst, trained for 60k steps on 64 NPUs with a cosine-scheduled learning rate starting at 1e-4.

## Results

On DL3DV-benchmark (8-view reconstruction), Reliev3R achieves a point-map absolute relative error of 0.122 (τ=0.663), camera-pose ATE of 0.018 (AUC=49.43°), and depth-map relative error of 0.115 (τ=0.657), all without any multi-view geometric annotations. It outperforms early FFRMs MVDUSt3R (point-map rel 0.593) and FLARE (point-map rel 0.134) on most metrics, and significantly surpasses AnyCam (point-map rel 0.262, depth rel 0.181) across all three task categories. In zero-shot evaluation on ScanNet++ (unseen focal length), Reliev3R matches the fully-supervised π³† overall while substantially outperforming it in depth estimation (rel 0.124 vs 0.220, τ 0.583 vs 0.171).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against early FFRMs, Reliev3R clearly wins: it reduces point-map relative error from 0.593 (MVDUSt3R) to 0.122 and from 0.134 (FLARE) to 0.122, while improving depth-map τ from 0.134/0.302 to 0.657; camera-pose ATE drops from 0.458/0.338 to 0.018. Against AnyCam (the only other method trained without MVG annotations), Reliev3R wins on every metric—point-map rel 0.122 vs 0.262, depth rel 0.115 vs 0.181, and pose ATE 0.018 vs 0.023—demonstrating that jointly predicting geometry and poses is superior to pose-only estimation. Against the fully-supervised π³† (same architecture, same DL3DV-10K data, but with MVG labels), Reliev3R trails on all in-domain metrics (e.g., point-map rel 0.122 vs 0.057, depth rel 0.115 vs 0.047), confirming a gap that full supervision still provides. However, in zero-shot ScanNet++ evaluation, Reliev3R closes that gap: it matches π³† overall and substantially outperforms it in depth estimation (rel 0.124 vs 0.220, τ 0.583 vs 0.171), suggesting the weakly-supervised paradigm generalizes more robustly to unseen camera parameters. Against the SOTA FFRMs (Map-Anything, π³, VGGT, CUT3R), Reliev3R remains behind, but the paper notes these were trained on roughly 20× more data, making direct comparison unfair.

## Relation to Prior Work

Reliev3R builds its architecture on π³, replacing the point-map prediction head with a depth prediction head, but fundamentally departs from the standard FFRM training paradigm established by DUSt3R, FLARE, VGGT, and Map-Anything, all of which require expensive SfM/MVS multi-view geometric annotations. Its closest conceptual neighbor is AnyCam, which also avoids geometric annotations but only predicts camera parameters rather than full scene geometry. The key departure is the use of pseudo monocular relative depths (Depth Pro) and pseudo sparse correspondences (CoTracker) as weak supervision signals, coupled with two novel losses (ambiguity-aware scale-invariant depth loss and trigonometry-based reprojection loss) to enforce multi-view geometric consistency without ground-truth 3D labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that feed-forward 3D reconstruction can be trained from scratch without the cost-prohibitive SfM/MVS annotation pipeline, opening a path toward scaling FFRMs to massive unlabeled video data. However, the empirical validation is limited to DL3DV-10K (roughly 5% of the data used by current SOTAs), and the authors explicitly acknowledge that large-scale data scaling has not been verified. The zero-shot result on ScanNet++—where Reliev3R matches or surpasses the fully-supervised π³† in depth estimation—is a promising sign of robustness, but dynamic scenes remain unhandled and the pseudo-label sources (Depth Pro, CoTracker) introduce inherited weaknesses. The broader open question is whether this weakly-supervised paradigm can close the gap with SOTA FFRMs when given comparable data scale.
