## Problem

Multimodal LLMs struggle with high information density (HID) charts due to three coupled failures: fine-grained perception omits critical visual cues buried in dense subplots and annotations, redundant visual information degrades reasoning accuracy, and fixed KL penalties in RL training over-constrain the exploration needed for longer reasoning chains over many visual cues. Additionally, existing chart benchmarks lack sufficient information density to rigorously expose these weaknesses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262191/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a chart image (RGB, up to ~2487×1716 px in HID-Chart) and a natural-language question as input. It produces a structured Focus-CoT reasoning chain in which each reasoning step is anchored by a <focus> tag containing OCR text extractions and bounding-box localizations, followed by a final answer. The output format is explicitly scored: a full Focus-CoT structure earns 1.0 format reward, a plain CoT earns 0.667, and unstructured output earns 0.

## Method

Chart-FR1 uses a two-stage training paradigm on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Stage 1 (cold-start) introduces Focus-CoT, a visual focusing chain-of-thought where a <focus> tag couples each reasoning step to OCR text and a local image region; training data is synthesized by a GPT-5 teacher that inserts focus tags into existing CoT paths, then the model is fine-tuned for one epoch. Stage 2 applies Focus-GRPO, a modified GRPO with three key changes: a relaxed-accuracy reward granting full credit within 5% relative error for numerical answers, an information-efficiency reward defined as exp(−α·P_redundancy) where P_redundancy averages OCR-OCR text similarity, Box-Box IoU, and OCR-Box label similarity to penalize redundant visual cues, and an adaptive KL penalty β′ = β/(1+log(1+N_info)) that relaxes the constraint as more focused cues are discovered, enabling deeper reasoning without destabilizing training.

## Results

Chart-FR1-7B achieves an average accuracy of 67.1% across five chart benchmarks (ChartQA, CharXiv, EvoChart, ChartBench, PlotQA), outperforming the base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 6.1% and the closed-source GPT-4o by 3.1%. On the newly introduced HID-Chart benchmark, Chart-FR1-7B reaches 53.0% average accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o (51.2%) and the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B (51.5%) by 1.8% and 1.5% respectively. The method also generalizes well across model scales, with Chart-FR1-3B improving over Qwen2.5-VL-3B by 9.0% average and Chart-FR1-8B over Qwen3-VL-8B by 5.8%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Chart-FR1-7B (7B parameters) outperforms all open-source baselines across the five standard chart benchmarks, beating the strongest open-source model InternVL2.5-78B by 1.2% in average accuracy and the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B by 6.1%. Against closed-source models, Chart-FR1-7B surpasses GPT-4o by 3.1% on average, though GPT-4o retains an edge on CharXiv (47.1 vs. 46.6) and EvoChart (63.9 vs. 59.2). Among chart-specific MLLMs, Chart-FR1-7B leads ChartSketcher-72B (a 72B model) by 4.3% on average, with particularly large gains on ChartBench (75.6 vs. 68.3) and CharXiv (46.6 vs. 36.6). On the HID-Chart benchmark, Chart-FR1-7B (53.0%) exceeds GPT-4o (51.2%), GPT-4.1 (63.7% is the top closed-source), and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (51.5%), while the performance gap between low-density and high-density intervals narrows compared to all baselines, confirming the method's robustness to information density. Among reasoning MLLMs, Chart-FR1-7B outperforms OpenVLThinker-7B (62.6%) by 4.5% and Vision-R1-7B (60.3%) by 6.8% on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends two established lines of work: (1) the GRPO reinforcement learning algorithm from DeepSeek-R1, which it modifies into Focus-GRPO by adding an information-efficiency reward that penalizes redundant visual cues and an adaptive KL penalty that relaxes constraints when more visual evidence is gathered; and (2) linguistic Chain-of-Thought prompting, which it augments with a <focus> tag mechanism that explicitly anchors reasoning steps to OCR text and bounding-box regions. It departs from prior chart-specific MLLMs (ChartReasoner, EvoChart, ChartPoint, ChartSketcher) by arguing that their instruction-tuning or code-generation approaches lack information-efficient supervision, and from reasoning MLLMs (R1-VL, Vision-R1, OpenVLThinker) by identifying the fixed KL penalty as a bottleneck for deep reasoning on dense charts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The HID-Chart benchmark and its information-density metric fill a genuine evaluation gap: existing chart benchmarks average 3.1–3.75 density while real-world scientific and financial charts routinely exceed 4.0, and the paper demonstrates that all current MLLMs degrade sharply in this regime. The adaptive KL penalty is a practical, low-cost modification to GRPO that could transfer to other RL-trained multimodal models facing variable reasoning-depth requirements. Open questions include whether the MLLM-scored density metric is sufficiently objective, whether the Focus-CoT mechanism generalizes beyond chart tasks to other dense visual documents (tables, diagrams, medical images), and whether the reliance on GPT-5 for cold-start data generation limits reproducibility.
