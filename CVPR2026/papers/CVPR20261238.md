## Problem

Existing depth completion methods are typically tailored to specific sensor configurations and degrade significantly under the extreme sparsity, irregular sampling patterns, and hardware-induced noise characteristic of direct Time-of-Flight (dToF) sensors. Approaches that achieve broader generalization rely on diffusion models, iterative optimization, or multi-stage refinement, incurring prohibitive computational costs for real-time or mobile deployment. This paper seeks a single lightweight model that generalizes zero-shot across diverse dToF device families without heavy post-processing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution RGB image (H×W×3) and a sparse dToF depth map of arbitrary resolution and sampling pattern (flash dots, sub-VGA grids, or rotating LiDAR line scans). Before encoding, the sparse depth is upsampled to RGB resolution, filled via nearest-neighbor interpolation, log-normalized, and packed into a 3-channel tensor (normalized depth duplicated twice plus a sparse validity mask as the third channel) scaled to [−1, 1] to match DINOv2's input distribution. The model outputs a dense metric depth map at full RGB resolution and a binary validity mask flagging unobservable regions (e.g., sky); metric scale is recovered by inverting the log-normalization with the per-frame α, β parameters, allowing extrapolation beyond the original sensor range.

## Method

The core architecture is a depth-guided dual-branch ViT encoder with two parallel DINOv2-initialized branches (one for RGB, one for the normalized depth tensor) that interact through a masked joint attention mechanism: queries, keys, and values are computed jointly from concatenated image and depth tokens, but a directional mask G = [[1,1],[0,1]] permits depth-to-image attention while suppressing image-to-depth attention, so depth tokens guide RGB features without being corrupted by unreliable appearance cues. A lightweight DPT decoder with two heads (validity mask and normalized depth) reconstructs the output, and the entire model is trained end-to-end with depth-weighted L1, global/local 3D scale-invariant, and binary cross-entropy mask losses. To overcome the scarcity of paired real dToF data, the authors build a comprehensive simulation pipeline that synthesizes sparse depth from large-scale RGB-D datasets by modeling flash (64–10K random points), sub-VGA flash (downsampled with Perlin-noise-driven erosion/dilation), and rotating LiDAR (stochastic ray sampling matching Velodyne VLP-16/32 specs) patterns, plus Gaussian noise, spatial jitter, outlier injection, and occlusion inpainting. The ViT-Small model (features from layers 6 and 12, dynamic token counts of 800–2000 RGB and 800–1200 depth) is trained for 100k iterations on 8 A100 GPUs and achieves zero-shot generalization across 6 datasets and 3 real dToF devices at 34 ms inference with 0.44 GB memory.

## Results

On the average across 6 datasets (2 real-sensor, 4 simulated), the proposed method achieves a relative depth error (Rel) of 3.46% and threshold accuracy (δ) of 77.9%, the best Rel among all compared methods. Inference takes only 34 ms with 0.44 GB memory on an A100 GPU, making it 20× faster and 10× lighter than OMNI-DC (638 ms, 3.49 GB) and ~190× faster than Marigold-DC (6470 ms, 5.71 GB). On the most challenging real-sensor benchmarks, the method achieves the best Rel on ETH3D (0.84%) and ties for best on ZJUL5 (11.13%), while on DDAD it attains the lowest Rel (4.61%) though slightly lower δ than OMNI-DC (68.8 vs. 72.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OMNI-DC (optimization-based, ICCV 2025), the proposed method achieves a lower average Rel (3.46 vs. 3.77) but a slightly lower average δ (77.9 vs. 79.0), while running 20× faster (34 ms vs. 638 ms) and using 10× less memory (0.44 vs. 3.49 GB). Against PromptDA (CVPR 2025), the method wins decisively on both metrics—average Rel improves from 5.09/5.14 to 3.46 and δ from 66.3/64.9 to 77.9—with comparable or better inference speed. Against PriorDA (two-stage refinement), the method reduces average Rel from 4.14/3.99 to 3.46 and δ from 73.2/74.5 to 77.9, while being 3–6× faster. Against Marigold-DC (diffusion-based, ICCV 2025), the improvement is the most dramatic: average Rel drops from 6.11 to 3.46 and δ rises from 59.1 to 77.9, with a ~190× speedup (34 ms vs. 6470 ms). The only per-dataset loss is on DDAD δ (68.8 vs. OMNI-DC's 72.7), where the rotating-LiDAR line pattern is particularly challenging for threshold accuracy despite the best Rel.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the DINOv2-pretrained ViT encoder and DPT decoder architecture from monocular depth estimation (MoGe, Depth Anything) into a dual-branch depth completion framework, departing from prior methods that treat sparse depth as a mere auxiliary signal injected at the decoder level (PromptDA, PriorDA) or that rely on diffusion priors (Marigold-DC) and iterative optimization (OMNI-DC). The key architectural departure is the masked joint attention mechanism, which enables directional depth-to-image feature flow while preserving depth token integrity—a design that is structurally isomorphic to standard ViT self-attention, allowing direct initialization from DINOv2 weights. The dToF simulation pipeline, which models flash, sub-VGA, and rotating sensor families with hardware-specific noise and sparsity patterns, is a new contribution that addresses the scarcity of paired real-sensor training data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× speedup and 10× memory reduction over OMNI-DC while matching or exceeding accuracy makes dense metric depth completion from sparse dToF practically deployable on mobile and embedded platforms, which is critical for VR/XR and robotics applications. The strong zero-shot generalization from purely synthetic training to three distinct real dToF devices (Velodyne LiDAR, VL53L5CX, Luminar-H2) suggests the simulation pipeline captures the essential sensor-agnostic structure of dToF measurements. Open questions include whether the approach degrades gracefully under even more extreme sparsity (below 100 points), whether the simulation pipeline adequately captures all real-world multipath and calibration artifacts, and whether the masked joint attention design generalizes to other multi-modal fusion tasks beyond depth completion.
