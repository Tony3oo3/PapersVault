## Problem

In multimodal sentiment analysis, dominant modalities (especially text) accumulate larger gradients during joint training, progressively suppressing weaker audio and visual signals in a 'Matthew effect' that degrades fusion quality and robustness. Existing methods largely assume balanced modality contributions and provide limited mechanisms to counteract this imbalance, particularly under noisy or missing-modality conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260855/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes pre-extracted per-frame features: 768-dim BERT hidden states plus 300-dim GloVe vectors for text, 35 facial action unit features for video, and 74-dim COVAREP acoustic descriptors for audio. It outputs a continuous sentiment intensity score (regression target in [-3, +3]) or discrete category labels (binary for MOSI, 7-class for MOSEI, 4-class for IEMOCAP), and is designed to operate under both full-modality and arbitrary modality-missing test conditions without architectural changes.

## Method

EBMC adopts a two-stage 'enhance-then-balance' design. Stage I first disentangles each modality into shared and specific components via lightweight MLPs constrained by an InfoNCE alignment loss, a batch-wise cosine disentanglement penalty, and a unimodal task loss; a Cross-modal Complementary Enhancement module then enriches each modality by injecting shared and specific cues from the other modalities through a small enhancement network trained with reconstruction and task losses. Stage II introduces an Energy-guided Modality Coordination mechanism that defines a per-modality energy potential (combining representation norm, task loss, and predictive entropy) and enforces both an energy-gap minimization objective and an energy-gradient descent update, implicitly rebalancing gradient flow so dominant modalities are suppressed and weaker ones receive corrective updates. Finally, an Instance-aware Modality Trust Distillation module converts teacher-model predictive variance into per-sample confidence scores that weight a KL-divergence distillation loss, adaptively down-weighting unreliable modalities at inference time.

## Results

On CMU-MOSI, EBMC achieves 86.26/87.84 Acc-2, 86.20/87.79 F1, 50.34% Acc-7, 0.833 Corr, and 0.694 MAE, outperforming the strongest baseline Semi-IIN by 3.84% in Acc-7 (50.34 vs. 46.50) and 0.79% in F1. On CMU-MOSEI, EBMC reaches 86.04/88.10 Acc-2, 86.23/88.07 F1, 57.32% Acc-7, 0.824 Corr, and 0.505 MAE, surpassing Semi-IIN by 1.43% in Acc-7 and 0.42% in F1. On the IEMOCAP ERC benchmark, EBMC attains an average F1 of 86.35%, exceeding the best baseline DMD (85.08%) by 1.27%. Under six incomplete-modality testing conditions on CMU-MOSEI, EBMC achieves an average F1 of 78.92%, outperforming the next-best method EUAR (74.50%) by 4.42%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EBMC outperforms all 11 MSA baselines on both CMU-MOSI and CMU-MOSEI across every reported metric. The largest margin is in Acc-7: EBMC exceeds Semi-IIN by 3.84% on MOSI (50.34 vs. 46.50) and by 1.43% on MOSEI (57.32 vs. 55.89), indicating that cross-modal complementary enhancement is particularly effective for fine-grained sentiment classification. On F1, EBMC surpasses GLoMo by 1.19% on MOSI (87.79 vs. 86.60) and 1.67% on MOSEI (88.07 vs. 86.40), and beats ConKI by 1.66% on MOSI (87.79 vs. 86.13). On the IEMOCAP ERC benchmark, EBMC achieves 86.35% average F1, outperforming DMD (85.08%) by 1.27%, with the largest gains in Happy (+0.9%) and Angry (+1.7%) categories. Under incomplete-modality testing on CMU-MOSEI, EBMC leads EUAR by 4.42% in average F1 (78.92 vs. 74.50) and CorrKD by 4.90% (78.92 vs. 74.02), with the most pronounced advantage in single-modality conditions (e.g., 71.67 vs. 66.54 for {A} only). Under intra-modality missingness (random frame-level dropout), EBMC surpasses LNLN by 1.52% in F1 on MOSI and improves Corr by 0.058 on MOSEI, confirming that instance-aware trust distillation provides robustness that fixed-weight fusion strategies cannot match.

## Relation to Prior Work

EBMC synthesizes three established research threads—modality semantic disentanglement (MISA, DMD), gradient-based modality rebalancing (PMR, Detached, on-the-fly gradient modulation), and robust fusion under missing modalities (GCNet, CorrKD)—into a unified two-stage architecture. Its most distinctive departure is the Energy-based Model (EBM) framing for modality coordination: rather than manually reweighting losses or applying heuristic gradient scaling, the paper constructs a differentiable energy landscape per modality and uses energy-gap minimization plus energy-descent updates to implicitly rebalance optimization. This EBM-inspired coordination mechanism is explicitly positioned as the first of its kind in the MSA literature, though the underlying idea of balancing gradient magnitudes across modalities has precedent in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical relevance is clear: modality imbalance (text dominance) and missing-modality robustness are persistent pain points in deployed multimodal systems, and EBMC's instance-level trust distillation offers a principled way to handle both without architectural changes at inference. However, the performance gains over strong baselines are modest (1–2% on standard MSA metrics), and the EBM framing, while conceptually elegant, may be largely a reparameterization of existing gradient-balancing objectives rather than a fundamentally new optimization principle. Open questions include whether the energy-based coordination generalizes beyond three modalities or to tasks with more extreme imbalance (e.g., text + sensor data), whether the hyperparameter sensitivity (all λ, β, γ, η set to 0.1) reflects a robust design or a narrow operating point, and whether the EBM perspective yields theoretical guarantees on convergence that pure empirical gradient balancing cannot.
