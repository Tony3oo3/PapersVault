## Problem

Existing SNN-based RGB tracking frameworks either decode spike signals into continuous values for computation (preventing fully spike-driven processing) or adopt one-stream architectures that mimic dense ANN interactions, underusing the spatiotemporal dynamics of spiking neurons. This creates an unresolved trade-off between energy efficiency and tracking accuracy, leaving no SNN tracker that is both fully spike-driven and competitive with state-of-the-art ANN methods.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB template image (target bounding box expanded by a factor of 4) and an RGB search region image at 256×256 or 384×384 resolution. For multi-timestep variants (T=3), the template branch receives T template images while the search branch processes a single frame, making the computation asymmetric. The output is a predicted bounding box (center, local offset, normalized width and height) plus a confidence score derived from the localization branch, with the template set updated online via a FIFO queue (interval 25, threshold 0.7).

## Method

SpikeTrack employs an asymmetric Siamese architecture where the template branch expands across T timesteps (jointly modeling template representations through NI-LIF neuron spatiotemporal dynamics with a learnable decay factor) while the search branch performs single-timestep inference, and information flows only from template to search. A Memory Retrieval Module (MRM) enables unidirectional transfer by pre-computing a compact memory matrix M = K_S^T V_S from template features, then recurrently querying it with temporally expanded search features through a three-stage loop (global contour encoding, detail construction via timestep-specific SSConvs, and feedback refinement). The backbone is a Spike-Driven Transformer v3 with CNN and Transformer SNN blocks, using an Efficient Spike-Driven Self-Attention (E-SDSA) that achieves linear complexity in token length by rearranging the computation order of binary spike tensors. The template branch runs only at initialization or update, caching intermediate-layer features as memory, so per-frame cost is dominated by the lightweight search branch. Training uses weighted focal loss plus L1 and GIoU losses on COCO, LaSOT, TrackingNet, and GOT-10k, with ImageNet-pretrained backbone weights and a two-stage curriculum (T=1 pretraining followed by T>1 fine-tuning).

## Results

SpikeTrack-B256-T3 achieves 72.2% AUC on GOT-10k and 67.1% AUC on LaSOT at only 9.8 mJ energy, surpassing the precision-oriented TransT (75.2 mJ) by 2.2% AUC on LaSOT while consuming only 1/7.6 of its energy. The smaller SpikeTrack-S256-T3 variant (3.7 mJ) matches the efficient ANN tracker AsymTrack-B (8.3 mJ) in GOT-10k AO (67.8% vs 67.7%) with 2.5× lower energy. On UAV123, SpikeTrack-S256-T3 outperforms the best prior SNN tracker SpikeSiamFC++ by 8.5% AUC (66.2% vs 57.8%). The largest variant SpikeTrack-B384-T3 reaches 73.1% AUC on GOT-10k and 66.7% on LaSOT at 27.3 mJ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the precision-oriented ANN tracker TransT (75.2 mJ), SpikeTrack-B256-T3 achieves 2.2% higher LaSOT AUC (67.1% vs 64.9%) at 7.6× lower energy. Compared to the efficient ANN AsymTrack-B (8.3 mJ), SpikeTrack-S256-T3 (3.7 mJ) matches GOT-10k AO (67.8% vs 67.7%) at 2.5× lower power, and SpikeTrack-B256-T1 (8.1 mJ) gains 1.4% LaSOT_ext AUC at comparable energy. Against the one-stream ANN OSTrack-256 (98.9 mJ), SpikeTrack-B384-T3 delivers 0.5% higher TNL2K AUC (54.8% vs 54.3%) at less than 1/3 the energy. Among SNN baselines, SpikeTrack-S256-T3 outperforms SpikeSiamFC++ by 8.5% AUC on UAV123 (66.2% vs 57.8%) and SiamSNN by 38.2% AO on GOT-10k (67.8% vs 31.4%). Against SwinTrack-224 (29.4 mJ), SpikeTrack-B256-T3 (9.8 mJ) matches AUC on TrackingNet (81.1% vs 81.1%) at 1/3 the energy, and against CSWinTT (75.4 mJ), SpikeTrack-B384-T3 reaches equivalent TrackingNet AUC (82.0% vs 81.9%) at 35% of the energy.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpikeTrack departs from two prior lines of SNN tracking: (1) RGB-based Siamese SNNs (SiamSNN, Spike-SiamFC++) that decode spike signals into continuous values, undermining true spike-driven computation, and (2) event-based one-stream SNN trackers (SDTrack, unified frame-event tracking) that concatenate template and search tokens for dense bidirectional interaction, wasting the spatiotemporal dynamics of spiking neurons. Conceptually, it adapts the asymmetric Siamese / unidirectional-modulation idea from the ANN tracker AsymTrack into the SNN setting, but replaces AsymTrack's template-as-convolution-kernel modulation with a neuroscience-inspired Memory Retrieval Module that recurrently queries a pre-computed memory matrix. The backbone is Spike-Driven Transformer v3, and the NI-LIF neuron with learnable decay extends prior integer-valued SNN training to allow adaptive cross-timestep correlation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpikeTrack is positioned as the first fully spike-driven RGB tracking framework, closing a gap where prior SNN trackers were either not truly spike-driven or not evaluated for energy. The 7.6× energy reduction versus TransT at higher accuracy is a concrete step toward deploying trackers on neuromorphic hardware, where SNNs' event-driven computation offers real power savings. The acknowledged limitation—difficulty distinguishing similar objects due to the coarse granularity of spike encoding—points to an open question about how to convey fine-grained semantic information through spike-based mechanisms, which will constrain the ceiling of SNN trackers until addressed.
