## Problem

In RGBT tracking, modality-missing events (sensor failure, occlusion, misalignment) cause incomplete and unstable feature representations that sharply degrade tracking performance. Existing reconstruction or expert-routing methods rely primarily on current-frame spatial cues, neglecting temporal correlations from historical frames, and require separate branches or explicit switching to handle missing versus complete modality configurations, limiting scalability and temporal consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261924/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired RGB and thermal video sequences (template 128×128, search 256×256) encoded by a shared ViT-B backbone, and outputs a single object bounding box per frame via a fully convolutional prediction head. Uniquely, the same network accepts either both modalities or only one: when a modality is absent, its feature is reconstructed from the available modality plus temporal context before fusion, so the downstream tracking head always receives a complete two-modality feature concatenation regardless of input availability.

## Method

SCDT reformulates multimodal fusion as a spatio-temporal conditional denoising process: the available modality's encoded features are perturbed with adaptive Gaussian noise (strong σ² for missing-modality reconstruction, weak σ² for complete-modality enhancement), and a transformer denoiser progressively refines the noisy tokens conditioned on spatial cues from the current frame and dual temporal cues. Short-term temporal context from recent neighboring frames of the complementary modality is injected via cross-attention to enforce fine-grained motion continuity, while a long-term modality-evolution token modulates features through a FiLM-style scale-and-shift operation for global coherence. A noise-modulated adaptation mechanism unifies both scenarios in a single architecture: strong noise paired with an L2 reconstruction loss drives missing-modality recovery, whereas weak noise paired with a first- and second-order statistical alignment loss promotes discriminative feature enhancement under complete conditions, with identical denoiser weights and computation across all modality configurations.

## Results

SCDT achieves state-of-the-art or near-SOTA performance across all six RGBT tracking benchmarks. On complete-modality datasets, it reaches 77.4% PR / 61.0% SR on LasHeR, 93.1% MPR / 69.6% MSR on RGBT234, and 93.6% PR / 78.9% SR on VTUAV. Under modality-missing conditions, SCDT sets new records of 69.3% PR / 54.4% SR on LasHeR-Miss, 88.1% MPR / 64.3% MSR on RGBT234-Miss, and 84.1% PR / 69.6% SR on VTUAV-Miss, outperforming the previous best (FlexTrack) by 4.2% PR on LasHeR-Miss and 4.0% MPR on RGBT234-Miss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCDT outperforms all 19 compared baselines on the majority of metrics across the six benchmarks. Against the closest competitor FlexTrack (ICCV 2025), SCDT gains +4.2% PR and +2.1% SR on LasHeR-Miss, +4.0% MPR and +1.7% MSR on RGBT234-Miss, while narrowly trailing by 1.0% SR on LasHeR (61.0 vs 62.0) and 0.3% MSR on RGBT234 (69.6 vs 69.9). Against IPL (IJCV 2025), the previous dedicated modality-missing tracker, SCDT improves by +7.6% PR on LasHeR-Miss, +6.1% MPR on RGBT234-Miss, and +3.2% PR on VTUAV-Miss. Among complete-modality trackers (STTrack, SUTrack, AINet, CAFormer, CKD), SCDT leads on all reported metrics, with the largest margins over STTrack (+14.8% PR on LasHeR-Miss) and SUTrack (+11.0% MPR on RGBT234-Miss). On VTUAV and VTUAV-Miss, where fewer baselines report results, SCDT achieves the best scores (93.6/78.9 and 84.1/69.6 respectively), surpassing CKD by +3.4% PR on VTUAV and IPL by +3.2% PR on VTUAV-Miss.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCDT builds directly on ODTrack's ViT-B encoder and tracking head, repurposing the diffusion denoising paradigm from image generation as a feature-reconstruction mechanism for RGBT tracking. It departs from prior modality-missing approaches (IPL's invertible prompt learning, FlexTrack's mixture-of-experts routing) by replacing scenario-specific architectural switching with a single noise-modulated denoiser that handles both complete and missing modalities through different noise intensities. The spatio-temporal conditioning strategy—combining short-term cross-attention from adjacent frames with long-term FiLM modulation from a global modality token—is a novel integration that prior RGBT trackers (which model intra-modal temporal dependencies) had not applied to cross-modal reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified single-model design eliminates the need for separate branches or explicit modality-availability switching, which simplifies deployment in real-world sensor systems where modalities fail unpredictably. The consistent SOTA across all six benchmarks (complete and missing) validates that temporal conditioning is a critical missing ingredient in prior reconstruction-based trackers. Open questions include whether the single-step denoising (as opposed to iterative multi-step diffusion) is sufficient for more severe or prolonged missing periods, whether the approach generalizes to modality combinations beyond RGB-thermal (e.g., RGB-IR, RGB-LiDAR), and whether the noise schedule can be learned end-to-end rather than hand-tuned per scenario.
