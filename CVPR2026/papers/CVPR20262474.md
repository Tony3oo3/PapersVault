## Problem

Existing self-supervised monocular depth estimation methods for endoscopy are largely confined to in-domain settings and fail to generalize across different surgical procedures (e.g., laparoscopy vs. colonoscopy) due to substantial variations in depth distributions, illumination, and tissue texture patterns. While recent works adapt Vision Foundation Models to endoscopic scenes, their adaptation is typically limited to a single domain, leaving cross-domain generalization unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262474/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of monocular RGB endoscopic frames (target and source) from video sequences, along with a discrete domain indicator identifying the source dataset. Beyond the primary depth map output, the framework jointly estimates 6-DOF camera pose, appearance flow for brightness correction, and camera intrinsics via a dedicated estimation network. The model is trained on ~57K mixed frames from four datasets and evaluated zero-shot on unseen endoscopic datasets (C3VD, C3VDv2, SERV-CT) as well as in-domain on SCARED and SimCol3D.

## Method

DAE builds on Depth Anything v2 and introduces a dual-level Mixture-of-Experts adaptation: an intra-model MoE dynamically routes each input through Top-1 selected LoRA experts (ranks 4/8/16/32) and convolutional adapter experts (kernel sizes 3/5/7/9) to flexibly adjust feature representations, while an extra-model MoE of domain-specific pre-trained teachers provides static depth guidance via scale-invariant and ranking losses. A learnable gradient harmonization mechanism maps the domain indicator through an embedding and MLP to produce a per-domain scaling factor that adaptively balances gradient magnitudes between the depth and pose networks during joint photometric-loss optimization. A semantic distribution calibration module projects DINOv3 features through a GRU and enforces alignment with the model's own features via KL divergence on normalized feature distributions, strengthening semantic consistency of depth predictions.

## Results

On zero-shot evaluation, DAE achieves AbsRel of 0.086 (RMSE 4.397, δ 0.934) on C3VD, 0.132 (RMSE 6.051, δ 0.846) on C3VDv2, and 0.078 (RMSE 9.064, δ 0.949) on SERV-CT, outperforming all compared methods across all three datasets. In the in-domain setting, DAE reaches AbsRel 0.047 (RMSE 4.156, δ 0.983) on SCARED and AbsRel 0.088 (RMSE 0.450, δ 0.949) on SimCol3D using a single unified model without per-dataset fine-tuning, surpassing methods trained independently on each domain. The training set comprises ~57K frames from four mixed endoscopic datasets (SCARED, Hamlyn, SimCol3D, Colondepth).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DAE outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric across all three zero-shot datasets. Against the strongest VFM-adapted baselines (EndoDAC, Endo-FASt3r, DARES), DAE reduces AbsRel by 3–4× on C3VD (0.086 vs. 0.256–0.320) and by ~2× on C3VDv2 (0.132 vs. 0.237–0.293), demonstrating that single-domain adaptation fails to generalize. Even when EndoDAC and Endo-FASt3r are retrained on the same 57K mixed frames (marked †), DAE still leads: 0.086 vs. 0.114/0.121 AbsRel on C3VD and 0.078 vs. 0.115/0.132 on SERV-CT. The general-purpose Depth Anything v2, despite 62.6M training samples, degrades severely on endoscopic data (AbsRel 0.208 on C3VD, 0.164 on SERV-CT), confirming the domain gap. In the in-domain setting (Table 2), DAE's unified model beats per-dataset-trained methods: on SCARED it achieves AbsRel 0.047 vs. 0.051–0.052 for Endo-FASt3r/DARES, and on SimCol3D it reaches AbsRel 0.088 vs. 0.093–0.104 for DARES/Endo-FASt3r, without any fine-tuning on the target domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of self-supervised monocular depth estimation (Godard et al., Zhou et al.), endoscopic-specific adaptations (Shao et al. 2022, Liu et al. 2019), and parameter-efficient fine-tuning of Vision Foundation Models (EndoDAC, DARES, Endo-FASt3r). It departs from prior endoscopic depth work, which is confined to single-domain in-domain training, by introducing a dual-level Mixture-of-Experts paradigm that simultaneously handles multiple surgical procedures (laparoscopy, colonoscopy) within one self-supervised model. The intra-model MoE (LoRA experts of varying ranks + convolutional adapters of varying kernel sizes) extends the fixed-rank LoRA adaptation used in EndoDAC and DARES, while the extra-model MoE with domain-specific frozen teachers is a new component not present in prior endoscopic depth methods. The learnable gradient harmonization and DINOv3-based semantic calibration are novel loss-level contributions layered on top of the standard photometric self-supervision framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a single unified model can now serve multiple endoscopic procedures without per-domain retraining, which is critical in surgical settings where ground-truth depth is unavailable and procedures vary widely. The finding that a unified MoE model outperforms domain-specific baselines even in in-domain evaluation (Table 2) suggests that cross-domain training acts as a regularizer rather than a liability. Limitations include evaluation on only three zero-shot datasets, qualitative-only arthroscopy results, and a relatively small training set (~57K frames); it remains open whether the dual-level MoE scales to more than two surgical domains or to other medical imaging tasks. The approach also depends on pre-trained domain-specific teachers for the extra-model MoE, which partially undermines the 'self-supervised' claim for the guidance signal.
