## Problem

Formal verification of neural networks has largely focused on ℓp-norm perturbations in pixel space or 2D affine transforms, neither of which can model 3D camera motion that changes perspective. Robustness to camera pose perturbations remains an open problem despite being essential for deploying vision systems in safety-critical domains like autonomous driving and aerospace, where the capturing camera moves relative to planar scene features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260536/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an original image, a target neural network, and interval bounds on 6-DOF camera pose parameters (Δroll, Δpitch, Δyaw, Δx, Δy, Δz) defining the Operational Design Domain. For each pixel in the transformed image, it computes piecewise-linear lower and upper bounds as functions of the pose parameters, accounting for bilinear interpolation at sub-pixel coordinates. These per-pixel bounds are then propagated through the VENUS MILP verifier to produce a complete robustness certificate (or counterexample) for the network's classification output.

## Method

The paper derives closed-form 3×3 homography matrices H(κ) parameterized by 6-DOF camera pose perturbations for planar scenes, using the standard pinhole camera model and plane-to-plane projective geometry. It then extends the PWL algorithm of Batten et al. to handle these non-affine transforms by computing the inverse homography T⁻¹(κ) for each pixel, evaluating the resulting pixel-value function G(κ) = I ∘ T⁻¹(κ) at sample points, and fitting piecewise-linear bounds (q=2 segments) via relaxed linear programs. The key technical challenge is that the homography denominator h₃(κ) introduces discontinuities in G(κ); the authors derive analytical Lipschitz constants by identifying candidate locations for maximum gradient values (including the critical angle where the denominator vanishes) and applying Lipschitz optimization to shift the initially unsound bounds to sound ones. Implementation improvements over PWL include half-pixel corrections for exact gradients, dichotomy-based splitting from the first iteration, and pixel-level parallelization, yielding up to 89% speedup and 7% tighter bounds.

## Results

Compared to the PWL baseline [7], the proposed method achieves up to 89% total speedup (87.6s → 7.0s on MNIST 5° rotation) and reduces branch-and-bound iterations by over 71% (124 → 17.5 steps), while producing up to 7% tighter polytope bounds. On the VNN-COMP benchmarks, GTSRB traffic-sign models show 0% certified robustness to all six 3D camera-motion perturbations (roll, pitch, yaw, Δx, Δy, Δz), and MNIST/CIFAR-10 models drop to 5–25% robustness under pitch/yaw/Δx/Δz. The runway visibility case study certifies only 16% of test images under 10 cm translations and 1% under 1° rotations, revealing practical vulnerabilities in a safety-critical aerospace classifier.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole quantitative baseline is PWL (Batten et al., ECAI 2024), the prior state-of-the-art for piecewise-linear geometric robustness verification. The proposed method dominates PWL on every reported metric: it reduces total verification time by 89% on 5° rotations (7.0s vs 87.6s on MNIST; 98.7s vs 910.3s on CIFAR-10) and by 40–60% on 20° rotations, while cutting BaB iterations by 71–86% on small transforms. Polytope area (a proxy for bound tightness) improves by up to 7% on more complex pixel curves, attributable to using q=2 piecewise segments for both upper and lower bounds. At a fixed BaB budget, per-pixel parallelization still yields 53–85% speedup. Crucially, the comparison is conducted under 2D rotations because PWL only supports affine transforms; the proposed method additionally handles non-affine perspective (homography) transforms, a capability PWL lacks entirely, making the speedup and tightness gains a lower bound on the practical advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the PWL framework of Batten et al. (ECAI 2024), which verified geometric robustness via piecewise-linear approximation and Lipschitz optimization, from 2D affine transforms to 3D projective transforms (homographies). The key technical departure is deriving closed-form homographies from 6-DOF camera pose perturbations and handling the resulting non-affine, potentially discontinuous pixel-value functions through modified Lipschitz constant analysis. It also adapts the VENUS MILP verifier (Botoeva et al., AAAI 2020) to propagate piecewise-linear input bounds rather than fixed intervals. The paper positions homographies as an analytically tractable middle ground between the limited scope of affine verification and the heavy computational cost of full 3D scene simulation (e.g., Hu et al.'s point-cloud-based approaches).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first formal verification framework for projective geometry transforms, opening a path toward certifying vision systems in safety-critical domains (autonomous driving, aerospace) where camera motion is an operational reality. The systematic finding that even PGD-trained networks show near-zero certified robustness to truly projective perturbations (pitch, yaw, Δx, Δz) while retaining some robustness to affine-equivalent ones (roll, Δy) validates the premise that ℓp-norm and affine robustness are insufficient for 3D geometric certification. Key limitations include the planar-scene assumption (no parallax or occlusion), small perturbation amplitudes (robustness drops to 0% beyond ~5° yaw), and reliance on complete verifiers that scale poorly. Open questions include extending to non-planar scenes, larger perturbation ranges, and integrating homography-aware objectives into robust training.
