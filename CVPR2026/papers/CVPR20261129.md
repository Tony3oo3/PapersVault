## Problem

Existing medical MLLMs for segmentation rely on implicit <SEG> tokens decoded by external pixel decoders, which disrupt the model's natural text output space, increase catastrophic forgetting risk, and limit cross-domain generalization. Moreover, these methods perform only single-pass reasoning, lacking the iterative, interactive refinement that human annotators naturally employ when using click-based segmentation tools.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261129/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a biomedical image, a free-form text query (which may require reasoning to identify the target), and optionally a pre-existing mask for the refinement task. At each step the model emits structured text containing a reasoning block, a click action specifying target class, positive/negative attribute, and 2D coordinates normalized to [0,1]², and the resulting observation is a composite image with the updated semi-transparent mask overlaid on the original. The final output is a segmentation mask accompanied by the full reasoning trace and click trajectory.

## Method

IBISAgent reformulates biomedical segmentation as a multi-step Markov Decision Process in which a Qwen2.5-VL-7B policy model generates interleaved textual reasoning and text-based click commands, invokes MedSAM2 as a plug-and-play segmentation tool, and observes the updated mask as a composite image—requiring no architectural modifications or implicit tokens. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) cold-start SFT on 456K samples whose click trajectories are auto-generated via a rule-based click-simulation algorithm from BiomedParseData masks, with reasoning traces synthesized by Gemini-2.5-Pro and GPT-5, plus self-reflection trajectories for error correction; (2) agentic RL using GRPO over 888K VQA samples with five fine-grained rewards—format validity, final-answer IoU, region-based click placement (rewarding clicks in FN/FP regions), progressive segmentation improvement (each step must beat the previous), and trajectory length penalty—enabling the model to discover efficient action policies beyond SFT imitation.

## Results

On the in-domain BiomedParseData test set, IBISAgent achieves 85.58 IoU, 92.21 DSC, and 96.39 F1, surpassing the best medical MLLM baseline UniBiomed (50.74 IoU) by over 34 points. On the out-of-domain MeCOVQA-G+ benchmark, IBISAgent reaches 80.63 IoU and 89.27 DSC, compared to 46.54 IoU for the strongest competitor Citrus-V. On a held-out in-house dataset of 1K CT/MRI/pathology images, IBISAgent attains 72.09 IoU and 91.76 F1, maintaining a 36+ IoU advantage over the next-best method (UniBiomed at 35.62 IoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IBISAgent decisively outperforms all 13 baselines across every benchmark and metric. Against general-purpose MLLMs (LISA, LISA++, SAM4MLLM, VisionReasoner), the gap is extreme: even after fine-tuning these models on the identical training data, IBISAgent leads by 56–75 IoU points in-domain (85.58 vs. 9.49–29.11). Among medical MLLMs, IBISAgent surpasses the strongest competitor UniBiomed by 34.84 IoU in-domain (85.58 vs. 50.74) and by 34.09 IoU out-of-domain (80.63 vs. 46.54 for Citrus-V), despite UniBiomed and Citrus-V being trained on substantially larger datasets. In the tool-augmented agent comparison (Table 3), IBISAgent (80.63 IoU on MeCOVQA-G+) exceeds GPT-4o+MedSAM2 (11.75), Lingshu+MedSAM2 (39.63), and Chiron+MedSAM2 (39.58) by 41–69 IoU points, demonstrating that iterative multi-step reasoning and refinement—rather than single-pass tool calling—drives the performance gain. The paper also notes that MMedAgent, which similarly uses a segmentation tool (MedSAM), is outperformed by IBISAgent by 49.45 IoU in-domain, confirming that the advantage stems from the agentic multi-step formulation and fine-grained RL rewards rather than tool integration alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

IBISAgent departs from the dominant LISA-style paradigm (LISA, LISA++, MedPLIB, Citrus-V, UniBiomed) that relies on implicit <SEG> tokens decoded by jointly fine-tuned pixel decoders, instead treating segmentation as a multi-step Markov Decision Process where the MLLM emits text-based click actions consumed by a plug-and-play segmentation tool (MedSAM2). It also extends the tool-augmented agent line (MMedAgent, SAM-R1, Seg-Zero) by moving from single-pass grounding to iterative, self-reflective mask refinement, and repurposes the click-simulation strategy from Deep Interactive Object Selection [42] to auto-generate training trajectories from existing masks. The RL stage uses GRPO with a novel fine-grained reward decomposition (region-based click placement, progressive improvement, trajectory length) that goes beyond the outcome-only rewards in prior RL-for-segmentation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that decoupling pixel prediction from the LLM's text output space—via explicit click actions and an external segmentation tool—avoids the catastrophic forgetting and reasoning degradation associated with implicit segmentation tokens, while the multi-step MDP formulation enables genuine iterative refinement analogous to human annotators. A key open question is the inference cost: at ~29 s per case versus 3–10 s for single-pass baselines, the approach trades latency for accuracy, which may limit real-time clinical deployment. Whether the agentic RL paradigm generalizes to 3D volumetric segmentation, multi-organ tasks, or settings without a strong off-the-shelf segmentation tool (MedSAM2) remains unexplored.
