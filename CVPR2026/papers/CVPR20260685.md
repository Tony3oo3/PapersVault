## Problem

Aligning two 3D meshes according to a text prompt describing their spatial relation is essential for content creation and scene assembly, yet object-object interaction lacks the large-scale contact-rich datasets and standardized benchmarks that human-object interaction enjoys (the most extensive existing benchmark, 2BY2, covers only 18 pairwise tasks). Prior approaches either rely on purely geometric alignment without semantic guidance or require training a new diffusion model over spatial parameters, leaving a gap for a zero-shot method that leverages pretrained vision-language models directly at test time.

## Input / Output

The method takes two input meshes (designated source and target, roles interchangeable) and a short text prompt (e.g., 'A sundae with a cherry on top'). The target mesh is first canonicalized via Auto-Align to a common upright frame, and the source mesh is remeshed if vertex density is low. The output is a single relative pose—translation, unit-quaternion rotation, and isotropic scale—applied to the source mesh, selected as the best of N=5 random restarts scored by the total objective after 2,000 optimization steps across 3 phases with 8 rendered views per step.

## Method

The core contribution is a test-time optimization loop that backpropagates CLIP text-image similarity through a differentiable renderer (Nvdiffrast) to update the source mesh's pose parameters, augmented with two geometry-aware losses: a novel fractional soft-ICP term that applies probabilistic vertex correspondences only to the closest r-fraction of source vertices (controlling contact extent), and a signed-depth penetration penalty adapted from ContactOpt. A phased schedule over P=3 phases progressively increases the ICP and penetration weights while interpolating camera look-at targets from the scene centroid toward the source mesh and reducing camera distance, balancing early exploration with late contact refinement. An LLM queried at test time with the prompt and object names sets three hyperparameters—penetration policy (binary), initial scale ratio, and attachment ratio—providing scene priors without any learned model. Per-step zero-mean noise on pose parameters and best-of-N restart selection mitigate sensitivity to initialization.

## Results

On the 50-pair OOA benchmark, the proposed method achieves the highest semantic scores across all three vision-language encoders: CLIP 0.3224, ALIGN 14.98, and SigLIP 0.0380, while maintaining a competitive intersection volume of 0.0112. On the GPT-4V-based VLM evaluator, it ranks first on all four criteria, with an Overall score of 1034.44 versus 1005.92 (B1), 1019.69 (B2), 963.80 (SceneTeller), and 991.19 (SMC). The scale-enabled variant (Ours+scale) further improves ALIGN to 15.035 and Text-Geometry to 1029.71, though it is reported separately since baselines operate in a rigid setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all four quantitative baselines on every semantic metric (CLIP, ALIGN, SigLIP) and all four VLM evaluator criteria. Against B1 (single-start shrinkwrap), it gains +0.0137 CLIP and +38.52 Overall VLM score, while B1 has a slightly lower intersection volume (0.0090 vs. 0.0112), reflecting its purely geometric projection. B2 (multi-start shrinkwrap with CLIP selection) narrows the semantic gap (CLIP 0.3157 vs. 0.3224) but still trails by ~2% on CLIP and ~15 points on Overall VLM, and shows higher intersection (0.0118). SceneTeller achieves the lowest intersection volume (0.0051) but at the cost of the weakest semantic alignment (CLIP 0.3040, ALIGN 13.25) and the lowest Overall VLM score (963.80), indicating that minimizing penetration alone does not capture the intended interaction. SMC performs worst on intersection volume (0.0244, more than 2× the proposed method) and trails on all semantic and VLM metrics. OOR-diffusion is compared only qualitatively (no public code); on the shared examples both methods produce text-aligned, physically plausible results.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of classical geometric alignment (ICP and its soft/probabilistic variants) and CLIP-guided test-time optimization via differentiable rendering. It departs from pure-geometric methods (shrinkwrap, ICP-based composition like SnapPaste) by injecting semantic language supervision, and from language-driven mesh editing methods (Text2Mesh, TextDeformer) by optimizing pose and scale rather than modifying geometry. The fractional soft-ICP term is a direct variant of Coherent Point Drift restricted to a subset of vertices, and the penetration loss adapts the signed-depth formulation from ContactOpt. Unlike OOR-diffusion, which trains a new score-based diffusion model, this work is entirely zero-shot, relying on pretrained CLIP and an LLM for hyperparameter priors at test time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot, training-free formulation is practically important for a task that lacks large-scale 3D alignment datasets (only 18 pairwise tasks in 2BY2), and the 50-pair benchmark provides a first standardized evaluation protocol for text-guided OOA. Key limitations remain: viewpoint-sensitive spatial predicates (left/right/beside) are unstable under finite-view supervision, extreme scale disparities weaken CLIP gradients, and residual interpenetration persists despite the penetration loss. Open questions include whether stronger multi-view-consistent VLMs or physics-based simulators can close these gaps, and whether the phased scheduling and camera zoom strategy generalize beyond two-object scenes to full multi-object assembly.
