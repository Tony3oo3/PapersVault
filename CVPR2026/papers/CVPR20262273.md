## Problem

Current video generation benchmarks (VBench, EvalCrafter, Morpheus) evaluate perceptual fidelity, motion smoothness, and physical plausibility but do not assess whether models generate socially coherent behavior—capturing intentions, beliefs, emotions, and norms that structure real human interactions. This paper introduces the first benchmark specifically targeting social reasoning in text-to-video generation, grounded in developmental and social psychology.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262273/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes text prompts describing concrete social scenarios (designed for 5–10 second clips, with only observable actions and no mental-state language) as input to video generation models, and the resulting video clips are fed to a VLM judge (Gemini 2.5 Pro). The judge outputs five binary scores (Core Paradigm Replication, Prompt Faithfulness, Social Coherence, Social Cue Effectiveness, Video Plausibility), an overall score computed as their mean, and a natural-language justification. The 135 evaluation prompts span 15 tasks × 3 difficulty levels × 3 prompts per level, with difficulty controlled by manipulating the presence of gaze, gesture, and contextual cues.

## Method

The core contribution is a fully training-free, four-agent pipeline that transforms 30 classic psychological experiments (15 short-video-feasible) into validated, difficulty-controlled video prompts and automatically evaluates generated outputs. The Experiment Understanding Agent distills each paradigm into a structured specification (description, key concepts, test point, ground truth); the Prompt Synthesis Agent instantiates these into concrete, action-oriented scenarios; and the Critic Agent enforces conceptual neutrality, detects ground-truth leakage, and generates easy/medium/hard variants by adjusting social cue visibility. For evaluation, the Evaluation Agent (EVA) uses a high-capacity VLM to score each video along five binary dimensions rather than continuous scales, reducing calibration noise and enabling disentanglement of generation failures from reasoning failures. The entire pipeline requires no human annotation at scale, and human validation confirms strong alignment between the VLM judge and human annotators on relative difficulty ordering.

## Results

Across 135 evaluation prompts (15 tasks × 3 difficulty levels × 3 prompts), Sora2pro achieves the highest overall social reasoning pass rate at 79.6%, followed by Veo3.1 at 72.4%. A substantial gap separates proprietary from open-source systems: the best open-source model (Longcat-Video) scores only 39.2%, while HunyuanVideo and LTX-1.0 trail at 30.8% and 27.6% respectively. Performance is most consistent across models on Emotion & Prosocial Behavior tasks (e.g., Empathic Concern reaches 100% for both Sora2pro and Veo3.1), while Social Norms & Spacing and Multi-Agent Social Strategy remain the weakest dimensions for all systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sora2pro leads all eight models by a wide margin (79.6% overall), outperforming the second-best Veo3.1 (72.4%) by 7.2 points, with particularly large advantages on Turn Taking (94.3 vs. 85.7), Costly Helping (95.0 vs. 75.0), and Helping Based on Visual Perspective (84.4 vs. 53.3). Veo3.1 in turn surpasses the next-tier proprietary models Hailuo02-S (56.4%) and Kling2.5-turbo (52.2%) by roughly 20 points, with the gap most pronounced on Goal-Directed Action tasks (e.g., Tool Selection: 85.0 vs. 55.0/45.0). All four proprietary models substantially outperform the four open-source systems, with the best open-source model Longcat-Video (39.2%) trailing Sora2pro by 40.4 points; HunyuanVideo (30.8%) and LTX-1.0 (27.6%) are weakest on Social Norms and Multi-Agent Strategy, scoring below 35% on most sub-tasks in those categories. Notably, Wan2.2 (48.3%) sits between the proprietary and open-source groups, suggesting partial social reasoning capability, yet it still lags Sora2pro by over 31 points overall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established lines of work: video generation evaluation (VBench, EvalCrafter, Morpheus, PhyCoBench) and social reasoning assessment (Social-IQ, R³-VQA, LLM Theory-of-Mind benchmarks). Prior video benchmarks focus on perceptual fidelity, physical plausibility, or action-level consistency, while prior social reasoning benchmarks evaluate whether models can *interpret* social cues in existing human-created videos. SVBench is the first to evaluate whether a generative video model can *synthesize* socially coherent multi-agent interactions from a text prompt, repurposing developmental psychology paradigms (30 classic experiments across 7 dimensions) and a training-free LLM/VLM agent pipeline for both prompt construction and automated evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial and previously unquantified gap between perceptual realism and social coherence in video generation: even the strongest proprietary models (Sora2pro at 79.6%, Veo3.1 at 72.4%) systematically fail on belief-based inference, subtle cue integration, and multi-agent coordination, while open-source models lag far behind (HunyuanVideo at 30.8%). The reversed difficulty trend for top models—performing better on harder, cue-sparse variants—suggests these systems possess strong intrinsic social priors that redundant cues can actually disrupt, a finding with implications for prompt engineering. Open questions include whether the VLM-judge approach (Gemini 2.5 Pro) generalizes beyond its own model family, whether the 15 short-form tasks adequately capture the full social reasoning landscape, and whether the gap will narrow as video generators extend beyond 5–10 second clips to the 15 long-horizon paradigms deferred to the appendix.
