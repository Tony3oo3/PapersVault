## Problem

Multimodal LLMs frequently produce contradictory answers for the same content when it is presented as an image versus as text, a failure mode that is especially critical in deployed settings like web navigation and document understanding. Existing label-free self-improvement methods rely on majority voting to generate pseudo-labels, but this approach is unstable in multimodal settings where cross-modal disagreement is the norm, and it amplifies systematic biases in 'majority-is-wrong' cases. The paper reframes this cross-modal inconsistency from a failure to be masked into a dense, label-free reward signal for reinforcement learning.

## Input / Output

Each training sample requires semantically aligned visual and textual views of the same content (e.g., a webpage screenshot paired with its HTML source); for image-only datasets, the textual view is synthesized by prompting the MLLM to generate a description. The model consumes both modalities plus a text query and produces a text answer, while the cycle-consistency mechanism additionally generates a binary reward (1 if the reconstructed answer matches the original candidate, 0 otherwise) that drives GRPO updates. The backward-inference step is the unusual component: the model is prompted to produce a query from a given answer conditioned on one modality, which is then fed forward through the alternate modality.

## Method

R-C² replaces majority-vote pseudo-labels with a cross-modal cycle consistency reward. Given a candidate answer, the model performs backward inference to generate a query conditioned on one modality (e.g., text view), then performs forward inference in the alternate modality (e.g., image view) to reconstruct the answer; a binary reward is assigned if the reconstruction matches the original. All four cycle paths (T→T, T→I, I→T, I→I) are evaluated, where same-modality cycles enforce intra-modal stability and cross-modality cycles force semantic alignment across the modality gap. Training uses the GRPO objective with this label-free reward, and an offline strategy pre-generates the full synthetic cycle dataset before RL begins for training efficiency. The candidate answer can be self-generated by the model or drawn from training-set references, and ablations show that cross-modal disagreement samples serve as effective hard examples that accelerate alignment.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, R-C² improves average text accuracy from 64.7% to 69.5% (+4.8) and vision accuracy from 75.1% to 77.9% (+2.8) across six benchmarks, with the largest gains on ScienceQA (+7.8/+7.3) and MathVista (+6.0/+2.8). On the larger Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, average improvements are +2.4 (text) and +1.1 (vision), with MathVista showing the strongest gain (+4.3/+3.1). Cross-modal consistency ratio also improves substantially, reaching +10.0 points on ScienceQA and +6.1 on ChartQA for the 3B model. The method requires no human-annotated query-answer pairs, using only a binary cycle-consistency reward under GRPO training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares R-C² against two majority-voting baselines derived from R0 [16]: text-only voting (Voting Text) and pooled multimodal voting (Voting Image+Text). R-C² outperforms both baselines on nearly every benchmark and both model scales. On Qwen2.5-VL-3B, R-C² achieves 69.5% average text accuracy versus 67.3% (Voting Text) and 67.9% (Voting Image+Text), and 77.9% vision accuracy versus 75.4% and 76.1% respectively. The gap is most pronounced on ScienceQA, where R-C² reaches 76.7/83.3 (text/vision) compared to 73.1/78.0 for multimodal voting — a 3.6-point text advantage. On the 8B model, R-C² still leads by +0.7 to +2.4 points over multimodal voting on average, with the largest margin on MathVista (+1.5 text, +0.4 vision). The paper attributes the voting baselines' weakness to cross-modal disagreement: when image and text predictions conflict, majority vote collapses to the dominant modality, leaving the underlying conflict unresolved and injecting noise into the training signal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (Shao et al., 2024) and the label-free self-rewarding paradigm of R0 (Huang et al., 2025), which uses majority voting over multiple rollouts as a pseudo-label reward. The key departure is replacing this consensus-based reward with a cross-modal cycle consistency signal: rather than aggregating answers from different modalities and voting, the model performs backward inference (answer → query), switches modalities, and forward-infers to reconstruct the answer, using the match as a binary reward. This reframes the modality gap—typically treated as a failure mode to be masked by voting—as a structured, dense supervision signal that forces the model to resolve its own internal conflicts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and underexplored problem: multimodal models that contradict themselves across visual and textual views of the same content, which is critical for deployed systems in document understanding, web navigation, and agentic AI. The label-free cycle-consistency reward is attractive because verifiable rewards are scarce in open-ended multimodal reasoning, and the approach requires no human annotation or curated QA pairs. Limitations include evaluation on only Qwen-family models, a binary (coarse) reward signal, and an offline training strategy whose scalability relative to online co-evolution remains untested. Open questions include whether the cycle-consistency principle generalizes beyond two modalities (e.g., audio, video) and whether the gains persist as base models improve further.
