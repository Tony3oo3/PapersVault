## Problem

Existing multi-view 3D hand reconstruction methods (e.g., POEM, MLPHand) aggregate multi-view features via implicit attention or naive pooling, treating all views equally regardless of per-joint visibility. This indiscriminate fusion is problematic because each hand joint exhibits highly variable reliability across views—a joint occluded in one view may be clearly visible in another—and blindly mixing noisy observations degrades reconstruction quality, especially under severe occlusion.

## Input / Output

The method takes N synchronized multi-view RGB images (resized and center-cropped to 256×256) along with calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics as input. It outputs 3D hand joint coordinates (21×3) and MANO mesh vertices (778×3). Internally, each view independently predicts 2D joint locations as Gaussian distributions (mean μ and diagonal covariance Σ), and the scalar variance σ² per joint per view serves as the gating signal throughout the pipeline. A shared ResNet-34 backbone extracts multi-scale features from all views.

## Method

JUMP-Hand reformulates multi-view hand reconstruction as a Mixture-of-Experts problem where each camera view is a specialized expert, and joint-wise Gaussian uncertainty (trained via NLL loss) acts as an explicit, physically interpretable gating signal. The pipeline is coarse-to-fine: first, Uncertainty-Guided DLT (UG-DLT) converts predicted variances into normalized weights α via softmax of negative variance, then applies Hadamard-weighted DLT triangulation to produce a robust 3D initialization that suppresses unreliable views per joint. Second, an iterative Transformer decoder refines the mesh using Uncertainty-Gated Cross-Attention (UGCA), where multi-scale deformable attention samples features around each projected joint in every view, and the same α weights route expert contributions (y_j = Σ α_{j,n} · y_{j,n}). Vertex-level uncertainty is propagated from joints through the MANO skinning weight matrix, unifying gating across the full mesh. Training combines NLL loss for the probabilistic 2D head with L1 losses on 3D joints/vertices, 2D reprojection, and intermediate DLT output.

## Results

JUMP-Hand achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three multi-view benchmarks. On the most challenging HO3D-MV, it reaches 13.39 mm MPVPE and 13.10 mm MPJPE, improving over POEM (17.20/17.28 mm) by 22.2% and over MLPHand (18.69/18.70 mm) by 28.4%. On DexYCB-MV and OakInk-MV, it achieves 5.45 mm and 5.94 mm MPVPE respectively, outperforming POEM by 11.1% and 4.2%. AUC scores of 0.72–0.73 consistently surpass all baselines, and on a challenging HO3D subset (top-10% 2D error frames), JUMP-Hand achieves a 29.9% MPVPE reduction over POEM (24.91 vs. 35.52 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JUMP-Hand outperforms all three baselines (MVP, POEM, MLPHand) on every reported metric across all three datasets, with no losses or ties. POEM is the strongest baseline, achieving 17.20 mm MPVPE on HO3D-MV, 6.13 mm on DexYCB-MV, and 6.20 mm on OakInk-MV; JUMP-Hand reduces these to 13.39, 5.45, and 5.94 mm respectively. MLPHand is marginally worse than POEM on HO3D-MV (18.69 vs. 17.20 mm MPVPE) and OakInk-MV (6.17 vs. 6.20 mm) but does not report AUC scores. MVP is the weakest baseline, trailing POEM by 3.76–3.57 mm MPVPE depending on dataset. The largest relative gains appear on HO3D-MV, where severe hand-object occlusions make view reliability highly joint-dependent—precisely the scenario JUMP-Hand's uncertainty gating is designed to exploit. On a deliberately degraded subset (top-10% 2D error frames from POEM), the advantage widens further: JUMP-Hand achieves 24.91 mm MPVPE vs. POEM's 35.52 mm (29.9% improvement) on HO3D, confirming that the uncertainty-based gating provides substantially greater robustness under challenging viewing conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Mixture-of-Experts paradigm from NLP/transformers to multi-view 3D hand reconstruction, treating each camera view as an independent expert rather than using separate FFN sub-networks. It departs from prior multi-view fusion methods (POEM, MLPHand, MVP) that rely on implicit attention or naive pooling by introducing joint-wise Gaussian uncertainty as an explicit, physically interpretable gating signal for both coarse triangulation (UG-DLT) and iterative refinement (UGCA). The uncertainty modeling itself draws on standard NLL-based probabilistic regression (Kendall & Gal), but the key conceptual shift is using learned variance as an active routing mechanism rather than a passive post-hoc confidence metric, as in prior works like UPose3D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that explicit, per-joint uncertainty gating substantially outperforms both implicit attention and hard top-k selection, with the largest gains (29.9% MPVPE reduction) on the most occluded scenarios—precisely where prior methods fail. The ablation revealing that a robust coarse initialization is far more critical than subsequent refinement (removing UG-DLT costs +8.45mm vs. +3.51mm for removing refinement) offers a useful design principle for multi-view pipelines. A key open question is whether the uni-modal Gaussian assumption suffices for severe multi-modal ambiguities (e.g., a joint equally likely at two positions), which the authors acknowledge as a limitation. The uncertainty-as-gating principle could generalize to other multi-view 3D reconstruction tasks beyond hands.
