## Problem

Existing general image fusion methods rely on shared backbones and task-agnostic modules but suffer from two fundamental limitations: the absence of a principled, modality-consistent feature extraction mechanism that robustly encodes heterogeneous signals, and progressive degradation of source-specific information during deep feature propagation. This leads to inconsistent modality representation and sub-optimal fusion quality when a single model must generalize across infrared-visible, medical, multi-exposure, and multi-focus tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261656/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts image pairs from four fusion tasks (infrared+visible, PET/MRI, multi-exposure sequences, multi-focus pairs), each randomly cropped to 128×128 patches and normalized to [0,1] during training. Beyond the single fused image output, the architecture produces auxiliary reconstructions of each source image from its respective adapter branch, which serve as self-supervised training signals. The same trained model handles all four tasks without task-specific fine-tuning, with the fusion network consisting of four cross-attention blocks and each reconstruction branch containing four transformer blocks.

## Method

UniFusion employs two frozen DINOv3 encoders as a universal semantic backbone, extracting intermediate features from layers l2, l5, l8, and l11 to form a multi-scale semantic hierarchy. Lightweight hierarchical adapters perform progressive feature recalibration across these scales, translating DINOv3's natural-image priors into modality-aligned embeddings while reducing distributional discrepancies. Fused features are aggregated via cross-attention modules, while paired reconstruction branches decode each adapter's output back to the original input, enforcing information retention through L1 self-reconstruction loss. The training is formulated as a bilevel optimization problem: an inner loop rapidly updates reconstruction-oriented parameters (φ) to capture modality-specific semantics, while an outer loop gradually adjusts fusion parameters (θ) based on the evolving feature space, with EMA regularization on the outer parameters for temporal stability. This decoupling prevents the fusion objective from overfitting to shared content at the expense of modality-specific cues.

## Results

On the M3FD infrared-visible fusion benchmark, UniFusion achieves MI=4.268, VIF=0.899, Q_abf=0.637, and Q_y=0.982, surpassing the strongest baseline TC-MoA (MI=3.466, VIF=0.870) by a substantial margin on MI and VIF. On the TNO dataset, UniFusion leads all four metrics (MI=3.599, VIF=0.805, Q_abf=0.565, Q_y=0.980), and on the MEFB multi-exposure benchmark it attains MI=6.861, VIF=1.484, CC=0.906, and PSNR=59.219, outperforming CDDFuse (MI=6.575, VIF=1.430) and TC-MoA (PSNR=59.152). The method also ranks among the top two on all five metrics for the MFIF multi-focus dataset without task-specific fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniFusion outperforms all ten baselines on MI across every dataset, with the largest gap on M3FD (4.268 vs. 3.776 for CDDFuse, the next best) and TNO (3.599 vs. 3.325 for SwinFusion). On M3FD, UniFusion edges out TC-MoA on VIF (0.899 vs. 0.870) and Q_abf (0.637 vs. 0.636) but trails by a negligible 0.001 on Q_y (0.982 vs. 0.983), making it effectively a tie on that single metric. On MEFB, UniFusion leads CDDFuse by 0.286 in MI and 0.054 in VIF, and surpasses TC-MoA in PSNR (59.219 vs. 59.152) and CC (0.906 vs. 0.885), while TC-MoA's MI (4.889) is notably lower, suggesting weaker information preservation in multi-exposure settings. Among task-specific methods, LRRNet and ReCoNet (both designed for IVIF) are consistently outperformed on all four M3FD/TNO metrics, confirming the advantage of the unified DINOv3-based architecture over modality-specific designs. On the MFIF multi-focus benchmark (reported in Fig. 3 rather than a table), UniFusion ranks in the top two on all five metrics without any task-specific fine-tuning, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization to an unseen fusion domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of unified image fusion work (U2Fusion, SwinFusion, TC-MoA) by replacing task-specific or randomly-initialized encoders with a frozen DINOv3 backbone plus lightweight hierarchical adapters, and by explicitly formulating the reconstruction-fusion tension as a bilevel optimization problem. Conceptually, it repurposes the adapter paradigm from TC-MoA (task-customized mixture of adapters) but applies it to bridge a pretrained semantic space to modality-specific representations rather than routing between task-specific modules. The reconstruction-alignment loss departs from prior pixel-level or appearance-based supervision by enforcing self-reconstruction of each source from its encoded features, and the bilevel framing (inner: reconstruction, outer: fusion) provides a principled decoupling that prior general fusion methods handled only implicitly through joint multi-task losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in a single model that handles IVIF, MIF, MEF, and MFF without task-specific fine-tuning, which is directly useful for multi-scenario deployment. The frozen-DINOv3-plus-adapter design is computationally attractive since the large encoder requires no gradient updates, and the bilevel formulation offers a clean inductive bias for balancing source fidelity against fusion quality. Open questions include whether the bilevel structure is genuinely necessary beyond simple alternating optimization with different learning rates (the implementation in Eq. 5 is essentially that), how the approach scales to more than two input modalities, and whether the DINOv3-specific gains would transfer to other pretrained ViTs. The paper does not ablate the choice of DINOv3 against other self-supervised encoders, leaving the source of the semantic prior advantage somewhat unexplained.
