## Problem

Two-hand reconstruction from monocular images suffers from two coupled failures: (1) heterogeneous 2D structural cues (keypoints, segmentation, depth) from vision foundation models are not jointly exploited for two-hand alignment, and directly running foundation encoders at inference is prohibitively expensive; (2) under mutual hand occlusion, 2D cues become unreliable and existing methods lack a dedicated mechanism to resolve inter-hand penetration, producing physically implausible reconstructions. No prior work addresses both 2D structural alignment and 3D interaction plausibility in a unified, occlusion-robust pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB image as input, crops hand regions via an RTMDet bounding-box detector, and outputs full 3D MANO parameters (pose, shape, and relative translation) for both hands, from which 3D meshes are rendered. A notable design choice is that the heavy Sapiens foundation-model encoders are used only during training to produce prior features; at inference the pipeline is encoder-free, relying solely on a lightweight ResNet-50-based Fusion Alignment Encoder. The two-hand diffusion refinement stage is conditionally activated only when the IoU of the two predicted hands exceeds zero, avoiding unnecessary diffusion inference for non-overlapping poses.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. In the 2D alignment stage, a lightweight Fusion Alignment Encoder (FAE) is trained with MSE distillation to absorb fused prior features (2D keypoints, segmentation, and depth) from the Sapiens foundation model, so that at inference the foundation model is entirely removed while retaining multi-prior structural guidance; the FAE output is concatenated with ResNet-50 image features and processed by a Transformer encoder feeding a MANO regressor. In the 3D spatial alignment stage, an MDM-style two-hand diffusion model is trained to map interpenetrated pose pairs (synthesized via a weak estimator or perturbed GT) to clean, collision-free configurations, with the penetrated pair serving as a conditioning input. During denoising, a collision gradient guidance step computes a hybrid distance-orientation collision set (Chamfer distance threshold combined with normal-vector cosine similarity) and applies a GMoF-based collision loss whose negative gradient is propagated back to the predicted MANO parameters at each DDIM step, iteratively pushing the hands onto the manifold of physically valid interactions.

## Results

On InterHand2.6M, the method achieves 5.36 mm MPJPE and 5.58 mm MPVPE, outperforming the previous best (4DHands at 7.49/7.72 mm) by 2.13/2.14 mm, and 21.60 mm MRRPE, surpassing InterWild (26.74 mm) by 5.14 mm. On the in-the-wild HIC dataset, it reaches 6.67 mm MPJPE and 6.93 mm MPVPE, beating 4DHands (9.32/9.93 mm) by 2.65/3.00 mm. For penetration suppression, the method reduces penetration volume from 0.76 (InterHandGen) to 0.11 and penetration distance from 0.04 to 0.01, while achieving a proximity ratio of 0.99.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest recent competitor 4DHands [11], it improves MPJPE by 2.13 mm (5.36 vs. 7.49) and MPVPE by 2.14 mm (5.58 vs. 7.72) on InterHand2.6M, and by 2.65 mm MPJPE (6.67 vs. 9.32) on HIC. InterHandGen [9], the closest diffusion-based baseline, is surpassed by 2.14 mm in MPJPE and 2.20 mm in MRRPE, and dramatically in penetration volume (0.11 vs. 0.76). InterWild [15], which the authors follow for training details, is beaten by 2.49 mm in MPJPE and 5.14 mm in MRRPE on InterHand2.6M, and by 8.95 mm in MPJPE on HIC. Earlier methods (IntagHand [10], Zuo et al. [34], ACR [28], Ren et al. [21], Zhang et al. [29], Moon et al. [16]) trail by 2.7–8.6 mm in MPJPE. Notably, the method uses fewer training datasets (5 vs. 12 for 4DHands) and achieves encoder-free inference at 18 FPS (vs. 3 FPS when using the full Sapiens foundation model), indicating a favorable accuracy–efficiency trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-hand reconstruction line (InterWild, 4DHands, ACR) by decoupling the problem into 2D structural alignment and 3D spatial interaction alignment, each handled by a dedicated module. For 2D alignment, it departs from prior work that uses a single prior type (e.g., WHAM's keypoints, TRAM's segmentation/depth) by unifying keypoints, segmentation, and depth from the Sapiens foundation model through a lightweight Fusion Alignment Encoder that distills their knowledge via MSE, eliminating foundation-model inference at test time. For 3D alignment, it moves beyond InterHandGen's diffusion-as-regularizer and Zuo et al.'s CNN-based interaction features by explicitly modeling a generative mapping from interpenetrated to collision-free poses with collision-gradient-guided DDIM denoising, building on the MDM-style diffusion process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The penetration-free diffusion with hybrid Chamfer-distance/normal-orientation collision detection and GMoF-based gradient guidance offers a reusable recipe for enforcing physical plausibility in articulated-body reconstruction beyond hands. The encoder-free deployment (18 FPS vs. 3 FPS with full foundation model) makes multi-prior guidance practically deployable, which is important for real-time AR/VR and robotics applications. The authors acknowledge that extreme motion blur degrades the 2D priors and suggest temporal processing as a natural extension, leaving open whether the diffusion refinement generalizes to more complex multi-body interactions or to video sequences where temporal consistency must be maintained.
