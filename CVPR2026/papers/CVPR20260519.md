## Problem

While Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision—especially long or visual CoT—has been widely adopted to improve vision-language model reasoning, it remains unclear which specific CoT designs actually contribute to generalizable visual reasoning versus merely accelerating training convergence. The paper asks whether and how different ways of externalizing intermediate reasoning (linguistic, spatial, visual) affect the model's ability to transfer learned reasoning to unseen task scales.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (a rendered maze grid of size 4×4 to 10×10, a FrozenLake grid, a 3×3 jigsaw layout, or a natural image for VQA) paired with a text instruction, and produces a reasoning trajectory followed by a final answer. The three CoT formats differ in how intermediate steps are structured: Language CoT emits purely textual direction tokens, Grounding CoT interleaves text with explicit spatial coordinates (points, lines, or regions), and Visual CoT additionally feeds back modified images (e.g., drawn path segments) at each step. The final answer is a path sequence, tile permutation, or VQA response, with the reasoning enclosed in think tags and the answer in a boxed format.

## Method

The paper conducts a controlled empirical comparison of three CoT formats—Language CoT, Grounding CoT (with explicit coordinates), and Visual CoT (with image manipulation)—under a unified SFT-then-RL pipeline on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, using maze navigation as a testbed where difficulty is tunable by grid size and all intermediate steps are auto-generatable. For each format, 8K reasoning trajectories are synthesized via Gemini-2.5-Pro, the model is SFT'd for 3 epochs, then further trained with GRPO-based RL (up to 1000 steps) on 20K additional maze samples. A key ablation introduces G-CoT-least, which retains only the minimal path sequence without explicit coordinate annotations, testing whether the model can internalize spatial reasoning without externally specified coordinate frames. The central finding is a 'short is long' effect: visual and longer CoT accelerate convergence but do not raise the final performance ceiling, while the minimal-grounding format generalizes best across unseen maze sizes and transfers to other vision-centric tasks (FrozenLake, Jigsaw, V* Bench, HR-Bench).

## Results

On V* Bench, G-CoT-least RL achieves 85.86% overall accuracy (87.83% Attr, 82.89% Spatial), surpassing both the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (72.25%) and V-CoT RL (83.25%). On HR-Bench 4K, G-CoT-least RL reaches 74.12% overall (90.75% FSP, 57.50% FCP) versus 72.50% for the base model. The gains are most dramatic on visual games: FrozenLake accuracy jumps from 20.00% to 90.33%, and Jigsaw from 0.00% to 75.60% with G-CoT-least RL. On the controlled maze task, G-CoT-least generalizes best to unseen maze sizes (7×7 and 10×10), while V-CoT saturates earlier and remains inferior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four CoT strategies (Language CoT, Grounding CoT, Visual CoT, and G-CoT-least) under an identical SFT-then-RL pipeline on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. G-CoT-least consistently outperforms V-CoT across all evaluated benchmarks: on V* Bench it gains +2.61 points overall (85.86 vs. 83.25), on HR-Bench 4K it gains +2.12 points (74.12 vs. 72.00), and on visual games it achieves 90.33% on FrozenLake and 75.60% on Jigsaw where V-CoT was not evaluated. On the maze task, V-CoT converges roughly twice as fast as Language CoT during RL but plateaus at a similar final accuracy, confirming that visual CoT accelerates training without raising the performance ceiling. G-CoT-least also converges faster than full Grounding CoT and generalizes best to unseen maze sizes (7×7 single-scale and 10×10 cross-scale), while V-CoT saturates after ~800 RL steps. The base Qwen2.5-VL-7B without any CoT training scores only 20% on FrozenLake and 0% on Jigsaw, highlighting the substantial gains from even minimal grounded supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a controlled empirical study that interrogates the widely adopted SFT-then-RL paradigm for VLMs, building directly on the visual CoT / 'think with image' line of work (o3, DeepEyes, Pixel Reasoner) and grounding CoT approaches (Shao et al., Traceable Evidence Enhanced Visual Grounded Reasoning). Rather than proposing a new architecture or training method, it repurposes the standard Qwen2.5-VL-7B + GRPO pipeline as a diagnostic instrument, using a synthetic maze task to isolate how the *format* of intermediate reasoning (linguistic, spatial-coordinate, or image-manipulation) affects generalization. It departs from prior work that treats longer or more visually explicit CoT as inherently beneficial, instead treating CoT format as a variable to be ablated under identical RL conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'short is long' finding challenges a core design assumption in the VLM reasoning community: that richer, more explicit, or visually interleaved CoT supervision yields better generalizable reasoning. If the result holds beyond mazes and single-model settings, it would substantially reduce the cost and complexity of constructing SFT data for visual reasoning, since minimal path-based supervision suffices. Key limitations include reliance on a single 7B model, a relatively simple controlled task as the primary testbed, and validation on only a handful of secondary tasks. Open questions include whether the effect persists for more complex multi-step visual reasoning (e.g., multi-object spatial planning), for larger or differently-architected models, and whether the RL stage is what enables the 'implicit reasoning' that makes short CoT sufficient.
