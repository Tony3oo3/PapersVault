## Problem

Existing color correction pipelines treat illuminant estimation, illuminant discounting, and color space transformation as independent modular stages, propagating errors between them. When multispectral data is incorporated, it is typically used only for illuminant estimation and then discarded, leaving valuable spectral cues unused in downstream processing. This paper addresses the gap by proposing a unified, end-to-end framework that jointly models all correction stages while exploiting multispectral information throughout the entire pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260409/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution RGB image (512×512) from a primary camera sensor and a low-resolution multispectral image (64×64, 15 narrowband channels from a Specificity S1 snapshot sensor) as dual inputs, and produces a single color-corrected image in the CIE XYZ color space under the canonical D65 illuminant. The MS input is intentionally low-resolution to emulate compact mobile spectral sensors, and the framework is designed to tolerate spatial misalignment between the two sensors, which is handled by fine-tuning only the spectral encoder branch.

## Method

The core contribution is a dual-input, end-to-end color correction framework that fuses RGB and MS features within a single model, implicitly performing illuminant estimation, discounting, and color space transformation without explicit intermediate stages. Two lightweight architectures are refactored: LPIENet (a U-Net-like topology with Inverted Residual Attention blocks, ~220K or 60K parameters) gains a spectral encoder of three IRA blocks whose features are fused via element-wise addition at skip connections, and cmKAN (a hypernetwork predicting spatially varying KAN spline parameters, ~18K parameters) gains a three-layer convolutional spectral encoder fused at two feature levels within the Illumination Estimator. Both are trained supervised with ΔE76 loss on a physically grounded dataset of 116,688 RGB-MS-CIE XYZ triplets rendered from hyperspectral reflectance under 102 illuminants and multiple camera sensitivities. The key design insight is that multispectral cues inform not just illuminant estimation but the entire correction mapping, yielding ~50% lower ΔE00 than RGB-only or MS-only baselines while remaining robust to exposure changes and spatial misalignment.

## Results

On the proposed Mobile-Spectral-CC dataset, cmKAN-light achieves the best mean ΔE₀₀ of 1.47 (mobile) and 1.60 (mirrorless), with a 95th-percentile ΔE₀₀ of 3.30 and 3.55 respectively. LPIENet follows closely at 1.66 (mobile) and 1.74 (mirrorless) mean ΔE₀₀. These results represent approximately a 50% reduction in mean ΔE₀₀ over the strongest MS-driven baseline (SpectralConvMean: 3.16 mobile, 3.17 mirrorless) and over 50% over the best RGB-only baseline (Squeezenet-FC⁴: 3.16 mobile, 3.28 mirrorless). In terms of Reproduction Error, cmKAN-light achieves 2.83 (mobile) and 2.91 (mirrorless) mean, compared to 4.35 and 4.24 for SpectralConvMean.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed framework consistently outperforms all baselines across both mirrorless and mobile camera categories. Against the strongest MS-driven baseline (SpectralConvMean, mean ΔE₀₀ = 3.17 mirrorless / 3.16 mobile), cmKAN-light reduces mean ΔE₀₀ by ~50% (to 1.60 / 1.47) and Reproduction Error by ~33% (to 2.91 / 2.83). Against the best RGB-only learning-based baseline (Squeezenet-FC⁴, mean ΔE₀₀ = 3.28 / 3.16), the improvement is comparable at roughly 50%. Statistical baselines (GW, WP, GE1, GE2) are substantially worse, with mean ΔE₀₀ ranging from 5.0 to 7.8, confirming that learned methods are necessary. The QU baseline performs poorly (mean ΔE₀₀ ≈ 6.4), likely due to its quasi-unsupervised training paradigm. Notably, the paper's models also maintain their advantage under spatial misalignment (Table 2), where cmKAN-light degrades only marginally (1.60→1.84 mirrorless ΔE₀₀) while MS-driven baselines remain unchanged, confirming the robustness of the unified fusion approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard modular color correction pipeline (AWB followed by CST) by unifying illuminant estimation, illuminant discounting, and color space transformation into a single end-to-end image-to-image model. It builds on two lightweight architectures—LPIENet (a U-Net-like MobileNet-based design) and cmKAN (a hypernetwork-driven Kolmogorov-Arnold Network)—by adding a spectral encoder branch that fuses low-resolution multispectral features with high-resolution RGB features. Conceptually, it extends prior MS-based color constancy work (e.g., SpectralFC4, SpectralConvMean) that uses spectral data only for illuminant estimation and then discards it, instead threading multispectral cues through every stage of the correction process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The constructed dataset of 116,688 RGB-MS-CIE XYZ triplets, rendered from real hyperspectral reflectance under 102 illuminants and multiple camera sensitivities, fills a gap: no existing real-world dataset provides the ground-truth colorimetric references needed to train and evaluate full-pipeline color correction. The architecture-agnostic formulation (validated on both a CNN U-Net and a KAN-based hypernetwork) suggests the framework can absorb future, more powerful backbones without redesign. Key open questions include whether the simulated dataset generalizes to real snapshot MS hardware with different noise characteristics, and whether the lack of explicit intermediate estimates (illuminant, reflectance) limits the method's utility in pipelines that require those diagnostics.
