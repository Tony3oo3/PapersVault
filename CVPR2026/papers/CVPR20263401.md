## Problem

Intrinsic image decomposition and inverse rendering models are predominantly trained on synthetic renderings with simplified BRDFs and limited material realism, causing them to fail on real-world photographs under uncontrolled lighting. No existing dataset provides real-world, polarization-separated reflectance measurements with full material attributes (albedo, normal, specular) across diverse objects and dense multiview/multi-illumination coverage, leaving a critical gap for physically grounded supervision.

## Input / Output

For inverse rendering, the model takes a single unpolarized RGB image (or a polarized capture at 6144×3240) and outputs either PBR components (diffuse albedo, specular albedo, surface normal) or polarization components (cross-polarized and parallel-polarized images), guided by text-prompt conditioning. For forward relighting, the model consumes intrinsic material components plus an irradiance map (computed as the integrated n·ω over all 346 light directions weighted by source color) and produces a relit RGB image. The dataset itself provides 1.2M+ images across 218 objects, 346 OLAT directions, 8 views, and 2 polarization states, with the ability to synthesize arbitrary HDRI lighting via weighted summation of OLAT captures.

## Method

The paper's primary contribution is a real-world capture pipeline using an 8-camera, 346-LED Light Stage with hexagonally tiled cross/parallel polarizers, enabling per-light, per-view diffuse-specular separation via Malus's Law (I_d = 2I_⊥, I_s = 2I_∥ − 2I_⊥). Material parameters are derived by minimizing a Lambertian residual to recover diffuse albedo and surface normal, then integrating the specular sequence to obtain specular albedo. For model evaluation, the authors fine-tune the diffusion-based RGB2X architecture using LoRA to prevent catastrophic forgetting, training two variants: a PBR workflow that predicts physically meaningful material maps and a polarization workflow that 'virtualizes' polarization by predicting cross/parallel-polarized outputs from unpolarized inputs. The forward rendering network additionally conditions on an irradiance map and is trained with three lighting augmentations (OLAT captures, HDRI-synthesized lighting, and all-white illumination) to improve generalization across illumination regimes.

## Results

Under HDRI lighting on the real Light Stage dataset, the proposed model achieves PSNR of 33.5 dB (albedo), 28.1 dB (normal), and 31.0 dB (specular), substantially outperforming Diffusion Renderer (20.0/20.5/22.6 dB) and RGB2X (18.1/18.6/17.2 dB). For forward relighting on the same dataset, the PBR workflow reaches 27.8 dB PSNR and 0.904 SSIM, compared to 17.0 dB / 0.775 for DR and 18.5 dB / 0.514 for RGB2X. On sparse-view 3D reconstruction, feeding predicted albedo images into Dust3r improves PSNR from 14.5 (raw) to 20.3 dB, and predicted diffuse images from 14.5 to 17.8 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed model dominates all full-decomposition baselines (DR and RGB2X) by a wide margin on the real Light Stage dataset: albedo PSNR improves from 20.0 dB (DR) and 18.1 dB (RGB2X) to 33.5 dB, and specular PSNR from 22.6/17.2 to 31.0 dB. For relighting, the PBR workflow lifts PSNR from 17.0 dB (DR) and 18.5 dB (RGB2X) to 27.8 dB on real objects, with SSIM jumping from 0.51–0.78 to 0.90. The polarization workflow is competitive with PBR on real data (26.1 dB PSNR) but slightly trails on synthetic Objaverse, reflecting the domain gap between polarization captures and renderer materials. For surface-normal-only baselines (Lotus-D, Dsine), the proposed model achieves 28.1 dB PSNR versus 24.7 and 25.5 dB respectively. On sparse-view 3D reconstruction, the inverse model's predicted albedo and diffuse inputs improve Dust3r's PSNR by 5.8 and 3.3 dB over raw images, while Mast3r shows smaller gains (15.6 vs 12.7 dB for albedo input), confirming that specular removal benefits geometry recovery most strongly for the more sensitive Dust3r pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the inverse rendering literature by introducing the first large-scale real-world measured dataset with polarization-based diffuse-specular separation for 3D objects, departing from the dominant reliance on synthetic datasets (Objaverse, Hypersim, CGIntrinsic) and the limited real-world alternatives (Multi-Illumination, OpenSVBRDF) that lack intrinsic ground truth or are restricted to flat samples. Methodologically, it builds directly on RGB2X as a pretrained diffusion-based decomposition backbone, fine-tuned via LoRA, and positions itself within the recent wave of diffusion-model-based inverse/forward rendering (Diffusion Renderer, RGB2X, IntrinsicDiffusion). The polarization workflow—predicting cross- and parallel-polarized components from unpolarized inputs—is a novel addition that repurposes a physical imaging cue (Malus's Law) as a learned intermediate representation, complementing the standard PBR parameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset addresses a fundamental bottleneck in inverse rendering: the synthetic-to-real gap that prevents models trained on CGI renderings from generalizing to uncontrolled lighting and real material responses. By providing 1.2M polarization-separated, multiview, OLAT-captured images with analytically derived albedo, normal, and specular ground truth, it enables direct supervised training and evaluation of physically grounded appearance models. The demonstration that a polarization-trained inverse model can produce specular-free diffuse images that improve sparse-view 3D reconstruction (Dust3r, Mast3r) opens a practical pathway for leveraging polarization cues without requiring polarized hardware at inference time. Key limitations include the restriction to static objects in a controlled Light Stage, absence of subsurface scattering parameters, and difficulty capturing highly transparent or strongly anisotropic materials, leaving open the question of scalable in-the-wild acquisition.
