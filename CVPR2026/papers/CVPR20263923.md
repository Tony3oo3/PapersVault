## Problem

In remote sensing image-text retrieval, contrastive learning relies on a single cross-modal similarity threshold to flag false negative samples, but this is fragile: cross-modal semantic overlap causes unmatched pairs to be wrongly discarded, while cross-modal semantic gaps cause matched pairs to be wrongly retained. No prior method models the joint relational structure among image-image, text-text, and image-text similarities to robustly separate true from false negatives.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263923/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes remote sensing images (224×224 on RSICD, 256×256 on RSITMD) and their associated text descriptions (5 reference sentences per image). Images are split into N_v patches and texts into N_t words, encoded by a pre-trained RemoteCLIP backbone, then refined by a 2-layer Transformer. The output is a cosine similarity score between the image [CLS] token and text [EOS] token, used to rank candidates in both image-to-text and text-to-image retrieval directions.

## Method

TriSim constructs a tri-dimensional similarity space (s^vt, s^vv, s^tt) for each image-text pair, then applies two complementary Extreme Value Theory strategies to identify false negatives as tail anomalies: (A) Mahalanobis distance filtering with a chi-squared test to capture peripheral outliers, and (B) a Generalized Pareto Distribution fit on excesses above a high quantile to capture upper-right high-similarity extremes. The intersection of both sets defines the FNS, which are then softly selected via Bernoulli sampling to modulate a probabilistic triplet loss. A second module (IGDO) computes intra-modal saliency differences between a false-negative image and its positive counterpart to generate discriminative-region masks, which supervise a lightweight MLP to produce a gain matrix that adaptively amplifies discriminative patches and suppresses ambiguous ones within the Transformer's self-attention.

## Results

On RSICD, TriSim achieves a mean recall (mR) of 37.55%, surpassing the strongest baseline GLISA (36.99%) by 0.56 percentage points, with the largest gains in text retrieval (R@1: 15.99% vs. 14.75%; R@10: 57.69% vs. 55.46%). On RSITMD, TriSim reaches mR of 51.35%, outperforming AIR (50.22%) by 1.13 points, with particularly strong text-retrieval R@10 of 78.58% (vs. AIR's 73.98%). TriSim achieves the best R@10 in text retrieval on both datasets and the best R@1 in text retrieval on RSITMD (26.95%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TriSim outperforms all 25 baselines on the mR metric for both datasets. On RSICD, it edges out GLISA (36.99% mR) by 0.56 points, primarily driven by superior text-retrieval R@1 (15.99% vs. 14.75%) and R@10 (57.69% vs. 55.46%), while GLISA retains a slight advantage in image-retrieval R@1 (19.52% vs. 18.96%). On RSITMD, TriSim surpasses AIR (50.22% mR) by 1.13 points, with the largest margin in text-retrieval R@10 (78.58% vs. 73.98%) and R@5 (61.86% vs. 57.04%), though AIR leads in image-retrieval R@1 (29.20% vs. 26.55%). Among general-purpose methods (SCAN, CAMP, MTFN, SGRAF, NAAF), TriSim leads by 20+ mR points on RSICD and 23+ points on RSITMD. Among RS-specific methods (SAM, PIR, DOVE, CFITR, etc.), TriSim exceeds the best (CFITR at 43.87% mR on RSITMD) by 7.48 points. Among CLIP-based methods, TriSim improves over the strongest prior (GLISA at 48.41% on RSITMD) by 2.94 mR points, confirming that the EVT-guided 3D similarity modeling and intra-modal saliency refinement provide consistent gains across all method families.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of false-negative mitigation in contrastive image-text retrieval, which has primarily relied on single cross-modal similarity thresholds or soft-labeling (e.g., FNE [23], MedCLIP [42]). It departs from this by constructing a tri-dimensional similarity space (image-text, image-image, text-text) and applying Extreme Value Theory (Generalized Pareto Distribution) to model the tail of the FNS distribution, a statistical tool not previously used in this retrieval context. The probabilistic triplet loss formulation is adapted from VSE++ [11], and the backbone follows the CLIP/RemoteCLIP paradigm. The intra-modal saliency-guided gain matrix for feature refinement is a new component not seen in prior RSITR work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3D similarity space offers a more principled and multi-signal criterion for FNS detection than single-threshold methods, addressing the well-documented problem of cross-modal semantic overlap and gaps in RS data. However, the practical gains are modest (1.51% and 2.25% mR over strong baselines like GLISA and AIR), and the method adds considerable complexity (EVT fitting, Mahalanobis filtering, Bernoulli sampling, saliency masks, gain matrix training) for these improvements. Open questions include whether the 3D space formulation generalizes beyond RS to other domains with high intra-class similarity, whether EVT is strictly necessary compared to simpler tail-quantile heuristics, and whether the approach scales to larger batch sizes or multi-GPU training where the per-batch statistical estimates become more stable.
