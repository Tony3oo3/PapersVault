## Problem

Mainstream zero-shot anomaly detection methods rely on vision-language models (e.g., CLIP) with text encoders to construct normal/abnormal prompts, introducing cross-modal alignment overhead, training instability, and parameter redundancy. This paper asks whether the text branch is truly indispensable, showing through a controlled ablation that removing it and learning two visual prototypes directly yields comparable or better performance with 99% fewer trainable parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image resized to 518×518 and processes it through a frozen ViT backbone (CLIP ViT-L/14@336px or DINOv2 ViT-L/14) with no text input. It produces a pixel-level anomaly map upsampled to the input resolution and an image-level anomaly score computed as the mean of the top 1% most anomalous pixels. Training uses only auxiliary industrial data (VisA), and inference is fully zero-shot on unseen industrial and medical categories with no per-category fine-tuning.

## Method

VisualAD inserts two learnable global tokens (anomaly and normal) into the token sequence of a frozen Vision Transformer, where multi-layer self-attention lets them progressively encode high-level normality/abnormality semantics while guiding patch tokens to highlight anomaly cues. A Spatial-Aware Cross-Attention (SCA) module at each selected layer uses m=4 learnable anchor queries with learnable 1D positional encoding to aggregate localized spatial evidence, then a token-guided gating mechanism selectively injects this evidence into the global tokens. In parallel, a lightweight Self-Alignment Function (SAF), a one-hidden-layer MLP per layer, nonlinearly recalibrates patch features before alignment. Anomaly scores are computed as the cosine-similarity difference between enhanced tokens and recalibrated patches, fused additively across layers {6, 12, 18, 24}. Training jointly optimizes binary cross-entropy (image-level), Focal + Dice loss (pixel-level), and a cosine-margin penalty enforcing >120° angular separation between the two tokens, while keeping the entire backbone frozen.

## Results

VisualAD achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot anomaly detection across 13 industrial and medical benchmarks. On industrial datasets with the CLIP ViT-L/14@336px backbone, it attains image-level AUROC of 92.2 (MVTec-AD), 84.7 (VisA), 94.9 (BTAD), 98.0 (KSDD2), 99.5 (DAGM), and 97.5 (DTD-Synthetic), while the DINOv2 variant delivers stronger pixel-level segmentation (e.g., 98.9 pixel AUROC on KSDD2, 96.4 on BrainAD). On medical datasets, VisualAD(DINOv2) leads on OCT17 (91.2 image AUROC) and BrainAD (87.1 image AUROC), outperforming the next-best AdaCLIP by 13.9 and 7.1 points respectively. The two backbone variants exhibit complementary strengths: CLIP favors image-level classification while DINOv2 excels at pixel-wise localization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisualAD(CLIP) outperforms all five baselines on every industrial image-level metric, with the largest margins over AnomalyCLIP on BTAD (+6.2 AUROC: 94.9 vs. 88.7) and DAGM (+1.5: 99.5 vs. 98.0), and over AdaCLIP on VisA (+5.0: 84.7 vs. 79.7). On medical image-level tasks, VisualAD(DINOv2) dominates: it leads on OCT17 by 13.9 AUROC over AdaCLIP (91.2 vs. 77.3) and on BrainAD by 7.1 over AdaCLIP (87.1 vs. 80.0), while VisualAD(CLIP) edges out AnomalyCLIP on BrainMRI (96.7 vs. 96.4). For pixel-level segmentation, VisualAD(DINOv2) achieves the best results on KSDD2 (98.9 vs. 95.0 for AnomalyCLIP), BrainAD (96.4 vs. 95.2), and Endo (86.8 vs. 86.5), whereas VisualAD(CLIP) leads on MVTec-AD (90.8 vs. 91.0 for AnomalyCLIP, a near-tie) and DTD-Synthetic (98.1 vs. 97.5). WinCLIP and CLIP-AD are consistently the weakest baselines, trailing VisualAD by 10–30 AUROC points on most datasets, confirming that the purely visual two-token design with SCA/SAF substantially surpasses both hand-crafted prompt and dual-branch text-guided approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisualAD directly departs from the dominant VLM-based zero-shot anomaly detection paradigm (WinCLIP, AnomalyCLIP, AdaCLIP, CLIP-AD) by eliminating the text encoder and cross-modal alignment entirely. Its conceptual lineage traces to AnomalyCLIP's two-prototype (normal/abnormal) formulation, but the paper's exploratory study shows that replacing learnable text prompts with two learnable visual tokens in a frozen ViT backbone yields comparable performance with 99% fewer parameters. The method then adds Spatial-Aware Cross-Attention and a Self-Alignment Function to ground these tokens in fine-grained spatial evidence, a design not present in prior prompt-based ZSAD methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a purely visual two-token mechanism can match or slightly exceed text-prompt-based ZSAD challenges the field's working assumption that cross-modal alignment is essential for open-set anomaly discrimination, potentially simplifying deployment and reducing training instability. However, the performance gains over strong baselines like AnomalyCLIP and AdaCLIP are modest (typically 1–3 points), and the method still relies on a frozen backbone trained on auxiliary industrial data, leaving open whether the two-token abstraction suffices for highly diverse or fine-grained anomaly types. Future directions noted include adaptive layer selection, richer structured tokens, and uncertainty estimation under strong domain shift.
