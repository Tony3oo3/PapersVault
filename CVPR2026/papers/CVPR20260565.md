## Problem

One-step DMD distillation suffers from limited model capacity, reducing generative diversity and degrading performance on complex tasks like dynamic video generation. Naive multi-step DMD increases computational graph depth and memory, while the stochastic gradient truncation strategy (SGTS) used in DMD2 can collapse to one-step distillation during training, erasing the diversity and motion-dynamics gains that multi-step generation should provide.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260565/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The distilled models take a text prompt (T2I/T2V) or a reference image plus text prompt (I2V) and produce a generated image (720×1280 or 1382×1382) or an 81-frame video (720×1280) in just 4 denoising steps with CFG scale 1, compared to 40 steps and CFG 4 for the base models. The output architecture is inherently a two-expert MoE: a low-SNR expert handles structural/motion modeling and a high-SNR expert refines fine details, with each expert invoked at its designated timestep during inference.

## Method

Phased DMD partitions the SNR range into subintervals and trains one expert per subinterval in a progressive, low-to-high-SNR schedule, so the backward simulation terminates at an intermediate timestep rather than x₀, eliminating the one-step degeneration of SGTS while keeping each phase to a single gradient-recorded step. Because clean samples are unavailable in all but the final phase, the paper derives a revised score-matching loss (Eq. 11/13) that yields unbiased score estimates over an arbitrary subinterval (s, 1], replacing the standard full-interval denoising objective. The generator update minimizes reverse KL at the intermediate timestep using the difference between the frozen teacher score and a phase-specific fake score estimator trained on the subinterval, while the fake estimator is re-initialized from the teacher at each phase boundary. The framework inherently produces a few-step MoE generator regardless of whether the teacher uses MoE, and can be further combined with SGTS to train a 4-step model in only 2 phases.

## Results

On text-to-video (T2V) with Wan2.2-T2V-A14B, Phased DMD achieves an optical flow score of 9.30 and dynamic degree of 82.27%, closely matching the base model (10.26, 79.55%) and substantially outperforming DMD2 (3.23, 65.45%). On image-to-video (I2V), Phased DMD reaches OF=9.84 and DD=83.64% versus DMD2's 7.87 and 80.00%. For text-to-image diversity, Phased DMD reduces DINOv3 pairwise similarity to 0.768 (Wan2.2) and 0.782 (Wan2.1) compared to DMD2's 0.828 and 0.826, while also improving LPIPS distance (0.481 vs 0.447 on Wan2.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DMD2 (the primary baseline using stochastic gradient truncation), Phased DMD wins decisively on all video generation metrics: on T2V, optical flow improves from 3.23 to 9.30 (nearly 3×) and dynamic degree from 65.45% to 82.27%, while FID drops from 55.70 to 47.24 and FVD from 763.1 to 700.9. On I2V, Phased DMD also leads on every metric (OF 9.84 vs 7.87, DD 83.64% vs 80.00%, FID 17.47 vs 18.45, FVD 334.7 vs 370.0). For image diversity, Phased DMD outperforms DMD2 on Wan2.1 (DINOv3 0.782 vs 0.826; LPIPS 0.544 vs 0.521) and Wan2.2 (DINOv3 0.768 vs 0.828; LPIPS 0.481 vs 0.447), though on Qwen-Image the DINOv3 gap is marginal (0.958 vs 0.941) while LPIPS still favors Phased DMD (0.322 vs 0.309). Against vanilla few-step DMD (no gradient truncation), Phased DMD achieves better diversity on Wan2.1 (DINOv3 0.782 vs 0.825; LPIPS 0.544 vs 0.522) while avoiding the computational graph depth and memory overhead that make vanilla DMD impractical for larger models. The paper notes that the Qwen-Image diversity gap is small because the base model itself has limited output diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) framework [45, 46] to few-step generation, building directly on DMD2's stochastic gradient truncation strategy (SGTS) and Self-Forcing [12]. It departs from prior few-step distillation work (DMD2, TDM [26]) by identifying that SGTS causes one-step degeneration—collapsing generative diversity to single-step levels—and replaces it with a phased, MoE-inspired approach where each expert is distilled over a specific SNR subinterval. The progressive structure is conceptually analogous to ProGAN [15], and the subinterval score-matching objective is a novel theoretical contribution that corrects the inapplicable full-interval loss when clean samples are unavailable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Phased DMD addresses a practical bottleneck in deploying large diffusion models: few-step distillation was losing the very diversity and motion dynamics that motivated multi-step generation in the first place. By validating on 20B–28B parameter models across T2I, T2V, and I2V tasks while remaining data-free, it sets a new practical ceiling for distillation scale. Open questions include whether the phased approach generalizes to other distillation objectives (e.g., Fisher divergence in SiD [49]) and whether incorporating pre-generated trajectory data could further close the gap to base-model quality without sacrificing the data-free paradigm.
