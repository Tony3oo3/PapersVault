## Problem

Existing multi-task representation compression methods (e.g., TAMC, SSSIC, OmniICM) rely on static, globally learned task dependency structures that remain fixed at inference time. This causes redundant bit allocation across correlated tasks and suboptimal rate-distortion performance, because the optimal compression order varies with image content. The paper asks whether task dependencies can be modeled in a content-adaptive manner to achieve predictive redundancy removal and asymmetric information sharing across tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260969/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (3×H×W) and produces compressed representations for six downstream tasks (semantic segmentation, keypoint 2D, edge texture, surface normal, depth z-buffer, and human-vision image compression) at H/16×W/16 spatial resolution. A key structural output is a per-image directed acyclic graph (DAG) that determines the adaptive encoding order; each task's representation is encoded conditionally on its decoded predecessors in the DAG. Bitrate is measured in bits per pixel, and the system supports scalable decoding where tasks can be reconstructed sequentially as more bits become available.

## Method

ATDC introduces a three-stage pipeline: (1) a lightweight proxy head estimates pairwise task predictability by minimizing conditional Gaussian negative log-likelihood, yielding a per-image task correlation matrix C(x); (2) a greedy DAG construction algorithm (Algorithm 1) selects edges via a conditional gain criterion with a threshold τ and max in-degree K_max=2, ensuring acyclicity and producing a content-specific topological encoding order; (3) a predictive representation codec encodes each task in DAG order—source tasks (no parents) use a standard factorized autoencoder with a factorized entropy model, while dependent tasks use a predictor (same architecture as the proxy head) to estimate conditional Gaussian parameters from decoded parent features, then compress the residual with a hyperprior-based entropy model. Training uses a two-stage schedule (5-epoch L1 warm-up followed by 20 epochs of full rate-distortion optimization) with an Xception backbone initialized from pretrained multi-task weights. The key distinction from prior work is that the compression order is inferred per-image from learned content-dependent correlations rather than fixed at training time, enabling the codec to adapt its information flow to the specific semantic structure of each scene.

## Results

ATDC achieves state-of-the-art rate-performance on five downstream tasks (semantic segmentation, surface normal, edge texture, depth Z-buffer, keypoint 2D) on the Taskonomy Tiny split, consistently surpassing all baselines across the full bitrate range with the largest gains at extremely low bitrates. In the scalable multi-task coding analysis, ATDC's cumulative utility curve overtakes TAMC under all bitrate budgets as more tasks are decoded. For pixel-level reconstruction, ATDC achieves comparable or marginally better PSNR and FID than TAMC while showing slightly lower LPIPS and MS-SSIM. The adaptive DAG introduces only 8.9% encoding latency overhead (44.8 ms vs. 41.3 ms) over a fixed-order baseline on an NVIDIA H20 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against traditional codecs (WebP, VTM-Intra) and learned image compression methods (ELIC, MLIC++, VQGAN), ATDC consistently achieves higher task accuracy at every tested bitrate, with the gap widening sharply at very low bitrates where pixel-domain codecs suffer severe degradation. The most direct comparison is with TAMC [16], the state-of-the-art multi-task representation compression method: ATDC yields notably higher accuracy on edge texture and surface normal, demonstrating that the adaptive DAG better captures geometric and low-level structures that TAMC's fixed causal ordering fails to preserve. In the scalable coding analysis (Fig. 3f), TAMC has an initial advantage because its first three tasks come from a shared representation, but ATDC's per-task adaptive decoding produces faster cumulative utility growth and ultimately exceeds TAMC under all bitrate budgets. On pixel-level reconstruction, ATDC matches or slightly exceeds TAMC on PSNR and FID but is marginally worse on LPIPS and MS-SSIM, indicating a small trade-off in perceptual fidelity. The ablation (Fig. 5) confirms that the adaptive ordering outperforms both a fixed topological order and random shuffling, validating that the content-dependent DAG is the source of the gains rather than the compression module itself.

## Relation to Prior Work

ATDC extends the multi-task representation compression line initiated by SSSIC, OmniICM, and most directly TAMC, which all impose a fixed or globally learned task dependency structure at inference time. The key departure is replacing this static ordering with a per-image adaptive DAG constructed from a learned pairwise predictability score, drawing on the information-theoretic framing of conditional entropy from learned image compression (ELIC, MLIC++) and the task-correlation insights from multi-task learning (Standley et al.). The codec components themselves (factorized and hyperprior entropy models, Xception backbone) are inherited from standard learned compression and multi-task training protocols, so the novelty is concentrated in the dependency-discovery and conditional-encoding pipeline rather than in the underlying codec architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that content-adaptive task ordering yields measurable rate-distortion gains over the best fixed-order baseline (TAMC) and produces interpretable, scene-dependent DAGs that reveal a consistent global hierarchy (n→s→d,k,t,a) with local variations. However, the evaluation is confined to six tasks on the Taskonomy Tiny split with a modest Xception backbone, and the codec modules are explicitly left as replaceable placeholders; whether the adaptive-ordering advantage persists with stronger entropy models, more tasks, or larger-scale datasets remains open. The +8.9% encoding overhead is small but non-trivial for real-time deployment, and the framework's scalability beyond the demonstrated incremental task addition (N=4→5→6) is not stress-tested.
