## Problem

Joint-embedding self-supervised methods (e.g., iBOT, DINO) designed for still images lack explicit modelling of temporal transformations, while predictive video methods (e.g., MAE variants) focus on local masked patches and miss the non-local, non-linear dynamics that govern frame-to-frame evolution. The paper asks how to adapt a strong image joint-embedding scheme to video by introducing a predictive component that captures full-scene temporal consistency without the ill-posedness of unconstrained future prediction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips from Kinetics-400, sampling a start frame and an end frame separated by a fixed 16-frame gap, plus two equidistant in-between frames; all frames are 224×224 RGB with multi-crop augmentation (10 crops at 96×96) applied to the endpoints. During pretraining the model produces reconstructed in-between frames (224×224 RGB) via lightweight decoders, and the final output is a pretrained ViT-S/8 or ViT-S/16 backbone whose frozen features are evaluated via linear probing on dense prediction and classification tasks.

## Method

TimeBridge extends iBOT by splitting each [CLS] token along the feature dimension: one portion feeds the standard iBOT self-distillation loss, while the other (reconstruction tokens) is cross-concatenated—student start with teacher end for decoder 1, teacher start with student end for decoder 2—and passed through deliberately simple convolutional decoders (no attention) that reconstruct the in-between frames with L2 loss. This cross-concatenation design makes each decoder's input individually incomplete, forcing the encoder to learn richer, more informative representations than serial concatenation would. The total pretraining loss is L_iBOT (masked image modeling + CLS distillation) + λ·L_Recon, with λ=1. By reconstructing frames *between* two observed endpoints rather than extrapolating into the future, the approach restricts the solution space of the otherwise ill-posed prediction problem while still capturing non-linear temporal evolution, and the weak convolutional decoder ensures the encoder bears the representational burden.

## Results

On DAVIS 2017 video object segmentation with ViT-S/8 pretrained on Kinetics-400 for 400 epochs, TimeBridge achieves 73.5 J&Fm, 70.6 Jm, and 76.5 Fm, setting new state-of-the-art. On VIP semantic part propagation, it reaches 47.5 mIoU, and on JHMDB pose propagation, 59.2 PCK@0.1 and 82.6 PCK@0.2. With the smaller ViT-S/16 backbone (400 epochs), the method scores 66.2 J&Fm on DAVIS and 39.4 mIoU on VIP, outperforming all prior video-pretrained methods at the same training budget. On video action classification (1-shot linear probing), ViT-S/8 achieves 81.5% on UCF101, 49.2% on Kinetics-400, and 45.6% on HMDB51.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest video-pretrained competitor T-CoRe (ViT-S/16, 400 epochs), TimeBridge wins on all DAVIS metrics (66.2 vs 64.7 J&Fm, +2.3%) and VIP mIoU (39.4 vs 37.8, +4.2%), but trails on JHMDB PCK@0.1 (45.8 vs 47.0) and PCK@0.2 (74.0 vs 75.2). Compared to SiamMAE at the same 400-epoch budget (ViT-S/16), TimeBridge improves J&Fm by 14.3 points (66.2 vs 57.9) and VIP mIoU by 6.2 points (39.4 vs 33.2), while SiamMAE retains a small edge on JHMDB PCK@0.1 (46.1 vs 45.8). With ViT-S/8, TimeBridge surpasses SiamMAE (2000 epochs) on DAVIS (73.5 vs 71.4 J&Fm) and VIP (47.5 vs 45.9 mIoU) but falls short on JHMDB (59.2 vs 61.9 PCK@0.1, 82.6 vs 83.8 PCK@0.2). Against image-pretrained DINO (ViT-S/8), TimeBridge leads on every dense metric, with the largest gap on VIP mIoU (+8.0 points). On classification, TimeBridge (ViT-S/8) outperforms DINO by 1.1% on UCF101, 4.0% on Kinetics-400, and 2.7% on HMDB51, while T-CoRe edges ahead on UCF101 (77.1% vs 76.2%) but trails on the other two datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

TimeBridge extends the iBOT joint-embedding framework (designed for still images) to video by introducing an auxiliary in-between frame reconstruction task, building on the authors' prior work on equivariant representation learning via whole-image reconstruction. It deliberately departs from the dominant masked autoencoder lineage (VideoMAE, SiamMAE, CropMAE) by reconstructing complete frames rather than masked patches, arguing that whole-frame reconstruction better captures non-local temporal dynamics. The cross-concatenation of split class tokens from start and end frames, combined with a lightweight convolutional decoder, distinguishes it from both future-prediction methods (which face unconstrained extrapolation) and T-CoRe (which reconstructs in feature space rather than pixel space).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves state-of-the-art dense video prediction results (73.5 J&F on DAVIS, 47.5 mIoU on VIP) with only 400 pretraining epochs—5% of SiamMAE's budget—suggesting that constrained interpolation between observed endpoints is a more efficient learning signal than unconstrained future prediction. The finding that a simple convolutional decoder substantially outperforms attention-based decoders (72.4 vs. 63.6–64.4 J&F) challenges the common assumption that attention is necessary for effective reconstruction in SSL, potentially influencing decoder design choices more broadly. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger backbones (ViT-B/L), longer temporal spans, and other joint-embedding bases beyond iBOT (e.g., DINOv2 with its additional regularization constraints), and whether the narrow λ sensitivity (catastrophic drops at 0.1 and 5) reflects a fundamental trade-off or a tuning artifact.
