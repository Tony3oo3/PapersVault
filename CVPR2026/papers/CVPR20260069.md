## Problem

Instruction-based video editing lags far behind its image counterpart due to a profound scarcity of large-scale, high-quality paired training data. Existing synthetic data pipelines face a persistent trade-off: they sacrifice editing diversity, temporal consistency, or visual fidelity for scalability, or vice versa, making it impossible to train a single model that handles both global style changes and local object edits at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260069/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a source video (1280×720, 101 frames at 20 FPS) and a free-form text editing instruction, producing a temporally coherent edited video of the same resolution and duration. During training, the model additionally consumes an edited reference keyframe and a depth video as visual scaffolds, which are progressively annealed away via curriculum learning so that the final model operates on text instructions alone.

## Method

The Ditto pipeline generates over one million source-instruction-edited video triplets by (1) using Qwen-Image to edit a selected keyframe per instruction, (2) feeding that edited frame plus a depth-predicted structural video and the text prompt into the VACE in-context video generator to synthesize the full edited sequence, and (3) applying a distilled few-step model with a Wan2.2 fine-denoiser enhancer to cut cost to ~20% while preserving temporal coherence. A VLM agent (Qwen2.5-VL) autonomously generates diverse instructions and filters outputs for instruction fidelity, motion preservation, visual quality, and safety. The resulting Editto model is trained on Ditto-1M using a modality curriculum learning strategy: it begins with the edited reference frame as a strong visual scaffold alongside the text instruction, then gradually anneals the probability of providing that scaffold to zero, compelling the model to learn the abstract text-to-edit mapping without per-video optimization.

## Results

On a held-out test set of 50 out-of-distribution videos (each with 5 editing instructions), Editto achieves the best scores across all six metrics: CLIP-T 25.54, CLIP-F 99.03, VLM score 8.10, Edit-Acc 3.85, Temp-Con 3.76, and Overall 3.86 (human evaluation from 1,000 votes). The largest margins are in human-rated instruction-following (Edit-Acc: 3.85 vs. 2.28 for the best baseline) and overall quality (3.86 vs. 2.36), indicating substantially better semantic adherence and visual fidelity. Automatic metrics also show consistent gains, with CLIP-T improving by ~1.9 points and VLM score by ~0.75 over the strongest prior method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Editto outperforms all three quantitative baselines across every metric. Against TokenFlow (inversion-based, ICLR 2024), it gains +1.91 CLIP-T, +0.60 CLIP-F, +1.00 VLM, +2.15 Edit-Acc, +1.79 Temp-Con, and +2.16 Overall. Against InsV2V (feed-forward, ICLR 2024), the margins are even larger in human-rated metrics: +1.68 Edit-Acc and +1.79 Overall. The closest competitor is InsViE (lift-and-propagate, 2025), which leads in CLIP-F (98.78) but is still surpassed by Editto on all six metrics, with the most notable gaps in Edit-Acc (+1.57) and Temp-Con (+1.46). A fourth baseline, Runway Gen4-Aleph, appears only in qualitative comparisons (Fig. 4); the paper notes it slightly alters the subject's identity on local attribute edits (e.g., 'black suit') where Editto preserves identity and background faithfully. Overall, the consistent dominance across both automatic and human-evaluated metrics confirms that the Ditto-1M dataset and modality curriculum learning strategy yield a clear state-of-the-art in instruction-based video editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VACE in-context video generator and Wan2.2 video diffusion model, repurposing them into a unified data synthesis pipeline for instruction-based video editing. It departs from prior inversion-based methods (TokenFlow, FateZero) and 'lift-and-propagate' feed-forward approaches (VEGGIE, InsViE) by conditioning a single in-context generator on an image-editor-produced keyframe plus a depth video, avoiding the quality ceiling of propagation models and the per-video optimization cost of inversion. The modality curriculum learning strategy—gradually annealing a visual reference frame during training to arrive at purely text-driven inference—is a novel training recipe not seen in prior video editing work, and the VLM-agent-driven instruction generation and filtering loop distinguishes the pipeline from Señorita's multi-expert system.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Ditto-1M dataset (1M+ triplets, 1280×720, 101 frames) is the first million-scale open-source collection specifically for instruction-based video editing, directly addressing the data scarcity bottleneck that has held the field back. The fully open-source, reproducible pipeline (all components: Qwen-Image, VACE, Wan2.2, Qwen2.5-VL, CoTracker3, DINOv2) lowers the barrier for the community to generate additional editing data. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer videos or multi-turn interactive editing, whether the Pexels-sourced aesthetic bias limits downstream robustness, and whether the modality curriculum strategy transfers to other vision-language tasks beyond video editing.
