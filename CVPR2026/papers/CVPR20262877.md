## Problem

Existing deepfake video detection benchmarks suffer from three critical limitations: limited content diversity (mostly facial forgeries without partially-edited content or natural distortions), narrow generative model coverage (few and often outdated models), and restricted evaluation targets that overlook the emerging potential of large multimodal models (LMMs). As generative video quality rapidly improves, detection models trained on these narrow datasets fail to generalize to modern, unseen generators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262877/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes video clips as input across three categories: 60K real videos from 8 sources (with natural distortions like compression and motion blur), 3,857 AI-edited videos from 12 editing models across 5 edit types, and 58,500 fully AI-generated videos from 30 generation models. For LMMs, input is consumed via a prompt-based QA interface with two alternating binary-choice instructions to minimize answer-order bias. Output is a binary real/fake label, evaluated via accuracy and F1-score per generator subset, with cross-generator generalization assessed by training on 18 open-source generators and testing on all 30 including 12 closed-source ones.

## Method

FVBench is a 121,902-video benchmark that systematically evaluates 25+ detection models spanning conventional architectures (ResNet3D_18, Swin3D_T, AIGVDet, DeMamba) and LMMs from 0.5B to 78B parameters (InternVL, Qwen2.5-VL, LLaVA, VideoLlama3, mPLUG-Owl3, Llama3.2-Vision) plus closed-source Gemini 1.5 Pro and GPT-4o. The evaluation protocol tests zero-shot performance, LoRA fine-tuning (r=16, 5 epochs, cosine annealing at lr=1e-5 on RTX A6000), and cross-generator generalization where models trained on 18 open-source generators are evaluated on 30 generators including unseen closed-source ones. The key empirical finding is that while fine-tuned models achieve near-100% accuracy on known generators, zero-shot generalization to unseen generators remains the core challenge, with LMMs like InternLM-XComposer2.5 (7B) showing the strongest zero-shot performance (92.98% overall) among open-source models.

## Results

On the FVBench AI-generated video subset (30 generators), InternLM-XC2.5 (7B) achieves the best zero-shot overall accuracy of 92.98%, followed by Qwen2.5-VL (3B) at 79.67% and ResNet3D_18 at 80.85%. On real videos, DeMamba leads with 97.03% overall accuracy, while on AI-edited videos InternLM-XC2.5 (7B) again dominates at 95.60%. After LoRA fine-tuning, all evaluated models (conventional detectors and LMMs) reach near-100% accuracy on their respective test sets, confirming that the core challenge is zero-shot generalization to unseen generators rather than in-domain detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InternLM-XC2.5 (7B) is the clear zero-shot winner on both AI-generated (92.98%) and AI-edited (95.60%) videos, outperforming the next-best open-source LMM (Qwen2.5-VL 3B at 79.67% AI-gen) by over 13 points. DeMamba [7] exhibits extreme specialization: it achieves 97.03% on real videos (best overall) but collapses to 3.30% on AI-generated and 0.00% on AI-edited content, indicating it learned real-data-specific features rather than generalizable artifacts. Among conventional detectors, ResNet3D_18 [24] (80.85% AI-gen) and AIGVDet [2] (92.74% real) are the strongest, while Swin3D_T [44] underperforms across all subsets (53–65%). Among closed-source LMMs, Gemini 1.5 Pro [60] (71.15% AI-gen, 91.41% AI-edit) substantially outperforms GPT-4o [65] (49.86% AI-gen, 86.87% AI-edit), with GPT-4o falling below chance on several individual generators. Cross-generator experiments (Tables 6–7) reveal that both Swin3D_T and InternVL2.5 (8B) drop from ~100% on seen generators to 13–23% (Swin3D_T) and 6–14% (InternVL2.5) on unseen closed-source generators, confirming that zero-shot generalization to novel generators remains the central unsolved challenge.

## Relation to Prior Work

FVBench extends the deepfake video detection dataset lineage (UADFV, FaceForensics++, DF40, GVD, IVY-FAKE) by addressing three gaps the authors identify: limited content diversity (adding partially AI-edited videos alongside fully synthetic ones), narrow model coverage (42 state-of-the-art generators including closed-source models like Sora, Kling, and Hailuo), and restricted evaluation targets (benchmarking LMMs alongside specialized detectors). Conceptually, it repurposes the LMM evaluation paradigm established in image deepfake benchmarks (DFBench) to the video domain, while the cross-generator generalization protocol draws on the train-on-seen/test-on-unseen setup from prior AIGC detection work. The paper does not propose a new detection architecture but rather carves new ground by systematically positioning LMMs as viable zero-shot detectors and exposing the generalization gap that prior benchmarks, focused on in-domain accuracy, obscured.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark's practical value is substantial: with 120K+ videos spanning 42 generators and 8 real sources, it provides the most comprehensive resource for evaluating deepfake detection in the 2025+ landscape where closed-source generators dominate. The central finding—that zero-shot cross-generator generalization, not in-domain accuracy, is the core challenge—redirects research attention toward building detectors that understand fundamental synthetic artifacts rather than memorizing model-specific signatures. Key open questions include whether LMMs can close the gap with specialized detectors on unseen generators, how to handle the rapidly expanding space of new generation models without retraining, and whether the partial-edit detection subtask (where even the best models achieve only ~95% zero-shot) can be solved with current architectures. A limitation is that the benchmark's closed-source model coverage depends on API access, making full reproduction difficult.
