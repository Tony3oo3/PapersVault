## Problem

Current defensive collaborative perception (CP) systems remain vulnerable to two critical weaknesses: (i) existing attacks lack systematic optimization of attack timing and target region, and (ii) shared collaboration data (feature maps, occupancy maps) inadvertently discloses implicit confidence information that reveals collective uncertainty regions. No prior attack framework unifies vulnerability knowledge across different defensive configurations or exploits temporal evolution of these vulnerabilities for adaptive, persistent attacks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes k=5 frames of historical feature maps from all N CAVs, occupancy grid maps (directly from CAD-style defenses or approximated via Blind Region Segmentation for systems without map sharing), and CAV position-pose vectors. It outputs a per-frame adversarial feature perturbation δ (constrained to ‖δ‖∞ ≤ 1.0, applied via PGD with 5 iterations at step size 0.01), a fabrication risk map S ∈ [0,1]^{H×W} predicting vulnerable regions m=2 frames ahead, and a binary attack mask M_t* that spatially constrains perturbations to vulnerable zones (default 20m×20m for spoofing, 40m×40m for removal). The perturbation is applied to the attacker's transmitted feature map, and the mask is warped across p consecutive frames for persistent attacks.

## Method

The core contribution is a Mutual View Information Graph (MVIG) that encodes each CAV as a node with features from occupancy distribution, position-pose, and multi-scale spatial pooling, connected by edges weighted by mutual information between pairwise occupancy grids—unifying vulnerability signals from both outlier-based defenses (ROBOSAC, CP-Guard, GCP) and occupancy-based defenses (CAD) into a single representation. MVIGNet predicts future vulnerability maps via three modules: graph convolution layers for spatial encoding (aggregating neighbor features weighted by mutual view information), a GRU for temporal modeling over the k-frame history, and a two-layer FCN score head producing the risk map. The attack employs a decoupled two-stage optimization: given a mask from MVIGNet, PGD maximizes a spoofing/removal objective over the perturbation; MVIGNet then trains its mask prediction using a multi-objective loss (attack effectiveness, box differentiation, defense evasion) without gradient backpropagation to PGD. For persistent attacks, an entropy-aware vulnerability search warps the initial mask across consecutive frames, with a continuation threshold (η) on local risk-map expectation determining when to terminate the attack to avoid detection from outdated information.

## Results

On the Adv-OPV2V benchmark, MVIG(spoof) achieves a Defense Success Rate (DSR) of 32.0% against the CAD defense, a 62% relative reduction over the second-best baseline RC(spoof) at 83.5%. Against other defenses, MVIG(spoof) maintains DSR values of 14.8% (ROBOSAC), 17.2% (CP-Guard), and 13.0% (GCP), consistently outperforming BAC (23.0–90.5%) and Basic (100%) across all settings. For removal attacks, MVIG(remove) achieves 78.2% DSR against CAD versus 90.1% for RC(remove). The method operates at 29.9 FPS, enabling real-time attack execution in CP systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the most advanced defense (CAD), MVIG(spoof) achieves a DSR of 32.0%, dramatically outperforming RC(spoof) at 83.5% (a 62% relative reduction) and BAC at 90.5%, while Basic is completely detected (100% DSR). Against CP-Guard, MVIG(spoof) edges out RC(spoof) (17.2% vs. 18.1% DSR) and substantially outperforms BAC (37.0%). Against ROBOSAC and GCP, RC(spoof) achieves marginally lower DSR than MVIG (12.0% vs. 14.8% and 12.2% vs. 13.0%), but both are far superior to BAC (23.0% and 28.0%) and Basic (100%). For removal attacks, MVIG(remove) outperforms RC(remove) against CAD (78.2% vs. 90.1% DSR) and ROBOSAC (12.2% vs. 14.2%), while matching or slightly trailing on CP-Guard and GCP. Overall, MVIG's key advantage is its systematic exploitation of mutual view information asymmetries, which is most impactful against occupancy-based defenses like CAD where prior attacks lack temporal and spatial optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of adversarial attacks on collaborative perception initiated by Tu et al. (basic feature perturbation), Tao et al. (blind area confusion), and Zhang et al. (LiDAR ray-casting), which the authors formalize as special cases of their unified k-frame perturbation framework. The key departure is the introduction of a mutual view information graph (MVIG) that unifies vulnerability knowledge across heterogeneous defensive systems (outlier-based like ROBOSAC/CP-Guard and occupancy-based like CAD) into a single spectral representation, combined with GRU-based temporal modeling to predict future attack windows. This repurposes graph neural network message passing and mutual information edge weighting—tools from the broader GNN literature—toward the specific problem of identifying collective perception blind spots that defenses inadvertently expose through shared occupancy or feature data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that current CP defenses, which rely on consensus or occupancy-grid verification, systematically leak the very information (coverage gaps, confidence patterns) that an adaptive attacker needs to target their fabrications, reducing defense success rates by up to 62% against CAD. The temporal consistency finding—that naive spatial attacks (BAC) become nearly trivially detectable under multi-frame validation (DSR > 98%) while MVIG maintains 63% evasion—highlights that future defenses must reason about attack trajectories, not just per-frame anomalies. Open questions include whether the framework transfers to camera-based CP (the authors note this as future work), whether the occupancy-estimation approximation for non-CAD defenses holds under distribution shift, and what countermeasures (e.g., differential privacy on shared maps, randomized validation) can close the disclosed-vulnerability gap without degrading legitimate perception.
