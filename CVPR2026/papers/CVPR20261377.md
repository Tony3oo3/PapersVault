## Problem

Existing proactive face-swapping defenses (noise, patch, makeup-based) either introduce visible artifacts that degrade the source image or fail to meaningfully disrupt the generated deepfake, because they shift identity features globally without targeting the specific facial components that drive identity recognition. There is no method that simultaneously preserves the visual fidelity of the protected image, disrupts face-swapping outputs, and allows user-controlled, attribute-specific distortion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261377/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB facial image and a short text prompt naming the target attribute (e.g., 'nose', 'eyebrows', 'lips'). It outputs a protected facial image at the same resolution, with an imperceptible adversarial watermark bounded by an ℓ∞ perturbation budget of 0.02. The protected image is visually near-identical to the original (PSNR ≈ 32 dB, SSIM ≈ 0.90) but, when fed into any identity-driven face-swapping model, produces a deepfake with substantially distorted appearance in the prompted region.

## Method

DeepProtect operates in the W+ latent space of StyleGAN2 in two stages. First, identity blending replaces the style vectors of middle layers (3–7) with the most similar counterparts from a CLIP-retrieved candidate set of visually similar but identity-different references, diluting the original identity; the generator is then partially fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 8) on the affine-transform modules with an identity-lock loss that penalizes deviation from the diluted identity, keeping only ~0.04 M trainable parameters. Second, attribute distortion uses an order-aware LDA objective—extending Fisher's LDA with a CLIP-rank regularization term—to extract a semantic direction v_attr in the entangled identity space for the prompted attribute, then embeds an imperceptible adversarial watermark along the target direction via sign-gradient steps projected onto the ℓ∞ ball, selectively amplifying or suppressing the specified facial component in downstream deepfake outputs.

## Results

On CelebA-HQ with SimSwap, DeepProtect (Combined) achieves a defense success rate (DSR) of 94.8%, outperforming the next-best method FaceShield (81.6%) by 13.2 points, while maintaining a protected-image SSIM† of 0.902 and the lowest identity score matching (ISM) of 0.201. Across all five face-swapping models (SimSwap, FaceDancer, BlendFace, FaceSwapper, DiffFace), DeepProtect (Combined) consistently achieves DSR above 92%, whereas baselines range from 24% (WDP on BlendFace) to 86% (DiffAM on DiffFace). The attribute-only variant achieves 60–67% DSR with the highest source-image SSIM† (0.944), offering a lightweight deployment option.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeepProtect (Combined) decisively outperforms all seven baselines on DSR across every face-swapping model tested, achieving 92–97% versus 72–86% for the strongest competitors (FaceShield, CLIP2Protect, DiffAM). On source-image quality (Table 1), DeepProtect (Combined) achieves the lowest ISM (0.201 on CelebA-HQ), indicating the most effective identity dilution, while retaining SSIM† of 0.902—only marginally below FaceShield (0.933) and far above makeup-based methods like DiffAM (0.608) and CLIP2Protect (0.660). Noise-based baselines (CMUA-Watermark, DF-RAP, WDP) preserve high PSNR/SSIM on protected images but fail to disrupt deepfakes, yielding DSR below 44%. The attribute-only variant of DeepProtect matches or exceeds makeup-based methods on DSR (60–67% vs. 60–86%) while maintaining substantially better visual fidelity (SSIM† 0.944 vs. 0.608–0.859), making it a practical lightweight alternative. In human evaluation, DeepProtect achieves the lowest deepfake MOS (1.35/5) while tying FaceShield on source MOS (4.7/5), confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proactive face-swapping defense line that includes adversarial noise (FaceShield, DF-RAP), makeup-based editing (DiffAM, CLIP2Protect), and latent-space identity obfuscation (SCOL). It departs from SCOL's full StyleGAN optimization by restricting tuning to middle-layer style vectors via LoRA, and from noise-based methods by operating in the identity embedding space rather than pixel space. The key conceptual departure is the two-stage design: global identity dilution in W+ space creates a 'relaxed' identity that makes subsequent local adversarial watermarking along CLIP-derived attribute directions more effective, a synergy not explored in prior single-mechanism defenses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 12-second inference with 0.04M trainable parameters makes this deployable as a pre-upload filter, whereas diffusion-based competitors require 25+ seconds and 100M+ parameters. The user-controllable text-prompt interface (e.g., 'distort my nose') adds a layer of agency absent from prior defenses, and the semantic cues embedded in distortions could support downstream traceability. Open questions include whether the method generalizes beyond StyleGAN2's training domain (FFHQ), how it performs against future face-swapping architectures that may not rely on the same identity encoders, and whether the pre-built feature bank (4,605 identities) is sufficient for long-tail demographics.
