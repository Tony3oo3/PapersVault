## Problem

Large-scale dataset distillation methods are typically evaluated under a soft-label + knowledge-distillation (SL+KD) regime where multiple teacher soft labels per augmented image are used for student training, yet this setup causes all subsets—regardless of quality or size—to saturate near full-dataset accuracy, masking whether DD methods genuinely outperform random baselines. The paper asks how data quality, size, and compute jointly shape performance across label regimes (SL+KD, fixed soft-label, hard-label) and whether current DD objectives scale to larger architectures.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a full training dataset (ImageNet-1K at 224×224 or TinyImageNet at 64×64) with class labels and a compute budget (e.g., 200 epochs for IPC-50) as input. CAD-Prune produces per-sample difficulty scores calibrated to that budget; CA2D then outputs an RDED-style synthetic set of stitched image patches at the target IPC. DCS takes a collection of precomputed subsets (coresets or distilled sets) and their downstream test errors, and outputs a Spearman-rank correlation score between a candidate distillation loss and generalization error, enabling zero-shot objective evaluation without re-synthesizing datasets.

## Method

CAD-Prune trains a model on the full dataset using the exact compute budget of the downstream task (matching learning-rate schedule), then computes per-sample uncertainty as the standard deviation of EL2N scores over a short window (W=2 epochs, J=6) near the end of that budget-limited run, identifying samples still being learned at that stage. CA2D uses these scores to select examples of appropriate difficulty, then crops, selects, and stitches confident patches in the RDED style to build a compute-aligned synthetic dataset. Separately, DCS evaluates distillation objectives by computing the Spearman correlation between a surrogate loss value and downstream test error across a diverse set of precomputed subsets, revealing that TM-based trajectory-matching objectives produce near-constant loss (~0.999) across subsets when using ResNet-18, confirming their failure to scale beyond small ConvNet-D4 architectures.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K with ResNet-18 in the hard-label (HL) setting, the proposed CA2D achieves 15.25% top-1 accuracy at IPC 10, 41.72% at IPC 50, and 46.32% at IPC 100, outperforming RDED (14.34%, 38.49%, 44.36%) across all budgets. The compute-aware coreset CAD-Prune reaches 40.21% at IPC 50, surpassing the sliding-window EL2N-Best (38.44%) while requiring 2–4× less compute. In the SL+KD regime, all methods (DD and coreset) converge to 62–63% at IPC 100 regardless of subset quality, demonstrating that soft-label supervision eliminates meaningful performance differentiation. On TinyImageNet with ConvNet-D4, TM and DATM outperform random baselines in HL (28.16% and 29.51% vs. 18.62% at IPC 50) but lose their advantage under fixed soft labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CA2D outperforms RDED by 0.91, 3.23, and 1.96 points at IPC 10, 50, and 100 respectively in the HL setting on ImageNet-1K, establishing a new state-of-the-art for large-scale DD. CAD-Prune beats the sliding-window EL2N-Best by 1.77 points at IPC 50 (40.21% vs. 38.44%) and 2.04 points at IPC 10 (12.57% vs. 10.53%), though it trails slightly at IPC 100 (47.40% vs. 47.76%), while requiring only a single compute-matched training run versus multiple full training cycles. In the SL+KD regime, all large-scale DD methods (SRe2L, DWA, D4M, Minimax Diffusion, RDED) and coreset methods (EL2N-Best, GraphCut, Forgetting-Easy) converge within ~1 point of each other at IPC 100 (62–63%), making quality-based differentiation meaningless. On TinyImageNet, TM and DATM outperform K-centers and random in HL (e.g., DATM 29.51% vs. K-centers 22.02% at IPC 50), but this gap vanishes in SL where DATM (37.39%) is essentially tied with K-centers (37.45%). The DCS analysis further shows that TM-based objectives fail to scale to ResNet-18, where the surrogate loss remains constant (~0.999) across subsets with vastly different generalization performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of dataset distillation (DD) and coreset selection, building directly on EL2N-style per-sample scoring [19], Dyn-Unc's uncertainty-based importance [11], and RDED's patch-based synthesis [25]. Its key conceptual departure is from the large-scale DD literature (SRe2L, DWA, D4M, Minimax Diffusion), which predominantly evaluates under a multi-soft-label (SL+KD) regime, by demonstrating that this regime renders data quality and size nearly irrelevant to downstream performance. The paper also extends the coreset literature's well-established finding that sample difficulty must be matched to compute budget [16, 23] into the DD setting, where prior methods like RDED use a fixed 'easy patch' heuristic rather than a compute-aware selection criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that SL+KD evaluation saturates to near-full-dataset accuracy regardless of subset quality or size—challenges the evaluation methodology underpinning most large-scale DD papers and suggests the field may have been measuring the wrong thing. Practically, this redirects attention toward the HL setting where data quality genuinely matters, and the proposed CAD-Prune/CA2D framework offers a compute-aligned alternative that outperforms both RDED and the best coreset on ImageNet-1K. Open questions include whether these label-regime effects generalize to multimodal or non-classification tasks, and whether DCS can be extended to non-optimizable distillation objectives. The model-stealing implication (small dataset + repeated soft-label supervision ≈ full-dataset performance) also raises security considerations worth further study.
