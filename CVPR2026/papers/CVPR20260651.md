## Problem

Diffusion-based anomaly detectors build decisions from prediction residuals, but reverse-process stochasticity and complex normal structure also inflate residuals, making raw magnitude non-diagnostic for separating true anomalies from benign intra-normal variability. This ambiguity is compounded in cross-domain settings where the target domain is unlabeled and the normal manifold shifts, so residual-based detectors degrade sharply without a mechanism to isolate the anomaly-aligned component and transfer it reliably.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260651/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from a labeled source domain (image-level or patch-level anomaly labels) and an unlabeled, anomaly-contaminated target domain. For each image it partitions the spatial lattice into patches, computes a spatio-temporal Residual-Evolution Field across T diffusion time steps, and outputs a 7-dimensional REF signature per patch (three energy terms, three non-stationarity indices, and one directional-variability term). A lightweight head maps these signatures to patch-level anomaly probabilities, which are aggregated via a MIL product into an image-level score and rendered as a pixel-level anomaly map.

## Method

The key insight is that under the learned normal dynamics, residual variability consistent with intra-normal statistics contracts toward stationarity, whereas anomalous regions sustain a persistent non-stationary signal. The Residual-Evolution Field (REF) captures this via three per-pixel, per-time-step vectors: an amplitude residual R (score difference between the noisy state and its Tweedie projection), a directional offset M (score component orthogonal to the reference direction), and a path-integrated drift Q (difference of probability-flow ODE solutions). A Field Transformer with adaptive time attention aggregates these into patch-level energy and non-stationarity statistics, which a supervised head classifies on the labeled source. For cross-domain transfer, Cross-domain Field Alignment (CFA) aligns target REF features to the source field space through three unsupervised operations—monotone temporal reparameterization, second-order whitening/recoloring (CORAL), and orthogonal Procrustes directional subspace matching—enabling the source-trained detector to be reused on the unlabeled target without any target labels.

## Results

REF+CFA achieves a mean AUROC of 95.22% across nine source-target transfers on MVTec AD, VisA, and DAGM, exceeding the strongest baseline (DDAD, 82.21%) by 13.01 pp. On VisA (candle → macaroni1/macaroni2/pcb2), the method reaches 100.00/99.50/98.95% AUROC, and on DAGM (Class 2 → Class 1/3/6) it attains 100.00% on all three targets. Gains over the best competing method range from +2.01 pp (MVTec capsule) to +14.00 pp (DAGM Class 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

REF+CFA outperforms all 16 baselines across every one of the nine source-target transfers. Among unsupervised methods, DDAD is the strongest (82.21% avg AUROC), yet REF+CFA still leads by 13.01 pp; GLASS (79.65%) and General-AD (77.63%) trail further behind. In the domain-adaptation group, FFTAT is the best (78.47% avg), followed by JWO (71.67%) and CMKD (73.61%), all of which are surpassed by 16–41 pp. Domain-generalization baselines are uniformly weaker, with DDDG (66.93%) and DKGPL (66.14%) as the top two, still 28–29 pp below REF+CFA. The largest absolute gains appear on VisA (up to +13.02 pp over DDAD on Pcb2) and DAGM (up to +14.00 pp over DDAD on Class 1), while the smallest gain is +2.01 pp on MVTec capsule, where baselines are already relatively strong.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends diffusion-based anomaly detection (AnoDDPM, DDAD, DiffAD), which typically scores anomalies by residual magnitude from denoising, by instead analyzing how residuals *evolve* across reverse diffusion steps. The key conceptual departure is a stationarity test: under the learned normal dynamics, intra-normal residual variability contracts toward stationarity, while anomaly-aligned residuals retain a persistent non-stationary signal that can be isolated via the Residual-Evolution Field (REF). For cross-domain transfer, the paper repurposes standard alignment tools (temporal reparameterization, CORAL whitening, Procrustes rotation) but applies them to the novel REF feature space rather than raw features, and grounds the transfer guarantee in a Wasserstein-contraction argument specific to the field operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stationarity-based separation of anomaly signal from stochastic noise offers a principled alternative to residual-magnitude scoring, and the CFA module provides a concrete recipe for label-free cross-domain reuse that is supported by a formal risk bound. The primary limitation is computational: training separate diffusion score networks in both source and target domains is expensive and limits scalability to many domain pairs. Open questions include whether the theoretical contraction guarantees hold under the approximate score networks used in practice, whether single-step or distilled field predictors can retain the REF signal at lower cost, and whether the near-perfect AUROC scores on small VisA/DAGM test sets generalize to larger, more heterogeneous industrial deployments.
