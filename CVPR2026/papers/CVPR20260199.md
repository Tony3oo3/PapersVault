## Problem

Inference-time optimization of diffusion noise latents for reward guidance or quality improvement pushes the latent away from the statistical structure of true Gaussian noise, causing artifacts, brittle behavior, and reward hacking. Existing regularizers (norm constraints, isotropic KL, moment matching) only align selected global features and fail to capture the local joint spatial statistics that define a typical Gaussian realization. The paper asks how to measure, from a single sample, whether its local statistics are consistent with those of a genuine Gaussian draw.

## Input / Output

The method takes a modified diffusion noise latent (in the latent space of models such as SDXL-Turbo, SD1.5, SD2.1) and a text prompt, and produces an optimized latent that remains within the Gaussian typical set along with the corresponding generated image. The regularizer operates on the latent tensor directly, constructing a 3-level downsampling pyramid (full, 1/2, 1/4 resolution) with variance-preserving mean pooling at each level. For the model-free image-to-noise matching application, a target image is additionally provided as a Pearson-correlation reward, and no diffusion model queries are made during optimization.

## Method

The core contribution is a differentiable Gaussianity regularizer that lifts a single sample into an empirical distribution modeled as a pairwise Markov Random Field, then approximates the KL divergence to the Gaussian prior via a Bethe-Kikuchi cluster expansion. This yields two complementary terms: a 1D Value Entropy aligning the marginal pixel distribution with N(0,1), and a 2D Spatial Entropy aligning the joint distribution of adjacent pixel pairs with N(0,I₂), combined with a Bethe correction factor γ to account for node over-counting. The differential entropy component is estimated end-to-end via Gaussian KDE (128 bins for linear complexity), while the cross-entropy uses a closed-form Monte Carlo average over the analytic Gaussian log-density. To capture longer-range correlations beyond the local pairwise MRF, the full KL objective is summed across a multi-scale pyramid of downsampled latents, producing the final loss L_full that is added as a weighted regularizer during gradient-based latent optimization.

## Results

On aesthetic image generation with SDXL-Turbo, the proposed method achieves the best HPSv2 (0.288), ImageReward (0.826), and PickScore (21.625) among all regularized baselines, while maintaining CLIP score (24.574) close to the unoptimized initial latent (24.997). On brightness minimization, it attains the highest Aesthetic score (5.768), HPSv2 (0.298), ImageReward (1.038), and PickScore (22.823) while reducing brightness from 0.504 to 0.270. The unregularized baseline (No Reg.) achieves the highest aesthetic score (7.975) but severely degrades all other quality metrics (CLIP drops to 19.682, ImageReward becomes negative at −0.950), illustrating the reward-hacking problem the regularizer addresses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares five Gaussianity regularizers for noise optimization. KL divergence loss [27] and ReNO [13] both suffer significant quality degradation (CLIP drops to ~22, ImageReward below 0.3), indicating their simpler distributional proxies (fitted isotropic Gaussian, norm constraint) are insufficient to preserve local spatial statistics. Pix2Pix-Zero [44] achieves a higher aesthetic score (7.031) but at the cost of the lowest CLIP (20.967) and ImageReward (0.071) among regularized methods, showing its autocorrelation+KL combination still permits reward hacking. ReNoise [15] is the closest competitor on CLIP (23.286) but lags on HPSv2 (0.257 vs 0.288) and ImageReward (0.248 vs 0.826). The concurrent work by Hwang et al. [21] (moment + power-spectrum matching) is the strongest baseline, matching or exceeding the proposed method on CLIP (25.019 vs 24.574) and Aesthetic score (5.863 vs 6.478), but the proposed method wins on HPSv2 (0.288 vs 0.277), ImageReward (0.826 vs 0.729), and PickScore (21.625 vs 21.381), and is reported to be more stable across learning rates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior Gaussianity regularizers for diffusion noise optimization (KL divergence [27], norm-based ReNO [13], moment/spectrum matching [21]) by reframing the problem distributionally: rather than asking whether a single latent point 'belongs' to the Gaussian prior or matching selected global moments, it lifts the sample into an empirical distribution modeled as a pairwise Markov Random Field and measures Gaussianity via KL divergence. The tractable objective is derived through the Bethe-Kikuchi cluster expansion [5, 25, 65], a tool borrowed from statistical physics and belief propagation literature, combined with a multi-scale pyramid to capture longer-range correlations that local pairwise models miss. This positions the work at the intersection of classical MRF theory and modern inference-time diffusion optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The regularizer offers a principled, differentiable, and deterministic way to prevent reward hacking and latent drift during inference-time noise optimization, which is increasingly important as reward-guided generation becomes a standard technique. The model-free image-to-noise matching application (recovering a general-purpose Gaussian latent from a target image without querying the denoiser) is a novel demonstration, though it remains qualitative and requires per-sample tuning. Key limitations include the computational cost of KDE for differential entropy estimation and the pairwise MRF's inability to fully eliminate complex long-range dependencies, leaving open the question of whether richer implicit distributions or more efficient entropy estimators could close this gap.
