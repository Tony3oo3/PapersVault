## Problem

Existing visual token reduction methods for LVLMs are designed for single-turn VQA and fail in multi-turn VQA (MT-VQA), where future questions are unpredictable and may reference arbitrary image regions. Prompt-dependent methods bias toward the initial query and discard information needed later, while prompt-agnostic methods rely on heuristic criteria (e.g., attention scores to [CLS] or prompt tokens) that the paper empirically shows to be suboptimal, with retained tokens bearing little correlation to these attention cues.

## Input / Output

MetaCompress takes only the encoded visual token sequence X_IMG (from a ViT vision tower, potentially multi-scale with variable length as in LLaVA-NeXT or InternLM-XComposer-2.5) and produces a sparse compression matrix P ∈ R^{m×n} (m ≪ n) that projects the full sequence down to m tokens. The module is prompt-agnostic and resolution-adaptive: it generates P of the correct shape for any input sequence length via spatial pooling with configurable kernel size and stride, requiring no re-compression per question turn. The compressed tokens are then fed into the LLM decoder alongside the text prompt for response generation.

## Method

MetaCompress is a lightweight meta generator that computes the compression matrix as a row-wise softmax of the scaled inner product between spatially downsampled queries (average-pooled image tokens with absolute position embeddings, projected by W_q) and linearly projected keys (image tokens projected by W_k), with a learnable diagonal scaling ω. This low-rank positive semi-definite form (Eq. 9) initializes as a weighted spatial pooling and is trained in a data-driven manner to minimize KL divergence between the LLM's output distributions with full vs. compressed visual tokens, plus entropy regularization on P and a collapse penalty to prevent all compressed tokens from deriving from the same source. Training is data-efficient (~20k samples, ~30 GPU-hours for LLaVA-NeXT-7B at 90% reduction), uses SGD with gradient clipping, and requires no ground-truth compression annotations. The key empirical finding is that the learned compression matrix retains tokens largely unrelated to attention-based importance scores, validating that heuristic reduction criteria are suboptimal for MT-VQA.

## Results

At a 90% token reduction rate, MetaCompress achieves 72.94% average accuracy on MT-VQA-v2, 59.48% on MT-GQA, and 5.20 on ConvBench with LLaVA-1.5-13b, consistently outperforming all baselines across five LVLM architectures. On the unseen ConvBench benchmark (no training data), MetaCompress still leads by a large margin (e.g., 9.88 vs. 9.76 Sample and 2.78 FastV on XComposer-2.5-7b). Notably, the prompt-dependent FastV baseline performs worse than even random pruning in most settings (e.g., 48.06% vs. 66.66% on MT-VQA-v2 with LLaVA-1.5-7b), confirming that attention-based heuristics are suboptimal for multi-turn dialogue.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaCompress outperforms both baselines across all five LVLM architectures and all three benchmarks. Against FastV (prompt-dependent, attention-guided pruning), the gap is dramatic: on LLaVA-1.5-7b MT-VQA-v2, MetaCompress scores 70.65% versus FastV's 48.06%, and FastV is actually worse than random pruning (66.66%) in most settings, confirming that prompt-conditioned attention heuristics discard information needed for subsequent turns. Against PruMerge (prompt-agnostic, attention-score-based merging), MetaCompress leads by 1.1–2.3 points on MT-VQA-v2 (e.g., 72.94% vs. 70.68% on LLaVA-1.5-13b) and by 1.3–1.6 points on MT-GQA (e.g., 59.48% vs. 58.11%). On the unseen ConvBench benchmark, MetaCompress also surpasses PruMerge (5.20 vs. 4.68 on LLaVA-1.5-13b), demonstrating transferability without additional training. PruMerge is only evaluated on LLaVA-1.5 models due to incompatibility with multi-scale vision towers, whereas MetaCompress handles variable-length sequences natively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual token reduction line of work (FastV, PruMerge, FitPrune, IVTP) by reframing the problem for multi-turn VQA, where future questions are unknown and cannot guide compression. It departs from prior prompt-dependent methods (which bias toward the first-turn query) and prompt-agnostic heuristics (which rely on attention scores as reduction criteria) by unifying pruning and merging into a single learnable compression matrix and showing empirically that attention-based heuristics are suboptimal. The meta-learning formulation—training a lightweight generator that produces per-image compression matrices from the visual sequence alone—repurposes the meta-learning paradigm in a novel way for token reduction, avoiding the need for per-image optimization at inference time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that attention-score-based heuristics (the standard guidance in FastV, PruMerge, and similar methods) are suboptimal for multi-turn dialogue challenges a widely-adopted assumption and motivates learning-based alternatives. The method is practically attractive: it requires only ~30 GPU hours of training, adds negligible inference overhead, and generalizes across five LVLM architectures and three benchmarks without retraining. Open questions include whether the approach extends to full-stage compression (vision tower + all LLM layers), video understanding, and whether the suboptimality of attention heuristics persists in single-turn settings or is specific to the multi-turn formulation. The paper also acknowledges that it does not address the interaction between token reduction and KV-cache strategies in long multi-turn conversations.
