## Problem

Existing score-fusion strategies for whole-body biometric recognition are static: they invoke all available models (face, gait, body) for every test sample regardless of input quality or modality reliability. This assumption of universal complementarity is frequently violated in practice—e.g., face scores are unreliable for back-view or occluded subjects—yet no prior method adaptively determines which models to use per sample and how to best combine their outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264009/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a multimodal biometric query (a 4-frame video clip for CCVID/MEVID or a single image for LTCC) along with pre-computed gallery embeddings from frozen biometric models. It outputs a predicted identity label, a fused score vector over the gallery, and a reasoning trace justifying its model selections. Notably, score vectors from invoked models are retained internally for fusion, while only the predicted label is surfaced to the agent for its reasoning loop, since raw score vectors are not directly interpretable by the MLLM.

## Method

FusionAgent wraps each biometric model (AdaFace, BigGait, CAL, AIM, AGRL) as a callable tool and uses a Qwen2.5-VL-3B MLLM in a ReAct-style multi-turn loop to sequentially decide which tools to invoke per query. The agent is trained via GRPO (N=6 rollouts, LoRA r=64) with four composite rewards: format compliance, tool-call success rate, answer accuracy, and a novel metric-based reward that evaluates the agent-selected model combination's fused performance (Rank-1, mAP, TAR, FNIR) on the training set, with Bernoulli-augmented exploration to encourage diverse combinations. For score integration, the proposed ACT method designates the first selected model as an anchor (its full score vector is used unconditionally), then adds Z-score-normalized, top-k-filtered contribution scores from all selected models, scaled by a normalization factor of (1+|M_q|). This anchor-plus-sparse-refinement design mitigates score misalignment from heterogeneous models while amplifying the match/non-match gap.

## Results

On CCVID, FusionAgent (CoT) achieves 93.4% Rank-1, 92.6% mAP, 85.9% TAR@1%FAR, and 10.1% FNIR@1%FPIR, surpassing the prior SoTA QME (94.1% Rank-1, 90.8% mAP, 76.2% TAR, 12.3% FNIR) on mAP, TAR, and FNIR while trailing by 0.7% on Rank-1. On LTCC, FusionAgent leads all metrics: 75.5% Rank-1, 41.0% mAP, 37.0% TAR, and 50.0% FNIR versus QME's 73.8/39.6/35.0/64.3. On MEVID, FusionAgent attains 54.7% Rank-1, 28.7% mAP, 34.9% TAR, and 58.6% FNIR, outperforming QME on mAP, TAR, and FNIR. The largest gains appear in TAR (up to +9.7 points on CCVID) and FNIR (up to −14.3 points on LTCC), confirming that dynamic model selection and ACT score-fusion most benefit verification and open-set search.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary SoTA competitor QME [67], FusionAgent wins on mAP (+1.8 on CCVID, +1.4 on LTCC, +0.5 on MEVID), TAR (+9.7 on CCVID, +2.0 on LTCC, +2.0 on MEVID), and FNIR (−2.2 on CCVID, −14.3 on LTCC, −6.0 on MEVID), while trailing by a narrow margin on Rank-1 (−0.7 on CCVID, +1.7 on LTCC, −1.0 on MEVID). Compared to rule-based fusion baselines (Min/Max-Fusion, Z-score, Min-max), FusionAgent achieves substantially higher TAR (up to +23.5 points) and lower FNIR (up to −38.4 points), confirming that static all-model fusion is suboptimal. Against learning-based fusion methods (BSSF, Weighted-sum, Asym-AO1), FusionAgent leads on mAP and TAR across all three datasets, with the gap widening on LTCC where model complementarity is weakest. Versus the multimodal foundation model SapiensID [25], FusionAgent improves mAP by +14.8 (CCVID) and +6.4 (LTCC) while also reporting TAR and FNIR metrics that SapiensID does not provide. On LTCC, where face quality is poor and gait is unavailable, FusionAgent still outperforms the best unimodal body model (AIM) by +0.7 Rank-1 and −16.2 FNIR, demonstrating that dynamic selection of ReID models under surveillance conditions is effective.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: (1) score-fusion in biometric recognition, where prior work (rule-based Z-score/Min-Max, learning-based QME, FarSight, BSSF) assumes a fixed model combination for all queries, and (2) tool-augmented MLLM agents trained via reinforcement fine-tuning (GRPO from DeepSeekMath, ReAct-style multi-turn controllers). FusionAgent departs from the first thread by replacing the universal-complementarity assumption with sample-specific model selection, and from the second by grounding the agent's tool-use in a concrete biometric pipeline with a metric-based reward tied to recognition performance rather than generic task success.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical insight—that a lightweight fusion rule (ACT) paired with adaptive model selection outperforms sophisticated learned fusion (QME) that uses all models—challenges the field's implicit assumption that fusion complexity is the primary lever for improvement. This opens a path toward more efficient deployment (fewer model invocations per query) and more interpretable recognition pipelines. Limitations include the compute cost of training the MLLM agent, the dependence on a training set for the metric-based reward, and degraded cross-domain transfer when the tool pool changes (CCVID→LTCC zero-shot). Open questions include scalability to larger model pools, whether the agent generalizes to entirely new biometric domains without retraining, and whether the paradigm extends beyond biometrics to other multi-expert vision tasks.
