## Problem

Existing 4D Gaussian Splatting methods model motion as a deterministic optimization problem with hand-crafted, fixed priors (polynomial deformations, rigidity constraints) applied uniformly across all primitives. This leaves them unable to quantify motion uncertainty, extrapolate beyond observed training frames, or adapt regularization strength to observation confidence—particularly problematic for occluded or sparsely sampled regions where fixed priors become overly restrictive or inappropriate.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261250/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video sequence with estimated camera poses (via Mega-SAM for DAVIS) and produces a 4D Gaussian Splatting scene where each primitive's temporal deformation is governed by a 9-dimensional GP posterior (3 translation + 6D continuous rotation). Beyond standard novel-view renderings, the framework outputs per-primitive motion uncertainty maps (projected via alpha-blending) and future motion predictions by querying the trained GP at timesteps beyond the training range, all without additional architectural modifications.

## Method

GP-4DGS replaces deterministic deformation fields with a multi-output variational GP over 4D inputs (canonical 3D position + time), using a composite kernel that separates anisotropic Matérn spatial components (for geometric smoothness and handling spatially disconnected objects) from per-axis Matérn kernels weighted by periodic temporal kernels (for motion periodicity and extrapolation). To scale to tens of thousands of primitives, inducing points (initialized via Chronos time-series features + k-means clustering) reduce inference from O(N³) to O(NM²+M³). Training follows a GP-GS dual optimization loop: Stage 1 trains the variational GP on a confidence-weighted subset of primitives (selected by cumulative alpha-blending contribution above a threshold, with Gaussian noise injected into spatial coordinates), while Stage 2 regularizes GS optimization with a GP guidance loss that penalizes deviations from GP posterior means, with an annealed threshold progressively tightening constraints. This creates a self-reinforcing feedback loop where reliable observations refine motion priors that reciprocally stabilize reconstruction in poorly observed regions.

## Results

On the DyCheck benchmark, GP-4DGS achieves mPSNR 17.38, mSSIM 0.65, and mLPIPS 0.37 across all seven scenes, outperforming the strongest baseline SoM (17.09 / 0.65 / 0.39). The advantage is most pronounced on the Challenging subset with reduced viewpoint overlap, where GP-4DGS reaches mPSNR 15.02 and mLPIPS 0.51 versus SoM's 14.56 and 0.53, demonstrating that GP-based spatiotemporal priors are particularly effective under sparse observations. For future motion extrapolation, GP-4DGS achieves PSNR 17.62 (5 frames) and 16.65 (15 frames) on periodic scenes, substantially exceeding linear extrapolation (11.55 and 8.11).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GP-4DGS consistently outperforms all eight baselines on the primary DyCheck evaluation. Against the closest competitor SoM [46], GP-4DGS gains +0.29 mPSNR and −0.02 mLPIPS on all scenes, with the gap widening to +0.46 mPSNR on the Challenging subset where sparse observations stress-test motion priors. Against Gaussian Marbles [42], the margin is larger: +1.54 mPSNR and −0.20 mLPIPS on all scenes. On the SoM5 subset, GP-4DGS surpasses older 4DGS methods (4DGS [48], D-3DGS [51], SC-GS [23]) by 3.5–5.0 mPSNR, and also beats NeRF-based approaches (HyperNeRF [34], DynIBaR [22]) by roughly 1–3.5 mPSNR. For uncertainty quantification (Table 3), GP-4DGS achieves the lowest AUSE-MSE (7.22×10⁻² on Top-20 frames) compared to UA-4DGS [19] (7.60×10⁻²) and Random (9.76×10⁻²), confirming better-calibrated uncertainty. The only near-tie is mSSIM on the All and Challenging subsets where GP-4DGS matches SoM at 0.65 and 0.46 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) line of work (SoM, Gaussian Marbles, D-3DGS, 4DGS) by replacing their deterministic, hand-crafted motion priors (polynomial deformations, rigidity constraints) with learned, data-adaptive Gaussian Process priors. It also builds on the emerging thread of probabilistic Gaussian Splatting (GP-GS, Stochastic GS, VBGS), but is the first to move beyond static scenes into the 4D dynamic setting. The scalable variational GP formulation with inducing points [43] and the composite Matérn + periodic kernel design are the key technical departures from standard GP practice, tailored to the anisotropic spatio-temporal structure of deformation fields.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GP-4DGS introduces three capabilities—uncertainty quantification, temporal extrapolation, and observation-adaptive motion priors—that were entirely absent from the 4DGS literature, offering a principled path toward trustworthy dynamic scene reconstruction for robotics and autonomous systems. However, the performance gains over strong baselines (SoM, Gaussian Marbles) are modest on standard benchmarks, and the evaluation is limited to small-scale datasets (DyCheck, DAVIS) with qualitative-only DAVIS results. Open questions include whether the periodic kernel assumption generalizes to non-repetitive or long-horizon motions, how the method scales to scenes with hundreds of thousands of primitives, and whether the GP-GS alternating optimization converges reliably in practice without careful threshold tuning.
