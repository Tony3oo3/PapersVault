## Problem

Existing gene expression estimation methods from pathology images treat expression as a population-level signal and do not model the underlying data-generation process in which observed bulk or spatial expression arises from aggregation of individual cell-level signals. Although single-cell RNA-seq datasets provide cell-resolved expression profiles, they are noisy, affected by batch effects, and lack matched histopathology images, making their direct integration into WSI-based estimation challenging.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262155/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes whole-slide pathology images (cropped into patches via CLAM preprocessing) and cell-type prototypes derived from public scRNA-seq datasets as inputs. It produces slide-level or patch-level gene expression profiles modeled under a Negative Binomial distribution, along with per-patch cell-type compositional weights that provide interpretable explanations. For patch-level spatial transcriptomics, CPNN can be plugged into existing architectures (e.g., STNet, TRIPLEX) by replacing their prediction head, using a Pearson correlation loss instead of the NB likelihood to handle noisy ST measurements.

## Method

CPNN models observed gene expression as a weighted mixture of cell-type prototypes: each prototype is a mean expression vector capturing stable gene-gene co-variation patterns for a cell type, estimated from scRNA-seq data via batch-agnostic Negative Binomial regression. A neural network (CONCH encoder + LLaVA-projector MLP with softmax) learns per-patch compositional weights from image features, and gene-specific scaling (α) and shift (β) parameters correct for the modality gap between single-cell and bulk/spatial measurements. Training minimizes a Negative Binomial NLL (slide-level) or Pearson correlation loss (patch-level) plus a regularization term that softly enforces consistency between the learned prototypes/weights and cell2location deconvolution results, preserving biological interpretability while allowing data-driven refinement of the prototypes.

## Results

On slide-level gene expression estimation, CPNN achieves the highest Spearman correlation (SCC) across all three TCGA datasets: 0.338 on BRCA, 0.318 on KIRC, and 0.304 on LUAD. For Pearson correlation (PCC), CPNN attains the best score on BRCA (0.304, tied with SRMambaMIL) and KIRC (0.291), while ranking third on LUAD (0.271 vs. MambaMIL's 0.280). On patch-level spatial transcriptomics, integrating CPNN into STNet and TRIPLEX consistently improves SCC, with the largest gain on CSCC (+0.0582 SCC over TRIPLEX baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPNN outperforms all 13 baselines on SCC across every dataset: by +0.024 over the next-best (MOSBY, 0.314) on BRCA, +0.026 over HE2RNA (0.292) on KIRC, and +0.018 over SRMambaMIL (0.286) on LUAD. On PCC, CPNN ties SRMambaMIL on BRCA (0.304) and beats tRNAformer (0.283) on KIRC by +0.008, but trails MambaMIL (0.280) on LUAD by −0.009. Among MIL baselines, attention- and state-space-based aggregators (AbMIL, ILRA, MambaMIL, SRMambaMIL) consistently outperform simple pooling (Max, Mean) by 0.05–0.10 SCC, yet none match CPNN's prototype-guided regularization. Among MIR baselines, HE2RNA and tRNAformer are the strongest conventional methods, but both remain 0.02–0.03 SCC below CPNN. On patch-level spatial transcriptomics, CPNN integrated into TRIPLEX improves SCC by +0.058 on CSCC and +0.033 on Her2st, while STNet+CPNN gains +0.051 SCC on CSCC, confirming that the prototype module acts as a plug-in regularizer that benefits existing architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in gene expression estimation from pathology images, where prior methods (AbMIL, HE2RNA, tRNAformer, MOSBY, STNet, TRIPLEX) treat observed expression as a monolithic slide- or spot-level signal to be regressed directly from image features. CPNN instead models the underlying data-generation process—observed expression as a mixture of cell-type-specific expression profiles—by extracting cell-type prototypes from public scRNA-seq data and learning compositional weights from histology. Conceptually, it draws on physics-informed neural networks (integrating domain structure into the architecture) and borrows the negative binomial likelihood and deconvolution machinery from cell2location, but repurposes them in reverse: rather than inferring composition from expression (deconvolution), it infers composition from images to predict expression. The MIL/CONCH/CLAM pipeline and LLaVA projector are used as off-the-shelf components, so the architectural novelty lies in the prototype-weight factorization and the modality-correction parameters rather than in the backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical contribution is interpretability: the inferred compositional weights reveal which cell types drive predicted expression, and the biological validation on BRCA subtypes (without subtype supervision) shows these weights align with known biology. This is a capability that purely feature-based regression methods cannot provide. However, the performance gains over strong baselines are modest (1–4 SCC points), and the method requires a matched or closely related single-cell dataset, limiting applicability to tissue types lacking such resources. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes when the scRNA-seq reference comes from a different tissue or patient population, how it scales to very large cell-type taxonomies, and whether the plug-in integration into existing ST architectures (where PCC sometimes drops slightly) can be made more robust across modalities.
