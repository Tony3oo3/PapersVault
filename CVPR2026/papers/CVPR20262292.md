## Problem

Existing medical image segmentation models optimize pixel-level accuracy but cannot reason about lesion location in human-interpretable spatial terms, and current evaluation metrics (Dice, IoU, BLEU, ROUGE) fail to assess whether generated reasoning text correctly localizes the region of interest. This paper addresses both gaps by unifying segmentation with positional reasoning in a single framework and proposing a metric that jointly measures spatial and semantic alignment between text and masks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a medical image (CT, MRI, X-ray, ultrasound, endoscopy, or RGB) paired with a free-form natural language spatial query (e.g., 'Where is the tumor located in the MRI image?') and produces three outputs: a binary segmentation mask, a positional reasoning sentence describing the lesion's location in image-relative terms (e.g., 'bottom left', 'center'), and normalized 2D centroid coordinates in [-1,1]. The text prompts are structured as QA pairs processed by the LLM, and the reasoning output is constrained to be spatially coherent with the predicted mask via the Text2Centroid grounding signal.

## Method

CG-Reasoner integrates four components: a ConvNeXt-Tiny-based vision encoder with multi-scale residual fusion and channel attention; a LLaVA-Med multimodal LLM fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 16) to generate spatially grounded reasoning embeddings; a lightweight decoder that projects prompt tokens into visual feature space and applies FiLM-based conditioning followed by MixFFN and transposed-convolution upsampling to produce the mask; and a Text2Centroid (T2C) module—a frozen Sentence-BERT encoder plus regression head pretrained to map reasoning sentences to normalized 2D centroids. Training uses a unified loss combining Dice+BCE segmentation loss, cross-entropy reasoning loss, and a spatial grounding loss derived from the frozen T2C module, which backpropagates into the LoRA adapter to enforce geometric alignment between linguistic reasoning and visual localization. The T2C module is pretrained in ~10 minutes on a single A100 GPU before being frozen and integrated, keeping the overall system computationally lightweight.

## Results

CG-Reasoner achieves state-of-the-art segmentation on 4 of 6 medical imaging modalities, with headline results of mDice 0.977 / mIoU 0.958 on Lung X-ray, mDice 0.970 / mIoU 0.948 on Lung CT-Scan, mDice 0.819 / mIoU 0.731 on Brain MRI, and mDice 0.904 / mIoU 0.840 on Skin RGB. On the proposed PRScore reasoning metric, CG-Reasoner attains the best scores on 4 of 6 datasets (e.g., 0.847 on Lung CT, 0.755 on Breast Ultrasound), outperforming PRS-Med by up to 0.017. The method uses a lightweight ConvNeXt-Tiny backbone (51M params) yet outperforms heavier foundation models like LISA-13B and G-Dino+SAM-Med2D across nearly all settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CG-Reasoner decisively outperforms G-Dino+SAM-Med2D, LISA-7B/13B, and PixelLM on all six datasets, with margins as large as +0.57 mDice over LISA-7B on Lung X-ray. Against the closest competitor PRS-Med, CG-Reasoner wins on 4 of 6 datasets (Brain MRI +0.016 mDice, Lung CT +0.002, Lung X-ray +0.008, Skin RGB +0.029) but trails on Breast Ultrasound (0.765 vs. 0.817) and Polyp Endoscopy (0.716 vs. 0.843), where PRS-Med retains a clear advantage. BiomedParse is competitive on Lung X-ray (0.972 vs. 0.977) and Skin RGB (0.924 vs. 0.904), where it actually edges out CG-Reasoner on mDice, but collapses on Brain MRI (0.294) and Lung CT (0.088). On the PRScore reasoning metric, CG-Reasoner surpasses PRS-Med on 4/6 modalities (up to +0.017 on Lung CT) and LISA-7B by a wide margin (up to +0.42 on Lung CT), though PRS-Med narrowly leads on Polyp Endoscopy (0.734 vs. 0.732) and Skin RGB (0.735 vs. 0.730).

## Relation to Prior Work

CG-Reasoner builds directly on LLaVA-Med as its multimodal LLM backbone, applying LoRA fine-tuning to adapt it for medical spatial reasoning. It positions itself against the closest prior work, PRS-Med, which first introduced positional reasoning segmentation for medical imaging but relied on binary Yes/No evaluation and lacked explicit spatial grounding. The paper departs from the broader reasoning-segmentation lineage (LISA, PixelLM, SegLLM) by coupling a lightweight ConvNeXt-Tiny encoder-decoder with a Text2Centroid module that predicts lesion centroids from reasoning text, thereby enforcing geometric consistency between language and mask outputs rather than treating them as independent tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PRScore metric addresses a genuine gap in evaluating multimodal segmentation-reasoning systems by jointly measuring semantic fidelity, spatial reasoning, and visual grounding in a single automated score, replacing brittle n-gram metrics and subjective LLM-as-judge approaches. However, the 'state-of-the-art' framing is somewhat overstated: CG-Reasoner does not consistently outperform PRS-Med across all six datasets (losing on Polyp Endoscopy and Skin RGB for reasoning, and on Breast Ultrasound and Polyp Endoscopy for segmentation). Open questions include whether the Text2Centroid spatial grounding generalizes to multi-lesion or 3D volumetric settings, and whether the lightweight design's competitive performance holds as dataset complexity and lesion diversity increase.
