## Problem

VLA pretraining is bottlenecked by the scarcity of large-scale robot data, and human manipulation videos offer a promising alternative but face a quality-variety trade-off: lab-collected datasets provide precise 3D hand tracking in limited scenarios, while in-the-wild videos offer vast diversity without reliable action labels. Existing reconstruction-based latent action methods are inefficient for fine-grained hand motions because latent action quality depends heavily on the forward dynamics model's ability to reconstruct future frames, introducing noise rather than stable grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261945/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the model consumes a first video frame, a text instruction, and (for labeled data) MANO-parameterized hand pose sequences tokenized into 128 discrete tokens per 15-frame chunk via GRQ-VAE; boundary frames from a separate visual encoder (DINOv3 or V-JEPA2) feed the latent action perceiver. During robot deployment, the VLA backbone's 19th-layer hidden states serve as predictive embeddings that condition a DiT-based flow-matching head, which also receives the robot's proprioceptive state and noised action chunks to produce continuous action commands via iterative Euler denoising.

## Method

JALA replaces reconstruction-driven latent action pipelines with joint alignment between two signals: (1) Masked Chunk Prediction (MCP), which uses bidirectional attention within masked motion chunks to shape predictive embeddings from the VLA's intermediate hidden states, and (2) a Latent Action Perceiver (LAP), a Perceiver module that maps boundary-frame features to latent actions via learnable queries, aligned to the predictive embeddings via L1 loss without any pixel reconstruction. A parameter-shared Latent State Perceiver (LSP) injects initial-frame context into the same latent space, and a decoupled asymmetric EMA update (backbone weights flow LSP→LAP, query weights flow LAP→LSP) prevents optimization collapse between the two heterogeneous modules. For unlabeled in-the-wild videos, MCP is simply skipped and only the alignment loss applies, enabling a unified training objective across heterogeneous data. Post-training, the pretrained predictive embeddings are fed as cross-attention conditioning into a flow-matching DiT head for precise robot action generation.

## Results

On the two-view LIBERO benchmark, JALA-dino achieves a 96.9% average success rate, outperforming UniVLA (95.5%), π0 (94.2%), and GR00T N1.5 (93.9%) without any robot-data pretraining. On the harder single-view LIBERO setting, JALA-dino reaches 92.3% average, setting a new SoTA among ≤3B-parameter models and surpassing GR00T N1.5 (92.1%). On real-world multi-step manipulation, JALA-dino achieves 60.0%/58.0% subtask completion on Put-Three-Object (seen/unseen), 83.3%/80.0% on Wipe-Board, and 73.3% on Water-Plant, substantially exceeding GR00T N1.5 (48.0%/28.0%, 56.7%/43.3%, 53.3%) and Being-H0 (38.0%/16.0%, 40.0%/33.3%, 36.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

JALA-dino outperforms all 16 compared baselines on the two-view LIBERO benchmark (96.9% vs. best baseline UniVLA at 95.5%), and on single-view LIBERO it edges out GR00T N1.5 (92.3% vs. 92.1%) while substantially beating Being-H0 (84.9%), OpenVLA (76.5%), and TriVLA (87.0%). Against reconstruction-based latent action methods, JALA consistently wins: it surpasses LAPA (79.5% two-view, 65.7% single-view) and UniVLA-human (88.7% single-view) by large margins, while approaching UniVLA-full (95.2%) which additionally uses large-scale robot data. On RoboCasa and GR1 Tabletop, JALA leads GR00T N1.5 by 6.75/0.25/5.92 points respectively, and outperforms LAPA by 11.33/13.00/14.91 points. On real-world tasks, JALA-dino's advantage is most pronounced under distribution shift: on unseen Put-Three-Object it scores 58.0% vs. 28.0% (GR00T N1.5) and 16.0% (Being-H0), and on unseen Wipe-Board it reaches 80.0% vs. 43.3% and 33.3%. The only near-tie is JALA-vjepa vs. GR00T N1.5 on single-view LIBERO (92.1% vs. 92.1%), but JALA-dino breaks this tie at 92.3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

JALA extends the Being-H0 [39] paradigm of VLA pretraining from human hand videos by replacing the reconstruction-based latent action pipeline (as in LAPA [69] and UniVLA [59]) with a joint alignment objective between VLA predictive embeddings and inverse-dynamics-derived latent actions. Rather than requiring a forward dynamics model to reconstruct future frames—a bottleneck for fine-grained hand motions—JALA uses a Perceiver-based inverse dynamics model to produce latent actions and aligns them directly with intermediate hidden states shaped by masked chunk prediction. The framework also generalizes the data regime from lab-annotated videos to a hybrid setting that incorporates in-the-wild Ego4D clips without hand tracking labels, bridging the quality-variety trade-off that prior methods (lab-only datasets vs. unlabeled video) could not resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that joint-aligned latent actions enable effective pretraining from 7.5M heterogeneous human video samples (including 2.5M unlabeled in-the-wild clips), JALA offers a scalable and computationally efficient pathway to VLA pretraining that avoids the heavy reconstruction overhead of prior latent-action methods. The UniHand-Mix dataset and the alignment technique are likely to become practical resources for the VLA community. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond human hands to other manipulators, whether the 0.5× temporal slowdown for in-the-wild data masks fundamental speed-mismatch issues, and whether the method scales to multi-hand or bimanual settings. The real-world evaluation, while positive, is limited to a single robot platform and three tasks, leaving broader cross-embodiment generalization partially untested.
