## Problem

Existing learning-based lossless compressors face two critical limitations: LLM-based methods require billions of parameters and take minutes to hours per file, making them impractical for deployment, while most approaches are designed for a single modality, forcing redundant compressor deployments in multi-modal systems. No prior work provides a unified, lightweight compressor that handles the structural and statistical heterogeneity across image, text, speech, gene, and database data while maintaining near real-time inference speed.

## Input / Output

Input data is first converted into a fully reversible token sequence: images are split into 16×16×3 patches with each RGB sub-pixel as a 256-vocabulary token, text uses SentencePiece BPE (16K vocab with domain-specific symbols for gene bases and SQL keywords), and speech is read as raw bytes (256-vocab). A modality-specific prefix token (<image>, <text>, <speech>, etc.) is prepended, and modality masking zeros out non-target token probabilities before arithmetic coding. The output is a lossless compressed bitstream produced by arithmetic coding that exactly reconstructs the original data upon decoding.

## Method

OmniZip is built on a lightweight RWKV-7 linear-attention backbone (4.8M–152M parameters) and introduces three modality-aware components: (1) a modality-unified tokenizer that reversibly maps all data types into a shared token space, (2) a mixture-of-experts routing mechanism applied to the V projection in the Time Mixing module, where a learnable router assigns each token to top-2 of 4 V-experts while sharing K and R layers, and (3) a modality-routing feedforward module replacing the standard MLP with 4 small MLP experts (2× hidden factor) selected by the same top-k routing. A reparameterization training strategy adds high-rank branches to R/K/V layers during training that are merged at inference, increasing capacity without extra cost. Training uses a three-stage schedule (freeze feedforward routing → freeze context routing → full training) with FusedAdam, optimizing cross-entropy plus Z-loss and load-balancing regularization terms.

## Results

OmniZip-L (152M params) achieves 0.980 bits/Byte on enwik9, 0.787 on WikiSQL, 1.740 on GenoSeq, and 3.810 on LibriSpeech, representing 62%, 53%, 27%, and 42% improvements over gzip respectively. On image-like data, OmniZip-L reaches 2.273 bits/Byte on CLIC-M and 0.987 on TouchandGo (42% and 57% better than gzip). Even the smallest variant (OmniZip-S, 4.8M params) outperforms gzip by 24–47% across all text-like datasets and 15–42% on image-like datasets, while running at ~1 MB/s on a MacBook CPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against classical general-purpose compressors (gzip, bzip2, zstd), OmniZip-L achieves 62% lower bits/Byte on enwik9 (0.980 vs. 2.590), 53% on WikiSQL (0.787 vs. 1.672), and 42% on LibriSpeech (3.810 vs. 6.511). On image-like data (Table 3), OmniZip-L outperforms the best classical image codecs (JPEG-XL, FLIF) by 15–36% on natural images and 16–57% on medical/tactile data. Among learning-based text compressors, OmniZip-L approaches NNCP (0.853) and CMIX (0.879) on enwik9 with 0.980 bits/Byte, while being 3–4× faster (420 vs. 4–180 KB/s) and supporting all modalities. The LLM-based multi-modal baselines (Llama3-8B, RWKV-7B from [21]) achieve slightly better compression on enwik9 (0.722, 0.774) but are 20–86× slower and 50–1600× larger in parameters. OmniZip-L matches or beats the best modality-specific learning-based methods (e.g., DLPR at 2.160 on CLIC-M vs. OmniZip-L's 2.273; BCM at 3.630 on Coronal vs. OmniZip-L's 3.837) while being the only method that handles all seven modalities in a single model.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniZip builds directly on the RWKV-7 linear-attention backbone and the reparameterization training strategy from L3TC [68], extending both from single-modality text compression to a unified multi-modal setting. It departs from prior multi-modal compressors (e.g., Deletang et al. [21]) that convert all data to ASCII text and rely on billion-parameter LLMs, instead introducing a modality-unified reversible tokenizer and modality-routing MoE mechanisms applied selectively to the V projection and feedforward layers. The architectural choice to route only the V layer—treating K as a shared semantic index and R as a shared memory gate—represents a novel decomposition of the RWKV Time Mixing module for multi-modal adaptivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a 4.8M–152M parameter model can serve as a single unified lossless compressor across seven data types while running at near real-time speeds (~1 MB/s) on consumer edge hardware, eliminating the need for separate per-modality compressor deployments. Open questions include whether the modality-routing mechanism generalizes to truly unseen modalities without retraining, whether the lightweight RWKV backbone can be further scaled to close the remaining gap with LLM-based compressors on natural language (where NNCP/CMIX still lead), and whether the approach extends to higher-dimensional or streaming data. The absence of any dedicated learning-based lossless speech compressor in the literature means the speech results, while promising, lack a strong learned baseline for comparison.
