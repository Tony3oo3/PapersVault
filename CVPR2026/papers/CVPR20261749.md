## Problem

Existing physics-based cooperative HOI frameworks are limited in two ways: they either use fixed-size MLP policies that cannot generalize across team sizes, or they omit explicit agent-to-agent communication entirely, relying only on shared object dynamics as an indirect channel. Additionally, the absence of multi-human reference motion data forces these methods to regularize toward single-human demonstrations, severely restricting the diversity of achievable cooperative behaviors.

## Input / Output

Each agent's observation is expressed entirely in its local coordinate frame and includes a 223-dim proprioceptive vector, the 3D object center, 64 candidate contact points along the table perimeter (ordered nearest-first, then counterclockwise), the two nearest hand-to-object points, the target location, and (n−1)×9-dim teammate cues (x,y position, 6D heading, relative angle around the table center). The policy outputs 28-dim target joint rotations for a PD controller. Critically, the same parameterized policy must produce valid actions for any team size from 2 to 8 agents, with teammate tokens simply varying in count.

## Method

TeamHOI employs a Transformer-based policy network (3 stacks of alternating self- and cross-attention, 2 heads, 512-dim FFN) where the observing agent's tokens attend to variable-length teammate tokens, removing the fixed-size input constraint of MLP policies. Training instantiates parallel environments with different team sizes (2–8 agents) and normalizes PPO advantages per configuration, so a single policy learns to adapt coordination to whatever team it encounters. To overcome the single-human reference motion limitation, a Masked AMP strategy trains two discriminators—one full-body and one that excludes object-interacting body parts (hands, forearms)—and blends their style rewards via a sigmoid of a continuous agent-object interaction indicator, freeing the masked regions to be shaped by task rewards instead. A shape- and team-size-agnostic formation reward combines angular spread (even angular gaps around the table) with principal-axes coverage (support polygon spanning the object's natural stability axes) to guide agents into stable carrying formations without oracle-assigned contact points.

## Results

TeamHOI achieves 99.1% success rate (2 agents), 99.2% (4 agents), and 97.5% (8 agents) on the cooperative carrying task, with mean target distances of 0.06 m, 0.08 m, and 0.18 m respectively. Under a 5× table weight stress test, only TeamHOI demonstrates meaningful cooperation among 8 agents (81.1% SR), while all CooHOI* baselines drop below 15%. The unified policy also maintains high cooperative time ratios (90–96%) and the lowest mean absolute jerk (34.2 m/s³ for 8 agents) across all team sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline family is CooHOI* (an adapted version of CooHOI [7] with masked AMP and a specialized single-agent reward), trained separately for 2, 4, and 8 agents with pre-defined contact-point formations. TeamHOI outperforms all CooHOI* variants on success rate across every team size: e.g., at 8 agents, TeamHOI reaches 97.5% SR versus 10.1% (CooHOI*-2), 61.5% (CooHOI*-4), and 42.2% (CooHOI*-8). The baselines show strong team-size specificity—each variant degrades sharply when evaluated at a different agent count—whereas TeamHOI's single policy generalizes seamlessly. Under the 5× weight stress test, the gap widens dramatically: TeamHOI achieves 81.1% SR with 8 agents while the best baseline (CooHOI*-4) manages only 14.2%. On motion smoothness (|J|), TeamHOI also leads at 8 agents (34.2 vs. 45.2–189.9 m/s³), indicating more stable collective transport. The key qualitative difference is that CooHOI* relies on oracle-assigned contact points and implicit object-dynamics communication, while TeamHOI learns autonomous formation and explicit teammate-state coordination.

## Relation to Prior Work

TeamHOI extends the AMP framework for physics-based character control into a multi-agent cooperative setting, departing from CooHOI's implicit communication through shared object dynamics by introducing explicit teammate tokens in a Transformer-based policy (inspired by TokenHSI). It also addresses the data-scarcity problem inherent in prior cooperative HOI work—where only single-human reference motions are available—by proposing a masked AMP strategy that excludes object-interacting body parts from discriminator supervision, a conceptual cousin of part-wise motion priors (PMP) but applied differently. The formation reward with principal-axes coverage is novel relative to prior cooperative HOI formulations that either assume oracle-assigned contact points or rely solely on angular spread.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to train a single decentralized policy that generalizes across 2–8 agents without retraining is practically important for both multi-robot coordination and multi-character animation, where fixed-size policies are a significant engineering bottleneck. However, the evaluation is limited to a single cooperative carrying task with simplified ball hands in MuJoCo simulation, leaving open questions about generalization to more complex contact-rich interactions, real-world deployment, and substantially larger teams. The masked AMP strategy is a reusable technique that could benefit other HOI tasks suffering from reference-motion scarcity, but its interaction with more diverse object geometries and interaction types remains untested.
