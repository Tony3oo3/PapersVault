## Problem

Existing audio-visual hallucination benchmarks rely on environmental sounds (e.g., dog barking, sirens) as simple event indicators, reducing evaluation to questions like 'Is the dog making sound?' Human speech, by contrast, carries rich semantics (objects, actions, instructions) and complex temporal structures (past, present, future references), yet it remains unexplored whether audio-visual LLMs can correctly align speech content with visual evidence without hallucinating non-existing entities or misplacing events in time.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation sample consists of a synchronized speech-video clip (procedure segments from YouCook2) paired with a text-format question in either two-choice (Yes/No) or three-option multiple-choice format. Models are evaluated zero-shot and produce a single option selection. Two-choice tasks are scored with accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 (treating 'Yes' as positive), while multiple-choice tasks use accuracy; the dataset is option-balanced so random-choice baselines are well-defined (50% for binary, 33.3% for ternary).

## Method

SVHalluc is a 2,405-sample benchmark organized along two complementary dimensions—semantic and temporal hallucination—each probed by three coarse-to-fine diagnostic tasks (GSA, FGSA, CMSB for semantic; TA, TF, CMTB for temporal). The dataset is constructed from YouCook2 by systematically creating controlled misalignments: audio is swapped across videos for global misalignment, GPT identifies visible vs. invisible objects for fine-grained probes, and cross-modal action-object combinations (speech-only action + vision-only object, and vice versa) generate impossible events for binding tests. Temporal samples are built using YouCook2 event annotations to classify narrated events as past, present, or future relative to the speech moment. The paper evaluates six state-of-the-art audio-visual LLMs (Qwen3-Omni, Qwen2.5-Omni, Video-SALMONN, Video-SALMONN-2, VideoLLaMA2, Gemini-2.5-Pro) and conducts ablations—speech recognition (WER), transcript augmentation, single-modality removal, and speech temporal localization (mIoU vs. Silero-VAD)—to isolate that failures stem from cross-modal integration rather than single-modality perception.

## Results

On the SVHalluc benchmark (2,405 video-question pairs), all open-source audio-visual LLMs achieve near-random accuracy on the Global Semantic Alignment (GSA) task (~50–55%) and Temporal Alignment (TA) task (~50–51%), indicating they cannot reliably detect speech-vision misalignment. Gemini-2.5-Pro significantly outperforms all open-source models, reaching 93.10% accuracy on GSA, 85.17% on TA, and 69.25% on CMTB, while the best open-source model (Qwen2.5-Omni) reaches only 77.70% on GSA and 50.27% on TA. On Temporal Forecasting (TF), even Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves only 53.89% accuracy (vs. 33.33% random), while open-source models score 30–33%, showing that temporal reasoning over speech remains a fundamental challenge across all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-2.5-Pro [8] dominates all six tasks, achieving 93.10% on GSA (vs. 50–78% for open-source models) and 85.17% on TA (vs. ~50%), establishing a large gap between commercial and open-source audio-visual LLMs. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-Omni [36] is the strongest on GSA (77.70%) and FGSA (81.08%), while Qwen3-Omni [37] shows the best CMTB performance (61.75%) among open-source models, suggesting improved cross-modal temporal grounding. Video-SALMONN [31] and VideoLLaMA 2 [4] perform at or near random on GSA and TA, and Video-SALMONN is notably weakest on FGSA (49.84%), indicating poor fine-grained object grounding. Video-SALMONN-2 [33] improves over its predecessor on FGSA (79.58%) but remains near-random on GSA (53.92%) and TA (50.00%). Overall, no open-source model exceeds 78% on any task, while Gemini-2.5-Pro exceeds 78% on five of six tasks, highlighting that cross-modal speech-vision integration remains an unsolved problem for open-source audio-visual LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio-visual hallucination evaluation line (AVHBench, DAVE, the curse of multi-modalities) by replacing environmental sounds (dog barking, sirens) as event indicators with human speech content, which carries fundamentally richer semantics and explicit temporal structures (past/present/future references). It repurposes the YouCook2 cooking-video dataset and standard ASR tools (Whisper) to construct controlled speech-vision misalignment samples, but the core conceptual departure is treating speech as a semantic and temporal reasoning challenge rather than a simple event trigger. The benchmark inherits the question-based, option-balanced evaluation format from prior hallucination benchmarks while introducing six coarse-to-fine diagnostic tasks specific to speech.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that state-of-the-art open-source audio-visual LLMs achieve near-random accuracy on speech-vision alignment, despite strong single-modality performance (WER 0.09, temporal mIoU 0.88), pinpointing cross-modality integration as the specific bottleneck. The large gap with Gemini-2.5-Pro (93% vs. ~55% on GSA) highlights a significant open-source limitation that prior environmental-sound benchmarks could not expose. Open questions include whether targeted cross-modality training data or architectural changes can close this gap, and whether the findings generalize beyond cooking-domain videos to more diverse speech-vision scenarios.
