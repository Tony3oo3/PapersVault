## Problem

EHT black hole images are static and resolution-limited (~20 μas), yet the plasma dynamics they encode are essential for inferring black hole properties like spin and inclination. GRMHD simulations that model these dynamics are computationally prohibitive (weeks per run on supercomputers), making parameter-space exploration infeasible. Existing neural surrogates for fluid dynamics fail on this task because they cannot simultaneously super-resolve blurry inputs and maintain stable autoregressive forecasts over the long horizons (300 GM/c³) required for accurate physical measurements.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single blurry image at EHT resolution (~20 μas beam, 100×100 pixels) as input and autoregressively produces a sequence of high-resolution (0.5 μas) forecast frames at 5 GM/c³ time resolution. From these frames, cylinder plots sampled at the critical impact parameter radius (√27 GM/c²) yield four quantitative plasma features: pattern speed, rotation curve slope, pitch angle, and asymmetry. These features feed an XGBoost classifier that outputs discrete black hole parameters—spin (prograde vs. retrograde) and inclination (four classes: negative/positive × face-on/edge-on).

## Method

BHCAST uses a U-Net as a next-frame dynamics surrogate trained with a multi-scale Laplacian pyramid loss that supervises at four spatial scales (k=0,1,2) plus a mean-flux term replacing the coarsest pyramid level. The key insight is that GRMHD simulation frames form a global attractor: the trained model projects blurry, off-manifold inputs back onto the physically-plausible state manifold, achieving super-resolution as a byproduct of dynamics learning. The mean-flux constraint is critical for long-term stability, as ablation shows the multi-scale loss without it diverges after ~20 steps, while the full loss remains stable over 100 autoregressive steps (500 GM/c³). Downstream, cylinder plots extracted from the forecasted movie provide four plasma features that serve as an interpretable information bottleneck, and XGBoost performs spin/inclination classification with quantitative feature importance scores and bootstrapped uncertainty quantification.

## Results

On Sgr A* GRMHD simulations, BHCAST achieves 56.41% inclination and 69.22% spin classification accuracy at standard EHT resolution (20 μas), outperforming the ResNet50 baseline (47.19% and 67.66%). Under increased blurring (30 μas), BHCAST degrades gracefully to 53.44% and 65.78%, while ResNet collapses to 25.47% and 44.37%. For plasma feature extraction, BHCAST achieves the lowest MAE on pattern speed (0.46 vs. 0.64 for ResNet) and comparable accuracy on pitch angle and rotation curve slope. On real EHT M87* images, BHCAST correctly predicts counterclockwise rotation and resolves the ring structure without any M87*-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BHCAST is compared against three types of baselines. Against the learning-based forecasting baseline (EDSR super-resolution + ConvLSTM dynamics), BHCAST clearly wins: the EDSR+ConvLSTM pipeline diverges after only a few autoregressive steps, while BHCAST maintains low LPIPS over 100 forecast steps. Against the oracle non-learning baseline (Wiener deconvolution + ground-truth optical flow), BHCAST achieves comparable or better super-resolution (matching ground-truth PSD by step 6) without requiring access to true optical flow. For plasma feature extraction (Table 1), BHCAST significantly outperforms four separate ResNet50 regressors on pattern speed (MAE 0.46 vs. 0.64) and ties on pitch angle (0.13 vs. 0.14) and rotation curve slope (0.24 vs. 0.25), though it is slightly worse on asymmetry (0.30 vs. 0.23). For end-to-end parameter inference (Table 2), BHCAST beats ResNet50 on inclination at all blur levels (56.41% vs. 47.19% at 20 μas) and on spin at 20 and 25 μas, with the most dramatic advantage under distribution shift: at 30 μas blurring, BHCAST retains 65.78% spin accuracy while ResNet drops to 44.37%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three distinct research threads: neural surrogates for fluid dynamics (departing from Fourier Neural Operators in favor of a data-efficient U-Net suited to local turbulence), multi-scale Laplacian pyramid losses from classical computer vision (repurposed here with a physics-motivated mean-flux term), and black hole parameter estimation from EHT data (departing from direct black-box image-to-parameter mapping by inserting an explicit forecasting stage). The key conceptual departure is recasting a traditionally static inverse problem—inferring black hole properties from a single EHT image—as a sequential forecasting-then-inference pipeline, where super-resolution emerges as a byproduct of projecting blurry inputs onto the GRMHD attractor manifold rather than being solved as a separate task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular forecasting-then-inference paradigm offers a general recipe for resolution-limited scientific imaging problems where dynamics encode physical information invisible in a single frame, and the attractor-based super-resolution mechanism provides a principled explanation for why dynamics forecasting can recover high-frequency detail. Limitations include evaluation on only two black hole systems with discrete parameter values, a classification head restricted to binary spin and four-class inclination, and reliance on GRMHD simulations whose fidelity to real accretion flows remains uncertain. Open questions include scaling to continuous parameter spaces, handling the full multi-day M87* movie campaign, and whether the attractor-projection super-resolution generalizes to other dissipative systems beyond black hole plasmas.
