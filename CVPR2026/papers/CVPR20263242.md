## Problem

Existing Distributed Image Compression (DIC) methods at extremely low bitrates (< 0.1 bpp) rely solely on visual side information fused via cross-attention, which cannot recover object-level details lost under severe quantization and tends to produce locally blurred reconstructions by averaging correlated cues. The core gap is the inability to exploit global semantic context and fine-grained object details from side information in a way that preserves perceptual quality rather than merely pixel-wise fidelity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a lossy-compressed primary image (encoded through a frozen VAE, refined by a HyperAlign module, and quantized via VQ-VAE into a transmitted bitstream) and a losslessly transmitted correlated view available only at the decoder. The side information is consumed in two forms: visual latent features (both quantized and unquantized) and textual descriptions generated by BLIP-2 and encoded by CLIP. The output is a single reconstructed RGB image at 128×256 resolution, produced by iterative denoising in the LDM latent space followed by VAE decoding.

## Method

MDIC builds a diffusion-based decoder on a pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model, conditioning the denoising process on three multimodal side signals: fine-grained visual details (z_d), clustered category features (z_xc), and global textual semantics (z_text). A Visual Mask Generation Module in Text Supervision (VMGM-TS) produces a spatial mask via Gumbel-Sigmoid sampling, supervised by an object-level masked-text prediction task that aligns visual regions with object nouns in BLIP-2 captions. This mask gates two pathways in the Mask-Gated Side Information Supplementary Module: one applies it to VQ-VAE-quantized side features to restore category information lost in extreme compression, and the other applies it to unquantized side features to extract fine-grained object details while suppressing redundant multi-view discrepancies. The overall training combines a diffusion noise-prediction loss, VQ-VAE commitment loss, a Transformer-based autoregressive entropy model for rate estimation, and a cross-entropy mask auxiliary loss, all optimized jointly with a frozen LDM backbone.

## Results

MDIC achieves the best performance across all perceptual quality metrics (LPIPS, FID, DISTS, KID, NIQE) on both KITTI Stereo and Cityscapes at extremely low bitrates (<0.1 bpp), significantly surpassing all prior DIC and SIC methods. On distortion metrics, MDIC attains the highest MS-SSIM and mIoU on KITTI Stereo, though it reports a lower PSNR than distortion-oriented DIC methods (NDIC, ATN, LDMIC) while maintaining comparable mIoU to the best of them. MDIC's distortion metrics also significantly outperform the diffusion-based LIC baselines (Perco, DiffEIC, RDEIC), demonstrating superior semantic consistency. Ablation studies confirm that removing both side-information streams (z_d and ẑ_xc) degrades LPIPS by 0.355 and PSNR by 8.64 dB, while removing the mask-gating mechanism (G1+G2) reduces LPIPS by 0.044 and PSNR by 0.60 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three DIC baselines (NDIC, ATN, LDMIC), MDIC wins decisively on all perceptual metrics (LPIPS, FID, DISTS, KID, NIQE) while trading some PSNR for substantially better MS-SSIM and mIoU, reflecting its perceptual-optimization focus. Against the four joint-coding SIC baselines (SASIC, ECSIC, CAMSIC, BiSIC), MDIC also achieves superior perceptual quality, whereas BiSIC in particular introduces local distortions and artifact patterns that MDIC avoids. Against the three diffusion-based LIC baselines (Perco, DiffEIC, RDEIC), MDIC wins on distortion metrics (PSNR, MS-SSIM, mIoU) because it leverages side information for fine-grained alignment, while these LIC methods—lacking side information—produce locally inconsistent semantics and local distortions despite competitive perceptual scores. Overall, MDIC is the only method that simultaneously leads on both perceptual and semantic-consistency metrics, with its main trade-off being a modest PSNR reduction relative to pixel-fidelity-oriented DIC methods at the same bitrate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines: VAE-based Distributed Image Compression (NDIC, ATN, LDMIC) that fuses visual side information via cross-attention, and diffusion-based Learned Image Compression (Perco, DiffEIC, RDEIC) that achieves perceptual realism at ultra-low bitrates. It departs from both by being the first to introduce textual side information (via BLIP-2 captioning + CLIP encoding) into the DIC paradigm and by replacing the VAE decoder with a pre-trained Latent Diffusion Model conditioned on multimodal cues. The HyperAlign module and rate-distortion formulation are directly adapted from Perco, while the Slepian-Wolf asymmetric coding structure follows the standard DIC framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that object-level textual semantics can serve as effective side information alongside visual cues, the paper opens a new axis for DIC research beyond purely pixel/feature-level correlation. The mask-gated mechanism provides a principled way to separate category-level and fine-grained detail recovery, which could inform other side-information-assisted reconstruction tasks. However, evaluation is limited to two driving-scene datasets (KITTI, Cityscapes) with a vocabulary of only 14 object nouns, and the 10-step diffusion inference adds latency concerns for real-time multi-view applications. The authors themselves flag stronger multi-view correlation modeling and unified pre-trained coders as future directions, leaving open whether the multimodal advantage generalizes to diverse, non-driving scenes.
