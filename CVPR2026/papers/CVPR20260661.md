## Problem

Joint optimization of 3D Gaussian Splatting and camera poses from RGB images alone is unstable because Gaussian primitive positions and counts change dynamically during training, causing discontinuous pose gradients. Unlike NeRF-based methods that leverage fixed MLP parameters and positional encoding for coarse-to-fine alignment, rasterization-based 3DGS lacks both a stable parameter set and a controllable spatial sampling mechanism, making direct photometric alignment prone to local minima.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260661/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method requires only multi-view RGB images and imperfect initial camera poses (e.g., from a rough SfM estimate with added noise); no depth maps, stereo models, sequential frame ordering, or feature correspondences are needed. It produces a fully optimized 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (positions, covariances, opacities, SH coefficients) together with refined camera extrinsic poses. The initial pose noise is randomly generated and identical across all compared methods for fair evaluation.

## Method

Energy-GS introduces two key mechanisms. First, it redesigns the pose gradient flow by freezing Gaussian primitive positions (making them non-learnable) during the pose-alignment phase and fixing the 3σ projection threshold so that a stable set of primitives contributes to each rendered tile; densification (cloning/splitting) is delayed until the energy level of the supervision signal exceeds a threshold L. Second, it performs an SVD-based energy decomposition of ground-truth images and applies a smooth mask ω(α) that progressively increases the number of retained singular values during training, forcing 3DGS to align poses from low-frequency (coarse) to high-frequency (fine) image content. The photometric loss is computed between the rendered image and this energy-masked target, and the lv=1 component is always fully preserved to avoid early-stage pose divergence.

## Results

On the NeRF Synthetic dataset, Energy-GS achieves the best rendering quality on 6 of 8 scenes (e.g., 32.90 PSNR on hotdog, 30.35 on lego) and the best pose estimation on all 8 scenes, with rotation errors as low as 0.037° (lego) and ATE as low as 0.002 m (hotdog, lego). On Mip-NeRF 360, Energy-GS matches or exceeds 3R-GS on most scenes for both rendering and pose accuracy, while dramatically outperforming CF-GS and SC-NeRF (which fail to converge, e.g., CF-GS achieves only 2.57 PSNR on hotdog). The ablation (Table 4) shows the full pipeline reaches 24.12 PSNR and 1.065° rotation error, versus 8.08 PSNR and 8.572° for vanilla 3DGS with learnable poses.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Energy-GS outperforms BARF on rendering for 6 of 8 synthetic scenes (e.g., +3.43 dB PSNR on lego, +1.00 dB on hotdog) and achieves substantially better pose accuracy (e.g., 0.037° rotation vs. 0.381° on lego; 0.002 m ATE vs. 0.023 m). Against 3R-GS, the closest 3DGS-based competitor, Energy-GS wins on rendering for 6/8 scenes and on ATE for all 8 scenes, though 3R-GS retains a slight edge on rotation for bicycle (1.489° vs. 1.824°) and stump (1.267° vs. 3.473°). CF-GS and SC-NeRF fail catastrophically on most scenes (CF-GS drops to 2.57 PSNR on hotdog; SC-NeRF stays below 18 PSNR everywhere), confirming that sequential or feature-based priors are insufficient without the energy-guided strategy. The original 3DGS with naive pose gradients also underperforms (10–16 PSNR range), validating the need for the redesigned gradient flow. Overall, Energy-GS is the only method that simultaneously achieves competitive rendering and best-in-class pose accuracy across both datasets using only RGB supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

Energy-GS extends the NeRF-based joint pose optimization lineage (BARF, SC-NeRF, MC-NeRF) to the 3D Gaussian Splatting setting, where prior methods (CF-GS, PCR-GS, 3R-GS, ISplat) have required additional priors such as stereo models, sequential frame consistency, or depth cues. The key conceptual departure is replacing NeRF's positional-encoding frequency control with an SVD-based image energy decomposition to achieve coarse-to-fine alignment in a rasterization-based pipeline that lacks spatial sampling. Additionally, the paper departs from standard 3DGS by fixing Gaussian primitive positions and deferring densification, a design choice motivated by the observation that the dynamically changing primitive set destabilizes pose gradients in a way that has no NeRF analogue.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By requiring only RGB images, Energy-GS removes the dependency on stereo models or sequential structure that limits the generality of existing 3DGS pose-refinement methods, making it more directly applicable to SLAM and visual localization pipelines. The SVD energy decomposition offers a general mechanism for coarse-to-fine supervision in any rasterization-based renderer, potentially transferable beyond 3DGS. However, the method is validated only on synthetic and Mip-NeRF 360 scenes, and the two-phase training (pose alignment followed by densification) introduces a hyperparameter (energy threshold L) that may be sensitive to scene complexity; the 'multi-shell' failure mode is acknowledged but not fully resolved, leaving open whether the approach scales to large, unstructured real-world captures.
