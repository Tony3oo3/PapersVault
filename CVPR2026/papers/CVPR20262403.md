## Problem

Previous human-human interaction generation methods either omit detailed hand motions due to their high dimensionality or generate entire motion sequences in a single forward pass, failing to capture the reactive, temporally adaptive nature of real interactions. This limits both the expressivity of generated motions and the model's ability to respond to evolving interaction dynamics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262403/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a free-form text prompt describing the interaction and produces full-body SMPL-X parameters for two individuals, decomposed into root translation (R³), root rotation (R⁶), 21 body joint rotations (R^{21×6}), and 30 hand joint rotations (R^{30×6}) using continuous 6D rotation representations. In autoregressive mode, the denoiser outputs sub-motions of n frames per step conditioned on a mixed memory buffer, requiring only 10 diffusion steps per sub-motion versus 1000 steps (DDIM-50) for the non-autoregressive variant. Body shapes are normalized to a neutral configuration due to limited shape diversity in the Inter-X dataset.

## Method

Interact2Ar builds on the cooperative denoiser architecture from InterGen, where parallel transformer streams with shared weights process each individual and exchange information via cross-attention, but extends it with an initial encoder that maps noised motion to a latent representation feeding three specialized denoising heads for global trajectory, body poses, and hand poses in parallel. The autoregressive pipeline decomposes the full interaction into contiguous sub-motions and generates them sequentially, with each step conditioned on a Mixed Memory buffer that concatenates a short-term component (m_s most recent frames at full framerate) and a long-term component (frames downsampled at interval δ over a window of m_l frames), achieving a 60-frame effective context with only 24 stored frames. Training uses a combined loss of L2 representation error, root orientation penalty, and four kinematic losses (joint positions, velocities, foot contacts, pairwise inter-person distances) computed through a differentiable forward kinematics layer, with the model directly predicting x̂⁰ at each denoising step to enable these losses.

## Results

On the Inter-X benchmark (Full-body evaluation), the autoregressive Interact2Ar achieves R-Precision Top-1 of 0.453, FID of 0.277, and MM Distance of 3.095, surpassing the previous SOTA InterMask (0.415, 0.671, 3.487) on every metric. The autoregressive variant also dramatically improves transition smoothness, reducing Peak Jerk from 2.328 (InterMask) to 0.136 and Area Under Jerk from 61.74 to 8.837, approaching ground-truth levels (PJ=0.021, AUJ=3.944). The non-autoregressive variant (Interact2Ar*) already beats InterMask on R-Precision and FID, confirming that the cooperative denoiser with body-part heads is the primary driver of quality gains, while autoregression further improves alignment and adaptability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Interact2Ar (autoregressive) outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric in the Full-body evaluation. Against the previous SOTA InterMask [19], it improves R-Precision Top-1 by 0.038 (0.453 vs 0.415), reduces FID by 59% (0.277 vs 0.671), and cuts AUJ by 86% (8.837 vs 61.74), indicating far smoother transitions. Against InterGen [23], the gains are even larger: FID drops from 0.874 to 0.277 and AUJ from 84.16 to 8.837. T2M [13], a single-person text-to-motion model, serves as a weak baseline and is dominated on all metrics (FID 9.079 vs 0.277). The non-autoregressive variant already surpasses InterMask on R-Precision and FID, isolating the contribution of the cooperative denoiser with body-part heads, while the autoregressive pipeline adds further gains in text alignment (MModality 1.427 vs 2.743) and transition smoothness (PJ 0.136 vs 2.110).

## Relation to Prior Work

Interact2Ar extends the cooperative denoiser architecture from InterGen [23] by adding body-part specialized heads (trajectory, body, hands) that operate in parallel over a shared latent encoding, and adopts the direct x̂⁰ prediction strategy from HMDM [44] to enable kinematic losses. Its autoregressive pipeline draws on single-human autoregressive diffusion work (CLoSD [45], MotionStreamer [49]) but is the first to apply this paradigm to multi-person interaction generation. The Mixed Memory strategy is novel, combining full-frame-rate short-term context with temporally downsampled long-term history to avoid the repetitive-motion artifacts that plague fixed-window autoregressive models. The paper also departs from prior interaction generation by using non-redundant SMPL-X parameters exclusively, motivated by findings that redundant representations bias diffusion evaluation [28].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses two persistent gaps in interaction generation: the omission of detailed hand kinematics (due to dimensionality overhead) and the inability of holistic diffusion models to capture the reactive, temporally adaptive nature of real interactions. The autoregressive + Mixed Memory combination enables practical downstream applications (temporal composition, disturbance adaptation, sequential multi-person) that were previously infeasible, broadening the utility of interaction models beyond single-shot generation. Key limitations remain: body shape diversity is absent (all shapes normalized to neutral), which limits hand-contact precision, and the evaluation relies on a single dataset (Inter-X, 11K interactions). Open questions include whether the Mixed Memory paradigm generalizes to longer-horizon or physically grounded interactions, and whether the specialized-head architecture scales beyond dyadic to truly simultaneous multi-person settings.
