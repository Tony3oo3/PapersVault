## Problem

Visuomotor imitation learning for robot manipulation is constrained by the cost of collecting diverse human demonstrations and poor generalization to unseen objects. Existing synthetic data generators (DemoGen, CPGen) only augment spatial configurations of a single source object, while affordance-based methods (FUNCTO, Robo-ABC) rely on open-loop planning and lack the reactive robustness of learned policies. No prior work systematically integrates affordance semantic correspondence into a data-generation pipeline that produces training data for closed-loop policy learning across object categories.

## Input / Output

The method takes a minimal set of expert demonstrations (as few as 1), a library of 3D meshes (from PAM or Hunyuan3D), and RGB-D point clouds segmented via SAM2 into robot/object/goal/others categories and downsampled with FPS. It produces 1000 diverse manipulation trajectories per task with full 6D poses across novel object instances, where each trajectory is composed of a replayed grasp segment, a replayed skill segment, and a planned transition segment. The generated trajectories are paired with hybrid real-simulated point clouds (real background with simulated robot and object) to train a closed-loop visuomotor policy that outputs end-effector actions conditioned on point cloud observations and proprioception.

## Method

AffordGen repurposes affordance correspondence as a generative prior for data synthesis rather than for online planning. It first extracts the affording point (gripper-object contact) and function point (task-relevant interaction location) from the source demonstration, then establishes 3D semantic correspondence to target meshes by normalizing all meshes into a canonical space via a 6D pose estimator, performing parallel multi-view rendering, and matching DINOv2 feature embeddings through cosine similarity with weighted averaging. Grasp and skill segments are transferred via keypoint-constrained trajectory replay: the end-effector trajectory is normalized into the source object's local frame, shifted by the keypoint displacement, and re-expressed in the world frame for an arbitrary new 6D pose of the target mesh. Transition segments are filled by motion planning or spherical linear interpolation, and point clouds are generated by rendering the robot and manipulated object in simulation and compositing them into the real background. A closed-loop visuomotor policy is then trained end-to-end on this large-scale, affordance-aware dataset, inheriting both the semantic generalizability of affordances and the reactive robustness of imitation learning.

## Results

On simulation tasks, AffordGen (100×10 configuration) achieves an average 24.1% improvement over the best baseline on unseen object tests across four manipulation tasks (teapot pouring, mug hanging, knife cutting, shoe organizing). In real-world experiments, AffordGen (100×10) reaches 74/162, 107/162, 96/108, and 45/60 success counts on unseen objects for the four tasks, outperforming all baselines. For zero-shot cross-category generalization, AffordGen is the only method achieving meaningful success rates (e.g., 83.07% on mug-to-handbag hanging in simulation, 7/12 in real-world), while DemoGen and CPGen remain near zero.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DemoGen, AffordGen dramatically outperforms on unseen objects in every task (e.g., 74/162 vs. 2/162 on teapot pouring, 107/162 vs. 74/162 on mug hanging), while remaining comparable on the source object, confirming that AffordGen preserves spatial generalization while adding shape diversity. Against CPGen, AffordGen wins on most unseen tests (74/162 vs. 15/162 teapot, 107/162 vs. 69/162 mug, 45/60 vs. 30/60 shoe), though CPGen is competitive on knife cutting unseen (88/108 vs. 96/108). Against FUNCTO (the planning-based affordance method), AffordGen achieves substantially higher success rates, particularly on mug hanging where FUNCTO drops to 7/27 source and 48/162 unseen due to keypoint occlusion, while AffordGen reaches 19/27 and 107/162. In cross-category settings (Table 3), AffordGen is the only method producing non-trivial success rates (e.g., 55% teapot-to-mug, 83% mug-to-handbag in simulation), whereas both DemoGen and CPGen remain below 3%, demonstrating that geometric augmentation alone cannot bridge semantic gaps between object categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

AffordGen sits at the intersection of two prior lines of work: (1) synthetic trajectory generation methods like DemoGen and CPGen, which augment spatial diversity for a single object but cannot generalize to new shapes, and (2) affordance-based semantic correspondence methods like Robo-ABC, DenseMatcher, and FUNCTO, which transfer manipulation knowledge across objects but rely on open-loop planning. The key conceptual departure is repurposing affordance correspondence—previously a signal for online planning—as an offline generative prior that produces large-scale, semantically grounded training data, after which a standard closed-loop visuomotor policy is learned end-to-end. This bridges the gap between the semantic generalizability of affordance methods and the reactive robustness of imitation learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path to data-efficient robot learning: a handful of human demonstrations can be scaled into thousands of trajectories across hundreds of novel meshes, enabling zero-shot cross-category generalization (e.g., teapot→mug pouring) without additional real-world data collection. The finding that generation quality peaks at an intermediate number of target meshes before degrading provides a useful empirical guideline for cross-instance data generation. Open questions include how the approach handles objects without well-defined affordance or function points, whether the hybrid real-sim point cloud pipeline introduces subtle sim-to-real artifacts at scale, and whether the method extends to multi-object or long-horizon tasks beyond the four evaluated.
