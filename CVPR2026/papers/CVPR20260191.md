## Problem

Existing 3D face representations face a fundamental trade-off: 3DMMs offer interpretability but are limited by fixed topology and low-dimensional parameter spaces; neural shape models add expressivity but lose localized control; implicit representations capture fine detail but lack semantic correspondence and require expensive isosurface extraction. No prior representation combines the local support property of B-splines with the high-frequency expressivity of learned features while guaranteeing dense semantic correspondence in the output mesh.

## Input / Output

The method accepts unstructured 3D point clouds (centered at origin, optionally with 10-band Fourier position embeddings, tokenized into s tokens) or monocular images (patchified into tokens) and produces an m×m×m lattice of d-dimensional CUBE control features. Querying the B-spline volume at fixed parametric coordinates derived from a normalized template mesh yields a 3D face mesh with the same topology as the template, ensuring dense semantic correspondence. The B-spline basis values are precomputed at initialization since they depend only on query coordinates and control-point indices, making volume evaluation extremely fast during training.

## Method

CUBE replaces the 3D control points of a standard NURBS volume with a lattice of high-dimensional (d ≫ 3) learnable control features, defining a continuous mapping from a 3D parametric domain to 3D Euclidean space via an intermediate feature space. Decoding is two-stage: B-spline interpolation of the control features yields a d-dimensional vector whose first three dimensions form a coarse base mesh, and a lightweight 4-layer MLP processes the full feature vector to predict a residual displacement that captures high-frequency geometric details. A ViT-based transformer encoder with XCiT attention predicts the control features by concatenating trainable control tokens with tokenized input (scan vertices or image patches) and extracting the control-token embeddings at the output. The model is trained end-to-end with an L1 loss on both the base mesh and the final refined mesh, and the B-spline local support property is preserved by design, enabling direct local editing by modifying individual control features without any locality-inducing losses.

## Results

On a test set of 18,000 real multi-view scans, CUBE-L (16³ control points, d=1024) with residual MLP achieves a point-to-scan (PTS) distance of 0.95 mm (median 0.71 mm) and a vertex-to-vertex (V2V) distance of 1.69 mm (median 1.45 mm), approaching the ground-truth registration PTS of 0.60 mm. CUBE-M achieves 1.11 mm PTS and 1.99 mm V2V, while CUBE-S reaches 1.17 mm PTS and 2.13 mm V2V. The ablation (Table 1) confirms that the residual MLP consistently improves the base B-spline shape across all encoder sizes and control-point counts, with the largest gains when fewer control points are used (e.g., 4³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUBE-L with residuals substantially outperforms both baselines on the real-scan test set. Against BPS [38], CUBE-L reduces PTS from 4.66–5.03 mm to 0.95 mm (roughly 5× improvement) and V2V from 14.8–17.8 mm to 1.69 mm; the paper notes BPS is sensitive to scan orientation and can collapse to an average-face prediction for certain orientations. Against TEMPEH [6] (the multi-view baseline), CUBE-L improves PTS from 1.13 mm to 0.95 mm and V2V from 2.27 mm to 1.69 mm, while also producing lower standard deviations (0.82 vs 0.98 mm PTS; 1.02 vs 1.27 mm V2V), indicating more consistent predictions. CUBE-S is roughly on par with TEMPEH-Refined on PTS (1.17 vs 1.13 mm) but still leads on V2V (2.13 vs 2.27 mm). The residual MLP contributes a consistent 0.05–0.15 mm PTS gain over the base B-spline shape at every model size, confirming that the learned high-frequency details complement the linear B-spline interpolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

CUBE generalizes classical NURBS volumes (Wu et al. 2000; Li 2022), which map a parametric domain to 3D coordinates or scalar fields via 3D control points, by replacing those control points with high-dimensional learned feature vectors and appending a lightweight residual MLP. It also extends the free-form deformation framework of Jung et al. (2021), which regressed 3D face shapes from images using a trivariate B-spline lattice, by introducing the high-dimensional control lattice and the two-stage base-plus-residual decoding. Unlike implicit neural face models (e.g., ImFace, NPGA) that require isosurface extraction and lack inherent semantic correspondence, CUBE is queried at fixed template-mesh coordinates, guaranteeing dense correspondence while retaining the local-support property of B-splines for interpretable editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CUBE offers a practical middle ground between the interpretability and local editability of B-spline/CAD representations and the expressivity of learned models, enabling applications like expression transfer, control interpolation, and per-region shape editing without auxiliary locality losses. The state-of-the-art scan registration results (CUBE-L: 0.95 mm PTS vs. 4.66–5.03 mm for BPS) demonstrate that a relatively simple B-spline-plus-MLP decoder can outperform more complex volumetric-convolution pipelines. Open questions include whether the fixed template-mesh requirement limits generalization to topologically different geometries, how the representation scales to full-body or non-rigid deformations, and whether the synthetic-data training protocol transfers robustly to diverse real-world capture conditions beyond the two public datasets tested.
