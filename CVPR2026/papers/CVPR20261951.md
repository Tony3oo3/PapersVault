## Problem

Current image super-resolution methods distort text glyphs (especially complex Chinese characters) while text-focused methods produce style inconsistencies with backgrounds, creating a persistent trade-off between readability and overall image quality. Additionally, existing scene text SR datasets are limited to mild degradation (≤×4), failing to capture real-world long-distance scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261951/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution scene text image as input, where an OCR detector first localizes text regions and extracts their content as semantic conditions. Stage 1 operates on individually cropped text regions in VAE latent space, while Stage 2 processes the full image. The output is a high-resolution super-resolved image with coherent text and background, evaluated at zoom factors up to ×14.29 on the UZ-ST benchmark.

## Method

TiGeSR decouples glyph restoration from image enhancement in a two-stage 'text-first, image-later' paradigm. Stage 1 uses a diffusion-based UNet with dual output branches (RGB appearance and binary mask) to reconstruct fine-grained stroke geometry from cropped text regions, guided by OCR-predicted text via cross-attention; it employs a two-phase training strategy (joint synthetic+real, then synthetic-only refinement) with a combined diffusion and segmentation-oriented loss (MSE+Focal+Dice). Stage 2 uses a ControlNet network built on Stable Diffusion 3.5 that conditions full-image denoising on the recovered text mask at a fixed timestep with null-text embedding, trained with MSE+LPIPS reconstruction loss plus a Sobel edge loss to preserve stroke boundaries. This injection of structure-aware control into the generative process allows global image quality enhancement while maintaining glyph fidelity.

## Results

On Real-CE (×4 degradation), TiGeSR achieves the best scores across all six metrics: PSNR 24.12, SSIM 0.839, LPIPS 0.164, DISTS 0.125, FID 38.72, and OCR-A 67.3%. On the more challenging UZ-ST benchmark (up to ×14.29 zoom), TiGeSR leads with PSNR 25.48, SSIM 0.830, LPIPS 0.196, DISTS 0.156, FID 20.01, and OCR-A 43.0%. Notably, TiGeSR is the only method that improves OCR accuracy over the LR input (ΔOCR-A = +2.5% on Real-CE, +1.3% on UZ-ST), while all baselines degrade text readability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TiGeSR outperforms all 12 baselines on every metric across both benchmarks. The most significant advantage is in text accuracy (OCR-A): on Real-CE, TiGeSR reaches 67.3% versus TADiSR's 64.7% (the closest competitor) and HAT's 56.6%; on UZ-ST, the gap widens dramatically to 43.0% vs. HAT's 37.9% and TADiSR's 36.6%. Among general image SR methods, HAT and OSEDiff achieve the best pixel-level fidelity (PSNR/SSIM) but sacrifice text readability, with OCR-A scores of 56.6% and 26.8% on Real-CE respectively. Text-focused methods (MARCONet, DiffTSR) improve glyph structure but produce poor full-image quality and style inconsistencies, scoring below 55% OCR-A on Real-CE. TADiSR, the most directly comparable text-aware method, is limited by cross-attention resolution and degrades under severe degradation (UZ-ST OCR-A drops to 36.6%). TiGeSR's two-stage decoupling consistently yields the best trade-off, being the only method that simultaneously leads in both image quality (FID 38.72/20.01) and text accuracy on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

TiGeSR sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: general real-world image super-resolution (Real-ESRGAN, DiffBIR, OSEDiff, DiT4SR) and text-specific super-resolution (MARCONet, DiffTSR, TADiSR). It builds directly on Latent Diffusion Models for the glyph restoration stage, ControlNet for conditional guidance in the enhancement stage, and Stable Diffusion 3.5 as the base generative backbone. The key conceptual departure is the explicit two-stage decoupling—restoring text structure first, then using it as structural conditioning for full-image SR—whereas prior methods either ignore text structure in a single generative pass or restore text in isolation without global background coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UZ-ST benchmark (×14.29 max zoom, 5,036 pairs, 49,675 annotated text lines) fills a critical gap: no prior scene text SR dataset tested beyond ×4 degradation, leaving models unvalidated for realistic long-distance capture. The two-stage paradigm offers a practical recipe for any structured-content SR task where a subset of pixels demands geometric precision. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other structured content (math formulas, diagrams, handwriting), whether the OCR dependency can be further reduced, and whether the cascade alignment strategy scales to larger, more diverse collections without heavy manual filtering.
