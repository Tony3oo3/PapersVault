## Problem

Existing contrastive decoding methods for multimodal hallucination mitigation (VCD, AVCD) are modality-agnostic: they apply uniform distortions across all modalities without considering which modality is actually relevant to the given task. This static approach cannot dynamically suppress cross-modal interference, where one modality inappropriately corrupts generation about another (e.g., visual cues causing fabricated audio descriptions). The paper addresses this gap by making contrastive decoding task-aware through explicit modality self-assessment.

## Input / Output

The method operates at inference time on an audio-visual LLM, consuming a video sequence, an audio waveform, and a textual question. Before generation, a fixed modality query prompt is appended to the input to elicit the model's self-assessment of which modalities are needed, from which three scalar weights (w_video, w_audio, w_both) are extracted via softmax over the corresponding token logits. During autoregressive decoding, four forward passes per step are required—one for each modality configuration (clean/perturbed video × clean/perturbed audio)—to compute the contrastive logit branches, making the method computationally heavier than standard decoding but still training-free.

## Method

MAD introduces a two-stage training-free decoding strategy. First, the model self-assesses modality relevance by answering a fixed query prompt, and the logits for 'video', 'audio', and 'both' tokens are softmax-normalized into task-specific weights (w_v, w_a, w_av). Second, these weights modulate a four-branch contrastive decoding formulation where each branch targets a specific modality configuration, and the contrastive strength for modality m is set to α_m = γ · w_m (with γ a fixed base strength). This means that for an audio-focused question, the audio contrastive branch is amplified while the visual branch is suppressed, and vice versa. The key distinction from prior work (VCD, AVCD) is that contrastive intensity is no longer uniform but dynamically scaled by the model's own judgment of which modality each question requires, enabling precise suppression of cross-modal interference without retraining.

## Results

On the CMM benchmark, MAD achieves 81.3% overall accuracy with VideoLLaMA2-AV (+7.8% over base) and 81.4% with Qwen2.5-Omni (+8.7% over base), with the largest gains in Visual Dominance (+9.3% and +12.3% respectively). On AVHBench, MAD reaches 79.4% (VideoLLaMA2-AV, +2.0%) and 81.6% (Qwen2.5-Omni, +4.7%), reducing both video-driven audio hallucination and audio-driven video hallucination. On general AVQA benchmarks (OmniBench, Worldsense, MUSIC-AVQA, FutureOmni, Daily-Omni), MAD maintains or slightly improves performance, confirming no degradation on standard tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAD consistently outperforms both baselines across all models and benchmarks. Against VCD-Extended, MAD gains +4.9% (VideoLLaMA2-AV) and +8.6% (Qwen2.5-Omni) on CMM overall accuracy, and a striking +9.0% and +7.9% on AVHBench—where VCD-Extended actually degrades performance below the base model (70.4% vs. 77.4% for VideoLLaMA2-AV), revealing that uniform multi-modality distortion is counterproductive. Against AVCD, MAD improves CMM accuracy by +5.5% (VideoLLaMA2-AV) and +8.1% (Qwen2.5-Omni), while on AVHBench the gap is smaller: +0.1% for VideoLLaMA2-AV (79.4% vs. 79.3%) but +3.8% for Qwen2.5-Omni (81.6% vs. 77.8%). The largest single-metric gain is Qwen2.5-Omni's Audio Dominance on CMM, where MAD reaches 84.3% versus 72.8% (AVCD) and 71.3% (VCD-Extended), a +12.0% improvement over the base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAD extends the contrastive decoding lineage from single-modality VCD to the audio-visual setting, building directly on AVCD's multi-modal contrastive framework. The key departure is that prior methods (VCD, AVCD) apply uniform or attention-based distortion weights across all modalities, treating hallucination as a modality-agnostic problem. MAD introduces a self-assessment step where the model itself queries which modalities are relevant to a given question, then uses the resulting softmax weights to adaptively scale each contrastive branch—shifting from static, modality-agnostic decoding to task-conditioned, modality-aware decoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-modal hallucinations (one modality corrupting another's content) are a distinct and practically harmful failure mode that single-modality hallucination metrics miss, and MAD provides a simple, training-free mechanism to address it without retraining. The self-assessment prompt is elegant in its simplicity, but the approach is currently limited to two non-text modalities (audio and video) and two model architectures, leaving open whether the modality-query mechanism generalizes to richer modality combinations (e.g., thermal, LiDAR) or to models where the self-assessment signal is less reliable. The authors note that a lightweight learned predictor could replace the autoregressive query step, and extending beyond audio-video is a natural next step.
