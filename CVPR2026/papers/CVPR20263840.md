## Problem

Existing remote sensing change generation methods can synthesize event-driven changes (e.g., new buildings) by modifying spatial conditions, but they cannot model cross-temporal background variations such as seasonal shifts or lighting changes. The broader task of conditional spatiotemporal image generation—producing a coherent future scene from a past observation under multimodal controls—remains largely unexplored, leaving a gap for both 'what-if' simulation and synthetic data generation for downstream change detection.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263840/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 256×256 pre-event satellite image together with one of three multimodal controls: coordinate text (object counts with precise bounding-box coordinates), a semantic mask, or an instance layout, and outputs a single post-event satellite image. The pre-event image is encoded by a VQGAN autoencoder into latent space, while the control is converted into a binary foreground mask and, for coordinate text, a text embedding via SkyCLIP. The generated output must simultaneously satisfy spatial alignment with the control (correct object placement/semantics) and temporal coherence with the pre-event background (preserving lighting, vegetation, and structural continuity).

## Method

ChangeBridge replaces the standard noise-to-image diffusion with a drift-asynchronous Brownian bridge that directly connects a composed pre-event state to the post-event target. Three modules drive the design: (1) Composed Bridge Initialization merges the pre-event background with the control-driven foreground to form the bridge's starting latent, eliminating the need to reconstruct structure from scratch; (2) Asynchronous Drift Diffusion introduces a pixel-wise drift magnitude map that assigns a higher drift coefficient to event regions (γ^fg = 1.0) and a lower one to background (γ^bg = 0.7–0.8), so the forward process evolves foreground and background at different rates; (3) Drift-Aware Denoising injects the drift latent into the denoising network (via channel concatenation for UNet or FiLM-style additive modulation for DiT) so the reverse process can differentially reconstruct fast-changing events versus slow temporal drift. The model is trained with a standard ε-prediction loss under the bridge schedule and is backbone-agnostic, instantiated on both SD 1.5 UNet and DiT-XL/2 architectures.

## Results

ChangeBridge (DiT variant, Ours-T) achieves the best FID across all three conditional settings: 31.45 on LEVIR-CC (coordinate text), 40.12 on WHU-CD and 56.42 on S2Looking (instance layout), and 59.33 on SECOND (semantic mask). It also attains the highest semantic alignment scores: 0.85 CosSim (vs. 0.89 real-data upper bound), 78.13% IoU on WHU-CD (vs. 81.30 real), 79.40% IoU on S2Looking (vs. 82.47 real), and 74.26% mIoU on SECOND (vs. 76.19 real). The UNet variant (Ours-U) is consistently competitive, e.g., 38.36 FID on LEVIR-CC and 75.30% IoU on WHU-CD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChangeBridge (Ours-T) outperforms all six baselines on every metric across all four datasets. Against the strongest change-generation baseline Changen2, it reduces FID by 8.73 (WHU-CD), 26.89 (S2Looking), and 10.10 (SECOND), while improving IoU/mIoU by 3.80%, 0.51%, and 1.06% respectively. Against ControlNet+IPA, the FID gap is even larger: 11.96 on WHU-CD, 38.26 on S2Looking, and 31.48 on SECOND, with IoU/mIoU gains of 6.59%, 4.20%, and 1.96%. Among multi-conditional methods, Instruct-Imagen is the closest text-conditioned competitor (FID 48.17 vs. 31.45 on LEVIR-CC), while UNITE and DreamBooth lag substantially in both fidelity and alignment. Notably, the UNet variant (Ours-U) already surpasses Changen2 on WHU-CD (FID 45.47 vs. 48.85) and SECOND (FID 62.24 vs. 69.43), confirming that the bridge formulation itself—rather than the backbone choice—drives most of the improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChangeBridge extends the Brownian Bridge Diffusion Model (BBDM, Li et al., CVPR 2023) from a uniform state-to-state transition to a spatially heterogeneous one, introducing a pixel-wise drift magnitude map that assigns different evolution intensities to foreground events versus background temporal dynamics. It departs from standard noise-to-image diffusion (Stable Diffusion, DiT) by initializing the bridge from a composed pre-event state rather than pure Gaussian noise, and from prior change generation methods (Changen2, ChangeDiff) that model only event-driven spatial changes by explicitly incorporating cross-temporal background evolution. The multi-conditional generation literature (ControlNet, IP-Adapter, DreamBooth) informs the conditioning mechanisms, but ChangeBridge repurposes them within the bridge framework to maintain cross-spatiotemporal coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: existing remote sensing change generators cannot simultaneously model drastic foreground events and subtle background temporal shifts (e.g., seasonal vegetation changes, lighting transitions), limiting their utility for realistic scenario simulation. The dual role as both a simulation tool and a data augmentation engine for change detection is practically valuable, though the downstream improvements (1–2% IoU/mIoU gains) are modest and suggest the generated data is useful but not transformative. Open questions include whether the fixed drift magnitudes (γ^fg, γ^bg) generalize across change types, whether the approach scales to higher resolutions or longer temporal gaps, and the authors' stated plan to replace the bridge with flow matching for efficiency.
