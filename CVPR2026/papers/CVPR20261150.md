## Problem

Existing video generation benchmarks (e.g., V-Bench) reduce aesthetic quality to a single holistic score and rely on external image-quality models (MUSIQ, DINO), yielding insufficient granularity, significant bias, and weak controllability. There is no unified framework that jointly evaluates fine-grained aesthetic dimensions (composition, lighting, color harmony, etc.), structured aesthetic tags, and generation quality in a single, interpretable pipeline for AIGC videos.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a generated video clip with its originating text prompt. Critically, the prompt is dimension-aligned: only the aesthetic or quality sub-dimensions explicitly mentioned in the prompt are scored, preventing subjective inference on unmentioned attributes. Outputs are structured as (1) ten continuous aesthetic quality scores on a 0–10 scale, (2) eleven categorical aesthetic tags (multi-label), and (3) thirty-one ordinal generation-quality levels grouped into Video-Text Consistency, Realism & Plausibility, and Basic Quality. The benchmark provides full (1,016-prompt) and two lightweight (508- and 127-prompt) subsets for flexible evaluation.

## Method

Three dedicated multi-task assessors are trained on human-annotated generated videos. VAQA-Net and VTag-Net inherit a video encoder pre-trained on the VADB dataset (a dual-text encoder with dynamic fusion module for language comments and aesthetic tags), which is frozen during fine-tuning on 1,300 generated videos from 12 models, allowing the models to transfer real-world cinematographic aesthetic understanding to AIGC content. VGQA-Net differs architecturally by adding a CLIP branch before the input MLP to jointly encode video and prompt, and is trained on 12,000 videos from six models with a strict train/test split by model provenance (3 models for training, 3 for testing) to ensure cross-model generalization. The annotation protocol uses an expert-led + crowd-assisted paradigm with majority voting and batch-level quality audits, and all scoring is conditioned on the prompt's explicit dimension mentions.

## Results

The three dedicated evaluators achieve strong alignment with human annotations: VAQA-Net reaches 76.9% 5-class accuracy on overall aesthetic score (sub-dimension range 67.9–77.4%), VTag-Net achieves 45–90% top-2 tag accuracy across 11 tag dimensions, and VGQA-Net attains 67.3–92.6% accuracy across 31 generation quality sub-dimensions. In the user study with 40 non-expert participants, model-based rankings achieve Recall@5 of 0.80 for aesthetic quality and 0.83 for generation quality against human rankings. Among the 12 evaluated generation models, Sora2 leads in aesthetic score (0.50) while Wan2.1 leads in tag classification (0.38), and SVD, CogVideoX, and HunyuanVideo tie for the highest generation level (0.55).

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, VGA-Bench does not pit its evaluation models against competing evaluators head-to-head; instead, it benchmarks 12 video generation models across 52 sub-dimensions. Sora2 achieves the highest aesthetic score (0.50) but the lowest tag classification accuracy (0.18), indicating strong holistic visual appeal yet poor adherence to specific compositional and lighting tag specifications. Wan2.1 (0.46) and HunyuanVideo (0.45) follow closely in aesthetic quality, while CogVideoX (0.41) and AnimateDiff (0.36) occupy the mid-range. For generation quality, SVD, CogVideoX, and HunyuanVideo tie at 0.55, whereas Show-1 lags significantly at 0.28, suggesting substantial issues with physical plausibility and basic visual fidelity. The user study (40 non-expert raters) validates the automated rankings, yielding Recall@5 of 0.80 for aesthetic quality and 0.83 for generation quality, confirming that the model-based scores capture human perceptual preferences reasonably well.

## Relation to Prior Work

VGA-Bench extends V-Bench's multi-dimensional evaluation framework by decomposing the single 'aesthetic' dimension into 21 fine-grained sub-dimensions (10 aesthetic quality scores + 11 aesthetic tags) and refining generation quality into 31 sub-dimensions across three categories. It inherits its aesthetic taxonomy and pre-trained video encoders from the VADB dataset, adapting professional cinematography annotations to the AIGC context. A key departure from prior benchmarks (V-Bench, V-Bench2.0) is the replacement of external scoring models (MUSIQ, DINO) with three dedicated multi-task neural assessors trained end-to-end on human-annotated generated videos, eliminating the bias and misalignment introduced by indirect inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As video generation models increasingly target professional creative applications, the field needs evaluation infrastructure that captures artistic quality beyond technical fidelity; VGA-Bench's 52-dimension taxonomy and 60K+ video dataset from 12 models provide the most comprehensive such platform to date. A notable open question is whether the aesthetic dimensions, adapted from real-video cinematography, fully transfer to the distinct failure modes of generated content (e.g., 'floating composition,' 'non-physical light sources'). The low user-study Recall@1 for aesthetic ranking (0.10) versus generation quality (0.50) suggests that automated aesthetic assessment remains substantially harder to align with human perception, pointing to a significant gap for future work.
