## Problem

Despite growing interest in extending RL-enhanced reasoning to multimodal domains, existing works lack transparent and reproducible training pipelines, with few providing details on data curation or conducting comprehensive ablation analyses. This opacity restricts reproducibility and obscures how reasoning-capable multimodal models are actually built. The paper addresses this gap by providing a fully open, unified recipe spanning both SFT and RL stages for training large multimodal reasoning models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263738/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model (initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) takes an RGB image paired with a text question as input and produces a structured, step-by-step reasoning trace followed by a final answer. All training data is standardized to a unified reasoning format with consistent step-wise outputs to avoid training instability from heterogeneous answer styles. During RL, a composite reward function weights task accuracy (R_acc) and answer format consistency (R_fmt) with λ_fmt=0.1, and an overlength penalty discourages excessively long reasoning trajectories.

## Method

OpenMMReasoner is a two-stage training recipe. The SFT stage constructs an 874K-sample cold-start dataset by (1) collecting ~103K raw questions from LLaVA-CoT, OpenVLThinker, and WeMath2.0, (2) distilling reasoning traces using Qwen3-VL-235B-Instruct as teacher with rejection sampling at ×8 answer diversity (verified via rule-based and LLM-as-judge checks), and (3) mixing in cross-domain math data from MMR1 (image-based) and MiroMind-M1 (text-based). Key SFT findings: answer diversity outperforms filtering, and cross-domain mixing improves generalization. The RL stage uses a 74K-sample dataset across science, math, charts, and puzzles, trained with GSPO (chosen over GRPO and DAPO for superior stability and convergence) at ×16 rollouts with temperature 1.0, no curriculum sampling, and an overlength penalty to balance reasoning depth with token efficiency.

## Results

OpenMMReasoner (OMR-7B) achieves an average of 54.3 across nine multimodal reasoning benchmarks, representing an 11.6% improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct baseline (average 49.4). Headline scores include 79.5 on MathVista testmini, 63.8 on MathVerse testmini, 79.0 on WeMath loose, and 57.8 on MMMU val, all setting new state-of-the-art among open-source 7B models. The SFT-only cold-start model (OMR-7B-ColdStart) already reaches 74.8 on MathVista and 57.7 on MathVerse, demonstrating that the 874K-sample SFT recipe alone provides a strong reasoning foundation before RL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OMR-7B achieves the best average score (54.3) among all open-source 7B models, outperforming the strongest RL-based competitor OVR-7B (average ~51.8) by a clear margin on MathVista (79.5 vs 72.1), MathVerse (63.8 vs 54.6), WeMath (79.0 vs 64.8), and MMMU (57.8 vs 51.8). However, OVR-7B retains an advantage on MathVision standard (51.8 vs 43.6) and MMMU-Pro vision reasoning (50.2 vs 44.1), suggesting OVR's longer reasoning traces benefit certain visual-math subtasks. Against the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct baseline, OMR-7B gains +10.3 on MathVista, +18.1 on MathVision, +22.7 on MathVerse, and +25.9 on WeMath, confirming the paper's claimed 11.6% average improvement. Compared to M2-Reasoning (6.2M SFT + 102k RL), OMR-7B uses far less data (874k SFT + 74k RL) yet surpasses it on MathVista (79.5 vs 75.0), MathVerse (63.8 vs 40.4), and WeMath (79.0 vs 50.6). The SFT-only cold-start model already outperforms all RL-based baselines on MathVerse (57.7) and WeMath (67.2), highlighting that data quality and diversity in the SFT stage are as critical as the RL phase.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR paradigm established for LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI o3) to the multimodal setting, building directly on GSPO for the RL algorithm, DAPO's overlength penalty for efficiency, and DeepSeek-R1's composite reward design. It departs from prior multimodal reasoning efforts (OVR, ThinkLite-VL, MM-Eureka) primarily in transparency: while those works release models or partial data, OpenMMReasoner is the first to open-source the full data curation pipeline, SFT and RL datasets, and training code. Conceptually, the paper reframes the problem from algorithmic innovation to data curation science, showing that answer diversity and cross-domain mixing are the dominant levers rather than novel RL objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The fully open recipe (874K SFT + 74K RL data, pipeline code, model weights) lowers the barrier for the community to reproduce and extend multimodal reasoning research, addressing a reproducibility gap the authors explicitly identify. The finding that over-filtering by length or difficulty actively degrades performance—contrary to common practice—challenges a default assumption in SFT data preparation and suggests that preserving answer diversity is more valuable than curating for apparent quality. Open questions include whether these data curation principles (×8 answer sampling, no-filtering, cross-domain mixing) transfer to larger model scales (70B+) or to video/audio modalities, and whether the GSPO advantage over GRPO/DAPO persists as model size and dataset scale grow.
