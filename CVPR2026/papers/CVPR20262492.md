## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models produce small-scale distortions in limbs, faces, and text that are difficult to detect and expensive to fix via full-image regeneration. Existing post-hoc editing pipelines require manually crafted masks or heuristic hints, and vision-language models—despite strong semantic reasoning—exhibit weak spatial grounding and hallucinate judgments on fine-grained artifacts, making them unreliable for autonomous localization and correction of localized failures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262492/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single generated T2I image and its original text prompt as input, with no manual masks or region cues required. It iteratively (typically 2–3 rounds) produces a retouched image alongside intermediate artifacts: a pixel-level distortion saliency map in [0,1]^{H×W} that is binarized and morphologically dilated into mask candidates, per-region distortion type labels from a 12-category taxonomy, and natural-language descriptions averaging ~12 words per region. The action agent dynamically selects between VLM-based (Qwen-Edit, Gemini 2.5 Flash Image) and mask-based (Flux-Fill, SD-inpainting) inpainting tools per region based on user preference or computational constraints.

## Method

Agentic Retoucher reformulates post-generation correction as a closed perception-reasoning-action loop with three collaborative agents. The Perception Agent uses a dual-encoder ViT-T5 backbone that fuses image and prompt representations via self-attention, followed by a lightweight multi-scale attention refinement module; it is trained with a hybrid MSE + KLD loss to balance pixel accuracy against human fixation distribution alignment. The Reasoning Agent (a VLM such as Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is aligned through a two-stage progressive paradigm: LoRA-based SFT (rank 64) for structured response formatting and taxonomy grounding, followed by GRPO reinforcement with rewards on distortion-type classification accuracy and description–label alignment to reduce hallucination. The Action Agent maps reasoning outputs (masks + descriptions) to targeted inpainting operations from a modular tool library, and the updated image is re-evaluated by the Perception Agent until saliency drops below a threshold, closing the self-corrective cycle.

## Results

On GenBlemish-27K, Agentic Retoucher (with Qwen-Edit) improves plausibility from 44.21 to 47.10 and overall perceptual score from 47.15 to 49.27, while on SynArtifacts-1K the overall score reaches 58.43 (with Gemini 2.5 Flash Image). In human preference evaluation, 83.2% of retouched outputs are rated superior to the originals (48.8% significantly better + 34.4% slightly better). The Context-Aware Perception Agent achieves state-of-the-art saliency metrics (AUC-Judd = 0.9336, NSS = 1.2087, KLD = 1.4313), surpassing RichHF and all VLM baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VLM-based inpainting baselines (Qwen-Edit, Gemini 2.5 Flash Image), Agentic Retoucher consistently outperforms by 2.5–3.0 points in plausibility and 1.7–2.1 points in overall score on GenBlemish-27K, confirming that autonomous perception and reasoning add substantial value over direct VLM editing. Against mask-based baselines (Flux-fill, SD-inpainting), the gains are 1.1–2.1 points in plausibility and 1.2–1.6 in overall, showing that learned localization surpasses static mask guidance. In the perception sub-task (Table 3), the proposed saliency predictor beats RichHF (AUC-Judd 0.9336 vs. 0.9211) and all general-purpose VLMs (InternVL3.5-8B: 0.8049, Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 0.6145, GLM-4.1V-9B: 0.5461), demonstrating that task-specific context-aware modeling is critical for distortion localization. In the reasoning sub-task (Table 4), progressive SFT+GRPO training yields 80.10% accuracy on Qwen2.5-VL, outperforming zero-shot GPT-5 (61.31%) and Gemini-2.5 Pro (60.28%) by ~19 points, and beating SFT-only by 1.76 points, validating the two-stage alignment strategy. Across all three VLM backbones tested (Qwen2.5-VL, GLM-4.1V, Ovis2.5), the full pipeline consistently achieves the highest scores, confirming generalizability of the framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new autonomous-correction niche at the intersection of T2I post-editing (Imagic, Bagel, Step1x-Edit), VLM-based quality assessment (RichHF, SynArtifacts-1K), and agentic multi-agent systems (AgenticIR, MoA-VR, JarvisArt). Prior post-hoc editing pipelines require manually crafted masks or heuristic prompts, while VLM-based critics fail at pixel-level spatial grounding for fine-grained artifacts. The key departure is reframing correction as a closed perception-reasoning-action loop where a task-specific saliency predictor (ViT-T5), a progressively aligned VLM reasoner (LoRA + GRPO), and a modular inpainting tool library operate iteratively without human intervention. The GenBlemish-27K dataset extends existing AIGC artifact benchmarks (SynArtifacts-1K, HADM, RichHF) by adding pixel-level masks, 12-category taxonomy, and natural-language descriptions at a 27× larger scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 83.2% human preference rate and consistent gains across both VLM-based and mask-based inpainting backends suggest the agentic loop is a practical path toward self-corrective T2I pipelines, reducing reliance on full regeneration. The GenBlemish-27K dataset is a standalone contribution that can improve VLM robustness for AIGC evaluation beyond this specific framework. However, the human evaluation involves only 5 participants, the perception agent is a relatively lightweight ViT-T5 model, and the 2–3 iteration convergence suggests the loop may not handle deeply nested or multi-region cascading failures. Open questions include whether this paradigm extends to video generation, whether the GRPO alignment generalizes to unseen distortion types, and whether the modular tool library can accommodate future inpainting architectures without retraining the perception and reasoning agents.
