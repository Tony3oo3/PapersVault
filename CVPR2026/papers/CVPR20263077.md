## Problem

Traditional image-based Visual Deformation Measurement (VDM) requires high-speed cameras to minimize inter-frame motion, incurring prohibitive storage and computational costs that limit practical deployment. Event cameras provide temporally dense motion cues without this redundancy, but their spatial sparsity and high noise introduce severe ambiguities in estimating dense deformation fields, and no prior method effectively fuses the two modalities for large-scale deformations involving significant object self-motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263077/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes temporally aligned event streams (asynchronous, polarity-tagged pixel-level brightness changes) and low-frame-rate grayscale frames (5 fps in the main configuration), acquired simultaneously via a 50:50 beam-splitter collocation. Events between consecutive frames are partitioned into M equal-count bins, and each event undergoes two warping operations (short-term within-bin and global across the time window) to construct multi-channel images of warped events. The output is a dense deformation field parameterized as affine transformations over a simplicial mesh of triangular sub-regions, from which von Mises strain fields and continuous anchor-point trajectories are derived.

## Method

The core contribution is the Affine Invariant Simplicial (AIS) framework, which exploits the reproduction property of barycentric interpolation to represent the high-dimensional nonlinear deformation field as a collection of low-parametric affine sub-regions (each defined by a 2×2 gradient matrix and translation vector at its three vertices). Events are associated to sub-regions via a same-side vector cross-product test, and displacements are recovered through affine-invariant barycentric interpolation rather than KNN averaging. Optimization proceeds in a coarse-to-fine hierarchy—first estimating global rigid motion, then iteratively splitting triangles and refining local affine parameters—using a joint objective of contrast maximization on warped events and zero-mean normalized cross-correlation on image intensity samples. A neighborhood-greedy strategy then fixes anchors of well-converged sub-regions and adds von Mises strain continuity constraints to guide poorly-converged neighbors, greedily accumulating fixed anchors until global convergence, which improves survival rate from 39% to 65.7% and reduces convergence time from 26.5 s to 7.2 s.

## Results

On the 100+ pixel displacement test set (the most challenging scenario), the proposed method achieves a survival rate of 65.7% with EPE 3.204 and SEPE 0.813, outperforming the SOTA baseline CoTrackerV3 (45.2% survival, EPE 8.763) by 1.6× in survival rate. On moderate deformations (20–100 pixels), the method achieves EPE 0.330 and 92.4% survival, substantially ahead of all baselines. The method also requires only 18.9% of the data storage of 100 fps high-speed video while matching its performance (SEPE 0.813 vs. 0.825).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks against five representative methods: OpenCorr (model-based DIC), StrainNet (learning-based VDM), E-RAFT (event-based optical flow), CoTrackerV3 (long-term point tracking, the SOTA baseline), and TimeLens-enhanced variants of the image-based methods for fair event-input comparison. On the hardest 100+ pixel displacement scenario, the proposed method achieves 65.7% survival rate versus 45.2% for CoTrackerV3 (1.6× improvement) and 41.3% for OpenCorr+TimeLens, while also reducing EPE from 8.763 to 3.204. E-RAFT, designed for short-term motion, collapses entirely on long-term tracking (2.1% survival at 100+ pixels). StrainNet, a learning-based method, degrades severely on large deformations (18.8% survival without TimeLens). On small deformations (5–20 pixels), all methods perform well (>91% survival), but the proposed method still achieves the best SEPE (0.121) among event-input methods, confirming that the simplicial framework does not sacrifice precision on easier cases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the contrast maximization (CM) framework for event-based motion estimation (Gallego et al., CVPR 2018; Paredes-Vallés et al., ICCV 2023) from pixel-level or globally-parametric settings to dense, non-rigid deformation measurement. It departs from the KNN-based anchor association in Hamann et al. (ECCV 2024) by introducing a same-side test with affine-invariant barycentric interpolation, and from traditional image-based DIC (OpenCorr, StrainNet) by replacing high-speed video with a hybrid event-frame pipeline. The Affine Invariant Simplicial (AIS) framework is a novel local linearization strategy that bridges the gap between low-parameter CM (which cannot capture complex deformations) and unconstrained dense tracking (which is ill-posed for sparse, noisy events).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first event-based VDM benchmark (120+ sequences with temporally aligned events and frames), filling a gap that has prevented systematic evaluation in this modality. The practical impact is significant: matching 100 fps high-speed video performance with only 18.9% of the data storage opens VDM to dynamic, large-displacement scenarios (tire rolling, rapid self-motion) that were previously infeasible without expensive high-speed cameras. Key limitations remain—topological changes (cracking) and specular reflections degrade performance—and the authors identify 3D event-based VDM as a natural next step. The neighborhood-greedy optimization strategy, which nearly doubles survival rate in ablation, is a transferable idea for any long-term dense tracking problem with local error accumulation.
