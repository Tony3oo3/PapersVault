## Problem

Document layout generation remains underexplored compared to layout analysis, largely because existing datasets are dominated by simple Manhattan-style academic papers while complex real-world layouts (newspapers, magazines, textbooks) are severely underrepresented. Moreover, current generative methods—whether diffusion-based or LLM-based—struggle to produce coherent long-sequence layouts in these complex, multi-element domains, and fine-grained annotated data for such domains is scarce.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261200/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a structured prompt consisting of document metadata (type, canvas size, total element count, valid category set) followed by task-specific conditioning tokens: either no condition (unconditional), category-only tokens, category+size tokens, a partial subset of elements (0–20% retained for completion), or fully perturbed tuples with Gaussian noise N(0, 10⁻²) for refinement. The output is an autoregressively generated string of element tokens, where each element is encoded as a prefix-aware serialization of its category and four normalized coordinates (x, y, w, h) uniformly quantized to integers in [0, 999], following the LGGPT string-based format rather than HTML.

## Method

OmniDocLayout-LLM is a 0.5B autoregressive LLM (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct) trained under a two-stage Coarse-to-Fine paradigm. In Stage 1, the model is pretrained on ~1M samples from the newly curated OmniDocLayout-1M dataset (six document types, ~48M element instances) using a coarse-grained label set (text, table, image, title, caption, footnote, etc.) to acquire universal spatial priors such as alignment and non-overlap. In Stage 2, a label mapping φ expands each coarse class into its fine-grained descendants (e.g., 'text' → {paragraph, lead, ordered_list}), and the model is fine-tuned on only a few hundred annotated samples from the target domain (e.g., M6Doc's 42-category textbook set) for 5 epochs. The key insight is that coarse pretraining on diverse layouts dramatically reduces the difficulty of fine-grained adaptation, enabling strong performance with minimal domain-specific supervision and a small parameter footprint, while the string-based tokenization avoids the redundant structural tokens of HTML representations.

## Results

On the M⁶Doc benchmark across five document types (Textbook, Newspaper, Magazine, Exam, Academic) and five generation tasks, OmniDocLayout-LLM (0.5B) achieves FID scores of 4–42, substantially outperforming all baselines. For example, on the C+S→P task, it reaches FID 5.42 on Exam and 6.13 on Newspaper, versus 14.58 (LayoutPrompter) and 170–245 (LGGPT). On mIoU, the method consistently leads, reaching 0.752 on Magazine Refinement and 0.681 on Textbook Refinement, while most baselines score below 0.30 on complex domains. Against general-purpose LLMs in 0-shot, it also dominates: e.g., FID 36.48 vs. 106.98 (Claude-3.7-Sonnet) on Academic U-Cond.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against diffusion-based experts (LayoutDM, LACE), OmniDocLayout-LLM achieves 4–10× lower FID across all tasks and domains; both diffusion methods fail to produce meaningful mIoU on U-Cond and Completion due to their inability to handle long element sequences in complex layouts. Among LLM-based layout experts, LayoutPrompter is the closest competitor, achieving competitive FID on Refinement (e.g., 9.23 on Academic vs. 8.25 for Ours) and even slightly lower FID on Academic C+S→P (14.58 vs. 9.02), but it is inconsistent across domains and cannot handle U-Cond at all. LGGPT, despite being domain-agnostic, produces incoherent outputs on long-sequence complex domains (mIoU ≈ 0 on most U-Cond and Completion settings) due to its GPT-2-XL backbone struggling with long prompts. Against general-purpose LLMs in 0-shot (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Flash, Claude-3.7-Sonnet), OmniDocLayout-LLM achieves 2–5× lower FID and substantially higher mIoU; Claude-3.7-Sonnet is the strongest general LLM baseline but still lags significantly (e.g., FID 106.98 vs. 36.48 on Academic U-Cond; mIoU 0.000 vs. 0.415). The only setting where a baseline approaches Ours is Refinement on Academic, where LayoutPrompter (FID 9.23, mIoU 0.667) and Claude-3.7-Sonnet (FID 1.67, mIoU 0.651) are competitive, though Ours still leads on mIoU (0.708).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LGGPT [56] for both the task formulation (five conditioning regimes: U-Cond, C→S+P, C+S→P, Completion, Refinement) and the string-based layout serialization scheme, explicitly rejecting HTML-style prompts in favor of pure token encoding. It departs from the broader LLM-based layout generation line (LayoutPrompter, LayoutCoT, LayoutRAG, LayoutNUWA) by introducing a two-stage Coarse-to-Fine learning paradigm: first learning universal spatial principles from a diverse million-scale dataset with coarse labels, then adapting to a specific domain with only a few hundred fine-grained samples. The dataset contribution (OmniDocLayout-1M) addresses a gap identified across prior resources—PubLayNet and DocBank cover only academic Manhattan layouts, while M6Doc and OmniDocBench are too small (<10K) for generative training—by providing ~1M samples across six contemporary document types via an automated MinerU-based annotation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OmniDocLayout-1M dataset is a significant resource for the document AI community, being the first million-scale collection of diverse, contemporary document layouts with automated annotation, and the coarse-to-fine paradigm demonstrates that complex layout generation can be achieved with minimal fine-grained supervision (a few hundred samples) by first learning general aesthetic principles. The practical finding that a 0.5B model trained for ~20 hours on 40 A100s consistently outperforms GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5-Flash, and Claude-3.7-Sonnet in 0-shot across all five document types and five tasks suggests that domain-specific small models remain highly competitive for structured generation tasks. Open questions include whether current evaluation metrics (FID, mIoU) are adequate for complex multi-element layouts with limited test samples, whether the coarse-to-fine paradigm generalizes to even more heterogeneous domains, and whether the automated annotation quality degrades on truly novel layout types not seen during training.
