## Problem

Chest X-rays are 2D projections limited by structural superposition, making it difficult to capture 3D anatomical structure for accurate disease diagnosis. Existing CXR world models such as CheXWorld remain confined to 2D local features and global geometry without embedding any volumetric knowledge, creating a substantial capability gap compared to radiologists who cognitively reconstruct 3D chest anatomy from frontal and lateral views. This paper addresses how to distill 3D spatial knowledge from chest CT into a CXR representation model.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263462/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single routine frontal or lateral CXR (512×512 pixels, patch size 16) as context input and an action specifying the yaw rotation angle of a virtual cone-beam X-ray source. During training, CT volumes from NLST are rendered into simulated projections via DiffDRR at various angular positions to serve as prediction targets. The primary output is a latent visual representation for downstream classification; additionally, a VQ-GAN decoder can render 2D projections from the learned latent space, which are then fed to an FDK algorithm to reconstruct a 3D CT volume.

## Method

X-WIN adopts a JEPA-style architecture with a ViT encoder for context CXRs, an EMA-updated encoder for target projections, an action-conditioned view predictor, and a mask predictor. The core mechanism is predictive sensing: given a context CXR and a rotation action, the model must predict the latent representation of the projection the X-ray source would produce at that new angle, forcing the latent space to internalize 3D anatomical structure. The training objective combines three novel components: (1) an affinity-guided contrastive alignment loss that replaces the hard one-hot InfoNCE target with a soft affinity matrix computed from mutual similarities among projections of the same CT volume, preserving rich anatomical correspondences; (2) masked image modeling on both real and simulated CXRs to encode local and contextual features; and (3) a structure-preserving domain adaptation loss that uses a domain classifier to push simulated-domain representations toward the real-domain distribution while maintaining patch-level structural consistency with targets.

## Results

On linear probing across five CXR benchmarks, X-WIN (ViT-Large) achieves an average AUROC of 0.892, outperforming the best CXR foundation model Ark+ (0.863) by 2.9 points and the prior CXR world model CheXWorld (0.844) by 4.8 points. The ViT-Base variant also leads with 0.883 average AUROC. In few-shot fine-tuning on COVIDx, X-WIN reaches 0.993 AUROC in the full-data setting and 0.924 in the 8-shot setting, surpassing all baselines except Ark+ in the 4-shot regime (0.868 vs. 0.872).

## Baselines & Comparisons

X-WIN outperforms all ten baselines on average AUROC in linear probing, with the largest margins over general-domain models (I-JEPA: +15.6 pts, DINOv2: +16.4 pts) and a consistent 2–5 point lead over CXR-specialized foundation models (Ark+: +2.0 pts at ViT-B, CheXFound: +2.5 pts at ViT-L). Against the closest world-model competitor CheXWorld, X-WIN gains 3.9 points on average, attributed to the integration of 3D CT-derived spatial knowledge versus CheXWorld's 2D-only modeling. In few-shot fine-tuning on COVIDx, X-WIN wins at 8-shot (0.924 vs. 0.908 for Ark+) and 16-shot (0.939 vs. 0.921), but narrowly trails Ark+ at 4-shot (0.868 vs. 0.872), where Ark+'s disease-label pretraining provides a slight edge. All four vision-language models (CARZero, CXR-Align, MaCo, CheXAgent) underperform the CXR foundation models, with CheXAgent (0.763) being the weakest, suggesting that text-report alignment remains a bottleneck for radiograph representation learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

X-WIN extends the JEPA-style predictive architecture (I-JEPA, V-JEPA) and the 2D CXR world model CheXWorld by introducing a 3D action-conditioned projection prediction objective: instead of predicting 2D crops or video frames, the model predicts new 2D X-ray projections generated by rotating a virtual cone-beam source around a CT volume. The affinity-guided contrastive alignment loss departs from standard InfoNCE by replacing the one-hot target with a soft affinity matrix computed from mutual similarities among projections of the same volume, acknowledging that all projections share rich anatomical correspondences. The structure-preserving domain adaptation loss adapts the adversarial discriminative domain adaptation framework (Tzeng et al.) to the sim-to-real gap between DiffDRR-generated projections and real CXRs, while the overall teacher-student EMA architecture follows the knowledge distillation lineage of BYOL and MoCo.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first CXR world model to internalize 3D volumetric anatomy from CT, closing a capability gap the authors identify between 2D-only models and radiologists' mental 3D reconstruction. The consistent ~4-point AUROC gain over CheXWorld across five benchmarks, plus the demonstrated ability to render projections sufficient for FDK-based 3D CT reconstruction (PSNR 27.87 dB), validates that the latent space genuinely encodes spatial structure rather than merely memorizing 2D patterns. Open questions include whether the sim-to-real gap can be further reduced (the authors note domain randomization and invariant feature learning as future directions), whether the approach scales to other modalities (e.g., abdominal CT → abdominal X-ray), and how performance compares to models trained directly on CT volumes. The reliance on 32K CT scans from NLST (a lung-cancer screening cohort) may limit generalizability to other thoracic pathologies.
