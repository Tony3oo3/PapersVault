## Problem

Camera-guided video diffusion models produce visually impressive clips but fail to generate multi-view-consistent content across diverse camera trajectories, making downstream 3D reconstruction ambiguous and incomplete. Extending sequences to cover more viewpoints degrades quality and is computationally prohibitive, while autoregressive approaches accumulate errors and lack precise camera control. The paper addresses this by enabling consistent multi-trajectory generation with geometry-aware memories, bridging the gap between video generation and reliable 3D scene reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (or a 360° panorama split into 27 perspective frames) plus a specified camera trajectory as input, and produces multiple medium-length bidirectional videos along complementary viewpoints (e.g., up, left, right rotations, and orbit). These videos are then reconstructed into a unified 3D point cloud via WorldMirror. Training operates at 480p with flexible aspect ratios, and the model generalizes to 720p inference; the DMD-distilled variant reduces denoising from 40 to 4 steps for a 20× speedup.

## Method

WorldStereo builds on Uni3C (Wan2.1-14B I2V with a ControlNet camera branch) and introduces two complementary memory mechanisms injected via separate ControlNet branches. The Global-Geometric Memory (GGM) incrementally updates a 3D point cloud cache (reconstructed by WorldMirror) and feeds it as extended point cloud guidance, with a masking strategy to prevent overfitting to novel-view geometry. The Spatial-Stereo Memory (SSM) retrieves spatially similar reference frames from a 2D memory bank, horizontally stitches each target-reference pair in latent space, adds 3D-correspondence pointmap latents derived from the 3D cache, and constrains attention to operate only within each pair—inspired by stereo matching—to preserve fine-grained detail consistency. All conditions are pixel-wise aligned and injected through ControlNet, making the framework compatible with Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) without joint training; the distilled 4-step generator retains both camera and memory control branches, achieving 20× inference speedup with negligible quality loss.

## Results

On the proposed 3D reconstruction benchmark, WorldStereo-Full achieves the best F1-Score of 0.578 and AUC of 0.437 on Tanks-and-Temples, and 0.406 F1-Score and 0.402 AUC on MipNeRF360, substantially outperforming all baselines. On the OOD camera-control benchmark (WorldScore), WorldStereo-GGM attains the lowest rotation error (0.129) and translation error (0.162), while the DMD-accelerated variant reduces inference time from 162 s to 9 s (≈20× speedup) with only marginal quality loss. The full model also improves camera trajectory fidelity (ATE 0.1501 vs. 0.1572 for Uni3C) on Tanks-and-Temples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WorldStereo-Full dominates all six baselines on the 3D reconstruction benchmark: on Tanks-and-Temples it improves F1-Score by 36% over Uni3C (0.578 vs. 0.424), 39% over Gen3C (0.578 vs. 0.416), and 102% over SEVA (0.578 vs. 0.286); on MipNeRF360 it leads with F1 0.406 vs. 0.356 (Gen3C) and 0.352 (Uni3C). The memory-free baseline WorldStereo* already beats Uni3C and Gen3C on F1, confirming that the architectural improvements (not just memory) contribute. GGM alone yields the best camera precision (RotErr 0.224, TransErr 0.0885 on T&T), while SSM is critical for fine-grained texture consistency that drives the F1/AUC gains. The DMD variant retains 92% of the full model's F1 on T&T (0.534 vs. 0.578) while cutting inference from 162 s to 9 s. Voyager [39] is only compared on the OOD camera benchmark (Table 2), where it trails all other methods by a wide margin (RotErr 0.678 vs. 0.129–0.155 for the rest).

## Relation to Prior Work

WorldStereo extends Uni3C's camera-guided video diffusion (built on Wan2.1-14B with a ControlNet branch for Plücker rays and point clouds) by adding two geometry-aware memory modules—Global-Geometric Memory for coarse structural priors via incrementally updated point clouds, and Spatial-Stereo Memory for fine-grained detail via 3D-correspondence-constrained attention. Conceptually, it departs from both long-sequence bidirectional generation and autoregressive video synthesis by instead producing multiple medium-length consistent trajectories with shared memory, sidestepping the quality degradation and error accumulation inherent in those paradigms. The DMD acceleration is adapted from Yin et al. but modified to freeze the control branch and omit the GAN loss, decoupling few-step distillation from memory training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical pipeline that turns a single image into a reconstructable 3D scene with 20× faster inference, which is directly useful for applications in virtual reality, digital content creation, and embodied AI. The new single-view 3D reconstruction benchmark (Tanks-and-Temples + MipNeRF360 with cropped ground-truth point clouds) fills a gap in evaluating camera-guided VDMs for reconstruction quality rather than just visual fidelity. Open questions include whether the memory mechanisms generalize beyond the specific Wan2.1-14B backbone, how well the approach scales to dynamic or large-scale scenes, and whether the reliance on feed-forward 3D reconstruction (WorldMirror) as the reconstruction backend limits ultimate 3D fidelity.
