## Problem

Existing temporal sentence grounding in video (TSGV) methods are developed and evaluated exclusively under closed-vocabulary settings, where test queries closely resemble training queries. This leaves them brittle to realistic open-vocabulary scenarios involving unseen objects, actions, or paraphrased expressions, with no dedicated benchmark to measure such generalization. The paper introduces the OV-TSGV task and the first benchmarks (Charades-OV, ActivityNet-OV) that enforce at least one novel lexical unit per test query.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted I3D video features (frame-level, T frames) and 300-dimensional GloVe word embeddings for the query, with all internal hidden dimensions fixed at 128. The query passes through a 6-layer Transformer encoder whose outputs at layers 2, 4, 6, plus the input embedding, yield four parallel semantic levels. The final output is a temporal segment (start, end) produced by a Span Predictor, accompanied by frame-level relevance scores from an MLP head; the four parallel branch outputs are fused via a learnable weighted summation before the final prediction.

## Method

HERO is a plug-and-play framework built on the EMB architecture, comprising a Hierarchical Embedding Module (HEM) and a Cross-modal Filtering and Refinement Engine (CFRE). HEM extracts four levels of text representations from a 6-layer Transformer encoder (input embedding plus layers 2, 4, 6), capturing semantics from lexical tokens to high-level abstractions. CFRE processes all four levels in parallel: the Semantic-Guided Visual Filter uses cross-attention (video as query, text as key/value) followed by sigmoid-gated element-wise multiplication to suppress irrelevant visual content, while the Contrastive Masked Text Refiner randomly masks query tokens and enforces KL-divergence consistency between relevance scores of the original and masked pairs. The refined features feed a standard Feature Fuser → Span Predictor pipeline, and the total loss combines the primary TSGV loss, a binary cross-entropy relevance score loss over both original and masked inputs, and the contrastive KL loss (λ₁=λ₂=0.1).

## Results

On the proposed open-vocabulary benchmarks, HERO achieves R1@0.3/0.5/0.7 of 64.74/45.51/27.20 on Charades-OV and 42.78/25.23/12.18 on ActivityNet-OV, outperforming the strongest baseline (EMB) by up to 3.53% (R1@0.5 on ActivityNet-OV) and 1.21% (R1@0.7 on Charades-OV). On the standard closed-vocabulary Charades-STA benchmark, HERO sets a new state-of-the-art with R1@0.5 = 61.05 and R1@0.7 = 41.29, surpassing the previous best (FlashVTG) by 0.94% and 3.28% respectively. Ablation confirms that the full HEM + CFRE pipeline contributes +3.20% R1@0.5 and +1.98% R1@0.7 over the EMB baseline on Charades-OV.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HERO consistently outperforms all five baselines on both OV benchmarks. On Charades-OV, the closest competitor is TR-DETR (61.87 R1@0.3), which HERO surpasses by 2.87 points; on R1@0.7, EMB (25.99) is the strongest baseline, beaten by HERO by 1.21 points. On ActivityNet-OV, EMB is the best baseline across all three thresholds, and HERO improves over it by 2.56% (R1@0.3), 3.53% (R1@0.5), and 1.40% (R1@0.7). On the closed-vocabulary Charades-STA benchmark, HERO (61.05 / 41.29) surpasses FlashVTG (60.11 / 38.01) and EMB (58.33 / 39.25), confirming that the hierarchical refinement also benefits standard settings. Notably, DETR-style methods (Moment-DETR, QD-DETR, TR-DETR) show larger relative drops on OV splits compared to EMB, suggesting that query-dependent attention mechanisms are particularly sensitive to unseen vocabulary, whereas HERO's multi-level semantic abstraction mitigates this vulnerability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the established Temporal Sentence Grounding in Video (TSGV) task by introducing an open-vocabulary evaluation setting, building directly on the EMB architecture [16] as a plug-and-play base. It departs from the debiasing line of work (Charades-CD, ActivityNet-CD [22]) by showing that those benchmarks, despite targeting distribution shift, remain largely closed-vocabulary (96% of test sentences use only training-seen words), and constructs OV variants via LLM-based query rewriting. Methodologically, it draws on hierarchical transformer representations and cross-attention mechanisms common in DETR-style grounding models, but repurposes them for multi-level semantic abstraction rather than proposal matching, and adds a contrastive masked-text objective borrowed from NLP robustness literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of Charades-OV and ActivityNet-OV provides the field with the first dedicated benchmarks for evaluating vocabulary generalization in video grounding, filling a gap that prior debiasing efforts inadvertently left open. The finding that even strong baselines like EMB degrade substantially under simple token substitutions (e.g., 'person' → 'human') underscores a practical vulnerability in deployed video-language systems. Open questions remain around whether the LLM-rewritten queries fully capture the diversity of real-world paraphrase, whether the hierarchical depth of four levels generalizes to longer or more complex queries, and how the approach extends to few-shot or continual open-vocabulary settings as the authors note for future work.
