## Problem

Reconstructing complete 3D articulated objects (geometry, texture, and kinematic structure) from sparse multi-state RGB images remains unsolved. Per-object optimization methods are slow, require dense views, and depend on fragile cross-state correspondences, while existing feed-forward models are restricted to a few object categories due to limited training data. No prior approach achieves category-agnostic, feed-forward reconstruction of simulation-ready articulated assets from sparse inputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261428/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes V views (V=1 for monocular, V=4 for multi-view) across T=2 articulation states at 128×128 resolution, with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics; objects are normalized to a unit bounding sphere. The part count P must be specified at inference (estimable via VLMs). Output is a set of P part slots, each yielding a hexa-plane geometry/texture representation and a 14+T-dimensional articulation vector (bounding box, motion type, axis direction, pivot, per-state dynamics), which together define a rigid-body assembly exportable as URDF for simulation.

## Method

ART formulates articulated reconstruction as part-based prediction: a transformer maps image tokens (8×8 patches augmented with stage embeddings, Plücker ray coordinates, and DINOv2 features) to P₀ learnable part slots via a mix of self-attention and cross-attention layers (~75% cross-attention for token efficiency and slot specialization). Two MLP heads per slot decode a hexa-plane representation for geometry/texture and a raw articulation vector remapped to physical parameters (bounding box via sigmoid, unit axis via normalization, motion type via 2-way softmax). Training uses a two-stage curriculum: pre-training on 130k static objects with part decomposition to learn geometry/part priors, followed by fine-tuning on articulated data with coarse-to-fine SDF sharpening and resolution annealing (128→256). Supervision is applied per-part (not on the composite image) using L2, LPIPS, and articulation-parameter losses, with SDF volume rendering providing differentiable rendering gradients.

## Results

On the PartNet-Mobility test set (4 views, 2 states), ART achieves PSNR 27.06, LPIPS 0.049, Chamfer Distance 0.009, and F-Score 0.762, outperforming all optimization-based baselines on image-level metrics by a wide margin. On the StorageFurniture test set (631 objects, monocular input), ART reduces part-level d_gIou to 0.472 and d_cDist to 0.054, roughly halving the error of the best feed-forward baseline SINGAPO (0.831 / 0.095) and cutting Chamfer Distance by 68% (0.0019 vs. 0.0059).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against feed-forward baselines on StorageFurniture, ART dominates: it cuts d_gIou from 1.071 (URDFormer) and 0.831 (SINGAPO) to 0.472, and reduces Chamfer Distance from 0.0536 and 0.0059 to 0.0019, indicating substantially more accurate part geometry and articulation structure. Against optimization-based methods on PartNet-Mobility, ART achieves the best PSNR (27.06 vs. 22.85/21.59/22.35) and LPIPS (0.049 vs. 0.183/0.165/0.176), showing far superior appearance recovery from sparse views. DTA achieves slightly better CD (0.008 vs. 0.009) and F-Score (0.821 vs. 0.762), but this comes at the cost of requiring depth inputs and still lags ART by 5.5 dB in PSNR. ArtGS, which relies on fragile cross-state correspondences, produces fragmented geometry under sparsity, while ART's learned prior yields coherent textured meshes in both states.

## Relation to Prior Work

ART extends the feed-forward 3D reconstruction paradigm established by LRM and LIRM to the articulated-object setting, replacing per-object optimization pipelines (PARIS, DTA, ArtGS) that require dense multi-view captures and fragile cross-state correspondences. It departs from prior feed-forward articulated methods (URDFormer, SINGAPO) by operating on sparse multi-state inputs and predicting a unified per-part representation—geometry, texture, and explicit kinematic parameters—via learnable part slots with cross-attention routing, rather than relying on category-specific priors or retrieval-based assembly. The hexa-plane representation and SDF volume rendering are inherited from the static reconstruction literature, while the canonical rest-state formulation and defined part ordering are novel design choices specific to articulated reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ART is the first category-agnostic, purely feed-forward model to reconstruct simulation-ready articulated assets (exportable as URDF) from sparse multi-state RGB images, addressing a practical bottleneck for VR/AR, robotics, and embodied AI where dense multi-view capture is infeasible. Key limitations include the assumption of known part count and pre-calibrated camera poses, both acknowledged as future work. Open questions include whether the approach scales to objects with highly variable part counts, whether self-calibrated (pose-free) variants are feasible, and whether the learned prior generalizes beyond the indoor-furniture-centric training distribution to outdoor or industrial articulated objects.
