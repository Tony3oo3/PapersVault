## Problem

Current 4D world modeling methods suffer from a fundamental scalability bottleneck: they either depend on expensive, specialized multi-view dynamic video data or require cumbersome offline preprocessing (heavy depth estimators, per-scene Gaussian reconstruction) to prepare training pairs. This prevents the full reconstruction-and-generation pipeline from leveraging the vast pool of cheap, diverse in-the-wild monocular videos, limiting generalization and versatility.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263424/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video (resized to longest edge 560 px) and a text prompt as input, and produces a 4D Gaussian Splatting representation along with a rendered novel-trajectory video at 336×560 resolution over 81 frames. A key design choice is that reconstruction consumes only K sparse key frames (e.g., 11–41 out of 81) while rendering conditions are produced for all N frames via temporal interpolation, keeping the expensive network pass short. The generation model receives multi-modal degraded renderings (RGB, depth, opacity masks) plus Plucker embeddings of the target camera trajectory as conditioning, with the original video frames serving as ground-truth targets.

## Method

NeoVerse couples a pose-free feed-forward 4DGS reconstruction model (built on the VGGT backbone with DINOv2 features) with a Rectified Flow video diffusion model (Wan-T2V 14B) in a single scalable training loop. The reconstruction model introduces bidirectional motion encoding—separate cross-attention branches predicting forward and backward linear/angular velocities—enabling efficient temporal interpolation of Gaussians between sparse key frames without re-running the network. For generation training, the paper simulates degraded novel-view renderings directly from monocular videos using two geometric operations: visibility-based Gaussian culling (to mimic occlusion) and an average geometry filter on rendered depth (to mimic flying-edge pixels and spatial distortion), eliminating the need for curated multi-view data. At inference, a global motion tracking step separates static from dynamic Gaussians for more complete temporal aggregation, and the full pipeline runs in ~20 s on a single A800 GPU (2 s reconstruction + 18 s generation with distillation).

## Results

On static reconstruction, NeoVerse achieves 20.73 PSNR on VRNeRF and 25.34 PSNR on ScanNet++, outperforming the closest competitor AnySplat (18.02 / 22.79) by 2.7 and 2.6 dB respectively. On dynamic reconstruction, it reaches 32.56 PSNR on ADT and 11.56 PSNR on DyCheck, surpassing 4DGT (30.09 / 9.94) by 2.5 and 1.6 dB. For end-to-end novel-trajectory video generation (VBench, 400 in-the-wild test cases), NeoVerse with full frames scores 89.42 Subjective Consistency, 92.79 Background Consistency, and 61.51 Image Quality, while completing inference in 28 s total (10 s recon + 18 s gen) versus 146 s for TrajectoryCrafter and 168 s for ReCamMaster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On static reconstruction (Table 1), NeoVerse decisively outperforms all three feed-forward baselines: AnySplat [32] is the closest at 18.02 PSNR on VRNeRF, trailing NeoVerse by 2.7 dB; Flare [92] and NoPoSplat [83] lag further behind at 12.62 and 11.27 PSNR respectively. On dynamic reconstruction (Table 2), 4DGT [78] (which requires posed input) is the strongest baseline at 30.09 PSNR on ADT, while NeoVerse reaches 32.56 without pose input; MonST3R [89] is far behind at 17.42 PSNR. For generation (Table 3), NeoVerse beats TrajectoryCrafter [87] on every VBench quality metric (e.g., 89.42 vs 83.02 Subjective Consistency, 61.51 vs 54.59 Image Quality) while being 5× faster in total inference (28 s vs 146 s). Against ReCamMaster [2], NeoVerse wins on Subjective Consistency (89.42 vs 88.21), Background Consistency (92.79 vs 91.60), Aesthetic Quality (44.78 vs 44.29), and Image Quality (61.51 vs 58.87), and is 6× faster (28 s vs 168 s); ReCamMaster retains a marginal edge only on Motion Smoothness (98.86 vs 98.67) and Temporal Flicker (96.56 vs 96.51). The paper attributes its generation advantage over TrajectoryCrafter to scalable in-the-wild training data and degradation-simulation-based artifact suppression, while its speed advantage over both baselines stems from sparse-keyframe reconstruction and distillation-compatible control-branch design.

## Relation to Prior Work

NeoVerse extends the VGGT feed-forward geometry backbone into a 4D Gaussian Splatting representation by adding a bidirectional motion-encoding branch (distinguishing forward and backward velocities), departing from the unidirectional temporal modeling in 4DGT. On the generation side, it follows the reconstruction-guided video generation paradigm (as in TrajectoryCrafter, ViewCrafter, GEN3C) but fundamentally restructures the training pipeline: instead of requiring offline depth estimation, offline Gaussian reconstruction, or specialized multi-view dynamic videos, it performs on-the-fly 4DGS reconstruction from sparse key frames and simulates degradation conditions online using simple geometric operations (visibility-based Gaussian culling and average geometry filtering). This positions NeoVerse as a scalability-focused departure from prior reconstruction-generation hybrids that were bottlenecked by data curation or preprocessing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By making the full 4D reconstruction-and-generation pipeline trainable on up to 1M in-the-wild monocular clips without offline preprocessing or specialized multi-view data, NeoVerse removes the primary data and compute bottleneck that has constrained 4D world modeling to narrow domains. The practical impact is broad: the same model supports novel trajectory generation, video editing, stabilization, super-resolution, and 3D tracking from a single monocular video. Key limitations remain—the method requires underlying 3D structure (failing on 2D cartoons), the curated dataset is still bounded by compute resources, and the generation quality, while SOTA, shows only moderate margins over strong baselines like TrajectoryCrafter on VBench metrics. Open questions include whether the simple geometric degradation simulations generalize to more extreme viewpoint changes or whether learned degradation models would further close the gap to purely generative methods.
