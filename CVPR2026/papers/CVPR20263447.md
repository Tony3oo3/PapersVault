## Problem

Existing content-style disentanglement methods rely on the SDXL architecture, which produces lower-quality results, while the newer Flux model's block-level characteristics for separating content from style remain largely unexplored. Two practical challenges persist: disentangled content frequently loses identity and structural integrity, and overfitting prevents the separated content from being flexibly re-embedded into new contexts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263447/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference image and a fixed prompt template ("A <c> object" for content, "A <s> style" for style) as input, fine-tuning only the single stream blocks of Flux via LoRA. It outputs two independent LoRA weight sets — a content LoRA (Blocks 20–29) and a style LoRA (Blocks 30–57) — that can be loaded separately to recover the disentangled content or style, or combined with other LoRAs for merger and recontextualization tasks. Training uses 40 images collected from prior datasets (B-LoRA, UnZipLoRA, StyleDrop) with 1000 steps on a single L20 GPU.

## Method

SplitFlux first identifies through prompt-replacement experiments that Flux's single stream blocks (20–57) govern image generation, with early blocks (20–29) encoding content and later blocks (30–57) encoding style. To prevent content leakage at the semantic boundary, Rank-Constrained Adaptation (RCA) compresses the LoRA rank (dividing by α=2) while amplifying the update magnitude at Blocks 30–31, acting as an implicit regularizer that preserves identity without degrading style quality. For recontextualization, Visual-Gated LoRA (VGRA) splits the content LoRA into a high-rank branch (r=48) capturing the primary subject and a low-rank residual branch (r=16), with a saliency-derived sigmoid gate routing image tokens between branches; a complementary loss enforces directional and positional orthogonality between the two branches to prevent overfitting and enable seamless transfer into new contexts.

## Results

On the Disentanglement task, SplitFlux achieves the best content preservation across all metrics: CLIP-C 0.890, DINO-C 0.808, and VLM-C 67.5%, substantially outperforming LoRA-Flux (0.859 / 0.756 / 17.5%) and UnZipLoRA (0.813 / 0.567 / 15%). For style, SplitFlux matches or slightly exceeds LoRA-Flux (CLIP-S 0.666 vs. 0.665; DINO-S 0.371 vs. 0.358). In the Merger task, SplitFlux again leads on content (CLIP-C 0.855, DINO-C 0.765, VLM-C 77.75%) while UnZipLoRA achieves the highest style scores (CLIP-S 0.701, DINO-S 0.415) at the cost of severe content degradation. SplitFlux uses the fewest trainable parameters (43.65M) among all compared methods, versus 185.8M for UnZipLoRA.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SplitFlux is compared against B-LoRA (SDXL-based), UnZipLoRA (SDXL-based), and LoRA-Flux (Flux-based, fine-tuning only single-stream blocks). Against B-LoRA, SplitFlux wins decisively on every content metric (e.g., VLM-C 67.5% vs. 0% in disentanglement) and also surpasses it on style (VLM-S 44% vs. 6%), while using fewer parameters (43.65M vs. 56.36M). Against UnZipLoRA, SplitFlux dominates on content preservation (CLIP-C 0.890 vs. 0.813; DINO-C 0.808 vs. 0.567; VLM-C 67.5% vs. 15%) and uses roughly 4× fewer trainable parameters (43.65M vs. 185.8M); however, UnZipLoRA achieves higher style scores in the Merger task (CLIP-S 0.701 vs. 0.657; DINO-S 0.415 vs. 0.368), indicating a content–style trade-off. Against the closest Flux-based baseline LoRA-Flux, SplitFlux improves content metrics by a clear margin (CLIP-C +0.031, DINO-C +0.052, VLM-C +50 pp) while matching or slightly exceeding style quality (CLIP-S 0.666 vs. 0.665; DINO-S 0.371 vs. 0.358), confirming that RCA and VGRA enhance content fidelity without sacrificing stylization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the content-style disentanglement line initiated by B-LoRA (Frenkel et al., ECCV 2024) and UnZipLoRA (Liu et al., 2024) from the SDXL/U-Net architecture to the Flux/DiT architecture, where the block structure (double stream vs. single stream) is fundamentally different. The key methodological departure is the systematic prompt-replacement analysis that identifies single stream blocks as the sole carriers of semantic information and further localizes content to early single stream blocks (20–29) and style to later ones (30–57). On top of this architectural insight, the paper introduces two novel LoRA modifications—Rank-Constrained Adaptation to prevent content leakage at the semantic boundary, and Visual-Gated LoRA inspired by Mixture-of-Experts routing—to address identity preservation and recontextualization, neither of which was addressed in prior SDXL-based disentanglement work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The clean identification of which Flux blocks govern content versus style provides a practical blueprint for anyone building personalized generation pipelines on Flux, and the recontextualization capability (re-embedding disentangled content into arbitrary new contexts) extends the utility beyond simple style transfer. However, the evaluation is limited to 40 images and a single GPU, and the block-specific findings are architecture-dependent, so their transferability to other DiT-based models (e.g., SD3, PixArt-σ) remains an open question. A broader limitation is that the method still requires per-image fine-tuning, and the complementary loss in VGRA adds hyperparameter sensitivity (rank split, λ) that may complicate practical deployment.
