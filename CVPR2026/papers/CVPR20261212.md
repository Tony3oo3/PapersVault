## Problem

Standard cross-entropy supervision with one-hot targets causes vanishing gradients on non-target classes once a sample is correctly classified, leaving class dissimilarity information unexploited. Existing regularization (label smoothing, MaxSup) and self-distillation methods partially address this but introduce heuristic biases against the target class or still suffer from exponentially decaying gradients for the most dissimilar classes, failing to enforce structured geometry on non-target representations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261212/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet) fed into conventional CNN or ViT backbones, producing a K-dimensional logit vector that is converted to a class label via softmax. The key operational detail is that the same logit vector is negated to form O-class logits, on which the complementary dissimilarity loss is computed, so no additional network parameters or forward passes are required beyond the standard classification head.

## Method

The paper introduces the Complementary Dissimilarity Loss (CDL), which assigns zero target probability to the correct class and distributes unit mass across all non-target classes (one-cold targets), explicitly supervising what a sample is NOT. Two prior variants are provided: a uniform prior (CDL_uni) that drives all non-target logits to a shared constant, promoting Simplex ETF geometry, and a self-KD prior (CDL_self) that adaptively weights non-target classes by the model's own temperature-scaled predictions, capturing asymmetric dissimilarity structure. The O-class logits are simply the negated standard logits (z̄ = −z), making CDL a drop-in addition to any cross-entropy-based loss via L = L_CE + γ·L_CD. Theoretically, the paper proves that while CE gradients vanish exponentially in the margin, CDL_uni contracts the spread of non-target logits by a margin-independent constant factor, enabling consistent equalization regardless of confidence and driving representations toward neural collapse from early training stages.

## Results

On ImageNet with ResNet-50, CE+CDL_uni reduces top-1 error from 23.72% to 22.61% (−1.11%), and the best configuration OLS+CDL_self achieves 22.31% top-1. On ResNet-101, CE+CDL_uni reduces top-1 error from 22.78% to 21.46% (−1.32%), and OLS+CDL_self reaches 21.07%. On DeiT-Small, CE+CDL_uni achieves 23.12% top-1 error, a 2.49% reduction over CE and 0.39% better than the second-best MaxSup (23.51%). Across CIFAR-100 and TinyImageNet, CDL_uni consistently improves every baseline loss by 0.5–2.5% in error rate, and CDL_self outperforms all self-KD methods (OLS, Zipf's LS, USKD) on both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDL_uni consistently outperforms all regularization baselines: on CIFAR-100 (RN18) it reduces error from 24.98% (CE) to 23.92%, from 23.41% (LS) to 22.97%, and from 23.42% (MaxSup) to 23.23%; on TinyImageNet (DN121) it improves CCE from 23.70% to 22.27% and MaxSup from 24.49% to 23.77%. Against self-KD methods, CE+CDL_self (α=1.0, τ=1.0) achieves 22.44% on CIFAR-100 (ResNet-34), beating OLS (22.62%), Zipf's LS (23.71%), and USKD (23.83%), and 34.81% on TinyImageNet, beating OLS (35.04%) and USKD (36.87%). In open-set recognition on CIFAR-100, CE+CDL_self reaches 66.82% OSCR (without CS) and 68.15% (with CS), surpassing ARPL (63.66% / 65.97%) by +3.16 / +2.18 points. For few-shot learning, CDL_self yields the largest gains in 1-shot CUB (e.g., MatchingNet: 26.85%→22.54%, a 4.31% drop) and 5-shot MiniImageNet (SimpleShot: 9.77%→8.55%). In domain generalization on PACS, adding CDL_uni to DANN reduces mean error from 16.18% to 15.60%, and to Mixup from 17.43% to 16.05%, the largest single-method improvement in that table.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying a gap shared across multiple lines of work: standard cross-entropy ignores non-target class relationships, label smoothing and its variants (LS, MaxSup, TfKD) introduce a global bias against the target class while still suffering vanishing gradients, complement-entropy methods (COT, CCE) only push non-target logits toward uniformity, and self-knowledge distillation (OLS, Zipf's LS, USKD) exploits class similarities but not dissimilarities. The authors introduce the 'opposite-class' concept and one-cold targets, which assign zero probability to the target class and structured nonzero values to all non-target classes, providing a supervision signal that is orthogonal to and combinable with all the above. The theoretical analysis connects this to neural collapse dynamics, showing that CDL contracts non-target logit spread by a margin-independent constant while CE's equalization rate decays exponentially with margin.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the method's simplicity (negate logits, add a weighted cross-entropy term) and its consistent, orthogonal gains across closed-set, open-set, few-shot, and domain-generalization settings with both CNNs and ViTs, suggesting the dissimilarity signal is broadly underexploited. The controllable geometry (uniform prior for Simplex ETF vs. self-KD prior for asymmetric class relationships) offers a principled knob for tuning representation structure. Open questions include whether gains persist under aggressive augmentation (MixUp/CutMix were disabled for the DeiT experiments), how the method interacts with modern foundation-model training recipes, and whether the self-KD adaptive priors can be further refined to capture richer semantic dissimilarity structure beyond what a single temperature-scaled softmax provides.
