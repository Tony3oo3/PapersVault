## Problem

Existing robot pose estimation methods reduce the inherently 3D problem to the 2D domain, neglecting rich priors available in pre-trained 3D models, and depend heavily on labeled data that is scarce in real-world deployments. No prior work leverages pre-trained 3D backbones for robot pose estimation, and the lack of a mechanism to co-evolve 2D and 3D representations limits generalization to out-of-distribution and real-world scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261855/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB image of an articulated robot (Panda, Kuka, or Baxter) and outputs the full system state: n robot joint angles, camera rotation R, and camera translation T relative to the robot base. Internally, the 2D branch (initialized from a pre-trained ResNet-50 rootnet) and the 3D branch (pre-trained DepthAnything-V2 Tiny) jointly produce intermediate states including 2D/3D keypoints, 3D point clouds, and a depth-regulated absolute depth map. Inference runs in a single forward pass at 35 ms, close to 2D-only baselines.

## Method

RoboTAG constructs a topological graph with dual branches where nodes represent system states (joint angles, camera pose, keypoints, point clouds) and edges are of two types: forward edges capturing variable dependencies (via transformations, URDF forward kinematics, or neural networks) and alignment edges denoting cross-branch correspondences. The key structural insight is defining closed loops in this topology—enclosed by exactly one alignment edge and several forward edges—on which 2D-3D consistency losses are applied, causing gradients to flow through the entire loop and train the neural networks on forward edges. This decentralized graph avoids the information bottleneck of direct feature fusion (e.g., Hiwin attention) and enables multi-target optimization across dense predictions like point clouds. Training proceeds in two phases: a supervised phase with both alignment and ground-truth losses on annotated data, followed by an optional alignment-only phase on unannotated in-the-wild images that requires no new labels, enabling the model to act as a semantic tagger for unlabeled robot videos.

## Results

On the DREAM benchmark, RoboTAG achieves the highest average AUC of 76.9% on the ADD curve, outperforming Holistic Pose (75.7%), RoboPEPP (74.0%), and RoboPose (71.3%) by 1.2% on average. The method wins on 5 out of 9 individual benchmarks, with notable gains on real-world datasets (Panda 3C-AK: 83.1 vs. 82.2 for Holistic Pose) and photorealistic synthetic sets (Panda Photo: 84.3 vs. 82.0). In terms of mean joint angle deviation, RoboTAG achieves the best average of 3.6° on Panda DR and 3.3° on Panda Photo, with SOTA on 4–5 of 6 joints per dataset. Inference latency is 35 ms, close to 2D-only baselines (23–27 ms) and far below optimization-based methods (200+ ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RoboTAG outperforms all three learned baselines in average AUC: Holistic Pose (75.7%), RoboPEPP (74.0%), and RoboPose (71.3%). Against Holistic Pose, RoboTAG wins on real-world Panda 3C-AK (83.1 vs. 82.2), Panda Photo (84.3 vs. 82.0), Kuka Photo (76.6 vs. 73.9), and ties on Baxter DR (58.8). Against RoboPEPP, RoboTAG wins on Panda 3C-AK (83.1 vs. 75.3), Panda Photo (84.3 vs. 84.1), Kuka Photo (76.6 vs. 76.1), and Baxter DR (58.8 vs. 34.4), but loses on Panda 3C-XK (75.7 vs. 78.5), Panda 3C-RS (78.3 vs. 80.5), Panda DR (82.5 vs. 83.0), and Kuka DR (75.0 vs. 76.2). The DREAM baselines (which use ground-truth joint angles and PnP for camera pose) are not directly comparable but show that even with oracle joint states, PnP-based camera estimation is fragile under occlusion. The paper attributes its OOD generalization advantage to the 3D branch constraining geometry and the closed-loop consistency supervision providing multi-directional gradients, whereas 2D-only methods suffer from spatial ambiguity and PnP sensitivity to keypoint noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

RoboTAG departs from the dominant 2D-only paradigm in robot pose estimation (RoboPose, RoboPEPP, Holistic Pose) by introducing a 3D branch built on a pre-trained depth model (DepthAnything-V2) and a topological graph structure that couples 2D and 3D representations through forward and alignment edges. The closed-loop consistency supervision mechanism is a novel architectural idea that repurposes the graph topology of the camera-robot system for gradient flow, rather than relying on PnP solvers or iterative render-and-compare loops. The paper positions itself as the first to integrate pre-trained 3D backbones into robot pose estimation, carving new ground at the intersection of 3D vision priors and articulated-object understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to train on unannotated in-the-wild images via 2D-3D alignment losses directly addresses the data scarcity bottleneck that limits real-world deployment of robot pose estimators. However, the in-distribution DR performance is slightly below supervised baselines, and the last joint (gripper) remains poorly constrained due to weak 3D signal, leaving open questions about unified training curricula and hierarchical perception for distal links. The topological graph framing, while conceptually elegant, yields only a 1.2% average AUC gain over strong 2D baselines, suggesting the practical ceiling of 3D priors in this setting may be higher than what current depth models provide.
