## Problem

Existing aerial object goal navigation methods suffer from three critical limitations: they fail to maintain geometrically faithful spatial memory over long-horizon 3D exploration, they couple VLM semantic reasoning directly to action output producing unreliable and uninterpretable policies, and they ignore VLM inference latency, forcing a stop-and-think execution model incompatible with continuous UAV flight. APEX addresses all three by decoupling the task into specialized modules operating at different frequencies.

## Input / Output

The agent consumes RGB-D observations, its own 6-DoF state, and a short high-level text goal description (e.g., 'a red tent') at each timestep. At the control frequency it outputs a discrete navigation action; asynchronously it maintains three 3D grid-based maps (attraction, exploration, obstacle) as persistent memory, and upon target detection it computes the target's precise 3D world coordinate via depth back-projection. Notably, the obstacle map is refreshed every step while the attraction and exploration maps may lag by several steps, reflecting the different computational costs of the modules that produce them.

## Method

APEX is a three-module hierarchical agent running in an asynchronous parallel framework. The Dynamic Spatio-Semantic Mapping module uses Qwen2.5-VL to identify objects and score their semantic relevance to the goal, then Grounded SAM to obtain pixel masks, which are back-projected into a 3D grid via a Reconstruction Projector with a 'closest observation first' update rule for the attraction map, an exponentially-decaying cumulative gain for the exploration map, and binary occupancy for the obstacle map. The RL-based Action Decision module feeds the three maps through separate CNN feature extractors whose concatenated features drive a PPO actor-critic network, trained with a composite reward (linearly-decayed attraction score + inverse exploration score + sparse success/penalty) under a two-stage curriculum that first learns goal-agnostic safe navigation before introducing goal-directed rewards. The Target Grounding module runs Grounding DINO at high frequency on each RGB frame to confirm the target and compute its 3D location. The key architectural insight is that the slow VLM-based mapping writes to shared map buffers asynchronously, so the fast RL policy and detector never block on semantic inference, enabling continuous smooth flight.

## Results

On the UAV-ON benchmark (Overall split), APEX achieves 13.33% Success Rate (SR), 20.00% Oracle Success Rate (OSR), and 10.14% Success-weighted Path Length (SPL), outperforming the strongest baseline L3MVN-Zeroshot by +4.16% SR and +2.77% SPL. APEX also attains the lowest step latency (0.97 s) and the highest average safe distance (345.51 m) among all compared methods, confirming the efficiency gains of its asynchronous parallel framework. On the Unseen split, APEX reaches 16.13% SR and 13.03% SPL, further ahead of the next-best method (L3MVN-Z at 9.69% SR / 9.68% SPL).

## Baselines & Comparisons

APEX outperforms all five baselines on every metric in the Overall split. Against the closest competitor L3MVN-Zeroshot [49] (a ground-based ObjectNav method using MLLM + frontier maps), APEX gains +4.16% SR (13.33 vs 9.17) and +2.77% SPL (10.14 vs 7.37), while also improving OSR from 15.83 to 20.00. The end-to-end MLLM direct-navigation baseline (Qwen2.5-VL, MLLM-N [3]) performs near-randomly (0.83% SR), confirming that raw VLM outputs are unreliable for aerial control. CLIP-H [43] and AOA-F [43], both from the UAV-ON paper, achieve 5.83% and 7.50% SR respectively, well below APEX. TravelUAV [39], an AVLN method adapted to discrete actions, reaches 7.48% SR but lags APEX by nearly 6 points. In terms of operational efficiency (Table 2), APEX's 0.97 s step latency is 23% faster than L3MVN-Z (1.26 s) and 5.5× faster than AOA-F (5.29 s), while its 345.51 m safe distance exceeds all baselines, validating the RL-based action module's contribution to collision avoidance.

## Relation to Prior Work

APEX carves new ground by decoupling the three sub-problems of aerial object navigation—spatial memory, action control, and target identification—into specialized modules operating at different frequencies, rather than following the end-to-end VLM-to-action paradigm used by TravelUAV, AOA-F, and MLLM-Direct Navigation. It repurposes existing components (Qwen2.5-VL for semantic grounding, Grounded SAM for segmentation, Grounding DINO for detection, PPO for control) but introduces a novel 3D grid-based map with attraction/exploration/obstacle channels as an interpretable memory carrier, and an asynchronous parallel framework that bypasses VLM inference latency. Conceptually, it extends ground-based ObjectNav methods (L3MVN, VoroNav) to the 3D aerial setting while departing from AVLN's reliance on step-by-step language instructions in favor of a single goal description.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The asynchronous parallel architecture offers a practical recipe for deploying VLM-based embodied agents in latency-sensitive settings, a concern largely overlooked by prior AVLN work that assumes a stop-and-think execution model. The decoupled design improves interpretability and safety (highest safe distance in Table 2), which is critical for real-world aerial deployment. However, absolute success rates remain modest (~13% SR), the higher Navigation Error suggests the exploration strategy trades off precision, and evaluation is limited to a single benchmark (UAV-ON, 14 environments). Open questions include whether fine-tuning the VLM backbone for aerial semantics, or replacing PPO with more sample-efficient RL, would yield further gains, and whether the 3D grid map scales to larger or more cluttered environments.
