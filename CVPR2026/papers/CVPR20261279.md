## Problem

Existing hyperbolic vision-language models (MERU, HyCoCLIP, ATMG) model part-whole entailment in hyperbolic space but treat all parts equally, ignoring that each part has a different degree of semantic representativeness to the whole scene. This uniform treatment leads to degraded multi-object alignment, collapse of embeddings into narrow regions, and inefficient use of the hyperbolic embedding space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261279/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes whole-scene RGB images, their paired text captions, and part images (cropped regions annotated in the GRIT dataset, which provides 20.5M grounded pairs and 35.9M part-level annotations). It produces hyperbolic embeddings in the Lorentz (hyperboloid) model for both image and text modalities at both global and part levels, along with a per-part uncertainty scalar derived from the embedding's geodesic radius. Downstream, these embeddings yield zero-shot class predictions, cross-modal retrieval rankings, and multi-label scores.

## Method

UNCHA quantifies part-to-whole semantic representativeness as hyperbolic uncertainty: the geodesic radius from the origin is mapped through a smooth monotonic function (log(1+exp(-‖x‖₂))) so that more representative parts (closer to the origin) receive lower uncertainty. This uncertainty modulates the global-local contrastive loss via per-part adaptive temperatures (τ_un = exp(u/2)·τ_gl), so less representative parts contribute less to the alignment signal. For entailment, UNCHA introduces a Leaky-ReLU-like angular term to the hinge-based entailment loss to preserve fine-grained gradients even inside the cone, and calibrates uncertainty through a stop-gradient-weighted entailment term plus an entropy regularizer that prevents collapse to uniform uncertainty. The full objective combines the uncertainty-guided contrastive loss (global, local, and global-local terms with separate temperatures) and the entailment loss (inter-modal, intra-modal, and uncertainty-calibration components), trained end-to-end with ViT-S/16 or ViT-B/16 encoders on GRIT.

## Results

On zero-shot image classification with ViT-B/16, UNCHA achieves 48.8% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet, 90.4% on CIFAR-10, and 63.2% on CIFAR-100, outperforming the strongest baseline HyCoCLIP (45.8%, 88.8%, 60.1%) by 3.0, 1.6, and 3.1 points respectively. On zero-shot retrieval (COCO), UNCHA reaches 72.7 R@1 for text retrieval and 60.0 R@1 for image retrieval, surpassing HyCoCLIP (72.0 and 58.4). On multi-object representation (ComCo, 5 objects), UNCHA achieves 81.18 mAP versus 72.90 for HyCoCLIP, and on zero-shot multi-label classification (COCO), it reaches 82.14 mAP versus 80.43.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UNCHA is compared against four baselines: CLIP (Euclidean contrastive VLM), MERU (hyperbolic inter-modal entailment), ATMG (angle-based hyperbolic contrastive), and HyCoCLIP (hyperbolic intra-modal part-whole entailment). UNCHA consistently outperforms all four across zero-shot classification, retrieval, and multi-object benchmarks. The closest competitor is HyCoCLIP, which UNCHA surpasses by 3.0 points on ImageNet (48.8 vs 45.8), 3.1 on CIFAR-100 (63.2 vs 60.1), and a substantial 8.3 points on ComCo 5-object mAP (81.18 vs 72.90). ATMG performs worst overall, trailing UNCHA by 14.5 points on ImageNet (34.3 vs 48.8) and 16.0 on ComCo 5-obj (45.82 vs 81.18), suggesting its angle-only metric struggles with compositional scenes. MERU sits between CLIP and HyCoCLIP on most tasks but is outperformed by UNCHA by 8.7 points on ImageNet. The largest relative gains for UNCHA appear on fine-grained and multi-object tasks (Cars: 14.0 vs 11.6; SimCo 5-obj: 90.65 vs 82.85), indicating that uncertainty-guided part-whole alignment is most beneficial when compositional structure is complex.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNCHA extends the hyperbolic vision-language pretraining line initiated by MERU (inter-modal entailment), refined by ATMG (angle-based alignment), and expanded by HyCoCLIP (intra-modal part-whole entailment). The key departure is that prior methods treat all part-whole relationships with uniform strength, whereas UNCHA introduces hyperbolic uncertainty—derived from the geodesic radius—as a per-part semantic representativeness signal that adaptively modulates both contrastive temperatures and entailment calibration. The uncertainty model itself builds on prior observations that hyperbolic radius correlates with abstractness and confidence in segmentation, retrieval, and multimodal settings, but repurposes it specifically for compositional part-whole weighting in VLM pretraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By explicitly modeling that parts contribute unequally to whole-scene semantics, UNCHA addresses a structural weakness in hyperbolic VLMs where embeddings collapse into narrow regions and part-whole ordering is poorly resolved. The entropy-regularized uncertainty calibration is a practical mechanism for preventing this collapse, and the consistent gains across 16 classification benchmarks, retrieval, and multi-object compositional tasks (ComCo/SimCo) suggest the approach generalizes beyond the GRIT training distribution. Open questions include whether the uncertainty model scales to open-vocabulary or video settings, whether the fixed GRIT part annotations limit the learned representativeness signal, and whether the modest but consistent improvements over HyCoCLIP (+2–3 points on ImageNet) translate to downstream applications like open-set detection or embodied reasoning where compositional fidelity is critical.
