## Problem

Existing faithfulness measures for unsupervised concept-based explanation methods (U-CBEMs) are inadequate: they either rely on complex reconstruction-based surrogates that hide interpretability costs from the user, or use deletion-based proxies that suffer from off-manifold and baseline issues. This has led the field to incorrectly conclude that state-of-the-art U-CBEMs are faithful, when in fact they are not.

## Input / Output

SURF takes an image, extracts the model's final-layer intermediate representation h, applies the U-CBEM's concept projection P(h; V_i) to obtain K concept activations, and combines them with the U-CBEM's concept importances A_i to produce a surrogate output. The output is a pair of faithfulness scores: SURF_MAE (mean absolute error between model and surrogate logits, averaged over all C classes) and SURF_EMD (Earth Mover's Distance between model and surrogate probability distributions). The surrogate operates exclusively on the final linear layer, so no spatial dimensions remain (H=W=1), and no additional parameters are learned.

## Method

SURF's key insight is that the model's final linear layer already decomposes each output as a sum of projections of embeddings onto learned class-specific directions, scaled by their norms. The surrogate simply replaces these learned directions with the U-CBEM's discovered CAVs and their importances, yielding a parameter-free linear prediction of the output: ŷ_i = Σ_j Σ_k α_{i,k} P(h_j; V_i)_k. This avoids reconstruction of the embedding and introduces no learnable parameters, unlike prior surrogates (ICE-Eval's inverse projection, C-SHAP-Eval's learned MLP). Two complementary metrics are defined: SURF_MAE captures logit-space error across all classes, while SURF_EMD captures probability-space error via Earth Mover's Distance, together preventing the failure mode where a surrogate gets the predicted class right but ignores the rest of the distribution. A measure-over-measure sanity check (Perfect → Rand Imp → Full Rand) validates that only SURF correctly detects decreasing faithfulness as explanations are randomized.

## Results

Using SURF to benchmark seven U-CBEMs on Caltech-101 (ResNet-50), no method achieves faithful explanations: the best, SAE, still yields a SURF_EMD of 0.195 and SURF_MAE of 1.04, indicating substantial probability- and logit-space errors. On CelebA multi-attribute prediction (MobileNetV2), only SAE (SURF_MAE=3.16, Attr-Acc=81.9%) and MCD (SURF_MAE=2.83, Attr-Acc=96.6%) perform meaningfully above random. On UTK-Face age estimation (ViT), SAE achieves the lowest error at 3.67 years (SURF_MAE). In the measure-over-measure sanity check, SURF is the only measure that correctly reports decreasing faithfulness as explanations are progressively randomized, while ICE-Eval reports perfect scores even with random importances and C-SHAP-Eval paradoxically improves under full randomization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAE is the most faithful U-CBEM across all three tasks, achieving SURF_MAE of 1.04 and SURF_EMD of 0.195 on Caltech-101, roughly 2× lower error than the next-best HU-MCD (1.97 / 0.384) and 3× lower than CDISCO (3.40 / 0.932). MCD and HU-MCD, which use concept subspaces rather than single CAVs, consistently outperform single-vector methods (ICE, CRAFT, C-SHAP, CDISCO) by 0.6–1.4 in SURF_MAE, confirming that subspace representations capture more of the model's computation. ICE achieves high Top-1 (98.9%) but a SURF_EMD of 0.628, revealing that its explanations reproduce the predicted class well while failing on the rest of the output distribution. C-SHAP and CDISCO perform near-random on Top-1 (6.3% and 0.2% respectively) and have the highest SURF_EMD values (0.882 and 0.932), indicating their class-specific CAVs are largely unfaithful when applied out-of-distribution. In the measure-over-measure validation, SURF correctly degrades from 0.00 (Perfect) to 2.70 (Rand Imp) to 3.17 (Full Rand) in SURF_MAE, whereas ICE-Eval stays at 0.00 through Rand Imp and C-SHAP-Eval(CEL) actually improves from 1.27 to 181.7 in Norm L1 under Full Rand, demonstrating that prior measures cannot reliably rank explanation faithfulness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the formal faithfulness definition from Ribeiro et al. (LIME) and the growing U-CBEM literature (ACE, C-SHAP, ICE, CRAFT, CDISCO, MCD, HU-MCD, SAE), but departs sharply from prior evaluation practice. It unifies the fragmented faithfulness measures used across U-CBEM papers under a common framework, then argues that both deletion-based proxies (unreliable due to off-manifold perturbations and ambiguous baselines) and prior surrogate-based measures (ICE-Eval, C-SHAP-Eval) fail basic desiderata: they use overly complex or reconstruction-based surrogates, ignore concept importances, and measure error only on the predicted class. SURF replaces all of this with a parameter-free linear surrogate that directly mirrors the model's final layer computation, making it the first measure to pass a randomized-explanation sanity check.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that SOTA U-CBEMs previously reported as faithful are not, even the best (SAE) shows substantial probability-space error—challenges a working assumption in the explainability community and suggests that the interpretability-appealing explanations users see may not actually reflect model computation. SURF is likely to become the standard faithfulness metric for U-CBEMs, and the faithfulness-vs-parsimony analysis (Fig. 2) gives practitioners a principled way to choose the number of concepts rather than setting it arbitrarily. Key limitations: SURF only applies to the final linear layer (not intermediate layers with non-linear downstream computation), and the paper does not address whether any U-CBEM can be made faithful with a reasonable number of concepts, leaving the fundamental question of whether concept-based explanations can simultaneously be interpretable and faithful open.
