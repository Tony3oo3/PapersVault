## Problem

Existing instruction-driven image editing methods operate purely in 2D and lack 3D spatial understanding, making them unable to execute precise object relocations in scenes with occlusion or multi-depth layers. Conversely, 3D-aware editing methods rely on explicit single-image reconstruction, which is inherently ill-posed and degrades under complex scene conditions. The paper asks whether 3D spatial priors can be implicitly injected into a 2D diffusion editing framework without requiring full 3D reconstruction at inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261867/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB image and a natural language instruction describing a spatial movement (e.g., 'move the cup onto the windowsill') as input, and produces an edited image with the target object relocated while preserving scene consistency. At inference, no explicit depth map, 3D model, or camera parameters are required; the 3D geometry encoder (VGGT) processes the input image internally to extract spatial features. During training, the target image's monocular depth map (from Depth Anything) serves as an auxiliary supervision signal but is not needed at test time.

## Method

SpatialDiff introduces two key mechanisms into a Flux-Kontext DiT backbone. First, Implicit 3D Spatial Modeling (ISM) uses VGGT's Transformer backbone as a 3D Visual Geometry Encoder to extract spatial-aware features from the input image, then a Connector module with 8 cross-attention layers and 1024 learnable query tokens aligns these features into the DiT latent space; the aligned spatial tokens are concatenated with image and instruction tokens for joint processing. Second, Global Spatial Supervision (GSS) applies Latent Depth Supervision: a learnable spatial decode head projects DiT-processed spatial tokens into VAE latent space, and an MSE loss enforces consistency with the VAE-encoded depth map of the target image, providing smooth geometric constraints without pixel-level over-constraining. Training proceeds in two stages—first optimizing only the Connector with standard flow-matching loss, then jointly fine-tuning the DiT backbone (via LoRA rank 64), Connector, and spatial decoder with the GSS loss weighted at λ=0.01.

## Results

On SpatialBench, SpatialDiff achieves the best scores across all six metrics evaluated by both GPT-5 and Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct. The headline Semantic Consistency (SC) score is 0.803 (GPT-SC) and 0.778 (Qwen-SC), substantially exceeding the next-best method Qwen-Image-Editing (0.666 / 0.632). Perceptual Quality (PQ) reaches 0.886 (GPT-PQ) and 0.838 (Qwen-PQ), while the overall score O = (SC × PQ)^{1/2} attains 0.843 (GPT-O) and 0.807 (Qwen-O), confirming balanced gains in both instruction adherence and visual fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialDiff outperforms all five baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, Qwen-Image-Editing, achieves a comparable PQ (0.882 vs. 0.886 GPT-PQ) but lags significantly in SC (0.666 vs. 0.803 GPT-SC), indicating its powerful pretraining preserves image quality yet struggles to follow fine-grained spatial movement instructions. Flux-Kontext attains a high PQ (0.848) but the lowest SC (0.292), revealing that it largely avoids editing rather than performing correct spatial relocations. Step1X-Edit sits in the middle (GPT-SC 0.484, GPT-PQ 0.785), while BAGEL and OmniGen2 trail further behind on both axes (GPT-O ≤ 0.511). The consistent ranking across GPT-5 and Qwen3-VL evaluators, as well as the user study (SpatialDiff scores 4.711/5 in H-SC vs. 3.513 for Qwen-Image-Editing), reinforces that the gap is not an artifact of a single evaluator.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpatialDiff sits at the intersection of two lines of work: instruction-driven 2D image editing (Flux-Kontext, Step1X-Edit, BAGEL, Qwen-Image-Edit) and explicit 3D-aware editing (Diffusion Handles, Diff3DEdit, LACONIC). It departs from the 3D-aware line by rejecting explicit single-image reconstruction—acknowledging its ill-posedness under occlusion and depth ambiguity—and instead injects implicit 3D geometric priors from VGGT into the DiT latent space via a cross-attention Connector. The Global Spatial Supervision mechanism, which uses latent-space depth as an auxiliary training signal, is a novel training strategy not seen in either prior line, bridging the gap between 2D flexibility and 3D spatial reasoning without requiring any 3D reconstruction at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that implicit 3D spatial modeling can substantially outperform both pure 2D editing and explicit 3D reconstruction pipelines for spatial object movement, offering a more practical and scalable path toward spatially coherent image editing. However, the evaluation is limited to 512×512 resolution, a single base model (Flux-Kontext), and a custom 100-image benchmark (SpatialBench), leaving open whether the approach generalizes to higher resolutions, more complex multi-object scenes, or editing tasks beyond rigid object relocation. The reliance on LLM-based evaluators (GPT-5, Qwen3-VL) for scoring also raises questions about metric robustness. Future work could explore whether the implicit 3D token mechanism transfers to video editing, 3D scene generation, or more general spatial reasoning tasks.
