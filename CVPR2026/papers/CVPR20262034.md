## Problem

Existing perceptual video codecs rely on image-based generative priors (GANs or diffusion models) that operate frame-by-frame without temporal modeling, causing severe perceptual flickering and motion inconsistency at ultra-low bitrates (< 0.03 bpp). No prior work has integrated a video-native diffusion prior into a neural video codec to perform sequence-level generative refinement that preserves both texture sharpness and temporal coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262034/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video clip of (1+T) frames at H×W×3 resolution, encodes it via a 3D causal VAE (Wan2.1) into spatio-temporal latents of shape (1+T/4) × H/8 × W/8 × 16, and produces a compact entropy-coded bitstream plus a reconstructed video. The refinement stage operates on the decoded latent sequence with 5 deterministic flow-matching steps at a fixed partial noise level t_N = 0.7, meaning the diffusion prior corrects compression artifacts rather than generating from scratch. Training uses 256×256 patches on 5-frame clips (Stage I) and 9- to 13-frame clips (Stage II), with evaluation on 96-frame sequences from HEVC-B, UVG, and MCL-JCV.

## Method

GNVC-VD unifies a contextual latent codec (following DCVC-RT's conditional coding design) with a flow-matching refinement module built on a frozen pre-trained VideoDiT (Wan2.1). The key insight is that refinement initializes from the decoded spatio-temporal latents at a partial noise level t_N rather than from pure Gaussian noise, so the model learns a correction velocity Δv_fine that adapts the generative prior to compression-induced degradation instead of performing full generation. Conditioning adaptor blocks (inspired by VACE) are inserted into intermediate DiT transformer layers to inject compression-aware contextual features extracted from the codec, aligning the diffusion manifold with the quantized latent distribution. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage I jointly optimizes the codec and adaptor with a latent-level rate-distortion objective plus a conditional flow-matching loss to align refined latents with ground-truth VAE latents; Stage II fine-tunes the full pipeline in the pixel domain with LPIPS, MSE, and rate terms to maximize perceptual quality. This sequence-level design jointly enhances I- and P-frame latents, capturing long-range temporal dependencies that frame-wise image priors cannot model.

## Results

On the ultra-low bitrate regime (< 0.03 bpp), GNVC-VD achieves BD-rate reductions of −89.4% (LPIPS) and −94.5% (DISTS) on HEVC-B, −90.8% and −96.2% on MCL-JCV, and −86.5% and −96.1% on UVG, all anchored to VVC. In temporal consistency on HEVC-B, GNVC-VD attains an FVD of 68.1 (vs. 103.9 for GLC-Video and 232.1 for DCVC-RT) and a CLIP-F of 0.982, indicating substantially more stable motion reconstruction than prior generative codecs. The method operates below 0.01 bpp in the most extreme settings while maintaining coherent fine textures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest generative baseline GLC-Video, GNVC-VD wins decisively in LPIPS BD-rate on all three datasets (e.g., −89.4% vs. −79.1% on HEVC-B, −86.5% vs. −60.0% on UVG) and in FVD (68.1 vs. 103.9), confirming superior temporal stability. On DISTS, the two methods are nearly tied on HEVC-B (−94.5 vs. −94.8) and MCL-JCV (−96.2 vs. −99.7), with GLC-Video holding a slight edge, but GNVC-VD dominates on UVG (−96.1 vs. −10.3). Compared to learned codecs DCVC-RT and DCVC-FM, GNVC-VD achieves dramatically lower BD-rates in LPIPS (−89.4% vs. −20.9% and −10.7% on HEVC-B) while incurring a modest increase in E_warp (66.6 vs. 59.2/59.8), a trade-off inherent to generative reconstruction. Against traditional codecs HEVC and VVC, GNVC-VD reduces LPIPS BD-rate by roughly 90 percentage points, though traditional codecs retain lower E_warp (23–24) due to their non-generative nature. PLVC is inconsistent across datasets (positive BD-rate on MCL-JCV LPIPS at +37.3%) and is clearly outperformed by GNVC-VD in both perceptual quality and temporal coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

GNVC-VD sits at the intersection of three research threads: conditional neural video compression (following DCVC-RT's context-based transform coding for the latent codec), generative image compression (which introduced diffusion priors for perceptual reconstruction at ultra-low bitrates), and video diffusion models (Wan2.1's VideoDiT as a sequence-level generative prior). The key conceptual departure is from prior generative video codecs like GLC-Video and DiffVC, which repurpose image-domain diffusion or GAN priors frame-by-frame and thus cannot model long-range temporal dynamics. By instead performing flow-matching refinement directly in the 3D spatio-temporal latent space of a pre-trained video DiT, GNVC-VD is the first to treat decoding as a sequence-level conditional denoising problem rather than independent per-frame enhancement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work demonstrates that the temporal flickering plaguing current generative video codecs is fundamentally an architectural limitation of image-based priors, and that a video-native diffusion prior can resolve it while simultaneously improving perceptual quality at extreme bitrates (<0.03 bpp). The practical impact is significant for streaming and storage applications where ultra-low bitrate is unavoidable, though the reliance on a large frozen VideoDiT backbone and a multi-step flow integration at inference raises questions about real-time deployability. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences (training uses only 5–13 frames), whether it generalizes to other video diffusion backbones, and whether the two-stage training protocol can be simplified or made end-to-end.
