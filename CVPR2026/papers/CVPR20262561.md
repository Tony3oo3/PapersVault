## Problem

Existing 3D point cloud domain adaptation methods are either computationally heavy encoder-based approaches or CLIP-based extensions that fail to bridge the synthetic-to-real gap due to CLIP's 2D RGB pretraining bias. No prior work combines CLIP's language-grounded semantic priors with unsupervised 3D domain adaptation in a parameter-efficient, few-shot setting, leaving both the 2D-to-3D modality gap and the source-target domain gap unaddressed simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262561/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each 3D point cloud is projected online into M=10 scatter-based depth maps (no post-rendering), which serve as the visual input to a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder. A lightweight PointNet encoder processes the raw point cloud to extract geometric descriptors used in prompt generation, and GPT-5 generates class-specific 3D-aware textual descriptions. The output is a single class label over 10 categories, aggregated across the entropy-selected confident views per point cloud.

## Method

CLIPoint3D adapts a frozen CLIP backbone through four complementary modules: (1) knowledge-driven prompt tuning, where LLM-generated 3D-aware text prompts and PointNet-derived geometric features are refined via a shared-query multi-head cross-attention to produce modality-specific visual and textual prompts; (2) LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning (rank 16) on both CLIP encoders to capture 3D-specific residual cues without disrupting pretrained alignment; (3) entropy-guided view selection that retains only the top-50% lowest-entropy projections to suppress ambiguous multi-view predictions; and (4) dual uncertainty-aware alignment losses — an entropy-weighted prototype alignment for class-conditional coupling and an entropy-regularized optimal transport for global distribution matching — jointly minimizing a unified objective with cross-entropy and geometric orthogonality regularizers. The paper further derives a surrogate generalization bound showing how the OT term and prototype term tighten the classical HΔH domain adaptation bound.

## Results

On GraspNetPC-10, CLIPoint3D-B (LoRA on both CLIP encoders) achieves 92.2% average accuracy across four adaptation directions, outperforming the best encoder-based baseline GAI (75.8%) by +16.4%. On PointDA-10, CLIPoint3D-V (LoRA on vision encoder only) reaches 75.7% average accuracy, surpassing the strongest prior method 3DeNet (72.2%) by +3.5%. Zero-shot CLIP baselines (ZS-CLIP, PointCLIP, PointCLIPv2) remain far behind, scoring only 17.4–43.3% across the two benchmarks, confirming that without explicit adaptation mechanisms CLIP cannot bridge the synthetic-to-real 3D gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLIPoint3D outperforms all 12 baselines on both benchmarks. Against the strongest encoder-based 3D UDA methods, CLIPoint3D-B gains +16.4% over GAI (75.8%→92.2%) and +17.8% over PointDAN (74.4%→92.2%) on GraspNetPC-10, while on PointDA-10 CLIPoint3D-V leads 3DeNet (72.2%) by +3.5% and MLSP (71.0%) by +4.7%. The adversarial baseline DANN [14] and self-supervised RS [48] are consistently the weakest encoder-based methods, scoring 56.8% and 66.0% on PointDA-10 respectively. CLIP-based zero-shot baselines (ZS-CLIP, PointCLIP, PointCLIPv2) are dramatically outperformed, with margins exceeding 50 percentage points on GraspNetPC-10 (e.g., 92.2% vs. 17.4% for ZS-CLIP), demonstrating that frozen CLIP without adaptation cannot handle 3D domain shift. Notably, CLIPoint3D achieves these gains with only 9–11M trainable parameters versus 161M for GAST, confirming a superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: (1) CLIP-based 3D understanding (PointCLIP, PointCLIP v2, CG3D), which projects point clouds into multi-view depth maps and leverages CLIP's image encoder for zero-/few-shot classification, and (2) conventional unsupervised 3D point cloud domain adaptation (DANN, PointDAN, GAST, MLSP), which trains heavy 3D encoders with adversarial, self-supervised, or pseudo-labeling objectives. CLIPoint3D departs from both by treating CLIP's frozen ViT backbone as the 3D feature extractor and introducing a knowledge-driven prompt tuning module that fuses LLM-generated semantic priors with PointNet-derived geometric cues, combined with LoRA-based PEFT and entropy-regularized optimal transport alignment—none of which appear in prior CLIP-3D or 3D-UDA work. The 2D CLIP UDA literature (AD-CLIP, DAPL, PADCLIP) informs the prompt-space alignment strategy but does not address the modality gap between textured RGB and sparse depth projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a frozen VLM backbone with only 9–11M trainable parameters can outperform purpose-built 3D encoders with 22–161M parameters on 3D UDA, suggesting a more scalable path for deploying domain-adaptive 3D perception in resource-constrained settings (robotics, AR). However, the evaluation is limited to 10-class classification on two small benchmarks, and the ScanNet (real indoor) domain still shows notable weakness, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to larger-scale or denser 3D tasks such as segmentation or detection. The reliance on multi-view depth projection also inherits information loss from 3D-to-2D flattening, and the use of GPT-5 for prompt generation raises reproducibility and cost concerns for the broader community.
