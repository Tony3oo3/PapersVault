## Problem

Extending diffusion transformers from ~1K to native 4K resolution across diverse aspect ratios exposes a tightly coupled failure mode spanning positional encoding drift, VAE high-frequency loss, and timestep/frequency-imbalanced optimization. Existing approaches address these factors in isolation, and public 4K corpora remain small (≤10⁵ images), AR-biased, and lack the structured metadata needed for resolution- and AR-aware training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263491/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

UltraFlux takes a natural-language text prompt (English or Chinese) and a target aspect ratio as input, and produces a single native 4K RGB image at the requested resolution (e.g., 4096×4096, 4096×2048, 5120×2880, 5952×2496). The model operates on F16-VAE latents (16× spatial downsampling) to keep 50-step sampling tractable, and supports a wide spectrum of ARs from 1:1 through 2.39:1 without per-AR fine-tuning. An optional LLM prompt refiner (GPT-4O) can preprocess the text prompt before generation.

## Method

UltraFlux keeps the Flux DiT backbone intact and co-designs four targeted modifications. (1) Resonance 2D RoPE with YaRN snaps each rotary frequency to the nearest integer number of cycles within the training window, then applies a band-aware linear ramp (parameterized by cycle count) to interpolate between position-interpolation scaling and no scaling at inference, making the encoding training-window-, frequency-, and AR-aware. (2) An SNR-Aware Huber Wavelet objective measures residuals in an orthonormal DWT space with a Pseudo-Huber penalty whose threshold c(t) grows with SNR, combined with Min-SNR timestep weighting to rebalance gradients across noise levels and frequency bands. (3) Stage-wise Aesthetic Curriculum Learning (SACL) first fine-tunes on the full 1M-image corpus over all timesteps, then restricts Stage 2 to high-noise timesteps and the top-5% ArtiMuse-scored images, concentrating high-aesthetic supervision where the generative prior is most underdetermined. (4) A non-adversarial VAE post-training stage fine-tunes the Flux F16 decoder with wavelet, perceptual, and L2 losses on a flatness-filtered subset, recovering fine 4K detail without GAN instability. All components are trained on the newly introduced MultiAspect-4K-1M corpus (1M native-4K images with controlled multi-AR coverage, VLM-based quality/aesthetic scores, and bilingual captions).

## Results

On the Aesthetic-Eval@4096 benchmark (4096×4096), UltraFlux achieves the best FID (143.11), HPSv3 (11.47), ArtiMuse (68.36), and MUSIQ (46.13) among all open-source baselines, outperforming the closest native-4K competitor Sana by 1.06 FID, 0.64 HPSv3, 4.64 ArtiMuse, and 1.05 MUSIQ. Against the closed-source Seedream 4.0 (with a GPT-4O prompt refiner), UltraFlux matches or exceeds it on HPSv3 (12.03 vs. 11.98), Q-Align (4.93 vs. 4.71), and MUSIQ (45.93 vs. 30.21), while trailing on FID (147.06 vs. 132.87) and PickScore (23.25 vs. 23.52). Across diverse non-square aspect ratios (2:1, 1:2, 16:9, 2.39:1), UltraFlux consistently beats Sana on FID and HPSv3, with the largest FID gap reaching 10.88 at 16:9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two training-free scaling methods, UltraFlux dominates: it beats ScaleCrafter by 20.91 FID and 4.64 HPSv3, and FouriScale by 21.60 FID and 0.28 HPSv3, while also leading on ArtiMuse and MUSIQ. Versus the native-4K foundation model Sana, UltraFlux wins on FID (143.11 vs. 144.17), HPSv3 (11.47 vs. 10.83), ArtiMuse (68.36 vs. 63.72), and MUSIQ (46.13 vs. 45.08), but trails slightly on PickScore (22.69 vs. 23.18), CLIP Score (34.62 vs. 35.49), and Q-Align (4.85 vs. 4.89). Compared to Diffusion-4K (another Flux-based 4K model), UltraFlux improves FID by 9.32, HPSv3 by 2.55, and MUSIQ by 18.62, indicating that the co-designed positional encoding, wavelet loss, and aesthetic curriculum collectively yield substantial gains over wavelet-only fine-tuning. Against the closed-source Seedream 4.0 (both with LLM prompt refiners), UltraFlux edges out on HPSv3 (12.03 vs. 11.98), Q-Align (4.93 vs. 4.71), and MUSIQ (45.93 vs. 30.21), but remains behind on FID (147.06 vs. 132.87) and PickScore (23.25 vs. 23.52), suggesting the proprietary model retains an advantage in distributional fidelity and human preference. In multi-AR settings (Tables 3–4), UltraFlux consistently outperforms Sana on FID and HPSv3 across all tested ratios, with the largest advantage at 16:9 (FID 142.43 vs. 153.31) and 2.39:1 (HPSv3 11.76 vs. 8.57), confirming that Resonance 2D RoPE with YaRN effectively stabilizes generation under extreme aspect-ratio extrapolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

UltraFlux keeps the Flux DiT backbone intact and addresses 4K bottlenecks through three targeted modifications: (i) adapting Resonance RoPE and YaRN—both originally developed for 1D LLM context-length extrapolation—to 2D spatial token grids with band-aware, AR-aware frequency snapping; (ii) extending Diffusion-4K's wavelet-based VAE post-training with an SNR-aware Pseudo-Huber penalty and Min-SNR weighting; and (iii) introducing a stage-wise aesthetic curriculum that couples noise-level scheduling with data filtering. The key conceptual departure from prior native-4K work (Sana, Diffusion-4K, PixArt-Σ) is the explicit data-model co-design framing: rather than treating positional encoding, VAE compression ratio, and training objective as orthogonal knobs, the paper jointly optimizes them alongside a purpose-built 1M-image multi-AR corpus, arguing that these factors interact in ways that leave substantial quality on the table when addressed in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MultiAspect-4K-1M dataset (1M images, controlled multi-AR coverage, bilingual captions, VLM/IQA metadata) is a significant open resource that addresses the chronic under-scale and AR imbalance of existing 4K corpora, enabling systematic study of resolution-AR coupling. The finding that a non-adversarial, ~4K-step VAE post-training recipe suffices to recover 4K reconstruction fidelity challenges the common assumption that GAN losses are necessary for high-resolution VAE decoders, potentially simplifying future 4K pipelines. Open questions include whether the co-design recipe transfers to other DiT backbones beyond Flux, whether 1M images is sufficient for full 4K convergence, and whether the multi-AR generalization holds for extreme ARs beyond the tested 2.39:1 range. The practical impact is high for open-source 4K generation, as the paper demonstrates that a 1M-image-trained model with a prompt refiner can match a leading proprietary system (Seedream 4.0) on most metrics.
