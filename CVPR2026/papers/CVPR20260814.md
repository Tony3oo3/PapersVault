## Problem

Existing 6-DOF grasp generation methods can generalize to novel objects and scenes but require full retraining whenever the gripper morphology or closing motion changes, making grasp generation the least transferable component in cross-embodiment pick-and-place systems. This paper asks whether a single model can achieve zero-shot 6-DOF grasping across both novel objects and entirely unseen gripper types (parallel, revolute, and high-DOF multi-finger) without hardware-specific retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260814/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an object/scene point cloud (encoded via PointTransformer or PointNet++) and a 12-dim gripper Swept Volume vector — two axis-aligned cube parameterizations (3-dim dimensions + 3-dim center translation each) computed at fully-open and half-open states — as joint conditioning. It outputs ranked SE(3) grasp poses from a diffusion generator, scored by a discriminator that produces per-grasp confidence values. The Swept Volume is derived from the gripper's URDF and its joint closing trajectory, requiring no learned gripper encoder beyond a 3-layer MLP.

## Method

GraspGen-X extends the GraspGen diffusion framework by conditioning both the SE(3) diffusion generator and the grasp-ranking discriminator on a gripper embedding computed from the Swept Volume heuristic via a 3-layer MLP (12-dim → 512-dim). The key architectural insight is that a compact 12-dim geometric summary of the finger sweep space (capturing both open and half-open configurations to encode closing dynamics) suffices for cross-embodiment conditioning, outperforming heavier representations like TSDF, cVAE latents, and PointNet++ encodings. Training uses 25 procedurally generated grippers (via Infinigen-Sim geometry nodes) spanning parallel, revolute, and 3-finger categories, producing 395M simulated grasps through the ACRONYM antipodal-sampling and Isaac-Sim labeling pipeline — the largest multi-embodiment grasp dataset to date. The procedural gripper distribution is tuned to cover the Swept Volume space of real-world grippers, yielding better in-distribution overlap with test grippers than the 10 real training grippers alone.

## Results

On zero-shot simulation evaluation across 10 novel test grippers and 453 test objects, GraspGen-X achieves a mean AUC of 0.506, outperforming GraspGen-RTG (0.398) by 25% and GraspGen-DTR (0.126) by 300%. The largest gain is on high-DOF 3-finger grippers (0.699 vs. 0.503 for RTG, a ~40% relative improvement). On a real industrial manipulator with a Robotiq-2F140 gripper, GraspGen-X achieves 79.0% overall grasp success rate, surpassing GraspGen-RTG (65.2%) and AnyGrasp (61.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GraspGen-X decisively outperforms both GraspGen variants: direct transfer (DTR) fails catastrophically on non-parallel grippers (0.033 AUC on revolute 2F), while retargeting (RTG) improves to 0.398 overall but still lags GraspGen-X (0.506) by 25%, particularly on high-DOF 3-finger grippers where the gap widens to ~40%. Against gripper-encoding baselines evaluated on the same 10 novel grippers, GraspGen-X's Swept Volume representation (0.528 mAUC) beats AdaGrasp's TSDF encoding (0.432) by 25% and UniGrasp's cVAE latent (0.418) by 26%, with PointNet++ encoding trailing at 0.349. On the real-robot evaluation, GraspGen-X (79.0% success) surpasses AnyGrasp (61.4%) by 17.6 percentage points overall, with the gap widening to 28.5 points in the cluttered shelf setting where AnyGrasp's scene-centric training distribution breaks down. The paper also demonstrates that GraspGen-X serves as a superior initialization for supervised fine-tuning on a new gripper, converging faster and reaching lower translation/rotation error than both training from scratch and fine-tuning a single-embodiment Franka model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GraspGen [43], a diffusion-based 6-DOF grasp generator with a discriminator trained via an on-generator data flywheel, by conditioning both the generator and discriminator on a gripper representation. It departs from prior multi-embodiment grasping methods (UniGrasp, AdaGrasp, D(R,O) Grasp) that use complex learned encodings (cVAE, TSDF, PointNet++) and train on only a handful of real grippers, instead proposing a simple 12-dim Swept Volume heuristic and training on procedurally generated grippers. The procedural gripper generation strategy, built on Infinigen-Sim, is a notable departure from the standard practice of collecting limited real-world gripper datasets for training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-embodiment grasp generation is identified as the least transferable component in generalized pick-and-place systems, and this work provides the first large-scale (395M grasps) multi-embodiment dataset with demonstrated zero-shot transfer to unseen real grippers. The finding that a 12-dim heuristic outperforms complex learned representations suggests the field may be over-engineering gripper encodings. Key limitations include training on only 3.5K objects and 25 procedural grippers, exclusion of 5-finger dexterous hands, and the gap between simulation and real-world clutter performance (71.4% vs 85.7%), leaving open whether further scaling and more diverse gripper categories will close the remaining performance gap.
