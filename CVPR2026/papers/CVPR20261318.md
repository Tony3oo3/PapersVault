## Problem

Sparsely trained diffusion models (using token masking or routing) achieve significant training efficiency gains but suffer from unreliable and weak inference performance because they fail to respond properly to Classifier-free Guidance (CFG). Prior work required additional dense finetuning stages to partially restore CFG effectiveness, undermining the efficiency benefits. This paper asks whether the train-time sparsity mechanism itself can be repurposed as an inference-time guidance signal that eliminates the need for such finetuning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261318/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For class-conditional ImageNet-256, the model takes a noisy latent (from the Stable Diffusion VAE) plus a class label and produces a 256×256 image via 40-step Euler sampling. For the 2.5B text-to-image model, input is a noisy latent plus a text prompt encoded by InternVL3-2b, producing 512×512 images with 50 Euler steps in bfloat16. Unusually, at each inference step the network is evaluated twice at two distinct token-sparsity rates (γ_strong < γ_weak), with token subsets sampled from Bernoulli(1−γ) masks, so the effective input to the 'weak' branch is a sparser subset of the same conditional tokens rather than an unconditional signal.

## Method

Sparse Guidance (SG) replaces the unconditional branch of CFG with a second conditional prediction made at a higher token-sparsity rate, creating a capacity gap that steers generation. Concretely, D_θ^SG(c, ω) = ω·D_θ(x_t, t, c; γ_strong) + (1−ω)·D_θ(x_t, t, c; γ_weak), where γ_strong < γ_weak; the low-capacity weak branch pushes the high-capacity strong branch toward sharper, higher-fidelity outputs. Because sparsity directly controls effective model capacity, γ acts as a continuous knob over distributional sharpness, and increasing ω tolerates higher total sparsity, yielding a smooth quality-throughput trade-off. The method is finetune-free and post-hoc: it copies train-time sparsity settings to inference and requires only γ_strong, γ_weak, and ω as hyperparameters. SG is composable with other guidance signals (e.g., AutoGuidance, where the weak branch is an undertrained checkpoint) and works with both masking (irreversible token deletion) and routing (temporary layer bypass with reinsertion) training strategies.

## Results

On ImageNet-256, Sparse Guidance (SG_FID) achieves an FID of 1.58 at 400 epochs, outperforming the dense SiT-XL/2 baseline (FID 2.06 at 1400 epochs) while using 25% fewer GFLOPs (173.16 vs. 228.84). The efficiency-focused variant SG_FLOPS reaches FID 2.14 with only 97.67 GFLOPs, a 58% reduction over CFG's 228.84 GFLOPs while still surpassing the no-guidance baseline quality (FID 4.89). On the 2.5B text-to-image model, SG raises HPSv3 from 9.21 (CFG) to 9.87 and GenEval from 0.61 to 0.62, while increasing throughput from 0.32 to 0.49 images/s on an H200 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SG decisively outperforms all competing guidance methods on the same sparse backbone: it reduces FID from 2.51 (APG, the best competitor) to 1.58 (SG_FID), a 0.93-point gap, while also cutting GFLOPs by 24.6% relative to CFG's 228.84. SG_FLOPS is the only method that both improves quality over the no-guidance baseline (FID 4.89 → 2.14) and reduces compute (97.67 vs. 114.42 GFLOPS), a unique efficiency-quality trade-off none of the other baselines achieve. On the 2.5B T2I model, SG lifts HPSv3 from 9.21 (CFG) to 9.87, enabling the model to surpass CogView4 (9.61), PixArt-σ (9.37), and Gemini 2.0 Flash (9.21) in overall human preference, though it still trails Kolors (10.55), Flux-dev (10.43), Playground v2.5 (10.27), and Infinity (10.26). On GenEval, SG yields a modest but consistent improvement over CFG (0.62 vs. 0.61 overall), with gains in every sub-category (e.g., color attribution 0.39 vs. 0.36, position 0.20 vs. 0.19). The paper also shows that even after dense finetuning to restore CFG compatibility, CFG still underperforms SG, confirming that the train-test gap inherent to sparse training cannot be closed by finetuning alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active threads: training-time token sparsity for diffusion transformers (TREAD routing, MaskDiT masking) and inference-time guidance (CFG, AutoGuidance). Prior sparse-training methods demonstrated that training on token subsets improves efficiency and even conditional quality, but all reported a critical failure mode: sparsely trained models respond poorly to CFG, forcing practitioners into expensive dense finetuning to recover inference quality. SG's conceptual departure is to repurpose the sparsity mechanism itself as the guidance signal—using two different token-sparsity rates at inference to create a capacity gap that steers generation—rather than treating sparsity as a training-only augmentation to be discarded at test time. The guidance formulation (Eq. 7) is structurally identical to CFG (Eq. 3), but replaces the conditional/unconditional split with a strong/sparse-conditional split, making it a minimal yet principled modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SG removes the primary practical barrier to adopting sparse training methods: the need for a dense finetuning stage to make CFG work. By showing that even after extensive dense finetuning, CFG still underperforms SG (Figure 5), the paper argues that the train-test gap is structural, not a finetuning artifact. The 1.58 FID on ImageNet-256 with 25% fewer FLOPs (and 58% savings at matched quality) plus the 2.5B T2I validation suggest this is not a small-model curiosity. Open questions include whether the (γ_strong, γ_weak, ω) hyperparameter landscape remains well-behaved for video or audio generation, whether SG composes cleanly with architectural changes beyond DiT/SiT, and whether the capacity-gap framing generalizes to other forms of model degradation (e.g., quantization, pruning) beyond token-level sparsity.
