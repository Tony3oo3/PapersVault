## Problem

Training vision-language models for complex multi-step reasoning is bottlenecked by the scarcity of high-quality image-text reasoning data, while text-based reasoning resources are abundant and scalable. Existing text-only transfer methods (e.g., X-Reasoner) train VLMs on text data but do not leverage the teacher model for ongoing guidance during RL, missing a key opportunity for efficient cross-modal reasoning transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260120/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the student VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) receives an RGB image paired with a text prompt and generates a free-form reasoning trace followed by a final answer extracted via a structured 'boxed{...}' format. Crucially, during training the model is exposed only to text-only mathematical reasoning prompts (no images), with the vision encoder kept frozen throughout; the text-to-vision transfer is evaluated zero-shot on visual benchmarks. The teacher (Qwen3-8B) and student share the same tokenizer, a requirement for meaningful token-level KL computation.

## Method

VOLD is a two-stage post-training pipeline. Stage 1 performs SFT on reasoning traces generated by the text-only teacher (Qwen3-8B) on Mixture-of-Thoughts prompts, aligning the student's output distribution with the teacher's. Stage 2 applies a unified objective that reuses the same on-policy rollouts to compute both a GRPO loss (binary reward from exact-match verification on orz-57k math problems) and a reverse-KL distillation loss against the teacher at every token position. The key architectural choice replaces GRPO's reference-policy KL penalty with the teacher distillation term, and a reward-guided KL mask zeroes out the distillation loss for correct rollouts so the student is free to retain novel successful strategies. The paper shows empirically and theoretically that without Stage 1 alignment to the specific teacher used in Stage 2, on-policy distillation provides no benefit because the student's sampled prefixes fall outside the teacher's high-probability support, yielding uninformative gradients.

## Results

VOLD (Qwen2.5-VL-3B student + Qwen3-8B text-only teacher) achieves 32.0% on MMMU-Pro, 28.0% on MathVision, 37.9% on MathVerse, 50.7% on DynaMath, and 45.0% on LogicVista, outperforming the base model (21.9% MathVision, 40.3% LogicVista) by 6.1 and 4.7 points respectively. Crucially, VOLD trains exclusively on text-only data (MoT-Teacher-8B for SFT, orz-57k for RL) yet surpasses baselines that use image-text data during fine-tuning on most benchmarks. The largest gains appear on reasoning-heavy tasks: MathVision (+3.6 over X-Reasoner), DynaMath (+3.5 over X-Reasoner), and LogicVista (+3.9 over X-Reasoner).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against X-Reasoner (the only text-only-data baseline, using identical MoT SFT + orz-57k RL but without on-policy distillation), VOLD wins on every benchmark except MMStar (55.2 vs 55.2, tie) and MathVista (61.9 vs 61.1, +0.8), with the largest margins on MathVision (28.0 vs 24.4, +3.6), LogicVista (45.0 vs 41.1, +3.9), and DynaMath (50.7 vs 47.2, +3.5). Against VLAA-Thinker (image-text RL), VOLD wins on MMMU-Pro (32.0 vs 24.6), MathVision (28.0 vs 24.4), MathVerse (37.9 vs 36.4), DynaMath (50.7 vs 47.5), WeMath (31.8 vs 31.5), and LogicVista (45.0 vs 38.5), but loses on MMStar (55.2 vs 55.6). Against VLM-R1-Math (image-text RL, trained on MathVista), VOLD wins on MMMU-Pro (32.0 vs 28.6), MathVision (28.0 vs 21.9), MathVerse (37.9 vs 32.2), DynaMath (50.7 vs 42.7), WeMath (31.8 vs 30.0), and LogicVista (45.0 vs 40.5), but trails on MMStar (55.2 vs 56.7) and MathVista (61.9 vs 62.7, the latter likely due to VLM-R1's training-set overlap). Overall, VOLD achieves the best or near-best score on 7 of 8 benchmarks while using no visual reasoning data during training.

## Relation to Prior Work

VOLD extends the text-to-text unified RL + on-policy distillation framework of KDRL to the cross-modal setting, using a text-only LLM teacher to guide a VLM student. It builds directly on X-Reasoner's demonstration that text-only post-training can transfer reasoning to visual tasks, but departs by fully leveraging the teacher model through on-policy distillation rather than discarding it after SFT. The GRPO algorithm from DeepSeekMath provides the RL backbone, and the reverse-KL on-policy distillation mechanism follows Agarwal et al., with the key novelty being the SFT cold-start alignment stage and reward-guided KL masking as prerequisites for effective cross-modal transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental scalability bottleneck: high-quality visual reasoning data is scarce and expensive to curate, while text-based reasoning data is abundant and automatable. The finding that SFT alignment is a strict prerequisite for cross-modal on-policy distillation (without it, distillation provides zero benefit) is a practical and somewhat non-obvious requirement that future cross-modal distillation work must account for. Limitations include evaluation on a single 3B student model, the SFT phase temporarily degrading performance due to unfiltered teacher traces, and the approach being validated primarily on mathematical reasoning. Open questions include whether the framework scales to larger models, generalizes to non-mathematical visual reasoning, and how it interacts with more advanced RL algorithms beyond vanilla GRPO.
