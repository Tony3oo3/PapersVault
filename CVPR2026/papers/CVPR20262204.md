## Problem

Unsupervised domain adaptation for multimodal 3D panoptic segmentation (mm-3DPS) has never been studied, despite being critical for deploying panoptic perception across diverse driving environments. Existing mm-3DPS models assume both LiDAR and RGB modalities are consistently reliable, an assumption that breaks under real-world domain shifts (nighttime, rain, new locations), and conventional confidence-thresholding pseudo-labels produce fragmented instance masks that are especially detrimental to panoptic segmentation's requirement for complete thing instances and clear thing-stuff boundaries.

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired LiDAR point clouds (discretized into cylindrical voxels of size 480×360×32) and six RGB camera images (resized to 640×360) from both a labeled source domain and an unlabeled target domain. It produces per-point semantic class labels and per-instance masks covering 10 thing and 6 stuff categories (nuScenes) or 1 thing and 5 stuff categories (SemanticKITTI→nuScenes), evaluated via Panoptic Quality (PQ) decomposed into segmentation quality and recognition quality for thing and stuff separately.

## Method

PanDA builds on the IAL transformer-based mm-3DPS backbone within a mean-teacher self-training framework, introducing two key components. First, Asymmetric Multimodal Drop (AMD) simulates modality degradation in the source domain by randomly selecting one modality (p=0.5) and applying structured dropout to boundary regions (Canny edge patches for images, geometric discontinuity voxels for LiDAR) and thing-instance interiors, with auxiliary semantic heads providing masked-modeling supervision. Second, Dual-Expert Pseudo-Label Refinement (DualRefine) operates on target-domain teacher predictions in two stages: a Grow stage that recovers truncated stuff masks by merging them with best-matched 3D geometric superpoints (from HDBSCAN clustering of non-ground LiDAR points), and a Class Reassignment stage that corrects low-confidence thing instance labels using 2D VFM proposals (Grounding DINO + SAM) reprojected to 3D space. Training jointly optimizes source GT loss, target pseudo-label loss, a cross-layer query consistency loss between teacher and student, and the auxiliary semantic loss.

## Results

PanDA achieves PQ of 77.3 (USA→SG), 72.4 (Sunny→Rainy), 73.1 (Day→Night), and 54.5 (SemanticKITTI→nuScenes), improving over the source-only baseline by +13.2%, +8.9%, +8.4%, and +53.3% respectively. Notably, in the Day→Night setting, PanDA surpasses the Oracle-Target upper bound (53.5% PQ) by a large margin, demonstrating that the combined AMD and DualRefine modules effectively bridge the supervision gap under severe illumination shifts. Thing-class PQ (PQth) reaches 79.6 in Day→Night and 79.3 in USA→SG, showing particularly strong instance-level robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanDA outperforms both adapted semantic UDA baselines across all four domain shifts. Against Pano-xMUDA, PanDA gains +10.1 PQ (USA→SG), +10.2 PQ (Sunny→Rainy), +3.4 PQ (Day→Night), and +5.4 PQ (SemKITTI→nuScenes). Against Pano-UniDSeg, the margins are +4.4 PQ (USA→SG), +6.9 PQ (Sunny→Rainy), +2.6 PQ (Day→Night), and +0.5 PQ (SemKITTI→nuScenes). The largest relative gains appear in Sunny→Rainy and Day→Night, where modality degradation is most severe, confirming that PanDA's asymmetric augmentation and dual-expert pseudo-label refinement are particularly effective under challenging conditions. On thing-class PQth, PanDA leads Pano-UniDSeg by up to +7.0 (Day→Night: 79.6 vs 75.3) and Pano-xMUDA by up to +18.8 (Sunny→Rainy: 72.3 vs 55.8), underscoring the importance of instance-level structure modeling that semantic-only UDA methods lack.

## Relation to Prior Work

PanDA builds directly on IAL, a transformer-based multimodal 3D panoptic segmentation backbone, and extends the mean-teacher UDA paradigm established for 3D semantic segmentation (xMUDA, UniDSeg) to the panoptic setting, where instance-level structure must be preserved. It departs from prior UDA work in two key ways: (1) replacing content-agnostic random masking with panoptic-aware asymmetric multimodal drop that targets instance interiors and boundaries, and (2) introducing DualRefine, which jointly exploits 3D geometric superpoints (HDBSCAN) and 2D VFM proposals (SAM, Grounding DINO) to refine pseudo-labels—going beyond the confidence-thresholding or single-modality VFM voting used in prior semantic UDA methods. The framework thus bridges the gap between semantic-level domain alignment and the instance-completeness requirements unique to panoptic segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first UDA framework for multimodal 3D panoptic segmentation, PanDA addresses a practically critical gap: autonomous driving systems must generalize across weather, time-of-day, and geographic shifts without re-annotation. The consistent gains across four diverse domain shifts (up to +53.3% PQ on cross-dataset transfer) and the ablation evidence that both AMD and DualRefine are necessary (neither alone suffices) provide a clear recipe for robust panoptic perception. Limitations include reliance on external VFMs (SAM, Grounding DINO) that add inference overhead, evaluation limited to nuScenes and SemanticKITTI, and the Day→Night oracle comparison being confounded by the small target set. Open questions include whether the approach extends to online/test-time adaptation, whether the VFM priors remain effective under extreme sensor degradation, and how the method scales to higher-resolution or multi-sensor configurations.
