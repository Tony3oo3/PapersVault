## Problem

Existing adversarial defenses for diffusion models primarily target text-to-image or image-to-image generation, while image-to-video (I2V) generation poses a distinct threat: a single photo can be animated into a coherent video of a real person. Most prior methods optimize perturbations in RGB space and focus on UNet-based architectures, leaving their effectiveness against modern DiT/MMDiT-based video diffusion models—which enforce stronger temporal consistency and retain richer intermediate features—largely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB reference image and a text prompt, and produces a visually indistinguishable protected image x_ξ = x + δ constrained by an RGB perturbation budget of 16/255. During optimization, the method also requires a reference video generated from the image (captioned by an LVLM) to provide the latent video conditioning input z_ξ alongside the noised latent z_t. The perturbation δ is decomposed into three components: δ_a*, δ_b* (in L*a*b* space, each bounded by Δ_lab), and δ_freq (in the DCT domain, masked to the top 25% low-frequency coefficients).

## Method

Anti-I2V introduces a Dual-Space Perturbation (DSP) strategy that optimizes adversarial noise simultaneously in the L*a*b* color space (perturbing only the decorrelated a* and b* channels) and the DCT frequency domain (directly modifying top-left low-frequency coefficients), rather than in raw RGB pixel space. This yields perturbations that are less perceptible and more robust to JPEG compression, blurring, and purification attacks. The training objective combines two novel layer-wise losses: Internal Representation Collapse (IRC), which aligns features from the last three transformer blocks with those of the 3rd block to collapse high-level semantic representations, and Internal Representation Anchor (IRA), which minimizes layer-wise Euclidean distance between features conditioned on the perturbed image versus an unrelated target image across both the denoising network and VAE intermediate layers. These are combined with auxiliary CLIP and LPIPS losses and the negative diffusion loss, optimized via PGD over 200 iterations on a single A100 40GB GPU.

## Results

Anti-I2V achieves the lowest (best) scores across all five metrics (ISM, C-FIQA, Q-A(F), Q-A(V), DINO) on both CelebV-Text and UCF101 for all three video diffusion models. The most dramatic gains appear on the UNet-based DynamiCrafter, where Anti-I2V drops ISM to 0.151 (vs. 0.528 clean) and Q-A(V) to 0.047 (vs. 0.794 clean), far exceeding the next-best baselines (MIST: ISM 0.262, AdvDM: Q-A(V) 0.207). On the MMDiT-based CogVideoX-5B, Anti-I2V reaches ISM 0.448 and Q-A(V) 0.532 on CelebV-Text, and on the DiT-based OpenSora it reaches ISM 0.461 and Q-A(V) 0.554, consistently outperforming all baselines by 3–12% on identity metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Anti-I2V outperforms all four baseline families (SDS±, AdvDM, MIST, VGMShield) on every metric, every model, and both datasets. The margin is largest on the UNet-based DynamiCrafter: Anti-I2V achieves ISM 0.151 and Q-A(V) 0.047 on CelebV-Text, beating the next-best MIST (ISM 0.262) by 42% and AdvDM (Q-A(V) 0.207) by 77%. On the MMDiT-based CogVideoX-5B, Anti-I2V leads VGMShield (the strongest baseline) by 19% on ISM (0.448 vs. 0.554) and by 4% on Q-A(V) (0.532 vs. 0.557). On the DiT-based OpenSora, the gap narrows but remains consistent: Anti-I2V beats MIST by 6.5% on ISM (0.461 vs. 0.493) and by 7% on Q-A(V) (0.554 vs. 0.594). SDS± variants, which rely on score distillation sampling, are the weakest baselines on DiT/MMDiT models, suggesting that pixel-space perturbations guided by final-output losses are insufficient for large transformer-based video generators.

## Relation to Prior Work

Anti-I2V extends the adversarial cloaking line of work from image generation (AdvDM, MIST, DiffProtect) to image-to-video generation, where the core challenge shifts from disrupting a single frame to breaking temporal coherence across frames. It repurposes the L*a*b*-space perturbation strategy from Anti-forgery [58] and the DCT-based frequency analysis from InMark [33], but combines them into a unified dual-space optimization that prior work had not explored. The layer-wise IRC and IRA losses generalize the final-output VAE/textual losses of AdvDM and MIST to intermediate transformer blocks, motivated by PCA-based analysis of where semantic features emerge in DiT and UNet architectures. This positions the paper as the first to explicitly target MMDiT/DiT-based video models (CogVideoX, OpenSora) rather than the UNet-based SVD that most prior video defenses address.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As DiT-based video models become the dominant architecture for high-quality I2V generation, defenses validated only on UNet-based SVD may become obsolete; this paper provides the first systematic evaluation and effective countermeasure for that regime. The dual-space perturbation (L*a*b* + DCT) offers a practical robustness improvement over RGB-space noise, resisting JPEG compression and purification methods better, which matters for real-world deployment where images pass through lossy pipelines. Key limitations include the per-image optimization cost (200 PGD iterations on an A100), the absence of comparisons against Vid-Freeze, I2VGuard, and DORMANT due to hardware or availability constraints, and the observed performance degradation in cross-architecture transfer. Open questions include whether the layer-selection heuristic generalizes to future, even larger DiT models and whether a universal (image-agnostic) perturbation could achieve comparable protection without per-image optimization.
