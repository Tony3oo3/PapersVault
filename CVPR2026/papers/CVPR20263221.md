## Problem

Real-world photographs suffer from complex, coupled degradations (e.g., noise + motion blur + haze simultaneously), which existing approaches handle poorly: specialized models are siloed, all-in-one models lack generalization to unseen real-world artifact combinations, and sequential agentic systems accumulate errors across chained tool invocations. The paper identifies that treating coupled corruptions as a series of independent sub-problems is fundamentally inadequate and proposes replacing sequential pipelines with a single, collaborative execution step.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a degraded RGB image and an optional, often ambiguous text prompt (e.g., 'make it clearer'). The VLM orchestrator internally produces two structured outputs: a semantically refined diffusion prompt and a dictionary of expert LoRA weights (each in [0.0, 1.0]) corresponding to diagnosed degradation severities. The final output is a single restored RGB image generated in one forward pass of the dynamically assembled diffusion model, with no intermediate artifacts exposed to the user.

## Method

A VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) performs holistic intent understanding and multi-degradation diagnosis, outputting a refined prompt and per-expert weights. A lightweight MLP allocation branch, attached to the VLM's frozen vision features, is trained via Direct Preference Optimization on human pairwise preferences over candidate weight sets to ensure perceptually optimal weight assignment. The expert LoRAs are trained to adapt only Key and Value projection matrices (freezing Query matrices) to preserve the backbone's pretrained attention structure and maximize composability. At inference, the weighted LoRA updates are additively merged into the frozen Flux-Kontext diffusion backbone in a single step, and the assembled model executes restoration in one forward pass conditioned on the refined prompt and the degraded image, synergistically addressing all diagnosed issues simultaneously.

## Results

On the Real-1000 dataset (zero-shot), the method achieves PSNR 22.90 / SSIM 0.7718 / LPIPS 0.1711 on Group 1 (single degradations), 21.10 / 0.7175 / 0.2528 on Group 2 (coupled degradations), and 19.25 / 0.7310 / 0.2650 on Group 3 (three-way degradations), setting new state-of-the-art on all full-reference and no-reference metrics across all groups. On the synthetic MiO100 Group C (3-type degradations), it reaches PSNR 19.85, SSIM 0.5765, LPIPS 0.3392, and MUSIQ 58.78, outperforming the strongest agentic baseline 4KAgent (PSNR 19.77, MUSIQ 55.56). The method also leads on perceptual metrics such as CLIPIQA (0.4098 on Group 1) and MUSIQ (60.67 on Group 1), confirming superior human-perceived quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 12 baselines across every metric in all three Real-1000 groups. Against the strongest all-in-one model InstructIR, it gains +1.18 dB PSNR and −0.066 LPIPS on Group 1, and the gap widens to +3.01 dB PSNR on Group 3 where coupled degradations stress-test generalization. The sequential agentic baseline AgenticIR suffers severe degradation on multi-degradation groups (PSNR drops from 18.68 to 14.80 from Group 1 to 3), while the proposed method degrades far more gracefully (22.90 → 19.25), confirming that single-pass fusion avoids error accumulation. General-purpose foundation models (Flux-Kontext, Qwen-Image) trail by 4–6 dB PSNR on specialized tasks like low-light enhancement, validating the need for expert LoRA 'pens.' On the synthetic MiO100 Group C, the method edges out 4KAgent (the strongest agentic baseline) by +0.08 dB PSNR and +3.22 MUSIQ, while substantially outperforming all-in-one methods like AutoDIR (+1.24 dB PSNR) and AirNet (+1.90 dB PSNR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the sequential agentic restoration paradigm (AgenticIR, MAIR, 4KAgent) by replacing iterative tool invocation with a single-pass, weighted fusion of multiple LoRA experts into a shared diffusion backbone (Flux-Kontext). It builds on the LoRA modular adaptation framework (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) and the validated linear composability of LoRAs (Zhong et al., 2024), but introduces a K-V-only adaptation constraint specifically to enhance multi-expert composability. The DPO-based weight allocation branch adapts the preference-learning paradigm (Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) from language model alignment to continuous-to-discrete expert weight selection, a novel application in the restoration domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The single-pass fusion architecture directly addresses the error-accumulation failure mode of sequential agents and the generalization gap of monolithic all-in-one models, offering a practical path toward truly universal post-processing. The modular 'expert pen' design is inherently extensible—new degradation types can be added by training a single LoRA without retraining the backbone or other experts. Key limitations include reliance on a single backbone (Flux-Kontext), a small DPO preference dataset (500 images), and the absence of user studies; open questions include whether the K-V-only constraint generalizes to other architectures, whether region-level (rather than global) expert fusion is feasible, and how the system scales to degradation combinations far outside the training distribution.
