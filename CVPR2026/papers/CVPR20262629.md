## Problem

Existing image quality assessment methods target low-level distortions or generic aesthetics and lack the multi-dimensional functional criteria (text correctness, product integrity, layout composition) needed to judge whether a Chinese e-commerce poster is commercially usable. Dense Chinese typography introduces subtle stroke-level rendering errors that general-purpose MLLMs and IQA models systematically overlook, creating a bottleneck for automated quality control in e-commerce design workflows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262629/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluator receives a single e-commerce poster image (RGB) paired with a text evaluation instruction and produces a structured JSON containing four dimension scores (Object, Background, Text, Layout) and an overall score, each on a continuous [1, 5] scale anchored to three expert-defined quality tiers (poor [1.0, 3.0), good [3.0, 4.0), excellent [4.0, 5.0]), plus a free-form Chinese Chain-of-Thought rationale averaging over 800 characters that grounds each score in concrete visual evidence.

## Method

E-comIQ-M fine-tunes Qwen-2.5-VL-7B in two stages. Stage 1 (SFT) trains on 15k expert-annotated posters with multi-dimensional scores and human-edited CoT rationales to learn the task format and domain-specific scoring behavior. Stage 2 (GRPO) refines score calibration on a curated hard subset of 3k samples (selected by worst SFT MSE) using a composite reward that combines a per-dimension accuracy term (with a 0.7 penalty when predictions cross expert-defined quality tiers) and a distribution term (exponential penalty on the Euclidean distance between predicted and ground-truth 4D sub-score vectors), plus a binary JSON-format reward. The supporting dataset E-comIQ-18k is built via a human-AI collaborative pipeline where Qwen-2.5-VL-Max generates initial rationales from expert scores and tags, which original annotators then correct using a NER-based editing interface, achieving Krippendorff's Alpha of 0.858 across six domain experts.

## Results

On the E-comIQ-18k test set, E-comIQ-M achieves an Overall PLCC of 0.425 and SRCC of 0.433, surpassing the strongest fine-tuned baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-7B+SFT at 0.346/0.346) and all general-purpose MLLMs (best: GPT-4o at 0.242/0.219). On the Text dimension—identified as the critical bottleneck for Chinese e-commerce posters—E-comIQ-M reaches PLCC 0.364 / SRCC 0.392, nearly tripling the best general MLLM (GPT-4o: 0.126/0.148). In terms of accuracy, E-comIQ-M attains 55.6% Acc@0.5 and 81.8% Acc@1.0 on the Overall score, compared to 51.0%/78.8% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B+SFT and 32.4%/59.0% for GPT-4o.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E-comIQ-M outperforms all 12 baselines across every dimension on both PLCC and SRCC. Against traditional NR-IQA models (MUSIQ, SPAQ), the improvement is dramatic: Overall PLCC jumps from 0.074 (MUSIQ) or −0.174 (SPAQ) to 0.425, confirming that low-level distortion metrics are essentially uninformative for e-commerce poster quality. Among general-purpose MLLMs, GPT-4o is the strongest (Overall PLCC 0.242), yet E-comIQ-M still exceeds it by 0.183; the gap widens substantially on the Text dimension (0.364 vs. 0.126), where Chinese character rendering errors are the dominant failure mode. Among specialized IQA evaluators, VQ-R1 leads with Overall PLCC 0.227, but E-comIQ-M surpasses it by 0.198, and on Text the margin is even larger (0.364 vs. 0.112). The closest competitor is the SFT-only variant of the same backbone (Qwen2.5-VL-7B+SFT at 0.346), which E-comIQ-M improves upon by 0.079 in Overall PLCC and by 0.092 on Text, demonstrating that the GRPO stage with the composite accuracy-plus-distribution reward provides meaningful calibration gains beyond supervised fine-tuning alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of MLLM-based image quality assessment and e-commerce visual design. It repurposes the Qwen-2.5-VL backbone and GRPO optimization paradigm from general-purpose IQA work (Q-Align, Q-Insight, VQ-R1) but departs from them in two key ways: (1) the training data is domain-specific, multi-dimensional, and includes expert-verified Chain-of-Thought rationales rather than holistic scores or pairwise preferences, and (2) the GRPO reward function introduces a novel distribution component that penalizes geometric deviation of the 4D sub-score vector, going beyond the accuracy-only rewards used in prior IQA-RL work. Unlike e-commerce-specific efforts like AIGuard (binary labels) or PosterCraft/PosterMaker (generation-focused), this is the first to provide a structured, explanation-rich evaluation framework for Chinese e-commerce posters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in commercial AIGC pipelines: the absence of scalable, human-aligned automated quality assessment for e-commerce visuals. The finding that OCR-based text metrics are unreliable for Chinese character rendering (models with low human text scores still achieve high phrase-level F1) has direct implications for anyone building evaluation pipelines for CJK text in generated images. Key limitations remain: the evaluator's PLCC/SRCC with human scores is still moderate (~0.34) on out-of-domain data, and as a non-reference model it cannot measure subject identity fidelity. Open questions include whether stronger calibration strategies or reference-based components can close this gap, and whether the multi-dimensional CoT framework generalizes to other languages or design domains.
