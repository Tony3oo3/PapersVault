## Problem

Existing 3D human reconstruction methods are limited to isolated individuals and fail in multi-human scenes, where naive per-person composition produces unrealistic overlaps, missing geometry in occluded regions, and distorted interactions. No prior work jointly addresses perspective distortion, interaction-aware geometric modeling, and occlusion-aware texture completion for multiple interacting people from a single image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261254/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single perspective RGB image of multiple interacting people and produces complete textured SMPL-X meshes with full surface geometry and appearance, including hallucinated content in occluded regions. Internally, the perspective input is converted to six canonical orthographic views (at 0°, 45°, 90°, 180°, 270°, 315° azimuth) via point-cloud reprojection, and the diffusion model generates complete multi-view RGB and normal maps from these partial inputs. All SMPL-X poses are provided as ground truth during evaluation to isolate reconstruction quality from pose estimation.

## Method

HUG3D is a three-stage pipeline. First, Pers2Ortho estimates an initial SMPL-X mesh (via RoBUDDI), refines it with Sapiens depth/normal supervision, and reprojects the input image through a dense point cloud into six canonical orthographic views to eliminate perspective distortion. Second, HUG-MVD—a diffusion model initialized from PSHuman (Stable Diffusion 2.1) with a frozen normal-map ControlNet—jointly denoises multi-view RGB and normal latents, trained on both single-human data (THuman2.0, CustomHumans) for diversity and multi-human data (Hi4D) for interaction priors, with an instance-to-group latent composition mechanism that injects per-person latents into the group representation at spatially corresponding regions. Third, HUG-GR optimizes the mesh via differentiable rendering against group- and instance-level normal targets, an interpenetration loss that penalizes surface overlap below a tolerance threshold using a smooth log-sum-exp formulation, and a visibility loss enforcing silhouette consistency, followed by occlusion-aware multi-view texture fusion.

## Results

On the MultiHuman dataset (80 images from 20 two-person sequences), HUG3D achieves the best scores across all geometric metrics: CD of 3.631 cm, P2S of 1.752 cm, NC of 0.811, F-score of 41.504, bbox-IoU of 0.847, NormL2 of 0.019, and a contact precision (CP) of 0.240. For texture quality, HUG3D reaches PSNR 16.456, SSIM 0.809, and LPIPS 0.168, outperforming all baselines. In occluded regions specifically, HUG3D reduces NormL2 to 0.140 and achieves PSNR 8.388, substantially ahead of the next-best method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HUG3D outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest single-human baseline SIFU, HUG3D reduces CD by 36% (3.631 vs. 5.644 cm) and more than doubles the contact precision score (0.240 vs. 0.089), indicating far more physically plausible inter-person contact. SiTH, while competitive on CP (0.135), lags significantly in CD (9.251 vs. 3.631) and P2S (3.185 vs. 1.752), reflecting its inability to handle multi-person geometry. PSHuman, the weakest baseline, shows the largest gaps (CD 15.579, CP 0.027), confirming that naively applying a single-human diffusion model to multi-person scenes produces severe artifacts. DeepMultiCap, the only multi-human baseline (designed for multi-view input), achieves a reasonable P2S (2.555) but suffers from poor bbox-IoU (0.513) and low CP (0.083) when forced into a single-image setting, highlighting the difficulty of its original multi-view assumption under monocular conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

HUG3D extends the single-human textured reconstruction line (PSHuman, SiTH, SIFU) to multi-human scenes by introducing a group-instance diffusion model and physics-based mesh optimization. Its architectural backbone is directly initialized from PSHuman's cross-scale diffusion, while its initial geometry and camera estimation pipeline builds on BUDDI's generative proxemics prior. The key conceptual departure is the explicit separation of group-level and instance-level supervision—both in the diffusion training data (Hi4D for interactions, THuman2.0/CustomHumans for diversity) and in the mesh optimization losses—rather than treating all humans as a single composite geometry. The perspective-to-orthographic transform (Pers2Ortho) is a practical bridge that makes multi-view diffusion tractable under real-world camera distortion, a problem prior perspective-aware methods (Era3D, MEAT) addressed only for single objects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to produce fully textured, physically plausible 3D reconstructions of multiple interacting people from a single RGB image, filling a gap between single-human textured reconstruction and multi-human pose/geometry estimation. The practical impact for AR/VR, digital humans, and social behavior understanding is significant, as prior approaches either required multi-view/video inputs or produced untextured SMPL-X fits. Key limitations remain: the method does not handle occlusions from external objects, can fail under severe depth ambiguity inherent to single-view settings, and evaluation is limited to 20 two-person sequences on one dataset. Open questions include whether the group-instance formulation scales to larger groups (3+ people), whether the physics-based interpenetration loss generalizes to non-contact interactions, and whether the canonical orthographic assumption holds for extreme camera angles.
