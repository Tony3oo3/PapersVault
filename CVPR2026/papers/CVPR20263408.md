## Problem

Existing part-aware 3D generation methods treat all parts at a single granularity level, ignoring the inherent hierarchical organization among parts, and encode multi-level semantics in Euclidean space where the distribution resembles graph structures with low manifold utilization. Prior hyperbolic 3D methods (e.g., HyperSDFusion) use hyperbolic geometry only as a coarse-to-fine structural prior rather than for explicit part-aware semantic hierarchy, and inject isotropic Gaussian noise in the tangent space that disrupts the anisotropic radial-angular geometry encoding hierarchical topology. The paper addresses all three gaps simultaneously: hierarchical part-aware semantics, structure-preserving diffusion, and efficient sampling in hyperbolic space.

## Input / Output

The method accepts text prompts, single reference images, class labels, or a subject condition paired with an edit prompt, and produces 36,864 3D Gaussian Splatting primitives (64×64×9 grid) along with their hierarchical part-aware semantic embeddings in the Poincaré ball. Hierarchical hyperbolic conditional features are extracted from inputs via the pretrained MERU encoder, and generation proceeds layer-by-layer through the H²MM hierarchy before producing the final 3DGS. All evaluation renderings are at 512×512 resolution, and the HDM-Solver reduces sampling to 50 steps from 1,000 training timesteps.

## Method

The framework has three components. First, the Hierarchical Hyperbolic Mixture Model (H²MM) is a hyperbolic encoder-decoder that adaptively constructs part-level semantics: a shared hyperbolic MLP with permutation-invariant aggregation produces a root latent, and a top-down splitting decoder uses hyperbolic cross-attention followed by a hyperbolic MLP to generate child latents at each level, with parameters decoded via Log₀ and a parameter decoder; training maximizes a regularized negative log-likelihood with inter-layer centroid separation and root-to-leaf geodesic distance penalties. Second, the Hyperbolic Semantically Consistent Diffusion process decouples noise into radial and angular components in the tangent space, applying a curvature-dependent scaling factor Λ_c(x₀) = tanh(√|c|‖x₀‖)/(√|c|‖x₀‖) to angular noise to preserve the anisotropic hierarchy; a tree-topology network adaptively scans data dependencies to avoid jointly generating nodes and edges, and generation proceeds progressively—root semantics under conditions, then layer-by-layer semantics, then 3DGS primitives conditioned on the full H²MM. Third, the HDM-Solver projects the reverse diffusion ODE into the tangent space and replaces Euclidean updates with Möbius scalar multiplication and subtraction, yielding first- and second-order (and third-order) Riemannian integrators with convergence-order guarantees that preserve manifold geometry throughout sampling.

## Results

On unconditional ShapeNet Car generation, the method achieves FID-50K of 9.89 and KID-50K of 6.24%, outperforming the closest competitor HGMMSplatting (11.03 / 7.16%) and GaussianCube (13.01 / 8.46%). On class-conditioned OmniObject3D, it reaches FID-50K 9.12 and KID-50K 1.93%, surpassing HGMMSplatting (10.57 / 2.02%) and DiffSplat (15.39 / 4.62%). For text-to-3D, it attains a CLIP score of 31.02 with 3.92 s inference, beating DiffSplat (28.32 / 8.64 s) and LGM (27.31 / 4.13 s). On image-to-3D, it achieves PSNR 27.63 and LPIPS 0.1102, outperforming GaussianCube (25.83 / 0.1531) and GRM (24.78 / 0.1597).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated task. Against the closest Euclidean part-aware competitor HGMMSplatting [48], it improves FID by 1.14 on ShapeNet Car (9.89 vs 11.03), 1.71 on Chair (11.03 vs 12.74), and 1.45 on OmniObject3D (9.12 vs 10.57), attributed to hyperbolic encoding of hierarchical semantics. GaussianCube [50] and DiffSplat [28] trail by larger margins (FID gaps of 2–6 points), reflecting their lack of explicit part-level semantic hierarchy. The tri-plane-based DiffTF [4] and implicit-representation GET3D [18] are substantially worse (FID > 17 on ShapeNet), confirming the advantage of 3DGS with hierarchical guidance. For text-to-3D, the method beats LGM [41] by 3.71 CLIP points and DiffSplat [28] by 2.70 points while also being faster (3.92 s vs 8.64 s). On image-to-3D, it surpasses GRM [47] by 2.85 dB PSNR and 0.0495 LPIPS, and on scene generation it leads DiffusionGS [3] by 1.90 FID and 0.81 KID, with GVGEN [19] remaining the weakest text-to-3D baseline at 25.67 CLIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in 3D generation: part-aware hierarchical modeling (HGMMSplatting, which encodes multi-level semantics in Euclidean space) and hyperbolic geometric priors (HyperSDFusion, which uses hyperbolic space only for coarse-to-fine resolution refinement without explicit part semantics). The key departure is embedding hierarchical part-aware Gaussian Mixture Model semantics directly in the Poincaré ball, combined with a geodesic diffusion process that decouples radial (hierarchical depth) and angular (intra-level semantic) noise to respect the anisotropic geometry. The HDM-Solver further extends the DPM-Solver lineage by deriving first- and second-order Riemannian integrators on hyperbolic manifolds, replacing Euclidean linear interpolation with Möbius operations in the tangent space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled framework for diffusion on hyperbolic manifolds that preserves hierarchical structure during both forward noising and reverse denoising, addressing a gap where prior hyperbolic diffusion methods applied isotropic tangent-space noise and disrupted the radial-angular geometry. The HDM-Solver is a reusable component for any hyperbolic diffusion model, not tied to 3D generation specifically. Limitations include fixed curvature (the authors note learnable curvature as future work) and evaluation primarily on ShapeNet-scale datasets; whether the hyperbolic advantage persists at the scale of Objaverse-level diversity with more complex part hierarchies remains open. The approach also depends on 3DGS as its geometric representation, so benefits may not transfer directly to SDF or mesh-based pipelines.
