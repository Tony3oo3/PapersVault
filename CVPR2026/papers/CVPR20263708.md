## Problem

Existing embodied agents fail to internalize shared physical and causal mechanisms across diverse interactive environments: VLMs reason semantically but lack forward prediction of physical consequences, world models predict but imitate surface visual patterns rather than analyzing underlying dynamics, and RL overfits to task-specific reward signals. The paper asks whether an agent can acquire human-like physical reasoning purely through diverse interactive experience and transfer it to unseen games with radically different visuals and controls.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263708/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives a single RGB game frame (from 1,000+ heterogeneous titles at 60 FPS) and a text goal prompt; the frame is encoded via DINOv3 into a feature vector that conditions both the VLM policy and the world model. The VLM (Qwen3-VL-8B with extended PhysCode tokens) emits a short sequence of discrete latent action codes, which a per-environment router maps to native keyboard/mouse or other control signals. Optical flow and T5 semantic embeddings are used only during PhysCode pretraining as privileged cues and are discarded at inference, where the encoder relies solely on appearance and semantics.

## Method

IPR is a three-stage pipeline. Stage 1 learns PhysCode, a VQ-VAE discrete action vocabulary conditioned on DINOv3 appearance, optical flow, and T5 semantics, so that codes cluster under matched physics and separate under different dynamics; flow is dropped at test time via modality dropout. Stage 2 trains a latent world model that predicts future DINOv3 features and a Q-value critic head conditioned on PhysCode sequences, operating in compressed feature space to filter appearance noise. Stage 3 performs prediction-reinforced reasoning: the VLM samples B candidate PhysCode sequences, the world model runs short-horizon imagined rollouts to assign predicted returns, and GRPO updates the VLM policy using the resulting advantages with a KL penalty against the initial policy. At inference, the VLM proposes candidates, the world model scores and prunes them, and the highest-scoring action is executed, closing a prediction-in-the-loop reasoning cycle.

## Results

On the G2U benchmark (200 games), IPR achieves an Overall Avg. Rank of 4.9/28, the best among all 30 methods, surpassing GPT-5 (best rank 13.3) and the strongest RL baseline DQN@curiosity (10.6). IPR's per-level means are 0.091 (Survival), 0.325 (Curiosity), and 0.493 (Utility), with a Ratio@Top-3 of 22.0% on Utility—nearly double the next-best method. Zero-shot transfer to 50 held-out games improves steadily as training games increase from small to large N, confirming scaling with interactive experience.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPR (Overall Avg. Rank 4.9) outperforms all 30 baselines across the three-level evaluation. Against RL methods, IPR beats the best DQN variant (rank 10.6) and all PPO variants (ranks 12.0–14.8), primarily because RL overfits to task-specific reward signals and collapses on Curiosity (PPO@curiosity: rank 17.3). Against world models, IPR surpasses DreamerV3 (10.7), V-JEPA2 (20.8), and GenieRedux (16.1); while WMs achieve higher raw Curiosity means (e.g., V-JEPA2: 1.402), they fail on Utility (means 0.10–0.30 vs. IPR's 0.493) because they imitate visual patterns rather than pursue goals. Against MLLMs, IPR beats GPT-5 (best rank 13.3) and GPT-4o (16.4–19.4) overall, despite GPT-5 achieving a higher Survival mean (0.140 vs. 0.091); IPR's advantage comes from its grounded prediction enabling strong Utility (0.493 vs. 0.371) and balanced performance across all three levels. Against imitation learning, IPR outperforms both ACT-BC (16.6) and Qwen3-VL-8B-BC (13.3), which excel at low-risk Survival but lack exploration and goal-directed reasoning. The ablation (Table 3) further confirms that the world-model prediction component is essential: removing it (VLM+GRPO alone) drops Curiosity from 2.77 to 1.78, while adding BC degrades all metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

IPR synthesizes three previously separate paradigms—latent world models (Dreamer, V-JEPA2, Genie), VLM-based semantic reasoning (Qwen3-VL), and RL-based policy optimization (GRPO, PPO)—into a single interactive loop where world-model rollouts score VLM-proposed actions in a shared latent space. The discrete action vocabulary (PhysCode) draws directly on VQ-VAE [50] but is conditioned on optical flow, DINOv3 appearance, and T5 semantics to encode physics-relevant dynamics rather than domain-specific controls. The key departure from prior work is the explicit use of a physics-centric action code as the common interface between prediction (world model) and reasoning (VLM), rather than planning in pixel space or acting in raw key/language spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The G2U benchmark of 1,000+ heterogeneous games with a three-level (Survival/Curiosity/Utility) evaluation provides a much-needed large-scale testbed for cross-domain physical reasoning that goes beyond single-environment RL or static VLM benchmarks. The zero-shot transfer results and scaling curves suggest that interactive physical experience can accumulate into reusable causal priors, a step toward agents that generalize to genuinely novel environments. Key limitations include restriction to game environments, short-horizon imagination, and an 8B backbone; open questions include whether the paradigm extends to real-world robotics, longer-horizon planning, and whether the physics-centric action space remains effective as the number of training domains grows further.
