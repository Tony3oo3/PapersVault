## Problem

Reconstructing 3D objects from daily monocular hand-object interaction videos is hindered by severe hand-induced occlusion and the coupled motion of camera, hands, and object. Existing methods either rely on fragile multi-stage pipelines (SfM + inpainting + novel-view synthesis) that accumulate view-inconsistent errors, or require hours of per-scene optimization, making them impractical for real-world use. No prior work achieves fast, end-to-end object reconstruction under such severe occlusion without pre-processing.

## Input / Output

The method takes a short monocular RGB video clip (2–6 frames at 518×518) as the sole input; no separate hand or object masks are required at inference since hand geometry is recovered internally via a WiLoR hand-pose estimator whose ViT patch features are fused patch-to-patch with DINOv2 image features. The output is a complete 3D object mesh (decoded from a 64×64×64 voxel latent via TRELLIS-style SLat flow), per-frame inpainted 2D object masks, and per-frame 6-DoF object poses estimated through a render-and-compare refinement loop using Mast3R correspondences.

## Method

ForeHOI is a feed-forward diffusion transformer (DiT) that jointly denoises a 3D voxel latent and per-frame 2D object masks via conditional flow matching, with a bidirectional cross-attention mechanism coupling the two branches: mask-branch features (shallow-to-deep) condition the geometry branch at each DiT block, while geometry-branch features are fed back into the mask branch as contextual signals, enabling mutual refinement under occlusion. The architecture fine-tunes the TRELLIS two-stage pipeline with LoRA (rank 64), replacing VGGT features with DINOv2 features for robustness under occlusion, and uses a masked multi-view SLat flow in the second stage to refine the coarse voxel surface into a high-fidelity mesh. Training is supervised end-to-end on a newly constructed 400K-sample synthetic dataset rendered in Blender from GraspXL grasps on Objaverse objects with textured MANO hands, providing ground-truth masks, poses, and depth. Inference completes in approximately 1 minute, roughly 100× faster than optimization-based baselines.

## Results

On HO3D, ForeHOI achieves the best geometry reconstruction with a Chamfer Distance of 0.79 cm, F@5 of 68.95%, and F@10 of 93.72%, outperforming MagicHOI (0.86 cm, 64.53%, 91.87%) and HOLD (1.36 cm, 66.42%, 82.43%). On the more challenging HOT3D dataset (egocentric, severe occlusion), ForeHOI achieves 1.03 cm CD, 60.5% F@5, and 89.1% F@10, dramatically surpassing EasyHOI (1.21 cm, 18.46%, 34.25%) and HORT (2.32 cm, 16.52%, 20.43%). Inference takes only 1.1 minutes, roughly 100× faster than optimization-based baselines (EasyHOI: 175 min, HOLD: 330 min, MagicHOI: 58 min). For object pose estimation, ForeHOI achieves 1.42 cm RPE and 0.13 m ATE, substantially better than HOLD (5.87 cm, 0.27 m) and Dynhor (4.25 cm, 0.24 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ForeHOI outperforms all five baselines on every metric where they overlap. On HO3D geometry, it beats the closest competitor MagicHOI by 0.07 cm in CD and 4.42 points in F@5, while also being ~50× faster (1.1 min vs 58 min). On HOT3D, the advantage is dramatic: ForeHOI's F@5 of 60.5% exceeds EasyHOI (18.46%) by over 42 points and HORT (16.52%) by over 44 points, reflecting the difficulty of egocentric clips with rapid hand occlusions. HOLD and MagicHOI both require SfM-based pose initialization and thus cannot be evaluated on HOT3D where COLMAP frequently fails on sparse views. For pose estimation (Table 1), ForeHOI achieves 1.42 cm RPE and 0.13 m ATE, roughly 4× better than HOLD (5.87 cm, 0.27 m) and 3× better than Dynhor (4.25 cm, 0.24 m), benefiting from its render-and-compare strategy with Mast3R matching rather than relying on SfM or text-prompted template generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ForeHOI extends the ReconViaGen [5] diffusion-based multi-view reconstruction framework by replacing its single-branch image-to-voxel cross-attention with a dual-branch architecture that jointly predicts 2D object mask inpainting and 3D shape completion via bidirectional cross-attention. The base two-stage diffusion pipeline (voxel generation + structured-latent surface refinement) is inherited from TRELLIS [69], fine-tuned with LoRA. The paper departs from prior optimization-based HOI reconstruction methods (MagicHOI, EasyHOI) that chain 2D inpainting, novel-view synthesis, and radiance-field optimization into multi-stage pipelines, instead learning the entire reconstruction in a single feed-forward network. It also introduces the first large-scale synthetic HOI video dataset (400K clips) built from GraspXL grasps on Objaverse objects with textured MANO hands, addressing a data bottleneck that prior methods worked around with foundation-model priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~100× speedup (1.1 min vs. 58–330 min) makes 3D object digitization from daily HOI videos practically deployable for embodied-intelligence pipelines, and the 400K-sample synthetic dataset provides a much-needed training resource for the field. The ablation showing that standard multi-view data with random masks fails badly (CD 1.23 cm) while purpose-built HOI data succeeds (CD 0.91 cm) underscores that hand-object interaction context is not interchangeable with generic occlusion. Open questions include how well the synthetic-to-real gap holds at scale beyond the 14 HO3D sequences and 6 HOT3D clips used for evaluation, and whether the feed-forward paradigm can extend to articulated or deformable objects.
