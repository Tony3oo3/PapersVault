## Problem

Existing world models rely on monocular video (where depth is implicit and scale-ambiguous) or RGB-D channels (where predicted depth is scene-dependent and unstable), and no end-to-end system can generate view-consistent stereo videos conditioned on explicit camera trajectories. Post-hoc stereo conversion pipelines (depth estimation + warping + inpainting) are non-end-to-end, computationally expensive, and accumulate errors in fine-detail regions. This paper introduces the first camera-conditioned stereo world model that jointly learns appearance and binocular geometry directly from RGB stereo pairs.

## Input / Output

The model takes a rectified stereo image pair (left and right, each 480×640×3), a per-frame camera trajectory of N timesteps specified by 3×3 intrinsics and 4×4 extrinsics, and a text/scene prompt, and produces a 49-frame stereo video (480×640) where both views are temporally smooth, follow the prescribed camera motion, and maintain left-right consistency at every timestep. Notably, the model operates exclusively in the RGB modality without any explicit depth channel, and disparity maps (and hence metric-scale depth) are derived post-hoc from the generated stereo pair rather than predicted directly.

## Method

StereoWorld fine-tunes the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video diffusion backbone (3D VAE + DiT with rectified flow) using two key architectural modifications. First, a unified camera-frame RoPE expands the self-attention token dimension from d to d+d_c, appending a camera-conditioned rotation block built from the per-frame projection matrix P_j = [K_j 0; 0 1] T_j while leaving the original d×d RoPE block untouched, thereby encoding relative camera relationships without disrupting pretrained positional priors (with a 'Copy Init' strategy that seeds the new subspace from temporal attention weights for stable convergence). Second, a stereo-aware attention mechanism decomposes the prohibitively expensive full 4D attention over 2f×h×w concatenated left-right tokens into 3D intra-view attention (f×h×w per view) plus horizontal row attention (f×h×(2w)²) that only attends among horizontally aligned tokens at the same timestep, exploiting the epipolar constraint of rectified stereo pairs to halve FLOPs while preserving disparity-aligned correspondence. Training uses 20k AdamW steps (lr=1e-4, batch 24) on a mixed dataset of real and synthetic stereo sequences with varying baselines and intrinsics, with no depth supervision—geometry is learned purely from binocular image signals.

## Results

On the stereo video generation benchmark (435 stereo images from FoundationStereo, Unreal-Stereo4K, TartanAir, and Middlebury), StereoWorld achieves FID 111.36 and FVD 83.04, substantially outperforming the best RGBD baseline Aether (FID 185.72, FVD 152.97) and the best RGB baseline SEVA (FID 195.70, FVD 170.92). For camera accuracy, StereoWorld attains the lowest rotation error (1.01°) and translation error (0.11), and for view synchronization it achieves the best FVD-V (22.00) and CLIP-V (97.50). In terms of efficiency, StereoWorld runs at 0.49 FPS, roughly 3–5× faster than warp-inpainting baselines (e.g., ViewCrafter at 0.13 FPS, Aether at 0.11 FPS), while also delivering a ~5% gain in viewpoint consistency over the strongest monocular-then-convert pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

StereoWorld outperforms all five external baselines across every reported metric. Against the RGBD world models (Voyager, DeepVerse, Aether), it reduces FID by 40–51% and FVD by 47–53%, while also achieving the best camera accuracy (RotErr 1.01° vs. 1.34–1.51°) and view synchronization (FVD-V 22.00 vs. 33.50–55.45). Against the RGB baselines (SEVA, ViewCrafter), it improves FID by 43–48% and FVD by 52–55%, and is 3.8–4.9× faster in generation speed (0.49 vs. 0.10–0.13 FPS). The internal monocular ablation (Ours Monocular) confirms the benefit of joint stereo generation: the stereo variant improves FID from 126.83 to 111.36 and FVD from 96.87 to 83.04, while also enabling view-synchronization metrics that the monocular version cannot produce. In the camera-injection ablation (Table 4), the proposed unified camera-frame RoPE with Copy Init beats both Plücker Ray (FID 142.46, RotErr 1.52) and PRoPE (FID 144.45, RotErr 1.33), achieving FID 122.41 and RotErr 1.16. The stereo-aware attention (Table 5) matches or slightly exceeds full 4D attention in view consistency (CLIP-V 96.63 vs. 97.50, Mat.Pix 4.52 vs. 4.51) while halving FLOPs (1.56×10¹⁰ vs. 3.11×10¹⁰) and boosting FPS from 0.34 to 0.49.

## Relation to Prior Work

StereoWorld builds on the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video diffusion backbone and extends the RoPE positional encoding framework (from RoFormer and PRoPE) by expanding the token dimension to inject camera conditioning orthogonally, rather than reparameterizing the existing rotation matrices. It departs from the dominant monocular-then-convert paradigm (StereoCrafter, SVG, StereoDiffusion) and RGB-D world models (Voyager, DeepVerse, Aether) by operating purely in RGB stereo and learning geometry directly from binocular disparity. The stereo-aware attention decomposition, which replaces full 4D cross-view attention with 3D intra-view plus horizontal row attention, is a novel architectural choice motivated by the epipolar constraint in rectified stereo pairs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end camera-conditioned stereo world model, eliminating the multi-stage depth-estimation-and-inpainting pipelines that introduce error accumulation and limit fine-detail consistency in VR/AR applications. The practical impact is significant: 3× faster generation with better viewpoint consistency than monocular-then-convert baselines, direct metric-scale depth recovery without depth supervision, and demonstrated applicability to embodied policy learning and long-video distillation. Key open questions include scalability given the scarcity of large-scale stereo training data, whether the epipolar attention decomposition generalizes to non-rectified or multi-camera (beyond binocular) settings, and how the approach performs under extreme baseline variations or dynamic scenes with occlusion.
