## Problem

Pure-vision autoregressive models lack a standardized multi-step output pathway analogous to Chain-of-Thought in language models, limiting their ability to handle complex visual tasks through explicit intermediate reasoning. Existing visual prompting methods like Chain-of-Focus produce only a single output rather than an explicit step-by-step chain, and are highly sensitive to prompt engineering and domain shift. This paper addresses the underexplored problem of how to equip task-free vision models with an explicit, task-agnostic chain-of-vision generation process.

## Input / Output

The model receives a sequence of in-context image-label demonstration pairs concatenated with a query image, all tokenized by a VQGAN encoder into 16×16 grids of 256 discrete tokens per 256×256 image. Unlike standard in-context learning that produces only a final prediction, the autoregressive model generates intermediate chain-of-vision images (e.g., 2 intermediate frames at α=0.33 and α=0.67) before the final task-specific output, all as a single continuous token sequence without special delimiters or task markers.

## Method

The framework has two key components: (1) a unified sequential prediction protocol that reformulates the in-context learning objective to jointly generate intermediate representations R_Q and the final prediction A autoregressively, trained with a standard cross-entropy loss; and (2) a diffusion-guided interpolation pipeline that constructs visually coherent intermediate images by first inverting source and target images to noise space via DDIM, computing intermediate latents through spherical linear interpolation (slerp) in the noise space, and then denoising back to image space using the pretrained Stable Diffusion v2.1 model. These diffusion-generated intermediates serve as task-free supervision signals that reduce the model's perplexity for final target generation. The approach is applied via LoRA fine-tuning (rank 32, alpha 64) on LVM-300M, LVM-1B, and LVM-7B base models, requiring no classifier-free guidance or text conditioning during interpolation.

## Results

On the LVM-7B model, Diffusion Guided Chain-of-Vision achieves an image segmentation IoU of 0.500 (vs. 0.160 for the base LVM and 0.196 for CoF), a pose estimation LPIPS of 0.221, and a low-light enhancement PSNR of 20.45. The method attains the best score on all 14 metrics for the 7B model, 12/14 for the 1B model, and 12/14 for the 300M model. Performance scales positively with model size: segmentation IoU rises from 0.441 (300M) to 0.471 (1B) to 0.500 (7B) under diffusion guided interpolation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base LVM models (no fine-tuning), the proposed method yields dramatic gains: on LVM-7B, segmentation IoU jumps from 0.160 to 0.500 and low-light PSNR from 10.68 to 20.45, confirming that multi-step CoV supervision is essential for unlocking in-context visual learning. Compared to Chain-of-Focus (CoF), which uses salient-region prompting without explicit intermediate steps, the proposed method achieves substantially higher segmentation IoU (0.500 vs. 0.196 on 7B) and lower pose LPIPS (0.221 vs. 0.260), demonstrating that an explicit multi-step generation pathway outperforms single-step focus-based prompting. Against the fine-tuning-without-interpolation baseline, diffusion guided interpolation consistently improves all metrics (e.g., 7B segmentation IoU 0.500 vs. 0.463; depth S.Rel 0.061 vs. 0.070), validating the benefit of intermediate supervisory signals. Finally, diffusion guided interpolation outperforms pixel-space linear interpolation on 12/14 metrics for 300M, 12/14 for 1B, and all 14 for 7B, with the largest relative gains in structure-sensitive tasks such as edge detection (P-ACC 0.531 vs. 0.525) and pose estimation (LPIPS 0.221 vs. 0.235) on the 7B model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Large Autoregressive Vision Models (LVMs) by introducing an explicit multi-step 'Chain-of-Vision' generation pathway, drawing a direct analogy to Chain-of-Thought in LLMs. It builds on the slerp-based latent interpolation technique from DiffMorpher [69] and the DDIM inversion framework, repurposing a pretrained diffusion model not as a generator but as a source of task-free intermediate supervision signals. The approach departs from Chain-of-Focus [80], which uses multi-step prompting but produces only a single final output, by requiring the autoregressive model to explicitly generate intermediate representations before the target label, all under a unified next-token prediction objective without task-specific markers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a general, task-agnostic mechanism to inject multi-step reasoning into pure-vision autoregressive models, potentially reducing the sensitivity to prompt engineering that plagues current visual ICL approaches. The finding that explicit multi-step supervision yields the largest relative gains at smaller model scales (300M) suggests a practical path for deploying capable visual reasoning on resource-constrained hardware. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D tasks, whether the diffusion model can be replaced by a lighter surrogate, and how the method interacts with longer context windows beyond the 8-image setting tested here.
