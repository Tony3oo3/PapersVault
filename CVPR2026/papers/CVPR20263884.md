## Problem

Existing Gaussian-based head avatar methods bind primitives rigidly to mesh triangles via Linear Blend Skinning, preventing fine-grained non-rigid deformations such as wrinkles and subtle expression changes. Additionally, the vanilla Adaptive Density Control (ADC) in 3DGS is designed for static scenes: it relies on average positional gradients that under-densify transiently visible regions (e.g., mouth interiors) and ignores textural discrepancies, leading to blurred or missing details in frequently occluded areas.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a monocular head video (cropped and resized to 512×512) along with a fixed reference image (defaulting to the first frame) whose texture and UV position map are rasterized into a 256×256 UV grid. FLAME parameters (expression, pose, translation) are estimated per-frame via VHAP and serve as control codes. The output is an animatable 3D Gaussian avatar parameterized in UV space that supports real-time novel-view rendering and cross-identity reenactment, with the Gaussian count dynamically adjusted during training via the proposed Temporal ADC.

## Method

STAvatar introduces a UV-Adaptive Soft Binding framework where a dual-branch network (a global branch and a local branch with region-specific masks for eyes, mouth, nose, and forehead) predicts a 13-dimensional per-Gaussian offset map in UV space, capturing spatial context for deformation while remaining compatible with dynamic Gaussian densification through UV-Adaptive Sampling. For density control, the Temporal ADC strategy combines FLAME-Conditioned Temporal Clustering (PCA + K-means on FLAME parameters, K∈[5,12]) to compute densification criteria among structurally coherent frames, and a Fused Perceptual Error with Average-Peak Criterion (FPE-AP) that replaces positional gradients with a combined L1 + D-SSIM error, selecting Gaussians by both average and peak error to trigger cloning. Training runs for 6 epochs with an L1 + D-SSIM + VGG perceptual loss (VGG activated only in the second half) plus offset regularization, achieving convergence faster than all compared baselines.

## Results

STAvatar achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four benchmark datasets (INSTA, PointAvatar, NerFace, HDTF), attaining the highest SSIM and lowest LPIPS on every dataset. On INSTA, it reaches 30.63 PSNR / 0.9587 SSIM / 0.0304 LPIPS, surpassing the second-best method (RGBA) by over 2 dB in PSNR. On NerFace, the margin is even larger: 30.08 PSNR vs. 27.13 for RGBA. On PointAvatar, STAvatar slightly trails Fate in PSNR (28.25 vs. 28.36) but leads in SSIM (0.9337 vs. 0.9287) and LPIPS (0.0495 vs. 0.0776), indicating superior perceptual fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAvatar outperforms all six baselines on SSIM and LPIPS across all four datasets, with the largest margins on INSTA (+0.0094 SSIM, −0.0214 LPIPS over RGBA) and NerFace (+0.0129 SSIM, −0.0239 LPIPS over RGBA). In PSNR, STAvatar leads on INSTA (30.63 vs. 28.41 for RGBA, +2.22 dB), NerFace (30.08 vs. 27.13, +2.95 dB), and HDTF (27.99 vs. 27.18 for Fate, +0.81 dB). The only PSNR loss is on PointAvatar, where Fate edges ahead by 0.11 dB (28.36 vs. 28.25), though STAvatar still wins on both SSIM and LPIPS there. Among the older baselines, GA and SA show the weakest performance, with GA trailing STAvatar by 3.65 dB on INSTA and 4.34 dB on NerFace, highlighting the substantial gains from the proposed soft binding and temporal ADC strategies over rigid LBS-based binding and vanilla density control.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting-based head avatar reconstruction line initiated by GaussianAvatars (GA) and SplattingAvatar, which bind Gaussians to mesh triangles via Linear Blend Skinning (LBS). It departs from the hard-binding paradigm by introducing a UV-space feature offset map (UV-Adaptive Soft Binding) that provides the flexibility of MLP-based soft binding (as in MonoGaussianAvatar, FlashAvatar) while preserving compatibility with Adaptive Density Control—a property those MLP methods sacrifice by fixing Gaussian counts. On the ADC side, it builds on static-scene densification improvements (Revising Densification, Pixel-GS, SteepGS) but re-frames the problem for dynamic avatars via temporal clustering (FTC) and a fused perceptual error criterion (FPE-AP) that jointly captures geometric and textural discrepancies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method achieves consistent SOTA across four benchmark datasets with particularly large LPIPS gains (e.g., 0.0304 vs. 0.0508 on INSTA), and converges in 6 epochs versus 10–100 for baselines, making it practically attractive for consumer-level avatar pipelines. The UV-space formulation is a reusable design pattern that resolves the long-standing tension between per-Gaussian flexibility and dynamic density control in 3DGS. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond heads to full-body avatars, whether the dual-branch U-Net scales to higher resolutions or more complex identities, and whether the temporal clustering strategy remains effective for very long or highly variable sequences.
