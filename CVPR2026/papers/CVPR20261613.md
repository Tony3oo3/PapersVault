## Problem

Existing approaches to constructing task-specific LoRA modules either merge pre-trained LoRAs via deterministic weighting (limiting parameter diversity) or train generative models under a closed-world assumption where training and test tasks share similar distributions. Neither handles the realistic scenario where a user queries an entirely unseen task with no raw data available, particularly under significant domain shifts between seen and unseen tasks. The paper formalizes this as Zero-Shot Open-world Adaptation (ZSOA) and asks whether one can rapidly generate high-performing LoRA parameters for arbitrary unseen tasks using only a textual description and a repository of expert LoRAs.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method requires only a short textual task description (e.g., 'a photo of a Yorkshire Terrier') and a pre-built expert LoRA repository; no images or labeled pairs for the target task are needed. The description is encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 text encoder into a semantic vector, which drives expert selection and conditioning. The output is a complete set of rank-2 LoRA matrices (B, A pairs) for the W_q, W_k, and W_v projections in every Transformer block of the visual encoder, directly pluggable into the frozen CLIP backbone for image-text retrieval.

## Method

SG-LoRA first constructs task semantics via a sparse aggregator: it computes cosine similarities between the unseen task's CLIP text embedding and all expert embeddings, selects the top-K most relevant experts, and applies softmax-weighted fusion to produce a Gaussian mean and variance (via the Law of Total Variance) representing the target task's parameter distribution. A conditional VAE is then trained with a semantic-aware prior p(z|c) parameterized by stacked MLPs (replacing the standard N(0,I) prior), where the encoder maps the LoRA tensor and task semantics to a latent code and the decoder reconstructs the LoRA parameters. Training minimizes the ELBO with a reconstruction term and a KL divergence against the task-specific prior. At inference, a latent sample is drawn from p(z|c) and decoded into LoRA weights, converting deterministic LoRA construction into probabilistic parameter sampling that introduces diversity and adaptability to evolving user intents.

## Results

On MS-COCO, SG-LoRA achieves 74.31% I2T R@1 and 54.42% T2I R@1, surpassing the Oracle fine-tuned LoRA (72.45% and 53.10% respectively) and all merging-based baselines. On OxfordPets, SG-LoRA reaches 57.15% I2T R@1 and 37.62% T2I R@1, again exceeding Oracle (55.84% and 40.99%) on I2T while falling slightly short on T2I. In cross-dataset transfer (MS-COCO→OxfordPets), SG-LoRA attains 55.41% I2T R@1, nearly matching the Oracle (55.84%) and outperforming the best merging baseline (Top-K LoRA Weighted) by 7.28 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Model Soups [45], which uniformly averages all expert LoRAs, SG-LoRA gains +4.94 points on MS-COCO I2T R@1 (74.31 vs. 69.37) and +14.11 points on OxfordPets T2I R@1 (37.62 vs. 33.51), demonstrating that indiscriminate merging is far less effective than semantic-guided generation. Against AdapterSoup [3] (top-k equal-weight merging), SG-LoRA improves by +3.61 points on MS-COCO I2T R@1 and +3.57 points on OxfordPets I2T R@1, confirming that equal weighting of selected experts underperforms compared to the learned generative approach. SG-LoRA also outperforms the stronger Top-K LoRA Weighted baseline (softmax-weighted merging) by +2.76 points on MS-COCO I2T R@1, showing that deterministic fusion—even with learned weights—cannot match the stochastic parameter sampling of the CVAE. Notably, SG-LoRA exceeds the Oracle (task-specific fine-tuned LoRA) on MS-COCO I2T R@1 (+1.86) and OxfordPets I2T R@1 (+1.31), suggesting that the generative process avoids overfitting on small task-specific datasets. The only case where SG-LoRA trails Oracle is OxfordPets T2I R@1 (37.62 vs. 40.99), a gap of 3.37 points, likely due to the limited fine-grained cat/dog distinction in that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

SG-LoRA sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: LoRA-based parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) and conditional variational autoencoders (Sohn et al., NeurIPS 2015). It departs from merging-based approaches (Model Soups, AdapterSoup, LoRAHub) by replacing deterministic weight fusion with stochastic parameter generation, and from prior weight-generation methods (diffusion-based, KDE-based) by introducing a semantic-aware prior conditioned on CLIP text embeddings rather than operating under a closed-world assumption. The sparse aggregator that selects and weights top-K expert LoRAs via cosine similarity in a shared embedding space is the key architectural novelty that bridges seen and unseen tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ZSOA formulation addresses a practically important gap: deploying personalized LoRA models on edge devices where raw task data is unavailable and the task space is unconstrained. The privacy-preserving, tuning-free generation pipeline is well-suited for real-time, user-intent-driven adaptation. However, experiments are confined to CLIP ViT-B/16 with rank-2 LoRA on relatively small retrieval datasets (MS-COCO, OxfordPets, Flowers102), leaving open whether the approach scales to larger backbones, higher-rank adapters, or more complex generative tasks. The reliance on a curated expert repository also raises questions about cold-start scenarios where few or no relevant experts exist.
