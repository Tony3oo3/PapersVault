## Problem

Open-set fine-grained retrieval requires generalizing to unseen subcategories, but existing metric-based and localization-based methods embed category-specific semantics from closed-set training labels, limiting their transferability. While diffusion transformers encode attribute-centric, label-agnostic knowledge through their hierarchical denoising process, vanilla DiTs are not optimized for fine-grained visual discrepancies and their massive parameter count (up to 12B) makes direct deployment infeasible for retrieval.

## Input / Output

At training time, the method consumes RGB images (resized to 256×256, center-cropped to 224×224) along with attribute-centric text descriptions generated by Qwen2.5-VL and object-centric crops extracted via GroundingDINO for the CDR stage. At inference, only the lightweight ViT-B/16 backbone is used (DiT-free), producing a single compact retrieval embedding per image for cosine-similarity-based top-K retrieval, with inference time of 2.6 ms per image.

## Method

DiT-Distill is a two-stage framework. Stage I (Conditional Discrepancy Refinement) fine-tunes FLUX DiT via LoRA to generate an object-centric latent from a noisy version while conditioned on the full context image and an attribute-centric text prompt, forcing the model to implicitly subtract background features and sharpen its internal representations toward fine-grained, discrepancy-aware details. Stage II (Generative Curriculum Distillation) transfers this refined knowledge into a ViT-B/16 student through a Generative Infusion Module (learnable query embeddings interrogate frozen teacher features via cross-attention, then self-attention fuses them with the student's class-token embedding) and a Curriculum Alignment Loss that progressively aligns the student's raw embedding to the enhanced embedding across four diffusion timesteps (10, 16, 22, 28), from coarse to fine. A proxy-based Discrepancy Representation Constraint loss simultaneously optimizes retrieval performance, while the alignment loss forces the student to internalize the teacher's hierarchical reasoning so the DiT can be discarded at test time.

## Results

DiT-Distill achieves state-of-the-art Recall@1 across all four open-set fine-grained retrieval benchmarks: 87.2% on CUB-200-2011, 91.4% on Stanford Cars, 89.4% on Stanford Dogs, and 83.7% on NABirds. Compared to the same ViT-B/16 backbone used as the student, the framework yields a +9.8% R@1 gain on CUB and a +18.6% gain on Stanford Cars, demonstrating the value of the generative curriculum distillation. Inference remains efficient at 2.6 ms per image (DiT-free), matching the baseline speed while substantially outperforming it in accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DiT-Distill outperforms all 11 compared baselines on every dataset where results are reported. On CUB-200-2011, it surpasses the previous best DVA (84.9% R@1) by 2.3 points and Hyp-ViT (84.0%) by 3.2 points. On Stanford Cars, the margin over DVA (90.7%) is 0.7 points and over FRPT (91.1%) is 0.3 points, while the gap over Hyp-ViT (86.0%) reaches 5.4 points. On Stanford Dogs, DiT-Distill leads Hyp-ViT (87.8%) by 1.6 points and PNCA++ (86.0%) by 3.4 points. The largest absolute gains appear on NABirds, where DiT-Distill (83.7% R@1) exceeds Hyp-ViT (79.2%) by 4.5 points and PNCA++ (63.4%) by 20.3 points, underscoring the advantage of label-agnostic generative curriculum knowledge over category-specific discriminative training on highly diverse, open-set benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant closed-set paradigm in open-set fine-grained retrieval (OSFR), where prior metric-based and localization-based methods (e.g., Hyp-ViT, DVA, FRPT) embed category-specific semantics that limit generalization to unseen subcategories. It also diverges from prior work that repurposes diffusion models as feature extractors (DIFT, VPD) by instead treating the DiT's hierarchical denoising dynamics as a structured knowledge source whose coarse-to-fine progression constitutes a 'generative curriculum.' The CDR strategy (fine-tuning DiT via LoRA to separate object from context) and the GCD mechanism (curriculum alignment across selected timesteps into a ViT student) are novel architectural and loss-level contributions that bridge generative and discriminative modeling for retrieval.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a 12B-parameter diffusion transformer's attribute-centric, label-agnostic knowledge can be compressed into a ViT-B/16 with 2.6 ms inference while substantially outperforming specialized SOTA methods, offering a practical path to deploy generative-model reasoning in resource-constrained retrieval pipelines. The robustness finding (detector-based CDR data matching oracle bounding boxes within 0.2%) is practically important, removing the need for manual annotations. Open questions include whether the timestep-curriculum distillation generalizes beyond fine-grained retrieval to other discriminative tasks, whether the approach scales to larger or more diverse DiT backbones, and how sensitive performance is to the specific curriculum schedule (only 4 timesteps were explored).
