## Problem

Existing video masked autoencoders (VideoMAE, V-JEPA) rely on symmetric spatio-temporal attention that treats time uniformly, neglecting the causal and directional nature of temporal dynamics, and are limited to short offline clips. Meanwhile, strong image encoders (DINOv2) excel at dense spatial features but cannot capture motion. No single architecture achieves competitive performance on both spatio-temporal and spatial tasks while remaining parameter-efficient, especially in the small-model regime without knowledge distillation.

## Input / Output

The model ingests a 64-frame video clip (256×256 RGB) from which 4 consecutive source frames are processed sequentially through the recurrent encoder, while 4 target frames sampled 4–48 frames ahead are heavily masked (95%) and used as reconstruction targets during training. At inference, the encoder is unrolled frame-by-frame over arbitrary-length sequences, producing a per-frame feature map (N+1 tokens of dimension D) at each time step with linear compute and memory cost. The recurrent state carries information forward, enabling stable feature propagation over long horizons far exceeding the 4-frame training window.

## Method

RVM couples a per-frame ViT encoder with a GRU-based recurrent core that aggregates temporal information: at each step, the current frame's encoded tokens serve as queries in a cross-attention block whose keys and values come from the gated previous state, with reset and update gates (à la GRU) controlling how much prior information is retained versus new information integrated. Training uses an asymmetric masking objective inspired by SiamMAE—unmasked source frames condition a cross-attention decoder to reconstruct a heavily masked future target frame via a simple pixel-level L2 loss, with no distillation, EMA, or auxiliary regularizers. The key architectural insight is replacing full spatio-temporal self-attention with this recurrent transformer-GRU hybrid, which reduces long-sequence inference from quadratic to linear complexity while preserving the ability to model directional, causal temporal dynamics. Ablations confirm that the RNN aggregator outperforms a matched self-attention baseline, that more source frames (1→4) consistently improve performance, and that the model continues to benefit from additional training data without overfitting.

## Results

RVM-L (375M params) achieves an average normalized score of 94.4 across 8 diverse benchmarks, outperforming DINOv2-g (85.0, 1135M) and VideoMAEv2-g (77.9, 1013M) by 9.4 and 16.5 points respectively. On spatio-temporal tasks, RVM-H reaches 68.7% SSv2 accuracy and 60.0% Kinetics-700 accuracy, competitive with V-JEPA2-H (68.9%/59.1%) while far exceeding DINOv2-g on point tracking (78.3 vs. 39.9 AJ). On spatial tasks, RVM-L achieves 66.0% DAVIS J&F and 38.0% VIP mIoU, substantially outperforming video models (e.g., VideoMAE-H: 54.4%/20.8%). In the small-model regime, RVM-S (34M) attains 96.1 average normalized score without distillation, surpassing both VideoMAEv2-g and DINOv2-g which are ~30× larger.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DINOv2 (image model), RVM wins decisively on all spatio-temporal tasks: RVM-L achieves 77.3 vs. 36.6 on Perception Test AJ and 73.2 vs. 51.7 on Waymo mIoU, while DINOv2 retains a narrow edge on JHMDB keypoint tracking (51.4 vs. 48.4) and VIP mIoU (40.6 vs. 38.0). Against VideoMAE (video model), RVM dominates on spatial correspondence: RVM-L scores 66.0% DAVIS J&F and 38.0% VIP mIoU versus VideoMAE-L's 54.3% and 18.9%, while remaining competitive on action recognition (66.7 vs. 62.7 SSv2). Against V-JEPA2, RVM-H matches or exceeds on SSv2 (68.7 vs. 68.9) and Kinetics (60.0 vs. 59.1) while far outperforming on spatial tasks (65.6 vs. 43.3 DAVIS, 37.3 vs. 16.0 VIP). In the small-model regime, RVM-S (34M) outperforms VideoMAE-B (87M, 4× larger) on 6 of 8 benchmarks and beats SiamMAE-S on SSv2 by +19.7 points (59.7 vs. 40.0) and Kinetics by +8.4 points, all without knowledge distillation. The 4DS model achieves the highest individual scores on Waymo (75.9) and Perception Test (81.5) but is not evaluated on spatial tasks, so a full comparison is not possible.

## Relation to Prior Work

RVM extends the asymmetric masking paradigm of SiamMAE (conditioning on unmasked source frames to reconstruct a heavily masked future target) by replacing the single-frame source with a sequence of frames aggregated through a transformer-based GRU recurrent core. It departs from the dominant spatio-temporal attention architectures of VideoMAE and V-JEPA, which treat time as a uniform, bidirectional axis, in favor of causal, sequential processing that carries state forward. The design also draws on earlier recurrent video architectures (RViT, RCNN) but introduces a hybrid GRU-Transformer module where the reset and update gates modulate cross-attention between the current frame's tokens and the accumulated state, rather than using simple gating over hidden states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RVM demonstrates that a simple pixel-reconstruction objective combined with recurrent temporal aggregation can produce a genuinely generalist video encoder that avoids the spatial-vs-temporal tradeoff plaguing current models, while achieving up to 30× greater parameter efficiency in the small-model regime without knowledge distillation. The linear-compute, linear-memory scaling for long sequences makes RVM particularly attractive for streaming and robotics applications where chunked offline attention is impractical. Key open questions include the data saturation point (performance still improving at 2B clips), whether the recurrent design can be further scaled, and how it performs in multi-modal or world-modeling settings such as robotic control. A practical limitation is that BPTT through a ViT at every step makes training memory-intensive, and the architecture is computationally heavier than spatio-temporal models for very short clips.
