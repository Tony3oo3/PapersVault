## Problem

Humanoid robots lack the ability to physically interact with humans because (1) standard motion retargeting from human-human demonstrations breaks essential physical contacts due to morphological mismatches, and (2) conventional imitation learning policies merely replay trajectories without understanding interactive timing or spatial targeting. The paper addresses both the data-generation bottleneck and the policy-design limitation simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260960/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a 12-frame observation history at 50 Hz, each frame concatenating robot proprioception (base linear/angular velocities, gravity projection, 29 joint states) with human SMPL joint positions estimated from a single 1280×720 @ 30 fps RGB camera via 4D-Humans, plus a short text command token selecting the interaction type. It outputs a 5-anchor horizon spanning 2 seconds of 36-D reference actions (29 joint targets + 3D root translation + 4D quaternion orientation) at 5 Hz, which is bilinearly densified to 50 Hz and tracked by a whole-body controller; on hardware, root pose is mapped to planar base velocity commands (v_x, v_y, yaw rate).

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. First, PAIR (Physics-Aware Interaction Retargeting) converts HHI sequences into physically consistent HHoI data via a coarse-to-fine two-stage optimization: Stage 1 finds a globally kinematically plausible motion with moderate contact weight, then Stage 2 aggressively refines contact alignment with an increased N×N pairwise-distance-matrix loss (L_con) while constraining the human partner's adaptation through a selective upper-body fidelity term. Second, D-STAR (Decoupled Spatio-Temporal Action Reasoner) is a hierarchical policy that explicitly separates temporal reasoning (Phase Attention over a 3-phase taxonomy: Preparation, Act, Follow-up) from spatial reasoning (Multi-Scale Spatial module encoding near/mid/far geometric neighborhoods at 0–0.3 m, 0.3–0.8 m, 0.8–3.0 m), both fed through a Long-Short Temporal Encoder with parallel Transformers. The two decoupled feature streams are fused by a diffusion-based planning head that generates the high-level action target, ensuring that timing decisions are not corrupted by spatial noise and vice versa, enabling synchronized whole-body interaction beyond simple trajectory mimicry.

## Results

On six whole-body interaction tasks (Hug, High-Five, Handshake, Wave, Bend, Fly-Kiss) in Isaac Gym with a Unitree G1, D-STAR achieves a 75.4% average success rate, outperforming the strongest architectural baseline (Transformer Policy, 64.3%) by 11.1 points and Diffusion Policy (58.7%) by 16.7 points. On the retargeting side, PAIR achieves a contact-preservation F1 of 0.841 at the 0.35 m threshold (micro-averaged), a 67.5% relative gain over ImitationNet (0.502) and 22.2% over the best non-interactive baseline (Simple MSE, 0.688), while also attaining the best kinematic similarity (JPE 0.174 m) and a 69% jerk reduction versus Simple MSE. Robustness to partner scale and speed (0.8×–1.2×) degrades gracefully, retaining 62.7% success at the most extreme grid corner.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ImitationNet [40] (the SOTA unsupervised retargeting baseline), PAIR wins decisively on contact preservation: F1 rises from 0.502 to 0.841 at the 0.35 m threshold (a 67.5% relative gain), while also improving kinematic similarity (JPE 0.174 m vs. 0.181 m) and reducing jerk by roughly 60%. ImitationNet's competitive JPE masks its failure to maintain physical contact, which is the critical property for downstream interaction. Against Diffusion Policy [9] (the strongest policy baseline using the same retargeted data and controller), D-STAR achieves 75.4% vs. 58.7% average success (16.7-point margin), with the largest gap on Handshake (61.3% vs. 38.7%) and High-Five (40.7% vs. 3.7%), indicating that decoupled spatio-temporal reasoning captures timing and targeting cues that a monolithic diffusion head misses. The Transformer Policy baseline (64.3%) is the closest competitor, but D-STAR still leads by 11.1 points, confirming that the gain comes from the PA/MSS decoupling rather than backbone capacity. Naive Mimicry (behavioral cloning without a low-level controller) scores 0% across all tasks, underscoring that a whole-body controller is indispensable for physical stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of motion retargeting and interactive policy learning, identifying two distinct failure modes that prior work addresses separately: retargeting methods (ImitationNet, IK-based trackers) optimize kinematic similarity but break physical contact under morphological mismatch, while imitation learning policies (Diffusion Policy, Transformer policies) replicate trajectories without interactive understanding. Rather than building on a single prior method, the paper repurposes diffusion models for hierarchical action planning and introduces a contact-preserving N×N distance-matrix loss for retargeting, drawing conceptually from graphics retargeting (Reconform, geometry-aware interaction retargeting) but adapting it to robotics-grade constraints. The decoupled spatio-temporal architecture (Phase Attention + Multi-Scale Spatial) departs from monolithic policy designs by explicitly separating temporal phase reasoning from spatial targeting, a design choice motivated by the observation that unified policies conflate 'when' and 'where' in interactive settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a practical, end-to-end pipeline (HHI data → PAIR retargeting → D-STAR policy → real-robot execution) that addresses the data scarcity bottleneck for human-humanoid interaction, a recognized frontier for humanoid deployment. The real-robot validation on Unitree G1 with standard whole-body controllers demonstrates practical executability beyond simulation. However, limitations remain: evaluation covers only six interaction types on a single robot platform, the robustness grid shows meaningful degradation at extreme scale/speed variations (down to 62.7%), and the sim-to-real gap is bridged by a task-agnostic fine-tuning pass whose generalizability to unseen interactions is untested. Open questions include scaling to richer interaction taxonomies, multi-robot or multi-human settings, and whether the decoupled architecture generalizes to contact-rich manipulation beyond social gestures.
