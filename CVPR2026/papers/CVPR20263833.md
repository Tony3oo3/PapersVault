## Problem

Existing conversational agents are confined to text and audio, while motion generation systems (co-speech gesture, text-to-motion) frame behavior as a fixed modality-translation task without agentic decision-making about when to move, what to do, or how to adapt across multi-turn dialogue. No prior system jointly plans language and movement, follows explicit body-action directives mid-conversation, or maintains cross-turn behavioral consistency within a unified 3D virtual body.

## Input / Output

The model consumes interleaved multimodal token streams aligned to a 25 fps master clock: speech tokens at 12.5 fps (from a Whisper-based VQ tokenizer), text sub-word tokens with word-level timestamps, FLAME face tokens at 25 fps, and SMPL-X body tokens at 6.25 fps (with ×4 temporal downsampling). It produces streaming speech audio, FLAME facial parameters (shape, expression, pose), SMPL-X body/hand joint poses, and text responses. A key design feature is mixed-initiative interaction: users can speak, type, or issue body-action directives (e.g., 'step back and wave') at any point, and the system exposes controllable behavior hooks for streaming output.

## Method

ViBES uses a Mixture-of-Modality-Experts (MoME) backbone with three modality-partitioned Transformer experts—text-speech (TS), facial, and body—routed deterministically by modality (hard routing, no learned router). Cross-modal information flows exclusively through SLB-Attn: face and body queries attend only to TS keys/values (one-directional cross-attention), while the TS expert performs self-attention over its own stream; ablations confirm that enabling face↔body attention yields no measurable gain. The TS expert is initialized from GLM-4-Voice and kept frozen, so the pretrained speech-language backbone remains intact and new modalities are added as lightweight per-layer side-car modules with minimal overhead. A multi-modal fractional RoPE scheme assigns each token a scalar (possibly non-integer) position index via linear interpolation between surrounding TS anchors, preserving precise cross-modal temporal alignment on a single rotary timeline. Training is supervised next-token prediction on the curated Converse3D corpus (~1,000 hours of time-synchronized audio-text-motion triplets from YouTube conversations, existing motion datasets, and synthesized tri-modal supervision).

## Results

On the novel Converse3D conversational behavior benchmark (4,921 samples), ViBES achieves R1-Balanced of 0.467, substantially outperforming the best co-speech gesture baseline (LoM, 0.323) and the best text-to-motion baseline (MoMask, 0.293), while the ground-truth upper bound is 0.712. On talking-head synthesis (TFHP), ViBES attains the best LVE (10.87), MOD (2.29), and FDD (6.05) among all baselines. On co-speech gesture generation (BEATv2), ViBES achieves the lowest FGD (5.257) and highest BC (8.103), though with slightly lower diversity (13.028) than LoM (15.167).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the primary conversational behavior benchmark, ViBES (R1-Balanced 0.467) outperforms all co-speech gesture baselines (LoM 0.323, SynTalker 0.288, EMAGE 0.258) and all text-to-motion baselines (MoMask 0.293, MotionGPT 0.244, T2M 0.223) by a wide margin, with FID dropping from 262–406 to 93.9. On talking-head synthesis (TFHP), ViBES beats the strongest audio-to-face baselines (MultiTalk: LVE 11.66, FDD 15.68; ARTalk: LVE 11.67, FDD 20.77) on all three metrics (LVE 10.87, MOD 2.29, FDD 6.05). On co-speech gesture generation (BEATv2), ViBES achieves the best FGD (5.257 vs. LoM 5.301) and BC (8.103 vs. LoM 7.780) but trails LoM on diversity (13.028 vs. 15.167). For speech quality, ViBES (Context Relevance 4.584, Character Consistency 4.376) approaches the ground-truth upper bound (4.838, 4.893) and clearly surpasses SOLAMI (0.824, 3.634) and LLM+Speech (3.859, 3.157). Overall, ViBES wins or ties on every headline metric across all four evaluation tasks, with the sole exception of diversity in co-speech gesture generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViBES departs from the dominant framing of co-speech gesture and text-to-motion as modality-translation tasks (audio/text → motion via diffusion or autoregressive decoders) by embedding motion generation inside a pretrained speech-language LLM backbone (GLM-4-Voice) as a side-car expert. The Mixture-of-Modality-Experts architecture with hard modality routing and cross-expert attention draws directly on MOT and MMDiT, but is the first to apply this pattern to a speech-language-behavior triad with a frozen language core. The facial tokenizer is inherited from LoM, and the closest prior system, SOLAMI, aligns speech with body motion but lacks an LLM reasoning backbone and explicit text conditioning, which ViBES addresses by keeping the full GLM-4-Voice language stack intact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1,000-hour Converse3D corpus and the balanced R-Precision conversational-behavior benchmark fill a critical gap: prior datasets offered only pairwise (text-motion or audio-motion) alignments, making unified multi-turn agent training infeasible. The finding that a model not explicitly designed for audio-to-face synthesis achieves SOTA talking-head results suggests that joint speech-language-behavior pretraining transfers beneficially across sub-tasks, potentially reducing the need for task-specific pipelines. Key open questions include whether the framework can exploit full LLM reasoning (currently the backbone is largely frozen), how monocular-reconstruction artifacts in the dataset affect generalization, and whether preference-based evaluation can replace the current CLIP-style contrastive metrics for more reliable social-appropriateness scoring.
