## Problem

Structure-from-motion methods frequently fail on complex real-world videos with dynamic objects, textureless regions, and challenging camera motion, yet the field lacks reliable ground-truth benchmarks for such scenarios. Existing evaluations rely on synthetic datasets or use COLMAP itself as ground truth, which is circular and masks real failure modes. This makes it impossible to gauge genuine progress or identify where improvements are most needed.

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 100 perspective video clips (16:9 or 9:16, 671×377 to 3356×1888, 150–1000 frames at 30 fps) as input to SfM methods, each paired with a ground-truth camera trajectory. The GT poses are derived from the source 360° panoramic videos via a rig-based SfM pipeline, then transformed to account for per-frame rotational perturbations added during reprojection. Evaluated methods output per-frame camera positions and rotations, scored via ATE, RPE-T, RPE-R, and binary success/failure thresholds.

## Method

The key insight is that 360° panoramic video provides a reliable source for ground-truth camera poses because the full spherical view ensures stable static features are always visible, intrinsics are known, and wide FoV improves pose estimation. Each equirectangular frame is reprojected into four overlapping 120° cube-face perspective sub-videos (front, back, left, right), which are treated as a rigid camera rig. ORB-SLAM2 bootstraps the trajectory on the front face, followed by a rig-constrained incremental bundle adjustment with SIFT matching across all four faces. Metric scale is recovered via DepthPro monocular depth, and cross-validation with independent ORB-SLAM2 runs on the remaining cube faces filters unreliable clips. Benchmark clips are then selected by reprojecting the 360° video along directions where the baseline SfM fails, with per-frame rotational jitter and slow yaw added to simulate handheld capture.

## Results

On the ORBIT benchmark (100 perspective video clips with GT camera trajectories), all evaluated SfM methods exhibit substantial failure: every method fails to meet strict success criteria (ATE < 0.5, RPE-R < 0.4, RPE-T < 2.0) on at least 60% of clips. MegaSaM achieves the highest strict success rate at 38.46% (51.64% relaxed), while VGGT-Long reports the lowest mean ATE (2.58 m) but only a 3.29% strict success rate. The ground-truth pipeline itself is validated on 360Loc (ATE 0.07 ± 0.04 m) and a synthetic 360° set (ATE 0.0 ± 0.0 m), confirming GT accuracy is orders of magnitude below baseline failure magnitudes (often > 4 m).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MegaSaM is the strongest overall performer, achieving the highest strict success rate (38.46%) and relaxed success rate (51.64%), and the best RPE-R (0.59); combining it with RoMo motion masks slightly reduces mean ATE (3.42 vs. 3.90) and RPE-T (0.62 vs. 3.10) without changing the strict success rate. COLMAP, despite the worst mean ATE (5.40 m) and RPE-T (10.69), still succeeds on 25.27% of clips under strict criteria, reflecting its reliability on the easier subset. VGGT-Long has the lowest mean ATE (2.58 m) and RPE-T (0.17) but a very low strict success rate (3.29%), indicating it produces consistently moderate errors rather than large failures. ORB-SLAM and MonST3R perform poorly on ORBIT, with success rates of only 1.09% and 0.0% respectively, largely due to complete tracking failures on dynamic or textureless clips. A key finding is the low pairwise correlation between methods' success/failure patterns (e.g., COLMAP vs. MegaSaM correlation is near zero), confirming that ORBIT exposes diverse, non-redundant failure modes rather than a single dominant difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the rig-based SfM paradigm from prior panoramic reconstruction work (Kangni & Laganière, Klingner et al., Uyttendaele et al.) by treating 360° equirectangular frames as a rigid array of perspective cube-face cameras, bootstrapped with ORB-SLAM2 and refined via COLMAP-style incremental bundle adjustment. It departs from existing SfM benchmarks (Sintel, TartanAir, RealEstate10k) that are either synthetic or feature simple static scenes, and from datasets like DynPose-100K and SpatialVID whose ground truth is circularly derived from the very methods being evaluated. The key conceptual contribution is using the omnidirectional nature of 360° video to obtain reliable pseudo-ground-truth trajectories, then cropping to perspective views to create challenging test cases where the full-sphere context is no longer available to the evaluated method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ORBIT addresses a critical gap: the absence of a reliable, in-the-wild SfM benchmark with verified ground truth for dynamic, textureless, and high-motion scenes. The finding that every evaluated method (COLMAP, MegaSaM, VGGT-Long, MonST3R, ORB-SLAM) fails on at least 20% of clips, with low inter-method failure correlation, suggests the field is far from solving real-world SfM and that current benchmarks are masking this. Limitations include the relatively small scale (100 clips), the reliance on a self-constructed GT pipeline (validated on 360Loc and synthetic data but not independently verified), and the restriction to a single field-of-view range in the main benchmark. Open questions include whether the benchmark's challenges generalize to other camera geometries, whether the GT pipeline's ~0.07m ATE error on 360Loc is sufficient for all evaluation regimes, and whether future methods can close the gap on the specific failure modes (fluid textures, parallel moving objects, crowded scenes) that ORBIT exposes.
