## Problem

Existing infrared and visible image fusion (IVIF) methods rely on fixed or sample-adaptive fusion paradigms where weights are static or derived from global pixel distributions, overlooking spatial inconsistencies within a single image. Under non-uniform illumination, some regions are over-exposed (favoring infrared) while others are well-lit (favoring visible), yet no prior method produces spatially adaptive fusion weights that vary across regions with differing modality reliability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263157/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes the Y channel of a visible image and the infrared image (both R^{1×H×W}) as input and outputs the Y channel of the fused image. Training is unsupervised with no ground-truth fused images; inputs are cropped into 128×128 patches. The same architecture generalizes to near-infrared and visible (NIR-VIS) fusion without architectural modification, as validated on the VIS-NIR Scene dataset.

## Method

RegionFuse decomposes fusion into region-level sub-tasks via a Mixture of Region Attention (MoRA) mechanism: feature maps are split into regional tokens with overlapping partitions, a pixel-distribution-aware router (MLP + softmax) assigns responsibility scores to each region, and attention masks restrict each expert to interact only with regions sharing similar local distributions, enabling distinct fusion behaviors per region. A Region Feature Compression Module (RFCM) with channel attention and 1×1 convolution follows each MoRA block to enhance informative regions and suppress redundant ones. The encoder uses a Multi-Granular Transformer (MGT) built on X-Restormer blocks for multi-scale feature extraction, and fusion proceeds through a progressive multi-stage strategy. Training is guided by an enhanced intensity loss that replaces the conventional element-wise maximum with a Sobel-gradient-weighted mask to adaptively combine modalities per local region, plus a gradient loss and a load-balancing loss for the router during early training.

## Results

On the four primary IVIF benchmarks, RegionFuse achieves the best or near-best scores across all five metrics (SF, AG, VIF, Qabf, Qcb). On MSRS it records SF=12.12, AG=3.99, VIF=1.06, Qabf=0.72, Qcb=0.60; on TNO it leads every metric (SF=13.57, AG=4.99, VIF=0.88, Qabf=0.62, Qcb=0.57). For the downstream object-detection task on M3FD, fused images from RegionFuse yield the highest mAP@0.5 of 86.50%, surpassing the next-best DDFM (85.05%) by 1.45 points. On the unseen VIS-NIR Scene dataset, RegionFuse again tops all metrics (VIF=1.12, Qabf=0.68, Qcb=0.62), confirming cross-modality generalizability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RegionFuse outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of metrics across all four IVIF benchmarks. On MSRS it leads every metric, with the closest competitors being SHIP on SF (11.81 vs 12.12) and DCEvo on Qabf/Qcb (0.71/0.59 vs 0.72/0.60). On RoadScene, CDDFuse narrowly edges RegionFuse on SF (18.93 vs 18.73), but RegionFuse wins on AG (6.81 vs 6.74), Qabf (0.59 vs 0.48), and Qcb (0.51 vs 0.49), while tying DCEvo on VIF (0.68). On M3FD, SHIP slightly surpasses RegionFuse on AG (5.16 vs 5.14), yet RegionFuse dominates SF (15.46 vs 15.26), VIF (0.92 vs 0.84), Qabf (0.69 vs 0.67), and Qcb (0.53 vs 0.50). On TNO, RegionFuse achieves the best score on all five metrics, with margins of 0.42 in SF over CDDFuse and 0.05 in VIF over DCEvo. For the downstream YOLOv5s object-detection task on M3FD, RegionFuse-fused images yield the highest mAP@0.5 of 86.50%, beating DDFM (85.05%), PIAFusion (84.41%), and SHIP (84.45%), with particularly large gains on Lamp (+3.72 over DDFM) and Motorcycle (+2.22 over PIAFusion).

## Relation to Prior Work

RegionFuse builds directly on the Restormer architecture (Zamir et al., CVPR 2022) for its RegionFormer Block and on X-Restormer's Multi-Granular Transformer blocks (Chen et al., ECCV 2024) for its encoder, adapting image-restoration backbones to the fusion setting. Conceptually, it departs from the sample-adaptive fusion paradigm established by PIAFusion and MoEFusion, which compute a single set of fusion weights per image, by decomposing the fusion task into region-level sub-problems routed through a Mixture of Region Attention mechanism. The Mixture of Experts idea is repurposed from its original sample-routing role to intra-image region routing, where experts specialize in distinct local pixel distributions rather than distinct input samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The region-adaptive fusion paradigm offers a principled generalization of sample-level adaptivity, and the paper demonstrates consistent gains across four IVIF benchmarks plus a downstream object-detection task, suggesting the approach transfers to practical vision pipelines under non-uniform illumination. The enhanced gradient-weighted intensity loss addresses a concrete failure mode of prior losses (over-fusing visible details in over-exposed regions), which is a useful design insight for future fusion work. Open questions include the computational overhead of multiple masked-attention experts at very high resolutions, whether the region-level routing generalizes to other multi-modal fusion tasks (e.g., SAR-optical), and how the method behaves when illumination non-uniformity is extreme or adversarial rather than natural.
