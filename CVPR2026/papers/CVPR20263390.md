## Problem

Existing driving world models rely on a single diffusion model as a black box to map driving actions directly to videos, lacking explicit physical modeling. This causes physically inconsistent outputs—e.g., the model ignores turn commands and generates straight-driving videos—because it overfits to biased training data distributions rather than understanding the underlying 4D scene dynamics and the causal relationship between controls and scene evolution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263390/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 4 past occupancy frames (at 2 Hz from Occ3D annotations) and driving controls (command + trajectory) as input to the occupancy world model, which autoregressively predicts future 4D occupancy volumes. The generated occupancy is then splatted into multi-view semantic maps that condition a pretrained Wan2.1-1.3B video diffusion model, producing multi-view driving videos at 12 Hz. Through rollout, the system supports up to 241-frame (~20 s) multi-view video generation, far exceeding the 6-frame limit of prior occupancy-guided methods.

## Method

GenieDrive is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 is a lightweight occupancy world model (3.47 M params, 41 FPS) that compresses occupancy into a tri-plane latent via a VAE (reducing latent size to 58% of prior methods), uses Mutual Control Attention to model bidirectional occupancy–control interaction with intermediate transformation-matrix supervision, and is trained end-to-end with the prediction module to align the continuous latent with the forecasting task. Stage 2 renders the predicted 4D occupancy into multi-view semantic maps via splatting and inserts a Normalized Multi-View Attention module into the pretrained DiT video model; the normalization rescales the new attention output to match the pretrained distribution, preventing collapse of the learned prior during fine-tuning while enabling cross-view coherence at the same spatial height. The continuous (non-discrete) representation is critical: ablations show that adding vector quantization causes severe degradation under end-to-end training, whereas the continuous tri-plane latent improves forecasting mIoU by 7.2% over the prior SOTA.

## Results

On NuScenes 4D occupancy forecasting (6 future frames from 4 past), GenieDrive achieves 42.59% average mIoU and 51.80% average IoU, surpassing the previous best I²-World (39.73% mIoU, 49.80% IoU) by 7.2% and 4% respectively, while using only 3.47M parameters (vs. 22.71M for I²-World) and running at 41 FPS. For multi-view driving video generation, GenieDrive-L achieves 92.78 FVD on 81 frames, outperforming MagicDrive-V2 (94.84 FVD on 241 frames) and UniScene (70.52 FVD on 8 frames), with a 20.7% FVD reduction over the best occupancy-based baseline. The method also supports 241-frame (~20 s) multi-view video generation via rollout, a capability no prior occupancy-based method offers.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenieDrive outperforms all 15 baselines across both occupancy forecasting and video generation. In 4D occupancy forecasting, the closest competitor I²-World (39.73% avg mIoU) is surpassed by 2.86 points, while diffusion-based methods DOME (27.10%) and COME (34.23%) trail by 15.5 and 8.4 points respectively; LLM-based methods OccLLaMA and Occ-LLM (>7B params) achieve only ~20% mIoU. In video generation, GenieDrive-S (55.93 FVD, 8 frames) beats UniScene (70.52 FVD) by 20.7%, and GenieDrive-L (92.78 FVD, 81 frames) slightly edges out MagicDrive-V2 (94.84 FVD, 241 frames) while generating at comparable quality. For trajectory-controlled generation (Figure 3), Vista and Epona both fail on Turn Left and Turn Right commands, producing stationary or straight-going videos, whereas GenieDrive generates physically plausible turns for all three trajectories. On longer-horizon forecasting (4–6 s, Table 2), GenieDrive maintains 23.66% mIoU at 6 s while I²-World drops to 22.55% and DOME collapses to 17.27%, confirming the advantage of the tri-plane VAE and end-to-end training over both diffusion and autoregressive baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenieDrive departs from the dominant paradigm of video-based driving world models (Vista, Epona, MagicDrive) that use a single diffusion model as a black box to map driving actions directly to videos. Instead, it introduces 4D occupancy as an explicit intermediate physical representation, building on the occupancy world model line (OccWorld, DOME, I2-World) and the occupancy-guided generation line (UniScene, WoVoGen). The tri-plane VAE borrows the low-rank decomposition idea from 3D GANs [Chan et al., 2022], the intermediate transformation supervision is adapted from I2-World [Liao et al., 2025], and the video generation stage fine-tunes the pretrained Wan2.1 flow-matching model [Wang et al., 2025] with a novel Normalized Multi-View Attention module. The key conceptual departure is the end-to-end joint training of the VAE and prediction module, which the paper shows is not universally beneficial (DOME collapses under E2E) but works well with continuous tri-plane representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding video generation in an explicit 4D occupancy representation, GenieDrive addresses a fundamental limitation of black-box driving video models: data-distribution bias leads to physically implausible outputs (e.g., ignoring turn commands). The 4D occupancy intermediate also enables 3D/4D scene editing (object insertion/removal) for out-of-distribution data synthesis, which is practically important for safety-critical autonomous driving testing. The extreme efficiency (3.47M parameters, 41 FPS for occupancy) makes the approach more deployable than prior occupancy world models. Open questions include whether the two-stage pipeline generalizes to more complex urban scenes with heavy occlusion, whether the tri-plane compression degrades at higher resolutions, and whether the physics-awareness claim holds under adversarial or rare driving conditions beyond NuScenes.
