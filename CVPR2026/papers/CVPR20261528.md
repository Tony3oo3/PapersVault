## Problem

While RL-based post-training has improved text-to-image generation quality, applying RLHF to diffusion-based image editing remains largely unexplored due to the absence of scalable human-preference datasets and task-aware reward models for diverse editing sub-tasks. SFT-based editing models misalign with real-world human preferences, and constructing preference-aligned editing data at scale is prohibitively expensive through manual annotation alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261528/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a real-world RGB input image and a natural-language editing instruction (e.g., 'remove the car', 'change the sky to sunset') and produces a single edited image. During RL post-training, the HP-Scorer consumes editing triples (input image A, edited output B, instruction T) and returns a 0–5 integer score, which is normalized via a sigmoid into a continuous reward in [0,1] for the Flow-GRPO objective. The base model (Qwen-Image-Edit-2509) is updated via a rank-32 LoRA while most parameters remain frozen.

## Method

HP-Edit is a three-stage post-training framework. First, a VLM-based HP-Scorer (Qwen3-VL-32B) is calibrated to approximate human 0–5 ratings using only ~50–100 annotated triples per sub-task, with iteratively refined task-specific scoring prompts that add reasoning questions (e.g., 'Is the original object completely replaced?'). Second, the HP-Scorer scores a large pool of real-world editing cases generated by the pretrained model, and high-score (5) samples are discarded to retain hard, low-score cases that provide informative RL gradients, yielding the RealPref-50K dataset. Third, the HP-Scorer serves as the reward model in an online Flow-GRPO loop: the deterministic flow-matching ODE is converted to an equivalent SDE for stochastic exploration, group-relative advantages are computed over G samples per prompt, and the LoRA parameters are updated with a clipped policy objective plus KL regularization against the reference model.

## Results

On the proposed RealPref-Bench, HP-Edit achieves an overall HP-Score of 4.667, outperforming the strongest baseline Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 (4.472) by 0.195 points and the next-best Step1X-Edit (4.07) by 0.597 points. HP-Edit ranks first on 7 out of 8 sub-tasks, with the largest gains in color change (4.75 vs 4.358), bokeh (4.545 vs 4.165), and relighting (3.913 vs 3.54). On GEdit-Bench-EN, HP-Edit also achieves state-of-the-art results with G_SC 8.35, G_PQ 8.54, and G_O 8.30, surpassing Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 (8.15 / 7.86 / 7.54) across all three metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HP-Edit outperforms all seven baselines on the overall RealPref-Bench HP-Score. The closest competitor is Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 (4.472), which HP-Edit surpasses by 0.195 points; the gap widens to 0.597 over Step1X-Edit (4.07) and 0.81 over FLUX.1-Kontext-Dev (3.59). On individual sub-tasks, HP-Edit leads on 7 of 8 categories, tying with Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 on object swapping (both 4.781). The most pronounced margins appear in color change (+0.39 over Qwen-Image-Edit-2509), bokeh (+0.38), and relighting (+0.37), tasks requiring fine-grained realism. On GEdit-Bench-EN, HP-Edit also wins on all three metrics (G_SC, G_PQ, G_O) against every baseline, with the largest gap over Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 on G_PQ (8.54 vs 7.86) and G_O (8.30 vs 7.54). The human-evaluation column in Table 1 closely tracks the HP-Scorer scores, confirming that the VLM-based scorer aligns well with actual human judgments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLHF paradigm—well established for LLMs and recently adapted to text-to-image generation via Diffusion-DPO and Flow-GRPO—to the underexplored domain of instruction-based image-to-image editing. It builds directly on Flow-GRPO as the online RL algorithm and on pretrained flow-matching editing models (Qwen-Image-Edit-2509, FLUX.1-Kontext) as the base policy, but departs from prior T2I alignment work by introducing a VLM-based task-aware reward model (HP-Scorer) and a hard-case-focused data filtering pipeline tailored to the dual requirements of editing accuracy and aesthetic preference. The RealPref-50K dataset and RealPref-Bench benchmark fill a gap the authors identify: the absence of a real-world, object-balanced evaluation suite for preference-aligned editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical takeaway is that a small amount of human-annotated scoring data (50–100 per task) can bootstrap a VLM reward model that closely tracks human judgments, making scalable preference-aligned editing post-training feasible without prohibitive annotation costs. The ablation finding that unfiltered data with a generic scorer actually degrades performance (4.391 vs. 4.472 baseline) highlights a non-obvious pitfall: RL on editing models needs difficulty-calibrated data to avoid reward saturation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the eight curated sub-tasks, whether the VLM scorer's accuracy degrades on out-of-distribution edits, and the acknowledged limitation on code-switching and mixed-language text editing inherited from the base models.
