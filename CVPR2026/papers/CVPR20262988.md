## Problem

Human motion analysis is fragmented between perception models (MLLMs that understand video but only output text) and generation models (that produce motion but cannot perceive raw visual input), with no single architecture handling both. Additionally, existing motion tokenizers build their vocabulary exclusively from skeleton data, severing the link to the visual domain and preventing visually grounded motion generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262988/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts three input modalities within a single architecture: RGB video clips (for pose estimation), 3D skeleton sequences (for prediction and in-betweening), and text prompts. The output is a sequence of discrete motion tokens (from a vocabulary of 8192) that are decoded back into 3D joint positions. For pose estimation, the model processes 16-frame video clips; for prediction and in-betweening, it operates purely on tokenized skeleton sequences, with the MAFT vision module deactivated in those cases.

## Method

The core contribution is a Vision-Guided Motion Tokenizer (VGMT), a VQ-VAE with a hybrid codebook where each of the 8192 tokens is a bimodal entity comprising paired visual and geometric prototypes. Quantization minimizes the joint Euclidean distance in both visual and skeletal feature spaces, grounding each token in appearance and geometry simultaneously. A single Qwen2.5-VL-7B MLLM, fine-tuned via LoRA, autoregressively generates motion tokens for all three tasks (pose estimation, prediction, in-betweening) under a unified cross-entropy objective. An optional Motion-Aware Fine-Tuning (MAFT) module injects skeletal geometry into the ViT visual stream via a Visual-Skeleton Attention block, adding less than 0.2% extra parameters. Training is two-stage: the tokenizer is trained independently and frozen, then the MLLM is jointly trained on a mixture of all task-specific formats to foster cross-task knowledge transfer.

## Results

On Human3.6M, Superman (w/MAFT) achieves the best 3D pose estimation with N-MPJPE of 39.41 mm and MPJPE of 51.61 mm, outperforming the strongest traditional baseline HiC (44.77/53.86) by 11.97% in N-MPJPE. For motion prediction, Superman achieves an average MPJPE of 26.13 mm (13.70 mm at 80 ms), surpassing HiC (26.66 mm avg) and all LLM-based methods (MotionGPT3: 42.30 mm). For motion in-betweening, Superman achieves 30.61 mm average error, beating HiC (31.13 mm) and MotionGPT3 (47.66 mm). On the unseen 3DPW dataset, Superman generalizes dramatically better, achieving 62.05 mm MP for prediction and 60.68 mm for in-betweening, compared to the best traditional baseline SiC at 140.71 mm and 103.97 mm respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Superman (w/MAFT) wins on all three tasks simultaneously on Human3.6M. For pose estimation, it beats the strongest traditional multi-task model HiC by 5.36 N-MPJPE (39.41 vs 44.77) and the best LLM-based method PoseLLaVA by 10.99 N-MPJPE (39.41 vs 50.40). For motion prediction, Superman (26.13 mm avg) narrowly edges out HiC (26.66 mm) and substantially outperforms MotionGPT3 (42.30 mm) and MotionGPT (48.81 mm). For in-betweening, Superman (30.61 mm) beats HiC (31.13 mm) and MotionGPT3 (47.66 mm) by wide margins. On the zero-shot 3DPW generalization test, Superman's advantage is overwhelming: 62.05 mm MP vs SiC's 140.71 mm (a 56% reduction) and 60.68 mm MIB vs SiC's 103.97 mm. Single-frame LLM models (LocLLM, UniPose, PoseLLaVA) are not applicable to MP or MIB, highlighting Superman's unique ability to handle temporal tasks within the same architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the motion-as-language paradigm pioneered by MotionGPT and MotionGPT3, which tokenize 3D poses via VQ-VAE and model them autoregressively with an LLM, by introducing a Vision-Guided Motion Tokenizer whose hybrid codebook pairs visual and geometric prototypes, grounding the discrete vocabulary in raw video rather than skeleton data alone. It also departs from MLLM-based pose models (PoseLLaVA, UniPose, MotionLLM) that are restricted to single-frame perception or text-conditioned generation, by unifying video-based 3D pose estimation (perception) with skeleton-based motion prediction and in-betweening (generation) in a single decoder-only MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B). The Visual-Skeleton Attention module draws on deformable attention from Deformable DETR to fuse pose-centric features into the ViT stream, bridging the gap between the tokenizer's dual-stream design and the MLLM's visual encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single MLLM can simultaneously achieve SOTA on 3D pose estimation, motion prediction, and in-betweening—and generalize dramatically to the unseen 3DPW dataset (62 mm vs. 140+ mm for the next best baseline)—the paper validates a scalable, unified paradigm for human motion analysis that reduces the need for task-specific architectures. The vision-guided tokenizer addresses a real gap: prior motion vocabularies were skeleton-only, severing the link to visual evidence, and the ablation confirms that joint visual-geometric quantization is essential (4.7 mm vs. 7.7 mm reconstruction error). Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-person scenes, real-time inference (the 7B LLM dominates latency at ~3300 ms), and whether the unified multi-task benefit persists as the number of tasks or motion complexity grows.
