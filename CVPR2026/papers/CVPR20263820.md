## Problem

Existing open-source audio-video generation methods rely on decoupled two-stage pipelines or end-to-end joint models that either cannot synthesize natural human speech or fail to achieve robust lip synchronization and semantic consistency. The core gap is the absence of effective cross-modal modeling that simultaneously ensures precise temporal alignment, timbre consistency, and emotional coherence in human-centric joint generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263820/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a reference speaker image, a video caption describing desired motion/expression, and speech content text as primary inputs, with optional reference audio for timbre control and conditional audio+video for continuation tasks. Video is processed at 16 fps and encoded via a pre-trained VAE, while audio is sampled at 24 kHz and converted to Mel spectrograms. The unified framework handles five task variants (joint generation, joint generation with reference audio, joint continuation, video-to-audio dubbing, and audio-driven video synthesis) by selectively providing or omitting conditional inputs to each branch.

## Method

UniAVGen employs a symmetric dual-branch architecture with two parallel Diffusion Transformers (Wan 2.2-5B for video, Wan 2.1-1.3B for audio) trained under Flow Matching, establishing representational parity across modalities. The key innovation is an Asymmetric Cross-Modal Interaction mechanism: the A2V aligner constructs contextualized audio windows spanning neighboring frames for each video latent, while the V2A aligner uses temporal neighbor interpolation to give each audio token a smoothly weighted visual context from two adjacent video latents. A Face-Aware Modulation module adds a lightweight mask-prediction head per interaction layer, supervised with a decaying loss weight, to dynamically constrain cross-attention to facial regions early in training and gradually relax toward full-scene interaction. At inference, Modality-Aware Classifier-Free Guidance (MA-CFG) computes a single shared unconditional estimate (with cross-modal interactions nullified) and uses it as the baseline to amplify cross-modal correlation signals in both branches simultaneously. Training proceeds in three stages: audio-branch pre-training on Emilia, end-to-end joint training on a real human audio-video dataset, and multi-task fine-tuning across all five task variants.

## Results

UniAVGen achieves the best audio quality among joint generation methods (PQ 7.00, CU 6.62, WER 0.151) and the highest video dynamism (DD 0.410) and imaging quality (IQ 0.779) across all compared methods. On audio-video consistency, it attains the best timbre consistency (TC 0.832) and emotion consistency (EC 0.573), while maintaining competitive lip synchronization (LS 5.95). Notably, these results are achieved with only 1.3M joint training samples, roughly 24× fewer than Ovi (30.7M) and 5× fewer than UniVerse-1 (6.4M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two-stage baselines (OmniAvatar, Wan-S2V), UniAVGen dramatically outperforms on cross-modal consistency—timbre consistency improves from 0.454/0.481 to 0.832 and emotion consistency from 0.349/0.375 to 0.573—while also achieving far higher video dynamism (DD 0.410 vs. 0.000/0.130), confirming that decoupled pipelines cannot produce audio-aligned motion. Among joint generation methods, UniAVGen dominates JavisDiT on every metric (e.g., WER 0.151 vs. 0.986, LS 5.95 vs. 1.23) and UniVerse-1 on all metrics except subject consistency (0.973 vs. 0.985). Compared to the strongest joint baseline Ovi, UniAVGen wins on audio quality (PQ 7.00 vs. 6.03, WER 0.151 vs. 0.216), video dynamism (0.410 vs. 0.360), timbre consistency (0.832 vs. 0.828), and emotion consistency (0.573 vs. 0.558), while Ovi retains a slight edge on lip synchronization (6.48 vs. 5.95) and subject consistency (0.972 vs. 0.973). Overall, UniAVGen achieves the best or near-best score on 9 of 11 metrics using only 1.3M training samples versus Ovi's 30.7M.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniAVGen positions itself within the joint audio-video generation paradigm, departing from two-stage cascaded pipelines (audio-driven video or video-to-audio) that suffer from inherent modality decoupling. Among concurrent joint-generation methods, it contrasts most directly with Ovi (symmetric dual-tower with symmetric global cross-modal interaction) and UniVerse-1 (stitching two pre-trained asymmetric models). The key architectural departure is the Asymmetric Cross-Modal Interaction mechanism—using modality-specific temporal alignment strategies (contextualized audio windows for A2V, interpolated video contexts for V2A) rather than the symmetric or global attention patterns in prior work—combined with a face-aware modulation module that has no direct precedent in the joint-generation literature. The backbone leverages the Wan 2.2-5B / Wan 2.1-1.3B DiT family with flow matching, repurposing these video-generation architectures for a symmetric dual-branch audio-video setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant finding is the 23× training-sample efficiency (1.3M vs. 30.7M for Ovi) while matching or exceeding performance on audio-video consistency metrics, suggesting that targeted cross-modal interaction design can substitute for brute-force data scaling. The multi-task unification (joint generation, video-to-audio, audio-driven video, continuation) in a single model eliminates the need for task-specific architectures, and the finding that joint-generation pre-training followed by multi-task fine-tuning (JFML) outperforms both joint-only and multi-task-only training provides a useful training recipe. Open questions include whether the asymmetric interaction and face-aware modulation generalize beyond human-centric content (e.g., multi-speaker scenes, non-human subjects), how the approach scales to longer video durations, and whether the reliance on Gemini-2.5-Pro for timbre/emotion consistency evaluation limits reproducibility.
