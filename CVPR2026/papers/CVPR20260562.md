## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods learn a direct mapping from raw text to full-body motion, which severely limits generalization to out-of-domain textual descriptions due to the limited scale and semantic diversity of paired datasets like HumanML3D. The paper identifies that while high-level motion semantics vary widely, motions share a common set of reusable atomic body-part movements, and proposes exploiting this structure as an intermediate representation to bridge the domain gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260562/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form natural language motion description (which may be entirely unseen in training) and produces a 3D human motion sequence. The input text is first decomposed into an L×P matrix of atomic motion descriptions (L body-part categories: spine, left/right upper limbs, left/right lower limbs, root trajectory; P temporal segments). The output motion is represented as discrete tokens via a Residual VQ-VAE (base layer plus R residual layers) with a downsampling factor r, and is generated iteratively through masked token prediction until all tokens are resolved.

## Method

The framework operates in two coupled stages. Textual Decomposition uses a rule-based fine-grained description conversion algorithm (extracting joint-level pose descriptors, aggregating them into clips with velocity and amplitude, and converting to behavior labels like 'bending significantly slowly') combined with LLM summarization to produce semantically faithful atomic motion texts. Atomic Recomposition employs a Text-Motion Alignment (TMA) module pretrained with InfoNCE contrastive loss on text-motion pairs to align text features into the motion space, and a Compositional Feature Fusion (CFF) module that splits motion features along the channel dimension into L body-part expert branches and fuses each with its corresponding atomic text embedding via cross-attention (atomic texts as Keys/Values, motion embeddings as Queries). The overall generative model uses a discrete mask modeling paradigm: motion tokens are randomly masked, and a stack of K Transformer-CFF blocks iteratively refines the representation, with a classification head predicting quantized motion indices via cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On the in-domain HumanML3D dataset, the method achieves FID 0.132, R-Precision 0.498, and Diversity 9.321, which is competitive with but slightly behind the best in-domain baselines (MMM: FID 0.080; ATTT2M: FID 0.112). On the out-of-domain IDEA400 dataset, the method achieves FID 0.847 and R-Precision 0.449, dramatically outperforming all baselines on R-Precision (next best: T2M-GPT at 0.211) while matching MDM's FID (0.821). On the out-of-domain Mixamo dataset, the method achieves the best FID (0.186) and R-Precision (0.516) among all compared methods, with R-Precision more than doubling the next-best baseline (T2M-GPT at 0.249).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five quantitative baselines (MDM, T2M-GPT, ATTT2M, ATOM, MMM) and one qualitative baseline (MotionMillion, a 7B-parameter model). On out-of-domain datasets, the method's advantage is most pronounced in R-Precision: on IDEA400 it scores 0.449 versus 0.186 (MDM), 0.211 (T2M-GPT), 0.161 (ATTT2M), 0.190 (ATOM), and 0.183 (MMM); on Mixamo it scores 0.516 versus 0.238–0.249 for the other methods. On FID, the method wins on Mixamo (0.186 vs. 0.211–0.471) and IDEA400 (0.847 vs. 0.933–1.051), though MDM edges it slightly on IDEA400 (0.821) and MMM/ATTT2M/T2M-GPT are slightly better on in-domain HumanML3D FID (0.080/0.112/0.116 vs. 0.132). ATOM is the weakest baseline across all settings, with FID 1.691 on HumanML3D and R-Precision below 0.25 on out-of-domain sets. Qualitatively, the method outperforms MotionMillion on composite motions (e.g., correctly generating a walking-while-lifting sequence where MotionMillion produces only a stationary lift), despite being trained on a far smaller dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant text-to-motion paradigm of learning a direct raw-text-to-motion mapping (as in MDM, T2M-GPT, ATTT2M) and the open-vocabulary strategies of cross-domain alignment (MotionCLIP) or pretrain-then-finetune (OMG). It draws on the contrastive text-motion alignment formulation from TMR [43] for its TMA module, and is conceptually related to ATOM [70]'s atomic action codebook, but replaces the learnable codebook with LLM-generated textual atomic descriptions and a structured channel-level cross-attention fusion (CFF). The key architectural lineage is discrete masked modeling (T2M-GPT, MMM) combined with Residual VQ-VAE quantization, while the decomposition-recomposition framing is inspired by PRIMAL's observation that short motion segments span the motion space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in T2M: paired datasets like HumanML3D are too small and semantically narrow to support open-vocabulary generation, and scaling data (MotionMillion) still leaves long-tail motions uncovered. The t-SNE evidence that atomic motion descriptions are nearly domain-invariant across HumanML3D, IDEA400, and Mixamo is a useful diagnostic that could inform future data curation and augmentation strategies. Limitations include reliance on a hand-crafted rule-based algorithm for training-time decomposition and an LLM for inference-time decomposition, which introduces a dependency on LLM quality and may not generalize to non-English or highly technical motion descriptions. Open questions include whether the atomic decomposition scales to multi-person, scene-aware, or very long-horizon motions, and whether the channel-splitting CFF design is optimal compared to learned routing or mixture-of-experts alternatives.
