## Problem

Existing micro-gesture recognition models passively process all spatio-temporal frames and regions, making them insensitive to the transient, low-amplitude nature of micro-gestures and prone to overconfidence on ambiguous or noisy samples. No prior work has formulated the selection of informative temporal segments and spatial regions as an active inference (uncertainty-minimization) problem tailored to the fine-grained, temporally sparse structure of micro-gestures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260598/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB video sequences (resized to 224×224 per frame) from the SMG dataset and outputs one of 17 micro-gesture class labels. Rather than processing all T frames uniformly, the framework adaptively selects a subset of temporal keyframes and reweights spatial regions within each selected frame, reducing redundant computation. During training, each sample undergoes T=5 stochastic forward passes with dropout active to estimate epistemic uncertainty before the final classification loss is computed.

## Method

UAAI formulates micro-gesture recognition as a POMDP under the active inference paradigm, jointly optimizing perception and observation selection via variational free energy minimization. Temporal keyframe selection is driven by Expected Free Energy (EFE) minimization: a lightweight MLP parameterizes the observation likelihood, and Bayesian updating over a uniform prior yields posterior beliefs; the frame whose observation most reduces KL divergence between predicted and target beliefs is selected. Spatial selection decomposes EFE over spatial locations and is implemented as a differentiable SE-Net-style attention mask (channel-wise avg+max pooling → convolution → sigmoid) that suppresses irrelevant regions. An uncertainty-aware augmentation module (UMIX) estimates per-sample epistemic uncertainty via Monte Carlo Dropout, then adaptively re-weights training samples and modulates Mixup interpolation ratios so that noisier or harder-to-classify examples receive lower loss contribution, acting as a data-driven implicit regularizer.

## Results

On the SMG dataset (17 micro-gesture categories, RGB modality), UAAI achieves 63.47% classification accuracy, outperforming the strongest RGB-based baseline TRN (59.51%) by 3.96 points and the next-best RGB method TSM (58.69%) by 4.78 points. UAAI narrows the gap to the best skeleton-based method MS-G3D (64.75%) to just 1.28 percentage points while using only RGB input. In the keyframe-selection comparison (Table 2), UAAI's EFE-guided temporal selection surpasses Logic-in-Frames (61.31%) by 2.16 points and VideoTree (59.51%) by 3.96 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UAAI outperforms all nine compared baselines on the SMG dataset. Among RGB-based methods, UAAI (63.47%) leads TRN (59.51%) by 3.96 points, TSM (58.69%) by 4.78 points, Video Mamba (55.08%) by 8.39 points, MA-Net (48.69%) by 14.78 points, C3D (45.90%) by 17.57 points, and MSTCN-VAE (42.59%) by 20.88 points. Against skeleton-based baselines, UAAI exceeds Shift-GCN (55.31%) by 8.16 points, 2S-GCN (43.11%) by 20.36 points, and ST-GCN (41.48%) by 21.99 points, though it trails the strongest skeleton method MS-G3D (64.75%) by 1.28 points. In the dedicated keyframe-selection comparison, UAAI's EFE-guided temporal selection beats Logic-in-Frames (61.31%) by 2.16 points and VideoTree (59.51%) by 3.96 points, confirming that uncertainty-minimizing frame selection is more effective than semantic-coverage-oriented strategies for the temporally sparse micro-gesture task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the active inference framework (Friston et al., 2017) from robotics and cognitive science into fine-grained video recognition, formulating temporal keyframe selection as an Expected Free Energy minimization problem under a POMDP rather than as heuristic attention or semantic-completeness optimization. It also extends the UMIX uncertainty-aware mixup principle (Han et al., NeurIPS 2022) by embedding epistemic uncertainty directly into the variational free-energy objective rather than treating it as a post-hoc calibration step. Conceptually, the paper departs from long-video keyframe selection methods (Logic-in-Frames, VideoTree) that optimize for semantic coverage, arguing instead that micro-gesture recognition requires uncertainty-minimizing observation under temporally sparse, low-amplitude signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The active inference framing offers an interpretable, principled alternative to ad-hoc attention mechanisms for fine-grained temporal behavior modeling, with potential applicability to other low-signal tasks in wearable sensing and clinical affective monitoring. However, the evaluation is limited to a single dataset (SMG) and a single backbone (ResNet-50), and the 1.28-point gap to the best skeleton-based method (MS-G3D) suggests the RGB-only formulation still has room to grow. Open questions include whether the EFE-guided POMDP formulation scales to longer sequences or more diverse gesture vocabularies, and whether the Monte Carlo Dropout uncertainty estimation remains reliable under distribution shift beyond the SMG protocol.
