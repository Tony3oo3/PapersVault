## Problem

TabPFN, a tabular foundation model pretrained on synthetic distributions, achieves strong in-context classification but is restricted to purely tabular inputs with no principled mechanism for integrating unstructured modalities such as images and text. Existing multimodal tabular methods either rely on heuristic fusion strategies that degrade under data scarcity or require expensive end-to-end training, leaving a gap for a scalable, prior-data-fitted approach that unifies heterogeneous modalities in a single forward pass.

## Input / Output

MMPFN accepts three input types simultaneously: structured tabular features (numeric and categorical columns), RGB images resized so both dimensions are divisible by 14 (processed by DINOv2 ViT-B/14 yielding a single [CLS] token), and text truncated to 512 tokens (processed by ELECTRA yielding a [CLS] embedding). The model produces posterior-predictive class label distributions via in-context inference, where labeled training rows and unlabeled test rows are concatenated into a single table and fed jointly to the backbone in one forward pass, with test rows restricted to cross-attend only to training rows.

## Method

MMPFN extends TabPFN with a modality projector that bridges non-tabular embeddings into the tabular feature space. The projector has two sublayers: a Multi-head Gated MLP (MGM) that expands the single [CLS] embedding into N parallel d-dimensional projections, each modulated by a Gated Linear Unit to encourage head-wise specialization and preserve diverse aspects of the non-tabular representation; and a Cross-Attention Pooler (CAP) that uses K learnable query vectors to cross-attend over the N MGM tokens, compressing them into K compact, calibrated embeddings that are concatenated with tabular tokens along the feature dimension. This two-stage design addresses two identified failure modes: overcompressed non-tabular embeddings (solved by MGM's multi-token expansion) and token-count-induced attention imbalance, where the modality with more tokens absorbs a disproportionate share of the attention budget (solved by CAP's compression to a balanced token count). During fine-tuning, all modality encoders remain frozen while the modality projector, TabPFN backbone, and decoder head are trained with cross-entropy loss over 100 iterations, leveraging TabPFN's pretrained tabular prior while adapting to multimodal inputs through lightweight parameter updates.

## Results

On tabular-image datasets, MMPFN achieves the best average rank (1.50) across PU20 (85.22%), Mass (74.53%), Calc (75.40%), and PetFinder (40.74%), outperforming the strongest baselines by 3.05 points on PU20 and 1.49 points on PetFinder. On tabular-text datasets, MMPFN attains the best average rank (2.25) with 47.78% on Airbnb, 46.17% on Salary, 66.26% on Cloth, and 39.04% on PetFinder. In low-data regimes (10% of training samples), MMPFN still outperforms TIP on all four medical datasets, with performance even improving on CBIS-DDSM Mass (+0.75% vs. full data).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMPFN consistently outperforms the tabular-only TabPFN baseline on every dataset (e.g., 85.22% vs. 82.17% on PU20, 47.78% vs. 46.96% on Airbnb), confirming that the modality projector successfully injects non-tabular signal. Against CatBoost, MMPFN wins on PU20 (+4.79), Calc (+3.31), and PetFinder (+2.05) but trails on Mass (74.53% vs. 78.31%), where CatBoost's use of raw image embeddings as features is particularly effective. Among multimodal-specific baselines, MMPFN dominates MMCL, TIP, and HEALNet by 5–18 points across all tabular-image datasets, and outperforms TIME (which also uses TabPFN as a tabular encoder) on PU20 (+4.87) and PetFinder (+1.49), indicating that the MGM+CAP projection is more effective than TIME's simpler fusion. On tabular-text datasets, MMPFN beats TTT on Airbnb (+9.48) and Cloth (+0.76) but loses on Salary (46.17% vs. 47.2%), and trails AutoGluon on Cloth (66.26% vs. 72.07%) where text carries most of the signal. Overall, MMPFN achieves the best average rank on both the image (1.50) and text (2.25) benchmark suites, with its largest margins appearing on datasets where both modalities contribute complementary information.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends TabPFN, a tabular foundation model pretrained via amortized Bayesian inference on synthetic data, to handle heterogeneous multimodal inputs (tabular + image + text) by introducing a modality projector that maps non-tabular embeddings into the tabular token space. It departs from prior multimodal tabular methods (MMCL, TIP, HEALNet, TIME) that either train fusion layers from scratch or use contrastive alignment, instead reusing the pretrained TabPFN backbone and only fine-tuning lightweight projection components. The multi-head gated MLP (MGM) and cross-attention pooler (CAP) are novel architectural contributions that address two identified failure modes—overcompressed non-tabular representations and token-count-induced attention imbalance—that prior multimodal tabular work had not formally characterized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMPFN provides a practical, lightweight framework for domains where structured and unstructured data co-occur (healthcare diagnostics, e-commerce), and its strong low-data performance (outperforming TIP even at 10% labeled data) is directly relevant to expert-annotated settings. The formal analysis of attention imbalance as a function of token-count disparity offers a diagnostic that could inform design choices in any multimodal transformer, not just tabular ones. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger datasets or more than two non-tabular modalities, whether the fine-tuning requirement can be eliminated for true zero-shot multimodal inference, and whether the modality projector generalizes to other backbone architectures beyond TabPFN.
