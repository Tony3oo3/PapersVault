## Problem

Pose estimation under extreme-baseline conditions (sparse views with minimal visual overlap) remains a major challenge for both classical SfM and 3D foundation models. While video generation models can synthesize intermediate frames to enrich contextual cues, their training objectives emphasize visual realism and temporal smoothness rather than 3D geometric consistency, producing interpolated frames that are temporally coherent yet spatially distorted, which degrades downstream pose accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263524/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes only a reference-target RGB image pair {I_ref, I_target} with large viewpoint separation and produces N intermediate video frames via a rectified-flow generator, which are then fed into a frozen 3D foundation model (VGGT or MapAnything) to yield a relative camera pose (rotation matrix R and unit translation direction u). During training, an auxiliary frame I_aux is additionally selected from the DL3DV dataset to construct geometry-consistent pseudo-video triplets for supervised fine-tuning, but this frame is discarded at test time. The generated video is an N-frame sequence (performance saturates around 7 intermediate frames) at the resolution supported by the LTX-Video backbone.

## Method

ExPose fine-tunes a rectified-flow video generator (LTX-Video) through a three-component training pipeline. First, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) trains the model to match pseudo videos generated by a pretrained generator conditioned on an auxiliary frame, using only the reference-target pair as input, establishing initial geometric plausibility. Second, pose-guided online RL converts the deterministic ODE of rectified flow into an SDE for stochastic candidate sampling, then applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) where a frozen VGGT pose estimator provides a scale-invariant reward combining geodesic rotation distance and translation-direction error, with a KL regularizer preventing mode collapse. Third, a point-tracking-based diversity reward (using CoTracker on reference-only conditioned videos) encourages the generator to explore diverse early camera trajectories, while a pose interpolation constraint penalizes discontinuous or multi-take camera paths. The composite loss combines the GRPO preference objective with the SFT reconstruction loss, and only the video generator is updated throughout training.

## Results

On DL3DV with VGGT, ExPose achieves MRE 33.78° and MTE 20.50°, reducing rotation error by 11.4° over the best baseline (InterPose, 45.22°) and improving AUC from 48.13 to 53.64. On NAVI, ExPose reaches MRE 13.10° and MTE 7.75°, outperforming all baselines including Aether (MRE 13.60°) and InterPose (MRE 17.30°). On Cambridge Landmarks, ExPose achieves MRE 11.48° and AUC 79.44, a 3.9° improvement over InterPose (15.36°). The method achieves state-of-the-art results on every metric of DL3DV, NAVI, and Cambridge Landmarks, and leads on most metrics of ScanNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ExPose outperforms all six baselines on the majority of metrics across all four datasets. Against the no-video baselines (VGGT and MapAnything), ExPose consistently reduces MRE by 10–20° and improves AUC by 5–10 points, confirming that generated intermediate frames substantially aid pose estimation under extreme baselines. Among video-generation baselines, ExPose beats the unmodified LTX-Video backbone by 10.3° MRE on DL3DV (33.78 vs. 44.13) and 5.8° on NAVI (13.10 vs. 18.85), demonstrating the value of the GRPO-based fine-tuning. InterPose (test-time multi-sample selection) is the strongest competitor, but ExPose still leads by 11.4° MRE on DL3DV, 4.2° on NAVI, and 0.8° on ScanNet; the only metric where InterPose edges out ExPose is 15° Racc on ScanNet (62.67 vs. 61.00). Aether, the geometric-aware video model, is competitive on NAVI (MRE 13.60 vs. 13.10) but trails ExPose by 9.3° on DL3DV and 3.4° on ScanNet. DynamiCrafter, the diffusion-based baseline, is consistently the weakest video generator, with ExPose improving MRE by 12.9° on DL3DV and 4.7° on NAVI over it.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three active research threads: (1) 3D foundation models (VGGT, MapAnything, DUSt3R) that predict camera poses from sparse views, (2) keyframe-conditioned video generation (LTX-Video, DynamiCrafter, Aether) used to synthesize intermediate frames for pose estimation, and (3) preference-based RL for generative models (GRPO, Flow-GRPO, DPO). The closest prior work—InterPose and Aether—uses off-the-shelf video generators to produce intermediate frames but suffers from 3D inconsistency. ExPose departs by fine-tuning the video generator itself via GRPO with pose-derived rewards from a frozen 3D foundation model, converting the deterministic rectified-flow ODE into a stochastic SDE to enable group-based exploration, and adding a CoTracker-based diversity reward to prevent trajectory collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that geometry-aware RL can align a video generation model's outputs with downstream 3D structure, bridging the long-standing gap between visual realism and spatial consistency in generative video. A notable practical finding is that the supervised fine-tuning stage (L1 loss on pseudo-videos) captures most of the performance gain, with GRPO providing incremental refinement—suggesting that even simple geometric supervision on synthetic intermediate frames is highly effective. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer sequences or more complex multi-object scenes, whether the RL stage becomes more critical as the base video model improves, and whether the same reward-driven paradigm can be extended to other geometry-sensitive tasks (e.g., depth, 3D reconstruction) beyond relative pose estimation.
