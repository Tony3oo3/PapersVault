## Problem

Training VLA models for dexterous manipulation requires large-scale demonstrations across many robotic hands, and the rapid emergence of new dexterous embodiments makes per-hand data collection costly and impractical. Because joint-space action parameterizations vary significantly across hand morphologies, standard VLA architectures cannot share a single policy across heterogeneous hands without substantial performance degradation. The paper asks how to define a unified, embodiment-invariant action representation that enables joint training and zero-shot transfer across diverse dexterous hands.

## Input / Output

The policy receives RGB images, a natural-language instruction, and a short history of joint states; the previous 64-frame action chunk (20 Hz, 3.2 s) is encoded by a hand-specific MLP into a compact latent token that replaces the raw proprioceptive state tokens in the VLA backbone. The model outputs a single latent vector per step, which is decoded by the corresponding hand-specific decoder into 64 continuous joint-position commands at 20 Hz. Notably, the hand identity is never passed as an explicit token to the transformer—it is used only to select the correct encoder/decoder pair, keeping the VLA backbone fully hand-agnostic.

## Method

XL-VLA introduces a multi-headed VAE-style autoencoder with hand-specific encoder and decoder MLPs that all map to a shared Gaussian latent space. The latent space is pretrained unsupervised on randomly sampled joint configurations (no demonstrations or IK trajectories) using three losses: per-hand reconstruction (MSE on joint angles), cross-hand retargeting (aligning fingertip pinch distances and directions via differentiable forward kinematics across all hand pairs), and KL regularization toward N(0,I). This pretrained latent space is then plugged into a π0-based VLA (PaliGemma vision-language backbone + flow-matching action expert), where the encoders and decoders remain frozen while the action expert is finetuned to regress latent action chunks conditioned on vision, language, and latent state history. The key mechanism is that the retargeting loss enforces geometric consistency of pinch behavior across embodiments, so a single latent code produces coordinated finger poses on any hand, enabling zero-shot transfer to unseen hand-task combinations without additional training.

## Results

On the 10-task, 4-hand cross-embodiment benchmark, XL-VLA achieves a mean success rate of 0.72, compared to 0.32 for the π0 baseline trained on the same multi-hand dataset (+40% absolute). Per-hand gains are consistent: Ability 0.37→0.73, Inspire 0.27→0.68, Paxini 0.35→0.78, and X-Hand1 0.29→0.70. In the zero-shot unseen-task setting (Fig. 4), XL-VLA also outperforms a π0+retargeting baseline on every hand-task combination, with particularly large margins on dexterity-heavy tasks like Hand over Bottle and Re-arrange Boxes. In the latent replay comparison (Table 4), XL-VLA's unsupervised latent space achieves 0.82/0.81 mean replay success on two hand pairs, versus 0.60/0.61 for the supervised LAD method.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline π0 (trained as a single shared policy on the same 4-hand, 10-task dataset), XL-VLA wins on every single hand-task cell in Table 2, improving the overall mean from 0.32 to 0.72. The largest per-task gains appear on dexterity-heavy tasks: Sort Cans (0.08→0.55), Hand over Bottle (0.40→0.95), and Re-arrange Boxes (0.45→0.90), while the smallest gain is on Push Sugar (0.25→0.35). Against the supervised latent-space retargeting method LAD [Bauer et al., 2025], XL-VLA's unsupervised latent autoencoder achieves substantially higher cross-hand replay success (0.82 vs. 0.60 on Ability+Inspire; 0.81 vs. 0.61 on Paxini+X-Hand1), with the gap widening on fine-grained tasks like Stack Cans (0.7 vs. 0.4) and Hand over Bottle (0.7 vs. 0.4). In the zero-shot unseen-task evaluation, XL-VLA also consistently outperforms a π0+kinematic-retargeting baseline across all four hands, never underperforming on any hand-task pair, confirming that the learned latent space provides genuine transfer benefits beyond geometric retargeting.

## Relation to Prior Work

XL-VLA builds directly on the π0 flow-matching VLA architecture, replacing its proprioceptive state tokens with hand-specific latent action tokens while keeping the vision-language backbone and action expert intact. Conceptually, it extends the cross-embodiment latent-action-space paradigm (UniVLA, ATE, LAD, CycleVAE) from the simpler parallel-gripper setting to the substantially more complex dexterous-hand domain, where joint spaces differ in dimensionality, actuation, and kinematics across embodiments. The key architectural departure is the use of a lightweight multi-headed VAE with differentiable forward-kinematics-based retargeting loss to align fingertip geometry across hands, rather than the discrete VQ tokens or diffusion-based generation used in prior latent action spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play latent space means a newly released dexterous hand only requires training a small encoder/decoder pair rather than retraining the entire VLA policy, which is practically significant given the rapid pace of hand hardware innovation. The demonstrated zero-shot transfer to unseen hand-task combinations suggests a path toward data-efficient deployment across a growing fleet of heterogeneous hands. However, the evaluation is limited to four hands, ten tasks, and two robot platforms (xArm tabletop and Unitree G1), and the latent space is trained purely on random joint configurations within hardware limits rather than task-relevant distributions, leaving open whether the approach generalizes to hands with fundamentally different kinematic structures (e.g., underactuated or tendon-driven designs) or to longer-horizon, contact-rich manipulation.
