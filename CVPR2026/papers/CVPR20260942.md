## Problem

Parallel thinking has shown promise for LLM reasoning but remains unexplored in multimodal settings, where diverse visual cues could be leveraged through multiple reasoning paths. Moreover, standard RL methods like GRPO assign a single uniform reward to all tokens in a reasoning chain, failing to distinguish between key points that are genuinely informative and those that are irrelevant or misleading, which limits fine-grained credit assignment in multi-point reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260942/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (or 16/32/64 video frames) paired with a text question and produces a structured multi-point chain-of-thought: each key point is wrapped in <p> tags, its associated reasoning in <r> tags, all enclosed in a think block, followed by a final answer in <answer> tags. During GPPO training, the multi-point output is additionally decomposed into single-point trajectories where the model re-reasons from one (point, reasoning) pair in isolation and emits a <pointanswer> for verification.

## Method

PointThinker first prompts the model to enumerate K distinct visual key points, then generates an independent reasoning path for each, synthesizing a final answer from all paths. For training, GPPO (Group Points Policy Optimization) extends GRPO with a three-stage dense reward: (1) decompose each multi-point rollout into K single-point trajectories by re-reasoning from one (p_i, r_i) pair in isolation; (2) verify each trajectory's answer correctness to obtain point-level signals; (3) apply selective masking so that tokens receive the rollout-level advantage only when their associated point's verification aligns with the overall rollout outcome (correct-correct or incorrect-incorrect). A second objective directly optimizes single-point trajectories via intra-rollout group comparison, incentivizing the model to generate points that can independently solve the problem. Training uses pure RL (no SFT cold-start) on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with the Easy-R1 framework.

## Results

On image benchmarks, PointThinker-7B (built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) achieves an average of 58.1% across 7 benchmarks, improving +4.1% over the base model. Headline gains include 58.7% on HallusionBench (+5.8%), 73.4% on MMVet (+6.3%), and 74.2% on MathVista (+5.1%). On video benchmarks (64 frames), PointThinker reaches 37.7% on VSI-Bench, 53.0% on VideoMMMU, and 66.1% on MMVU, outperforming Video-R1-7B on all six video benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PointThinker-7B outperforms its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B on all seven image benchmarks, with the largest gains on HallusionBench (+5.8%) and MMVet (+6.3%). Against the closest same-base competitor WeThink-VL-7B, PointThinker wins on 6 of 7 benchmarks (e.g., +2.9% on HallusionBench, +2.6% on MathVista) and ties on MMMU (59.3%). Compared to Ovis2-8B and InternVL2.5-8B, PointThinker leads by 3.3% and 10.2% in average score respectively. On video benchmarks, PointThinker-7B (64 frames) surpasses Video-R1-7B on all six metrics, including +0.6% on VSI-Bench (37.7 vs. 37.1) and +2.3% on MMVU (66.1 vs. 63.8). The only near-tie is MathVista where MUG-U-7B scores 74.8 vs. PointThinker's 74.2, but PointThinker dominates on all other benchmarks including the average.

## Relation to Prior Work

PointThinker extends the parallel thinking paradigm (Self-Consistency, Tree-of-Thoughts, Parallel-R1) from text-only LLMs to multimodal settings, where it had been unexplored. Its training mechanism, GPPO, is a direct extension of GRPO (DeepSeekMath), adding a point-level selective masking layer on top of the standard rollout-level advantage to enable fine-grained credit assignment across multiple reasoning paths. The explicit key-point enumeration distinguishes it from prior parallel approaches that generate undifferentiated reasoning chains, addressing the redundancy problem where multiple paths revisit similar aspects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that structured point-based parallel reasoning plus dense point-level rewards can improve MLLM reasoning without SFT cold-start, the paper offers a practical recipe for enhancing multimodal reasoning quality. The finding that SFT initialization can actually degrade performance (−2.9% on HallusionBench) challenges the common assumption that imitation-based pre-training is a necessary prerequisite for RL. Open questions include how the number of key points should scale with problem complexity, whether GPPO's additional verification forward passes remain tractable at larger model scales, and whether the point structure generalizes to open-ended generation tasks beyond multiple-choice or short-answer formats.
