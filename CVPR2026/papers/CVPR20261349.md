## Problem

State-of-the-art dense point trackers rely on cost volumes to match features across frames, but computing these volumes scales quadratically with spatial resolution, forcing methods to operate at low resolution or restrict search regions. The paper asks whether the warping-based matching mechanism recently shown to work for optical flow (WAFT) can replace cost volumes entirely in dense point tracking, enabling higher-resolution feature indexing without the quadratic memory and compute overhead.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video of T+1 frames (336×560 in training) and a grid of query points in the reference frame I_0, producing a dense displacement field u_t(p) for every query pixel in every target frame, along with per-point visibility probabilities and confidence scores. Features are extracted at stride 2 (near pixel resolution) via a VGGT backbone and DPT upsampler, in contrast to the stride-14/16 features typical of cost-volume methods. The same architecture, applied to a two-frame 'video' with no flow-specific training, directly outputs optical flow.

## Method

CoWTracker replaces cost volumes with an iterative warping loop: at each of K=5 refinement steps, target-frame features are bilinearly sampled at locations specified by the current displacement estimate and brought into correspondence with the query frame. The warped target features, query features, current displacement, and a learned hidden state are concatenated and processed by a spatiotemporal transformer that alternates spatial self-attention (across pixels within each frame) and temporal self-attention (across frames at each pixel), predicting a residual displacement update. The only cross-frame feature pairing is the single bilinear sample in the warping step, so compute scales linearly with spatial resolution rather than quadratically. The backbone (VGGT) is fine-tuned jointly with a DPT upsampler and the tracker head, trained end-to-end on Kubric with Huber loss on displacements and BCE on visibility/confidence.

## Results

On the TAP-Vid benchmark (DAVIS, RGB-Stacking, Kinetics) and RoboTAP, CoWTracker trained only on Kubric achieves mean AJ 71.3, δ_avg 81.8, and OA 93.3, outperforming the strongest dense baseline AllTracker (Kub+Mix) by +2.4 AJ, +1.3 δ_avg, and +1.8 OA, and the strongest sparse baseline CoTracker3 (offline) by +9.3 AJ, +7.4 δ_avg, and +3.7 OA. On zero-shot optical flow (no flow-specific training), CoWTracker attains 0.78 EPE on Sintel Clean and 1.04 EPE on KITTI-2015, beating the best specialized flow model WAFT (0.94 and 1.15 EPE respectively) by 17% and 9.6% relative.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoWTracker outperforms all 13 baselines on the primary tracking benchmarks. Against the strongest dense tracker AllTracker (Kub+Mix), CoWTracker gains +2.4 AJ, +1.3 δ_avg, and +1.8 OA on average, with the largest per-dataset margin on DAVIS (+2.2 AJ, +1.7 δ_avg, +2.0 OA); against AllTracker trained on Kubric only, the gap widens to +3.3/+2.2/+3.0. Among sparse trackers, CoWTracker surpasses CoTracker3 (offline) by +9.3 AJ and +7.4 δ_avg on average, and beats BootsTAPIR by +10.9 AJ, confirming that the warping-based dense head is substantially more accurate than cost-volume-based sparse methods. On optical flow, CoWTracker (zero-shot, no flow training) beats RAFT (1.15→0.78 EPE on Sintel Clean), SEA-RAFT (0.97→0.78), and WAFT twins-a2 (0.94→0.78) on Sintel, and outperforms all three on KITTI (1.04 vs. 1.15–1.60 EPE). On Spring, CoWTracker (0.17 EPE) is slightly behind WAFT (0.11–0.13 EPE) but within 0.06 EPE, a gap the authors attribute to Spring's very low motion regime (3.5 px average flow). The paper also shows CoWTracker is more robust to large displacements: in the highest-motion bin on Sintel, EPE is 46% lower than SEA-RAFT, whereas the average improvement is ~22.5%.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoWTracker draws a direct conceptual bridge between two previously separate lines of work: WAFT's warping-based optical flow (which replaces cost volumes with iterative feature warping) and CoTracker's joint tracking via cross-track self-attention. The paper observes that CoTracker's spatial self-attention across tracks is functionally equivalent to WAFT's global correspondence reasoning, and unifies them into a single spatiotemporal transformer head that refines dense tracks through iterative warping. This departs from the dominant TAP paradigm (PIPs, TAPIR, AllTracker) that relies on correlation volumes for matching, and instead inherits the iterative refinement recurrence from PIPs while substituting warping for explicit feature correlation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that eliminating cost volumes removes the quadratic memory bottleneck, enabling the tracker to operate at near-pixel-level resolution (stride 2) without multi-resolution pyramids, which directly improves tracking of thin structures and boundaries. The zero-shot transfer to optical flow—beating specialized flow models on Sintel without any flow training data—suggests that dense correspondence tasks may be more unified than the field currently assumes, potentially reducing the need for task-specific architectures. Open questions include whether the approach scales to very long sequences (beyond 600 frames), whether it extends naturally to 3D/depth-aware tracking, and whether the strong dependence on a powerful video backbone (VGGT) limits applicability when such models are unavailable.
