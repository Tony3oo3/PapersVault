## Problem

State-of-the-art diffusion models produce photorealistic imagery but fail on the long, multi-goal prompts (18–22 tightly coupled objectives) that professional designers actually issue, yet existing benchmarks test only 1–2 goals per prompt, masking this brittleness. The paper asks how to improve multi-goal instruction following without retraining the underlying diffusion backbones.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260403/cropped_page0_idx5.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a long-form natural-language instruction (averaging 18 goals for T2I, 11.2 for I2I) and, for editing tasks, a paired reference image. It outputs a final generated or edited image together with a full goal-level reasoning trace recording every extracted goal, edit directive, verification verdict, and rollback event. Internally, the planner (Qwen3-VL-8B) communicates with stateless diffusion editors (Qwen-Image / Qwen-Image-Edit) and a verifier (Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct) purely through natural-language messages, so any component can be swapped without retraining.

## Method

VisionDirector is a training-free, VLM-guided closed-loop controller that decomposes a long instruction into typed, conflict-flagged goals, then adaptively chooses between one-shot generation and staged micro-edits (batches of 1–2 goals ordered global-to-local). Each edit step runs micro-grid sampling—generating multiple candidates with different seeds and selecting the best via a lightweight VLM judge—followed by semantic verification; if the new edit degrades overall alignment, the system rolls back to the previous best image and reshuffles remaining goals. The loop terminates when all goals pass or a self-query confidence check triggers early stopping. To further reduce latency, the planner is post-trained with GRPO using token-level masking (so gradients apply only to the planner's textual actions, not tool outputs) and a 0–5 alignment reward from a separate VLM, cutting median editing rounds from 4.2 to 3.1 (~26% fewer diffusion calls) while improving GenEval and ImgEdit scores.

## Results

On GenEval (T2I), VisionDirector achieves an overall score of 0.94, surpassing Qwen-Image (0.87), GPT Image 1 (0.84), and Seedream 3.0 (0.84), with particularly strong gains in attribute binding (0.95) and position reasoning (0.88). On ImgEdit (I2I), VisionDirector scores 4.35/5 overall, outperforming Qwen-Image-Edit (4.27), GPT Image 1 (4.20), and FLUX.1 Kontext Pro (4.00) across all nine edit primitives. On the authors' own LGBench, VisionDirector lifts the weakest baseline (Flux-Dev) from 40.0% to 62.4% finish score and improves the strongest (Qwen-Image) from 71.8% to 74.8%, with the largest absolute gains on text goals (+7.5 to +28.7 points depending on backbone).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, VisionDirector (0.94) outperforms all 13 baselines, with the largest margins over older architectures: +0.41 over PixArt-α (0.48), +0.40 over Show-o (0.53), and +0.28 over SD3 Medium (0.62). Among the strongest recent systems, it leads Qwen-Image by +0.07, GPT Image 1 by +0.10, and Seedream 3.0 by +0.10, with the most notable sub-metric gains in attribute binding (+0.18 over Qwen-Image) and position reasoning (+0.12 over Qwen-Image). On ImgEdit, VisionDirector (4.35) beats all 13 editing baselines, including Qwen-Image-Edit (4.27, +0.08), GPT Image 1 (4.20, +0.15), and FLUX.1 Kontext Pro (4.00, +0.35); the gap widens substantially over open-source methods like OmniGen2 (3.44, +0.91) and MagicBrush (1.90, +2.45). On LGBench, VisionDirector provides the largest absolute improvement for weaker backbones—Flux-Dev gains +22.4 finish points (40.0→62.4) and Flux-Krea gains +8.1 (66.8→74.9)—while the already-strong Qwen-Image still improves by +3.0 (71.8→74.8). The GRPO-tuned planner further reduces median editing rounds from 4.2 to 3.1 (≈26% fewer diffusion calls) without sacrificing verification accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

VisionDirector extends the agentic image-creation line (GenArtist, JarvisArt) by replacing single-step or short-horizon editing with a closed-loop director that plans, verifies, and rolls back over 10–23 interdependent goals. The RL component adapts GRPO from DeepSeekMath to the visual-editing setting, introducing token-level masking for tool outputs and an alignment-VLM reward that scores pixel-level attribute satisfaction. On the benchmark side, LGBench generalizes prior evaluation suites (GenEval, ImgEdit, DrawBench, TIFA) from 1–2 goal prompts to long, multi-constraint design briefs with automated goal-level verification, filling a gap the authors argue has masked model brittleness in realistic production workflows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LGBench provides reproducible infrastructure for diagnosing exactly where diffusion and editing models break down under realistic multi-goal briefs, and the training-free VisionDirector loop can be bolted onto any existing backbone without retraining, making it immediately practical. The GRPO post-training phase shows that a lightweight RL stage on the planner alone can cut inference cost by ~26% while improving goal coverage, suggesting a general recipe for making VLM-guided generation more efficient. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, whether the VLM verifier's confidence threshold (0.81) is robust across domains, and whether the 26% efficiency gain holds as goal counts grow beyond the 23-goal ceiling tested here.
