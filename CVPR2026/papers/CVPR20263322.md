## Problem

Existing video codecs face a fundamental trade-off at low bitrates: traditional and distortion-optimized neural codecs produce visible blocking or over-smoothed artifacts, while diffusion-based video codecs that improve perceptual quality either operate at comparatively high bitrates or incur prohibitive multi-step sampling costs. No prior method combines single-step diffusion efficiency with the temporal coherence required for realistic video reconstruction at ultra-low bitrates.

## Input / Output

The codec consumes 1920×1080 video clips under a causal low-delay structure (one I-frame followed by P-frames), with the I-frame compressed by the OneDC image codec and P-frames processed through the conditional coding + single-step diffusion pipeline. The diffusion generator operates in the SD1.5 VAE latent space, and the final reconstruction is produced by a pretrained VAE decoder. Evaluation uses the first 96 frames of each sequence, with metrics computed on 256×256 overlapping patches for FID.

## Method

S²VC integrates a simplified conditional coding framework (from DCVC-RT, without explicit motion compression) with a single-step diffusion generator initialized from DMD2 SD1.5 and adapted via LoRA. Two complementary guidance mechanisms adapt the image diffusion model for video: Contextual Semantic Guidance (CSG) extracts frame-adaptive semantic features from the buffered pair {f̂_t, f̂_{t−1}} through a Semantic Adapter, replacing text embeddings in cross-attention layers, with DINOv3 distillation ensuring temporal stability; Temporal Consistency Guidance (TCG) inserts zero-initialized plug-in blocks at multiple U-Net encoder scales that fuse intermediate features from a diffusion buffer across frames, combined with cascade training that propagates gradients backward through the temporal chain. The overall training uses a perceptual RD loss (L1 + LPIPS distortion, semantic distillation, and RAFT-based motion loss) optimized end-to-end with AdamW.

## Results

S²VC achieves state-of-the-art perceptual quality at ultra-low bitrates (below 0.02 bpp), delivering an average 51.62% bitrate saving over the prior perceptual codec PLVC under the DISTS metric, with per-dataset savings of 57.69% (HEVC-B), 32.69% (UVG), and 64.49% (MCL-JCV). It also attains the best FID across all three evaluation datasets, confirming superior generation realism, and outperforms all compared codecs on the motion-aware FloLPIPS metric on HEVC-B and MCL-JCV. Under LPIPS, S²VC achieves comparable or superior results to PLVC while operating at substantially lower bitrates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against traditional codecs (HM, VTM, ECM), S²VC exhibits substantially lower DISTS and LPIPS at equivalent bitrates, eliminating the blocking and ringing artifacts that plague these methods under extreme compression. Compared to distortion-oriented neural codecs DCVC-FM and DCVC-RT, S²VC achieves markedly better perceptual metrics (DISTS, LPIPS) while also producing visually sharper reconstructions free from the over-smoothing typical of MSE/MS-SSIM-optimized codecs. The most direct comparison is with PLVC, the prior state-of-the-art perceptual video codec: S²VC saves 51.62% average bitrate on DISTS and matches or exceeds PLVC on LPIPS, while also avoiding the variegated and mottled artifacts that PLVC introduces in motion regions. On FloLPIPS (temporal consistency), S²VC outperforms all baselines on HEVC-B and MCL-JCV, which feature extensive motion, whereas PLVC exhibits noticeable jitter. The diffusion-based DiffVC is compared using its publicly reported numbers; S²VC operates at lower bitrates with single-step sampling versus DiffVC's multi-step process, reducing computational overhead. Overall, S²VC wins or ties on every reported metric across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

S²VC sits at the intersection of three research threads: conditional neural video coding (DCVC/DCVC-RT), single-step diffusion for image compression (OneDC, DMD2), and perceptual video codecs (PLVC, DiffVC). It departs from OneDC by replacing hyperprior-based semantic guidance with Contextual Semantic Guidance extracted from buffered decoded features, and by adding Temporal Consistency Guidance blocks in the U-Net to extend the image-level single-step generator to the video domain. The cascade training mechanism from the DCVC series is repurposed to propagate gradients across frames through the TCG blocks, creating a temporal optimization chain that has no direct analogue in prior diffusion-based codecs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 51.62% average bitrate saving over PLVC under DISTS demonstrates that single-step diffusion can deliver substantially better perceptual quality than GAN-based perceptual codecs at the same bitrate, while avoiding the prohibitive multi-step sampling cost that limited prior diffusion-based video codecs (DiffVC, I²VC). The CSG design—using buffered features plus DINOv3 distillation instead of captioning or fixed prompts—offers a practical, content-adaptive conditioning mechanism that could transfer to other generative compression settings. The main limitation acknowledged is the narrow operating bitrate range, and the comparison with the closest single-step diffusion video codec (DiffVC-OSD) is limited to reported numbers rather than a direct head-to-head, leaving open whether the advantage persists at higher bitrates or with stronger baselines.
