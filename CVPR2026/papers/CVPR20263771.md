## Problem

Descriptor-free 2D-3D matching methods rely exclusively on low-level geometric cues (bearing vectors and color), which limits their ability to resolve ambiguities in textureless regions, handle dynamic objects absent from the point cloud, and bridge the 2D-3D modality gap. This paper asks whether high-level semantic information can be incorporated into the descriptor-free paradigm to improve matching accuracy and robustness without incurring the heavy storage costs of descriptor-based approaches.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263771/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D query image keypoints (bearing vectors and RGB color) and a 3D point cloud (bearing vectors and RGB color) as geometric inputs, augmented with compact 16-dimensional semantic probability distributions for each point. The 3D semantics are not extracted directly from the sparse SfM point cloud but are obtained by backprojecting 2D open-vocabulary segmentation labels from the database images used in reconstruction, ensuring consistency with the query image's semantic space. The output is a set of 2D-3D correspondences refined by outlier rejection, which are then fed into PnP-RANSAC to estimate the 6-DoF camera pose.

## Method

SAG-GNN integrates semantic priors into a GNN-based descriptor-free matching pipeline through three mechanisms. First, a ProxyCLIP-based open-vocabulary segmentation model produces 16-category probability distributions for 2D keypoints, which are backprojected onto 3D points to form compact semantic priors. Second, a bidirectionally-aligned fusion block performs channel-wise cross-correlation with alternating query/key/value roles to generate two complementary intermediate features (geometry-informed-by-semantics and semantics-informed-by-geometry), then applies spatial attention to fuse them into balanced representations. Third, a semantic-guided interaction mechanism modulates cross-modal attention scores by a semantic similarity matrix Z = S^A(S^B)^T with a learnable smoothness parameter β, prioritizing semantically consistent correspondences during GNN message passing. The overall architecture follows A2-GNN's annular convolution and k-NN graph structure, with the fusion block inserted before and after each self-aggregation layer (4 integration steps total), and final matching is performed via Sinkhorn optimal transport followed by a lightweight outlier rejection network.

## Results

On MegaDepth with k=1 retrieval, SAG-GNN achieves 16.35/53.16/60.56% reprojection AUC at 1/5/10 px, outperforming the strongest baseline A2-GNN (12.72/41.84/48.02%) by ~12 percentage points at 5 px. With k=10 retrieval, the gap widens to 21.02/65.81/74.20% vs. 17.29/54.41/62.24%. Camera pose errors are reduced by approximately 50% relative to A2-GNN (e.g., median rotation error drops from 0.79° to 0.25° at k=1). On Cambridge Landmarks, SAG-GNN achieves 18.6 cm / 0.69° median pose error with only 164 MB storage, halving the error of A2-GNN (39.6 cm / 1.47°) while remaining an order of magnitude lighter than descriptor-based methods (SP+SG: 3215 MB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAG-GNN outperforms all four descriptor-free baselines (BPnPNet, GoMatch, DGC-GNN, A2-GNN) on both k=1 and k=10 MegaDepth matching, with the largest gains over A2-GNN (the strongest prior method): +11.3 pp at 5 px AUC and a 3.6× reduction in median rotation error at k=1. On Cambridge Landmarks, SAG-GNN (18.6 cm / 0.69°) more than halves the median pose error of A2-GNN (39.6 cm / 1.47°) and DGC-GNN (42.8 cm / 1.70°), and narrows the gap to the descriptor-based SP+SG (9.5 cm / 0.23°) while using 1/20th the storage (164 MB vs. 3215 MB). On 7Scenes, SAG-GNN (14.60 cm / 4.07°) marginally beats A2-GNN (14.81 cm / 4.23°) and DGC-GNN (16.50 cm / 4.50°), confirming consistent gains across indoor and outdoor scenes. Against scene-coordinate regression methods (MS-Trans, DSAC*, HSCNet), SAG-GNN achieves competitive or superior pose accuracy on Cambridge Landmarks without per-scene retraining. The runtime cost of the added semantic encoding (~327 ms) brings total inference to 712 ms, comparable to DGC-GNN (701 ms) and only ~2× A2-GNN (369 ms), while delivering substantially better matching quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAG-GNN extends the descriptor-free 2D-3D matching paradigm established by GoMatch (bearing-vector formulation) and A2-GNN (annular-convolution GNN with k-NN graphs) by injecting high-level semantic priors into a pipeline that previously relied solely on low-level geometric cues. The bidirectionally-aligned fusion block generalizes the unidirectional geometric-semantic fusion from the authors' prior 2D image-matching work (Zhang et al., AAAI 2025) to the cross-modal 2D-3D setting, where modality gap makes one-directional alignment insufficient. For semantic extraction, the paper repurposes open-vocabulary segmentation (ProxyCLIP) and backprojects 2D semantic maps onto SfM point clouds, sidestepping the need for 3D-specific semantic models that struggle with sparse reconstructions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that compact 16-dimensional semantic probability distributions can substantially improve descriptor-free matching (≈50% pose-error reduction over A2-GNN) while preserving the storage and generalization advantages of the descriptor-free paradigm, this work opens a practical path toward scalable visual localization without per-scene retraining or large descriptor databases. The demonstrated generalization across keypoint detectors (SIFT→SuperPoint) and semantic label sets suggests the approach is not tied to a specific front-end, which is important for real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the fixed 16-category set is sufficient for diverse environments, how the method scales to very large outdoor scenes with millions of points, and whether the additional ~350 ms semantic-encoding cost is acceptable in latency-sensitive applications like AR or robotics.
