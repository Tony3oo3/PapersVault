## Problem

Real-time video generation has shifted its bottleneck from denoising to decoding: 3D convolutional VAE decoders consume over 64% of inference time and require spatial-temporal tiling at high resolutions, making streaming impractical. Existing Transformer decoders face a fundamental tradeoff—causal variants need explicit masks that break FlashAttention at high resolution, while bidirectional variants cannot stream. No prior decoder simultaneously achieves frame-by-frame streaming, convolutional-matching quality, bounded memory, and tiling-free high-resolution decoding.

## Input / Output

The decoder takes a video latent tensor z ∈ R^{B×C'×T'×H'×W'} from a pretrained encoder (Wan2.1 at 4×8×8 or Wan2.2 at 4×16×16 compression) and produces pixel-space video frames at up to 1080p. Latent frames are consumed strictly one at a time in temporal order; each latent frame is expanded by a temporal factor of 4 (yielding 4 pixel frames per latent frame) and spatially upsampled by 16× (or 8× with PixelUnshuffle for 8×-compression latents). Because the KV cache window is fixed and RoPE positions are assigned relative to the current window, the decoder supports theoretically unbounded video length with constant per-frame latency and memory.

## Method

FlashDecoder is a pure-Transformer decoder that processes one latent frame per forward pass, maintaining a fixed-size rolling KV cache of W_frm=2 frames so that each frame attends bidirectionally within itself and causally to the immediately preceding frame. The key insight is that temporal causality is enforced by processing order rather than explicit attention masks, which eliminates the need for mask materialization and enables stable training at 1080p with standard FlashAttention—something that causes OOM failures in conventional causal-mask approaches. The architecture uses GQA, 3D-RoPE, RMSNorm, and KV-norm, followed by a temporal-first upsampling strategy: a linear channel expansion converts the temporal dimension (O(r_t²) cost) before two refinement Transformer blocks, then a 2-layer MLP with PixelShuffle handles spatial upsampling (avoiding the O(r_s⁴) attention blowup of spatial token expansion). Training and inference follow an identical streaming protocol, and the model is trained with L1, LPIPS, and 3D patch-based adversarial losses across three resolution stages (224→480p→1080p).

## Results

On the Wan2.2 latent space (4×16×16), FlashDecoder-XL matches the convolutional Wan2.2 decoder in reconstruction quality (41.55 vs. 41.49 dB PSNR at 1080p, 38.38 vs. 38.29 at 720p) while decoding 3.6×–4.7× faster (25.4 vs. 7.1 FPS at 1080p) with up to 11× less peak memory (3.7 GB vs. 41.0 GB). With architecture-aware inference optimizations (torch.compile, CUDA graphs, FP8), FlashDecoder-XL-Opt widens the throughput gap to 12× (43.0 vs. 7.1 FPS at 480p) while using under 2 GB memory. On the Wan2.1 latent space (4×8×8), FlashDecoder-XL achieves 40.74 vs. 40.36 dB PSNR at 1080p with 3.7× higher throughput and 10× lower memory than the Wan2.1 decoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary convolutional baseline Wan2.2, FlashDecoder-XL matches PSNR (38.38 vs. 38.29 at 720p; 41.55 vs. 41.49 at 1080p) and LPIPS while delivering 4.7× higher throughput (76.3 vs. 16.1 FPS at 720p) and 8× lower peak memory (2.4 vs. 19.3 GB). Against the lightweight TAEHV decoder, FlashDecoder-XL achieves substantially better quality (38.38 vs. 30.68 PSNR at 720p) at the cost of lower raw FPS, though the optimized variant (151.0 FPS) still trails TAEHV's 1177.6 FPS by design. Among Transformer-based decoders, FlashDecoder-XL outperforms AToken in both PSNR (38.38 vs. 36.72) and rFVD (12.75 vs. 22.91) at 720p while streaming at 2.7× higher throughput, and dominates MAGI-1 (40.74 vs. 37.02 PSNR at 1080p in the 4×8×8 group) by 13× in throughput. In the 4×8×8 group, FlashDecoder-XL slightly trails HunyuanVideo in PSNR (40.74 vs. 41.93 at 1080p) but runs 12× faster (25.9 vs. 2.1 FPS) with 10× less memory. OmniTokenizer, the only other streaming Transformer decoder, is far behind in quality (17.25 PSNR at 480p) and is limited to 256px resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

FlashDecoder carves new ground at the intersection of latent diffusion decoders and streaming Transformer inference. Prior video decoders are overwhelmingly 3D convolutional (Wan2.1/2.2, HunyuanVideo), while existing Transformer-based decoders (OmniTokenizer, AToken, MAGI-1) face a fundamental tradeoff: causal variants require explicit attention masks that prevent efficient FlashAttention at high resolutions, and bidirectional variants cannot stream. The key conceptual departure is enforcing temporal causality through sequential frame processing with a fixed-size rolling KV cache rather than through explicit causal masks, a technique borrowed from LLM inference but not previously applied to video VAE decoding. The temporal-first upsampling strategy (channel expansion + PixelShuffle) repurposes standard LLM-style architecture choices to avoid the O(r_s^4) attention cost of spatial upsampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

With latent generation nearing real-time speed, the decoder has become the dominant bottleneck (64.6% of inference time for Wan2.2 at 720p), and FlashDecoder directly addresses this by delivering 3.6×–12× faster streaming with matching reconstruction quality and up to 11× lower memory on a single H100. The finding that mask-free causal processing enables stable training up to 1080p—where mask-based approaches OOM—removes a practical barrier that has limited prior Transformer decoders to lower resolutions. Open questions include whether pairing FlashDecoder with a streaming Transformer encoder and training the full VAE from scratch can unlock latent spaces better suited to end-to-end Transformer pipelines, and whether the modest rFVD gap versus heavily-trained convolutional decoders closes with more compute and data.
