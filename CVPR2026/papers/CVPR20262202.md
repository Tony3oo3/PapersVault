## Problem

Dual-encoder 3D video MLLMs (e.g., VG LLM) produce long multi-view visual token sequences whose quadratic attention cost dominates inference, yet existing 2D token compression methods that rely solely on semantic similarity discard spatially critical tokens and degrade 3D grounding. No prior method jointly exploits the complementary strengths of 2D semantic saliency and 3D geometric proximity for token selection and merging in this dual-encoder setting.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262202/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of m RGB frames (no explicit 3D inputs such as point clouds, depth maps, or BEV maps) and a text prompt. Each frame is patchified by a 2D encoder and processed by a 3D geometry encoder (VGGT) over the full sequence; after element-wise fusion the visual token grid has n = m·h·w tokens. After SemGeo Merger compression, the shortened visual sequence is concatenated with the tokenized text query and decoded by a frozen Qwen2.5-VL backbone to produce 3D bounding boxes, dense scene captions, or spatial-reasoning answers depending on the task.

## Method

Merge3D inserts a Semantic-Geometric Token Merger (SemGeo Merger) between the 2D-3D fusion stage and the LLM decoder, keeping all encoder and decoder parameters frozen. Dominant tokens are selected via 2D encoder attention scores (top-K by summed attention), capturing semantically salient, query-relevant regions. The remaining contextual tokens are then merged using a multiplicative hybrid similarity s_fuse(k,c) = exp(v_k^T v_c / τ_sem) · exp(g_k^T g_c / τ_geo), where v and g are the 2D and 3D feature vectors respectively; each contextual token is assigned to the dominant token with the highest fused similarity and averaged into it. This multiplicative design ensures that only tokens simultaneously semantically relevant and geometrically proximate are merged, preventing cross-object or cross-viewpoint confusion that harms 3D grounding. Training fine-tunes only the SemGeo Merger with a next-token prediction objective on ScanRefer, Scan2Cap, SPAR-7M, and LLaVA-Video178K, converging in roughly one quarter epoch on 8×H100 GPUs, and achieves up to 70% visual token reduction with ~3× end-to-end speedup while retaining strong performance across grounding, captioning, and spatial reasoning benchmarks.

## Results

On Scan2Cap, Merge3D at 30% token retention achieves CIDEr 73.4 (vs. 78.6 for the full VG LLM-4B baseline), preserving ~93.4% of baseline CIDEr while delivering a 2.5× inference speedup; at 5% retention it still reaches CIDEr 57.9 with a 3.1× speedup. On CV-Bench, 30% retention preserves 97.0% of the baseline average accuracy (79.6% vs. 82.1%), and on BLINK it retains 98.7% (67.5% vs. 68.4%). Across all three benchmarks, Merge3D consistently outperforms semantic-only (Visionzip-2D), geometry-only (Visionzip-3D), and random (Randomzip) merging baselines at every retention ratio, with the largest margins under extreme 5% compression.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Scan2Cap, the VG LLM-4B baseline (CIDEr 78.6) is already competitive with 3D-input methods like Video-3D LLM (80.0) and LLaVA-3D (79.2), and Merge3D at 30% retention (73.4) remains close to Chat-Scene (77.1) and LEO (72.4) despite using 70% fewer tokens. Among the four merging strategies, Merge3D wins on CIDEr at every retention ratio: at 5% it scores 57.9 vs. Visionzip-3D (52.4, +5.5), Visionzip-2D (49.5, +8.4), and Randomzip (51.2, +6.7); at 30% it leads by +1.2 over Visionzip-3D and +1.5 over Visionzip-2D. On CV-Bench, Merge3D at 5% retention (74.8% avg) outperforms Visionzip-2D (68.9%), Visionzip-3D (63.6%), and Randomzip (65.1%) by 5.9–11.2 points, with the gap driven primarily by the 3D subset where geometric coherence matters most. On BLINK, Merge3D at 30% retention (67.5% avg) beats Visionzip-2D (65.9%) and Visionzip-3D (62.2%), and at 10% it reaches 64.1% vs. 62.9% for Visionzip-2D and 56.6% for Visionzip-3D. The Multi-View subset of BLINK remains a challenge for all methods (scores cluster around 55–57%), suggesting that large viewpoint changes require mechanisms beyond token merging alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Merge3D extends the dual-encoder 3D video MLLM paradigm introduced by VG LLM [82], which fuses 2D semantic features (from a CLIP-style encoder) with 3D geometry features (from VGGT) via element-wise addition. For token compression, it adapts VisionZip's [70] dominant/contextual token merging formulation but departs from the purely semantic similarity used in 2D settings by introducing a multiplicative hybrid of 2D semantic and 3D geometric similarity for contextual token assignment. This positions the work at the intersection of two active threads: geometry-aware 3D MLLMs (Spatial-MLLM, VG LLM) and efficient token compression (VisionZip, SparseVLM, Dynamic-VLM), which had previously been treated independently.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment: removing 70% of visual tokens while retaining ~93% of CIDEr and ~97% of spatial-reasoning accuracy, with a 2.5–3× inference speedup, makes dual-encoder 3D MLLMs viable on resource-constrained hardware (e.g., edge robotics). The clean ablation showing that 2D similarity suppresses class confusion while 3D similarity stabilizes spatial localization validates the complementary-modality hypothesis and provides a design principle for future hybrid-encoder compression. However, the method is evaluated only on the VG LLM backbone, the Multi-View subset of BLINK remains stubbornly flat across all methods (~55–57%), and the sub-quadratic speedup suggests diminishing returns at extreme compression. Open questions include whether adaptive, task- or view-aware retention policies can close the remaining gap, and whether the approach generalizes to other dual-encoder architectures or longer video sequences.
