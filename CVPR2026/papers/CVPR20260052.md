## Problem

Open-world object detectors can flag unknown objects but leave them as anonymous placeholders requiring manual annotation, while open-vocabulary detectors need explicit text prompts at test time and cannot discover truly unanticipated categories. In remote sensing, where unknown categories arise routinely during disaster response and infrastructure monitoring, no existing method autonomously generates semantic labels for discovered unknowns or supports continual vocabulary expansion without external language models or human intervention.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260052/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB remote sensing image (or natural image for COCO) and extracts multi-scale features via a frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 encoder. It outputs bounding boxes with known class labels, bounding boxes flagged as unknown (when max attention weight falls below τ_unk=0.4), and for each unknown, a synthesized text embedding in CLIP's continuous embedding space that serves as its semantic label. New categories are registered in the hierarchical semantic graph only after a buffer-and-cluster validation (M=5 embeddings with pairwise cosine similarity >0.7), and human-readable labels are recovered via a post-hoc nearest-neighbor search under the parent node.

## Method

HSGDet builds on Deformable DETR and introduces two modules operating over a hierarchical semantic graph derived from WordNet IS-A taxonomies. Deformable Hierarchical Graph Attention (DHGA) performs scene-conditioned coarse-to-fine classification by maintaining a learnable Scene Context Token that aggregates spatial co-occurrence patterns across all queries via cross-attention at each decoder layer; context-enriched queries then navigate the graph through Top-K node sampling and attention-weighted fusion of CLIP text embeddings, with layer-wise refinement shifting focus from broad parent nodes to specific child nodes. Context-Aware Region-to-Text (CR2T) synthesizes a text embedding for each flagged unknown by fusing the refined visual query, the scene context token, and the CLIP embedding of the nearest hierarchical parent through a 3-layer MLP, producing an embedding directly in CLIP text space. Training uses a multi-task loss combining bounding-box regression (L1+GIoU), a hierarchical navigation loss that supervises attention on all ancestral path nodes, and a CR2T loss with cosine alignment plus contrastive sibling separation, where 30% of known classes are randomly masked as pseudo-unknowns to teach the embedding synthesis mechanism.

## Results

On DOTA-v2 Task 1, HSGDet achieves 54.8 K-mAP and 41.2% Unknown Recall with 5.8 Wilderness Impact, surpassing the strongest baseline SkySense-O (50.2 K-mAP, 31.5% U-R, 8.1 WI) by +4.6 and +9.7 points respectively. On FAIR1M, HSGDet reaches 52.4 K-mAP and 38.5% U-R, yielding the headline +6.6 K-mAP and +9.9 U-R gains over the best prior method. On COCO Task 1, HSGDet attains 81.3 K-mAP and 78.8% U-R, and maintains stable K-mAP (80.9–82.4) across all four incremental tasks, whereas OW-OVD degrades by −13.8 points. Wilderness Impact is reduced by 36% relative to the best baseline on DOTA-v2 Task 1 (5.8 vs. 8.1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HSGDet outperforms all 13 compared baselines across every dataset and every incremental task. On DOTA-v2, it leads the strongest remote-sensing baseline SkySense-O [47] by +4.6 K-mAP and +9.7 U-R on Task 1, and the gap widens to +22.0 K-mAP and +10.7 U-R by Task 4, where SkySense-O degrades by −15.9 K-mAP while HSGDet steadily improves. On FAIR1M, HSGDet gains +6.6 K-mAP and +9.9 U-R over SkySense-O, and on DIOR it leads by +6.4 K-mAP and +9.2 U-R. On COCO, HSGDet surpasses the unified OW-OVD [42] by +2.7 K-mAP and +2.6 U-R on Task 1, and by +16.4 K-mAP on Task 4 (81.2 vs. 64.8), where OW-OVD suffers a severe −13.8-point drop. Among earlier OWOD methods, OrthogonalDet [38] (CVPR-24) is the closest competitor on DOTA-v2 Task 1 (47.8 K-mAP, 28.6 U-R), yet HSGDet still leads by +7.0 K-mAP and +12.6 U-R. The consistent WI reduction (5.8 vs. 8.1 on DOTA-v2 T1; 6.2 vs. 9.7 on FAIR1M) indicates that HSGDet's hierarchical navigation and scene-context conditioning yield materially better known-unknown discrimination than energy-based or orthogonality-based baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

HSGDet extends the Deformable DETR detection backbone and the Deformable Graph Attention mechanism (Park et al., TPAMI 2025) by introducing hierarchical, scene-conditioned navigation over a WordNet-derived semantic taxonomy. It departs from the OWOD line (ORE, OW-DETR, OrthogonalDet, OW-OVD) that flags unknowns but leaves them semantically ungrounded, and from unified OW-OVD systems that rely on external LLMs or foundation-model pseudo-labels for naming. The key conceptual departure is treating semantic label generation as a learned, end-to-end capability (via CR2T's multi-source fusion into CLIP text space) rather than a post-hoc retrieval or external-model query step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the manual-annotation bottleneck for vocabulary expansion, HSGDet addresses a practical scalability barrier in autonomous aerial monitoring (disaster response, infrastructure inspection) where new object categories appear routinely. The buffer-and-cluster validation (M=5, τ_sim=0.7) and hierarchical parent inheritance provide a principled mechanism for vocabulary growth, though the sensitivity of these hyperparameters to domain shift remains untested. Open questions include how well the WordNet-derived hierarchy covers fine-grained RS categories absent from general taxonomies, whether the 0.79 CLIP-space alignment degrades for objects far from any known parent, and whether the approach transfers to non-aerial domains without re-deriving the graph structure.
