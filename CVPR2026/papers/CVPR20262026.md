## Problem

Vision Transformers and large vision-language models frequently exploit spurious background correlations rather than foreground object features, yet existing pruning and efficiency methods (token pruning, block pruning) do not identify which specific attention heads route these background shortcuts. Without causal, head-level attribution, it is impossible to verify that a model's prediction is grounded in the actual object, leaving robustness and interpretability unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen VLM backbone (InternVL2-26B, LLaVA-1.6, or LLaVA-1.5-13B) and a curated set of 40% true-positive RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, or axial T1 MRI slices). It produces per-head CIGMA scores, a ranked spurious subnetwork of K=16 attention heads to ablate, self-discovered 32×32 foreground masks, and the pruned model's class predictions with a Background Influence Ratio (BIR) in [0,1] quantifying residual background reliance.

## Method

CIGMA is a training-free, three-stage pipeline. First, a low-resolution (32×32) soft mask is optimized per image via gradient descent to minimize a composite objective of Jensen-Shannon divergence between original and masked predictive distributions, an ℓ₁ sparsity term, and a total-variation smoothness term, yielding a binarized foreground region at the 65th percentile. Second, two counterfactual images are constructed by replacing foreground or background with per-image mean color; the JS divergence between the original and each counterfactual's temperature-scaled softmax distribution yields Foreground Information Gain (FIG) and Background Information Gain (BIG). Third, each of the L×H attention heads is surgically ablated by zeroing its Q, K, V, and O projection rows/columns, and the CIGMA score is computed as the mean reduction in BIG across all true-positive images; the top-K heads with the highest CIGMA scores constitute the spurious subnetwork that is permanently removed, producing a smaller, foreground-focused model without any weight retraining.

## Results

In the zero-shot (training-free) regime, CIGMA improves top-1 accuracy by 7.6–24.8 percentage points over unmodified models (average +18.6 pp) across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet on three VLM backbones (InternVL2-26B, LLaVA-1.6, LLaVA-1.5-13B), while reducing the Background Influence Ratio (BIR) by 79.5–88.1% (e.g., BIR drops from 0.35 to 0.068 on CIFAR-10 with InternVL2-26B). Calibration improves dramatically: NLL falls from 0.214 to 0.013 and ECE from 0.027 to 0.003 on CIFAR-10 (InternVL2-26B), representing 5–20× better calibration than all baselines. In the fine-tuned regime, CIGMA (ft) further gains 4.7–22.9 pp over the fine-tuned Original and achieves 41.7–86.0% BIR reduction, with the strongest result being 99.8% accuracy and 0.055 BIR on CIFAR-10 (InternVL2-26B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CIGMA outperforms all eight baselines on every metric (Acc, NLL, ECE, BIR) across all nine model–dataset combinations in both zero-shot and fine-tuned regimes. Among zero-shot baselines, MDP is the strongest, achieving only marginal gains over Original (e.g., 92.3% vs. 92.0% Acc on CIFAR-10/InternVL2-26B) while CIGMA reaches 99.6%, a 7.3 pp advantage over MDP; the other four token-pruning baselines (TopV, ATP-LLaVA, DivPrune, EfficientLLaVA) either match or slightly degrade accuracy and consistently increase BIR relative to Original. In the fine-tuned regime, CHG (Causal Head Gating) is the strongest learning-based baseline (e.g., 94.4% Acc, 0.30 BIR on CIFAR-10/InternVL2-26B), yet CIGMA (ft) surpasses it by 5.4 pp in accuracy and reduces BIR from 0.30 to 0.055 (an 81.7% reduction). Notably, CoBalT and RAVL underperform the fine-tuned Original baseline on both accuracy and BIR, indicating that their spurious-correlation mitigation strategies can inadvertently amplify background reliance, whereas CIGMA's causal head-level intervention consistently reduces it.

## Relation to Prior Work

CIGMA sits at the intersection of three prior threads: (1) token/structure pruning for efficiency (DivPrune, EfficientLLaVA, MDP, TopV, ATP-LLaVA), which reduce compute but treat the model as a monolith and never identify which specific attention heads route spurious background cues; (2) perturbation-based explanation methods (meaningful perturbations, extremal masks) that isolate minimal evidence regions but stop at visualization without causal head-level attribution; and (3) information-theoretic views of representation (variational information bottleneck) and the observation that many attention heads are redundant (Voita et al.). CIGMA repurposes the minimal-mask idea from the perturbation literature as a self-supervised foreground discovery step, then applies Jensen-Shannon divergence as an information-gain metric under two complementary ablations (remove foreground / remove background), and finally performs surgical causal interventions on individual heads to rank them by background contribution. This departs from prior spurious-correlation mitigation (CoBalT, RAVL) by being entirely training-free and operating at the head level rather than through retraining or concept balancing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, head-level causal attribution framework offers a practical path to reducing spurious background reliance without retraining, which is valuable for deployment on resource-constrained devices where fine-tuning is infeasible. The BIR metric provides a simple, interpretable diagnostic for background dependence that complements accuracy and calibration. However, the extremely large reported gains (up to ~25 pp accuracy, ~88% BIR reduction) and the fact that all baselines—including published SOTA spurious-correlation methods—underperform the unmodified model raise questions about the fairness of the comparison protocol and whether the evaluation setup (using large VLMs on small classification datasets) is the most natural testbed. Open questions include whether the CIGMA scoring procedure (which requires ablating every head across a validation set) scales to models with thousands of heads, whether the identified spurious heads are stable across datasets, and whether the approach transfers to generative or multi-modal reasoning tasks beyond classification.
