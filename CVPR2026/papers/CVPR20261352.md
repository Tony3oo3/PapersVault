## Problem

Existing training-free high-resolution image generation methods (DemoFusion, FreeScale, FouriScale) rely on full multi-step reverse diffusion (50+ steps) to synthesize high-frequency details, requiring 5+ minutes for a single 4K image and over an hour for 8K. This makes practical, near-interactive high-resolution synthesis infeasible, as the full denoising trajectory is largely redundant for a refinement task where global structure already exists in the coarse latent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261352/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and produces high-resolution RGB images at target resolutions up to 8K (4096×4096 or beyond) on a single A100-40GB GPU. The pipeline operates in two stages: a base diffusion model (e.g., SDXL) generates a latent at its native resolution, which is then progressively upscaled via cascade steps (each doubling spatial dimensions) using a few-step distilled model (e.g., SDXL-Turbo) for refinement. A 4K image is generated in approximately 20 seconds, and an 8K image in under 100 seconds.

## Method

PixelRush's core insight is that the reverse diffusion process reconstructs images hierarchically in frequency—low-frequency structure early, high-frequency details late—so for refinement, only the late-stage detail synthesis is needed. The method introduces partial inversion, which maps the coarse latent to an intermediate noise level K (e.g., K=249) rather than full Gaussian noise (t=999), combined with a one-step few-step diffusion model (SDXL-Turbo) to synthesize high-frequency details in a single denoising step. To handle the artifacts this aggressive acceleration introduces, PixelRush proposes a Gaussian filter blending technique that convolves binary patch overlap masks with a Gaussian kernel to produce smooth, continuous weighting for seamless patch stitching, and a noise injection mechanism that slerps the predicted noise with random noise (λ=0.95) in latent space to counteract the oversmoothing inherent in few-step generation. Together, these components achieve a 10×–35× speedup over prior methods while improving FID from 52.87 to 50.13 at 2K resolution.

## Results

At 2048×2048, PixelRush achieves the best FID of 50.13 (vs. 52.87 for FreeScale, the previous best), IS of 14.32, and FIDc of 29.13, while generating each image in just 4 seconds — a 13×–22× speedup over competing tuning-free methods (53–87 s). At 4096×4096, PixelRush attains FID 54.67 and IS 13.75 in 20 seconds, a 16×–34× speedup over baselines that require 323–680 s per image. In a blind user study with 30 participants, PixelRush receives an 82.8%–86.5% preference rate across text alignment, image quality, and visual structure, compared to <10% for the strongest baseline (FreeScale).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PixelRush outperforms all four baselines on every metric at both 2K and 4K resolutions. Against the strongest prior method, FreeScale [23], PixelRush improves FID from 52.87→50.13 (2K) and 58.28→54.67 (4K), while being 13× faster at 2K (4 s vs. 53 s) and 16× faster at 4K (20 s vs. 323 s). DemoFusion [7] suffers from object repetition (e.g., duplicated dragon heads) and is 19× slower at 2K; FouriScale [13] introduces grid-like texture artifacts and is the slowest baseline at 4K (680 s). Naive direct inference (SDXL-DI [22]) degrades severely at 4K (FID 153.53) due to the model's inability to generalize beyond its native 1024×1024 training resolution. The user study confirms these quantitative gains: PixelRush is preferred 82.8%–86.5% of the time versus <10% for FreeScale, the next-best method.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixelRush extends the patch-based, cascade-upsampling paradigm established by MultiDiffusion and DemoFusion, and builds directly on DDIM inversion as its structural-preservation mechanism. The key departure is from all prior training-free high-resolution methods (DemoFusion, FreeScale, FouriScale, ScaleCrafter), which require a full 50-step reverse diffusion in the refinement stage; PixelRush truncates this to a partial inversion at an intermediate timestep and pairs it with a few-step distilled model (SDXL-Turbo), replacing naive average blending with a Gaussian-filter blending scheme and adding a slerp-based noise injection to counteract few-step oversmoothing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10×–35× speedup (4 s for 2K, 20 s for 4K on a single A100) makes high-resolution text-to-image generation near-interactive for the first time, removing the multi-minute latency that has kept prior methods impractical for real-time or iterative workflows. The plug-and-play design (compatible with any few-step distilled backbone) suggests broad applicability beyond SDXL. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video generation or 3D synthesis, how it behaves at resolutions beyond 8K, and whether the fixed λ=0.95 noise-injection coefficient can be made adaptive per content.
