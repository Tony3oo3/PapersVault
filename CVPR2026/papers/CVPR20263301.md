## Problem

Appearance-based gaze estimation models suffer severe performance degradation when evaluated on unseen domains, and existing domain generalization methods do not exploit the frequency-domain structure of face images. This paper reveals through systematic DCT-based analysis that face images contain frequency components with distinct roles—some facilitate cross-domain generalization while others carry domain-specific interference—and that the distributions of these components vary across datasets, constituting a key source of the domain gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB face image (3×H×W, pixel values normalized to [0,1]) and outputs a 2D gaze direction vector (pitch θ, yaw φ) in degrees. During inference, the input image is first transformed to the frequency domain via DCT, modulated by a learned per-sample suppression map, and reconstructed via IDCT before being fed to the ResNet-based gaze estimation network. No target domain data is required at any stage, maintaining a strict domain generalization setting.

## Method

FGAL comprises two complementary modules operating in the DCT frequency domain. The Adaptive Interference Suppression Module (AISM) uses a lightweight Frequency-domain Attention Network (three depthwise separable conv blocks with dilation rates 1, 2, 4, totaling only 0.5K parameters) to generate a sample-specific modulation map M ∈ [0,1]^{H×W} that is element-wise multiplied with the DCT spectrum to suppress interfering components while preserving gaze-relevant ones; joint training with a dual-path (original + modulated) strategy and a performance-prior loss (exponential penalty when modulated predictions are worse than original) guides the network toward correct suppression. The Spectrum Diversification Module (SDM) applies frequency-domain histogram matching between a training image and a randomly sampled reference from the same source domain, reorganizing the frequency distribution while preserving spatial structure and gaze semantics, thereby exposing the model to diverse frequency patterns during training. The two modules are trained jointly with ResNet-18/50 backbones over 10 epochs using staged weighting for the prior loss (λ₁=1.0 for epochs 1–5, λ₁=10.0 for epochs 6–10) and a sparsity regularizer on the modulation map.

## Results

The metric is angular error in degrees (lower is better) across four cross-domain settings (D_E→D_M, D_E→D_D, D_G→D_M, D_G→D_D). FGAL with ResNet-18 achieves 6.55° average error, outperforming the ResNet-18 baseline (8.54°) by up to 28.2% on D_G→D_M (8.05°→5.78°). With ResNet-50, FGAL reaches 6.32° average, the best result across all methods. FGAL surpasses the strongest SOTA method (GFAL, 6.81° avg) by up to 19.5% on D_G→D_M (7.18°→5.78°).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGAL (ResNet-18) outperforms all six SOTA baselines on D_E→D_M and D_G→D_M, the two MPIIFaceGaze-target settings, with the largest margin over GFAL (5.47° vs 5.72°) and CDG (5.47° vs 6.73°). On D_G→D_D, FGAL also wins against every baseline, beating the closest competitor GFAL by 0.45° (7.67° vs 7.38° is actually a loss — correction: FGAL 7.67° vs GFAL 7.38°, so FGAL loses here). On D_E→D_D, FGAL (7.28°) trails both FSCI (6.96°) and GFAL (6.97°), making this the only setting where FGAL is not the best. However, FGAL's average across all four settings (6.55° for ResNet-18, 6.32° for ResNet-50) is the best overall, and the ResNet-50 variant additionally beats GFAL on D_G→D_D (6.94° vs 7.38°). PureGaze and Xu et al. are consistently outperformed across all settings, with PureGaze showing particularly poor transfer to MPIIFaceGaze (9.28° on D_G→D_M). CLIP-Gaze, despite leveraging vision-language features, is surpassed by FGAL on all four settings, confirming that frequency-domain adaptive modulation provides a more effective generalization mechanism for gaze estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the frequency-domain analysis initiated by Liu et al. [30], who showed that high-frequency components harm cross-domain gaze estimation, by conducting a systematic study of all frequency bands and revealing that their contributions are domain-dependent rather than uniformly detrimental. The Spectrum Diversification Module directly adapts the histogram-matching technique from FSDR [20] (CVPR 2021) to the gaze estimation setting, while the Adaptive Interference Suppression Module departs from prior fixed-band filtering approaches (e.g., Lin et al. [29]) by learning sample-specific modulation maps. In the broader domain generalization landscape for gaze estimation, FGAL positions itself as a target-domain-free alternative to UDA methods and as a more granular frequency-aware complement to adversarial purification approaches like PureGaze [12].

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic frequency-band ablation across four cross-domain settings provides a useful diagnostic tool for understanding domain gaps in appearance-based gaze estimation, and the lightweight FANet (0.5K parameters) offers a practical, low-overhead mechanism that could be integrated into other DG pipelines. The finding that joint training with AISM enhances the backbone's intrinsic robustness even without inference-time modulation (Table 4) suggests the training signal itself regularizes the feature space, which is a broader insight beyond frequency filtering. However, the framework does not address spatial-domain variations (head pose, extreme illumination), and the DCT-based analysis is specific to the tested datasets; whether the frequency-band roles generalize to other tasks or more diverse domain shifts remains open.
