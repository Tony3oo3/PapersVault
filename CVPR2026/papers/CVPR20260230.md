## Problem

Cooperative perception systems are highly vulnerable to diverse real-world corruptions—environmental (fog, rain, EMI) and sensor-level (motion blur, beam missing, echo)—that degrade or eliminate shared data. Existing robustness methods target specific corruption types and degrade sharply on unseen or compound degradations, leaving no unified, corruption-agnostic mechanism for multi-agent feature denoising.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260230/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes voxelized LiDAR point cloud features from an ego agent and N−1 cooperative agents (2 cooperating vehicles in experiments), with intermediate features compressed by 16×. It produces 3D bounding boxes with class labels via a standard detection head. Notably, the teacher model operates exclusively during training to generate clean supervision targets, while inference uses deterministic DDIM sampling with a tunable step count (2–20) to trade off accuracy against throughput (ranging from ~10 FPS at 2 steps to ~3 FPS at 20 steps).

## Method

CoopDiff employs a teacher-student paradigm that reframes intermediate fusion as a generative denoising task in feature space. The Quality-Aware Teacher performs voxel-level early fusion weighted by per-voxel Quality-of-Interest scores and semantic priors, then applies a DDPM forward process and a Gated Conditional Modulation (GCM, FiLM-like) denoiser to produce a clean target feature map. The Dual-Branch Student disentangles ego and cooperative streams: the local branch conditions on ego features plus timestep embedding, while the cooperative branch uses Cooperative Deformable Attention (CDA) with confidence-based decoupling to adaptively sample informative tokens from other agents. An Ego-Guided Cross-Attention (EGCA) decoder fuses both branches by querying from the local stream and attending to concatenated keys/values from both. Training combines a heteroscedastic diffusion noise-prediction loss, KL-divergence logit distillation from the teacher, a BCE cooperative-region supervision loss, and standard detection loss, all balanced by hyperparameters.

## Results

On OPV2Vn, CoopDiff achieves 0.9053/0.8357 AP@0.5/AP@0.7 on clean data, surpassing the best baseline DSRC (0.8941/0.8035) by 1.12%/3.22%. Across all six corruption types, CoopDiff outperforms the average of all baselines by 8.40%/13.16% (AP@0.5/AP@0.7) on OPV2Vn and 10.24%/10.13% on DAIR-V2Xn. The mean Relative Corruption Error (mRCE) is 12.94% on OPV2Vn and 26.79% on DAIR-V2Xn, the lowest among all compared methods. With only 2 DDIM steps at inference, the model reaches 9.45 FPS on OPV2V while already exceeding prior SOTA accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoopDiff outperforms all nine baselines across every corruption type on both OPV2Vn and DAIR-V2Xn. The closest competitor is DSRC [45], which leads most baselines on clean data (0.8941 AP@0.5 on OPV2Vn) but trails CoopDiff by 1.12%/3.22% on clean and by 1.5–5.5% under each corruption. Coalign [21] is the strongest baseline on DAIR-V2Xn (0.7773 clean AP@0.5) yet still falls 2.96%/3.60% behind CoopDiff on clean and 2–4% under corruptions. MDD [10], designed for LiDAR–4D-radar fusion, degrades severely when restricted to LiDAR-only inputs (0.7792 clean, dropping to 0.5645 under EMI), confirming its reliance on the radar modality. ERMVP [44] and MRCNet [7], which target specific interference types, show the largest drops under unseen corruptions (e.g., ERMVP falls to 0.6202 under EMI on OPV2Vn). The remaining baselines—F-Cooper [2], V2VNet [33], V2X-ViT [37], and Where2Comm [8]—are consistently 3–10% below CoopDiff under corrupted conditions, with V2VNet [33] suffering the most on DAIR-V2Xn (0.3132 AP@0.7 clean, dropping to 0.1408 under EMI).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes cooperative perception under corruption as a generative denoising problem in feature space, drawing on the DDPM/DDIM diffusion paradigm (Ho et al., 2020) and FiLM-style conditioning. It departs from prior robustness-oriented methods (ERMVP, MDD, V2X-DGW, DSRC) that each target a specific corruption type by proposing a corruption-agnostic teacher-student framework where a quality-aware early-fusion teacher produces clean supervision and a dual-branch student reconstructs it via guided diffusion. The architectural components—Gated Conditional Modulation, Cooperative Deformable Attention, and Ego-Guided Cross-Attention—are assembled from existing attention and conditioning primitives but are novel in their joint application to multi-agent feature denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of OPV2Vn and DAIR-V2Xn, each with six corruption types spanning environmental and sensor-level distortions, provides the field with a systematic multi-degradation evaluation protocol that prior single-corruption benchmarks lacked. The tunable diffusion-step tradeoff (2 steps already surpass prior SOTA at ~9.5 FPS) makes the approach practically deployable. The authors acknowledge that diffusion-based reconstruction may generate geometries not perfectly aligned with ground truth, and the framework is currently validated only on LiDAR point clouds, leaving open whether the denoising paradigm transfers to camera or multi-modal cooperative settings.
