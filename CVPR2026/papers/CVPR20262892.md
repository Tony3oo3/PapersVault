## Problem

Diffusion-based novel view synthesis relies on stochastic noise-to-data transitions that obscure deterministic geometric structure, producing inconsistent predictions across viewpoints. Existing flow matching formulations for paired-view generation use simple linear interpolants that fail to capture the nonlinear curvature of the data manifold in latent space, leading to suboptimal and geometrically incoherent transitions between camera poses.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262892/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single source RGB image (256×256) and a target camera pose (intrinsics K, extrinsics R,T) as input, and outputs a rendered novel view at the target pose. The source image is consumed through two parallel encoders: a VAE for spatial latents and CLIP for semantic conditioning, while the target pose is converted to per-pixel Plücker ray coordinates and injected via Ray Conditional Normalization. The source view serves as the canonical reference frame, with all target rays expressed relative to it.

## Method

PDG-FM replaces the standard noise-to-data flow matching paradigm with a Data-to-Data formulation that learns deterministic velocity fields between paired view latents (x₀, x₁) using a U-Net conditioned on Plücker rays, CLIP features, and source VAE latents. The key geometric innovation is a probability-density geodesic interpolant x_t = (1−t)x₀ + tx₁ + φ_η(x₀,x₁,t), where the correction network φ_η is trained via a teacher-student distillation: a teacher φ_ξ minimizes the Euler-Lagrange residual of the geodesic functional in DDIM-F latent space using a pretrained diffusion score as a density proxy (metric G(x)=p(x)⁻²I), while the student φ_η distills these manifold-aligned paths into the ambient VAE space via a DDIM-B reconstruction loss. This two-phase design decouples expensive score-dependent geodesic optimization from the flow matching training, making the final velocity network v_θ independent of the Riemannian metric at inference time.

## Results

On Objaverse (100 NFE), Linear-D2D-FM achieves the best FID of 5.43, PSNR of 20.84 dB, and SSIM of 0.863, outperforming all diffusion-based baselines. On GSO30, it attains the highest SSIM (0.865) and PSNR (19.87 dB) among all compared methods. In the reduced-training geodesic ablation (Table 3), PDG-FM further reduces FID to 10.40 (vs. 11.81 for Linear-D2D-FM and 11.96 for Metric FM) while improving SSIM to 0.877, confirming that density-aware geodesic alignment yields more geometrically coherent novel views.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Linear-D2D-FM consistently outperforms all diffusion-based NVS baselines on Objaverse: it beats Zero-1-to-3 by 0.56 FID and 1.26 dB PSNR, EscherNet by 2.47 FID and 2.09 dB PSNR, and Free3D by 0.11 FID and 0.52 dB PSNR, while also achieving the lowest LPIPS (0.081). Against the Noise-to-Data FM baseline (Naive FM), the data-to-data coupling yields a small but consistent gain in FID (5.43 vs. 5.51) and PSNR (20.84 vs. 20.82 dB), confirming that explicit paired-view supervision improves over noise-prior flow matching. On GSO30, Linear-D2D-FM achieves the best SSIM and PSNR but trails Free3D in FID (15.05 vs. 12.06) and CLIP-S (89.57 vs. 91.14), indicating that the advantage of deterministic data coupling is most pronounced on in-distribution data. In the geodesic ablation (Table 3), PDG-FM surpasses Metric FM [24] on FID (10.40 vs. 11.96), SSIM (0.877 vs. 0.866), and LPIPS (0.080 vs. 0.090), while slightly trailing Linear-D2D-FM in CLIP-S (92.34 vs. 94.35), suggesting that geodesic alignment trades a small amount of semantic similarity for improved structural fidelity and lower reconstruction error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of conditional flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) and probability-density geodesics in diffusion latent space (Yu et al., 2025), repurposing both for the novel view synthesis task. It departs from the standard noise-to-data paradigm of diffusion-based NVS (Zero-1-to-3, Free3D, EscherNet) by formulating view synthesis as a deterministic data-to-data transport problem, and further departs from Metric Flow Matching (Kapusniak et al., 2024) by using a pretrained diffusion score as a fixed density proxy rather than learning a Riemannian metric from scratch. The teacher-student distillation via DDIM forward/backward operators (Song et al., 2021) is a novel architectural choice that separates expensive geodesic optimization in the diffusion latent space from efficient ambient-space path prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that deterministic data-to-data flow matching is a more stable and sample-efficient formulation for NVS than stochastic noise-to-data approaches, particularly under accelerated inference (10 NFE), which is practically relevant for real-time applications. The geodesic alignment provides a principled geometric regularization that improves structural coherence, though the empirical gains over linear interpolants are modest (FID 11.8→10.4 on a reduced training setup). Key limitations include the multi-stage training pipeline (teacher distillation followed by flow matching), computational intensity of the geodesic optimization, and evaluation limited to single-object datasets; scaling to multi-object scenes and reducing inference cost remain open challenges.
