## Problem

Event-based video reconstruction from motion-triggered events is inherently ill-posed because these events are spatially sparse, concentrated at edges and moving regions, leaving static backgrounds nearly unobserved. Existing recurrent and transformer-based methods accumulate prediction errors over time and fail to faithfully reconstruct static scene structure. The paper addresses this gap by introducing a physical aperture modulation mechanism that actively generates dense, intensity-encoding events complementary to the sparse motion-triggered ones.

## Input / Output

The method consumes two temporally interleaved event streams per observation window: aperture-modulation-triggered events (captured during a ~0.13 s aperture-opening phase, converted to a first-positive-event temporal matrix) and motion-triggered events (the standard event stream over the remaining ~5 s window, binned into 5-bin voxel grids). Output is a sequence of K=20 reconstructed intensity frames per window at the sensor resolution (1280×720 on the real AMED dataset), with a gamma correction of 1.2 applied. The physical requirement of a motorized aperture shutter (Computar LensConnect BH Series) on a Prophesee EVK4 camera makes the input acquisition non-trivial compared to standard event-camera-only pipelines.

## Method

AE2VID decomposes reconstruction into two dedicated subnetworks. AENet processes aperture-modulation events through three stages: an FPE-based Intensity Reconstruction (FIR) module that inverts the first-positive-event timestamp to estimate per-pixel irradiance via Eq. (6), a SwinIR-based denoising (IDN) module, and a Hidden State Generation (HSG) module that produces feature-aligned hidden states for MENet. MENet is a bidirectional ConvLSTM recurrent network (initialized from V2V-E2VID) that runs forward and backward passes over motion-triggered voxel grids, then fuses the two directional predictions with the AENet reference frames via a lightweight pixel-wise softmax mixer. A periodic aperture closing-and-reopening strategy (interval τ ≈ 5.26 s) resets the observation window to prevent error accumulation. Training is two-stage: first the HSG module is aligned to MENet's hidden-state space with MENet frozen, then the full framework is fine-tuned end-to-end on ESIM-simulated and Blender-synthesized sequences.

## Results

On the EvAid benchmark, AE2VID achieves the best MSE (0.037), SSIM (0.707), and MS-SSIM (0.544), representing a 27.4% MSE improvement over the prior best (ETNet, 0.051) and a 10.1% SSIM gain over V2V-E2VID (0.642). On HQF, it attains the best MSE (0.039), SSIM (0.585), and MS-SSIM (0.503), though its LPIPS (0.352) trails BDE2VID (0.272) and ETNet (0.294). On the real-captured AMED dataset, qualitative comparisons show superior background structure preservation and colorchecker detail reconstruction over all seven baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest baseline V2V-E2VID on EvAid, AE2VID wins on MSE (0.037 vs. 0.052, −28.8%), SSIM (0.707 vs. 0.642, +10.1%), and MS-SSIM (0.544 vs. 0.524, +3.8%), while LPIPS is essentially tied (0.411 vs. 0.409). On HQF, AE2VID outperforms BDE2VID on MSE (0.039 vs. 0.041), SSIM (0.585 vs. 0.523), and MS-SSIM (0.503 vs. 0.477), but trails it on LPIPS (0.352 vs. 0.272); similarly, ETNet achieves a lower LPIPS (0.294) than AE2VID (0.352) on HQF despite worse structural metrics. Against the remaining baselines (E2VID, FireNet, SPADE-E2VID, PAEVSNN), AE2VID dominates on all four metrics across both datasets, with the largest gaps on MSE (up to 83% lower than E2VID on EvAid) and SSIM. The consistent pattern is that aperture-modulation-triggered events yield the largest gains in structural fidelity (SSIM/MS-SSIM) and pixel-level accuracy (MSE), while perceptual quality (LPIPS) shows a smaller margin or slight regression on HQF, suggesting the method's advantage is most pronounced in scenes with significant static content.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the E2VID/V2V-E2VID line of recurrent event-to-video reconstruction by introducing a second, complementary event source: aperture-modulation-triggered events. The FPE-based intensity reconstruction (FIR module) is directly inherited from Bao et al.'s temporal mapping photography, which used aperture shutters for static-scene radiance estimation; here it is repurposed as a periodic dense-reference generator within a dynamic video pipeline. Architecturally, the dual-network design (AENet for dense references, MENet for motion-driven frame synthesis) departs from prior single-stream recurrent or Transformer approaches by explicitly separating the two event modalities and fusing them via a pixel-wise mixer, while the bidirectional LSTM in MENet generalizes the unidirectional ConvLSTM backbone of E2VID.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a fundamental ill-posedness in event-based video reconstruction—static regions produce no motion-triggered events—by providing a practical, hardware-light solution (aperture is already present in most imaging systems) that periodically resets the observation window and mitigates error accumulation. The released AMED dataset is the first real-world capture under a controlled aperture-modulation protocol, filling a gap that prior methods could only address with synthetic or semi-real data. Key open questions include whether the fixed modulation parameters (A_E, v_A, τ) can be adapted online to varying illumination and motion speeds, and whether the approach generalizes to high-speed or extremely low-light scenarios where the current prototype degrades.
