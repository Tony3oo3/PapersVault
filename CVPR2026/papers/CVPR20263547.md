## Problem

Existing Gloss-to-Pose (G2P) methods for sign language production treat each pose as an indivisible unit, failing to capture fine-grained joint-level dependencies and degrading pose quality. Additionally, diffusion-based G2P models rely solely on regression losses to fit coordinates, leaving no mechanism to enforce semantic consistency between generated poses and their intended gloss meanings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263547/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a discrete gloss sequence (L sign-language units) as input and produces a continuous 3D sign pose sequence of S frames, where each frame contains J keypoints in 3D (x, y, z) coordinates. For PHOENIX14T, ground-truth poses are derived from 50 OpenPose 2D keypoints converted to 3D via a skeletal correction model; for USTC-CSL, 25 native 3D keypoints per frame are used. The generated pose sequence can directly drive digital avatar animation or serve as input for sign video synthesis.

## Method

FGDM introduces a two-stage denoising architecture: a Focal stage using Adaptive Sign GCN (ASGCN) and TCN to model joint-level spatial and temporal dependencies, followed by a General stage using a Transformer decoder for global sequence coherence. ASGCN constructs a frame-wise adaptive adjacency matrix by combining contextual correlations across neighboring frames, a fixed skeletal topology, and a semantic mask derived from gloss features, enabling dynamic modulation of joint connection strengths per frame. A Semantic Consistent Guidance (SCG) mechanism bridges the cross-modal gap by training a V2S Adapter and Semantic Decoder (LSD + GSD + Gloss Classifier) that project generated pose features into the gloss semantic space, where a reformulated CTC loss enforces alignment with the target gloss sequence. The SCG loss is dynamically scaled by the current diffusion timestep to ensure stable convergence during the iterative denoising process.

## Results

On PHOENIX14T (the primary benchmark), FGDM achieves BLEU-1 of 26.51, BLEU-4 of 9.67, ROUGE of 28.45, WER of 70.70, and FID of 2.31 on the TEST set, setting new SOTA across all metrics. On USTC-CSL Split-I, FGDM reaches BLEU-1 of 92.04 and WER of 12.98; on the more challenging Split-II, it achieves BLEU-2 of 26.46 and BLEU-3 of 15.71, substantially outperforming the only other diffusion baseline (Sign-IDD) by +13.03% on BLEU-3. Notably, FGDM even achieves a lower WER (70.70) than the ground-truth back-translation (71.94) on PHOENIX14T TEST, suggesting its generated poses are closer to the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FGDM outperforms all eight baselines on every reported metric for PHOENIX14T. Against the strongest non-diffusion method GEN-OBT [39], FGDM gains +3.43 BLEU-1, +1.66 BLEU-4, +4.96 ROUGE, and −11.08 WER on TEST. Among diffusion-based methods, FGDM surpasses the closest competitor Sign-IDD [41] by +3.35 BLEU-1, +1.45 BLEU-4, +3.94 ROUGE, and −8.45 WER on TEST, while also achieving a lower FID (2.31 vs. 2.44). The earlier autoregressive baselines (PT-base, PT-GN [31]) and non-autoregressive NAT-EA [20] are far behind, with WER gaps exceeding 10–30 points. On USTC-CSL Split-II, where all methods struggle (BLEU-4 = 0 for all), FGDM still leads Sign-IDD [41] by +13.03% on BLEU-3 (15.71 vs. 2.68) and +13.01% on BLEU-2, demonstrating stronger generalization to unseen sentences. The discrete-diffusion G2P-DDM [47] and the multi-hypothesis GCDM [42] are both clearly outperformed, with FGDM reducing WER by 6.56 and 1.24 points respectively on TEST.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based G2P line (G2P-DDM, GCDM, Sign-IDD, Sign-D2C) by introducing a two-stage denoising architecture that separates local joint-level modeling (via a frame-wise adaptive GCN, ASGCN) from global sequence coherence (via a Transformer decoder). ASGCN itself builds on the adaptive adjacency matrix concept from AGCN and the spatial decomposition from ST-GCN, but departs from the frame-shared paradigm by constructing per-frame adjacency matrices from contextual correlations, skeletal topology, and a semantic mask. The SCG loss reformulates CTC for diffusion training, drawing on the CTC objective from Graves et al. and the semantic decoder architecture from sign language recognition, to inject cross-modal gloss supervision that prior diffusion-based G2P methods lacked entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two identified gaps in G2P: the neglect of fine-grained joint dependencies in favor of global modeling, and the absence of semantic supervision in diffusion-based generation. The SCG mechanism—dynamically scaling CTC loss by diffusion timestep—is a practical recipe for integrating sequence-level semantic constraints into any diffusion model, potentially transferable beyond sign language. Limitations include the semantic mask generator being a simple linear layer (acknowledged as future work), evaluation relying on a fixed NSLT back-translation model, and the approach being validated on only two datasets. Open questions include whether the two-stage Focal-General decomposition generalizes to other structured sequential generation tasks and how the method scales to longer sequences or additional sign languages.
