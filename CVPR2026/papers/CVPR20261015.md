## Problem

Most LLM knowledge distillation pipelines apply a uniform token-wise loss regardless of teacher confidence, forcing the student to imitate high-entropy, noisy predictions that are especially harmful under large teacher-student capacity gaps. The paper reframes the central question from 'how to measure divergence' to 'where to apply supervision,' arguing that loss geometry (KL vs. RKL vs. SKL) is less critical than selectively filtering which tokens receive the learning signal.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261015/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on autoregressive text generation: given a prompt x, the student produces a token sequence y while the teacher provides per-position probability distributions over the vocabulary. In the VLM extension, image inputs are additionally consumed by the InternVL2 architecture. The training signal is not a single scalar loss but a per-token verification weight V_t ∈ {0, β, 1} that gates the divergence at each position, making the effective input to the optimizer a sparse, token-weighted version of the standard KD objective.

## Method

SelecTKD introduces a propose-and-verify mechanism at each token position: the student proposes tokens (either a single greedy argmax or k i.i.d. samples), and the teacher verifies them via either Top-k membership (greedy variant) or the standard speculative-decoding acceptance test a = min(1, p/q) (non-greedy Spec-k variant). Accepted tokens receive full divergence loss while rejected tokens are down-weighted by a small β (default 0.01), yielding the objective L = Σ_t V_t · D(p_t ‖ q_t) where D can be any of KL, RKL, SKL, or SRKL. A single hyperparameter k controls verification strictness, and the resulting Token Acceptance Rate (TAR) monotonically increases during training, formalizing an implicit curriculum that starts on easy, high-confidence tokens and gradually incorporates harder ones. The paper proves a lower bound on per-step TAR improvement and shows empirically that this selective masking produces a flatter loss landscape and mitigates the 'curse of the powerful teacher,' where larger teachers paradoxically degrade student performance under uniform KD.

## Results

On instruction-following (Qwen2-7B-Inst → 1.5B), SelecTKD applied on top of DistiLLM-2 raises average win rate from 58.69% to 59.47% (+0.78), and on top of GKD from 56.14% to 57.10% (+0.96). For mathematical reasoning, SelecTKD improves GSM8K Pass@1 from 76.27 to 77.82 and MATH from 35.58 to 36.89 over DistiLLM-2. In code generation, HumanEval Pass@1 increases from 59.92 to 61.72 and MBPP from 75.66 to 76.27. The VLM variant (SelecTKD-VLM-2B) achieves 57.9 average on the Open VLM Leaderboard, surpassing TAID-VLM-2B (56.4) and Phi-3-Vision (53.6), while speculative decoding speedup reaches ×2.05 with Phi-3-medium (vs. ×1.97 for DistiLLM-2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelecTKD consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every task and model pair. Against the strongest baseline DistiLLM-2, gains are +0.72 to +0.90 in average win rate on instruction-following, +1.12 to +1.43 in Pass@1 on math, and +1.21 to +1.51 on code, confirming that selective token weighting is orthogonal to and composable with advanced loss designs. Compared to GKD (on-policy distillation), SelecTKD adds +0.84 to +1.33 average WR; against DistiLLM (skew-KL), +0.82 to +1.15. On the VLM leaderboard, SelecTKD-VLM-2B (57.9 avg) beats TAID-VLM-2B (56.4), Phi-3-Vision (53.6), and the base InternVL2-2B (54.0). In speculative decoding, SelecTKD-trained drafters achieve the highest speedup (×2.05) and acceptance rate (0.492) with Phi-3-medium, surpassing DistiLLM-2 (×1.97, 0.487) and GKD (×1.64, 0.464). The paper also reports that SelecTKD outperforms two recent token-selective methods—AdaSPEC (40.21% WR) and ATKD (45.13% WR)—on Evol-Instruct, reaching 48.27% without requiring an extra reference model.

## Relation to Prior Work

SelecTKD reframes LLM knowledge distillation from optimizing loss geometry (KL vs. RKL vs. SKL/SRKL, as explored by MiniLLM, DistiLLM, and DistiLLM-2) to controlling where supervision is applied at the token level. Its propose-and-verify mechanism is directly borrowed from speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., Chen et al.), repurposing the acceptance-probability test as a teacher-verification gate rather than an inference-time speedup tool. Unlike prior token-selective methods (RHO-1, AdaSPEC, ATKD) that either reweight gradients, filter datasets, or require an extra reference model, SelecTKD is objective-agnostic and plug-and-play, composing additively with any existing divergence and data regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that loss-function choice matters less than token-level signal filtering challenges a dominant assumption in the KD literature and suggests future work should invest more in selective supervision than in new divergence formulations. The mitigation of the 'curse of the powerful teacher'—where larger teachers paradoxically degrade smaller students—is practically important for deploying frontier-model distillation at scale. Open questions include whether the Top-k/Spec-k mechanism generalizes to much larger capacity gaps (e.g., 70B→1B), whether the implicit curriculum can be made explicit for faster convergence, and whether the modest per-benchmark gains (+0.7–2.8%) compound meaningfully in downstream agent or multi-turn settings.
