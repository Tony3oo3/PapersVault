## Problem

Jointly optimizing multiple reward models in RLHF for generative models incurs an alignment tax: weighted-sum scalarization is dominated by easily optimized objectives, and one-shot weight souping (Rewarded Soup) still underperforms individually fine-tuned experts. No existing framework efficiently advances the Pareto front across multiple human-aligned preferences without sacrificing any single dimension.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as primary input and optionally appends one or more RaTE special-token embeddings (each a single learned vector distilled from a reward-specific LoRA expert) to the prompt for composable preference control at inference. Outputs are generated images (512×512 or 1024×1024 depending on the base model), generated video clips, or text responses, depending on the downstream task. The RaTE tokens are lightweight (training cost is 0.1579× that of MapReduce LoRA) and can be freely composed or reordered without retraining the backbone.

## Method

MapReduce LoRA decomposes multi-reward optimization into a Map phase (training reward-specific LoRA experts in parallel via GRPO) and a Reduce phase (averaging expert weights, folding the merged adapter into the base model, and repeating for k iterations). The key theoretical result shows that under a Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, each iteration applies an averaged proximal map that contracts geometrically toward the joint optimum, so progressive souping strictly outperforms one-shot souping. Complementarily, RaTE distills each trained LoRA expert into a single trainable token embedding using a Flow Matching MSE objective, where the teacher is the expert-augmented model and the student is the frozen backbone conditioned on the special token. At inference, appending RaTE tokens to the prompt injects the corresponding preference, enabling a posteriori, composable control without any retraining of the transformer weights.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium, MapReduce LoRA + RaTE improves GenEval by 36.1%, PickScore by 4.6%, and OCR by 55.7% over the base model, while also boosting untargeted rewards (VQAScore +1.85%, MPS +6.49%, VILA +19.96%). On FLUX.1-dev, the same method yields +32.7% GenEval, +4.3% PickScore, and +67.1% OCR. On HunyuanVideo (Text-to-Video), visual quality and motion quality improve by 48.1% and 90.0% respectively. On Llama-2 7B (Helpful Assistant), helpful and harmless rewards improve by 43.4% and 136.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CaPO (a priori weighted multi-reward optimization), MapReduce LoRA substantially outperforms on all four shared metrics: GenEval +31.88% vs. CaPO's +4.41%, VQAScore +2.78% vs. +0.66%, MPS +4.89% vs. +1.42%, and VILA +3.85% vs. +2.59%. Against Flow-GRPO individual experts, which boost a single targeted reward but transfer poorly (e.g., the OCR expert scores only 0.879 on GenEval Overall vs. 0.93 for the GenEval expert), MapReduce LoRA achieves competitive or superior scores across all three in-domain rewards simultaneously (0.90 / 22.552 / 0.923). Against Rewarded Soup (one-shot weight averaging), MapReduce LoRA wins decisively on GenEval (0.90 vs. 0.68) and PickScore (22.552 vs. 21.800), while Rewarded Soup marginally leads on OCR (0.934 vs. 0.923) but degrades on out-of-domain VILA (6.014 vs. 6.266). For Text-to-Video, MapReduce LoRA beats both DanceGRPO individual experts (VQ 4.81 vs. 4.37; MQ 1.81 vs. 1.20) and Rewarded Soup (VQ 4.81 vs. 3.80; MQ 1.81 vs. 1.66), confirming that iterative merging consistently outperforms one-shot souping across modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

MapReduce LoRA extends the one-shot weight-merging paradigm of Rewarded Soup [35] into an iterative, progressive-souping process with a convergence guarantee under the Polyak-Łojasiewicz condition, while RaTE repurposes the single-token mechanism of Textual Inversion [13] from concept personalization to reward-specific preference control. The training phase relies on Flow-GRPO [26] / DanceGRPO [44] for per-reward expert optimization, and the overall a-posteriori design contrasts with a-priori scalarization methods like CaPO [21] and MORL variants that combine rewards into a fixed weighted sum. The iterative averaging step shares structural similarity with FedAvg [40] and DiLoCo [10], but is repurposed here to navigate the Pareto front rather than reduce communication cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, low-overhead recipe (train LoRA experts in parallel, merge iteratively, optionally distill into token embeddings) that practitioners can adopt without architectural changes, and the cross-modal validation (T2I, T2V, language) strengthens the claim that the approach is general. Key limitations include RaTE's incompatibility with joint text-image sequence models like FLUX.1-dev, the reliance on a PL-condition assumption for the convergence proof, and the observation that gains are largest on metrics where the base model is weakest. Open questions include whether iterative merging scales gracefully to many more than three rewards, how the method behaves under highly non-convex reward landscapes, and whether the token-distillation approach can be adapted to architectures without explicit cross-attention.
