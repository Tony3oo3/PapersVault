## Problem

Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) methods face a fundamental efficiency-generalization trade-off: updating more parameters improves adaptation to shifting domains but inflates inference cost and amplifies parameter drift, degrading long-term performance. The paper asks whether there exists a minimal feature-update subspace—the 'golden subspace'—that achieves the necessary output correction with the smallest possible perturbation, and how to maintain it online without retraining the classifier.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261354/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes unlabeled test image batches sequentially (no source data access), a frozen pretrained backbone and classifier, and pre-computed class prototypes extracted offline from the source domain. At each step it produces class labels (classification) or pixel-level segmentation masks (segmentation). The only trainable parameters per step are a compact scaling vector of dimension r=64 and a small set of batch-norm statistics; the backbone and classifier weights remain frozen throughout, keeping per-batch adaptation cost around 0.25 seconds.

## Method

GOLD is built on two theoretical insights: (1) the least-norm feature correction for a desired output change lies in the row space of the classifier W, making the golden subspace inherently low-rank (bounded by the number of classes); and (2) the Average Gradient Outer Product (AGOP) computed from high-confidence test samples provides a faithful online proxy for W^T W without retraining. Concretely, GOLD maintains an auxiliary matrix G initialized from W^T W and updated via an exponential moving average of per-batch AGOP from samples exceeding a confidence threshold τ. Every T_eig batches, a symmetric eigendecomposition of G yields the top-r eigenvectors V_t that span the current golden subspace. Features from the frozen backbone are projected onto V_t and rescaled by a learned vector S_t in a residual form (f + V_t(S_t ⊙ V_t^T f)), so S_t = 0 recovers the identity. S_t is optimized with a single gradient step combining a symmetric cross-entropy self-training loss against an EMA teacher and an InfoNCE-style prototype contrastive loss that anchors adapted features to source-domain class centroids.

## Results

On CIFAR-10-C, CIFAR-100-C, and ImageNet-C (all at severity level 5), GOLD achieves mean error rates of 14.1%, 28.6%, and 59.3% respectively, setting new state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks. The closest competitor OBAO achieves 14.6%, 29.5%, and 59.6% on the same datasets, giving GOLD a consistent 0.3–0.9 percentage-point margin. On the CarlaTTA segmentation benchmark, GOLD attains the highest mIoU on three of five sequences (day2night: 61.8%, clear2fog: 57.1%, highway: 34.5%), outperforming CoTTA by 0.7% on the challenging highway sequence that involves both covariate and label distribution shift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOLD outperforms all 10 classification baselines on every dataset. The closest competitor is OBAO (ECCV 2024), which GOLD beats by 0.5 pp on CIFAR-10-C (14.1 vs 14.6), 0.9 pp on CIFAR-100-C (28.6 vs 29.5), and 0.3 pp on ImageNet-C (59.3 vs 59.6). Among the earlier CTTA methods, CoTTA (CVPR 2022) and DSS (CVPR 2024) are the strongest on CIFAR-10-C (16.2% and 16.0% respectively), while RMT (CVPR 2023) and SANTA (TMLR 2023) lead on CIFAR-100-C (30.4% each); GOLD improves over these by 2.1–2.6 pp. On ImageNet-C, SANTA (60.1%) and OBAO (59.6%) are the nearest baselines, with GOLD achieving a 0.8 pp and 0.3 pp advantage respectively. On the CarlaTTA segmentation benchmark, GOLD surpasses CoTTA on day2night (+0.4), clear2fog (+0.3), and highway (+0.7 mIoU), but trails TENT on the dynamic sequence (48.9 vs 50.8) and the unadapted Source model on clear2rain (71.0 vs 71.8), indicating that the low-rank subspace constraint is most beneficial under strong or compound distribution shifts.

## Relation to Prior Work

GOLD departs from the dominant CTTA paradigm (CoTTA, PETAL, RMT, SANTA, DSS, TCA) that updates large portions of the network at test time via self-supervised or entropy-based objectives. Instead, it grounds adaptation in a least-norm linear-algebraic analysis showing that the minimal feature correction lies in the row space of the classifier, and repurposes the Average Gradient Outer Product (AGOP) literature from Beaglehole et al. and Radhakrishnan et al. as an online, label-free proxy for tracking that subspace without retraining. The resulting low-rank scaling adapter is conceptually related to LoRA-style parameter-efficient fine-tuning but is driven by a principled subspace estimate rather than arbitrary low-rank factors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The efficiency–generalization trade-off in CTTA is a genuine deployment bottleneck (autonomous driving, medical imaging), and GOLD's ~0.25 s/batch runtime with competitive accuracy makes structured, minimal-update adaptation practically viable. The clean theoretical link between the Moore-Penrose least-norm solution and the classifier row space gives the method a principled inductive bias that prior heuristic CTTA methods lack. Open questions include whether the low-rank golden-subspace structure persists for very deep or multi-modal backbones, how sensitive the method is to the confidence threshold τ and EMA teacher quality under severe label noise, and whether the AGOP estimator remains reliable when the target distribution shifts non-stationarily within a single batch.
