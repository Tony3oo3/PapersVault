## Problem

Existing LiDAR novel view synthesis methods either require accurate camera poses (impractical in real-world deployment) or, in pose-free settings, rely solely on geometric interpolation that produces geometric holes and discontinuous surfaces due to LiDAR sparsity and lack of texture. These issues are particularly severe in low-frequency sequences where large inter-frame motion and reduced overlap undermine multi-view consistency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261148/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view LiDAR point cloud sequences (64-beam KITTI-360 or 32-beam nuScenes, resampled to 2 Hz for low-frequency evaluation) and projects them into range images with 768 uniformly sampled points per laser beam, all scaled to unit cube space. It jointly outputs a continuous implicit scene representation that synthesizes novel LiDAR views (depth, intensity, and ray-drop probability per ray) and estimates global poses parameterized in 6D Lie algebra, optimized with 20° rotation and 3 m translation noise perturbation during training.

## Method

SG-NLF introduces a hybrid representation that fuses multi-resolution hash grid geometric encodings (capturing high-frequency local structure) with differentiable spectral embeddings (the first K Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions approximated by MLPs and optimized via Rayleigh quotient minimization with orthogonality and normalization constraints), enabling smooth surface reconstruction in unobserved regions. For pose estimation, it constructs a confidence-aware pose graph where edges are selected via coarse-to-fine mutual nearest neighbor matching on hybrid features, scored by mean cosine similarity, and weighted by spatial consistency of correspondences; global poses are then optimized through a weighted Chamfer Distance loss over the graph. A multi-scale PatchGAN discriminator enforces cross-frame consistency by distinguishing real concatenated depth maps (ground-truth point clouds transformed via GT relative pose) from fake ones (synthesized point clouds transformed via estimated relative pose), providing discriminative supervision beyond per-frame photometric losses.

## Results

On KITTI-360 (low-frequency, 2 Hz), SG-NLF achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.1695 and depth PSNR of 28.71 dB, outperforming the best pose-free baseline GeoNLF (CD 0.2363, depth PSNR 25.28 dB) by 28.3% in CD and 13.6% in depth PSNR. On nuScenes (low-frequency), SG-NLF reduces CD by 35.8% and depth RMSE by 47.2% relative to GeoNLF, while also improving intensity PSNR from 28.61 to 30.50 dB. For pose estimation, SG-NLF achieves an ATE of 0.074 m on KITTI-360 and 0.071 m on nuScenes, reducing ATE by 56.4% and 68.8% respectively over GeoNLF. Even against the pose-dependent LiDAR4D (which uses ground-truth poses), SG-NLF surpasses it by over 38.5% in CD on KITTI-360 low-frequency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SG-NLF outperforms all 13 baselines across every metric on both KITTI-360 and nuScenes in the low-frequency setting. Against the closest pose-free competitor GeoNLF [50], SG-NLF reduces CD by 28.3% (KITTI-360) and 35.8% (nuScenes), depth RMSE by 26.8% and 47.2%, and ATE by 56.4% and 68.8% respectively. Notably, SG-NLF also surpasses the pose-dependent LiDAR4D [55] (which uses ground-truth poses) by 38.5% in CD and 37.5% in depth RMSE on KITTI-360 low-frequency, demonstrating that the pose-free formulation does not sacrifice reconstruction quality. Among traditional simulators, LiDARsim [31] and PCGen [20] are far behind (CD > 1.0 vs. 0.17), confirming the advantage of implicit neural representations. For pose estimation, SG-NLF (ATE 0.074 m) substantially outperforms pairwise registration methods like ICP (1.894 m), GeoTrans (0.246 m), and SGHR (2.539 m) on KITTI-360, as well as pose-free NeRF methods BARF-LN (2.763 m) and HASH-LN (2.666 m). On standard-frequency KITTI-360, SG-NLF also leads all pose-dependent methods (LiDAR-NeRF, LiDAR4D, NKSR) in CD, depth PSNR, and intensity PSNR, confirming generalization beyond the low-frequency regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

SG-NLF extends the pose-free LiDAR NeRF line initiated by GeoNLF, which itself builds on LiDAR-NeRF's volume rendering formulation and BARF/HASH-style pose optimization. The key departure is twofold: (1) replacing pure geometric interpolation (multi-resolution hash encoding) with a hybrid representation that adds differentiable Laplace-Beltrami spectral embeddings (following Spherical Neural Surfaces) to provide global structural priors absent in sparse LiDAR data, and (2) upgrading GeoNLF's pairwise alignment constraints to a confidence-aware pose graph that incorporates non-adjacent edges weighted by feature compatibility and spatial consistency. An adversarial PatchGAN for cross-frame depth consistency is also introduced, drawing on GAN-based registration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pose-free LiDAR view synthesis is practically important for autonomous driving simulation where accurate sensor poses are unavailable or noisy, and the 35.8% CD / 68.8% ATE improvements over GeoNLF on low-frequency sequences (hundreds of meters of travel) address a genuinely challenging regime. The use of spectral embeddings—tools from differential geometry—to regularize sparse LiDAR reconstruction is a conceptually interesting bridge between shape analysis and sensor simulation. However, the paper acknowledges it provides only one implementation, and open questions remain about scalability to larger scenes, dynamic objects, and whether the spectral component's benefit persists at higher LiDAR frequencies where geometric interpolation is less problematic.
