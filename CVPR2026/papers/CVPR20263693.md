## Problem

Existing Novel Class Discovery methods for point cloud segmentation rely on binary (pairwise) associations between classes for label assignment and novel class reasoning, which limits their ability to capture high-order interactions among multiple classes. Additionally, these methods overemphasize semantic feature extraction while neglecting the 3D geometric and spatial structure inherent to point cloud data, leading to imprecise segmentation of novel classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263693/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw 3D point clouds (SemanticKITTI with 19 classes, SemanticPOSS with 13 classes) as input, processed through a MinkowskiUNet-34C backbone to extract point-wise features. During training, only known classes carry ground-truth labels; novel classes are entirely unlabeled. The output is point-wise class assignments for all classes, where novel class labels are generated as pseudo-labels via Hungarian matching after the hypergraph-based discovery process identifies and names new clusters.

## Method

The method constructs Geometric-Aware Prototypes by concatenating semantic features (from multi-layer 3D convolutions) with geometric features (Linearity, Planarity, Scattering derived from eigenvalues of the Euclidean distance covariance matrix of KNN neighborhoods), then updates them via soft-weighted residual sums with L2 normalization. These prototypes serve as nodes in a hypergraph where each hyperedge connects a prototype to its M=8 most similar neighbors, with similarity computed as a weighted combination of geometric proximity (Gaussian kernel on average local neighborhood distance) and semantic cosine similarity. A Dynamic Hyperedge Adjustment Mechanism recomputes edge weights and reconstructs the hyperedge set across training batches, allowing the structure to adapt as novel classes emerge. Novel class discovery proceeds by comparing each prototype's similarity to known prototypes: those exceeding a threshold receive pseudo-labels, while those below it are assigned new class labels and incorporated into the evolving hypergraph.

## Results

On the SemanticPOSS dataset, the proposed method achieves the best overall IoU across all four splits (41.3, 45.1, 47.0, 46.8), with particularly strong novel-class IoU gains on Splits 2 and 3 (22.3 and 37.8) compared to the next-best method DASL (12.6 and 17.7). On SemanticKITTI, the method also attains the highest average IoU across all four splits, approaching supervised (Full) performance on Splits 1 and 3. The ablation confirms that each component contributes: adding Geometric-Aware Prototypes, Hypergraph Structure Construction, and Dynamic Hyperedge Adjustment sequentially improves the overall average from 23.2 (baseline) to 36.5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines (EUMS, NOPS, SNOPS, DASL) on the overall 'All' IoU metric across all four SemanticPOSS splits. The closest competitor is DASL [31], which the method surpasses by 0.1, 1.1, 2.7, and 2.1 points on Splits 0–3 respectively; the gap widens substantially on novel-class IoU, where Ours achieves 22.3 vs. DASL's 12.6 on Split 2 and 37.8 vs. 17.7 on Split 3. Against NOPS [18] and SNOPS [19], the improvements are larger (8–11 points on 'All' IoU), reflecting the advantage of hypergraph-based high-order reasoning over binary pairwise clustering. EUMS [36], a 2D segmentation NCD method adapted here, is consistently the weakest baseline, trailing by 10–19 points on 'All' IoU. Notably, on Split 0 the method's novel-class IoU (47.2) is slightly below SNOPS (51.2) and DASL (48.4), but it compensates with the best known-class IoU (38.7), yielding the highest overall score.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NCD-for-point-cloud-segmentation line initiated by NOPS (Riz et al., CVPR 2023) and DASL (Xu et al., ECCV 2024), both of which rely on binary pairwise relations between classes or regions for label assignment and information propagation. The key conceptual departure is replacing these binary associations with a hypergraph structure that connects multiple class prototypes per hyperedge, enabling collaborative high-order reasoning for novel classes. Additionally, it introduces Geometric-Aware Prototypes that fuse local curvature descriptors (linearity, planarity, scattering from the Euclidean distance covariance matrix) with semantic features, addressing the tendency of prior NCD methods to over-emphasize semantic features while neglecting 3D spatial structure. The backbone (MinkowskiUNet-34C) and overall training protocol are shared with NOPS and DASL, making the contribution squarely methodological rather than architectural.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NCD in 3D point clouds is a practically important task for open-world autonomous driving and robotics, where new object categories inevitably appear in deployment. The hypergraph formulation offers a principled way to exploit multi-class geometric and semantic context simultaneously, and the dynamic hyperedge adjustment mechanism provides a lightweight mechanism for handling class imbalance and novel-class emergence without retraining. Open questions include whether the hypergraph approach scales to datasets with substantially more than 19 classes, whether the fixed KNN-based geometric descriptors generalize to highly non-rigid or occluded scenes, and whether the computational overhead of per-batch hyperedge reconstruction becomes prohibitive at larger batch sizes or longer training runs.
