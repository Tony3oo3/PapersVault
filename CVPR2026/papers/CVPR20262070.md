## Problem

Medical vision-language models frequently hallucinate pathologies and fail to ground textual findings in the correct image regions, largely because standard multi-task training treats all datasets and anatomical classes uniformly despite severe inter- and intra-dataset imbalance. Existing grounded models like MAIRA-2 develop a bias toward associating visual regions with abnormal findings, producing false positives. CURE addresses this by introducing an error-aware curriculum that dynamically re-weights sampling based on the model's current performance, without requiring any additional data.

## Input / Output

The model receives a frontal-view chest X-ray image paired with a text instruction (e.g., 'Locate and describe the left clavicle' or 'Generate a grounded report') and produces structured outputs where anatomical descriptions are explicitly linked to normalized bounding box coordinates in [cx, cy, w, h] format. For the AGRG task, a single image can yield up to 36 separate instruction-response pairs (one per annotated anatomical location), and the full report is assembled by concatenating descriptions across 29 distinct locations. The base model is MedGemma-4B-IT fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 16, 4-bit precision) with an effective batch size of 25.

## Method

CURE reformulates three heterogeneous tasks—phrase grounding, grounded report generation, and anatomy-grounded report generation—into a unified fine-grained instructional format, then trains with a two-level error-aware curriculum. At the inter-dataset level, sampling probabilities are re-weighted every 3000 steps using a composite error score (1 − α·IoU − (1−α)·CXRFEScore) computed on fixed validation subsets; at the intra-dataset level, individual anatomical regions or semantic classes are re-weighted by their per-class error. The AGRG task is decomposed into three subtasks (Locate, Describe, Locate-and-Describe) that explicitly disentangle spatial localization from textual description, exposing the model to both normal and abnormal findings per region. Training proceeds in two phases: a 3000-step Chest ImaGenome pre-training stage followed by a 6000-step multi-task curriculum phase, with optimizer states reset between phases. This adaptive scheduling lets the model progressively focus on underperforming datasets and rare anatomical regions, improving grounding accuracy by +0.35 IoU and reducing hallucinations by 18.6% over MAIRA-2.

## Results

On the Chest ImaGenome AGRG task, CURE achieves 0.601 mean IoU, more than doubling MAIRA-2's 0.249 (+0.35 IoU), and attains the best CXRFEScore (0.549 vs. 0.357 for MAIRA-2, +0.192). For phrase grounding, CURE outperforms MAIRA-2 on all Micro/Macro IoU metrics across MS-CXR (0.552 vs. 0.495), PadChest-GR (0.453 vs. 0.280), and zero-shot VinDr-CXR (0.243 vs. 0.161). On the MIMIC-CXR report generation benchmark, CURE (AGRG+GRG) achieves the highest CheXbert Cosine Similarity (0.792), RaTEScore (0.597), and CXRFEScore (0.655). Hallucination analysis shows CURE reduces abnormal-finding hallucinations from 26.50% to 8.78% (−18.6%) and halves the NLI contradiction rate (17.44% vs. 33.22%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MAIRA-2, CURE wins decisively on all grounding metrics: +0.35 IoU on AGRG (0.601 vs. 0.249), +0.173 IoU on PadChest-GR PG (0.453 vs. 0.280), and +0.082 IoU on zero-shot VinDr-CXR PG (0.243 vs. 0.161). CURE also substantially outperforms MAIRA-2 on hallucination (8.78% vs. 26.50%) and NLI entailment (39.50% vs. 15.94%), though MAIRA-2 retains a slight edge on GRG text metrics (CXRFEScore 0.616 vs. 0.574 on PadChest-GR) owing to its proprietary USMix pretraining. Against the MedGemma-4B-IT base model, CURE instills visual grounding from scratch (0.601 IoU where the base model has none) and lifts CXRFEScore from 0.467 to 0.549 on AGRG. On the MIMIC-CXR RG benchmark, CURE (AGRG+GRG) trails CXRMate-RRG24 on RadGraph F1 (0.176 vs. 0.255) but surpasses it on CheXbert Cosine (0.792 vs. 0.764), RaTEScore (0.597 vs. 0.577), and CXRFEScore (0.655 vs. 0.656, near-tie), while achieving the best recall (R-Mi 0.781, R-Ma 0.582) among all compared models.

## Relation to Prior Work

CURE extends MAIRA-2's multi-task grounding training paradigm by replacing the conventional finding-generation objective with an anatomy-grounded report generation (AGRG) task that decomposes reports into per-anatomical-region locate and describe subtasks. The curriculum mechanism builds on self-paced curriculum learning (Jiang et al., 2015) but departs from loss-based difficulty estimation by integrating both spatial (IoU) and textual (CXRFEScore) feedback to drive sampling, operating at both dataset and anatomical-class granularity. Unlike prior medical VLM work that assumes the base model already possesses grounding capabilities, CURE demonstrates that grounding can be effectively instilled into MedGemma-4B-IT, a model that originally lacks any visual grounding mechanism, using only publicly available chest X-ray data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 18.6% hallucination reduction—particularly the dramatic drop for bone structures (clavicles from ~59% to 1%)—addresses a critical barrier to clinical deployment of medical VLMs, and the insight that exposing models to balanced normal/abnormal descriptions (via AGRG) mitigates the abnormality bias inherent in standard phrase grounding offers a reusable design principle. However, the evaluation is confined to chest X-rays and a single 4B-parameter base model, leaving open whether the curriculum transfers to other modalities (CT, MRI), larger models, or non-frontal-view imaging. The reliance on Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite as an NLI judge for hallucination scoring, and the use of only ~1.74% of available training instances due to compute constraints, are practical limitations that temper the generalizability of the reported gains.
