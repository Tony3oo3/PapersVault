## Problem

Fine-Grained Cross-View Geolocalization (FG-CVG) requires estimating a ground camera's precise 2-DoF location relative to a satellite image, but existing methods force a difficult accuracy-speed trade-off: matching-based approaches suffer quantization errors from discretization, while regression-based methods achieve higher precision at the cost of heavy geometric projections, BEV estimation, or complex matching decoders that preclude real-time deployment. No prior method simultaneously operates in continuous pose space, provides probabilistic uncertainty, and runs at speeds suitable for autonomous navigation in GPS-denied areas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260004/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a ground RGB query image (e.g., vehicle-mounted, limited FoV on KITTI or panoramic on VIGOR) and a corresponding geo-referenced satellite RGB map (~70 m × 70 m on VIGOR, ~100 m × 100 m on KITTI), plus an arbitrary initial location hypothesis q₀ sampled within the satellite map. Rather than outputting a single point estimate, the model predicts a probabilistic displacement in polar coordinates: a Gaussian distribution over distance (μ_r, σ_r²) and a von Mises-Fisher distribution over direction (μ_θ, κ). The final 2-DoF location is the mean of a population of N hypotheses after R iterative refinement rounds, with the spread of the converged population serving as a natural confidence measure.

## Method

GeoFlow uses two lightweight EfficientNet-B0 backbones to extract ground and satellite features, which are positionally encoded and fused via cross-attention (ground tokens as queries, satellite tokens as keys/values) into a single visual vector f_vis. An initial location q₀ is projected through a fully connected layer and concatenated with f_vis, then decoded by a small MLP into two probabilistic heads: a Gaussian for displacement distance and a von Mises-Fisher for displacement direction, trained with a combined NLL objective. At inference, the Iterative Refinement Sampling (IRS) algorithm initializes N random hypotheses and iteratively applies the learned single-step displacement for R rounds, with the expensive backbone and cross-attention computed only once while only the lightweight MLP is re-run per iteration. This design yields a 7.38 M-parameter model running at 29.5 FPS, with inference-time scaling (adjusting N and R) offering a controllable accuracy-speed trade-off without re-training.

## Results

On KITTI, GeoFlow achieves 29.49 FPS (the fastest among all compared methods) with a 0.98 m mean localization error in the same-area setting and 8.42 m in the cross-area setting. On VIGOR, it reaches 3.51 m mean error (same-area) and 4.62 m (cross-area) at the same 29.49 FPS. The model uses only 7.38 M parameters and 686 MiB memory, representing a 7.8× parameter reduction and 6.9× memory reduction compared to CCVPE while maintaining competitive accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoFlow is the fastest method across all comparisons, running at 29.49 FPS versus 24.00 (CCVPE), 25.00 (HC-Net), 7.30 (DenseFlow), 4.20 (FG²), and 4.17 (GGCVT). On KITTI cross-area mean error, GeoFlow (8.42 m) beats CCVPE (9.16 m) by 8.1% and is comparable to HC-Net (8.47 m), but trails DenseFlow (7.97 m) and FG² (7.45 m). On VIGOR, GeoFlow outperforms CCVPE in both same-area (3.51 m vs. 3.60 m) and cross-area (4.62 m vs. 4.97 m) settings, and also beats GGCVT and DenseFlow on cross-area, but is outperformed by HC-Net (2.65 m same-area, 3.35 m cross-area) and FG² (2.18 m same-area, 2.74 m cross-area). The paper positions GeoFlow as the best speed–accuracy trade-off: it sacrifices a small amount of peak accuracy relative to FG² and HC-Net in exchange for 5–14× higher inference speed and a 7.8× smaller model, making it the only method suitable for real-time deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoFlow carves new ground by repurposing the iterative refinement concept from flow matching models in image synthesis (Lipman et al. 2023; Esser et al. 2024) for cross-view geolocalization, but deliberately departs from learning a true continuous flow field in favor of a simpler direct displacement regression. It inherits the cross-attention fusion paradigm from prior cross-view matching work (Yuan et al. 2024) and the von Mises-Fisher probabilistic formulation from surface normal estimation (Bae et al. 2021), but combines these into a novel multi-hypothesis iterative inference algorithm (IRS) that has no direct precedent in the FG-CVG literature. The paper positions itself against both matching-based methods (which suffer quantization error) and heavier regression-based methods (which require camera intrinsics, BEV projections, or dense matching), offering a lightweight continuous-space alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that real-time (29 FPS) fine-grained geolocalization with competitive accuracy is achievable with a fraction of the computational cost of prior SOTA, which is critical for autonomous navigation in GPS-denied environments. The inference-time scaling property—trading accuracy for speed by adjusting N and R without retraining—is a genuinely new paradigm for this task and could influence how practitioners deploy geolocalization models under varying latency constraints. Key limitations include the 2-DoF restriction (orientation assumed known), the use of a deliberately lightweight backbone that caps peak accuracy below heavier models like FG², and evaluation limited to two datasets. Open questions include whether the flow-inspired iterative approach scales to full 6-DoF estimation and whether the IRS consensus mechanism generalizes to larger search areas or more diverse scene types.
