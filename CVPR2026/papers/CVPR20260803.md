## Problem

Current sign language translation systems degrade significantly on long or discourse-level inputs due to autoregressive error accumulation, where early prediction mistakes cascade and distort later output. Additionally, gloss-based methods that achieve stronger linguistic alignment require expensive manual annotation of precise gloss boundaries, limiting scalability. The community lacks a solution that improves long-form coherence without gloss supervision and without modifying existing model architectures.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes 25 fps RGB video frames and HRNet-extracted hand pose keypoints (2D coordinates with confidence scores from COCO-WholeBody). The video is first partitioned into short clips of controlled length via unsupervised energy-based segmentation; each clip is independently translated by a plug-and-play SLT backbone, and the resulting short text fragments (not raw video) serve as the initialization for the diffusion model. The final output is a single coherent translated paragraph with a token-to-segment alignment map for interpretability.

## Method

BoostSLT is a three-stage plug-and-play framework. First, Energy-Aware Temporal Segmentation (EAT-Seg) computes confidence-weighted hand-joint motion energy, smooths it with a moving average, and applies a length-aware local-cut optimization (minimizing energy plus a centering penalty within adaptive search windows) to partition the video into semantically coherent segments without any gloss or boundary annotations. Second, a modular Sign2Text backbone (any existing SLT model) translates each segment independently. Third, a Diffusion-Based Semantic Reconstruction (DSR) module fine-tunes LLaDA-8B to iteratively denoise from the concatenated short phrases—rather than pure Gaussian noise—into a fluent paragraph over T=25 steps, using a LexMasker that preserves high-information content words as anchors while selectively re-masking function words to refine discourse structure. The key distinction from standard text diffusion is that the initial state is a structured linguistic prior, making the model operate as a semantic diffuser that restores inter-segment coherence without overwriting established content.

## Results

BoostSLT achieves average gains of +3.8 BLEU-4 and +3.2 ROUGE-L on PHOENIX-2014T, +4.1 BLEU-4 on CSL-Daily, and +3.5 BLEU-4 on Auslan-Daily across six gloss-free backbones. The largest absolute improvement is on CV-SLT (PHOENIX-2014T Test BLEU-4: 29.27 → 33.32, a >14% relative gain), while even the strongest baseline (TwoStreamNetwork) improves from 28.95 to 30.12 BLEU-4. On Auslan-Daily News, MMTLB sees the most dramatic jump (BLEU-4: 2.31 → 12.46), reflecting the benefit of diffusion-based reconstruction on very low-quality segment translations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BoostSLT outperforms all six baselines on every dataset and metric, confirming its plug-and-play nature. The weakest baseline, GASLT, benefits most in relative terms: PHOENIX-2014T Test BLEU-4 jumps from 15.62 to 21.95 (+40%) and CSL-Daily from 4.07 to 7.26 (+78%), indicating that diffusion reconstruction is most impactful when segment-level translations are highly fragmented. Among stronger baselines, CV-SLT shows the largest absolute gain (PHOENIX B4: 29.27→33.32; CSL B4: 28.94→32.49), while TwoStreamNetwork, already near the top, improves more modestly (PHOENIX B4: 28.95→30.12). LLM-integrated models Sign2GPT and LiTFiC also improve (up to +4.8 BLEU-4 on Auslan-Daily), showing that diffusion-based refinement complements rather than duplicates LLM-based linguistic reasoning. On Auslan-Daily, where absolute scores are lower due to domain difficulty, MMTLB sees the largest relative boost (News BLEU-4: 2.31→12.46), while the already-competitive LiTFiC still gains +2.1 BLEU-4 on the News subset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: gloss-free sign language translation (building on MMTLB, TwoStreamNetwork, CV-SLT, GASLT, Sign2GPT, and LiTFiC) and diffusion-based language modeling (building on LLaDA, D3PM, and Diffusion-LM). It departs from prior SLT work by introducing a plug-and-play post-processing module rather than a new end-to-end architecture, and from prior temporal segmentation work by replacing supervised boundary detection with unsupervised motion-energy analysis. The key conceptual departure is initializing a diffusion denoising process from meaningful fragment-level translations rather than pure Gaussian noise, repurposing the diffusion framework as a semantic stitcher for discourse-level coherence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means practitioners can immediately boost any existing SLT backbone without retraining, which is practically valuable given the high annotation cost of gloss-based pipelines. The consistent gains across three languages (German, Chinese, Australian) and six architectures strengthen the generalizability claim. However, the 8B-parameter diffusion model introduces non-trivial inference overhead, and the reliance on pseudo phrase-level pairs generated by the segmentation module means quality is bounded by upstream translation accuracy. Open questions include whether joint optimization of segmentation, translation, and diffusion refinement could yield further gains, and whether the approach extends to truly open-ended discourse (multi-turn conversation) rather than single-paragraph signing.
