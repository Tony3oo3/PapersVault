## Problem

Semi-supervised document layout detection relies on teacher-student pseudo-labeling that inherits systematic visual biases, struggling with rare layout elements and fine-grained distinctions like captions versus footers. Existing semi-supervised methods operate almost exclusively on visual cues, leaving the rich structural semantics available in document text underutilized. This paper asks whether LLM-derived structural priors can be principledly fused with visual detector predictions to produce more accurate pseudo-labels under extreme label scarcity (5% annotations).

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes document images (resized to 512×512) and Tesseract-extracted OCR text blocks with line-level bounding box coordinates. The LLM receives these text blocks with spatial positions and returns structural regions as (bounding box, class, confidence score) tuples; the teacher detector outputs standard DETR-style class-and-box predictions. The fused output is a set of refined pseudo-labels (bounding boxes with layout class labels and calibrated confidence scores) used to train a student detector that ultimately produces standard COCO-format detection results across 5 (PubLayNet) or 11 (DocLayNet) layout categories.

## Method

The core mechanism fuses teacher detector predictions with LLM-inferred structural regions through inverse-variance weighting: matched pairs (aligned via IoU ≥ τ and class compatibility) are combined as a precision-weighted average of bounding boxes, while confidence scores are merged as a geometric mean in logit space after temperature calibration. A lightweight 64K-parameter MLP gate predicts instance-specific fusion weights from a 3-dimensional sufficient statistic (teacher confidence, LLM confidence, IoU), improving over fixed weights by +0.9 AP. A cross-modal consistency loss aligns visual decoder query features with frozen CLIP text embeddings of the corresponding OCR content, providing auxiliary supervision robust to pseudo-label noise. Training follows a curriculum (teacher-only → fused → LLM-only soft labels) with EMA teacher updates and pseudo-label regeneration every 2 epochs. Theoretically, the paper derives a data-dependent PAC bound showing the gating function operates in a low-dimensional complementarity space (k≈22 vs. d=64K parameters), explaining sample-efficient learning with only 26K labeled samples.

## Results

On PubLayNet with only 5% labeled data, the lightweight SwiftFormer variant (26M params) achieves 88.2±0.3 AP with adaptive fusion, outperforming all semi-supervised baselines by at least +2.9 AP. When applied to a document-pretrained LayoutLMv3 teacher (133M params), the framework reaches 89.7±0.4 AP, surpassing standard semi-supervised LayoutLMv3 (89.1±0.4 AP, p=0.02) and essentially matching UDOP (89.8 AP) which requires 100M+ pages of multimodal pretraining. On DocLayNet (11 categories), the method achieves 84.8 macro AP, with the largest gains on semantically distinctive classes: captions (+8.4 AP), headers (+7.2 AP), and titles (+6.8 AP) over the supervised baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method decisively outperforms all semi-supervised detection baselines: Dense Teacher (85.3 AP, +2.9 AP gap), STEP-DETR (84.8 AP, +3.4 AP), SoftTeacher (84.1 AP, +4.1 AP), and Mean Teacher (83.5 AP, +4.7 AP) on the lightweight variant. Against document-pretrained models, the LayoutLMv3-based variant (89.7 AP) surpasses standard semi-supervised LayoutLMv3 (89.1 AP, p=0.02) and essentially matches UDOP (89.8 AP) without requiring 100M+ pages of multimodal pretraining. The method also beats heavyweight pretrained detectors DocFormer (87.9 AP), DiT (88.1 AP), and Donut (86.4 AP) while using 5× fewer parameters and 2–3× less training time. GPT-4V zero-shot (74.3 AP) is substantially outperformed, confirming that end-to-end VLMs do not yet solve layout detection without task-specific training. On DocLayNet, the method achieves 84.8 macro AP vs. 81.2 for SoftTeacher, with the largest per-class gains on captions (+6.7 AP over SoftTeacher) and headers (+5.6 AP), validating that LLM structural priors are most valuable for semantically ambiguous categories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends semi-supervised detection frameworks (Mean Teacher, SoftTeacher, STEP-DETR, Dense Teacher) by injecting LLM-derived structural priors into the pseudo-label refinement loop, rather than relying solely on visual teacher predictions. It departs from unified document pretraining approaches (UDOP, LayoutLMv3) that require 100M+ pages of multimodal data by instead using off-the-shelf text-pretrained LLMs as lightweight structural prior generators fused via inverse-variance weighting. The theoretical contribution draws on classical estimation theory (minimum-variance unbiased estimation) and adapts it to the cross-modal setting with a learned gating network, providing data-dependent PAC bounds that exploit the low-dimensional complementarity structure (k=22) between visual and textual predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that organizations without access to massive document corpora or multimodal pretraining infrastructure can achieve near-UDOP-level performance (89.7 vs 89.8 AP) using only 5% labels and a $12 API budget, with open-source LLMs (Llama-3-70B) enabling privacy-preserving deployment. Limitations include dependence on OCR quality (performance degrades ≤4 AP across quality buckets), non-Latin script handling (12.4% of errors), and the PAC bound being 3× looser than observed gaps. Open questions include whether the fusion framework generalizes to other structured prediction tasks beyond document layout, whether smaller LLMs (7B) can close the remaining 0.3–0.4 AP gap to commercial models, and whether the low-dimensional complementarity structure (k=22) holds for more complex or multilingual document domains.
