## Problem

In existing 3D visual grounding methods, relative spatial relations between target and anchor objects are tightly entangled with entity semantics, causing models to exploit lexical shortcuts from object names rather than genuine geometric reasoning. This tight coupling degrades generalization under multi-view and complex multi-object scenarios where the same relational predicate (e.g., 'closest to') refers to different objects. The paper asks whether explicitly decoupling relational geometry from entity semantics can yield more robust and generalizable 3D grounding.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D point cloud segmented into object instances (1,024 points uniformly subsampled per object, up to 52 objects per scene) and a free-form natural language query (max 24 tokens) as input. It produces a 3D bounding box for the target object along with auxiliary anchor predictions: a Transformer decoder outputs dedicated slots for k anchors plus the target, each refined by a regression head. A scene-level 'ultra-object' token derived from averaged pairwise edge geometry provides a unified scale and viewpoint prior that conditions all downstream relation encoding.

## Method

ORD introduces three coupled mechanisms. First, an anchor-centric relative geometry encoder builds a directed scene graph over object proposals, representing each edge by unit direction and normalized length, and aggregates these into per-object relation features augmented by a scene-level ultra-object token that calibrates scale and mitigates reference-frame drift. Second, a predicate-decoupled masking strategy replaces all non-relational tokens (entity names, attributes) with MASK so the text encoder sees only spatial predicates (e.g., 'on the right side of'), and two InfoNCE contrastive objectives—text-to-relation and feature-to-relation—align these pure predicate embeddings with the geometric relation features, suppressing lexical leakage. Third, an Anchor-Guided Regression (AGR) module predicts auxiliary anchor indices via a linear head, samples the corresponding anchor features, and feeds them jointly with aggregated scene features into a Transformer decoder that outputs dedicated anchor and target slots refined by a final regression head, explicitly injecting target-anchor priors to disambiguate complex multi-object scenes.

## Results

On NR3D, ORD achieves 71.6% overall accuracy (76.4% Easy, 67.0% Hard, 69.8% View-Dep., 72.5% View-Indep.), surpassing the strongest baseline ViewSRD (69.9%) by +1.7% and CoT3DRef (64.4%) by +7.2%. On SR3D, ORD reaches 76.2% overall (78.4% Easy, 71.0% Hard, 64.2% View-Dep., 76.8% View-Indep.), edging out ViewSRD (76.0%) and MiKASA (75.2%). On ranking metrics, ORD attains MRR=0.81, MR=1.85, and MedR=1.01, substantially outperforming ViewSRD (MRR=0.69, MR=3.11) and CoT3DRef (MRR=0.65, MR=4.29).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORD outperforms all 12 baselines on NR3D overall accuracy, with the largest margins over absolute-position-only methods: +17.1% over 3D-SPS (51.5%), +17.0% over BUTD-DETR (54.6%), and +7.2% over CoT3DRef (64.4%). Among relative-geometry methods, ORD surpasses MiKASA (64.4%) by +7.2%, MA2TransVG (65.2%) by +6.4%, and ViL3DRel (64.4%) by +7.2%. On SR3D, the gap narrows: ORD (76.2%) leads ViewSRD (76.0%) by only +0.2% and MiKASA (75.2%) by +1.0%, suggesting SR3D's templated language makes absolute-position methods more competitive. On viewpoint-dependent relations (left/right/front/behind), ORD beats ViewSRD on all four categories, with a notable +7.2% gain on 'behind' (70.5% vs. 63.3%). However, on viewpoint-independent relations, ORD loses to ViewSRD on 'farthest' (59.7% vs. 64.6%) and 'above' (70.5% vs. 80.4%), while winning decisively on 'under' (+7.8%) and 'between' (+3.3%). On ranking metrics, ORD's MRR of 0.81 and MedR of 1.01 indicate near-perfect target ranking, far exceeding ViewSRD (MRR 0.69, MedR 1.08) and CoT3DRef (MRR 0.65, MedR 1.96).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of three existing lines in 3D visual grounding: (i) relative-geometry encoding methods (ViL3DRel, 3DVGTransformer) that fuse spatial relations with entity semantics in a single pathway, (ii) entity-relation decoupling at the representation level (TransRefer3D, G3-LQ) that still relies on global-coordinate modeling, and (iii) anchor-aware reasoning (CoT3DRef, MiKASA) that treats anchors as auxiliary supervision. Its key departure is the predicate-decoupled masking strategy, which strips entity names from the text stream entirely and aligns only spatial predicates with geometric relation features via InfoNCE contrastive objectives—a cleaner separation than prior role-tagging or attention-based approaches. The ultra-object token borrows the 'bone/ultra-bone' normalization idea from 3D human pose estimation (KasportsFormer) and repurposes it as a scene-level scale/viewpoint prior for object-relation encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The predicate-decoupling framing offers a principled remedy for the well-known shortcut problem in 3DVG where models exploit entity-name co-occurrence rather than genuine spatial reasoning, and the approach is conceptually simple enough to be adopted in other cross-modal grounding settings. However, the method's reliance on explicit anchor annotations (CoT3DRef-style) limits its applicability to datasets without such supervision, and evaluation is confined to the ReferIt3D/ScanRefer family (NR3D, SR3D), leaving open whether the decoupling strategy transfers to open-vocabulary or unconstrained-language benchmarks. The notably weaker performance on 'farthest' (59.7%) versus 'closest' (73.4%) suggests the relative-geometry encoding may not fully capture all relational semantics, pointing to room for improvement in how negation and superlative relations are modeled.
