## Problem

Existing 3D human-object interaction (HOI) methods treat reconstruction (recovering observed geometry) and generation (synthesizing plausible future interactions) as independent tasks, causing reconstruction models to lack semantic reasoning and generation models to violate geometric and physical consistency. This separation prevents the model from leveraging the shared spatial intelligence—maintaining geometric consistency, ensuring semantic coherence, and reasoning about 3D spatial relations—that underlies both perception and prediction of human-object engagement.

## Input / Output

For reconstruction, the method takes a single RGB image and retrieves a category-matched 3D object mesh from ShapeNet; both are converted to 2k-point clouds in unified camera coordinates. For generation, it accepts a natural-language instruction and a VQ-VAE-tokenized motion sequence. Outputs include a dense contact probability field over the combined human and object point clouds, refined 3D human and object meshes with physically consistent alignment, and a decoded motion sequence. The contact field is thresholded to yield candidate contact sets, which are further refined by the reasoning trace mechanism into final bijective contact pairs.

## Method

The framework encodes both tasks into a shared latent space: PointNet++ extracts hierarchical geometric features from human and object point clouds, which are projected (along with image features for reconstruction or text + VQ-VAE motion tokens for generation) into a unified embedding aligned via contrastive and cross-modal losses. An LLM decoder then performs 3D Contact Reasoning (3CR) directly on point clouds—avoiding 2D-to-3D lifting ambiguities—and a Reasoning Trace Mechanism (RTM) iteratively refines contact predictions by integrating local surface proximity with action-specific semantic priors. A modified Gravity-Field Based Diffusion Bridge (GBDB) formulates fine-grained contact refinement as a stochastic differential equation that pulls human points toward the object surface under a learned potential field while enforcing SMPL-X anatomical constraints and surface-normal alignment, solved via Euler-Maruyama with adaptive step size. Training combines binary cross-entropy contact loss, contrastive geometric-textual alignment, a reasoning-path regularization term, and the diffusion bridge geometric loss, with the motion generation component pretrained and then frozen during contact-reasoning training.

## Results

On the DAMON benchmark for human contact prediction, ReGenHOI achieves an F1 of 78.4%, Precision of 77.8%, Recall of 78.6%, and Geodesic distance of 2.65 cm, outperforming the previous best method InteractVLM (F1 75.6%, Geodesic 2.89 cm) by 2.8 F1 points. On the FullBodyManipulation dataset for HOI motion generation, the method attains HandJPE of 26.91, MPJPE of 16.28, contact F1 of 0.79, and FID of 1.02, surpassing SemGeoMo (F1 0.77, FID 1.17) and OMOMO (F1 0.75, FID 1.98). On PICO for 3D reconstruction, it achieves the lowest Procrustes-aligned Chamfer Distance (5.42 cm for human, 12.68 cm for object), interaction volume (0.87 cm³), and penetration depth (2.08 mm) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReGenHOI outperforms all eight baselines across both reconstruction and generation tasks. On DAMON contact prediction, it beats InteractVLM [11] by 2.8 F1 points (78.4 vs. 75.6) and reduces geodesic error from 2.89 to 2.65 cm; the gap over DECO [38] is dramatic (78.4 vs. 55.0 F1), reflecting the advantage of direct 3D reasoning over 2D-to-3D lifting. BSTRO [19] trails by over 32 F1 points, confirming that older contact estimation approaches are substantially outperformed. On FullBodyManipulation motion generation, ReGenHOI achieves the best HandJPE (26.91 vs. 27.84 for SemGeoMo [8]), highest contact F1 (0.79 vs. 0.77), and lowest FID (1.02 vs. 1.17), while also leading in diversity (10.42 vs. 10.15) and Fréchet Score (0.44 vs. 0.57). On BEHAVE, the same pattern holds with F1 of 0.76 vs. 0.74 for SemGeoMo and FID of 1.38 vs. 1.47. For 3D reconstruction on PICO, ReGenHOI reduces interaction volume from 2.78 to 0.87 cm³ and penetration depth from 3.15 to 2.08 mm compared to InteractVLM, demonstrating the physical plausibility gains from the Gravity-Field Based Diffusion Bridge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D HOI reconstruction line (DECO, InteractVLM) by replacing 2D-to-3D lifting with direct 3D contact reasoning on point clouds, and extends the HOI motion generation line (OMOMO, SemGeoMo) by grounding generation in a shared latent space with reconstruction. The Gravity-Field Based Diffusion Bridge is adapted from the authors' own prior work [43], and the LLM-based reasoning backbone builds on MotionGPT and the LLaVA+SAM feature-lifting strategy introduced in InteractVLM. The key conceptual departure is treating reconstruction and generation as two views of a single semantic-geometric reasoning process rather than independent objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified latent-space formulation offers a principled way to ensure that generated motions are geometrically grounded in observed contact structure, and vice versa, which could reduce the physical inconsistencies that plague current HOI generators. However, the practical gains over strong baselines (InteractVLM, SemGeoMo) are modest (≈2–3 F1 points, small FID reductions), and the framework's reliance on SMPL-X, ShapeNet object retrieval, and 8×A100 training limits immediate deployability. Open questions include whether the shared-space benefit persists with larger, more diverse object categories, whether the reasoning-trace mechanism scales to multi-object or multi-person scenes, and whether the diffusion-bridge refinement can operate in real time.
