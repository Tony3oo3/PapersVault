## Problem

Existing watermark removal attacks either operate in pixel space (ineffective against semantic watermarks) or rely on heavy latent-space diffusion purification that degrades visual quality, and many require privileged access to detectors or training data. This paper exposes a fundamental gap: watermarks robust to pixel-space and frequency-domain manipulations remain vulnerable to semantic-preserving viewpoint transformations, a class of attack that has not been systematically explored.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 512×512 RGB watermarked image as input and requires no knowledge of the watermarking scheme, detector, or original generation prompt (empty text prompts are used). It produces a watermark-free image at the same resolution, though the output involves a small spatial shift (diagonal translation of 24–32 pixels) due to the latent viewpoint modulation, so pixel-level fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM) are computed only over the overlapping region. The entire pipeline runs in a zero-shot manner on a frozen pre-trained Stable Diffusion image-to-image model on a single GPU.

## Method

RAVEN reframes watermark removal as novel view synthesis by applying three stages in the latent space of a frozen diffusion model. First, partial DDIM inversion maps the watermarked image to a noisy latent z_τ at an intermediate timestep τ = ⌊s·T⌋, preserving semantic structure while exposing the watermark-entangled representation. Second, latent viewpoint modulation applies a global spatial warping (camera translation) to z_τ, coherently displacing scene structures to disrupt watermark alignment without altering semantics. Third, a view-guided correspondence attention mechanism replaces standard self-attention in the UNet: at each denoising step, queries are computed from the transformed latent while keys and values come from a reference latent denoised in parallel from the untransformed z_τ, anchoring reconstruction to the original appearance. A final CIELAB color and contrast transfer corrects residual chromatic shifts. The entire pipeline is zero-shot, requiring no training, no detector access, and no watermark-specific knowledge.

## Results

On MS-COCO, RAVEN achieves an average TPR@1%FPR of 0.026 across six in-generation semantic watermarking methods (Tree-Ring, Zodiac, HSTR, RingID, HSQR, ROBIN), outperforming the strongest baseline UnMarker (0.078) by over 60%. For seven post-hoc bitstream methods, RAVEN reaches 0.533 average Bit Accuracy (0.5 = full randomization), again beating UnMarker (0.573). In terms of perceptual quality, RAVEN attains the best FID of 40.18 on MS-COCO and 47.11 on DiffusionDB, with the highest CLIP-Text scores (0.328 and 0.350 respectively), and the best PSNR of 25.34 dB under bounded global translation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAVEN outperforms all 14 baselines on watermark suppression. Against the strongest prior method UnMarker [Kassis, S&P 25], RAVEN reduces average TPR@1%FPR from 0.078 to 0.026 on MS-COCO (a 67% relative improvement) and from 0.083 to 0.031 on SD-Prompts, while also achieving lower Bit Accuracy (0.533 vs. 0.573). CtrlGen+ [Liu, ICLR 25] is the second-strongest baseline (0.585 TPR) but requires 8-GPU multi-node training and still leaves RingID and HSQR undetectable (TPR = 1.000), whereas RAVEN suppresses all six semantic methods below 0.07. Regen [Zhao, NeurIPS 24] and its iterative variant Rinse achieve only moderate suppression (0.898 and 0.856 TPR) and introduce visible artifacts (FID 42.36 and 47.92). VAE-based compression baselines (VAE-B [Ballé], VAE-C [Cheng]) and classical BM3D denoising [Dabov] are largely ineffective against semantic watermarks (TPR > 0.87) and degrade quality substantially. Notably, RAVEN also achieves the best FID (40.18) and highest CLIP score (0.328) among all methods on MS-COCO, meaning it wins on both removal efficacy and perceptual quality simultaneously.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on latent diffusion models (Stable Diffusion) as a frozen backbone but departs conceptually from prior watermark removal work by reframing the problem as novel view synthesis rather than signal erasure or noise purification. Prior regeneration attacks (Regen, Rinse, CtrlGen+) suppress watermarks by injecting substantial noise and re-synthesizing, which degrades structural fidelity, while optimization-based methods (UnMarker) require iterative per-image optimization. RAVEN instead applies a small, coherent spatial shift in latent space (24–32 pixel global translation) combined with a view-guided correspondence attention mechanism that anchors denoising to the original scene, borrowing the conceptual framing of NVS without requiring multi-view training data or 3D consistency modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core finding—that watermarks engineered to survive compression, noise, cropping, and denoising are defeated by a semantically-preserving viewpoint shift—exposes a gap in current robustness evaluation protocols and motivates redesign of watermarking schemes to be invariant to geometric re-observation. The attack is zero-shot, black-box, and runs in seconds on a single GPU, making it practically deployable. Open questions include whether watermarking can be made robust to viewpoint invariance without sacrificing detectability, whether the approach extends to video or 3D content, and whether the simple global-translation formulation generalizes to more complex camera motions or scene-level edits.
