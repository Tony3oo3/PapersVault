## Problem

Single-image 3D human reconstruction has advanced significantly for individual subjects, but multi-person crowd scenes remain largely unexplored. In dense crowds, severe inter-person occlusions produce incomplete person crops, low per-person resolution degrades high-frequency detail recovery, and the sheer number of individuals demands a unified, efficient pipeline rather than sequential per-person processing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262573/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single in-the-wild RGB crowd image of arbitrary resolution as input. It first estimates per-person SMPL-X poses and camera-space positions via multi-person HMR, then segments each individual with SAM to yield N (often occluded) person crops. The output is a coherent multi-person 3D Gaussian Splatting scene with complete geometry and high-fidelity view-dependent appearance, renderable from arbitrary novel viewpoints.

## Method

CrowdGaussian is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 (LORM) adapts the pre-trained LHM large human reconstruction model via a self-supervised teacher-student scheme: a frozen teacher renders clean multi-view pseudo-ground truths from complete images, while a student with LoRA modules injected only into the MBHT transformer learns to hallucinate complete 3D Gaussians from occluded crops, trained with a self-distillation loss (L2 + SSIM) over 24 rendered views. Stage 2 (CrowdRefiner) is a single-step diffusion model built on SD-Turbo, conditioned on the coarse RGB rendering and an SMPL normal map, trained with Self-Calibrated Learning (SCL) that randomly mixes degradation-to-clean pairs with identity-preserving (GT, GT) samples to prevent over-refinement and facial distortion. The refined 2D outputs serve as pseudo-ground truths that are distilled back into the 3D Gaussians via iterative differentiable rendering optimization (L1 + SSIM loss), transferring generative detail into the volumetric representation.

## Results

On THuman2.1 with randomly applied occlusion masks, the full CrowdGaussian pipeline (LORM + CrowdRefiner) achieves PSNR 18.619, SSIM 0.931, and LPIPS 0.914, outperforming IDOL (18.063 / 0.919 / 0.994) and LHM (18.171 / 0.918 / 1.012) on all three metrics. Under increasing occlusion ratios (20%→60%), LORM maintains stable PSNR (18.428→18.116) while IDOL degrades sharply (18.196→16.667) and LHM drops moderately (17.945→17.551), confirming superior occlusion robustness. The ablation in Table 3 shows that Self-Calibrated Learning (SCL) and SMPL normal-map conditioning each contribute incremental gains, with the full configuration reaching PSNR 20.790 vs. 20.013 without either.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrowdGaussian (LORM + CrowdRefiner) wins on all three metrics against both quantitative baselines: it improves PSNR by +0.556 dB over IDOL and +0.448 dB over LHM, while reducing LPIPS by 0.080 and 0.098 respectively. LORM alone already surpasses both baselines (PSNR 18.566 vs. 18.063/18.171), indicating that the self-supervised occlusion adaptation is the primary driver of the gain, with CrowdRefiner providing an additional ~0.05 dB PSNR boost and a notable LPIPS improvement (0.956→0.914). Under 60% occlusion, LORM retains PSNR 18.116 while IDOL drops to 16.667 (−1.53 dB) and LHM to 17.551 (−0.39 dB), highlighting a clear robustness advantage. PSHuman and SyncHuman are compared only qualitatively (Figure 7); both mesh-based methods fail to recover complete geometry under occlusion, producing visible transparency artifacts, whereas CrowdGaussian yields structurally complete and texture-coherent reconstructions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends single-image 3D human reconstruction (LHM, IDOL) from isolated, well-lit subjects to the multi-person crowd setting, where severe inter-person occlusions and low per-person resolution are the norm. It builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the scene representation and on recent diffusion-based 3DGS refinement methods (DIFIX, GSFix3D, GSFIXER) for detail enhancement, but departs from them by introducing a self-supervised teacher-student adaptation (LORM) that requires no 3D annotations and a Self-Calibrated Learning strategy that mixes identity-preserving pairs with degradation pairs to prevent over-refinement. The pipeline also leverages off-the-shelf multi-person HMR and SAM for pose estimation and segmentation, positioning itself as a unified framework rather than a concatenation of individually trained modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Crowd scenes are a prevalent yet largely unaddressed scenario for 3D human reconstruction, with direct implications for VR, teleconferencing, and digital content creation. The self-supervised adaptation and SCL strategy are practical, annotation-free techniques that could transfer to other occlusion-robust reconstruction or refinement tasks. However, the method inherits errors from upstream pose/shape estimators, and hallucinated details in heavily occluded or low-resolution regions may not be factually faithful (e.g., specific logos or patterns), leaving open the question of how to verify identity consistency in unobserved regions. Scalability to very large crowds (hundreds of people) and to dynamic/animated scenes remains unexplored.
