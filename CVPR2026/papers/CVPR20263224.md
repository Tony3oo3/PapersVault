## Problem

Sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting suffers from a fundamental geometry-appearance discrepancy: with limited observations, appearance overfits individual training views by compensating for geometric errors, while 3D geometry remains severely underconstrained, producing floaters and blurriness on novel views. Existing remedies either rely on external monocular depth priors (introducing scale ambiguity and noise) or fail to maintain geometric accuracy throughout iterative optimization, leaving the intrinsic coupling between geometry and appearance unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263224/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 3, 6, or 9 sparse RGB training views (downsampled 8×, 4×, or 2× for LLFF, DTU, and Blender respectively) along with camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation that renders novel-view RGB images and depth maps in real time. It builds on the BinocularGS framework with dense point-cloud initialization for LLFF/DTU and random initialization for Blender, and uses a frozen Cascade Cost Volume feature network (precomputed once) for illumination-robust matching, incurring negligible per-iteration overhead.

## Method

ICO-GS enforces intrinsic geometry-appearance consistency through two synergistic components trained via a three-stage curriculum. First, robust geometric regularization warps pixels between training views using rendered depth and enforces feature-based multi-view photometric consistency with pixel-wise top-k selection to handle occlusions, supplemented by edge-aware depth smoothness for monocularly visible regions. Second, geometry-guided appearance optimization validates rendered depth via cycle-consistency filtering (forward-backward reprojection with a depth-error threshold), then synthesizes virtual views from the filtered depth to provide photometric supervision that prevents appearance overfitting. The curriculum progresses from vanilla 3DGS loss (Stage 1), to geometric regularization (Stage 2), to virtual-view appearance loss (Stage 3), ensuring stable convergence while progressively tightening the geometry-appearance coupling.

## Results

On LLFF, ICO-GS achieves 22.20 dB PSNR at 3 views (+0.76 dB over BinocularGS), 25.37 dB at 6 views (+0.17 dB over ComapGS), and 26.45 dB at 9 views (2nd place, 0.28 dB behind ComapGS). On DTU, it leads all baselines across every setting: 21.77 dB (3-view, +1.06 dB over BinocularGS), 25.09 dB (6-view, +0.58 dB over CoR-GS), and 27.19 dB (9-view, +0.01 dB over CoR-GS). On Blender (8 views), ICO-GS achieves the best PSNR while trading slightly lower SSIM/LPIPS, reflecting its prioritization of geometric accuracy over perceptual metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among 3DGS-based sparse-view methods, ICO-GS most directly competes with BinocularGS (its base framework), ComapGS, CoR-GS, and DropGaussians. On LLFF, it outperforms BinocularGS by +0.76 dB at 3 views and +0.50 dB at 6 views, and beats ComapGS by +0.17 dB at 6 views, though ComapGS retains a 0.28 dB PSNR edge at 9 views. On DTU, ICO-GS dominates all 3DGS baselines at every view count, with the largest margin of +1.06 dB over BinocularGS at 3 views narrowing to a near-tie (+0.01 dB) against CoR-GS at 9 views. All NeRF-based baselines (DietNeRF, RegNeRF, FreeNeRF, SparseNeRF) are substantially outperformed by 2–7 dB in PSNR, confirming the advantage of explicit 3DGS representations for sparse-view synthesis. The feed-forward MVSplat baseline on DTU lags significantly (17.33 dB at 3 views vs. 21.77 dB), highlighting the gap between per-scene optimization and feed-forward approaches under extreme sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the BinocularGS framework [15] for sparse-view 3DGS, inheriting its binocular consistency loss and dense initialization, while introducing two new components: feature-based multi-view photometric regularization with pixel-wise top-k occlusion handling, and cycle-consistency depth filtering to validate rendered depth before virtual-view appearance supervision. It departs from prior sparse-view 3DGS methods (FSGS, DNGaussian, NexusGS) that rely on external monocular depth priors with inherent scale ambiguity, and from CoR-GS/ComapGS that use co-regularization or covisibility maps, by grounding the solution in an explicit geometry-appearance consistency principle rather than ad-hoc regularization. The feature matching leverages a frozen Cascade Cost Volume network [14], and the curriculum learning schedule [4] stages the activation of geometric and appearance losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean conceptual diagnosis—'intrinsic geometry-appearance consistency'—for why sparse-view 3DGS fails, and a practical solution that avoids external depth priors entirely, which is appealing for deployment where pretrained depth models introduce noise and scale ambiguity. The cycle-consistency depth filtering is a simple but effective mechanism that prevents the circular dependency problem identified in BinocularGS (unreliable depth → corrupted virtual views → worse geometry). Limitations include the assumption of view-independent appearance during virtual view synthesis, which restricts applicability to scenes with strong specular or reflective surfaces. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, whether the top-k selection and cycle-consistency thresholds generalize across very different scene scales, and whether the curriculum schedule is optimal or could be replaced by adaptive weighting.
