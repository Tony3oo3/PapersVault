## Problem

Existing CLIP-based continual learning methods overlook Asymmetric Drift: the visual encoder undergoes substantially larger updates than the text encoder because visual inputs shift dramatically across tasks while textual prompts remain nearly static. This imbalance causes visual and textual features to diverge in the feature space over time, increasing modality distance and progressively degrading cross-modal alignment and classification accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images and handcrafted text prompts of the form 'a photo of [CLS]' as input, producing a class label via cosine similarity in the CCA-aligned subspace. CLIP (ViT-B/16) is frozen after a brief first-task fine-tuning, so no exemplars or replay buffers are needed; the method only accumulates D×D covariance matrices across tasks. The RFP module expands features into a 6144-dimensional space before CCA projection, and the final classification operates on the projected features rather than raw CLIP embeddings.

## Method

CCA-CL accumulates cross-task covariance statistics (Σxx, Σyy, Σxy) between visual and textual features extracted by a frozen CLIP, then solves a closed-form CCA problem via eigen-decomposition of the whitened cross-covariance to obtain projection matrices A and B that define a shared subspace maximizing visual-textual correlation. To capture nonlinear dependencies beyond linear CCA, a Random Fourier Projection (φ(z) = √(2/D′)cos(Wz+b)) is applied to both modalities before covariance accumulation, implicitly approximating an RBF kernel without trainable parameters. A top-r energy criterion (η=0.99) selects the most informative canonical directions, keeping the subspace compact (≈47 dimensions on average). The pipeline includes a First-Task Training stage where CLIP is lightly fine-tuned (lr=1e-6, 5 epochs) on the first task before being fully frozen, balancing downstream adaptation with preservation of pre-trained knowledge. At inference, both image features and text prototypes are mapped through RFP and projected into the CCA subspace, and classification is performed by cosine similarity in this aligned space.

## Results

CCA-CL achieves the best Last Accuracy on all four benchmarks: 83.0% on CIFAR-100, 81.3% on ImageNet-R, 80.2% on ImageNet-100, and 79.7% on CUB-200, all without using exemplars or replay. In terms of Average Accuracy, CCA-CL leads on CIFAR-100 (87.2%), ImageNet-R (86.8%), and CUB-200 (86.3%), while on ImageNet-100 it trails slightly behind replay-based CLAP (87.3%) and RAPF (87.5%). The training-free variant (CCA-CL without first-task fine-tuning) still reaches 81.0% Last on CIFAR-100 in only 3.9 minutes, outperforming most competing methods. Overall, CCA-CL outperforms the second-best exemplar-free method (MagMax) by up to 3.8% in Last Accuracy on CIFAR-100.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCA-CL (exemplar-free) outperforms all other exemplar-free methods across nearly all metrics. On CIFAR-100, it beats the strongest exemplar-free competitor MagMax [25] by 1.6% in Avg (87.2 vs. 85.6) and 4.0% in Last (83.0 vs. 79.0), and surpasses L2P [38] and DualPrompt [37] by over 5% in Last Accuracy. Against replay-based methods, CCA-CL still leads in Last Accuracy on all four datasets: it exceeds ENGINE [48] (79.2) by 3.8% on CIFAR-100, PROOF [49] (79.3) by 0.4% on CUB-200, and RAPF [16] (80.2) by 0.1% on ImageNet-100. The only metric where CCA-CL trails is Avg Accuracy on ImageNet-100, where CLAP [18] (87.3) and RAPF [16] (87.5) edge it out by 0.4–0.6%, both relying on pseudo-feature replay. The training-free variant (no FTT) remains competitive, ranking second on CIFAR-100 Last (81.0) and matching or exceeding most baselines on the other datasets, confirming that the CCA subspace alignment itself provides most of the gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes Hotelling's (1936) Canonical Correlation Analysis—a classical closed-form statistical method—for the modern problem of CLIP-based class-incremental learning, a setting where prior work has focused on learned projection modules (L2P, DualPrompt, PROOF, RAPF) or sequential fine-tuning of CLIP parameters (MagMax, SLCA, ENGINE). It departs from these approaches by identifying 'Asymmetric Drift' (unequal visual vs. textual encoder updates) as the root cause of cross-modal misalignment and addressing it through a shared CCA subspace rather than parameter updates or replay. The Random Fourier Projection extension draws on Rahimi & Recht's (2007) random features for kernel approximation to inject nonlinearity without additional trainable parameters, positioning the method as a training-free statistical alignment layer on top of a frozen CLIP backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The exemplar-free, closed-form nature of CCA-CL makes it attractive for privacy-sensitive or memory-constrained continual learning deployments, and the ~10× speedup over competing methods (5.8 min vs. 80+ min) is practically significant for iterative or resource-limited settings. However, the method still requires a first-task fine-tuning stage, and the linear CCA core—despite the RFP extension—may struggle with highly nonlinear or long-horizon task sequences where the accumulated covariance statistics become stale. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-modal (beyond image-text) continual settings, whether the energy-threshold subspace selection remains stable over very long task streams, and whether parameter-efficient tuning (e.g., LoRA) could replace the first-task fine-tuning entirely.
