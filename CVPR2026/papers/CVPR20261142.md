## Problem

Physics-based reconstruction of human-object dynamics from monocular video remains under-explored because prior methods either assume motion is driven solely by gravity and ground-plane contacts—omitting the internal actuation generated by human motion—or rely on idealized homogeneous, isotropic constitutive laws that underfit the heterogeneous and anisotropic materials found in real scenes. PhysHO addresses both gaps by explicitly modeling human-driven internal forces and learning spatially varying, anisotropic material behavior from a single video.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261142/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single static-camera monocular video (1080p, 30 FPS) of a human interacting with a deformable object, along with SMPL pose sequences estimated by off-the-shelf tools. The video is split into a spin stage (for canonical 3D Gaussian reconstruction) and a dynamic stage (for physics learning), and the dynamic stage is further divided into observation and prediction segments. Outputs include a mass-preserving fixed-count 3D Gaussian set that doubles as MPM particles, learned per-particle material parameters (Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, latent features), rendered frames, and the ability to simulate future motion or novel human actions by feeding new SMPL poses into the trained simulator.

## Method

PhysHO tightly couples SMPL-driven Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) with a Material Point Method (MPM) simulator, using LBS as an interpretable actuation prior and MPM as the physics engine that propagates forces through contact. A PD controller driven by LBS reference trajectories injects forces, which are gated per-particle by a learnable LBS-impact factor (ω^i) so that only particles strictly inside the SMPL volume receive direct actuation while object particles move solely through physical contact. Material behavior is modeled by spatially varying Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio augmented with neural residual constitutive laws (elasticity E_θ and plasticity P_θ) conditioned on per-particle latents, layered on top of expert elastic-plastic backbones to capture heterogeneity and anisotropy while preserving stability. Training is stabilized by first optimizing per-frame particle positions with RGB, optical-flow, and ARAP losses to obtain structure-preserving 3D flow supervision, then performing progressive loss-balanced end-to-end optimization that allocates more iterations to harder frames.

## Results

On the PhysHO dataset (8 sequences, 6 objects), PhysHO achieves the best LPIPS across most reconstruction sequences (e.g., 0.0676 on C-shape Pillow #1 Full, 0.0804 on Cloth Bag Full) while remaining competitive on PSNR and SSIM. For future prediction, PhysHO consistently outperforms GART on LPIPS (e.g., 0.0788 vs. 0.0893 on C-shape Pillow #1 Full; 0.0944 vs. 0.1269 on C-shape Pillow #2 10–30% subset) and on PSNR for most sequences. 4D-Gaussian achieves higher PSNR/SSIM on reconstruction (e.g., 29.03 vs. 24.86 on C-shape Pillow #1) due to its ability to overfit pixel-aligned metrics, but cannot produce predictions beyond training frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GART [30], PhysHO wins on LPIPS for reconstruction on 3 of 4 objects (Square Pillow: 0.1079 vs. 0.1282; Cloth Bag: 0.0804 vs. 0.0845; C-Pillow #2: 0.0651 vs. 0.0984) and on all 4 prediction sequences (e.g., C-Pillow #2 10–30%: 0.0944 vs. 0.1269 LPIPS). GART slightly edges PhysHO on SSIM for Square Pillow reconstruction (0.9145 vs. 0.8872) and on PSNR for C-Pillow #1 Full prediction (21.69 vs. 21.12), but these gaps are small. Against 4D-Gaussian [63], PhysHO loses on PSNR and SSIM for reconstruction (e.g., 24.86 vs. 29.03 PSNR on C-Pillow #1) because 4D-Gaus continuously optimizes Gaussians to match ground-truth pixels, enabling overfitting; however, PhysHO wins on LPIPS for 3 of 4 reconstruction sequences, indicating better perceptual and texture fidelity. Critically, 4D-Gaussian cannot produce any future predictions, making it inapplicable to the prediction task where PhysHO demonstrates its physics-based advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysHO sits at the intersection of two previously separate lines of work: kinematic human reconstruction (GART, 4D-Gaussian) and object-centric physics-based reconstruction (PhysGaussian, Pac-NeRF, Gaussim). It repurposes GART's canonical 3D Gaussian + LBS representation but strips out latent bones and learned skinning in favor of fixed SMPL bones, then couples the resulting LBS trajectories as a PD-controller actuation prior into an MPM simulator borrowed from the PhysGaussian lineage. The neural residual constitutive laws extend NCLaw's learned elastic-plastic models with per-particle conditioning in the spirit of NeuMA's LoRA-style residual adaptation, but applied to spatially heterogeneous materials rather than a single pretrained network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first monocular framework that explicitly models human-generated internal actuation in physics-based reconstruction, closing a gap where prior methods either ignored human forces (gravity-only object simulation) or required synthetic 3D supervision (PhysRig). The LBS-impact factor provides a clean, interpretable mechanism for spatially targeted actuation that prevents spurious forces on objects, and the residual constitutive laws offer a practical path to heterogeneous/anisotropic materials without sacrificing simulator stability. Key limitations include the very small dataset (8 sequences, 6 objects), dependence on off-the-shelf SMPL pose estimation, and the absence of comparisons against other physics-based baselines (PhysGaussian, Pac-NeRF, Gaussim). Open questions include scalability to multi-view or longer sequences, real-time inference, and whether the PD-controller + LBS-impact formulation generalizes to more complex multi-contact interactions.
