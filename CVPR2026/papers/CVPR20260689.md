## Problem

Existing video generation methods either predict future frames from a single reference image without visual guidance toward a target state, or interpolate smooth appearance transitions between two images without modeling the semantic, action-induced transformation steps in between. No prior approach combines egocentric scene understanding, reasoning about multi-step physical transformations, and controllable frame generation that preserves object appearance across the transition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260689/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an initial frame I₁, a target frame I_N, and a brief textual action instruction as input, and produces a sequence of N−2 intermediate frames depicting the object's state transition. The TransitionVLM first infers K intermediate steps with associated temporal ranges, which are then used as frame-wise conditions for the diffusion-based generator (built on DC-Interp, sampled with 50 DDIM steps). The generated sequence must remain visually consistent with both anchor frames while following the instructed action.

## Method

EgoIn follows a two-stage divide-and-conquer strategy. First, a TransitionVLM (Qwen2.5-VL 7B fine-tuned with LoRA and separate S-Adapter/T-Adapter modules on GPT-4o-curated data) infers K intermediate transition steps and their temporal ranges from the two anchor frames and action instruction, reducing hallucinated content. Second, a Transition Conditioning module extracts state-aware features via BLIP-inspired SQ-Formers and generates frame-wise transition features by resampling Gaussian weights over the predicted step ranges; these conditions are injected into the denoising U-Net through frame-wise weighted cross-attention. An Object-aware Auxiliary Supervision loss, using a localization head trained on SAM2-generated masks (training only), enforces consistent appearance of manipulated objects throughout the sequence.

## Results

EgoIn achieves the best FVD across all four datasets: 215.27 on Epic100, 203.85 on EgoFHO, 209.63 on DualArm, and 191.09 on Bridge, outperforming the strongest baseline DC-Interp by 27–34 FVD points. It also leads on all VBench metrics (VTQ, VTC, VIC) across every dataset, e.g., reaching VTQ 0.9374 and VIC 0.9511 on Bridge. In a user study with 40 participants, EgoIn received 74.92% preference votes for transition reasonability, 70.85% for instruction alignment, and 77.06% for motion quality, far exceeding the next-best method (DC-Interp at ~11–13%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoIn outperforms all five baselines on every metric across all four datasets. Against the closest competitor DC-Interp (a text-guided frame interpolation model), EgoIn reduces FVD by 27.4 (Epic100), 86.5 (EgoFHO), 88.9 (DualArm), and 96.4 (Bridge) points, while improving VTC by 0.033–0.030 and VIC by 0.023–0.021. The video interpolation methods FILM, TRF, and GI, which lack textual guidance, show substantially higher FVD (325–765) and lower VTC (0.17–0.18), confirming that action-instruction conditioning is critical for semantically meaningful transitions. SEINE, designed for scene transitions, achieves competitive VTC but lags on FVD and VIC, indicating it models appearance blending rather than action-induced state changes. All baselines were fine-tuned on the EIVST training data for a fair comparison, yet EgoIn's advantage persists, suggesting the TransitionVLM + Transition Conditioning pipeline provides a structural benefit beyond data exposure alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoIn builds on the DynamiCrafter/DC-Interp image-to-video diffusion backbone and extends it with a VLM-based reasoning front-end, drawing on the SQ-Former architecture from BLIP for multimodal state alignment. It departs from prior frame interpolation and video transition work (FILM, TRF, GI, SEINE) by requiring explicit semantic reasoning about action-induced multi-step transformations rather than purely visual smoothness, and from text-conditioned video prediction (Seer, AID) by conditioning on two visual anchors plus an instruction rather than a single reference frame. The use of a fine-tuned VLM (TransitionVLM) to infer intermediate steps and their temporal ranges, followed by frame-wise cross-attention injection into the denoising U-Net, is the key architectural departure from prior VLM-guided generation methods that typically decompose prompts into per-frame text descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EIVST formulation is directly relevant to embodied AI and robotic manipulation, where visualizing the step-by-step physical transformation between states is essential for planning and demonstration. The paper's main limitation—acknowledged by the authors—is that long-horizon transitions involving substantial scene or viewpoint changes remain unaddressed, and the current framework assumes a relatively stable egocentric viewpoint. An open question is whether the VLM-inferred transition steps generalize to novel object categories or compositional actions beyond the kitchen and manipulation settings evaluated here, and whether the Gaussian-weighted temporal allocation in the TC module scales gracefully to longer sequences with more complex multi-object interactions.
