## Problem

Existing learning-based multi-view face reconstruction methods (ToFu, TEMPEH, GRAPE) still depend on precomputed manual registrations from classical pipelines for training supervision, creating a circular dependency that undermines their goal of replacing labor-intensive workflows. This paper eliminates that dependency by training directly on raw multi-view scans without any registered ground truth.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260626/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes K calibrated RGB views (8 for FaMoS, 6 for CoMA) with known intrinsics and extrinsics, and optionally a raw unstructured 3D scan at test time for test-time optimization. It outputs a 5023-vertex mesh with fixed FLAME topology, spatially aligned to the captured geometry, along with recovered FLAME shape (β) and expression (ψ) parameters. The 2D landmark detector that provides semantic supervision is itself trained exclusively on 25,000 synthetic Blender renders, requiring no real-image annotations.

## Method

MOCHI uses a two-stage coarse-to-fine architecture (volumetric feature fusion + local refinement) adapted from TEMPEH, but replaces registration-based supervision with three registration-free mechanisms: (1) a PLIKS pseudo-linear inverse kinematic solver that recovers FLAME parameters from predicted vertices and re-forwards them through the parametric model to enforce topology via vertex-position and edge-length losses; (2) differentiable point-map and surface-normal rendering losses with a German-McClure robust penalty, which replace the unstable point-to-surface distance and provide smoother gradients; and (3) dense 2D landmark reprojection losses from a DINOv3-Large + LoRA detector trained on synthetic data, acting as a semantic regularizer for ambiguous regions. At test time, an optional TTO stage fine-tunes only the refinement module for ~50 iterations in the learned latent space, surpassing classical manual registration accuracy without any precomputed correspondences.

## Results

On FaMoS (images only), MOCHI achieves a median point-to-surface error of 0.26 mm for the full head, compared to 0.36 mm for TEMPEH, a ~28% reduction. On CoMA (zero-shot, images only), MOCHI reaches 0.53 mm median versus TEMPEH's 0.81 mm, a ~35% improvement. When a scan is available at test time, MOCHI with 50 iterations of TTO achieves 0.10 mm median error on FaMoS, matching or surpassing the classic manual registration pipeline. Region-specific gains are most pronounced on the face (0.23 vs 0.32 mm median on FaMoS) and lips (0.37 vs 0.47 mm).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is TEMPEH [Bolkart et al., CVPR 2023], the only other open-source learnable multi-view registration method. MOCHI outperforms TEMPEH on both FaMoS (0.26 vs 0.36 mm median, ~28% reduction) and CoMA zero-shot (0.53 vs 0.81 mm median, ~35% reduction) under the images-only setting, despite training without any precomputed registrations. The second comparison is against the classic manual registration pipeline (optimization-based FLAME fitting with semi-manual supervision). With 50 iterations of test-time optimization, MOCHI TTO achieves 0.10 mm median error on FaMoS, matching or slightly surpassing the classic pipeline's 0.10 mm, while eliminating the need for per-subject manual intervention. The paper notes that MOCHI's advantage over TEMPEH is consistent across all sub-regions (face, lips, neck) on both datasets, with the largest relative gains on the face region. On the neck region, MOCHI is slightly worse than TEMPEH on FaMoS (0.46 vs 0.63 mm median) but better on CoMA (1.02 vs 1.26 mm), suggesting the topology enforcement via PLIKS is most beneficial for facial regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOCHI builds directly on TEMPEH's volumetric multi-view fusion architecture (coarse stage, refinement stage, surface-aware feature sampling) and repurposes PLIKS, originally designed for 3D human body estimation, as a differentiable topology regularizer for FLAME meshes. The key conceptual departure from prior learnable multi-view methods (ToFu, TEMPEH, GRAPE, ReFA) is eliminating the dependency on precomputed registrations: instead of supervising with registered ground-truth meshes, MOCHI enforces topology through an inverse-kinematics loop, replaces point-to-surface scan losses with differentiable point-map and normal-map rendering losses, and adds semantic guidance from a synthetic-data-trained dense 2D landmark detector. The test-time optimization stage further distinguishes MOCHI by operating in the learned refinement module's latent space rather than optimizing raw vertices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the manual registration bottleneck, MOCHI makes high-fidelity multi-view face capture substantially more scalable and accessible, potentially enabling rapid dataset creation for downstream animation, VFX, and embodied AI applications. The finding that standard point-to-surface losses induce training instabilities (self-intersections, artifacts) in registration-free settings, while differentiable rendering losses provide smoother gradients, is a practically useful insight for anyone training geometry from scans. Limitations include the additional per-iteration cost of rendering point/normal maps (~60 ms) and the PLIKS solver (~150 ms), and rare failures when scans contain large teeth regions. Open questions include generalization to novel camera rigs beyond the 3dMD setup, handling of visible hair, and whether the approach extends to full-body or non-rigid object capture.
