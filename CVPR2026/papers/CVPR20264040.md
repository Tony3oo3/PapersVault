## Problem

3D intraoral scan (IOS) models frequently lack usable color textures due to impression-plaster-cast workflows or texture loss during export, causing existing geometry-only segmentation methods to fail at fine boundaries such as tight interproximal contacts and tooth-gingiva interfaces. Although intraoral photos are trivially available in clinics and provide rich chromatic and semantic cues, no prior work has fused such photos into a 3D tooth segmentation network.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264040/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a 3D IOS mesh (upper or lower jaw) together with an arbitrary number of intraoral photos (frontal, upper-arch, lower-arch views) captured by professional cameras or smartphones. Before fusion, each photo is pre-segmented by a YOLOv8 model to extract tooth contour points, and the mesh receives a coarse binary tooth-gingiva label to restrict alignment to tooth-surface vertices. The output is a per-vertex instance segmentation mask on the IOS mesh, assigning each vertex to a specific tooth ID or gingiva/background.

## Method

PMTSeg comprises three tightly coupled modules. (1) A Camera Alignment Module (CAM) estimates per-view intrinsics and extrinsics by minimizing a differentiable Chamfer Distance between projected 3D tooth-surface points and YOLOv8-extracted 2D tooth contours, establishing pixel-to-point correspondences. (2) A Feature Filtering Gate (FFG) runs a shared UNet on each photo and a PointNet++ on the mesh, then applies a sigmoid-gated MLP that adaptively weights multi-view 2D features per 3D point based on local geometric context, concatenating the weighted texture features with the original 3D features while zeroing contributions for invisible points. (3) A Consistent Feature Learning (CFL) module enhances visible-point features via cross-attention between 3D queries and 2D keys/values, then trains a learnable sub-network S (geometry-only input) to match the texture-enhanced features through an InfoNCE-style contrastive loss, thereby transferring semantic priors to occluded or texture-less regions. The full network is trained end-to-end with a combined cross-entropy + Dice segmentation loss plus the contrastive term (λ=0.5).

## Results

On the PMTSeg dataset (1,240 IOS samples from 620 patients), PMTSeg achieves 96.17±1.8 mIoU, 98.04±1.0 DSC, and 92.53±2.5 B-IoU, outperforming all seven baselines on every metric. The closest competitor, ATSL, scores 95.28 mIoU and 90.53 B-IoU, yielding PMTSeg a +0.89 mIoU and +2.0 B-IoU advantage. The boundary metric (B-IoU) shows the largest relative gains, confirming that photo-guided fusion most improves the hardest regions—interproximal contacts and tooth-gingiva margins.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMTSeg wins on all three metrics against every baseline. Against the strongest geometry-only method ATSL (95.28 mIoU, 90.53 B-IoU), PMTSeg gains +0.89 mIoU and +2.0 B-IoU, with the boundary improvement being the most clinically meaningful. The adapted cross-modal baseline RPVNet (92.99 mIoU, 86.87 B-IoU) trails PMTSeg by +3.18 mIoU and +5.66 B-IoU, indicating that the CFL consistency mechanism is critical for propagating photo semantics to occluded regions. Geometry-centric methods MeshSegNet (81.41 mIoU) and TSGCNet (74.20 mIoU) show large standard deviations (±7.7 and ±4.8), reflecting instability on ambiguous boundaries. CrossTooth (86.73±10.1 mIoU) exhibits the highest variance, attributed to its dependence on rendering quality. The general-purpose 3D backbone PTv3 (90.30 mIoU) confirms that architectural strength alone cannot compensate for the absence of appearance cues in dental segmentation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of geometry-only 3D tooth segmentation (point-cloud and mesh-based methods like ATSL, TSGCNet, DBGANet) by introducing intraoral photographs as an auxiliary input modality for the first time in dental segmentation. Conceptually, it repurposes cross-modal fusion strategies from autonomous driving (RPVNet's range-point-voxel fusion, camera-LiDAR alignment) and indoor RGB-D segmentation, adapting them to the dental domain with a dedicated Camera Alignment Module and a Feature Filtering Gate for arbitrary multi-view photos. The Consistent Feature Learning mechanism draws on contrastive learning and cross-attention to propagate semantics from photo-visible to photo-invisible 3D regions, a design not previously applied to dental geometry. The paper also contributes a new multi-modal dataset pairing IOS meshes with multi-view intraoral photos, filling a gap where no public benchmark existed for this cross-modal setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PMTSeg addresses a practical and persistent limitation in digital dentistry: most IOS models lack usable texture, causing segmentation failures at tooth-gingiva boundaries and tight interproximal contacts where geometry alone is ambiguous. The new 620-patient multi-modal dataset and the demonstrated boundary improvements (+2 B-IoU over the strongest baseline) make this a useful reference for clinics seeking to leverage routinely-captured photos. However, the approach assumes photo availability and quality, and the authors acknowledge that motion blur and optical distortion remain unaddressed. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to single-photo or smartphone-only settings, how it interacts with CBCT-based volumetric priors for root-level context, and whether the contrastive consistency mechanism can be extended to other texture-deficient 3D medical segmentation tasks.
