## Problem

Existing dual-stream SIRR methods with feature interaction mechanisms suffer from two overlooked gaps: a semantic gap between frozen pre-trained model features and the reflection removal network's learned representations, and a training data gap caused by inconsistent reflection supervision labels (R for synthetic data vs. I−T for real-world data) that undermines generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260256/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 384×384 RGB superimposed image and produces three outputs: the transmission layer, the reflection layer, and a residual term (estimated via a NAFBlock-based residual estimator). The reflection supervision label is unified as the low-frequency component of I−T (obtained via a 2D low-pass filter), while the residual term is directly supervised by the high-frequency remainder I−T−(I−T)_low, ensuring no information is lost in the decomposition.

## Method

GFRRN employs a dual-encoder architecture: a pre-trained Swin-Transformer with inserted Mona adapter layers (PEFT) for global semantic priors, and a dual-stream CNN (from DSIT) for local priors, feeding into a shared decoder. The key insight is bridging the semantic gap by fine-tuning only the lightweight Mona layers (convolution-based multi-cognitive filters) rather than the full pre-trained backbone, which avoids the overfitting of full fine-tuning on limited SIRR data. In the decoder, a Gaussian-based Adaptive Frequency Learning Block (G-AFLB) replaces binary frequency masks with smoothed Gaussian coefficients to adaptively match reflection blurriness, and a Dynamic Agent Attention (DAA) replaces window-based multi-head self-attention by combining agent attention with a window-based importance estimator (WIE) that assigns learned significance weights to each window based on reflection presence.

## Results

GFRRN achieves an average PSNR of 27.33 dB and SSIM of 0.929 across five real-world test datasets (Real20, Object200, Postcard199, Wild55, Nature20), ranking first on every individual dataset for both metrics. This represents a 0.7 dB PSNR and 0.01 SSIM improvement over the previous best method RDNet (26.63 dB, 0.915). The largest per-dataset gains are on Nature20 (+1.18 dB over RDNet) and Wild55 (+0.59 dB), indicating stronger performance on challenging real-world scenes with weak or concealed reflections.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GFRRN outperforms all 11 compared baselines on every dataset and both metrics (PSNR and SSIM). The closest competitor is RDNet (CVPR'25), which achieves 26.63 dB average PSNR; GFRRN surpasses it by 0.7 dB on average, with the largest per-dataset margin on Nature20 (+1.18 dB) and the smallest on Real20 (+0.26 dB). DSIT (NeurIPS'24), the architectural backbone of GFRRN, scores 26.50 dB, so the proposed components (Mona-tuning, unified label, G-AFLB, DAA) collectively contribute +0.83 dB. Among earlier dual-stream methods, DSRNet (ICCV'23) and DExNet (TPAMI'25) trail by 1.61 dB and 1.42 dB respectively. Single-stream methods (ERRNet, IBCLN, RRW) and adversarial-robust methods (RobustSIRR) lag further behind, with RobustSIRR notably weak on Postcard199 (19.91 dB) and Nature20 (20.97 dB), suggesting limited generalization to diverse real-world scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dual-stream SIRR lineage (YTMT, DSRNet, DSIT) that pairs a pre-trained backbone for global semantics with a task-specific CNN for local features. Its primary departure is the introduction of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (Mona-tuning) into the pre-trained Swin-Transformer encoder, addressing a semantic gap that prior methods (and RDNet's full fine-tuning) left unaddressed. A second conceptual contribution is the unified reflection label—replacing the inconsistent R vs. I−T supervision with a low-pass-filtered (I−T)ₗₒw—framed as a plug-and-drop fix applicable to existing architectures like DSIT and DSRNet. The decoder further departs from DSIT by substituting window-based multi-head self-attention with a dynamic agent attention (DAA) and adding a Gaussian-based frequency learning block (G-AFLB).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified label strategy is the most broadly transferable contribution: it is a simple low-pass filter that improves reflection supervision across multiple architectures (DSIT, DSRNet, GFRRN) without architectural changes, making it a low-cost drop-in for the SIRR community. The demonstration that full fine-tuning of a pre-trained Swin-Transformer actively degrades SIRR performance (25.35 dB vs. 26.70 dB frozen baseline) provides a useful cautionary data point for practitioners considering FFT on small-scale restoration datasets. However, the unified label is acknowledged as not rigorously accurate—some genuine reflection content is filtered into the residual term—and the overall gains over the strong DSIT baseline (~0.8 dB PSNR) are incremental. Open questions include whether the frequency-prior and DAA components generalize beyond the 384×384 training resolution and whether the PEFT strategy transfers to other low-level restoration tasks.
