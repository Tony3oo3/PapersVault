## Problem

Existing approaches to improving VLM reasoning face a fundamental trade-off: inference-time scaling methods (tree search, MCTS) achieve strong accuracy but incur high token and latency costs, while post-training methods (SFT, RL) require large curated CoT datasets and fine-tuning the VLM backbone. No prior method achieves the flexibility of multi-path exploration with single-pass efficiency while keeping the underlying VLM frozen and avoiding expensive search at deployment.

## Input / Output

The GEN receives three inputs: (1) image embeddings extracted from the frozen VLM's vision tower, (2) question embeddings from the final language layer, and (3) a compact cross-stage attention matrix A (N×N) computed from one multi-stage execution under a default CoT, which encodes how strongly each reasoning stage attends to earlier stages' outputs. The output is an autoregressively predicted sequence of discrete stage IDs (from a vocabulary of ~20 stages across four functional groups: Perception, Grounding, Reasoning, Explanation) terminated by an EOS token, which the frozen VLM then executes step-by-step with a shared memory to produce the final answer.

## Method

ReaGEN operates in two phases. First, a teacher-guided evolutionary search (Evaluate → Select → Mutate) discovers query-specific optimal CoT structures: the frozen student VLM executes each candidate stage sequence, and a cross-stage attention mass matrix is computed to derive per-stage importance scores (combining direct and indirect contributions via a discounted recurrence). A stronger teacher VLM (Qwen3-VL-32B) then proposes importance-aware mutations—pruning low-impact stages or reordering critical ones—until convergence. Second, a lightweight 4-layer transformer encoder-decoder GEN (18.3M parameters) is trained via supervised learning on the search-generated trajectories to map (image embeddings, question embeddings, attention matrix, initial CoT) to the optimal stage sequence. At inference, the GEN produces a tailored CoT in a single pass (or with a few iterative refinement steps using newly observed attention), eliminating the need for any search or VLM fine-tuning while retaining the structural flexibility of deep exploration.

## Results

On Qwen3-VL-4B, ReaGEN (4 iter) achieves up to +26.5 points over VReST on MMStar (75.77% vs. 49.27%) and +8.4 points on MMMU-Pro 4-option (64.51% vs. 56.13%), while matching VReST on MathVision (44.60% vs. 44.67%) and MathVerse (47.59% vs. 47.97%). ReaGEN reduces average inference token usage by 79% (≈4× fewer tokens) and VLM calls by 53% relative to VReST. The 18M-parameter GEN is trained on only ~2,500 samples, and the offline evolutionary search costs a one-time 18 GPU-hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VReST [28] (the primary test-time scaling baseline using the same Qwen3-VL-4B student), ReaGEN (4 iter) wins decisively on MMStar (+26.5 pts, 75.77% vs. 49.27%) and MMMU-Pro 4-opt (+8.4 pts), ties within 0.5 pts on MathVision and MathVerse, and slightly trails on VStar (84.49% vs. 83.42% is actually a +1.1 win). ReaGEN also outperforms the stronger VReST (Teacher-Reward, 32B) variant on MMMU-Pro and MMStar without requiring the 32B model at inference. Against AStar [23] (Qwen2-VL-7B), ReaGEN surpasses it on MMStar (75.77% vs. 61.70%) and MathVision (44.60% vs. 32.40%) despite using a smaller backbone. Against Socratic-MCTS [1] (InternVL2.5-78B), ReaGEN achieves comparable MMStar accuracy (75.77% vs. 71.10%) with a 4B model versus a 78B model. Overall, ReaGEN matches or exceeds all three test-time scaling baselines while using 79% fewer tokens and 53% fewer VLM calls.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReaGEN carves new ground at the intersection of three existing lines of work: (i) structured CoT prompting for VLMs (VisualCoT, LLaVA-CoT, multimodal CoT), (ii) inference-time search methods (VReST, AStar, Socratic-MCTS), and (iii) evolutionary/optimization-based structure discovery. Rather than fine-tuning the VLM or performing expensive tree search at inference, it repurposes attention-derived stage-importance signals—previously used only for interpretability—as a structural supervision signal for a tiny 18M generator. The key conceptual departure is treating the CoT stage sequence itself as a learnable, query-adaptive object predicted from internal model state, rather than a fixed template or a product of runtime exploration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 79% token reduction and 4× fewer VLM calls versus VReST make structured multimodal reasoning practical for self-hosted, open-source VLMs where inference cost is a real constraint. The cross-dataset generalization (a GEN trained on MathVision transferring +26 points to MMStar) suggests that structured reasoning patterns are more domain-agnostic than previously assumed. However, the approach is limited to models with accessible internal attention (ruling out API-only deployments), is validated on a single 4B student model, and still shows a notable gap to teacher-guided search on diagram-heavy math tasks—open questions include whether the method scales to larger backbones and whether attention-free proxies could substitute for the internal signals.
