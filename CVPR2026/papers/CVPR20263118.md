## Problem

UHD image restoration (3840×2160, over 8 million pixels) is trapped in a scalability crisis: existing SSM-based methods like Mamba, despite offering linear complexity, still perform pixel-serial scanning that demands prohibitive memory and computation on consumer-grade GPUs. The paper asks whether every pixel must be individually processed to capture global context, or whether the semantic redundancy in natural images can be exploited to reduce the scanning unit from pixels to a sparse set of learned clusters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263118/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB UHD image (3840×2160) as input and produces a full-resolution restored image by adding a learned residual to the degraded input. During training, 768×768 random crops are used with a batch size of 16 over 150K iterations. The same architecture is applied across five degradation types—low-light enhancement, deraining, deblurring, dehazing, and desnowing—without task-specific modifications, and the model operates at full resolution without the aggressive downsampling (typically 8×) required by competing Mamba-based methods.

## Method

C²SSM replaces pixel-serial Mamba scanning with a cluster-centric dual-path framework inside an asymmetric U-Net decoder. The Cluster-Centric Scanning Module (CCSM) first initializes n learnable centroids, computes an n-dimensional cosine-similarity distribution between every pixel and each centroid, and refines the centroids via a learnable gating function that softly selects relevant pixels in one step (no iterative EM). Mamba's S6 selective scanning is then applied only to the n refined centroids (complexity O(C·n²) vs. O(C·H²W²) for full-pixel scanning), and the resulting global weights are diffused back to all pixels through a softmax-normalized similarity distribution via the law of total probability. In parallel, the Spatial-Channel Feature Modulator (SCFM) uses dual-branch spatial and channel attention to preserve high-frequency details attenuated by the clustering step. The encoder contains only FFNs to reduce load, while the decoder integrates CCSM, SCFM, and FFNs across 3 levels with N₂=[2,4,4] blocks and a base embedding dimension of 32.

## Results

C2SSM achieves state-of-the-art results across all five UHD restoration tasks with only 2.71M parameters. On UHD-LOL4K (low-light enhancement), it reaches 39.61 dB PSNR / 0.992 SSIM, surpassing the previous best MixNet (39.22 dB) by 0.39 dB. On UHD-Blur (deblurring), it achieves 31.53 dB PSNR / 0.890 SSIM, a 1.81 dB margin over ERR. On 4K-RealRain (no-reference deraining), it attains the best NIQE (8.198) and PIQE (54.90), outperforming DRSformer (8.49 / 60.441). In terms of efficiency, C2SSM requires only 0.407G FLOPs per 64×64 block, roughly 12× fewer than MambaIR (4.774G) and 12× fewer than MambaIRv2 (4.940G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C2SSM outperforms all 31 compared baselines across every evaluated task. In low-light enhancement, it beats the strongest non-Mamba baseline MixNet by 0.39 dB (UHD-LOL4K) and 0.09 dB (UHD-LL), and surpasses the closest Mamba-based competitor Wave-Mamba by 2.18 dB on UHD-LOL4K. For deraining, it leads ERR by 0.65 dB on 4K-Rain13k and by a large margin on no-reference metrics (NIQE 8.198 vs. 9.136; PIQE 54.90 vs. 59.735 on 4K-RealRain), while also exceeding MambaIRv2 by 1.96 dB. In deblurring, the 1.81 dB PSNR gap over ERR (31.53 vs. 29.72) is the largest single-task margin in the paper. For dehazing and desnowing, C2SSM edges out UHD-processer by 0.84 dB and UHDDIP by 0.89 dB respectively. Notably, C2SSM achieves these gains with only 2.71M parameters—less than half of MixNet (7.77M) and a fraction of Restormer (26.11M)—while requiring 12× fewer FLOPs than MambaIR and MambaIRv2, enabling full-resolution 4K inference on consumer-grade GPUs where those methods cannot run without aggressive downsampling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mamba's S6 selective scanning mechanism but fundamentally repositions its operational unit: instead of scanning millions of pixels sequentially (as in MambaIR, MambaIRv2, Wave-Mamba, and Vmamba), it scans a small set of learned cluster centroids and diffuses the resulting global context back to pixels via a probabilistic similarity distribution. Conceptually, it repurposes the mixture-model / soft-assignment idea from clustering and the weighted-aggregation logic from attention, wrapping them in a state-space framework. The U-Net backbone and the CBAM-style spatial-channel modulator (SCFM) follow standard restoration conventions, so the novelty is concentrated in the CCSM's cluster-centric scanning and its probabilistic justification via the law of total probability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~10× FLOPs reduction (0.407G vs. 4.7–4.9G for pixel-serial Mamba baselines) while matching or exceeding SOTA across five UHD restoration tasks is practically significant: it makes full-resolution 4K processing feasible on consumer-grade GPUs, a regime where prior Mamba-based methods simply cannot run. The cluster-centric scanning paradigm is potentially generalizable to any sequential model applied to spatial data (segmentation, video, point clouds), though the paper validates it only on restoration. Open questions include whether the fixed small number of centroids (4) is sufficient for highly diverse or adversarial content, how the method behaves on video or 3D data where temporal/spatial redundancy differs, and whether the probabilistic framing (cosine-similarity PDFs, softmax-normalized assignment) is truly necessary or whether simpler k-means-style hard assignment would suffice.
