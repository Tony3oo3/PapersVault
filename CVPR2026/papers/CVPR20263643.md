## Problem

Existing single-image 3D avatar reconstruction methods are either head-only or full-body, rely on pose- and framing-sensitive patch-level features that cause identity drift under partial visibility, and depend on expensive multi-view or studio-captured training data. No unified framework robustly handles the full spectrum of real-world inputs (headshot, half-body, full-body) while training on scalable synthetic data alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263643/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB portrait of arbitrary framing (head-only, half-body, or full-body) together with a proxy mesh estimated by a hierarchical multi-estimator tracker (fusing OSX, Multi-HMR, EMICA, and HaMeR based on detected body-part visibility). It produces an animatable 3D Gaussian avatar as 262K Gaussians defined on a 512×512 UV attribute map (color, opacity, offset, rotation, scale), which is rigged to a target mesh for arbitrary-viewpoint rendering. The frozen Sapiens-1B encoder processes the input image, and the lightweight decoder (<0.1B parameters, 8 self-attention layers) operates entirely in canonical UV space rather than image patch space.

## Method

The core contribution is a Dual-UV representation that deterministically maps image features into a geometry-aligned canonical UV space, eliminating pose- and framing-induced token shifts. The Core-UV branch projects uniformly sampled mesh surface points into the image, gathers features via differentiable bilinear sampling from the frozen encoder, and scatters them onto a regular UV grid; the Shell-UV branch offsets the mesh along vertex normals to capture off-surface details (hair, loose garments) using only the shell-exclusive visible region. A shallow 8-layer transformer fuses both token streams and predicts UV-space Gaussian attributes, exploiting a masked-autoencoding-style asymmetry where visible UV cells are filled and occluded regions are inferred by the compact decoder. Training uses a factorized synthetic data manifold combining 150K geometry-accurate 3D renders with 300K photorealistic video clips generated by Wan2.2, whose prompts are refined by Qwen2.5-14B as a filmic realism regularizer and augmented with side/back views from Qwen-ImageEdit under asymmetric cross-view consistency constraints.

## Results

On upper-body reconstruction (Upper-Wild), the method achieves PSNR 21.09, SSIM 0.8510, and LPIPS 0.1426, outperforming GUAVA (20.59/0.7864/0.1957) and LRM-based baselines LHM-HF (13.95/0.7835/0.3335) and IDOL (12.93/0.7617/0.3465) by large margins. On head reconstruction, it reaches PSNR 19.04 and LPIPS 0.1613, surpassing LAM (14.81/0.6789/0.2613) and LAM† (17.19/0.7526/0.2207). For full-body, the method attains PSNR 24.53 and LPIPS 0.0916, competitive with LHM (21.53/0.9151/0.0725) despite being trained almost exclusively on upper-body data. On synthesized upper-body clips (Upper-Wan), it scores PSNR 20.38, SSIM 0.7867, LPIPS 0.1635, again leading all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GUAVA (ICCV 2025), the proposed method wins on all three metrics for both real (Upper-Wild) and synthesized (Upper-Wan) upper-body inputs, with a notable LPIPS gap of 0.1426 vs. 0.1957 on real data; GUAVA's strict requirement for visible hands causes unstable poses under occlusion. Against LHM-HF (ICCV 2025), the gap is dramatic: the method achieves PSNR 21.09 vs. 13.95 and LPIPS 0.1426 vs. 0.3335 on Upper-Wild, reflecting the advantage of geometry-aligned UV encoding over learnable-token cross-attention under partial visibility. Against IDOL (CVPR 2025), the method dominates on upper-body (PSNR 21.09 vs. 12.93) and full-body (PSNR 24.53 vs. 18.51, LPIPS 0.0916 vs. 0.1256) settings. Against LAM (SIGGRAPH 2025) on head reconstruction, the method leads on all metrics (PSNR 19.04 vs. 14.81, LPIPS 0.1613 vs. 0.2613) and additionally reconstructs regions below the shoulders that LAM cannot represent. The one setting where a baseline edges ahead is full-body SSIM/LPIPS, where LHM (0.9151/0.0725) slightly outperforms the proposed method (0.8642/0.0916), though the proposed method still leads on PSNR (24.53 vs. 21.53) despite never seeing lower-body training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant LRM paradigm (LHM, LAM) that uses learnable tokens with cross-attention to query image patch features, replacing it with a deterministic, non-learnable projection of image features into a canonical UV space (Dual-UV). While prior UV-space avatar methods (Gaussian Shell Maps, GGHead, URAvatar) modulate UV attributes via StyleGAN or cross-attention, this work uses a closed-form geometric mapping that preserves identity without generative hallucination. The data pipeline also diverges from IDOL's approach of fine-tuning 2D generators for multi-view consistency; instead, it embraces the raw appearance diversity of 2D generative models (Wan2.2) and anchors geometry separately via 3D rendering, organized in a factorized manifold refined by LLM-based realism regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training entirely on synthetic data while achieving state-of-the-art in-the-wild generalization addresses a critical scalability bottleneck for avatar reconstruction, removing the need for expensive multi-view studio captures or labor-intensive video curation. The unified handling of head, half-body, and full-body inputs in a single model is practically important for telepresence and VR applications where input framing is unpredictable. Key limitations remain: reliance on a proxy mesh that can fail under extreme poses, and sparse viewpoint coverage in the synthetic training data (limited side/rear views). Open questions include whether weakly pose-dependent representations can eliminate the proxy mesh dependency and whether the factorized data manifold can be extended to denser viewpoint coverage.
