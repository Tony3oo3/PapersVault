## Problem

Weighted sampling from pretrained score-based generative models (SGMs) is essential for variance reduction, reward-aligned generation, and bias mitigation, but existing training-free guidance methods either require costly second-order derivatives (Hessians) of the pretrained score network or rely on particle-based resampling schemes that scale poorly with model size. This paper addresses how to approximate the score of the reweighted distribution q(x) ∝ w(x)p(x) in a principled, Hessian-free, and resampling-free manner that remains accurate and stable across the full diffusion trajectory.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained SGM score function ∇_x log p_t(x) (e.g., from Stable Diffusion or SDXL), a differentiable weight function w(x) (e.g., PickScore for text-image alignment), and optionally a text prompt for conditional generation, and produces samples from the target weighted distribution q(x) ∝ w(x)p(x). No retraining or fine-tuning is required; the only additional inputs are two scalar hyperparameters (ε for the finite-difference step and c for the scheduler). In the text-to-image setting, the output is a set of images whose expected preference score is maximized, with the method completing SDXL sampling in ~85 seconds per image.

## Method

LAGS (Lightweight Approximation with uncertainty-adaptive Guidance Scheduling) decomposes the target score as ∇_x log q_t(x) = ∇_x log p_t(x) + g(x,t) and approximates the guidance term g in two stages. First, a first-order Taylor expansion of w around the Tweedie conditional mean x̄₀′|x,t yields a tractable form for g, and the resulting Hessian-vector product is replaced by a finite-difference approximation of the score function, eliminating all second-order derivatives. Second, a theoretical analysis (Theorem 1) shows the approximation error is bounded by the conditional variance of X₀ given X_t, which vanishes as t→0; this motivates a scheduler τ(t) = ᾱ(t)²/(ᾱ(t)² + c(1−ᾱ(t))²) that downweights the guidance during early reverse steps (high uncertainty) and approaches full strength near t=0. The final approximated score requires only one extra score evaluation and one gradient backpropagation through w per diffusion step, yielding 1.2×–4.7× speedups over resampling-based baselines while matching or exceeding their task performance.

## Results

On SDXL, LAGS achieves the highest PickScore and HPS among all guidance methods while completing sampling in 85 seconds per image (1.0× baseline), compared to 4.72× for DAS and 1.93× for FreeDoM. On the 2D multimodal benchmark, LAGS attains the lowest Wasserstein Distance to the target distribution in 0.33 seconds for 10⁴ samples, outperforming DPS, FreeDoM, and DAS on both accuracy and speed. Across SD and SDXL, LAGS consistently breaks the typical trade-off between target-metric performance (PickScore, HPS) and computational cost, delivering up to 4.7× speedup over DAS while matching or exceeding its alignment scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LAGS outperforms all four guidance baselines (DPS, FreeDoM, DAS, DAS-1P) on the primary target metric PickScore and on HPS for both SD and SDXL, while also achieving the lowest runtime among guidance methods. Against DAS (the strongest baseline), LAGS is 4.7× faster on SDXL (85 s vs. ~400 s) and 1.9× faster than FreeDoM, yet still matches or exceeds DAS on PickScore and HPS; DAS does retain a slight edge on BRISQUE (19.55 vs. 26.40) and on ImageReward/CLIP Score by less than 0.1/0.06 points. Compared to the single-particle variant DAS-1P, LAGS is ~17% faster on SDXL and ~6% faster on SD while delivering higher PickScore. Against the unguided SDXL default, LAGS adds only ~2.6× the base inference time (1.0× vs. 0.377×) to obtain the alignment gains, whereas DAS requires ~12.5× the base time. The method's advantage grows with model scale: the speedup over DAS widens from ~1.2× on SD to 4.7× on SDXL, reflecting the avoidance of Hessian evaluations and particle resampling that become increasingly costly for larger architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the guidance-based framework for weighted sampling with pretrained score-based generative models, where the target score is decomposed into the base score plus a correction term (as in DPS, FreeDoM, and DAS). It departs from prior methods in two key ways: (1) replacing Hessian-based or particle-resampling corrections with a first-order Taylor approximation of the weight function combined with a finite-difference Hessian-vector product, eliminating all second-order derivative computations; and (2) introducing an uncertainty-adaptive scheduler derived from a theoretical error bound (Theorem 1) that modulates guidance strength over the reverse diffusion trajectory, rather than relying on heuristic time-travel resampling (FreeDoM) or sequential Monte Carlo (DAS). The Tweedie denoising mean estimator connects the conditional expectation in the guidance term to the pretrained score, following the lineage of DPS and Noise2Score.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Hessian-free, resampling-free formulation makes weighted sampling with large pretrained diffusion models (SDXL and beyond) substantially more tractable, with 1.2×–4.7× speedups while matching or exceeding SOTA on human-preference alignment metrics. The single tunable hyperparameter (c=10) that generalizes across models and tasks lowers the practical barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether the orthogonality assumption (Assumption 3) holds broadly in practice, how the finite-difference approximation behaves in extremely high-dimensional latent spaces, and whether the scheduler's single-parameter form captures the full temporal structure of the approximation error for more complex weight functions. The method's training-free nature also positions it as a lightweight alternative to fine-tuning or RL-based alignment (e.g., DDPO, DPO for diffusion) when only a scalar reward signal is available.
