## Problem

Omnidirectional image super-resolution at extreme magnifications (8×, 16×) suffers from two compounding issues: projection-specific distortions that degrade edge integrity, and severe data scarcity (only ~1200 public samples) that limits generalization. Existing ODISR methods rely on patch-based or region-based stitching with complex architectures, resulting in poor edge preservation and discontinuities, particularly in highly distorted polar regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262867/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a full low-resolution equirectangular omnidirectional image (1024×2048 for training) as input and produces a high-resolution equirectangular image at 8× or 16× scale as output. Unlike many prior ODISR methods that process patches or regions and stitch results, EAM operates end-to-end on the entire image without downsampling, preserving the full 360° spatial structure. Training uses the ODI-SR dataset augmented via spherical 3D rotation-translation, and evaluation is performed on both ODI-SR and SUN360 using WS-PSNR and WS-SSIM metrics.

## Method

The proposed Edge-Aware Multi-Scale (EAM) pipeline consists of: (1) a shallow 3×3 convolution with MeanShift for bias-free feature extraction at original resolution; (2) cascaded Edge Focused Blocks (EFB), each containing an Edge Enhanced Block (EEB) that fuses multi-scale dilated convolutions (rates 1, 2, 3) with channel attention and 7×7 spatial attention, followed by an Edge Refined Block (ERB) that combines 7×7 depthwise convolution, multi-path 1×1/3×3/5×5 convolutions, and a learnable edge-reliability mask (α-weighted); (3) a Global Integration Block (GIB) using 7×7 and 9×9 (dilation=2) depthwise convolutions with attention-based channel fusion for long-range context; and (4) progressive 2× PixelShuffle upsampling with 3×3 convolutions. A key complementary contribution is a spherical geometric augmentation strategy that maps ERP pixels to 3D unit-sphere coordinates, applies rotation matrices around X/Y axes (±15°) and Z-axis (0–360°), then maps back to 2D, preserving the 360° ring topology and scene continuity that 2D augmentation destroys. Training uses a composite loss of L1 + 0.01×VGG perceptual + 0.1×(1−SSIM) with Adam (lr=0.001, batch=4).

## Results

On the ODI-SR dataset, EAM achieves 25.69 dB WS-PSNR (×8) and 23.86 dB WS-PSNR (×16), outperforming the strongest baseline LAPR by 0.97 dB and 0.96 dB respectively. On SUN360, EAM reaches 25.81 dB (×8) and 23.49 dB (×16), surpassing GDGT-OSR by 0.81 dB at ×8. In terms of efficiency, EAM requires only 38G FLOPs and 2.0M parameters for ×16 SR, compared to 372G/7.8M for LAPR and 587G/8.7M for SphereSR, with a runtime of 0.022 s per image.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EAM achieves the highest WS-PSNR across all four evaluation settings (ODI-SR and SUN360 at ×8 and ×16), with margins of 0.81–1.12 dB over the strongest baselines. On ODI-SR, the closest competitor is LAPR [2] (24.72/22.90 dB), which EAM surpasses by 0.97 dB (×8) and 0.96 dB (×16); however, LAPR retains a slight WS-SSIM advantage on ODI-SR (0.6886 vs. 0.6839 at ×8; 0.6480 vs. 0.6290 at ×16). On SUN360, EAM leads GDGT-OSR [29] by 0.81 dB in WS-PSNR at ×8 while also winning on WS-SSIM (0.7102 vs. 0.7068), and beats LAPR by 1.12 dB at ×16. Compared to the transformer-based FATO [1], EAM gains 1.15 dB (×8) and 1.13 dB (×16) on ODI-SR. In terms of efficiency, EAM (38G FLOPs, 2.0M params, 0.022 s) is roughly 10× lighter than LAPR (372G, 7.8M, 0.312 s) and 15× lighter than SphereSR (587G, 8.7M, 0.401 s), making it the most efficient method in the comparison while delivering the best PSNR.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of single-image super-resolution (SISR) and omnidirectional image super-resolution (ODISR), drawing architectural ideas from CNN-based SISR (multi-scale dilated convolutions, channel/spatial attention, PixelShuffle upsampling) while addressing ODISR-specific challenges of projection distortion and data scarcity. It departs from prior ODISR methods (LAU-Net, SphereSR, OSRT, BPOSR, FATO) that primarily focus on multi-projection fusion or distortion-aware transformers, instead introducing a spherical-geometry-based data augmentation strategy and an explicitly edge-focused network design. The augmentation approach repurposes 3D rotation/translation matrices from spherical coordinate geometry into a training-time data expansion tool, a use not previously explored in the ODISR literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spherical rotation-translation augmentation directly addresses the critical data scarcity bottleneck in ODISR (only ~1200 training samples), and the ~1 dB improvement from augmentation alone suggests that prior methods were significantly undertrained. The striking efficiency profile (38G FLOPs, 2.0M params vs. 372–900G for competitors) while outperforming SOTA makes this practically relevant for real-time or resource-constrained omnidirectional applications. However, the ablation reveals that individual architectural components (EEB, ERB, GIB) each contribute only marginal gains, raising questions about whether the edge-focused design is truly necessary or whether the augmentation and overall pipeline structure drive most of the improvement. Open questions include whether the augmentation generalizes beyond equirectangular projection, whether the method handles real-world degradations (noise, compression) rather than idealized bicubic downsampling, and whether the edge focus transfers to other projection types like fisheye or cubemap.
