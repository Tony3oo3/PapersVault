## Problem

Existing prototype- and slot-based segmentation methods model each semantic concept as a point or isotropic region in feature space, failing to capture the inherently anisotropic (elliptical) geometry of real lesion feature distributions. This geometric mismatch is catastrophically amplified by semantic ambiguity and class imbalance, as isotropic balls must oversize to cover anisotropic clusters, creating false overlap with dense background features. Additionally, classical Granular-Ball Computing relies on non-differentiable clustering, preventing end-to-end integration into deep networks.

## Input / Output

The method operates on 2D medical images resized to 256×256 (BUSI, CVC-ClinicDB, ISIC17) or 512×512 (GlaS) and produces pixel-wise binary segmentation masks. The AD-GBC module itself does not consume raw images; it is a plug-and-play refiner inserted at deeper skip-connection levels of a UNet backbone, operating on intermediate feature maps of shape B×C_in×H×W and outputting a refined map of identical dimensions. The module is weight-shared between encoder and decoder pathways, so the same learned anisotropic anchors refine both sides before fusion.

## Method

AD-GBC replaces point prototypes with K learnable anisotropic granular balls, each parameterized by a center c_k ∈ R^D and a vector scale σ_k ∈ R^D (constrained positive via Softplus), forming a learnable hyper-ellipsoid in feature space. The forward pass follows a Set→Ball→Set pathway: pixel features are softly assigned to balls via scaled L2 distances (measuring how many radii a pixel is from a center), aggregated into region-level consensus descriptors, and broadcast back to refine each pixel's representation. Two coupled geometric regularizers stabilize training: a Wasserstein-based diversity loss (centering + spectral uniformity) prevents anchor collapse into a low-rank subspace, and a scale consistency loss drives the learned σ_k toward the observed per-ball variance with a detach to break circular dependency. The module is integrated as a weight-shared semantic skip-connection refiner at the third encoder/decoder level of UNet backbones (Rolling-UNet, U-KAN), with a residual connection and a 3×3 Conv-BN-ReLU refinement block producing the final output.

## Results

On the four medical segmentation benchmarks, Rolling-UNet-L + AD-GBC achieves the best average IoU of 82.77% and F1 of 90.26%, while U-KAN + AD-GBC reaches 82.03% IoU and 89.86% F1. The strongest per-dataset results are 89.37% IoU on GlaS, 87.45% IoU on CVC-ClinicDB, 84.95% IoU on ISIC17 (all Rolling-UNet-L + AD-GBC), and 69.86% IoU on BUSI (U-KAN + AD-GBC). The AD-GBC module adds only ~2% parameters and ~15–20% GFLOPs while delivering consistent +1.3 to +1.7 IoU gains across all tested backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against seven baselines spanning classic and recent architectures. Rolling-UNet-L + AD-GBC outperforms U-Net (2015) by +15.0 IoU on GlaS (89.37 vs. 87.26) and +7.6 IoU on ISIC17 (84.95 vs. 77.38), and beats U-NeXt (2022) by +5.2 IoU on CVC-ClinicDB (87.45 vs. 74.58). Against the strongest non-AD-GBC baselines, Rolling-UNet-L + AD-GBC surpasses U-Mamba (2024) by +1.9 IoU on GlaS (89.37 vs. 87.48) and +3.7 IoU on CVC-ClinicDB (87.45 vs. 84.35), and outperforms U-KAN (2025) by +1.7 IoU on GlaS (89.37 vs. 87.65). On BUSI, U-KAN + AD-GBC (69.86 IoU) edges out Rolling-UNet-L + AD-GBC (69.30 IoU) and all other baselines by a margin of +2.0 IoU over the next-best (Rolling-UNet-L at 67.85). The AD-GBC module consistently improves every backbone it is attached to by +1.3 to +1.7 average IoU, confirming its plug-and-play effectiveness without any backbone-specific tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the prototype/slot-based skip-refinement line (ProtoSeg, Slot Attention, Gaussian Attention) by replacing point anchors with learnable anisotropic hyper-ellipsoids, and simultaneously makes Granular-Ball Computing differentiable and end-to-end trainable—something classical GBC could not do due to its reliance on non-differentiable clustering and manually set isotropic radii. The module is positioned as a weight-shared semantic refiner inserted into existing UNet-style backbones (Rolling-UNet, U-KAN) rather than a new architecture, and two geometric regularizers (Wasserstein-based center diversity and scale consistency) are introduced to stabilize the anisotropic parameters during training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AD-GBC offers a lightweight, plug-and-play geometric refinement layer (~2% extra parameters) that consistently improves boundary-sensitive metrics (HD95) across four diverse medical imaging modalities, suggesting the anisotropic region formulation captures real structure in feature distributions that isotropic or point-based anchors miss. The Theorem 1 result—that pairwise cosine diversity cannot detect rank collapse of the anchor set—provides a clean theoretical justification for the Wasserstein-based regularizer over the more common heuristic. However, the study is limited to CNN-based encoder-decoder backbones; whether the module transfers to Transformer-based segmenters (TransUNet, Swin-UNet) remains an open question the authors explicitly flag for future work.
