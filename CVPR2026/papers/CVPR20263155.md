## Problem

Current 3D generation models conditioned on images or text lack a mechanism to respect real 3D measurements, even though visible-region point clouds are readily available from LiDAR, depth cameras, or feed-forward predictors like VGGT. These explicit geometric constraints are underused: existing pipelines initialize their structural latents from pure noise and rely solely on 2D or text embeddings, making it impossible to anchor generated geometry to actual 3D observations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263155/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a visible-region point cloud (either sensor-captured or VGGT-predicted from a single image) as a hard geometric constraint, along with a condition image and/or text prompt. The point cloud is voxelized into a 64³ binary occupancy grid and encoded into a 16³ sparse structure latent; only the visible portion is preserved while unobserved regions are filled with noise. The output is a complete 3D asset (mesh, Gaussian splats, or radiance field) that faithfully aligns with the input point cloud in observed regions while plausibly synthesizing the remainder.

## Method

Points-to-3D redefines the initialization of TRELLIS's sparse structure latent: instead of pure Gaussian noise, the visible point cloud is voxelized and encoded via TRELLIS's structure VAE encoder, then combined with a downsampled occupancy mask to form a partially observed latent (visible regions preserved, rest filled with noise). A modified flow transformer (the inpainting network G_inp) is trained with a new input projection layer accepting the concatenated [q_comb, mask] channels, supervised by Conditional Flow Matching loss against ground-truth sparse structure latents. Training data is constructed by rendering depth maps from 24 viewpoints per object, sampling 50,000 surface points, and applying depth-consistency visibility masks to create partial–complete structure pairs. At inference, a staged sampling strategy first performs 25 steps of structural inpainting (preserving the visibility mask each step) to establish global geometry, then 25 steps of boundary refinement (replacing the mask with all-ones, converting to standard denoising) to eliminate geometric 'holes' at the inpainting boundary without disturbing anchored regions.

## Results

On Toys4K single-object generation, Points-to-3D with explicit point cloud priors achieves an F-Score of 0.964 and Chamfer Distance of 0.013, substantially outperforming TRELLIS (F-Score 0.832, CD 0.034) and SAM3D (F-Score 0.835, CD 0.033). Even with VGGT-estimated point clouds (no ground-truth priors), the method reaches F-Score 0.881 and CD 0.024, still leading all baselines. On 3D-FRONT scene-level generation, the gap widens further: Points-to-3D (P.C. Priors) achieves F-Score 0.886 vs. 0.478 for TRELLIS and 0.513 for MIDI, with PSNR of 21.63 dB vs. 18.21 dB for TRELLIS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Points-to-3D outperforms all eight baselines on every metric in both the Toys4K and 3D-FRONT evaluations. The closest competitor is SAM3D, which also leverages point maps but integrates them indirectly through attention; Points-to-3D (P.C. Priors) improves F-Score from 0.835 to 0.964 and reduces Chamfer Distance from 0.033 to 0.013 on Toys4K. TRELLIS, the underlying framework, shows a clear gap in geometry (F-Score 0.832 vs. 0.964), confirming that the inpainting-based latent initialization is the key contribution. VoxHammer, which uses the same structural voxels as a 3D inversion baseline, achieves F-Score 0.724 but struggles to complete unobserved regions because the image condition cannot provide cues for missing geometry. On 3D-FRONT scenes, the advantage is even more pronounced: Points-to-3D (P.C. Priors) reaches F-Score 0.886 versus 0.478 for TRELLIS and 0.513 for MIDI, demonstrating that explicit point-cloud priors are especially valuable for complex multi-object scenes. Notably, even the VGGT-estimated variant (no ground-truth point clouds) surpasses all baselines, indicating robustness to prediction noise in the priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TRELLIS, a two-stage latent 3D diffusion model (sparse structure generation followed by structured latent generation), by redefining the structure stage as a latent inpainting problem: instead of initializing the sparse structure latent from pure Gaussian noise, the visible point cloud is voxelized and VAE-encoded to produce a partially observed latent that anchors generation to measured geometry. It departs from prior 3D completion methods (SDFusion, DiffComplete, SC-Diff) that operate as separate post-hoc modules, and from SAM3D which injects point maps indirectly through attention, by embedding geometric priors explicitly into the latent initialization itself. The connection to feed-forward point cloud predictors like VGGT positions the work at the intersection of reconstruction and generation, treating predicted geometry as a first-class conditioning signal rather than a soft hint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes a practical and generalizable recipe for grounding 3D generative models in explicit sensor or predicted geometry, which is increasingly relevant as LiDAR, depth cameras, and feed-forward predictors (VGGT, DUSt3R) become widely accessible. The near-perfect visible-region preservation (F-score 0.998) demonstrates that hard geometric constraints can coexist with plausible completion of unobserved regions, a property that indirect conditioning methods like SAM3D fail to achieve. Open questions include whether the approach extends to the appearance/SLAT stage for texture-controllable generation, how it behaves under significantly noisy or sparse point clouds beyond VGGT quality, and whether the staged sampling strategy generalizes to other latent 3D architectures beyond TRELLIS.
