## Problem

Prior floor plan-guided navigation work has been restricted to PointNav in small-scale environments, leaving the potential of floor-plan spatial priors for ObjectNav and ImageNav largely unexplored. Meanwhile, mapping-based methods incur heavy overhead from explicit map construction, while mapless methods lack global guidance when the goal is not directly observable. The paper asks whether a single unified policy can exploit floor plans to navigate efficiently across all three goal modalities in unseen scenes.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives an egocentric RGB image, a depth image, its 2D pose (x, y, θ), a binary 2D floor plan, and a goal specified as either a 2D point, a text object label, or a goal image; during training exactly one goal modality is active while the other two are masked. The model outputs a sequence of 16 continuous 2D waypoints, of which the first 10 are executed; at inference, 30 trajectories are sampled per step and averaged to reduce stochasticity. For the refiner stage, the depth image is additionally projected into a local 20×50 BEV occupancy grid at 0.1 m resolution and converted to a signed distance field.

## Method

ThreeDiff is a two-stage diffusion policy trained by imitation learning on 240 K expert trajectories. The first stage (planner) is a conditional U-Net with a DDPM scheduler that takes a masked goal condition (encoded via MLP for points, CLIP for object labels, or EfficientNet for image goals), floor-plan features from EfficientNet, and observation embeddings from a 4-head self-attention module over the past l timesteps of RGB, depth, and pose features; it is trained with standard MSE noise-prediction loss. The second stage (refiner) is a second diffusion model that conditions on the planner's coarse trajectory and an SDF embedding of the local depth-derived occupancy map, trained with an additional collision loss that penalizes waypoints close to obstacles via an exponential-of-negative-distance term. A key design choice is random modality masking during training, which forces the shared encoder to learn cross-modality complementarity: PointNav supervision drives explicit geometric reasoning while ImageNav/ObjectNav supervision contributes implicit layout-level semantic cues. The two stages are trained sequentially (planner first to convergence, then jointly with the refiner) using AdamW with cosine annealing on 4× NVIDIA 4090 GPUs.

## Results

On PointNav in Gibson 4+, ThreeDiff achieves 54.4% SR and 50.0% SPL, substantially outperforming the best floor-plan baseline FloDiff (38.8% SR, 29.6% SPL) and the RL baseline DD-PPO (18.3% SR, 10.5% SPL). On HM3D PointNav, ThreeDiff reaches 38.1% SR / 32.5% SPL versus FloDiff's 27.7% / 21.1%. For ImageNav, ThreeDiff attains 28.9% SR and 22.4% SPL, exceeding fine-tuned Nomad (22.8% / 17.0%); for ObjectNav it reaches 28.6% SR / 22.3% SPL, far surpassing ZSON (7.2% / 1.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ThreeDiff decisively outperforms DD-PPO [32] on PointNav, achieving 54.4% SR vs. 18.3% on Gibson 4+ and 38.1% vs. 13.7% on HM3D, a gap the authors attribute to DD-PPO's lack of obstacle-avoidance capability. Against FloDiff [18], the closest floor-plan-guided baseline, ThreeDiff gains 15.6% SR and 20.4% SPL on Gibson 4+ and 10.4% SR / 11.4% SPL on HM3D, benefiting from multi-modal training and the depth-based refiner. On ObjectNav, ThreeDiff (28.6% SR, 22.3% SPL) dramatically surpasses ZSON [19] (7.2% SR, 1.1% SPL), which was trained on short episodes under 5 m while FloVerse episodes exceed 7 m. On ImageNav, ThreeDiff (28.9% SR, 22.4% SPL) outperforms fine-tuned Nomad [28] (22.8% SR, 17.0% SPL) by 6.1% SR and 5.4% SPL despite both being trained on the same expert trajectories. On GOAT-Bench [15], ThreeDiff beats all three baselines on ObjectNav (34.5% SR, 30.8% SPL vs. Modular Goat's 29.4% / 17.0%) but trails RL Skill Chain on ImageNav SR (31.0% vs. 42.2%), though it recovers with a 7.6-point SPL advantage (25.6% vs. 18.0%), indicating more efficient path planning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends FloNa [18], the first end-to-end floor plan-guided navigation framework, from PointNav-only to a unified multi-modal setting covering PointNav, ObjectNav, and ImageNav. The trajectory generation builds on diffusion-based navigation policies (Nomad [28], NavDP [3]), while the multi-modal goal conditioning with random modality masking draws from multi-task navigation architectures like VIENNA [30] and Goat-Bench [15]. The two-stage design—global diffusion planner followed by a depth-based SDF refiner—departs from prior single-stage approaches by explicitly separating spatial reasoning from local obstacle avoidance, and the dataset construction leverages SpatialLM [20] for scalable object annotation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FloVerse-1.6K (1,627 scenes, 240K trajectories, 12M RGBD frames) is a substantial resource that fills a gap: prior floor plan-guided navigation work was limited to small-scale PointNav evaluations, leaving the impact of spatial priors on semantic goal modalities unexplored. The consistent but modality-dependent gains (16.2% SR for PointNav vs. ~6–8% for ImageNav/ObjectNav) clarify that floor plans primarily provide geometric rather than semantic priors, a useful distinction for future system design. Key open questions include generalization beyond residential layouts, real-world deployment, and whether richer floor plan encodings (e.g., with furniture or semantic room labels) could close the gap for ObjectNav and ImageNav.
