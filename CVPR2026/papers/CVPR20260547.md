## Problem

Dense pixel-level annotations for semantic segmentation remain prohibitively expensive, and sparse annotations (points, scribbles, coarse labels) that reduce labeling cost still leave a significant performance gap to fully supervised models. Existing weakly-supervised methods propagate supervision only within the 2D image plane, struggling to resolve ambiguities in geometrically complex outdoor scenes with occlusions and long-range dependencies. This paper asks whether 3D geometric structure, reconstructed purely from 2D video, can serve as an auxiliary supervisory signal to close that gap without requiring 3D sensors at inference.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single RGB image and produces a dense pixel-level semantic segmentation mask, requiring no 3D sensors or multi-view context. During training, it additionally consumes 2D video sequences (up to 200 frames) that are pre-processed into dense metric point clouds via MapAnything, along with sparse annotations (points, scribbles, or coarse polygons) covering roughly 2–4% of pixels. The 3D branch operates on 120K-point subsamples per image, and the 2D branch uses SegFormer-B4 while the 3D branch uses Point Transformer V3.

## Method

Rewis3d introduces a dual student-teacher architecture where both a 2D and a 3D branch use Mean Teacher setups, and a bidirectional Cross-Modal Consistency (CMC) loss lets each modality's teacher supervise the other's student. The 3D geometry is generated as a pre-processing step by MapAnything from 2D video, and sparse 2D labels are transferred to 3D via direct unprojection, creating a unified sparsely-labeled point cloud. A dual confidence weighting mechanism multiplies the 3D teacher's prediction confidence with MapAnything's per-point reconstruction confidence to filter unreliable pseudo-labels, while a view-aware sampling strategy allocates 60% of the 120K-point subsample to the target view (ensuring dense 2D–3D correspondences) and 40% to surrounding scene context. Training proceeds in two stages: an initial base-training phase establishes independent student-teacher pairs per modality, followed by a CMC phase where the cross-modal loss is ramped up linearly over 5 epochs. The framework is architecture-agnostic and requires no 3D data at inference, making it applicable to any 2D segmentation backbone.

## Results

On scribble-supervised semantic segmentation, Rewis3d (Recon) achieves 53.3% mIoU on Waymo, 63.4% on KITTI-360, and 46.1% on NYUv2, closing 90.3%, 93.4%, and 90.2% of the gap to fully supervised baselines respectively. On Cityscapes, the method generalizes across annotation types, improving mIoU by +6.0 (points), +6.9 (scribbles), and +2.1 (coarse) over the EMA baseline. Notably, reconstructed 3D geometry outperforms ground-truth LiDAR/depth (Ours Recon vs. Ours Real 3D), attributed to denser point clouds and the availability of per-point reconstruction confidence for dual filtering.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Rewis3d consistently outperforms both specialized WSSS baselines across all three primary datasets. Against TEL [15], gains range from +4.2 mIoU (KITTI-360) to +10.9 (Waymo) and +7.8 (NYUv2). Against SASFormer [22], improvements are even larger: +15.5 on Waymo, +17.0 on KITTI-360, and +1.4 on NYUv2, with the smallest margin on the indoor dataset where 3D structure is less distinctive. Compared to the EMA (Mean Teacher) baseline that serves as the architectural foundation, Rewis3d adds +3.9, +3.1, and +3.2 mIoU on Waymo, KITTI-360, and NYUv2 respectively, confirming that the cross-modal consistency loss is the primary source of improvement. On Cityscapes (Table 2), the same pattern holds across all three annotation types, with the largest relative gains under the sparsest supervision (points: +6.0 over EMA, +3.4 over TEL).

## Relation to Prior Work

Rewis3d extends the Mean Teacher framework for sparse-label segmentation (Boettcher et al., 2024) by introducing a 3D geometric branch that enforces bidirectional cross-modal consistency between 2D image predictions and 3D point-cloud predictions. Unlike prior WSSS methods (SASFormer, TEL) that propagate sparse supervision purely within the 2D image plane, this work repurposes feed-forward multi-view stereo reconstruction (MapAnything) as a training-time auxiliary signal, keeping inference entirely 2D. The dual student-teacher architecture with confidence-weighted pseudo-labels builds directly on the EMA-based consistency regularization of Tarvainen & Valpola (2017), while the view-aware sampling and dual confidence filtering are novel contributions specific to the 2D–3D correspondence problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework closes 2–7% of the supervision gap to fully supervised baselines across four datasets and three annotation types without requiring additional labels, 3D sensors, or inference-time overhead, making geometric supervision broadly accessible from standard video. A key open question is how the method behaves under dynamic scenes, since the underlying reconstruction model (MapAnything) is not explicitly optimized for moving objects; the authors note that integrating dynamic-scene-aware reconstruction is a natural next step. The finding that reconstructed geometry can outperform real LiDAR also raises questions about whether confidence-weighted synthetic geometry could replace expensive sensor data in other 3D-assisted learning pipelines.
