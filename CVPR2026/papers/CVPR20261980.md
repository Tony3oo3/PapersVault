## Problem

Diffusion-based video frame interpolation methods struggle with large, complex motions, producing discontinuous transitions and inconsistent object appearances. The paper identifies two root causes: (1) full-sequence interpolation generates all intermediate frames simultaneously, so frames far from the inputs accumulate errors and lose temporal continuity, and (2) pixel-level reconstruction losses and low-level motion representations like optical flow lack the semantic invariance needed to supervise complex, non-rigid motion generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB input frames (e.g., 576×1024) and produces a sequence of intermediate frames (e.g., 24 frames for ×25 interpolation). Both input frames are encoded into latent space via a frozen VAE encoder, and DINOv3 ViT-S patch features (384-dim per 16×16 patch, concatenated across 4 frames to 1536 channels) are extracted as motion representations. Inference requires only 15 diffusion sampling steps, yielding 0.78 s per frame at 576×1024 resolution—roughly 30% of the runtime of the Wan backbone.

## Method

ARVFI performs two-stage bidirectional autoregressive interpolation built on the Wan2.1-Fun-InP-1.3B diffusion transformer. In the first stage, a dedicated motion-estimation DiT (G_θd) interpolates intermediate DINOv3 features, trained with an additional similarity loss that enforces patch-similarity consistency between generated and ground-truth features relative to the input frames. In the second stage, a frame-generation DiT (G_θf) produces intermediate frames conditioned on the estimated DINOv3 features via a Motion-Guided Frame Generation mechanism: DINOv3-derived attention keys and queries (produced by shared MLPs) are concatenated with the original frame attention keys and queries in every DiTBlock, injecting motion priors without altering the pretrained architecture. Both stages use a 'soft' autoregressive scheme where noise levels increase with temporal distance from the inputs, coupled with a bidirectional causal attention mask that prevents each frame from attending to unprocessed (noisier) frames, ensuring each prediction is grounded in all previously denoised frames.

## Results

ARVFI achieves the best FID and FVD across all three evaluation datasets: FID 21.65 / FVD 188.77 on DAVIS-7 (×8), FID 19.03 / FVD 201.47 on the FCVG evaluation set (×25), and FID 17.60 / FVD 101.71 on the Pixels benchmark (×25). It also attains the best LPIPS on the FCVG set (0.206) and Pixels (0.247), though AMT edges it out on DAVIS-7 LPIPS (0.254 vs. 0.257). In terms of efficiency, ARVFI requires only 15 diffusion sampling steps and 0.775 s per frame at 1024×576 for ×25 interpolation, roughly 3× faster than the Wan backbone (50 steps, 2.582 s/frame).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARVFI outperforms all eight baselines on FID and FVD across every dataset, with the largest margins over non-generative methods (e.g., FID 21.65 vs. AMT's 34.65 on DAVIS-7) and over the closest diffusion-based competitor Wan [33] (FID 21.65 vs. 26.97 on DAVIS-7; FVD 101.71 vs. 131.22 on Pixels). Among diffusion-based baselines, FCVG [42] is the strongest on FVD for DAVIS-7 (207.17 vs. ARVFI's 188.77) and LDMVFI [5] is competitive on FID for DAVIS-7 (22.10 vs. 21.65), but both are clearly surpassed on the ×25 FCVG and Pixels benchmarks. On LPIPS, ARVFI wins on the FCVG evaluation set (0.206) and Pixels (0.247) but trails AMT [19] on DAVIS-7 (0.257 vs. 0.254), consistent with the paper's observation that flow-based methods produce blurrier frames that score well on pixel-level metrics under large motion. In human evaluation, 85% of 20 observers ranked ARVFI as the best method, with Wan [33] receiving 12% and all other baselines under 3%.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARVFI builds on the Wan2.1-Fun-InP-1.3B video diffusion transformer as its backbone and adapts the Diffusion Forcing paradigm (sequence noise as soft masks for autoregressive generation) to the interpolation setting, where two boundary frames constrain the sequence rather than a single starting frame. It departs from prior generative VFI methods (Wan, GI, FCVG) that generate all intermediate frames simultaneously, and from joint motion-frame generation approaches (VideoJAM, Motion-I2V) that use optical flow or flow visualization as motion cues. The key conceptual departure is a two-stage pipeline—first interpolating DINOv3 features as high-level motion representations, then conditionally generating frames via a motion-guided attention mechanism—rather than unifying motion and appearance in a single diffusion process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and persistent failure mode in diffusion-based VFI: large, non-rigid, and nonlinear motions that cause temporal discontinuities and object deformations. The 3× speedup over the Wan backbone with simultaneously better FID/FVD scores makes the approach practically attractive for real-time or near-real-time applications. However, the authors acknowledge a clear limitation—physical scene interactions (bouncing balls, pendulums) remain unsolved—and the method's reliance on DINOv3 features as a fixed motion representation raises open questions about whether this generalizes to domains where semantic patch similarity is less informative (e.g., textureless surfaces, extreme viewpoint changes).
