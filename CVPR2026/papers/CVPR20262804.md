## Problem

Existing rotation-equivariant illumination priors such as RENI++ rely on autodecoder architectures where latent codes are randomly initialized per image and jointly optimized, producing a non-unique latent space in which the same image can be represented by many different codes. This non-uniqueness degrades interpolation quality and prevents scaling to large datasets, since similar images are initialized with unrelated latent codes. The paper addresses this by replacing the autodecoder with a rotation-equivariant variational autoencoder that encodes images to latent codes via a forward pass, ensuring similar images map to similar codes.

## Input / Output

The model takes 360° HDR equirectangular environment maps (128×64 from RENI++, plus 43K StreetLearn images converted to HDR) as input. Pixels are sampled as 64 vertical stripes on the sphere with evenly distributed azimuth and arccos-distributed polar angle, and each pixel's RGB color is concatenated with its 3D direction vector to form the patch embedding. The encoder outputs a 3D latent code (dimension D ∈ {27, 147, 300}) sampled from a spherical normal distribution, which is decoded by a rotation-equivariant conditional neural field into a continuous HDR environment map queryable at any direction.

## Method

The core contribution is a Vector Neuron Vision Transformer (VN-ViT) encoder that operates directly on spherical data without 2D projection, eliminating distortion handling. A novel SO(2)-equivariant fully connected layer extends Vector Neurons by combining invariant operations (on z and color dimensions) with equivariant operations (on x-y direction dimensions) in a single neuron, reducing the equivariance guarantee from SO(3) to SO(2) to match the physical constraint that only rotations about the up-axis produce valid illumination. Patches are sampled as vertical stripes with direction vectors concatenated to color values (no positional encoding, preserving equivariance), fed through a standard SO(3)-equivariant VN-Transformer, and projected to μ and σ via SO(2)-equivariant layers. Variational sampling uses a spherical normal distribution (3D mean, 1D variance) to maintain isotropy. Training uses a curriculum: pretraining on 43K StreetLearn images then fine-tuning on 1,694 RENI++ images, with a composite loss of MAGE (gradient-based), scale-invariant, cosine similarity, and KLD terms.

## Results

On the RENI++ test set, the full autoencoder (AE) pass achieves PSNR of 18.78 (D=27), 19.40 (D=147), and 19.47 (D=300) in LDR tone-mapped space, while the direct latent optimization variant reaches 20.33, 21.89, and 22.68 respectively. In linear HDR space, PSNR_HDR ranges from 33.37 to 35.59 across latent dimensions. On latent-space quality, the model achieves a uniqueness score of 0.04 (vs. 1.46 for RENI++ at D=27) and a reconstruction consistency Spearman correlation of 0.50 (vs. 0.17), indicating a substantially more well-behaved latent space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is RENI++ [Gardner et al., 2023], a rotation-equivariant autodecoder that optimizes per-image latent codes. In the direct latent optimization setting (most comparable to RENI++), VENI outperforms RENI++ on all metrics at every latent dimension: at D=27, PSNR improves from 18.02 to 20.33 (+2.31 dB) and PSNR_HDR from 33.00 to 33.92; at D=300, PSNR improves from 22.10 to 22.68 and LPIPS drops from 0.52 to 0.48. The full autoencoder pass is competitive at D=27 (18.78 vs. 18.02 PSNR) but trails RENI++ at higher dimensions (19.47 vs. 22.10 at D=300), reflecting the trade-off between a single forward pass and per-image optimization. On latent-space quality, VENI is dramatically superior: uniqueness MSE drops from 1.46 to 0.04 at D=27, and reconstruction consistency correlation rises from 0.17 to 0.50. On dataset scaling (Table 3), RENI++ degrades from 20.11 to 17.14 PSNR when training data grows from 1,500 to 43,260 images, whereas VENI's AE pass improves from 16.00 to 16.77, confirming that the autoencoder architecture scales better to larger datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RENI++ rotation-equivariant illumination prior by replacing its autodecoder architecture (where latent codes are randomly initialized and optimized per image) with a proper variational autoencoder featuring a learned encoder. The encoder is built on the VN-Transformer framework, which itself relies on the Vector Neurons SO(3)-equivariant building blocks; the key architectural departure is a novel SO(2)-equivariant fully connected layer that reduces the equivariance guarantee from SO(3) to SO(2), exploiting the fact that only rotations about the up-axis produce realistic illumination environments. The decoder is inherited directly from RENI++, making the contribution primarily on the encoder side and the latent-space structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The latent-space uniqueness improvement is practically important for downstream inverse rendering, where a well-structured latent space enables meaningful interpolation and more stable optimization. The finding that RENI++'s autodecoder approach degrades in quality as dataset size grows while the VAE approach improves highlights a scalability limitation of per-image latent optimization that the field should consider. Open questions include whether the SO(2)-equivariant encoder generalizes to other spherical signal tasks beyond illumination, and whether the latent space can support more complex generative or editing operations beyond simple interpolation.
