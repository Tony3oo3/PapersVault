## Problem

Diffusion-based object erasure methods frequently hallucinate unwanted objects (sundries) in the masked region after removing the target, producing contextually inconsistent results. The paper identifies two root causes: training exclusively on synthetic paired data that cannot represent real-world erasure scenarios, and relying solely on pixel-level SFT losses that teach denoising rather than coherent erasure. Existing mitigations (attention modification, geometric priors, synthetic data) still fail in complex open-world scenes.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a binary inpainting mask (derived from entity segmentation) specifying the object to remove, and produces a single erased image in one forward pass using only 2 denoising steps (LCM scheduler). The model is built on Stable Diffusion Inpainting 1.5 (860M parameters) and operates in latent space. Inference runs on a single A100 GPU with a latency of 0.21 s, substantially faster than Flux-based competitors (6–68 s).

## Method

YOEO distills a multi-step teacher denoiser into a 2-step student using Flash Diffusion with LPIPS, DMD, and GAN losses, then adds two pair-free erasure-specific losses trained on real-world unpaired data (1.75M Open Images). The Sundries Suppression (SS) loss feeds the student's output through a pretrained Mask2Former entity segmentor, identifies entities whose IoS with the inpainting mask exceeds 0.9, and penalizes their positive probability and pixel activations to suppress unwanted object generation. The Entity Feature Coherence (EFC) loss computes cosine similarity between segmentation features inside the inpainted region and the mean feature cluster of the corresponding visible entity, enforcing semantic and structural alignment with surrounding context. Training proceeds in three stages: teacher fine-tuning on synthetic paired masks, student distillation on paired data, and joint training on both paired and unpaired data with the SS and EFC losses applied to the unpaired subset.

## Results

On both EntitySeg and COCO, YOEO achieves the lowest sundries metrics by a large margin: MSN of 0.049 (EntitySeg) and 0.22 (COCO), and MARS of 0.005 and 0.017 respectively, substantially outperforming the next-best EntityErasure (MSN 0.122 / 0.51, MARS 0.037 / 0.086). It also attains the best context-consistency score (CFD 0.311 on EntitySeg, 0.528 on COCO). The method runs in 0.21 s per image with only 860 M parameters, far faster than Flux-based competitors (8–68 s) while matching or exceeding their erasure quality. FID is competitive but not the best (58.8 / 22.7 vs. MAT's 50.0 / ASUKA's 19.8), reflecting the trade-off of a smaller SD-1.5 backbone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

YOEO decisively wins on the two sundries metrics (MSN, MARS) across both datasets, reducing MSN by 2.5× over the next-best EntityErasure on EntitySeg (0.049 vs 0.122) and by 2.3× on COCO (0.22 vs 0.51), while MARS drops to 0.005 and 0.017 versus 0.037 and 0.086. It also achieves the best CFD (context consistency) on both benchmarks (0.311 / 0.528), beating EntityErasure (0.363 / 0.584) and OmniPaint (0.407 / 0.705). On FID, YOEO trails MAT (50.0 vs 58.8 on EntitySeg) and ASUKA (19.8 vs 22.7 on COCO), reflecting the smaller SD-1.5 backbone (860 M vs 12 B parameters). Inference speed is a major advantage: at 0.21 s per image, YOEO is ~11× faster than EntityErasure, ~38× faster than ASUKA, and ~326× faster than GeoRemover (68.4 s), while using an order of magnitude fewer parameters than the Flux-based methods. Overall, the paper's method trades a modest FID gap for dramatically fewer hallucinated objects and superior context coherence at a fraction of the compute cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Flash Diffusion's progressive distillation framework and DMD2's distribution-matching objective to create a few-step student denoiser from a Stable Diffusion Inpainting 1.5 teacher. It departs from prior erasure methods (EntityErasure, SmartEraser, ASUKA, GeoRemover) that rely on synthetic paired data or condition-guided pipelines (predicting segmentation/depth before inpainting) by introducing pair-free, task-specific supervision: a sundries suppression loss and an entity feature coherence loss, both computed via a pretrained Mask2Former segmentor. The key conceptual departure is that distilling to few steps first is what makes segmentation-based supervision feasible, since multi-step denoising produces blurred intermediates that obscure entity detection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dramatic reduction in sundries metrics (MSN drops from 0.51 to 0.22 on COCO, MARS from 0.086 to 0.017) while achieving 0.21s inference on a single A100 makes this practically relevant for real-time editing applications where hallucinated objects are unacceptable. However, the FID (58.8) remains competitive but not best-in-class compared to larger Flux-based methods, and the approach is tied to SD-1.5's architecture. Open questions include whether the pair-free supervision strategy transfers to larger foundation models (Flux, SDXL) and whether the IoS-based sundries detection generalizes to non-entity artifacts like texture inconsistencies or color shifts.
