## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models such as VGGT lack explicit outlier-rejection mechanisms, so irrelevant or distractor views in in-the-wild image collections silently corrupt pose estimation and geometry. While classical SfM pipelines handle this through RANSAC and geometric verification, feed-forward architectures have no analogous stage, and prior work has not explored whether their internal representations already encode discriminative signals for view filtering.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260809/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N uncalibrated RGB images (e.g., 30 clean + 10–50 distractors sampled from other scenes) and produces camera poses, per-pixel depth maps, 3D pointmaps, and confidence maps. The pipeline is two-pass: a first forward pass through VGGT extracts final-layer attention and feature maps to score each context view against a query, and the filtered subset is re-fed for the final reconstruction. A single global threshold (τ=0.05 for attention, τ=0.65 for feature similarity) is shared across all datasets without per-scene tuning.

## Method

The paper discovers that VGGT's final-layer cross-view attention and intermediate feature maps exhibit emergent outlier suppression: the gap between clean-view and distractor-view scores grows monotonically with layer depth and peaks at the last layer, despite no explicit noise-aware training. Two training-free probes are computed per view pair: (i) the spatial mean of multi-head-averaged attention weights from query-key projections, and (ii) the spatial mean of pixel-wise cosine similarity between ℓ₂-normalized feature maps. Views whose score falls below a single fixed threshold are rejected, and the surviving context set is re-fed to the unmodified VGGT backbone for final pose, depth, and pointmap prediction. No architectural changes, additional parameters, or fine-tuning are required; the entire filtering mechanism is a post-hoc scoring-and-thresholding step on internal representations already produced during inference.

## Results

On camera pose estimation, RobustVGGT-𝓕 achieves the best average ATE across three of four datasets: 0.2650 on Phototourism, 0.0583 on On-the-Go, and 0.1379 on RobustNeRF, while RobustVGGT-𝓐 leads on ETH3D (0.7331 ATE). Critically, RobustVGGT-𝓕's ATE on On-the-Go degrades only from 0.0521 (Small) to 0.0660 (Large) noise, versus VGGT's 0.0788→0.1562, demonstrating near-constant performance under increasing distractor counts. On multi-view depth (ETH3D), RobustVGGT-𝓕 attains the best average AbsRel of 0.0301 and δ<1.25 of 0.9829, outperforming VGGT (0.0419 / 0.9730) and MegaLoc*+VGGT (0.0325 / 0.9813).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobustVGGT-𝓕 outperforms unfiltered VGGT by 24% in average ATE on Phototourism (0.2650 vs. 0.3504) and 52% on On-the-Go (0.0583 vs. 0.1210), with the gap widening at higher noise levels. Against the strongest pre-filtering baseline MegaLoc*+VGGT (which uses oracle-tuned thresholds), RobustVGGT-𝓕 still wins on Phototourism (0.2650 vs. 0.2853) and On-the-Go (0.0583 vs. 0.0582, essentially tied) while using a single global threshold. DINOv3+VGGT and DINOv2†+VGGT produce results nearly identical to raw VGGT (e.g., 0.3504 vs. 0.3504 ATE on Phototourism), confirming that appearance-driven global descriptors fail to filter geometrically irrelevant views. MASt3R-SfM is competitive on RobustNeRF (0.1177 vs. 0.1379 ATE) due to its iterative bundle adjustment on small, highly-overlapping scenes, but is substantially worse on all other datasets. On depth estimation (ETH3D), RobustVGGT-𝓕 achieves the best AbsRel (0.0301) and δ<1.25 (0.9829), beating MegaLoc*+VGGT (0.0325 / 0.9813) and VGGT (0.0419 / 0.9730).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VGGT, a feed-forward transformer-based 3D reconstruction model, and repurposes its internal attention and feature representations as a training-free outlier-detection signal. It departs from classical SfM pipelines (COLMAP) that rely on explicit geometric verification and RANSAC-based rejection, and from VPR-based pre-filtering approaches (MegaLoc, DINOv2/v3) that require per-scene hyperparameter tuning. The key conceptual departure is the discovery that VGGT's late-layer representations, trained solely for multi-view geometric consistency, emergently encode a discriminative signal separating geometrically consistent views from distractors—eliminating the need for any additional module, retraining, or hand-tuned retrieval pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single global threshold on VGGT's internal representations generalizes across four diverse datasets and three noise levels offers a practical, zero-cost robustness mechanism for feed-forward 3D reconstruction in in-the-wild settings. However, the analysis is limited to VGGT; whether the same emergent property holds in DUSt3R, Pi3, or MASt3R remains untested. Open questions include whether the threshold can be made adaptive to scene complexity, whether the signal degrades at very high distractor ratios beyond 50, and how this approach composes with large-scale retrieval pipelines where VPR still handles the initial candidate pool. The paper also notes that on small, highly overlapping scenes (RobustNeRF), global view-graph optimization (MASt3R-SfM) still outperforms, suggesting the benefit is most pronounced in large, heterogeneous collections.
