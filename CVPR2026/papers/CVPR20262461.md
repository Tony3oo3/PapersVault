## Problem

Existing jigsaw puzzle solvers operate exclusively on square pieces with fixed or linear gaps, relying on boundary-matching strategies that fail when erosion eliminates original edge information. This setting bears little resemblance to real-world archaeological reconstruction, where fragments are irregularly shaped, heavily eroded, and separated by variable non-linear spacing. The paper addresses this gap by introducing both a realistic benchmark (GAP) and a solver (PuzzleFlow) capable of handling arbitrary fragment geometries.

## Input / Output

The method takes N shuffled RGBA puzzle pieces (128×128 px each) from a k×k grid (k=3 or 5), where the alpha channel explicitly encodes the irregular fragment mask. At inference, it performs 20 steps of iterative refinement starting from a random permutation, using greedy O(N²) assignment at each step to produce the final piece-to-position permutation. The output is a discrete permutation over grid positions, from which the reconstructed puzzle layout is assembled.

## Method

PuzzleFlow formulates puzzle reassembly as discrete flow matching over the symmetric group: a time-dependent distribution p_t(π_t|X) interpolates from a uniform random permutation at t=0 to the correct permutation at t=1 via stochastic interpolation (each piece jumps to its target with probability t). Visual features are extracted by a fine-tuned ViT-Base with a learned 1×1 convolution projecting RGBA→RGB to preserve shape information from the alpha channel. Four transformer encoder layers enable holistic relational reasoning across all piece representations, conditioned on learned position embeddings and sinusoidal time embeddings. The VAE-based fragment generator, trained on 958 real archaeological masks from the REPAIR dataset, produces the irregular shapes used in the GAP benchmark, validated against real fragments across eight geometric features.

## Results

On GAP-3 (3×3, 9 pieces), PuzzleFlow achieves 28.5% Perfect Accuracy (PA), 62.9% Absolute Accuracy (AA), and 55.7% Spatial Relationship Accuracy (SRA), outperforming the strongest baseline FCViT (25.2% PA, 60.7% AA, 47.6% SRA). On the harder GAP-5 (5×5, 25 pieces), PuzzleFlow reaches 0.3% PA, 29.1% AA, and 19.8% SRA, beating the next-best DiffAssemble (0.0% PA, 21.9% AA, 14.7% SRA) by +7.2 AA and +5.1 SRA points. Classical methods (Greedy, GA) and several deep learning baselines (JPDVT, PuzLM) collapse to near-random performance (0% PA, 11–15% AA) on both GAP datasets, confirming that irregular fragment geometries and erosion break boundary-matching assumptions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PuzzleFlow wins on all three metrics across both GAP-3 and GAP-5 against all seven baselines. On GAP-3, the closest competitor is FCViT [22] (25.2% PA, 60.7% AA, 47.6% SRA), which PuzzleFlow surpasses by +3.3 PA, +2.2 AA, and notably +8.1 SRA points, indicating superior spatial coherence. On GAP-5, DiffAssemble [44] is the strongest baseline (21.9% AA, 14.7% SRA), and PuzzleFlow extends its lead to +7.2 AA and +5.1 SRA, showing the performance gap widens with puzzle complexity. Classical solvers (Greedy [39], GA [46]) and boundary-matching deep methods (JPDVT [29], PuzLM [12]) degrade to near-random performance (0% PA, 11–15% AA) on both GAP datasets, confirming that irregular erosion eliminates the edge-continuity cues these methods depend on. JigsawGAN [26] retains moderate performance on GAP-3 (4.6% PA, 45.3% AA) but drops to 0% PA on GAP-5, while FCViT [22] also falls to 0% PA on GAP-5 despite leading among baselines on GAP-3. The widening gap from GAP-3 to GAP-5 validates that PuzzleFlow's holistic relational reasoning across entire fragment surfaces partially survives the combinatorial explosion (9! → 25!), whereas local boundary-feature methods do not.

## Relation to Prior Work

PuzzleFlow extends the discrete flow matching framework (Lipman et al. 2022; Alfonso et al. 2023) from continuous generative modeling to the combinatorial problem of permutation prediction, using stochastic interpolation over the symmetric group. The broader puzzle-solving lineage it departs from has progressed from hand-crafted boundary-similarity solvers (greedy, genetic algorithms) through CNN-based compatibility networks (DNN-Buddies, Deepzzle, JigsawGAN) to transformer and diffusion architectures (FCViT, JPDVT, DiffAssemble), but all of these operate exclusively on square pieces with fixed or linear gaps. The key conceptual departure here is twofold: the VAE-generated irregular fragment shapes learned from the REPAIR archaeological dataset replace the square geometry, and the learned RGBA-to-RGB projection preserves shape information that square-piece methods simply discard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The GAP datasets (40,000 puzzles total) fill a genuine gap between simplified academic benchmarks and the geometric complexity of real archaeological reconstruction, and their public release with a VAE generator makes them a reusable, extensible testbed. The finding that boundary-matching and local-feature methods collapse to near-random performance on irregular fragments (0% PA for Greedy, GA, JPDVT, PuzLM) underscores that the field's implicit reliance on edge continuity is a serious limitation for heritage applications. However, even PuzzleFlow's 28.5% PA on GAP-3 and 0.3% on GAP-5 leave substantial headroom, and the method still assumes a regular grid topology, does not handle missing fragments, and has not been validated on truly unstructured archaeological layouts—key open questions for practical deployment.
