## Problem

Human sketches are inherently ambiguous: a single sketch can correspond to multiple plausible images, and natural images often contain background or co-occurring objects unrelated to the sketch intent. Prior zero-shot sketch-based image retrieval methods treat all sketch-image training pairs as equally informative, so ambiguous pairs act as noisy supervision that causes model distraction and performance collapse, particularly in fine-grained retrieval. This paper is the first to formally quantify this ambiguity and show its measurable impact on retrieval quality.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 224×224 query sketch and a gallery of 224×224 natural images, along with per-category semantic labels used to construct CLIP text prompts ("a photo of [category]") for AmbiScore computation. At inference time, the system is a standard dual-encoder producing a ranked image list; all ambiguity-aware components (E-Match, P-Match) operate exclusively during training, adding zero inference cost. For downstream adaptation, the pre-trained SBIR encoder's latent distance serves as a supervisory signal for segmentation (producing binary masks) and generation (producing photorealistic images).

## Method

DisAmb builds on a dual ViT-B/16 encoder (weight-shared for sketch and image) and introduces two training-only components. Elastic Matching computes an AmbiScore per image via CLIP image-text similarity against category prompts, then adaptively scales the triplet loss margin: ambiguous pairs receive smaller margins (less strict supervision) while clean pairs receive larger margins, preventing noisy pairs from corrupting the embedding space. Purified Matching uses Grounded SAM (Grounding DINO for localization + SAM for segmentation) to generate ambiguity-agnostic binary masks that isolate sketch-relevant regions, then applies two auxiliary tasks on the masked features: a shape jigsaw task that shuffles sketch patches and requires the model to recover spatial order against masked image features, and a texture swapping task that uses AdaIN style transfer to create synthetic negatives sharing the same shape but different texture, enforcing semantic rather than shape-based matching. The total loss combines the elastic triplet loss with weighted shape jigsaw and texture swapping losses, all computed offline for masks and scores to keep training efficient.

## Results

On ZS-SBIR, C-DisAmb (CLIP-based) achieves 0.812 mAP@200 and 0.789 Prec@200 on Sketchy, 0.707 mAP and 0.760 Prec@100 on TU-Berlin, and 0.245 mAP and 0.412 Prec@200 on QuickDraw, setting new state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks. The ViT-B/16 variant (B-DisAmb) reaches 0.682 mAP@200 on Sketchy, surpassing the previous best ViT-based method (Sketch3T, 0.575) by 10.7 points. On fine-grained ZS-FG-SBIR (Sketchy), C-DisAmb attains 33.83% acc@1 and 69.61% acc@5, outperforming SketchFusion (33.01/67.92) and SD-PL (31.94/65.81).

## Baselines & Comparisons

C-DisAmb outperforms the strongest foundation-model baselines on all three ZS-SBIR benchmarks: it gains +5.1 mAP@200 over SketchFusion (0.812 vs. 0.761) and +6.6 over SD-PL (0.812 vs. 0.746) on Sketchy, while also leading on TU-Berlin (+1.2 mAP) and QuickDraw (+0.3 mAP). The ViT-B/16 variant B-DisAmb surpasses all prior ViT-based methods by a wide margin, improving mAP@200 on Sketchy from 0.575 (Sketch3T) to 0.682 (+10.7) and from 0.525 (ZSE-SBIR) to 0.682 (+15.7). On fine-grained ZS-FG-SBIR, C-DisAmb achieves 33.83% acc@1, edging out SketchFusion (33.01%) and SD-PL (31.94%), while B-DisAmb (28.09%) beats ZSE-SBIR (23.97%) by 4.12 points despite both using masking mechanisms. The paper also shows that under high AmbiScore intervals (>0.6), SketchLVM's robustness collapses sharply whereas DisAmb maintains consistent gains, confirming that ambiguity-aware supervision is the key differentiator rather than backbone strength alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ZS-SBIR line of work (SAKE, Sketch3T, ZSE-SBIR, SketchLVM, SketchFusion) by reframing the core challenge as one of noisy cross-modal supervision rather than purely an architectural or alignment problem. It borrows the conceptual toolkit from the noisy-label literature (sample re-weighting, co-teaching, loss correction) and applies it to sketch-image matching for the first time, using CLIP-based semantic relevance (AmbiScore) as a proxy for label noise. The purified matching component repurposes Grounded SAM (Grounding DINO + SAM) for mask generation and introduces shape jigsaw and texture swapping as auxiliary objectives, drawing on masked-image-modeling and style-transfer ideas respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ambiguity lens is broadly applicable: any sketch-guided task (retrieval, segmentation, generation) inherits the same noisy-supervision problem, and the paper demonstrates that a purified SBIR encoder transfers as a supervisory signal to downstream tasks with reduced data requirements. A key open question is whether AmbiScore's reliance on hand-crafted CLIP prompts generalizes to open-vocabulary or multi-object sketches, a limitation the authors acknowledge and suggest addressing with MLLMs. The finding that SBIR models exhibit near-identical performance on binary masks, edge maps, and full images (Table 5) raises the broader question of whether the field has been optimizing shape matching rather than true semantic correspondence.
