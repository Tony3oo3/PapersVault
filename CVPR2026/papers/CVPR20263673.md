## Problem

Unregistered HSI super-resolution methods typically rely on explicit alignment (e.g., pre-trained optical flow) before fusion, which introduces textural distortions and artifacts due to resolution and distribution disparities. Additionally, coupled spatial-spectral fusion constrains the learning capacity of the network. This paper addresses both issues by reframing the task through spectral unmixing, which decouples the problem into learning residual abundance maps guided by an unregistered reference, making the formulation inherently more robust to geometric misalignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution HSI (h×w×B) and an unregistered high-resolution RGB reference (H×W×b) as input, and produces a high-resolution HSI (H×W×B) as output. The LR HSI is first upsampled to the target spatial resolution, then decomposed via SVD into K=3 endmembers and an initial abundance map; the network learns a residual abundance map that, when mixed with the fixed endmembers and added to the upsampled HSI, yields the final reconstruction. Experiments cover scale factors of ×4, ×8, and ×16 on both simulated (ICVL) and real (REAL) datasets.

## Method

The framework is a multi-scale encoder-decoder (C=64 channels) built around three key modules. First, a Coarse-to-Fine Deformable Aggregation (CFDA) module estimates a pixel-level flow via a lightweight pyramid predictor, then refines it with sub-pixel frequency positional encoding and deformable convolution to implicitly aggregate unregistered reference features without explicit warping. Second, a Spatial-Channel Abundance Cross-Attention (SCACA) block applies window-based spatial cross-attention followed by channel cross-attention, where the Value vectors are modulated by the aggregated reference features to refine the abundance representation. Third, a Spatial-Channel Modulated Fusion (SCMF) module in the decoder uses parallel spatial and channel gating pathways to dynamically merge encoder-decoder features. The entire network is trained end-to-end with L1 loss under a supervised paradigm, and the final HSI is reconstructed by mixing the learned residual abundance with the SVD-derived endmembers and adding the result to the upsampled input HSI.

## Results

On the simulated ICVL dataset (×4), the method achieves a PSNR of 41.84 dB, SSIM of 0.986, and SAM of 0.025, outperforming the closest competitor SSCH-S (41.38 dB). On the real-world REAL dataset, it leads across all scale factors: 42.05 dB (×4), 37.23 dB (×8), and 32.28 dB (×16) PSNR, with the gap over SSCH-S widening to 0.37 dB at ×16. The method also achieves this with only 5.94M parameters and 96.17G FLOPs, roughly half the parameters and 42% fewer FLOPs than SSCH-S (11.01M, 165.68G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric (PSNR, SSIM, SAM) across both datasets and all scale factors. The closest competitor is SSCH-S [53], which also uses a pre-trained flow model for alignment; the proposed method beats it by 0.89 dB (×4), 1.04 dB (×8), and 0.37 dB (×16) PSNR on REAL, while using roughly half the parameters (5.94M vs. 11.01M) and 42% fewer FLOPs. Among single-HSI SR methods, ESSA [49] is the strongest (39.94 dB at ×4 on REAL), but the proposed method surpasses it by 2.11 dB, demonstrating the clear benefit of reference-guided fusion. Unregistered methods like NonReg [56] and MoE-PNP [38] lag significantly (31.60 and 32.57 dB at ×4), with NonReg showing severe spectral degradation (SAM 0.584 at ×16). Registered methods LRTN [30] and SRLF [31] are competitive at larger scales (31.41 and 31.12 dB at ×16) but still trail the proposed method by 0.87 and 1.16 dB respectively, confirming the advantage of the unmixing-based decoupling strategy over direct spatial-spectral fusion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage paradigm for unregistered HSI super-resolution (explicit alignment via optical flow or spatial transformers, followed by spatial-spectral coupled fusion, as in SSCH [53] and HSIFN [21]) by reframing the problem through spectral unmixing. It repurposes the low-rank SVD decomposition to decouple endmembers from abundance maps, then applies a coarse-to-fine deformable aggregation module (building on DCNv2 and sub-pixel positional encoding from video SR [45]) and hierarchical cross-attention (inspired by SpecAT [47]) to enhance the abundance maps using the unregistered reference. The conceptual lineage traces back to work [9] showing unmixing-based methods are inherently robust to geometric misalignment, but this paper is the first to operationalize that insight into a full end-to-end deep learning pipeline with implicit feature aggregation rather than explicit pixel-level warping.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unmixing-based decoupling offers a principled way to handle unregistered fusion without the textural artifacts that plague explicit alignment, and the resulting efficiency (5.94M params, 96.17G FLOPs — roughly half the parameters of SSCH-S) makes the approach more deployable in resource-constrained remote sensing settings. However, the fixed number of endmembers (K=3) and the assumption of a global low-rank structure may limit generalization to scenes with high spectral diversity or complex non-rigid misalignments. Open questions include whether the abundance-residual formulation extends to multi-reference or multi-scale unregistered settings, and whether the SVD-based initialization remains effective when the LR HSI is heavily degraded by noise or mixed pixels.
