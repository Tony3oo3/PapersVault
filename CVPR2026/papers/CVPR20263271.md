## Problem

Existing video character replacement methods rely on a reconstruction-based paradigm that requires dense per-frame segmentation masks and explicit structural guidance (skeletons, depth maps), which are error-prone in complex scenarios involving occlusions, unusual poses, and multi-character interactions. This dependency propagates inaccuracies into the generation process, causing visual artifacts and temporal inconsistencies, while also incurring substantial computational overhead. No prior method achieves end-to-end character replacement using only a single arbitrary frame mask without any structural priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video, a single binary mask from any arbitrary frame (not a per-frame sequence), and one or more reference character images as input, producing a character-replaced video that preserves the original background, scene dynamics, and character motion. The mask can be placed at any frame index, and the number of reference images is flexible (one base reference plus an optional face-centric reference). All inputs are encoded at 480×832 resolution, and the model supports variable output video lengths (trained on 21 to 81 frames) without architectural modification.

## Method

MoCha builds on the Wan-2.1-T2V-14B DiT backbone under the Rectified Flow framework and employs in-context condition training, where source video, mask, and reference image latents are concatenated along the temporal dimension with the target video tokens and processed jointly by full self-attention. The key architectural contribution is a condition-aware RoPE that assigns shared frame indices to source and target tokens, a fixed index of −1 to reference images (distinguished by spatial offsets), and a variable index to the single mask based on its designated frame, enabling arbitrary mask placement and flexible reference counts. The core insight is that the video diffusion model's inherent temporal tracking ability allows a single-frame mask to implicitly identify the character across all frames, eliminating the need for per-frame masks or structural guidance. Training proceeds in two stages: (1) fine-tuning all self-attention layers on 100K paired samples from three synthetic/real data sources (UE5-rendered pairs, Flux+LivePortrait expression-driven pairs, and augmented VIVID10M/VPData video-mask pairs), and (2) an RL-based post-training stage using LoRA (rank 64) that optimizes an ArcFace cosine-similarity facial reward combined with pixel-wise MSE, backpropagating only through the final K sampling steps to enhance identity consistency without degrading generation quality.

## Results

On the synthetic benchmark, MoCha achieves SSIM 0.746, LPIPS 0.152, and PSNR 23.09, substantially outperforming the best baseline Wan-Animate (0.692, 0.213, 19.20) by 7.8% in SSIM and 28.6% in LPIPS. On the real-world benchmark evaluated with VBench, MoCha leads on Subject Consistency (92.25), Background Consistency (94.40), Aesthetic Quality (60.24), and Motion Smoothness (98.79), while matching the best baseline on Temporal Flickering (97.98). The method is the only one to exceed 60 on Aesthetic Quality, indicating superior visual realism.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoCha outperforms all three open-source baselines (VACE, HunyuanCustom, Wan-Animate) on every VBench metric in the real-world benchmark, with the largest margin on Aesthetic Quality (+3.5 over HunyuanCustom) and Subject Consistency (+1.0 over Wan-Animate). On the synthetic benchmark, MoCha's advantage is even more pronounced: it surpasses Wan-Animate by 5.9 dB in PSNR (23.09 vs. 19.20) and reduces LPIPS by 28.6% (0.152 vs. 0.213). VACE is the weakest baseline across all metrics, particularly on Subject Consistency (71.19 vs. 92.25), reflecting its reliance on structural guidance that degrades under complex scenes. Kling is included only in qualitative comparison (Fig. 5) because its commercial API does not support automated batch evaluation; qualitatively, Kling preserves reference identity but fails to maintain the original character motion and lighting. The paper notes that reconstruction-based methods (VACE, Wan-Animate) lose original video information through their mask-and-reconstruct pipeline, which explains their inferior lighting and shading fidelity compared to MoCha's end-to-end approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoCha departs from the dominant reconstruction-based paradigm for video character replacement (VACE, HunyuanCustom, Wan-Animate), which requires dense per-frame segmentation masks and explicit structural guidance such as skeletons or depth maps. Instead, it builds on the in-context condition training framework demonstrated by Recammaster and FullDiT, concatenating multi-modal conditions (source video, single frame mask, reference images) along the temporal axis of a DiT backbone (Wan-2.1-T2V-14B) with a novel condition-aware RoPE to handle variable-length generation and arbitrary mask placement. The RL-based post-training stage with an ArcFace reward extends the diffusion post-training literature (DPO-style alignment) to the specific objective of facial identity preservation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By showing that a video diffusion model's implicit temporal reasoning can substitute for explicit per-frame tracking, MoCha removes a major practical bottleneck in character replacement pipelines and opens the door to more robust handling of occlusions, unusual poses, and multi-character interactions where dense masks and structural priors fail. The comprehensive three-source data construction pipeline (UE5-rendered, expression-driven, and augmented real-video pairs) addresses a critical data scarcity problem that has limited progress in this area. Open questions include how well the single-mask tracking generalizes to very long videos or extreme occlusion, whether the tracking ability can be further exploited for other video editing tasks, and whether the heavy reliance on synthetic training data limits real-world fidelity in edge cases.
