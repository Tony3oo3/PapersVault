## Problem

Embodied agents trained on action-centric tasks (VLN) or reasoning-centric tasks (EQA) lack a learning signal that forces them to encode fine-grained spatial relationships—topology, distance, and relations—over long-range, fragmented experiences. Imitation learning overfits to expert trajectories, and LLM-based reasoning relies on linguistic priors detached from a grounded world model. The paper argues that a structured, queriable cognitive map is the missing module that converts raw {observation, action} streams into a consistent spatial representation enabling robust generalization.

## Input / Output

At each timestep the agent receives an RGB observation, a natural-language navigation instruction, and an optional cognitive query (categorized as retrospective, introspective, or prospective). The RGB image is processed by two frozen encoders—SigLIP for 2D semantic features and VGGT for 3D geometric features—fused via 8-head cross-attention before entering the memory system. The model autoregressively outputs a navigation action (encoded as special tokens in the VLM vocabulary, e.g., 'move forward 75cm') and, when a query is present, a free-text answer; internally it also maintains a single-vector latent cognitive map that is not directly exposed but conditions the LLM's reasoning.

## Method

LASAR maintains a dual-memory system: an episodic memory storing the temporal sequence of geometry-aware visual features, and a spatial semantic memory that generates a latent cognitive map by cross-attending an attention-pooled context vector against a learnable 512-code Spatial Semantic Atlas. A Qwen2-7B LLM backbone integrates the instruction embedding, sampled episodic frames (32 via LongVILA), and the cognitive map to produce the action and answer. The core training innovation is ST-CRL, a contrastive objective applied to a query-conditioned 'Activated Cognitive Map' extracted from the LLM's hidden state at a special [MAP] token; it constructs InfoNCE pairs using spatial hard negatives (same query, different answer), semantic hard negatives (same region, different query), and unrelated distractors, with region IDs from the simulator as privileged training-only signals. Two auxiliary losses—vector-quantization grounding of atlas primitives to visual features and an episodic-discriminability contrastive loss—stabilize the representation. The MindCraft dataset, procedurally generated on VLN-CE trajectories, injects concurrent cognitive queries into navigation episodes to supply the supervisory signal for ST-CRL.

## Results

On the MindCraft-Test benchmark, LASAR (Ours) achieves 65.3% QA-Acc, 70.4% GCA, and 75.8% CMC, outperforming the naive multi-task baseline LASAR (IL+QA) by +4.7, +7.2, and +5.7 percentage points respectively. On downstream navigation, LASAR improves over NaVILA by +2.2% SR and +4.9% SPL on R2R val-unseen, and +2.8% SR and +3.9% SPL on RxR val-unseen. On the zero-shot VSI-Bench reasoning benchmark, LASAR achieves 48.9 average score, surpassing all LLM-based VLN baselines and even Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.4), while the navigation-only variant (LASAR IL) scores only 37.8, highlighting the critical role of ST-CRL in building transferable spatial representations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LASAR (Ours) outperforms all retrained embodied navigation baselines across every evaluated metric. On R2R val-unseen, it surpasses NaVILA (the strongest navigation baseline) by +2.2% SR and +4.9% SPL, and Uni-NaVid by +8.9% SR and +9.8% SPL. On RxR val-unseen, the gains over NaVILA are +2.8% SR and +3.9% SPL. On the MindCraft-Test reasoning benchmark, LASAR leads all baselines in QA-Acc (65.3% vs. 60.6% for LASAR IL+QA, 56.6% for NaVid) and CMC (75.8% vs. 70.1% for IL+QA), with the largest margin over NavCoT (+17.1% QA-Acc). On zero-shot VSI-Bench, LASAR achieves 48.9 average, beating Gemini-1.5 Pro (45.4) and GPT-4o (34.0), while the ablation LASAR (IL+QA) without ST-CRL drops to 44.8, confirming that the contrastive objective—not merely multi-task training—drives the generalization gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of action-centric VLN (building on VLN-CE, Krantz et al. ECCV 2020) and reasoning-centric EQA, arguing that both paradigms in isolation fail to encode fine-grained spatial structure. Architecturally, it draws on dual-memory systems from cognitive science (episodic + semantic), vector-quantized codebooks (VQ-VAE lineage), and LLM-based agent frameworks (Qwen2-7B backbone with SigLIP/VGGT front-ends). The key conceptual departure is ST-CRL: rather than using raw sensory proximity or data augmentations as contrastive signals (as in prior spatiotemporal SSL), it uses spatio-temporal cognitive queries with simulator-privileged region metadata to sculpt a task-aware latent cognitive map, effectively repurposing contrastive learning as a high-level spatial-logic inductive bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a concrete recipe for bridging the long-standing action/reasoning divide in embodied AI: the MindCraft task provides the supervisory signal, and ST-CRL converts it into a structured latent representation that simultaneously improves reasoning accuracy and navigation quality. However, several limitations temper the impact: the region_id used for negative sampling is privileged simulator information unavailable at inference, all experiments are confined to Matterport3D-based VLN-CE environments, and the absolute gains (2–5% on navigation, ~4–5% on reasoning) are moderate. Open questions include whether the cognitive map generalizes to open-world or multi-floor environments, whether the LLM backbone is truly necessary for the query-conditioned map refinement, and whether the approach scales beyond the 512-code atlas without additional regularization.
