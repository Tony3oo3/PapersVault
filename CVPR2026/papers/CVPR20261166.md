## Problem

Autoregressive models struggle with general image editing due to weak conditioning: decoding-stage injection methods (e.g., ControlAR) disrupt the autoregressive process with strong spatial guidance, while prefilling-stage methods (e.g., EditAR) rely on VQ tokens that double sequence length and carry only sparse, low-level semantics insufficient for complex instruction-driven edits.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261166/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For controllable generation, the method takes a control condition map (Canny, Depth, Normal, HED, or Sketch) plus a class label or text prompt and produces a 256×256 or 512×512 image; for instruction editing, it takes a source RGB image and a natural-language editing instruction and outputs the edited image at 512×512. The source image is encoded by a frozen DINOv2 into 1024 semantic tokens, which are then compressed 4× to 256 tokens before being prepended to the autoregressive sequence, keeping the prefix compact while preserving high-level structure.

## Method

SCAR introduces two components within a prefilling-stage conditioning framework. First, Compressed Semantic Prefilling replaces VQ token prefixes with rich DINOv2 features reduced by a learnable module combining spatial downsampling and parallel stride convolution (4× compression from 1024 to 256 tokens), trained with a pixel-shuffle-based semantic preservation loss to retain essential information while discarding redundancy. Second, Semantic Alignment Guidance computes the target image's compressed DINOv2 representation and applies an L2 alignment loss between the AR model's last hidden states at the source-semantic positions and this target representation, providing dense in-context supervision that steers internal reasoning toward the edit goal before any VQ token is generated. The input sequence is [compressed source semantics; text embedding; target VQ tokens] with bidirectional attention between the semantic prefix and text, and causal attention for VQ generation, making the approach compatible with both next-token (LlamaGen) and next-set (VAR) autoregressive paradigms with minimal architectural changes.

## Results

On C2I controllable generation (ImageNet-256), SCAR with VAR-d20 achieves FID 1.97 on Canny and 3.29 on Depth, substantially outperforming the best prior AR method ControlAR (FID 7.69/4.19 with LlamaGen-L) and ControlVAR (FID 7.85/6.50 with VAR-d30). On T2I controllable generation (MultiGen-20M), SCAR (LlamaGen-XL) achieves the lowest FID across all four conditions (e.g., HED: 8.41 vs. 10.53 for ControlAR; Depth: 13.77 vs. 14.61). On instruction editing (PIE-Bench), SCAR reduces structure distance to 30.98 (vs. 39.43 for EditAR and 116.99 for ControlAR*), LPIPS to 105.09, and MSE to 83.47, while achieving the best PSNR (22.59) and whole-image CLIP similarity (25.08).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCAR substantially outperforms all AR-based baselines. Against ControlAR [28] (decoding-stage injection), SCAR reduces Canny FID from 7.69 to 1.97 (VAR-d20) and HED FID from 10.53 to 8.41 (T2I), while on instruction editing it cuts structure distance from 116.99 to 30.98 and MSE from 590.63 to 83.47. Against EditAR [42] (prefilling with VQ tokens), SCAR improves structure distance (30.98 vs. 39.43), LPIPS (105.09 vs. 117.15), and MSE (83.47 vs. 130.27) on PIE-Bench, while matching or exceeding CLIP similarity. Against next-set AR methods ControlVAR [26] and CAR [71], SCAR achieves dramatically lower FID (e.g., 1.97 vs. 7.85 on Canny; 1.51 vs. 5.60 on HED). Against diffusion-based methods (ControlNet [75], T2I-Adapter [40], ControlNet++ [25], GLIGEN [27], Uni-ControlNet [78], UniControl [47]), SCAR achieves the best FID on all four T2I conditions (e.g., HED: 8.41 vs. 15.01 for ControlNet++; Depth: 13.77 vs. 16.66) while remaining competitive on consistency metrics. On instruction editing, SCAR outperforms all diffusion-based editors (InstructPix2Pix [2], InstructDiffusion [11], MGIE [9], SEED-X-Edit [10]) on structure distance, LPIPS, MSE, and PSNR, confirming that prefilling-based semantic conditioning is superior to both decoding-stage injection and diffusion inversion for AR editing.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCAR revisits the prefilling-stage conditioning paradigm established by EditAR and unified multimodal models, identifying its core weakness as reliance on semantically sparse VQ tokens that are both computationally expensive (doubling sequence length) and lacking in high-level representation. It departs from this lineage by replacing VQ prefilling with compressed DINOv2 semantic features and adding an alignment loss that guides internal hidden states toward target semantics, while explicitly contrasting with decoding-stage injection methods (ControlAR, CAR) that the authors argue disrupt the autoregressive process for general editing. The method is designed to be paradigm-agnostic, working with both next-token (LlamaGen) and next-set (VAR) AR architectures with minimal architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As AR models gain traction for unified multimodal generation and understanding, efficient and semantically rich conditioning becomes a critical bottleneck; SCAR's 4× compression (reducing GPU memory by ~24% and accelerating training by 1.42×) while maintaining or improving quality offers a practical path toward scalable AR editing. The conceptual reframing—that the prefilling bottleneck is semantic rather than merely computational—may influence how future UMMs design their visual conditioning. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video editing and larger parameter regimes, and whether the DINOv2-based semantic prefix can be replaced by even more compact or task-adaptive representations.
