## Problem

Reconstructing dynamic 3D scenes from monocular UAV video requires simultaneously recovering camera poses and modeling scene dynamics, yet existing methods handle only one of these: pose-free 3DGS assumes scene rigidity and misattributes moving objects as outliers, while dynamic reconstruction methods depend on SfM/SLAM poses that fail in texture-sparse, long-baseline aerial sequences. The coupling between camera ego-motion and object motion, compounded by extreme scale variation (pedestrians spanning from a few pixels at high altitude to fine-grained articulations at low altitude), causes degenerate optimization when both are solved jointly without explicit decoupling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a long monocular UAV video with no camera poses, processing frames incrementally through a sliding window: the first N_init=3 frames are bootstrapped via MASt3R for initial poses and point-cloud anchor initialization, and each subsequent frame is localized via PnP-RANSAC before joint photometric optimization within a visibility-adapted local window. The output is a 4D dynamic Gaussian scene represented as hierarchical octree S²A-Anchors (each holding a 32D canonical feature, static confidence, and motion parameters), from which camera trajectories, per-frame rendered images, and clean static/dynamic layer decompositions are all derived.

## Method

AeroGS introduces S²A-Anchors, scale-aware spatio-temporal anchors on an incremental octree, unified by three decoupling mechanisms. First, ego-motion and object-motion are separated via a learnable static confidence α_i per anchor that performs soft gradient routing: high-α anchors contribute to pose gradients while low-α anchors are supervised by a self-supervised 3D flow cycle-consistency loss, with entropy regularization pushing α_i toward binary assignments. Second, appearance and deformation are decoupled at the representation level: a time-invariant canonical feature f_i feeds a lightweight MLP decoder for Gaussian attributes, while a Hierarchical Spatio-Temporal Deformation (HSTD) module with multi-level B-spline control points and motion-guided modulation predicts per-frame positional offsets, gated by (1−α_i) so static anchors remain undeformed. Third, scale and complexity are decoupled by conditioning HSTD control-point networks on positional encodings of spatial scale γ(s_i) (suppressing overfitting at coarse scales) and adaptively densifying anchors whose temporal deformation variance exceeds a threshold (allocating capacity to motion-complex regions). The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a joint loss combining static photometric/geometric reconstruction, dynamic cycle consistency, entropy, and sparsity terms, optimized incrementally with periodic global synchronization and a final 10,000-iteration refinement.

## Results

On VisDrone, AeroGS (pose-free) achieves 29.40 dB PSNR for scene reconstruction and 28.56 dB for novel view synthesis, surpassing the strongest pose-required baseline DeSiRe-GS by +1.09 dB and +1.57 dB respectively, with LPIPS of 0.228 vs. 0.262. On UAVDT, gains are larger: 27.77 dB PSNR (recon) and 26.07 dB (NVS), beating PVG by +2.71 dB and DeSiRe-GS by +2.16 dB. On KITTI Odometry, AeroGS reduces ATE to 0.006 m (57% lower than LongSplat's 0.014 m) and RPE_t to 0.143 (72% lower than LongSplat's 0.512), while also improving rendering PSNR to 26.10 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AeroGS outperforms all 10 baselines across both VisDrone and UAVDT. Against the strongest pose-required dynamic methods, it beats DeSiRe-GS by +1.09 dB (VisDrone recon) and +2.16 dB (UAVDT NVS), and PVG by +1.46 dB (VisDrone recon) and +2.71 dB (UAVDT recon), despite not using ground-truth poses. Among pose-free static baselines, AeroGS surpasses HT-3DGS by +4.68 dB and LongSplat by +5.24 dB on VisDrone reconstruction PSNR, demonstrating that joint pose-dynamics optimization far exceeds treating dynamic content as outliers. On KITTI, AeroGS reduces ATE by 57% and RPE_t by 72% relative to LongSplat, while the naive CF-3DGS+4DGS combination fails catastrophically (PSNR drops to 19.03 dB, ATE inflates to 0.060 m), confirming that explicit decoupling is essential for stable joint optimization. Grid4D and AD-GS, despite being pose-required, underperform AeroGS by 1.9–3.8 dB on UAVDT, indicating that scale-aware modeling is particularly critical for altitude-varying aerial scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

AeroGS sits at the intersection of three research threads that have each addressed only part of the problem: pose-free 3DGS (e.g., LongSplat, CF-3DGS) assumes static scenes; dynamic 4DGS methods (e.g., 4DGS, Grid4D, DeSiRe-GS) require accurate SfM/SLAM poses; and dynamic SLAM systems rely on short-baseline matching or RGB-D sensors. The paper builds its implementation directly on LongSplat's incremental pose-free pipeline and bootstraps with MASt3R for initial pose estimation, but departs fundamentally by introducing S²A-Anchors with three explicit decoupling mechanisms (ego-motion/object-motion via soft gradient routing, appearance/deformation via canonical-feature separation, and scale/complexity via scale-conditioned HSTD) that none of the prior threads jointly address. The HSTD module draws on B-spline temporal bases and multi-scale hash-encoding ideas from 4DGS literature, but repurposes them within a pose-free, scale-adaptive anchor framework rather than a fixed-pose deformation field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to jointly recover camera trajectories and reconstruct both rigid and non-rigid dynamics from pose-free monocular UAV video, closing a gap that has left practitioners forced to either discard dynamic content or rely on unreliable SfM poses in texture-sparse aerial footage. The practical impact is significant for large-scale aerial mapping where synchronized multi-view or LiDAR data is unavailable. However, the method assumes relatively smooth motion and incurs substantial computational cost (10,000-iteration final refinement on a single GPU), limiting real-time or very large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the scale-aware densification strategy generalizes to indoor or close-range dynamic scenes, and whether the cycle-consistency motion supervision can handle occlusions and re-entries without additional priors.
