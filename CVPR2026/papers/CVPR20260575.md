## Problem

Existing human motion control for video generation relies on either 2D poses (which rigidly bind motion to the driving viewpoint, precluding novel-view synthesis) or explicit 3D parametric models like SMPL (which suffer from depth ambiguity and inaccurate dynamics, and when injected as strong constraints, override the intrinsic 3D spatial priors of large-scale video generators). The paper asks whether motion control can instead be learned implicitly, end-to-end, so that the motion representation naturally aligns with the generator's own 3D understanding rather than depending on externally reconstructed geometric signals.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference image of a subject, a driving video providing motion cues, and a free-form text prompt that can specify camera trajectories, and produces a 121-frame video at 480×854 pixels showing the reference subject reenacting the driving motion under the prompted camera motion. Driving frames are augmented with random perspective transformations before encoding to decouple motion from view-specific 2D projections, and the text prompt interacts with the generator through its native mechanism rather than through explicit camera parameters.

## Method

3DiMo jointly trains a Transformer-based 1D motion encoder with a pretrained DiT-based video generator (using flow matching with v-prediction), where the encoder compresses each driving frame into K=5 learnable latent tokens that discard spatial layout, enforcing a semantic bottleneck for view-agnostic motion abstraction. Motion tokens are injected via cross-attention (not pixel-aligned projection), enabling flexible semantic conditioning that coexists with the generator's native text-driven camera control. To foster genuine 3D awareness, training uses view-rich supervision spanning single-view reconstruction, multi-view motion reproduction, and moving-camera reproduction across a dataset combining internet videos, UE5 renderings, and real multi-view captures. A lightweight MLP auxiliary decoder provides early-stage geometric supervision by regressing motion features to SMPL/MANO pose parameters, with its loss annealed to zero so the model transitions from externally guided geometry to learning 3D spatial motion from data and the generator's priors. A dual-scale design with separate body and hand encoders captures both global articulation and fine-grained hand dynamics.

## Results

On the combined evaluation set (50 TikTok + 100 internet videos), 3DiMo achieves the best LPIPS (0.2206), FID (36.92), and FVD (297.4), outperforming the strongest baseline Uni3C by 16% on FID and 7.5% on FVD. In the user study (30 participants, 5-point Likert scale), 3DiMo leads on all four subjective metrics—motion accuracy (4.28), naturalness (4.18), 3D plausibility (4.05), and overall quality (4.38)—with the largest margin over baselines in 3D plausibility (+0.08 over Uni3C, +0.85 over AnimateAnyone). SSIM and PSNR are marginally lower than MTVCrafter (0.7390 vs. 0.7489; 17.96 vs. 18.03), which the authors attribute to pixel-wise sensitivity to minor viewpoint deviations that their text-driven static-camera prompt suppresses for geometric consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DiMo outperforms all four baselines on perceptual and distributional metrics (LPIPS, FID, FVD) and on every user-study dimension. Against the 2D-pose baselines AnimateAnyone and MimicMotion, the gains are substantial: FID drops from 68.72/62.45 to 36.92, and 3D plausibility in the user study jumps from 3.20/3.01 to 4.05, reflecting the elimination of depth-ordering errors inherent in 2D conditioning. Against the SMPL-based 3D baselines MTVCrafter and Uni3C, 3DiMo still leads on LPIPS (0.2206 vs. 0.2542/0.2639), FID (36.92 vs. 57.21/41.28), and FVD (297.4 vs. 379.6/321.9), while trailing MTVCrafter by a narrow margin on SSIM (0.7390 vs. 0.7489) and PSNR (17.96 vs. 18.03)—a gap the authors attribute to pixel-level sensitivity to small viewpoint shifts that their static-camera prompt intentionally suppresses. In the user study, 3DiMo edges Uni3C (the closest competitor) by +0.56 on accuracy, +0.15 on naturalness, +0.08 on 3D plausibility, and +0.14 on overall quality, with the largest relative advantage over the 2D methods in 3D plausibility (+0.85 over AnimateAnyone). Qualitatively, the paper highlights that SMPL-based methods fail to preserve physical contact (e.g., hand-on-hip) under viewpoint change, while 3DiMo's implicit representation resolves such depth ambiguities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two dominant paradigms in human image animation: 2D pose-based conditioning (AnimateAnyone, MimicMotion) that rigidly binds motion to the driving viewpoint, and explicit 3D parametric methods (Uni3C, MTVCrafter) that impose externally reconstructed SMPL/SMPL-X constraints. It draws on the 1D tokenizer design from Yu et al. [33] and the dual-scale body/hand encoder structure from X-UniMotion [28] and X-Nemo [36], but departs fundamentally by rejecting both 2D projection alignment and external 3D reconstruction in favor of end-to-end learned implicit motion tokens injected via cross-attention. The key conceptual departure is the argument that large-scale DiT video generators already possess strong 3D spatial priors, and that imposing rigid geometric constraints from imperfect SMPL estimates actively degrades the generator's native 3D reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical training recipe—view-rich data (single-view, multi-view, moving-camera) combined with annealed geometric supervision—that could be adopted by other controllable video generation systems seeking 3D-aware motion without explicit 3D reconstruction. A notable open question is whether the implicit motion tokens remain interpretable or generalizable beyond the specific DiT backbone and human subject domain, and whether the approach extends to non-articulated or multi-subject scenes. The evaluation, while thorough in ablation, is limited to 150 videos and a single architecture, leaving open how the method scales to longer sequences, diverse subjects, or different generator families. The finding that 3D-awareness (measured by camera-following ability) is largely orthogonal to standard pixel-level metrics (SSIM/PSNR) suggests the field needs better evaluation protocols for spatial reasoning in generated video.
