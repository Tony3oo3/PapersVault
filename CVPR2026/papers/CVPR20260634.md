## Problem

Flow-based video frame interpolation faces a fundamental efficiency-quality dilemma: forward warping is fast but produces hole artifacts, while backward warping yields higher fidelity but requires per-frame optical flow estimation, making multi-frame interpolation prohibitively expensive. Existing event-based VFI methods, despite leveraging dense motion cues, remain constrained by the backward warping paradigm and cannot simultaneously achieve high quality and low computational cost for arbitrary-time interpolation.

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB keyframes (I_0, I_1) and the intervening event stream (converted to a voxel grid) as input, and produces an arbitrary number of intermediate frames at any continuous timestamp t ∈ (0,1). Critically, the bidirectional optical flow is computed in a single forward pass regardless of how many frames are subsequently requested, so the per-frame cost decreases as more frames are interpolated. During training, images and events are randomly cropped to 256×256, and the method supports non-uniform timestamps (e.g., t=0.51, t=0.88) rather than only discrete skip intervals.

## Method

The framework has three core components. The Bidirectional Flow Estimation Block (BiFEB) divides the inter-keyframe interval into n=16 short temporal segments and, for each segment, aggregates event features with contextual and motion states from the previous segment via residual blocks; a GRU-based Forward Flow Estimator and a Backward Flow Estimator then produce bidirectional optical flows, with events processed in both forward and reverse order to obtain flows from each keyframe. The Flow Query (FQ) module retrieves the exact flow at any target time t by identifying the containing segment, querying pre-computed boundary flows, and linearly interpolating with a weight λ. The Bidirectional Warping (BiW) module generates forward- and backward-warped frames, computes explicit hole and difference masks, and uses a Mask Guide network to produce a refine mask that directs a refinement network to repair only the unreliable regions; results from both keyframe directions are fused via a softmax-weighted combination that favors the temporally closer keyframe and down-weights regions flagged for refinement.

## Results

On the synthetic GOPRO dataset (Skip 7), the proposed method achieves 37.66 PSNR / 0.976 SSIM, surpassing the strongest baseline TimeTracker (37.13 / 0.962) by +0.53 dB. On SNU-FILM extreme, it reaches 36.96 PSNR / 0.967 SSIM, outperforming TLXNet (36.10 / 0.962) by +0.86 dB. On the real-world HS-ERGB (Skip 7), the method attains 34.19 PSNR / 0.881 SSIM, exceeding TimeTracker (32.68 / 0.861) by +1.51 dB. In terms of efficiency, per-frame MACs decrease from 887G (31 frames) to 665G (127 frames), while TLXNet runs out of GPU memory beyond 31 frames.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all nine baselines on the synthetic GOPRO and SNU-FILM benchmarks. Against the strongest E-VFI competitor TimeTracker (CVPR'25), it gains +0.53 dB PSNR on GOPRO Skip 7 and +0.86 dB on SNU-FILM extreme, while also improving SSIM by 0.008–0.014. TLXNet (ECCV'24) is surpassed by +0.60 dB on GOPRO Skip 7 and +0.86 dB on SNU-FILM extreme; notably, TLXNet runs out of GPU memory when interpolating more than 31 frames, whereas the proposed method scales to 127 frames with decreasing per-frame cost. Among conventional (non-event) baselines, the gap is largest: RIFE (ECCV'22) and M2M (CVPR'22) trail by over 7 dB on GOPRO, and forward-warping methods like UPR-Net and IQ-VFI are outperformed by 6–8 dB. On real-world BS-ERGB, the method is marginally behind TimeTracker at Skip 1 (29.76 vs 29.85 PSNR) but pulls ahead at Skip 5 (+1.04 dB) and dominates HS-ERGB Skip 7 by +1.51 dB, confirming that the advantage grows with motion difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by unifying three previously separate threads in VFI: the efficiency of forward warping (M2M, UPR-Net), the quality of backward warping (RIFE, TimeLens), and the dense motion cues from event cameras (CBM-Net, TLXNet, TimeTracker). Rather than building on a single prior architecture, it repurposes the forward-warping idea of computing flow once and reusing it, but replaces the linear motion assumption with a GRU-based recurrent decomposition (BiFEB) over event-derived temporal segments. The Bidirectional Warping module then departs from prior E-VFI methods that rely solely on backward warping by explicitly fusing forward and backward warped results with a learned refinement mask, a strategy not previously explored in the event-based setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-shot flow estimation with arbitrary-time querying addresses a genuine scalability bottleneck: existing E-VFI methods either incur per-frame flow cost (TimeLens, CBM-Net) or suffer memory blowup at high frame counts (TLXNet OOMs beyond 31 frames). This makes the framework practically relevant for high-frame-rate synthesis where backward methods become infeasible. However, the method's applicability is constrained by the need for event camera data (or synthetic events via V2E), and the quality gains over the strongest baseline (TimeTracker) are consistent but modest (~0.5 dB PSNR), leaving open whether the efficiency-quality unification holds under even more extreme motion or very long temporal spans beyond the tested 127-frame setting.
