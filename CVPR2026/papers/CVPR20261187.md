## Problem

Controllable generation of 3D multi-class anatomical segmentations based on localized geometric and topological features remains difficult. Prior work (CardioComposer) could only enforce globally defined geometric properties in 3D, leaving no mechanism for controlling substructure-specific attributes (size, shape, position, orientation) or topological properties (connected components, loops, voids) at arbitrary spatial locations, dimensionalities, or coordinate systems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261187/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes user-specified target geometric features (mass, centroid, normalized covariance) and topological priors (desired Betti numbers B₀, B₁, B₂) defined over one or more cuboidal control domains, each parameterized by a rotation, scaling, and translation that position it in 3D space. Control domains can be volumetric, planar, or linear and may be arranged in Cartesian, cylindrical, or spherical coordinate systems. The output is a 128³ 3D multi-class anatomical segmentation (e.g., 11-channel cardiac, 7-channel aortic, 25-channel spinal, or 4-channel coronary), generated at inference time without any conditional retraining of the diffusion model.

## Method

Anatomica is an inference-time guidance framework for an unconditional latent diffusion model built on a VAE with a neural field decoder. At each denoising step, the clean latent prediction is parsed into K substructures via cuboidal control domains—spatially transformed template point grids that slice the latent or voxel space at arbitrary resolutions and positions. Geometric properties are measured differentiably through voxel-wise moments (mass, centroid, covariance) within each control domain, while topological properties are measured via persistent homology (Cubical Ripser) on super-level sets of the substructure. Composite potential functions penalize deviations from target features, and their gradients with respect to the latent are subtracted from the denoising prediction to steer sampling. Two partial decoding strategies—coarse L-parsing (global, low-resolution grid) and localized L-parsing (local, high-resolution grid)—exploit the neural field decoder's ability to decode arbitrary point sets, avoiding full-volume decoding and enabling efficient guidance at inference time.

## Results

On four geometric control tasks (Right Ventricle, Mitral Valve, Aortic Trunk, Myocardium Wall), Anatomica-V and Anatomica-L consistently outperform both Explicit and Implicit conditioning baselines across mass, centroid, and covariance fidelity. For example, on Right Ventricle, Anatomica-V achieves mass fidelity of 12.3 (×10⁻⁵) vs. 154.5 for Explicit and 60.6 for Implicit, while also improving FMD from 164.7 to 84.9. On topological control, Anatomica-L dramatically improves Betti precision over unconditional sampling: Atrial Separation B₀ precision jumps from 7.81% to 78.9%, and Calcium Count B₀ from 0.00% to 60.9%, while maintaining or improving generation quality (1-NNA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares Anatomica (inference-time guidance, training-free) against two conditional-training baselines: Explicit Conditioning (geometric moments encoded as a 13-dim vector concatenated to latents) and Implicit Conditioning (geometric moments embedded as 3D Gaussian heatmaps). Anatomica variants dominate on most geometric fidelity metrics: on Right Ventricle, Anatomica-V reduces mass error by 12× vs. Explicit (12.3 vs. 154.5) and 5× vs. Implicit (12.3 vs. 60.6), and centroid error by 7.5× vs. Explicit (30.2 vs. 227.1). On Mitral Valve, Anatomica-L achieves the best mass (3.22) and centroid (11.4) fidelity, outperforming Implicit by 2.8× and 7.6× respectively. The one exception is Aortic Trunk, where Implicit Conditioning achieves the best mass (0.81) and centroid (35.2) fidelity, though Anatomica-V still wins on FMD (82.0 vs. 104.8) and 1-NNA (0.561 vs. 0.565). For topological control, Anatomica-L improves B₀ precision by 10× on Atrial Separation (78.9% vs. 7.81% unconditional) and from 0% to 60.9% on Calcium Count, while maintaining comparable generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CardioComposer's inference-time geometric guidance for anatomical diffusion models from globally defined 3D properties to arbitrarily localized attributes via cuboidal control domains of varying scale, position, orientation, and dimensionality. It further unifies this geometric control with persistent homology-based topological guidance, which prior work (Clough et al., Gupta et al.) had applied only as training-time losses or to 2D/3D surface generation rather than multi-class 3D voxel segmentations. The neural field decoder architecture, adapted from hybrid implicit-explicit representations, enables partial decoding strategies that avoid full-volume decoding during guidance, a departure from prior spatial conditioning methods that relied on mid-level representations like bounding boxes or ellipsoids.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By enabling training-free, compositional control over both geometry and topology of 3D anatomical segmentations, this framework directly supports virtual clinical trials and semantically controllable data augmentation for medical ML pipelines. Key limitations include the need to manually tune loss weights and the computational cost of persistent homology at high resolutions, which constrains the decoding resolution for topological guidance. Open questions include whether control domains can be automatically inferred from anatomical priors, whether the framework generalizes to more complex topological constraints (e.g., genus beyond simple loops), and whether the approach extends to image-space generation rather than segmentation-only.
