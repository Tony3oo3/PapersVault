## Problem

3D Large Multimodal Models typically depend on heavy pre-trained visual encoders (e.g., CLIP, Sparse 3D U-Net) to extract geometric features before alignment with an LLM, imposing substantial computational overhead and limiting input flexibility. While 2D LMMs have begun eliminating such encoders, extending this paradigm to 3D remains largely unexplored because point clouds are unordered, irregular, and large-scale, requiring specialized permutation-invariant operators or serialization that naive 2D approaches cannot handle.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a uniformly sampled 50k-point cloud per scene (3D coordinates plus color/normal features) along with a text prompt and, for grounding tasks, explicit 3D coordinates or bounding-box centers. The point cloud is first clustered into 256 superpoints, then progressively merged down to T compact tokens (T < 256) before being fed to the LLM alongside text tokens. Outputs are free-form text responses (captions or QA answers); for dense captioning, 3D object masks are optionally generated via spectral clustering on the superpoint graph without any learned segmentation head.

## Method

Fase3D replaces the 3D encoder with a lightweight tokenizer that (1) projects points through a shallow MLP and non-parametric Fourier positional encoding, then average-pools within geometric superpoints to form initial tokens; (2) serializes superpoints along four space-filling curves (Z-order, transpose Z-order, Hilbert, transpose Hilbert) and applies a windowed FFT with a learnable frequency-domain gate to inject global context at O(M log M) cost, approximating self-attention; (3) merges tokens from M to T via a sparse superpoint graph built by SFC window-voting, using farthest-point-sampled seeds and 1-hop graph pooling. In the LLM (frozen Qwen2.5-3B), Fourier-augmented LoRA adapters apply a per-channel FFT filter with a learnable gate vector to the token features before the LoRA-adapted FFN layers, adding only D parameters per head while injecting frequency-aware global interactions. Training uses a two-stage supervised protocol (general 3D instruction tuning followed by task-specific fine-tuning) with LoRA rank 768 on the first 8 layers, requiring only ~10.5M activated 3D parameters versus ~119M for encoder-based baselines.

## Results

On ScanQA (val), Fase3D with Qwen2.5-3B achieves CIDEr 90.11, ROUGE-L 42.56, METEOR 18.24, and BLEU-4 17.12, while using only 10.54M activated parameters and 2.04G FLOPs for the 3D front-end—roughly 5.5× fewer parameters and 18.5× fewer FLOPs than 3D-LLaVA (58.26M / 37.75G). On SQA3D (test), Fase3D reaches 53.9 EM@1, closely matching 3D-LLaVA's 54.5. For dense captioning on ScanRefer, Fase3D with an external segmenter attains CIDEr@0.5 of 78.14, nearly matching 3D-LLaVA (78.80) and substantially outperforming PerLA (69.41) and LL3DA (65.19).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LL3DA, Fase3D (Qwen2.5-3B) wins decisively on every ScanQA metric—CIDEr 90.11 vs. 76.79 (+13.32), ROUGE-L 42.56 vs. 37.31 (+5.25), METEOR 18.24 vs. 15.88 (+2.36), and BLEU-4 17.12 vs. 13.53 (+3.59)—while using only 10.54M parameters versus 118.87M and 2.04G FLOPs versus 40.21G. Against PerLA, the margin is similarly large: CIDEr 90.11 vs. 78.13 (+11.98) and ROUGE-L 42.56 vs. 39.60 (+2.96), with FLOPs reduced from 163.38G to 2.04G (≈80×). Against the strongest encoder-based baseline 3D-LLaVA, Fase3D is within 2.5 CIDEr points (90.11 vs. 92.60) and 0.54 ROUGE-L points (42.56 vs. 43.10) on ScanQA, and within 0.6 EM@1 on SQA3D (53.9 vs. 54.5), yet activates only 10.54M parameters (5.5× fewer) and 2.04G FLOPs (18.5× fewer). On dense captioning (ScanRefer), Fase3D with Mask3D nearly ties 3D-LLaVA on CIDEr@0.5 (78.14 vs. 78.80) and surpasses it on BLEU-4@0.5 (41.34 vs. 36.90), while clearly outperforming PerLA (69.41) and LL3DA (65.19) on CIDEr. Overall, Fase3D matches or exceeds all encoder-based baselines in quality while reducing 3D front-end computation by one to two orders of magnitude.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fase3D extends the encoder-free paradigm established in 2D LMMs (EVE/EVEv2, Mono-InternVL, SOLO) to the 3D point cloud domain, where the unordered, large-scale nature of scenes makes naive serialization infeasible. It draws on space-filling curve serialization from PTv3 and FFT-based attention approximation from Fourier Transformer/FNet, repurposing both as a lightweight tokenizer that replaces heavy 3D encoders (Sparse 3D U-Net, Point Transformer) used in prior 3D LMMs like LL3DA, PerLA, and 3D-LLaVA. The Fourier-augmented LoRA adapter further departs from standard LoRA by injecting frequency-domain filtering into the LLM's FFN layers, a design not previously explored in multimodal fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a ~10M-parameter, 2G-FLOP tokenizer can match encoder-based 3D LMMs (58–120M params, 37–163G FLOPs) on ScanQA, SQA3D, ScanRefer, and Nr3D, Fase3D substantially lowers the computational barrier to scene-level 3D understanding and suggests that heavy geometric backbones may be unnecessary. Key limitations include reliance on serialization (which may underperform on non-Euclidean long-range relationships in cluttered scenes), evaluation restricted to indoor ScanNet scenes, and the absence of RGB or multi-view integration. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned serialization can close the remaining gap on fine-grained spatial reasoning, and whether the approach scales to outdoor or large-scale urban scenes.
