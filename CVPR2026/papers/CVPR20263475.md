## Problem

Existing continuous attribute manipulation methods for T2I diffusion models follow a One-for-One paradigm, requiring a dedicated slider module trained per attribute. This causes parameter redundancy, limits compositional control over multiple attributes simultaneously, and prevents zero-shot generalization to unseen attributes without additional training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263475/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a base text prompt and a target attribute text (e.g., 'smile', 'old'), both encoded by SDXL's dual text encoders, along with a scalar steering strength λ that controls manipulation intensity. It operates on intermediate token embeddings extracted from specific transformer layers (11th and 29th) of the dual encoders rather than on image latents. For real-image editing, a real image is additionally inverted via ReNoise to recover latent representations before the same embedding-level manipulation is applied.

## Method

The core contribution is an Attribute Sparse Autoencoder (ALS), a K-Sparse Autoencoder trained in a self-supervised manner on 52,000 text embeddings to auto-encode them into a high-dimensional latent space using top-k activation, which forces each attribute to occupy a distinct, sparse subset of neurons. An auxiliary reconstruction loss on the least-activated neurons prevents dead units and ensures broader semantic coverage. At inference, the attribute text is encoded to identify its sparse latent direction, which is then scaled by λ and added to the prompt embedding via the decoder, enabling continuous, disentangled, and composable manipulation. Because the latent space is compositional, multiple attribute directions can be summed for multi-attribute editing, and unseen attributes (e.g., races, celebrities) can be manipulated zero-shot by their text embeddings activating appropriate combinations of learned components.

## Results

On single-attribute manipulation, the All-in-One Slider achieves the highest Identity Score (IS) for Old (0.7155) and Makeup (0.7423), and the highest Qwen Score (QS) for Old (4.0490). In the more challenging multi-attribute setting, the method achieves the best QS across all three combinations (Old+Smile: 4.2124, Old+Makeup: 4.4281, Smile+Makeup: 4.2973) and the best IS in two out of three cases. The largest margin over the second-best baseline appears in the Old+Makeup combination, where QS reaches 4.4281 versus 4.0555 for AttributeControl.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two One-for-One baselines: ConceptSlider (CSlider, ECCV 2024) and AttributeControl (AttCtrl, CVPR 2025). In single-attribute tasks, the All-in-One Slider wins on IS for Old (0.7155 vs. 0.6005) and Makeup (0.7423 vs. 0.6529), but loses to AttCtrl on both QS and IS for Smile (4.2647/0.6366 vs. 4.3954/0.6952). In multi-attribute tasks, the advantage becomes decisive: the method achieves the highest QS in all three combinations, with the largest gap in Old+Makeup (4.4281 vs. 4.0555 for AttCtrl and 3.8006 for CSlider, a ~0.37 and ~0.63 margin respectively). For IS in multi-attribute settings, the method leads in Old+Smile (0.6882 vs. 0.6347) and Smile+Makeup (0.6351 vs. 0.5145), while AttCtrl narrowly edges it in Old+Makeup (0.6347 vs. 0.6277). Overall, the All-in-One Slider's key advantage is its compositional robustness: it degrades far less when combining multiple attributes, whereas both One-for-One baselines suffer significant IS drops (e.g., CSlider's IS falls from 0.6529 on single Makeup to 0.4786 on Smile+Makeup).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant One-for-One paradigm in attribute manipulation (ConceptSlider, AttributeControl), where each attribute requires a dedicated LoRA or optimized direction, by instead training a single K-Sparse Autoencoder on text encoder embeddings to produce a unified, disentangled latent space. The core encoding mechanism is directly inherited from K-SAE (Makhzani & Frey) and the auxiliary dead-neuron recovery loss from Gao et al.'s SAE scaling work, both originally developed for LLM interpretability. The conceptual bridge from LLM sparse feature discovery to diffusion-model text-embedding manipulation is the paper's key architectural move, repurposing a technique from mechanistic interpretability as a generative control tool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The All-in-One framing addresses a real scalability bottleneck: practitioners no longer need to train and maintain a separate slider per attribute, and the compositional sparse space naturally supports multi-attribute editing without conflict. The zero-shot generalization to unseen attributes (races, celebrities) is the most compelling claim, but it is demonstrated only qualitatively without quantitative metrics, leaving its reliability and boundary conditions unclear. Open questions include how the method behaves for non-facial attributes at scale, whether the sparse latent space remains disentangled as the attribute vocabulary grows, and whether the approach transfers cleanly to newer architectures (e.g., DiT-based models) beyond the limited SDXL/SD v1.4 experiments shown.
