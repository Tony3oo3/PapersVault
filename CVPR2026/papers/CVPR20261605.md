## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) systems are vulnerable to computation cost attacks (e.g., Poison-splat) where attackers inject high-frequency textures into input images to trigger excessive Gaussian proliferation, causing denial-of-service through GPU memory exhaustion and training delays. Existing defenses—image smoothing and capping Gaussian counts—are either ineffective against complex non-linear attack transformations or uniformly degrade reconstruction quality by up to 10 dB for all users. No effective black-box defense framework existed for 3DGS-as-a-service systems.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes multi-view RGB images (960×540 resolution in training) and operates in a selective pipeline: the detector first classifies each image as poisoned or clean, and only flagged images are passed to the purifier, which outputs a restored RGB image. Clean images bypass purification entirely, preserving full reconstruction fidelity for legitimate users. The purified images are then fed into standard 3DGS training to produce 3D Gaussian primitives, with safety measured by Gaussian count and peak GPU memory, and utility by PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS on rendered novel views.

## Method

RemedyGS is a two-stage black-box defense: (1) a detector (4 stacked 2D conv layers + linear head, trained with cross-entropy) identifies poisoned images by their unnatural high-frequency texture signatures arising from TV-score maximization; (2) a purifier (5-layer conv encoder + transposed conv decoder with additive skip connections) recovers clean images from poisoned inputs, trained to maximize mutual information between recovered and original images via a Barber-Agakov bound that reduces to an MSE objective. To counter the blurring artifact inherent in MSE-trained CNNs, adversarial training is incorporated: a discriminator (4 conv layers) conditioned on encoder latents enforces distributional alignment between purified and clean image distributions, with the final purifier loss combining MSE, LPIPS, and adversarial terms. The additive (rather than concatenation-based) skip connections are critical, as concatenation propagates attacker-induced noise alongside useful features, while addition selectively preserves original details.

## Results

On the three benchmark datasets (NeRF-Synthetic, Mip-NeRF360, Tanks-and-Temples), RemedyGS reduces the number of Gaussians triggered by Poison-splat attacks to near-clean levels (e.g., 0.491M vs. 0.495M clean on NS-chair, vs. 0.942M poisoned) while maintaining PSNR within ~1 dB of the clean baseline (33.07 vs. 33.87 on NS-Avg; 27.31 vs. 27.52 on MIP-Avg). Peak GPU memory under RemedyGS stays close to clean (4601 MB vs. 4308 MB clean on NS-Avg, vs. 11259 MB poisoned). The detector achieves 94–99% accuracy in distinguishing poisoned from clean images, and on clean inputs RemedyGS preserves utility nearly identical to vanilla 3DGS (e.g., 35.776 PSNR on NS-chair, matching the ground-truth clean result).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RemedyGS against two baselines from Poison-splat [33]: (1) image smoothing (Gaussian filter on all inputs) and (2) limiting the maximum number of Gaussians during training. Image smoothing over-defends by aggressively removing high-frequency content, reducing Gaussian counts to well below clean levels (e.g., 0.130M vs. 0.289M clean on NS-Avg) and degrading PSNR by up to 9 dB (30.03 vs. 33.87 on NS-Avg), because it cannot distinguish natural textures from attack-induced noise. Limiting the Gaussian number controls computational cost more moderately (0.448M vs. 0.289M clean) but still loses utility (30.77 PSNR on NS-Avg) because the upper bound forces reallocation of Gaussians away from legitimately detailed regions. RemedyGS outperforms both baselines on every metric: it achieves PSNR within ~1 dB of the clean ground truth (33.07 vs. 33.87 NS-Avg; 27.31 vs. 27.52 MIP-Avg; 24.07 vs. 24.26 TT-Avg), improves SSIM by up to 0.24 over the limiting baseline, and keeps Gaussian counts and GPU memory closest to the clean case among all defenses. The detector further ensures that clean inputs bypass purification entirely, preserving exact vanilla-3DGS utility for legitimate users (Table 4 shows identical PSNR to clean on NS-chair and MIP-bonsai).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly addresses the computation cost attack identified by Poison-splat [33], which exploits 3DGS's adaptive densification mechanism to trigger excessive Gaussian proliferation. It departs from prior DoS defenses in ML (game-theoretic approaches, adversarial training with fixed shared models) that are incompatible with 3DGS's per-scene retraining paradigm, and from naive defenses (Gaussian smoothing, Gaussian count capping) that uniformly degrade all users' reconstruction quality. The purifier's training objective is derived from the Barber-Agakov mutual information bound [2], repurposing an information-theoretic variational framework for image purification, while the adversarial training component borrows the GAN paradigm to enforce distributional alignment between purified and clean images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS powers commercial reconstruction services (Spline, KIRI, Polycam), the first effective black-box defense against its most critical DoS vulnerability has immediate practical relevance for safe deployment. The selective detector-then-purifier design is a useful architectural pattern for any per-scene ML service where uniform preprocessing is too costly. However, the defense is validated primarily against the specific Poison-splat attack (TV-score maximization), and generalization to other attack paradigms (e.g., StealthAttack) or to adaptive attacks that account for the defense remains an open question. The reliance on a large curated training set (1M image pairs from DL3DV) also raises questions about scalability to new attack families without retraining.
