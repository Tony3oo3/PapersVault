## Problem

Unified multimodal models like BAGEL can produce plausible reasoning over interleaved image-text prompts, yet the generated images frequently fail to reflect that reasoning, revealing a critical misalignment between understanding and generation in in-context image generation and editing (ICGE). Existing reasoning mechanisms designed for text-to-image or single-image editing do not transfer to complex multi-reference interleaved prompts, and no effective reward signal exists to align structured reasoning with the resulting image in this setting.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a flexible interleaved prompt P consisting of one to five reference images (characters, objects, or scenes) and a natural-language instruction that may use ambiguous references (e.g., 'put them together'). It sequentially produces a structured IC-CoT reasoning string—containing an <out_caption> block with the predicted output caption and one <relation_i> block per reference image specifying its role—followed by a 1024×1024 RGB image generated via 50-step Rectified Flow denoising. The structured format is what enables downstream reward computation and distinguishes it from free-form prompt expansion.

## Method

Re-Align builds on the BAGEL unified multimodal architecture and introduces In-Context Chain-of-Thought (IC-CoT), a structured reasoning paradigm that decouples semantic guidance (predicting the target image caption to reduce the task toward text-to-image generation) from reference association (explicitly mapping each reference image to its role in the output to prevent cross-image confusion). Training proceeds in two stages: supervised fine-tuning on the newly constructed Re-Align-410K dataset (built via an automated pipeline using Gemini 2.5 for instruction/reasoning generation and GPT-4o for target image synthesis, followed by multi-dimensional filtering), then Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a surrogate reward computed as CLIP cosine similarity between the IC-CoT's predicted caption and the generated image. A reasoning-induced diversity strategy generates distinct IC-CoT chains for each sample within a GRPO group, increasing reward variance without the image-quality degradation caused by higher SDE noise, thereby stabilizing policy optimization.

## Results

On the OmniContext benchmark, Re-Align achieves an average score of 8.21, the highest among open-source models of comparable scale, trailing only GPT-4o (8.80) and Emu3.5 (8.82) which use substantially more resources. On DreamOmni2Bench, Re-Align dominates all editing categories: Add (9.27), Replace (8.61), Global (7.85), and Local (6.35), while also leading in generation with an Overall score of 7.24. The largest margins appear in Replace editing (+1.56 over DreamOmni2) and Local editing (+0.91 over DreamOmni2), where prior methods struggle most.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Re-Align outperforms all open-source baselines on DreamOmni2Bench across every editing and generation sub-task. The closest competitor, DreamOmni2 (which uses separate parameters for editing vs. generation), is surpassed by +2.40 on Add, +1.56 on Replace, +0.09 on Global, +0.91 on Local, and +0.68 on generation Overall. Echo-4o is competitive on Add (8.51 vs. 9.27) but collapses on Replace (4.51) and Local (2.41), revealing its inability to handle attribute-level edits. On OmniContext, Re-Align (8.21) trails GPT-4o (8.80) and Emu3.5 (8.82) by ~0.6 points but leads all other open-source methods including Qwen-Image-Edit (7.69), OmniGen2 (7.18), and BAGEL (5.73). Notably, Re-Align achieves the best scores in the MULTIPLE and SCENE categories on OmniContext, where multi-reference reasoning is most critical, while Qwen-Image-Edit leads only in the simpler SINGLE category.

## Relation to Prior Work

Re-Align builds directly on BAGEL, a unified multimodal foundation model that natively supports interleaved image-text understanding and generation, but departs from its unstructured prompt-expansion reasoning by introducing IC-CoT, a structured paradigm that explicitly decouples semantic guidance (predicted caption) from reference association (per-image role specification). For the RL alignment stage, it adapts GRPO from the LLM reasoning literature (DeepSeekMath) to visual generation, but replaces the task-specific reward models used in FlowGRPO and DanceGRPO with a lightweight CLIP-based surrogate reward computed between the structured reasoning caption and the generated image. The reasoning-induced diversity strategy—generating distinct CoT chains per group member rather than increasing SDE noise—represents a novel departure from prior RL-for-diffusion approaches that relied on stochastic sampling noise for diversity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a concrete, practical recipe for closing the understanding-generation gap in unified multimodal models: structured reasoning that explicitly names what each reference image contributes, combined with a cheap alignment reward, yields consistent gains across both generation and editing without requiring a dedicated reward model per task. The Re-Align-410K dataset and its automated construction pipeline (leveraging GPT-4o for target images and Gemini 2.5 for instructions) lower the barrier for future ICGE research. Key limitations include the model's scale relative to production systems like GPT-4o, and the purely textual nature of IC-CoT, which the authors identify as a constraint; extending to visual chain-of-thought reasoning is flagged as a promising but open direction.
