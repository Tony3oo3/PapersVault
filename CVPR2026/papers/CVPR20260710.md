## Problem

Inferring a plant's full architecture—branching topology, organ geometry, and semantic labels—from raw 3D scans is challenging due to self-occlusion and thin structures. Existing methods are either task-specific (addressing only reconstruction, skeletonization, or segmentation) or require costly pre-segmented and manually annotated inputs. This paper tackles the gap by learning a unified parameterized plant representation directly from unstructured point clouds, trained exclusively on synthetic data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260710/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an unstructured 3D point cloud of a plant (clean, Gaussian-noisy, or derived from monocular depth maps) as input and produces a parameterized L-String: a bracketed string of modules (stem, branch, petiole, leaf, cotyledon) each carrying continuous geometric parameters (angles, lengths, diameters, curvature) organized in a binary axial tree. From this single L-String, three downstream outputs are derived—3D mesh reconstruction, a skeleton (full or branch-only), and per-point organ-type labels—without any additional network inference.

## Method

The architecture combines a recursive neural network (RvNN) autoencoder with a PointNet point-cloud encoder. The RvNN learns a latent shape space S for a plant species by first mapping each node type to a fixed-dim latent point via per-type node auto-encoders, then recursively merging pairs of nodes bottom-up using two encoder-decoder pairs (sibling and parent-child) that respect the binary tree structure; auxiliary classifiers predict the split type and node type during decoding. A PointNet encoder is then trained to map input point clouds to the same latent space S, supervised by the RvNN's encoding of paired L-Strings. Training is entirely on synthetic Chenopodium Album plants generated via L-Py L-systems with simulated acquisition noise. At inference, a test-time optimization step refines stem, petiole, and leaf parameters bottom-up using bidirectional Chamfer Distance to the input, mitigating error accumulation along the main axis.

## Results

On 3D reconstruction of Chenopodium Album plants, the method achieves 98% leaf number accuracy, 93% LAI accuracy, and 75% topology accuracy on clean point clouds, with a compact 17.56 KB output (vs. 780.88 KB for SIREN) and 4 min 1 s inference time. On noisy inputs, the method outperforms SIREN in accuracy (0.0054 vs. 0.0121 Chamfer distance) while maintaining a single connected component (vs. 268 for SIREN). For skeletonization, the method achieves 0.0178 bidirectional Chamfer distance on clean data and 0.0199 on depth maps, and is the only method capable of branch-level skeletonization (0.0161). Segmentation results are on-par with PlantNet and PSegNet, with superior petiole segmentation compared to PSegNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SIREN [37] on 3D reconstruction, the method trades slightly worse clean-data Chamfer distance (0.0059 vs. 0.0012 accuracy) for a 44× smaller output (17.56 KB vs. 780.88 KB), 2× faster inference, a single connected component, and the ability to report leaf count, LAI, and topology metrics that SIREN cannot provide; on noisy inputs the method clearly wins (0.0054 vs. 0.0121 accuracy). For skeletonization, the method outperforms Chaudhury et al. [3] (0.0178 vs. 0.0139 clean; 0.0199 vs. 0.0449 depth) and Livny et al. [26] (0.0178 vs. 0.0257 clean) on Chamfer distance, but is outperformed by Xu et al. [47] on clean (0.0178 vs. 0.0102) and noisy (0.0174 vs. 0.0110) inputs, though it wins on depth maps (0.0199 vs. 0.0145) and uniquely supports branch-level output. For segmentation, the method is on-par with PlantNet [21] across clean, noisy, and partial inputs, and outperforms PSegNet [20] specifically on petiole segmentation, while both baselines are limited to semantic labels without the structural information the L-String representation provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the recursive auto-encoder architecture for binary trees from GRASS (Li et al., 2017) to the domain of plant L-Strings, combining it with a PointNet-based encoder to map raw 3D point clouds into a learned latent shape space. It departs from prior inverse plant modeling work (Guo et al., Stáva et al.), which infers growth rules from 2D images of leafless structures, and from task-specific methods that handle reconstruction, skeletonization, or segmentation in isolation. The closest prior work, Demeter (Cheng et al., 2025), also learns a parametric plant model but requires manually annotated 3D scans and pre-segmented input, whereas this method trains purely on synthetic L-Py-generated data and operates directly on unstructured point clouds.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying 3D reconstruction, skeleton extraction, and organ segmentation under a single parametric L-String inference framework, this work offers a compact (17 KB vs. 780 KB) and multi-task-capable alternative to task-specific pipelines in plant phenotyping. The demonstration that a model trained exclusively on synthetic Chenopodium Album plants generalizes to real 3D scans is practically valuable, reducing the need for costly annotated datasets. Key limitations include the requirement to train a new model per species, the restriction to binary axial trees (excluding monocotyledons like wheat and maize), and the manual definition of node combination rules. An open question is whether this approach can scale to more architecturally complex plants or be made species-agnostic.
