## Problem

Marker-based motion capture systems are costly, require manual marker placement and extensive post-processing, and struggle with close multi-person interactions where markers occlude or fall off. Existing markerless methods are largely designed for single-person capture, rely on sparse keypoints, or fail under heavy occlusion and physical contact. MAMMA asks whether a fully markerless pipeline can match the accuracy of commercial marker-based systems (e.g., Vicon) for close two-person interactions without any manual cleanup.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes synchronized multi-view RGB video (16–32 calibrated cameras) at 512×384 per crop, optionally conditioned on SAM2 instance-segmentation masks that track each person across frames. For each person and frame, MammaNet outputs 512 dense surface landmark locations with per-landmark Gaussian uncertainty, a binary visibility probability, and two contact probabilities (person-person and floor). These per-view predictions are triangulated across views via symmetric epipolar distance and Hungarian assignment, then fed into an LBFGS optimization that recovers full SMPL-X pose, shape (16 coefficients), and global translation parameters.

## Method

MammaNet replaces the single-query transformer decoder of CameraHMR with 512 independent learnable landmark queries, each attending to image patches (ViT-Base) and mask features (CNN) via cross-attention, while self-attention captures pairwise landmark correlations. The network is trained on MammaSyn (2.5M synthetic multi-view crops extending BEDLAM with close-interaction, extreme-pose, and hand-motion sequences) using Gaussian NLL for landmark regression, binary cross-entropy for visibility, and focal loss for the imbalanced contact labels. A four-stage LBFGS fitting procedure first solves translation/rotation via reprojection error with a Geman-McClure robust kernel, then jointly optimizes pose and shape with L2 shape priors, refines uncertainty using reprojection evidence, and finally minimizes an SDF-based contact energy that repels interpenetrating vertices while attracting predicted contact points. SAM2 masks are element-wise summed with image features to condition the network, enabling clean separation of overlapping subjects in two-person scenes.

## Results

On the primary 3D fitting benchmark (Table 4), MAMMA-C achieves the best MPJPE across all six evaluation datasets, with particularly large margins on interaction-heavy scenes: 17.73 mm on MammaEval-Dance (vs. 20.41 mm for CameraHMR and 27.98 mm for Look-Ma*) and 45.35 mm on Harmony4D (vs. 58.59 mm for CameraHMR). On the Hi4D benchmark (Table 5), MAMMA-C achieves 12.44 mm MPJPE, outperforming the next-best method AvatarPose (32.10 mm) by a factor of ~2.6×. In the unique Vicon comparison (Section 5.5), MAMMA's held-out marker error is 22.481 mm versus 21.619 mm for the Vicon+MoSh++ pipeline—a difference of only 0.862 mm—demonstrating near-equivalent accuracy to the gold-standard marker-based system.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAMMA-C outperforms all academic baselines on every dataset and metric. Against CameraHMR (the closest competitor), MAMMA-C reduces MPJPE by 2.3 mm on MammaEval-S (12.96 vs. 15.25), 13.2 mm on Harmony4D (45.35 vs. 58.59), and 10.8 mm on MOYO (22.95 vs. 33.75). Against Look-Ma*, the margins are even larger (e.g., 13.0 mm on MammaEval-S, 14.0 mm on MOYO). On Hi4D, MAMMA-C (12.44 mm) beats the previous best AvatarPose (32.10 mm) by 2.6× and the multi-view methods MVPose (42.63 mm) and MvP (92.77 mm) by 3.4× and 7.4× respectively. In the Vicon comparison, MAMMA's 22.481 mm held-out marker error is within 0.862 mm of the Vicon+MoSh++ pipeline (21.619 mm), effectively matching the commercial gold standard. Additionally, MAMMA-C reduces body interpenetration to 8.46 mm mean depth (vs. 13.41 mm for CameraHMR and 13.73 mm for Look-Ma*), even surpassing the ground-truth Vicon fit (9.84 mm) in penetration metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAMMA extends the dense-landmark-to-SMPL-X fitting paradigm established by CameraHMR and Look-Ma, but departs from their single-query or regression-based decoders by introducing per-landmark learnable queries in a transformer decoder, enabling the network to learn pairwise landmark correlations and match each query to relevant image patches. It builds on the BEDLAM synthetic rendering pipeline but substantially extends it with multi-person interaction sequences, 32-camera multi-view rendering, and per-vertex contact/visibility annotations. The use of SAM2 segmentation masks as conditioning represents a departure from prior markerless methods that rely solely on raw pixels, and the SDF-based contact optimization stage has no direct analogue in existing markerless mocap pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The headline result—that MAMMA achieves only 0.862mm greater held-out marker error than the Vicon+MoSh++ pipeline—provides the first quantitative evidence that a fully markerless system can serve as a practical replacement for the marker-based 'gold standard' in SMPL-X capture, reducing the full pipeline from ~72 hours to 26 hours. The introduction of MammaSyn (2.5M samples with dense contact/visibility annotations) and the MammaEval benchmarks addresses a critical gap in the field: the absence of standardized evaluation for dense-landmark prediction and markerless multi-person mocap. Open questions include generalization beyond the two-person setting, scalability to larger groups, and whether the multi-view requirement (16–32 cameras) can be relaxed; the paper also notes that SAM2 masks can degrade when subjects are extremely close, suggesting room for improvement in the segmentation front-end.
