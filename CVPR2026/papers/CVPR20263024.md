## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting requires hundreds of thousands to millions of single-color primitives to represent high-frequency surfaces (e.g., checkerboard patterns, fur), because each primitive can encode only one color per view direction. Existing remedies—Gabor-kernel primitives and fixed-resolution textured Gaussians—remain constrained by their predefined bases or texture resolution, limiting flexibility under tight memory budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263024/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (resized to 1600×1066) and an SfM point cloud for initialization, along with known camera poses. It produces a 3D scene representation as a set of 2D Gaussian primitives, each carrying position, scale, rotation, opacity, and a per-primitive SIREN MLP (6 hidden neurons) whose weights are the learned appearance parameters. At render time, each primitive's MLP receives its local 2D coordinates (u, v) and the camera ray direction to output an RGB color, which is alpha-blended across primitives to synthesize the novel view.

## Method

Each 2D Gaussian primitive is augmented with a lightweight single-hidden-layer SIREN network (6 neurons, frequency ω₀=30) that maps the concatenation of local 2D coordinates and view direction (a 5-D vector) to RGB via sinusoidal activations, replacing spherical-harmonic coefficients and enabling a single primitive to model spatially varying, view-dependent color patterns without fixed texture resolution. For primitive-count control, the paper introduces a frequency-aware densification strategy: per-pixel errors are computed in three band-limited frequency ranges (0.01–0.10, 0.10–0.20, 0.20–0.40) via FFT/IFFT with local averaging, and primitives whose accumulated frequency-domain error exceeds a threshold are cloned or split, with MLP weights inherited from the parent. Training uses 20k iterations with a combined L1 + SSIM loss and a gradual opacity reset, building on the 2D Gaussian Splatting rasterization pipeline.

## Results

On the High-Frequency dataset (20k primitives), Neural Gabor Splatting achieves 26.49 PSNR / 0.8808 SSIM / 0.2115 LPIPS, outperforming 3DGS (23.97/0.8335/0.2769) by +2.52 dB PSNR and 3D Gabor Splatting (25.28/0.8530/0.2449) by +1.21 dB. On Mip-NeRF360, the method reaches 26.98 PSNR / 0.810 SSIM / 0.2521 LPIPS, and on DTU it attains 29.15 PSNR / 0.8519 SSIM / 0.3705 LPIPS. Against neural splatting baselines NTS and NEST on the High-Frequency dataset, the method leads by +3.01 dB over NTS and +4.27 dB over NEST in PSNR, with the gap widening under tight data budgets (1–5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against 3DGS and 2DGS (both with matched data budgets), Neural Gabor Splatting wins on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all four datasets, with the largest margins on the High-Frequency dataset (+2.5 dB PSNR over 3DGS). Against 3D Gabor Splatting, the method leads by +1.2 dB PSNR on High-Frequency and +0.3 dB on DTU, though 3D Gabor* edges out on SSIM for Mip-NeRF360 (0.8265 vs 0.810) and DTU (0.8573 vs 0.8519). Against NTS and NEST on the High-Frequency dataset, the proposed method achieves 26.49 PSNR versus 23.48 (NTS) and 22.22 (NEST), a gap of +3.0 and +4.3 dB respectively; on Mip-NeRF360 (BONSAI scene), NTS slightly leads in PSNR (29.49 vs 29.18) but the proposed method wins on SSIM (0.9067 vs 0.9028) and LPIPS (0.2412 vs 0.2544). Under aggressive budget reduction (1–5% data), NTS and NEST degrade rapidly while the proposed method maintains coherent geometry, confirming its robustness in the low-budget regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends 2D Gaussian Splatting by replacing the single-color-plus-spherical-harmonics appearance model with a per-primitive SIREN MLP that maps local 2D coordinates and view direction to RGB, drawing on the deferred neural rendering / neural texture lineage. It departs from prior high-frequency extensions of Gaussian splatting—3D Gabor Splatting (predefined Gabor noise bases), textured Gaussians (fixed-resolution textures), and NTS/NEST (fixed-capacity hash grids or tri-planes)—by using a continuous, resolution-independent neural representation with no predefined basis. The frequency-aware densification strategy is a novel adaptation of error-based densification, substituting band-limited FFT-domain error for spatial-domain error to selectively allocate primitives to under-represented frequency bands.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core practical bottleneck in Gaussian splatting—explosive primitive counts for high-frequency scenes—by showing that a 6-neuron SIREN MLP per primitive can encode complex stripe and texture patterns that would otherwise require many single-color primitives. The frequency-aware densification offers a practical knob for controlling where primitives are allocated, which is useful under strict memory budgets. However, the method is limited to surface (non-volumetric) reconstruction, incurs per-primitive MLP evaluation overhead, and the authors acknowledge that parameter sharing or codebook compression would be needed for further compression. Open questions include extension to dynamic scenes, volumetric effects, and whether the per-primitive MLP capacity can be shared or compressed without quality loss.
