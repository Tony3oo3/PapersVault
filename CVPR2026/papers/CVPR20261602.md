## Problem

Pre-trained latent diffusion models fail to perceive reflection-corrupted composite images as linear superpositions of their constituent layers in latent space, causing poor separation and generalization. Additionally, natural language prompts are inherently ambiguous for describing the reflection-removal task, and the stochasticity of multi-step denoising produces inconsistent output quality without a reliable selection mechanism.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261602/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB image containing a reflection superimposed on a background scene and produces the clean transmission (background) layer. At inference, the DEBS strategy requires k parallel one-step denoising passes scored by a monocular depth estimator (Depth Anything V2) before committing to a full T-step denoising trajectory, adding roughly 25% overhead for k=4. No ground-truth depth or reflection mask is needed at test time.

## Method

GenSIRR adapts a FLUX.1 Kontext DiT for SIRR through three synergistic components. First, a reflection-equivariant VAE (re-VAE) is fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 8) on PD-12M with an equivalence loss ‖E(I_obs) − ((1−α)E(B) + αE(R))‖² that explicitly aligns the latent geometry with the linear physics of reflection formation, rather than merely improving pixel-level reconstruction. Second, a Learnable Task-Specific Text Embedding (LTE) is initialized from the text encoding of 'please remove the reflection within the image' and then optimized end-to-end via backpropagation, replacing the text encoder output with a compact, task-specific vector in the model's native embedding space. Third, the Depth-guided Early-Branching Sampling (DEBS) strategy runs k parallel one-step denoising passes from different noise seeds, scores each candidate with a lightweight depth estimator (hypothesizing that successful reflection removal reveals a deeper, more coherent scene), and continues full denoising only for the highest-scoring trajectory. The DiT is fine-tuned from the FLUX.1 Kontext checkpoint at learning rate 1e-5 on a mixture of real and synthetic reflection pairs.

## Results

GenSIRR achieves a new state-of-the-art across all three benchmark datasets, with an average PSNR of 27.93 dB and SSIM of 0.916 (28.03 dB / 0.931 with DEBS k=4). On the most challenging Real20 set, it reaches 27.27 dB PSNR / 0.871 SSIM, surpassing the best non-generative method RDNet (25.71 / 0.850) by 1.56 dB and the best generative method DAI (25.21 / 0.841) by 2.06 dB. The human evaluation confirms this advantage: GenSIRR achieves a 96.6% average success rate on OpenRR-val versus 41.2% for DAI and 30.4% for RDNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenSIRR outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest non-generative method RDNet (CVPR'25), it gains +1.56 dB PSNR on Real20 (27.27 vs 25.71) and +1.30 dB on average (27.93 vs 26.63), with a consistent 0.013–0.021 SSIM improvement. Against the closest generative competitor DAI (AAAI'26), the advantage is +2.06 dB on Real20 and +0.58 dB on average PSNR, while DAI retains a slight edge on SIR2 SSIM (0.919 vs 0.921) and Nature PSNR (26.81 vs 27.30) is actually won by GenSIRR. The gap widens further with DEBS (k=4), pushing average PSNR to 28.03 and SSIM to 0.931. In the human evaluation, GenSIRR achieves 96.6% average success rate on OpenRR-val compared to 41.2% (DAI) and 30.4% (RDNet), with 87% full-evaluator consensus versus 20% and 12% respectively. The paper attributes the large human-evaluation gap to non-generative methods' reliance on pixel-wise losses that suppress rather than structurally remove reflections.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the FLUX.1 Kontext large-scale diffusion model and the LDM architecture, repurposing a general-purpose image editing model for the specialist task of single-image reflection removal. It departs from prior SIRR methods (ERRNet, DSRNet, DSIT, RDNet) that rely on CNN/Transformer semantic encoders ill-suited to composite inputs, and from earlier generative SIRR attempts (L-DiffER, PromptRR, DAI) that depend on ambiguous natural-language prompts. The key conceptual departure is identifying that pre-trained VAE latent spaces do not respect the linear superposition physics of reflection formation, and proposing a reflection-equivariant VAE to explicitly enforce this structure, alongside a learnable task embedding that replaces text prompts entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that latent-space geometry (not reconstruction fidelity) is the critical factor for layer separation is potentially generalizable to other superposition problems like watermark removal and alpha matting, as the authors note. The 90.5% human-evaluated success rate versus 36–46% for faster baselines represents a large practical gap, though it comes at ~25× the inference cost (~2.5 s vs <100 ms), which limits real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the equivariance principle can be made architecture-agnostic, whether the 2.5 s latency can be reduced via distillation or few-step sampling without sacrificing the success-rate advantage, and whether the LTE approach transfers to other restoration tasks beyond reflection removal.
