## Problem

Existing generative data augmentation methods treat images as holistic entities, failing to account for the uneven distribution of discriminative information between foreground subjects and background context. This leads to three coupled failures: semantic drift of the subject identity, limited background diversity, and incoherent boundaries between the two regions. The paper frames this through an ontological lens, arguing that category recognition depends on a stable subject essence while tolerating environmental variation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262932/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a foreground mask extracted by the Transparent-Background segmentation model, a class label, and a randomly sampled background prompt word from an LLM-expanded vocabulary. It produces four augmented images per input at the same resolution, where the foreground subject is preserved (with geometric layout variations) and only the background region is regenerated by the diffusion model. The text prompt follows the template 'An image of <Class> with the background of <Background word>' to guide semantically meaningful background synthesis.

## Method

OntoAug operates in two stages: ontological decomposition and ontology-guided generation. In decomposition, a Transparent-Background model extracts a foreground mask that partitions the image into an ontological part (category-defining subject) and an incidental part (background). In generation, a layout structure augmentation module applies random combinations of scaling, rotation, translation, and horizontal flip to the foreground bounding box, while a background context augmentation module selects a diverse background word from a manually curated and LLM-expanded vocabulary. A modified trainable ControlNet, integrated with a frozen Stable Diffusion v2-inpainting backbone, conditions on both the foreground mask and the foreground image to perform inpainting exclusively in the background region, using the mask as a hard spatial constraint that prevents any modification of the subject pixels during denoising.

## Results

On fine-grained classification with ResNet-50 trained from scratch (300 epochs), OntoAug achieves top-1 accuracy of 84.62% on CUB, 94.29% on Stanford Cars, and 87.91% on Aircraft, setting new state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks. In the 10-shot few-shot setting on CUB, OntoAug reaches 67.55% average accuracy across three backbones, surpassing the second-best method (De-DA, 53.67%) by 13.88%. On the out-of-distribution Waterbirds dataset, OntoAug attains 74.82% average accuracy, outperforming De-DA by 1.75%. OntoAug also achieves the best fidelity-diversity trade-off, recording the lowest FID (e.g., 11.71 on Cars) and highest LPIPS (e.g., 0.69 on Cars) among all generative baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OntoAug outperforms all 14 baselines on every dataset and metric reported. Among generative methods, the closest competitor is Diff-Mix (81.62% CUB, 85.84% Aircraft), which OntoAug surpasses by 3.00% and 2.07% respectively; on Cars, De-DA (93.04%) is the nearest generative rival, beaten by 1.25%. Against the strongest non-generative baseline Guided-AP (77.08% CUB, 90.27% Cars, 84.32% Aircraft), OntoAug gains 7.54%, 4.02%, and 3.59% respectively. In the 10-shot few-shot regime, OntoAug's 67.55% average dwarfs De-DA (53.67%) and SaSPA (51.11%), a gap of over 13 points that no other method approaches. On WSOL (CUB), OntoAug leads with 55.39% average IoU accuracy versus Co-Mixup's 53.65% and SaSPA's 53.79%. On the OOD Waterbirds benchmark, OntoAug (74.82%) edges out De-DA (73.07%) by 1.75%, confirming that its diverse background vocabulary generalizes beyond the training distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

OntoAug extends the line of diffusion-based generative data augmentation (SaSPA, DiffuseMix, De-DA, Diff-Mix) by introducing an explicit ontological decomposition: the foreground subject is treated as the essential semantic core to be preserved, while the background is the incidental part to be diversified. It departs from prior methods that either alter the subject (Diff-Mix's inter-class mixing), constrain generation to style changes (DiffuseMix), or composite foreground pixels onto new backgrounds without semantic coherence (De-DA). The generation pipeline builds on the PBG salient-object-aware inpainting paradigm and a modified ControlNet conditioned on both a foreground mask and the original foreground image, making the mask a hard spatial constraint during denoising rather than a soft guidance signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that explicitly separating 'what the object is' from 'where it is' during augmentation simultaneously improves fidelity (lowest FID across datasets) and diversity (highest LPIPS), a trade-off that prior methods could not resolve. The 10-shot CUB result (67.55% vs. 53.67% for the next-best method) suggests the approach is particularly valuable in data-scarce regimes where preserving fine-grained subject identity while expanding contextual coverage is most critical. Open questions include whether the manually curated + LLM-expanded background vocabulary generalizes to open-world or multi-object scenes, whether the reliance on a single frozen inpainting backbone limits scalability, and whether the training-cost overhead of generating four augmented images per sample remains prohibitive at very large dataset scales.
