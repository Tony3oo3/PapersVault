## Problem

Frozen geometry foundation models (e.g., MASt3R) produce inconsistent sequential reconstructions on novel scenes because their geometric priors cannot adapt to unseen environments. Existing sequential reconstruction methods either train dedicated architectures with limited generalization or apply post-hoc optimization to frozen model outputs, neither of which enables the model to learn scene-specific knowledge during streaming inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263971/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a monocular RGB video stream at full resolution (longest side resized to 512 px), processing each incoming frame paired with the most recent keyframe through the frozen MASt3R network. It outputs per-pixel 3D pointmaps (H×W×3), confidence maps (H×W×1), and Sim(3) camera poses for each frame, which are fused into a dense point cloud. The system operates at approximately 10 FPS on a single A100 GPU, with prompt updates triggered only at keyframe creation events to maintain real-time performance.

## Method

Online3R inserts 32 learnable visual prompt tokens (dimension 1024) into the ViT encoder layers of a frozen MASt3R backbone, modulating feature extraction without altering pretrained weights. A local-global self-supervised loss guides prompt optimization: the local constraint uses confidence-weighted multi-view fused pointmaps as pseudo ground truth to enforce agreement with single-shot predictions, while the global constraint randomly samples two distant historical keyframes and penalizes disagreement in the current keyframe's predicted geometry across different reference frames. The total loss (λ=0.5 weighting) is backpropagated through the frozen network to update only the prompt parameters via AdamW (lr=1e-4) at each keyframe event, enabling the model to progressively encode scene-specific 3D structure while preserving the foundation model's general geometric capability.

## Results

On 7-Scenes, Online3R achieves the best accuracy (Acc) of 0.039 m and Chamfer distance of 0.053 m, surpassing the strongest online baseline MASt3R-SLAM* (Acc 0.068, Chamfer 0.056) and even offline methods like Test3R (Acc 0.105, Chamfer 0.121) and DUSt3R-GA (Acc 0.146, Chamfer 0.164). On NRGBD, Online3R achieves Acc 0.053 m and Chamfer 0.073 m, outperforming MASt3R-SLAM* (Acc 0.065, Chamfer 0.080) and all other online methods. For camera pose estimation, Online3R attains the lowest average ATE of 0.027 m (calibrated) and 0.056 m (uncalibrated) on TUM RGB-D, and 0.076 m on NRGBD, consistently beating MASt3R-SLAM and all prior online reconstruction methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Online3R outperforms all online baselines on both 7-Scenes and NRGBD for accuracy and Chamfer distance. On 7-Scenes, it reduces accuracy from 0.068 m (MASt3R-SLAM*) to 0.039 m (a 43% improvement) and from 0.124 m (Point3R) to 0.039 m (a 69% improvement). Notably, Online3R also surpasses all offline methods including Test3R (Acc 0.105) and DUSt3R-GA (Acc 0.146) on 7-Scenes, demonstrating that online prompt tuning can exceed the quality of batch-processed offline reconstruction. On NRGBD, Online3R (Acc 0.053) beats MASt3R-SLAM* (0.065) and Point3R (0.079), though MASt3R-GA offline (0.085) and Test3R (0.083) remain competitive on accuracy while Online3R wins on Chamfer (0.073 vs. 0.074 and 0.081). For pose estimation on TUM RGB-D, Online3R achieves the best average ATE in both calibrated (0.027 m vs. 0.030 m for MASt3R-SLAM) and uncalibrated (0.056 m vs. 0.060 m) settings, and on NRGBD it reduces ATE from 0.090 m to 0.076 m over MASt3R-SLAM* while dramatically outperforming Spann3R (1.444 m), CUT3R (0.861 m), and Point3R (0.615 m).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MASt3R-SLAM pipeline by inserting a small set of learnable visual prompt tokens (32 × 1024-d) into the frozen MASt3R encoder, adapting the Visual Prompt Tuning paradigm from NLP/2D vision to 3D geometry foundation models. It departs from Test3R, which also applies prompt tuning to geometry models but in an offline, triplet-based self-consistency regime with exponential cost, by making the adaptation fully online and keyframe-triggered. The local-global self-supervised loss is a new formulation: local pseudo-ground-truth comes from confidence-weighted multi-view fusion (inherited from MASt3R-SLAM), while global consistency enforces invariance of a keyframe's predicted geometry across randomly sampled historical reference frames—a constraint not present in prior sequential reconstruction methods (Spann3R, CUT3R, Point3R) that rely solely on recurrent or memory-based state.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a frozen, general-purpose geometry foundation model can be made scene-adaptive at test time with fewer than 1% additional parameters, closing a gap that purely frozen pipelines (MASt3R-SLAM, VGGT-SLAM) leave open in novel environments. The practical impact is significant for robotics and AR where deployment scenes differ from training distributions, and the ~10 FPS throughput makes it viable for real-time use. Key limitations remain: the system is restricted to static scenes, the global constraint only samples two keyframes per update (limiting long-horizon coverage), and the additional forward passes for loss computation introduce non-trivial overhead. Extending the approach to dynamic 4D scenes and to multi-scene or lifelong learning settings are natural open directions.
