## Problem

Current VLM-based embodied agents can describe what they see but cannot reason about what humans believe, desire, or intend, nor can they reason from their own perspective to make autonomous decisions. Existing Theory-of-Mind benchmarks are role-centric (inferring only the human's mental state via multiple-choice questions) and lack a Level-3 component that connects mental reasoning to concrete decision-making and action generation, leaving a fundamental gap between perception and embodied behavior.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263728/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips from home-environment simulators (VirtualHome, ThreeDWorld) along with text scenario context; for image-only models like GPT-4o, 64 uniformly sampled frames serve as input. The output is a structured, open-ended six-layer reasoning chain (<Perception> → <Belief> → <Desire> → <Intention> → <Decision> → <Action>), where the final <Action> layer emits a sequence of atomic operations in the form action(object) (e.g., open(fridge), pick_up(milk)). Unlike prior ToM benchmarks that use multiple-choice answers, MindPower requires free-form generation at every layer, and the <Belief> layer explicitly encodes second-order belief by modeling both the agent's own beliefs and its prediction of the human's beliefs.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage training pipeline (SFT followed by GRPO) built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, guided by a novel Mind-Reward that enforces BDI consistency and Robot-Centric perspective across the six-layer reasoning hierarchy. Each reasoning layer is decomposed into atomic actions of the form action(agent, object) with a perspective tag (human or embodied agent), and Mind-Reward computes a weighted sum of Atomic Accuracy (ROUGE-1 over matched atomic actions), Local Consistency (ROUGE-2 over adjacent pairs), and Global Consistency (ROUGE-L over the full sequence), with a separate Format-Reward verifying correct layer ordering via regex. The GRPO objective (following DeepSeekMath) samples groups of outputs, normalizes rewards within each group to compute advantages, and updates the policy with a clipped surrogate plus KL regularization against a reference model. The key design insight is that the Robot-Centric perspective—requiring simultaneous first-order (agent's own) and second-order (human's) mental-state inference at every layer—combined with the continuity-enforcing Mind-Reward, is what distinguishes this approach from standard step-by-step CoT or role-centric ToM evaluation.

## Results

On the MindPower benchmark (590 examples), the proposed model (SFT + Mind-Reward) achieves the best overall performance across all three levels: Perception (Sentence Transformer 59.93), Mental Reasoning (Belief S 46.49, Desire S 45.75, Intention S 42.57), and Decision Making & Action (Decision S 47.12, Action SR 11.75, AC 15.40, BPC 8.87). Compared to GPT-4o, the model improves Decision S by 12.77% (47.12 vs. 34.35) and Action AC by 12.49% (15.40 vs. 2.91). Against the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct (video-input), gains reach +20.04% in Perception S, +23.33% in Decision S, +11.6% in SR, and +15.25% in AC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed model (SFT + Mind-Reward) outperforms all 10 baselines on every metric in Table 2. Against GPT-4o (image-input), it gains +12.77% in Decision S (47.12 vs. 34.35) and +12.49% in Action AC (15.40 vs. 2.91), while also leading in Perception S (59.93 vs. 48.37) and BPC (8.87 vs. 8.05). Versus Gemini-2.5 Pro (video-input), the advantage is even larger on action metrics: SR 11.75 vs. 2.08 and AC 15.40 vs. 2.54, with Decision S at 47.12 vs. 33.87. Among open-source video models, Video-R1 is the strongest baseline (Decision S 30.33, SR 1.43, AC 1.72), yet the proposed model still leads by +16.79 in Decision S and +10.32 in SR. Notably, InternVL3.5-8B, LLaVA-OV-8B, and Video-ChatGPT score zero on both SR and AC because they produce non-executable expressions (e.g., 'identify()', 'scan()') rather than concrete atomic actions. The SFT-only ablation already surpasses all baselines, confirming that the MindPower Reasoning Hierarchy itself is the primary driver, with Mind-Reward providing further gains (e.g., SR 8.50→11.75, AC 10.48→15.40).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Theory-of-Mind benchmarking line (MuMA-ToM, MMToM-QA, FANToM, etc.) by shifting from a role-centric, multiple-choice formulation to a Robot-Centric, open-ended one that requires the agent to reason about both its own and the human's mental states and then produce executable actions. Methodologically, it adopts the two-stage SFT + GRPO paradigm from Visual-RFT and DeepSeekMath, but introduces a novel Mind-Reward that decomposes reasoning quality into atomic accuracy, local consistency, and global consistency over a structured BDI hierarchy. The key conceptual departure is the explicit linkage of ToM reasoning to embodied decision-making and action generation, which prior ToM benchmarks leave entirely unaddressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a concrete gap: even frontier VLMs (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.5 Pro) and human annotators achieve very low action correctness (~2–26%), suggesting that robot-centric ToM reasoning is a genuinely hard and underexplored capability. The finding that a structured six-layer BDI hierarchy substantially outperforms generic chain-of-thought for action generation (2.91% vs. 0.90% AC) provides evidence that explicit mental-state scaffolding matters for embodied agents. Limitations include the small scale (590 examples), restriction to home environments, and reliance on GPT-4o as an automatic judge for BDI consistency, which raises questions about evaluation reliability. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to multi-agent or outdoor settings and whether the Mind-Reward signal transfers to real robotic control.
