## Problem

Existing text-guided 3D editing methods cannot simultaneously understand complex natural-language instructions, automatically localize the intended editing region in 3D without user-provided masks, and preserve unedited geometry and appearance. Prior approaches either rely on expensive per-scene optimization (SDS-based), suffer from multi-view inconsistency (2D-edit-then-reconstruct), or still require manual 3D masks (VoxHammer). No prior system integrates these capabilities into a single intelligent agent.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260364/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D asset (represented as Trellis SLAT sparse voxels at 64³ resolution, decodable to 3D Gaussians or meshes) and a free-form text editing prompt as the sole inputs. It produces an edited 3D asset in the same SLAT representation. Notably, no user-provided 3D mask is required; the system automatically determines which voxels belong to the editing region. The pipeline supports three edit types (addition, modification, deletion) and handles both single objects and scenes.

## Method

Vinedresser3D is an agentic framework centered on Gemini-2.5-flash that coordinates four specialized tools. The MLLM first parses the prompt to produce decomposed text guidance (stage-1 geometry descriptions and stage-2 appearance descriptions) and selects the most informative rendered view, which is then edited by Nano Banana to yield image guidance. For automatic 3D region detection, PartField segments the asset into semantic parts at multiple granularities, and the MLLM selects which parts to edit; a k-NN proportion rule then extends the mask to boundary voxels to prevent unintended modification of preserved geometry. The actual editing operates in Trellis's SLAT latent space: RF-Solver performs second-order rectified-flow inversion of the original asset, followed by mask-guided inpainting where an interleaved denoising schedule alternates one timestep of Trellis-text and one timestep of Trellis-image, combining semantic prompt alignment with high-fidelity visual detail while replacing preserved-region features from the original inversion trajectory at every step.

## Results

On the main quantitative comparison (Table 1), Vinedresser3D without human-provided masks achieves the best CLIP-T score (0.252) and the best FID (29.49) among all methods, while with human-provided masks it achieves the best scores across all metrics (CLIP-T 0.252, CD 0.008, PSNR 37.69, SSIM 0.984, LPIPS 0.015, FID 27.38). In the user study (Table 2), Vinedresser3D wins 92.5% of comparisons against Trellis and 89.8% against VoxHammer on text alignment, and achieves 90.8% and 90.2% win rates respectively on overall 3D quality. The method is evaluated on 57 diverse 3D assets from GSO, PartObjaverseTiny, and Trellis-generated outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vinedresser3D outperforms all three baselines on text alignment (CLIP-T): 0.252 vs. 0.247 (Trellis), 0.235 (VoxHammer), and 0.227 (Instant3dit). On overall 3D quality (FID), the mask-free variant achieves 29.49, beating Trellis (31.10), VoxHammer (34.95), and Instant3dit (80.35); with human masks, FID drops further to 27.38. However, without human-provided masks, Vinedresser3D does not match Trellis on unedited-region preservation (PSNR 29.45 vs. 37.35, LPIPS 0.045 vs. 0.017), though it remains competitive with VoxHammer (PSNR 29.45 vs. 24.36). With human masks, Vinedresser3D surpasses Trellis on all preservation metrics (CD 0.008 vs. 0.010, PSNR 37.69 vs. 37.35, LPIPS 0.015 vs. 0.017). In the user study, Vinedresser3D achieves 92.5% and 89.8% win rates over Trellis and VoxHammer on text alignment, and 90.8% and 90.2% on overall 3D quality, confirming consistent human-preference advantages.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of three recent developments: native 3D flow-based generation (Trellis with its structured latent representation), accurate rectified-flow inversion (RF-Solver), and multimodal LLM reasoning. It departs from the dominant '2D editing + 3D reconstruction' paradigm and from concurrent work like VoxHammer, which also edits in native 3D space but still requires user-provided 3D masks. The key conceptual departure is framing 3D editing as an agentic orchestration problem: a 2D-trained MLLM (Gemini-2.5-flash) plans the edit, selects views, and coordinates specialized tools (PartField for segmentation, Nano Banana for image editing, Trellis for 3D generation), rather than relying on a single monolithic model. The interleaved Trellis-text/Trellis-image denoising strategy is a novel practical design that exploits the complementary strengths of text-conditioned (broad semantic alignment) and image-conditioned (high-fidelity detail) generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Vinedresser3D demonstrates that a 2D-trained MLLM can serve as an effective 'brain' for 3D editing pipelines, automatically localizing edit regions and generating decomposed structural/appearance guidance without any user-provided masks—a practical barrier that prior native-3D editing methods still require. The agentic framing is timely and suggests a path toward more flexible, multi-turn, and instruction-following 3D content creation. However, the evaluation is limited to 57 assets with hand-crafted prompts, the system depends on proprietary tools (Gemini, Nano Banana) that may not be reproducible, and the MLLM still cannot consume native 3D input, leaving a gap between 2D-rendered understanding and true 3D spatial reasoning. Open questions include whether this agentic approach scales to complex scenes with many interacting objects, how robust it is to ambiguous or multi-step editing instructions, and whether native 3D MLLMs will eventually subsume the multi-view rendering workaround.
