## Problem

Video super-resolution faces a fundamental efficiency-quality trade-off: single-step models are fast but produce over-smoothed, low-creativity outputs, while multi-step diffusion models generate rich high-frequency details at prohibitive computational cost. Existing distillation approaches that compress multi-step diffusion into a single forward pass inevitably lose the iterative reasoning that enables plausible detail hallucination, resulting in a noticeable drop in visual richness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263106/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality video (synthesized via RealESRGAN degradation or real-world Internet-sourced clips) and produces a high-quality super-resolved video. Processing occurs in the latent space of a 3D VAE (from Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B), where the input is encoded to a latent z_L and the output is decoded from the refined latent. Experiments use 29-frame 720×1280 videos, and long videos are handled via overlapping clip splitting with temporal position-aware merging.

## Method

PS-SR introduces a Speculative Diffusion pipeline with two key mechanisms. First, a computationally asymmetric design: a powerful base model (Wan2.1 DiT fine-tuned with LoRA) performs a single comprehensive flow-matching step to establish global structure, after which a lightweight draft model (the same DiT pruned from 30 to 10 blocks, with base-model features concatenated channel-wise) executes T−1=3 refinement steps. Second, a frequency-domain update rule applies a high-pass filter to each draft prediction in YUV luminance space, adaptively blending only high-frequency components while preserving the low-frequency content from the previous step, thereby preventing semantic drift. The base model is trained with L2, Variational Score Distillation, and adversarial losses in latent space followed by patch-wise pixel-space L2+LPIPS refinement; the draft model is trained with L2 and pixel losses only, omitting distribution-level alignment to focus on detail synthesis.

## Results

On UDM10, PS-SR achieves the best SSIM (0.7547), LPIPS (0.2444), DISTS (0.1277), and NIQE (4.9902) among all compared methods, with a PSNR of 23.913 (second only to DOVE's 24.039). On SPMCS, PS-SR leads in PSNR (22.092), SSIM (0.6287), LPIPS (0.2940), DISTS (0.1454), and NIQE (3.9542). Inference time is 21.11 s/sample on an A800 GPU for 29-frame 720×1280 video, comparable to single-step methods (DOVE: 20.43 s, SeedVR2: 22.36 s) and far faster than multi-step baselines (STAR: 98.61 s, SeedVR: 188.93 s). PS-SR also achieves the lowest flow warping error on all four datasets, confirming superior temporal consistency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two multi-step diffusion baselines (STAR with 15 steps, SeedVR with 50 steps), PS-SR wins on nearly all reconstruction and perceptual metrics while running 4.7×–9× faster (21 s vs. 99 s and 189 s per sample). Among single-step methods, PS-SR consistently outperforms DLoRAL and SeedVR2 on pixel-fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and NIQE across all four datasets; for example, on SPMCS PS-SR reaches SSIM 0.6287 vs. DLoRAL's 0.5710 and SeedVR2's 0.5950. The main trade-off is with DOVE, which leads on no-reference perceptual sharpness (CLIP-IQA and MUSIQ) by a notable margin (e.g., MUSIQ 69.6 vs. 61.0 on SPMCS), but PS-SR surpasses DOVE on every reference-based metric (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DISTS) and on NIQE, indicating that DOVE's higher sharpness scores come at the cost of greater semantic drift from the input. PS-SR also achieves the lowest flow warping error on all four datasets (e.g., 0.82 on SPMCS vs. DOVE's 1.16 and STAR's 1.41), confirming better temporal coherence than all five baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

PS-SR departs from the prevailing binary in diffusion-based VSR—slow multi-step sampling (STAR, SeedVR) versus fast but creativity-limited single-step distillation (SeedVR2, DOVE, DLoRAL)—by repurposing the speculative sampling paradigm from LLM inference (Chen et al. 2023; Leviathan et al. 2023) into an asymmetric base/draft model pipeline for video diffusion. The mathematical backbone is conditional flow matching with paired data (Albergo et al., ICML 2024), and the base model inherits generative priors from the Wan2.1 DiT via LoRA fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is the frequency-domain update rule, which has no direct analogue in prior VSR or diffusion distillation work: it explicitly decouples low-frequency structure (locked after step one) from high-frequency detail (freely refined by the draft model), preventing the semantic drift that plagues unconstrained multi-step refinement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PS-SR offers a practically deployable path to near-single-step inference speed (21.1 s/sample) with reconstruction fidelity (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, DISTS) that matches or exceeds 50-step diffusion baselines, which is significant for real-time video enhancement pipelines. The frequency-domain update rule is a simple, architecture-agnostic mechanism that could be transplanted to other generative restoration or enhancement tasks where content fidelity must be preserved while adding detail. Open questions include whether the approach scales to substantially longer videos or higher resolutions without the overlapping-clip heuristic, whether the base model's single-step quality ceiling limits the overall system, and whether the speculative diffusion paradigm generalizes beyond VSR to other conditional video generation tasks.
