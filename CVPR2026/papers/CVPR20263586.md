## Problem

Existing training-free panoramic depth estimation methods decompose equirectangular panoramas into independent perspective views and fuse their predictions without enforcing cross-view geometric consistency, resulting in scale ambiguity and depth discontinuities. Additionally, VGGT-like 3D foundation models, which inherently produce globally coherent multi-view reconstructions, have not been adapted to panoramic inputs due to the domain gap between perspective training data and ERP geometry (spherical distortion, non-uniform resolution, 360° wrap-around).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263586/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single equirectangular (ERP) panoramic image of arbitrary resolution (H×W×3) and produces a corresponding ERP depth map (H×W). Internally, the panorama is adaptively sliced into a set of perspective views (8 base views plus up to 4 adaptive neighbor views), processed by a VGGT-like model to reconstruct a 3D point cloud, and then reprojected back to the ERP grid. The framework is entirely training-free and plug-and-play, supporting interchangeable VGGT backbones (VGGT, π³, FastVGGT) without any fine-tuning.

## Method

VGGT-360 is a three-stage, training-free pipeline that reformulates panoramic depth estimation as reprojection from a globally consistent 3D model. First, uncertainty-guided adaptive projection scores each base view using Sobel-gradient-based geometric uncertainty and allocates additional neighboring views to the top-K most uncertain (geometry-poor) regions, yielding denser sampling where it matters most. Second, structure-saliency enhanced attention injects a pixel-level confidence map—combining gradient saliency, an edge-band prior for overlap regions, and a validity mask—into VGGT's intra-frame attention as an additive log-confidence bias, steering the model toward geometrically reliable regions without modifying pretrained weights. Third, correlation-weighted 3D model correction derives per-point reliability scores from VGGT's final attention layer using three complementary metrics (sharpness via normalized Shannon entropy, locality via Gaussian-weighted spatial compactness, and symmetry via Bhattacharyya coefficient), then performs a weighted aggregation of multi-view depth observations to produce the final ERP depth map.

## Results

On Matterport3D, VGGT-360 with FastVGGT achieves AbsRel 0.078 and δ₁ 0.943, outperforming the best supervised method (Depth Anywhere + BiFuse++, AbsRel 0.085) and the best training-free method (RPG360 + Metric3D v2, AbsRel 0.203). On Stanford2D3D, VGGT-360 with π³ reaches AbsRel 0.065 and δ₁ 0.952, surpassing the best supervised baseline (Depth Anywhere + UniFuse, AbsRel 0.082) by ~21% and the training-free 360MD (AbsRel 0.268) by ~76%. On Replica360-2K, VGGT-360 with FastVGGT achieves AbsRel 0.069 and δ₁ 0.950, beating the best supervised method (DAC, AbsRel 0.142) by ~51% and the best training-free method (HDE360 + HoHoNet, AbsRel 0.107) by ~35%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VGGT-360 outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset where they share evaluation. Against the strongest supervised method, Depth Anywhere [39] (AbsRel 0.085 on Matterport3D, 0.082 on Stanford2D3D, 0.219 on Replica360-2K), VGGT-360 achieves lower AbsRel by 8–35% while requiring no training data. Against the strongest training-free baselines, VGGT-360 reduces AbsRel from 0.203 (RPG360) to 0.078 on Matterport3D (62% reduction), from 0.268 (360MD) to 0.065 on Stanford2D3D (76% reduction), and from 0.107 (HDE360) to 0.069 on Replica360-2K (35% reduction). Among supervised baselines, VGGT-360 also beats BiFuse++ [37] (0.107→0.068 on Stanford2D3D), Elite360D [1] (0.105→0.078 on Matterport3D), EGFormer [44] (0.147→0.078), HRDFuse [2] (0.117→0.078), BiFuse [36] (0.120→0.065 on Stanford2D3D, 0.318→0.069 on Replica360-2K), UniFuse [19] (0.094→0.065, 0.233→0.069), HoHoNet [31] (0.259→0.069), and DAC [15] (0.124→0.065, 0.142→0.069). The consistent margin across all three VGGT backbones (VGGT, FastVGGT, π³) confirms that the gains come from the three plug-and-play modules rather than a specific backbone choice.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant training-free panoramic depth paradigm (360MD, RPG360, HDE360), which slices panoramas into perspective views and fuses independently predicted per-view depths, by instead leveraging VGGT-like 3D foundation models to reconstruct a globally coherent 3D representation and reprojecting it back to the ERP domain. It builds directly on VGGT, π³, and FastVGGT as interchangeable backbones, injecting three plug-and-play modules (uncertainty-guided adaptive projection, structure-saliency enhanced attention, correlation-weighted 3D correction) that adapt these perspective-trained models to panoramic inputs without any fine-tuning. Conceptually, it shifts the problem from multi-view depth fusion to multi-view 3D reconstruction followed by reprojection, exploiting VGGT's intrinsic cross-view geometric consistency rather than post-hoc blending.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a training-free method outperforms supervised panoramic depth estimators by 27–36% in Abs Rel (even in-domain on Matterport3D) challenges the assumption that labeled panoramic data is necessary for competitive depth estimation, and the plug-and-play design means the approach immediately benefits from future VGGT improvements. However, quantitative evaluation is limited to three indoor benchmarks; outdoor generalization is assessed only qualitatively on OmniPhotos without ground truth, leaving open whether the 3D-consistency advantage holds in unstructured outdoor scenes with large depth ranges. The computational overhead of running multi-view VGGT inference (1.4–1.5 s per panorama on an RTX 4090) and the sensitivity to the number of base views remain practical constraints for real-time applications.
