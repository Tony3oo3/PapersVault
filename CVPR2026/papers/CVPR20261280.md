## Problem

MeanFlow enables high-quality one-step image generation by jointly learning instantaneous and average velocity fields, but its training dynamics remain poorly understood. The standard training applies a fixed loss and sampling scheme throughout, ignoring the critical interplay between the two coupled velocities, which leads to slow convergence and suboptimal few-step generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261280/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (from ImageNet's 1000 categories) and a random Gaussian noise vector as input, and produces a 256×256 RGB image in pixel space using DiT transformer backbones. Generation is performed via a single ODE integration step z_r = z_t − (t−r)·u_θ(z_t, r, t), requiring only 1 or 2 network function evaluations (NFE) for few-step inference, in contrast to the 250+ steps needed by standard diffusion/flow models.

## Method

The paper identifies three key training dynamics through controlled experiments: (i) instantaneous velocity v must be well-formed before average velocity u can be learned, (ii) small temporal gaps (Δt) in u-supervision facilitate v-formation while large gaps destabilize it, and (iii) task affinity analysis shows small-Δt pretraining creates a more favorable initialization for large-Δt learning than pure v-loss pretraining. Building on these insights, the method introduces two components: (1) acceleration of v-learning via established techniques (MinSNR loss weighting or DTD timestep sampling) to rapidly establish the instantaneous velocity, and (2) a progressive weighting β(Δt, s) = 1 − s + λs(1−Δt) on the u-loss that prioritizes small temporal gaps at the start of training (s=1) and linearly transitions to uniform weighting across all gaps (s=0). This curriculum-style scheduling suppresses destabilizing large-Δt supervision early while gradually introducing it, yielding 2.5× faster convergence and a 16% FID reduction (3.43→2.87) on 1-NFE ImageNet 256×256 with DiT-XL.

## Results

On the ImageNet 256×256 benchmark with DiT-XL/2 (676M params, 240 epochs), the proposed method with DTD achieves a 1-NFE FID of 2.87 and 2-NFE FID of 2.64, improving over MeanFlow's 3.43 and 2.93 respectively — a 16% FID reduction. The method also achieves ~2.5× faster convergence, matching MeanFlow's final performance in roughly 96 epochs instead of 240. Notably, a smaller DiT-L/2 backbone (459M) with the proposed training reaches 1-NFE FID 3.47, matching the full DiT-XL/2 MeanFlow baseline (3.43).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline MeanFlow-XL/2 (240 epochs), the proposed method with DTD wins decisively: 1-NFE FID drops from 3.43 to 2.87 and 2-NFE FID from 2.93 to 2.64. Compared to other end-to-end few-step methods, the gap is even more pronounced — iCT-XL/2 (34.24 1-NFE FID), Shortcut-XL/2 (10.60), and iMM-XL/2 (7.77 2-NFE) are all far behind. The only method that outperforms the proposed approach is MeanFlow-XL/2+ trained for 1000 epochs (2.20 2-NFE FID), but the proposed method reaches 2.64 in just 240 epochs, i.e., 4× less training. Against multi-step diffusion/flow references (DiT-XL/2: 2.27, SiT-XL/2: 2.06 at 250×2 NFE), the 1-NFE result of 2.87 substantially narrows the gap, and the 2-NFE result of 2.64 is competitive with StyleGAN-XL (2.30) and STARFlow (2.40) at 1 NFE. Across model sizes (B/4, M/2, L/2), the method consistently outperforms vanilla MeanFlow, with the largest relative gains on smaller models (e.g., B/4: 11.58→10.20 1-NFE FID).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MeanFlow [18], which introduced the joint learning of instantaneous and average velocity fields for one-step generative modeling, and on the broader flow matching framework [39]. It departs from prior few-step methods (consistency models, shortcut models, IMM) by retaining MeanFlow's end-to-end formulation while providing the first systematic empirical analysis of how the two coupled velocity components interact during training. The training acceleration components (MinSNR loss weighting, DTD timestep sampling) are repurposed from standard diffusion training literature [25, 35] and applied here to the specific problem of rapidly establishing the instantaneous velocity before average velocity learning can proceed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard MeanFlow training is actively suboptimal—because uniform temporal-gap weighting from the start destabilizes the instantaneous velocity that average velocity learning depends on—reframes how practitioners should approach few-step generative model training. The 16% FID reduction (3.43→2.87) on 1-NFE ImageNet 256×256 and 2.5× training speedup are practically significant for real-time generation applications. Open questions include whether these learning dynamics generalize to latent-space models, video generation, or non-DiT architectures, and whether the simple linear progressive schedule is optimal or whether more adaptive scheduling could yield further gains.
