## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods are restricted to a fixed number of agents or rely on autoregressive reaction synthesis that accumulates errors across agents. Multi-person interaction datasets are small and less diverse than single-person datasets, making it difficult to train generalist models that generalize to arbitrary group sizes. The paper tackles how to synthesize realistic motions for any number of agents from text while effectively leveraging heterogeneous single-person and multi-person data.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a target agent count N as input, and produces N synchronized 3D motion sequences in a unified non-canonical SMPL skeleton representation (22 joints). Generation operates in a compact multi-token latent space (16×32 tokens per agent) rather than raw joint-space, with the motion prior generated once via P-Flow and subsequent agent reactions generated autoregressively via S-Flow. The framework is trained on dual-agent data but generalizes zero-shot to N>2 agents by iteratively applying S-Flow with all previously generated motions as context.

## Method

UMF decouples number-free generation into a single-pass motion prior stage (P-Flow) and multi-pass reaction stages (S-Flow), both operating in a shared multi-token VAE latent space that bridges heterogeneous single-person (HumanML3D) and multi-person (InterHuman) datasets. P-Flow reinterprets the flow matching trajectory as a piecewise pyramid: early timesteps interpolate between downsampled latents at lower resolution while later timesteps operate at full resolution, all within one transformer, reducing multi-token computational cost by ~1/K. S-Flow learns a joint probabilistic path with two coupled objectives: a reaction transformation path (context C → reaction W) and an auxiliary context reconstruction path (Gaussian noise → C), where a transformer-based context adapter encodes all previously generated motions into a global context that serves as the adaptive starting distribution. This dual-path design replaces deterministic conditioning (e.g., ControlNet) with a learned, context-aware transition that balances reaction prediction against global interaction awareness, thereby mitigating autoregressive error accumulation. An asymmetric inference budget (50 P-Flow steps at 45:5 low-to-high resolution ratio, 10 S-Flow steps) keeps total computation tractable as N grows.

## Results

On the InterHuman benchmark, UMF achieves the best FID of 4.772, outperforming the strongest specialist baseline InterMask (5.154) by 7% and the primary generalist baseline FreeMotion (6.740) by 29%. UMF also attains the best R Top-1 (0.467) and second-best R Top-3 (0.694) and MM-Distance (3.784), with a Diversity score (8.039) closely matching ground truth (7.948). On InterHuman-AS, UMF improves Top-3 R-Precision by over 30% (0.530 vs. 0.407) and reduces MM-Distance by 27% (4.987 vs. 6.860) compared to ReGenNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary generalist baseline FreeMotion [12], UMF wins decisively on all fidelity metrics: FID drops from 6.740 to 4.772 (−29%), R Top-3 rises from 0.544 to 0.694 (+28%), and MM-Distance improves from 3.848 to 3.784. Versus the strongest specialist dual-agent method InterMask [22], UMF achieves a 7% FID improvement (4.772 vs. 5.154) and better R Top-1/Top-2, though InterMask retains a slight edge on MModality (1.737 vs. 1.398). Compared to TIMotion [57], UMF trades off R-Precision (0.694 vs. 0.724 Top-3) for a substantially lower FID (4.772 vs. 5.433), indicating UMF generates more realistic motions at the cost of slightly weaker text alignment. On the action-reaction benchmark InterHuman-AS, UMF surpasses ReGenNet [62] by 30% in R Top-3 (0.530 vs. 0.407) and 27% in MM-Distance (4.987 vs. 6.860), while ReGenNet retains a marginal FID advantage (2.265 vs. 2.577). Single-agent baselines (TEMOS, T2M, MDM, ComMDM) are all substantially outperformed, confirming that the unified multi-person framework does not sacrifice individual motion quality. UMF also outperforms in2IN [43] and MoMat-MoGen [4] on FID while matching or exceeding their R-Precision scores.

## Relation to Prior Work

UMF builds directly on the flow matching framework (Lipman et al.) and rectified flow (Liu et al.) as its generative backbone, while adapting the pyramidal flow matching idea from video generation (Jin et al.) into a single-transformer hierarchical resolution scheme for motion. It adopts FreeMotion's (Fan et al.) 1+N decoupled paradigm—motion prior followed by autoregressive reactions—but replaces FreeMotion's deterministic ControlNet-style conditioning with S-Flow's joint probabilistic path that simultaneously learns reaction transformation and context reconstruction. The multi-token latent space with a latent adapter (inspired by Dai et al.) departs from both FreeMotion's raw-motion-space training and single-token latent approaches, unifying heterogeneous single-person (HumanML3D) and multi-person (InterHuman) datasets within a shared representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Number-free text-to-motion generation is critical for applications in robotics, VR, and interactive media where the number of agents is not known a priori, yet prior methods were restricted to fixed or dual-agent settings. UMF's zero-shot generalization to N>2 agents (validated via user study) and its ~5× efficiency gain over FreeMotion at better quality make it a practically deployable generalist model. However, the paper acknowledges a ceiling around ~10 agents and the absence of quantitative benchmarks for N>2 scenarios, leaving open whether the autoregressive S-Flow paradigm scales to dense crowd dynamics (~100 agents) without visual priors from large-scale video diffusion models.
