## Problem

Existing CLIP-based Zero-Shot Anomaly Detection methods apply a uniform, patch-agnostic adaptation to all image patches, ignoring that different regions (object components, backgrounds, anomalies) carry distinct semantic content. This monolithic design fundamentally limits the model's ability to capture fine-grained anomaly patterns, and the application of Mixture-of-Experts to the generalization-critical ZSAD task remains entirely unexplored.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image resized to 518×518 and produces two outputs: an image-level anomaly score in [0,1] and a pixel-level anomaly map in [0,1]^(H×W). During inference, the anomaly map is computed by comparing multi-scale PAA-refined patch features (at window sizes s∈{1,3,5}) against averaged text embeddings for 'normal' and 'anomalous' prompts, while the image-level score uses a Depth-wise Adapter on final-layer features. Training is supervised on a single auxiliary dataset (VisA or MVTec-AD), and evaluation is performed on 14 unseen-category datasets spanning industrial and medical domains.

## Method

MoECLIP integrates Mixture-of-Experts modules (K=4 LoRA experts, Top-2 routing, rank r=8) at four layers (6, 12, 18, 24) of a frozen CLIP ViT/L/14-336, dynamically routing each patch to specialized experts. To prevent functional redundancy, Frozen Orthogonal Feature Separation (FOFS) partitions the d-dimensional input into K non-overlapping subspaces via frozen random orthogonal matrices (QR decomposition), so each expert's LoRA down-projection A matrix is a block-diagonal with only its assigned subspace active. At the output stage, a simplex Equiangular Tight Frame (ETF) loss regularizes expert outputs to approximate the ideal Gram matrix with pairwise cosine similarity of −1/(K−1), complementing FOFS's input-stage separation. A parameter-free Patch Average Aggregation (PAA) module is applied during training to inject multi-scale context, and a lightweight Depth-wise Adapter refines final-layer features for the image-level score. The total loss combines Focal+Dice segmentation, BCE classification, ETF, and balance losses, with all CLIP backbone weights kept frozen to preserve generalization.

## Results

Across 14 benchmark datasets (5 industrial, 9 medical), MoECLIP achieves the best average image-level AUROC of 89.6% and AP of 90.6%, improving over the second-best Bayes-PFL by 3.0% AUROC and 2.4% AP. On pixel-level segmentation, MoECLIP reaches 88.1% average AUROC and 57.6% AP, outperforming the runner-up AA-CLIP (86.0% AUROC, 52.9% AP). The method is trained solely on VisA (industrial) yet generalizes strongly to medical domains, e.g., achieving 88.5% image-level AUROC on Brain MRI and 97.3% pixel-level AUROC on Brain MRI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoECLIP is compared against six CLIP-based ZSAD methods: WinCLIP (CVPR 2023, handcrafted prompts + multi-scale patches), April-GAN (CVPRw 2023, linear adapter), AnomalyCLIP (ICLR 2024, object-agnostic prompt learning), AdaCLIP (ECCV 2024, hybrid learnable prompts), AA-CLIP (CVPR 2025, anomaly-aware residual adapter), and Bayes-PFL (CVPR 2025, Bayesian prompt fine-tuning). MoECLIP achieves the best average performance on both image-level (89.6% AUROC) and pixel-level (88.1% AUROC) metrics, surpassing the strongest baselines Bayes-PFL (86.6% / 85.6%) and AA-CLIP (85.5% / 86.0%) by 3.0% and 2.1% AUROC respectively. The largest margins appear on medical datasets: on Brain MRI, MoECLIP leads Bayes-PFL by 6.6% image-level AUROC (88.5 vs 81.9) and 1.6% pixel-level AUROC (97.3 vs 95.7). MoECLIP does not win every individual dataset—Bayes-PFL edges it on VisA image-level AUROC (86.8 vs 83.6) and AA-CLIP on BTAD image-level AUROC (94.8 vs 93.1)—but its consistent top or near-top results across all 14 datasets yield the best overall average. All competing methods were re-trained under a unified protocol (VisA as auxiliary training set) to ensure fair comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the CLIP-based Zero-Shot Anomaly Detection (ZSAD) line of work (WinCLIP, AnomalyCLIP, AdaCLIP, AA-CLIP, Bayes-PFL) by identifying a shared limitation across all prior methods: a patch-agnostic design that applies uniform adaptation to every image patch regardless of its content. It departs from this by introducing a Mixture-of-Experts module built from LoRA experts into the frozen CLIP Vision Encoder, enabling content-based dynamic routing of each patch to a specialized expert. The expert specialization mechanisms—Frozen Orthogonal Feature Separation (FOFS) at the input and a simplex ETF loss at the output—are novel contributions that address functional redundancy, a known problem in MoE architectures previously tackled only at the output level. The norm-matching residual connection and text prompt averaging are directly adapted from AA-CLIP, while the Top-k routing and weighted-sum output follow the standard MoE formulation from Shazeer et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoECLIP establishes that patch-level dynamic routing is substantially more effective than uniform adaptation for ZSAD, offering a principled way to specialize a frozen VLM for fine-grained anomaly localization without sacrificing its generalization. The FOFS + ETF combination provides a reusable recipe for preventing expert collapse in LoRA-based MoE, which could transfer to other PEFT settings beyond anomaly detection. However, the approach is evaluated only on ViT-L/14 with a fixed K=4 and Top-2 routing; whether the benefits scale to larger backbones, more experts, or other VLMs (e.g., SigLIP, DINOv2) remains open. The moderate SOTA margins (≈3% AUROC on average) and the reliance on a single auxiliary training dataset (VisA) leave questions about robustness to training data choice and domain shift beyond the industrial-to-medical transfer demonstrated here.
