## Problem

Exact P3P solvers require solving cubic or quartic polynomial equations, which introduces numerical fragility and computational overhead. Although affine camera models (weak and para perspective) simplify projection to a linear mapping, no unified direct solver existed for the affine P3P problem, and the relationship between affine and exact P3P solutions remained unexplored as a practical pathway to camera pose estimation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes three 2D-3D point correspondences expressed in normalized image coordinates (camera intrinsics assumed known) and outputs a 3x3 rotation matrix and 3x1 translation vector. A distinguishing feature is that the solver operates under a constant-depth affine assumption (weak or para perspective), so the 2D measurements are treated as linear projections rather than perspective divisions; the output can optionally be refined to satisfy full perspective constraints via a few Newton iterations on the rotation.

## Method

The paper derives a unified algebraic formulation for both weak and para perspective P3P: after centroid-shifting the 2D and 3D points to eliminate translation, the problem is parameterized via the nullspace of a 2x4 coefficient matrix, yielding two unknown scalars (alpha, beta). Rotation-matrix orthogonality constraints reduce the system to a single bi-quadratic equation in alpha, solvable by the quadratic formula without complex arithmetic. To bridge the gap between affine and exact perspective, the authors propose an iterative upgrade that applies Newton's method on the Lie algebra (small-angle approximation) to refine only the rotation matrix via a 3x3 linear system per iteration, then recovers translation from a 2x3 linear solve. Two upgrade iterations suffice to match the state-of-the-art Ding solver in accuracy while retaining a ~4% speed advantage over the quartic-based approach.

## Results

On the IMC2023 dataset (large depth variation), the upgraded affine solvers (Weak₂ and Para₂) achieve recall values of 25.0–25.1% at 0.5°/1%, 40.7–40.9% at 1°/2%, and 51.7–51.9% at 3°/3%, matching the Ding solver (25.1/40.9/51.9%) within 0.2 percentage points. On the EPOS dataset (small depth variation), Weak₂ and Para₂ reach 8.4–8.5% / 19.3% / 37.1% recall, again essentially identical to Ding (8.3/19.3/36.8%). In terms of speed, the affine solvers with two upgrade iterations run in ~45–48 μs per solve versus 50.1 μs for Ding, a ~4% speedup, while the vanilla affine solvers (0 upgrades) are ~20% faster but sacrifice 1.5–1.7 percentage points of recall under large depth variation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the Ding et al. [12] exact P3P solver (CVPR 2023), the current state-of-the-art. With two Newton-based upgrade iterations, both Weak₂ and Para₂ match Ding's recall to within 0.2 percentage points on both EPOS and IMC2023, while being ~4% faster per solve (45–48 μs vs. 50.1 μs) due to the bi-quadratic equation replacing a cubic/quartic. Without any upgrade (Weak₀, Para₀), the affine solvers are ~20% faster but lose 1.5–1.7 pp of recall on IMC2023 (large depth variation) and 0.4–1.6 pp on EPOS (small depth variation), with the gap widening as depth deviation increases. The vanilla affine solvers also require more RANSAC iterations to converge (2.00M vs. 1.51M on IMC2023), partially offsetting their per-solve speed advantage. Overall, the paper demonstrates a tie in accuracy with a modest speed win for the upgraded affine solvers, and a clear speed-accuracy trade-off for the un-upgraded variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate affine camera models—weak perspective (Alter 1994, Huang et al. 1995) and para perspective (Ohta et al., Basri 1996)—under a single P3P derivation that collapses to a bi-quadratic equation, in contrast to the quartic or cubic+quadratic formulations of exact P3P solvers (Grunert, Haralick et al., Ke et al. 2017, Ding et al. 2023). The upgrade procedure from affine to exact P3P builds directly on Ke et al.'s algebraic formulation, applying Newton's method on the Lie algebra to refine the rotation. The paper positions affine P3P as a historically underexplored sub-problem that fell out of favor as perspective PnP solvers matured, and revives it with a practical iterative bridge to full perspective accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The bi-quadratic reduction offers a numerically simpler and slightly faster (4–10%) alternative to the state-of-the-art Ding solver, with the added benefit that real solutions are found via the quadratic formula without complex arithmetic. The two-iteration upgrade to exact P3P is a lightweight, guaranteed-valid-on-SO(3) refinement that avoids the 6×6 joint optimization common in SLAM. Open questions include extension to superzoom/telecentric cameras (no public benchmark exists), and whether the affine-then-upgrade strategy generalizes to harder problems like P4Pfr, P1P, or multi-view geometry. The main limitation is that vanilla affine solvers remain fragile under large depth variation, restricting their standalone use to near-field or small-object scenarios.
