## Problem

Existing long-video understanding benchmarks either evaluate short isolated clips or coarse video-level summarization, conflating temporal granularity with content variability and failing to isolate whether models truly reason over long temporal structure. This paper asks whether current VLMs can maintain coherent understanding across scene-level contexts (segments where visual and semantic consistency holds), and reveals a sharp accuracy drop as the temporal distance between question and supporting evidence increases, exposing a fundamental long-context forgetting problem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260313/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SceneBench provides 2,485 videos (avg. 1,978 s, up to 4+ hours) with paired audio tracks; each of the 8,903 QA pairs is annotated with the temporal span of required evidence (SceneQA cues must span ≥2 minutes). For Scene-RAG, the input is a text query plus the full video; the system decomposes the query into fine-grained textual clues via Qwen3-14B, retrieves top-k scene segments (visual embeddings from InternVideo2 + audio captions from Qwen-Audio2), and fuses retrieved frames with uniformly sampled frames at a 0.5 ratio before passing to the MLLM. Outputs vary by task: multiple-choice answers (SceneQA, ClipQA, I-VQA), free-form comment or title predictions, and retrieved multimodal scene memories as intermediate context.

## Method

SceneBench introduces six task types (SceneQA, SceneQA-Audio, ClipQA, I-VQA, Comment Prediction, Title Prediction) designed so that scene-level questions require integrating non-instantaneous cues spanning multiple minutes, preventing models from exploiting short local patterns or simple plot matching. To mitigate the observed forgetting, Scene-RAG organizes video memory around semantically coherent scenes rather than uniform clips: (1) Scene Tiling applies TV-L1 regularization to a frame-similarity sequence to detect piecewise-constant segments, discarding those below a minimum length; (2) Multimodal Memory Construction encodes each segment with InternVideo2 for visual semantics and Qwen-Audio2 for audio transcription/captioning, aligning both along the timeline; (3) Query Retrieval uses Qwen3-14B to decompose the user query into fine-grained textual clues, performs similarity search over the scene embedding bank, and feeds the top-k retrieved scenes to the MLLM. Across three 7B baselines, Scene-RAG yields an average +1.40% gain on SceneBench and +10.47 points on Video-MME over the non-RAG baseline, with the largest improvements on SceneQA-Audio (+2.73) and Comment Prediction (+1.97), confirming that scene-structured retrieval specifically targets the long-range evidence aggregation bottleneck.

## Results

On SceneBench, the average score across all 16 evaluated MLLMs is 52.3, with Gemini 2.5 Pro achieving the highest average of 80.3. A critical finding is the sharp performance drop from clip-level to scene-level tasks: open-source models average 51.73 on ClipQA but only 26.11 on SceneQA (a 49.5% decrease) and 29.08 on SceneQA-Audio. Scene-RAG provides consistent gains over the no-RAG baseline, with an average improvement of +1.40 points on SceneBench and +10.47 points on Video-MME, outperforming Video-RAG by an additional +1.20 points on average across the three tested models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Scene-RAG consistently outperforms both the no-RAG baseline and Video-RAG across all three tested models (LongVA, Long-LLaVA, LLaVA-OneVision) on both SceneBench and Video-MME. On SceneBench, Scene-RAG yields gains of +1.1 to +1.7 over the baseline, compared to only +0.0 to +0.2 for Video-RAG; the largest advantage is on SceneQA-Audio (+2.73 avg gain vs. +0.13 for Video-RAG). On Video-MME, Scene-RAG achieves an average gain of +10.47 over the no-RAG baseline versus +9.23 for Video-RAG, with the best individual result being +11.8 for Long-LLaVA. Among the 16 benchmarked MLLMs, Gemini 2.5 Pro dominates all tasks (80.3 avg), while open-source models show a dramatic 49.5% accuracy drop from ClipQA to SceneQA, confirming that long-range scene reasoning remains the primary bottleneck. Several open-source models (e.g., TimeChat at 18.8 avg, MovieChat at 28.5 avg) perform near or below random-guessing levels on I-VQA (25% baseline), underscoring the difficulty of bidirectional temporal reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by reframing long-video understanding around the cognitive unit of the 'scene' rather than uniform temporal clips, drawing on film-studies and event-segmentation literature to define scene-level problems where effective cues must span multiple minutes. It departs from existing long-video benchmarks (LVBench, Video-MME, MLVU, ScaleLong) that either evaluate different temporal scales on different videos or rely on short isolated clips, by constructing a within-video, scene-structured evaluation that disentangles temporal granularity from content variability. The Scene-RAG method extends Video-RAG [29] by replacing frame-level retrieval with scene-tiling (TV-L1 segmentation) and multimodal (visual + audio) memory construction, positioning retrieval augmentation as a practical mitigation for the identified forgetting phenomenon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a concrete and quantified failure mode—open-source MLLMs drop ~50% from clip-level to scene-level QA, and several perform near random on I-VQA—providing the field with a diagnostic tool to track progress on long-context retention. The modest gains from Scene-RAG (~1–2.5% average) suggest that retrieval alone is insufficient to solve the problem, pointing toward the need for architectural or training-level solutions for multi-minute narrative coherence. Open questions include whether scene-structured pretraining or longer native context windows can close the gap, and whether the audio modality (which provides only a small boost over visual-only SceneQA) will become more critical as models mature. The benchmark's scale (2,485 videos, 8,903 QA pairs, 6 task types) and multi-level design make it a likely standard evaluation for future long-video MLLMs.
