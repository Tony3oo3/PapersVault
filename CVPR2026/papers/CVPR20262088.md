## Problem

While DINOv3 demonstrates exceptional transferability across natural image and remote sensing tasks, direct fine-tuning reveals a critical failure mode: its global attention mechanism cannot preserve local boundary cues for low-contrast objects that visually blend with shadows or vegetation in complex RS backgrounds. This paper asks whether DINOv3 can be effectively adapted into a remote sensing foundation model and identifies the domain gap in local contrast sensitivity as the key obstacle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262088/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB optical remote sensing images and, depending on the downstream head, outputs scene class labels (30 or 45 categories), horizontal or oriented bounding boxes with class scores, or pixel-level semantic segmentation masks. Pre-training operates on 1M images from Million-AID, and all downstream evaluations use a frozen ViT-B backbone with only the adapter parameters fine-tuned, keeping the model lightweight relative to multi-modal rivals trained on 21M+ samples.

## Method

ORSATR-X inserts two trainable sub-blocks after each of the 12 frozen DINOv3-B Transformer layers: a Weber Local Adapter (WLA) and a Multi-scale Aggregation Module (MSAM). The WLA computes center-surround contrast via a learnable depthwise kernel with a fixed −1 center weight, splits responses into positive/negative polarities, and fuses them with directional gradient features from anti-symmetric horizontal/vertical kernels through a channel-spatial attention gate. The MSAM extracts multi-scale context via parallel depthwise convolutions (kernels 3, 5, 7), applies softmax-weighted scale attention, and refines features with spatial attention plus dual residual connections. The entire adapter stack is pre-trained by distilling a frozen DINOv3-L teacher into the adapter-enhanced DINOv3-B student using an L2 loss on per-token features, requiring no labeled RS data and only 1M unlabeled images.

## Results

On the primary object detection task, ORSATR-X achieves 80.20% mAP₅₀ on DIOR (horizontal) and 75.30% mAP₅₀ on DIOR-R (oriented), using a ViT-B backbone pre-trained on only 1M single-modality samples. On DIOR-R, this surpasses the multi-modal SkySense V2 (75.29%, trained on 21M samples) and the single-modality MTP (74.54%) by +0.76%. On scene classification, the method reaches 96.33% on RESISC-45 (TR=20%) and 97.07% on AID (TR=20%), and on semantic segmentation achieves 91.36% mF1 on Potsdam.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DIOR-R (oriented detection), ORSATR-X (75.30%) outperforms all single-modality baselines, beating MTP (74.54%) by +0.76%, BFM (73.62%) by +1.68%, and RVSA (71.05%) by +4.25%, while also marginally exceeding the multi-modal SkySense V2 (75.29%) trained on 21× more data. On DIOR (horizontal detection), the method reaches 80.20%, surpassing the DINOv3-B baseline by +0.50% and all single-modality methods, though it trails MTP (81.10%) by 0.90% and RingMoE (82.45%) by 2.25%. On scene classification, ORSATR-X achieves 96.33% on RESISC-45 and 97.07% on AID, outperforming all single-modality methods (best: RVSA at 95.69%/97.03%) and approaching SkySense V2 (97.24%/98.34%) with far less pre-training data. On semantic segmentation (Potsdam), the gains are more modest at 91.36% mF1 (+0.23% over baseline), remaining competitive with single-modality methods like RingMo (91.27%) and SatLas (91.28%) but trailing multi-modal SkySense V2 (95.86%) by a wider margin, which the authors attribute to the patch-level focus of their pre-training strategy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two research threads: (1) the growing trend of adapting natural-image foundation models (DINO, SAM, CLIP) to remote sensing via fine-tuning or adapter-based methods, and (2) the long line of RS-specific SSL pre-training (SatMAE, RingMo, Scale-MAE, SkySense) that trains from scratch on large curated RS datasets. It departs from both by freezing DINOv3-B entirely and inserting lightweight domain-specific adapters (WLA and MSAM) between transformer blocks, pre-trained via distillation from DINOv3-L rather than from scratch on RS data. The WLA module draws conceptually from difference convolutional networks and Weber's Law in visual perception, while the MSAM extends standard multi-scale depthwise convolution with learned scale attention—a combination not previously applied in the RSFM context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in data efficiency: a ViT-B backbone with ~1M pre-training samples and frozen weights matches or slightly exceeds multi-modal models trained on 21M samples (SkySense V2) on oriented detection, suggesting that targeted inductive biases can substitute for raw data scale in RS. However, the gains are modest (0.23–0.66% over baseline) and the segmentation improvement is particularly small, revealing that the natural-image prior in DINOv3 still constrains dense prediction. An open question is whether the adapter-based paradigm generalizes beyond optical imagery (SAR, hyperspectral) or whether the Weber-inspired local contrast mechanism is specific to the low-contrast boundary problem in optical RS. The authors themselves note that adaptive region selection mechanisms are needed to further embed RS knowledge into the frozen backbone.
