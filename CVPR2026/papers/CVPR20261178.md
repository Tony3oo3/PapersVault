## Problem

While prior interpretability work on diffusion transformers has localized static objects (nouns) spatially, how Video DiTs process and generate motion concepts (verbs like 'walking' or 'paddling') remains largely unexplored. Specifically, it is unclear which internal attention mechanisms drive temporal movement and where in the video the model focuses given a motion word, leaving a gap in understanding the black-box video generation process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261178/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a real video (chunked into 49-frame clips, noised per the diffusion schedule) and a text prompt containing a motion concept as input. The concept token is injected via the pre-trained concept-token stream of the double-stream MM-DiT blocks, requiring no gradient computation or parameter updates. Outputs include per-frame spatial saliency maps (GramCol) for any concept and a spatiotemporal saliency map (IMAP) for motion concepts, which can also be thresholded to produce zero-shot video segmentation masks without any clustering or classifier.

## Method

The core mechanism is GramCol: for each frame, Query-Key matching between visual tokens and the concept token selects a single 'text-surrogate' visual token, and the corresponding column of the Gram matrix of visual token embeddings yields a positive-highlighted spatial similarity map, avoiding cross-modal artifact issues inherent in ConceptAttention. For temporal localization, the paper identifies 'motion heads' by computing the Calinski-Harabasz index (CHI) of per-frame visual token clusters per attention head—heads with high inter-frame separation scores are retained (top-5). Layer selection uses the second-largest eigenvalue (λ₂) of the attention matrix under a DTMC interpretation to pick semantically informative layers. IMAP aggregates GramCol across selected timesteps, layers, and motion heads in a fully automatic, training-free, and gradient-free pipeline, producing interpretable spatiotemporal maps that reveal when and which object moves for a given motion concept.

## Results

On the motion localization benchmark (504 videos, 150 motion types), IMAP achieves the highest average score across all five LLM-judged metrics (SL, TL, PR, SS, OBJ) for every DiT backbone tested. On CogVideoX-5B, IMAP reaches an average of 0.62 (SL=0.68, TL=0.48, PR=0.69, SS=0.61, OBJ=0.64), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline ConceptAttention (0.45). On HunyuanVideo, IMAP scores 0.55 average versus 0.36 for ConceptAttention. In zero-shot video semantic segmentation on VSPW, GramCol attains mIoU 28.9, surpassing ConceptAttention (25.0) and Cross Attention (16.8) on CogVideoX-5B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IMAP consistently outperforms all eight baselines across every DiT backbone and all five localization metrics. Against the strongest same-backbone baseline, ConceptAttention, IMAP gains +0.17 average on CogVideoX-5B (0.62 vs. 0.45), +0.18 on HunyuanVideo (0.55 vs. 0.36), and +0.18 on CogVideoX-2B (0.54 vs. 0.36). The gap is most pronounced on Spatial Localization and Prompt Relevance, where IMAP on CogVideoX-5B reaches 0.68 and 0.69 respectively versus 0.50 and 0.51 for ConceptAttention. Non-diffusion baselines (ViCLIP at 0.28, DAAM at 0.32) trail significantly, confirming that diffusion-transformer internal features are far more informative for motion localization than external CLIP or U-Net features. In the zero-shot VSS task on VSPW, GramCol (mIoU 28.9) beats ConceptAttention (25.0) and Cross Attention (16.8) on CogVideoX-5B, though it still lags behind specialized segmentation models like DVIS++ (63.8) and VidSegDiff (60.6).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interpretability line of work on diffusion transformers (ConceptAttention, DAAM) from spatial-only saliency maps to spatiotemporal motion localization in Video DiTs. It builds directly on ConceptAttention's concept-token stream for injecting user-defined concepts and on TokenRank's DTMC/λ₂ framework for layer selection, while departing from both by replacing cross-modal embedding products with a Gram-matrix-based similarity map (GramCol) and by introducing a cluster-separation criterion (CHI) to identify motion-specific attention heads. Prior temporal analyses of Video DiTs (DiTFlow, DiffTrack) focus on inter-frame token dynamics for motion transfer or correspondence, but do not address how text-described motion concepts are spatially and temporally localized within the model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a clear gap: prior interpretability work on diffusion models targets static objects, leaving the question of how Video DiTs encode and localize motion from text largely unanswered. The training-free, gradient-free pipeline is immediately applicable to any existing Video DiT (joint or cross-attention), making it a practical diagnostic tool for model developers and a potential feature source for downstream perception tasks like zero-shot segmentation. Open questions include whether the CHI-based motion-head identification generalizes to compositional or multi-agent motion, whether the approach transfers to autoregressive video models, and whether the identified motion heads correspond to a stable, learnable sub-circuit that could be exploited for controllable generation.
