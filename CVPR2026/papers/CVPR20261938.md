## Problem

Unsupervised anomaly detection (UAD) and anomaly classification are typically executed sequentially, causing Local Visual Ambiguity (LVA) where different defect types exhibit highly similar local visual patterns, and preventing effective transfer of normal-pattern knowledge to the classifier. Directly applying multi-task learning to jointly train both tasks is hindered by feature preference incompatibility: UAD requires mid-level detail-preserving features while classification demands high-level semantic abstractions, leading to conflicting gradients and mutual interference in a shared feature space.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image resized to 448×448 as input and simultaneously produces a pixel-level anomaly score map (for localization) and an image-level anomaly class label (for fine-grained classification). Training uses only normal samples for the reconstruction-based UAD branch plus image-level category labels for the classification branch (no pixel-level or region-level annotations), with no data augmentation applied. The backbone is a frozen DINOv2 ViT-Small encoder whose mid-level and high-level feature maps are separately adapted via lightweight adapters for the two tasks.

## Method

PG-SFD performs a paradigm shift from implicit feature sharing to explicit feature disentanglement via three coupled modules. The Dual-Prototype Representation Module (DPRM) constructs normal prototypes by momentum-updating feature vectors at high-reconstruction-error locations, and class prototypes supervised by image-level labels through cosine-similarity classification, explicitly decoupling normal and abnormal semantics. The Differential Gated Interaction (DGI) module injects normal-prototype priors into the classification branch by computing a position-wise sigmoid-gated differential between the upsampled normal prototype and adapted high-level features, enabling adaptive fusion that emphasizes global semantics for abnormal samples while balancing for normal ones. The Geometry-Regularized Optimization (GRO) strategy adds a prototype separation loss (enforcing minimum cosine distance between normal and class prototypes) and a task-level consistency loss (aligning projected reconstruction features with classification features) to proactively structure the disentangled feature space and mitigate gradient conflicts. The overall objective combines reconstruction, classification, separation, and consistency losses with a three-stage curriculum that activates all terms jointly from epoch 60.

## Results

On MVTec-AD, PG-SFD achieves 99.4% I-AUROC and 95.6% P-AUPRO for anomaly detection, while simultaneously attaining 61.6% Cls-F1 for fine-grained anomaly classification in a single end-to-end framework. Cross-domain evaluation confirms strong generalizability: 99.9% I-AUROC and 71.1% Cls-F1 on the industrial IHSP dataset, 97.9% I-AUROC and 97.2% P-AUPRO on VisA, and 84.3% I-AUROC and 77.6% Cls-F1 on the medical Uni-Medical dataset. The ablation study shows that the full PG-SFD (V3) improves P-AUPRO by 75.1 points and Cls-F1 by 21.1 points over the baseline without any of the proposed modules.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest unsupervised detector INPformer (I-AUROC 99.3%, P-AUPRO 96.0%), PG-SFD marginally wins on I-AUROC (99.4%) and trades 0.4 points on P-AUPRO (95.6%), while uniquely providing fine-grained classification (61.6% Cls-F1) that INPformer cannot offer. Compared to Dinomaly (I-AUROC 99.1%, P-AUPRO 95.7%), PG-SFD gains 0.3 points on I-AUROC at a negligible 0.1-point P-AUPRO cost. For classification, PG-SFD outperforms the Two-Stage pipeline by 13.1 points in Cls-F1 (61.6% vs. 48.5%), demonstrating that end-to-end joint training preserves global context lost when cropping anomaly regions. Against DinoCLS (Cls-F1 60.9%), PG-SFD achieves a modest 0.7-point gain, confirming that the dual-prototype mechanism extracts slightly more discriminative features than a plain DINOv2 classifier. On the cross-domain IHSP dataset, PG-SFD leads all baselines with 99.9% I-AUROC and 71.1% Cls-F1, surpassing INPformer (99.0% I-AUROC, no classification) and the Two-Stage method (51.2% Cls-F1) by a wide margin on the classification task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage locate-then-classify paradigm (CutPaste, WinCLIP, clustering-based post-processing) and the implicit feature-sharing multi-task frameworks (DRAEM, AnomalyCLIP, FAPM) by introducing an explicit dual-prototype disentanglement mechanism. It builds on the DINOv2 self-supervised backbone and prototype-based representation learning (CFA, ProtoAD) but extends the single-normal-prototype idea to a dual (normal + anomaly) prototype formulation with a differential gated interaction for cross-task feature injection. The key conceptual departure is reframing the joint UAD-classification problem as a dual-prototype collaborative optimization task rather than a shared-encoder multi-task problem, explicitly targeting Local Visual Ambiguity (LVA) as the root cause of classification failures on locally similar defects.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in industrial and medical inspection pipelines where both pixel-level localization and fine-grained defect classification are needed, and demonstrates that explicit prototype-based disentanglement can mitigate the feature conflicts that plague naive multi-task training. Cross-domain validation across industrial (IHSP, MVTec-AD), general-object (VisA), and medical (Uni-Medical) datasets strengthens the generalizability claim. However, the classification performance remains modest (61.6% Cls-F1 on MVTec-AD, 71.1% on IHSP), and the framework still requires image-level labels for the classification branch, leaving the fully unsupervised or zero-shot joint perception setting open. The authors themselves note future work on extending the prototype decoupling to unsupervised and zero-shot anomaly perception.
