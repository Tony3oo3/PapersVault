## Problem

Transformer-based multi-view geometry models like VGGT, π³, and MapAnything rely on dense global attention whose quadratic cost dominates runtime as the number of input frames grows, limiting scalability to large image collections. The paper identifies that these attention maps are in fact highly sparse, with probability mass concentrated on a small subset of patch-patch interactions corresponding to cross-view geometric correspondences, yet no prior work has exploited this structure for training-free acceleration of geometry transformers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262830/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a drop-in replacement for the global attention layer in existing models, so it inherits their I/O: multiple RGB images (up to 200+ frames at resolutions such as 518×294, yielding over 512K patch tokens) are encoded into 14×14-pixel patch tokens via DINOv2 plus per-frame camera and register tokens, and the model outputs 6DoF camera poses, dense 3D point maps, depth maps, and point tracks. The only architectural change is that patch-patch global attention is computed sparsely while all interactions involving special tokens remain dense, preserving the original output format and task heads unchanged.

## Method

The proposed mechanism is a training-free, adaptive block-sparse attention that replaces dense global attention in the aggregator. Queries and keys are average-pooled to a block size b to produce a low-resolution similarity matrix, from which a binary block mask is derived by combining a CDF threshold τ (ensuring a minimum fraction of predicted probability mass is covered) with a top-k sparsity ratio ρ (enforcing a minimum number of active blocks). Only patch-patch interactions are sparsified; camera and register tokens retain full dense attention and cross-attention with patches, which the authors show is essential to avoid large accuracy drops. The approach leverages SpargeAttention's block-sparse kernel but omits its Hilbert-curve permutation, self-similarity filtering, and sparse online softmax, making it kernel-agnostic and simpler to maintain. Applied to VGGT, π³, and MapAnything, the method achieves over 3× end-to-end speedup at 75% effective sparsity while maintaining task performance comparable to the dense baselines.

## Results

The proposed block-sparse attention achieves more than 3× end-to-end inference speedup on large-scale scenes while maintaining task performance comparable to the original dense-attention models. On Tanks & Temples with 200 input frames, sparse π³ at 75% effective sparsity runs approximately 2× faster than the dense baseline on an H100 GPU, with speedups increasing further on longer sequences. Across pose estimation (AUC@30 on CO3Dv2, Real Estate 10K; ATE on TUM, ScanNet) and pointmap estimation (7Scenes, NRGBD, ETH3D, DTU), performance degrades only marginally as sparsity increases from 0% to 75%, remaining on par with other state-of-the-art methods even at the highest sparsity settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the dense-attention baselines (VGGT, π³, MapAnything), the sparse variants maintain comparable accuracy across all evaluated benchmarks at sparsity ratios up to 75%, with only minor degradation in pose AUC@30 and pointmap metrics; the paper explicitly notes it does not expect the sparse models to outperform the dense baselines. Compared to SpargeAttention [49], the proposed method (combining Top-K and CDF thresholding) is more robust at high sparsity ratios, where SpargeAttention's CDF-only plus intra-block similarity strategy shows a steeper performance drop (Fig. 10). A random-masking baseline confirms that the learned sparsity structure is essential: random masking causes rapid performance collapse as sparsity increases. The method also outperforms SeerAttention [15] in the supplementary ablation, which relies on a learned gating mechanism requiring optimization. Overall, the paper positions its contribution as a training-free, kernel-agnostic acceleration that matches dense-attention quality while enabling scalability to 512K+ token sequences at >75% sparsity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the block-sparse attention paradigm from LLM efficiency research (specifically SpargeAttention) to multi-view geometry transformers, but departs from it by omitting Hilbert-curve permutation, self-similarity filtering, and sparse online softmax in favor of a simpler, kernel-agnostic top-k + CDF thresholding scheme. The method is designed around VGGT's architecture (alternating frame-wise and global attention, special token handling) and extends naturally to π³ and MapAnything, which share the global cross-view attention bottleneck. Unlike learned sparse attention methods (SeerAttention, PixelatedButterfly) that require optimization, this approach is entirely training-free, requiring no additional annotations or backward passes through the original model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3×+ inference speedup at 75%+ sparsity while preserving task performance makes large-scale multi-view reconstruction (hundreds to thousands of frames) substantially more tractable on a single GPU, which is practically important for applications like autonomous driving and photogrammetry. The training-free nature means the technique can be dropped into any existing global-attention geometry model without retraining, lowering the adoption barrier considerably. Open questions include whether integrating sparsity into the training loop could further close the small accuracy gap at high sparsity ratios, whether the approach extends to higher-resolution inputs (where the quadratic bottleneck worsens by 16×), and whether combining attention sparsity with weight sparsity yields compounding gains.
