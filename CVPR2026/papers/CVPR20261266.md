## Problem

Reward-centric diffusion reinforcement learning (RDRL) is susceptible to reward hacking, where the reward score increases without corresponding gains in perceptual quality or text-prompt alignment. The paper identifies this vulnerability as arising from sharp regions in the reward model's landscape, drawing an analogy to adversarial attacks on classifiers, and shows that reward sharpness is negatively correlated with true human preference (Pearson r = -0.802 with PickScore). Prior work on robust classifier guidance and SAM has not been unified within the RDRL setting to address this failure mode.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a plug-and-play module on top of any existing RDRL framework (ReFL, DRaFT-K, AlignProp, DRTune), requiring only a pretrained diffusion backbone (SD1.5 at 512×512, SDXL/SD3 at 1024×1024, or Flux.1-dev) and a differentiable reward model (HPSv2). During training, each generated sample is perturbed in image space (radius ρ = 10⁻²) and the diffusion model weights are perturbed in parameter space (radius ρ_ω = 10⁻²) before the reward is evaluated, adding two extra forward passes per step without altering the reward model itself. The output is a fine-tuned diffusion model that, at inference time, takes a text prompt and produces an image with improved multi-reward alignment.

## Method

RSA-FT replaces the standard reward gradient with gradients from a locally flattened reward function, defined as the minimum reward within a ρ-neighborhood, approximated by a one-step descent along the reward gradient. This flattening is applied jointly in two spaces: (1) an input-space perturbation δ_x₀ that shifts the generated image toward the worst-case reward direction (analogous to adversarial training), and (2) a parameter-space perturbation ε_θ that shifts the diffusion model weights toward the worst-case reward direction (analogous to SAM's two-step update with stop-gradient on ε_θ). The final objective maximizes r(x₀(x_T, c; θ + ε_θ) + δ_x₀, c), enforcing smoothness in both the image and parameter manifolds simultaneously. The paper establishes the theoretical connection between these two perturbations via the known duality between adversarial training and SAM, showing that their combination yields complementary robustness gains that neither achieves alone, as confirmed by ablation studies.

## Results

On SD1.5 (512×512), RSA-FT consistently improves all four RDRL baselines across HPSv2.1, PickScore, and ImageReward on both DrawBench and HPD benchmarks. The largest gains appear for AlignProp, which jumps from 24.93 to 32.02 HPSv2.1 (+7.09) and from 0.032 to 0.528 ImageReward (+0.49) on HPD. Even strong baselines benefit: ReFL improves from 34.95 to 35.81 HPSv2.1 and DRTune from 0.842 to 1.452 ImageReward on HPD. The same pattern holds on SDXL (1024×1024) and SD3, confirming plug-and-play generalization across backbones and resolutions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RSA-FT is evaluated as a plug-and-play module on top of four RDRL frameworks: DRaFT-LV, AlignProp, ReFL, and DRTune. The method wins on every comparison across all three metrics (HPSv2.1, PickScore, ImageReward) on both DrawBench and HPD. Gains are largest for the weaker baselines: AlignProp improves by +7.09 HPSv2.1 and +0.49 ImageReward on HPD, and DRaFT-LV by +1.41 HPSv2.1 and +0.07 ImageReward. For the stronger baselines, improvements are smaller but still consistent: ReFL gains +0.69 HPSv2.1 and +0.136 ImageReward, while DRTune gains +0.64 HPSv2.1 and +0.61 ImageReward on HPD. Notably, the baselines without RSA-FT often show reward hacking (e.g., DRaFT-LV's PickScore drops below vanilla on DrawBench), whereas RSA-FT eliminates this degradation and produces simultaneous gains across all metrics, confirming that the method mitigates reward-specific overfitting rather than merely inflating the training reward.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate robustness principles—adversarial training (input-space perturbation, Madry et al.) and Sharpness-Aware Minimization (parameter-space perturbation, Foret et al.)—within the reward-centric diffusion RL (RDRL) framework, building directly on the recently formalized SAM–AT duality (Zhang et al., 2024). While prior work has shown that non-robust classifiers degrade diffusion sample quality under classifier guidance, and SAM has been independently explored in RL and diffusion contexts, no prior study applied the dual-perturbation principle to reward hacking in RDRL. The key departure from adversarial training of the reward model itself is that RSA-FT requires no retraining of the reward model; instead, it extracts gradients from a locally flattened version of the fixed reward, analogous to randomized smoothing but applied to reward landscapes rather than classifier outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reward hacking is a practical bottleneck for deploying RDRL in production text-to-image systems, and RSA-FT offers a simple, plug-and-play mitigation that is compatible with multiple RDRL frameworks (ReFL, DRaFT, AlignProp, DRTune) and backbones (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3, Flux) without architectural changes. The demonstrated negative correlation between reward sharpness and proxy human-preference scores provides a useful diagnostic for monitoring training. However, evaluation relies heavily on model-based proxy metrics (HPSv2, PickScore, ImageReward) with only a small-scale human study (17 annotators), leaving open whether the gains translate to genuine perceptual improvement at scale. Future directions include extending the framework to PPO/DPO-based diffusion fine-tuning, multi-reward settings, and selective sharpness-aware weighting.
