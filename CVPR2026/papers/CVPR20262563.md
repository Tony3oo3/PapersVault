## Problem

Existing FSCIL methods either rely on vision-only backbones that overfit from few samples, or use VLMs whose contrastive pretraining decorrelates fine-grained visual attributes, suppressing the geometric cues needed for few-shot generalization. Moreover, current prompt-based approaches are either static (rigid) or fully optimizable (prone to semantic drift), failing to balance the plasticity-stability trade-off across incremental sessions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262563/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input, encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone into 512-d visual embeddings, paired with textual embeddings from class-name templates. The key intermediate signal is the visual-textual residual (x^v − x^t), which is discretized and transformed into a continuous prompt vector appended to the text template before being fed through the frozen CLIP text encoder. The final output is a class label via cosine similarity between the adapted text feature and the visual feature, evaluated in a sequential session protocol (e.g., 100 base classes followed by ten 10-way 5-shot incremental sessions on CUB200).

## Method

QR-Prompt reconstructs suppressed manifold curvature through a discrete-to-continuous pipeline with three components. Discriminative Subspace Quantization (DSQ) partitions the residual space into M subspaces, learns an orthogonal rotation aligned with principal curvature directions, and applies Fisher-style discriminative regularization to preserve inter-class separability; the resulting codebook is frozen after the base session to prevent overfitting on few-shot incremental data. A Hierarchical Prompt Encoder (HPE) maps each discrete code to a continuous embedding via per-subspace lookup tables, then applies cross-subspace multi-head attention to model inter-attribute dependencies (e.g., combining 'red' and 'black face' into a coherent description). A Prompt Composer (PC) aggregates these refined embeddings into a single class-adaptive prompt using a learnable query modulated by the class mean residual, which is projected into CLIP text-encoder space. HPE and PC are jointly trained with InfoNCE loss during the base session and fine-tuned in each incremental session, while DSQ remains fixed, yielding a principled stability-plasticity split. The paper further derives PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds showing that freezing the codebook tightens the bound, and margin-preservation bounds linking codebook size (M, K) to classification margin loss.

## Results

On CUB200 (100 base + 10 novel sessions, 10-way 5-shot), QR-Prompt achieves 82.12% average top-1 accuracy with only 6.17% performance drop (PD), outperforming the strongest baseline BiMC (80.49% avg, 7.00% PD) by 1.63 points in accuracy and 0.83 points in stability. The method maintains session-wise accuracy above 80% through all 10 incremental sessions, whereas VQ-Prompt degrades from 84.5% to 59.24% (PD = 26.87%). On CIFAR100 and miniImageNet (60 base + 8 novel sessions, 5-way 5-shot), QR-Prompt also achieves the best average accuracy and lowest PD among all compared methods, with particularly notable gains in later sessions where baselines suffer accumulated drift.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QR-Prompt outperforms all eight baselines on CUB200 in both average accuracy and performance drop. The closest competitor, BiMC (training-free, 0 params), achieves 80.49% avg with 7.00% PD, trailing QR-Prompt by 1.63% in accuracy and 0.83% in stability. FDR (ICCV 2025, 0 params) reaches 79.01% avg but suffers a larger 10.82% PD, indicating greater forgetting in later sessions. Among prompt-based methods, DualPrompt (78.49% avg) and CODA-Prompt (74.60% avg) show progressive degradation, while VQ-Prompt collapses dramatically after session 2 (from 83.51% to 59.24%, PD = 26.87%), confirming the paper's concern about quantized prompts losing intra-task discrimination. QR-Prompt's advantage is most pronounced in sessions 5–10, where it maintains >80% accuracy while most baselines fall below 76%, demonstrating that the frozen DSQ codebook plus hierarchical prompt composition provides superior long-horizon stability. On CIFAR100 and miniImageNet, QR-Prompt similarly leads all baselines, with the gap widening in later sessions due to reduced image resolution limiting residual variance on CIFAR100.

## Relation to Prior Work

QR-Prompt extends the vector-quantization-for-prompting line initiated by VQ-Prompt [14] and the subspace partitioning machinery of Optimized Product Quantization [9], but departs from both in a key conceptual way: it quantizes the visual-textual residual rather than the visual embedding itself, motivated by the observation that CLIP's contrastive pretraining suppresses fine-grained attribute correlations that the residual retains. The Prompt Composer's learned-query mechanism draws on Arar et al. [1], while the overall stability-plasticity design (frozen codebook + fine-tuned encoder) contrasts with fully optimizable prompts (CODA-Prompt, L2P) and training-free calibration (BiMC, TEEN). The Fisher-style discriminative regularization on the rotation matrix distinguishes DSQ from standard OPQ, which optimizes subspace alignment purely for reconstruction fidelity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, parameter-efficient (454K trainable params) recipe for FSCIL with VLMs, backed by PAC-Bayes generalization and margin-preservation bounds that give practitioners concrete guidance on codebook sizing (M, K). A notable limitation acknowledged by the authors is the assumption of partial attribute overlap between base and novel classes; extending QR-Prompt to genuinely unseen attribute distributions remains open. The finding that subspace count M dominates codeword count K in determining performance is practically useful for hyperparameter selection but also suggests the method's expressiveness is bounded by the base-session geometry, which could limit transfer to highly dissimilar incremental domains.
