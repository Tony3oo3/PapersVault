## Problem

Existing video summarization methods—whether extractive (frame-level importance regression) or abstractive (MLLM-based captioning)—operate as one-shot pipelines that lack autonomous reasoning, self-correction, and iterative decision-making. They either rely on hand-crafted prompts defining importance criteria or produce scores that cannot be automatically refined, leaving missed keyframes unaddressed. This paper asks whether an agentic, self-reflecting workflow can close this gap by letting an MLLM iteratively predict, verify, and correct its own summarization decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260762/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a video as a sequence of N RGB frames (N×3×H×W), which are encoded by two parallel encoders: VideoMAEv2 processes sliding windows of 8 consecutive frames (ω=8) to produce inter-frame embeddings, while GoogLeNet extracts per-frame image embeddings. The output is a set of N frame-level importance scores refined through a multi-agent loop, from which keyframes are selected to form the final summary. Notably, the Verifier and Reflector stages are entirely training-free, operating purely through MLLM dialogue and CLIP similarity computation at inference time.

## Method

AgenticVS decomposes video summarization into three atomic agents forming a closed predict-verify-reflect loop. The Summarizer aligns video-level (VideoMAEv2) and image-level (GoogLeNet) embeddings via a V2I Alignment module—using temporal attention pooling to compress the temporal dimension and an AdaptMLP adapter to map into a shared feature space—then feeds the unified embeddings into a CSTA regressor for initial score prediction. The Verifier employs a multi-round memory prompt with Qwen2.5-VL-7B to re-evaluate low-scoring frames against learned scoring criteria, computing an adaptive threshold (mean − 0.5·std) to flag frames needing recalibration. The Reflector performs self-reflection by having the MLLM generate a holistic video text summary, then uses CLIP ViT-B/32 to compute cosine similarity between flagged frames and that caption, producing calibrated scores that replace inaccurate initial predictions. Only the V2I alignment module (MSE + residual regularization loss) and the CSTA regressor (squared-error loss) are trained; the Verifier and Reflector require no training, making the refinement loop fully training-free and adaptable across scenarios.

## Results

AgenticVS achieves Kendall's τ = 0.274 and Spearman's ρ = 0.308 on SumMe, and τ = 0.220 and ρ = 0.290 on TVSum, setting new state-of-the-art across all four ranking-based metrics. These results surpass the best purely visual method (CSTA, ρ = 0.274 on SumMe) by 3.4 points and the best visual-text method (LLMVS, ρ = 0.282 on SumMe) by 2.6 points. The method also substantially exceeds the human baseline (ρ = 0.213 on SumMe, ρ = 0.204 on TVSum), demonstrating that the agentic self-reflecting workflow captures video semantics more effectively than individual human annotators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgenticVS outperforms all 13 baselines on every metric across both datasets. Among purely visual methods, CSTA is the strongest competitor (SumMe ρ = 0.274, TVSum ρ = 0.255), but AgenticVS exceeds it by 3.4 and 3.5 points in ρ respectively. Among visual-text methods, LLMVS is the closest rival (SumMe ρ = 0.282, TVSum ρ = 0.275), with AgenticVS gaining 2.6 and 1.5 points in ρ. The gap over the human baseline is particularly large (SumMe ρ: 0.308 vs. 0.213; TVSum ρ: 0.290 vs. 0.204), indicating the agentic workflow's self-reflecting loop captures global video semantics that individual annotators miss. Older visual methods such as VASNet, DSNet, DMASum, and iPTNet trail by 10–20+ points in ρ, confirming that the combination of V2I-aligned embeddings with MLLM-based verification and CLIP-based reflection yields a substantial leap over prior extractive architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the agentic workflow paradigm (previously applied to video QA and spatial reasoning in VideoAgent, VADAR, AoTD) for the first time in video summarization, decomposing the task into a Summarizer–Verifier–Reflector loop. Architecturally, it builds on CSTA as the base regression model and introduces a V2I Alignment module that fuses VideoMAEv2 video-level embeddings with GoogLeNet image-level embeddings via temporal attention pooling and an AdaptMLP adapter. The Verifier and Reflector are training-free, leveraging Qwen2.5-VL for multi-round reasoning and CLIP for fine-grained frame-caption similarity scoring, respectively. Unlike prior MLLM-based summarizers (LLMVS, Kim et al.) that use the model in a single forward pass for captioning or embedding extraction, AgenticVS positions the MLLM as an active reasoner that iteratively critiques and corrects its own outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a closed-loop predict–verify–reflect workflow can recover keyframes missed by a single-pass regressor, and that a training-free CLIP-based correction step outperforms direct MLLM scoring for frame-level discrimination. However, the practical impact is tempered by modest absolute gains over the strongest baseline (LLMVS: +0.021 τ on SumMe, +0.009 τ on TVSum) and evaluation on only two small benchmarks (25 and 50 videos). Open questions include whether the agentic framing scales to long-form or multi-view video, whether the fixed three-stage pipeline can be replaced by truly autonomous agent planning, and whether the V2I alignment generalizes beyond the GoogLeNet/VideoMAEv2 pairing used here.
