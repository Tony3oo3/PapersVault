## Problem

Vision-language models degrade sharply on long documents because key evidence is sparse, scattered across pages and modalities, and buried in redundant context. Existing multi-agent and RAG-based approaches still feed lengthy, noisy retrieved content into the VLM, and no prior work explicitly targets the construction of concise, evidence-dense multimodal contexts from retrieval results to resolve this sparsity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261851/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language question and a document represented as a sequence of RGB page images (no OCR text is required); ColPali encodes the question into textual embeddings and each page into visual embeddings for page-level retrieval. The output is a free-text answer paired with a structured evidence reference table listing page index, evidence content, and evidence source for verifiability. Notably, the effective context length fed to the final reasoning model is kept fixed regardless of retrieval top-K, and the system gracefully handles modality absence (e.g., all page images filtered out) by adapting its prompt template.

## Method

SLEUTH is a training-free, plug-and-play multi-agent framework that builds a coarse-to-fine pipeline: (1) ColPali visual retrieval narrows N pages to top-K candidates; (2) in parallel, a Clue Discovery Agent extracts structured per-region evidence records (page, spatial coordinates, content, insight, chain-of-thought rationale) from each page, while a Page Screening Agent performs holistic semantic-visual reasoning to classify pages as Completely Relevant, Relevant, or Irrelevant and retains only the salient ones; (3) a Difficulty Assessment Agent assigns a binary complexity level and generates task-specific reasoning instructions (e.g., 'requires cross-page aggregation'); (4) a Core Decision Agent selects an Instruct-type or Thinking-type VLM backbone accordingly and produces the final answer with evidence references. The key architectural insight is that by operating page-wise and distilling evidence before the final reasoning step, the input context length remains bounded, so increasing retrieval top-K improves recall without amplifying hallucinations. The framework is backbone-agnostic, validated across Qwen3-VL-8B, GLM-4.1V-Thinking-9B, and Gemini-2.5-Flash.

## Results

On MMLongBench-Doc, SLEUTH achieves 52.77% average accuracy, surpassing the strongest RAG baseline MoLoRAG (48.75%) by +4.02 points and the direct-inference baseline (42.71%) by +10.06 points. On LongDocURL, SLEUTH reaches 59.96% average (vs. 57.57% for MoLoRAG), 43.09% on PaperTab (vs. 42.59%), and 70.46% on FetaTab (vs. 69.41%). The framework is model-agnostic: with GLM-4.1V-Thinking-9B it reaches 57.93% on MMLongBench-Doc and 62.02% on LongDocURL, and with Gemini-2.5-Flash it reaches 51.86% and 60.62% respectively, all outperforming their respective base configurations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLEUTH outperforms all five compared baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest RAG baseline MoLoRAG [39], SLEUTH gains +4.02 points on MMLongBench-Doc (52.77% vs. 48.75%), +2.39 on LongDocURL (59.96% vs. 57.57%), +0.50 on PaperTab (43.09% vs. 42.59%), and +1.05 on FetaTab (70.46% vs. 69.41%). Against M3DocRAG [6], the margins are larger: +6.87, +5.37, +3.98, and +6.38 points respectively. The multi-agent baseline MDocAgent [12] is consistently outperformed by +4.95 to +6.85 points on MMLongBench-Doc and LongDocURL. In comparisons with closed-source commercial models (Figure 3), SLEUTH using Qwen3-VL-8B surpasses both Gemini 2.5 Pro [7] and GPT-5 [24] on MMLongBench-Doc, demonstrating that evidence-dense context construction can compensate for smaller model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLEUTH sits at the intersection of three established threads in long-document understanding: multi-agent reasoning (MACT, MDocAgent), retrieval-augmented generation (M3DocRAG, MoLoRAG, SV-RAG), and context engineering (ACE, Doc-React, CoA). Rather than improving any single component, the paper reframes the problem around *context construction*: it repurposes a standard visual retriever (ColPali) and off-the-shelf VLMs to build a compact, evidence-dense multimodal context through parallel clue extraction and page screening, arguing that the bottleneck is not retrieval recall or reasoning depth but the quality and density of the context fed to the final model. The framework is explicitly training-free and plug-and-play, distinguishing it from RL-trained agent systems (VRAG-RL, MACT, ReMemR1) that require costly optimization loops.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that MLLM performance degrades with context length and that the solution is to engineer shorter, denser inputs rather than simply retrieve more or reason harder—offers a practical, model-agnostic recipe for long-document QA that requires no fine-tuning. Two limitations are acknowledged: heavy dependence on the initial retriever (missed pages cannot be recovered downstream) and the purely prompt-driven design, which precludes self-improvement through experience. Open questions include whether feedback-based retrieval, RL integration, and extension to multilingual or handwritten documents can close the remaining gap, and whether the visual-only-over-multimodal input finding generalizes beyond the tested benchmarks.
