## Problem

Mainstream vision encoders and auto-encoders process diagrams as pixel-level continuous embeddings, fundamentally misaligned with the structured, hierarchical nature of geometric diagrams where points define lines, lines form shapes, and shapes participate in relations. This pixel-based paradigm forces models to approximate symbolic understanding from weak supervision, and existing Process Reward Models for step-level supervision have not been adapted to enforce the compositional dependencies inherent in geometric parsing. Additionally, directly applying GRPO with visual reconstruction rewards to train such symbolic encoders causes training collapse due to near-zero reward variance on semantically sparse diagrams.

## Input / Output

The encoder takes a geometric diagram image (RGB) and produces a structured logic form: labeled points with normalized (x,y) coordinates, lines as vertex pairs, typed shapes (e.g., RightTriangle with perpendicular edges specified), shape indicators (parallel, equal sides), and geometric relations (IntersectAt, Incircle, Tangent). A deterministic matplotlib-based rendering engine converts the logic form back into a diagram image for self-supervised reconstruction. For downstream reasoning, the logic form is serialized as text and prepended to the question prompt for an LLM reasoner, bypassing the conventional visual-token-to-projector pipeline entirely.

## Method

The architecture is a three-stage symbolic auto-encoder built on Qwen2.5-VL: (1) SFT cold-start on 100K synthetic diagram–logic-form pairs, (2) SymHPR, which applies GRPO with six task-specific rewards (point F1, position L2, line F1, shape F1, indicator F1, relation F1) structured hierarchically so that child-component rewards are multiplicatively modulated by parent-component rewards (α=0.9 blending), enforcing point-on-line, line-on-shape, and shape-on-relation consistency, and (3) SymVAE, a self-supervised stage where visual rewards (weighted MSE + SSIM + DINOv2 cosine similarity between input and rendered reconstruction) drive further RL. To prevent GRPO collapse from sparse visual rewards, two stabilization strategies are introduced: progressive Gaussian noise annealing on half the rollouts (hard negative contrastive learning) and power normalization of group-normalized rewards (r^α with α annealed from 3.0 to 1.0), both decaying exponentially over training to transition from exploration to exploitation.

## Results

On geometric diagram reconstruction (Table 2), SymVAE-7B achieves MSE of 6.01×10⁻³ on synthetic diagrams and 16.8×10⁻³ on Geo170K, a 98.2% MSE reduction over the VAE baseline (12.9×10⁻³). On the MathGlance diagram understanding benchmark (Table 4), SymVAE+GeoPeP-7B reaches 72.6% average accuracy, a +13.4% absolute improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-7B (59.2%), with relation identification jumping from 52.0% to 100.0%. On ChartMimic (Table 3), SymVAE+chart-7B surpasses GPT-4o by +0.6% on low-level average (79.6% vs. 79.0%) and by +6.5% on layout accuracy. On reasoning benchmarks, SymVAE+CoTs-7B achieves 51.8% on MathVerse (+2.6% over Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and 79.4% on GeoQA (+3.0% over Qwen2.5-VL-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SymVAE-7B outperforms all open-source MLLM baselines across every evaluated task. On diagram reconstruction (Table 2), it reduces MSE by 49.4% over VQ-GAN (6.01 vs. 11.7×10⁻³) and by 99.1% over VAE (6.01 vs. 12.9×10⁻³) on synthetic diagrams, while also surpassing the code-based PyhParser-3B (6.01 vs. 8.98). On ChartMimic (Table 3), SymVAE+chart-7B (79.6% avg) beats GPT-4o (79.0%), VisCodex-8B (74.8%), and the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (33.2%) by a wide margin. On MathGlance understanding (Table 4), the +13.4% gain over Qwen2.5-VL-7B is the largest among all open-source comparisons, with particularly dramatic improvements in relation identification (100.0% vs. 52.0%) and solid geometry (67.6% vs. 63.1%). On MathVerse (Table 5), SymVAE+CoTs-7B (51.8%) surpasses Qwen2.5-VL-7B (49.2%), MultiMath-7B (26.9%), Math-LLaVA-13B (24.1%), and LLaVA-NeXT-110B (28.3%). On GeoQA (Table 6), it reaches 79.4%, exceeding Qwen2.5-VL-7B (76.4%), MultiMath-7B (74.1%), and Math-LLaVA-13B (60.7%). The only closed-source model it does not surpass is GPT-4o on solid geometry (67.6% vs. 72.1%), though it leads on plane geometry and graphs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the auto-encoder paradigm (VAE, MAE) from pixel-level latent spaces to symbolic logic-form spaces, where the bottleneck encodes explicit geometric primitives (points, lines, shapes, relations) rather than continuous feature vectors. It adapts the Process Reward Model framework from textual reasoning (Let's Verify Step by Step) to hierarchical geometric parsing, introducing dependency-aware reward blending that enforces point-on-line and line-on-shape consistency. The RL training leverages GRPO from DeepSeekMath but departs from both pixel-based vision encoders (CLIP, DINOv2) and code-based diagram parsers (VisCodex, ChartMimic) by treating diagrams as structured logic forms rendered by a deterministic engine, positioning itself as the first self-supervised symbolic vision encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that symbolic latent spaces can outperform both pixel-level auto-encoders and code-based parsers on diagram reconstruction and understanding, with the striking finding that logic forms are inherently compatible with LLM reasoning (projector-free GeoQA accuracy matches projector-based). However, the evaluation is concentrated on planar geometric diagrams, and the neuro-symbolic pipeline (logic forms → LLM) is acknowledged as less flexible than visual-token integration. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dense scientific schematics, whether hierarchical PRMs can be extended to 3D or dynamic diagrams, and whether the RL stabilization strategies (noise annealing, power normalization) transfer to other sparse-reward visual tasks.
