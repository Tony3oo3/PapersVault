## Problem

SVG files are organized for rendering efficiency (draw order, geometric grouping) rather than semantic clarity, so visually coherent parts like 'bunny ears' are fragmented across unrelated primitives. This structural mismatch causes VLMs to fail at SVG animation because they cannot identify which elements should move together, producing visually broken or uniform-motion results. No prior method recovers the animation-relevant semantic structure from raw SVG syntax.

## Input / Output

The method takes a raw SVG file (containing primitives such as <path>, <rect>, <circle>, etc.) and a free-text animation instruction. Each primitive is rendered at 512×512 resolution through multiple focused views for VLM analysis. The output is a semantically restructured SVG—primitives regrouped by recovered semantic labels with class attributes, flattened hierarchy, and original paint order preserved—along with CSS keyframe animations generated iteratively per semantic category to stay within token limits.

## Method

Vector Prism recovers semantic structure via a multi-view statistical inference pipeline. Each SVG primitive is rendered through M complementary views (highlighting, isolation, zoom-in, outlining, bounding boxes), and a lightweight VLM (GPT-5-nano) assigns a tentative semantic label under each view. Rather than majority voting, the method models label agreement across views with a Dawid-Skene model: the centered pairwise agreement matrix is shown to be rank-one in expectation, so a single eigendecomposition recovers the reliability p_i of each rendering method. A Bayes decision rule then performs weighted voting with log-odds weights w_i = log((k-1)p_i)/(1-p_i), downweighting unreliable views and yielding stable labels even when some views are near-chance. Finally, GPT-5 generates CSS animations per semantic group on the restructured SVG, with iterative generation and mutual-exclusivity constraints to avoid conflicting effects.

## Results

On a curated dataset of 114 SVG–instruction pairs, Vector Prism achieves the best scores across all three evaluation metrics: CLIP-T2V (21.55), GPT-T2V (76.14), and DOVER (4.97). The largest margin is on GPT-T2V (instruction faithfulness), where Vector Prism outperforms the strongest video-generation baseline Sora 2 by 7.06 points (76.14 vs. 69.08) and the vector-based GPT-5 baseline by 35.22 points (76.14 vs. 40.92). On perceptual quality (DOVER), Vector Prism (4.97) matches GPT-5 (4.92) and surpasses both video models (Wan 2.2: 3.72, Sora 2: 4.19). A user study with 760 pairwise comparisons from 19 participants confirms consistent human preference for Vector Prism over all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vector Prism outperforms all four baselines on every metric. Against the optimization-based AniClipart, the gains are dramatic on instruction faithfulness (GPT-T2V: 76.14 vs. 23.96), reflecting AniClipart's tendency to produce uniform, semantically incoherent motion. Against GPT-5 (augmented with the same planning pipeline), Vector Prism more than doubles the GPT-T2V score (76.14 vs. 40.92), demonstrating that the semantic restructuring step is the critical missing component. Against the two video-generation models, Vector Prism leads on GPT-T2V by 10.93 points over Wan 2.2 (76.14 vs. 65.21) and 7.06 points over Sora 2 (76.14 vs. 69.08), while also matching or exceeding their perceptual quality (DOVER 4.97 vs. 3.72 and 4.19). Notably, Vector Prism is the only method that simultaneously produces vector-based output and achieves top instruction-following scores, whereas the video models, despite strong motion quality, output rasterized .mp4 files unsuitable for web animation. The user study (760 pairwise comparisons) corroborates these quantitative results, with participants consistently preferring Vector Prism animations over all baselines including Sora 2.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant lines of SVG animation research: optimization-based methods (e.g., AniClipart) that use score distillation sampling on rasterized renderings, and LLM fine-tuning approaches that require millions of paired vector-graphics examples. Rather than optimizing parameters or scaling data, Vector Prism identifies an overlooked prerequisite—recovering semantic structure from the SVG's rendering-oriented hierarchy—and solves it via a Dawid-Skene statistical inference framework [9] that aggregates noisy multi-view VLM predictions into reliable part labels. The method is zero-shot with respect to the VLM (using GPT-5-nano for labeling), positioning it as a structural preprocessing step orthogonal to both diffusion-prior optimization and data-hungry fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the semantic-syntactic gap in SVGs, not VLM planning or coding ability, is the true bottleneck for reliable animation, and that a lightweight statistical inference layer can close it without any fine-tuning. The 54× compression advantage over video models and the ability to outperform Sora 2 on instruction faithfulness make this practically relevant for web environments where lightweight, resolution-independent assets are essential. Key limitations include the inability to subdivide atomic primitives (e.g., a single <path> that must 'shatter'), and the approach's generalizability to other symbolic domains (3D scenes, CAD) remains an open question the authors raise but do not explore.
