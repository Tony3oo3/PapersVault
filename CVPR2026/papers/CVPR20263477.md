## Problem

Existing conformal prediction methods for LLMs rely on a single global verifier-score threshold, providing only marginal coverage that can systematically under-cover hard prompts while over-covering easy ones, inflating prediction sets unnecessarily. No prior method offers a feature-conditional coverage guarantee for sampled LLM candidates together with a finite-sample generalization certificate on the deployed rule itself.

## Input / Output

Given a prompt X (text or image), the method samples M candidate completions from a frozen base LLM/VLM, scores each with an external verifier V(X,y) ∈ [0,1] (smaller is better), and maps the prompt to a low-dimensional feature vector Φ(X) (e.g., mean verifier loss or answer-distribution entropy). It outputs a subset of accepted candidates whose verifier scores fall below a prompt-specific threshold λ̂_α(X), which can be empty (abstention) when no candidate passes. The entire procedure is post-hoc: neither the generator nor the verifier is finetuned.

## Method

CFC learns a continuous, feature-conditional acceptance threshold via augmented quantile regression: for a candidate score s, it solves a pinball-loss objective over both N calibration pairs and the test point, producing a map g_X(s) = Φ(X)ᵀβ_s, and deploys the largest fixed point λ̂_α(X) = sup{s : s ≤ g_X(s)} as the acceptance cutoff. This fixed-point construction, inherited from Gibbs et al.'s function-class conditional conformal framework, yields a threshold that tightens on easy prompts and loosens on hard ones, guaranteeing conditional coverage over a chosen linear feature class under exchangeability. CFC-PAC adds ridge regularization to the quantile-regression objective and shrinks the nominal risk α by a stability slack ε_N(δ) = O(√(log(1/δ)/N)), converting the expectation-level guarantee into a high-probability certificate that the deployed rule's conditional miscoverage deviates from target by at most ε_N(δ). An efficiency analysis proves that, under mild monotonicity and convexity assumptions on the score CDF, the oracle conditional rule is strictly more sample-efficient than any marginal rule at the same coverage level, and the learned CFC threshold inherits this advantage asymptotically.

## Results

On synthetic data (α=0.10), CFC achieves the best group-stratified coverage (GSC) of 88.7% with the smallest average prediction set size (APSS) of 15.53, compared to ICP's GSC of 57.4% and APSS of 16.71. On GSM8K (α=0.05), CFC reduces APSS from 4.73 (ICP) to 2.35 while lifting GSC from 79.85% to 88.48%, and CFC-P-F further raises GSC to 88.79%. On Flickr8k (α=0.03, target 97%), CFC-P-F attains 97.27% ECR with 95.21% GSC, the strongest subgroup reliability among all methods, while CFC is the most size-efficient at 0.99 APSS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares CFC and CFC-PAC against three baselines: TopK (fixed prefix size), ICP (single global conformal threshold), and Learnt CP (feature-conditional threshold without the exact conformal correction). CFC dominates ICP on every dataset and metric: on synthetic data it improves GSC by 31 points (88.7% vs. 57.4%) while reducing APSS by 7% (15.53 vs. 16.71); on GSM8K it halves APSS (2.35 vs. 4.73) and lifts GSC by 8.6 points. Against Learnt CP, CFC gains 4.4 points of GSC on synthetic data (88.7% vs. 84.3%) with a smaller APSS, isolating the benefit of the exact conditional conformal correction beyond merely learning a difficulty-aware threshold. On TriviaQA at α=0.30, CFC-P-F matches the target coverage most closely (70.0% ECR) while CFC achieves the smallest APSS (0.88) and still raises GSC from 49.3% (ICP) to 62.8%. On the Flickr8k VLM setting, CFC-P-F is closest to the 97% target (97.27%) with the best GSC (95.21%), confirming that the post-hoc conformal layer transfers across modalities without finetuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the function-class conditional conformal framework of Gibbs et al. [7]—which learns feature-dependent thresholds via augmented quantile regression—to the specific problem of conformal factuality for sampled LLM candidates scored by a verifier. It departs from the dominant line of LLM conformal prediction work (Kumar et al. [11], Mohri & Hashimoto [12], Quach et al. [14], Su et al. [18]), which all rely on a single global nonconformity threshold and therefore inherit only marginal coverage. The paper's two main departures from [7] are (i) a PAC-style finite-sample certificate via ridge-regularized stability bounds, and (ii) an efficiency analysis proving that conditional thresholds are strictly more sample-efficient than marginal ones under mild monotonicity/concavity assumptions on score distributions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For safety-critical LLM deployments, the shift from marginal to conditional coverage directly addresses the failure mode where hard prompts are systematically under-covered while easy ones are over-covered—a practical concern that marginal guarantees hide. The post-hoc, no-finetuning design and the demonstrated transfer to a VLM (Qwen2-VL on Flickr8k) make the method broadly applicable. Open questions include: how the function-class conditional guarantee behaves when the feature map is misspecified or high-dimensional, whether the O(√(log(1/δ)/N)) PAC slack is tight in practice, and whether the efficiency advantage persists when the verifier score distribution is heavy-tailed or multimodal rather than satisfying the concavity assumptions.
