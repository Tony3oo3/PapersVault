## Problem

Point tracking models trained on synthetic data suffer significant performance degradation on real-world videos due to domain gaps in appearance, motion, and occlusion patterns. While self-training with pseudo-labels from pretrained trackers is a practical remedy, naive pseudo-labeling is brittle because no single teacher is uniformly reliable across frames and scenes, and fixed heuristics cannot reconcile heterogeneous error profiles. The paper addresses how to learn per-frame reliability estimation that adaptively selects the most trustworthy tracker prediction without requiring real-world annotations.

## Input / Output

The verifier takes an RGB video (downsampled to 256×256, or 384×512 for EgoPoints), a 2D query point at frame t₀, and M=6 candidate trajectories (each L×2) produced by off-the-shelf trackers, and outputs a per-frame reliability distribution over the M candidates (an M-dimensional softmax vector at each time step). The selected highest-scoring candidate at each frame forms the pseudo-label trajectory (2D coordinates plus visibility via majority vote) used to fine-tune the student tracker. At inference, the same per-frame scores serve as a plug-and-play ensemble selector without any retraining.

## Method

The verifier is a meta-model that does not predict trajectories but scores which of M candidate tracks is most reliable at each frame. It extracts local visual features via a frozen CoTracker3 CNN encoder, applies deformable attention at both the query and each candidate location to produce appearance descriptors relative to the initial query, and appends sinusoidal displacement and learned identity embeddings. A Candidate Transformer then performs restricted cross-attention (each frame's query attends only to its M candidates) followed by temporal self-attention across frames, and outputs per-frame reliability via temperature-scaled softmax over cosine similarities. Training uses only synthetic data (K-EPIC): ground-truth trajectories are randomly perturbed to create M candidates simulating drift, jitter, and occlusion, and the verifier is supervised with a visibility-masked cross-entropy loss against a soft target computed from negative distances to ground truth. During real-world fine-tuning, the verifier selects the argmax candidate per frame as the pseudo-label for Track-On2, with a scheduled curriculum that gradually increases the loss weight of real-world samples over synthetic ones.

## Results

Track-On-R achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four real-world benchmarks: EgoPoints (δ_avg^x = 67.3, OA = 90.2), RoboTAP (AJ = 70.9, δ_avg^x = 82.6, OA = 94.0), TAP-Vid Kinetics (AJ = 57.8, δ_avg^x = 71.0, OA = 90.5), and TAP-Vid DAVIS (AJ = 68.1, δ_avg^x = 80.3, OA = 92.5). Compared to its synthetic-pretrained baseline Track-On2, the verifier-guided fine-tuning yields gains of +5.6 δ_avg^x on EgoPoints, +2.8 AJ on RoboTAP, +2.5 AJ on Kinetics, and +1.1 AJ on DAVIS. The method also outperforms the strongest real-world fine-tuning competitor AllTracker by +5.3 δ_avg^x on EgoPoints and +4.4 AJ on DAVIS, while using only 4,864 unlabeled real-world videos for adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Track-On-R outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across all four benchmarks. Against the synthetic-pretrained baseline Track-On2, the verifier-guided fine-tuning yields the largest gain on EgoPoints (+5.6 δ_avg^x, 67.3 vs. 61.7), reflecting the benefit of real-world adaptation for long egocentric sequences with extended occlusions. Among real-world fine-tuning methods, AllTracker† is the closest competitor, but Track-On-R surpasses it by +5.3 δ_avg^x on EgoPoints, +2.1 AJ on RoboTAP, +1.0 AJ on Kinetics, and +4.4 AJ on DAVIS. BootsTAPNext-B, which relies on large-scale student-teacher distillation over millions of videos, is outperformed on Kinetics (57.8 vs. 57.3 AJ) and DAVIS (68.1 vs. 65.2 AJ) despite using far more training data. CoTracker3 (Window), the most directly comparable pseudo-labeling approach using random teacher selection, is beaten by +4.5 AJ on RoboTAP and +4.3 AJ on DAVIS, confirming that learned per-frame reliability selection substantially outperforms random teacher sampling. Notably, the real-world training set contains no robotic sequences, yet Track-On-R achieves the highest RoboTAP scores, demonstrating strong cross-domain transfer of the verifier-guided pseudo-labels.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-labeling paradigm for real-world point tracking established by BootsTAPIR and CoTracker3, which rely on random or fixed teacher selection. The key departure is introducing a learned verifier — a meta-model that scores per-frame reliability across multiple off-the-shelf trackers using deformable-attention-based local feature comparison and a contrastive objective trained on synthetic data. The base student model is Track-On2, and the frozen CoTracker3 CNN encoder provides the visual backbone for the verifier's feature extraction, positioning the work as a coordination layer atop existing architectures rather than a new tracker design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The verifier-guided approach achieves state-of-the-art results on four real-world benchmarks using only ~5K unlabeled sequences, compared to the millions of videos required by prior self-training methods like BootsTAPIR, making real-world adaptation substantially more data-efficient. The dual use of the verifier (training-time pseudo-label selection and inference-time ensemble) offers a practical, plug-and-play mechanism for combining heterogeneous trackers. However, the upper bound is constrained by teacher quality, and the method's effectiveness depends on having a diverse set of complementary trackers; developing stronger individual pretrained models remains an open prerequisite for further gains.
