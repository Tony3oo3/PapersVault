## Problem

Zeroth-order (ZO) methods offer a memory-efficient alternative to backpropagation for test-time adaptation on resource-constrained devices, but their gradient estimates suffer from high variance due to isotropic random perturbations, requiring O(d) more iterations than first-order methods. Existing BP-free TTA methods either ignore curvature structure entirely or rely on population-based search, leaving a gap in how to efficiently exploit the local loss-landscape geometry to reduce ZO estimation variance.

## Input / Output

CAZO consumes unlabeled test images from a shifted distribution, a frozen pretrained ViT-B/16 backbone, and precomputed feature statistics from clean source data. It outputs updated lightweight adapter parameters (a single bottleneck module at layer 3 with down-sampling ratio 384) that are then used for standard forward-pass classification. The adaptation objective combines unsupervised entropy minimization on test predictions with an MSE loss aligning feature statistics to the clean-data reference, and the entire procedure requires only forward passes—no backward computation or gradient storage.

## Method

CAZO's key insight is that the Hessian of the TTA loss exhibits a persistent low-rank structure (top 20 eigenvalues capture >96% of variance) whose principal subspace evolves slowly across adaptation steps (projection ratio ≈ 0.9). Exploiting this, CAZO maintains a sliding exponential moving average of element-wise squared ZO gradients to build a diagonal curvature proxy H̃_t, then samples perturbations from a preconditioned Gaussian N(0, H̃_t⁻¹) rather than the standard isotropic N(0, I). This anisotropic sampling attenuates perturbations along high-curvature directions and amplifies them along flat ones, yielding lower-variance gradient estimates with the same number of forward evaluations. Only the small adapter parameters are updated via SGD on the ZO-estimated gradient, while all pretrained weights remain frozen, reducing peak memory by ~70% compared to BP-based TTA. The paper provides a nonconvex convergence guarantee of O(1/√T) whose constants explicitly depend on the curvature condition number β_u/β_l, showing that well-conditioned curvature proxies yield tighter bounds than isotropic sampling.

## Results

On ImageNet-C (severity 5, model reset), CAZO achieves 69.0% average top-1 accuracy, outperforming the strongest BP-free baseline ZOA (67.5%) by +1.5% and the best BP-based method EATA (66.8%) by +2.2%. In the more challenging continual TTA setting (no model reset), CAZO reaches 65.3%, surpassing LCoTTA (62.3%) and ETA (61.7%). On ImageNet-R/V2/Sketch, CAZO attains 63.5% average accuracy, competitive with DeYO (63.8%) while using no backward passes. Memory usage is reduced to 1,695 MB versus 6,404 MB for TENT, a ~73% saving.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CAZO outperforms all 11 baselines on ImageNet-C (severity 5). Among BP-free methods, it beats ZOA by +1.5% and FOA by +3.2%, while older BP-free baselines (LAME, T3A) remain near NoAdapt levels (~55–57%). Against BP-based methods, CAZO surpasses EATA (+2.2%), DeYO (+4.3%), SAR (+6.3%), CoTTA (+7.1%), TENT (+9.2%), and RoTTA (+12.7%), all without any backward pass. In the continual TTA protocol (Table 2), CAZO leads LCoTTA by +3.0% and ETA by +3.6%. On ImageNet-R/V2/Sketch (Table 3), CAZO (63.5%) is marginally behind DeYO (63.8%) but ahead of all other methods including FOA (62.7%) and CoTTA (63.0%). Under 8-bit quantization, CAZO retains 67.8% accuracy versus 63.3% for FOA and 61.8% for vanilla ZO, confirming robustness in low-bit deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zeroth-order optimization framework (Duchi et al. 2015; Nesterov & Spokoiny 2017) by replacing the standard isotropic Gaussian perturbation in the RGE estimator with curvature-aware anisotropic sampling guided by a diagonal Hessian approximation. It builds directly on FOA (Niu et al. ICML 2024), which demonstrated that forward-pass-only adaptation is viable for TTA, and adopts its composite loss (entropy + feature-alignment MSE) and adapter-based parameterization. The key conceptual departure is the empirical observation that the Hessian over adapter parameters maintains a persistent low-rank, slow-varying structure throughout TTA, which motivates using an EMA of squared ZO gradients as a lightweight curvature proxy to shape the perturbation covariance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 70% memory reduction relative to BP-based TTA methods while simultaneously achieving the highest reported accuracy on ImageNet-C (severity-5) makes forward-only adaptation genuinely practical for edge-device deployment where gradient storage is infeasible. The persistent low-rank Hessian observation is a useful empirical regularity that could inform other ZO or second-order methods in low-dimensional adaptation settings. Open questions include whether the diagonal approximation suffices for higher-dimensional adapters or non-ViT backbones, whether the convergence constants improve meaningfully in practice beyond the standard O(1/√T) rate, and how the method scales when the adapter dimensionality grows (e.g., multi-layer adapters). The runtime cost of 40 forward passes per sample (k=20) remains a practical trade-off versus a single BP pass.
