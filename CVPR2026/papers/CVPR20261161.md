## Problem

Existing RL-based geolocation methods rely on AI-generated chain-of-thought data that may not reflect genuine human reasoning and can amplify VLLM biases, while their reward functions judge predictions by exact text equality—ignoring the non-unique mapping between natural-language descriptions and geographic locations (e.g., 'Hefei' vs. 'Hefei City'). Additionally, prior datasets lack fine-grained human-annotated reasoning and suffer from geographic sampling bias, limiting both interpretability and open-world generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261161/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single high-resolution street-view RGB image as input and produces a three-level hierarchical geographic location (country → city → precise address) accompanied by a structured chain-of-thought reasoning process that progressively narrows from broad geographic cues to a specific place. Predicted location strings are resolved to coordinates via OpenCage inverse geocoding for evaluation, and the reasoning is expected to be self-consistent—i.e., a reader following only the CoT (without seeing the final answer) should be able to derive the same conclusion.

## Method

GeoAgent is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B with LoRA (rank 64) and trained in two stages: a supervised fine-tuning cold start on 10k human-annotated CoT samples (GeoSeek-CoT, labeled by geography experts and geolocation game players), followed by one epoch of GRPO reinforcement learning on 20k globally sampled street-view images (GeoSeek-Loc). The key innovation is a composite reward: a geo-similarity reward combining spatial similarity (exponential decay of haversine distance between predicted and ground-truth coordinates) and hierarchical semantic similarity (cosine similarity of multilingual sentence-transformer embeddings at each address level, with thresholding and level-dependent weighting), plus a consistency reward computed by a separate Qwen3-32B agent that attempts to derive the answer from the CoT alone, penalizing overly short or inconsistent reasoning. This design encourages the model to converge toward correct answers both physically and semantically while maintaining a complete, human-like reasoning chain.

## Results

On IM2GPS3K, GeoAgent achieves 76.21% country-level accuracy and 89.90% continent-level accuracy, surpassing the previous best (G3 at 71.24% country) by nearly 5 points using only LoRA fine-tuning (1.91% parameters). On the street-view benchmark GeoSeek-Val, GeoAgent reaches 60.37% country accuracy and a GeoScore of 3314.1, outperforming GeoCLIP (56.13%, 3172.3) and GLOBBE (39.17%, 2412.5). Notably, GeoAgent-SFT trained on only 10K human-annotated CoT samples already exceeds SFT-only baselines trained on 20K–5M samples (GeoReasoner, GaGA, GRE-Suite-SFT), demonstrating the quality advantage of expert-annotated data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoAgent outperforms all geolocation-specific VLLM baselines on both benchmarks. On IM2GPS3K country accuracy, it leads GLOBBE (71.45%) by 4.76 points and PIGEOTTO (72.40%) by 3.81 points, while using only 30K training samples versus 4M for the retrieval-based methods. On GeoSeek-Val, the gap widens substantially: GeoAgent achieves 60.37% country accuracy versus 56.13% for GeoCLIP, 53.54% for GeoReasoner, and 39.17% for GLOBBE, with a GeoScore of 3314.1 versus 3172.3 (GeoCLIP) and 2412.5 (GLOBBE). Against general VLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Gemma3-27B, InternVL3-14B), GeoAgent improves country accuracy by 12–40 points on GeoSeek-Val, confirming that task-specific RL training with geographic rewards is essential. A particularly striking result is that GeoAgent-SFT (10K human-annotated samples) already surpasses GeoReasoner (133K), GaGA (5M), and GRE-Suite-SFT (20K) on SFT-only performance, validating the quality of expert-annotated CoT over AI-generated alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the recent line of RL-based geolocation methods (GRE-Suite, GLOBBE, GeoReasoner) that fine-tune VLLMs with chain-of-thought reasoning, but departs from them in two key ways. First, it replaces AI-generated CoT annotations with reasoning processes labeled by geographic experts and professional geolocation game players, arguing that AI-generated CoTs inherit and amplify base-model biases. Second, it replaces the binary text-matching reward used by prior GRPO-based geolocation work with a geo-similarity reward (combining spatial distance and multilingual semantic similarity) plus a consistency reward from a dedicated agent, motivated by the non-unique mapping between natural-language place descriptions and geographic coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geo-similarity reward addresses a genuine and previously underappreciated mismatch in how RL rewards are designed for geographic tasks, and the consistency agent offers a practical mechanism for enforcing CoT integrity without task-specific priors. The GeoSeek dataset, with human expert annotations and stratified sampling accounting for population and road mileage, provides a more balanced evaluation resource than prior geolocation benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond street-view imagery, whether the 32B consistency agent's computational overhead is prohibitive at scale, and whether the geo-similarity reward design transfers to other tasks with non-unique answer spaces (e.g., entity resolution, medical diagnosis).
