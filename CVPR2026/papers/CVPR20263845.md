## Problem

Standard LLM pretraining relies on text extracted from documents, a process that discards figures, layout, and structural cues while incurring OCR costs and information loss. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis suggests that at sufficient scale, representations converge across modalities, raising the question of whether an LLM pretrained directly on raw document images could match or exceed a text-pretrained counterpart. No prior work has demonstrated that visual pretraining on document corpora can serve as a viable, scalable route to stronger language intelligence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw PDF page images (rendered as RGB, bucketed to canonical resolutions and tiled into non-overlapping 256×256 crops) alongside standard text sequences from math corpora. A frozen VAE encoder (latent dim 16, stride 16) converts each crop to a latent patch; a foreground mask then drops blank regions, yielding a sparse sequence of latent tokens ordered in raster scan. The LLM outputs latent hypotheses for masked patches, which a MAR+VAE decoder reconstructs back to pixel-level page images, while simultaneously producing next-token predictions on the text stream.

## Method

MAPLE couples a Masked Autoregressive (MAR) encoder-decoder stack with a standard LLM backbone via a Harmon-style wrapper, so the LLM sees a unified sequence of text tokens and visual latents under a causal mask. Document pages are encoded into sparse foreground latent tokens with 1D raster + 2D normalized positional encodings, projected into the LLM hidden space; the LLM autoregressively predicts latent hypotheses for masked regions, which a MAR decoder (K=64 forward passes, min mask ratio 0.7) and frozen VAE decoder reconstruct to pixels. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 warms up the MAR encoder-decoder on VAE latents with the LLM frozen, then Stage 2 jointly optimizes the LLM, MAR stack, and projection layers under a multi-task loss combining text cross-entropy, latent diffusion, and pixel MSE with a scheduled image-to-text batch ratio (default 1:2). No image-text pairs or cross-modal supervision are required—the shared LLM parameters implicitly bridge the two domains, and CKA analyses confirm emergent layer-wise alignment between text and image representations after training.

## Results

MAPLE consistently outperforms both the base model and text-only pretraining (TP) across three LLM backbones (InternLM2-1.8B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, Llama3.1-8B) on four math reasoning benchmarks. In the Base Pass@1 (4-shot) setting, MAPLE wins 10/12 benchmark-backbone combinations, with headline gains such as 34.82% on MATH-500 (vs. 20.34% Base and 24.24% TP) for Llama3.1-8B, and 66.26% on GSM8K (vs. 61.88% Base) for Qwen2.5-1.5B. After SFT (0-shot), MAPLE+SFT wins 11/12, reaching 76.20% on MATH-500 and 88.30% on GSM8K for Llama3.1-8B. The paper reports relative accuracy gains of up to 40.2% over text-only pretraining on average across the four benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MAPLE against two baselines on each of three LLM backbones: (1) the original Base model with no additional pretraining, and (2) Text Pretraining (TP), which performs standard next-token CPT on OCR-extracted text from the same PDF corpus. Against the Base model, MAPLE achieves substantial gains on all benchmarks—for example, Llama3.1-8B improves from 20.34% to 34.82% on MATH-500 (+71% relative) and from 56.25% to 66.03% on GSM8K. Against Text Pretraining, MAPLE wins 10/12 in the Base setting and 11/12 in the SFT setting; the sole loss is Qwen2.5-1.5B on MMLU-Pro-Math (13.03% vs. 13.55% for TP), and one tie occurs on Llama3.1-8B MMLU-Pro-Math (23.76% for both). The most dramatic TP-vs-MAPLE gap appears on InternLM2-1.8B GSM8K, where TP actually degrades from 31.24% (Base) to 18.20%, while MAPLE improves to 36.23%, suggesting that text extraction from these PDFs introduces harmful noise for smaller models. After SFT, MAPLE+SFT dominates across nearly all benchmarks, with the largest margin on Olympiad (37.93% vs. 26.34% for TP+SFT on Llama3.1-8B, a +44% relative gain).

## Relation to Prior Work

MAPLE builds directly on the Masked Autoregressive (MAR) image generation framework [27] for its visual encoder-decoder and on the Harmon wrapper [62] for injecting visual latents into an LLM's token stream. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of OCR-based text extraction for document pretraining (which discards layout, figures, and structural cues) and from multimodal LLMs that treat vision as an auxiliary input for downstream VL tasks. Instead, it repurposes a text-only LLM as the autoregressive backbone for a label-free masked image modeling objective over raw document pages, motivated by the Platonic Representation Hypothesis that representations converge across modalities at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first empirical demonstration that pretraining an LLM on raw document images can match or exceed text-only pretraining on the same corpus for language reasoning, opening a new data axis (unparsed PDFs, scanned materials) to mitigate text-scaling bottlenecks. The emergent CKA alignment between text and image streams—without any paired supervision or cross-attention—suggests a principled mechanism for cross-modal knowledge transfer. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to math reasoning at 1.5B–8B scale (~40B tokens), leaving open whether the advantage persists at frontier scale, in non-math domains, or with different document types. The optimal text-to-image mixing ratio and its interaction with model size remain to be characterized.
