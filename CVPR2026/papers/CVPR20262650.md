## Problem

Video LLMs using multimodal RoPE (M-RoPE) exhibit temporal inconsistency: the inverse Fourier time kernel induced by RoPE's temporal frequency lines is intrinsically rippled at the frame scale, so small shifts in frame timing or sampling offsets can flip attention weights and suppress key frames in low-gain intervals. This fragility is a property of the spectral structure itself rather than a side effect of sparse sampling, and no prior work provides a training-free mechanism to stabilize temporal encoding without altering the positional structure or token budget.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a standard Video LLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) that consumes merged video frame tokens and a text prompt, producing either a free-form text response (general video understanding) or a discrete action class label (recognition benchmarks). PAS modifies only the query stream of attention heads on video tokens after the base positional encoding is applied, leaving spatial encoding, tokenization, and all model parameters untouched. The per-head phase offsets are expressed in bin units (one bin = one merged frame token), with a practical default of K=2 groups at offsets [0, 0.5].

## Method

Phase Aggregated Smoothing (PAS) partitions attention heads into K groups (typically K=2) and applies a small, opposed temporal phase offset δ_h to the query stream of each group via a phase operator Γ_δ that acts only on the temporal half-dimensions of Q. Because a time shift rotates only the phase of each frequency line, each head's recovered spectrum magnitude is preserved (Theorem 4), but standard multi-head aggregation then averages the shifted observations, acting as a controlled moving average that attenuates high-frequency ripples of the IFT temporal kernel (Theorem 3). This smoothing tightens a Lipschitz bound on how much the RoPE-rotated logit can change under small temporal perturbations (Theorem 2), stabilizing attention against sampling offsets. The entire mechanism is training-free, adds negligible compute (overhead ratio ≤ p_t·S_v/S² per layer), and layers cleanly on top of existing training-free pipelines like SlowFast-LLaVA and TS-LLaVA without changing inputs or token budgets.

## Results

Under matched token budgets on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, PAS (K=2, offsets [0, 0.5] bin units) improves over the backbone on all nine benchmarks, with the largest gains on phase-sensitive action recognition: 20BN-Jester accuracy rises from 16.0% to 18.3% (+2.3), Kinetics-700 from 44.9% to 48.2% (+3.3), and Something-Something V2 from 14.8% to 16.3% (+1.5). On general video-LLM suites, PAS lifts MVBench from 67.2 to 69.5, TempCompass from 71.5 to 73.3, and MMBench-Video from 1.71 to 1.78. Stacking PAS on top of SlowFast-LLaVA or TS-LLaVA yields the strongest overall numbers (e.g., 49.8% on Kinetics-700, 71.0 on MVBench) with negligible inference overhead (76.8 vs. 77.2 ×10³ tokens/s on A100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SlowFast-LLaVA, PAS wins on 7 of 9 benchmarks (notably 20BN-Jester 18.3 vs. 14.9, Kinetics-700 48.2 vs. 45.1, MVBench 69.5 vs. 69.2) but trails slightly on SSv2 (16.3 vs. 16.8) and TempCompass (73.3 vs. 73.5), where SlowFast's dual-rate pathway provides complementary temporal coverage. Against TS-LLaVA, PAS wins on 7 of 9 benchmarks (20BN-Jester 18.3 vs. 15.4, Kinetics-700 48.2 vs. 44.7, MVBench 69.5 vs. 67.8, TempCompass 73.3 vs. 71.4) but is outperformed on EgoSchema (63.9 vs. 65.8) and PerceptionTest (68.9 vs. 69.3), where TS-LLaVA's thumbnail stream aids long-horizon and multimodal reasoning. Stacking PAS on either baseline consistently improves over both the backbone and the individual baseline across nearly all metrics, confirming that PAS is orthogonal to and composable with existing training-free video-LLM strategies. On the sub-Nyquist Breakfast Actions benchmark, all methods show only modest changes, consistent with the theoretical prediction that aliasing limits the benefit of phase smoothing.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RoPE (RoFormer) and its multimodal extension M-RoPE (Qwen2-VL), which assign a finite set of temporal frequency lines to the video axis. It departs from prior training-free video-LLM strategies (SlowFast-LLaVA, TS-LLaVA) that address temporal fragility at the input-pipeline level (dual-rate pathways, thumbnail sampling) by instead intervening at the attention-head level: a Fourier analysis shows the IFT of the RoPE line spectrum is intrinsically rippled at the frame scale, and PAS smooths this kernel by distributing small opposed phase offsets across heads. The approach is explicitly orthogonal to and stackable with existing input-side methods, and its theoretical grounding (Lipschitz stability, multi-phase averaging, spectral invariance under time shifts) provides a principled lens absent from prior empirical treatments of frame-rate sensitivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PAS offers a zero-training, near-zero-overhead plug-in that stabilizes temporal attention in RoPE-based video LLMs, with a clean theoretical story linking kernel smoothness to phase-stable logits. The practical impact is real but bounded: gains are typically 1–3 points, the sub-Nyquist (aliased) regime shows diminished benefit consistent with theory, and the method is specific to RoPE/M-RoPE architectures. Open questions include whether the offset design transfers to non-RoPE positional encodings (e.g., relative-position or learned encodings), whether the theoretical guarantees tighten at larger model scales, and whether the modest per-head phase diversification interacts with other attention modifications (e.g., GQA, sliding-window attention) in ways not yet characterized.
