## Problem

Generic image super-resolution and off-the-shelf diffusion models transfer poorly to fluid SR: they are sampling-intensive, ignore physical constraints (producing spurious divergence and spectral mismatch), and treat the coarse-to-fine mapping as a one-shot regression rather than exploiting the iterative residual-correction structure natural to multigrid methods. The paper asks whether embedding a physics-aware, multiscale residual correction inside the reverse diffusion process can yield spectrally faithful, low-divergence fluid reconstructions with far fewer sampling steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261587/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a coarse scalar or vector fluid field (e.g., velocity component, temperature) at a given resolution and produces the corresponding high-resolution field, with fixed upscaling factors of 2× for Navier-Stokes and 4× for ERA5 and Ocean benchmarks. The coarse input may originate from any upstream pipeline (classical solvers, neural operators, or reanalysis products) and is degraded via RealESRGAN to form training pairs. At inference, only 2–5 DDIM reverse steps are needed, and the method optionally accepts a binary fluid mask to suppress coastline-induced artifacts.

## Method

ReMD (Residual-Multigrid Diffusion) reframes fluid SR as few-step residual diffusion: at each reverse step it computes a composite residual combining a data-consistency term (u_LR − R(u_t)) and lightweight equation-free physics cues (Laplacian/biharmonic smoothing, Perona-Malik anisotropic edge-preserving diffusion, and FFT-based radial spectrum alignment), then corrects this residual across scales via a time-gated multigrid V-cycle. The V-cycle uses fixed, parameter-free multiwavelet restriction/prolongation operators (separable Kronecker-product low-pass filters) for inter-level transfers and small learned depthwise 3×3 convolutions as within-level smoothers, with timestep-conditioned sigmoid gates that shift emphasis from coarse to fine levels over the reverse trajectory. A zero-initialized learned head provides a minor per-step refinement on top of the multigrid drift. Training uses standard ε-prediction diffusion loss with a cosine noise schedule, and inference employs deterministic DDIM (σ_t = 0) with 5–10 NFEs, keeping the trajectory close to the restriction/physics manifold for efficient convergence.

## Results

On the three fluid SR benchmarks, ReMD-5 achieves the lowest RMSE across all datasets: 2.09E-02 on NS (2×), 8.02E-02 on ERA5 (4×), and 1.33E-02 on Ocean (4×), while also attaining the highest PSNR (49.94, 58.19, 47.71) and SSIM (0.998, 0.999, 0.983). Notably, even ReMD-2 (two reverse steps) surpasses the 15-step ResShift diffusion baseline on every metric, and ReMD-5 runs ~1.4× faster at inference than ResShift-15 (5.84 s vs. 8.07 s per epoch on NS). On physics-proxy metrics, ReMD achieves the lowest vorticity error (VE = 2.34E-03), enstrophy error (EE = 3.56E-06), and energy discrepancy (GED = 4.24E-03), confirming reduced spurious divergence and better spectral fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReMD outperforms all eight baselines on RMSE, PSNR, and SSIM across all three datasets. Against the strongest image-SR baselines (EDSR, SwinIR), ReMD-5 reduces NS RMSE from 2.97E-02/3.63E-02 to 2.09E-02 and ERA5 RMSE from 9.06E-02/9.10E-02 to 8.02E-02, while also improving PSNR by ~3 dB on NS. Neural-operator baselines (FNO, MWT, HiNOTE, Galerkin) degrade substantially on ERA5 and Ocean (e.g., MWT RMSE reaches 6.13E-01 on ERA5), whereas ReMD maintains consistent accuracy, confirming that the multigrid residual correction transfers beyond synthetic flows. Against the closest diffusion baseline ResShift-15, ReMD-5 achieves lower RMSE on every dataset (e.g., 2.09E-02 vs. 2.21E-02 on NS; 8.02E-02 vs. 8.79E-02 on ERA5) while using only 5 reverse steps versus 15, and ReMD-2 already beats ResShift-15 on all metrics. SR3 performs poorly across the board (NS RMSE 3.34E-01, ERA5 RMSE 7.13), confirming that vanilla image diffusion is ill-suited to fluid SR. On physics-proxy metrics (Table 3), ReMD achieves the lowest VE (2.34E-03 vs. 3.26E-03 for ResShift), EE (3.56E-06 vs. 1.11E-05), and GED (4.24E-03 vs. 4.51E-03), indicating reduced spurious divergence and better energy conservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

ReMD builds directly on ResShift's residual-space diffusion formulation but departs in three key ways: the residual is recomputed at every reverse step (incorporating data-consistency and equation-free physics cues) rather than being a fixed HR–LR difference; the correction operator is a time-gated multigrid V-cycle with fixed multiwavelet restriction/prolongation (inspired by M2NO) rather than a learned denoiser; and the design is explicitly tailored to fluid fields (divergence, spectral slopes, masks). The multigrid V-cycle principle from classical numerical PDE solvers is repurposed as the per-step drift inside a DDIM sampler, a conceptual bridge between iterative linear algebra and generative modeling that prior diffusion-SR work had not exploited. The paper positions itself against both image-SR baselines (EDSR, SwinIR) and neural-operator baselines (FNO, MWT, HiNOTE), arguing that neither captures the multiscale, physics-consistent structure of turbulent and geophysical flows.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is a 3–7× reduction in reverse steps (2–5 vs. 15 for ResShift) while simultaneously improving RMSE and spectral fidelity across NS, ERA5, and Ocean benchmarks, making diffusion-based fluid SR viable for near-operational use. The equation-free physics residuals (divergence, Perona-Malik edge preservation, spectrum alignment) offer a lightweight path to physical plausibility without requiring the governing PDE at test time, which is important when the upstream coarse solution comes from heterogeneous pipelines. Key open questions remain: pixel-space operation limits scalability to very high resolutions (latent-domain extension is needed), mid-frequency errors are reduced less effectively than low or high bands, and all evaluation is single-frame—temporal rollout stability and integration into predictor-corrector forecasting loops are unaddressed.
