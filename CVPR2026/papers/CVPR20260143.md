## Problem

Conventional feed-forward depth estimators require massive training data (tens of millions of images) and still underperform on fine-grained detail and boundary precision, while diffusion-based methods leverage rich generative priors but struggle with synthetic-to-real domain transfer. The central question is whether a model can simultaneously preserve fine details, generalize strongly across domains, and match or surpass large-scale feed-forward models using only modest data and compute budgets.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (resized to 576×768 for indoor scenes or 352×1216 for outdoor driving) and produces a per-pixel depth map predicted in disparity space (d = 1/d′), normalized to [−1, 1] to match the VAE's input range. Both the input image and the target depth are encoded into a shared VAE latent space, and the U-Net denoiser operates entirely in this latent representation. Inference is fully deterministic and single-step (no iterative denoising), taking approximately 1.3 seconds at 1536² resolution on an A100 GPU.

## Method

Iris introduces a two-stage Priors-to-Geometry Deterministic (PGD) schedule on a Stable Diffusion V2 U-Net with shared weights. In the prior-alignment stage (high timestep t=1000, low-SNR regime), a frozen Depth Anything V2 teacher provides pseudo labels on real images, and Spectral-Gated Distillation (SGD) uses a three-parameter learnable Fourier low-pass gate to transfer only reliable low-frequency layout and scale priors while leaving high-frequency content unconstrained. In the geometry-refinement stage (low timestep t=500, high-SNR regime), the same network is trained on synthetic ground truth for metric calibration and high-frequency detail. Spectral-Gated Consistency (SGC) then enforces high-frequency agreement between the two stages via a complementary high-pass gate with a stop-gradient on stage-1, plus an over-activation penalty to prevent stage-1 from amplifying spurious high-frequency content. An auxiliary image-reconstruction loss at stage-2 preserves the text-to-image backbone's fine-detail modeling capacity against catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

Iris achieves the best overall zero-shot affine-invariant depth estimation, ranking first in both All Avg Ranking (3.1) and Group Avg Ranking (1.6) across 16 methods. On the five benchmarks, Iris records AbsRel/δ₁ of 7.2/94.5 (KITTI), 4.9/97.4 (NYUv2), 5.5/97.6 (ETH3D), 5.0/97.1 (ScanNet), and 24.3/74.3 (DIODE), using only 59K synthetic + 100K pseudo-labeled real images. Inference takes 1.3 s at 1536² resolution on an A100, faster than DAv2 (2.2 s) and orders of magnitude faster than multi-step Marigold (377.7 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest deterministic feed-forward baseline DAv2 (trained on 62.6M images), Iris wins on KITTI (AbsRel 7.2 vs 7.4), ETH3D (5.5 vs 13.1), and DIODE (24.3 vs 26.5), but trails on NYUv2 (4.9 vs 4.5) and ScanNet (5.0 vs 4.2); overall Iris ranks 3.1 vs DAv2's 5.4. Among diffusion-based methods, Iris outperforms Lotus-D on KITTI (7.2 vs 8.1), NYUv2 (4.9 vs 5.1), ETH3D (5.5 vs 6.1), and ScanNet (5.0 vs 5.5), though Lotus-D edges ahead on DIODE (22.8 vs 24.3). Iris also beats GenPercept on four of five datasets (e.g., ETH3D 5.5 vs 9.4) and Marigold on all five (e.g., KITTI 7.2 vs 9.9). Notably, Iris achieves these gains with only 59K synthetic + 100K pseudo-labeled real images, roughly 1/1000th the training data of DAv2, while running 1.7× faster at inference (1.3 s vs 2.2 s on A100 at 1536² resolution).

## Relation to Prior Work

Iris builds on the deterministic single-step diffusion paradigm established by Lotus and GenPercept, which collapse multi-step denoising into a feed-forward U-Net conditioned on a fixed timestep, and repurposes the Stable Diffusion V2 backbone for dense geometry prediction. The key departure is the two-stage Priors-to-Geometry Deterministic (PGD) schedule: prior diffusion-based perception methods train exclusively on synthetic ground truth, leaving a synthetic-to-real domain gap, while large-scale feed-forward methods like Depth Anything V2 rely on 62.6M real images. Iris introduces Spectral-Gated Distillation and Spectral-Gated Consistency—lightweight learnable Fourier-domain low-pass and high-pass gates with only three parameters each—to selectively transfer low-frequency real-world priors from a frozen teacher and enforce high-frequency consistency between stages, decoupling the two supervision signals that would otherwise cause gradient interference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Iris demonstrates that a diffusion-based depth estimator trained on only 59K synthetic images plus 100K pseudo-labeled real images can match or exceed models trained on 62.6M real images, offering a practical path to high-quality monocular depth estimation under resource constraints. The spectral gating mechanism is architecturally lightweight and conceptually generalizable to other dense prediction tasks (the authors note unification with surface-normal estimation), though the paper does not yet validate this multi-task claim empirically. Open questions include whether the fixed timestep choices (t=1000 for prior, t=500 for geometry) are optimal, whether the approach degrades gracefully without a strong teacher like DAv2, and whether the spectral gating strategy transfers to tasks where the frequency-reliability mismatch is less pronounced.
