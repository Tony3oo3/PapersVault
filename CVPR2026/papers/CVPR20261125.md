## Problem

Generalizable feed-forward novel view synthesis models are fundamentally limited by the scarcity of large-scale training data with diverse and accurate camera trajectories. Real-world captures are photorealistic but sparse and discrete, synthetic data scales but introduces a domain gap, and diffusion-based generators lack the accurate, consistent camera poses essential for NVS training. This paper tackles the gap of generating photorealistic, multi-view consistent free-views with precise poses directly from reconstructed real-world scene geometry.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse set of real-world image-pose pairs per scene (e.g., 4 input views in the feed-forward setting), first reconstructs a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation, then samples and renders a large pool of candidate free-views (>2000 per scene) from 10 predefined camera trajectory modes. The output is a filtered set of high-fidelity free-view images with accurate camera poses, used either as training augmentation for feed-forward models (expanding the dataset to 1.22× its original size) or as pseudo ground-truth targets to refine the per-scene 3DGS optimization.

## Method

FreeScale's core mechanism is a certainty-aware sampling strategy built on a voxel grid (R=128) where each voxel's certainty score accumulates opacity/volume contributions from contained 3D Gaussians, highlighting well-reconstructed regions. A view graph is then constructed over all original and candidate poses, with edges weighted by Weighted Intersection-over-Union (WIoU) of certainty-weighted visibility, enabling efficient redundancy filtering and geometrically meaningful reference selection. After rendering, free-views undergo BRISQUE-based quality assessment, pose rectification via interpolation toward high-quality anchor poses, and one-step DIFIX3D diffusion enhancement guided by view-graph second-order connections rather than spatial proximity. For feed-forward training, a curriculum learning strategy progressively shifts from high-WIoU (stable) to low-WIoU (diverse) view pairs; for per-scene optimization, the top-K lowest-WIoU free-views are added as auxiliary training targets with a quality-decayed L1+SSIM loss.

## Results

On the large camera motion setting (the headline result), FreeScale improves LVSM's PSNR from 18.75 to 21.45 dB on DL3DV (+2.7 dB), from 13.88 to 17.27 dB on MipNeRF360 (+3.4 dB), and from 13.89 to 14.67 dB on Tanks & Temples. On the small camera motion setting, the gain is 22.20→24.20 dB PSNR on DL3DV. For per-scene 3DGS optimization, FreeScale achieves 19.57 dB PSNR on DL3DV (vs. 19.18 for vanilla 3DGS) with only a 2-minute runtime increase (37.22 vs. 35.19 min), while substantially outperforming DIFIX3D+ (17.99 dB, 81.40 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LVSM [16] (the primary feed-forward baseline), FreeScale consistently wins across all three evaluation datasets and both motion settings, with the largest gain being +2.7 dB PSNR on DL3DV and +3.4 dB on MipNeRF360 under large camera motion. Against vanilla 3DGS [17] in the per-scene optimization setting, FreeScale achieves +0.39 dB PSNR on DL3DV (19.57 vs. 19.18) and +0.26 dB on Tanks & Temples (20.66 vs. 20.37), with a negligible 2-minute runtime overhead. Against DIFIX3D+ [43], FreeScale achieves substantially higher PSNR (19.57 vs. 17.99 on DL3DV, 20.66 vs. 18.59 on T&T) while running more than 2× faster (37.22 min vs. 81.40 min), since DIFIX3D+ requires multiple costly diffusion inference passes. Against Nerfbusters [42], FreeScale achieves +2.12 dB PSNR on DL3DV (19.57 vs. 17.45) and +0.68 dB on the Nerfbusters dataset (18.40 vs. 17.72), demonstrating that geometry-guided free-view generation outperforms diffusion-prior-based artifact removal for sparse-view reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeScale builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting as the underlying scene representation and borrows DIFIX3D's single-step diffusion for final image rectification, but departs from both in critical ways: rather than treating the reconstruction as a final product, it uses the imperfect geometry as a 'certainty proxy' to guide where to sample new views. Conceptually, it sits between synthetic data generation (Blender, Megasynth, LRM-Zero) and diffusion-based image synthesis, carving out a middle path that preserves photorealism and accurate poses while avoiding the domain gap of pure simulation. The certainty grid and WIoU-based view graph draw on uncertainty-quantification ideas from Bayes' rays and FisherRF but repurpose them for data selection rather than pruning or active view selection during optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeScale addresses a genuine and widely acknowledged bottleneck in 3D vision: the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, pose-accurate training data for generalizable NVS models. The 2.7 dB PSNR gain on large camera motion and consistent per-scene improvements across three datasets demonstrate that geometry-guided data augmentation is a practical, scalable lever. However, a residual synthetic-to-real gap persists (the paper acknowledges this), and the pipeline's quality is upper-bounded by the initial 3DGS reconstruction, limiting its utility for extremely sparse or poorly calibrated inputs. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to other scene representations (NeRF, mesh-based) and whether the view graph can be made more efficient for very large-scale scene collections.
