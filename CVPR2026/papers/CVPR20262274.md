## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods for gaze estimation focus on learning domain-invariant representations but overlook the pervasive label noise in gaze datasets, which arises from unstable attention, lighting variations, and occlusion during collection. This paper is the first to systematically investigate how source-domain label noise degrades cross-domain generalization in gaze regression and to propose a dedicated mitigation strategy, noting that standard noisy-label learning techniques designed for classification do not transfer to the continuous regression setting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes preprocessed RGB frontal-face crops (e.g., 84,902 images from Gaze360) and regresses a continuous 3D gaze direction vector. During training, the semantic manifold and affinity-based partitioning operate on mini-batches of 128 samples; at inference, only the ResNet backbone and regression head are used, so the manifold machinery adds no test-time overhead. The output is a 3D gaze angle evaluated by mean angular error in degrees.

## Method

SeeTN constructs a semantic manifold by projecting MLP-transformed features onto a unit hypersphere and expressing them in a basis of K=12 prototypes updated via EMA, then aligning the manifold's pairwise cosine-affinity matrix with the gaze-label affinity matrix via MAE. A cross-entropy indicator η, computed between the softmax-normalized label-affinity row and manifold-affinity row for each sample, partitions the batch into clean and noisy subsets (top t% by η). Clean samples are supervised by standard L1 gaze loss plus a rigid MAE affinity-alignment loss, while noisy samples receive a soft row-wise cosine-similarity loss that aligns their raw feature affinities with the manifold affinities, effectively transferring gaze-relevant structure from clean to noisy samples without relying on their corrupted labels.

## Results

The primary metric is mean angular error (°) in cross-domain gaze estimation, where lower is better. When trained on Gaze360, ResNet-50+SeeTN achieves 6.75° on D_G→D_M and 7.42° on D_G→D_D, outperforming all compared methods by at least 0.31°. When trained on ETH-XGaze, ResNet-50+SeeTN reaches 6.31° (D_E→D_M) and 6.84° (D_E→D_D), slightly behind AGG* (5.91°, 6.75°) and FSCI (5.79°, 6.96°). SeeTN also improves within-domain accuracy by 3–5% over the plain ResNet baseline, unlike many generalization methods that sacrifice source-domain performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

When the source domain is Gaze360 (the noisier dataset), SeeTN decisively outperforms all baselines: it beats FSCI by 0.49° on D_G→D_M and 0.42° on D_G→D_D, and surpasses AGG by 1.30° and 0.36° respectively. When the source domain is ETH-XGaze, SeeTN trails AGG* (5.91° vs 6.31°) and FSCI (5.79° vs 6.58°) on D_E→D_M, and AGG* (6.75° vs 6.84°) on D_E→D_D, but remains competitive with CGaG (6.47°) and GazeCF (6.50°). Against noisy-label-learning baselines, SeeTN dominates: DivideMix actually degrades below the plain ResNet baseline (e.g., 9.72° vs 8.07° on D_E→D_M), while SUGE achieves only marginal gains (7.04° vs 7.94° on D_G→D_M) and SeeTN reaches 6.57°. The authors attribute the ETH-XGaze gap to baseline instability under cross-domain settings and note that Gaze360's higher inherent noise amplifies SeeTN's advantage, confirming that the gains stem primarily from explicit noise handling rather than a generic regularization effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of cross-domain gaze estimation and noisy label learning, two areas that have developed largely independently. Prior domain generalization methods for gaze (PureGaze, AGG, GazeCF, CGaG, FSCI) focus on learning domain-invariant representations but treat source labels as clean, while prior noisy label learning methods (DivideMix, SUGE) target classification tasks or within-domain settings. SeeTN repurposes the prototype-based manifold construction (reminiscent of SwAV-style EMA-updated prototypes) to create a semantic space where continuous gaze labels and features can be jointly structured, then adapts clean/noisy sample separation from classification LNL to the regression setting via an affinity-discrepancy indicator rather than small-loss criteria.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to demonstrate that label noise in gaze datasets meaningfully degrades cross-domain generalization (up to ~6° additional error at 30% noise), establishing a previously overlooked failure mode for the field. The semantic manifold approach offers a principled way to handle continuous-label noise that could potentially transfer to other regression tasks beyond gaze. However, the partitioning strategy (top t% by η) is acknowledged as simplistic, and the paper leaves open whether more sophisticated LNL techniques (e.g., transition-matrix estimation adapted for regression) could be integrated with the manifold framework. The practical relevance is high given that all major gaze datasets contain non-trivial annotation noise.
