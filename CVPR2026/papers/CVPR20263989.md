## Problem

Multi-expert models for long-tailed visual recognition rely on mutual knowledge distillation to enable collaboration, but this indiscriminate transfer introduces two critical failures: bias propagation, where a single expert's erroneous prediction contaminates others, and error consolidation, where all experts collectively reinforce the same wrong prediction with high confidence, producing gradients that oppose the supervised signal. Existing methods implicitly assume all expert knowledge is beneficial and lack any mechanism to filter unreliable predictions or correct collective misjudgments.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263989/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet/Places/iNaturalist) and outputs a single class label per image. Internally, K experts (default 3) share a common CNN backbone (ResNet-32/50/152) with expert-specific final classification layers; the final prediction is the average of all expert logits. Each expert's classifier applies a per-expert logit adjustment (δ values like {0.9, 1.0, 1.1}) to balance head-tail bias during training.

## Method

TCL introduces two modules that replace blind mutual distillation with trust-calibrated cooperation. The Trustworthy Knowledge Orchestration (TKO) module gates each expert's distillation contribution by a binary correctness indicator (only correctly-predicting experts propagate knowledge) and applies square-root inverse class-accuracy weighting to amplify rare but correct tail-class knowledge. The Consensus Error Calibration (CEC) module detects non-target classes whose average logits across all experts exceed the true class logit, then constructs a dynamic calibration teacher that suppresses these consensus-negative classes to the mean logit value while setting the target class to a fixed negative value (κ=−30); each expert is trained to match this teacher via KL divergence, producing a calibration gradient aligned with ground-truth supervision. The total objective combines supervised classification loss, TKO loss, and a weighted CEC loss (β=0.02), trained end-to-end for 400 epochs with SGD and cosine decay.

## Results

On CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100), TCL achieves 58.7% Top-1 accuracy (Many: 75.5%, Medium: 59.3%, Few: 38.3%), surpassing the previous SOTA multi-expert method SHIKE (56.3%) by 2.4% and MDCS (55.4%) by 3.3%. On CIFAR-100-LT (IF=50), TCL reaches 62.5%, and on CIFAR-10-LT (IF=100/50) it achieves 86.8%/88.8%. On large-scale benchmarks, TCL attains 61.6% (ResNet-50) / 63.0% (ResNeXt-50) on ImageNet-LT, 44.2% on Places-LT, and 76.9% on iNaturalist 2018, setting new state-of-the-art on all five datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCL outperforms all 24 compared baselines across all five benchmarks. On CIFAR-100-LT (IF=100), TCL (58.7%) surpasses the strongest multi-expert baselines SHIKE (56.3%) and MDCS (55.4%) by 2.4% and 3.3% respectively, and leads the best single-model methods ProCo (54.2%) and BCL (53.1%) by 4.5% and 5.6%. On ImageNet-LT, TCL (61.6% with ResNet-50) beats SHIKE (59.7%) by 1.9% and MDCS (60.7%) by 0.9%; with ResNeXt-50 the gap widens to 3.4% over SHIKE. On iNaturalist 2018, TCL (76.9%) exceeds DO (75.8%) and MDCS (75.6%) by 1.1% and 1.3%, while on Places-LT it leads DO (42.8%) by 1.4%. The largest relative gains appear on the Few-shot split (38.3% vs. 36.0% for SHIKE, +2.3%), confirming that the trust-calibrated distillation and consensus error calibration specifically benefit tail-class recognition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper refines the multi-expert + mutual knowledge distillation paradigm established by SHIKE, NCL, and MDCS for long-tailed recognition. Rather than proposing a new architecture, it identifies two specific failure modes of unconditional mutual KD—bias propagation (a single expert's error contaminates others) and error consolidation (consensus errors are amplified by mutual reinforcement)—and addresses them through a trust-calibrated distillation mechanism (knowledge quality gate + tail-class compensation) and a consensus error calibration module. The backbone architecture is directly adopted from SHIKE, and the overall training paradigm (shared backbone, expert-specific heads, logit-adjusted classification) follows the established multi-expert recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The diagnostic framing of mutual KD as a potential 'error amplifier' in long-tailed settings is a useful conceptual contribution that could inform design choices in other ensemble + distillation pipelines. The trust-calibration principle (gate by correctness, compensate for class scarcity, calibrate consensus errors) is modular and could potentially transfer to other imbalanced or multi-expert settings beyond long-tailed recognition, as the authors note in their future work. However, the approach remains specific to the multi-expert KD formulation, and open questions include whether soft confidence-based gating would outperform the binary correctness gate, whether the method extends to transformer backbones or non-ensemble settings, and whether the gains persist under more extreme imbalance factors.
