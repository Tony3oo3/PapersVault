## Problem

Cross-view pose estimation must bridge the large viewpoint gap between ground and satellite images, but existing omnidirectional methods either rely on semantic similarity that ignores spatial correspondences or use geometry-based transformations (polar, projective) that resolve horizontal misalignment while leaving vertical misalignment—especially around buildings—unaddressed. This leaves a fundamental gap in accurately matching cross-view features without reliable camera calibration or depth information.

## Input / Output

The method takes a ground-view image (panoramic or limited horizontal FoV under cylindrical projection) and a geo-referenced satellite image patch (100 m × 100 m at 0.20 m/pixel on KITTI; 70 m × 70 m on VIGOR) and outputs a 3-DoF camera pose (x, y, yaw) with pitch and roll assumed negligible. Candidate poses are sampled on a uniform spatial grid over the satellite image (e.g., 20×20 for KITTI, 25×25 for VIGOR at test time), with 70–80 orientation candidates per position, and the final pose is refined by a regression head that predicts sub-grid residuals.

## Method

VIRD constructs view-invariant descriptors through a dual-axis transformation: a polar transformation aligns the satellite view horizontally with the ground view's azimuthal axis, and a context-enhanced positional attention (CEPA) module resolves vertical misalignment by projecting both views onto a learned shared virtual vertical coordinate via sinusoidal positional encodings, with an additional convolutional context-enhancement branch that adapts ground attention weights to local scene structure. A view-reconstruction loss (ℓ₁) trains separate decoders to reconstruct both the original and cross-view images from the aligned descriptors, explicitly encouraging the model to encode cross-view-consistent vertical structures. Coarse pose is estimated by cosine-similarity matching between the ground descriptor and satellite descriptors at all candidate poses (trained with InfoNCE), followed by a regression module that predicts sub-grid residuals from the descriptor difference. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a VGG16 or EfficientNet-B0 backbone pretrained on ImageNet.

## Results

On KITTI (cross-area, EfficientNet-B0), VIRD achieves a median position error of 5.41 m and median orientation error of 1.87°, reducing these by 50.7% and 76.5% over the best prior omnidirectional method (CCVPE: 10.98 m, 63.84°). On VIGOR (unaligned, cross-area, EfficientNet-B0), VIRD reaches a median position error of 1.55 m and median orientation error of 1.17°, a 18.0% and 46.8% improvement over CCVPE (1.89 m, 13.58°). VIRD also attains 98.41% position recall at 5 m (R@5m) on KITTI same-area, the highest among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIRD outperforms all seven baselines on both KITTI and VIGOR in the omnidirectional (no orientation prior) setting. On KITTI cross-area, VIRD (EfficientNet-B0) reduces median position error from 10.98 m (CCVPE) and 11.72 m (FG2) to 5.41 m, and median orientation error from 63.84° (CCVPE) and 90.42° (FG2) to 1.87°. The largest gap is against geometry-based methods (HighlyAccurate, DenseFlow) that rely on projective transformations or narrow search ranges, which degrade severely under random orientation. On VIGOR unaligned cross-area, VIRD (EfficientNet-B0) achieves 1.55 m median position error vs. 1.89 m (CCVPE) and 2.40 m (FG2†), and 1.17° median orientation error vs. 13.58° (CCVPE) and 2.20° (FG2†). FG2 [31] performs competitively in the aligned setting on VIGOR (1.08 m median position) but degrades sharply without orientation priors, confirming VIRD's advantage in realistic no-prior scenarios. SliceMatch [11] and MCC [34] are consistently outperformed across all metrics on both datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIRD extends the omnidirectional cross-view pose estimation line (SliceMatch, CCVPE, FG2) by explicitly modeling spatial correspondences rather than relying on semantic similarity or contrastive learning. For the horizontal axis it adopts the polar transformation from Shi et al. (2019, 2020), but departs from prior geometry-based methods (HighlyAccurate, Shi et al. 2023) that use camera-parameterized projective transformations to handle vertical misalignment, which are sensitive to calibration errors and produce artifacts around vertical structures. The key methodological departure is the context-enhanced positional attention (CEPA) module, which repurposes fixed positional attention (de Luca et al., 2024) as a learned, parameter-free vertical coordinate transformation, and a view-reconstruction loss that enforces cross-view invariance through image reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VIRD offers a practical, sensor-light alternative to projective-transform-based CVPE that avoids dependence on accurate camera intrinsics or LiDAR depth, making it more deployable in real-world autonomous driving where calibration drift and occlusion are common. The 50.7% and 76.5% median error reductions on KITTI (cross-area, no orientation prior) are substantial, though part of the gap reflects that some baselines (HighlyAccurate, FG2) were not originally designed for omnidirectional search. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 6-DoF estimation, whether the cylindrical-projection assumption limits applicability to non-panoramic sensors, and how performance scales to denser urban scenes with more vertical structure ambiguity.
