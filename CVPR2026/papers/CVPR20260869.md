## Problem

CASSI systems tightly couple physical encoding with computational decoding, so even subpixel mask misalignments (from thermal drift or mechanical vibration) severely degrade reconstruction quality. Existing approaches either assume ideal imaging conditions or rely on offline calibration with reference targets, making them impractical for dynamic perturbations. No self-supervised method existed that could estimate geometric mask misalignments without reference targets or device-specific training data while generalizing across different CASSI devices.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 2D compressed CASSI measurement (H×(W+D(C-1)) grayscale image) and the ideal sensing matrix Φ derived from the nominal mask pattern; no ground-truth HSI or reference target is required. It outputs a 6-parameter affine transformation (a11, a12, a21, a22, tx, ty) describing the mask misalignment, plus the reconstructed HSI (H×W×C). The estimated affine parameters can be directly plugged into any existing reconstruction algorithm as a drop-in correction, making the method a standalone degradation estimation module.

## Method

SGDE models mask misalignment as a 2D affine transformation that warps the mask and hence the sensing matrix, then formulates joint estimation of the affine parameters and HSI as a MAP problem solved via the Deep Image Prior paradigm: an LCTC network generates the HSI while a lightweight MLP generates the affine parameters, both optimized solely from the measurement with no external training data. Because CASSI's high sensitivity limits single-kernel convergence to a narrow parameter range, a multi-kernel strategy initializes K networks at different offsets in the affine parameter space, runs short-term optimization on each, selects the best kernel by Spectral-Spatial Total Variation (SSTV) of the reconstructed HSI, and then performs single-kernel refinement. SSTV cannot serve as a direct loss because no differentiable path exists from the reconstructed HSI back to the affine generator, motivating this two-stage selection-then-refine design. The entire pipeline is self-supervised and device-agnostic, requiring no simulation data augmentation or reference targets.

## Results

Under ideal conditions (no perturbation), all methods achieve comparable PSNR (~38–39 dB), with SK-LCTC at 38.64 dB and MK-LCTC at 38.39 dB. Under a 1-pixel mask translation, MK-LCTC maintains 38.15 dB PSNR / 0.962 SSIM, while the closest supervised baseline (GST) drops to 33.21 dB and the DIP baseline (LCTC) collapses to 18.37 dB. Across all tested perturbations (up to 1-pixel shift, 0.4° rotation, 0.004 scaling, and combinations), MK-LCTC consistently sustains PSNR > 35 dB, whereas all other methods degrade by 5–20 dB. The multi-kernel variant adds ~116 s over single-kernel (531 s vs. 415 s) but can be parallelized across GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the DIP baseline LCTC [12], MK-LCTC improves PSNR by ~20 dB under 1-pixel translation (38.15 vs. 18.37) and by ~14 dB under 0.4° rotation (35.41 vs. 21.15), confirming that self-supervised degradation estimation is essential for robustness. Compared to supervised deep-unfolding methods (RDLUF [15], DERNN [16], MIDET [27]), which were explicitly designed to model degradation, MK-LCTC still outperforms by 12–15 dB under 1-pixel shift (38.15 vs. 22.56–23.27), indicating that their learned degradation models fail to generalize to geometric mask misalignment. The end-to-end supervised GST [42] is the most stable baseline under perturbations (maintaining ~30–33 dB), but MK-LCTC exceeds it by 5–8 dB across all perturbation conditions while requiring no training data. The model-based GAP-TV [50] and the dual-DIP DDIP [28] are the weakest under perturbations, dropping below 20 dB for most non-ideal conditions. On real-world TSA data, transferring MK-LCTC's estimated affine parameters into DERNN [16] visibly suppresses reconstruction artifacts, demonstrating plug-and-play compatibility with existing reconstruction pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Deep Image Prior (DIP) paradigm for CASSI reconstruction (LCTC, DDIP) by coupling it with a self-supervised affine parameter estimation branch, drawing on the multi-kernel initialization strategy from blind image deblurring (Lee et al., 2024). It departs from prior supervised degradation-estimation methods (GST, RDLUF, DERNN, D2PL-Net) that require device-specific training data or simulated degradation augmentation, and from offline calibration approaches (Arguello, Song, Paillet) that depend on reference targets and specialized instruments. The key conceptual shift is modeling mask misalignment as a physically interpretable affine transformation rather than a learned degradation matrix, making the estimated parameters directly transferable to any reconstruction algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of SGDE—where estimated affine parameters can be injected into any existing CASSI reconstruction pipeline without retraining—addresses a practical gap for deployed systems that experience thermal drift or mechanical vibration. The self-supervised design eliminates the need for device-specific calibration data, enabling generalization across different CASSI hardware. Limitations include the affine model's inability to capture higher-order optical distortions, the computational overhead of multi-kernel search (531s vs. 412s for single-kernel), and the fundamental constraint that the estimation range is bounded by the mask's minimum encoding unit. Open questions include whether more expressive degradation models (e.g., local warps) could be estimated self-supervised, and whether the optimization can be accelerated for real-time applications.
