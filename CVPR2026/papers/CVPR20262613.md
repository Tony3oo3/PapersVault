## Problem

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) exhibit a fundamental structural inconsistency: the understanding branch favors compact, task-oriented embeddings while the generation branch demands reconstruction-rich representations, producing misaligned decision boundaries and degraded cross-modal coherence. Existing post-training methods (reconstruction-based alignment, reward-based optimization) only polish model behavior on a fixed training distribution without explicitly constraining the two coupling branches, leaving the inconsistency largely unresolved. The paper asks whether a UMM can expose and correct its own inconsistencies from within, using its generation pathway as an active adversary against its understanding pathway.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (encoded via a frozen SigLIP encoder into visual tokens in R^{N×H}) paired with text queries or prompts, and produces either a text answer (understanding) or a generated image at 384×384 resolution (generation). Internally, the perturber operates on the post-LM fused visual-token states, and the hard-sample buffer stores decoded adversarial images that must pass a CLIP semantic-consistency threshold before being replayed. Only LoRA adapters on the LLM backbone and the 2.1M-parameter perturber are trainable; the vision encoder and generation decoder remain frozen.

## Method

UniGame formulates UMM post-training as a minimax self-play game between the understanding and generation branches, instantiated with a lightweight 3-layer MLP perturber (2.1M parameters) at the shared visual-token interface that produces bounded perturbations (‖δ‖ ≤ ε_max). The key mechanism is decoder-constrained adversarial training: perturbed tokens are rendered through the model's own image decoder into candidate images, filtered by CLIP text-image semantic similarity, and stored in a hard-example buffer for replay. The perturber is optimized to maximize the understanding cross-entropy loss on these decoded adversarial images (generation challenges understanding), while the understanding branch is trained to correctly answer on both clean inputs and the mined hard cases (understanding challenges generation). This differs from standard adversarial training in that perturbations must remain on-manifold and semantically valid by passing through the native decoder, and differs from GANs by operating entirely within a single UMM without an external discriminator. The framework adds <1% parameters, is architecture-agnostic, and is complementary to existing post-training methods like RecA.

## Results

On understanding benchmarks, UniGame achieves 75.0% overall (VQAv2 83.4, MMMU 43.8, MMBench 83.2, POPE 89.6), a +3.6% improvement over the Janus-Pro-7B baseline (71.4) and +3.1% over SFT. On consistency, UniGame scores 68.32 on UnifiedBench (+4.66 over baseline) and 0.43 on WISE, outperforming all compared UMMs including BAGEL (66.49) and Janus-Pro+SFT (64.72). For generation, UniGame reaches 0.82 on GenEval, surpassing the baseline (0.80) and SFT (0.81) but trailing UAE and RecA (0.86). Robustness gains are +4.8% on NaturalBench (OOD) and +6.2% on AdVQA (adversarial).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniGame (75.0 overall) outperforms the Janus-Pro-7B baseline (71.4) and SFT (71.9) by 3.6% and 3.1% respectively, and also surpasses larger models like TokenFlow-XL (71.3, 14B) and Emu3 (62.6, 8B) on the overall understanding score. On individual benchmarks, UniGame leads on VQAv2 (83.4 vs. 83.1 for BLIP-3o) and POPE (89.6 vs. 87.8 for TokenFlow-XL), but trails UniWorld-V1 (58.6) and BAGEL (55.3) on MMMU, suggesting the self-play approach is most effective for perceptual rather than expert-level reasoning. On generation (GenEval), UniGame (0.82) beats Janus-Pro (0.80), SFT (0.81), and OminiGen2 (0.70) but falls short of UAE and RecA (both 0.86), which the authors attribute to their explicit generation-focused post-training. On consistency (UnifiedBench), UniGame (68.32) achieves the highest score among all 7B+ models, exceeding BAGEL (66.49), Janus-Pro+SFT (64.72), and OmniGen2 (61.99). The method also demonstrates extensibility: when plugged into a RecA-trained Harmon-1.5B model, it adds +0.5 on MMMU and +1.27 on UnifiedBench with only ~10 GPU-hours of additional training.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniGame extends adversarial training (Madry et al., 2017) from single-task robustness to the dual-branch setting of Unified Multimodal Models, where the generation pathway itself becomes the adversary rather than an external perturbation oracle. It departs from reconstruction-based post-training (RecA) and reward/RL-based methods (T2I-R1), which optimize handcrafted surrogate objectives on fixed data distributions, by formulating a minimax self-play game that actively expands the shared generative manifold toward decision-critical regions. The decoder-constrained perturbation mechanism distinguishes UniGame from prior embedding-space adversarial approaches (e.g., VILLA) that produce off-manifold artifacts, by forcing perturbations through the model's native image decoder to remain semantically valid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniGame offers a lightweight (<1% extra parameters), architecture-agnostic post-training module that can be plugged into existing UMM pipelines (demonstrated with RecA) to simultaneously improve consistency, understanding, generation, and robustness—addressing a structural weakness that prior alignment methods leave largely unresolved. The finding that decoder constraints are critical (ablation: +2.0% vs. +0.7% for embedding-only) provides a principled design guideline for future adversarial augmentation in generative models. Open questions include whether the self-play dynamics remain stable at larger scales (evaluation is limited to 7B and 1.5B models), whether the approach transfers to video or 3D modalities, and whether the hard-example buffer can be replaced by online generation to reduce memory overhead.
