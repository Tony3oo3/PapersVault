## Problem

Existing 'thinking with images' methods for MLLM visual reasoning rely on a fixed set of external tools (grounding, cropping, code execution), limiting flexibility and generalization. Prior attempts at latent visual reasoning face two core challenges: high computational cost from aligning thousands of image tokens, and insufficient optimization of latent embeddings because standard NTP loss overfits text tokens and GRPO cannot compute policy gradients for continuous embeddings. This paper addresses both gaps to enable flexible, human-like abstract visual thinking within an internal perceptual space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262924/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text question paired with an RGB image and produces a text-latent interleaved reasoning chain: it autonomously emits a special '<latent>' token, generates K continuous latent embeddings (fed back from the last decoder layer as the next input embedding), then emits '</latent>' and resumes text generation to produce the final answer. The latent size K is fixed at training (8 for SFT, 10 for RL) and selected from {8, 10, 12, 16} at test time, enabling a form of test-time scaling over the number of intermediate visual thoughts.

## Method

Monet trains Qwen2.5-VL-7B through a three-stage SFT pipeline followed by a novel RL algorithm. Stage 1 warms up the model on image-text interleaved CoTs; Stage 2 uses a teacher-student setup where the student generates latent embeddings under a controlled attention mask (auxiliary image → latent → observation flow) and is supervised by cosine-similarity alignment on key observation-token hidden representations, with gradients restricted to flow only through latent embeddings; Stage 3 distills the Stage-2 target latent embeddings into the model without auxiliary-image access, aligning all-layer representations. For RL, the authors propose VLPO, which models each rolled-out latent embedding as a sample from a Gaussian centered at the current policy's latent output, yielding a closed-form probability ratio that plugs directly into the GRPO clipped-objective, thereby enabling outcome-reward-driven policy gradient updates on the continuous latent embeddings themselves—something GRPO fundamentally cannot do.

## Results

Monet-7B (SFT+VLPO) achieves 83.25 on V*, 71.00 on HRBench4K, 68.00 on HRBench8K, 55.50 on MME-RealWorld-Lite, and 35.02 on VisualPuzzles (OOD). Compared to the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model, Monet improves overall scores by +6.81% (V*), +3.00% (HRBench4K), +4.25% (HRBench8K), +9.75% (MME-RealWorld), and +2.31% (VisualPuzzles). On the OOD VisualPuzzles benchmark, Monet-7B outperforms all open-source baselines including Pangea-7B (31.3) and LLaVA-OneVision-72B (30.8), though it remains below proprietary models GPT-4o (41.3) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (49.5).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Monet-7B outperforms the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model on every benchmark, with the largest gain on MME-RealWorld (+9.75%) and V* (+6.81%). Against vanilla SFT and SFT+GRPO trained on the same Monet-SFT-125K data, Monet wins on all benchmarks; notably, SFT+GRPO actually degrades V* performance (78.53 vs. 81.68 for SFT alone), confirming that GRPO is poorly suited for latent reasoning. Compared to DeepEyes (a cropping-based 'think with images' method), Monet ties on V* (83.25) and HRBench4K (71.00 vs. 71.25, a 0.25 gap) but clearly wins on HRBench8K (+2.87), MME-RealWorld (+1.22), and VisualPuzzles (+2.06). Monet also surpasses LVR (83.25 vs. 80.6 on V*) and all open-source OOD baselines on VisualPuzzles (35.02 vs. Pangea-7B 31.3, LLaVA-OneVision-72B 30.8). The only losses are to proprietary models: GPT-4o leads on VisualPuzzles (41.3) and Gemini 2.5 Pro leads further (49.5), though Monet-7B is the strongest open-source model on that benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

Monet sits at the intersection of two recent threads: 'think with images' methods (DeepEyes, Visual Sketchpad, ReFocus) that inject auxiliary images or tool outputs into CoT, and latent-reasoning approaches (LVR, Yang et al.) that replace discrete tokens with continuous embeddings. It departs from both by eliminating external tools entirely and by replacing the direct latent-to-image-embedding alignment used in LVR and Yang et al. with a dual-supervision scheme (observation-token alignment plus controlled attention flow) that avoids the high cost of aligning thousands of image tokens. On the RL side, it identifies that GRPO—standard in MLLM post-training—cannot optimize continuous latent embeddings because they lack an explicit probability distribution, and introduces VLPO, which models latent outputs as Gaussian samples to recover a policy-gradient signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that GRPO improves only text-token reasoning while leaving latent embeddings untrained is a practical warning for the growing body of work applying RL to MLLMs with continuous intermediate representations. The OOD result on VisualPuzzles—where only the VLPO-enhanced model benefits from increasing test-time latent length—suggests that SFT alone teaches the model to *use* latent slots but not to *reason* with them in novel settings, pointing to an open question about what additional signal (curriculum, intrinsic reward, larger-scale RL) is needed for truly general latent visual thinking. Limitations include evaluation on a single 7B backbone, a fixed latent length at inference, and the reliance on a 3.2K RL subset, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models or longer latent sequences.
