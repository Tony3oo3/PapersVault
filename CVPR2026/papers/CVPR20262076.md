## Problem

Quadrifocal tensors encode richer geometric information than pairwise essential matrices or triplewise trifocal tensors, yet they have long been regarded as impractical and of only theoretical interest. No prior work has addressed the global synchronization of a collection of quadrifocal tensors, leaving a gap in leveraging higher-order measurements to improve structure-from-motion reconstruction quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262076/cropped_page7_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a collection of estimated quadrifocal tensors (3×3×3×3), trifocal tensors (3×3×3), and essential matrices (3×3), each defined only up to an unknown nonzero scale, organized into block tensors of size 3n×3n×3n×3n, 3n×3n×3n, and 3n×3n respectively. Missing measurements are zero-filled, and the method requires a sufficiently dense 4-uniform viewing hypergraph (vertices with density ≥ 0.05 are retained). The output is the set of n global camera matrices (3×4) up to a projective frame ambiguity, with the joint optimization variant additionally recovering line projection matrices.

## Method

The key theoretical contribution is the block quadrifocal tensor, which admits an exact Tucker decomposition with the stacked camera matrices as factor matrices and a constant sparse core, yielding a multilinear rank of (4,4,4,4) independent of the number of cameras. The QuadSync algorithm solves a nonconvex optimization over unknown block scales and camera factors using an outer IRLS loop for L1 robustness and an inner ADMM loop that alternates between closed-form updates for each factor matrix (via 4×4 linear solves per row), a convex scale update, and dual variable updates. A joint optimization framework extends this by simultaneously synchronizing the block quadrifocal, trifocal, and essential tensors, exploiting the fact that they share the same 3n×4 camera factor matrices and that the block essential matrix admits a new factorization through 3n×6 line projection matrices. The low multilinear rank constraint is shown to be sufficient to uniquely determine the unknown quadrifocal tensor scales, enabling camera pose recovery without additional calibration.

## Results

On 11 ETH3D stereo multiview datasets and 6 EPFL high-resolution datasets, QuadSync and the Joint Optimization method achieve the best or near-best mean location error in 7/11 and 4/6 datasets, respectively. Performance is strongly correlated with the density of estimated quadrifocal blocks: when the ratio of observed quadrifocal blocks to total blocks exceeds 70%, the proposed methods consistently outperform SOTA baselines, whereas performance degrades when this ratio drops below 30%. In a near-collinear camera configuration from the plant_scene_1 ETH3D SLAM dataset, QuadSync successfully recovers camera poses where pairwise fundamental-matrix-based methods fail entirely due to rank degeneracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six global synchronization baselines: TrifocalSync [27], NRFM [4], MPLS [13], LUD [16], BATA [18], and Cycle-Sync [19] (a recent SOTA method). QuadSync and Joint Opt. achieve the best or near-best mean location error on 7/11 ETH3D and 4/6 EPFL datasets, outperforming all six baselines on those datasets. The advantage is most pronounced on dense viewing graphs where quadrifocal block completion exceeds 70%, suggesting that the higher-order constraints provide stronger geometric regularization than pairwise or triplewise measurements alone. On sparser datasets where quadrifocal estimation is incomplete (<30% completion), the methods fall behind baselines like Cycle-Sync and BATA, which rely only on pairwise measurements and thus are less sensitive to missing higher-order data. In the near-collinear configuration experiment, all pairwise-based baselines (NRFM, LUD, BATA, Cycle-Sync) fail to recover poses due to the rank drop of the fundamental matrix, while QuadSync succeeds, highlighting a qualitative advantage of the quadrifocal formulation in degenerate geometries.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the block trifocal tensor synchronization framework of Miao, Lerman, and Kileel (NeurIPS 2024) from three views to four, introducing the block quadrifocal tensor and proving it admits a Tucker decomposition with multilinear rank (4,4,4,4) independent of the number of cameras. It also builds on the multiview fundamental matrix construction of Sengupta et al. (CVPR 2017) to formulate a joint optimization that simultaneously synchronizes quadrifocal, trifocal, and essential measurements. Conceptually, the paper departs from the long-held view that quadrifocal tensors are 'useful only from a theoretical standpoint' by showing their block structure imposes a strictly stronger algebraic constraint (codimension Ω(n⁴) vs. Ω(n³) for trifocal and Ω(n²) for pairwise) that is both necessary and sufficient for camera recovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to make quadrifocal tensor synchronization practical, opening a path toward higher-order measurements in SfM pipelines that could improve robustness, particularly in near-collinear configurations where pairwise methods provably fail. Key limitations include the requirement for dense viewing graphs (quadrifocal blocks must be estimable), the additional error introduced by the current indirect estimation pipeline (via trifocal tensors and cycle-consistency heuristics), and the computational cost of the 4th-order tensor optimization. Open questions include developing distributed synchronization for higher-order tensors, direct and robust quadrifocal tensor estimation, and whether the theoretical advantage of stronger constraints translates to meaningful gains in sparse or large-scale real-world settings.
