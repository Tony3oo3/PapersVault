## Problem

Vision-language models frequently attend to the correct image region for a question yet still produce incorrect answers, a seeing-perception misalignment that prior work attributes to noisy visual information entering the decoding path. This paper identifies the root cause as suboptimal information flow: text tokens spread cross-attention across many irrelevant visual tokens, diluting the signal from truly informative regions. The key question is whether this flow can be selectively modulated at inference time, without retraining, to restore correct perception.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard RGB image and a free-form text prompt (question or instruction), encoded into visual and text token sequences by the VLM's existing encoder and tokenizer. It produces a natural-language text answer, but requires one additional single-token decoding pass beforehand to collect per-layer attention statistics and generate a modulated causal mask. The mask is a (M+N)×(M+N) matrix identical to the standard causal mask except that entries allowing text tokens to attend to selected irrelevant visual tokens are set to −∞, while all visual-to-visual and visual-to-earlier-visual entries remain unchanged so no image content is lost.

## Method

The core mechanism is a training-free, token-dynamics-based entropy measurement that identifies irrelevant visual tokens and blocks their interaction with text tokens via causal mask modulation. First, one-step decoding collects each visual token's maximum attention to text tokens across all LLM layers, forming a per-token dynamics vector; the entropy of this vector quantifies activation randomness, with high entropy indicating an irrelevant token. An adaptive thresholding step then selects a mask ratio (from 0.1 to 0.9) that maximally shifts the aggregate attention distribution of retained tokens away from the original distribution, ensuring the remaining attention concentrates on question-relevant regions. The resulting modulated causal mask is fed back into the LLM for the actual answer generation, requiring only one extra decoding step of overhead. Crucially, only text-to-visual edges are severed; visual-to-visual attention is preserved, distinguishing this from token pruning and keeping the full image representation intact.

## Results

On general VQA, AIF improves LLaVA-1.5-7B by +7.9 on V* (42.4→50.3), +4.9 on RealWorldQA (55.6→60.5), and +6.4 on MMStar (33.1→39.5), while Qwen2.5-VL-7B gains +6.3 on V* (78.5→84.8), +6.0 on RealWorldQA (68.5→74.5), and +7.0 on MMStar (63.9→70.9). On OCR and counting, improvements are more modest but consistent: TextVQA +2.1 (LLaVA) / +1.1 (Qwen), SeedBench2-Plus +3.6 / +6.1, and CountBench +3.1 / +2.4. On visual grounding (RefCOCO overall), Qwen2.5-VL-7B AIF reaches 88.9, surpassing the specialized Grounding-DINO-L detector (86.6) and InternVL-2.5-8B (87.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base models, AIF delivers consistent gains across all six benchmarks for both LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B, with the largest improvements on fine-grained VQA (V*, MMStar) and OCR (SeedBench2-Plus). On visual grounding, Qwen2.5-VL-7B + AIF (88.9 overall) outperforms the dedicated detector Grounding-DINO-L (86.6) and the larger InternVL-2.5-8B (87.6), while also beating Shikra-7B (82.9) and InternVL2-8B (82.9). In the head-to-head comparison with other training-free and training-based perception methods (Table 5, LLaVA-1.5-7B base), AIF (MMStar 39.5, RealWorldQA 60.5, POPE 88.7) clearly surpasses both ViCrop (35.2, 56.7, 87.2) and CCA (33.2, 51.8, 86.9) without any retraining. Qwen2.5-VL-7B + AIF approaches but does not yet match the 72B variant (e.g., 84.8 vs. 86.4 on V*, 70.9 vs. 70.8 on MMStar), and remains below GPT-4o on most metrics, though it narrows the gap substantially (e.g., 74.5 vs. 75.4 on RealWorldQA). The method also improves hallucination metrics (POPE: 85.4→88.7 for LLaVA, 87.8→89.5 for Qwen), confirming that information flow modulation does not introduce spurious objects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of information-flow analysis in VLMs (Kaduri et al., Zhang et al., Yin et al.) and causal-mask redesign (Pei et al., Xing et al., Wang et al.), but departs from both in a key way: rather than using flow analysis diagnostically or altering the mask architecturally at training time, it treats information flow as a runtime control signal. Unlike visual token pruning (Chen et al.), which removes tokens entirely and discards their content, AIF preserves all visual tokens in the sequence and only blocks text-to-visual attention edges via a modulated causal mask. It also differs from training-free cropping (ViCrop) and training-based interaction strengthening (CCA) by requiring no input modification or weight updates—only one additional decoding step to compute token-dynamics entropy and regenerate the mask.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the standard causal mask is actively harmful—allowing irrelevant visual tokens to inject noise into the text decoding path—and that a simple entropy-based masking strategy can correct this without retraining offers a practical, model-agnostic lever for improving VLM perception. The oracle experiment (Sec. 3.3) showing that the *right* mask ratio can yield substantial gains suggests the current default mask is suboptimal, opening a broader question of whether other aspects of the decoding process (e.g., layer-specific gating, dynamic mask schedules) could be similarly tuned. Limitations include sensitivity to lengthy or indirect prompts and the fact that the method adds one decoding step of latency, though this is negligible in practice. Whether the token-dynamics signal generalizes to more complex multi-step reasoning or video understanding remains open.
