## Problem

Cross-view geo-localization (CVGL) and cross-view image synthesis (CVIS) are typically addressed as separate tasks with task-specific geometric modules that limit generalizability. Geometric Foundation Models (GFMs) like VGGT offer generalizable 3D geometric features, but directly applying them to ground-satellite pairs fails due to the large viewpoint gap and panoramic distortions, leaving their potential for unified cross-view geo-spatial learning largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263690/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes ground panoramic images in equiangular (spherical) format and single-perspective satellite images as input. Ground panoramas are first converted via an equiangular-to-perspective (E2P) transformation into V perspective crops to mitigate spherical distortion before geometric feature extraction. Outputs include geometry-aware latent embeddings for satellite retrieval (CVGL) and synthesized images in both ground-to-satellite and satellite-to-ground directions, with the latter achieved by simply reversing the flow-matching integral without any retraining.

## Method

Geo² comprises two core modules: GeoMap, a dual-branch encoder that extracts VGGT geometric features separately for ground (multi-view from E2P crops) and satellite (single-view) images, then fuses them with semantic CNN tokens via cross-attention to produce aligned embeddings in a shared geometry-aware latent space; and GeoFlow, a flow-matching generator using optimal transport displacement interpolation with lightweight DiTs and DDT heads, conditioned on GeoMap embeddings. The key architectural insight is that flow matching defines a reversible ODE, so training only in the ground-to-satellite direction automatically yields satellite-to-ground synthesis by reversing the integration direction. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) contrastive pre-training of GeoMap with InfoNCE loss, (2) flow-matching training of GeoFlow with L2 vector-field loss, and (3) joint fine-tuning with an additional KL consistency loss that explicitly aligns the ground and satellite embedding distributions, allowing the two tasks to mutually reinforce each other.

## Results

On CVGL, Geo² achieves R@1 of 98.83% on CVUSA, 94.36% on CVACT Val, and 75.08% on CVACT Test, outperforming the previous best (PanoBEV) by 0.12%, 2.46%, and 1.40% respectively. On the challenging VIGOR cross-area setting, Geo² improves R@1 by 5.01% over Sample4Geo (66.71% vs 61.70%) and achieves the best Hit Rate (72.13%). For CVIS, Geo² attains FID of 31.72 on CVACT (G2S) and 22.90 on VIGOR (S2G), substantially outperforming prior methods, while also achieving bidirectional synthesis without retraining.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CVGL, Geo² outperforms all baselines on CVUSA (R@1: 98.83% vs. PanoBEV 98.71%) and both CVACT splits (R@1: 94.36% vs. 91.90% Val; 75.08% vs. 73.68% Test), with the largest margin over Sample4Geo (+4.01% on CVACT Val). On VIGOR same-area, Geo² slightly trails PanoBEV on R@1 (81.59% vs. 82.18%) but achieves a higher Hit Rate (90.35%); on VIGOR cross-area, it also trails PanoBEV on R@1 (66.71% vs. 72.19%) yet surpasses Sample4Geo by 5.01%. For cross-dataset generalization, Geo² leads on CVACT→CVUSA (R@1: 55.14% vs. Sample4Geo 44.95%) but falls short of PanoBEV on CVUSA→CVACT (63.17% vs. 67.79%). For G2S synthesis, Geo² achieves the best FID on CVACT (31.72 vs. SkyDiffusion 36.48) and VIGOR (30.09 vs. 45.29) but is slightly behind SkyDiffusion on CVUSA (33.68 vs. 29.18). For S2G synthesis, Geo² substantially outperforms CrossViewDiff on CVACT (FID 27.77 vs. 41.94) and VIGOR (22.90 vs. 26.57), though it trails on CVUSA (29.05 vs. 23.67).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in cross-view geo-spatial learning, where CVGL and CVIS are treated as separate tasks solved with task-specific geometric modules (polar transformations, BEV estimation, volume density modeling). It repurposes Geometric Foundation Models—specifically VGGT—as a generalizable 3D geometric prior that simultaneously serves both localization (via GeoMap's shared latent space) and synthesis (via GeoFlow's conditioning). The flow-matching formulation with optimal-transport interpolation (Lipman et al., Liu et al.) is borrowed from the generative modeling literature and adapted here to enable bidirectional synthesis through simple ODE reversal, a departure from prior GAN- or diffusion-based CVIS methods that require separate training per direction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single GFM-based representation can jointly drive both retrieval and bidirectional generation, this work opens a path toward unified cross-view geo-spatial systems that were previously fragmented across specialized pipelines. The bidirectional synthesis without retraining is practically valuable for deployment scenarios where both ground-to-satellite and satellite-to-ground views are needed. However, S2G quality remains noticeably weaker than G2S, and the reliance on VGGT as a frozen backbone limits adaptability; whether other GFMs (DUSt3R, MASt3R) or fine-tuned variants yield comparable or superior results remains an open question. The framework's scalability to more than two views (e.g., multi-satellite, multi-panorama) is also unexplored.
