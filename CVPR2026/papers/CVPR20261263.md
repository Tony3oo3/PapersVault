## Problem

World model planning via model-predictive control is computationally prohibitive because conventional tokenizers encode each observation into hundreds of latent tokens, causing quadratic attention costs during rollout (e.g., NWM requires up to 3 minutes per episode). The paper asks whether extreme compression to as few as 8 discrete tokens per image can preserve the planning-critical semantic information needed for effective decision-time planning while achieving orders-of-magnitude speedup.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261263/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer takes a 224×224 RGB image and produces 8 or 16 discrete tokens (16-bit each, ~128 bits total per image) from a vocabulary of size K. At inference, the compact tokens condition a masked generative decoder that produces 196 VQGAN tokens, which are then decoded to pixels by a frozen VQGAN decoder. The world model consumes these compact tokens plus a 3D action vector and predicts the next frame's compact tokens; during MPC, a goal image is encoded to provide the cost target, and the output is an optimal action sequence of length H.

## Method

CompACT's encoder freezes a DINOv3 vision transformer and uses a transformer-decoder-based latent resampler where N learnable query tokens attend to DINOv3 patch features via cross-attention, distilling only high-level semantic cues (object identities, spatial layout) before discretizing with finite scalar quantization. Rather than attempting direct pixel reconstruction from 8–16 tokens, the decoder is a masked generative model (following MaskGIT) that learns to unmask 196 VQGAN tokens conditioned on the compact tokens, transforming an ill-posed decompression problem into a tractable conditional generation task. The action-conditioned world model is trained in this compact discrete space using the same masked generative objective, enabling non-autoregressive single-step token prediction per rollout step instead of iterative denoising. This design yields ~40× planning speedup over 784-token SD-VAE baselines while matching or exceeding their planning accuracy, and outperforms 64-token FlexTok at the same 16-token budget.

## Results

On goal-conditioned visual navigation (RECON), CompACT with 16 tokens achieves ATE 1.330 and RPE 0.390, comparable to the SD-VAE baseline using 784 tokens (ATE 1.262, RPE 0.354), while reducing planning latency from 178.78 s to 5.78 s (~31× speedup). The 8-token variant further reduces latency to 4.83 s with only a small accuracy drop (ATE 1.373). On action-conditioned video prediction (RoboNet), CompACT (16 tokens) achieves 3× lower action prediction error (APE 0.1122 vs 0.3383) than the 256-token MaskGIT-VQGAN baseline, with 5.2× faster generation. For image reconstruction on ImageNet, CompACT (16 tokens) attains rFID 2.40 and IS 209.0, competitive with 32-token baselines despite using 2× fewer tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SD-VAE (784 continuous tokens), CompACT (16 tokens) trades a small accuracy gap (ATE 1.330 vs 1.262 on RECON) for a ~31× reduction in planning latency (5.78 s vs 178.78 s), making real-time planning feasible. Against FlexTok, CompACT clearly wins at matched token counts: at 16 tokens, CompACT achieves ATE 1.330 vs FlexTok's 1.625, and even outperforms FlexTok at 64 tokens (ATE 1.484), demonstrating that semantic encoding via frozen DINOv3 features is more effective than reconstruction-oriented tokenization for planning. On the RoboNet manipulation task, CompACT (16 tokens) surpasses the 256-token MaskGIT-VQGAN target tokenizer on both inverse dynamics (L1 0.091 vs 0.093, R² 0.716 vs 0.684) and action-conditioned video prediction (APE 0.1122 vs 0.3383, a 3× improvement), confirming that modular semantic tokens better capture action-relevant dynamics. For reconstruction fidelity on ImageNet, CompACT (16 tokens, rFID 2.40) is competitive with 32-token TA-TiTok (rFID 1.93–3.95) but trails the 256-token MaskGIT-VQGAN (rFID 1.83) and 1024-token SD-VAE (rFID 0.64), which is expected given the extreme compression ratio.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of world-model-based planning (NWM, DINO-WM, TDMPC2) and image tokenization (FlexTok, TA-TiTok, MaskGIT-VQGAN), departing from both lines in a key way: rather than following the conventional path of increasing token count for higher reconstruction fidelity, CompACT pushes compression to an extreme (8–16 discrete tokens per image) and argues that planning requires only semantic abstraction, not photorealistic detail. Architecturally, it repurposes frozen DINOv3 features as a semantic backbone with a learnable latent resampler, and formulates decoding as a conditional masked-generative task over a pretrained VQGAN codebook rather than direct pixel reconstruction. The masked-generative world model training (Eq. 5) is a discrete analogue of diffusion forcing, connecting to recent work on non-autoregressive sequence generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40× planning speedup (from ~179 s to ~5.8 s per trajectory on RECON) while matching or exceeding the accuracy of 784-token baselines makes real-time world-model planning practically viable for the first time in these settings, a bottleneck that has limited deployment of otherwise promising approaches like NWM. The finding that 8 tokens can outperform 64-token FlexTok on both planning and inverse-dynamics metrics challenges the field's implicit assumption that more tokens monotonically improve decision-making. Open questions include whether the frozen-encoder strategy generalizes to domains with substantial visual distribution shift, how the approach scales to higher-resolution or multi-view observations, and whether the generative decoding overhead (iterative unmasking of 196 target tokens) becomes a bottleneck at even tighter latency budgets.
