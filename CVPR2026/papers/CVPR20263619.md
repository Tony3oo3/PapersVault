## Problem

High-fidelity 3D Gaussian Splatting avatars with pose-dependent appearance require heavy neural network decoding, making mobile deployment impractical. Existing mobile-capable methods (TaoAvatar, SqueezeMe) rely on pretraining a large convolutional network followed by distillation, and use a single global pose feature to linearly combine all blendshapes, which introduces large errors due to the nonlinearity of Gaussian attributes with respect to pose. The paper asks whether one can achieve comparable or better fidelity on mobile without pretraining, by exploiting the local correlation structure among nearby Gaussians.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video (16–160 cameras at 2K resolution) with pre-computed SMPL-X skeleton registrations and FLAME expression coefficients (first 10 components) as input. It produces a compact animatable avatar of 200K Gaussians (~19.4 MB after float16/int16 quantization) that renders at 2K resolution, achieving 1683 FPS on an RTX 4090 and 120 FPS on a Snapdragon 8 Elite phone via a WebGPU-based browser application. The avatar accepts arbitrary 63-dimensional pose and expression parameters at inference time to produce novel-view and novel-pose renderings.

## Method

The core insight is that nearby Gaussians on the body surface exhibit strong local covariance (e.g., a group darkens together under occlusion), so partitioning the body into 256 parts and modeling each part's Gaussian correctives with a small local MLP (outputting a 16-dim feature) plus local blendshapes captures nonlinear pose-dependent changes far better than a single global feature. Position correctives are computed via 10K control-point nodes with inverse-distance interpolation from the three nearest nodes, enabling fully local evaluation. Training is end-to-end in two stages: first, an overparameterized blendshape model is trained for 200K iterations with a corrective-sparsity constraint; second, 90% of blendshape parameters are pruned by keeping only the top-variance Gaussians per attribute (rotation, scale, color) and fine-tuning for 80K iterations. The final model uses no convolutional network at inference—only per-part MLPs, sparse blendshape lookups, and linear blend skinning—all executed in WebGPU compute shaders.

## Results

On AvatarRex (following SqueezeMe's protocol), the method achieves PSNR 25.35, SSIM 0.861, LPIPS 0.158, and L1 0.0234, while running at 1683 FPS on an RTX 4090 and 120 FPS at 2K resolution on a Snapdragon 8 Elite mobile phone. On Talking-Body4D, it reaches PSNR 34.44 (novel view) and 27.90 (novel pose) with LPIPS 0.036 and 0.056 respectively. The pruned model is only 19.4 MB in size, and the blendshape combination step takes just 0.52 ms per frame on a 3050 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AnimatableGS [39], the proposed method trades a small quality gap (PSNR 25.35 vs. 25.51, LPIPS 0.158 vs. 0.155) for a 105× speedup (1683 vs. 16 FPS on RTX 4090) and mobile deployability. Against mmlphuman [82], quality is nearly identical (PSNR 25.35 vs. 25.28) while the proposed method is 5.3× faster (1683 vs. 315 FPS) and runs on mobile. Against SqueezeMe [20], the proposed method substantially outperforms in reconstruction quality (PSNR 25.35 vs. 20.05, L1 0.0234 vs. 0.059) while also being mobile-capable. On Talking-Body4D, the method beats TaoAvatar [6] on novel-view synthesis (PSNR 34.44 vs. 33.81, LPIPS 0.036 vs. 0.064) but is slightly behind on novel-pose PSNR (27.90 vs. 28.38), though it still wins on LPIPS (0.056 vs. 0.089) and SSIM (0.940 vs. 0.939) for novel pose.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) avatar line initiated by AnimatableGS and mmlphuman, which model pose-dependent Gaussian attributes via neural networks or spatially distributed MLPs. It departs from TaoAvatar and SqueezeMe, which rely on a pretrain-then-distill pipeline and a single global pose feature to linearly combine blendshapes, by instead learning local per-body-part blendshapes end-to-end and pruning 90% of them based on attribute variance. The position-based interpolation of control points for non-rigid displacement is adapted from mmlphuman, but restructured for local evaluation to reduce computation. The WebGPU implementation and float16 quantization represent a deployment contribution not present in prior 3DGS avatar work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first open-source full-body 3DGS avatar method that achieves real-time (120 FPS at 2K) pose-dependent appearance rendering on mobile hardware, lowering the barrier for interactive avatar applications in browsers and on phones. The variance-based pruning strategy is a simple but effective compression technique that could transfer to other Gaussian-based representations. Limitations include the requirement for multi-view video with SMPL-X tracking and a template mesh, and the fixed partitioning of the body into 256 parts; whether the approach extends to monocular capture or more complex clothing remains open. The method's reliance on a fixed number of blendshape dimensions (N_B=16) per part and a fixed pruning budget (N_P=20K) per attribute type leaves room for adaptive, content-aware compression.
