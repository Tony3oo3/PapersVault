## Problem

Existing image fusion methods require task-specific architectures and training data, limiting their ability to generalize to unseen fusion tasks. A fundamental tension exists between preserving high-level semantic fidelity and retaining fine-grained pixel-level detail, and the absence of a unified, task-agnostic optimization objective forces models to rely on extensive data fitting and auxiliary task-specific branches. The paper asks whether a single framework trained only on infrared-visible pairs can achieve zero-shot generalization across diverse fusion tasks while maintaining both semantic and pixel-level quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261832/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of aligned source images (infrared + visible during training; medical, near-infrared, multi-focus, or multi-exposure pairs at test time) and produces a single fused image. During training, the reconstruction path additionally consumes a 50%-masked version of each input to learn pixel-level reconstruction, and the perception path consumes object-detection ground-truth labels from the M3FD dataset. Downstream evaluation feeds the fused image into frozen YOLOv8/YOLOv11 detectors and Mask2Former/DPT segmentors, so the fused output must simultaneously preserve detectable object structure and segmentable scene semantics.

## Method

DECC formulates fusion as variational free-energy minimization, decomposing it into a Perception Path that minimizes semantic entropy (via joint fusion + object detection) and a Reconstruction Path that maximizes pixel entropy (via joint fusion + masked-image reconstruction), sharing a common encoder so the two paths mutually regularize each other. A Grouped Frequency Dynamic Convolution module decomposes encoder features into frequency-aware groups; during alternating training, semantic-oriented and pixel-oriented groups are selectively frozen to decouple cross-domain interference, while a cross-modal/cross-frequency fusion mechanism recombines them. An unsupervised KL-divergence trade-off loss aligns the fused image with each path's intermediate features (reducing semantic entropy in PP, increasing pixel entropy in RP), and an Elastic Weight Consolidation term prevents catastrophic forgetting across alternating training phases. The entire framework is trained solely on infrared-visible pairs from M3FD without any task-specific branches, yet generalizes zero-shot to medical, near-infrared, multi-focus, and multi-exposure fusion.

## Results

Trained exclusively on infrared-visible pairs (M3FD), DECC achieves zero-shot generalization across four unseen fusion tasks. On medical image fusion (MIF), DECC attains the highest entropy (EN=7.10) and ties for best Qabf (0.66); on near-infrared/visible fusion (NVIF), it achieves the highest mutual information (MI=4.63); on multi-focus fusion (MFIF), it leads in EN (7.37) and SD (57.51); and on multi-exposure fusion (MEIF), it tops EN (7.20) and SCD (1.25). For downstream tasks, DECC achieves the best mAP@[0.5:0.95] of 35.34 (YOLOv8) and 36.85 (YOLOv11) on M3FD, and the highest mIoU of 55.39 (Mask2Former) and 49.38 (DPT) on FMB, outperforming all 11 compared baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DECC consistently achieves the highest entropy (EN) across all four unseen tasks (7.10 MIF, 7.26 NVIF, 7.37 MFIF, 7.20 MEIF), indicating superior visual information retention compared to all baselines including MUF (7.36 NVIF), IFCN (7.31 MFIF), and GIF (7.06 MEIF). On NVIF, DECC leads in mutual information (4.63 vs. DDBF's 4.41 and TCM's 4.39), while on MFIF it dominates SD (57.51 vs. DDBF's 52.47) and on MEIF it tops SCD (1.25 vs. GIF's 1.23). However, DECC trails on some structural metrics: EMMA achieves higher SD on MIF (92.57 vs. 85.52), IFCN/CCSR/MDLS surpass DECC on VIFF for MFIF (0.95 vs. 0.84), and IFCN leads VIFF on MEIF (1.00 vs. 0.78). For downstream tasks, DECC outperforms all 11 baselines on both object detection (mAP 35.34/36.85 vs. next-best TC-MoA at 34.76/36.25) and semantic segmentation (mIoU 55.39/49.38 vs. GIFNet at 55.28/48.72), confirming that its fused images preserve both semantic and pixel-level information more effectively than task-specific or unified fusion methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified image fusion line (U2Fusion, MUFusion, GIF) by removing the need for explicit task identification at inference, grounding the design in Friston's free-energy principle to derive a dual-entropy objective (minimize semantic entropy, maximize pixel entropy). Architecturally, it builds directly on grouped frequency dynamic convolution (Chen et al., CVPR 2025) to decouple semantic and pixel features in the frequency domain, and on EWC for stable alternating dual-path training. Conceptually, it departs from both vision-driven methods (which sacrifice semantic fidelity) and semantic-driven methods (which sacrifice pixel detail) by treating the two as symmetric, complementary paths under a single variational objective rather than competing losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot generalization claim—training solely on infrared-visible pairs yet transferring to medical, multi-focus, multi-exposure, and near-infrared fusion without task-specific branches—is practically significant for deployment scenarios where new sensor combinations arise frequently. The lightweight parameter count and unsupervised training paradigm lower the barrier for real-world adoption. Open questions include whether the free-energy framing is genuinely necessary for the observed generalization or merely a post-hoc rationalization, whether the approach extends to truly novel modalities (e.g., LiDAR, hyperspectral), and whether the 50% masking strategy in the reconstruction path is optimal or could be replaced by more structured corruption.
