## Problem

Current text-to-video diffusion models are optimized for single-event generation and apply the same caption representation uniformly across all frames via cross-attention, causing multi-event prompts to produce blended, collapsed, or temporally disordered scenes. Existing solutions either require expensive fine-tuning with dense temporal annotations or use clip-wise stitching that lacks global context and suffers from transition discontinuities and drift.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-event text prompt (2–4 events) and optional relative duration weights per event as input. An LLM extracts compact anchor tokens for each event, which are mapped to text-token index sets; the user-specified weights (or an equal split) allocate contiguous latent frame spans to each event. The output is a 5-second video at 832×480 resolution generated on the Wan 2.1 14B backbone with 50 denoising steps, where steering is active only in the first 20 blocks of the first 20 steps to shape layout and motion while leaving later steps to refine appearance.

## Method

SwitchCraft introduces two training-free inference-time modules that edit cross-attention queries within each event's temporal span. Event-Aligned Query Steering (EAQS) constructs right projectors from the anchor-token key subspaces of the target and competing events, then updates each frame's query by enhancing its projection onto the target subspace and suppressing its projection onto the competing subspace, operating per attention head and only on queries to avoid propagating changes to other frames. Auto-Balance Strength Solver (ABSS) adaptively determines the enhancement and suppression coefficients by compressing each event's keys to a dominant direction via SVD, measuring the current query-key alignment margin deficit, and solving a small convex quadratic program with a scale-aware resistance matrix to enlarge the target margin while limiting query displacement. The entire pipeline requires no weight updates, no additional hyperparameters, and no prompt-specific tuning.

## Results

On 60 multi-event prompts, SwitchCraft achieves the best text alignment across all metrics: CLIP-T 0.275 (vs. 0.256 for the Wan 2.1 base) and VideoScore2 T2V alignment 4.30 (vs. 3.47 for Wan 2.1, a 24% relative gain). It also leads on visual quality (VideoScore2 4.33, VBench imaging quality 0.741) and motion smoothness (VBench 0.989) while maintaining subject and background consistency within 0.003 of the base model. In human evaluation (5-point scale), SwitchCraft scores 4.04 on no-leakage, 3.93 on transition smoothness, and 4.24 on visual quality, all the highest among the six compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Wan 2.1 base model, SwitchCraft improves CLIP-T by 0.019 (0.275 vs. 0.256) and VideoScore2 T2V alignment by 0.83 (4.30 vs. 3.47), confirming that the base model's uniform cross-attention fails to localize events temporally; subject and background consistency drop by only 0.002–0.003, a negligible cost. Compared to the training-based MinT [39] (human eval only), SwitchCraft matches on no-omission (4.21 vs. 4.31) but clearly wins on no-leakage (4.04 vs. 3.69), transition smoothness (3.93 vs. 3.76), and visual quality (4.24 vs. 3.83). Against the training-free DiTCtrl [3], SwitchCraft leads on every metric, with the largest gap in VideoScore2 T2V alignment (4.30 vs. 3.27) and VBench aesthetic quality (0.648 vs. 0.511). LongLive [40] produces the most temporally smooth clips (CLIP-F 0.984) but omits events, scoring only 2.07 on no-omission in human eval versus SwitchCraft's 4.21. The stitching baseline (Wan 2.1 I2V) inherits motion bias from the previous segment's last frame, yielding the lowest no-leakage score (2.07) and transition smoothness (2.14) among all methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

SwitchCraft occupies a middle ground between two existing lines of multi-event video generation: training-based temporal grounding (Mind the Time [39], which fine-tunes cross-attention with dense temporal labels) and sequential stitching (MEVG [28], LongLive [40], Phenaki [33], which generate segments independently and condition on prior frames). It is most closely related to DiTCtrl [3], which also edits attention in a pretrained diffusion transformer at inference time, but departs by operating on frame-specific query vectors rather than global attention weights, and by introducing a principled SVD-based convex solver (ABSS) to determine steering magnitudes rather than using fixed or heuristic strengths. The method repurposes the standard cross-attention mechanism of Wan 2.1 without any architectural modification or retraining, positioning itself as a lightweight inference-time intervention on an existing backbone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free nature of SwitchCraft makes multi-event temporal control accessible without the dense annotation and compute costs of fine-tuning-based approaches, which is practically significant for practitioners who want to steer existing T2V models. However, the evaluation is limited to 5-second, 832×480 videos on a single backbone (Wan 2.1 14B), and the gains over the base model in visual quality and consistency metrics are marginal (e.g., subject consistency 0.945 vs. 0.947), suggesting the method trades some global coherence for event specificity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer durations, more than four events, or different diffusion transformer architectures, and whether the early-step/early-block steering heuristic generalizes across model families. The reliance on an LLM for anchor token extraction also introduces a dependency that could be a failure point for ambiguous or novel prompts.
