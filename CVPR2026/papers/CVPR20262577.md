## Problem

Test-time scaling via repeated sampling and verification has proven effective for LLMs and autoregressive VLA models, but remains unexplored for flow-based VLA models that achieve superior dexterous control through flow matching. Naive repeated sampling in flow-based models requires multiple full Euler denoising passes per candidate, drastically reducing control frequency and making it impractical for real-time dexterous robot manipulation. The paper asks: how can flow-based VLAs benefit from test-time computing without sacrificing the high control rates required for dynamic robot tasks?

## Input / Output

The system takes RGB images, a language instruction, and robot state as input and produces a sequence of H future robot actions (an action chunk) as output. Unusually, the pipeline operates on intermediate noisy action candidates rather than fully denoised actions: the original VLA outputs N candidate noisy action chunks at varying noise levels (τ_n = T − (n−1)ξ), and a separate Lite-Flow denoiser refines the selected candidate into the final control signal. The two components run at different frequencies—4 Hz for value-guided sampling and 90 Hz for action denoising—enabling asynchronous, real-time execution on a single GPU.

## Method

FM-Steer augments a flow-based VLA with two modules: an intermediate flow verifier and a Lite-Flow denoiser. For value-guided test-time sampling, the method injects stochasticity into the Euler forward process (modeling each step as a Gaussian with learned mean and tunable variance) and assigns different noise-level endpoints to N candidates, breaking the determinism of ODE sampling. A lightweight verifier, trained with calibrated Q-learning on the same demonstration data, estimates state-action Q-values for each noisy candidate via a query token appended to the VLM backbone, and Best-of-N selects the highest-value flow point. For cascaded action denoising, the selected noisy chunk is segmented into K sub-chunks and passed asynchronously to a Lite-Flow denoiser—a small transformer trained with flow-matching loss starting from noise level τ* rather than 0—which completes the remaining denoising steps at high frequency. This split means the original VLA only integrates from 0 to τ* (reducing per-candidate cost) while the Lite-Flow denoiser handles τ* to 1, combining slow test-time reasoning with fast reactive control.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, FM-Steer built on π0 achieves the highest overall success rate of 98.6%, surpassing π0.5 (96.8%), OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%), and the base π0 (94.2%). On SimplerEnv WidowX tasks, FM-Steer(π0) reaches 79.5% average success rate, a +42.5% improvement over V-GPS (37.0%) and +31.7% over RoboMonkey (47.8%). In real-world evaluations, FM-Steer achieves 91% on WidowX (outperforming π0 by 12%), 87% on Franka (outperforming π0 by 14.75%), and 82% on AgiBot G-1, while maintaining a 90 Hz control frequency via the Lite-Flow denoiser.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FM-Steer(π0) outperforms all baselines on LIBERO, achieving 98.6% overall vs. π0.5 (96.8%), OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%), and the base π0 (94.2%), with the largest margin on LIBERO-Long (+11.5% over π0). On SimplerEnv, FM-Steer decisively beats prior test-time computing methods: it surpasses V-GPS by +42.5% and RoboMonkey by +31.7% on WidowX tasks, and outperforms π0 by +10.3% (79.5% vs. 69.2%). On Google Robot tasks, FM-Steer achieves 76.6% (matching) and 60.3% (aggregation), improving over π0 by +5.2% and +5.6% respectively. In real-world WidowX evaluation, FM-Steer reaches 91% average success rate, outperforming π0 by 12% and OpenVLA by 33%, with particularly strong failure-recovery behavior on complex tasks. On high-frequency platforms (Franka at 20 Hz, AgiBot G-1 at 30 Hz), FM-Steer outperforms π0 by 14.75% and 20% respectively, demonstrating that cascaded denoising preserves dexterous control while adding test-time reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

FM-Steer extends the test-time scaling paradigm (repeated sampling + verification) from autoregressive VLAs (V-GPS, RoboMonkey) to flow-based VLA models like π0 and GR00T N1, which prior work could not handle due to the deterministic nature of ODE-based flow matching. The cascaded action denoising mechanism adapts the concept of cascaded diffusion (originally for high-resolution image generation) to the action generation domain for the first time, splitting the flow-matching trajectory between the heavy VLA and a lightweight LiteFlow denoiser. The intermediate flow verifier draws on calibrated Q-learning from offline RL, but is novel in estimating values of intermediate noisy flow points rather than final actions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FM-Steer demonstrates that test-time computing and real-time control frequency need not be in conflict for flow-based VLAs, resolving a key tension that limited prior test-time methods to low-frequency, non-dexterous tasks. The plug-and-play nature of the framework (applicable to any flow-based VLA without retraining the base model) makes it broadly adoptable. Open questions include how the verifier's quality degrades under significant distribution shift, whether the cascaded denoising approach generalizes to longer horizons or multi-embodiment settings, and whether the 4 Hz verifier frequency is sufficient for highly dynamic tasks. The approach also raises the question of whether the value-guided selection can be further improved with learned (non-greedy) selection strategies.
