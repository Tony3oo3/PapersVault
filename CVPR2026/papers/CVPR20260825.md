## Problem

Automatically generating publication-quality figures for academic papers requires simultaneously satisfying semantic accuracy, visual aesthetics, and structural editability, yet no existing method achieves all three. LLM code-based approaches (SVG, Mermaid) preserve structure but produce cluttered, visually unappealing layouts, while text-to-image models (GPT-Image-1, Nano Banana) yield attractive but logically inaccurate and non-editable raster outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260825/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a natural-language textual description of a figure (e.g., a caption or method pipeline summary) or a free-form user instruction as input. It produces a FigScript specification—a hierarchical graph encoding nodes, edges, containers, and configurable style attributes (colors, fonts, arrow shapes, padding)—which is deterministically rendered into a raster figure image. The FigScript serves as a persistent, editable intermediate that users can further modify through the web editor's conversational panel or direct canvas manipulation.

## Method

Paper2Figure introduces a dual multi-agent architecture built around FigScript, a structured intermediate language positioned between SVG's low-level control and Mermaid's high-level abstraction. In the generation stage, three specialized agents (Plan, Module, Layout) sequentially extract entities, construct visual modules, and refine spatial organization to produce an initial FigScript. In the refinement stage, a Critic Agent inspects the rendered image for visual defects, a Refine Agent formulates a structured revision plan, and an Edit Agent applies updates to the FigScript, forming a closed feedback loop (default: one iteration). All agents are powered by GPT-4o, and the pipeline is wrapped in an interactive web editor with a Conversational Panel, Live Canvas, and FigScript Inspector that enables both agent-assisted and manual editing of the final figure.

## Results

On Paper2Figure Bench (100 academic figures), Paper2Figure (full) achieves an overall score of 79.2, surpassing the strongest baseline (GPT-5 generating SVG, 65.1) by 14.1 points. The full system scores 78.7 in Accuracy, 81.5 in Beauty, and 77.5 in Completeness, improving over the best SVG baseline by 12.0%, 13.5%, and 17.0% respectively. The generation-only variant (without Refinement Agents) already reaches 74.8 overall, confirming that the structured FigScript representation and multi-agent generation pipeline are the primary drivers of quality, with refinement providing an additional ~4.4-point gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Paper2Figure (full, 79.2) outperforms all seven baseline families across every dimension. Among SVG-generating LLMs, GPT-5 is the strongest (65.1 overall, 66.7 Accuracy) but still trails Paper2Figure by 14.1 points overall and 12.0 points in Accuracy; its main weakness is visual clutter (overlapping elements, misaligned lines) despite good textual fidelity. Claude 4.5 Sonnet (SVG, 63.8) and Claude Opus 4 (63.0) are competitive on Accuracy (~65%) but lose significantly on Beauty (~67%) and Completeness (~63%). Mermaid-based outputs (GPT-5: 51.3, Claude 4.5 Sonnet: 56.9) suffer from rigid, homogeneous layouts that hurt both Beauty and Completeness. Image-generation models are far behind: GPT-Image-1 (34.9) and Nano Banana (45.0) achieve the lowest Accuracy (27.3% and 38.3%) due to distorted text, missing modules, and incorrect logical connections, though Nano Banana edges out GPT-Image-1 on Beauty (55.7 vs 47.7) due to richer visual styling. The Refinement Agents contribute a consistent ~4.4-point overall gain, with the largest improvements in Completeness (+6.5) and Accuracy (+4.4), confirming that the critic–refine–edit loop effectively corrects structural and alignment errors that the generation stage alone misses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from both dominant paradigms for text-to-figure generation: LLM code-based methods (SVG, Mermaid) that preserve structure but produce visually cluttered output, and text-to-image diffusion models (GPT-Image-1, Nano Banana) that look appealing but fail at logical accuracy and editability. It carves new ground by introducing FigScript, a structured intermediate language positioned between SVG's low-level control and Mermaid's high-level abstraction, and by applying a dual multi-agent architecture (Generation + Refinement) inspired by the broader multi-agent literature (MetaGPT, AutoGen) to the specific domain of scientific figure creation. The interactive web editor further distinguishes the work by closing the loop between automated generation and human-in-the-loop refinement, a dimension largely absent from prior code- or image-based pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Paper2Figure offers a practically deployable pipeline for researchers who need publication-quality figures without manual design expertise, and Paper2Figure Bench (100 paired figure-description pairs) provides a much-needed evaluation resource for a task that previously lacked standardized benchmarks. However, the benchmark is relatively small, all agents rely on a single model (GPT-4o), and the evaluation itself is LLM-based, leaving open questions about robustness to more complex multi-panel figures, non-English content, and whether the FigScript abstraction generalizes beyond flowchart-style academic diagrams. The moderate gain from the Refinement stage (~4.4% overall) also suggests that the current single-iteration refinement loop may not fully exploit the iterative self-correction potential of the multi-agent design.
