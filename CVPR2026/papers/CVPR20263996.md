## Problem

CLIP's pretraining aligns full images with coarse text prompts via contrastive learning, which entangles normal and abnormal semantics in the text embedding space and prevents the model from establishing a clear boundary between the two. This weak abnormality awareness causes video features to assign nearly indistinguishable similarity scores to normal and anomalous prompts, fundamentally limiting CLIP's effectiveness as a backbone for video anomaly detection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video clips as sampled frames encoded by a frozen CLIP ViT-L/32 visual encoder, aggregated into clip-level embeddings via a lightweight temporal transformer; for abnormal regions, ROI Align extracts spatially grounded features from annotated bounding boxes. Text inputs include global captions, region-level captions, and Qwen-VL-generated hard-negative captions, all constrained to CLIP's 77-token limit. The output is a set of ℓ₂-normalized embeddings (clip-level and region-level) that produce anomaly scores via cosine-similarity margins between normal and abnormal prompt sets, making the checkpoint a drop-in replacement for standard CLIP-style inference in existing VAD pipelines.

## Method

Alert-CLIP reshapes CLIP's embedding geometry through a three-level contrastive alignment strategy: (1) global video-text alignment via InfoNCE on clip-level embeddings and global captions to establish coarse normal/abnormal separation; (2) region-text alignment that pairs ROI-Align-extracted region features with their corresponding event descriptions to inject spatially grounded fine-grained semantics; and (3) region-semantic alignment that contrasts each region embedding against K hard-negative captions (visually similar but semantically reversed) to sharpen the normal-abnormal decision boundary. Training follows a two-stage curriculum—Stage I optimizes only the global loss to stabilize the semantic space, then Stage II jointly minimizes all three losses with weighted balancing—while keeping both CLIP encoders frozen. The approach is trained on VAGTA, a newly constructed dataset of 4,212 clips from UCF-Crime and MSAD with standardized quality, bounding-box annotations, multi-level captions, and generated hard negatives, providing the multi-granularity supervision that the three alignment branches require.

## Results

In the zero-shot setting, Alert-CLIP achieves 75.75 AUC on UCF-Crime and 55.18 AP on XD-Violence, surpassing CLIP (61.91 AUC / 34.35 AP) by over 13 and 20 points respectively. Under weakly supervised training within the VadCLIP framework, Alert-CLIP reaches 89.32 AUC and 33.57 AP on UCF-Crime, and 89.63 AUC and 78.24 AP on MSAD, outperforming the CLIP backbone (88.02 / 32.72 and 87.45 / 71.09). On the open-vocabulary benchmark, Alert-CLIP attains 70.12 AP on UCF-Crime, exceeding Anomize (69.31) and OVVAD (66.53). Generalization to unseen domains is confirmed by 75.39 AUC on UBnormal (vs. 72.08 for CLIP) and 91.83 overall AP on XD-Violence (vs. 90.01 for CLIP).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Alert-CLIP consistently outperforms all compared baselines across every evaluation setting. Against CLIP, the gains are most dramatic in zero-shot mode: +13.84 AUC on UCF-Crime and +20.83 AP on XD-Violence, confirming that the multi-level alignment reshapes the embedding space far beyond what frozen CLIP provides. VideoCLIP, the strongest video-specific backbone, is surpassed by 3.62 AUC (UCF) and 0.82 AP (XD) in zero-shot, while in weakly supervised mode Alert-CLIP leads by 2.91 AUC on UCF-Crime. Among VLM baselines, Alert-CLIP beats SigLIP by 6.02 AUC and 2.93 AP, and LLaVA-1.5-7B by 2.91 AUC and 4.92 AP in zero-shot, showing that a tuned CLIP backbone can rival much larger multimodal models for anomaly scoring. In the open-vocabulary setting, Alert-CLIP edges out Anomize by 0.81 AP and 1.65 AUC on UCF-Crime, and surpasses OVVAD by 3.59 AP, though it trails OVVAD by 0.26 on AUCn (novel-category AUC), indicating a slight trade-off on the hardest subset. On the unseen XD-Violence open-vocabulary test, Alert-CLIP improves overall AP from 90.01 (CLIP) to 91.83, with the novel-category gap widening from 94.23 to 96.01.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in CLIP-based VAD (VadCLIP, STPrompt, Ex-VAD, VarCMP), which keeps the CLIP backbone frozen and layers task-specific modules on top of its features. Alert-CLIP instead directly reshapes CLIP's embedding geometry through a multi-level contrastive alignment strategy (global video-text, region-text, and semantic hard-negative), positioning itself as a representation-tuning contribution rather than a downstream-architecture contribution. It also distinguishes itself from prior CLIP fine-tuning efforts (LongCLIP, RegionCLIP, grounding-based pre-training) by arguing that anomaly awareness is a distinct failure mode—deviation-from-norm rather than object-attribute recognition—that requires context-sensitive semantic separation rather than simply longer captions or region supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is that Alert-CLIP serves as a drop-in backbone replacement for existing VAD pipelines (demonstrated within VadCLIP), and the VAGTA dataset with region-level annotations and hard-negative captions fills a gap in fine-grained VAD supervision. The diagnostic framework (MSS metric, text-space geometry analysis) gives the community a reusable tool for measuring abnormality awareness in any VLM. Key open questions include whether the annotation pipeline can be automated at scale, whether the 77-token caption constraint limits semantic richness, and whether the gains transfer to non-surveillance anomaly domains (e.g., medical or industrial video).
