## Problem

Existing vision-language models are designed for narrow-field-of-view pinhole images and must stitch multiple crops to approximate a 360° scene, fundamentally breaking the seamless spatial continuity and wrap-around topology that a single panorama preserves. Compounding this architectural mismatch, no large-scale panoramic VQA benchmark exists that covers adverse driving scenarios such as occlusions and vehicular accidents, leaving holistic omni-scene reasoning untested.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260561/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single equirectangular projection (ERP) panorama image—significantly higher resolution than pinhole inputs and subject to severe geometric distortion—along with a free-form text question, and produces a natural-language answer. The PanoVQA benchmark spans 12 question categories (scene captioning, object identification, spatial relationships, occlusion reasoning, collision risk, time-to-collision, etc.) with 653K QA pairs, and evaluation uses a GPT-4o-mini judge scoring answers on a 1–5 scale normalized to 0–100.

## Method

The core contribution is a Panoramic Sparse Attention (PSA) module that plugs into the ViT of an existing pinhole-based VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) without architectural retraining. PSA runs in parallel with Sliding Window Attention (SWA): SWA captures fine-grained local patterns within non-overlapping windows (reducing complexity from O(L²) to O(L·L_w)), while PSA dynamically selects Top-K most relevant key tokens per query via a learned selector with multi-head gating and position-aware embeddings, capturing long-range dependencies such as front-back or left-right wrap-around connections. The combined Panoramic Hybrid Attention (PHA) is inserted into each Transformer block, and the full model is trained via full-parameter supervised fine-tuning on PanoVQA. The PSA module adds only ~95M trainable parameters (vs. 668M for standard SFT of the vision encoder) while matching or exceeding full fine-tuning performance, and the dynamic Top-K selection (K=512, bottleneck dim=196) is tuned via parameter-efficiency ablations.

## Results

On the PanoVQA benchmark (normalized GPT-score, 0–100), the proposed PanoLM-7B achieves an overall average of 45.48%, surpassing the strongest zero-shot open-source model Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Ins. (35.56%) by nearly 10 points and the best 7B–9B zero-shot model InternVL3-8B-Ins. (34.48%) by over 11 points. After panoramic SFT, the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B reaches 45.21%, and PanoLM-7B with the Panoramic Sparse Attention module edges ahead at 45.48%. The largest absolute gains over zero-shot baselines appear in the accident sub-tasks (D3–D5), where PanoLM-7B scores 75.72–76.74% versus 48–65% for the best zero-shot models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PanoLM-7B (45.48% avg) decisively outperforms all nine zero-shot baselines, with the largest gaps over Chameleon-7B (+25.5 pts), LLaVA-NEXT-7B (+23.7 pts), and DeepSeek-VL2-Small (+19.8 pts). Among the 7B–9B open-source zero-shot models, InternVL3-8B-Ins. is the strongest at 34.48%, still trailing PanoLM-7B by 11 points; GLM-4-9B (29.57%) and Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Ins. (29.77%) are mid-pack. The proprietary Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite (30.74%) underperforms several open-source 7B models, while Grok-3-Mini (21.74%) is the weakest overall. The most informative comparison is against the same base model after SFT: Qwen2.5-VL-7B without PSA reaches 45.21%, and adding the PSA module yields a modest but consistent gain to 45.48%, with the largest per-task improvements in D3 (75.72 vs. 74.07) and D4 (76.74 vs. 74.90). The 32B Qwen2.5-VL-Ins. (35.56%) confirms that simply scaling parameters without panoramic-specific architecture cannot close the gap to a 7B model with PSA and panoramic SFT.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: panoramic scene understanding (SphereNet, PanoFormer, OneBEV) and vision-language models (LLaVA, Qwen-VL, InternVL). Prior panoramic methods handle 360° imagery for detection or segmentation but lack language grounding, while existing VLMs and driving VQA benchmarks (NuScenes-QA, DriveLM, OmniDrive) operate exclusively on pinhole or multi-camera inputs. The key departure is the Panoramic Sparse Attention (PSA) module, which extends the sliding-window attention already present in Qwen2.5-VL with a dynamic Top-K global selector, enabling a single equirectangular panorama to be processed without the 'stitching' of multiple narrow crops. The PanoVQA dataset itself is constructed by reformatting three existing driving datasets (NuScenes, BlendPASS, DeepAccident) into panoramic form and generating VQA pairs via GPT-5-mini.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PanoVQA fills a genuine gap as the first large-scale (653K QA pairs) benchmark for panoramic VQA, providing the community a standardized resource for evaluating holistic 360° scene reasoning that no prior dataset offered. The plug-and-play PSA module is practically useful for adapting existing VLMs to panoramic inputs without architectural overhaul. However, the performance gains are modest: PLM-7B (45.91%) barely surpasses the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B after SFT (45.21%), and the 1-Pano vs. 6-Cam advantage (41.42% vs. 40.22%) is small. Open questions include whether the approach scales to higher-resolution panoramas, generalizes beyond driving to indoor or outdoor non-driving scenes, and whether the GPT-generated QA pairs introduce systematic biases that limit real-world transfer.
