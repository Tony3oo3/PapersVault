## Problem

Pre-trained weights are a cornerstone of transfer learning, yet obtaining weights that encode more knowledge than the available training data typically requires collecting and curating larger datasets, which is costly and often impractical. This paper asks whether the structured forgetting induced by sequential fine-tuning on progressively smaller data subsets can be modeled and inverted to synthesize weights that behave as if trained on a larger dataset, without any additional data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261061/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of S weight snapshots [Θ⁰, Θ¹, …, Θ^{S-1}] (S=5 empirically) obtained by iteratively fine-tuning on subsets sampled at ratio r (e.g., r=0.5 or 0.33), along with their coordinate-wise differences dW. KNOWN, a two-stream MLP hypernetwork with only 9,425 parameters, predicts a per-parameter residual that is added to the current weights to yield the knowledge-enriched output Θ̂⁻¹. The process is iterative: the predicted Θ̂⁻¹ can be prepended to the trajectory to predict Θ̂⁻², enabling ×2, ×4, ×8 knowledge scaling in successive steps.

## Method

The core mechanism is retrodiction of progressive forgetting: sequential fine-tuning on geometrically shrinking datasets produces a smooth, structured trajectory in weight space, and KNOWN learns to extrapolate one step 'backward' along this trajectory to recover weights corresponding to a larger (virtual) dataset. KNOWN follows the two-stream MLP architecture of WNN, taking both the raw weight window W_i^t and its first-order differences dW_i^t as inputs, and is meta-trained with an L1 residual objective on ~50 GB of trajectories collected from diverse small architectures (CNN, ResNet, DenseNet, ShuffleNet, MobileNetV2) across CIFAR-10, MNIST, and Fashion-MNIST with randomized sampling rates, learning rates, and batch sizes. Operation-specific variants (KNOWN_Conv, KNOWN_FC, KNOWN_Bias) are trained separately to capture layer-type-specific dynamics. Once meta-trained, KNOWN generalizes zero-shot to larger architectures (ResNet-18, PVTv2, DeepLabV3+) and diverse downstream tasks (classification, domain generalization, captioning, segmentation) without any additional meta-training, and the predicted weights serve as improved initializations for standard fine-tuning.

## Results

On the primary benchmark (ResNet18 pre-trained on CIFAR-100, fine-tuned on CIFAR-10), KNOWN with ×8 iterative prediction achieves 93.55% top-1 accuracy at 100% pre-training data, surpassing the Naive Transfer baseline (92.40%) by 1.15 points and the strongest baseline TSV (92.72%) by 0.83 points. The advantage grows as pre-training data shrinks: at 25% data, KNOWN (×8) reaches 92.92% versus 92.25% for TSV and 92.03% for Task Vector. Across the PVTv2/ImageNet experiments (Table 2), KNOWN (×9) improves TinyImageNet accuracy by +1.41 points and CUB by +0.81 points over Naive Transfer, while on PACS domain generalization (Table 3) it gains +4.73 points average over baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KNOWN consistently outperforms all four weight-prediction baselines across every data fraction and prediction depth. At 100% CIFAR-100 pre-training with ×8 prediction, KNOWN (93.55%) beats TSV [11] (92.72%) by 0.83 pts, Task Vector [24] (92.65%) by 0.90 pts, ConsensusTA [55] (92.43%) by 1.12 pts, and MagMax [44] (92.39%) by 1.16 pts. The gap widens at lower data fractions: at 25% data, KNOWN (×8) leads TSV by 0.67 pts and Task Vector by 0.89 pts, while MagMax and ConsensusTA degrade more sharply. On the PVTv2/ImageNet transfer experiments (Table 2), KNOWN (×9) achieves the best or near-best result on all five downstream datasets (e.g., +1.41 on TinyImageNet, +0.81 on CUB), whereas TSV and MagMax occasionally fall below the Naive Transfer baseline (e.g., TSV drops −1.51 on Oxford Flowers). On PACS domain generalization (Table 3), KNOWN (×9) gains +4.85 average points over baseline, while TSV and MagMax actually underperform the baseline (−0.03 and −0.31 respectively), highlighting that fixed-extrapolation baselines struggle with domain-shift settings where the forgetting trajectory is more complex.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the Weight Nowcasting Network (WNN) [27] by repurposing its two-stream MLP architecture for a new objective: instead of predicting future weights to accelerate training, KNOWN predicts 'past' weights (retrodiction) to simulate training on a larger dataset. Conceptually, it departs from the forgetting/unlearning literature [14, 28] by inverting the typical framing—rather than treating forgetting as a problem to mitigate, the paper deliberately induces structured forgetting via progressive fine-tuning and then reverses the trajectory. The approach also intersects with task arithmetic [24] and model merging [55, 11], which manipulate weight differences for transfer, but KNOW prediction operates on the full sequential trajectory rather than a single weight difference, and is learned via meta-learning rather than applied as a fixed formula.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical, lightweight mechanism (a 9,425-parameter hypernetwork) to obtain 'virtual' pre-trained weights that outperform those trained on the original dataset, without requiring additional data collection or full retraining. This is particularly relevant in data-scarce or data-expensive settings where scaling the training set is impractical. However, open questions remain: the scaling-law assumption (more data → better weights) may not hold universally, the iterative prediction could compound errors over many steps, and the method has only been validated on vision tasks with relatively small models. Whether the approach transfers to LLMs, diffusion models, or other modalities where the weight-loss landscape is far more complex is unexplored.
