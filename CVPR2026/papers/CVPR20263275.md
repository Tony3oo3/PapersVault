## Problem

Existing multi-modal test-time adaptation methods rely on black-box neural network outputs without explicitly modeling category-conditional distributions, leading to sub-optimal predictions and irregular decision boundaries. While canonical Gaussian discriminant analysis (GDA) provides such modeling in uni-modal TTA, the inherent modality distribution asymmetry in multi-modal settings—where only one modality may be corrupted—biases the estimation of category means and covariances, undermining GDA's effectiveness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263275/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes streaming batches (size 16) of paired video and audio clips at test time, with no access to labels or source data. It extracts penultimate-layer features from three perspectives—two uni-modal encoders (video, audio) and one fused multi-modal branch—each producing d-dimensional representations. The output is a single class label per sample (50 classes for Kinetics50-C, 309 for VGGSound-C), obtained by fusing the source model's logits with GDA log-posterior scores on the fused view.

## Method

AdaPGC replaces the shared-covariance canonical GDA with category-specific covariance matrices, initialized from the source model's linear classification head and progressively updated via streaming sufficient statistics (soft counts N, first-order S, second-order Q) with EMA smoothing (α=0.9). Predictions fuse source logits with GDA log-posteriors through a weighted sum, stabilized by a soft-prediction alignment loss that gently guides the source branch toward the GDA posterior. To counteract modality asymmetry, the method computes symmetric KL divergence between each uni-modal prediction and the multi-modal prediction to identify the corrupted modality per sample, then applies a one-sided InfoNCE contrastive loss that pulls the unreliable modality's features toward the reliable one while stop-gradients on the reliable side. The overall loss combines these with confidence and balance entropy regularizers, updating only fusion-layer attention parameters (for GDA-related losses) and LayerNorm modules in the corrupted modality's encoder (for contrastive loss).

## Results

On Kinetics50-C with corrupted video, AdaPGC achieves 66.1% average accuracy across 15 corruption types, outperforming the strongest baseline TSA (64.5%) by 1.6 points and SuMi (63.9%) by 2.2 points. On Kinetics50-C with corrupted audio, AdaPGC reaches 73.2% average, surpassing SuMi (71.9%) and TSA (71.5%). On VGGSound-C with corrupted audio, AdaPGC attains 36.8% versus TSA's 34.7% and SuMi's 33.2%. On VGGSound-C with corrupted video, AdaPGC (57.0%) is competitive with SuMi (57.3%) and READ (56.9%), with the gap narrowing to within 0.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaPGC outperforms all seven baselines on Kinetics50-C video corruption (66.1% vs. TSA 64.5%, SuMi 63.9%, READ 62.5%) and Kinetics50-C audio corruption (73.2% vs. SuMi 71.9%, TSA 71.5%). On VGGSound-C audio corruption, AdaPGC leads by a wider margin (36.8% vs. TSA 34.7%, SuMi 33.2%, READ 32.4%), indicating stronger robustness on the more challenging 309-class dataset. On VGGSound-C video corruption, AdaPGC (57.0%) is essentially tied with SuMi (57.3%) and READ (56.9%), with the gap within 0.3 points. Uni-modal TTA baselines (Tent, EATA, SAR) consistently underperform multi-modal methods by 3–7 points, confirming that cross-modal information is critical under corruption. MM-TTA degrades severely on VGGSound-C video (32.0% avg), suggesting its pseudo-labeling strategy is unreliable under strong visual corruption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the uni-modal GDA-based test-time adaptation paradigm (established by DOTA and BayesTTA) to the multi-modal setting, where it identifies that canonical GDA's shared-covariance assumption and equal treatment of modalities break down under modality distribution asymmetry. It departs from existing multi-modal TTA methods (READ, SuMi, TSA) that rely solely on entropy minimization or pseudo-labeling over black-box network outputs, by introducing an explicit probabilistic Gaussian model with category-specific covariances updated via streaming sufficient statistics. The adaptive contrastive asymmetry rectification module, which uses symmetric KL divergence to detect the corrupted modality and a one-sided InfoNCE loss to align it toward the reliable one, is a novel combination not present in prior multi-modal TTA work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The explicit modeling of category-conditional distributions provides interpretable, smooth decision boundaries that black-box entropy-based TTA methods cannot offer, which is practically valuable for safety-critical multi-modal deployments. The modality asymmetry rectification addresses a real-world concern (corrupting factors often affect only one modality) that prior methods handle only implicitly. Limitations include evaluation on only two benchmarks with two modalities (video + audio), and the per-category covariance matrices introduce memory overhead that may not scale to very high-dimensional or many-class settings. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more than two modalities, to missing-modality scenarios, and to streaming settings with non-stationary corruption patterns.
