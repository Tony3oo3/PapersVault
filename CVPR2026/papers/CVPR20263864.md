## Problem

Cross-modal ship re-identification between optical and SAR imagery suffers from a large modality gap caused by fundamentally different imaging mechanisms, compounded by coherent speckle noise in SAR images that corrupts feature extraction. During retrieval, models tend to rank same-modality gallery samples above correct cross-modal matches, severely limiting SAR-to-Optical performance. This task remains underexplored, with only one public benchmark (HOSS ReID) and a single prior method (TransOSS).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263864/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts optical and SAR ship images (H×W spatial resolution) and produces d-dimensional feature embeddings for retrieval ranking via Euclidean or cosine distance. During inference, optical queries additionally trigger a Brownian Bridge Diffusion Model that generates K=5 pseudo-SAR images, whose features are fused with the original optical embedding using a fixed weight τ=0.2. SAR inputs undergo a preprocessing step that discards the lowest α=5% of pixel values (identified as noise) and linearly rescales the remainder to [0,255] before feature extraction.

## Method

MOS builds on the TransOSS ViT backbone and introduces two complementary modules. The Modality-Consistent Representation Learning (MCRL) module first denoises SAR images by removing the lowest α% pixels, then applies a class-wise modality alignment loss (CMAL) that approximates the Wasserstein-2 distance between per-identity optical and SAR feature distributions under a diagonal-covariance assumption, jointly optimizing mean and variance consistency across modalities. The Cross-modal Data Generation and Feature Fusion (CDGF) module trains a Brownian Bridge Diffusion Model (pre-trained on QXS-SAROPT, fine-tuned on HOSS ReID) to synthesize pseudo-SAR images from optical inputs at inference, then fuses the ensemble of generated SAR features with the original optical feature via a weighted L2-normalized combination. Training uses identity cross-entropy, triplet loss, and CMAL (λ_cmal=2.0), while inference leverages the diffusion-generated samples to bridge the modality gap without additional training.

## Results

On the HOSS ReID dataset, MOS achieves 60.4% mAP and 68.8% R1 under the ALL to ALL protocol, 51.4% mAP and 40.0% R1 under Optical to SAR, and 48.7% mAP and 46.3% R1 under SAR to Optical. Compared to the strongest baseline TransOSS, MOS improves R1 by +2.9%, +6.2%, and +16.4% across the three protocols respectively, with the largest gain in the SAR-to-Optical direction where the modality gap is most severe. MOS also outperforms the best visible-infrared ReID method (VersReID) by +11.1% mAP and +9.1% R1 in the ALL to ALL setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MOS outperforms all 14 compared baselines across all three evaluation protocols. The most direct comparison is against TransOSS [42], the only prior method specifically designed for optical-SAR ship ReID: MOS gains +3.0% mAP / +2.9% R1 (ALL→ALL), +2.5% mAP / +6.2% R1 (Opt→SAR), and +10.0% mAP / +16.4% R1 (SAR→Opt), with the largest improvement in the SAR-to-Optical direction where the modality gap is most pronounced. Among ship ReID methods, D2InterNet [26] is the strongest single-modality baseline (50.2% mAP, 59.1% R1 in ALL→ALL), still trailing MOS by 10.2% mAP. Visible-infrared ReID methods (VersReID, DEEN, MCJA, etc.) perform substantially worse, with VersReID [60] being the best at 49.3% mAP / 59.7% R1 in ALL→ALL but only 25.7% mAP in Opt→SAR, confirming that infrared-specific architectures do not transfer well to SAR. General vision backbones (ViT, DeiT) and person ReID methods (TransReID, SOLIDER) are the weakest, achieving below 50% mAP in ALL→ALL and single-digit R1 in cross-modal settings, underscoring the difficulty of the optical-SAR gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TransOSS (ICCV 2025), the first dedicated optical-SAR ship ReID method using a Vision Transformer backbone, and extends it with two complementary modules. The MCRL component introduces a class-wise modality alignment loss derived from a diagonal-covariance approximation of the Wasserstein-2 distance, departing from prior cross-modal ReID losses (triplet, contrastive) that operate at the sample level rather than the class-distribution level. The CDGF module repurposes the Brownian Bridge Diffusion Model (CVPR 2023), originally designed for general image-to-image translation, as an inference-time pseudo-SAR generator whose features are fused with original optical embeddings — a use case not explored in prior diffusion-for-ReID work, which typically generates training data rather than inference-time features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Optical-SAR ship ReID is a practically critical but nearly unexplored task with only one public benchmark (HOSS ReID, 1,063 training images), so establishing a strong baseline and identifying effective modality-bridging strategies is valuable for the maritime surveillance community. The substantial SAR-to-Optical gains (+16.4% R1) highlight how much prior methods underperform in the direction where SAR speckle noise most severely degrades features, and the ablation confirms that training-time distribution alignment (MCRL) is a prerequisite for inference-time generative fusion (CDGF) to be effective. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to larger, multi-scene ship datasets, whether the simple pixel-threshold denoising generalizes to different SAR sensors, and whether the Wasserstein-2 class alignment transfers to other extreme modality pairs (e.g., thermal-infrared, LiDAR).
