## Problem

Existing hand-object interaction (HOI) benchmarks evaluate either manipulation (actions, procedural steps) or effects (object state changes) in isolation, at a coarse semantic level. This leaves a gap in assessing whether models can reason about the full spatio-temporal dynamics of HOI—how a specific manipulation style produces a specific, localized effect on an object or its parts over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262742/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For MCQ, the model receives a 5-second egocentric video clip (16 uniformly sampled frames at model-specific resolutions from 224×224 to 480×854) plus a question with five options, and outputs a single selected option (or multiple for Objects, which averages 5.7 options and 1.6 correct answers). For ReasoningVOS, the same video plus an implicit question (not an explicit referring expression) yields per-frame segmentation masks for the queried object or object part. In the ablation study, additional structured inputs—63-dim 3D hand poses (WiLoR), 4-dim object bounding-box tracklets (AMEGO), and 768-dim CLIP object features—are concatenated with the video feature before a projection layer.

## Method

HanDyVQA is constructed via a collaborative LLM-plus-human pipeline: LLMs filter Ego4D narrations for relevant HOI verbs, generate template-based question candidates across six types (Action, Process, Objects, Location, State Change, Object Parts), and propose distractors; human annotators then verify, revise, and add challenging options, yielding 11.1K MCQ pairs and 10.3K segmentation masks. The ReasoningVOS task uses implicit queries requiring world-knowledge reasoning rather than explicit textual grounding, distinguishing it from ReferringVOS. For the empirical study, the authors fine-tune InternVideo2-Stage2 with separate frame-wise MLP + LSTM encoders for each HOI cue modality, concatenated with the frozen video feature and trained via contrastive loss on a 1.1K-question instruction-tuning split. They further evaluate zero-shot performance of eight video-language models (dual-encoder and LLM-integrated) and conduct error-type analysis (Class/Side, Interaction/Spatial, Motion, Structural Change) across varying frame counts and resolutions to diagnose where models fail.

## Results

On HanDyVQA, the best-performing model Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves 72.6% average top-1 accuracy across six question categories (Action, Process, Objects, Location, State Change, Object Parts), substantially below human performance of 96.6%. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B at full resolution (480×854) leads with 69.9% average accuracy, while the best 7B-scale model LLaVA-Video-7B reaches 54.8%. For ReasoningVOS, the best model (Sa2VA-8B with video input and GT option prompt) achieves only 34.5 Jaccard on Objects and 11.5 on Parts, far below prior ReasoningVOS benchmarks (40+ J).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-2.5-Pro [7] leads all models at 72.6% average accuracy, outperforming GPT-4o (vision) [16] by 13.7 points (72.6 vs. 58.9) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B [40] by 2.7 points (72.6 vs. 69.9). Among 7B-scale open-source models, LLaVA-Video-7B [23] (fine-tuned on Ego4D) achieves the highest average at 54.8%, edging out Qwen2.5-VL-7B [40] (53.8%) and mPLUG-Owl3-8B [51] (50.9%). The dual-encoder LaViLa [57] trained on Ego4D outperforms InternVideo2-Stage2 [42] by 9.3 points (42.6 vs. 33.3), particularly in Action (61.6 vs. 41.1) and Objects (68.5 vs. 37.0), but trails all LLM-integrated models in Location and State Change. VideoLLaMA2.1-7B [6] underperforms despite its spatio-temporal modeling focus, scoring only 42.1% average. For ReasoningVOS, Sa2VA-8B [52] with video input and GT option prompt achieves the best Objects score (34.5 J), while VideoLISA-3.8B [1] with GT option prompt leads on Parts (3.2 J), though all scores remain far below prior ReasoningVOS benchmarks. Hand/object-aware models HelpingHands [55] and EgoHOD [26] show mixed results: EgoHOD improves Objects (+6.8 AP) and State (+3.4 Acc) over LaViLa but degrades Process (−2.8), while HelpingHands improves Objects (+1.7 AP) but worsens Action (−2.9) and Process (−3.2).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of HOI recognition benchmarks and video object segmentation. Prior HOI benchmarks (EgoTaskQA, EgoThink, HOI-QA, EgoHOIBench, OSCAR, HD-EPIC) each isolate a single aspect—either manipulation (actions, procedural steps) or effects (state changes, object states)—and typically operate on single frames or coarse temporal windows. HanDyVQA unifies both aspects within 5-second egocentric clips and extends ReasoningVOS from object-level to part-level grounding, a granularity not addressed by prior RVOS/ReasoningVOS benchmarks (VideoLISA, Sa2VA, EgoMask). The ablation study repurposes existing tools (WiLoR for 3D hand pose, AMEGO for object tracking, CLIP features) as auxiliary inputs to a contrastive-learning baseline, testing whether explicit HOI cues close the performance gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial and specific gap: even Gemini-2.5-Pro reaches only 72.6% average MCQ accuracy versus 96.6% for humans, with the largest deficits in spatial-relationship and motion reasoning. The ReasoningVOS scores (7–32 Jaccard) are dramatically lower than prior ReasoningVOS benchmarks (40+ J), establishing part-level grounding in dynamic egocentric scenes as a genuinely harder open problem. A notable negative result is that purpose-built hand/object-aware models (HelpingHands, EgoHOD) fail to improve—or even degrade—performance, suggesting current HOI-aware encoders do not capture the fine-grained spatio-temporal dynamics the task demands. Open questions include how to architect video encoders that explicitly model local hand-object contact, part-level structural evolution, and motion at sufficient temporal resolution.
