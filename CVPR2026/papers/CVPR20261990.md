## Problem

Existing learned image compression methods face a dual limitation: standard Window-based Multi-head Self-attention is confined to local receptive fields while shifted-window alternatives introduce higher computational complexity, and the few methods that incorporate frequency-domain transforms rely on fixed, human-designed bases (e.g., wavelets) that cannot adapt to diverse image content. This paper tackles both the spatial efficiency-effectiveness trade-off and the lack of content-adaptive frequency processing in LIC.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input, trained on 256×256 patches from OpenImages (300k images, short side ≥256) and evaluated on Kodak (768×512), CLIC Professional Validation (2k resolution), and Tecnick (1200×1200). It produces a compressed bitstream via a hyperprior-based autoregressive entropy model and a reconstructed image, with six models trained at different Lagrangian multipliers (λ = 0.05 to 0.0018) to cover a range of operating rates. The Denoising-as-Regularizer module is active only during training, imposing zero additional inference cost.

## Method

SAAF introduces a spatial-frequency dual-path transform with three synergistic components. First, Cross-Sparse Window Attention (CSWA) replaces standard WMSA by combining Local Window Attention with a Global Sparse Attention mechanism that uses only N_g=2 window-conditioned learnable tokens to capture cross-window dependencies at M²×N_g attention cost, plus a precomputed relative position bias table to eliminate dynamic MLP overhead. Second, the Adaptive Frequency Block employs a lightweight Decomposition Weight Generator (convolutional network) to produce four content-dependent weight maps simulating LL/LH/HL/HH frequency bands, modulated by learnable global weights and stabilized by an Orthogonal Linear Projection layer. Third, the Denoising-as-Regularizer (DaR) module corrupts the latent y with scaled Gaussian noise and trains a lightweight conditional noise predictor (conditioned on timestep embedding and hyper-latent ẑ) to estimate the noise, regularizing the latent space structure via a diffusion-inspired objective at zero inference cost. The total loss combines the standard rate-distortion objective with orthogonality and DaR regularization terms (λ_OLP=0.1, λ_DaR=0.01).

## Results

SAAF achieves the best BD-rate over VTM-9.1 on all three evaluation datasets: −17.40% on Kodak, −17.35% on CLIC, and −20.57% on Tecnick. It also attains the lowest inference latency (67 ms) among all compared methods, outperforming the closest RD competitor DCAE (74 ms) by 7 ms while maintaining comparable FLOPs (434 G vs. 427 G) and parameters (123 M vs. 119 M). The ablation study confirms that each proposed module contributes: SAB reduces latency from 61 ms to 52 ms while improving BD-rate, AFB adds substantial BD-rate gains with only +4 ms latency, and DaR improves BD-rate at zero additional inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAAF outperforms all 11 baselines on BD-rate across Kodak, CLIC, and Tecnick. The closest competitor is DCAE (CVPR'25), which SAAF beats by 0.40% on Kodak (−17.40 vs. −17.00), 0.37% on CLIC (−17.35 vs. −16.98), and 0.46% on Tecnick (−20.57 vs. −20.11), while also reducing latency from 74 ms to 67 ms. Among SSM-based methods, LALIC (−15.30% Kodak) and MambaIC (−14.47% Kodak) trail SAAF by 2.1% and 2.9% respectively on Kodak. FLIC (ICLR'24), which also uses frequency-aware transforms, is surpassed by SAAF by 2.8% on Kodak and 6.2% on CLIC, highlighting the advantage of content-adaptive frequency processing over fixed frequency transforms. On the efficiency axis, SAAF's 67 ms latency is 144 ms faster than MLIC++ (211 ms), 66 ms faster than TCM (133 ms), and 52 ms faster than FLIC (119 ms), making it the fastest method with top RD performance. AuxT, which shares the spatial-frequency dual-path design, is outperformed by SAAF by 7.2% on Kodak and 10.6% on Tecnick, demonstrating the gains from sparse attention and the denoising regularizer.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAAF builds directly on the hyperprior autoencoder framework (Ballé et al., 2018) and adopts the spatial-frequency dual-path architecture introduced by AuxT (Li et al., 2025), including its Orthogonal Linear Projection layer. Its spatial path departs from standard Window-based Multi-head Self-attention (inherited from Swin Transformer) by replacing it with Cross-Sparse Window Attention, which uses a small set of window-conditioned learnable global tokens to capture cross-window dependencies at lower cost. The frequency path extends fixed wavelet-like transforms used in prior LIC work (WeConvene, FLIC) into a fully content-adaptive mechanism via a Decomposition Weight Generator. The Denoising-as-Regularizer module is a novel training-only addition that borrows the conditional denoising objective from diffusion models to regularize the latent space, a concept not previously applied to LIC.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The simultaneous achievement of best-in-class BD-rate and lowest inference latency (67 ms) across three standard benchmarks addresses a persistent tension in LIC between compression quality and practical deployability, making the method relevant for real-time applications. The sparse global token mechanism and the zero-cost training regularizer are modular ideas that could transfer to other learned compression or representation-learning settings. However, the BD-rate margins over the closest competitor (DCAE) are modest (~0.4 percentage points), and the ablation was conducted on a reduced training set (30k vs 300k images), leaving open whether the full gains hold at scale. Whether the adaptive frequency block generalizes beyond still images to video or 3D content remains unexplored.
