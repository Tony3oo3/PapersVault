## Problem

Current 3D indoor layout generation models are constrained by the limited scale, diversity, and annotation quality of existing datasets. No prior dataset provides comprehensive layout annotations covering large furniture, small objects, and decorative items paired with structured textual descriptions of global scene organization and fine-grained spatial relations, leaving a critical gap for text-conditioned layout and scene generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263716/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a structured text description with three hierarchical levels—global scene summary (room type, style, geometric features, functional zoning), large furniture placement (absolute positioning and relative spatial relations), and small object arrangements (surface placement and distribution patterns)—and produces a set of N oriented 3D bounding boxes, each parameterized by semantic class, 3D center (x, y, z), dimensions (w, h, d), and yaw rotation θ. The dataset spans 21,367 layouts with 433,842 object instances averaging 20.3 objects per scene, ranging from minimalistic 5-object arrangements to densely populated 26+ object scenes.

## Method

M3DLayout integrates three complementary sources—Matterport3D real-world scans, 3D-FRONT professional CAD designs, and a newly created Inf3DLayout subset procedurally generated via Infinigen for five common room types—then annotates each layout with structured descriptions through a multi-stage pipeline: GPT-4o processes multi-view renders (top-down, side, close-up) for scan and procedural scenes, while rule-based template extraction handles the simpler 3D-FRONT layouts. For benchmarking, the authors train two baselines: a diffusion model following DiffuScene's denoising framework with a noise-prediction objective and IoU-based collision penalty, and an autoregressive transformer that factorizes the layout likelihood sequentially, conditioning each object prediction on the text and all previously generated objects. The Inf3DLayout subset is the key differentiator, contributing 68.5% small objects and 26.8 objects per scene on average, enabling generation of far more complex and detailed scenes than prior datasets support.

## Results

DIFF-M3DLayout achieves FID of 87.89 on Matterport3D and 70.85 on Inf3DLayout, substantially outperforming DiffuScene (98.03 and 102.12) and InstructScene (100.54 and 159.27). AR-M3DLayout attains the best FID (57.90) and KID (41.04) on Inf3DLayout, demonstrating strong generalization to procedurally generated scenes. Both proposed methods surpass all baselines on CLIP-Score (0.2001 and 0.2026 vs. 0.1982 and 0.1944), indicating superior text-scene alignment. On 3D-FRONT, DIFF-M3DLayout trails DiffuScene (FID 57.64 vs. 29.47) due to a mismatch in object count distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DiffuScene, DIFF-M3DLayout wins decisively on Matterport3D (FID 87.89 vs. 98.03; KID 34.62 vs. 47.92) and Inf3DLayout (FID 70.85 vs. 102.12; KID 50.94 vs. 75.49), but loses on 3D-FRONT (FID 57.64 vs. 29.47; KID 36.80 vs. 10.32) because 3D-FRONT scenes contain only 5–12 objects while the model typically generates 12+. Against InstructScene, both proposed methods dominate: DIFF-M3DLayout improves FID by 12.7 points on Matterport and 88.4 points on Inf3DLayout, while AR-M3DLayout achieves FID 57.90 on Inf3DLayout versus InstructScene's 159.27. Both methods also surpass both baselines on CLIP-Score (0.2001/0.2026 vs. 0.1982/0.1944), confirming stronger prompt controllability. The paper attributes the 3D-FRONT gap to scene-complexity mismatch rather than inferior spatial modeling, and notes that the richer object counts align better with the multi-source M3DLayout distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

M3DLayout positions itself as a dataset resource that fills a specific gap in the 3D indoor layout generation literature: prior datasets (ScanNet, Matterport3D, 3D-FRONT, Structured3D) either provide real-scanned geometry without fine-grained object annotations or offer professionally designed layouts lacking small objects and scene-level textual descriptions. The paper draws on Infinigen's procedural generation pipeline to create the Inf3DLayout subset, and on GPT-4o-based rendering-to-text annotation to produce structured descriptions, combining ideas from procedural scene synthesis (Infinigen, ProcTHOR) and LLM-assisted annotation (FlairGPT, HoloDeck). For the generation baselines, it adapts the diffusion framework of DiffuScene and a transformer-based autoregressive approach in the spirit of ATISS and SceneFormer, but the core contribution is the multi-source dataset and its annotation pipeline rather than a new generative architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset's multi-source composition (real scans, professional designs, procedural generation) and its 60.8% small-object proportion address a concrete bottleneck: prior layout datasets were too sparse in small-object detail to train models that generate richly furnished scenes. The density-controllability demonstration via text prompts is a practical feature for downstream applications like interior design. However, the baselines are limited to two prior methods (DiffuScene, InstructScene), the 3D-FRONT FID/KID results are actually worse than DiffuScene due to object-count mismatch, and the paper stops at layout generation without demonstrating full 3D scene synthesis, leaving open whether the structured descriptions transfer effectively to geometry-level generation. The reliance on GPT-4o for annotation also raises questions about systematic biases in the generated text.
