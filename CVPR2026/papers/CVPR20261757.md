## Problem

Generative 3D models can produce striking vehicle shapes from text or images, but converting these into aerodynamically efficient, regulation-compliant designs still requires weeks of high-fidelity CFD and manual iteration. Existing pipelines address only one leg of the problem—generation-only, surrogate-only, or CFD-only—without a coherent, budget-aware loop that a styling team can run end-to-end. The paper asks how an AI agent can close the full generation-to-physics-to-decision loop under a strict CFD budget.

## Input / Output

The system accepts heterogeneous design intent—pure text prompts, real-world vehicle photos, text-conditioned images, or three-view drawings—and internally normalizes all inputs to a fixed front three-quarter viewpoint with body type restricted to five classes (sedan, SUV, MPV, pickup, sport car). Each candidate is packaged as a standardized watertight STL (X-forward, Y-width, Z-up, units in metres) plus a meta.json carrying bounding box, wheelbase, coordinate transform, mesh-health summary, and FFD edit history. The physics module consumes these meshes on shared 3D grids and outputs a scalar drag coefficient, surface pressure, and volumetric velocity fields; the decision module returns a refined candidate pool and a top-K set (K ≤ B_hf) flagged for high-fidelity CFD confirmation.

## Method

AeroAgent unifies three modules around a single editable 3D surface representation. The vision module converts text or 2D references into diverse standardized STL candidates via a diffusion-based text-to-image model (Nano Banana) and Hunyuan3D 3.0 image-to-3D reconstruction, followed by PCA axis alignment, regulatory size projection, and parameterized free-form deformation (FFD) for centimetre-scale mesh edits. The physics module, AeroFormer, is a mesh-free, geometry-guided Transformer that decouples a volumetric Cartesian grid branch from a surface-geometry branch and fuses them through linear cross-attention (kernel-map formulation reducing complexity to near O((N_vol + N_surf)C²)), with three independently trained heads predicting drag, surface pressure, and velocity on shared 3D grids; it is trained on ~50k Lattice-Boltzmann CFD simulations across five body classes. The decision module enforces regulatory size, drag-threshold, and LLM-scored aesthetic feasibility as concurrent hard constraints, uses a curated bank of low-drag prototype priors plus surrogate field sensitivities to select FFD actions, and runs a budget-aware propose-evaluate-refine loop in which only the final top-K shapes are confirmed by high-fidelity CFD while all inner iterations rely solely on the surrogate.

## Results

On the held-out test set (100 cases across five vehicle classes), AeroFormer achieves the lowest relative L₂ error on pressure (0.1072) and velocity (0.0279) fields, and the highest R² on drag coefficient (0.9484), outperforming all five baselines. In the five-step propose-evaluate-refine loop, AeroAgent reduces CFD-verified drag by 2–12% on average across five body classes (up to 23.1% for a sports car) while cutting high-fidelity CFD calls by 50–80%. Generalization is strong: R² for Cd prediction exceeds 0.96 in every class, and per-shape agreement on 50 SUVs reaches R² = 0.98 with no systematic bias.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AeroFormer wins on every metric against all five baselines. Against 3D-GeoCA [6] (the closest competitor on pressure), AeroFormer reduces relative L₂ by 40.3% (0.1072 vs. 0.1853) and improves Cd R² from 0.9310 to 0.9484. Against Transolver [41], the gains are 40.3% on pressure L₂ and 59.0% on velocity L₂ (0.0279 vs. 0.0681), with Cd R² rising from 0.9134 to 0.9484. Transolver++ [26] and TripNet [5] are notably weaker on velocity fields (L₂ of 0.0775 and 0.1007 respectively vs. AeroFormer's 0.0279), and both are restricted to volumetric outputs without surface-field prediction. AB-UPT [1] shows the largest gap on Cd (R² 0.7821 vs. 0.9484), suggesting its anchored-branched architecture struggles with the multi-class vehicle geometry distribution. In the scalability comparison (Table 2), AeroFormer also uses less GPU memory and achieves higher throughput than both 3D-GeoCA and Transolver at 5×10⁶ points, confirming that the linear-attention design scales favorably.

## Relation to Prior Work

AeroAgent sits at the intersection of three previously separate research threads: generative 3D shape synthesis (Hunyuan3D, text-to-3D diffusion), neural CFD surrogates (Transolver, 3D-GeoCA, TripNet), and classical aerodynamic shape optimization (adjoint methods, FFD). The physics module explicitly builds on Transolver's slicing/aggregation encoder for the surface branch and adopts linear attention to scale to million-point domains, while the vision module wraps existing commercial image-to-3D pipelines. The key departure is the integration itself: prior work treats generation, surrogate prediction, and design optimization as disconnected stages, whereas AeroAgent closes them into a single budget-aware propose-evaluate-refine loop where the surrogate drives all inner iterations and high-fidelity CFD is reserved for final confirmation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial for automotive styling teams: a 50–80% reduction in expensive CFD runs while still achieving 2–12% drag reduction in only five iterations could compress weeks of iteration into hours. The 50k-sample Lattice-Boltzmann dataset spanning five body classes is a valuable public resource for training vehicle-specific flow surrogates. However, the framework is limited to five passenger-vehicle classes, the aesthetic predicate relies on an LLM image scorer without user-study validation, and generalization to trucks, buses, or extreme aero concepts is untested. Open questions include whether the surrogate's accuracy degrades under more complex flow regimes (e.g., crosswinds, open windows) and whether the agent's edit strategy scales to multi-objective optimization beyond drag and size.
