## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction methods are limited by one-shot prediction: they lack scene-specific refinement and cannot incorporate generative priors without sacrificing their second-scale inference speed. Optimization-based pipelines (e.g., 3DGS with diffusion enhancement) achieve higher quality but require lengthy test-time gradient descent and suffer under sparse views. The core gap is how to enable iterative, scene-adaptive refinement and stable generative prior injection while preserving purely feed-forward efficiency.

## Input / Output

The method takes sparse unposed RGB images as input—2 views at 256×256 (RealEstate10K, DTU) or 8 views at 448×448 (DL3DV)—with no camera intrinsics or extrinsics provided. It outputs a full 3D Gaussian Splatting scene (per-Gaussian position, scale, orientation, color, and opacity) along with estimated camera poses, all produced in seconds of forward-only inference without any test-time gradient optimization.

## Method

GIFSplat uses a two-stage architecture: a fine-tuned AnySplat-based initializer (with voxelization removed) predicts camera poses and an initial 3DGS, followed by a single weight-shared iterative Gaussian head that applies T=3 forward-only residual updates. At each step, observation cues are computed as frozen-feature differences between input and rendered views, pooled to Gaussians via front-to-back rasterization weights; a frozen DiFiX diffusion enhancer then refines rendered views, and the feature-space enhancement delta is similarly pooled into Gaussian-level prior cues. The iterative head concatenates current Gaussian state with both cue types and predicts per-Gaussian residuals (position, scale, color, opacity) using window attention over local 3D neighborhoods rather than image tokens. Unlike concurrent work iLRM that unrolls non-shared transformer layers per step, GIFSplat reuses one module across all iterations, keeping parameter count constant while allowing flexible step count at inference. Training is two-stage: the initializer is trained with reconstruction and geometric distillation losses, then frozen while the iterative head is trained end-to-end by unrolling T steps with weighted per-step reconstruction loss.

## Results

On RealEstate10K (2 input views, averaged across overlap settings), GIFSplat achieves 26.56 dB PSNR, 0.867 SSIM, and 0.138 LPIPS, outperforming the strongest pose-free baseline AnySplat (25.18 dB PSNR) by +1.38 dB. On DL3DV (8 input views), GIFSplat reaches 24.91 dB PSNR and 0.824 SSIM, surpassing AnySplat (23.76 dB) by +1.15 dB. On the out-of-domain DTU benchmark (2 views, model trained only on RealEstate10K), GIFSplat attains 20.21 dB PSNR, a +2.09 dB gain over AnySplat (18.12 dB), confirming strong cross-domain generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GIFSplat outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and metric. Among pose-free methods, the closest competitors are AnySplat (+1.38 dB PSNR on RealEstate10K avg, +1.15 dB on DL3DV, +2.09 dB on DTU), NoPoSplat (+1.53 dB on RealEstate10K avg), and FLARE (+1.67 dB on RealEstate10K avg). The generative prior contributes an additional +0.27 dB PSNR and −0.007 LPIPS over the observation-only IFSplat variant, with the largest perceptual gains (LPIPS) in sparse-view and out-of-domain settings. Pose-required methods (PixelSplat, MVSplat) trail the pose-free baselines by roughly 1–2 dB on RealEstate10K, while older pose-free methods (DUSt3R, MAST3R, Splatt3R) lag by over 10 dB, highlighting the maturity of recent feed-forward 3DGS approaches. On the out-of-domain DTU benchmark, GIFSplat's advantage over AnySplat widens to +2.09 dB PSNR, demonstrating that the iterative refinement and diffusion prior are particularly effective under domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

GIFSplat builds directly on AnySplat's feed-forward 3DGS architecture as its initializer (removing voxelization and fine-tuning with ground-truth poses), then departs from the one-shot prediction paradigm by introducing a weight-shared iterative residual head that applies T forward-only updates. Conceptually, the iterative refinement draws on the recurrent-update lineage of RAFT and DROID-SLAM, while the generative prior fusion departs from optimization-based pipelines like Difix3D+ and GenFusion by distilling a frozen diffusion enhancer into Gaussian-level feature-space cues rather than feeding enhanced views back into a training loop. This positions GIFSplat as a bridge between feed-forward efficiency and per-scene optimization quality, uniquely combining pose-free operation, iterative refinement, and prior injection without test-time gradients or view-set expansion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a small number (T=3) of forward-only residual steps can close a meaningful gap between one-shot feed-forward and optimization-based 3DGS reconstruction while preserving seconds-scale inference, which is practically important for real-time applications like AR/VR and robotic perception. The generative prior fusion mechanism—converting diffusion-enhanced renderings into lightweight per-Gaussian cues without backpropagation or view explosion—offers a reusable pattern for injecting generative knowledge into feed-forward 3D pipelines. Open questions include scalability to unbounded or very large scenes, whether the approach benefits from more than 3 iterations in complex environments, and how the frozen DiFiX dependency affects robustness when the diffusion prior is itself out-of-distribution.
