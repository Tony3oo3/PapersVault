## Problem

Existing geoscience and remote sensing benchmarks are narrow in scope, focusing primarily on perception tasks over optical imagery while ignoring multi-disciplinary knowledge, diverse sensor modalities (SAR, HSI, LiDAR, DEM, thermal), and high-level spatial reasoning. This leaves a critical gap in measuring whether MLLMs possess the expert-level, multi-sensor, multi-disciplinary capabilities required for professional geospatial interpretation, and no framework exists to augment MLLMs with the domain-specific tools that human experts rely on.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263181/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark item pairs a geospatial image—which may be raw sensor data, a thematic map, a diagram, a table, a plot, or an RS mapping product—with a multiple-choice text question requiring domain expertise across four disciplines (RS, photogrammetry, GIS, GNSS) and four task levels (theoretical, low-level perception, mid-level recognition, high-level application). Models are evaluated zero-shot under a unified prompt, and answers are extracted via rule-based regex matching of option letters or numerical values; the primary metric is micro-averaged accuracy over 1,016 test questions, with a 37-question validation set reserved for human-expert calibration and model selection.

## Method

GeoMMAgent follows a plan-execute-evaluate paradigm in which a unified coordinator first decomposes an image-query pair into structured subgoals with explicit tool and dependency specifications. Subtasks are dispatched to three categories of specialized agents: retrieval agents (Wikipedia/Google search plus GME multimodal retrieval for evidence grounding), perception agents (supervised RS models for object detection, scene classification, and land-cover segmentation), and reasoning agents (Qwen-VL-Max for complex logical inference). A self-evaluation module then reviews the reasoning trace against the original plan, triggering selective re-execution if inconsistencies are detected. All tools are implemented under the Model Context Protocol (MCP) for modular, scalable integration of new domain-specific components, and the entire pipeline operates without fine-tuning the underlying LLMs.

## Results

On GeoMMBench (1,053 expert-level multiple-choice questions), GeoMMAgent achieves 88.4% micro-averaged accuracy on the test set, matching the human expert validation accuracy of 86.5% and surpassing all 36 evaluated MLLMs. The best closed-source model, Gemini-1.5 Pro, reaches 70.7% test accuracy, while the best open-source model, Qwen3-VL-30B, achieves 66.7%. Random guessing yields 25.2%, and the text-only GPT-4 baseline scores only 31.2%, confirming that visual input is essential but insufficient for expert-level geospatial reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoMMAgent (88.4% test) outperforms every evaluated MLLM by a wide margin, beating the best closed-source model Gemini-1.5 Pro (70.7%) by 17.7 points and the best open-source model Qwen3-VL-30B (66.7%) by 21.7 points. Among geoscience-specialized models, VHM (39.1%), GeoChat (35.4%), and TeoChat (31.1%) all fall far below general-purpose MLLMs, indicating that narrow perception-focused fine-tuning does not transfer to expert-level multidisciplinary reasoning. Notably, GPT-5 (46.1%) underperforms GPT-4o (68.5%) by over 22 points, suggesting regression in geospatial multimodal capability. The text-only GPT-4 baseline (31.2%) confirms that visual input is critical, yet even the strongest vision-language models remain 18+ points below human expert performance (86.5% val), a gap that GeoMMAgent effectively closes through its retrieval-perception-reasoning multi-agent pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of MLLM evaluation and geoscience, departing from prior RS benchmarks (GeoChat-Bench, EarthVQA, VRSBench, TeoChat-Bench) that are confined to optical imagery and perception-level tasks. Conceptually, it extends the Expert AGI benchmarking paradigm established by MMMU and ScienceQA into a domain where multi-sensor integration, spatiotemporal reasoning, and cross-disciplinary knowledge (photogrammetry, GIS, GNSS) are essential. The GeoMMAgent framework repurposes the broader multi-agent/RAG literature—tool-augmented LLMs with retrieval, perception, and reasoning modules—into a domain-specific orchestration system, but does not build on a single prior agent architecture; rather, it composes existing components (GME for retrieval, supervised RS models for perception, Qwen-VL-Max for reasoning) under a plan-execute-evaluate loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a substantial and systematic gap: the best general-purpose MLLM (Gemini-1.5 Pro, 70.7%) trails human experts (86.5%) by over 15 points, and domain-specialized RS models (GeoChat, TeoChat, LHRS-Bot) perform markedly worse than general-purpose models, challenging the assumption that fine-tuning on RS data improves broader geospatial reasoning. The visual-linguistic misalignment finding—that models possess correct textual knowledge but fail to ground it in the image—points to a concrete architectural bottleneck. Open questions include whether GeoMMAgent's 88.4% (surpassing human experts) reflects genuine reasoning or retrieval-dependent lookup, whether the 1,053-question benchmark is sufficiently large for stable rankings, and whether the multi-agent paradigm generalizes beyond the specific tool set tested.
