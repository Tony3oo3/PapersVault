## Problem

Existing VLA models perform intermediate reasoning in the vision-language input space (e.g., predicting sub-tasks or synthesizing goal images), which creates a semantic-kinematic gap: the rich, abstract representations from web-scale pre-training are poorly aligned with the granular, low-level motor commands required for precise manipulation. This paper argues that the most effective form of deliberation for a robot policy is one that operates directly in the action space, providing kinematically coherent guidance rather than indirect semantic or visual cues.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB frame (resized to 224×224) and a natural language instruction as input, encoded by a SigLIP visual encoder and a Gemma 2B LLM backbone (18 layers, d=2048). It outputs a continuous robot action sequence of horizon H=10 (action shift 1) via a flow-matching denoising head. Additionally, the EAR internally generates a coarser reference trajectory of horizon H^ref=15 (action shift 2) that serves as explicit guidance for the final policy, though this reference is not directly executed.

## Method

ACoT-VLA introduces two complementary action-space reasoners built on a shared VLM backbone. The Explicit Action Reasoner (EAR) is a lightweight 18-layer transformer that, via self-attention over the action sequence and cross-attention with the VLM's per-layer KV cache, synthesizes a coarse reference trajectory trained with flow matching; this trajectory is projected into an explicit action embedding Z^ex. The Implicit Action Reasoner (IAR) applies cross-attention between learnable queries and downsampled (d'=128) VLM KV cache at each layer, pooling the results into a compact implicit action prior Z^im. The Action-Guided Prediction (AGP) head then performs dual cross-attention—using the noisy action embedding as query against both Z^ex and Z^im—followed by self-attention fusion before the final flow-matching denoising step. Training uses a combined flow-matching MSE loss for both EAR and the action head, with a teacher-forcing stabilization trick that feeds ground-truth reference trajectories into Z^ex during training to prevent optimization interference, switching to fully self-conditioned generation at inference.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, ACoT-VLA achieves a 98.5% average success rate (both with frozen and unfrozen LLM backbones), surpassing the previous best method VLA-Adapter (97.3%) by 1.2% and π_{0.5} (96.9%) by 1.6%. On LIBERO-Plus under zero-shot transfer, the method reaches 86.6% average, outperforming π_{0.5}* (85.7%), and under supervised fine-tuning it achieves 88.0% versus 75.7% for π_{0.5}†. On VLABench, ACoT-VLA attains the best Intention Score (63.5%) and Progress Score (47.4%), with particularly large gains on the unseen-texture track (+12.6% IS over π_{0.5}†). In real-world deployment on AgiBot G1, the method achieves 66.7% average success rate versus 61.0% for π_{0.5} and 33.8% for π_{0}.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACoT-VLA outperforms all 27 baselines on the LIBERO benchmark, achieving 98.5% average success rate. Against the strongest visual-guidance methods (GE-Act at 96.5%, F1 at 95.7%, UniVLA at 95.5%), the improvement is 2.0–3.0%, with the largest gap on the Long-horizon suite (96.0–97.0% vs. 94.4% for GE-Act). Against the strongest linguistic-guidance methods (VLA-Adapter at 97.3%, OpenVLA-OFT at 97.1%, π_{0.5} at 96.9%), the gain is 1.2–1.6%, with the most pronounced advantage on Long-horizon tasks (96.0–97.0% vs. 95.0% for VLA-Adapter and 92.4% for π_{0.5}). On LIBERO-Plus zero-shot transfer, ACoT-VLA (86.6%) edges out π_{0.5}* (85.7%) and substantially surpasses all other methods (next best: OpenVLA-OFT at 69.6%), showing particular robustness to robot initial-state (+3.2%) and language-variation (+4.2%) perturbations. On VLABench, the method leads π_{0.5}† by 3.3% in IS and 4.3% in PS, with the largest margin on the unseen-texture track (+12.6% IS). In real-world experiments, ACoT-VLA achieves 66.7% average success, beating π_{0.5} by 5.7% and π_{0} by 32.9%, and generalizes across embodiments (AgiBot G1 and AgileX).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the π_{0.5} architecture (SigLIP visual encoder + Gemma 2B LLM backbone + flow-matching action head) and departs from two established paradigms for intermediate reasoning in VLA models: language-level CoT (sub-task prediction, as in TraceVLA, ThinkAct) and visual-level CoT (goal-image synthesis, as in CoT-VLA, WorldVLA, DreamVLA). The key conceptual departure is relocating the 'thinking' process from the input space (language or vision) into the action space itself, where the Explicit Action Reasoner synthesizes coarse reference trajectories (analogous to self-conditioning in generative models) and the Implicit Action Reasoner extracts latent action priors from the VLM's internal key-value cache via cross-attention. The Action-Guided Prediction head then fuses both explicit and implicit action-space signals through dual cross-attention before the final denoising step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ACoT paradigm offers a principled argument that the semantic-kinematic gap in current VLA models is best bridged by reasoning directly in the action space rather than through indirect linguistic or visual intermediaries, which could reshape how future generalist robot policies are designed. However, several open questions remain: the EAR relies on teacher forcing during training (ground-truth trajectories), and the paper does not deeply analyze failure modes when the self-generated reference at inference is poor; the approach is validated primarily on π_{0.5} with a specific VLM backbone, leaving generalizability to other architectures unclear; and real-world evaluation covers only three tasks on two platforms. The modest but consistent gains across LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and VLABench suggest the action-space guidance is a useful inductive bias, but whether it scales to substantially more complex or longer-horizon tasks remains to be seen.
