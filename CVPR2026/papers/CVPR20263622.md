## Problem

Existing hybrid generative simulators like WonderPlay decouple physical state from visual representation, so generative refinements from video models cannot propagate back to update the underlying physics. This prevents long-horizon sequential interactions, as the physical simulator remains blind to corrections from the previous time window, causing errors to accumulate and the scene to degrade over multiple action steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263622/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a sequence of physical actions (local forces such as pushes/pokes specified as f(t), or global forces such as gravity and wind fields specified as f(x,y,z,t)) and produces a dynamic 4D scene sequence renderable from arbitrary viewpoints. Each simulation window spans 392 physics steps, with the video generation model operating at 704×1280 resolution over 49 frames (sampled every 8 physics steps). The scene state is decomposed into a static background and dynamic foreground objects, and the system supports multiple sequential windows (typically 3) with different actions per window.

## Method

PerpetualWonder introduces the Visual-Physical Aligned Particle (VPP), a unified representation where each physics particle p_j anchors K Gaussian primitives via learnable position offsets (μ_{j,k} = p_j + tanh(p̃_{j,k})·δ), creating a bidirectional bridge: the forward physics pass moves particles to drive appearance, while the backward optimization refines Gaussian attributes to correct dynamics. The closed-loop simulation iterates between a forward physics pass (using Genesis solvers for cloth, sand, liquid, smoke, elastic, and rigid bodies) and a backward neural optimization that refines the coarse 4D scene using a video generation model conditioned on RGB and optical flow. To resolve multi-view ambiguity, the method initializes a full 3D scene via GEN3C dense view synthesis + COLMAP + 3DGS, then applies progressive multi-view optimization: first optimizing from the input viewpoint, then adding side views with reduced control weight, and finally jointly optimizing from all three views. A simulation consistency loss (L_sim) penalizes Gaussian primitives for deviating from their physics particle anchors, and loop closure updates physics particle positions by averaging optimized Gaussian positions at the end of each window, enabling perpetual sequential interaction.

## Results

On 10 diverse scenes evaluated with World-Score metrics, PerpetualWonder achieves the best camera controllability (93.26) and 3D consistency (80.41), substantially outperforming the next-best methods (GEN3C at 80.29 for controllability, Veo3.1 at 73.93 for consistency). Its imaging quality (66.98) is competitive with top video generators (Veo3.1: 67.82, Wan2.2: 67.03) while far exceeding the hybrid simulator WonderPlay (36.80). In a 2AFC human study, participants prefer PerpetualWonder over all six baselines on both physics plausibility (62.0%–93.5%) and motion fidelity (70.8%–86.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PerpetualWonder decisively outperforms all six baselines on camera controllability and 3D consistency, the two metrics most critical for action-conditioned 4D generation. Against the closest hybrid simulator baseline WonderPlay, it gains +17.3 in controllability, +16.5 in 3D consistency, and a massive +30.2 in imaging quality, reflecting WonderPlay's severe artifacts from single-view optimization. Among video generators, GEN3C is the strongest on controllability (80.29) but still trails by 13 points, while Veo3.1 leads on 3D consistency (73.93) yet falls 6.5 points short. On imaging quality, PerpetualWonder (66.98) is within 0.8 of the best (Veo3.1 at 67.82) and essentially tied with Wan2.2 (67.03), showing that the physics-driven approach does not sacrifice visual fidelity. In the human study, PerpetualWonder is preferred over every baseline on both physics plausibility and motion fidelity, with the largest margins over GEN3C (93.5%/83.5%) and WonderPlay (80.8%/86.3%), and the smallest over Veo3.1 (62.0%/70.8%), confirming that the closed-loop VPP representation and multi-view optimization yield more physically plausible and temporally coherent dynamics than any single-view or non-3D approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends WonderPlay's hybrid generative simulator paradigm (physics solver for coarse dynamics + video diffusion model for visual refinement) by solving its fundamental architectural flaw: the decoupling between physical state and visual representation. The key departure is the Visual-Physical Aligned Particle (VPP), which binds each physics particle to a set of Gaussian primitives via learnable offsets, creating a bidirectional bridge that allows generative refinements to correct the underlying dynamics. The 3D scene initialization pipeline repurposes GEN3C for dense view synthesis, COLMAP for point cloud extraction, SAM2 for object segmentation, and TSDFusion for mesh-based particle sampling, assembling these into a unified multi-view reconstruction that prior single-view methods lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PerpetualWonder establishes the first true closed-loop hybrid generative simulator capable of sequential, long-horizon interactions, a capability that was architecturally impossible in prior decoupled designs. The VPP representation and progressive multi-view optimization are general-purpose components that could be adopted by other physics-grounded generation systems. Open questions include scalability to scenes with topological changes (object creation/destruction), real-time performance given the iterative optimization loop, and whether the reliance on pre-trained video models for refinement limits the range of physically novel phenomena that can be simulated.
