## Problem

Existing supervised and unsupervised homography estimation methods achieve high accuracy only when trained and tested on the same modality, with performance degrading substantially on unseen modalities. Collecting large amounts of aligned multimodal image pairs with ground-truth offsets is costly and impractical, and current networks neglect cross-scale feature interactions while entangling color information into their representations, further limiting multimodal robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263534/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network takes a pair of unaligned images (source and target, each 3×S×S) and outputs four corner-point offsets (4×2) that parameterize the homography matrix via direct linear transform. Offsets are refined iteratively over K=2 steps at three spatial scales (S×S, S/2×S/2, S/4×S/4), with each iteration producing a residual offset added to the cumulative estimate. During training, ground-truth offsets are integer perturbations in {−p, …, p} applied to the source image before center-cropping.

## Method

The paper contributes two components. First, a training data synthesis pipeline renders a single content image in multiple styles using a pretrained style-transfer network (IEContraAST) with randomized content weights and smoothing parameters, producing diverse unaligned image pairs with known offsets from unimodal RGB sources (MSCOCO + Painter by Numbers), enabling zero-shot multimodal estimation. Second, CCNet is a cross-scale and color-invariant estimation network: its multiscale extractor fuses features in both top-to-bottom (residual blocks with max-pooling concatenation) and bottom-to-top (nearest-neighbor upsampling with convolutional fusion) directions; a color-decoupling module splits each scale's features into a color representation and a color-invariant representation, enforced by a color-histogram reconstruction loss and a cosine-similarity orthogonality loss; and an iterative estimation module performs multiscale correlation searching over the color-invariant features, updating residual offsets over K iterations at each scale before aggregating from coarse to fine.

## Results

On the within-dataset evaluation (MACE ↓), CCNet achieves 0.184, 0.526, 2.992, and 1.001 on GoogleMap, GoogleEarth, RGB-NIR, and PDSCOCO respectively, outperforming the best supervised baseline MCNet (0.261, 0.577, 3.226, 1.062) by 29.50%, 8.83%, 7.25%, and 5.74%. In the zero-shot setting (trained only on synthetic data from MSCOCO), CCNet records 4.383, 1.399, 4.461, and 1.368, surpassing MCNet's zero-shot results (5.093, 1.402, 5.239, 1.423) by up to 14.85% on RGB-NIR. The data synthesis method also improves generalization of existing baselines by 1.93%–93.17% in cross-dataset evaluation, with over 50% improvement in roughly half of the cases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CCNet outperforms all four supervised baselines (DHN, MHN, IHN, MCNet) on every dataset in both within-dataset and zero-shot settings, with the largest margin over MCNet being 29.50% on GoogleMap (0.184 vs. 0.261 MACE). In the zero-shot setting, CCNet also surpasses the strongest unsupervised method SSHNet on GoogleEarth (1.399 vs. 5.888), RGB-NIR (4.461 vs. 6.743), and PDSCOCO (1.368 vs. 1.610), though SSHNet retains an advantage on GoogleMap (1.394 vs. 4.383) due to its image-translation-based modality unification. The data synthesis method, when applied as augmentation to existing datasets (IHN+, MCNet+), improves cross-dataset generalization by 8.82%–79.54% over the unaugmented versions, confirming its utility beyond zero-shot scenarios. Among unsupervised baselines, SCPNet and AltO show moderate cross-dataset performance but are substantially outperformed by CCNet's zero-shot results on most datasets. The cross-dataset tables (Tabs. 1–4) further demonstrate that baselines trained on a single real dataset (e.g., GoogleMap) suffer severe accuracy drops (MACE > 20) when tested on unseen modalities, whereas models trained on the synthetic data maintain MACE below 6 on all four test datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

CCNet extends the iterative homography estimation lineage (IHN, MCNet) by adding bidirectional cross-scale feature fusion and a color-decoupling mechanism borrowed from re-identification literature, while retaining the residual-offset refinement loop. The training data synthesis strategy departs from prior self-supervised approaches (SCPNet, SSHNet) that generate pseudo-unaligned pairs from existing modality-specific data; instead, it leverages a pretrained style transfer network (IEContraAST) to render a single content image in diverse textures and colors, producing supervised training pairs without any modality-specific collection. This positions the work at the intersection of style transfer and geometric estimation, repurposing appearance-variation tools to solve a generalization problem that prior supervised and unsupervised methods address only within a fixed modality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The zero-shot capability—training once on synthetic MSCOCO/Painter-by-Numbers data and estimating homographies on GoogleMap, GoogleEarth, RGB-NIR, and PDSCOCO without ever seeing those modalities—removes the costly and often infeasible requirement of collecting aligned multimodal pairs, which is a major practical bottleneck in the field. The synthesis method also functions as a plug-and-play augmentation that improves generalization of existing supervised models by up to ~80%, making it broadly applicable. Open questions include whether the approach extends to truly heterogeneous sensor pairs (e.g., LiDAR-to-RGB, thermal-to-visible) where structural correspondence is weaker, and whether the within-dataset accuracy trade-off observed when augmenting with synthetic data can be mitigated. The reliance on a fixed style transfer network also raises questions about how well the method handles modalities whose appearance differences are not well captured by 2D style transfer.
