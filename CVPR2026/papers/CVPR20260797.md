## Problem

Learned 3D point cloud registration methods remain strongly domain-dependent, degrading across sensing modalities, point densities, and environments, while no 3D foundation model analogous to image-domain VFMs yet exists. Prior generative registration approaches (e.g., GPCR) rely on single-view diffusion models that require fine-tuning to enforce multi-view consistency, limiting zero-shot generalization. C-GenReg addresses this gap by leveraging a pretrained World Foundation Model that produces multi-view-consistent RGB views from geometry out of the box, eliminating any retraining.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260797/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source point cloud P ∈ R^{N×3} and a target point cloud Q ∈ R^{M×3} (rendered as L=50 depth-frame sequences, or projected onto a virtual camera for LiDAR scans) plus a short text prompt providing coarse scene context (e.g., 'indoor scene'). The source and target depth sequences are temporally concatenated into a single video fed to the WFM. The output is a rigid transformation (R, t) ∈ SE(3) estimated via SC2PCR from a fused correspondence set obtained by mutual nearest-neighbor matching on the fused posterior matrix.

## Method

C-GenReg operates two parallel frozen branches: (1) a Generated-RGB Branch where Cosmos-Transfer WFM synthesizes multi-view-consistent RGB from the concatenated depth video, and MASt3R (a task-specific VFM for dense geometric matching) extracts 2D features from K=4 sampled view pairs per domain, which are lifted back to 3D via original depth; and (2) a Geometric Branch where GeoTransformer encodes per-point features directly from the raw point clouds. The key contribution is a 'Match-then-Fuse' probabilistic scheme: each branch independently computes a correspondence posterior via row-wise softmax on ℓ₂-normalized similarity matrices (with temperature τ=0.1), and the two posteriors are combined under a conditional-independence assumption using a Noisy-AND (joint posterior) formulation that favors correspondences jointly supported by both modalities. This preserves each modality's inductive bias and yields calibrated confidence without any additional learning, in contrast to prior feature-concatenation fusion strategies.

## Results

On the primary 3DMatch benchmark, C-GenReg achieves 98.3% rotation accuracy at 45° and a mean RRE of 3.8°, outperforming the strongest learned baseline GeoTransformer (93.3%, 12.0°) and the generative baseline GPCR (98.1%, 4.5°). For translation, C-GenReg reaches 95.7% accuracy at 25 cm with a mean RTE of 11.9 cm, roughly halving the error of GeoTransformer (24.6 cm). On the outdoor Waymo LiDAR benchmark, C-GenReg achieves 86.3% rotation accuracy at 5° and a mean RRE of 2.4°, substantially surpassing the best learned baseline Predator (65.1%, 10.0°), demonstrating the first successful generative registration on real outdoor LiDAR data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest point-cloud-only baseline GeoTransformer, C-GenReg improves mean RRE from 12.0° to 3.8° (3.2× reduction) and mean RTE from 24.6 cm to 11.9 cm (2.1× reduction), while GeoTransformer retains a marginal edge at the strict 5 cm translation threshold (59.8% vs. 57.5%) and median RTE (4.0 vs. 4.3 cm). Compared to the closest generative competitor GPCR, C-GenReg wins on 10° and 45° rotation accuracy (97.5% vs. 96.7%, 98.3% vs. 98.1%), mean RRE (3.8° vs. 4.5°), and all translation metrics (e.g., 95.7% vs. 93.1% at 25 cm), with GPCR holding a 0.1 pp edge only at 5° rotation. Against the RGB-D baselines ZeroMatch and PointMBF (which use real color input), C-GenReg—operating on point clouds alone—matches or exceeds ZeroMatch on rotation accuracy at 5° and 10° and on translation accuracy at all thresholds, while clearly outperforming PointMBF across every metric. On the outdoor Waymo LiDAR benchmark, C-GenReg dominates all learned baselines trained on KITTI, achieving 61.8% rotation accuracy at 1° versus at most 21.0% (Predator), confirming that the generative pipeline generalizes to unseen sensor modalities where learned 3D extractors fail.

## Relation to Prior Work

C-GenReg repurposes two recently emerged model families—World Foundation Models (Cosmos-Transfer) for multi-view-consistent RGB generation and task-specific Vision Foundation Models (MASt3R) for dense correspondence—into a registration pipeline, without fine-tuning either. It departs from GPCR, the closest prior generative registration method, by replacing fine-tuned single-view diffusion with a pretrained WFM that provides multi-view consistency out of the box, and by substituting simple feature concatenation with a principled Noisy-AND probabilistic fusion of independent correspondence posteriors. The geometric branch (GeoTransformer, FCGF, Predator) is treated as a swappable plug-in, positioning C-GenReg as a general performance enhancer layered on top of existing learned feature extractors rather than a replacement for them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework is the first generative registration pipeline to operate successfully on real outdoor LiDAR data (Waymo), where no RGB imagery exists, and it does so entirely zero-shot—no fine-tuning, no task-specific training. This opens a practical path for registration in sensor-modalities and environments where training data is scarce or unavailable. Key limitations include sensitivity to semantically incorrect text prompts, reliance on the WFM's geometric fidelity (which may degrade in highly structured or repetitive scenes), and the computational overhead of generating and processing K² view-pair combinations. Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic scenes, whether the conditional-independence assumption in the Noisy-AND fusion holds under severe domain shift, and whether a 3D foundation model (currently absent) would eventually subsume the geometry-to-image transfer entirely.
