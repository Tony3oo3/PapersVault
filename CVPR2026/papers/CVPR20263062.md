## Problem

Existing proactive forensics methods treat deepfake detection, tampering localization, and source tracing as independent or at best paired tasks, with bifunctional approaches relying on complex dual-decoder architectures and entirely neglecting the critical question of *where* a face has been manipulated. No prior method provides a unified, single-payload framework that simultaneously answers all three forensic questions—detection, localization, and tracing—while maintaining robustness, imperceptibility, and high payload capacity.

## Input / Output

At inference the framework operates in a fully blind-reference setting: it takes a single potentially manipulated RGB face image (128×128 or 256×256) and requires neither the original cover image nor ground-truth landmarks. It produces three outputs from one forward pass through the FHD: (1) a global real/fake verdict via an AED threshold (3.2375 px) on the intrinsic-extrinsic landmark consistency, (2) a region-level tampering map by computing per-facial-component AED against the same threshold, and (3) a 16-bit bipolar source identifier decoded by the classification head for provenance verification.

## Method

The core contribution is the 152-D LIDMark, a composite watermark that structurally interweaves a 136-D normalized 68-point facial landmark vector (tamper-sensitive geometric signal) with a 16-D bipolar source identifier derived from SHA-256 hashing of the filename. A two-stream encoder (image backbone + watermark backbone with DiffusionNet upsampling) fuses the cover image and watermark via channel concatenation and a global skip-connection to produce an imperceptible watermarked image. The novel Factorized-Head Decoder (FHD) uses a shared convolutional backbone (ConvBlocks + SEResNetDecoder) whose features are factorized into two parallel FC heads—a regression head for the 136-D landmarks and a classification head for the 16-D identifier—decoupling the heterogeneous sub-tasks while enabling joint optimization. Detection and localization are realized through an 'intrinsic-extrinsic' consistency check that compares FHD-recovered landmarks against landmarks re-detected from the manipulated image, while tracing is handled by the classification head. Training follows a two-stage adversarial protocol: pre-training on common distortions (resize, blur, JPEG) followed by fine-tuning on four deepfake models (SimSwap, UniFace, CSCS, StarGAN-v2) with a stochastic manipulation operator, a generative consistency loss, and a decoder stability loss to prevent catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

Under deepfake manipulations on CelebA-HQ (128×128), LIDMark achieves an average BER of 3.33% for source tracing and an AED of 1.58 px for landmark recovery, outperforming the best baseline DiffMark (3.52% BER) and KAD-NET (6.47% BER). Under common distortions, the average BER drops to 0.27% (128×128) and 0.10% (256×256), with AED of 1.17 px. For imperceptibility, LIDMark achieves PSNR of 40.22 dB / SSIM 0.98 at 128×128 and 44.31 dB / SSIM 0.99 at 256×256, surpassing all baselines while embedding a 152-D payload (vs. 30-D or 128-D for prior work). The 'intrinsic-extrinsic' consistency check yields an AUC of 0.9388 for deepfake detection with a decision threshold of 3.2375 px.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LIDMark outperforms all seven baselines on both BER (source tracing) and imperceptibility metrics. The closest competitor is DiffMark [31], which achieves 3.52% / 2.98% average BER under deepfakes at 128/256 resolution, compared to LIDMark's 3.33% / 2.55%; however, DiffMark degrades sharply under StarGAN-v2 (6.13% / 3.89% BER) while LIDMark also struggles there (16.42% / 8.47%) due to global attribute changes. KAD-NET [16] is the second-best baseline at 6.47% / 3.08% BER, roughly 2× worse than LIDMark. SepMark [40] and LampMark [38] show moderate performance (6.04% / 11.34% and 7.53% / 10.21% BER respectively), while EditGuard [45] and MBRS [21] degrade substantially (16.80% / 22.56% and 11.34% / 20.48%). CIN [24] performs worst overall (26.71% / 31.34% BER), failing catastrophically under UniFace and StarGAN-v2. On imperceptibility, LIDMark achieves the highest PSNR (40.22 / 44.31 dB) and SSIM (0.98 / 0.99) at both resolutions, exceeding LampMark [38] (40.20 / 42.52 dB) and DiffMark [31] (40.12 / 41.96 dB) despite embedding a 5× larger payload (152-D vs. 30-D).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the landmark-based watermarking paradigm introduced by LampMark [38], which uses facial landmarks for detection and localization, by fusing that tamper-sensitive geometric signal with a robust 16-D source identifier into a single 152-D payload. It departs from the prevailing dual-decoder architecture (SepMark, KAD-Net, WaveGuard) that requires two separate decoders tuned to different robustness levels, instead proposing a single Factorized-Head Decoder that splits shared backbone features into a regression head (landmarks) and a classification head (identifier). The 'intrinsic-extrinsic' consistency check for detection/localization is directly inherited from LampMark, while the robust tracing component draws on the broader deep-watermarking literature (FakeTagger, DiffMark).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying detection, localization, and tracing in a single model and single payload, LIDMark removes the architectural complexity of dual-decoder systems and provides a more practical forensic pipeline that answers all three fundamental questions from one inference pass. However, the framework is limited to facial regions and 2D images, and the StarGAN-v2 results (16.42% BER at 128×128) reveal vulnerability to global, attribute-based modifications that alter the entire image rather than just the face. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video, whether the 152-D payload can be further expanded without degrading imperceptibility, and how the method performs against future, unseen deepfake architectures beyond the five tested.
