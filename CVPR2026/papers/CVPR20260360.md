## Problem

Existing All-in-One Image Restoration methods rely on predefined degradation types and global textual or visual prompts, limiting their ability to handle real-world degradations that are compound, spatially heterogeneous, and label-agnostic. Diffusion-based approaches further suffer from detail loss due to VAE compression and the progressive nature of iterative sampling, producing blurred textures and incomplete structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260360/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded RGB image (processed at 512×512 resolution) and an optional text prompt generated by ChatGPT 4o or a manual task label. The degraded image is projected into latent space via a trainable VAE encoder to extract multi-scale degradation features, while the text prompt provides global semantic guidance. The output is a single restored high-quality RGB image; in zero-shot real-world evaluation (under-display camera), no text prompt is supplied, testing pure degradation-agnostic generalization.

## Method

UniLDiff builds on Stable Diffusion XL and introduces two key modules. The Degradation-Aware Feature Fusion (DAFF) module, inserted into early UNet layers, uses a cascaded double-stream + single-stream architecture (inspired by FLUX) to dynamically align low-quality features with evolving latent variables at every denoising timestep, with timestep-conditioned modulation controlling fusion strength to avoid interference in later stages. The Detail-Aware Expert Module (DAEM) sits in the VAE decoder and employs a Mixture-of-Experts design with NAFBlocks of varied receptive fields, routed by a lightweight top-k gate, combined with a shared transposed self-attention global branch to recover high-frequency details lost to VAE compression. Training proceeds in two stages: first, DAFF is trained (initially with frozen backbone, then jointly with the LQ encoder and denoising UNet) under a standard diffusion noise-prediction loss; second, DAEM is trained with a composite pixel-wise + SSIM + load-balancing loss to refine fine structures without disturbing the learned degradation priors.

## Results

On the three-task average (dehazing, deraining, denoising), UniLDiff achieves PSNR 32.18 dB, SSIM 0.9105, LPIPS 0.0651, DISTS 0.0639, MUSIQ 68.89, and MANIQA 0.7038, attaining the best scores on all perceptual and no-reference metrics while trailing the top non-diffusion methods (DFPIR, DA-RCOT) by ~0.4–0.6 dB in PSNR. On the five-task setup, UniLDiff leads in MUSIQ for dehazing (66.42), deraining (68.69), denoising (68.49), and deblurring (38.91), and in MANIQA for deraining (0.6903) and deblurring (0.4440). For composite degradations on CDD11, it achieves 29.35 dB average PSNR and 0.886 SSIM, outperforming MoCE-IR-S by 0.30 dB. In zero-shot under-display camera restoration, it reaches 28.27 dB / 0.856 SSIM on T-OLED and 15.39 dB / 0.603 SSIM on P-OLED, surpassing the next-best method (Unirestore) by 1.23 dB and 0.063 SSIM respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against non-diffusion baselines, UniLDiff trades a small PSNR/SSIM deficit (−0.4 to −0.6 dB vs. DFPIR and DA-RCOT) for substantially better perceptual quality: MUSIQ 68.89 vs. 67.34/67.82, MANIQA 0.7038 vs. 0.6679/0.6658, and LPIPS 0.0651 vs. 0.0758/0.0622. Among diffusion-based methods, UniLDiff dominates all baselines on every metric in Table 1, improving over DA-CLIP by +1.91 dB PSNR and +1.58 MUSIQ, and over DiffUIR by +0.29 dB PSNR and +1.38 MUSIQ. On composite degradations (Table 3), UniLDiff outperforms MoCE-IR-S by 0.26–0.39 dB PSNR on six of seven degradation combinations and leads OneRestore by 0.71–0.87 dB. In zero-shot under-display camera restoration (Table 4), UniLDiff surpasses Unirestore (the strongest non-diffusion baseline) by 1.23 dB on T-OLED and 0.58 dB on P-OLED, and outperforms DiffUIR-L by 2.24 dB on T-OLED. For inference efficiency (Table 5), UniLDiff requires 20 steps and 2.05 s, compared to 200 steps / 15.49 s for AutoDIR and 100 steps / 10.80 s for DA-CLIP, though DiffUIR is faster at 3 steps / 0.30 s due to its distillation-based design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Stable Diffusion XL as its latent diffusion backbone and departs from the dominant prompt-conditioning paradigm in diffusion-based AiOIR (AutoDIR, DA-CLIP, DiffUIR) by injecting low-quality features directly into the denoising UNet via a FLUX-inspired double-stream/single-stream fusion (DAFF) with timestep-aware modulation, rather than relying on global textual or visual prompts. The Detail-Aware Expert Module (DAEM) introduces a Mixture-of-Experts decoder with NAFBlocks and skip-connected encoder features to counteract VAE compression loss, a component absent in prior diffusion AiOIR methods. Conceptually, the work bridges the gap between feed-forward AiOIR methods (which handle spatially heterogeneous degradations well but lack generative priors) and diffusion-based approaches (which have strong generative capacity but struggle with fine-grained, spatially variable degradations under prompt conditioning).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion priors can match or exceed the best non-diffusion AiOIR methods on perceptual quality (MUSIQ, MANIQA, LPIPS, DISTS) while also achieving strong zero-shot generalization to unseen real-world degradations (under-display cameras) without any task prompt, suggesting a path toward truly label-agnostic restoration. However, the 20-step inference (2.05 s on A800) remains substantially slower than feed-forward methods and even DiffUIR's 3-step pipeline, and PSNR/SSIM still trail the best non-diffusion baselines by a small margin, indicating that the generative prior has not yet fully closed the fidelity gap. Open questions include whether the number of diffusion steps can be further reduced without quality loss, how the framework scales to even more degradation types or video restoration, and whether the two-stage training (DAFF then DAEM) can be unified into a single end-to-end objective.
