## Problem

Generalizable NeRFs (GeNeRFs) assume static scenes, but real-world captures contain transient distractors (dynamic objects, shadow changes) that break cross-view consistency and corrupt supervision. Existing distractor-free NeRF methods estimate uncertainty from per-view reconstruction errors under per-scene optimization, a paradigm that does not transfer to GeNeRFs where reconstruction errors conflate transient distractors with structural inconsistencies across source views, leading to misjudgment of static geometry as distractors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262103/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N source images (4 during training, 8 during evaluation) with known camera intrinsics/extrinsics and a target image, all resized to 320×640. It produces a rendered novel-view image along with two uncertainty maps: a per-pixel Source-view Uncertainty derived from the feed-forward rendering pass, and a dense Target-view Uncertainty map predicted by an independent DINOv2-based decoder. The Target-view Uncertainty is used only during training for loss modulation and is discarded at inference, while the Source-view Uncertainty is an intrinsic byproduct of the GMM-based rendering.

## Method

MU-GeNeRF decomposes distractor awareness into two complementary uncertainties. Source-view Uncertainty is predicted per sample point by a Transformer-based feed-forward network (built on VolRecon/ReTR architecture) that ingests projected multi-view features and color samples, then aggregates along each ray via a Gaussian Mixture Model to yield a pixel-level uncertainty reflecting cross-view structural disagreement. Target-view Uncertainty is a dense spatial map produced by a CNN+MLP decoder operating on DINOv2 semantic features of the target image, localizing transient distractors without participating in the rendering pass. The two are fused as β_ts = ω·β^t + (1−ω)·β^s and plugged into a heteroscedastic reconstruction loss combining patch-based SSIM and MSE terms, where the SSIM component provides spatially coherent gradients essential for learning reliable uncertainty distributions. Training proceeds in two stages: 60 epochs of multi-scene generalization training followed by 60K iterations of per-scene fine-tuning.

## Results

On the On-the-go dataset (4 scenes), MU-GeNeRF without per-scene fine-tuning achieves 17.96/18.63/19.35/17.76 PSNR on Corner/Patio/Spot/Patio-High, outperforming ReTR (16.33–17.43) and MuRF (11.78–14.18) by 1.6–5.9 dB. After 60K-iteration fine-tuning, MU-GeNeRF reaches 21.77/20.72/21.38/20.33 PSNR, surpassing UP-NeRF (14.52–19.34) by 1.4–5.8 dB on all scenes. On RobustNeRF, MU-GeNeRF ft achieves 19.97/22.34 PSNR on Statue/Android, trailing only the per-scene NeRF on-the-go (21.25/23.17) while requiring ~4× fewer optimization iterations (60K vs 250K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two GeNeRF baselines, MU-GeNeRF dominates: without fine-tuning it beats ReTR by 1.6–5.9 dB PSNR and MuRF by 3.8–7.8 dB across all four On-the-go scenes, with the gap widening after fine-tuning (e.g., 21.77 vs 19.76 vs 13.57 on Corner). Against UP-NeRF (a per-scene distractor-free NeRF), MU-GeNeRF ft wins on every scene by 1.4–5.8 dB PSNR, with the largest margin on Patio-High (20.33 vs 14.52). The only method that outperforms MU-GeNeRF ft is NeRF on-the-go, which leads by 1.4–1.7 dB PSNR (e.g., 23.15 vs 21.77 on Corner), but this comparison is asymmetric: NeRF on-the-go is a per-scene NeRF requiring 250K iterations (~48 h) of optimization, whereas MU-GeNeRF is a feed-forward GeNeRF needing only 60K fine-tuning iterations (~2 h) and generalizes across scenes without per-scene training. On RobustNeRF, the same pattern holds: MU-GeNeRF ft trails NeRF on-the-go by 1.3–0.8 dB but surpasses UP-NeRF by 1.9–1.9 dB and ReTR ft by 1.2–2.1 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Transformer-based feed-forward architectures of ReTR (NeurIPS 2023) and VolRecon (CVPR 2023), adapting their View-Transformer and Render-Transformer components to also output a per-point Source-view Uncertainty. Conceptually, it departs from the per-scene distractor-free NeRF line (NeRF-W, UP-NeRF, NeRF on-the-go), which estimates uncertainty from single-view reconstruction errors during scene-specific optimization, by arguing that this paradigm is fundamentally incompatible with GeNeRF's cross-scene feed-forward setting where errors conflate distractors with structural inconsistencies. The key methodological departure is the decomposition of reconstruction uncertainty into two complementary channels—Source-view (geometric/appearance conflicts across source views) and Target-view (semantic anomalies in the target image via DINOv2 features)—fused through a heteroscedastic loss, rather than relying on a single scalar uncertainty as in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a practical gap: GeNeRFs, which promise cross-scene generalization without per-scene optimization, have been largely untested in the presence of transient distractors that are ubiquitous in real-world capture. The two-uncertainty decomposition provides a principled explanation for why naive uncertainty modeling fails in the GeNeRF setting (misjudging static structures as distractors or missing target-view anomalies), and the ablations convincingly validate each component's necessity. However, the method still trails per-scene NeRF on-the-go in absolute quality, cannot explicitly identify or remove distractors, and relies on a fixed linear fusion weight (ω=0.5) rather than learned or adaptive combination. Open questions include whether explicit distractor segmentation or dynamic scene decomposition could complement the implicit robust-supervision approach, and whether the DINOv2-based target-view uncertainty generalizes to unseen object categories beyond its pre-training distribution.
