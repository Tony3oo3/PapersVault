## Problem

PD-based blind-spot networks face an intrinsic trade-off in real-world denoising: a large pixel-shuffle downsampling factor effectively decorrelates spatially structured noise but fragments high-frequency structures, while a smaller factor preserves detail but leaves residual noise correlation that the network cannot remove. No existing self-supervised method resolves this conflict, as both objectives act on the same spatial dependencies in opposite directions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262466/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single real-world noisy RGB image (no clean reference) and produces a denoised image at the same resolution. During training, the image is decomposed into s×s patches (s=5), from which cross-scale pairs are constructed: a 1×1 pixel sub-image as input and a t×t (t=2) high-scale target as supervision. At test time, t²=4 sub-images are independently processed and averaged; alternatively, pixel-shuffle upsampling of the t² denoised sub-images yields a 2× super-resolved output without any retraining.

## Method

NSP reformulates self-supervised denoising as a next-scale prediction problem: the BSN receives low-resolution sub-images produced with a large PD factor (s=5), where spatially correlated noise is largely decorrelated, and learns to predict their higher-resolution counterparts at scale t=2, where fine structural details are preserved. A data-pair construction strategy divides each s×s patch into a t×t consecutive-pixel target and distributes the remaining s²−t² pixels across separate sub-images, ensuring that noise-correlated pixels never co-occur in the same input-target pair. A pixel-shuffle layer is inserted between 1×1 convolutional blocks near the network tail to enable the scale transformation while preserving the blind-spot property. The framework is backbone-agnostic, demonstrated with both a CNN (DBSN, 3.75M params) and a Transformer (TBSN, 12.77M params), and is trained with L1 loss over 750 epochs on 320 SIDD Medium images.

## Results

On SIDD Validation, NSP(DBSN) achieves 37.02 dB PSNR / 0.8865 SSIM and NSP(TBSN) achieves 37.12 dB / 0.8853, setting new state-of-the-art among self-supervised methods. On SIDD Benchmark, NSP(TBSN) leads with 37.46 dB / 0.8744 SSIM, and on DND, NSP(TBSN) reaches 37.87 dB / 0.9342 SSIM. As a by-product, NSP(DBSN) also achieves 35.19 dB / 0.8490 SSIM on noisy image super-resolution (×2), outperforming two-stage denoise-then-SR pipelines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NSP(DBSN) outperforms SDAP (same DBSN backbone, 3.66M params) by 0.44 dB PSNR and 0.0235 SSIM on SIDD Validation (37.02 vs. 36.58), and NSP(TBSN) beats TBSN (same Transformer backbone) by 0.53 dB PSNR on SIDD Validation (37.12 vs. 36.59). The only marginal loss is NSP(TBSN) trailing TBSN by 0.03 dB PSNR on DND (37.87 vs. 37.90). Against earlier self-supervised PD-based methods, NSP gains 2.56 dB over AP-BSN and 7.95 dB over N2V on SIDD Validation. Among supervised and pseudo-supervised methods, NSP(DBSN) surpasses DnCNN, TNRD, CBNDet, PDD, GCBD, and C2N by at least 1.66 dB PSNR, though it remains below fully supervised methods like DeamNet (39.40 dB) and VDNet (39.29 dB). For the noisy image super-resolution task (Table 2), NSP(DBSN) achieves 35.19 dB / 0.8490 SSIM, beating the best two-stage pipeline SDAP+U-ZSSR (35.10 dB / 0.8373) and SDAP+dualSR (34.67 dB / 0.8380) while performing denoising and SR jointly in a single forward pass.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the blind-spot network (BSN) lineage from Noise2Void and Noise2Self, which assume pixel-wise independent noise, and the pixel-shuffle downsampling (PD) strategy from PDD that was introduced to handle spatially correlated real-world noise. The key departure is the identification of an antagonistic trade-off in PD-based BSN methods—large downsampling decorrelates noise but fragments fine structure, while small downsampling preserves detail but retains noise correlation—and the resolution of this conflict through a cross-scale prediction paradigm inspired by Visual Autoregressive Modeling (VAR). Rather than operating at a single fixed PD scale, NSP trains the BSN to predict a higher-resolution target from a lower-resolution, fully decorrelated input, explicitly decoupling noise removal from detail recovery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By resolving the long-standing PD trade-off, NSP provides a principled, architecture-agnostic paradigm (validated on both CNN and Transformer backbones) that practitioners can adopt to improve self-supervised real-world denoising without changing the underlying network. The by-product of noisy image super-resolution without retraining or architectural modification is a practical bonus that broadens the method's utility. Open questions include whether the cross-scale formulation generalizes to multi-step (beyond one scale jump) prediction, how the empirically chosen hyperparameters (s=5, t=2) transfer across noise types and sensor pipelines, and whether the paradigm extends to other restoration tasks such as deblurring or inpainting where similar decorrelation-preservation tensions arise.
