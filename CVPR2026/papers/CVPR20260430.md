## Problem

Neural solvers for industrial-scale physics simulations must capture both fine-grained local interactions (e.g., stress localization around loads) and long-range global dependencies (e.g., incompressible pressure coupling) across millions of spatial elements, but existing architectures force a trade-off: Transolver compresses the domain into a fixed set of global slices that oversmooth local detail, while Erwin's strictly local ball-tree attention lacks efficient long-range information propagation. No prior method handles both scales in a single, unified, near-linear-complexity operation on arbitrary geometries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260430/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts point clouds, structured grids, or unstructured meshes as spatial input, with each point carrying coordinates concatenated with geometric descriptors (e.g., boundary condition indicators) that are embedded via a shared MLP. The ball-tree partitioning is computed once before the first transformer block and reused across all blocks, so the spatial permutation is fixed per geometry. Outputs are per-point PDE solution fields (velocity, pressure, stress) at the same spatial resolution as the input, and for aerodynamic benchmarks the model additionally predicts a scalar drag coefficient C_D and surface pressure distributions.

## Method

MSPT introduces the Parallelized Multi-Scale Attention (PMSA) mechanism, which partitions the point cloud into K spatially coherent patches of size L via a balanced ball tree, pools each patch into Q supernodes (mean, max, or learned linear projection), and then augments each patch's local feature block with the full global supernode matrix before computing a single multi-head self-attention. This one attention operation simultaneously captures local-to-local interactions within the patch and local-to-global interactions with all KQ pooled tokens, decomposing naturally into four attention sub-blocks; only the local-token rows are retained as output. The full architecture stacks B such pre-normalized transformer blocks (PMSA + residual + FFN), with the final block replaced by a task-specific head. The computational complexity is O(NL + N²Q/L), which is near-linear in practice because the quadratic coefficient Q/L is small, enabling million-point inference on a single GPU with tunable patch size L controlling the local/global trade-off.

## Results

On the six standard PDE benchmarks, MSPT achieves state-of-the-art relative L₂ error on four of six tasks: Plasticity (0.10×10⁻², tied with Erwin), Airfoil (0.51×10⁻²), Pipe (0.31×10⁻²), and Navier-Stokes (6.32×10⁻², a 30% improvement over Transolver's 9.00×10⁻²). On the industry-scale ShapeNet-Car benchmark, MSPT is the best single-branch model with volume L₂ of 1.89×10⁻² and drag coefficient error of 0.98×10⁻², outperforming Transolver (2.07×10⁻² / 1.03×10⁻²). On AhmedML (2×10⁷ points), MSPT achieves the best single-branch surface L₂ of 3.22×10⁻² versus Transolver's 3.45×10⁻². MSPT trails Erwin on Elasticity (0.48 vs 0.34×10⁻²) and Transolver on Darcy (0.63 vs 0.57×10⁻²).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSPT's strongest gains are over Transolver [44], the closest single-branch competitor: a 30% relative improvement on Navier-Stokes (6.32 vs 9.00×10⁻²), 6% on Pipe (0.31 vs 0.33), and 4% on Airfoil (0.51 vs 0.53), attributed to MSPT's patch-based local attention preserving fine-grained detail that Transolver's fixed global slices oversmooth. Against Erwin [47], MSPT is clearly stronger on Airfoil (0.51 vs 2.57×10⁻²) thanks to its global supernode communication, but loses on Elasticity (0.48 vs 0.34) where Erwin's strictly local ball-tree attention suffices. On ShapeNet-Car, MSPT outperforms Transolver on volume (1.89 vs 2.07×10⁻²), surface (7.41 vs 7.45), and drag (0.98 vs 1.03×10⁻²), and beats all other single-branch baselines including GNOT [14] (3.29 volume) and 3D-GEOCA [7] (3.19 volume). On AhmedML, MSPT edges out Transolver on surface (3.22 vs 3.45×10⁻²) and matches it on volume (2.04 vs 2.05), while the two-branched AB-UPT [2] retains an advantage in its original report (1.90/3.01) though the authors' reproduction degrades to 2.39/4.33. Spectral operator baselines (FNO, geo-FNO, U-NO, F-FNO) are consistently outperformed by 2–10× on most benchmarks, confirming the advantage of attention-based local-global modeling over frequency-domain methods on unstructured and irregular geometries.

## Relation to Prior Work

MSPT sits at the intersection of three prior threads: (1) ball-tree spatial partitioning from Erwin [47], which is repurposed here not merely for local attention but to form patches that also feed global supernode communication; (2) the patch-tokenization paradigm from ViT [8] and PatchFormer [46], adapted to unstructured point clouds; and (3) the global-context bottleneck of Transolver [44] and UPT [1], which MSPT replaces with per-patch pooled supernodes to avoid a fixed-size latent compression. The key conceptual departure is the Parallelized Multi-Scale Attention (PMSA) mechanism, which fuses local patch self-attention and cross-patch global attention into a single augmented self-attention call rather than separate local and global stages, yielding near-linear scaling while preserving fine-grained structure that Transolver's global slices sacrifice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is the demonstrated ability to process million-point meshes on a single GPU with competitive accuracy, which is a concrete step toward real-time industrial CFD surrogates and large-scale design optimization. The 30% relative improvement on Navier-Stokes and consistent gains on aerodynamic benchmarks (ShapeNet-Car, AhmedML) suggest the local-global coupling is more effective than either purely local (Erwin) or purely global-bottleneck (Transolver) strategies. Open questions include whether the branched surface/volume variant (analogous to AB-UPT) can close the remaining gap to two-branch architectures, whether the non-monotonic patch-count sensitivity generalizes across problem types, and how the method behaves at truly extreme scales (10^8+ points) where even near-linear attention becomes memory-bound.
