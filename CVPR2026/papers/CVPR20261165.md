## Problem

Existing MLLM-based industrial anomaly detection methods either remain confined to image-level classification with textual reasoning, or rely on external vision modules (e.g., SAM, vision experts) and dense pixel-level annotations to produce localization maps. This creates a fundamental gap: no prior approach achieves end-to-end pixel-level anomaly localization from an MLLM using only image-level supervision, without auxiliary components or pixel-wise labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261165/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB industrial image (resized to 420×420) and a fixed text prompt (system prompt instructing the model to reason then answer, plus the question 'Are there any defects or anomalies in the image?') as input. It autoregressively generates a reasoning process followed by a Yes/No answer, and simultaneously produces a pixel-level anomaly map by aggregating the visual attention of selected reasoning tokens. Training requires only image-level normal/anomaly labels on 4K images; no pixel masks or bounding boxes are used.

## Method

The framework builds on Qwen2.5-VL with LoRA adaptation and introduces two key components. ReAL (Reasoning-Driven Anomaly Localization) scores each autoregressive reasoning token on two criteria—semantic relevance to anomaly-related input tokens (S_T) and spatial entropy of its visual attention (S_I)—then selectively aggregates the visual attention maps of tokens passing both thresholds into a weighted pixel-level anomaly map, eliminating the need for external segmentation modules. CGRO (Consistency-Guided Reasoning Optimization) applies GRPO-style reinforcement learning with a composite reward combining format, accuracy, and a class-conditional consistency reward: for anomaly images, high Jaccard overlap among top-weighted tokens' attention regions is rewarded (encouraging concentrated focus on defects), while for normal images, low overlap is rewarded (discouraging spurious concentration). This consistency signal forces the model to align its textual reasoning with its visual attention, producing coherent explanations and sharper localization maps under pure image-level supervision.

## Results

On the four zero-shot industrial anomaly detection benchmarks (MVTec-AD, WFDD, SDD, DTD), Qwen2.5-VL-7B+CGRO achieves 83.9 AUROC / 86.9 ACC at the image level and 80.7 AUROC / 97.1 AUPR at the pixel level, using only image-level supervision. For reasoning quality on MVTec-COT, it attains the highest ROUGE-L (27.1) and SBERT (74.7) among all compared models. The 3B variant (Qwen2.5-VL-3B+CGRO) reaches 67.6 AUROC / 72.4 ACC (image) and 73.5 AUROC / 94.3 AUPR (pixel), already surpassing AnomalyGPT and EIAD on pixel-level metrics despite using far less supervision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest commercial MLLM (GPT-4.1, 87.2 AUROC / 88.4 ACC), the 7B+CGRO model trails by ~3.3 AUROC on image-level detection but is the only method that simultaneously produces pixel-level anomaly maps (80.7 AUROC) and the best reasoning scores (27.1 ROUGE-L, 74.7 SBERT), whereas GPT-4.1 reports N/A for pixel-level metrics. Compared to specialized anomaly frameworks, the method substantially outperforms AnomalyGPT (71.1→83.9 AUROC image; 77.8→80.7 AUROC pixel; 11.9→27.1 ROUGE-L) and EIAD (68.4→83.9 AUROC image; 57.6→80.7 AUROC pixel) while using only image-level labels instead of pixel-level masks or task-specific instruction data. Against Triad (85.5 AUROC image, no pixel-level output), the 7B+CGRO model is slightly lower on image-level AUROC (83.9 vs 85.5) but uniquely provides pixel-level localization and far superior reasoning (27.1 vs 8.6 ROUGE-L). Relative to the R1-only baseline (Qwen2.5-VL+R1, 80.0 AUROC / 78.5 pixel AUROC), adding CGRO yields consistent gains of +3.9 image AUROC, +2.2 pixel AUROC, and +0.8 ROUGE-L, confirming the consistency reward's contribution. The 3B+CGRO variant (67.6 AUROC image, 73.5 AUROC pixel) already surpasses both AnomalyGPT and EIAD on pixel-level metrics despite being trained with only image-level supervision, demonstrating strong data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeek-R1 [12] and applies it to industrial anomaly detection, but departs fundamentally from prior MLLM-based IAD methods (AnomalyGPT, EIAD, Triad) that rely on external vision modules like SAM or Vision Experts for pixel-level localization. The key conceptual departure is ReAL, which repurposes the MLLM's own cross-modal attention patterns from reasoning tokens as a localization signal, eliminating the need for any auxiliary segmentation architecture. CGRO further extends the RL objective with a class-conditional Jaccard-based consistency reward that has no direct analogue in prior IAD-RL work (IAD-R1, AD-FM), which focus on accuracy and format rewards without explicitly aligning reasoning tokens to visual attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial for industrial deployment: eliminating pixel-level annotation costs and external module dependencies (SAM, Vision Experts) simplifies the pipeline and reduces domain bias from dense supervision. However, the evidence is confined to Qwen2.5-VL at 3B and 7B scales on four datasets at 420×420 resolution, leaving open whether the attention-based localization generalizes to other MLLM architectures, higher resolutions, or more diverse defect types. A key open question is whether the small subset of 'anomaly-relevant' reasoning tokens identified by ReAL remains stable and informative as model scale increases or as the reasoning process becomes more elaborate.
