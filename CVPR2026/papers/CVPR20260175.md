## Problem

Partially annotated multi-task learning for dense prediction is limited by single-domain training that restricts generalization and by conflicting task objectives that require careful per-task loss balancing. No prior method trains a holistic multi-task model across multiple synthetic datasets where each dataset only provides labels for a subset of tasks, while also generalizing to real-world data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260175/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images (or channel-wise concatenated pairs of RGB images for temporal tasks like optical and scene flow) and produces seven dense prediction outputs: semantic segmentation (class indices), surface normals, depth maps, 2D optical flow, 3D scene flow, shading, and albedo. All predictions are made in a shared 4-channel latent space via a frozen Stable Diffusion VAE, then decoded back to RGB and post-processed task-specifically (e.g., argmax for semantic, least-squares fitting for unbounded quantities like depth and flow).

## Method

StableMTL repurposes a single-step latent diffusion model (Lotus-D) for multi-task latent regression in two stages. Stage 1 fine-tunes a UNet with task-token cross-attention conditioning, trained with a unified MSE loss in latent space and a task-gradient isolation scheme (single-task mini-batches with per-task gradient accumulation) to prevent strong-gradient tasks from dominating weak ones. Stage 2 introduces a multi-stream architecture where a trainable main UNet (initialized from Stage 1) attends to frozen auxiliary UNets—one per task—via an N-to-one task-attention layer inserted after spatial attention in each transformer block, with attention-guided task masking to prevent early attention saturation on dominant tasks. This design eliminates task-specific losses and complex balancing heuristics while enabling cross-task knowledge transfer that scales linearly rather than quadratically with the number of tasks.

## Results

On seven dense prediction tasks across eight real-world benchmarks, StableMTL achieves +4.78% Δm (multi-task performance over single-task baselines), substantially outperforming the single-stream variant (−1.57% Δm) and all partial-annotation baselines. Headline numbers include 55.79% mIoU on Cityscapes (semantic), 23.27° mAE on DIODE (normal), 14.98% AbsRel on KITTI (depth), 10.76 px EPE-2D on KITTI (optical flow), and 0.2313 m EPE-3D on KITTI (scene flow). The adapted baselines JTR* and DiffusionMTL* suffer severe degradation (−106.87% and −78.76% Δm respectively) when scaled beyond their original three-task setting, while StableMTL's unified latent loss maintains stability across all seven tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the single-task Lotus-D baseline [16], StableMTL wins on semantic (+7.62 mIoU), optical flow (−0.40 EPE-2D), scene flow (−0.0422 EPE-3D), and albedo (−0.0535 RMSE), but slightly underperforms on normal (+1.00 mAE), depth (+0.77/+0.47 AbsRel), and shading (+0.0201 RMSE); the multi-stream architecture recovers most of this gap, yielding a net +4.78% Δm versus 0.00% for single-task. Against JTR [36] and its adapted variant JTR*, StableMTL dominates across every task: JTR* collapses to 20.46% mIoU (vs. 55.79%) and 0.5176 m EPE-3D (vs. 0.2313 m), with an overall Δm of −106.87% versus +4.78%. Similarly, DiffusionMTL* [60] degrades severely when extended to seven tasks (−78.76% Δm), with optical flow at 36.60 px versus StableMTL's 10.76 px. On the NYU-v2 partial-label protocol [22], StableMTL outperforms MTPSL, JTR, and DiffusionMTL by 3.34–17.45 mIoU (semantic), 3.39–9.63° mAE (normal), and 0.0623–0.1952 AbsRel (depth) under both 'One' and 'Random' label regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work repurposing latent diffusion models for dense prediction (Marigold, Lotus-D) from single-task to a multi-task setting, and simultaneously extends partially labeled MTL (MTPSL, JTR, DiffusionMTL) from single-dataset training to multi-dataset synthetic training with domain generalization. Architecturally, it builds on the Stable Diffusion UNet with task-token conditioning (from task-switching networks and RGB↔X), but replaces the N-to-N cross-task attention of prior MTL methods with a more scalable N-to-one multi-stream attention. The unified latent MSE loss departs from the task-specific pixel-level losses and complex balancing heuristics (GradNorm, uncertainty weighting, Pareto optimization) that dominate the MTL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By training on only ~80k synthetic images with partial labels across three datasets, StableMTL demonstrates that multi-task dense prediction can scale to seven heterogeneous tasks without per-task loss engineering, offering a practical path to reducing annotation costs for autonomous driving and robotics perception. Key open questions remain: inference is currently sequential across tasks, adding new tasks (e.g., shading) still degrades others, and the uniform task sampling strategy is suboptimal. The finding that task-specific losses with different weight combinations can catastrophically collapse (−68.94% Δm) even with just three tasks, while the unified latent loss remains stable, suggests the field may over-invest in balancing heuristics that are fragile at scale.
