## Problem

Transformer-based video diffusion models are bottlenecked by the quadratic cost of self-attention, which dominates over 76% of compute in models like Wan2.1 1.3B. While linear attention offers sub-quadratic complexity, no practical method exists for converting pretrained softmax-attention video diffusion models to linear or hybrid attention without prohibitively expensive retraining from scratch, due to the expressiveness gap between exponential and polynomial kernels.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pretrained Wan2.1 1.3B video diffusion transformer, taking a text prompt as input and generating 81-frame videos at 480×832 resolution (or 320×480 for ablations). The surgery modifies the internal attention mechanism of selected transformer blocks while preserving the overall DiT architecture, so the input/output interface remains identical to the original model. Evaluation is conducted at the original Wan2.1 resolution and length using VBench and VBench-2.0 prompt suites.

## Method

Attention Surgery is a three-stage framework: (1) per-block attention distillation, where each softmax attention layer is independently distilled into a hybrid attention layer using learnable polynomial feature maps φ_q and φ_k (2-layer MLP with degree-2 polynomial expansion) that approximate the exponential kernel; (2) heterogeneous block-rate optimization, formulated as a multiple-choice knapsack problem that assigns each transformer block a hybrid rate (R∈{1,2,4,8}) to minimize accumulated approximation error under a global FLOPs/latency budget; (3) lightweight finetuning for a few hundred iterations on a modest prompt/video set. The hybrid attention uniformly subsamples tokens at rate R for full softmax attention while the remaining tokens use linear attention, providing global temporal anchors that prevent the drift seen with local-window approaches. The entire pipeline requires fewer than 0.4k GPU-hours, compared to hundreds of thousands of GPU-hours for training from scratch.

## Results

On VBench, the Attention Surgery 15×R2 variant achieves a Total score of 83.21 (Quality 85.19, Semantic 75.25), essentially matching the original Wan2.1 1.3B baseline (83.31 / 85.23 / 75.65) and its reproduction (83.10 / 85.10 / 75.12). On VBench-2.0, the method scores 55.1 Total versus 56.0 for the full-attention Wan2.1 1.3B. A blind human preference study over 562 paired comparisons shows 31.0% preference for the hybrid model versus 29.3% for the original, with 39.7% indicating no significant difference. On Snapdragon 8-Gen4, the hybrid attention yields approximately 6× faster per-block inference for 7.5-second videos at 320×480 resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base model Wan2.1 1.3B, the 15×R2 hybrid variant (83.21 Total) is within 0.1 of the reproduction (83.10) and 0.1 below the original (83.31), confirming near-lossless linearization. The method outperforms all other efficient VDMs in Table 1 except SANA-Video (83.71), which trains from scratch with block-linear attention and thus has a different training budget; it also edges out STA (83.00) and Mobile Video DiT-Server (83.09) by roughly 0.1–0.2 points. On VBench-2.0, the hybrid model (55.1) trails the full-attention Wan2.1 (56.0) by 0.9 but surpasses CogVideoX-1.5 5B (53.4) by 1.7. The human preference study shows no statistically meaningful gap: 31.0% prefer the hybrid, 29.3% prefer the original, and 39.7% see no difference, with the hybrid actually winning on appearance style (51.8%), color (52.2%), and spatial relationship (43.9%) dimensions. The key efficiency gain is that the hybrid model requires less than 0.4k GPU-hours of surgery versus the hundreds of thousands to millions of GPU-hours needed to train SOTA video diffusion models from scratch, while delivering comparable quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hybrid attention concept from LoLCATs (Zhang et al., ICLR 2025) — which mixes softmax and linear tokens in language models — to video diffusion transformers, replacing local-window softmax tokens with uniform subsampling to preserve global temporal coherence. The linear attention formulation follows the kernel-trick framework of Katharopoulos et al. (ICML 2020), but introduces learnable polynomial feature maps to better approximate the exponential kernel. Unlike prior efficient-attention work for video (SANA-Video, M4V) that requires training from scratch or architectural replacement, this paper positions itself as a lightweight surgical procedure on already-pretrained DiTs, using per-block distillation and a multiple-choice knapsack formulation for heterogeneous block-rate assignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe — distilling each block independently then finetuning for a few hundred iterations within 0.4k GPU hours — dramatically lowers the barrier to deploying sub-quadratic attention in production video diffusion models, which previously required millions of GPU-hours of retraining. The ~6× mobile latency reduction for 7.5-second videos on Snapdragon 8-Gen4 is a concrete efficiency win for on-device deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger models (14B+), whether causal linear attention can enable RNN-like inference with constant memory for arbitrarily long videos, and whether the small but persistent quality gap (e.g., 83.21 vs. 83.31 VBench) closes with more finetuning or larger training sets.
