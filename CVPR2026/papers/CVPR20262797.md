## Problem

Camera-only 3D object detectors are biased toward frequent head classes (cars, adults) while severely underperforming on rare but safety-critical tail classes (children, strollers, emergency vehicles), a gap that is further worsened by inter-class ambiguity among visually similar subclasses and large intra-class diversity in appearance, scale, and pose. Unlike LiDAR-based long-tail detection, which has received substantial attention, the long-tail problem in the camera-only 3D setting remains largely unexplored, and existing camera-based methods lack mechanisms to inject semantic priors that could disambiguate confusing or heterogeneous tail categories.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images from surround cameras (6 views on nuScenes, 7 views on Argoverse 2) together with camera extrinsic parameters, and outputs 3D bounding boxes with category labels across an extended 18-class (nuScenes) or 26-class (AV2) taxonomy. A key implementation detail is that 3D object queries are projected into per-camera 2D representations via Hadamard product with linearly transformed extrinsics, enabling direct supervision from CLIP image features cropped at matched ground-truth 2D locations. The frozen CLIP-B/16 teacher operates on these cropped 2D regions and on category-name text prompts, so no additional sensor modalities are required at inference time.

## Method

SemLT3D replaces the standard FFN layers in a query-based 3D detector (StreamPETR or Far3D) with a Language-guided Mixture-of-Experts (LMoE) module in which the router computes cosine similarity between projected 3D queries and CLIP text embeddings of category names, then assigns each query to its top-k experts based on this semantic affinity rather than raw feature similarity; a shared expert retains generalizable knowledge across all classes. In parallel, a Semantic Projection Distillation pipeline projects refined 3D tokens into 2D camera-aligned space, extracts CLIP visual features from cropped regions of Hungarian-matched ground-truth objects, and minimizes KL divergence between the student's and teacher's language-visual alignment distributions, injecting high-level semantic priors that benefit rare and visually ambiguous categories. A focal-loss-based query-language contrastive term at each decoder layer further stabilizes training by directly aligning 3D queries with CLIP text embeddings. The full framework is plug-and-play, adding only ~9% inference overhead while maintaining the original FFN parameter budget (4 experts × 512-dim + 1 shared × 1024-dim ≈ 2048-dim).

## Results

On nuScenes (18-class long-tailed setting), SemLT3D achieves 29.59 mAP / 40.94 NDS with ResNet-50 and 31.12 mAP / 42.79 NDS with ResNet-101, improving over the StreamPETR baseline by +2.62/+0.96 mAP and +2.75/+1.62 NDS respectively. On Argoverse 2, the method reaches 25.9 mAP / 19.4 CDS, surpassing Far3D by +1.5 mAP and +1.3 CDS. In the long-tail breakdown, the most striking gain is on the 'Few' category group: +2.81 mAP over StreamPETR (6.03 vs. 3.22) and +6.02 mAP on 'Medium' (34.55 vs. 28.53), while incurring only a marginal −2.4 mAP drop on 'Many'.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On nuScenes (ResNet-50), SemLT3D outperforms all eight camera-only baselines, with the closest competitors being SparseBEV (27.94 mAP) and RayDN (27.41 mAP); the proposed method leads by +1.65 mAP over SparseBEV and +2.18 mAP over RayDN. On the ResNet-101 setting, it surpasses StreamPETR (30.16→31.12 mAP) and SparseBEV (29.78→31.12 mAP) by roughly 1 mAP. On Argoverse 2, SemLT3D beats Far3D (24.4→25.9 mAP) and StreamPETR (20.3→25.9 mAP) by +1.5 and +5.6 mAP respectively, while also improving CDS from 18.1 to 19.4. In the long-tail 'Few' category group, SemLT3D (6.03 mAP) substantially outperforms StreamPETR (3.22), SparseBEV (4.1), and RayDN (0.6), and even with a ViT backbone reaches 20.77 mAP on Few—exceeding the LiDAR+camera BEVFusion (12.8) by +7.97 mAP on that group. The only minor trade-off is a small drop on the 'Many' group (50.92 vs. 53.32 for StreamPETR), reflecting the expected optimization shift toward tail classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

SemLT3D builds directly on the query-based 3D detection paradigm of StreamPETR (nuScenes) and Far3D (Argoverse 2), replacing their standard FFN layers with a Language-guided Mixture-of-Experts (LMoE) module and adding a CLIP-based semantic projection distillation pipeline. It departs from prior long-tail 3D detection work (LT3D, FOMO-3D, CBGS) that relies on LiDAR or multi-modal fusion pipelines, instead operating entirely within a camera-only, single-stage framework. The semantic routing mechanism draws on CLIP text embeddings to guide expert assignment, contrasting with the uniform or feature-based routing used in DETR-style MoE adaptations, and the distillation strategy bridges 2D long-tail learning techniques (CLIP-enriched semantics) into the 3D detection setting without requiring multi-stage training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practically critical but underexplored gap: safety-critical rare classes (children, emergency vehicles, strollers) remain poorly detected by camera-only 3D detectors despite their high real-world stakes. The plug-and-play design and modest compute overhead (~9% inference increase) make it deployable on existing pipelines, and the finding that a camera-only method can outperform LiDAR+camera fusion (BEVFusion) by +10.17 mAP on Few categories with a ViT backbone suggests semantic priors can partially compensate for sensor limitations. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the 18-class nuScenes taxonomy to truly open-vocabulary settings, whether the CLIP teacher dependency limits adaptability to novel domains, and whether the slight degradation on head classes (Many) becomes problematic as tail classes grow in number.
