## Problem

Instruction-based 3D editing lags significantly behind 3D generation in quality because existing approaches rely on 2D diffusion priors or multi-view editing strategies that produce view-inconsistent editing signals, leading to multi-face Janus artifacts, blurry reconstructions, and an inability to perform large geometric transformations. The core gap is the lack of a method that can directly manipulate 3D geometry in a fully 3D-consistent manner without inheriting the limitations of 2D supervision.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D asset rendered from V uniformly distributed camera viewpoints (yielding RGB, depth, and normal maps per view), a source text prompt describing the original object, and a target edit prompt. The 3D asset is encoded into structured multi-view Gaussian splat grids of shape V×12×H×W (12 attributes per pixel: RGB, 3D location, scale, rotation quaternion, opacity), which are then inverted into a noise latent. The output is an edited pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian splat representation that can be rendered as multi-view images, optionally enhanced via an iterative 2D super-resolution pass using ControlNetTile.

## Method

3D-LATTE operates zero-shot within the latent space of DiffSplat, a 3D-native diffusion model over 3D Gaussian Splatting grids, eliminating reliance on 2D priors or SDS losses. The core mechanism is 3D attention injection: at each denoising step guided by the edit prompt, cross-attention maps (restricted to tokens shared between source and target prompts) and self-attention maps from the source-prompt denoising trajectory are blended into the edit trajectory, preserving 3D layout and part composition while allowing semantic change. Region-specific edits are achieved by generating multi-view consistent 2D masks via GPT-4o + GroundingDINO + SAM2, which naturally lift to 3D segmentation over the pixel-aligned Gaussians. Three auxiliary components enhance quality: a geometry-aware classifier guidance term that softly penalizes opacity and scale collapse in edit-relevant regions, a frequency annealing strategy that applies Fourier-domain spectral modulation on U-Net skip connections to prioritize low-frequency structure early and high-frequency detail later, and an iterative 3D enhancement step that re-renders, super-resolves via ControlNetTile, and re-optimizes the Gaussian representation for fine-grained texture recovery.

## Results

On a benchmark of 100 edit samples (25 3D assets × 4 instructions each) from Objaverse and Google Scanned Objects, 3D-LATTE achieves the highest CLIP directional similarity (0.178) and CLIP direction consistency (0.77), outperforming the next-best Edit360 (0.149, 0.59) by 19% and 31% respectively. It also attains the lowest CLIP Diff No-Edit score (0.039) among methods that successfully apply edits, indicating strong preservation of unedited regions. In GPTEval3D pairwise comparisons, 3D-LATTE is preferred over all baselines on all three criteria (prompt alignment, 3D plausibility, texture), with win rates ranging from 67% (vs. Edit360 on prompt alignment) to 94% (vs. GaussCTRL on prompt alignment).

## Baselines & Comparisons

3D-LATTE outperforms all six baselines on the primary CLIP-dir metric (0.178 vs. 0.149 for Edit360, 0.129 for Vox-E, 0.121 for MVEdit), confirming superior semantic alignment to edit instructions. On CLIP-Dir-Con (cross-view consistency), 3D-LATTE leads by a wide margin (0.77 vs. 0.68 for Vox-E and 0.67 for MVEdit), reflecting the advantage of operating in a native 3D latent space over multi-view 2D priors. GaussCTRL achieves the lowest CLIP Diff No-Edit (0.035) but at the cost of the worst CLIP-dir (0.076), indicating it frequently fails to apply edits at all. In GPTEval3D pairwise comparisons, 3D-LATTE wins against GaussCTRL on all three criteria (94%/83%/81%), against MVEdit (87%/71%/70%), against Vox-E (78%/81%/78%), and against Edit360 (67%/90%/72%), with the smallest margin against Edit360 on prompt alignment. A user study with 57 participants further confirms that 3D-LATTE is significantly preferred over the three main baselines on both visual quality and instruction faithfulness.

## Relation to Prior Work

3D-LATTE departs from the dominant paradigm of 2D-prior distillation (SDS, iterative dataset updates) and multi-view editing strategies for instruction-based 3D editing, instead operating entirely within the latent space of a native 3D diffusion model (DiffSplat). The core mechanism—injecting attention maps from a source prompt into the denoising process—is a direct 3D adaptation of Prompt-to-Prompt [16], while the inversion step follows the edit-friendly DDPM noise space of [20] and the 3D enhancement module repurposes the iterative dataset update idea from InstructNeRF2NeRF [15]. Unlike hybrid 2D-3D methods (MVEdit, GaussCTRL) that fuse multi-view images between denoising steps, or trajectory-alignment approaches (Edit360), this method applies all editing signals within a single 3D-consistent latent representation, eliminating the need for cross-view consistency enforcement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By performing edits directly in 3D latent space, this work addresses the fundamental multi-view inconsistency and Janus artifacts that plague 2D-prior-based editing, enabling geometric (not just appearance) transformations with 3D structural coherence. However, the evaluation benchmark is relatively small (25 assets, 100 edit pairs), and the 3D enhancement step still relies on a 2D diffusion model (ControlNetTile), leaving a residual dependency on 2D priors for fine detail. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-object scenes, whether it transfers to other 3D diffusion backbones beyond DiffSplat, and how it scales to large structural edits that require significant topology changes.
