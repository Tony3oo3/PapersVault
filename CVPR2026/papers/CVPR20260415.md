## Problem

Existing diffusion-based video editing systems are trained on paired data limited to simple, predefined operations (single-object add/remove/swap within fixed masks), creating a fundamental generalization gap when faced with complex, open-domain instructions such as multi-task edits, global environment changes, or semantically-aware controls. The core challenge is both the scarcity of diverse paired training data and the lack of mechanisms to make the model robustly interpret and faithfully execute arbitrary natural-language editing instructions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260415/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video, a natural-language editing instruction, and an optional reference image (e.g., a teddy bear to be added) as input, and produces a temporally coherent edited video. The VLM instructor consumes only the first frame of the source video alongside the instruction and reference image to produce conditioning tokens, while the full source video is encoded via VAE and channel-concatenated with the noise latent to provide spatially and temporally aligned structural guidance. During training, images are treated as single-frame videos to incorporate 5.4M image editing pairs alongside 1.5M synthetic video pairs.

## Method

VIVA couples a frozen VLM instructor (aligned with HunyuanVideo-T2V-13B's VLM space) that encodes the instruction, first frame, and optional reference image into grounded multimodal tokens, with a trainable Token Refiner that projects these tokens into the DiT's latent space. The source video's VAE latent is channel-concatenated with the noise latent and passed through a trainable projector, providing strong structural and motion context to the denoising process. Supervised fine-tuning uses Rectified Flow matching with a masked loss that up-weights edited regions. A post-training stage, Edit-GRPO, applies Group Relative Policy Optimization with three composite rewards—CLIP-based instruction following, CLIP-based source preservation, and PickScore-based human preference alignment—using Flow-SDE for diverse sample generation and LoRA for efficient policy updates.

## Results

On VIE-Bench, VIVA achieves an average VLM evaluation score of 8.86 on Add, 8.86 on Replace, 9.44 on Remove, and 5.88 on Hybrid Edit, outperforming all four open-source baselines (ICVE, Lucy-Edit-Dev, Ditto, InsV2V) by 1.6–3.6 points on average across tasks. Against the commercial Runway Gen-4 Aleph, VIVA is competitive: it edges ahead on Add (8.86 vs. 8.44) and reference-based Replace (8.74 vs. 8.07), while trailing slightly on Remove (9.44 vs. 9.79) and Hybrid Edit (5.88 vs. 7.85). On video quality metrics, VIVA maintains CLIP consistency above 95% and PickScore above 20 across all tasks, on par with Runway. A user study with 14 domain experts confirms VIVA is preferred over all baselines on instruction following, source preservation, and editing quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VIVA outperforms all four open-source baselines (ICVE, Lucy-Edit-Dev, Ditto, InsV2V) across every editing task and every VLM evaluation dimension, with the largest margins on Hybrid Edit where Ditto scores only 2.25 and InsV2V 3.51 versus VIVA's 5.88. Against the commercial Runway Gen-4 Aleph, VIVA is broadly competitive: it wins on Add (8.86 vs. 8.44 avg) and reference-based Replace (8.74 vs. 8.07), ties closely on reference-based Add (8.96 vs. 8.95), but trails on Remove (9.44 vs. 9.79) and Hybrid Edit (5.88 vs. 7.85). On video quality metrics (CLIP/DINO consistency, PickScore), VIVA remains within ~1 point of Runway across all tasks, indicating no quality degradation from the editing process. The user study with 14 domain experts confirms VIVA is preferred over all five baselines on instruction following, source preservation, and editing quality, with no category where a baseline is consistently preferred. The paper notes that open-source models do not yet support reference-image-based editing, so reference-based comparisons are limited to Runway.

## Relation to Prior Work

VIVA sits at the intersection of two research threads: instruction-based video editing (InsV2V, Ditto, ICVE, Lucy-Edit) and RL-based alignment of generative models (Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO). It departs from prior editing methods that condition on T5 text embeddings by replacing them with VLM-encoded multimodal tokens (instruction + first frame + optional reference image), leveraging the fact that HunyuanVideo-T2V-13B is already aligned with a VLM feature space. The Edit-GRPO stage adapts the GRPO algorithm from DeepSeek Math and Flow-GRPO to the video editing domain, where prior RL work has focused on text-to-image or text-to-video generation rather than conditional editing. The synthetic data pipeline (MMDiT-based inpainting with Grounding-DINO/SAM 2) extends the data construction approach of InsV2V and Ditto but adds a VLM-based quality filtering stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real bottleneck in instruction-based video editing: models trained on simple, mask-constrained edits fail to generalize to complex, open-domain instructions. The VLM instructor + GRPO combination offers a practical recipe that could be adopted by other conditional video generation systems, and the 1.5M-pair synthetic dataset (though not publicly released) demonstrates a scalable data construction strategy. However, the notably weaker performance on hybrid edits (5.88 avg vs. 8.86–9.44 for single-operation tasks) reveals that the generalization gap is not fully closed, and the reward system's reliance on CLIP similarity and PickScore may not capture all dimensions of editing quality. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly compositional multi-step edits, interactive editing, and whether a purpose-built video editing reward model could replace the current heuristic combination.
