## Problem

Current video generation models achieve high visual fidelity but consistently violate fundamental physical laws (gravity, momentum, material behavior) because the next-frame prediction objective does not explicitly enforce physical reasoning. Simply scaling data and model capacity leads to memorization rather than internalized physical understanding, leaving a gap between visually plausible synthesis and true physical consistency.

## Input / Output

The model accepts a text prompt plus 1–45 conditioning video frames (or a single conditioning image) at 480×832 resolution, supporting up to 121 total frames. In text-to-video mode, both the visual and physical latent branches are denoised from pure Gaussian noise; in video-to-video mode, conditioning frames are concatenated along the temporal axis with their flow-matching timestep fixed to t=0 so they remain unperturbed. The output is a sequence of future video frames decoded from the visual VAE, jointly with evolved latent physical states in V-JEPA2 embedding space.

## Method

PHANTOM augments the frozen Wan2.2-TI2V video diffusion backbone with a parallel physics branch (initialized from scratch) that operates in the V-JEPA2 latent space, which prior work shows encodes intuitive physics such as object permanence, collisions, and gravity. The two branches are coupled via symmetric dual cross-attention layers (Vis-Attention and Phy-Attention) inserted at matching depths, allowing physics cues to guide visual generation and visual evidence to refine physics reasoning without collapsing the modalities into a single entangled representation. Training uses a joint flow-matching objective that regresses velocity fields for both the visual latent v and the physical latent z simultaneously, with a recursive loss-weight schedule on the physics loss (α_z) that resets to zero whenever the physics-branch gradient norm exceeds a threshold, preventing destabilization. Only the physics branch and cross-attention modules are trained; all pretrained video-branch parameters remain frozen to preserve the generative prior.

## Results

On VideoPhy, PHANTOM achieves a Physical Commonsense (PC) score of 37.9, a 50.4% improvement over its Wan2.2-TI2V backbone (25.2), and the highest PC among all compared methods. On VideoPhy-2, PHANTOM scores 71.74 PC (+2.6% over Wan2.2) and 27.75 SA (+13.1%). On Physics-IQ (single-frame), PHANTOM reaches 29.59, a 33.9% gain over Wan2.2 (22.10), outperforming CogVideoX1.5-I2V (27.90) and RDPO (25.21). On VBench-2, PHANTOM improves Physics from 40.19 to 43.61 and Human Fidelity from 86.10 to 88.39 while maintaining a competitive Total score (51.84 vs. 51.57).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PHANTOM achieves the best PC score on VideoPhy (37.9), narrowly edging out PhyT2V (37) and substantially outperforming WISA (33), CogVideoX-5B (31.4), and its own Wan2.2 backbone (25.2). On VideoPhy-2, VideoREPA retains a slight PC edge (72.54 vs. 71.74), but PHANTOM surpasses it on SA (27.75 vs. 21.02) and significantly outperforms the Wan2.2 baseline on both metrics. On Physics-IQ single-frame, PHANTOM (29.59) beats CogVideoX1.5-I2V (27.90), RDPO (25.21), Runway Gen 3 (22.80), and Wan2.2 (22.10); in the multi-frame setting, VideoPoet (29.50) remains ahead of PHANTOM (27.53). On VBench-2, PHANTOM improves Physics (+6.0%), Human Fidelity (+2.7%), and Controllability (+9.4%) over Wan2.2, but drops in Creativity (45.51 vs. 52.50), primarily due to reduced Diversity (64.67→45.95) while Composition improves (+11.7%). Overall, PHANTOM's gains are concentrated in physics-related metrics, with the trade-off being a modest reduction in generative diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

PHANTOM builds on the Wan2.2-TI2V pretrained video diffusion backbone and the V-JEPA2 self-supervised video encoder, repurposing the latter's latent space as a physics-aware representation for a dedicated dynamics branch. It departs from prior physics-aware video generation approaches that rely on external simulators (PhysGen, MotionCraft), inference-time LLM guidance (PhyT2V, DiffPhy), or post-hoc representation alignment (VideoREPA) by integrating physical reasoning directly into the generative process through a dual-branch flow-matching architecture with bidirectional cross-attention. The key conceptual departure is treating latent physical dynamics as a jointly predicted output alongside video frames, rather than as an external constraint or conditioning signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50.4% physical commonsense improvement on VideoPhy without external simulators or inference-time guidance suggests that physics-aware latent representations can be internalized during training, offering a practical path toward physically consistent video generation. However, the notable diversity drop on VBench-2 (64.67 → 45.95) raises questions about whether enforcing physical consistency inherently constrains creative variation, and whether this trade-off persists at scale. Open questions include whether the V-JEPA2 representation captures sufficiently rich physics for complex multi-body or long-horizon interactions, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the single backbone and dataset used here.
