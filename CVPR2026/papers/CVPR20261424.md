## Problem

Existing LiDAR ground segmentation methods are either handcrafted geometric algorithms tuned to specific sensor configurations and environments, or supervised networks requiring expensive per-point manual annotations. No prior approach achieves self-supervised, domain-agnostic ground segmentation that generalizes zero-shot across diverse LiDAR sensors and datasets without any human labels.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261424/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single raw LiDAR scan as a set of (x,y,z) coordinates, processed independently per frame without temporal fusion. Initial point features are restricted to a 3-dimensional vector (constant ones, normalized height, normalized horizontal range), while raw coordinates are used solely for constructing the spatial voxel grid at 0.05 m resolution. The model outputs a per-point confidence score in [0,1] for the non-ground class, which is binarized via a tuned decision threshold to yield the final binary ground/non-ground segmentation mask.

## Method

TerraSeg comprises three components: (1) OmniLiDAR, a unified corpus of ~22 million standardized scans from 12 public driving datasets spanning 15 LiDAR models; (2) PseudoLabeler, a self-supervised module that fits a per-scan MLP elevation map via asymmetric loss (quadratic below-surface, Huber above-surface) with 0.5% lower-quantile denoising and pillar-based post-processing to recover misclassified non-ground points; and (3) an adapted Point Transformer v3 backbone with Group Normalization (replacing Batch Normalization) and disabled dataset-specific normalization to enforce domain-agnostic geometric priors. The network is trained end-to-end on PseudoLabeler-generated pseudo-labels using a combination of class-balanced Binary Cross-Entropy and symmetric Lovász-Softmax loss, with dynamic positive-class weighting via EMA to handle class imbalance across diverse scenes. Two model sizes are provided: TerraSeg-B (46M params, 10–28 Hz) and TerraSeg-S (12M params, 17–50 Hz), enabling real-time deployment while the pseudo-labeling runs offline at 0.3–0.5 Hz.

## Results

TerraSeg-B (46M params) achieves state-of-the-art mIoU of 92.47 on nuScenes, 93.52 on SemanticKITTI, and 94.40 on Waymo Perception, all without any manual labels. The efficient TerraSeg-S (12M params) achieves 91.45, 93.06, and 94.11 mIoU respectively while maintaining real-time throughput of 17–50 Hz. TerraSeg-B runs at 10–28 Hz on an NVIDIA A100 GPU, making it suitable for near real-time deployment. The PseudoLabeler module alone (offline, 0.3–0.5 Hz) already achieves 90.63–92.95 mIoU, demonstrating the quality of the self-supervised labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TerraSeg-B outperforms all five baselines on mIoU across all three evaluation datasets. Against the strongest handcrafted baseline TRAVEL [28], TerraSeg-B gains +4.01 mIoU on nuScenes (92.47 vs. 88.46), +5.33 on SemanticKITTI (93.52 vs. 88.19), and +4.00 on Waymo (94.40 vs. 90.40). The supervised GndNet [31] generalizes poorly across sensors, collapsing to 61.83 mIoU on Waymo Perception (vs. TerraSeg-B's 94.40), a 32.57-point gap attributable to its handcrafted pre-processing tied to a specific LiDAR configuration. The closest self-supervised baseline, Chodosh et al. [6], trails TerraSeg-B by 2.34 mIoU on nuScenes (80.13 vs. 92.47) and 1.37 on Waymo (85.03 vs. 94.40), with the gap driven by PseudoLabeler's improved pre/post-processing and the learned PTv3 backbone. RANSAC [11] and PatchWork++ [19] are competitive on flat urban scenes but degrade sharply on non-planar terrain (e.g., PatchWork++ drops to 65.23 ground IoU on Waymo). Notably, TerraSeg-S achieves second-best mIoU overall while running at 17–50 Hz, offering a practical speed-accuracy tradeoff for real-time robotics deployment.

## Relation to Prior Work

TerraSeg builds directly on Chodosh et al.'s (WACV 2024) self-supervised runtime optimization for fitting a piecewise-linear elevation surface, extending it with improved pre-processing (quantile-based denoising), a pillar-based post-processing step, and a more resilient training schedule to produce higher-quality pseudo-labels. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant handcrafted paradigm (RANSAC, PatchWork++, TRAVEL) that requires per-sensor tuning, and from supervised approaches (GndNet) that depend on expensive per-point annotations, by framing ground segmentation as a large-scale, label-free learning problem. The architectural choice of an adapted Point Transformer v3 backbone with Group Normalization and restricted 3D features positions the work within the modern 3D point cloud transformer literature while explicitly targeting cross-sensor invariance rather than dataset-specific accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a single self-supervised model can achieve near-supervised ground segmentation accuracy (within ~3 mIoU of the labeled oracle) across three major benchmarks and 15 LiDAR hardware models without any manual annotations, which is practically significant for robotics deployments where per-sensor retraining or re-annotation is infeasible. The OmniLiDAR dataset (22M scans, 12 sources) is a reusable resource for the community. Open questions include whether temporal context can further improve pseudo-label quality in sparse regions, whether iterative self-training can close the remaining gap to supervised performance, and whether foundation-model initialization (e.g., Sonata) can reduce the data scale needed for convergence.
