## Problem

Conventional directly-trained SNNs apply identical supervision across all timesteps, causing channel activations to converge toward the same representational subspace and producing temporally redundant, highly correlated features. This temporal redundancy means later timesteps contribute little novel discriminative information, fundamentally limiting the multi-timestep advantage that SNNs are designed to exploit.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261880/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts static RGB images (CIFAR-100 at 32×32, ImageNet at 224×224) or DVS event streams (DVS-CIFAR10) and outputs a single class label. During training, each input is processed over T timesteps (T=4 for static images, T=10 for event streams) with LIF neurons; the TRE gating mechanism is applied only at timesteps t>1 during training and is completely disabled at inference, so the deployed network incurs zero additional latency or memory cost relative to the unmodified backbone.

## Method

TRE reformulates multi-timestep SNN training as a learning-to-forget process: at each timestep t>1, it estimates a class-specific contribution score for every channel via a class-conditional attribution formula that measures the marginal change in softmax confidence attributable to that channel, using temporally accumulated features and logits as a prior. Channels whose contribution exceeds a hybrid threshold (the maximum of a fixed baseline and a data-dependent mean-plus-sigma threshold) are suppressed through a binary gating vector applied via broadcasted element-wise multiplication on the feature tensor. The modulated features are then passed through the shared classifier and supervised with cross-entropy, while timestep 1 retains unmodulated supervision. This forces each subsequent timestep to rely on complementary, underutilized semantic channels rather than re-firing the same dominant patterns, and the adaptive (rather than fixed top-k) threshold prevents over-suppression of informative features.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, TRE achieves 81.27% top-1 accuracy with ResNet-19 at T=4 timesteps, surpassing the strongest directly-trained baseline TTS (80.20%) by +1.07% and the conversion-based SlipReLU (78.55% at T=128) by +2.72% with 32× fewer timesteps. On ImageNet, TRE reaches 71.60% with ResNet-34 (T=4), exceeding PSN (70.54%) by +1.06% and MPBN (64.71%) by +6.89%. On the event-based DVS-CIFAR10 dataset, TRE attains 83.60% with ResNet-19 (T=10), outperforming TET (83.00%) by +0.60%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-100, TRE (81.27%, ResNet-19, T=4) outperforms all directly-trained SNN baselines: TTS (80.20%, T=2), GAC-SNN (79.83%, T=4), SLTT (74.67%, T=6), TET (74.47%, T=4), and STBP-tdBN (71.12%, T=6), and also surpasses the strongest conversion method SlipReLU (78.55%) by +2.72% while using 32× fewer timesteps. On ImageNet, TRE with ResNet-34 (71.60%) beats PSN (70.54%) by +1.06%, MPBN (64.71%) by +6.89%, Sew ResNet (67.04%) by +4.56%, and Real Spike (63.68%) by +7.92%, all at T=4. On DVS-CIFAR10, TRE (83.60%, ResNet-19, T=10) edges out TET (83.00%) by +0.60% and substantially outperforms LM-H (79.10%), LSG (77.90%), SLTT (77.30%), and all other baselines. The consistent gains across static and event-based datasets, and across ResNet-18/19/34 backbones, indicate that TRE's channel-gating mechanism is architecture- and modality-agnostic. Notably, TRE requires no architectural modification and adds zero inference overhead, as the gating is disabled at test time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of directly-trained SNN work (TET, SLTT, PSN, MPBN) that seeks to stabilize or align temporal representations through auxiliary consistency constraints or decoupled supervision. Instead, it repurposes the spatial masking/erasing idea from CNNs (self-erasing networks, dropblock) and transplants it into the temporal domain of SNNs, reframing temporal learning as a 'learning-to-forget' process driven by class-specific channel contribution estimation. The mechanism is parameter-free and operates as a training-time-only modulation, distinguishing it from architectural modifications (SEW-ResNet, PLIF) or normalization-based approaches (tdBN, TEBN, PSP-BN).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRE offers a lightweight, inference-free mechanism to combat temporal redundancy—a well-motivated but under-addressed limitation in multi-timestep SNN training—making it practically attractive for neuromorphic deployment where latency and energy are critical. However, the evaluation is limited to ResNet-19/34 backbones with 4–10 timesteps on classification tasks; whether the binary hard-gating strategy scales to deeper architectures, longer temporal windows, or non-classification tasks (detection, segmentation) remains open. The ~10% training overhead and the sensitivity of performance to the fixed threshold θ₀ᶜ also suggest the method may require careful tuning per architecture.
