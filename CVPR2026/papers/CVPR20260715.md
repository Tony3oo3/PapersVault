## Problem

Continual Test-Time Adaptation (CTTA) for semantic segmentation suffers from error accumulation under persistent domain shifts, where noisy pseudo-labels at the onset propagate and amplify over time. The paper identifies a geometric root cause: Euclidean feature spaces produce non-uniform, fragile inter-class margins that make models highly sensitive to distributional shifts, and existing CTTA methods lack a principled mechanism to balance plasticity (rapid adaptation) against stability (preventing drift).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260715/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential stream of unlabeled RGB frames from evolving target domains (e.g., 1920×1080 for ACDC, 1280×720 for IDD-AW, 1280×800 for SHIFT) and produces a dense per-pixel segmentation mask over N closed-set semantic classes. Adaptation is fully online: each incoming frame triggers a single Riemannian gradient step on the Student network, with no access to source-domain data. The source model is pre-trained on labeled data (Cityscapes or IDD) at 1024×1024 resolution before being deployed on the target stream.

## Method

HyperProtoSeg reframes segmentation as metric learning in the Poincaré ball: a SegFormer-B5 backbone produces Euclidean features that are projected via the exponential map into a 128-D hyperbolic manifold, where each class is represented by a learnable interior prototype and pixels are classified by nearest-prototype under geodesic distance. Source-domain training combines a softmax-over-geodesic-distances classification loss, a norm regularization that prevents tanh saturation at the manifold boundary, and a prototype separation loss enforcing a minimum geodesic distance between all prototype pairs. For online adaptation, HBCA partitions pixels into confident 'core' and uncertain 'boundary' sets via cross-view prediction consistency with a Teacher (EMA of the Student); core pixels drive adaptation through geodesic distance minimization (high plasticity), while boundary pixels undergo a scale-invariant directional alignment loss in the tangent space relative to the Teacher's nearest prototype (high stability), preventing noisy pseudo-labels from corrupting the geometric structure.

## Results

On the IDD→IDD-AW benchmark (10 sequential adaptation rounds across fog, night, rain, and snow), HBCA achieves 57.12% mean mIoU, a +11.3% gain over the non-adapting source model (51.31%) and +2.21% over the strongest baseline Hybrid TTA (54.91%). On Cityscapes→ACDC, HBCA reaches 57.47% mean mIoU, outperforming the best prior method TCA (56.38%) by 1.09%. On the SHIFT synthetic-to-real benchmark, HBCA attains 69.36% mean mIoU. The paper reports average improvements of 1.94%, 4.02%, and 1.24% over state-of-the-art CTTA methods on Cityscapes→ACDC, IDD→IDD-AW, and SHIFT respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HBCA outperforms all ten compared baselines on every benchmark. Against the most recent CTTA methods, it leads Hybrid TTA [35] (ICCV'25) by 2.21% and TCA [30] (CVPR'25) by 2.36% mean mIoU on IDD→IDD-AW, with similar margins on Cityscapes→ACDC (57.47% vs. 55.95% and 56.38% respectively). The earlier entropy- and prompt-based methods—TENT [46], CoTTA [47], DePT [15], and SVDP [53]—show substantially smaller gains (+0.2% to +4.2% over source) and degrade noticeably by round 10, whereas HBCA maintains a near-flat trajectory (57.84% → 56.82%). Reconstruction-based Continual-MAE [24] and uncertainty-guided DAT [31] improve stability over entropy minimization but still trail HBCA by ~2.8%, confirming that geometric (hyperbolic) supervision and directional boundary regularization provide a distinct advantage over Euclidean pseudo-label or reconstruction objectives. In the source-domain ablation (Table 2), HyperProtoSeg's hyperbolic prototypical head surpasses the Euclidean ProtoSeg (74.34% vs. 73.87% on IDD) and the prior hyperbolic MLR classifier [1] (74.34% vs. 73.91%), while in the uncertainty-estimation ablation (Table 6) HBCA's consistency-driven masking beats MC-Dropout [13] by 4.34% mean mIoU, demonstrating that cross-view prediction consistency is a far more reliable uncertainty signal under domain shift than stochastic dropout or embedding-norm heuristics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: hyperbolic geometric segmentation (Atigh et al., Weber et al., Berg et al.), which encodes class structure in the Poincaré ball but operates in a static, single-domain setting, and Euclidean CTTA methods (CoTTA, DAT, TCA, Hybrid-TTA), which handle sequential domain shifts but suffer from distortion-prone feature spaces. The prototypical classification paradigm from Snell et al. is transplanted into hyperbolic space, and the CoTTA teacher-student EMA loop is retained but augmented with a novel uncertainty-aware dual geometric loss (geodesic distance for confident pixels, tangent-space directional alignment for uncertain ones). The key departure from prior hyperbolic segmentation is the use of learnable interior prototypes rather than ideal-boundary ones, enabling the prototypes to shift during continual adaptation while preserving geometric stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean geometric explanation for why CTTA degrades over time—Euclidean polynomial volume growth produces fragile, narrow inter-class margins—and shows that hyperbolic exponential volume growth mitigates this, reducing 10-round degradation from −1.46% to −0.62% mIoU. The uncertainty-aware boundary loss (Table 7) offers a practical, well-ablated recipe for preventing error amplification in pseudo-label-driven adaptation. Limitations include the closed-set assumption, reliance on a single backbone (SegFormer-B5), evaluation restricted to driving-scene benchmarks, and the theoretical analysis remaining at the Rademacher-complexity sketch level without tight constants. Open questions include whether adaptive curvature learning (mentioned as future work) can further tighten margins, whether the hyperbolic advantage persists at ImageNet-scale class counts, and whether the framework extends to open-vocabulary or video segmentation.
