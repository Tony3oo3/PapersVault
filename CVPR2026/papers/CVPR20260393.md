## Problem

Fine-tuning Vision-Language Models faces a persistent three-way trade-off: improving in-distribution accuracy, preserving out-of-distribution generalization, and resisting adversarial attacks. Existing robust fine-tuning strategies resolve at most two of these axes simultaneously—generalization-preserving methods remain adversarially vulnerable, while adversarial training degrades ID and OOD accuracy. The paper identifies the root cause as two coupled geometric failures: sharp, anisotropic minima in parameter space and feature manifolds that deform under perturbation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260393/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (processed at 224×224 through a CLIP ViT-B/32 backbone) paired with text prompts for classification, producing a class label over ImageNet-1K categories. During training, each input is simultaneously processed in three states—clean, PGD-adversarial (ε=4/255, 10 steps), and low-rank weight-perturbed—to compute the Gram-volume alignment loss, making the training loop consume three forward passes per sample. Evaluation spans clean, PGD-adversarial (AutoAttack/APGD-CE), and natural adversarial (ImageNet-A/A-Plus) test conditions across ID, OOD, and zero-shot settings.

## Method

GRACE (Gram-aligned Robustness via Adaptive Curvature Estimation) is a unified fine-tuning framework grounded in a robust PAC-Bayesian bound that decomposes robust risk into proximity-to-prior, parameter-space sharpness, and cross-domain feature discrepancy terms. It instantiates these via three modules: (1) LoRA adapters constrain updates to a low-rank subspace around frozen pretrained weights, controlling the KL proximity term; (2) Layer-wise Adaptive Low-Rank Adversarial Weight Perturbation (LAR-AWP) injects additional low-rank weight perturbations whose rank is allocated per layer proportional to local curvature (estimated via Sophia-style mini-batch gradient Hessian-diagonal approximation), driving optimization toward flatter minima; and (3) a Gram-Volume Alignment Loss constructs a 3×3 Gram matrix from ℓ₂-normalized clean, adversarial, and AWP-perturbed image embeddings, then minimizes its determinant to enforce that all three representations remain geometrically close, preserving class-conditional structure across distribution shifts. The combined objective L_task + λ_LAR·L_LAR-AWP + λ_GV·L_GV is optimized with alternating inner maximization over weight perturbations and outer minimization over LoRA parameters.

## Results

On ImageNet fine-tuning of CLIP ViT-B/32, GRACE achieves 74.21% ID top-1 accuracy, 57.01% average OOD accuracy (ImageNet-V2/S/R), and 22.44% average adversarial accuracy (PGD-ℓ∞, ε=4/255, AutoAttack), yielding a harmonic mean of 39.69. This represents a +10.86 pp gain in ID accuracy over the zero-shot CLIP baseline (63.35%) and a +13.49 pp gain in adversarial accuracy over vanilla fine-tuning (8.95%), while OOD accuracy (57.01%) remains within 0.43 pp of the zero-shot CLIP baseline (57.44%). GRACE outperforms all prior robust fine-tuning and adversarial fine-tuning baselines on the harmonic mean, which simultaneously penalizes weakness on any single axis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GRACE is the only method that simultaneously leads on all three axes (ID, OOD, adversarial) in the harmonic-mean sense. Against generalization-focused baselines (WiSE-FT, FLYP, TPGM, SPD), GRACE matches or exceeds ID accuracy (74.21% vs. 70.20–74.13%) while improving adversarial accuracy by 13.4–13.9 pp (22.44% vs. 8.49–9.04%), at the cost of a ≤1.0 pp OOD drop. Against adversarial-training baselines (TeCoA, FARE, PMG-AFT, LAAT), GRACE dominates on ID by 16.0–26.8 pp and on OOD by 13.6–19.1 pp, while also achieving higher adversarial accuracy than all four (22.44% vs. 13.43–19.57%). The closest competitor on adversarial accuracy is PMG-AFT (19.57%), but PMG-AFT sacrifices 16.0 pp of ID and 13.6 pp of OOD accuracy to reach it. On zero-shot transfer (8 datasets), GRACE achieves 59.61% clean / 21.10% adversarial average, outperforming WiSE-FT (60.40% / 0.00%) on adversarial robustness while remaining within 0.8 pp on clean accuracy, and substantially exceeding TeCoA (38.75% / 20.78%) on clean accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

GRACE synthesizes three distinct research threads into a single fine-tuning objective: adversarial weight perturbation (AWP, Wu et al. 2020) for parameter-space flattening, Sophia's gradient-based Hessian-diagonal estimation (Liu et al. 2024) for curvature-aware rank allocation, and invariance-based feature alignment from FARE (Schlarmann et al. 2024) for representation stability. The key departure from prior work is the explicit PAC-Bayes decomposition of robust risk into proximity, sharpness, and domain-discrepancy terms, which motivates the joint optimization rather than the sequential or single-axis approaches used by generalization-focused methods (WiSE-FT, TPGM, SPD) or adversarial-training methods (TeCoA, FARE, PMG-AFT, LAAT). By restricting all updates to a shared low-rank LoRA subspace, GRACE unifies the perturbation geometry of both the adaptation and the adversarial weight perturbation, a structural choice absent from prior AWP or invariance-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The three-way ID/OOD/adversarial trade-off is a central deployment bottleneck for VLMs, and GRACE provides the first framework with a unified theoretical bound (robust PAC-Bayes) that explicitly targets all three axes simultaneously. However, the practical gains are more nuanced than the headline suggests: OOD accuracy is essentially maintained (57.0% vs. 57.4% zero-shot CLIP) rather than improved, and all experiments use only the ViT-B/32 backbone on ImageNet, leaving open whether the curvature-adaptive rank curriculum and Gram-volume loss transfer to larger backbones (ViT-L, ViT-H) or non-ImageNet domains. The modest OOD delta and single-backbone evaluation limit the generality claim, and it remains unclear whether the Gram-volume term's small ablation contribution (+1.5% OOD) justifies its computational overhead at scale.
