## Problem

Existing instruction-driven 3D editing methods rely on iterative 2D-3D-2D optimization loops over explicit 3D representations (NeRF, Gaussian Splatting), which are both task-specific—appearance editing requires preserving source geometry while removal demands geometric deformation—and extremely slow, often requiring tens of minutes per edit. No prior learning-based framework unifies diverse editing tasks (removal, addition, appearance) for scene-level assets with arbitrary viewpoints in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260285/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N=10 multi-view RGB images (576×576) of a source 3D scene from arbitrary viewpoints along with a free-form text editing instruction. Camera poses are extracted via VGGT and converted to Plücker embeddings. A single randomly selected view is edited by Qwen-Image to produce a reference condition image. The output is a set of consistently edited multi-view images, which can optionally be reconstructed into an edited 3D Gaussian splatting asset via AnySplat in seconds. Notably, the method operates in a mask-free manner, requiring no object segmentation or point-matching pre-processing.

## Method

Omni-3DEdit repurposes the pre-trained multi-view diffusion model SEVA into an editing model (OmniNet) by concatenating source view latents, a condition (edited reference) latent, and noisy target latents in sequence space, distinguished by indicator tokens (−1, 1, 0) and supplemented with Plücker camera-pose embeddings. The key architectural contribution is a dual-stream LoRA module: a Geometry LoRA encodes source view latents to capture geometric priors, while a Guidance LoRA propagates editing cues from the condition latent to the target, with the two streams exchanging information through shared multi-view attention layers. This disentangled design resolves the learning conflict that arises when functionally distinct inputs (context vs. editing signal) are processed by shared projection layers, which the authors show causes both generative degradation and loss of unedited context. Training uses an EDM noise schedule with Eps-weighted MSE loss computed only on target view latents, and the model is trained on ~50K synthetic paired multi-view samples generated via a four-stage pipeline (Gemini instruction generation → Qwen-Image per-view editing → SDEdit-based consistency refinement → quality filtering) across CO3Dv2, DL3DV, and WildRGB-D.

## Results

On the 360-USID 3D removal benchmark, Omni-3DEdit achieves an average PSNR of 17.72 and LPIPS of 0.395, outperforming the strongest baseline Aurafusion360 (PSNR 17.66, LPIPS 0.388) in PSNR while being competitive in LPIPS, and substantially surpassing SPIn-NeRF (PSNR 16.73) and 2DGS+LaMa (PSNR 16.20). On CO3Dv2 for 3D addition, Omni-3DEdit reaches PSNR 20.67 and LPIPS 0.278, beating MVInpainter (PSNR 19.20, LPIPS 0.344) by a clear margin. For complex multi-step editing, Omni-3DEdit attains the highest Gemini score (4.0) and CLIP directional score (0.170) while completing inference in ~2 minutes, compared to 5–28 minutes for iterative baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On 3D removal (360-USID), Omni-3DEdit achieves the highest average PSNR (17.72) among all 8 baselines, edging out Aurafusion360 (17.66) by 0.06 dB while Aurafusion360 retains a slight LPIPS advantage (0.388 vs. 0.395); the gap widens substantially over SPIn-NeRF (+0.99 dB) and 2DGS+LaMa (+1.53 dB). On 3D addition (CO3Dv2), Omni-3DEdit surpasses MVInpainter by 1.47 dB in PSNR and 0.066 in LPIPS, and also leads in CLIP-T (0.277 vs. 0.271), while ZeroNVS trails by over 6 dB due to its inability to leverage source-view context. For complex multi-step editing, Omni-3DEdit outperforms all iterative 3D-representation baselines (DGE, GaussianEditor, ViCANeRF) on every metric—CLIP-T/I, CLIP-Dir, and Gemini score—by margins of 0.029–0.040 in CLIP-T/I and 1.8–2.3 in Gemini score, while also being 2.5×–14× faster (2 min vs. 5–28 min). Against the 2D in-context editor Nano-banana (Gemini 2.5), Omni-3DEdit achieves a marginally higher Gemini score (4.0 vs. 3.8) and CLIP-Dir (0.170 vs. 0.165) while producing more geometrically consistent multi-view results without requiring the source views to be concatenated into a single image.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant paradigms in instruction-driven 3D editing: (1) iterative 2D-3D-2D optimization loops that maintain explicit NeRF/Gaussian representations (InstructN2N, DGE, SPIn-NeRF), and (2) object-level 3D latent editing (Tailor3D, CMD) constrained to object-centric camera distributions. Instead, it repurposes the pre-trained multi-view generative model SEVA as a backbone, performing editing directly in multi-view latent space with a novel dual-stream LoRA that disentangles source geometry cues from editing guidance. The data synthesis pipeline borrows SDEdit-style consistency refinement and the reverse-pairing trick from VIVID-10M, while leveraging Qwen-Image and VGGT as off-the-shelf components for reference editing and camera estimation respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from iterative optimization to single-pass inference (tens of minutes → ~2 minutes) makes instruction-based 3D editing practically usable in interactive settings, and the unified treatment of removal, addition, and appearance editing removes the need for task-specific pipelines. However, the training dataset is limited to ~0.1M pairs, which the authors acknowledge constrains fine-grained editing (e.g., adding a bracelet to a wrist). Open questions include whether the dual-stream LoRA architecture generalizes to even more diverse editing operations, whether the data synthesis pipeline can be scaled with better 2D editors, and whether the approach extends to dynamic or video-based 3D content.
