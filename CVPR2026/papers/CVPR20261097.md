## Problem

Existing instruction-guided image editing benchmarks oversimplify scene complexity (sparse layouts, minimal occlusions), rely on atomic-level instructions that lack spatial reasoning and compositional logic, and suffer from instruction-alignment inaccuracies and visual artifacts in their ground-truth edits. These deficiencies inflate model scores on simplified tasks while failing to expose the gaps that matter for real-world editing involving dense multi-object scenes, multi-turn interactions, and implicit contextual reasoning.

## Input / Output

Each benchmark sample pairs a source RGB image (sourced from the MOSE video object segmentation dataset to ensure complex, cluttered scenes with high occlusion and out-of-frame rates) with a structured text editing instruction, and the expected output is a single edited image. Evaluation employs a foreground-background decoupling strategy: foreground quality is measured via CLIP image similarity (LC-I) and local CLIP text alignment (LC-T), while background preservation is scored with PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS. For action, location, and viewpoint editing where morphology changes significantly, automatic metrics are supplemented by multi-perspective 0–10 scoring from GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, and trained human annotators.

## Method

CompBench is constructed through an MLLM-Human Collaborative Framework with four task-specific pipelines (local editing, action/scene spatial editing, complex reasoning, and multi-editing) that generate initial instructions via multimodal LLMs and validate them through iterative human review, discarding unsuccessful edits. A key design choice is the Instruction Decomposition Strategy, which structures every editing instruction along four orthogonal dimensions—spatial positioning, visual attributes, motion states, and object entities—converting ambiguous requests into well-defined specifications while preserving natural-language expressiveness. The benchmark spans five major categories and nine tasks (object addition/removal/replacement, multi-object editing, multi-turn editing, implicit reasoning, action editing, location editing, viewpoint editing) with over 3,000 high-quality samples. Source images are filtered from MOSE using NIQE quality metrics, single-object mask decomposition, and MLLM-based occlusion/continuity checks, yielding scenes with ~13.6 average objects and 98.5% occlusion rate—substantially more complex than prior benchmarks.

## Results

On CompBench's most challenging tasks (Action, Location, Viewpoint editing), Qwen-Image-Edit achieves the highest average scores (6.352, 5.603, 5.615 respectively), closely followed by Bagel (6.195, 6.530, 4.583). Bagel is the most consistent performer overall, securing top results in 18 out of 37 metrics across all nine tasks. In contrast, HQ-Edit performs substantially worse than all other models (e.g., 1.003 avg on Action, 0.253 on Viewpoint), and InstructPix2pix shows a notable decline of ~2.5 PSNR and 0.4 CLIP-Score on CompBench compared to its performance on the simpler ReasonEdit benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Bagel [5] is the most consistent top performer, achieving the best score in 18 of 37 metrics across all nine CompBench tasks, and leads on Location editing (6.530 avg) and foreground fidelity (LC-I) across local/multi-object tasks. Qwen-Image-Edit [38] edges out Bagel on Action editing (6.352 vs. 6.195) and Viewpoint editing (5.615 vs. 4.583), making it the strongest model on the two hardest spatial tasks. Step1X-Edit [21] is the clear third tier, scoring 5.187 on Action and 4.769 on Location—roughly 1–2 points below the leaders—while FLUX.1 Kontext [16] excels at background preservation (SSIM 0.941–0.952 on local/multi-object) but lags on Action (4.296) and Viewpoint (2.984). HQ-Edit [15] and MGIE [11] are substantially outperformed by all other models on every task, with HQ-Edit scoring below 1.1 on all three spatial editing categories. On multi-turn editing, all models show a marked drop in background SSIM from Turn 1 to Turn 2 (e.g., InstructPix2pix drops from 0.682 to 0.573), though SmartEdit [14] retains the most stable second-turn performance among mid-tier models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper does not build on a specific editing method but rather carves new ground in the benchmarking landscape for instruction-guided image editing. It departs from prior benchmarks (MagicBrush, EMU-Edit, ReasonEdit, I²EBench, GEdit-Bench, Complex-Edit) by sourcing scenes from the MOSE video segmentation dataset to achieve substantially higher scene complexity (13.58 avg. objects vs. ~10 for prior benchmarks), and by introducing task categories—multi-object, action, viewpoint, and implicit reasoning editing—that are entirely absent from existing benchmarks. The MLLM-Human collaborative construction framework and the four-dimensional instruction decomposition strategy (location, appearance, dynamics, objects) are methodological contributions to benchmark construction rather than to the editing task itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CompBench provides the first systematic evaluation showing that no current model dominates across all complex editing dimensions, and identifies planner-executor misalignment (MLLM correctly identifies targets but diffusion model fails at precise masking) as a widespread architectural bottleneck. The finding that geometric hallucination remains severe for action and viewpoint editing, even in top-performing models like Bagel and Qwen-Image-Edit, highlights that physical consistency is an unsolved problem. However, the benchmark's relatively small size (3K samples) and reliance on GPT-4o/Qwen2.5-VL-72B for subjective scoring of action/location/viewpoint tasks limit statistical robustness and introduce potential evaluator bias, leaving open whether conclusions generalize to larger-scale or more diverse evaluation protocols.
