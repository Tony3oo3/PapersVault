## Problem

Latent diffusion models like Stable Diffusion 1.5 encode powerful generative priors essential for high-quality image restoration, yet their full pipelines are too computationally heavy for edge deployment. Existing lightweight variants predominantly compress the denoising U-Net or shorten the diffusion trajectory, which disrupts the underlying latent manifold and limits generalization beyond a single task. The paper addresses the need for a full-pipeline co-design that preserves the generative prior while producing models deployable on mobile-class NPUs in real time.

## Input / Output

NanoSD operates as a drop-in latent diffusion backbone: a VAE encoder compresses the input RGB image (or degraded image plus text prompt) into a latent representation, the U-Net performs denoising in latent space, and a VAE decoder reconstructs the output. Depending on the attached task framework (OSEDiff, S3Diff, OSDFace, DiffPlugin, DiffBIR, or Marigold), the input ranges from a low-resolution image for super-resolution to a blurred/hazy/rainy/snowy image for restoration, or a single RGB image for depth estimation. Outputs are high-quality restored images at the target resolution, monocular depth maps, or text-conditioned generated images, all produced in a single or few diffusion steps to meet real-time latency budgets (down to 20 ms on a Qualcomm NPU with 8-bit weights and 16-bit activations).

## Method

NanoSD is constructed through a four-stage pipeline: (1) hardware-aware decomposition of the SD 1.5 U-Net into six retained stages (three encoders and three decoders, with the deepest encoder, middle block, and deepest decoder removed), where each stage is replaced by shape-preserving residual/attention block variants forming a 32,768-architecture combinatorial search space; (2) feature-wise generative distillation, where each candidate block is independently trained to match its SD 1.5 teacher block via an L2 feature-matching loss, enabling massively parallel training without full-model retraining; (3) multi-objective Bayesian optimization over teacher-aligned FID (taFID), on-device latency, and parameter count to identify a Pareto-optimal set of U-Net backbones, from which a balanced architecture (NanoSD-Prime, ~315M parameters, 27 ms latency) is selected; and (4) VAE encoder-decoder distillation followed by end-to-end fine-tuning with the standard diffusion denoising objective to correct accumulated block-level discrepancies. The key insight is that parameter reduction alone does not predict hardware efficiency—architectural balance, feature routing, and latent-space preservation jointly determine true on-device latency, which is why the search explicitly profiles candidates on the target NPU rather than relying on FLOPs or parameter counts.

## Results

On DIV2K-Val for single-step SR, Nano-S3Diff achieves the best NIQE (4.09) and MUSIQ (70.44) with a competitive FID of 22.34, while Nano-OSEDiff attains the highest PSNR (24.29) and SSIM (0.628) among all compared methods. For face restoration on CelebA-Test, Nano-OSDFace matches OSDFace closely (FID 45.92 vs 45.41, NIQE 3.913 vs 3.884) while reducing MACs from 2465G to 479G and parameters from 1887M to 415M. Across multi-task restoration (desnowing, dehazing, deblurring, deraining), Nano-Diff-Plugin achieves FID scores within 0.5–0.8 of the full Diff-Plugin while cutting MACs by ~44% (17120G vs 30400G) and parameters by ~43% (712M vs 1256M). For monocular depth estimation, Nano-Marigold achieves AbsRel of 7.2 on NYUv2 and 11.8 on KITTI, slightly behind Marigold (5.5/9.9) but with substantially lower computational cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In SR, Nano-S3Diff outperforms all baselines on perceptual quality (NIQE 4.09 vs 4.15–4.41; MUSIQ 70.44 vs 66.38–69.90) and achieves the best FID (22.34 vs 22.94–25.52), while Nano-OSEDiff leads on fidelity (PSNR 24.29, SSIM 0.628) but trails on perceptual metrics. Both Nano variants use fewer MACs than AdcSR (496G) and TinySR (427G), though Edge-SD-SR reports no MACs and PocketSR is lighter (225G) at the cost of worse perceptual scores. In face restoration, Nano-OSDFace nearly matches OSDFace on all metrics (FID 45.92 vs 45.41; NIQE 3.913 vs 3.884; LMD 5.172 vs 5.286) while reducing MACs by 81% (479G vs 2465G) and parameters by 78% (415M vs 1887M); it also outperforms DiffBIR on FID (45.92 vs 71.77) and NIQE (3.913 vs 6.280). For multi-task restoration, Nano-Diff-Plugin is within 0.5 FID of Diff-Plugin across all four tasks (e.g., desnowing 34.83 vs 34.30; deblurring 52.41 vs 51.81) while halving MACs (17120G vs 30400G) and parameters (712M vs 1256M), and substantially outperforms ControlNet and Null-Text-Inversion. In depth estimation, Nano-Marigold trails Marigold by 1.7 AbsRel on NYUv2 (7.2 vs 5.5) and 1.9 on KITTI (11.8 vs 9.9), but still surpasses MiDAS, DPT, and HDN on KITTI, confirming that the distilled backbone retains strong generative priors for dense prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

NanoSD builds directly on Stable Diffusion 1.5 as its generative backbone but departs from prior lightweight diffusion efforts (SnapFusion, MobileDiff, SnapGen, Segmind TinySD) that compress only the U-Net or shorten the diffusion trajectory, and from task-specific distillation methods (AdcSR, Edge-SD-SR, TinySR, PocketSR) that sacrifice the full generative prior for a single restoration task. Its distinguishing move is a full-pipeline co-design: hardware-aware block-level decomposition of the U-Net, independent feature-wise distillation of each block variant, Bayesian optimization over a 32,768-architecture search space to obtain a Pareto frontier, and subsequent VAE distillation—treating the entire latent diffusion pipeline as a jointly optimizable unit rather than compressing components in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 130–315M-parameter diffusion pipeline can run in as little as 12–27 ms on a mobile-class NPU while retaining competitive restoration quality across SR, face restoration, deblurring, dehazing, deraining, desnowing, and depth estimation, NanoSD makes diffusion-based low-level vision practically deployable on consumer hardware for the first time in a multi-task setting. The finding that parameter count alone is a poor proxy for on-device latency (architectural balance and operator patterns matter more) is a useful caution for future edge-ML design. Open questions include whether the block-level distillation + BO recipe transfers to newer, larger diffusion backbones (SD3, Flux), whether the SD 1.5 latent space remains the best substrate as generative models evolve, and how well the Pareto family generalizes to tasks beyond those evaluated (e.g., video restoration, 3D generation).
