## Problem

Existing source-free domain adaptation (SFDA) and partial domain adaptation (PDA) methods inadvertently transfer knowledge of source-exclusive classes into the target domain, creating a privacy risk when sensitive classes (e.g., military sites, mental health conditions) must not be retained. Prior machine unlearning methods either require access to forget data or are designed for stationary single-domain settings and fail under distribution shift, leaving no practical solution for unlearning source-exclusive classes during domain adaptation without source data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260481/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained source classifier (CNN), a set of unlabeled target-domain images, and the labels of source-exclusive classes to forget; it never accesses source training data or any target-domain samples of the forget classes. Internally, it generates adversarial representative samples for each forget class by optimizing inputs to maximize the model's confidence on that class. The output is a single adapted model whose parameters have been modified to eliminate the forget-class representations while preserving (and improving) performance on retain classes in the target domain.

## Method

The core mechanism is a joint min-max optimization that simultaneously adapts the model to the target domain and unlearned forget classes. For each forget class, an adversarial sample is generated by minimizing cross-entropy loss (i.e., maximizing the model's probability of that class), and is then fed back into training with a novel rescaled label: the forget-class probability is zeroed and the remaining probabilities are renormalized over retain classes, providing a natural soft target that minimizes conflict with the adaptation objective. The model is updated each step by minimizing a combined loss of the SFDA adaptation term (following SF(DA)²) and the cross-entropy unlearning term on the adversarial sample with rescaled labels, while the adversarial sample is re-optimized to track the evolving model. A theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that this rescaled labeling induces strictly larger gradient magnitudes on retain-class output weights than on forget-class weights, ensuring the model progressively erases forget-class representations while strengthening retain-class learning.

## Results

On the multi-class SCADA-UL setting (forgetting 3 source-exclusive classes), the proposed method achieves 0.0% target forget accuracy across all three benchmark datasets (OfficeHome, Office31, DomainNet-126), matching the retraining upper bound exactly. Retain accuracy is within 0.7–1.1% of retraining: 75.1% vs 76.3% (OfficeHome), 76.6% vs 77.4% (Office31), and 65.6% vs 66.3% (DomainNet). The unlearning score (A_r/(100+A_f)) reaches 0.75, 0.77, and 0.66 respectively, compared to 0.48, 0.40, and 0.50 for the original SF(DA)² model that leaks source-exclusive classes with 58–90% zero-shot accuracy on the target forget set.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves retraining-level unlearning (0.0% forget accuracy) while retaining within 1% of retraining accuracy, outperforming all 10 baselines on the unlearning score across all three datasets. Compared to the original SF(DA)² adaptation, the method eliminates the severe source-exclusive class leak (reducing forget accuracy from 58–90% to 0%) with negligible retain accuracy loss. PDA methods (PADA, SHOT) fail to fully suppress the leak, retaining 25–90% forget accuracy, confirming that partial adaptation is insufficient for the stricter unlearning requirement. Among existing MU methods, UNSIR and Nabla Tau achieve low forget accuracy but at the cost of catastrophic retain degradation (35% and 44–63% respectively), while ZSMU, Lipschitz, and Unlearned(+) still leak 25–85% on the forget set. The method also outperforms the Finetune baseline, which retains 20–83% forget accuracy, demonstrating that simple target-domain fine-tuning cannot remove source-exclusive class knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of machine unlearning (MU) and source-free domain adaptation (SFDA), two areas that have developed largely independently. The Bayesian unlearning objective is derived from the posterior decomposition in Panda et al. [44] (Partially Blinded Unlearning) and the Laplace posterior approximation from Ritter et al. [46], while the adaptation component leverages SF(DA)² [28] and SHOT [34]. The key departure is that existing MU methods (including data-free variants like ZSMU and UNSIR) assume a stationary single-domain setting and fail under distribution shift, while existing SFDA/PDA methods align source and target distributions globally without addressing unlearning. The paper's adversarial sample generation with rescaled labeling is a novel bridge that enables simultaneous unlearning and adaptation without access to source or target forget data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As vision models are increasingly adapted across domains (medical imaging, satellite imagery, fraud detection), regulatory frameworks like GDPR and HIPAA mandate that sensitive source-exclusive classes be fully removed from adapted models — a requirement that current SFDA and PDA methods silently violate by leaking these classes into the target domain. The paper's empirical demonstration that existing data-free MU methods degrade substantially under domain shift is a useful caution for practitioners attempting to compose unlearning and adaptation pipelines. Open questions include whether adversarial samples generated from a single model's decision boundary adequately represent the full forget-class distribution, how the approach scales to very large class spaces or non-classification tasks (segmentation, detection), and whether the continual and unknown-class variants maintain the same guarantees under repeated or adversarial unlearning requests.
