## Problem

Existing reference-based color grading methods suffer from two complementary failures: photorealistic style transfer approaches produce excessive tonal shifts and texture distortions, while filter-based methods retain or accumulate pre-existing stylistic bias in the input rather than adaptively restyling toward the reference. No prior method provides a mechanism to neutralize an input's intrinsic tonal bias before applying a reference style, leading to unstable and inconsistent tone mappings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261290/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB input image X and an RGB reference image R, both resized to a shorter side of 480 pixels during training. Internally, a MobileNet-v2-based grade extractor encodes each image into a latent grading vector, and two separate LUT generators produce a 3D canonical LUT T_c and a 3D grading LUT T_g (each of size N×N×N×3) that are applied sequentially to yield the final color-graded output Z. The LUT-based formulation ensures deterministic, spatially consistent tone adjustment across the entire image.

## Method

CanonCGT introduces a canonical pivot — a style-neutral intermediate representation — and decomposes color grading into two decoupled stages: a canonicalizer that removes the input's intrinsic tonal bias (many-to-one mapping) and a grader that imposes the reference tone (one-to-many mapping). Both stages share a conditioned LUT generator architecture that combines a CNN feature extractor with transformer encoder/decoder blocks, where FiLM layers modulate features using the extracted grading vector to produce image-adaptive 3D LUTs. Training follows a dual-phase scheme (DP-CGT): a supervised phase on FiveK with 56 Lightroom presets using contrastive learning for the grade extractor and reconstruction losses for the LUT generators, followed by a self-supervised phase on ~88K unlabeled photographs where two crops from the same image are symmetrically perturbed and reconstructed to generalize beyond preset-defined styles.

## Results

Across all six unsupervised test datasets (LSDIR, PPR10K, DIV2K, Food-101, GLD-v2, and FiveK val.), CanonCGT achieves 28.99 PSNR, 0.9608 SSIM, 5.46 ΔE_ab, 0.0665 LPIPS, 0.8933 SSIM_ED, 0.5204 H-Corr, and 0.1785 H-Chi — substantially outperforming the strongest baseline (Deep Preset) by ~10.4 dB in PSNR, ~64% lower ΔE_ab, and ~62% lower LPIPS. In a 25-participant user study over 1,250 trials, CanonCGT also attains the best (lowest) average ranks in both tonal consistency (1.78) and perceptual integrity (1.54).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CanonCGT wins on every metric against all five baselines, with the largest margins over the photorealistic style-transfer methods. Against the closest competitor, Deep Preset [14] (filter-based), CanonCGT improves PSNR from 18.62 to 28.99 (+10.4 dB), reduces ΔE_ab from 15.21 to 5.46 (−64%), and lowers LPIPS from 0.1750 to 0.0665 (−62%), indicating far superior tonal fidelity and perceptual quality. Against the photorealistic methods (PhotoNAS, PhotoWCT², Neural Preset, CAP-VST), the gains are even more pronounced: PSNR improves by 9.4–12.6 dB and ΔE_ab drops by 60–73%, reflecting that these methods suffer from excessive tonal shifts and texture distortions. On style-alignment metrics, CanonCGT's H-Corr of 0.5204 nearly doubles the best baseline (CAP-VST, 0.3249), and its H-Chi of 0.1785 is 38% lower than the next best (PhotoWCT², 0.2869), confirming much tighter tonal distribution matching. In the user study, CanonCGT ranks best in both tonal consistency (1.78 vs. 1.89 for CAP-VST and 2.33 for Deep Preset) and perceptual integrity (1.54 vs. 1.97 for Deep Preset and 2.49 for CAP-VST), validating the quantitative findings with human perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

CanonCGT carves new ground by introducing the canonical pivot — a style-neutral intermediate representation — which no prior color grading or style transfer method explicitly constructs. It repurposes LUT-based color mapping (from LUTFormer, NLUT, and related work) as the output mechanism, borrows FiLM conditioning for grade-aware feature modulation, and uses contrastive learning (supervised contrastive loss) to organize the grade embedding space. Conceptually, it departs from photorealistic style transfer (WCT, PhotoNAS, Neural Preset, CAP-VST), which operates in feature space and causes tonal exaggeration, and from filter-based methods (Deep Preset), which are limited to natural-to-filtered pairs and fail on already-styled inputs, by decoupling style removal from style application into two explicit stages.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The canonical pivot addresses a practical pain point in photo editing: applying a reference style to an already-filtered image without accumulating or retaining the original tone. The self-supervised phase (DP-CGT) is notable for enabling generalization beyond the 56 Lightroom presets used in supervised training, suggesting the framework can adapt to continuous, unseen tonal distributions. However, the paper acknowledges a limitation with extreme references (strong global tints, black-and-white), where CanonCGT selectively transfers hue to preserve photorealism rather than fully committing to the reference style. An open question is whether the canonicalization-grading paradigm extends naturally to video, multi-reference blending, or more complex style dimensions beyond tone and color.
