## Problem

Medical VQA models suffer from train-test distribution shifts that limit generalization, and a natural remedy—pseudo-supervised training on unlabeled test samples—introduces confirmation bias and error amplification when pseudo answers are unreliable. Existing confidence estimation methods (fixed thresholds, loss filtering, prediction discrepancy) are inadequate for generative multimodal settings where the output space is open-ended and a single signal cannot capture both supervision consistency and semantic coherence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262195/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a medical RGB image and a free-form text question as input, formatted as a prompt-like concatenation (question embeddings + image features + answer tokens) fed to a decoder-only LLM. The visual backbone is CLIP-ViT/B16, and the language decoder can be GPT-2 (1.5B), StableLM (1.6B), Mistral (7B), or Llama2 (7B). Output is autoregressive text generation of a clinically relevant answer; during pseudo-supervised training, the model also generates pseudo answers for unlabeled test pairs, which are iteratively re-generated each epoch and re-weighted by confidence.

## Method

DuCoR estimates per-sample pseudo-label reliability from two complementary perspectives and fuses them to guide pseudo-supervised optimization. At the loss level, the autoregressive cross-entropy loss of each pseudo answer is log-transformed, standardized, and modeled with a two-component Gaussian mixture (clean vs. noisy) fitted via EM, where training samples are anchored to the clean component; the posterior clean probability yields the loss-level confidence. At the feature level, a sample's multimodal embedding (independent of answer supervision) is compared to its pseudo-answer class prototype via Mahalanobis distance, converted to confidence through an exponential mapping; prototypes are computed from clean training embeddings or, for unseen classes, from pseudo-labeled test embeddings. The two confidences are fused as a geometric mean with a balance parameter α, and a prototype-based InfoNCE contrastive loss is added to structure the embedding space. The overall objective reweights each pseudo loss by its fused confidence while keeping clean training samples at weight 1, enabling iterative self-refinement that progressively aligns the supervision distribution with the target domain.

## Results

On the three medical VQA benchmarks, DuCoR(Llama2) achieves the best generative performance on SLAKE (88.87% open, 87.52% closed) and PathVQA (42.95% open, 89.84% closed), while DuCoR(Mistral) leads on PathVQA open-ended (43.63%) and DuCoR(StableLM) on PathVQA closed-ended (91.87%). Compared to the strongest prior generative method LLaVA-Med, DuCoR(Llama2) gains +4.12% on VQA-RAD open-ended, +1.76% on SLAKE open-ended, and +3.35% on PathVQA open-ended. In cross-domain transfer (e.g., SLAKE→PathVQA), DuCoR improves F1 from 18.50% (zero-shot) to 26.25%, a +7.75% gain over zero-shot and +3.87% over AdaContrast.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest generative competitor LLaVA-Med (NeurIPS 2023), DuCoR(Llama2) wins on VQA-RAD open-ended (68.87 vs. 64.75), SLAKE open-ended (88.87 vs. 87.11), and PathVQA open-ended (42.95 vs. 39.60), but trails on PathVQA closed-ended (89.84 vs. 91.09). FAVP (AAAI 2025) remains stronger on VQA-RAD closed-ended (89.00 vs. 87.13), while DuCoR(Llama2) surpasses it on VQA-RAD open-ended (68.87 vs. 68.10) and SLAKE open-ended (88.87 vs. 85.60). In the pseudo-labeling comparison (Table 2), DuCoR outperforms AdaContrast (CVPR 2022) by +2.86% overall on VQA-RAD and +2.42% on PathVQA, and beats DivideMix, FixMatch, and CoDis by 3–12% overall. In cross-domain transfer (Tables 4–5), DuCoR consistently exceeds both zero-shot transfer and AdaContrast, with the largest F1 gains on open-ended questions (+7.75% on SLAKE→PathVQA, +13.35% on PathVQA→SLAKE over zero-shot). Among discriminative baselines, MUMC retains the top VQA-RAD open-ended score (71.50 vs. 68.87), but no single discriminative method matches DuCoR's combined performance across all three datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-loss confidence paradigm of PLCM (Huang et al., ICCV 2021) from discriminative few-shot classification to generative multimodal medical VQA, where autoregressive losses over free-form text replace discrete classification losses. It builds on the LLaVA-Med (Li et al., NeurIPS 2023) CLIP+LLM architecture and PMC-15M pretraining pipeline as its backbone. The key departure is the introduction of a second, feature-level confidence signal based on Mahalanobis distance to answer-class prototypes, which has no direct analogue in prior pseudo-labeling work for discriminative tasks. The paper explicitly positions itself at the intersection of two previously parallel research threads—pseudo-label reliability modeling in semi-supervised vision and confidence calibration in large language/vision-language models—arguing that neither alone suffices for open-ended generative settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dual-level confidence framework offers a principled alternative to ad-hoc thresholding or single-cue filtering for pseudo-label selection in generative VQA, with the cross-domain transfer results (e.g., F1 improving from 18.50 to 26.25 on SLAKE→PathVQA) suggesting practical value for deploying medical VQA systems across imaging modalities. However, the method requires transductive access to the full test set and assumes a two-component GMM structure for the loss distribution, which may not hold under more severe distribution shifts or with larger, more diverse answer spaces. Open questions include whether the GMM assumption generalizes to longer-form or multi-turn medical reasoning, whether the approach scales to much larger LLM backbones where per-sample loss statistics become less discriminative, and whether the prototype-based feature confidence remains informative when the answer vocabulary is truly open-ended rather than a fixed set of categories.
