## Problem

Existing debiasing methods either require access to an unbiased training set (impractical in real deployments) or demand full model retraining (computationally prohibitive for large-scale systems). This paper asks whether bias-free subnetworks already exist within conventionally trained models and can be extracted purely through structural pruning, without retraining the original parameters or relying on additional balanced data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260458/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen, vanilla-trained neural network (CNN, ResNet-18, or BERT) and its original biased training set, where each sample carries a target label y and a bias label b. It outputs a binary structured pruning mask over the encoder's neurons/filters, which when applied yields a smaller subnetwork (20–92% sparsity depending on dataset) that can be deployed as-is or optionally finetuned. The original dense model's weights are never modified; only the mask parameters and a small auxiliary head are learned.

## Method

BISE learns structured pruning masks end-to-end via a composite objective: a reweighted cross-entropy loss that up-weights bias-conflicting samples (inversely proportional to group size) to counteract the dominance of bias-aligned data, plus a mutual information regularization term that minimizes bias information extractable from the encoder bottleneck. The mutual information is estimated by training an auxiliary classifier head to predict bias labels from the bottleneck representation, providing an upper bound on the bias information available to the task classifier. Masks are parameterized as continuous variables passed through a temperature-scaled sigmoid with a straight-through estimator for the hard threshold, and the temperature is annealed geometrically (factor κ=0.5 every v epochs) to progressively commit the mask toward binary decisions. Throughout training, the original network parameters remain frozen—only the mask variables and the auxiliary head are updated—making the procedure a lightweight post-hoc intervention rather than a retraining pipeline.

## Results

On BiasedMNIST, BISE extracts subnetworks achieving 96.1%, 92.2%, and 90.8% unbiased test accuracy at ρ=0.99, 0.995, and 0.997 respectively, substantially outperforming the vanilla model (88.9%, 75.1%, 66.1%) while pruning 21–35% of parameters. On CelebA, BISE reaches 89.7% accuracy and 80.4% worst-group accuracy (WGA), surpassing the vanilla model (76.5% / 59.6%) and matching or exceeding most debiasing baselines. With optional finetuning, BISE further improves to 98.1% on BiasedMNIST (ρ=0.99) and 91.8% on CelebA, reaching or exceeding state-of-the-art while maintaining 20–68% sparsity and reduced FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On BiasedMNIST, BISE outperforms LfF [67] by 1.0–27.1 points across ρ values (e.g., 90.8% vs 63.7% at ρ=0.997) and beats SoftCon [30] at ρ=0.997 (90.8% vs 88.6%), but trails BCon+BBal [30] at ρ=0.995 and 0.997 (92.2%/90.8% vs 97.7%/97.3%), which requires retraining with bias-aware objectives. On Corrupted-CIFAR10, BISE(last)+finetuning achieves the best accuracy at all ρ values (e.g., 57.22% at ρ=0.95 vs 51.13% for DFA [49] and 50.27% for LfF [67]). On CelebA, BISE matches Group DRO [77] on WGA (80.4%) and beats LfF [67] (84.2% acc.) and LWBC [40] (85.5% acc.), while BISE+finetuning (91.8%) surpasses CNC [102] (89.9%) and BCon+BBal [30] (91.4%). On Multi-Color MNIST, BISE(last)+finetuning reaches 70.6% unbiased accuracy, below VCBA [65] (73.1%) and DebiAN [51] (72.0%) which exploit unbiased training data, but above EIIL [18] (69.7%) and the vanilla model (58.2%). On CivilComments, BISE achieves 80.4% WGA, outperforming DeDiER [90] (78.3%), RWG [34] (72.0%), and JTT [55] (67.8%), establishing state-of-the-art among methods not requiring unbiased data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Nahon et al. (ECCV 2024), who demonstrated that unbiased subnetworks can exist within biased vanilla-trained models, but required an unbiased training dataset to locate them. BISE departs from this by showing such subnetworks can be found using only the original biased training set, via a learnable structured pruning mask trained with a composite objective combining reweighted cross-entropy and a mutual-information regularization term (inspired by Tartaglione et al.'s bottleneck information-removal framework). Conceptually, the work bridges post-processing debiasing (pruning) with in-processing techniques (loss reweighting, adversarial-like auxiliary training), and notably inverts the prior finding that pruning tends to worsen bias by showing that *targeted* structured pruning can selectively remove bias-dependent pathways while preserving task-relevant features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical appeal is significant: no retraining of the base model, no access to unbiased data, and the output is a smaller, faster model — a triple win for deployment-constrained settings. However, the method's success is fundamentally conditioned on unbiased subnetworks existing within the vanilla model, a precondition that may not hold in complex real-world scenarios with many interacting biases. The authors acknowledge that for multi-bias settings (e.g., Multi-Color MNIST), the extracted subnetwork struggles without subsequent finetuning, and scenarios with more bias sources (e.g., BiasedMNISTv2) remain unexplored. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to settings where bias is not explicitly labeled and where the number of bias dimensions grows, and whether the straight-through masking mechanism can be replaced with differentiable alternatives that avoid the temperature-annealing schedule.
