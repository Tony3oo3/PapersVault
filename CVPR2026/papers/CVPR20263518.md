## Problem

Existing long-form video understanding methods either rely on hand-crafted reasoning pipelines that limit MLLM autonomy (e.g., VideoTree's fixed tree construction) or perform exhaustive, token-consuming preprocessing of every clip before reasoning (e.g., DVD's 10-second clip database). Neither approach allows the model to adaptively focus on query-relevant temporal segments on the fly, leading to substantial redundancy and high token costs for videos spanning minutes to hours.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263518/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a long-form video (with its audio track) and a natural-language question as input, and produces a single text answer. Video frames are consumed adaptively: the Interval Localizer samples N₁ frames (30–150, controller-decided) resized to 256px short edge and tiled into 3×2 grids for global context, while the Clip Explorer stores higher-resolution frames (512px short edge) and audio segments (≤25 MB) for local analysis. The agent operates within a fixed step budget of N=10 iterations, after which it must commit to an answer.

## Method

VideoARM introduces an agentic reasoning-over-hierarchical-memory paradigm in which a controller (OpenAI o3) executes an observe-think-act-memorize loop, adaptively invoking tools from two complementary sets: Temporal Scoping Tools (Interval Localizer for global frame-interval selection, Clip Explorer for local fine-grained probing) and Multimodal Understanding Tools (Scene Snapper for scene captioning, Audio Transcriber via whisper-1, and Clip Analyzer for sub-question answering with confidence scores). A three-tier Hierarchical Multimodal Memory (HM³) — Sensory Memory (long-term and short-term perception pools), Result Memory (temporally ordered tool outputs), and Working Memory (controller reasoning traces) — is dynamically constructed and updated throughout execution, allowing the agent to accumulate evidence, avoid redundant actions, and progressively narrow from coarse temporal spans to micro-event-level verification. The entire pipeline is training-free and query-guided: no static database is pre-built, and the controller decides at each step which tool to call and what parameters to use, reducing token consumption to roughly 1/34 of DVD's while achieving higher accuracy across Video-MME, LongVideoBench, EgoSchema, MLVU, and LVBench.

## Results

On Video-MME (w/o subtitles), VideoARM (o3+GPT-4o) achieves 82.8% overall accuracy (85.8/81.3/81.2 on short/medium/long), surpassing the previous SOTA DVD (71.6% overall) and GPT-4o (71.9%). On LongVideoBench, VideoARM reaches 78.0% overall (76.4% on the long subset), and on EgoSchema it scores 78.2% (o3+GPT-4.1 configuration). Additionally, VideoARM sets new SOTA on MLVU (81.2%) and LVBench (79.7%), while consuming only 1/34 of the tokens required by DVD on Video-MME.

## Baselines & Comparisons

With the same backbone (o3+GPT-4.1), VideoARM outperforms DVD on every reported metric: Video-MME Long 75.3 vs 67.3 (+8.0), LongVideoBench Overall 73.7 vs 71.6 (+2.1), and EgoSchema 78.2 vs 76.6 (+1.6), while consuming 1/34 of DVD's tokens. Against its own base models, VideoARM (o3+GPT-4o) improves over GPT-4o alone by +10.9 on Video-MME Overall (82.8 vs 71.9) and +11.3 on LongVideoBench Overall (78.0 vs 66.7); the o3+GPT-4.1 configuration beats OpenAI o3 alone by +12.1 on Video-MME Long (75.3 vs 63.2) and +15.0 on EgoSchema (78.2 vs 63.2). VideoARM also surpasses VideoLucy on Video-MME Overall (80.1 vs 72.5), MLVU (81.2 vs 76.1), and LVBench (79.7 vs 58.8), and outperforms Gemini-1.5-Pro on all three primary benchmarks (Video-MME 80.1 vs 75.0, LongVideoBench 73.7 vs 64.0, EgoSchema 78.2 vs 71.1). The only configuration where VideoARM trails a baseline is EgoSchema with the o3+GPT-4o toolset (76.2 vs DVD's 76.6), a 0.4-point gap, while the o3+GPT-4.1 configuration leads by 1.6 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

VideoARM carves new ground at the intersection of two existing lines of work: hand-crafted video reasoning pipelines (VideoTree, LLoVi) and agentic search over pre-built clip databases (DVD). It departs from both by eliminating static, exhaustive preprocessing in favor of an adaptive, on-the-fly observe-think-act-memorize loop, and by introducing a three-tier hierarchical multimodal memory (HM³) that is dynamically constructed and evolves during execution rather than being fixed beforehand. Conceptually, it extends the ReAct-style agent paradigm by grounding the controller's decisions in a persistent, multi-level memory substrate that transitions from raw perception to semantic reasoning traces, rather than relying on retrieval from a frozen database.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1/50 theoretical (and 1/34 experimental) token reduction relative to DVD while matching or exceeding accuracy is a practically significant efficiency gain for long-form video understanding, making agentic video reasoning more accessible under API cost constraints. The training-free, tool-augmented design lowers the barrier to deployment since no model fine-tuning is required. However, the approach's heavy dependence on frontier API models (OpenAI o3 as controller, GPT-4o for understanding tools) limits its applicability to settings where such models are available, and the fixed step budget of 10 may be insufficient for extremely long or complex videos. Open questions include whether the hierarchical memory design transfers to smaller or locally-deployed MLLMs, and how the framework scales to multi-query or streaming video scenarios.
