## Problem

Existing zero-shot captioners rely on global CLIP representations and can only produce whole-image captions, while region-level captioning methods require expensive manually labeled region-text pairs that do not scale. The paper asks whether arbitrary image regions—from a single patch to non-contiguous areas to the full image—can be captioned in a unified zero-shot framework without any region-level or image-text supervision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260105/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a region specification (bounding box, mask, free-form mouse trace, or the full image) and produces a single natural-language caption for that region. The image is processed by a frozen vision-language backbone that outputs a dense grid of patch embeddings (e.g., 14×14 for DINOv2 B/14); the region is defined as the set of patches it intersects, and a single forward pass yields all patch features reusable across multiple regions without re-encoding. The output is a free-form caption generated autoregressively, with no fixed length or structural constraint beyond the decoder's EOS token.

## Method

The framework reformulates captioning around patches as atomic units: a frozen DINOv2-based vision-language backbone (Talk2DINO or DINO.txt) produces dense, language-aligned patch embeddings; a parameter-free aggregation (mean over selected patch indices) forms the region representation; and a GPT-2-style autoregressive decoder, trained exclusively on text data to reconstruct captions from their text embeddings, generates the final caption from the region embedding. Three modality-gap mitigation strategies are studied—memory-based projection into the text subspace (DeCap-style), noise-injection during decoder training (CLOSE/CapDec-style), and a diffusion model that iteratively refines visual features toward the textual manifold (Diffusion Bridge-style)—with the memory-based approach performing best. The key empirical finding is that DINOv2-based backbones, which produce semantically rich localized patch features, are essential: standard CLIP patch tokens lack the spatial detail needed, while DINOv2 variants bridged to language (Talk2DINO, DINO.txt) achieve state-of-the-art results across trace, dense, region-set, and image captioning tasks.

## Results

On the newly introduced Trace Captioning task (COCO), the best Patch-ioner variant (T2D + Diffusion) achieves CIDEr 29.4 and RefPAC 77.6, outperforming the strongest whole-image baseline ViECap (CIDEr 24.3) by over 5 points. On Dense Captioning (VG v1.2), T2D + Mem. reaches mAP 21.31 and CIDEr 31.9, surpassing the best crop-based baseline DeCap (mAP 17.75, CIDEr 24.6) and the region-supervised AlphaCLIP (mAP 14.63). On Region-Set Captioning (COCO Entities), T2D + Diffusion attains CIDEr 114.4, exceeding ViECap (102.7) and AlphaCLIP (95.1). On whole-image COCO captioning, the framework is competitive (T2D + Diffusion: CIDEr 92.8) but trails dedicated image-centric methods MERCap (96.0) and EntroCap (94.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against whole-image zero-shot captioners (DeCap, ViECap, MeaCap, IFCap, Diffusion Bridge, MERCap, EntroCap), Patch-ioner wins decisively on all local/fine-grained tasks: on Trace Captioning (COCO) it gains +5.1 CIDEr over ViECap (29.4 vs. 24.3) and +8.9 over DeCap; on Dense Captioning (VG v1.2) it gains +3.6 mAP over DeCap-crop (21.31 vs. 17.75) and +6.4 CIDEr over MeaCap-crop (31.9 vs. 28.6). Against region-supervised baselines (AlphaCLIP, RegionCLIP), Patch-ioner also wins on all regional tasks without any region-level training data, e.g., +8.1 CIDEr over AlphaCLIP on Trace (29.4 vs. 21.3) and +19.3 CIDEr on Region-Set COCO Entities (114.4 vs. 95.1). On whole-image COCO captioning, the gap narrows: T2D + Diffusion (CIDEr 92.8) trails MERCap (96.0) and EntroCap (94.3) by 3–4 points, while T2D + Mem. (88.5) is roughly on par with DeCap (87.4) but slightly behind on CLIP-Score (76.0 vs. 79.3). The paper notes that adding external knowledge modules (ViECap-style entity prompts, MeaCap-style filtered retrieval) to the noise-based decoder improves image-captioning fluency but does not close the gap to the strongest dedicated image-centric methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the zero-shot captioning pipeline established by DeCap, CLOSE/CapDec, and Diffusion Bridge—where a text-only-trained decoder reconstructs captions from CLIP's shared vision-language space—but shifts the visual input from a single global CLS token to a dense grid of patch embeddings. The critical architectural departure is replacing CLIP's visual encoder with DINOv2-based backbones (Talk2DINO, DINO.txt) that produce semantically meaningful patch-level features, since standard CLIP patch tokens are known to lack fine-grained spatial detail. The paper also introduces trace captioning as a new evaluation task built on the Localized Narratives dataset, extending the region-captioning literature beyond bounding boxes to free-form mouse traces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, supervision-free route to interactive and fine-grained captioning: a single backbone forward pass yields patch features reusable for arbitrary regions, enabling applications like real-time region description without expensive region-text annotation. However, the paper acknowledges that performance still lags behind fully supervised task-specific models, the fixed patch receptive field limits contextual adaptability, and the modality gap can induce hallucinations. Open questions include whether weakly-supervised image-level captioning losses or refined patch-to-text projections can close the remaining gap, and whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D content.
