## Problem

Methods that bridge pre-trained VLMs and diffusion models via a fixed set of learnable query tokens suffer from task generalization collapse: after pre-training on text-to-image and reconstruction, the tokens converge to a rigid, task-specific mean representation, causing the model to ignore textual instructions on new tasks (e.g., image editing) and merely reconstruct the input. Re-initializing or fine-tuning these tokens for new tasks leads to rapid training collapse, making sustainable continual learning infeasible without full retraining from scratch.

## Input / Output

The model accepts text prompts for generation, a single RGB image plus an editing instruction, or multiple RGB images plus an instruction for multi-image editing, and outputs a generated or edited image at dynamic resolutions (base 512×512 through 1024×1024). The number of Noisy Query Tokens dynamically matches the number of image patches from the VLM's vision encoder, and a VAE branch injects frozen encoder features (resized to match ViT patch count) via a single linear layer to preserve high-frequency details that the LLM's semantic consolidation would otherwise discard.

## Method

WeMMU replaces fixed learnable query tokens with Noisy Query Tokens sampled fresh from N(0, I) at every training step, forcing the frozen Qwen2.5-VL-3B to learn a distributed intermediate representation space rather than memorizing a task-specific shortcut, which enables stable continual learning across diverse tasks. A parallel trainable generative pathway (initialized from the VLM's weights) uses bidirectional attention over these noisy tokens to aggregate image and text features, while the original VLM pathway remains frozen to preserve understanding. A VAE branch projects frozen VAE encoder features into the LLM via a single linear layer, offloading fine-grained reconstruction from the semantic pathway. A Position MLP superimposes learnable 2D absolute positional embeddings (dynamically cropped for resolution flexibility) and projects features to the Sana 1.6B diffusion model's conditioner dimension. Training follows a four-stage progressive curriculum using Contrastive Flow Matching (early) and Conditional Flow Matching (later), incrementally increasing resolution, data quality, and task complexity from 80M samples down to 4.3M.

## Results

On GenEval, WeMMU (Stage 3/4) achieves an Overall score of 0.88, matching Bagel (0.88) and Query-Kontext (0.88) and surpassing QWen-Image (0.87), SD3-Medium (0.74), and FLUX.1-dev (0.67). On DPG-Bench, WeMMU scores 83.69/83.60 Overall, competitive with SD3-Medium (84.08) and FLUX.1-dev (84.0) but trailing QWen-Image (88.32) and EMU3.5 (88.26). On ImageEdit-Bench, WeMMU achieves 3.31 Overall, outperforming Bagel (3.2) and UniWorld-V1 (3.26) but below OmniGen2 (3.44) and QWen-Image (4.27). On GEdit-Bench-EN, WeMMU scores 5.75 G_O, surpassing UniWorld-V1 (4.85) but trailing Query-Kontext (7.66) and EMU3.5 (7.59).

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeMMU (8B) achieves a GenEval Overall of 0.88, tying with Bagel (14B) and Query-Kontext (17B) while outperforming QWen-Image (27B, 0.87), OmniGen2 (7B, 0.86), UniWorld-V1 (20B, 0.84), MetaQuery-XL (9B, 0.80), and Bifrost-1 (19B, 0.81), all at a fraction of the parameter count. On DPG-Bench Overall, WeMMU (83.69) trails QWen-Image (88.32), EMU3.5 (88.26), SD3-Medium (84.08), and Bagel (85.07) but edges out OmniGen2 (83.57), UniWorld-V1 (81.38), MetaQuery-XL (82.05), and Bifrost-1 (77.67). For image editing on ImageEdit-Bench, WeMMU (3.31) surpasses Bagel (3.2) and UniWorld-V1 (3.26) but falls short of OmniGen2 (3.44), QWen-Image (4.27), GPT-4o (4.2), and EMU3.5 (4.41). On GEdit-Bench-EN, WeMMU (G_O 5.75) clearly beats UniWorld-V1 (4.85) but lags behind Query-Kontext (7.66), EMU3.5 (7.59), GPT-4o (7.53), and QWen-Image (7.56). The paper's central claim is not SOTA on every benchmark but rather that Noisy Query Tokens enable stable continual learning: after Stage 4 multi-image editing training, WeMMU retains its Stage 3 single-image editing and text-to-image performance without catastrophic forgetting, whereas learnable-query baselines produce incoherent outputs on the new task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the 'bridging' paradigm for unified MLLMs, directly extending the MetaQueries line of work that uses learnable query tokens to connect frozen VLMs with diffusion models. It departs from this lineage by identifying a fundamental failure mode—task generalization collapse—where fixed learnable queries overfit to a task-specific mean representation and cannot adapt to novel tasks without full retraining. The Noisy Query Token mechanism replaces deterministic queries with per-step Gaussian samples, while the VAE branch with a single linear projection addresses a complementary fidelity gap that prior bridging methods (Bifrost-1, Query-Kontext) leave unaddressed. The four-stage progressive curriculum and use of Contrastive Flow Matching for early-stage training are practical training-strategy contributions layered on top of the architectural change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to stably acquire new tasks (e.g., multi-image editing) without catastrophic forgetting is a practically important property for deploying unified MLLMs in production, where task distributions shift over time. The 8B parameter budget and ~130M total training samples make the approach accessible compared to monolithic 14–34B unified models, though editing quality still trails GPT-4o and Gemini by a noticeable margin. Open questions include whether the noise distribution can be made adaptive (e.g., task-conditioned variance), whether the approach scales to video or 3D generation, and whether RL-based fine-tuning can close the remaining editing gap. The finding that unfreezing the native ViT causes catastrophic collapse while a frozen VAE + linear layer works stably is a useful practical warning for practitioners modifying VLM components.
