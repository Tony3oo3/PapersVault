## Problem

Frame-level missingness in multimodal sentiment analysis fragments emotional cues and creates cross-modal inconsistency, yet existing methods rely on passive single-pass feedforward completion with static cross-modal fusion, lacking self-adaptive correction to eliminate redundancy and noise introduced during completion. The brain, by contrast, performs active perceptual inference through recurrent corticothalamic circuits, a mechanism that has not been applied to the data completion task.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263628/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes video clips with three modalities (vision, audio, language) where random frame-level segments are missing—zero vectors for vision/audio and [UNK] tokens for language—with missing rates ranging from 0 to 0.9 at test time. It produces a continuous sentiment score (regression target: −3 to 3 for MOSI/MOSEI, −1 to 1 for SIMS) via a linear classifier over globally pooled fused features, alongside completed per-modality feature sequences that can be reconstructed toward the original complete representations.

## Method

DNRNet is a dynamic nested recurrent network that iteratively refines incomplete features through two coupled loops per modality. The Local Recurrent Loop (simulating intra-cortical pattern completion) uses a 2-layer Transformer as a High-Level Inference Module to generate top-down inference signals, which a 2-layer MLP Local Perception Module converts into local correction features. The Global Recurrent Loop (simulating the corticothalamic circuit) employs a Confidence-Aware Module to estimate per-modality reliability scores, then a Global Perception Module performs cross-modal attention—using the local correction as Query and confidence-weighted other-modality features as Key/Value—scaled by (1 − w_m) to produce global correction features. Each iteration fuses both corrections into the current features via LayerNorm with a trainable scaling factor, and a convergence check on relative L2 norm change (threshold 0.0001) terminates the loop early. Training is supervised with a weighted sum of sentiment prediction MSE, a confidence-aware MSE against soft ground-truth labels derived from missing ratios, and a per-modality reconstruction MSE against complete-feature encodings.

## Results

On MOSI (averaged across all missing rates), DNRNet achieves the best Acc-7 (35.36%), Acc-5 (39.11%), Acc-2 (74.22%), F1 (74.24%), and Corr (0.538), with a near-best MAE of 1.036. On MOSEI, DNRNet attains the highest Acc-5 (47.84%) and best MAE (0.655), while on the Chinese SIMS dataset it leads in Acc-5 (35.71%), Acc-3 (59.54%), MAE (0.498), and Corr (0.416). Overall, DNRNet delivers an average accuracy improvement of 1.5%–2.0% over the strongest baseline (P-RMF) across all missing rates from 0 to 0.9.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DNRNet outperforms all eight baselines on MOSI across every reported metric, with the largest margins over P-RMF (the strongest competitor): +1.17% on Acc-7, +0.61% on Acc-5, +1.41% on Acc-2, and +1.31% on F1. On MOSEI, DNRNet achieves the best Acc-5 (47.84% vs. CENET's 47.83%) and lowest MAE (0.655 vs. P-RMF's 0.658), though CENET edges it slightly on Acc-7 (47.18% vs. 46.89%) and P-RMF marginally leads on F1 (79.33/80.39 vs. 78.74/80.18). On SIMS, DNRNet dominates in Acc-5 (35.71% vs. P-RMF's 34.83%), Acc-3 (59.54% vs. 54.75%), MAE (0.498 vs. 0.500), and Corr (0.416 vs. 0.414), but LNLN retains a higher F1 (79.43 vs. 77.50). Weaker baselines such as MISA, Self-MM, MMIM, TETFN, and TFR-Net trail DNRNet by 5–10+ percentage points on accuracy metrics, confirming that the dynamic nested recurrent mechanism provides a substantial advantage over single-pass feedforward completion approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in frame-level missingness for MSA (P-RMF, LNLN, EMTDLFR), which relies on single-pass feedforward cross-modal fusion for passive feature completion, by introducing an iterative recurrent inference loop inspired by corticothalamic circuits and intra-cortical pattern completion. It also extends the brain-inspired MSA line (BIG-FUSION, MI-MAMI, BrainyHGN), which has focused on parallel processing or attention modulation, by specifically modeling the brain's active, multi-pass perceptual inference as a nested local-global recurrent architecture. The core conceptual move is reframing data completion as an active inference problem with self-adaptive correction rather than a one-shot reconstruction task.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The nested recurrent design with confidence-aware cross-modal gating offers a principled mechanism for iteratively refining incomplete features, and the convergence-driven early stopping provides practical efficiency. However, the 1.5–2.0% average improvement over strong baselines like P-RMF is modest, and the neuroscience framing, while motivating, maps fairly directly onto standard iterative attention with residual updates. The authors themselves flag the co-occurrence of missing data and class imbalance as an open challenge, and it remains unclear whether the recurrent mechanism generalizes beyond the three-modal sentiment setting or scales to longer sequences and additional modalities.
