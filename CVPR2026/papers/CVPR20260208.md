## Problem

Single-reference novel object pose estimation requires establishing reliable cross-view correspondences under large viewpoint changes and partial observations, but existing methods rely on discrete one-to-one matching (e.g., argmax) that is non-differentiable, collapses onto a few dominant keypoints, and prevents unsupervised training. This paper addresses the gap by formulating correspondence estimation as a confidence-aware optimal transport problem where per-point confidences serve as transport marginals, enabling end-to-end unsupervised learning of both correspondences and pose.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query and a reference RGB-D image of the same novel object, segments the object via UnoSeg, back-projects masked depth into point clouds (1024 points in the fine phase, 256 via FPS in the coarse phase), and extracts per-pixel DINOv2 patch features as RGB descriptors. It outputs a 6DoF relative rigid transformation (rotation in SO(3) and translation in R³), per-point confidence scores in [0,1] that indicate overlap validity, and row-stochastic soft correspondence matrices that map each point to a convex combination of points in the other view. A coarse-to-fine pipeline with optional iterative refinement progressively refines the pose estimate.

## Method

COG uses a coarse-to-fine geometric transformer backbone to predict per-point geometric features and confidence scores, with DINO features processed through a STEGO-style semantic denoising head. The key insight is formulating correspondence as an optimal transport problem where predicted confidences (normalized by their means) serve as target marginals rather than uniform distributions, and the affinity kernel combines cosine similarity of geometric features with a temperature-scaled semantic similarity term. The Sinkhorn algorithm solves for the transport plan, which is row-normalized into directional correspondence matrices used as soft projection operators; pose is then recovered via a confidence-weighted Umeyama (SVD) solver on concatenated correspondences. For unsupervised confidence learning, pseudo-labels are generated by multiplying Gaussian RBF kernels of cycle consistency, pose alignment, and semantic consistency, providing graded supervision that downweights uncertain points without discarding them. Training uses a composite loss combining cycle consistency, weighted Chamfer pose loss, semantic consistency, and binary cross-entropy on pseudo confidence labels.

## Results

On the three BOP benchmarks (LMO, TUD-L, YCB-V), unsupervised COG achieves a mean mAP of 68.8, outperforming all unsupervised baselines (Dustbin OT: 61.1, Robust OT: 59.3, FreeZe: 59.8) and reaching within 2.1% of the supervised SOTA UnoPose (70.9). Supervised COG achieves a mean mAP of 73.8, setting a new state of the art across LMO (60.8) and TUD-L (80.0), though UnoPose retains an edge on YCB-V (83.1 vs. 80.5). Inference time is 4.0 s, comparable to other OT-based methods and far faster than FreeZe (53.0 s) or FPFH+MAC (136.9 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Unsupervised COG (mean 68.8) decisively outperforms all other unsupervised methods: Dustbin OT (61.1), Robust OT (59.3), FreeZe (59.8), and classical pipelines like PPF+ICP (46.9) and FPFH+RANSAC (37.3). Against the strongest supervised baseline UnoPose (70.9), unsupervised COG trails by only 2.1% on average but actually exceeds it on TUD-L (73.8 vs. 71.0, +2.8%), while UnoPose retains a larger lead on YCB-V (83.1 vs. 75.9) and LMO (58.7 vs. 56.7). Supervised COG (73.8) surpasses UnoPose on LMO (+2.1) and TUD-L (+9.0) but falls slightly short on YCB-V (80.5 vs. 83.1). Among OT-based methods, COG's confidence-marginal formulation consistently beats uniform-marginal variants (Robust OT, Dustbin OT) and RPM-Net (30.2), confirming that learned confidence as transport marginals is more effective than post-hoc calibration. Inference speed is competitive at 4.0 s, matching Robust OT and Dustbin OT while being 13× faster than FreeZe (53.0 s) and 34× faster than FPFH+MAC (136.9 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

COG builds on the geometric transformer backbone (Qin et al., CVPR 2022) for point cloud encoding and the STEGO self-label refinement strategy (Hamilton et al., ICLR 2022) for semantic denoising of DINOv2 features. Its key conceptual departure is from prior OT-based registration methods (RPM-Net, Robust OT, Dustbin OT), which use uniform marginals and apply confidence only post-hoc, and from single-reference pose estimators (UnoPose, SAM-6D) that rely on discrete argmax matching. By injecting learned point-wise confidence directly as OT target marginals, COG unifies correspondence finding and confidence estimation into a single differentiable, end-to-end pipeline that supports unsupervised training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The confidence-as-marginals formulation offers a principled way to suppress non-overlapping regions and outliers without hard thresholding, and the pseudo-label strategy for confidence learning removes the need for ground-truth overlap annotations. The practical impact is that unsupervised COG closes most of the gap to supervised SOTA (2.1% mean mAP behind UnoPose) while the supervised variant sets a new state of the art. Open questions remain: the performance gap widens on cluttered, heavily occluded benchmarks (LMO, YCB-V), suggesting the pseudo-label confidence may be less reliable in complex scenes, and it is unclear whether the approach scales to articulated or deformable objects.
