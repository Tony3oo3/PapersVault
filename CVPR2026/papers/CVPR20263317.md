## Problem

Existing personalized image description models condition generation solely on linguistic style (vocabulary, tone) and ignore individual viewing behavior. Cognitive research shows that where, in what order, and for how long a person looks at an image strongly shapes what they describe, yet no prior work has incorporated personalized attention patterns into description generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263317/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At training time, DEPER consumes triplets of (RGB image, text description, attention trajectory) where the trajectory is a sequence of bounding boxes with fixation durations. At inference, only the RGB image and a precomputed subject embedding are required—no gaze or mouse-tracking data is needed at test time. The output is a free-form natural-language description generated by a frozen Qwen2-VL-2B model, prompted with 'Write a description of this photo in the style of <subj_x>.' where the subject token is replaced by the adapted embedding vector.

## Method

DEPER learns a content-invariant subject embedding z_s through three coupled modules: (1) a dual-context encoder where text-token and trajectory-token streams iteratively exchange information via cross-attention with image patch features, using sinusoidal positional encoding to encode fixation duration and scan order; (2) a subject embedding extractor that distills the fused representation via a learnable query under joint classification and supervised contrastive loss to enforce inter-subject discriminability; and (3) a trajectory decoder that reconstructs the attention sequence conditioned on z_s through FiLM modulation, reinforcing the embedding's capacity to capture personalized viewing dynamics. A single linear adapter then projects z_s into the frozen VLM's token space, replacing a dedicated subject token in the prompt. Training proceeds in two stages: Stage 1 optimizes the encoder, extractor, and trajectory decoder jointly; Stage 2 freezes the encoder and aligns the subject embedding to the VLM via supervised fine-tuning on held-out image-description pairs from the same subject, preventing information leakage.

## Results

On seen subjects across four datasets, Qwen+DEPER achieves the best performance on all metrics, with average improvements of 62% on BLEU-4 and 28% on CIDEr over the strongest baseline (Qwen+PT). On the personalization-specific metrics, DEPER gains 13.0% on Object Sequence Score (OSS) and 15.4% on top-1 subject classification accuracy (CLS) over Qwen+PT. On the unseen (few-shot) split, DEPER maintains strong performance, e.g., BLEU-4 of 0.202 on Flickr30k-LN and CIDEr of 1.053 on Kollenda et al., outperforming all baselines without per-subject fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEPER outperforms all baselines on every metric across all four datasets. Against the strongest personalization baseline Qwen+PT (prompt tuning with subject tokens), DEPER improves BLEU-4 by 82% on COCO-LN (0.264 vs. 0.145), 131% on Flickr30k-LN (0.312 vs. 0.135), and 101% on Kollenda et al. (0.135 vs. 0.067), while also gaining 13–24% on CIDEr and 13–15% on OSS/CLS. Against CSMN, the only prior personalized captioning model with public code, DEPER's advantage is overwhelming: CSMN scores near-zero BLEU-4 on Flickr30k-LN (0.010) and Kollenda (0.001), while DEPER achieves 0.312 and 0.135 respectively. Against MITR-FT (per-subject fine-tuning of a population-level attention model), DEPER wins on all personalization metrics (OSS, CLS) by 59–88%, showing that learned subject embeddings capture viewing patterns far better than per-subject parameter updates. Against Qwen Zero-shot (no personalization), DEPER gains 1040% on BLEU-4 for COCO-LN (0.264 vs. 0.023) and 260% for Flickr30k-LN (0.312 vs. 0.024), confirming that personalization is essential for matching individual description styles.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEPER departs from the personalized image captioning line (CSMN, MHTN, UMCap), which encodes personalization solely through linguistic style (TF-IDF over prior posts), by introducing individual attention trajectories as a first-class personalization signal. It also extends the human-attention-in-captioning literature (MITR, saliency-guided models), which treats attention as a population-level or image-specific cue, by learning a content-invariant subject embedding that captures stable, cross-image viewing preferences. The VLM conditioning mechanism (injecting a subject token into a frozen Qwen2-VL) follows the prompt-tuning paradigm, but the subject embedding is learned from a dual-context encoder that jointly models scanpaths and descriptions—a formulation absent from prior identity-token approaches like DreamBooth or Yo'LLaVA, which focus on recognizing depicted entities rather than viewer behavior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that individual viewing patterns carry personalization signal beyond linguistic style, with clean ablations confirming that removing attention trajectories or the reconstruction objective degrades performance substantially. The few-shot capability (5 support samples, no per-subject fine-tuning) is practically important for accessibility and assistive-vision applications where collecting large per-user datasets is infeasible. Limitations include reliance on attention data during training (mouse traces or eye-tracking), evaluation on relatively small datasets (≤108 subjects), and baselines that are somewhat dated (CSMN from 2017). Open questions include whether the approach transfers to larger VLMs, whether it extends to tasks like VQA or robotics where implicit human behavior matters, and whether the subject embedding generalizes beyond the specific viewing tasks studied.
