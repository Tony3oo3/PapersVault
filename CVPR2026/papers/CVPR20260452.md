## Problem

GRPO and its variants, when applied to fine-tune multimodal LLMs for visual perception, suffer from three underexplored limitations: (1) rule-based rewards produce near-identical scores within a group, yielding vanishing advantage estimates and weak policy gradients; (2) token-level KL regularization constrains the policy's exploration of the broader output space; and (3) pairwise alignment rewards and arithmetic reward aggregation allow dominant signals to overshadow weaker ones, degrading overall performance. DEVA targets all three gaps simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260452/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image paired with a natural-language query and produces task-specific outputs: a class label (with a reasoning trace) for fine-grained classification, a bounding box (with reasoning) for detection and reasoning grounding, all wrapped in a structured think/answer format. For the alignment-volume reward, the image is not used in full; instead, a mask derived from self-attention scores between image and text tokens in the language decoder extracts only the relevant patches before embedding. Fine-tuning is performed in a few-shot regime (1-16 samples per class) on Qwen2-VL-2B/7B.

## Method

DEVA augments the GRPO objective with four components. First, a GFlowNet detailed-balance loss (L_div) is added as an auxiliary training signal that increases reward diversity within each sampled group, strengthening the advantage estimates that drive policy updates. Second, the standard per-token KL divergence is replaced by a global entropic divergence term (L_reg) that penalizes the squared difference between the mean token entropy of the policy and the reference model, enabling broader exploration without per-token constraints. Third, a non-verifiable alignment reward (r_v) is computed as the inverse of the hyper-volume of the parallelotope spanned by unit-norm embeddings of the masked image, the query, and the response, encouraging unified three-way alignment. Fourth, all rewards (format, task, alignment) are combined via a scaled harmonic mean rather than arithmetic summation, forcing simultaneous improvement across all signals. The framework is model-agnostic and can be layered on top of GRPO, DAPO, PAPO, GSPO, and other RL variants.

## Results

On the LISA reasoning grounding benchmark (239 training samples), full DEVA applied to Qwen2-VL-2B achieves 48.9 mIoU_test and 42.3 gIoU_test, surpassing the strongest RL baseline GSPO (41.3 mIoU) by +7.6 points and ViRFT (37.6 mIoU) by +11.3 points. For Qwen2-VL-7B, DEVA reaches 49.5 mIoU_test vs. 46.0 for GSPO (+3.5) and 43.9 for ViRFT (+5.6). On 4-shot COCO detection, DEVA achieves 47.3 mAP (2B) and 60.0 mAP (7B), outperforming GSPO by +4.7 and +4.0 points respectively. Even the diversity-loss-only ablation (43.6 mIoU on LISA-2B) already exceeds GSPO, indicating that reward diversity is the single most impactful component.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEVA (full) decisively outperforms all 13 RL baselines on LISA reasoning grounding. Against the strongest sequence-level optimizer GSPO, DEVA gains +7.6 mIoU on Qwen2-VL-2B (48.9 vs. 41.3) and +3.5 on 7B (49.5 vs. 46.0). Compared to the direct predecessor ViRFT (vanilla GRPO with verifiable rewards), DEVA improves by +11.3 mIoU (2B) and +5.6 (7B), confirming that the diversity, exploration, and alignment-volume components address real limitations of GRPO. Notably, even the diversity-loss-only variant (43.6 mIoU) surpasses GSPO by +2.3 points, suggesting that reward diversity within groups is the dominant bottleneck. On 4-shot COCO detection, DEVA achieves 47.3 mAP (2B) and 60.0 mAP (7B), beating GSPO by +4.7 and +4.0 points respectively, and ViRFT by +6.7 and +5.7. DEVA also outperforms specialized grounding models (OV-Seg, X-Decoder, GroundedSAM) by 15–23 mIoU points, demonstrating that RL-enhanced MLLMs can surpass task-specific architectures on reasoning-based grounding.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEVA extends the GRPO-based fine-tuning paradigm introduced by DeepSeekMath [35] and adapted to multimodal perception by VisualRFT [26], which established rule-based verifiable rewards for visual tasks. The key departure is the integration of GFlowNet balance-condition losses [2] as an auxiliary diversity objective alongside the RL policy gradient — the first such combination, repurposing GFlowNet's trajectory-level flow matching to increase within-group reward variance rather than to generate diverse reasoning paths. The framework also draws on entropic regularization concepts from RL theory and Gram-matrix-based multimodal alignment [7], but repositions them as sequence-level exploration control and a non-verifiable volume reward, respectively, rather than token-level KL penalties or pairwise similarity scores.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEVA provides a modular, plug-in framework that can be layered onto any GRPO variant (demonstrated on ViRFT, DAPO, PAPO, GSPO), making it practically useful for the growing ecosystem of RL-based MLLM fine-tuning. The finding that a simple GFlowNet diversity loss alone outperforms the sequence-level GSPO baseline suggests that reward diversity, not just policy optimization granularity, is a critical bottleneck in visual perception RL. Open questions include whether these gains persist at larger model scales (beyond 7B), whether the alignment volume reward generalizes to tasks without clear geometric structure (e.g., open-ended VQA), and how the framework interacts with longer-horizon visual-agentic tasks the authors mention as future work.
