## Problem

Conventional joint adversarial training (JAT) for deep image watermarking suffers from two critical issues: decoder adversarial training degrades clean accuracy on unattacked images, and simultaneously optimizing against distortion, regeneration, and adversarial attacks within a single monolithic process yields limited robustness. The paper identifies that existing watermarking methods remain vulnerable to advanced threats like diffusion-based regeneration (DDIM) and WEvade adversarial attacks, and that the standard noise-layer JAT paradigm cannot adequately address all three attack categories without sacrificing either clean accuracy or robustness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (128×128 or 256×256) and a binary secret message of variable length (30–100 bits) as input, and produces a single watermarked RGB image of the same resolution. The encoder is a CNN-based architecture fine-tuned from the pretrained MBRS model, and the decoder extracts the message via a standard classification head. Unlike typical watermarking pipelines that rely solely on a noise layer during training, AdvMark additionally performs per-image direct optimization at inference time (Stage 2), meaning the final watermarked image is produced by a short PGD-style optimization loop rather than a single forward pass through the encoder.

## Method

AdvMark decouples defense into two sequential stages. In Stage 1, a defender-tailored adversarial attack (optimizing the decoded message toward random rather than a fixed target label) generates adversarial examples, and the encoder is fine-tuned to move images into a non-attackable region while the decoder is updated only conditionally when bit accuracy drops below a threshold, preserving clean accuracy. In Stage 2, the encoded image is directly optimized (not the model) against distortion and regeneration attacks using a weighted attack loss, while a novel constrained image loss penalizes deviation from the Stage-1 encoded image to preserve adversarial robustness—formalized in a theorem showing that if ‖x_w2 − x_w1‖ ≤ δ < α, the robustness budget shrinks by at most δ. A quality-aware early-stop mapping replaces the standard ε-ball projection, guaranteeing a PSNR lower bound throughout optimization.

## Results

On MS-COCO, AdvMark achieves 1.00 clean bit accuracy and outperforms all 9 baselines across distortion, regeneration, and adversarial attacks. Headline improvements include up to 29% accuracy gain on combined distortion (0.83 vs. 0.54 for DADW), 33% on regeneration V1-4 (0.87 vs. 0.54 for DADW), and 46% on WEvade adversarial attack (0.98 vs. 0.52 for DADW). AdvMark also achieves the highest visual quality with PSNR 37.0, SSIM 0.99, and LPIPS 0.01 at 128×128 with n=30, surpassing the base MBRS model (PSNR 32.1) by nearly 5 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdvMark decisively outperforms all 9 baselines on the COCO dataset. Against the closest competitor MBRS (its base model), AdvMark gains 16% on WEvade (0.98 vs. 0.82), 11% on combined distortion (0.83 vs. 0.76), and 17% on regeneration V1-4 (0.87 vs. 0.70), while also improving PSNR from 32.1 to 37.0. DADW and EditGuard, despite achieving 1.00 clean accuracy, are the weakest on adversarial attacks (0.52 and 0.48 on WEvade respectively), where AdvMark reaches 0.98. VINE, designed for editing defense, shows strong distortion robustness (0.96 JPEG) but collapses on adversarial attacks (0.51 WEvade). HiDDeN, PIMoG, and Stable Signature trail across all three attack categories, with HiDDeN the weakest overall (0.70 JPEG, 0.57 V1-4, 0.57 WEvade). DwtDctSvd is not applicable to adversarial attacks due to non-differentiable transforms. The only metric where AdvMark does not lead is Black-Q, where all methods tie at ~0.73 because the attack guarantees evasion below the 0.75 threshold by design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on MBRS (Jia et al., ACM MM 2021) as its base encoder-decoder architecture, but fundamentally departs from the joint adversarial training (JAT) paradigm that underpins most prior deep watermarking methods (HiDDeN, DADW, PIMoG). Rather than simultaneously optimizing encoder and decoder against all attacks through a noise layer, AdvMark decouples defense into two stages: encoder-centric adversarial fine-tuning (inspired by adversarial training literature but applied to move images into safe regions rather than reshape decision boundaries) followed by per-image optimization for distortion and regeneration. The threat model extends prior work by incorporating WEvade-style adversarial attacks (Jiang et al., CCS 2023) and diffusion-based regeneration attacks (Zhao et al., 2023) alongside standard distortion, and the constrained image loss in Stage 2 introduces a theoretical robustness-preservation guarantee not present in prior watermarking methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The systematic demonstration that joint training simultaneously degrades clean accuracy and limits robustness against non-trivial attacks provides a clear diagnostic for the field, and the decoupling strategy is shown to be transferable (applied to HiDDeN in Table 4). However, the Black-Q attack still achieves 0.73 bit accuracy (below the 0.75 evasion threshold), indicating the method does not fully neutralize query-based adversarial evasion. The per-image optimization in Stage 2 introduces inference overhead that may limit deployment in high-throughput settings, and the theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) relies on a practical but unverified assumption about the constrained loss. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to video watermarking, larger message lengths, or in-processing methods like Stable Signature without the same per-image optimization cost.
