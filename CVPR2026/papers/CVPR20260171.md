## Problem

Existing 3D generation methods produce visually appealing assets but lack the physical attributes (density, absolute scale, joint constraints) and articulation structure required for direct deployment in physics simulators. Even prior physical 3D generators like PhysXGen do not support plug-and-play integration with standard engines such as MuJoCo, leaving a substantial gap between synthetic 3D content and practical use in embodied AI and robotic manipulation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260171/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Given a single in-the-wild RGB image, the system produces simulation-ready 3D assets in six formats: URDF, XML, part-level meshes, radiance fields, and 3D Gaussians. The VLM first emits a tree-structured JSON representation encoding overall physical properties (material, affordance, absolute scale) and per-part kinematic parameters (axis location, motion range, direction) in voxel space, which is then decoded into the final deployable assets. No additional point clouds, multi-view inputs, or manual part annotations are required.

## Method

PhysX-Anything fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL via a multi-round dialogue to jointly predict a global physical description and per-part coarse geometry on a 32³ voxel grid, using a novel representation that linearizes the grid, serializes only occupied voxels, and merges neighboring indices with hyphens to achieve 193× token compression without introducing any special tokens or new tokenizer. A controllable flow transformer (ControlNet-style) then refines the coarse voxels into fine-grained geometry, conditioned on the input image, followed by a pre-trained structured latent diffusion model that synthesizes mesh surfaces, radiance fields, and 3D Gaussians. A nearest-neighbor segmentation step splits the reconstructed mesh into part-level components guided by voxel assignments, and the global structural information is combined with the refined geometry to emit URDF/XML files directly importable into MuJoCo-style simulators.

## Results

On PhysX-Mobility, PhysX-Anything achieves the best scores across all geometry and physical metrics: PSNR 20.35, CD 14.43, F-score 77.50, and kinematic parameter accuracy 0.83. The most striking gain is on absolute scale, where PhysX-Anything reduces the error from 43.44 (PhysXGen) to 0.30, a >99% relative improvement. On physical attributes, it scores 17.52 (Material), 14.28 (Affordance), and 19.36 (Description), all substantially ahead of the next-best method. In-the-wild VLM evaluation (GPT-5) confirms strong generalization with 0.94 on both geometry and kinematic parameters, and user studies yield 0.98 geometry and 0.95–0.98 physical attribute scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PhysX-Anything is compared against three baselines: URDFformer (retrieval-based, no physical attributes), Articulate-Anything (retrieval-based, no physical attributes), and PhysXGen (diffusion-based, supports physical properties but not sim-ready). Against URDFformer and Articulate-Anything, PhysX-Anything wins decisively on every reported metric, as those methods lack physical attribute outputs entirely and produce substantially worse geometry (e.g., PSNR 7.97 and 16.90 vs. 20.35). Against the closest competitor PhysXGen, PhysX-Anything achieves comparable geometry (PSNR 20.35 vs. 20.33; CD 14.43 vs. 14.55) but dominates on all physical attributes: absolute scale error drops from 43.44 to 0.30 (>99% improvement), material score rises from 6.29 to 17.52, affordance from 9.75 to 14.28, kinematic parameters from 0.71 to 0.83, and description from 12.89 to 19.36. In in-the-wild VLM evaluation, PhysX-Anything scores 0.94 on both geometry and kinematic parameters versus 0.65/0.61 for PhysXGen, confirming superior generalization beyond the training distribution. User studies (1,568 ratings from 14 volunteers) further validate the gap, with PhysX-Anything achieving 0.98 geometry and 0.94–0.98 physical scores versus 0.61 and 0.32–0.48 for PhysXGen.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhysX-Anything builds on two key prior works: the structured 3D latent diffusion framework of Xiang et al. [37], which provides the flow transformer architecture and pre-trained decoder for fine-grained geometry synthesis, and PhysX3D [4], whose tree-structured VLM-friendly representation is adopted for encoding overall physical and kinematic information. The paper departs from the retrieval-based paradigm of URDFformer and Articulate-Anything (which attach motions to existing meshes) and from diffusion-based physical generation (PhysXGen) by introducing a unified VLM-based pipeline that jointly predicts geometry, articulation, and physical properties. Its key methodological departure is a voxel-based tokenization with a merging strategy that achieves 193× compression without introducing special tokens or a new tokenizer, sidestepping the training complexity of ShapeLLM-Omni's 3D VQ-VAE approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing URDF and XML files directly importable into standard physics engines, PhysX-Anything closes the gap between 3D generation and practical deployment in embodied AI and robotic manipulation, a gap that prior physical 3D generators (including PhysXGen) left open. The PhysX-Mobility dataset (47 categories, 2K+ objects) meaningfully broadens the category coverage of existing physical 3D benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to deformable or soft-body objects, whether the 47-category training distribution is sufficient for long-tail real-world objects, and whether the MuJoCo-style validation (limited to a few contact-rich tasks) scales to more complex multi-object manipulation scenarios.
