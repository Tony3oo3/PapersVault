## Problem

Reasoning VLAs improve generalization and long-horizon planning by generating explicit chain-of-thought traces, but these lengthy textual CoTs (~250 tokens) introduce inference latencies of several seconds per decision (~0.1 Hz), which is incompatible with real-time embodied applications requiring 1–15 Hz control frequencies. The core challenge is how to compress reasoning into compact representations that preserve both linguistic planning quality and spatial-temporal grounding without the latency cost of autoregressive text generation.

## Input / Output

The system takes a visual observation (single or multi-view RGB image/video frame) paired with a language instruction and produces a continuous robot action chunk (7-DOF for single-arm or 14-DOF for bimanual control) via a diffusion Transformer policy. Internally, the VLM backbone (Qwen2.5-VL 3B) generates only M compact continuous latent vectors (e.g., 6) plus K learnable spatial tokens (e.g., 5) that are projected in parallel to 2D waypoints, replacing the ~250-token autoregressive CoT and 60–70-token waypoint sequence of prior reasoning VLAs. A verbalizer LLM decodes the latents back to text solely during training for preference-based supervision and optional interpretability, and is absent at inference time.

## Method

Fast-ThinkAct employs a teacher-student distillation framework where a textual teacher VLM is first trained with GRPO using action-aligned visual rewards to produce explicit CoTs of varying quality. The student VLM compresses reasoning into M continuous latent vectors, trained with three jointly optimized losses: (1) a DPO-style preference loss (L_verb) that uses a verbalizer LLM to decode latents into text, rewarding high-advantage (GRPO-selected) reasoning traces over low-advantage ones; (2) an L2 distillation loss (L_distill) aligning the hidden state at the <answer> token between teacher and student to transfer visual planning capability; and (3) a waypoint regression loss (L_ans) from K parallel spatial tokens that simultaneously predict 2D trajectory waypoints. For action execution, the visual latent planning c_t is extracted from the spatial tokens' KV cache in early VLM layers and concatenated with state-encoder KV pairs, conditioning a frozen-backbone diffusion Transformer action model (RDT or DiT-Policy) via cross-attention, trained with imitation learning on large-scale robot data.

## Results

On RoboTwin2.0 (bimanual manipulation), Fast-ThinkAct achieves 65.7% (Easy) and 26.4% (Hard) average success rate, surpassing ThinkAct (62.4/24.7) and RDT (56.4/22.8). On embodied reasoning benchmarks, Fast-ThinkAct-3B scores 46.4 on EgoPlan-Bench2, 60.8 BLEU on RoboVQA, and 51.2 on OpenEQA (52.8 overall), exceeding the runner-up ThinkAct-3B by 2.4, 5.5, and 1.1 points respectively. Inference latency is reduced by 89.3% compared to ThinkAct-7B and 88.0% compared to MolmoAct-7B, while maintaining or improving task success rates across LIBERO and SimplerEnv.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fast-ThinkAct outperforms all 17 compared baselines across every benchmark. On RoboTwin2.0, it beats the closest reasoning VLA ThinkAct by 3.3% (Easy) and 1.7% (Hard) while being 7× faster in inference; against the non-reasoning RDT it gains 9.3% (Easy) and 3.6% (Hard). On embodied reasoning, Fast-ThinkAct-3B surpasses ThinkAct-3B by 2.4 points on EgoPlan-Bench2, 5.5 BLEU on RoboVQA, and 1.1 on OpenEQA, and also exceeds proprietary models GPT-4V (by 13.8 overall) and Gemini-2.5-Flash (by 13.9 overall). On LIBERO and SimplerEnv, it achieves the highest success rates among all 7B reasoning VLAs including CoT-VLA, MolmoAct, and ThinkAct. On RoboFAC failure recovery, it outperforms the second-best RoboFAC-3B by 10.9 points (simulation) and 16.4 points (real-world). The key efficiency gain is 89.3% latency reduction vs ThinkAct-7B and 88.0% vs MolmoAct-7B, achieved by replacing ~250-token textual CoT with 6 compact latent tokens plus K parallel spatial tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ThinkAct's RL-based (GRPO) reasoning framework for VLA models by replacing its lengthy explicit textual CoTs (~250 tokens) with compact continuous latent tokens (~6), using a DPO-inspired preference loss to distill high-quality reasoning patterns from the teacher's GRPO advantage signals. It departs from prior latent-reasoning work in LLMs (Coconut, CODI, Soft Thinking) by grounding the latent space in embodied spatial-temporal dynamics through action-aligned trajectory distillation and learnable spatial tokens for parallel waypoint prediction. The reasoning-enhanced policy learning stage, which connects the student's KV-cache planning latents to a diffusion action model (RDT/DiT-Policy), bridges the gap between high-level visual planning and low-level control that prior reasoning VLAs leave implicit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 89.3% inference latency reduction while matching or exceeding ThinkAct's success rates addresses a critical bottleneck for real-time embodied AI (1–15 Hz control vs. ~0.1 Hz reasoning), making reasoning-enhanced VLA models practically deployable. The ablation revealing that naive CoT-SFT actually degrades structured reasoning benchmarks (EgoPlan, RoboVQA) relative to plain SFT—while helping open-ended QA—suggests that verbosity in supervised reasoning traces can be counterproductive, a finding that may reshape how the community designs CoT supervision for embodied tasks. Open questions include whether the verbalizable latent representation generalizes to truly novel task distributions beyond the training coverage, how the approach scales to longer-horizon or multi-robot settings, and whether the fixed 6-token budget is optimal across task complexities.
