## Problem

Existing 4D Gaussian Splatting methods overfit to discrete input frame indices, producing ghosting artifacts when rendering at intermediate timestamps — a form of temporal aliasing. Deformation-based approaches struggle with visibility and appearance changes under large motion, while 4D primitive-based methods suffer from temporal opacity collapse to sub-frame support. The paper identifies these as complementary limitations and seeks a representation that dynamically appears/disappears, is regularized against sparse temporal sampling, and maintains consistent trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262961/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video (C cameras × T frames at 1K resolution, 17-frame clips at 15–22 FPS) along with bidirectional per-pixel optical flow computed off-the-shelf via WAFT. It produces a 4D Gaussian Splatting representation parameterized per-primitive by temporal boundaries, a pseudo-mean, three velocity components, scale, rotation polynomial, spherical harmonics, and base opacity, from which frames can be rendered at any continuous timestamp. Evaluation uses held-out intermediate frames (every other frame) as ground truth, with metrics computed on foreground-masked regions to avoid static-background inflation.

## Method

RetimeGS replaces the 1D Gaussian temporal opacity with a short-tailed kernel formed by the product of two boundary-aware sigmoid functions, preventing collapse to sub-frame support while allowing primitives to smoothly fade in/out between adjacent input frames. Spatial trajectories are modeled as Catmull-Rom splines parameterized by a pseudo-mean and three velocity components (from adjacent intervals), explicitly supervised by bidirectional optical flow via rasterized 3D flow maps. Training employs triple-rendering supervision (rendering all primitives plus each temporal subset separately against the same ground-truth frame), dynamic stretching that extends temporal boundaries of static primitives to reduce redundancy, MCMC-based periodic relocation weighted by a duration-normalized sampling score, and flow-aware initialization from VGGT point clouds. The core design principle is that a single set of primitives must jointly explain two adjacent input frames and the continuous interval between them, integrating the flexibility of 4D primitives with the trajectory consistency of deformation-based methods.

## Results

On the Stage-Capture Dataset (foreground region), RetimeGS achieves 30.08 PSNR, 0.904 SSIM, and 0.0225 LPIPS on held-out intermediate frames, outperforming all four baselines across every metric. The closest competitor, the 2D Lifting baseline (FILM + 4DGS), trails by 1.29 dB in PSNR and 0.018 in SSIM. The largest margin is against STGS (4D primitive-based), where RetimeGS gains 4.74 dB in PSNR and reduces LPIPS by 0.0132, reflecting the elimination of ghosting artifacts under large inter-frame motion.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RetimeGS wins on all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) against every baseline. Against the deformation-based Deform-GS [39], it gains 1.63 dB PSNR and 0.037 SSIM; the paper attributes Deform-GS's weakness to unreliable correspondence estimation under fast motion and visibility changes, causing blur and split geometry. Against the 4D-primitive-based STGS [44], the margin is largest (+4.74 dB PSNR), as STGS's unregularized temporal opacity collapses to sub-frame support, producing prominent ghosting at intermediate timestamps. GaussianFlow [6], which adds forward optical flow supervision to a 4D-primitive representation, improves over STGS but still lags RetimeGS by 4.17 dB PSNR because its primitives remain prone to temporal overfitting. The strongest baseline, 2D Lifting (applying FILM frame interpolation per view then reconstructing with 4DGS [44]), achieves 28.79 PSNR but suffers from view and temporal inconsistencies—ghosting, blurred textures, and objects reconstructed at incorrect spatial positions—whereas RetimeGS enforces a single coherent 3D representation supervised by both RGB and bidirectional flow.

## Relation to Prior Work

RetimeGS sits at the intersection of two dominant 4DGS paradigms: deformation-based methods (Deform-GS, 4DGS) that model dynamics via canonical-space deformation fields, and 4D-primitive methods (STGS, FreeTimeGS) that use temporal opacity to let Gaussians appear and disappear. It departs from both by adopting the appear/disappear flexibility of 4D primitives but constraining them with a short-tailed sigmoid temporal opacity that spans exactly one inter-frame interval, while borrowing the efficiency of deformation methods through dynamic stretching of static primitives. The Catmull-Rom spline trajectory supervised by bidirectional optical flow extends GaussianFlow's pairwise flow supervision into a multi-frame smooth trajectory, and the triple-rendering loss is a novel training strategy not present in prior 4DGS work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Temporal retiming at arbitrary timestamps is a practical requirement for VR rendering, slow-motion playback, and VFX pipelines, yet existing 4DGS methods produce ghosting artifacts when forced to render between discrete input frames. By identifying this failure as temporal aliasing and addressing it with a principled representation design, RetimeGS provides a clear recipe that other 4DGS methods can adopt. The main open limitation is at extremely low frame rates where optical flow correspondence breaks down, reducing the problem to a 4D multi-start-end interpolation task that remains unsolved. The reliance on off-the-shelf flow (WAFT) and point clouds (VGGT) also means quality is bounded by those upstream estimates.
