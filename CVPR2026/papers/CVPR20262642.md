## Problem

Existing weakly-supervised video anomaly detection methods face a fundamental trade-off: learning a single generic anomaly pattern yields strong cross-scene generalization but poor discrimination, while class-specific diversity modeling improves separability but overfits to coarse labels and ignores shared semantics. No prior approach jointly captures both the general regularities common to all anomalies and the fine-grained semantic diversity across anomaly types without relying on rigid class-to-expert assignments.

## Input / Output

The method consumes untrimmed video clips of fixed length T=256 frames, processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder to obtain per-frame visual features, along with a single video-level binary label (normal or anomalous). At inference it outputs a continuous anomaly score in [0,1] for every frame. During training, the model also receives LLM-generated anomaly prototype text descriptions (one per anomaly category) encoded by CLIP's text encoder, which guide the routing of anomalous snippets to semantically relevant experts.

## Method

MoME comprises a Mixture of Normal Memory Experts (MoNE) and a Mixture of Anomaly Memory Experts (MoAE), where each expert contains an internal memory for specialized patterns and all experts share an external memory updated via an erase-and-write mechanism to encode general knowledge. The Anomaly Prototype Router (APR) computes CLIP-based visual-textual similarity between anomalous snippets and LLM-derived semantic prototypes, then maps prototypes to experts through a learnable allocation matrix regularized for sparsity and orthogonality; a separate Latent Router captures implicit diversity, and an Adaptive Fuser re-weights both routing pathways per snippet. A sparse Top-K routing scheme (k=1) assigns each snippet to a single expert, while a distinctiveness loss maximizes pairwise L1 distances among expert internal memories to enforce specialization. Training jointly optimizes six losses: binary classification, memory-based supervision, expert load balancing, expert distinctiveness, APR regularization, and a reconstruction task that enforces correct normal/anomaly feature alignment.

## Results

MoME achieves state-of-the-art performance on XD-Violence with 86.15% AP, surpassing the previous best CLIP-based method DEN-VAD (86.13%) and VadCLIP (84.51%). On UCF-Crime, MoME reaches 88.32% AUC, competitive among CLIP-based approaches (second only to ITC at 89.04%) but trailing the I3D-based GS-MoE (91.58%). The method demonstrates a more balanced performance profile across both datasets compared to class-specific approaches like GS-MoE and ITC, which excel on UCF-Crime but degrade more sharply on the more diverse XD-Violence benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among CLIP-based weakly-supervised methods, MoME outperforms the closest competitor VadCLIP [37] on both datasets (88.32% vs 88.02% AUC on UCF-Crime; 86.15% vs 84.51% AP on XD-Violence), with the largest margin on XD-Violence (+1.64%). MoME also surpasses DEN-VAD [39] on both benchmarks (88.32% vs 87.86% AUC; 86.15% vs 86.13% AP) and TPWNG [38] by a wider margin (88.32% vs 87.79% AUC; 86.15% vs 83.68% AP). However, MoME trails ITC [17] on UCF-Crime (88.32% vs 89.04% AUC) while leading on XD-Violence (86.15% vs 85.45% AP), and is substantially behind the I3D-based GS-MoE [2] on UCF-Crime (88.32% vs 91.58% AUC) but ahead on XD-Violence (86.15% vs 82.89% AP). The authors attribute GS-MoE's UCF-Crime advantage to the lower diversity and higher class separability of that dataset, whereas MoME's joint general-and-diverse modeling yields more balanced cross-dataset performance. Overall, MoME is the only CLIP-based method to simultaneously achieve top-2 performance on both benchmarks, validating the benefit of jointly learning general and semantically diverse patterns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse Mixture-of-Experts paradigm (Shazeer et al.) and memory-augmented architectures (UR-DMU) into the weakly-supervised video anomaly detection setting, departing from GS-MoE's dense, class-tied expert design by decoupling expert count from anomaly categories and introducing a shared external memory for general pattern learning. It builds directly on VadCLIP's temporal modeling pipeline and CLIP-based feature extraction, while introducing the Anomaly Prototype Router as a novel mechanism that leverages LLM-generated semantic prototypes to guide expert routing beyond coarse class labels. The key conceptual departure is the explicit joint modeling of general (shared memory) and diverse (expert-specific internal memory) patterns, addressing the generalization-discrimination tradeoff that prior methods handle by choosing one extreme or the other.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental tension in wVAD—generic patterns generalize but lack discrimination, while class-specific patterns discriminate but overfit—by unifying both through a memory-expert architecture, which is a practically useful design principle for any multi-pattern detection task. The LLM-based prototype routing is an interesting direction for injecting open-world semantic knowledge into detection pipelines, though the current implementation relies on manual selection of LLM outputs, limiting full automation. Open questions include whether the approach scales to truly open-vocabulary anomaly detection without any predefined class set, and whether the modest UCF-Crime gap versus ITC/GS-MoE reflects a fundamental limitation of the memory-based scoring mechanism on highly structured datasets.
