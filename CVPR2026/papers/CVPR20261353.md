## Problem

Existing reward models for image generation (e.g., HPSv3, UnifiedReward) target general image aesthetics and largely overlook the dimensions critical to graphic design—typography accuracy, text rendering, and layout composition. Compounding this, the scarcity of poster-specific preference data (design accounts for only 9.9% of HPDv3) limits both evaluation and reward-model-driven post-training of text-to-image models for poster creation.

## Input / Output

PosterReward (discriminative) consumes a single poster image, its text prompt, and a multi-dimensional analysis text generated by a first-stage module, then outputs a scalar reward score via a two-layer MLP head (SiLU-activated) replacing the final layer of Qwen3-VL-8B. PosterReward-Pairwise (generative) takes two poster images and a shared prompt, and outputs a binary Yes/No preference judgment followed by a Chain-of-Thought reasoning over five dimensions (Foundational Visual Quality, AI Artifacts, Textual Accuracy, Prompt Fidelity, Aesthetic Value); at inference the CoT can be omitted for speed, and scores are derived from the judgment-token logits. A simplified variant, PosterReward-Lite, skips the analysis module entirely for latency-sensitive settings.

## Method

The core architecture is a two-stage discriminative model where a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-8B analysis module produces explicit multi-dimensional textual reasoning about a poster, which is then concatenated with the image and prompt and fed to a scoring module (same backbone with an MLP regression head) that outputs the final scalar score. Training follows a four-stage cascaded pipeline: (1) Joint SFT on 246k single-image analyses and 160k paired comparisons annotated by Gemini-2.5-Pro, (2) Joint Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning using Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite as a quality filter, (3) Scoring-module training on preference triplets (image, prompt, analysis) with Bradley-Terry loss, and (4) GRPO fine-tuning of the analysis module with the frozen scoring module as the reward signal. The key insight is that image analysis synergistically improves both model families: the analysis task directly boosts PosterReward-Pairwise's judgment accuracy, while the refined analysis text significantly enhances PosterReward's scoring quality and provides interpretability plus test-time scaling. The 70K training dataset is constructed entirely via an automated MLLM-consensus pipeline (Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-5, GLM-4.5v with positional-bias mitigation) rather than manual annotation.

## Results

On PosterRewardBench-Advanced (the harder subset with subtler quality differences), PosterReward achieves 86.0% pairwise accuracy, substantially outperforming HPSv3 (41.2%), UnifiedReward (52.7%), and HPSv2 (43.7%). On the OOD PosterRewardBench-Basic, PosterReward reaches 86.7%, again far ahead of all baselines (best baseline: HPSv3 at 72.9%). On the general-purpose HPDv3 benchmark, PosterReward attains 77.8% accuracy, marginally surpassing HPSv3 (76.9%). The pairwise variant, PosterReward-Pairwise, scores 83.8% on PRB-Advanced and 83.0% on PRB-Basic, ranking first on the advanced subset and second only to GPT-5 (85.4%) on the basic subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PosterReward decisively outperforms all general-purpose reward models on poster-specific benchmarks: on PRB-Advanced it leads HPSv3 by 44.8 points (86.0 vs. 41.2), UnifiedReward by 33.3 points (86.0 vs. 52.7), and HPSv2 by 42.3 points (86.0 vs. 43.7). On the OOD PRB-Basic, the margin over HPSv3 narrows to 13.8 points (86.7 vs. 72.9), but PosterReward still dominates. On the general HPDv3 benchmark, PosterReward (77.8%) edges out HPSv3 (76.9%) by less than 1 point, indicating comparable general image-preference capability. The pairwise variant PosterReward-Pairwise (83.8% on PRB-Ad) surpasses Gemini-2.5-Pro (75.2%) and UnifiedReward-think (50.6%) by large margins, but trails GPT-5 (82.9%) by only 0.9 points on PRB-Ad while losing 2.4 points on PRB-Basic (83.0 vs. 85.4). Notably, PosterReward-Pairwise exhibits minimal positional bias (84.1% Yes vs. 83.6% No on PRB-Ad), whereas MLLM baselines like Qwen3-VL-Plus show extreme bias (98.7% Yes vs. 14.2% No).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pointwise and pairwise reward modeling literature (HPSv3, UnifiedReward, RewardDance, BaseReward) from general image aesthetics to the specific multi-dimensional evaluation of graphic design, where typography accuracy, layout composition, and text rendering must be assessed jointly rather than as a single scalar aesthetic score. It departs from prior preference-data collection (HPDv3, Pick-a-Pic) by replacing costly human annotation with a multi-MLLM consensus pipeline, and from prior graphic-design evaluation (Design-O-Meter, AesthetIQ, agentic review systems) by providing a unified scalar reward signal rather than fragmented, layout-centric feedback. The cascaded training pipeline that co-trains a discriminative scorer and a generative pairwise judge through shared analysis tasks is a new architectural combination not seen in prior reward-model work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first dedicated poster reward model and two new benchmarks, this work unblocks reward-model-driven post-training (Flow-GRPO, Pref-GRPO, Diffusion-NFT) for graphic design generation, a domain previously underserved by general-purpose reward models. The automated MLLM-consensus data pipeline offers a reusable blueprint for constructing preference datasets in other specialized visual domains where human annotation is prohibitively expensive. Open questions include whether the 70K MLLM-annotated preferences truly capture the full nuance of human design judgment at scale, whether the approach generalizes beyond posters to broader design tasks (UI, packaging, editorial), and whether the modest ~1% gain from the analysis module over the Lite variant justifies its inference cost in production settings.
