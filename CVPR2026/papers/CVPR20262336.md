## Problem

Current instruction-based diffusion editing models (e.g., FLUX.1-Kontext) uniformly denoise every image token at every timestep, even though most edits modify only a small region. This full-image regeneration wastes computation on unmodified areas and can introduce subtle artifacts in non-edited regions. The paper asks whether it is truly necessary to regenerate every region and proposes a training-free mechanism to skip computation on stable, non-edited tokens.

## Input / Output

SpotEdit operates on 1024×1024 RGB condition images paired with free-form text editing instructions, producing a single edited image at the same resolution. It is applied as a drop-in acceleration layer on top of existing DiT-based editing models (FLUX.1-Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit) without any retraining. Non-edited regions in the output are directly overwritten with the corresponding condition-image latent tokens before VAE decoding, guaranteeing pixel-level background fidelity.

## Method

SpotEdit comprises two training-free components. SpotSelector exploits the closed-form reconstruction property of Rectified Flow (X̂₀ = X_t − t·v_θ) to estimate the fully denoised latent at each timestep, then computes a token-level LPIPS-like perceptual score from VAE decoder activations comparing X̂₀ to the condition image; tokens whose score falls below a threshold τ are classified as non-edited and removed from DiT forward passes. SpotFusion addresses the resulting context loss by maintaining a temporally consistent KV cache: at each block and timestep it blends cached non-edited token KV pairs with condition-image KV pairs using a cosine-squared schedule α(t)=cos²(πt/2), shifting weight from cached features toward reference features as denoising progresses. Attention is then computed with queries restricted to regenerated tokens plus prompt tokens, while keys and values are completed by concatenating the fused cached features, concentrating computation exclusively on edited regions while preserving full spatial context.

## Results

On imgEdit-Benchmark, SpotEdit matches the original FLUX.1-Kontext inference quality (CLIP 0.699, SSIMc 0.67, PSNR 16.45 vs. 16.40) while achieving a 1.67× speedup (16.23 s vs. 27.10 s latency). On PIE-Bench++, SpotEdit attains near-identical fidelity (CLIP 0.741, SSIMc 0.792, PSNR 18.73, DISTS 0.136) with a 1.95× speedup. In the VL-score evaluation across eight imgEdit subsets, SpotEdit achieves the highest average score of 3.77, compared to 3.91 for the original model and 3.41–3.70 for baselines. Cross-model validation on Qwen-Image-Edit confirms generalizability with 1.59× and 1.72× speedups while preserving or slightly improving fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpotEdit is compared against four baselines spanning two paradigms: cache-acceleration methods (TeaCache, TaylorSeer) and precise-editing methods (FollowYourShape in single/multi configurations). Against FollowYourShape, SpotEdit dominates on every metric: it is 5–7× faster (1.67× vs. 0.27–0.33× speedup) and achieves substantially higher structural fidelity (PSNR 16.45 vs. 11.47–11.73 on imgEdit; 18.73 vs. 12.58–12.91 on PIE-Bench++), indicating that FollowYourShape's trajectory-guided control distorts non-edited regions. Against the cache-acceleration baselines TeaCache and TaylorSeer, SpotEdit trades some speed (1.67–1.95× vs. 3.4–3.9×) for markedly better quality: PSNR is 1.4–2.1 dB higher on imgEdit and 0.9–0.9 dB higher on PIE-Bench++, while DISTS is 0.05–0.21 lower, confirming that full-token caching degrades edited regions disproportionately. On the VL-score evaluation (Table 2), SpotEdit achieves the highest average (3.77) among all baselines, outperforming TeaCache (3.70) and TaylorSeer (3.67), and approaching the original model (3.91). Cross-model validation against Qwen-Image-Edit (Table 4) shows SpotEdit preserves fidelity (+0.01 PSNR on imgEdit, +1.08 PSNR on PIE-Bench++) while delivering 1.59× and 1.72× speedups, confirming the method's architecture-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpotEdit builds on the Diffusion Transformer editing paradigm (FLUX.1-Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit) where condition images are encoded and jointly processed with noise tokens, and on Rectified Flow's closed-form reconstruction property that enables early-stage latent estimation. It departs from full-token acceleration methods (TeaCache, TaylorSeer) that uniformly cache or forecast all spatial positions, and from token-space strategies (ToCa, DUCA, RAS) that reduce timesteps or compress representations without distinguishing edited from non-edited regions. The key conceptual departure is treating image editing as inherently sparse: only a subset of tokens requires denoising, and the rest can be identified via perceptual similarity (LPIPS-like VAE decoder scores) and exempted from DiT computation entirely, with a temporally consistent KV fusion mechanism (SpotFusion) preserving contextual coherence for the active tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SpotEdit offers a training-free, plug-and-play acceleration that preserves non-edited region fidelity almost perfectly (near-zero PSNR/SSIM loss) at a moderate 1.7–1.9× speedup, addressing a practical pain point where full-image regeneration introduces subtle background artifacts and wastes compute. The approach is validated across two architectures (FLUX-Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit) and two benchmarks, but the speedup remains modest compared to aggressive caching methods (3.4–3.9×), and the method's effectiveness depends on the editing task being genuinely local (single-region edits). Open questions include how the approach generalizes to multi-region or global edits, video editing, and whether the perceptual threshold can be made fully adaptive without manual tuning.
