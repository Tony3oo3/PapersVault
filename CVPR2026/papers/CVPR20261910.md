## Problem

Prior video hallucination benchmarks attribute errors to language priors, missing frames, or visual encoder biases, but overlook a distinct failure mode where frame-level semantics are correctly perceived yet misaggregated across temporal events. This Semantic Aggregation Hallucination (SAH) becomes prevalent in complex, multi-event videos but remains unmeasured because existing benchmarks target short clips with limited semantic complexity where such cross-event confusion is rare.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261910/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark presents event-by-event videos (2–10 events, average duration ~680 s) alongside binary caption-verification questions of the form 'Is the following caption totally correct? Reply with Yes or No.' Each video yields 24 questions organized into adversarial triplets: a ground-truth caption paired with an in-video hallucinated caption (object swapped from another event in the same video) and an out-of-video hallucinated caption (object fabricated entirely). The model's binary answers are aggregated into per-aspect accuracy (visual details, object, action, declarative content) and the SAH Ratio, which normalizes the in/out-video accuracy gap by the total error rate.

## Method

ELV-Halluc is constructed via a semi-automated pipeline: Gemini-2.5-Flash generates initial event-segmented captions on 348 manually curated YouTube videos, which annotators then verify and refine; GPT-4o introduces controlled single-aspect modifications to create in-video and out-of-video hallucinated captions, yielding 4,800 binary QA pairs (3,200 adversarial pairs). The SAH Ratio metric, (OutAcc − InAcc)/(1 − InAcc), isolates cross-event aggregation errors from general hallucination by subtracting the out-video mislead rate (where SAH is impossible) from the in-video mislead rate. For mitigation, the paper evaluates four RoPE variants (Vanilla, TAD, mRoPE, VideoRoPE) on Qwen2-VL checkpoints, finding VideoRoPE reduces SAH by preserving temporal structure, and applies DPO with 8K curated positive–negative caption pairs (in-video and out-video hallucinations) to Qwen2.5-VL-7B, achieving a 27.7% SAH reduction while improving VideoMME accuracy by 0.4%.

## Results

On ELV-Halluc, the best open-source model (Qwen3-VL-32B) achieves 34.97% overall accuracy with a 3.49% SAH ratio, while the lowest SAH ratio among open-source models is 0.18% (Qwen2.5-VL-32B). Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Flash leads with 53.09% accuracy but exhibits a high 9.78% SAH ratio, whereas GPT-4o achieves 8.70% accuracy with a 1.04% SAH ratio. The DPO mitigation strategy using in-video pairs reduces the SAH ratio by 27.7% (from 8.3% to 6.0%) on Qwen2.5-VL-7B while improving VideoMME average accuracy by +0.4%. VideoRoPE achieves the lowest SAH ratio (0.88) among positional encoding variants tested on Qwen2-VL checkpoints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 17 open-source and 2 proprietary Video-MLLMs on ELV-Halluc, revealing that SAH does not correlate with overall hallucination accuracy: Qwen2.5-VL-32B has the lowest SAH ratio (0.18%) despite moderate accuracy (17.73%), while Gemini-2.5-Flash achieves the highest accuracy (53.09%) but the worst SAH ratio (9.78%). Among the Qwen2.5-VL family, the 32B variant (with RL post-training) dramatically outperforms its 3B (4.59%), 7B (8.88%), and 72B (5.89%) counterparts on SAH ratio, suggesting RL training specifically mitigates semantic aggregation errors. The InternVL3 series shows a consistent trend where larger models (78B: 5.41%) do not uniformly beat smaller ones (1B: 1.68%) on SAH ratio. For positional encoding, VideoRoPE (0.88 SAH) and M-RoPE (0.95) both substantially outperform vanilla RoPE (1.82) and TAD-RoPE (2.18) on Qwen2-VL checkpoints. The DPO mitigation with in-video pairs reduces SAH by 27.7% on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (8.3→6.0), while out-video DPO paradoxically increases SAH by 3.6%, confirming that in-video semantic alignment is the critical factor for SAH reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new sub-problem within the existing video hallucination evaluation landscape (VideoHallucer, EventHallusion, VidHalluc, ARGUS) by identifying Semantic Aggregation Hallucination (SAH) as a distinct error type where frame-level perception is correct but cross-event semantic grouping fails. Prior benchmarks target short, single-event clips where such errors are rare, so SAH has been conflated with language-prior or vision-encoder biases. For mitigation, the paper builds on DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) for preference alignment and on video-specific rotary position embeddings (VideoRoPE, mRoPE) to strengthen temporal binding, combining these into a unified SAH-reduction pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By isolating SAH from general hallucination, the paper shows that the two can move in opposite directions (more frames improve overall accuracy yet worsen SAH) and that model scale does not consistently reduce SAH—challenging the assumption that larger models uniformly handle all error modes. The 27.7% SAH reduction via in-video DPO pairs offers a practical, low-cost mitigation for video MLLM developers. Open questions include whether the binary Yes/No QA format captures the full spectrum of SAH, whether findings generalize beyond event-by-event videos, and whether the Gemini-assisted annotation pipeline introduces systematic bias that inflates certain models' scores.
