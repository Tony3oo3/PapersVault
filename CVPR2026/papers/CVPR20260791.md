## Problem

Reconstructing 3D scenes from sparse, unposed internet photos is hindered by wide illumination variation and transient occluders (people, vehicles). Optimization-based in-the-wild methods require 2–8 hours of per-scene training and still fail under sparse views, while existing feed-forward models assume fixed lighting and cannot handle dynamic content. No prior approach integrates both appearance adaptation and occlusion removal within a single feed-forward 3D reconstruction pipeline.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260791/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 2–6 unposed, uncalibrated RGB images of an outdoor scene (no COLMAP or any pose estimation required) plus a single target lighting image that may come from an entirely different scene. It outputs a canonical 3D Gaussian representation (position, opacity, rotation, scale, and 75-dim SH coefficients per Gaussian), per-view depth maps, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics, all in a single ~3-second forward pass. Novel views are rendered by rasterizing the appearance-modulated Gaussians under the target light code, enabling cross-scene illumination transfer without any test-time optimization.

## Method

GenWildSplat builds on AnySplat's VGGT transformer backbone, which extracts multi-view features decoded by DPT heads into depth, camera parameters, and per-pixel Gaussian attributes that are voxelized into a compact canonical 3D Gaussian set. The key architectural addition is an Appearance Adapter: a 2D CNN light encoder produces a 16-dim per-view light code, and an MLP uses this code to modulate the canonical 75-dim spherical-harmonic color coefficients, mapping them to the target lighting space in 3D rather than per-pixel in 2D. For occlusions, a frozen YOLOv8 segmentation network generates binary transient masks (person, car, etc.) that zero out dynamic regions in both the target and rendered images, preventing the model from collapsing visibility estimates on hard regions. Training follows a three-stage curriculum—(1) single-scene illumination variation, (2) multi-scene geometric/appearance generalization, (3) synthetic occlusion compositing from COCO segments—on 700+ DL3DV scenes with DiffusionRenderer relighting, using self-supervised masked reconstruction loss (MSE + perceptual) with no ground-truth 3D or paired multi-lighting data.

## Results

On the MegaScenes dataset (6-view setting), GenWildSplat achieves PSNR 15.84, SSIM 0.440, and LPIPS 0.407, outperforming all optimization-based in-the-wild baselines by a wide margin. In the sparser 3-view setting, it reaches PSNR 14.43, SSIM 0.402, and LPIPS 0.496. Critically, GenWildSplat completes inference in 3 seconds (feed-forward), whereas the strongest baseline (NexusSplats) requires 2.4 hours of per-scene optimization. Against feed-forward baselines (Table 3), GenWildSplat improves PSNR from 13.59 (best feed-forward baseline) to 15.84 while being the only method that is view-consistent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenWildSplat outperforms all four baselines on every metric in both 3-view and 6-view settings. Against the strongest optimization-based baseline, NexusSplats [35], it gains +1.92 dB PSNR (15.84 vs 13.92) and reduces LPIPS from 0.518 to 0.407 in the 6-view setting, while being ~2880× faster (3 s vs 2.4 hrs). WildGaussians [16] and GS-W [53] trail even further, with PSNR gaps of 2.55 and 3.83 dB respectively at 6 views. Against the feed-forward baseline AnySplat [11] (Table 3), GenWildSplat improves PSNR from 12.65 to 15.84 (+3.19 dB) and SSIM from 0.311 to 0.440, and is the only method that produces view-consistent renderings. The paper notes that all baselines were given VGGT poses (instead of COLMAP) to ensure a fair comparison, which already benefits the optimization-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenWildSplat builds directly on AnySplat, a feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting framework that reconstructs scenes from unposed images in a single pass, and extends it with two key modules: an appearance adapter that modulates canonical Gaussian colors via learned light codes, and a YOLOv8-based occlusion mask that excludes transient objects during training. This departs fundamentally from the dominant in-the-wild reconstruction paradigm (WildGaussians, NexusSplats, GS-W), which relies on per-scene optimization of appearance embeddings and dynamic masks over hours of test-time training. The curriculum learning strategy—progressively introducing lighting variation, multi-scene diversity, and synthetic occlusions on DL3DV—addresses the ill-posedness of jointly learning geometry, appearance, and occlusion from sparse unposed views, a problem prior feed-forward methods sidestep by assuming fixed lighting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a 3-second feed-forward pipeline can match or exceed hours-long per-scene optimization on challenging sparse-view benchmarks, this work shifts the practical viability of in-the-wild 3D reconstruction toward real-time applications (AR/VR, navigation). The cross-scene appearance transfer capability—applying lighting from one scene to another while preserving geometry—is absent in all prior in-the-wild methods and opens a path toward controllable, relightable 3D scenes from internet photos. However, significant limitations remain: no physically consistent shadow modeling, degraded performance on indoor scenes, incomplete geometry in sparsely observed regions, and evaluation on only 20 MegaScenes and 3 PhotoTourism scenes, leaving open whether the approach scales to truly diverse real-world collections.
