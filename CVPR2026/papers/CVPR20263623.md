## Problem

Remote sensing object detectors exhibit strong orientation bias and fail under geometric variation or partial occlusion because standard convolutions lack explicit mechanisms for rotation invariance and structural symmetry. Existing rotation-aware methods either rely on heavy feature realignment or equivariant architectures with high computational cost, while symmetry-aware approaches remain limited to architecture-level modifications. The paper asks whether SO(2) rotation geometry and symmetry priors can be directly embedded into the convolution operator itself without altering the detection framework.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB remote sensing images cropped into 1024×1024 patches with 200–500 pixel overlap (following LSKNet preprocessing) and outputs oriented bounding boxes with class labels. The key architectural detail is that the standard convolution in the backbone and detection head is replaced by the RIS-PDC operator, which operates on the same spatial patches but applies rotated kernel averaging and harmonic modulation internally, producing feature maps of identical spatial dimensions to vanilla convolution. The model is trained with ImageNet-1K pretraining followed by supervised fine-tuning on detection datasets.

## Method

RIS-PiDiNet introduces RIS-PDC, a unified convolutional operator that fuses three geometric mechanisms: (1) SO(2) kernel-domain rotation averaging over 8 discrete angles to achieve rotation invariance without rotating feature maps; (2) Polar Harmonic Transform (PHT) harmonic kernels H_i^(n,l) = cos(2πnr_i² + lθ_i) with learnable coefficients α_{n,l} to encode K-fold rotational and reflectional symmetry; and (3) Pixel Difference Convolution with Local Binary Patterns for high-frequency local texture sensitivity. The operator is plug-and-play, inserted at selected backbone layers and the detection head of an LSKNet-style stacked architecture, adding no extra parameters while increasing FLOPs modestly (161G→206G for the S variant). The symmetry-aware component (S-PDC) approximately satisfies S-PDC(S_k x) ≈ S-PDC(x) for symmetry operators S_k, while the full RIS-PDC satisfies RIS-PDC(R_φ x) ≈ RIS-PDC(x) for arbitrary input rotations R_φ.

## Results

On DOTA-v1.0, RIS-PiDiNet-S achieves 78.53% mAP (single-scale) and 81.81% mAP (multi-scale), surpassing LSKNet-S by +1.04% and +0.17% respectively, with the same 31.0M parameter count. On HRSC2016, it reaches 98.60% mAP(12), the highest among all compared methods. On DIOR-R, it attains 67.28% mAP, outperforming PKINet-S (67.03%) and LSKNet-S (65.90%). The method adds ~45G FLOPs over LSKNet-S (206G vs 161G) without increasing parameters, and demonstrates near-uniform accuracy across all rotation angles (Figure 5), unlike baselines that show significant directional variance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RIS-PiDiNet-S consistently outperforms all compared baselines across three benchmarks. On DOTA-v1.0 single-scale, it leads PKINet-S (78.39%) by +0.14%, PSC (78.10%) by +0.43%, MA3E (77.93%) by +0.60%, and LSKNet-S (77.49%) by +1.04% mAP, while matching LSKNet-S's 31.0M parameter budget. In multi-scale DOTA-v1.0, it surpasses LSKNet-S (81.64%) by +0.17% and RTMDet-R (81.33%) by +0.48%, though RVSA (81.24%) uses 3.7× more parameters (114.4M). On HRSC2016, the margin is tighter: +0.06% over PKINet-S (98.54%) and +0.14% over LSKNet-S (98.46%). On DIOR-R, it beats PKINet-S by +0.25% and LSKNet-S by +1.38%. The most distinctive advantage is rotation robustness: Figure 5 shows RIS-PiDiNet-S maintains nearly flat accuracy across 0°–360° rotations, whereas LSKNet-S, PKINet-S, and MA3E exhibit 2–5% directional swings, confirming the SO(2) kernel-averaging mechanism works as intended.

## Relation to Prior Work

RIS-PiDiNet builds directly on LSKNet's stacked backbone architecture and the Pixel Difference Convolution (PDC) operator, extending PDC with two geometric priors: SO(2) kernel-domain rotation averaging for invariance and Polar Harmonic Transform (PHT) kernels for symmetry awareness. Unlike prior rotation-invariant approaches (ReDet, FPNFormer, group-equivariant convolutions) that modify the overall detection pipeline or incur heavy computational cost, RIS-PDC is a plug-and-play convolutional operator that embeds Lie group structure and harmonic symmetry constraints directly into kernel weights without altering the surrounding detector. The paper positions itself as the first remote sensing detector to jointly model rotation invariance and internal structural symmetry within a single unified operator, rather than treating them as separate architectural concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play nature of RIS-PDC makes it immediately applicable to existing oriented detection frameworks (validated across YOLO, O-RCNN, RoI Transformer, S²A-Net, and R3Det), which is practically valuable for the RS detection community. However, the performance gains are modest (+1.04% on DOTA single-scale, +0.14% on DIOR-R), and the symmetry assumption may not hold for all object categories (e.g., irregularly shaped vehicles or asymmetric infrastructure). Open questions include whether the 8-direction SO(2) discretization is truly sufficient for continuous rotation invariance, whether the approach generalizes to non-RS domains, and whether the learned harmonic orders can be further compressed without accuracy loss.
