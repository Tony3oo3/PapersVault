## Problem

Existing domain-incremental learning (DIL) methods for medical imaging assume a fixed label space and pre-defined domain boundaries, which fails to capture the reality of clinical deployment where new disease subtypes, imaging protocols, and institutions continuously emerge. Under rehearsal-free constraints imposed by privacy regulations, models must adapt to evolving domains with expanding label spaces while retaining competence on all previously seen categories—a stability-plasticity balance that current prototype-based and prompt-based DIL approaches do not adequately address.

## Input / Output

The method accepts 2D dermoscopic images (skin pathology), 3D MRI volumes (Cyst-X pancreatic IPMN cohort), and natural RGB images (OfficeHome) as input, processed through a frozen ViT-B/16 backbone. Output is a class label predicted via cosine similarity between the extracted feature and a dynamically growing prototype memory bank, where the classifier head expands row-wise as new categories are introduced in later domains. Only ~0.26% of parameters are trainable (LoRA adapters with dynamic rank between 4 and 128), making the approach highly parameter-efficient compared to full fine-tuning baselines.

## Method

DK-DDIL builds on LoRA with two synergistic modules. The Dynamic Adaptation Module (DAM) replaces fixed-rank LoRA with a rank-adaptive adapter: a learnable score vector is passed through a temperature-scaled sigmoid with a straight-through estimator to produce a binary mask selecting active latent dimensions per domain, subject to a minimum-rank constraint and a dynamic scaling factor inversely proportional to the number of activated components, plus an L1-style sparsity regularization on the sigmoid outputs. The Knowledge Inheritance and Refinement (KIR) module operates at two levels: (i) Model Fusion based Knowledge Inheritance (MKI) selectively fuses only the B projection matrices from all historical DAMs into the current one using a cosine-annealed coefficient that decays from high inheritance to full domain-specific learning over epochs; and (ii) Domain Contrastive Learning (DCL) refines the embedding space through four terms—positive prototype alignment, intra-domain InfoNCE separation, cross-domain negative suppression that penalizes alignment with semantically unrelated historical prototypes, and instance-level intra-class compactness—weighted by a curriculum schedule that strengthens negative contrast as more samples are observed. The total loss combines cross-entropy, rank sparsity regularization, and the DCL objective.

## Results

DK-DDIL achieves the best average accuracy (Ā) and final accuracy (A_T) across all three benchmarks: 77.03%/71.52% on Skin Pathology Diagnosis, 53.34%/51.08% on Cyst-X, and 84.35%/86.29% on OfficeHome, while using only 0.26% trainable parameters. On the most challenging Cyst-X 3D MRI benchmark, DK-DDIL surpasses the second-best method (RanPAC, 52.56% Ā) by approximately 0.8% in average accuracy and 2.9% in final accuracy. On OfficeHome, DK-DDIL leads DUCT (85.80% A_T) by 0.49% in final accuracy with roughly one order of magnitude fewer trainable parameters (0.26% vs. 100%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DK-DDIL outperforms all 11 baselines on every benchmark and metric. The closest competitors are RanPAC [18] (74.79% Ā on Skin, 52.56% Ā on Cyst-X, 82.22% Ā on OfficeHome) and DUCT [41] (71.44% Ā on Skin, 52.51% Ā on Cyst-X, 85.80% A_T on OfficeHome), with DK-DDIL leading by 2.2–4.8% in average accuracy depending on the dataset. On the Cyst-X 3D MRI benchmark, where cross-institutional domain gaps are most severe, DK-DDIL achieves 53.34% Ā vs. 52.56% for RanPAC and 52.51% for DUCT, a margin of roughly 0.8–1.0%. On OfficeHome, DK-DDIL's 86.29% final accuracy exceeds DUCT's 85.80% and RanPAC's 84.70%, while using only 0.26% trainable parameters compared to DUCT's 100% and RanPAC's 2.03%. Prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt) trail by 4–13% in average accuracy on medical benchmarks, suggesting limited representational capacity for complex domain shifts. Adapter-based methods like CL-LoRA [9] (72.53% Ā on Skin, 40.82% Ā on Cyst-X) and MOS [28] (71.48% Ā on Skin, 38.63% Ā on Cyst-X) show that fixed-rank or manually designed adapters struggle with the dynamic label-space expansion that DK-DDIL handles natively.

## Relation to Prior Work

DK-DDIL extends the LoRA-based continual learning line (CL-LoRA, CoDyRA, DoRA) by replacing fixed-rank adapters with a learnable, data-driven rank regulation mechanism using straight-through estimators, and combines this with model-fusion-inspired parameter inheritance (akin to FedAvg-style averaging) and prototype-level contrastive refinement. It departs from prior DIL work (L2P, DualPrompt, CODA-Prompt, RanPAC, DUCT) by explicitly formulating the problem with an evolving label space rather than a fixed one, and by operating in a rehearsal-free regime motivated by clinical privacy constraints. The framework is built on ViT-B/16 as a frozen backbone, positioning it within the broader PEFT-for-continual-learning paradigm while adding domain-aware capacity scaling that prior adapter-based DIL methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dynamic label-space formulation addresses a gap between academic DIL benchmarks and real clinical deployment, where annotation taxonomies and disease subtypes genuinely evolve across institutions and time. The sub-0.3% trainable parameter budget makes the approach practical for resource-constrained or privacy-sensitive settings where full fine-tuning is infeasible. Open questions include scalability to very long domain sequences (beyond the 7-domain evaluation), whether the dynamic rank mechanism generalizes to non-ViT backbones or 3D architectures natively, and how the method performs when domain shifts are adversarial rather than merely distributional. The authors themselves flag cross-modal continual learning and integration with large foundation models as future directions.
