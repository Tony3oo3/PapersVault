## Problem

Existing multimodal image fusion methods either assume clean source images or rely on decoupled restoration-then-fusion pipelines that accumulate errors across stages. Diffusion models offer structured, interpretable inference but are difficult to apply to fusion because they require a target data distribution (no natural fused labels exist) and operate on single-domain distributions, while real-world fusion must simultaneously handle arbitrary degradations (noise, blur, low resolution) across multiple inputs. No prior framework unifies degradation-aware restoration and multi-source fusion within a single, efficient diffusion process.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of degraded source images (infrared + visible, or PET + MRI) where each image may suffer from noise, blur, low resolution, or composite combinations of these degradations, and directly produces a single fused image without any separate pre-restoration step. The fusion weights W1 and W2 are learned data-driven per-pixel (with W1 + W2 = 1) rather than fixed, and the model operates with a limited number of DDIM sampling steps for efficient inference. Unlike standard diffusion models that require clean target images for training, the framework is trained in a fully unsupervised manner using only the degraded inputs and task-specific loss constraints.

## Method

The framework discards explicit noise prediction and instead directly regresses the fused image through a limited number of accelerated DDIM iterations, making the architecture closer to an end-to-end network while retaining the structured sampling trajectory of diffusion. At each step, a joint observation correction mechanism projects the intermediate estimate onto a constraint set defined by a block-matrix system that simultaneously encodes the degradation operators for both sources (y1 = A1·X1, y2 = A2·X2) and the fusion constraint (Xf = W1*X1 + W2*X2), using a derived block pseudoinverse (Eq. 15) that avoids explicit matrix inversion. The noise predictor is a multi-task network that outputs both the noise estimate and a per-pixel weight map W1, enabling learned fusion. For noisy observations, a timestep-dependent scaling factor Σ_t modulates the correction magnitude to reduce noise amplification, and composite degradations are handled by chaining pseudoinverse operators. Training uses standard unsupervised fusion losses (L1 reconstruction plus task-specific gradient or SSIM terms) rather than a noise-matching objective, allowing flexible adaptation to different fusion tasks.

## Results

On the M3FD infrared-visible fusion benchmark, the proposed method achieves the best Q_MI (0.3505), Q_NCIE (0.8052), and Q_W (0.7810) under noise, and dominates all six metrics under blur (e.g., Q_MI = 0.4477 vs. 0.3771 for the next-best RFfusion) and composite degradation (Q_MI = 0.3732, Q^{AB/F} = 0.2199, Q_W = 0.6237). On the Harvard PET-MRI dataset, it similarly leads on Q^{AB/F}, Q_P, and Q_W across all three degradation settings, with the largest margin under composite degradation (Q_W = 0.7344 vs. 0.6148 for Mask-DiFuser). The method also substantially outperforms all baselines on the most challenging composite scenario, where cascaded restoration-then-fusion pipelines degrade most severely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method is compared against three CNN-based baselines (IFCNN, U2Fusion, MURF) and five diffusion/flow-based baselines (DDFM, Text-DiFuse, VDMUFusion, RFfusion, Mask-DiFuser), all of which are first applied to restored images via a separate restoration step. Under the most challenging composite degradation on M3FD, the proposed method wins all six metrics, with the largest gaps on Q^{AB/F} (0.2199 vs. 0.1986 for Mask-DiFuser) and Q_W (0.6237 vs. 0.5845 for Mask-DiFuser). Under blur, the advantage is even more pronounced: Q_MI reaches 0.4477 versus 0.3771 for RFfusion (the next best), a ~19% relative improvement. Under noise, the method still leads on Q_MI (0.3505 vs. 0.3021 for RFfusion) and Q_W (0.7810 vs. 0.7778 for IFCNN), but trails U2Fusion on Q^{AB/F} (0.4083 vs. 0.4281) and Mask-DiFuser on Q_P (0.1825 vs. 0.2161). On the Harvard PET-MRI dataset, the method achieves the best Q^{AB/F}, Q_P, and Q_W in all three scenarios, with a particularly large Q_W margin under composite degradation (0.7344 vs. 0.6148 for Mask-DiFuser, ~19% relative gain). Overall, the unified degradation-aware diffusion framework consistently outperforms both the cascaded restoration-then-fusion baselines and prior diffusion-based fusion methods, with the largest advantages appearing under compound degradations where error accumulation in decoupled pipelines is most severe.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the direct image-regression formulation from invertible diffusion models for compressed sensing [Chen et al., 2025] to the multimodal fusion setting, discarding the explicit noise-prediction head that standard DDPM/DDIM pipelines require. The joint observation correction mechanism generalizes the DDNM null-space projection [Wang et al., 2022] from single-image restoration to a multi-input fusion problem by constructing a block-structured joint degradation matrix whose pseudoinverse can be computed implicitly. Conceptually, the work bridges the gap between fast but black-box end-to-end fusion networks (IFCNN, U2Fusion) and interpretable but slow diffusion-based fusion methods (DDFM, Mask-DiFuser), positioning itself as a middle ground that retains diffusion's structured sampling while approaching neural-network-level inference speed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical path toward deployment-ready degradation-aware fusion: by requiring only a handful of DDIM steps and operating without fused ground-truth labels, it removes two major barriers (computational cost and supervision) that have kept diffusion models out of real-world fusion pipelines. The joint observation model provides a clean, extensible mathematical template for incorporating arbitrary degradation operators (noise, blur, downscaling, or compositions thereof) into the sampling loop, which could be adopted by other generative fusion approaches. Open questions include whether the learned fusion weights generalize to unseen degradation combinations, how the method scales to more than two input modalities, and whether the efficiency gains persist at higher resolutions or with more complex architectures.
