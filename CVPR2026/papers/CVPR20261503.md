## Problem

Existing federated learning backdoor attacks ignore the heterogeneous contribution of individual model layers to attack success and induce conspicuous L1 distance differences between backdoor and clean models during early poisoning rounds. These distance signals are precisely what distance-based defenses (e.g., Multi-Krum, MultiMetric, AlignIns) exploit to filter malicious updates, leaving a gap: no systematic mechanism exists to localize poisoning to the most sensitive layers while suppressing inter-model distance to evade detection.

## Input / Output

The attacker receives the current server model (ResNet18 or EfficientNet) and must locally train both a clean and a backdoor copy for N epochs to compute per-layer sensitivity scores before the actual attack begins. During the attack rounds, the malicious client consumes the server parameters, its local backdoor dataset (with embedded triggers: 3×3/3×5 pixel patches for BadNet or a 5×5 learned block for A3FL), and produces a single set of poisoned model parameters to upload. The output is structured so that only a subset of layers (top-k by sensitivity, default 60 for ResNet18, 150 for EfficientNet) carry the backdoor signal, while all remaining layers are clipped to stay within τ=0.05 of the server values.

## Method

LaySelFL operates in three stages. First, Sensitive Layer Identification computes a per-layer sensitivity score S_l = S_d(l) + S_c(l), where S_d is the difference between average epoch-to-epoch L1 parameter changes of the clean and backdoor models (dynamic assessment) and S_c is the final L1 distance between converged clean and backdoor layer parameters (convergent assessment); the top-k layers by score are selected. Second, Targeted and Constrained Training partitions the sensitive layers into P disjoint subsets and, in each federated round r, trains only subset L_{r mod P} while freezing all other layers, using a loss that adds a β-weighted (default β=20) L2 penalty on the deviation of sensitive-layer parameters from the server model to keep each update small. Third, Target Clipping applies element-wise clipping to the parameter difference of every non-sensitive layer relative to the server model, capping each element at ±τ (default 0.05), thereby masking residual drift introduced by the attack and rendering the backdoor model nearly indistinguishable from a clean model under distance-based metrics.

## Results

On ResNet18 with the A3FL attack, LaySelFL restores the attack success rate (ASR) from near-zero to above 99% under distance-based defenses (Multi-Krum: 1.0%→99.8% on CIFAR-10; MultiMetric: 1.4%→76.2% on CIFAR-10, 1.3%→99.9% on FMNIST). Overall, LaySelFL increases attack effectiveness by 25% (evading 15 of 60 defense-attack combinations), reducing the defense success rate from 26.6% (basic attacks) to just 4% (LaySelFL). Clean accuracy (ACC) is preserved within 0.5–1.5% of the unpoisoned baseline across all three datasets (CIFAR-10, Fashion-MNIST, SVHN).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between the original (basic) backdoor attacks and LaySelFL-enhanced attacks under five SOTA FL defenses. The most dramatic gains are against distance-based defenses: Multi-Krum reduces basic A3FL ASR to 1.0% on CIFAR-10, while LaySelFL restores it to 99.8%; MultiMetric drops basic ASR to 1.4% on CIFAR-10, and LaySelFL recovers it to 76.2% (CIFAR-10) or 99.9% (FMNIST/SVHN). Against similarity-based defenses (Foolsgold, AlignIns, RFA), the basic attack already evades detection (ASR ≈ 100%), so LaySelFL's benefit is marginal—maintaining ASR in the 93–100% range while further reducing model distance. The one notable failure is RFA on SVHN, where LaySelFL's ASR drops to 0.7%, suggesting RFA's geometric-median aggregation remains partially effective in that specific setting. Overall, LaySelFL converts 15 previously detected attack-defense combinations into successful evasions, with the largest relative improvements (40–90%) on Multi-Krum and MultiMetric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of stealthy backdoor attacks in federated learning initiated by Cerberus [20] and adaptive layer-wise gradient alignment [35], both of which apply uniform constraints across all model layers to reduce detectable deviation. LaySelFL departs from this uniform-constraint paradigm by introducing a layer-sensitivity assessment (combining dynamic per-epoch L1 differences and final convergent parameter gaps) to identify the subset of layers most critical to backdoor success, then localizes poisoning exclusively to those layers while clipping the remainder. The constrained training loss (Eq. 7) and the element-wise clipping on non-sensitive layers are new mechanisms not present in prior stealthy attack formulations, and the overall pipeline is positioned as the first systematic layer-selective backdoor attack in FL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated 96% evasion rate across five SOTA defenses (Multi-Krum, MultiMetric, Foolsgold, AlignIns, RFA) exposes a concrete vulnerability in distance-based and direction-based detection, which are the dominant defense paradigms in FL. However, the attack's effectiveness degrades noticeably on EfficientNet (5–50% ASR drop, with some complete failures), suggesting that architectural redundancy matters and that the method is not universally transferable. Open questions include whether defenses can be redesigned to detect layer-localized poisoning patterns, whether the approach generalizes to non-CNN architectures or other FL paradigms (e.g., decentralized FL), and whether the sensitivity scores remain stable under adaptive or multi-round attack scenarios.
