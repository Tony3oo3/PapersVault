## Problem

Lightweight multi-zone ToF sensors (e.g., VL53L8CH) provide metrically calibrated depth at very low resolution (8×8), while monocular depth networks achieve high resolution but lack reliable absolute scale. Prior RGB-ToF fusion (DELTAR) approximated the sensor's compact normalized histogram (CNH) with a single Gaussian, discarding distributional detail, and still required too many parameters for edge deployment. The paper asks whether the full CNH signal can be exploited in a truly lightweight end-to-end network that recovers high-resolution metric depth.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three synchronized inputs: a 416×416 RGB image, an 8×8 ToF distance grid (upsampled and concatenated as a fourth channel to form RGB-D), and 64 zone-wise CNH histograms (18 bins each, spanning 0–5.4 m in 0.3 m steps). It outputs a single dense metric depth map at full input resolution, valid only within the spatial region jointly covered by the camera and ToF sensor FOV. The CNH is consumed as a separate 1-D distribution per zone rather than being merged into the spatial depth map, preserving its physical interpretability.

## Method

LiteSense uses a U-Net-style encoder-decoder built from MobileNetV4 spatial blocks (4 downsampling stages at 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16) to process the RGB-D input, while an MLP encoder (no downsampling) independently encodes the 64 CNH distributions. The key architectural contribution is the Patch-wise CNH Spatial Injection (PCSI) module: both the RGB-D feature map and CNH embeddings are partitioned into N×N non-overlapping patches, and each RGB-D patch is modulated by its spatially aligned CNH patch via cross-attention (RGB-D as Query, CNH as Key/Value), preventing cross-region interference that global attention would introduce. PCSI is applied only at the 1/2 and 1/4 decoder scales to keep patch sizes large enough for meaningful local structure and to avoid tiling artifacts at finer resolutions. Training uses a combined SILog + Gradient MAE loss under supervised learning on NYUv2 (24K samples), yielding a 5.48 M-parameter model at 33.87 GFLOPs.

## Results

On NYUv2, LiteSense achieves δ₁=0.982, RMSE=0.197 m, and AbsRel=0.029 with only 5.48 M parameters and 33.87 G FLOPs, outperforming the closest auxiliary-guided baseline DELTAR (δ₁=0.952, AbsRel=0.064) by 3.1% in δ₁ and 55% in AbsRel. In zero-shot evaluation on SUN RGB-D, LiteSense attains δ₁=0.980 and AbsRel=0.032, surpassing DELTAR (δ₁=0.950, AbsRel=0.063) and all monocular baselines. On the real-sensor THDR3K dataset, the fine-tuned LiteSense reaches δ₁=0.914 and AbsRel=0.091, matching or exceeding Intel RealSense depth quality in mid-range distances.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiteSense outperforms all monocular MMDE baselines (AdaBins, UniDepth, ZoeDepth, Metric3D-V2-L, DA-V2-L/S) on every metric while using 4–60× fewer parameters; the gap to the SOTA HybridDepth is small (δ₁: 0.982 vs. 0.989, AbsRel: 0.029 vs. 0.026) but LiteSense uses only 9% of its parameters and 9% of its FLOPs. Against the closest auxiliary-guided competitor DELTAR, LiteSense improves δ₁ from 0.952 to 0.982 (+3.1%), reduces AbsRel from 0.064 to 0.029 (−55%), and cuts parameters by 70% (18.55 M → 5.48 M). PriorDA-S, which uses LiDAR prompts with a large pretrained backbone, is substantially outperformed (δ₁: 0.843 vs. 0.982), confirming that lightweight ToF-CNH fusion is more effective than sparse LiDAR prompting for this task. In zero-shot transfer to SUN RGB-D, LiteSense leads all baselines by a wide margin (AbsRel 0.032 vs. 0.063 for DELTAR and 0.105 for ZoeDepth), demonstrating that the CNH-guided fusion generalizes better than both pure monocular and prior ToF-fusion approaches. On the real-sensor THDR3K dataset, LiteSense-Real (AbsRel 0.091) slightly trails DELTAR (0.088) but surpasses all monocular methods, and LiteSense-Sim (0.054) clearly leads, indicating the fusion strategy is robust under domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

LiteSense directly extends DELTAR (ECCV 2022), the first work to fuse a lightweight multi-zone ToF sensor with RGB for metric depth estimation, by replacing DELTAR's Gaussian-sampling approximation of the depth distribution with the sensor's native Compact Normalized Histogram (CNH) signal. The PCSI module draws conceptual inspiration from tile-based super-resolution frameworks (PatchFusion, PatchRefiner) to enforce spatially localized cross-attention between RGB-D and CNH features, avoiding the cross-region interference of global attention. The overall architecture sits within the broader auxiliary-guided metric depth estimation lineage (LiDAR-prompted Depth Anything, PriorDA) but targets a much lighter sensor and a fully end-to-end lightweight network built on MobileNetV4 blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of CNH as a depth-estimation input and the THDR3K dataset (first indoor RGB-ToF-CNH collection) open a practical path toward RealSense-quality metric depth from a fraction of the hardware cost, which is relevant for edge robotics and AR/VR. However, the evaluation is confined to indoor scenes and a single ToF sensor model (VL53L8CH), and the RealSense comparison relies on a narrow wall-plane protocol; generalization to outdoor, dynamic, or multi-sensor settings remains untested. An open question is whether the CNH-based PCSI strategy transfers to other histogram-emitting sensors or to higher-resolution ToF grids, and whether the simulated-to-real gap (visible in THDR3K results) can be closed without per-scene fine-tuning.
