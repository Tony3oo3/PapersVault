## Problem

Existing training-free 3D editing methods, particularly inversion-free approaches like FlowEdit, rely on stochastic Gaussian noise as latent anchors at every timestep. Because 3D flow models are highly sensitive to noise perturbations, these timestep-wise anchors drift unpredictably, causing flow directions to cancel out and producing insufficient semantic modifications or geometric distortions. The paper identifies this latent anchor inconsistency as the fundamental limitation preventing strong, structurally stable edits.

## Input / Output

The method takes a source 3D mesh (encoded into a latent code via the Hunyuan3D 2.1 3D VAE) and a free-form text editing instruction. It renders 8 image views of the source mesh, uses Gemini-2.5-Flash to select the most instruction-aligned view as the source condition, and an image editing model to generate the target condition image. The output is an edited 3D mesh decoded from the latent space, requiring no mask annotation or model finetuning. The entire pipeline operates in the vecset latent space of the base flow model.

## Method

AnchorFlow introduces a global latent anchor shared by both source and target trajectories, replacing the per-timestep stochastic noise anchors of inversion-free editing. It defines a relaxed anchor-alignment loss L_align that minimizes the squared distance between single-step inversions F_t(X_t^tar) and F_t(X_t^src), where F_t is approximated as X_t + (1-t)·v_θ(X_t, t, c). The editing update replaces the conventional velocity-difference step with a gradient descent step on L_align with respect to the editing state, using a Jacobian-free approximation (following DreamFusion) that replaces the Jacobian transpose with a scalar (2-t)·I. This yields the anchor-aligned update rule X_{t-δt}^{FE} = X_t^{FE} − δ_t·(2−t)·(F_t(X_t^tar) − F_t(X_t^src)), which progressively transforms the source toward the target while maintaining a consistent latent reference throughout the denoising process. The method is fully training-free and mask-free, operating entirely within the pretrained flow model's latent space.

## Results

On the Eval3DEdit benchmark (100 samples across 5 editing categories), AnchorFlow achieves the best overall performance with CLIP_img = 0.7173 (identity preservation) and CLIP_txt = 0.4866 (semantic modification). Compared to the closest baseline, Inversion-free Editing (FlowEdit), AnchorFlow improves CLIP_img by 0.0067 and CLIP_txt by 0.0161. The method also matches the runtime of FlowEdit (26.71 s vs. 25.77 s) while delivering higher editing quality, and substantially outperforms LRM-based methods (e.g., MVEdit: 0.5074 CLIP_img) and optimization-based methods (e.g., TextDeformer: 0.5074 CLIP_img).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AnchorFlow outperforms all seven baselines on both CLIP_img and CLIP_txt across the overall evaluation. Against the two closest LFM-based competitors, it beats Inversion-free Editing (FlowEdit [27]) by +0.0067 CLIP_img and +0.0161 CLIP_txt, and Editing-by-Inversion (UniEdit-Flow [22]) by +0.0054 CLIP_img and +0.0129 CLIP_txt. The gap is most pronounced on Action Change, where AnchorFlow reaches 0.7313/0.5085 versus 0.7096/0.4710 for FlowEdit and 0.7109/0.4742 for Editing-by-Inversion, reflecting stronger pose/articulation edits. Direct Editing [53] (using the edited image as a single-view input to Hunyuan3D 2.1) lags by ~0.10 in CLIP_img, confirming that 2D-to-3D generation loses identity information. LRM-based methods (MVEdit [6], EditP23 [1]) and optimization-based methods (TextDeformer [15], MeshUp [26]) trail by 0.15–0.25 in CLIP_img, attributed to cross-view inconsistencies and unstable SDS gradients respectively. Notably, AnchorFlow achieves these gains without requiring 3D masks or model finetuning, and at comparable runtime to FlowEdit (26.71 s vs. 25.77 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

AnchorFlow extends the inversion-free editing paradigm introduced by FlowEdit (Kulikov et al., ICCV 2025) to 3D vecset-based large foundation models (LFMs) such as Hunyuan3D 2.1. The key departure is a diagnosis that FlowEdit's timestep-wise stochastic Gaussian noise acts as inconsistent latent anchors in 3D, causing velocity cancellation and under-editing; AnchorFlow replaces this with a single global latent anchor enforced via a relaxed alignment loss and a Jacobian-free gradient update. It also builds on the broader LFM-based editing line (direct editing, editing-by-inversion via UniEdit-Flow) and positions itself against LRM-based and SDS-based 3D editing methods that rely on multi-view diffusion or score distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A training-free, mask-free editing pipeline on top of an off-the-shelf LFM lowers the barrier to scalable 3D content curation and pairwise editing-data generation, which is practically valuable for downstream fine-tuning. The latent-anchor-consistency principle is a clean, generalizable diagnostic that could inform other inversion-free or flow-based editing settings beyond 3D. However, the method is bounded by the 3D VAE reconstruction fidelity (high-frequency detail loss), the Eval3DEdit benchmark is small (100 samples), and the quantitative gains over FlowEdit are modest, leaving open whether the anchor-alignment mechanism yields larger benefits as base LFM quality improves or on more diverse editing distributions.
