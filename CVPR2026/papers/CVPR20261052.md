## Problem

Chart recognition VLMs trained via standard SFT lack explicit spatial grounding between textual phrases and their corresponding chart regions, limiting fine-grained understanding of chart elements. While phrase grounding has been shown to improve object detection and captioning, it remains underexplored for chart recognition due to the absence of phrase-to-region alignment datasets and a mechanism to incorporate such grounding during fine-tuning without costly additional pretraining of vision-language connectors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261052/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a chart image and a question text as input and produces a text answer as output. During training, it additionally consumes automatically generated phrase-to-mask alignments: text-region masks extracted via Hi-SAM segmentation and phrase-to-region correspondences produced by GPT-o4-mini using alpha-blended visual tags. At inference time, all mask annotations and the SMAM module are entirely removed, so the model operates as the original pretrained VLM with no architectural changes or additional inputs.

## Method

The core contribution is the Side-Masked Attention Module (SMAM), inserted alongside each transformer attention block in the LLM. SMAM reuses the existing attention weights, value states, and inverse-embedding matrix (introducing no new parameters) to perform Mask2Former-style masked attention restricted to language tokens and vision patches within the annotation mask, producing an additional per-token logit. Training uses two auxiliary losses: a cross-entropy term (L_CE+) that directly supervises the mask-region logit, and a DPO-style relative constraint (L_G) that encourages the model's native output probability to exceed the mask-region probability, thereby grounding predictions in phrase-relevant visual evidence while still leveraging broader image context. An automatic data generation pipeline augments existing chart datasets by extracting text-region masks with Hi-SAM and using GPT-o4-mini's visual prompting to align each mask with its corresponding phrase in the QA text, requiring no manual annotation.

## Results

On ChartQA (Relaxed Accuracy), the proposed method achieves 86.5% average (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and 51.9% (LLaVA-7B), outperforming standard SFT by 0.9 and 1.7 points respectively. On Chart-to-table (F1), it reaches 94.2% (Qwen7B) and 74.1% (LLaVA), surpassing SFT by 0.9 and 1.7 points. Gains are most pronounced on the Human split (e.g., +1.9 on ChartQA Hum. for Qwen7B, +2.1 on C2T Hum. for LLaVA), while the Augmented split shows smaller improvements due to near-saturation. The method also improves specialized chart VLMs: UniChart gains +1.5 on C2T and +1.5 on ChartQA, and ChartInstruct gains +2.0 and +1.9 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The segmentation-based baseline (Seg., adapted from GLaMM) performs substantially worse than all other methods, dropping to 18.9 F1 on C2T and 41.0 RA on ChartQA for LLaVA, because the additional SAM decoder is difficult to optimize with only ~25k SFT samples and faces a domain gap to chart imagery. The text-localization baseline (TL., reproducing the bounding-box prediction format from mPLUG-DocOwl and RefChartQA) is competitive on ChartQA (50.9 vs. 50.2 for LLaVA) but degrades sharply on C2T (36.0 vs. 72.4), where the model must localize multiple phrases simultaneously alongside table structure. The proposed SMAM method consistently outperforms both baselines and standard SFT across all five models and both tasks, with the largest margins on the Human split (e.g., +2.1 C2T F1 and +2.1 ChartQA RA for LLaVA; +1.9 and +0.9 for Qwen7B). On QA-CoT (Table 3), the same pattern holds: Ours achieves 58.4/87.3/89.2 average RA for LLaVA/Qwen3B/Qwen7B, beating TL. by 2.5/3.1/2.1 points, confirming that the advantage persists when intermediate reasoning traces are included.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Ferrando et al.'s (ACL 2023) logit-contribution analysis from text-only transformers to VLMs by treating vision patch tokens analogously to text tokens, and repurposes Mask2Former's masked-attention mechanism as a training-only side module (SMAM) that reuses the base LLM's existing attention weights, value states, and inverse-embedding matrix without introducing new parameters. The grounding loss borrows the sigmoid-based pairwise form from DPO but applies it to compare two computation paths (native vs. mask-restricted) for the same token rather than two output sequences. Conceptually, the work bridges the object-detection literature—where joint phrase grounding and generation have shown mutual benefit—with chart recognition, where prior efforts have focused on visual-encoder improvements or textual enrichment (CoT, table pretraining) rather than explicit spatial supervision during SFT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practically appealing recipe: phrase grounding can be injected into any pretrained VLM's fine-tuning stage with zero architectural change at inference and no additional pretraining, which is valuable in domain-specific settings with limited data. However, the gains are modest (typically 1–2 points on ChartQA and QA-CoT), the training data is small (~25k samples, single epoch for Qwen), and the automatic alignment pipeline depends on GPT's visual prompting, which may not generalize to chart types with heavy non-textual elements. Open questions include whether the SMAM mechanism transfers to other visual-reasoning domains beyond charts, whether gains compound with larger training corpora, and whether the grounding loss form is optimal compared to alternatives like contrastive or KL-based objectives.
