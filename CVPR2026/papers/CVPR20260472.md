## Problem

3D Vision Foundation Models like VGGT can predict camera poses and 3D structure from uncalibrated images in a single forward pass, but their feature spaces lack explicit multi-view geometric consistency, resulting in substantially lower novel view synthesis quality compared to optimization-based methods. The paper asks whether a VFM's own outputs can be leveraged as a self-supervised signal to learn a geometrically aligned feature space that bridges this gap without any 3D ground-truth annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260472/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of unposed, uncalibrated RGB images (6–36 frames at 294×518 resolution) and produces novel view renderings via feed-forward 3D Gaussian splatting, along with refined camera poses and geometrically aligned 24-channel feature maps at full image resolution. VGGT's predicted per-frame depth maps and relative camera poses serve as pseudo-ground-truth during training, while at inference the pipeline is fully self-contained: VGGT provides initial geometry, the aligned features drive both Gaussian prediction and bundle adjustment, and the depth-shift correction propagates BA pose updates into the dense Gaussian representation.

## Method

Selfi freezes a pre-trained VGGT backbone and trains a lightweight DPT adapter via a reprojection-based feature consistency loss: query pixels are unprojected using VGGT's pseudo-depth, reprojected into target views, and the adapter is optimized so that features at corresponding 3D locations exhibit high cosine similarity (aggregated via a temperature-scaled softmax weighted average over all target pixels). A separate U-Net decoder, trained on the frozen aligned features concatenated with the source image, predicts per-pixel 3D Gaussian parameters including quaternions, scales, RGB spherical harmonics, and—critically—density spherical harmonics that act as a learned view-dependent confidence score to suppress unreliable Gaussians. A final bundle adjustment step uses the aligned features for robust 2D-2D correspondences to refine VGGT's initial poses, and an affine depth-shift correction (estimated from sparse BA point displacements) is applied to all Gaussian centers and scales to maintain geometric consistency after the pose update, yielding further NVS improvements.

## Results

On the average-overlap setting (Table 2), Selfi achieves 22.30 PSNR / 0.7929 SSIM / 0.2019 LPIPS on DL3DV and 24.47 PSNR / 0.8810 SSIM / 0.1402 LPIPS on RealEstate10K, outperforming all pose-free feed-forward baselines (AnySplat, Flare, WorldMirror) by 4–7 dB in PSNR. In the two-view evaluation (Table 3), Selfi attains 29.01 PSNR, 0.942 SSIM, and 0.053 LPIPS, achieving the best SSIM and LPIPS among all methods (both pose-required and pose-free). For pose estimation (Table 5), Selfi's feature-based BA improves AUC@3 from 0.7900 (VGGT alone) to 0.8670 on 10-frame inputs, and remains robust at 100 frames where Co-Tracker fails with out-of-memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Selfi decisively outperforms all pose-free feed-forward Gaussian methods: on DL3DV (average overlap), it beats AnySplat by 7.36 dB PSNR (22.30 vs 14.94), Flare by 6.28 dB (22.30 vs 16.02), and WorldMirror by 4.38 dB (22.30 vs 17.92); on RealEstate10K the margins are 3.81 dB over AnySplat, 2.46 dB over Flare, and 1.30 dB over WorldMirror. Against the per-scene 3DGS upper bound (which uses ground-truth camera poses and SfM point initialization), Selfi is within 1.29 dB on DL3DV and 0.41 dB on RealEstate10K in the average setting, and actually matches or exceeds 3DGS in the dense-overlap setting on both datasets. In the two-view evaluation (Table 3), Selfi achieves the best SSIM (0.942) and LPIPS (0.053) among all 14 compared methods, while its PSNR (29.01) is slightly below ReSplat (29.72) and LVSM dec-only (29.67) but well above all other pose-free methods (Flare 26.91, NoPoSplat 26.82). For pose estimation, Selfi's feature-based BA improves AUC@3 over VGGT alone by 7.7 points (0.8670 vs 0.7900) and over VGGT+CoTracker BA by 3.2 points (0.8670 vs 0.8350) on 10-frame inputs, and is the only method that scales to 100 frames without memory failure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on VGGT as a frozen 3D Vision Foundation Model backbone and 3D Gaussian Splatting as the rendering primitive, but departs from prior feed-forward NVS methods (AnySplat, WorldMirror, NoPoSplat) that directly decode VFM features into Gaussians without enforcing multi-view geometric consistency. The key conceptual departure is a self-supervised feature alignment stage that uses VGGT's own depth and pose predictions as pseudo-ground-truth to train a lightweight DPT adapter, creating a geometrically aligned feature space before any Gaussian prediction. The method also connects to classical bundle adjustment by using the aligned features for correspondence matching, closing a self-improving loop where refined poses feed back into the NVS pipeline with a depth-shift correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that VFM features, despite being powerful for geometric prediction, lack the explicit multi-view consistency needed for high-fidelity NVS is a useful diagnostic for the field, and the reprojection-based alignment loss offers a simple, annotation-free remedy. The density spherical harmonics as a learned view-dependent confidence metric is a practical design choice that addresses pose/depth noise without per-scene optimization. Limitations include reliance on VGGT's pseudo-GT quality (errors propagate), performance degradation with longer sequences (though less severe than baselines), and evaluation limited to two large-scale datasets plus two conventional benchmarks. Open questions include whether the self-improving alignment paradigm generalizes to other VFMs or to dynamic scenes, and whether the pseudo-GT bottleneck can be further reduced.
