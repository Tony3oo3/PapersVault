## Problem

Existing image personalization methods rely on holistic embeddings from general-purpose encoders (CLIP, DINOv2, VAEs) that compress all visual information into a single representation, causing irrelevant attributes like lighting and clothing to leak into generated images as 'copy-and-paste' artifacts. No prior method learns attribute-specific, disentangled representations that can be specified in open-vocabulary text form, making it impossible to selectively transfer only a user-chosen attribute (e.g., identity without pose or background) into a new context.

## Input / Output

The encoder jointly consumes a reference RGB image and a free-form textual attribute description (e.g., 'hairstyle', 'lighting', 'expression') through a LoRA-tuned MLLM, producing a sequence of l attribute-specific tokens. These embeddings condition a frozen FLUX diffusion generator via trainable IP-Adapter cross-attention layers to synthesize a personalized image in a new text-specified context. For compositional generation, multiple image-attribute pairs are encoded independently and their conditional flow fields are linearly combined with per-source weights to produce a single coherent output image.

## Method

Omni-Attribute introduces a dual-objective training paradigm on semantically linked image pairs annotated with positive attributes (shared semantics) and negative attributes (differing characteristics), generated via a two-stage MLLM pipeline (72B teacher with chain-of-thought prompting, then a 32B student for scalable annotation). The generative loss reconstructs the target image from the reference image's attribute embeddings plus a text prompt, forcing the encoder to capture fine-grained attribute details; the contrastive loss simultaneously attracts positive-attribute embedding pairs and repels negative-attribute pairs, driving disentanglement. The encoder is a LoRA-tuned Qwen-VL backbone followed by a lightweight trainable connector (8 self-attention + 1 linear layers), preserving the pretrained vision-language prior while adapting to attribute-level representation. For composition, the paper extends composable diffusion to the flow-matching setting by linearly combining per-attribute conditional flow fields, enabling seamless integration of multiple attribute embeddings from different source images.

## Results

On the open-vocabulary attribute personalization benchmark (375 samples across 15 attributes), Omni-Attribute achieves an average conditioning fidelity of 0.789 (text fidelity 0.896, attribute fidelity 0.641, image naturalness 0.831), evaluated by both GPT-4o and a 10-participant user study (11.25K ratings). The learned embeddings also show a cosine-similarity gap of 0.608 between positive and negative attribute pairs on 1K validation pairs, confirming effective disentanglement. Results are reported separately for concrete objects and abstract concepts, with the largest gains observed on abstract concepts where baselines degrade most.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two groups of baselines: (1) encoder-based methods using CLIP, DINOv2, and Qwen-VL embeddings fed into IP-Adapter, and (2) image-editing models (OmniGen2, FLUX-Kontext, Qwen-Image-Edit) prompted to preserve a specified attribute. CLIP and DINOv2, which accept only the reference image without an attribute text input, struggle most on abstract concepts (e.g., lighting, expression, art style) because they cannot condition on which attribute to isolate. Qwen-VL does accept a multimodal prompt with the attribute description but, lacking the contrastive disentanglement objective, still produces entangled embeddings with noticeable leakage. The editing models generate outputs that more closely resemble the reference image overall but fail to disentangle the target attribute, resulting in prominent 'copy-and-paste' artifacts and weaker text-prompt alignment. Omni-Attribute outperforms all six baselines on both MLLM and human evaluations across concrete and abstract attributes, achieving the best balance between attribute fidelity, text fidelity, and image naturalness, with the margin being largest on abstract concepts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant encoder-based personalization paradigm (IP-Adapter, CLIP/DINOv2 embeddings) by moving attribute disentanglement to the encoder side: instead of feeding a holistic image embedding into a frozen generator, it trains a LoRA-tuned MLLM (Qwen-VL) to produce attribute-specific representations conditioned on a textual attribute description. The composition mechanism directly adapts Composable Diffusion's linear combination of conditional flow fields to a flow-matching generator, and the contrastive disentanglement objective is conceptually related to OADis and DeCLIP but extended to an open-vocabulary setting via the novel positive/negative attribute annotation scheme. The dual-objective training (generative fidelity + contrastive separation) is a new formulation that has no direct precedent in the personalization literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing the first open-vocabulary attribute encoder, this work offers a practical path toward fine-grained, user-controllable personalization without test-time optimization, directly addressing the 'copy-and-paste' leakage that limits current IP-Adapter-style methods. The composability of embeddings through linear flow-field combination opens the door to multi-attribute scene assembly from heterogeneous references. Key open questions include scalability of the annotation pipeline (which relies on MLLM-generated positive/negative attribute pairs), whether the approach transfers to video or 3D generation, and how performance degrades when the target attribute is not well-described by a short text phrase. The reliance on curated semantically-linked image pairs also raises questions about data efficiency and generalization to truly novel attribute combinations.
