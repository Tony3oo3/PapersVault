## Problem

Global visual geo-localization requires mapping an image to a location anywhere on Earth, but learning expressive geographic representations remains difficult because GPS coordinates are inherently low-dimensional, forcing prior methods to rely on specialized components (e.g., Random Fourier Features in GeoCLIP) or to sacrifice spatial resolution for global coverage. Existing approaches are split between retrieval-based methods (expensive large-scale similarity search) and classification-based methods (discrete geocell labels), and neither paradigm adequately captures the multi-scale, relational structure of geographic space.

## Input / Output

GeoSURGE takes a single RGB image as input and additionally derives a semantic segmentation map (ADE20K classes) via OneFormer, which is fused with CLIP ViT-Large-Patch14-336 appearance features. The output is a geographic location predicted through hierarchical inference over 7 levels of S2 geocell partitions: the predicted location is the geocell in the finest partition whose product of softmax-normalized similarity probabilities across all containing parent geocells is highest. Evaluation uses great-circle distance thresholds (1 km to 2500 km) corresponding to street, city, region, country, and continent levels.

## Method

GeoSURGE formulates geo-localization as matching a query image's visual features against a learned hierarchy of geographic embeddings. The Earth is partitioned into S2 geocells at 7 spatial resolutions (τ_max from 25000 down to 500, τ_min=50), and each geocell is assigned a learnable 768-dimensional embedding, forming a distributed multi-scale geographic representation rather than discrete class labels. On the visual side, a semantic fusion module uses latent cross-attention (3 blocks, latent dim 64) where OneFormer-derived semantic tokens act as queries and CLIP ViT-Large RGB tokens act as keys and values, producing enriched fused tokens that capture both fine-grained appearance and invariant scene structure while suppressing unreliable regions like people and vehicles. Training uses InfoNCE contrastive loss to align each image's fused CLS token with its corresponding geocell embedding at every partition level, with each level's embeddings learned independently to promote diversity across scales. At inference, cosine similarities between the query visual feature and all geocell embeddings are computed at each hierarchy level, normalized via softmax, and integrated by multiplying probabilities across parent geocells to yield a single hierarchical location prediction.

## Results

GeoSURGE achieves state-of-the-art GCD accuracy on 22 of 25 metrics across five benchmarks (IM2GPS, IM2GPS3k, YFCC4k, YFCC26k, GWS15k), evaluated at five distance thresholds (1 km, 25 km, 200 km, 750 km, 2500 km). On IM2GPS, GeoSURGE reaches 27.0% (1 km), 54.4% (25 km), 70.0% (200 km), 84.4% (750 km), and 93.2% (2500 km), surpassing the previous best non-LVLM method GeoDecoder (22.1/50.2/69.0/80.0/89.1) by 4.9–4.5 percentage points. Excluding LVLM-based methods (Img2Loc, G3), GeoSURGE outperforms all baselines on all 25 metrics. The three losses are at fine-grained thresholds (1 km and 25 km on YFCC4k, 25 km on IM2GPS3k) where GPT-4V-based methods leverage memorized landmark cues.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GeoCLIP (same CLIP-ViT-L/14@336 backbone, but GPS-coordinate embeddings with Random Fourier Features), GeoSURGE wins on every comparable metric across all five datasets, e.g., 27.0% vs 16.5% at 1 km and 93.2% vs 88.6% at 2500 km on IM2GPS, confirming that learned geocell embeddings outperform low-dimensional GPS representations. Against the LVLM-based methods Img2Loc/GPT-4V and G3/GPT-4V (reported on IM2GPS3k and YFCC4k), GeoSURGE wins 7 of 10 metrics; the losses are at 25 km on IM2GPS3k (42.5 vs 45.1 for Img2Loc) and at 1 km / 25 km on YFCC4k (19.9/33.6 vs 24.0/35.9 for G3), which the authors attribute to LVLMs memorizing fine-grained landmark cues from Internet-scale data. Against PIGEOTTO, GeoSURGE leads on 4 of 5 IM2GPS metrics (e.g., 27.0 vs 11.8 at 1 km) and on all 5 YFCC26k metrics (17.8 vs 10.1 at 1 km). Against GeoDecoder, the strongest prior non-LVLM classifier, GeoSURGE gains 4.9 pp at 1 km and 4.1 pp at 2500 km on IM2GPS. Against Translocator and ISNs, GeoSURGE improves by roughly 7–10 pp at street level and 3–5 pp at continent level, demonstrating that the hierarchical geographic embedding and semantic fusion jointly drive the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoSURGE bridges the two dominant paradigms in global visual geo-localization—retrieval-based (GeoCLIP, Img2Loc) and classification-based (PlaNet, GeoDecoder)—by learning distributed feature embeddings for S2 geocells in a hierarchical partition and formulating localization as a contrastive matching problem between visual and geographic representations. It builds directly on GeoCLIP's CLIP-style contrastive training but replaces low-dimensional GPS coordinate embeddings (which required Random Fourier Features) with learned multi-scale geocell embeddings, and on CLIP's ViT backbone for appearance features. The semantic fusion module, which uses latent cross-attention to integrate OneFormer segmentation tokens with CLIP patch tokens, draws on the latent attention mechanism from DeepSeek-R1 and repurposes semantic segmentation as a structural query signal rather than a parallel classification head.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoSURGE demonstrates that explicit, structured geographic modeling (hierarchical geocell embeddings) remains essential for robust global geo-localization even as LVLM-based methods (GPT-4V, Qwen-VL) achieve competitive results, particularly at street and city granularity where internet-scale memorization of landmarks provides an edge. The ablations cleanly isolate that both the hierarchy depth and the learned embeddings are necessary—neither alone recovers full performance—giving practitioners a clear design recipe. Open questions include how to efficiently combine the latent world knowledge of LVLMs with structured geographic representations, and whether the assumed availability of semantic segmentation (requiring a separate OneFormer pass) limits deployment in latency-sensitive or resource-constrained settings.
