## Problem

Current MLLMs are limited to single-image spatial understanding, restricting their perception to a static field-of-view without dynamic or multi-view information. This makes them ill-suited for physical-world applications like robotics and autonomous driving that require reasoning across multiple frames. The paper addresses this gap by equipping MLLMs with multi-frame spatial understanding spanning depth perception, visual correspondence, and dynamic (camera/object) motion perception.

## Input / Output

The model consumes multiple RGB images (pairs or sets of 2+ views) alongside text prompts that reference specific pixels via visual dots, normalized pixel coordinates (scaled to 0–1000 via floor(x/W×1000)), or semantic labels. Outputs span a wide range of formats: qualitative text, multiple-choice selections, scalar values in millimeters, 2D pixel coordinates, and 3D displacement vectors. A regular expression extracts answers from free-form model responses, and correctness for scalar/vector outputs is judged by a 20% relative L2 tolerance, while pixel coordinates require within-5%-image-width accuracy.

## Method

The core contribution is a data generation pipeline that leverages annotated 3D/4D scene datasets (ScanNet, Aria Digital Twin, Panoptic Studio, TAPVid3D) to produce 27M+ high-quality QA samples from 1.1M images. The pipeline samples image pairs with a uniform overlap distribution (6%–35% IoU of visible points via binned sampling), backprojects point clouds to establish pixel correspondences, and uses GPT-4o-generated templates to create diverse QA pairs across five spatial tasks. Unlike prior work relying on noisy monocular depth estimators or object masks from in-the-wild images, this approach uses ground-truth 3D geometry for both referencing and answers. Training employs LoRA (rank 16) on the InternVL2-8B LLM backbone while freezing the vision encoder and projection layer, using 3M QA samples mixed with 60K general instruction-following data for one epoch. The model supports multiple referencing modalities (dots, coordinates, semantic labels) and output types (qualitative, MCQ, scalar, coordinate, vector), broadening applicability beyond the single referencing/output format used in prior spatial datasets.

## Results

On the MultiSPA benchmark, Multi-SpatialMLLM (8B) achieves 56.11% average accuracy, a +35.68% gain over the InternVL-8B base model (20.43%) and substantially outperforming all proprietary models (Claude-3.5: 27.50%, Gemini-2.0: 30.31%, GPT-4o: 28.87%). On qualitative subtasks (depth comparison, camera direction), the model reaches 74–91% accuracy, while on the most challenging quantitative tasks (camera displacement vector, object movement vector) it achieves 18.00% and 12.92% respectively, where all baselines remain near 0%. On the held-out BLINK benchmark, Multi-SpatialMLLM scores 84.3% average, surpassing Gemini-2.0 (75.7%) and GPT-4o (73.8%), with near-90% accuracy on Visual Correspondence and Multi-View Reasoning splits where all baselines fail.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Multi-SpatialMLLM (8B) decisively outperforms all five baseline groups on the MultiSPA benchmark. Against the base models InternVL-8B and InternVL-26B, it gains +35.68% on average, with the largest margins on quantitative tasks (e.g., depth value: 75.33% vs. 3.34%; camera vector: 18.00% vs. 0.33%). SpatialRGPT, despite being trained on 1M single-image spatial samples, scores only 16.24%—worse than zero-shot InternVL-8B—confirming that single-image spatial supervision does not transfer to multi-frame tasks. Against proprietary models, Multi-SpatialMLLM leads by a wide margin: +28.61% over Claude-3.5 (27.50%), +25.80% over Gemini-2.0 (30.31%), and +27.24% over GPT-4o (28.87%), despite having far fewer parameters. On the external BLINK benchmark, the 8B model also surpasses all proprietary systems (84.3% vs. 75.7% for Gemini-2.0), particularly on Multi-View Reasoning (94.7% vs. 42.9–54.1%) and Visual Correspondence (89.5% vs. 74.4–88.4%). The only task where proprietary models remain competitive is Object Size perception, where Claude-3.5 (46.11%) is close to Multi-SpatialMLLM (49.11%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the spatial understanding line of work initiated by SpatialVLM and SpatialRGPT, which demonstrated that fine-tuning MLLMs on curated spatial data improves single-image spatial reasoning. The key departure is the shift from single-frame to multi-frame spatial understanding, drawing on Structure-from-Motion principles from 3D computer vision to define depth perception, visual correspondence, and dynamic perception as complementary capabilities. Unlike concurrent work SAT, which relies on simulated data, Multi-SpatialMLLM is trained on real-world 3D/4D scene datasets (ScanNet, TAPVid3D, ADT, PStudio) with a data generation pipeline that produces spatially and temporally aligned QA pairs at 27M scale. The model is built on InternVL2 with LoRA fine-tuning, preserving the base architecture's general capabilities while injecting multi-frame spatial skills.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first large-scale dataset and benchmark dedicated to multi-frame spatial understanding, filling a critical gap for embodied AI applications (robotics, autonomous driving) that require reasoning across multiple viewpoints. The finding that an 8B model fine-tuned on MultiSPA matches or exceeds proprietary systems (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0, Claude-3.5) on spatial tasks suggests that targeted spatial data is more effective than raw model scale for this capability. The emergent behavior observation—that only the 26B variant can learn from hard visual correspondence samples—hints at a capacity threshold for multi-frame spatial reasoning, though the evidence is limited to one task and three model sizes. Open questions include whether the 27M dataset can be further scaled, whether the approach generalizes to video-length sequences, and whether the multi-frame reward annotation capability can be deployed in closed-loop robot learning.
