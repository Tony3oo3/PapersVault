## Problem

Existing video token compression methods for multimodal LLMs predominantly rely on attention-based importance scoring, which introduces cross-frame redundancy, overlooks fine-grained details under aggressive compression, and requires numerous architecture-specific hyperparameters. The paper argues that the fundamental principle of compression should be information uniqueness rather than attention saliency, and provides a missing information-theoretic formulation linking token uniqueness to reconstruction error.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263585/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-frame visual token features extracted from a ViT encoder (e.g., 196 tokens per frame for LLaVA-OneVision, 256 for Eagle2.5) and produces a compressed set of visual tokens—some retained, some fused with neighbors—totaling a user-specified token budget (e.g., 6,272 tokens for 32 frames). It is entirely training-free and plug-and-play, requiring only two hyperparameters (frame uniqueness threshold U_f and token uniqueness threshold U_c) that transfer across different ViT-LLM backbones without re-tuning. A newline token is appended after each frame to preserve inter-frame boundaries in the LLM input.

## Method

UniComp formulates video compression as minimizing conditional entropy H(X|S) between the full token set and a retained subset, then derives an upper bound showing reconstruction error is bounded by the minimum uniqueness (1 − cosine similarity) between each discarded token and its nearest retained neighbor. Three modules operate under this unified principle: Frame Group Fusion sequentially merges temporally redundant frames into semantic groups when their pairwise uniqueness falls below a threshold; Token Allocation distributes the global token budget across frames proportionally to their softmax-normalized uniqueness scores, giving more tokens to semantically distinctive frames; Spatial Dynamic Compression greedily selects tokens by descending uniqueness within each frame, fusing redundant neighbors into the selected representative via averaging. The entire pipeline is training-free, requires only two hyperparameters, and reduces Time-To-First-Token by up to 4.15× while matching or exceeding uncompressed baseline accuracy.

## Results

On LLaVA-OneVision-7B with 32 input frames, UniComp achieves the highest average accuracy across four benchmarks (LongVideoBench, EgoSchema, MLVU, VideoMME) at every tested retention ratio. At 25% retention, UniComp reaches 60.78% average accuracy (101.4% relative to the uncompressed baseline), surpassing the strongest competitor HoliTom (60.30%) by 0.48 points. Under aggressive 10% compression, UniComp maintains 59.80% average accuracy (99.7% of baseline), outperforming HoliTom (58.90%) by 0.9 points and VisionZip (55.10%) by 4.7 points. With 320 input frames compressed to the same 6,272-token budget, UniComp achieves 62.45% average accuracy, exceeding the uncompressed 32-frame baseline by 2.5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniComp outperforms all seven compared baselines across every retention ratio (25%, 20%, 15%, 10%) on the four-benchmark average. Against the closest competitor HoliTom [24], UniComp leads by 0.48 points at 25% retention (60.78 vs. 60.30) and by 0.9 points at 10% (59.80 vs. 58.90), with the gap widening under more aggressive compression. VisionZip [38] is competitive at 25% (59.95 vs. 60.78, a 0.83-point gap) but degrades sharply at 10% retention (55.10 vs. 59.80, a 4.7-point gap), indicating its attention-based selection loses fine-grained details under extreme compression. FastVid [25] trails UniComp by 1.15–1.35 points across all ratios, while DyCoke [30] underperforms by 5.13 points at 25% due to its KV-cache pruning strategy. FastV [5] and PruneVid [13] are consistently 1.5–5 points behind UniComp, and PDrop [36] (reported only at 25%) achieves 56.7 on LongVideoBench and 58.0 on EgoSchema, below UniComp's 57.6 and 61.6 respectively. Notably, UniComp exceeds the uncompressed 100%-token baseline at all compression levels, suggesting that removing redundant tokens actually improves downstream LLM reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant attention-based paradigm for video token compression (VisionZip, FastV, HoliTom, DyCoke) by reframing the compression objective through an information-theoretic lens: minimizing conditional entropy between retained and full tokens, operationalized via a pairwise 'information uniqueness' score. While prior methods like ToMe and VisionZip also target redundancy, they use attention scores or cosine similarity heuristics without a formal link to reconstruction error; UniComp derives an explicit upper bound connecting uniqueness to reconstruction error, unifying temporal grouping (FGF), global allocation (TA), and spatial compression (SDC) under a single greedy principle. The method is also positioned against training-time compression approaches (MovieChat, LLaMA-VID, LongVU) that require fine-tuning, and against inner-LLM modification methods (DyCoke, HoliTom) that alter attention layers, by being entirely training-free and plug-and-play with only two hyperparameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The information-theoretic formulation provides a principled alternative to the ad-hoc attention-score heuristics that dominate current video compression, and the derivation of a reconstruction-error upper bound in terms of uniqueness gives the greedy selection strategy a theoretical justification that prior methods lack. Practically, the two-hyperparameter, architecture-agnostic design lowers the barrier to adoption compared to competitors requiring five or more tuned parameters. Open questions include whether the isotropic Gaussian assumption underlying the entropy-to-reconstruction-error equivalence holds for real ViT features, whether the greedy uniqueness selection is near-optimal versus the combinatorial problem it approximates, and whether the principle transfers to other modalities (audio, 3D) or to compression at the LLM decoder level rather than the ViT encoder level.
