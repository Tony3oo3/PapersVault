## Problem

RLVR-based training of generative multimodal reward models (MRMs) depends on large volumes of human-annotated multimodal preference data, which are prohibitively expensive to collect. This data bottleneck prevents the field from exploiting the well-established insight that scaling reinforcement learning substantially strengthens reasoning capabilities, a benefit already demonstrated in text-only LLMs but inaccessible to MRMs under the current paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262918/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For visual understanding tasks, the MRM receives an image or video paired with a textual query and two candidate text responses; for visual generation tasks, it receives a text caption and two generated images or videos. The model outputs a structured chain-of-thought rationale (tagged with <think>) that first identifies the task type (e.g., <type>Image Understanding</type>), optionally generates a visual caption (<caption>), evaluates each candidate, and concludes with a preference token (A or B) inside an <answer> tag. This structured output format is enforced by a format reward during RL training.

## Method

MSRL is a three-stage reinforcement learning curriculum that decouples reward-reasoning acquisition from multimodal adaptation. Stage 1 performs SFT followed by RLVR (via GRPO) on ~400k textual preference examples from GRAM-R², with the vision encoder and projector frozen, to build a strong text-only reward policy. Stage 2 bridges the task and modality gaps: it runs RL on caption-based data (images/videos replaced by GPT-5-generated captions) with a task-recognition reward and experience replay to prevent catastrophic forgetting, and introduces Cross-Modal Knowledge Distillation (CMKD), which samples rationales from the caption-trained model, selects the highest-confidence correct rationale via majority voting and format filtering, and fine-tunes the MRM to reproduce that reasoning when conditioned on the actual visual input. Stage 3 performs a short RL pass on only 20k (or as few as 5k) multimodal preference examples for final adaptation. The key scaling insight is that the textual RL stage provides an accessible, unbounded axis for improving MRM quality without additional multimodal annotations, and larger backbones (1B→14B) benefit increasingly from this textual RL.

## Results

On VL-RewardBench, MSRL (InternVL3.5-8B) achieves 75.9% average accuracy, improving over the generative MRM baseline (66.6%) by +9.3% and the discriminative MRM (64.3%) by +11.6%, using the same multimodal preference data. On Multimodal RewardBench, MSRL reaches 80.5% average, surpassing the generative baseline (76.2%) by +4.3%. With majority voting (+voting@16), the 8B model further improves to 77.5% and 81.3% respectively. On video tasks (Table 3), MSRL (8B) achieves 85.5% on ShareGPTVideo and 81.4% on GenAI-Bench (video generation), outperforming the generative MRM by +4.9% and +13.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSRL (8B) outperforms all four compared baselines on both benchmarks. Against InternVL3.5-8B used as an MLLM-as-a-Judge, MSRL gains +23.2% on VL-RewardBench (75.9% vs. 52.7%) and +4.7% on Multimodal RewardBench (80.5% vs. 75.8%), showing that a dedicated reward model trained with multi-stage RL substantially exceeds zero-shot prompting of the same backbone. Against R1-Reward (7B, RLVR on multimodal data only), MSRL improves VL-RewardBench by +4.5% (75.9% vs. 71.4%) and Multimodal RewardBench by +0.5% (80.5% vs. 80.0%), with the largest gap on the Generation subtask (+11.6%). Against UnifiedReward-think (7B, CoT + RLVR on multimodal data), MSRL leads by +3.7% on VL-RewardBench (75.9% vs. 72.2%) and +8.0% on Multimodal RewardBench (80.5% vs. 72.5%), particularly on the Knowledge subtask (+9.4%). Against LLaVA-Critic (7B), MSRL achieves a decisive margin of +25.2% on VL-RewardBench and +16.5% on Multimodal RewardBench, confirming that the multi-stage textual-to-multimodal RL curriculum is far more effective than single-stage multimodal training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR-based training paradigm for generative MRMs (UnifiedReward, R1-Reward) by replacing the requirement for large-scale multimodal preference data with a multi-stage curriculum that first learns reward reasoning from abundant textual preferences (GRAM-R², HelpSteer3) and then transfers via caption-based RL and cross-modal knowledge distillation. The caption-based intermediate stage draws on the LLaVA-style insight that textual grounding can bridge modality gaps, while the CMKD mechanism is a novel distillation strategy that aligns textual and visual reward reasoning without additional annotations. The approach departs from prior work (e.g., RoVRM) that used auxiliary textual data as a regularizer, instead making textual RL the primary training signal and reducing multimodal data to a small adaptation stage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MSRL directly addresses the fundamental data bottleneck in multimodal reward modeling by showing that large-scale textual RL can substitute for most of the multimodal RL signal, with only 5k–20k multimodal examples needed for final adaptation. The consistent scaling behavior across 1B–14B backbones and across understanding/generation tasks suggests this is a general, practical recipe rather than a narrow trick. Open questions include whether the GPT-5-generated captions in Stage 2 introduce a quality ceiling, whether the approach extends to audio or 3D modalities, and how much of the gain is attributable to the CMKD versus the sheer volume of textual RL. The finding that removing the final multimodal RL stage still yields substantial gains over baselines raises the question of whether even smaller multimodal adaptation sets (or none at all) could suffice.
