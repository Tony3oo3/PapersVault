## Problem

Visual geometric transformers (e.g., VGGT, Pi3) achieve state-of-the-art 3D reconstruction but incur quadratic attention cost that precludes real-time deployment. Existing acceleration strategies—token pruning loses spatial cues critical for dense geometry, and similarity-based merging (ToMe, FastVGGT) provides only modest speedups on moderate-length sequences. The paper asks how to identify and reduce redundant tokens in a confidence-aware manner that preserves geometric fidelity without retraining the base model.

## Input / Output

Co-Me operates as a plug-in on frozen ViT backbones, consuming multi-view RGB image sequences of variable length (1–512 frames) and producing the same outputs as the underlying model: per-pixel depth maps, 6-DoF camera poses, and dense 3D point clouds. The confidence predictor is inserted at the encoder midpoint, so merging and splitting are applied only to the remaining decoder layers; a merge ratio hyperparameter (default 0.5) and a fixed 3×3 spatial group size control the degree of token reduction, with per-image merge counts varying within a sequence based on relative confidence.

## Method

Co-Me consists of two stages. First, a lightweight confidence predictor (MLP → single-head attention → Conv2D head, <2% overhead) is distilled in a self-supervised manner from the frozen ViT's own confidence output using a logistic ranking loss over uniformly sampled token pairs, trained on TartanAir in under 2,000 steps on a single H100. Second, at inference, tokens are partitioned into fixed-size spatial groups; groups whose mean predicted confidence falls below the p-th percentile across the sequence are merged by averaging, while high-confidence groups are preserved. A key technical detail is an attention bias correction that adds log n to the raw logit of each merged group, compensating for the softmax mass loss when n tokens collapse into one. After the remaining layers process the reduced token set, a replication-based split restores the original token count for downstream prediction heads. The method accelerates both the attention and MLP modules and is implemented with a custom variable-length FlashAttention kernel and TensorRT plugin for edge deployment.

## Results

Co-Me achieves up to 21.5× speedup on VGGT (512-frame sequences) and 20.4× on Pi3, with the most dramatic gains on multi-view tasks: 7.73× on DTU-MVS (32 frames) and 9.94× on KITTI Depth (48 frames) for VGGT, while maintaining comparable depth accuracy (L1 error increases from 0.89 to 1.10 cm on DTU; from 4.647 to 4.727 m on KITTI). On single-frame inputs, Co-Me still delivers 5.17× speedup on MapAnything (NYUd-v2) where ToMeSD and FastVGGT either fail or are slower than the original model. For pose estimation on RE10K (128 frames), Co-Me-accelerated VGGT reaches 16.2× speedup with AUC₁₀ᵗ dropping only from 0.991 to 0.984.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original VGGT, Co-Me delivers 7.73× speedup on DTU (32 frames) and 9.94× on KITTI (48 frames) with only modest L1 degradation (0.89→1.10 cm; 4.647→4.727 m). Compared to the FlashAttention-enhanced VGGT* baseline, Co-Me still wins decisively (7.73× vs 4.81× on DTU), showing that confidence-guided merging complements rather than competes with efficient attention. Against ToMeSD (similarity-based merging at the same 0.5 ratio), Co-Me achieves higher speedup (7.73× vs 5.59× on DTU) and notably, ToMeSD is actually *slower* than the original model on single-frame inputs (0.48×), while Co-Me maintains a 1.09× speedup. FastVGGT (norm+similarity merging at 0.9 ratio, only in global attention) achieves just 3.04× on DTU and cannot handle single-frame input at all, whereas Co-Me operates across all layers and all sequence lengths. When applied to MapAnything, Co-Me achieves the largest absolute speedup (13.3× on DTU, 5.17× on single-frame NYUd-v2) because MA's architecture is more compute-heavy per token. For π³, Co-Me yields 6.47× on DTU and 8.72× on KITTI, confirming the method generalizes across different visual geometric transformer architectures without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

Co-Me extends the token merging paradigm pioneered by ToMe and ToMeSD by replacing the similarity-based merging criterion with a confidence-guided one, distilled from the visual geometric model's own confidence predictions. It departs from token pruning approaches (DynamicViT, SparseViT) that the authors show are unsuitable for dense geometric tasks, and generalizes FastVGGT's limited global-attention-only merging to the full network (attention + MLP). The key conceptual departure is using the model's inherent confidence signal—already computed for geometric reliability—as a proxy for token importance, rather than relying on feature similarity or norm heuristics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving up to 21.5× speedup on VGGT and 20.4× on Pi3 without retraining, Co-Me makes billion-parameter visual geometric transformers viable for real-time 3D perception, validated on edge hardware (Jetson Thor at 3.5 FPS). The confidence-as-importance-signal insight is broadly applicable and could transfer to other transformer-based perception models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to vision-language-action models, streaming/online settings, and whether confidence-guided merging can be integrated into training for further gains. Performance degradation remains notable in single-frame and low-overlap scenarios (e.g., KITTI), limiting applicability to certain deployment contexts.
