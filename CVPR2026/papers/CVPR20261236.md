## Problem

Video-instructed robotic programming must transfer task procedures from a demonstration domain to a deployment domain with different environmental layouts, object configurations, and embodiment constraints. Current VLM-based methods either directly imitate the demonstrated behavior (failing when physical preconditions are violated) or rely on unverified VLM reasoning that produces logically inconsistent plans under domain shifts. The core gap is the lack of a mechanism to identify *which* procedural steps become causally incompatible under deployment conditions and to revise them while preserving task intent.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a video demonstration (a sequence of RGB keyframes plus an optional language description) from the source domain and a single target observation from the deployment domain. The VLM parses keyframes into grounded scene graphs (object entities and spatial predicates), and the target observation is translated into a counterfactual symbolic state. The output is a STRIPS-style adapted task specification (a sequence of symbolic action operators with verified preconditions and effects) that is compiled into executable control code using predefined visual-motor APIs, with scenarios requiring up to 116 API calls.

## Method

NESYCR operates in two phases without any training. In Phase 1 (symbolic world model construction), the VLM abstracts the demonstration into a sequence of symbolic states (scene graphs) and abduces STRIPS-style action operators between consecutive states; a symbolic tool (VAL) verifies the entire trajectory via forward simulation, ensuring the reconstructed world model is logically coherent. In Phase 2 (neurosymbolic counterfactual adaptation), the VLM generates a counterfactual initial state from the deployment observation, and the symbolic tool simulates the demonstrated procedure forward to flag actions whose preconditions are violated or whose effects mismatch the expected next state. For each inconsistent step, the VLM proposes alternative or removal actions, and the symbolic tool iteratively verifies their causal validity until the adapted procedure reaches the goal state. The key design choice is the tight VLM-symbolic-tool loop: the VLM supplies commonsense procedural alternatives while the symbolic tool enforces logical consistency, preventing the semantically plausible but causally invalid plans that plague pure VLM baselines.

## Results

NESYCR achieves an average 31.14% improvement in Success Rate (SR) over the strongest baseline Statler across all cross-domain settings in simulation. In the combined domain-factor setting (Obstruction + Object Affordance + Kinematic Configuration + Gripper Type), NESYCR reaches 64.17% average SR versus 50.83% for Statler and 35.00% for GPT4V-Robotics. In the real-world Franka Emika deployment, NESYCR achieves 87.50% SR and 98.21% Goal Condition (GC), compared to 50.00% SR for both GPT4V-Robotics and Statler. Procedure Deviation (PD) remains low (≤12.50) for NESYCR across all settings, indicating the adapted procedures stay close to the demonstrated task intent.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NESYCR outperforms all six baselines on SR and GC across every domain-factor and complexity setting. Against the strongest world-model-based baseline Statler, NESYCR gains 24.77% in SR and 13.20% in GC on average; the gap widens dramatically at high complexity (e.g., 60.00% vs. 5.00% SR under Obstruction + Object Affordance). GPT4V-Robotics, the strongest VLM-reasoning baseline, trails NESYCR by 27.73% in SR on average and suffers a 40.91% SR drop from low to high complexity due to error propagation in multi-stage reasoning. LLM-DM, despite using symbolic tools, degrades by 41.82% in SR because constructing a complete domain model from a single demonstration frequently yields invalid plans. In the real-world Franka Emika experiment, NESYCR (87.50% SR) doubles the performance of both GPT4V-Robotics and Statler (50.00% SR), which fail to enforce the mutual-interference constraint between perpendicular drawers. Demo2Code, lacking any adaptation mechanism, performs worst in simulation (19–26% SR) and achieves 0% SR in the real-world setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

NESYCR sits at the intersection of the Code-as-Policies paradigm (Liang et al., 2022), video-instructed robotic programming (Demo2Code, Wang et al. 2023; Robotic Programmer, Xie et al. 2025), and neurosymbolic embodied planning (NESYC, Choi et al. 2025; VisualPredicator, Liang et al. 2024). Its key conceptual departure is framing cross-domain demo-to-code as a counterfactual reasoning problem: rather than directly imitating or re-planning from scratch (as Statler or LLM-DM do), it asks 'what would the demonstrated procedure look like under the target domain's conditions?' and uses a symbolic tool (VAL) to verify each proposed revision. This contrasts with VLM-only reasoning baselines (GPT4V-Robotics, Critic-V, MoReVQA) that lack any formal verification step, and with world-model approaches that maintain state but do not explicitly identify and repair causal inconsistencies via counterfactual intervention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled, verifiable mechanism for adapting demonstrated procedures across embodiment and environmental shifts, which is a persistent bottleneck in video-instructed robotic programming. The 31.14% SR improvement over the strongest baseline (Statler) and the real-world validation on a Franka Emika arm demonstrate practical relevance. However, the paper itself acknowledges a hard boundary: when the task complexity gap between demonstration and deployment grows large, the demonstration no longer provides sufficient causal structure for counterfactual reasoning, and a fundamentally new demonstration becomes necessary. Future work on causal re-grounding—inferring new causal relations in novel contexts—would be needed to push beyond this limitation, and the evaluation is confined to table-organization tasks with up to 116 API calls, leaving open whether the approach scales to more diverse manipulation domains.
