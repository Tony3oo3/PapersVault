## Problem

Diffusion-based generative image codecs achieve strong perceptual quality at ultra-low bitrates but fail to make the compression process content-adaptive. Specifically, isotropic quantization misaligns with the diffusion model's noise-dependent prior, a dimensional mismatch between high-dimensional noisy latents and the 4-channel diffusion decoder creates an information concentration bottleneck, and existing textual conditioning either incurs bitrate overhead or relies on generic content-agnostic prompts.

## Input / Output

The codec takes a single RGB image (evaluated at 768×512 for Kodak and 2K resolution for DIV2K Val and CLIC 2020 Test) and produces a compressed bitstream via arithmetic encoding of quantized latents with hyperprior and spatial-prior entropy models, plus a reconstructed image decoded through a one-step diffusion process. The decoder leverages a pre-trained Stable Diffusion 2.1 VAE, a frozen BLIP captioning model for zero-bitrate text generation, and a distilled one-step diffusion model (adversarial diffusion distillation) for efficient inference.

## Method

CADC introduces three content-adaptive mechanisms on top of a StableCodec-style auto-encoder with one-step diffusion decoding. (1) Uncertainty-Guided Adaptive Quantization (UGAQ) computes a spatial uncertainty map from the residual between the main latent and upsampled hyperprior latent, then divides the latent by this map before quantization, creating a locally varying SNR at the decoder input so that textured regions receive stronger generative intervention while smooth regions preserve structural fidelity. (2) Auxiliary Decoder-Guided Information Concentration (ADGIC) adds a lightweight decoder that reconstructs an image from only the first four channels of the noisy latent; the L2 loss between this auxiliary reconstruction and the original image forces semantically critical information into the primary channels consumed by the diffusion decoder. (3) Bitrate-Free Adaptive Textual Conditioning (BFATC) captions the auxiliary reconstruction with a frozen BLIP model and concatenates the result with a fixed quality prompt, providing content-specific semantic guidance to the one-step diffusion denoiser at zero textual bitrate cost.

## Results

CADC achieves state-of-the-art perceptual quality at ultra-low bitrates, consistently outperforming all compared diffusion-based codecs (DiffEIC, ResULIC, MKIC, OSCAR, StableCodec) across Kodak, DIV2K Val, and CLIC 2020 Test on LPIPS, DISTS, FID, and KID. The ablation study quantifies the contribution of each component via BD-rate: UGAQ alone yields −3.7% (LPIPS) and −2.7% (DISTS), adding ADGIC brings the total to −5.3% and −3.5%, and the full model with BFATC reaches −6.8% LPIPS and −5.5% DISTS relative to the baseline. Qualitative comparisons on 2K-resolution CLIC 2020 images show CADC preserves complex textures and avoids the blurring and color-shifting artifacts visible in DLF and StableCodec reconstructions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CADC outperforms all eight baselines on perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DISTS, FID, KID) across all three test datasets at ultra-low bitrates. Among diffusion-based codecs, it surpasses StableCodec (its direct architectural predecessor), OSCAR, MKIC, ResULIC, and DiffEIC, with the largest gaps appearing at the lowest bitrates where generative priors matter most. Against non-diffusion baselines, CADC also beats the GAN-based HiFiC and the VQ-based DLF and GLC, which exhibit noticeable blurring and texture loss in qualitative comparisons. The ablation confirms that each of the three proposed components (UGAQ, ADGIC, BFATC) contributes independently, with BFATC providing the largest single-metric gain on DISTS (+2.0% BD-rate) by supplying content-adaptive textual guidance at zero bitrate cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based image compression line initiated by StableCodec, DiffEIC, and ResULIC, which condition a pre-trained diffusion decoder on noisy latents extracted by a learned encoder. It departs from prior work by identifying and addressing three specific content-adaptation failures: replacing isotropic quantization with uncertainty-guided spatially-varying quantization (UGAQ), introducing an auxiliary decoder to force semantic information into the primary four channels (ADGIC), and deriving content-aware captions from the auxiliary reconstruction via BLIP to eliminate textual bitrate overhead (BFATC). The entropy modeling (hyperprior + autoregressive spatial prior) and one-step diffusion decoder follow the StableCodec architecture, positioning CADC as a targeted refinement rather than a new framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The content-adaptive framing provides a principled lens for understanding why uniform quantization and generic prompts underperform in diffusion-based codecs, and the three proposed mechanisms are modular enough to be adopted in other generative compression pipelines. The bitrate-free text conditioning via the auxiliary decoder is a practical trick that avoids the tradeoff between semantic guidance and bit budget that plagues methods transmitting BLIP captions. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video compression, whether the BLIP captioning step introduces prohibitive decoding latency in real-time applications, and whether the uncertainty-guided quantization generalizes to multi-step diffusion decoders or other generative priors beyond Stable Diffusion.
