## Problem

Existing reasoning paradigms—'Thinking with Text' (chain-of-thought in LLMs) and 'Thinking with Images' (image outputs in VLM CoT)—suffer from two inherent limitations: static images cannot represent dynamic processes or continuous transformations, and the separation of text and vision into distinct modalities prevents unified multimodal understanding and generation. This paper asks whether video generation models can serve as a unified reasoning medium that naturally integrates drawing, imagination, and textual reasoning within a coherent temporal structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262756/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a text prompt containing the problem statement along with a reference image that displays the full problem visually. It produces a generated video in which the solution process is rendered as visual content (e.g., drawing geometric lines, writing mathematical steps, or filling in grid cells) and an accompanying audio track that verbally states the final answer. Evaluation extracts the last frame, a majority-vote frame (Major Frame) across the video duration, and the transcribed audio independently, with an LLM-as-a-Judge (GPT-4o) scoring correctness against ground truth.

## Method

The paper introduces VideoThinkBench, a benchmark spanning five reasoning abilities organized in a hierarchy: geometric intuition (eyeballing puzzles), visual pattern induction (PuzzleVQA-adapted tasks), abstract rule induction (ARC-AGI-2), spatial planning (mazes), and language/mathematical reasoning (GSM8K, MATH-500, MMLU, MMMU, MathVista, etc.). Rather than proposing a new architecture, the work is an empirical study evaluating Sora-2 (a video generation model) as a reasoner, comparing it against SOTA VLMs (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Claude Sonnet 4.5). Analysis experiments probe the source of Sora-2's abilities: (1) in-context learning is tested by varying the number of ARC-AGI-2 examples, showing performance improves with more demonstrations; (2) self-consistency via majority voting over five video generations improves accuracy from 68% to 90% on verifiable tasks, revealing a test-time scaling direction; (3) a leakage check using numerically perturbed problems confirms inherent capability; and (4) a controlled experiment with Wan 2.5 (which exposes a prompt-rewriter toggle) shows that disabling the rewriter drops accuracy to near zero, suggesting Sora-2's text-centric reasoning is largely delegated to an internal prompt rewriter rather than the video generation component itself.

## Results

On the VideoThinkBench vision-centric tasks, Sora-2 achieves an average accuracy of 40.4%, comparable to SOTA VLMs (GPT-5 high: 42.6%, Claude Sonnet 4.5: 43.8%, Gemini 2.5 Pro: 41.4%), and notably surpasses GPT-5 by 10% on eyeballing puzzles (44.7% vs 33.6% on Point tasks). On text-centric tasks, Sora-2's audio accuracy reaches 98.9% on GSM8K, 92.0% on MATH-500, 75.7% on MathVista, and 69.2% on MMMU, though it trails SOTA VLMs on harder benchmarks like AIME (46.7% vs 93–95%) and GPQA (57.6% vs 83–86%). The overall average across all tasks is 49.8% for Sora-2 versus 55.0–56.2% for the VLM baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sora-2 clearly outperforms all three VLM baselines on eyeballing puzzles (44.7% vs 27.8–36.2% on Point; 38.0% vs 21.0–26.3% on Line) and is the only model to solve any mazes (13.3% vs 0.0% for all VLMs), demonstrating unique spatial reasoning through drawing. On MathVista, Sora-2's audio accuracy (75.7%) exceeds GPT-5 high (67.5%) and Claude Sonnet 4.5 (72.5%), making it the top performer on that multimodal math benchmark. However, Sora-2 trails significantly on most text-centric tasks: GSM8K (98.9% vs 100% for GPT-5 and Claude), MATH-500 (92.0% vs 98–99%), MMLU (67.3% vs 86–89.5%), and MMMU (69.2% vs 77–82%). On visual pattern induction tasks, Sora-2 is competitive on symmetry (81.9% vs Claude's 80.1%) but lags behind GPT-5 on gradient (51.9% vs 66.7%) and compositionality (57.5% vs 85.0%). Overall, Sora-2 is a strong but not dominant reasoner: it wins on tasks requiring dynamic visual manipulation (drawing, spatial planning) while losing on tasks where text-based chain-of-thought reasoning is more natural.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'Thinking with Text' (Chain-of-Thought) and 'Thinking with Images' paradigms by proposing 'Thinking with Video' as a unified multimodal reasoning medium, where video generation models like Sora-2 produce reasoning chains as video frames rather than text tokens or static images. Conceptually, it repurposes video generation models—previously treated as content-creation tools—as reasoning agents, drawing on the spatial and temporal expressiveness of video to support drawing, imagination, and dynamic process visualization. The evaluation framework (VideoThinkBench) adapts existing benchmarks (ARC-AGI-2, PuzzleVQA, GSM8K, MATH, MMMU) to a video-generation input-output format, and the analysis borrows techniques from the CoT literature (self-consistency, in-context learning) to probe video-model reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a genuinely new research direction by showing that video generation models can perform spatial reasoning through drawing and imagination, with Sora-2 surpassing GPT-5 on eyeballing puzzles. However, the most striking text-centric results (98.9% GSM8K, 92% MATH) are substantially undermined by the finding that a prompt rewriter model likely performs the actual reasoning, leaving open whether video generation models can natively reason over text. Key open questions include whether the vision-centric advantages generalize beyond Sora-2, whether test-time scaling (self-consistency, more examples) can close the gap with SOTA VLMs on harder tasks, and whether a truly unified video-based reasoning model can emerge without relying on external text-rewriting components.
