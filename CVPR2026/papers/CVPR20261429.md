## Problem

Pretrained VLA models lack the tactile feedback needed for precise contact-rich manipulation, yet naively injecting tactile tokens during finetuning disrupts pretrained visual grounding and object localization. Additionally, the inherently slow inference of large VLMs prevents real-time reaction to high-frequency tactile signals, undermining closed-loop control where swift force-based adjustments are critical.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261429/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes RGB frames from a head camera and two wrist cameras, a natural-language instruction, a 6D resultant contact force (3D normal + 3D tangential) from Xense Robotics gripper sensors, and the robot's proprioceptive state, producing a 14-DoF dual-arm end-effector pose action chunk spanning H timesteps. During contact, the tactile stream runs at 3× the frequency of the vision-language stream, so the action expert receives asynchronous, heterogeneous inputs: the latest tactile token as query and the most recent VLM latent as key/value within the same chunk horizon.

## Method

AT-VLA builds on the GO-1 VLA (Intern-VL-2B + DiT action expert) and introduces two key mechanisms. First, Adaptive Tactile Injection uses a lightweight MLP Tactile Gate to classify contact vs. non-contact from the tactile token; when the gate is inactive the action expert's cross-attention query remains the proprioceptive state token (identical to vanilla VLA), and when active the query switches to the tactile token via an Adaptive Cross Attention module that conditionally changes the query source without altering feature dimensionality or model structure. Second, the Tactile Reaction Dual-Stream decouples the slow VLM stream (low-frequency visual-language reasoning) from the fast tactile stream (high-frequency force feedback at 3:1 ratio), enabling 0.04 s closed-loop inference. A Tactile Generation auxiliary head predicts the next-step 6D force to encourage richer physical-dynamics representations. All objectives (action diffusion loss, binary gating loss, MSE tactile-prediction loss) are trained jointly with small balancing weights (λ₁=λ₂=0.01).

## Results

On four contact-rich manipulation tasks (Unzip Bag, Stamp, Wipe Vase, Unscrew Lid), AT-VLA achieves overall success rates of 0.33, 0.46, 0.33, and 0.46 respectively, outperforming the vanilla GO-1 VLA (0.20, 0.33, 0.07, 0.27) by 13–26 percentage points across tasks. In the modality-agnostic evaluation, AT-VLA with tactile input reaches an average success rate of 0.79 across Pick-and-Place, Open Drawer, and Stamp, while the same weights without tactile input at inference still achieve 0.70, matching π0.5 and exceeding GO-1 (0.68). The Tactile Reaction Dual-Stream enables closed-loop tactile response within 0.04 s inference latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AT-VLA outperforms the vanilla GO-1 VLA on all four contact-rich tasks, with the largest gain on Wipe Vase (0.33 vs. 0.07, +26 pp), confirming that adaptive tactile injection preserves pretrained visual grounding while adding contact-phase precision. Against π0.5, AT-VLA wins on Unzip Bag (0.33 vs. 0.00) and Stamp (0.46 vs. 0.20), ties on Wipe Vase (0.33), and narrowly trails on Unscrew Lid (0.46 vs. 0.47). The tactile-specialized baselines VTLA and RDP are evaluated only on the contact-rich sub-phase with the robot manually pre-positioned in an ideal grasp, so they are not directly comparable on full-task success; under that restricted protocol, VTLA (0.60) and RDP (0.87) exceed AT-VLA on Wipe Vase and Unscrew Lid respectively, which the authors attribute to AT-VLA's occasional grip slippage during unscrewing. In the modality-agnostic setting (Table 2), AT-VLA without tactile input at inference matches π0.5 (0.70 avg) and slightly beats GO-1 (0.68 avg), demonstrating that the adaptive training strategy does not degrade performance when the tactile sensor is unavailable.

## Relation to Prior Work

AT-VLA builds directly on the GO-1 VLA (AgiBot's diffusion-based action expert with Intern-VL-2B) and extends the slow-fast dual-system paradigm established by Groot-N1 and Fast-in-Slow, but replaces their visual fast stream with a high-frequency tactile stream. It departs from prior tactile-VLA work (VTLA, RDP, TLA, TA-VLA) that naively appends tactile tokens to the model's input sequence, by introducing an adaptive gating mechanism that conditionally switches the cross-attention query source rather than augmenting the token sequence. The tactile generation objective (predicting future force) draws on predictive coding ideas but is applied here specifically to improve the fast stream's physical dynamics understanding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: pretrained VLAs lack the physical interaction feedback needed for contact-rich manipulation, yet naively adding tactile input degrades their core visual grounding. The modality-agnostic robustness (maintaining performance without tactile at inference) is practically important for real-world deployment where sensors fail. However, evaluation is limited to a single robot platform (AgiBot Genie1), six tasks with 30–50 demonstrations each, and the 0.04 s closed-loop claim relies on a fixed 3:1 frequency ratio that may not generalize to all contact dynamics. Open questions include whether the adaptive gating transfers across embodiments, whether the tactile generation objective scales to richer contact scenarios, and how the approach performs with larger-scale pretraining data that already includes tactile channels.
