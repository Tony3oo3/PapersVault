## Problem

Modern DETR-style 3D object detectors produce overconfident and miscalibrated uncertainty estimates, and standard post-hoc calibration methods (Temperature Scaling, Platt Scaling, Isotonic Regression) fail under distribution shift because they apply a fixed correction regardless of input. No prior approach jointly recalibrates both classification confidences and bounding-box regression variances in a way that remains robust when test data deviates from the training distribution.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260085/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes either six multi-view camera images (processed by PETR) or a LiDAR point cloud (processed by SECOND) and produces, per detected object, a calibrated class score and a full probabilistic 3D box: mean center (x,y,z), size (l,w,h), yaw θ, velocity (vx,vy), plus per-parameter variances σ² and a von-Mises concentration κ_θ. The density signal is computed on the 256-dimensional latent query vectors from the final transformer decoder layer, not on raw sensor data, making it a compact, class- and location-aware representation of each detection hypothesis.

## Method

The core idea is to fit a class-conditional RealNVP normalizing flow on the latent query features of True Positive matches from the training set, yielding a per-query density that measures alignment with the in-distribution feature manifold. At test time, this density is standardized (z_dens) and injected into classic post-hoc calibrators: for classification, it modulates the temperature in DA-TS, the scale and shift in DA-PS, and the input feature in DA-IR; for regression, it applies a bounded multiplicative correction to each predicted variance and yaw concentration. All calibration parameters (original calibrator parameters plus density-specific scaling factors) are jointly optimized by minimizing NLL (classification) or MCA (regression) on a held-out calibration set. Under distribution shift, queries drift away from the training manifold, the density drops, and the calibrators automatically increase uncertainty and reduce confidence, providing per-instance adaptive correction without retraining.

## Results

On the nuScenes in-distribution split, the best density-aware calibrator (DA-IR, class-wise) achieves D-ECE of 2.96% (PETR) and 1.84% (SECOND), marginally improving over standard Isotonic Regression (2.99% and 1.87%). Under MultiCorrupt distribution shift, the gains become substantial: DA-IR (Cls.) reduces D-ECE to 7.21% (PETR) and 6.90% (SECOND) versus 7.45% and 7.08% for vanilla IR, while DA-PS (Cls.) drops D-ECE to 7.77% and 6.78% versus 7.99% and 7.04% for PS. For regression calibration under shift, DA-TS (Cls.) achieves MCA_xyz of 3.55% (PETR) and 4.04% (SECOND), outperforming the TS baseline (3.78% and 5.26%) and the KL-divergence uncertainty head (5.10% and 6.36%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sample-based methods (MCD, Deep Ensembles) perform worst across all settings, often degrading below the uncalibrated baseline (e.g., MCD D-ECE 9.49% vs. 8.56% uncalibrated on PETR ID) while also reducing mAP, due to over-grouping of adjacent boxes in dense nuScenes scenes. Train-time calibration methods (CalDETR, TCD) also fail to improve over uncalibrated models on 3D detection, as their 2D-oriented IoU-based losses do not transfer well to 3D boxes with severe class imbalance. Among post-hoc baselines, class-wise IR and PS are the strongest in-distribution (D-ECE 2.99% and 4.00% on PETR), but under MultiCorrupt shift their advantage narrows and the density-aware variants (DA-IR, DA-PS) consistently win by 0.2–0.3 D-ECE points. For regression, the KL-divergence uncertainty head provides the best train-time baseline (MCA_xyz 4.38% ID on PETR), but DA-TS post-hoc calibration improves it to 1.54% (ID) and 3.55% (shift), while the depth-based affine calibrator and energy-based loss (ES) are clearly inferior. The key differentiator is that under distribution shift, standard post-hoc calibrators (TS, PS, IR) degrade to near-uncalibrated levels, whereas the density-aware versions maintain their advantage, confirming that feature density provides a useful OOD signal for adaptive calibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the density-aware calibration paradigm from 2D image classification (Tomani et al.) to query-based 3D object detection by using latent object queries as the feature space for density estimation. It couples this density signal with classic post-hoc calibrators (Temperature Scaling, Platt Scaling, Isotonic Regression) and a KL/von-Mises regression uncertainty head, creating a unified framework that jointly recalibrates classification confidences and bounding-box variances. The base detectors (PETR for multi-view cameras, SECOND for LiDAR) follow the standard DETR-style query architecture, and the RealNVP normalizing flow serves as the density estimator fitted on True Positive query features from training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes the first comprehensive uncertainty evaluation benchmark for 3D object detection on nuScenes, filling a gap where no unified framework existed for assessing both classification and regression calibration quality. The practical implication for autonomous driving is significant: miscalibrated uncertainties propagated to tracking and planning stacks pose safety risks, and the demonstrated failure of standard post-hoc calibrators under distribution shift (rain, snow, sensor corruption) highlights a critical deployment gap. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond DETR-style architectures, whether the normalizing-flow density estimator (8.99 ms latency) can be made lighter for real-time use, and whether the calibration set requirement can be eliminated for truly zero-shot deployment.
