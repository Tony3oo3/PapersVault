## Problem

Existing training-free diffusion segmentors, which extract cross-attention maps from pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models for semantic segmentation, fail to scale with stronger generative models. The paper identifies two gaps: (i) individual attention maps from multiple heads and layers are aggregated with manually tuned weights that become impractical for more complex architectures, and (ii) even a global attention map does not directly yield accurate semantic correlation due to score imbalances, particularly from semantic special tokens that distort cross-pixel comparisons.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text prompt describing scene objects (e.g., 'a cat on grass before wall'), with content word tokens identified via GPT-4o-assisted object detection to modulate the prompt. It produces a pixel-level class label assignment (segmentation mask) at the latent resolution of the diffusion model, where each pixel is assigned to the content word token with the highest refined attention score. No additional training or fine-tuning is required; the entire pipeline operates on a frozen pre-trained diffusion model.

## Method

GoCA (Generative scaling of Cross-Attention) bridges two gaps with two techniques. For auto aggregation, head-wise weights are computed by reformulating multi-head attention as vector summation and measuring each head's dot-product similarity to the summed layer output, yielding per-pixel head weights; layer-wise weights are computed by comparing per-layer self-attention maps against a pseudo global attention map derived from dense diffusion features. For per-pixel rescaling, semantic special tokens and stop words are excluded, and the remaining content word token scores are normalized to sum to 1 per pixel before a standard per-token min-max re-normalization to [0,1], mitigating the dominance of special tokens that otherwise corrupts cross-pixel score comparisons. The final refined cross-attention map is multiplied by self-attention maps for spatial refinement, and segmentation is obtained by argmax over content word tokens per pixel.

## Results

GoCA (auto aggregation + per-pixel rescaling) achieves the best mIoU across all five segmentation benchmarks when paired with the strongest model Flux: 70.7 on Pascal VOC 2012, 51.1 on Pascal-Context-59, 48.1 on COCO-Object, 27.1 on Cityscapes, and 29.3 on ADE20K. Even with the weakest backbone (SD v1.5), GoCA reaches 60.7 mIoU on VOC, surpassing all prior training-free diffusion segmentors. The key scaling property is confirmed: mIoU on VOC increases monotonically from 60.7 (SD v1.5) → 63.6 (PixArt-Sigma) → 65.6 (SD XL) → 70.7 (Flux), demonstrating that stronger generative models now yield proportionally better segmentation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GoCA with Flux dominates all prior training-free diffusion segmentors by large margins: it outperforms FTTM [39] by +21.8 mIoU on VOC (70.7 vs. 48.9), +21.1 on Context (51.1 vs. 30.0), and +15.8 on ADE20K (29.3 vs. 20.3). Against DiffSegmentor [36], GoCA (SD v1.5) already leads on VOC (60.7 vs. 60.1) and Context (40.4 vs. 27.5), with the gap widening to +10.5 on VOC when using Flux. MaskDiffusion [17] is the only baseline that GoCA (SD v1.5) does not beat on every reported metric (16.1 vs. 17.1 on Cityscapes), but GoCA with any stronger backbone surpasses it by 4–10 mIoU on Cityscapes. Among non-diffusion baselines, GoCA (Flux) exceeds MaskCLIP [49] by +31.9 mIoU on VOC and +19.5 on ADE20K, and ReCO [32] by +45.6 on VOC and +18.1 on ADE20K. In the generation experiment (S-CFG integration), GoCA+S-CFG consistently achieves the lowest FID and highest CLIP score across all CFG strengths (e.g., FID 18.82 vs. 19.15 for S-CFG at CFG=5.0), confirming the benefit transfers to downstream generative tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of training-free diffusion segmentors (MaskDiffusion, DiffSegmentor, FTTM) that extract cross-attention maps from pre-trained text-to-image diffusion models for semantic segmentation. The key departure is identifying why these methods fail to scale to stronger, architecturally diverse models (U-Net, DiT, MMDiT): prior work relied on manually tuned layer aggregation weights and overlooked score imbalance from semantic special tokens. The proposed GoCA framework replaces manual weights with data-driven auto aggregation (head-wise via dot-product similarity, layer-wise via pseudo self-attention) and introduces per-pixel rescaling to neutralize token-scale interference, positioning itself as a bridge between generative capability and discriminative performance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that stronger diffusion models (SDXL, PixArt-Sigma, Flux) actually yield worse segmentation than SD v1.5 under existing methods challenges the natural assumption that generative power should transfer to discriminative tasks, and the cross-architecture validation (U-Net, DiT, MMDiT) makes this a robust observation. Practically, GoCA provides a drop-in, training-free recipe that works across model families without per-architecture tuning, which is valuable as the diffusion model landscape continues to diversify. However, the method still depends on external object detectors (GPT-4o) for prompt construction, and the authors acknowledge the approach is limited to semantic segmentation, leaving open whether the same scaling principles apply to detection, depth estimation, or other discriminative tasks.
