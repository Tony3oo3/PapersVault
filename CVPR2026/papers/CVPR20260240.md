## Problem

Existing multimodal AI models for Alzheimer's disease diagnosis operate as black boxes, outputting labels or risk scores without transparent reasoning or explicit links to the clinical evidence and brain anatomy that justify each decision. This is particularly problematic in AD, where differentiating overlapping neurodegenerative conditions requires clinicians to trace every diagnostic claim back to specific measurements and structures. No prior method simultaneously grounds generated report sentences to clinical fields and localizes those fields to 3D MRI anatomy in a hierarchical, auditable chain.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260240/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3D sMRI volume (skull-stripped, intensity-normalized) alongside structured clinical variables spanning demographics, APOE genotype, neuropsychological test scores, CSF biomarkers (Aβ, tTau, pTau), and comorbidities, which are pre-converted into standardized textual descriptors (e.g., z-normalized hippocampal volumes mapped to atrophy levels). The output is a structured report containing Reasoning, Diagnosis (CN/MCI/Dementia), and Confidence fields, accompanied by per-sentence evidence links to specific clinical entries and evidence-conditioned 3D volumetric segmentation masks for the corresponding brain structures.

## Method

EMAD fuses a 3D Vision Transformer (sMRI) and a Longformer (clinical text) through a Bidirectional Cross-Attention module, then decodes a report via LLaMA 3.2-1B with LoRA adapters. The key architectural contribution is the SEA Grounding head: a multi-positive InfoNCE loss aligns each generated sentence to clinical evidence phrases, and an evidence-conditioned SegFormer3D decoder (with cross-attention to the grounded evidence text injected at every layer) localizes the corresponding anatomy in 3D. To avoid expensive dense annotation, GTX-Distill trains a teacher grounder on a small labeled subset and distills its soft evidence/anatomy distributions into a student operating on model-generated reports via temperature-scaled KL divergence. A final Executable-Rule GRPO stage applies reinforcement fine-tuning with three verifiable rewards—structured format validity, NIA-AA diagnostic consistency (category, biomarker thresholds, clinical feature coverage), and NLI-based reasoning-to-diagnosis entailment—steering the policy toward clinically faithful outputs without human preference labels.

## Results

On the AD-MultiSense dataset, EMAD achieves 93.33% accuracy and 91.83% AUC on the CN vs. CI task, and 92.82% accuracy and 90.09% AUC on the more challenging CN vs. MCI task, outperforming the strongest MLLM baseline (M3D-LaMed) by 2.05% ACC and 2.67% AUC on CN vs. CI. For three-way CN/MCI/AD classification, EMAD reaches 89.4% accuracy and 87.8% macro-F1, surpassing the multimodal baseline ALIFUSE (84.7% ACC) by 4.7 points. On report quality, EMAD scores 0.9120 BERTScore and 0.5422 BLEU on CN vs. CI, substantially exceeding M3D-LaMed (0.8748 BERTScore, 0.3627 BLEU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMAD outperforms all four MLLM baselines (LLaVA-1.5-7B, LLaVA-Med, Med-PaLM-M, M3D-LaMed) on every reported metric for both binary tasks. Against the strongest MLLM baseline M3D-LaMed [1], EMAD gains +2.05% ACC and +2.67% AUC on CN vs. CI, and +3.35% ACC and +2.03% AUC on CN vs. MCI, while also leading in all report-quality metrics (e.g., BLEU 0.5422 vs. 0.3627). For three-way CN/MCI/AD classification, EMAD (89.4% ACC, 87.8% macro-F1) beats the multimodal classification baseline ALIFUSE [5] (84.7% ACC, 82.5% macro-F1) by 4.7 and 5.3 points respectively, and the text-only Longformer [2] baseline (82.1% ACC) by 7.3 points. The margin over M3D-LaMed is particularly notable on the harder CN vs. MCI task, where early detection is most clinically relevant. EMAD also demonstrates cross-cohort generalization (ADNI→AIBL) reported in the appendix, further supporting robustness beyond the primary evaluation split.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMAD extends the MLLM-based medical diagnosis line (M3D, Med-PaLM-M, ALIFUSE) by replacing opaque scalar predictions with structured reports whose every claim is hierarchically grounded: sentence → clinical evidence (via multi-positive InfoNCE, building on CLIP) → 3D anatomy (via evidence-conditioned SegFormer3D). The pre-training stage follows BLIP's momentum-encoder contrastive recipe, and the reinforcement stage adapts GRPO with executable NIA-AA clinical rules rather than the heuristic similarity rewards used in prior medical RL work. The key departure from prior grounding literature (which typically performs 2D phrase-region alignment or post-hoc saliency) is the explicit volumetric, evidence-conditioned segmentation tied to individual report sentences, combined with GTX-Distill to amortize the annotation cost.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real clinical trust gap: AD diagnosis involves multiple modalities and guidelines (NIA-AA), yet current AI systems offer no auditable chain from a generated sentence to the specific biomarker value or brain region that justifies it. The AD-MultiSense dataset (10,378 multimodal samples from ADNI/AIBL with grounding annotations) and the executable-rule GRPO recipe are reusable assets for other neurodegenerative or multi-criteria diagnostic tasks. Open questions include whether the 1B-parameter model and single-disease scope generalize to rarer conditions or to prospective clinical deployment, and whether the NLI-based entailment reward is robust enough to catch subtle clinical contradictions at scale.
