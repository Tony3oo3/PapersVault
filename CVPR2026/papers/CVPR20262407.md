## Problem

Most audio-visual learning methods rely on monaural audio and focus on semantic or temporal alignment between sound and visual objects, overlooking the spatial cues that binaural hearing provides. When semantic matching is ambiguous (multiple objects share the same sound category) or misleading (a sound emanates from a visually unrelated object), spatial reasoning becomes essential but remains unaddressed in audio-visual LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262407/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 224×812 panoramic RGB image (a 360° view stitched from 18 frames of 20° horizontal FoV, aligned to the agent's front-facing direction), a 10-second binaural audio waveform sampled at 32 kHz, and a natural-language question. It produces a free-form text answer spanning nine question types that require sound classification, direction-of-arrival estimation, relative distance/direction reasoning, and disambiguation among multiple visually similar objects. The accompanying Hear You Are QA dataset contains 1 million question-answer pairs rendered in SoundSpaces 2.0 with Matterport3D scenes and VGGSound audio sources.

## Method

Hear You Are LLM pairs a SigLIP2 NaFLEX vision encoder (fine-tuned with LoRA) and a frozen Spatial-AST binaural audio encoder, each followed by a Q-Former projector (128 visual query tokens, 64 audio query tokens) that maps modality features into the hidden space of a Qwen2-7B-Instruct LLM. Training follows a two-stage protocol: a warm-start phase pretrains each encoder unimodally on classification and localization questions (e.g., 'What are the predicted azimuth and elevation angles, and the distance to the sound source?'), followed by end-to-end multimodal fine-tuning on the full 1M QA dataset for 3 epochs with LoRA rank 16. The key design insight is that binaural audio encodes explicit spatial cues (azimuth, elevation, distance) that monaural audio cannot provide, enabling the model to disambiguate among multiple visually similar objects sharing the same sound category—a capability the ablations confirm is absent in all monaural baselines.

## Results

On the Hear You Are QA benchmark, the proposed Hear You Are LLM (R+B+Q) achieves 61.67% on Q1(non-matching) and 64.27% on Q8(DoA), where spatial reasoning is essential, substantially outperforming the closest monaural baseline VideoLLaMA2 (50.75% and 46.37% respectively). On tasks where semantic cues suffice (Q1 aligned, Q7 DoA), the method performs comparably to VideoLLaMA2 (77.61% vs 77.44% and 73.21% vs 68.57%). The monaural sound-source-localization baselines (ISSL, ACL-SSL) lag far behind on all spatial metrics, with Q1(non-matching) scores of only 12.94% and 10.61%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four baselines: ISSL [43,44] and ACL-SSL [32] (monaural sound-source-localization methods), and VideoLLaMA2 [10] (a monaural MLLM with the same encoders and LLM backbone). The proposed method decisively outperforms all baselines on the two metrics that require true spatial disambiguation: Q1(non-matching) (61.67% vs 50.75% for VideoLLaMA2, 12.94% for ISSL) and Q8(DoA) (64.27% vs 46.37% for VideoLLaMA2, 21.00% for ISSL), where multiple visually similar objects share the same sound category. On Q8(class), VideoLLaMA2 slightly edges out the proposed method (75.33% vs 70.27%), likely because class identification benefits from the richer visual context of the MLLM even without binaural cues. On Q1(aligned) and Q7(DoA), where a single semantically matching object makes localization straightforward, the proposed method ties or narrowly beats VideoLLaMA2 (77.61% vs 77.44%; 73.21% vs 68.57%), confirming that binaural audio is most critical when semantic cues are ambiguous. The two monaural localization baselines (ISSL, ACL-SSL) are consistently 20–50 points below the MLLM baselines across all spatial metrics, underscoring that language-based reasoning plus binaural input is necessary for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: audio-visual sound source localization (ISSL, ACL-SSL), which relies on monaural audio and semantic matching; spatial audio reasoning (BAT, Devnani et al.), which uses binaural audio but excludes vision; and audio-visual LLMs (VideoLLaMA2, GroundingGPT, Meerkat), which integrate vision and monaural audio with language models but lack spatial audio. The key departure is the explicit introduction of binaural spatial audio into an audio-visual LLM framework, combined with a dataset designed to stress-test spatial reasoning scenarios where semantic cues are absent or ambiguous. Architecturally, it reuses existing components (SigLIP2, Spatial-AST, Q-Former, Qwen2-7B) but introduces a warm-start pretraining strategy for modality-specific encoders before joint multi-modal fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 1M-pair Hear You Are QA dataset is the first large-scale benchmark specifically targeting audio-visual spatial reasoning, filling a clear gap between semantic sound source localization and spatial audio QA. The ablation results provide a clean, well-controlled demonstration that binaural audio is not merely complementary but essential when multiple visually similar objects share a sound category—a finding that has direct implications for designing audio-visual systems in cluttered real-world scenes. Key limitations include the entirely simulation-based pipeline (SoundSpaces 2.0, synthetic 3D objects from Stable Diffusion/InstantMesh), single sound source per scene, and the use of a fixed HRTF, all of which raise questions about generalization to real-world recordings with multiple simultaneous sources, varied room acoustics, and individual HRTF differences. Open questions include whether the spatial reasoning gains persist with real binaural recordings and whether the approach scales to multi-source scenes.
