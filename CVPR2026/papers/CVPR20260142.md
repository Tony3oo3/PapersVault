## Problem

Multi-person gaze estimation from a single upward-facing tabletop fisheye camera remains largely unexplored: existing approaches either rely on forward-facing cameras with constrained viewpoints or use multi-stage pipelines (e.g., GAM360) that are slow, hard to deploy, and suffer from cumulative errors such as face splitting at panoramic boundaries. No public dataset exists for this specific fisheye-from-below multi-person setting, and the severe radial distortion and extreme viewing angles inherent to 180° fisheye imagery are not addressed by standard perspective-camera gaze models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260142/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 1024×1024 RGB fisheye image (180° equidistant projection, camera facing upward from a tabletop) and simultaneously predicts, for each of N detected individuals, a 3D gaze direction vector in camera coordinates, a face bounding box, head pose (rotation and translation), head distance, facial landmarks, and eye landmarks (eye centers and pupil centers). The architecture is anchor-based: during training the local high-resolution branch crops faces using ground-truth boxes, while at inference it relies on the global branch's predicted boxes, making the entire pipeline a single forward pass.

## Method

GazeOnce360 is a dual-resolution anchor-based CNN that processes the fisheye image at two scales in parallel: a low-resolution (512) global branch captures scene layout and detects face bounding boxes, while a high-resolution (1024) local branch extracts fine-grained ocular features from cropped faces. Rotational convolution is inserted at the top FPN level to enforce rotation invariance under fisheye distortion, outperforming both standard and deformable convolutions. The two branches are fused via a cross-attention mechanism where low-resolution spatial positions act as queries and pooled high-resolution facial descriptors serve as keys and values, with a per-face spatial mask restricting attention to the relevant region. Multi-task supervision (gaze, head pose, distance, face/eye landmarks) is applied jointly, with eye landmark regression providing precise ocular geometry cues that are only available through the synthetic MetaHuman pipeline. The model is trained end-to-end on the newly introduced MPSGaze360 dataset (23,496 fisheye images, 1–7 people per frame, rendered in Unreal Engine 5), achieving 10.39° gaze error at 16.23 FPS under cross-scene/cross-identity evaluation, compared to 18.96° at 4.23 FPS for the multi-stage GAM360 baseline.

## Results

On the standard evaluation split of MPSGaze360, GazeOnce360 achieves a gaze direction error of 8.945° with 99.81% detection precision and 99.33% recall. Under the challenging cross-scene + cross-identity (D2) setting, the model maintains a gaze error of 10.39° at 16.23 FPS. The dual-resolution architecture matches the full-resolution model's accuracy (8.968° vs. 8.945°) while delivering 22% higher inference speed (16.23 vs. 13.30 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is GAM360 [8], a multi-stage pipeline that detects faces in fisheye images, projects each to a frontal perspective, and estimates gaze per-face. GazeOnce360 substantially outperforms GAM360 on the D2 cross-scene + cross-identity benchmark, reducing gaze error from 18.96° to 10.39° (a 45% relative reduction) and adjusted gaze error from 18.76° to 9.99°, while running nearly 4× faster (16.23 vs. 4.23 FPS). The end-to-end design eliminates the cumulative errors and face-splitting issues inherent in GAM360's reprojection step. Additionally, an internal ablation shows that rotational convolution outperforms deformable convolution (10.39° vs. 11.05° gaze error under D2), confirming that explicit rotational modeling is more effective than spatially adaptive kernels for fisheye distortion.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the anchor-based multi-person gaze estimation framework of GazeOnce [36] to the upward-facing fisheye setting, incorporating rotational convolution [35] to handle the severe geometric distortion inherent in equidistant fisheye projections. It departs from the multi-stage pipeline of GAM360 [8]—which detects faces, reprojects them to frontal views, and estimates gaze separately—by proposing a single end-to-end dual-resolution network that fuses global low-resolution context with high-resolution local eye crops via cross-attention. The accompanying MPSGaze360 dataset is the first synthetic resource purpose-built for this camera configuration, filling a gap left by existing gaze datasets (EYEDIAP, MPIIGaze, Gaze360, GazeGene) that all assume perspective cameras.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing both the first large-scale synthetic dataset and the first end-to-end model for 360° multi-person gaze from a tabletop fisheye camera, this work opens a practical sensing configuration for collaborative workspaces, service robots, and reception counters that was previously limited to cumbersome multi-device or multi-stage pipelines. Key limitations remain: performance degrades under extreme head poses and large subject-to-camera distances, and the model is trained entirely on synthetic data, leaving cross-domain robustness to real-world lighting, occlusion, and texture variation as an open question. The finding that eye-landmark supervision outperforms combined face-and-eye landmark supervision suggests that coarse geometric cues can be counterproductive, a nuance worth exploring in future multi-task gaze architectures.
