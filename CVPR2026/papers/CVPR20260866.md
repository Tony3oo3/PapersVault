## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting represents scenes as discrete anisotropic primitives, which causes abrupt color and opacity transitions at object boundaries during segmentation, producing aliasing artifacts and inaccurate edge delineation. Existing mask-based 3DGS segmentation methods either ignore this discretization or simply remove boundary Gaussians, the latter degrading visual quality. The paper addresses how to enforce spatial continuity at semantic boundaries without sacrificing the rendering fidelity of the explicit Gaussian representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260866/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes a pre-trained 3DGS model (with per-Gaussian position, covariance, opacity, and SH coefficients), 2D segmentation masks from a model like SAM applied to multi-view images, and calibrated camera intrinsics/extrinsics. It outputs refined per-Gaussian continuous mask labels in (0,1) that are alpha-composited into 2D pixel-space masks, along with a jointly optimized lightweight NeRF module that provides continuous density and color predictions specifically in boundary regions. The boundary region is defined as a conservative bounding box (expanded by δ=5 pixels) around Gaussians whose multi-view mask variance exceeds a threshold τ=0.6.

## Method

NG-GS operates in two stages. First, it identifies boundary Gaussians by computing the variance of multi-view mask signals per Gaussian and selecting those exceeding a threshold, then constructs a spatially continuous feature field via Gaussian-kernel RBF interpolation over K-NN neighbors of uniformly sampled query points along camera rays, augmented with multi-resolution hash encoding for multi-scale positional features. Second, a lightweight NeRF module receives the hash-encoded features as primary input and the RBF-interpolated features as a conditioning vector that modulates every hidden layer via learned scale (γ) and shift (β) parameters, predicting volume density and RGB color in the boundary region. A joint optimization aligns the NeRF and 3DGS outputs through four losses: a boundary alignment loss penalizing color and opacity discrepancies plus local color variance, a spatial continuity loss enforcing smoothness between neighboring boundary Gaussians, a gradient smoothness loss via Jacobian products, and a mask loss weighted by NeRF volumetric density to focus supervision on boundary pixels.

## Results

On NVOS, NG-GS achieves 84.7% B-mIoU, 99.2% mAcc, and 92.6% mIoU, outperforming the strongest baseline COB-GS (79.1% B-mIoU) by 5.6 points in boundary accuracy. On LERF-OVS, it reaches 72.8% B-mIoU and 82.9% mIoU, surpassing COB-GS by 4.4 points in B-mIoU. On ScanNet, the method attains 59.6% B-mIoU and 64.3% mIoU, a 6.8-point B-mIoU gain over COB-GS. The consistent B-mIoU improvements across all three benchmarks confirm the framework's effectiveness at resolving boundary discretization artifacts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NG-GS outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all three benchmarks, with the largest margins in B-mIoU. Against the closest competitor COB-GS [37], it gains +5.6% B-mIoU on NVOS (84.7 vs 79.1), +4.4% on LERF-OVS (72.8 vs 68.4), and +6.8% on ScanNet (59.6 vs 52.8), while also improving mAcc by 0.6–3.9 points. Among other mask-based methods, FlashSplat [33] and SAGA [2] trail by 5.6–8.3 B-mIoU points on LERF-OVS, and SA3D-GS [3] lags by 7.5 points. Feedforward methods (LSeg, LangSplat, LangSurf, LSM, LanScene-X) are substantially weaker, with B-mIoU ranging from 10.6 to 42.2 on LERF-OVS, underscoring that per-scene optimization with boundary-aware continuity modeling is critical for high-accuracy segmentation. In terms of efficiency, NG-GS matches COB-GS in inference time (0.8 s) and trains slightly faster (9 min vs 10 min on the fortress scene), so the accuracy gains come at no additional computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits within the mask-based 3DGS segmentation lineage (SAGA, FlashSplat, COB-GS), which lifts 2D masks from models like SAM into per-Gaussian labels via alpha compositing. Its key departure is explicitly acknowledging the discretization artifact at object boundaries and importing a continuous NeRF module—augmented with RBF interpolation and multi-resolution hash encoding—to refine boundary Gaussians rather than simply removing or splitting them as COB-GS does. The joint optimization with alignment and spatial continuity losses is a new architectural coupling between an explicit (3DGS) and implicit (NeRF) representation for segmentation, building on the volume-rendering machinery of NeRF and the hash-encoding efficiency of Instant-NGP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Boundary accuracy is a persistent pain point in 3DGS segmentation that affects downstream tasks like object manipulation and scene editing; the 5–7% B-mIoU gains across three benchmarks demonstrate that hybrid explicit-implicit modeling can close a gap that purely discrete methods struggle with. However, the approach requires a pre-trained 3DGS model plus an additional NeRF optimization pass, limiting real-time applicability, and the overall mIoU improvements remain modest (≤2.7%), suggesting the benefit is concentrated at boundaries rather than globally. Open questions include whether the framework extends to dynamic scenes, whether the NeRF module can be distilled into the Gaussians for single-pass inference, and whether the RBF+MRHE feature field generalizes beyond the relatively small NVOS/LERF-OVS scenes to large-scale ScanNet sequences.
