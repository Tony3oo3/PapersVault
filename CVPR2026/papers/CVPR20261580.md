## Problem

Existing unified multimodal models rely on external components—Vision Transformers for understanding and diffusion models or vision tokenizers for generation—creating multi-stage pipelines with architectural bottlenecks that limit early cross-modal fusion and inflate inference latency. No prior work has demonstrated that a single decoder-only autoregressive transformer, without any external vision encoder or diffusion module, can simultaneously achieve state-of-the-art performance across understanding, generation, and editing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261580/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For understanding, raw images are fed directly through a lightweight patch embedding layer (14×14 conv, 2×2 pixel unshuffle, 2-layer MLP) without any ViT, supporting native resolutions up to 1792×1792 plus a 448×448 thumbnail for global context. For generation, text prompts produce images at dynamic resolutions (side lengths 288–1776 px) via a multi-scale VAE (16× downsampling, 32 latent channels, bitwise quantizer) whose encoder is only used during training; at inference only the detokenizer reconstructs pixels. For editing, a reference image is processed by the same patch embedding layer (replacing a separate VAE tokenizer) and combined with a text instruction to condition autoregressive generation of new discrete visual tokens.

## Method

OneCAT is a pure decoder-only transformer initialized from Qwen2.5, augmented with a Modality-MoE layer that hard-routes tokens to three specialized FFN experts (Text, Understanding, Generation) while sharing QKV and attention layers for cross-modal alignment. Visual generation uses Next-Scale Prediction over multi-scale discrete tokens from an Infinity VAE, with a Scale-Aware Adapter (parallel low-rank rank-64 modules as skip connections over each Gen. FFN linear layer, one per VAE scale) to extract scale-specific representations for coarse-to-fine hierarchical synthesis. A multimodal versatile attention mechanism applies causal attention to text, full attention to continuous visual tokens, and block-causal attention across scales for discrete tokens. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) expert pretraining with understanding distillation from a custom MLLM teacher (InternViT + Qwen2.5 with a trained MLP connector, aligning all-layer hidden states via MSE) and parallel generation pretraining; (2) unified mid-training with all parameters unfrozen, incorporating SAA and native/dynamic resolutions; (3) unified SFT on curated high-quality data. The encoder-free design eliminates ViT and vision tokenizer at inference, yielding ~61% reduction in first-token latency for high-resolution understanding and ~10× speedup over diffusion-based BAGEL for 1024×1024 image generation.

## Results

OneCAT-3B achieves a SOTA GenEval overall score of 0.90 (vs. 0.89 for Mogao-7B with prompt rewriting and 0.88 for BAGEL-7B with rewriting) and a DPG-Bench overall of 84.53, surpassing Janus-Pro-7B (84.19) and Mogao-7B (84.33). On multimodal understanding, OneCAT-3B sets new encoder-free SOTA on text-centric VQA benchmarks (AI2D 77.8, ChartQA 81.2, InfoVQA 64.8, DocVQA 91.2) and leads unified models on MME-P (1630) and MM-Vet (52.2). On ImgEdit-Bench, OneCAT-3B scores 3.43 overall, competitive with the best unified model OmniGen2 (3.44). Inference efficiency is dramatically improved: OneCAT-3B generates a 1024×1024 image in 2.85 s (T2I) and 4.61 s (editing), roughly 10× faster than BAGEL-7B, and reduces first-token latency by 61% on 1792×1792 inputs versus Qwen2.5-VL-3B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On text-to-image generation, OneCAT-3B (GenEval 0.90, DPG-Bench 84.53) outperforms all unified baselines including Mogao-7B (0.89/84.33, both with LLM prompt rewriting), BAGEL-7B (0.88/–, with rewriting), Janus-Pro-7B (0.80/84.19), and Tar-7B (0.84/84.19), while using only 3B activated parameters versus 7–8B for competitors. On image editing (ImgEdit-Bench), OneCAT-3B (3.43) is marginally behind OmniGen2 (3.44) but clearly ahead of BAGEL-7B (3.20), UniWorld-V1-20B (3.26), and all editing-only models (≤3.06). For multimodal understanding, OneCAT-3B leads all unified models on MME-P (1630 vs. 1620 for Show-o2-7B) and MM-Vet (52.2 vs. 50.0 for Janus-Pro-7B), and achieves the best VQA average (75.3) among all compared models; however, it trails Qwen2.5-VL-3B (encoder-based, 4T training tokens vs. 0.5T for OneCAT) on some benchmarks such as MMMU (41.9 vs. 53.1) and MMB (78.8 vs. 79.1). In inference efficiency, OneCAT-3B is ~10× faster than BAGEL-7B for 1024×1024 generation (2.85 s vs. 26.29 s for T2I) and reduces TTFT by 61% on 1792×1792 inputs relative to Qwen2.5-VL-3B (0.225 s vs. 0.583 s), attributable to the encoder-free design and multi-scale AR mechanism.

## Relation to Prior Work

OneCAT departs from the dominant unified-model paradigm (BAGEL, Mogao, Janus-Pro) that couples a Mixture-of-Transformers with a separate diffusion head and external ViT/vision-tokenizer, replacing all of these with a single decoder-only transformer whose only modality-specific component is an FFN-level Mixture-of-Experts. It builds directly on Qwen2.5 as the LLM backbone, Infinity's multi-scale VAE for discrete visual tokenization, and Next-Scale Prediction for hierarchical coarse-to-fine generation, but is the first to integrate multi-scale autoregressive generation natively inside an LLM via a Scale-Aware Adapter. Compared to encoder-free understanding-only models (EVE, VoRA, Mono-InternVL), OneCAT extends the encoder-free principle to generation and editing, and introduces a custom-teacher distillation strategy that matches the student's LLM backbone for more stable knowledge transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a pure autoregressive, encoder-free decoder can match or exceed diffusion-based unified models on generation benchmarks (GenEval 0.90, DPG-Bench 84.53) while being roughly 10× faster at 1024×1024, challenging the working assumption that iterative denoising is necessary for high-quality image synthesis within a unified LLM. The practical efficiency gains (61% lower TTFT for understanding, 89–90% lower generation latency) make high-resolution multimodal inference substantially more accessible. Key limitations include a training-data gap (0.5T vs. 4T tokens for understanding) that leaves a measurable performance gap versus Qwen2.5-VL, and the generation quality at very high resolutions (>1024) remains to be validated. Open questions include whether the simple FFN-level MoE scales as well as full-transformer MoT at much larger model sizes, and whether pure AR can close the remaining quality gap with diffusion at 2K+ resolutions.
