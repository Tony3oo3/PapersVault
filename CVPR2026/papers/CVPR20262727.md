## Problem

Existing 3D vision-language models face two critical limitations: (1) scene tokenization pipelines rely on downsampling or single-scale representations, sacrificing fine-grained geometric details while failing to capture abstract global structure needed for multi-scale spatial reasoning; and (2) methods are typically task-specific with limited support for human-interactive prompting, preventing a single architecture from unifying diverse 3D understanding tasks such as VQA, dense captioning, and referring segmentation.

## Input / Output

The model consumes raw high-resolution 3D point clouds without any downsampling, multi-view 2D RGB images (projected onto NDT cell centers for color), natural language instructions, and optional user visual prompts (clicks, bounding boxes, masks). Outputs are generated as free-form text (VQA answers, dense captions) through the LLM, and 3D segmentation masks are produced when the LLM emits a special [SEG] token, which triggers MSDec to decode a per-point mask over the finest-scale NDT cells. The NDT cell descriptor is a 15-dimensional vector (3D mean, 3×3 covariance, RGB), and the LLM endpoint is LLaVA-1.5-7B with 850 learnable scene queries.

## Method

NDTokenizer3D introduces a three-stage tokenization pipeline: (1) a multi-scale Normal Distributions Transform partitions the raw point cloud into Gaussian cells at R resolutions, where fine-scale cells preserve local geometry via per-cell mean/covariance statistics and coarse-scale cells encode global scene structure; (2) a Point Transformer v3 encoder extracts multi-scale features from these NDT descriptors; (3) a Multi-Scale NDT Decoder (MSDec), a transformer decoder with R layers, progressively fuses cross-scale features from coarse to fine using learnable queries that attend to each scale's features as Key/Value, yielding holistic scene tokens projected into LLM space. Crucially, MSDec is repurposed as a unified interface: user visual prompts are average-pooled into an additional query processed through the same decoder to form a guidance token, and a [SEG] token from the LLM is decoded through MSDec plus a mask head to produce 3D segmentation masks. Training proceeds in two stages—pre-training the encoder and MSDec on 3D instance segmentation with CLIP-based 2D-to-3D feature alignment, followed by multi-task instruction tuning (with LoRA on the LLM) on combined referring segmentation, VQA, and dense captioning data.

## Results

NDTokenizer3D achieves 46.0 mIoU on Multi3DRefer (3D Referring Segmentation), surpassing the closest generalist baseline 3D-LLaVA (42.7) by +3.3. On ScanQA (3D VQA), it attains 98.6 CiDEr and 19.4 METEOR, exceeding the second-best by +6.0 and +1.0 respectively. On SQA3D it reaches 54.4 EM / 57.1 EM-R, and on Scan2Cap (Dense Captioning) it scores 79.0 CiDEr@0.5. On the 3D-POPE hallucination benchmark, NDTokenizer3D achieves the highest accuracy across all three settings (84.12% Random, 75.51% Popular, 72.03% Adversarial), outperforming 3D-LLaVA by 3.8–4.5 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NDTokenizer3D is compared against seven other generalist 3D VLMs: Spatial 3D-LLM, LEO, Scene-LLM, Chat-Scene, Grounded 3D-LLM, Oryx MLLM, LSceneLLM, and 3D-LLaVA. On 3D Referring Segmentation (Multi3DRefer), NDTokenizer3D wins decisively with 46.0 mIoU vs. 3D-LLaVA's 42.7 (+3.3); no other generalist baseline reports this metric. On ScanQA, NDTokenizer3D leads in CiDEr (98.6 vs. LEO's 101.4 under a different setting, and 92.6 for 3D-LLaVA) and METEOR (19.4 vs. 18.4 for 3D-LLaVA), while 3D-LLaVA edges ahead by 0.1 on BLEU-4. On SQA3D, Chat-Scene holds a slight advantage in EM (54.6 vs. 54.4) and EM-R (57.5 vs. 57.1), but NDTokenizer3D is competitive within 0.2–0.4 points. On Scan2Cap, NDTokenizer3D achieves the best CiDEr@0.5 (79.0) and ties 3D-LLaVA on Rouge-L@0.5 (57.7), while LEO and Chat-Scene lead on BLEU-4@0.5 (38.2) and METEOR@0.5 (28.0) respectively. On the 3D-POPE hallucination benchmark, NDTokenizer3D outperforms both 3D-LLaVA and LEO across all three sampling settings (Random, Popular, Adversarial) by 3.8–4.5 accuracy points over 3D-LLaVA, indicating more faithful scene representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the Normal Distributions Transform (NDT) from SLAM [49] as a multi-scale 3D scene representation, departing from the dominant downsampling and superpoint-pooling strategies used by prior 3D VLMs such as 3D-LLaVA [15], LEO [22], and Chat-Scene [21]. While those methods either compress point clouds into single-scale tokens or decompose scenes into object sequences, NDTokenizer3D constructs Gaussian-statistic cells at multiple resolutions to simultaneously preserve fine-grained geometry and global context without aggressive downsampling. The Multi-Scale NDT Decoder (MSDec) further unifies three previously separate functions—scene tokenization, interactive user prompting, and segmentation mask decoding—into a single transformer decoder, contrasting with prior systems that attach task-specific heads or modules for each capability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a single decoder can serve as both the scene tokenizer and the interface for human-interactive prompting and spatial prediction, the paper offers a streamlined architectural template for generalist 3D VLMs that avoids the proliferation of task-specific components. The substantial hallucination reduction on 3D-POPE (Table 2) suggests that information-preserving tokenization directly improves LLM grounding fidelity, a finding relevant to any 3D VLM design. However, evaluation is confined to indoor ScanNet scenes with a 7B LLM, leaving open questions about scalability to large-scale outdoor environments, higher-resolution point clouds, and whether the NDT cell formulation generalizes to other 3D representations (meshes, Gaussian splatting, NeRFs).
