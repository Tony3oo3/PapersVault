## Problem

Multimodal LLMs struggle with multi-view spatial reasoning, where models must establish object correspondences across different viewpoints and infer coherent scene semantics. The paper identifies two specific failures: Supervised Fine-Tuning memorizes annotated transformation patterns but cannot generalize to view shifts (dropping from 84.2% to 30.9% TAcc on OOD TVR), while vanilla RL without cold-start data fails to reliably identify key referential objects and produces unstructured outputs. The core question is how to structure RL so that models spontaneously learn human-like cross-view object anchoring and spatial mapping.

## Input / Output

The model receives two or more RGB images of the same scene captured from different viewpoints (e.g., center/left/right views, or initial/final states) along with a text question. For the TVR task, the output is a sequence of (object_index, attribute, value) transformation triplets; for real-world benchmarks, the output is a structured three-step reasoning chain—per-view referential analysis with [object1, object2, relation] triplets, cross-view spatial mapping, and final answer inference—wrapped in think and answer tags. The base model is Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and training data spans 4.1k (SFT) and 19.2k (RL) samples drawn from MindCube and SPAR-7M.

## Method

STAR-R1 is a two-stage training framework built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Stage 1 performs process-supervised SFT using Gemini-2.5-Pro-generated Chain-of-Thoughts that explicitly follow a three-step paradigm (per-view referential analysis → cross-view spatial mapping → answer inference), retaining only samples with correct final answers to ensure supervision quality. Stage 2 applies RL with a composite reward R_total = R_ans + R_ref, where R_ref encourages accurate identification of key referential objects across views and R_ans rewards final answer correctness, enabling the model to explore diverse reasoning trajectories while maintaining cross-view grounding. For the TVR exploratory task, the authors extend GRPO with a dense per-transformation reward (5.0/1.5/0.5 for exact/partial/index-only matches) plus a dual punishment mechanism that penalizes both incorrect predictions (−1.0 each) and under-prediction of transformation count, preventing reward hacking through exhaustive enumeration. The key empirical finding motivating this design is that RL-trained models spontaneously establish cross-view object correspondences in 81% of OOD cases versus 67% for ID, revealing an inherent tendency toward exhaustive cross-view verification that SFT lacks.

## Results

On the TVR benchmark, STAR-R1 (7B) achieves 61.4% Total Accuracy, 0.8 Attribute Difference, and 0.3 Normalized Difference, surpassing the strongest closed-source model o3 (36.0% TAcc) by 25.4% and GPT-4o (23.5%) by 37.9%. On MindCube-Tiny, STAR-R1 reaches 98.5% on Rotation (vs. 53.0% prior SOTA), 82.0% on Among, and 82.8% on Around. On SPAR-Bench, it scores 86.0% on ObjRel-OC-MV and 76.7% on ObjRel-OO-MV, outperforming methods trained on over 10× more data by 22.0% and 17.7% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAR-R1 (7B) decisively outperforms all closed-source models on TVR: it beats o3 (36.0% TAcc) by 25.4%, GPT-4o (23.5%) by 37.9%, and Gemini-1.5-Pro (15.9%) by 45.5%. Among open-source baselines, it surpasses the largest Qwen2.5-VL-32B (9.1% TAcc) by over 52 points and InternVL2.5-8B (3.3%) by 58 points on TVR. On MindCube-Tiny Rotation, STAR-R1 achieves 98.5% versus Claude-4-Sonnet's 48.4% and the prior open-source SOTA of 53.0%, a 45.5% absolute gain. On SPAR-Bench, STAR-R1 (86.0/76.7) exceeds GPT-4o (58.0/46.0) by 28.0/30.7 points and the prior SOTA (64.0/59.0) by 22.0/17.7 points, despite being trained on only 4.1k+19.2k samples versus over 10× more data for competing methods. The SFT-only variant (STAR-SFT) already provides substantial gains over the base model, but the full two-stage STAR-R1 further improves Rotation by 44.5% over STAR-SFT, confirming the critical role of RL in cross-view spatial mapping.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-for-reasoning paradigm (GRPO from DeepSeekMath, and its multimodal adaptations in MM-Eureka, LMM-R1, Video-R1) to the specific and underexplored domain of multi-view spatial understanding. It departs from prior RL-for-MLLM work in two key ways: (1) introducing a structured three-step reasoning trajectory (per-view referential analysis → cross-view spatial mapping → answer inference) as the target behavior, and (2) designing task-specific dense rewards that decompose correctness into referential object selection and final answer accuracy, rather than relying on binary outcome signals. The SFT cold-start stage repurposes Gemini-2.5-Pro as a CoT generator with outcome-based filtering, following the process-supervised data curation pattern seen in recent reasoning-focused MLLM training, but applied specifically to spatial transformation tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic evidence that RL, when properly structured, can induce qualitatively different spatial reasoning behaviors in MLLMs—specifically exhaustive cross-view object verification that resembles human anchoring strategies—rather than the superficial pattern-matching that SFT produces. This opens a path toward more interpretable and robust spatial reasoning in MLLMs, potentially transferable to embodied AI and 3D scene understanding. However, significant gaps remain: MMSI-Bench accuracy (31.4%) is still far below human level (97.2%), results are demonstrated only on a 7B base model, and the structured CoT format may constrain the model's reasoning flexibility. Open questions include whether the referential-aware RL paradigm scales to larger models, more views, or continuous 3D scenes, and whether the gains persist when the structured format is removed at inference time.
