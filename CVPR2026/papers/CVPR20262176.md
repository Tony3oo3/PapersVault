## Problem

Visual localization on resource-constrained edge devices (smart glasses, drones) requires small models, but smaller models suffer significant accuracy drops. The paper introduces the asymmetric localization setting—where a large teacher processes database images offline and a lightweight student processes queries online—but no prior method has addressed how to match features extracted by two different models without resorting to heavy learned matchers like SuperGlue or LightGlue.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262176/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image processed online by the student (0.04M–0.13M parameter CNN) and pre-mapped database images processed offline by the teacher (SiLK or SuperPoint, ~1M parameters). Each model outputs N keypoints with associated detector confidence scores and D-dimensional descriptors; the student's features are matched directly against the teacher's database features via parameterless mutual nearest neighbor matching, and the resulting correspondences are used to estimate a 6-DoF SE(3) camera pose. No learned matcher is invoked at inference time, keeping the online compute identical to a standalone tiny model.

## Method

AsymLoc aligns a frozen teacher and a trainable student through two complementary losses computed on synthetic image pairs with known homographies. First, a geometric matching loss constructs a mutual matching matrix that multiplies detector confidences with row- and column-softmax-normalized descriptor similarities, then applies negative log-likelihood on ground-truth correspondences filtered by a teacher confidence threshold. Second, a joint detector-descriptor distillation loss builds detector-weighted similarity matrices for both student-teacher and teacher-teacher pairs (scaling each descriptor dot product by the corresponding detector confidence divided by a temperature), converts them to probability distributions via row and column softmax, and minimizes the sum of KL divergences between the two distributions. The key insight is that alignment must occur in the joint detector-descriptor space—where detection reliability modulates descriptor similarity—rather than treating detection and description independently, which naive feature-level distillation fails to capture. The total loss is a weighted sum of the two terms, and the student is trained for 50 epochs on COCO-derived homography pairs with the teacher held frozen.

## Results

With a SiLK teacher (1M params) and a 0.13M student, AsymLoc achieves 0.60/0.84 homography accuracy on HPatches (vs. 0.62/0.86 for the teacher), 32.9/48.9 AUC on ScanNet (vs. 34.1/50.2), 0.51 MLA on IMC2022 (vs. 0.56), and 83.3/87.8 (Day) / 71.2/84.4 (Night) on Aachen (vs. 87.2/91.5 / 74.5/86.8), while using only 6.6 GFLOPs vs. 47.3 for the teacher. At the extreme 0.04M student size (25× smaller), AsymLoc still retains 95.5% of the SiLK teacher's Aachen accuracy and 93% of the SuperPoint teacher's accuracy. Across all four datasets and both teacher architectures, AsymLoc consistently outperforms every asymmetric distillation baseline (AML, RKD, CSD, D3Still) and the symmetric Standard setup at the same inference cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AsymLoc outperforms all four adapted asymmetric distillation baselines (AML, RKD, CSD, D3Still) across nearly every metric on all four datasets; the sole exception is D3Still edging out AsymLoc by 0.1% on ScanNet @20° (49.0 vs. 48.9). Against the symmetric Standard setup (same student size, no teacher), AsymLoc yields consistent gains of 3–7% on HPatches, 3–5 AUC points on ScanNet, and 2–4 points on Aachen, with no added inference cost. Naive feature-level distillation (both symmetric and asymmetric) provides negligible improvement over Standard, confirming that direct descriptor alignment is insufficient for cross-model compatibility. Compared to the much larger reference pipelines, AsymLoc with a 0.13M student uses 7× fewer FLOPs than SiLK (6.6 vs. 47.3 GFLOPs) and 10× fewer than SuperPoint+LightGlue (6.6 vs. 63.3 GFLOPs), while LoFTR (28M params, 223 GFLOPs) achieves higher absolute accuracy but at 34× the compute cost. The 0.04M student variant (1.97 GFLOPs) retains 95.5% of the SiLK teacher's Aachen accuracy, demonstrating that AsymLoc scales gracefully down to ultra-compact edge-deployable models.

## Relation to Prior Work

AsymLoc sits at the intersection of knowledge distillation for local features and the asymmetric image retrieval literature (AML, CSD, D3Still), but departs from both in a key way: prior asymmetric work targets global descriptors for retrieval, whereas this paper addresses the local detector-descriptor matching pipeline where both *where to look* and *how to match* must remain compatible across heterogeneous models. The method builds on the joint detector-descriptor formulation of SiLK and SuperPoint, and on the mutual nearest-neighbor matching paradigm, but introduces a novel probabilistic alignment objective in the joint detector-descriptor space coupled with a geometry-driven matching loss. The authors explicitly position this as the first work to apply an asymmetric framework to the image-matching step of visual localization, as opposed to the well-studied asymmetric retrieval step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for edge deployment: a 0.04M-parameter student achieves ~95% of a 1M-parameter teacher's localization accuracy at 25× fewer parameters and ~7× fewer FLOPs, making real-time 6-DoF localization feasible on smart glasses and single-board computers without learned matchers. An open question is whether the approach generalizes beyond CNN backbones and synthetic COCO-based training to more diverse architectures (e.g., transformer-based detectors) and real-world domain shifts. The paper also leaves open whether the joint detector-descriptor distillation space can be extended to handle learned matchers or dense matching methods in the asymmetric setting, and whether the gains persist at even smaller model scales or with larger teacher-student capacity gaps.
