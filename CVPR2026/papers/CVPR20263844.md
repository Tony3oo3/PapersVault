## Problem

Self-supervised pre-training has driven rapid progress in language, 2D, and video foundation models, yet learning 3D-aware representations from unlabeled multi-view images remains largely unexplored. Existing 3D vision models rely on supervised training with SfM-derived pseudo-labels that are imperfect and unscalable, while the prior self-supervised approach RayZer uses implicit latent-space view synthesis that permits shortcut solutions (e.g., frame interpolation) rather than genuine 3D understanding.

## Input / Output

E-RayZer takes 10 sparse-view RGB images, split into 5 reference views and 5 target views, and predicts shared camera intrinsics plus per-view extrinsics (SE(3) poses) for all 10 images. From the reference views it regresses per-pixel 3D Gaussian parameters (ray distance, quaternion orientation, spherical harmonic color, scale, opacity) that collectively encode scene geometry; the target views are then rendered via differentiable Gaussian splatting under self-predicted cameras for photometric self-supervision. The learned backbone features serve as 3D-aware representations transferable to downstream tasks such as depth estimation, pose estimation, and optical flow.

## Method

E-RayZer replaces RayZer's implicit latent scene tokens with explicit 3D Gaussians, injecting a lightweight 3D inductive bias that eliminates shortcut solutions and yields geometrically grounded poses and representations. A VGGT-style multi-view transformer with alternating local-global attention performs pairwise relative pose prediction (canonical-to-target camera tokens), and image index embeddings are removed to prevent frame-interpolation shortcuts. Posed reference views are encoded into latent tokens via multi-view aggregation, then decoded by a lightweight head into per-pixel Gaussian parameters along each camera ray; a modified gsplat renderer supports gradient back-propagation to intrinsics. To stabilize training from scratch, a fine-grained curriculum based on visual overlap (measured unsupervised via DINOv2 cosine similarity or UFM covisibility) schedules samples from high-overlap (near-identity poses) to low-overlap (large camera motion), providing a unified difficulty metric that also harmonizes heterogeneous data sources without manual per-dataset tuning.

## Results

On in-domain DL3DV pose estimation, E-RayZer achieves RPA@5° = 72.0 and RPA@15° = 88.4, approaching supervised VGGT* (79.6 / 94.2) while using zero 3D annotations. On out-of-domain datasets, E-RayZer surpasses VGGT* on WildRGB-D (51.1 vs 32.5 @5°), CamLand (18.1 vs 11.1 @5°), and BlendedMVS (22.9 vs 17.0 @5°). Initializing VGGT* with E-RayZer weights (E-RayZer→VGGT*) yields further gains, reaching 87.3 @5° on DL3DV and 56.2 @5° on WildRGB-D. On frozen-backbone depth estimation (ScanNet++), E-RayZer achieves AbsRel = 0.116 and δ<1.25 = 87.1, outperforming RayZer (0.161 / 79.3) and all 2D pre-training baselines (DINOv3, CroCo v2, VideoMAE V2, Perception Encoder).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RayZer (the implicit self-supervised predecessor), E-RayZer dramatically outperforms on pose estimation: on DL3DV with 7-dataset training, E-RayZer reaches RPA@5° = 59.9 vs RayZer's 0.0, while achieving comparable NVS PSNR (19.7 vs 20.8), confirming that explicit 3D Gaussians yield geometrically grounded poses rather than interpolation shortcuts. Against SPFSplat (initialized from supervised MASt3R), E-RayZer wins on most pose metrics (e.g., 40.3 vs 31.5 @5° on WildRGB-D) despite being truly self-supervised. Against supervised VGGT*, E-RayZer is competitive in-domain but surpasses it on several OOD datasets (WildRGB-D, CamLand, BlendedMVS), and pre-training VGGT* with E-RayZer weights yields the best overall results. On frozen-backbone depth/pose probing (Table 3), E-RayZer outperforms all 2D pre-training baselines (DINOv2, DINOv3, CroCo v2, VideoMAE V2, Perception Encoder) by large margins—e.g., RPA@5° of 13.8 vs 0.1–4.7 on ScanNet++—and also beats RayZer (4.7). On pairwise flow (Table 4), E-RayZer (EPE 1.254) is slightly behind RayZer (1.105) but ahead of CroCo v2 (1.273) and VideoMAE V2 (2.028), as RayZer's implicit formulation is naturally suited to low-level motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

E-RayZer directly extends RayZer (ICCV 2025), which performed self-supervised view synthesis through implicit latent-space rendering, by replacing the latent scene representation with explicit 3D Gaussians (Kerbl et al., 2023) and a differentiable Gaussian renderer (gsplat). Architecturally, it adopts VGGT's (CVPR 2025) multi-view transformer with alternating local-global attention but modifies it for pairwise pose prediction and removes image index embeddings to prevent frame-interpolation shortcuts. The key conceptual departure is from implicit to explicit 3D modeling in a self-supervised setting, combined with a visual-overlap-based curriculum that replaces RayZer's fixed frame-interval scheduling, enabling principled scaling across heterogeneous data sources without 3D annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

E-RayZer demonstrates that zero-annotation self-supervised 3D reconstruction can match or surpass fully supervised models (VGGT) on pose estimation and out-of-domain generalization, challenging the assumption that dense 3D supervision is necessary for geometrically grounded representations. Its learned features outperform all prior visual pre-training models (DINOv3, CroCo v2, VideoMAE V2, Perception Encoder) on 3D downstream tasks, establishing a viable paradigm for spatial visual pre-training. Limitations include restriction to static scenes and an assumption of continuous video with uniform camera motion; extending to dynamic scenes and sparse/unstructured image collections, as well as scaling to internet-scale data, are the most impactful open directions.
