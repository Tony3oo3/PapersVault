## Problem

Existing action tokenizers for VLA models are trained holistically over full manipulation trajectories that blend heterogeneous skills (e.g., translational movement, rotation, grasping), forcing a single quantizer to compromise across conflicting kinematic patterns and temporal scales. This mixed-signal optimization degrades representation fidelity and prevents downstream VLAs from grounding observations to precise action primitives.

## Input / Output

The VLA model consumes an RGB frame (640×480), a 7-DoF proprioceptive state vector, and a natural-language instruction, producing a k-step action chunk (k=8 for bimanual RoboTwin, k=4 for single-arm BridgeV2). Visual observations are encoded by SigLIP-SO400M into 256 continuous tokens, proprioceptive state is discretized into 256 bins per dimension following FAST, and actions are tokenized by MoEActok into discrete tokens from skill-specific codebooks. Special delimiter tokens (t_bos/t_eos, s_bos/s_eos, i_bos/i_eos, a_bos/a_eos) maintain modality boundaries in the autoregressive sequence.

## Method

MoEActok first applies k-means clustering on aggregate action-chunk representations (element-wise sum of the first 6 DoF dimensions plus endpoint gripper displacement) to partition actions into K skill clusters. A shared CNN+Transformer encoder maps each action chunk to a latent vector, which is then routed to one of K expert VQ quantizers (codebook size 2048) via the cluster assignment; skill-specific pre- and post-quantization adapters (MLP with ReLU) project between the shared latent space and each expert's specialized subspace before and after quantization. The VLA training is augmented with a skill-aware objective that first predicts the skill cluster h from multimodal context, then generates action tokens conditioned on h, decomposing the problem into coarse skill identification followed by fine-grained token prediction. Training proceeds in two stages: MoEActok is pre-trained with standard VQ-VAE losses (reconstruction + codebook + commitment), then the LLM backbone (Qwen2.5-0.5B) is fine-tuned with the frozen tokenizer using cross-entropy over the skill-conditioned autoregressive objective.

## Results

On the RoboTwin benchmark (12 bimanual manipulation tasks), MoEActok achieves an average success rate of 0.56, substantially outperforming VQ-VLA (0.45), VQ-BET (0.29), Binning (0.24), and FAST (0.17). On Simpler-Env (4 WidowX single-arm tasks), MoEActok reaches 0.38 average success rate versus 0.21 for VQ-VLA, 0.12 for FAST, 0.03 for Binning, and 0.02 for VQ-BET. In zero-shot real-world transfer on the AgileX Cobot Magic platform (3 tasks, 20 trials each), MoEActok achieves 0.37 average success rate compared to 0.28 for VQ-VLA and 0.15 for VQ-BET.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoEActok outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation setting. On RoboTwin, it leads VQ-VLA (the strongest baseline) by 11 percentage points (0.56 vs. 0.45) and Binning by 32 points (0.56 vs. 0.24). On Simpler-Env, the margin over VQ-VLA widens to 17 points (0.38 vs. 0.21), while VQ-BET and Binning perform near-zero (0.02 and 0.03 respectively), suggesting that single-quantizer approaches struggle severely with the heterogeneous skill distributions in single-arm tasks. In zero-shot real-world transfer, MoEActok achieves 12/20 on Click Bell and 9/20 on Place Container on Plate, compared to 10/20 and 7/20 for VQ-VLA, and 1/20 for Pick Diverse Bottles versus 0/20 for all baselines. The consistent advantage across simulation and real-world settings indicates that the MoE skill-specialized quantization provides a structural benefit beyond what residual VQ or frequency-domain tokenization can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoEActok extends the vector-quantization-based action tokenization line (VQ-BET, VQ-VLA) and the frequency-domain tokenization approach (FAST) by introducing a mixture-of-experts routing mechanism inspired by Switch Transformers. The key conceptual departure is that prior tokenizers train a single unified quantizer over all trajectories, which the authors argue creates representation conflicts when trajectories blend heterogeneous skills (e.g., locomotion vs. grasping). MoEActok addresses this by first applying k-means clustering to partition action chunks into skill groups, then assigning each cluster to a dedicated VQ expert, with adapters bridging the shared encoder-decoder and skill-specific quantization spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The skill-aware decomposition offers a principled way to handle the heterogeneous skill distributions inherent in manipulation datasets, and the coarse-to-fine training paradigm (predict skill cluster, then action tokens) provides an interpretable inductive bias that could transfer to other multi-skill domains. However, the approach relies on offline k-means clustering over the training data, raising questions about generalization to unseen skill combinations or out-of-distribution actions. Absolute success rates remain modest (0.56 on RoboTwin, 0.38 on Simpler-Env, 0.37 zero-shot real-world), and the method has only been validated with a single LLM backbone (Qwen2.5-0.5B) and up to 4 experts, leaving open whether the gains persist at scale or with more diverse embodiments.
