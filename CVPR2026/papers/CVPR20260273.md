## Problem

Existing MLLM-based web agents depend on handcrafted execution pipelines or costly human-annotated expert trajectories, making them rigid and poorly adapted to the diversity and dynamism of real-world websites. Moreover, prior methods largely ignore the agent's own cognitive boundaries—the specific actions and page elements it cannot reliably interpret—so there is no mechanism for the agent to autonomously identify and expand its limitations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260273/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes only pure visual input: webpage screenshots annotated with Set-of-Mark (SOM) numeric labels, deliberately excluding DOM trees, accessibility trees, or HTML metadata to match real-world conditions where such structural information is often unavailable. During exploration, the agent outputs a sequence of GUI actions (click, type, navigate) along with predicted outcomes and reasoning traces; the final deliverable is a fine-tuned MLLM policy plus the SCALE-20k dataset (15k single-step tasks, 3.8k multi-step tasks, 6.9k page-comprehension QA pairs) reverse-engineered from exploration traces across 19 real websites.

## Method

SCALE assigns three adversarial roles—Selector, Predictor, and Judger—all instantiated by the same MLLM, forming a self-contained feedback loop: the Selector deliberately targets unfamiliar or rarely-encountered UI elements to propose challenging actions, the Predictor forecasts the outcome with an explanation, and the Judger compares the prediction against the actual post-action observation. When the prediction is wrong (j_i = 0), the mismatched case is stored as training data and used to SFT both the Selector and Predictor, while the Judger remains fixed; when correct, the environment resets and the Selector proposes a new action, ensuring only boundary-revealing interactions generate learning signal. SCALE-Hop extends this local loop with a directed graph of visited web states (nodes keyed by URL + SSIM-verified visual similarity) and a verification-guided backtracking mechanism that marks nodes as Fully Explored after N random-action predictions all succeed, then routes the agent to the nearest under-explored node, preventing local traps and enabling global coverage.

## Results

SCALE achieves average task success rate improvements of 231.8% for InternVL2.5-8B and 176.3% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B over their respective zero-shot baselines. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, SCALE reaches 14.4% SR on Shopping, 12.0% on Classifieds, 4.8% on Reddit, and 7.9% on WebVoyager, while also achieving the lowest average steps (AS) in most domains (5.7, 4.1, 8.4, 15.0). The SCALE-20k dataset, when used to fine-tune an unrelated model (LLaVA-NeXT-8B), lifts its Shopping SR from 0% to 2.1% and Classifieds SR from 0% to 0.8%, confirming dataset generality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two open-source agent baselines, SCALE dominates: Qwen2.5-VL-7B + SCALE achieves 14.4% Shopping SR vs. Augvis's 4.3% and ViGoRL's 5.6%, and 12.0% Classifieds SR vs. 0.4% and 6.9% respectively. Compared to GPT-4o zero-shot (17.2/13.7/6.7/9.6 SR), SCALE with Qwen2.5-VL-7B (14.4/12.0/4.8/7.9) remains below on absolute SR but narrows the gap substantially over the zero-shot open-source baseline. Against GPT Trajectory Imitation, SCALE wins on Classifieds (12.0 vs. 10.7) and Reddit (4.8 vs. 3.3) for Qwen2.5-VL-7B, though GPT imitation edges ahead on Shopping (18.3 vs. 14.4); for InternVL2.5-8B, SCALE wins across all three VisualWebArena domains. SCALE clearly outperforms OS-Genesis on Qwen2.5-VL-7B across all four benchmarks (e.g., 14.4 vs. 11.2 Shopping, 7.9 vs. 6.7 WebVoyager) and on Classifieds for InternVL2.5-8B (6.4 vs. 3.0), though OS-Genesis slightly leads on Shopping (11.6 vs. 11.0) and Reddit (4.3 vs. 3.3) for InternVL2.5-8B. Tree Search is consistently the weakest baseline, with SCALE surpassing it by 3–10× on SR in nearly every domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms in web agent research—handcrafted execution pipelines (ReAct, Tree-of-Thought, world-model planning) and expert-trajectory fine-tuning (Mind2Web, OS-World, AGUVIS)—by proposing a fully self-driven exploration loop where a single MLLM assumes three adversarial roles (Selector, Predictor, Judger) to identify and expand its own cognitive boundaries. It also extends the unsupervised trajectory-generation line (OS-Genesis, Learn-by-Interact, OpenWebVoyager) by replacing random or task-templated exploration with targeted, self-cognitive-aware probing, and adds a graph-based global planning layer (SCALE-Hop with verification-guided backtracking) absent from prior exploration-based methods. The Set-of-Mark visual encoding and SFT-based iterative policy updates are standard components repurposed within this new self-play framing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SCALE framework offers a practical path toward reducing dependence on expensive human-annotated trajectories for web agents, and the SCALE-20k dataset (25k+ tasks across 19 real websites with three task types) provides a useful resource for the community. However, absolute success rates remain low (11–14% on VisualWebArena), raising questions about whether the self-adversarial loop scales to more complex, multi-page workflows or larger model families. Open questions include whether the Selector-Predictor-Judger dynamic degrades when the same model must simultaneously challenge and predict, whether SCALE-Hop's graph representation generalizes beyond the 19-website scope, and whether the 231.8% relative gain (from a ~4% baseline) translates to meaningful real-world utility at higher absolute performance levels.
