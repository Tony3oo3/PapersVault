## Problem

Caching-based acceleration methods designed for standard diffusion models fail when naively applied to autoregressive diffusion models (ARDMs) because approximation errors introduced in early segments propagate and accumulate across sequentially generated segments, causing progressive quality degradation in long videos. No existing caching framework accounts for the sequential dependency structure unique to ARDMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262116/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ARCache is a training-free inference-time acceleration layer applied on top of existing ARDMs, tested across three configurations: FramePack-F1 (reference image → 145 frames at dynamic resolution, 25 steps), SkyReels-V2 (text prompt → 257 frames at 960×544, 30 steps), and Matrix-Game (image + action → 113 frames at 1280×720, 50 steps). The method operates entirely at inference time by selectively reusing cached intermediate features and correcting residuals, without modifying model weights or requiring additional training data.

## Method

ARCache comprises two modules targeting error accumulation at different scales. History-Guided Cache (HGC) schedules cache refreshes by monitoring normalized L1 deviations in history tokens between adjacent denoising steps, using a cumulative threshold δ to trigger recomputation; this is motivated by Spearman correlation analysis showing history tokens correlate far more strongly with output changes than noisy tokens. Enhanced Residual Correction (ERC) mitigates inter-segment error drift by approximating each segment's residual via a first-order trajectory formulation, but replaces the segment-specific trajectory parameter with that computed from the first segment (which is free of historical errors), thereby correcting subsequent segments along a clean reference trajectory rather than amplifying contamination. This residual-level correction avoids the memory overhead of TaylorSeer's layer-wise feature forecasting while effectively suppressing cascading artifacts.

## Results

ARCache achieves 2.88×, 1.87×, and 3.13× speedups (fast mode) on FramePack-F1, SkyReels-V2, and Matrix-Game respectively, while maintaining PSNR of 24.34, 25.70, and 19.37. In slow mode, ARCache attains the highest fidelity with PSNR 28.13 (FramePack-F1), 29.10 (SkyReels-V2), and 22.77 (Matrix-Game), alongside the best task-specific scores of 88.82%, 77.47%, and 79.39%. The method consistently outperforms all baselines on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS across all three auto-regressive diffusion models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARCache outperforms PAB (I=2) across all three models in both speed and quality: on FramePack-F1, ARCache-fast achieves PSNR 24.34 vs. PAB's 21.19 at a comparable 2.88× vs. 2.86× speedup; on SkyReels-V2, the gap widens dramatically (PSNR 25.70 vs. 14.77), where PAB's static schedule causes severe degradation (task score drops to 62.07%). Against TeaCache, ARCache-slow consistently leads on fidelity (PSNR 28.13 vs. 26.16 on FramePack-F1; 29.10 vs. 26.65 on SkyReels-V2; 22.77 vs. 21.53 on Matrix-Game) while matching or exceeding its speedup. TaylorSeer is the weakest competitor: it OOMs on both SkyReels-V2 and Matrix-Game, and on FramePack-F1 it trails ARCache-fast by ~3 dB in PSNR (21.36 vs. 24.34) with substantially higher LPIPS (0.2023 vs. 0.1254). Overall, ARCache dominates in the speed–quality Pareto frontier, with its slow mode matching or exceeding the unaccelerated model's task score while its fast mode delivers 2–3× speedup with minimal quality loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-caching acceleration paradigm (PAB, TeaCache, TaylorSeer, DeepCache) from standard diffusion models to auto-regressive diffusion models (ARDMs), where sequential segment generation introduces a fundamentally different error-dynamics problem. The key departure is twofold: (1) HGC replaces the standard full-input fluctuation signal with history-token deviation for cache scheduling, motivated by a correlation analysis showing history inputs dominate output variation in ARDMs; and (2) ERC replaces TaylorSeer's layer-wise Taylor expansion with a lightweight residual-level correction that borrows the trajectory parameter from the first (error-free) segment to stabilize subsequent ones. The work is explicitly training-free and builds on the ARDM architectures of FramePack-F1, SkyReels-V2, and Matrix-Game without modifying their training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As ARDMs become the dominant paradigm for long-form and interactive video generation, efficient inference is a practical bottleneck; ARCache's 2.88×–3.13× speedups with minimal quality loss make real-time streaming and world-model applications more feasible. The paper's core insight—that history tokens, not the full model input, are the reliable signal for cache timing in ARDMs—provides a principled design principle for future ARDM-specific acceleration. A stated limitation is the reliance on a manually tuned threshold δ; the authors note that fully adaptive, threshold-free scheduling remains open. Broader open questions include whether the first-segment trajectory reference generalizes to models with very different segment lengths or to multi-modal conditioning beyond text and images.
