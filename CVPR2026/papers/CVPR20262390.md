## Problem

Split DNNs expose intermediate features during edge-to-cloud transmission, creating a direct attack surface for reconstructing private input images. Existing Feature Inversion Attack (FIA) methods suffer from white-box assumptions, heavy data dependence (requiring thousands of paired samples), and high computational cost from per-sample iterative optimization, limiting their practicality and making it difficult to assess the true extent of privacy leakage in real-world split computing deployments.

## Input / Output

The method takes intermediate feature tensors of varying spatial resolutions and channel dimensions from arbitrary split layers (e.g., AlexNet F-10, ResNet-50 L1-2/L4-2, Swin-B F3-2, YOLO11n M-8, DINOv2-B B-11) and produces a full-resolution RGB reconstruction of the original private image. The attack operates entirely in a black-box setting, requiring only query access to intermediate features without knowledge of the victim model's architecture or weights, and is trained on fewer than 4,096 image-feature pairs (less than 0.32% of ImageNet1K). Inference is a single forward pass, eliminating the thousands of iterative optimization steps required by prior methods.

## Method

FIA-Flow decouples the inversion mapping into a two-stage alignment-refinement pipeline. The first stage, LFSAM, maps task-specific intermediate features into a VAE latent space using a PixelShuffle-based spatialization layer, a hierarchical encoder-decoder backbone with self-attention, and a Feature Aggregation Network that fuses multi-scale features via 1×1 convolutions and skip connections. The second stage, DIFM, learns a deterministic vector field via flow matching that projects the structurally-aligned but off-manifold latent code onto the natural data manifold, correcting distributional mismatch. Because LFSAM already produces a starting point close to the target latent, the learned vector field is simple enough to be applied in a single forward Euler step (t=0 to t=1), enabling one-step high-fidelity inference. Training is two-stage: LFSAM is first trained with L2 latent-space and L1 image-space losses, then frozen while DIFM is trained with a flow-matching regression loss plus LPIPS and L1 reconstruction losses. DIFM is initialized from Stable Diffusion 2.1's U-Net with a rank-4 LoRA adapter, leveraging pre-trained generative priors while keeping the base network frozen.

## Results

FIA-Flow achieves state-of-the-art feature inversion across six victim models and layers. On ResNet-50 L1-2 (shallow, information-rich features), it reaches 71.3% top-1 classification accuracy and 0.100 LPIPS, outperforming SG-DIP (65.2% Acc, 0.193 LPIPS) and DIP (61.0% Acc, 0.236 LPIPS). On the deeper L4-2 layer where information loss is severe, FIA-Flow still attains 36.8% Acc and 0.397 LPIPS, while SG-DIP drops to 8.1% Acc and DIP to 27.3% Acc. On DINOv2-B (a foundation model), FIA-Flow achieves 42.8% Acc versus only 17.7% for SG-DIP and 1.3% for DIP, demonstrating broad applicability across CNN, Transformer, detection, and self-supervised architectures.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIA-Flow consistently outperforms all four baselines across every victim model and layer. Against M&V (Mahendran & Vedaldi, 2015), the gap is most dramatic: on DINOv2-B B-11, FIA-Flow achieves 42.8% Acc versus 0.1% for M&V, and on AlexNet F-10, 28.8% versus 0.0%. Compared to DIP (Ulyanov et al., 2018), FIA-Flow wins on LPIPS and Acc in nearly all settings; the largest margin appears on ResNet-50 L4-2 where FIA-Flow reaches 36.8% Acc and 0.397 LPIPS while DIP only achieves 27.3% Acc and 0.711 LPIPS. Against SG-DIP (Liang et al., 2025), the closest competitor, FIA-Flow still leads on Acc by 6.1 points on ResNet-50 L1-2 (71.3% vs. 65.2%) and by 28.7 points on L4-2 (36.8% vs. 8.1%), while also achieving lower LPIPS (0.100 vs. 0.193 on L1-2). AR (Rojas-Gomez et al., 2022) is only evaluated on AlexNet F-10, where FIA-Flow surpasses it by 24.7 points in Acc (28.8% vs. 4.1%) and 0.169 in LPIPS (0.405 vs. 0.574). Notably, FIA-Flow is the only method that is both black-box and one-step, whereas all baselines require either white-box access or per-sample iterative optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) from its standard generative role (sampling from a Gaussian prior) into a deterministic residual-correction mechanism that projects structurally-aligned features onto the data manifold in a single step. It departs from the dominant FIA literature (M&V, DIP, SG-DIP, AR), which relies on white-box access and per-sample iterative optimization, by introducing a black-box, one-step, data-efficient framework. The alignment-refinement decomposition—using a VAE latent space as an intermediate bridge between task-specific intermediate features and image space—is a novel architectural choice not present in prior FIA methods, which either optimize directly in pixel space or use diffusion from noise.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is significant: split DNN deployments, widely adopted for edge-cloud inference, face a more severe and harder-to-detect privacy threat than previously quantified, since the attack requires fewer than 4,096 training pairs and a single forward pass. The proposed LVLM-based metrics (LVLM-C, LVLM-PL) shift evaluation from pixel-level IQA toward human-perceivable semantic leakage, a direction likely to influence future privacy auditing. Open questions include whether the attack generalizes to non-visual modalities (audio, text), whether stronger defenses (e.g., feature quantization, cryptographic protocols) can close the gap, and whether the one-step flow-matching formulation extends to video or 3D reconstruction.
