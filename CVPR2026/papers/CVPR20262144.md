## Problem

Instruction-driven segmentation in remote sensing is fragmented across single-purpose tasks (referring, interactive, reasoning), with limited dataset scale and diversity, preventing unified cross-task understanding and open-world generalization in complex geospatial scenes.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 512×512 remote sensing image, a natural language instruction (ranging from ~3-word spatial references to ~24-word multi-step reasoning queries), and optionally point or bounding-box spatial cues for interactive segmentation. It produces pixel-level segmentation masks; in visual grounding experiments it also outputs tight bounding boxes. The training corpus spans 117 semantic categories across 0.05 m–153 m spatial resolutions, with instructions generated by GPT-4o and quality-controlled by InternVL3-78B and QwenVL2-72B.

## Method

UniGeoSeg couples a Swin-B visual encoder, a Phi-1.5 language backbone, and a Mask2Former pixel decoder, augmented by three mechanisms. Task-Adaptive Text Enhancement (TATE) applies distinct lightweight pathways per task: coordinate projection and linear fusion for interactive cues, single-query attention for referring keywords, and multi-query attention plus global linear aggregation with LayerNorm for reasoning. Latent Knowledge Memory (LKM) maintains N=4 learnable slots storing task-agnostic representations, retrieved via attention over LLM hidden states and blended with a fusion weight λ=0.2 to enable cross-task semantic sharing. Progressive Task Scheduling (PTS) dynamically shifts the sampling distribution from interactive toward reasoning samples over training, implementing a curriculum that balances task difficulty and data volume. The full model is trained for 3 epochs in bfloat16 on 8 A800 GPUs using AdamW with cosine decay, with the visual encoder frozen.

## Results

On GeoSeg-Bench, UniGeoSeg achieves state-of-the-art gIoU of 75.56 (interactive), 74.58 (referring), and 53.12 (reasoning), outperforming the best fine-tuned baseline SegEarth-R1 by 0.56, 1.60, and 1.56 points respectively. On EarthReason test, UniGeoSeg reaches 74.61 cIoU / 73.08 gIoU, surpassing the previous best (RemoteReasoner: 69.02 / 70.96) by 5.59 and 2.12 points. For zero-shot interactive segmentation on SIOR, UniGeoSeg attains 88.77 average gIoU, exceeding SAM2 (84.36) by 4.41 points, and on zero-shot visual grounding (RSVG-DIOR) it achieves 70.82 cIoU versus 31.28 for the prior best (RSVG-ZeroOV).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among fine-tuned baselines on GeoSeg-Bench, UniGeoSeg outperforms SegEarth-R1 (the strongest competitor) by 2.35 cIoU on interactive, 2.17 cIoU on referring, and 5.04 cIoU on reasoning segmentation, with the largest margin on the most challenging reasoning task. PSALM, while competitive on interactive (74.10 gIoU vs. 75.56), lags significantly on reasoning (49.59 vs. 53.12 gIoU). On EarthReason, UniGeoSeg surpasses RemoteReasoner by 5.59 cIoU and 2.12 gIoU, and on RRSIS-D it achieves 69.25 gIoU versus GeoPixel's 67.99. In zero-shot settings, UniGeoSeg beats SAM2 by 4.41 average gIoU on SIOR interactive segmentation and dramatically outperforms RSVG-ZeroOV on visual grounding (70.82 vs. 31.28 cIoU), indicating strong spatial generalization without task-specific training. All zero-shot baselines (LISA, PixelLM, PSALM, etc.) score below 20 gIoU on GeoSeg-Bench without fine-tuning, highlighting the difficulty of the benchmark and the advantage of the unified multi-task training paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper synthesizes three previously fragmented lines of work—referring segmentation (RRSIS-D, GeoPixel), interactive/visual-prompting segmentation (SAM, RemoteSAM), and geospatial reasoning segmentation (SegEarth-R1, EarthReason)—into a single unified framework. Architecturally, it follows the LLM-plus-vision-encoder-plus-mask-decoder pattern established by LISA and PSALM, but departs by introducing task-adaptive text enhancement (TATE) that routes instructions through different lightweight attention pathways depending on task type, and a latent knowledge memory (LKM) module for cross-task semantic sharing. The dataset construction pipeline repurposes GPT-4o and open-source VLMs (InternVL3, QwenVL2) for automated instruction generation and quality control, a strategy borrowed from general-purpose segmentation datasets but applied at unprecedented scale in the remote sensing domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GeoSeg-1M (590K images, 1.1M triplets) and GeoSeg-Bench fill a critical gap: prior RS instruction-driven datasets were small (5K–71K images) and single-task, making it impossible to train or fairly evaluate unified models. The benchmark reveals that even strong general-purpose LMMs (LISA, PixelLM, PSALM) score below 11 gIoU on GeoSeg-Bench without RS-specific fine-tuning, underscoring how poorly current models transfer to geospatial scenes. The persistent weakness on reasoning tasks (53 gIoU vs. 75 on interactive) suggests that long-context spatial reasoning in RS remains an open challenge. Future work could explore whether the unified formulation extends to video/temporal RS segmentation or multi-sensor (SAR, hyperspectral) inputs, and whether the LKM mechanism generalizes beyond the three tasks considered here.
