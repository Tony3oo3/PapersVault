## Problem

Multi-character narrative image generation suffers from identity blending, poor prompt following, and visual artifacts that existing evaluation metrics (CLIP, ArcFace, conventional reward models) fail to capture, creating a gap between automated scores and human perceptual judgment. General-purpose reward models exhibit distributional bias toward narrative-specific criteria, lack interpretability in their scoring rationale, and existing preference optimization methods over-optimize easily-tractable dimensions while neglecting others.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260650/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The generative model takes a narrative text prompt (describing actions, expressions, locations, and styles for 1–2 characters) together with high-resolution frontal portrait reference images and produces a multi-character narrative image. The NIREward model consumes a generated image, the narrative prompt, reference images, and a dimension-specific instruction (prompt following, identity consistency, or visual quality), and outputs a structured textual critique followed by a scalar reward score. The NI-RLHF dataset contains 10k preference pairs with multi-dimensional critiques, while NI-Bench provides 400 held-out pairs per dimension for evaluation.

## Method

NIREward is a critique-based reward model built on a Qwen2-VL MLLM backbone with two heads: a critique head that generates interpretable textual evaluations via chain-of-thought reasoning, and a reward head that produces scalar scores conditioned on the critique. Training uses a combined objective (token-level cross-entropy for critiques plus pairwise log-likelihood for rewards), with human-annotated critiques serving as intermediate reasoning to stabilize reward learning. ADPO then aligns the diffusion model (PhotoMaker V2) through four stages: sampling N images per prompt, scoring each with NIREward across three dimensions, constructing preference pairs via a Dominating Comparison Strategy (the winner must outperform the loser on every dimension simultaneously) combined with per-dimension rejection sampling, and optimizing with an adaptive β that scales with the mean reward margin to up-weight high-confidence pairs. This replaces the fixed β in Diffusion-DPO, ensuring balanced optimization across prompt following, identity consistency, and visual quality rather than over-fitting to the most easily-tractable signal.

## Results

On NI-Bench, NIREward achieves 86.07% preference accuracy on prompt following, 85.10% on identity consistency, and 83.00% on visual quality, outperforming all 7 baselines on at least two of the three dimensions. Most notably, NIREward exceeds GPT-4o-mini by 9.34% on prompt following and by 38.35% on identity consistency, while trailing it by 5.42% on visual quality. In the downstream optimization task (Table 2), ADPO with NIREward improves ID Sim from 28.83 (baseline) to 28.88 and NIREward identity consistency from −0.036 to +0.010, while all Diffusion-DPO variants degrade identity consistency relative to the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NIREward dominates all single-aspect metrics (CLIP 74.26%, ArcFace 76.52%, Aesthetic Score 67.73%) and the general-purpose ImageReward (73.39%/71.18%) on their respective dimensions, confirming that task-agnostic reward models fail to capture narrative-specific preferences. Among MLLM baselines, Gemini-1.5-flash (50.25%/18.75%/54.93%) and Qwen2.5-VL-32B (65.09%/48.00%/51.72%) are substantially outperformed across all three dimensions, with identity consistency gaps exceeding 37 and 37 percentage points respectively. GPT-4o-mini is the strongest competitor, leading on visual quality (88.42% vs. 83.00%) but trailing NIREward by 9.34% on prompt following and by a large 38.35% margin on identity consistency. In the optimization comparison (Table 2), ADPO with NIREward outperforms Diffusion-DPO with ImageReward, HPSv2, and PickScore on ID Sim (28.88 vs. 27.55/26.77/26.83) and NIREward identity consistency (+0.010 vs. −0.060/−0.041/−0.052), while the Diffusion-DPO variants all degrade identity consistency below the unoptimized baseline (28.83), indicating that generic reward signals actively harm character identity preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multi-character personalized image generation (PhotoMaker, PuLID, StoryMaker, OMG) and RLHF for diffusion models (Diffusion-DPO, DPOK, DDPO). It departs from prior preference-optimization work in two key ways: (1) replacing opaque scalar reward models with a critique-based MLLM reward model (NIREward) that generates interpretable textual reasoning before scoring, and (2) introducing ADPO, which uses dominating comparison and adaptive margin-weighted learning to address the optimization imbalance that plagues standard DPO across multiple preference dimensions. The NI-RLHF dataset and NI-Bench benchmark are novel contributions, as no prior dataset provides fine-grained multi-dimensional critiques specifically for narrative multi-character generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a concrete and underappreciated gap: standard metrics (CLIP, ArcFace, ImageReward) actively mislead optimization for multi-character narrative images, with DPO using these rewards degrading identity consistency below baseline. The critique-based reward modeling paradigm and the dominating comparison strategy are generalizable ideas that could transfer to other multi-dimensional preference alignment problems beyond image generation. However, the evaluation is limited to a single base model (PhotoMaker V2), a 10k-pair training set, and a 400-prompt benchmark, leaving open whether the approach scales to more characters, longer narratives, or video generation. The reliance on MLLM-assisted annotation also raises questions about the ceiling of human preference signal quality in the dataset.
