## Problem

Existing hand-object interaction (HOI) reconstruction methods are largely restricted to rigid objects, while 4D articulated object reconstruction approaches typically require pre-scanned object geometry or multi-view video initialization. Reconstructing physically plausible 4D hand-articulated-object interactions from a single casually captured monocular RGB video—without any pre-defined templates, pre-scans, or multi-view inputs—remains an unexplored and highly ill-posed challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262820/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single monocular RGB video (e.g., 960×540, 100 frames) as its sole input, with no requirement for pre-scanned object geometry, multi-view captures, or object templates. It produces a complete 4D reconstruction: a metric-scaled articulated object mesh with per-part SE(3) motion trajectories over time, a MANO-parameterized 4D hand mesh, and a spatially aligned hand-object interaction where contact frames are physically consistent. The full pipeline runs in approximately 1 hour on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU.

## Method

ArtHOI is a test-time optimization framework that integrates and refines priors from multiple foundation models across four stages: (1) preprocessing with SAM2, Video-Depth-Anything, and DiffuEraser to extract masks, metric depth, and inpainted object-only frames; (2) canonical mesh generation via HunYuan3D followed by the proposed Adaptive Sampling Refinement (ASR), which iteratively samples candidate metric scales, queries FoundationPose for each to obtain a 6-DoF pose hypothesis, and selects the best by rendering the posed mesh and matching its silhouette IoU against the object mask, adaptively expanding the search range when no improvement is observed; (3) part-wise motion reconstruction using CoTracker3 point tracks lifted to 3D via depth, optimized with a visibility-aware tracking loss and temporal smoothness regularizer to recover per-part SE(3) transformations; and (4) MLLM-guided hand-object alignment, where Qwen-VL is prompted with structured queries (incorporating camera perspective, temporal context, and colorized depth) to infer frame-wise binary contact states and contacting fingers, which are then used as optimization constraints to jointly refine object scale and hand pose parameters, minimizing fingertip-to-object-surface distance on contact frames while regularizing motion smoothness and pose deviation.

## Results

On the ArtHOI-RGBD dataset, ArtHOI achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance across all five articulated objects, ranging from 3.33 mm (CD Drive) to 8.12 mm (Headphone), compared to RSRD's 7.77–288.70 mm and EasyHOI's 50.69–648.70 mm. On the RSRD dataset, ArtHOI matches or exceeds RSRD on 3 of 4 objects (e.g., Scissor: 5.45 mm vs. 68.56 mm CD) while being slightly worse on LED Light (10.84 vs. 10.14 mm) and Bear (12.37 vs. 8.74 mm). For HOI alignment quality, the full MLLM-guided pipeline achieves Co² scores of 0.029 (ArtHOI-RGBD), 0.022 (RSRD), and 0.039 (ArtHOI-Wild), substantially lower than unaligned baselines (0.972/0.517/0.514) and RSRD+WiLoR (0.392/0.166/N/A). Notably, RSRD fails entirely on ArtHOI-Wild and ARCTIC due to its requirement for pre-scanned object geometry.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ArtHOI decisively outperforms RSRD [24] on the ArtHOI-RGBD dataset, reducing CD by 45–98% across all five objects (e.g., 3.33 mm vs. 282.33 mm on CD Drive, 4.49 mm vs. 288.70 mm on Stapler), despite RSRD having access to pre-scanned object geometry. On the RSRD dataset itself, ArtHOI matches or beats RSRD on Scissor (5.45 vs. 68.56 mm CD) and Sunglasses (9.96 vs. 31.99 mm), but is slightly worse on LED Light (10.84 vs. 10.14 mm) and Bear (12.37 vs. 8.74 mm), confirming comparable performance without the pre-scan requirement. EasyHOI [39], applied frame-by-frame, is consistently 10–100× worse in CD across all datasets, confirming that per-frame rigid-object methods cannot handle articulated motion. RSRD fails entirely on ArtHOI-Wild and ARCTIC (no surrounding scan available), while ArtHOI successfully reconstructs all sequences. For HOI alignment (Co² score), ArtHOI with MLLM-guided contact reasoning achieves 0.022–0.039, an order of magnitude better than unaligned outputs (0.514–0.972) and RSRD+WiLoR (0.166–0.392), demonstrating the critical role of the proposed alignment module.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: hand-object interaction (HOI) reconstruction, which has largely assumed rigid objects, and 4D articulated object reconstruction, which typically requires pre-scanned canonical geometry or multi-view inputs. ArtHOI departs from both by jointly reconstructing hands and articulated objects from a single monocular video without any pre-scanning, integrating priors from image-to-3D (HunYuan3D), 6D pose estimation (FoundationPose), dense tracking (CoTracker3), hand reconstruction (WiLoR), and MLLMs (Qwen-VL) through a unified optimization pipeline. The key methodological departure is the Adaptive Sampling Refinement (ASR) strategy, which replaces direct FoundationPose invocation with an iterative scale-sampling-and-rendering loop to reconcile the normalized mesh with noisy depth, and the use of MLLM contact reasoning as geometric optimization constraints for hand-object alignment—neither of which appears in prior HOI or articulated-object work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the pre-scanning requirement, ArtHOI makes 4D articulated HOI reconstruction applicable to casual, in-the-wild interaction videos that were previously intractable, with direct implications for robotic manipulation learning, AR, and human behavior analysis. The two new datasets (ArtHOI-RGBD with metric depth, ArtHOI-Wild from the internet) provide much-needed benchmarks for this setting. However, the method remains optimization-based (~1 hour per 100-frame video), MLLM contact reasoning still carries ~10–15% error, and the framework has not been validated on deformable objects or multi-object interactions—open questions that limit immediate deployment in real-time or highly complex scenarios.
