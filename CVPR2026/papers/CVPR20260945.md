## Problem

Existing SAM-based mix-supervised segmentation methods rely solely on sparse spatial prompts (boxes, points), which are insufficient for ambiguous-boundary scenarios like polyp and camouflaged object segmentation, and they exclude class-labeled or unlabeled images that lack any spatial annotation. Additionally, these methods process each sample in isolation, overlooking the potential of cross-sample collaborative learning to improve pseudo-label quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260945/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes RGB images annotated with any combination of mask, box, scribble, point, class, or no label, plus a dynamically retrieved set of T=4 reference image-mask pairs from a unified data pool. Unlike standard SAM which only accepts spatial prompts, SA-SAM2 treats the reference set as dense contextual prompts via its memory attention module, while optional sparse spatial prompts from weak labels are fused in the decoder. The output is a per-pixel segmentation mask with a confidence score, which supervises a co-trained mean-teacher segmentation model.

## Method

SAMIX adapts SAM2 into SA-SAM2 by inserting a lightweight AdaptFormer adapter (down-project → ReLU → up-project, d̂=384) into each ViT block of the Hiera encoder, steering features from instance-distinct toward semantically consistent representations that support cross-image semantic tracking. A Selecting Policy Network (SPNet), a two-stage Transformer with 3-layer encoder and decoder, auto-regressively retrieves T=4 reference images from a candidate pool filtered by the Hierarchical Guidance Principle (only samples with finer supervision than the query are eligible). SPNet is trained via GRPO reinforcement learning using stochastic beam search to sample N=4 diverse reference sets, scored by three verifiable rewards: mask confidence (quality), Dice agreement with the Seg-model (consistency), and supervised loss against available labels (supervision). The highest-advantage reference set produces the final pseudo-mask, which is re-encoded into the memory pool via momentum update, while a frozen SAM2 backbone and a co-trained mean-teacher Seg-model complete the training loop.

## Results

Under the unified mix-supervised setting (5% mask, 10% box, 15% scribble, 20% point, 25% class, 25% unlabeled), SAMIX achieves 75.9 mIoU on PASCAL VOC 2012 and 78.3 mIoU on Cityscapes, approaching the fully-supervised upper bound of 77.3 and 79.4 respectively. On the challenging ambiguous-boundary tasks, SAMIX reaches 75.9 S_m on CAMO, 79.1 S_m on COD10K, and 80.1 mDice on the polyp segmentation benchmark. These results represent improvements of 4.1% and 3.3% mIoU over the strongest SAM-based baseline (WISH) on the two general datasets, and 3.5% and 2.7% S_m over SAM-COD on the two COD test sets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAMIX outperforms all four primary mix-supervised baselines (MixSegNet, MixPolyp, SAM-COD, WISH) across every dataset and metric under the unified data setting. The largest margins are over optimization-based methods: SAMIX surpasses MixSegNet by 7.9% mIoU on VOC (75.9 vs. 68.0) and MixPolyp by 7.6% mIoU (75.9 vs. 68.3), reflecting the advantage of dense contextual prompts over multi-branch loss design. Among SAM-based methods, SAMIX leads WISH by 4.1% mIoU on VOC and 3.3% on Cityscapes, and beats SAM-COD by 3.5% S_m on CAMO and 2.7% on COD10K, confirming that the semantic adapter plus RL-based reference selection substantially improves pseudo-label quality over sparse-prompt-only strategies. In the equal-annotation-cost experiment (Figure 4), SAMIX (73.7 mIoU) trails the specialized weakly-supervised FMA-WSSS (72.7) by a narrow margin but surpasses CLIMS, BoxTeacher+UniMatch V2, and WISH, validating the mix-supervision paradigm. Notably, under the unified setting SAMIX with only 5% fully-labeled data reaches performance within 1.4% of the fully-supervised upper bound on VOC, a gap no baseline achieves.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SAM-based mix-supervised segmentation paradigm (SAM-COD, WISH) by repurposing SAM2's memory mechanism—originally designed for instance-level video tracking—into a cross-image semantic tracking tool. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) a lightweight AdaptFormer adapter transforms SAM2's instance-distinct Hiera features into semantically consistent representations, enabling the memory attention to operate meaningfully across different images; and (2) a GRPO-trained autoregressive policy network (SPNet) replaces heuristic reference selection with reinforcement learning, actively exploring reference combinations that maximize pseudo-label quality. The mean-teacher co-training framework and the hierarchical guidance principle (finer-supervision samples guide weaker ones) further distinguish it from optimization-based mix-supervised methods that treat each annotation type in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAMIX addresses a practical gap in SAM-based segmentation: the inability to leverage unlabeled or class-labeled data and the poor performance on ambiguous-boundary scenarios (polyps, camouflaged objects) where sparse spatial prompts are insufficient. The plug-and-play design (compatible with Mask2Former, Polyp-PVT, PFNet) and the finding that only ~10M adapter parameters are needed to unlock SAM2's latent semantic consistency make the approach broadly applicable. However, the authors acknowledge limited cross-domain generalization of the trained SA-SAM2 and SPNet, requiring dataset-specific training and scaling of reference data. Open questions include whether the RL-based selection generalizes to larger reference pools or multi-class settings, and whether the semantic adapter can be replaced by more principled feature alignment objectives.
