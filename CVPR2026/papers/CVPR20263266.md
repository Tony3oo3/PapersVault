## Problem

Existing vision-language methods for few-shot WSI classification treat LLM-generated textual descriptions as static, class-level priors shared across all samples, without sample-wise refinement. This collapses complex pathological concepts into a single global query and ignores the structural diversity of clinical language, limiting both the precision of visual-semantic alignment and the model's generalization under extreme label scarcity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263266/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes whole slide images at 40× magnification, tiled into 512×512 patches via the CLAM toolset, along with a category name (e.g., 'metastatic lymph node') augmented with learnable prompt vectors. A frozen CONCH foundation model encodes both the patch features and the text. The output is a slide-level class label predicted via a shared MLP over the fused semantic prior. Additionally, an offline LLM-generated knowledge base of 300 multi-view pathological descriptions per category serves as an external retrieval source during training.

## Method

MUSE comprises two components: Sample-wise Fine-grained Semantic Enhancement (SFSE) and Stochastic Multi-view Model Optimization (SMMO). SFSE first decomposes each category's text prompt into k fine-grained semantic cues via a Mixture-of-Experts router with input-dependent noise, then uses multi-head cross-attention with top-r% patch filtering to produce a sample-specific semantic prior that reflects which sub-concepts are visually supported. SMMO retrieves m semantically relevant texts from the LLM-generated knowledge base using cosine similarity against the sample prior, then stochastically samples one text per training iteration, re-processes it through DSR and SVTI to yield an auxiliary prior, and fuses it with the primary prior via logit averaging before cross-entropy optimization. This stochastic multi-view exposure compensates for semantic gaps in any single textual description and mitigates overfitting to specific phrasings under few-shot conditions.

## Results

On CAMELYON (4-shot), MUSE achieves 74.86% ACC, 76.65% AUC, and 68.66% F1, surpassing the best baseline FOCUS (68.13% ACC) by 6.73 points. On TCGA-NSCLC (4-shot), MUSE reaches 79.90% ACC and 87.57% AUC, edging out FOCUS (79.14% ACC) by 0.76 points. On TCGA-BRCA (4-shot), MUSE attains 84.14% ACC and 73.81% F1, outperforming ViLa-MIL (81.80% ACC) by 2.34 points. Across all three datasets, the performance gap between MUSE and the strongest baseline widens as the number of shots decreases, with the largest relative gains in the 4-shot regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MUSE outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and shot setting. The most dramatic margin is on CAMELYON 4-shot, where MUSE (74.86% ACC) exceeds FOCUS (68.13%) by 6.73 points and ViLa-MIL (65.20%) by 9.66 points. On TCGA-BRCA 4-shot, MUSE (84.14% ACC, 73.81% F1) leads ViLa-MIL (81.80% ACC, 68.90% F1) by 2.34 and 4.91 points respectively, while FOCUS (80.16% ACC) trails by 3.98 points. Among traditional MIL methods, ABMIL and DSMIL are the strongest visual-only baselines but are consistently 5–12 points below MUSE in 4-shot ACC. VLM-based methods (Top, ViLa-MIL, FOCUS) generally surpass pure MIL approaches under low-shot conditions, confirming the value of semantic priors; however, MUSE still leads all VLM baselines in both 4-shot and 8-shot settings, with the gap narrowing (but not closing) at 16-shot where FOCUS on CAMELYON (88.62% ACC) comes within 1.08 points of MUSE (89.70%).

## Relation to Prior Work

MUSE extends the few-shot WSI classification (FSWC) paradigm established by Top [33], ViLa-MIL [38], and FOCUS [14], which all leverage pathology-specific VLMs (CONCH, PLIP) to align patch features with textual semantics under limited labels. It departs from these methods in two conceptual ways: (1) replacing static class-level text prompts with MoE-based decompositional refinement that produces sample-wise fine-grained semantic priors, and (2) augmenting a single prompt per class with an LLM-generated multi-view knowledge base whose entries are stochastically retrieved and integrated during training. The authors frame this as the first work to treat semantic optimization—both precision and diversity—as the central lever for few-shot pathology generalization, rather than merely adding a text modality to an existing MIL pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The stochastic multi-view optimization strategy offers a practical recipe for injecting semantic diversity into few-shot medical imaging without requiring additional labeled data, and the MoE-based semantic decomposition provides a reusable mechanism for disentangling fine-grained diagnostic attributes. However, all experiments are limited to binary classification on three datasets, the knowledge base generation depends on GPT-4 for concept decomposition (raising cost and reproducibility concerns), and the large standard deviations in 4-shot results (e.g., ±9.22 ACC on CAMELYON) temper confidence in the reported margins. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-class or multi-label pathology tasks, whether the knowledge base can be updated or personalized per institution, and whether the gains persist with alternative pathology foundation models beyond CONCH.
