## Problem

Existing image-to-video diffusion models struggle to preserve human facial identity across generated frames, particularly when the face occupies a small region of the input image and the motion prompt involves large-scale movements. This challenge is underexplored in I2V (most prior work targets T2V with auxiliary identity modules), and supervised fine-tuning approaches suffer from exposure bias that causes progressive identity drift over time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262497/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image containing a human face (targeting small-face scenarios where the largest face bounding box is ≤100×100 pixels at 960p resolution) and a text motion prompt as input, and produces a temporally coherent video in which the person's identity remains consistent across all frames. Training uses 8-step distilled sampling (Wan2.2-Lightning) without classifier-free guidance, and the reward signal is computed by decoding predicted latents through a frozen VAE and scoring faces with ArcFace embeddings.

## Method

IPRO optimizes a video diffusion model via reinforcement learning using a differentiable ArcFace identity reward, without modifying the model architecture or adding auxiliary identity modules. The key mechanism backpropagates the face reward gradient through only the last K=4 denoising steps (DRaFT-style truncation), concentrating optimization on fine-grained appearance details while reducing memory cost. A Facial Scoring Mechanism (FSM) computes each generated frame's average cosine similarity to all ground-truth video frames (treated as a multi-view feature pool) rather than to a single reference, which suppresses copy-paste rigidity and provides richer supervision. A multi-step KL-divergence regularization penalizes deviation from the reference model's velocity predictions at each denoising step, preventing reward hacking (stiff faces, ignored prompts) while preserving the foundation model's original generation quality. Training is on-policy from pure Gaussian noise, aligning the training distribution with inference and eliminating the exposure bias inherent in teacher-forced SFT.

## Results

On the Wan 2.2 I2V model, IPRO achieves a FaceSim score of 0.6942, substantially outperforming the base model (0.5780) and all compared baselines. The method also improves face similarity by 45.9% on the in-house 15B I2V model (0.4769 → 0.6960) and by 44.1% on Wan 2.2 5B (0.3788 → 0.5460), while preserving or slightly improving all VBench-I2V quality metrics (Subject Consistency, Background Consistency, Aesthetic Quality, etc.). The reward hacking rate is kept at 10% (vs. 58% without KL regularization and 52% without FSM), confirming that identity gains do not come at the cost of facial expressiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IPRO outperforms all four baselines on FaceSim. Against the architecture-based T2V methods adapted to I2V, MoCA† (latent-space identity loss) scores 0.5820 and Concat-ID† (reference face tokens + 3D self-attention) scores 0.6056, while IPRO reaches 0.6942 — a margin of +0.112 and +0.089 respectively. Against the RL-based alternatives, DPO (0.6284) and GRPO (0.6334) both trail IPRO by 0.066 and 0.061, which the authors attribute to DPO's reliance on relative preference ranking (vulnerable to confounds) and GRPO's low response diversity within a single prompt undermining group-wise advantage estimation. The consistent win across all baselines suggests that direct, calibrated facial reward feedback is more effective than both architectural identity injection and preference-based or group-normalized RL for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of reward-guided diffusion optimization (DRaFT, AlignProp, VADER) and identity-preserving video generation. It departs from the dominant T2V identity-preservation paradigm—which relies on auxiliary face adapters, 3D geometry modules, or per-person LoRA fine-tuning—by reframing I2V identity preservation as a direct reward-optimization problem over the diffusion sampling trajectory. The DRaFT truncation strategy is repurposed to backpropagate an ArcFace-based identity reward through only the last K denoising steps, while a novel facial scoring mechanism (treating all GT video frames as a feature pool) and multi-step KL regularization are introduced to prevent reward hacking and copy-paste artifacts. Unlike DPO or GRPO, which optimize relative preferences or group-normalized advantages, IPRO uses a dense, calibrated absolute reward signal specifically suited to the quantifiable nature of face identity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IPRO demonstrates that identity preservation in I2V can be substantially improved (up to ~46% FaceSim gain) without any architectural modification or auxiliary identity network, making it a lightweight, plug-and-play technique applicable to any diffusion-based I2V model. The on-policy training from pure noise directly addresses exposure bias, a known failure mode of SFT-based identity methods that causes progressive identity drift over long video sequences. However, the current scope is limited to facial identity; non-facial attributes (clothing, accessories, jewelry) remain unaddressed, and the evaluation relies on a single 600-scene small-face benchmark with adapted T2V baselines rather than purpose-built I2V identity methods. A unified identity reward covering both facial and non-facial attributes, along with broader multi-person and long-horizon evaluation, would be needed to fully validate the approach's generalizability.
