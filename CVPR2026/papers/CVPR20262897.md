## Problem

Existing continual visual instruction tuning (CVIT) research exclusively targets pre-safety-alignment MLLMs, leaving the practically critical post-SA CVIT setting unexplored. In this setting, safety-aligned MLLMs suffer a dual degradation during sequential adaptation: catastrophic forgetting of previously learned tasks and progressive erosion of their safety alignment, a trade-off that existing CVIT and safety-preservation methods fail to address simultaneously.

## Input / Output

HPA operates as a post-training step applied after each standard LoRA fine-tuning pass on a vision instruction dataset (text instruction + RGB image → text answer). It consumes two small calibration sets per step: a safety calibration set D_s* (8 harmful image-instruction pairs with the aligned model's safe responses) and a task calibration set D_t* (128 samples from the current tuning dataset), from which it computes Hessian-based importance scores. The output is an adapted weight matrix per linear layer that replaces the raw fine-tuned weights, preserving the original training pipeline unchanged.

## Method

HPA partitions each layer's parameters into safety-focused and task-focused subsets using Hessian-based importance scores computed from the squared parameter change between pre- and post-tuning weights, normalized by the inverse Hessian diagonal. A harmoniously balanced selection then constructs a binary mask that retains safety-focused parameters from the old model while using an adaptive balancing score φ = ε̄ − α·ζ̄ (where α is modulated by the log-ratio of safety to task importance via a tanh function) to decide retention at overlapping shared-focused positions, with a layer-wise linearly decreasing retention ratio p^l that allocates more preservation to shallow layers. Finally, an orthogonal parameter adjustment projects the update ΔW onto the previous weight matrix and subtracts this projection, ensuring new task-specific updates remain approximately orthogonal to previously learned representations and thereby reducing catastrophic forgetting across the continual sequence.

## Results

On the CVIT benchmark (6 sequential tasks: AD → ImageNet → Flickr30k → Fin → ScienceQA → TextVQA), HPA achieves 75.73% AP and −4.87 BWT under Original Data, outperforming the strongest baseline Safe Delta (73.32% AP, −6.91 BWT) by +2.41% AP and +2.04% BWT. On safety, HPA attains 4.75% MASR and 1.89% DASR, beating Safe Delta (5.02% MASR, 2.15% DASR) and dramatically outperforming CVIT-only methods like Model Tailor (28.29% MASR) and SeqFT (42.56% MASR). Under the more challenging 0.1% Harmful Data Injected condition, HPA further widens its lead: 76.62% AP and 7.22% MASR versus Safe Delta's 73.20% AP and 24.26% MASR, demonstrating robustness to adversarial data contamination.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HPA outperforms all six baselines on both task performance and safety metrics. Against the closest competitor Safe Delta [18], HPA gains +2.41% AP and +2.04% BWT while also reducing MASR by 0.27% and DASR by 0.26% under Original Data; under 0.1% harmful data injection the gap widens dramatically to +3.42% AP and a 17.04% MASR reduction (7.22% vs. 24.26%). Against CVIT-specific methods, HPA surpasses Model Tailor [39] by +6.94% AP and reduces MASR from 28.29% to 4.75%, and beats SEFE [4] by +9.24% AP with a 37.71% MASR reduction. Classical continual learning baselines EWC [15] and Replay [1] achieve comparable task accuracy to SeqFT but suffer severe safety degradation (MASR > 31%), while SPPFT [16]—designed for LLM safety—performs worst on both task accuracy (61.06% AP) and safety (46.35% MASR), indicating that text-only safety protection transfers poorly to the multimodal setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of continual visual instruction tuning (CVIT) and safety alignment for MLLMs. Prior CVIT methods (Eproj, MoeLoRA, SMoRA, BranchLoRA, SEFE) focus exclusively on task forgetting and either modify the training pipeline with additional parameter modules or ignore safety entirely, while safety alignment work (VLGuard, SPA-VL, ADPO) addresses initial alignment but not preservation during subsequent adaptation. HPA departs from both lines by operating purely as a post-training parameter adaptation step—requiring no changes to the fine-tuning pipeline—and by repurposing Hessian-based importance scoring (from the pruning literature) to identify safety-critical versus task-critical parameters, combined with an orthogonal projection constraint reminiscent of EWC but applied to weight-space updates rather than loss regularization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical urgency is clear: deployed MLLMs are safety-aligned and will inevitably undergo continual updates, yet this paper is the first to systematically show that such updates erode safety alignment alongside task knowledge. The post-training design (no pipeline modification, small calibration sets) makes the approach deployable in settings where re-running safety alignment is infeasible due to privacy or cost. Limitations include evaluation on a single 7B model (LLaVA-v1.5) and six tasks, and the Hessian-based scoring requires a safety calibration set that must be constructed from the aligned model's own outputs, which may not generalize to all safety alignment paradigms. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, longer task sequences, and whether the layer-wise retention schedule transfers across architectures.
