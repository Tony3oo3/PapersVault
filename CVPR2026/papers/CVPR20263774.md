## Problem

Unified diffusion transformer models like Bagel are restricted to single-condition inputs (one reference image or prompt), making them unable to compose information from multiple heterogeneous sources such as identity, style, and layout simultaneously. The binding problem—associating specific semantic attributes from different reference images with their correct visual targets in a coherent output—remains unsolved in autoregressive diffusion architectures, where task-specific encoders or overfitting to a single modality fail to generalize.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263774/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an interleaved text-image sequence where multiple reference images are each preceded by one or more attribute tokens (e.g., <style>, <identity>, <subject>, <layout>) and separated by natural-language spans that describe their relationships. At inference, users can freely mix any number of condition images in arbitrary order with text, and the model produces a single RGB image that selectively composes the designated attributes from each source. The conditioning is structured so that each image is locally bound to its attribute tokens, enabling partial editing and multi-reference fusion without fine-tuning.

## Method

SIGMA post-trains the Bagel unified diffusion transformer on 700K interleaved examples using three key mechanisms. First, selective multi-attribute tokens assign each condition image a role-specific embedding (style, identity, subject, layout, etc.) that modulates which latent subspace is activated via a learnable projection: t_i = v_i + W_τ(τ_i). Second, an interleaved conditioning scheme concatenates text and image tokens in the user-specified order, allowing the transformer to parse multimodal context jointly through cross- and self-attention. Third, a group-scoped attention mask restricts special tokens from attending to image patches outside their own reference group, preventing cross-condition attribute leakage while preserving intra-image bidirectional attention and the original causal ordering. Training uses the same denoising objective as the backbone with full-parameter fine-tuning (50K steps, 4×H200 GPUs, token packing at 30K tokens/batch), and the 700K dataset spans six task families including compositional generation, selective extraction, stylization, relation transfer, editing, and layout-based synthesis.

## Results

On the compositional generation benchmark (Our Bench), SIGMA achieves CLIP 30.29, CLIP-I 78.94, DINO 64.08, and DreamSim 62.45, surpassing Bagel by +12.38, +26.42, +22.46, and +19.18 respectively. On XVerseBench, SIGMA scores CLIP 31.96, CLIP-I 75.57, DINO 59.52, and DreamSim 67.87, approaching GPT-4o (CLIP 32.94, CLIP-I 77.74) and Nano-Banana (CLIP 32.45, CLIP-I 75.50). For selective generation, SIGMA attains the lowest CLIP-ES (58.02) among all methods, indicating superior subject exclusivity, while matching GPT-4o on CLIP-I (80.26 vs. 80.14). On layout-based generation, SIGMA achieves F1 0.44 (layout only) and 0.35 (layout + reference), substantially outperforming Bagel (0.10 and 0.25) and all other baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Bagel (the direct backbone), SIGMA delivers large gains across all tasks: +12.38 CLIP and +26.42 CLIP-I on compositional generation, +9.65 CLIP-I and −9.88 CLIP-ES on selective generation, and +0.34 F1 on layout-only generation. Compared to XVerse, SIGMA achieves lower CLIP (30.29 vs. 32.33 on Our Bench) but substantially higher CLIP-I (78.94 vs. 44.15), DINO (64.08 vs. 42.76), and DreamSim (62.45 vs. 54.63), indicating stronger structural and perceptual alignment. Against SSR, SIGMA wins on every metric in both compositional and selective settings (e.g., CLIP-I 78.94 vs. 65.34, DreamSim 62.45 vs. 52.65). Versus the closed-source GPT-4o, SIGMA is within 0.78 CLIP and 1.01 CLIP-I on Our Bench compositional generation, and matches GPT-4o on CLIP-I for selective generation (80.26 vs. 80.14) while achieving a lower (better) CLIP-ES (58.02 vs. 60.22). Against Nano-Banana, SIGMA trails slightly on CLIP (30.29 vs. 30.65) and DreamSim (62.45 vs. 62.78) but leads on CLIP-I (78.94 vs. 75.63) and DINO (64.08 vs. 62.22). For layout-based generation, SIGMA outperforms EasyControl (F1 0.44 vs. 0.16) and Bagel (F1 0.44 vs. 0.10) by a wide margin, though it lags behind Bagel on FID in the layout-only setting (121.08 vs. 103.72).

## Relation to Prior Work

SIGMA extends the Bagel unified diffusion transformer, which demonstrated that paired image-edit data can align multiple visual tasks in a single model, by introducing a post-training phase that replaces single-condition inputs with interleaved multi-condition sequences. The multi-attribute token design draws conceptually from the binding problem literature and object-centric representation learning, while the group-scoped attention mask is a novel architectural mechanism not present in prior unified models. Unlike task-specific conditional pipelines (ControlNet, IP-Adapter, T2I-Adapter) that require separate modules per condition type, SIGMA unifies heterogeneous conditioning within one transformer via a shared token vocabulary, positioning itself at the intersection of unified generative models and fine-grained conditional control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical limitation of current unified diffusion models—the inability to compose multiple heterogeneous references (identity, style, layout) in a single forward pass—which is a common need in real-world creative workflows. The 700K interleaved dataset and the model-agnostic token/mask design provide a reusable recipe that could be applied to future diffusion transformer backbones. However, the evaluation is limited to a single backbone (Bagel) trained for only 50K steps on 4 GPUs, and the dataset is heavily synthetic (GPT-4o/Nano-Banana generated), leaving open questions about scalability to more conditions, video generation, and robustness to distribution shift in real user inputs.
