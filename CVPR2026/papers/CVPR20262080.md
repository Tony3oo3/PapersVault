## Problem

Existing object hallucination benchmarks are confined to single-image settings with binary questions, and multi-image benchmarks lack controlled manipulation of visual difficulty factors. This makes it impossible to systematically diagnose which specific visual conditions (perceptual challenges, contextual biases, image scale) and which reasoning demands (aggregation, comparison, retrieval) trigger hallucination in MLLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262080/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance presents a set of 2, 4, 8, or 10 RGB images alongside a text question drawn from 26 question types spanning four tasks (existence, counting, attribute, position) and three reasoning patterns (comprehensive, comparative, selective). The model produces a structured text answer: a yes/no judgment, an integer count, a specific image index, an attribute-object pair, or a spatial relation. Adversarial variants swap in hard-positive images (small or occluded target objects) or hard-negative images (contextually plausible but visually absent objects) while keeping the question template fixed.

## Method

MIOH constructs a diagnostic evaluation space by crossing four object-centric tasks with three multi-image reasoning patterns under three independently controllable adversarial pressures: visual context scale (number of images from 2 to 10), perceptual difficulty (hard positives curated via bounding-box/occlusion filtering and CLIP semantic ambiguity scoring), and contextual bias (hard negatives selected using COCO co-occurrence probabilities and CLIP-based visual-text confusion). The 3,484 questions are sourced from COCO-ReM, PACO, and SVG to ensure reliable ground-truth annotations for existence, attributes, and spatial relations respectively. The key design insight is that uniform failure across all reasoning patterns indicates a perceptual bottleneck, whereas selective-reasoning failure with intact comprehensive reasoning isolates an integration-stage breakdown. An ablation comparing multi-image comprehensive questions against decomposed single-image questions on identical content confirms that cross-image integration itself—beyond error accumulation—is the primary hallucination amplifier.

## Results

Across 29 MLLMs evaluated on the MIOH benchmark (3,484 questions, 11,732 images), the overall average accuracy is only 36.1%, with Gemini-2.5-Pro (64.4%) and GPT-5 (63.1%) leading among proprietary models and Qwen2-VL-7B (49.1%) and MiniCPM-V-2.6 (48.5%) topping open-source models. Task difficulty follows a clear hierarchy: Existence (49.4%) > Position (37.3%) > Attribute (32.4%) > Counting (25.4%). Increasing the number of input images to 8 (NI) causes the most severe degradation, dropping Existence accuracy to 30.0% (a >50% relative drop from the 62.4% easy baseline), while Selective reasoning is the hardest pattern overall (34.2% average).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, Gemini-2.5-Pro leads on Counting (57.5% avg) and Position (66.6%), while GPT-5 edges ahead on Existence (78.4%) and Attribute (57.8%); both suffer the largest drops under the NI=8 condition (Existence falls to ~55–56%). Among open-source models, Qwen2-VL-7B achieves the best overall profile (Existence 67.5%, Position 61.1%) but collapses under NI (Existence 30.7%), whereas MiniCPM-V-2.6 shows the most robust NI performance (Existence 38.7%) among open-source models. Counting is the universal weak point: even the best model (Gemini-2.5-Pro) reaches only 57.5%, and most open-source models score below 30%. The ablation (Fig. 5) further shows that all models perform substantially worse on multi-image comprehensive questions than on decomposed single-image equivalents, confirming that cross-image integration—not single-image perception—is the primary hallucination amplifier. No single model dominates across all four tasks and all adversarial conditions, revealing distinct failure profiles per model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends single-image object hallucination benchmarks (POPE, NOPE, THORe, AMBER) and general multi-image understanding benchmarks (MMDU, MMIU, MUIRBench) into a unified diagnostic framework specifically targeting object hallucination across multiple images. The closest prior work, MIHBench, explores multi-image hallucination but varies only image quantity and restricts to binary existence questions; MIOH departs by systematically crossing four object-centric tasks with three reasoning patterns under three controlled adversarial pressures (perceptual difficulty, contextual bias, visual context scale). Conceptually, it repurposes the adversarial-robustness evaluation paradigm from single-image hallucination literature and applies it to the multi-image integration setting, where no prior benchmark provides fine-grained diagnostic separation of perceptual vs. integration-stage failures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even frontier models (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro) achieve only ~63% overall accuracy on multi-image object hallucination, with counting dropping to ~25% and the number-of-images pressure causing >50% relative degradation from easy baselines. The ablation showing that multi-image processing introduces systematic errors beyond simple accumulation of per-image mistakes identifies cross-image integration as a distinct failure mode, pointing toward targeted architectural or training interventions. A key open question is whether the accuracy-robustness trade-off observed here (larger models not necessarily more robust) reflects a fundamental limitation of current MLLM training paradigms or can be addressed through better multi-image pretraining data and objectives.
