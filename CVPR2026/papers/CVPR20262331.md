## Problem

Existing image attribution methods assume a fixed, closed set of known generators, but new diffusion, adversarial, and autoregressive models appear almost monthly, rendering static classifiers obsolete upon release. The core challenge is not recognizing known generators but continuously attributing newly released ones without erasing past knowledge, while also detecting images from entirely unseen models. Conventional class-incremental learning methods fail here because generative models form overlapping hierarchical lineages rather than independent, separable classes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single 256×256 RGB image, extracts features via a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 encoder, and produces two outputs: a multi-class attribution label over all seen generative models (28 categories spanning GAN, diffusion, and autoregressive families) and a binary unseen-detection flag triggered when the maximum softmax confidence falls below a calibrated threshold τ=0.65. The decision space dynamically expands as new models are introduced in sequential tasks, and the threshold is re-calibrated after each incremental step to maintain consistent open-set behavior.

## Method

IncreFA reformulates attribution as structured class-incremental learning that exploits the hierarchical ancestry of generative models. Two complementary mechanisms drive the design: (1) Hierarchical Constraints learn orthogonal anchor priors at both model-level (fine-grained) and family-level (coarse-grained), enforcing angular separation across families via Q^TQ=I regularization while promoting intra-family knowledge sharing through mean-prototype alignment; (2) a Latent Memory Bank stores compact encoder features (reducing memory by >2 orders of magnitude vs. raw-image replay) selected via herding, and generates pseudo-unseen samples by randomly interpolating latent pairs from distinct classes to push the model toward low-density decision-boundary regions. The overall loss combines cross-entropy classification, both hierarchical orthogonality terms, an open-set confidence penalty on mixed latents, and a replay term, all trained with only the projection head and classifier updated on top of the frozen backbone.

## Results

Under the incremental attribution protocol (EP1) on IABench (28 generative models, 2022–2025), IncreFA achieves 78.80% average attribution accuracy at the final task T₇, surpassing the second-best method DGR (75.68%) by 3.12 percentage points, and reaches 98.93% unseen detection accuracy. Under the static closed-world protocol (EP2), IncreFA attains 95.93% average attribution accuracy and 99.97% authenticity accuracy, outperforming the strongest static baseline RepMix (91.16% avg. acc.) by nearly 5 points. The method maintains stable performance across all 8 incremental tasks, with only a 21.2-point drop from T₀ (99.99%) to T₇ (78.80%), whereas most baselines degrade by 40–65 points over the same span.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IncreFA outperforms all 10 incremental baselines on both attribution accuracy and unseen detection under EP1. The closest competitor, DGR (CVPR 2024), reaches 75.68% at T₇ and 94.21% unseen detection, trailing IncreFA by 3.12 and 4.72 points respectively. Prompt-based methods (L2P, DualPrompt, TUNA) suffer severe degradation—L2P drops to 29.51% by T₇—because their semantic prompts fail to capture generator-specific artefacts in overlapping diffusion-family distributions. Rehearsal-based ICaRL and Foster retain moderate accuracy (~72%) but lack open-set calibration, yielding unseen detection below 84%. Under the static EP2 protocol, IncreFA (95.93% avg. acc.) beats the strongest static attribution method RepMix (91.16%) by 4.77 points, while POSE achieves comparable unseen detection (98.23% vs. 98.78%) but far lower attribution accuracy (74.69%). The Siamese baseline [1] and DNA-Net [65] trail by 12+ points on average accuracy, confirming that hierarchical latent structuring provides gains beyond generic feature-matching approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the class-incremental learning (CIL) toolkit—replay, orthogonal regularisation, and prompt/adapter-based adaptation—into the image-attribution domain, where prior work has treated attribution as a static, closed-set classification problem. It departs from standard CIL by exploiting the hierarchical lineage of generative models (GAN, diffusion, autoregressive families) through learnable orthogonal priors at both family and model levels, an assumption that has no analogue in typical CIL benchmarks with independent classes. The open-set component (threshold-based calibration on pseudo-unseen latents) extends the attribution task beyond the closed taxonomy that dominates existing classifier-based and inversion-based attribution methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IABench benchmark (28 models, 2022–2025, temporally ordered) fills a genuine gap: no prior benchmark systematically evaluates attribution under continual and open-set conditions, making it a useful reference for the community. However, the predefined family taxonomy, linear latent mixing for unseen simulation, and empirically tuned threshold limit generalisability to hybrid or structurally novel generators. Open questions include adaptive taxonomy discovery, extension to multimodal or multi-frame attribution, and whether the orthogonal-prior structure holds when new model families emerge that do not fit the GAN/diffusion/AR trichotomy.
