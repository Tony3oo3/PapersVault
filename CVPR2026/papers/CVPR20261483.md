## Problem

Learning unsigned distance functions (UDFs) from multi-view images is difficult because ground-truth surfaces are unavailable for supervision and UDF gradients are undefined exactly on the underlying surface, undermining gradient-based regularizers such as eikonal and normal-alignment terms. Existing methods that rely on global objectives and gradient priors in the absence of surface ground truth suffer from unstable training, over-smoothing, and loss of fine geometric details on open or thin structures.

## Input / Output

The method takes calibrated multi-view RGB images (72 views at 1024×1024 on DF3D; 49–64 views at 1600×1200 on DTU) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics. It jointly optimizes 2D Gaussian primitives (center, color, opacity, rotation, anisotropic scaling) and a lightweight 8-layer MLP student UDF, producing a 3D surface mesh extracted from the learned UDF zero level set. At inference the student requires only a single 3D query point—no patch conditioning—making it compatible with standard 3DGS rendering pipelines.

## Method

The core contribution is a band-limited knowledge distillation scheme in which a frozen LoSF-UDF teacher, pretrained on synthetic algebraic surfaces with closed-form distance expressions, provides reliable near-surface UDF supervision to a lightweight student network optimized jointly with the 2DGS pipeline. Distillation is restricted to a narrow band B (offsets ±τ along Gaussian normals from each center), where the teacher's local patch-based predictions are most trustworthy. A visibility- and geometry-aware confidence weight w(q) modulates teacher influence by combining reprojection-error-based occlusion detection with a sign-invariant cosine alignment between teacher and student gradients, effectively down-weighting supervision in ambiguous or weakly constrained regions. The student is trained with a composite loss combining photometric reconstruction, SSIM, L_Far, the confidence-weighted KD loss (with per-scene affine calibration), and a geometry normal alignment term; training proceeds in two phases—first optimizing the Gaussian splatting for T_warm iterations, then ramping up UDF-related losses to avoid early over-regularization.

## Results

On the DF3D dataset (12 garments), the method achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 1.49×10⁻³, outperforming the closest UDF-based baseline GaussianUDF (1.60×10⁻³) and the best SDF-based method GOF (2.49×10⁻³), while running in 1.8 h versus 8.6 h for NeuralUDF. On the DTU dataset (15 scenes), it attains a mean CD of 0.60×10⁻³, beating the best SDF-based Gaussian methods UANGGS (0.62×10⁻³) and G2SDF (0.64×10⁻³) as well as all UDF-based competitors (GaussianUDF 0.68, 2S-UDF 0.71, VRPrior 0.91). The ablation confirms that band-limited distillation, visibility/geometry weighting, and the joint normal regularizer each contribute incrementally (0.99 → 0.83 → 0.71 → 0.60 CD on DTU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 13 baselines on both datasets by mean Chamfer Distance. On DF3D, it beats the strongest UDF baseline GaussianUDF by 0.11×10⁻³ (1.49 vs 1.60) and the best SDF method GOF by a large margin (1.49 vs 2.49), while also being ~5× faster than NeRF-based UDF methods (1.8 h vs 7.8–9.2 h). On DTU, the gains over the closest SDF-based Gaussian competitors are tighter: 0.60 vs 0.62 (UANGGS) and 0.60 vs 0.64 (G2SDF), showing that the UDF formulation can match or exceed SDF methods despite the inherent sign-ambiguity challenge. Among UDF-based methods, the improvement over GaussianUDF (0.60 vs 0.68) and 2S-UDF (0.60 vs 0.71) is consistent, while the gap over NeRF-based UDF methods (NeuralUDF 0.91, VRPrior 0.91) is substantial, reflecting the efficiency and accuracy advantage of the Gaussian-splatting backbone. The method also surpasses the 2DGS baseline (0.83) by 0.23×10⁻³ on DTU, confirming that the distillation and weighting components add meaningful geometric fidelity beyond photometric supervision alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of 2D Gaussian Splatting (2DGS) for rendering and unsigned distance function (UDF) learning for open-surface reconstruction. It departs from prior UDF methods (GaussianUDF, NeuralUDF, 2S-UDF, VRPrior) that rely on gradient-based regularizers (eikonal, normal alignment) which are fundamentally ill-posed on the UDF zero level set due to non-differentiability. Instead, it introduces a band-limited knowledge distillation scheme where a frozen patch-based teacher (LoSF-UDF, trained on synthetic algebraic surfaces with closed-form distances) provides near-surface supervision to a lightweight student UDF optimized jointly with the 2DGS pipeline. The visibility- and geometry-aware confidence weighting further distinguishes this work by modulating teacher influence based on multi-view render evidence, addressing a gap in prior point-based UDF priors that lack render awareness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UDF-based reconstruction is essential for open, thin, or incomplete geometries where SDF-based methods are structurally biased toward watertight surfaces, and this paper offers a practical recipe for stable UDF training without ground-truth surface supervision. The distillation-from-synthetic-algebraic-surfaces strategy is a clean workaround for the UDF gradient non-differentiability problem that has plagued the field. However, the method still assumes reasonably dense multi-view coverage and accurate camera calibration, and the authors acknowledge performance degradation in sparse-view or high-pose-error settings. Open questions include whether the band-limited distillation idea extends to dynamic scenes, whether semantic priors can further enforce structural coherence, and whether the approach generalizes to uncalibrated or in-the-wild image collections.
