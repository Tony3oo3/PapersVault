## Problem

CLIP models fine-tuned on long captions (e.g., Long-CLIP) exhibit a strong bias toward early tokens and the opening summary sentence, treating it as a shortcut that minimizes contrastive loss without engaging the full context window. This makes retrieval performance highly sensitive to sentence order and degrades sharply when the summary sentence is removed or moved, a failure mode that persists across CLIP, OpenCLIP, SigLIP, and SigLIP2 variants.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images paired with multi-sentence text captions (averaging ~130–175 tokens, well beyond CLIP's native 77-token limit) and produces aligned image and text embeddings in a shared contrastive space. The text encoder's positional embeddings are linearly interpolated from 77 to 248 tokens (freezing the first 20), and the model is trained with a dual contrastive objective over both a full long caption and a shorter augmented caption, yielding a single similarity score per image–text pair for retrieval.

## Method

DeBias-CLIP is a drop-in replacement for Long-CLIP that introduces three caption-level training augmentations without adding any trainable parameters: (i) the short caption is redefined as the long caption minus its first (summary) sentence, forcing the model to align with fine-grained details rather than a global overview; (ii) sentences from this remaining set are randomly sampled without replacement and without preserving order, creating variable-length captions that expose the model to diverse token positions; and (iii) a random number of padding tokens are prepended before the sampled text (transferred from post-caption padding), pushing informative tokens to later positions and encouraging uniform attention across the sequence. Training uses a weighted sum of short- and long-caption contrastive losses (λ^s = 0.1 optimal), and the entire procedure is applied on top of the standard Long-CLIP positional-embedding stretching and PCA-based short-caption alignment.

## Results

On long-text retrieval, DeBias-CLIP achieves 93.0% top-1 T2I on Urban1k and 80.0% on DOCCI (ViT-B/16), and 95.2% on Urban1k and 85.6% on DOCCI (ViT-L/14), setting new state-of-the-art across all four long-text benchmarks. Relative to the Long-CLIP baseline, gains range from +8.6% to +13.5% on T2I retrieval. On short-text retrieval, DeBias-CLIP reaches 56.6% top-1 I2T on Flickr30k (ViT-B/16) and 64.8% (ViT-L/14), outperforming Long-CLIP by +9.8% and +11.4% respectively, while remaining competitive with SmartCLIP on COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeBias-CLIP outperforms all seven baselines on the majority of long-text retrieval metrics. Against Long-CLIP (the direct architectural baseline), gains are substantial: +13.5% on Urban1k T2I and +8.6% on DOCCI T2I for ViT-B/16, confirming that the summary-sentence shortcut is the primary bottleneck. Against SmartCLIP (the closest competing method, which also uses sentence sampling but retains the summary sentence), DeBias-CLIP leads by +5.6% on Urban1k T2I and +3.7% on DCI T2I (ViT-B/16), with similar margins at ViT-L/14. Against FineLIP, which adds a cross-modal refinement module at inference, DeBias-CLIP wins on Urban1k by +3.0% (ViT-B) and +1.3% (ViT-L) and on DOCCI T2I by +1.9% and +1.2% respectively, while FineLIP edges out on DOCCI I2T for ViT-B/16 (80.0 vs. 79.7). On short-text retrieval, DeBias-CLIP surpasses Long-CLIP by +9.8% (ViT-B) and +11.4% (ViT-L) on Flickr30k I2T, and slightly trails SmartCLIP on COCO T2I (43.0 vs. 42.4 for ViT-B; 48.1 vs. 48.5 for ViT-L), indicating a minor trade-off when emphasizing long-caption training. The method also generalizes across pretraining distributions (OpenAI CLIP, OpenCLIP/LAION-2B, SigLIP, SigLIP2), consistently outperforming the corresponding Long-CLIP variant on DOCCI T2I.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Long-CLIP, which extended CLIP's positional embeddings via linear interpolation and fine-tuned on ShareGPT4V long captions. The key departure is identifying that the summary-first structure common in long-caption datasets creates a training shortcut that preserves early-token bias even after Long-CLIP's fine-tuning, and proposing a parameter-free data augmentation (summary removal, sentence sub-sampling, prefix padding) to counteract it. Unlike SmartCLIP (which adds a text-conditional masking network), FineLIP (which adds a cross-modal refinement module), or TULIP (which requires two-stage rotary-embedding training), DeBias-CLIP introduces no new trainable parameters and no additional training stages, positioning itself as the simplest drop-in improvement over the Long-CLIP recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single summary sentence acts as a shortcut preventing CLIP from learning to attend to later tokens is a useful diagnostic for anyone fine-tuning vision-language models on long captions, and the fix is trivially implementable. However, the paper itself notes that most long-text retrieval benchmarks (DOCCI, DCI, Urban1k) share the same summary-first caption structure, which can mask residual position bias in standard evaluations; the authors call for new benchmarks that better reflect real-world retrieval over chunked document passages. An open question is whether the remaining position sensitivity in SigLIP/SigLIP2 (−6% under permutation even after DeBias-CLIP) can be further reduced, and whether the approach generalizes to even longer contexts beyond the 248-token window tested here.
