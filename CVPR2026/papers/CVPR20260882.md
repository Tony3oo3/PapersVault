## Problem

Existing agentic models are locked into a single, human-specified action space (e.g., raw keyboard/mouse events, grounding coordinates, or high-level API calls), making them brittle when the optimal interaction granularity shifts mid-trajectory. No prior work formulates the choice of action space itself as a learnable decision variable, so agents cannot autonomously switch between high-level and low-level interfaces to balance efficiency and precision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260882/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a first-person RGB frame at 360x640x3 resolution together with a natural-language task instruction. At each step it emits a single action string whose surface form can belong to any of several heterogeneous subspaces (grounding boxes, motion primitives, raw keyboard/mouse sequences, latent tokens, or language-level commands); a deterministic parser maps the string to a canonical raw representation that the environment executes, making the reward agnostic to which subspace was chosen.

## Method

CrossHA is built on Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct and trained through a three-stage curriculum: (1) Mixed-Space SFT on a balanced dataset spanning all action subspaces to establish decoding competence; (2) Single-Turn RL (STRL) using GRPO with an action-space-agnostic reward that credits any surface form whose parsed raw action matches ground truth, teaching the model to strategically pick the most reliable interface per step; (3) Multi-Turn RL (MTRL) using GRPO over full Minecraft episodes with a binary success reward minus a token-length penalty, which drives the policy to prefer concise high-level actions when they suffice and fall back to fine-grained primitives only when necessary. The STRL stage also produces a relabeled dataset that initializes MTRL with the model's learned space preferences, accelerating convergence. The key architectural insight is that action-space selection is not a fixed routing rule but an emergent policy behavior shaped entirely by RL rewards over heterogeneous trajectories.

## Results

On the OpenHA benchmark (800+ Minecraft tasks), CrossHA achieves the highest Average Success Rate (ASR) of 54.6% across all tasks, substantially outperforming the next-best VLM-based agent Game-TARS (42.2%) and OpenHA (31.5%). CrossHA also attains the best FT (Finished Tasks) in Mine Blocks (94.7%) and Craft Items (83.3%), and the best ASR in Craft Items (72.7%) and Kill Entities (58.1%). Notably, these gains are achieved with RL fine-tuning on only 30 tasks, yet generalize to 800+ unseen evaluation tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrossHA achieves the highest overall ASR (54.6%) among all compared agents, surpassing Game-TARS (42.2%) by 12.4 points and OpenHA (31.5%) by 23.1 points. Against the strongest single-action-space baselines, CrossHA wins decisively on ASR: GroundingHA reaches 90.1% ASR on Kill Entities but only 27.5% on Craft Items, while CrossHA maintains 58.1% and 72.7% respectively, demonstrating balanced omni-category performance. On FT, JARVIS-VLA (63.8%) and OpenHA (62.8%) slightly edge out CrossHA (58.7%) on All Tasks, but CrossHA dominates in Mine Blocks FT (94.7% vs. 80.0% for OpenHA) and Craft Items FT (83.3% vs. 80.0%). Compared to instruction-conditioned policies (VPT, STEVE-1, ROCKET-1), CrossHA improves All-Tasks ASR by 39–51 points. The ablation variant without STRL (41.6% ASR) confirms that the single-turn RL stage contributes a 13-point ASR gain, validating the three-stage training pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of hierarchical agentic models (OpenHA, JARVIS-VLA, MotionHA, GroundingHA) that integrate multiple action spaces within a single architecture, but departs from them by formulating action-space selection as a learnable policy variable optimized via reinforcement learning rather than a fixed, human-specified design choice. The training pipeline builds on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) for stable, resource-efficient policy optimization, and leverages Qwen2-VL as the multimodal backbone. The key conceptual departure is the three-stage curriculum (mixed-space SFT → single-turn RL → multi-turn RL) that progressively teaches the model to switch between high-level and low-level interfaces, whereas prior unified agents like OpenHA enable flexible switching during execution without explicitly optimizing it as a decision variable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that RL fine-tuning on only 30 tasks generalizes to 800+ unseen Minecraft tasks, with a smaller ID-to-OOD performance gap than specialized single-space agents, suggests that dynamic action-space selection acts as a regularizer against overfitting to narrow interaction modes. The finding that a cheap single-turn RL warm-start (STRL) substantially accelerates convergence in the expensive multi-turn RL stage is practically useful for training pipelines. Open questions include whether this framework transfers to real-world robotics where safety constraints and latency budgets differ radically from Minecraft, and whether the approach scales beyond a single 7B VLM family. The paper is limited to one environment and one model architecture, so broader validation is needed.
