## Problem

Existing video realism evaluators are trained on low-quality generated videos with LLM-generated annotations, resulting in scores that misalign with human preferences and lack interpretability. No prior benchmark provides both a rigorously defined quantitative scoring rubric grounded in human perception and a chain-of-thought rationale that explains *why* a video received a particular realism score.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263459/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluator takes a single generated video clip (human-centric, sourced from 7 text-to-video models) as input and produces a structured three-part textual output: a natural-language <Problem Description> categorizing errors into human states, object states, and human-object interactions; a <Standard Adherence> section mapping observed errors to quantitative thresholds (e.g., >40% of visual composition or >80% of temporal duration → score 1); and a final <Answer> with an integer score from 1 to 5. The scoring explicitly decouples from text-prompt alignment, focusing solely on physical and biomechanical plausibility of the visual content.

## Method

VideoRealEval is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B fine-tuned via LoRA (lr=1e-4, 10 epochs, 4×V100) on 2,309 videos from VideoRealDataset, where each video was annotated by three human raters using a strictly defined 5-point scale based on the spatial proportion of erroneous content and the temporal fraction of error frames. The key design choice is a three-step chain-of-thought annotation protocol (<Problem Description> → <Standard Adherence> → <Answer>) that forces the model to first identify specific realism violations, then reason about their severity against explicit quantitative criteria, and finally emit a score—rather than producing an opaque single number. CoT responses were polished with DeepSeek and manually verified for logical consistency. Evaluation uses PLCC, SROCC for score alignment and Sentence-BERT cosine similarity (STS) for rationale quality, with the model achieving 57.07% PLCC and 56.78% SROCC, outperforming all prior evaluators including VideoPhy-2-AutoEval and Gemini-2.5-pro.

## Results

On VideoRealDataset, VideoRealEval achieves a PLCC of 57.07% and SROCC of 56.78%, surpassing the strongest baseline VideoPhy-2-AutoEval* (trained on the same data) by approximately 6 percentage points in both metrics (50.89% PLCC, 50.69% SROCC). It also attains the highest STS score of 56.17%, indicating superior semantic alignment in its chain-of-thought rationales. Among zero-shot multimodal models, Gemini-2.5-pro (33.79% PLCC) and UnifiedReward-Think (34.99% PLCC) are the closest competitors, yet both trail VideoRealEval by over 20 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VideoRealEval outperforms all 13 baselines on the primary VideoRealDataset evaluation. The closest competitor is VideoPhy-2-AutoEval* (retrained on VideoRealDataset), which reaches 50.89% PLCC / 50.69% SROCC, still trailing VideoRealEval by ~6 points; the original VideoPhy-2-AutoEval scores 38.31% PLCC. Among zero-shot MLLMs, Gemini-2.5-pro (33.79% PLCC) and UnifiedReward-Think (34.99% PLCC) lead, while VideoCon-Physics (33.72%) and VideoScore-v1.1 (23.47%) lag further behind. Smaller open-source models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 18.93%, LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B at 3.10%, VideoLLaVA at 2.11%) show negligible alignment with human preferences. On the generalization benchmarks (Table 10), VideoRealEval surpasses GPT-4o on Video-Bench Temporal Consistency (SROCC 0.474 vs. 0.402) and EvalCrafter Temporal Consistency (0.573 vs. 0.567), but is marginally below GPT-4o on Video-Bench Motion Effects (0.508 vs. 0.514). Traditional metrics like CLIP (0.260) and RAFT (0.329) remain far below all VLM-based evaluators on the generalization tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of video generation evaluation and chain-of-thought reasoning, departing from prior realism benchmarks (VideoPhy-2, PhyGen-Bench, VideoCon-Physics) that relied on LLM-generated annotations and opaque scoring. Rather than building on a specific method lineage, it repurposes a general-purpose VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) with LoRA fine-tuning on a meticulously human-annotated dataset, introducing a three-step CoT rationale (Problem Description → Standard Adherence → Answer) that no prior video realism benchmark provides. The scoring criteria are deliberately simplified to a 5-point integer scale defined by error proportion in frame and duration, contrasting with the multi-dimensional, overlapping taxonomies used by VideoScore and VideoPhy-2.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark addresses a critical gap: existing video generation models produce visually impressive but physically implausible human-centric content, and no prior evaluator reliably captures this with interpretable reasoning. The finding that VideoPhy-2's published scores systematically misalign with human preferences (PCR 0.754 vs. 0.925) raises questions about the reliability of other LLM-annotated benchmarks in the field. Open questions include whether the 5-point scale and three error categories generalize beyond human-centric scenarios, whether the evaluator's performance (PLCC ~57%) is sufficient for practical model selection, and whether the annotation protocol scales to the thousands of videos needed for large-scale generation model evaluation.
