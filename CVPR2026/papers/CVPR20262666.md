## Problem

Existing generalizable 3D reconstruction methods focus exclusively on geometry and appearance, while semantic-aware approaches like LangSplat and Feature-3DGS require costly per-scene optimization. More recent unified methods (LSM, UniForward) are built on DUSt3R and restricted to two-view inputs, making multi-view extension expensive and producing fragmented reconstructions due to the absence of global 3D context. No prior framework jointly delivers high-fidelity novel view synthesis, open-vocabulary 3D semantic segmentation, and depth estimation from arbitrary unposed multi-view images in a single feed-forward pass.

## Input / Output

The model accepts an arbitrary number of unposed RGB images (evaluated at 2, 4, 8, and 16 views) along with per-camera intrinsics (focal length and principal point), and at inference time also takes free-form text prompts for semantic queries. It outputs a dense set of pixel-aligned 3D Gaussian primitives, each carrying position, scale, rotation, opacity, color, and a high-dimensional semantic feature vector. These Gaussians are rendered via a differentiable rasterizer to produce novel views, depth maps, and per-pixel semantic segmentation maps (via cosine similarity against CLIP text prototypes), all in a single forward pass at 256×256 resolution.

## Method

Uni3R employs a Cross-View Transformer encoder (initialized from VGGT) that alternates intra-frame self-attention with cross-frame global attention to fuse features from all input views into a globally consistent, view-agnostic latent representation, with a learnable camera token ensuring permutation equivariance. A DPT decoder followed by dedicated MLP heads regresses per-pixel 3D Gaussian parameters, including a semantic feature vector compressed by an autoencoder to keep rendering memory tractable. Training combines three losses: a photometric loss (L1 + LPIPS) for rendering fidelity, a cosine-similarity semantic loss distilling frozen LSeg 2D features into the 3D Gaussian field, and a novel point-map geometry loss that uses confidence-masked Chamfer distance against frozen VGGT point predictions to enforce structural consistency and prevent local minima. The intrinsic embedding (focal length and principal point projected and concatenated before patch tokenization) resolves monocular scale ambiguity, while the scale is normalized by the median predicted depth to maintain cross-scene stability.

## Results

On ScanNet (Table 1), Uni3R achieves state-of-the-art across all three tasks simultaneously: 0.5584 mIoU and 0.8268 accuracy for target-view open-vocabulary segmentation, 25.53 dB PSNR / 0.8727 SSIM / 0.1380 LPIPS for novel view synthesis, and 3.87 AbsRel / 61.37 τ for depth estimation. On RE10K with 8 views (Table 4), Uni3R reaches 26.63 dB PSNR and 0.874 SSIM, outperforming the strongest pose-free baseline VicaSplat (24.50 dB) by over 2 dB. Zero-shot on Mip-NeRF360 (Table 5), Uni3R attains 19.20 dB PSNR at 8 views, surpassing AnySplat (18.81 dB) without any domain-specific training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni3R outperforms all feed-forward baselines on ScanNet: it beats LSM (the closest unified competitor) by +0.051 mIoU on target-view segmentation and +1.14 dB PSNR on rendering, while also eliminating LSM's dependency on ground-truth point clouds. Against per-scene methods, Uni3R surpasses Feature-3DGS by +0.136 mIoU (target) and +1.04 dB PSNR while being ~67× faster (0.162 s vs. ~18 min + SfM). On RE10K (2-view, Table 3), Uni3R edges out NoPoSplat by +0.04 dB PSNR and VicaSplat by +0.04 dB, and clearly beats pose-required methods PixelSplat and MVSplat by ~1.7 dB. At 8 views (Table 4), the gap widens: Uni3R leads VicaSplat by +2.13 dB PSNR on RE10K and +2.36 dB on ScanNet. On zero-shot Mip-NeRF360 (Table 5), Uni3R surpasses AnySplat by +0.39 dB PSNR at 8 views, though AnySplat retains a slight LPIPS advantage (0.308 vs. 0.355), suggesting Uni3R's perceptual quality degrades more in the out-of-domain regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

Uni3R sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting methods (PixelSplat, MVSplat, NoPoSplat) that eliminate per-scene optimization but typically require known camera poses; (2) pose-free reconstruction from DUSt3R/MASt3R and its extensions (Splatt3R, LSM, UniForward), which are architecturally limited to two-view inputs and struggle with global consistency; and (3) semantic 3DGS methods (LangSplat, Feature-3DGS) that embed CLIP features into Gaussians but rely on costly per-scene optimization. Uni3R departs from the DUSt3R lineage by adopting VGGT's Cross-View Transformer to fuse an arbitrary number of views into a globally consistent latent, and from the semantic-3DGS lineage by making the entire pipeline feed-forward and pose-free. The point-map-guided Chamfer loss, using a frozen VGGT as a soft geometric prior, is a novel training strategy not present in prior feed-forward splatting work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying novel view synthesis, open-vocabulary 3D segmentation, and depth estimation in a single 0.16-second forward pass (vs. ~18 minutes for per-scene Feature-3DGS), Uni3R makes dense 3D scene understanding practically deployable in robotics and AR pipelines where per-scene optimization is infeasible. The multi-view generalization beyond DUSt3R's two-view constraint opens the door to video-length inputs, though the non-monotonic performance across view counts (Table 6: mIoU drops at 4 views before recovering at 8) suggests the model has not yet fully learned to exploit additional views. A notable limitation is the significant performance gap on unbounded outdoor scenes (Mip-NeRF360 PSNR ~17–19 vs. ~25–28 indoors), indicating the method's geometric priors are tuned for bounded indoor environments. Open questions include whether the VGGT-based geometric supervision can be replaced by self-supervised signals to reduce dependency on a frozen foundation model, and whether the approach scales to very large or dynamic scenes.
