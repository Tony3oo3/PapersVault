## Problem

Diffusion priors improve sparse-view 3D reconstruction by denoising rendered novel views, but they inevitably introduce hallucinated content—elements absent from the input views—into the final 3D model. While prior work has tried to make diffusion priors more view-consistent, multi-view inconsistency persists due to the generative nature of diffusion. No prior method explicitly detects and filters hallucinated pixels before they are incorporated into the 3D representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261817/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes N calibrated RGB images with known camera poses (9 sparse views at 960×540 in experiments) and produces a 3DGS model for novel view synthesis. Intermediate outputs include pixel-wise hallucination score maps (thresholded at 0.9 into binary masks) and K fused augmented novel-view images per target pose. The hallucination score network itself is trained on a small curated set of 116 scenes where ground-truth scores are computed as the MAE between diffusion-generated and ground-truth images, requiring a full 3DGS→render→diffusion pipeline per sample.

## Method

HAD combines a pretrained single-step diffusion prior (Difix3D) with a hallucination score network that predicts per-pixel reliability for each augmented image. The score network uses the frozen feature backbone of LVSM (a feedforward NVS transformer) as a multi-view encoder—exploiting its multi-view reasoning learned from large-scale 3D data—paired with a lightweight 3-layer UNet head trained via L2 loss against MAE ground-truth on a small curated dataset. A multi-sampling strategy generates K augmented versions of each novel view by conditioning the diffusion prior on K different input reference views, then fuses them pixel-wise via ArgMin over hallucination scores to select the most reliable pixel from each candidate. During 3DGS optimization, the inverted hallucination mask is applied via Hadamard product to the rendering loss, effectively excluding unreliable pixels from supervision and enabling a simplified single-phase training (no pre-trained 3DGS initialization needed, unlike Difix3D's two-phase approach).

## Results

On DL3DV (in-domain, 9 input views), HAD achieves PSNR 22.134, SSIM 0.757, and LPIPS 0.190, surpassing the strongest diffusion-prior baseline Difix3D (21.355 / 0.734 / 0.199) by 0.78 dB in PSNR. On MipNeRF 360 (cross-domain, 9 input views), HAD reaches PSNR 18.689, SSIM 0.509, and LPIPS 0.334, improving over Difix3D by 0.69 dB and over GenFusion (video-diffusion-based) by 0.33 dB. In the post-rendering setting on DL3DV, HAD+ also outperforms Difix3D+ (21.213 vs. 20.673 PSNR), confirming that hallucination-aware masking benefits both training-time and inference-time refinement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HAD outperforms all seven baselines across both datasets. Against the primary diffusion-prior competitor Difix3D [41], HAD gains 0.78 dB PSNR on DL3DV (22.134 vs. 21.355) and 0.69 dB on MipNeRF 360 (18.689 vs. 18.001), with consistent SSIM and LPIPS improvements. On MipNeRF 360, HAD also surpasses GenFusion [43] (which uses video diffusion for better multi-view consistency) by 0.33 dB PSNR and 0.131 LPIPS, showing that explicit hallucination masking is more effective than relying solely on temporally coherent generation. Among feedforward NVS baselines, LVSM [20] (19.855 PSNR) and DepthSplat [44] (18.324 PSNR) trail HAD by 2.3 and 3.8 dB respectively on DL3DV, confirming that optimization-based 3DGS with hallucination-aware augmentation substantially outperforms purely feedforward approaches. Against vanilla 3DGS variants (Gsplat-3DGS [21] at 19.004 and Gsplat-MCMC [22] at 20.532 PSNR on DL3DV), HAD improves by 3.1 and 1.6 dB, demonstrating the value of diffusion priors when combined with hallucination filtering. FSGS [52] (17.940 PSNR on MipNeRF 360) is outperformed by 0.75 dB, further validating the approach in the cross-domain setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Difix3D pipeline, which uses a single-step diffusion prior to denoise 3DGS-rendered novel views conditioned on a reference input image, by adding an explicit hallucination score estimation stage. The key architectural departure is repurposing the pretrained feature backbone of LVSM, a feedforward novel view synthesis transformer, as a frozen multi-view encoder that provides the contextual understanding needed to detect which pixels in a diffusion-augmented image are hallucinated. The multi-sampling fusion strategy further differentiates the work from Difix3D by conditioning the diffusion prior on multiple reference views and selecting the most reliable pixel across versions, rather than relying on a single nearest reference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Hallucination in diffusion-assisted 3D reconstruction is a widely acknowledged but previously unaddressed failure mode; this paper provides the first explicit mechanism (pixel-wise hallucination score maps with masking) to filter unreliable content before it propagates into the 3D model. The practical benefit is a simplified single-phase 3DGS training loop that avoids Difix3D's two-phase strategy, and the small curated training set (116 scenes) makes the approach accessible. However, the method is bounded by the quality of the pretrained multi-view encoder, is validated only on reconstruction of seen regions, and the cross-domain generalization to unseen-region generation remains an open question that could limit broader applicability.
