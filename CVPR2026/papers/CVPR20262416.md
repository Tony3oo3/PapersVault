## Problem

Reconstruction-based diffusion anomaly detection methods suffer from a fundamental tension between fidelity and efficiency: they require careful noise-strength tuning (stronger noise corrupts normal regions, weaker noise fails to remove anomalies) and computationally expensive multi-step denoising, typically achieving only ~1 FPS. The authors argue these limitations stem from the prevailing 'detection via denoising in RGB space' paradigm, which necessitates explicit reconstruction to compute anomaly scores.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, encodes it into a feature map via a pre-trained EfficientNet-B4 backbone (producing z ∈ R^{C×h×w}), and performs only 3 DDIM inversion steps (timesteps [333, 666, 999]) in this feature space. It outputs an image-level anomaly score computed as the difference between the maximum and minimum channel-wise Euclidean norms plus their sum, and a pixel-level anomaly map obtained by bilinearly upsampled per-pixel norms from feature resolution (h×w) to the original image resolution (H×W).

## Method

InvAD replaces the conventional denoising pipeline (x₀ → x_t → x̂₀) with a forward inversion that directly infers the final latent state x_T from the input by tracing the DDIM probability flow ODE trajectory using the Euler method. The key insight is that since the diffusion model is trained only on normal data, normal images map to high-density regions of the Gaussian noise prior while anomalies map to low-density regions, so anomaly scoring reduces to measuring deviation under the known prior—no reconstruction is needed. To achieve efficiency, only S=3 inversion steps are used (vs. T=1000 total diffusion steps), which is possible because the norm-based scoring is robust to the Euler step size. The entire pipeline operates in pre-trained feature space rather than pixel space, further reducing computation. The anomaly score combines negative log-likelihood under the Gaussian prior with a spatial max-min norm difference to mitigate the reverse-scoring problem for sparse anomalies, and the method is plug-and-play at the inference stage of existing diffusion-based AD models.

## Results

On MVTecAD (multi-class), InvAD achieves 99.0 image-level AU-ROC and 97.5 pixel-level AU-ROC with 88.1 FPS, outperforming the strongest diffusion-based baseline OmiAD (98.8 / 97.7 AU-ROC, 39.4 FPS) by ~2.2× in inference speed while matching or exceeding accuracy. On VisA and MPDD, InvAD leads in image-level AU-ROC (96.9 and 96.5) and achieves 74.1 and 120 FPS respectively. On the BMAD medical benchmark (single-class), InvAD attains the best mAD of 87.2 with 88 FPS, surpassing PatchCore (86.4 mAD, 20 FPS) by 4.4× in throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest diffusion-based baseline OmiAD (1 NFE, 39.4 FPS on MVTecAD), InvAD matches or exceeds image-level AU-ROC on all three industrial benchmarks (99.0 vs 98.8 on MVTecAD, 96.9 vs 95.3 on VisA, 96.5 vs 93.7 on MPDD) while running 2.2×–2.4× faster (88.1 vs 39.4 FPS). On pixel-level AU-ROC, InvAD is marginally below OmiAD on MVTecAD (97.5 vs 97.7) and MPDD (98.3 vs 98.6) but leads on VisA (99.1 vs 98.9). Against non-diffusion baselines, InvAD outperforms HVQ-Trans (98.0 img AU-ROC, 5.6 FPS) and DiAD (97.2 img AU-ROC, 0.1 FPS) on MVTecAD image-level detection by 1.0 and 1.8 points respectively, with 15×–880× speedup. On the BMAD medical benchmark, InvAD (mAD 87.2, 88 FPS) surpasses PatchCore (86.4, 20 FPS) and RD4AD (84.2, 20 FPS) in both accuracy and throughput. The plug-and-play generalizability test shows that inserting InvAD into DiAD improves image-level AU-ROC by +1.0 and FPS by 880×, while inserting into MDM yields +6.3 AU-ROC and +60.8 FPS, confirming model-agnostic applicability.

## Relation to Prior Work

InvAD departs from the dominant 'detection via denoising in RGB space' paradigm used by prior diffusion-based AD methods (DiAD, GLAD, MDM, OmiAD), which add noise to an input and then iteratively denoise to reconstruct a normal version, scoring via pixel/feature MSE. Instead, it repurposes the DDIM inversion mechanism (Song et al., ICLR 2021) and the PF-ODE formulation (Song et al., ICLR 2021) to trace the trajectory *forward* from data to noise, scoring anomaly by typicality in the Gaussian prior rather than reconstruction error. The training objective remains the standard DDPM epsilon-prediction loss (Ho et al., 2020), and the feature-space diffusion design follows the latent-diffusion approach common in recent AD work, but the inference stage is fundamentally restructured around a few Euler steps of the score function rather than multi-step denoising.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating reconstruction entirely, InvAD removes the noise-strength hyperparameter that has plagued diffusion-based AD and reduces NFE from 10–750 to just 3, yielding 88 FPS on MVTecAD versus 39 FPS for the prior best diffusion method (OmiAD, 1 NFE). The plug-and-play design means it can be grafted onto existing diffusion-based AD pipelines (demonstrated with DiAD and MDM) without retraining, making it a practical drop-in for real-time industrial inspection. Open questions include whether diffusion distillation can compress the 3-step inversion to a single NFE, and whether the inversion-based scoring generalizes to video or 3D anomaly detection where the PF-ODE trajectory structure may differ.
