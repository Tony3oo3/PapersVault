## Problem

Existing robotic manipulation planners either generate computationally expensive semantic artifacts (subgoal images or text) or plan in latent spaces that treat proprioceptive and semantic modalities independently. Because kinematic and visual transitions are causally coupled through the underlying action, unimodal latent planning risks producing trajectories that are physically or logically inconsistent. CLaD addresses this by explicitly modeling how proprioceptive and semantic states co-evolve under actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261069/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a proprioceptive state vector (joint angles and velocities), an RGB image, and a language instruction. The image and instruction are fused via FiLM through a frozen DecisionNCE VLM to form a semantic state, while the proprioceptive state is encoded separately; both are tokenized into short sequences (4 tokens each, hidden dim 1024). The output is a diffusion-policy action chunk of horizon τ=6, generated by denoising conditioned on predicted latent foresights that are adaptively modulated with the current observation via FiLM layers.

## Method

CLaD is a two-stage framework. In Stage 1, modality-specific cross-attention modules extract transition representations from past states and actions, and an asymmetric cross-attention layer (proprioceptive transitions query semantic transitions) produces a compact cross-modal dynamics vector z_dyn via Perceiver-style pooling. Lightweight MLPs then predict future latent foresights for both modalities, trained with a BYOL-style self-supervised objective (EMA target encoders, L2-normalized MSE) plus auxiliary L1 reconstruction losses that anchor the latent space to observable proprioceptive and visual quantities. In Stage 2, a frozen CLaD provides foresight embeddings that are FiLM-modulated with current observations and fed as conditioning to a standard DDPM diffusion policy, which learns to denoise action chunks. The entire system uses only 0.66B parameters (0.1B VLM + 0.33B CLaD + 0.23B policy) and achieves 94.7% on LIBERO-LONG, competitive with 7B OpenVLA.

## Results

On LIBERO-LONG (10 long-horizon manipulation tasks), CLaD achieves a 94.7% average success rate with only 0.66B parameters, surpassing OpenVLA (93.8%, 7B) and π₀.₅ (93.2%, 3.3B). CLaD also outperforms all smaller-scale baselines: LBP (88.6%, 0.19B), Seer (87.7%, 0.32B), and SuSIE (76.3%, 0.86B). In terms of computational efficiency, CLaD runs at 25 Hz inference with 4 GB memory, compared to 6 Hz / 15 GB for OpenVLA and 10 Hz / 19 GB for π₀.₅.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLaD (94.7%) outperforms all seven baselines on LIBERO-LONG average success rate. Against the largest VLA models, CLaD edges out OpenVLA (93.8%, 7B) by 0.9 points and π₀.₅ (93.2%, 3.3B) by 1.5 points, while using 10.6× and 5× fewer parameters respectively. Among same-scale latent-planning methods, CLaD surpasses LBP (88.6%, 0.19B) by 6.1 points and UVA (90.0%, 0.5B) by 4.7 points, with a planning latency of 0.012 s (vs. 0.008 s for LBP and 0.195 s for UVA). Against semantic-generation planners, CLaD leads Seer (87.7%, 0.32B) by 7.0 points and SuSIE (76.3%, 0.86B) by 18.4 points. The most notable per-task gap is on Task 4 (put bowl in drawer and close it), where CLaD scores 93.7% versus only 22.0% for π₀ and 78.0% for OpenVLA, suggesting cross-modal dynamics help with multi-step sequential subtasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLaD extends the latent-space planning paradigm (LBP, TD-MPC2, Dreamer) by introducing an explicit cross-modal structure: rather than planning in a single unimodal latent space, it learns how proprioceptive and semantic transitions co-evolve under actions via asymmetric cross-attention. The self-supervised foresight prediction borrows the EMA target-encoder mechanism from BYOL-style SSL, while the action generation stage is a standard Diffusion Policy conditioned on predicted latent subgoals. The key departure from prior cross-modal representation work (R3M, GNFactor, PVP) is shifting from aligning static observations at individual timesteps to modeling correlations between dynamic transitions, formalized as proprioceptive transitions querying semantic transitions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 0.66B-parameter model matching 7B OpenVLA on LIBERO-LONG demonstrates that grounded cross-modal latent foresight can substitute for large-scale VLM capacity in long-horizon manipulation, with 25 Hz inference and 4 GB memory enabling real-time deployment on consumer hardware. The ablation showing proprioceptive foresight alone actively degrades performance below the no-foresight baseline (50.4% vs 84.8%) highlights that kinematic predictions are misleading without semantic grounding—a non-obvious finding for practitioners combining modalities. Open questions include whether the two-stage framework generalizes beyond LIBERO-LONG to diverse embodiments and mobile manipulation, whether Stage 1 dynamics pre-training can be amortized over large-scale heterogeneous datasets, and whether spatially-structured or object-centric foresights can address the perceptual-ambility weakness (Task 9: 81.3%).
