## Problem

Existing red-teaming methods for text-to-image models face a fundamental tension: approaches that bypass safety filters often fail to produce genuinely toxic images, while those that generate harmful content rely on easily detectable adversarial prompts. This imbalance leaves a gap in reliable tools for evaluating the safety of defended T2I systems, as no prior method simultaneously achieves high image toxicity, filter evasion, and prompt stealthiness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a seed text prompt from a curated dataset spanning three harmful domains (nudity, violence, hate) and produces a single adversarial text prompt designed to elicit a toxic RGB image from a safeguarded T2I model while evading both text-based and image-based safety filters. The generated prompts are constrained to contain no blacklisted sensitive terms, maintain semantic fluency, and differ from recent prompts in a dynamic reference pool, ensuring diversity across the attack suite.

## Method

GenBreak fine-tunes a Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct LLM in two stages: first, supervised fine-tuning on two curated datasets (a Category Rewrite Dataset of 15,000 prompt-rewrite pairs generated via Gemini 2.0 Flash, and a Pre-Attack Dataset of ~6,000 high-scoring attack instances collected by iteratively attacking SD 2.1); second, reinforcement learning via GRPO against a surrogate T2I model (SD 2.1 or SD 3 Medium) equipped with an integrated three-component safety filter. The RL stage employs a six-component reward function combining image toxicity (aggregated from MHSC, LLaVAGuard, and NudeNet), a bypass reward gated by toxicity, a clean reward penalizing blacklisted terms, and three diversity rewards (lexical via negative SelfBLEU, semantic via sentence-embedding cosine distance, and image via DreamSim perceptual similarity) all computed against a dynamic reference pool of the most recent 1000 test cases. A prompt template encodes three evasion strategies—prompt dilution, image obfuscation, and conceptual confusion—to guide exploration, and a rule-based Symbol Regulation Reward suppresses degenerate symbolic variations in later training steps.

## Results

On safeguarded Stable Diffusion 2.1, GenBreak achieves Toxic Bypass Rates (TBR) of 60.8% (nudity), 89.7% (violence), and 84.6% (hate), with corresponding Toxic Clean Bypass Rates (TCBR) of 57.9%, 86.2%, and 78.9%—dramatically outperforming all baselines whose TBRs range from 0% to 18.7%. Average image toxicity remains high (0.805, 0.875, 0.542) while maintaining prompt fluency. In black-box transfer attacks on three commercial T2I APIs, GenBreak achieves single-attempt TBRs of 70% (Leonardo.Ai), 30% (fal.ai), and 47% (Stability AI) in the nudity domain, compared to 0–22% for the strongest baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenBreak dominates all six baselines on the two most critical metrics—TBR and TCBR—by a wide margin. Vanilla RL [24] and CRT [10] achieve high image toxicity (0.76–0.99) but near-zero bypass rates (≤2.9% TBR), confirming that generating harmful images without evading filters is insufficient. Prompt-based methods (SneakyPrompt [37], ART [16], MMA-Diffusion [36], PGJ [12]) achieve modest bypass (0–4.8% TBR) but produce images with very low toxicity (0.04–0.25), failing to generate genuinely harmful content. GenBreak's TBR of 60.8–89.7% represents a 12–180× improvement over the best baseline on each category. The trade-off is in diversity: GenBreak's LexDiv and SemDiv scores are lower than CRT or MMA-Diffusion, reflecting the narrower solution space imposed by simultaneously optimizing toxicity, stealth, and fluency. On commercial transfer attacks, GenBreak achieves 70% TBR on Leonardo.Ai (nudity) versus 0% for CRT and 8% for PGJ, demonstrating that the multi-objective RL training generalizes to unseen safety mechanisms.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenBreak extends the RL-based red-teaming paradigm from CRT (Hong et al., ICLR 2024) to the T2I domain, adapting its curiosity-driven exploration to a surrogate diffusion model environment. It departs from prior T2I jailbreak methods (SneakyPrompt, ART, MMA-Diffusion, PGJ) by formulating the problem as a multi-objective optimization that simultaneously maximizes image toxicity, filter bypass, keyword-cleanliness, and diversity—addressing the fundamental stealth-toxicity trade-off that each prior method resolves by sacrificing one objective. The two-stage SFT-then-GRPO pipeline, with a dynamic reference pool for diversity rewards and a composite toxicity evaluator aggregating three expert models, represents the methodological core distinguishing it from single-reward or iterative-search baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 70% single-attempt toxic bypass rate on Leonardo.Ai's FLUX.1 in the nudity domain is a concrete, actionable finding that deployed T2I services have significant exploitable vulnerabilities, providing safety teams with a systematic evaluation tool rather than ad-hoc prompt lists. A key limitation is the gray-box training assumption (access to toxicity scores during RL), though the authors argue this is realistic for internal red-teaming and that transfer attacks work in pure black-box settings. Open questions include whether the discovered vulnerabilities will be patched by commercial providers, whether the approach generalizes to video or 3D generation, and whether the multi-objective reward design can be extended to handle more nuanced harm categories beyond nudity, violence, and hate.
