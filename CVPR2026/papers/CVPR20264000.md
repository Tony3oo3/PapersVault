## Problem

Gaussian-centric representations offer an efficient yet expressive scene description for autonomous driving, but their reliance on dense manual annotations hinders scalable deployment. While self-supervised pre-training has been explored for voxel or BEV representations, a holistic pre-training strategy covering the full lifecycle of Gaussian-centric models (perception, forecasting, and planning) remains unexplored. Additionally, latent world models have been applied to motion planning but have rarely been integrated with Gaussian-centric representations for perception and forecasting tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 6-camera RGB images at 2 Hz keyframes from nuScenes sequences. During pre-training, sparse LiDAR depth provides geometric supervision, Metric3D generates dense pseudo-depth maps, and Grounded SAM produces semantic labels—all without human annotation. The model outputs 25,600 sparse 3D semantic Gaussians per frame, which are then splatted into 3D/4D occupancy grids (18 semantic classes) or used to condition ego trajectory prediction over a 3-second horizon. A key design choice is rasterizing sparse Gaussians into a dense BEV latent grid, which resolves the permutation-equivalence problem of raw Gaussian queries and enables inter-frame temporal supervision.

## Method

DLWM is a two-stage self-supervised pre-training paradigm. Stage 1 trains a Gaussian perception module (25,600 learnable queries with 4 transformer blocks, 4D sparse convolution for temporal propagation) to reconstruct multi-view depth and semantic images via differentiable alpha-blending rendering, supervised by sparse LiDAR depth, dense Metric3D pseudo-depth, and Grounded SAM semantics. Stage 2 trains two independent latent world models: (a) a Gaussian-flow-guided model that predicts per-Gaussian displacement vectors, propagates them to the next frame via ego-motion alignment, rasterizes the result into a BEV latent, and supervises it against the frozen perception module's BEV output—improving occupancy perception and 4D forecasting; and (b) an ego-planning-guided model that conditions future scene queries on the predicted trajectory via Motion-Aware Layer Normalization and predicts the next BEV latent, jointly optimizing trajectory regression and scene reconstruction for motion planning. The critical insight is that BEV rasterization of sparse Gaussians provides a dense, height-preserving latent with clear inter-frame correspondence, making it suitable for temporal supervision where raw Gaussian queries cannot be directly matched across frames.

## Results

On the SurroundOcc-nuScenes validation set, DLWM achieves 21.85 mIoU and 34.61 IoU for 3D occupancy perception, surpassing the previous SOTA GaussianWorld (21.79 mIoU) and the no-pre-training baseline (20.83 mIoU) by +1.02 mIoU. For 4D occupancy forecasting (1–3 s average), DLWM reaches 17.77 mIoU and 30.60 IoU, outperforming OccWorld-O (17.14 mIoU) and the baseline (15.09 mIoU). On nuScenes motion planning, DLWM attains 0.46 m average L2 error and 0.19% average collision rate, tying BEV-Planner on L2 while reducing collision rate from 0.24% (baseline) to 0.19%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

For 3D occupancy perception, DLWM outperforms all Gaussian-centric baselines: GaussianWorld (21.79 mIoU) by +0.06 mIoU, GaussianFormer-2 (20.82 mIoU) by +1.03 mIoU, and GaussianFormer (19.10 mIoU) by +2.75 mIoU. It also surpasses dense-grid methods like SurroundOcc (20.30 mIoU) and OccFormer (19.03 mIoU) by 1.55 and 2.82 mIoU respectively. For 4D occupancy forecasting, DLWM (17.77 mIoU avg) beats OccWorld-O (17.14 mIoU), the strongest 3D-Occ-input method, and substantially outperforms camera-only methods like PreWorld (9.55 mIoU) and all OccWorld camera variants (≤8.62 mIoU). On motion planning, DLWM (0.46 m L2, 0.19% collision) ties BEV-Planner on L2 while achieving a lower collision rate (0.19% vs 0.49%), and outperforms the latent world model LAW (0.61 m L2, 0.30% collision) by 0.15 m L2. Compared to multi-task systems like UniAD (0.69 m L2) and PARA-Drive (0.48 m L2), DLWM achieves better L2 with fewer auxiliary tasks, confirming the effectiveness of the dual latent world model pre-training without relying on detection, tracking, or mapping heads.

## Relation to Prior Work

DLWM sits at the intersection of three research threads: Gaussian-centric scene representations (GaussianFormer, GaussianWorld), self-supervised rendering-based pre-training (UniPAD, SQS, ViDAR), and latent world models for temporal prediction (LAW, SSR). It departs from prior Gaussian-centric work by introducing a unified two-stage pre-training paradigm that covers the full downstream lifecycle—perception, forecasting, and planning—rather than targeting a single task. The key conceptual departure is using BEV rasterization of sparse Gaussians as the latent space for temporal supervision, which resolves the permutation-equivalence problem that makes direct inter-frame Gaussian query supervision infeasible. The ego-planning-guided world model extends the LAW/SSR line by conditioning future latent prediction on predicted trajectories rather than observed ego motion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides the first holistic self-supervised pre-training recipe specifically designed for Gaussian-centric autonomous driving models, offering a practical blueprint for teams adopting 3DGS-based representations who lack large-scale labeled data. The dual latent world model design—separating perception/forecasting from planning—suggests that different downstream tasks benefit from different temporal supervision signals, a finding that could inform future multi-task pre-training. Limitations include reliance on LiDAR depth and foundation-model-generated semantic labels (Grounded SAM) during Stage 1, which partially undermines the 'self-supervised' claim, and evaluation is confined to nuScenes/SurroundOcc at a single scale. Open questions include whether the two-stage pipeline can be collapsed into end-to-end training, whether the approach generalizes to higher-resolution or longer-horizon forecasting, and whether the BEV latent bottleneck limits expressiveness for very dynamic scenes.
