## Problem

Standard Neural Processes assume Gaussian likelihoods, restricting predictive distributions to unimodal forms and failing to capture inherently multimodal function behaviors common in real-world tasks. The design of latent variable structures and conditioning mechanisms within the NP framework remains underexplored, leaving a gap in how to flexibly represent diverse functional distributions while maintaining tractable inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260497/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a context set of (x, y) pairs (or observed pixel locations and intensity values for image tasks) and produces a predictive mixture distribution over target points, where the mixture weights are the mean of a learned Dirichlet prior. The latent variable z is constrained to an (L−1)-dimensional simplex, yielding a compact compositional task representation. At test time, the predictive distribution admits a closed-form expression—simply the weighted sum of L expert density networks with weights α_l / Σα_l'—so no iterative inference is needed beyond evaluating the prior network once on the context.

## Method

NMDP decomposes the generative model into task-agnostic shared density experts {p_ψ_l} and a task-specific Dirichlet latent variable z ∈ Δ^(L-1) that linearly modulates the mixture over experts. A permutation-invariant context encoder parameterizes the Dirichlet concentration α, while L separate probabilistic density networks (e.g., Gaussian experts) form the generative module. Training departs from standard ELBO-based variational inference: instead, an EM/MM-style surrogate objective is optimized using self-normalized importance sampling with the current prior as proposal, inspired by reweighted wake-sleep. This yields a monotonic-improvement guarantee under exact inference and avoids introducing an auxiliary sleep-phase network. The result is a model that, in the infinite-component limit, connects to Dirichlet-process mixture modeling and can approximate arbitrarily flexible distributions over functions.

## Results

On the 1D synthetic GP regression benchmark (cross-kernel mixture of RBF, Weakly Periodic, and Matérn-5/2), NMDP achieves the best log-likelihood across all three kernels (1.26, 1.19, 1.15), outperforming the strongest baseline TNP (1.23, 1.13, 1.06). On image completion, NMDP attains the highest target log-likelihood on all four datasets, with the largest margin on CIFAR-10 (4.19 vs. TNP's 4.03, a 3.9% relative gain). On multi-output regression, NMDP achieves the lowest MSE on all three benchmarks (SARCOS 0.82, WQ 0.67, SCM20D 0.75), beating the best baselines by 2–9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NMDP outperforms all seven baselines on every dataset and metric reported. The closest competitor is TNP [40], which NMDP surpasses on target log-likelihood across all four image datasets (e.g., CIFAR-10: 4.19 vs. 4.03) and on all three 1D GP kernels (e.g., Matérn-5/2: 1.15 vs. 1.06). Among gradient-based meta-learners, CAVIA [64] is the strongest but trails NMDP by a wide margin (e.g., CIFAR-10 target: 2.85 vs. 4.19). The standard neural process baselines—CNP [13], ANP [25], and ConvCNP [17]—are consistently outperformed, with ANP being the strongest NP variant on CIFAR-10 context (3.95) yet still below NMDP (4.33). DNP [52], which adds geometry-aware biLipschitz regularization, is outperformed on all tasks, suggesting that mixture-density expressiveness provides a more fundamental gain than geometric constraints alone. On multi-output regression (Table 3), NMDP achieves the lowest MSE on SARCOS (0.82 vs. CAVIA 0.84), WQ (0.67 vs. ANP 0.69), and SCM20D (0.75 vs. ConvCNP 0.82), confirming consistent advantages in high-dimensional tabular settings as well.

## Relation to Prior Work

NMDP extends the Neural Process family (CNP, ANP, ConvCNP, TNP, DNP) by replacing the standard Gaussian-likelihood generative module with a mixture-of-experts density network governed by a Dirichlet simplex latent variable, drawing on the classical Mixture Density Network framework (Bishop, 1994) and the conceptual machinery of Dirichlet-process mixture modeling. For optimization, it departs from the ELBO-based variational inference used in prior NPs and instead adopts an importance-weighted EM/MM-style surrogate inspired by the reweighted wake-sleep algorithm (Bornschein & Bengio, 2014), with the key difference that the Dirichlet prior is learned (context-conditioned) rather than fixed. The task-agnostic/task-specific decomposition—shared density experts versus per-task simplex weights—echoes the mixture-of-experts NP of Wang & van Hoof (2022) but introduces a principled probabilistic gating mechanism and a monotonic-improvement guarantee under exact inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NMDP addresses a concrete expressiveness gap in the NP family: standard variants are restricted to unimodal predictive distributions, which limits their ability to model inherently multimodal function behaviors. The Dirichlet simplex representation also yields interpretable task embeddings (visualized via CLR + UMAP), offering a diagnostic tool for understanding task structure that prior NPs lack. Key limitations remain: performance degrades when the test task lies far from the meta-training distribution (high-entropy prior → averaged experts), the finite-expert assumption may be insufficient for highly heterogeneous task spaces, and the particle-based importance sampling adds computational overhead. Open questions include whether the framework scales gracefully to infinite or growing expert sets and whether the EM/MM-style objective can be further tightened (e.g., via sequential importance resampling) to reduce variance in sparse-context regimes.
