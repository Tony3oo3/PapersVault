## Problem

Municipal sewer pipe defect detection requires identifying co-occurring defects from images, but rare defects manifest only after decades of operation, making annotated training samples for all categories prohibitively scarce. Existing multi-label zero-shot learning methods fail in this domain because frozen CLIP image encoders cannot bridge the large domain gap between general pre-training data and the harsh pipe environment (dim lighting, subtle cracks), resulting in critical visual-semantic alignment ambiguity.

## Input / Output

The method takes 224×224 sewer pipe RGB images (producing 14×14=196 ViT patches) and LLM-generated (Gemini-2.5 Flash) category-specific text descriptions as dual inputs, producing a vector of 17 multi-label defect prediction scores. The text side uses two distinct LLM outputs: rich detailed descriptions fed to the CLIP text encoder for visual-semantic alignment, and concise explicit phrases used to construct the category relationship graph for score refinement. Evaluation follows a ranking protocol reporting P@K, R@K, F1@K, and mAP for both pure zero-shot (unseen-only) and generalized zero-shot (seen+unseen) settings.

## Method

SFR-Net follows a three-stage paradigm built on CLIP ViT-B/16. First, the Representation Steering (RS) module inserts lightweight bottleneck adapter blocks (Linear→GELU→Dropout→Linear, hidden dim 128) in parallel with every Transformer layer of both encoders, adding a residual domain-specific correction that achieves parameter-efficient adaptation (6.05M learnable params) without catastrophic forgetting. Second, the Multi-Granularity Evidence Fusion (MEF) module decouples prediction into a global path (CLS token cosine similarity with text embeddings) and a local path (softmax-normalized token-to-text affinity used to aggregate category-specific local evidence through an MLP), then sums both score vectors. Third, the Generalized Relational Score Refining (GR) module constructs a binarized semantic adjacency matrix from CLIP-encoded LLM phrases, runs a two-layer GCN over initial prediction scores to learn a transferable score-correction function from seen-category relationships, and adds the correction to produce final scores. Training uses a Synergistic Contrastive Loss combining a batch-wise Multi-Matching Contrastive term (temperature 0.1) with a margin-based Rank Loss (margin 0.2, weight 10), optimized with AdamW (lr 1e-3) for 8 epochs at batch size 896.

## Results

On the Sewer-ML dataset (1.3M images, 17 categories), SFR-Net achieves 12.58% mAP in ML-ZSL and 43.28% mAP in ML-GZSL, surpassing the strongest baselines (DualCoOp at 6.58% and ML-Decoder at 38.57% respectively). On the private WZ-Pipe dataset (64K images), SFR-Net reaches 9.88% mAP in ML-ZSL—more than 2.4× the second-best method (ML-Decoder, 4.07%)—and 37.42% mAP in ML-GZSL, nearly 38% above MKT (26.99%). The method also introduces only 6.05M learnable parameters with 110.62G FLOPs, comparable to RAM's overhead while delivering substantially higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SFR-Net outperforms all six baselines (CLIP, BiAM, DualCoOp, ML-Decoder, MKT, RAM) across both ML-ZSL and ML-GZSL tasks on both datasets. On Sewer-ML ML-ZSL, SFR-Net's 12.58% mAP nearly doubles DualCoOp (6.58%) and more than doubles ML-Decoder (6.24%), while its F1@1 of 13.59% exceeds the next best (ML-Decoder, 8.22%) by 5.4 points. In ML-GZSL on Sewer-ML, SFR-Net leads ML-Decoder (38.57%) by 4.71% mAP and MKT (23.14%) by 20.14%. On WZ-Pipe, the advantage is even more pronounced: SFR-Net's ML-ZSL mAP of 9.88% is 2.4× ML-Decoder (4.07%) and 3.2× RAM (3.04%), and its ML-GZSL mAP of 37.42% surpasses MKT (26.99%) by 10.43 points. Notably, SFR-Net achieves these gains with only 6.05M learnable parameters—less than half of RAM (13.02M) and far fewer than MKT (112.15M) or ML-Decoder (94.47M)—while maintaining comparable FLOPs (110.62G vs. CLIP's 106.74G).

## Relation to Prior Work

SFR-Net builds on the CLIP-based ML-ZSL lineage (CoOp, CoCoOp, MKT, RAM) but departs from its reliance on frozen or minimally adapted image encoders by inserting parameter-efficient steering adapters (RS) into every transformer layer, drawing from the PEFT/adapter literature (CLIP-Adapter, Visual Prompt Tuning). The multi-granularity evidence fusion (MEF) module extends the standard CLIP global-token matching with a local-token attention path, while the GCN-based score refinement (GR) module repurposes graph message passing to learn a transferable relational correction from seen-category semantics. The use of LLM-generated (Gemini-2.5) defect descriptions as text-side input is a practical departure from the simple template prompts used by most prior ML-ZSL methods, and the WZ-Pipe dataset is the first purpose-built benchmark for ML-ZSL in the sewer inspection domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic application of ML-ZSL to sewer pipe defect detection, a domain where annotation costs are prohibitive and rare defects may only appear after decades of operation, making the zero-shot paradigm practically essential. The WZ-Pipe dataset (64K images, 17 categories) provides a much-needed dedicated benchmark, and the finding that domain-adaptive steering alone accounts for the bulk of the GZSL performance jump (9.92→39.62 mAP) suggests that for specialized industrial domains, closing the visual domain gap may matter more than the zero-shot mechanism itself. Open questions include whether the GR module's relational-transfer hypothesis generalizes beyond a single domain, how the method handles fine-grained severity grading, and whether the approach scales to even rarer or entirely novel defect types not captured by the 17-category taxonomy.
