## Problem

Existing VLM adaptation methods—prompt-tuning, adapter injection, and fine-tuning—parameterize each class independently, ignoring latent semantic dependencies across related classes and modalities. This class-specific, structure-agnostic design limits compositional reasoning and cross-domain generalization, particularly in few-shot and Base-to-Novel settings where shared conceptual structure could enable efficient transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260201/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

CASPA consumes RGB images (processed by a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 or ViT-B/32 image encoder) and class-name text (processed by the frozen text encoder) to produce cosine-similarity logits between image features and adapted class prototypes, yielding class labels and probabilities. In the Base-to-Novel regime, novel-class logits are further scaled by a confidence-adaptive factor derived from the maximum softmax confidence over Base classes, requiring no additional training samples for unseen categories. The entire adapter introduces only 1.1M parameters (0.73% of CLIP) and trains in ~5 minutes on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

CASPA replaces per-class prompt vectors with two shared anchor banks (textual and visual), each containing K learnable direction vectors in the CLIP embedding space. Each class is represented as a softmax-weighted mixture over its modality's anchors plus a small learnable residual shift, so related classes naturally share anchor directions (e.g., 'car' and 'truck' both activate anchors encoding wheels or metal body). A Semantic Cross-Consistency Regularization (S-XCR) loss enforces per-class cosine alignment between the text- and image-conditioned anchor mixtures, while an orthogonality regularizer prevents anchor collapse. For zero-shot novel classes, association weights are computed at inference time via softmax over dot products between the novel class's frozen CLIP text embedding and the textual anchor bank, then combined with the base embedding to form an adapted prototype—requiring no gradient updates. The total training objective couples cross-entropy classification with S-XCR and anchor-diversity terms, optimized with ASAM for flat-minima generalization.

## Results

On the Base-to-Novel generalization benchmark across 11 datasets, CASPA-G achieves average Base, Novel, and HM accuracies of 85.24%, 77.18%, and 81.01% respectively, setting the best Novel and HM scores among all compared methods. On fine-grained datasets, CASPA-G attains 78.81% HM on Cars, 86.76% on Flowers, and 92.21% on Food-101, substantially outperforming prior methods. In the cross-dataset transfer setting (trained on ImageNet 16-shot, evaluated on 10 target datasets without retraining), CASPA-G achieves the highest average target accuracy of 66.70% and the best source-domain accuracy of 73.24%. In the 16-shot all-to-all setting with ViT-B/16, CASPA-G reaches a mean accuracy of 83.0%, surpassing 2SFS (82.9%) and MMA (81.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CASPA-G achieves the best average Novel (77.18%) and HM (81.01%) across all 11 datasets, outperforming the strongest recent baselines: 2SFS (75.48% Novel, 80.20% HM), DPC (74.78%, 80.04%), RAda (76.25%, 80.08%), and MMA (76.80%, 79.87%). On fine-grained benchmarks the gains are most pronounced: CASPA-G leads by +0.35% HM on Cars (78.81% vs 78.46% for 2SFS/DPC), +0.93% on Flowers (86.76% vs 85.83% for 2SFS), and +0.72% on Food-101 (92.21% vs 91.49% for DPC). However, CASPA-G underperforms on DTD (70.78% HM vs RAda's 73.86%) and SUN (80.01% vs DPC's 80.76%), indicating sensitivity to texture-heavy and scene-level datasets. On the Base metric, DPC (86.10%) and 2SFS (85.55%) slightly exceed CASPA-G (85.24%), but CASPA-G's superior Novel accuracy more than compensates in the harmonic mean. In cross-dataset transfer, CASPA-G achieves the highest average target accuracy (66.70%) and source accuracy (73.24%), beating DeKgTCP (66.64%, 72.33%) and MMA (66.61%, 71.00%). In the 16-shot all-to-all setting with ViT-B/16, CASPA-G (83.0% avg) edges out 2SFS (82.9%) and MMA (81.0%), while with ViT-B/32 it reaches 79.4% vs 2SFS's 79.2%.

## Relation to Prior Work

CASPA departs from the dominant class-specific prompt-tuning paradigm (CoOp, CoCoOp, MaPLe, ProGrad) and adapter-based approaches (CLIP-Adapter, Tip-Adapter, MMA) by reframing adaptation as learning a shared compositional basis rather than per-class specialization. Each class is represented as a probability-weighted mixture over a compact set of dual modality-specific anchors, with cross-modal consistency enforced via a novel S-XCR loss. This is conceptually closer to dictionary learning or mixture-of-experts decomposition than to the context-token or residual-adapter formulations that dominate the CLIP adaptation literature, and it avoids backbone modification, MLPs, and LoRA entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in extreme parameter and compute efficiency: 1.1M trainable parameters (0.73% of CLIP) and ~5 minutes of training on an A100, while matching or exceeding methods like KgCoOp (124M params, ~4 hours) and CoOp (~17 hours) across 11 datasets. The zero-shot Base-to-Novel transfer and cross-dataset transfer (ImageNet-trained model evaluated on 10 other datasets without retraining) demonstrate that the anchor structure captures transferable mid-level visual primitives. Open questions include whether the anchor formulation scales to larger backbones (ViT-L, ViT-H), whether the fixed anchor count K=48 is optimal for very large class spaces, and whether the approach extends naturally to generation or retrieval tasks beyond classification. The DTD and EuroSAT degradations suggest texture-heavy and small-class-count regimes remain challenging.
