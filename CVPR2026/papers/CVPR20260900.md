## Problem

In knowledge-based visual question answering (KB-VQA), retrieval-augmented generation introduces knowledge conflicts between a VLM's static parametric knowledge and dynamically retrieved external contexts, causing the model to either ignore retrieved information or be misled by contradictory passages. Existing conflict-mitigation methods adapted from text-only RAG neglect the critical role of visual information in identifying and resolving these conflicts, and they operate at the context level without accounting for the substantial redundancy within retrieved passages that obscures the few sentences actually relevant to the query.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260900/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a query RGB image, a text question, and top-3 retrieved Wikipedia sections (retrieved via EVA-CLIP-8B image similarity with EchoSight reranking), plus a self-generated parametric context produced by the VLM itself. It outputs a single text answer. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates on Qwen2.5-VL-7B; the only learned components are the frozen EVA-CLIP-8B encoder for retrieval and correlation scoring, and the EchoSight reranker.

## Method

CC-VQA comprises two training-free components. First, Visual-Centric Contextual Conflict Reasoning (VCCR) externalizes the model's parametric knowledge by prompting the VLM to produce an answer with supporting background, then uses the query image to extract visual rationales for each context and synthesizes them into a structured visual-conflict summary (R_vis) that explicitly identifies which visual features drive the disagreement. Second, Correlation-Guided Encoding and Decoding computes sentence-level relevance scores via EVA-CLIP against a disambiguated question and the image; during encoding, it compresses RoPE position indices for the bottom-τ-percentile (low-correlation) sentences by a factor α=0.5, reducing their attentional footprint; during decoding, it augments the CoCoA adaptive contrastive decoding conflict score with a correlation-concentration term K that penalizes dispersed weak evidence, so that high-divergence, high-entropy-gap tokens in low-correlation regions receive elevated conflict scores and are suppressed in the blended output distribution.

## Results

On InfoSeek, CC-VQA achieves 45.1% VQA accuracy (All), surpassing the vanilla RAG baseline (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) by +3.3% and the fine-tuned Wiki-PRF by +2.3%. On E-VQA, it reaches 36.1% (All) and 41.4% (Single-Hop), improving over vanilla RAG by +4.9% and +4.7% respectively. On OK-VQA, CC-VQA attains 78.8% accuracy, outperforming the fine-tuned Wiki-PRF (77.8%) and the training-free MMKB-RAG (65.4%). The method also reduces the harmful ratio of retrieval from 10.53% to 7.69% while increasing the helpful ratio from 16.82% to 18.63% on 10K InfoSeek samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CC-VQA is a training-free method that outperforms all 13 baselines across E-VQA, InfoSeek, and OK-VQA. Against the strongest fine-tuned competitor Wiki-PRF (NeurIPS 2025), CC-VQA gains +2.3% on InfoSeek All (45.1 vs 42.8) and +1.0% on OK-VQA (78.8 vs 77.8) without any generator fine-tuning. Against the closest training-free baseline MMKB-RAG, CC-VQA leads by +8.7% on InfoSeek All (45.1 vs 36.4) and +13.4% on OK-VQA (78.8 vs 65.4). Over the vanilla RAG setup (same Qwen2.5-VL-7B + EVA-CLIP-8B retriever), CC-VQA adds +3.3% on InfoSeek All and +4.9% on E-VQA All, confirming that the gains come from conflict mitigation rather than a stronger backbone. On E-VQA Single-Hop, CC-VQA (41.4) surpasses even the fine-tuned ReflectiVA (28.0) and Wiki-PRF (37.1), indicating robustness on multi-hop reasoning. The oracle experiment further shows CC-VQA reaches 66.5% accuracy with ground-truth articles versus 55.3% for the vanilla RAG baseline, demonstrating superior knowledge localization when retrieval is perfect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends knowledge conflict mitigation from text-only RAG (CoCoA, AdaCAD) to the multimodal KB-VQA setting, introducing a vision-centric perspective where visual semantic features from the query image are used to identify and resolve conflicts between parametric and retrieved knowledge. It builds directly on RoPE and Position Interpolation by inverting the scaling direction—compressing positional indices for low-correlation sentences rather than expanding them for longer contexts—and augments CoCoA's adaptive contrastive decoding with fine-grained sentence-level correlation weights. Unlike prior multimodal RAG methods (EchoSight, Wiki-LLaVA, ReflectiVA) that focus on retrieval quality or require fine-tuning, CC-VQA operates entirely at the generation stage in a training-free manner, treating the conflict problem as a decoding-time issue rather than a retrieval or training problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper quantifies a critical but underappreciated failure mode: vanilla multimodal RAG introduces errors in ~10.5% of cases where the VLM alone was correct, and CC-VQA reduces this harmful ratio to ~7.7% while increasing the helpful ratio. The training-free design (no fine-tuning, no RL) makes the approach immediately deployable on existing VLMs, and the positional encoding compression technique for low-relevance content is a general-purpose mechanism potentially applicable beyond KB-VQA. Open questions include whether the EVA-CLIP-based correlation scoring generalizes to domains with weaker image-text alignment, whether the method scales to longer multi-document contexts, and whether the vision-centric conflict reasoning adds value when the query image provides limited discriminative information (e.g., abstract or text-heavy questions).
