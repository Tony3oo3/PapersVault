## Problem

Video editing models are bottlenecked by the scarcity of naturally aligned original-edited video pairs, which is especially acute for local edits involving complex geometric and appearance changes. Existing workarounds either synthesize large-scale paired data (introducing artifacts) or propagate edits from a single first frame (suffering structural drift and temporal incoherence under significant motion). NOVA targets this gap by learning video editing entirely from unpaired data while preserving source motion and texture fidelity.

## Input / Output

At inference, the user provides a source video (832×480, 81 frames), a text prompt, and optionally spatial masks for targeted edits. The system first edits keyframes at indices {0, 10, …, 80} using FLUX.1 Kontext Inpainting (each conditioned on the first edited frame for consistency), then feeds the interpolated keyframe sequence into the sparse branch and the original unedited video into the dense branch to produce the final edited video at the same resolution and length. No per-video fine-tuning is required.

## Method

NOVA adopts a dual-branch DiT architecture built on WAN 2.1 VACE 1.3B: a Sparse Branch encodes multiple edited keyframes as semantic anchors, while a structurally identical Dense Branch encodes the original video to supply motion and texture cues via trainable cross-attention modules (the only parameters trained; the base model remains frozen). Training is self-supervised through two complementary pipelines: an Anchored Control Pipeline that stochastically degrades keyframes (Gaussian blur, random affine warps) and linearly interpolates between them to simulate inconsistent edits, and a Source Fidelity Pipeline that applies a temporally coherent cut-and-paste mask from a random video to create a spatially misaligned pseudo-source. At inference, a consistency-aware scheme edits all keyframes sequentially with explicit reference to the first edited frame, reducing flicker, and the resulting interpolated sequence drives the sparse branch while the clean source video drives the dense branch, enabling guided reconstruction of non-edited regions without hallucination.

## Results

NOVA (1.3B params, no per-video finetuning) achieves the best Success Rate (0.93), Temporal Consistency (0.935), Frame Consistency (0.882), and Motion Smoothness (0.993) among all compared methods. It also attains the highest Background SSIM (0.917) among methods that do not require per-video finetuning, though Senorita-2M (5B) edges it slightly at 0.921. On Background Consistency (VBench), Senorita-2M leads at 0.953 versus NOVA's 0.946, while NOVA surpasses all other baselines. Notably, NOVA matches or exceeds methods that require per-video LoRA fine-tuning (I2VEdit, LoRA-Edit) or 14B parameter models while using only 1.3B parameters and zero per-video adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NOVA outperforms all five baselines on Success Rate (0.93 vs. 0.90 for VACE Multi-keyframe, the closest competitor), Temporal Consistency (0.935 vs. 0.933 for LoRA-Edit Multi-keyframe), Frame Consistency (0.882 vs. 0.880 for LoRA-Edit), and Motion Smoothness (0.993 vs. 0.991 for I2VEdit and VACE). Against the largest baseline, Senorita-2M (5B params), NOVA wins on SR, TC, FC, and MS but trails slightly on BG-SSIM (0.917 vs. 0.921) and Background Consistency (0.946 vs. 0.953). Compared to per-video finetuning methods (I2VEdit, LoRA-Edit), NOVA achieves superior or equal results on every metric without any per-video adaptation, while using 1.3B parameters versus LoRA-Edit's 14B. The standard VACE baseline shows a dramatic SR drop (0.36) without multi-keyframe guidance, highlighting the importance of the sparse control mechanism that NOVA incorporates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the WAN 2.1 VACE 1.3B architecture, training only new cross-attention modules while freezing the base model. Conceptually, it departs from two dominant paradigms: first-frame-guided propagation methods (AnyV2V, I2VEdit, LoRA-Edit) that entangle appearance generation with motion preservation, and unified generative architectures (VACE, Señorita-2M) that merge control and synthesis into a single pathway. The key departure is the 'Sparse Control, Dense Synthesis' formulation, which explicitly decouples semantic guidance (sparse edited keyframes) from fidelity preservation (dense original video) into separate branches, and introduces a degradation-simulation training strategy that eliminates the need for paired editing data entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine bottleneck in video editing: the near-impossibility of collecting large-scale paired original/edited video data, especially for local edits. The dual-branch decoupling and self-supervised degradation training offer a principled path toward scalable video editing without per-video fine-tuning or synthetic paired datasets. However, the evaluation is limited to 81-frame clips at 832×480 trained on only 5,000 Pexels videos, and the keyframe editing step relies on a proprietary model (FLUX.1 Kontext), leaving open questions about scalability to longer/higher-resolution videos, robustness to more complex multi-object edits, and whether the paradigm transfers to tasks beyond addition/removal/replacement.
