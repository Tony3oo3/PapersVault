## Problem

Advanced OCR models exhibit an order-of-magnitude higher output entropy on formatted content (formulas, tables) than on plain text, revealing that these models face genuine reasoning uncertainty when interpreting diverse valid structural expressions. Existing RL-based OCR approaches construct reward mechanisms empirically without targeting this high-entropy regime or designing format-specific supervision, leaving format-intensive parsing as an underexplored bottleneck.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263935/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model (Qwen3-VL-4B) consumes a single document image (PDF page scan at native resolution) and generates a structured Markdown string in which plain text appears as raw characters, mathematical expressions are emitted as LaTeX, and tabular content is rendered as HTML. The output preserves reading order across the page, and the model is expected to follow format-specific instructions (e.g., 'output the table as HTML') rather than defaulting to unstructured text.

## Method

FD-RL employs a two-stage SFT-then-RL paradigm on Qwen3-VL-4B, freezing the vision encoder during both stages. The RL stage introduces two key mechanisms: (1) entropy-based data filtration, where the SFT model's per-token log-probabilities are used to compute average output entropy per document, and only samples exceeding a threshold (50% filtration rate) are retained for RL, concentrating training on high-uncertainty format-intensive instances; and (2) format decoupled reward, which uses regex to partition both prediction and ground truth into plain text, formulas, and tables, then applies a type-specific metric—NED for text, BLEU for formulas, TEDS-S for tables—aggregated via an indicator-weighted average so that each format receives targeted structural feedback rather than a single token-level signal. Optimization uses GRPO with group-normalized advantages over sampled responses, and a fallback mechanism degrades all rewards to NED if regex parsing fails, preventing sparse zero-reward collapse.

## Results

FD-RL achieves an overall score of 90.41 on OmniDocBench, the best among all end-to-end specialized VLMs, surpassing dots.ocr (88.41) by 2.0 points and DeepSeek-OCR (87.01) by 3.4 points. It attains the best formula CDM score (88.67), best table TEDS (87.35) and TEDS-S (92.10) among end-to-end models, and a text edit distance of 0.049 (second only to dots.ocr's 0.048). Among all methods including pipeline-based systems, FD-RL ranks behind PaddleOCR-VL (92.56) and MinerU2.5 (90.67), both of which use pre-detected layout information.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among end-to-end specialized VLMs, FD-RL outperforms all competitors: it beats dots.ocr (88.41) by 2.0 points overall, DeepSeek-OCR (87.01) by 3.4 points, and Nanonets-OCR-s (85.59) by 4.8 points. The largest gains appear in formula recognition (CDM 88.67 vs. dots.ocr's 83.22, a 5.45-point margin) and table structure (TEDS-S 92.10 vs. dots.ocr's 90.62), confirming the format-decoupled reward's targeted benefit. Against general-purpose VLMs, FD-RL surpasses Gemini-2.5 Pro (88.03) by 2.38 points and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (87.02) by 3.39 points. However, FD-RL trails the two strongest pipeline-based specialized VLMs—PaddleOCR-VL (92.56) and MinerU2.5 (90.67)—by 2.15 and 0.26 points respectively, with the gap concentrated in text edit distance (0.049 vs. 0.035 and 0.047) and reading order (0.055 vs. 0.043 and 0.044), suggesting that pre-detected layout information still provides an advantage for sequential ordering tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SFT-then-RL training paradigm (well-established in LLM reasoning) to document OCR, building on the VLM-based OCR line of work (dots.ocr, DeepSeek-OCR, PaddleOCR-VL, MinerU2.5) that has focused primarily on data engineering and supervised fine-tuning. It departs from prior RL-based OCR efforts (olmOCR 2, HunyuanOCR, OCRVerse) by grounding the RL design in an empirical entropy observation: formatted content (formulas, tables) exhibits an order-of-magnitude higher token entropy than plain text, motivating targeted data filtration and format-specific reward functions rather than unified token-level objectives. The format-decoupled reward (NED for text, BLEU for formulas, TEDS-S for tables) repurposes standard NLP/structural metrics as RL signals, a design choice not previously explored in the document OCR RL literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entropy-based observation provides a principled, model-agnostic signal for identifying which document samples benefit most from RL, potentially applicable beyond OCR to other structured-output generation tasks. The format-decoupled reward design offers a practical recipe for multi-format document parsing where a single unified metric (e.g., edit distance) dilutes format-specific error signals. However, the gains are modest (+2.0 over dots.ocr, +3.4 over DeepSeek-OCR), and the approach is validated on a single base model (Qwen3-VL-4B) and a single benchmark (OmniDocBench), leaving open whether the entropy-filtration threshold and reward choices transfer across architectures, languages, or document domains. The paper also does not explore whether the RL gains could be achieved through more targeted SFT data curation alone, making it difficult to isolate the unique contribution of RL versus better data selection.
