## Problem

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) achieve strong fine-grained visual recognition for known categories but fail at hierarchical visual recognition (HVR): their predictions violate taxonomic paths (e.g., predicting a species whose genus is wrong) and they cannot recognize novel categories absent from training data. This limits LMMs as general-purpose visual understanding systems that should map inputs to consistent label paths across all levels of a taxonomy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260878/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (resized to 328×328, no augmentation) and a text prompt specifying the target taxonomic level (e.g., 'what is its taxonomic classification at the species level?'). Output is a four-choice VQA answer (single option letter), where the three distractors are selected via SigLIP cosine similarity to ensure semantic confusion at the same taxonomy level. During inference, the model is directly prompted without any BFM or projector in the loop.

## Method

TARA injects taxonomic knowledge into LMMs by aligning their intermediate representations with those of a pretrained biology foundation model (BioCLIP2) at two levels: (1) a visual alignment loss (L_V) that minimizes negative cosine similarity between the LMM's visual token embeddings at a chosen layer and the BFM's visual encoder output, and (2) a label alignment loss (L_C) that aligns the first answer-token embedding with the BFM's text encoder output for the ground-truth label at the target granularity. Both losses use lightweight 3-layer MLP projectors (SiLU activations) to map LMM features into BFM space. Training alternates between No-Thinking RFT (accuracy-only reward, no format reward, explicit prohibition of reasoning) and the TARA alignment objective, updating the LMM and projectors jointly. The key insight is that BFMs trained with hierarchical contrastive learning encode rich taxonomic structure in their embedding spaces, so aligning LMM representations to these targets regularizes the model to extract discriminative, hierarchy-aware visual cues without requiring additional labeled data.

## Results

On iNat21-Plant, TARA (combined with No-Thinking RFT) improves HCA from 9.23% to 12.78% (+3.55) and POR from 64.58% to 68.57% (+3.99) for Qwen3-VL-2B, and from 17.91% to 19.53% (+1.62) HCA for Qwen2.5-VL-3B. On iNat21-Animal, TARA yields +1.69 HCA and +1.76 TOR for Qwen3-VL-2B. On the TerraIncognita benchmark, TARA delivers the largest gains on known species (Order F1: 23.30→41.56, Family F1: 11.47→25.47) and consistent but smaller improvements on novel species (Order F1: 23.30→33.45, Family F1: 11.47→12.67).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external HVR methods; instead, it ablates the incremental contribution of TARA on top of No-Thinking RFT across two base LMMs. TARA consistently outperforms the RL-only baseline on every metric for both Qwen3-VL-2B and Qwen2.5-VL-3B, with the largest relative gains on hierarchical consistency metrics (HCA, S-POR, TOR) rather than leaf-level accuracy, confirming that the alignment loss primarily enforces path-level coherence. On TerraIncognita known species, TARA nearly doubles Order F1 (23.30→41.56) and more than doubles Family F1 (11.47→25.47) over RL alone, while on novel species the Order F1 gain is +10.15 and Family F1 gain is +1.20, suggesting the taxonomic prior transfers well to higher-level categories even for unseen species. The gains are larger for the smaller Qwen3-VL-2B model (+3.55 HCA) than for Qwen2.5-VL-3B (+1.62 HCA), indicating TARA is most beneficial when the base model has weaker intrinsic taxonomic structure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent threads: (1) No-Thinking Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) for LMMs, which showed that explicit reasoning traces are unnecessary for classification tasks, and (2) biology foundation models (BFMs) like BioCLIP2 that encode taxonomic structure via hierarchical contrastive learning. Prior HVR work focused on CLIP-style models (e.g., hierarchy-consistent prompt tuning, hyperbolic embeddings) or on diagnosing LMM failures, but none injected domain-specific taxonomic knowledge into LMMs through representation alignment. TARA departs from standard knowledge distillation by aligning both visual intermediate features and first-token answer embeddings with BFM targets, rather than distilling final logits, and does so within an alternating RL training loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a lightweight, training-time-only alignment strategy (two cosine-similarity losses with small MLP projectors) that requires no architectural changes and no BFM at inference, making it easy to adopt for any LMM-based classifier with a hierarchical label space. The most compelling result is generalization to novel species on TerraIncognita, suggesting that BFM-aligned representations encode transferable taxonomic structure rather than memorized category boundaries. However, all experiments are confined to biological taxonomies (iNaturalist, TerraIncognita) and two Qwen variants; whether the same alignment strategy transfers to other hierarchical domains (e.g., product catalogs, medical ontologies, knowledge graphs) remains an open question the authors explicitly flag.
