## Problem

Online visual SLAM in dynamic natural environments is severely degraded by moving objects, which cause incorrect data associations and inaccurate pose estimates. While feed-forward reconstruction models encode rich geometric and semantic priors that can handle dynamics, their incremental (batch-wise) usage introduces scale drift that prevents consistent long-sequence reconstruction. The paper asks whether the priors in feed-forward models can be effectively exploited to overcome the limitations of geometric SLAM in dynamic scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263929/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a monocular RGB image sequence (H×W×3) captured in a dynamic environment and, for each new frame, pairs it with a set of selected historical keyframes for batch-wise inference through π_mos³. It outputs per-frame camera poses in SE(3), scale-consistent dense depth maps (H×W), and per-pixel motion probability masks (H×W) in [0,1]. The depth from the feed-forward model is inherently defined only up to an unknown scale, so an explicit scale-alignment step against previously optimized patch depths is required before the depth can be used in bundle adjustment.

## Method

The approach tightly integrates DPV-SLAM's patch-based sliding-window bundle adjustment with a new feed-forward model π_mos³, which extends π³ by adding a moving-object-segmentation head on top of DINOv2 features processed through alternating frame-wise and global self-attention. Three key mechanisms enable the fusion: (1) motion probability masks from π_mos³ restrict patch sampling to static regions, preventing dynamic objects from entering the BA window; (2) a single inverse-depth scale factor is optimized via Huber loss against previously estimated patch depths to resolve the batch-wise scale ambiguity; (3) an uncertainty-aware BA module adaptively weights a depth-prior loss using the median relative standard deviation of patch inverse depths (derived from the Schur-complement covariance), increasing reliance on the feed-forward prior when geometric tracking is ill-constrained and reducing it when flow constraints are reliable. Only the MOS head is trained (binary cross-entropy on Kubric, Dynamic Replica, Virtual KITTI 2, and HOI4D), while the rest of π_mos³ is initialized from pretrained π³ weights.

## Results

On the Bonn RGB-D Dynamic Dataset, the proposed method achieves 2.20 cm average ATE RMSE, outperforming the closest competitor WildGS-SLAM (2.36 cm) and the offline MegaSaM (3.51 cm). On the more challenging Sintel dataset with fast camera motion and large depth variation, the method achieves 1.9 cm ATE, 1.0 cm RTE, and 0.11° RRE, substantially beating BA-Track (3.4/2.3/0.12) and MonST3R (7.8/3.8/0.49). For moving object segmentation, the model reaches 68.1 JM / 78.3 JR on DAVIS-16 and 70.6 JM / 81.3 JR on DAVIS-17, surpassing the best prior method Easi3R_MonST3R (57.7/71.6 and 56.5/68.6) by over 10 points in both metrics. For video depth estimation, the method achieves 0.287 Abs Rel on Sintel and 0.054 on Bonn, the best among all online approaches and close to the offline π³ (0.233 and 0.049).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all 24 baselines across every evaluated task. In camera tracking on Bonn, it beats the closest online competitor WildGS-SLAM by 0.16 cm average ATE and the offline MegaSaM by 1.31 cm; on Wild-SLAM MoCap the margin widens to 0.24 cm over WildGS-SLAM and 2.74 cm over MegaSaM. On Sintel, where fast motion and large depth variation stress-test all methods, the advantage is most dramatic: 1.9 cm ATE vs. 3.4 cm for BA-Track and 7.8 cm for MonST3R, with RRE of 0.11° matching BA-Track's 0.12° while halving its ATE. For moving object segmentation, the model surpasses Easi3R_MonST3R by 10.4 JM and 6.7 JR on DAVIS-16, and by 14.1 JM and 12.7 JR on the harder DAVIS-17, indicating that the DINOv2-based multi-view architecture captures spatial context that pair-based DUSt3R/MASt3R backbones lack. In depth estimation, the method is the best online approach on both Sintel (0.287 Abs Rel vs. 0.323 for StreamVGGT) and Bonn (0.054 vs. 0.059 for StreamVGGT), approaching the offline π³ (0.233 and 0.049) while operating incrementally with constant memory. Methods relying on closed-world object detection (DynaSLAM, DDN-SLAM) or long-term point tracking (LEAP-VO, BA-Track) degrade significantly when dynamic objects exceed training categories or dominate the scene, whereas the feed-forward MOS head generalizes to arbitrary moving objects without category-specific supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper tightly integrates the patch-based SLAM framework of DPV-SLAM (built on Deep Patch Visual Odometry) with a feed-forward reconstruction model π³ extended by a new moving-object-segmentation head. It departs from prior dynamic SLAM systems that rely on closed-world object detectors (DynaSLAM, DDN-SLAM) or long-term point-trajectory analysis (LEAP-VO, BA-Track) by instead exploiting the geometric and semantic priors already encoded in a large-scale trained reconstruction network. The uncertainty-aware depth-prior weighting is inspired by MegaSaM, while the scale-alignment strategy is novel, addressing the batch-wise scale ambiguity inherent to incremental use of feed-forward models. Conceptually, the paper positions feed-forward reconstruction not as a replacement for geometric SLAM but as a complementary prior source that resolves specific failure modes (dynamic objects, insufficient parallax) of classical bundle adjustment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a practical pathway for integrating scalable feed-forward reconstruction models into online monocular SLAM, achieving state-of-the-art camera tracking across three dynamic-scene benchmarks and depth estimation competitive with offline methods while remaining online. The observation that moving-object segmentation emerges as a by-product of training a reconstruction model on dynamic data suggests a broader principle: large-scale geometric learning can solve traditionally separate subproblems without dedicated supervision. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-camera or stereo setups, how it behaves under extreme dynamics (e.g., fully occluded static background), and whether the GPU cost of running π³ per frame is acceptable for embedded robotic deployment. The monocular-only evaluation and reliance on a single SLAM backbone (DPV-SLAM) leave generalizability partially untested.
