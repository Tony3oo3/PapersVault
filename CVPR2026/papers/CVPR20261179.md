## Problem

Existing distributed parallelism methods for diffusion inference—patch-based data parallelism (DistriFusion) and static pipeline parallelism (AsyncDiff)—suffer from boundary artifacts, accumulated asynchronous denoising errors, and sublinear speed-up relative to GPU count. No prior approach achieves beyond-linear scaling while preserving generation fidelity, leaving a gap between theoretical N× speed-up and practical multi-GPU acceleration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261179/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a text prompt and a pre-trained diffusion model (U-Net or DiT) as input and produces a single generated image, operating at resolutions from 1024×1024 up to 2560×2560. The key structural detail is that the input latent x_T is fed simultaneously into two denoising branches—the conditional path f_θ(x_t, c, t) and the unconditional path f_θ(x_t, t)—which are assigned to separate GPUs, so each device processes the full spatial extent of the image rather than a local patch. Evaluation uses 5,000 MS-COCO 2014 validation prompts with FID, LPIPS, and PSNR measured against both ground-truth images and single-GPU original outputs.

## Method

The proposed hybrid parallelism combines two complementary mechanisms. First, condition-based partitioning replaces patch-based data parallelism by assigning the conditional and unconditional CFG branches to different GPUs, so each device denoises the entire image and global spatial coherence is preserved. Second, adaptive parallelism switching dynamically activates pipeline parallelism only during a middle 'Parallelism Stage' where the denoising discrepancy (rel-MAE between conditional and unconditional noise estimates) is small and stable; the start point τ₁ is determined automatically by detecting when the slope of the discrepancy curve drops below a threshold, and the end point τ₂ is set k steps later. Outside this window, the framework reverts to pure condition-based partitioning (Warm-Up and Fully-Connecting stages), avoiding error accumulation from asynchronous denoising when the two branches diverge. A score-decomposition analysis (Eq. 4) provides theoretical grounding, showing the discrepancy ratio tracks the relative magnitude of conditional information strength versus the unconditional data prior across timesteps.

## Results

On SDXL (U-Net) with 2 GPUs, the proposed hybrid parallelism achieves a 2.31× speed-up (7.12 s vs. 16.49 s single-GPU) with FID 23.831 (w/ G.T.) and LPIPS 0.796 (w/ Orig.), outperforming DistriFusion (1.22×) and AsyncDiff (1.31×) while using 19.6× less communication than AsyncDiff. On SD3 (DiT/flow-matching) with 2 GPUs, it reaches 2.07× speed-up (9.33 s vs. 19.36 s) with the best FID (33.322), LPIPS (0.780), and PSNR (8.229) among all compared methods. With 4 GPUs, the method scales to 3.41× (SDXL) and 3.50× (SD3) speed-up while maintaining comparable generation fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SDXL with 2 GPUs, the proposed method (2.31×) nearly doubles the speed-up of DistriFusion (1.22×) and AsyncDiff (1.31×) while achieving the best FID (4.100 w/Orig.) and lowest communication cost (0.516 GB vs. 9.830 GB for AsyncDiff). On SD3 with 2 GPUs, it outperforms all four baselines in speed-up (2.07× vs. 1.97× AsyncDiff, 1.35× xDiT-Ring, 1.94× ParaStep, 1.39× Compact-2bit) and simultaneously achieves the best generation quality (FID 1.878, LPIPS 0.046, PSNR 27.875 w/Orig.). With 4 GPUs on SD3, AsyncDiff (stride=2) reaches a higher nominal speed-up (3.98×) but at severe quality cost (FID 8.939 vs. 2.109 for Ours), while xDiT-Ring is the weakest baseline (1.08×) due to its massive communication overhead (130.8 GB). ParaStep achieves competitive speed (3.08×) with minimal communication (0.038 GB) but trails Ours on both speed and quality. Overall, the proposed method dominates the speed–quality–communication trade-off frontier across both U-Net and DiT architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions the two dominant axes of distributed diffusion inference—data parallelism (DistriFusion's spatial patch splitting) and pipeline parallelism (AsyncDiff's fixed layer-wise segmentation)—by replacing them with condition-based partitioning along the CFG conditional/unconditional branches and adaptive pipeline switching driven by a denoising-discrepancy metric. Conceptually, it repurposes the classifier-free guidance mechanism, originally designed for quality improvement, as the natural data-parallelism axis, arguing that each branch covers the full image and thus avoids the boundary artifacts inherent to patch-based splitting. The adaptive switching generalizes AsyncDiff's static warm-up by automatically determining when the two branches are sufficiently aligned to tolerate asynchronous execution, grounded in a score-decomposition analysis of the rel-MAE curve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework delivers beyond-linear scaling (2.31× with 2 GPUs, 3.41× with 4) while preserving FID/LPIPS/PSNR fidelity, and does so across both U-Net (SDXL) and DiT/flow-matching (SD3) architectures, suggesting broad applicability to future diffusion backbones. The 19.6× communication reduction over AsyncDiff makes it practically attractive for bandwidth-constrained deployments. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video or audio diffusion where temporal coherence adds another dimension of discrepancy, how it interacts with step-reduction methods (e.g., distillation to 4–8 steps) that shrink the parallelism window, and whether the fixed k parameter can be made fully automatic without quality loss.
