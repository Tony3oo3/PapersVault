## Problem

Generating complete digital twins from video requires precise camera control and global scene coverage, but perspective video generators are fundamentally limited by their narrow field of view, forcing long or multi-view trajectories that amplify cross-view inconsistency and temporal drift. Existing 360° video generation methods either support only high-level action control (e.g., 'move forward') or are validated exclusively on synthetic data, leaving exact camera trajectory following for in-the-wild 360° scenes unsolved.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260292/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes one or a few sparse 360° equirectangular images plus a user-defined camera trajectory as input. Each 360° frame is cropped into 8 perspective views (at 45° yaw intervals) for 3D reconstruction and CLIP feature extraction. The output is a temporally consistent 360° video in equirectangular projection at 1024×512 resolution, with the geometric scaffold rendered in ERP format along the target path. For interpolation tasks, a dual-anchor variant conditions on both start and end frames with latent-level fusion to ensure smooth transitions between distant viewpoints.

## Method

The core contribution is an explicit 3D Cache—a point cloud reconstructed from sparse inputs via foundation models (PI3 or VGGT)—that is rendered along the user-defined trajectory to produce a geometry-only equirectangular video V_geo. This V_geo is encoded through the SVD VAE and concatenated with noised latents at every diffusion step, enforcing global geometric consistency while the fine-tuned SVD U-Net focuses solely on photorealistic texture synthesis. Semantic conditioning is provided by CLIP features extracted from 8 perspective crops of the first frame, injected via cross-attention. The model is trained with a standard diffusion denoising objective on the 360-1M dataset, where training pairs (Y_equi, V_geo) are generated on-the-fly using ViPE for pose estimation and Droid-SLAM for point cloud construction. A dual-anchor variant incorporates Time Reversal Fusion to blend start and end frame information at the latent level, mitigating geometric inconsistencies from sparse reconstruction during interpolation.

## Results

On the Web360 single-view-to-video task, Pantheon360 achieves FVD 356.2, SSIM 0.746, PSNR 22.84, LPIPS 0.065, and MET3R 0.284, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On the Habitat sparse-views task, it reaches FVD 450.7, SSIM 0.756, PSNR 20.39, LPIPS 0.091, and MET3R 0.303, with the largest margin over GEN3C in geometric consistency (MET3R 0.303 vs. 0.452). The method is trained at 1024×512 resolution on 4 A100 GPUs for 5 days, using the 360-1M dataset with on-the-fly 3D annotation via ViPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pantheon360 outperforms all three perspective-based baselines (ViewCrafter, TrajectoryCrafter, GEN3C) on every metric in both the Web360 and Habitat evaluations. The largest gains are in geometric consistency (MET3R): Pantheon360 scores 0.284 vs. 0.350 for GEN3C on Web360 and 0.303 vs. 0.452 on Habitat, confirming that the explicit 3D Cache enforces stronger cross-view coherence than perspective cropping. Against the 360° world model GenEX, Pantheon360 maintains consistent quality over long trajectories while GenEX degrades rapidly with increasing geometric inconsistencies (qualitative, Fig. 7). In the two-view novel view synthesis comparison, Pantheon360 produces geometrically accurate interpolations whereas PanoSplatt3R exhibits visible distortions and structural artifacts (qualitative, Fig. 6). The ablation on latent fusion (Table 3) shows the full Dual+Latent Fusion model achieves the best end-frame alignment (PSNR 28.95) and lowest interpolation error (IE 7.44) among four variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the '3D cache' paradigm—reconstructing scene geometry and rendering it as a geometric scaffold for a diffusion model—from the perspective video domain (GEN3C, ViewCrafter, TrajectoryCrafter) to the 360° equirectangular setting. It builds on Stable Video Diffusion as the base generator and incorporates Time Reversal Fusion's latent blending for dual-anchor interpolation, but the core novelty is repurposing 3D foundation models (PI3, VGGT) to construct the cache from sparse 360° inputs and training the diffusion model on real-world 360° data (360-1M) rather than synthetic scenes. This departs from prior 360° generators like GenEX (action-based control only) and CamPVG (synthetic-only validation) by achieving exact trajectory following on in-the-wild panoramas.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides a practical pipeline for generating geometrically consistent 360° digital twins from minimal input, with demonstrated applications in Street View stitching, video stabilization, and motion interpolation—tasks relevant to robotics simulation and autonomous driving evaluation. A key open question is handling dynamic scenes: the 3D Cache encodes only static geometry, and object-level motion relies entirely on the diffusion model's learned priors, limiting fine-grained control. The approach also depends on the quality of the 3D reconstruction (PI3/VGGT), and the paper acknowledges that suboptimal point clouds can cause discontinuities, partially mitigated by latent fusion but not fully resolved.
