## Problem

Existing 3D texturing methods rely on 2D-centric paradigms—multi-view projection or UV-space generation—that inherently produce seams, occlusion artifacts, and topology-dependent distortions. The fundamental roadblock to truly 3D-native texturing is the absence of a latent representation that is simultaneously expressive enough for high-frequency detail, decoupled from mesh topology and UV coordinates, and compact enough for effective generative modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260267/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 3D mesh converted into a densely sampled colored point cloud (2M points for VAE training, 5M for diffusion training), optionally paired with a text prompt or reference image. The geometry conditioning is derived by running the same VAE encoder on an uncolored (white) point cloud, yielding a pure shape latent at 64³ or 128³ sparse voxel resolution. The output is a continuous implicit 3D color field C(p) queryable at any spatial point, which is then baked into a standard 2D UV texture map; PBR roughness and metallic maps are generated hierarchically from the albedo latent.

## Method

Lafite learns a sparse latent color field via a VAE whose encoder replaces PointNet with a two-stage point-voxel attention mechanism (intra-voxel self-attention followed by point-voxel cross-attention) to robustly fuse per-point features into per-voxel latent codes, which are then processed by a sparse-voxel transformer with shifted window attention. A key design insight is that the same encoder, fed an uncolored point cloud, produces a pure geometry latent that provides an occlusion-free 3D shape prior perfectly aligned with the texture latent space, eliminating the need for a separate geometry network. Texture synthesis uses a conditional rectified flow model trained with flow matching, where the geometry latent is concatenated with the noisy latent at each denoising step, and a progressive curriculum first trains at 64³ then fine-tunes at 128³ resolution. PBR material generation extends this hierarchically (geometry → albedo → roughness/metallic) by reusing the VAE with swapped channels and conditioning the RM flow on the albedo latent. The VAE is supervised directly in 3D with L1 color loss on sampled points (no rendering-based losses like LPIPS), augmented with Gaussian color noise to encourage robust high-frequency representation.

## Results

On the VAE reconstruction benchmark (Table 2), Lafite achieves a PSNR of 34.62 dB at 128³ resolution, exceeding the best TRELLIS baseline (23.07 dB) by over 10 dB, with SSIM of 0.967 and LPIPS of 0.039. On the end-to-end conditional texture generation task (Table 1), Lafite (image-conditioned) achieves the best shaded FID of 101.91, shaded FD_CLIP of 46.28, and shaded KD_DINO of 0.027, outperforming the strongest competitors MaterialMVP and UniTEX. In a user study with 600 evaluations, Lafite is preferred in 58.5% of cases versus 24.7% for MaterialMVP and 16.8% for UniTEX.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two strongest image-conditioned baselines, Lafite achieves the best shaded FD_CLIP (46.28 vs. 48.71 for MaterialMVP and 51.62 for UniTEX) and the best shaded KD_CLIP (0.026 vs. 0.035 and 0.038) and KD_DINO (0.027 vs. 0.032 and 0.034), indicating superior geometric alignment and perceptual fidelity. In the unshaded setting, UniTEX edges out Lafite on FID (106.45 vs. 107.46) and FD_CLIP (51.08 vs. 53.49), but Lafite wins on FD_DINO (67.16 vs. 69.74) and KD_DINO (0.032 vs. 0.034). Against text-conditioned methods (MVPaint, MaterialAnything, SyncMVD, TEXGen, SeqTex, Paint3D), Lafite is not directly comparable since it uses image conditioning, but the gap is substantial: the best text method (SeqTex) scores 119.31 unshaded FID versus Lafite's 107.46. On the VAE reconstruction sub-task, Lafite's direct point-cloud encoding surpasses TRELLIS's DINO-feature projection by over 10 dB PSNR (34.62 vs. 23.07 at 128³), confirming that the representation advantage translates into end-to-end generation quality. The user study further validates these quantitative gains, with Lafite preferred in 58.5% of pairwise comparisons versus 24.7% for MaterialMVP and 16.8% for UniTEX.

## Relation to Prior Work

Lafite builds on the sparse voxel encoder architecture from SparseFlex [25], replacing its PointNet-based point aggregation with a dedicated point-voxel attention mechanism, and employs Rectified Flow [43] as the generative backbone. It departs fundamentally from the dominant multi-view projection paradigm (MVPaint, SyncMVD, MaterialMVP) and UV-based methods (TEXGen, SeqTex) by operating entirely in 3D space, and specifically contrasts with TRELLIS [73], which projects pre-trained 2D DINO features onto sparse voxels; Lafite instead directly encodes a dense colored point cloud, eliminating view-dependent projection ambiguities. The key architectural departure is the insight that the texture VAE encoder, when fed an uncolored point cloud, yields a pure geometry latent that can condition generation without a separate geometry network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The >10 dB PSNR reconstruction improvement over TRELLIS establishes a new fidelity standard for 3D texture representations, and the unified encoder design (one network serving as both texture and geometry encoder) eliminates a common architectural redundancy. The framework's practical impact is significant: it enables seamless texture baking, local 3D editing, and PBR material synthesis within a single representation, potentially becoming a standard backend for 3D content creation pipelines. However, the authors acknowledge a key limitation—the 3D-native approach lacks the rich semantic priors of large-scale 2D diffusion models, limiting generation of specific 2D patterns like text—and future work on distilling 2D knowledge into the 3D latent space remains an open challenge.
