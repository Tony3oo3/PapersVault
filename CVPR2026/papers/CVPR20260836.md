## Problem

Existing controllable hand image generation methods inject pose and appearance control signals with fixed strength across all denoising timesteps, ignoring the progressive nature of the diffusion process. The paper reveals two overlooked phenomena: strong pose-appearance coupling in early timesteps where the conditions jointly determine global layout, and a significant influence of condition complexity (pose difficulty, texture richness) on the required modulation strength. This static strategy leads to pose distortion and local texture blur that dynamic, state-aware modulation could address.

## Input / Output

The method takes N multi-pose reference images of the same identity (extracted via CLIP patch-level features) and a single target pose image (processed by a lightweight posenet into four hierarchical feature scales) as input, and produces a photorealistic RGB hand image in the latent space of Stable Diffusion 1.5. Appearance features are consumed as patch-level tokens (H×W×D) aggregated with spatial attention, while pose features are injected at multiple scales into the UNet downsampling path. The model is trained with classifier-free guidance (w=2.0 for both pose and identity) and 10% condition dropout.

## Method

TCCA introduces a query-based interaction mechanism that projects three heterogeneous representations—the noise latent z_t (temporal), flattened pose features (content-geometry), and appearance features (content-texture)—into a unified space via three sets of learnable queries (Q_c, Q_p, Q_a) refined by transformer decoders. A dual-driven adaptive modulation then predicts base pose/appearance weights from the timestep embedding and refines them with content-aware residual corrections (channel-level for pose, token-level for appearance) through a Transformer encoder where the timestep embedding acts as a [CLS] token. The Pose-Invariant Appearance Encoder (PIAE) extracts CLIP patch features from multiple reference images, applies SVD-based orthogonal decomposition to project out pose-specific variation directions, and aggregates the resulting pose-invariant embeddings with spatial attention plus the global [CLS] token. Modulated pose features are added to UNet downsampling blocks while modulated appearance features are injected via cross-attention in every block, enabling dynamic disentanglement throughout the denoising trajectory.

## Results

On InterHand2.6M, TCCA achieves FID 9.046, LPIPS 0.4078, SSIM 0.5714, and PSNR 14.965, outperforming all baselines across every image-quality metric. For geometric consistency, the synthesized images yield PCK@0.05 of 87.34% and MPE of 7.81 mm via the HaMer 3D pose estimator, approaching the real-image baseline (91.82% / 6.36 mm) and surpassing FoundHand (83.93% / 8.76 mm). On the full FoundHand-10M dataset for hand-object interaction, TCCA further improves to FID 8.183 and PSNR 15.845, beating FoundHand (FID 9.981, PSNR 15.112).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCCA outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric. Against the GAN-based GestureGAN (2018), the gains are substantial: FID drops from 31.09 to 9.05 and PCK@0.05 rises from 60.93% to 87.34%, reflecting the generality of diffusion over GANs for this task. Among diffusion-based methods, CosHand (ECCV 2024) and CFLD (CVPR 2024) are intermediate baselines; TCCA improves FID by 8.3 and 6.6 points respectively, and narrows the gap to real-image pose accuracy (MPE 7.81 vs. 10.27 and 9.31 mm). The closest competitor is FoundHand (CVPR 2025), which uses input-level fusion on the full FoundHand-10M dataset; TCCA still leads by 1.42 FID, 0.031 LPIPS, 0.034 SSIM, and 0.57 dB PSNR on InterHand2.6M, and by 1.80 FID and 0.73 dB PSNR on the full FoundHand-10M HOI benchmark. Notably, TCCA's synthetic images achieve pose-estimation scores (PCK@0.05 = 87.34%) that are within 4.5 points of real images, whereas FoundHand lags by 7.9 points, indicating superior geometric fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the latent diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) backbone and the controllable hand image generation line of work (GestureGAN, CosHand, CFLD, FoundHand), which inject pose and appearance signals via input-level concatenation or cross-attention with fixed strength across all denoising timesteps. Its key departure is the empirical demonstration—via a time-segmented feature injection study—that pose and appearance modulation is not uniform over timesteps and depends on condition complexity, motivating a dynamic, query-based dual-driven modulation mechanism. For appearance encoding, it repurposes CLIP patch-level features and introduces an SVD-based orthogonal decomposition to enforce pose-invariance, a technique borrowed from subspace analysis but applied here to disentangle identity from pose-specific artifacts in multi-reference settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that pose-appearance coupling is strongest in early denoising steps and that condition complexity modulates the required control strength provides a concrete, actionable principle for designing controllable diffusion pipelines beyond hand generation (e.g., faces, full-body synthesis). The SVD-based pose-invariant appearance encoder offers a lightweight alternative to heavier disentanglement networks, though its reliance on CLIP features and a fixed top-r truncation may limit robustness to out-of-distribution appearances. Open questions include whether the query-based temporal-content co-awareness generalizes to other articulated structures or to video generation, and whether the dynamic modulation can be distilled into a simpler schedule for inference efficiency.
