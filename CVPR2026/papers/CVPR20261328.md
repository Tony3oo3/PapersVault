## Problem

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), the dominant RL framework for multimodal reasoning, suffers from three critical limitations in long-horizon audio-video tasks: data inefficiency from discarding experiences after a single on-policy update, a vanishing advantage problem where uniform rewards within a group zero out all learning signals, and uniform credit assignment that treats all tokens in a reasoning chain equally regardless of their contribution. These issues are especially acute for video reasoning, where annotated data is scarce and expensive, and where multi-step cross-modal alignment demands targeted reinforcement of planning and synthesis phases.

## Input / Output

AVATAR operates on 7B open-source MLLMs (Qwen2.5-Omni, Ola-7B) that natively consume 32 uniformly sampled video frames, a synchronized audio track, and a text query. The model produces a structured output with an intermediate reasoning trace enclosed in  tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags. LoRA adapters (r=16, α=32) are attached to the vision encoder, audio encoder, MLP, and LLM, keeping the base weights frozen during RL fine-tuning.

## Method

AVATAR introduces two complementary mechanisms on top of GRPO. First, an off-policy architecture with a stratified replay buffer (10k capacity, split into Easy/Medium/Hard tiers at 25%/35%/40%) reuses past trajectories to maintain reward diversity within training groups, resolving the vanishing advantage problem; a Video-Context Reference Score (VCRS) provides a stable moving-average baseline for off-policy advantage computation, and a hinting mechanism triggers pre-computed guidance when the policy stagnates on hard prompts. Second, Temporal Advantage Shaping (TAS) applies a U-shaped parabolic weight w_t = 1.0 + λ_TAS·(2t̃−1)² to each token's advantage, amplifying learning signals at the planning (beginning) and synthesis (end) phases while downweighting intermediate tokens. Training follows a four-stage curriculum—SFT cold start, visual reasoning RL, audio-visual reasoning RL, and audio-object localization RL—each with tailored reward compositions (format, accuracy, self-rewarding, and VLM-judge components), achieving 5× sample efficiency over standard GRPO.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-Omni, AVATAR achieves 65.6 on MMVU (+5.4 over baseline), 45.1 on Video-Holmes (+4.5), 62.8 on Video-MME (+4.5), and 30.8 on TOMATO (+1.8), outperforming standard GRPO by +1.6 on MMVU and +1.9 on Video-Holmes. On Ola-7B, AVATAR gains +5.3 on MVBench and +2.3 on both Video-MME and Video-Holmes. AVATAR also demonstrates 5× sample efficiency, reaching 0.80 accuracy in 400 iterations (1,600 completions) versus GRPO's failure to reach that threshold after 1,000 iterations (8,000 completions).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVATAR (on Qwen2.5-Omni) outperforms standard GRPO on every video benchmark, with the largest gaps on Video-Holmes (45.1 vs. 43.2, +1.9) and MMVU (65.6 vs. 64.0, +1.6), and on audio-visual OmniBench (49.1 vs. 45.4, +3.7). Against SOTA audio-visual models, AVATAR surpasses EchoInk on Video-Holmes (45.1 vs. 42.5) and Video-MME (62.8 vs. 60.8), and beats Omni-R1 on Video-Holmes (45.1 vs. 44.2) and Video-MME (62.8 vs. 60.7). Among video-only reasoning models, AVATAR leads Video-R1 (45.1 vs. 41.0 on Video-Holmes), VideoRFT (45.1 vs. 40.5), and TW-GRPO (45.1 vs. 38.4) on causal reasoning, while matching or exceeding Qwen2.5-VL on MMVU (65.6 vs. 61.9). On TOMATO (temporal ordering), standard GRPO yields no statistically significant gain over baseline, whereas AVATAR achieves a significant +1.8, highlighting the benefit of TAS's position-dependent weighting. AVATAR also achieves 5× sample efficiency over GRPO, requiring 80% fewer generated completions to reach target performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

AVATAR builds directly on GRPO (itself an extension of PPO-style clipped objectives for LLM fine-tuning) and departs from it along two axes: an off-policy stratified replay buffer that reuses past trajectories to maintain within-group reward variance, and Temporal Advantage Shaping (TAS), a U-shaped parabolic token weighting inspired by the attention-sink literature. It positions itself against a cluster of recent multimodal RL methods (Video-R1, Omni-R1, HumanOmni, AV-Reasoner, TW-GRPO) that all remain on-policy with uniform credit assignment, and against prior replay-based approaches (RePO, PER) that reuse data but do not explicitly guarantee reward diversity within training groups. The VCRS mechanism (a moving-average baseline for stale off-policy samples) is a practical stabilization trick not seen in prior GRPO variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5× sample-efficiency gain (80% fewer generated completions) is practically significant for video/audio RL, where each rollout is expensive and annotated data is scarce; this could lower the barrier to training multimodal reasoners. However, the evaluation is confined to 7B-parameter models (Qwen2.5-Omni, Ola-7B) and a fixed 32-frame sampling protocol, leaving open whether the off-policy replay and TAS benefits persist at larger scale or with streaming/variable-length video. The paper itself flags extension to streaming audio-visual reasoning as future work, and the hinting mechanism's reliance on a 72B teacher (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) introduces a practical dependency that limits the framework's self-containedness.
