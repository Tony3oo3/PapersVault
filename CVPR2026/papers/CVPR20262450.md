## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models are limited to erasing only a few hundred homogeneous concepts, rely on heuristic anchor concept selection that does not scale, and remain vulnerable to white-box attacks such as module removal. No prior work demonstrates effective, pin-point erasure of thousands of heterogeneous concepts (celebrities, artistic styles, characters) across diverse domains while preserving generation quality for remaining concepts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262450/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained T2I diffusion model (SDv1.4 or SDv3.5-L), a set of target concept words, and a set of mapping concept triplets. For each concept, it feeds thousands of sentence templates through the text encoder to build per-concept embedding distributions, compresses them via PCA, and fits a tMM. The output is the original diffusion model augmented with a lightweight MoEraser module inserted before the text embedding projection layer; at inference time, standard text prompts produce images in which target concepts are replaced by novel anonymous visuals while remaining concepts and overall image quality are preserved.

## Method

ETC operates in two stages. First, it models each concept's text-embedding distribution as a Student's t-distribution Mixture Model (tMM) on PCA-compressed embeddings, then computes an Affine Optimal Transport (AOT) map from the target distribution to a merged three-concept distribution, yielding mapping embeddings; target embeddings are sampled from high-probability regions and anchor embeddings from low-probability regions of the target tMM, eliminating the need for curated anchor concepts. Second, it trains a Mixture-of-Experts module (MoEraser) with Gated Linear Unit experts to transform target embeddings into mapping embeddings while leaving anchor embeddings unchanged, using a combined erasure-preservation loss. A Noise Injection-Restore (NIR) step then injects structured low-rank noise (aligned with target PCA eigenvectors) into the text embedding projector and fine-tunes MoEraser to reconstruct the original embeddings, so that removing the module renders the model unable to generate coherent images, providing robustness against white-box module-removal attacks.

## Results

On the large-scale erasure of 2,072 concepts across three heterogeneous domains (949 celebrities, 693 artistic styles, 430 characters) using SDv1.4, ETC achieves harmonic mean H₀ scores of 0.780, 0.787, and 0.787 respectively, while maintaining high image quality (QS ≥ 0.814) and strong preservation of remaining concepts (CRS_r ≥ 0.688). On SDv3.5-L with 515 concepts, ETC reaches H₀ of 0.748, 0.810, and 0.777 across the three domains. In the smaller-scale 50-celebrity setting, ETC attains GCD target accuracy of 0.24% (near-complete erasure) with 89.37% remaining-concept accuracy and H₀ = 0.943, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ETC outperforms all seven baselines on the harmonic mean H₀ across every domain and model. SAFREE [62] preserves remaining concepts well (CRS_r ≈ 0.90) but fails to erase targets (CRS_t ≈ 0.83–0.95), yielding the lowest H₀ (0.099–0.283); it is effectively a no-op for erasure. SPEED [36] achieves strong target erasure (CRS_t as low as 0.098) but severely degrades image quality (QS drops to 0.098–0.195 on SDv3.5-L), limiting its H₀ to 0.222–0.725. CPE [34] is the closest competitor, reaching H₀ = 0.777 on Artistic Style (SDv1.4) and 0.936 on the 50-celebrity task, but ETC surpasses it by 0.010 and 0.007 respectively while maintaining substantially higher image quality (QS 0.894 vs. 0.784). MACE [38] and UCE [17] show moderate erasure but suffer from quality degradation and weaker preservation at scale (H₀ ≤ 0.671). In the small-scale 50-celebrity setting, ETC achieves the best GCD target accuracy (0.24%) and H₀ (0.943), narrowly beating CPE (0.37%, 0.936) and MACE (3.29%, 0.903), while FMN [63] and ESD [16] perform worst with target accuracies of 59.98% and 7.30–21.20% respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept erasure line of work (UCE, MACE, CPE, SPEED) by replacing linear or single-module erasers with a Mixture-of-Experts architecture (MoEraser) inspired by the gating-based designs in CPE [34], and by substituting heuristic anchor-concept selection with a principled distribution-boundary approach using Student's t-distribution Mixture Models and Affine Optimal Transport. It departs from prior methods that either fine-tune the base model or attach removable linear modules by introducing a Noise Injection-Restore (NIR) training scheme that makes the erasure module non-removable without degrading generation. The work also generalizes the evaluation setting from homogeneous, small-scale erasure (≤100 concepts) to heterogeneous, large-scale erasure (2,000+ concepts across celebrities, artistic styles, and characters).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ETC is, to the authors' knowledge, the first framework to effectively erase over 2,000 concepts across multiple heterogeneous domains while maintaining generation quality, addressing a practical gap that limits real-world deployment of T2I safety measures. The NIR mechanism offers a concrete defense against white-box module-removal attacks, a vulnerability acknowledged but not resolved by prior non-linear erasure methods. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video diffusion models, how it behaves under adversarial prompt rephrasing at scale, and whether the MoE module's inference overhead remains acceptable as the number of erased concepts grows further. The reliance on large-scale user studies (8,120 responses) for evaluation, while thorough, makes replication costly and limits the frequency of community validation.
