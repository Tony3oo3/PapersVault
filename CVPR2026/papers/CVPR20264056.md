## Problem

Existing accident anticipation methods rely on binary frame-level supervision where all pre-collision frames are labeled identically as positive, and the boundary between 'safe' and 'risky' is determined by manually annotated anomaly onset frames that are subjective and inconsistent across annotators. This forces the model to treat all pre-collision frames as equally dangerous, ignoring the gradual, scenario-dependent nature of risk evolution and producing temporally volatile or physically implausible risk curves.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a snippet of 5 consecutive RGB frames (resized to 224×224, resampled to 10 fps) from a dashcam video and outputs a single risk score in (0,1) for the current frame via a sigmoid head. During inference, a causal sliding window is applied over the pre-collision portion of each video to produce frame-level risk trajectories, and an alert is triggered whenever the score exceeds a predefined threshold. The encoder is a SlowOnly 3D CNN with adaptive spatial average pooling that preserves the temporal dimension of the feature sequence.

## Method

RiskProp replaces dense binary labels with a collision-anchored self-supervised paradigm: only the collision frame receives a hard positive label, while all intermediate pre-collision frames are supervised through two observation-driven losses. The Future-Frame Regularization Loss uses the model's own detached next-frame prediction as a soft target for the current frame, creating a backward credit-assignment chain that propagates the high-risk signal from the collision frame to earlier timesteps. The Adaptive Monotonic Constraint Loss penalizes frame pairs where a later frame is predicted less risky than an earlier one, with a margin that scales with temporal distance and average prediction confidence, allowing short-term fluctuations while enforcing a globally non-decreasing trend. For non-accident videos, both self-supervised losses are disabled and all frames receive a negative BCE label. The full objective combines weighted BCE (applied only to the start and collision frames for accident videos) with the two auxiliary losses, trained end-to-end on a SlowOnly backbone initialized from video-recognition pretraining.

## Results

On the CAP dataset, RiskProp achieves a mAUC^0.1 of 0.483, mAP of 0.890, and mTTA^0.1 of 1.207 s, outperforming the strongest baseline CRASH (0.401 mAUC^0.1) by 0.082 and delivering the earliest warnings among all compared methods. On the more challenging Nexar dataset, RiskProp sets new state-of-the-art results across all four metrics: mAUC^0.1 = 0.472, mAUC = 0.869, mAP = 0.870, and mTTA^0.1 = 0.958 s, surpassing the second-best method CRASH by 0.079, 0.037, 0.024, and 0.101 respectively. The only metric where RiskProp does not lead is the unconstrained mAUC on CAP (0.853 vs. DSTA's 0.895), though it remains competitive.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RiskProp outperforms all six baselines on the constrained mAUC^0.1 metric on both datasets, with the largest margin over CRASH (the strongest prior method): +0.082 on CAP and +0.079 on Nexar. On Nexar, RiskProp achieves the best score on every reported metric, beating CRASH by 0.037 in mAUC, 0.024 in mAP, and 0.101 s in mTTA^0.1. On CAP, RiskProp leads in mAUC^0.1, mAP (tied with GSC at 0.890), and mTTA^0.1 (1.207 s vs. AdaLEA's 1.115 s), but trails DSTA on unconstrained mAUC (0.853 vs. 0.895). DSTA, while strong on CAP mAUC, performs poorly on Nexar (mAUC^0.1 = 0.242, mTTA^0.1 = 0.493 s), indicating limited generalizability. Overall, RiskProp's self-supervised risk propagation yields the most consistent and earliest anticipation across both datasets without requiring subjective anomaly-onset annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant binary frame-level supervision paradigm in accident anticipation, where all pre-collision frames receive a hard label of 1 and risk evolution is shaped only through decaying loss weights or subjective anomaly-onset annotations. Conceptually, it repurposes the self-distillation idea (using the model's own next-frame prediction as a frozen target) from the action-anticipation literature into a backward risk-propagation mechanism anchored solely at the collision frame. The adaptive monotonic constraint draws on prior work enforcing non-decreasing trends in sequential prediction but introduces a confidence- and distance-adaptive margin that prior pairwise-ranking or fixed-penalty approaches lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: eliminating the need for subjective anomaly-onset annotation reduces labeling cost and inter-annotator inconsistency, making large-scale accident-anticipation training more scalable. The finding that a single collision-frame label plus self-supervised constraints matches or exceeds densely-annotated baselines suggests the field may over-rely on dense temporal labels that encode assumptions the data itself can reveal. Open questions include whether the non-decreasing risk assumption holds in false-alarm or multi-hazard scenarios, whether the approach generalizes beyond 3D-CNN backbones to transformer architectures, and how it performs on longer-horizon anticipation (beyond ~2 s) where the monotonic trend may break down.
