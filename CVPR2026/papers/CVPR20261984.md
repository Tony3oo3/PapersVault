## Problem

Existing prompt-learning methods for VLMs rely on a coarse layer-centric assumption (shallow layers = general, deep layers = task-specific) to decide where to inject learnable tokens, leading to uncontrolled interactions that degrade zero-shot generalization. This creates a fundamental trade-off: adapting to a downstream task erodes the model's core generalization ability, and no principled mechanism exists to selectively route new knowledge while preserving pre-trained capabilities.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed by a frozen ViT-B/16 encoder) and a set of class text embeddings (processed by a frozen text encoder) as input. At inference, it produces a final class prediction via a Task-Adaptive Fusion that combines logits from a protected [CLS] feature and five specialized attribute features (color, shape, texture, object, location), each computed by matching against the text embeddings with CLIP's frozen temperature. The attribute features are also available as intermediate outputs for attribute-conditioned retrieval.

## Method

DeAR's core insight is that functional specialization in VLMs resides at the individual attention-head level in deeper layers, not at the layer level. It introduces a Concept Entropy metric that quantifies each head's specialization by clustering TEXTSPAN-generated descriptive phrases with HDBSCAN and computing Shannon entropy over the resulting attribute distribution, classifying heads into Attribute, Generalization, and Mixed roles. Learnable attribute tokens are inserted into both vision and text encoders starting at layer 9, with a β-mixing mechanism (inspired by MMRL++) to balance contextual adaptation against semantic drift. A Role-Based Attention Mask then precisely controls information flow: Generalization heads are fully isolated from attribute tokens, Core Attribute heads attend only to their corresponding attribute token, and Mixed heads remain unrestricted. Training combines cross-entropy with a self-regularization loss (cosine similarity to frozen CLIP features) and a fusion-weight regularization that biases the inference toward the protected class feature.

## Results

On the primary base-to-novel generalization benchmark (11 datasets, 16-shot), DeAR achieves a harmonic mean of 82.72% (Base 85.94%, Novel 79.73%), surpassing the previous best MMRL (HM 81.20%) by +1.52% on HM and +2.57% on novel-class accuracy. The largest gains appear on fine-grained and texture-heavy datasets: DTD novel accuracy jumps from 65.00% (MMRL) to 75.60%, and EuroSAT novel from 80.17% to 87.90%. On domain generalization, DeAR achieves the best ImageNet-A (51.80%) and ImageNet-R (78.83%) scores, and on cross-dataset transfer it sets a new average of 67.60%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DeAR outperforms all 11 compared baselines on the primary base-to-novel harmonic mean (82.72% vs. 81.24% for TAC and 81.20% for MMRL), with the largest margin over the strongest competitor MMRL being +2.57% on novel-class accuracy. The advantage is most pronounced on texture- and attribute-heavy datasets: DTD novel accuracy improves by +10.6% over MMRL (75.60% vs. 65.00%) and +11.3% over PromptKD (75.60% vs. 69.15%), while EuroSAT novel gains +7.7% over MMRL. On domain generalization, DeAR achieves the best ImageNet-A (51.80%) and ImageNet-R (78.83%) scores, narrowly beating TAC (51.73%, 78.50%), though TAC edges out DeAR on the four-variant average (61.63% vs. 61.25%). For cross-dataset transfer (train on ImageNet, test zero-shot on 10 unseen datasets), DeAR sets the best average at 67.60%, surpassing MMRL (67.25%) and TAC (66.53%). In few-shot settings (1–16 shots), DeAR consistently outperforms all baselines across all shot counts, confirming robustness under limited labeled data.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeAR builds directly on the multi-modal prompt learning paradigm established by MaPLe (simultaneous vision and text prompt injection) and borrows the β-mixing mechanism from MMRL++ to control token information flow across layers. Its key conceptual departure is from the prevailing layer-centric view in prompt learning—where methods like MaPLe target early layers and MMRL targets deeper ones—by arguing that functional specialization in VLMs resides at the individual attention-head level rather than the layer level. The head-role identification pipeline draws on Gandelsman et al.'s TEXTSPAN interpretability tool and extends it with a novel Concept Entropy metric and HDBSCAN-based clustering to automatically discover attribute-specialized heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The head-level decomposition offers a more principled and interpretable mechanism for the long-standing adaptation-vs-generalization trade-off in VLM fine-tuning, potentially guiding future work on controllable, attribute-aware prompt design. However, the approach is validated only on ViT-B/16 with a fixed set of five core attributes, and the static head-role assignment (computed once on a reference dataset) may not hold across architectures, scales, or task domains. Open questions include whether Concept Entropy-based role classification transfers to larger ViT variants or non-CLIP VLMs, and whether the five-attribute taxonomy is sufficient for tasks requiring finer or different semantic decompositions.
