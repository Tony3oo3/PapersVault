## Problem

Existing event-based object trackers either rely on RGB-derived architectures (correlation filters, Siamese networks) that underperform under fast motion and extreme lighting, or employ hybrid CNN-SNN designs that do not fully exploit the low-energy, spike-driven benefits of SNNs. Pure SNN architectures for event-based tracking remain under-researched, and the fixed maximum spike count in I-LIF neurons cannot adapt to the highly variable motion and appearance dynamics inherent in tracking.

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes an asynchronous event stream aggregated into polarity-aware dual-channel voxel grids (positive and negative polarities), with a 128×128 template frame and 256×256 search frames. The MSST paradigm feeds 10 consecutive search frames as a temporally ordered spike train alongside the single template, and the model is fully end-to-end requiring no data augmentation or post-processing. Output is a bounding box parameterized by a center heatmap, size vector (width, height), and local spatial offsets, decoded via a 1×1 convolution at the peak response location.

## Method

SpikeTrack is a pure SNN pipeline comprising three stages: an I-LIF-based SNN Conv Module (four depthwise-separable blocks) for spatiotemporal feature extraction, a DI-LIF-based SNN Transformer Module (eight blocks with spiking self-attention) for template–search interaction, and an I-LIF-based SNN Tracking Head with three parallel branches for center, size, and offset prediction. The key novelty is the DI-LIF neuron, which computes a batch-level scalar α from the mean feature activation via a learnable linear layer and sigmoid, then sets the maximum emitted integer value as D_batch = ⌊α·D_init + D_init⌋, increasing spike capacity under dense event input and reducing it under sparse input to balance information fidelity against energy cost. The MSST training paradigm distributes 10 search frames across time while sharing a single reference template, leveraging the SNN's membrane-potential accumulation to model inter-frame trajectory evolution without auxiliary recurrent modules. Training uses focal loss for classification and GIoU + L1 loss for box regression, with integer-valued activations during training converted to binary spikes at inference.

## Results

SpikeTrack achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three event-based tracking benchmarks: 60.3% SR / 92.7% PR on FE108, 41.0% SR / 52.3% PR on FELT, and 38.9% SR / 54.3% PR on VisEvent. The model uses only 25.26M parameters and 7.92 mJ energy per frame, substantially lower than the closest competitor HDETrack (97.82M params, 120.8 mJ). On VisEvent, SpikeTrack outperforms the runner-up by 1.6% in SR and 1.8% in PR; on FE108 it leads by 0.5% in both metrics. In long-term tracking (2000 frames on VisEvent), SpikeTrack surpasses HDETrack by 1.5% SR and 1.9% PR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpikeTrack outperforms all 13 baselines on every dataset where they share evaluation. On VisEvent, it beats the strongest competitor HDETrack [22] by 1.6% SR (38.9 vs 37.3) and 1.8% PR (54.3 vs 52.5), while using only 25.8% of HDETrack's parameters (25.26M vs 97.82M) and 6.6% of its energy (7.92 vs 120.8 mJ). On FE108, SpikeTrack leads HDETrack by 0.5% in both SR (60.3 vs 59.8) and PR (92.7 vs 92.2). On FELT, where most RGB-based trackers (OSTrack, ARTrack, ODTrack) score below 40% SR, SpikeTrack reaches 41.0% SR and 52.3% PR, the best among all methods. Against the other SNN-based tracker SNNTrack [34], SpikeTrack achieves higher SR and PR on both FE108 and VisEvent while consuming comparable energy (7.92 vs 8.25 mJ) with fewer parameters (25.26M vs 31.40M). RGB-based methods such as ARTrack (202.56M params, 174.80 mJ) and ODTrack (92.83M params, 335.80 mJ) are consistently outperformed in both accuracy and efficiency, confirming the advantage of the pure SNN architecture for event data.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpikeTrack extends the I-LIF neuron framework from Luo et al. (ECCV 2024), which introduced integer-valued training with spike-driven inference, by making the maximum emitted integer value D input-adaptive through the proposed DI-LIF neuron. It departs from prior SNN-based event trackers (STNet, SNNTrack) that relied on CNN-SNN hybrid backbones, instead adopting a fully spike-driven Spiking Transformer architecture. The tracking head design follows OSTrack's center-size-offset prediction scheme, while the MSST multi-search-sequence training paradigm is a novel adaptation that leverages SNN membrane dynamics to model temporal evolution without auxiliary recurrent modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a pure SNN (no CNN feature extractor) can achieve state-of-the-art event-based tracking accuracy while consuming only ~7% of the energy of comparable RGB-based trackers, suggesting a viable path toward neuromorphic-hardware deployment. The DI-LIF neuron's sample-adaptive quantization addresses a practical limitation of fixed-scale I-LIF that could generalize beyond tracking to other SNN applications. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-object tracking or video understanding tasks, whether the energy savings translate to actual neuromorphic hardware (e.g., Loihi, TrueNorth), and whether the modest 1–2% accuracy margins over strong baselines persist under more diverse or adversarial conditions.
