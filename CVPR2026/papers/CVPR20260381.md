## Problem

Existing CXR lesion segmentation models are constrained to a single lesion type (e.g., COVID-19) and require long, expert-level radiological descriptions as input, making them impractical for both physicians who want to segment diverse lesions across anatomical subregions and non-experts who cannot interpret CXRs. No large-scale dataset exists that pairs simple, user-friendly segmentation instructions with pixel-level lesion masks and supports absence confirmation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260381/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a single CXR image and a short natural-language instruction of one of three types: basic (specifying lesion type and anatomical location, e.g., 'Segment the pneumonia in the right lung'), global (lesion type only, e.g., 'Segment the opacity'), or lesion inference (asking to predict the type of an opacity in a given location). It outputs a binary segmentation mask (or an empty mask for negative cases) together with a free-text description that confirms presence, states the lesion's location, or reports absence. The [SEG] special token in the text output triggers the mask decoder, so the textual and segmentation outputs are jointly generated in a single forward pass.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is a fully automated two-stage pipeline that constructs MIMIC-ILS (1.1M instruction-answer pairs from 192K images and 91K masks) without human annotation. Stage 1 (grounded lesion mask generation) uses an LLM to structure radiology reports into six-element tuples, then fuses three visual cues—an anomaly map from RadEdit diffusion editing, anatomy masks from CXAS, and lesion bounding boxes from a pretrained YOLO detector—through a four-condition filtering algorithm (overlap, confidence, signal ratio, size) to extract and refine lesion masks, followed by a location verification step that classifies each reported location as grounded, empty, or ungrounded. Stage 2 generates diverse instruction-answer pairs (positive, negative, and lesion-inference) from the verified masks and structured tuples. For the model, ROSALIA fine-tunes LISA-7B (LLaVA VLM backbone + SAM-H mask decoder) on MIMIC-ILS using LoRA (rank 128) on the VLM and full fine-tuning on the mask decoder, trained with a combined autoregressive cross-entropy loss on the text and a BCE+DICE loss on the mask, achieving 71.2 gIoU and 91.8% empty-target accuracy.

## Results

On the MIMIC-ILS test set, ROSALIA (LISA-7B fine-tuned on MIMIC-ILS) achieves a gIoU of 71.2%, cIoU of 75.6%, and empty-target accuracy (N-Acc.) of 91.8%, substantially outperforming all baselines. The best general-domain baseline (PixelLM-13B) reaches only 12.8% gIoU, and the best medical-domain baseline (BiomedParse) reaches 23.8% gIoU. Per-lesion gIoU ranges from 57.2% (pneumonia) to 89.0% (cardiomegaly), and text response accuracy reaches 94.4% overall across basic, global, and lesion-inference instruction types.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ROSALIA dominates all six baselines by a wide margin on every metric. On gIoU, ROSALIA (71.2%) outperforms the strongest general-domain baseline, PixelLM-13B (12.8%), by 58.4 points, and the strongest medical-domain baseline, BiomedParse (23.8%), by 47.4 points. The gap is even more stark on N-Acc.: ROSALIA correctly identifies absent lesions 91.8% of the time, whereas all baselines score below 22% (most near 0%), indicating they almost always hallucinate a mask when none exists. Among medical-domain baselines, BiomedParse and RecLMIS are the closest competitors on gIoU (23.8% and 22.4% respectively), but both still fall far short of ROSALIA. The results confirm that no existing general- or medical-domain VLM can handle instruction-guided CXR lesion segmentation without task-specific training, validating the need for the MIMIC-ILS dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions instruction-guided lesion segmentation (ILS) as a medical-domain adaptation of referring image segmentation (RIS), building directly on the LISA architecture (VLM + SAM) for the segmentation backbone. It departs from prior CXR segmentation work (RecLMIS, IMIS-Net) that required expert-level, detailed text descriptions and was limited to single lesion types, by introducing simpler user-friendly instructions and multi-lesion support. The automated dataset construction pipeline is novel in combining RadEdit (diffusion-based anomaly detection), CXAS (anatomy segmentation), YOLO (lesion detection), and LLM-based report structuring into a unified, human-free annotation pipeline, which has no direct precedent in the CXR literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIMIC-ILS (1.1M instruction-answer pairs, 192K images, 91K masks) is the first large-scale resource for pixel-level CXR lesion grounding with natural language instructions, and its fully automated construction with >95% expert acceptance rate demonstrates that high-quality medical segmentation datasets can be built without manual annotation. The 3× gIoU gap over the best zero-shot baseline (71.2 vs. 23.8) underscores that existing general-domain and medical VLMs are fundamentally unprepared for this task without domain-specific training. Open questions include whether the pipeline generalizes to other imaging modalities (CT, MRI), whether the seven lesion types suffice for clinical deployment, and whether the automated masks introduce systematic biases that expert review at 95% acceptance might miss.
