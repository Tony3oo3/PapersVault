## Problem

Vision Transformers rely on carefully designed token grouping strategies (window-based, cross-window, quadtree, bi-level routing) to mitigate the quadratic complexity of self-attention, but these methods introduce significant computational overhead and deployment complexity. The paper asks whether such intricate grouping designs are truly necessary, or whether a simpler, unified strategy could match or exceed their performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260566/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on vision tokens of shape h×w×d extracted from RGB images (224×224 for classification, 800×1333 for detection, 512×512 for segmentation), point clouds, or image-text pairs. A random tensor P of shape h×w (or n×h×w for n attention heads) is generated once and fixed; for higher-resolution downstream tasks, P is upsampled via nearest-neighbor interpolation. The output is identical to the base model's: class labels, bounding boxes with instance masks, pixel-wise segmentation maps, per-point labels, or text responses.

## Method

The random grouping strategy generates a fixed random tensor P matching the token grid, sorts tokens by P's values, and splits the sorted sequence into equal contiguous groups on which self-attention (or pooling) is computed. For multi-head attention, each head receives an independent random tensor, ensuring distinct groupings and thus diverse learned features across heads. The fixed nature of P (same grouping for every image) is critical, as fully random per-image grouping degrades performance by 4–7 points. The paper identifies four necessary conditions for this simplicity to work: explicit positional encoding (CPE replaces Swin's RPE), per-head random tensors for feature diversity, a global receptive field (tokens from all spatial regions appear in every group), and a fixed grouping pattern. Under these conditions, random grouping outperforms window, cross-window, quadtree, and bi-level routing strategies across classification, detection, segmentation, point clouds, and vision-language tasks while also improving inference speed.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, random grouping consistently improves top-1 accuracy across all backbone families: +1.4% on Swin-T (82.7% vs 81.3%), +1.1% on DeiT-S (80.9% vs 79.8%), and +0.8% on BiFormer-B (85.1% vs 84.3%). On COCO object detection with Mask R-CNN, Random-Swin-S achieves 48.0 AP^b (+2.3 over Swin-S) and 43.2 AP^m (+2.1). On ScanNet point cloud segmentation, Random-PTv3 reaches 77.8% mIoU (+0.2) with 23% lower latency (68 ms vs 88 ms). On LLaVA-1.6, the random pattern improves MME perception from 1519 to 1593 and POPE from 86.5 to 87.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Random grouping wins against all nine baselines on ImageNet-1K top-1 accuracy. The largest gains appear over Swin Transformer (+0.9 to +1.4 pp) and DeiT (+0.7 to +1.1 pp), while gains over the more sophisticated Quadtree (+0.5 to +0.7 pp) and BiFormer (+0.2 to +0.8 pp) are smaller but still consistent. A striking efficiency advantage emerges over Quadtree: Random-Quadtree-b3 runs at 1204 img/s versus 298 img/s for the original (~4× faster) while also improving accuracy. On COCO detection, Random-Swin-S gains +2.3 AP^b and +2.1 AP^m over Swin-S with Mask R-CNN, and Random-PVTv2-B2 gains +1.8 AP^b over PVTv2-B2. On ScanNet, Random-PTv3 matches PTv3's mIoU (77.8 vs 77.6) while cutting latency from 88 ms to 68 ms. On LLaVA-1.5/1.6, the random pattern improves every benchmark (e.g., MME +67/+74, POPE +1.3/+1.1), confirming generality across modalities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper challenges the entire lineage of designed token grouping strategies in Vision Transformers, from Swin's spatial windows through CSwin's cross-shaped windows, Quadtree's hierarchical grouping, BiFormer's bi-level routing, and PVTv2's pooling-based grouping. Rather than extending any single method, it repurposes the multi-head attention mechanism (assigning a distinct fixed random permutation per head) and positional encoding literature (CPE, LePE) to argue that the spatial or semantic structure of grouping is largely unnecessary. The conceptual departure is stark: a fixed random shuffle, once generated, replaces all carefully engineered grouping rules across partition-based, pooling-based, and plain backbones alike.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If random grouping is sufficient, the design space for efficient Vision Transformers collapses dramatically, removing a major source of engineering complexity and deployment friction. The four identified conditions (positional information, head feature diversity, global receptive field, fixed grouping pattern) offer a useful diagnostic framework for evaluating any grouping strategy. Open questions include whether the fixed-permutation requirement can be relaxed, whether the approach transfers to non-Transformer attention mechanisms, and whether the modest per-task gains (0.5–1.5 points) compound meaningfully in very large-scale or multi-task settings. The paper does not explore adaptive or learned randomization, leaving room for follow-up work on whether the 'fixed' constraint is truly load-bearing or an artifact of training stability.
