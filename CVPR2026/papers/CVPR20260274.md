## Problem

Annotated 3D scene data is scarce and expensive to acquire, requiring specialized hardware, reconstruction, and manual labeling, causing quantitative scaling to stagnate since ScanNet. Meanwhile, the internet contains orders of magnitude more unlabeled video capturing real 3D environments. The paper asks whether carefully designed automated data engines can convert these unlabeled web-scale videos into effective training data for comprehensive 3D scene understanding, and identifies which pipeline components most critically determine downstream performance.

## Input / Output

The pipeline ingests raw, unlabeled room-tour videos from open internet platforms (8,217 videos) and produces 6,687 scenes with camera poses, dense watertight meshes, per-instance 3D segmentations with ScanNet-aligned semantic labels, 632K spatial VQA pairs in VSI-Bench format, and 9,631 navigation trajectories each paired with three stylistically varied natural-language instructions. The only human involvement is a sub-10-second quality check per scene; all 3D geometry, segmentation, and language annotations are generated automatically. Keyframe selection is parallax-based rather than uniform to ensure well-constrained triangulation across potentially long (>300 frame) videos.

## Method

The core contribution is a modular, end-to-end data engine that chains five stages: (1) video curation with shot splitting (TransNetV2), content filtering, and parallax-based keyframe extraction; (2) Structure-from-Motion via dense pixel matching and global bundle adjustment (building on Mast3R-SFM with optimized pseudo-track pixels for memory efficiency on long videos); (3) dense reconstruction by projecting sparse SfM points into sparse depth priors, running PriorDA for metric depth, and fusing via TSDF into watertight meshes; (4) instance segmentation by lifting CropFormer 2D masks into 3D through neighboring-frame view consensus, then using Describe Anything and Qwen2-VL for automatic text descriptions and ScanNet label alignment; and (5) task-specific annotation generation—scene-graph-based spatial VQA templates and a three-stage VLN pipeline (trajectory clustering/backtracking removal, R2R-convention action discretization, and CoT-guided VLM instruction generation). The paper's key empirical finding is that data quality (trajectory refinement, instruction enrichment) and domain alignment matter more than raw data volume, and that models operating on raw modalities (voxels, RGB) scale more robustly than those dependent on task-specific pre-computed segments.

## Results

On 3D object detection (SpatialLM), pretraining on SceneVerse++ followed by ScanNet finetuning achieves F1@0.25 of 58.6, a +20.6 improvement over the original SpatialLM synthetic-data pretraining (38.0). On VSI-Bench (3D spatial VQA), Qwen2.5-VL-3B fine-tuned on SceneVerse++ data improves average accuracy from 27.9 to 42.8 on the full set (+14.9), with comparable performance to VLM-3R data from ground-truth ScanNet/ScanNet++ on the ARKitScenes subset (48.0 vs. 49.0). On R2R VLN, SceneVerse++ pretraining plus R2R finetuning raises Success Rate from 0.088 to 0.228, a +14 percentage-point gain over R2R-only training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SpatialLM's original synthetic-data pretraining [74], SceneVerse++ pretraining yields a decisive win on 3D detection: after ScanNet finetuning, F1@0.25 jumps from 38.0 to 58.6 (+20.6) and F1@0.5 from 28.7 to 45.4 (+16.7), while zero-shot on ARKitScenes is roughly on par (35.8 vs. 35.1). Against VLM-3R's ground-truth ScanNet/ScanNet++ data [33] on VSI-Bench, SceneVerse++ is competitive on the ARKitScenes subset (48.0 vs. 49.0 for Qwen2.5-VL-3B) but trails on the full set (42.8 vs. 48.7), reflecting a domain gap in categories like Object Count and Room Size; combining both data sources (All) further improves to 49.3, confirming complementary coverage. On 3D instance segmentation (Mask3D), SceneVerse++ pretraining alone underperforms ScanNet-from-scratch (AP 8.3 vs. 22.8) due to domain-specific sensitivity to reconstruction pipelines, but after ScanNet finetuning it edges out the from-scratch baseline (AP 23.6 vs. 22.8), indicating the benefit is realized only with task-specific adaptation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D-to-3D lifting paradigm (CropFormer, MaskClustering, Describe Anything) and SfM pipelines (building on Mast3R-SFM) into a unified, multi-task automated data engine for 3D scene understanding. It departs from prior internet-video-to-3D work (RoomTour3D, NaVILA, Miao et al.) by addressing comprehensive scene understanding—detection, segmentation, spatial VQA, and navigation—rather than a single task, and by providing systematic bottleneck analysis of where modular pipelines fail. The spatial VQA data generation follows VLM-3R's scene-graph template approach, while the VLN trajectory conversion introduces a novel three-stage pipeline (path pre-processing, action encoding, instruction generation) not present in prior YouTube-based VLN data efforts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SceneVerse++ (6,687 scenes) directly addresses the stagnation in 3D dataset scaling since ScanNet, offering a reproducible recipe to convert unlabeled internet video into multi-task 3D training data. The finding that Mask3D's zero-shot transfer degrades sharply when pretrained on SceneVerse++ (AP25: 15.4 vs. 36.1 from scratch) while SpatialLM improves substantially reveals that model architecture sensitivity to domain-specific bias is a critical, underappreciated factor in 3D data scaling. Open questions include whether the domain gap between internet video and simulator-rendered scenes (evident in VLN) can be closed with better trajectory alignment, and whether the VSI-Bench evaluation bias noted by the authors limits the generalizability of the spatial reasoning gains.
