## Problem

Prior mistake-understanding work in egocentric videos is limited to binary detection or coarse categorization, unable to answer what specific semantic role in the instruction is violated, when the deviation becomes irreversible (Point-of-No-Return), or where the mistake manifests spatially. Compounding this, existing mistake datasets contain only hundreds to a few thousand samples and lack the triplet (semantic, temporal, spatial) attribution annotations needed to train and benchmark fine-grained mistake models at real-world scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261561/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an instruction text (e.g., "pick up hammer") and an egocentric video clip of the user's attempt, where the text is first decomposed via Semantic Role Labeling into per-role substrings (predicate, object) that are encoded as individual tokens. The output is a structured triplet: a binary label per semantic role indicating violation, a PNR frame index, and a bounding box in that PNR frame. At inference, the temporal and spatial heads are gated—they activate only when at least one role is predicted as mistaken, making the pipeline efficient for correct attempts.

## Method

MisFormer is a unified transformer with a dual-branch feature extractor (InternVideo2 text and video encoders) feeding a shared projection block that uses self-attention across role tokens and cross-attention from text to video patches, producing role-conditioned, video-grounded embeddings. Three specialized heads consume these: a semantic head (FFN + sigmoid per role), a temporal head (spatial downsampling + cross-attention decoder producing a frame probability distribution via softmax), and a spatial head (cross-attention weight extraction at the PNR frame, refinement into a 2D saliency heatmap, and CNN-based box regression). The companion data engine MisEngine automatically constructs large-scale mistake samples by cross-matching SRL-parsed instruction texts with mismatched action videos, yielding semantic attribution labels for free while inheriting PNR timestamps and hand/object bounding boxes from source corpora, producing Ego4D-M (257K samples) and EPIC-KITCHENS-M (221K samples)—two orders of magnitude larger than prior mistake datasets.

## Results

On semantic attribution, MisFormer achieves 83.89% F1 on EPIC-KITCHENS-M and 56.24% F1 on Ego4D-M, surpassing the best baseline (ChatGPT-4o) by 6.66% and 5.30% respectively. For temporal attribution (PNR localization), MisFormer reduces MAE to 19.14 frames (0.638 s) on Ego4D-M, a 21.81% improvement over the best prior method (EgoT2 at 24.48 frames). On mistake detection, MisFormer reaches 78.05% F1@0.5 on EPIC-KITCHENS-M and 57.55% on Ego4D-M, dramatically outperforming specialized detectors AMNAR (16.34%/12.57%) and EgoPED (21.18%/15.62%). For spatial attribution, MisFormer achieves 59.21% mIoU, beating hand-detection baselines by 18.7% mIoU but trailing the dedicated hand-object detector SSDA (64.54% mIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MisFormer wins decisively on semantic attribution against all three video-language baselines: it beats ChatGPT-4o by 6.66% F1 on EK and 5.30% on Ego, and outperforms open-source LLaVA-Vid and Vid-Chat by 13.55% and 20.62% F1 on EK respectively. On temporal PNR localization, MisFormer reduces MAE by 21.81% over EgoT2 (19.14 vs. 24.48 frames) and by 60.9% over EgoMotion-COMPASS (19.14 vs. 48.96 frames), benefiting from instruction-text conditioning that prior video-only methods lack. For spatial attribution, MisFormer substantially outperforms MediaPipe hand detection (+9.33 mIoU) but is outperformed by the dedicated hand-object interaction detector SSDA (59.21 vs. 64.54 mIoU), though it runs 2.4× faster (68.9 vs. 28.44 FPS). On mistake detection, MisFormer achieves 78.05% F1 on EK and 57.55% on Ego, far exceeding AMNAR (16.34%/12.57%) and EgoPED (21.18%/15.62%), which struggle due to their OOD-detection design on limited activity sets. On the smaller EgoPER benchmark, MisFormer trained from scratch underperforms specialized detectors (26.78% vs. 32.18% F1), but with pretraining on EPIC-KITCHENS-M it surpasses both (35.18% F1), demonstrating that data scale from MisEngine is critical for competitive detection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formulating Mistake Attribution (MATT) as a joint semantic-temporal-spatial problem, departing from prior mistake understanding work that is limited to binary detection (EgoPED, PREGO, AMNAR), predefined category classification (Lee et al.), or free-form natural language explanation (MistSense) without alignment to instruction structure. Methodologically, it repurposes Semantic Role Labeling (AllenNLP) both as a data-construction tool (MisEngine cross-matches instructions across videos by role) and as a model input decomposition, and builds on InternVideo2's video-language pre-training for shared-embedding feature extraction. The unified transformer architecture with cross-attention heads for each attribution axis is a straightforward composition of standard components rather than a departure from existing transformer design patterns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MisEngine-constructed datasets (Ego4D-M, EPIC-KITCHENS-M) are two orders of magnitude larger than all prior mistake datasets and provide the first large-scale supervision for fine-grained mistake attribution, making them likely to become standard benchmarks for the egocentric video community. However, the task is currently restricted to predicate and object roles with short instructions, and the spatial attribution head underperforms specialized hand-object detectors (SSDA), suggesting the unified framework has not yet fully solved the localization subproblem. Open questions include whether MATT generalizes to longer compositional instructions with richer role inventories, and whether task-specific video-instruction representations (beyond repurposed video-language pre-training) could close the remaining gaps.
