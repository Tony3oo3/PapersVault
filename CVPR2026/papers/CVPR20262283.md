## Problem

State-of-the-art modulation-based personalization methods (e.g., XVerse, TokenVerse) require clean, pre-segmented reference images, severely limiting their utility for real-world in-the-wild photos containing multiple objects. Moreover, prior approaches either rely on overly simplistic single-word prompts that cannot disambiguate specific subjects in cluttered scenes or bypass the problem entirely with segmentation masks that are unavailable for abstract concepts like artistic styles or textures. The paper addresses the gap of enabling prompt-guided, segmentation-free concept decomposition and composition from unsegmented multi-object reference images.

## Input / Output

The method takes one or more unsegmented reference images, a reference prompt (a text description identifying which concept to extract from each reference, e.g., 'the man on the left'), and a target text prompt describing the desired output scene. The reference prompt may intentionally contradict the target prompt (e.g., a subject 'sitting on grass' in the reference becomes 'riding a horse' in the target). Output is a 768×768 personalized image; the method reliably composes up to six distinct subjects before identity crosstalk or object omission degrades quality.

## Method

The core contribution is the Reference Condition Extractor (RCE), a single module that simultaneously produces two semantically aligned conditions: (1) a visual latent z_ref obtained by modulating CLIP image features with CLIP text features via FiLM and projecting to DiT latent space, which is appended to all denoising inputs; and (2) a textual modulation offset computed by injecting the FiLM-modulated visual features into T5 prompt-token embeddings via a Perceiver layer, yielding both a shared offset Δ^s across all DiT blocks and block-specific offsets Δ^j. Training is two-stage: Stage 1 pretrains only the FiLM layer on PhraseCut with a CLIPSeg decoder providing segmentation supervision (BCE loss), teaching the module to localize concepts from a prompt; Stage 2 jointly finetunes the full RCE and LoRA-adapted DiT (rank 128) on a multi-concept dataset using standard diffusion loss, with a cross-reference augmentation that horizontally combines reference images to encourage correct concept disambiguation.

## Results

On XVerseBench, UniVerse achieves the best overall score of 74.16, outperforming the second-best XVerse (71.15) by 3.01 points. In single-subject generation, UniVerse leads in identity preservation (ID-S: 82.77 vs. XVerse 79.80) and appearance similarity (IP-S: 75.88 vs. XVerse 67.68), yielding a single-subject average of 78.14. In multi-subject generation, UniVerse achieves 70.18 average, surpassing XVerse (67.93) primarily through a large IP-S margin (71.60 vs. 62.59). On the proposed UniVerseBench, UniVerse also attains the highest overall score (51.05), confirming its advantage in prompt-guided concept decomposition from cluttered reference images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniVerse outperforms all seven baselines on overall XVerseBench score (74.16), with the closest competitor being XVerse (71.15), a gap of 3.01 points driven largely by superior IP-S in both single-subject (75.88 vs. 67.68) and multi-subject (71.60 vs. 62.59) settings. UniVerse also leads in single-subject ID-S (82.77), beating XVerse (79.80) and OmniGen (76.63), indicating stronger identity preservation. However, UniVerse trails DreamO on DPG (prompt alignment) in both single (91.93 vs. 97.19) and multi (87.95 vs. 89.73) settings, and slightly lags XVerse on single-subject AES (55.86 vs. 57.43), suggesting minor trade-offs in prompt fidelity and aesthetic quality. On the proposed UniVerseBench, which specifically tests concept decomposition from cluttered references, UniVerse achieves the best overall score (51.05) over MS-Diffusion (50.49) and OmniGen (49.53), with the largest margins over MIP-Adapter (46.31) and OmniGen2 (46.50). In multi-subject XVerseBench, OmniGen achieves the highest ID-S (74.34) but UniVerse still wins on IP-S and overall average, while UNO and MIP-Adapter show the weakest multi-subject performance (59.34 and 51.92 respectively), highlighting their difficulty with compositional generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniVerse extends the DiT modulation line of work initiated by TokenVerse and XVerse, which inject personalized offsets into the AdaLN conditioning pathway for multi-concept control. The key departure is the introduction of a Reference Condition Extractor (RCE) that, guided by a natural-language reference prompt, simultaneously produces a FiLM-modulated visual latent and a Perceiver-derived textual offset from a single unsegmented reference image—removing the clean-segmentation prerequisite that limited prior modulation methods. The two-stage training strategy (segmentation-supervised pretraining of the FiLM module, followed by joint diffusion training with LoRA) is a novel curriculum that teaches the extractor to localize concepts before it is asked to generate, a design not present in XVerse or TokenVerse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the segmentation dependency, UniVerse makes prompt-guided multi-concept personalization practical for in-the-wild photos where masks are unavailable, and the new UniVerseBench provides the first dedicated evaluation of concept decomposition from co-occurring subjects. However, the compositional capacity plateaus around six objects, residual concept leakage persists under ambiguous prompts, and the benchmark is small (20 references, 200 prompts), leaving open whether the RCE generalizes to denser scenes or more abstract attribute combinations. A broader, segmentation-free multi-reference benchmark with richer attribute diversity would be needed to fully validate the approach at scale.
