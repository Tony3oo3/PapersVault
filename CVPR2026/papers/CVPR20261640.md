## Problem

Existing low-light understanding methods either overfit to labeled training sets or to dataset-specific degradation patterns, failing to generalize to unseen real-world lighting conditions. The core gap is the absence of a unified framework that simultaneously enforces signal-level invariance and injects generalizable semantic knowledge from vision foundation models, all without requiring any real low-light training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261640/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single low-light RGB image (224×224×3 for classification) and produces task-specific outputs: class labels, pixel-wise segmentation masks, or face bounding boxes, depending on the downstream backbone (ResNet-18, RefineNet, or DSFD). Optionally, the TTA enhancer first produces a per-sample enhanced image in pixel space before feeding it to the understanding model. DINOv2 and CLIP serve as frozen reference models providing correlation maps and text-embedding cues, respectively, while the illumination-invariant prior extractor (QuadPrior) generates a 6-channel auxiliary signal from the input.

## Method

UniPrior is a two-stage, zero-real-low-light-data framework. Stage 1 (offline adaptation) stabilizes features via an illumination-invariant prior fed as a zero-initialized auxiliary channel into the backbone, enforced by an outlier-robust prior consistency loss and a lightweight decoding-based regularization that acts as an information bottleneck; it then enriches semantics through DINOv2 self-correlation maps that define spatially-aware positive/negative masks for InfoNCE contrastive learning, plus a cross-entropy correlation alignment loss between backbone and DINOv2 self-similarity matrices. Stage 2 (test-time adaptation) runs a CoLIE-based per-sample enhancer guided by three machine-oriented losses: a light-effect suppression loss penalizing overexposed regions, a signal-prior consistency loss, and a CLIP-based max-entropy intermediate loss that pushes enhanced-image embeddings away from both day and night text manifolds to erase illumination attributes. The entire pipeline is trained only on DarkISP-synthesized low-light pairs, requiring no real low-light data.

## Results

On CoDaN classification, UniPrior achieves 74.52% Top-1 accuracy (75.72% with TTA Enhancer), surpassing the previous best zero-shot method DAI-Net (68.44%) by 6.08 points and the best enhancement method GEFU (60.92%) by 13.6 points. On Nighttime Driving segmentation, it reaches 48.2% mIoU (48.6% with TTA), outperforming Sim-MinMax (44.9%) by 3.3 points. On DarkFace detection, it achieves 31.3% mAP (33.6% with TTA), exceeding DAI-Net (28.0%) by 3.3 points. The main model adds only 0.24G FLOPs and 0.02M parameters over the ResNet-18 baseline, with inference time unchanged at 0.005s.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniPrior outperforms all 15 baselines across every task and dataset. In classification, it beats the strongest zero-shot adaptation method DAI-Net by 6.08 points and the best enhancement method GEFU by 13.6 points, while adding negligible overhead (2.04G vs 1.80G FLOPs). Among zero-shot adaptation methods, Sim-MinMax (65.87%) and CIConv (60.32%) trail significantly, confirming that physics-only priors without semantic enrichment are insufficient. In segmentation, UniPrior surpasses Sim-MinMax by 3.3 mIoU on ND and 0.3 mIoU on ACDC-N, and outperforms the RefineNet baseline by 13.9 and 4.1 mIoU respectively. In face detection, it exceeds DAI-Net by 3.3 mAP and the DSFD baseline by 15.2 mAP on DarkFace. Notably, all enhancement-only methods (Zero-DCE, CoLIE, SCI, etc.) underperform or barely exceed the unadapted baseline on segmentation, validating the paper's argument that pixel-level correction alone cannot guarantee downstream understanding performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work: physically-informed illumination-invariant priors (building directly on QuadPrior [40]) and semantic priors from vision foundation models (DINOv2, CLIP). Prior zero-shot adaptation methods (MAET, CIConv, Sim-MinMax, DAI-Net) leverage physics priors but lack generalizable semantic representations, while data-required adaptation methods (WiiD, HLA-Face) overfit to specific degradation distributions. The key departure is the correlation-map-based alignment strategy, which aligns dynamic self-similarity structures rather than static feature embeddings, and the decoding-based regularization that uses a lightweight auxiliary decoder as an information bottleneck to enforce illumination-invariant compactness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework's strict requirement of zero real low-light training data makes it directly deployable in scenarios where collecting matched low-light data is infeasible (autonomous driving, surveillance), and the demonstrated sensitivity of data-required methods to both distribution shift and data quantity (Fig. 6) strengthens the practical case for prior-based approaches. However, the TTA enhancer introduces a ~500× computational overhead (2.593s vs 0.005s per image), limiting real-time applicability, and the evaluation is confined to relatively small benchmarks (2,500–4,000 test images). Open questions include whether the VFM-guided contrastive strategy scales to larger backbones or more extreme degradations (e.g., heavy motion blur, sensor-specific noise), and whether the correlation alignment principle generalizes beyond DINOv2 to other foundation model families.
