## Problem

Existing B-Rep generation methods suffer from two intertwined forms of brittleness: representation brittleness, where padding noise and multi-hop feature contamination corrupt the latent space, and generation brittleness, where cascaded geometry-then-topology pipelines propagate errors and defer validity enforcement to non-differentiable post-processing, creating a train-inference mismatch. No prior method simultaneously achieves a compact high-fidelity latent representation, joint geometry-topology decoding, and differentiable enforcement of manifold validity constraints.

## Input / Output

The model accepts optional conditioning signals—class labels, point clouds (encoded by PointNet++), or RGB images (encoded by DINOv2)—and produces a parametric B-Rep solid consisting of Bezier surface faces, Bezier curve edges with explicit endpoint vertices, and a binary edge-face adjacency matrix. The output is a closed, watertight manifold solid that must pass OpenCascade kernel-level validation (exportable as a STEP file with consistent edge-face incidence). Input B-Reps are canonicalized by lexicographic sorting of bounding-box centers and padded to fixed face/edge budgets (F_max, E_max) defined by dataset maxima.

## Method

HiFi-BRep is a two-stage pipeline: a VAE learns a high-fidelity latent space, and a DiT-based latent diffusion model generates samples within it. The VAE encoder uses a dual-stream architecture where face and edge tokens undergo per-stream self-attention and cross-stream attention restricted by a Topo-Mask (the edge-face incidence matrix as a hard attention mask), then learnable queries pool the variable-length tokens into a fixed-length latent, eliminating padding noise. The single-stage decoder jointly predicts face/edge counts, geometric parameters (Bezier control points, bounding boxes, endpoints), and the edge-face adjacency matrix in one pass; topology is supervised by a row-wise two-peak loss that assigns equal probability mass to the two incident faces of each edge, turning the manifold constraint into a differentiable training objective rather than a post-hoc repair step. This design enables bidirectional geometry-topology optimization and avoids cascaded error propagation inherent in multi-stage baselines.

## Results

On DeepCAD, HiFi-BRep achieves the highest structural validity (72.20%) and the lowest MMD-CD (1.05), while maintaining a Compilability–Validity gap of only 18.18 points (vs. 49.28 for DTGBrepGen). On ABC, HiFi-BRep delivers the best Validity (32.66%) with competitive MMD-CD (1.45) and JSD (1.81), and a gap of just 2.95 points. In terms of runtime, HiFi-BRep is the fastest method at 3.83 s/shape, 2.1× faster than BRepGen, 6.2× faster than DTGBrepGen, and 6.9× faster than BrepDiff.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DTGBrepGen (CVPR 2025), HiFi-BRep wins decisively on structural validity: 72.20% vs. 43.20% on DeepCAD and 32.66% vs. 24.88% on ABC, while the Compilability–Validity gap shrinks from 49.28 to 18.18 (DeepCAD) and from 25.67 to 2.95 (ABC). However, DTGBrepGen retains an edge on distributional fidelity on ABC (COV 70.63 vs. 57.93, MMD-CD 1.30 vs. 1.45, JSD 1.55 vs. 1.81) and on DeepCAD JSD (0.98 vs. 1.72). Against BrepDiff (SIGGRAPH 2025), HiFi-BRep dominates on both validity (72.20% vs. 63.69% on DeepCAD; 32.66% vs. 20.05% on ABC) and MMD-CD, while BrepDiff's single-stage design still suffers from a large post-processing cost (23.07 s). Against BRepGen (CVPR 2024), HiFi-BRep is a clear winner across nearly all metrics, with validity jumping from 20.76% to 72.20% on DeepCAD and from 20.19% to 32.66% on ABC. The programmatic DeepCAD baseline remains the reference for distributional coverage (COV 76.67) but is not a learned generative model; HiFi-BRep approaches its validity (72.20% vs. 68.20%) while being a fully neural pipeline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant cascaded paradigm in B-Rep generation (SolidGen, DTGBrepGen) where geometry and topology are predicted sequentially, and from post-processing-based validity enforcement (BRepGen, BRepDiff) that creates a train-inference mismatch. It repurposes the VAE + latent diffusion architecture common to prior B-Rep work but restructures both the encoder (learnable queries to eliminate padding, Topo-Mask to prevent feature contamination) and the decoder (single-stage parallel geometry-topology prediction with a differentiable row-wise two-peak manifold objective). The conceptual departure is treating the 'two faces per edge' constraint as a learnable training signal rather than a discrete post-hoc check, which no prior B-Rep generator achieves in a single decoding pass.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-30-point validity improvement on DeepCAD (72.2% vs. 43.2% for the strongest baseline) and 6–7× speedup address two of the most practical barriers to deploying neural B-Rep generators in CAD pipelines. The differentiable manifold constraint is a clean, generalizable idea that could transfer to other structured generation tasks with hard combinatorial validity rules. However, the method is limited to closed watertight solids under a fixed face/edge budget, and residual failures from trimming disagreement and junction inconsistency (Fig. 6) indicate that exact surface-curve intersection remains delegated to the CAD kernel. Open questions include extending to open-boundary parts, large assemblies, and dynamic-capacity decoding for long-tailed topologies.
