## Problem

Large vision-language models and CLIP-based classifiers remain highly vulnerable to small adversarial perturbations, and existing test-time defenses (prompt tuning, multi-view ensembling, diffusion-based purification) incur substantial inference overhead or require auxiliary models. This paper asks whether a lightweight, training-free transformation applied at inference can provably restore correct classification without the computational cost of prior test-time adaptation methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262084/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single test image (potentially adversarial) and a fixed text label set (e.g., the full ImageNet-21K vocabulary) used to compute the energy function; no auxiliary model or additional views are required. It produces a perturbed image x̃ = x + z constrained to an ℓ₂ ball of radius ε (typically 4–5/255), which is then fed through the same classifier or CLIP encoder to yield a class label, caption, or VQA answer. For LVLMs, only the visual encoder participates in the optimization; the language model receives the refined visual embedding unchanged.

## Method

ET3 reinterprets a softmax classifier's logits as an energy function E(x) = −log Σ_k exp(f_θ(x)_k) (negative LogSumExp) and minimizes it via 1–2 gradient descent steps on the input image, projecting back onto an ℓ₂ ball after each step. For CLIP zero-shot classification, image–text similarity scores serve as the logits; for LVLMs, the energy is computed solely through the CLIP vision encoder, leaving the language model untouched. The key theoretical contribution proves that, under local linearity and a gradient-norm-ratio condition (the ground-truth logit's energy gradient dominates the other class's), a single ET3 step provably flips an adversarial binary classifier's prediction to the correct class. This makes ET3 a plug-and-play, training-free defense that adds only ~2% inference overhead while being composable with existing methods like test-time prompt tuning or augmentation ensembles for further gains.

## Results

On 14 zero-shot benchmark datasets under AutoAttack (ε=4/255), ET3 improves robust accuracy by +8.08 points for TeCoA (32.85→40.80) and +11.36 points for FARE trained at εt=2/255 (20.39→31.75). On 8 fine-grained datasets, ET3 alone achieves 16.51% average robust accuracy, surpassing TTC (13.90%) and matching TPT (16.50%) while requiring no training or multiple augmentations. Under adaptive attacks on ImageNet (ResNet-50), ET3 achieves 37.70% worst-case robust accuracy, outperforming the base model (34.96%) and other TTT methods (Singh et al. 34.68%, Kulkarni et al. 35.48%). On LLaVA 1.5-7B, ET3 boosts adversarial robustness by up to +37.8 points (standard CLIP) and +11.0 points (FARE4) across captioning and VQA tasks with only 2.3% inference overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the lightweight (single-image transformation) scope, ET3 (16.51% avg robust) clearly outperforms TTC (13.90%) by +2.61 points and C-TPT (13.90%) by +2.61 points, while matching TPT (16.50%) without requiring multiple augmented views or prompt optimization. In the multi-augmentation scope, ET3 combined with ensemble averaging (22.26%) surpasses MTA (17.80%) by +4.46 points and matches R-TPT (21.41%) with a simpler pipeline. When combined with R-TPT, ET3 pushes the state-of-the-art to 23.88%, exceeding R-TPT alone by +2.47 points. Under adaptive attacks on ImageNet (Table 4), ET3 achieves 37.70% worst-case robust accuracy, beating the base model (34.96%) by +2.74 points, Singh et al.'s spectral projection (34.68%), and Kulkarni et al.'s interpretability-guided defense (35.48%). Against diffusion-based purification methods (DiffPure, GDMP), ET3's underlying robust classifier is far more resilient: DiffBreak reduces DiffPure to 12.11–25.00% robust accuracy, while ET3 on a robust ResNet-50 maintains 37.70%.

## Relation to Prior Work

ET3 sits at the intersection of two research threads: the energy-based model (EBM) perspective on classifiers, formalized by Grathwohl et al. [30] who showed that a softmax classifier's logits can be interpreted as energies, and the growing family of test-time transformation (TTT) defenses for adversarial robustness (TPT, C-TPT, R-TPT, TTC, MTA). Unlike prior TTT methods that require prompt optimization, multiple augmented views, or auxiliary generative models (e.g., diffusion-based purification), ET3 performs a lightweight gradient descent on the input image to minimize the LogSumExp energy through the existing encoder, requiring no additional model or training. The paper also departs from prior TTT work by providing a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) proving correct classification after transformation under local linearity and a gradient-norm ratio condition, a property no prior test-time defense has established.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ET3 offers a practically attractive defense: two gradient steps through the CLIP encoder add only ~2.3% inference overhead while yielding 8–11 point robust accuracy gains on zero-shot CLIP and substantial improvements on LVLM captioning/VQA tasks, and it composes additively with existing methods like R-TPT. The theoretical guarantee, though restricted to binary classifiers with strong assumptions (local linearity, gradient ratio C), is a first for TTT defenses and opens a path toward designing networks that provably satisfy these conditions. Key open questions include whether the energy-minimization objective generalizes beyond the ImageNet-21K proxy label set to truly open-world or domain-specific settings, whether the local linearity assumption holds for large ViT encoders under strong adaptive attacks, and whether training the encoder to explicitly increase the energy gradient ratio could further amplify the defense.
