## Problem

Multimodal LLMs struggle with geometric reasoning due to extreme scarcity of high-quality image-text training pairs, where human annotation is prohibitively expensive and automated synthesis methods fail to balance correctness, diversity, and training effectiveness. Existing approaches—whether passive image-based augmentation, symbolic random generation with post-hoc filtering, or LLM-driven black-box amplification—produce static datasets that decouple data generation from model learning, missing opportunities for iterative, learner-driven improvement.

## Input / Output

The Solver consumes a geometric diagram (rendered from Python code) paired with a natural-language problem statement and produces a step-by-step reasoning solution with a final answer, evaluated via rule-based extraction plus LLM-as-judge. The Generator takes a structured natural-language drawing instruction (e.g., 'draw triangle ABC with angle BAC = 60°, add point P on arc BC') and outputs a geometrically precise diagram image. The Teacher internally operates on parameterized Python geometry scripts, executing them to render images and verifying both mathematical solvability (Reflect) and visual rendering correctness (RePI) before admitting any triplet to the curriculum.

## Method

Socratic-Geo is a closed-loop multi-agent framework where a Teacher (Qwen3-VL-235B) diagnoses the Solver's (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) failures and programmatically modifies Python geometry code to invent targeted problems, verified through a Reflect-RePI pipeline that checks both mathematical solvability and rendering fidelity. The Solver is optimized via GRPO, with a key mechanism: when all k sampling attempts fail, the Teacher's verified reference solution is injected as the sole positive example, ensuring a gold-standard learning signal even under complete failure. The curriculum evolves iteratively—only problems the Solver cannot solve trigger new synthesis, making data generation learner-driven rather than random. Independently, the Teacher translates each new problem's programmatic representation into a natural-language drawing instruction, and a diffusion-based Generator (Qwen-Image) is fine-tuned via standard SFT on these (instruction, image) pairs, distilling the Teacher's symbolic drawing intelligence into generative weights. Starting from 108 seed problems, this yields 2.5k high-quality triplets sufficient to achieve +4.13 points over zero-shot baselines while the Generator reaches 42.4% relaxed score on GenExam-Math.

## Results

On the geometric reasoning track, Socratic-Solver-Geo (Stage 3) achieves 49.11% Mean@1 overall accuracy across five benchmarks (MathVerse, GeoQA, MathVision, MathVista, WeMath), a +4.13 point gain over the zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct baseline (44.98%) while using only 2.5k synthetic samples versus 7.2k–10k for all baselines. On the generation track, Socratic-Generator-Image scores 42.4% Relaxed and 6.0% Strict on GenExam-Math, establishing a new open-source state-of-the-art that surpasses Seedream 4.0 (39.8% Relaxed) and approaches Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image (43.1% Relaxed). The framework also generalizes to Chart Reasoning (+3.9 to +7.6 points) and Multimodal Coding (+5.2 to +5.6 points) without task-specific modifications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the reasoning track, Socratic-Solver-Geo (Stage 3) outperforms all four data-synthesis baselines: it beats GeoReasoning (46.68%) by 2.43 points, Geo170k/G-LLaVA (46.26%) by 2.85 points, R-CoT (46.05%) by 3.06 points, and PGPS9k (45.89%) by 3.22 points on Overall accuracy, while using roughly one-quarter of the training data (2.5k vs. 7.2k–10k). The largest per-benchmark margins appear on MathVerse (+4.06 over GeoReasoning) and WeMath (+3.68 over GeoReasoning). On the generation track, Socratic-Generator-Image (42.4% Relaxed) surpasses all open-source T2I and unified MLLM baselines by a wide margin (next-best open-source is Qwen-Image at 18.9%), and beats closed-source Seedream 4.0 (39.8%) and Imagen-4-Ultra (35.9%), but trails GPT-Image-1 (52.0%) and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image (43.1%) by 9.6 and 0.7 points respectively. The ablation confirms that the Reflect (Qualify) verification module is essential: removing it drops MathVerse from 40.33% to 37.09% despite tripling the training set, and removing Instruction Rewriting collapses the generator's Strict score from 6.0% to 0.0%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Socratic-Zero's multi-agent co-evolution framework (Solver-Teacher-Generator) from text-only mathematical reasoning to geometric reasoning, where the critical adaptation is replacing free-text problem generation with programmatic Python code modification (RePI) and dual verification (Reflect for solvability, RePI for visual validity). It departs from the three existing categories of geometric data synthesis—image-based textual augmentation (R-CoT, G-LLaVA), symbolic random generation (InterGPS, TrustGeoGen), and LLM-driven augmentation—by making data generation reactive to the Solver's specific failure patterns rather than static or randomly explored. The GRPO-based Solver optimization and diffusion-based Generator training follow established paradigms, but their tight coupling within a single closed-loop curriculum is the architectural novelty.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a 2.5k-sample curriculum generated through failure-driven synthesis can outperform 7.2k–10k static datasets, offering a practical path to reducing annotation costs for geometric reasoning. The Generator-as-byproduct angle—distilling programmatic drawing intelligence into a diffusion model via instruction-image pairs—is a clean conceptual contribution that could generalize to other structured visual domains. However, the approach's reliance on a 235B-parameter Teacher (Qwen3-VL-235B) to generate and verify all problems raises open questions about true autonomy and scalability; the generalizability results (Table 2) are presented without detailed ablations, and the framework has not been tested on 3D geometry or more complex multi-step constructions. Whether the data-efficiency advantage persists as the Solver scales beyond 7B parameters remains unexplored.
