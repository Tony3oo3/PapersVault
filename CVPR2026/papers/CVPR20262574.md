## Problem

Existing language-guided geo-localization methods follow a static, one-shot retrieval paradigm where a fixed textual query is processed once to return the best-matching location. This passive design cannot handle the ambiguity and incompleteness inherent in real-world natural language descriptions, as the system cannot actively seek clarification or gather additional discriminative information when the initial input is vague or erroneous.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262574/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an initial ambiguous text description (simulating a user's incomplete memory, e.g., 'I see a bank with a telephone booth beside it') and iteratively consumes user responses to generated questions, concatenating all answers into an enriched retrieval query. The CMPL retriever (CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone) matches this evolving text against a database of geo-tagged street-view images, while DQ-pilot (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with LoRA) outputs a structured chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a single discriminative question per round. The full interaction spans up to 5 dialogue rounds, with each round's answer progressively narrowing the candidate set.

## Method

The framework couples two components in an iterative loop: a Cross-Modal Progressive Learning (CMPL) retriever that aligns hierarchical visual and textual features from intermediate ViT layers using a shared fine-grained extractor with learnable instance-concept queries, a saliency filtering module, multi-level similarity distribution matching (SDM) loss, and a Hard-Negative Isolation (HI) loss for geometric separability; and DQ-pilot, an MLLM questioner that analyzes candidate locations and dialogue history via chain-of-thought to generate high-information-gain questions. DQ-pilot is trained in a two-stage curriculum: supervised fine-tuning on 20k low-difficulty samples (DQ-cities-20k) followed by GRPO reinforcement learning on 10k high-difficulty samples (DQ-cities-10k), where the reward combines a binary format reward and a Positional Retrieval Gain (PRG) reward that directly measures the rank improvement of positive samples induced by each generated question. The curriculum is guided by a Discriminative Difficulty Index (DDI) that weights Semantic Ambiguity and Retriever-Informed Difficulty to ensure progressive learning from basic perception to advanced reasoning.

## Results

On the DlgQuest-Cities benchmark (five cities), DlgQuest (SFT+GRPO) achieves R@1 of 58.4–61.8 and R@5 of 76.5–80.1 at Round 5 across cities, with the lowest BRI score of 1.18, indicating the most efficient questioning. Compared to its Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, the method improves R@1 by 9.2% (Round 3) and 13.4% (Round 5) on Los Angeles, and outperforms the 72B variant by 7.3% R@1. In static retrieval (Table 2), the CMPL retriever reaches R@1=82.5, R@5=95.1, R@10=97.4 (averaged over five cities), surpassing the best prior method FG-CLIP by 4.3 points in R@1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, DlgQuest (SFT+GRPO) improves R@1 by 9.2% at Round 3 and 13.4% at Round 5 on Los Angeles (58.4 vs. 43.2), while reducing BRI from 1.58 to 1.18. It also surpasses the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-72B by 7.3% R@1 at Round 5 (58.4 vs. 49.5 on LA), demonstrating that task-specific training outweighs raw model scale. Compared to PlugIR, the strongest interactive retrieval baseline, DlgQuest gains 7.2% R@1 and 6.2% R@5 at Round 5 on LA while achieving a lower BRI (1.18 vs. 1.41), indicating both higher accuracy and more efficient questioning. In the static retrieval setting (Table 2), the CMPL retriever outperforms all CLIP-based baselines: it leads FG-CLIP (R@1=78.2) and Flair (R@1=78.1) by ~4.3 points, and Long-CLIP (R@1=69.1) and vanilla CLIP (R@1=64.0) by 13.4 and 18.5 points respectively, confirming the benefit of hierarchical progressive alignment and hard-negative isolation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the static, one-shot retrieval paradigm dominant in language-driven geo-localization (Text2Pose, Text2Loc, 'Where am I?') by reframing place recognition as an iterative, dialogue-driven reasoning process. It draws on interactive retrieval methods from text-to-image and person re-ID (PlugIR, LLaVA-ReID, WhittleSearch) but is the first to apply multi-turn interactive reasoning to the geolocation domain. The training pipeline repurposes GRPO-based visual reinforcement learning (VLM-R1, Visual-RFT) with a novel task-aligned reward (Positional Retrieval Gain), while the retriever builds on CLIP-style contrastive learning augmented with hierarchical similarity distribution matching and hard-negative isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of DlgPR as a task and DQ-cities as the first large-scale dialogue-based place recognition benchmark (106k images, 30k multi-turn dialogues) provides a concrete foundation for future research on interactive geo-localization. The finding that a 7B model with task-specific SFT+GRPO training outperforms a 72B model by 7.3% in R@1 suggests that targeted reasoning training can be more effective than raw model scale for structured interactive tasks. Key limitations include the reliance on GPT-4o-synthesized dialogues (no real human interaction), evaluation on only five cities, and the absence of real-time deployment; open questions include co-training the retriever and questioner jointly, adaptive dialogue policies, and whether the paradigm generalizes to 3D or indoor settings.
