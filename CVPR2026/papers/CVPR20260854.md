## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for isotropic reconstruction of anisotropic volume EM data rely on the assumption that local tissue structures are approximately isotropic across x, y, and z dimensions, using lateral (xy) priors to extrapolate along the axial direction. This assumption breaks down for morphologically anisotropic structures such as elongated neuronal fibers, leading to inaccurate segmentation and analysis. The paper addresses the need for a framework that directly reasons about continuous 3D geometry without relying on isotropy-based 2D image priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sparse sequence of anisotropic 2D xy-slices (typically 10–20% of the full z-axis, with anisotropy ratios of 6× or 10×) and a continuous normalized timestamp t ∈ [0,1] specifying the desired interpolation depth. It outputs rendered isotropic xy-slices at that arbitrary continuous z-position, enabling free interpolation at any sub-slice coordinate (e.g., t = 0.53) rather than being restricted to discrete intermediate positions. The entire pipeline operates on a 500×500×50 sub-volume and requires no external isotropic reference data or paired HR-LR training sets.

## Method

EMGauss reframes axial slice interpolation as a dynamic 2D Gaussian splatting rendering problem: a canonical set of 2D Gaussians is initialized from observed slices, and a lightweight MLP deformation network predicts per-Gaussian lateral offsets (Δx, Δy), in-plane scale changes (Δsx, Δsy), and opacity modulation (Δo) conditioned on the normalized z-coordinate t, while fixing z-position and z-scale to shared constants to prevent degenerate depth deformation. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) a 2k-iteration warm-up optimizing canonical Gaussians with the deformation MLP frozen, (2) a brief 1k-iteration joint training phase, and (3) a 15k-iteration phase where an EMA-based teacher (decay α=0.995) generates pseudo-labels on progressively more unseen timestamps, with the pseudo-loss weight ramped from 0.1 to 1.0. The photometric objective combines L1 and D-SSIM losses, and the teacher-student bootstrapping regularizes the model against overfitting to the sparse observed slices, promoting smooth geometry-aware interpolation across the full volume.

## Results

On the simulated anisotropic EPFL dataset (×6 subsampling), EMGauss achieves 26.59 PSNR, 0.6977 SSIM, and 0.943 FSIM, outperforming all baselines by 2.7–4.5 dB in PSNR. On FIB-25 (×6), it reaches 27.37 PSNR, 0.7275 SSIM, and 0.920 FSIM, with the largest margin over IsoVEM (+5.86 dB PSNR). For downstream video segmentation (SAM2 IoU on reconstructed EPFL slices), EMGauss scores 0.9687 versus 0.9555 for EMDiffuse and 0.9099 for CycleGAN-IR, confirming that its reconstructions better preserve structural continuity for 3D analysis.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMGauss outperforms all three baselines on every metric across both simulated datasets. Against CycleGAN-IR (GAN-based, unsupervised), the gains are the largest: +4.54 dB PSNR on EPFL and +4.98 dB on FIB-25, with SSIM improvements of ~0.20–0.17, reflecting CycleGAN-IR's tendency to hallucinate structures absent in the ground truth. Against EMDiffuse (diffusion-based, requires additional same-tissue isotropic sub-volumes for training), EMGauss leads by +3.25 dB (EPFL) and +3.27 dB (FIB-25) in PSNR while operating in a fully self-contained loop without auxiliary data. Against IsoVEM (transformer-based), EMGauss wins by +2.68 dB on EPFL and a substantial +5.86 dB on FIB-25, where IsoVEM's PSNR actually drops below CycleGAN-IR. In the downstream SAM2 segmentation task, EMGauss achieves the highest IoU (0.9687), edging out EMDiffuse (0.9555) by 1.3 points and CycleGAN-IR (0.9099) by 5.9 points, indicating that its reconstructions better preserve the structural continuity needed for 3D morphological analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMGauss repurposes the Deformable 3D Gaussians framework—originally designed for monocular dynamic scene reconstruction—by reinterpreting the z-axis of anisotropic EM volumes as a temporal dimension, so that slice-to-3D interpolation becomes a 2D dynamic Gaussian splatting problem. This departs fundamentally from prior vEM isotropic reconstruction methods (CycleGAN-IR, EMDiffuse, IsoVEM), which operate in a slice-wise 2D manner and implicitly assume spatial isotropy between lateral and axial planes. The teacher-student EMA bootstrapping for pseudo-labeling on unobserved slices is borrowed from self-supervised learning literature but applied here to regularize the deformation field under sparse axial supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the isotropy assumption, EMGauss addresses a core limitation that causes prior methods to fail on morphologically anisotropic structures (e.g., elongated neuronal fibers), and it does so without requiring external isotropic training data or large-scale pretraining. The ability to synthesize slices at arbitrary continuous z-positions (not just discrete intervals) is a practical advantage for downstream 3D reconstruction and segmentation. Key limitations include Gaussian primitive proliferation in noisy regions leading to memory overhead, and the method has only been validated on sub-volumes (500×500×500 or smaller); scalability to whole-brain volumes and robustness to substantial acquisition noise remain open questions.
