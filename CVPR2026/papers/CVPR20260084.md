## Problem

Rectified Flow Distillation Sampling (RFDS) extends score distillation to rectified flow models for text-to-image editing but suffers from severe over-smoothing, where background and high-frequency details are unintentionally destroyed because the gradient does not distinguish regions needing editing from those that should be preserved. Additionally, existing editing methods—score-based optimization (DDS), velocity-based optimization (RFDS), and ODE-based inversion-free editing (FlowEdit)—lack a unified theoretical framework connecting them.

## Input / Output

Given a source image x_0^src, a source prompt φ^src describing it, and a target prompt φ^tgt specifying the desired edit, DRFS directly optimizes the target latent x_0^tgt (initialized to x_0^src) in the latent space of a pre-trained rectified flow model (SD3, SD3.5, or Flux) without any inversion step or architectural modification. The output is a single edited image aligned with φ^tgt while preserving unchanged regions. Optimization uses a descending timestep schedule with a single Monte-Carlo sample per step (batch size 1), and CFG scales of 6 (source) and 16.5 (target).

## Method

DRFS defines an energy function that minimizes the difference between full residuals (model velocity minus data dynamics) for the target and source prompts, rather than subtracting raw conditional predictions as in RFDS; this cancels components shared by both dynamics and suppresses gradients in regions that should remain unchanged. A time-dependent shift term c_t(x_0^tgt − x_0^src) is added to the target latent during interpolation, pushing noisy latents closer to the target trajectory and reducing model-data mismatch early in optimization. The gradient is approximated by replacing the network Jacobian with the identity and directly optimizing x_0^tgt, yielding a closed-form update involving the difference of target and source velocities minus a drift term. Theoretically, setting c_t = 0 recovers Delta Denoising Score (DDS), while setting c_t = t under rectified flow parameterization recovers FlowEdit as a strict special case, providing a unifying view of score-based, velocity-based, and ODE-based editing. A descending timestep scheduler (high-noise to low-noise) is used for coarse-to-fine optimization, and the shift coefficient follows a gradual schedule c_t ≈ (1−t)t to avoid error amplification in early noisy steps.

## Results

On the PIE benchmark, DRFS (SD3) achieves the highest CLIP Edited similarity (23.83) among all SD3-based methods, surpassing FlowEdit (23.67) and iRFDS (21.67), while also improving background preservation (LPIPS 93.81 vs. 105.46 for FlowEdit; MSE 67.49 vs. 87.34). On SD3.5, DRFS attains the best structure distance (12.00×10³), LPIPS (55.83×10³), MSE (30.76×10⁴), and CLIP Edited (23.17), outperforming FlowEdit, FTEdit, and DNAEdit on nearly all metrics. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that the shift term c_t≈(1−t)t is critical: setting c_t=0 degrades CLIP Edited from 23.83 to 22.53, while c_t=t (FlowEdit's implicit choice) worsens background preservation (LPIPS 143.06 vs. 93.81).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DRFS (SD3) outperforms FlowEdit (SD3) on every reported metric: CLIP Edited 23.83 vs. 23.67, LPIPS 93.81 vs. 105.46, MSE 67.49 vs. 87.34, and SSIM 84.85 vs. 83.48, confirming that the delta-residual formulation and shift term reduce over-smoothing while improving semantic alignment. Against iRFDS (the only other distillation-based RF baseline), DRFS yields dramatically better background preservation (LPIPS 93.81 vs. 186.39; MSE 67.49 vs. 179.76) and higher CLIP Edited (23.83 vs. 21.67), validating that the source-target residual subtraction eliminates the non-zero gradients in unchanged regions that plague single-prompt RFDS. On SD3.5, DRFS beats FlowEdit on all seven metrics (e.g., Structure Distance 12.00 vs. 12.73, CLIP Edited 23.17 vs. 23.00) and surpasses FTEdit on structure distance (12.00 vs. 18.17), LPIPS (55.83 vs. 80.55), and CLIP Edited (23.17 vs. 22.27), though FTEdit retains a slight SSIM advantage (91.50 vs. 89.41). DRFS (SD3.5) also outperforms DNAEdit on structure distance (12.00 vs. 14.19), LPIPS (55.83 vs. 74.57), and CLIP Edited (23.17 vs. 22.71). Among Flux-based baselines, DNAEdit achieves the best structure distance (18.87×10³) and LPIPS (95.06×10³), but DRFS on SD3.5 still matches or exceeds these numbers, suggesting that the choice of backbone (SD3.5 vs. Flux) contributes comparably to the methodological gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

DRFS extends the delta formulation of Delta Denoising Score (DDS) from diffusion noise residuals to rectified-flow velocity residuals, and modifies RFDS by replacing its single-residual energy with a source-target residual difference that cancels shared dynamics. The key conceptual departure is the introduction of a time-dependent shift coefficient c_t that corrects the model-data mismatch when evaluating target velocities off the forward posterior, yielding a path-aware energy function. The paper's central theoretical contribution is proving that c_t=0 recovers DDS and c_t=t recovers FlowEdit's ODE trajectory, thereby unifying score-based distillation optimization and inversion-free ODE editing under a single energy-minimization framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unification of optimization-based (DDS/RFDS) and ODE-based (FlowEdit) editing under one energy function provides a principled design space for future editing methods, where the shift schedule c_t becomes a tunable knob controlling editing strength, path straightness, and error propagation. Practically, DRFS is inversion-free, training-free, and requires no architectural modifications, making it a drop-in improvement for SD3/SD3.5-based editing pipelines. Open questions include whether the identity approximation for the network Jacobian can be tightened, whether the gradual c_t ≈ (1-t)t schedule is truly optimal or merely a heuristic, and whether the framework extends naturally to video or 3D editing where trajectory mismatch may be more severe.
