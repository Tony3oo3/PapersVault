## Problem

Subquadratic attention alternatives like GSPN propagate context on 2D grids with linear complexity, but the original design hits GPU concurrency limits—growing batch size or channels serializes scans and spikes latency—preventing scaling to foundation-model sizes. No subquadratic operator has been pretrained at CLIP scale (tens of billions of image-text pairs), and cross-operator transfer from attention teachers to propagation students is non-trivial due to fundamentally different computation patterns (pairwise mixing vs. sequential local propagation).

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB images at resolutions from 378×378 up to 1036×1036 (and 2058×2058 for latency benchmarks) and produces dense feature representations usable for zero-shot classification, pixel-level segmentation, and object detection. A key property is that C-GSPN requires no positional embeddings, so resolution changes do not alter the architecture—only the training procedure. Training uses 600M image-text pairs with a SigLIP-style contrastive objective, and high-resolution transfer uses a 3M-sample curriculum (1M per stage) with upsampling self-distillation rather than full-scale retraining.

## Method

C-GSPN replaces the attention sublayer in a ViT block with 2D spatial propagation performed in a compressed latent space (C→C_c via 1×1 convolutions), which avoids GPU SM concurrency saturation and yields ~10× propagation latency reduction; a fused CUDA kernel further accelerates the row-stochastic normalization (sigmoid + local reduction + clamp + division) by 2.15×. Training uses a two-stage cross-operator distillation from a pretrained SigLIP/OpenCLIP teacher: Stage 1 independently aligns each propagation sublayer with its paired attention sublayer (no cross-block backprop), and Stage 2 fine-tunes end-to-end with dual supervision taps (post-propagation and post-block) using MSE + KL losses plus lightweight feature adaptors to bridge the operator gap. For high-resolution transfer, a curriculum (378→518→756→1036) with bilinearly upsampled self-distillation from the previous-resolution checkpoint enables dense-task gains with only 1/200 of the data needed for from-scratch training. A hybrid variant retaining 3/27 layers as standard attention achieves the best accuracy-latency frontier, preserving ~3.9× speedup at 2K over pure attention while recovering most accuracy lost from removing non-propagation overhead.

## Results

C-GSPN (365M params) achieves 81.3% ImageNet Top-1 and 46.0% ADE20K mIoU, matching or exceeding the ViT-Distill baseline (82.2% Top-1, 45.5% ADE20K) with 15% fewer parameters, and surpassing the original GSPN (80.5% Top-1, 45.3% ADE20K) on all dense-prediction metrics. In terms of efficiency, C-GSPN delivers a 4.28× end-to-end block speedup over FlashAttention at 2K resolution (147.52 ms vs. 631.63 ms) and a 1097× sublayer speedup over standard attention at 1K. At 1K resolution, C-GSPN achieves 28.28 img/s throughput versus 16.89 for FlashAttention and 4.83 for standard attention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the original GSPN [32], C-GSPN wins on both efficiency and accuracy: it is 55.3× faster at the sublayer level (0.18 ms vs. 9.95 ms at 1K) and 3.64× faster at the block level (36.60 ms vs. 133.12 ms), while also improving ADE20K segmentation by +0.7% (46.0 vs. 45.3) and Top-1 by +0.8% (81.3 vs. 80.5) with 23% fewer parameters (365M vs. 477M). Against FlashAttention [11], C-GSPN achieves a 182× sublayer speedup at 1K (0.18 ms vs. 32.81 ms) and a 4.28× block-level speedup at 2K (147.52 ms vs. 631.63 ms), with 1.67× and 3.82× higher throughput at 1K and 2K respectively. On task accuracy, C-GSPN slightly trails the isomorphic ViT-Distill baseline by 0.9% on Top-1 (81.3 vs. 82.2) but exceeds it on ADE20K (+0.5%) and ADE20K-F (+1.5%), and even surpasses the OpenCLIP teacher on ADE20K (+0.2%). The hybrid variant (3/27 attention layers) recovers most of the Top-1 gap while remaining ≈3.9× faster than pure attention at 2K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Generalized Spatial Propagation Networks (GSPN) [32], which itself generalizes earlier spatial propagation networks [22] to four-direction linear-complexity propagation on 2D grids. It departs from the broader subquadratic-attention literature (linear attention, SSMs like Mamba, sparse/windowed attention) by preserving native 2D spatial structure rather than serializing images into 1D sequences, and by eliminating positional embeddings entirely. The key methodological departure from prior GSPN is the latent-space compression that resolves GPU concurrency saturation, combined with a two-stage cross-operator distillation recipe that adapts ideas from transformer-to-SSM distillation [4] and hybrid Mamba-Transformer designs [18] to the foundation-scale vision setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that a subquadratic spatial-propagation operator can be pretrained at CLIP scale (600M pairs) while maintaining competitive zero-shot accuracy and even exceeding the attention teacher on segmentation, opening a practical path to high-resolution encoders without tiling or quadratic cost. The cross-operator distillation recipe (sublayer-wise alignment followed by dual-tap end-to-end fine-tuning with feature adaptors) is a reusable technique for transferring knowledge between fundamentally different operator families. A significant open limitation is that the feed-forward MLP now accounts for 52% of block latency at high resolution, capping end-to-end speedups; whether the approach scales to much larger models (e.g., 1B+ parameters) or benefits from analogous MLP compression remains unexplored.
