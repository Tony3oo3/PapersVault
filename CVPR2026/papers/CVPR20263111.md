## Problem

Existing articulated object reconstruction methods (REArtGS, ArtGS) rely on pre-determined joint type priors (rotation or translation), causing significant errors on screw-joint and multi-part objects. Additionally, with only two-state supervision, these methods lack a mechanism to impose temporal geometric constraints on unseen intermediate states, degrading both surface quality and joint parameter accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263111/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 60–100 posed RGB images per state at two arbitrary configurations of an unseen articulated object (no depth or joint-type supervision required) and produces part-level dynamic surface meshes extractable at any continuous state t∈[0,1] via TSDF fusion, along with per-joint parameters (rotation angle, axis, pivot, translation) and per-Gaussian part segmentation probabilities. The framework also extends naturally to multi-state inputs {I₀, I_{t₁}, …, I₁} by expanding the time set.

## Method

REArtGS++ models each joint as a decoupled SE(3) screw motion (quaternion q(θ,a), pivot o, translation t) without requiring joint-type classification, and derives per-Gaussian motion by blending part motions weighted by learned segmentation probabilities (Mahalanobis distance to learnable part centers plus an MLP residual). Gaussians are flattened into 2D planes (following PGSR) to yield accurate normals and unbiased depth, enabling a novel temporal geometric constraint: a Taylor first-order expansion of the normal map over t∈[0,1] regularizes both the normal and its time derivative against depth-derived normals, bridging the gap between the two observed states. A local consistent voting mechanism (KNN boundary detection + k-means regional clustering + KL-divergence loss) resolves ambiguous segmentation at part boundaries. The full pipeline is optimized end-to-end with a combined loss of rendering, scale-flattening, center alignment, temporal geometry, and voting terms, initialized via a fast two-state 3DGS pass followed by K-means clustering of dynamic Gaussians.

## Results

On the PARIS dataset (10 synthetic + 2 real objects), REArtGS++ achieves mean Axis Ang of 0.01° and Part Motion of 0.01° on synthetic objects, and dramatically reduces real-object CD-m from 89.33 (REArtGS) to 7.80. On the ArtGS-Multi dataset with multi-part and screw-joint objects, REArtGS++ achieves mean Axis Ang of 0.55° and Axis Pos of 0.50 mm for multi-part objects, and 0.07° / 2.54 mm for screw-joint objects, representing a 27.6% CD-w error reduction over REArtGS on synthetic objects. The method also achieves the best CD-w of 3.25 (synthetic) and 6.08 (real) on PARIS, outperforming all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REArtGS++ outperforms all four baselines on nearly every metric across both datasets. Against REArtGS [25] (the direct predecessor), the gains are most dramatic on real-world objects: CD-m drops from 89.33 to 7.80 and Part Motion from 19.45 to 1.15, while Axis Ang on real objects improves from 44.85° to 2.63°. Against ArtGS [13], REArtGS++ achieves a 27.6% reduction in CD-w on synthetic PARIS objects (3.25 vs 4.78) and dominates on multi-part/screw-joint objects in Table 2 (e.g., Axis Ang 0.55° vs 25.08° for multi-part, 0.07° vs 64.93° for screw). Against PARIS [12] and DTA [23], the improvements are orders of magnitude on joint parameter estimation (Axis Ang 0.01° vs 10.34° and 0.13°) and competitive or superior on surface reconstruction metrics. The only marginal losses are individual per-object CD-m values (e.g., FoldChair 0.77 vs ArtGS 0.52), but REArtGS++ wins decisively on all mean aggregates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the authors' own REArtGS by replacing its rotation/translation-only joint model with a decoupled SE(3) screw motion parameterization that requires no joint-type prior, and by substituting REArtGS's static SDF geometric constraint with a time-continuous regularization derived from Taylor first-order expansion of planar Gaussian normals. The planar Gaussian representation is borrowed from PGSR to obtain accurate normals and unbiased depth, while the part-center parameterization (Mahalanobis distance with learnable O, V, Λ) follows REACTO, and the two-state initialization pipeline (fast 3DGS optimization followed by dynamic Gaussian identification via Chamfer Distance) is inherited from ArtGS. The key conceptual departure from the broader 3DGS-for-articulated-objects line (ArtGS, REArtGS, GaussianArt) is the elimination of joint-type priors and the introduction of a temporal constraint that regularizes the entire interpolation interval rather than just the two observed states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to handle screw joints and multi-part objects without pre-determined joint types removes a practical bottleneck for deploying articulated-object reconstruction in robotics and embodied intelligence, where real objects frequently exhibit helical or compound motions. The temporal geometric constraint via Taylor expansion is a lightweight, unsupervised mechanism that could be adapted to other two-state or few-state dynamic reconstruction settings beyond articulated objects. However, the method still requires camera pose alignment between the two states and struggles with transparent surfaces, limiting its applicability to a subset of real-world scenarios. The real-world evaluation is limited to only 2–5 objects, leaving open questions about robustness to occlusion, textureless surfaces, and highly complex multi-joint assemblies.
