## Problem

Existing few-step autoregressive video diffusion models use static initial frames as attention sink tokens to prevent error accumulation, but this causes over-attention on the starting frame, producing copied initial frames and severely diminished motion dynamics. Additionally, vanilla distribution matching distillation treats all training samples equally, making it unable to prioritize dynamic content during knowledge transfer from teacher to student models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261919/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces streaming video at 832×480 resolution, generating 5-second clips (or indefinitely long sequences) at 23.1 FPS on a single H100 GPU. Generation proceeds chunk-wise with 3 latent frames per chunk and an attention window of 9, using a KV cache mechanism. The system also supports interactive prompt modification mid-generation by clearing and recomputing the cross-attention cache with a new prompt while maintaining temporal consistency.

## Method

Reward Forcing introduces two key mechanisms. First, EMA-Sink replaces static initial-frame sink tokens with fixed-size compressed states that are continuously updated via exponential moving average (α=0.99) as tokens exit the sliding window, preserving both long-term global context and recent dynamics without additional computational cost. Second, Rewarded Distribution Matching Distillation (Re-DMD) reweights the DMD gradient using motion-quality scores from a vision-language model (VideoAlign) as a reward function, biasing the student's output distribution toward high-dynamics regions while maintaining data fidelity. The reward acts as a static weighting factor (β=1/2), avoiding backpropagation through the reward model and the instability of noisy reward gradients. Training follows Self Forcing's autoregressive self-rollout paradigm on 16k ODE solution pairs from Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B, completing in ~3 hours on 64 H200 GPUs.

## Results

On the 5-second VBench benchmark, Reward Forcing achieves a total score of 84.13 (Quality 84.84, Semantic 81.32) at 23.1 FPS on a single H100 GPU, surpassing all autoregressive baselines and matching the bidirectional Wan-2.1 diffusion model (84.26) while being 29.6× faster. On the 60-second VBench Long benchmark, it reaches a total score of 81.41 with a dynamic degree of 66.95 (an 88.38% improvement over LongLive's 35.54) and the lowest quality drift (2.505). The method also achieves the best Qwen3-VL-235B scores at 55–60 seconds across visual quality (4.82), motion dynamics (4.18), and text alignment (4.04).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among autoregressive methods, Reward Forcing outperforms Self Forcing (83.80) by 0.33 VBench total points while running 36% faster (23.1 vs. 17.0 FPS), and beats LongLive (83.22) by 0.91 points at 11% higher speed. On the long-video benchmark, the dynamic degree score jumps from 35.54 (LongLive) and 54.94 (Self Forcing) to 66.95, an 88.38% improvement over the closest competitor, while maintaining the lowest drift (2.505 vs. 2.531 for LongLive and 5.075 for Self Forcing). Against the bidirectional Wan-2.1 diffusion model, Reward Forcing achieves a near-identical total score (84.13 vs. 84.26) at 29.6× the inference speed. Rolling Forcing, despite similar speed (17.5 FPS), trails significantly on semantic alignment (69.78 vs. 81.32), and CausVid lags by 1.25 total points. The Qwen3-VL evaluation at 55–60 seconds confirms the advantage: Reward Forcing leads on all three axes (visual 4.82, dynamic 4.18, text 4.04) versus LongLive (4.79, 3.81, 3.98) and Self Forcing (3.89, 3.44, 3.11).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Self Forcing / CausVid line of work on distilling bidirectional video diffusion models into few-step autoregressive generators, and directly modifies LongLive's static attention-sink mechanism by replacing fixed initial-frame tokens with EMA-Sink, a dynamically updated compressed state that fuses evicted tokens via exponential moving average. The second contribution, Re-DMD, departs from vanilla distribution matching distillation (DMD) by incorporating reward-weighted regression principles from RL, reweighting the DMD gradient with a vision-language model's motion-quality score to bias the student toward high-dynamics regions. Conceptually, the paper sits at the intersection of efficient autoregressive video generation and reward-guided distillation, combining a state-management trick (EMA-Sink) with a loss-function modification (Re-DMD) within a single training pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 23.1 FPS real-time streaming generation on a single H100, combined with an 88% boost in dynamic amplitude over the closest baseline, makes interactive and long-horizon video generation practically viable for applications like world simulation and real-time content creation. The EMA-Sink mechanism is lightweight (O(1) eviction, no extra FLOPs) and could be adopted by other autoregressive video or language models that suffer from information bottlenecks in sliding-window attention. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher resolutions, longer horizons beyond one minute, and different base architectures, and whether the reward-weighted distillation can be extended to other quality dimensions (aesthetic, text alignment) without the trade-offs observed with the β parameter.
