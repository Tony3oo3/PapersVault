## Problem

Existing computational pathology MLLMs compress an entire gigapixel Whole Slide Image into a single global embedding aligned with a pathology report, which discards fine-grained spatial semantics and ignores the multi-scale diagnostic workflow that pathologists actually follow. This global-only representation hinders fine-grained grounding of textual descriptions to localized visual evidence and limits interpretable, evidence-based reasoning across the cell-to-WSI hierarchy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260018/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 20×-magnification WSI hierarchically decomposed into 4096×4096 regions, 256×256 patches, and individually segmented cells, alongside a free-form text instruction or query. A Semantic Patch Filtering step prunes redundant homogeneous patches using cosine similarity against pathology-report keywords, and a Cell-Cell Attention Fusion module collapses potentially >100K cell embeddings per WSI into one compact 784-d token per patch. Outputs span six task types: VQA answers, full pathology reports, short captions, zero-shot/linear-probe classification labels, WSI retrieval rankings, and cross-modal (report↔slide) retrieval rankings.

## Method

MLLM-HWSI employs a four-level hierarchical encoder (CellViT for cells, CONCH for patches, HIPT ViT_p/ViT_r for regions, and a pre-trained ViT_WSI for the global slide) whose outputs are projected into a shared multimodal space by four scale-specific VL projectors and fused with tokenized text before feeding a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct LLM. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) joint fine-tuning of all encoders and the text encoder under a combined hierarchical contrastive alignment loss (scale-specific InfoNCE at cell/patch/region plus a WSI-level variant) and a cross-scale consistency loss that penalizes L2 divergence between adjacent hierarchy levels; (2) training only the two-layer VL projectors to bridge frozen encoder features into the LLM embedding space; (3) LoRA-based (rank 128, α=256) instruction tuning on 175K WSI-level VQA pairs with DeepSpeed ZeRO-3. The key architectural insight is that each spatial scale receives its own projector and contrastive objective, while the cross-scale consistency term explicitly enforces that cell-level semantics aggregate coherently into patch, region, and WSI representations—mirroring the bottom-up evidence integration pathologists perform.

## Results

MLLM-HWSI achieves state-of-the-art results across all six evaluated CPath tasks. On zero-shot WSI classification, it reaches an average Balanced Accuracy of 71.86%, surpassing TITAN (64.56%) by 7.30% and WSI-LLaVA (61.01%) by 10.85%. On VQA, it attains 89.60% average accuracy on SlideBench-VQA (TCGA), 97.90% on WSI-Bench, 68.70% on SlideBench-VQA (BCNB), and 69.20% on WSI-VQA—outperforming all prior CPath MLLMs and general-purpose LMMs including GPT-4V. For report generation, it achieves BLEU-1 of 0.556/0.667 and METEOR of 0.513/0.706 on WSI-Bench/HistGen, and for cross-modal retrieval it reaches an average Recall of 0.920, exceeding WSI-LLaVA by 6.10%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLLM-HWSI outperforms all 30 compared baselines across every evaluated task. Against the strongest CPath MLLMs, it surpasses WSI-LLaVA by 7.10% on SlideBench-VQA (TCGA) (0.896 vs 0.825), 13.40% on SlideBench-VQA (BCNB) (0.687 vs 0.553), and 14.60% on WSI-VQA (0.692 vs 0.546). It also beats TITAN by 9.00% on SlideBench-VQA (TCGA) and 10.60% on WSI-VQA, and SlideChat by 8.00% on SlideBench-VQA (TCGA) and 9.10% on WSI-VQA. Among general-purpose MLLMs, the gap is even larger: GPT-4V trails by 30.90% on SlideBench-VQA (TCGA) and 38.80% on WSI-VQA, while GeminiProV and Qwen-VL-MAX lag by over 40% on WSI-VQA. In zero-shot classification, MLLM-HWSI leads TITAN by 7.30% BA and CONCH by a wider margin; in WSI retrieval it exceeds TITAN by 5.56% and CONCH by 11.88% top-1% accuracy. For report and caption generation, it surpasses WSI-LLaVA by 7.60% BLEU-1 and 5.10% METEOR on WSI-Bench, and by 15.60% METEOR on SlideBench-Caption (0.627 vs 0.551). No baseline matches or exceeds MLLM-HWSI on any reported metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hierarchical ViT lineage (HIPT) and patch-level VLMs (CONCH, CellViT) into a unified multi-scale MLLM framework, departing from prior WSI-level MLLMs (SlideChat, WSI-LLaVA, TITAN) that compress entire slides into a single global embedding. The key conceptual departure is the explicit four-level decomposition (cell→patch→region→WSI) with scale-specific projectors and a cross-scale consistency loss, rather than the single-vector alignment used by existing CPath MLLMs. The LLM backbone (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) and training recipe (LoRA, DeepSpeed ZeRO-3) follow standard MLLM practice, so the novelty is concentrated in the visual encoder hierarchy and the joint contrastive/consistency objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By grounding pathology language at multiple spatial granularities, MLLM-HWSI addresses a genuine interpretability gap: prior models cannot link a phrase like 'pleomorphic nuclei' to the specific cells that evidence it. The consistent gains across 13 benchmarks and 6 tasks suggest the hierarchical formulation is broadly beneficial rather than task-specific. Open questions include the computational cost of processing ~91M patches and 100K+ cells per WSI at inference time, whether the cross-scale consistency loss generalizes to non-hierarchical modalities (e.g., radiology), and whether the four-level decomposition is optimal or whether intermediate scales (e.g., super-patch, sub-region) would further improve grounding. The authors note future extension toward multi-modal medical integration (radiology, genomics, clinical records).
