## Problem

Image-to-video (I2V) models fine-tuned from text-to-video (T2V) architectures consistently produce significantly more static videos than their T2V counterparts, with an 18.6% drop in dynamic degree on VBench. The paper identifies the root cause as premature exposure to high-frequency details in the conditioning image, which biases the denoising trajectory into a 'shortcut' that locks onto the reference image's fine appearance and prevents large-scale motion from developing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260380/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on existing I2V models (Wan 2.1, Wan 2.2, LTX-Video) and takes a reference RGB image and a text prompt as input, producing a temporally coherent video. The reference image is first encoded into latent space via a 3D VAE, and the low-pass filtering is applied to this latent representation (bilinear downsample-then-upsample by a factor of 2.5 by default). The method is entirely training-free, modifying only the sampling procedure at inference time with no architectural changes or additional parameters.

## Method

Adaptive Low-Pass Guidance (ALG) is a training-free inference technique that adaptively modulates the frequency content of the conditioning image latent across denoising timesteps. At early timesteps (t < t_trans, default 0.1), a strong low-pass filter (e.g., bilinear downsampling by κ* = 2.5) is applied to the conditioning image, removing high-frequency components that trigger the shortcut effect; at later timesteps, the original unfiltered image is restored to recover fine details. A key design choice is that the filtered image is used only in the conditional terms of classifier-free guidance (Eq. 3), while the unconditional term retains the original image, which algebraically decomposes into a motion-enhanced CFG component plus a fidelity compensation term. This prevents the sampling trajectory from converging to a static shortcut solution early on while still allowing the model to reconstruct high-frequency details in later stages, achieving a 33% average improvement in dynamic degree across models without sacrificing video quality or input image fidelity.

## Results

On VBench, ALG improves Dynamic Degree by 23% on Wan 2.2 (31.7→39.0), 36% on Wan 2.1 (28.9→39.4), and 39% on LTX-Video (15.5→21.5), yielding a 33% average improvement across models. Quality metrics remain stable or slightly improve: VBench-QS changes by less than 1 point, I2V Subject Consistency is preserved (98.5→98.5 on Wan 2.2), and DOVER/VisionReward scores are essentially unchanged. The gains are consistent across three evaluation datasets (VBench, PVD, VidProM), with Dynamic Degree improving by 4–12 points on each while VBench-Avg. increases by 0.4–0.9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the default classifier-free guidance (CFG) inference of each I2V model. On Wan 2.2, ALG raises Dynamic Degree from 31.7 to 39.0 (+23%) while VBench-Avg. improves from 79.6 to 80.5 and I2V Subject Consistency is unchanged at 98.5. On Wan 2.1, the gain is even larger: Dynamic Degree jumps from 28.9 to 39.4 (+36%), and VBench-Avg. rises from 79.1 to 80.0, though VBench-QS drops slightly (85.3→84.5). On LTX-Video, ALG improves Dynamic Degree from 15.5 to 21.5 (+39%) with VBench-Avg. increasing from 77.8 to 78.2. In all three model comparisons, ALG wins decisively on motion dynamics while quality metrics (DOVER, VisionReward, I2V consistency) are either preserved or marginally improved, confirming that the motion gain does not come at the cost of visual fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-free guidance (CFG) framework for I2V models by adaptively modulating the frequency content of the conditioning image during sampling. It builds directly on the I2V CFG formulation (Eq. 2) and positions itself alongside prior remedies for motion suppression—Zhao et al.'s motion-specific module and earlier-timestep initialization, Tian et al.'s model merging, and Ge et al.'s Fourier-guided latent shifting—but departs from all of them by being entirely training-free and operating purely at inference time. The key conceptual departure is the diagnosis of a 'shortcut effect' where high-frequency details in the reference image prematurely lock the denoising trajectory, which the authors verify via PCA feature-map visualization and address with a timestep-adaptive low-pass filter rather than architectural or training modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ALG offers a drop-in, zero-training fix that recovers a substantial fraction (33% on VBench) of the motion gap between T2V and I2V models across three architectures, making it immediately deployable for practitioners using open-source I2V pipelines. The finding that only the first ~10% of denoising steps require low-pass filtering to unlock dynamic motion, while later steps must see the original image to preserve fidelity, provides a clean mechanistic understanding of where in the generation process motion is determined. Open questions include whether the shortcut effect and its remedy generalize to other conditioning modalities (e.g., multi-frame or video conditioning), whether the optimal transition point and filter strength are model-agnostic, and whether the technique composes well with other motion-enhancement strategies beyond prompt augmentation.
