## Problem

In federated multi-label learning, client-specific label spaces and heterogeneous co-occurrence patterns cause locally learned label correlations to deviate from the global structure—a phenomenon the authors term label correlation drift. Existing aggregation methods weight clients solely by data size, allowing clients with large but poorly correlated datasets to dominate the global model, and no prior work systematically addresses how to harmonize these divergent correlation structures across clients.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261316/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client holds a private set of RGB images with multi-label annotations (e.g., 1,628 fine-grained labels on FLAIR, 80 object categories on MS-COCO, 20 categories on VOC). The model uses a ViT-B/16 backbone with a C-way sigmoid head, producing a C-dimensional soft prediction vector per image. In the federated protocol, clients exchange only model parameters and correlation statistics (never raw data), and the server aggregates weighted parameter updates over T=50 communication rounds with 5 local epochs per round.

## Method

FedHarmony introduces a consensus correlation computed as a leave-one-out aggregation of all other clients' phi-style label correlation matrices, which serves as a global teacher to correct each client's biased local correlation estimates via an alignment loss. To make this efficient, the paper applies spectral clustering on the expert correlation to partition labels into G clusters and restricts alignment to within-cluster pairs, with two theorems proving this block-restricted objective converges at a strictly faster linear rate while incurring at most O(γ_out²‖E‖²_F) additional loss. At aggregation, a dynamic weighting scheme blends data-size proportionality (early rounds) with correlation-quality scores (later rounds) via a linearly decaying schedule, so that clients with well-aligned structures increasingly dominate the global model. The full pipeline—consensus-guided local correction, block-wise clustering, and quality-aware aggregation—is trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy plus the alignment loss, and an accelerated optimization algorithm further speeds convergence without sacrificing accuracy.

## Results

FedHarmony achieves the best performance on all eight metrics across all three federated multi-label benchmarks. On FLAIR, it reaches 51.0% mAP and 75.1% OF1, surpassing the strongest baseline (FedProx, 39.6% mAP) by over 11 points. On COCO-80, it attains 71.4% mAP and 72.7% OF1, outperforming FedLGT (64.5% mAP) by ~7 points. On VOC2007, it achieves 86.9% mAP and 83.4% OF1, exceeding FedRDN (78.3% mAP) by ~9 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedHarmony dominates all seven baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the standard aggregation baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova), the gains are most dramatic on FLAIR: FedHarmony's 51.0% mAP exceeds FedProx (39.6%) by 11.4 points and FedAvg (35.4%) by 15.6 points, while FedNova collapses to 30.3% mAP. Among geometry-aware methods, FedCurv and SphereFed show only marginal improvements over FedAvg (≤1 mAP), confirming that curvature correction alone cannot address label correlation drift. The task-specific baselines FedLGT (64.5% mAP on COCO-80) and FedRDN (78.3% mAP on VOC2007) are the closest competitors, yet FedHarmony still leads by 6.9 and 8.6 mAP respectively. Notably, FedNova fails catastrophically on COCO-80 (4.3% mAP) and VOC2007 (18.1% mAP), suggesting its objective-inconsistency correction is incompatible with multi-label sigmoid heads under severe non-IID conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedHarmony sits at the intersection of federated learning aggregation strategies and multi-label correlation modeling. It departs from standard parameter-averaging methods (FedAvg, FedProx, FedNova) and geometry-aware variants (FedCurv, SphereFed) by elevating the label correlation matrix to a first-class object that is explicitly harmonized across clients, rather than being an implicit byproduct of parameter averaging. The consensus-correlation-as-teacher mechanism draws conceptually on ensemble distillation for model fusion [25], but applies it to the structural statistics of label co-occurrence rather than to output logits. The block-wise spectral clustering for efficient alignment connects to sparse local embedding ideas in extreme multi-label classification [2] and clustering-based multi-label methods [31], while the dynamic quality-weighted aggregation extends the quantity-only weighting of FedAvg/FedProx with a structural-quality signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formalizes 'label correlation drift' as a distinct failure mode in federated multi-label learning, providing a principled mechanism (consensus-guided correction) to address it. The >10 mAP gap on FLAIR and the flat robustness curves under severe label skew (γ from 1.0 to 0.25) suggest that correlation-structure alignment is a more stable inductive bias than parameter averaging under extreme heterogeneity. Open questions include: the communication overhead of uploading full C×C correlation matrices per round (potentially prohibitive for FLAIR's 1,628 fine labels), whether the consensus mechanism degrades when a large fraction of clients share a biased sub-distribution, and how the approach extends to dynamic or open-set label spaces where the label vocabulary itself changes across rounds.
