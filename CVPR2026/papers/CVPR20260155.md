## Problem

Despite attention's proven universal approximation capability, removing FFN layers from Transformers and stacking attention-only blocks causes a marked accuracy drop — a gap the authors term the weak-independence challenge. The paper asks why attention's theoretical expressivity is hard to realize in isolation, identifying two structural tensions (a granularity-reliability trade-off and a row-column budgeting dilemma) that cap attention's stand-alone representational ceiling.

## Input / Output

CSaN is a drop-in wrapper around standard attention blocks that consumes the same RGB images (224×224 for ImageNet/CIFAR-100/ImageNet-LT classification; standard multi-scale inputs for COCO detection) and produces the same outputs (class labels or bounding boxes) as the underlying backbone. It operates per-window or globally depending on the attention paradigm (shifted-window for Swin, global for ViT, local-then-global for Hiera), adding only ~5% overhead in parameters and FLOPs without altering the Q/K/V projections or residual topology.

## Method

Guided by the Selection-as-Nonlinearity (SaN) lens — which frames each attention row as a unit-mass cooperative allocation game over shared values — CSaN introduces two compensation mechanisms. First, hierarchical budget calibration re-weights row distributions using a context-derived descriptor to produce a row temperature (inter-query scaling) and a column magnifier (column-side congestion relief), turning single-stage allocation into a two-stage hierarchical scheme while preserving the softmax normalizer. Second, public-private cooperation augments the standard cross-token attention pathway (public) with a per-token private value pathway that grants each row an exclusive descent direction, bypassing the row-simplex and column-sharing constraints that cause first-order gradient incompatibility. The method is validated across Swin, ViT, and Hiera families, enabling lighter models to rival same-family counterparts roughly 2× larger.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, CSaN delivers consistent Top-1 gains across three Transformer families with only ~5% overhead in parameters and FLOPs. The headline result is that CSaN enables lighter variants to rival or surpass same-family counterparts roughly 2× larger: Swin-Small+CSaN reaches 83.5% (matching Swin-Base at 83.5%), ViT-Base-Slim/16+CSaN reaches 82.1% (surpassing ViT-Base/16 at 81.8%), and Hiera-Tiny-Plus+CSaN reaches 82.4% (matching Hiera-Base at 82.4%). On MS COCO object detection, Swin-Tiny+CSaN with RetinaNet improves mAP from 37.3% to 38.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Swin Transformer baseline [Liu et al., ICCV 2021], CSaN wins at every tested scale: +2.8 pp on Swin-Min (75.0 vs 72.2), +1.4 pp on Swin-Tiny (82.7 vs 81.3), and +0.5 pp on Swin-Small (83.5 vs 83.0), with the latter now matching the much larger Swin-Base (83.5%) at roughly half the parameters. Against the ViT/DeiT baseline [Touvron et al., ICML 2021], CSaN achieves the most dramatic level-jump: ViT-Base-Slim/16+CSaN (82.1%) surpasses the full ViT-Base/16 (81.8%) while using only ~47% of its parameters, and ViT-Small/16+CSaN (81.3%) nearly closes the gap to ViT-Base. Against the Hiera baseline [Ryali et al., ICML 2023], CSaN lifts Hiera-Tiny-Plus from 81.5% to 82.4%, exactly matching Hiera-Base (82.4%) at ~55% of its FLOPs, and improves Hiera-Small by +1.2 pp (82.5 vs 81.3). On COCO detection, Swin-Tiny+CSaN with RetinaNet improves mAP by +0.7 pp (38.0 vs 37.3) and AP_L by +1.6 pp (51.3 vs 49.7), confirming transferability beyond classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new conceptual ground by reframing self-attention through a joint game-decision lens (Selection-as-Nonlinearity), connecting it to the author's prior line of work on self-gated activation [2,3,5] and to universal approximation results for Transformers [7,20,52,53], but asking a different question: why attention's theoretical expressivity fails to materialize in practice when FFNs are removed. It positions itself as complementary to MetaFormer [50,51] by zooming into the operator level rather than the macro scaffold, and as orthogonal to efficiency-focused attention variants (windowed, sparse, kernel-based) by targeting structural rather than computational bottlenecks. The practical remedy, CSaN, draws on ideas from GLU-style gating [13,39,40] and sparse attention [33,36] but unifies them under the budget-constrained selection interpretation to derive hierarchical budget calibration and a public-private readout.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The weak-independence finding—attention-only stacks drop ~10 points on ImageNet—combined with the first-order incompatibility theorem (Thm. 3.8) gives a mechanistic, testable account of why FFNs are practically indispensable, which could inform future architecture design beyond simply interleaving attention and FFNs. CSaN's ~5% overhead enabling smaller models to rival ~2× larger same-family counterparts is practically attractive for deployment-constrained settings, and the drop-in nature makes it easy to adopt. Open questions include whether the game-theoretic framing yields further algorithmic insights (e.g., principled head-count selection), whether the private pathway generalizes to non-vision modalities, and whether the diminishing returns observed at higher accuracy regimes (motivating CSaN-V2's added convolution) indicate a fundamental ceiling for pure attention compensation.
