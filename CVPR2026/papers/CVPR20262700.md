## Problem

Aesthetic viewpoint suggestion requires understanding how visual appeal varies across 3D space, but existing approaches are either single-view adjustments that lack geometric awareness or 3D exploration methods that depend on dense captures and costly RL-based search. No prior work enables geometry-grounded aesthetic reasoning from only a few sparse observations without iterative physical exploration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262700/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2–6 sparse RGB images (256×256 for indoor RE10k scenes, 256×448 for diverse DL3DV scenes) with their corresponding camera poses as input. It produces a continuous 3D aesthetic field (per-Gaussian 32-dim aesthetic embeddings) and, via a two-stage search, outputs a small set of refined camera poses (5-DoF: 3D translation, yaw, pitch) that maximize predicted aesthetic quality. The aesthetic field itself is a differentiable mapping from any camera pose to an aesthetic score, enabling evaluation at arbitrary novel viewpoints.

## Method

The core contribution is distilling high-level aesthetic representations from a pretrained 2D teacher (VEN, using its 23rd-layer 14×14×512 features) into a feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting backbone (DepthSplat), so that each 3D Gaussian carries a compact 32-dim aesthetic embedding alongside its geometric attributes. A lightweight aesthetic DPT head regresses these per-Gaussian embeddings by fusing multi-view features with the teacher's multi-scale aesthetic features, and a transformer downsampler aligns the rendered feature maps to the teacher's spatial resolution. The backbone and aesthetic encoder are frozen; only the additional aesthetic modules are trained end-to-end with MSE loss on hold-out views. For viewpoint search, a two-stage pipeline first samples candidates along an interpolated input trajectory with local perturbations and selects top-K by rendered aesthetic score, then refines each candidate via 25 steps of gradient ascent on the 5-DoF pose. Operating in the distilled feature space rather than raw RGB yields a smooth score landscape that is robust to rendering artifacts and supports stable gradient-based optimization, unlike direct pixel-level scoring.

## Results

On RE10k with 4 input views, the proposed method achieves VEN 2.03 and SAMPNet 2.45, outperforming the strongest single-view baseline (Rotation*) by 0.08 VEN and 0.03 SAMPNet. On DL3DV with 4 input views, it reaches VEN 2.76 and SAMPNet 2.36, surpassing the next-best method (Uchida et al.) by 0.08 VEN and 0.04 SAMPNet. The advantage grows with fewer input views: with only 2 views on RE10k, the method scores VEN 1.89 versus 1.78 for Rotation* and 1.58 for Uchida et al., demonstrating strong 3D reasoning from minimal observations. Performance improves monotonically as input views increase from 2 to 6, with the 6-view setting reaching VEN 2.20 (RE10k) and 2.91 (DL3DV).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on both VEN and SAMPNet metrics across every input-view setting and both datasets. Against the strongest single-view approximation (Rotation*, which exhaustively samples nearby rotations), the gap is 0.11 VEN at 2 views on RE10k (1.89 vs 1.78) and narrows to 0.05 at 6 views (2.20 vs 2.13), consistent with the authors' note that rotation serves as an upper bound for single-view methods. On DL3DV, the advantage over Rotation* is 0.04 VEN at 2 views (2.56 vs 2.52) and 0.06 at 6 views (2.91 vs 2.85). Among open-sourced methods, UNIC [14] is the weakest baseline (VEN 1.15 at 2 views on RE10k), while Uchida et al. [24] is the closest competitor, trailing by 0.31 VEN at 2 views on RE10k (1.89 vs 1.58) and 0.10 at 6 views on DL3DV (2.91 vs 2.81). The in-plane shift approximation [20, 22] consistently underperforms, confirming that local translation alone is insufficient for 3D-aware composition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feedforward 3D Gaussian Splatting paradigm (DepthSplat) by distilling 2D aesthetic features (from VEN) into per-Gaussian embeddings, creating a differentiable 3D aesthetic field. It departs from single-view adjustment methods (cropping, in-plane shifts, rotations) that lack geometric awareness, and from 3D-exploration methods (RL, genetic algorithms) that require dense captures and costly iterative search. The key conceptual move is treating aesthetic quality as a spatially varying 3D property rather than a per-image score, and using feature-level distillation instead of pixel-level RGB scoring to obtain a smooth, optimizable landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides a practical path toward 3D-aware aesthetic reasoning from minimal observations, which is relevant to photography assistants, VR/AR view planning, and autonomous systems. Key limitations include the dependence on known camera poses, sensitivity to geometry reconstruction quality, and a search space constrained to regions supported by initial observations. Open questions include pose-free variants, active perception loops to expand the aesthetic field, and whether the approach generalizes to outdoor or highly dynamic scenes beyond the indoor-centric training data.
