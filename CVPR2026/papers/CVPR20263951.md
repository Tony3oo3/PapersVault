## Problem

Existing GCN-based skeleton action recognition methods either rely on static predefined topologies that cannot capture movement-specific joint correlations, or employ self-attention for dynamic topology at high computational cost. Meanwhile, prior Mamba-GCN hybrids simply stack the two modules in parallel, ignoring the fundamental incompatibility between Mamba's sequential processing and the non-linear graph structure of skeleton data, and conventional TCNs remain limited to local temporal windows.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263951/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D skeleton sequences as tensors of shape C×T×V (channels × temporal frames × joints), with 25 joints for NTU RGB+D and 15 for NW-UCLA. Four data streams (joint coordinates, bone vectors, joint motion, bone motion) are fed through a multi-branch DeGCN backbone and fused before classification. The output is a single action class label from 60, 120, or 10 categories depending on the dataset.

## Method

Gamba introduces a Cls-Mamba module that resolves the graph-vs-sequence mismatch by first classifying joints into k=64 semantic categories via a Gumbel-Softmax node classification module (NCM), then reorganizing the node sequence so Mamba processes same-category nodes contiguously (capturing local intra-class correlations) followed by a global pass over all categories (capturing inter-class dependencies), all with single-direction scanning. A complementary Mamba-TCN module reshapes the tensor to (B·V)×T×C so each joint's temporal sequence is independently processed by Mamba for long-range dependencies, then aggregated locally by a Naive TCN with kernel size 5. The full architecture stacks ST-GCN (first layer) and CTR-GCN (subsequent layers) with Cls-Mamba and Mamba-TCN at each of L=10 levels within a DeGCN multi-branch backbone, trained end-to-end with SGD on four fused data streams.

## Results

Gamba achieves top-1 accuracy of 93.4% (X-Sub) and 97.3% (X-View) on NTU RGB+D 60, 90.1% (X-Sub) and 91.9% (X-Set) on NTU RGB+D 120, and 97.3% on NW-UCLA. On NTU RGB+D 60, it surpasses the previous best GCN method BlockGCN by 0.3% on X-Sub and the best Transformer method Skeleton MixFormer by 0.3% on X-View. On NTU RGB+D 120 X-Set, Gamba leads BlockGCN by 0.4%. The model uses 4.5M trainable parameters and 11.1G FLOPs, comparable to or lower than competing GCN methods (BlockGCN: 1.3M/11.9G, CTR-GCN: 1.2M/14.4G).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gamba outperforms all 23 baselines on four of the five evaluation splits. On NTU RGB+D 60, it achieves 93.4% X-Sub (beating BlockGCN's 93.1% and HD-GCN/Skeleton MixFormer's 93.0%) and 97.3% X-View (beating MDR-GCN's 97.2% and ACFL-CTR/SkeletonGCN's 97.1%). On NTU RGB+D 120 X-Set, Gamba reaches 91.9%, surpassing BlockGCN (91.5%) and MDR-GCN/Skeleton MixFormer (91.3%). On NW-UCLA, Gamba scores 97.3%, edging out Skeleton MixFormer (97.2%) and HD-GCN/BlockGCN (96.9%). The sole exception is NTU RGB+D 120 X-Sub, where BlockGCN leads with 90.3% versus Gamba's 90.1% — a 0.2% gap. Compared to the closest Transformer-based competitor Skeleton MixFormer, Gamba wins on all four splits where both report results, with margins of 0.3–0.6%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CTR-GCN channel-decoupled topology framework and the DeGCN multi-branch backbone for spatial modeling, while introducing Mamba's selective state space model as a complementary sequential component. It departs sharply from prior Mamba-GCN hybrids (e.g., Simba, Zhang et al.) that simply stack the two modules in parallel or series, arguing that feeding raw memory-ordered graph nodes into Mamba captures spurious correlations. The key conceptual departure is the Cls-Mamba module, which uses a Gumbel-Softmax node classification step to semantically reorganize the node sequence before SSM processing, thereby aligning Mamba's unidirectional scanning with the physical kinematic structure of the skeleton. The Mamba-TCN module further extends the temporal modeling line beyond fixed-kernel TCNs by letting Mamba's selective mechanism adaptively capture long-range dependencies before local TCN aggregation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a timely and underexplored integration question—how to combine graph-structured spatial modeling with efficient sequential state-space models—providing a principled answer (category-guided reordering) rather than an ad-hoc stacking. The modest but consistent accuracy gains (0.5–1.0% over CTR-GCN/BlockGCN) combined with lower FLOPs (11.1G vs. 14.4G) suggest a practical efficiency-accuracy tradeoff that could influence future skeleton-based architectures. Open questions include whether the node classification mechanism generalizes beyond 25-joint skeletons to other graph domains, whether the specific backbone choices (DeGCN + CTR-GCN) are essential or interchangeable, and whether the approach scales to longer sequences or higher-resolution temporal data where Mamba's linear complexity advantage would be more pronounced.
