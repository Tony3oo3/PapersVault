## Problem

Existing image restoration models can remove degradations like noise and scratches from old photos but cannot produce accurate, vivid colorization because the unique fading artifacts (reduced brightness, altered hues, low saturation) create a substantial domain gap from modern photo distributions. Additionally, text-based prompts used by generative colorization methods fail to capture fine-grained semantic details, leading to color bleeding and semantically inconsistent results on complex old photo scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261459/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a degraded old photo as input, first preprocesses it with S3Diff to remove noise and blur, then resizes to 1024×1024 for the FLUX-based colorization pipeline. The preprocessed grayscale image feeds into a ControlNet branch while a fine-tuned Redux model extracts a visual semantic prompt (color embeddings) from the same image; both condition the diffusion process. After 8 Euler flow-matching sampling steps, the output is resized back to the original resolution, yielding a fully colorized photo.

## Method

ColorFLUX builds on FLUX with a three-stage structure-color decoupling strategy: (1) Structure Consistency Training fine-tunes a dual-input ControlNet (grayscale image + Redux color prompt) with flow matching loss while freezing FLUX and Redux, forcing the model to learn structure without color; (2) Basic Color Learning fine-tunes the Redux model using a combined flow matching and distillation loss (L_FM + α·L_distill) to extract semantic embeddings aligned with FLUX's color prior space while discarding old-photo color bias; (3) Fine-Color Adjustment applies a Progressive DPO (Pro-DPO) strategy via LoRA on FLUX, where preference triplets are constructed by simulating fading artifacts (brightness, contrast, saturation reduction) and training proceeds from strong to mild augmentations so the model learns subtle color corrections rather than only obvious ones. The key architectural insight is that entangling structure and color learning in a single stage causes mutual interference, and that visual semantic prompts from a fine-tuned Redux encoder outperform text prompts for capturing fine-grained scene semantics in degraded inputs.

## Results

On the DIV2K-valid-synthesized benchmark, ColorFLUX achieves the best DeQA (4.293), VQ-R1 (4.739), AES (83.12), and Overall (OA 83.73) scores, outperforming the closest competitor FLUX-Kontext (OA 83.48). On the more challenging DIV2K-valid-augmented setting (simulating fading artifacts), ColorFLUX maintains its lead with OA 83.53 vs. FLUX-Kontext's 82.55. On the real-world RealOldPhotos benchmark, ColorFLUX achieves OA 83.20, substantially outperforming FLUX-Kontext (77.70) and the commercial Doubao model (75.24), while running in 7.45s per image versus 28–29s for those baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ColorFLUX outperforms all five baselines on the Overall (OA) score across all three benchmarks. Against DeOldify and DDColor (encoder-decoder/GAN-based methods), ColorFLUX leads by 2.4–4.1 OA points, with the largest gap in Color Richness (CRI 80.5–80.8 vs. 64.7–76.2), confirming that generative priors are essential for vivid colorization. Against CtrlColor (diffusion-based, IJCV 2025), ColorFLUX wins on DeQA, VQ-R1, CRI, and OA by 0.3–1.0 points, while CtrlColor retains a slight edge on Structure Consistency (SCS 94.53 vs. 94.05 on DIV2K-valid-synthesized). The most striking comparison is with FLUX-Kontext: on synthetic benchmarks the gap is modest (OA 83.73 vs. 83.48), but on RealOldPhotos FLUX-Kontext degrades severely (OA 77.70, CRI 66.00), while ColorFLUX maintains OA 83.20 and CRI 80.50, demonstrating superior robustness to real-world fading. The commercial Doubao model achieves the highest CRI (83.10) on RealOldPhotos but at the cost of poor Color Rationality (CRA 64.90) and the lowest OA (75.24), indicating over-saturation; ColorFLUX balances richness and rationality more effectively (CRI 80.50, CRA 81.30). In terms of speed, ColorFLUX (7.45s) is roughly 3.8× faster than FLUX-Kontext and Doubao (~28–29s) while delivering substantially better quality on real photos.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based colorization lineage (CtrlColor, DDColor) by specifically targeting the unique degradation profile of old photos—faded brightness, altered hues, and low saturation—that creates a domain gap absent in standard grayscale colorization. Architecturally, it builds on FLUX as the generative backbone, ControlNet for structural conditioning, and Diffusion-DPO for preference alignment, but introduces a structure-color decoupling strategy that trains these components in separate stages rather than jointly. The visual semantic prompt (fine-tuned Redux) replaces the text-prompt guidance common in prior colorization work, and the progressive DPO strategy is a novel application of preference optimization specifically to subtle color-aesthetic refinement rather than broad prompt alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Old photo colorization is a high-impact, emotionally resonant task with clear practical demand, and this paper provides the first framework that explicitly addresses the fading-induced domain gap through both architectural decoupling and preference-based fine-tuning. The structure-color decoupling insight—that entangling structural and color learning degrades both—is a reusable principle for other conditional generation tasks. However, the reliance on FLUX (a large, resource-intensive model) limits deployment on edge devices, and the evaluation heavily depends on a single VLM-based metric (Qwen2.5-VL-72B), raising questions about metric robustness. Open questions include whether the progressive DPO strategy generalizes to other aesthetic refinement tasks and whether lighter backbones can preserve the colorization quality.
