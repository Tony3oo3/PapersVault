## Problem

Existing camera-controlled video generators struggle to maintain long-range 3D geometric coherence under aggressive 6-DoF trajectories, often either dampening motion to preserve structure or committing to the requested path at the cost of distortions and drift. Additionally, most benchmarks focus on structured, constrained settings (indoor tours, driving scenes) rather than in-the-wild outdoor FPV flights with strong parallax and complex layouts, leaving a gap in both method and evaluation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a 6-DoF camera trajectory sampled at 4 fps, and a source video from which 3D-aware memory features are extracted via StreamVGGT (4 layers × 782 tokens, yielding M=3128 tokens per frame at d_m=1024). It produces 5-second clips at 24 fps, 720p, 16:9, generated from 15-second source videos. The query pose is the terminal pose p_{t1} (the farthest viewpoint where drift accumulates), and the local memory window is bounded to L=5 s, keeping the conditioning set compact while ensuring temporal compatibility with the target clip.

## Method

Captain Safari maintains an implicit world memory bank of 3D-aware tokens (from StreamVGGT) and introduces a pose-conditioned retriever that, given a target camera pose, assembles a compact set of pose-aligned world tokens via cross-attention over a local memory window. The retriever uses a MemEnc transformer with 3D RoPE to jointly encode pose-memory pairs, then a QryEnc module generates pose-aware queries that perform cross-attention to extract M world tokens summarizing the scene from the query viewpoint. These tokens are injected into a Wan2.2-Fun-5B Control-Camera DiT through a dedicated memory cross-attention at every layer, providing a stable 3D-consistent prior that shapes denoising of all spatio-temporal tokens. Training is two-stage: the retriever is first warmed up on pose-aligned memory reconstruction, then jointly trained end-to-end with the DiT via LoRA. The key design choice is decoupling retrieval from the denoising loop so the conditioning cost is fixed regardless of trajectory length, and using pose-conditioned queries instead of rigid temporal indexing to softly route each viewpoint to the most relevant past observations.

## Results

On OpenSafari, Captain Safari achieves the best 3D consistency (MEt3R 0.3690, reconstruction rate 0.968) and trajectory following (AUC@30 0.200, CosSim 0.563) among all camera-controlled baselines, while maintaining competitive video quality (FVD 1023.46, LPIPS 0.512). In a 50-participant, 5-way human study, Captain Safari receives 67.33% of total preference votes across video quality, 3D consistency, and trajectory following, compared to 23.07% for its memory-removed ablation and ≤5% for all external baselines. The memory module is critical: removing it degrades reconstruction rate from 0.968 to 0.912 and CosSim from 0.563 to 0.508, while only marginally improving FVD (998.47 vs. 1023.46).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Captain Safari outperforms all three baselines on 3D consistency and trajectory following. Against the strongest baseline Real-CamI2V [20], it reduces MEt3R from 0.3703 to 0.3690 (10% relative variance decrease, Levene p=0.0439), raises reconstruction rate from 0.923 to 0.968, and improves AUC@30 from 0.174 to 0.200 and CosSim from 0.296 to 0.563. Against Wan2.2-5B-Control-Camera [41] (its base DiT), Captain Safari lowers FVD from 1387.75 to 1023.46, reduces MEt3R from 0.3932 to 0.3690, and lifts AUC@30 from 0.181 to 0.200. Geometry Forcing [47] is the weakest baseline across all axes (FVD 2662.75, MEt3R 0.4834, AUC@30 0.168). In the human study, Captain Safari captures 67.33% of votes on average, while the three baselines collectively receive only ~10%, confirming that the gains are perceptually salient rather than metric artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

Captain Safari sits at the intersection of camera-controlled video generation (CameraCtrl, RealCam-I2V, PoseTraj, Wan2.2-5B-Control-Camera) and 3D-consistent world modeling (Geometry Forcing, Memory Forcing, Wonderland, WorldPlay). Its key architectural departure is a decoupled, pose-conditioned retrieval module that maps a query camera pose to a fixed-size set of world tokens via cross-attention over a local memory bank, rather than the implicit clip-bound memories or heavy explicit point clouds used by prior world models. The base generator is a LoRA-tuned Wan2.2 DiT, and 3D-aware memory features come from StreamVGGT, positioning the method as a lightweight conditioning layer on top of an existing large-scale video diffusion backbone rather than a from-scratch architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The pose-aligned world memory mechanism offers a general, fixed-cost recipe for injecting persistent 3D structure into video diffusion without inflating the denoising loop, which could transfer to other long-horizon generation settings. OpenSafari fills a notable gap as a benchmark of aggressive 6-DoF FPV drone footage with verified trajectories, a regime largely absent from prior camera-controlled datasets. Key limitations temper the impact: the memory bank is constructed from ground-truth video (not autoregressively from generated frames), clips are short (5 s), and the MEt3R improvement over the strongest baseline is only 0.0013 in absolute terms. Future work on real-time, autoregressive memory construction is the natural next step to make this a true interactive world engine.
