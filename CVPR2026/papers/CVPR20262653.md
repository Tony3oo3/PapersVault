## Problem

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) for visual generation are bottlenecked by two issues: their generative components require large-scale, often proprietary text-image paired data for pre-training, and prevailing training objectives are computationally inefficient. This paper asks whether a data- and compute-efficient paradigm can be designed that removes the paired-data dependency for the costly pre-training phase while simultaneously improving generative quality and instruction-following fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262653/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the model takes a text prompt and produces a 512×512 or 1024×1024 RGB image via a flow-matching diffusion process (50 NFE steps for IOMM-B). For image editing, it accepts a reference image plus a text instruction and outputs a modified image. During the image-only pre-training stage, no text is provided at all; instead, the conditioning signal is self-derived from the target image's own ViT patch embeddings concatenated with a fixed auxiliary prompt, making the entire pre-training corpus unlabeled.

## Method

IOMM is a two-stage framework: (1) image-only pre-training where a frozen InternVL3-2B MLLM provides conditioning features for a flow-matching MM-DiT generator, and (2) mixed-data fine-tuning on a 50/50 blend of unlabeled images and curated text-image pairs. Two key technical components enable this: a Residual Query Adapter (RQA), a 29M-parameter cross-attention module with 256 learned query tokens that refines the conditioning sequence before it enters the frozen MLLM, avoiding costly full-model fine-tuning and catastrophic forgetting; and a Masked Image Modeling strategy that randomly zeros out a fraction (optimal ratio 0.45) of the image patch tokens in the conditioning signal, transforming the task from trivial dense identity reconstruction into a sparse-to-dense inference problem that forces the model to learn compositional visual priors. The generator backbone follows the FLUX MM-DiT architecture with a flow-matching objective, trained with AdamW (B/L variants) or Muon (XL variant) and EMA (decay 0.999).

## Results

IOMM-B (1.6B, 512px) achieves a new SOTA GenEval score of 0.89, surpassing BAGEL-7B (0.88†), BLIP3-o-8B* (0.84), and MetaQuery-XL (0.80†), while also scoring 0.55 on WISE and 82.95 on DPG Bench. The model was trained from scratch using only ~1050 H800 GPU hours (1000 hours for the image-only pre-training stage), with no proprietary data. On the zero-shot image editing benchmark (ImgEdit-Bench), the image-only pre-trained IOMM-B scores 2.82, exceeding UltraEdit (2.70) which was explicitly trained on editing data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IOMM-B 512 (GenEval 0.89) outperforms all unified-model baselines: BAGEL-7B (0.88†), BLIP3-o-8B* (0.84, which used an extra 30M proprietary pairs), MetaQuery-XL (0.80†), Janus-Pro-7B (0.80), and BLIP3-o-4B (0.81). On WISE, IOMM-B ties MetaQuery-XL at 0.55 and beats BAGEL-7B (0.52) and BLIP3-o-4B (0.50), but trails BLIP3-o-8B* (0.62). On DPG Bench, IOMM-B (82.95) is competitive with SD3-Medium (84.08) and MetaQuery-XL (82.05) but below Show-o2-7B (86.14). For zero-shot image editing on ImgEdit-Bench, the image-only pre-trained IOMM-B (2.82) surpasses UltraEdit (2.70) and Instruct-Pix2Pix (1.88) without any editing-specific training, though it remains below BAGEL (3.20) and Step1X-Edit (3.06) which were trained on editing data. The mixed-data fine-tuning strategy also generalizes: applied to Qwen-Image via LoRA, it improves GenEval from 0.85→0.89 (512px) and 0.87→0.89 (1024px).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the MM-DiT/FLUX diffusion backbone and flow-matching objective, but departs fundamentally from the standard UMM training paradigm that couples the generative component to paired text-image data. Conceptually, it extends the masked autoencoder (MAE) self-supervised principle to the generative conditioning signal of a UMM, and introduces a lightweight Residual Query Adapter as a parameter-efficient alternative to MetaQuery's cross-attention queries for adapting frozen MLLMs. The closest prior work is Lumos-T2I, which demonstrated image-only pre-training for standalone text-to-image models, but IOMM is the first to apply this strategy within a unified understanding-and-generation framework and to show that a small mixed-data fine-tuning stage suffices to recover text alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: by removing the dependency on large-scale proprietary text-image pairs for the costly pre-training phase, IOMM lowers the barrier to open, reproducible UMM research and achieves SOTA GenEval (0.89) with only ~1050 H800 GPU hours. The mixed-data fine-tuning strategy generalizes to existing UMMs (Qwen-Image, OpenUni), suggesting it can be adopted as a drop-in improvement without retraining from scratch. Open questions include whether the frozen-MLLM assumption holds when scaling to much larger language backbones, whether the approach extends to video or 3D generation, and whether the emergent zero-shot editing capability can be further strengthened without task-specific data.
