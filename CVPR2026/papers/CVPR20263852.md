## Problem

In Federated Graph Learning, pronounced topological heterogeneity across clients causes a subset of structurally advantaged clients to dominate the global model, undermining generalization fairness. Existing FGL methods target overall accuracy or statistical heterogeneity but lack a standardized, structure-aware metric to quantify each client's functional contribution, leaving aggregation bias and blind uniform local optimization unaddressed.

## Input / Output

Each client holds graph data (node features X ∈ R^{n×p} and adjacency matrix A) from a specific domain, and all clients additionally process a fixed CSBM probe graph (20 nodes, average degree 3, homophily 0.75, feature dimension 128) to compute their structural profile. The method outputs a trained 2-layer GIN for graph classification, a per-client structural profile metric S_st(k) (a single 32-bit scalar, reducing structural communication cost versus methods that transmit high-dimensional encoder parameters), and a derived fairness weight w_k that drives both local training allocation and global aggregation.

## Method

FedSST introduces a unified structure-based signal: each client propagates a shared CSBM probe graph through its current GIN, extracts post-ReLU embeddings from the first layer, and computes a graph smoothness score S_st(k) via a Laplacian-based metric on the embedding cosine-similarity matrix. This scalar is mapped to a fairness weight w_k through a non-linear bell-shaped function (baseline + saturating distant-compensation + Gaussian mid-range peak) that prioritizes clients with moderate structural profiles. The weight then drives two mechanisms: SLTA allocates differentiated local training epochs E_k proportional to w_k (clamped between E_min and E_max), and SSCA performs two-level fusion—on the client side, an adaptive interpolation α_k blends the local model with the global model (higher α for high-weight clients to promote convergence, lower α for distant clients to preserve personalization), and on the server side, a weighted average with normalized w_k amplifies moderate clients while suppressing extreme ones.

## Results

FedSST achieves the highest average accuracy (AVG) on 4 of 5 cross-domain settings and the lowest client-accuracy standard deviation (STD) on 4 of 5 settings. On the single-domain SM setting, FedSST reaches 82.29% AVG with 10.37 STD, outperforming the next-best FedIGL (79.43% AVG) by 2.86 points. On the multi-domain SM-SN-CV setting, FedSST attains 79.48% AVG and 13.29 STD, the best on both metrics. The only losses are on BIO-SM STD (13.86 vs. APPLE's 12.85) and SM-CV AVG (82.17 vs. FedSSP's 83.70).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSST dominates the comparison on 8 of 10 metric-setting combinations. Against the strongest FGL baselines, FedSST beats FedSSP (NeurIPS 2024) by 3.22 points on SM AVG (82.29 vs. 79.07) and by 1.62 points on SM-SN-CV AVG (79.48 vs. 79.00), while FedSSP narrowly edges FedSST on SM-CV AVG (83.70 vs. 82.17). FedSST outperforms FedIGL (NeurIPS 2025) on every setting for AVG, with the largest gap on BIO-SM (76.17 vs. 72.46, +3.71). Against fairness-aware FL methods, FedSST surpasses q-FedAvg by 3.47 points on SM AVG and AFL by 3.95 points, while also achieving substantially lower STD (10.37 vs. 11.18–11.95). The only notable loss is on BIO-SM STD, where APPLE (12.85) and GCFL (13.18) achieve lower variance than FedSST (13.86), though FedSST still leads that setting on AVG by 3.34 points over the next-best method. Traditional FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx) are consistently the weakest, with FedProx dropping below the Local-only baseline on multi-domain settings, confirming that standard aggregation fails under topological heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedSST extends the FedAvg training loop with two structure-aware adaptive mechanisms (SLTA and SSCA) driven by a single scalar signal computed from a CSBM probe graph. It departs from prior FGL methods (FedStar, FedSSP, FedIGL) that handle topological heterogeneity via spectral or invariant feature extraction but do not explicitly rebalance client contributions during aggregation, and from fairness-aware FL methods (q-FedAvg, AFL) that re-weight based on loss signals rather than structural profiles. The key conceptual departure is treating the structural profile itself—quantified through a privacy-preserving probe graph—as the unified signal that simultaneously governs local training intensity and global aggregation weights, rather than relying on performance feedback or high-dimensional structural encodings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work identifies topological heterogeneity as a distinct driver of generalization inequity in FGL, separate from the statistical heterogeneity that most fairness methods target, and provides a lightweight (O(1) overhead, single scalar communication) mechanism to address it. The 50% reduction in communication rounds via adaptive local training is practically significant for deployment. Open questions include whether the fixed CSBM probe graph generalizes to more diverse or larger-scale graph topologies, whether the bell-shaped mid-range preference holds under different GNN architectures, and how the framework extends to intra-graph FGL settings where clients hold subgraphs rather than independent graphs.
