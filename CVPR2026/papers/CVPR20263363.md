## Problem

Existing vision datasets like COCO overwhelmingly contain objects in their natural, contextually coherent settings, creating a fundamental data scarcity for training models to detect contextual inconsistencies. This gap hinders progress in context-aware visual understanding, out-of-context object detection, and context-informed image forensics, since real-world examples of contextual violations are inherently rare.

## Input / Output

For dataset construction, the pipeline takes a COCO RGB image and a dilated bounding-box mask around a randomly selected object, producing a new inpainted image via Stable Diffusion. For the Objects-from-Context task, the model consumes an RGB image plus a binary mask indicating the query region, with the VAE encoder from Stable Diffusion extracting latents from both inputs before an MLP predicts across 80 COCO classes or 12 supercategories. Context classification outputs a binary in/out-of-context label accompanied by natural-language reasoning text for each of three criteria (location, size, co-occurrence), while fake localization produces a pixel-level probability map enhanced by a multiplicative factor γ=5 in out-of-context regions.

## Method

COinCO is built by systematically replacing exactly one object per COCO image using Stable Diffusion inpainting with a randomly sampled COCO class as the text prompt, followed by YOLOv8x-based verification (up to 3 rounds) and unanimous consensus labeling from three LVLMs (Molmo-72B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, InternVL3.5-38B) across three context criteria. For fine-grained context classification, knowledge distillation transfers reasoning from the 72B Qwen2.5-VL teacher into three independent 3B student models—one per criterion—trained on 20,000 balanced images, reducing model size by 24× while preserving interpretable reasoning. The Objects-from-Context model uses Stable Diffusion's VAE encoder to extract latents from the image and mask, feeding them into an MLP that predicts the original object's class, outperforming co-occurrence-frequency baselines by over 10× at the instance level. For fake detection, a simple post-hoc enhancement multiplies pixel-level fake scores by γ=5 within out-of-context regions identified by Molmo-7B, improving SOTA detectors (CAT-Net, TruFor, PSCC-Net, ManTraNet) without any fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Objects-from-Context task, the proposed model achieves 16.32% instance-level Top-1 accuracy and 35.10% clique-level Top-1 accuracy, substantially outperforming the co-occurrence baseline (1.54% and 9.37% respectively) and random (1.25% and 8.33%). For fine-grained context classification, knowledge distillation from Qwen2.5-VL-72B to 3B student models yields an average accuracy improvement of +7.6% on COinCO (66.2%→73.7%) and +24.1% on original COCO images (63.1%→87.2%). On fake localization, CAT-Net achieves the best SOTA performance with 92.7% accuracy, 76.5% F1, 97.4% AUC, and 90.3% AP, and the context-enhancement strategy further improves all SOTA detectors without fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Objects-from-Context model decisively outperforms both the random baseline and the co-occurrence frequency baseline of Mack & Eckstein [40] across all metrics: at the instance level, Top-5 accuracy jumps from 7.29% (co-occurrence) to 42.80% (ours), a ~6x improvement; at the clique level, Top-1 accuracy rises from 9.37% to 35.10%. For fake localization on COinCO, the paper benchmarks four SOTA detectors rather than proposing a new one: CAT-Net [32] leads all metrics (92.7% Acc, 76.5% F1, 97.4% AUC, 90.3% AP), followed by TruFor [20] (55.9% F1, 73.6% AP), PSCC-Net [39] (95.4% AUC, 79.5% AP), and ManTraNet [60] (30.7% F1, 50.7% AP), revealing a wide performance gap among existing methods. The key finding is that the proposed context-enhancement strategy (multiplying prediction scores by γ=5 in out-of-context regions) improves all four SOTA detectors without any fine-tuning, with the largest relative gains observed for weaker methods like ManTraNet. The co-occurrence baseline [40] is particularly weak because it relies solely on static frequency statistics and cannot capture spatial or size-based contextual cues, which the learned model exploits effectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the COCO dataset lineage by introducing controlled object replacement via diffusion-based inpainting, departing from prior inpainting datasets (COCOGlide, TGIF) that only swap objects within the same category and thus leave context unaltered. Conceptually, it builds on the context-reasoning tradition of Biederman et al. and Choi et al. (relational principles like support, size, probability) and the graph-based detection of Acharya et al., but is the first to operationalize these criteria at scale using multi-LVLM consensus labeling. For fake detection, it repurposes the pixel-level localization literature (CAT-Net, TruFor, PSCC-Net, ManTraNet) by showing that a simple context-based score boost in out-of-context regions improves all four SOTA detectors without any fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

COinCO fills a genuine gap: real-world datasets almost exclusively contain objects in their natural settings, making it impossible to train or evaluate context-aware models without synthetic violations. The dataset's three-criterion annotation scheme (location, size, co-occurrence) and the Objects-from-Context task open a new direction for predicting what *should* be in a scene rather than what *is* there. Key limitations include restriction to COCO's 80 classes, single-object replacement per image, and reliance on LVLM consensus that discards ~25% of images due to labeling ambiguity. The authors note future work on open-vocabulary context reasoning beyond COCO's predefined categories, and the practical question of whether these findings transfer to more complex, multi-object forgeries remains open.
