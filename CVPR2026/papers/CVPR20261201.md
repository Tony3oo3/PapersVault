## Problem

Medical image segmentation faces a critical accuracy-efficiency trade-off: CNNs lack global context while transformers incur prohibitive computational costs for deployment on resource-constrained devices, and existing lightweight models under 2M parameters degrade noticeably on small structures. Rather than scaling up parameters to compensate for weak inductive bias, this paper asks whether physics-informed priors (symplectic geometry, renormalization group theory, entropy diffusion) can guide feature learning to maintain competitive accuracy at extreme parameter budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261201/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PMRNet takes standard 2D medical images (ultrasound, endoscopy, dermoscopy, histopathology) as input and produces pixel-wise binary segmentation masks as output. The network is designed for real-time inference on embedded hardware, operating at 152 FPS on an RTX 4090 with only 0.87M parameters and 3.43 GFLOPs, and is evaluated across 12 datasets spanning diverse imaging modalities and anatomical structures.

## Method

PMRNet is a lightweight encoder-decoder built around three physics-inspired mechanisms. The encoder uses Enhanced Symplectic Convolution, which pairs spatial features (position) with their Sobel-initialized spatial gradients (momentum) and applies a coupled residual update resembling a first-order symplectic integrator to prevent boundary signals from being washed out during training; downsampling is replaced by a renormalization-group-inspired scheme that learns per-channel additive and multiplicative corrections before halving resolution, preserving fine-scale structure. The bottleneck employs a Pseudo-Global Receptive Field module that discretizes the heat equation with learned, variance-adaptive diffusion coefficients over 3 Euler steps, propagating context from confident to uncertain regions at O(N) cost without self-attention. The decoder uses Boundary-aware Fusion, which extracts a local boundary map from encoder features and dynamically gates the skip-connection fusion so that boundary regions receive enhanced edge features while interior regions rely on standard region features.

## Results

PMRNet achieves 87.25% IoU and 92.56% Dice on the Clinic dataset and 76.19% IoU and 84.73% Dice on TG3K, using only 0.87M parameters and 3.43 GFLOPs. Across all 12 medical imaging datasets, PMRNet attains the best IoU, Dice, and HD95 in every case, outperforming models with 10–100× more parameters. Inference speed reaches 152.03 FPS on an RTX 4090, surpassing EMCAD (146.41 FPS), PVT-CASCADE (104.59 FPS), TransUNet (96.37 FPS), Rolling-UNet (33.90 FPS), and Deformable-LKA (24.73 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PMRNet (0.87M params) outperforms all 15 baselines on both TG3K and Clinic, with the largest margins over lightweight models: it beats MALUNet (0.18M) by 9.52% IoU on TG3K and 17.66% on Clinic, and ConvUNeXt (3.54M) by 11.43% and 14.05% respectively. Against the strongest SOTA competitors, PMRNet leads EMCAD-B2 (26.76M) by 1.31% IoU on TG3K and 2.95% on Clinic, and Deformable-LKA (101.64M) by 3.15% and 1.60% on the two datasets. On the 10 additional datasets (Table 2), PMRNet achieves the best IoU in all 10 cases, with particularly large gains on Kvasir-SEG (+2.59% over EMCAD-B2) and PH2 (+1.15% over Deformable-LKA). In inference speed, PMRNet at 152.03 FPS is the fastest among all post-2021 methods tested, exceeding EMCAD by 5.6 FPS and TransUNet by 55.7 FPS, while simultaneously delivering the highest accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

PMRNet departs from the dominant scaling-up strategy in medical image segmentation (exemplified by TransUNet's 105M+ parameters) by repurposing physics-inspired priors—symplectic geometry for coupled feature-gradient updates, renormalization group coarse-graining for information-preserving downsampling, and entropy-driven heat diffusion for global context—as structural inductive biases within a lightweight CNN. It extends the U-Net encoder-decoder skeleton and the depthwise-separable convolution toolkit from MobileNet/UNeXt, but replaces standard skip connections with boundary-gated fusion and substitutes self-attention with a linear-complexity diffusion process. The paper explicitly positions against both the heavy-attention camp (TransUNet, Deformable-LKA) and the lightweight-but-weak camp (UNeXt, MALUNet, ConvUNeXt), claiming a middle path that prior work has not occupied.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A 0.87M-parameter model matching or exceeding 10–100× larger architectures across 12 diverse clinical datasets is practically significant for point-of-care and embedded deployment where memory and latency are hard constraints. The physics-inspired design choices (symplectic coupling, RG downsampling, anisotropic diffusion) offer a principled alternative to ad-hoc module stacking, though the connections to actual physical theory remain largely metaphorical rather than formally derived. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to 3D volumetric segmentation, whether the physics priors transfer to foundation-model-based segmentation, and whether the gains persist under distribution shift or with fewer training epochs.
