## Problem

Existing self-supervised forensic methods (e.g., NoiPri, ForSim, ExifMeta) are limited to camera-specific traces such as PRNU, making them poorly suited for non-camera tasks like synthetic image detection and social network provenance. Additionally, these methods require tens of thousands to over a million annotated training images, which is costly and cannot cover future camera models. This paper asks whether a single forensic feature can capture both in-camera and out-camera processing history while being trainable from as few as 10 raw images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263075/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes raw DNG images (10 from FiveK or SIDD) that are online-processed through randomly sampled editing chains to produce RGB images paired with automatically generated text labels describing the chain. At inference, a single RGB image is fed into the trained EfficientNet encoder to produce a D-dimensional Editprint vector, which supports open-set verification via cosine similarity (AUC) or closed-set classification via nearest-centroid assignment (precision, recall, F1). The text labels are natural-language descriptions of operation sequences (e.g., 'DM-AHD JPEG-75'), not one-hot vectors, enabling the model to capture semantic similarity between related editing chains.

## Method

The core mechanism is an online editing pool that simulates in-camera ISP operations (demosaicing, white balance, tone mapping) and out-camera post-processing (compression, scaling, blurring, noise) to generate over 10^7 diverse editing chains from 10 raw images, each paired with an auto-generated text label. Training uses a Self-Augmentation Training (SAT) paradigm with two branches: a self-constructed branch that performs CLIP-style image-text contrastive learning enhanced by self-constructed positive pairs (multiple images under the same chain) and a fuzzy-mapping aggregation that models inter-chain ambiguity via a fuzzy membership matrix; and an adaptive transfer branch that applies a domain-adaptive MLP classifier with soft cross-entropy loss (using text-feature self-distillation as soft labels) and a WGAN-inspired nuclear-norm Wasserstein constraint to stabilize batch-to-batch category discrimination. The image encoder is EfficientNet and the text encoder is a Transformer, jointly optimized end-to-end with a learned temperature parameter.

## Results

On the SID task (open-set verification, AUC), Editprint achieves a mean AUC of 0.9625 across 10 GAN and diffusion-model generators, outperforming the best self-supervised baseline ExifMeta (0.8539) by 10.86% and remaining competitive with supervised methods HiFi (0.9627) and UniDet (0.9700). On the SNP task, Editprint reaches a mean AUC of 0.8853, surpassing ExifMeta (0.7525) by 13.28% and approaching supervised Seq2Seq (0.8952) and MultiClue (0.8986) within ~1–1.3%. On the CSI task, Editprint attains a mean AUC of 0.9199, essentially matching the best supervised method ExifMeta (0.9211) while using only 10 training images versus over 1.5 million for ExifMeta.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among self-supervised baselines, Editprint decisively outperforms ForSim (mean AUC 0.7414), NoiPri (0.6998), and NoiPri++ (0.7660) on the SID task, with the gap widening to 10.86% over the strongest self-supervised competitor ExifMeta (0.8539). On the SNP task, Editprint (0.8853) beats ExifMeta (0.7525) by 13.28% and NoiPri++ (0.6835) by 20.18%, while on CSI it essentially ties ExifMeta (0.9199 vs. 0.9211) and surpasses ForSim (0.9155) and NoiPri (0.9096). Against supervised baselines, Editprint slightly trails UniDet (0.9700) and HiFi (0.9627) on SID by 0.75% and 0.02% respectively, and is marginally below MultiClue (0.8986) and Seq2Seq (0.8952) on SNP by 1.33% and 0.99%, yet it exceeds CNNDet (0.9481) on SID by 1.44%. Notably, Editprint achieves these results with only 10 training images, whereas ExifMeta requires over 1.5 million labeled samples and the supervised methods require task-specific annotated data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised general forensics line (ForSim, NoiPri, NoiPri++, ExifMeta) by shifting the learning objective from in-camera sensor traces (PRNU) to joint in- and out-camera editing traces, and by replacing large annotated camera datasets with an online editing pool that simulates the full ISP and post-processing pipeline from raw images. The training paradigm adapts CLIP-style image-text contrastive learning, but introduces self-constructed positive pairs and a fuzzy-mapping aggregation (rooted in fuzzy c-means) to handle the many-to-one correspondence between images and editing-chain text labels. The adaptive transfer branch borrows the nuclear-norm Lipschitz critic from discriminator-free domain adaptation (Chen et al., CVPR 2022) and the WGAN formulation (Arjovsky et al., ICML 2017) to stabilize batch-level classification supervision across the vast, dynamically sampled category space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The headline result—that 10 raw training images suffice to produce a forensic feature competitive with methods trained on 24K–1.5M labeled images—dramatically lowers the barrier to deploying general forensics and sidesteps the camera-model coverage problem that plagues PRNU-based approaches. The editing pool concept is a reusable recipe: any new in- or out-camera operation can be added to the pool without re-collecting data, making the framework extensible to future processing pipelines. Open questions include whether the simulated editing chains faithfully capture the stochastic and device-specific variations of real-world ISP implementations, how performance degrades when test-time operations fall outside the pool's sampling distribution, and whether the approach extends naturally to video or multi-frame forensics.
