## Problem

Training implicit neural representations on high-resolution signals requires optimizing over millions of coordinates, making full-batch gradient descent prohibitively slow. Existing sampling-based acceleration methods select coordinates using static heuristics (e.g., output error magnitude) that implicitly assume the Neural Tangent Kernel is diagonal and isotropic, ignoring the heterogeneous self-leverage and cross-coordinate functional coupling that actually govern parameter updates in finite-width MLPs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263734/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a discrete signal as N coordinate-attribute pairs (e.g., 2D pixel locations with RGB values for images, spatiotemporal points for video, or 3D coordinates with density for shapes) along with a standard MLP architecture (typically a 5×256 SIREN). At each iteration it computes NTK rows for all N coordinates to score them, then selects a batch of size B (default 20% of N) for the gradient step; the NTK is recomputed every α iterations (default 10) to balance accuracy against overhead. The output is the trained MLP parameter vector θ, which encodes the entire signal implicitly and supports continuous evaluation at arbitrary coordinates and resolutions without storing the discrete data.

## Method

NINT derives the continuous-time functional evolution of an INR under gradient descent via first-order Taylor expansion, showing that the instantaneous change in f_θ(x) at any point x is governed by the product of the NTK row K_θ(x_i, ·) and the loss gradient vector. Rather than selecting coordinates by output error alone (which assumes a scaled-identity NTK), NINT scores each coordinate by the norm of its NTK-augmented gradient — the NTK row dotted with the full loss gradient vector — thereby jointly capturing local fitting discrepancy, self-leverage (diagonal NTK entries), and cross-coordinate coupling (off-diagonal entries). The selected batch maximizes the expected magnitude of the global functional update per step. In practice, NINT uses a hybrid schedule: a fixed random fraction ξ (default 0.7), a decaying NTK-guided fraction (1−ξ)exp(−λt/α), and an error-based remainder, with the NTK recomputed every α iterations to limit overhead.

## Results

On the Kodak and DIV2K image fitting tasks, NINT (ξ=0.7) achieves PSNR 28.96 dB, SSIM 0.776, and LPIPS 0.414 at 250 iterations, and PSNR 39.09 dB, SSIM 0.958, and LPIPS 0.029 at 5000 iterations, leading all baselines at every checkpoint. In terms of efficiency, NINT reduces the time to reach PSNR 30 dB from 49.11 s (full-batch) to 25.05 s (a 49% reduction) and the time to reach PSNR 35 dB from 184.78 s to 102.88 s, while requiring only 20% of the full coordinate set per step. Across network sizes (1×64 to 5×256 SIREN) and architectures (MLP, FFN, FINER, GAUSS, PEMLP, SIREN, WIRE), NINT consistently improves PSNR and cuts training time by up to 43.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NINT (ξ=0.7) outperforms all five sampling-based baselines at every iteration checkpoint. At 250 iterations, NINT achieves 28.96 dB PSNR, beating EVOS (28.02 dB) by ~0.94 dB, Expan. (28.03 dB) by ~0.92 dB, EGRA (27.67 dB) by ~1.28 dB, SoftM. (27.65 dB) by ~1.31 dB, and INT (27.57 dB) by ~1.39 dB. At 5000 iterations, NINT reaches 39.09 dB PSNR, surpassing INT (39.02 dB) and Expan. (38.35 dB), though it remains slightly below full-batch Stand. (39.76 dB) — a gap attributable to using only 20% of coordinates per step. On the efficiency front (Table 2), NINT reaches PSNR 30 dB in 384 iterations / 25.05 s versus 523 / 49.11 s for full-batch (49% time savings) and PSNR 35 dB in 1644 / 102.88 s versus 2043 / 184.78 s (44% time savings), outpacing all other samplers. The closest competitor is Expan. (ξ=0.5), which achieves 38.35 dB at 5000 iterations but requires 1802 iterations to hit PSNR 35 dB versus NINT's 1644. NINT's advantage is most pronounced in early training (250–1000 iterations), where the NTK-guided selection provides the largest PSNR gains over error-only methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

NINT extends the nonparametric teaching framework for INR acceleration introduced by INT [75], which selects coordinates via greedy functional algorithms but implicitly approximates the NTK with a scaled identity matrix. The key departure is incorporating the full Neural Tangent Kernel [17] to score examples by the norm of their NTK-augmented loss gradients, thereby capturing heterogeneous self-leverage and cross-coordinate coupling that error-only heuristics (EGRA, EVOS, SoftM., Expan.) neglect. This positions NINT as a principled upgrade within the sampling-based acceleration family, moving from static output-space criteria to dynamic parameter-space influence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~50% training time reduction with maintained or improved fidelity is practically significant for INR applications where training budgets are tight (high-resolution images, video, 3D volumes), and the plug-and-play, architecture-agnostic design lowers adoption barriers. However, the NTK computation itself introduces overhead that scales with dataset size, and the paper acknowledges that NTK approximations are needed to reduce this cost for very large signals. Open questions include whether the NTK-guided advantage persists in the infinite-width regime where the NTK is constant, and how the method interacts with hybrid explicit-implicit architectures that change the effective kernel structure.
