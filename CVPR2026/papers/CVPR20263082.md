## Problem

LiDAR-based world models lag behind camera-video and occupancy counterparts due to two core gaps: the unstructured, sparse nature of point clouds resists direct application of techniques designed for structured data, and raw LiDAR lacks the discriminative cues (texture, semantic labels) needed to separate dynamic objects from static structure. Existing methods either sacrifice geometric fidelity through voxel/BEV projections or rely on convolution/transformer architectures misaligned with LiDAR's sequential scanning, and none provide explicit scene decomposition or autonomous rollout.

## Input / Output

The model consumes τ_p historical LiDAR sweeps (converted to 2.5D range maps via spherical projection) along with their ego status, and optionally a BEV layout as a control signal. It produces τ_f future LiDAR point clouds decoded from latent features. The ego status can be ground-truth, predicted by an integrated planner for autonomous rollout, or externally specified for counterfactual reasoning. Generation operates in a vector-quantized latent space, with the range map resolution determined by the number of vertical lasers and horizontal azimuth bins.

## Method

GEM builds on the structural analogy between sequential laser scanning and Mamba's selective state-space processing. A LiDAR scene tokenizer encodes range maps through a Mamba-based encoder into compact latents, discretized via a VQ codebook and reconstructed with an adversarial discriminator to preserve high-frequency geometric detail. An unsupervised dynamic-static separator then disentangles features using frame-wise differences (dynamic) and sliding-window temporal averaging (static), fused through adaptive gated attention. The core tri-path deformable Mamba runs three parallel branches—generic, dynamic, and static—each with a learned scan path that deforms the standard 3D grid to concentrate sampling on the relevant scene elements, followed by Mamba processing and gated fusion; four such blocks are stacked iteratively. A latent diffusion process generates future frames, with an optional jointly-trained planner for autonomous ego-status prediction and a BEV layout controller for controllable 'what-if' scenario synthesis.

## Results

On nuScenes 1s prediction, GEM achieves a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.38, reducing it by 81.1% over the second-best method (Copilot4D, CD=2.01), while also leading on L1 (0.98 vs. 1.30) and AbsRel (6.67 vs. 8.58). For 3s prediction, GEM attains CD=0.67 and L1=1.43, outperforming Copilot4D (CD=2.47, L1=1.51) on all metrics except AbsRel (11.13 vs. 10.38). On KITTI Odometry, GEM achieves CD=0.17 (1s) and CD=0.28 (3s), comprehensively outperforming all baselines including Copilot4D (CD=0.21 and 0.67 respectively). GEM also demonstrates superior prediction stability with L1_sr=0.01 and AbsRel_sr=0.07 on nuScenes 1s, far below competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GEM outperforms all six LiDAR world model baselines (SPFNet, S2Net, Ray Tracing, 4D-Occ, Copilot4D) on nuScenes across both 1s and 3s horizons, with the most dramatic gain being an 81.1% CD reduction over Copilot4D at 1s (0.38 vs. 2.01). On KITTI Odometry, GEM similarly dominates, achieving CD=0.17 at 1s versus Copilot4D's 0.21 and 4D-Occ's 0.61, and CD=0.28 at 3s versus Copilot4D's 0.67. The only metric where GEM does not lead is AbsRel at 3s on nuScenes (11.13 vs. Copilot4D's 10.38), a marginal 7.3% gap. For generated distribution realism on KITTI-360, GEM achieves the best FPVD (18.7) and JSD (0.125) among all methods including single-frame generators (UltraLiDAR, LiDM, Text2LiDAR, WeatherGen), while 4D-Occ trails on FRID (164.2 vs. 94.0). GEM also demonstrates superior temporal stability (L1_sr, AbsRel_sr) compared to all baselines, which the authors attribute to the explicit dynamic-static disentanglement.

## Relation to Prior Work

GEM positions itself at the intersection of state-space sequence modeling (Mamba, DefMamba) and latent diffusion-based world modeling (LDM, COME), applying them to LiDAR point cloud forecasting. It departs from prior LiDAR world models (4D-Occ, Copilot4D, SPFNet, S2Net) in two key ways: (1) it exploits the structural isomorphism between LiDAR's sequential laser scanning and Mamba's selective state-space scanning, replacing the CNN/Transformer-on-range-image or voxel/BEV pipelines that prior methods use; and (2) it introduces an unsupervised dynamic-static disentanglement with deformable scan paths, a mechanism absent from all compared baselines. The control-signal encoding (ego status, BEV layouts) follows the COME framework, and the adversarial discriminator for range-map reconstruction borrows from LiDM.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LiDAR world models have lagged behind camera-video and occupancy counterparts, and GEM's 81% CD reduction on nuScenes 1s prediction plus its autonomous-rollout and BEV-conditioned 'what-if' generation capabilities address a real gap in scalable driving simulation. The explicit dynamic-static separation is particularly relevant because LiDAR lacks the texture and semantic cues that make camera-based disentanglement easier, and the unsupervised formulation avoids costly per-frame annotations. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to longer prediction horizons (beyond 3s), multi-sensor fusion settings, and whether the deformable Mamba paths remain effective under sensor degradation (rain, fog). The modest 3s AbsRel result (slightly worse than Copilot4D) suggests temporal consistency over longer windows remains an active challenge.
