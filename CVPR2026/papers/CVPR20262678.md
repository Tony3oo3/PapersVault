## Problem

Existing VLA policies keep plan intent in latent representations, making them brittle to spatial ambiguity in cluttered scenes (e.g., resolving 'the cup left of the mug') and opaque to human oversight during long-horizon execution. This limits task decomposition, causal explanation of action choices, and the ability for a human to intervene and correct the robot's spatial understanding mid-task.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a token sequence comprising multi-view ego-camera images (left-wrist, right-wrist, base), a natural-language task instruction, a history of completed subtasks, the current subtask label, and a rendered Visual Sketch image overlaid on the ego-view. Output is a hybrid discrete-continuous stream: autoregressive text tokens for temporal/spatial reasoning and structured sketch descriptions (which are then rendered into an image), followed by continuous action chunks predicted via flow-matching. The model autonomously emits a <BOR> token to enter Reasoning Mode or a <BOA> token to enter Action Mode, so the output structure is event-driven rather than fixed-length.

## Method

Action-Sketcher introduces Visual Sketch, a sparse tuple of geometric primitives (bounding boxes for object selection, keypoints for part-level affordances, and translation/rotation arrows for motion direction) rendered on the robot's ego-view image plane, serving as an explicit, human-editable contract between high-level reasoning and low-level control. Built on the π₀ backbone, the model operates in a See→Think→Sketch→Act loop where a token-gated mechanism adaptively switches between Reasoning Mode (autoregressive generation of subtask rationale and text-based sketch, then rendering to image) and Action Mode (flow-matching action-chunk prediction conditioned on the sketch and robot state). Training follows a three-stage curriculum: (1) foundational spatiotemporal pre-training on 3.4M grounding/pointing/scene samples plus 870K egocentric video sequences with GPT-4o-annotated rationales; (2) reasoning-to-sketch fine-tuning on 21K curated long-horizon episodes (2–16 subtasks) from real-world collection and LIBERO/RoboTwin 2.0 annotations; (3) joint sketch-to-action and mode-adaptation training with sketch perturbation augmentation (IoU≥0.8 box jitter, radius-c point noise) and a mode-balanced sampling strategy that equalizes Reasoning and Action token frequencies to prevent <BOA> bias.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, Action-Sketcher achieves 96.9% average success rate, with its strongest advantage in the Long-horizon category (96.0%) where it outperforms the next-best baseline OpenVLA-OFT (94.5%) by 1.5 points and π₀.5 (92.4%) by 3.6 points. On the more challenging RoboTwin 2.0 and real-world tasks (Table 2), Action-Sketcher shows substantially larger margins: e.g., 34.5% on Stack Blocks vs. 7.0% for π₀.5 and 12.4% for OpenVLA-OFT, and 67.0% on real-world Pick & Place vs. 34.5% for π₀.5. With human-in-the-loop sketch correction, real-world subtask completion improves by 16–23 percentage points (e.g., Tidy Table from 52.0% to 75.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Action-Sketcher's most decisive wins are on long-horizon and spatially complex tasks. On LIBERO-Long, it achieves 96.0%, surpassing OpenVLA-OFT (94.5%) and π₀.5 (92.4%), while on the harder RoboTwin 2.0 tasks it leads by large margins (e.g., 34.5% vs. 7.0% for π₀.5 on Stack Blocks; 43.0% vs. 11.0% on Place A2B Left). On the real-world Pick & Place task, Action-Sketcher reaches 67.0% subtask completion, nearly doubling π₀.5 (34.5%) and exceeding OpenVLA-OFT (52.5%) by 14.5 points. However, on LIBERO's Spatial and Goal categories, Action-Sketcher (97.2%, 94.8%) trails OpenVLA-OFT (97.6%, 97.9%) and π₀.5 (98.8%, 98.0%), and its overall LIBERO average (96.9%) is marginally below OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%). Among visual-prompting baselines, Action-Sketcher outperforms Molmo-ACT (86.6% avg) and PixelVLA (86.7% avg) by roughly 10 points on average, and TraceVLA (74.8%) by over 22 points. The one task where Action-Sketcher does not lead is Hang Mugs on RoboTwin 2.0 (25.0% vs. 27.0% for π₀.5), suggesting that certain rotation-heavy subtasks remain challenging.

## Relation to Prior Work

Action-Sketcher builds directly on π₀'s flow-matching action expert as its backbone, but departs from the broader VLA lineage by inserting an explicit, human-editable Visual Sketch (points, boxes, arrows) as a structured intermediate between high-level reasoning and low-level control. This positions it against two prior threads: (1) hierarchical and think-before-act VLAs (π₀.5, OneTwoVLA, ThinkAct) that keep plan intent in latent or text-only form, and (2) visual-prompting works (MolmoAct, TraceVLA, RT-Sketch) that use static or non-editable geometric cues. The key conceptual departure is making the sketch persistent, subtask-scoped, and directly correctable by a human, with a token-gated mechanism (<BOR>/<BOA>) that adaptively switches between reasoning and action modes within a single autoregressive model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in VLA systems: the opacity of plan intent, which hinders both long-horizon reliability and human oversight. The human-in-the-loop results (up to +23% subtask completion via sketch correction) and the ablation showing the sketch is the single most critical component (removing it drops success to 9.8%) make a compelling case for explicit spatial intermediates. However, absolute real-world success rates remain modest (27–67%), and the sketch representation (2D primitives on an ego-view) may not suffice for depth-critical or multi-view coordination tasks. Open questions include whether the token-gated switching generalizes to highly dynamic or multi-robot settings, and whether the three-stage curriculum can be simplified or replaced by end-to-end training at scale.
