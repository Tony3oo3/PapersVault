## Problem

Multi-modal LLMs suffer from semantic drift during autoregressive decoding, where visual grounding weakens over long sequences and hallucinated objects progressively dominate the output. Existing mitigation strategies either require costly retraining, rely on expensive contrastive decoding with multiple forward passes, or sacrifice recall for precision. The paper identifies attention sink tokens—characterized by high attention row-entropy—as the mechanistic root cause, showing they systematically inflate hallucinated-object logits while diluting visually grounded signals.

## Input / Output

KVSmooth operates as a plug-and-play inference-time module on standard MLLM pipelines (LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4, InstructBLIP 7B), consuming an RGB image and a text prompt to produce a generated caption (up to 512 tokens, greedy decoding). The method modifies only the KV-Cache entries at selected decoder layers (3–31) during each autoregressive step, requiring no architectural changes, additional forward passes, or training data. A fixed FIFO queue of length 15 maintains recent row-entropy values for adaptive coefficient computation, adding negligible overhead (3.61 s per caption vs. 3.36 s baseline).

## Method

KVSmooth applies an exponential moving average (EMA) to both Key and Value vectors in the KV-Cache, derived from a Bayesian MAP estimator under a Gaussian random-walk prior on hidden states, which yields a closed-form smoother balancing current observation fidelity with temporal consistency. The smoothing coefficient is not fixed but adaptively determined per token via attention row-entropy: at each step, the token's row-entropy is computed across all heads, its percentile rank within a sliding FIFO queue is calculated, and this rank (clipped to a narrow window around a reference value λ_ref) sets the EMA weight. Tokens with high row-entropy—identified as attention sinks that average across the entire context and amplify hallucinated-object logits—receive stronger smoothing, suppressing their influence on subsequent generation. The method is training-free, requires no model modification, and is applied jointly to K and V (rather than attention outputs or hidden states alone), which ablation studies confirm yields the best hallucination-precision-recall trade-off.

## Results

On the CHAIR benchmark, KVSmooth reduces sentence-level hallucination (CHAIR_S) from 41.8 to 18.2 (−56%) on LLaVA-1.5 while simultaneously improving F1 from 77.5 to 79.2. On MiniGPT-4, CHAIR_S drops from 31.8 to 17.0 with F1 rising from 69.9 to 71.7; on InstructBLIP, CHAIR_S falls from 61.4 to 42.2 with F1 increasing from 71.6 to 75.1. On OPOPE, KVSmooth achieves the highest Precision (91.20 on LLaVA-1.5) and F_β=0.2 (88.90) across all three models, and on Object HalBench it reduces sentence-level hallucination rate (CHAIR_SR) by 63.1%, 40.3%, and 41.6% for LLaVA-1.5, MiniGPT-4, and InstructBLIP respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KVSmooth outperforms all five baselines on F1 across every model, and achieves the lowest CHAIR_S on MiniGPT-4 (17.0 vs. best baseline 24.6) and InstructBLIP (42.2 vs. best baseline 47.8). On LLaVA-1.5, MiddleLayer [15] achieves a marginally lower CHAIR_S (17.8 vs. 18.2) but at a substantial F1 cost (75.9 vs. 79.2), indicating it suppresses hallucinations by over-pruning correct objects. OPERA [12] and SPARC [16] improve F1 over baseline but fail to reduce CHAIR_S meaningfully (44.2 and 45.6 vs. 41.8 on LLaVA-1.5), while VCD [18] actually worsens hallucination (56.0) at the expense of F1. PAI [25] reduces CHAIR_S to 22.6 on LLaVA-1.5 but drops F1 to 75.5, confirming the precision-recall trade-off that KVSmooth avoids through its entropy-guided adaptive smoothing. On OPOPE, KVSmooth achieves the best Precision and F_β=0.2 for all three models, with the largest margin over MiddleLayer [15] on LLaVA-1.5 (91.20 vs. 89.43 Precision).

## Relation to Prior Work

KVSmooth extends the attention-sink line of work (StreamingLLM, OPERA) from identifying and penalizing sink tokens to directly regularizing their hidden-state contributions via EMA smoothing on the KV-Cache. It departs from prior training-free hallucination methods (VCD, PAI, SPARC, MiddleLayer) that operate at the logit or attention-weight level by intervening at the representation level, and from OPERA's column-sum sink metric by proposing attention row-entropy as a real-time, single-step proxy. The Bayesian MAP derivation connecting Gaussian transition priors to EMA provides a principled justification for the smoothing operation, distinguishing it from heuristic attention reweighting approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play method with minimal overhead (3.61 s vs. 3.36 s baseline), KVSmooth offers a practical deployment path for reducing hallucination without retraining or architectural changes. The consistent 56% CHAIR_S reduction across three 7B MLLMs and four benchmarks, while maintaining or improving F1, addresses a key limitation of prior methods that trade recall for precision. Open questions include whether the fixed layer range (3–31) and queue length (15) generalize to larger or differently-architected models, whether the approach extends to video or multi-turn dialogue settings, and whether the row-entropy sink metric captures all hallucination mechanisms beyond object-level errors.
