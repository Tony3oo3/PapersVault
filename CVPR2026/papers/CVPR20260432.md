## Problem

Dual-encoder semantic correspondence models combining DINOv2 with diffusion backbones achieve strong benchmark accuracy but generalize poorly to unseen keypoints and categories, because sparse keypoint supervision causes the representation to collapse around annotated landmarks. This reveals a gap between in-domain performance and real-world usability, where queried points rarely match those seen during training. The paper asks whether a single, compact backbone can simultaneously improve fine-grained localization and generalize beyond the training keypoint vocabulary.

## Input / Output

The model takes a pair of source and target RGB images and produces a dense correspondence field mapping every source pixel to its semantically matching target location. During training, a sparse set of annotated keypoint correspondences (typically fewer than 20 per image) plus SAM-derived object masks constrain the computation to object regions. At inference, a Window soft-argmax over the upsampled similarity map yields final pixel-level keypoint predictions at 4× the native DINOv2 patch resolution (i.e., 3.5 px per feature token rather than 14 px).

## Method

MARCO builds on a frozen DINOv2 ViT-L/14 backbone, inserting lightweight AdaptFormer bottleneck adapters in the last 12 transformer layers and a compact 4× upsampling head (ConvTranspose 2×2 followed by a 3×3 depthwise convolution) to restore sub-patch resolution, adding less than 5% parameter overhead. Training combines two objectives: (1) a coarse-to-fine Gaussian RBF cross-entropy loss whose bandwidth σ is cosine-annealed from 3 to 1, progressively sharpening predictions from region-level alignment to sub-patch precision; and (2) a dense self-distillation loss where an EMA teacher's features are used to mine mutual nearest-neighbor matches, which are densified over the object surface via piecewise-affine warping on a Delaunay triangulation, clustered in displacement space via k-means, and anchored to ground-truth keypoints to filter symmetric or occlusion-induced errors. The student is then trained with L2 regression on the resulting pseudo-correspondences, expanding supervision from a handful of keypoints to thousands of reliable matches and preserving the global geometric coherence of the pre-trained features.

## Results

On SPair-71k, MARCO (with object masks) achieves 27.0 PCK@0.01, 77.6 PCK@0.05, and 87.2 PCK@0.10, setting a new state of the art with a single DINOv2 backbone. On AP-10K, MARCO leads across all splits (intra-species 89.1, cross-species 88.3, cross-family 83.4 PCK@0.10), with the largest margin in the hardest cross-family setting (+4.9 over Geo-SC). On PF-PASCAL, MARCO reaches 98.6 PCK@0.15. In generalization, MARCO scores 67.5 PCK@0.10 on SPair-U (unseen keypoints) and leads all five MP-100 macro-domains, reaching 87.5 on Human face and 60.4 on Home furniture.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARCO outperforms all eight baselines across every reported benchmark. Against the previous state-of-the-art Geo-SC [54] (dual DINOv2+SD encoder), MARCO gains +5.3 PCK@0.01 and +4.0 PCK@0.10 on SPair-71k, +9.4 PCK@0.01 on AP-10K intra-species, and +4.9 PCK@0.10 on the hardest cross-family split, while being 3× smaller and 10× faster. Against Jamais Vu [32], the strongest generalization-focused baseline, MARCO leads by +5.1 PCK@0.10 on SPair-U (unseen keypoints) and by +2.0 to +10.2 PCK across the five MP-100 macro-domains (unseen keypoints and unseen categories). Among single-backbone methods, GECO [14] is the closest competitor in-domain (73.6 vs. 87.2 PCK@0.10 on SPair-71k) but degrades sharply out-of-domain. Unsupervised baselines (DINOv2+NN, DIFT, SD+DINOv2) and the pseudo-label method DIY-SC [7] trail by 10–30+ PCK points, confirming that the coarse-to-fine supervised objective plus dense self-distillation is essential for both precision and generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARCO departs from the dominant dual-encoder paradigm (DINOv2 + Stable Diffusion) that has defined recent semantic correspondence work, instead pursuing a single-backbone design built entirely on DINOv2 with lightweight AdaptFormer modules and a compact upsampling head. Conceptually, it extends the self-supervised correspondence line (SCorrSAN, DistillDIFT, GECO) by replacing local pseudo-label mining or optimal-transport objectives with a global dense self-distillation that exploits the partially coherent flow already present in DINOv2's feature space. The coarse-to-fine Gaussian RBF loss replaces the soft-argmax + L2 regression used by prior supervised methods, and the flow-anchoring strategy (Delaunay densification + k-means clustering + GT anchoring) offers an alternative to Jamais Vu's 3D canonical templates for addressing the generalization gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical failure mode in supervised semantic correspondence: fine-tuning on sparse keypoints actively degrades generalization, causing the model to underperform the frozen encoder on unseen landmarks. The dense self-distillation via flow anchoring addresses this without requiring 3D geometry, category priors, or external depth models, making it broadly applicable. The introduction of the MP-100-based benchmark (unseen keypoints on novel categories) fills a gap left by SPair-U, which only tests a few unseen keypoints within known categories. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video correspondence, whether the SAM mask dependency can be removed, and whether the self-distillation signal remains reliable as the student diverges further from the teacher.
