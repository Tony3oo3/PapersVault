## Problem

Existing gait recognition research is dominated by RGB-derived modalities, and the few multi-modal benchmarks available (e.g., LidarGait, FreeGait) cover only RGB and LiDAR, leaving heterogeneous sensor interactions and unified cross-sensor retrieval largely unexplored. No prior work provides a single model that simultaneously supports single-modal, cross-modal, and multi-modal gait recognition across diverse sensing modalities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262917/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

OmniGait consumes nine image-based modalities (RGB, RGB silhouette, 2D pose heatmaps, event, IR, IR silhouette, depth, LiDAR-projected depth, radar-projected depth), all normalized to 64×64 spatial resolution with T=16 frames per sequence. The output is a 256×16 identity embedding vector produced by Temporal Pooling, Horizontal Pyramid Pooling (16 partitions), an FC layer, and a BNNeck, which supports arbitrary single-modal, bidirectional cross-modal, and pairwise multi-modal retrieval.

## Method

OmniGait comprises three stages: (i) independent modal-specific encoders that map each raw modality into a 128×64×64 feature map while preserving sensor-specific characteristics; (ii) a lightweight cross-modal fusion module that concatenates two modality features, applies a 1×1 convolution, and uses a softmax-gated residual connection to adaptively weight each modality's contribution; and (iii) a shared residual backbone (producing 512×16×16 features) through which all unimodal and fused features are jointly processed, so that batch normalization layers learn mixed-domain statistics that implicitly align heterogeneous representations. Training optimizes a joint cross-entropy and triplet loss over all modalities simultaneously, and at inference only the required modality-specific encoder(s) are activated, making the model fully compatible with single-, cross-, and multi-modal tasks without architectural changes.

## Results

On the MMGait benchmark, RGB and IR modalities achieve the highest single-modal recognition, with 99.7% Rank-1 under normal walking (NM) and 60.7%/78.8% under clothing change (CL), respectively. LiDAR point clouds reach 97.0% R1 (NM) and 76.6% (CL), while 4D Radar point clouds are the weakest at 39.5% (NM) and 28.3% (CL). Multi-modal fusion of RGB silhouettes with depth or LiDAR projected depth yields a substantial +19.3% and +19.7% Rank-1 improvement on CL. The proposed OmniGait unified model matches or exceeds dedicated cross-modal baselines, e.g., achieving 87.0% R1 for RGB→Depth retrieval versus 77.5% for a pair-specific model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among silhouette-based methods, GaitBase [14] (98.5% R1 NM, 61.0% CL) and DeepGaitV2-P3D [13] (98.7% NM, 58.7% CL) outperform GaitSet [7] (95.2% NM), GaitPart [12] (96.4% NM), and GaitGL [36] (93.1% NM), with GaitBase leading under the challenging CL condition. For pose-based methods, GPGait++ [39] (84.7% NM, 51.2% CL) and SkeletonGait [15] (82.7% NM, 43.4% CL) substantially surpass earlier approaches like GaitGraph [48] (31.5% NM) and GaitTR [63] (72.6% NM). In multi-modal fusion, the two-stream strategy from MultiGait++ [29] shows that adding depth to RGB silhouettes boosts CL Rank-1 from 61.0% to 80.3% (+19.3%), and adding LiDAR projected depth reaches 80.7% (+19.7%). OmniGait's unified model outperforms dedicated cross-modal baselines in NM and BG settings (e.g., RGB→Depth: 87.0% vs. 77.5% R1), though it still lags behind modality-specific experts under extreme CL conditions. The 4D Radar modality, evaluated with LidarGait++ [46], remains the weakest sensor at 39.5% R1 (NM), confirming its limited standalone discriminative power despite its robustness to adverse weather.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-modal gait recognition line initiated by LidarGait and FreeGait, which were limited to RGB + LiDAR pairs, by introducing three additional sensor types (IR, Depth, 4D Radar) and a unified evaluation protocol. Architecturally, OmniGait inherits the Temporal Pooling + Horizontal Pyramid Pooling + BNNeck pipeline from GaitBase/OpenGait for shared representation learning and adopts the two-stream modality-specific encoder design from MultiGait++ for fusion. The Omni Multi-Modal Gait Recognition task draws conceptual inspiration from 'all-in-one' multi-modal re-identification frameworks (e.g., ReID50) but is the first to formalize this unification specifically for gait recognition across heterogeneous physical sensors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMGait is the first gait benchmark spanning five physically distinct sensing modalities, enabling systematic study of modality complementarity, robustness, and cross-sensor retrieval that was previously impossible. The finding that IR outperforms RGB under clothing variation (78.8% vs 61.0% R1) and that 4D Radar remains severely limited (≤22% R1) provides concrete guidance for sensor selection in deployment. The OmniGait task opens a new research direction toward single-model, modality-agnostic gait systems, but significant challenges remain: the CL condition still causes substantial degradation across all modalities, the unified model lags modality-specific experts under hard conditions, and scaling beyond pairwise fusion to arbitrary multi-sensor combinations is unexplored.
