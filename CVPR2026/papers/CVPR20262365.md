## Problem

Existing dynamic 3D reconstruction methods model deformation as a function of discrete time, which suffices for interpolation within observed frames but fails to extrapolate to unseen future states because they do not learn the underlying physical laws governing motion. Prior approaches that inject physical priors (PINNs, differentiable simulators) require manually specified forces or restrictive assumptions, while velocity-field methods capture only low-order dynamics insufficient for complex deformations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262365/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes multi-view video sequences (first 75% of frames) as input and produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation capable of rendering novel views at future times beyond the observed temporal range (the remaining 25% of frames). The Gaussian set is initialized from SfM point clouds, and the model is trained with L1 + D-SSIM photometric loss. A key structural detail is that the physical state is factorized into N particle-level static features and F system-level dynamic fields (F ≪ N), so the evolver operates on only F fields rather than all N particles, reducing complexity from O(NG) to O(FG).

## Method

ParticleGS reformulates dynamic 3DGS as a physical particle system with three components: (1) a Dynamics Latent Space Encoder (Mini-PointNet++ with cross-attention) that maps canonical Gaussians to an initial physical state factorized into static properties and F shared dynamic fields; (2) a Neural ODE-based Dynamics Evolver that learns the continuous-time state transition dg/dt = f_evolver(g, t), where the dynamic fields serve as an augmented state representing high-order derivatives, integrated via RK4; and (3) a Gaussian Kernel Space Decoder (multi-head MLP) that maps evolved states to translation, rotation (via Rodrigues' formula), and deformation parameters. The key insight is that by learning local high-order derivatives in a continuous latent space rather than memorizing discrete temporal deformations, the model can stably extrapolate by simply integrating the learned dynamics forward. Training uses a progressive schedule (geometry warm-up → dynamics warm-up → joint optimization) plus online neighborhood regularization to handle Gaussian densification during training.

## Results

On the Dynamic Object Dataset (extrapolation), ParticleGS achieves 36.47 PSNR / 0.984 SSIM / 0.012 LPIPS, outperforming the best velocity-field baseline FreeGave (33.63 PSNR) by ~2.8 dB. On the Dynamic Indoor Scene Dataset (extrapolation), it reaches 31.10 PSNR / 0.892 SSIM / 0.133 LPIPS, beating TRACE (29.48 PSNR) by ~1.6 dB. On the real-world FreeGave-GoPro dataset, ParticleGS achieves 26.79 PSNR / 0.903 SSIM / 0.150 LPIPS in extrapolation, surpassing FreeGave (26.51 PSNR). In reconstruction, ParticleGS also leads on all four datasets, e.g., 39.78 PSNR on Dynamic Object and 25.50 PSNR on Dynamic Indoor Scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParticleGS outperforms all eight baselines on both reconstruction and extrapolation across all four datasets. Against time-conditioned reconstruction methods (HexPlane, TiNeuVox, DeformGS, Grid4D), it gains over 5 dB PSNR on extrapolation (e.g., 36.47 vs. 27.67 for Grid4D on Dynamic Object), confirming that learning physical dynamics rather than temporal deformation is critical for future prediction. Against physics-prior methods (NVFi with Navier-Stokes constraints, GSPrediction with GCN rigid-body relations), the improvement is similarly >5 dB, showing that explicit physical priors are less effective than learning dynamics directly from video. Against the strongest velocity-field baselines (TRACE, FreeGave), ParticleGS leads by ~2.5 dB PSNR on synthetic datasets (36.47 vs. 33.63 on Dynamic Object; 31.10 vs. 29.48 on Indoor Scene) and by ~0.3 dB on the real-world FreeGave-GoPro dataset (26.79 vs. 26.51), indicating that higher-order Neural ODE dynamics capture complex deformations better than divergence-free velocity fields. In reconstruction, ParticleGS also achieves the best PSNR on every dataset, e.g., 39.78 on Dynamic Object and 25.50 on Indoor Scene, demonstrating that the physics-based formulation does not sacrifice rendering fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

ParticleGS sits at the intersection of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for scene representation and Neural ODEs for continuous-time dynamics modeling. It departs from time-conditioned deformation methods (DeformGS, Grid4D) that treat motion as a function of a timestamp, and from physics-prior injection approaches (NVFi's PINN formulation, GaussianPrediction's GCN constraints) that require manually specified governing equations or geometric structure. The key conceptual departure is from velocity-field-based extrapolation methods (TRACE, FreeGave) that model only first-order dynamics: ParticleGS instead learns a higher-order continuous state transition in a factorized latent space (static particle properties + shared dynamic fields), inspired by the Material Point Method's separation of per-particle and system-level quantities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By learning dynamics directly from video without requiring predefined physical laws or structured geometric inputs, ParticleGS offers a practical path toward general-purpose physical motion prediction that could apply to diverse scenes without per-scene physics specification. The factorized encoding (F shared dynamic fields rather than N per-particle states) provides a useful efficiency pattern that keeps Neural ODE integration tractable at 3DGS scale. However, the paper acknowledges a fundamental limitation: the model cannot predict motions that deviate from the dynamics observed during training (e.g., sudden object breakage), leaving open the question of whether truly novel physical events can be extrapolated. Whether the learned 'physical laws' are genuinely interpretable as physical quantities or merely learned patterns that happen to extrapolate smoothly remains an open question.
