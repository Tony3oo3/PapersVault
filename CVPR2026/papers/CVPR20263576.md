## Problem

Current biomedical vision-language pretraining compresses rich multi-panel scientific figures into coarse figure-level image-text pairs, discarding the fine-grained region-level correspondences that clinicians rely on when zooming into local structures. Existing pipelines face a fundamental scalability-granularity tradeoff: annotation-intensive natural CV methods achieve region-level alignment but do not generalize to biomedical domains, while scalable literature-mining approaches either treat multi-panel figures as single instances or decompose panels while retaining entangled figure-level captions. No prior method produces hierarchical supervision spanning all three granularities—multi-panel figures, individual panels, and fine-grained regions—with corresponding aligned captions.

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes raw multi-panel biomedical figures and their paired captions/narrative text from the Biomedica corpus (~350K figures), processing them with Qwen2.5-VL-72B under set-of-marks prompting to produce approximately 1M single-panel crops plus marker-anchored region crops with two complementary text sources per region (grounded caption fragments and LVLM-generated patch descriptions). The trained model is a ViT-L/14 dual-encoder (initialized from BMC-CLIP, with only the last five vision transformer blocks updated) that outputs a shared d-dimensional embedding space where panel-level embeddings serve as the primary representation for downstream tasks, while figure- and region-level embeddings provide auxiliary contextual scaffolding.

## Method

Panel2Patch exploits the inherent pedagogical structure of scientific figures—panel identifiers, arrows, brackets, and zoom-in crops—as implicit localization cues, using SoM-style prompting to decompose multi-panel figures into panels and marker-guided fusion (caption boxes gated by marker proximity, inflated marker boxes, IoU-NMS) to mine high-precision region crops without explicit region-text annotations. On top of this hierarchical corpus, a single CLIP-style encoder is trained with three intra-level contrastive losses (figure, panel, region), a fine-grained ROI-pooling alignment loss that enforces pixel-level consistency, and inter-level message passing losses that align each figure embedding with the average-pooled embeddings of its constituent panels (top-down context) and each panel embedding with the average-pooled embeddings of its regions (bottom-up evidence). An alternating coarse-to-fine training schedule (M→P→R cycling) prevents catastrophic forgetting across granularities and handles the strong data imbalance between levels, yielding a unified encoder where panel embeddings are explicitly refined by both global figure context and fine-grained regional evidence.

## Results

On zero-shot classification across six biomedical specialties (Biology, Dermatology, Microscopy, Ophthalmology, Pathology, Radiology), the proposed model trained on only 400K hierarchical pairs achieves 50.25% average accuracy, surpassing BMC-CLIP (47.85%, 24M pairs) and BiomedCLIP (41.93%, 15M pairs) by 2.4 and 8.3 points respectively. In single-panel short-context retrieval (Table 2, Panel A), the model reaches 36.60% R@1 (I2T) and 38.24% R@1 (T2I), outperforming BMC-CLIP (34.15%/32.03%) and BiomedCLIP (33.66%/30.07%). For bounding-box-to-text retrieval (Panel B), it achieves 8.64% R@1 (I2T) and 9.38% R@1 (T2I), the best among all compared models. The model uses approximately 60% less pretraining data than prior state-of-the-art biomedical VLP models while achieving superior performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (400K pairs) outperforms all five biomedical baselines on average zero-shot classification accuracy. Against BMC-CLIP (24M pairs, 47.85% avg), the largest prior biomedical VLP model, it gains +2.4 points, with particularly strong margins in Radiology (+5.9) and Microscopy (+3.9). Against BiomedCLIP (15M pairs, 41.93%), the gap widens to +8.3 points on average. BMC-LongCLIP (1M pairs, 48.18%) is the closest competitor, but the proposed method still leads by +2.1 points on average while using 2.5× less data. MedSigLIP (33M pairs, 35.38%) and PMC-CLIP (80K pairs, 18.71%) are substantially outperformed. In retrieval (Table 2), the method leads all baselines on both single-panel short-context R@1 (36.60% vs. 34.15% for BMC-CLIP) and bounding-box-to-text R@1 (8.64% vs. 8.04% for BMC-CLIP), confirming that hierarchical supervision improves both coarse and fine-grained alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CLIP-style dual-encoder paradigm (Radford et al., 2021) and the Set-of-Marks prompting technique (Yang et al., 2023), repurposing the latter not for open-vocabulary detection but as a structural parsing tool for scientific figure layouts. It departs from prior biomedical VLP pipelines (PMC-CLIP, BiomedCLIP, BIOMEDICA) that treat multi-panel figures as single coarse instances, and from region-level approaches (FineCLIP, FG-CLIP, MEDTRINITY-25M) that rely on expensive detectors or GPT-based captioners, by exploiting the inherent pedagogical structure of scientific figures—panel labels, arrows, zoom-in crops—as free localization cues. The hierarchical zoom-in pretraining with inter-level message passing extends standard contrastive learning by coupling three granularities (figure, panel, region) through aggregated embedding alignment, a design not previously applied to multi-panel scientific documents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical takeaway is that supervision quality can substantially outweigh raw data scale in biomedical VLP: 400K hierarchically structured pairs outperform models trained on 15M–24M coarse figure-caption pairs, suggesting a path toward more data-efficient foundation models without architectural changes. However, the data generation pipeline itself requires a 72B LVLM (Qwen2.5-VL) and ~2,900 GPU-hours, so the 'efficiency' is in pretraining corpus size rather than total compute, and the ~80% correct panel decomposition rate introduces noise that limits the upper bound. Open questions include whether the marker-guided mining generalizes to figure styles lacking explicit visual cues (e.g., unlabeled composites), whether the approach transfers to non-biomedical scientific domains, and whether the inter-level message passing design scales to deeper hierarchies or more granular region decompositions.
