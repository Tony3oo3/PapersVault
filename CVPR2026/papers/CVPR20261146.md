## Problem

Cross-domain few-shot object detection suffers from a previously overlooked phenomenon the authors term target-domain Astigmatism: pretrained detectors exhibit persistently dispersed and unfocused attention in target domains, causing oversized bounding boxes and redundant predictions. While regular fine-tuning inherently trends toward reducing this attention dispersion, the domain gap and data scarcity leave results far from satisfactory, and no prior work has explicitly diagnosed or targeted this attention-level failure mode.

## Input / Output

The method takes a target-domain RGB image at inference, a compact support set of K annotated examples per novel class (used during fine-tuning to extract prototypes), and simple negative text prompts of the form 'not [class]' processed by BERT. It outputs standard COCO-style bounding boxes with class labels, evaluated via mAP over IoU 0.5–0.95. The baseline is GLIP with a Swin-Tiny backbone and DynamicConv fusion; the three proposed modules operate on multi-scale visual features extracted by the backbone before the detection heads.

## Method

The paper proposes a bio-inspired center-periphery attention refinement framework with three complementary modules that reduce attention distance by reallocating attention weights. (1) Positive Pattern Refinement (PPR) computes class-specific foreground prototypes from support-set bounding boxes and, at inference, up-weights features at positions with high cosine similarity to these prototypes via temperature-scaled softmax, strengthening same-object attention. (2) Negative Context Modulation (NCM) builds a single unified background prototype from regions outside all ground-truth boxes and down-weights features matching it, suppressing spurious foreground-background affinity. (3) Textual Semantic Alignment (TSA) encodes 'not [class]' prompts with BERT and aligns them to the background visual prototype through a contrastive InfoNCE-style loss, sharpening the center-periphery boundary via cross-modal supervision. During fine-tuning the model is optimized with detection loss plus the TSA alignment loss; at inference, PPR and NCM enhanced features are additively fused and fed to the detection heads, transforming dispersed astigmatic attention into focused object-centric patterns.

## Results

On six cross-domain few-shot object detection benchmarks, the proposed method achieves average mAP of 23.81 (1-shot), 33.73 (5-shot), and 39.17 (10-shot), establishing new state-of-the-art results across all settings. In the standard 5-shot protocol, the method outperforms the strongest baseline VFM-MoE (28.45 mAP) by +5.28 mAP and the GLIP backbone (29.36 mAP) by +4.37 mAP. Gains are most pronounced on domains with severe visual shifts: Clipart1k (+5.70 over VFM-MoE) and DeepFish (+6.29 over VFM-MoE) in the 5-shot setting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves consistent wins over all eight baselines across every dataset and shot setting. Against the strongest recent baselines, VFM-MoE (NeurIPS'25, 28.45 avg mAP in 5-shot) and Domain-RAG (NeurIPS'25, 27.98), the proposed method gains +5.28 and +5.75 mAP respectively, with the largest per-dataset margins on Clipart1k (+5.70 over VFM-MoE) and DeepFish (+6.29). The GLIP backbone (29.36), which serves as the base detector, is improved by +4.37 mAP, confirming that the center-periphery attention refinement adds substantial value beyond the pretrained model. CD-ViTO (ECCV'24, 28.30), the prior CD-FSOD-specific method, is outperformed by +5.43 mAP, with particularly large gains on UODD (+6.42) and NEU-DET (+6.80) where domain shifts are most challenging. Earlier baselines (DE-ViT, ViTDet, Detic, Distill) trail by 9–17 mAP, indicating that the attention-refinement strategy addresses a fundamentally different failure mode than prior distillation or domain-adaptation approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the CD-FSOD task established by CD-ViTO and Distill-CDFSOD, using GLIP (grounded language-image pre-training) as the base detector, and draws on the attention distance metric from Raghu et al. (ICLR 2023) to diagnose a previously overlooked failure mode: dispersed attention in target domains. Unlike prior CD-FSOD methods that focus on distillation, domain-adaptive optimization, or retrieval-augmented generation, this work reframes the problem as an attention-focusing challenge inspired by the human fovea-style visual system, combining class-specific foreground prototypes, unified background prototypes, and negative textual prompts into a center-periphery refinement framework. The approach departs from feature-alignment-centric paradigms by directly reshaping attention distributions rather than aligning feature spaces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of target-domain Astigmatism as a distinct, measurable phenomenon (via attention distance across transformer layers) provides a new diagnostic lens for understanding why cross-domain few-shot detection underperforms, and the finding that regular fine-tuning only marginally reduces dispersion motivates targeted intervention. The substantial SOTA gains across six diverse domains (up to +6 mAP in 1-shot) suggest that attention-focusing is a meaningful axis of improvement beyond feature alignment. However, the evaluation is limited to a single backbone (Swin-Tiny) and a single detector (GLIP), leaving open whether the center-periphery framework generalizes to other architectures (e.g., DETR variants, YOLO) or to settings without access to support-set prototypes at inference time.
