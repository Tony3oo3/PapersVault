## Problem

Diffusion-based real-world image super-resolution faces a fundamental trade-off: multi-step methods (e.g., SUPIR, DiT4SR) offer prompt controllability through iterative denoising but suffer from generative randomness, low fidelity, and expensive inference, while one-step methods (e.g., PiSA-SR, TSD-SR) achieve high fidelity through targeted fine-tuning but lose the prompt controllability of the pretrained diffusion model. No existing method simultaneously delivers fidelity, bilingual prompt controllability, and single-step inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260584/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-quality RGB image, an optional bilingual text prompt (English or Chinese), and a user-specified Fidelity Weight f ∈ [0,1] that linearly maps to the noise level in the Control Noise stream (f=1 yields zero added noise for maximum fidelity; f=0 matches the Prior Noise level for maximum prompt controllability). It produces a ×4 super-resolved RGB image in a single forward pass through the DiT, operating entirely in the VAE latent space. Notably, ODTSR is the only compared method that natively accepts Chinese prompts without additional training on scene-text datasets.

## Method

ODTSR extends the Qwen-Image double-stream DiT (>20B parameters) with a Noise-hybrid Visual Stream (NVS) design that splits the visual stream into two branches: a frozen Prior Noise stream that receives the LQ latent with a fixed noise level (t_p=0.43) to preserve the pretrained diffusion prior, and a LoRA-finetuned Control Noise stream that receives the LQ latent with a dynamically adjustable noise level t_c=(1−f)·t_p governed by the Fidelity Weight. The two streams are jointly attended in the multimodal DiT to predict a single-step velocity field that maps the noisy latent directly to the HR target. For training, ODTSR employs Fidelity-aware Adversarial Training (FAA) using a relativistic GAN loss (inspired by R3GAN) whose weight is dynamically modulated by f—higher adversarial pressure at low f encourages prompt-guided detail synthesis, while lower pressure at high f favors reconstruction fidelity. The discriminator is a Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B DiT with appended 2D convolutional layers producing patch-wise scores, regularized with an approximate R1 penalty, and the generator is trained with a combined MSE+LPIPS reconstruction loss plus the fidelity-modulated adversarial loss over 10,000 iterations on LSDIR and FFHQ with RealESRGAN-synthesized degradations.

## Results

On RealSR (f=1.0, no prompt), ODTSR achieves LPIPS 0.2398, DISTS 0.1894, and FID 101.49 in a single step, outperforming the best one-step baselines TVT (LPIPS 0.2597, FID 109.90) and PiSA-SR (LPIPS 0.2672, FID 124.09), as well as multi-step methods SUPIR (LPIPS 0.3541, FID 130.38, 50 steps) and DiT4SR (LPIPS 0.3215, FID 118.55, 40 steps). On DRealSR, ODTSR leads with LPIPS 0.2592 and FID 119.86, and on DIV2K-Val it achieves the best DISTS (0.1700) and FID (21.47). In the controllable setting (Table 3), ODTSR with f=1.0 attains CLIP-T 34.01 and NED 0.8475 (with prompt) on RealCE-val, surpassing all baselines on text fidelity while maintaining competitive perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against one-step baselines, ODTSR consistently leads on perceptual and distribution metrics: on RealSR it improves LPIPS by 0.0199 over TVT (0.2398 vs. 0.2597) and 0.0274 over PiSA-SR, while reducing FID by 8.41 over TVT and 22.60 over PiSA-SR. On DRealSR the margins are larger (LPIPS 0.2592 vs. 0.2899 for TVT; FID 119.86 vs. 134.28). On DIV2K-Val, TSD-SR edges ODTSR slightly on LPIPS (0.2673 vs. 0.2761), but ODTSR still wins on DISTS (0.1700 vs. 0.1821) and FID (21.47 vs. 23.29). Against multi-step baselines, ODTSR (1 step) substantially outperforms SUPIR (50 steps) and DiT4SR (40 steps) on all perceptual metrics—for example, on RealSR ODTSR achieves LPIPS 0.2398 vs. 0.3541 (SUPIR) and 0.3215 (DiT4SR)—while also being the only method supporting bilingual (English + Chinese) prompt controllability. In the controllable setting (Table 3), ODTSR at f=1.0 achieves CLIP-T 34.01, surpassing DiT4SR at CFG=8 (33.49) and SUPIR at CFG=4 (33.97), and on RealCE-val it reaches NED 0.8475 with prompt versus 0.7205 for TSD-SR without any text-specific training. In the user study, ODTSR receives 53.25% of votes compared to 19.4% (TSD-SR), 14.6% (PiSA-SR), and 12.75% (DiT4SR).

## Relation to Prior Work

ODTSR builds directly on the Qwen-Image DiT architecture and its rectified-flow formulation, extending the pretrained visual stream into a dual-stream Noise-hybrid Visual Stream (NVS) design where a frozen Prior Noise branch preserves the diffusion prior and a LoRA-tuned Control Noise branch provides fidelity-controllable conditioning. For one-step inference, it adapts the Diffusion Adversarial Post-Training (APT) paradigm but skips consistency distillation (since input is noised LQ rather than pure noise) and replaces the standard GAN loss with a relativistic formulation from R3GAN. The key departure from prior one-step Real-ISR methods (OSEDiff, PiSA-SR, TSD-SR, TVT) is that they sacrifice prompt controllability for fidelity, while multi-step methods (SUPIR, DiT4SR) retain controllability at the cost of 40–50 sampling steps; ODTSR claims to recover both in a single step by making the adversarial signal fidelity-aware.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single forward pass through a 20B+ parameter model yields controllable, bilingual (English/Chinese) super-resolution without domain-specific fine-tuning, which reduces deployment cost and annotation burden for sub-domains like scene text. The NVS design is a clean, generalizable architectural idea—splitting a pretrained stream into a frozen prior branch and a controllable branch—that could transfer to other restoration or editing tasks. Open questions include whether the single-scalar Fidelity Weight is sufficient for multi-objective control, how the approach scales to higher magnifications (×8, ×16) or video, and whether the adversarial training recipe remains stable at even larger model scales or with different base architectures.
