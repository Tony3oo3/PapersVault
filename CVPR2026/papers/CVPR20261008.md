## Problem

Single-image reflection separation is inherently ill-posed, and existing deep learning methods fail in two extreme regimes: strong reflections that occlude the transmission layer, and weak reflections where the reflection signal is too subtle to extract. Prior diffusion-based approaches either recover only the transmission layer or rely on detailed language annotations, leaving the joint recovery of both layers under challenging conditions unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261008/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image at 960×960 resolution containing a superposition of transmission and reflection, along with two fixed text prompts ('Transmission' and 'Reflection') that condition a single unified diffusion model to generate both layers. The input image is VAE-encoded into a latent representation and concatenated channel-wise with the noisy latent at each diffusion step. Outputs are two full-resolution RGB layers: the clean transmission (background) and the extracted reflection, with the reflection layer evaluated quantitatively only on the SIR² dataset where ground truth is available.

## Method

The approach fine-tunes Stable Diffusion v2.1 as a single unified model that jointly denoises both transmission and reflection latents, guided by distinct text prompts. Three key mechanisms differentiate it: (1) cross-layer self-attention modifies the U-Net so that queries from each layer attend to concatenated keys and values from both layers, promoting feature disentanglement under supervision; (2) disjoint sampling, inspired by classifier-free guidance, uses the predicted noise difference ε_T − ε_R as mutual negative guidance at each denoising step to explicitly push the two latent trajectories apart and suppress cross-contamination; (3) a test-time latent optimization stage refines the initial outputs by minimizing a composition loss through a learned latent-space composition network (replacing the naive pixel-space I = T + R assumption), which is faster and more memory-efficient than pixel-space backpropagation. A Fidelity-Guided Feature Modulation (FGFM) module further anchors decoded features to the original mixture image to prevent color drift.

## Results

On the transmission layer, the method achieves the best or tied-best results across all three real-world datasets. On Real20, it reaches PSNR 25.32, SSIM 0.850, LPIPS 0.107, and DISTS 0.089; on Nature, PSNR 26.71, SSIM 0.837, LPIPS 0.080, DISTS 0.064; and on SIR², PSNR 25.35, SSIM 0.911, LPIPS 0.075, DISTS 0.065. For the reflection layer (evaluated only on SIR²), it achieves PSNR 21.14, SSIM 0.681, LPIPS 0.373, and DISTS 0.275, substantially outperforming all baselines. The largest gains are in perceptual metrics (LPIPS, DISTS), where the method leads by 0.02–0.04 over the next-best competitor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method is compared against seven baselines: YTMT, RobustSIRR, DSRNet, RRW, DSIT, RDNet, and ControlNet (as a diffusion-based reference). On Real20 and Nature, the proposed method wins on all four metrics, with the closest competitor being RDNet (e.g., LPIPS 0.107 vs. 0.145 on Real20; 0.080 vs. 0.114 on Nature). On SIR², RDNet edges out the proposed method on PSNR (25.62 vs. 25.35), but the proposed method ties DSIT on SSIM (0.911) and leads decisively on perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.075 vs. 0.108 for DSIT; DISTS 0.065 vs. 0.074 for RDNet). For the reflection layer on SIR², the method dominates all baselines by a wide margin (PSNR 21.14 vs. 20.59 for DSRNet; LPIPS 0.373 vs. 0.520 for DSIT). ControlNet, used as a naive diffusion baseline, performs poorly across all datasets (e.g., PSNR 18.68 on Real20), confirming that direct fine-tuning without the proposed cross-layer attention and disjoint sampling is insufficient. All baselines were retrained on a common dataset to ensure fair comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of diffusion-based image processing (ControlNet-style conditioning, classifier-free guidance) to the specific problem of single-image reflection separation, building on the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion v2.1. It departs from L-DiffER, the closest prior work, by jointly separating both transmission and reflection layers rather than recovering only transmission, and by replacing complex language annotations with simple fixed prompts augmented by a novel cross-layer self-attention mechanism. The disjoint sampling strategy is a creative repurposing of classifier-free guidance as mutual negative guidance between two layers, and the latent-space composition optimization replaces the standard pixel-space additive constraint (I = T + R) with a learned composition network, drawing on recent latent-space optimization techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion model explicitly fine-tuned for joint reflection separation, demonstrating that generative priors can recover both the intended scene and the undesired reflection layer — a capability previously assumed to be limited to transmission-only hallucination. The latent-space composition optimization offers a practical efficiency win (10× faster per step than pixel-space optimization) while improving quality, making the approach more deployable. Open questions include whether the method extends to video reflection separation (noted as future work), whether the FGFM module can fully eliminate residual reflections without manual parameter tuning, and whether the cross-layer attention and disjoint sampling ideas transfer to other layered decomposition tasks (e.g., matting, deblending).
