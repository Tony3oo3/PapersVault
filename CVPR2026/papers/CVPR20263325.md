## Problem

Existing temporal learning components in visual trackers (multi-template strategies, trajectory tokens, larger backbones) increase FLOPs and parameters, making it impossible to determine whether performance gains stem from temporal modeling itself or merely from added computational budget. The community lacks a criterion to decouple and fairly evaluate the actual effectiveness of temporal modules, which is especially critical for resource-constrained UAV deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263325/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

LETrack takes a 128×128 template image and a 256×256 search image per frame, plus a set of K=16 propagated temporal tokens (h^{t-1}) from the previous frame that are concatenated with the template and search sequences before encoding. The prediction head outputs a classification score map, local offsets, and a bounding box size; the final target location is determined by the peak classification score combined with the offset and size, with a Hamming window applied for positional priors.

## Method

The core contribution is the LETL module, which consists of a Token Extractor and a Token Combiner. The Token Extractor computes per-token importance by element-wise multiplying row-averaged search-to-template attention scores (across all heads) with the flattened classification score map, then selects the top-K tokens as representative appearance tokens h^t. The Token Combiner computes pairwise cosine similarity between h^t and the previous frame's h^{t-1}, merges the top-r most similar pairs (using the current token's value rather than averaging to avoid diluting fine-grained features), and concatenates the merged tokens with unmerged previous tokens to form h^{t+1} for propagation. This entire temporal mechanism adds only ~0.09 GFLOPs over the one-stream baseline (DeiT-Tiny backbone), achieving a PPF of 55 versus 8–10 for competing strategies. The paper also introduces the PPF metric (Δprec/Δflop) to enable efficiency-aware comparison of temporal components, and trains the full tracker for 300 epochs on LaSOT, COCO, TrackingNet, and GOT-10k with focal loss plus L1 and GIoU losses.

## Results

LETrack achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five UAV benchmarks in Table 1, reaching 69.6% AUC / 85.8% precision on UAVTrack112, 68.2% / 84.8% on UAVTrack112_L, 68.0% / 87.0% on UAV123, 67.4% / 85.9% on UAV123@10FPS, and 65.2% / 85.0% on DTB70. On VisDrone2018, LETrack further attains 66.1% AUC and 87.2% precision, surpassing all compared trackers. In terms of efficiency, LETrack requires only 2.48G FLOPs and 7.98M parameters while running at 204 FPS on an A100 GPU and 26 FPS on a Jetson TX2 without any acceleration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LETrack outperforms all 19 compared trackers on every dataset in Table 1, with the largest margins over older CF-based methods (e.g., +23.5 AUC over fDSST on UAVTrack112) and consistent 2–5 AUC gains over the strongest recent ViT-based UAV trackers. The closest competitors are SGLATrack (CVPR'25, 67.5/82.8 on UAVTrack112) and ORTrack (CVPR'25, 66.6/82.1), both of which focus on efficiency-oriented layer adaptation; LETrack surpasses them by +2.1 and +3.0 AUC respectively, demonstrating that the LETL temporal module contributes meaningful robustness beyond what efficiency tricks alone provide. On VisDrone2018 (Table 2), LETrack (66.1/87.2) beats AVTrack (65.3/86.0) and ORTrack (63.9/84.6), and notably outperforms MCITrack-T224 (62.5/79.4), a tracker specifically designed for temporal modeling, suggesting that MCITrack's multi-template strategy is suboptimal under UAV-specific challenges. In the efficiency comparison (Table 3), LETrack's 2.48G FLOPs are modestly higher than SGLATrack (1.68G) and ORTrack (1.54G) but dramatically lower than SeqTrack (65.86G) and ARTrack (40.33G), while still achieving 204 FPS—confirming that the LETL module adds only 0.09G FLOPs over the one-stream baseline (Table 4) yet yields the highest PPF score of 55, far exceeding dynamic templates (9.5), dense video sampling (8.7), and autoregressive queries (8.4).

## Relation to Prior Work

LETrack builds on the OSTrack one-stream ViT framework (DeiT-Tiny backbone, center-based prediction head) and augments it with a lightweight temporal learning module (LETL) that propagates and merges a small set of appearance tokens across frames. Conceptually, it departs from the two dominant temporal strategies in tracking—multi-template approaches (STARK, MixFormer, ODTrack) that add heavy computational overhead, and trajectory-token approaches (AQATrack, TemTrack) that compress global search-region information into a few queries—by instead propagating fine-grained local appearance tokens selected via attention and classification scores. The paper also introduces the PPF (precision per FLOP) metric, which has no direct precedent in the VOT literature, to isolate and quantify the marginal contribution of a temporal component independent of backbone or input-size changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PPF metric offers the tracking community a principled way to decouple temporal-module effectiveness from overall model scale, addressing a gap the authors identify as largely overlooked. The finding that established temporal strategies (ODTrack, EVPTrack, HIPTrack) achieve surprisingly low PPF scores—often below 10—while a minimal token-merge mechanism reaches 55, challenges the implicit assumption that more elaborate temporal modeling is necessary for robust tracking. Open questions include whether PPF generalizes to non-UAV tracking or to longer-horizon sequences where token-set staleness may degrade, and whether the fixed-size token budget (K=16) remains sufficient under extreme appearance drift. For UAV practitioners, the practical takeaway is that a 204-FPS tracker on Jetson TX2 with SOTA accuracy is achievable without the FLOP budgets of prior methods.
