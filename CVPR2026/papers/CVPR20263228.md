## Problem

Multi-view VLMs for autonomous driving process synchronized images from six surrounding cameras, generating thousands of visual tokens whose quadratic self-attention cost makes real-time deployment impractical. Existing token pruning methods are designed for single-image inputs, rely on attention weights (incompatible with Flash Attention), and ignore both the spatial-semantic diversity across views and the unequal contribution of different camera perspectives to driving decisions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263228/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes high-resolution images from six surround-view cameras (front, front-left, front-right, rear, rear-left, rear-right) along with a text prompt, and outputs a pruned subset of visual tokens (e.g., 10–25% of the original count) that are concatenated with text tokens for LLM generation. Crucially, the pruning is plug-and-play: it operates on the projected token embeddings after the vision encoder without requiring model retraining, attention map access, or architectural modification, making it directly compatible with Flash Attention and other efficient attention kernels.

## Method

Prune2Drive combines two complementary mechanisms. First, T-FPS (Token-wise Farthest Point Sampling) selects visual tokens by iteratively picking the token with maximum cosine distance to the already-chosen set, acting as a greedy k-center solver that maximizes semantic and spatial coverage rather than individual salience; this preserves informative but low-attention tokens that attention-based methods discard. Second, a view-adaptive pruning controller formulates per-camera retention ratios as a reward-penalty optimization (language-similarity reward minus total token-usage penalty) solved via TPE on a small validation subset, automatically allocating more tokens to task-critical views (e.g., front camera) and fewer to redundant ones. The entire ratio optimization converges in approximately 3 H100 GPU hours, and the T-FPS selection itself adds less than 0.1% overhead to total FLOPs. A theoretical analysis under a view-weighted Lipschitz continuity assumption proves that the two-level design (intra-view diversity maximization plus inter-view budget allocation) yields a tighter prediction-error bound than uniform-ratio random sampling at the same total token budget.

## Results

On the DriveLM benchmark (DriveMM model), Prune2Drive achieves a 58.3 average score when retaining 25% of visual tokens (↓75%), only 0.8 below the vanilla model's 59.1, while the best baseline (SparseVLM) reaches 57.4. Under aggressive 90% pruning, Prune2Drive maintains 57.4 average versus 54.1 for FastV and 55.9 for SparseVLM. On DriveLMM-o1, Prune2Drive scores 68.3 overall reasoning at 90% token reduction, outperforming all baselines by 1.3–3.5 points. In terms of efficiency, retaining only 10% of visual tokens yields a 6.40× prefilling speedup and reduces FLOPs to 13.4% of the original on DriveMM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Prune2Drive outperforms all four baselines (FastV, DART, SparseVLM, PACT) on every benchmark and both pruning ratios tested. On DriveLM at 75% pruning, Prune2Drive leads by 0.9 points over SparseVLM (58.3 vs. 57.4) and by 1.7 over FastV (58.3 vs. 56.6); at 90% pruning the gap widens to 1.5 over SparseVLM (57.4 vs. 55.9) and 3.3 over FastV (57.4 vs. 54.1). On DriveLMM-o1 at 90% pruning, Prune2Drive achieves 68.3 overall reasoning versus 67.4 for DART, 67.0 for PACT, 65.3 for FastV, and 64.8 for SparseVLM. Notably, Prune2Drive's Match score at 75% pruning (34.0) even exceeds the vanilla model (33.9), suggesting that diversity-aware selection can improve object identification over unpruned inference. The advantage is most pronounced under aggressive pruning (90%), where attention-based methods like FastV degrade sharply due to positional bias, while Prune2Drive's FPS-based selection maintains robust spatial coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the training-free visual token pruning line (FastV, DART, SparseVLM, PACT) from single-image VLMs to the multi-view autonomous driving setting, where six synchronized camera views create both spatial redundancy and view-dependent importance. It departs from the dominant attention-score-based selection paradigm by repurposing Farthest Point Sampling from point cloud processing as a diversity-aware token selector, and introduces an optimization-based view-adaptive pruning controller that allocates per-camera token budgets rather than applying a uniform ratio. The theoretical framing via Hausdorff distance and view-weighted Lipschitz continuity provides a formal justification absent from prior pruning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6.4× prefilling speedup with only ~3% average performance drop at 10% token retention is practically significant for real-time AD deployment, where latency budgets are tight and multi-view inputs multiply token counts. The plug-and-play design (no retraining, no attention-map access) ensures compatibility with Flash Attention and other efficient inference stacks, broadening applicability. Open questions include whether the view-adaptive ratios generalize across different camera configurations or driving scenarios, whether the 3 H100-hour optimization cost is acceptable for production pipelines, and whether the theoretical error bounds are tight enough to provide meaningful guarantees under aggressive pruning.
