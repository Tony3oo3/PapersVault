## Problem

VLMs struggle to ground answers in fine-grained visual evidence (e.g., thin polylines, irregular contours), and existing remedies—external cropping/masking tools or learned latent visual tokens at inference time—are shape-rigid, domain-specific, and add inference overhead. The paper asks whether perception can be shaped at training time using programmatically generated, question-conditioned visual cues that require no test-time cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263779/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference the model consumes a standard RGB image and a natural-language question and emits a free-form text answer, with no auxiliary visual tokens, crops, or tool calls. During training, each (image, question) pair is augmented with two programmatically rendered counterparts: an evidence-preserving view that retains only question-relevant chart elements and an evidence-ablated view that removes critical fine-grained cues while keeping global context. These paired views are synthesized by editing the executable chart-rendering code from the ECD corpus, yielding 13K high-quality training tuples without human annotation.

## Method

BiPS is a two-stage training curriculum integrated into the GRPO reinforcement-learning framework. Stage 1 (Consistency) minimizes a clipped KL divergence between the policy on the original image and a stop-gradient target computed on the evidence-preserving view, pulling probability mass toward evidence-supported answers and teaching coarse 'where-to-look' focus. Stage 2 (Separation) maximizes a clipped KL divergence between the policy on the original image and a stop-gradient target on the evidence-ablated view, pushing the model away from text-only shortcuts and enforcing fine-grained visual reliance. The coarse-to-fine ordering decouples the attractive and repulsive gradient signals to avoid the instability observed with joint or reversed optimization. A programmatic data pipeline edits ECD chart source code via an LLM arbitrator to generate semantically precise paired views, and an optional 39K-sample GRPO fine-tune on ViRL39k further boosts general reasoning.

## Results

BiPS-Chart-7B, trained on only 13K chart samples, improves the average score across eight benchmarks from 44.3 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) to 51.6 (+7.3%), with the largest gains on Evochart (+16.2) and ChartQAPro (+15.3). Adding 39K math-focused samples via standard GRPO yields BiPS-General-7B with an average of 52.5 (+8.2% over base), reaching 50.6 on CharXiv, 68.7 on Evochart, 75.0 on MathVista, and 65.7 on MMStar. Both variants surpass GPT-4o (avg 47.2) and all open-source baselines, including chart-specialized models trained on 100K–1.7M examples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiPS-Chart-7B (13K samples) outperforms all open-source baselines on average: it beats Qwen2.5-VL-7B by +7.3, InternVL3-8B by +7.0, DeepEyes-7B by +4.1, Chart-R1-7B (258K) by +6.1, and R1-OneVision-7B by +11.8. On chart-specific benchmarks, BiPS-Chart-7B achieves 49.4 on CharXiv and 51.9 on ChartQAPro, surpassing GPT-4o (47.1 / 37.7) and all chart-specialized models including TinyChart-3B (1.3M data) and BigCharts-R1-7B (1.7M data). BiPS-General-7B further extends these gains to general reasoning, reaching 75.0 on MathVista (vs. 68.2 base) and 65.7 on MMStar (vs. 62.1 base), while maintaining chart performance. The only model that exceeds BiPS on individual benchmarks is Claude-3.7-Sonnet (e.g., 64.2 on CharXiv, 74.5 on MathVista), but no average is reported for it. Notably, BiPS achieves these results with 13K–52K training samples, far fewer than Chart-R1 (258K), BigCharts-R1 (1.7M), or TinyChart (1.3M), highlighting strong data efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiPS departs from the dominant paradigm of injecting intermediate visual cues (crops, masks, bounding boxes, latent visual tokens) at inference time by repurposing such cues purely as training-time shaping signals within a GRPO reinforcement learning loop. The bidirectional KL design—pulling toward an evidence-preserving view and pushing away from an evidence-ablated view—extends the negative-perturbation idea in ChiP and PAPO (which use Gaussian noise or random masks) to semantically precise, question-conditioned counterparts generated programmatically from executable chart code. The programmatic data pipeline leveraging ECD's code-provenance is a practical contribution that avoids the shape rigidity and domain coupling of prior tool-based or latent-token approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that a VLM can internalize fine-grained visual grounding from only 13K programmatically generated chart examples, achieving +7.3% average improvement across eight benchmarks and surpassing chart-specialized models trained on 100K–1.7M samples, all without any inference-time overhead. This suggests that perception can be shaped more efficiently through targeted training signals than through large-scale data or test-time tool use. Open questions include whether the programmatic pipeline generalizes to non-code-generated visual domains (medical imaging, geometry diagrams, natural scenes), whether the approach transfers to larger or differently-architected VLMs, and how the method interacts with emerging test-time compute strategies.
