## Problem

Existing few-shot semantic segmentation methods rely on complex category-specific modeling—either explicit class prototypes or heavy pretrained MLLMs/diffusion models—leading to high computational cost and limited generalization under low-data regimes. This paper asks whether FSS can be reformulated as a composition of lightweight, interpretable probabilistic components that avoid direct class-prototype modeling altogether.

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image and one or more support images with binary masks as input, and produces a binary segmentation mask for the query image. EfficientSAM region proposals for the query are precomputed and cached to save GPU memory during training. The CALM module operates on region-level features (global average pooled vectors) rather than pixel-level dense predictions, and the final mask is assembled by merging SAM proposals whose combined local-global similarity exceeds a 0.5 threshold.

## Method

BPNet decomposes the FSS objective p(y|x,s,m) via Bayes' theorem into three tractable terms: a prior p(y|x) (EfficientSAM region proposals), a likelihood p(m|s,x,y), and a class-consistency p(s|x,y). The likelihood and consistency are jointly estimated by a single lightweight binary classifier (CALM) built on ResNet-18: the sigmoid output gives class-consistency, while backpropagating that probability through the support branch yields LayerCAM activations whose IoU with the support mask approximates the likelihood. The core region is selected by maximizing the product P_l·P_c across all SAM proposals. A Semantic Completion Module then merges fragmented proposals into a complete mask by combining local cosine similarity with a cross-attention-based global similarity (weighted by P_l and P_c) through a learnable scalar α. Training requires only binary labels (proposal–GT overlap > 0.7), avoiding the need for precise pixel-level supervision.

## Results

BPNet achieves state-of-the-art mIoU on both PASCAL-5i and COCO-20i under 1-shot and 5-shot settings. On PASCAL-5i, BPNet reaches 75.7% (1-shot) and 77.1% (5-shot) mean mIoU, surpassing the previous best LLaFS (73.5% / 75.6%) by 2.2 and 1.5 points respectively. On the more challenging COCO-20i, BPNet obtains 54.2% (1-shot) and 61.6% (5-shot), outperforming LLaFS (53.9% / 60.0%) and VRP-SAM (53.9% / –). The method also runs at 5.5 FPS with a ResNet-18 backbone, significantly faster than segmentation-head variants (1.2 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BPNet outperforms all eight baselines on every reported setting. Against the strongest competitor LLaFS (LLM-based), BPNet gains +2.2 mIoU on PASCAL-5i 1-shot (75.7 vs 73.5) and +1.6 on COCO-20i 5-shot (61.6 vs 60.0). Compared to the SAM-based VRP-SAM, BPNet improves by +3.8 mIoU on PASCAL-5i 1-shot (75.7 vs 71.9) and +0.3 on COCO-20i 1-shot (54.2 vs 53.9); VRP-SAM does not report 5-shot results. Against the plug-in PAHNet, BPNet leads by +4.2 (PASCAL-5i 1-shot) and +2.4 (COCO-20i 5-shot). The diffusion-based methods (DiffSeg, AlignDiff) trail BPNet by 5–7 mIoU on PASCAL-5i and 5–8 mIoU on COCO-20i, while the classic CNN-based baselines (HDMNet, SCCAN, ABCB) lag by 5–9 mIoU, confirming that the Bayesian decomposition with EfficientSAM proposals and the lightweight CALM classifier surpasses both prototype-matching and large-model approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

BPNet departs from the dominant prototype-matching paradigm in FSS (HDMNet, SCCAN, ABCB) and the recent trend of leveraging large pretrained models (LLaFS, DiffSeg, AlignDiff) by reformulating the task through a Bayesian decomposition into a prior, a likelihood, and a class-consistency term. The prior is instantiated with EfficientSAM for class-agnostic structural proposals, while the likelihood and consistency terms are jointly estimated by a lightweight binary classifier (CALM) that uses LayerCAM-style gradient-based activation maps rather than explicit segmentation. The Semantic Completion Module draws on cross-attention mechanisms to merge fragmented SAM proposals, extending the idea that SAM alone is insufficient for semantic-level segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework demonstrates that a ResNet-18 backbone with a binary classification head can match or exceed methods built on diffusion models or large language models, suggesting that the probabilistic decomposition—not raw model capacity—drives few-shot segmentation quality. The near-identical performance across ResNet-18, ResNet-101, and DenseNet-121 backbones is a notable robustness property that could simplify deployment. However, the approach is limited to the two standard FSS benchmarks, and the acknowledged failure mode (SAM proposals mixing foreground and background) remains unresolved; whether the Bayesian decomposition generalizes to denser or more open-vocabulary settings is an open question.
