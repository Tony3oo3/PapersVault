## Problem

Existing Generalist Anomaly Detection methods (e.g., ResAD) directly model the distribution of residual features to discriminate anomalies, but residuals are inconsistent with instance features: subtle defects can yield small residuals (false negatives) while normal diversity can produce large residuals (false positives). No prior work re-purposes residuals as a guidance signal to learn instance-level normality patterns from the support set, thereby avoiding the risks of direct residual distribution modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262901/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image and K normal support images (K ∈ {1,2,4,8}) from the same class, all resized to 448×448 and processed by a frozen ViT-Small/14 DINOv2 encoder to extract patch-level features. It produces a pixel-level anomaly score map S₃ (combining a normality-similarity map and a nearest-neighbor distance map) and an image-level anomaly score via max-pooling of S₃. The episodic structure means the model dynamically generates class-specific normality proxies at inference time for each new query–support pair.

## Method

The framework introduces two attention-based modules that re-purpose residuals as guidance rather than modeling their distribution. The Residual Feature Learning (RFL) module uses M=25 learnable proxies as queries in a cross-attention block over residual features (computed via NN search and subtraction) to produce residual proxies capturing diverse residual patterns. The Normality Learning from Support (NLS) module then performs a residual-guided cross-attention where residual proxies serve as queries, residuals serve as keys (encoding the residual–instance correspondence), and mean support features serve as values, yielding normality proxies in the instance feature space. Anomaly localization is achieved by computing cosine similarity between these normality proxies and query patch features, combined with a standard NN-distance map. Training uses episodic meta-learning with Focal + Dice loss for pixel-level localization and BCE loss for image-level classification, updating only the two attention modules (24.4M total parameters) while keeping the backbone frozen.

## Results

On MVTec AD (trained on VisA), the method achieves 97.6 image-level AUROC and 97.1 pixel-level AUROC in the 4-shot setting, outperforming the next best method PromptAD (96.6 / 96.5) and ResAD (90.7 / 95.8). On VisA (trained on MVTec AD), it reaches 90.3 image-level AUROC and 97.8 pixel-level AUROC at 4-shot, surpassing PromptAD (89.1 / 97.4) and WinCLIP (87.3 / 97.2). On the cross-domain BraTS benchmark (trained on MVTec AD), the method attains 85.4 image-level and 97.6 pixel-level AUROC at 4-shot, substantially exceeding ResAD (74.9 / 94.3) and WinCLIP (67.3 / 93.2). The method also uses only 24.4M parameters and runs at 15.8 FPS, compared to 59.2M / 7.8 FPS for ResAD and 117.5M / 1.2 FPS for InCTRL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method outperforms all nine baselines across both MVTec AD and VisA in every shot setting. On MVTec AD (4-shot), it leads PromptAD by +1.0 I-AUROC (97.6 vs 96.6) and +0.6 P-AUROC (97.1 vs 96.5), and beats ResAD by a wide margin of +6.9 I-AUROC (97.6 vs 90.7). On VisA (4-shot), it surpasses PromptAD by +1.2 I-AUROC (90.3 vs 89.1) and ResAD by +1.0 (90.3 vs 89.3). On the cross-domain BraTS benchmark (4-shot), the advantage is most pronounced: the method achieves 85.4 image-level AUROC versus 74.9 for ResAD (+10.5), 76.9 for InCTRL (+8.5), and 67.3 for WinCLIP (+18.1), while also reaching 97.6 pixel-level AUROC versus 94.3 for ResAD. The ablation (Variant B) confirms that using residual proxies directly to hunt normality actually degrades performance (90.4 vs 93.2 baseline I-AUROC on MVTec AD), validating the paper's core claim that residual-instance inconsistency makes direct residual modeling risky.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the residual-based Generalist Anomaly Detection (GAD) line initiated by InCTRL (CVPR 2024) and ResAD (NeurIPS 2024), both of which compute query-to-support residuals via nearest-neighbor subtraction to build a class-invariant space. The key departure is conceptual: rather than modeling the residual distribution directly (ResAD) or using residual distance as the anomaly score (InCTRL), this work re-purposes residuals as a *guidance signal* that steers the learning of instance-level normality from support features through two attention modules (RFL and NLS). This positions the paper as a corrective to the residual-distribution paradigm, arguing that residuals are an unreliable proxy for abnormality due to their inconsistency with instance features.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is notable: with only 24.4M parameters, 0.5 hours of training, and 15.8 FPS inference, the method is dramatically more efficient than ResAD (59.2M params, 20.6h training) while outperforming it across all benchmarks, making GAD more accessible for deployment. The cross-domain result (industrial MVTec → medical BraTS) demonstrates that the residual-guided normality learning transfers beyond industrial inspection. Open questions include whether the frozen ViT-Small/DINOv2 encoder is a bottleneck for more complex anomaly types, whether the episodic structure with K normal supports scales to open-world settings with no reliable support, and whether the pixel-level localization gap (e.g., 97.6 AUROC vs. 92.5 PRO on MVTec) can be closed with higher-resolution feature extraction.
