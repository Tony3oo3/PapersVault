## Problem

Existing segmentation methods that leverage pretrained diffusion models treat them as implicit feature backbones, extracting hidden representations via diffusion inversion or activation aggregation and feeding them into separate task-specific decoders. This indirect pipeline creates a representational mismatch—diffusion models are pretrained on low-level VAE feature distributions while segmentation requires compact semantic-level predictions—and complicates the workflow. The paper argues that segmentation should instead be trained directly under the original generative objective, but identifies a key obstacle: VAE latents of binary masks are sharply distributed, noise-robust, and linearly separable, fundamentally different from natural image latents, making naive joint training infeasible.

## Input / Output

For segmentation, the model takes an RGB image plus a natural-language instruction (e.g., 'Please segment the {target} in the image'), encoded jointly by a frozen Qwen2.5-VL-7B VLM whose final-layer hidden states serve as cross-attention conditioning. The VAE-encoded latent of the input image is additionally concatenated with noise as a low-level shortcut into the DiT, with its time embedding set to zero to indicate a clean signal. For generation, only a text prompt is provided. The segmentation output is a binary mask produced in a single forward pass at t=1 (no iterative denoising), decoded by the VAE decoder; the generation output is a standard RGB image produced via multi-step flow-matching sampling.

## Method

GenMask trains a pretrained WAN-2.1 DiT (1.3B) end-to-end under a unified rectified-flow objective to generate either colorful images or black-and-white masks, with the sole architectural change being the replacement of umT5 with Qwen2.5-VL-7B as the instruction encoder and the injection of the input image's VAE latent as a low-level shortcut. The core technical contribution is a task-specific timestep sampling strategy: segmentation samples from an extreme long-tailed distribution p(t) = 2a²t/(t²+a²)² with a=0.05, concentrating 90% of training mass in the high-noise region (t < 0.85), while generation uses the standard SD3 logit-normal schedule emphasizing intermediate noise. This separation is motivated by the finding that binary mask VAE latents remain linearly separable under low-to-moderate noise and only collapse at high noise levels, so the model must learn mask structure primarily from the high-noise regime. Training uses MSE loss in VAE space (avoiding backpropagation through the frozen VAE decoder), no classifier-free guidance for segmentation (since it is deterministic), and a 1:1 mix of segmentation and generation data. At inference, segmentation requires only one DiT forward pass at t=1, yielding a deterministic mask without multi-step denoising.

## Results

On referring segmentation, GenMask achieves state-of-the-art oIoU of 83.3/79.4 (RefCOCO test A/B), 78.7/68.1 (RefCOCO+), and 75.6/76.5 (RefCOCO-g), surpassing the previous best (UNINEXT-L, GLaMM, u-LLaVA) by 0.7–3.5 points. On mIoU, it reaches 83.7/80.7, 80.0/73.1, and 77.2/78.2, outperforming C3VG by 0.8–1.1 points across all splits. On ReasonSeg, GenMask attains test gIoU 52.3 and cIoU 45.8, exceeding the finetuned LISA* (47.3/34.1) by 5.0 and 11.7 points respectively, though it trails LISA* slightly on the validation set (51.1 vs. 52.9 gIoU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenMask outperforms all 29 compared baselines on every RefCOCO split under both oIoU and mIoU metrics. On oIoU, it leads UNINEXT-L (82.6/77.8) by 0.7/1.6 on RefCOCO, GLaMM (78.7/64.6) by 0.0/3.5 on RefCOCO+, and u-LLaVA (74.8/75.6) by 0.8/0.9 on RefCOCO-g. On mIoU, it surpasses C3VG (82.9/79.1, 79.6/72.4, 76.3/77.1) by 0.8/1.6, 0.4/0.7, and 0.9/1.1 respectively. On ReasonSeg, GenMask achieves the best test-set performance (gIoU 52.3, cIoU 45.8), beating finetuned LISA* (47.3/34.1) by a substantial margin, though it trails LISA* on the validation set (51.1 vs. 52.9 gIoU). Notably, GenMask is the only method that simultaneously reports competitive results on both oIoU and mIoU benchmarks, whereas most prior methods specialize in one metric family.

## Relation to Prior Work

GenMask departs from the dominant paradigm of using pretrained diffusion models as implicit feature backbones for segmentation (via diffusion inversion, activation aggregation, or feature distillation) by instead training the DiT to directly generate binary masks under the original generative objective. It builds on the WAN-2.1 DiT architecture and the rectified flow / flow matching framework, replacing the text-only umT5 encoder with Qwen2.5-VL to handle joint image-text conditioning. The key conceptual departure is the timestep sampling strategy: the authors identify that VAE latents of binary masks are sharply distributed and linearly separable (unlike natural image latents), and introduce an extreme long-tailed noise schedule for masks that is complementary to the logit-normal schedule used for image generation, enabling conflict-free joint training within a single DiT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying segmentation and generation under one generative objective with no architectural changes, GenMask eliminates the multi-stage feature-extraction pipelines that have complicated prior diffusion-based segmentation methods and closes the optimization gap between pretraining and downstream adaptation. The one-step deterministic inference (fixing t=1) is practically significant, making the model deployable without iterative denoising. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger DiT backbones, whether the VAE shortcut can be replaced by learned low-level features, and whether the unified generative paradigm extends cleanly to medical or audio-visual segmentation as the authors suggest.
