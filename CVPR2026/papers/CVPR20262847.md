## Problem

Existing online video super-resolution methods rely on computationally expensive optical flow or unconstrained deformable offsets for frame alignment, and process all frames with uniform compute, making real-time inference infeasible at higher resolutions such as 2K. Moreover, these methods ignore compressed-domain information (motion vectors, residual maps, frame types) that is already available in the H.264 bitstream at essentially zero cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262847/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 4×-downsampled LR frames encoded with H.264 in capped-CRF mode (CRF 18/23/28), and additionally parses the bitstream to extract per-frame motion vectors, residual maps, and frame type labels as auxiliary inputs. It produces the corresponding HR frame in a strictly causal (online) setting using only past and current frames, targeting ≥60 FPS for gaming real-time at 320×180 LR input and ≥24 FPS at 2K resolution.

## Method

CDA-VSR is a recurrent CNN that exploits three types of compressed-domain priors through dedicated modules. The MV-guided Deformable Alignment (MVGDA) module warps previous-frame features using motion vectors for coarse alignment, then applies a deformable convolution whose offsets are initialized from the MVs and refined by a lightweight network predicting only local residual offsets, avoiding the cost of full optical flow. The Residual Map Gated Fusion (RMGF) module transforms the bitstream residual map into a spatial gating map via a small network, which element-wise modulates aligned temporal features to suppress misaligned regions before channel-wise fusion with current-frame features. The Frame-Type-Aware Reconstruction (FTAR) module routes I-frames through a 24-residual-block fine-grained branch and P-frames through a 12-block lightweight branch, allocating compute where it matters most. The full model is trained end-to-end with Charbonnier loss on 15-frame clips from REDS, achieving 93 FPS at 320×180 while surpassing TMP by up to 0.13 dB PSNR.

## Results

On the REDS4 dataset (4× upscaling, H.264 CRF=18/23/28), CDA-VSR achieves average PSNR of 27.76 / 26.70 / 25.30 dB and SSIM of 0.7779 / 0.7384 / 0.6869, outperforming the SOTA method TMP by up to +0.13 dB while running at 93 FPS (10.8 ms) versus TMP's 45 FPS (22.2 ms) — more than 2× faster. On the Inter4K dataset, CDA-VSR leads at all resolutions (720p: 27.13 dB, 1080p: 28.64 dB, 2K: 29.98 dB) and is the only method exceeding 24 FPS at 2K resolution (25.1 FPS vs. 11.4 FPS for TMP). CDA-VSR is the sole method achieving gaming real-time (≥60 FPS) on REDS4, with the lowest MACs (78G) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CDA-VSR outperforms all seven baselines on PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, runtime, and MACs across all three compression levels. Against the closest competitor TMP [53], CDA-VSR gains +0.13 dB PSNR at CRF18, +0.12 dB at CRF23, and +0.13 dB at CRF28, while more than doubling the inference speed (93 vs. 45 FPS). Against flow-based methods (SSL-uni, KSNet-uni), the PSNR advantage is +0.22 dB and +0.18 dB respectively at CRF18, with roughly 2× speedup. Against implicit alignment methods (RRN, RSDN), the gains are larger (+0.66 dB and +0.65 dB at CRF18) due to their weaker handling of large motion. On the Inter4K dataset at 2K resolution, CDA-VSR leads TMP by +0.22 dB PSNR and runs at 25.1 FPS while TMP drops to 11.4 FPS, confirming that the efficiency advantage grows with resolution. CDA-VSR is the only method achieving gaming real-time (≥60 FPS) on REDS4 and the only one maintaining film real-time (≥24 FPS) at 2K resolution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of recurrent online VSR methods (RRN, RSDN, DAP, TMP, KSNet) by introducing compressed-domain priors—motion vectors, residual maps, and frame types—extracted directly from the H.264 bitstream. Prior compressed-domain VSR work (CDVSR, CIAF, CAVSR) demonstrated the value of bitstream information but was not designed for causal, real-time inference, while prior online VSR methods relied solely on decoded LR frames and expensive optical-flow or deformable-convolution alignment. CDA-VSR's key departure is designing a dedicated module for each type of compressed-domain signal (MVGDA for motion vectors, RMGF for residual maps, FTAR for frame types) rather than using them generically, and explicitly satisfying the causality and latency constraints of online streaming.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2×+ speedup at comparable or slightly better quality is practically significant for bandwidth-limited streaming (video conferencing, live streaming) where GPU budgets are tight, especially at 2K resolutions where all prior methods fall below real-time thresholds. The reliance on codec-specific information (H.264 motion vectors, residual maps, I/P frame types) limits generalizability to other codecs (HEVC, AV1) and to B-frame-heavy GOP structures, which the authors acknowledge by excluding B-frames. Open questions include whether the compressed-domain priors remain informative under very aggressive compression (CRF > 30), whether the approach transfers to other video restoration tasks (denoising, deblurring), and how performance degrades when the encoder's motion estimation is itself poor.
