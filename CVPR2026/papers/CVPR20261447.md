## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models face a fundamental trade-off: text-only approaches (guidance-based, attention-optimization, closed-form editing) fail to fully suppress target concepts under semantically related or adversarial prompts, while image-assisted methods risk over-erasing semantically unrelated content that shares similar visual patterns. No prior method simultaneously achieves comprehensive erasure across diverse linguistic expressions and preserves morphologically or contextually related benign concepts.

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N semantically related text prompts (auto-generated via GPT-5.0 from a base keyword) and a set of reference images (synthesized by a clean diffusion model using concept-specific prompts). Prompts are encoded into a prompt bank and consumed as Dirichlet-weighted convex combinations during training; reference images are VAE-encoded, noised at random timesteps, resized to three spatial scales (1.0, 0.75, 0.5), and flattened into token sequences. The output is an edited diffusion model (modified U-Net plus a jointly trained transformer encoder) that suppresses the target concept while retaining generation quality for unrelated prompts.

## Method

TICoE combines two complementary mechanisms: (1) a Continuous Convex Concept Manifold (CCCM) that samples interpolated text embeddings as Dirichlet-distributed convex combinations of N prompt encodings, ensuring the conditioning embedding stays within the semantic hull of the target concept and resists reactivation under adversarial phrasing; and (2) Hierarchical Visual Representation Learning (HVRL) that encodes noised reference-image latents at multiple spatial scales, concatenates them into a single token sequence, processes them through jointly trained transformer encoder layers, and fuses the result back into the latent via a residual connection. During training, the trainable U-Net is supervised to match a classifier-free-guidance reference target constructed with a negative guidance weight γ from the frozen pre-trained model, driving suppression of the target concept while a constraint on benign-prompt likelihood prevents over-erasure. The transformer encoder and U-Net are optimized jointly, allowing adaptive cross-scale visual disambiguation.

## Results

On the 'Erase gun' task, TICoE achieves the best erasure strength with ASR=0.00, UDA=0.02, and P4D=0.04, while also attaining the lowest FID (30.86) among all methods, indicating superior generation fidelity. On the MCP metric for semantically related concepts (e.g., 'camera' when erasing 'gun'), TICoE scores 92.06%, closely matching the clean Stable Diffusion baseline (92.54%) and substantially outperforming Co-Erasing (39.68%) and ESD (68.25%). Across the I2P nudity-erasure task, TICoE achieves near-zero failure counts in sensitive exposure categories, whereas prior methods retain residual activations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TICoE dominates all five baselines on erasure strength: it reduces UDA from 0.20 (ESD) and 0.64 (FMN) to 0.02, and P4D from 0.47 (ESD) to 0.04, while matching Co-Erasing's perfect ASR of 0.00. On generation fidelity (FID), TICoE achieves 30.86, beating ESD (31.76), SPM (33.43), FMN (34.46), UCE (35.56), and Co-Erasing (35.94) by 0.9–5.1 points. The CLIP score (0.304) is competitive but slightly below UCE (0.312) and FMN/SPM (0.310), suggesting a minor trade-off in general prompt-image alignment. The critical differentiator is the MCP metric: when erasing 'gun', TICoE preserves 'camera' at 92.06% (near the 92.54% clean baseline), whereas Co-Erasing drops to 39.68% and ESD to 68.25%, confirming that image-assisted methods without hierarchical representation suffer severe over-erasure of visually similar concepts. Similarly, for 'tench' erasure, TICoE retains 'dolphin' at 98.18% versus 87.27% (Co-Erasing) and 94.54% (ESD).

## Relation to Prior Work

TICoE extends the text-image collaborative erasing paradigm introduced by Co-Erasing [24], which first combined reference images with textual prompts to improve erasure coverage. The key departure is addressing Co-Erasing's visual entanglement problem through two structured mechanisms: a Continuous Convex Concept Manifold (Dirichlet-weighted interpolation over GPT-generated prompt sets) to ensure semantic completeness without out-of-distribution extrapolation, and Hierarchical Visual Representation Learning (multi-scale transformer encoding of reference latents) to disentangle causally relevant visual features from merely correlated ones. This positions TICoE as a refinement of the text-only → image-assisted → text-image collaborative lineage, adding geometric structure (convexity) and scale-awareness to what was previously a flat prompt-image pairing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MCP metric makes the over-erasure problem quantitatively visible in a way prior evaluations (FID, CLIP on COCO-10k) could not, revealing that image-assisted methods like Co-Erasing can destroy 50–60% of semantically related content (e.g., camera when erasing gun). This reframes the evaluation landscape for concept erasure and provides a practical tool for practitioners who need to verify that erasure hasn't collateral-damaged adjacent concepts. Open questions include whether the convex manifold approach generalizes to transformer-based diffusion backbones (SD3, DiT), how it scales to dozens of simultaneous erasures, and whether the GPT-5.0 dependency for prompt expansion introduces its own biases or failure modes for novel or culturally specific concepts.
