## Problem

Imaging through colored glass introduces two coupled degradations—ghost shadows from multi-bounce internal reflections and color bias from wavelength-selective absorption—that existing reflection-removal and color-correction methods fail to address jointly. Prior approaches either assume a simple linear mixture of reflection and transmission layers or use polarization only as an auxiliary weighting, without explicitly modeling how polarized light propagates, reflects, and is absorbed within the glass medium.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262939/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network consumes four polarization intensity images at 0°, 45°, 90°, and 135° (256×256 pixels) captured through colored glass and produces the corresponding four-angle reflection-free transmission images, preserving the full Stokes-vector structure rather than collapsing to RGB. This polarization-preserving output enables direct downstream use in tasks such as Shape-from-Polarization without additional estimation. Training is supervised with paired data: the input is the mixed observation and the target is the glass-free ground truth captured from the identical scene setup.

## Method

The core contribution is the Polarization State Tracing Model (PSTM), a physics-based imaging model that traces polarized light along up to four propagation paths (first- and second-order reflections and transmissions) through a planar dielectric slab, applying Fresnel Muller matrices at each interface and Beer-Lambert exponential attenuation for wavelength-selective absorption. Guided by PSTM, the PANet architecture is a U-shaped Transformer (Uformer backbone) whose bottleneck PFCubic Block integrates three complementary mechanisms: PFC-MSA performs multi-head self-attention jointly over spatial locations and polarization channels within small cubes; Channel Ring Attention (CRA) models the cyclic 4-angle polarization correlation via a circulant rank-constrained 4×4 attention matrix, reflecting the periodic intensity relation I_θ = ½(S₀ + S₁cos2θ + S₂sin2θ); and LAFN refines fine spatial details via local attention and feed-forward filtering. The network is trained end-to-end with a weighted L1 + SSIM loss on both PSTM-synthetic pairs (1,400 train / 160 test) and the newly constructed real-world GlassPol dataset (500 train / 200 test, 32 scenes under diverse colored glass).

## Results

On the real-world GlassPol dataset, PANet achieves PSNR 31.11 dB, SSIM 0.895, and LPIPS 0.094, outperforming the strongest baseline PolarFree (28.03 dB, 0.871, 0.118) by approximately 3.1 dB in PSNR. On the PSTM-synthetic dataset, the method reaches PSNR 32.80 dB, SSIM 0.947, and LPIPS 0.109, surpassing the next-best method IBCLN (26.83 dB) by nearly 6 dB. The method consistently leads on all three metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) across both datasets, with the largest margins observed against RGB-only baselines that lack physical polarization modeling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PANet outperforms all seven baselines on every metric across both the synthetic and real GlassPol datasets. The closest competitor is PolarFree [44], the only other polarization-based method evaluated on GlassPol, which achieves 28.03 dB PSNR and 0.118 LPIPS; PANet improves by 3.08 dB in PSNR and reduces LPIPS by 0.024, attributed to explicit multi-path reflection modeling and wavelength-selective absorption in PSTM. Among RGB-based methods, DSRNet [13] is the strongest at 24.85 dB PSNR, still trailing PANet by 6.26 dB, while DExNet [14] (23.83 dB) and IBCLN [22] (22.21 dB) lag further behind. The other polarization baseline, Lei et al. [19], scores only 19.60 dB PSNR because it uses simplified linear polarization decomposition without modeling absorption. The paper notes that PolarFree's reliance on intensity-based normalization without multi-path reflection modeling causes residual ghosting and color shifts under strong absorption, a failure mode PANet avoids through its physically grounded PSTM formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant reflection-removal paradigm that treats the observed image as a simple linear mixture of reflection and transmission layers, and from polarization-based methods (Lei et al., PolarFree) that use polarization only as an auxiliary weighting cue. Instead, it repurposes classical polarization optics—Fresnel Muller matrices, Stokes vector propagation, and Beer-Lambert absorption—into a unified imaging model (PSTM) that explicitly traces multi-bounce polarized light through a wavelength-selective absorbing slab. The network backbone is a U-shaped Transformer (Uformer) augmented with a novel Channel Ring Attention module that encodes the cyclic structure of the four polarization angles, a design choice motivated directly by the physics rather than by empirical feature engineering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By jointly modeling multi-path reflection and wavelength-selective absorption, the paper addresses a degradation mode (colored glass) that prior RGB and polarization methods handle poorly, and the recovered polarization field enables downstream tasks like Shape-from-Polarization without additional capture. The GlassPol dataset (700 real paired samples across diverse glass types) fills a gap in publicly available benchmarks for this setting. Open questions include whether the planar-slab, optically-homogeneous, two-bounce assumptions hold for curved, laminated, or non-uniform glass, and whether the PSTM framework generalizes to multi-pane or multi-material scenarios. The small real-world test set (200 images) and self-generated synthetic data limit confidence in the reported margins.
