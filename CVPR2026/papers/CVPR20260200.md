## Problem

Existing dynamic reconstruction methods require dense temporal coverage (multi-view or monocular video at every time step), making them inapplicable to real-world sparse observations such as security camera feeds where only a handful of posed images from arbitrary viewpoints are available. Under such sparse supervision, temporal correspondences are difficult to establish and appearance changes dramatically, rendering standard deformation-based approaches ill-posed and producing noisy, structurally inconsistent results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260200/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a set of sparse posed RGB images (one per time step, up to 20× fewer frames than standard methods, each from an arbitrary viewpoint), a noisy skeleton graph providing only 3D node positions and parent-child connectivity at the first frame, and an initial static 3D reconstruction (from multi-view optimization or a single image via diffusion prior). It outputs a continuous 4D deformation field over the canonical 3D Gaussians, enabling novel view synthesis at any intermediate time step without direct observations. Notably, the multi-view initialization can be replaced by a single reference image optimized with Score Distillation Sampling from a pre-trained 2D diffusion model (Zero-1-to-3).

## Method

SV-GS learns a skeleton-driven deformation field over a fixed canonical 3DGS representation, decomposed into three components: (1) a time-dependent MLP that predicts per-joint local rotations (quaternions) and root translation, propagated to global poses via forward kinematics; (2) a learnable Linear Blend Skinning layer where each bone's influence is modeled as a Radial Basis Function with learnable per-bone radii, refined by a position-dependent correction MLP to handle noisy skeleton input; and (3) a pose-dependent detail deformation MLP that adds small per-Gaussian offsets for fine-grained non-rigid motion. The key design choice is that only the joint pose estimator depends on time, while the skinning weights and detail field are time-invariant, enabling smooth test-time interpolation of joint poses at unseen time steps while preserving learned geometric details. Training jointly optimizes all deformation parameters (with canonical Gaussians frozen) by minimizing a perceptual loss (L1 + D-SSIM) plus a temporal Laplacian regularization on joint poses and an L2 penalty on detail offsets, requiring only 40k steps on a single GPU.

## Results

On the D-NeRF dataset downsampled to 11 frames (up to 20× fewer than the original), SV-GS achieves 27.75 PSNR, 0.950 SSIM, and 5.79 LPIPS (×100), outperforming all baselines by a wide margin. The largest PSNR gap is against SK-GS (19.43), a 8.3 dB improvement, and against 4DGS (21.70), a 6.05 dB improvement. On the real-world ZJU-MoCap dataset, SV-GS using only 1/10 of the original frames achieves 28.13 PSNR, surpassing AP-NeRF (25.62) which uses the full monocular video, though it remains below RigGS (33.54) which also uses the full video.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SV-GS outperforms all four baselines on every metric across the D-NeRF and DG-Mesh synthetic datasets. Against 4DGS (which uses no structural constraint), SV-GS gains 6.05 dB PSNR (27.75 vs. 21.70) and reduces LPIPS from 7.85 to 5.79, as 4DGS produces diverging deformations that fail to preserve object structure under sparse supervision. Against SK-GS, the gap is even larger at 8.32 dB PSNR (27.75 vs. 19.43), since SK-GS's learned skeleton and skinning weights become inaccurate with only 11 frames. Against RigGS, SV-GS leads by 3.52 dB PSNR (27.75 vs. 24.23); when RigGS is modified to use the same noisy skeleton input (RigGS†), its performance drops further to 22.63 PSNR and 13.82 LPIPS, confirming that without SV-GS's RBF-based skinning and correction field, the noisy skeleton leads to unstable deformations. On the real-world ZJU-MoCap dataset, SV-GS with 10× fewer frames (28.13 PSNR) surpasses AP-NeRF (25.62 PSNR) which uses the full video, but remains below RigGS (33.54 PSNR) which also uses the full video, indicating that the skeleton-driven approach narrows but does not fully close the gap to dense-video methods on real data.

## Relation to Prior Work

SV-GS builds directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting as its scene representation and draws from the skeleton-driven deformation line of work (SK-GS, RigGS, SC-GS) that uses LBS to model articulated motion. The key conceptual departure is operating under sparse temporal observations (up to 20× fewer frames) from arbitrary viewpoints rather than the dense monocular video assumed by all prior skeleton-driven methods. Additionally, the paper connects to the generative-prior trend (Zero-1-to-3, SDS loss) by showing that multi-view initialization can be replaced with a single-image diffusion-based reconstruction, a practical relaxation not explored in prior dynamic reconstruction work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world surveillance and security-camera scenarios where observations are sparse, from arbitrary viewpoints, and the target exhibits self-occlusion. The 34% PSNR improvement over SOTA under sparse observations and the demonstration that diffusion-based initialization can replace multi-view capture make the approach more deployable in the wild. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to non-articulated or highly non-rigid deformations, whether the diffusion prior can be made more robust to severe self-occlusion, and whether category-specific priors or skeleton-conditioned generative models could further improve motion estimation under extreme sparsity.
