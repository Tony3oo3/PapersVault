## Problem

In federated graph learning, random structural noise (missing and spurious edges) in client graphs disrupts GNN message-passing, causing corrupted clients to inject harmful knowledge into the global model while simultaneously degrading local inference on those same clients. Existing robust FL methods target adversarial attacks or natural topological heterogeneity, leaving this prevalent form of non-malicious structural corruption undetected and unaddressed.

## Input / Output

Each client provides node feature matrices X^k ∈ R^{N_k×f} and a local adjacency matrix A^k, from which the structural fidelity metric S_ide^k is computed via the Laplacian quadratic form D^TLD/D^TD without exposing raw topology to the server. The global model parameters θ are used to generate node embeddings H^k for the feature similarity matrix C^k that drives edge pruning and reconnection. Outputs include per-node class logits, a repaired adjacency matrix Ã^k per client, and reliability-aware aggregation weights w_k that modulate each client's contribution to the global update.

## Method

FedSDR combines two synergistic components. SNAA detects structural corruption via a spectral fidelity metric S_ide^k = ⟨D^{kT}L^kD^k, 1⟩_F / ⟨D^{kT}D^k, 1⟩_F, which quantifies total degree discrepancy across connected edges; random noise homogenizes the degree distribution toward an Erdős–Rényi state, lowering this metric. Corrupted clients are then down-weighted through a negative exponential of their normalized noise bias δ_k, smoothly redistributing aggregation mass toward structurally clean clients. RLSR repairs local graphs by computing a cosine-similarity matrix C^k from global model embeddings, pruning existing edges whose similarity falls below the α-quantile threshold τ_p^k, and reconnecting an equal number of non-edges whose similarity exceeds τ_r^k, thereby replacing spurious connections with feature-consistent ones while preserving valid local structure. The two components form a feedback loop: SNAA produces a cleaner global model whose embeddings better guide RLSR, and RLSR restores spectral properties that improve subsequent SNAA detection.

## Results

FedSDR achieves the best average test accuracy across all seven datasets under high-structural-noise settings (corruption ratio 1, noise extent 0.5). Headline numbers include 82.57% on PubMed, 89.37% on Coauthor-Physics, 48.33% on Roman-empire, 47.41% on ogbn-mag, and 78.57% on ogbn-products. The largest absolute gains over the strongest baseline appear on heterophilic and large-scale datasets: +5.82 pp on Roman-empire (vs. MOON at 42.51%) and +5.91 pp on ogbn-products (vs. FedAvg at 72.66%). Notably, simple baselines like FedAvg and FedProx outperform several complex robust-FL methods (e.g., Scaffold, FedProto, FedTAD) under structural noise, underscoring how disruptive the corruption is to existing approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedSDR outperforms all 17 baselines on every dataset. The most striking margins are on heterophilic and large-scale graphs: +5.82 pp over MOON (42.51%) on Roman-empire, +4.45 pp over MOON (42.96%) on ogbn-mag, and +5.91 pp over FedAvg (72.66%) on ogbn-products. On homophilic datasets the gains are more modest but consistent: +4.16 pp over FedAvg on PubMed, +1.05 pp over FedGTA on Coauthor-CS, and +2.18 pp over AdaFGL on Coauthor-Physics. A notable finding is that simple baselines (FedAvg, FedProx) substantially outperform complex robust-FL methods such as Scaffold (41.76% vs. 78.41% on PubMed), FedProto (75.24%), and FedTAD (which OOMs on three datasets), indicating that structural noise disrupts the assumptions underlying those methods. Among structure-aware FGL baselines, FedGTA and FGSSL are the closest competitors on homophilic data, while MOON leads on heterophilic data—but all are surpassed by FedSDR. The ablation (Table 2) confirms both SNAA and RLSR contribute independently, with the full combination yielding the best results (e.g., 82.57% vs. 81.82% for SNAA-only and 82.26% for RLSR-only on PubMed).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a distinct problem space within federated graph learning by identifying random structural noise (missing and spurious edges from data imperfections) as fundamentally different from both adversarial attacks and natural topological heterogeneity. It departs from robust FL methods (FedTGE, RHFL) that target Byzantine or backdoor threats, and from heterogeneity-handling approaches (FedATH, AdaFGL) that assume originally intact topology, arguing that structural noise 'masquerades as benign heterogeneity' and evades both. Conceptually, it repurposes spectral graph theory (Laplacian quadratic forms, degree-discrepancy analysis) from the GNN architecture literature into a federated aggregation and graph-repair context, where no prior work had applied spectral invariants for client-level noise detection. The RLSR component draws on feature-similarity-based graph repair ideas but uniquely uses the healthy global model's embeddings as the repair signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and previously unformalized failure mode in deployed federated graph systems—random edge corruption from data collection imperfections (e.g., fake social-network profiles, sensor dropout)—and provides a clean, privacy-preserving detection-and-repair pipeline. The spectral fidelity metric is a lightweight, locally computable signal that avoids exposing raw topology, making it deployable in privacy-sensitive settings. Limitations include reliance on random add-and-delete corruption (not correlated or adversarial structural damage), the Louvain partitioning assumption for non-IID simulation, and the need to tune the pruning proportion α. Open questions include whether the spectral metric generalizes to directed or temporal graphs, how performance scales to very large client graphs, and whether the reconstruction step can be made differentiable for end-to-end training.
