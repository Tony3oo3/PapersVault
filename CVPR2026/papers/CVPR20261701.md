## Problem

Autoregressive image generation models are inherently slow because each forward pass produces only one token, requiring hundreds or thousands of sequential iterations. Existing Jacobi-style decoding accelerates this by refining a 1D draft sequence in parallel, but the speedup quickly saturates as later tokens attend to an increasing number of uncertain candidates, causing error propagation that hinders convergence. The paper identifies that this 1D expansion scheme is suboptimal for images, which exhibit strong local spatial correlations rather than the long-range dependencies characteristic of text.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image (768×768 for Lumina-mGPT, 512×512 for LlamaGen) as output. It is entirely training-free: it modifies only the decoding schedule of a pre-trained autoregressive transformer over discrete VQ tokens, leaving the model weights, tokenizer, and image decoder unchanged. The generation proceeds over a 2D token grid (h×w) in raster-scan order, with the method controlling which rows are active for parallel refinement at each iteration.

## Method

Parallel Jacobi Decoding (PJD) extends Jacobi decoding from a 1D sequence to a 2D spatial grid by activating multiple rows for concurrent draft-token refinement. Each iteration has three stages: (1) Dynamic Token Preparation identifies rows whose predecessor has accumulated at least T_ctx generated tokens and appends a bounded number of draft tokens to them; (2) Parallel Autoregressive Prediction runs a single transformer forward pass with a row-causal attention mask that allows tokens to attend to committed tokens in preceding rows and earlier positions in the same row, but blocks attention to draft tokens in other rows; (3) Probabilistic Token Validation accepts a token if its conditional likelihood ratio between successive iterations passes a stochastic threshold (u ≤ min(1, p^(k)/p^(k-1))), with rejected tokens in the topmost row resampled from a calibrated distribution emphasizing newly increased probability mass. This spatially parallel refinement preserves the autoregressive dependency structure while reducing long-range error propagation, achieving 4.8×–6.4× speedup without quality degradation.

## Results

On MS-COCO, PJD achieves 4.95×–7.18× step reduction on Lumina-mGPT (c=16 to c=9) and 4.13×–4.81× on LlamaGen-XL (c=8 to c=6), while maintaining FID within ~1.2 of vanilla AR (e.g., 31.94 vs. 30.79 for Lumina-mGPT c=16; 45.12 vs. 45.02 for LlamaGen-XL c=6). On PartiPrompt, the step compression reaches up to 7.27× (Lumina-mGPT, c=9) and 4.83× (LlamaGen-XL, c=6) with CLIP-Score and IS comparable to or exceeding vanilla AR. Wall-clock latency drops from 197.16 s to 22.62 s (Lumina-mGPT) and from 49.58 s to 11.84 s (LlamaGen-XL) on MS-COCO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PJD outperforms both SJD and GSD on every metric in both models. On Lumina-mGPT (MS-COCO), PJD (c=16) reduces steps to 476 vs. GSD's 698 (4.95× vs. 3.38× speedup) and SJD's 1056 (2.23×), while also achieving a lower FID (31.94) than GSD (33.41) and matching SJD (30.87) within 1.1. On LlamaGen-XL (MS-COCO), PJD (c=6) achieves 4.81× step compression versus GSD's 2.67× and SJD's 1.62×, with FID 45.12 beating both GSD (47.13) and SJD (46.18). The gap widens at higher resolutions: at 1024×1024, PJD reaches 6.9× step compression while SJD and GSD plateau at lower ratios. In summary, PJD dominates on speed (2–3× more step reduction than GSD) while simultaneously matching or improving image quality, confirming that 2D spatial parallelism is strictly superior to 1D Jacobi expansion for image tokens.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Jacobi decoding paradigm (Song et al., ICML 2021) from its 1D sequence formulation to a 2D spatial formulation for autoregressive image generation. It builds directly on SJD (So et al., ICCV 2025), which first adapted Jacobi-style iterative refinement to visual AR models with a probabilistic acceptance criterion, and on GSD (So et al., ICCV 2025), which groups candidate tokens for parallel verification. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that 1D draft-sequence expansion saturates due to error propagation along the sequence, while the strong local spatial attention patterns in AR image models (visualized in Figure 2) motivate expanding drafts row-by-row in 2D, a design choice not explored in prior Jacobi or speculative decoding work for images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PJD offers a training-free, plug-and-play speedup (4.8×–6.4×) for AR image generation that grows with resolution, making it particularly relevant as these models scale to higher resolutions where sequential decoding becomes most prohibitive. The context-token-count trade-off between speed and fidelity (Figure 6) is a practical knob that practitioners must tune, and the method's dependence on strong spatial locality means its applicability to architectures with more global attention (e.g., unified multimodal models) remains an open question. Evaluation is limited to two 7B models (Lumina-mGPT and LlamaGen), so generalization to other tokenizers, resolutions beyond 1024×1024, or video generation is untested.
