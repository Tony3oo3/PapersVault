## Problem

Transferring a reference texture onto a target object in images or videos while preserving the target's geometry is an underexplored editing task. Naive approaches that condition on raw reference images and conventional structural cues (edges, depth) fail because they cannot cleanly disentangle texture from structure: the reference leaks unwanted shape and pose information, and residual texture cues in control signals prevent the target's appearance from being freely modified.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261007/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video or image, a binary object mask, the corresponding background (source with the object removed), and a reference image containing the desired texture. The source is first passed through a texture remover to produce a geometry-only video, and the reference is jigsaw-permuted before encoding. The output is a retextured video or image where the masked object region carries the reference's surface appearance while the surrounding background and the object's shape, pose, and motion remain unchanged.

## Method

Refaçade combines two decoupling strategies within a VACE-based diffusion framework trained with flow matching. First, a dedicated texture remover (initialized from Wan2.1/VACE, trained on 576K paired textured/untextured 3D mesh renderings from Hunyuan3D, then distilled via DMD2 from 50 to 3 sampling steps) converts the source into a texture-free geometry representation that preserves motion and shape while eliminating all color and material cues. Second, a jigsaw permutation strategy cuts the reference foreground into square patches (16×16 up to the maximum inscribed rectangle), randomly shuffles and flips them, and rearranges them into a new layout, destroying the reference's global spatial structure so the model learns to transfer local texture statistics rather than object geometry. The architecture uses MM-DiT-inspired separate parameter sets for reference and source conditioning tokens while sharing attention, and is trained in two stages: large-scale pretraining on ~1.8M WebVid-10M videos plus synthetic data, followed by high-quality finetuning on 180K Pexels videos.

## Results

On the video benchmark (Pexels, 50 videos), Refaçade (stage 2) achieves the best background preservation (LPIPS 0.0344, PSNR 36.48) and foreground texture fidelity (CLIP 0.7524, DINO 0.3241, LPIPS 0.6080, GLCM 0.9033), outperforming the strongest inpainting baseline VideoPainter (BG LPIPS 0.1052) and general editing methods. On the image benchmark (UHRSD, 988 images), stage 2 similarly leads in background preservation (LPIPS 0.0487 vs. Flux-Fill's 0.0730) and foreground alignment (CLIP 0.7774, DINO 0.4516, GLCM 0.9033). LLM-based evaluation (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5) and user preference scores confirm consistent superiority: 2.82/3.25 (video) and 2.89/2.77 (image) on LLM scores, with user preference of 0.7391 (video) and 0.8944 (image).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Refaçade (stage 2) decisively outperforms all 26 baselines on the video benchmark across nearly every metric. On background preservation, it achieves LPIPS 0.0344, roughly 3× better than the next-best inpainting method VideoPainter (0.1052) and 7× better than general editing methods like TokenFlow (0.2941) or InsVIE (0.5428). On foreground texture transfer, it leads in CLIP (0.7524 vs. 0.7455 for Flux-Kont-I), DINO (0.3241 vs. 0.3209 for Flux-Kont-I), and GLCM (0.9033 vs. 0.8480 for Pair Diffusion), while also achieving the lowest foreground LPIPS (0.6080). The closest competitor on motion consistency is Ditto (EWarp 1.3656) and Señorita (1.3519), slightly ahead of Refaçade stage 2 (1.4248), though stage 1 is competitive at 1.3510. On the image benchmark (UHRSD), Refaçade stage 2 similarly dominates: background LPIPS 0.0487 vs. Flux-Fill's 0.0730, foreground CLIP 0.7774 vs. Pair Diffusion's 0.7776 (near-tie), DINO 0.4516 (best by a wide margin over ZeST's 0.3324), and user preference 0.8944 vs. Pair Diffusion's 0.4969. LLM-based evaluation (GPT-5, Gemini-2.5) consistently ranks Refaçade highest across both benchmarks, confirming that automatic metrics align with perceptual quality judgments.

## Relation to Prior Work

Refaçade builds directly on the VACE video editing framework and adopts flow matching as its training objective, while leveraging DMD2 distillation to compress the texture-remover's inference from 50 to 3 steps. Conceptually, it departs from the standard ControlNet paradigm for appearance editing: rather than conditioning on hand-crafted structural cues (Canny, HED, depth) that retain residual texture, the paper learns a dedicated texture-remover to produce geometry-only conditioning, and introduces a jigsaw permutation of the reference image to prevent structural leakage from the reference. The jigsaw idea draws on Jigsaw3D [77] for 3D style transfer, repurposed here for 2D texture transfer in a diffusion setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By extending Object Retexture from the image domain to video, Refaçade removes the need for ambiguous text prompts when a user simply wants to swap an object's surface appearance, offering a more direct and controllable editing interface. The ablation quantifying texture leakage in conventional control signals (Canny, HED, depth all underperform the learned untextured representation) provides useful guidance for future appearance-editing pipelines. Open questions include scalability to multi-object scenes, handling of occlusions and non-rigid deformations, and whether the two-model pipeline (texture remover + main editor) can be unified into a single end-to-end system without the distillation overhead.
