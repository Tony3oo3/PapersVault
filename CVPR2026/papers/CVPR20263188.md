## Problem

Video diffusion models produce visually rich 2D motion but lack explicit 3D structure, making their outputs unusable for animation or simulation. Existing 4D generation approaches either entangle geometry and appearance via score distillation, rely on implicit representations incompatible with standard animation pipelines, or produce quasi-static LBS-based motion that lacks physical realism such as inertia and elasticity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263188/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static textured 3D mesh (artist-created or from text/image-to-3D generators) and a text prompt as input. The mesh is first rendered to a single canonical-view image, which is then fed into the Kling image-to-video diffusion model to synthesize a monocular reference video. The output is a temporally coherent animated 3D mesh sequence with an explicit skeleton, per-vertex skinning weights, per-frame joint rotation/translation parameters, and physics-refined vertex positions—making the result directly editable in standard animation software and renderable from arbitrary novel viewpoints.

## Method

AniMimic operates in two optimization stages. In the first stage, UniRig automatically predicts a skeletal hierarchy and skinning weights for the input mesh, and joint parameters (6D rotations + translations) are optimized via differentiable SoftPhong/silhouette rendering (PyTorch3D), AllTracker pixel tracking, and VGGT depth supervision against the diffusion-generated reference video. In the second stage, the surface mesh is converted to a tetrahedral volume via TetWild, and a differentiable FEM simulation using the Fixed Corotated elasticity model refines vertex positions by optimizing per-cluster Young's moduli in a coarse-to-fine manner, with joint transformations serving as boundary conditions. Differentiability through the simulation is achieved via the adjoint method and automatic differentiation (following Dress-1-to-3), enabling end-to-end gradient flow from image-space losses back to material parameters. The physics stage captures inertial and elastic secondary motion that LBS alone cannot produce, while preserving the creative motion cues from the video diffusion prior.

## Results

On the quantitative evaluation (20 motion sequences, multi-view rendering at ±45°), AniMimic achieves the best LPIPS (0.0849) and VBAQ (0.581) among all methods, and ties for the best VBOC (0.176). It trails SC4D on SSIM (0.9318 vs. 0.9403) and Puppeteer on VBIQ (0.606 vs. 0.632). In the user study (2AFC protocol), AniMimic is preferred over SC4D, DreamMesh4D, and Puppeteer in 91–96%, 88–95%, and 69–78% of trials respectively across visual quality, temporal consistency, motion plausibility, and overall preference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SC4D (Gaussian Splatting-based), AniMimic wins on LPIPS (0.0849 vs. 0.0924), VBAQ (0.581 vs. 0.541), and VBIQ (0.606 vs. 0.392), but loses on SSIM (0.9318 vs. 0.9403), which the authors attribute to SC4D preserving low-frequency structure at the cost of texture and geometry fidelity. Against DreamMesh4D (Gaussian-mesh hybrid), AniMimic dominates on SSIM (0.9318 vs. 0.8662), LPIPS (0.0849 vs. 0.1482), and VBAQ (0.581 vs. 0.543), while DreamMesh4D edges ahead on VBIQ (0.550 vs. 0.606—actually AniMimic wins here too; DreamMesh4D's VBIQ is 0.550). Against Puppeteer (mesh-based rigging without physics), AniMimic wins on SSIM (0.9318 vs. 0.9023), LPIPS (0.0849 vs. 0.1097), and VBAQ (0.581 vs. 0.572), but Puppeteer achieves a higher VBIQ (0.632 vs. 0.606), reflecting its sharper per-frame rendering at the expense of physically plausible motion. In the user study, AniMimic is preferred over all three baselines on every criterion, with the largest margins over Puppeteer (69–78%) and the smallest over SC4D (91–96% still strongly favoring AniMimic).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant lines of 4D generation: score-distillation approaches (4D-fy, TC4D) that optimize implicit 3D representations under video diffusion supervision, and reconstruction-based methods (SC4D, DreamMesh4D) that recover geometry from generated videos. Instead of reconstructing geometry, AniMimic animates an existing explicit mesh by combining automatic rigging (UniRig), differentiable rendering with multi-signal supervision (RGB, mask, tracking, depth), and a differentiable elastic simulation stage for physics-based refinement. The differentiable simulation component builds directly on Dress-1-to-3 [30], which established adjoint-method-based gradient backpropagation through FEM time integration, and extends the LBS-based animation pipeline of Puppeteer and AKD by adding the physically grounded soft-tynamics stage that those methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing rigged, editable mesh animations rather than implicit 4D representations, AniMimic offers a practical bridge between generative video models and standard animation pipelines (Maya, Blender), which is a significant workflow advantage for production studios. The physics-based refinement stage addresses a real limitation of pure LBS animation—quasi-static deformation that cannot capture inertia or elasticity—without sacrificing the compact parameterization that makes the result artist-editable. Open questions include scalability to multi-object scenes with contact, the dependence on video diffusion model quality (hallucinations, inconsistent lighting), and whether the optimization-based approach can be accelerated toward interactive use. The method also inherits limitations of automatic rigging: imperfect skinning weights in complex topologies can still produce artifacts that the simulation stage only partially corrects.
