## Problem

Existing 3D part segmentation methods offer only coarse, global control over granularity (e.g., a scale parameter in SAMPart3D or a fixed cluster count in PartField), making it impossible to target specific parts with spatial precision. Moreover, the most expressive pipelines (SAMPart3D, SAMesh) require per-shape optimization taking minutes per object, while fast feedforward approaches sacrifice controllability. No prior method aligns explicit 2D spatial prompts with 3D part outcomes to enable intuitive, region-specific segmentation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a textureless 3D model (mesh or point cloud) and renders it into 12 normal maps and corresponding point maps from canonical viewpoints arranged counterclockwise at three elevation angles (25°, 0°, −25°). The user provides a single 2D prompt (point click or bounding box) on one chosen view, which serves as the video's starting frame; occluded parts on other views are segmented automatically. The output is a per-face or per-point 3D part label map on the original model, obtained by back-projecting per-view masks and aggregating via visibility-aware k-NN voting.

## Method

GeoSAM2 reformulates 3D part segmentation as a 12-frame multi-view 2D mask prediction task processed by a shared SAM2 backbone. Two key architectural modifications enable adaptation to textureless geometry: (1) LoRA adapters (rank 4) are injected into every Q, K, V attention layer of the frozen ViT encoder, with independent branches for normal-map and point-map inputs; (2) at each FPN resolution, a zero-initialized 3×3 convolution fuses concatenated normal and point-map features back into the normal branch via a residual connection, preserving pretrained RGB statistics while gradually learning cross-modal associations. The memory mechanism is redesigned from SAM2's FIFO bank to retain all 12 view embeddings simultaneously, and a frame-duplication bootstrapping trick populates the memory before the first prompt is processed. Training uses supervised fine-tuning on ~4,700 self-annotated objects (50 epochs, 8×A800, lr 5e-5) with the standard SAM2 loss plus an IoU prediction head; a lightweight post-processing step removes small connected components and smooths labels via k-NN voting on mesh connectivity.

## Results

On PartObjaverse-Tiny, GeoSAM2 achieves a class-agnostic mean IoU of 84.06%, outperforming the strongest baseline PartField (79.18%) by nearly 5 points. On PartNetE, GeoSAM2 reaches 74.42% mIoU, surpassing PartField (59.10%) by 15.3 points and SAMPart3D (56.17%) by 18.3 points. The method's runtime is >30s per object, slower than PartField (>10s) but far faster than SAMPart3D (~15 min) and SAMesh (~7 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoSAM2 outperforms all four baselines on both benchmarks. Against the closest competitor PartField [17] (a feedforward triplane feature-field method), GeoSAM2 gains +4.88 mIoU on PartObjaverse-Tiny (84.06 vs. 79.18) and a substantial +15.32 mIoU on PartNetE (74.42 vs. 59.10), where PartField's reliance on mesh connectivity priors causes a 20-point collapse. SAMPart3D [39] (scale-conditioned clustering with per-shape MLP fitting, ~15 min runtime) trails by 10.6 and 18.3 mIoU on the two datasets respectively. SAMesh [29] (community-detection lifting of SAM2 masks, ~7 min runtime) is weaker still at 56.86 and 26.67 mIoU, suffering from over-segmentation and lack of part-specific control. Find3D [19] (text-prompted feedforward alignment) is the weakest baseline at 21.28 and 12.69 mIoU, confirming that open-vocabulary text prompts remain unreliable for fine-grained 3D part detection. Notably, GeoSAM2's advantage is largest on PartNetE, where its prompt-driven, region-specific control avoids the global granularity limitations that hurt scale-based and clustering-based methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoSAM2 repurposes SAM2's video segmentation architecture for 3D part segmentation by treating 12 static multi-view renderings as a synthetic video sequence, a conceptual bridge not previously exploited. It departs from prior 3D part segmentation methods (SAMPart3D, PartField, SAMesh) that rely on global scale knobs or text prompts for coarse control, instead introducing explicit 2D click/box prompts for part-specific, spatially grounded interaction. The technical contributions—LoRA-based adaptation of SAM2's frozen backbone to geometric modalities (normal and point maps), zero-initialized residual fusion at FPN levels, and a full-view memory bank replacing SAM2's FIFO mechanism—are natural extensions of parameter-efficient fine-tuning and multi-modal fusion ideas applied to a new domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a prompt-driven, part-specific interaction paradigm can substantially outperform global scale-based or clustering-based controls for 3D part segmentation, with particularly large gains on point-cloud data (PartNetE) where mesh connectivity priors are unavailable. This opens practical pathways for interactive 3D editing, robotic manipulation, and integration with part-completion models (e.g., HoloPart) without per-shape optimization. A key open question is how the method handles heavy occlusions, which the authors acknowledge as a limitation; incorporating 3D-aware semantic completion could address this. The simplicity of the frame-repetition bootstrapping trick also suggests further low-cost improvements to SAM2's memory initialization may be possible.
