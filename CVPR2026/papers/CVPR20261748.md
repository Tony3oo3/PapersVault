## Problem

Mainstream dense scene flow frameworks such as RAFT-3D rely primarily on 2D feature correlations to optimize 3D motion fields, using depth only at the input stage while iterative refinement remains confined to 2D correlation space. This prevents the network from exploiting 3D structural priors during matching, limiting robustness in complex scenes with non-rigid motion, motion blur, and low-light conditions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261748/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two consecutive RGB images (H×W×3) and their corresponding disparity maps (H×W×1, from GA-Net or MonSter) as input, and produces a dense 3D motion field decomposed into a 2D optical flow μ and a disparity change Δd. Refinement operates at three scales (1/16, 1/8, 1/4) with 2, 4, and 3 recurrent iterations respectively, and predictions are upsampled to full resolution via a combination of a dynamic hidden-state mask (MU) and precomputed context-guided masks (CU) for the remaining 2× transitions. Training uses 320×720 (KITTI) or 320×640 (Sintel) resolution with a batch size of 4–6.

## Method

SEA-Flow3D's core contribution is the Spatial Vector Sampling (SVS) module, which at each iteration samples the disparity-augmented projection field (u, v, D₂) within a local neighborhood of the matched point and computes explicit 3D direction vectors [Δu, Δv, ΔD] between the source pixel and each neighbor, providing continuous geometric guidance alongside standard 2D correlation lookups. The architecture follows a RAFT-style coarse-to-fine pipeline with a lightweight U-Net RNN optimizer built from ConvNeXtV2 blocks (replacing GRU), a split motion encoder that processes correlation and spatial-vector features in separate branches to avoid representational interference, and an independent coarse-scale optimizer at 1/16 with a larger sampling radius for aggressive global initialization. Correlation is computed on-the-fly via a CUDA dynamic sampling operator (no 4D cost volume), and context-guided upsampling masks precomputed from multi-scale context features enable full-resolution supervision with minimal overhead. Training is supervised with a decay-weighted L1 loss (γ=0.8) on both optical flow and disparity change across all iterations and scales.

## Results

On the KITTI Scene Flow Benchmark, SEA-Flow3D with MonSter depth (SEA-Flow3D+M) achieves a scene flow outlier rate (SF-all) of 3.55%, optical flow outlier rate (Fl-all) of 2.53%, and disparity outlier rate (D2-all) of 2.18%, setting new state-of-the-art across all metrics. With the more common GA-Net depth (SEA-Flow3D+G), it still achieves SF-all of 4.17%, outperforming the previous best MS-RAFT-3D (4.04%) and CamLiRAFT (4.26%). On Sintel, SEA-Flow3D attains a Final endpoint error of 2.04 px and Clean error of 1.04 px while running in only 72 ms, compared to 2.22 px and 1710 ms for MS-RAFT-3D.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEA-Flow3D+M outperforms the strongest prior method MS-RAFT-3D by 0.49 points on KITTI SF-all (3.55 vs. 4.04) and by 0.18 px on Sintel Final error (2.04 vs. 2.22), while reducing inference time from 1710 ms to 72 ms—a 24× speedup. Against the point-cloud-based CamLiRAFT, SEA-Flow3D+M improves KITTI SF-all from 4.26 to 3.55 and Sintel Final from 2.38 to 2.04, despite using only RGB-D input rather than LiDAR point clouds. With the same GA-Net depth as most competitors, SEA-Flow3D+G still beats CamLiRAFT (4.17 vs. 4.26 SF-all) and RAFT-3D (4.17 vs. 5.77), confirming the advantage is not solely due to superior depth input. On Sintel, SEA-Flow3D also surpasses the optical-flow-only baselines RAFT (2.71), FlowNet2 (3.54), and PWC-Net (3.93) on Final error, demonstrating that the SVS module provides meaningful 3D geometric guidance even without LiDAR. The method is competitive with or superior to all 14 baselines across both benchmarks, with the largest margins observed against older methods like OpticalExp (8.12) and ISF (8.08) on KITTI SF-all.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEA-Flow3D sits at the intersection of the RAFT family of iterative refinement architectures (RAFT, MS-RAFT, CCMR) and the dense RGB-D scene flow line (RAFT-3D, MS-RAFT-3D). Its core departure is the Spatial Vector Sampling (SVS) module, which injects explicit 3D displacement vectors into every refinement iteration, addressing the key limitation of prior dense methods that use depth only at the input stage while keeping iterative updates confined to 2D correlation space. The RNN-style U-Net optimizer with ConvNeXtV2 blocks follows SEA-RAFT's design philosophy, and the multi-scale coarse-to-fine pipeline with context-guided upsampling is adapted from CCMR and MS-RAFT. Conceptually, the paper argues that continuous geometric awareness during matching—not just at initialization—is what separates robust 3D motion estimation from 2D flow estimation with a depth prior bolted on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 24× inference speedup over MS-RAFT-3D (72 ms vs. 1710 ms) while simultaneously achieving lower error (Final 2.04 vs. 2.22 on Sintel) makes dense scene flow estimation viable for real-time applications in autonomous driving and embodied AI, where latency budgets are tight. The SVS module is a lightweight, drop-in addition that provides continuous 3D geometric guidance without heavy 3D convolutions or point-cloud architectures, suggesting a practical path for other correlation-based methods to incorporate spatial structure. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to pure RGB settings without depth, how it performs at higher resolutions or on more diverse real-world scenes beyond KITTI/Sintel, and whether the independent coarse-scale optimizer design transfers to other iterative refinement tasks.
