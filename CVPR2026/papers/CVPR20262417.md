## Problem

Existing co-speech gesture generation methods are designed for offline use, requiring access to complete utterances before producing motion, which incurs high latency and prevents truly interactive real-time applications. Moreover, prior approaches either model body regions independently or entangle all joints in a single decoder, failing to capture fine-grained inter-region coordination. No prior framework supports fully streamable, zero-look-ahead full-body gesture generation over arbitrary-length speech.

## Input / Output

The model consumes continuously arriving audio waveforms (converted to log-mel frames with a short analysis window and small hop to match the streaming latency budget) and optional online transcript tokens, with strict causality enforced so that at time t only past motion history, the current audio token, and current text are available. Output is full-body SMPL-X parameters partitioned into four regions (lower body, upper body, hands, face), generated as discrete motion tokens at a 4× downsampled frame rate and decoded causally, achieving <50 ms latency per 200 ms chunk with a 250 ms first-token latency.

## Method

LiveGesture combines a Streamable Vector-Quantized Motion Tokenizer (SVQ) with a Hierarchical Autoregressive Transformer (HAR). SVQ uses an asymmetric design—bidirectional encoder, strictly causal decoder, and region-specific VQ codebook—trained in two stages (autoencoder pretraining, then quantization learning with frozen encoder/decoder) to produce discrete, time-synchronous motion tokens compatible with causal streaming. HAR stacks four region-eXpert causal AR transformers (one per body region) that model audio-conditioned next-token distributions, followed by a causal spatial-temporal fusion module (xAR-Fuse) that aligns frozen expert hidden states via lightweight PILOR residual adapters and applies inter-region spatial attention, global temporal attention, and audio-motion cross-attention for whole-body coordination. A streamable causal audio encoder (dilated 1D convolutions with left-only padding) provides multi-scale audio tokens at the motion frame rate. Training employs autoregressive masking—uncertainty-guided token masking with a cosine-annealed schedule plus random region masking—to expose the fusion model to corrupted histories and reduce exposure bias, combined with classifier-free guidance on discrete token logits for stronger audio-motion alignment.

## Results

On BEAT2, LiveGesture achieves FGD 4.57, Beat Constancy 0.794, Diversity 13.91, and facial MSE 1.241 (×10⁻⁷), while being the only method operating under strict zero-look-ahead streaming. It attains the best BC (0.794) and highest Diversity (13.91) among all 12 compared methods, surpassing the best offline baselines (MambaTalk BC 0.781, TalkShow Div. 13.47). Its FGD of 4.57 is second only to GestureLSM (4.247), and its MSE of 1.241 is second only to GestureLSM (1.021), both of which are offline-only methods. First-token latency is 250 ms with <50 ms per 200 ms chunk.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LiveGesture is the only zero-look-ahead streaming method in the comparison and still outperforms all 11 offline baselines on Beat Constancy (0.794 vs. best offline 0.781 by MambaTalk) and Diversity (13.91 vs. best offline 13.76 by GestureLSM). On FGD, LiveGesture (4.57) trails only GestureLSM (4.247) by 0.32, while beating all other offline methods including SynTalker (4.687), ProbTalk (5.040), and MambaTalk (5.366). On facial MSE, LiveGesture (1.241) is second only to GestureLSM (1.021) and substantially outperforms TalkShow (7.791), EMAGE (7.680), and MambaTalk (7.680). Against the most recent offline methods (GestureLSM, RAG-Gesture, MambaTalk), LiveGesture trades a small FGD/MSE gap for the critical advantage of real-time streaming with 250 ms first-token latency. The audio encoder ablation (Table 2d) further shows that LiveGesture's 0.5M-parameter causal encoder matches Magic-Codec (103M params) in FGD (4.57 vs. 4.51) while achieving the best BC (0.794 vs. 0.793).

## Relation to Prior Work

LiveGesture repurposes the autoregressive transformer + VQ-VAE tokenization paradigm (inherited from GPT-style text generation and VQ-GAN) into the co-speech gesture domain, but departs from all prior gesture methods by enforcing strict causality at every component: the motion tokenizer uses an asymmetric bidirectional-encoder/causal-decoder design, the region experts and fusion transformer use only causal attention, and the audio encoder is a lightweight causal CNN rather than a pretrained codec. Unlike offline methods (EMAGE, MambaTalk, GestureLSM, SynTalker) that assume access to the full utterance, and unlike GestureLSM which is fast but still non-streamable, LiveGesture is the first to formulate and solve the zero-look-ahead streaming variant, introducing the hierarchical region-eXpert + xAR-Fuse architecture and uncertainty-guided masking training specifically to handle the error accumulation inherent in autoregressive streaming.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a strictly causal, zero-look-ahead model can match or exceed offline baselines on beat synchronization (BC 0.794) and diversity (13.91) while achieving <50 ms per 200 ms chunk latency, LiveGesture opens the door to truly interactive avatar systems (VTubers, telepresence, social robots) where gesture generation must begin before the speaker finishes a phrase. The ablation showing that a 0.5M-parameter task-specific causal audio encoder rivals a 103M-parameter pretrained codec (Magic-Codec) suggests that domain-aligned lightweight encoders can replace heavy general-purpose ones in streaming pipelines. Open questions include generalization beyond the single BEAT2 dataset, robustness to very long multi-turn conversations, and whether the hierarchical region-expert design scales to additional body regions or different motion representations.
