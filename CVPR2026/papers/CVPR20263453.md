## Problem

Existing image deraining methods are typically designed for a single rain degradation type (e.g., daytime streaks or nighttime raindrops) and degrade significantly when applied to other conditions, making multi-scenario deployment cumbersome. No unified framework exists that simultaneously handles rain streaks and raindrops under both daytime and nighttime conditions, partly because directly merging all public deraining datasets introduces severe quality disparity and optimization imbalance across degradation types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263453/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a single rainy RGB image (trained on 128×128 crops, evaluated at 256×256) and outputs a derained image, handling all four degradation types—daytime rain streaks, daytime raindrops, nighttime rain streaks, and nighttime raindrops—within one network without requiring an explicit degradation-type label at inference. The training data is the distilled RainRAG dataset (52,869 pairs, ~2.6% of >2,000k original pairs), while evaluation spans both the proposed RainRAG test set and real-world benchmarks (RealRain1k, RainDS-real, WeatherBench).

## Method

UniRain combines three key components: (1) a RAG-based dataset distillation pipeline that builds a CLIP/BLIP-indexed database of real rainy images, retrieves semantically and structurally consistent references via hierarchical similarity search (text → visual → SSIM), and uses majority voting across three VLMs (InternVL2.5-8B, LLaVA-NeXT-7B, MobileVLM-3B) to filter reliable training samples; (2) an asymmetric MoE architecture where a soft-MoE encoder (continuous routing over all experts) preserves diverse degradation cues and a hard-MoE decoder (top-k routing) selectively activates experts for fine texture reconstruction; and (3) a multi-objective reweighted optimization strategy that estimates per-type convergence slopes via sliding-window linear regression and dynamically computes Type Balance Score, Type Stability Score, and an Adaptivity Factor to reweight losses, preventing the model from overfitting easier rain types while under-optimizing harder ones.

## Results

On the real-world public benchmarks (RealRain-1k, RainDS-real, WeatherBench), UniRain achieves an average PSNR of 29.42 dB and SSIM of 0.8222, outperforming the closest unified baseline URIR by 1.73 dB in PSNR. On the proposed RainRAG dataset, UniRain attains the best average performance across all four rain degradation types (DRS, DRD, NRS, NRD), with notable gains of 1.35 dB and 1.41 dB over Restormer and NeRD-Rain respectively on the daytime raindrops subset. In the all-in-one weather restoration extension, UniRain reaches 26.01 dB PSNR on WeatherBench, surpassing TransWeather (24.70 dB) and Histoformer (24.59 dB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniRain consistently outperforms all 10 deraining baselines across every real-world benchmark. The most direct comparison is against URIR [46], the only other unified deraining method, where UniRain gains 1.73 dB in average PSNR (29.42 vs. 27.69) and 0.016 in SSIM. Among single-task deraining methods, NeRD-Rain [6] is the strongest baseline at 27.81 dB average PSNR, still trailing UniRain by 1.61 dB; MSDT [4] and MPRNet [50] follow at 27.62 and 27.06 dB respectively. On the challenging RainDS-real-RD (raindrop) subset, UniRain achieves 22.07 dB, surpassing the best single-task method RLP [53] (20.86 dB) by over 1.2 dB. In the all-in-one weather restoration extension (Table 7), UniRain (26.01 dB PSNR) beats TransWeather [39] by 1.31 dB, Histoformer [38] by 1.42 dB, and WGWS-Net [56] by 2.12 dB, confirming that the unified framework generalizes beyond rain to snow and haze. The model also maintains a favorable complexity trade-off at 204 GFLOPs and 28.1 M parameters, comparable to DRSformer (220 GFLOPs) while delivering substantially higher accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of single-image deraining methods (PReNet, Restormer, DRSformer, NeRD-Rain, etc.), which are each specialized to one rain degradation type, toward a unified model handling streaks and raindrops under both daytime and nighttime conditions. It builds conceptually on URIR [46], the first universal deraining network for driving scenarios, but broadens the scope to four distinct degradation categories. The RAG-based data distillation pipeline repurposes retrieval-augmented generation—previously applied to LLMs and recently to low-level vision via ReFIR [15]—for dataset curation rather than inference-time reference injection. The multi-objective reweighted optimization draws from convergence-balancing strategies in multi-task LLM fine-tuning (Coba [11]) and adapts them to the deraining setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value lies in replacing multiple specialized deraining models with a single deployable system, reducing switching overhead in real-world applications like autonomous driving and UAV imaging. The finding that distilling only ~2.6% of a 2M+ pair corpus outperforms training on the full merged collection challenges the common assumption that more data always helps, and the RAG+VLM curation pipeline offers a transferable recipe for quality control in other low-level vision tasks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to additional degradation types (fog, snow, motion blur) without further architectural changes, whether the RAG pipeline's computational cost is prohibitive at larger scale, and whether the MoE routing decisions remain interpretable as the number of experts grows.
