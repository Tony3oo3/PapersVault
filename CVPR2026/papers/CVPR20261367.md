## Problem

Dense 3D scene flow estimation in high-speed, illumination-varying scenes remains difficult because RGB stereo captures rich spatial structure but suffers from motion blur and limited temporal resolution, while event stereo resolves fine temporal motion but lacks dense spatial geometry. No prior framework unifies asymmetric RGB-event sensing with probabilistic temporal modeling to jointly recover geometrically consistent and temporally stable 3D motion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261367/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

ARES takes an asymmetric stereo pair consisting of one event camera and one RGB camera (left/right views), with RGB frames at 640×480 (DSEC) or 346×260 (MVSEC) and asynchronously recorded event streams that are voxelized over the inter-frame interval. A key preprocessing step computes a bridging representation from both modalities—log-intensity difference for RGB and integrated event count for events—providing a temporally aligned signal that anchors cross-view fusion. The network outputs a forward optical flow field, disparity maps at two consecutive timestamps, and a left-referenced scene flow vector (u, v, u^R) expressed in pixel space, where the right-view horizontal component is derived from the disparity change to enforce geometric consistency.

## Method

ARES comprises two core modules. Multimodal Contextual Attention (MCA) fuses modality-specific features—ConvNeXt DINOv3 for RGB, recurrent CNN with FPN for events, and a lightweight CNN for the bridging signal—through interleaved self-attention and cross-attention layers that alternately enrich a shared query with spatial structure and temporal motion, using view-independent projection weights and Axial RoPE positional encoding to produce a unified correspondence representation for both flow and disparity matching. Temporal Disparity Posterior Fusion (TDPF) then warps the disparity correlation volume (posterior) from t₁ to t₀ using the estimated optical flow, interpolates to a fixed bin count, and concatenates aligned posteriors with disparity predictions and MCA features into a compact tensor; a small 2D CNN predicts a residual correction to the coarse disparity change, yielding a probabilistically grounded, temporally coherent scene flow. Training combines sparse supervised losses on flow and disparity with dense self-supervised photometric and temporal disparity-warping objectives, avoiding the need for dense scene flow annotations.

## Results

On DSEC, ARES achieves the best scene flow EPE of 4.608 (vs. 5.131 for RAFT-3D and 6.225 for EMatch) and the lowest scene flow outlier rate (>5px) of 0.300, while remaining competitive on disparity (EPE 0.502 vs. 0.486 for RAFT-3D) and optical flow (EPE 0.485 vs. 0.452 for EMatch). On MVSEC, ARES leads on all three tasks: disparity EPE 0.110, flow EPE 0.815, and scene flow EPE 0.752 (vs. 0.939 for RAFT-3D and 1.251 for EMatch). The scene flow improvement over the strongest baseline (RAFT-3D) is 10.4% on DSEC and 20.0% on MVSEC in EPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARES outperforms RAFT-3D [29] on scene flow EPE by 10.4% on DSEC (4.608 vs. 5.131) and 20.0% on MVSEC (0.752 vs. 0.939), and also reduces the scene flow outlier rate substantially (0.300 vs. 0.544 on DSEC >5px; 0.240 vs. 0.291 on MVSEC >1px). Against EMatch [35], ARES trades a small optical-flow margin (0.485 vs. 0.452 EPE on DSEC) for a large scene-flow gain (4.608 vs. 6.225 EPE), indicating that EMatch's event-only temporal precision does not translate into coherent 3D motion without RGB spatial grounding. On disparity, ARES is within 3.3% of RAFT-3D on DSEC (0.502 vs. 0.486) and actually surpasses it on MVSEC (0.110 vs. 0.117), confirming that the RGB branch and Multimodal Contextual Attention recover dense geometry even under asymmetric sensing. ZEST [21] is included only for disparity (it does not predict flow or scene flow) and is outperformed by ARES on both datasets (0.502 vs. 0.683 on DSEC; 0.110 vs. 0.183 on MVSEC). Overall, ARES is the only method that simultaneously achieves top-tier disparity, competitive flow, and best-in-class scene flow across both benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARES extends the asymmetric event-RGB stereo line (ZEST, Chen et al.) from static disparity estimation to the dynamic domain of joint scene flow, and builds on event-based stereo (EMatch) by adding an RGB spatial anchor to recover dense geometry that events alone cannot provide. The Multimodal Contextual Attention module adapts transformer-based cross-modal fusion (Perceiver IO, BEVFusion) to the asymmetric, asynchronous sensing setting by interleaving spatial and temporal cross-attention over a shared bridging representation. The Temporal Disparity Posterior Fusion module departs from prior scene flow methods (RAFT-3D, point-based approaches) that rely on dense depth supervision or rigid-motion embeddings, instead modeling disparity evolution probabilistically through posterior fusion across time.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARES establishes a principled framework for combining heterogeneous sensing modalities (asynchronous events + synchronous RGB) into a single geometrically and temporally coherent 3D motion estimator, which is practically relevant for autonomous driving where motion blur and illumination changes degrade frame-based stereo. The probabilistic disparity-evolution formulation (TDPF) offers a general recipe for enforcing temporal consistency without requiring dense scene-flow ground truth, which is scarce in practice. Open questions include whether the near-synchronous bridging assumption holds under real calibration drift, whether physical rigidity or motion-segmentation priors can further improve long-range consistency, and whether the approach generalizes beyond the two driving datasets evaluated.
