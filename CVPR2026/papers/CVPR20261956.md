## Problem

Existing 3D shape VAEs suffer from a fundamental representation mismatch: ground-truth meshes have arbitrary, variable topology while networks predict fixed-structure implicit fields (e.g., SDF on regular grids). This structural gap prevents explicit vertex- and face-level correspondences, forcing prior work to rely on indirect supervision signals (SDF values or rendered images) that blur or lose sharp geometric features during reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261956/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VAE takes a 3D mesh represented as a point cloud of vertices with normals as input and produces a mesh in Dual Marching Cubes (DMC) format (explicit vertices and faces) as output, alongside a sparse voxel latent code. Before encoding, any input mesh is converted to a DMC-compliant representation via the Topo-Remesh pipeline at up to 1024³ resolution, ensuring both ground-truth and predicted meshes share identical topological structure. For the downstream image-to-3D generation task, an RGB image serves as input to a diffusion model operating in the VAE's latent space.

## Method

TopoMesh unifies ground-truth and predicted meshes under a shared DMC topological framework, enabling direct supervision on topology (occupancy at grid corners via BCE), vertex positions (L1), and face normals (L1). The encoder uses sparse voxel-point cross-attention where each voxel attends only to points within it via a single shared learnable query, reducing attention storage from O(N×P) to O(P). The decoder builds on FlexiCubes but decouples SDF into occupancy (topology) and magnitude (geometry) components, so faces are determined solely by topological parameters while vertices depend on all parameters. Training employs Teacher Forcing (feeding GT topology to the decoder to avoid a topology-geometry tug-of-war), GT-guided voxel pruning, and progressive resolution training (32³→64³→128³). The Topo-Remesh component converts arbitrary meshes to DMC-compliant form using an L∞ distance metric that preserves sharp angles by considering all incident triangle planes, combined with an ODC extractor, fully GPU-accelerated at ~15 s for 1024³ resolution.

## Results

On the VAE autoencoding task at 1024³ resolution, TopoMesh achieves the best sharp-feature preservation (F1-Sharp) on both benchmarks: 0.932 on Topo-Bench and 0.915 on Dora-Bench, outperforming the closest competitor SparseFlex (0.873 and 0.844) by 5.9% and 7.1% respectively. Overall reconstruction quality is competitive, with F1 of 0.917 (Topo-Bench) and 0.973 (Dora-Bench), while using only 56k latent tokens versus SparseFlex's 245k. The remeshing pipeline processes 1024³ meshes in 18.5 s on GPU with 28.7 MB average output size, substantially faster than all baselines (≥79 s). For image-to-3D generation on Toys4K, TopoMesh achieves FID 42.48 and KID 4.63×10⁻³, outperforming Direct3D-S2 (45.33 / 5.47) and Hunyuan3D-2.1 (59.43 / 5.97).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TopoMesh's closest competitor is SparseFlex, which uses rendering-based supervision on a FlexiCubes decoder; TopoMesh matches or exceeds its overall F1 (0.917 vs 0.920 on Topo-Bench, tied at 0.973 on Dora-Bench) while achieving substantially better sharp-feature preservation (F1-S: 0.932 vs 0.873, 0.915 vs 0.844) with only ~23% of the latent tokens (56k vs 245k). Among VecSet-based VAEs (TripoSG, Dora, Hunyuan3D-2.1), TopoMesh leads on F1-S by 15–22 points, confirming that global latent representations struggle with fine geometric detail. Direct3D-S2, which uses SDF supervision with MC-based extraction, lags on F1-S (0.694/0.819) due to grid-alignment constraints that smooth sharp edges. Trellis, evaluated at 256³ resolution, shows dramatically worse metrics (F1-S ≈ 0.3) and is not directly comparable. For remeshing, TopoMesh (L∞-based) outperforms L2-based methods (Mesh2SDF, Dora, Sparc3D) on ANC and matches ManifoldPlus on F1 while being 4–30× faster. In image-to-3D generation, TopoMesh achieves the best FID (42.48) and KID (4.63×10⁻³), beating Direct3D-S2, Hunyuan3D-2.1, and Trellis, with visibly sharper details and fewer artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

TopoMesh builds on the sparse voxel VAE paradigm (Trellis, SparseFlex, Direct3D-S2) and the FlexiCubes differentiable isosurface extraction framework, but introduces a key conceptual departure: Topological Unification, which forces both ground-truth and predicted meshes to share an identical Dual Marching Cubes structure. This unification, enabled by the Topo-Remesh pipeline (using an L∞ distance metric and ODC extraction), replaces the indirect supervision signals (SDF losses, rendering losses) used by prior work with direct mesh-level losses on topology, vertex positions, and face orientations. The decoder further decouples FlexiCubes' coupled SDF into separate occupancy (topology) and magnitude (geometry) parameters, and the training strategy introduces Teacher Forcing to resolve a topology-geometry 'tug-of-war' instability not previously identified in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Since VAE reconstruction fidelity sets the upper bound for 3D diffusion generation quality, a substantially better VAE directly improves the entire 3D generation pipeline; the 8% F-Score and 5.9–7.1% F1-Sharp gains over SparseFlex (with 1/4 the tokens) are practically significant for downstream generation. The Topological Unification principle is a general design insight that could transfer to other reconstruction or generation tasks where structural alignment between targets and predictions is needed. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 1024³ resolution without prohibitive compute, whether the remeshing step (limited by base voxel resolution) can be made adaptive, and whether the Teacher Forcing gap between training and inference remains negligible at scale or with noisier latent spaces.
