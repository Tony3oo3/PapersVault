## Problem

In multimodal LLM knowledge distillation, the training objective combines multiple heterogeneous loss channels (cross-entropy, KL-based distillation, feature-level matching) whose relative weights must be manually tuned via expensive grid search. Moreover, different samples exhibit varying degrees of data noise and teacher uncertainty, so a single fixed weight cannot optimally balance data supervision against teacher guidance across the dataset.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262665/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The distillation pipeline takes an RGB image paired with a text question as input and produces a free-form text answer. During training, both the vision encoder and tokenizer are frozen while only the language backbone is fine-tuned; the uncertainty network (a two-layer MLP with ~524K parameters for instance-level mode) consumes input features to predict per-sample β weights. The teacher (MobileVLM V2 7B) and student (MobileVLM V2 1.7B) share the same vocabulary and produce token-level logits over the full vocabulary at each autoregressive step.

## Method

Beta-KD reframes knowledge distillation as amortized MAP inference over student activations, where the teacher-student discrepancy serves as an energy function defining a Gibbs prior p(a^s | a^t, β) ∝ exp(−β·ℓ(a^s; a^t)). Applying the Laplace approximation to the intractable partition function yields a closed-form surrogate objective: min −log p(y|a^s) + β·ℓ(a^s; a^t) − (d/2)log β, where the −(d/2)log β term prevents β from diverging and provides explicit regularization. The precision parameter β is learned end-to-end via backpropagation in two granularities: task-level (a small set of positive scalars shared across all samples) and instance-level (predicted by a lightweight MLP from input features, enforcing positivity via softplus). This mechanism is loss-agnostic, supporting arbitrary energy functions (FKL, RKL, cosine distance, MSE, etc.) and arbitrary numbers of distillation channels, eliminating manual hyperparameter search while adaptively up-weighting teacher guidance on uncertain samples and down-weighting it where the student is already confident.

## Results

On the six-benchmark generalization study (Table 4), the best configuration (Cosine KD + Beta-KD Instance) achieves an average score of 65.5 across MME, GQA, TextVQA, POPE, MMBench, and ScienceQA, improving over the Align-KD baseline (63.4) by +2.0 points. On the ScienceQA two-loss setting, CE+Cosine-Probs with instance-level Beta-KD reaches 54.9% VQA accuracy and 67.5% image-question accuracy, the highest among all tested distillation objectives. The method also generalizes to a LLaVA-Qwen architecture, where instance-level Beta-KD yields +2.9 and +3.0 points on TextVQA for 0.5B and 1.5B students respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Beta-KD consistently outperforms all compared distillation baselines. Against Align-KD [15] (the primary KD baseline), instance-level Beta-KD improves the six-benchmark average from 63.4 to 64.6 (+1.2) with FKL, and to 65.5 (+2.0) when combined with the proposed Cosine-Probs energy. On ScienceQA two-loss, CE+Cosine-Probs with instance-level Beta-KD (54.9% VQA-Acc) surpasses CE+TAID [44] (54.8%), CE+AdaptiveKL [54] (54.2%), and CE+SFKL [30] (51.0%). The method also beats the no-distillation MobileVLM-V2 1.7B baseline (62.5 avg) by +3.0 points, and substantially outperforms LLAVADI [55] (59.2 avg) by +6.3 points. On the LLaVA-Qwen architecture (Table 5), Beta-KD (Instance) improves over LLaVA-KD [5] by +2.9/+3.0 on TextVQA and +3.8/+1.9 on ScienceQA for 0.5B/1.5B students. The only case where Beta-KD does not improve is CE+SFKL+FD (three-loss), where instance-level weighting slightly degrades VQA-Acc by 1.3 points, suggesting that for some loss combinations the fixed-weight baseline is already near-optimal.

## Relation to Prior Work

Beta-KD extends Kendall & Gal's (CVPR 2018) uncertainty-based multi-task weighting from Gaussian-likelihood task losses to arbitrary distillation objectives by modeling teacher supervision as a Gibbs prior over student activations, yielding a closed-form precision parameter via Laplace approximation. It departs from the standard KD lineage (Hinton et al., MiniLLM, DistiLLM, Align-KD) by reframing the fixed hyperparameter λ as a learnable, uncertainty-driven quantity rather than a manually tuned constant. The paper also generalizes beyond task-level (homoscedastic) uncertainty to instance-level (heteroscedastic) weighting, a distinction not present in the original Kendall & Gal formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The multi-channel loss balancing problem in MLLM distillation is a practical bottleneck: grid-searching over multiple λ values is infeasible at scale, and the optimal balance shifts across samples and training stages. Beta-KD's Bayesian framing provides a principled, zero-tuning alternative that consistently improves accuracy by 1–5 points across diverse loss combinations and architectures (MobileVLM, Qwen2.5-LLaVA). Open questions include whether the Laplace approximation remains valid in the very high-dimensional activation spaces of frontier-scale MLLMs, and whether the gains persist when the teacher-student capacity gap widens further. The method's negligible computational overhead (<0.01% training time) makes it a low-risk drop-in for existing KD pipelines.
