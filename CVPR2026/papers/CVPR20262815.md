## Problem

Indoor monocular semantic scene completion is harder than its outdoor counterpart due to complex multi-level spatial structures, severe occlusions, and high object density, all of which demand strong global contextual reasoning. Existing CNN-based methods suffer from inherently limited receptive fields that degrade with depth, while applying full transformers directly to dense 3D voxel volumes incurs prohibitive memory and compute costs, leaving a gap in efficient global modeling for indoor MSSC.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input; depth is estimated by an off-the-shelf model (Depth Anything) and 2D features from EfficientNet are projected into 3D via surface projection using camera intrinsics/extrinsics. The output is a dense 3D semantic voxel grid (60×36×60 for NYUv2, 60×60×36 for Occ-ScanNet) where each voxel receives an occupancy flag and one of 12 semantic labels (empty plus 11 indoor categories). The 3D encoder operates only on non-empty voxels to avoid padding overhead, and a lightweight convolutional DDR decoder (rather than a transformer) produces the final predictions to keep inference efficient.

## Method

AdaSFormer is a hybrid CNN–Transformer encoder that alternates Adaptive Serialized Attention (ASA) blocks with convolutional modules. ASA linearizes 3D voxels along a space-filling curve (Z-curve or Hilbert curve) into 1D token sequences, then applies learnable discrete shifts—parameterized via straight-through Gumbel-Softmax with a temperature annealing schedule—to dynamically adjust the serialization starting point and thus the receptive field at each layer. A Center-Relative Positional Encoding (CRPE) computes per-voxel yaw and pitch angles relative to the scene center (mean of occupied voxels) and feeds them through an MLP as a bias to the attention, encoding spatial information richness. A Convolution-Modulated Layer Normalization (CMLN) generates normalization parameters (γ, β) from the voxel features to harmonize the statistical mismatch between transformer and convolutional representations before feeding into the DDR decoder. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy, geometric affinity, and scene-class affinity losses.

## Results

On NYUv2, AdaSFormer achieves 51.33% SC IoU and 32.50% SSC mIoU, surpassing the previous best SSC mIoU (ISO, 31.25%) by 1.25 points, though MonoMRN reports a slightly higher SC IoU (53.16%). On Occ-ScanNet, AdaSFormer achieves a dominant 54.63% IoU and 45.33% mIoU, outperforming the next-best method (SurroundOCC, 30.83% mIoU) by a margin of 14.50 mIoU. In terms of efficiency, AdaSFormer runs at 178.9 ms per frame versus 535.2 ms for ISO, a roughly 3× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NYUv2, AdaSFormer achieves the best SSC mIoU (32.50%), beating ISO (31.25%) by 1.25 points and MonoMRN (30.73%) by 1.77 points, though MonoMRN edges it out on SC IoU (53.16% vs. 51.33%). Per-class, AdaSFormer leads on chair (23.93%), bed (53.89%), sofa (44.52%), and furniture (35.97%), while MonoMRN retains an advantage on ceiling (26.80% vs. 16.18%) and wall (19.39% vs. 16.96%). On Occ-ScanNet, AdaSFormer dominates all baselines by a wide margin: 45.33% mIoU vs. SurroundOCC (30.83%), ISO (28.71%), GaussianFormer (29.93%), MonoScene (24.62%), and TPVFormer (24.94%), a gap of 14.50 mIoU over the runner-up. The efficiency comparison further favors AdaSFormer, which runs at 178.9 ms per frame compared to ISO's 535.2 ms, a ~3× reduction in latency while simultaneously improving accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaSFormer extends the serialization-based transformer paradigm (OctFormer, FlatFormer, PTv3) from 3D point cloud segmentation to indoor monocular semantic scene completion, where prior work has been dominated by CNN-based pipelines (MonoScene, NDC-Scene, ISO, MonoMRN) limited by small receptive fields. The key departure is replacing fixed serialization offsets with learnable, Gumbel-Softmax-optimized shifts that adaptively adjust the receptive field per layer, analogous to Swin Transformer's window shifting but operating along a 1D voxel sequence for greater flexibility. The 2D-to-3D projection and training losses are inherited from MonoScene, while the DDR convolutional decoder from Li et al. handles final voxel refinement, positioning the transformer as a global-context encoder rather than a full replacement for convolutional processing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14.5 mIoU gap on Occ-ScanNet (45.33 vs. 30.83) and 3× latency reduction over ISO highlight that indoor MSSC has been under-served by methods designed for outdoor driving, and that adaptive serialization can close this gap efficiently. However, the Occ-ScanNet baselines include outdoor-oriented methods (TPVFormer, GaussianFormer, SurroundOCC) rather than dedicated indoor MSSC approaches, so the true competitive margin against indoor-specific methods remains unclear. Open questions include whether the learnable shift mechanism generalizes to higher-resolution or outdoor voxel grids, and whether the center-relative encoding's reliance on a single scene center degrades in multi-room or very large-scale indoor environments.
