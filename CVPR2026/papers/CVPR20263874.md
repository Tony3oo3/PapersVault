## Problem

Training-based virtual try-on methods overfit to curated paired datasets and degrade on in-the-wild inputs, while existing training-free pipelines (e.g., OmniVTON) require pose estimation, semantic correspondence, and multiple diffusion passes, making them slow and fragile. The paper asks whether the robustness of training-free VTON can be retained in a single diffusion pass without any auxiliary geometric or correspondence modules.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263874/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a person image, a reference garment image, a binary clothing-region mask on the person, a garment mask (to remove background), and an optional fixed text prompt. Following the ICL formulation, the masked person and garment are concatenated vertically and fed to a frozen FLUX.1-fill inpainting backbone at 1024×768 (or 512×320 for StreetTryOn). The output is a single try-on image from one diffusion pass; the same pipeline also produces subject-insertion results when given a background region and a reference subject.

## Method

PG-VTON operates entirely at inference time on a frozen MM-DiT inpainting model (FLUX.1-fill) using an in-context-learning input layout. Two lightweight controllers steer generation: (1) Patch-Anchored Identity Priming (PIP) samples K small garment patches, maps them via bounding-box correspondence into the masked person region, and uses this patched image as the conditioning input only during the first α fraction of denoising steps, after which the original masked input is restored—this transient anchor locks garment identity (color, pattern, logo) without hard-paste artifacts. (2) Reference-Aware Attention (RAA) modifies every DiT self-attention layer by element-wise scaling the person-to-garment logit block S_PG by a factor γ (default 3) before softmax, forcing person tokens to attend more strongly to garment tokens for fine-grained detail transfer. Both components are plug-and-play, require no parameter updates, and together enable a single-pass try-on that outperforms multi-stage training-free baselines on VITON-HD, DressCode, and StreetTryOn.

## Results

On VITON-HD, PG-VTON achieves FID_u = 9.873, FID_p = 6.749, SSIM_p = 0.877, and LPIPS_p = 0.086, outperforming the strongest training-free baseline OmniVTON (FID_u = 13.167, FID_p = 10.168) and the supervised Any2AnyTryon (FID_u = 10.971, FID_p = 8.188) that shares the same FLUX.1-fill backbone. On DressCode, the gains are even larger: FID_u = 6.693 and FID_p = 4.195 versus OmniVTON's 13.415/12.394 and Any2AnyTryon's 8.182/5.478. On the in-the-wild StreetTryOn benchmark, PG-VTON achieves the best Street-to-Street FID (21.028 vs. 23.470 for OmniVTON) and consistently lower CMMD across all four cross-domain settings (e.g., 0.301 vs. 0.688 on St2St), though it trails OmniVTON on Model-to-Model FID (11.911 vs. 8.983).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PG-VTON outperforms the primary training-free baseline OmniVTON on every metric for both VITON-HD and DressCode, with the largest gap on DressCode FID_p (4.195 vs. 12.394, a 66% reduction). Against the supervised Any2AnyTryon—which uses the same FLUX.1-fill backbone but is trained jointly on five datasets—PG-VTON still wins on all four VITON-HD metrics and all four DressCode metrics, demonstrating that inference-time guidance can match or exceed heavy multi-dataset supervision. Among earlier supervised methods, CatVTON (FID_u 15.228 on VITON-HD) and Leffa (FID_u 34.916) are substantially outperformed, while Leffa retains a slight edge on LPIPS_p for DressCode (0.071 vs. 0.055 for PG-VTON—actually PG-VTON wins here too). On the in-the-wild StreetTryOn benchmark, PG-VTON achieves the best Street-to-Street FID (21.028 vs. 23.470 for OmniVTON) and dominates on the more discriminative CMMD metric across all four settings (e.g., 0.301 vs. 0.688 on St2St), but trails OmniVTON on Model-to-Model FID (11.911 vs. 8.983) and Model-to-Street FID (37.098 vs. 33.450), suggesting the single-pass approach is slightly less effective when the source and target domains are closely matched.

## Relation to Prior Work

PG-VTON builds directly on CatVTON's in-context learning (ICL) formulation, which concatenates a masked person and a garment image vertically as input to a diffusion inpainting model. It departs from the broader VTON literature in two key ways: (1) it eliminates the supervised paired-data dependency of warp-based and diffusion-based VTON pipelines, and (2) it removes the multi-stage inference (pose estimation, semantic matching, multiple diffusion calls) required by prior training-free methods like OmniVTON. The conceptual departure is the observation that a frozen MM-DiT inpainting model already possesses strong contextual completion, so the task reduces to two lightweight inference-time controllers—Patch-Anchored Identity Priming and Reference-Aware Attention—rather than architectural changes or auxiliary networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is substantial: a single diffusion pass on a frozen FLUX.1-fill model replaces multi-stage pipelines, making training-free VTON viable for interactive e-commerce and large-scale deployment where latency and engineering complexity are critical. The finding that inference-time steering (early-step patch injection plus attention re-weighting) can match or exceed heavily trained multi-dataset models like Any2AnyTryon challenges the assumption that VTON quality requires task-specific supervision. However, the evaluation is limited to a single backbone (FLUX.1-fill) and two task families (VTON, subject insertion), leaving open whether the patch-guided controller generalizes to other architectures (e.g., UNet-based models), other garment categories, or broader image-editing settings. The paper itself acknowledges these as future directions.
