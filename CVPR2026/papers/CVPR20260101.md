## Problem

Existing approaches to incorporating Vision Foundation Models into latent diffusion tokenizers rely on distillation-based alignment, which the authors show inevitably degrades representation robustness under semantic-preserving perturbations. The paper asks whether a frozen VFM can serve directly as the tokenizer encoder without any distillation, and how the quality of tokenizer representations shapes representation learning throughout diffusion training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260101/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VFM-VAE takes a 256×256 (or 512×512) RGB image and produces a 32-channel latent at 1/16 spatial resolution (f16d32), which is then consumed by a diffusion model (LightningDiT-XL or REG/SiT-XL) to generate images. The decoder reconstructs the full-resolution image from this compact latent, and the system is evaluated on ImageNet 256×256 for both reconstruction (rFID, rIS) and generation (gFID, gIS, precision, recall), with additional text-to-image evaluation on DPG-Bench and MJHQ-30K at 256px.

## Method

VFM-VAE bypasses distillation by directly using a frozen VFM (SigLIP2-Large by default) as the encoder front: multi-scale features from shallow, middle, and final VFM layers are concatenated and projected by a lightweight network into a diagonal Gaussian posterior, from which the latent z is sampled. The decoder addresses the semantic-to-pixel gap via two mechanisms: Multi-Scale Latent Fusion decomposes z into a global style vector and spatial components at multiple scales, and Progressive Resolution Reconstruction Blocks (six Modulated ConvNeXt blocks from 8→256 resolution) synthesize the image with global style injected via modulated convolutions and spatial information supplied only to early low-resolution blocks. Training combines KL divergence, a VF loss enforcing cosine similarity and matrix distance to VFM features, multi-resolution L1 reconstruction, a DINOv2-based GAN loss, and LPIPS. The authors further introduce SE-CKNNA (averaging CKNNA over semantic-preserving perturbations) as a diagnostic metric and demonstrate that dual-side alignment—VFM-VAE on the tokenizer side plus REG on the diffusion side—yields consistently higher layer-wise VFM alignment and a 10× training speedup, reaching gFID 2.22 (w/o CFG) in 80 epochs and 1.62 at 640 epochs.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, VFM-VAE + REG achieves a gFID of 2.22 (w/o CFG) in just 80 epochs, a 10× speedup over prior tokenizers, and further reaches 1.62 gFID (w/o CFG) / 1.31 gFID (w/ CFG) at 640 epochs. As a standalone tokenizer, VFM-VAE attains rFID 0.52 and rIS 214.1, with a SE-CKNNA of 0.191 (only +1.6% relative change under perturbation) versus VA-VAE's 0.135 (−33.2% drop). Linear probing top-1 accuracy reaches 43.2%, substantially above VA-VAE's 31.9% and SD-VAE's 8.0%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SD-VAE [25], VFM-VAE delivers a dramatic improvement: VFM-VAE + REG reaches gFID 2.22 (w/o CFG) at 80 epochs, while SD-VAE + DiT requires 1400 epochs to reach 9.62, and even SD-VAE + REG needs 480 epochs for 2.20. Compared to VA-VAE [40], VFM-VAE + LightningDiT achieves gFID 3.80 at 64 epochs versus VA-VAE's 5.14 at the same budget, and VFM-VAE + REG attains 1.62 at 640 epochs versus VA-VAE + LightningDiT's 2.17 at 800 epochs. Against REPA-E [14], VFM-VAE + REG matches its 80-epoch gFID of 3.46 with 2.22, and surpasses its 800-epoch result of 1.83 with 1.62 at 640 epochs. Versus REPA [43] (SD-VAE + REPA), VFM-VAE + REG improves gFID from 7.90 to 2.22 at 80 epochs and from 5.90 to 1.62 at comparable budgets. Finally, compared to REG [37] with SD-VAE, VFM-VAE + REG reduces gFID from 3.40 to 2.22 at 80 epochs and from 2.20 to 1.67 at 480 epochs, demonstrating that dual-side alignment (tokenizer + diffusion) yields consistent gains over diffusion-side alignment alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant distillation-based paradigm for VFM-aware tokenizers (VA-VAE, REPA-E) by directly using a frozen VFM encoder within a VAE framework, motivated by the finding that alignment-based distillation inevitably degrades representation robustness. It builds on the representation-alignment literature (REPA, REG) that aligns diffusion model internals with VFM features, but shifts the focus to the tokenizer side and introduces a dual-side alignment strategy. The decoder design draws on progressive multi-scale synthesis (StyleGAN-T, ConvNeXt) and modulated convolutions, while the SE-CKNNA metric extends CKNNA from the Platonic Representation Hypothesis work to evaluate alignment stability under semantic-preserving perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 10× training speedup (gFID 2.22 in 80 epochs vs. ~800 for prior tokenizers) is practically significant for the LDM community, reducing compute costs for iterative research. The conceptual finding that distillation-based VFM alignment introduces brittleness under perturbations challenges a widely adopted assumption and suggests that preserving native VFM semantics is preferable to learned approximation. Limitations include sacrificed high-frequency fidelity from frozen encoders, complex multi-loss training objectives, and SE-CKNNA's dependence on the specific VFM used for comparison. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to higher resolutions, video, or text-to-image settings beyond the ImageNet-256 and limited text-to-image evaluations shown.
