## Problem

Existing 3D single object trackers are category-specific, requiring a separate model per object class, which severely limits scalability in multi-category deployment. Simply unifying these models into a single tracker fails because large inter-category variations in scale and structure make dense point-to-point correspondence unreliable without category-specific priors, particularly under sparse and occluded LiDAR point clouds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263180/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each frame, the tracker receives a template point cloud P_{t-1} and a search region point cloud P_t, both processed by a shared VoxelNet backbone into BEV feature maps of shape HW×C. The initial 3D bounding box (center, size, heading) provides the spatial prior for keypoint grid initialization; the target size is assumed constant over time. The output is the relative translation (Δx, Δy, Δz) and yaw rotation (Δθ) between consecutive frames, regressed via a residual log-likelihood loss.

## Method

UniKPT replaces dense point-to-point matching with a sparse keypoint-centric pipeline of three modules. The Adaptive Keypoint Extractor initializes N_q = n_x·n_y·n_z learnable queries by grid-sampling the template bounding box, then refines them through L transformer layers (MHSA + MHCA + FFN) with 3D coordinate encoding to produce scale-adaptive, structurally aware keypoint features. The Progressive Correspondence Aligner refines template-to-search correspondences over S=3 stages, each combining self-attention (intra-keypoint structure), global cross-attention (full search context), and deformable cross-attention (local sparse sampling), with coordinate offsets predicted per stage and supervised by L1 loss on ground-truth-transformed template keypoints. The Confidence-Aware Structural Localization module estimates per-correspondence reliability by comparing pairwise feature-difference relations (compressed via 1D convolution) between template and search, suppresses low-confidence matches, and aggregates the remaining high-confidence pairs through an MLP-Mixer for final bounding box regression.

## Results

On the challenging nuScenes dataset, UniKPT achieves 64.21% Success and 77.29% Precision (mean across Car, Pedestrian, Truck, Trailer, Bus), surpassing the previous category-unified SOTA TrackAny3D by 9.64%/11.04% and the best category-specific tracker P2P by 4.37%/5.16%. On KITTI, UniKPT achieves 69.7/81.4 (Car), 65.2/89.3 (Pedestrian), 68.7/81.4 (Van), and 76.4/94.9 (Cyclist), remaining competitive with category-specific trackers while outperforming the prior unified baseline MoCUT on all four categories. The method also reduces FLOPs from 1.11G to 0.55G and increases FPS from 32 to 37 compared to a point-to-point baseline with matched parameter count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On nuScenes, UniKPT achieves the best Success and Precision across all five object categories, outperforming the strongest category-specific tracker P2P (59.84/72.13) by 4.37%/5.16% in mean, and the prior unified SOTA TrackAny3D (54.57/66.25) by 9.64%/11.04%. The largest per-category gains over P2P appear on Truck (+7.96% Success) and Trailer (+4.16% Success), where structural variation is most pronounced. On KITTI, UniKPT trails the best category-specific P2P on Car (69.7 vs 73.6 Success), Pedestrian (65.2 vs 69.6), and Van (68.7 vs 70.3), but slightly edges it on Cyclist (76.4 vs 75.5) and substantially surpasses the previous unified baseline MoCUT on every category (e.g., +4.2% Success on Van). Under category-specific training, UniKPT still outperforms P2P on Car (+5.39% Success) and Trailer (+1.68% Success), confirming that the structural keypoint paradigm is effective regardless of training protocol. The keypoint-based design also halves FLOPs (0.55G vs 1.11G) and improves inference speed (37 vs 32 FPS) relative to a point-to-point baseline with matched parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant dense point-to-point Siamese tracking paradigm (SC3D, P2B, BAT, PTTR, P2P, etc.) by replacing exhaustive point interactions with a sparse, adaptive keypoint-to-keypoint matching strategy. It builds on the Siamese template-search formulation and Transformer-based feature interaction, but repurposes deformable attention (from Deformable DETR) and MLP-Mixer in a novel progressive alignment pipeline where keypoints are initialized from the target's bounding box geometry and refined over multiple stages. The category-unified setting itself was introduced by MoCUT (ICLR'24) and TrackAny3D (ICCV'25), but those methods still rely on dense point-level interactions; UniKPT's conceptual shift is that a small set of structurally meaningful keypoints, combined with confidence-weighted relational reasoning, can substitute for dense matching without category-specific priors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

A single model that tracks all object categories outperforming per-category specialists is practically significant for deployment in autonomous driving, where maintaining dozens of trackers is costly. The efficiency gain (0.55G vs. 1.11G FLOPs, 37 vs. 32 FPS) from sparse keypoint matching over dense point-to-point interaction makes the approach more attractive for real-time systems. Open questions include whether the rigid-body assumption (constant box size) limits applicability to deformable objects, whether the box-level pseudo-supervision for keypoint coordinates (Eq. 13) degrades under large pose changes, and whether the framework extends to other sparse 3D modalities or multi-object tracking settings.
