## Problem

Existing surgical video benchmarks either cover narrow procedural domains with frame/clip-level VQAs that ignore cross-temporal dependencies, or span broad scenarios but only test generic recognition (phases, instruments). No unified benchmark evaluates whether MLLMs can perform expert-level progressive spatiotemporal reasoning—causal action ordering, anomaly onset tracking, micro-transition localization—across diverse surgical specialties, leaving a critical gap between model capabilities and clinical reasoning demands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262989/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 2,841 surgical videos (standardized clips with ASR-aligned captions, tool masks, tissue bounding boxes, and action/anomaly tracks) and evaluates MLLMs under three progressive settings: BL (video + question only), KE (adds a Knowledge field with clinical background), and FC (adds a Clue field with targeted spatiotemporal evidence such as temporal windows and spatial ROIs). Each main question decomposes into three sub-questions (Q1→Q2→Q3) answered as multiple-choice, with the prior stage's answer carried forward as conditioning context, yielding 19,345 main and 59,177 sub-questions across five reasoning dimensions.

## Method

SurgCoT introduces a three-stage progressive reasoning framework (Q1 video-level comprehension → Q2 clip-level spatiotemporal localization → Q3 frame/patch-level localization) coupled with a five-tuple annotation protocol (Question → Option → Knowledge → Clue → Answer) at each stage. The Knowledge field supplies clinically relevant domain priors independent of video content, while the Clue field provides video-grounded spatiotemporal evidence (temporal windows, spatial ROIs, landmarks), jointly enabling a 'reason-then-decide' chain that progressively narrows scope from minutes to sub-second/pixel level. Five clinical reasoning dimensions—Causal Action Ordering, Cue-Action Alignment, Affordance Mapping, Micro-Transition Localization, and Anomaly Onset Tracking—are instantiated via ontology-driven VQA templates with spatial, temporal, and semantic distractors. Spatial and temporal supervision is generated through an evidence-mining pipeline combining YOLOv10 tissue detection, SAM2 tool segmentation, ByteTrack cross-frame tracking, and ASR-anchored action onset detection. The benchmark is validated by evaluating 10 MLLMs (commercial, open-source, and medical-specialized) under BL/KE/FC settings, revealing that while commercial models lead, all exhibit significant CoT reasoning gaps with performance dropping sharply at intermediate sub-question stages.

## Results

On the SurgCoT benchmark (19,345 main questions across 5 reasoning dimensions), commercial models achieve the highest Full-Context (FC) accuracy: GPT-5 (87.58%), Claude-Sonnet-4.5 (87.54%), and Gemini-2.5-Pro (87.20%), compared to the best open-source model Qwen3-VL-8B (86.92%) and best medical model MedGemma-27B-IT (86.37%). The five-tuple annotation protocol yields substantial gains: GPT-5 improves +10.96% from Baseline (BL) to FC, while Claude-Sonnet-4.5 gains +13.44% (74.10%→87.54%). However, sub-question analysis reveals a critical CoT gap: GPT-5 drops from 76.62% on main questions to only 47.60% on Q3 (frame-level localization), and Claude-Sonnet-4.5 falls to 30.44% on Q3, indicating models often answer correctly without valid intermediate reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SurgCoT is a benchmark paper, so the 'method' is the evaluation framework itself, and the 10 MLLMs serve as the compared systems. Commercial models (GPT-5, Claude-Sonnet-4.5, Gemini-2.5-Pro) consistently outperform both open-source and medical-specialized models across all five reasoning dimensions (CAO, CAA, AM, MTL, AOT) in all three settings. Among commercial models, GPT-5 leads on CAO (94.79% FC) and CAA (90.64% FC), while Claude-Sonnet-4.5 excels on AM (87.51% FC, +27.71% over BL) and AOT (86.39% FC, +20.60% over BL). Medical-specialized models benefit most from knowledge augmentation: LLaVA-Med-7B gains ~7% from BL to KE, whereas GPT-5 gains only ~4%, suggesting commercial models have stronger inherent reasoning. Open-source Qwen3-VL-8B (86.92% FC) nearly matches commercial models, but Qwen2.5-VL-7B (79.45% FC) and Lingshu-7B (74.95% FC) lag by 7–13 points. Critically, even top commercial models show severe CoT degradation at the sub-question level: GPT-5's Q3 accuracy is only 47.60% (KE) / 50.51% (FC) versus 76.62% on the main question, and Claude-Sonnet-4.5 drops to 30.44% on Q3, exposing a fundamental gap in sustained multi-step surgical reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of surgical video understanding and chain-of-thought evaluation. Prior surgical VQA benchmarks (Surgical-VQA, Cholec80-VQA, SSG-VQA, SurgBench, SurgVLM-Bench) treat videos as discrete frame/clip segments and evaluate single-step recognition or localization, explicitly ignoring cross-temporal dependencies and causal reasoning. SurgCoT departs from this by introducing a three-stage progressive reasoning framework (video→clip→frame) with a five-tuple annotation protocol that scaffolds multi-step CoT, repurposing the CoT paradigm from NLP into a structured, clinically grounded evaluation protocol for surgical spatiotemporal reasoning. The evidence-mining pipeline (YOLOv10, SAM2, ByteTrack) builds on standard detection/segmentation/tracking tools but applies them to generate the spatiotemporal supervision units that anchor the Clue field.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SurgCoT is the first benchmark to systematically evaluate whether MLLMs can sustain logically coherent multi-step reasoning chains in surgical video understanding, revealing that even top commercial models (GPT-5, Claude-Sonnet-4.5) achieve correct main-question answers while failing on intermediate sub-questions — suggesting pattern-matching rather than genuine progressive reasoning. The finding that structured Knowledge and Clue annotations substantially close this gap (especially for medical-specialized models) provides a concrete, reproducible scaffold for improving surgical MLLM reasoning. Open questions include whether the five reasoning dimensions fully capture the cognitive load of real surgical decision-making, whether the annotation protocol generalizes beyond the 35 procedures covered, and whether CoT scaffolding translates to improved clinical safety in deployment settings.
