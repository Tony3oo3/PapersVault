## Problem

Multimodal LLMs hallucinate objects not present in images due to two biases: text-visual bias (overreliance on prompts and prior outputs over visual evidence) and co-occurrence bias (spurious correlations causing prediction of frequently co-occurring objects). Existing mitigation methods either depend on auxiliary models (segmentation, detection, or additional MLLMs), rely on heuristically tuned priors that fail to generalize, or apply uniform weighting across all visual features without token-level granularity, leaving the root causes of hallucination insufficiently understood and unaddressed at the individual feature level.

## Input / Output

The method operates as a white-box inference-time decoder on any existing MLLM, consuming an RGB image (or video) encoded into visual token embeddings and a text prompt, and producing a generated text sequence (caption or VQA answer) of up to 256 tokens. It requires gradient access to the model's logit function via PyTorch autograd on input token embeddings, and uses spaCy (en_core_web_sm) for noun identification in previously generated tokens. No fine-tuning, auxiliary models, or external supervision is needed; the method is a drop-in replacement for the standard decoding loop.

## Method

GACD (Gradient-based Influence-Aware Constrained Decoding) estimates per-token influence on the current output logit via first-order Taylor expansion gradients, measuring how each visual token and text token perturbs the prediction. It then partitions visual tokens into object-related (linked to nouns already emitted) and unrelated sets using the most-influential visual token per detected noun. During decoding, it extends contrastive decoding by computing negative guidance logits from only the object-related visual tokens and amplifying the unrelated visual tokens with a computed weight α_m that explicitly rebalances visual influence to match the dominant text influence level. A sample-dependent early stopping criterion halts generation when the visual influence ratio drops below a threshold after an EOS token, preventing further ungrounded output. The entire procedure requires no auxiliary models, no fine-tuning, and no dataset-specific tuning beyond a per-model early-stopping threshold.

## Results

On the AMBER benchmark, GACD achieves the highest overall score across all six evaluated MLLMs, reaching 90.2 on LLaVA-v1.5 (vs. 83.5 base) and 91.1 on Qwen2-VL (vs. 90.1 base), with up to 33% reduction in sentence-level hallucination (hal) and 57% reduction in co-occurrence hallucination (cog). On POPE (adversarial), GACD improves F1 by up to 6.2 points (mPLUG-Owl2: 83.7 vs. 77.5 base). On LLaVA-QA90, GACD boosts accuracy by up to 92% (mPLUG-Owl2: 6.69 vs. 4.07 base) and detailedness by up to 45% (6.28 vs. 4.33 base), while maintaining or improving object recall (cov) relative to competing methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GACD outperforms all four baselines (RLAIF-V, VCD, M3ID, AVISC) on the AMBER overall score for every evaluated model, with the largest margin over VCD and M3ID (e.g., LLaVA-v1.5: 90.2 vs. 83.8/84.7) and a consistent 0.6–1.1 point lead over RLAIF-V. On POPE (adversarial F1), GACD achieves the best score for all four models, with the most dramatic gain on mPLUG-Owl2 (83.7 vs. 78.8 for VCD, 79.1 for M3ID, 79.7 for AVISC) and a 0.5–0.6 point edge over AVISC on LLaVA-v1.5 and InstructBLIP. On LLaVA-QA90, GACD leads all baselines on both accuracy and detailedness for every model (e.g., mPLUG-Owl2: 6.69 Acc / 6.28 Det vs. RLAIF-V's 5.03 / 5.33), confirming that gradient-based token-level adjustment surpasses both alignment-based (RLAIF-V) and image-level contrastive (VCD, M3ID) approaches. Notably, GACD also preserves or improves object recall (cov) relative to baselines, whereas VCD and M3ID typically sacrifice 2–5% recall to reduce hallucination.

## Relation to Prior Work

GACD extends the contrastive decoding lineage (VCD, M3ID, AVISC) by replacing their uniform or image-level visual suppression with a gradient-based, token-level influence estimation via first-order Taylor expansion. Unlike prior inference-time methods that rely on auxiliary segmentation/detection models or heuristically tuned priors, GACD derives object-aware visual token grouping directly from token-level gradient sensitivities, enabling sample-specific bias correction without external supervision. It also departs from training-based approaches (RLAIF-V, LPOI) by operating entirely at inference time, and from attention-based interventions by avoiding model-specific layer selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a principled, interpretable mechanism for quantifying and correcting the two dominant hallucination biases (text-visual and co-occurrence) at the individual token level, which prior work could only address at the image or sequence level. Its practical appeal—no fine-tuning, no auxiliary models, ~100% runtime overhead comparable to existing contrastive decoding—makes it a drop-in improvement for white-box MLLM deployments. Key limitations include its restriction to white-box models (gradient access required) and its dependence on the baseline model's visual influence ratio, meaning models already well-grounded visually (e.g., InternVL2) see minimal gains. Open questions include whether the gradient-based bias estimates can inform training objectives and how the method generalizes to tasks requiring relational or compositional reasoning beyond object presence.
