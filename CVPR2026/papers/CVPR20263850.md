## Problem

Existing model-decoupled personalized federated learning methods enforce homogeneity in the shared feature extractor, making them inapplicable to resource-heterogeneous edge clients that require different model complexities. Additionally, non-IID local data causes feature-space drift across clients, creating inconsistent representations that degrade both global knowledge aggregation and local personalization in a vicious cycle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263850/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) from its local non-IID dataset and produces a personalized class prediction. Unusually, the model is split into a low-rank feature extractor whose rank r_k and decomposed layer set Q_k are chosen per-client based on device resources, and a personalized classifier retained locally. Only the low-rank FE parameters are uploaded to the server, which reconstructs them to full-rank before weighted aggregation, keeping communication proportional to the rank rather than the full model size.

## Method

FedARA introduces a low-rank decomposition–reconstruction fusion scheme where each client autonomously selects a rank r_k and a subset of layers Q_k to factorize (W = U_q V_q^T), yielding a resource-adaptive feature extractor; the server decomposes the global FE into client-specific low-rank forms before distribution and reconstructs uploaded low-rank parameters back to full-rank for FedAvg-style aggregation. To counter feature-space inconsistency, an anchor-driven representation consistency mechanism computes per-class consistency anchors from the frozen GFE parameters (mean feature over local samples of each class) and imposes a Feature Alignment Consistency loss (L2 distance between each sample's representation and its class anchor) alongside the standard cross-entropy loss. The total local objective is L_CE + λ·L_FAC, with λ ramped linearly over the first 10 rounds to avoid over-regularizing an undertrained GFE. A Frobenius-norm decay regularization on each factorized layer stabilizes low-rank training, and the entire decomposition/reconstruction pipeline is offloaded to the server so clients incur no extra computational burden beyond the lighter low-rank forward/backward passes.

## Results

Under model heterogeneity (Table 3), FedARA achieves 90.84% on CIFAR-10, 54.54% on CIFAR-100, and 38.70% on Tiny-ImageNet under the Practical (α=0.1) setting, outperforming the best baselines (FedTGP and FedKD) by 1.45%, 5.79%, and 9.51% respectively. Under the Pathological setting, gains of 2.06%, 5.68%, and 5.65% are observed. Under homogeneous models, FedARA reaches 88.21% average accuracy on CIFAR-10 and 60.49% on CIFAR-100, surpassing the strongest baseline FedAS by 1.44% and 5.13% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedARA outperforms all 17 baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting. Under model heterogeneity, the closest competitors are FedTGP (best on CIFAR-10 at 89.39% and Tiny-ImageNet at 29.19% under Practical) and FedKD (best on CIFAR-100 at 48.75% under Practical); FedARA surpasses them by 1.45–9.51% depending on dataset. Under homogeneous models, FedAS is the strongest baseline (86.77% avg on CIFAR-10, 55.36% avg on CIFAR-100), with FedARA leading by 1.44% and 5.13% respectively. Methods relying on knowledge distillation (FedGen, FedKD) or mutual learning (FML, FedMRL) consistently underperform, with FML and FedMRL failing to converge on CIFAR-100 under Practical (α=0.1). The low-rank variant FedARA* retains most of the accuracy gain (e.g., 87.93% vs. 88.21% on CIFAR-10 homogeneous) while reducing communication and computation, confirming the efficiency–accuracy trade-off is favorable.

## Relation to Prior Work

FedARA extends the model-decoupled PFL paradigm (FedPer, FedRep, FedBABU, FedAS) by removing the homogeneity constraint on the shared feature extractor through low-rank decomposition, where each client independently selects a rank value to match its resource budget. The low-rank factorization technique itself follows Khodak et al. [10] for initialization and Frobenius regularization, but repurposes it from a compression tool (as in FedLMT, dual-side low-rank FL) into a mechanism for enabling heterogeneous shared components across clients. The anchor-driven representation alignment mechanism is a new contribution that addresses the feature-space drift problem identified in prior model-decoupled PFL work, where inconsistent shared representations degrade both global aggregation and local personalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedARA offers a practical path toward deploying PFL on genuinely heterogeneous edge devices by letting each client choose its model complexity without sacrificing cross-client knowledge transfer, while the anchor mechanism provides a lightweight (no extra model, no public dataset) solution to the representation inconsistency problem that plagues model-decoupled PFL. Limitations include evaluation restricted to image classification with relatively small CNNs on CIFAR/Tiny-ImageNet, and the low-rank approach is architecture-specific (FC and Conv layers). Open questions include whether the anchor mechanism generalizes to transformer-based backbones, whether the rank-selection strategy can be automated rather than manually configured, and how the method scales to larger datasets and deeper networks where the full-rank GFE reconstruction on the server may become a bottleneck.
