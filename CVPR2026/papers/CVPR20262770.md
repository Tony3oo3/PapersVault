## Problem

Unified text-to-image models consistently fail on reasoning-intensive generation tasks (e.g., solving mazes, assembling tangrams), while VLMs and LLMs can accurately solve the same tasks but cannot produce images. The paper identifies the bottleneck not as a lack of reasoning capacity but as the absence of a structured interface to translate high-level symbolic reasoning into precise, spatially grounded visual output.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262770/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes an input image (or a blank canvas for text-to-image), a natural-language instruction, and a rasterized SVG/HTML hint image that explicitly encodes reasoning steps (arrows, bounding boxes, geometric constraints) overlaid on the image plane. Images are resized to approximately 512×512 while preserving native aspect ratios. The hint image is consumed by encoding it through the same VAE as the input image and adding its latent directly to the noised input latent at every diffusion timestep, rather than being processed as a separate conditioning stream.

## Method

Hint2Gen builds on FLUX.1 Kontext, replacing its CLIP text encoder with Qwen2.5-VL-7B to jointly encode instructions and images. The core architectural modification is minimal: the rasterized SVG/HTML hint is encoded via the shared VAE, and its latent is added element-wise to the noised input latent at every denoising step, injecting strong spatial priors without altering the transformer backbone. Training data is constructed via an automated pipeline where GPT-5 generates HTML/SVG hints from source, edited, and difference images (for editing) or from prompts and edge maps (for T2I), supplemented by 20K web-rendered reasoning-intensive examples (chess, Sudoku, mazes, etc.). A two-stage post-processing and filtering pipeline (IoU-based alignment plus Gemini-2.5 Pro semantic validation) yields ~120K high-quality training pairs. The key insight is that code-structured hints serve as a general-purpose bridge: even without retraining, simply feeding such hints as auxiliary inputs to off-the-shelf models (GPT-Image, Nano Banana) significantly improves reasoning accuracy.

## Results

On the Reason2Gen benchmark (3,300 samples, 22 tasks, 7 reasoning dimensions), Hint2Gen achieves 31.04% overall accuracy, substantially outperforming the strongest closed-source baseline Nano Banana Pro (17.96%) and the best open-source model FLUX.1 Kontext (3.85%). Even when providing GPT-5-generated reasoning text as auxiliary input without retraining (denoted *), the best baseline Nano Banana Pro* reaches only 22.77%, still well below Hint2Gen. On GenEval for general text-to-image generation, Hint2Gen with hint input achieves 0.91 overall, matching BAGEL* (0.91) and surpassing GPT-Image* (0.89), confirming the generality of the hint-guided approach beyond reasoning-intensive tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Hint2Gen (31.04% overall) decisively outperforms all 20 baselines on Reason2Gen. Among closed-source models, Nano Banana Pro is the strongest standard baseline at 17.96%, and even with GPT-5 reasoning text as auxiliary input (Nano Banana Pro*, 22.77%), it trails Hint2Gen by 8.3 points; GPT-Image (11.33%) and Seedream 4.0 (6.89%) are further behind. Among open-source models, FLUX.1 Kontext (3.85%) is the best, but Hint2Gen—built on the same backbone—achieves 8× higher accuracy, demonstrating that the hint-conditioned training data and architecture modification are the primary drivers of the gain. On GenEval (general T2I), Hint2Gen with hint input (0.91) matches BAGEL* (0.91) and edges out GPT-Image* (0.89) and MoGao (0.89), while surpassing SDXL (0.55), DALL-E 3 (0.67), and SD3-medium (0.74) among generation-only models. The ablation (Table 2) confirms that removing the hint image from training drops overall accuracy from 31.04% to 6.93%, and that the structured SVG/HTML format itself (rather than the specific generator model) is the critical factor, as swapping GPT-5 for Qwen2.5-VL as hint generator only reduces accuracy by 1.5 points.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on FLUX.1 Kontext, a diffusion transformer for unified image generation and editing, by adding a minimal architectural modification: rasterized SVG/HTML hint latents are injected into the noised input latent at every diffusion timestep. Conceptually, it departs from prior unified multimodal models (BAGEL, MoGao, Show-o, Transfusion) that focus on perceptual fidelity, and from existing reasoning-aware T2I benchmarks (R2I-Bench, T2I-ReasonBench, Commonsense-T2I) that evaluate reasoning without providing a structured visual interface. The key positioning is that code-structured hints (SVG/HTML) serve as an executable intermediate representation between symbolic reasoning (VLMs/LLMs) and pixel-space generation—a role that prior unstructured cues like segmentation masks or bounding boxes do not fulfill, since they lack geometric relationships, layer hierarchies, and compositional logic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that the bottleneck for reasoning-intensive generation is the absence of a structured interface rather than insufficient reasoning capacity—reframes how the field should approach this problem, suggesting that bridging understanding and generation via explicit visual scaffolds is more tractable than improving reasoning within generative models themselves. The practical finding that hint injection improves off-the-shelf models without retraining offers an immediately deployable technique. However, absolute accuracy remains low (best ~31% overall on Reason2Gen), the approach depends on an external VLM/LLM to produce hints per task, and it is unclear whether this paradigm scales to open-ended, multi-step reasoning beyond the structured puzzle-like tasks evaluated. Open questions include whether hint generation can be internalized into the generative model and whether the approach transfers to continuous, non-discrete visual reasoning.
