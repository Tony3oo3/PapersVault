## Problem

Existing world-grounded human mesh recovery methods rely on future frames or global optimization, making them infeasible for real-time interactive applications such as AR/VR and telepresence. No prior system jointly satisfies the four essential criteria of online processing—system-level causality, reconstruction faithfulness, per-frame efficiency, and long-term temporal consistency—while recovering full 3D human motion in world coordinates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263871/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming RGB video one frame at a time in a strictly causal manner, maintaining a key-value cache of N previous frames (no future access). It outputs per-frame SMPL parameters (23×3 axis-angle joint rotations, 10-dim shape, root rotation and translation) in world coordinates, along with the corresponding camera trajectory (quaternion + metric-scaled translation). The camera trajectory is anchored to metric scale via MoGe-V2 monocular depth, with human-region pixels explicitly excluded from scale computation to handle dolly-zoom effects.

## Method

OnlineHMR uses a two-branch architecture that decouples local human motion from global camera trajectory. The camera-coordinate HMR branch is a ViT (initialized from HMR2.0) trained with overlapping sliding windows (step size 1) where the last frame attends to preceding frames via cross-attention; at inference, a KV cache over N prior frames enables constant-time causal streaming, and velocity/acceleration regularization losses suppress jitter. The human-centric SLAM branch runs MASt3R-SLAM incrementally on images with a soft human mask (SAM2 segmentation + dilation + Gaussian blur) to prevent dynamic-region feature matching, then applies EMA-based translation clamping and quaternion smoothing to the camera trajectory to indirectly regularize human motion. Metric scale is recovered per-frame from MoGe-V2 depth (ignoring human pixels) and held constant across the sequence, converting arbitrary-scale SLAM output to a metric world frame.

## Results

On the EMDB-2 world-coordinate benchmark, OnlineHMR achieves WA-MPJPE100 of 93.5 mm and RTE of 2.2%, outperforming the best prior online method Human3R (WA-MPJPE100 112.2 mm, RTE 2.2%) while remaining competitive with offline methods like TRAM (WA-MPJPE100 76.4 mm, RTE 1.4%). On camera-coordinate evaluation (3DPW/EMDB-1), OnlineHMR achieves PA-MPJPE of 43.7/46.0 mm and Accel of 6.4/9.0 mm, the best among all online approaches and within ~2 mm of the strongest offline baselines (TRAM: 35.6/45.7 mm). The method runs at 3.3 FPS with 0.30 s average per-frame delay, compared to 14–2435 s for offline methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among online methods, OnlineHMR clearly outperforms Human3R [4] on WA-MPJPE100 (93.5 vs. 112.2 mm) and matches it on RTE (2.2%), while lagging on W-MPJPE100 (310.4 vs. 267.9 mm) due to accumulated metric-scale drift over long sequences. It also substantially surpasses TRACE [36] (WA-MPJPE100 529.0 mm, RTE 17.7%) and JOSH3R [19] (WA-MPJPE100 220.0 mm, RTE 13.1%) on every reported metric. Against offline baselines, OnlineHMR trails TRAM [44] (WA-MPJPE100 76.4 mm, RTE 1.4%) and JOSH [19] (WA-MPJPE100 68.9 mm, RTE 1.3%) by roughly 17–25 mm in WA-MPJPE100 and ~0.9–1.0% in RTE, but narrows the gap to within 2 mm on camera-coordinate PA-MPJPE (46.0 vs. 45.7 on EMDB-1). On camera-coordinate metrics (Table 1), OnlineHMR achieves the best Accel among online methods (6.4 mm on 3DPW, 9.0 mm on EMDB-1), approaching offline TRAM (4.9 mm) and WHAM (5.3 mm), while beating Human3R which does not report Accel. The efficiency comparison (Table 3) shows OnlineHMR at 0.30 s average delay versus 0.21 s for Human3R but 14–2435 s for offline methods, confirming the practical advantage of the streaming design.

## Relation to Prior Work

OnlineHMR extends the TRAM framework from offline chunk-based inference to fully causal streaming processing, introducing a key-value cache mechanism and sliding-window learning strategy that decouple training parallelism from test-time causality. It departs from prior video HMR methods (TCMR, GLoT, WHAM) that rely on fixed-length windows requiring future frames or globally optimized SLAM backends, and from the concurrent Human3R which achieves online inference but with less faithful local motion. The two-branch architecture—camera-coordinate HMR with velocity regularization paired with a human-centric incremental SLAM using soft masking and EMA trajectory correction—represents a deliberate decoupling of local motion estimation from global trajectory anchoring, rather than the end-to-end joint modeling pursued by Human3R or the optimization-based approaches of JOSH and SLAHMR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work makes world-grounded human mesh recovery viable in interactive, low-latency settings (AR/VR, telepresence, perception-action loops) where offline pipelines are impractical, and formalizes four criteria (causality, faithfulness, temporal consistency, efficiency) that future online HMR systems should satisfy. The frequency-domain jitter metric offers a more perceptually aligned evaluation than standard acceleration-based measures. Key limitations remain: the method assumes continuous single-camera viewpoints and struggles with abrupt cuts or multi-camera inputs, and the W-MPJPE gap relative to offline methods reveals residual scale drift in long sequences that EMA correction only partially addresses. Whether the KV-cache streaming paradigm generalizes to multi-person or scene-interactive settings is an open question.
