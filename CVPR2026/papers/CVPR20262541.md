## Problem

Existing physics-based humanoid controllers focus on single-person motion tracking or use kinematic replay for interaction partners, which breaks physical consistency in tightly coupled assistive scenarios where the recipient cannot move independently without external support. Learning reactive, force-exchanging coordination between two physically entangled agents remains an open challenge, compounded by noisy motion-capture references from severe occlusion in close-range interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262541/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired reference motion sequences (joint rotations, positions, angular and linear velocities) from motion-capture datasets, along with real-time proprioceptive states and a partner-aware assistive state (partner joint kinematics, binary hand-contact indicators, 3D contact forces on elbows/wrists/fingertips, and previous actions). It outputs joint torque actions for both humanoid agents in a physics simulator, where the recipient operates under constrained dynamics (reduced PD gains and max torque limits per Table 1) to simulate physical impairment. The policy is goal-conditioned, receiving reference delta states between the current and next-timestep reference as the goal signal.

## Method

AssistMimic formulates assistive motion imitation as a fully coupled multi-agent MDP, jointly training symmetric goal-conditioned policies for both supporter and recipient via PPO. Three key mechanisms make this tractable: (1) weight initialization from a pre-trained single-person PHC tracking controller, with zero-padding for the expanded assistive-state input layer to preserve prior locomotion skills; (2) dynamic reference retargeting that continuously re-anchors the assistant's hand targets to the recipient's actual body pose (via nearest-joint anchoring) when agents are within a proximity threshold, preventing spatial misalignment; (3) contact-promoting rewards that suppress strict kinematic tracking and activate force-scaled, distance-decaying objectives when hands are near the recipient, enabling learning from noisy occluded demonstrations. Specialist policies are trained per motion cluster and distilled into a generalist via DAgger, with Physical State Initialization used to seed episodes from recent rollouts rather than noisy reference trajectories.

## Results

On the Inter-X dataset (specialist), AssistMimic achieves a 74.9% success rate and 113 mm MPJPE, outperforming the Sequential Training baseline (62.4% SR, 92.3 mm MPJPE). On HHI-Assist, AssistMimic reaches 85.8% SR with 127 mm MPJPE. Under unseen dynamics (Kp/Kd halved), AssistMimic retains 72.8% SR on Inter-X versus only 50.5% for Sequential Training. The generalist policy (single controller over 30 diverse clips) achieves 77.3% SR, rising to 94.7% after DAgger distillation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AssistMimic is the first method to successfully track closely interacting, force-exchanging human-human motions; all prior paradigms fail in this setting. The Sequential Training (Frozen-Recipient) baseline, which fine-tunes a single-agent tracker on the recipient and then freezes it, achieves 62.4% SR on Inter-X versus AssistMimic's 74.9%, and the gap widens under unseen dynamics (50.5% vs. 72.8% with halved PD gains), confirming that bidirectional co-adaptation is essential. The Kinematic-Recipient baseline (fixed replay of the recipient's motion, as in Human-X) fails qualitatively: the recipient 'stands up' independently regardless of the assistant's actions, and interpenetration generates large restorative forces that push the characters apart. Phys-Reaction, which relies on an isolated single-agent rollout, could not produce any stable recipient trajectory in assistive scenarios and was therefore inapplicable as a quantitative baseline. Removing the motion-prior weight initialization causes complete training failure (0% SR), underscoring that single-human locomotion priors are a prerequisite for convergence in this high-contact MARL setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

AssistMimic extends the single-agent physics-based motion tracking paradigm (PHC, DeepMimic, AMP) to a fully coupled multi-agent setting, where both a supporter and a recipient are trained jointly via MARL rather than using the kinematic-replay strategy employed by Human-X and Phys-Reaction. The key conceptual departure is that in assistive scenarios the recipient's motion is physically unrealizable without external support, making decoupled or open-loop partner modeling fundamentally ill-posed. Three practical innovations—zero-padded weight initialization from a single-agent prior, dynamic reference retargeting of the supporter's hand targets, and contact-promoting rewards that replace strict kinematic tracking during close proximity—make the coupled MARL problem tractable where naive joint training fails completely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first physics-based method to successfully track tightly coupled, force-exchanging assistive interactions, opening a path toward physically grounded assistive humanoid robots that can adapt to a partner's real-time dynamics rather than following a fixed script. However, the work remains entirely in simulation with no sim-to-real transfer, limited hand dexterity causes failures on precise grasping tasks, and the absence of visual observations restricts robustness to dynamic partner states. Open questions include whether the joint MARL formulation scales to more than two agents, whether the contact-promoting reward generalizes to manipulation tasks beyond support, and how to bridge the gap to real humanoid hardware.
