## Problem

Prior multi-modal social interaction understanding (MMSI) research assumes oracle-preprocessed identity-attributed cues (who said what, where each person is), but realistic AI assistants must extract these cues from raw audio-video. In multi-party scenes, identity attribution is the critical bottleneck: off-the-shelf extractors were designed for single-person settings, and even advanced Omni-LLMs like Gemini 2.5 Pro frequently misassign speech or bounding boxes to the wrong individual, causing prior pipelines to drop by 28.1% and advanced models by 9.52% when moving from oracle cues to raw input.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261579/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a raw audio-video query segment standardized to 5 dialogue turns (~14 seconds), a set of manually curated reference audio-image pairs (5-second voice clips and upper-body crops) for each participant in the scene, and a system prompt specifying the task (Speaking Target Identification or Pronoun Coreference Resolution). It produces a predicted referent identity (which participant the last speaker is addressing or referring to) along with a structured chain-of-thought trace that first confirms the last speaker and then infers the referent using verbal and non-verbal evidence.

## Method

Omni-MMSI-R is a reference-guided pipeline built on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 8) via LLaMA-Factory. Task-specific tools handle identity attribution: Whisper transcribes audio, SpeechBrain performs speaker verification by matching utterance embeddings against reference voice embeddings via cosine similarity, YOLO detects persons in the last frame, and OSNet re-identifies them against reference image embeddings. The extracted identity-attributed cues, raw references, and query video are jointly fed to the Omni-LLM, which performs a 2-step CoT (last-speaker confirmation, then referent inference) supervised by rejection-sampled reasoning traces generated by Gemini 2.5 Pro and lightly human-reviewed. Ablations show that the 2-step CoT outperforms a 3-step variant that additionally forces explicit cue extraction, and that combining raw references with tool-extracted cues yields the strongest performance (43.06% on Ego4D, 47.04% on YouTube), surpassing prior MMSI pipelines by 12–15% and advanced Omni-LLMs by 5–24% depending on the task.

## Results

On the Omni-MMSI task, Omni-MMSI-R achieves 43.06% average accuracy on Ego4D (STI 40.57%, PCR 45.54%) and 47.04% on YouTube (STI 37.46%, PCR 56.62%), outperforming the strongest Omni-LLM baseline (Gemini 2.5 Pro) by 5.36% on Ego4D and 2.24% on YouTube. For identity attribution, Omni-MMSI-R reaches 78.79% average attribution accuracy on Ego4D and 76.95% on YouTube, exceeding the best Omni-LLM by 23.68% and 18.91% respectively. Compared to prior MMSI pipelines that assume oracle-preprocessed cues, the improvement is 12.06% on Ego4D and 15.13% on YouTube.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Omni-MMSI-R outperforms all nine baselines on both datasets for social interaction understanding. Against the strongest Omni-LLM, Gemini 2.5 Pro (37.70% Ego4D, 44.80% YouTube), Omni-MMSI-R gains 5.36% and 2.24% respectively, with the gap narrowing on YouTube where Gemini's PCR score (53.47%) is closer. The prior MMSI pipelines (Lee et al. at 30.56%/31.91% and Li et al. at 31.00%/28.22%) are surpassed by 12.06% on Ego4D and 15.13% on YouTube, confirming that removing oracle cues and adding reference-guided attribution is critical. Among open-source Omni-LLMs, Qwen3 Omni 30B (29.72%/27.83%) and OmniVinci (31.04%/24.37%) trail by 13–23 points, while smaller models like Qwen2.5 Omni 7B (27.43%/20.09%) and R1-Omni (0.29%/0.17%) perform near chance level. For identity attribution specifically, Omni-MMSI-R's tool-based approach (78.79% Ego4D, 76.95% YouTube) substantially exceeds Qwen3 Omni 30B (55.11%/55.14%) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (35.64%/58.04%), demonstrating that lightweight verification tools (SpeechBrain, OSNet) are more reliable than end-to-end LLM attribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from prior MMSI work (Lee et al. 2024, Li et al. 2025) that assumes oracle-preprocessed identity-attributed social cues as input, instead formulating a harder task where the system must extract and attribute cues from raw audio-video. Methodologically, it repurposes off-the-shelf tools (Whisper, SpeechBrain, YOLO, OSNet) in a reference-guided pipeline architecture, combining them with LoRA fine-tuning of an Omni-LLM (Qwen2.5-Omni-7B) for CoT social reasoning. The reference-guided attribution strategy draws on the human cognitive insight that familiar faces and voices anchor identity across modalities, a principle previously applied in speaker verification and person ReID but not integrated into a unified social interaction understanding pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper quantifies a critical deployment gap: prior MMSI pipelines lose 28.1% accuracy and even humans/advanced Omni-LLMs lose 9.52% when transitioning from oracle cues to raw input, establishing identity attribution as the central bottleneck for socially intelligent AI. The reference-guided approach is practically motivated (references are collectible via enrollment) and the CoT data curation via rejection sampling provides a reusable recipe. However, absolute accuracy remains modest (43–47%), evaluation is limited to social deduction games with ~5 participants, and the 7B model's reliance on external tools raises questions about real-time latency and scalability to larger groups or more naturalistic settings.
