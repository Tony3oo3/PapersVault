## Problem

Existing video reasoning RL methods organize training data by task format (e.g., multiple-choice, open-ended QA) rather than by the underlying cognitive abilities required, causing models to learn superficial task cues instead of transferable reasoning skills. This mismatch forces broad coverage over many ability-task combinations, making RL training costly, and prior work further compounds the problem with complex algorithmic designs like specialized temporal architectures or multi-objective reward frameworks. The paper asks whether comparable reasoning performance can be achieved with substantially fewer training samples by reorganizing data along cognitive ability dimensions.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 16 video frames (256 patches of 28×28 resolution each) alongside a text question, and produces a structured output consisting of a chain-of-thought reasoning trace enclosed in think tags followed by a final answer. The consistency reward is computed externally by a Qwen2.5-72B model that evaluates whether the intermediate reasoning steps logically support the final prediction, yielding a binary alignment signal. Training uses 8 rollout responses per prompt for GRPO advantage estimation over 2 epochs.

## Method

VAST organizes video understanding into three cognitive layers inspired by Piaget's developmental stages—Perception (spatial entities, spatio-temporal dynamics), Reasoning (causal, intentional, procedural, relational), and Cognition (rule discovery, symbol comprehension, theme inference, expression analysis)—and uses this taxonomy to curate VAST-15K with balanced 5K-per-layer coverage from Video-R1-260K via multi-model classification, difficulty filtering, and stratified selection. The VideoVAST training framework applies GRPO with KL regularization disabled (β=0) and instead introduces a lightweight consistency reward (λ=0.7) that bonuses responses whose reasoning traces align with their final answers, establishing a preference ranking where consistent correct responses receive the highest reinforcement. This design eliminates the supervised fine-tuning initialization stage entirely, reducing training from 425K to 15K samples (96% fewer) and ~440 to 123 GPU hours (72% fewer) while achieving 66.3% on MVBench and 57.4% on VAST-Bench, outperforming Video-R1 under identical settings.

## Results

VideoVAST-7B (16 frames) achieves 66.3% on MVBench, 66.1% on MMVU, 74.2% on TempCompass, 57.7% on VideoMME, and 42.5% on Video-Holmes, outperforming Video-R1-7B by +3.6, +1.9, +1.6, +0.3, and +2.4 points respectively. On the capability-stratified VAST-Bench, VideoVAST scores 57.4% overall versus 54.3% for Video-R1 and 55.3% for VideoRFT, with the largest gains on Causal Reasoning (+8.7) and Relational Reasoning (+7.7). Critically, VideoVAST achieves these results using only 15K training samples and ~123 GPU hours, compared to Video-R1's 425K samples and ~440 GPU hours (96% and 72% reductions respectively).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline Video-R1-7B (same 16-frame setting), VideoVAST wins on 6 of 7 benchmarks: MVBench (+3.6), Video-Holmes (+2.4), MMVU (+1.9), TempCompass (+1.6), VideoMMMU (+0.8), and VideoMME (+0.3), while losing slightly on VSI-Bench (−1.1). Compared to VideoRFT-7B, VideoVAST leads on MVBench (+2.4), Video-Holmes (+5.9), VideoMMMU (+2.4), and VideoMME (+1.8), with a marginal edge on MMVU (+0.1) and TempCompass (+0.7), again trailing on VSI-Bench (−1.1). Against the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model (CoT, 16 frames), VideoVAST gains +8.9 on MVBench, +6.9 on MMVU, +5.8 on VSI-Bench, and +14.7 on Video-Holmes, confirming that ability-stratified RL training substantially lifts reasoning beyond the pretrained checkpoint. On the capability-level VAST-Bench, VideoVAST's 57.4% overall surpasses Video-R1 (54.3%) and VideoRFT (55.3%), with the largest per-ability margins on Causal Reasoning (+8.7 over Video-R1) and Expression Analysis (+6.7). The efficiency comparison is stark: VideoVAST uses 96% fewer training samples (15K vs. 425K) and 72% fewer GPU hours (~123 vs. ~440) than Video-R1 while matching or exceeding its accuracy, and it eliminates the SFT initialization stage entirely (RL-only training), unlike both Video-R1 and VideoRFT which require 165K and 102K annotated CoT samples for supervised warm-up.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (from DeepSeekMath) as its RL backbone but departs from the dominant video-reasoning paradigm (Video-R1, VideoRFT) by shifting the innovation from algorithmic design to data organization. Rather than introducing specialized temporal architectures or multi-objective reward frameworks, it restructures training data along a Piaget-inspired cognitive hierarchy (Perception → Reasoning → Cognition) and replaces KL regularization with a lightweight consistency reward. The key conceptual departure is the claim that ability-stratified curation, not algorithmic complexity, is the primary driver of efficient video reasoning, and that supervised fine-tuning initialization can be eliminated entirely when data is well-organized.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 96% reduction in training samples and 72% reduction in GPU hours, if it generalizes beyond the single 7B Qwen2.5-VL model tested, would substantially lower the barrier to video reasoning research. The VAST taxonomy and VAST-Bench provide a useful diagnostic lens for identifying which cognitive layer a model is weak on, moving beyond aggregate benchmark scores. Open questions include whether the taxonomy transfers to larger models or different architectures, whether the 72B consistency-model judge is practical at scale, and whether the modest absolute gains over Video-R1 (+3.6 on MVBench) persist as base models improve. The paper's evidence is confined to one model family and size, limiting confidence in the generality of the efficiency claims.
