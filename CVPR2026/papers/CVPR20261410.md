## Problem

Learning new robot manipulation tasks on new platforms from only a handful of demonstrations remains challenging, while abundant cross-embodiment videos (humans, other robots) are difficult to reuse due to embodiment, camera, and scene disparities. Existing world models either generate pixels (wasting capacity on irrelevant backgrounds and textures) or produce language tokens (lacking spatial precision for fine-grained motion), and both are computationally expensive for real-time planning. The paper asks whether a compact, embodiment-agnostic 3D representation can bridge this gap.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB-D frame and a natural-language task instruction as input. It outputs a 3D scene trace: a 20×20 uniform grid of keypoint trajectories over 32 future timesteps, where each point is (x, y, z) in the reference camera frame (x, y are image-plane coordinates, z is depth). Rather than predicting absolute positions, the network predicts velocity-like temporal increments (ΔT) between consecutive frames, which are integrated via 100-step ODE sampling at test time. The resulting trace is converted to robot joint commands through inverse kinematics for execution on a Franka Research 3.

## Method

TraceGen is a 0.67B-parameter world model that predicts future scene-level 3D motion in trace-space rather than pixel space. It uses a Prismatic-VLM multi-encoder front end (frozen DINOv3 and SigLIP for RGB, SigLIP with a learnable 1×1 stem adapter for depth, frozen T5 for text) whose features are concatenated and linearly projected into unified visual tokens. These condition a flow-based decoder adapted from CogVideoX's 3D transformer, which applies spatial 2×2 patchification over the 20×20 keypoint grid and uses Adaptive LayerNorms for conditioning fusion. The decoder is trained with a linear-interpolation stochastic interpolant (flow matching) to predict velocity-like keypoint increments, minimizing an L2 loss against ground-truth temporal differences. The model is pretrained on the TraceForge-123K corpus (123K videos, 1.8M observation-trace-language triplets from 8 heterogeneous sources spanning human and robot data), where TraceForge compensates camera motion, reconstructs 3D point trajectories via TAPIP3D/CoTracker3, and applies arc-length-based speed retargeting to normalize embodiment-dependent timing.

## Results

On four real-world manipulation tasks (Clothes, Ball, Brush, Block) on a Franka Research 3 robot, TraceGen (0.67B params) achieves 80% overall success with a 5-video robot warm-up and 82.5% with 15 videos, compared to 25% for a from-scratch model under the same warm-up. In the human-to-robot transfer setting (5 uncalibrated handheld phone videos, no target-robot data), TraceGen reaches 67.5% success. Inference is 3.8× faster than trace-based baselines and 50–600× faster than video-generation models (NovaFlow Wan2.2 requires >600× longer inference). In the zero-shot setting, all sub-10B-parameter methods except TraceGen produce 0% success across all tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TraceGen outperforms all three baseline families on both success rate and inference speed. Against video-generation baselines (AVDC and NovaFlow with Wan2.2/Veo3.1 backbones), TraceGen achieves 80% success with 5-video warm-up while these methods either fail entirely (sub-10B variants) or require >600× longer inference (NovaFlow Wan2.2); no practical few-shot warm-up path exists for the large video models due to proprietary APIs or compute cost. Against the trace-based baseline 3DFlowAction, TraceGen is 3.8× faster and achieves higher success; 3DFlowAction additionally required ground-truth segmentation masks because its built-in mask estimator failed frequently. A key differentiator is that TraceGen is the only sub-10B model to produce executable trajectories in the zero-shot setting, whereas all other sub-10B baselines score 0% across all four tasks. The ablation in Table 2 further shows that cross-embodiment pretraining (70% on Ball+Block) substantially outperforms single-source pretraining (SSV2: 25%, Agibot: 45%) under identical 5-video warm-up.

## Relation to Prior Work

TraceGen departs from the dominant pixel-space (NovaFlow, AVDC) and token-space (VLM planners) world-model paradigms by predicting future motion in a compact 3D trace-space, building on the trace-prediction line (Track2Act, FLIP, 3DFlowAction) while extending it from 2D/in-lab/object-centric settings to scene-level 3D trajectories from in-the-wild videos with moving cameras. Architecturally, it adapts CogVideoX's 3D transformer and the Prismatic-VLM multi-encoder fusion strategy (DINOv3 + SigLIP + T5) to a flow-matching decoder operating on keypoint increments, and leverages the Stochastic Interpolant framework for generation. The TraceForge data pipeline extends prior trace-annotation efforts by adding camera-motion compensation, multi-viewpoint 3D point tracking (TAPIP3D + CoTracker3 + VGGT), and speed retargeting to unify heterogeneous human and robot sources.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50–600× inference speedup over video-generation world models makes closed-loop, real-time planning in trace space practically viable, and the 5-video warm-up requirement dramatically lowers the data barrier for deploying new robot skills. The human→robot transfer result (67.5% from five uncalibrated phone videos) suggests trace space is a genuinely embodiment-agnostic interface, though the evaluation is limited to four tabletop tasks on a single Franka arm with a basic IK controller. Open questions include whether richer downstream policies (beyond tracking) can further exploit scene-level traces, whether the 20×20 grid suffices for high-DOF or deformable-object manipulation, and whether the approach scales to multi-robot or long-horizon tasks.
