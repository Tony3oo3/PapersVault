## Problem

Existing spatiotemporal multi-view representation learning (SMVRL) methods for event-based action recognition suffer from two critical flaws: translation-variant spatial binning that fragments coherent action events and degrades feature discriminability, and naive early concatenation fusion that ignores the dimension misalignment and semantic discrepancy between T-H and T-W views. Additionally, frame-like H-W representations embed temporal motion cues between a limited number of frames, falling short in capturing the physically continuous dynamics of human actions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260567/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw sparse event streams (x, y, t, polarity) at 346×260 sensor resolution, discretizing the T-axis into 224 bins and resizing the resulting T-H and T-W maps to 224×224. The TISM conversion produces compact 2-channel maps per view (global event count and polarity sum), and the DDCF outputs per-view logits that are dynamically fused into a single action class prediction over C classes. The entire pipeline operates on dense 2D maps rather than preserving sparsity, trading memory for translation-invariance and CNN compatibility.

## Method

SMV-EAR comprises three components: (1) TISM representation, which uses a global bin-less window with sum aggregation on count and polarity measurements to produce translation-invariant 2-channel T-H and T-W maps, avoiding the spatial binning that fragments events; (2) DDCF architecture, where independent ResNet-18 branches extract view-specific logits, and a multi-head attention block over globally pooled 512-dim features (pre-classification-head) generates sample-wise dynamic weights to fuse the two views, respecting their semantic heterogeneity; (3) DTW augmentation, which applies randomly selected non-uniform warping functions (linear, power, exponential, cosine) to non-overlapping timestamp intervals to mimic diverse speed variations in real-world actions while preserving temporal ordering and cross-view consistency. The full system achieves SOTA accuracy on three benchmarks with 30% fewer parameters and 36% fewer MACs than the MVF-Net baseline.

## Results

On HARDVS, SMV-EAR achieves 59.63% Top-1 and 67.56% Top-5 accuracy, surpassing the prior best multi-view method MVF-Net (52.61%) by +7.0%. On DailyDVS-200, it reaches 54.65% Top-1 (+10.7% over MVF-Net's 43.98%), and on THU-EACT-50-CHL it attains 66.7% Top-1 (+10.2% over MVF-Net's 56.5%). Despite these gains, SMV-EAR uses only 23.5M parameters and 1.8G MACs, representing a 30.1% parameter reduction and 35.7% compute reduction compared to MVF-Net (33.6M params, 2.8G MACs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMV-EAR outperforms all 20 compared baselines across all three datasets. Against the closest multi-view competitor MVF-Net [8], it gains +7.0% on HARDVS (59.63% vs 52.61%), +10.7% on DailyDVS-200 (54.65% vs 43.98%), and +10.2% on THU-EACT-50-CHL (66.7% vs 56.5%), while using 30.1% fewer parameters and 35.7% fewer MACs. Among frame-based methods, it surpasses TSM (52.63% on HARDVS) by +7.0% and SlowFast (50.63%) by +9.0%, despite using a much lighter ResNet-18 backbone versus their ResNet-50. Token-based methods like Swin-T (51.91% HARDVS, 48.06% DailyDVS-200) and TimeSformer (50.77%, 44.25%) are outperformed by 7.7–10.4% while requiring 10–60× more MACs. Spike-based methods (SDT, Spikeformer, HMAX SNN, Motion SNN) remain 15–25% behind in accuracy, confirming that dense multi-view representations still dominate for action recognition. On THU-EACT-50-CHL, SMV-EAR also beats the dedicated event-based method EV-ACT (58.5%) by +8.2% and EventMG (58.8%) by +7.9%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repositions spatiotemporal multi-view representation learning (SMVRL) from event-based object recognition to action recognition, building directly on MVF-Net's multi-view fusion architecture and Zubić et al.'s window-measurement-aggregation decomposition for event encoding. It departs from prior SMVRL methods by identifying two specific pathologies—translation-variant spatial binning and naive early concatenation—that degrade performance when the paradigm is naively transferred, and addresses them through a principled translation-invariant representation (TISM), a dual-branch dynamic cross-view fusion architecture (DDCF), and a bio-inspired temporal warping augmentation (DTW). The core conceptual shift is embedding temporal motion cues within T-H and T-W projected maps rather than between sequential H-W frames, which the authors argue better captures the physically continuous nature of human actions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a more principled SMVRL formulation can simultaneously improve accuracy by 7–10.7% and reduce parameters/computations by ~30–36% relative to the prior SMVRL baseline, suggesting that representation quality and architectural alignment matter more than model scale for event-based action recognition. Open questions remain around handling camera-induced background motion (which produces near-linear streaks on temporal maps that occlude action-relevant patterns), modeling micro-actions at limited sensor resolution, and whether frequency-domain processing or learned augmentation policies could further exploit the non-stationary dynamics visible in T-H/T-W maps. The translation-invariance guarantee also opens a path toward more robust deployment where action location varies across scenes.
