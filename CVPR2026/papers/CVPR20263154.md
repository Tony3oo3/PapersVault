key_contribution: KnowVal introduces a knowledge-augmented autonomous driving system that achieves visual-language reasoning through mutual guidance between open-world perception and a driving knowledge graph (encoding traffic laws, defensive driving principles, and ethical norms), and integrates a Value Model trained on a 160K human-preference dataset to select trajectories via interpretable, knowledge-grounded scoring, achieving the lowest collision rate on nuScenes and state-of-the-art results on Bench2Drive and NVISIM.
limitations: The paper does not include a dedicated limitations section, but acknowledges that current benchmarks lack sufficient data and metrics to evaluate many forms of improper driving behavior (e.g., failing to yield, overtaking in tunnels), and that the slight increase in L2 trajectory deviation from human baselines—while attributed to safer driving—remains difficult to fully validate without value-sensitive evaluation protocols. Additionally, the system's reliance on multiple LLM calls at inference (perception verbalization, entity extraction, retrieval) and on LLM-annotated preference labels for Value Model training raises practical concerns about real-time latency and annotation reliability that are not quantitatively addressed.

surprise_factor: No particularly surprising findings — the performance gains are moderate and consistent across benchmarks (lowest collision rate on nuScenes, +3.35 DS on Bench2Drive, +2.8 PDMS on NVISIM). ## Problem

Existing end-to-end and VLA autonomous driving systems rely on data-driven imitation or limited reinforcement rewards, making it difficult to capture the complex decision logic of traffic laws, ethical norms, and defensive driving principles. They also lack genuine visual-language reasoning (where perception and language mutually guide each other) and a dedicated mechanism to judge whether predicted future states are desirable, limiting safety, interpretability, and generalization to open-world scenarios.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263154/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes multi-camera surround-view images, radar/LiDAR, navigational data, and optional user instructions, and produces a final planning trajectory selected from N_T diverse candidates. Unusually, the pipeline operates in a closed feedback loop: perception outputs are verbalized into retrieval queries, and the retrieval module returns both ranked knowledge entries (as vector embeddings for the Value Model) and a list of supplementary perception targets that are fed back into the next timestep's perception stage. The Value Model outputs per-rule scalar scores in [-1, 1] (negative = rule violation, positive = compliance, zero = irrelevant), which are aggregated via a weighted-decay scheme into a single trajectory score.

## Method

KnowVal integrates three components in a mutual-guidance loop. First, retrieval-guided open-world perception combines standard specialized perception with VL-SAMv2/OpenAD for long-tail 3D objects and a VLM for abstract scene concepts, with the retrieval module from the previous timestep directing targeted re-perception. Second, a driving knowledge graph is pre-constructed by organizing traffic laws, defensive-driving video extractions (via Gemini 2.5-Pro), driver interview transcripts, and ethical guidelines into a hierarchical forest, then linking them into a graph via LLM-based entity extraction (Traffic-Sign-Device, Road-User, Driving-Maneuver, Road-Condition) with semantic edge weights and text keys; at inference, a perception verbalizer converts structured outputs into queries, a lightweight LLM performs two-layer keyword extraction, and Top-K graph expansion retrieves relevant entries—critically, only 'native' (verbatim, unprocessed) text nodes are returned to prevent LLM hallucination. Third, the planner (e.g., HENet++ or GenAD) is modified to generate N_T diverse trajectories via Gaussian noise injection with a diversity penalty, and a Transformer Encoder–MLP Decoder Value Model scores each trajectory against each retrieved knowledge entry using future-state tokens as key-value pairs; the final trajectory is selected by a normalized weighted-decay sum over per-rule scores. The Value Model is trained on a 160K trajectory-knowledge preference dataset annotated by Qwen-VL-Max with manual review, using MSE loss.

## Results

On nuScenes, KnowVal-HENet++ achieves the lowest collision rate among all compared methods (0.03% under VAD/STP3 metrics and 0.12% under UniAD metrics), while KnowVal-GenAD reaches 0.11% VAD/STP3 collision rate. On Bench2Drive, KnowVal-SimLingo sets a new state-of-the-art with a Driving Score of 88.42 and Success Rate of 69.03%, improving +3.35 DS and +1.76% SR over the previous best VLA method SimLingo. On NAVSIM (NVISIM), KnowVal-DiffusionDrive achieves a PDMS of 90.9 (+2.8 over DiffusionDrive) and KnowVal-iPad reaches 92.9 (+1.2 over iPad), confirming plug-and-play compatibility with existing planners.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On nuScenes, KnowVal-HENet++ achieves the lowest collision rate (0.03% VAD/STP3, 0.12% UniAD), outperforming HENet++ (0.05%/0.13%), SSR (0.06%/0.15%), and SparseDrive (0.08%/0.32%), though at a slight L2 cost (1.32m vs 1.29m avg). KnowVal-GenAD reduces collision rate from GenAD's 0.19% to 0.11% (VAD/STP3) but increases L2 from 0.52m to 0.65m, reflecting a safety-over-imitation trade-off. On Bench2Drive, KnowVal-SimLingo (DS 88.42, SR 69.03%) surpasses all 11 baselines, with the largest gap over SimLingo (+3.35 DS) and ORION (+10.68 DS); the E2E baselines (TCP, ThinkTwice, DriveAdapter, DriveTransformer) all score below 65 DS. On NAVSIM, KnowVal-DiffusionDrive improves PDMS from 88.1 to 90.9 (+2.8) and KnowVal-iPad from 91.7 to 92.9 (+1.2), with gains concentrated in the No-Collision (NC) and Drivable Area Compliance (DAC) sub-metrics, confirming that knowledge-guided value assessment primarily enhances safety-relevant decisions rather than comfort or progress.

## Relation to Prior Work

KnowVal positions itself at the intersection of three research threads: end-to-end autonomous driving (UniAD, VAD, GenAD, HENet++, DiffusionDrive), vision-language-action models (SimLingo, ORION), and retrieval-augmented generation (GraphRAG, LightRAG). It departs from purely data-driven planning by introducing an explicit driving knowledge graph (constructed from traffic laws, defensive driving videos, and driver interviews) with a perception-guided retrieval mechanism that grounds knowledge access in visual observations rather than user text prompts. The mutual guidance loop—where retrieval identifies missing perceptual information and perception refines queries for retrieval—is a conceptual departure from both linear RAG pipelines and existing VLA architectures that confine reasoning to chain-of-thought. The Value Model, trained on a curated preference dataset, extends the world-model concept (as in ThinkTwice) by adding an interpretable, knowledge-grounded evaluation layer for candidate trajectories.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in autonomous driving: the inability of imitation-based and reward-based methods to capture the full logic of traffic laws, defensive driving principles, and ethical norms. By grounding decisions in verbatim legal and ethical text (rather than LLM-summarized paraphrases), the system offers a path toward explainable, value-aligned driving that current benchmarks cannot adequately measure. Key open questions include the scalability and maintenance cost of the knowledge graph, the reliance on LLM-annotated preference labels (Qwen-VL-Max) for Value Model training, and whether the slight L2 increase reflects genuinely better driving or simply deviation from human norms. The paper also highlights that existing evaluation metrics (collision rate, driving score) fail to capture many forms of improper behavior, pointing to a broader need for value-sensitive benchmarks.
