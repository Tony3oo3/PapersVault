## Problem

Current Video-LLMs exhibit Time-Agnosticism: they treat videos as unordered bags of evidence rather than causally ordered sequences, causing two coupled failures in streaming settings—Temporal Order Ambiguity (inability to reason over correct chronological order) and Past-Current Focus Blindness (failure to distinguish present observations from accumulated history). Existing streaming approaches either require expensive specialized datasets and heavy training, or adopt memory mechanisms that yield unsatisfactory performance, leaving a gap for a lightweight, model-agnostic solution under strict online, time-causal constraints.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frames arriving sequentially as a stream (not the full video upfront) and a text question issued at an arbitrary time t, requiring a causally valid answer using only frames f₁ through f_t. At inference, the model first attempts to answer from a short local window of recent KV-cache tokens; only when prediction entropy exceeds a threshold (δ=0.6) does it expand retrieval into the full growing memory buffer, with a maximum of 64 recalled frames. The output is a free-form text answer generated by the LLM from the loaded context.

## Method

WeaveTime is a two-stage, Video-LLM-agnostic framework. At training time, Streaming Order Perception Enhancement (SOPE) appends a Temporal Reconstruction sub-question to the QA prompt: frames are shuffled with explicit timestamp tokens, and the model must first reconstruct the correct temporal order before answering the original question, instilling order-aware representations via LoRA fine-tuning on 30K general video samples with no streaming-specific data. At inference time, the Past-Current Dynamic Focus Cache (PCDF-Cache) implements an uncertainty-gated policy: low-entropy queries are answered from the current short window alone, while high-entropy queries trigger a Coarse-to-Fine recall that first narrows candidates via frame-level cosine similarity, then refines with ColBERT-style max-sim late-interaction over individual token vectors for precise temporal localization. This coupling of order-aware training with uncertainty-triggered hierarchical retrieval eliminates the need for architectural modifications to the underlying Video-LLM.

## Results

On OVO-Bench Real-Time (multi-turn), WeaveTime with LLaVA-OV-7B achieves 68.82% average accuracy, surpassing StreamBridge (61.64%) and ReKV (61.72%) by +7.18% and +7.10% respectively. On Streaming-Bench Real-Time, WeaveTime reaches 72.13% average, improving over StreamBridge (68.39%) and ReKV (66.15%) by +3.74% and +5.98%. Gains are most pronounced on temporal-aware sub-tasks: Action Perception (+7.56%), Event Understanding (+9.04%), and Action Recognition (+11.09%) over the strongest baseline. With Qwen2-VL-7B, WeaveTime similarly leads at 66.28% (OVO-Bench) and 75.39% (Streaming-Bench), outperforming StreamBridge by +2.93% and +3.38%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WeaveTime consistently outperforms both direct streaming baselines (StreamBridge and ReKV) across both backbones and both benchmarks in the multi-turn setting. With LLaVA-OV-7B, WeaveTime gains +7.10% over ReKV and +7.18% over StreamBridge on OVO-Bench, and +5.98% over ReKV on Streaming-Bench; with Qwen2-VL-7B, it leads StreamBridge by +2.93% (OVO-Bench) and +3.38% (Streaming-Bench). Against proprietary offline models (Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4o), WeaveTime with LLaVA-OV-7B narrows the gap substantially on OVO-Bench (68.82 vs. 69.32 for Gemini) and matches or exceeds GPT-4o on Streaming-Bench (72.13 vs. 73.28), all under strict online time-causal constraints. On the C2F retrieval ablation (Table 4), WeaveTime's coarse-to-fine strategy improves QAEGO4D recall from 23.9 to 25.2 and accuracy from 54.3 to 55.2 over ReKV, while fine-only retrieval causes OOM on MLVU. In the data-efficiency comparison (Table 5), WeaveTime's SOPE training achieves 55.7 on OVO-Bench using only 30K offline samples and 8 GPUs, matching StreamForest's 55.6 which requires 32 GPUs and 121K stream-tailored samples.

## Relation to Prior Work

WeaveTime builds directly on the retrieval-based streaming Video-LLM paradigm established by ReKV (KV-cache retrieval) and StreamBridge (proactive streaming adaptation), extending them with two targeted interventions: a training-time Temporal Reconstruction auxiliary task that leverages the LLM's own next-token prediction to enforce order awareness, and an inference-time entropy-gated coarse-to-fine memory controller inspired by ColBERT-style late interaction. Conceptually, it departs from prior streaming approaches that either require large-scale stream-specific instruction-tuning data (VideoLLM-Online, StreamForest) or treat memory as a flat retrieval pool, by explicitly separating the problem into 'teach order' (training) and 'use order' (inference). The plug-and-play design—requiring no architectural modification to the backbone Video-LLM and only 30K offline samples with LoRA—positions it as a lightweight complement rather than a replacement for existing streaming pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The empirical diagnosis of Time-Agnosticism (shuffled frames barely degrade, and sometimes improve, model accuracy while human performance collapses) provides a clear, actionable target for improving streaming Video-LLMs, and the plug-and-play nature of WeaveTime makes the fix broadly applicable across backbones. The data/compute efficiency comparison (30K samples, 8 GPUs vs. 121K stream-specific data, 32 GPUs for StreamForest) is practically significant for groups without large-scale streaming data. Open questions include whether the single fixed entropy threshold generalizes across domains and video lengths, whether the Temporal Reconstruction task scales to much longer streams where order ambiguity compounds, and whether the gains persist when the backbone is scaled beyond 7B parameters.
