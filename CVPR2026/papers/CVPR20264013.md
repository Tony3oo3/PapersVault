## Problem

Existing pixel-space weather editing methods entangle weather effects with scene appearance, making it difficult to preserve underlying material properties and geometry while producing natural illumination. Diffusion-based intrinsic decomposition approaches (e.g., RGB↔X, DiffusionRenderer) achieve impressive results on indoor scenes and small objects but fail to generalize to large-scale outdoor autonomous driving scenarios with diverse weather conditions. Additionally, no large-scale dataset provides paired images and intrinsic map annotations for outdoor driving scenes under varied weather.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a single outdoor RGB image and a text prompt describing the target weather condition (e.g., 'a rainy day'). The inverse renderer outputs five intrinsic maps—albedo, roughness, metallicity (weather-invariant material), a normal map (geometry), and an irradiance map (weather-variant illumination)—while the forward renderer consumes these maps plus the prompt to produce a weather-edited image. Fine-grained weather intensity is controlled via a continuous alpha parameter that interpolates between a weather-neutral CLIP embedding and the target weather direction, enabling smooth transitions (e.g., light to heavy rain) rather than discrete weather swaps.

## Method

IntrinsicWeather repurposes Stable Diffusion 3.5's DiT backbone into two diffusion-based components: a weather-aware inverse renderer that decomposes an image into intrinsic maps, and a weather-conditioned forward renderer that re-synthesizes an image from those maps under a new weather prompt. The key architectural contribution is Intrinsic Map-Aware Attention (IMAA), which extracts DINOv2 patch tokens, applies a learnable per-map embedding through a gating MLP to produce a spatial mask, and injects this as an additive bias into the DiT's joint text-image attention, steering the model to attend to regions most relevant to each target map (e.g., sharp normal variations for geometry, metallic objects for metallicity). For forward rendering, the method computes a weather transition direction in CLIP embedding space and shifts a neutral embedding by α·e to achieve fine-grained, continuous weather control, while feature distillation aligns intermediate features with the original Stable Diffusion to preserve its generative priors. Training uses a heuristic-guided progressive strategy for IMAA and is conducted on two newly constructed datasets: WeatherSynthetic (38k images rendered in Unreal Engine 5 across seven weather types) and WeatherReal (18k real driving images from Waymo/KITTI with pseudo-labeled intrinsic maps for fine-tuning).

## Results

On the WeatherSynthetic test set, IntrinsicWeather achieves 27.99 PSNR on albedo, 25.06 PSNR on normal, and 29.66 PSNR on irradiance, surpassing the best prior method (DiffusionRenderer) by over 10 dB across all intrinsic maps. For weather editing, the method attains the highest CLIP-S (27.66) among all compared approaches and the highest DINO-S (73.63) among rendering-based methods. On the ACDC benchmark, applying IntrinsicWeather before detection/segmentation boosts AP75 from 13.15% to 24.60% (+87.1%) and mIOU from 24.13% to 30.05% (+24.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IntrinsicWeather decisively outperforms all rendering-based baselines (RGB↔X, DiffusionRenderer, IID, GeoWizard, IDArb) on inverse rendering, achieving 10–21 dB higher PSNR on albedo and normal maps; even after fine-tuning RGB↔X and IID on WeatherSynthetic, they remain far below (e.g., 11.35 vs. 27.99 PSNR on albedo). Among pixel-space editing methods, Flux-Kontext achieves a higher DINO-S (85.50 vs. 73.63) but the paper notes it fails to actually remove or synthesize weather effects, while Qwen-Image-Edit edges out on PickScore (20.77 vs. 20.59 for sunny) yet introduces inconsistent textures and geometry. WeatherWeaver and Instruct-Pix2Pix are outperformed on all three editing metrics (CLIP-S, DINO-S, PickScore). For weather restoration, AWRaCLe and Histoformer are compared qualitatively: both remove airborne particles but fail to correct surface material or lighting, whereas IntrinsicWeather restores full scene appearance. The IMAA ablation confirms its contribution: removing it drops albedo PSNR from 27.99 to 26.78 and normal MAE from 4.24° to 6.33°.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based intrinsic decomposition line (RGB↔X, IID, DiffusionRenderer) from indoor/object-level scenes to large-scale outdoor autonomous driving, and simultaneously departs from pixel-space weather editing (WeatherWeaver, Flux-Kontext, InstructPix2Pix) by operating in a physically disentangled intrinsic space. The core architectural backbone is Stable Diffusion 3.5's DiT, augmented with a novel intrinsic map-aware attention (IMAA) mechanism that uses DINOv2 patch tokens and per-map learnable embeddings to guide spatial correspondence in scenes with extreme scale variation. For forward rendering, the paper introduces CLIP-space interpolation of weather prompts as a fine-grained control mechanism, a technique not previously applied to weather synthesis in this form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact on autonomous driving perception is substantial: applying IntrinsicWeather as a preprocessing step nearly doubles AP75 (13.15%→24.60%) and boosts mIOU by 24.5% on the ACDC benchmark, demonstrating that intrinsic-space weather correction is a viable strategy for robust perception. The two introduced datasets (38k synthetic + 18k real images with full intrinsic map annotations) fill a notable gap in weather-diverse driving data with paired physical annotations. Key limitations include single-image operation (no temporal consistency for video) and reliance on a pre-trained image editing model (Flux-Kontext) to generate WeatherReal variants, which may introduce domain bias. Extending the framework to video and reducing dependence on synthetic-to-real bridging remain open questions.
