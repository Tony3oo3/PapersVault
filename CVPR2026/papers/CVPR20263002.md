## Problem

Generative models face a fundamental trade-off rooted in their training objective: diffusion models supervise local PF-ODE dynamics (accurate only for infinitesimal steps, requiring many NFEs), while few-step methods like consistency models supervise fixed endpoint maps (efficient but unable to refine with additional steps). This forces a choice between high fidelity at high compute cost or efficiency at a hard quality ceiling, with no single model that scales monotonically across the full range of sampling budgets.

## Input / Output

The model takes a noisy latent state x_t at time t, a target time r (equivalently a transition interval Δt = t − r), and a conditioning signal (text prompt encoded by Gemma3-1B-it for T2I, or a class label for ImageNet), and outputs the predicted transition target that, combined with x_t via a closed-form recombination, yields the previous state x_r. Because the model is parameterized by both absolute time and the interval, a single network supports arbitrary-step sampling from 1-NFE single leaps to 128-NFE fine-grained refinement. Native-resolution training preserves original aspect ratios and resolutions, enabling generation up to 4096×4096 without architectural changes.

## Method

TiM derives a State Transition Identity (Eq. 8), a product-derivative invariant that must hold for any finite interval Δt on the PF-ODE trajectory, unifying local PF-ODE supervision and finite-horizon consistency under a single constraint. The resulting training target (Eq. 9) combines the standard PF-ODE residual with a time-slope matching term (the derivative of the residual), providing higher-order supervision that enforces trajectory consistency and smooth refinement. The architecture is a SiT-based DiT transformer with decoupled time encoders—separate embeddings for absolute time t and transition interval Δt—modulating AdaLN layers. A key practical contribution is the Differential Derivation Equation (DDE), a forward-pass-only finite-difference approximation of the network's time derivative that is ~2× faster than JVP and natively compatible with FSDP and FlashAttention, enabling from-scratch training of billion-parameter models. A tangent-space loss weighting w(t,r) = (σ_data + tan(t) − tan(r))^(−1/2) stabilizes training by prioritizing short-interval transitions, and the 865M-parameter model is trained from scratch on 33M images without any distillation from a teacher.

## Results

On GenEval, TiM (865M params) achieves 0.67 at 1-NFE, 0.76 at 8-NFE, and 0.83 at 128-NFE, outperforming SD3.5-Large (8B, 0.69 at 128-NFE) and FLUX.1-Dev (12B, 0.65 at 128-NFE) while exhibiting monotonic quality improvement with increasing NFE. On MJHQ-30K, TiM reaches FID 5.28 at 8-NFE, surpassing FLUX.1-Schnell (7.94) and SD3.5-Turbo (11.97). On ImageNet-256, TiM-XL achieves FID 1.68 at 100-NFE (best overall) and 2.47 at 2-NFE (best few-step), outperforming Shortcut-XL (10.60) and MeanFlow-XL (2.93) at 2-NFE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TiM (865M) outperforms all multi-step diffusion baselines at 128-NFE: SD3.5-Large (8B, 0.69), FLUX.1-Dev (12B, 0.65), and SD-XL (2.6B, 0.55) on GenEval Overall, while SANA-1.5 (4.6B, 0.81 at 40-NFE) remains the closest competitor. At 8-NFE, TiM (0.76) surpasses all few-step distilled models including FLUX.1-Schnell (12B, 0.67), SD3.5-Turbo (8B, 0.66), and SANA-Sprint (1.6B, 0.72). Critically, TiM is the only model showing monotonic improvement with NFE (0.67→0.76→0.80→0.83), whereas FLUX.1-Schnell degrades (0.68→0.67→0.63→0.58) and SD3.5-Large plateaus (0.50→0.69→0.70). On ImageNet-256, TiM-XL (675M) achieves FID 2.47 at 2-NFE, beating Shortcut-XL (10.60), MeanFlow-XL (2.93), and IMM-XL (7.77), and reaches 1.68 at 100-NFE, surpassing DiT-XL (2.27) and SiT-XL (2.06). TiM trails only GPT-4o (0.84) and SANA-1.5 (0.81) on GenEval Overall, but matches or exceeds them on positional and attribute sub-metrics at 128-NFE.

## Relation to Prior Work

TiM departs from the fundamental dichotomy in generative training objectives: diffusion models supervise local PF-ODE dynamics (accurate only in the Δt→0 limit), while consistency models and distillation methods supervise fixed endpoint mappings (efficient but unable to refine with more steps). Building on the score-based SDE/PF-ODE framework of Song et al. [71] and the SiT transformer architecture [51], the paper derives a State Transition Identity that unifies both regimes into a single exact identity valid for arbitrary time intervals. It also replaces the JVP-based time-derivative computation used in MeanFlow [26] with a simple finite-difference approximation (DDE), which is the key enabler for FSDP-compatible, billion-parameter training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that an 865M-parameter model trained from scratch outperforms 8B (SD3.5) and 12B (FLUX.1) models across all NFEs, while exhibiting monotonic quality improvement as steps increase (a property absent in all prior few-step methods), suggests the step-quality trade-off is an artifact of the training objective rather than a fundamental constraint. The DDE trick has broad practical implications for any method requiring network time-derivatives during training. Open questions include whether the approach scales to video generation, whether fine-grained detail (text rendering, hands) improves with more parameters, and whether the native-resolution strategy generalizes beyond the current autoencoder's limitations at extreme resolutions.
