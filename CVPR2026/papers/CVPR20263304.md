## Problem

Conventional learned image compression optimizes for pixel-level fidelity at fixed bitrate points, misaligning with the semantic consistency and flexible bitrate control that Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) require for downstream reasoning. This misalignment becomes critical at ultra-low bitrates, where latent representations lose semantic relevance and struggle to disentangle meaningful content from redundant visual details, causing severe degradation in machine understanding tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263304/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a 256×256 RGB image, tokenizes it into a 1D sequence via TiTok-sl256, and applies a variable token masker whose removal ratio is set by the target bitrate. The surviving tokens are entropy-coded into a bitstream via an arithmetic encoder. At decode time, the bitstream yields a reconstructed image through the detokenizer (human path) and, simultaneously, a semantically guided token sequence fed directly into a frozen LLM (machine path), bypassing the conventional decode-then-re-encode loop.

## Method

TFGC builds on the TiTok-sl256 1D tokenizer and introduces two key modules. The Token Flow Propagation (TFP) module exploits the holistic information-propagation property of 1D tokens: it models masked tokens as a conditional Gaussian given unmasked tokens, where a self-attention layer propagates context, an MLP predicts the conditional mean and variance, and a reparameterization trick samples the missing tokens, enabling fine-grained variable-bitrate control within a single model. The Token Semantic Guidance (TSG) module injects CLIP-driven semantic priors and projects reconstructed tokens into the LLM's embedding space via stacked normalization–self-attention–MLP blocks, allowing direct token-based reasoning without image reconstruction. Training follows a Progressive Semantic Alignment (PSA) paradigm: Stage I aligns TSG outputs with the frozen vision encoder's features via MSE loss to establish a semantic anchor, and Stage II refines task-level alignment through joint cross-entropy and MSE objectives for next-token prediction, bridging the gap between perceptual reconstruction and linguistic reasoning.

## Results

TFGC achieves state-of-the-art LVLM understanding across all three bitrate groups (0.06, 0.04, 0.02 bpp) with only 332M parameters. At 0.06 bpp, it scores 49.94 ROUGE-L on MSCOCO captioning (vs. 42.31 for TiTok and 39.00 for DiffEIC) and 43.28 accuracy on OKVQA. Even at the extreme 0.02 bpp regime, TFGC maintains 48.32 ROUGE-L on MSCOCO and 62.37 accuracy on VQAv2, outperforming all baselines by 6–11 points. On human-oriented metrics, TFGC achieves the lowest LPIPS (0.12) and DISTS (0.11) at 0.06 bpp among learned methods, confirming that semantic optimization does not sacrifice perceptual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFGC dominates at 0.04 and 0.02 bpp, winning all six machine-understanding metrics against every baseline. At 0.06 bpp, TFGC leads on MSCOCO captioning (49.94 vs. 42.31 TiTok, 39.00 DiffEIC), Flickr30k (39.04), RefCOCOg (54.35), and OKVQA (43.28), but trails DiffEIC by small margins on RefCOCO (61.49 vs. 62.19) and VQAv2 (66.41 vs. 66.92). Compared to the variable-bitrate FlexTok (950M params), TFGC (332M params) gains 10.8 ROUGE-L on MSCOCO and 7.2 Acc on VQAv2 at 0.06 bpp while using 65% fewer parameters. Against the classical VVC codec, TFGC improves MSCOCO ROUGE-L by 16.5 points (49.94 vs. 33.44) and RefCOCO Acc@0.5 by 35 points (61.49 vs. 26.53) at comparable bitrate, highlighting the semantic advantage of token-based representation over pixel-level coding. Notably, TFGC matches or exceeds the single-rate TiTok_sl256 on captioning while also supporting variable bitrate in one model, eliminating the need for separate checkpoints per operating point.

## Relation to Prior Work

TFGC builds on the 1D token compression line (TiTok, FlexTok) and the emerging compression-for-machine literature (Li et al. 2024, Liu et al. 2024), but departs in two key ways. First, it replaces static token-filling strategies (learnable registers, positional-encoding tokens) with a conditional Gaussian model (TFP) that explicitly captures inter-token dependencies, grounded in a KL-divergence argument showing context-free replacement is suboptimal. Second, it bypasses the conventional decode-then-encode pipeline by introducing a Token Semantic Guidance module that projects compressed tokens directly into the LVLM's embedding space, eliminating the modality gap that plagues prior machine-oriented compression methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The direct token-to-LLM pathway is practically significant for bandwidth-constrained scenarios (satellite links, edge devices) where reconstructing an image only to re-encode it into visual features is wasteful. However, the evaluation is limited to a single small LVLM (InternVL2-1B) at 256×256 resolution, leaving open whether the token-flow property and TSG alignment generalize to larger models, higher resolutions, or different tokenizer architectures. The 'token flow' phenomenon, while intuitively plausible given self-attention's global receptive field, is demonstrated primarily through qualitative perturbation experiments rather than rigorous quantitative characterization, and its robustness across diverse tokenizers remains untested.
