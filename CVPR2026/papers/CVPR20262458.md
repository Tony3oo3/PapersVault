## Problem

Modern cryo-EM direct detectors produce raw data streams of several gigabytes per second, quickly overwhelming local storage and network bandwidth and constraining practical acquisition throughput. Existing mitigations—subframe summing, shortened acquisition with microscope idling, and post-acquisition compression—either sacrifice temporal resolution, waste instrument time, or fail to address real-time bandwidth bottlenecks. No prior work has demonstrated that compressive acquisition during imaging can recover cryo-EM images with sufficient fidelity to preserve downstream 3D structural resolution.

## Input / Output

The method takes m << n compressed measurements obtained by applying either pixel-space masking (binarized random masks followed by non-overlapping K×K kernel-wise summation, yielding low-resolution measurements) or Fourier-space masking (a single binary mask subsampling m Fourier coefficients via uniform, annular-ring, or radial-spoke strategies). Inputs are 128×128 or 256×256 single-particle cryo-EM images with known CTF and pose metadata. Outputs are full-resolution 2D reconstructed images that feed directly into standard downstream pipelines: 3D volume reconstruction via backprojection, conformational heterogeneity analysis via CryoDRGN, and atomic model building via ModelAngelo.

## Method

cryoSENSE formulates image recovery as an inverse problem y = A(x*) + η and solves it with two complementary priors. For sparse priors, it minimizes a data-fidelity term plus an L1 regularization in a predefined basis (DCT, wavelet, or total variation) via proximal gradient descent with soft-thresholding at each iteration. For the generative prior, a DDPM trained on EMPIAR cryo-EM data approximates the low-dimensional image manifold M; reconstruction proceeds by posterior sampling that modifies the reverse-time SDE with a measurement-consistency gradient computed through Tweedie's formula to obtain a denoised estimate x̂₀ at each timestep, then backpropagating the log-likelihood gradient ∇log p(y|x̂₀) through the differentiable measurement operator A. Nesterov-accelerated gradients are integrated over the fixed T-step reverse diffusion to improve convergence. The framework is modular across both sampling domains and both prior types, with empirical results showing sparse priors favor Fourier-space masking at moderate compression while generative priors excel at pixel-space masking under extreme downsampling.

## Results

cryoSENSE faithfully reconstructs 2D cryo-EM particle images at compression factors up to 2.7× (pixel-space) and 2.5× (Fourier-space), defined by an LPIPS threshold of ≈0.15. For 3D volume reconstruction, the ground-truth FSC resolution of 6.65 Å is recovered at C=1.45 (DCT, pixel-space K=8) and C=2 (both priors, Fourier-space), with volume correlations of 0.97–0.99. Downstream biological analyses confirm fidelity: CryoDRGN conformational cluster agreement reaches 80.8% (DCT) and 87.9% (DDPM), and ModelAngelo atomic model building yields backbone RMSDs of 2.07 Å (DCT) and 2.34 Å (DDPM) against the original structure.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison in the paper is internal: sparse priors (DCT, wavelet, TV) versus the generative DDPM prior across pixel-space and Fourier-space masking. Sparse-DCT outperforms Gen-DDPM in Fourier-space masking at all compression factors (e.g., FSC 6.65 Å vs. 6.76 Å at C=2.5; VC 0.98 vs. 0.98), while Gen-DDPM outperforms sparse priors at extreme pixel-space downsampling (K=16, 32), achieving FSC 6.99 Å and 7.91 Å versus 7.35 Å and 14.46 Å for DCT. The only external baseline is DMPlug [77], a diffusion-based super-resolution method that does not enforce measurement consistency; cryoSENSE outperforms DMPlug across all downstream tasks (reported in the supplementary material). For atomic model building on EMPIAR-10648, DCT achieves a marginally higher FSC (3.78 Å vs. 3.82 Å), higher mean confidence (64.71 vs. 62.5), and more matched chains (58 vs. 42) than DDPM at K=2, C=1.33, with backbone RMSDs of 2.07 Å and 2.34 Å respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

cryoSENSE sits at the intersection of two well-established lines of work: classical compressed sensing with handcrafted sparsity priors (DCT, wavelet, total variation) solved via proximal gradient descent, and diffusion-based inverse problem solving (DPS, score-based SDEs) that replaces fixed-basis sparsity with a learned low-dimensional image manifold. The paper departs from prior compressed-sensing applications in MRI, fluorescence microscopy, and 4D-STEM by targeting cryo-EM specifically and by systematically comparing sparse and generative priors under both pixel-space and Fourier-space masking. The generative prior is a DDPM trained on EMPIAR data, and the posterior-sampling formulation directly adapts Chung et al.'s DPS framework with Tweedie-based denoised estimates and Nesterov-accelerated gradients.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first computational demonstration that cryo-EM protein images can be acquired at 2.5× higher throughput via compressed sensing while preserving 3D FSC resolution, conformational heterogeneity (80–88% CryoDRGN cluster agreement), and atomic model quality (2.1–2.3 Å backbone RMSD), directly addressing the storage and bandwidth bottleneck that currently forces microscopes to idle. The empirical finding that sparse priors favor Fourier-space masking at moderate compression while generative priors dominate pixel-space masking at extreme downsampling provides practical guidance for hardware-software co-design. Key open questions include scaling the DDPM to full-micrograph acquisition (rather than single-particle images), integrating programmable coded apertures or back-focal-plane phase plates into real electron microscopes, and whether the learned manifold generalizes across specimen types without retraining.
