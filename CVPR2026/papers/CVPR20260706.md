## Problem

Existing autonomous driving datasets are dominated by urban, low-speed scenarios with effective perception ranges of only 70–100 m, which is fundamentally insufficient for heavy trucks at highway speeds where braking distances exceed 150–200 m. State-of-the-art perception and planning models trained on these urban benchmarks exhibit 31–99% performance drops beyond 150 m, exposing a systematic long-range gap that current architectures and training signals cannot close.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260706/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 475k cross-modal synchronized samples from 37 sensors, including 7 long-range FMCW LiDARs (400 m range with per-point radial velocity), 3 short-range LiDARs, 10 4D FMCW radars, and 11–15 8MP cameras (3848×2168) with wide-baseline stereo. Annotations comprise 165k manually labeled frames with 3D cuboids (9 semantic categories, 85 fine-grained classes) up to 400 m and 2D boxes up to 1000 m, plus 310k unlabeled frames for self-supervised research. Evaluation outputs span distance-binned mAP for detection, AMOTA for tracking, distance-binned MAE for depth, Chamfer distance for forecasting, F1/IoU for segmentation, PSNR/SSIM for reconstruction, and L2 trajectory error for E2E planning.

## Method

TruckDrive is a dataset and benchmark contribution built around a purpose-designed sensor suite mounted on a semi-truck, with FMCW LiDAR providing instantaneous radial velocity via Doppler phase shift and a PPK pipeline fusing 2 GNSS + 4 IMUs for centimeter-level ego poses. The annotation pipeline is three-stage: (1) human annotators label 3D cuboids and 2D boxes across 2000+ curated scenes; (2) 3D primitives are projected into all camera views and matched to 2D detector outputs via Hungarian assignment with IoU cost, augmenting coverage; (3) matched 3D tracks are refined through a kinematically constrained optimization enforcing a unicycle motion model with Huber-robust position, yaw, and size losses plus smoothness regularization, while unmatched 2D candidates are lifted to 3D by multi-view IoU fitting against a LiDAR-derived ground plane. The benchmark evaluates 25+ SOTA methods across 8 task categories, all retrained on TruckDrive with a consistent 140k/25k split, using unified distance-binned metrics (SR/MR/LR/UR) to expose range-dependent degradation that standard urban metrics mask.

## Results

On the TruckDrive benchmark, 3D object detection performance degrades sharply with range: BEVFusion (LiDAR+Camera) achieves 32.32% mAP at short range (0–50 m) but drops to 22.69% at long range (150–250 m), while the camera-only Far3D collapses from 35.54% to 0.33% mAP (a 99% drop). For 2D detection, DINO leads with 37.80% overall mAP but falls from 63.90% (SR) to 15.30% (UR, 250+ m). 3D multi-object tracking is severely degraded, with the best method (CenterPoint) achieving only 13.0% AMOTA and 21.5% recall, and end-to-end planning (UniAD) yields 2.00 m average L2 error over 6 prediction steps on a 250 m ROI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks 26 state-of-the-art methods across 7 tasks, all trained on TruckDrive, to expose the long-range gap. In 3D detection, camera-only Far3D [31] is catastrophically outperformed by LiDAR-based TransFusion-L [1] and fusion BEVFusion [40] at long range (0.33% vs. ~22% mAP at LR), confirming that pixel-level resolution alone cannot sustain 3D perception beyond 150 m. In 2D detection, DINO [66] leads all baselines (37.80% mAP) but still drops to 15.30% at ultra-long range, while DETR [8] and YOLO11x [32] are 2–3× weaker overall. For tracking, all three methods (MUTR3D [67], Immortal Tracker [59], CenterPoint [65]) achieve AMOTA below 13%, indicating that long-horizon association at highway speeds remains unsolved. In depth estimation, UniDepthv2 [51] (single-view) achieves the best short-range MAE (2.66 m) but all methods exceed 100 m MAE at ultra-long range, and stereo methods (BridgeDepth [25], NMRF [24]) show 8× MAE inflation at LR due to reduced pixel disparity. For E2E planning, UniAD [29] with a 250 m ROI produces 2.00 m average L2 error, with the 3-step prediction already at 1.71 m, demonstrating that urban-centric architectures cannot encode sufficient spatial detail at highway scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in the autonomous driving dataset landscape by addressing a range gap that prior benchmarks (KITTI, nuScenes, Waymo, Argoverse) leave unaddressed: all are urban-centric with effective perception limited to 70–100 m. While MAN TruckScenes and aiMotive explored highway trucking, they remain small-scale (30k and 26.5k samples) and shorter-range (226 m and 200 m). TruckDrive departs from the urban-dataset lineage by purpose-building a 37-sensor suite (7 FMCW LiDARs, 3 short-range LiDARs, 10 4D radars, 11–15 8MP cameras) on a semi-truck to push 3D annotations to 400 m and 2D to 1 km, and by using FMCW radial velocity as a first-class signal for dynamic-object filtering and ground-truth construction. The empirical evaluation repurposes existing SOTA architectures (UniAD, BEVFusion, TransFusion, Far3D, DINO, etc.) as probes rather than building on any single method, positioning the contribution as a diagnostic benchmark rather than a new model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset exposes a systematic long-range failure: SOTA 3D perception methods drop 31–99% beyond 150 m even when trained on TruckDrive, pointing to architectural (quadratic BEV scaling, downsampling of 8MP inputs) rather than purely data-driven limitations. This reframes the open problem from 'collect more data' to 'design range-aware representations and fusion strategies that do not degrade with distance,' opening concrete research directions in sparse/range-aware encoders, efficient long-horizon planning, and multi-sensor fusion at highway scale. A key limitation is that all evaluated models still perform poorly, so the benchmark currently serves more as a stress test than a solvable challenge; the field needs new inductive biases before these numbers become competitive. Practically, the dataset is likely to become the standard reference for heavy-vehicle and highway autonomy research given its scale (475 k samples, 165 k annotated) and sensor richness.
