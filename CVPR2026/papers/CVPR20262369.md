## Problem

Strict equivariance is rarely satisfied in real-world data, yet existing soft equivariance methods (augmentation, regularization, residual mixing) provide no theoretical guarantees on the resulting equivariance error and rely on specialized architectures that cannot be adapted from off-the-shelf pre-trained models. The paper addresses the need for an architecture-agnostic framework that allows principled, tunable control over the equivariance-expressiveness trade-off with provable error bounds.

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard inputs without altering their format: RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR, 224×224 for ImageNet, 320×320 for PASCAL VOC) and 2D position sequences (10 past timesteps of (x,y) coordinates for trajectory prediction). Outputs remain unchanged—class probabilities, per-pixel segmentation logits, or 10 future 2D positions. The key structural detail is that the soft equivariance constraint is imposed entirely on internal layer weights via a projection operator, introducing no additional learnable parameters or architectural modifications to the input/output pipeline.

## Method

The core mechanism projects layer weights into a subspace defined by the singular vectors of the Lie algebra representation (continuous groups) or a forward-difference operator (discrete groups), keeping only components with singular values below a tunable cutoff b. For invariant layers, the projection uses left singular vectors of dρ_X(A); for equivariant layers, it uses right singular vectors of the Kronecker-product matrix L = (dρ_X(A)ᵀ ⊗ I − I ⊗ dρ_Y(A)). The cutoff b directly controls softness: smaller b enforces stronger equivariance, and the paper proves the equivariance error is bounded by η_b = b√(n_G)·r_G + ε_G. For computational efficiency with normal Lie algebra representations (e.g., 2D/3D rotations), an alternative Schur-decomposition-based projection reduces complexity from O((d·d′)³) to O(max(d,d′)³). The method is applied to convolution, patch embedding, positional encoding, and fully connected layers in pre-trained ViT, DINOv2, ResNet, and SegFormer backbones during fine-tuning, requiring no architectural changes.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, the proposed soft equivariance layer improves top-1 accuracy while simultaneously reducing invariance error across all three backbones: ViT-B/16 reaches 82.28% Acc (+0.61) with iErr 0.15 (vs. 0.36 for Base), DINOv2-Base reaches 85.31% Acc (+1.04) with iErr 0.05 (vs. 0.13), and ResNet-50 reaches 77.96% Acc (+0.06) with iErr 0.11 (vs. 0.24). On CIFAR-100, Ours achieves 86.60% Acc with ViT (vs. 86.36% Base) and 91.03% with DINOv2 (vs. 91.01%), while reducing iErr. For semantic segmentation on PASCAL VOC, Ours improves mIoU by +1.38 (ViT) and +0.99 (SegFormer) while lowering equivariance error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Canonicalizer baseline (Mondal et al., NeurIPS 2023), Ours dominates on every metric: on ImageNet, Canon. drops ViT accuracy by 5.16% and ResNet-50 by 5.84% while Ours improves both; on PASCAL VOC segmentation, Canon. reduces mIoU by 8.03 (ViT) and 7.84 (SegFormer) while Ours increases them. Against RPP (Finzi et al., NeurIPS 2021), the tunability experiment on MNIST (Fig. 2) shows Ours achieves comparable combined accuracy (cAcc) with consistently lower invariance error (iErr) across all softness levels for both rotation and roto-reflection groups. Against EqAuto (Deng et al., ICCV 2021) on human trajectory prediction, Ours achieves the best cADE and cFDE on 4 of 5 scenes (e.g., ETH: cADE 4.58 vs. 5.40; ZARA1: cADE 3.40 vs. 3.66) while maintaining non-zero equivariance error, demonstrating that full equivariance is not necessary for optimal performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Zhang's [76] anti-aliasing/blurring filter idea for shift invariance in CNNs to arbitrary compact Lie groups and discrete groups, and generalizes Finzi et al.'s [17] Kronecker-product construction of exactly equivariant MLPs by replacing the hard equivariance constraint with a tunable projection onto a bandlimited subspace. It departs from residual-mixing approaches (RPP [16], van der Ouderaa [61,62]) that require specialized equivariant architectures, and from canonicalizer-based adaptation [41] that predicts group elements at inference time, by offering an architecture-agnostic weight projection that can be applied post-hoc to any pre-trained backbone. The Schur-decomposition-based efficient construction is a practical departure from the SVD-based design, reducing precomputation from cubic in the full parameter dimension to cubic in the individual input/output dimensions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to impose a theoretically bounded, tunable equivariance constraint on arbitrary pre-trained models (ViT, DINOv2, ResNet, SegFormer) without architectural modification or additional learnable parameters makes this a practical tool for practitioners who want equivariance guarantees without retraining from scratch. The finding that soft equivariance can simultaneously improve accuracy and reduce equivariance error on ImageNet—rather than enforcing the usual expressiveness-accuracy trade-off—suggests that mild structural constraints can act as a regularizer on large pre-trained models. Open questions include whether the approach extends cleanly to 3D rotation groups, non-compact groups, or higher-order equivariance (e.g., scale + rotation jointly), and whether the benefits persist when the pre-trained model is already highly equivariant (as DINOv2 appears to be).
