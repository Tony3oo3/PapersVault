## Problem

In CAV-MAE-style audio-visual pretraining, masked reconstruction and contrastive alignment are jointly optimized on the same randomly masked view, causing two issues: (1) reconstruction-oriented masking exposes patches with limited cross-modal relevance (silent spectrogram regions, uninformative backgrounds), injecting semantic noise into the contrastive branch; and (2) the competing gradient signals from generative and discriminative objectives interfere with each other on shared token representations, degrading cross-modal alignment quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262977/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single video frame (RGB) and its temporally aligned log-Mel spectrogram segment, both patchified into token sequences with learnable global and register tokens prepended. Each training sample is processed through two separate forward passes with different masking ratios (75% for reconstruction, 50% for contrastive), so the same input is effectively consumed twice per step. At inference, the model outputs modality-specific global embeddings for cosine-similarity-based retrieval and, via an attention-probing head over the temporal sequence of global tokens, produces multi-label or single-label class predictions.

## Method

TG-DP decouples reconstruction and contrastive learning into two objective-specific forward passes that share encoder weights but operate on independently masked views, eliminating gradient interference between the generative and discriminative objectives. A lightweight teacher network processes full unmasked inputs and provides two forms of guidance to the contrastive branch: (i) an MSE distillation loss anchoring the student's global embeddings to the teacher's full-view representations (teacher updated via EMA), and (ii) a deterministic token-priority masking strategy where attention weights from the teacher's joint encoder (measuring patch-to-global-token interaction) select the top-k most informative visible tokens for the student, replacing random masking with a semantically structured view. The final training objective combines reconstruction loss, distillation loss, and InfoNCE contrastive loss with fixed hyperparameters, and all teacher components are discarded at inference, incurring no additional deployment cost.

## Results

On zero-shot audio-visual retrieval, TG-DP achieves R@1 of 37.4% (AudioSet V→A) and 37.1% (AudioSet A→V), and 31.3% (VGGSound V→A) and 30.3% (VGGSound A→V), setting new state-of-the-art among training-free methods across all four retrieval directions. For audio-visual classification with frozen encoders, it reaches 32.0 mAP on AS20K and 52.7% Top-1 accuracy on VGGSound. Unimodal transfer also improves, with audio-only mAP rising from 29.3 to 31.2 and vision-only mAP from 14.3 to 17.8 over CAV-MAE Sync.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest baseline CAV-MAE Sync [5], TG-DP improves R@1 by +2.2 (AudioSet V→A), +3.4 (VGGSound V→A), +9.2 (AudioSet A→V), and +7.1 (VGGSound A→V), with the largest gains in the more challenging Audio→Visual direction. Over ImageBind [15] and DenseAV [19], the improvements are even more substantial (e.g., +15.3 R@1 on AudioSet V→A over ImageBind), confirming that the dual-path decoupling and teacher-guided masking yield meaningfully better cross-modal alignment than prior multi-modal binding or dense grounding approaches. On classification (Table 2), TG-DP achieves 32.0 mAP on AS20K, surpassing CAV-MAE Sync (30.5), MaViL (30.0), and CAV-MAE (27.3), while matching CAV-MAE Sync's 52.7% Top-1 on VGGSound. The fine-tuned VAB-Encodec [33] reference still leads on retrieval (e.g., 39.5 vs. 37.4 R@1 on AudioSet V→A), but it requires task-specific adaptation on the target dataset and is not directly comparable to the training-free protocol. Unimodal transfer (Table 3) further shows TG-DP outperforming CAV-MAE Sync on both audio-only (31.2 vs. 29.3 mAP) and vision-only (17.8 vs. 14.3 mAP) AS20K classification, indicating that the decoupled training strengthens each modality's representation independently.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CAV-MAE Sync (CVPR 2025), which itself extends CAV-MAE (ICLR 2023) with fine-grained temporal alignment and separate global/register tokens. The key departure is architectural: rather than jointly optimizing masked reconstruction and contrastive alignment in a single forward pass with a shared masking regime, TG-DP splits them into two objective-specific passes with asymmetric masking ratios (75% for reconstruction, 50% for contrastive). The teacher-student component draws on the DINO/BYOL/SimSiam lineage of EMA-based self-distillation, but repurposes it not for general representation smoothing but specifically to generate attention-based token-priority cues that structure the contrastive branch's masking, a use not previously explored in audio-visual MAE frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a concrete and previously underappreciated failure mode in CAV-MAE-style pretraining: the contrastive branch inherits a masking regime designed for reconstruction, which can expose semantically irrelevant patches (silent spectrogram regions, static backgrounds) and inject noise into cross-modal alignment. The dual-path solution is simple and modular, and the consistent gains across retrieval and classification (including unimodal transfer) suggest the decoupling principle may generalize to other multimodal MAE settings. Limitations include a ~43% increase in per-epoch training cost, evaluation restricted to two datasets and one backbone family, and the fact that the teacher-guided masking's benefit over random masking is modest and dataset-dependent. Open questions include whether the asymmetric masking strategy transfers to text-audio or text-video pretraining, and whether more adaptive (per-sample) masking ratios could close the remaining gap to the 0.00-masking contrastive setting.
