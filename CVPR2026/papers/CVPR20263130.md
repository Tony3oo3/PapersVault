## Problem

Existing backdoor attacks on text-to-image diffusion models rely on dirty-label poisoning, where misaligned image-caption pairs are injected into training data. This semantic mismatch makes poisoned samples easily detectable by automated sanitization tools or human inspection, limiting practical relevance. The paper proposes the first clean-label backdoor attack for text-to-image generative models, where poisoned pairs preserve full image-text semantic consistency while still implanting a hidden malicious behavior.

## Input / Output

At inference, the compromised Stable Diffusion v1.5 model takes a text prompt as input and generates a 512×512 RGB image; the backdoor activates only when the prompt simultaneously contains a specific synonym (e.g., 'female' replacing 'woman') and a present-participle-as-adverbial sentence structure. During training, the model is fine-tuned on 512×512 image-caption pairs drawn from LAION-5B (clean) and MSCOCO (poisoned), with the poisoned subset containing 50 pairs whose images carry latent-space perturbations and whose captions carry the composite textual trigger.

## Method

The attack employs a dual-modality manipulation strategy. On the text side, the most salient noun in each caption is identified via CLIP cross-modal similarity, replaced with a semantically equivalent synonym selected by an LLM, and the resulting sentence is restructured into a present-participle-as-adverbial form, so the trigger requires both the keyword and the syntactic pattern to co-occur. On the image side, a constrained perturbation δ (‖δ‖∞ ≤ ε) is optimized in the VAE latent space via projected gradient descent to shift the source image's latent representation toward a target concept while keeping pixel-space changes imperceptible. Two types of cover samples—clean images paired with captions containing only the synonym or only the syntactic structure—are mixed in at a 1:3:3 ratio with poison samples to teach the model that the target output fires exclusively on the full composite trigger, suppressing false activations.

## Results

Across three attack scenarios on Stable Diffusion v1.5, the proposed clean-label backdoor attack achieves an average human-evaluated Attack Success Rate (ASR-H) of 97.2% and an NSFW-detector-based ASR-N of 90.4%, with the highest ASR-H of 99.4% in the woman→nude woman task. The composite trigger yields a low average False Trigger Rate (FTR) of 5.1%, and the Unsafe Content Rate (UCR) is 0% in all scenarios, meaning poisoned images fully evade commercial NSFW filters. Semantic Consistency between poisoned and original captions averages 65.0%, and the average perplexity shift (ΔPPL) is only −8 relative to the clean baseline of 153, confirming textual naturalness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its clean-label attack against two dirty-label baselines: Wu et al. [30] (multimodal data poisoning) and Huang et al. [9] (few-shot personalization-based backdoor). On raw attack effectiveness, the dirty-label methods edge out the proposed method: Huang et al. achieves 99.9% ASR-H vs. 97.2% for the proposed method, and Wu et al. reaches 97.8% ASR-H. However, the critical differentiator is stealth: both dirty-label baselines produce poisoned samples that are flagged 100% of the time by NSFW content filters (UCR = 100%), whereas the proposed clean-label method achieves UCR = 0%, meaning its poisoned images are indistinguishable from clean data to automated safety detectors. On trigger specificity, the proposed method (FTR 5.1%) substantially outperforms Wu et al. (FTR 19.5%) but is slightly worse than Huang et al. (FTR 2.4%). Overall, the paper trades a modest 2–3% drop in ASR for a complete elimination of detectability, positioning the clean-label approach as the more practically viable attack in real-world deployment where data sanitization and content moderation are standard.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the backdoor attack literature on text-to-image diffusion models from the well-studied dirty-label regime (BadT2I, BAGM, Huang et al., Zhai et al.) to the clean-label setting, which had not been explored in the multimodal T2I context. It repurposes two ideas from separate domains—latent-space perturbation optimization from adversarial robustness and composite semantic triggers from NLP clean-label backdoors—into a unified dual-modality manipulation strategy. The cover-sample mechanism, borrowed conceptually from classification clean-label attacks, is adapted here to enforce a strict two-condition trigger (synonym + syntactic structure) that is specific to the image-text pairing problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a clean-label backdoor can achieve >97% attack success while scoring 0% on commercial NSFW detectors and evading human inspection raises urgent questions about the security of fine-tuning pipelines for T2I models, especially those ingesting public or semi-trusted data. However, the evaluation is limited to Stable Diffusion v1.5 and a single (weak) defense—Gaussian noise—leaving open whether the attack transfers to newer architectures (SDXL, DALL-E 3, Flux) and whether more principled defenses (e.g., latent-space anomaly detection, trigger-aware sanitization) can neutralize it. The paper also does not explore whether the composite trigger's strictness degrades under distribution shift in real user prompts.
