## Problem

While feed-forward and streaming 3D reconstruction methods can estimate per-frame point maps from video, they are limited to reconstructing instantaneous geometry at observed timestamps and cannot forecast how a dynamic scene will evolve in the future. The task of 3D point map forecasting from streaming 2D video frames—predicting scene geometry at unobserved future time instants—remains entirely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261843/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous stream of monocular RGB frames (H×W×3) one at a time, updating a fixed-size latent state (N=4096 tokens, C=1024 channels) with each new observation. At inference, the user queries any previously seen frame together with an arbitrary time instant t (which may lie beyond the last observed frame), and the model returns a dense 3D point map (H×W×3) in a shared coordinate system plus 9 camera parameters (intrinsics and extrinsics). Scene flow between any two time steps is obtained for free by differencing the corresponding point maps, requiring no additional head or supervision.

## Method

Point4Cast maintains a persistently evolving latent spacetime representation that is updated frame-by-frame via an Update Transformer performing interleaved self- and cross-attention between the current image features and the previous latent state. For readout, the latent state is first modulated by a FiLM-style time-conditioning layer (a learned time embedding produces per-channel scale and shift parameters applied after batch normalization), then fused with the query frame's image tokens and a learnable pose token through a Readout Transformer to yield temporally conditioned point-map and camera-parameter predictions. Training follows an online streaming paradigm: after each frame update, the model is queried at all prior frames and all time instants in the sequence, supervised with L1 loss on point maps and camera parameters, encouraging a temporally coherent representation that generalizes to unseen future times. The architecture is initialized from VGGT pretrained weights (ViT encoder, DPT attention blocks, prediction heads) and fine-tuned on a curated mixture of synthetic (Kubric, PointOdyssey, Blender-rendered) and real (Stereo4D) dynamic-scene datasets.

## Results

On PointOdyssey reconstruction, Point4Cast (VGGT backbone) achieves the best Accuracy of 0.428 and Completion of 0.472, outperforming the strongest offline baseline VGGT (0.464/0.491) and the best streaming baseline CUT3R (0.530/0.557). For the novel 3D point map forecasting task on PointOdyssey, Point4Cast (VGGT) attains next-frame Accuracy of 0.481 and 10-step Accuracy of 0.533, substantially outperforming all frame-generation and scene-flow-continuation variants of baselines. On the zero-shot TAPVid-3D benchmark, Point4Cast (VGGT) achieves reconstruction Accuracy of 0.711 and Completion of 0.476, again leading all baselines. For scene flow estimation, Point4Cast achieves EPE of 1.355 and Accuracy of 0.848 without any flow-specific training, beating the best baseline MonST3R (EPE 2.058, Acc 0.741).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Point4Cast outperforms all four baselines across every metric on both PointOdyssey and TAPVid-3D. Against the offline feed-forward baselines, Point4Cast (VGGT backbone) improves Accuracy from 0.464 (VGGT) and 0.481 (MonST3R) to 0.428 on PointOdyssey, and from 0.757/0.775 to 0.711 on the zero-shot TAPVid-3D. Against the streaming baselines, the gains are even more pronounced: CUT3R achieves Accuracy 0.530 and StreamVGGT 0.525 on PointOdyssey, while Point4Cast reaches 0.428 (VGGT) or 0.410 (CUT3R backbone), a relative improvement of roughly 20%. For the novel forecasting task, Point4Cast's inherent time-conditioned readout avoids the error compounding seen in frame-generation and scene-flow-continuation pipelines; for example, on 10-step forecasting on PointOdyssey, Point4Cast (VGGT) achieves Accuracy 0.533 versus 0.603 (StreamVGGT frame-gen) and 0.785 (VGGT scene-flow cont.). On scene flow, Point4Cast achieves EPE 1.355 and Accuracy 0.848 without any flow supervision, beating MonST3R (EPE 2.058, Acc 0.741) by a wide margin, confirming that the latent spacetime representation implicitly captures 3D motion.

## Relation to Prior Work

Point4Cast sits at the intersection of streaming feed-forward 3D reconstruction (CUT3R, StreamingVGGT, Spann3R) and dynamic scene modeling (MonST3R, VGGT), but departs from both by introducing a persistently evolving latent spacetime representation that is explicitly conditioned on arbitrary query times via FiLM-style modulation. While prior streaming methods (CUT3R, StreamingVGGT) maintain a persistent state, they only decode geometry at the current observation timestamp; Point4Cast extends this state to support readout at past, present, and future times. The time-conditioned decoding mechanism is conceptually related to temporal conditioning in dynamic NeRFs and 4D Gaussian Splatting, but is applied here in a feed-forward, per-frame-agnostic setting rather than a per-scene optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying reconstruction and forecasting in a single streaming architecture, Point4Cast eliminates the need for separate video-generation or scene-flow-propagation pipelines for future 3D prediction, and yields dense scene flow as a zero-cost byproduct. The zero-shot transfer to TAPVid-3D (not seen in training) suggests the spacetime representation captures generalizable dynamics rather than dataset-specific patterns. Open questions include how the approach scales to very long sequences (the latent state is fixed-size), whether the current deterministic forecasting can be extended to model uncertainty over future trajectories (acknowledged by the authors), and whether the FiLM time conditioning generalizes to non-uniform or irregular time sampling.
