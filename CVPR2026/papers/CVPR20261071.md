## Problem

GNN-based spatial transcriptomics methods rely on pre-defined sparse graphs (spatial adjacency or feature similarity) that structurally exclude the vast majority of spot pairs, making it impossible to capture tissue-wide interactions. Additionally, standard softmax attention constrains weights to [0,1], modeling only positive similarity while ignoring inhibitory (negative) biological relationships, and fixed patch extraction truncates or ignores morphological context in off-grid regions between spots.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261071/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 256×256 pixel patches extracted at ×20 magnification from H&E-stained WSIs, centered on ST spot coordinates, plus additional pseudo-spot patches sampled from off-grid intermediate locations. Features are extracted by a frozen UNI2-h foundation model. The output is a per-spot gene expression vector over the top 250 most expressed genes (SPCS-smoothed), predicted by an MLP head applied to the final spot representations.

## Method

FEAST replaces sparse GNN graphs with self-attention over all spots, augmented with a distance-scaled positional bias (ALiBi-style) where each head uses a distinct non-learned scalar m_h to specialize in local vs. global fields of view. The key novelty is negative-aware attention: raw scores S and their sign-inversion −S are each combined with the positional bias, passed through softmax (with a sharpening temperature τ_neg < 1 for the negative branch), and combined as A_final = A_pos − β·A_neg, yielding attention weights that can be negative to represent inhibitory interactions. To handle the O(N²) cost introduced by off-grid pseudo-spots, a two-stage hierarchical block first applies local k-NN attention (k=32) over all spots to absorb spatial context, then applies global self-attention only over original spots. The framework is trained end-to-end with MSE loss using frozen UNI2-h features.

## Results

FEAST achieves state-of-the-art performance on 7 out of 9 evaluation metrics across three spatial transcriptomics datasets (ST-Net, Her2ST, SCC). On ST-Net, it attains the best PCC of 0.7155 and MSE of 0.1177, outperforming the previous best (MERGE) by 3.6 PCC points. On Her2ST, FEAST records PCC 0.5524 and MSE 0.5761, improving over MERGE by 4.9 PCC points. On SCC, FEAST achieves the best PCC (0.5811) but HisToGene retains the best MSE (0.3095) and MAE (0.4367).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEAST outperforms all six baselines on both breast cancer datasets (ST-Net and Her2ST) across all three metrics, with the largest margin over MERGE (the strongest prior method): +3.6 PCC on ST-Net (0.7155 vs 0.6795) and +4.9 PCC on Her2ST (0.5524 vs 0.5037). On the SCC skin cancer dataset, FEAST wins on PCC (0.5811 vs MERGE's 0.5512) but HisToGene achieves lower MSE (0.3095 vs 0.4501) and MAE (0.4367 vs 0.5239), suggesting FEAST captures stronger linear correlation at the cost of higher absolute error on this dataset. Compared to TRIPLEX, FEAST improves PCC by 48.4 points on ST-Net (0.7155 vs 0.2320) and 15.9 points on Her2ST (0.5524 vs 0.3927), indicating that the fully connected attention with negative-aware modeling substantially outperforms the fixed multi-resolution fusion approach. Against GNN-based methods (Hist2ST, THItoGene, MERGE), FEAST consistently achieves the best PCC, supporting the paper's claim that pre-defined sparse graphs limit interaction modeling. The gap over BLEEP and HisToGene is particularly large on PCC (e.g., 0.7155 vs 0.0784 on ST-Net), highlighting the importance of modeling inter-spot spatial relationships rather than processing patches independently.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEAST departs from the dominant GNN-based paradigm for spatial transcriptomics gene expression prediction (Hist2ST, MERGE, THItoGene), which relies on pre-defined sparse graphs encoding spatial adjacency or feature similarity, and instead models tissue as a fully connected graph via self-attention. It borrows the ALiBi-style distance-based positional bias from Press et al. [20] and TITAN [7] to inject spatial awareness into attention heads, and uses the frozen UNI2-h pathology foundation model as a feature extractor. The two novel components—negative-aware attention (modeling inhibitory interactions) and off-grid pseudo-spot sampling (recovering truncated morphological context)—are introduced to address specific biological and practical limitations that neither sparse GNNs nor prior ViT-based ST methods (HisToGene, TRIPLEX) adequately handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from hand-crafted sparse graphs to learned fully connected attention offers a more principled way to capture long-range and non-local tissue interactions without committing to a specific neighborhood structure, and the negative-aware attention provides an interpretable mechanism for distinguishing excitatory from inhibitory cell-cell relationships—a distinction with direct biological relevance (e.g., immune checkpoint pathways). However, evaluation is limited to three small datasets (≤68 samples each) with SPCS-smoothed targets, and the fully connected attention's O(N²) cost, while mitigated by the hierarchical design, raises open questions about scalability to high-density platforms like Visium HD with thousands of spots. Biological validation of the negative attention patterns beyond qualitative inspection remains an important next step.
