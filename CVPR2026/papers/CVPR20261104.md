## Problem

Anchor-based multi-view clustering methods rely on anchor quality, yet existing approaches neglect the interactive relationships among cross-view anchors, failing to directly exploit consistent and complementary information at the anchor level. Tensor-based MVC methods attempt to capture cross-view structure but regularize sample-dependent anchor graphs (scaling with n), incurring prohibitive computational and memory costs on large-scale datasets. There is a need for a method that directly couples anchors across views while remaining independent of the number of samples.

## Input / Output

The method takes v multi-view feature matrices X_i ∈ R^{d_i × n} (with potentially different dimensions d_i per view), the number of clusters k, and the number of anchors m (typically chosen from {k, 3k, 5k}). Unlike methods requiring pre-selected anchors, SMVS-TAG initializes all anchor matrices as zero and learns them end-to-end. The output is a set of cluster assignments obtained by applying k-means to the left singular vectors of the learned consensus anchor graph Z ∈ R^{m×n}.

## Method

SMVS-TAG learns view-specific anchors A_i ∈ R^{k×m} in a shared k-dimensional latent space via the factorization X_i ≈ W_i A_i Z with orthogonality constraints on W_i and A_i. The key innovation is constructing a third-order anchor tensor A ∈ R^{k×v×m} from the view-specific anchors and imposing a tensor Schatten p-norm constraint (0 < p ≤ 1) on it. Applying FFT along the anchor dimension decomposes the tensor into frequency-domain frontal slices: low-frequency slices aggregate cross-view consistent information while high-frequency slices retain view-specific details and noise, and a low-rank constraint is enforced on each slice. Because the anchor tensor has size k×v×m (independent of n), the tensor operations (t-SVD, FFT) scale only with the number of anchors and views, reducing both time and space complexity to be linear in n. Optimization proceeds via augmented Lagrange multipliers with alternating updates of W_i (SVD), A_i (SVD), Z (n independent QP subproblems), and the auxiliary tensor H (closed-form via Theorem 1 in the Fourier domain).

## Results

SMVS-TAG achieves the best ACC across all seven datasets, with headline values of 97.49% (Dermatology), 61.00% (Scene15), 90.60% (COIL100), 92.52% (Hdigit), 10.67% (VGGFace), 11.45% (AwA), and 36.62% (YoutubeFace). The method surpasses the second-best competitor by 4.36%–39.19% in ACC depending on the dataset, with the largest margin on YoutubeFace (39.19% over LMTC's 26.31%). On large-scale datasets (VGGFace, AwA, YoutubeFace), all four tensor-based baselines (TBGL, Orth-NTF, TC-MVSC, MVSC-HFD) fail with out-of-memory or out-of-time errors, while SMVS-TAG completes within 905–1531 seconds.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SMVS-TAG outperforms all ten baselines on every dataset and every metric (ACC, NMI, Purity). Against the strongest non-tensor baseline AEVC (CVPR'24), the gap is 4.07% ACC on Dermatology (97.49 vs 93.42) and 14.82% on COIL100 (90.60 vs 75.78). Against the most recent tensor-based method LMTC (CVPR'25), SMVS-TAG leads by 3.59% on COIL100 (90.60 vs 87.01) and 10.31% on YoutubeFace (36.62 vs 26.31). All four tensor-based baselines that operate on sample-dependent anchor-graph tensors (TBGL, Orth-NTF, TC-MVSC, MVSC-HFD) fail with out-of-memory or out-of-time errors on Hdigit, VGGFace, AwA, and YoutubeFace, whereas SMVS-TAG completes all runs in under 1531 seconds. Compared to the unified-anchor method MVSC-HFD and the view-specific-anchor method SMVAGC, SMVS-TAG consistently wins, validating that direct tensor-level coupling of view-specific anchors is more effective than graph-level regularization. On running time, SMVS-TAG is competitive with the fastest methods (e.g., 3.60 s on Dermatology vs. LMVSC's 0.25 s) while being 12–65× faster than tensor-based methods on mid-scale datasets like COIL100 (51.39 s vs. TBGL's 6253 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: anchor-based multi-view subspace clustering (which reduces complexity from O(n²) to O(nm) via representative anchors) and tensor-based multi-view clustering (which exploits cross-view structure via tensor Schatten p-norm constraints). Prior tensor-based MVC methods (TBGL, Orth-NTF, TC-MVSC) apply the tensor regularization to the anchor *graph* tensor of size m×v×n, which scales linearly with the number of samples. SMVS-TAG departs from this by constructing a third-order tensor from the view-specific *anchor representations* (k×v×m) and imposing the Schatten p-norm there, thereby decoupling the tensor operation from sample count. It also differs from unified-anchor methods (e.g., MVSC-HFD) by learning view-specific anchors while still enforcing cross-view coherence through the tensor constraint, rather than sharing a single anchor set across views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that tensor regularization on the small anchor tensor (independent of n) makes tensor-based MVC feasible on datasets with 100K+ samples where competing tensor methods simply run out of memory or time, while simultaneously improving clustering accuracy by directly shaping anchor quality rather than post-hoc graph structure. An open question is whether the low-rank tensor structure assumption on anchors holds universally or is dataset-dependent, as the parameter p sensitivity analysis is limited to two datasets. Extending the framework to incomplete multi-view settings (where some views are missing for certain samples) or to deep representation learning pipelines would be natural next steps.
