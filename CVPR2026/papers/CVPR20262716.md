## Problem

On-policy RLVR for GUI automation suffers from a zero-advantage trap: when task difficulty exceeds model capacity, all sampled rollouts fail, producing uniform zero rewards that eliminate learning signals (73.2% of tasks in early training). The natural remedy of incorporating off-policy expert demonstrations backfires in GUI tasks because the reasoning from much larger models lies far outside the policy's distributional support, causing persistent entropy spikes and training instability rather than effective learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262716/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives a natural-language task description, the current high-resolution screenshot, and the sequence of previously executed actions. It produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trajectory enclosed in think tags followed by a structured action in action tags specifying one of eight types (click, long-press, swipe, type, system-button, open, wait, terminate) with corresponding parameters such as pixel coordinates or input text. Rewards are sparse and binary at the episode level but decomposed per-step into format correctness, action-type match, and parameter accuracy (distance-based for coordinates, token-level F1 for text).

## Method

GUI-SAGE addresses both challenges through an entropy-aware framework built on GRPO. First, Self-Explanation Generation conditions the policy on the ground-truth action as a hint and lets it articulate why that action is correct in its own vocabulary, producing in-distribution reasoning trajectories that guarantee non-zero rewards while avoiding the entropy collapse caused by out-of-distribution expert CoT. Second, Entropy-Modulated Credit Assignment (EMCA) computes per-token entropy during generation, normalizes it across the batch, applies an exponential decay to obtain a modulation factor, and multiplies it with the group-normalized advantage—amplifying updates for confident low-entropy predictions and attenuating noisy high-entropy explorations. The composite reward combines format, action-type, and parameter components with equal weighting, and the full objective integrates EMCA-modulated advantages into the clipped GRPO loss with KL regularization against a reference policy.

## Results

GUI-SAGE-3B achieves 81.1% average step success rate across AndroidControl-Low (93.4%), AndroidControl-High (75.4%), and GUI-Odyssey (74.6%), while GUI-SAGE-7B further improves to 82.1% (93.7% / 76.8% / 75.8%). On the real-device AndroidWorld benchmark (116 tasks), GUI-SAGE-3B and -7B achieve 19.8% and 23.3% SR respectively, substantially outperforming their Qwen2.5-VL base models (3.5% and 19.0%). The 3B model surpasses the larger AgentCPM-GUI-8B (78.1%) by 3.0% and matches UI-Venus-Navi-7B (80.0%), while the 7B model sets a new competitive result at 82.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GUI-SAGE-3B outperforms the strongest RL baseline InfiGUI-R1-3B (75.5%) by 5.6% average SR, with particularly large gains on GUI-Odyssey (74.6% vs. 64.7%) and action type accuracy (92.1% vs. 81.6%). It surpasses the larger AgentCPM-GUI-8B (78.1%) by 3.0% and matches UI-Venus-Navi-7B (80.0%), while GUI-SAGE-7B exceeds UI-Venus-Navi-7B by 1.1%. Against SFT baselines, GUI-SAGE-3B improves over UI-TARS-2B (80.5%) by 0.6% and OS-Atlas-7B (72.8%) by 8.3%, and dramatically outperforms PAL-UI-3B by 26.1% on AndroidControl-High and 40.0% on GUI-Odyssey. On the real-device AndroidWorld benchmark, GUI-SAGE-7B (23.3%) substantially outperforms its Qwen2.5-VL-7B base (19.0%), and GUI-SAGE-3B (19.8%) nearly matches the 7B base while being smaller. The 3B model also substantially outperforms GUI-R1-7B (52.4%) and SE-GUI-7B (52.8% on AC-High), validating that self-explanation with entropy modulation is more effective than prior RL approaches regardless of model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR-for-GUI-automation line (UI-R1, GUI-R1, InfiGUI-R1) that adopted GRPO-style group-relative policy optimization with sparse binary rewards. It departs from the natural off-policy augmentation strategy (LUFFY, ExGRPO, DAgger) by demonstrating that expert demonstrations from significantly more capable models cause distribution mismatch and entropy collapse in GUI settings, and instead proposes self-explanation conditioned on ground-truth actions as an in-distribution alternative. The Entropy-Modulated Credit Assignment (EMCA) component is a new loss modification that recalibrates GRPO advantages using per-token entropy as a confidence proxy, building on the observation that standard group-normalized advantages treat all samples uniformly regardless of prediction quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies the zero-advantage trap (73.2% of early-training samples) as a fundamental bottleneck for on-policy RLVR in GUI automation, and provides a practical remedy that a 3B model can use to surpass 7B/8B competitors. The negative result—that expert CoT from a 72B model actively degrades training dynamics—challenges the common assumption that stronger-model reasoning is always beneficial for weaker-model training, and the entropy-based diagnosis gives practitioners a concrete diagnostic tool. Open questions include whether self-explanation generalizes beyond mobile GUI to desktop or web agents, whether the approach scales to longer-horizon multi-app tasks (AndroidWorld SR remains only ~20-23%), and whether EMCA's entropy normalization is stable in larger-scale or multi-task training regimes.
