## Problem

Semi-supervised regression (SSR) lacks a principled mechanism for pseudo-label quality control because continuous outputs have no inherent confidence measure analogous to class probabilities in classification. Without reliable filtering, low-quality pseudo-labels propagate during training and amplify prediction errors, a problem that existing consistency-regularization and uncertainty-based SSR methods fail to adequately address.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262890/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts face images (UTKFace, 224×224), face video clips (VIPL), or text reviews (Yelp) as input and produces a single continuous scalar (age in years, heart rate in BPM, or rating on a 1–5 scale). For each unlabeled sample, K=8 weakly augmented views are generated and passed through the network to obtain K predictions; the mean of these predictions serves as the pseudo-label, while a strongly augmented view provides the consistency target. The filtering decision is binary per sample: either all K predictions must fall within a Gaussian-derived confidence interval around their mean, or the sample is discarded from the unlabeled loss.

## Method

GaussianMatch introduces two coupled components for pseudo-label filtering in SSR. First, the Gaussian Consistency Filter (GCF) scores each of K weak-augmentation predictions via a Gaussian similarity S_j(k)=exp(−(q_j^(k)−μ_j)²/(2σ_j²)) and retains a sample only if the minimum similarity across all views exceeds a threshold τ, equivalently requiring |q_j^(k)−μ_j|≤ρσ_j for every k. Second, Adaptive Gaussian Standard Deviation Smoothing (AGDS) regularizes σ_j with an inverse-Gamma prior (hyperparameters α₀, β₀) to prevent the confidence interval from collapsing to zero width when predictions are highly consistent; β₀ follows a self-calibrating decay schedule that starts conservative during warmup and tightens over training. The overall objective combines an L1 loss on MixMatch-augmented labeled data with an MSE consistency loss between the strongly augmented prediction and the filtered pseudo-label (the mean of K weak predictions), weighted by the GCF mask.

## Results

On UTKFace (age estimation), GaussianMatch achieves MAE=9.80, R²=0.539, and SRCC=0.743 with only 30 labels, outperforming the strongest baseline RankUp (MAE=11.58, R²=0.359) by 15.4% in MAE and 50.1% in R². With 250 labels, it reaches MAE=6.38 and R²=0.794, surpassing RankUp (MAE=7.06, R²=0.751) and closing the gap to fully-supervised (MAE=4.85) to 10.5%. On VIPL (heart rate), GaussianMatch achieves MAE=7.795 and r=0.569 with 2,500 labels vs. RankUp's MAE=8.135 and r=0.518. On Yelp Review (rating prediction), it attains MAE=0.630 and SRCC=0.832 with 250 labels, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GaussianMatch outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and label regime. Against the strongest baseline RankUp (NeurIPS 2024), it reduces MAE by 15.4% (9.80 vs. 11.58) and improves R² by 50.1% (0.539 vs. 0.359) on UTKFace with 30 labels, and by 9.6% in MAE (6.38 vs. 7.06) with 250 labels. On VIPL, it beats RankUp by 4.2% MAE (7.795 vs. 8.135) and 9.8% in Pearson r (0.569 vs. 0.518) with 2,500 labels. MixMatch and UCVME, which lack regression-specific pseudo-label filtering, are consistently outperformed by 10–20% in MAE across all settings. The older consistency baselines (II-Model, Mean Teacher) and the contrastive method CLSS show the largest gaps, with GaussianMatch achieving 30–40% lower MAE under extreme label scarcity (30–50 labels). In the direct SSC-paradigm comparison (Table 3), FixMatch yields only marginal gains over supervised (MAE 9.30 vs. 9.42), while GaussianMatch achieves MAE=6.38, confirming that discrete confidence thresholding is fundamentally misaligned with continuous regression targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

GaussianMatch extends the consistency-regularization paradigm established by MixMatch (multi-view pseudo-label averaging) and FixMatch (weak/strong augmentation consistency with confidence gating) from discrete classification to continuous regression. The key conceptual departure is replacing class-probability thresholding—which has no natural analogue for unbounded continuous targets—with a Gaussian similarity score that measures prediction consensus across K weakly augmented views, grounded in the feature-space smoothness assumption. The Adaptive Gaussian Standard Deviation Smoothing (AGDS) component introduces a Bayesian inverse-Gamma prior to regularize the variance estimate, addressing a practical failure mode (confidence-interval collapse) that prior SSR methods like UCVME and CLSS do not explicitly handle.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Semi-supervised regression remains significantly underexplored relative to classification, largely because the absence of probabilistic confidence measures makes pseudo-label quality control difficult; this paper provides a principled, modality-agnostic mechanism that directly targets that bottleneck. The empirical demonstration that prediction standard deviation across augmentations correlates with pseudo-label error (Figure 4) offers a reusable diagnostic for any SSR pipeline. Open questions include whether the Gaussian assumption holds for highly multimodal or discontinuous target distributions, how the method scales to very large unlabeled pools, and whether the approach transfers to multi-output or structured regression settings. The authors note future extensions to imbalanced scenarios, suggesting the current framework assumes roughly uniform label distributions.
