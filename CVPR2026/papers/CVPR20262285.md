## Problem

Existing video-conditioned audio generation models are limited to producing on-screen environmental sounds, neglecting off-screen auditory events essential for complete auditory scenes. Recent holistic frameworks that jointly model on- and off-screen sounds remain restricted to non-speech domains, unable to generate or integrate human speech. This paper introduces UniHAGen, a new task that unifies speech and environmental sound generation across visible and invisible sources within a single multimodal paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262285/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes four conditioning inputs: video frames (encoded via CLIP), an on-screen environmental sound caption (which may be an empty placeholder when the on-screen content is speech), an off-screen environmental sound caption, and a speech transcription. The output is a single mixed audio waveform containing all on-screen and off-screen sound components (speech, ambient events, music) synthesized simultaneously. Generation occurs in the latent space of a pretrained AudioLDM VAE operating on Mel-spectrograms, with final waveforms reconstructed via a HiFi-GAN vocoder.

## Method

OmniSonic is a flow-matching diffusion framework whose backbone is a TriAttn-DiT architecture that extends standard DiT blocks with three parallel cross-attention branches (on-screen environmental, off-screen environmental, and speech), each receiving RoPE-encoded visual-text concatenated embeddings as keys/values. A Mixture-of-Experts gating module in each block computes softmax weights from mean-pooled condition embeddings via a lightweight MLP, then adaptively fuses the three attended representations into a single velocity prediction, enabling dynamic balancing among condition types per time step. Temporal alignment is enforced through a frame-aligned adaptive layer normalization that fuses CLIP visual features with the timestep embedding and upsamples to match audio latent resolution. The model is trained with a flow-matching objective (linear interpolation between noise and data) in the AudioLDM VAE latent space, using frozen FLAN-T5 and CLIP encoders alongside a trainable SpeechT5 + Durator speech encoder.

## Results

On UniHAGen-Bench (1,003 samples across three on/off-screen speech-environment scenarios), OmniSonic achieves the best FAD (3.07), MKL (2.79), AT score (28.07), mean semantic alignment (18.54), WER (0.14), CER (0.05), and PER (0.06) among all baselines. In subjective evaluation, OmniSonic attains the highest MOS across all four dimensions: overall quality (4.35), environmental faithfulness (4.42), speech faithfulness (4.74), and temporal alignment (4.29). The only metrics where OmniSonic trails are AV score (9.01 vs. MMAudio's 11.12) and DeSync (0.72 vs. HunyuanVideo-Foley's 0.38), attributed to the absence of Synchformer-based fine-grained temporal features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OmniSonic dominates the environmental-sound-only baselines (VINTAGe, MMAudio, HunyuanVideo-Foley) on every speech-related metric (WER, CER, PER) by a wide margin (e.g., WER 0.14 vs. 1.99/1.50/1.36), confirming that these models cannot synthesize intelligible speech. Against VoiceLDM, the strongest speech-only baseline, OmniSonic wins on all nine objective metrics, with the largest gaps in FAD (3.07 vs. 3.58) and MKL (2.79 vs. 5.74), reflecting VoiceLDM's inability to model visual grounding and off-screen sounds. The two closest competitors are MMAudio and HunyuanVideo-Foley: OmniSonic loses to them on AV score (9.01 vs. 11.12/10.86) and DeSync (0.72 vs. 0.51/0.38), which the authors attribute to those models' use of Synchformer features for fine-grained audio-visual synchronization, whereas OmniSonic relies solely on CLIP visual embeddings. In subjective evaluation, OmniSonic leads all baselines on every MOS dimension, with the most dramatic advantage in speech faithfulness (MOS-SF 4.74 vs. 1.15–1.17 for MMAudio/HunyuanVideo-Foley), underscoring that video-conditioned environmental models essentially fail at speech generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniSonic sits at the intersection of two previously separate research threads: video-conditioned environmental sound generation (VINTAGe, MMAudio, HunyuanVideo-Foley) and text-conditioned speech synthesis with environmental context (VoiceLDM). It builds on the flow-matching generative framework and the DiT backbone, adopts the frame-aligned adaLN mechanism from MMAudio for temporal alignment, and reuses the SpeechT5 + Durator encoder pipeline from VoiceLDM. The key departure is the TriAttn-DiT architecture with MoE gating, which unifies on-screen environmental captions, off-screen environmental captions, and speech transcriptions as three parallel cross-attention branches with adaptive weighting—something no prior model has attempted in a single video-conditioned framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing UniHAGen as a task and providing UniHAGen-Bench, this paper gives the community a standardized evaluation setting for the increasingly realistic goal of generating complete auditory scenes (speech + ambient + music) from video and text, rather than isolated sound categories. The MoE gating ablation (FAD nearly doubling, WER quadrupling without it) suggests that static multi-condition fusion is fundamentally inadequate when conditions have heterogeneous relevance across time steps—a finding that could inform broader multi-modal generation design. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond three sound sources, whether the reliance on CLIP visual features (lacking fine-grained temporal cues from Synchformer) can be addressed without sacrificing the unified architecture, and whether the synthetically constructed training data generalizes to truly wild, uncontrolled scenes.
