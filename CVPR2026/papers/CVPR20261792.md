## Problem

Existing semi-supervised medical image segmentation methods, whether general or class-imbalance-aware, rely on a single decoder to produce all-class outputs, forcing shared parameters to handle organs spanning orders of magnitude in size. This architecture is easily dominated by majority-class gradients, fundamentally capping tail-class segmentation accuracy in multi-organ CT scenarios where organ proportions are inherently imbalanced.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D abdominal CT volumes cropped into 96×96×96 patches during training (batch of 2 labeled + 2 unlabeled) and uses a 32×32×32 sliding window at inference. A key structural detail is the label-split mechanism: each expert decoder receives only a partial ground-truth mask where non-target classes are remapped to background, so the three decoders never jointly supervise all K+1 classes. The final output is a per-voxel class prediction over all foreground organs plus background, produced by the aggregation decoder.

## Method

DCA follows a three-stage pipeline. First, a Logarithmic Gap Analysis computes the log-ratio between adjacent sorted voxel proportions in the labeled set and identifies two natural breakpoints to statically partition foreground classes into Head, Medium, and Tail sets aligned with anatomical priors. Second, three architecturally asymmetric V-Net decoders are trained in parallel on a shared encoder: the Head decoder uses dilated convolutions (dilation=2) with fewer layers for large organs, the Medium decoder retains the standard V-Net configuration, and the Tail decoder increases depth (n_stages={4,4,3,2}) for fine-grained small-organ features; each is supervised only on its own class subset via the label-split strategy. Third, a two-level aggregation fuses expert knowledge: at the prediction level, logit stitching assembles a class-unbiased pseudo-label by taking each foreground class's logit exclusively from its designated expert and averaging the background channel; at the feature level, a Dynamic Feature Aggregation Module (DFAM) at every upsampling stage applies a lightweight gate generator (3×3×3 conv → ReLU → 1×1×1 conv → channel-wise softmax) to produce voxel-wise competitive attention maps that adaptively select the most competent expert prior, which is then residually added to the aggregation decoder's own features.

## Results

On the Synapse dataset (20% labeled), DCA achieves 73.20% average Dice and 1.78 ASD, surpassing the second-best method GA by 4.77% in Dice and ranking in the top three across all 13 organ classes. On the AMOS dataset (5% labeled), DCA achieves 69.90% average Dice and 2.66 ASD, outperforming GA by 6.39% and attaining the highest Dice on all 15 organs. The fully supervised V-Net upper bound on Synapse is 62.09% Dice, meaning DCA exceeds it by 11.11% with only 20% of labels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCA outperforms all 18 baselines (9 general + 9 class-imbalance SSMIS methods) on both Synapse and AMOS. The closest competitor is GA (ECCV 2024), which redesigns Dice/CE losses to correct gradient disparities across categories; DCA still leads by 4.77% Dice on Synapse (73.20 vs. 68.43) and 6.39% on AMOS (69.90 vs. 63.51). Among general SSMIS methods, the best performers are MagicNet (60.57% on Synapse) and β-FFT (54.89% on AMOS), both trailing DCA by over 12% and 15% respectively, highlighting that general methods fail to handle class imbalance. Among imbalance-specific methods, SKCDF (64.27% Synapse, 53.81% AMOS) and A&D (60.88% Synapse, 50.03% AMOS) are the next best but still fall short by 8.93% and 16.09% on Synapse. DCA's advantage is most pronounced on tail organs: on Synapse, LAG improves from 61.4 (GA) to 63.7, and on AMOS, Du improves from 38.3 (GA) to 46.1, confirming the asymmetric expert design effectively alleviates majority-class dominance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of class-imbalanced SSMIS work (DHC, GA, SKCDF) that modifies loss functions or pseudo-labeling strategies within a single-decoder architecture, arguing instead that the single decoder itself is the fundamental bottleneck when handling vastly different organ scales. It repurposes the 'divide and conquer' paradigm from unrelated domains (salient object detection, MRI acceleration, novel class discovery) into a concrete architectural decomposition: three asymmetric expert decoders trained with label-split supervision, plus a dynamic aggregation decoder. The Log-Gap Analysis for class partitioning is a data-driven alternative to the fixed-threshold or re-weighting heuristics used by prior imbalance methods, and the DFAM's voxel-wise competitive attention for feature fusion is a novel module not seen in prior SSMIS architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong evidence that architectural specialization (splitting the decoder by organ scale) can outperform loss-level debiasing by a wide margin, which may redirect future class-imbalanced SSMIS research toward multi-head or expert-mixture designs. However, the framework's complexity (three full decoders plus an aggregation decoder, all on a V-Net backbone) raises practical concerns about training cost and generalizability to other backbones, 2D modalities, or datasets with very different class counts. Open questions include whether the Log-Gap partitioning remains stable under extreme label scarcity (below 2%), whether the approach extends to instance or panoptic segmentation, and whether the DFAM's competitive attention generalizes beyond exactly three experts.
