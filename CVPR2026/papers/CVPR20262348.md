## Problem

Few-step (especially one-step) distilled diffusion models for text-to-image generation suffer from poor prompt fidelity and image quality, often failing to render key visual concepts from the prompt. While retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) has been explored for image synthesis, it has not been combined with few-step diffusion models, and existing RAG-based image generation methods are not optimized for the efficiency constraints of one-step inference.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262348/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single text prompt as input, which is encoded via a pretrained CLIP text encoder (OpenCLIP-ViT-H-14) to both condition the denoiser and query a 0.63M-pair OpenImage retrieval database using ScaNN. The output is a single 512×512 RGB image produced in one denoising step (1 NFE), with the retrieval step adding only ~1.9 ms of overhead on an NVIDIA L40S GPU.

## Method

The key insight is that the deepest-layer feature maps (H-space) of the UNet denoiser already encode high-level semantic content, so injecting retrieved H-space features can simplify the noise-to-image mapping. The paper first demonstrates that spherical normalized interpolation between target and retrieved H-space features improves prompt fidelity without any training, but requires an impractical per-prompt exhaustive search over blending weights. To eliminate this, they introduce a lightweight trainable H-space adapter (36M parameters, ~4% of the model) that uses cross-attention to automatically blend retrieved and target H-space features, with the blending strength modulated by the cosine similarity of the CLIP text embeddings. Training freezes both the teacher and student denoisers and only updates the H-space adapter plus LoRA on the student decoder, using latent adversarial distillation with a discriminator built from the frozen teacher UNet encoder and trainable projection layers stabilized by spectral normalization. The total loss combines a hinge adversarial objective, a smoothed L1 score-distillation loss, and a latent LPIPS loss, trained for 20K iterations on 64 L40S GPUs.

## Results

On the TIFA benchmark, ImageRAGTurbo (1 NFE) achieves a TIFA score of 0.801 and AES of 5.88, outperforming the 1-step Stable Diffusion Turbo baseline (TIFA 0.779, AES 5.85) by 2.2% in prompt fidelity while matching aesthetic quality. On MS-COCO, it achieves FID 25.59 and CLIP 0.323, surpassing the 4-step Latent Consistency Model (FID 36.52, CLIP 0.307) by a large margin and approaching the 50-step Stable Diffusion v2-1 teacher (FID 25.33, CLIP 0.330) with only a 1.03% FID gap. Inference latency is 116.7 ms/image, only 2.5% slower than SD Turbo (113.8 ms) and approximately 25× faster than the 50-step teacher model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest baseline, Stable Diffusion Turbo v2-1 (1-step, no retrieval), ImageRAGTurbo improves CLIP by 1.37% (0.323 vs 0.319), reduces FID by 0.45 (25.59 vs 26.04), and boosts TIFA by 2.2% (0.801 vs 0.779), confirming that retrieval augmentation adds meaningful prompt fidelity at negligible latency cost. Compared to the 50-step Stable Diffusion v2-1 teacher, the 1-step ImageRAGTurbo closes most of the gap: FID is within 1.03% and TIFA within 1.2%, though CLIP lags by 2.1% and AES by 0.16. Against the 4-step Latent Consistency Model, ImageRAGTurbo dominates on every metric (FID 25.59 vs 36.52, TIFA 0.801 vs 0.764) while using fewer steps. Versus the retrieval-augmented RDM baseline (50 steps), ImageRAGTurbo achieves a 10% higher CLIP score (0.323 vs 0.293) and 7.6% higher TIFA (0.801 vs 0.725) with 50× fewer function evaluations, demonstrating that the H-space adapter is far more effective than RDM's CLIP-embedding conditioning for few-step generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: few-step diffusion distillation (latent adversarial distillation [43], consistency models [30]) and retrieval-augmented image generation (RDM [1], Re-Imagen [3], ImageRAG [47]). The key conceptual departure is targeting the H-space of the UNet denoiser—identified as semantically meaningful in prior work [22, 34]—as the injection point for retrieved content, rather than conditioning at the text-embedding or pixel level as prior RAIG methods do. Unlike ImageRAG and FineRAG, which rely on expensive iterative MLLM calls, ImageRAGTurbo uses a lightweight cross-attention adapter (36M params, ~4% of total) trained via latent adversarial distillation, making it compatible with single-step inference. The method also departs from prior RAIG work by being explicitly designed for few-step (especially one-step) generation, a regime where prompt fidelity degrades most severely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1-step model that closes most of the gap to 50-step Stable Diffusion (1.2% TIFA drop) while adding only 2.5% latency overhead opens the door to real-time, interactive text-to-image generation with strong prompt fidelity. The striking result that 1-step ImageRAGTurbo (TIFA 0.801) substantially outperforms 50-step RDM (TIFA 0.725) suggests that retrieval augmentation interacts synergistically with few-step generation in ways not captured by full-step RAIG. Open questions include whether the H-space adapter generalizes to diffusion transformers (DiTs), whether compositional or multi-granularity retrieval can further close the remaining gap, and whether the CLIP-based retrieval bottleneck limits scalability to more complex prompts.
