## Problem

Existing Chain-of-Thought paradigms for image editing face a fundamental trade-off: generic CoT (e.g., Bagel) fails to stimulate fine-grained understanding, while specialized CoT (e.g., GoT's spatial localization) does not generalize across diverse editing tasks. The paper asks what CoT form and training strategy can simultaneously enhance understanding granularity and cross-task generalization in unified editing models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263726/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a source RGB image and a free-form text editing instruction, and produces both a structured CoT reasoning trace (comprising Meta-task Summary, Task Thinking, and Target-wise Editing Traversal) and the final edited image. The CoT is generated autoregressively before the diffusion-based image generation step within the unified Bagel transformer, and the target-wise traversal explicitly enumerates each object in the scene to decide whether and how it should be modified, providing interpretable per-target editing decisions.

## Method

Meta-CoT introduces a two-level decomposition: (1) Triplet Decomposition breaks each editing operation into (task, target, required understanding ability), guiding the model through Task Summary → Task Thinking → Target-wise Editing Traversal to reason over both what to edit and where; (2) Meta-task Decomposition further reduces the task element to five primitive meta-tasks (add, remove, replace, camera motion, position change), so training on only these five suffices to generalize to all 21 editing tasks via compositional reasoning. A CoT-Editing Consistency Reward, scored by Qwen2.5-VL on task and target alignment, is optimized via Flow-GRPO on early denoising timesteps to close the gap between CoT reasoning and actual editing behavior. Training proceeds in two stages: SFT on 1.5M image-instruction-CoT samples jointly with 100K visual understanding data, followed by RL fine-tuning on 20K editing samples with the CEC reward while freezing the understanding encoder.

## Results

On the 21-task editing benchmark, Meta-CoT+RL achieves an average VIEScore of 6.415, improving by 15.8% over the Train-Edit-Only baseline (5.538) trained on identical data without Meta-CoT, and by 20.1% over Bagel with thinking (5.307). On ImgEdit, the method reaches 3.83 overall, a 13.0% gain over Bagel(think) (3.39) and 19.7% over Bagel(w/o think) (3.20). The largest per-task gains on ImgEdit are in Hybrid editing (+25.9%), Remove (+23.4%), and Replace (+17.2%) over the base model. The method wins on all 21 tasks except text editing, where the extensive textual reasoning in CoT interferes with identifying the correct text to modify.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline Bagel (the unified model the method builds upon), Meta-CoT+RL improves the 21-task average by 13.1% over Bagel(w/o think) and 20.1% over Bagel(w/ think), with the largest per-task gains in Remove (+18.0%), Structure (+16.3%), and Camera Motion (+11.5%). The critical controlled comparison is against Train Editing Only (same data, same hyperparameters, no Meta-CoT), where the 15.8% gap isolates the contribution of the Meta-CoT paradigm itself. On ImgEdit, the method outperforms all open-source unified models—GoT (2.61), Ming-UniVision (3.06), UniWorld-V1 (3.26), OmniGen2 (3.44), and BLIP3o-NEXT (3.62)—but remains below the closed-source GPT Image 1 (4.20) and FLUX.1 Kontext Pro (4.00). The method also surpasses all diffusion-only editing systems (MagicBrush 1.90, Instruct-Pix2Pix 1.88, AnyEdit 2.45, UltraEdit 2.70, OmniGen 2.96, ICEdit 3.05, Step1X-Edit 3.06) and the multi-round IEAP (2.77). The sole task where Meta-CoT underperforms the base model is text editing (3.328 vs. 5.170 for Bagel w/o think), attributed to textual reasoning interfering with text localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Bagel, a unified multimodal transformer with native CoT-based editing, and extends the CoT-for-editing line of work (Bagel, GoT) by proposing a structured two-level decomposition paradigm. It departs from prior CoT approaches that are either too generic to stimulate understanding (Bagel's free-form thinking) or too specialized to generalize across tasks (GoT's localization-based CoT), instead formalizing editing as a (task, target, understanding-ability) triplet and further reducing tasks to five composable meta-tasks. The RL alignment stage leverages Flow-GRPO with a novel VLM-scored CoT-Editing Consistency Reward, a mechanism not present in prior CoT-editing work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that training on only five meta-tasks (add, delete, replace, camera motion, position change) yields performance comparable to full-task training across 21 diverse editing categories is a practically significant result for data-efficient training of editing models. The 21-task benchmark, which fills gaps left by GEdit-Bench, RiseBench, and ComplexEdit, provides the community a more comprehensive evaluation suite. A notable limitation is the regression on text editing, where the reasoning process interferes with accurate text perception—an open problem the authors flag for future work. Whether the meta-task decomposition framework extends beyond single-image editing (e.g., multi-image or video editing) remains unexplored.
