## Problem

Diffusion-based face swapping suffers from two intertwined issues: identity and attribute conditions work in opposition during joint training (identity pulls output toward the source face while attributes pull toward the target), and existing methods lack a principled way to prioritize identity preservation over attribute alignment. Prior approaches either add identity loss directly to the noise-prediction objective (relaxing the ELBO and degrading sample quality) or apply it only at inference via expensive multi-step sampling.

## Input / Output

The model takes a source face image (providing identity) and a target face image (providing pose, expression, and background context) at 512×512 resolution. During training, each image is annotated with a BLIP-2 text caption, and an off-the-shelf face detector produces an inpainting mask that separates the facial foreground from the background. The output is a single face-swapped image where the source identity is rendered with the target's expression, pose, and background.

## Method

The core contribution is a three-stage identity-constrained training pipeline with decoupled condition injection. Identity features are extracted from the source face via ArcFace (extended to n tokens via MLP) enriched with DINOv2 spatial embeddings, while attribute features come from the target face through SimSwap's 3-layer downsampling encoder; the two are fused via a cross-attention module and injected into Stable Diffusion 1.5's UNet through a GLIGEN adapter. Stage 1 fine-tunes with identity-only conditioning (λ_fuse=0) to constrain the solution space; Stage 2 introduces attribute conditioning with zero-initialized fusion weights and a weakened identity factor (λ_id=0.2) to prevent catastrophic forgetting; Stage 3 performs end-to-end refinement by treating the full 50-step DDIM chain as a differentiable pipeline and applying SNGAN adversarial loss plus ArcFace identity loss, with gradients computed on only k randomly sampled steps per batch to manage memory. Training data uses paired images of the same identity with different attributes to enforce data-wise condition decoupling and avoid leakage.

## Results

On FFHQ, the proposed method achieves the best FID of 3.61, substantially outperforming the next-best diffusion-based method REFace (5.62) and GAN-based InfoSwap (4.26). It also attains the highest identity retrieval accuracy (Top-1: 97.9%, Top-5: 99.7%), surpassing the previous best (SimSwap at 93.4% Top-1). In human evaluation, the method receives the most votes for fidelity (57.1%) and attribute consistency (33.2%), while ranking second for identity preservation (31.6%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method achieves the best FID (3.61) across all six baselines, with a notable margin over the closest diffusion-based competitor REFace (5.62) and GAN-based InfoSwap (4.26). For identity retrieval, it leads all methods by a wide margin (97.9% Top-1 vs. 93.4% for SimSwap and 92.8% for InfoSwap), validating the identity-constrained training scheme. On pose distance, the method (3.69) trails DiffSwap (2.43) and SimSwap (2.62), and on expression it is marginally behind SimSwap (0.97 vs. 0.95), though it remains competitive with all other baselines. In the human study, the method dominates fidelity (57.1% vs. 15.3% for DiffSwap) and attribute consistency (33.2% vs. 30.6% for DiffFace), while E4S edges it out on identity votes (36.7% vs. 31.6%), likely due to E4S overfitting to identity at the cost of artifacts. Overall, the method wins on the most perceptually important metrics (FID, fidelity, ID retrieval) while remaining competitive on pose and expression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the latent diffusion model framework (Stable Diffusion 1.5) for face swapping, following the trajectory from GAN-based methods (SimSwap, InfoSwap, E4S) to diffusion-based approaches (DiffFace, DiffSwap, REFace). Its key departure is a multi-stage identity-constrained training scheme that first narrows the solution space with identity-only conditioning before introducing attribute conditions, combined with a decoupled injection strategy using same-identity image pairs rather than augmented copies. The condition injection mechanism repurposes GLIGEN's cross-attention adapter, while identity encoding leverages ArcFace and DINOv2, and attribute encoding reuses SimSwap's downsampling network—each component is borrowed from prior work but assembled to resolve the identity-attribute conflict that plagues joint single-stage training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical and well-motivated recipe for handling conflicting conditioning signals in diffusion models, which is a broader challenge beyond face swapping. The identity-constrained multi-stage approach and decoupled data pairing offer transferable insights for any conditional generation task where multiple conditions pull the output in opposing directions. Limitations include evaluation solely on FFHQ, a pose metric that trails SimSwap, and dependence on 4.5M carefully paired images. Open questions include whether the multi-stage paradigm generalizes to other identity-attribute tradeoffs (e.g., age, ethnicity) and whether the post-training DDIM refinement can be made more efficient than the 50-step cascade.
