## Problem

Video diffusion transformers (vDiTs) suffer from substantial inference latency because self-attention dominates execution time (~78% on average). Prior acceleration methods transplant sparsity patterns from LLMs (sliding windows, tiling, masking) but overlook the inherent spatio-temporal correlations in video latent space that actually govern attention map structure. No prior work identified the root cause of diverse attention patterns at the channel level nor exploited it for principled compute reduction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262041/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a training-free, inference-time acceleration applied to existing vDiT models (HunyuanVideo, Wan2.1, CogVideoX, Open-Sora-Plan) that take a text prompt and produce a generated video. Evaluated configurations range from 1.2-second clips at 640×480 (Open-Sora-Plan) to 5.33-second clips at 544×960 (HunyuanVideo). The technique modifies only the self-attention computation within each transformer block, leaving cross-attention, MLP layers, and the overall denoising pipeline unchanged.

## Method

TIMERIPPLE identifies that attention map patterns in vDiTs arise from spatio-temporal correlations at the token channel level: RoPE encodes temporal information in early channels and spatial (x, y) information in later channels, causing correlated tokens to have similar values along individual channels. The method reuses partial attention scores of spatially or temporally similar tokens rather than masking them to zero, preserving the full value range and reducing MSE by an order of magnitude over masking baselines. Similarity is measured via a standard-error metric over a window of size 2 along each of the three axes (temporal, x, y), and a token is marked reusable if it meets the threshold on any axis (logical OR). An adaptive thresholding schedule linearly increases the reuse threshold from step i_min to i_max (e.g., 11 to 21 for HunyuanVideo), matching the observed MSE sensitivity curve so that each denoising step introduces a consistent, controllable amount of approximation error. This yields up to 85% self-attention compute savings with <0.06% VBench quality loss and >9 dB PSNR improvement over state-of-the-art baselines.

## Results

TIMERIPPLE achieves up to 85% self-attention computational savings across four vDiTs (HunyuanVideo, Wan2.1, CogVideoX, Open-Sora-Plan), yielding end-to-end speedups of 2.3–2.7× while preserving near-identical video quality (<0.06% VBench loss). On HunyuanVideo, TIMERIPPLE₈₅% reaches 80.44% VBench (vs. 80.28% original) with 31.28 dB PSNR and 105.52×10¹⁵ FLOPs (vs. 214.27×10¹⁵ original). The method consistently outperforms all baselines on PSNR by >5 dB, SSIM by >0.05, and LPIPS by >0.03, while delivering the highest speedup (2.66× on HunyuanVideo vs. 1.69× for the best baseline SVG₇₀%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIMERIPPLE is compared against four baselines: Δ-DiT (coarse blockwise reuse), PAB₅,₉ (caches intermediate attention in the middle 80% of denoising steps), MINFERENCE (52% self-attention reduction via predefined sparse patterns), and SVG₇₀% (full computation in first 12 steps, 30% sparsity thereafter). On VBench, TIMERIPPLE matches or exceeds the original model on 3 of 4 models (HunyuanVideo 80.44 vs. 80.28, CogVideoX 81.16 vs. 80.95, Open-Sora-Plan 73.18 vs. 72.44) and trails SVG₇₀% by only 0.44 points on Wan2.1 (81.03 vs. 81.47). On image-quality metrics, TIMERIPPLE dominates all baselines by wide margins: on HunyuanVideo it achieves 31.28 dB PSNR vs. 26.09 (Δ-DiT), 25.78 (SVG), and 25.00 (MINFERENCE), with SSIM 0.915 vs. 0.862/0.843/0.825 respectively. In terms of speedup, TIMERIPPLE₈₅% delivers 2.66× on HunyuanVideo, outpacing SVG₇₀% (1.69×), MINFERENCE (1.44×), PAB (1.23×), and Δ-DiT (1.22×); similar gaps hold on the other three models (2.31–2.34× vs. 1.11–1.66×). PAB₅,₉ suffers the largest quality degradation (e.g., VBench drops to 68.17 on CogVideoX, LPIPS 0.716), while MINFERENCE and Δ-DiT show moderate quality loss but far lower speedups than TIMERIPPLE.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of vDiT acceleration that transplants LLM sparsity techniques (Minference, SVG, AdaSpa) by arguing that attention map patterns are not the primary signal but rather *artifacts* of spatio-temporal correlations at the token-channel level induced by RoPE's structured encoding. It repurposes the long-standing observation from video compression and tracking literature that adjacent frames/rows share redundant information, applying it to the channel dimension of Q and K tokens rather than to the attention map itself. The method is conceptually closer to classical inter-frame prediction in codecs than to the sparse-attention lineage it benchmarks against, and it introduces an adaptive per-denoising-step thresholding scheme that has no direct analogue in prior vDiT acceleration work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Achieving 85% self-attention savings with <0.06% VBench degradation across four mainstream vDiTs is a meaningful efficiency gain for commercial video generation deployment. However, the unstructured sparsity pattern is incompatible with FlashAttention and similar IO-aware kernels, meaning the reported speedups are theoretical (proportional FLOP reduction) rather than measured end-to-end with optimized attention implementations — a significant practical gap. The open question of integrating channel-level reuse with structured, hardware-friendly attention kernels is explicitly flagged as future work and will determine whether this technique translates into real wall-clock speedups. The finding that a single shared threshold across all prompts suffices (sensitivity is to denoising step, not prompt) is a useful practical simplification for deployment.
