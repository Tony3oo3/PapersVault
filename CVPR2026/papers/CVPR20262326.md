## Problem

Existing computational aberration correction (CAC) methods are tailored to specific optical systems, resulting in poor cross-lens generalization and labor-intensive retraining for new lenses. No comprehensive benchmark covering diverse photographic lens designs exists to evaluate universal CAC, and the specific factors (prior utilization, architecture, training strategy) that govern CAC performance remain unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262326/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark provides 256×256 aberration image patches generated by convolving high-resolution GT images with spatially and spectrally varying PSFs from 120 test lenses (873 training lenses for model training). Non-blind methods additionally receive the corresponding PSF or FoV coordinates as conditioning input. Evaluation is performed across five ODE-defined severity levels using six metrics spanning image fidelity (PSNR, SSIM), optical quality (OIQE), and perceptual quality (LPIPS, FID, ClipIQA), combined into a single Overall Performance score.

## Method

The paper constructs the UNICAC benchmark by extending the OptiFusion automatic optical design method to include aspherical surface parameters, generating a library of physically valid spherical and aspherical lenses across four specification dimensions (piece number, aperture position, half FoV, F-number). A novel Optical Degradation Evaluator (ODE) framework quantifies lens degradation severity by integrating image fidelity metrics (PSNR, SSIM), MTF-based optical quality (OIQE), spatial uniformity across five FoVs, and channel uniformity across three RGB channels, enabling stratified lens sampling into five severity levels. The benchmark is validated against Zemax ray-tracing and real-captured images, then used to systematically evaluate 24 methods (11 CAC-specific and 13 general image restoration) under identical training protocols. Through this evaluation, the paper identifies three dominant performance factors: optical/clear-image prior utilization, CNN vs. Transformer architecture choice, and regression vs. GAN vs. diffusion training paradigm.

## Results

Across 24 methods evaluated on the UNICAC benchmark (120 test lenses, 5 aberration severity levels), FeMaSR achieves the best Overall Performance (O.P. = 1.618), driven by the lowest LPIPS (0.136) and highest ClipIQA (0.520), while NAFNet (O.P. = 1.549) and DiffBIR (O.P. = 1.547) follow closely. For image fidelity, PART attains the highest PSNR (28.10 dB) and SSIM (0.866), whereas Uformer leads in PSNR among CNN-based methods (27.95 dB) with the fastest inference (13.07 ms). Optimization-based methods (Wiener, DPIR, Eboli et al.) consistently rank in the bottom 5 (O.P. 0.97–1.04), confirming that learning-based approaches substantially outperform them across all six metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This benchmark paper evaluates 24 methods rather than proposing a new one, so the key comparisons are cross-method. FeMaSR (Transformer + GAN + codebook prior) leads in O.P. (1.618) and dominates perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.136, ClipIQA 0.520), outperforming the next-best NAFNet (O.P. 1.549) by 0.069, but trails PART in PSNR by 1.16 dB. Among CAC-specific methods, FOV-KPN (O.P. 1.502) and PART (O.P. 1.494) outperform all other CAC baselines, while optimization-based methods (Wiener 0.972, Eboli 0.998, DPIR 1.043) are 0.5–0.6 O.P. points behind the best learning-based methods. PART's PSF-attention mechanism gives it a consistent edge over SwinIR (same backbone) across all metrics, confirming the value of explicit PSF conditioning. DiffBIR (O.P. 1.547) ranks 3rd overall and improves in relative ranking as aberration severity increases, whereas FOV-KPN's ranking drops from mild to severe levels, showing that low-dimensional FoV priors are insufficient for complex aberrations. CNN-based regression methods (NAFNet, MIMOUNet, Uformer) achieve the best performance-to-latency trade-off, with NAFNet reaching O.P. 1.549 in just 0.64 ms versus DiffBIR's 3168 ms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the automatic optical design method OptiFusion [13] by incorporating aspherical parameters, enabling generation of a much larger and more diverse lens library than prior universal CAC efforts [14, 21, 24] that relied on limited spherical-only or manually designed lenses. It departs from the dominant lens-specific CAC paradigm (FOV-KPN, PART, DFUnet) by constructing a cross-lens benchmark, and introduces the ODE framework to replace the traditional RMS radius metric for quantifying aberration severity, drawing on MTF-based OIQE and image fidelity metrics. The evaluation spans both dedicated CAC methods and general image restoration models (CNN, Transformer, GAN, diffusion), positioning the work as a unifying empirical study rather than a single-method contribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive benchmark for universal CAC in photographic cameras, providing the field with a standardized evaluation protocol (ODE-based severity levels, six metrics across fidelity/optical/perceptual dimensions) and a large-scale lens library (873 training, 120 test) that was previously unavailable. Key open questions remain: how to design training strategies that simultaneously optimize image fidelity, perceptual quality, and optical quality (OIQE), and whether the simulation-based benchmark fully captures real-world manufacturing tolerances. The finding that chromatic aberration has surprisingly limited impact on CAC performance, and that CNNs outperform Transformers for this task, may redirect research effort away from currently popular architectures toward simpler, faster designs.
