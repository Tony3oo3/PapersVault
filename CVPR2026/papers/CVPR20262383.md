## Problem

Flow matching models inject Gaussian noise uniformly in the spatial or latent domain, but this corruption affects frequency components non-uniformly: low-frequency structure is reconstructed early while high-frequency details emerge only late in the reverse process. Without explicit frequency-aware conditioning, models produce slightly blurred outputs with degraded fine details, because the non-uniform frequency recovery dynamics are neither modeled nor leveraged during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262383/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label and a Gaussian noise sample as input and produces a generated RGB image. For 64×64 generation it operates directly in pixel space; for 256×256 and 512×512 it works in the latent space of a pre-trained Stable Diffusion VAE (32×32 and 64×64 latents, respectively). During training, the noisy input is additionally decomposed via DFT into low- and high-frequency components using Gaussian filters, which feed the frequency branch alongside the original noisy signal feeding the spatial branch.

## Method

FreqFlow introduces a two-branch architecture with dual-domain supervision. The frequency branch (a Vision Transformer) applies DFT to the noisy input, separates it into low-frequency and high-frequency components via Gaussian band-pass/low-pass filters, and predicts velocity fields for each band; a time-dependent MLP produces an adaptive weight ω_t that blends the two frequency feature maps, shifting emphasis from low-frequency structure (early timesteps) to high-frequency detail (late timesteps). The spatial branch (ConvNeXt) receives the noisy input plus the frequency branch's integrated features via element-wise addition and predicts the overall velocity field. Training uses a composite loss combining spatial L2 and FFT-domain L2 terms applied to both the spatial branch output and the frequency branch's individual low/high-frequency predictions, with a hyperparameter α balancing the frequency-branch terms (default 0.5). This design explicitly aligns the generation trajectory with the natural frequency evolution of flow matching, yielding sharper details and better structural coherence.

## Results

On the class-conditional ImageNet-256 benchmark, FreqFlow-H (1.08B parameters) achieves a state-of-the-art FID of 1.38 with IS 298.5, surpassing the prior best flow matching model SiT-XL/2 (FID 2.06) by 0.68 and the diffusion model DiT-XL/2 (FID 2.27) by 0.89. The smaller FreqFlow-L (507M) reaches FID 1.54, outperforming DiMR-G/2R (1.06B, FID 1.63) by 0.09 FID with fewer parameters. Without classifier-free guidance, FreqFlow-H achieves FID 2.45, beating DiMR-G/2R (3.56) by 1.11 FID. On ImageNet-512, FreqFlow-L attains FID 2.02 and IS 285.3, outperforming DiT-XL/2 (FID 3.04) by 1.02.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FreqFlow-H (1.08B) achieves the best FID (1.38) among all flow-based and diffusion-based methods, beating SiT-XL/2 (2.06) by 0.68 FID and DiT-XL/2 (2.27) by 0.89 FID, while also surpassing the larger DiMR-G/2R (1.06B, FID 1.63) by 0.25. The smaller FreqFlow-L (507M) outperforms DiMR-G/2R (1.06B) by 0.09 FID and MDTv2-XL/2 (676M, FID 1.58) by 0.04, demonstrating strong parameter efficiency. Against autoregressive and mask-prediction methods, FreqFlow-H beats MAR-H (943M, FID 1.55) by 0.17 and FlowAR-H (1.9B, FID 1.65) by 0.27, though MaskBit (305M, FID 1.52) remains slightly ahead by 0.02 FID. Without classifier-free guidance, FreqFlow-H (FID 2.45) substantially outperforms DiMR-G/2R (3.56) and DiT-XL/2 (9.62), indicating the frequency-aware design provides intrinsic quality gains beyond guidance. On ImageNet-512, FreqFlow-L (FID 2.02) leads all listed baselines, exceeding DiT-XL/2 (3.04) and U-ViT-H/4 (4.05) by 1.02 and 2.03 FID respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreqFlow extends the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022) and its most direct predecessor SiT (Ma et al., 2024) by introducing an explicit frequency-domain decomposition into the generation process. While SiT and DiT operate entirely in the spatial/latent domain, FreqFlow adds a dedicated frequency branch (ViT-based) that separately models low- and high-frequency components, coupled with a spatial branch (ConvNeXt-based) that synthesizes the final image guided by time-adaptive frequency conditioning. The dual-domain loss (spatial + FFT-based frequency supervision) is a new training strategy not present in prior flow matching or diffusion work. The latent space is inherited from the Stable Diffusion VAE (Rombach et al., 2022), consistent with the broader latent generative modeling paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency error analysis (Table 1, Fig. 3) provides a useful diagnostic lens for understanding why flow matching models produce slightly blurred outputs: high-frequency components are systematically under-recovered compared to low-frequency ones. The SOTA FID of 1.38 on ImageNet-256 with fewer parameters than DiT-XL/2 demonstrates that explicit frequency modeling is a practical and efficient path to sharper generation. Open questions include whether the two-branch overhead scales favorably to text-to-image or video settings, whether the Gaussian frequency filters are optimal versus learned decompositions, and whether the coarse-to-fine temporal weighting generalizes beyond class-conditional generation. The paper is limited to ImageNet class-conditional benchmarks, so broader applicability remains to be validated.
