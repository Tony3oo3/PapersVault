## Problem

Disentangling visual layers in real-world images is difficult when layers interact non-linearly through shading, perspective distortion, and surface reflectance, as in logo-object decomposition. Existing layered decomposition methods rely on linear alpha compositing or explicit physical priors, which fail for globally coupled, non-linear interactions that require semantic understanding of what constitutes an overlaid element versus its supporting surface.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260735/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a composite RGB image, a binary mask delineating the logo region, and a text prompt, all encoded into latent space via a VAE. It produces two outputs simultaneously: a rectified logo layer (fronto-parallel and largely illumination-invariant) and a logo-free clean object image. Supervision is structured as a three-panel grid image following the in-context learning paradigm, and the decomposition and composition functions share a single LoRA parameter space for efficiency.

## Method

The method fine-tunes FLUX.1-Fill-dev (a Diffusion Transformer inpainting model) via lightweight LoRA adaptation to perform logo-object decomposition in latent space using flow-matching loss. The key innovation is a cycle-consistent training scheme where a decomposition function F_D and a composition function F_C are jointly trained under a shared LoRA, with a cycle-consistency loss enforcing that F_C(F_D(I)) ≈ I and F_D(F_C(A,B)) ≈ (A,B) at different timesteps, allowing the two tasks to mutually regularize each other. A progressive self-improving data loop starts from 100 manually curated triplets, iteratively generates candidate decompositions with an IC-LoRA, filters them via Qwen-VL for visual plausibility, and feeds accepted samples back into training, progressively improving data quality and model stability across rounds.

## Results

On the logo-object decomposition benchmark (1.5K synthetic test samples), the proposed method achieves the highest logo VQAScore (0.43) and the best average VLMScore (4.22) across three VLM evaluators (Qwen, GPT-4o, Gemini), narrowly edging out Gemini (4.20) and substantially outperforming Flux-Kontext (3.79) and ICEdit (2.55). On intrinsic decomposition (MAW dataset), the method reports Intensity error of 0.57×100 and Chromaticity error of 3.54, approaching the SOTA Careaga et al. (0.54 / 3.37) without task-specific priors. A user study with 30 participants confirms the method is ranked top-1 in over 50% of cases on both consistency and perceptual reasonableness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against AssetDropper [25], the proposed method achieves a higher logo VQAScore (0.43 vs. 0.42) and, critically, produces a clean object layer, whereas AssetDropper only extracts the logo and cannot recover the underlying object. Compared to ICEdit [48], the method dominates across every metric, with an average VLMScore of 4.22 versus 2.55 and a logo VQAScore of 0.43 versus 0.31, reflecting ICEdit's difficulty with multi-output decomposition. Against Flux-Kontext [23], the method wins on logo VQAScore (0.43 vs. 0.40) and average VLMScore (4.22 vs. 3.79), though Kontext slightly edges ahead on object VQAScore (0.32 vs. 0.31), consistent with its strength in logo removal. The closest competitor is Gemini [9], which matches the method on object VQAScore (0.32 vs. 0.31) and trails by only 0.02 in average VLMScore (4.20 vs. 4.22); however, the user study reveals Gemini's VLM advantage does not translate to human preference, likely due to artifacts like pseudo-transparent outputs. On intrinsic decomposition (Table 2), the method (Intensity 0.57, Chromaticity 3.54) is within 0.03–0.17 of the dedicated SOTA method Careaga et al. [6] (0.54, 3.37), and the cycle-consistency ablation confirms a consistent improvement over the no-cycle variant (0.59, 3.65).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on FLUX.1-Fill-dev as a pretrained inpainting diffusion transformer and adopts the In-Context LoRA (IC-LoRA) paradigm for task-specific fine-tuning, but repurposes the ICL grid from composition/editing to the inverse operation of decomposition. It departs from prior layered decomposition work that assumes linear alpha blending or relies on explicit masks and handcrafted priors, and from asset-extraction methods (AssetDropper, RRM) that require fixed extraction views or reward-driven optimization. The key conceptual departure is jointly training decomposition and composition in a single LoRA under cycle consistency, contrasting with DecompDiffusion which trains separate models, and extending the ICL paradigm from single-input-single-output to single-input-multi-output.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that diffusion-based in-context models can learn to 'disassemble' images, not just compose or edit them, and that cycle consistency between dual decomposition-composition functions provides effective regularization for the inherently ill-posed decomposition problem without requiring dense ground-truth annotations. Generalization to intrinsic decomposition and foreground-background separation suggests a unified framework, though the current formulation is limited to two layers and struggles when the overlaid element dominates the scene. Open questions include extending to multi-layer decomposition, incorporating motion or 3D structure, and whether the progressive self-improving loop can scale beyond the modest dataset sizes used here.
