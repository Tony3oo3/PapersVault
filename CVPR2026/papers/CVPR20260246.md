## Problem

Semantic segmentation of ultra-high-resolution (UHR) remote sensing imagery (>4K) faces a fundamental trade-off: downsampling loses fine structural details while patch-based processing fragments global context. Existing multi-branch architectures that jointly model local and global information suffer from high computational cost due to redundant patch inference and conflicting gradient dynamics across branches during joint training, leading to optimization imbalance and misaligned features.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260246/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes full UHR remote sensing images (2448×2448 on DeepGlobe, 5000×5000 on Inria Aerial) as input and produces a pixel-wise semantic segmentation map with class-specific probabilities. Internally, the input is decomposed into a full-resolution high-frequency component and a downsampled (1/4 resolution) low-frequency component, so the network simultaneously processes the image at two different spatial scales without any spatial cropping or patch partitioning.

## Method

F2Net introduces an Adaptive Frequency Decomposition module that generates spatially-varying low-pass and high-pass filters via channel-wise grouping and softmax-normalized depthwise convolution, splitting the input into complementary frequency components. Three specialized branches process these: a full-resolution high-frequency branch built on VMamba (SSM-based) for boundary and texture detail, a short-range sub-branch (DeepLabv3+ with ResNet-18) and a long-range sub-branch (TinyViT) both operating on the downsampled low-frequency input for local and global semantics. A Hybrid-Frequency Fusion (HFF) module integrates branch outputs using channel-wise attention augmented with a cross-branch interaction matrix. Two novel training objectives are introduced: Cross-Frequency Alignment Loss (symmetric KL divergence) enforces semantic consistency between frequency branches, and Cross-Frequency Balance Loss regularizes gradient magnitudes across branches to prevent one branch from dominating optimization.

## Results

On DeepGlobe (2448×2448 satellite images, 6 classes), F2Net achieves 80.22 mIoU and 87.09 F1 at 24.30 FPS with 2,767 MB memory, outperforming the previous best UHR method BPT (76.60 mIoU) by +3.62 points. On Inria Aerial (5000×5000 aerial images, binary building detection), F2Net reaches 83.39 mIoU and 91.19 F1, improving over the prior best RUE (79.00 mIoU) by +4.39 points. F2Net is the only method exceeding 80% mIoU on DeepGlobe and the third-fastest among UHR methods with reported FPS (behind RUE at 44.38 FPS and ISDNet at 27.70 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

F2Net outperforms all 17 baselines on both benchmarks. On DeepGlobe, it leads BPT [31] (76.60 mIoU) by +3.62, GPWFormer [14] (75.80) by +4.42, SGNet [34] (75.44) by +4.78, and ISDNet [12] (73.30) by +6.92, while also surpassing the best conventional method (FCN-8s cropping at 71.80) by +8.42. On Inria Aerial, F2Net (83.39 mIoU) beats RUE [25] (79.00) by +4.39, BPT [31] (77.10) by +6.29, and ISDNet [12] (74.23) by +9.16. In terms of speed, F2Net (24.30 FPS on DeepGlobe) is slower than RUE (44.38 FPS) and ISDNet (27.70 FPS) but substantially faster than GLNet (0.17 FPS), FCtL (0.13 FPS), and SGNet (0.66 FPS), which rely on multi-patch inference. The memory footprint (2,767 MB on DeepGlobe) is moderate—higher than ISDNet (1,948 MB) and RUE (1,638 MB) but lower than FCtL (4,450 MB) and Mask2Former (4,500 MB). On Inria Aerial, F2Net's 5.50 FPS is slower than RUE (10.89 FPS) and ISDNet (6.90 FPS), reflecting the larger 5000×5000 input size, but it still far exceeds patch-based methods like GLNet (0.05 FPS) and FCtL (0.04 FPS).

## Relation to Prior Work

F2Net extends the multi-branch UHR segmentation paradigm established by GLNet, ISDNet, and SGNet, but replaces their spatial partitioning strategy (cropping or patching) with a frequency-domain decomposition that avoids spatial artifacts and object fragmentation. The adaptive frequency decomposition module draws on dynamic filtering ideas from Magid et al. (ICCV 2021), while the three branches combine heterogeneous backbones—VMamba (SSM) for full-resolution high-frequency detail, DeepLabv3 for short-range context, and TinyViT for long-range semantics. The cross-frequency alignment and balance losses are novel training objectives that address gradient conflicts in multi-branch optimization, building conceptually on GradNorm and multi-task learning literature but applied specifically to frequency-asymmetric branches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

F2Net demonstrates that frequency-domain decomposition is a viable alternative to spatial partitioning for UHR segmentation, potentially reducing the boundary artifacts and redundant inference that plague patch-based methods. The cross-frequency losses offer a general recipe for stabilizing multi-branch training that could transfer to other asymmetric-branch architectures. However, the memory footprint (5.5 GB on Inria Aerial) and moderate FPS (5.5 on Inria, 24.3 on DeepGlobe) limit real-time deployment, and the approach has only been validated on two benchmarks with relatively small image counts. Open questions include whether the frequency decomposition generalizes to video or multi-spectral UHR data, and whether the SSM backbone is essential or merely one option among long-sequence models.
