## Problem

Vision-language models excel at single-view spatial reasoning but lack the ability to reason about geometric relationships between multiple viewpoints, which is essential for embodied AI with multi-camera setups. Existing multi-view datasets focus on identifying objects within individual views rather than supervising how viewpoints relate geometrically, leaving VLMs unable to perform cross-view correspondence, consistency checking, or viewpoint localization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261073/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Each XVR sample presents 2–6 images (mean 4.32) from either calibrated multi-view scene captures or robotic manipulation trajectories, paired with a natural-language question in a consistent reference-target format where multiple reference views provide context and the model selects from spatially separated distractors. The output is a multiple-choice text answer (e.g., a color label, image index, or directional term). In the VLA transfer setting, the same multi-view image input is consumed by a VLM backbone whose representations feed a diffusion action head that outputs continuous robot action sequences for a Franka Emika arm.

## Method

XVR is a 100K-sample dataset organized into three SfM-inspired categories—Correspondence (point and directional matching across views), Verification (spatial and temporal consistency checking), and Localization (viewpoint, directional view, cross-scenario, and language-conditioned positioning)—instantiated through eight specific tasks. The generation pipeline uses 3D-to-2D projection of sampled primitives (points, camera positions) with calibrated camera parameters for geometry-based tasks, and spatio-temporal metadata with SSIM-based perceptual filtering for robotic trajectory tasks, ensuring questions are non-trivial. The primary experimental protocol fine-tunes Qwen3-VL-2B on XVR via supervised learning, yielding a 1.8× relative accuracy gain on XVR-Eval and consistent improvements on MindCube-Tiny and RoboSpatial-Home. For embodied transfer, a diffusion action head is appended to the XVR-trained VLM following the GR00T-N1.5 architecture, and the resulting VLA is trained on RoboCasa simulation data, improving manipulation success rates by an average of 13% absolute across three tasks requiring cross-view spatial disambiguation.

## Results

On XVR-Eval (1,866 held-out samples), Qwen3-VL-2B-XVR achieves 68.06% overall accuracy, a 1.8× relative improvement over its base model Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct (36.82%) and surpassing all closed-source models including GPT-5 (61.74%) and Claude-4.5-Sonnet (51.18%). The model exceeds human performance (83.85%) on Point Correspondence (94.32% vs. 92.31%) and Spatial Verification (84.85% vs. 88.46%, approaching human level). On external benchmarks, XVR training yields +7.0% on MindCube Among and +7.6% on RoboSpatial Compatibility, and when integrated as a VLA backbone, improves RoboCasa manipulation success rates by an average of 13% absolute across three tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen3-VL-2B-XVR (68.06% overall) outperforms all ten baselines on XVR-Eval, with the largest margin over GPT-5 (61.74%, +6.3 pts) and Claude-4.5-Sonnet (51.18%, +16.9 pts). Among closed-source models, GPT-5 leads at 61.74% but still trails the XVR-trained 2B model by 6.3 points; Gemini-Robotics-ER-1.5, despite robotics-specific training, scores lowest among closed models (47.48%) with Viewpoint Localization accuracy (6.22%) below random chance (33.33%). Among open-source baselines, the 4B Qwen3-VL (45.02%) and InternVL-3.5-4B (32.32%) are the strongest, yet both fall 23+ points below the XVR-trained 2B model, demonstrating that explicit cross-view supervision outweighs model scale. The only task where XVR training causes a regression is Temporal Verification (41.18% vs. 45.29% base), revealing a spatial-over-temporal tradeoff. On external benchmarks, XVR training consistently improves Qwen3-VL-2B across MindCube-Tiny and RoboSpatial-Home subtasks, with the largest gains on tasks aligned with cross-view reasoning (Among +7.0%, Compatibility +7.6%) and minimal gains on camera-motion tasks (Around, Rotation), confirming that transfer is strongest where geometric cross-view relations are the core skill.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends multi-view spatial reasoning from object-centric formulations (what appears in each view, as in MindCube, All-Angles-Bench, and MMSI-Bench) to explicit geometric relations *between* views themselves. The three task categories—correspondence, verification, and localization—directly mirror the pipeline of Structure-from-Motion, repurposing a classical 3D reconstruction framework as a supervision structure for VLM training. For the embodied transfer experiments, the paper builds on the GR00T-N1.5 VLA architecture, adding a diffusion action head to XVR-trained VLM representations, and draws robotic trajectory data from OXE and AgiBot-World to complement calibrated multi-view captures from WildRGB-D.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

XVR is the first large-scale dataset providing explicit cross-view geometric supervision spanning both general and robotic domains, filling a gap where prior multi-view benchmarks tested object properties rather than inter-view relations. The finding that a 2B model fine-tuned on XVR surpasses GPT-5, Claude, and Gemini on cross-view reasoning suggests that targeted geometric supervision can be more effective than raw model scale for spatial tasks—a useful signal for practitioners with limited compute. Key open questions remain: temporal reasoning degrades after XVR training (the only task that worsens), the VLA transfer is validated only in simulation, and whether these gains persist on real-robot platforms is untested. Extending cross-view supervision to incorporate dynamic camera motion and temporal coherence is a natural next step.
