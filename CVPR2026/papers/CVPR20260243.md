## Problem

Existing MLLM spatial reasoning approaches either depend on explicit 3D priors (point clouds, depth maps, camera poses) that impose heavy data preparation and alignment costs, or rely on purely 2D frame-by-frame processing that fails to establish cross-frame spatial associations. Without 3D geometry, MLLMs struggle to track viewpoint continuity across frames and miss implicit objects that serve as spatial bridges, leading to fragmented and incorrect multi-frame reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260243/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives 16 uniformly sampled video frames (resolution capped at 256×28×28, proportionally downsampled if exceeded) and a natural language spatial question. It outputs a structured four-field chain-of-thought (Summary → Role-Play Caption → Progressive Spatial Analysis → Reasoning) followed by a final answer in either multiple-choice or numerical format. Training requires only 5K auto-generated CoT samples for SFT and 20K samples from SpaceR-91k for RL, with no human annotation or geometric labels.

## Method

EgoMind is a geometry-free CoT framework built on two components: Role-Play Caption (RPC) simulates a first-person agent navigating the scene, generating per-frame object descriptions and linguistic viewpoint transitions (e.g., 'I move forward and turn right') to construct a coherent global scene graph without any 3D input; Progressive Spatial Analysis (PSA) then identifies explicitly mentioned objects, iteratively expands to their spatial neighborhoods to capture implicit anchor objects, and constructs a localized reasoning chain over the resulting subgraph. The full CoT is trained in two stages: SFT on 5K samples auto-generated by a GPT-4o + Qwen2.5-72B pipeline (no human annotation), followed by GRPO reinforcement learning on 20K samples with a composite reward balancing format adherence and answer accuracy. The base model is Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and the key insight is that structured linguistic reasoning over cross-frame transitions and implicit spatial bridges can substitute for explicit geometric supervision, achieving competitive performance with only 25K total training samples.

## Results

EgoMind (7B, 25K total training samples) achieves 50.16 on VSI-Bench Overall, 58.03 on SPAR-Bench, 55.02 on SITE-Bench, and 55.02 on SPBench, improving its Qwen2.5-VL-7B base from 30.02 to 50.16 on VSI-Bench (+20.14) and from 41.65 to 55.02 on SPBench (+13.37). It outperforms all open-source MLLMs without explicit 3D inputs and surpasses 3D-prior-based methods (e.g., Spatial-MLLM, SpaceVista) despite using only 2.5% of their training data. On VSI-Bench, EgoMind trails only closed-source GPT-5 (51.66) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (50.62) by a narrow margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoMind outperforms all open-source baselines without 3D inputs: it beats ViLaSR (46.35) by 3.81 on VSI-Bench, SpaceR (45.76) by 4.40, and R1-Zero-VSI (40.70) by 9.46, while using substantially less training data (25K vs. 75K–151K). Against 3D-prior-based methods, EgoMind surpasses Spatial-MLLM (48.40) by 1.76 on VSI-Bench and by 14.04 on SPAR-Bench (58.03 vs. 43.99), and exceeds SpaceVista (48.60) by 1.56 on VSI-Bench despite using only 2.5% of its 1M training samples. Among pure image-language MLLMs, EgoMind leads InternVL3.5 (44.17) by 5.99 on VSI-Bench and Ovis2.5 (40.93) by 9.23. The only models that exceed EgoMind on VSI-Bench are closed-source GPT-5 (51.66, +1.50) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (50.62, +0.46), indicating that the linguistic-reasoning approach closes most of the gap to proprietary systems while remaining fully open-source and geometry-free.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoMind carves a distinct niche between two dominant lines of spatial-reasoning work in MLLMs: (1) 3D-prior methods (GPT4Scene, Struct2D, See&Trek, Spatial-MLLM) that inject point clouds, depth, BEV, or camera poses, and (2) no-3D methods (SpaceR, R1-Zero-VSI, Spatial-Ladder) that still rely on large-scale geometrically annotated data or multi-stage training. Rather than adding a new modality or supervision signal, EgoMind repurposes the chain-of-thought paradigm—previously applied to general multimodal reasoning (LLaVA-CoT, Mulberry)—into a structured linguistic scene-graph construction (Role-Play Caption) combined with progressive object-neighborhood expansion (Progressive Spatial Analysis), all trained with a small SFT+GRPO recipe inherited from the DeepSeek-R1/GRPO lineage. Conceptually, it treats spatial reasoning as a problem of coherent narrative construction over frames rather than geometric estimation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical implication is that a 7B MLLM can reach near-closed-source spatial-reasoning scores (50.16 on VSI-Bench vs. 51.66 for GPT-5) using only 25K training samples—roughly 40× less data than 3D-prior baselines like SpaceVista (1M)—without any 3D annotation pipeline. This suggests that structured linguistic CoT may be a scalable, low-cost path to spatial cognition in embodied and AR/VR applications where 3D data is expensive or unavailable. Open questions remain: the authors acknowledge limitations in temporal reasoning, synthetic trace diversity, and lack of validation on larger models (>7B), and the single-base-model evaluation (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) leaves unclear whether the CoT formulation transfers across architectures. Whether the linguistic scene-graph approach can handle long-horizon, multi-room, or dynamic-scene reasoning beyond the current benchmarks is an important next step.
