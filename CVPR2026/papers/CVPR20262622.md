## Problem

Kinematics-based 3D human animation (e.g., SMPL mesh rigging) cannot model non-rigid dynamics such as clothing flow, while video-diffusion-based methods that can synthesize such motion suffer from identity loss and quality artifacts. The core technical obstacle is that coarse mesh-rigged renderings are out-of-distribution for pretrained video diffusion models, causing standard deterministic ODE samplers to follow incorrect trajectories and produce low-quality results.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262622/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single reference human image (reconstructed into a canonical 3D Gaussian Splatting via LHM) and a target SMPL parameter sequence of T frames as input. It produces a photorealistic 3D human animation renderable from arbitrary viewpoints at 832×480 resolution over 81 frames, capturing both rigid skeletal motion and non-rigid garment dynamics. The 3D representation is a 3DGS scene animated by a layered motion field, so the output is a fully animatable 4D asset rather than a fixed video.

## Method

ANI3DHUMAN decomposes human motion into a mesh-rigged rigid component (SMPL/SMPLX driving 3DGS via bijective point correspondence) and a residual non-rigid field (Hexplane + MLP predicting Gaussian parameter offsets). The key contribution is a self-guided stochastic sampling algorithm that restores coarse mesh-rigged renderings into high-fidelity videos: it injects noise into the OOD input, then uses a stochastic SDE discretization (re-noising the clean-noise prediction x̂₁|ₜ) to actively pull samples back onto the data manifold, combined with a DPS-inspired self-guidance step that applies an L2 gradient on the posterior mean x̂₀|ₜ within a SAM2-segmented preserved mask to maintain identity. These restored videos supervise progressive 4D optimization of the residual field, using diagonal view-time sampling (simultaneously evolving camera and time) to minimize inter-trajectory inconsistency, with a fine-tuned Wan2.1-1.3B video diffusion model conditioned on the reference image and 2D pose as the personalized prior.

## Results

On the ActorsHQ dataset (10 cases, single-view reconstruction), ANI3DHUMAN achieves the best PSNR (20.08), LPIPS (0.2125), CLIP-I (0.9160), FID (105.3), and FVD (295.2) among all compared methods, surpassing the closest baseline LHM by 0.57 dB PSNR and 18.8 FID points. In the user study on novel motion (no ground truth), the method leads in frame quality (35.3%), motion realism (35.3%), non-rigid physical plausibility (61.8%), and overall preference (54.4%), while matching LHM on identity preservation (40.1% vs. 39.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ANI3DHUMAN outperforms all four baselines on most metrics. Against LHM (the strongest baseline, which uses mesh-rigged motion only), it gains +0.57 dB PSNR, −0.0044 LPIPS, +0.0151 CLIP-I, −18.8 FID, and −44.7 FVD, while losing a marginal 0.007 SSIM. Against PERSONA (pose-driven video diffusion with direct reconstruction), the improvement is substantial: +3.07 dB PSNR, −0.0477 LPIPS, −93.8 FID, and −71.8 FVD, reflecting PERSONA's severe identity loss. Against SV4D 2.0 (multi-view video diffusion) and Disco4D (SDS-based), the gaps are even larger (e.g., +4.83 dB PSNR over SV4D 2.0, +8.03 dB over Disco4D). In the user study, ANI3DHUMAN dominates on non-rigid physical plausibility (61.8% vs. 14.7% for LHM and 19.1% for PERSONA), confirming its unique ability to capture realistic clothing dynamics that kinematics-only methods cannot model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of kinematics-based 3D human animation (SMPL/LBS, LHM) and video diffusion priors (Wan2.1, Rectified Flow), departing from both SDS-based distillation (Disco4D, 4D-Fy) and direct photometric reconstruction from generated videos (PERSONA, SV4D). Its key conceptual departure is using mesh-rigged kinematics as a structural and identity prior, then framing the restoration of the resulting coarse rendering as a conditional sampling problem rather than a generation-from-scratch task. The self-guided stochastic sampler draws on SDEdit's noise-injection paradigm, EDM's proof that SDEs correct OOD samples, and DPS's data-space gradient approximation, but combines them in a novel way for flow-matching video models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The self-guided stochastic sampling technique is potentially transferable beyond human animation to any setting where a flow-matching model must restore or edit an out-of-distribution input while preserving identity, making it a broadly useful tool. The clean ablation separating stochasticity (quality) from self-guidance (fidelity) provides a practical recipe for practitioners. Limitations include dependence on accurate SMPL parameters, a single reference image, and the iterative generation-optimization loop that limits real-time applicability; open questions include scaling to longer sequences, more complex multi-layer clothing, and whether the personalized fine-tuning of the video model can be replaced by in-context conditioning.
