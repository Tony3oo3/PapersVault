## Problem

Image captions increasingly serve as stand-ins for images in retrieval, recommendation, and agentic pipelines, yet current evaluation benchmarks (BLEU, CIDEr, VLM-as-judge) measure textual overlap or verbosity rather than whether a caption actually preserves the image-level information needed for downstream tasks. This creates a disconnect between how MLLMs are assessed and how their captions are deployed in practice.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263360/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The evaluation pipeline takes an RGB image, prompts a target MLLM to generate a caption under one of four prompt settings (Long, Simple, Short, Taxonomy-Hinted), then feeds that caption to a text-only QA LLM (Qwen2.5-72B) which answers 50.3 multiple-choice questions per image without seeing the image. Each question is tied to a specific taxonomy node, and an extra 'Cannot answer from the caption' option is appended to non-yes/no questions. The final output is a per-question score (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect, 1/K+0.05 for 'cannot answer') averaged across all 33,027 questions, reported as overall accuracy and cannot ratio per domain.

## Method

CaptionQA introduces a utility-centric evaluation protocol where caption quality is defined by downstream task support rather than descriptive completeness. For each of four domains (Natural, Document, E-commerce, Embodied AI), human experts design a two-level taxonomy (25 top-level, 69 subcategories) specifying which visual information is task-relevant. A multi-generator pipeline (GPT-5, GPT-4o, o4-mini) produces dense MCQs per image per taxonomy node, which are then filtered by a text-only blind test (removing questions answerable from world knowledge), deduplicated via embedding-space k-NN clustering, and validated by dual-VLM quality control with human refinement. The key scoring mechanism appends a 'Cannot answer' option so that captions are penalized for both hallucinated wrong answers and insufficient coverage, explicitly favoring precision over verbosity. The protocol is fully deterministic and domain-agnostic: extending to a new domain requires only writing a new taxonomy and running the same question-generation pipeline.

## Results

On the CaptionQA benchmark (Simple prompt, the recommended default), GPT-5 achieves the highest overall score at 90.29%, followed by Gemini 2.5 Flash (89.64%) and Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B (87.02%) as the best open-source model. The utility gap between QA-on-image and QA-on-caption is 9.2–16.4% for proprietary models and 11–32.4% for open-source models, with Embodied AI showing the largest domain-specific drops (up to 41.8% for LLaVA-OneVision-7B). E-commerce is the easiest domain (75–96% scores) while Embodied AI is the hardest (61–87%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among proprietary models, GPT-5 (90.29%) narrowly edges out Gemini 2.5 Flash (89.64%) on the Simple prompt, while Claude Sonnet 4.5 trails significantly at 78.95%, losing 11.3 points to GPT-5. Among open-source models, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B (87.02%) leads by a wide margin over GLM-4.1V-9B (84.28%) and Qwen2.5-VL-32B (81.20%), with LLaVA-OneVision-7B (66.03%) at the bottom. The most striking finding is that models nearly tied on QA-on-image diverge dramatically on caption utility: Claude Sonnet 4.5 and LLaVA-OneVision-7B differ by only 1.1% on image-based QA but by 17.2% on caption-based QA, and GPT-5 vs. LLaVA-OneVision-7B differ by ~9% on image QA but 32.3% on caption QA. In the Embodied AI domain specifically, even the best open-source model (Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B) loses 16.96% switching from image to caption, while LLaVA-OneVision-7B loses over 40%, and proprietary models also struggle (GPT-5: 13.81%, Claude Sonnet 4.5: 29.06% gap). E-commerce shows the smallest gaps across all models (4.87–24.97%), indicating product-centric information transfers most easily to text.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the three dominant paradigms for caption evaluation—n-gram overlap metrics (BLEU, CIDEr), fact-based parsers (SPICE, CHAIR), and the VLM-as-Judge paradigm (CapArena, CAPability)—by reframing the question from 'is the caption accurate or detailed?' to 'can the caption replace the image for a downstream task?' It repurposes multiple-choice QA as a deterministic evaluation mechanism (a technique with precedent in text-to-visual generation evaluation), but applies it in a fundamentally different way: a text-only LLM answers questions using only the caption, directly measuring information preservation rather than perceptual accuracy. The taxonomy-driven, domain-specific construction pipeline is novel in its combination of structured utility dimensions with automated question generation and multi-stage quality filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a substantial and previously unquantified blind spot: models that appear nearly identical on standard image-QA benchmarks (e.g., Claude Sonnet 4.5 vs. LLaVA-OneVision-7B, ~1% apart) diverge by up to 32% in caption utility, meaning current evaluation practices systematically overestimate caption readiness for deployment. The counterintuitive finding that Taxonomy-Hinted prompts degrade performance in 23 of 25 categories challenges the assumption that more explicit instruction improves captioning, pointing to a tension between instruction-following and visual grounding that the field has not addressed. Open questions include whether the gap can be closed through targeted training, whether domain-specific caption strategies (verbose for Document/E-commerce vs. richer visual understanding for Embodied AI) can be learned, and whether the 657-image scale is sufficient for robust cross-domain conclusions.
