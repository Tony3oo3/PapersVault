## Problem

Existing incomplete multi-view clustering methods focus on reconstructing or imputing missing data but implicitly assume all views contribute equally during feature fusion. In practice, heterogeneous missing rates create structurally imbalanced view contributions where 'strong views' dominate the fusion process while 'weak views' are underutilized, distorting cross-view representations and degrading clustering. No prior work provides a unified framework to jointly quantify this missing-induced contribution imbalance and adaptively mitigate it during optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes V incomplete multi-view feature matrices X^v ∈ R^{N×d_v} (with heterogeneous view-specific missing rates r_v = α_v·r under a global average rate r) and a binary visibility indicator matrix A ∈ {0,1}^{N×V}, with the constraint that every sample is observable in at least one view. It produces per-view soft cluster assignment matrices Q^v ∈ R^{N×K}, a fused consensus distribution, hard cluster labels via argmax, per-view normalized Shapley contribution scores ψ^v, and a scalar Gini-based contribution imbalance index I_ψ that quantifies cross-view collaboration disparity.

## Method

ICER equips each view with an autoencoder projecting features into a shared latent space, followed by DEC-style soft clustering with view-specific learnable prototypes. The core novelty is a two-stage evaluation-and-refinement loop: (1) View Contribution Evaluation models incomplete multi-view clustering as a cooperative game where each view is a player, computes coalition fused distributions q^(S) via weighted aggregation over visible views, measures each coalition's utility as E(S)=exp(−UOT(p, q^(S))) using Unbalanced Optimal Transport with KL-relaxed marginals to handle mass non-conservation from missingness, and derives per-view Shapley values φ^v as the fair marginal contribution; (2) Contribution-Balanced Enhancement uses a Gini-based imbalance metric I_ψ to drive a View-Adaptive Curriculum Learning (VACL) schedule where the per-view gradient weight γ_v(t)=(t/T_c)[(1−ψ^v)²+ε] progressively amplifies weak views over a curriculum period, transitioning from strong-view-dominated to balanced multi-view optimization. The total objective combines reconstruction loss, a UOT-based cross-view consistency loss, and a KL-divergence clustering loss, all modulated by the VACL weights during training.

## Results

ICER achieves the best ACC, NMI, and ARI across all five datasets (Synthetic3d, Reuters, Caltech101, Wikipedia, Animal) at every missing rate from 0.1 to 0.5. At the moderate missing rate r=0.3, ICER reaches ACC 0.9133 / NMI 0.7182 / ARI 0.7616 on Synthetic3d, ACC 0.5109 / NMI 0.4377 / ARI 0.3548 on Caltech101, and ACC 0.5471 / NMI 0.6373 / ARI 0.4088 on Animal, consistently outperforming the strongest baselines. Even at the extreme r=0.5, ICER retains the top position on every dataset, e.g. ACC 0.4546 on Wikipedia and ACC 0.4628 on Animal, demonstrating robustness under severe incompleteness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ICER outperforms all seven baselines (CPSPAN, RPCIC, GHICMC, PMIMC, FREECSL, NBIMVC, BURG) on every dataset and missing rate tested. The closest competitor varies by dataset: on Synthetic3d, GHICMC (ACC 0.8525) and PMIMC (ACC 0.8417) are nearest at r=0.3, yet ICER still leads by 6–7 ACC points; on Caltech101, NBIMVC (ACC 0.4491) and BURG (ACC 0.3946) trail ICER (0.5109) by 6–12 points; on Animal, GHICMC (ACC 0.5153) and BURG (ACC 0.4385) are the strongest rivals, with ICER ahead by 3–11 ACC points. On the harder Reuters and Wikipedia datasets, the gap widens substantially—ICER's ACC of 0.4325 on Reuters at r=0.3 exceeds the next-best RPCIC (0.4008) by over 3 points, while on Wikipedia ICER (0.4972) surpasses BURG (0.4934) and NBIMVC (0.4470) by 0.4 and 5 points respectively. Notably, several baselines (e.g., BURG on Reuters, NBIMVC on Animal) collapse to near-zero ARI at higher missing rates, whereas ICER degrades gracefully, confirming that explicit view-imbalance modeling provides a structural advantage under severe incompleteness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the incomplete multi-view clustering (IMVC) literature by identifying and explicitly modeling a previously overlooked phenomenon: non-uniform missingness induces view contribution imbalance during feature fusion. It builds on the DEC soft-clustering mechanism (extended to the incomplete setting) and repurposes unbalanced optimal transport (from Vo et al., ICML 2024) as a distributional discrepancy measure between coalition-specific fused distributions and an ideal global target. The Shapley-value-based contribution attribution draws directly on Wang et al.'s (NeurIPS 2024) game-theoretic view cooperation framework, but the key departure is coupling Shapley values with UOT to define a principled imbalance metric and feeding it into a view-adaptive curriculum learning schedule—a combination absent from prior IMVC or imbalanced multi-view learning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic formalization of how missingness patterns create structural bias in cross-view collaboration, offering both a diagnostic metric (I_ψ) and a corrective mechanism (VACL) that practitioners can adopt in any multi-view pipeline with heterogeneous data availability. A notable limitation is that exact Shapley computation is exponential in the number of views, making the approach practical only for small V (≤6 in experiments); scalability to dozens of views would require Monte Carlo or sampling approximations. Open questions include whether the imbalance metric generalizes beyond clustering to other multi-view tasks (classification, retrieval), and whether the curriculum schedule's hyperparameters (T_c, ε) transfer across domains without re-tuning.
