## Problem

Decoupled dataset distillation with residual matching relies on static real patches as anchors, which induces two coupled failure modes: a fit-complexity gap where fixed patches either misalign with evolving synthetic features or inject excessive hypothesis complexity, and a pull-to-anchor effect where repeated anchoring to teacher-nearest samples contracts intra-class feature distances and causes premature cluster merging. The paper asks whether an adaptive residual-matching mechanism can simultaneously balance feature fit against complexity while preserving class-level topological diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261510/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a large real dataset (CIFAR-100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet-1K at 224×224) and a frozen teacher encoder φ(·), producing a compact synthetic set with IPC ∈ {1, 10, 50} images per class at the source resolution. Per-class retrieval pools are prebuilt as 1×1-grid (full-image) real patches; at each residual step the current synthetic image is resampled to the evolving resolution D_t before the selected patch is injected. The frozen teacher remains in the computation graph so that topology-loss gradients flow back to synthetic pixels through ∂φ/∂x, while a feature cache refreshed every T steps controls overhead.

## Method

RETA comprises two jointly optimized modules. Dynamic Retrieval Connection (DRC) replaces fixed anchors with per-stage, per-class retrieval: a fit-complexity score J(o|x̃_t) = (1−λ)‖q(x̃_t)−z(o)‖² + λ·c(o) selects the real patch that minimizes teacher-space distance to the current synthetic query while penalizing gradient-variance complexity c(o) (variance of smoothed gradient magnitudes), then injects the winner via a residual connection x̃_t ← αx̃_t + (1−α)Resample(o*, D_t). Persistent Topology Alignment (PTA) constructs a class-balanced mutual k-NN graph over the union of real and synthetic teacher features, induces a Vietoris-Rips-like filtration, computes persistence images for connected components (q=0) and loops (q=1) on a 32×32 grid, and adds an L2 penalty between real and synthetic PIs weighted by λ_topo. A Rademacher-complexity theorem (Thm. 4.1) formalizes why both the fit gap Δ and the complexity gap must be controlled simultaneously, and a first-order linearization (Eq. 8) shows how repeated residual updates contract intra-class distances, motivating the topological regularizer.

## Results

RETA achieves state-of-the-art top-1 accuracy across all five benchmarks and three ResNet backbones. The headline result is 64.3% on ImageNet-1K (ResNet-18, IPC=50), surpassing the strongest prior method FADRM+ (61.2%) by 3.1 points. At the more common IPC=10 setting with ResNet-18, RETA gains +2.4 to +3.5 points over FADRM+ across CIFAR-100 (70.3%), Tiny-ImageNet (56.2%), ImageNette (72.5%), ImageWoof (60.3%), and ImageNet-1K (53.2%). Gains persist with deeper backbones (ResNet-50/101) and in the extreme data-scarce regime (IPC=1), where RETA still leads by 1.2–3.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RETA outperforms all 11 compared baselines on every dataset and IPC setting where numbers are available. The primary baseline FADRM+ [6] (NeurIPS 2025), which uses static residual matching, is consistently beaten by 1.5–3.5 points; the gap is largest on Tiny-ImageNet (+3.4) and ImageNette (+3.5) at IPC=10 with ResNet-18. Among other decoupled methods, EDC [35] and NRR-DD [39] are the closest competitors on CIFAR-100 (63.7% and 62.7% vs. RETA's 70.3%), while CaO2 [40] and WMDD [26] trail by 5–10 points on ImageNette/ImageWoof. On corruption robustness (IPC=1, 15 corruption types × 5 severities), RETA leads FADRM+ by ~3.2 points on average across six ImageNet subsets, and also surpasses trajectory-matching MTT [3] and parameterization-based IDC [17] by 3–10 points. In the cross-architecture study (Table 2, ImageNet-1K IPC=10), RETA beats FADRM+ on all eight student architectures (CNNs and Swin-Tiny ViT), with the largest margin of +3.4 points on ShuffleNetV2-0.5×. On computational efficiency (Fig. 5), RETA adds only ~0.2 s and 2.4 GB over FADRM+ while remaining substantially faster and lighter than EDC (4.99 s, 17.9 GB) and CV-DD [7] (8.20 s, 23.4 GB).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the decoupled dataset distillation lineage, building directly on FADRM's residual-matching mechanism that periodically injects real patches to counter information vanishing. It departs from FADRM's static, preselected anchors in two ways: (i) Dynamic Retrieval Connection replaces fixed patches with per-stage, per-class retrieval scored by a fit-complexity objective grounded in Rademacher complexity bounds, and (ii) Persistent Topology Alignment introduces a persistent-homology regularizer (Vietoris-Rips filtration → persistence images) that has not previously been applied to dataset distillation. The broader decoupled-optimization framework (SRe²L, EDC, CV-DD) provides the architectural scaffold, while the topological-data-analysis toolkit (Betti curves, mutual k-NN graphs, PI grids) is repurposed from computational topology into a differentiable training signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RETA's 64.3% top-1 on ImageNet-1K at 50 IPC (+3.1% over FADRM+) and consistent gains across 15+ dataset/architecture/corruption configurations make it a strong practical option for large-scale distillation. The conceptual framing of the pull-to-anchor effect—showing via first-order linearization that repeated residual mixing contracts intra-class distances—is a useful diagnostic that could inform other anchor-based or retrieval-augmented synthesis methods. Open questions include whether the hand-crafted gradient-variance complexity proxy in DRC can be replaced by learned or data-driven measures, whether PTA's overhead (k-NN graph construction, PI computation) remains tractable at IPC=100+ or video/multimodal settings, and whether the frozen-teacher assumption limits transfer to domains where no strong pretrained encoder exists.
