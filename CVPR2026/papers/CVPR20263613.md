## Problem

Existing audio-visual navigation methods rely on precomputed room impulse responses, confining agents to discrete grid positions with spatially discontinuous observations, and assume the goal emits sound continuously throughout an episode. In realistic settings, the goal sound is intermittent or entirely absent, causing agents to lose both spatial and semantic goal information. This paper introduces SAVN-CE, a continuous-environment benchmark where the goal is semantically grounded, emits sound only for a limited window, and may be accompanied by distractor sounds.

## Input / Output

At each 0.25 s step the agent receives 128×128 RGB-D images (90° FOV), 4,000-sample binaural audio waveforms (16 kHz), the previous discrete action, and its pose (x, y, θ, t) relative to the initial state. The audio is converted to a complex spectrogram via 512-point STFT (hop 160) and decomposed into four channels: mean magnitude, sine/cosine interaural phase difference, and interaural level difference. The network outputs a discrete navigation action (MoveForward 0.25 m, TurnLeft/Right 15°, Stop) and, internally, an ACCDDOA goal descriptor comprising a binary activity flag, a unit-norm Cartesian DOA vector, and a normalized distance, which is used for supervised training of the goal descriptor network.

## Method

MAGNet comprises three modules: a multimodal observation encoder (dual ResNet-18 for RGB-D, CNN for audio, MLPs for action/pose) that maintains a scene memory of the last 150 steps; a Memory-Augmented Goal Descriptor Network (GDN) that fuses binaural audio, self-motion cues (previous action and current pose), and an episodic memory of 128 past goal-relevant embeddings through a causal-attention transformer, producing both a goal embedding for the policy and ACCDDOA-formatted goal descriptions for supervised loss; and a context-aware transformer encoder-decoder policy that attends over the scene memory to predict the next action. The key insight is that self-motion cues explicitly encode how the agent's movement shifts the goal's relative azimuth and distance, while episodic memory preserves temporal continuity of goal estimates across silent gaps. The GDN is trained online with MSE on oracle ACCDDOA labels, and the policy is trained with DD-PPO using a reward combining success (+10), geodesic-distance progress, and a per-step time penalty.

## Results

On the SAVN-CE test set (1,000 episodes), MAGNet achieves 37.7% success rate (SR) and 32.9% SPL under Clean Environments, outperforming the strongest baseline SAVi (25.6% SR, 21.2% SPL) by 12.1 and 11.7 percentage points respectively. Under Distracted Environments, MAGNet reaches 19.3% SR and 16.5% SPL, still leading all baselines but with a smaller margin. The oracle upper bound (Oracle2, with persistent goal labels) reaches 75.0% SR, indicating substantial room for improvement in goal tracking after sound cessation. MAGNet also achieves the best SWS (10.6%) and lowest DTG (8.0 m) in Clean settings, confirming superior silent-period navigation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGNet decisively outperforms all four baselines across every navigation metric in both Clean and Distracted settings. Against the closest competitor SAVi (Chen et al., CVPR 2021), MAGNet gains +12.1% SR and +11.7% SPL in Clean Environments, with the advantage driven primarily by superior silent-period tracking (SWS: 10.6% vs. 6.0%) and shorter final distance to goal (DTG: 8.0 m vs. 10.1 m). Compared to SMT+Audio (Fang et al., CVPR 2019), the improvement is +12.9% SR, showing that explicit goal inference via the memory-augmented GDN is critical beyond generic scene memory. AV-Nav (Chen et al., ECCV 2020), which uses a simple GRU for temporal encoding, trails MAGNet by 16.4% SR, highlighting the limitations of non-memory architectures for long-horizon goal tracking. The Random baseline (Krantz et al., ECCV 2020) confirms the task's difficulty with near-zero success. Notably, under Distracted Environments MAGNet's SR advantage over SAVi narrows to 0.8 points and it records the highest DSR (7.8%), indicating vulnerability to acoustically similar distractors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semantic audio-visual navigation (SAVN) line initiated by SAVi (Chen et al., CVPR 2021) by removing three key simplifying assumptions: discrete grid positions, precomputed room impulse responses, and continuous goal sound emission. The memory-augmented goal descriptor network draws on the episodic transformer architecture from VLN-CE (Pashevich et al., ICCV 2021) and the ACCDDOA representation from SELD literature, while the continuous-environment formulation follows the VLN-CE paradigm (Krantz et al., ECCV 2020) of fine-grained low-level actions in 3D space. The simulator leverages SoundSpaces 2.0's dynamic binaural rendering to avoid the terabyte-scale RIR storage that constrained all prior audio-visual navigation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SAVN-CE benchmark is practically important because it removes the artificial constraints (discrete grids, always-on sound) that made prior audio-visual navigation tasks unrealistic, and the 0.5M-episode dataset with unseen scenes and sounds provides a meaningful generalization test. However, the absolute success rate of 37.7% versus Oracle2's 75.0% reveals that maintaining goal awareness after sound cessation remains a major unsolved bottleneck, and the highest DSR among all methods in distracted settings suggests the memory mechanism can amplify susceptibility to acoustically similar distractors. Open questions include scaling to multiple or moving goals, reducing the large gap to oracle performance, and transferring to real-world acoustic environments where reverberation and noise are far more complex.
