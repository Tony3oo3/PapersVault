## Problem

Under-display camera images suffer from spatially varying degradations (diffraction, scattering, reduced transmittance) that differ across panel types and even within a single image between lens-center and boundary regions. Existing PSF-based and frequency-separation methods reconstruct low-frequency structures but fail to recover fine high-frequency details in regions with non-uniform degradation, because they apply uniform restoration without modeling the local uncertainty of the degradation pattern.

## Input / Output

The network takes a single degraded UDC RGB image (1024×2048 for POLED/TOLED, 800×800 for SYNTH) and directly produces a restored RGB image without any handcrafted pre-processing (unlike BNUDC or FSI). During training, Gaussian noise (σ=10⁻³) is added to inputs for robustness. Each decoding block also outputs a per-pixel uncertainty map, and the final restored image is accompanied by multi-scale uncertainty maps that highlight regions of high reconstruction ambiguity.

## Method

UCMNet is a U-shaped encoder-decoder (3.2M params) where each block contains a Frequency Convolution Module (FCM) for Fourier-domain feature enhancement, and each decoding block additionally contains an Uncertainty-Prior Transformer (UPT). The UPT first predicts a pixel-wise uncertainty map via a variance estimator, then uses cosine similarity between the uncertainty features and a learnable Memory Bank to retrieve matching high-frequency context tokens from a paired Context Bank. The retrieved context is fused with the input feature through parallel vertical and horizontal cross-attention (directional cross-attention), followed by a vanilla transformer for global channel-wise self-attention. Training uses a novel High-Frequency Uncertainty-Driven Loss (HF-UDL) that applies a Laplacian operator to the standard UDL, forcing the variance estimators to capture uncertainty specifically in high-frequency regions; the total loss combines HF-UDL (λ₁=100) with a PSNR loss (λ₂=0.5) applied at every decoding stage.

## Results

UCMNet achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmarks with only 3.2M parameters (30% fewer than prior methods). On the POLED test set, it reaches 33.81 dB PSNR and 0.9625 SSIM, outperforming the closest competitor BNUDC (33.39 dB, 0.9610) by +0.42 dB. On TOLED, it attains 38.37 dB PSNR and 0.9802 SSIM, and on the SYNTH dataset it achieves 46.71 dB PSNR and 0.9942 SSIM, surpassing FSI (45.69 dB) by over 1 dB. UCMNet also leads on perceptual metrics (LPIPS 0.1718 on POLED, 0.0933 on TOLED) and texture similarity (DISTS 0.1440 on POLED, 0.0897 on TOLED).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UCMNet outperforms all seven baselines on the POLED test set across every metric, with the largest margin over MSUNet (+4.64 dB PSNR) and UDC-UNet (+7.56 dB PSNR), and a more competitive +0.42 dB PSNR gain over the strongest competitor BNUDC. On TOLED, UCMNet leads BNUDC by +0.15 dB PSNR and FSI by +0.16 dB, while also achieving the best LPIPS (0.0933 vs. 0.0988 for BNUDC) and DISTS (0.0897 vs. 0.0964). On the SYNTH dataset, UCMNet surpasses FSI by +1.02 dB PSNR and BNUDC by +1.15 dB, while matching BNUDC's LPIPS (0.0110) and improving DISTS (0.0150 vs. 0.0155). Notably, UCMNet achieves these gains with only 3.2M parameters, compared to 4.6–8.9M for all other methods, and 151.13 G FLOPs versus up to 496.70 G for BNUDC. The closest overall competitor is BNUDC, which uses a two-branch frequency-separation design but lacks the spatially adaptive uncertainty mechanism that gives UCMNet its edge in handling non-uniform degradations.

## Relation to Prior Work

UCMNet extends the uncertainty-driven loss (UDL) framework from Ning et al. (NeurIPS 2021) by applying a Laplacian operator to the loss, targeting high-frequency degradation specific to under-display camera imaging, and adopts the multi-stage loss application strategy from Chen et al. (ICCV 2023). Architecturally, it departs from prior UDC restoration methods that rely on PSF-based physical modeling or frequency-separation networks by introducing an Uncertainty-Prior Transformer that uses learned Memory and Context Banks to perform spatially adaptive, uncertainty-guided feature retrieval. The Frequency Convolution Module draws from NAFNet-style Fourier-domain feature enhancement, while the overall U-shaped encoder-decoder backbone follows standard restoration architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UCDC image restoration is a practically important problem for full-screen mobile device design, and the spatially varying nature of display-induced degradation (diffraction, scattering, low transmittance) makes uniform restoration strategies fundamentally limited. UCMNet's uncertainty-guided memory retrieval offers a principled mechanism for handling this non-uniformity, and achieving SOTA with 30% fewer parameters than prior methods suggests the approach is both effective and deployable. Open questions include whether the learned uncertainty patterns generalize to unseen panel types or manufacturing variations, and whether the memory-bank retrieval mechanism scales to more complex multi-degradation scenarios beyond UDC imaging.
