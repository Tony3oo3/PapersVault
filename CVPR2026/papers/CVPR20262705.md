## Problem

Standard coordinate denoising for molecular representation learning implicitly assumes molecules reside at equilibrium (zero inherent forces), making the denoising-to-force equivalence valid. For non-equilibrium structures—ubiquitous in MD trajectories—this equivalence breaks down because the molecule's inherent non-zero forces confound the denoising objective, yielding suboptimal representations. The paper derives a theoretically grounded denoising objective that remains valid for arbitrary molecular conformations.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D Cartesian coordinates and atomic numbers for N-atom molecules, producing a scalar energy prediction and per-atom 3D force vectors as primary outputs. For the auxiliary NDeM task, a subset of atoms is randomly masked and perturbed with Gaussian noise (σ sampled per batch from [σ_min, σ_max]), and a separate equivariant head outputs a 'noisy force' vector of the same shape as the main force head. The auxiliary loss enforces finite-difference consistency between the main and noisy force predictions rather than direct noise reconstruction.

## Method

NDeM derives its objective from a second-order Taylor expansion of the potential energy surface, yielding the relation F(X̃) − F(X) ≈ −cε, where c is a learnable scalar approximating local PES curvature (Hessian ≈ cI). This explicitly decouples the molecule's inherent force F(X) from the perturbation-induced force change, making the denoising target valid for both equilibrium (F(X)≈0, recovering classical denoising) and non-equilibrium states. Implementation uses a separate equivariant output head sharing the backbone with the main force head, with multi-scale masked noise injection to balance Taylor-expansion validity against exploration. The total training objective is L_total = L_orig + λ_NDeM · L_NDeM, where L_NDeM penalizes the deviation of (f̂(X) − f̂′(X̃)) from the normalized noise ε*, enabling joint optimization of absolute forces and local force gradients without pre-training.

## Results

On the MD17 non-equilibrium force prediction benchmark, NDeM achieves the best MAE on 5 out of 8 molecules (e.g., Aspirin 0.1654, Naphthalene 0.0480, Toluene 0.0536), outperforming the pre-trained Frad (0.2087 on Aspirin) and SliDe (0.1740 on Aspirin) baselines. On the large-scale OC20 S2EF task, EquiformerV2+NDeM (157M params) achieves a test force MAE of 12.9 meV/Å, surpassing the base EquiformerV2 (13.8 meV/Å) and approaching the DeNS pre-training result (13.4 meV/Å) without any pre-training stage. On QM9 equilibrium properties, NDeM matches or exceeds Frad and SliDe on most of the 12 DFT properties (e.g., dipole moment 0.0082 D, HOMO 13.6 meV, ZPVE 1.210 meV), confirming generalizability to equilibrium data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two strongest pre-trained denoising baselines (Frad and SliDe), NDeM wins on 5 of 8 MD17 molecules without any pre-training, with the largest margin on Aspirin (0.1654 vs. 0.2087 for Frad, a 20.7% relative improvement). NDeM trails SliDe on Benzene (0.1802 vs. 0.1691) and Frad on Malonaldehyde (0.1439 vs. 0.1415) and Uracil (0.0806 vs. 0.0760), indicating that pre-training still provides a slight edge on a few molecules. On OC20, EquiformerV2+NDeM (λ_E=2) reduces test force MAE from 13.8 to 12.9 meV/Å over the base model, outperforming the base EquiformerV2 but slightly trailing the DeNS pre-training variant (13.4 meV/Å) while using fewer parameters (157M vs. 160M) and no pre-training. On QM9, NDeM achieves the best or tied-best MAE on 8 of 12 properties (including dipole moment, polarizability, HOMO/LUMO, gap, ZPVE, and enthalpy), while SliDe retains an advantage on internal energy (4.28 vs. 5.31 meV) and heat capacity-related properties. Across all three datasets, NDeM consistently outperforms classical supervised baselines (SchNet, DimeNet, PaiNN, TorchMD-NET, SE(3)-DDM) by large margins, confirming that the auxiliary denoising task provides substantial gains even without dedicated pre-training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the coordinate denoising / score-matching paradigm for molecular force learning (established by Frad, SliDe, SE(3)-DDM, and Zaidi et al.) by identifying and fixing a theoretical gap: the denoising-to-force equivalence implicitly assumes zero inherent forces, which fails for non-equilibrium MD trajectories. It builds most directly on DeNS (Liao et al., 2024), which also targets non-equilibrium denoising, but departs by formulating the correction as a second-order finite-difference constraint on the PES rather than a modified noise schedule, and by casting it as a lightweight auxiliary loss rather than a pre-training stage. The method is architecture-agnostic, plugging into any equivariant GNN backbone (TorchMD-NET, EquiformerV2) without structural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Non-equilibrium structures dominate realistic MD simulations of reactions, catalysis, and protein folding, yet standard denoising objectives have been applied to them without theoretical justification; NDeM closes this gap with a physically grounded correction that requires no pre-training. The key limitation is the isotropic Hessian approximation (H ≈ cI), which may be insufficient for anisotropic PES curvature in complex systems, and the paper acknowledges higher-order approximations as future work. Open questions include whether the scalar-curvature assumption holds for large, multi-site catalytic surfaces and whether the auxiliary loss interacts favorably with longer training schedules or larger model scales beyond the 157M-parameter EquiformerV2 tested here.
