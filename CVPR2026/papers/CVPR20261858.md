## Problem

Existing SAR representation learning is constrained by three interrelated gaps: the absence of million-scale SAR pretraining datasets, the neglect of SAR's physically grounded multiplicative speckle noise in self-supervised objectives, and the limited semantic richness of SAR-only pretraining that overlooks complementary optical priors. These limitations collectively cap the generalizability and robustness of learned SAR representations across downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261858/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the method consumes SAR images (expanded to 3-channel format for channel consistency across sensors) and, when available, geographically co-registered optical counterparts. The SAR input is first synthetically corrupted with Gamma-distributed speckle noise (reduced look number), then randomly masked at 75% ratio before being fed to the encoder. The pretrained ViT-B or ViT-L encoder outputs patch-level feature representations that are subsequently fine-tuned with a linear classifier (classification), Faster R-CNN / Oriented R-CNN (detection), or UperNet (segmentation) to produce class labels, bounding boxes, or pixel-wise masks.

## Method

SARMAE builds on the MAE architecture with a ViT encoder and Transformer decoder, introducing two key mechanisms. SARE reformulates the reconstruction target: instead of reconstructing the original noisy patch, the model synthesizes a more heavily corrupted observation by sampling from a Gamma distribution with a reduced synthetic look number, then learns to denoise it back to the original patch, thereby learning noise-disentangled representations. SARC introduces a frozen DINOv3 optical branch that produces semantic anchor embeddings from paired optical images; a patchwise cosine distance loss aligns visible SAR patch features with their spatially corresponding optical features, injecting high-level semantic structure into the SAR encoder. The overall pretraining loss combines the SARE denoising MSE and the SARC alignment term (weighted by λ=0.1), and the model is trained for 300 epochs on the newly constructed SAR-1M dataset (1.3M SAR + 1M paired optical images spanning 57 categories, multiple sensors, and frequency bands).

## Results

On target classification, SARMAE (ViT-B) achieves 89.30% top-1 accuracy on FUSAR-SHIP (40-shot), 96.70% on MSTAR (40-shot), and 95.06% on SAR-ACD (30% labels), setting new state-of-the-art across all three benchmarks. For object detection, SARMAE (ViT-L) reaches 63.10 mAP on SARDet-100k, 69.30 mAP on SSDD, and 72.20 mAP on RSAR (oriented detection), surpassing the previous best by 5.8 and 7.4 mAP respectively. On semantic segmentation (AIR-PolSAR-Seg), SARMAE (ViT-B) attains 66.53 mIoU for multi-class and 92.31 IoU for water extraction, outperforming the strongest baseline (DANet) by 26.97 mIoU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SARMAE consistently outperforms all SAR-specific baselines across every evaluation setting. On FUSAR-SHIP (30% labels), SARMAE (ViT-B) achieves 92.92%, surpassing SUMMIT (71.91%) by 21.01 points and BEiT (71.13%) by 21.79 points; on MSTAR (40-shot), it beats SAR-JEPA (91.60%) by 5.10 points. For detection, SARMAE (ViT-L) leads SARDet-100k (63.10 vs. SARATR-X 57.30 mAP) and RSAR oriented detection (72.20 vs. O-RCNN 64.82 mAP), with the ViT-B variant already matching or exceeding SARATR-X on SARDet-100k (57.90 vs. 57.30) despite using a simpler backbone. On segmentation, SARMAE (ViT-B) achieves 66.53 mIoU, exceeding the best prior method DANet (39.56 mIoU) by a margin of 26.97 points, and 92.31 IoU on water extraction versus DANet's 89.29. The only setting where SARMAE does not hold the top spot is MSTAR 30% labels, where ViT-B (99.61%) slightly edges ViT-L (98.92%), though both still surpass SUMMIT (98.39%). General-purpose models (Copernicus FM, CROMA) are outperformed on all shared benchmarks, confirming the advantage of SAR-specific pretraining with noise-aware and cross-modal objectives.

## Relation to Prior Work

SARMAE extends the MAE-based self-supervised pretraining paradigm to SAR imagery, building directly on the ViT-encoder/Transformer-decoder architecture of He et al. (CVPR 2022). It departs from prior SAR-specific pretraining efforts (SARATR-X, SUMMIT, SAR-JEPA) in two key ways: (1) it explicitly models the multiplicative Gamma-distributed speckle noise inherent to SAR imaging rather than treating it as an unmodeled nuisance, and (2) it introduces a frozen DINOv3 optical branch to provide cross-modal semantic anchoring, a strategy absent from all prior SAR-only pretraining frameworks. The SAR-1M dataset (1.3M SAR + 1M paired optical) also represents a substantial scale increase over existing SAR pretraining corpora (180k for SARATR-X, 560k for SUMMIT).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAR-1M is likely to become the standard large-scale pretraining resource for SAR representation learning, given its scale, sensor diversity, and the inclusion of paired optical imagery that no prior SAR dataset offers. The physics-informed noise injection (SARE) provides a clean, interpretable mechanism for noise-robust feature learning that could transfer to other coherent-imaging modalities. A key open question is how much of the performance gain is attributable to data scale versus the SARE/SARC mechanisms, since the ablation compares against a much smaller pretraining corpus; additionally, the SARC component requires geographically co-registered optical pairs, which limits applicability in regions or scenarios where such pairs are unavailable.
