## Problem

One-step flow matching methods like Mean Flow can produce locally expansive transport maps that amplify perturbations, leading to ambiguous trajectories, exploding Jacobian-vector products (NaN/Inf), and degraded sample quality. No existing one-step formulation enforces a principled constraint on the local geometry of the learned map to prevent these pathologies.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263791/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single Gaussian noise vector ε ~ N(0, I_d) and produces a generated image in one network evaluation (NFE = 1) via z_0 ≈ ε − u_θ(ε, 0, 1). During training, the network u_θ receives a triple (z_t, r, t) where z_t is an interpolated state and (r, t) indexes the averaging window; the stability term additionally requires a second forward pass at the perturbed state z_t + δξ. Experiments use 32×32 CIFAR-10 and 256×256 ImageNet.

## Method

Stable Mean Flow augments the Mean Flow average-velocity regression loss with a Lyapunov-inspired non-expansivity (NE) regularizer that penalizes local stretching of the one-step update map φ_r^θ(t, z) = z − (t−r)u_θ(z, r, t). Concretely, a random perturbation Δz of fixed radius δ is drawn, and a hinge-squared penalty ℓ_stab = [max{0, ‖Δz − (t−r)Δu‖ − ‖Δz‖}]² is added with weight μ, vanishing whenever the map is locally non-expansive. Theoretically, NE guarantees uniqueness of ODE characteristics and yields an explicit constant bound on the JVP magnitude, eliminating the NaN/Inf failure mode that plagues vanilla Mean Flow. The stability weight μ is scheduled to decay over training so the regularizer shapes early dynamics without biasing the final solution, and the perturbation radius δ is kept small relative to the typical step scale to preserve a reliable stochastic estimate.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with single-step inference (NFE=1), Stable Mean Flow achieves a FID of 2.86, improving over Mean Flow's 2.92 and substantially outperforming all other one-step methods (DenseFlow: 34.9, GLFlow: 44.6, Glow: 48.9, 1-Rectified Flow: 378). It also surpasses two-step methods (Consistency Flow Matching: 5.34, Consistency Model: 5.83) while requiring fewer function evaluations. On ImageNet, Stable MeanFlow-XL/2 reaches FID 3.37 by Epoch 240, slightly below MeanFlow-XL/2's reported 3.43, with noticeably faster early-training convergence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stable Mean Flow (FID 2.86, NFE=1) narrowly beats its direct predecessor Mean Flow (FID 2.92, NFE=1) on CIFAR-10, confirming that the Lyapunov-inspired non-expansivity regularizer yields a small but consistent quality gain without extra inference cost. Against all other one-step baselines the margin is large: DenseFlow (34.9), GLFlow (44.6), Residual Flow (46.4), Glow (48.9), and 1-Rectified Flow (378) are all far behind. SMFM also outperforms the two-step methods Consistency Flow Matching (5.34) and Consistency Model (5.83) while using half the function evaluations. On ImageNet, Stable MeanFlow-XL/2 achieves FID 3.37 versus MeanFlow-XL/2's 3.43, a modest improvement that the authors note is not substantial, though early-training convergence is visibly faster. The hyperparameter ablation (Table 1) shows the stability term is sensitive: the best setting (Δz=0.01, μ=0.1) gives FID 79.86 in a reduced-iteration sweep, while larger values of either parameter rapidly degrade quality, indicating a narrow effective regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Mean Flow Matching (Geng et al., NeurIPS 2025), which introduced an analytically defined average-velocity target enabling true one-step generation from scratch. It departs from the broader flow-matching lineage (Rectified Flow, Consistency Flow Matching, Optimal Flow Matching) by repurposing Lyapunov stability theory from dynamical systems as a training regularizer: a hinge-style non-expansivity penalty on the single-step transport map that was absent from all prior flow-matching objectives. The conceptual framing—Mean Flow as 'GPS' and the stability term as 'traction control'—positions the contribution as a minimal additive correction rather than a new generative paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-documented failure mode of Mean Flow (JVP NaN/Inf, ambiguous trajectories) with a theoretically grounded fix, providing explicit error-growth bounds for both one-step and multi-step generation that prior one-step methods lacked. However, the empirical gains are modest (FID 2.92→2.86 on CIFAR-10, 3.43→3.37 on ImageNet), the effective hyperparameter regime is narrow, and the authors note that at NFE=5 the advantage largely disappears. Open questions include whether the non-expansivity constraint scales to larger architectures and higher-resolution datasets, whether the narrow (Δz, μ) band reflects a fundamental tension between stability and expressivity, and whether the endpoint-error bounds can be tightened to yield larger practical FID improvements.
