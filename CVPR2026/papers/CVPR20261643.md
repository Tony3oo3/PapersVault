## Problem

Existing learning paradigms each address only a subset of the challenges faced by real-world multimodal models: semi-supervised multimodal learning ignores domain shifts, multimodal domain generalization cannot exploit unlabeled data, and semi-supervised domain generalization is restricted to single-modality inputs. This paper introduces Semi-Supervised Multimodal Domain Generalization (SSMDG), a new problem setting that simultaneously requires generalizing to unseen domains, operating with very few labeled samples, and leveraging cross-modal interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261643/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes paired video and audio clips (optionally augmented with optical flow) from multiple source domains, where only 5–10 labeled samples per class (or 5–10% of samples) are available alongside abundant unlabeled data. It outputs a single action class label per sample. A distinctive aspect is the leave-one-domain-out evaluation protocol (e.g., train on domains A and C, test on unseen H), and the framework additionally supports missing-modality inference by synthesizing absent features via learned cross-modal translators.

## Method

The framework combines three complementary components operating on weakly and strongly augmented views. Consensus-Driven Consistency Regularization (CDCR) generates pseudo-labels from the fused prediction only when at least one unimodal prediction agrees and both exceed a confidence threshold, then enforces FixMatch-style consistency across augmented views. Disagreement-Aware Regularization (DAR) recovers the remaining high-confidence but non-consensus samples by applying a noise-robust Generalized Cross-Entropy loss across all unimodal and fused predictions in both views. Cross-Modal Prototype Alignment (CMPA) maintains exponential-moving-average class prototypes per modality, class, and domain, aligning both original and cross-modally-translated features to intra-domain and cross-domain prototype averages to enforce joint modality- and domain-invariance while enabling missing-modality robustness at test time.

## Results

Under the challenging 5-labels-per-class SSMDG setting, the proposed method achieves a mean top-1 accuracy of 60.77% on HAC and 39.94% on EPIC-Kitchens, outperforming all 16 baselines across both datasets. On HAC, the best competing methods are STiL (58.34%) and NIED-LR^M (57.64%), while on EPIC-Kitchens the strongest baselines are NIED-LR^M (37.12%) and STiL (36.66%). The method also scales to 3-modality inputs (Video+Audio+Flow), reaching 66.95% mean accuracy on HAC with 10 labels per class, and demonstrates robustness to missing modalities via cross-modal translation, outperforming zero-filling by up to 7.59%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all 16 baselines on both HAC and EPIC-Kitchens under the 5-labels-per-class setting. Against the strongest SSML baseline STiL [11], it gains +2.43% on HAC (60.77 vs. 58.34) and +3.28% on EPIC-Kitchens (39.94 vs. 36.66). Against the best SSDG multimodal baseline NIED-LR^M [16], the gains are +3.13% on HAC and +2.82% on EPIC-Kitchens. The best SSL multimodal baseline CGMatch^M [4] trails by a wider margin of +5.32% (HAC) and +3.90% (EPIC-Kitchens), reflecting its lack of domain-generalization mechanisms. MMDG methods, which cannot exploit unlabeled data, lag substantially: the best MMDG method SimMMDG [8] achieves only 44.39% on HAC and 31.11% on EPIC-Kitchens, a gap of 16.38% and 8.83% respectively. Unimodal baselines (video-only or audio-only) are consistently weaker, with audio-only methods particularly disadvantaged (e.g., CGMatch audio-only: 30.35% HAC, 24.99% EPIC), underscoring the value of multimodal fusion under domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of three established but disjoint paradigms: Semi-Supervised Multimodal Learning (SSML), Multimodal Domain Generalization (MMDG), and Semi-Supervised Domain Generalization (SSDG), arguing that none adequately addresses the combined challenge of sparse labels, domain shift, and multimodal inputs simultaneously. Methodologically, it builds on the FixMatch consistency framework (weak/strong augmentation with confidence thresholding) for pseudo-label generation and on SimMMDG's cross-modal feature translation for handling missing modalities at inference. The key departure is the design of a modality-consensus selection strategy (CDCR) that gates pseudo-labels on cross-modal agreement, combined with a GCE-based robust loss for non-consensus samples and a prototype-alignment module that jointly enforces domain and modality invariance in feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing the first SSMDG benchmark on HAC and EPIC-Kitchens with multiple label budgets and missing-modality protocols, this paper gives the community a concrete evaluation setting for a practically important but previously unstudied combination of constraints. The cross-modal prototype alignment with learned translators is a reusable component that could benefit other multimodal DG or semi-supervised settings. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more than three modalities or to foundation-model backbones, how it behaves under covariate shifts that also alter the conditional P(Y|X), and whether the consensus thresholding strategy generalizes to open-set or long-tail label distributions.
