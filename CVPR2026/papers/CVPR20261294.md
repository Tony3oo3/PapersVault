## Problem

Reconstructing physically based rendering (PBR) materials from multi-view images is highly underconstrained: inverse path tracing is computationally expensive and its Monte-Carlo noise propagates into unstable material estimates, while single-view diffusion-based estimators (e.g., RGBX) produce high-quality but mutually inconsistent decompositions across views. The paper addresses how to distill the rich 2D priors of generative material estimators into a 3D-consistent, physically grounded PBR representation without letting rendering noise corrupt the result.

## Input / Output

The method takes posed multi-view RGB images (512×512 for synthetic, laser-scan resolution for real ScanNet++ scenes), a reconstructed 3D mesh, and 3D-consistent instance segmentation (via MaskClustering + SAM) as input. For real scenes with incomplete geometry (holes, missing windows), an environment map of resolution 16×32 is added to the lighting representation. The output is a full PBR representation: albedo, roughness, and metallic textures stored in an InstantNGP-based neural implicit, plus a per-triangle emission map and a camera response function (CRF) for LDR inputs, all suitable for arbitrary relighting and re-rendering.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages. First, K=16 RGBX predictions per view are parametrized with per-object affine transformations (3×4 for albedo, 1×2 for roughness/metallic) that absorb the scale ambiguity between lighting and reflectance; a Laplacian distribution is then fitted over the predictions using learnable assignment logits that softly select the most consistent candidate per object. Second, an InstantNGP-based BRDF network predicts 3D-consistent PBR textures, and a distribution-matching loss (KL-divergence between the 3D Laplacian and the 2D reference distributions) plus a label loss on the assignment logits jointly optimize the network, logits, and affine transforms over ~10 epochs. Third, only the low-dimensional per-object affine parameters are refined via FIPT-style alternating inverse path tracing (lighting optimization → light-transport caching → BRDF fitting), which drastically reduces the number of free parameters exposed to Monte-Carlo noise and yields sharp, physically grounded materials.

## Results

On four synthetic scenes, IIF achieves Albedo PSNR 20.72, SSIM 0.846, and LPIPS 0.201, substantially outperforming all baselines. For roughness and metallic maps, IIF reports L2 errors of 0.028 and 0.007 respectively, roughly 2–4× lower than the nearest competitor (IRIS: 0.056 and 0.040). The emission L2 error is 0.384, an order-of-magnitude improvement over FIPT (2.208) and IRIS (2.046). Qualitative results on real ScanNet++ scenes confirm that IIF avoids the baked-in shading and contour artifacts seen in prior methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IIF outperforms all three baselines across every reported metric. Against NeILF++ [31], the gains are largest on albedo (PSNR 20.72 vs. 13.18, a 7.5 dB improvement) and roughness (L2 0.028 vs. 0.103, ~3.7× lower), reflecting NeILF++'s difficulty separating shading from reflectance under noisy inter-reflection constraints. Against FIPT [25], IIF improves albedo PSNR by 10.09 dB and reduces emission L2 by 5.8× (0.384 vs. 2.208); metallic L2 is nearly tied (0.007 vs. 0.006), consistent with FIPT's strength on sparse metallic maps. Against IRIS [18], which already uses a per-object single-view prior as a regularizer, IIF still leads on all metrics—albedo PSNR by 4.86 dB, roughness L2 by 2×, and emission L2 by 5.3×—because IRIS still optimizes the full texture through the noisy rendering loss, whereas IIF restricts path-traced optimization to low-dimensional per-object affine parameters. Qualitatively, on real ScanNet++ scenes, IIF avoids the projected contour artifacts and baked-in lighting visible in FIPT and NeILF++ outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of diffusion-based single-view material estimation (RGBX) and inverse path tracing (FIPT, IRIS). While IRIS uses per-object averaging of single-view predictions as a material proxy and RGBX provides the 2D prior, this work departs by modeling the multi-candidate predictions as an explicit Laplacian distribution with per-object affine transformations, then performing distribution matching to select the most consistent prediction across views rather than averaging. The parametric BRDF formulation (affine transforms over a base texture) is the key architectural novelty, reducing the optimization to a low-dimensional per-object parameter space that makes inverse path tracing far more stable than full-texture optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core insight—that a low-dimensional parametric distribution over 2D predictions can be distilled into a 3D-consistent texture via distribution matching, with only a small residual optimized by path tracing—offers a generalizable paradigm for incorporating generative priors into 3D reconstruction while avoiding noise amplification. Limitations include reliance on fixed (potentially incomplete) geometry, computational cost of sampling 16 predictions per view, and dependence on the quality of the pretrained RGBX estimator. Open questions include whether joint geometry-material optimization is feasible, whether the prior can be incorporated directly into the rendering loop to avoid separate prediction sampling, and whether uncertainty-aware selection of predictions could further improve robustness.
