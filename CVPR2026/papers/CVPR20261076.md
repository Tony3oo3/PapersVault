## Problem

Merging multiple LoRA adapters into a single model is challenging because LoRA update directions span different subspaces and contribute unevenly to task losses. Existing merging methods either ignore the LoRA factorized structure (vanilla methods like Task Arithmetic, TIES) or only partially exploit it (LoRA-aware methods like KnOTS, LoRA-LEGO), but none jointly preserve subspace coverage (how broadly LoRA directions span the representational space) and address directional anisotropy (the imbalance in how different LoRA directions affect task losses).

## Input / Output

The method takes N LoRA adapters (each with factor matrices B_i ∈ R^{d×r_i} and A_i ∈ R^{m×r_i}), the frozen base model weights W_0, and a user-specified task preference vector ρ on the (N−1)-simplex, and produces merged model weights W = W_0 + Σ φ_ik S_ik. The direction-selection weights φ are learned via AdamW optimization (lr=0.001, batch size 16, 500 iterations) using a preference-weighted cross-entropy pseudoloss, so the method is learning-based rather than training-free. Operating at the adapter level keeps memory and compute overhead low even at foundation-model scale, making gradient-based optimization feasible.

## Method

TARA (Task-Rank Anisotropy Alignment) introduces two variants for constructing and weighting LoRA directions. Variant A reweights individual rank-1 factors b_ij a_ij^T with per-direction scalars φ_ij, directly controlling anisotropy within each adapter. Variant B first concatenates all adapters into a single matrix, computes its SVD, and uses the top-R shared orthonormal left singular vectors as a common basis, then assigns per-task weights to the resulting rank-1 components u_k v_ki^T, which preserves subspace coverage by retaining the top-R column space. Both variants optimize φ under a Smooth Tchebycheff Scalarization objective (Eq. 16) that incorporates a preference-weighted entropy pseudoloss (following AdaMerging), so the merged model aligns with user-specified task priorities. The theoretical grounding comes from Proposition 1, which bounds task-loss changes via the singular values of the direction-restricted Jacobian, showing that large condition numbers (anisotropy) make equal-norm direction updates produce disproportionate loss changes, motivating the direction-wise reweighting.

## Results

On eight vision classification benchmarks (CLIP ViT-B/32, rank-16 LoRA), TARA-Variant B achieves 76.3% average normalized accuracy, outperforming the strongest vanilla baseline Iso-C (73.6%) and the best LoRA-aware baseline KnOTS-TIES (68.0%). On six NLI benchmarks (LLaMA-3-8B), TARA-Variant B reaches 80.3% average normalized accuracy versus AdaMerging's 78.7% and KnOTS-DARE-TIES's 76.5%. In joint-task evaluation, TARA-Variant A attains the highest Hits@1 (51.1%) and TARA-Variant B the best Hits@5 (85.1%), both surpassing AdaMerging (48.1% / 83.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TARA-Variant B (76.3% avg) outperforms all 13 baselines on the eight vision benchmarks. The closest competitors are Iso-C (73.6%) and Iso-CTS (73.5%), which exploit isotropic subspace decomposition but still trail TARA by ~2.7–2.8 points; AdaMerging (70.0%) learns global layer-wise weights but lacks per-direction anisotropy control, yielding a 6.3-point gap. Among LoRA-aware methods, KnOTS-TIES (68.0%) is the strongest, yet TARA still leads by 8.3 points, while LoRA-LEGO (62.9%) and SVD (62.6%) perform near vanilla baselines, confirming that merely preserving rank-1 structure without sensitivity-aware reweighting is insufficient. On the six NLI benchmarks, TARA-Variant B (80.3%) again leads all baselines, with AdaMerging (78.7%) and LoRA-LEGO (76.6%) as the nearest competitors; notably, Iso-C and Iso-CTS collapse to 58.1% and 56.4% respectively on NLI, indicating their isotropic decomposition is poorly suited to language tasks. In joint-task evaluation, TARA-Variant A achieves the best Hits@1 (51.1% vs. AdaMerging's 48.1%), and TARA-Variant B achieves the best Hits@5 (85.1% vs. 83.0%), while all vanilla baselines (TA, TIES, DARE-TIES) remain below 45% Hits@1. TARA also generalizes best to unseen tasks (70.9–72.1% all-task avg vs. AdaMerging's 66.4–66.7%), demonstrating that direction-wise reweighting preserves transferable representational capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the model merging literature (Task Arithmetic, TIES, DARE, AdaMerging) and the emerging LoRA-aware merging line (KnOTS, LoRA-LEGO, SVD-based merging), but departs from both by identifying two specific structural properties—subspace coverage and directional anisotropy—that prior methods address at most partially. Vanilla methods collapse LoRA subspaces through destructive interference, while LoRA-aware methods like KnOTS and LoRA-LEGO preserve rank-1 structure but ignore per-direction loss sensitivity. TARA unifies these concerns by combining direction-wise reweighting (to mitigate anisotropy) with subspace-preserving basis construction (Variant B's shared SVD) under a smooth Tchebycheff scalarization objective borrowed from multi-objective optimization, yielding a preference-aligned merge that no single prior method achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LoRA becomes the default fine-tuning paradigm for LLMs and VLMs, principled merging of many adapters is essential for building general-purpose models without costly multi-task training. The paper's decomposition of the merging problem into coverage and anisotropy provides a useful diagnostic lens for the field, and the consistent gains across 14 benchmarks (vision + NLI) with both CLIP and LLaMA-3 backbones suggest broad applicability. Open questions include scalability to very large adapter counts or high-rank adapters where the SVD in Variant B becomes expensive, whether the preference vector can be learned automatically rather than specified a priori, and whether the framework extends to non-linear or iterative merging settings. The paper also notes that the learning-based optimization, while justified for LoRA-scale parameters, may still be a barrier in some deployment pipelines.
