## Problem

Creatively blending two images requires identifying and merging their most relevant shared attributes (the 'vibe'), but current diffusion-based morphing methods and multimodal LLMs either interpolate pixels, transfer style, or compose parts without recognizing which attributes matter. Naive linear interpolation in latent feature spaces like CLIP produces incoherent intermediate images for conceptually distant pairs because the true semantic path is nonlinear and the latent space contains implausible 'holes' between distant concepts.

## Input / Output

The method takes two RGB images (plus optional related exemplar images) and produces a continuous sequence of blended images {I_α} for α ∈ [0,1]. DINO patch-token features and CLIP image embeddings are extracted from the inputs; the Vibe Space is trained on-the-fly in under 30 seconds with fewer than 1M parameters. A secondary mode, Vibe Analogy, reuses the learned displacement field Δ_{A→B} to extrapolate the same vibe onto a new image A′, yielding an analogous output B′.

## Method

Vibe Space learns a hierarchical graph manifold from DINO patch-token features: an affinity graph is built, its Laplacian eigenvectors (graph diffusion map) provide multi-scale manifold coordinates, and a 'flag space' of nested eigen-embeddings captures both global and local geometry simultaneously. Two lightweight MLPs (encoder f: DINO→Vibe, decoder g: Vibe→CLIP) are trained to mimic the forward and inverse diffusion mapping, with losses enforcing Gram-matrix alignment to the flag-space kernel plus a reconstruction loss bridging DINO to CLIP. Semantic correspondence between the two images is established via k-way NCut segmentation and Hungarian matching, which determines which attributes to blend. Linear interpolation in the learned Vibe Space then approximates the multiscale inverse-diffusion geodesic in closed form, and a frozen IP-Adapter renders pixel images from the decoded CLIP features without any fine-tuning.

## Results

On the Totally Looks Like dataset, Vibe Space achieves 60.0% human preference on high-difficulty pairs, 3× the next-best method (GPT at 20.0%), and 50.0% on medium-difficulty pairs (2.4× GPT's 21.4%). On the Architecture dataset, Vibe Space leads with 42.0% human preference, narrowly ahead of CLIP Avg (39.0%) and well ahead of GPT (14.0%) and Gemini (5.0%). The LLM judge (GPT-5) also favors Vibe Space most often on high-difficulty TLL pairs (40.0%, tied with GPT) and on Architecture (39.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Gemini 2.5 Flash Image, Vibe Space wins decisively on every difficulty tier for human preference (60% vs 6.7% on high-difficulty TLL; 42% vs 5% on Architecture), as Gemini tends to perform part-level composition or style transfer rather than identifying the relevant shared attributes. Against GPT Image 1, Vibe Space wins on high-difficulty TLL (60% vs 20%) and medium-difficulty TLL (50% vs 21.4%), but GPT overtakes on low-difficulty TLL (40% vs 26.7%), where simpler linear blending suffices. On the Architecture dataset, Vibe Space leads GPT by 28 percentage points (42% vs 14%), suggesting GPT struggles when shared attributes are subtle and spatially misaligned. Against CLIP Avg (simple embedding averaging + IP-Adapter), Vibe Space wins on high- and medium-difficulty TLL (60% vs 13.3%; 50% vs 21.4%) and Architecture (42% vs 39%), but ties on low-difficulty TLL (26.7% each), confirming that the manifold-based path finding adds value primarily when concepts are distant. The LLM judge (GPT-5 with chain-of-thought) shows 55.1% agreement with human top-2 preferences on TLL, indicating it captures a partial but meaningful overlap with human creativity judgments.

## Relation to Prior Work

The paper's core mathematical machinery is the graph diffusion map of Coifman et al. [9], repurposed here to learn a low-dimensional 'Vibe Space' from DINO patch tokens rather than operating on raw data points. It departs from diffusion-based morphing methods (DiffMorpher, Yu et al.) and attention-interpolation approaches (AID) that treat all attributes uniformly in a fixed latent space, instead learning a task-specific manifold from a few context images and using flag-space (hierarchical nested eigenvector embeddings) to capture multi-scale geometry. For generation, it bridges to CLIP-conditioned diffusion via IP-Adapter, positioning itself against both pixel-level morphing and language-mediated creative blending (Peng et al., Sun et al.) by operating directly in image feature space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled geometric framework for identifying *which* attributes matter when blending two images, rather than interpolating everything uniformly—a gap that both diffusion morphing and LLM-based approaches leave open. The path nonlinearity score (PNS) offers a practical, model-free proxy for blend difficulty that correlates with human judgments at 80% agreement, enabling systematic curation of challenging pairs. Key open questions include whether the approach scales beyond the small evaluation sets (44 + 300 pairs), whether the IP-Adapter bottleneck limits generation quality, and how to improve LLM judges so they reliably attend to the correct 'vibe' rather than superficial attributes like color or texture.
