## Problem

Exemplar replay methods for federated continual learning focus on selecting representative samples but largely overlook how to effectively utilize the limited replayed data under continual dynamic heterogeneity. Two persistent challenges remain: replayed data exacerbates inter-client representation misalignment, and the long-tailed distribution between past (tail) and current (head) tasks causes rare-class features to drift toward majority-class directions, degrading prediction accuracy on underrepresented classes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262309/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet-Subset) in a federated class-incremental setting where each client holds a Dirichlet-partitioned local dataset and a fixed-size exemplar buffer. At inference, the model produces a class label via ETF-prototype similarity scores computed on a geometrically corrected feature vector, rather than a standard softmax over learned classifier weights. The correction is applied per-sample at test time using only two scalar EMA statistics uploaded per client per round, adding negligible communication overhead.

## Method

FEAT introduces two orthogonal modules that can be plugged into any exemplar replay framework. The Geometric Structure Alignment (GSA) module performs structural knowledge distillation by computing pairwise cosine-similarity matrices over a mini-batch of features and over fixed, globally shared Equiangular Tight Frame (ETF) prototypes, then minimizing a class-balanced row-wise KL divergence between the two (with a temperature parameter) to enforce angular consistency across clients and tasks. The Energy-based Geometric Correction (EGC) module operates at inference: it projects each normalized feature onto head-class and tail-class ETF subspaces via Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse projectors, computes rank-normalized projection energies, and applies a confidence gate derived from the deviation above a globally aggregated tail prior to suppress the head-aligned component and amplify the tail-aligned component before ℓ₂-normalization and final ETF-based classification. The GSA loss is added to the standard cross-entropy only for tasks t > 1, while EGC requires no additional training and introduces no extra communication beyond two scalar EMA values per client per round.

## Results

FEAT_F (FEAT integrated with FedCBDR) achieves the highest Top-1 accuracy across all 12 dataset/heterogeneity/client configurations. On CIFAR-10 (3 tasks, β=0.5, 5 clients), it reaches 72.67%, surpassing the strongest baseline FedCBDR (63.32%) by 9.35 points. On CIFAR-100 (5 tasks, β=0.1, 5 clients), FEAT_F attains 50.14% vs. FedCBDR's 45.84%, and on TinyImageNet-Subset (5 tasks, β=0.5, 5 clients) it scores 29.31% vs. 26.38%. The advantage persists and does not shrink under stronger heterogeneity (smaller β) or more clients (K=10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEAT_F dominates all seven baselines (FedEWC, FedLwF, TARGET, LANDER, Re-Fed+, FedSSI, FedCBDR) across every dataset, heterogeneity level, and client count. The largest gap is against FedCBDR on CIFAR-10 (3 tasks, β=0.5, K=5): 72.67% vs. 63.32% (+9.35 pp), while on CIFAR-100 (5 tasks, β=0.1) the margin narrows to +4.30 pp (50.14 vs. 45.84) and on TinyImageNet (5 tasks, β=0.5) to +2.93 pp (29.31 vs. 26.38). FEAT_R, built on Re-Fed+, also consistently improves over its base (e.g., 70.28 vs. 61.64 on CIFAR-10 3-task β=1.0), confirming the plug-and-play nature. In the alternative-strategy comparison (Table 3), FEAT added to FedCBDR outperforms MOON (+7.63 pp on CIFAR-10 β=0.5), FedRCL (+11.45 pp), CLIP2FL (+7.86 pp), and FedFSA (+5.77 pp), with the largest gains under the most heterogeneous settings (smallest β). Notably, the advantage does not diminish as heterogeneity increases or client count grows from 5 to 10, indicating robustness to scaling.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two threads: (1) the Neural Collapse / ETF classifier line of work (Seo et al., CVPR 2024; Li et al., ICCV 2023), which fixes a globally shared simplex-ETF to reduce classifier bias in federated learning, and (2) exemplar-replay FCL methods such as Re-Fed+ and FedCBDR, which focus on *selecting* representative samples from past tasks. The key departure is that FEAT is orthogonal to the selection policy: rather than proposing a new replay strategy, it adds a geometric structure alignment loss (angular distillation toward ETF prototypes) during training and an energy-based subspace correction at inference, targeting the *utilization* of already-selected exemplars under inter-client heterogeneity and head-tail class imbalance.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means FEAT can be layered onto any existing exemplar-replay pipeline without altering memory allocation or selection criteria, which is practically valuable for the FCL community. However, all experiments use ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10/100 and TinyImageNet-Subset, so it remains open whether the geometric correction generalizes to larger backbones (ViT, deeper ResNets) or higher-resolution datasets like ImageNet. The paper also notes that gains do not strictly increase with more local epochs, suggesting an interaction between local overfitting and the geometric regularizer that warrants further study.
