## Problem

Co-speech gesture generation methods produce only spontaneous gestures synchronized with speech, neglecting text-driven non-spontaneous motions (e.g., bowing, pointing) that are essential for natural full-body speaker movement. This limitation stems from two gaps: gesture datasets lack descriptive text annotations (semantic prior gap), and existing approaches cannot harmoniously coordinate heterogeneous multimodal conditions (audio + caption) without control conflicts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260579/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes speech audio and a free-form text caption describing desired non-spontaneous motion as joint conditions, producing a full-body 3D gesture sequence over 55 SMPL-X joints (659-dimensional per-frame features). Captions are generated offline by a frozen MotionGPT-based captioning framework and cached, incurring no inference overhead. The model is jointly trained on BEAT (audio-gesture) and HumanML3D (text-motion) with a unified motion representation; missing modalities in each dataset are zero-padded.

## Method

CoordSpeaker comprises two stages: (1) a Gesture Captioning Framework that uses a VQ-VAE motion tokenizer and a frozen MotionGPT to produce multi-granular captions (local segment-level and global sequence-level) via three strategies—Regular, Dynamic, and Hierarchical—enabling precise temporal-semantic alignment; and (2) a Coordinated Gesture Generation Model built on a transformer VAE that learns a unified latent motion space across datasets, followed by a conditional latent diffusion model with a hierarchically controlled denoiser. The denoiser injects local conditions (segment caption + audio) through self-attention in its encoder and global conditions (full-sequence caption) through cross-attention in its decoder, separating fine-grained rhythmic/semantic control from holistic coherence. Classifier-free guidance with independent scales for audio and caption allows flexible control strength at inference, and DDIM sampling with 50 steps yields an average inference time of 0.84 s per sentence—over 6× faster than prior diffusion-based baselines.

## Results

On the coordinated gesture generation task (BEAT + HumanML3D), CoordSpeaker outperforms FreeTalker on reconstruction quality (Jerk 1.190 vs. 0.611, closer to GT 1.165; Accel 0.039 vs. 0.030, closer to GT 0.043), beat consistency (BC 1.327 vs. 1.147), and diversity (Div 5.558 vs. 5.396, closer to GT 5.512), while FreeTalker leads on FGD (2.101 vs. 3.173) and FID (0.761 vs. 1.118). On the text-to-motion benchmark (HumanML3D), CoordSpeaker achieves the best motion-text alignment (MM-Dist 3.584) and second-best generation fidelity (FID 0.405) among six SOTA methods. Inference speed is a major advantage: CoordSpeaker runs at 0.842 s/sentence, over 6× faster than FreeTalker (6.632 s) and SynTalker (5.804 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FreeTalker [47] (the only prior method targeting joint speech+text gesture generation), CoordSpeaker wins on reconstruction fidelity (Jerk, Accel), speech synchronization (BC +0.180), and diversity (Div +0.162), but trails on gesture realism (FGD +1.072, FID +0.357) and text alignment (MM-Dist +0.077, R-Precision −0.002). Against SynTalker [4] in the user study, CoordSpeaker is preferred by 4.0% more participants on naturalness, 5.5% on synchrony, and 1.5% on caption matching (all p<0.05). On the text-to-motion benchmark, CoordSpeaker achieves the best MM-Dist (3.584) among all six methods, surpassing MoMask (3.620), T2M-GPT (3.867), MLD (3.898), MDM (4.599), and SynTalker (4.499), while MoMask retains the best FID (0.222 vs. 0.405) and R-Precision (Top-1 0.461 vs. 0.424). CoordSpeaker dramatically outperforms SynTalker on FID (0.405 vs. 4.385) and MM-Dist (3.584 vs. 4.499), confirming superior semantic control. Inference speed is the clearest differentiator: 0.842 s/sentence vs. 6.632 s (FreeTalker) and 5.804 s (SynTalker), a >6× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoordSpeaker sits at the intersection of co-speech gesture generation and text-to-motion synthesis, building on the latent diffusion paradigm (Rombach et al. [35]) and transformer VAE motion encoders (Petrovich et al. [31]). For the gesture captioning component, it repurposes MotionGPT [17] and the VQ-VAE tokenizer from TM2T [13]—both originally designed for general motion-language tasks—into a frozen, offline captioning pipeline specifically for gesture data. The key departure from prior coordinated generation work (FreeTalker [47], SynTalker [4]) is the introduction of a hierarchically controlled denoiser that injects local (audio + local caption) and global (global caption) conditions at different architectural stages, rather than simply concatenating all conditions as earlier methods do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By generating descriptive captions offline via a frozen motion-language model, CoordSpeaker eliminates the need for expensive manual annotation while enabling fine-grained semantic control over non-spontaneous gestures (e.g., bowing, pointing) that were previously inaccessible in co-speech settings. The 6× inference speedup over prior diffusion-based baselines makes coordinated gesture generation more practical for real-time avatar applications. However, the approach is limited to four BEAT speakers, the captioning model struggles with temporal ordering in longer sequences, and the evaluation relies on a single 200-sample expert-annotated set for caption quality—leaving open questions about scalability to diverse gesture vocabularies and cross-lingual generalization.
