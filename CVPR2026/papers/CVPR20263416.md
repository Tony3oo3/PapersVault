## Problem

Retrieval-based high-resolution image understanding methods (e.g., RAP) suffer from three dominant failure modes: object fragmentation from fixed grid partitioning, scale sensitivity of crop resolution, and background interference from spurious high-similarity patches. These two primary failure modes (fragmentation and background) account for 89.6% of all retrieval failures, severely limiting the reliability of vision-based RAG for HR image understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263416/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a high-resolution RGB image (4K or 8K) and a free-form text query as input. The image is partitioned into two proportional-resolution crop grids (a low-resolution set at resolution l and a high-resolution set at resolution k·l), and the query is encoded via a VLM text encoder. The method produces a fused 2D similarity map (H×W) that combines multi-resolution semantic similarity with open-vocabulary detection confidence, which then drives the RAP Retrieved-Exploration Search pipeline to select K most relevant crops for the MLLM to generate a text answer.

## Method

MRD is a training-free framework with two complementary modules operating at different scales. The Multi-resolution Semantic Fusion module computes cosine similarity between the query embedding and crops at two proportional resolutions, projects the higher-resolution scores onto the lower-resolution grid via spatial correspondence, and fuses them element-wise via geometric mean (√(s̃·s)) to enforce cross-scale consistency and correct fragmentation-induced semantic bias. The Open-vocabulary Detector Enhancement module uses LLM in-context learning to extract target entities from the query, runs LLMDet with a sliding-window strategy spatially aligned to the crop grid, and aggregates per-window detection confidences into a global confidence map via averaging. The two maps are combined via a weighted linear fusion (s^f = (1−w)·s^m + w·c^g), amplifying genuine target regions while suppressing background false positives, and the resulting fused map guides the downstream search for more accurate and efficient crop retrieval.

## Results

On V* Bench with LLaVA-v1.5-7B, MRD achieves 95.6% overall accuracy, a 46.9% absolute gain over the vanilla baseline (48.7%) and a 4.5% improvement over the SOTA RAP method (91.1%). On HR-Bench 4K and 8K, MRD reaches 59.9% and 54.8% overall respectively, outperforming RAP by 2.8% and 1.0%. With the smaller LLaVA-ov-0.5B backbone, MRD achieves 88.9% on V* Bench, 64.6% on HR-Bench 4K, and 64.5% on HR-Bench 8K, surpassing GPT-4o (66.0%, 59.0%, 55.5%) on all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRD consistently outperforms the strongest prior method RAP across all benchmarks and both backbone sizes: +4.5% on V* Bench (95.6 vs 91.1), +2.8% on HR-Bench 4K (59.9 vs 57.1), and +1.0% on HR-Bench 8K (54.8 vs 53.8) with LLaVA-v1.5-7B; the gains are larger with LLaVA-ov-0.5B (+5.3%, +3.3%, +1.0%). Against Zoom Eye, MRD leads by 12.3% on V* Bench and 4.0–6.6% on HR-Bench, confirming that retrieval-based methods substantially outperform tree-search zooming in multi-object settings. The most striking comparison is against the vanilla LLaVA-v1.5-7B baseline, where MRD nearly doubles V* Bench accuracy (95.6% vs 48.7%). Notably, the 0.5B MRD model surpasses the much larger closed-source GPT-4o on all three benchmarks (88.9 vs 66.0 on V* Bench; 64.6 vs 59.0 on HR-Bench 4K; 64.5 vs 55.5 on HR-Bench 8K), demonstrating that the retrieval-detection fusion strategy compensates for model scale. The ablation (Table 2) further shows that the two core modules are complementary: multi-resolution fusion primarily reduces fragmentation errors (−40.4%) while OVD primarily reduces background interference (−54.2%), and their combination yields the largest overall error reduction (−48.3% in BG/FRAG rate).

## Relation to Prior Work

MRD extends the retrieval-augmented perception (RAP) paradigm for high-resolution image understanding by addressing its two dominant failure modes—object fragmentation and background interference—through a dual-branch design. Locally, it builds on VisRAG's cosine-similarity-based crop-query matching but augments it with cross-resolution semantic fusion to correct single-scale bias. Globally, it introduces LLMDet as an open-vocabulary detection prior, a component absent from prior retrieval-based HR methods, and fuses the resulting confidence map with the semantic similarity map via a simple linear combination. The framework remains training-free and plugs into RAP's existing Retrieved-Exploration Search pipeline, positioning it as a drop-in enhancement rather than a new search architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a lightweight multi-resolution fusion plus a sliding-window OVD module can consistently outperform RAP across multiple MLLM backbones and benchmarks without any parameter updates, MRD offers a practical, deployable recipe for improving HR image understanding in production MLLM pipelines. The efficiency finding—that the fused map reduces search steps by over 50%, yielding a net runtime reduction despite added detection cost—makes the approach more attractive for real-time applications. Open questions include whether the detection module's benefit persists at even higher resolutions (16K+), whether the linear fusion weight w can be made adaptive per query, and whether the framework extends naturally to video or 3D understanding where temporal/spatial consistency adds another dimension.
