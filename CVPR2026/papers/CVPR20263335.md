## Problem

INR-based arbitrary-scale MRI super-resolution methods suffer from spectral bias, where over-parameterized networks under gradient optimization converge to low-frequency solutions first, producing over-smoothed reconstructions that lose sharp anatomical boundaries and high-frequency tissue details critical for clinical diagnosis. This paper addresses the gap by reformulating the super-resolution task as learning anisotropic 2D Gaussian kernel parameters rather than directly regressing pixel intensities, transforming high-frequency recovery into a smoother parameter optimization problem.

## Input / Output

The model takes a low-resolution target MRI image (e.g., T2) and a high-resolution reference MRI image (e.g., T1) of the same anatomy as inputs, with LR images generated via k-space cropping from 256×256 ground-truth scans at arbitrary scale factors S∈(1,4]. It outputs a super-resolved HR target image at any requested scale (tested up to 6×, including out-of-distribution scales). The model allocates exactly one 2D Gaussian per reference-image pixel, so the number of Gaussians scales with reference resolution, and the two inputs must be spatially aligned.

## Method

GaussM²ASR replaces INR's per-pixel MLP interpolation with a field of anisotropic 2D Gaussians whose covariance (σx, σy, ρ) adapts locally—narrow kernels capture high-frequency edges while wide kernels cover smooth regions. Three anatomy-guided modules drive this adaptation: SPMF uses global reference statistics to modulate target features and a pixel-wise gate to suppress background; AG-DDCA performs cross-attention in both spatial and Fourier-magnitude domains with a scale-embedded learnable Gaussian prompt to enhance high-frequency content; and AGGP uses a Sobel gradient map of the reference image with top-T sparse attention to steer Gaussian centers toward anatomical boundaries. Training follows a two-stage strategy (pre-train AGGP on HR data, then freeze it and fine-tune on LR inputs) with a composite loss of spatial MAE, Fourier-magnitude MAE, and reference-reconstruction consistency.

## Results

GaussM²ASR is evaluated on three multi-contrast MRI datasets (IXI, BraTS, fastMRI) using PSNR and SSIM at in-scale factors (1.5×, 2×, 3×, 4×) and out-of-scale factors (5×, 6×). The method consistently achieves the highest PSNR and SSIM across all datasets and all scaling factors, demonstrating robust generalization beyond the training scale range. The paper highlights a particularly significant SSIM advantage over all competing approaches, indicating superior preservation of anatomical structure and high-frequency detail. The two-stage training strategy (HR pre-training followed by LR fine-tuning with frozen AGGP) is confirmed as critical, with single-stage training causing a notable performance drop.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against six baselines: three natural-image SR methods adapted for multi-contrast MRI (LIIF [6], GaussianSR [11], GSASR [4]) and three MRI-specific arbitrary-scale SR methods (DualArbNet [41], McASSR [16], DINet [36]). GaussM²ASR outperforms all six methods on both PSNR and SSIM across all three datasets (IXI, BraTS, fastMRI) and all tested scaling factors from 1.5× to 6×. The INR-based baselines (LIIF, DualArbNet, McASSR, DINet) suffer from spectral bias, producing over-smoothed reconstructions that lose high-frequency anatomical boundaries, while the GS-based baselines (GaussianSR, GSASR) lack anatomy-guided mechanisms and thus cannot as effectively exploit the HR reference image's structural priors. The SSIM gap is especially pronounced, reflecting GaussM²ASR's superior ability to preserve diagnostically critical tissue boundaries and fine structural coherence. All competing methods were retrained on the same datasets using their official implementations to ensure a fair comparison.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the 3D Gaussian Splatting paradigm (Kerbl et al., 2023) to the 2D multi-contrast MRI super-resolution setting, following a recent line of work that has applied 2DGS to natural-image SR (GaussianSR, GSASR, ContinuousSR). It departs from the dominant INR-based approach (LIIF, McASSR, DualArbNet, DINet) by replacing implicit per-pixel coordinate-to-intensity regression with explicit anisotropic Gaussian parameterization, motivated by the spectral bias that causes INR models to underfit high-frequency anatomical boundaries. The anatomy-guided modules (SPMF, AG-DDCA, AGGP) are novel contributions specific to the multi-contrast MRI domain, leveraging reference-image structural priors to modulate Gaussian placement and shape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a clinically relevant gap: INR-based arbitrary-scale MRI SR methods produce over-smooth reconstructions that lose diagnostically critical tissue boundaries, and this work offers a principled alternative with consistent gains across three datasets and out-of-distribution scales. However, the approach requires spatially aligned target and reference images (a non-trivial pre-processing step in practice) and uses a fixed number of Gaussians tied to reference resolution, limiting efficiency for simpler textures. Open questions include whether the anatomy-guided Gaussian placement generalizes to 3D volumetric SR, whether adaptive Gaussian allocation can reduce redundancy, and whether the two-stage training strategy transfers to other Gaussian-based imaging tasks.
