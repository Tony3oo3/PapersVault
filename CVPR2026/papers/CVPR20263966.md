## Problem

Existing RL post-training for 3D mesh generation relies on offline DPO, which suffers from low training efficiency (synchronous online RL is impractical due to highly varying mesh token lengths causing severe GPU idle) and poor generalization because DPO's implicit reward modeling cannot capture complex reward distributions. This paper addresses both gaps by designing an asynchronous online RL framework and a new algorithm that explicitly models reward distributions while maintaining fast, stable convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263966/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a densely sampled point cloud (40,960 points) from a target mesh as input and autoregressively generates a native quadrilateral-dominated mesh as output. The mesh is represented via a diagonal-aware tokenization where each face begins with its minimum-index vertex, triangles are terminated with a padding token, and quads encode their internal diagonal orientation as an offset (flag × 2^n_bits) appended to the fourth vertex index. The 1.1B-parameter Hourglass Transformer decoder operates on 10-bit quantized coordinates normalized to [-0.95, 0.95]³, with inference at ~310 token/s using KV caching and CUDA graph acceleration.

## Method

Mesh-Pro combines three key innovations: (1) an asynchronous online RL framework where rollout workers continuously generate data into a replay buffer while trainer workers update the policy without interrupting rollouts, achieving 3.75× speedup over synchronous RL, with a pre-start stage that discards initial data to stabilize training; (2) ARPO (Advantage-guided Ranking Preference Optimization), which uses a Plackett-Luce ranking objective weighted by normalized advantage values to simultaneously achieve DPO-like fast convergence and GRPO-like explicit reward distribution modeling; (3) a diagonal-aware mixed triangle-quad tokenization that defers the triangle-vs-quad decision to the last token position and enforces a global canonical vertex ordering, paired with a ray-casting integrity reward and a quad-ring/quad-line topological reward to guide generation toward artist-quality quad topology.

## Results

On dense meshes (Hunyuan3D 2.5), Mesh-Pro achieves CD=0.028, HD=0.090, BR=22%, QR=81%, and US=5.2, outperforming the strongest baseline QuadGPT (CD=0.059, HD=0.163, BR=50%, QR=78%, US=4.5) by 52% in CD, 45% in HD, and 56% in BR. On artist meshes (Toys4k), Mesh-Pro records CD=0.038, HD=0.094, BR=32%, QR=78%, and US=4.9, again leading all baselines on every metric. The asynchronous ARPO training is 3.75× faster than synchronous RL under identical hardware, and the full pipeline (pre-training + RL post-training) runs on 64+32 NVIDIA H20 GPUs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mesh-Pro outperforms all six baselines on every metric in both dense and artist mesh settings. Against the closest competitor QuadGPT [30], Mesh-Pro reduces CD by 52% (0.028 vs 0.059) and HD by 45% (0.090 vs 0.163) on dense meshes, while cutting the broken ratio from 50% to 22% and lifting the user-study score from 4.5 to 5.2. On artist meshes, Mesh-Pro improves CD (0.038 vs 0.042), HD (0.094 vs 0.101), BR (32% vs 39%), and US (4.9 vs 4.4) over QuadGPT, with a 2-point QR advantage (78% vs 76%). Mesh-RFT [32], the strongest non-quad-native baseline, is surpassed by Mesh-Pro by 7% in CD, 31% in HD, and 1.4 points in US on dense meshes. The non-RL baselines (MeshAnyv2, BPT, FastMesh, DeepMesh) lag substantially, with broken ratios of 72–99% and user-study scores below 3.0, confirming that RL post-training is essential for artist-level topology.

## Relation to Prior Work

Mesh-Pro builds directly on the Hourglass Transformer architecture from Meshtron for autoregressive mesh decoding and on QuadGPT's RL-based alignment paradigm for quad mesh generation, while departing from both in key ways. The RL algorithm (ARPO) is derived from DPO's KL-regularized preference objective but replaces DPO's implicit reward with explicit advantage-weighted Plackett-Luce ranking, addressing DPO's poor generalization while avoiding GRPO's slow convergence. The diagonal-aware tokenization corrects QuadGPT's premature face-type commitment and non-canonical vertex ordering, and the asynchronous online framework replaces the offline DPO pipeline used in DeepMesh, Mesh-RFT, and QuadGPT with a continuous rollout-train loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.75× training speedup from asynchronous RL is practically significant for any group doing RL post-training on variable-length 3D sequences, where synchronous methods suffer severe GPU idle time. The finding that GRPO—despite its success in LLM reasoning—underperforms in 3D mesh generation due to limited pretrained model capacity and complex reward distributions is a useful cautionary result for transferring RL algorithms across modalities. Open questions include whether ARPO's advantage-weighted ranking generalizes to other 3D generation tasks (e.g., textured meshes, multi-object scenes), whether the ray-based reward can be replaced by learned reward models, and whether the approach scales beyond the 1.1B-parameter decoder used here.
