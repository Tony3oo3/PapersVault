## Problem

Existing data curation methods for vision-language pretraining are offline (permanently discarding data via static filters) and concept-agnostic (ignoring the distribution of visual concepts within training batches). Since different downstream tasks—zero-shot classification versus image-text retrieval—benefit from fundamentally different concept distributions, no prior method provides flexible, task-adaptive, online control over concept-level batch composition without sacrificing data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 128M image-text pairs from DataComp, each augmented with a concept set C_i (drawn from a 12,253-concept vocabulary via RAM++ tagging and GroundingDINO localization), per-concept confidence scores, bounding boxes, and a concept-aware synthetic caption generated by Qwen2-VL-7B. Critically, the concept annotations are used exclusively for batch selection and do not enter the contrastive loss. The output is a filtered sub-batch of size b=(1−f)B (default f=0.8, B=20,480) drawn from each super-batch, which is then fed into standard CLIP or SigLIP contrastive training at 224×224 or 256×256 resolution.

## Method

CABS is a parameterized online sampling framework: for each super-batch B, a concept-aware heuristic gain function h(C_i; B, θ_h) scores every sample, and the top-b samples form the training sub-batch. Two task-specific instantiations are proposed. CABS-DM (Diversity Maximization) uses a state-dependent, greedy gain function that combines a balance term (t_c − n_c)/t_c prioritizing under-represented concepts with a rarity bonus 1/F_c upweighting long-tailed concepts, iteratively selecting samples until the sub-batch approximates a uniform concept distribution. CABS-FM (Frequency Maximization) uses the simple state-independent score h_FM(i)=|C_i|, selecting samples with the highest object multiplicity to enrich batches for retrieval. Unlike offline curation, no data is permanently discarded—the same annotated pool can be re-sampled with different scoring functions for different target tasks, and the stochastic super-batch draws ensure variability across training steps.

## Results

On zero-shot classification, CABS-DM achieves 21.9% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-Val (CLIP ViT-B/32, alt-text) and 24.1% (SigLIP ViT-B/16-256), yielding absolute gains of +5.0% and +6.9% over IID sampling respectively. On image-text retrieval, CABS-FM reaches 30.4% R@1 on MSCOCO and 52.9% on Flickr (CLIP ViT-B/32, re-captions), improving over IID by +9.0% on average. Across 28 benchmarks and 4 model architectures, CABS variants consistently outperform all baselines, with the largest gains observed on long-tailed evaluations (Let-It-Wag! +1.0–2.4%) and retrieval tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CABS-DM substantially outperforms the IID DataComp baseline by +2.5% average classification accuracy (CLIP ViT-B/32) and +4.5% (SigLIP ViT-B/16-256), with the largest single-benchmark gain being +6.9% on ImageNet-Val for SigLIP. Against MetaCLIP (offline concept-balanced curation), CABS-DM wins by +3.8% on ImageNet and +2.9% on average classification, demonstrating that online batch-level balancing is more effective than static offline filtering. Both GRIT-VLP and MAFA fail to surpass IID sampling for CLIP ViT-B/32 (scoring 27.5% and 27.9% vs. 28.2% average), while CABS-DM achieves 30.7%; for SigLIP, CABS-DM still leads by +3.7% over GRIT-VLP and +3.8% over MAFA. On retrieval, CABS-FM improves over GRIT-VLP by +3.9% (CLIP) and +2.5% (SigLIP) on average R@1, and over MAFA by +4.0% and +3.2% respectively, while both baselines again underperform or barely match IID for CLIP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of offline, concept-agnostic data curation (e.g., CLIP-score filtering in DataComp) by introducing online, concept-aware batch sampling. It builds on the observation that batch composition shapes contrastive learning (GRIT-VLP, MAFA) but replaces embedding-similarity-based hard-negative selection with explicit concept-level heuristics, and on MetaCLIP's concept-balancing objective while moving it from a static offline filter to a dynamic per-batch procedure. The DATA CONCEPT dataset synthesizes existing tools (RAM++ tagging, GroundingDINO grounding, Qwen2-VL recaptioning) into a unified concept-annotated pool, carving new ground by making concept metadata directly actionable during training rather than as a one-time filtering criterion.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CABS provides a practical, open-source framework for task-adaptive data curation that avoids the irreversibility of offline filtering and the opacity of black-box model-based selection, with the 128M-sample DATA CONCEPT dataset serving as a reusable resource for future concept-centric research. Key limitations include the annotation cost (amortizable but non-trivial), increased runtime at high filter ratios, and evaluation restricted to ViT-B-scale models at 128M–1.28B samples seen, leaving open whether the approach transfers to larger architectures or more complex multimodal objectives. An important open direction is adapting the scoring function dynamically during training (e.g., curriculum from single-object to multi-object scenes) rather than fixing it a priori.
