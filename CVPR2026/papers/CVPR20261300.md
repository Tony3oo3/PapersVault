## Problem

ViTs pre-trained at a fixed resolution degrade in accuracy on dense prediction tasks like open-vocabulary segmentation when processing high-resolution inputs. Single-pass inference with interpolated positional encodings is fast but inaccurate, while sliding-window inference with small strides recovers accuracy at a prohibitive computational cost (up to 52× slower). The paper addresses how to achieve the spatial reasoning quality of finely-strided sliding-window inference within a single forward pass.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image of arbitrary resolution and aspect ratio (no fixed size constraint) along with a set of text class names specified at inference time, and produces a per-pixel class similarity map at the input's native resolution. The vision encoder outputs a feature map with spatial dimensions h×w = H/P × W/P (where P is the patch size), which is upsampled via bilinear interpolation or AnyUp to full image resolution. Unlike standard ViT inference that assumes a fixed K×K input, SPAR handles any H×W in one pass without windowing.

## Method

SPAR distills a frozen sliding-window teacher (overlapping K×K windows with stride s=24, features stitched by averaging overlaps) into a single-pass student of identical architecture initialized with the same pre-trained weights. The student minimizes an MSE regression loss between its single-pass feature map and the teacher's stitched feature map, requiring no pixel-level labels or architectural modifications. Training uses 25k unlabeled images from SA-1B at diverse resolutions (short side 512–2048) and aspect ratios, unfreezing only the last two transformer blocks (14.2M params) with AdamW at lr 2e-5 for 10 epochs. A key design choice is using a stride not divisible by the patch size, which exposes sub-patch regions to diverse contexts and improves stitched feature quality; in such cases, features are upsampled by a factor r before stitching and downsampled back. At inference, the student processes the full image in one forward pass, matching or exceeding the teacher's accuracy while being up to 52× faster.

## Results

On the six-dataset average (Mean₆ mIoU), SPAR with SigLIP2-ViT-B-16 achieves 43.6 mIoU in a single forward pass, improving over the pre-trained single-pass baseline by +10.5 mIoU and surpassing the sliding-window teacher (stride 24) by +2.4 mIoU while being 52× faster. For OpenCLIP-ViT-B-16, SPAR gains +6.7 mIoU over single-pass (34.4 vs. 27.7) and +0.8 over the teacher. For DINOv3.txt-ViT-L-16, which already incorporates resolution-robust design choices (RoPE, high-res fine-tuning), SPAR still yields a modest +0.6 mIoU over single-pass and +0.4 over the teacher, with a notable +4.2 mIoU gain on Cityscapes (40.1 vs. 35.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NaFlex (SigLIP2's native resolution-agnostic variant), SPAR wins decisively: 43.6 vs. 31.7 Mean₆ mIoU (+11.9), showing that NaFlex's image-level optimization does not transfer to dense prediction. Against the sliding-window teacher (stride 24), SPAR surpasses it on Mean₆ for all three backbones (+2.4 for SigLIP2, +0.8 for OpenCLIP, +0.4 for DINOv3.txt) while being 52× faster in inference. Against LPOSS, SPAR is complementary rather than competitive: LPOSS+SPAR (46.7) outperforms LPOSS+Pre-trained (42.7) by +4.0 mIoU, demonstrating that SPAR's improved spatial features propagate well through downstream label-propagation pipelines. Against AnyUp as an upsampler, SPAR+AnyUp (46.8) beats Pre-trained+AnyUp (40.9) by +5.9 mIoU on SigLIP2, confirming that SPAR's gains are orthogonal to upsampling strategy. On DINOv3.txt, where the backbone already includes RoPE and high-res fine-tuning, SPAR's gains are smaller but still positive, with the largest improvement on Cityscapes (+4.2 mIoU) where test resolution deviates most from training.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPAR positions itself as a post-hoc, architecture-free adaptation of existing ViT-based VLMs, in contrast to prior resolution-agnostic ViT work (NaViT, FlexiViT, ResFormer, NaFlex) that modifies pre-training curricula or introduces new positional encoding schemes. Conceptually, it repurposes the standard teacher-student distillation paradigm: the 'teacher' is simply the same frozen ViT run in a costly sliding-window mode, and the 'student' is the same ViT trained in single-pass mode to match the teacher's stitched feature maps via MSE regression. The method is explicitly complementary to existing OVS refinements (LPOSS, AnyUp) rather than a replacement, and it requires no architectural changes, no pixel-level labels, and no modification to the VLM's text encoder or feature-space alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 52× inference speedup over the sliding-window teacher while matching or exceeding its mIoU makes high-resolution OVS practical for real-world deployment, removing the main computational barrier that previously forced a speed-accuracy trade-off. The finding that SPAR also benefits DINOv3.txt—a model already designed with RoPE and high-res fine-tuning for resolution robustness—suggests that even state-of-the-art resolution-tolerant ViTs retain exploitable headroom for dense prediction. Open questions include how well the approach scales to very large or video inputs, whether the distillation gap widens with more aggressive stride choices, and whether the technique transfers cleanly to other dense tasks (detection, depth) without task-specific tuning.
