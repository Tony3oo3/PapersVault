## Problem

Imitative end-to-end planners suffer from multimodal mode collapse, producing near-identical trajectories despite nominally diverse outputs, while generative planners (diffusion or flow-based) lack a mechanism to enforce hard safety and physical constraints during generation, requiring a separate post-hoc optimization stage. No prior method directly embeds explicit constraint satisfaction into the generative sampling process while simultaneously preserving multimodal diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261816/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view camera images (6 cameras, 360° FOV) processed through a BEV perception module into agent and map tokens, plus dynamic conditioning signals: a plan anchor drawn from a 256-entry vocabulary (constructed via farthest-point sampling), a goal point, a one-hot driving command, and a continuous aggressiveness score (EP ∈ [0,1]). Output is a set of N=256 candidate trajectories, each a T×2 waypoint sequence, generated by integrating the learned velocity field over K=100 discrete steps from a Gaussian prior, with classifier-free guidance (masking probability 0.2) controlling how strongly conditions steer the motion.

## Method

GuideFlow builds on rectified flow matching and introduces three complementary constraint-enforcement mechanisms applied during the ODE integration: (1) Constraining the Velocity Field (CVF) projects the predicted velocity toward a constraint-satisfying reference direction via a reflection formula with small weight λ=0.1; (2) Constraining the Flow States (CF) performs a single truncation at step k_c=50, replacing the flow state with a constraint-satisfying anchor and continuing integration, activated only at inference; (3) Refining the Flow by EBM (RFE) unifies the flow model with an energy-based model whose energy is defined as the squared constraint-violation gap, trained to raise energy of violating samples and lower it for satisfying ones, enabling autonomous exploration toward feasible regions. The perception backbone encodes multi-view images into BEV features, queried into agent and map tokens that condition the velocity field via sequential cross-attention. Classifier-free guidance with a tunable scale γ allows switching between aggressive and conservative driving styles at inference by modulating the EP reward signal.

## Results

On the NavSim Navhard split, GuideFlow achieves 27.1 EPDMS without an external scorer (vs. 23.1 for TransFuser/LTF and 24.2 for DiffusionDrive), and reaches a SOTA 43.0 EPDMS when combined with the GTRS-Dense scorer, surpassing DriveSuprim (42.1) and GTRS-Dense alone (41.7). On Bench2Drive, GuideFlow attains a Driving Score of 75.21 and Success Rate of 51.36%, outperforming the Hydra-Next baseline (73.86 / 50.00%). In open-loop evaluation, GuideFlow records the lowest average collision rate: 0.07% on NuScenes and 0.73% on ADV-NuScenes, beating SparseDrive by 0.01% and 0.29% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On NavSim Navhard without a scorer, GuideFlow (27.1 EPDMS) outperforms all imitative and generative baselines: TransFuser/LTF (23.1), DiffusionDrive (24.2), and GTRS-DP (23.8), with the largest gap over LTF (+4.0). With the GTRS-Dense scorer, GuideFlow (43.0) surpasses DriveSuprim (42.1) and GTRS-Dense alone (41.7), but trails DiffVLA (45.0) by 2.0 points; however, with an adjusted scoring strategy (†), GuideFlow reaches 46.7, exceeding DiffVLA. On Bench2Drive, GuideFlow (75.21 DS / 51.36% SR) beats Hydra-Next (73.86 / 50.00%), ThinkTwice (62.44 / 31.23%), DriveAdapter (64.22 / 33.08%), UniAD (45.81 / 16.36%), and VAD (42.35 / 15.00%). In open-loop collision-rate evaluation, GuideFlow achieves the best average on both NuScenes (0.07%, tied with DIVER) and ADV-NuScenes (0.73%), outperforming SparseDrive (0.08% / 1.02%), DiffusionDrive (0.08% / 1.67%), and UniAD (0.61% / 3.95%). GuideFlow also reports the highest trajectory diversity (Div = 0.24) among no-scorer methods, confirming its advantage in mitigating mode collapse.

## Relation to Prior Work

GuideFlow sits at the intersection of three research threads: rectified flow as a fast generative backbone, classifier-free guidance for conditional control, and energy-based models for multi-modal refinement. It departs from prior generative E2E planners (DiffusionDrive, Diff-VLA, HE-Drive) by moving constraint enforcement from post-hoc scoring or implicit encoding into the generative process itself, via three explicit mechanisms (velocity-field correction, flow-state truncation, and EBM-based refinement). The unification of flow matching training with an EBM objective (RFE) is a direct adaptation of the recent Energy Matching formulation, repurposed here to make the velocity field internally aware of constraint satisfaction rather than relying on an external scorer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical deployment bottleneck: generative planners produce diverse trajectories but cannot guarantee hard safety constraints without a separate optimization stage, while imitative planners are safe but mode-collapsed. GuideFlow's constrained-generation pipeline offers a principled middle ground that could be adopted by other E2E driving stacks. However, the constraint mechanisms remain somewhat heuristic (manual anchor selection, fixed truncation step k_c, hand-crafted energy surrogate), and sampling efficiency (100 ODE steps) is a clear limitation the authors acknowledge. Open questions include whether the EBM integration can be made fully differentiable and end-to-end, whether the approach scales to richer constraint sets (e.g., multi-agent interaction constraints), and whether reflow/meanflow can reduce inference cost without sacrificing constraint compliance.
