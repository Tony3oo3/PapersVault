## Problem

Existing multi-camera multi-target tracking methods adopt a two-stage paradigm (single-camera tracking followed by inter-camera tracking) where multi-view information is used only to recover missed matches, contributing marginally to overall tracking accuracy. This design limits the tracker's ability to exploit the richer visual representations available from multiple viewpoints, especially under occlusion or appearance changes, and introduces additional error risk from treating cross-view association as an independent stage.

## Input / Output

The method takes synchronized RGB frames (1920×1080 at 30 FPS) from a set of overlapping cameras (2 moving UAVs in VisionTrack, up to 7 fixed cameras in WildTrack) and produces globally consistent target trajectories with unified IDs across all views. A CenterNet detector first extracts bounding boxes and appearance features per view; the tracker then outputs a single association result per target per frame that simultaneously resolves both temporal continuity and cross-view identity, eliminating the need for a separate inter-camera matching step.

## Method

GMT reformulates MCMT tracking as a unified global-level trajectory-target association by encoding the same target across all views into a single global trajectory, directly exploiting inter-view cues during tracking. A Cross-View Feature Consistency Enhancement (CFCE) module aligns features across views: VFCE projects visual features into a trajectory-centric space via a two-layer MLP supervised with cross-entropy, triplet, and center losses, while RPCE encodes relative positional relationships between neighboring targets (with a distance-based filtering threshold) to provide geometric consistency cues. The Global Trajectory Association (GTA) module, built on a DETR-style encoder-decoder, concatenates features from the most recent T frames with shared ID embeddings to form global trajectory representations, then uses a decoder where current target features interact with trajectory features to learn discriminative inter-trajectory cues before computing a similarity matrix resolved by the Hungarian algorithm. Training is decomposed into two stages: first the detector and VFCE are trained jointly, then the full model is trained with an association loss (softmax cross-entropy over target-trajectory similarity) and RPCE losses, using DLA-34 as backbone.

## Results

On the proposed VisionTrack dataset, GMT achieves 75.2% CVMA, 81.3% CVIDF1, 78.0% MOTA, 66.2% HOTA, 82.1% IDF1, and 69.4% ASSA, outperforming the second-best method (MvMHAT++) by 5.1% CVMA, 11.3% CVIDF1, 9.8% IDF1, and 12.2% ASSA. On the challenging WildTrack dataset (7 views, dense targets), GMT achieves 61.7% CVMA and 72.0% CVIDF1, surpassing the best baseline (CrossMOT) by 21.3% and 17.2% respectively. On MvMHAT, GMT reaches 94.1% CVMA and 95.6% CVIDF1, leading all competitors by 3.2% and 5.2% over CrossMOT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMT outperforms all five two-stage MCMT baselines (AGW, CT, MvMHATprev, CrossMOT, MvMHAT++) across all six datasets and all six metrics. The most dramatic gains appear on WildTrack, where the 7-view dense-pedestrian setting causes severe cross-view matching failures for existing methods (e.g., MvMHATprev drops to 9.4% CVMA, MvMHAT++ cannot even be trained due to memory constraints), while GMT maintains 61.7% CVMA and 72.0% CVIDF1. On the proposed VisionTrack dataset, GMT leads MvMHAT++ (the strongest two-stage baseline) by 5.1% CVMA, 11.3% CVIDF1, 9.8% IDF1, and 12.2% ASSA, confirming that the global trajectory formulation directly exploits multi-view cues rather than using them only for post-hoc correction. Against the DETR-based single-view trackers (MOTR, MOTRv2, GTR) evaluated in the ablation, GMT achieves 4.8–7.8% higher HOTA and 6.2–12.8% higher IDF1 despite slightly lower detection quality (MOTP), demonstrating that the global trajectory encoding itself—rather than better detection—drives the tracking gains. On CAMPUS, GMT achieves the best CVMA (66.4%) and CVIDF1 (66.8%) but trails MvMHATprev by 0.5% on MOTA, the only metric where a baseline edges ahead.

## Relation to Prior Work

GMT departs from the dominant two-stage MCMT paradigm (independent single-camera tracking followed by inter-camera association) by reformulating the problem as a single global-level trajectory-target association. Architecturally, it builds on DETR-based trackers (MOTR, GTR) for the association module and on metric learning (triplet/center loss) from the Re-ID literature for cross-view feature alignment, but introduces the CFCE module—combining visual feature projection with relative-position encoding—as a novel mechanism for cross-view consistency. The concept of encoding multi-frame history into trajectory representations follows GTR, but GMT extends this by merging the same target's history across all views into a unified global trajectory, eliminating the separate cross-view matching step entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The global framework addresses a well-recognized limitation of two-stage MCMT methods—multi-view information is used only marginally for error correction rather than actively during tracking—and the consistent 5–12% gains across six diverse datasets validate the reformulation. The VisionTrack dataset (116K frames, 15 scenes, 88 sequences, dual moving UAVs) fills a notable gap in scale and diversity for MCMT benchmarks. Open questions include scalability beyond two cameras, applicability to non-overlapping or sparsely overlapping views, and whether the global-trajectory approach extends to other multi-sensor tracking settings (e.g., LiDAR + camera). The framework's reliance on overlapping fields of view and the Hungarian-algorithm matching at inference may limit real-time deployment in high-camera-count scenarios.
