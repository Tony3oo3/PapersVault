## Problem

Existing agent-based methods for long-video understanding invoke external micro-tools (shot segmentation, object detection, tracking, OCR) but remain static: the toolset does not grow with experience, and the agent must repeatedly construct long chains of fine-grained operations for each task without any mechanism for accumulating or reusing multi-step perceptual structures. This leads to error accumulation over long horizons and prevents the agent from developing generalizable, higher-level video reasoning skills.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260993/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

META takes a long video (ranging from minutes to hours of footage) paired with a text question as input, and produces a text answer along with an evolved meta toolset containing both primitive micro-tools and abstracted macro-tools. The system operates under an online evaluation protocol where samples are processed sequentially and the agent updates its internal context only after completing each prediction. The Orchestrator drives up to 10 reasoning rounds with concurrency of 5 parallel Workers, each executing up to 5 rounds, with micro-tools implemented as Python-wrapped calls to Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct.

## Method

META is a training-free multi-agent framework operating through a dual Solving/Evolving loop. In the Solving loop, a frozen Orchestrator VLM decomposes the question into subtask-tool assignments, dispatches them to parallel frozen Workers that apply micro- and macro-tools to the video, and aggregates results until sufficient evidence is gathered to answer. In the Evolving loop, a Reflector performs symbolic analysis over the recorded tool trajectory: correct trajectories have their core multi-step compositions abstracted into new reusable macro-tools (with redundancy checks to prevent functional duplication), while failed trajectories are distilled into structured failure priors that refine tool-specific usage constraints. Only the meta toolset evolves across tasks—no model parameters are updated—allowing META to progressively compress long micro-tool chains into shorter, more reliable macro-level operations that reduce long-horizon error propagation and generalize across tasks.

## Results

META with Qwen3-VL-8B achieves 83.6 on MLVU (M-Avg), 77.7 on Video-MME (Avg w/o subtitles), and 63.6 on LongVideoBench (Total Avg. Acc.), setting new state-of-the-art across all three long-video benchmarks. With the smaller Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone, META scores 78.0, 73.7, and 61.9 respectively, still outperforming all prior agent-based systems. The largest absolute gains appear on LongVideoBench (+5.6 over Qwen3-VL baseline), where macro-tool compression most effectively reduces long-horizon error accumulation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

META outperforms all ten baselines on every benchmark where they overlap. Against the strongest end-to-end VLM, Qwen3-VL-Instruct (8B), META gains +5.5 on MLVU, +6.3 on Video-MME, and +5.6 on LongVideoBench. Among agent-based systems, the closest competitor is VideoLucy (7B), which META surpasses by +1.9 (MLVU), +1.2 (Video-MME), and +3.1 (LongVideoBench) under the Qwen2.5-VL backbone. The gap widens substantially against earlier agent methods: Vgent is beaten by +5.9/+4.8/+2.2 and VideoRAG by +5.6/+11.6/+3.2 across the three benchmarks. Notably, META's advantage is largest on LongVideoBench, where tasks demand stable reasoning over hundreds of shots and static micro-tool chains accumulate errors most severely.

## Relation to Prior Work

META carves new ground at the intersection of two lines of work: agent-based long-video understanding (VideoAgent, VideoRAG, Vgent, VideoLucy) and self-evolving agent systems (Alita, Memento, Agentic Context Engineering). Prior agent-based video methods treat their toolsets as fixed and static, repeatedly composing long chains of micro-tools per task without accumulating reusable perceptual skills. META repurposes the trajectory-recording mechanism already implicit in multi-step agent pipelines as a supervision signal for a symbolic evolution loop, abstracting recurring multi-step patterns into macro-tools and distilling failures into usage constraints—without any parameter updates. This positions it as the first framework to close the loop between tool execution and toolset growth in the long-video domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, model-agnostic nature of META's evolution loop makes it a practical drop-in enhancement for existing VLM-based video agents, and the demonstrated transferability of evolved macro-tools to ReAct and Plan&Execute agents suggests the distilled skills are genuinely general rather than workflow-specific. However, the evolution mechanism is currently confined to the tool level; the authors explicitly note that structural evolution of the agent architecture itself remains open. Additional open questions include how the approach scales to substantially longer videos (beyond the ~1-hour segments in LongVideoBench), whether the redundancy-checking mechanism prevents useful tool proliferation in highly diverse task distributions, and the computational overhead of running a 235B Reflector alongside the solving loop.
