## Problem

Existing vision-language-action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation rely on static, wrist-mounted cameras that provide a fixed end-effector-centric viewpoint, preventing the robot from adaptively selecting informative viewpoints or adjusting resolution during task execution. This perceptual rigidity severely limits performance in long-horizon tasks, fine-grained manipulation of small objects, and scenarios with heavy occlusion, where critical details are invisible from the default camera angle.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB-D images from calibrated cameras (reconstructed into a 3D point cloud) and a natural-language instruction. The point cloud is rendered into three orthographic projections (top, front, right), each a 7-channel image (RGB + depth + 3D world-frame coordinates) to enable cross-view pixel correspondence. The output is a single next-keyframe action comprising a 6-DoF end-effector pose in SE(3), a binary gripper open/close state, and a binary collision flag; intermediate 2D attention heatmaps are produced at both the coarse and fine stages to localize critical regions and target end-effector positions.

## Method

ActiveVLA introduces a coarse-to-fine active perception pipeline built on the PaliGemma VLM backbone (SigLIP encoder + Gemma decoder, pre-trained on 120K RoboPoint images). In the coarse stage, three orthographic projections of the 3D scene are fed to the VLM, which predicts 2D heatmaps via a learned convex upsampling block over rearranged patch tokens; these heatmaps are back-projected to identify the critical 3D region. In the fine stage, Active View Selection generates candidate camera poses on a sphere around the key region using recursive icosahedron subdivision, then scores each candidate on visibility (KDTree-based ray occlusion check), normalized distance, and angular diversity to select the top-K unoccluded, diverse viewpoints. Active 3D Zoom-in re-renders the scene from the best viewpoint with a narrowed field-of-view (zoom factor z), effectively magnifying the local region while preserving pixel resolution for precise gripper placement. Translation is predicted by back-projecting fine-stage heatmaps onto a 3D grid and taking the argmax, while rotation (Euler angles, 72 bins each), gripper state, and collision flag are predicted by an MLP head fusing global max-pooled scene tokens with ROI-sampled local tokens.

## Results

On RLBench, ActiveVLA achieves a 91.8% average success rate with an average rank of 1.22 across 18 tasks, reaching 100% on 10 tasks including Close Jar, Drag Stick, Meat off Grill, Open Drawer, and Push Buttons. On COLOSSEUM, it attains 65.9% average success rate (rank 1.07) across 14 generalization perturbations, with the highest single-scenario score of 78.3% on Table Color. On GemBench, ActiveVLA leads on levels L1–L3 (92.4%, 66.3%, 45.1%) with a 51.3% overall average, surpassing the previous best (BridgeVLA, 50.0%) by 1.3 percentage points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ActiveVLA outperforms all 13 baselines on all three benchmarks. On RLBench, it leads the closest competitor BridgeVLA (88.2%) by 3.6 percentage points and RVT-2 (81.4%) by 10.4 points, with particularly large margins on occlusion-sensitive tasks like Place Cups (65.6% vs. 58.4% for BridgeVLA) and Insert Peg (92.4% vs. 88.0%). On COLOSSEUM, the gap over BridgeVLA narrows to 1.9 points (65.9% vs. 64.0%), but ActiveVLA still wins on 12 of 14 perturbation types, including Camera Pose (76.3% vs. 73.8%) and Table Color (78.3% vs. 75.7%). On GemBench, ActiveVLA edges out BridgeVLA (51.3% vs. 50.0%) and 3D-LOTUS++ (48.0%) on the overall average, with the largest advantage on L2 (66.3% vs. 65.0% and 64.5%). Older 2D-only baselines (Image-BC, C2F-ARM-BC) and early 3D methods (PerAct, PolarNet, HiveFormer) trail by 40+ points on RLBench, underscoring the benefit of combining VLM backbones with active 3D perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

ActiveVLA builds directly on BridgeVLA's PaliGemma-based VLM backbone and its 2D-heatmap-to-3D-action prediction pipeline, but departs from the static, end-effector-centric camera assumption shared by nearly all prior VLA methods (RVT, RVT-2, 3D Diffuser Actor, Act3D, BridgeVLA). It introduces a coarse-to-fine active perception stage—multi-view orthographic projection for critical-region localization, followed by icosahedron-sampled viewpoint selection and virtual 3D zoom-in—that has no direct analogue in the existing 3D-aware manipulation literature. Conceptually, it repurposes the hypothesis-testing framing from embodied cognition (Gregory) into a concrete, differentiable rendering-and-scoring pipeline, bridging the gap between passive multi-view aggregation (RVT) and truly adaptive observation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that dynamically chosen viewpoints and local zoom-in yield consistent gains across RLBench, COLOSSEUM, and GemBench—and transfer to a real KINOVA GEN2 arm—ActiveVLA provides a practical recipe for making VLA policies more robust to occlusion and fine-grained detail without requiring additional physical cameras. However, the added perception latency (0.26 s → 0.53 s per step) and the still-very-low L4 GemBench success rate (1.2%) highlight that active perception has not yet solved long-horizon compositional generalization. Open questions include whether the icosahedron-based view search can be learned end-to-end, how the approach scales to multi-arm or mobile-base settings, and whether the virtual zoom-in advantage persists when the 3D reconstruction is noisy in unstructured real-world scenes.
