## Problem

Most remote-sensing foundation models are trained on heavily preprocessed, cloud-filtered composites or temporal averages, discarding partially-clouded observations that carry critical phenological and transient-event information. This biases learned representations toward idealized cloud-free conditions and limits robustness to the irregular, incomplete sampling inherent in operational satellite time series. TESSERA addresses this gap by learning pixel-wise embeddings that are explicitly invariant to which subset of valid observations is selected.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260620/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-temporal Sentinel-2 (13-band multispectral) and Sentinel-1 (dual-polarization SAR backscatter) observations at 10 m resolution, organized as 'd-pixels'—all spectral channels at a single spatial location over a one-year window, accompanied by binary validity masks marking cloud-free timesteps. At inference, 40 valid observations are randomly sampled (with replacement if fewer exist) per modality per pixel, and the frozen dual-encoder produces a single 128-dimensional embedding per 10 m pixel, quantized to int8 via quantization-aware training. These embeddings are released as global annual geospatial layers; downstream tasks attach lightweight heads (2-layer MLP for pixel-wise classification, small UNet for segmentation/regression) without fine-tuning the backbone.

## Method

TESSERA uses a dual-branch architecture where each modality (S1, S2) is encoded independently by a 4-layer Transformer with Day-of-Year positional encodings followed by GRU temporal pooling, producing 128-dim vectors that are fused via a 2-layer MLP into the final 128-dim embedding. Training employs the Barlow Twins objective (invariance + redundancy reduction) on two independently augmented views—each view being a random 40-step subsample of the valid observations—so the model learns representations invariant to temporal sampling. Two complementary regularizers enhance robustness: global shuffling randomizes d-pixels across all geographic tiles before batching to break spatial autocorrelation, and a mix-based loss enforces that linear interpolation in input space maps to linear interpolation in embedding space, stabilizing learning under extreme sparsity. Quantization-aware training compresses the output to int8 with negligible accuracy loss, and a 16,384-dim projector network (discarded at inference) expands the embedding for the Barlow Twins cross-correlation computation.

## Results

On TreeSatAI-TS (tree species classification), TESSERA achieves a state-of-the-art F1 of 77.96 with full labels and 60.58 at 1% labels, outperforming the next-best model AlphaEarth (76.90 / 52.79). On Austrian Crop segmentation, TESSERA reaches 53.12 mIoU (full) and 28.20 mIoU (1%), surpassing AlphaEarth by +27.4 and +13.6 points respectively. For regression, TESSERA attains the lowest RMSE on both Biomassters (27.43 t/ha) and Borneo Canopy Height (12.21 m), beating AlphaEarth by 2.16 t/ha and 3.9 m. Across all six benchmarks, TESSERA consistently leads or ties at every label ratio (1%, 30%, 100%), with the largest margins in the low-label regime.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TESSERA outperforms all 13 baselines on the majority of tasks and label ratios. Against the closest competitor AlphaEarth [13], TESSERA wins by +1.06 F1 on TreeSatAI-TS (full), +25.73 F1 on Austrian Crop classification (1%), +27.42 mIoU on Austrian Crop segmentation (full), +2.16 t/ha lower RMSE on Biomassters, and +3.9 m lower RMSE on Borneo CHM; the only narrow loss is PASTIS-R full-label segmentation (50.68 vs. 51.08 mIoU, a 0.4-point gap). Against Presto [91], TESSERA leads by +28.9 F1 on Austrian Crop (1%) and +19.08 mIoU on Austrian Crop segmentation (full). Among fine-tuned patch-based RSFMs, TESSERA surpasses the strongest (Skysense [33] at 72.24 F1 on TreeSatAI-TS, 36.41 mIoU on PASTIS-R, 30.78 RMSE on Biomassters) by +5.7 F1, +14.3 mIoU, and 3.35 t/ha respectively. The gap widens dramatically under label scarcity: at 1% labels, TESSERA leads AlphaEarth by +7.8 F1 (TreeSatAI-TS) and +28.9 F1 (Austrian Crop), while fine-tuned baselines like CROMA [31] and Scale-MAE [73] trail by 15–25 points. Overall, TESSERA's pixel-wise temporal embeddings with a lightweight head match or exceed models that require full backbone fine-tuning with 10–20× more parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

TESSERA extends the Barlow Twins formulation for cloud-corrupted EO time series introduced by Lisaius et al. [53] from single-sensor optical data to a multi-modal (Sentinel-1/2) dual-branch architecture with a novel 'd-pixel' representation that preserves full temporal phenology. It departs from the dominant patch-based RSFM paradigm (SatMAE, SkySense, CROMA, etc.) that assumes aggressive cloud filtering or temporal averaging, instead enforcing invariance to random subsets of valid observations via sparse temporal sampling. Within the 'embeddings-as-data' line (Presto, AlphaEarth), TESSERA is the first to release global, annual, 10 m pixel-wise int8 embeddings with open weights and a retrieval library, adding global shuffling and mix-based regularization as complementary robustness mechanisms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By releasing analysis-ready global pixel-wise embeddings with the GeoTessera Python library, TESSERA substantially lowers the barrier for EO practitioners who lack the compute or expertise to run large foundation models, aligning with FAIR principles. The strong label efficiency (e.g., 66 F1 at 1% labels on Austrian Crop vs. 37 for AlphaEarth) suggests that well-regularized temporal embeddings can substitute for large labeled datasets, which is critical for regions with sparse ground truth. Open questions include whether the pixel-wise formulation can match patch-based spatial context for very fine-grained segmentation, how the approach generalizes beyond Sentinel-1/2 to other sensor constellations, and whether the 40-observation minimum degrades performance in persistently cloudy tropical regions.
