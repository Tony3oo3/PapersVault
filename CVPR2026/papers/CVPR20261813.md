## Problem

Autoregressive vision-language models impose a rigid sequential, token-by-token generation process that is structurally misaligned with the parallel, object-centric nature of geospatial data. This misalignment causes systematic failures in structured-output tasks: scene descriptions anchor prematurely to the first salient entity, and multi-object detection falls into path-dependent loops that redundantly predict the same bounding box. The paper asks whether reframing geospatial text generation as a parallel, iterative refinement process can resolve these structural limitations.

## Input / Output

The model takes a remote sensing image (processed into N patch embeddings by a SigLIP-2 ViT-SO400M encoder, projected to d=4096 via a two-layer MLP) and a text prompt as input. Outputs are generated as discrete text tokens over a fixed-length template (16 tokens for captioning, 32 for detection, 8 for VQA/classification), with bounding box coordinates and class labels encoded as text. Generation proceeds over N=8 iterative refinement steps from a fully masked sequence, using greedy decoding without classifier-free guidance.

## Method

GeoDiT pairs a frozen SigLIP-2 visual backbone with a 32-layer bidirectional Transformer (LLaDA-8B, d=4096, 32 heads) as the generative core, operating under a discrete mask-and-predict diffusion objective. Training is two-stage: Stage I trains only the MLP projector on SkyScript to align visual and text embedding spaces, then Stage II fine-tunes the full model end-to-end on the MMRS-1M optical subset (34 remote sensing datasets in unified instruction format). The training loss is computed only on masked positions, where each token is independently masked with probability t sampled uniformly from [0,1]. At inference, the model starts from a fully masked template and iteratively predicts the full sequence, then applies a low-confidence remasking strategy that preserves high-confidence tokens and re-masks uncertain ones for the next refinement step. This parallel, coarse-to-fine process allows all semantic elements (object identities, spatial coordinates, scene context) to be resolved simultaneously rather than sequentially.

## Results

GeoDiT achieves state-of-the-art across all evaluated tasks. On image captioning, it reaches CIDEr 135.6 on RSICD (+17.6% over EarthDial's 115.3) and 128.3 on Sydney-Captions (+13.5% over EarthDial's 113.0). On visual grounding and detection, GeoDiT scores 60.4 VG / 20.8 mAP@0.5 on DIOR-RSVG and 63.7 VG / 24.9 mAP@0.5 on VRSBench, substantially outperforming the strongest autoregressive baselines. On VQA and classification, it attains 98.1% on RSVQA-LR-Rural and 95.0% on WHU-RS19, both new records.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoDiT outperforms all 10 baselines across every task and metric. On captioning CIDEr (the object-centric metric central to the paper's thesis), GeoDiT leads EarthDial by 17.6% on RSICD (135.6 vs. 115.3) and by 13.5% on Sydney-Captions (128.3 vs. 113.0), while also surpassing VHM (64.2→73.8 on UCM-Captions). The most dramatic gap appears in detection: GeoDiT achieves 20.8 mAP@0.5 on DIOR-RSVG and 11.4 on AVVG, whereas the best autoregressive baselines (Qwen2.5-VL, EarthDial) reach only 17.9 and 0.0 respectively, and all three general-domain diffusion baselines (LLaDA-V, LaVida, MMaDA) score 0.0 on every VG/DET benchmark. Among open-source autoregressive RS-VLMs, GeoDiT beats VHM on UCM-Captions BLEU-4 (44.7 vs. 42.4) and CIDEr (73.8 vs. 64.2), and beats EarthDial on RSICD CIDEr (135.6 vs. 115.3) and NWPU-Captions BLEU-4 (62.2 vs. 35.9). On VQA, GeoDiT leads GeoChat (98.1 vs. 96.2 on LR-Rural) and VHM (90.2 vs. 86.3 on LR-Comparison), and on classification it surpasses VHM (95.0 vs. 91.8 on WHU-RS19). Commercial models (GPT-4V, Claude-4) are far behind on all geospatial-specific benchmarks, confirming that domain-specific architecture matters more than raw model scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoDiT departs from the dominant autoregressive RS-VLM lineage (GeoChat, VHM, EarthDial) by replacing sequential token-by-token generation with a parallel, iterative mask-and-predict diffusion process. Architecturally, it repurposes the LLaDA-8B bidirectional transformer—originally designed for general-domain discrete text diffusion—as its generative core, pairing it with a SigLIP-2 visual backbone and a lightweight MLP projector. The paper positions itself as the first systematic adaptation of diffusion-based text generation (previously explored in general-domain systems like LLaDA-V, LaViDA, and MMaDA) to the structurally distinct, non-narrative semantics of remote sensing imagery.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work provides the first evidence that aligning the generative process with the parallel, object-centric structure of geospatial scenes yields substantial gains on structured-output tasks, particularly where autoregressive models exhibit path-dependent failure modes (e.g., repeated bounding boxes). However, absolute detection performance (20.8 mAP on DIOR-RSVG) remains far below dedicated detection pipelines, and the iterative inference (N=8 steps) introduces latency that may limit real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the advantage persists for truly narrative or long-form geospatial reasoning, whether the approach generalizes beyond optical imagery to SAR or multi-sensor inputs, and whether the LLaDA-8B backbone is the optimal foundation or whether purpose-built geospatial diffusion backbones would close the remaining gap.
