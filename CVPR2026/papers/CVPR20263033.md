## Problem

Pose graph initialization in SfM pipelines relies on per-image k-nearest-neighbor retrieval, evaluating each candidate edge in isolation and ignoring global structural consistency. This greedy, pairwise approach produces fragmented or elongated graphs with weakly coupled substructures, and because edges are rarely added after initialization, missed connections are lost permanently. The paper asks whether edges can be ranked by their global utility for SfM rather than by local visual similarity alone.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263033/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a set of N RGB images, encodes each into a fixed-length descriptor via MegaLoc (frozen DINOv2 backbone with SALAD aggregation), and constructs a complete graph over these embeddings. It outputs a continuous edge rank score for every candidate pair, which drives the selection of a sparse initial pose graph (the union of k minimum spanning trees) for downstream COLMAP geometric verification. For collections exceeding 500 images, METIS graph partitioning splits the complete graph into subgraphs to fit GPU memory, with overlapping predictions averaged to recover the global rank matrix.

## Method

A two-iteration edge-node GNN performs message passing over the complete image graph, allowing each edge to aggregate context from all other pairs before a prediction MLP outputs a scalar rank. The model is trained with self-supervised, geometry-derived labels: for each pair, RANSAC inlier count and jointly visible 3D point count are normalized and averaged to form a continuous target, optimized via a differentiable NDCG ranking loss (NDCGLoss2++) rather than regression. At inference, edge weights are set to 1 minus the predicted rank, and k MSTs are computed iteratively via Kruskal's algorithm with previously selected edges masked out, yielding a sparse yet redundantly connected pose graph. A connectivity-aware score modulation step then blends each edge's predicted rank with its normalized shortest-path distance in the current partial graph, boosting edges that bridge weakly connected regions and reducing overall graph diameter before the next MST is extracted.

## Results

On IMC23-PhotoTourism, the proposed method achieves the highest AUC@2.5° and AUC@5° across all MST counts (k=1–5), with the largest margins at k=1 and k=2 where pose graphs are extremely sparse. On VisymScenes, the method reaches 75.6% AUC@5° at k=5 (with modulation), exceeding MegaLoc (74.7%) and outperforming DoppelGanger++ filtering (70.7%). On MegaDepth, the method is consistently best across all k values. The method also lies on the Pareto frontier for AUC@5° versus COLMAP runtime, achieving higher accuracy than all baselines at comparable or lower mapping times.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four retrieval baselines (MegaLoc, CosPlace, DINOv2-SALAD, AnyLoc) on IMC23-PhotoTourism, MegaDepth, and VisymScenes, with the largest gaps in the sparse regime (k=1–2 MSTs) where global reasoning is most critical. On VisymScenes at k=5, the method achieves 75.6% AUC@5° versus MegaLoc's 74.7% (with modulation) or 70.5% (without), and notably surpasses DoppelGanger++ (70.7%) despite operating before geometric verification. A key finding is that all baselines improve substantially under the multi-MST selection framework compared to their native kNN-based selection, indicating that MST construction itself provides a strong structural prior. The method also achieves the best accuracy at lower or comparable COLMAP runtimes, placing it on the Pareto frontier. Applying DoppelGanger++ on top of the proposed scores yields no further benefit, suggesting the GNN predictor inherently suppresses misleading doppelganger edges.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant kNN-retrieval paradigm for pose graph initialization by reframing the problem as global edge ranking. It repurposes GNN message passing (following the edge-initialization scheme of Turkoglu et al. [45]) to predict edge reliability from image embeddings, supervised by geometry-derived signals (RANSAC inliers and 3D point overlap) rather than retrieval labels. The multi-MST construction builds on prior single-tree strategies in Light3R-SfM [17] and Barath et al. [3], but extends them to multiple complementary trees with a novel distance-based score modulation that has no direct precedent in the SfM literature. The ranking loss (NDCG-Loss2++) is borrowed from information retrieval, and the overall pipeline sits upstream of existing GNN-based pose graph optimization methods like PoGO-Net [29] and Damblon et al. [15], which operate after geometric verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Pose graph initialization is a well-recognized bottleneck in SfM where early mistakes are irreversible, and this work demonstrates that integrating global reasoning at the very first stage yields more compact, well-connected graphs with fewer verified edges. The practical impact is significant: the method operates before any geometric verification, reducing computational cost, and it generalizes to ambiguous scenes (VisymScenes) without requiring dedicated doppelganger filtering. Open questions include how the approach interacts with incremental SfM pipelines (where all images are not available upfront), whether the GNN can be made streaming or approximate for very large collections, and whether the geometry-derived supervision transfers to domains with different camera motion patterns (e.g., video, drone footage). The reliance on a complete graph over all image embeddings, even with METIS partitioning, limits scalability to extremely large datasets.
