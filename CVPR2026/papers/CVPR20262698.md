## Problem

Rectified flow models require dozens of iterative sampling steps, making real-time deployment impractical. Existing training-free acceleration methods rely on caching and reusing intermediate features, but this static-cache/dynamic-input mismatch causes the approximated output to fail to respond to the current input state, degrading visual fidelity as errors accumulate across steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262698/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

VDE is a drop-in replacement for the sampling loop of rectified flow models (Flux, Qwen-Image, Wan2.1), taking the same text prompt and producing the same output format: 512×512 or up to 1024×1024 images, or 81-frame videos at 832×480. It reduces the number of full model forward passes (NFE) from 50 to 16–24 depending on the chosen anchor interval, while the per-step computation for estimated steps is negligible arithmetic on the current latent.

## Method

VDE decomposes the model-predicted velocity v_t into a component parallel to the input latent (scalar α_t) and an orthogonal component (scalar β_t times a unit direction u_t). Empirical analysis across 500 samples per model reveals that during a stable phase, α_t and β_t exhibit strong local linearity (enabling two-step linear extrapolation with <2% error), while u_t remains nearly constant over adjacent steps (cosine similarity >0.99, enabling safe reuse). At periodic anchor steps, VDE performs a full forward pass to obtain the ground-truth decomposition; for non-anchor steps, it extrapolates the scalar coefficients from the two most recent anchors and reuses the latest orthogonal direction, then synthesizes the velocity as α̂_t·x_t + β̂_t·‖x_t‖·û_t. Because the estimated velocity is constructed from the current input x_t at every step, VDE is inherently input-adaptive, fundamentally eliminating the cache-input mismatch that plagues prior caching-based methods.

## Results

On FLUX.1 [dev], VDE-fast achieves a 3.01× speedup (16 NFEs) with SSIM 0.8267 and LPIPS 0.1997, substantially outperforming EasyCache-fast (2.91×, SSIM 0.7240, LPIPS 0.3197) at nearly identical latency. On Qwen-Image, VDE-fast (2.70×, 18 NFEs) reaches SSIM 0.8967 and LPIPS 0.1096, while VDE-slow (2.04×, 24 NFEs) achieves LPIPS 0.0691—a 52.2% reduction over the best baseline (EasyCache-slow, LPIPS 0.1445). On video generation with Wan2.1, VDE-fast (2.50×, 20 NFEs) attains SSIM 0.8658 and LPIPS 0.0754, surpassing EasyCache (2.54×, SSIM 0.8337, LPIPS 0.0952) while maintaining a VBench score of 80.43% close to the full 50-step baseline (81.30%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VDE consistently outperforms all caching-based baselines on visual retention metrics at comparable or better speedups. On FLUX.1, VDE-fast (3.01×) improves SSIM by 14.2% and reduces LPIPS by 37.5% over EasyCache-fast (2.91×), while VDE-slow (2.21×) beats EasyCache-slow (2.09×) by 19.5% in SSIM and 55.4% in LPIPS. On Qwen-Image, the gap widens dramatically: VDE-fast (2.70×) achieves SSIM 0.8967 vs. EasyCache-fast's 0.7027 (+27.6%) and LPIPS 0.1096 vs. 0.4239 (−74.1%), and VDE-slow's LPIPS of 0.0691 represents a 52.2% reduction over the best baseline. TeaCache performs worst among baselines on Qwen-Image (SSIM 0.5596, LPIPS 0.4773 for the fast variant), confirming the cache-input mismatch problem. On video generation (Wan2.1), VDE-fast (2.50×) edges out EasyCache (2.54×) in SSIM (0.8658 vs. 0.8337) and LPIPS (0.0754 vs. 0.0952), while PAB (1.72×) lags significantly in both speed and quality (SSIM 0.6484, LPIPS 0.3010). VDE-slow on Qwen-Image matches the full T=50 baseline's ImageReward (1.295), indicating near-lossless perceptual quality at 2.04× speedup.

## Relation to Prior Work

VDE departs from the dominant caching-and-reusing paradigm in training-free acceleration (TeaCache, EasyCache, PAB, Δ-DiT, T-GATE, DeepCache), which stores and replays intermediate features across timesteps. Rather than reusing stale features, VDE reframes acceleration as estimating the model's velocity output directly from the current input by decomposing it into parallel and orthogonal components relative to the latent. This shifts the problem from 'which features to cache and when to reuse them' to 'how to analytically propagate the velocity's decomposed components forward in time,' eliminating the cache-input mismatch that the authors identify as the root cause of fidelity degradation in prior methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 3.22× speedup on Flux with near-lossless visual quality (LPIPS 0.069 on Qwen-Image, matching the T=50 baseline's ImageReward) makes VDE a practical drop-in accelerator for production deployment of rectified flow models. The method's simplicity—linear extrapolation of two scalars plus reuse of one unit vector—means it adds negligible overhead and is easy to integrate. Open questions include whether the stable-phase regularity holds for non-DiT architectures, for models with classifier-free guidance or multi-step conditioning, and whether the fixed warm-up heuristic generalizes across prompt difficulty distributions without per-sample profiling.
