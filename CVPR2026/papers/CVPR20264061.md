## Problem

Existing Chain-of-Thought reasoning for Vision-and-Language Navigation either restricts reasoning to the textual modality (lacking visual perception) or generates imagined visual observations in pixel space, causing severe token inflation (3k–5k tokens per reasoning step vs. <500 for textual CoT) that makes real-time navigation infeasible. No prior framework unifies textual, visual, and multimodal CoT paradigms within a single model while preserving inference efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264061/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language instruction and a sequence of multi-view RGB observations accumulated over timesteps in a continuous 3D environment, and outputs a sequence of navigation actions (including a stop action). A binary gating pair (g_T, g_V) selects among four reasoning modes at each step, and during visual CoT the model predicts early-scale VAR latent tokens (≈a dozen per image at scale 4) rather than full-resolution pixel frames, keeping the autoregressive sequence compact.

## Method

FANTASYVLN introduces a unified implicit reasoning framework built on a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL) with a frozen pretrained VAR model providing a compact latent space for visual CoT. A gating mechanism with two binary signals routes the model through four modes—non-CoT, textual CoT, compact visual CoT, and multimodal CoT—under shared parameters, with stochastic mode sampling during training. A cross-mode alignment constraint alternately optimizes the non-CoT pathway against ground-truth actions and then aligns all CoT-mode action predictions to the non-CoT soft targets via cross-entropy, enforcing modality-invariant reasoning representations. At inference, the agent performs direct instruction-to-action mapping (non-CoT mode) without generating any explicit CoT tokens, achieving reasoning-aware navigation at ~1.03 actions/second versus 0.19 for explicit pixel-space visual CoT.

## Results

On the LH-VLN benchmark, FANTASYVLN achieves state-of-the-art performance across all four metrics: SR=2.44, ISR=11.01, CSR=9.64, and CGT=8.99. It is the only method to achieve a non-zero Success Rate, with the next-best method (Aux-Think) reaching only SR=0.65 and ISR=3.16. In terms of inference efficiency, FANTASYVLN achieves 1.03 actions per second (APS) with a 7B model, matching other implicit-reasoning methods and operating approximately 5× faster than the explicit CoT-VLA (0.19 APS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FANTASYVLN decisively outperforms all four baselines on every metric. Against Aux-Think (textual CoT, NeurIPS 2025), the closest competitor, FANTASYVLN improves SR from 0.65 to 2.44 (3.7×), ISR from 3.16 to 11.01 (3.5×), CSR from 2.04 to 9.64 (4.7×), and CGT from 1.47 to 8.99 (6.1×). Against the memory-based MGDM (CVPR 2025), FANTASYVLN is the only method to achieve any full-task success (SR 2.44 vs. 0) and nearly quintuples ISR (11.01 vs. 2.34). Both pixel-space visual CoT methods—CoT-VLA (CVPR 2025) and WorldVLA (arXiv 2025)—score zero across all metrics, which the authors attribute to the difficulty of generating future navigation frames in continuous 3D scenes with limited training data. On inference efficiency, FANTASYVLN (1.03 APS) matches WorldVLA (1.02) and Aux-Think (0.97) while being roughly 5× faster than explicit CoT-VLA (0.19 APS), confirming that the compact latent-space visual CoT eliminates the token-inflation bottleneck of pixel-level generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

FANTASYVLN extends the train-with-CoT, infer-without-CoT paradigm of Aux-Think from textual-only reasoning to a unified multimodal setting, and repurposes the VAR latent space (originally designed for scalable image generation) as a compact representation for visual CoT, replacing the pixel-space decoding used by CoT-VLA and WorldVLA. The multimodal CoT formulation follows the paired textual-visual chain concept from Visual Thoughts (Cheng et al.), while the gating mechanism and cross-mode alignment constraint are novel architectural contributions that bind all four reasoning modes (non-CoT, textual, visual, multimodal) into a single shared-parameter model. The work departs from prior explicit CoT navigation methods (NavCoT, NavGPT-2, OmniNav) by eliminating inference-time CoT generation entirely, and from pixel-space visual CoT by compressing imagined observations into a dozen latent tokens rather than hundreds of pixel tokens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and addresses a concrete bottleneck—token inflation in multimodal CoT—that has prevented visual and multimodal reasoning from being practically deployable in real-time navigation, and the 4.6× training speedup from latent-space CoT is a tangible efficiency gain. However, absolute performance remains very low (SR = 2.44% on LH-VLN), the evaluation is confined to a single benchmark with limited training data, and the base model (Qwen2.5-VL) lacks native image generation, capping the visual CoT ceiling. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger datasets, whether reinforcement learning can replace supervised CoT annotation, and whether an adaptive VAR scale mechanism could unlock multi-level visual reasoning. The complete failure of pixel-space visual CoT baselines (SR = 0) in navigation, contrasted with their success in manipulation, suggests a fundamental modality-task mismatch that warrants further investigation.
