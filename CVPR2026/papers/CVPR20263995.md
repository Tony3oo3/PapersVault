## Problem

Cache-based test-time adaptation for large multimodal 3D vision-language models suffers from two fundamental limitations: fixed-size caches cause progressive information loss as the test stream evolves, and the fusion between cached and zero-shot logits relies on empirically tuned hyperparameters, making adaptation unstable across domains. This paper asks whether a principled, distribution-based formulation can replace discrete sample caching to achieve stable, training-free adaptation under distribution shifts.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a streaming sequence of point clouds (uniformly sampled to 1,024 points and normalized to the unit sphere) alongside a fixed set of text class prompts (e.g., 'a 3D object of [classname]'), which are expanded by an LLM into M paraphrases per class to capture semantic variability. A frozen point encoder and a frozen text encoder project both modalities into a shared d-dimensional embedding space. The output is a unified posterior probability distribution over C classes, from which the argmax yields the predicted class label, with no gradient-based updates at any point.

## Method

BayesMM models each class's textual and geometric features as Gaussian distributions and fuses them via Bayesian model averaging. Textual distributions are computed offline from LLM-generated prompt paraphrases (empirical mean and covariance), providing class-wise semantic priors. Geometric distributions are initialized from the textual prototypes and then updated online at each time step via a closed-form recursive Bayesian update (Eq. 12), where the prior at step t is the posterior at step t−1, enabling continual adaptation without storing individual samples. The final class posterior is a weighted sum of the textual and geometric GDA posteriors, where the weights are the respective marginal evidences p(Ω^c|x_t) and p(Θ_t^c|x_t), automatically balancing modality contributions based on posterior evidence rather than hand-tuned hyperparameters. The entire pipeline is training-free: all encoders remain frozen, and adaptation occurs solely through distribution parameter updates.

## Results

On ModelNet-C (7 corruption types, severity level 2), BayesMM improves average recognition accuracy by +10.82% for ULIP (48.60→59.42%), +5.60% for ULIP-2 (59.61→65.21%), +3.74% for OpenShape (74.87→78.61%), and +6.87% for Uni3D (69.69→76.56%). On the multi-benchmark generalization suite (ModelNet40, ScanObjectNN S-PB_RS_T50, Objaverse-LVIS, OmniObject3D), BayesMM yields +3.63% to +4.23% average gains across all four backbones. Inference throughput is preserved at >97% of zero-shot speed, and memory overhead is negligible (e.g., only +4 MB for Uni3D scaling from 40 to 1,156 classes).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BayesMM is compared against two Point-Cache variants (Global and Hierarchical) and four zero-shot multimodal 3D backbones (ULIP, ULIP-2, OpenShape, Uni3D) across all evaluation settings. BayesMM consistently outperforms both Point-Cache variants on every corruption type and clean data across all four backbones; for example, on ModelNet-C it beats Point-Cache (Hierarchical) by +4.40% (ULIP), +0.96% (ULIP-2), +1.09% (OpenShape), and +1.93% (Uni3D) in average accuracy. The largest absolute gains over the zero-shot baseline occur for ULIP (+10.82%) and Uni3D (+6.87%), suggesting that weaker backbones benefit most from the Bayesian distribution learning. On the generalization suite (Table 2), BayesMM also wins on all four datasets for every backbone, with the most notable improvement on ScanObjectNN S-PB_RS_T50 (e.g., +8.52% for OpenShape: 54.60→66.12%). In the Sim-to-Real setting (Table 3), BayesMM achieves the highest average accuracy for ULIP (60.38%) and OpenShape (68.00%), while Uni3D with BayesMM (70.94%) slightly trails Point-Cache Global (71.01%), indicating a near-tie for the strongest backbone. Overall, BayesMM wins or ties on 23 of 24 backbone–setting combinations, with the single exception being Uni3D on Sim-to-Real where it is within 0.07% of the best Point-Cache variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

BayesMM directly extends the cache-based test-time adaptation paradigm of Point-Cache (Sun et al., CVPR 2025) by replacing discrete sample storage with continuous Gaussian distribution modeling, addressing the information-loss problem inherent in fixed-size caches. It also generalizes the 2D distributional TTA framework of DOTA (Han et al., 2024) from vision-language models to multimodal 3D point cloud recognition, adding a textual modality modeled via LLM-generated prompt paraphrases (building on the authors' prior prompt distribution learning work). The fusion mechanism draws on Bayesian model averaging (Park, 2025) to replace the heuristic logit-weighting (λ, γ) used in cache-based methods with principled posterior-evidence-based modality balancing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, low-overhead nature of BayesMM makes it practically attractive for deploying frozen 3D foundation models in shifting real-world environments (autonomous driving, robotics) where retraining is infeasible. The Bayesian formulation eliminates the need for manually tuned fusion hyperparameters, which is a meaningful step toward robustness across domains. However, the Gaussian assumption may be limiting for highly multimodal or complex 3D shape distributions, and the evaluation is confined to classification—open questions remain about whether this distributional approach transfers to detection, segmentation, or open-vocabulary tasks. The modest but consistent gains (+3.7 to +10.8 points) across four backbones and multiple corruption types suggest the framework is a solid building block rather than a paradigm shift.
