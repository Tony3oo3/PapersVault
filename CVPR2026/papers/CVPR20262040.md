## Problem

Reinforcement learning for text-to-image generation relies on pre-trained scalar reward models (e.g., PickScore, HPS, OCR) that are vulnerable to reward hacking, where the generator exploits specific biases (oversaturated colors, excessive text emphasis) to inflate scores without genuine quality improvement. KL regularization partially mitigates this but constrains optimization and degrades performance, and the inherent biases of each reward model persist regardless of optimization strategy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated image via the SD3 flow-matching generator. During training, each prompt yields a group of 16 samples, and 8 reference images per prompt (generated by Qwen-Image) serve as positive supervision for the adversarial reward model. The reward signal can be a scalar from a human-preference model, a rule-based metric (OCR accuracy), or dense visual features from a frozen DINOv2 backbone with a lightweight classification head.

## Method

Adv-GRPO extends GRPO to an adversarial setting where the generator and reward model are jointly optimized: the generator maximizes rewards via the standard clipped GRPO objective, while the reward model is trained as a discriminator to distinguish high-quality reference images (positives) from generated samples (negatives). For human-preference rewards, adversarial fine-tuning is triggered when the average reward of generated images surpasses that of references, signaling reward hacking. For non-differentiable rule-based rewards (OCR), a multi-reward formulation combines the task-specific score with CLIP similarity to reference images. The key extension uses a frozen DINOv2 backbone with a trainable classification head that produces dense visual rewards at both global ([CLS] token) and local (randomly sampled patch tokens) levels, trained with a hinge loss to discriminate references from generations, providing richer and less biased guidance than any single scalar reward.

## Results

Under the PickScore reward, Adv-GRPO achieves a PickScore of 22.78, matching Flow-GRPO (22.82) and substantially outperforming the SD3 base (21.70). Under the OCR reward, both Adv-GRPO and Flow-GRPO reach 0.91 accuracy versus 0.58 for SD3. When using DINO as the reward, Adv-GRPO improves OCR accuracy from 0.59 to 0.69 and GenEval from 0.61 to 0.69 over SD3, while also raising PickScore from 21.70 to 21.90. In human evaluation, Adv-GRPO achieves 70.0% and 85.3% win rates in image quality and aesthetics over Flow-GRPO, and 72.4% in aesthetics over SD3 under the DINO reward.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the SD3 base model, Adv-GRPO matches or exceeds benchmark scores (PickScore 22.78 vs 21.70; OCR 0.91 vs 0.58) while achieving large human-evaluation gains: 70.0% win rate in image quality under PickScore and 85.3% in aesthetics under OCR. Against Flow-GRPO, Adv-GRPO ties on quantitative benchmarks (PickScore 22.78 vs 22.82; OCR 0.91 vs 0.91) but decisively wins in human evaluation, reaching 70.0% win rate in quality (PickScore) and 85.3% in aesthetics (OCR), indicating that Flow-GRPO's higher benchmark scores come at the cost of perceptual quality due to reward hacking. Under the DINO reward setting, Adv-GRPO outperforms Flow-GRPO (DINO similarity) with 66.3% win rate in quality and 75.2% in aesthetics, and achieves a 93.5% win rate in quality over Flow-GRPO (PickScore), demonstrating that the adversarial DINO reward provides more holistic guidance than scalar similarity or preference-based rewards.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Flow-GRPO, which reformulates diffusion/flow-matching sampling as an SDE-based MDP for GRPO optimization, by introducing an adversarial co-training loop where the reward model is jointly updated with the generator. The GRPO algorithm itself is inherited from DeepSeekMath's group-relative policy optimization, and the adversarial discriminator formulation draws on GAN-style minimax training, but repurposed to keep a learned reward model honest rather than to generate images. The key conceptual departure from prior RL-based T2I work (DanceGRPO, Flow-GRPO) is treating the reward model as a trainable discriminator supervised by reference images, and further replacing scalar preference rewards with dense visual-foundation-model (DINO) features as the reward signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reward hacking is a fundamental bottleneck for RL-based image generation, and this paper offers a practical, training-time remedy (adversarial reward re-alignment) that avoids the performance cost of KL regularization. The finding that a frozen DINO backbone with a lightweight classification head provides a more comprehensive and bias-free reward than any single scalar preference model is a useful recipe for practitioners. Limitations include reliance on Qwen-Image-generated reference images (a potential circularity), evaluation only on SD3 at 1000 iterations, and the adversarial loop's long-term stability at scale remaining untested. Open questions include whether this framework transfers to video generation, whether the DINO reward generalizes beyond the tested benchmarks, and whether the reference-image requirement can be eliminated.
