## Problem

Most shape completion methods optimize pointwise discrepancies, recovering local geometry but leaving the structural regularities needed for downstream primitive assembly underconstrained. The prevailing 'complete first, assemble later' cascade is fragile because assembly solvers expect structured, complete primitives that pointwise completion does not provide, and early errors in primitive count or parameters propagate into the association step. The paper asks how to jointly predict primitives and points in a single coordinated pass so that the output is directly assembly-ready.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260330/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a partial point cloud (2,048 input points downsampled from scans at 25%, 50%, or 75% incompleteness on synthetic benchmarks; native point counts on Building-PCC LiDAR) and produces two outputs in one feed-forward pass: a dense completed point set (8,192 target points) and a fixed set of K quadratic primitives, each carrying a 4×4 symmetric geometry matrix, a semantic label (plane, cylinder, sphere, cone, or empty), and a per-point inlier membership vector. At inference, a confidence score filters valid primitives, which are then passed directly to assembly solvers (PrimFit, PolyFit, KSR, COMPOD) without any intermediate extraction step.

## Method

UniCo decouples point completion and primitive inference into two coordinated pathways that share latent shape features. The point pathway (instantiated with AdaPoinTr) decodes dense completed points, while the primitive pathway uses a fixed set of learnable primitive proxies that are contextualized via cross-attention to the shared features followed by self-attention among proxies, yielding unified primitive-level representations decoded by separate heads for geometry, semantics, and membership. Because predicted points evolve during training, the paper introduces online target updates: at each iteration, ground-truth primitive labels are assigned to current predictions via nearest-neighbor matching and majority vote, and the resulting membership targets are recomputed. An unordered set of predicted primitives is aligned to ground truth through a bipartite Hungarian matching that combines semantic, membership (cross-entropy + Dice), and geometry (Chamfer + L1 parameter) costs, producing a self-consistent optimization loop that the ablations show is critical (removing online targets increases CD by ~5×).

## Results

On ABC-multi with the PrimFit assembly solver, UniCo achieves CD 2.18, HD 7.53, NC 0.935, and FR 1.49%, reducing Chamfer distance by 40–50% over the strongest pointwise-completion-plus-extractor baseline (ODGNet + HPNet: CD 4.33, HD 13.63, NC 0.873, FR 7.41%). On ABC-plane, UniCo reaches CD 1.63 and NC 0.952 with COMPOD (zero failure rate), outperforming the cascaded PaCo baseline (CD 1.94, NC 0.940, FR 0.25%). On real LiDAR scans (Building-PCC), UniCo attains the best CD across all three solvers (e.g., 3.84 with PolyFit vs. 3.97 for ODGNet) and the lowest failure rates (0.39% vs. 0.87%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniCo outperforms all five pointwise completion baselines (GRNet, PoinTr, AdaPoinTr, ODGNet, SymmComplete) by a wide margin on reconstruction quality: the best baseline (ODGNet + HPNet) achieves CD 4.33 and NC 0.873, while UniCo reaches CD 2.18 and NC 0.935, a roughly 50% reduction in Chamfer distance and a 6.2-point gain in normal consistency. Notably, UniCo's pointwise CD (0.686) is actually worse than ODGNet (0.632) and SymmComplete (0.487), yet its structured primitives yield far superior assembly results, demonstrating that pointwise fidelity does not translate to reconstruction quality. Against the cascaded structured baseline PaCo on ABC-plane, UniCo improves CD from 1.94 to 1.63 and eliminates all solver failures (FR 0.25% → 0.00%) with COMPOD. On real Building-PCC scans, UniCo beats ODGNet by 3.3% CD with PolyFit (3.84 vs. 3.97) and reduces the failure rate from 0.87% to 0.39%, while SymmComplete degrades severely (CD 7.55, FR 24.98%). Against direct reconstruction methods (BSP-Net, Point2CAD, CAPRI-Net), UniCo lowers CD by 36–74% and HD by 37–70%, confirming that structured completion plus assembly dominates both direct mesh reconstruction and completion-then-reconstruction pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniCo departs from the prevailing 'complete first, assemble later' cascade (exemplified by PaCo for plane-only primitives) by jointly optimizing point completion and primitive inference in coordinated pathways sharing latent features. It repurposes the DETR-style learnable query mechanism as 'primitive proxies' that attend to shared shape features, adapting an instance-segmentation paradigm to the completion setting where targets are not fixed. The point pathway instantiates AdaPoinTr as its backbone, while the primitive pathway introduces quadric-parameter geometry heads, semantic classifiers, and membership predictors driven by the contextualized proxies. The online target update strategy with permutation-invariant Hungarian matching addresses a training challenge specific to joint completion-primitive learning that prior cascaded or fixed-target methods do not face.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By producing assembly-ready primitives directly from partial scans, UniCo eliminates the error-prone intermediate step of running a separate primitive extractor on completed points, yielding 40–50% lower Chamfer distance and zero solver failure rates on plane-only benchmarks. The finding that superior pointwise completion metrics do not translate to better assembly quality challenges the implicit assumption in the field that minimizing pointwise error is sufficient for downstream structured tasks. Limitations include dependence on the downstream solver's quality, restriction to parametric primitive families (planes, cylinders, spheres, cones), and inapplicability to highly unstructured geometry. Open questions include whether the emergent proxy-level semantics observed during training can be exploited for part-aware assembly, and whether the approach scales to larger scenes or richer primitive families.
