## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods suffer from a common pattern imbalance: coreset-based approaches either overemphasize class-general patterns (heuristic methods) or marginal patterns (importance-based methods), while synthetic-based methods inherently skew toward prevalent class-general patterns during optimization. No prior method explicitly models the multi-pattern structure within each class or enforces a balanced selection between these two complementary types of visual information.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262509/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a labeled RGB image dataset (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet, 224×224 for ImageNet-1K) and an IPC budget as input, and produces a pattern-balanced coreset — an index set of selected real images — along with a distilled model trained on that coreset. A key practical property is 'Optimize-Once-for-All-IPCs': the multi-pattern distribution modeling (Stage 1) runs only once, and different IPC budgets require only re-selection of samples without re-optimization, making it substantially cheaper than synthetic-based methods that must re-run optimization per IPC.

## Method

BPS operates in three stages. Stage 1 trains a MoCo v2 encoder to model the multi-pattern distribution within each class via a hierarchical semantic structure (instance → pattern → class), combining InfoNCE contrastive loss (minimizing intra-pattern radius), Infomap-based adaptive clustering with a custom clustering loss (maximizing inter-pattern separability), and cross-entropy (ensuring inter-class separation). Stage 2 constructs the coreset by allocating half the IPC budget to pattern-center samples (closest to each discovered pattern centroid, representing class-general patterns) and the other half to pattern-margin samples (identified via low prediction confidence from a full-data model, filtered through a noisy-label learning procedure to retain only informative hard samples). Stage 3 trains the final model on the coreset via knowledge distillation, minimizing KL divergence between the student's output and soft labels produced by the full-data teacher. The approach is model-agnostic in selection, and theoretical guarantees are provided via fill-distance bounds showing the coreset covers the original data manifold and the empirical risk gap is bounded.

## Results

BPS achieves the best top-1 accuracy across all four benchmarks and all IPC budgets. On CIFAR-10, BPS reaches 48.8% (IPC=10) and 93.2% (IPC=500), surpassing the second-best method by 5.8 and 0.7 points respectively. On ImageNet-1K, BPS attains 52.4% (IPC=10) and 63.5% (IPC=50), improving over the runner-up by 2.5 and 1.5 points. On Tiny-ImageNet at IPC=50, BPS narrows the gap to the full-data model (60.5%) to just 1.5 percentage points using only 5% of the data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BPS outperforms all nine baselines across every dataset and IPC setting. The strongest competitors are CCFS (synthetic-based, 92.5% on CIFAR-10 IPC=500), DCS (generative-based, 90.3% on CIFAR-10 IPC=500), and RDED (coreset-based, 88.7% on CIFAR-10 IPC=500); BPS leads these by 0.7, 2.9, and 4.5 points respectively at that setting. At low IPC (10), the margins are larger: BPS beats CCFS by 14.7 points on CIFAR-10 (48.8 vs 34.1) and by 2.5 points on ImageNet-1K (52.4 vs 49.9). Coreset-based baselines (Herding, Forgetting, Glister) trail significantly, with Herding achieving only 20.1% on CIFAR-10 IPC=10 versus BPS's 48.8%. In the cross-architecture generalization study (Table 2, ImageNet-1K IPC=10), BPS leads all six evaluation architectures, with the largest gap on ViT-Small (30.2% vs 24.0% for CCFS, a 6.2-point margin), confirming its model-agnostic advantage over synthetic-based methods that inherit architecture-specific biases.

## Relation to Prior Work

BPS carves new ground by unifying ideas from contrastive representation learning (MoCo v2), adaptive graph-based clustering (Infomap), and scattered data approximation theory (fill distance) into a single coreset-selection framework, rather than building on any single prior DD method. It explicitly departs from the prevailing assumption in both coreset-based and synthetic-based DD that each class forms a single cluster, instead modeling a multi-pattern (GMM) structure within each class. The method also repurposes knowledge distillation not as the primary compression mechanism (as in synthetic-based DD) but as a lightweight Stage-3 training objective on an already-selected, pattern-balanced index set, making the entire pipeline model-agnostic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'pattern imbalance' diagnosis provides a unifying lens that explains why both heuristic-based and importance-based coreset methods, as well as synthetic-image optimization, systematically underperform: they all skew toward class-general patterns at the expense of marginal ones. The Optimize-Once-for-All-IPCs property and model-agnostic selection are practically significant for NAS and multi-architecture evaluation pipelines where re-optimizing synthetic images per architecture is prohibitive. The authors acknowledge that uniform center/margin allocation may be suboptimal for classes with heterogeneous pattern structures, pointing toward class-adaptive balancing as a natural next step. Open questions include whether the GMM-based pattern modeling scales gracefully to extremely fine-grained or open-vocabulary settings, and whether the fill-distance theoretical guarantees tighten under non-Gaussian pattern geometries.
