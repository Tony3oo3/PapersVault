## Problem

Existing Decoding-Time Intervention (DTI) methods for reducing hallucination in LVLMs inadvertently amplify the severity of residual hallucinations through autoregressive error accumulation (the 'snowball' effect). This stems from three limitations: DTI applies a single modality-agnostic steering vector, targets coarse-grained hidden states rather than fine-grained representations, and intervenes reactively during decoding after initial errors have already formed. The paper asks whether shifting intervention to the prefill stage—before error accumulation begins—can correct hallucination-prone representations at their source.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263745/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

PTI takes a standard RGB image and text prompt as input to an LVLM and produces a generated text response, identical to the vanilla inference pipeline. The key operational detail is that PTI modifies the initial Key-Value cache computed during the prefill stage before any decoding step begins, introducing negligible computational overhead. Steering vectors are pre-extracted from 100 MSCOCO image-caption pairs using object segmentation masks and NLP-based anchor-word identification, then applied as one-time additive shifts to the cache with scalar coefficients (λ) tuned via grid search.

## Method

PTI extracts two sets of modality-specific steering vectors from contrastive KV caches: a visual direction derived by averaging the difference between object-only and background-only image encodings across all visual token positions, and a textual direction derived from the contrast between object-mention anchor words and non-anchor context words at the last textual token position. Both directions are decomposed into separate key and value components and denoised via PCA. During inference, the visual direction is injected additively into all visual token positions in the KV cache (steering keys toward object-centric attention and values toward filtering background noise), while the textual direction is applied only to the last textual token position to ground the linguistic initial state. This one-time, decoupled, modality-aware intervention corrects the initial representation before autoregressive decoding begins, making it orthogonal to and composable with existing decoding-time methods.

## Results

On the CHAIR benchmark (greedy decoding), PTI reduces hallucination frequency by 32.0 points in CHAIR_S (47.4→15.4) and 8.3 points in CHAIR_I (13.7→5.4) on LLaVA-1.5, with consistent gains on Qwen-VL-Chat (CHAIR_S: 39.6→20.6) and DeepSeek-VL-Chat (CHAIR_S: 25.8→19.2). On POPE (average across splits), PTI achieves 82.21% accuracy and 82.85% F1 on LLaVA-1.5, 85.69%/84.62% on Qwen-VL-Chat, and 85.14%/85.01% on DeepSeek-VL-Chat, outperforming all baselines. On the comprehensive MME benchmark, PTI yields the largest absolute gains: +40.0 points on both LLaVA-1.5 (611.6→651.6) and Qwen-VL-Chat (598.3→638.3), and +20.0 on DeepSeek-VL-Chat (651.6→671.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTI outperforms all five baselines on CHAIR across every model and decoding strategy. Against VISTA [23], the closest DTI competitor, PTI achieves 5.0 lower CHAIR_S on LLaVA-1.5 (15.4 vs. 20.4) and 18.4 lower on Qwen-VL-Chat (20.6 vs. 39.0), where VISTA actually worsens CHAIR_I from 12.0 to 16.8. VTI [29] shows moderate gains on Qwen-VL-Chat (CHAIR_S: 29.2) but underperforms PTI by 8.6 points; on POPE, VTI even degrades Qwen's F1 from 82.92% to 82.19%. PAI [30] achieves strong CHAIR_S on LLaVA (22.8) but fails to improve Qwen-VL-Chat at all (41.6 vs. vanilla 39.6), while PTI consistently improves all three models. On POPE, PTI leads on LLaVA-1.5 (82.21% avg Acc vs. 81.73% for PAI, 80.41% for VTI) and Qwen-VL-Chat (85.69% vs. 84.91% for PAI), with DeepSeek-VL-Chat showing the smallest margin (85.14% vs. 85.57% for PAI). The paper further demonstrates that PTI is orthogonal to decoding-stage methods: combining PTI with PAI adds +1.83% accuracy on LLaVA and +0.67% on Qwen, and combining with VISTA adds +0.80% on LLaVA and +0.76% on DeepSeek.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation-steering paradigm for LVLM hallucination mitigation (VISTA, VTI) by relocating the intervention from the decoding stage to the prefill stage, operating directly on the initial KV cache rather than hidden states. It departs from prior DTI methods in three specific ways: (1) a single proactive intervention before autoregressive error accumulation begins, (2) modality-aware steering with separate visual and textual directions, and (3) decoupled key/value interventions that exploit the attention mechanism's natural separation of 'where to attend' and 'what to aggregate.' The contrastive direction extraction draws on the object-vs-background formulation from VISTA and the textual anchor-word contrast from VTI, but applies them to the KV cache rather than hidden states.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the 'snowball hallucination' tradeoff—where DTI methods reduce hallucination frequency but worsen the severity of residual errors—reframes how the field should think about intervention timing and provides a principled motivation for prefill-stage correction. PTI's orthogonality to existing decoding-stage methods makes it a practical, plug-and-play module that can be stacked with VISTA or PAI for additive gains, lowering the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether the 100-sample MSCOCO-based direction extraction generalizes to domains with different object distributions, whether the approach extends to video or multi-image settings, and whether the F1-score tradeoff observed in the ablation (visual-only intervention drops F1 from 75.3 to 70.3) indicates a fundamental tension between hallucination reduction and generation quality.
