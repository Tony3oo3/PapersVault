## Problem

Causal transformer-based streaming 3D reconstruction (e.g., Causal-VGGT variants like StreamVGGT and STream3R) maintains a KV cache that grows linearly with stream length, creating a severe memory bottleneck that limits real-time deployment. Under constrained memory budgets, uniform token eviction prematurely discards globally informative tokens, degrading both reconstruction quality and long-term temporal consistency. The paper identifies that attention in these models exhibits structured spatio-temporal sparsity—spatial redundancy in view-dependent tokens and persistent temporal anchoring on a small set of reference tokens—that prior streaming methods ignore.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a continuous stream of RGB frames at 518×392 resolution, processing them in chunks of 4 consecutive frames with causal attention only over past frames. It produces per-frame camera parameters, depth maps, 3D point maps, and 3D point tracks via the underlying Causal-VGGT prediction heads. The total KV cache budget is fixed at 8× the number of frame tokens (stored in float16), allocated as 50% to a sliding temporal window, 25% to dynamically selected anchor tokens, and 25% to spatially retrieved merged tokens from a voxel grid.

## Method

STAC is a training-free, plug-and-play KV cache compression framework with three components. (1) Working Temporal Token Caching maintains a compact short-term memory combining fixed first-frame reference tokens, a sliding local window (size 4), and anchor tokens whose importance is tracked via exponentially decayed cumulative attention scores (γ=0.9) with Top-K selection. (2) Long-term Spatial Token Caching assigns evicted tokens to a 3D voxel grid (resolution 0.05) using Morton codes for locality-preserving indexing; each voxel maintains a dual-cache with one-to-one merging (cosine similarity threshold λ=0.8, weighted fusion) and many-to-one aggregation of buffered dissimilar tokens, plus a re-merging compaction step when capacity is reached. (3) Chunk-based Multi-frame Optimization groups 4 consecutive frames for joint bidirectional attention within the chunk while preserving the causal streaming constraint at chunk boundaries, improving both temporal coherence and GPU utilization. A count-based log-bias corrects attention logits to compensate for identity loss from token merging, and a custom CUDA kernel computes merge-aware attention and importance scores in a single pass.

## Results

On NRGBD, STream3R-STAC achieves ACC 0.065, Comp 0.014, and NC 0.700, while using only 2.20 GB (0.86 GB runtime) of memory and reaching 10.53 FPS—reducing memory by ~9× and accelerating inference by ~4× compared to full STream3R (19.75 GB, 2.52 FPS). On 7-Scenes, STream3R-STAC matches full STream3R almost exactly (ACC 0.047 vs. 0.044, NC 0.606 vs. 0.606). StreamVGGT-STAC similarly achieves 10.49 FPS at 2.57 GB (0.86 GB runtime) while matching or exceeding full StreamVGGT on Comp and NC across both datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the full Causal-VGGT baselines (STream3R and StreamVGGT), STAC achieves near-identical reconstruction quality (e.g., STream3R-STAC: ACC 0.065 vs. 0.053 on NRGBD; NC 0.700 vs. 0.703) while reducing memory from 19.75 GB to 2.20 GB (~9×) and increasing throughput from 2.52 to 10.53 FPS (~4×). Compared to sliding-window variants at matched memory budgets (STream3R-W22 at 2.19 GB, StreamVGGT-W26 at 2.57 GB), STAC consistently delivers lower ACC and Comp with higher NC, confirming that structured spatio-temporal caching outperforms uniform eviction. Among other online methods, Spann3R achieves the highest FPS (57.21) and lowest memory (0.37 GB) but lags STAC on NC (0.637 vs. 0.700 on NRGBD); Point3R (1.50 GB, 8.34 FPS) is outperformed by STAC on all three geometric metrics. Offline pair-based methods (DUSt3R-GA, MASt3R-GA, MonST3R-GA) and the offline VGGT are substantially worse in quality or infeasible in streaming (<1 FPS), while VGGT remains the quality upper bound (ACC 0.017 on NRGBD) that no online method matches. On camera pose estimation (Table 2), STream3R-STAC achieves ATE 0.198 on Sintel and 0.030 on TUM, competitive with full STream3R (0.219, 0.026) under a fraction of the memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

STAC builds directly on the Causal-VGGT lineage (STream3R, StreamVGGT), which adapted the offline VGGT transformer to streaming via causal self-attention and a growing KV cache. It departs from prior streaming 3D methods (Spann3R, CUT3R, Point3R) that require architectural specialization or fine-tuning, by introducing a training-free, plug-and-play cache compression module. Conceptually, it bridges two previously separate literatures: KV cache compression in LLMs (H2O, StreamLLM, token merging) and 3D spatial memory, by identifying that the KV cache in causal 3D transformers exhibits structured spatio-temporal sparsity—spatial redundancy aligned with 3D positions and temporal persistence in anchor tokens—that can be exploited via voxel-based merging and decayed attention scoring.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-10× memory reduction and 4× speedup while preserving reconstruction quality make long-sequence streaming 3D reconstruction practically feasible on a single GPU, which is critical for AR/VR, robotics, and autonomous driving applications. The plug-and-play design means any Causal-VGGT variant can benefit without retraining, lowering the barrier for adoption. Key limitations remain: the fixed-resolution voxel grid scales poorly to large outdoor scenes, and fast dynamic motion can destabilize cached representations. Open questions include whether adaptive or learned cache policies could outperform the hand-designed decay and threshold heuristics, and whether the spatio-temporal sparsity structure generalizes to other causal transformer tasks beyond 3D reconstruction.
