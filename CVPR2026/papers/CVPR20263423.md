## Problem

Existing Deep Unrolling Networks for compressive sensing suffer from three interrelated limitations: homogenized cross-stage feature extraction due to rigid iterative module designs, information bottlenecks in single-channel gradient guidance that cause irreversible feature loss across iterations, and an inherent local-global trade-off in spatial feature extraction that limits detail reconstruction at low CS rates.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263423/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single-channel grayscale image, partitions it into 128×128 blocks, and applies a learnable global convolution (reshaped sensing matrix Φ) to produce a compressed measurement vector y at a specified CS rate τ (tested at 0.01–0.25). The output is a full-resolution reconstructed image. Notably, the sensing matrix is jointly optimized with the network rather than being a fixed random matrix, and the reconstruction operates on 256×256 images in the main experiments.

## Method

MambaCS unrolls the Proximal Gradient Descent algorithm across 8 stages in a U-shaped (UPIN) architecture using Shuffle/UnShuffle for multi-scale processing. The core building block is the Adaptive State-Space Block (A-SSB), which pairs a Mamba-based Visual State Space Block (VSSB) with 2D multi-directional selective scanning (SS2D) for spatial long-range modeling with a Gated Channel Attention Block (GCAB) for cross-channel adaptive attention. The High-Dimensional Gradient Fusion (HDGF) module replaces the standard gradient descent step by concatenating x^(k), A^T A x^(k), and A^T y along the channel dimension and processing them through DConv + A-SSB + Sigmoid to produce rich multi-channel gradient guidance, eliminating the single-channel information bottleneck. The Feature-Adaptive Proximal Operator (FAPO) augments the PGD proximal step by using A-SSB as a learned sparse basis Ψ with soft-thresholding, enabling global spatial-channel adaptive attention during the shrinkage operation. The entire network is trained end-to-end with MSE loss, and this work is the first to integrate Mamba's selective state-space mechanism into a DUN framework for compressive sensing.

## Results

MambaCS achieves the best average PSNR and SSIM across all five natural-image datasets (General100, LIVE29, OST300, Set14, BSD68) at every CS rate from 0.01 to 0.25. On General100 at τ=0.04, MambaCS reaches 29.64 dB / 0.8325 SSIM, improving by 2.39 dB over TransCS, 1.72 dB over DGU-Net+, and 3.03 dB over DPC-DUN. On FastMRI (CS-MRI), MambaCS attains 28.61 dB / 0.6453 average, outperforming the next-best CPP-Net (28.53 dB). Notably, MambaCS records the lowest GFLOPs (87.74) among all compared methods despite having the highest parameter count (44.91 M).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MambaCS outperforms all 11 baselines on every dataset and CS rate in both PSNR and SSIM. The closest competitor is CPP-Net (CVPR 2024), which trails by 0.54 dB on General100 (31.17 vs. 31.71 avg) and 0.69 dB on BSD68 (27.36 vs. 28.05). Among Transformer-based DUNs, OCTUF and CSformer are the strongest, but MambaCS leads them by 1.04 dB and 1.89 dB respectively on General100. DPC-DUN (TIP 2023) shows the largest gap at low CS rates, with MambaCS achieving 3.03 dB higher PSNR at τ=0.04 on General100. On CS-MRI (FastMRI), MambaCS leads CPP-Net by 0.08 dB and UFC-Net by 0.31 dB in average PSNR. In terms of efficiency, MambaCS achieves the lowest GFLOPs (87.74) among all compared methods despite having 3.6× more parameters than CPP-Net, indicating that the Mamba-based A-SSB provides a more computationally efficient feature-extraction pathway than CNN/Transformer alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two active research threads: deep unrolling networks (DUNs) for compressive sensing, which unroll iterative optimization algorithms like PGD/ISTA into learnable architectures (TransCS, OCTUF, CPP-Net, UFC-Net), and Mamba-based state space models for vision (VMamba, RS-Mamba, FourierMamba), which replace quadratic-complexity attention with linear-complexity selective scanning. MambaCS is explicitly positioned as the first integration of Mamba into a DUN framework for CS, replacing the CNN or Transformer feature extractors in each unrolling stage with a custom Adaptive State-Space Block (A-SSB) that combines a multi-directional 2D selective scan (SS2D, inspired by VMamba's cross-scanning) with a gated channel attention block. The PGD unrolling structure and soft-thresholding proximal operator follow the standard ISTA lineage (Beck & Teboulle), while the multi-scale U-shaped architecture with up/down-sampling extends prior single-scale DUNs to address the local-global feature extraction trade-off.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By embedding Mamba's linear-complexity sequence modeling within an interpretable optimization-unrolling framework, this work establishes a template for combining SSMs with algorithmic priors in inverse problems, potentially extending beyond CS to other reconstruction tasks (denoising, deblurring, MRI). The HDGF mechanism addressing gradient information bottlenecks and the FAPO's learned sparse basis are design choices that could inform future DUN architectures regardless of backbone. However, the paper is limited to single-channel 2D images at moderate resolutions, and the performance gains over the strongest baselines (CPP-Net, UFC-Net) are consistent but modest (~0.5–1 dB PSNR), leaving open whether the Mamba advantage persists at higher resolutions, for color/video CS, or under more aggressive undersampling. The parameter count (44.91M) is the highest among compared methods, raising questions about the parameter-efficiency trade-off of the A-SSB design.
