## Problem

VLA models trained via imitation learning degrade sharply under data scarcity, and RL-based post-training is blocked by the non-resettable, safety-critical nature of real-world robotic environments. Additionally, existing VLA pipelines lack a reliable mechanism for detecting task completion, causing policies to execute redundant post-success actions that perturb object states and reduce success rates.

## Input / Output

The VLA policy consumes a history of RGB observations, proprioceptive states (6D end-effector pose plus 1D gripper state), and a natural-language instruction, and outputs a continuous action vector sampled from a factorized Laplace distribution whose location and log-scale parameters are produced by separate policy and scale heads. The world simulator takes the next proprioceptive state (computed via forward kinematics) and a historical observation to generate the next RGB frame. The instant reflector ingests the full imagined video trajectory and the language instruction, producing a continuous reward in [0,1] per timestep and a binary termination signal triggered when the reward exceeds 0.5.

## Method

RehearseVLA replaces physical interaction with a two-component virtual environment for RL post-training. The physically-consistent world simulator is a U-Net denoising diffusion network conditioned on pixel-level action maps (projected end-effector poses) and a memory-bank observation, with a geometry-aware feature injection strategy that feeds VGGT latent features (fine-grained geometry) and CLIP features (global semantics) into cross-attention layers at multiple resolutions to enforce spatial and physical coherence. The VLM-guided instant reflector pairs a frozen vision encoder and frozen LLM with a lightweight trainable reward head (sigmoid output) that scores task-progress continuously and emits a termination signal, eliminating the degenerate advantage estimates that plague binary-reward RL. Training data for the simulator is augmented by deploying the SFT OpenVLA-OFT policy in the LIBERO simulator with Laplace-perturbed actions to collect diverse state-action transitions beyond expert demonstrations. Policy optimization uses Leave-One-Out PPO (LOOP) with N=8 rollouts per iteration, broadcasting a single trajectory-wise reward from the reflector across all timesteps, and LoRA (rank 32) for parameter-efficient fine-tuning of the VLA backbone.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark with only 5 expert demonstrations per task, RehearseVLA achieves an average success rate of 79.6%, outperforming the best SFT baseline OpenVLA-OFT (74.85%) by 4.75 points. The largest gains appear on LIBERO-Object (86.6% vs. 74.2%, +12.4 points) and LIBERO-Goal (86.4% vs. 84.0%, +2.4 points), while LIBERO-Long shows a more modest improvement (57.8% vs. 57.0%). Against the simulator-based RL method RIPT-VLA, RehearseVLA achieves comparable performance (86.4/86.6/87.6/57.8 vs. 86.2/83.4/88.6/58.4) while being deployable in real-world settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RehearseVLA outperforms all five SFT baselines on the LIBERO benchmark under the 5-demonstration-per-task setting. The largest margin is over π₀+FAST (79.6% vs. 55.0% average, +24.6 points) and π₀ (79.6% vs. 61.1%, +18.5 points), while the closest SFT competitor is OpenVLA-OFT (74.85%), which RehearseVLA surpasses by 4.75 points on average, with the biggest per-suite gain on LIBERO-Object (+12.4 points). Against the simulator-based RL method RIPT-VLA [42], RehearseVLA achieves comparable overall performance: it wins on LIBERO-Object (86.6% vs. 83.4%) and LIBERO-Goal (86.4% vs. 86.2%) but trails slightly on LIBERO-Spatial (87.6% vs. 88.6%) and LIBERO-Long (57.8% vs. 58.4%). A key practical distinction is that RIPT-VLA is restricted to simulated environments, whereas RehearseVLA transfers to real-world robotic tasks, where it outperforms OpenVLA-OFT on all four tested tasks (e.g., 30 vs. 20 on 'clean table', 50 vs. 30 on 'put green toy'). The paper also shows that without the proposed termination mechanism, all baselines suffer significant degradation (e.g., OpenVLA-OFT drops from 74.85% to 63.05% average) due to redundant post-success actions, while RehearseVLA retains 74.9% under the same no-external-termination constraint.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL post-training paradigm for VLA models (as in RIPT-VLA and VLA-RL) by replacing the physical or conventional simulator environment with a video-based world model that generates action-conditioned future observations. It builds on OpenVLA-OFT as the base policy and incorporates geometry-aware feature injection from VGGT and CLIP to ensure physical consistency in the world simulator, drawing on recent advances in diffusion-based video generation. The VLM-guided instant reflector departs from prior binary success/failure reward schemes by providing continuous progress signals and real-time termination detection, addressing a gap identified in existing RL-based VLA post-training methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RehearseVLA offers a practical path to improving VLA policies in safety-critical, data-scarce settings (5 demos per task) without requiring physical interaction or purpose-built simulators, which is significant for industrial deployment. The termination mechanism addresses a previously underappreciated failure mode where post-success actions degrade task outcomes. However, the approach remains limited to the LIBERO benchmark and a single VLA architecture, and the authors acknowledge that both the world simulator and reflector depend on diverse training data, with policy optimization being slower than concurrent methods due to simulator-based trajectory generation. Open questions include generalization to more complex, open-world manipulation tasks and whether the world model approach scales beyond the current 48-hour training budget on 8 GPUs.
