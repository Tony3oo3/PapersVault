## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on RL agents are largely confined to low-dimensional vector states or discrete-action visual environments (e.g., Atari), where the policy resembles a classifier. Continuous-action visual RL presents a substantially harder setting—high-dimensional pixel observations, an infinite action space, and long-horizon dynamics—yet no effective black-box attack framework has been developed for this regime, where the attacker cannot access gradients or internal parameters and must minimize costly environment queries.

## Input / Output

The attacker receives clean RGB observation frames (84×84×3 for MuJoCo, standard Atari resolutions for discrete-action tasks) and black-box access to a pre-trained victim policy π(a|s) that can be queried for actions but whose weights and gradients are hidden. It outputs a bounded pixel perturbation δ = clip(G_φ(s), −ε, ε) with ε = 8/255, which is added to the clean frame and re-clipped to [0,1] to form the adversarial observation s′. At attack execution time, SEBA requires zero victim queries per step, unlike gradient-based or query-based baselines that need 11–400 queries per step.

## Method

SEBA combines a shadow Q-critic (trained with a Double-DQN TD objective on perturbed states to estimate the victim's cumulative reward under attack), a GAN module where the generator G_φ produces perturbations and the discriminator D_ψ enforces visual imperceptibility, and an IRIS-style world model (discrete tokenizer + autoregressive Transformer) that generates H synthetic transitions per real step to cut environment queries by O(H). Training follows a two-stage alternating schedule: Stage 1 freezes the GAN and updates the shadow critic on real and model-based rollouts; Stage 2 freezes the critic and updates the GAN with a joint loss balancing FID-style realism against Q_shadow-driven reward minimization. The key architectural choice is to optimize a generator directly guided by the shadow critic's gradient rather than learning an RL policy over the ~2×10⁴-dimensional perturbation space, which avoids the sample-complexity explosion that degrades PA-AD and OPTIMAL in pixel space.

## Results

On five pixel-based MuJoCo tasks with DrQ-SAC victims, SEBA achieves the lowest cumulative reward across all tasks, e.g., 1.61±3.97 on Cheetah Run (vs. 150.72 for PGD and 52.15 for SimBA) and 0.3±0.9 on Reacher Hard (vs. 592.64 for OPTIMAL). SEBA also attains the lowest FID (62.43) among all methods, indicating the most visually imperceptible perturbations. In terms of query efficiency, SEBA requires only 160K environment and 800K victim queries during training, compared to 4M each for PA-AD and OPTIMAL, and uses zero victim queries at attack time (vs. 400 for SimBA and 202 for Square).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEBA outperforms all four image-space baselines (PGD, C&W, SimBA, Square) on both reward degradation and FID across every MuJoCo task; for instance, on Cheetah Run SEBA reduces reward to 1.61 versus 150.72 (PGD) and 52.15 (SimBA), while achieving a 47-point lower FID than PGD. Against the RL-specific baselines (Critic-Based, MAD, PA-AD, OPTIMAL), SEBA also achieves the lowest reward on all tasks, with the largest gap on Reacher Hard (0.3 vs. 592.64 for OPTIMAL) and Cheetah Run (1.61 vs. 271.73 for OPTIMAL). SEBA's query cost is dramatically lower: 160K environment and 800K victim queries versus 4M each for PA-AD and OPTIMAL, and it requires zero victim queries at attack time compared to 400 (SimBA) and 202 (Square). On Atari with Rainbow victims, SEBA is second only to the white-box PA-AD (e.g., Alien: 982 vs. 443) but uses 25× fewer environment queries (80K vs. 2M) and achieves the lowest FID (81.7). The only setting where SEBA does not achieve the absolute best reward is Hopper Stand, where PGD (1.85) and SimBA (4.86) are competitive, though SEBA still matches or exceeds them in FID and query efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

SEBA departs from the dominant paradigm in adversarial RL attacks (PA-AD, OPTIMAL, SA-MDP), which treat the attacker as a policy learning perturbations in a high-dimensional action space via RL-based exploration. Instead, SEBA repurposes the GAN-based perturbation generation idea from image adversarial attacks and couples it with an RL-specific shadow Q critic for reward-guided optimization, avoiding the sample complexity of exploring R^d. The world model component directly builds on the IRIS framework [29] (discrete tokenizer + autoregressive Transformer), adapting it from planning to synthetic rollout generation for attack training. Conceptually, the paper bridges three previously separate threads—adversarial image generation, model-based RL, and RL-specific attack formulations—into a unified black-box attack pipeline for visual continuous control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic study of black-box adversarial attacks on image-based continuous-control RL, a setting critical for real-world embodied AI (robotics, autonomous navigation) where gradient access is unavailable and environment queries are expensive. The 25× reduction in environment queries (160K vs. 4M) via the world model makes adversarial evaluation practically feasible under limited-access threat models. Open questions include whether the approach transfers to multi-agent or partially observable settings, whether the shadow critic remains reliable under distribution shift from the world model over long horizons, and whether the targeted-attack capability generalizes beyond single-dimension steering to complex behavioral constraints. The paper does not evaluate on real-world robotic hardware, leaving a gap between simulation and deployment.
