## Problem

Prompt tuning of CLIP improves classification accuracy but introduces a dual miscalibration problem: underconfidence on base classes (reduced inter-class logit margins) and overconfidence on novel classes (inflated margins). Existing calibration methods either address only one of these failure modes or rely on post-hoc corrections that cannot constrain how prompt tuning distorts the pretrained embedding geometry, leaving spurious semantic relationships that degrade open-vocabulary reliability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262484/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a set of class-name text prompts (each prepended with learnable prompt tokens) as input, processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image encoder and text encoder. It outputs a class label and a calibrated confidence score computed via temperature-scaled softmax over cosine similarities between the image embedding and class text embeddings. Training uses only 16 labeled samples per base class, and the calibration guarantee must extend to novel classes never seen during prompt tuning.

## Method

The paper proposes a plug-and-play training-time regularization with two complementary terms added to standard cross-entropy. First, a mean-variance margin loss defines each sample's logit margin as the gap between the ground-truth logit and the highest incorrect logit, then maximizes the batch mean margin (to lift underconfident base-class predictions) while minimizing its variance (to suppress spurious confidence spikes on novel classes). Second, a text moment-matching loss aligns the first and second moments (mean vector and covariance matrix) of prompt-tuned text embeddings with their frozen zero-shot CLIP counterparts, preserving the global semantic geometry without imposing rigid per-instance alignment. The total objective is L_CE + λ_Margin·L_Margin + λ_mom·L_mom, is agnostic to the underlying prompt-tuning framework (CoOp, MaPLe, KGCoOp, etc.), and requires no additional inference-time computation.

## Results

On base classes, the proposed method reduces ECE from 6.35% to 2.93% with CoOp and from 3.19% to 2.78% with MaPLe, while maintaining or slightly improving top-1 accuracy (e.g., MaPLe: 82.41% → 82.75%). On novel classes, the gains are even more dramatic: CoOp ECE drops from 12.45% to 4.79% and MaPLe ECE from 5.76% to 4.23%, with accuracy preserved (MaPLe: 75.17% → 75.14%). Across all 11 datasets and 7 prompt-tuning frameworks, the method consistently achieves the lowest ECE among all compared calibration techniques on both base and novel classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all calibration baselines on ECE for both base and novel classes across all three prompt-tuning backbones. Against the strongest post-hoc method, DAC, our approach reduces novel-class ECE from 7.00% to 4.79% (CoOp) and from 4.61% to 4.23% (MaPLe), while also improving accuracy. Compared to the training-time ZS-Norm and Penalty methods from [20], our method achieves substantially lower ECE (4.79% vs. 7.14%/5.20% for CoOp; 4.23% vs. 6.93%/5.95% for MaPLe) without the accuracy degradation those methods suffer (e.g., Penalty drops MaPLe accuracy from 75.17% to 70.06%). Temperature Scaling, while competitive on base classes (2.96% ECE for CoOp), degrades significantly on novel classes (9.45% ECE), whereas our method handles both regimes simultaneously. MBLS, a margin-based label smoothing approach, performs poorly on novel classes (9.66% ECE for CoOp) because it caps extreme margins without addressing the underlying embedding geometry, a limitation our moment-matching term explicitly corrects.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of prompt tuning for vision-language models (CoOp, MaPLe, KGCoOp) and neural network calibration, carving out the specific problem of calibrating prompt-tuned CLIP across both base and novel classes. It departs from post-hoc calibration methods (Temperature Scaling, DAC) that cannot constrain how prompt tuning distorts the embedding space, and from prior training-time calibration (ZS-Norm, Penalty) that address base or novel miscalibration in isolation. The key conceptual departure is framing the problem as a *dual* miscalibration—underconfidence on base classes from tightened margins and overconfidence on novel classes from inflated margins—and solving it with two complementary regularizers operating in different spaces (logit-space margin shaping and embedding-space moment matching), rather than a single corrective mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reliable confidence estimates are essential for deploying prompt-tuned VLMs in safety-critical settings (autonomous driving, medical imaging), where overconfident errors on unseen classes can be catastrophic. The plug-and-play, inference-free nature of the proposed regularizers makes them broadly applicable across prompt-tuning frameworks without architectural changes, lowering the barrier to adopting calibrated VLMs. Limitations include evaluation restricted to CLIP ViT-B/16 and a few-shot (16-shot) regime; whether the moment-matching constraint generalizes to larger models, different VLM architectures, or zero-shot prompt tuning remains open. The paper also does not explore how the method interacts with test-time adaptation or multi-modal calibration.
