## Problem

Existing Mirror Illusion Art design methods (topology-based and shadow-art approaches) rely heavily on manual intuition, optimize shape only without color, and produce non-smooth or internally fractured 3D geometry that is often not physically printable. No prior method automates the full pipeline from two target 2D images to a smooth, color-rich, fabrication-ready 3D object.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264015/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes two user-specified 2D colored images (a front-view target A and a mirror-view target B) as input, with the constraint that both images should have similar widths and their resolution should not exceed the 128×128×128 voxel grid. The output is a 3D voxel model with per-voxel density and color, subsequently converted to a smooth 3D mesh suitable for 3D printing, achieving the illusion that the object and its mirror reflection appear as two distinct objects.

## Method

AutoMIA formulates the problem as inverse design: jointly optimizing voxel positions, densities, and colors via differentiable rendering (PyTorch3D) to minimize BCE shape loss and L1 color loss under two non-orthogonal viewing angles (direct and mirror). Four stabilization mechanisms address distinct failure modes: PAC selects spatially connected voxel components by IoU alignment with both target masks to remove surface noise; PWA applies distance-adaptive pixel weighting to penalize background noise far from the target mask; IVP identifies internal voxels via a sliding solid-voxel kernel and enforces a density lower bound to prevent internal fractures; and SCD stages optimization into shape-only, joint, and color-only phases to balance the two objectives. The full pipeline runs in ~76 s and 2.6 GB on a single RTX 3090.

## Results

On the Mirror-2D dataset (1200 images across 6 categories), AutoMIA achieves a Shape Score (SS) of 0.931, Smooth Level (SL) of 0.989, and Noise Level (NL) of 0.049, substantially outperforming both baselines on all three metrics. In terms of efficiency, AutoMIA requires only ~76 seconds and 2.6 GB memory on a single RTX 3090, making it faster than SAR (140 s, 3.3 GB) while delivering far superior reconstruction quality. The method additionally supports joint shape-and-color optimization, a capability neither baseline provides.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoMIA decisively outperforms both baselines on all quality metrics. Compared to Shadow Art (SA) [12], AutoMIA improves SS from 0.499 to 0.931 (+87%), reduces NL from 0.507 to 0.049 (10× less noise), and raises SL from 0.827 to 0.989, while running only 1.5× slower (76 s vs 50 s). Against Shadow Art Revisited (SAR) [17], AutoMIA improves SS from 0.668 to 0.931 (+39%), reduces NL from 0.120 to 0.049 (2.4× less noise), and nearly doubles speed (76 s vs 140 s) with lower memory (2.6 GB vs 3.3 GB). A key qualitative advantage is that AutoMIA jointly optimizes shape and color, whereas both SA and SAR are restricted to shape-only optimization, meaning they cannot reproduce colored target patterns. The paper attributes the performance gap to SA/SAR being designed for orthogonal multi-view settings and struggling with the non-orthogonal two-view geometry of Mirror Illusion Art, where AutoMIA's PAC and PWA modules specifically suppress the resulting surface and background noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the differentiable-rendering and voxel-optimization paradigm (via PyTorch3D) for a problem that prior work addressed only manually or through shape-only proxies. Sugihara's topology-based Mirror Illusion Art [18] established the concept but required expert intuition and supported no color; Shadow Art [12] and Shadow Art Revisited [17] offer automated shape optimization but were designed for orthogonal shadow reconstruction, not the non-orthogonal front/mirror geometry, and produce non-smooth, internally fractured objects. AutoMIA carves new ground by formulating the mirror illusion as a joint shape-and-color inverse design problem and introducing four targeted regularization mechanisms (PAC, PWA, IVP, SCD) that address defects specific to the two-view, non-orthogonal supervision setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoMIA is the first fully automated pipeline that produces physically printable, color-rich Mirror Illusion Art in under 80 seconds on a single GPU, bridging a gap between computational inverse graphics and tangible art fabrication. The viewing-angle constraint (θ₁ < Θ < θ₂) and the requirement for similar-width input images remain practical limitations that restrict the method's generality. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-object scenes, dynamic viewpoints, or more complex reflective surfaces (e.g., curved mirrors), and whether the staged SCD schedule generalizes to other multi-objective 3D inverse problems beyond mirror illusions.
