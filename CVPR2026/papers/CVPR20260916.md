## Problem

Vision-language models over-rely on linguistic priors even when images are present, producing confident hallucinated objects. Existing mitigations either retrain with expensive hallucination-aware supervision or apply inference-time contrastive decoding, but none convert the image/no-image uncertainty gap—typically treated only as a diagnostic—into a learnable training signal that directly rewards visual reliance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260916/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image and a text query as input and produces a text response (binary yes/no for POPE VQA) or a free-form caption (for CHAIR/AMBER). During training, each sample requires two parallel forward passes—one with the image and one with image tokens masked out—to compute the VES entropy gap over a task-specific decision variable Z (binary for POPE, factorized Bernoulli over an object vocabulary for captioning). At inference, the model operates in a single forward pass conditioned on the image, with no extra decoding heuristics or forward passes.

## Method

VES-RFT defines a Visual Evidence Sensitivity (VES) score as the entropy gap ΔH(x,v) = H(p(z|x,v=∅)) − H(p(z|x,v)), which measures how much the image tightens the model's predictive distribution relative to a text-only baseline; this is interpreted as a tractable surrogate for conditional mutual information I(Z;V|X). The VES reward is the rectified gap max(0, ΔH), paired with a verifiable reward r_verif ∈ [0,1] that automatically checks generated object mentions against reference annotations to prevent confidently wrong answers. The combined reward r = r_verif + λ·r_ves is optimized via critic-free GRPO with KL regularization, requiring only the parallel with/without-image passes during training while preserving single-pass inference. The framework is verifier-agnostic and model-agnostic, instantiated on LLaVA-1.5 and Qwen2.5-VL with only 2.8k training pairs.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5, VES-RFT (2.8k pairs) achieves 86.96% average POPE accuracy and 85.61% average F1, outperforming the baseline (82.04% / 80.43%) and all training-based methods. On MS-COCO captioning, it reduces CHAIR_S from 55.6 to 42.8 and CHAIR_I from 15.8 to 14.0, and on AMBER it cuts the hallucination rate from 34.7 to 18.9. On Qwen2.5-VL, VES-RFT reaches 88.93% POPE accuracy and 28.7 CHAIR_S, again the best among all compared methods. The method requires only 2.8k preference pairs—25–100× fewer than SFT (220k) or LLaVA-RLHF (122k)—while preserving single-pass inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among decoding-only methods, VES-RFT outperforms VCD (85.61 vs 84.39 avg F1), ICD (85.61 vs 82.53), and M3ID (85.61 vs 84.71) on POPE, and substantially beats them on CHAIR_S (42.8 vs 51.0 for VCD, 57.0 for M3ID). It trails RAR (86.43) and HIO (87.37) on POPE average F1, but those methods require extra inference-time forward passes or mixed training+decoding pipelines, whereas VES-RFT adds no inference overhead. Among training-based baselines, VES-RFT dominates SFT (85.61 vs 82.70 F1), LLaVA-RLHF (85.61 vs 81.50), and LURE (85.61 vs 81.64) while using 25–79× fewer training pairs. In the DPO comparison (Table 3), VES-RFT achieves a Pareto-better balance: it matches or beats V-DPO on CHAIR_S (5.2 vs 6.6) and HA-DPO on both metrics, while OPA-DPO achieves a lower CHAIR_S (2.4) but suffers a severe POPE accuracy drop (82.60 vs 86.96), indicating a trade-off that VES-RFT avoids. On Qwen2.5-VL, VES-RFT edges out SFT (88.93 vs 88.44 Acc) and all decoding methods, confirming that the gains transfer to stronger backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a third paradigm for VLM hallucination mitigation, distinct from (a) retraining with hallucination-aware supervision and feature re-injection, and (b) inference-time interventions like contrastive decoding (VCD, M3ID). It builds on the GRPO critic-free optimization framework from DeepSeekMath [24] and the RFT-with-verifiable-rewards paradigm, but introduces a fundamentally different reward signal: the entropy gap between with-image and no-image forward passes (Visual Evidence Sensitivity), which is annotation-free and interpretable as a surrogate for conditional mutual information. Unlike DPO-based methods (V-DPO, OPA-DPO) that require discrete preference pairs, VES-RFT uses a continuous, automatically computed signal, and unlike inference-time methods, it bakes the correction into model weights during training while preserving single-pass inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is extreme data efficiency: 2.8k training pairs achieve competitive or superior hallucination metrics compared to 220k SFT or 122k RLHF examples, while adding zero inference-time overhead. The entropy-gap-as-reward formulation is elegant and generalizable in principle to any task where a counterfactual no-evidence condition is computable, though the paper only validates on object-level VQA and captioning. Open questions include whether the VES signal transfers to non-object tasks (spatial reasoning, OCR, multi-image), whether the rectified-linear shaping function is optimal, and whether the approach scales to larger models or more diverse hallucination types beyond object presence.
