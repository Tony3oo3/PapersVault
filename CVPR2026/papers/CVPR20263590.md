## Problem

Existing multimodal LLMs inject visual tokens into the LLM input space, causing quadratic computational overhead from long visual sequences and disrupting pretrained linguistic priors as visual tokens dominate the training signal. The paper asks how to inject visual semantics into an LLM without expanding the input context while preserving the geometric structure of pretrained parameters.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263590/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts an RGB image (processed at 384×384 by SigLIP2-So400m-384) and a text prompt, producing a standard autoregressive text response. Unusually, the visual tokens never appear in the LLM's input sequence; instead they are consumed exclusively by the VRMG modules to generate rotation matrices that modulate the LLM's internal parameters, so the LLM processes only the text tokens in its forward pass.

## Method

ROSE shifts vision injection from the input space to the parameter space by applying vision-conditioned orthogonal rotations to the LLM's linear projection matrices. A Vision-guided Rotation Matrix Generator (VRMG) uses learnable queries with cross-attention over visual tokens to produce skew-symmetric matrices, which are converted to orthogonal matrices via the Cayley transform; these are sparsified into 64 block-diagonal sub-blocks to reduce cost from O(d²) to O(d²/r). The resulting orthogonal matrix R_v left-multiplies each projection W, rotating all column vectors uniformly so that pairwise angular relationships (and thus pretrained semantic structure) are preserved while individual vector directions are adjusted to encode visual semantics. VRMGs are inserted every 4 layers (9 layers total), each handling 7 projections (W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o, W_up, W_down, W_gate) with 7r learnable queries. Training proceeds in three stages: perceptual pretraining on 60M LAION-2B pairs (VRMG only), semantic pretraining on 90M DataComp1B synthetic captions (VRMG + vision encoder), and end-to-end fine-tuning on 24.3M FineVision instruction samples.

## Results

ROSE-7B achieves an average score of 74.5 across 12 multimodal benchmarks, with only 3.7T FLOPs and 145.1 ms inference latency. Compared to Qwen2.5-VL-7B (76.0 avg, 19.2T FLOPs, 333.4 ms), ROSE reduces FLOPs by 80.7% and latency by 56.4% while remaining within 1.5 points on average. ROSE outperforms all ≤4B baselines (best: Qwen2.5-VL-3B at 71.8 avg) and the 7B LLaVA-OV-7B (71.6 avg) by 2.7–20.5 points, while using only 40% of LLaVA-OV-0.5B's FLOPs. On individual benchmarks, ROSE leads on MMBench (81.5), SEED (77.4), AI2D (80.8), and MMStar (60.7), and is competitive on MMMU (50.8 vs. 51.0 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest 7B baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B (76.0 avg), ROSE-7B (74.5 avg) trails by 1.5 points on average but uses only 19.3% of its FLOPs (3.7T vs. 19.2T) and 43.5% of its latency (145.1 ms vs. 333.4 ms); ROSE actually leads on MMBench (81.5 vs. 83.8 is a loss, but on SEED 77.4 vs. 77.6 is near-tie, and on AI2D 80.8 vs. 82.6 is close). Against Qwen2-VL-7B (73.1 avg), ROSE wins by 1.4 points while using 37% of its FLOPs. Against LLaVA-OV-7B (71.6 avg), ROSE wins by 2.9 points with 81.4% fewer FLOPs (3.7T vs. 62.9T). Against the 4B-scale models, ROSE outperforms Qwen2.5-VL-3B (71.8) by 2.7 points, Gemma3-4B (60.4) by 14.1 points, and Phi-3.5-V-4B (65.2) by 9.3 points, while matching or exceeding their FLOPs budget. Against the 2B and 0.5B models, ROSE achieves 9.5–20.5 point average improvements (74.5 vs. 65.0 for Qwen2-VL-2B, 74.5 vs. 54.0 for LLaVA-OV-0.5B) with comparable or lower FLOPs. The fair-comparison ablation (Table 2) further shows that under identical data and backbone, the ROSE-style injection retains language scores within 1.2 points of the original Qwen2.5-7B (e.g., MMLU 70.0 vs. 71.2), whereas the LLaVA-style injection drops MMLU to 67.3, confirming the parameter-space rotation better preserves pretrained priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs fundamentally from the dominant input-space injection paradigm (LLaVA, Qwen-VL, BLIP-2) where visual tokens are concatenated with text tokens, instead proposing to modulate LLM parameters directly via vision-derived orthogonal rotations. The mathematical machinery draws on the Cayley transform (a classical tool for generating orthogonal matrices from skew-symmetric ones) and the empirical observation from prior work that vector direction, not magnitude, predominantly encodes semantic knowledge in pretrained weights. Conceptually, it is the parameter-space analogue of the authors' earlier LAVI work on internal feature modulation, but replaces learned additive/modulative adjustments with structure-preserving rotations that provably maintain pairwise angular relationships among parameter vectors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80.7% FLOPs reduction and 56.4% latency reduction at competitive accuracy represent a potentially paradigm-shifting efficiency gain for MLLM deployment, particularly as the field pushes toward higher resolutions and multi-image/video inputs where quadratic attention cost becomes prohibitive. The demonstrated preservation of linguistic priors (matching the base LLM on 5 NLP benchmarks) addresses a well-known failure mode of input-space injection. Open questions include whether the block-diagonal rotation approximation scales gracefully to much larger models (e.g., 70B+), whether the approach extends naturally to video or audio modalities, and whether the limited expressiveness of orthogonal transformations (which preserve vector norms) becomes a bottleneck for tasks requiring more aggressive cross-modal reweighting.
