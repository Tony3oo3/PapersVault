## Problem

The existing SARD baseline detects anomalous scene-graph relationships via an enhanced counting approach that is fundamentally vulnerable to the long-tail distribution of triplets, where most normal relationships appear only rarely. No learning-based method had previously been proposed for scene-specific anomalous relationship detection, leaving the distributional bias of counting-based scoring unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262441/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes RGB images, converts them to scene graphs via a frozen EGTR model, and selects the top 30 triplets by confidence score. Each object-relation-object triplet is encoded through frozen GloVe embeddings into a 900-dimensional concatenated vector (3 × 300-dim). The output is a per-triplet anomaly score computed as the negative log-likelihood under the learned flow, where higher scores flag more anomalous relationships.

## Method

BUSSARD is a four-stage pipeline: a frozen EGTR scene graph generator extracts triplets, a frozen GloVe model encodes each token into 300-dim vectors, an autoencoder compresses the 900-dim concatenated triplet representation to a 512-dim latent space, and a three-layer RealNVP normalizing flow models the density of normal triplets. The autoencoder is critical because normalizing flows enforce bijective (equal-dimension) mappings, making them infeasible on the raw 900-dim embeddings without prior compression. Training is fully unsupervised on normal data only: the autoencoder first minimizes MSE reconstruction loss, then the flow maximizes log-likelihood of the compressed normal representations using a plateau-scheduled AdamW optimizer. The semantic embedding design provides universality—synonym-substituted triplets remain close in embedding space, mitigating the long-tail vulnerability that degrades counting-based baselines by up to 17.5% under vocabulary variation.

## Results

On the SARD dataset, BUSSARD achieves 97.85% AUROC on the dining room scene and 95.29% AUROC on the office scene, outperforming the corrected counting-based baseline (SARD-c) by an average of 10.07% in AUROC. In terms of ranking quality, BUSSARD scores 92.85% AUC-Recall@k (dining room) and 89.57% (office), a 4.11% average improvement over SARD-c. Additionally, BUSSARD is approximately five times faster at inference than the SARD-c baseline on an NVIDIA RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the counting-based SARD method of Lai et al. (CVPR Workshops 2025), evaluated in two variants: SARD-o (original, with some ground-truth anomalies missing from the top-30 triplets) and SARD-c (re-run with corrected data). BUSSARD decisively outperforms both variants on both scenes: on the dining room, it gains +7.35 AUROC over SARD-c (97.85 vs. 90.50) and +17.35 over SARD-o; on the office scene, it gains +12.79 AUROC over SARD-c (95.29 vs. 82.50) and +13.49 over SARD-o. In AUC-Recall@k, BUSSARD improves by +1.35 (dining room) and +6.87 (office) over SARD-c, and by a much larger margin (+23.37 and +25.05) over SARD-o. The paper also reports that BUSSARD is ~5× faster at inference than SARD-c, and in a synonym-robustness ablation, BUSSARD shows only ~2% performance deviation while SARD-c deviates up to 17.5%, highlighting the advantage of learned semantic embeddings over frequency counting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first learning-based solution to the Scene-Specific Anomalous Relationship Detection (SARD) task, which was introduced by Lai et al. with a counting-based baseline. Architecturally, it builds directly on RealNVP coupling layers for density estimation and repurposes frozen pretrained components (EGTR for scene graph generation, GloVe for word embeddings) as fixed feature extractors, with an autoencoder bridging the dimensionality gap between high-dimensional embeddings and the bijective flow. The key conceptual departure from prior SARD work is replacing frequency-based anomaly scoring with a learned likelihood model, and the multimodal embedding strategy (placing semantically similar terms in close vector proximity) is borrowed from the NLP word-embedding literature to address the long-tail distribution problem that undermines counting approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BUSSARD establishes that a relatively lightweight learned pipeline (frozen SGG + frozen embeddings + AE + 3-layer RealNVP) can substantially outperform the only prior SARD method while being faster, suggesting that distributional modeling is a more principled route than frequency counting for relationship anomaly detection. The synonym robustness result (~2% vs. 17.5% deviation) is practically important for open-vocabulary deployment where vocabulary is not fixed. However, evaluation is confined to two indoor scenes in the SARD dataset, and cross-dataset transfer to the office scene collapses (60% AUROC with MIT-67 training), leaving open whether the approach generalizes beyond tightly controlled settings. The authors note future directions including video extension via dynamic scene graphs and LLM-based textual feedback, but these remain unexplored.
