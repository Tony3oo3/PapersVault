## Problem

Vision-language models are trained almost entirely on 2D images and 1D text, leaving them fundamentally unable to reason about volumetric, physical spaces such as relative positions under viewpoint changes or object interaction outcomes. Existing 3D-aware VLMs rely on dense 3D supervision, curated datasets, or architectural modifications that limit scalability and prevent leveraging proprietary models like GPT-5. The paper asks whether a VLM can achieve 3D spatial reasoning through minimal, symbolic abstractions—mirroring how humans use coarse relational understanding—rather than precise geometric reconstruction.

## Input / Output

The system accepts a single RGB image (or a small set of views) and a natural-language query, and produces a structured text answer with an explicit <thinking> reasoning block followed by an <answer> block. No training or fine-tuning is required; the framework is a plug-and-play test-time pipeline that wraps around any existing VLM API (GPT-4o, GPT-5-mini, GPT-5, etc.). The VLM backbone is invoked multiple times: once to select a camera-motion direction, once to identify task-relevant object categories with approximate pixel centers, and finally to reason over the rendered 3D sandbox context.

## Method

SandboxVLM is a training-free, four-stage pipeline that converts a 2D image into a compact set of abstract 3D oriented bounding boxes for VLM reasoning. First, a video diffusion prior (SEVA) generates multi-view observations along trajectories whose direction is abstractly controlled by the VLM selecting from a discrete set of camera motions (left, fwd-left, fwd, fwd-right, right). Second, the Proxy Elevation module uses the VLM to identify task-relevant objects, SAM 2 to segment them, morphological erosion plus Farthest Point Sampling to select ~30 interior proxy pixels per object per view, and VGGT depth/camera estimation to back-project these into 3D. Third, Multi-View Voting and Clustering applies a cross-view consistency filter (a point is retained only if N other views contain a matching point within threshold δ), then DBSCAN separates instances and PCA-based OBB fitting produces oriented bounding boxes. Finally, the abstract boxes are rendered from a step-back view (2 m behind the original camera) and a top-down bird's-eye view, and these renderings are composed with the original image and query into a prompt for the VLM to perform spatial and physical reasoning.

## Results

SandboxVLM achieves 81.4% average accuracy across spatial reasoning benchmarks (BLINK-Spatial, BLINK-Depth, EmbSpatial, SAT-Real) and 58.3% on PhysBench, outperforming all generalist VLMs, training-based models, and the test-time scaling baseline MindJourney. On SAT-Real specifically, SandboxVLM reaches 84.1%, surpassing the strongest generalist baseline GPT-5-mini (75.4%) by 8.7% and MindJourney (78.7%) by 5.4%. With GPT-4o as backbone, the method yields a 17.4% absolute gain over the vanilla baseline (60.3% → 77.7%), and with GPT-5 it adds a further 4.2% (80.1% → 84.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against generalist VLMs, SandboxVLM (81.4% Spatial-Avg) outperforms GPT-5-mini (78.5%) by 2.9%, Gemini-2.5-Pro (80.3%) by 1.1%, and Qwen2.5-VL-32B (77.5%) by 3.9%; the largest gap is over GPT-4o (71.0%) at +10.4%. Among training-based models, SandboxVLM edges out RoboBrain2.0-32B (81.0%) by 0.4% on Spatial-Avg and leads on SAT-Real (84.1% vs. 80.3%), but trails on BLINK-Spatial (83.7% vs. 87.4%) and EmbSpatial (75.4% vs. 76.6%), where task-specific fine-tuning provides an advantage. Against Robix-32B-Base, SandboxVLM wins on SAT-Real (84.1% vs. 79.6%) but loses on EmbSpatial (75.4% vs. 79.0%). The closest and most relevant comparison is MindJourney (79.1%), the only other test-time scaling method: SandboxVLM leads by 2.3% on Spatial-Avg, 5.4% on SAT-Real, and 3.4% on PhysBench (58.3% vs. 54.9%), confirming that explicit 3D abstraction outperforms sequential 2D image generation for spatial reasoning. Cosmos-Reason1 (65.6%) and Magma-8B (66.9%) are substantially outperformed by 15.8% and 14.5% respectively, while VeBrain-8B (72.0%) trails by 9.4%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant training-based 3D-LLM paradigm (3D-LLM, Cube-LLM, ShapeLLM, MiniGPT-3D) that injects dense point-cloud or multi-view features into VLM backbones, arguing that such approaches require heavy 3D supervision and cannot leverage proprietary models. It also contrasts with test-time scaling methods like MindJourney that enrich VLMs with generated multi-view sequences but remain in 2D/sequential representations. SandboxVLM repurposes off-the-shelf components (video diffusion for multi-view priors, depth estimation, segmentation) into a novel pipeline whose conceptual contribution is the 'abstract perception' principle: representing scenes as sparse oriented bounding boxes rather than dense geometry, inspired by how humans reason spatially through coarse relational cues.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play design means the framework can immediately benefit from future proprietary VLM improvements without retraining, offering a scalable path to spatial intelligence. The ablation finding that abstract bounding boxes outperform both dense point clouds and raw proxy points for VLM consumption suggests a broader principle: VLMs may benefit more from interpretable structural summaries than from high-fidelity 3D data. Key limitations remain—static scenes only, no explicit physical properties (mass, friction), and modest gains on simpler benchmarks like BLINK—leaving open whether this abstraction level suffices for dynamic embodied tasks or fine-grained physical reasoning.
