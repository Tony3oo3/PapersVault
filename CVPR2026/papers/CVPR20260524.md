## Problem

Existing video token compression methods rely on two-stage spatiotemporal strategies with independent scoring per dimension, implicitly assuming spatiotemporal redundancy is separable. Under ultra-low retention ratios (≤5%), this assumption breaks down, causing unbalanced token allocation that preserves redundant tokens while discarding critical visual evidence. Additionally, inner-LLM pruning methods that use only last-token attention introduce positional bias and fail to capture query-relevant content.

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of video frames (32 frames × 196 tokens for LLaVA-OneVision, 64 frames × 169 tokens for LLaVA-Video) along with a text query, and produces a compressed set of visual tokens as low as 2% of the original (approximately 4 tokens per frame) that are fed into the LLM to generate a text answer. The compression operates in two phases: an external unified spatiotemporal filter before the LLM, and an internal text-aware merging step starting at layer 18 that further halves the retained tokens. The entire pipeline is training-free and plug-and-play, requiring no architectural modification to the underlying Video-LLM.

## Method

The paper reformulates video token compression as a global spatiotemporal allocation problem under a single retention pool constraint, rather than separate spatial and temporal stages. A selection module jointly scores each token by CLS-attention contribution and maximum cosine similarity to already-retained tokens, iteratively admitting high-contribution, low-redundancy tokens until the pool is full. Unselected tokens are merged via DPC-KNN density-peaks clustering in the recycle pool, and the resulting cluster centroids are refilled into the retention pool to preserve semantic completeness. Inside the LLM, a text-aware merging mechanism computes a weighted decision score combining normalized cross-attention from text tokens to each visual token and maximum cosine similarity between visual and text tokens, then merges pruned tokens into their most similar retained counterparts via averaging. This dual mechanism mitigates RoPE-induced positional bias and ensures query-relevant visual content is preferentially preserved.

## Results

On LLaVA-OneVision-7B, the proposed method retains 90.1% of baseline performance (50.7 avg score vs. 56.3) while reducing FLOPs to only 2.6% at a 2% token retention ratio. At the extreme 1% retention setting, it still preserves 84.1% of original performance (47.3 avg score) with 2.0% FLOPs. Across five benchmarks (MVBench, EgoSchema, MLVU, LongVideoBench, VideoMME), the method consistently outperforms all five baselines at every tested retention ratio from 10% down to 1%, with the largest margins appearing at the most aggressive compression levels.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five training-free baselines: FastV (inner-LLM attention pruning), VisionZip (spatial compression), LLaVA-Scissor (semantic connected components), FastVID (density clustering with spatiotemporal merging), and HoliTom (dynamic programming with two-stage pruning). The closest competitor is HoliTom, which the proposed method outperforms at every retention ratio: 90.1% vs. 87.7% at 2% retention and 84.1% vs. 82.9% at 1% retention, with the gap widening on MLVU (40.3 vs. 37.4 at 2%). FastVID, the second-strongest baseline, trails by a larger margin (83.3% at 2%, 77.7% at 1%), confirming that the unified spatiotemporal allocation strategy avoids the unbalanced resource allocation inherent in staged approaches. VisionZip and FastV, which operate on only one dimension (spatial or temporal respectively), suffer the largest performance drops (75.3% and 81.0% at 2%), and FastV additionally incurs 5× higher FLOPs (13.3% vs. 2.6%) because it defers compression to inside the LLM. LLaVA-Scissor is only reported at 10% and 5% retention, where the proposed method also leads (98.4% vs. not fully reported at 10%; 95.4% vs. partial scores at 5%). The advantage is most pronounced at ultra-low retention (≤2%), validating the paper's core claim that the spatiotemporal separability assumption breaks down under extreme compression.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage spatiotemporal compression paradigm (exemplified by FastVid and HoliTom) that treats spatial and temporal redundancy as separable, instead reformulating token selection as a single global allocation problem over a unified retention pool. It builds on attention-based spatial pruning (VisionZip, PruMerge) and temporal merging (DyCoke, TempMe) but integrates them into a joint scoring mechanism combining CLS-attention contribution with cosine-similarity redundancy. For inner-LLM compression, it extends the FastV/PDrop line of last-token-attention pruning by introducing a text-aware merging strategy that incorporates cross-attention from query tokens and semantic similarity to counteract RoPE-induced positional bias. The DPC-KNN density-peaks clustering algorithm from the classical clustering literature is repurposed for merging unselected tokens into the retention pool.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: retaining only ~2% of visual tokens (roughly 4 tokens per frame) while preserving 90.1% of baseline performance and reducing FLOPs to 2.6% makes long-video understanding feasible on consumer hardware, and the training-free, plug-and-play design means immediate adoption without retraining. The finding that the spatiotemporal separability assumption breaks down at ultra-low retention ratios provides a useful diagnostic for why staged methods degrade disproportionately. Open questions include whether the CLS-attention proxy generalizes to encoders without a natural global token, whether the method extends to streaming or arbitrarily long videos, and whether the 74 ms processing overhead of the unified selection becomes a bottleneck at very high frame counts.
