## Problem

Existing AIGC systems struggle with visual effect (VFX) generation because supernatural and stylized effects are out-of-domain for real-world video distributions, effect-specific training data is scarce, and prior methods require per-effect LoRA fine-tuning that limits scalability to open-set effects. Text prompts alone cannot capture the nuanced texture, motion dynamics, and spatial structure of complex VFX, making reference-based transfer a more natural but technically challenging alternative.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference VFX video (temporally downsampled to 17 frames, shorter side 448 px) and a user-provided target image (shorter side 704 px) as input, and produces an 81-frame VFX video (longer side adjusted to preserve the target's aspect ratio) in which the reference effect is transferred onto the target subject. The MLLM additionally consumes a system prompt and user instruction to guide its reasoning. In a secondary 'reference rephrasing' mode, only the reference video is provided and the model regenerates a new video reproducing both content and effect.

## Method

EffectMaker couples a Qwen3-VL-8B MLLM (understanding/reasoning) with a Wan2.2-TI2V-5B video DiT (generation) in a semantic-visual dual-path guidance scheme. The MLLM produces two complementary feature streams—semantic-understanding features from its last-layer hidden states and semantic-reasoning features from autoregressively predicted text tokens—which are injected into the DiT via a decoupled cross-attention mechanism (separate key/value projections for each stream, fused by addition, applied only to target tokens). For visual-level conditioning, reference and target latents are concatenated along the sequence dimension and processed by a dual-stream self-attention where reference and target tokens use distinct Q/K/V projections while still attending bidirectionally, with a scaled-and-biased 3D RoPE to separate their positional spaces. The model is trained end-to-end for ~50k steps on 32 H20 GPUs over EffectData (130k synthetic VFX videos across 3k categories) supplemented by OpenVFX and Higgsfield samples, eliminating the need for per-effect fine-tuning.

## Results

On the OpenVFX benchmark (14 effect classes, 10 subjects each), EffectMaker achieves the best average scores across all four metrics: Visual Quality (VQ) 2.84, Motion Quality (MQ) 0.25, Text Alignment (TA) −0.24, and Class Alignment Score (CAS) 4.63. The largest margins are in VQ (+0.57 over Omni-Effect, +0.95 over Wan2.2-FT) and CAS (+0.23 over Omni-Effect, +1.04 over Wan2.2-FT). The ablation confirms that combining semantic and visual conditioning (VQ 2.92, CAS 4.40, RAS 4.16) outperforms either alone, and scaling training data from 100 to 1000 effect classes improves RAS from 3.22 to 4.04.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EffectMaker outperforms all three baselines on every metric. Against VFXCreator [25], which requires per-effect LoRA fine-tuning, EffectMaker gains +0.78 in VQ, +0.28 in MQ, +0.68 in TA, and +0.29 in CAS, while being a single feed-forward model. Against Omni-Effect [27], which uses a mixture-of-LoRA over 55 categories, EffectMaker leads by +0.57 VQ, +0.39 MQ, +0.37 TA, and +0.23 CAS. The most telling comparison is against Wan2.2-FT (the same DiT backbone fine-tuned on the same data without reference conditioning): EffectMaker's reference-based paradigm yields +0.95 VQ and +1.04 CAS, demonstrating that in-context visual conditioning captures fine-grained effect dynamics that text-only conditioning cannot. On open-set unseen effects (portal, plastic model, green tree glowing), Omni-Effect fails to produce plausible results entirely, while EffectMaker successfully reproduces the reference effect with high consistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

EffectMaker builds on the Wan2.2-TI2V-5B diffusion transformer backbone and adapts the in-context learning paradigm from FullDiT (reference-target latent concatenation with shared attention) to the VFX transfer setting. It departs from prior VFX methods (VFXCreator, Omni-Effect) that required per-effect LoRA fine-tuning or mixture-of-LoRA training on closed effect sets, instead framing effect creation as a reference-based, feed-forward task. The key architectural departure is the introduction of a Qwen3-VL-8B MLLM as a reasoning front-end that produces both understanding and reasoning features, which are injected via a decoupled cross-attention mechanism alongside the visual in-context conditioning—forming a semantic-visual dual-path guidance not present in prior VFX or reference-based video generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two practical bottlenecks in VFX generation: data scarcity (mitigated by EffectData, a 130k-video / 3k-category synthetic dataset) and the inflexibility of per-effect fine-tuning (eliminated by the reference-based, reasoning-guided paradigm). The MLLM reasoning component lowers the user barrier by automatically adapting effect descriptions to novel subjects, making the system more interactive than prior methods that required manually crafted prompts. Open questions remain around handling rapid or large-amplitude motions (limited by the base model's capacity) and whether synthetic training data can capture the noise, ambiguity, and multi-effect overlap of real-world VFX. The dataset and the dual-path conditioning recipe are likely to be useful building blocks for future reference-based video generation work beyond VFX.
