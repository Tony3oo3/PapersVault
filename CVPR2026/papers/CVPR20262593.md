## Problem

Despite extensive study of MLLM internals, it remains unclear at which specific layers visual and textual information are fused, how attention patterns evolve across depth, and why final-layer attention often exhibits persistent high-activation noise on task-irrelevant regions. This paper addresses both the interpretability gap (where and how fusion occurs) and the practical consequence (attention noise degrading visual grounding) by systematically probing layer-wise fusion behavior and proposing a training-free remedy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262593/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image paired with a free-form text question and produces a text answer, but does so via a two-pass inference: the first pass computes layer-wise attention maps to derive a contrastive attention signal, which is used to segment the image into task-relevant patches; the second pass re-feeds the original image concatenated with the segmented crop back into the MLLM. No additional training or fine-tuning is required, and the approach is model-agnostic across LLaVA, InstructBLIP, and Qwen2VL-family architectures.

## Method

The core mechanism is a training-free contrastive attention framework that captures how visual attention shifts between an early 'pre-integrated' layer and the final layer. The pre-integrated layer is identified dynamically by computing the Hellinger distance between each layer's attention distribution over image tokens and that of the final layer, selecting the layer with maximum divergence (constrained to empirically identified fusion layers for best results). The contrastive attention is the element-wise difference A^(k) − A^(i*), which suppresses persistent noise shared across layers and highlights text-guided attention shifts. This signal is used to segment the original image, retaining patches with the largest attention change, and the segmented crop is concatenated with the full image for a second inference pass. The approach is grounded in systematic layer-wise visual masking experiments across seven MLLMs and six benchmarks, which reveal a consistent shallow-to-middle fusion pattern and a late-stage 'review' phenomenon in certain architectures.

## Results

The proposed contrastive attention method achieves the highest average accuracy across all three evaluated MLLMs: 59.16% on LLaVA-1.5, 68.65% on LLaVA-Next, and 77.66% on Qwen2.5VL, outperforming the strongest baseline (OPERA or ViCrop) by 1.05, 0.92, and 0.37 points respectively. Gains are most pronounced on TextVQA (e.g., 59.86% vs. 58.72% for OPERA on LLaVA-1.5; 75.80% vs. 73.49% for ViCrop on LLaVA-Next) and VizWiz (60.35% vs. 58.61% for OPERA on LLaVA-1.5). The method also maintains comparable inference time to other training-free approaches, with only minimal overhead from the contrastive attention computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method consistently outperforms all four baselines (CD, DoLA, OPERA, ViCrop) across every model and benchmark. On LLaVA-1.5, it beats the strongest baseline OPERA by 1.05 points in average accuracy (59.16% vs. 58.11%), with the largest margin on TextVQA (+1.14) and VizWiz (+1.74). On LLaVA-Next, the advantage over OPERA is 0.92 points (68.65% vs. 67.73%), driven primarily by TextVQA (+2.32) and DocVQA (+1.16). On Qwen2.5VL, the margin over the best baseline ViCrop is 0.37 points (77.66% vs. 77.29%), with gains on GQA (+0.86), VQAv2 (+0.33), and TextVQA (+0.68). Notably, CD and DoLA—logit-contrast methods designed for text-only factuality—underperform or even degrade below the base model on several benchmarks (e.g., CD drops OKVQA from 52.49% to 50.11% on LLaVA-Next), confirming that text-level contrastive decoding is less suited to visual grounding tasks. ViCrop, the closest method in paradigm (re-input with cropped regions), is the strongest competitor but requires manually tuned layer selection per model, whereas the proposed method selects layers dynamically via Hellinger distance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the interpretability line of work on MLLMs (causal tracing by Palit et al., attention-level surveys by Zhao et al.) by providing the first systematic layer-wise masking analysis across seven architectures, revealing where visual-text fusion actually occurs. Methodologically, it builds on ViCrop's re-input paradigm for visual refinement but departs from its fixed, manually-selected layer design by introducing a dynamic Hellinger-distance-based layer selection. The contrastive principle is borrowed from logit-level contrastive decoding (CD, DoLA) but repurposed to operate on visual attention maps across layers rather than on output distributions, targeting a different failure mode (persistent attention noise on irrelevant regions).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of specific fusion layers and the 'review' phenomenon in certain architectures provides a concrete, interpretable map of where vision-language integration happens, which could inform future architectural design or targeted fine-tuning. The training-free contrastive attention method offers a practical, plug-and-play improvement (1–3 points over strong baselines) without additional training, making it immediately deployable. However, the method requires a second inference pass (re-input), doubling compute for the visual branch, and the modest gains raise questions about whether the attention-noise hypothesis fully explains the remaining error. Open questions include whether the fusion-layer positions are stable across training regimes and whether the approach generalizes to video or multi-image inputs.
