## Problem

Existing video-based commonsense captioning models follow a 'video → content caption → commonsense' reasoning chain that causes excessive reliance on textual captions, producing generic commonsense with limited visual grounding. Additionally, these models employ isolated decoders for each commonsense category (intentions, effects, attributes), failing to exploit the mutual correlations between different categories that could improve generation quality.

## Input / Output

The model takes a video clip as input and extracts multi-modal features via ResNet152 (2D visual), SoundNet (1D audio), I3D (3D motion), and UMT (fine-grained overall video features), which are mapped into a shared semantic space. It produces four outputs: a video content caption describing visible actions, plus three commonsense captions—intention (why the action occurs), effect (what changes result), and attribute (how to characterize the agent). The commonsense captions are generated by three separate transformer decoders that must operate without access to ground-truth commonsense from other categories at test time.

## Method

SCD-Net introduces two complementary mechanisms to optimize the reasoning chain. First, a self-critical reinforcement strategy generates commonsense captions under two paradigms (with and without visual features), then uses the CIDER score difference as a reward signal to reinforce or penalize visual utilization via a non-differentiable gradient: −γ·tanh·(r(y^v) − r(y^wv))·∇log p(y^v). Second, a joint reasoning distillation framework employs a teacher model with a two-level cascaded decoder (first level uses ground-truth commonsense from other categories; second level uses generated commonsense) and a student model with three independent decoders, bridging the train-test gap. To handle variable-length misalignment between teacher and student outputs, Language Adaptive Wrapping Distillation (LAWD) replaces KL divergence with a dynamic-programming path-cost minimization over a teacher-student embedding distance matrix, enabling effective inter-category knowledge transfer while preserving test-time fairness.

## Results

On the Video-to-Commonsense (V2C) dataset, SCD-Net achieves CIDER scores of 106.3 (intention), 80.6 (effect), and 34.9 (attribute), outperforming the non-LLM baseline HybridNet (92.6, 66.2, 32.5) by 13.7, 14.4, and 2.4 points respectively. SCD-Net also surpasses the LLM-based TKG-Net (100.6, 77.7, 33.6) on all three CIDER metrics while using only 216M parameters versus 601M and requiring ~10% of the training time (3.2 vs. 23.5 min/epoch). ROUGE-L and BLEU-4 scores follow the same trend, with SCD-Net reaching 63.7/58.1 (intention), 46.5/44.8 (effect), and 42.5/56.5 (attribute).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCD-Net outperforms all seven baselines across every reported metric. Against the primary non-LLM baseline HybridNet, gains are substantial: +13.7 CIDER on intention, +14.4 on effect, and +2.4 on attribute, with corresponding ROUGE-L improvements of +3.6, +5.0, and +1.5. SCD-Net also beats the LLM-based TKG-Net (which incorporates GPT) by +5.7, +2.9, and +1.3 CIDER on intention, effect, and attribute respectively, while using roughly one-third the parameters (216M vs. 601M) and ~7.3× less training time per epoch (3.2 vs. 23.5 min). Compared to LLCP, SCD-Net leads by +12.3, +7.9, and +3.4 CIDER on the three categories. Older methods (S2VT, Att-ED, Dense-cap, CMS) trail by 40+ CIDER points on intention and 40+ on effect, confirming that the joint reasoning distillation and self-critical reinforcement strategies are critical for competitive performance. Zero-shot GPT performs poorly (CIDER 61.3/52.4/24.8), underscoring that task-specific training remains necessary even for strong LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

SCD-Net builds directly on HybridNet [31] as its backbone encoder-decoder architecture for video-based commonsense captioning, extending the 'video → content caption → commonsense' reasoning chain that prior methods (HybridNet, CMS, S2VT) rely on. The key methodological departures are twofold: (1) adapting self-critical reinforcement learning from image captioning [21] to a video commonsense setting with a novel reward that contrasts visual vs. text-only generation, and (2) introducing a two-level cascaded teacher-student distillation framework with Language Adaptive Wrapping Distillation (LAWD) borrowed from cross-lingual summarization [16] to transfer inter-category commonsense knowledge without requiring ground-truth inputs at test time. The paper explicitly positions itself as an alternative to LLM-augmented approaches like TKG-Net [34], arguing that the semantic gap can be bridged without GPT-level resource costs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a 216M-parameter model trained on a single V100 can match or exceed a 601M-parameter LLM-based pipeline (TKG-Net) across all three commonsense categories, reducing per-epoch training time from 23.5 to 3.2 minutes. However, the CIDER margins over the LLM baseline are modest (5.7, 2.9, 1.3 for intention/effect/attribute), and all results are confined to a single dataset (V2C) with no multi-seed or cross-dataset validation. Open questions include whether the inter-category correlation hypothesis generalizes to richer commonsense taxonomies, whether the self-critical reward signal remains informative at larger model scales, and whether the LAWD alignment strategy transfers to other variable-length distillation settings beyond commonsense captioning.
