## Problem

Existing benchmarks for proactive mobile agents (e.g., ProactiveAgent, FingerTip-20K) oversimplify the task by assuming a single correct action per scenario, rely on coarse or semantically shallow evaluation metrics (binary rewards, cosine similarity), and produce non-executable natural language recommendations. This confluence of an ill-defined one-to-one task, superficial evaluation, and a gap between suggestion and execution critically bottlenecks systematic progress toward truly proactive mobile agents.

## Input / Output

The model receives a four-dimensional context at a decision moment: user profile, device status, and world information as natural language text, plus behavioral trajectories as either textual action logs or sequences of GUI screenshots (averaging ~7–8 images per instance). The output is a paired natural-language recommendation instruction and an executable function-call sequence drawn from a predefined pool of 63 APIs; a zero-function sequence signals that no action should be taken. Each instance carries 1–3 valid ground-truth intent-function pairs, making evaluation a one-to-many matching problem rather than a single-answer classification.

## Method

ProactiveMobile is constructed via a five-stage generation pipeline: (1) contextual information (profile, device, world) is synthesized by randomly sampling from Claude-Sonnet-4, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and GPT-5, then plausibility-checked by o1; (2) six SOTA MLLMs generate candidate intentions, which Gemini-2.5-Flash clusters to extract top-3 representatives; (3) task-irrelevant noise (5–20× the relevant signal) is injected to stress robustness; (4) Claude-Sonnet-4 maps intentions to function sequences from the 63-API pool; (5) a three-stage review (rule-based filters, Gemini-2.5-Pro agent evaluation, and 30-expert human audit over four months) validates every entry. For training, full-parameter SFT is applied to Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and MiMo-VL-7B-SFT-2508 on 8,876 instances, with the model co-generating both the recommendation text and the function sequence. Evaluation uses a two-stage Best-Match Selection Protocol: an LLM judge (Gemini-2.5-Pro) first checks for perfect functional equivalence against any ground-truth sequence, and if none is found, falls back to F1-score matching on unordered function-name sets to identify the closest ground truth for analysis.

## Results

On the ProactiveMobile test set, the fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B+Proactive achieves a Success Rate (SR) of 20.82% and a False Trigger Rate (FTR) of 13.76%, establishing a new state-of-the-art that surpasses the strongest proprietary baseline, o1 (SR 17.02%, FTR 14.09%). The fine-tuned MiMo-VL-7B-SFT+Proactive also outperforms all closed-source models except o1, reaching 13.47% SR. On the out-of-distribution (OOD) test set (64 instances from two unseen scenarios), Qwen2.5-VL-7B+Proactive maintains a 20.31% SR, still leading o1 (15.63%) and GPT-5 (14.55%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B+Proactive (20.82% SR) outperforms all six baselines: o1 (17.02%), GPT-5 (11.37%), Gemini-2.5-Pro (9.62%), GPT-4o (6.60%), and the zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-7B (1.61%) and MiMo-VL-7B (1.31%). On the safety metric (FTR), Qwen+Proactive (13.76%) is on par with o1 (14.09%) and substantially better than GPT-5 (39.20%), GPT-4o (65.32%), and Gemini-2.5-Pro (74.98%). The second fine-tuned model, MiMo-VL-7B-SFT+Proactive (13.47% SR), also beats GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, and GPT-4o but trails o1 by ~3.5 points. On the OOD split, Qwen+Proactive (20.31% SR) leads o1 (15.63%) and GPT-5 (14.55%), confirming that the learned proactive skill generalizes beyond in-distribution scenarios. Notably, the zero-shot versions of both open-source models perform near-randomly (1.3–1.6% SR), underscoring that proactivity is not an emergent capability of current MLLMs without targeted training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the reactive mobile-agent paradigm (GUI grounding, task planning) and the early proactive-agent benchmarks (ProactiveAgent, FingerTip-20K), which the authors argue oversimplify the task by assuming a single correct action and relying on natural-language output with coarse or semantic-similarity evaluation. ProactiveMobile carves new ground by formalizing proactivity as a four-dimensional context-to-executable-function-sequence mapping with multi-answer annotations and a 63-API function pool, bridging the gap between intent inference and on-device execution that prior benchmarks left open. It also builds on the broader MLLM-based mobile agent literature (GUI-Odyssey, AITZ, CAGUI) for behavioral trajectory data, but repurposes these sources to construct proactive rather than reactive training signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical capability gap: even frontier models (GPT-5, o1, Gemini-2.5-Pro) achieve only 9–17% success rate, while a fine-tuned 7B model reaches 21%, confirming proactivity as a specialized, learnable skill rather than an emergent property of scale. The persistent multimodal-to-text performance gap (15.6% vs. 26.0% SR) and the safety trade-off revealed by the ablation (adding a Think step slashes FTR to 2% but drops SR to 8%) highlight open problems in visual grounding for intent prediction and reliable non-triggering. As the first benchmark mandating executable function-call output with objective evaluation, ProactiveMobile is likely to become a standard testbed for the proactive-agent subfield, though its 3,660-instance scale and 14-scenario coverage remain modest for capturing the full diversity of real-world mobile use.
