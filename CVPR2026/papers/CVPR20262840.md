## Problem

Existing bimanual manipulation methods face a fundamental tension: decoupling the two arms preserves independence but sacrifices cross-arm consistency, while enforcing strong inter-arm coupling improves coordination but undermines disentanglability and robustness. No prior framework unifies these opposing objectives, leaving a gap in how to achieve both arm-level autonomy and synchronized collaboration within a single architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262840/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes three RGB camera views (left wrist, right wrist, and a shared head-mounted view) along with proprioceptive joint states, encoded with observation horizon O=1 and prediction horizon H=8. Each view is processed by an independent ResNet-18 backbone while joint states pass through a lightweight MLP. The output is a synchronized dual-arm action trajectory of length 8, generated via DDIM sampling in 10 steps at inference, where the action vector is partitioned along the motion dimension into left-arm and right-arm components.

## Method

CUBic reformulates bimanual coordination as a unified perceptual modeling problem through three components: (1) a masked-attention transformer that aggregates multi-view features into arm-specific latent tokens while enforcing strict information insulation between arms; (2) a bidirectional coordination module that quantizes both arms' latent tokens into dual codebooks sharing a unified mapping space, where the optimal codebook index minimizes the *combined* L2 distance from both arms, creating implicit cross-arm coupling without explicit attention; and (3) a DiT-based diffusion policy that decodes coordinated tokens into action trajectories. A two-stage training paradigm is critical: stage 1 trains each arm's policy branch independently with separate self-attention to stabilize discrete representation learning, then stage 2 freezes all perception parameters and merges the self-attention layers into a shared module visible to both arms, introducing structured cross-arm correlations in the diffusion noise while keeping cross-attention pathways to perceptual tokens separated. Residual vector quantization (RVQ) with a straight-through gradient estimator and a commitment loss (β-weighted) stabilizes the codebook learning.

## Results

On the RoboTwin benchmark (7 bimanual manipulation tasks, 100 demos each, 50 eval trials × 3 seeds), CUBic achieves a 51.8% average success rate, surpassing DP3 (39.8%) by 12.0%, DP (38.5%) by 13.3%, and GR-MG (8.0%) by 43.8%. The largest gains appear on coordination-heavy tasks: Pick Apple Messy (40.0% vs. 9.7% for DP3) and Block Handover (85.7% vs. 77.3% for DP3). In real-world experiments on a dual-arm Agibot, CUBic scores 43.1/100 in-domain and 34.7/100 out-of-domain, compared to DP's 19.5 and 12.7 respectively (+23.6 and +22.0 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CUBic outperforms all three baselines on average across the 7 RoboTwin tasks. Against DP3 (3D Diffusion Policy), CUBic gains +12.0% on average, with the most dramatic improvement on Pick Apple Messy (40.0% vs. 9.7%) and Block Handover (85.7% vs. 77.3%), while matching DP3 on Dual Bottles Pick (Hard) at 58.0%. Against DP (standard Diffusion Policy), CUBic gains +13.3% on average, winning decisively on Put Apple Cabinet (68.7% vs. 8.0%) and Pick Apple Messy (40.0% vs. 29.3%), though DP edges out CUBic slightly on Dual Bottles Pick (Easy) (85.7% vs. 84.3%). GR-MG is far behind on every task (8.0% average), failing completely on Blocks Stack, Block Handover, Dual Shoes Place, and Put Apple Cabinet. In real-world evaluation, CUBic consistently beats DP by 22–24 points across both in-domain and out-of-domain settings, with the largest gap on Bottle Grasp (77.5 vs. 10.0 in-domain).

## Relation to Prior Work

CUBic builds directly on the Diffusion Policy framework (Chi et al., IJRR 2024) for action generation and the DiT architecture (Peebles & Xie, 2022) as the transformer backbone, while incorporating residual vector quantization (Lee et al., CVPR 2022) to structure the latent perceptual space. Its key conceptual departure is from the two competing bimanual paradigms—decoupling (AnyBimanual, VoxAct-B) and explicit cross-arm coupling (Mobile ALOHA, INTERACT)—by reframing coordination as a unified perceptual modeling problem where independence and interaction both emerge from a shared dual-codebook token space rather than being enforced by handcrafted structural choices. The masked-attention aggregation stage draws on standard transformer masking, while the two-stage training recipe (independent pretraining → shared self-attention posttraining) is a practical adaptation of curriculum-style training to the diffusion policy setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified tokenized representation offers a principled way to resolve the long-standing independence-vs-coordination tension in bimanual manipulation, and the ablation confirming that the shared codebook mapping and two-stage training each contribute ~8–10 points validates the design choices. However, absolute success rates remain modest (51.8% average in simulation, 43.1/100 in real-world), and the real-world evaluation is limited to six tasks with 100 demonstrations each on a single Agibot platform, leaving open whether the approach scales to more arms, longer horizons, or diverse embodiments. A key open question is whether the dual-codebook mechanism generalizes beyond two arms or whether the shared-mapping constraint becomes a bottleneck in higher-dimensional coordination spaces.
