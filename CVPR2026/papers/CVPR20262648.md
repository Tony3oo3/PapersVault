## Problem

Zero-shot identity-preserving generation models (e.g., Insert Anything, OminiControl) perform well on humans and rigid objects but fail to maintain fine-grained identity on non-rigid categories like birds, where subtle visual cues distinguish species and individuals. This gap is compounded by the absence of subject-consistent training data and a dedicated evaluation benchmark for bird identity preservation, making it difficult to measure or improve generation fidelity in scientifically important fine-grained domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262648/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a subject bird image (identity source), a control image derived from a target image, and optionally a text caption. Three control modes are supported: Fill (SAM2-segmented mask with background for inpainting), Depth (Video-Depth-Anything depth map of the target), and Keypoints (11 annotated body parts rendered as a colored skeleton). All inputs and outputs are 1024×1024 RGB images; the generated image should match the subject's appearance while adopting the target's pose and background context.

## Method

The key insight is proxy identity training: rather than requiring true same-individual pairs (which don't exist at scale for birds), the authors sample training pairs from the same NABirds class (species, age, sex, breeding status), treating taxonomic identity as a coarse proxy for individual identity. Two architectures are fine-tuned: Insert Anything, where subject and masked-background images are stitched into a diptych panel and a FLUX.1-Fill backbone is trained with LoRA, and OminiControl, where subject and condition latent tokens are appended to noisy latents before a DiT, with spatially-aware encoding applied only to the control image. Each control mode is trained separately on FLUX.1-Schnell and FLUX.1-Kontext backbones for 10,000 iterations on 4 A100/H100 GPUs. The authors also introduce NABLA, a 4,759-pair expert-curated benchmark where bird experts selected visually identical individuals within each species, and validate that NABLA scores correlate strongly with ground-truth identity pairs from iNaturalist multi-image observations.

## Results

On the NABLA benchmark (4,759 expert-curated bird pairs across 401 species), the best fine-tuned model (OminiControl with FLUX-Kontext, Fill control) achieves DINOv2 similarity of 0.78, SigLIP similarity of 0.94, LPIPS of 0.060, and MSE of 51.0, substantially outperforming the unfine-tuned OminiControl-Schnell baseline (MSE 77.1, DINO 0.41). The paper reports a 41% MSE reduction over the baseline on NABLA. Critically, improvements generalize to unseen species: on iNat-Unseen, the fine-tuned Om-K Fill model achieves MSE 46.9 versus 80.8 for the unfine-tuned baseline, confirming that proxy-identity training (species, age, sex) transfers beyond the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the unfine-tuned OminiControl-Schnell baseline (Om-S*), fine-tuning yields dramatic gains: on NABLA Fill, MSE drops from 77.1 to 51.0 (Om-K) and DINOv2 similarity rises from 0.41 to 0.78, while on iNat-Unseen MSE improves from 80.8 to 46.9. Against the unfine-tuned Insert Anything baseline (Ins-A*), the fine-tuned Om-K Fill model achieves lower MSE (51.0 vs. 62.9 on NABLA; 46.9 vs. 54.4 on iNat-Unseen) and higher DINOv2 (0.78 vs. 0.75), though the fine-tuned Ins-A also improves over its own baseline (MSE 62.9→55.0). Qualitatively, both proprietary models—Nano Banana (Gemini 2.5) and GPT-4V—fail to preserve subject identity or match target pose in the bird domain, introducing incorrect patterns, wrong head/wing positions, and altered species-level features (Figures 7–8), whereas the fine-tuned open models maintain both identity and pose constraints. Inpainting (Fill) control outperforms depth-map control across all datasets despite masks being less pose-specific, likely because depth-based training introduces background ambiguity that degrades the diffusion loss. Keypoint-based control is the weakest mode (MSE 66.7 on NABLA), confirming that birds' deformable anatomy makes skeleton-based specification insufficient.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the identity-preserving generation line of work (DreamBooth, Insert Anything, OminiControl) from its traditional domains—virtual try-on, rigid objects, and AI-generated training data—into the fine-grained natural category of birds, where non-rigid deformations and class-specific visual details make the task substantially harder. The key methodological departure is using taxonomic structure (species, age, sex, breeding status) as a proxy for identity during training, since true same-subject multi-view data for birds is scarce and low quality. The NABLA benchmark itself is novel: no prior evaluation set existed for identity-preserving generation in fine-grained natural categories, and the paper validates it by showing strong correlation with ground-truth same-individual pairs from iNaturalist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NABLA provides the first rigorous, expert-curated benchmark for identity-preserving generation in a fine-grained domain, filling a gap that limits scientific and educational applications where accuracy is paramount. The demonstration that proxy-identity training generalizes to unseen species suggests a practical path for domains where true same-subject data is unavailable. However, the task remains unsolved: drastic pose changes still fail, keypoint control is far inferior to inpainting/depth, and the authors note that a simple depth+fill combination yielded little improvement—leaving open how to achieve both flexible control and faithful identity preservation simultaneously.
