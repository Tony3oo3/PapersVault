## Problem

High-performing spiking neural networks still rely on dense multilayer architectures that demand extensive neuron-to-neuron communication and large state storage, limiting spatial efficiency for resource-constrained and neuromorphic deployment. While single biological neurons (e.g., cerebellar Purkinje cells) achieve rich computation through autaptic self-feedback, existing engineering approaches to single-neuron computation either use non-biological continuous-valued hardware or neglect the role of temporal delay in enhancing spiking dynamics. The paper asks whether a single LIF neuron augmented with time-delayed autapses can constructively replicate the computational roles of entire SNN layers.

## Input / Output

Inputs span physiological signal sequences (DEAP, SHD), static RGB images (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10 at 28×28 or 32×32), and event-based frames (DVS Gesture), all converted into spike trains or continuous inputs injected at designated temporal nodes of the single neuron. The spatial structure of image or event inputs is mapped onto the neuron's internal temporal unfolding rather than distributed across parallel neurons. Outputs are binary spike sequences over a fixed time window, decoded into class labels via a spatiotemporal prototype-matching loss rather than a conventional linear readout.

## Method

The TDA-LIF neuron extends the standard LIF model by feeding its own past spikes back to its membrane potential through multiple delayed autaptic connections (Eq. 1–2), creating intrinsic temporal memory within a single unit. By unfolding the neuron's dynamics into a sequence of internal temporal nodes, the same autaptic mechanism constructively realizes three SNN architectures: reservoir computing (all nodes active with cross-delay feedback), MLP (autapses pruned to only cross segment boundaries, yielding a feedforward weight matrix), and convolution-like layers (shared autaptic weights across spatially parallel output nodes emulate kernel weight-sharing). Training uses spatio-temporal backpropagation with an arctangent surrogate gradient for spike non-differentiability, combined with a prototype-learning classification loss that matches output spike patterns to learnable binary prototypes. The key architectural insight is that spatial connectivity in standard SNNs is replaced by temporal self-feedback, reducing the physical neuron count per layer from N to 1 at the cost of additional temporal latency.

## Results

In the reservoir-computing setting, TDA-SNN outperforms the standard SNN baseline at larger scales, reaching 88.65 ± 0.48% on DEAP and 80.68 ± 0.83% on SHD (128 nodes) versus 79.92 ± 0.54% and 77.63 ± 2.53% for STD-SNN, respectively. In the MLP setting, TDA-SNN achieves 98.23 ± 0.09% on MNIST, 89.16 ± 0.12% on Fashion-MNIST, and 72.95 ± 2.02% on DVS Gesture at 256 nodes, matching or approaching STD-SNN performance. In the convolutional setting, TDA-SNN underperforms (57.58% vs. 73.61% on DVS Gesture; 37.64% vs. 47.31% on CIFAR-10), revealing a clear space-time trade-off. Per-neuron information content increases dramatically (e.g., 32,096 bits vs. 490 bits in RC), reflecting the extreme neuron-count reduction from N to 1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is a structurally equivalent standard SNN (STD-SNN) with matched neuron counts and training protocols. In the RC setting, TDA-SNN surpasses STD-SNN at medium-to-large reservoir sizes: on DEAP, TDA-SNN reaches 88.65% at 256 nodes versus 79.92% for STD-SNN (a ~8.7-point gain), and on SHD it achieves 80.68% at 128 nodes versus 77.63% for STD-SNN. At small scales (16 nodes), TDA-SNN lags slightly (77.59% vs. 81.21% on DEAP), indicating that temporal multiplexing requires sufficient unfolded depth to be advantageous. In the MLP setting, TDA-SNN approaches or matches STD-SNN at 256 nodes (e.g., 98.23% on MNIST), with the gap narrowing as node count increases. In the convolutional setting, TDA-SNN clearly underperforms STD-SNN (57.58% vs. 73.61% on DVS Gesture; 37.64% vs. 47.31% on CIFAR-10), attributed to optimization difficulty from delayed feedback interfering with hierarchical feature aggregation. The key efficiency trade-off is that TDA-SNN reduces neuron count from N to 1 per layer, increasing per-neuron information content by ~65× in RC and ~64× in MLP, but at the cost of 178× training-time overhead in the extreme single-neuron convolutional case (reducible to 0.92× inference time with 512 parallel neurons).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of single-node temporal computation (one-core reservoir computing, single-neuron deep learning) into the spiking domain by introducing time-delayed autapses into the LIF neuron, drawing biological inspiration from cerebellar Purkinje cell self-feedback. Unlike prior single-node approaches that rely on continuous-valued signals and non-biological hardware, TDA-SNN preserves spike-based, event-driven dynamics while using the autaptic delay as the mechanism for temporal multiplexing. The training framework builds directly on the authors' prior spatiotemporal prototype learning work [6] and standard STBP with surrogate gradients, positioning the contribution as an architectural unification rather than a new learning algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is a dramatic reduction in state memory (8 KB → 4 bytes on CIFAR-10) and neuron count, which is highly relevant for resource-constrained neuromorphic hardware. However, the convolutional setting reveals a clear limitation: the single-neuron construction underperforms standard SNNs substantially, suggesting that spatial parallelism cannot be fully subsumed by temporal recursion. Open questions include whether multi-neuron parallelization can close the convolutional gap, whether adaptive or learned delay schedules improve performance, and whether the space-time trade-off can be optimized for specific deployment constraints (latency vs. memory).
