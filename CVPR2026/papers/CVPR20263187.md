## Problem

Stereo vision systems face a fundamental trade-off: monocular setups are hardware-compact but rely on data-driven priors with limited generalization, while binocular or multi-view systems improve accuracy at the cost of increased hardware complexity and data bandwidth, a problem that worsens at high frame rates. This paper asks whether a single sensor can achieve high-frame-rate (240 FPS) stereo vision with the accuracy of a binocular system while preserving the compactness and data efficiency of a monocular one.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a 1-bit mixed spike stream from a single monocular spike camera, where two views are optically blended via a beam splitter and one view is periodically attenuated at 60 Hz by an LCD modulator. Spikes are sliced by a sliding temporal window T_w, and each 60 Hz modulation period yields 4 decoupled binocular frame pairs, producing a 240 FPS stereo video. The reconstructed stereo pair is then fed into off-the-shelf stereo matchers (e.g., DEFOM-Stereo, CREStereo) to produce dense depth maps.

## Method

The approach is a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 formulates a per-pixel linear system over n spikes within a temporal window, exploiting the 60 Hz modulation to make observations linearly independent, and solves for the two view intensities via regularized least-squares (λ=1e-3), yielding a fast baseline at ~0.1 s per frame. Stage 2, SMS-Net, is a supervised CNN with three modules: an Adaptive Brightness Consistency (ABC) module that aligns multi-scale features to a full-cycle intensity map to remove temporal brightness inconsistency; a Collaborative Binocular Augment (CBA) module using Stereo Cross-Attention (SCAM) along epipolar lines to suppress spatially inconsistent cross-view artifacts; and a Recurrent Stereo Fusion (RSF) module that applies deformable convolution for spatial alignment of historical states and Guided Temporal Attention to fuse temporal context, recovering texture lost during low-transmittance phases. The network is trained end-to-end with L1 loss on synthetic mixed-spike data (TartanAir, KITTI) for 300 epochs on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

On TartanAir, the refined stereo pipeline (Refine-DEFOM) achieves AbsRel 0.0940, RMSE 1.7852, and δ₁ 0.9202, substantially outperforming the baseline decoupling (AbsRel 0.1695) and approaching the ground-truth upper bound (AbsRel 0.0498). On KITTI, Refine-DEFOM reaches AbsRel 0.4288, RMSE 4.1719, and δ₁ 0.9630, again beating the baseline (AbsRel 0.4477) and all monocular depth methods. The refinement network (SMS-Net) improves AbsRel by ~45% over the least-squares baseline on TartanAir and by ~4% on KITTI, while the 240 FPS binocular video is reconstructed from a single monocular spike camera.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's Refine-DEFOM pipeline decisively outperforms all monocular depth baselines on both datasets. Against Spike-T (monocular spike-based depth), the improvement is dramatic: AbsRel drops from 0.9675 to 0.0940 on TartanAir (10× reduction) and from 0.8000 to 0.4288 on KITTI. Compared to STIR+DepthPro (the strongest monocular baseline), Refine-DEFOM achieves lower AbsRel on both TartanAir (0.0940 vs. 0.1085) and KITTI (0.4288 vs. 0.4409), with a larger RMSE gap on KITTI (4.17 vs. 5.43). Against STIR+DepthAnythingV2, the advantage is even more pronounced, with AbsRel of 0.0940 vs. 0.4839 on TartanAir. The paper's own baseline (least-squares decoupling without SMS-Net) is also clearly surpassed, confirming the refinement network's contribution. Notably, Refine-DEFOM approaches the GT-DEFOM upper bound (AbsRel 0.0498 on TartanAir), suggesting the decoupled stereo pair is nearly as informative as ground-truth binocular input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two established lines of monocular stereo work: spatial mirror-based systems (Nene & Nayar, Gluckman & Nayar) that project views onto separate sensor sub-regions and require epipolar rectification, and mixed-view systems (Pachidis & Lygouras) that overlay views on the same sensor but rely on global disparity constraints for separation. The key conceptual shift is introducing *temporal* optical modulation (a 60 Hz LCD attenuator on one view) combined with a monocular spike camera, which converts the decoupling problem into a well-posed linear system solvable by least squares. For the refinement stage, the paper adopts the Stereo Cross-Attention Module (SCAM) from Nafssr [6] as the core of its cross-view artifact suppression, and positions itself against dual-spike-camera stereo systems (Li et al. [14], Wang et al. [29], Gao et al. [8]) by achieving comparable accuracy with a single sensor.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work demonstrates that a single spike camera with a simple LCD modulator can produce 240 FPS binocular video, offering a hardware-compact and data-efficient alternative to multi-camera stereo rigs in high-speed applications (autonomous driving, sports, aerospace). The two-stage design—fast least-squares baseline followed by a learned refinement—makes the pipeline practical, with the baseline alone already competitive with monocular depth methods. However, the approach is tightly coupled to specialized hardware (beam splitter, planar mirror, LCD modulator, spike camera), and the 60 Hz LCD constraint caps the achievable frame rate; faster modulators or alternative temporal encoding schemes would be needed to push beyond 240 FPS. An open question is whether the temporal-modulation-plus-spike-camera paradigm can be generalized to more than two views or to non-periodic modulation patterns.
