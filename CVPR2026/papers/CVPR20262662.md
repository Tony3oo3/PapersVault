## Problem

Existing DPO-based alignment for diffusion models assigns a single preference label to an entire image, creating a spatially sparse reward problem: a globally preferred image may contain locally defective instances (e.g., an eagle with four claws), and uniformly propagating the positive signal across all pixels perversely reinforces underperforming regions while suppressing well-generated ones. This misalignment between image-level supervision and instance-level semantics distorts the optimization trajectory and hampers training efficiency.

## Input / Output

At inference, the fine-tuned model (SD1.5 or SDXL) takes a text prompt and produces a single RGB image, identical in interface to the base diffusion model. During training, the method consumes image pairs annotated with per-instance bounding boxes and binary preference labels (ρ_n ∈ {0,1}), where the annotation pipeline automatically identifies matched instances across pairs using Qwen2.5-VL-7B as planner/judge and Grounding-DINO as detector, yielding 1,205,593 instance preference pairs from 959,040 image pairs in Pick-a-Pic v2.

## Method

IAPO introduces a two-stage framework for instance-level credit assignment. First, an automated annotation pipeline (Planner → Detector → Judge) localizes corresponding instances in winner/loser image pairs and reassigns preference labels at the instance level, revealing that ~46.3% of instance pairs have preferences conflicting with the global image-level label. Second, a dynamic reweighting mask M* is constructed by setting pixel weights to w_neg (≤1) within bounding boxes where the instance preference contradicts the image-level preference, averaging overlapping regions, and normalizing to unit mean. This mask is applied via element-wise (Hadamard) multiplication to the per-pixel DPO loss terms for the winner and loser images separately (Eq. 11), effectively suppressing gradient signal in regions that are locally inferior in the preferred image while preserving it in well-generated regions. The loss is optimized using few-step DDIM inversion (≤10 steps) for SDXL or direct noise sampling for SD1.5, with β=5000 and β=2000 respectively.

## Results

On the HPD v2 benchmark with SD1.5, IAPO achieves Aesthetic 5.9261, PickScore 22.0227, HPS 28.6379, and CLIP 36.4029 (mean), outperforming the strongest baseline InPO by +0.1197 (Aesthetic), +0.1096 (PickScore), and +0.1376 (HPS). Across all three test sets (Parti-Prompts, HPD v2, Pick-a-Pic v2), IAPO-SD1.5 attains the best scores on Aesthetic, PickScore, and HPS, while CLIP scores are competitive but slightly below InPO on two of three sets. In terms of training efficiency, IAPO requires only ~17.6 GPU hours on a single H800, making it 3.27× faster than InPO (~57.6 h), 11.64× faster than DPO (~204.8 h), and 60× faster than KTO (~1056 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IAPO consistently outperforms the unmodified SD1.5 base model by large margins on all four metrics across all three test sets (e.g., +0.49 Aesthetic, +1.18 PickScore, +1.76 HPS on HPD v2). Against DPO and KTO, IAPO wins on every metric and dataset, with the gap over KTO being particularly pronounced on HPS (+0.33 on HPD v2). The closest competitor is InPO: IAPO surpasses it on Aesthetic (+0.12), PickScore (+0.11), and HPS (+0.14) on HPD v2, but trails slightly on CLIP (36.40 vs 36.48) and on Parti-Prompts CLIP (34.59 vs 34.70). On Pick-a-Pic v2, IAPO edges out InPO on CLIP (34.70 vs 34.70, essentially tied) while leading on the other three metrics. In terms of training efficiency, IAPO requires only ~17.6 GPU hours versus ~57.6 h for InPO (3.27× faster), ~204.8 h for DPO (11.64× faster), and ~1056 h for KTO (60× faster), while simultaneously producing higher-quality outputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DPO-based alignment framework for diffusion models (Diffusion-DPO, InPO, KTO) by shifting the credit-assignment granularity from the image level to the instance level. It inherits the simplified DPO loss formulation from Diffusion-DPO [35] and the few-step DDIM inversion technique from InPO [22], but departs from both by introducing a spatially adaptive reweighting mask that modulates the loss within detected bounding boxes. The automated annotation pipeline (Qwen2.5-VL as planner/judge + Grounding-DINO as detector) is novel in the preference-optimization context, though each component is an off-the-shelf model repurposed for fine-grained label construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that ~46.3% of instance pairs exhibit a preference conflict with the global image label validates that spatially sparse rewards are a non-trivial source of misalignment, not a marginal edge case. The 3.27× training-efficiency gain over InPO makes fine-grained alignment practically accessible, and the constructed 1.2M-instance dataset is a reusable resource for the community. Limitations include reliance on bounding-box-level (not pixel-precise) masks, the modest SDXL gains (attributed to the base model already exceeding dataset quality), and the open question of whether this approach generalizes to non-object-centric preferences (e.g., style, composition, color harmony) where discrete instance boundaries are ill-defined.
