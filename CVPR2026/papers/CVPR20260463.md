## Problem

Text-aerial person retrieval (TAPR) extends text-image person retrieval to UAV-captured images, but extreme variations in shooting angle and altitude cause nonlinear distortions in appearance, posture, and geometric proportions, leaving many visual cues sparse or entirely missing. This creates two compounding challenges: token-level semantic misalignment when text tokens have no corresponding visual evidence in the aerial view, and a large cross-modal gap that makes direct text-to-aerial alignment unreliable compared to text-to-ground alignment.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language person description (enhanced via random masking during training), an aerial person image, and optionally a ground-view image of the same identity as a semantic bridge. All images are resized to 384×128 and encoded by a shared CLIP visual encoder, while text is encoded by the CLIP text encoder. The output is a ranked list of gallery person images scored by a bidirectional similarity distribution matching (SDM) objective that combines sample-level and token-level alignment scores.

## Method

CFAN comprises two complementary modules. The Context-Aware Dynamic Alignment (CDA) module computes the per-sample similarity gap Δ between text-aerial and text-ground cosine similarities, maps it through a sigmoid gate α, and adaptively weights a direct SDM loss against a bridge SDM loss (text→ground→aerial with stop-gradient on the ground feature), so low-difficulty samples rely on direct alignment while high-difficulty samples lean on the ground-view bridge. The Fuzzy Token Alignment (FTA) module projects both modalities into a shared semantic space via 4 learnable query tokens processed by a CrossFormer, then assigns each token a Gaussian fuzzy membership degree derived from its cosine similarity to the modality's global class token (with a learnable scale σ). Joint membership is computed as a multiplicative fuzzy AND across modalities, and token-level cosine similarities are weighted by this joint membership before feeding into the SDM loss, effectively suppressing unobservable or noisy tokens. The full model is trained end-to-end with Adam (lr 5×10⁻⁶, cosine decay, 60 epochs, batch 64) on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

On AERI-PEDES, CFAN with ground-view assistance achieves 47.16% Rank-1, 44.79% mAP, and 186.65% RSum, surpassing the previous best (HAM, CVPR 2025) by 2.58% in Rank-1 and 5.88% in RSum. Without ground images, the method still leads all baselines with 45.06% Rank-1 and 182.80% RSum. On TBAPR, CFAN reaches 49.47% Rank-1, 43.96% mAP, and 189.03% RSum (with ground), outperforming the strongest baseline CFAM (CVPR 2024) by 1.13% in Rank-1 and 4.25% in RSum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFAN outperforms all 10 compared baselines on every metric across both AERI-PEDES and TBAPR. On AERI-PEDES, the largest gains are over CFAM (CVPR 2024, +8.82% Rank-1) and IRRA (CVPR 2023, +12.02% Rank-1), while the closest competitor HAM (CVPR 2025) is surpassed by 2.58% Rank-1 and 5.88% RSum. On TBAPR, the margin is tighter: CFAM (CVPR 2024) is the nearest baseline at 48.34% Rank-1 vs. 49.47% for CFAN (+1.13%), and HAM trails by 1.66% Rank-1. Notably, even without ground-view images, CFAN already exceeds all baselines on both datasets, indicating that the Fuzzy Token Alignment module alone provides a substantial advantage. The ground-view bridge (CDA module) contributes an additional ~2% Rank-1 on AERI-PEDES but only ~0.3% on TBAPR, consistent with the paper's observation that TBAPR contains many low-altitude aerial images where the aerial-ground gap is smaller.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Text-Aerial Person Retrieval (TAPR) task first defined by Wang et al. (TBAPR) by introducing a substantially larger benchmark and a new alignment architecture. Methodologically, it builds on the CLIP-based cross-modal retrieval line (IRRA, NAM, HAM) and the SDM loss framework, but departs from prior TIPR work by incorporating ground-view images as a semantic bridge and by applying fuzzy membership functions to model token-level reliability—drawing from the fuzzy deep learning literature (Duan et al., Xu et al.) that has seen limited application in person retrieval. The CoT-based caption generation framework for dataset construction is a methodological departure from the fully manual annotation paradigm used in CUHK-PEDES and RSTPReid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The AERI-PEDES benchmark (112K+ images, 4,659 identities, multi-source aerial-ground pairs) fills a critical gap in aerial person retrieval evaluation, offering a scale and cross-view diversity that TBAPR lacks. The ground-view bridging strategy is practically relevant for deployment settings where both aerial and ground cameras are available (e.g., integrated surveillance systems), though its applicability is limited to such dual-camera scenarios. Open questions include whether the fuzzy token alignment generalizes beyond CLIP-based encoders, whether the CoT captioning framework scales to other cross-view retrieval tasks, and how performance degrades when ground-view images are partially occluded or unavailable at test time.
