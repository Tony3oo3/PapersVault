## Problem

Fine-grained facial motion understanding and text-driven facial animation remain underexplored because existing V-LLMs must process every video frame as hundreds of image tokens, causing micro-expressions to vanish under temporal downsampling, and because no large, emotion-balanced, text-paired facial-motion corpus exists. The paper asks whether low-dimensional geometric signals (3DMM parameters) can replace redundant visual tokens to enable both interpretation and synthesis of expressive facial behavior through language models.

## Input / Output

For Motion2Language, the model consumes a sequence of discrete geometry tokens (one per frame, replacing 300–500 image tokens) derived from FLAME 3DMM parameters fitted to 25 FPS, 618×360 clips of 4–6 s, and produces free-form natural-language descriptions of emotion, intensity, micro-expressions, and head dynamics. For Language2Motion, the model takes a natural-language prompt, converts it into word-level LLM embeddings via a text encoder, and autoregressively generates a sequence of geometry tokens that decode into a 3DMM trajectory (expression + yaw/roll/pitch). The geometry VQ-VAE operates on reconstructed facial geometry rather than raw 3DMM coefficients, so visually similar expressions map to consistent tokens regardless of parameter-space ambiguity.

## Method

The pipeline has three components. First, a Geometry VQ-VAE quantizes per-frame facial geometry (not raw 3DMM parameters) into a discrete codebook, producing compact tokens that preserve subtle dynamics while eliminating the many-to-one ambiguity of coefficient-space discretization. Second, for Motion2Language, an LLM is instruction-tuned to read geometry-token sequences as symbolic observations and emit natural-language descriptions, with annotations expanded via LLM paraphrasing for linguistic coverage. Third, for Language2Motion, an autoregressive transformer predicts future geometry tokens conditioned on a word-level text prefix produced by a pretrained LLM text encoder, so individual words can steer localized facial movements without modifying the LLM vocabulary. The entire system is trained on Open3DFaceVid (~80 h, 50.2K clips), a synthetic corpus generated by multiple T2V backbones (Wan-2.1/2.2, Open-Sora, HuMo, Veo) using a curated lexicon of ~200 emotion and facial-action descriptors to ensure balanced affective coverage, with FLAME parameters regressed per frame to form paired text–3DMM training data.

## Results

On Motion2Language (geometry-token input), the proposed method achieves Cor_E=4.02, Cor_M=3.35, Cor_I=3.63 (GPT-4 judge) and USER_E=4.29, USER_M=3.79 (human raters), substantially outperforming HumanOmni (1.84/1.17/1.09) and Gemini 2.5 VLM (2.45/2.91/3.51). On Language2Motion, the method achieves L2=0.219, FD=31.75, Tok=0.920, Cor_E=4.13, and USER=3.95, surpassing T2M-GPT (0.226/37.04/0.895/3.57/3.91) and T2M-X (0.471/47.59/0.671/2.21/3.40) across all metrics. In the natural-image evaluation setting (Table 2), Gemini 2.5 VLM edges out the proposed method on Cor_E (4.21 vs 4.02) and Cor_I (3.92 vs 3.63), though the proposed method still leads on USER_E (4.29 vs 4.17) while using a single geometry token per frame versus hundreds of visual tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against HumanOmni [72], the proposed method wins decisively on every Motion2Language metric: Cor_E improves from 1.84 to 4.02, Cor_M from 1.17 to 3.35, and Cor_I from 1.09 to 3.63, indicating that pixel-space VLMs struggle to interpret temporal facial dynamics from sparse frames. Against Gemini 2.5 VLM [56], the proposed method leads on Cor_E (4.02 vs 2.45) and USER_E (4.29 vs 2.88) in the geometry-token setting, though Gemini retains a slight edge on Cor_I (3.51 vs 3.63 is actually a win for Ours) and Cor_M (2.91 vs 3.35 is also a win for Ours); in the natural-image setting (Table 2), Gemini narrows the gap on Cor_E (4.21 vs 4.02) and Cor_I (3.92 vs 3.63) but still trails on USER_E (4.17 vs 4.29). For Language2Motion, the proposed method outperforms T2M-GPT [69] on all five metrics (L2 0.219 vs 0.226, FD 31.75 vs 37.04, Tok 0.920 vs 0.895, Cor_E 4.13 vs 3.57, USER 3.95 vs 3.91), with the largest gap on semantic alignment (Cor_E +0.56). Against T2M-X [25], the margins are even wider: L2 drops from 0.471 to 0.219, FD from 47.59 to 31.75, and Cor_E rises from 2.21 to 4.13, confirming that word-level LLM conditioning and the geometry-token VQ representation together yield substantially better text-motion alignment than prior autoregressive motion tokenizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the text-to-motion generation line (T2M-X, T2M-GPT, MotionCLIP) from full-body to facial motion, but departs from prior work in two key ways: it replaces image-token VLMs with a compact geometry-token representation (one VQ token per frame via a 3DMM-based VQ-VAE), and it introduces a bidirectional LLM framework that handles both motion-to-language and language-to-motion within a single model. The dataset construction repurposes foundation T2V models (Wan, Open-Sora, HuMo, Veo) as a synthetic data engine for 3DMM-parameterized facial corpora, a use of these generators not previously explored for structured geometric annotation. Conceptually, the paper positions itself as the first to treat 3D facial parameters as a language problem, bridging the gap between the well-developed full-body text-motion literature and the underexplored facial domain.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Open3DFaceVid dataset (80 hours, 50K clips with paired text and FLAME parameters) addresses a genuine data bottleneck in facial animation research and is likely to become a standard resource. The geometry-token approach offers a practical efficiency win—replacing 300–500 image tokens per frame with a single discrete token—making temporal facial reasoning tractable for LLMs. Open questions include whether synthetic T2V-generated data generalizes to real-world capture conditions, how the framework handles multi-person or occluded scenes, and whether the scaling asymmetry (Motion2Language saturating at ~4B while Language2Motion keeps improving) reflects a fundamental difference in task complexity or simply data limitations.
