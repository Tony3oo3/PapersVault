## Problem

Self-supervised representations have shown strong results for deepfake detection, but prior work uses them in isolation or buried within complex architectures, leaving unclear which features capture meaningful forensic cues versus spurious artifacts, how they generalize across domains, and whether different modalities encode complementary information. This paper systematically evaluates a wide range of audio, visual, and multimodal SSL features for audio-visual deepfake detection, probing their effectiveness, interpretability, and complementarity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260115/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an audio-visual video clip and extracts locally temporal embeddings (per-frame or per-chunk) from frozen SSL encoders operating at different granularities: frame-level (CLIP, FSFM), chunk-level (Video-MAE over 16-frame windows), or full-context (Wav2Vec2, AV-HuBERT, BRAVEn, Auto-AVSR). A single learnable linear layer is applied per frame, and predictions are aggregated via log-sum-exp to yield a video-level binary real/fake score. For interpretability, the same per-frame scores serve as temporal explanations, and patch-level contributions are extracted via linear propagation or Grad-CAM to produce spatial relevance maps.

## Method

The core approach is linear probing on frozen self-supervised representations: a minimal linear classifier is trained on top of per-frame embeddings from 14 different SSL encoders (audio-only, visual-only, and multimodal), with log-sum-exp pooling so the model flags a video as fake if any single temporal region is anomalous. To guard against spurious correlations, the authors introduce two anomaly-detection proxy tasks trained exclusively on real data: next-token prediction (a decoder-only Transformer predicting the next frame's embedding) and audio-video synchronization (an alignment network measuring cross-modal consistency). They further extract temporal and spatial explanations from the linear classifier and validate them against ground-truth manipulation segments and human click annotations, and quantify complementarity via prediction correlation and fusion performance across feature pairs.

## Results

Using linear probing on self-supervised features, BRAVEn(V) achieves the best overall generalization with 90.5% AUC averaged across three out-of-domain datasets (FAVC, AVLips, DFE-2024), outperforming all prior methods. On the in-the-wild DeepfakeEval 2024 benchmark, the best result is 76.0% AUC (BRAVEn(V)), while in-domain performance on AV1M and FAVC reaches 99–100% AUC for audio-informed features (Wav2Vec2, AV-HuBERT(A)). Anomaly detection (next-token prediction and audio-video synchronization) trained only on real data provides robustness against spurious cues but remains below supervised performance, with the best anomaly-detection result (87.3% AUC on AV1M) achieved by synchronization on AV-HuBERT(A+V) features.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's linear-probing approach with BRAVEn(V) features achieves 90.5% AUC on the Last3 average (excluding the training set AV1M), surpassing the closest competitor SpeechForensics (89.4%) by 1.1 points and RealForensics (80.1%) by 10.4 points. On the in-the-wild DFE-2024 benchmark, BRAVEn(V) leads with 76.0% AUC, narrowly beating SpeechForensics (75.6%) and RealForensics (74.4%), while AVFF (58.1%) and AuViRe (63.8%) lag significantly. On AVLips, BRAVEn(V) (96.7%) substantially outperforms SpeechForensics (92.7%), RealForensics (76.8%), and AVAD (73.2%). However, SpeechForensics retains an edge on FAVC (100% vs. 98.8%) and on AV1M in-domain (68.1% vs. 93.0% for BRAVEn(V), though Wav2Vec2 and AuViRe reach 100% in-domain). The unsupervised AVAD baseline (77.7% Last3) is consistently outperformed, confirming that supervised linear probing on strong SSL features is more effective than purely anomaly-detection-based approaches for this task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the linear probing paradigm from NLP and vision [3,7,28] to the video domain for audio-visual deepfake detection, and adopts the audio-video synchronization alignment network from Klemt et al. [73] as one of its anomaly detection proxy tasks. Unlike prior work that embeds SSL features (CLIP, Wav2Vec2, AV-HuBERT) within complex architectures or evaluates them in isolation, this paper systematically compares a broad set of audio, visual, and multimodal SSL representations under a unified minimal classifier, and introduces a multi-faceted evaluation protocol (anomaly detection, temporal/spatial explainability, complementarity analysis) that goes beyond standard supervised AUC reporting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that randomly initialized features achieve near-perfect in-domain AUC exposes a critical vulnerability in standard deepfake detection evaluation: strong scores can be driven by dataset shortcuts (e.g., leading silence) rather than meaningful forensic cues. Practically, the result that a simple linear probe on BRAVEn's visual encoder achieves state-of-the-art performance across four datasets suggests that the field may be over-engineering detection architectures when the bottleneck is representation quality. The persistent gap on in-the-wild data (best OOD ~76% AUC) remains an open challenge, pointing to the need for methods that explicitly handle real-world diversity (missing modalities, unknown manipulation types, domain shifts) rather than relying on the semantic alignment that current SSL features provide.
