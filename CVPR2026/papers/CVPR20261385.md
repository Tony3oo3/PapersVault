## Problem

Existing few-shot class-incremental learning (FSCIL) methods assume homogeneous visual domains and balanced data distributions, but real-world data arises from heterogeneous disciplines with imbalanced sample availability and varying visual complexity. The paper identifies 'Domain Gravity,' a representational asymmetry where overrepresented or low-entropy domains disproportionately distort the shared embedding space, causing prototype drift and degraded performance on underrepresented or high-entropy domains. No prior training-free method addresses this asymmetric representational force under cross-discipline, variable-shot conditions.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a single test image and the set of previously learned class text labels. For each candidate class c, it constructs a class-conditional query embedding by summing the frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 image embedding of the test image with the text embedding of class c's label, producing a fused vector in the joint vision-language space. The output is a single predicted class label selected via argmax over the hybrid distance score across all seen classes. No gradient updates, additional parameters, or model expansion are required at any stage.

## Method

HYCAL is a training-free prototype calibration method operating entirely on frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 embeddings. During incremental learning, each new class prototype stores three quantities: the mean fused embedding μ_c, a regularized precision matrix Σ_c^{-1} (with λ-regularized covariance for few-shot robustness), and the sample count K_c^t. At inference, for each candidate class, HYCAL computes two complementary metrics between the query embedding and the prototype: cosine similarity (capturing global directional alignment) and Mahalanobis distance (encoding domain-specific covariance and entropy differences). These are combined via a dynamic weighted sum where the weight w_c is a sigmoid of (K_c^t − α)/β, so classes with fewer samples rely more on directional cues while data-rich classes leverage covariance structure. The paper proves via information-theoretic arguments that cosine and Mahalanobis capture statistically independent geometric information (direction vs. magnitude under isotropic conditions), so their combination yields strictly greater mutual information with the class label than either metric alone.

## Results

Under the primary High-Scale Domain Imbalance setting (8 heterogeneous domains, frozen CLIP ViT-B/16), HYCAL achieves 54.48% Average accuracy and 63.50% Last accuracy, outperforming the best training-free baseline RanPAC (49.98% / 61.13%) by 4.5%p and 2.4%p respectively. On the proposed S_CDE metric, HYCAL scores 60.92 (Balanced-in-Class), 59.24 (Cross-Scale Imbalance), and 53.70 (High-Scale Domain Imbalance), ranking first in all three settings. In standard FSCIL evaluation (Table 4), HYCAL leads on Last accuracy across all shot counts (e.g., 60.82% at 5-shot vs. 59.40% for Primal-RAIL) and achieves the highest S_CDE of 60.36 at 5-shot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HYCAL outperforms all four baselines on Average accuracy in the High-Scale Domain Imbalance setting: 54.48% vs. Primal-RAIL (53.49%), RanPAC (49.98%), KLDA (41.06%), and FeCAM (8.91%). On Last accuracy, HYCAL (63.50%) beats Primal-RAIL (59.86%) by 3.6%p and RanPAC (61.13%) by 2.4%p, while slightly exceeding KLDA (61.43%) by 2.1%p. FeCAM collapses catastrophically across nearly all domains (e.g., 0.00% on ArtBench, EuroSAT, Galaxy, MNIST, OrganMNIST), confirming its inability to handle domain heterogeneity. On the S_CDE metric, HYCAL ranks first in all three imbalance settings, though the margin over KLDA narrows in Cross-Scale Imbalance (59.24 vs. 59.61, where KLDA edges ahead by 0.37). In standard FSCIL (Table 4), HYCAL consistently leads on Last accuracy and S_CDE across 5/10/15/20-shot, with the largest gap at 5-shot (S_CDE 60.36 vs. 52.58 for Primal-RAIL), highlighting its advantage under extreme data scarcity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the cross-domain FSCIL line of work (Primal-RAIL, RanPAC, FeCAM, KLDA), which it argues implicitly assumes homogeneous feature distributions and balanced few-shot configurations. Rather than introducing a new backbone adaptation or projection mechanism, HYCAL repurposes two classical distance metrics—cosine similarity and Mahalanobis distance—into a dynamically weighted prototype calibration rule operating entirely on frozen CLIP embeddings. The 'Domain Gravity' framing is a new conceptual lens on the well-known anisotropy of VLM embedding spaces, but the underlying phenomenon (data-rich domains dominating shared geometry) has been observed in prior CLIP analysis; the paper's contribution is formalizing it as a named failure mode and showing it intensifies under sequential heterogeneous tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The XD-VSCIL benchmark with eight genuinely heterogeneous disciplines and variable per-class sample counts fills a practical gap: prior cross-domain FSCIL evaluations used fixed shots and comparable domain sizes, masking the degradation that real deployment would expose. The training-free design makes HYCAL trivially deployable alongside any frozen VLM, and the CDE metric offers a more holistic evaluation than average/last accuracy alone. However, the method's generality is untested beyond CLIP ViT-B/16 and eight domains, the dynamic weighting is a single sigmoid heuristic without learned or per-domain adaptation, and the theoretical complementarity argument relies on an isotropic Gaussian assumption that the authors acknowledge is far from actual CLIP geometry—leaving open whether the cosine-Mahalanobis fusion remains optimal under more extreme anisotropy or with larger VLMs.
