## Problem

Existing energy-based predictive world models learn latent representations in Euclidean space, neglecting the geometric and hierarchical structure among states, and suffer from rapid performance degradation as the planning horizon increases. These two limitations—geometric neglect and multi-step shortcoming—prevent stable, geometry-consistent long-horizon visual planning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263692/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a current observation and a goal observation, each provided as either a single RGB image (procedural planning setup) or a short video clip (visual planning with videos setup), along with a planning horizon T (typically 3–6 steps). A frozen ViT encoder (pretrained on VideoMix22M) maps each observation to a latent vector, which is then projected onto a Poincaré ball. The output is a discrete sequence of T action labels, obtained by running CEM (N=800 samples, K=80 elites, I=10 iterations) over the predictor's hyperbolic energy landscape to minimize the geodesic distance between the predicted terminal state and the goal state.

## Method

GeoWorld introduces two key components. First, Hyperbolic JEPA (H-JEPA) maps the encoder's Euclidean output onto a hyperbolic manifold via the exponential map at the origin of a Poincaré ball with learnable curvature, so that geodesic distances naturally encode hierarchical relations among latent states; the predictor (a ~300M-parameter, 24-layer transformer) is trained with a combined teacher-forcing loss and a two-step rollout loss, both measured as Poincaré-ball hyperbolic distance, enforcing geodesically consistent transitions. Second, Geometric Reinforcement Learning (GRL) reformulates multi-step planning as minimizing a hyperbolic value function—where lower energy corresponds to higher cumulative reward—by directly optimizing the predictor through discounted hyperbolic distance minimization plus a triangle-inequality regularization term that penalizes rollouts violating geodesic consistency. At inference, energy-based planning with CEM searches for the action sequence that minimizes the hyperbolic energy cost between the current and goal latent states, yielding geometry-aware multi-step plans without pixel generation.

## Results

On the video-based planning setup (Table 2), GeoWorld ViT-g₃₈₄ achieves 51.71% SR on CrossTask (T=3) and 37.04% SR (T=4), surpassing V-JEPA 2 ViT-g₃₈₄ (50.16% / 35.01%) and GPT-5 (50.03% / 30.20%). On COIN, GeoWorld ViT-g₃₈₄ reaches 45.29% SR (T=3) and 33.29% SR (T=4), outperforming V-JEPA 2 ViT-g₃₈₄ (42.74% / 31.63%) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (42.07% / 30.20%). In the image-based procedural planning setup (Table 1), GeoWorld ViT-g₃₈₄ improves mIoU by up to 25 points over V-JEPA 2 (e.g., 89.88 vs. 64.53 on COIN T=3), while SR gains are more modest (~1–4%). In long-horizon planning (Table 3), GeoWorld ViT-g₃₈₄ maintains the highest SR from T=3 through T=6 on CrossTask, reaching 18.26% at T=6 versus 16.88% for V-JEPA 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoWorld consistently outperforms V-JEPA 2 across all model scales and both evaluation setups. In the video setup, GeoWorld ViT-g₃₈₄ gains +1.55 SR on CrossTask T=3 and +2.03 SR on T=4 over V-JEPA 2 ViT-g₃₈₄, while in the image setup the mIoU advantage is far larger (+17 to +25 points), reflecting the structured hyperbolic energy landscape. Against LLM-based planners, GeoWorld ViT-g₃₈₄ surpasses GPT-5 by +1.68 SR on CrossTask T=3 and +6.84 SR on T=4, and beats Gemini 2.5 Pro by +2.80 SR on CrossTask T=3. In the image-based procedural planning setup, GeoWorld ViT-g₃₈₄ leads all generative and predictive baselines on SR (47.47 vs. 45.58 for V-JEPA 2, 40.45 for MTID, 38.93 for SCHEMA) and dominates on mIoU (86.55 vs. 69.42 for V-JEPA 2). In long-horizon planning (T=5, T=6), GeoWorld maintains the highest SR among all methods, with the gap over V-JEPA 2 widening from +1.66 at T=3 to +1.38 at T=6, demonstrating reduced error accumulation. The only setting where GeoWorld does not lead is mAcc on COIN T=4 in the video setup, where it trails V-JEPA 2 ViT-g₃₈₄ by 2.27 points (55.97 vs. 59.28), suggesting the hyperbolic mapping slightly reduces per-step action accuracy while improving overall trajectory coherence.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoWorld extends the predictive world model lineage initiated by JEPA (LeCun, 2022) and operationalized in V-JEPA 2 (Assran et al., 2025), which learns an energy landscape in Euclidean latent space for multi-step planning without pixel generation. The key departure is mapping latent representations onto a Poincaré-ball hyperbolic manifold so that geodesic distances encode hierarchical state relations, and introducing a Geometric Reinforcement Learning stage that refines the predictor via hyperbolic energy minimization and triangle-inequality regularization—neither of which appears in prior energy-based world models. The planning stage reuses the Cross-Entropy Method from V-JEPA 2, so the architectural backbone (frozen ViT encoder + transformer predictor) is inherited directly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the geometric structure of latent space is not merely a representational nicety but measurably affects long-horizon planning stability, with GeoWorld maintaining higher SR at T=5 and T=6 where Euclidean baselines degrade more sharply. However, the absolute gains remain modest (~3% SR at T=3, ~2% at T=4), and the evaluation is confined to two instructional-video benchmarks with relatively short action sequences, leaving open whether hyperbolic latent dynamics transfer to continuous-control or higher-dimensional state spaces. The learnable curvature parameter and the specific two-step rollout loss design are under-motivated ablation-wise, and it is unclear whether the mIoU gains (which are larger than SR gains) reflect genuine geometric structure or simply a smoother energy landscape that CEM exploits more easily.
