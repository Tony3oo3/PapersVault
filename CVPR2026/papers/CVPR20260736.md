## Problem

Mainstream remote sensing rotated object detectors suffer from two structural bottlenecks: directional incoherence at the FPN neck, where high-level (coarse, low-frequency) and low-level (sharp, high-frequency) features carry conflicting orientation signals that degrade angle prediction when naively fused, and task conflict at the detection head, where a single RoI feature must simultaneously support rotation-invariant classification and rotation-sensitive angle regression. Existing methods operate entirely in the spatial domain and lack an explicit mechanism to estimate and reconcile orientation across these stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260736/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method is a plug-and-play module inserted into existing two-stage ROD pipelines (e.g., Oriented R-CNN with ResNet50, LSKNet-S, or StripNet-S backbones). It consumes intermediate feature maps at the FPN neck (multi-scale, channel-projected to a common intermediate size) and RoI-aligned features at the head, operating on square patches extracted via unfold. The final output remains standard oriented bounding boxes (center, width, height, angle, class) evaluated at 1024×1024 input resolution for DOTA and 800×800 for HRSC2016, with single-scale training and testing.

## Method

Fourier Angle Alignment (FAA) exploits Fourier rotation equivariance to estimate dominant object orientation in the frequency domain: a 2D DFT is applied to a feature patch, the spectrum is converted to polar coordinates, and the angular energy distribution (radially summed) reveals the principal direction, which for rectangular objects is perpendicular to the major axis. Two lightweight modules deploy this: FAAFusion replaces FPN element-wise addition by estimating orientation from low-level local features and rotating high-level features to match before fusion, ensuring directional consistency across scales; FAA Head replaces the detection head by rotating each RoI feature to a canonical 0° orientation (yielding a rotation-invariant component for classification) and adding it residually to the original RoI feature (preserving rotation-sensitive cues for regression). The modules add minimal overhead (~1.2M params, ~3.5G FLOPs for FAAFusion; ~17M params, ~3.5G FLOPs for FAA Head) and achieve new SOTA mAP of 78.72% on DOTA-v1.0 and 72.28% on DOTA-v1.5.

## Results

On DOTA-v1.0, the best configuration (S-RCNN + FAA) achieves a new state-of-the-art 78.72% mAP with single-scale training and testing, surpassing PKINet (78.39%) by 0.33%. On DOTA-v1.5, LSKNet + FAA reaches 72.28% mAP, outperforming PKINet (71.47%) by 0.81%. On HRSC2016, LSKNet + FAA achieves 70.74% mAP under VOC2007 metrics (vs. 68.78% for the original LSKNet), a gain of 1.96%. Across all three datasets and three backbones (ResNet50, LSKNet-S, StripNet-S), the plug-and-play FAA modules consistently improve mAP by 0.37%–2.17% over the respective baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's FAA modules are added to three representative backbones (ResNet50, LSKNet-S, StripNet-S) within the Oriented R-CNN framework and compared against 19 prior methods. On DOTA-v1.0, S-RCNN + FAA (78.72%) surpasses the previous SOTA PKINet (78.39%) by 0.33% and LSKNet (77.49%) by 1.23%; LSKNet + FAA (78.49%) also exceeds PKINet by 0.10%. On DOTA-v1.5, LSKNet + FAA (72.28%) beats PKINet (71.47%) by 0.81% and the original LSKNet (70.26%) by 2.02%, while S-RCNN + FAA (71.57%) improves over S-RCNN (69.84%) by 1.73%. On HRSC2016, all three configurations improve over their baselines: LSKNet + FAA gains 1.96% mAP (70.74 vs. 68.78), S-RCNN + FAA gains 1.23% (70.41 vs. 69.18), and O-RCNN + FAA gains 2.17% (66.94 vs. 64.77) under VOC2007 metrics. The method also shows superior high-IoU performance (AP75–AP90), indicating better geometric precision than baselines whose AP50 scores are near-saturated. FAA Head outperforms the Strip Head [48] on all three backbones with comparable parameters but substantially lower FLOPs (e.g., 177.15G vs. 220.60G on LSKNet).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Oriented R-CNN framework and positions itself against the dominant spatial-domain approaches to oriented object detection, including rotation-sensitive convolutions (ARC, GRA), rotation-equivariant backbones (ReDet, LSKNet, StripNet), and spatial-domain head designs (RoI Transformer, S2A-Net). Its key conceptual departure is introducing frequency-domain analysis—specifically Fourier rotation equivariance and the spectral alignment property of rectangles—as a principled mechanism for estimating and aligning object orientation, rather than relying on learned spatial transformations. The two plug-and-play modules (FAAFusion at the neck, FAA Head at the detection head) are designed to address two identified bottlenecks (directional incoherence across FPN scales and the classification-vs-regression task conflict) that prior spatial-domain methods handle only implicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design and consistent gains across three backbones and three datasets make FAA a practical, low-overhead add-on for existing oriented detection pipelines, and the high-IoU analysis suggests the frequency-domain alignment is particularly beneficial for precision-critical applications like ship detection. However, the absolute improvements (0.37–2.17% mAP) are modest, and the method adds non-trivial parameter overhead (e.g., 30.98M → 49.56M for LSKNet-S). Open questions include whether the Fourier-based orientation estimation generalizes to non-rectangular or highly irregular objects, whether it transfers to other tasks (segmentation, change detection), and how to make the per-pixel FFT and rotation operations efficient enough for real-time deployment.
