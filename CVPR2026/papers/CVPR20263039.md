## Problem

MLLMs excel at semantic-level commonsense reasoning but cannot directly produce the precise physical quantities (exact poses, force vectors) that robot control policies require, and natural-language encodings of physical knowledge are too imprecise for high-accuracy manipulation. This paper addresses the gap by introducing analytic concepts—procedurally defined mathematical representations of object structure and manipulation—that serve as a computable bridge between MLLM-inferred semantics and the physical world.

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB-D image (from a RealSense D415 or SAPIEN camera with known intrinsics) and a natural-language task description as input. GPT-4o identifies the target part and its category, GroundedSAM produces a pixel-level segmentation mask, and the depth channel is used to crop a 2048-point point cloud of that part. The output is a mathematically defined grasp pose G (a specific 6-DoF pose computed from estimated parameters of the selected analytic concept) and a procedurally computed force direction F, which together instruct a parallel gripper to execute the manipulation.

## Method

The pipeline has three stages: (1) GPT-4o identifies the target part and its category, and GroundedSAM segments it; (2) GPT-4o selects the best-matching analytic concept from a library of 153 hand-crafted concepts (each defined by geometric primitives with variable parameters), a Point-Transformer encoder regresses the structural parameters from the point cloud, and a second Point-Transformer + Umeyama/RANSAC pipeline estimates the 6-DoF pose in world coordinates; (3) GPT-4o selects the appropriate grasp-pose type and force direction from the concept's manipulation knowledge, a conditional GAN (Point-Transformer-based generator and discriminator) estimates the grasp-pose parameters from the point cloud, and the force direction is procedurally computed from the structural parameters and grasp pose. The key insight is that analytic concepts encode commonsense knowledge as machine-computable mathematical procedures rather than natural-language descriptions, allowing the MLLM to handle high-level reasoning while deterministic computation handles precise physical quantities.

## Results

On the PartNet-mobility simulation benchmark with a parallel gripper, the proposed method achieves 42.5% average success rate on training categories and 40.8% on testing categories, outperforming the strongest baseline A3VLM (37.4% / 32.1%) by 15.2% and 27.1% respectively. The gap is particularly large on complex multi-joint objects (e.g., Table: 51.6% vs. 42.5% for A3VLM). In real-world experiments across 8 household objects, the method also achieves high success rates, further validating the physically grounded analytic concept pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against five baselines spanning three framework types: vanilla pixel/point-cloud methods (Where2Act, Where2Explore), structure-based methods (GAPartNet), and MLLM-based methods (ManipLLM, A3VLM). The proposed method outperforms all five baselines on both training and testing category averages. Against the strongest MLLM baseline A3VLM, the improvement is 15.2% on training categories (42.5% vs. 37.4%) and 27.1% on testing categories (40.8% vs. 32.1%), with the largest per-category gain on Table (+9.1%). Compared to ManipLLM, gains are 10.5% (train) and 10.2% (test). Against the structure-based GAPartNet, the method leads by 12.8% (train) and 12.1% (test). The vanilla baselines Where2Act and Where2Explore trail by 16.4% and 15.6% on training categories respectively, confirming that neither pure pixel-level affordance nor few-shot transfer alone is sufficient without physically grounded structural knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MLLM-instructed manipulation paradigm (ManipLLM, A3VLM) by introducing 'analytic concepts'—procedurally defined mathematical templates—as an explicit bridge between the semantic-level reasoning of MLLMs and the physical-level control policies that robots require. It departs from structure-based approaches like GAPartNet, which use learned 6-DoF poses as structured representations, by instead using hand-crafted, human-interpretable geometric formulations (cylinders, cuboids, etc.) that can be directly computed. The concept of analytic concepts builds on the authors' own prior work on analytic ontology templates [39] and digital genes [38], but here it is specifically instantiated for articulated object manipulation with a full pipeline from MLLM reasoning to robot execution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated gap: MLLMs excel at commonsense reasoning but cannot produce the precise numerical outputs (grasp poses, force directions) that robot control demands, while purely geometric methods lack the semantic flexibility for open-ended tasks. The 27.1% improvement on unseen categories and the real-world validation suggest the analytic-concept bridge is a viable generalization strategy. However, the ablation reveals that structural parameter estimation and 6-DoF pose estimation remain the dominant error sources (20.8% and 14.3% gaps), and the approach depends on GPT-4o for concept identification, raising questions about scalability, cost, and robustness when the MLLM selects the wrong concept. Open questions include whether the 153 manually created concepts suffice for truly open-world settings and whether the geometric estimation bottlenecks can be addressed without sacrificing the interpretability that makes analytic concepts appealing.
