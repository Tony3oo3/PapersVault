## Problem

Conventional autonomous driving systems depend on high-definition maps for route planning, rendering them ineffective in mapless environments such as indoor parking lots. Existing Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) methods target indoor robotics with fine-grained step-by-step instructions, leaving a gap for mapless driving where only a coarse destination description is available. DriveVLN formalizes this missing setting: closed-loop, destination-only language-guided driving without any map prior.

## Input / Output

The system consumes egocentric RGB images (800×600), LiDAR point clouds, and a single natural-language destination phrase (e.g., 'the EV charger near the north exit') rather than a step-by-step route. The planner produces K destination-agnostic candidate trajectories, each consisting of 8 waypoints over a 4-second horizon; the selector then outputs a discrete color-coded choice identifying one trajectory plus a binary arrival flag. The selector maintains a rolling multi-turn history of past visual frames and decisions to preserve temporal context across the episode.

## Method

DriveVLN decomposes mapless driving VLN into two modules: a DiffusionDrive-based planner that samples feasible, destination-agnostic trajectories from sensor inputs, and a Qwen2.5-VL-3B navigation selector (fine-tuned via LoRA) that rasterizes candidate trajectories onto the forward camera image and selects the correct one by answering its color, conditioned on the language instruction and visual context. Training follows a two-stage strategy: first, supervised fine-tuning on 352K simulated frames for the planner and 40K real-world frames for the selector (Trajectory Foundry); second, reinforcement learning via GRPO in CARLA (Policy Tempering) with a composite reward combining local trajectory-match/collision penalties and global route-prefix/edge-overlap/arrival terms, plus a KL regularizer toward the SFT policy. The benchmark comprises 200 digital-twin scenes reconstructed from real road scans via a Topo2Sim pipeline, with GPT-4-augmented instruction paraphrases and stochastic scene augmentation for diversity, evaluated under closed-loop rollouts using both driving metrics (L2 error, collision rate) and VLN metrics (success rate, navigation error).

## Results

On the DriveVLN benchmark, the proposed model achieves the best overall Driving Score of 0.67, with a success rate (SR) of 0.44 and navigation error (NE) of 0.49. It also records the lowest average L2 trajectory error (0.283 m) and collision rate (9.6%) among all compared models. Notably, all methods—including proprietary VLMs—achieve arrival rates below 0.5, indicating substantial headroom for improvement on this mapless navigation task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DriveVLN outperforms all three baselines on every reported metric in the closed-loop CARLA evaluation. The closest competitor, Seed-1.6, trails by 0.07 in Driving Score (0.60 vs. 0.67) and by 0.06 in success rate (0.38 vs. 0.44), while GPT-5 and Qwen2.5-VL-72B lag substantially, with Driving Scores of 0.48 and 0.45 respectively. The gap is most pronounced in trajectory quality: GPT-5 and Qwen2.5-VL-72B exhibit average L2 errors of 1.55 m and 1.87 m, roughly 5–6× higher than DriveVLN's 0.28 m, and collision rates of 17.0% and 24.0% versus 9.6%. On real-world production data (Table 2), DriveVLN again leads with 91.4% arrival-detection accuracy and 63.75% navigation-path-selection accuracy overall, surpassing GPT-5 (81.89% / 58.43%) and Seed-1.6 (56.15% / 49.68%), while Qwen2.5-VL-72B struggles particularly on parking-slot arrival detection (1.34%).

## Relation to Prior Work

DriveVLN repurposes the Vision-and-Language Navigation paradigm from indoor robotics (R2R, REVERIE, SOON) into the autonomous driving domain, but deliberately departs from the step-by-step instruction format that defines prior VLN work, replacing it with destination-only language prompts in mapless settings. On the driving side, it sits adjacent to language-guided driving efforts (LINGO, DriveLM, Talk2Car, LMDrive) that either operate open-loop or assume navigation map priors; DriveVLN is the first to close the loop under mapless conditions. The model architecture composes two existing components—DiffusionDrive for trajectory generation and a Qwen2.5-VL-based VLM for selection—rather than introducing a new backbone, with the novelty residing in the task formulation, the Topo2Sim data pipeline, and the two-stage SFT+GRPO training strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Mapless navigation (indoor parking lots, uncharted lots) is a practical gap that blocks seamless point-to-point autonomy, and this paper provides the first formalized benchmark and baseline for addressing it with language. However, all evaluated methods—including GPT-5—achieve arrival rates below 0.5, and the benchmark is confined to 200 CARLA-reconstructed scenes with only 20 test routes, leaving open whether the formulation generalizes to truly open, unstructured environments. The substantial RL gain (Driving Score 0.49→0.67) suggests that global route awareness cannot be learned from single-frame supervision alone, pointing to a broader open question about how much sequential decision-making structure is needed for language-guided driving. Scaling to larger real-world datasets and more diverse mapless scenarios is explicitly flagged as future work.
