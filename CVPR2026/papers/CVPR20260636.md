## Problem

Existing camera-controlled video re-rendering methods (e.g., ReCamMaster, TrajectoryCrafter) succeed in single-view settings but fail to maintain consistent spatio-temporal hallucinations across multiple camera trajectories. The inherent stochasticity of diffusion models combined with limited long-range spatial memory causes geometric misalignment and view desynchronization in regions unseen from the source view, preventing coherent multi-view scene reconstruction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260636/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source video (432×768, 93 frames), its camera trajectory, a text caption, and N target camera trajectories (specified by extrinsic and intrinsic parameters) as input, and produces N novel-view videos along those trajectories with synchronized hallucinated content. Camera poses are encoded as 6D Plücker raymaps, and a VAE maps videos between pixel and latent space. The framework supports diverse transformations including third-person→third-person rotations/zooms and head-view→gripper-view in robotic manipulation, with long-video generation handled via overlapping sub-chunks.

## Method

PlenopticDreamer reformulates multi-view video re-rendering as an autoregressive, multi-in-single-out process: at each step, a 3D FOV-based retrieval mechanism (Monte Carlo sampling within camera frustums to measure spatial co-visibility) selects the top-k most relevant previously generated video-camera pairs from a memory bank as conditioning inputs for the next generation. The backbone is a flow-matching DiT (Cosmos-Predict2.5 2B) where retrieved video tokens and Plücker raymap tokens are temporally concatenated and channel-wise added before self-attention. Two training strategies enhance robustness: progressive context-scaling (gradually increasing conditioning video count from 1 to k) stabilizes convergence, and self-conditioned training (fine-tuning on the model's own synthetic outputs) mitigates error accumulation over long autoregressive chains. When retrieved videos exceed the context window, a divide-and-conquer inference strategy recursively merges trajectories to cover diverse viewpoints.

## Results

On the Basic Benchmark (100 in-the-wild videos, 12 camera trajectories), PlenopticDreamer achieves the best FVD of 425.8 (vs. 665.9 for TrajectoryCrafter, the next best) and the highest view synchronization (Mat. Pix.) across all shot counts: 41.4K (3 shots), 40.8K (6 shots), 45.4K (9 shots), and 41.2K (12 shots), substantially outperforming all baselines. Camera accuracy is also strong, with the lowest RotErr (0.21 rad) and a TransErr of 0.54, comparable to the retrained ReCamMaster* (0.52). On the Agibot Benchmark (200 robotic manipulation videos), PlenopticDreamer achieves PSNR 14.54 and view synchronization 15.3K, surpassing ReCamMaster* (13.84 PSNR, 13.2K Mat. Pix.).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PlenopticDreamer decisively outperforms all three baselines on view synchronization, the paper's primary metric. Against TrajectoryCrafter (FVD 665.9, best view sync 36.2K at 12 shots), PlenopticDreamer improves FVD by ~36% and view sync by ~14% at 12 shots (41.2K vs. 36.2K). Against Trajectory-Attention (FVD 734.1, best view sync 29.1K), the gap is even larger: ~42% FVD reduction and ~42% more matched pixels at 12 shots. Against the original ReCamMaster (FVD 731.6, view sync 27.6K at 12 shots), PlenopticDreamer nearly doubles the view synchronization score. The retrained ReCamMaster* (FVD 675.4) narrows the camera-accuracy gap (TransErr 0.52 vs. 0.54) but still lags significantly in view synchronization (31.2K vs. 41.2K at 12 shots) and FVD. On the Agibot robotic manipulation benchmark, PlenopticDreamer beats ReCamMaster* by 0.7 dB in PSNR and 2.1K in matched pixels, confirming the advantage generalizes to head-view → gripper-view transformations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends ReCamMaster's single-shot, in-context conditioning paradigm for camera-controlled video re-rendering into an autoregressive multi-in-single-out formulation, where each new view is generated conditioned on a retrieved set of previously generated video-camera pairs. The 3D FOV-based retrieval mechanism draws conceptually from memory-augmented video generation (e.g., VMem, SPMem) but operates at the video-segment level rather than 3D-reconstruction or latent-token level. Training strategies—progressive context-scaling and self-conditioned fine-tuning—borrow from the long-video autoregressive generation literature (Self-Forcing, Diffusion Forcing) to mitigate error accumulation, while the backbone (Cosmos-Predict2.5 DiT with flow matching) and Plücker raymap camera encoding follow established conventions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in camera-controlled video generation: maintaining geometric and hallucination consistency across multiple simultaneously generated views, which is essential for embodied AI (e.g., head-view to gripper-view in robotic manipulation) and immersive content creation. The Agibot benchmark and the demonstrated head-to-gripper transformation provide a concrete evaluation setting for robotic video re-rendering that prior work lacked. Open questions remain around computational cost scaling with the number of memory views, occasional over-exposure and distortion in long-shot sequences despite self-conditioning, and degraded performance on complex human motions (e.g., dancing) likely inherited from pretraining data biases. A Self-Forcing-style paradigm is suggested as a future direction to further close the train-test gap.
