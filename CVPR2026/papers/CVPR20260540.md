## Problem

Most public driving datasets are recorded on well-paved, largely flat roads and provide only sparse LiDAR as depth ground truth, leaving both the geometric diversity of real-world road topography (speed bumps, potholes, off-road segments) and the fine-grained 3D supervision needed for depth estimation underrepresented. This gap is critical for safety-critical perception, where inconsistent handling of irregular road geometry is a primary trigger for driver takeovers from autonomous stacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260540/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The dataset delivers synchronized 2248×2252 global-shutter stereo RGB pairs, raw dual Hesai XT32 LiDAR scans (32-channel, ~120 m range), 6-DoF ENU poses from LiDAR-inertial odometry, per-wheel ground-contact trajectories, and YOLO-format 2D bounding boxes for road irregularities, all at 10 Hz. The quasi-dense ground truth is provided as (N,3) point arrays in the left camera frame (~500K points/frame, ~18% scene coverage), and the benchmark evaluates both full-image and bounding-box-restricted depth/height metrics, with height computed relative to the road surface via wheel-excitation calibration.

## Method

The core contribution is a multi-LiDAR fusion pipeline that aggregates motion-compensated scans from front and rear Hesai XT32 units into 10 cm voxels, then applies adaptive multi-view voting where the required number of confirming observations decreases as the inter-sensor baseline grows (front votes drop from 4 to 1 over 0.20–0.90 m), followed by ICP-flow dynamic pruning, MAD-based per-voxel outlier trimming, and hidden-point removal from the camera viewpoint. For the benchmark, the authors fine-tune MoGe2L by combining its affine-invariant loss with an L1 height-space loss (MoGe2L†), and introduce a DMD3C variant that replaces the monocular teacher with FoundationStereo and swaps the scale-invariant loss for direct L1 metric-depth supervision. Evaluations use per-image median scaling for relative geometry and report both standard depth metrics (AbsRel, RMSE, δ₁/₂/₃) and road-height metrics (Abs. Diff, δ₁₀cm) under full-image and irregularity-box protocols.

## Results

On the CARD benchmark, FoundationStereo (zero-shot stereo) achieves the best bounding-box-restricted AbsRel of 0.014 and RMSE of 0.185 m, outperforming all monocular methods on road-irregularity regions. Among monocular models, fine-tuned MoGe2L† achieves the strongest full-image AbsRel of 0.029 and bounding-box AbsRel of 0.018, with a Height δ₁₀ of 0.981 on irregularity boxes. Zero-shot monocular estimators (e.g., DAV2, DepthPro) show substantially higher errors on bounding-box metrics (AbsRel 0.045–0.055) compared to their full-image scores, confirming that road-surface irregularities remain a challenging regime. For depth completion, the DMD3C+FS variant reduces full-image RMSE to 0.751 m versus 0.774 m for the DMD3C baseline, though FoundationStereo still leads on per-box metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FoundationStereo [52] (zero-shot stereo) dominates on bounding-box metrics, achieving AbsRel 0.014 and RMSE 0.185 m on road-irregularity regions, beating the best monocular method MoGe2L† [51] (AbsRel 0.018, RMSE 0.209 m) by roughly 22% in AbsRel. Among zero-shot monocular models, MoGe2L [51] and UniDepth2L [39] are the strongest, with MoGe2L slightly ahead on bounding-box RMSE (0.337 vs. 0.382 m), while Metric3D2 [22] trails by ~15% in bounding-box AbsRel. DAV2 [59] and DepthPro [6] are the weakest monocular baselines, with full-image RMSE of 4.107 m and 3.270 m respectively, and bounding-box AbsRel of 0.055 and 0.045—roughly 3–4× worse than FoundationStereo on the same metric. For depth completion, DMD3C [28] and BP-NET [45] achieve similar bounding-box RMSE (~0.19 m), but the DMD3C+FS variant (replacing the monocular teacher with FoundationStereo) reduces full-image RMSE from 0.774 to 0.751 m and bounding-box RMSE from 0.195 to 0.192 m, a modest but consistent gain. Notably, even the improved DMD3C+FS does not surpass FoundationStereo on per-box metrics, suggesting that LiDAR-image depth completion may oversmooth the fine road topographies that CARD is designed to evaluate.

## Relation to Prior Work

CARD extends the multi-LiDAR densification strategy pioneered by KITTI-DC and DrivingStereo to a dual front-and-rear LiDAR configuration with an adaptive multi-view voting scheme, achieving ~500K fused depth points per frame. It also builds on the road-surface reconstruction benchmark of RSRD but replaces its downward-facing cameras with standard forward-looking stereo, scaling from ~16K pairs to ~175K stereo pairs across 21 cities. On the method side, the paper modifies DMD3C by substituting its monocular teacher with FoundationStereo and replacing the scale-invariant loss with a direct L1 metric-depth loss, and fine-tunes MoGe2 with an additional height-space L1 term to recover centimeter-level road geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CARD fills a clear gap: no prior public dataset combines quasi-dense 3D ground truth with forward-looking sensors and explicit road-irregularity annotations at this scale, making it the first dedicated benchmark for evaluating fine road-surface geometry in realistic driving. A notable open question surfaced by the benchmarks is that LiDAR-image depth completion, despite densifying the scene, tends to oversmooth or suppress the very topographic features (speed bumps, potholes) the dataset targets, suggesting that current completion architectures may be ill-suited for centimeter-scale road geometry. Limitations include residual dynamic artifacts in rare slow-mover cases, anonymization false positives, and inherent ambiguity in delineating depression boundaries, all of which constrain the dataset's use for standard object-detection-style evaluation.
