## Problem

Existing cooperative 3D perception methods face two critical bottlenecks at long ranges (100–150 m): dense BEV representations incur quadratic computational and communication scaling with perception range, and fixed-distance-threshold query association becomes highly fragile under the severe observation and alignment errors inherent to distant targets. Even recent sparse-communication frameworks still rely on dense BEV backbones for query generation, inheriting the same computational bottleneck, while their heuristic matching heuristics collapse under positional noise.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB images from an ego-vehicle and one or more cooperative agents (roadside units or drones), resized to 960×540 (V2X-Seq) or 800×448 (Griffin), together with known camera intrinsics, extrinsics, and inter-agent relative pose. It produces a unified set of 3D object detections and tracking IDs within an ego-centered ROI, evaluated at extended ranges up to 150 m (V2X-Seq) and 100 m (Griffin). Crucially, only sparse object queries (≤940 per agent) are transmitted rather than dense BEV feature maps, keeping communication cost linear in the number of scene targets.

## Method

Long-SCOPE is a fully sparse, query-centric framework that eliminates all BEV feature maps. Its first novel module, Geometry-guided Query Generation (GQG), augments static anchors with dynamically generated 3D queries: for high-vantage agents it predicts a stable global object height (rather than ill-posed depth) and derives camera depth via a similar-triangle geometric relation; for ground-level agents it falls back to direct depth regression, then unprojects 2D proposals into 3D to initialize new queries. Its second novel module, Context-Aware Association (CAA), replaces rigid distance-threshold matching with a multi-layer Transformer that first applies intra-agent self-attention with positional encodings to encode local spatial topology, then inter-agent self-attention without positional encodings to achieve invariance to projection noise, and finally extracts discrete matches via Sinkhorn-normalized optimal transport with LightGlue-style per-descriptor matchability scores. The architecture builds on SparseCoop's multi-layer decoder, spatio-temporal alignment, and fusion pipeline, but replaces its fragile 30 m heuristic cutoff with this learnable, context-driven association that generalizes to N agents and correctly preserves ego-missed and ego-invisible queries.

## Results

On V2X-Seq (0–150 m), Long-SCOPE achieves 0.399 AP and 0.444 AMOTA, surpassing the closest sparse baseline SparseCoop (0.334 AP, 0.328 AMOTA) by 6.5 AP points. In the critical 100–150 m long-range bracket, Long-SCOPE reaches 0.113 AP—nearly 2× CoopTrack (0.059) and 3.6× SparseCoop (0.031)—while dense methods like V2X-ViT drop to 0.054 AP. On Griffin-25m (0–100 m aerial-ground), Long-SCOPE attains 0.354 AP and 0.327 AMOTA, outperforming CoopTrack by 7.5 AP points. Transmission cost remains competitive at 1.90×10⁵ BPS (~17× less than V2X-ViT) with 7.68 FPS inference.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Long-SCOPE outperforms all five baselines on both datasets across every distance bracket. Against the closest sparse competitor SparseCoop (AAAI'26), Long-SCOPE gains +6.5 AP overall on V2X-Seq and +8.9 AP on Griffin-25m, with the gap widening to +8.2 AP in the 100–150 m range where SparseCoop's heuristic 30 m cutoff causes it to forward unassociated queries blindly. Against the dense BEV-based V2X-ViT (ECCV'22), Long-SCOPE leads by +11.0 AP on V2X-Seq while using ~17× less bandwidth (1.90×10⁵ vs 3.21×10⁶ BPS). CoopTrack (ICCV'25) is the strongest dense baseline in the 0–50 m range (0.327 AP) but collapses at long range (0.059 AP at 100–150 m), where Long-SCOPE nearly doubles it. Where2Comm (NeurIPS'22) and UniV2X (AAAI'25) are substantially weaker, with Where2Comm achieving only 0.059 AP overall on V2X-Seq and UniV2X suffering near-zero tracking (0.019 AMOTA), indicating their association mechanisms fail under long-range positional noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

Long-SCOPE builds directly on SparseCoop, the first fully sparse cooperative perception architecture, adopting its multi-layer refinement, spatio-temporal alignment, and fusion pipeline while replacing its fragile heuristic-based query association (a fixed 30m/15m distance cutoff) with a learnable Context-Aware Association module. The CAA module draws conceptually from the feature-matching literature (SuperGlue's Sinkhorn normalization, LightGlue's matchability scoring) and repurposes these mechanisms for multi-agent 3D query correspondence. For query generation, it adapts the height-prediction strategy from BEVHeight and the 2D-to-3D lifting from Far3D, but applies them specifically to initialize sparse object queries rather than dense BEV features. The paper departs from the broader dense-BEV cooperative perception line (V2X-ViT, Where2Comm, CoopTrack) by eliminating BEV representations entirely, and from sparse-communication methods (SparseAlign, IFTR) that still rely on dense BEV backbones for query generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work identifies and addresses what the authors argue is the true bottleneck for practical long-range V2X deployment: not detection quality but the robustness of cross-agent query association under positional noise. The finding that removing CAA collapses 100–150 m AP from 0.113 to 0.041 (nearly eliminating the long-range advantage) provides strong evidence that heuristic matching is the primary failure mode, a diagnosis that should redirect future work in cooperative perception. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond two cooperative agents in dense urban scenes, whether 40 dynamic queries suffice for very high object densities, and whether the height-derived depth strategy generalizes to all high-vantage geometries (e.g., very low drone altitudes). The established long-range benchmarks (100–150 m on V2X-Seq, 50–100 m on Griffin) fill a gap in the evaluation landscape that prior work largely ignored.
