## Problem

While 3D Gaussian Splatting enables photorealistic, real-time head rendering, intuitive interactive creation and editing of such models remains difficult. Existing sketch-based 3D face methods either rely on slow per-instance optimization with error accumulation (e.g., SketchFaceNeRF) or lack fine-grained geometric control, and the inherent sparsity and depth ambiguity of 2D strokes make it hard to infer dense, geometrically consistent 3D structures under real-time constraints.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261623/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single hand-drawn 2D sketch (sparse line strokes defining facial geometry) and an optional reference portrait image (providing color and appearance cues) as input, and produces a full 3D Gaussian Splatting head model parameterized as a UV map over a FLAME template, with per-Gaussian position, scale, rotation, opacity, and color attributes. For editing, the user modifies the sketch (draw/erase strokes) and the system outputs an updated 3D Gaussian head in ~0.3 s with 243 FPS rendering, preserving unedited regions exactly.

## Method

SketchFaceGS uses a feed-forward, coarse-to-fine architecture. In the coarse stage, two parallel Transformer branches extract geometric features from the sketch and appearance features from the reference image via DINOv2-encoded features and learnable FLAME-vertex queries; an AdaIN-based alignment network then reconciles geometry-appearance conflicts to produce a coherent coarse UV map. In the fine stage, a U-Net predicts a global latent and multi-scale spatial modulation parameters that condition a pretrained GGHead StyleGAN2 generator, injecting high-frequency photorealistic detail while preserving identity via global identity vectors from the coarse stage. For editing, UV Mask Fusion back-projects 2D pixel edits to 3D Gaussians, maps them to canonical UV space, and performs layer-by-layer feature fusion within the StyleGAN generator's multi-scale feature maps—selectively preserving unedited regions at every resolution level—thereby avoiding the seam artifacts of direct 3D Gaussian compositing and enabling continuous, view-independent, real-time editing without per-instance optimization.

## Results

For sketch-to-3D head generation, SketchFaceGS achieves the best FID of 92.65 and KID of 4.00×10⁻² on a held-out set of 100 hand-drawn sketches, outperforming SketchFaceNeRF (FID 94.94) and S3D (FID 96.03). For interactive editing, the method attains FID 44.60 and KID 0.69×10⁻² with an end-to-end edit latency of ~0.3 s and 243 FPS rendering, substantially faster than SketchFaceNeRF (~10 s, 42 FPS) and Nano-LAM (~15 s, 281 FPS). In unedited-region identity preservation, SketchFaceGS scores PSNR 31.12 and SSIM 0.97, exceeding SketchFaceNeRF even after its per-instance optimization (PSNR 27.78, SSIM 0.95).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SketchFaceGS outperforms all five baselines on both generation and editing quality. In generation (Table 1), it beats SketchFaceNeRF by 2.29 FID points (92.65 vs 94.94) and S3D by 3.38 points, while Nano-LAM (a two-stage Nano-Banana + LAM pipeline) lags far behind at FID 133.72 due to geometric distortions from single-view 3D lifting. In editing (Table 2), the advantage is more pronounced: SketchFaceGS achieves FID 44.60 versus MagicQuill's 46.48, SketchFaceNeRF's 62.49, and Nano-LAM's 74.26, while being 33× faster than SketchFaceNeRF (0.3 s vs ~10 s) and 50× faster than Nano-LAM. MagicQuill is the closest competitor on editing FID but still trails by ~1.9 points and requires ~6 s per edit, and it introduces stylization and identity shifts. On identity preservation in unedited regions (Table 3), SketchFaceGS (PSNR 31.12, SSIM 0.97) surpasses SketchFaceNeRF both before optimization (PSNR 22.30) and after its 10 s per-instance optimization (PSNR 27.78), confirming that the feed-forward UV Mask Fusion avoids the error accumulation inherent in iterative optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: sketch-based 3D face modeling (SketchFaceNeRF, S3D, DeepSketch2Face), 3D Gaussian Splatting head generation (GGHead, GSGAN), and feed-forward 3D reconstruction (LAM, LRM). It departs from prior sketch-to-3D methods by replacing per-instance NeRF optimization with a feed-forward, coarse-to-fine Transformer pipeline that predicts UV features directly, and by repurposing GGHead's StyleGAN2 backbone as a controllable decoder rather than a random generator. The UV Mask Fusion editing mechanism is novel, operating in the generator's multi-scale feature space rather than compositing Gaussians in 3D, drawing conceptual inspiration from GFP-GAN's spatial modulation but applied to 3D UV feature maps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 33× speedup in editing latency (0.3 s vs. 10 s for SketchFaceNeRF) and 243 FPS rendering make interactive, artist-friendly 3D face creation from sketches practically viable for the first time, bridging a gap between intuitive 2D sketching and photorealistic 3D output. The layer-wise feature fusion strategy is a reusable technique that could benefit other 3DGS editing pipelines. Key limitations remain: identity shifts under geometric sketch-reference discrepancies, dependence on the GGHead prior restricting OOD handling (rare accessories, extreme occlusions), and the static-head constraint that precludes animation. Extending the framework to drivable avatars and improving identity consistency are natural next steps.
