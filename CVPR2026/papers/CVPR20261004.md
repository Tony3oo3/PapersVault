## Problem

Non-LLM-based AIGI detectors are efficient at catching low-level artifacts but generalize poorly to unseen generators, while LLM-based detectors offer semantic reasoning and cross-domain robustness at the cost of heavy inference and poor sensitivity to pixel-level forgeries. It remains unclear whether the explanatory reasoning texts produced by LLMs actually contribute to detection accuracy, and whether their generalization benefits can be transferred to a lightweight model without retaining the full LLM at inference time.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261004/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, ReAlign takes a single RGB image and outputs a binary real/fake probability via a two-layer MLP detection head; no text prompt or reasoning chain is required at test time. During training, the model consumes text-image pairs where the text side is a GRPO-optimized LLM's reasoning passage (extracted from  tags) prefixed with a class label sentence, while the image side is the original RGB image encoded by a CLIP ViT-L/14-336 backbone. The text encoder remains frozen throughout, so the only learned visual pathway is the LoRA-adapted image encoder plus the classification head.

## Method

ReAlign is a two-stage pipeline. First, a multimodal LLM (AIGI-R1) is trained with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) using outcome-based rewards (detection accuracy + format compliance) to produce high-quality, discriminative reasoning texts for each image. Second, these reasoning texts are paired with their source images to form a contrastive training set, and a CLIP-based detector is fine-tuned via joint optimization: a symmetric image-to-text contrastive loss aligns the LoRA-adapted image encoder's embeddings with the frozen text encoder's reasoning-text embeddings, while a weighted binary cross-entropy classification loss (α=8) drives the detection head. The key insight is that GRPO-optimized reasoning texts form a representation space with three exploitable properties—strong real/fake discriminability, cross-domain invariance, and semantic-error sensitivity—that, when distilled into a compact CLIP model via contrastive alignment, yield SOTA detection accuracy (96–99% across benchmarks) with only a single-GPU, LoRA-rank-6 training cost and no LLM at inference.

## Results

On AIGCDetectBenchmark (16 generators), ReAlign achieves a mean accuracy of 96.14%, surpassing the strongest non-LLM baseline AIDE (92.77%) by 3.37 points and the GRPO-trained LLM baseline AIGI-R1 (91.77%) by 4.37 points. On the more challenging AIGI-Holmes benchmark (10 advanced generators including FLUX and SD3.5-L), ReAlign reaches 99.44% mean accuracy, outperforming AIDE (97.00%) by 2.44 points. On the newly constructed UltraSynth-10k (5 proprietary generators: Qwen-Image, Seedream, GPT-4o, Gemini, HunYuan-Image), ReAlign attains 97.09% mean accuracy, exceeding AIDE (81.08%) by 16.01 points and AIGI-R1 (96.42%) by 0.67 points, demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization to unseen generators.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReAlign achieves the highest mean accuracy across all three benchmarks, winning against every compared method. On AIGCDetectBenchmark, it outperforms the strongest non-LLM baseline AIDE (92.77%) by 3.37 points and the LLM-based AIGI-R1 (91.77%) by 4.37 points; among non-LLM methods, PatchCraft (89.31%) and NPR (82.91%) are the next closest, while frequency-based methods like FreDect (64.03%) and DIRE (68.68–71.53%) lag significantly. On AIGI-Holmes, ReAlign (99.44%) surpasses AIDE (97.00%) by 2.44 points, RINE (96.20%) by 3.24 points, and the LLM-based AIGI-Holmes (92.30%) by 7.14 points. On UltraSynth-10k, the gap widens dramatically: ReAlign (97.09%) beats AIDE (81.08%) by 16.01 points, RINE (73.84%) by 23.25 points, and even the GRPO-optimized AIGI-R1 (96.42%) by 0.67 points, with the largest margins over frequency- and reconstruction-based methods (CNNSpot 71.93%, UniFD 66.35%). Notably, ReAlign is the only method to exceed 97% on all five proprietary generators in UltraSynth-10k, while AIGI-R1 is the only other method to surpass 89% on any of them, confirming that the distilled reasoning-text alignment transfers generalization without requiring an LLM at inference time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by identifying reasoning texts from RL-optimized LLMs as a transferable representation space that bridges two previously separate AIGI detection paradigms: lightweight non-LLM detectors (AIDE, PatchCraft, UniFD) that excel at low-level artifacts but lack semantic generalization, and LLM-based detectors (FakeShield, ForgeryGPT, AIGI-Holmes) that offer semantic reasoning but are computationally prohibitive. Rather than building on a single prior architecture, ReAlign repurposes the GRPO-optimized LLM (AIGI-R1, inspired by DeepSeek-R1) purely as a text generator whose outputs are distilled into a frozen-CLIP + LoRA image encoder via contrastive learning. The conceptual departure is treating LLM reasoning not as an inference-time explanation but as a training-time supervisory signal that reshapes the visual encoder's feature geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single RTX 3090 can train the final detector, and inference requires only a CLIP image encoder plus a two-layer MLP, making deployment on resource-constrained devices feasible—something no current LLM-based detector offers. The UltraSynth-10k benchmark, covering five frontier proprietary generators (GPT-4o, Gemini, Seedream, Qwen-Image, HunYuan-Image), fills a real gap since existing benchmarks lag behind the latest commercial models. Open questions remain: the two-stage pipeline (GRPO-train AIGI-R1, then contrastive-train ReAlign) is computationally non-trivial to reproduce, the method still inherits CLIP's blind spot for subtle low-level artifacts, and it is unclear whether the reasoning-text distillation strategy transfers to video forgery or adversarially robust detection settings.
