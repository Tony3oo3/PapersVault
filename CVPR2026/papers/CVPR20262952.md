## Problem

Accelerating VAR-based image generation is challenging because attention patterns across scales exhibit strong semantic biases (coarse-scale attention sinks and spatial locality), making naive token pruning or fixed masks degrade quality non-uniformly. Existing methods rely on handcrafted heuristics without a quantitative criterion for the speed-quality trade-off, and they ignore the VQ-VAE decoder, which further degrades when fed pruned latent sequences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262952/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on the inference pipeline of VAR-based models, taking a class label (ImageNet-1K), a text prompt (GenEval/Infinity), or an image-plus-question pair (VARGPT VQA) as input and producing a generated image (256×256 for ImageNet, higher resolution for Infinity) or a text answer. The SCA-Mask is pre-computed offline on ~50K reference images (50 per class) before any inference run, and PAA requires only the start-of-sequence token derived from the input label or prompt—no external image dataset is needed during adaptation.

## Method

RADAR is a two-stage acceleration framework. First, SCA-Mask partitions each attention matrix into GPU-aligned tiles and accumulates two semantic scores per tile across all generation steps: Score^Q (how often the tile's queries attend to other tiles) and Score^KV (how often the tile's keys/values are queried). Mask design is then cast as a budget-constrained optimization that maximizes retained semantic content subject to compute and memory limits, solved as a mixed-integer program before inference. Second, PAA performs a two-branch forward: the unmasked VAR produces teacher latents while the masked VAR produces degraded student latents, and a LoRA module attached to the frozen VQ-VAE decoder is trained via internal knowledge distillation (MSE + LPIPS loss) to project degraded latents back to high-fidelity images. At test time only the accelerated branch runs, so PAA adds zero inference overhead, and the entire adaptation is data-free and tunes only a tiny fraction of model parameters.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K class-conditional generation, RADAR applied to VAR-d20 achieves FID 2.68, IS 293.1, Precision 0.83, and Recall 0.54 at 92.74 it/s throughput (1.92× speedup over vanilla VAR's 51.42 it/s), closely matching the unaccelerated VAR-d20 (FID 2.61) while the degraded VAR without compensation drops to FID 5.02. For VAR-d24, RADAR recovers FID to 2.19 (vs. 4.29 degraded) at 57.93 it/s (1.80× speedup), and for VAR-d30, FID 2.01 at 35.65 it/s (1.57× speedup). On the GenEval text-to-image benchmark with Infinity, RADAR achieves GenEval 0.69 and FID 22.97 at 2.08 it/s, outperforming both ScaleKV (GenEval 0.64) and FastVAR (GenEval 0.60) in quality while also achieving the highest throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against diffusion and token-wise autoregressive baselines (DiT-XL/2, MAR-B, AIM-XL, LlamaGen-XXL), RADAR-equipped VAR-d20 trades a small FID gap (2.68 vs. 2.27–2.54) for a massive throughput advantage of 12–100× (92.74 it/s vs. 0.93–7.27 it/s), making it the fastest method by an order of magnitude. On the text-to-image GenEval benchmark with Infinity, RADAR outperforms ScaleKV on both GenEval (0.69 vs. 0.64) and throughput (2.08 vs. 1.87 it/s), and substantially outperforms FastVAR on FID (22.97 vs. 26.44), GenEval (0.69 vs. 0.60), and throughput (2.08 vs. 1.98 it/s), though it trails the unaccelerated Infinity baseline in FID (22.97 vs. 19.61) and GenEval (0.69 vs. 0.73). On VQA benchmarks with VARGPT-v1.1, RADAR incurs modest drops (GQA 63.8 vs. 66.2, TextVQA 77.3 vs. 82.0, VQAv2 79.7 vs. 80.4) while delivering 1.6× throughput improvement, still outperforming Qwen-VL-Chat, mPLUG-Owl2, and LLaVA-1.5 on all three VQA tasks. Overall, RADAR consistently wins on the speed–quality Pareto frontier: it sacrifices at most ~0.1–0.3 FID or ~0.01–0.04 GenEval relative to the unaccelerated model while achieving 1.5–1.9× speedup, and it dominates all prior acceleration methods (ScaleKV, FastVAR) on quality at equal or higher throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VAR's next-scale prediction paradigm and its text-to-image extension Infinity, targeting the inference acceleration problem that prior works (LiteVAR, FastVAR, HACK, SkipVAR, MVAR) address through heuristic attention masks or token pruning. It departs from this lineage in two ways: SCA-Mask replaces handcrafted mask patterns with a budget-constrained optimization over tile-level semantic scores, and PAA introduces decoder-side LoRA adaptation via internal knowledge distillation, a component entirely absent from prior VAR acceleration methods that focus exclusively on the autoregressive transformer. The internal knowledge distillation framework from Aguilar et al. [1] is repurposed here in a data-free, two-branch setting where the unmasked model serves as its own teacher.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The PAA module offers a plug-and-play, data-free mechanism to recover quality after aggressive acceleration, which could be retrofitted onto any existing VAR acceleration method without retraining the large transformer. This is practically significant because retraining-based compensation (e.g., MVAR) costs orders of magnitude more GPU-hours. Open questions include whether the optimization-based mask design generalizes to non-uniform tile costs on heterogeneous hardware, whether PAA can handle even more extreme compression ratios where latent degradation becomes irrecoverable, and whether the approach transfers to other autoregressive paradigms beyond next-scale prediction. The paper's evaluation is limited to a single GPU (RTX 5090) and does not explore interaction with other inference optimizations like FlashAttention or quantization.
