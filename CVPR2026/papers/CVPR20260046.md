## Problem

Existing 3D scene representations for generation either rely on explicit 3D structures (Gaussians, NeRFs) that are prohibitively large and expensive to train, or on high-dimensional view-aligned latent fields (e.g., >3K tokens of dimension ≥512) that are ill-suited for latent diffusion. No prior method encodes a scene into a small, unstructured, permutation-invariant token set that is simultaneously highly compressed, renderable from arbitrary novel trajectories, and amenable to efficient generative modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260046/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The autoencoder takes N posed RGB context views (e.g., 5–16 images with 6-DoF camera poses) and produces K continuous scene tokens (e.g., 32.76K floats for 12 views, 65.54K for 16 views), which are 1–3 orders of magnitude smaller than competing representations. The generative decoder renders M novel views from any trajectory by sampling a rectified flow in latent image space, while the SceneGen stage takes a single conditioning image plus N camera anchor poses (each a 6D origin-direction pair) and outputs a full set of scene tokens for a new scene. Scenes are centered at the origin during encoding, with all poses expressed relative to this origin.

## Method

SceneTok is a two-stage pipeline. The encoder chains a frozen VA-VAE image compressor (16× spatial downsample) with a scene perceiver that has two branches: a multi-view branch where per-patch tokens are AdaLN-modulated by ray-map embeddings and processed with multi-view attention, and a scene-query branch where K learned queries attend to the multi-view features via cross-attention, yielding unstructured tokens. 2D RoPE (not 3D) is used to enforce permutation invariance and avoid temporal bias. The decoder is a lightweight rectified-flow diffusion transformer (≈595M params, LightningDiT-style) that denoises latent image patches conditioned on scene tokens via cross-attention and ray maps via AdaLN, then decodes to pixels with a frozen VideoDCAE; training uses a flow-matching MSE loss end-to-end. The second stage, SceneGen, is a diffusion transformer trained on the frozen token space with rectified flows, conditioned on a conditioning image embedding and averaged anchor-ray embeddings, enabling 5-second scene generation on a consumer GPU.

## Results

On RealEstate10K (12 context views), SceneTok achieves PSNR 23.99, LPIPS 0.159, rFVD 79.80, and rFID 11.12 with only 32.76K floats in the scene token representation — a 1–3 order-of-magnitude compression over explicit 3D representations (46M–75M floats) while outperforming them on all quality metrics. For single-view scene generation, SceneGen produces 192 views in 26 s (11 s generation + 16 s rendering) on an H100, or 10 s on a consumer RTX 4090, achieving gFID 18.90 and MEt3R 0.0156, competitive with SEVA (gFID 17.69) but 60× faster. On transferability (TPS), SceneTok reaches 89.86% rotation accuracy at 30° threshold, far exceeding LVSM (74.56%) and RayZer (54.45%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against explicit 3D representations (MVSplat, MVSplat360, DepthSplat, Long-LRM), SceneTok wins on PSNR, LPIPS, rFVD, and rFID across all settings, while using 1–3 orders of magnitude fewer parameters; the only loss is SSIM vs. DepthSplat (0.783 vs. 0.809), which benefits from explicit depth supervision. Against the closest latent-representation baseline LVSM, SceneTok dominates on every metric (e.g., rFVD 79.80 vs. 211.66, rFID 11.12 vs. 26.40) and is 48× more compressed (32.76K vs. 1.57M floats). RayZer achieves higher absolute NVS quality (PSNR 26.89 on DL3DV) but requires target views as input, making it incapable of true novel-trajectory rendering; on the transferability benchmark (TPS), SceneTok scores 89.86% rotation accuracy at 30° vs. RayZer's 54.45% and LVSM's 74.56%. For scene generation, SceneGen matches or exceeds DFM and DFoT on all quality metrics (gFID 18.90 vs. 52.64 and 35.40) while being 24× and 5.6× faster respectively; it slightly trails SEVA on gFVD (157.89 vs. 133.00) and gFID (18.90 vs. 17.69), which the authors attribute to SEVA's access to a large closed-source training corpus, but SceneGen is 62× faster (26 s vs. 1620 s) and runs on a 24 GB consumer GPU where SEVA and DFoT run out of memory.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneTok sits at the intersection of vision tokenizers (VA-VAE, VideoDCAE from Open-Sora 2.0), feed-forward 3D reconstruction (LVSM, RayZer), and 3D scene generation (DFM, DFoT, SEVA). It departs from prior latent-reconstruction methods (LVSM, RayZer) by compressing scenes into an unstructured, permutation-invariant token set of only ~32K floats—1–3 orders of magnitude smaller—rather than thousands of high-dimensional tokens tied to a spatial grid. The key architectural departure is the use of a generative rectified-flow decoder (resembling LightningDiT) instead of a deterministic renderer, which allows the model to gracefully handle uncertainty in novel views. The cascaded design (autoencoder → latent diffusion transformer) explicitly decouples view rendering from scene generation, a paradigm not present in prior view-space or 3D-space generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The decoupling of rendering from generation is a practical paradigm shift: it allows scaling the generative model independently of rendering speed, and the compressed token space makes latent diffusion tractable where prior high-dimensional representations made it infeasible. The 5-second single-GPU generation (vs. 146–1620 s for baselines that OOM on the same hardware) has direct implications for interactive 3D content creation. Limitations include weaker high-frequency detail compared to pixel-space generators (DFoT, SEVA) and training restricted to indoor real-estate scenes; open questions include whether the unstructured token space scales to outdoor or large-scale scenes, and whether structured latent spaces or better image compressors can close the detail gap.
