## Problem

Existing panorama stitching methods—whether traditional feature-based or learning-based—are confined to pairwise 2D correspondences, causing accumulated projection errors, severe distortion, and misalignment in scenes with weak textures, large parallax, and repetitive patterns. No prior approach explicitly preserves 3D geometric consistency across all views for global alignment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264020/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The network ingests a batch of N partially overlapping perspective RGB images (2048×2048 in the Pano360 dataset) ranging from a few to hundreds of frames, with no external camera parameters required. It jointly predicts per-image camera intrinsics and extrinsics, a local mesh warp field for parallax correction, and a seam mask, then composites them into a single seamless panorama in a user-selectable projection format (planar, equirectangular, or spherical).

## Method

Pano360 is a dual-branch transformer that patchifies all input images with a pre-trained DINO encoder, prepends a learnable camera token to the concatenated sequence, and processes it through L alternating-attention layers initialized from frozen VGGT weights to achieve global 3D awareness. The projection head decodes the camera token into shared-focal-length intrinsics and per-image extrinsics (first camera fixed as reference), which define a projection function that warps each image onto a common panoramic canvas; a local mesh warp corrects residual parallax. The seam head predicts globally optimal seam masks in a single forward pass, trained with pseudo-labels generated by a multi-feature joint energy minimization over color difference, gradient magnitude, and texture complexity across all overlapping images simultaneously. Training uses a multi-task loss combining Huber loss on camera parameters, L1 loss on seam masks, and a projection-format loss, with the alternating-attention module kept frozen throughout.

## Results

On the Pano360 dataset, Pano360 achieves the best perceptual quality across all three challenging scenes, with QA_q scores of 4.08, 3.57, and 4.09 (vs. 3.95, 3.45, and 3.74 for the best baseline GES-GSP) and the lowest BRISQUE scores (34.65, 28.79, 37.96). In terms of robustness, Pano360 achieves a 97.8% stitching success rate in ~5 s, compared to 83.3% in ~20 s for the best geometric-feature baseline (GES-GSP). On the UDIS-D dataset (pairwise stitching), Pano360 attains the highest PSNR (25.97 dB), SSIM (0.852), and lowest PIQE (42.12) and NIQE (5.78), outperforming UDIS2 (25.43 dB, 0.838, 48.09, 6.11) despite not being trained on that dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Pano360 outperforms all 13 baselines on every metric in the primary Pano360 evaluation (Table 1). Against the strongest geometric-feature baseline GES-GSP [6], Pano360 improves QA_q by 0.13–0.35 points and reduces BRISQUE by 1.8–5.4 points across the three scenes, while also achieving a 97.8% success rate versus 83.3% and a ~4× speedup (~5 s vs. ~20 s). Pairwise deep-learning methods (LPC [8], UDIS2 [27]) perform substantially worse on multi-image scenes (QA_q drops to 2.57–3.02) because they cannot handle more than two images without expensive post-processing. On the UDIS-D pairwise benchmark (Table 3), Pano360 edges out UDIS2 [27] (PSNR 25.97 vs. 25.43 dB; SSIM 0.852 vs. 0.838) and DHS [22] (PSNR 25.97 vs. 25.88 dB) while achieving the lowest PIQE (42.12 vs. 45.73) and NIQE (5.78 vs. 6.11), confirming strong generalization despite no training on that dataset. Traditional feature-matching pipelines (SIFT+RANSAC, LoFTR+RANSAC, LightGlue+RANSAC) all trail Pano360 in both alignment accuracy and perceptual quality, with success rates capped at 63–67% on the challenging Pano360 scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

Pano360 departs fundamentally from the dominant pairwise paradigm in panorama stitching (SIFT/SURF/ORB-based homography estimation, LoFTR/LightGlue matching, and CNN-based pairwise methods like UDIS/UDIS2) by formulating alignment as a global 3D photogrammetric problem. It builds directly on VGGT's alternating-attention transformer architecture, repurposing its 3D-aware camera tokens (initialized from pre-trained weights and kept frozen) to predict per-image camera parameters that define a shared projection coordinate system. The seam detection module extends the classical graph-cut energy minimization by jointly optimizing color, gradient, and texture terms across all overlapping images simultaneously, rather than the pairwise formulation used in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from pairwise 2D correspondences to global 3D alignment addresses a long-standing failure mode in panorama stitching—error accumulation across many images—yielding a 97.8% success rate versus 83.3% for the best prior method and a ~10× speedup in large-scale scenes. The accompanying Pano360 dataset (14,400 images, 200 real-world scenes with ground-truth camera parameters) fills a gap left by existing synthetic or pairwise-only benchmarks. Open questions remain: the method cannot handle inputs with inherent lens distortion, and extremely large parallax still requires explicit 3D reconstruction, suggesting a path toward integrating learned depth or Gaussian splatting for full generality.
