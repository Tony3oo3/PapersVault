## Problem

Existing monocular metric depth estimation methods either require calibrated camera intrinsics at training and inference (Metric3D, Metric3D v2) or learn to predict focal length from image content (Depth Pro, UniDepth). When focal length and scene depth vary together, RGB appearance can remain nearly invariant, making scale recovery from pixels alone difficult. This paper exploits the observation that edge-frequency statistics in the image undergo systematic, scale-correlated shifts under such coupled changes, providing a camera-free cue for metric calibration.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image as input with no camera intrinsics, resolution metadata, or other camera parameters required at either training or inference time. It produces two outputs: a metric depth map (in absolute units) and a predicted edge map. The edge map serves a dual role — it is fed to the Spectral Quantile Estimator to compute a scalar scale score that calibrates the depth, and it is used as a boundary regularizer to sharpen depth discontinuities.

## Method

MD2E builds on a DINOv2-L encoder with a DPT decoder (adapted from Metric3D v2) that outputs both an initial depth field and an edge map. Dense depth annotations are converted into edge supervision via a depth-to-edge transformation that operates on inverse depth using anisotropic finite differences, local softmax contrast, and a center-minus-minimum edge strength formulation. The predicted edge map is processed by a Spectral Quantile Estimator (SQE) that computes a windowed Fourier power spectrum, applies a smooth radial high-pass filter, aggregates energy into a differentiable radial histogram via Gaussian soft assignment, and extracts a soft quantile of the cumulative radial energy to yield a single scalar score t_pred. This score calibrates the depth logits through a sigmoid scaling, converting relative estimates to metric units. Training uses a composite loss combining class-balanced cross-entropy for edge prediction, L1 alignment between predicted and GT SQE scores, a standard metric depth loss, and an MSE term enforcing consistency between the predicted edge map and edges derived from the predicted depth.

## Results

In zero-shot evaluation on six unseen benchmarks, MD2E achieves the best A.Rel on all six (e.g., 0.089 on DIODE indoor, 0.087 on iBIMS-1, 0.057 on NYUv2, 0.209 on DIODE outdoor, 0.277 on ETH3D, 0.050 on KITTI) and the best RMS on ETH3D (1.512) and KITTI (1.801). In in-domain fine-tuning, MD2E attains the best δ₁ (0.992 NYUv2, 0.990 KITTI), A.Rel (0.044/0.035), and RMS (0.168/1.520) on both NYUv2 and KITTI, surpassing all baselines including UniDepthV2 and Metric3D v2. The ablation confirms that removing the SQE calibration causes a 58% drop in δ₁ and a 126% increase in A.Rel, validating the spectral-quantile edge score as the critical component.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest no-intrinsics competitor UniDepthV2 [41], MD2E reduces A.Rel by 45% on DIODE indoor (0.089 vs 0.161), 3% on iBIMS-1 (0.087 vs 0.090), 25% on NYUv2 (0.057 vs 0.076), and 83% on DIODE outdoor (0.209 vs 1.231); on ETH3D, MD2E achieves lower RMS (1.512 vs 1.617) but slightly higher A.Rel (0.277 vs 0.256). Compared to Depth Pro [6], MD2E halves the RMS on ETH3D (1.512 vs 3.699) and KITTI (1.801 vs 4.368) while also improving A.Rel on all six benchmarks. Versus Metric3D v2 [22], which requires calibrated intrinsics at both training and inference, MD2E matches or beats it on A.Rel for five of six benchmarks and on RMS for ETH3D and KITTI, without any camera parameter input. In in-domain fine-tuning (Table 2), MD2E outperforms Depth Anything V2 [61] by 21–22% in A.Rel and 18% in KITTI RMS, and surpasses IEBins [46] by 49% in NYUv2 A.Rel and 31% in KITTI A.Rel. Older baselines Adabins [3], NewCRFs [63], and ZoeDepth [5] are outperformed by large margins (2–10× in A.Rel) across all benchmarks where they report results.

## Relation to Prior Work

MD2E builds directly on the Metric3D v2 architecture (DINOv2-L encoder + DPT decoder) and the MiDaS/Depth Anything lineage of heterogeneous-dataset training for robust monocular depth estimation. It departs from prior zero-shot MMDE methods (Metric3D v2, UniDepth, UniDepthV2, Depth Pro) by eliminating the need for camera intrinsics at both training and inference, replacing explicit focal-length conditioning with a Spectral Quantile Estimator (SQE) that reads a single scale proxy from the Fourier spectrum of a predicted edge map. The depth-to-edge transformation, which converts dense depth annotations into edge supervision, extends the edge-cue literature (Zhu et al., Talker et al., Li et al.) that previously used edges only for boundary sharpening, repurposing them here as a carrier of camera-induced scale information.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Removing the intrinsics dependency makes metric depth estimation applicable in settings where camera calibration is unavailable or unreliable (e.g., consumer devices, multi-camera rigs with unknown settings), broadening the practical reach of MMDE. The SQE provides an interpretable, per-image scale signal that adapts to both focal length and scene layout, but the moderate Pearson correlation (~0.698) with the true (f_x/W, d_max) pair suggests the cue is informative yet incomplete, leaving room for richer spectral or multi-scale descriptors. An open question is how the approach behaves under extreme focal-length ranges or scene types not well represented in the 4M training pool, and whether the depth-to-edge transformation can be replaced by self-supervised edge learning to reduce reliance on dense depth annotations.
