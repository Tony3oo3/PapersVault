## Problem

VecSet-based image-to-3D generation treats shape tokens as an unordered set without positional encoding, forcing the diffusion model to simultaneously learn visible image-to-shape correspondences and hallucinate occluded geometry within a single undifferentiated token space. This conflation of two fundamentally different learning tasks—constrained visible reconstruction versus unconstrained invisible hallucination—significantly inflates the effective mapping space and severely hinders training convergence speed and stability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260335/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image (encoded via DINOv2 into image tokens) and generates a complete 3D mesh through a VecSet decoder. During training, it additionally requires visible surface points rendered from the ground-truth shape at the conditioning camera viewpoint to perform visibility grouping. Shape tokens are produced by a pretrained VecSet VAE (DORA) with 2048 tokens (up to 4096 for the scaled model), and the diffusion model is trained with conditional flow matching on these tokens.

## Method

ViLearn introduces two synergistic components: Visibility Grouping (VG) partitions the unordered shape token set into visible (VST) and invisible (IST) subsets by computing cross-attention scores between rendered visible surface points and shape tokens in the pretrained VecSet decoder, exploiting the decoder's spatial locality. Visibility-Aware Positional Encoding (VAPE) then injects this partition into the MM-DiT double-stream attention by modifying query and key matrices: image tokens and VST share the same positional encoding (a rotation matrix R_m or learnable embedding e_v) to strengthen cross-modal correspondence, while IST receives a distinct encoding (R_n or e_i) to signal hallucination from learned priors. Two VAPE variants are explored—VA-RoPE (repurposing rotary embeddings as categorical visibility codes) and VA-LPE (learnable additive embeddings). At inference, a fixed partition (first third visible, remaining two-thirds invisible) approximates the training visibility distribution without requiring explicit visibility estimation.

## Results

On a 1,100-image test set, ViLearn (1.1B) achieves the best scores across all four metrics: IS-AS 0.702, Floater 31.5, GP 5.988, and GD 2.688. Compared to the vanilla VecSet baseline trained under identical conditions (32 GPUs, 110K steps), ViLearn delivers a 57.8% reduction in floaters (31.5 vs. 74.8) and a 7% gain in IS-AS (0.702 vs. 0.656). In terms of convergence speed, ViLearn with VA-RoPE reaches the baseline's 110K-step performance in only 25K steps, corresponding to a 4.4× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViLearn outperforms all three SOTA VecSet-based models (Hunyuan3D 2.0, TripoSG, Step1X-3D) on every metric despite using only 32 GPUs, whereas those models typically require hundreds of GPUs for weeks of training. Against the closest competitor Hunyuan3D 2.0 (1.1B), ViLearn leads by a small but consistent margin: IS-AS 0.702 vs. 0.699, Floater 31.5 vs. 32.7, GP 5.988 vs. 5.949, and GD 2.688 vs. 2.668. The gap widens substantially against TripoSG (1.3B) and Step1X-3D (1.3B), where ViLearn reduces floaters by 55% and 58% respectively and improves IS-AS by 0.033 and 0.056. Against the vanilla baseline trained under identical conditions, ViLearn achieves a 57.8% floater reduction and 7% IS-AS improvement, confirming that the visibility-aware training paradigm—not merely more compute—drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the MM-DiT backbone (Esser et al., 2024) and the DORA VecSet VAE decoder (Chen et al., CVPR 2025), repurposing the decoder's cross-attention to partition shape tokens by visibility. It departs from leading VecSet-based 3D generators (CLAY, TripoSG, Step1X-3D, Hunyuan3D 2.0) that treat shape tokens as a permutation-invariant set without positional encoding, by arguing that this uniform treatment conflates two fundamentally different learning sub-tasks: visible correspondence and invisible hallucination. The visibility-aware positional encoding (VAPE) adapts RoPE (Su et al., 2021) from continuous positional encoding to a discrete two-category visibility signal, a repurposing that is conceptually simple but targets a specific structural asymmetry absent in prior 3D-native generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 4.4× convergence speedup is practically significant for 3D generation pipelines where training costs dominate, and the visibility-aware inductive bias is lightweight enough to be adopted by existing VecSet-based systems with minimal architectural change. However, the inference-time approximation of a fixed one-third visible token partition, while empirically sufficient, raises open questions about whether explicit visibility estimation at inference could further improve quality for atypical viewpoints. The finding that naive RoPE without visibility structure actually degrades performance below the no-PE baseline suggests that positional encoding in set-based 3D representations must be semantically grounded, not merely geometric—a principle that may generalize to other unordered-token generative models. Whether this visibility-disentanglement strategy extends to multi-view conditioning or to sparse-voxel representations with their own positional structure remains unexplored.
