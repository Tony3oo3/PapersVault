## Problem

Certified unlearning in fully decentralized networks with fixed topologies remains underexplored: existing approaches either require all clients (or all neighbors) to participate in unlearning, rely on dynamic topologies, or use decentralized differential privacy (DDP) whose noise scales linearly with the forget-set size via group privacy. The paper asks whether unlearning can be performed autonomously by a single client on a fixed graph, and how decentralization (routing, mixing, trust regions) affects the privacy-utility trade-off and deletion capacity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a trained model parameter vector θ, a forget set D_f of size m held by a specific unlearning user u, the fixed communication graph G, and a routing probability p (typically 1/N). It produces an unlearned model after T_u random-walk hops, certified per non-deleting client view O_v with (ε,δ)-indistinguishability from a certifier that never accessed D_f. Unusually, only the unlearning client u injects noise; all other clients perform noiseless updates, so the privacy guarantee is view-specific and post-deletion rather than a global transcript guarantee.

## Method

RR-DU operates a single token carrying θ on a random walk over the graph: at the unlearning user u (visited with probability p per hop), it takes one projected gradient ascent step on the forget set with additive Gaussian noise, projected onto a trust ball B(θ_ref, ϱ) around the original model; at all other users, it takes noiseless projected gradient descent steps on local data, optionally averaging s ≥ 1 mini-batches to reduce variance. The key insight is gradient alignment: by choosing the corrective direction at u as either −∇ℓ_{u\f} (exact) or (m/n_u)∇ℓ_f (lightweight), the expected update recovers the retraining gradient ∇L_{\f}, making the required noise scale independent of the forget-set size m—unlike DDP where group privacy forces noise to grow linearly with m. Privacy is certified via Rényi DP with segment-level subsampling over the geometric number of sensitive visits, combined with network-mixing amplification (√(ln N / N)) so that each observer sees only a limited fraction of noisy events. The paper proves last-iterate excess-risk and stationarity bounds for convex, strongly convex, and smooth nonconvex objectives, and characterizes a two-regime deletion capacity separating a variance term (independent of m) from an alignment-bias term O(Lm/n_u).

## Results

On MNIST/FLNet, RR-DU drives backdoor accuracy (ASR) to ≈10% (matching the retraining-from-scratch baseline) while maintaining ≈99.1–99.2% clean accuracy, compared to DDP plateauing at ≈35% ASR with ≈96.7% clean accuracy and DP-SGD retaining ≈60% ASR. On CIFAR-10/ResNet-18, RR-DU again reaches ≈10% ASR and ≈88–89% clean accuracy (scratch baseline ≈89%), whereas DP-SGD and DDP saturate at only ≈50–55% clean accuracy and retain substantial backdoor signal. Fine-tuning after data removal stabilizes at ≈18% ASR (MNIST) and ≈25–30% ASR (CIFAR-10), still well above RR-DU. All experiments use ε=1, δ=10⁻⁵, N=10 clients on a complete graph, T_u=100 unlearning hops, and s=4 local averaging.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDP [14] (the primary decentralized-privacy baseline), RR-DU wins decisively on both metrics: on MNIST it reduces ASR from ≈35% to ≈10% and improves clean accuracy from ≈96.7% to ≈99.1%; on CIFAR-10 the gap is even larger, with DDP stuck at ≈50–55% clean accuracy while RR-DU reaches ≈88–89%. Against DP-SGD [1], RR-DU similarly dominates, cutting MNIST ASR from ≈60% to ≈10% and CIFAR-10 clean accuracy from ≈50–55% to ≈88–89%. Against fine-tuning (removing poisoned data and re-optimizing), RR-DU achieves substantially better backdoor removal (≈10% vs. ≈18% on MNIST, ≈10% vs. ≈25–30% on CIFAR-10) while matching or exceeding clean accuracy. The comparison with PDUDT [42] is limited and not fully head-to-head because PDUDT relies on dynamic topologies and gossip whereas RR-DU uses a random walk on a fixed graph; the paper reports this comparison separately in the appendix. The theoretical advantage is clear: DDP's noise scales linearly with the forget-set size m (group privacy), while RR-DU's noise is independent of m, yielding a two-regime deletion capacity that is linear in local data size n_u once the variance term is controlled.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the network differential privacy framework of Cyffers and Bellet (AISTATS 2022), which established view-based privacy amplification through decentralization on fixed graphs, and extends the centralized certified unlearning literature (Ginart et al., Guo et al., Allouah et al., Sekhari et al.) to the fully decentralized setting. It departs from Qiao et al.'s prior certified decentralized unlearning (dynamic topologies, all-clients-involved, past-gradient storage) by restricting to fixed topologies, localizing noise to a single unlearning client, and introducing a view-based formulation with deletion-capacity analysis. A key conceptual departure is the explicit separation of DDP-as-certifier from certified unlearning, showing that group-privacy-based DDP is theoretically valid but practically suboptimal because its noise scales linearly with the forget-set size.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first rigorous view-based formulation of certified unlearning on fixed decentralized graphs and a two-regime deletion-capacity characterization that clarifies when localized-noise methods outperform network-wide DDP. Practically, RR-DU reduces backdoor accuracy to the random-guessing baseline while preserving near-scratch clean accuracy under matched (ε,δ) budgets, suggesting a viable path for privacy-preserving unlearning in coordinator-free deployments. Key limitations include fixed topologies, a single token and single deleting client, honest-but-curious observers, and predominantly i.i.d. data; open questions include concurrent deletions, dynamic/gossip topologies, strong heterogeneity, and whether the m-independent noise advantage persists under non-i.i.d. or adversarial routing.
