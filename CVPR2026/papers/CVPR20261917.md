## Problem

Brain tumor lesions occupy only ~10.7% of the 160×160 image area, creating severe spatial sparsity that causes segmentation networks to waste computation on background regions. Moreover, existing methods implicitly assume uniform lesion distribution, ignoring well-documented clinical spatial priors (e.g., tumors concentrate near the frontal-temporal junction and are rare in the occipital lobe), which leads to redundant feature computation and suboptimal localization accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261917/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes 2D slices (160×160, cropped from 155×240×240 3D volumes) across four MRI modalities (T1, T1ce, T2, FLAIR) with Z-score normalization applied to the foreground. It produces pixel-wise segmentation masks for three BraTS-convention regions: Whole Tumor (WT = labels 1+2+4), Tumor Core (TC = 1+4), and Enhancing Tumor (ET = 4). All 3D volumes are sliced along the height axis and processed in 2D form, with black background regions cropped before normalization.

## Method

PGR-Net constructs a data-driven ROI prior set by extracting lesion center distributions and scale peaks from the training set, then uses these priors to drive a Hierarchical Top-K (HTK) mechanism that progressively narrows candidate ROI regions across encoder layers via lightweight MLP scoring. A WinGS-ROI module generates center-enhanced guidance maps using multi-window Gaussian templates with radial spatial decay, applied multiplicatively to modulate features at every layer (backbone, skip-connections, and up-sampling). The visual backbone is a windowed RetNet that processes each selected ROI window as a sequence via the retention mechanism, with confidence-weighted aggregation across candidate ROIs. During decoding, ROI-Only up-sampling and ROI-Aware skip-connections restrict feature propagation to the selected region, while a fallback to full-image mode activates when the HTK confidence gap or entropy indicates uncertain localization. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with a weighted Dice+BCE loss (2:8 ratio) for 300 epochs.

## Results

PGR-Net achieves the best Dice scores across all three datasets and all tumor sub-regions: on BraTS-2023 it reaches 91.82% (WT), 94.07% (TC), and 93.88% (ET); on BraTS-2019, 89.02% (WT), 90.69% (TC), 93.61% (ET); and on MSD Task01, 89.67% (WT), 89.27% (TC), 90.63% (ET). It also attains the lowest HD95 values (e.g., 1.1334 on BraTS-2023 WT). The model uses only 8.64M parameters and 39.05G FLOPs, with an inference time of 9 min 41 s—substantially less than all compared methods (e.g., UNet: 39.40M params, 321.19G FLOPs, 12 min 32 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PGR-Net outperforms all 14 compared methods on every Dice metric across all three datasets. On BraTS-2023 WT, it leads the strongest SSM-based competitors—M-Net (91.33%), Mamba Sea (91.12%), and Swin UNETR (91.11%)—by 0.5–0.7 percentage points, and surpasses the convolutional UNet (90.71%) by over 1.1 points. The margin is largest on the ET sub-region, where PGR-Net's 93.88% on BraTS-2023 exceeds the next-best Mamba Sea (93.47%) by 0.41 points and the Transformer-based Swin UNETR (93.42%) by 0.46 points. On MSD Task01, PGR-Net's WT Dice of 89.67% beats M-Net (89.04%) and Mamba Sea (89.15%) by roughly 0.5–0.6 points. In terms of boundary fidelity (HD95), PGR-Net also achieves the lowest values across all regions and datasets, e.g., 1.1334 on BraTS-2023 WT versus 1.1534 for M-Net and 1.1837 for Mamba Sea. Notably, PGR-Net attains these gains with only 8.64M parameters and 39.05G FLOPs, compared to 25–105M parameters and 61–568G FLOPs for the other methods, and completes inference in 9 min 41 s versus 13–30 min for the nearest competitors.

## Relation to Prior Work

PGR-Net adapts RetNet's retention mechanism (Sun et al., 2023) from NLP to medical image segmentation by processing ROI windows as sequences, positioning it alongside Mamba-based SSM backbones (Mamba-UNet, VM-UNet, M-Net) but with a distinct architectural choice. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant assumption in brain tumor segmentation that lesion locations follow a uniform distribution, instead deriving explicit spatial priors (center locations and scale peaks) from training-set statistics and injecting them as soft Gaussian guidance throughout the encoder-decoder. This contrasts with prior ROI-guided approaches [9, 22] that use hard, hand-specified regions, and with attention-based methods that implicitly learn spatial focus without any prior constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency-accuracy trade-off: PGR-Net achieves SOTA-level Dice scores with only 8.64M parameters and 39G FLOPs, roughly 3–28× fewer parameters than competing methods, by concentrating computation on statistically likely lesion regions rather than the full 160×160 image. However, the framework is limited to 2D slices of 3D volumes, the spatial priors are currently derived only for Whole Tumor (WT) and would need separate construction for TC and ET sub-regions, and the fallback-to-full-image mechanism activates in 5–7% of cases, indicating the prior does not yet cover all morphological variants. Extending the prior-guided strategy to 3D volumetric processing, multi-subregion priors, and other tumor types (e.g., metastases, meningiomas) are natural next steps that the authors acknowledge.
