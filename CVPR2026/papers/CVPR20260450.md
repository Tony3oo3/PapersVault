## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) process an identical number of patchified tokens in every block, making them computationally heavy despite operating on relatively few tokens (<1k in latent space). While global-local attention has been explored within individual attention layers, it yields only negligible efficiency gains and often degrades performance. The paper asks whether applying a coarse-to-fine patch hierarchy at the architectural level—rather than within each attention layer—can substantially reduce FLOPs while preserving generation quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260450/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model operates in the latent space of a VAE with 32×32×4 representations (1024 spatial tokens). A class index from 1000 ImageNet classes and a random Gaussian noise latent z_t are the inputs; the model predicts a velocity field for flow matching. The key structural detail is that the token sequence is not uniform: the first N−k transformer blocks receive only 64 tokens (patch size 4), while the final k blocks receive 256 tokens (patch size 2) after an upsampling step with a skip connection from the original p=2 patch embedding. The final output is a 256×256 RGB image decoded from the clean latent.

## Method

MPDiT replaces the isotropic DiT with a two-level patch hierarchy: early blocks (N−k) operate on 64 large-patch tokens to capture global context, then a lightweight Upsample Block (linear projection + pixel-unshuffle + skip connection + refinement linear layer) expands the sequence to 256 fine-grained tokens for the last k refinement blocks (k=4–6), reducing GFLOPs by up to 50% for the XL configuration. Two conditioning modules are redesigned: a Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) time embedding replaces the standard sinusoidal+MLP scheme, using three MixedFNO blocks on a 32-point 1D grid to capture smooth temporal dynamics, and a multi-token class embedding (m=16 learnable tokens prepended to the image sequence) replaces the single-token or AdaIN approach, providing richer class semantics. The time embedding is shared across all blocks (following PixArt-α), and time/class conditioning is decoupled—class information flows through prefix tokens rather than AdaIN modulation. Together, these changes yield roughly a 10-point FID improvement over the baseline DiT-B/2 at 80 epochs, with the XL variant reaching FID 2.05 (CFG) in only 240 epochs versus 1400 for SiT-XL/2.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256, MPDiT-XL achieves a non-CFG FID of 7.36 and a CFG FID of 2.05 (scale 1.4) after only 240 training epochs, while using just 59.30 GFLOPs — a 50% reduction compared to DiT-XL/2 (118.66 GFLOPs). At the same 80-epoch budget, MPDiT-B reaches FID 24.74 with 16.60 GFLOPs, outperforming SiT-B/2 (FID 34.84, 23.02 GFLOPs) and DiT-B/2 (FID 43.47, 23.02 GFLOPs). The sampling throughput of MPDiT-XL is more than 2× faster than DiT-XL/2, and the model can be trained on lower-memory GPUs with a batch size of 1024.

## Baselines & Comparisons

At 80 epochs, MPDiT-XL (FID 9.92, 59.30 GFLOPs) outperforms all transformer and convolutional baselines: SiT-XL/2 (FID 18.04), DiT-XL/2 (FID 19.47), DiG-XL/2 (FID 18.53), DiC-XL (FID 13.11), and DiCo-XL (FID 11.67), while using roughly half the GFLOPs of DiT-XL/2 and DiC-XL. At 240 epochs, MPDiT-XL achieves FID 7.36, beating DiG-XL/2 (FID 8.60) by 1.24 points and surpassing SiT-XL/2 and DiT-XL/2 (FID 9.35 and 9.62) which required 1400 epochs — a ~6× training budget. With CFG (scale 1.4), MPDiT-XL-G reaches FID 2.05 at 240 epochs, marginally beating DiG-XL/2-G (FID 2.07, same 240 epochs) and clearly outperforming SiT-XL/2-G (FID 2.15) and DiT-XL/2-G (FID 2.27) which needed 1400 epochs. The B-size variant similarly dominates: MPDiT-B (FID 24.74) beats DiCo-B (FID 27.20), DiC-B (FID 32.33), SiT-B/2 (FID 34.84), and DiT-B/2 (FID 43.47) at the same 80-epoch budget with the lowest GFLOPs (16.60). Overall, MPDiT consistently wins on both FID and computational efficiency across all compared architectures, with the largest margins against the original DiT and SiT baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

MPDiT extends the DiT architecture by replacing its isotropic token processing with a hierarchical multi-patch design: early blocks operate on coarse (p=4) tokens for global context, then an upsample module expands to fine (p=2) tokens for a small number of refinement blocks. This applies the global-local attention concept (from Swin, Focal Attention, GC Attention) at the architectural level rather than within individual attention layers, avoiding the repeated reshaping overhead and performance degradation that in-block global-local variants suffer. The conditioning modules draw from UViT's prefix class-token approach (extended to multiple tokens) and PixArt-α's shared time embedding, while the FNO time embedding repurposes Fourier Neural Operator layers from PDE solving to model continuous diffusion trajectories. The paper positions itself against linear-attention (SANA, LIT, DiG), state-space (Mamba-based), and convolution-based (DiCo, DiC) alternatives, arguing that a simpler architectural change to patch granularity achieves comparable or better efficiency without sacrificing attention quality.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 50% GFLOPs reduction for MPDiT-XL while matching or exceeding baseline FID at the same training budget is a practical efficiency win that lowers the barrier to training large diffusion transformers, and the finding that only 4–6 fine-resolution blocks are sufficient for local refinement is a useful design principle. The FNO time embedding and multi-token class embedding are simple, low-cost modifications that together yield ~10 FID improvement, suggesting that conditioning design in diffusion transformers has been underexplored relative to backbone architecture. However, validation is limited to ImageNet-256 class-conditional generation; the authors explicitly note that extension to text-to-image (SD3, Flux) and text-to-video (Sora-scale) models remains open and computationally demanding, which is where the efficiency gains would be most impactful.
