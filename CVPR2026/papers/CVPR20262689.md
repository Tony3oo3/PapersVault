## Problem

Existing motion-customized video generation methods rely on either semantic-level alignment (which overlooks the visual complexity and temporal dynamics of motion) or visual-level adaptation (which captures rigid, instance-specific patterns and limits subject generalization). Neither approach alone can simultaneously achieve precise motion reproduction, diverse subject transfer, and output diversity, leaving a gap for a unified framework that balances both semantic comprehension and visual fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262689/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of exemplar videos (typically 20 per motion category) demonstrating the target action, a text prompt structured as <subject, motion> (e.g., 'a lizard throws'), and optionally an input image for the I2V setting. During training, an auxiliary Subject Prior Video (SPV) dataset of diverse subject-motion pairs (e.g., 'a zebra walks') is also used to regularize subject generalization. The output is a temporally coherent video in which the prompted subject performs the customized motion, generated via the HunyuanVideo backbone on 8 H20 GPUs over 2,000 iterations per motion category.

## Method

SynMotion operates on two complementary levels. At the semantic level, a dual-embedding mechanism decomposes MLLM-generated text embeddings into subject and motion components via prompt-aware partitioning; learnable residual embeddings (e_mot^l initialized from the full motion phrase, e_sub^l randomly initialized) are attached through Zero-Conv layers, and an Embedding Refiner R fuses the two components in latent space before re-injection. At the visual level, lightweight LoRA adapters (rank r ≪ d) are injected into the Q, K, V projections of every attention layer across the frozen MM-DiT backbone (Text, Video, and Single-Stream DiT Blocks) to enhance motion fidelity and temporal coherence. The Embedding-Specific Training Strategy alternates between real exemplar videos (probability α=0.75, updating both embeddings) and SPV-generated videos (probability 1−α, freezing the motion embedding to regularize subject generalization), preventing semantic interference between the two embedding streams.

## Results

On MotionBench, SynMotion achieves 68.60% motion accuracy and 97.67% subject accuracy, substantially outperforming the best visual-level baseline MotionInversion (59.31% / 73.21%) and the best semantic-level baseline DreamBooth (37.56% / 69.76%). It also leads on dynamic degree (88.24% vs. 69.77% for DreamBooth), CLIP-T text-video alignment (0.322 vs. 0.299 for MotionDirector), and FVD-3DRN50 (212.05, marginally better than MotionInversion's 213.04). In the user study, SynMotion is preferred by 78.3% of raters for motion alignment, 81.9% for subject alignment, and 83.9% for overall video quality, compared to at most 8.1% for the next-best method (DMT).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SynMotion dominates all seven baselines on the two most critical metrics: motion accuracy (68.60% vs. 59.31% for MotionInversion, the strongest visual-level method) and subject accuracy (97.67% vs. 73.21% for MotionInversion). Visual-level methods (VMC, DMT, MotionDirector, MotionInversion) preserve the exemplar's spatial layout and subject morphology, yielding high motion consistency but very low subject accuracy (34–73%) and dynamic degree (3–21%), as they rigidly copy the reference scene. Semantic-level methods (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth) allow flexible subject recombination and achieve higher dynamic degree (47–70%) but fail to capture the target motion accurately (21–38% motion accuracy). In the user study, SynMotion's 78.3% motion-alignment preference dwarfs the next-best DMT at 8.1%, confirming a large perceptual gap. The only near-tie is FVD-3DRN50, where SynMotion (212.05) edges out MotionInversion (213.04) by a negligible margin, while all other baselines score 385–466.

## Relation to Prior Work

SynMotion positions itself at the intersection of two established lines of motion-customization research: semantic-level methods (Textual Inversion, DreamBooth, ADI, ReVersion) that learn concept embeddings but struggle with video's temporal complexity, and visual-level methods (VMC, DMT, Motion Inversion, Motion Director) that capture motion in feature space but overfit to instance-specific trajectories and preserve rigid scene layouts. The paper's core departure is arguing that neither paradigm alone suffices and proposing a hybrid that combines a dual-embedding semantic decomposition (subject vs. motion, built on HunyuanVideo's MLLM encoder) with parameter-efficient LoRA-style motion adapters in the MM-DiT backbone. The embedding-specific training strategy, which alternates between real exemplar videos and a constructed Subject Prior Video (SPV) dataset to prevent subject-motion interference, is a novel training recipe not seen in prior customization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Motion-customized video generation is a practically important capability (e.g., transferring a human handstand to a lizard), and SynMotion provides the first framework that simultaneously supports both T2V and I2V settings with strong subject generalization. The introduction of MotionBench (26 non-trivial actions × 20 exemplar videos) gives the community a standardized evaluation protocol that was previously absent. However, the evaluation relies heavily on VLM-based (QwenVL) yes/no judgments for motion and subject accuracy, which introduces a single-model bias and limits confidence in the large reported margins; the SPV dataset construction is also somewhat ad-hoc (common motions paired with animal categories), raising questions about whether the training strategy generalizes to more complex or multi-subject motion scenarios.
