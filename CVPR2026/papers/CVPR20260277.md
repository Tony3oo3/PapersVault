## Problem

Radar frequency maps are inherently anisotropic, multiscale, sparse, and noisy, yet conventional CNN and Transformer architectures designed for isotropic RGB imagery fail to account for these properties, leading to degraded segmentation performance. Existing radar semantic segmentation methods do not explicitly disentangle the directional components (range vs. Doppler/angle) embedded in the radar spectrum, leaving a gap in handling the fundamental physical structure of radar data.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 5 consecutive FMCW radar frames projected into three 2D views: Range-Doppler (256×64), Angle-Doppler (256×64), and Range-Angle (256×256), processed through separate encoder paths. It produces per-pixel semantic segmentation masks (4 classes: pedestrian, cyclist, car, background) for both the RD and RA views, trained with a combined weighted cross-entropy, Dice, and cross-view temporal coherence loss that enforces consistency between the two output views.

## Method

MARSS is an encoder-decoder framework embedding three radar-specific modules at different stages. In the encoder, RADE applies CBAM-style channel and spatial attention after a wavelet-based REM (Radar Enhancement Module) that decouples anisotropic features into LL/LH/HL/HH subbands via axial convolutions. At intermediate layers, RFAF performs multi-scale atrous fusion followed by a dual-branch region-attention stage that processes horizontal and vertical neighborhoods separately to capture directional patterns like Doppler trails and range streaks. In the decoder, RADM splits features into spatial and Doppler branches, each combining a Mamba-based Vision State Space Block (VSSB) for linear-complexity local interaction with axial self-attention for long-range dependency modeling along individual axes, then fuses the branches with a residual connection. This modular design targets noise suppression, multi-scale anisotropic feature isolation, and directional long-range modeling simultaneously, achieving 63.26% mIoU on RD and 46.97% mIoU on RA with only 9.3M parameters.

## Results

On the CARRADA dataset, MARSS achieves 63.26% mIoU and 75.18% mDice on the Range-Doppler (RD) view, and 46.97% mIoU and 58.78% mDice on the Range-Angle (RA) view, setting new state-of-the-art across all metrics. On the recalibrated CARRADA-RAC dataset, MARSS reaches 55.2% global mIoU, outperforming the strongest prior method (AdaPKCξ-Net^FiT) by 0.4%. The model uses only 9.3M parameters with 5 input frames, substantially fewer than T-RODNet (162.0M) while delivering superior accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MARSS outperforms all five compared baselines on every reported metric. Against the strongest prior method AdaPKCξ-Net^FiT (NeurIPS 2024), MARSS gains +1.16% mIoU on RD and +2.67% mIoU on RA, with the RA improvement being particularly notable given the anisotropy challenges in that view. Compared to TransRadar (WACV 2024), MARSS leads by +6.06% RD mIoU and +7.07% RA mIoU, demonstrating the advantage of explicit anisotropy-aware modules over adaptive-directional transformers. Against the classical U-Net baseline, MARSS achieves +7.86% RD mIoU and +14.17% RA mIoU, confirming that generic encoder-decoder architectures are poorly suited to radar frequency maps. RAMP-CNN, despite using 106.4M parameters and 9 frames, is surpassed by MARSS by +6.66% RD mIoU and +19.07% RA mIoU, highlighting the efficiency of the modular design. RSS-Net, the earliest radar-specific method, trails MARSS by a wide margin (+31.16% RD mIoU), underscoring the progress in radar-aware architecture design.

## Relation to Prior Work

MARSS extends the encoder-decoder paradigm for radar semantic segmentation (U-Net, RSS-Net, TransRadar) by embedding radar-specific inductive biases at each network stage. The RADE module adapts CBAM-style channel and spatial attention for noise-robust encoding, the RFAF module draws on multi-scale atrous fusion and region-level attention (inspired by YOLOv12's attention-centric design) to handle directional imbalance, and the RADM decoder introduces Mamba-based Vision State Space Blocks combined with axial self-attention—a combination previously explored in medical imaging (HCMA-UNet) but not in radar segmentation. The wavelet-transform-based REM module for anisotropic subband decomposition is a novel addition that explicitly decouples range and Doppler/angle axes, departing from prior methods (PeakConv, AdaPKC) that handle anisotropy implicitly through peak-receptive-field convolutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MARSS sets new state-of-the-art on both CARRADA and CARRADA-RAC with a compact 9.3M-parameter model, demonstrating that explicit modular handling of radar's anisotropy, sparsity, and multi-scale structure outperforms larger monolithic architectures (e.g., T-RODNet at 162M parameters). The ablation cleanly attributes gains to each module, providing a useful template for designing radar-specific components. However, the paper acknowledges limitations in generalization across diverse radar hardware and extreme weather, and the multi-module design increases structural complexity. Open questions include whether the Mamba-based decoder generalizes beyond FMCW radar to other frequency-domain sensing modalities, and whether model compression can preserve the anisotropy-aware benefits for real-time deployment.
