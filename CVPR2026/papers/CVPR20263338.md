## Problem

Human-centric multi-view video compression is bottlenecked by the dynamic human component: existing 4DGS-based methods encode per-frame Gaussian deformations or motion fields, ignoring the fact that articulated human motion lies in a low-dimensional parametric space defined by pose and shape. This leads to high bitrates that scale with both frame count and view count, making long, densely captured sequences impractical for immersive applications.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multi-view video sequence of a single human in a static scene (e.g., 54 surrounding cameras on AvatarRex, ~2000 frames; or semi-surrounding cameras on ENeRF-Outdoor, ~600 frames) and produces a three-part bitstream: a quantized 3D Gaussian Splatting background model, 3–5 compressed key views (encoded via VTM), and per-frame SMPL parameters (72-dim pose, 10-dim shape, SE(3) root). At decode time, these drive a generative renderer that synthesizes arbitrary-viewpoint video frames. Because only the SMPL stream scales with sequence length, bits-per-pixel decreases monotonically as duration or view density increases.

## Method

GauMVC decouples the scene into a Static Batch and a Dynamic Batch. The static branch performs spatio-temporal background extraction (temporal fusion of per-frame segmentation masks to recover occluded regions), initializes 3D Gaussians via SfM on the fused background, filters out Gaussians projecting into permanently occluded pixels, optimizes under a visibility-mask-restricted loss, and compresses via attribute-adaptive quantization plus Huffman coding. The dynamic branch compresses motion as SMPL parameters (initialized from the least-occluded view and refined multi-view), selects a minimal set of key views by partitioning the body surface into K=5 anatomical regions and assigning each region its highest-visibility-confidence view, then fuses region-specific Gaussian avatars generated by LHM from each key view using distance-weighted blending at boundaries. At render time, the canonical fused avatar is deformed per-frame via Linear Blend Skinning with the decoded SMPL pose and alpha-composited over the static Gaussian background, enabling high-fidelity multi-view synthesis at ultra-low bitrate without any per-frame Gaussian transmission.

## Results

On ENeRF-Outdoor (Actor05), GauMVC achieves the best perceptual quality across all metrics—VIFp 0.3099, DSS 0.6413, LPIPS 0.2785, DISTS 0.0862, and PieAPP 0.8428—while using the lowest bitrate (0.0042 BPP), less than half of MV-HEVC (0.0082) and roughly 1/6 of 4DGS baselines (0.025–0.285 BPP). On AvatarRex, it attains the best face distance (0.2205) and expression similarity (0.9448) at 0.0012 BPP. The method trades a small PSNR gap (25.39 dB vs. E-D3DGS's 27.03 dB) for substantially better perceptual fidelity and a 67× bitrate reduction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GauMVC outperforms all five baselines on every perceptual metric (VIFp, DSS, LPIPS, DISTS, PieAPP) in Table 1, with the largest margins over HUST-4DGS (e.g., LPIPS 0.2785 vs. 0.4469) and SpaceGaussians (DISTS 0.0862 vs. 0.1337). It also achieves the lowest BPP (0.0042), roughly 2× lower than MV-HEVC (0.0082) and 6–68× lower than the 4DGS methods (0.025–0.285 BPP). On pixel-level metrics, E-D3DGS edges out GauMVC on PSNR (27.03 vs. 25.39 dB) and SSIM (0.9380 vs. 0.9341), but the authors argue these metrics understate perceptual quality for generative compression. On AvatarRex (Table 2), GauMVC achieves the best face distance (0.2205 vs. 0.2666 for MV-HEVC, 0.88 for 4DGS methods) and expression similarity (0.9448 vs. 0.6974 for MV-HEVC) at the lowest BPP (0.0012), confirming superior identity preservation. Overall, the method wins decisively on perceptual quality and bitrate, at the cost of a modest PSNR/SSIM gap versus the strongest 4DGS baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on 3D Gaussian Splatting for the static background representation, the SMPL parametric body model for motion encoding, and LHM for single-view Gaussian avatar generation, but departs fundamentally from prior dynamic 3DGS compression methods (4DGS, E-D3DGS, HUST-4DGS, ADC-GS) that treat human motion as generic per-frame deformations or motion fields. Where those approaches encode high-dimensional temporal residuals, GauMVC replaces them with a low-dimensional SMPL parameter stream plus a small set of region-wise key views, shifting the compression paradigm from signal coding to semantics-driven synthesis. The spatio-temporal background separation via visibility-mask fusion is a practical extension of prior static/dynamic decomposition work, but the occlusion-aware Gaussian filtering and mask-guided optimization are specific to this pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The sublinear bitrate growth with sequence length and viewpoint count (vs. linear for MV-HEVC) is a structurally important property for long, densely captured immersive sequences, and the semantic editing capability (identity swap, motion retargeting at the bitstream level) opens practical applications in virtual production. However, the framework is currently limited to single-human scenes with clean foreground/background separation, and SMPL cannot capture non-rigid effects like loose clothing or human-object interactions. Extending to multi-person scenes, integrating instance-level segmentation, and incorporating more expressive deformation models are clear open directions that would determine whether this paradigm scales beyond controlled studio captures.
