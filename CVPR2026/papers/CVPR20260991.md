## Problem

Fine-tuning pretrained Vision-Language Models into Vision-Language-Action models causes catastrophic forgetting of geometric and semantic priors, degrading out-of-distribution generalization. Existing remedies—freezing modules or applying uniform projection-based regularization—either impose brittle inductive biases or treat all layers identically, ignoring that visual encoders and language layers play fundamentally different roles in preserving generalization versus adapting to the action space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260991/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VLA policy consumes an RGB observation, a natural-language instruction, and a proprioceptive state vector, and outputs either a continuous low-level control vector or a sequence of discrete action tokens depending on the backbone. MAPS itself does not alter the model's I/O interface; it operates entirely within the optimization loop, constraining how far each parameter group drifts from its pretrained VLM initialization during action fine-tuning on robot demonstration data.

## Method

MAPS extends Selective Projection Decay (SPD) by replacing its single global proximity hyperparameter λ with a layer-wise linear schedule that decays from λ_max on early visual layers (DINOv2) to 0 on late language layers, encoding the empirically discovered hierarchy DINOv2 > SigLIP > early language > late language. At each gradient step, when the gradient-displacement correlation c_t < 0 signals that the update would erode pretrained structure, MAPS projects the updated weights back toward θ_0 with strength λ_k · r_t, where r_t is the SPD deviation ratio. Modules initialized from scratch (action head, proprioceptive projector) receive λ = 0 and undergo unconstrained fine-tuning. The result is a plug-and-play regularization that introduces no extra parameters, data, or auxiliary networks, and can be dropped into any existing VLA architecture without modification.

## Results

On the Franka Emika Panda real-robot platform, MAPS boosts MiniVLA-OFT's average ID success from 40.0% to 72.5% (+32.5%) and OOD success from 22.5% to 52.5% (+30.0%). On SimplerEnv, MAPS applied to MiniVLA-OFT raises average OOD success from 8.9% to 35.8% (+26.9%) while matching ID performance (30.0% vs. 13.5%). On LIBERO, MAPS consistently improves OOD across all four backbones (MiniVLA-VQ: 0.0%→4.75%, MiniVLA-OFT: 4.75%→7.0%, VLA-Adapter: 2.5%→4.75%, OpenVLA-OFT: 5.0%→5.5%), with ID gains of up to +5% (VLA-Adapter). On CALVIN (ABC→D), MAPS adds +0.7 average sequence length and ~15% success-rate improvement on 2–5 task horizons for MiniVLA-OFT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAPS consistently improves OOD generalization over its direct backbone baselines: MiniVLA-OFT gains +26.9% OOD on SimplerEnv and +2.25% on LIBERO; OpenVLA-OFT gains +8.4% OOD on SimplerEnv and +0.5% on LIBERO; VLA-Adapter gains +2.25% OOD and +5% ID on LIBERO; MiniVLA-VQ gains +1.5% OOD on SimplerEnv and +4.75% OOD on LIBERO. Against external baselines on SimplerEnv, MAPS-augmented MiniVLA-OFT (35.8% OOD) surpasses RT-1-X (3.4%) and Octo (10.6%) by large margins, though it remains below π₀ (40.9%) which benefits from substantially more pretraining data. On LIBERO, MAPS-augmented MiniVLA-VQ (82.0% ID) exceeds OpenVLA (62.0% ID) and all prior MiniVLA variants, while MAPS-augmented VLA-Adapter (4.75% avg OOD) outperforms SpatialVLA (0.5% avg OOD) by nearly 10×. On CALVIN, MAPS improves MiniVLA-OFT by ~15% on multi-task success rates and OpenVLA-OFT by ~3%, confirming gains scale with backbone quality. ID performance is largely preserved (within ±2%) across all backbones, with the notable exception of OpenVLA-OFT on LIBERO where ID drops 2% while OOD improves.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAPS extends the projection-based robust fine-tuning lineage (L2-SP → TPGM → Selective Projection Decay) by replacing SPD's single global proximity hyperparameter with a linear, module-wise schedule that decays from strong constraint on early visual layers (DINOv2) to no constraint on late language layers. This departs from prior VLA generalization work that relies on architectural modifications (dual encoders, frozen cross-attention, weight interpolation) by achieving selective preservation purely through a training-time scheduling rule with zero additional parameters. The paper's systematic freezing ablation (Sec. 4.2) provides the empirical hierarchy—DINOv2 > SigLIP > Early language > Late language—that motivates the schedule, a quantification the authors note has been widely assumed but rarely measured.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAPS offers a drop-in, parameter-free regularization strategy that consistently improves OOD generalization across five VLA backbones and four simulation benchmarks plus a real Franka robot, making it immediately adoptable by the VLA community without architectural changes. The empirical hierarchy finding is broadly useful: it suggests that future VLA pretraining and scaling efforts should treat visual and language components asymmetrically when preserving pretrained priors. Open questions include whether the linear schedule is optimal (the paper shows it beats constant/cosine but does not explore learned or data-driven schedules), whether the hierarchy holds for non-OpenVLA-family backbones with different visual encoders, and whether the modest LIBERO gains (0.5–4.75%) reflect a ceiling for proximity-based methods on harder OOD splits.
