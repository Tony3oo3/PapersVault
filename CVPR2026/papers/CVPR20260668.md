## Problem

Current Spatio-Temporal Video Grounding (STVG) benchmarks are too narrow in scope—focusing on single categories (HC-STVG), simple scenarios (VidSTG), or limited object diversity (ST-Align)—causing models to develop category bias, oversimplified reasoning, and poor robustness to complex linguistic queries. This gap between benchmark simplicity and real-world demands (uncommon objects, multiple similar targets, nested spatial relations) prevents reliable evaluation of STVG-MLLMs for practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260668/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The task takes an untrimmed video clip (3–140 s, average 18.2 s) and a natural-language query (average 16.2 words, sentence-level NEI 0.918) as input, and outputs a spatio-temporal tube: a temporal segment [T_start, T_end] with per-frame bounding boxes at native FPS. Queries include rich spatial relations, diverse action predicates, and deep syntactic structures (e.g., 'the right man in red clothes among three people'), and the benchmark spans 81 balanced object categories to stress-test generalization beyond person-centric or common-object settings.

## Method

The paper's core method, PG-TAF, is a training-free two-stage framework that decouples STVG into (i) MLLM-guided temporal grounding using Qwen3-VL-8B to identify the relevant temporal segment via holistic video understanding, and (ii) agent-based spatial propagation within that segment, where a composite scoring mechanism (α·S_seg from EVF-SAM + β·S_align from AlphaCLIP, α=0.6, β=0.4) selects top-K reference frames and a pixel-level tracker propagates object masks to produce the final tube. This is complemented by the FBR annotation pipeline (multi-point bi-directional tracking with IoU-based fusion voting and Kalman-filter gap recovery) for generating high-quality ground-truth tubes, and the DeepSTG evaluation framework (CMA, FCI, VSBI, NEI) for systematically quantifying dataset quality across annotation fidelity, visual complexity, linguistic balance, and distributional uniformity.

## Results

PG-TAF achieves 49.2% m_tIoU and 36.2% m_vIoU on OmniGround, outperforming all evaluated MLLMs (best: Qwen2.5-VL at 36.6%/23.2%) and even surpassing task-specific models like CG-STVG (47.5%/30.4%) in a fully training-free manner. Across four benchmarks, PG-TAF attains an average of 49.6% m_tIoU and 28.2% m_vIoU, with the largest gains on OmniGround where it improves m_tIoU by 25.6% and m_vIoU by 35.6% over the strongest MLLM baseline. On HC-STVG, PG-TAF reaches 56.2% m_tIoU, narrowly trailing SpaceVLLM (58.0%) but matching its m_vIoU (34.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PG-TAF decisively outperforms LLaVA-ST across all four benchmarks, with the largest gap on OmniGround (49.2% vs. 19.7% m_tIoU, a 2.5× improvement) and HC-STVG (56.2% vs. 21.2%). Against Qwen2.5-VL, PG-TAF wins on every benchmark, most dramatically on VidSTG-Declarative (48.4% vs. 16.8% m_tIoU) and OmniGround (49.2% vs. 36.6%). Compared to VideoMolmo, PG-TAF leads on all metrics, with a 19.0-point m_tIoU advantage on OmniGround (49.2% vs. 30.2%). The closest competitor is SpaceVLLM, which edges out PG-TAF on HC-STVG m_tIoU (58.0% vs. 56.2%) and VidSTG-Interrogative (48.5% vs. 46.4% m_tIoU), but PG-TAF matches its HC-STVG m_vIoU (34.0%) and wins on VidSTG-Declarative (48.4% vs. 47.7% m_tIoU); notably, SpaceVLLM has no OmniGround results, while PG-TAF achieves 49.2%/36.2% there. Against the task-specific baselines CG-STVG and TA-STVG (reported only on OmniGround in Table 2), PG-TAF surpasses both in m_tIoU (49.2% vs. 47.5% and 44.6%) and m_vIoU (36.2% vs. 30.4% and 28.0%), all without any task-specific training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of STVG benchmarking and MLLM-based video grounding, identifying that existing benchmarks (HC-STVG, VidSTG, ST-Align) are too narrow in category coverage and scenario complexity to reflect real-world demands. Rather than building on a single prior method, PG-TAF repurposes the complementary strengths of two model families—MLLMs for holistic temporal reasoning and specialized pixel-level trackers for fine-grained spatial propagation—into a training-free two-stage pipeline. The DeepSTG evaluation framework departs from prior dataset assessments that relied on superficial statistics (clip counts, duration) by introducing multi-dimensional automated metrics (CMA, FCI, VSBI, NEI) grounded in task-aware design principles.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniGround and DeepSTG together provide the STVG community with both a more realistic evaluation target and a principled way to diagnose why existing benchmarks underrepresent real-world difficulty, which should redirect future dataset construction and model training. The finding that MLLMs exhibit better linguistic robustness but worse spatial precision than task-specific models—revealing a fundamental reasoning-localization trade-off—suggests that hybrid architectures like PG-TAF may be a productive direction, though the training-free approach's ceiling remains unclear. Open questions include whether the 81-category benchmark is sufficiently large for robust generalization testing, whether the FBR annotation pipeline scales to much larger collections, and whether the decoupling strategy transfers to other video grounding variants (e.g., multi-object or egocentric settings).
