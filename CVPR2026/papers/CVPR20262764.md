## Problem

Current MLLMs excel at instance-level pointing but fail at part-level pointing (e.g., the keyboard of a laptop), because parts are fine-grained and their boundaries are often ambiguous. This paper asks whether a training-free, few-shot mechanism can bridge this gap without post-training the MLLM on part annotations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262764/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target image, a text query naming a part (e.g., 'the mug's handle'), and K support images each annotated with a single 2D point (not a full mask). It outputs a single 2D point in the target image's coordinate system. DINOv3 operates at 1024×1024 resolution for patch-level features, while the MLLM's attention maps are at a coarser token grid; the two are aligned via bilinear upsampling before fusion. Unlike segmentation baselines that consume full support masks, POP requires only one point per support example.

## Method

POP fuses two complementary localization signals in a fully training-free pipeline. First, it extracts language-guided score maps from the top-3 localization heads of a frozen MLLM, which provide semantic grounding and referring ability (disambiguating which object among similar ones). Second, it computes visual semantic correspondences using DINOv3 patch features via a bidirectional strategy: forward similarity compares the support patch containing the labeled point against all target patches, while backward similarity, for each target patch, finds its nearest support patch and measures that patch's similarity to the labeled one; the two are combined by element-wise multiplication. The final score map is the element-wise product of the upsampled text map and the visual correspondence map, and the predicted point is the center of the highest-scoring patch after a 2× bilinear upsampling to reduce quantization error. This plug-and-play design requires no gradient updates and works identically for pointing-capable and non-pointing-capable MLLMs.

## Results

On average across PACO-LVIS, InstructPart, and PartImageNet++, POP improves pointing-capable MLLMs by up to 8.9 accuracy points in the 1-shot setting (Qwen2.5-VL: 51.6/87.9/80.1; Ovis2.5: 53.0/88.5/81.7; Molmo: 55.8/90.9/84.1) and up to 16.4 points in the 3-shot setting. For non-pointing-capable MLLMs, POP achieves gains of 25.3 points (InternVL-3-8B) and 30.9 points (Kimi-VL-A3B) with a single shot, reaching performance comparable to zero-shot pointing-capable models. The metric is binary accuracy: a predicted point scores 1 if it falls inside the ground-truth part mask, 0 otherwise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

POP outperforms all zero-shot segmentation baselines (X-Decoder, SEEM, LISA-7B, VL-Part) and GPT-4.1 by large margins in both 1-shot and 3-shot settings; for example, on PACO, POP+Molmo reaches 55.8 (1-shot) vs. VL-Part's 38.3 and GPT-4.1's 19.8. Against few-shot segmentation methods, POP surpasses Matcher (16.6/19.8 on PACO) and GF-SAM (33.3/40.5 on PACO) by 18–42 points, while using only a single support point rather than full masks. Notably, in-context learning (ICL) with multiple images often degrades pointing-capable MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL drops from 47.7 to 14.1 on PACO), whereas POP consistently improves over the 0-shot baseline. On non-pointing-capable MLLMs (InternVL-3-8B, Kimi-VL-A3B), POP enables competitive pointing from scratch, with Kimi-VL-A3B reaching 49.7/90.3/79.4 (1-shot) versus its 0-shot attention-only scores of 24.9/57.2/44.6. The purely visual DINOv3 baseline (no text) trails POP on PACO (41.5 vs. 55.8 for Molmo), confirming that language-guided attention is essential for disambiguating among similar objects in complex scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: (1) attention-based visual grounding in frozen MLLMs, where Kang et al. [13] identified 'localization heads' whose text-to-image attention maps can be repurposed for pointing without any fine-tuning, and (2) DINO-based visual semantic correspondence, where Matcher [19] and GF-SAM [37] demonstrated that self-supervised patch features enable training-free few-shot segmentation via bidirectional patch matching. POP's conceptual contribution is the observation that these two signals are complementary for the harder part-level pointing task—MLLM attention provides semantic referring ability but coarse localization, while DINOv3 correspondences provide fine-grained spatial precision but lack disambiguation across similar objects—and that their element-wise product yields a simple, training-free fusion. The paper departs from prior pointing work (Molmo, Qwen2.5-VL, Ovis2.5) by targeting part-level rather than instance-level grounding, and from prior few-shot segmentation by reducing the support signal from full masks to a single point.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, plug-and-play nature of POP makes it immediately applicable to any MLLM without retraining, which is practically significant given the rapid proliferation of new MLLM architectures. The finding that non-pointing MLLMs (InternVL3, Kimi-VL) gain 25–31 points with a single exemplar suggests that general-purpose MLLMs already encode useful spatial-semantic information in their attention, and that the bottleneck for part-level grounding is not model capacity but the absence of a fine-grained localization mechanism. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D pointing or video, how it behaves when support and target images differ substantially in viewpoint or occlusion, and whether the reliance on DINOv3's part-level feature quality limits generalization to novel object categories. The observation that in-context learning (ICL) can degrade pointing performance is a useful caution for practitioners assuming multi-image context always helps.
