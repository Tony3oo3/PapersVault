## Problem

Existing infrared small target detection methods rely on target-centered feature learning, but infrared small targets are inherently dim and lack distinct shapes or textures, making them difficult to learn directly. This causes detectors to latch onto background confounders (bright cloud edges, sensor noise) rather than true targets, producing false alarms. The paper reformulates moving ISTD as an anomaly-discovery task in backgrounds, where targets are treated as deviations from learned background normality, and introduces causal learning to distinguish true anomalies from spurious ones.

## Input / Output

The method takes a clip of 5 consecutive 512×512 infrared frames as input, processed through a shared CSPDarknet backbone with FPN to extract multi-scale features. It produces bounding boxes for small targets in the keyframe (last frame) via a YOLOX-based detection head, along with intermediate anomaly maps (appearance anomalies, reconstructed temporal anomalies, and final causal anomalies) that provide hierarchical supervision. Training is fully supervised with standard detection losses (sigmoid focal loss for classification, NWD loss for localization, and probability loss for objectness).

## Method

CHAL comprises three core components operating on multi-frame features. First, Spatio-Temporal Neural Fields (SNF) model background evolution by encoding scene context through a disentangled scene encoder (spatial appearance + dynamic motion branches) and constructing 3D spatio-temporal coordinates with Fourier position encoding, then generating counterfactual background predictions via a neural field decoder. Second, Hierarchical Anomaly-Aware Learning (HAL) decomposes anomaly discovery into two stages: mining appearance anomalies via cosine similarity between frame features and predicted backgrounds, then verifying them through motion consistency using a spatiotemporal Swin Transformer with shifted-window self-attention and temporal-weighted aggregation. Third, Causal Relation Guiding (CRG) implements a differentiable backdoor adjustment: it uses normalized anomaly scores as a learned proxy for the confounder Z, applies a nonlinear gating filter with learnable scaling factors to stratify features into two causal strata, and multiplies the appearance-attended features by (1 + anomaly scores) to block the spurious confounding path F←Z→Y while preserving the true causal chain T→F→Y.

## Results

CHAL achieves the highest mAP50 across all three infrared benchmarks: 54.28% on DAUB-H, 75.25% on NUDT-MIRSDT, and 71.37% on IRDST-R. It also secures the best F1 scores (74.15%, 87.41%, and 84.76% respectively) while maintaining a moderate parameter count of 15.69M. On the visible-light RsCarData dataset, CHAL reaches 57.37% mAP50 and 76.14% F1, surpassing the best multi-frame baseline STF by 4.96% in mAP50. General anomaly detection methods (DiffusionAD, INPFormer) fail to converge on NUDT-MIRSDT and IRDST-R, confirming their inapplicability to infrared scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHAL outperforms all 17 compared methods on mAP50 and F1 across all three infrared datasets. On NUDT-MIRSDT, it surpasses the strongest multi-frame baseline Tridos by 2.24% in mAP50 (75.25% vs. 73.01%) and 1.54% in F1 (87.41% vs. 85.87%), while on DAUB-H it leads SSTNet by 2.03% in mAP50 (54.28% vs. 52.25%). The only metric where CHAL trails is Precision on NUDT-MIRSDT (86.35% vs. ADSUNet's 92.50%), but ADSUNet achieves this by heavily sacrificing Recall (52.12%), yielding a much lower F1 (66.67% vs. 87.41%). General anomaly detection methods are decisively inferior: DiffusionAD scores only 19.50% mAP50 on DAUB-H and fails to converge on the other two datasets, while INPFormer achieves a near-zero 1.31% mAP50. On the visible-light RsCarData benchmark, CHAL beats the best multi-frame method STF by 4.96% in mAP50 (57.37% vs. 52.41%) and 4.41% in F1 (76.14% vs. 71.73%), demonstrating cross-modality generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHAL departs from the dominant target-centered paradigm in moving infrared small target detection (MISTD), where methods like ISNet, SSTNet, Tridos, and DTUM directly learn discriminative target features from cluttered backgrounds. Instead, it repurposes three conceptually distinct ideas—spatio-temporal neural fields (from generative scene modeling), hierarchical anomaly detection (from industrial/video AD literature), and backdoor adjustment (from causal inference)—into a unified background-centered framework where targets are treated as deviations from learned normality. The paper does not build on a single prior architecture but rather recombines components from neural fields, Swin Transformers, and causal debiasing (UFR, CRCL) in a way not previously applied to ISTD.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The background-centered reformulation offers a principled alternative to the target-centered approach that struggles with dim, shapeless infrared targets, and the causal framing of confounders (cloud edges, sensor noise) provides a theoretically grounded mechanism for suppressing false alarms. However, the practical impact is tempered by the model's moderate parameter count (15.69M) and high computational cost (137.04 GFLOPs), and the causal intervention reduces to a relatively simple feature-scaling operation whose gains over standard attention (CBAM, self-attention) are meaningful but not transformative. Open questions include whether the framework can be made significantly lighter-weight, whether the causal framing generalizes beyond the specific confounder types studied, and whether the anomaly-detection reformulation truly captures the physics of infrared target-background separation or merely provides a convenient inductive bias.
