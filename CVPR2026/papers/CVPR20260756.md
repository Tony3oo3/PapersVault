## Problem

Existing think-with-image methods directly apply standard GRPO to multi-turn visual reasoning, but this causes training instability and excessive redundant tool calls at inference. The instability stems from two compounding issues: tool calls spike token entropy (expanding the action space) while GRPO still permits only a single rollout branch per node, and terminal-only reward supervision propagates high-variance gradients back to early reasoning steps. The paper provides formal variance proofs for both effects and asks how to redesign the rollout sampling strategy to match the hierarchical, multi-turn nature of visual tool-calling.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image and a text question, then iteratively produces a sequence of (thought, crop/zoom action, image-crop observation) triples over up to five turns before emitting a final text answer. Tool-return payloads (the cropped image tokens) are appended to the conversation history but are masked out of the policy-gradient loss so they neither contribute to nor distort the learning signal. The output is thus a structured multi-turn dialogue interleaving internal reasoning, spatial tool invocations, and a terminal answer.

## Method

VisionLeaf replaces GRPO's independent root-to-leaf rollouts with an entropy-guided, leaf-first tree expansion: it maintains a frontier of partial trajectories, computes the next-token entropy at each active node, and spawns B new branches from the highest-entropy node, repeating until a fixed rollout budget N is met. Because sibling branches share long prefixes, the resulting trajectories exhibit substantial prefix overlap, which the paper proves (via a law-of-total-variance argument) strictly reduces the conditional variance of advantage estimates compared to independent rollouts from the root. Training uses a modified GRPO objective with a binary mask that excludes tool-return tokens from the clipped surrogate, and a composite reward combining accuracy (λ₁=0.8), format compliance (λ₂=−0.2), and a tool-calling bonus (λ₃=1.2) granted only when a successful tool invocation contributes to a correct answer. Built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and trained on the same 47k-sample DeepEyes dataset, VisionLeaf achieves ~4–5% accuracy gains on VStar and HR-Bench while halving the average number of inference tool calls.

## Results

On VStar, VisionLeaf (7B) achieves 84.8% overall accuracy, surpassing the 55B PaLI-X-VPD model (76.6%) and the directly comparable DeepEyes (80.6%) by 4.2 percentage points. On HR-Bench 8K, it reaches 72.1% versus DeepEyes' 68.8% (+3.3%), and on MME-RealWorld it scores 54.0% versus 51.9%. Notably, VisionLeaf also reduces the average number of tool-calling steps by nearly half compared to DeepEyes, demonstrating simultaneous gains in accuracy and inference efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisionLeaf is trained on the same DeepEyes-Datasets-47k with the same Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model, making DeepEyes the most direct comparison: VisionLeaf wins on every reported benchmark (VStar +4.2%, MME-RealWorld +2.1%, HR-Bench 4K +2.3%, HR-Bench 8K +3.3%) while using roughly half as many tool calls at inference. Against Pixel Reasoner (7B), VisionLeaf leads on VStar overall (84.8% vs. 84.3%) and HR-Bench 8K (72.1% vs. 66.1%), though Pixel Reasoner's VStar sub-scores are not reported. The 55B PaLI-X-VPD model, despite being ~8× larger, is outperformed on VStar overall (76.6% vs. 84.8%). Among non-agentic baselines, VisionLeaf surpasses LLaVA-OV (75.4%), Qwen2.5-VL (74.4%), SEAL (75.4%), and DyFo (81.2%) on VStar overall. The only method that exceeds VisionLeaf on VStar is OpenAI's o3 (95.7%), which is a proprietary system not directly comparable in training setup.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) as the RL backbone, following the approach taken by DeepEyes and Pixel Reasoner for training think-with-image VLMs. Its key departure is identifying that vanilla GRPO's single-path, root-to-leaf rollout is fundamentally mismatched with the multi-turn, tool-calling structure of visual reasoning, where each tool call injects new information and spikes token entropy. The entropy-guided node selection draws on the high-entropy token insight from Wang et al. [39], while the tree-based expansion from leaf nodes (rather than the root) is a novel reorganization of the sampling process. The theoretical variance analysis in Sec. 5 formalizes why terminal-only supervision combined with entropy-inflating tool calls destabilizes early-turn gradients under standard GRPO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, well-evidenced diagnosis (entropy spikes after tool calls → exploration-capacity mismatch → training instability) that explains why naively applying LLM RL recipes to agentic visual reasoning fails, and offers a targeted fix that improves accuracy by ~4% while halving inference tool calls without any model or data changes. This is practically significant for anyone deploying think-with-image systems where latency and token cost matter. Open questions include whether the entropy-guided tree strategy generalizes to larger models or other multi-turn agentic settings (e.g., code execution, web navigation), whether the fixed 5-turn cap and branching factor B are optimal, and whether the prefix-sharing efficiency gain holds at scale with more aggressive tree structures.
