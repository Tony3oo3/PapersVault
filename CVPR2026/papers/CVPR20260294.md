## Problem

Unseen 6D object pose estimation has traditionally relied on explicit feature matching between a query image and object model or template views, followed by geometric solvers like PnP. This match-then-localize or match-then-refine pipeline is fragile when correspondences are unreliable, and existing multi-view foundation models (e.g., VGGT) lack explicit 3D object geometry and are sensitive to appearance gaps between CAD renderings and real images. The paper asks whether pose can be predicted end-to-end by a multi-view network that directly reasons over the query image and template views without any explicit matching step.

## Input / Output

The method takes a segmented RGB query image and a CAD mesh as input. The mesh is rendered into N template views (default 10, sampled via farthest-point sampling on a sphere) with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics, along with corresponding point maps in world coordinates and per-point geometry features from a point cloud network. The query image receives a learnable camera token (since its pose is unknown), while template views use camera tokens computed from known poses. The output is a single 6D object-to-camera transformation matrix, decoded from the query camera token via a lightweight head.

## Method

PoseGAM adapts the VGGT alternating inter/intra-frame self-attention architecture, using DINOv2 to extract image tokens and a lightweight encoder for camera tokens. Object geometry is injected through two complementary channels: (1) explicit point maps rendered from the mesh and processed by a small CNN, and (2) per-point features from PointTransformer v3 that are spatially reorganized into view-map format before encoding. Both geometry channels are fused via cross-attention (multi-view tokens as queries, geometry tokens as key-value) rather than direct addition, which the authors show causes training collapse due to modality gap with the pretrained image distribution. The network is initialized from VGGT pretrained weights and trained on a 190k-object synthetic dataset spanning four rendering scenarios (centric, uncentric, HDR-lit, and FLUX-edited appearances) to build robustness to lighting and appearance variation.

## Results

PoseGAM achieves an average AR of 41.1 across five BOP benchmark datasets (LM-O, T-LESS, TUD-L, IC-BIN, YCB-V), outperforming the previous best method RayPose (39.1) by 5.1% in relative terms. The largest single-dataset gain is on TUD-L, where PoseGAM reaches 56.8 AR versus 48.3 for RayPose, a 17.6% relative improvement attributed to TUD-L's single-object, low-occlusion setting closely matching the synthetic training distribution. On LM-O, PoseGAM scores 43.0 AR, surpassing RayPose (42.1) and FoundPose (39.6). The adapted VGGT baseline, which lacks explicit geometry injection, drops to 16.3 average AR, confirming that multi-view foundation models alone are insufficient for object pose estimation without geometry-aware modifications.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PoseGAM outperforms all eight baselines on average AR (41.1 vs. 39.1 for the runner-up RayPose). Against the strongest matching-based competitor FoundPose (37.8 avg), PoseGAM gains 3.3 points on average, with the largest margin on TUD-L (+10.1) and LM-O (+3.4). Against the feedforward diffusion-based RayPose (39.1 avg), PoseGAM wins on four of five datasets (LM-O +0.9, TUD-L +8.5, IC-BIN +2.5, YCB-V +1.2) but loses on T-LESS (34.1 vs. 36.9), where RayPose's ray-bundling diffusion strategy appears more effective for texture-less objects. The older matching-based methods (GigaPose 27.9, GenFlow 24.2, MegaPose 21.9) are substantially outperformed, with gaps of 13–20 AR points. The adapted VGGT baseline (16.3 avg) confirms that multi-view foundation models without explicit geometry injection and without domain-specific fine-tuning are poorly suited to object pose estimation, motivating PoseGAM's geometry-aware design.

## Relation to Prior Work

PoseGAM departs from the dominant match-then-localize / match-then-refine paradigm in unseen 6D pose estimation (MegaPose, GigaPose, FoundPose, RayPose) by replacing explicit feature correspondence and PnP solving with an end-to-end multi-view transformer that directly regresses pose. Architecturally, it inherits the alternating inter/intra-frame attention design of VGGT and the relative-geometry formulation of DUSt3R/MASt3R, but augments these purely visual pipelines with two geometry channels: explicit point maps and learned per-point features from PointTransformer v3, injected via cross-attention rather than direct addition. The large-scale synthetic dataset (190k+ objects with four rendering scenarios) extends prior pose-estimation training data by explicitly targeting appearance and lighting domain gaps between CAD renderings and real images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the explicit matching bottleneck, PoseGAM offers a cleaner and potentially more scalable path to unseen-object pose estimation, and the 5.1% average AR gain (up to 17.6% on TUD-L) over strong matching-based baselines validates the multi-view-reasoning formulation. The practical finding that geometry features must be projected into view-map format before fusion—rather than injected as raw sequential tokens—provides a reusable design principle for combining 3D geometry with image-based transformers. Open questions remain around articulated/deformable objects, transparent or reflective materials, and whether the approach generalizes to multi-object or cluttered scenes beyond the single-object regime of the current benchmarks.
