## Problem

Existing audio-to-image models generate images of sound sources (e.g., a bird for a bird call) rather than the surrounding geographic environment, and they rely on generic datasets lacking geo-contextual information. No prior method incorporates scene-level geographic context or evaluates whether generated landscapes are geographically coherent with the input soundscape, limiting practical utility in geography, urban planning, and environmental design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262982/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a 10-second environmental soundscape clip (encoded via ImageBind-Huge into a shared latent embedding) and an optional text scene prompt (e.g., 'park', 'beach', 'street') as conditioning signals, and produces a photorealistic landscape image of the geographic environment. Generation occurs in the latent space of a pretrained Stable Diffusion VAE, and the scene prompt provides user-controllable geographic context to disambiguate environments that share similar acoustic signatures (e.g., a bird call in a rural field vs. an urban park).

## Method

SounDiT is a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) model that injects geo-contextual information through three novel modules within each block: (1) a Mixture-of-Experts Soundscape Conditioning module where M experts share soundscape-derived K/V projections but maintain expert-specific low-rank queries, with routing weights computed via temperature-scaled dot products between an audio summary and learnable prototypes augmented by timestep embeddings; (2) a Scene Low-Rank Content Mixer (SLRCM) that applies a compact, diagonally gated rank-r residual transform parameterized by the scene embedding to modulate token features; and (3) a Scene AdaLN (S-AdaLN) that extends AdaLN-Zero by deriving scale-shift parameters from both timestep and scene embeddings via learnable bounded mixing. The model is trained with classifier-free guidance on two new large-scale geo-contextual datasets (SoundingSVI: 169K pairs across 90 countries; SonicUrban: 237K pairs across 131 cities), and evaluated with a proposed Place Similarity Score (PSS) framework measuring element-, scene-, and human-perception-level geographic consistency.

## Results

On SoundingSVI (169K pairs), SounDiT achieves a FID of 16.84, nearly halving the best baseline (PixArt+MHCA at 34.11), and leads on IIS (0.753 vs. 0.578), PSS Element (0.572 vs. 0.474), and PSS Scene (0.753 vs. 0.390). On SonicUrban (237K pairs), the FID gap widens further to 11.55 vs. 41.46, with SounDiT also topping IIS (0.706 vs. 0.592) and PSS Scene (0.739 vs. 0.396). SounDiT achieves the best PSS Perception score (lower is better) on both datasets (0.729 and 0.759), while CoDi retains a slight edge on AIS (0.641 vs. 0.538 on SoundingSVI). A user study with 17 participants confirms 86.13% average matching accuracy between soundscapes and generated landscapes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SounDiT decisively outperforms all five baselines on FID, IIS, PSS Element, PSS Scene, and PSS Perception across both datasets. The closest competitor, PixArt+MHCA, trails by a factor of ~2× on FID (34.1 vs. 16.8 on SoundingSVI; 41.5 vs. 11.6 on SonicUrban) and by 0.17–0.18 on IIS. The pre-trained AudioToken models perform worst overall (FID > 100), suggesting that generic audio-to-image pretraining does not transfer well to geo-contextual landscape generation. CoDi achieves the highest AIS on SoundingSVI (0.641 vs. SounDiT's 0.538), indicating it captures audio-image semantic alignment better, but this comes at the cost of much worse FID (87.0) and PSS Scene (0.302). Sound2Scene and GlueGen occupy the middle range, with Sound2Scene slightly ahead on IIS for SoundingSVI (0.623 vs. 0.566) but far behind on FID and all PSS metrics. Overall, SounDiT's advantage is most pronounced on the geo-contextual PSS metrics (Scene: 0.753 vs. 0.390 best baseline), confirming that the MoE Soundscape Conditioning and scene-conditioning modules specifically improve geographic coherence rather than just visual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the audio-to-image (A2I) synthesis line (Sound2Scene, AudioToken, GlueGen, CoDi) by reframing the task from sound-source-to-object to soundscape-to-landscape with explicit geo-contextual scene conditioning. Architecturally, it adopts the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) paradigm from image/video generation and combines it with a Mixture-of-Experts cross-attention module for soundscape conditioning and a low-rank scene mixer, building on the latent diffusion framework of Stable Diffusion and the multimodal encoding of ImageBind. It departs from prior geographic A2I explorations (Zhuang et al. 2024, 2025) by adding scene-prompt conditioning, a DiT backbone, and a dedicated geo-contextual evaluation suite rather than relying solely on generic metrics like FID.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides two large-scale geo-contextual datasets (169K + 237K pairs) and a practically motivated evaluation framework (PSS) that together establish a benchmark for soundscape-to-landscape generation, a task with direct applications in urban design, noise mitigation, and environmental planning. The PSS framework's multi-level design (element, scene, human perception) addresses a real gap in prior A2I evaluation, which measured visual fidelity but not geographic coherence. Open questions include whether the VLM-annotated scene prompts introduce systematic bias, whether the ground-level street-view viewpoint generalizes to aerial or panoramic settings, and whether the MoE expert count can scale further without diminishing returns.
