## Problem

Multimodal semantic segmentation models suffer severe performance degradation when one or more sensor modalities are missing or corrupted, primarily because training assumes full-modality availability and models develop unimodal bias toward dominant sensors like RGB. Existing self-distillation approaches for robustness either cause noticeable full-modality accuracy drops or propagate the teacher's modality bias to the student through unimodal feature transfer, failing to balance robustness and accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262693/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework accepts up to four modalities (RGB image, depth map, event stream, LiDAR point cloud) at 1024×1024 resolution, each encoded by a dedicated ViT encoder producing four-stage feature pyramids. During training, the student model receives partially missing modalities via Anymodal Dropout, while the teacher sees all modalities; at inference, the model must produce dense pixel-wise semantic segmentation masks under any arbitrary subset of available modalities. Evaluation covers all 15 non-empty modality combinations plus three sensor-failure protocols (Entire-Missing, Random-Missing, and Noisy Modality).

## Method

RobustSeg is a feedback-based teacher-student self-distillation framework whose core module, Hybrid Prototype Distillation (HPD), decouples cross-modal and modality-specific knowledge transfer. For cross-modal alignment, features are converted into class-wise semantic prototypes via masked average pooling, then randomly permuted across modalities before computing KL divergence between student and teacher prototypes, reducing modality bias without introducing modality-specific noise. For modality-specific detail, an Arbitrary-Modal Selection Module identifies dominant modalities by cosine similarity to the fused feature, and only their Intra-class Feature Variation (IFV) maps are distilled to the student, preserving fine-grained structural cues. A feedback loop closes the cycle: the student's non-dominant modality IFV maps are fed back to the teacher, which fine-tunes only the non-dominant modality encoders (freezing dominant encoders and the fusion block) at a reduced learning rate (0.1×), gradually correcting the teacher's own modality bias.

## Results

On the DeLiVER anymodal benchmark (MIT-B0 backbone), RobustSeg achieves 49.91% mean mIoU across all 15 modality combinations, surpassing the previous SoTA AnySeg (47.51%) by +2.40%. Under the sensor-failure robustness protocol (MIT-B2 backbone), RobustSeg attains 45.16% mean mIoU on DeLiVER, 32.58% on MUSES, and 29.19% on MCubeS, outperforming the best prior methods by +3.70%, +5.58%, and +3.94% respectively. Crucially, full-modality performance degrades by only −0.07% to −0.25% mIoU, confirming that robustness gains come at negligible accuracy cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RobustSeg outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated dataset and metric. Against the previous SoTA AnySeg [54], it gains +2.40% mean mIoU on DeLiVER anymodal and +3.70% on the DeLiVER robustness benchmark (45.16 vs 41.46), with the largest per-modality improvement on the Event+LiDAR pair (+5.44%). On MUSES and MCubeS, RobustSeg surpasses the strongest prior method MAGIC++ [51] by +5.58% (32.58 vs 27.00) and +3.94% (29.19 vs 25.25) mean mIoU respectively, and also beats Any2Seg [52] and StitchFusion [19] by similar margins. The base CMNeXt [43] model, which suffers a severe drop from 66.30% (RDEL) to 22.92% (REL), is outperformed by 15–16% in mean robustness mIoU. GeminiFusion [17] and MAGIC [53] trail RobustSeg by 18% and 9.7% on DeLiVER robustness respectively. Notably, RobustSeg achieves these gains while incurring only −0.07% to −0.25% full-modality mIoU loss, whereas AnySeg drops −0.58% and unimodal feature distillation drops −2.13%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-distillation paradigm for robust multimodal segmentation (AnySeg [54], SDSTrack [14]) by replacing unimodal feature distillation with a hybrid scheme that separates cross-modal semantic alignment (via class-wise prototypes) from modality-specific detail transfer (via IFV maps restricted to dominant modalities). It builds on the IFV distillation framework [38] and prototype-based knowledge transfer [35], but departs from prior work by showing that IFV maps are harmful for cross-modal transfer due to modality-specific noise, and by introducing a student-to-teacher feedback loop that fine-tunes the teacher's weaker modality encoders — a reversal of the standard frozen-teacher assumption in KD. The framework is validated on both ViT-based (SegFormer) and CNN-based (CMNeXt) backbones, positioning it as a backbone-agnostic add-on rather than a new architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in deployment: MMSS models trained on full-modality data collapse under sensor failure (e.g., CMNeXt drops from 66.30% to 22.92% mIoU), and prior robustness methods sacrifice full-modality accuracy. RobustSeg's near-zero full-modality cost (−0.07% to −0.25%) while gaining 2.4–4.5% in robustness metrics makes it a practical drop-in for existing pipelines. Open questions include whether the feedback mechanism scales beyond four modalities, whether the 120-epoch joint pre-training requirement is prohibitive for large-scale deployment, and whether the approach generalizes to non-driving domains (the authors mention medical imaging as future work). The reliance on ground-truth labels for prototype computation also limits applicability to weakly- or unsupervised settings.
