## Problem

Existing video face swapping methods face a fundamental trade-off: GAN-based approaches process frames independently and suffer from temporal flickering in long sequences, while diffusion-based methods rely on intermediate representations (landmarks, 3DMM coefficients) that discard the rich pixel-level information in source videos. No prior method directly leverages the full source video as a visual reference to simultaneously preserve fine-grained attributes (lighting, expression, makeup) and maintain temporal coherence across cinematic-length sequences.

## Input / Output

The model takes an 81-frame source video chunk at 640 resolution, a binary mask sequence marking the facial editing region, a target identity image, and a pair of edited keyframes (start and end frames of the chunk, produced by Inswapper with optional manual refinement). The source video is fed in its entirety as a visual reference rather than being masked, and the output is an 81-frame face-swapped video. For longer videos, chunks are processed sequentially with the final frame of each chunk propagated as the start-frame guidance for the next, enabling arbitrary-length generation.

## Method

LIVINGSWAP builds on the VACE 14B DiT backbone with rectified flow training and decomposes long-video face swapping into three stages: keyframe identity injection, video reference completion, and temporal stitching. The key architectural contribution is feeding the complete source video segment (unmasked) as a visual reference through a VAE encoder, concatenating its latent tokens with the target identity and keyframe latents in temporally aligned order, and injecting them via a parallel attribute encoder (DiT blocks initialized from pretrained weights) that adds features element-wise at each backbone layer. For temporal stitching, chunks are generated sequentially where the last output frame of chunk i becomes the start-frame condition for chunk i+1, preventing boundary discontinuities. Training uses the Face2Face dataset constructed by running Inswapper per-frame on CelebV-Text and VFHQ, then reversing the data roles so the GAN-swapped (noisy) video serves as input and the original clean video provides ground-truth supervision, enabling the model to learn beyond the quality of its synthetic training pairs.

## Results

On the proposed CineFaceBench, LivingSwap achieves the best average rank (1.667) across all metrics, with the lowest FVD (54.32 easy / 63.97 hard), best Gaze similarity (0.752 / 0.755), and best Pose error (3.108 / 3.399). On FaceForensics++, LivingSwap also attains the best average rank (3.17) and lowest FVD (19.29), though Inswapper leads on ID Similarity (0.636 vs. 0.592). The method reduces manual editing effort by approximately 40× compared to per-frame industrial pipelines, requiring only keyframe-level intervention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LivingSwap outperforms all five CineFaceBench baselines in average rank (1.667 vs. 2.500 for Inswapper, the next best), driven primarily by substantially lower FVD (54.32 vs. 66.62 for Inswapper, 104.19 for CanonSwap, and 176.96 for Face-Adapter) and superior pose/gaze preservation. Inswapper, the industrial per-frame GAN baseline, retains the highest ID Similarity (0.567 easy / 0.422 hard) but lags LivingSwap on every other metric, particularly FVD and pose consistency, confirming that frame-by-frame processing introduces temporal artifacts. On FaceForensics++, LivingSwap achieves the best FVD (19.29) and average rank (3.17), beating Inswapper (FVD 20.63, rank 3.83) and CanonSwap (FVD 30.30, rank 3.83), while Inswapper still leads on ID Similarity (0.636 vs. 0.592). Diffusion-based baselines (DiffSwap, Face-Adapter) show markedly weaker identity transfer (ID Sim 0.261 and 0.247 on FF++), highlighting the difficulty of identity injection without explicit keyframe conditioning. GAN-based methods (SimSwap, BlendFace, CanonSwap) cluster in the middle on identity but suffer from higher FVD and pose errors, reflecting their frame-by-frame temporal inconsistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reference-guided video editing paradigm of VACE to the face-swapping domain, departing from the dominant inpainting-based formulation (mask the face, regenerate with structural cues) that characterizes prior diffusion-based swappers like DiffSwap and Face-Adapter. It also borrows the keyframe-guided interpolation strategy from recent video inpainting work (Guo et al., 2025) and repurposes GAN-based per-frame swappers (Inswapper) not as the final system but as a keyframe generator and synthetic-data engine. The DiT + rectified-flow backbone and the attribute-encoder injection mechanism are inherited directly from VACE, making this primarily an architectural application and pipeline design contribution rather than a new generative formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 40× reduction in manual editing effort and the introduction of CineFaceBench (cinematic scenarios with occlusions, makeup, complex lighting) address a real gap between academic benchmarks and production needs in film/TV. The role-reversing data strategy—using noisy GAN outputs as inputs and clean originals as targets—is a practical recipe for training reference-guided video models when paired data is scarce, and the finding that the full noisy dataset outperforms filtered subsets suggests strong pretrained priors can absorb supervision noise. Open questions include scalability to multi-face scenes, real-time or near-real-time inference, and whether the keyframe pipeline generalizes to non-rigid or heavily stylized (e.g., animated) content.
