## Problem

Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction and view synthesis methods are largely limited to static scenes, while dynamic scene approaches rely on costly per-scene optimization or treat appearance, geometry, and motion as separate tasks. No prior work unifies all three aspects into a single learned model that processes dynamic monocular video in a single forward pass.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261737/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a posed monocular video (RGB frames, SE(3) camera poses, 3×3 intrinsics, and normalized timestamps in [0,1]) and produces per-pixel 3D Gaussian primitives in a shared canonical coordinate system, each carrying a time-conditioned deformation field. A single forward pass yields novel-view renderings at arbitrary query timestamps, dense depth maps, 3D point trajectories, and per-pixel motion vectors that can be directly converted to scene flow or thresholded for moving-object segmentation. Evaluation uses 13 frames at 518×518 (DyCheck) or 12 views at 379×672 (NVIDIA), with inference completing in under 1 second per scene.

## Method

MoVieS introduces dynamic splatter pixels: each input pixel is mapped to a 3D Gaussian in the first frame's canonical space, augmented with a time-dependent deformation field Δx(t), Δa(t) that displaces the Gaussian's position and attributes toward any query timestamp. The architecture builds on VGGT's pretrained DINOv2 encoder and attention backbone, injecting camera information via both pixel-aligned Plücker embeddings and a global camera token, plus sinusoidal timestamp tokens. Three parallel DPT heads predict depth, Gaussian appearance attributes (with a direct RGB shortcut for color fidelity), and time-conditioned motion (conditioned via AdaLN on the query time). Training uses a multi-task loss combining depth MSE, rendering loss (MSE + LPIPS over M random target views), and a motion loss that pairs point-wise L1 with a distribution-level term preserving relative displacement structure, all learned across eight heterogeneous datasets via a curriculum from static to dynamic to high-resolution fine-tuning.

## Results

On dynamic scene novel view synthesis, MoVieS achieves 18.46 mPSNR / 58.87 mSSIM / 0.3094 mLPIPS on DyCheck and 19.16 PSNR / 51.41 SSIM / 0.3152 LPIPS on NVIDIA, outperforming all optimization-based baselines (MoSca, Shape-of-Motion, Splatter-a-Video) on DyCheck while running in 0.93 s per scene versus 10–45 min for prior methods. On static RealEstate10K, MoVieS reaches 26.98 PSNR / 81.75 SSIM / 0.111 LPIPS, competitive with dedicated static feed-forward models. For 3D point tracking on TAPVid-3D, MoVieS achieves 0.2153 EPE₃D on Aria Digital Twin, 0.0472 on DriveTrack, and 0.0352 on Panoptic Studio, substantially outperforming the best 2D and 3D tracking baselines by 2–3× in EPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DyCheck (dynamic), MoVieS (18.46 mPSNR) beats MoSca (18.24), Shape-of-Motion (17.96), and Splatter-a-Video (13.61) across all three metrics, with the largest gap over Splatter-a-Video (~4.8 dB mPSNR). On NVIDIA, MoSca still leads MoVieS on PSNR (21.45 vs 19.16) and SSIM (71.23 vs 51.41), but MoVieS is ~29× faster (0.93 s vs 45 min) and more robust to camera shake without requiring motion masks. On static RealEstate10K, MoVieS's static-only variant (27.60 PSNR) edges out DepthSplat (26.57) and GS-LRM (26.94), confirming the unified model does not sacrifice static performance. For 3D point tracking, MoVieS dominates all baselines: on Aria Digital Twin it achieves 0.2153 EPE₃D versus 0.5413 (SpatialTracker, best 3D baseline) and 0.5539 (BootsTAP), a 2.5× improvement; on Panoptic Studio it reaches 87.88% δ₃D⁰·⁰⁵ versus 72.91% for SpatialTracker. The key advantage is that MoVieS estimates 3D positions directly in a shared world coordinate without relying on external monocular depth estimators, avoiding the noise and inconsistency that degrade 2D-to-3D unprojection baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

MoVieS extends the feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction line (DUSt3R, VGGT, DepthSplat, GS-LRM) from static to dynamic scenes by introducing a time-conditioned motion head that predicts per-pixel 3D displacements via AdaLN. It departs from optimization-based dynamic reconstruction (Shape-of-Motion, MoSca, Splatter-a-Video) by learning a single feed-forward model rather than per-scene iterative fitting, and from prior feed-forward dynamic methods (BTimer, NutWorld) by explicitly supervising motion with tracking data and jointly training appearance, geometry, and motion in one architecture. The backbone is VGGT (itself built on DINOv2 and attention blocks), with DPT-style heads for dense prediction, positioning MoVieS as a natural dynamic extension of the VGGT family.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying appearance, geometry, and motion in a single feed-forward model, MoVieS eliminates the per-scene optimization bottleneck (0.93s vs. 10–45 min) and enables zero-shot downstream tasks like scene flow estimation and moving object segmentation without any task-specific supervision. The key open limitation is the requirement for posed input videos; handling unposed or in-the-wild footage (where poses must be estimated) remains future work. Broader implications include a path toward generalizable 4D scene understanding for embodied agents, though the current formulation is constrained to relatively short clips and moderate resolutions.
