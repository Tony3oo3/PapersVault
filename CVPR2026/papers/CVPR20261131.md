## Problem

Prevailing text-to-motion generation methods rely on joint text-motion embeddings that struggle to capture fine-grained temporal semantics (e.g., exact step counts and action ordering) and offer no interpretability into why a generated motion takes its particular form. No existing framework enables large language models to autonomously compose motions through explicit, editable symbolic representations rather than opaque latent-space interpolation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261131/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes free-form natural language instructions (descriptive or prescriptive, e.g., 'Walk forward in 5 steps and then walk backward in 3 steps') and produces a 3D human motion sequence at the frame resolution of the underlying skeleton. Unusually, the pipeline operates through an intermediate LabanLite representation: a frame-wise, body-part-wise symbolic encoding where each frame carries discrete Laban symbols across four body-part groups (left/right arms, left/right legs) with attribute fields for direction, level, orientation, bend, and effort, making the generation process inspectable and editable at the symbol level.

## Method

LaMoGen introduces LabanLite, a digital adaptation of Labanotation that converts event-wise symbolic scores into frame-wise, body-part-wise instances with a fixed set of conceptual symbols (direction, level) and detail symbols (orientation, bend, effort), each paired with a standardized textual description. The generation pipeline is two-stage: first, an LLM (e.g., GPT-4.1) composes a sequence of conceptual Laban symbols via retrieval-augmented prompting, where CLIP retrieves top-K entries from a Conceptual Description Database as in-context examples; second, a Kinematic Detail Augmentor (a transformer) autoregressively enriches the conceptual sequence with detail attributes over a learned Laban codebook of N learnable embeddings, using binary cross-entropy on activation probabilities. A Laban-Motion Encoder-Decoder jointly optimizes the codebook and a transformer decoder to reconstruct motion from summed active code embeddings, trained with L1 pose and velocity losses. This separation of high-level symbolic planning (LLM) from low-level kinematic synthesis (transformer over codebook) is the key architectural distinction from prior joint-embedding or pose-code approaches.

## Results

On HumanML3D, LaMoGen with GPT-4.1 achieves R-Precision Top-3 of 0.796, FID of 0.252, and MM-Dist of 3.087, ranking top-3 across all five standard metrics. On KIT-ML, the same configuration reaches R@3 of 0.775 and FID of 0.415. On the proposed Laban Benchmark (HumanML3D-Laban), LaMoGen with GPT-4.1 attains the best scores on all three Labanotation-based metrics (SMT, TMP, HMN) and R@3, demonstrating superior symbolic, temporal, and harmonic text-motion alignment. The automatic Laban symbol detection workflow achieves SMT=0.871, TMP=0.852, and HMN=0.786, substantially outperforming the hardcoded baseline (0.751/0.632/0.611).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On standard HumanML3D metrics, LaMoGen (GPT-4.1) is competitive but not uniformly best: it matches MoDiff on R@1 (0.491) and edges out ReMoDiff on R@3 (0.796 vs 0.795), but trails ReMoDiff on FID (0.252 vs 0.103) and MotionGPT on Multi-Modality (1.131 vs 2.008). The authors attribute the FID gap to LabanLite's high-level abstraction collapsing low-level speed/style variations into identical symbols. On KIT-ML, LaMoGen (GPT-4.1) achieves the best R@3 (0.775) and is second-best on R@2 (0.649), but again lags ReMoDiff on FID (0.415 vs 0.155). The paper's key differentiator is the Laban Benchmark, where LaMoGen with GPT-4.1 outperforms all baselines on SMT, TMP, and HMN, while methods like MDM and CoMo that rely on joint text-motion embeddings fail on compositional instructions (e.g., step counts, temporal ordering). For Laban symbol detection, the proposed workflow improves SMT from 0.751 to 0.871 and TMP from 0.632 to 0.852 over the hardcoded Ikeuchi et al. baseline. The 'Human' composer condition (ground-truth Laban annotations) consistently outperforms all LLM configurations, confirming that the symbolic interface is the bottleneck and that stronger LLMs (GPT-4.1 > GPT-4.1-mini > Qwen3/DeepSeekV3) yield progressively better motion quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant text-to-motion paradigm of joint text-motion embeddings (MDM, ReMoDiff, MoDiff) and vector-quantized motion tokens (CoMo, MotionGPT) by introducing LabanLite, a symbolic intermediate representation derived from the traditional Labanotation dance notation system. Rather than fine-tuning LLMs directly on motion tokens as in MotionGPT, LaMoGen uses retrieval-augmented prompting to let LLMs compose interpretable Laban symbol sequences, drawing a conceptual parallel to how CoMo uses pose codes but arguing that Laban symbols capture both start/end poses and the transformation between them. The framework also extends the retrieval-augmented generation idea from ReMoDiff (which retrieves latent motion embeddings) to the symbolic level, where retrieved examples are structured Laban scores with standardized textual descriptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Laban Benchmark with its three metrics (SMT, TMP, HMN) addresses a real gap: standard FID and R-precision fail to distinguish methods that get the right motion type but wrong temporal ordering or body-part coordination, as the paper demonstrates with MotionGPT outperforming CoMo on Laban metrics despite trailing on conventional scores. A key limitation is that LabanLite's high-level abstraction inherently cannot capture low-level kinematic variations (e.g., speed differences in the same gesture), resulting in consistently higher FID than diffusion-based competitors. Open questions include whether the symbolic approach generalizes beyond the predominantly locomotion-heavy benchmark to more complex, multi-joint choreography, and whether the LLM's 'composition' is genuine symbolic reasoning or pattern-matching over retrieved examples.
