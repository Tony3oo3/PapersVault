## Problem

Existing uncertainty-aware gaze tracking models produce numerically inaccurate uncertainty estimates under domain shifts because their learned input-to-uncertainty mappings encode training-domain-specific bias, rendering absolute uncertainty values unreliable across subjects or datasets. Moreover, the standard evaluation metric (error-uncertainty correlation) is spurious since prediction error and uncertainty are non-causally related, making it impossible to faithfully assess whether an uncertainty model is actually well-calibrated.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a left eye patch, a right eye patch, and head angle readings (yaw/pitch) as a composite input vector, and outputs two independent Gaussian distributions (one for yaw, one for pitch) parameterized by mean and variance. After the post-hoc calibration step, the system produces corrected quantile values and confidence intervals (e.g., 95% CI) that require only ~50 calibration samples from the target domain, with no modification to the underlying CNN weights.

## Method

The paper formulates uncertainty correction as an output-level conditional distribution matching problem: a heteroskedastic regression CNN (ResNet-18 or ResNet-50) trained with negative log-likelihood loss produces uncalibrated Gaussian predictions, and a nonparametric isotonic regression model R is then fitted on a small calibration set to map nominal cumulative probabilities to empirically observed coverage probabilities. At test time, the corrected quantile is computed as F⁻¹(R(p)) rather than F⁻¹(p), effectively warping the predicted CDF to match the true distribution without touching model parameters. The isotonic constraint preserves CDF monotonicity while allowing arbitrary nonlinear corrections, avoiding the parametric assumptions of temperature or linear scaling. The authors also introduce Coverage Probability Error (CPE), a proper scoring rule inspired by the Brier score, that computes the RMSE between nominal and empirical cumulative probabilities across the full [0,1] range, providing a distribution-level calibration metric that replaces the misleading EUC.

## Results

Across all eight test cases (within-dataset cross-subject and cross-dataset, on MPIIGaze and RTGene with ResNet-18 and ResNet-50), the post-hoc isotonic calibration reduces average CPE from a wide 17.5–27.2% range (uncalibrated) to a stable 4.6–5.3% range, representing 70–82% improvement, all statistically significant (Mann-Whitney U, p<0.05). Angular error also decreases by 7–32% in 7 of 8 cases (e.g., 13.89°→9.50° for MPII→RTGene cross-dataset with ResNet-50), with only one case showing a slight 3% increase. The 95% confidence interval coverage probability improves dramatically, from as low as 16.4% (uncalibrated, Test Case 8) to 86.5–89.0% (calibrated), approaching the nominal 95% target.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is between uncalibrated and calibrated versions of the same heteroskedastic regression model (ResNet-18/50), rather than against external competing methods. Calibration consistently wins on CPE across all eight test cases, reducing it by 70–82% (e.g., 27.21%→4.84% for RTGene→MPII cross-dataset with ResNet-18). On angular error, calibration wins in 7 of 8 cases with reductions of 7–32%, with the sole exception being Test Case 8 (RTGene→MPII, ResNet-50) where error increases by 3%. The paper also contrasts its proposed CPE metric against the widely used EUC metric, demonstrating that EUC produces misleadingly low values (0.04–0.26) even for well-calibrated models, while CPE correctly reflects the improvement from ~40% to near-zero. In the 95% CI case study (Table 2), calibrated models achieve 86.5–89.0% coverage versus 16.4–67.3% for uncalibrated models, and outperform quantile regression baselines which also fail to reach nominal coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the heteroskedastic Gaussian regression framework (Nix & Weigend, 1994) as the base uncertainty-aware gaze estimator, a formulation widely adopted in prior gaze-tracking uncertainty work (Her et al., 2023; Kellnhofer et al., 2019; Zhong et al., 2024). It departs from the dominant adaptation paradigm (meta-learning, transfer learning) by proposing a parameter-free, post-hoc output calibration via isotonic regression, drawing on proper scoring rules (Brier, 1950) and the calibration literature. The paper also explicitly challenges the field's reliance on error-uncertainty correlation (EUC) as an evaluation metric, arguing it is spurious, and introduces Coverage Probability Error (CPE) as a distribution-level alternative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The critique of EUC as a misleading evaluation metric has broad implications for how the gaze-tracking community assesses uncertainty models, potentially reshaping how future papers report and compare uncertainty quality. The data-efficient calibration (≈50 samples) offers a practical, model-agnostic remedy for domain-shift-induced miscalibration in safety-critical applications like driver monitoring. Limitations include evaluation on only two datasets and two CNN backbones, restriction to variance-based (Gaussian) uncertainty outputs, and the assumption that calibration and test data share similar error patterns; extending the approach to range-based or contrastive uncertainty models and to more severe or heterogeneous domain shifts remains open.
