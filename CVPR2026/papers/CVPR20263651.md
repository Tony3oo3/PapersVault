## Problem

Existing pseudo-bag augmentation methods for MIL-based WSI classification (self-augmentation and Mixup-style) sample instances from only one or two bags, limiting pseudo-bag diversity. Their reduction-and-merge strategy discards critical instances during sampling, introducing label noise. Furthermore, naively increasing tumor instances in pseudo-bags reduces the occurrence of pseudo-bags with few critical instances, degrading performance on slides with small tumor areas.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263651/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on pre-extracted patch features (from ResNet50 or Prov-Gigapath) organized into variable-size bags, with only bag-level labels available during training. During training, C²Aug generates two pseudo-bags per input bag via different augmentation configurations, feeding them into student and teacher MIL branches. At inference, no augmentation is applied—the original bag is passed directly to the MIL model to produce a single WSI class label.

## Method

C²Aug adopts an increase-and-merge strategy: it preserves all instances from the input bag while sampling additional instances from all same-class bags in the dataset. Cross-Bag Augmentation comprises three sub-modules—Multi-View Fusion (Cross-Attention fusing each instance with m instances from other same-class bags, with row-wise random masking to vary the number of fused views per instance), Instance Expansion (concatenating randomly sampled instances to increase bag size), and Instance Compression (Cross-Attention with a learnable query and randomly sampled compression ratio to reduce bag size). To counteract the reduced diversity of pseudo-bags with few critical instances, the method introduces Bag-level Contrastive Learning (student-teacher with EMA, memory bank, and cosine-similarity-based loss) and Group-level Contrastive Learning (assigning instances to learnable prototypes, performing size alignment via Cross-Attention, and minimizing KL divergence between student and teacher group representations). The total training loss combines classification loss with weighted bag-level and group-level contrastive losses.

## Results

On CAMELYON16 with Prov-Gigapath features and TransMIL, C²Aug achieves 97.3% ACC, 98.9% AUC, and 97.6% F1, improving AUC by 5.6% over the vanilla TransMIL baseline. On TCGA-LUNG, C²Aug outperforms the second-best method by 1.0% in AUC. With ResNet50 features on CAMELYON16, C²Aug reaches 91.9% ACC and 94.7% AUC. The method consistently achieves the highest scores across all three datasets (CAMELYON16, TCGA-LUNG, TCGA-BRCA) and both feature extractors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

C²Aug outperforms all five baselines across all three datasets and both feature extractors. On CAMELYON16 (Prov-Gigapath), C²Aug achieves 98.9% AUC, surpassing AugDiff (97.0%), MixupMIL (96.1%), RankMix (97.3%), DPBAug (95.5%), and PRDL (93.7%)—a 5.6% AUC gain over the vanilla TransMIL baseline. On TCGA-LUNG, C²Aug leads the second-best method by 1.0% in AUC. The advantage is most pronounced on CAMELYON16, where the small tumor area (<10% of tissue) makes the method's bag-level and group-level contrastive learning particularly effective for distinguishing rare tumor instances. Among baselines, AugDiff and DPBAug are the closest competitors on CAMELYON16, while on TCGA-LUNG and TCGA-BRCA the gaps narrow but C²Aug still maintains the top position on all metrics (ACC, AUC, F1).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pseudo-bag augmentation line of work (RankMix, MixupMIL, DPBAug, AugDiff, PRDL) by replacing the prevailing 'reduction-and-merge' strategy—sampling a few critical instances from one or two bags—with an 'increase-and-merge' strategy that draws instances from all bags sharing the same class label while retaining the full input bag. The contrastive learning component adapts the DINO teacher-student EMA framework to the bag level and builds on RetCCL's group-level contrastive learning, but substitutes K-means cluster centers with learnable prototypes to make group representations trainable. The three augmentation sub-modules (Multi-View Fusion via Cross-Attention, Instance Compression, Instance Expansion) repurpose standard attention mechanisms in a new combinatorial arrangement rather than introducing fundamentally new architectural primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'increase-and-merge' framing offers a useful conceptual lens for future pseudo-bag augmentation work, and the ablation showing that retaining the input bag is critical (Table 4) provides practical guidance for practitioners designing augmentation pipelines. The most practically relevant finding is the targeted improvement on slides with <1% tumor ratio (+1.8% AUC), which is the hardest regime in clinical deployment. However, the method's complexity (three augmentation strategies plus two contrastive losses with multiple hyperparameters) and evaluation on only three datasets limit generalizability claims; whether the learnable-prototype advantage over K-means persists at larger scale or in other MIL domains remains open.
