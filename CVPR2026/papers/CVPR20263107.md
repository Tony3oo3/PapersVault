## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based reflective object reconstruction methods rely on RGB images that do not encode sufficient per-pixel reflection state, leading to incorrect decomposition of albedo from diffuse and specular components and preventing physically consistent relighting. This paper identifies polarization cues as the missing signal that disambiguates these physical attributes, since specular and diffuse reflection produce distinct, predictable polarization signatures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263107/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts multiview polarization images (full Stokes vectors from a polarization camera, or partial linearly-polarized images from RGB cameras with off-the-shelf linear polarizers) alongside standard RGB views. It produces a 2D Gaussian representation carrying per-primitive albedo, index of refraction, roughness, and surface normal, from which it renders decomposed albedo/diffuse/specular maps, a learned environment cube mipmap, and relit novel views under arbitrary environment maps. Notably, the pipeline is trainable with only two LP-filtered RGB cameras, eliminating the need for a dedicated polarization camera.

## Method

PhyGaP extends Ref-Gaussian (2DGS + deferred rendering) with two key components. First, PolarDR replaces the standard deferred rendering equation with a polarimetric BRDF (pBRDF) that computes pixel-wise Stokes vectors by modeling specular reflection (strong linear polarization via Fresnel R coefficients) and diffuse reflection (weaker polarization with 90° angle-of-polarization shift via Fresnel T coefficients) separately, supervised directly by ground-truth polarization. Second, GridMap addresses self-occlusion on non-convex objects by placing 52 anchor cameras on the bounding box, constructing local cubemaps via one-step ray tracing that blend object color with the global environment, and computing distance-weighted diffuse/specular Stokes from all local maps—avoiding both scene-specific SH indirect light (which breaks relighting) and expensive multi-bounce ray tracing. Training is supervised with combined RGB, polarization (L1 on s₁, s₂), mask, depth-normal consistency, and edge-aware normal smoothness losses, and the albedo is stored as a view-independent attribute rather than spherical harmonics.

## Results

On novel view synthesis (PSNR), PhyGaP achieves 28.14 dB on owl, 32.92 dB on frog, and 29.69 dB on teapot, outperforming all RGB-based baselines (R3DG, GS-IR, GIR, 3DGS-DR, Ref-Gaussian) and the concurrent PolGS on average by ~2 dB. For surface normal reconstruction (Cosine Distance, lower is better), PhyGaP achieves 0.0079 on david and 0.0261 on dog, improving over Ref-Gaussian by 45.7% on average. On relighting (david scene with sunset envmap), PhyGaP reaches 19.18 PSNR / 0.973 SSIM / 0.0255 LPIPS, surpassing the best RGB-based baseline GIR (18.02 / 0.960 / 0.0327) and R3DG (11.42 / 0.916 / 0.0754).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the five RGB-only deferred-rendering baselines (R3DG, GS-IR, GIR, 3DGS-DR, Ref-Gaussian), PhyGaP wins on PSNR for 7–8 out of 9 scenes and on Cosine Distance for 6–7 out of 7 comparable scenes, with the largest margins over Ref-Gaussian (e.g., CD 0.0482 vs 0.1122 on frog; PSNR 32.92 vs 34.13 on frog where 3DGS-DR is closest). Against the concurrent polarization-based PolGS, PhyGaP achieves higher PSNR on 5 of 7 shared scenes (e.g., 32.92 vs 28.25 on frog) and lower CD on 4 of 5 shared scenes (e.g., 0.0467 vs 0.0491 on owl). Against the NeRF-based PANDORA, PhyGaP wins on PSNR for frog (32.92 vs 28.42) and dog (37.82 vs 26.29) but trails on squirrel (20.01 vs 27.97), snail (28.18 vs 31.56), and david (24.62 vs 30.54), likely because PANDORA was trained with more views and 150k iterations. Against NeRSP, PhyGaP is competitive on CD for squirrel (0.0597 vs 0.0243 — NeRSP wins) but PhyGaP is the only method that supports relighting. On the relighting task (david + sunset envmap), PhyGaP achieves 19.18 PSNR and 0.0255 LPIPS, beating GIR (18.02 / 0.0327) by ~1.2 dB and R3DG (11.42 / 0.0754) by ~7.8 dB, confirming that polarization-grounded decomposition is essential for realistic relighting.

## Relation to Prior Work

PhyGaP builds directly on Ref-Gaussian's 2DGS + deferred rendering pipeline, replacing its RGB-only material splatting with a polarimetric deferred rendering (PolarDR) process that computes pixel-wise Stokes vectors via the pSVBRDF model. It extends the polarization-based NeRF line (PANDORA, NeRSP) into the 3DGS domain, while departing from RGB-only GS methods (GS-IR, 3DGS-DR, R3DG, GIR) that cannot correctly decompose albedo from reflection. The concurrent PolGS is the only other attempt to fuse polarization into 3DGS, but PhyGaP distinguishes itself by supporting relighting through its GridMap technique, which resolves self-occlusion for non-convex objects without learning scene-specific indirect lighting parameters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PhyGaP is the first polarization-based 3DGS method to support physically consistent relighting, addressing a fundamental gap where RGB-only methods conflate albedo with reflected light and produce color shifts under new illumination. The GridMap approach (52 anchor cameras with local cubemaps) offers a practical middle ground between per-Gaussian SH indirect light (which breaks under relighting) and full multi-bounce ray tracing (too expensive for training). Open questions remain around metal surfaces (complex-valued IoR), highly irregular geometries with multiple inter-reflection bounces, and the gap between the infinite-depth environment map assumption and real-world finite scenes. The demonstration that partial polarization from two off-the-shelf LP cameras suffices broadens practical accessibility.
