key_contribution: ORCA introduces a five-stage multi-agent framework for DocVQA that combines a reasoning agent for question decomposition, a VLM-based router with Turbo DFS decoding to select from nine specialized document agents, sequential orchestration with reasoning-path masking to prevent confirmation bias, and a conditional adversarial debate mechanism (activated in only 8.3% of cases), achieving SOTA performance of 97.2 ANLS on DocVQA and 88.0 on InfographicsVQA over single-model baselines.
limitations: The thinker agent (GLM-4.5V-9B) is identified as the primary error source (43% of failures), and the framework is restricted to single-page documents; the authors plan to extend to multi-page understanding, replace the supervised router with reinforcement learning, and refine the debate mechanism via multi-agent PPO, indicating these are current architectural bottlenecks.

surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that the elaborate multi-turn debate mechanism (Stage 4) activates in only 8.3% of instances and contributes just 0.3–0.8 points, while the core gains come almost entirely from reasoning-guided agent selection (Stages 1–2). This suggests that for DocVQA, the primary value of multi-agent systems lies in specialized routing rather than adversarial verification, a somewhat counterintuitive result given the paper's emphasis on debate. The OCRBench-v2 finding that gains are inversely correlated with model capacity (+3.6 for 7B vs. +1.7 for 8B) is also interesting but based on only two model sizes.

## Problem

Existing Vision-Language Models for Document Visual Question Answering rely on a single model to handle all document modalities and reasoning steps, lacking mechanisms for content-aware specialization, adaptive agent selection, and self-verification. Even reasoning-enhanced approaches (CoT, tool-use) do not decompose questions into modality-specific sub-tasks, stress-test predictions through adversarial debate, or resolve conflicts between competing interpretations, limiting reliability on complex multi-element documents.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261361/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a single-page document image (which may contain heterogeneous elements such as tables, figures, forms, handwritten text, and free-form text) paired with a natural language question, and produces a text answer formatted to match the document's original conventions (spacing, punctuation). During inference, the pipeline also emits intermediate artifacts—a structured reasoning path, a binary agent-activation vector, and per-agent sequential outputs—that can be logged as supervision signals for downstream training.

## Method

ORCA is a five-stage multi-agent pipeline: (1) a thinker agent (GLM-4.5V-9B) decomposes the question into a reasoning path and produces an initial answer; (2) a router (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, trained via constrained generation with Turbo DFS decoding) selects from nine specialized Qwen3VL-8B agents (OCR, table, figure, form, layout, etc.), and an orchestrator sequences them so each agent receives the predecessor's output, with the final agent receiving a masked reasoning path to prevent confirmation bias; (3) if the expert answer disagrees with the thinker's, a debate agent generates challenging follow-up questions and an evaluation agent scores the specialized agent's responses over two turns; (4) on failure, a thesis–antithesis debate (InternVL3-8B as antithesis) runs for three structured turns under an LLM judge that assesses conviction and confidence; (5) a sanity checker corrects formatting. Critically, the verification stages (3–4) activate conditionally—only in ~8.3% of instances—concentrating compute where genuine uncertainty arises rather than applying debate universally.

## Results

On DocVQA, ORCA(Qwen3VL-8B) achieves 97.2 ANLS, the best among all compared methods, with a +1.1 point gain over its single-model baseline (96.1). On InfographicsVQA, ORCA reaches 88.0 ANLS, a substantial +4.9 improvement over the baseline (83.1) and +6.4% average gain across the three ORCA configurations. On OCRBench-v2, ORCA(Qwen3VL-8B) attains 67.1 average across eight subtasks (+1.7 over baseline), with the largest gains on Understanding (+15.5) and Reasoning (+18.5). ORCA also generalizes to ChartQA (90.1% vs. 85.7% baseline, +4.4%) and VQAv2 (+4.7% over single-model).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ORCA outperforms all 25 baselines on the average of DocVQA and InfographicsVQA. Against document-understanding models (LayoutLMv2, DocOwl-1.5, Text-Monkey), ORCA leads by 25–35 points on average, reflecting the gap between task-specific extractive models and orchestrated multi-agent reasoning. Among general-purpose VLMs, ORCA(Qwen3VL-8B) surpasses Qwen2-VL (90.7 avg) by 1.9 points and GPT-4o (87.6) by 5.0 points, while also beating closed-source systems like Claude-3.7 Sonnet (79.8) and Gemini Pro 1.5 (79.6) by over 12 points. The closest competitor is MiMo-VL-7B-RL (91.6 avg), which edges out ORCA(Qwen3VL-8B) on InfographicsVQA by 0.1 (88.1 vs. 88.0) but trails on DocVQA (95.0 vs. 97.2); ORCA's advantage is most pronounced on InfographicsVQA, where the +6.4% average gain over baselines indicates that multi-agent collaboration excels in complex cross-modal reasoning. On OCRBench-v2, ORCA consistently improves over its single-model baselines at every scale (+3.6 for 7B, +1.1 for 4B, +1.7 for 8B), with the largest relative gains on smaller models, suggesting the framework compensates for individual capacity limitations through specialized agent expertise.

## Relation to Prior Work

ORCA synthesizes three research threads—multi-agent orchestration (Visual ChatGPT, HuggingGPT), chain-of-thought reasoning with self-consistency, and multi-agent debate for verification—into a unified pipeline specifically designed for document VQA. Its key departures from prior orchestration frameworks are the use of a VLM trained for document-type routing via constrained generation with Turbo DFS decoding (rather than hand-crafted routing rules), a reasoning-path masking mechanism to prevent confirmation bias in downstream agents, and conditional activation of debate stages (triggered in only 8.3% of cases). The specialized agent dock (nine fine-tuned Qwen3VL-8B variants) repurposes the document-understanding specialization idea from models like DocOwl and LayoutLM but applies it at the agent level rather than the architecture level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, agent-based architecture allows individual components (router, specialists, debate agents) to be upgraded independently as foundation models improve, offering a practical path for continuous system enhancement without re-architecting. However, the error analysis reveals that 43% of failures originate in the thinker agent's reasoning path, suggesting the current GLM-4.5V-9B thinker is a significant bottleneck that limits the framework's ceiling. Open questions include whether the elaborate verification machinery (Stages 3–5) justifies its complexity given its modest aggregate contribution, whether the approach extends to multi-page documents with inter-page reasoning, and whether reinforcement learning can replace the supervised router to handle out-of-distribution document types.
