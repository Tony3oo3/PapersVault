## Problem

Existing appliance digital assets (PartNet-Mobility, CheckManual, ArtVIP, Infinite Mobility) suffer from low rendering quality, missing physical/electronic mechanisms, and no alignment with real user manuals, creating a simulation-reality gap that prevents realistic evaluation of appliance manipulation planning. No prior dataset simultaneously provides real dimensions, real textures, complete physical and electronic mechanisms, program logic, and corresponding real manuals for a diverse set of appliances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260675/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes multilingual appliance manuals (text + illustrations), natural-language task instructions, RGB observation images of the simulated appliance, and execution history with real-time state feedback. Outputs are structured as: (1) relevant manual page indices, (2) ordered sequences drawn from 9 appliance-specific atomic actions (e.g., Press, Rotate, Open/Close) and 4 object-manipulation actions (Pick, Place, Move), (3) axis-aligned bounding boxes in [x1,y1,x2,y2] pixel format for part grounding, and (4) a single corrected next action for closed-loop adjustment. Action execution during evaluation uses 'magic manipulation' to decouple planning quality from low-level policy errors, and any part localization with IoU < 0.5 is treated as a hard failure in the full-process task.

## Method

The core contribution is a pipeline that produces 100 high-fidelity appliance assets in NVIDIA Isaac Sim (USD format), each aligned to a real manual collected from multiple countries. Assets are modeled in Autodesk 3ds Max with TurboSmooth subdivision, UV-unwrapped in Unfold3D, and textured in Photoshop to replicate logos, scales, and surface materials from reference photos. Each asset is equipped with modular mechanism classes spanning two categories—physical (inner spring, magnetic attraction, mechanical trigger, knob countdown drive, safety lock) and electronic (real-time screen display, touch sensing, illumination, logo indicator, rotary motor)—all following a unified interface for flexible composition. Per-appliance program logic is then scripted using manual-defined setting parameters (power, temperature, time, mode) as state variables that drive component updates, reproducing real operational workflows in simulation. The resulting RealAppliance-Bench evaluates five tasks (manual page retrieval, open-loop planning, part grounding, closed-loop adjustment, full-process inference) across 14 appliance categories, 589 operable components, and 979 manipulation tasks with 941 intermediate interference steps.

## Results

On RealAppliance-Bench, proprietary MLLMs lead across all five tasks. For Manual Page Retrieval (Task 1), Gemini 2.5 Pro achieves the best total Recall of 90.00% (F1 79.40), followed by GPT-5 Mini (87.66/77.86) and GPT-5 (86.50/80.89). For Open-loop Manipulation Planning (Task 2), the best task completion rate is 4.36% (Qwen3-VL 235B Thinking) with a success rate of only 1.73%, while Gemini 2.5 Flash achieves the highest success rate of 2.65%. Appliance Part Grounding (Task 3) is the weakest task overall, with GPT-5 scoring only 12.15/8.59 (avg IoU / mAP@0.5). Critically, on the Full-process Inference task (Task 5), nearly all models achieve a zero success rate due to error accumulation across the sequential pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Proprietary MLLMs (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash) consistently outperform open-source MLLMs (Qwen3-VL, GLM) on Manual Page Retrieval, with Gemini 2.5 Pro leading at 90.00% Recall vs. Qwen3-VL 235B Thinking at 81.00%. On Open-loop Planning, the gap narrows considerably: Gemini 2.5 Flash (4.26/2.65) and Qwen3-VL 235B Thinking (4.36/1.73) are competitive, while embodied planning models (RoboBrain 2.0, ManualPlan, ApBot) lag far behind, with RoboBrain 2.0-7B achieving only 0.16% completion. For Part Grounding, GPT-5 leads at 12.15 avg IoU, but all models score below 15%, indicating this remains an open challenge. The most striking finding is that all 13 baselines achieve 0% success on the Full-process Inference task, confirming that no current model can reliably chain retrieval, planning, grounding, and closed-loop adjustment into a complete appliance operation pipeline. Among embodied-specific baselines, ManualPlan (5.61% completion on Task 2) slightly outperforms RoboBrain 2.0-32B (0.37%) on planning, but both are far below general MLLMs, suggesting domain-specific training has not yet closed the gap with general-purpose multimodal models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the line of appliance digital asset work (PartNet-Mobility, Infinite Mobility, ArtVIP) by being the first to construct 3D appliance models directly from real user manuals, ensuring alignment in dimensions, textures, physical mechanisms, electronic mechanisms, and program logic simultaneously. It also extends the manual-based manipulation planning task introduced by ManualPlan and ApBot, which relied on synthetic manuals and unrealistic evaluation protocols (state-machine checks without visual feedback), by grounding the entire pipeline in real manuals and controllable, visually-faithful simulation assets. The object-oriented mechanism architecture (physical + electronic mechanism classes with unified interfaces) is a novel engineering contribution not present in prior articulated-object datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals a stark capability gap: even the strongest proprietary MLLMs (GPT-5, Gemini 2.5 Pro) achieve only ~4–5% task completion on open-loop planning and 0% on full-process inference, while part grounding IoUs cluster near zero. This suggests that current multimodal models lack the fine-grained spatial reasoning and procedural logic comprehension needed for real appliance manipulation, pointing to concrete research directions (spatial association between manual images and live observations, closed-loop state tracking, appliance-specific reasoning). A key limitation is that the 100-asset scale and 14 categories, while a first, may not yet capture the full diversity of household appliances, and the 'magic manipulation' execution in full-process evaluation sidesteps low-level policy errors that would compound in real deployment.
