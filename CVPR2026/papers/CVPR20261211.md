## Problem

Existing multi-rater medical segmentation frameworks operate primarily in the spatial domain, conflating scanner-specific acquisition artifacts with genuine inter-rater diagnostic variability in the latent space. This entanglement produces overconfident, poorly calibrated predictions that fail to distinguish clinically meaningful uncertainty from irrelevant noise, and limits cross-scanner generalization. The paper addresses the intertwined challenge of simultaneously standardizing data-level features and capturing rater-specific stylistic differences without collapsing to a single consensus.

## Input / Output

The model takes a medical image (128×128 CT patches at 0.5 mm isotropic resolution for LIDC-IDRI, or multi-modal T1/T2/T1c MRI slices for NPC-170) together with a rater identifier, and produces (i) a set of K stochastic segmentation samples from the shared probabilistic prior, (ii) a personalized segmentation mask tailored to the specified rater, and (iii) implicit uncertainty reflected in the spread of generated samples. Training is two-phase: the backbone and Noise Harmonizer are trained jointly for 100 epochs, then the backbone is frozen while only the lightweight Personalization Module (0.07 M parameters) is fine-tuned for 150 epochs, enabling efficient few-shot personalization without retraining the full network.

## Method

Built on the Probabilistic U-Net CVAE backbone, the framework introduces two cooperating modules. The Noise Harmonizer applies data-driven affine modulation (γ, β) at each decoder layer, where parameters are predicted via a small MLP over global-average-pooled features cross-attended with a bank of learnable artifact tokens, dynamically suppressing scanner-specific intensity drifts and motion artifacts. The Personalization Module operates in the spectral domain: features are decomposed via a Haar DWT into low- and high-frequency sub-bands, and N learnable prompt components are adaptively weighted by a rater-specific scalar to form a context-conditioned prompt that interacts with high-frequency features through Large Kernel Attention; a cross-attention step against a bank of prior samples and an inverse DWT then resynthesize a rater-adaptive latent code. A GED-based loss (using 1−IoU as the base metric) regularizes the generative distribution to match empirical annotation variability, while a harmonization penalty keeps the affine parameters near identity. The total objective combines Dice+CE segmentation loss, KL divergence, the harmonization penalty, and the GED term, trained with Adam at 1e-4 (Phase 1) and 5e-5 (Phase 2) on a single RTX 3090.

## Results

On LIDC-IDRI, the Harmonizer Network achieves the best GED of 0.1419, Dice_soft of 91.35%, Dice_max of 92.65%, Dice_match of 90.00%, and mean per-rater Dice of 90.78%, outperforming all baselines across every metric. On the more challenging multimodal NPC-170 dataset, it reaches GED 0.2685, Dice_soft 83.10%, Dice_max 84.46%, Dice_match 81.63%, and Dice_mean 81.63%, again leading all compared methods. The model adds only 0.21 M parameters over the Probabilistic U-Net backbone (30.31 M total) and requires ~0.42 s per forward pass on a single RTX 3090.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Harmonizer Network outperforms all seven baselines on every reported metric across both LIDC-IDRI and NPC-170. Against the closest competitor D-Persona [28], it improves Dice_mean by +1.61 pp on LIDC-IDRI (90.78 vs. 89.17) and +1.23 pp on NPC-170 (81.63 vs. 80.40), while also reducing GED from 0.1444 to 0.1419 (LIDC) and 0.2970 to 0.2685 (NPC). The gap over the Probabilistic U-Net [10] is larger in distributional fit (GED 0.1048 vs. 0.2168 on LIDC with 50 samples), reflecting the Noise Harmonizer's artifact suppression. Transformer-based TAB [14] and probabilistic Pionono [19] trail by 4.81 pp and 1.94 pp in Dice_mean on LIDC-IDRI, respectively, with Pionono being the strongest non-personalized baseline. On NPC-170, the method also surpasses Geometric-Structural [24] by +2.39 pp in Dice_mean (81.63 vs. 79.24) and CM-Global [20] by +1.50 pp (81.63 vs. 80.13). Notably, the narrow Dice_max–Dice_match gap (92.65 vs. 90.00 on LIDC) indicates that personalized predictions are genuinely tailored to each rater rather than being random samples that happen to match one annotation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Probabilistic U-Net backbone by inserting two cooperating modules: a Noise Harmonizer that performs data-driven affine modulation to suppress scanner-specific artifacts before latent sampling, and High-Frequency Prompt Modules that decompose features via Haar wavelet transforms to encode rater-specific stylistic biases in the spectral domain. It departs from prior personalized segmentation work (D-Persona, DiffOSeg, TAB) which conditions on rater identity through spatial feature modulation or learned latent codes, by explicitly separating acquisition noise from annotator variability and operating personalization in the frequency domain. The GED-based distributional alignment loss follows the Probabilistic U-Net and PHiSeg lineage but is applied jointly with the harmonization penalty to regularize the latent space toward the empirical annotation manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-phase training strategy (harmonize first, then personalize) offers a clean inductive bias that could be adopted by other multi-rater or multi-domain segmentation frameworks, and the frequency-domain personalization provides an interpretable handle on what distinguishes rater styles (edge sharpness, texture sensitivity). However, the evaluation is limited to two 2D-patch datasets with exactly four raters each, the ablation is deferred to supplementary material, and the Dice improvements over D-Persona (~1.6 pp on LIDC, ~1.2 pp on NPC-170) are meaningful but modest. Open questions include whether the wavelet-based decomposition generalizes to 3D volumetric segmentation, to more than four raters, or to modalities where rater differences are not primarily high-frequency (e.g., low-contrast soft-tissue MRI).
