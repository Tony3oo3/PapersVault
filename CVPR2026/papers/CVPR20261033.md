## Problem

Existing synthetic data generation methods for CLIP training focus on scaling data volume but produce captions that are uniform, single-perspective, and semantically repetitive, failing to capture the multifaceted nature of visual content. This semantic impoverishment constrains the diversity and discriminative capacity of learned vision-language representations, creating a quality bottleneck that cannot be resolved by simply generating more pairs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261033/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes 1M RGB images (resized to 224×224) from ShareGPT4V without paired captions and produces multi-perspective synthetic captions: long captions averaging 92.4 words and short captions averaging 17.7 words, each generated under a distinct expert role persona. The text encoder is extended from 77 to 248 tokens via positional embedding interpolation to accommodate the dense long-form captions. A distilled Qwen2.5-VL-3B filter (trained from GPT-5 teacher signals) validates each caption against both the image content and the assigned role's responsibilities before inclusion.

## Method

Role-SynthCLIP operates as a three-stage pipeline: (1) an LLM generates a set of specialized expert roles (Observer, Interpreter, Compositional Analyst, Narrative Setter, Emotional Responder), each with defined specialty and responsibilities; (2) Qwen2.5-VL-7B generates captions for each image under every role persona, producing semantically complementary descriptions from distinct cognitive perspectives; (3) a Role-Aware Filter, fine-tuned via multi-task knowledge distillation from GPT-5, scores each (image, caption, role) triple for visual fidelity and role-consistency, pruning low-quality pairs. For training, the paper adopts Long-CLIP's positional embedding extension (77→248 tokens) and introduces a multi-positive contrastive loss that treats all captions belonging to the same image as valid positives, avoiding the false-negative penalty that a standard one-hot objective would impose when multiple captions of one image co-occur in a batch.

## Results

On zero-shot retrieval (Recall@1), Role-SynthCLIP with CLIP-B/16 trained on only 1M synthetic pairs achieves an average of 77.01% across MS COCO, Flickr-1k, and Urban-1k, with a headline 64.1% I-to-T on MS COCO. For CLIP-L/14, the average rises to 80.43%. On zero-shot classification (Top-1 accuracy), Role-SynthCLIP B/16 reaches 69.62% average across five benchmarks, with the best OOD result on ImageNet-O (44.5%), while L/14 attains 73.56% average and the best ImageNet-O score (36.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Role-SynthCLIP (1M pairs) outperforms all six baselines on average retrieval Recall@1. Against the closest same-scale competitor, FIX-CLIP (1M), it gains +4.76 points (77.01 vs 72.25), and even surpasses FIX-CLIP trained on 5× more data (5M, 75.95) by +1.06 points. Compared to Long-CLIP (1M), the improvement is +7.48 points on average, with the largest gap on Urban-1k I-to-T (96.3 vs 79.0). Against fully synthetic baselines, Role-SynthCLIP beats SynthCLIP-20M by +11.48 points and SigLIP (12B) by +13.48 points, demonstrating that curated multi-perspective diversity far exceeds raw data volume. On zero-shot classification, Role-SynthCLIP B/16 (69.62% avg) is slightly below the original CLIP (70.30%) and Long-CLIP (70.28%) on in-distribution sets, but achieves the best OOD accuracy on ImageNet-O (44.5% vs 42.7% for Long-CLIP and 40.4% for CLIP), indicating stronger generalization from multi-perspective captions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the synthetic-data-for-CLIP line (SynthCLIP, FIX-CLIP, LaCLIP) by replacing generic or entity-balanced prompts with multi-expert role-playing prompts that steer an MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL) to produce captions from distinct cognitive perspectives (compositional, narrative, emotional, etc.). It builds directly on Long-CLIP's positional-embedding extension and training recipe, and borrows the role-play prompting paradigm well established in text-only LLMs, applying it here for the first time to multimodal caption synthesis. A secondary methodological contribution is a multi-positive contrastive loss motivated by a birthday-paradox collision analysis, and a knowledge-distilled role-aware filter (GPT-5 teacher → Qwen2.5-VL-3B student) that validates both visual fidelity and role consistency.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central practical takeaway is that semantically diverse, role-conditioned captions on 1M images can outperform 5M-pair baselines on image-to-text retrieval, suggesting a quality-over-volume path for resource-constrained CLIP training. However, the acknowledged T2I gap on COCO (43.2% vs. 47.0% for 5M FIX-CLIP) reveals a text-encoder bottleneck that limits the method's transfer to generation tasks, and the evaluation is confined to a single 1M-image training corpus (ShareGPT4V), leaving open whether the advantage persists at larger scales or with different MLLM captioners. The role-playing framework is training-free and modular, making it easy to adopt, but its gains depend on the MLLM's instruction-following quality and the filter's calibration.
