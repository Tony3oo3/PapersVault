## Problem

Existing unified multimodal models (UMMs) face a fundamental tension in visual representation design: decoupled encoders for understanding and generation introduce format mismatches (differing spatial/temporal compression and channel dimensions) and require costly MoE-style routing, while existing unified approaches like Show-o2's late-fusion produce representations biased toward semantic features, degrading generation quality. No prior native UMM achieves strong performance on both understanding and generation within a single balanced representation space.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261878/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

TUNA accepts RGB images (base resolution 512×512), video clips, and text prompts/questions as input. The 3D causal VAE encoder downsamples spatially by 16× and temporally by 4×, and for video the representation encoder processes frames in 4-frame windows via batch-dimension reshaping to avoid excessively long sequences. Outputs include autoregressive text responses for understanding, flow-matching-generated images or videos for generation, and edited images for instruction-driven editing tasks, all produced from the same unified visual token space.

## Method

TUNA constructs its unified visual representation by cascading a Wan 2.2 3D causal VAE encoder with a SigLIP2 representation encoder: VAE latents are optionally noised (t sampled in [0,1] for generation, fixed at t=1 for understanding), then passed through a modified SigLIP2 (with a randomly initialized 1×1 patch embedding replacing the original 16×16 layer to match token lengths) and a two-layer MLP connector. These unified visual tokens are concatenated with text tokens and processed by a Qwen2.5 LLM decoder that performs autoregressive next-token prediction for text and a flow-matching head (with AdaLN Zero timestep conditioning and 3D-RoPE) for visual generation. Training follows a three-stage pipeline: (1) pretraining the representation encoder and flow matching head on image captioning and text-to-image generation with the LLM frozen, (2) full-model continued pretraining adding instruction-following, editing, and video-captioning data, and (3) supervised fine-tuning at reduced learning rate. The key design insight is that extracting semantic features directly from VAE latents via a strong pretrained representation encoder yields a single balanced representation optimized jointly for both tasks, avoiding the semantic bias of late-fusion strategies and the format conflicts of decoupled encoders.

## Results

On multimodal understanding, TUNA-7B achieves 61.2% on MMStar, 74.3 on OCRBench, and 85.8 on ChartQA, setting new state-of-the-art among native UMMs and surpassing the larger BAGEL-14B on most benchmarks. For image generation, TUNA-7B scores 0.90 on GenEval and 86.76 on DPG-Bench, outperforming Show-o2-7B (0.76 / 86.14) and Janus-Pro-7B (0.80 / 84.19). The 1.5B variant also leads its scale, reaching 0.88 on GenEval and 84.06 on VBench for video generation. On image editing (ImgEdit-Bench), TUNA-7B attains an overall score of 4.31, the highest among all UMMs and competitive with dedicated editing models like Qwen-Image (4.27).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On understanding, TUNA-7B outperforms Show-o2-7B by 4.6 points on MMStar (61.2 vs. 56.6) and by 41.9 points on OCRBench (74.3 vs. 32.4), while also surpassing the 14B BAGEL on OCRBench (74.3 vs. 73.3) and ChartQA (85.8 vs. 78.5). At the 1.5B scale, TUNA leads Show-o2-1.5B by 11.2 points on MMStar (54.6 vs. 43.4) and by 47.4 points on OCRBench (71.9 vs. 24.5). For image generation, TUNA-7B achieves 0.90 on GenEval, beating Show-o2-7B (0.76) and Janus-Pro-7B (0.80) while matching Mogao-7B (0.89); the 1.5B variant (0.88) surpasses all 1.5B-scale UMMs including Show-o2 (0.73) and Harmon (0.76). On DPG-Bench, TUNA-7B (86.76) edges out Show-o2-7B (86.14) and BAGEL-14B (85.07). For video generation on VBench, TUNA-1.5B (84.06) surpasses Show-o2-1.5B (81.34) and even the generation-only CogVideoX-5B (81.61). On image editing, TUNA-7B (4.31) outperforms all UMM baselines (best: GPT-Image-1 at 4.20) and is competitive with dedicated editors Qwen-Image (4.27) and FLUX.1 Kontext (4.00).

## Relation to Prior Work

TUNA extends the native unified multimodal model (UMM) line initiated by Show-o and Show-o2, which introduced single-transformer architectures for joint understanding and generation. It departs from Show-o2's dual-path late-fusion strategy (merging separate VAE and SigLIP features via a fusion layer) by instead cascading a VAE encoder directly into a representation encoder, producing a single unified representation through deep feature interaction across all encoder layers. It also contrasts with decoupled-encoder UMMs (BAGEL, Mogao, Janus-Pro) that use MoE-style routing to handle incompatible representation formats, arguing that a single shared space avoids format mismatches and reduces parameter overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The CKNNA alignment analysis provides a mechanistic explanation for why late-fusion unified representations underperform: Show-o2's fused features correlate strongly with its understanding branch (CKNNA=0.45) but negligibly with its generation branch (CKNNA=0.07), revealing an implicit semantic bias that limits generation quality. The finding that joint training on understanding and generation data yields mutual benefit rather than interference is well-supported by ablations and suggests a productive direction for future UMM training recipes. Open questions include whether the 7B model (trained without video data due to compute constraints) can match the 1.5B model's video generation quality at scale, and whether the specific SigLIP2 choice generalizes to even stronger or differently-structured representation encoders.
