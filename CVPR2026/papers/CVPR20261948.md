## Problem

Despite decades of progress, optical flow estimation remains framed as a discrete correspondence problem that recovers pixel displacements between adjacent frames without modeling the underlying continuous motion dynamics. This discrete paradigm detaches motion from the physical velocity fields that generate it, limiting temporal consistency and interpretability. The paper asks whether optical flow can be reformulated as a continuous transport process governed by a learned time-dependent velocity field, analogous to Flow Matching in generative modeling.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261948/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of RGB images (I1, I2) and produces a dense 2D optical flow field as the displacement between final and initial pixel coordinates. Unconventionally, the network operates in coordinate space rather than feature space: each pixel's initial coordinate is perturbed with a scaled Gaussian offset (x0 = xi + α·ε), and the flow is recovered by integrating a learned velocity field via Euler ODE steps (default K=3) to evolve the perturbed coordinate to its target position. The architecture is a RAFT-style recurrent decoder augmented with time and coordinate inputs, using TwinsSVT encoders and parameter-free global matching for the coarse reference flow.

## Method

OFM reframes optical flow as continuous coordinate transport using conditional optimal transport with linear interpolation paths (xt = t·x1 + (1−t)·x0), where the network learns a time-dependent velocity field V_θ^OF(xt, t | I1, I2). The key innovation is Triangle Velocities Synergy (TVS), which introduces a learnable reference point xl and two auxiliary trajectories, decomposing the Flow Matching velocity into a triangle of three constant vectors so that the network is supervised with standard optical flow ground truth (f_gt) rather than the abstract 'flow minus noise' target. During training, the proxy velocity v_t(yt|x1) = x1 − xi is directly regressed against f_gt; during inference, the Euler ODE solver computes the true transport velocity as v_t = v̂_t − v̄_t using the TVS triangle relation. The approach is architecture-agnostic, demonstrated on RAFT and SKFlow backbones, and requires tuning only a single hyperparameter (scale factor α=10).

## Results

On Sintel (train), OFM achieves the best EPE of 0.81 (clean) and 2.16 (final), outperforming FlowDiffuser (0.86/2.19) and DPFlow (1.02/2.26). On KITTI-15 (train), OFM sets new records with EPE 3.32 and Fl-all 10.9%, beating DPFlow (3.37/11.1%) and FlowDiffuser (3.61/11.8%). On the Sintel online test, OFM reaches EPE 0.94 (clean) and 1.85 (final), surpassing SEA-RAFT(L) by 28.5% and DPFlow by 8.4%. On the Spring online test (zero-shot), OFM achieves best-in-class 1px 3.660, EPE 0.468, Fl 1.477, and WAUC 94.462, outperforming all two-frame methods and remaining competitive with multi-frame approaches.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OFM achieves the best average rank (1.1) across all Sintel and KITTI splits, winning on 6 of 7 reported metrics. The closest competitor is DPFlow (CVPR'25), which OFM outperforms on Sintel train (0.81 vs 1.02 clean EPE), Sintel test (0.94 vs 1.04 clean EPE), and KITTI train (3.32 vs 3.37 EPE), but DPFlow edges out OFM on KITTI test Fl-all (3.56 vs 3.78). FlowDiffuser (CVPR'24), the prior best two-frame method, is surpassed by OFM on every Sintel and KITTI train metric, with the largest gap on Sintel clean EPE (0.81 vs 0.86) and KITTI Fl-all (10.9% vs 11.8%). On the Spring benchmark (zero-shot), OFM beats the best two-frame method DPFlow by 14.2% on 1px (3.660 vs 4.277) and 7.8% on Fl (1.477 vs 1.602), while remaining within 1.7% of the best multi-frame method MEMFOF on Fl (1.477 vs 1.353) despite using only two frames. The ablation confirms that the TVS strategy is essential: OFM-Naive (direct Flow Matching objective) fails to converge (EPE 15.73 on Sintel clean), while OFM-TVS with 3 NFE steps achieves the full performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the continuous transport framework of Flow Matching (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) into optical flow estimation, departing from the discrete correspondence paradigm that has dominated the field since PWC-Net and RAFT. Architecturally, it builds on RAFT-style iterative refinement and borrows the time-conditioned decoder design from FlowDiffuser (Luo et al., CVPR 2024) and the parameter-free global matching from GMFlow (Xu et al., CVPR 2022) for coarse flow estimation. The key conceptual departure is the Triangle Velocities Synergy (TVS) mechanism, which resolves a fundamental incompatibility: the raw Flow Matching objective (predicting flow minus Gaussian noise) is physically meaningless to standard optical flow architectures, and TVS re-expresses the target velocity through a geometric triangle decomposition so that the network is supervised with standard flow ground truth while preserving the continuous transport structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By reframing optical flow as continuous coordinate transport governed by a learned velocity field, OFM offers a physically grounded alternative to displacement regression that could unify motion estimation with broader dynamical modeling (video generation, scene flow, motion prediction). The TVS mechanism is a practical and general bridge between generative transport theory and discriminative flow architectures, demonstrated by plug-and-play compatibility with RAFT and SKFlow. However, the current implementation uses a classical linear OT path and a first-order Euler solver, both acknowledged as suboptimal; the authors point to MeanFlow and Shortcut Models as natural next steps for reducing NFE. Open questions include whether the continuous formulation provides genuine advantages in multi-frame or video settings beyond the two-frame scope tested here, and whether the approach extends to 3D scene flow or event-based flow.
