## Problem

Scaling 3D visual geometry reconstruction models is significantly harder than in language or 2D vision due to the complexity of geometric supervision and the heterogeneous nature of 3D data spanning indoor, outdoor, object-centric, human-centric, and dynamic scenes. Existing feed-forward 3D reconstruction models (e.g., VGGT, Pi3, Fast3R) rely on a single shared representation for all scenarios, limiting their scalability and generalization across diverse 3D domains.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260666/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

MoRE takes a sequence of N unposed RGB images (no camera calibration required) as input and produces dense per-pixel 3D predictions in a single forward pass: a 3×H×W pointmap, an H×W depth map, 9-dimensional camera parameters (intrinsics + extrinsics), a C×H×W tracking feature grid, and a 3×H×W surface normal map per frame. Unlike optimization-based pipelines, the model is fully feed-forward, and unlike pairwise methods (DUSt3R, MASt3R), it avoids a separate global alignment stage for multi-view consistency.

## Method

MoRE builds on the VGGT dense visual transformer backbone and introduces a Mixture-of-Experts layer that replaces the feed-forward sub-networks: a lightweight linear router assigns each token to the top-K experts (initialized by replicating VGGT's alternating global/frame attention FFNs), enabling domain-specific specialization without proportional compute increase, regularized by a differentiable load-balancing loss. A confidence-based depth refinement module uses MoGe-2 predictions to compute per-pixel confidence masks that filter noisy depth supervision, adding a prior-guided gradient loss restricted to high-confidence regions. For normal estimation, globally aligned 3D backbone features are concatenated with DINOv2 dense semantic features before DPT heads, recovering fine geometric detail lost in multi-view smoothing. Training proceeds in two stages (multi-task pretraining, then MoE fine-tuning) with a composite loss over pointmap, camera, depth, tracking, local-point, point-normal, and predicted-normal terms, plus an adaptive k-sigma loss clipping mechanism to stabilize optimization against annotation noise.

## Results

On pointmap estimation, MoRE achieves the best Accuracy across all four benchmarks: 1.011 on DTU, 0.234 on ETH3D, 0.015 on 7-Scenes, and 0.012 on NRGBD, outperforming the closest competitor Pi3 (1.198, 0.194, 0.015, 0.015). For camera pose estimation, MoRE sets a new state of the art on RealEstate10K with AUC@30 of 86.28 (vs. 85.90 for Pi3) and achieves the lowest ATE (0.010) and RPE rot (0.305) on TUM-Dynamics. On normal estimation, MoRE reduces mean angular error to 15.1° on NYUv2, 16.1° on ScanNet, and 15.0° on IBims-1, surpassing the previous best (StableNormal) by 2–4.6° across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoRE outperforms all feed-forward 3D reconstruction baselines (Fast3R, CUT3R, FLARE, VGGT, Pi3) on pointmap accuracy across every dataset, with the largest gains over Fast3R (3.3× lower DTU Acc.) and CUT3R (4.7× lower DTU Acc.). Against the closest competitor Pi3, MoRE improves DTU Accuracy from 1.198 to 1.011 and ETH3D Accuracy from 0.194 to 0.234 (lower is better), while also achieving higher Normal Consistency on 7-Scenes (0.694 vs. 0.687) and NRGBD (0.907 vs. 0.898). On monocular depth, MoRE matches or slightly exceeds the dedicated monocular model MoGe (Abs Rel 0.274 vs. 0.273 on Sintel; 0.051 vs. 0.055 on NYUv2) while remaining competitive with Pi3. For camera pose, MoRE achieves the best AUC@30 on RealEstate10K (86.28 vs. 85.90 for Pi3) and the lowest trajectory errors on TUM-Dynamics (ATE 0.010, RPE rot 0.305), beating VGGT (0.012, 0.311) and Pi3 (0.014, 0.312). On surface normal estimation, MoRE dominates all diffusion-based baselines (Marigold, Lotus, GeoWizard, StableNormal) by 2–6° in mean angular error, with the largest margin over GeoWizard (15.1° vs. 20.4° on NYUv2).

## Relation to Prior Work

MoRE extends the feed-forward 3D reconstruction lineage (DUSt3R → MASt3R → Fast3R → VGGT) by introducing a Mixture-of-Experts architecture into the geometric prediction pipeline, drawing on the MoE scaling paradigm established in LLMs (DeepSeek-MoE, Switch Transformers, Qwen series). It builds directly on VGGT's dense visual transformer backbone and task heads, augmenting them with MoGe-2-based confidence filtering for depth supervision and DINOv2 dense semantic features for normal estimation. The key conceptual departure is treating 3D geometry reconstruction as a domain-diverse problem (indoor, outdoor, object, human, dynamic) where expert specialization can replace a single shared representation, mirroring how MoE addresses data heterogeneity in language modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MoRE addresses a practical bottleneck in 3D geometry: scaling model capacity without proportional compute cost, which is especially challenging given the heterogeneous nature of 3D training data and the complexity of geometric supervision. The confidence-based depth refinement and dense semantic fusion are straightforward but effective techniques that practitioners can adopt independently of the MoE component. Open questions remain about whether the learned expert specializations genuinely correspond to meaningful domain partitions (the paper does not provide expert-activation visualizations), and whether the MoE gains persist at larger scales or with more experts. The approach also inherits VGGT's reliance on large-scale annotated 3D data, limiting accessibility.
