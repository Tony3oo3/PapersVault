## Problem

Existing multimodal benchmarks evaluate poster-related capabilities in a largely text-centric manner, prioritizing generic aesthetics while overlooking the compositional, constraint-driven nature of real poster design. No prior benchmark systematically assesses both the understanding side (OCR robustness, font perception, layout reasoning, style/intention interpretation) and the generation side (dense content, typography, composition-aware synthesis, metaphor-driven creation) under unified, design-theory-grounded metrics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260611/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The understanding module consumes 7,765 annotated poster images (real, professionally designed, and synthetic) alongside structured queries: font-rendered text pairs for attribute matching, 7×7 grid-divided posters for empty-space perception, and textual layout specifications (≤5 elements with relative sizes/placements) for layout generation. Bounding-box outputs are normalized to [0,1] to handle varying resolutions. The generation module takes 822 text prompts specifying themes, target styles (from 17 curated categories), compositional operations (misalignment, nesting, cutouts, etc.), or metaphorical intentions, and produces full poster images. Evaluation outputs include word-level recall for OCR, IoU for spatial tasks, multiple-choice scores in [0,1] for font/style tasks, point-coverage scores for composition/intention, and cosine similarity against human ratings for the holistic quality task.

## Method

PosterIQ is structured as a decoupled, multi-task benchmark spanning 24 task types across two coupled modules. The understanding module decomposes poster cognition into five families—OCR (logo, real-world, simple, hard, multi-size), font perception (matching, 37-attribute perception, traditional/advanced effect recognition), spatial reasoning (localization, alignment/rotation, empty-space, layout comparison, layout generation), advanced visual design (17-style classification, composition-structure description, metaphor/intention interpretation), and overall rating—each with task-specific metrics (word-level recall, IoU, normalized multiple-choice scores, point-coverage, cosine similarity). The generation module probes five axes: dense multi-entity rendering, font diversity, style fidelity, composition-aware synthesis, and intention/metaphor communication, evaluated by an MLLM verifier checking element presence and attribute coverage. A key design choice is the decoupling of evaluation dimensions rather than relying on a single holistic score, motivated by the finding that commercial MLLMs act as insensitive automatic raters (low correlation with human judgments) while excelling at fine-grained sub-tasks. The benchmark further validates its soundness through an iterative VLM-supervised T2I loop, demonstrating that stronger understanding directly improves generated poster quality without human design hints.

## Results

On the PosterIQ understanding benchmark (7,765 instances, 24 task types), Gemini-2.5-Pro achieves the highest overall score of 0.647, followed by GPT-5 (0.622) and Claude-Sonnet-4.5 (0.601), while the best open-source model (Qwen3-VL-8B) reaches only 0.543. For generation (822 prompts), Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image leads with an average of 0.626 across five tasks, with Composition Generation being the strongest sub-task (up to 0.866) and Font Generation the clear bottleneck (best 0.391). Overall Rating correlation with human judgments is low across all models (best: Grok-4-fast at 0.465; Gemini-2.5-Pro at 0.399), confirming that commercial MLLMs are insensitive as automatic poster quality raters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among closed-source MLLMs, Gemini-2.5-Pro outperforms GPT-5 and Claude-Sonnet-4.5 on the aggregated understanding score (0.647 vs. 0.622 vs. 0.601), driven by superior Layout (0.571 vs. 0.511 vs. 0.453) and Advanced Understanding (0.806 vs. 0.801 vs. 0.727) scores, while GPT-5 leads on Font Matching (0.668) and Intention Understanding (0.824). All open-source models (Qwen3-VL-8B at 0.543, Qwen3-VL-4B at 0.538, Gemma-3n at 0.486, MiniCPM-V-4.5 at 0.466) trail the top closed models by 0.10–0.18, with the largest gaps in Font perception (best open: 0.480 vs. best closed: 0.615) and Layout reasoning. Grok-4-fast is a clear outlier among closed models, scoring 0.475 on OCR and 0.313 on Layout, roughly 0.35–0.26 below its peers. On the generation side, Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image (0.626) edges out Seedream-4.0 (0.609) and GPT-Image-1 (0.593), with Qwen-Image last (0.552); GPT-Image-1 leads Style (0.633) and Intention (0.670) but lags on Dense (0.508) and Font (0.299), while Seedream-4.0 is strongest on Dense (0.618) and Composition (0.848). Font Generation remains the universal bottleneck—no model exceeds 0.391—highlighting a persistent gap in typographic fidelity across all four generators.

## Relation to Prior Work

PosterIQ carves new ground at the intersection of MLLM evaluation and generative image assessment by adopting an explicit visual-design perspective that prior benchmarks lack. General multimodal suites (MMBench, Creation-MMBench, Seed-Bench-2-Plus) and OCR-focused benchmarks (OCRBench, OCRBench V2) treat text recognition and scene understanding in isolation, while image-generation benchmarks (GenEval, ELLA/DPG, CVTG-2K, LongTextBench) prioritize aesthetic quality or text fidelity without modeling compositional constraints. Poster-specific generation methods (PosterLLaVA, COLE, VASCAR, FontCLIP, DreamPoster) and ideation benchmarks (DesignProbe, LiveIdeaBench) address individual pipeline stages but do not provide a unified, decoupled evaluation spanning understanding and generation under design-theory-informed criteria (typographic hierarchy, spatial balance, metaphor, visual rhetoric). PosterIQ unifies these threads into a single benchmark with 24 task types, explicitly positioning itself as more challenging and closer to real-world design practice than any prior evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes systematic, previously unmeasured gaps: frontier MLLMs that excel at high-level reasoning are surprisingly insensitive as automatic quality raters (cosine similarity with human ratings only 0.35–0.40), and current generators render text well but fail at composition-aware synthesis and typographic diversity (font scores cap at 0.391 while composition reaches 0.866). The iterative VLM-supervised generation loop demonstrates a practical paradigm where understanding directly improves generation without human design hints, suggesting a viable pipeline for constrained creative tasks. Open questions include whether the global-local tradeoff (strong composition vs. weak font) can be resolved with targeted supervision, how multilingual and low-contrast scripts will behave, and whether decoupled design dimensions can be learned jointly rather than evaluated in isolation.
