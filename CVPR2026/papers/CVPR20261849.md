## Problem

CLIP's dense features suffer from weak cross-modal alignment and poor feature coherence, yet existing self-distillation methods that bridge global cls-token semantics to the dense level introduce noise (via residual connections in CLIPSelf) or semantic contamination from irrelevant patches (via spatial aggregation in DeCLIP). The fundamental question of how to construct dense representations that are intrinsically consistent with CLIP's global embedding space remains largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261849/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

During distillation, RGB images are resized to 1024x1024 and partitioned into m x n grids (m, n randomly sampled from {1,...,6}) to generate local alignment targets from the frozen cls token of corresponding crops. The resulting dense representations (N x D feature maps) are then plugged into downstream OVDP pipelines: F-ViT for detection (producing bounding boxes with class labels) and CAT-Seg for segmentation (producing pixel-level masks), without modifying any downstream hyperparameters. Text prompts define the open vocabulary at inference time.

## Method

DenseRC constructs cls-compatible dense features by summing value embeddings from the last three Transformer layers, motivated by the finding that the cls token fundamentally aggregates multi-layer value information. A lightweight Head-Selective Gating (HSG) module, implemented as a single shared linear projection followed by sigmoid, adaptively reweights each attention head token-wise based on its cross-modal semantic contribution and locality preference, replacing spatial aggregation which the paper proves introduces a strictly positive interference term in the alignment loss. Feature coherence is enforced by distilling patch-wise correlations from a frozen DINOv2 model directly on the same dense feature stream, unifying semantic alignment and spatial structure in a single optimization objective (L_total = L_semantics + lambda * L_coherence, lambda=0.025). Training uses AdamW (lr=1e-5, weight decay=0.1) for 6 epochs on COCO train2017 with cosine-similarity alignment loss and MSE coherence loss.

## Results

On OV-COCO with ViT-B/16, DenseRC achieves 45.6 AP Novel50, outperforming the previous best DeCLIP (41.1) by 4.5 points and R-SC-CLIPSelf (40.9) by 4.7 points. With ViT-L/14, the gap widens to 8.6 points over DeCLIP (54.8 vs. 46.2). On OV-LVIS (ViT-L/14), DenseRC reaches 39.6 mAPr, surpassing DeCLIP and R-SC-CLIPSelf (both 37.2) by 2.4 points. On open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (ViT-L/14, CAT-Seg), DenseRC attains 18.4/26.7/41.5/64.0 mIoU on A-847/PC-459/A-150/PC-59, setting new state-of-the-art across all four benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DenseRC consistently outperforms all self-distillation baselines (CLIPSelf, R-SC-CLIPSelf, DeCLIP) across every benchmark and backbone size. On OV-COCO (ViT-B/16), it surpasses DeCLIP by 4.5 AP and CLIPSelf by 8.0 AP; on the larger ViT-L/14 backbone the margin over DeCLIP grows to 8.6 AP. On OV-LVIS (ViT-L/14), DenseRC leads both DeCLIP and R-SC-CLIPSelf (tied at 37.2) by 2.4 mAPr. In open-vocabulary semantic segmentation, DenseRC beats DeCLIP by 0.6–1.3 mIoU across A-847, PC-459, A-150, and PC-59 with ViT-B/16, and by 0.8–1.0 mIoU with ViT-L/14. Region-supervised methods such as FineCLIP (which requires 40M curated region-text pairs) are substantially outperformed—e.g., 45.6 vs. 29.8 AP on OV-COCO ViT-B/16—demonstrating that DenseRC's self-distillation approach achieves superior dense alignment without any additional annotation. Cross-dataset transfer (LVIS→COCO/Objects365) further confirms generalization, with DenseRC leading DeCLIP by 2.4 AP on COCO and 0.8 AP on Objects365.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-distillation paradigm introduced by CLIPSelf (Wu et al., 2024) and refined by DeCLIP (Wang et al., 2025) and R-SC-CLIPSelf (Qiu et al., 2025), which transfer CLIP's global cls-token semantics to dense features without additional supervision. The key departure is a principled re-examination of *how* dense features should be constructed: rather than using final spatial outputs (CLIPSelf) or self-self attention aggregation (DeCLIP), DenseRC identifies multi-layer value embeddings as the semantically consistent basis and replaces spatial attention with a lightweight Head-Selective Gating module. Feature coherence is enforced via DINOv2 distillation (Oquab et al., 2023) applied directly to the same dense feature stream, unifying alignment and coherence in a single objective rather than the decoupled losses used by DeCLIP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that spatial aggregation provably increases alignment error (Eq. 12) challenges a natural design assumption in prior self-distillation methods and provides a clear guideline for future dense feature construction. The HSG module is parameter-efficient (a single shared linear layer) and the multi-layer value embedding insight could transfer to other VLMs beyond CLIP (e.g., SigLIP, EVA-CLIP). Open questions include whether the head-heterogeneity finding generalizes to non-CLIP architectures, whether the approach extends to video or 3D dense perception, and whether the COCO-dependent distillation can be made fully annotation-free. The method remains tied to the CLIP self-distillation paradigm and does not address the fundamental limitation that CLIP was never trained for pixel-level semantics.
