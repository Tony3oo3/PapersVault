## Problem

Adversarial-example-based model fingerprinting faces an inherent trade-off: fingerprints placed near a single decision boundary achieve good uniqueness but are fragile to model modifications (fine-tuning, pruning, etc.), while fingerprints pushed deep inside class regions gain robustness at the cost of uniqueness. The paper asks whether a fingerprinting method can simultaneously enhance both properties.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260698/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a protected DNN model and clean input images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100/Fashion-MNIST/MNIST, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) and produces composite-sample fingerprints, where each fingerprint is a set of T+1 perturbed samples (one seed plus T variants) each paired with a target label, rather than a single input-output pair. Verification outputs a binary ownership decision via a two-step process: per-fingerprint matching rates are compared against individually computed thresholds, then aggregated across all K retained fingerprints against a global decision threshold α.

## Method

IrisFP generates fingerprints in three phases. First, fingerprint seeds are optimized to reside near the intersection of all C-class decision boundaries by minimizing KL divergence toward a biased target distribution (target class gets 1/C + τ, others share the remainder evenly), which increases the prediction margin without placing samples deep inside class regions. Second, each seed is jointly perturbed into T variants assigned to different random target classes, forming a composite-sample fingerprint whose collective behavior pattern is highly model-specific yet difficult for independently-trained models to replicate. Third, a refinement step computes Cohen's d effect size over matching-rate distributions on reference pirated and independent model sets to select the top-K most discriminative fingerprints, and assigns each a threshold as a variance-weighted average of the two group means. Ownership verification then computes per-fingerprint matching rates, compares them to their individual thresholds, and aggregates the binary outcomes into a final verification score.

## Results

IrisFP achieves the highest AUC across all three protected model architectures (ResNet-18, MobileNet-V2, ViT-B/16) and five datasets. On CIFAR-100, IrisFP reaches 0.916 AUC (ResNet-18) and 0.937 AUC (MobileNet-V2), improving over the best baselines by 13.7% and 8.1% respectively. On Fashion-MNIST with MobileNet-V2, IrisFP attains 0.963 AUC, the highest value in the entire table. For the larger ViT-B/16 architecture on Tiny-ImageNet, IrisFP still leads with 0.887 AUC versus 0.832 for the best baseline (ADV-TRA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IrisFP outperforms all four baselines (IPGuard, UAP, ADV-TRA, AKH) on every dataset and architecture combination in Table 1. The largest margins appear on CIFAR-100: IrisFP (0.916) beats ADV-TRA (0.806) by 13.7% and AKH (0.785) by 16.7% under ResNet-18. IPGuard is consistently the weakest baseline, dropping to 0.471 AUC on MNIST (ResNet-18), below random. ADV-TRA shows strong robustness (high TPR) but poor uniqueness (low TNR), while UAP shows the opposite pattern—stable uniqueness but limited robustness, especially on Fashion-MNIST. AKH is inconsistent, performing well on some datasets (e.g., 0.820 on MNIST) but failing on others (e.g., 0.797 on Fashion-MNIST with MobileNet-V2). IrisFP is the only method that simultaneously achieves high TNR and high TPR across all evaluated settings, as confirmed by the TNR-FNR and TPR-FPR curves in Figure 5.

## Relation to Prior Work

IrisFP builds directly on the multi-boundary intersection concept introduced by IBSF [1] and SDBF [2] for DNN tampering detection, repurposing it for the distinct task of ownership verification against model modification attacks. It departs from the dominant adversarial-example fingerprinting lineage (IPGuard, UAP, ADV-TRA, AKH), which places individual fingerprints near a single decision boundary, by instead positioning seeds at the intersection of all class boundaries and expanding each seed into a composite-sample fingerprint with diverse variants. The fingerprint refinement stage—using Cohen's d separability and per-fingerprint thresholds—represents a further departure, as prior methods apply a single global threshold and do not explicitly assess discriminative power against both pirated and independently-trained reference sets during construction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IrisFP addresses a long-standing trade-off in adversarial-example fingerprinting: methods that maximize robustness (by placing fingerprints deep inside class regions) sacrifice uniqueness, and vice versa. The demonstrated ability to achieve high TNR and TPR simultaneously across five datasets and three architectures (including ViT-B/16) makes it a practically stronger baseline for model IP protection. However, performance still degrades notably under knowledge distillation and adversarial training (AUCs dropping to ~0.5–0.7), and the method requires constructing reference model sets during fingerprint generation, which may be impractical in some deployment scenarios. Open questions include whether the composite-sample and multi-boundary strategies extend to non-classification tasks (e.g., segmentation, generation) and to larger-scale models where boundary geometry is more complex.
