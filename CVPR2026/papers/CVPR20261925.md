## Problem

LiDAR-Camera Cross-modal Gait Recognition (LCCGR) must bridge the substantial modality gap between 2D silhouette sequences and 3D point-cloud sequences, yet existing approaches either rely on synthetic pre-training (introducing domain bias), use learnable prototypes (lacking generalization), or employ black-box disentanglement (unreliable and uninterpretable). No prior method provides a principled, semantically grounded mechanism to isolate modality-shared gait identity cues from modality-specific artifacts across these heterogeneous sensors.

## Input / Output

The method consumes preprocessed 2D gait silhouettes and 3D LiDAR depth maps (both resized to 64×64, sampled as s-frame sequences) alongside a pre-constructed Gait Modality Text Dictionary (GMTD) of m×8×l LLM-generated descriptions covering two modalities, eight viewpoints, and l multi-turn augmentation rounds. At inference, the text dictionary is frozen and only the visual sequence is fed through the network; the output is a set of n_p part-based gait embeddings (each d-dimensional) used for cross-modal retrieval, with the internal representation explicitly decomposed into a modality-shared component and a modality-specific component.

## Method

TCFDNet projects both visual gait sequences and GMTD text entries into a shared CLIP vision-language space, then uses a Text-guided Feature Disentanglement (TFD) module that retrieves the top-k_t most semantically aligned text prototypes (via cosine similarity with the CLIP [CLS] token) to reconstruct the modality-specific component through attention-weighted fusion, yielding the modality-shared feature as a residual with an orthogonality constraint. A CLIP-based multi-grained encoder (frozen ViT + trainable ResNet-9 adapter) feeds a Multi-grained Fusion module with bidirectional multi-head cross-attention to integrate global and local spatiotemporal features, followed by a Spatial Weighting module for identity-discriminative region emphasis. A Feature Stability Enhancement (FSE) module then applies a 3×3 convolution for local spatial receptive fields and a bottleneck + 1D-convolution channel-attention to model inter-channel dependencies, strengthening the fragile shared representation. Training jointly optimizes triplet and cross-entropy losses alongside three disentanglement losses—Modality Alignment (cosine similarity to text prototypes), Modality Orthogonality (inner-product penalty), and HSIC-based statistical independence—while a Patch Exchange augmentation swaps localized spatial regions between modalities at the input level to improve cross-modal robustness.

## Results

On SUSTech1K (3D→2D), TCFDNet achieves 61.7% Rank-1 and 82.5% Rank-5 overall accuracy, surpassing the previous best (SCR) by 4.0 and 3.0 percentage points respectively. On the 2D→3D direction, TCFDNet reaches 55.9% Rank-1 and 78.1% Rank-5, edging out SCR (54.9% / 78.1%). On the in-the-wild FreeGait dataset, TCFDNet achieves 52.1% / 85.3% (2D→3D) and 57.9% / 87.2% (3D→2D) Rank-1 / Rank-5, outperforming the prior best (SCR) by over 12 percentage points in Rank-1 accuracy in both directions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TCFDNet outperforms all 10 baselines across both retrieval directions on SUSTech1K and FreeGait. The closest competitor is SCR (Information Fusion 2025), which TCFDNet beats by 4.0 pp in Rank-1 (61.7 vs 57.7) and 3.0 pp in Rank-5 (82.5 vs 79.5) on SUSTech1K 3D→2D; on 2D→3D the gap narrows to 1.0 pp in Rank-1 (55.9 vs 54.9) with a tie in Rank-5 (78.1). On FreeGait, the margin widens dramatically: TCFDNet surpasses SCR by 12.0 pp (52.1 vs 40.1) in 2D→3D Rank-1 and 14.6 pp (57.9 vs 43.3) in 3D→2D Rank-1, indicating strong generalization to unconstrained outdoor settings. Earlier cross-modal gait methods (CrossGait, CL-Gait) report only overall or partial-condition numbers and trail TCFDNet by 5–9 pp in Rank-1. Visible-infrared ReID methods adapted as baselines (IDKL, TVI-LFM, TSKD) consistently underperform TCFDNet by 4–15 pp, confirming that gait-specific text-guided disentanglement yields gains beyond generic cross-modal alignment. The only condition where TCFDNet does not lead is Night, where it scores 11.1% (3D→2D) versus SCR's 10.2%, a modest 0.9 pp advantage that the authors attribute to the compounded day-night and cross-modal domain shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CLIP's vision-language alignment framework and the OpenGait gait recognition toolkit, repurposing both for the LiDAR-Camera cross-modal gait recognition (LCCGR) task. It departs from prior LCCGR approaches—CL-Gait's synthetic-data contrastive pretraining and CrossGait's learnable shared prototypes—by introducing LLM-generated modality-specific text descriptions as explicit semantic anchors that guide feature disentanglement, rather than relying on implicit alignment or black-box disentanglement. The residual decomposition plus orthogonality/HSIC loss strategy for separating shared from specific features follows the broader disentanglement literature but is novel in being text-conditioned and applied to gait.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The text-guided disentanglement paradigm offers a more interpretable and principled alternative to prior black-box cross-modal alignment methods, and the strong FreeGait results (12-point Rank-1 gain over the previous SOTA) suggest the approach generalizes well to unconstrained outdoor settings. However, performance still degrades notably under Night conditions, revealing an unresolved day-night cross-domain gap that limits real-world deployment. An open question is whether the LLM-generated text dictionary quality and coverage constitute a bottleneck, and whether this text-anchored disentanglement strategy transfers to other cross-modal biometric tasks beyond gait.
