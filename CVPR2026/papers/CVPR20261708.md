## Problem

Most image retrieval systems rely on a fixed similarity metric that cannot adapt to user-specified conditions such as species, location, or action. Existing conditional retrieval methods that focus on specific attributes require training on paired data (limiting them to closed-set conditions) and must recompute all database embeddings whenever the condition changes, making them impractical for large-scale adaptive retrieval. The 'focus on' direction of conditional retrieval remains underexplored compared to the more studied 'change' direction (Composed Image Retrieval).

## Input / Output

The method takes a query image, a set of database images, and a free-form text condition (e.g., 'dog species', 'background', 'age'). The text condition is expanded into multiple condition-related prompts via an LLM (ChatGPT-5) using the template 'a photo of {c}', which are encoded by the VLM text encoder to form a text feature matrix. The output is a ranked list of database images scored by conditional cosine similarity. A key design property is that visual embeddings of all database images are computed once and remain fixed across conditions; only a lightweight projection matrix (precomputed per condition) changes at inference, enabling multi-condition retrieval by simply swapping or combining projection matrices.

## Method

CLAY is a training-free method that modulates the similarity computation space of a pretrained VLM (CLIP or SigLIP) into a condition-aware subspace without re-encoding visual features. Given a text condition, it generates related prompts via an LLM, encodes them with the VLM text encoder, and constructs a manifold-aware textual subspace: text features are mapped from the unit hypersphere to a local tangent space at their normalized mean via the logarithm map, then SVD is applied to extract top-k right singular vectors forming the projection matrix P_c = V_k V_k^T. At inference, an orthonormal rotation aligns the visual feature mean with the text feature mean (preserving intra-visual relationships), the rotated visual features are mapped to the same tangent space via the logarithm map, and projected onto the textual subspace with P_c. Conditional similarity is then computed as cosine similarity between the projected query and database features. This decoupling of conditioning from visual feature extraction eliminates the need to re-encode the database for each new condition, while the symmetric formulation (conditioning both query and database) yields higher accuracy than asymmetric alternatives.

## Results

On real-world single-conditional retrieval (Table 2a), CLAY with SigLIP-B achieves mAP of 66.2 (Action), 59.5 (Location), and 58.7 (Mood) on Stanford40, and an average of ~75.0 mAP across seven fine-grained datasets (Cat 82.1, Dog 84.7, Flower 92.7, Car 78.0, Aircraft 57.9, Food 66.1, Instrument 63.4), outperforming the strongest baseline InstructBLIP by 10–25 mAP points on most conditions. On synthetic datasets (Table 2b), CLAY(SigLIP-B) reaches 88.6 mAP on CLEVR-Shape and 94.3 on CLAY-Object Category. Under multi-conditional retrieval (Table 3), CLAY(CLIP-B) achieves 58.0 mAP on the full CLAY-Human condition combination, versus 44.1 for InstructBLIP and 39.5 for SigLIP-B baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CLAY consistently outperforms all eight baselines across nearly every condition and dataset. Against the primary conditional-retrieval competitor GeneCIS [47], CLAY(SigLIP-B) gains 16–65 mAP points (e.g., 82.1 vs 24.7 on Cat, 66.2 vs 50.0 on Stanford40-Action), and additionally runs in symmetric form without recomputing database features (0.126 s vs 1.657 s for GeneCIS† on the second condition). The strongest baseline, InstructBLIP [9], is the only method that approaches CLAY on CLEVR-Shape (83.4 vs 88.6) and CLAY-Human-Action (72.2 vs 80.4), but CLAY still leads by 5–30 mAP on most other conditions. CIR methods SEARLE [1] and MagicLens [56] are substantially weaker (27–30 mAP average on fine-grained), confirming that the 'change' paradigm does not transfer well to the 'focus' task. The mLLM embedding baselines Qwen3-VL-Embedding [29] and VLM2Vec [35] achieve high accuracy in symmetric form (85.3 and 81.1 mAP on fine-grained) but incur 12–14 s per query, whereas CLAY(SigLIP-L) reaches 84.3 mAP in 0.126 s—a 100× speedup. The unconditioned VLM baselines CLIP-B and SigLIP-B serve as lower bounds, showing that text-conditioned modulation adds 10–25 mAP over raw cosine similarity.

## Relation to Prior Work

CLAY extends the subspace-projection idea from IP-Composer and CSD-VAR, which use SVD on text features to define concept subspaces in VLM embedding space, by replacing the Euclidean SVD with a manifold-aware tangent-space construction that respects the hyperspherical geometry of VLM embeddings. It departs from prior conditional retrieval methods (GeneCIS, FocalLens) that couple text conditioning with visual feature extraction through a trained neural modulator, instead decoupling the two so that database visual embeddings remain fixed and only the similarity computation space is modulated. The paper also positions itself in the underexplored 'focus on' direction of conditional retrieval (as opposed to the dominant 'change' direction of Composed Image Retrieval) and extends it to multi-condition settings that prior single-condition methods do not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, no-database-recomputation design makes conditional retrieval practically deployable at scale, where prior symmetric methods incur prohibitive inference cost when conditions change. The CLAY-EVAL synthetic dataset addresses a real evaluation gap for multi-conditional retrieval, and the demonstrated rankable property along condition axes (e.g., age) suggests the modulation captures structured semantic relationships beyond mere clustering. Open questions include whether the tangent-space approximation degrades for conditions with high curvature in embedding space, how the method scales to very large databases with diverse condition vocabularies, and whether the LLM-generated prompt template ('a photo of {c}') is sufficient for all condition types or requires task-specific adaptation.
