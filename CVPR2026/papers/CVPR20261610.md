## Problem

Domain generalization for semantic segmentation typically requires fine-tuning backbone parameters, but in real-world deployments models are often frozen due to privacy, intellectual property, or security constraints, making parameter-level adaptation infeasible. Existing input-level prompting methods either require access to internal model layers or rely on a single style-fixed prompt that cannot generalize across the high diversity of unseen target domain appearances.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261610/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image (resolutions ranging from 1280×720 to 2048×1024 depending on the dataset) and produces a pixel-wise semantic segmentation mask. Unusually, the raw input image is not fed directly to the frozen SegFormer-B5 backbone; instead, a learned visual prompt—a border-shaped template with a zero-filled center—is dynamically generated and appended to the image before inference, so the model effectively sees an augmented input while its internal weights remain untouched.

## Method

SAGE operates in two phases. In Style-Prompt Generation (SPG), the source domain is augmented via image-to-image translation using n randomly sampled ImageNet style references, and a dedicated prompt generator is trained for each style; each generator combines a border-shaped learnable prompt template with a lightweight modulation network (cascaded residual blocks with global pooling) that adaptively reweights the four semantic borders based on input content. In Adaptive Prompt Fusion (APF), at inference all n generators produce L2-normalized prompts for the query image, and a cross-attention module (using a shared ImageNet-pretrained ResNet18 encoder with separate linear heads for image and prompt features) computes per-prompt attention scores, which are passed through softmax and tanh to prevent overconfident single-prompt dominance before a weighted summation yields the final fused prompt. Only the modulation networks (SPG) and the two linear attention heads (APF) are trained—totaling 1.53M parameters—while the SegFormer-B5 backbone remains fully frozen throughout.

## Results

SAGE achieves the best average mIoU among all privacy-preserving methods across three cross-domain settings: 42.09% (G→{C,B,M,S}), 43.90% (C→{B,M,G,S}), and 37.58% (S→{C,B,M,G}), using only 1.53M trainable parameters. It outperforms the privacy baseline (frozen SegFormer-B5) by 3–5% on average and surpasses the only other privacy method, A2XP, by approximately 12–14%. Notably, SAGE also exceeds the Full Fine-Tuning baseline (84.61M params) in all three settings (e.g., 42.09% vs. 39.14% on G→targets), and in the S→{C,B,M,G} setting it beats every non-privacy DGSS method (best non-privacy: SAW at 34.46%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE decisively outperforms the only other privacy-preserving method, A2XP [52], by 12.59%, 12.94%, and 10.18% average mIoU on the G→, C→, and S→ settings respectively, while using a comparable parameter budget (1.53M vs. 1.21M). Against the non-privacy DGSS baselines, SAGE trails the strongest methods (FAMix at 44.99% on G→targets, SAW at 47.09% on C→targets) but narrows the gap substantially and, in the S→{C,B,M,G} setting, actually surpasses all non-privacy methods (37.58% vs. SAW's 34.46%). SAGE also beats Full Fine-Tuning (which has 55× more trainable parameters) in every setting, with margins of 2.95%, 2.83%, and 1.60% on average mIoU. Among the normalization- and style-based methods (IBN-Net, IW, ISW, GTR, SAW), SAGE exceeds all of them on the G→ and S→ settings and is competitive on C→, where SAW leads by 3.19%. The privacy baseline (frozen model with no adaptation) is outperformed by 3–5% on average, confirming that the adaptive prompt mechanism provides meaningful gains over simply running the frozen model.

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGE sits at the intersection of input-level visual prompting (Bahng et al., who showed that perturbing the input image can steer frozen models without touching internal parameters) and style-augmentation-based domain generalization for semantic segmentation (Peng et al., SAW, which stylize source data to improve robustness). It departs from internal prompt-tuning methods (VPT, X-Prompt, SHIP) that require architectural access, and from style-fixed prompting approaches (A2XP) that fail under diverse target-domain appearances, by introducing multiple style-conditioned prompt generators fused via cross-attention. The cross-attention fusion mechanism is directly inspired by A2XP's multi-expert prompt integration, repurposed here to combine style-specific prompts rather than domain-specific ones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper formalizes a practically important but previously underexplored deployment scenario—frozen, IP-protected segmentation models that cannot be fine-tuned—and shows that input-level style-adaptive prompting with only 1.53M parameters can match or exceed full fine-tuning (84.61M parameters) across five urban-scene datasets. Limitations include reliance on the quality of CycleGAN-based style transfer, a fixed set of ImageNet style references that may not cover all target-domain variations, and evaluation restricted to urban driving scenes. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-urban domains (medical, remote sensing), whether the number of style generators scales gracefully, and whether test-time adaptation could further close the gap to full fine-tuning.
