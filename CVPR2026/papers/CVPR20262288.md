## Problem

The F1 score (and more generally F_β) is universally used to rank classifiers by combining precision and recall, yet no prior work has established whether these scores induce meaningful rankings or whether F1 is actually the optimal compromise between the two base rankings. This paper shows that F1 and its skew-insensitive variant (SIVF) can produce rankings far from optimal, and in extreme cases perfectly mimic one base score while ignoring the other, depending on the class priors and performance distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a finite set of two-class classification performances (each represented by a confusion matrix with tn, fp, fn, tp counts) or a probability distribution over the space of all such performances. It outputs a closed-form optimal β² value (Eq. 12, the median of pairwise θ statistics), the corresponding optimal F_β ranking, Kendall's rank correlations τ(Pr;F_β) and τ(F_β;Re), and a degree-of-optimality score O quantifying how close a chosen β is to the optimum. A practical heuristic (Eq. 22) replaces the exact computation with β² = E[PFP]/E[PFN], computed from aggregate false-positive and false-negative probabilities across the set.

## Method

The core insight is that F_β is the weighted harmonic mean of Pr and Re, which implies (via the properties of generalized f-means) that the Kendall distance satisfies d_τ(Pr;Re) = d_τ(Pr;F_β) + d_τ(F_β;Re) for all β, meaning the F_β rankings trace a geodesic (shortest path) between the Pr and Re rankings. The optimal tradeoff is then defined as the Karcher mean on this geodesic—the F_β that minimizes the Fréchet variance σ²(β) = d_τ²(Pr;F_β) + d_τ²(F_β;Re), equivalently the point equidistant from both endpoints. For a finite set of n performances, the optimal β² is given in closed form as the median of the n(n−1)/2 pairwise values θ(P_i,P_j) (Eq. 11), each computed from PTP, PFP, and PFN. The paper further derives analytical expressions for the optimal β under uniform distributions over fixed-prior ROC rectangles (Eq. 20: β² ≈ 0.61585·π₋/π₊) and above-no-skill triangles (Eq. 21: β² ≈ 0.48·π₋/π₊), and validates all results on 53 real background-subtraction datasets from CDnet 2014 and the CADA-RRE medical imaging challenge.

## Results

The paper introduces the degree of optimality O (Eq. 16) to quantify how close a given F_β ranking is to the optimal tradeoff between precision and recall rankings. On 53 real sets from CDnet 2014 (~60 methods each), F₁ achieves a mean O of only 78.44% (range 52.23%–100%), SIVF achieves 56.71% (range 50.00%–99.13%), and the simple heuristic β²=E[PFP]/E[PFN] achieves 89.53% (range 69.69%–100%). For uniform distributions with fixed class priors, F₁ is optimal only at π₊≈0.381, while SIVF achieves O≈88.63% regardless of priors. The proposed closed-form expression (Eq. 12) always yields O=100% by construction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares the proposed optimal tradeoff against two established baselines: the traditional F₁ score (β²=1) and the skew-insensitive version SIVF from Flach [10, ICML 2003]. F₁ is optimal (O=100%) only for unconstrained uniform distributions (all performances or fixed P(tn)), but degrades severely under fixed class priors where it can reach O as low as 50%—meaning it perfectly mimics either precision or recall while ignoring the other. SIVF [10] is consistently worse than F₁ on the 53 real CDnet 2014 sets (mean O=56.71% vs. 78.44%) and is entirely meaningless (violates ranking axioms) for unconstrained distributions. The simple heuristic β²=E[PFP]/E[PFN] outperforms both F₁ and SIVF on real data (mean O=89.53%) and never produces catastrophic cases, but remains suboptimal in some settings. The proposed closed-form solution (Eq. 12) achieves O=100% in all cases, confirming that the optimal β is distribution-dependent and rarely equals 1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the axiomatic theory of performance-based ranking (Piérard et al. [31]) and on Flach's geometric analysis of ROC isometrics and the SIVF score [10]. It departs from prior work by reframing the choice of ranking score as a geodesic optimization problem in the space of permutations, using Kendall rank correlations rather than the value-based averaging that has dominated the F-score literature. The key theoretical departure is proving that the F_β family forms a shortest path (geodesic) between the precision and recall rankings via the harmonic-mean property, and then identifying the midpoint (Karcher mean) as the optimal tradeoff — a question the authors note has not been formally addressed in prior studies of score correlations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that F1 — the default 'balanced' score used ubiquitously in CV, NLP, and medical imaging for ranking classifiers — is rarely the optimal ranking tradeoff (mean degree of optimality ~78% on real data, dropping to 50% in extreme cases) challenges a deeply entrenched practice. The closed-form solution (median of pairwise θ values) is computationally trivial and immediately actionable for anyone maintaining a leaderboard. Limitations include the restriction to two-class crisp classification and the fact that the simple heuristic β² = E[PFP]/E[PFN], while never catastrophic, is suboptimal in some cases; extending the framework to multi-class, probabilistic, or cost-sensitive settings remains open.
