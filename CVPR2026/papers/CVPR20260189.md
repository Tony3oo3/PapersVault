## Problem

Existing generative dataset distillation methods optimize all synthetic samples uniformly toward average training trajectories or visual diversity, causing severe redundancy: disjoint subsets of a 50 IPC distilled dataset capture 80–90% overlapping training signals. No prior approach conditions synthesis on the model's evolving learning state to ensure each new sample contributes complementary rather than duplicative information across training stages.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260189/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a target dataset of 256×256 RGB images with class labels, a pretrained DiT diffusion backbone, and an initial 10 IPC seed dataset (from IGD) as input. It produces a distilled synthetic dataset of M images (specified in IPC, e.g., 50 or 100) structured as K sequential increments of 10 IPC each, where each increment is generated conditioned on the model trained on all prior increments. The final dataset is compatible with both standard static training (train from scratch on all M images) and incremental training protocols, and is evaluated by training target networks (ConvNet-6, ResNet-AP-10, ResNet-18) from scratch.

## Method

LGD constructs the distilled dataset incrementally: at each stage i, a model θ_{i-1} is trained on the cumulative dataset, then new samples are generated via diffusion sampling guided by a learnability score S(x,y) = L(θ_{i-1},x,y) − ω·L(θ*,x,y), which is high when the current model struggles but a reference model θ* (trained on the full dataset) succeeds. This score's gradient modulates the diffusion noise prediction (ε̃ = ε_φ + λ·ρ_t·∇_{x_t} S), steering the denoising trajectory toward the learnable gap between the current model's capability and the reference model's validity boundary. A deviation guidance term repels generation from previously synthesized samples to maintain intra-class diversity, and a per-sample selection step retains only the top-scoring candidate from κ generated versions. The result is an automatic curriculum where early increments capture coarse, easily-learned features and later increments introduce progressively harder, complementary information, reducing cross-increment redundancy by 39.1% relative to DiT.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (50 IPC, ResNet-18), LGD achieves 60.1% top-1 accuracy, matching MGD³ (60.2%) and outperforming DiT+IGD (59.8%) and the base DiT diffusion (52.9%) by 7.2 points. On ImageNette, LGD reaches 82.6–85.0% at 50 IPC and 86.9–87.2% at 100 IPC across ConvNet-6, ResNet-AP-10, and ResNet-18. On ImageWoof, it achieves 53.9–65.1% at 50 IPC and 61.9–72.9% at 100 IPC. The method also reduces cross-increment redundancy by 39.1% relative to DiT (average off-diagonal accuracy drops from 94.7% to 57.65%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LGD consistently outperforms the base DiT diffusion backbone by 4–10 points across all datasets and architectures (e.g., 82.6% vs. 74.1% on Nette 50 IPC ConvNet-6; 60.1% vs. 52.9% on ImageNet-1K). Against the closest generative baselines, LGD beats IGD by 1.7–3.5 points on Nette (82.6% vs. 80.9% at 50 IPC) and by 1.2–2.3 points on Woof (65.1% vs. 63.9% at 50 IPC ResNet-18), while on ImageNet-1K it edges IGD by 0.3 points (60.1% vs. 59.8%). LGD also surpasses MGD³ on most Nette/Woof settings (e.g., 85.0% vs. 81.5% on Nette 50 IPC ResNet-18) but is essentially tied on ImageNet-1K (60.1% vs. 60.2%). Against non-generative baselines (DM, IDC-1, SRe²L, G-VBSM, RDED), LGD leads by 5–14 points on ImageNet-1K and by 8–12 points on Nette/Woof, confirming that learnability-guided incremental synthesis substantially outperforms both pixel-optimization and uniform-guidance generative approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the generative dataset distillation line (Minimax Diffusion, MGD³, IGD) by replacing uniform, trajectory-averaged guidance with an incremental, model-state-conditioned synthesis loop. It repurposes learnability scores from the data-curation literature (Mindermann et al., Evans et al.) as a diffusion guidance signal, and borrows the deviation-guidance mechanism from IGD for intra-class diversity. The key conceptual departure is reframing distillation as a sequential curriculum problem—each increment is generated to maximize marginal learning gain for the current model rather than to match an aggregate training trajectory—drawing on curriculum learning and active learning principles previously unexplored in the distillation setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80–90% cross-increment redundancy diagnosis in existing distilled datasets is a useful and somewhat underappreciated finding that reframes how the community should think about sample efficiency in distillation. The incremental framework doubles as a general-purpose diagnostic tool for quantifying information overlap in any distilled dataset, which could guide future method design. Open questions include whether the sequential training overhead (retraining after each increment) becomes prohibitive at very high IPC or on larger datasets, whether the approach extends to non-diffusion generative backbones, and whether the learnability guidance can be made differentiable to enable end-to-end optimization rather than the current generate-then-select pipeline.
