## Problem

CNN-based learned image compression models demand prohibitive peak memory and computation on high-resolution images, motivating block-based processing. However, naive block partitioning introduces blocking artifacts, and the JPEG-AI patch-based workaround relies on empirical overlap search that is time-consuming, architecture-specific, and does not guarantee artifact-free reconstruction. No prior work provides an analytic, one-shot derivation of the exact minimal overlap required at every layer for blocking-free block-based LIC.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a high-resolution RGB image (evaluated at 2K and 4K resolutions) and produces a compressed bitstream plus a reconstructed image that is bit-identical to full-image inference. The image is partitioned into non-overlapping blocks (256×256, 512×512, 192×192, or 448×448) at the input level, with analytically computed per-layer overlaps extending beyond block boundaries. Crucially, no retraining is required: the same pre-trained weights used for full-image inference are applied directly in block mode.

## Method

The paper derives a closed-form recurrence to compute the minimum left/right overlap at every convolution, transposed convolution, and PixelShuffle layer. For standard convolutions, overlap propagates from output to input as l_n = s_n·l_{n-1} + k_n^l; for transposed convolutions, it propagates in the reverse direction with a two-pass algorithm to find the smallest non-negative top-layer overlap; for PixelShuffle, overlap simply scales by the upscaling factor. The implementation replaces all padding with the computed overlap, applies a zero-reset step after each layer on boundary blocks to eliminate kernel-bias contamination from extra zero-padding, and crops skip/gating paths to align spatial supports in multi-path networks. Applied to Hyperprior, Cheng, and ELIC models on 4K images, the method reduces average peak memory to ~14% and peak MACs to ~2–3% of full-image inference while preserving rate-distortion performance to within 0.01% BD-rate.

## Results

On 4K images partitioned into 256×256 and 512×512 blocks, the proposed block-based framework reduces average peak memory to 13.94% (encoder) and 13.33% (decoder) of full-image inference, while peak MACs drop to 2.6% and 1.24%, respectively. BD-rate differences from full-image reconstruction are negligible (≤0.0081% across four LIC models on 2K/4K), confirming bit-identical rate-distortion performance without retraining. On the JPEG-AI reference software, the proposed method achieves ≤0.01% BD-rate (effectively bit-identical) while reducing peak encoder MACs by ~19–19% and peak decoder MACs by ~23–24% compared to the JPEG-AI baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the JPEG-AI reference software's native patch-based tiling mechanism, which uses empirically determined symmetric overlaps. The proposed method decisively outperforms this baseline on reconstruction quality: the JPEG-AI baseline exhibits visible block-boundary artifacts (MSE up to 2.59% BD-rate on U channel at 4K/192×192), while the proposed method achieves ≤0.01% BD-rate, i.e., bit-identical output to full-image inference. On computational efficiency, the proposed method also wins, reducing peak encoder MACs by 19.3% (2K/192) to 19.3% (4K/448) and peak decoder MACs by 23.4% to 12.1% relative to the baseline, thanks to analytically minimal (and asymmetric) overlaps versus the baseline's larger symmetric overlaps. The only trade-off is a modest increase in decoding time (e.g., 1.45 s vs. 1.24 s at 2K/192×192), attributed to the custom asymmetric overlap implementation, which the authors note is compensated by the lower peak computation and artifact-free output. Additionally, the method is validated against full-image LIC on four models (Hyperprior, Cheng, ELIC), where BD-rate differences are at most 0.0081%, confirming that the analytic overlap calculation is both necessary and sufficient for blocking-free reconstruction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the convolution arithmetic formalism of Dumoulin et al. [9] to derive closed-form recurrence relations for overlap propagation through convolution, transposed convolution, and PixelShuffle layers. It departs from the JPEG-AI standard's [10] empirical, iterative search for patch overlap sizes by replacing it with a one-shot analytic computation that guarantees the minimal sufficient overlap. The work also contrasts with hybrid LIC approaches [24, 25, 27] that suppress blocking artifacts via learned post-processing networks, and with Zhang et al. [26], which achieves inter-block continuity through dedicated architectural components but cannot handle odd-sized kernels or generalize across architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: reducing peak memory to ~14% and peak MACs to ~2.6% for 4K images without retraining makes CNN-based LIC viable on mobile and embedded platforms where ELIC alone demands ~4 GB of decoder memory. The method is architecture-agnostic within the CNN family and applies to any CNN-based image processing network, not just LIC. A key limitation is that the framework does not extend to Transformer-based models, which the authors acknowledge as requiring further study. An open question is whether the analytic overlap framework can be adapted to attention-based architectures or to video compression settings where temporal context adds another dimension of overlap propagation.
