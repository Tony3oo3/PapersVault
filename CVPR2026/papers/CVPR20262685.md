## Problem

Existing driving VLA models use natural-language chain-of-thought for inference-time reasoning, but text is poorly suited to representing spatiotemporal geometry and multi-agent interactions, introduces significant autoregressive latency, and suffers from weak action-text alignment where generated rationales diverge from the final trajectory. No prior work has explored performing driving reasoning entirely in a latent space that is both action-aligned and grounded in a learned world model.

## Input / Output

The model consumes two front-view camera streams (wide 120° and telephoto 30° at 320×512) over a 1.6 s history window at 10 Hz, encoded by a DINOv2 ViT, plus ego-motion history (speed, yaw rate, past control actions) embedded as compact tokens. It outputs 64 discrete trajectory tokens (6.4 s at 10 Hz) from a 1024-code k-means vocabulary over ego-frame Δ-poses, preceded by a latent CoT sequence of B=2 branches × K=5 interleaved 1.0 s action-proposal blocks and LWM tokens. The LWM state encodes vectorized bounding boxes and poses of the ego vehicle plus K nearest agents over a 1.0 s window.

## Method

LCDrive replaces textual CoT with an interleaved latent reasoning trace where action-proposal tokens (drawn from the same 1024-code trajectory vocabulary as the final output) alternate with latent world-model tokens predicted by a lightweight MLP head on the VLA hidden state, enabling counterfactual rollouts of multi-agent futures directly in latent space. Training proceeds in three stages: (1) non-reasoning supervised pretraining on PhysicalAI-AV, (2) CoT cold start via teacher forcing where a frozen non-reasoning model generates action proposals and ground-truth agent boxes are re-centered into each proposal's ego frame to produce LWM supervision targets, and (3) GRPO reinforcement learning with trajectory-level ADE rewards that refines both the latent reasoning tokens and final action prediction. The multi-branch design (B=2, K=5) lets the model explore diverse counterfactual strategies under a bounded token budget, and the shared action vocabulary between proposals and final output ensures tight action-reasoning alignment that text-based CoT cannot provide.

## Results

On the PhysicalAI-AV benchmark (23,758 validation clips, 53 hours), LCDrive with RL post-training achieves ADE 1.626 m, OffRoad₂.₅ 1.219%, and Coll₅.₀ 0.836%, outperforming the non-reasoning baseline (ADE 1.762 m, OffRoad₂.₅ 1.753%, Coll₅.₀ 2.207%) and the Text-CoT baseline (ADE 1.650 m, OffRoad₂.₅ 1.391%, Coll₅.₀ 0.905%). With ground-truth LWM tokens (oracle setting), Latent CoT + RL reaches ADE 1.197 m and Coll₅.₀ 0.867%, demonstrating the upper bound of latent reasoning. LCDrive also achieves a 1.8× reasoning speedup over Text CoT on an RTX A5000 due to its shorter, structured latent token sequence.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Text-CoT baseline (AR1, trained on a >100× larger proprietary driving corpus with curated CoT annotations), LCDrive achieves a lower overall ADE (1.626 vs. 1.650 m) and better safety metrics (OffRoad₂.₅ 1.219% vs. 1.391%; Coll₅.₀ 0.836% vs. 0.905%), while Text CoT retains an edge on Corner Dist (0.642 vs. 0.880 m) and Coll₂.₅ (0.276% vs. 0.289%). Against the non-reasoning baseline (∅), LCDrive reduces ADE by 7.7% (1.626 vs. 1.762 m) and cuts Coll₅.₀ by 62% (0.836% vs. 2.207%), showing that learned latent world-model tokens are highly informative at inference time. A key finding is that RL post-training yields substantial gains for LCDrive (ADE 1.668→1.626) but provides no benefit (and even degrades safety) for the LWM₀-only condition, indicating that RL specifically helps the model exploit the interleaved latent CoT rollouts. Per-scenario, LCDrive wins by large margins on General Driving (1.166 vs. 1.434), Speed Control (1.376 vs. 2.037), and Nudge Static Obstacle (1.387 vs. 1.855), while Text CoT is stronger on Cut-In (1.884 vs. 2.385) and Lead Vehicle Following (1.455 vs. 1.708), suggesting latent CoT excels at proactive multi-agent anticipation while text CoT handles reactive scenarios slightly better.

## Relation to Prior Work

LCDrive builds directly on the AR1 architecture (Tian et al., 2025), which established text-based chain-of-thought reasoning in driving VLA models, but departs fundamentally from the textual reasoning substrate by replacing natural-language CoT tokens with action-aligned latent tokens grounded in a learned ego-centric world model. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of two threads: the emerging latent-reasoning paradigm in LLMs (where intermediate computations occur in continuous vector space rather than language) and the latent world model literature in driving, which has previously been used for data augmentation or neural simulation rather than as an in-loop reasoning mechanism. The three-stage training pipeline (non-reasoning pretraining → teacher-forced cold start → GRPO reinforcement learning) is a novel combination that specifically targets the challenge of bootstrapping and refining latent reasoning without human-annotated reasoning traces.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a concrete, well-evidenced argument that natural language is a suboptimal reasoning medium for driving VLA models, offering a 1.8× inference speedup and larger RL post-training gains over text-CoT baselines while matching or exceeding their trajectory quality. The finding that RL is only beneficial when the model actually performs reasoning (LWM₀-only + RL degrades performance) suggests a non-trivial interaction between reasoning structure and reward optimization that the field has not previously characterized. Key open questions include: whether the approach can be extended to adaptive reasoning lengths, whether the reliance on ground-truth agent bounding boxes for LWM supervision can be fully eliminated via self-supervised or auto-labeled perception, and whether the latent tokens can be made human-interpretable for safety auditing. The scenario-level results (strong on proactive multi-agent scenarios, weaker on reactive ones like Cut-In) also point to where latent world modeling may need complementary reactive mechanisms.
