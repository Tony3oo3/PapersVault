## Problem

CLIP-based visual encoders in MLLMs emphasize global semantic alignment but lose fine-grained pixel-level detail, while pixel-level encoders like DINOv3 excel at texture perception yet lack coarse-grained semantic abstraction. Prior multi-encoder designs that combine both are computationally costly and do not provide a single encoder with unified, task-adaptive granularity. The paper asks whether a pixel-level encoder can be augmented with a controllable semantic hierarchy to achieve both fine and coarse understanding in one forward pass.

## Input / Output

The model takes an RGB image and a natural-language question as input. The image is encoded by a frozen DINOv3 ViT into a spatial feature map, while the question is tokenized and passed through the first LLM block to produce a granularity prediction (spatial pooling kernel size and cluster cardinality) before any visual tokens reach the LLM. The output is a free-form text response generated autoregressively by the LLM backbone (Qwen2.5-1.5B or Llama3.2-3B), with the visual stream consisting of both original pixel-level tokens and a compact set of aggregated semantic tokens.

## Method

Granulon introduces two coupled modules on top of a frozen DINOv3 encoder. First, a text-conditioned Granularity Controller (first LLM block → mean-pooling projection → MLP head) predicts a discrete granularity configuration (α for spatial down-sampling, β for cluster count) from the question's linguistic scope. Second, an Adaptive Token Aggregation (AdaTA) module uses α to perform granularity-guided spatial pooling on both features and attention maps, then applies relation-aware mini-k-means clustering (with an attention-distance regularizer) to form M_β candidate centroids, and finally selects top-K clusters via a composite quality score balancing spatial support, semantic coherence, and dispersion. The selected semantic tokens are concatenated with the original pixel tokens, projected through a multimodal adapter, and fed jointly with text embeddings into the LLM. Training minimizes a joint loss combining the standard task cross-entropy with negative pixel-level and semantic-level contribution objectives, encouraging the model to allocate informative content across both token streams adaptively.

## Results

With Qwen2.5 as the LLM backbone, Granulon achieves 58.80% Recall on SEED-Bench and 57.13% Recall on A-OKVQA, surpassing the strongest single-encoder baseline (DINOv3) by +3.06% and +9.70% respectively. On the FLUX-Reason benchmark with Llama3.2, Granulon reaches 56.67% GPTscore, outperforming DINOv3 (34.87%) by +21.80% and CLIP (28.97%) by +27.70%. The paper reports an overall ~30% accuracy improvement and ~20% hallucination reduction relative to CLIP-based baselines under identical training settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Granulon consistently outperforms all three baselines across every benchmark and both LLM backbones. Against SigLIP, the gains are most dramatic on A-OKVQA (57.13% vs. 21.89% Recall, +35.24% with Qwen2.5) and on FLUX-Reason (56.67% vs. 44.87% GPTscore, +11.80% with Llama3.2), reflecting SigLIP's weaker fine-grained perception. Against DINOv2, Granulon leads by +17.40% Recall on SEED-Bench and +37.18% GPTscore on FLUX-Reason (Llama3.2), confirming that self-distillation alone without adaptive granularity is insufficient for multi-scale reasoning. Against DINOv3 (the closest baseline and the encoder Granulon builds upon), improvements are more modest on VQA tasks (+3.06% Recall on SEED-Bench, +9.70% on A-OKVQA) but substantial on reasoning (+21.80% GPTscore on FLUX-Reason with Llama3.2), indicating that the Controller and AdaTA modules primarily unlock DINOv3's underused coarse-grained semantic capacity rather than improving its already-strong pixel-level features.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant CLIP-based paradigm in MLLMs (CLIP, SigLIP, EVA-CLIP) and instead builds on the self-supervised pixel-level encoder DINOv3, which excels at fine-grained texture but lacks coarse semantic abstraction. It also distinguishes itself from prior multi-encoder approaches (CLIP+DINO hybrids) and from bottom-up token-pruning or text-guided region-selection methods (DynamicViT, EViT, LLaVA-NeXT, InternVL) by introducing a text-conditioned granularity Controller and an adaptive token aggregation (AdaTA) module that dynamically modulate visual abstraction within a single encoder. Conceptually, the paper reframes the problem: rather than improving semantic encoders for MLLMs, it asks whether pixel-level encoders can be augmented to acquire the multi-granularity semantics they intrinsically lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a DINOv3-based MLLM with adaptive granularity can outperform CLIP-based counterparts by ~30% in accuracy while reducing hallucination by ~20% challenges the assumption that CLIP's contrastive image-text alignment is a prerequisite for strong multimodal reasoning. This opens a practical direction for MLLM designers: one can retain the strong pixel-level perception of self-supervised encoders and selectively inject semantic abstraction rather than committing to a fixed-resolution semantic encoder. Open questions include whether the GPT-4o-annotated granularity supervision generalizes beyond the tested question types, how the approach scales to video or multi-image inputs, and whether the ~10% additional token overhead from the Controller remains acceptable at larger model scales.
