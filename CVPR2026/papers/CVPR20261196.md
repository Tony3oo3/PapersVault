## Problem

Existing 6D object pose tracking methods rely on RGB or depth at conventional frame rates (≤30 FPS), making them fail under fast, dynamic motion due to motion blur and large inter-frame displacements. While event cameras offer microsecond temporal resolution, no prior work provides a generalizable event-based 6D tracking framework for novel objects, and available event-based 6D datasets are small, object-specific, and limited in motion diversity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261196/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an asynchronous event stream (pixel location, timestamp, polarity), the most recent depth frame, a CAD model, an initial pose, and camera intrinsics, operating on a region-of-interest crop around the object. It produces a 6D pose (rotation + translation) at arbitrary normalized timestamps τ∈[0,1) between consecutive depth frames, along with a reconstructed intensity image and depth map at that timestamp. The key constraint is that depth is only available at 30 FPS while events arrive continuously, so the method must extrapolate both modalities to intermediate times where no sensor measurement exists.

## Method

EventTrack6D comprises a dual-modal reconstruction module and a render-and-compare pose refinement module. For intensity reconstruction, a short-range event stream is encoded, temporally aggregated via ConvLSTM, and decoded into a dense image. For depth reconstruction, a long-range event stream and the most recent depth frame are processed through separate encoders to extract motion and geometry features; a coarse motion field is predicted, depth features are warped, a correlation-layer cost volume is computed, and a residual motion refinement yields the final depth by fusing warped geometry features with event cues and temporally integrated image features. The pose refinement module then iteratively aligns CAD renderings against the reconstructed intensity and depth using a render-and-compare paradigm, decomposing large motion into small per-step updates. The entire system is trained supervised on the synthetic EventBlender6D dataset (495k samples, 1033 objects) and generalizes to real-world and simulated evaluation without fine-tuning, running at over 120 FPS with TensorRT.

## Results

On the Event6D dataset at 120 FPS (where RGB-D data is unavailable), EventTrack6D achieves ADD-S 52.79, ADD 25.26, and AR 64.38, running at 50 FPS (128 FPS with TensorRT). This substantially outperforms the strongest event-based baseline E2VID+FP (ADD-S 37.24, ADD 16.97, AR 48.72) and the fine-tuned FP† (ADD-S 22.93, ADD 4.31, AR 25.72). At 30 FPS, EventTrack6D (ADD-S 56.08, ADD 29.40, AR 63.71) also surpasses the RGB-D SOTA baselines MegaPose (ADD-S 11.08) and FoundationPose (ADD-S 16.63). On the EventHO3D dataset, EventTrack6D achieves ADD-S 64.75 and AR 66.27, outperforming E2VID+FP (ADD-S 57.10, AR 56.77) and fine-tuned FP† (ADD-S 35.31, AR 27.64).

## Baselines & Comparisons

EventTrack6D decisively outperforms all four baselines on the 120 FPS Event6D benchmark. Against E2VID+MegaPose (ADD-S 6.78) and E2VID+FoundationPose (ADD-S 37.24), the gap is large: the dual-modal reconstruction provides continuous geometric and photometric cues that event-to-image conversion alone cannot supply between sparse depth frames. ETAP, which tracks query points from the CAD surface and fits a rigid transform via ICP/PnP, achieves only ADD-S 7.77 and runs at 1.56 FPS, degrading severely under dynamic motion and occlusion. The fine-tuned FoundationPose† (ADD-S 22.93, 108.70 FPS) is the closest competitor in speed but suffers from modality mismatch between CAD renderings and event streams plus intermittent depth, yielding ADD 4.31 versus EventTrack6D's 25.26. At 30 FPS, EventTrack6D (ADD-S 56.08) also surpasses the native RGB-D baselines MegaPose (11.08) and FoundationPose (16.63), confirming that the event-based reconstruction provides more robust observations even when depth is available. On EventHO3D, EventTrack6D leads E2VID+FP by 7.65 ADD-S points (64.75 vs. 57.10) and fine-tuned FP† by 29.44 points (64.75 vs. 35.31), demonstrating cross-dataset generalization from synthetic training alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the render-and-compare paradigm established by MegaPose and FoundationPose for novel-object 6D pose tracking into the event-camera domain, where conventional RGB-D frame rates (≤30 FPS) are insufficient for dynamic scenes. The key architectural departure is a dual-modal reconstruction module that simultaneously predicts intensity and depth at arbitrary timestamps between sparse depth frames, using event streams as the driving signal—drawing on motion-vector estimation and correlation layers from the event-based video interpolation literature. Unlike prior event-based 6D work (RGB-DE, E-POSE, YCB-Ev), which is confined to instance-level tracking on small, controlled datasets, EventTrack6D targets novel-object generalization at 120+ FPS with a large-scale synthetic training set.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The introduction of three complementary datasets (EventBlender6D with 495k samples/1033 objects for training, Event6D with 120 Hz motion-capture ground truth, and EventHO3D for cross-domain evaluation) fills a critical gap in the event-based 6D pose literature, which previously lacked large-scale, high-frame-rate benchmarks. The demonstrated synthetic-to-real generalization without fine-tuning suggests a viable path toward deploying event-based 6D tracking in real-time robotic manipulation where motion blur and latency are prohibitive. Open questions include scalability to multi-object scenes, handling of severe occlusions, and whether the dual-reconstruction approach can be further accelerated for edge deployment; the current 160×160 ROI crop also limits applicability to objects that occupy a small image fraction.
