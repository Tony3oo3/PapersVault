## Problem

Vanilla RoPE in 3D LVLMs flattens point-cloud tokens into a 1D raster sequence and encodes position solely via sequence-index differences, which (i) destroys the inherent 3D spatial neighborhood structure of point clouds and (ii) is direction-agnostic, unable to capture angular or orientation variations between tokens. This mismatch produces a pronounced spatial perception bias where cross-modal attention collapses onto a few hotspots while large regions and small objects receive near-uniformly low attention, degrading 3D layout estimation and object detection.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a 3D point cloud (encoded by the Sonata encoder) and a free-form text prompt, with point-cloud tokens flattened into a 1D sequence of length P and text tokens of length T, bridged by a two-layer MLP projector into the Qwen2.5-0.5B LLM. Each point-cloud token is assigned a 4D positional index (t, r, θ, φ) derived from its Cartesian coordinates and sequence position, rather than a single scalar index. Outputs include predicted architectural element layouts (evaluated via IoU₂D), 3D object bounding boxes (evaluated via IoU₃D at 0.25 and 0.50 thresholds), and semantic scene labels that can be converted into a textual scene graph for downstream robot planning.

## Method

SoPE is a drop-in replacement for RoPE in SpatialLM that reparameterizes each point-cloud token's position from a 1D raster index into a 4D spherical tuple (t, r, θ, φ), where t preserves temporal ordering and (r, θ, φ) are computed from the token's Cartesian coordinates via standard spherical conversion. The RoPE frequency spectrum is then split across the four components with a 24:2:3:3 allocation ratio, placing the spherical components in higher-frequency subbands to capture fine-grained spatial and angular variation while reserving a large low-frequency block for temporal coherence. A multi-scale frequency mixing strategy further computes three deterministic phase transforms per component—linear (absolute precision), log-compressed (local neighborhood), and periodic (global structure)—and averages them with fixed uniform weights at the RoPE phase level, introducing no additional learnable parameters. The resulting relative rotation matrix decomposes displacement across all four components (Δt, Δr, Δθ, Δφ), enabling the model to simultaneously encode spatial location, depth, and directional orientation in a geometry-consistent manner.

## Results

On ARKitScenes 3D object detection, SpatialSoPE achieves IoU₃D F1@0.25 = 66.1 and IoU₃D F1@0.50 = 63.2, improving over SpatialLM by +2.2 and +2.5 points respectively. On Structured3D layout estimation, SpatialSoPE reaches IoU₂D@0.25 = 88.7 and IoU₂D@0.5 = 86.2, surpassing SpatialLM (86.5/84.6) and prior methods like SceneScript (83.1/80.8). On the SpatialLM Dataset, gains of +1.7/+1.4 over the baseline confirm consistent improvements across all three benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialSoPE outperforms all compared baselines on every reported metric. Against the primary baseline SpatialLM, it gains +2.2/+2.5 on ARKitScenes, +2.2/+1.6 on Structured3D layout, and +1.7/+1.4 on the SpatialLM Dataset. Among dedicated 3D detectors, it surpasses UniDet3D (62.8/48.3) by a wide margin of +3.3/+14.9 on ARKitScenes, and outperforms VoteNet, H3DNet, and NeRF-Det by 10+ points on IoU₃D@0.25. For layout estimation, it exceeds SceneScript (83.1/80.8) by +5.6/+5.4 and RoomFormer (70.4/67.2) by +18.3/+19.0 on Structured3D. Among positional-encoding variants tested in Table 4, RoPE-3D (64.2/61.4), CCA (64.1/60.5), and MCA (63.7/60.2) all underperform SpatialSoPE (66.1/63.2) on ARKitScenes, confirming that spherical reparameterization with multi-scale mixing is more effective than 2D index reassignment or Cartesian coordinate encoding alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RoPE lineage from 1D sequence indexing to 3D point-cloud tokens by reparameterizing positions into a spherical coordinate tuple (t, r, θ, φ), departing from prior 2D-projection heuristics (CCA, MCA) and the Cartesian-coordinate approach of RoPE-3D. It is built directly on SpatialLM as the base 3D LVLM, replacing its vanilla RoPE with a geometry-aware variant that explicitly encodes both spatial location and angular orientation. The multi-scale frequency mixing strategy (linear, log, periodic) is a novel addition not present in prior RoPE variants for vision, though it draws on the general idea of multi-frequency decomposition seen in VideoRoPE and M-RoPE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The spatial perception bias identified here—where cross-modal attention collapses onto a few hotspots and ignores geometrically salient regions—is a concrete and well-diagnosed failure mode of current 3D LVLMs that practitioners building on RoPE-based architectures will encounter. However, the gains are modest (+2–3 F1 points) and validated only on a single base model (SpatialLM + Qwen2.5-0.5B + Sonata), leaving open whether the spherical reparameterization and the 24:2:3:3 frequency allocation transfer to other 3D LVLM backbones or larger point-cloud encoders. The real-world robotic deployment, while encouraging, is a single-scene demonstration and does not yet establish the method's robustness across diverse environments or task distributions.
