## Problem

Existing open-access surgical video datasets are small (fewer than 100 videos, under 30 hours), cover only one or a few procedures, and often retain non-surgical content that introduces spurious features. This data scarcity limits the generalization of surgical perception models across diverse procedures and downstream tasks, and no prior work has demonstrated that a rigorously curated, large-scale public surgical corpus can support a foundation model that outperforms both specialist architectures and existing surgical foundation models.

## Input / Output

LemonFM consumes individual endoscopic RGB frames extracted at 1 fps from surgical videos; during pretraining, each frame is augmented into two global views plus four crops, and the augmented distillation additionally retrieves k-nearest-neighbor frames from other videos of the same procedure type (filtered by a 3× cosine-distance threshold relative to the preceding frame) and adjacent temporal frames to form the W_i supervisory set. Downstream, the frozen or fine-tuned backbone produces per-frame embeddings that feed task-specific heads (linear, MSTCN, or FPN decoder) yielding phase labels, multi-label tool presence, action triplets, pixel-wise segmentation masks, or—via the typicality-weighted LemonFM-Vid head—video-level procedure and surgery-type labels.

## Method

The paper introduces a multi-stage curation pipeline (ResNet18 storyboard classifier → ResNet18 frame classifier for trimming → YOLOv8 for in-frame non-surgical region obliteration → manual QC) that yields LEMON, a 4,194-video / 938-hour / 85M-frame dataset spanning 35 minimally invasive procedure types. On top of LEMON, LemonFM is pretrained with a DINO-style teacher-student framework using ConvNeXt-L for both networks, but augments the standard cross-entropy distillation loss (Eq. 1) with an extra W_i term: two nearest-neighbor images from other same-procedure videos (for cross-patient appearance invariance) and their augmented crops, plus adjacent temporal frames (for inter-frame motion invariance). The teacher is updated via exponential moving average of the student, and the student is trained to match the teacher's softmax distribution over C=2^16 clusters across all views in U_i ∪ V_i ∪ W_i. For video-level tasks, LemonFM-Vid aggregates frame embeddings using a typicality weight (inverse mean cosine distance to 20 nearest neighbors) before a single-layer MLP, outperforming SlowFast, TimeSformer, MViTv2, and Video Swin Transformer on the proposed procedure-type and surgery-type classification benchmarks.

## Results

Under full fine-tuning, LemonFM achieves 85.5/64.8 Acc/Jacc on AutoLaparo, 92.7/85.1 on Cholec80, and 89.9/79.4 on M2CAI16 for phase recognition; 93.7 and 94.4 mAP on Cholec80 and GraSP for tool detection; 61.9 mAP on CholecT50 for action recognition; and 81.3 mDice on CholecSeg8k for semantic segmentation. These represent gains of +8.4 to +9.5 pp Jaccard over the best prior surgical foundation model (SurgeNetXL) on phase recognition, +5.3 to +10.2 pp mAP on tool detection, +4.4 pp mAP on action recognition, and +10.3 pp mDice on segmentation. Even with only 50% of labeled training data, LemonFM (e.g., 84.9/62.4 on AutoLaparo, 78.2 mDice on CholecSeg8k) still outperforms all competing foundation models trained on 100% of their data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LemonFM outperforms all six surgical foundation model baselines (MAE, VideoMAEv2, Endo-FM, EndoViT, GSViT, SurgeNetXL) across every downstream task and dataset. The closest competitor, SurgeNetXL, is surpassed by +9.7 pp Jaccard on AutoLaparo (64.8 vs. 55.1), +13.1 pp on Cholec80 (85.1 vs. 72.0), +20.6 pp on M2CAI16 (79.4 vs. 58.8), +7.2 pp mAP on Cholec80 tool detection (93.7 vs. 86.5), +10.6 pp on GraSP (94.4 vs. 83.8), and +4.4 pp on CholecT50 (61.9 vs. 57.5). On semantic segmentation (Table 4), LemonFM achieves 81.3 mDice versus SurgeNetXL's 69.0 and EndoViT's 71.0, a +10.3 pp margin over the best prior model. LemonFM also exceeds the best specialist models (TeCNO, Trans-SVNet, LoViT) on phase recognition Jaccard, e.g., 85.1 vs. 81.2 on Cholec80. On the LEMON leaderboard (Table 7), LemonFM-Vid achieves 57.8 mAP for multi-label procedure classification, beating Video Swin Transformer (51.4) by 6.4 pp, and matches its 98.9% accuracy on binary surgery type classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends DINO's self-supervised knowledge distillation framework by introducing an augmented distillation term (W_i) that enforces invariance to cross-patient appearance variation and inter-frame motion through nearest-neighbor retrieval within the same procedure type. It departs from prior web-sourced surgical datasets (GenSurgery, SurgeNetXL) by introducing a rigorous multi-stage curation pipeline—storyboard classification, frame-level trimming, and YOLOv8-based non-surgical region removal—that prior efforts lacked, addressing the risk of spurious features from non-surgical content. The choice of ConvNeXt-L over ViT backbones for both teacher and student networks represents a deliberate departure from the ViT-centric trend in surgical SSL, motivated by the need to preserve fine-grained spatial detail for dense prediction tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LEMON (4,194 videos, 938 hours, 85M frames, 35 procedures) becomes the largest open-access surgical dataset, providing a critical resource that prior smaller datasets (<100 videos, <30 hours) could not support for generalizable foundation model training. The finding that LemonFM fine-tuned on only 50% of labeled data outperforms all competing foundation models trained on 100% of data suggests that pretraining quality on a well-curated, diverse corpus can substitute for downstream annotation effort—a practically significant result for a field where annotation is expensive and regulated. Open questions include whether the YouTube-sourced data adequately represents the full distribution of clinical surgical video (e.g., different camera systems, lighting, patient populations), and the authors note that a surgery-specific video foundation model remains future work.
