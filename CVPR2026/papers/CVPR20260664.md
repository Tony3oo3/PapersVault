## Problem

3D medical imaging lacks foundation models comparable to those in natural vision and language. Existing self-supervised methods (reconstruction, contrastive) optimize for low-level features that miss the dense, spatially-grounded anatomical semantics critical for clinical analysis, while supervised pretraining is constrained by predefined class taxonomies and prohibitively expensive expert annotations. The paper asks whether generalizable 3D medical representations can be learned entirely from automatically generated, class-agnostic masks without any human annotation.

## Input / Output

During pretraining, the method consumes unlabeled 3D volumes (CT, MRI, PET) paired with hundreds to thousands of class-agnostic masks auto-generated per volume via SAM2 (2D slice sampling with dense point prompts, then 3D propagation). At inference in in-context mode, the model takes a reference image-mask pair (x_s, y_s) that implicitly defines the segmentation target and a query 3D volume x_q, producing a predicted mask ŷ_q without parameter updates. In finetuned or classification modes, the encoder outputs fixed-length feature vectors that feed a task-specific head for segmentation or multi-class scoring.

## Method

MASS formulates pretraining as in-context segmentation: for each step, two augmented views (reference and query) are created from the same image-mask pair, with spatial transforms applied to both and appearance transforms (brightness, contrast, gamma, noise) applied only to images. Following the Iris architecture, a 3D ResUNet encoder E_θ encodes both views, a task encoding module T_φ extracts a compact task embedding from the reference features and mask, and a decoder D_ψ uses this embedding to guide query mask prediction, optimized with Dice + BCE loss. The key mechanism is that appearance and spatial augmentations create an invariance constraint: the model cannot rely on intensity, texture, position, or orientation shortcuts, forcing it to learn the semantic identity of structures as the only stable signal across views. Training on thousands of open-set, class-agnostic masks spanning organs, sub-anatomical regions, and pathologies provides diverse weak supervision that captures compositional visual primitives (boundaries, textures, spatial configurations) transferable to both segmentation and classification downstream.

## Results

On large-scale multi-modal pretraining (5K CT/MRI/PET volumes), MASS-FT achieves 70.2% Dice on BCV 1-shot (vs. 53.1% for best SSL baseline AnatoMix, +17.1 pts) and 74.3% on AMOS MR 1-shot (vs. 37.9% for Merlin, +36.4 pts). With only 10 shots (25–40% of training data), MASS-FT matches full supervision on BCV (84.2% vs. 83.6%) and AMOS MR (85.0% vs. 85.5%). On out-of-distribution datasets, MASS-FT outperforms supervised pretraining: Pelvic 1-shot reaches 92.8% vs. Iris 86.9% and SuPreM 85.4%. For frozen-encoder classification on unseen pathologies, MASS achieves 75.4% AUC on RSNA ICH with only 5% data (761 samples), surpassing full training from scratch (72.8%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASS substantially outperforms all self-supervised baselines across every dataset and shot setting. On BCV 1-shot, MASS-FT (70.2%) exceeds AnatoMix (53.1%) by +17.1 pts, Merlin (50.1%) by +20.1 pts, and OpenMind-SimCLR (45.6%) by +24.6 pts; on AMOS MR 1-shot the gap widens to +36.4 pts over Merlin. Against supervised pretraining, the picture is nuanced: Iris (83.4%) and SuPreM (63.9%) still lead on in-distribution BCV 1-shot where their annotation taxonomies align with evaluation targets, but on out-of-distribution datasets MASS reverses the advantage—Pelvic 1-shot (92.8% vs. Iris 86.9%, SuPreM 85.4%) and BraTS 30-shot (63.0% vs. Iris 60.7%, SuPreM 60.3%). For frozen-encoder classification on entirely unseen pathologies, MASS (75.4% AUC on RSNA ICH 5%) beats all SSL baselines (best: SimCLR 58.9%) and even surpasses full training from scratch (72.8%), while SuPreM (73.5%) remains the closest supervised competitor. On KiTS tumor segmentation, where in-context inference fails for all methods due to high inter-patient variability, MASS-FT at 30-shot (68.5%) still outperforms the best SSL baseline (SimCLR 46.8%) by +21.7 pts, confirming that the pretrained representations transfer effectively to pathological structures through finetuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MASS builds directly on the Iris in-context segmentation architecture [21], repurposing it as a self-supervised pretext task by replacing expert-annotated masks with class-agnostic masks auto-generated by SAM2. It departs from the dominant SSL paradigms in medical imaging (reconstruction via MAE/S3D, contrastive via SimCLR, corruption-restoration via Model Genesis) by arguing that these low-level objectives fail to capture the spatially-grounded anatomical semantics that clinicians rely on. It also distinguishes itself from supervised pretraining (SuPreM, STU-Net) and synthetic-data approaches (AnatoMix) by eliminating annotation costs and distribution gaps, while using SAM2 purely as a mask generator rather than a backbone, since SAM's representations lack semantic content.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MASS provides a practical, annotation-free path toward 3D medical imaging foundation models, demonstrating that diverse class-agnostic masks spanning organs, vessels, and pathologies yield representations that transfer to both segmentation and classification on unseen tasks. The finding that data diversity (anatomical and modal) drives gains more than raw volume, and that weak masks with only ~15% average ground-truth overlap suffice, challenges the assumption that high-quality supervision is necessary for semantic learning. Open questions include whether combining auto-generated masks with expert annotations during pretraining yields synergistic gains, whether the approach scales to even larger corpora, and how to align the learned representations with textual modalities for vision-language applications.
