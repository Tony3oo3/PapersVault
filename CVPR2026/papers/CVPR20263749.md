## Problem

Existing UAV-based multi-object tracking methods rely on a single matching strategy (motion or appearance) and fail under multi-scale targets, occlusions, lens shake/rotation, and high inter-target similarity. The paper identifies that no prior method adapts its matching mechanism to the specific difficulty of each target, leading to systematic failures for small or occluded objects in UAV footage.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263749/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes two inputs per frame: the current UAV video frame (processed by a locally trained YOLOX detector) and the previous frame's tracking state containing each tracked object's bounding box, category label, and persistent ID. Output is a set of tracked bounding boxes with stable object IDs and category labels, where the three-stage progressive matching ensures large, small, and mixed-scale targets are each resolved by the most appropriate strategy before post-processing assigns new IDs to unmatched detections.

## Method

ProgTrack introduces a three-stage progressive matching pipeline inspired by human eye tracking: Stage 1 matches large targets via Local Motion Information (IoU-based), Stage 2 matches small targets via a Context Enhanced Feature (CE-Feature) extracted by the CE-ReID module (combining Spatial-Channel Attention, Contextual Depthwise Separable Pointwise Convolution, and a fusion block that encodes target-background relational features), and Stage 3 resolves remaining unmatched targets via Global Motion Information using the GRNED module, which computes sorted inter-target Euclidean distance vectors per frame and matches them across frames via min-max normalized cost matrix and Hungarian assignment. A Pure Kalman Filter (PKF) strategy extends tracking for up to 30 frames without detection by skipping the observation update step, preserving trajectory continuity through occlusions. The CE-ReID module is trained with a combined local feature loss and context-enhanced feature loss under supervised learning.

## Results

On VisDrone2019, ProgTrack achieves MOTA 40.2, MOTP 77.5, and IDF1 52.8, attaining the best MOTP and IDF1 among all compared methods while matching StrongSORT's MOTA (40.3). On MDMT, ProgTrack leads all three key metrics with MOTA 57.2, MOTP 77.3, and IDF1 69.2. The CE-ReID module achieves an AUC of 0.950 on the VisDrone2019 test set, surpassing DeepReID (0.917) and FastReID (0.931). Processing speed is 6.5 FPS on VisDrone2019, comparable to BoT-SORT (7.9 FPS) and StrongSORT (8 FPS) but slower than ByteTrack (29.5 FPS) and OCSORT (28 FPS).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ProgTrack achieves the best MOTP (77.5) and IDF1 (52.8) on VisDrone2019, outperforming the next-best MOTP from MOTRv2 (74.4) by 3.1 points and the next-best IDF1 from StrongSORT (49.4) by 3.4 points. On MDMT, ProgTrack leads all three headline metrics, beating StrongSORT (the closest competitor) by 0.1 MOTA, 3.0 MOTP, and 2.4 IDF1. In terms of identity switches, ProgTrack records 21,295 IDs on VisDrone2019, slightly higher than StrongSORT's 21,102 but lower than all other methods. The main trade-off is speed: at 6.5 FPS, ProgTrack is slower than ByteTrack (29.5 FPS), OCSORT (28 FPS), and DiffMOT (27.1 FPS), though comparable to BoT-SORT (7.9 FPS) and StrongSORT (8 FPS). For re-identification quality, CE-ReID achieves an AUC of 0.950, exceeding FastReID (0.931) and DeepReID (0.917) on the VisDrone2019 test set, confirming the benefit of context-enhanced features for small and multi-scale UAV targets.

## Relation to Prior Work

ProgTrack sits within the Track-by-Detection paradigm, building on the SORT/DeepSORT/ByteTrack lineage of Kalman-filter-plus-Hungarian-matching trackers, and specifically targets the UAV sub-domain where prior work (UAVMOT, SFSORT) has addressed scale variation and viewpoint change. Its key departure is the progressive matching strategy—sequentially applying LMI (IoU) for large targets, CE-Feature (context-enhanced ReID) for small targets, and GMI (global relative positional invariance) for the remainder—rather than relying on a single matching criterion. The CE-ReID module repurposes CBAM-style attention and depthwise separable convolutions to inject background context into ReID features, while GRNED introduces a novel sorted-distance-vector representation for cross-frame matching that has no direct analogue in prior MOT literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The progressive matching framing offers a practical, interpretable recipe for UAV MOT that addresses three distinct failure modes (weak small-target appearance, lens shake, occlusion) with targeted mechanisms rather than a monolithic model. However, the 6.5 FPS throughput limits real-time deployment, and the method's reliance on a locally trained YOLOX detector and a custom CE-ReID network adds engineering complexity. Open questions include whether the GMI strategy generalizes to scenes with very few targets (where relative-position vectors become degenerate), whether the 30-frame PKF window is optimal, and whether the progressive ordering can be learned end-to-end rather than hand-designed.
