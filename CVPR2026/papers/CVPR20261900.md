## Problem

Multimodal LLMs are vulnerable to indirect prompt injection (IPI) attacks that embed malicious instructions in images, videos, or audio, but no defense has been designed specifically for these cross-modal threats. Existing IPI defenses for text-only LLMs—prompt engineering, detection models, and adversarial training—are easily bypassed, modality-dependent, or generalize poorly to unseen attacks, leaving a critical gap in protecting MLLM-integrated applications.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard MLLM inference input: a text user instruction paired with multimodal data (image, video, or audio) that may contain a covertly injected instruction. It operates entirely on the model's internal activations during a single forward pass without modifying the input data, producing either the model's text response (when defense succeeds) or a pre-defined refusal string (when post-filtering detects a failed defense). The injection detection stage gates the entire pipeline, so benign inputs pass through with zero additional intervention.

## Method

ARGUS is a three-stage inference-time defense built on the discovery that 'following user instruction' and 'following injected instruction' are linearly separable in MLLM activation space, forming a multi-dimensional safety subspace. Stage 1 (Injection Detection) uses a lightweight logistic regression probe on early-layer activations to classify whether the input contains an injection, activating defense only on-demand. Stage 2 (Activation Steering) searches for an optimal defense direction within the safety subspace by training softmax-weighted coefficients over orthogonal probe directions via gradient descent to maximize user-instruction accuracy, then applies adaptive per-token steering strength computed in closed form to just cross a calibrated safety margin. Stage 3 (Post-filtering) reuses the original probe on late-layer activations to verify defense success, replacing the output with a refusal if the model still appears to follow the injected instruction. All three stages execute within a single inference pass, adding only a few milliseconds of overhead.

## Results

ARGUS achieves near-perfect safety across all three modalities, reducing Attacker Instruction Accuracy (AIA) to 0.1% (image), 0.1% (video), and 0.0% (audio), and Attacker Instruction Following Rate (AIFR) to 0.1%, 0.1%, and 0.0% respectively, compared to 25.1–28.2% AIA for the undefended model. Utility is well preserved: User Instruction Accuracy on clean inputs (UIA_clean) remains identical to the no-defense baseline (49.6% image, 37.6% video, 65.7% audio), and UIA_inject reaches 46.3% (image), 37.8% (video), and 58.0% (audio), approaching or exceeding the single-instruction upper bound. The additional inference overhead is negligible at only 3–6 ms per sample.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARGUS decisively outperforms both prompt-engineering baselines: System Prompt reduces AIA only to 10.7% (image) and 27.9% (audio), while Ignore Prompt actually worsens safety (AIA 31.5% image, 28.0% audio), likely because the counter-instructions draw the model's attention toward the injected text. Against the Noise baseline, ARGUS matches its perfect AIA of 0.0% on audio but substantially improves UIA_inject (58.0% vs. 42.8% audio; 46.3% vs. 34.3% image), since indiscriminate noise degrades the model's ability to process the original multimodal content. The Removal baseline achieves comparable safety on images (AIA 0.0% vs. 0.1%) and slightly higher UIA_inject (48.5% vs. 46.3%), but incurs 12,885 ms of extra inference time (≈4,300× ARGUS) and is entirely inapplicable to audio. Against Adversarial Training (the strongest learned baseline), ARGUS improves AIA from 2.3%→0.1% (image), 1.6%→0.1% (video), and 1.4%→0.0% (audio), while also gaining 5.2, 1.9, and 2.2 points on UIA_inject respectively, demonstrating better generalization to unseen injection tasks in the test set.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Representation Engineering (RepE) and activation steering from jailbreak defense (COSMIC, RepBend) to the distinct problem of indirect prompt injection (IPI), where injected instructions are semantically harmless and thus cannot be countered by steering toward 'rejection' or 'harmfulness' directions. It departs from existing IPI defenses (prompt engineering, detection-based removal, adversarial training) that are designed for text-only LLMs and suffer from modality dependence, fragility, or poor generalization. The key conceptual shift is identifying a 'safety subspace' encoding instruction-following behavior rather than content safety, enabling a modality-agnostic defense that operates on internal activations rather than raw multimodal inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic cross-modal (image, video, audio) IPI defense benchmark and the first demonstration that a single, lightweight activation-steering mechanism can defend against multimodal IPI with near-zero attacker success while preserving model utility, at only a few milliseconds of overhead. The finding that instruction-following behaviors are linearly separable in MLLM activations provides a principled foundation for future cross-modal safety work. Key limitations include evaluation on only two 7B models, a self-constructed benchmark, and restriction to single-instruction/single-injection scenarios; open questions include robustness to adaptive attacks, multi-injection settings, and generalization to emerging modalities (e.g., EEG, 3D).
