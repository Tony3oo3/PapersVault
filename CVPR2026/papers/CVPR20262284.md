## Problem

Existing white-box VLM jailbreak methods face two persistent limitations: suffix-optimization approaches (e.g., GCG, UMK) rely on gradient approximations over discrete token spaces, producing weak guidance that causes optimization to stall in local optima, while image-perturbation methods degrade visual fidelity and transfer poorly across models. This paper addresses both gaps by replacing gradient-based textual search with a gradient-free dissonance signal and by introducing a complementary image-phrase injection that exploits VLMs' cross-modal OCR sensitivity.

## Input / Output

The attack takes a standard RGB image and a harmful text prompt as input, and produces a 20-token adversarial text suffix (initialized as a repeating '!' sequence) paired with a modified image in which a short phrase is rendered at optimized font size (10–30), rotation (±15°), RGB color, and relative position (0.2–0.8). The method is gradient-free and gray-box: it requires only output logits from the target VLM, not full parameter access. A separate unaligned guide model (LLaMA-2-7B fine-tuned on Beavertails) is used internally to score dissonance and propose phrases but is not part of the deployed attack.

## Method

DGSIP alternates between two coupled modules. First, dissonance-guided suffix optimization computes a position-wise dissonance score D(v; pre) = p_g(v|pre)·log(p_g(v|pre)/p_t(v|pre)) between the target VLM F_t and an unaligned guide model F_g; tokens with high scores are those actively suppressed by safety alignment, and the top-K such tokens per suffix position form a candidate pool from which single-token substitutions generate 128 full-length suffix candidates per iteration. Second, when the suffix loss stagnates for a configurable number of rounds, the image-phrase injection module is triggered: the guide model generates M candidate phrases, selects the most steering-effective one via a multiple-choice prediction task, and Bayesian Optimization then searches the discrete rendering-parameter space (font size, rotation, color, position) to minimize the target model's cross-entropy loss on the harmful continuation. This dual-module design lets the attack escape local optima that purely textual search cannot overcome, while the dissonance signal provides a more informative and transferable optimization direction than raw gradients.

## Results

On AdvBench (50 queries), DGSIP achieves 100% Attack Success Rate (ASR) on MiniGPT-4 and InstructBLIP, and 98% on LLaVA, substantially outperforming all baselines. On MM-SafetyBench (1,130 queries across 13 topics), DGSIP attains average ASR of 96.37% (MiniGPT-4), 82.12% (InstructBLIP), and 92.74% (LLaVA). In black-box transfer attacks on commercial VLMs, DGSIP reaches 52% ASR on GPT-4o-Mini and 46% on Qwen 2.5-VL, the highest among all methods tested. DGSIP also runs roughly 2× faster than GCG (e.g., 101 s vs. 232 s per query on MiniGPT-4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGSIP dominates all four baselines on the primary AdvBench benchmark: it outperforms GCG by 22/66/48 percentage points on MiniGPT-4/InstructBLIP/LLaVA, UMK by 18/58/32 pp, VAJM by 44/76/72 pp, and FigStep by 64/82/82 pp. On the more diverse MM-SafetyBench, DGSIP maintains the highest average ASR across all three models (96.37%, 82.12%, 92.74%) and reaches 100% on multiple high-risk topics (Illegal Activity, Hate Speech, Financial Advice, Health Consultation) where baselines plateau at 80–95%. In the black-box transfer setting (Table 3), DGSIP achieves the best ASR on GPT-4o-Mini (52% vs. UMK's 49%) and Qwen 2.5-VL (46% vs. FigStep's 44%), while remaining competitive on Gemini 2.0 Flash (34% vs. VAJM's 36%). Notably, DGSIP is a gradient-free method requiring only output logits, yet it surpasses gradient-based white-box methods (GCG, VAJM, UMK) and the black-box FigStep, and also runs approximately 2× faster than GCG per query.

## Relation to Prior Work

DGSIP departs from the dominant gradient-based suffix optimization paradigm (GCG, VAJM, UMK) by replacing gradient signals with a gradient-free dissonance score computed between an aligned target VLM and an unaligned guide model, motivated by the finding that safety fine-tuning produces only shallow alignment [30]. The image-phrase injection module builds directly on FigStep's observation that VLMs can be jailbroken via typographic visual prompts [8], but extends it by jointly optimizing the phrase's semantic content (selected via the guide model) and its visual rendering parameters (via Bayesian Optimization) rather than treating the image as a fixed adversarial perturbation. The coordinated alternation between these two modules—triggered by a stagnation detector—represents a new architectural choice not present in prior single-modality or fixed-schedule joint optimization methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that safety-suppressed tokens identified via dissonance transfer across architectures and to commercial black-box VLMs (GPT-4o-Mini, Gemini 2.0 Flash, Qwen 2.5-VL), suggesting that current alignment mechanisms share common structural vulnerabilities that a single dissonance signal can exploit. The 2× speedup over GCG while achieving higher ASR makes the attack more practically deployable. However, the transfer ASR gains over the strongest baseline (UMK) are modest (e.g., 52% vs. 49% on GPT-4o-Mini), and the white-box 100% results are on older, smaller open-source models; whether the dissonance signal remains effective as commercial models adopt deeper or more heterogeneous alignment strategies remains an open question. The paper also highlights the need for defenses that go beyond surface-level token suppression, such as scrutinizing long-tail token distributions.
