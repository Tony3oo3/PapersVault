## Problem

Generating 4D scene-level motion for multiple interacting dynamic objects remains unsolved: existing data-driven methods lack the necessary multi-object interaction data, category-specific pipelines are not scalable, and prior distillation-based approaches produce only minor, artifact-laden single-object motions due to the high dimensionality of 4D deformations and architectural incompatibility with modern rectified flow video models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261969/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D scene of static meshes (converted to 3D-Gaussian Splatting representations for differentiable rendering) and a free-form text prompt describing how the scene should evolve over time. It outputs a full 4D deformation sequence supporting arbitrary-view 360° rendering, as well as dense per-object flow trajectories that can directly drive real-robot manipulation planning via a kinematic optimizer.

## Method

CHORD iteratively optimizes a hierarchical 4D motion representation by distilling guidance from a rectified flow video model (Wan 2.2) via a novel Weighted RF-SDS gradient, where the noise level is annealed according to the normalized training weight schedule so that coarse motion forms early and fine details refine later. The 4D representation combines a spatial hierarchy of bi-level control points (coarse and fine, optimized in a coarse-to-fine manner matching the noise schedule, with linear blend skinning for deformation) and a temporal hierarchy using a Fenwick tree that stores cumulative range-based deformations, enabling nearby frames to share parameters and enforcing temporal coherence. Two regularization terms—a rendered 3D flow magnitude loss for temporal smoothness and an ARAP loss on surface point clouds for spatial consistency—stabilize the optimization. After convergence, the learned Gaussian deformations are transferred back to the original meshes by substituting vertex positions into the control-point blending equations.

## Results

In a user study with 99 participants across six multi-object scenes, CHORD achieves 87.71% alignment and 87.37% realism, dramatically outperforming the next-best baseline TrajectoryCrafter (9.60% / 10.44%). On the automated VideoPhy-2 benchmark, CHORD attains the highest Semantic Adherence score (4.33) and the second-highest Physical Commonsense score (4.25), trailing only AnimateAnyMesh (4.5) whose objects frequently remain static. The method also supports long-horizon generation (four sequential actions), real-world scanned object animation, and zero-shot robot manipulation of rigid, articulated, and deformable objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHORD decisively outperforms all four baselines in the user study, with alignment and realism scores roughly 8–9× higher than the runner-up TrajectoryCrafter (87.71% vs. 9.60% alignment). On VideoPhy-2 Semantic Adherence, CHORD (4.33) edges out TrajectoryCrafter (4.17) and clearly surpasses Animate3D (3.83), MotionDreamer (3.42–3.50), and AnimateAnyMesh (3.50). On Physical Commonsense, AnimateAnyMesh scores highest (4.50) but the authors note this is a degenerate result: its objects often remain static, which is trivially physically plausible yet fails to follow the prompt. TrajectoryCrafter produces prompt-following motion but suffers from temporal inconsistencies due to multi-view video discrepancies, while Animate3D and AnimateAnyMesh fail to generate multi-object interactions at all. MotionDreamer (both DynamiCrafter and Wan 2.2 variants) exhibits severe artifacts from diffusion feature matching errors when fitting meshes.

## Relation to Prior Work

CHORD extends the Score Distillation Sampling paradigm from DreamFusion to the scene-level 4D setting, but makes two critical departures: it derives a novel SDS target for rectified flow models (the architecture underlying modern video generators like Wan 2.2), and it introduces a hierarchical 4D representation combining spatial control points (inspired by SC-GS) with a Fenwick tree–based temporal structure borrowed from theoretical algorithm design. Prior distillation-based 4D methods (4D-Fy, MotionDreamer, Animate3D) operate at the single-object level and suffer from artifacts due to the high dimensionality and temporal ill-regularization of 4D deformations; CHORD addresses this by injecting locality in both space and time. The paper also departs from category-specific kinematic models and data-driven 4D generators that require large-scale 4D datasets, positioning itself as a universal, category-agnostic pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first method to generate scene-level 4D motion of multiple interacting objects without category-specific inductive bias or large 4D training data, filling a clear gap between single-object animation and full scene dynamics. The rectified-flow SDS derivation is a reusable technical contribution that could benefit any distillation pipeline targeting modern video models. The robot manipulation application demonstrates practical downstream value, though it remains a proof-of-concept with limited evaluation. Open questions include scalability to larger scenes with many objects, the computational cost of iterative optimization versus feed-forward generation, and whether the generated motions satisfy true physical constraints beyond visual plausibility.
