## Problem

MIL-based weakly supervised video anomaly detection is highly susceptible to noisy supervision: in early training, normal snippets within abnormal bags are misselected as pseudo-anomalies due to unreliable anomaly scores, injecting false supervision that disrupts learning. Compounding this, genuinely hard abnormal instances also exhibit high training losses and are indistinguishable from noise, so naively discarding all high-loss samples removes valuable hard examples and impairs generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263860/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes untrimmed video clips segmented into non-overlapping snippets, supervised only by video-level binary labels (normal/abnormal) with no frame-level annotations. It outputs a continuous anomaly score per frame (or snippet), from which frame-level ROC AUC is computed. The VLM debiasing stage additionally extracts the middle frame of each snippet as a representative keyframe for zero-shot visual comparison, requiring no extra annotations or fine-tuning.

## Method

D²MIL is a plug-and-play two-stage framework that wraps around any existing MIL-based VAD model. The Denoising Module sorts per-bag contrastive losses each iteration and drops the top τ(T) fraction using a linearly increasing drop rate within each epoch, exploiting the observation that noisy samples produce high, volatile losses while true anomalies converge to low, stable losses. The Debiasing Module then feeds the discarded high-loss instance pairs (middle frames of the normal and abnormal snippets) into a frozen Qwen-VL-Max with a conservative zero-shot prompt asking which image clearly shows an abnormal event; only instances the VLM confidently identifies as more anomalous are recalled. A curriculum-learning schedule with a linearly increasing recall ratio ρ(T) gradually reintroduces these hard samples to avoid training instability. The final loss is computed over the union of retained low-loss pairs and recalled hard samples, and the entire pipeline requires no additional annotations or model fine-tuning.

## Results

The primary evaluation metric is frame-level AUC. On UCF-Crime, UR-DMU + D²MIL achieves 87.80% AUC, establishing a new state-of-the-art among MIL-based methods and surpassing ProDisc-VAD (87.12%), VERA (86.55%), and UMIL (86.75%). On ShanghaiTech, TEVAD + D²MIL reaches 98.13% AUC, outperforming ProDisc-VAD (97.98%). On MSAD, RTFM + D²MIL attains 88.52% AUC, the best reported result on that dataset. Across all three datasets, D²MIL consistently improves every integrated baseline by 0.16%–1.90% AUC.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²MIL is a plug-and-play module, so the paper evaluates it by bolting onto five MIL baselines (Sultani et al., RTFM, TEVAD, MGFN, UR-DMU) and reporting the delta. The largest gains appear on the weakest baselines: Sultani et al. improves by +1.90% AUC (83.34→85.24) and RTFM by +1.16% (83.14→84.30) on UCF-Crime, while the already-strong UR-DMU still gains +1.61% (86.19→87.80). The enhanced UR-DMU + D²MIL (87.80%) surpasses all non-MIL competitors on UCF-Crime, including the LLM-based Holmes-VAD (84.61%), the VLM-based VERA (86.55%), and the progressive-discrepancy ProDisc-VAD (87.12%). On ShanghaiTech, TEVAD + D²MIL (98.13%) edges out ProDisc-VAD (97.98%), and on MSAD, RTFM + D²MIL (88.52%) sets the best reported score. The method never degrades any baseline; the smallest improvement is +0.16% AUC (MGFN on ShanghaiTech), confirming that the denoising-debiasing pipeline is universally beneficial under the WS-VAD setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MIL paradigm for weakly supervised video anomaly detection (originally formalized by Sultani et al., CVPR 2018) by explicitly addressing noisy supervision during training. It departs from prior noise-mitigation efforts (UMIL, Normality-Guided MIL, ProDisc-VAD) that treat high-loss instances uniformly, by distinguishing harmful noise from genuinely hard abnormal samples and recovering the latter via a frozen VLM (Qwen-VL-Max) in zero-shot mode. The denoising module's dynamic loss-thresholding is a simple scheduling mechanism, while the debiasing module repurposes VLM reasoning as an auxiliary filter rather than a primary detector, contrasting with LLM-based VAD methods (Holmes-VAD, VERA) that require fine-tuning or serve as the main model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means D²MIL can be dropped into any existing MIL-based VAD pipeline without architectural changes or additional annotations, making it immediately adoptable by the WS-VAD community. The key open question is how sensitive the debiasing stage is to the underlying VLM's reasoning quality—the authors acknowledge this limitation, and the zero-shot comparison prompt may not generalize well to subtle or domain-specific anomalies. A broader open question is whether the loss-based noise/hard-sample distinction holds under different training dynamics (e.g., different optimizers, loss formulations, or top-k values), since the current validation is primarily on contrastive MIL losses with I3D features.
