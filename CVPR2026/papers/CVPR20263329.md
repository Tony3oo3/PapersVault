## Problem

Text-centric training for LVLMs—where only textual descriptions are available and no real images are provided—suffers from the image-text modality gap, causing representations learned from raw text to diverge from those needed for visual inputs at inference time. Existing remedies are inadequate: text-to-image models fail to faithfully preserve the semantics of the source text, and CLIP-style alignment methods assume fixed-dimensionality text features that do not hold for token-based LVLMs. The paper asks whether a simple, architecture-agnostic rendering strategy can bridge this gap while maintaining full semantic fidelity.

## Input / Output

During training, the method consumes a textual description (auto-generated from ground-truth images via Qwen2.5-VL-32B) and a text query, and produces a text response. The textual description is rendered as a 336×336 px RGB image with a white background and black text (font size capped at 32 pt, downscaled to fit), which is then fed through the standard vision encoder and projector as if it were a natural image. At inference time, no modification is required—the model simply processes real images through the same visual pathway.

## Method

TPI (Text-Printed Image) renders the given textual description directly onto a plain white canvas using a deterministic, training-free renderer (Python/Pillow), producing an RGB image that is processed by the LVLM's existing vision encoder and projector. The key insight is that routing text through the image encoder forces the learned representations to occupy the visual manifold, thereby closing the modality gap, while the explicit text content guarantees full semantic fidelity to the source description. Training is performed with LoRA fine-tuning (vision encoder frozen, LLM updated) on triplets of (TPI, query, response), requiring no architectural changes or inference-time modifications. The approach is validated across four LVLMs (LLaVA 7B/13B, Qwen2.5-VL 7B, Llama 3.2 11B Vision) and seven VQA benchmarks, showing TPI outperforms both text-only and T2I-based training and approaches ground-truth image performance. A secondary contribution demonstrates TPI as a data-augmentation strategy, where LLM-generated synthetic descriptions rendered as TPIs yield measurable gains even when seeded from only 1% of the original dataset.

## Results

Across four LVLMs (LLaVA 7B/13B, Qwen2.5-VL 7B, Llama 3.2 11B Vision) and seven VQA benchmarks, TPI training achieves the highest average score among all non-oracle methods: 49.97 (LLaVA 7B), 52.24 (LLaVA 13B), 77.94 (Qwen2.5-VL), and 72.27 (Llama Vision), consistently outperforming both Text-only and T2I (SDXL) baselines. TPI recovers a substantial fraction of the gap to GT-Image training, e.g., 49.97 vs. 55.58 for LLaVA 7B and 72.27 vs. 74.43 for Llama Vision. On Text VQA tasks (ChartQA, InfoVQA, DocVQA), TPI's advantage over T2I is most pronounced, with T2I relevance scores dropping to 23–26% of GT while TPI maintains 85–112%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares TPI against three baselines: Text-only (raw text fed directly to the LLM), Text-to-Image (SDXL-generated images), and GT-Image (oracle upper bound). TPI wins against both Text-only and T2I on the average score for all four models, with the largest margin over Text-only on Llama Vision (72.27 vs. 58.50, a +13.77 gap) and over T2I on Qwen2.5-VL (77.94 vs. 72.47, a +5.47 gap). T2I (SDXL) is particularly weak on Text VQA tasks: its relevance score drops to 23–26% of GT on ChartQA, InfoVQA, and DocVQA, while TPI maintains 85–112%, explaining T2I's large deficits on those benchmarks (e.g., ChartQA: TPI 23.28 vs. T2I 18.88 for LLaVA 7B). TPI approaches GT-Image on several tasks—within 1–3 points on ScienceQA and OK-VQA for most models—but still trails the oracle by 3–13 points on average, with the gap largest on Text VQA and DriveLM where visual detail matters most. In the data-augmentation setting (Section 5), TPI also outperforms both Text-only and T2I augmentation when seeded with only 1% or 10% of the original data, confirming its advantage generalizes beyond the main fine-tuning protocol.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions TPI as a third path for text-centric LVLM training, distinct from both CLIP-style alignment methods (which assume fixed-dimension text-image feature matching and don't transfer to token-based LVLMs) and T2I-based synthetic image generation (which loses semantic fidelity to the paired QA). It builds on the Self-Instruct data augmentation framework [56] for the LLM-driven description generation component, adapting it minimally to the LVLM setting. The key conceptual departure is treating the modality gap not as a representation-alignment problem to be solved architecturally, but as a routing problem: simply forcing text through the visual encoder via a deterministic renderer sidesteps the gap without any model modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TPI offers a drop-in, GPU-free training signal that is roughly three orders of magnitude cheaper than diffusion-based image synthesis while preserving semantic fidelity, making text-centric LVLM fine-tuning practical for resource-constrained or privacy-sensitive settings. The main limitation is that TPI implicitly assumes the vision encoder already possesses the visual concepts being described and has sufficient OCR ability; it cannot teach entirely new visual categories from scratch. Open questions include whether more sophisticated rendering (layout, color, shape cues) could extend TPI's applicability, and whether the data-augmentation pipeline can be further optimized beyond the minimal Self-Instruct adaptation used here.
