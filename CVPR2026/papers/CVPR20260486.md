## Problem

Existing diffusion-based methods for full-body human-object interaction generation (e.g., HIMO-Gen) treat multi-object interactions as purely kinematic prediction, producing physically implausible artifacts such as hand penetration, object floating, and misaligned contact timing. The core gap is the absence of explicit physical reasoning about why objects move the way they do, particularly in complex multi-object scenarios where contact dynamics are intricate.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language command (encoded via a frozen CLIP-ViT-B/32), object geometry (encoded via Basis Point Set representation), and the initial state of the human (SMPL-X parameters) and all N objects (translation in R³ and rotation in R⁶ via R6D) as conditions. It outputs a temporally coherent sequence of T frames, each containing full-body human motion (global translation, 22 body joint positions/rotations, 30 hand joint positions/rotations) and per-object translation and rotation. The generation is hierarchical: the coarse stage produces torso motion and object translations, while the fine stage refines hand articulation and object rotations conditioned on the coarse output.

## Method

PAMotion is a two-stage coarse-to-fine conditional diffusion framework. The coarse stage uses a dual-branch diffusion network (following HIMO-Gen) to generate global torso motion and object translations, supervised by kinematic and geometric consistency losses. The fine stage employs a separate dual-branch network to generate hand poses and object rotations, guided by a novel Physics-Aware Interaction Loss (L_phy) that exploits the observation that object acceleration reveals contact state: when acceleration deviates from gravity, the object must be in contact. Formally, L_phy = E_t[|log(d_t/β)| · |(a_t − g)·t|], where d_t is the minimum distance from the object to hands or other objects; the log term creates an asymmetric penalty (sharp for penetration, gradual for floating), while the acceleration term acts as a dynamic gate that activates the distance constraint only during contact. This design jointly penalizes floating and penetration without requiring explicit contact labels or rigid-body simulation, and the coarse-to-fine decomposition prevents the network from conflating high-level text-goal alignment with low-level physical fidelity.

## Results

On the HIMO dataset (three-object setting), PAMotion achieves R-Precision 0.6750, FID 1.3763, MM-Dist 3.7707, and Diversity 9.4573, outperforming HIMO-Gen by 14.0% on R-Precision and 3.395 on FID. On the ParaHome dataset, PAMotion achieves FID 0.7962 versus HIMO-Gen's 3.2398, a 4x improvement in distributional fidelity, while also leading on R-Precision (0.6364 vs. 0.5909) and MM-Dist (5.3356 vs. 5.4455). In the two-object HIMO setting, PAMotion leads on R-Precision (+5.45%) and FID (−0.653) but shows a slight MM-Dist degradation (+0.335) relative to HIMO-Gen.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAMotion is compared against three baselines: HIMO-Gen [53] (the primary dual-branch diffusion method it builds upon), IMoS [19] (intent-driven full-body motion synthesis), and priorMDM [74] (human motion diffusion as a generative prior). Against HIMO-Gen, PAMotion wins decisively in the three-object HIMO setting on all four metrics (R-Precision +14.0%, FID −3.395, MM-Dist −1.316, Diversity +0.51) and in the ParaHome setting on all four metrics (FID 0.7962 vs. 3.2398 being the most striking gap). In the two-object HIMO setting, PAMotion leads on R-Precision and FID but trails HIMO-Gen slightly on MM-Dist (3.9841 vs. 3.6491), suggesting that with fewer objects the text-motion alignment is already well captured by the baseline. Both IMoS and priorMDM are substantially outperformed across all settings, with FID gaps exceeding 3.5 points, confirming that the physics-aware interaction loss and coarse-to-fine decomposition provide meaningful gains over generic motion priors.

## Relation to Prior Work

PAMotion builds directly on HIMO-Gen [53], adopting its dual-branch diffusion architecture and mutual interaction module for the coarse stage, but departs from it in two key ways: introducing a physics-aware interaction loss that uses object accelerations as a proxy for contact state, and a coarse-to-fine pipeline that separates global torso/object translation from fine-grained hand pose and object rotation. Conceptually, it bridges the kinematics-based HOI generation line (which treats motion as geometric signals) and the physics-based line (which models forces and contacts) by embedding a lightweight, differentiable physical constraint into the diffusion training objective rather than requiring full rigid-body simulation. The text conditioning via CLIP and object geometry via BPS follow standard practice in recent motion generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and visible failure mode in diffusion-based HOI generation—object floating and hand penetration—that prior methods like HIMO-Gen cannot resolve, and does so with a surprisingly simple acceleration-based loss that requires no physics engine at inference time. However, the approach has clear limitations: it only models hand-object contact (ignoring torso contact), does not constrain grasp configuration (as shown by the bulb failure case), and is restricted to single-human scenarios. Open questions include whether the acceleration heuristic generalizes to fast or dynamic interactions beyond 'slow-motion' everyday scenarios, whether incorporating a pretrained grasp model (e.g., GraspNet) can fix the grasp-implausibility gap, and whether the framework extends to multi-human multi-object settings as the authors plan.
