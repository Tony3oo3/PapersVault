## Problem

Existing decentralized diffusion model (DDM) frameworks require all experts to share a homogeneous training objective and demand prohibitive compute (1176 A100-days on 158M images), limiting participation to well-resourced institutions. This paper addresses both constraints by enabling experts to train with different diffusion objectives (DDPM ε-prediction and Flow Matching velocity-prediction) in complete isolation, while reducing compute by 16× and data by 14× through architectural efficiency and pretrained checkpoint transfer.

## Input / Output

The system takes a free-form text prompt as input and produces 256×256 RGB images. Internally, experts operate on 32×32×4 VAE latents (256-token sequences after 2×2 patch embedding), and at inference a learned router dynamically selects and weights expert predictions per timestep. The key structural detail is that DDPM experts output ε-predictions while Flow Matching experts output velocity predictions, and a deterministic schedule-aware conversion unifies all outputs into a common velocity space for ODE-based sampling without any retraining.

## Method

The framework partitions a dataset into K semantic clusters using DINOv2 features and hierarchical k-means, then trains each DiT expert (with PixArt-α's AdaLN-Single conditioning for 30% parameter reduction) independently on its cluster using either DDPM or Flow Matching objectives. At inference, a router network predicts per-timestep cluster probabilities to weight expert contributions, while a schedule-aware deterministic conversion (inverting the DDPM forward map to recover x̂₀, then differentiating to obtain velocity) maps ε-predictions into the FM velocity space, enabling seamless ensemble sampling. Pretrained ImageNet DiT checkpoints are converted to Flow Matching by transferring patch embeddings, transformer blocks, and the final linear layer while reinitializing positional embeddings, text projection, and applying runtime timestep scaling (t_DiT = 999·t), achieving 1.2× faster convergence. The paper further provides a theoretical analysis (Proposition 1) showing that ε-prediction and velocity-prediction induce complementary timestep weightings (ratio 1/α_t²), explaining why heterogeneous objectives yield greater generation diversity.

## Results

Under aligned inference settings (CFG=7.5, 50 steps) on LAION-Aesthetics, the heterogeneous 2DDPM:6FM configuration achieves FID-50K of 11.88, outperforming the homogeneous 8FM baseline (12.45) and yielding higher intra-prompt diversity (mean pairwise LPIPS 0.631 vs. 0.617). Relative to the prior DDM work, the framework reduces compute from 1176 to 72 A100-equivalent GPU-days (16×) and data from 158M to 11M images (14×). On LAION-Art with DiT-B/2 experts, Top-2 decentralized inference achieves FID 22.60, a 23.7% improvement over the monolithic baseline (29.64) at matched aggregate FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is DDM (McAllister et al., 2025), which trains 8 homogeneous Flow Matching experts on 158M images requiring 1176 A100-days. The paper's framework achieves FID 11.88 (heterogeneous) and 12.45 (homogeneous) using only 72 A100-days and 11M images—a 16× compute and 14× data reduction. The authors explicitly note that DDM's reported FID range of 5.5–10.5 is achieved at substantially larger training scale, so absolute FID values are not directly comparable; the comparison instead demonstrates that competitive generation quality is attainable at a fraction of the resources. Internally, the heterogeneous 2DDPM:6FM configuration beats the homogeneous 8FM baseline on both FID (11.88 vs. 12.45) and intra-prompt diversity (LPIPS 0.631 vs. 0.617) under identical inference settings, confirming that objective diversity provides a quality gain at no additional training cost. On the LAION-Art monolithic comparison, decentralized Top-2 inference (FID 22.60) outperforms a single monolithic model (FID 29.64) by 7.04 points, while Full Ensemble combining all 8 experts underperforms (FID 47.89), highlighting that selective expert activation is critical.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Decentralized Diffusion Models (DDM) framework of McAllister et al. (2025), which trains isolated experts on semantically clustered data partitions and fuses them via a learned router, by relaxing the requirement that all experts share a homogeneous training objective. The heterogeneous conversion mechanism draws on the parameterization analyses of Kingma et al. (VDM) and Salimans & Ho, which relate ε-prediction and velocity-prediction losses through noise-level weightings, to derive a deterministic schedule-aware mapping at inference time. Architecturally, each expert adopts PixArt-α's AdaLN-Single conditioning for parameter efficiency, and the paper introduces a checkpoint conversion strategy that transfers pretrained ImageNet DiT weights into Flow Matching training, building on the rectified flow formulation of Liu et al.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 16× compute and 14× data reduction relative to prior DDM-scale training makes decentralized generative model training accessible to contributors with single 24–48 GB GPUs, potentially broadening participation beyond well-resourced institutions. However, the conversion mechanism relies on hand-tuned numerical safeguards (clamping, safe-α, adaptive scaling) and is empirically strongest only in the low-noise regime, leaving open whether it generalizes robustly across arbitrary schedules. The paper tests only a single DDPM:FM ratio (2:6) and two objectives (ε and velocity), so the optimal allocation and extension to x₀-prediction or consistency targets remain unexplored. The finding that indiscriminate Full Ensemble fusion (FID 47.89) dramatically underperforms selective Top-2 routing (FID 22.60) also raises open questions about how routing strategies should scale with expert count and heterogeneity.
