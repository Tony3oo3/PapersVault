## Problem

Recent SOTA PEFT multimodal trackers have inflated their tunable parameter budgets up to 16× over pioneering work, undermining the efficiency principle that justifies PEFT. Additionally, existing two-stream methods produce conflicting intra-modal attention maps due to domain gaps between RGB and X-modal inputs, hindering effective joint representation learning, while cross-modal fusion strategies remain stuck in a trade-off between the expressiveness of global attention and the efficiency of local fusion.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262611/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SEATrack takes a 128×128 template and 256×256 search region in both RGB and an X-modality (thermal, depth, or event), processed through a shared frozen ViT-Base backbone in a two-stream configuration. The prediction head outputs a target center score map, center coordinate offset, and bounding box size, trained with a combined focal + IoU + L1 loss. Only 0.6M parameters (AMG-LoRA + HMoE) are trainable, and the model runs at 63.5 FPS with ~1 GB memory on an RTX 4090.

## Method

SEATrack introduces two complementary modules inserted every 2 ViT layers. First, AMG-LoRA applies LoRA to the attention K/V projection matrices for domain adaptation, then uses Adaptive Mutual Guidance (inspired by classifier-free guidance) to dynamically align cross-modal attention maps via bidirectional learnable linear interpolation with scaling factors w_X and w_rgb, treating one modality's matching response as a conditional prior for the other. Second, HMoE performs cross-modal fusion through a hierarchical soft-routing mechanism: tokens are split into h sub-tokens, mixed across e expert heads at the sub-token level via a learnable gating matrix, processed by low-rank expert functions, and recombined at the token level through a reconstructed token-to-expert affinity matrix. This hierarchical design achieves ~35% FPS speedup over cross-attention fusion while maintaining comparable accuracy, and the entire trainable parameter budget is only 0.6M.

## Results

SEATrack achieves 71.6% PR on LasHeR, 87.8% MPR on RGBT234, 63.2 F-score on DepthTrack, 73.6 EAO on VOT-RGBD2022, and 77.1% PR on VisEvent, outperforming all PEFT-based trackers across all five benchmarks. It uses only 0.6M learnable parameters and runs at 63.5 FPS, compared to 14.8M/20.8 FPS for SDSTrack and 5.4M/10.3 FPS for XTrack. Against the strongest FFT baseline IIMF (72.4% PR on LasHeR, 182M params), SEATrack is within 0.8% while using roughly 0.3% of the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SEATrack outperforms all PEFT-based baselines on LasHeR PR (71.6 vs. 70.2 for TATrack/BAT, 69.1 for XTrack) and DepthTrack F-score (63.2 vs. 61.5 for XTrack, 61.4 for SDSTrack). On RGBT234 MPR, SEATrack leads all PEFT methods (87.8 vs. 87.4 for XTrack) and is competitive with FFT methods. On VOT-RGBD2022 EAO, SEATrack (73.6) slightly trails XTrack (74.0) but surpasses SDSTrack (72.8) by 0.8%. On VisEvent PR, SEATrack (77.1) is marginally below XTrack (77.5) but exceeds OneTracker (76.7) and SDSTrack (76.7). The most striking advantage is efficiency: SEATrack uses 0.6M learnable parameters at 63.5 FPS, versus 14.8M/20.8 FPS for SDSTrack and 5.4M/10.3 FPS for XTrack, while remaining within 0.8% PR of the strongest FFT method IIMF (72.4, 182M params).

## Relation to Prior Work

SEATrack builds on OSTrack (ViT-Base) as a frozen foundation tracker and extends the PEFT paradigm for multimodal tracking, departing from prior prompt-based and adapter-based methods (ViPT, BAT, UnTrack, OneTracker, SDSTrack, XTrack) that focus primarily on cross-modal fusion. Its key conceptual departure is introducing cross-modal attention alignment (AMG-LoRA) as a prerequisite to fusion, drawing an analogy to classifier-free guidance to dynamically reconcile modality-specific matching biases. For the fusion stage, it replaces quadratic-complexity cross-attention with a Hierarchical Mixture-of-Experts (HMoE) that operates at both sub-token and token levels, distinguishing it from prior MoE trackers that only perform expert-level routing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes the performance-efficiency dilemma in PEFT multimodal tracking by showing that cross-modal attention alignment—rather than ever-larger fusion modules—is the underexplored lever that breaks the trade-off, with AMG-LoRA delivering large gains at only 0.14M parameters. The 'alignment-before-fusion' design principle and the HMoE's 35% FPS speedup over attention-based fusion offer practical, adoptable components for the broader multimodal tracking community. Open questions remain around input-dependent alignment for greater robustness, handling spatially heterogeneous modalities (e.g., vision-language), and whether the approach generalizes beyond the three modality pairs evaluated.
