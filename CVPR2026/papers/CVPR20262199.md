## Problem

Publicly available mammography datasets are limited in vendor diversity, clinical annotation completeness, and explicit encoding of acquisition energy, making it difficult to develop AI models that generalize across real-world multi-vendor clinical settings. Existing benchmarks like CBIS-DDSM and INbreast are either film-based, single-vendor, or lack pathology-confirmed outcomes combined with BI-RADS and density labels, leaving vendor- and energy-induced domain shifts unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262199/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes 12–14-bit DICOM FFDM mammograms (CC and MLO views) at native resolutions ranging from 2364×2964 to 4800×6000 pixels, along with a reference low-energy image whose foreground CDF serves as the harmonization target. For the two-view classification tasks, feature embeddings from both projections are concatenated through a shared-weight backbone before a fully connected head. Outputs include a harmonized pixel-space image (background preserved as zero), a benign/malignant prediction, a BI-RADS risk group (binary or 3-class), and a four-class breast density label (A–D).

## Method

The core technical contribution is a foreground-only CDF histogram-matching algorithm that restricts histogram computation to breast pixels (intensity > 0) to avoid bias from large black background regions, then maps each source image's normalized CDF to a low-energy reference CDF via argmin over intensity bins, producing a monotone pixel-level transfer function. This harmonization is model-agnostic, applied identically across all backbones and tasks as a lightweight pre-processing step. For classification, the paper benchmarks four pretrained backbones (EfficientNet-B0, ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, Swin-T) in both single-view and two-view configurations, where the two-view variant processes CC and MLO through a shared-weight backbone, concatenates the resulting embeddings, and passes them through fully connected layers. Training uses 5-fold cross-validation with AdamW, cosine-free step LR decay, and model selection by best validation AUC. The LUMINA dataset itself (1,824 images, 468 patients, six vendors, high- and low-energy) provides the controlled multi-vendor, energy-annotated setting needed to stress-test these domain-shift effects.

## Results

On the primary breast cancer diagnosis task, two-view EfficientNet-B0 at 512² input achieves the best AUC of 93.54% (±3.88) and 85.04% accuracy, outperforming single-view configurations by 1.4–1.9 AUC points across all backbones. For BI-RADS two-class classification, EfficientNet-B0 reaches 92.80% AUC at 224², while for four-class breast density prediction, Swin-T achieves the highest macro-AUC of 89.43% at 224². Foreground-only histogram harmonization consistently improves AUC by 1–3 points across all architectures and tasks, and yields more focal Grad-CAM attention on lesion regions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks four architectures (ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, EfficientNet-B0, Swin-T) under a unified protocol rather than comparing against external methods. EfficientNet-B0 dominates the diagnosis task, achieving 93.54% AUC (two-view, 512²) versus 87.56% for ResNet-50 and 88.33% for DenseNet-121 under the same configuration — a 5–6 AUC-point margin. Swin-T, despite having 6.4× more parameters (27.72M vs. 4.34M), trails EfficientNet-B0 by 1.12 AUC points on diagnosis but wins the density task with 89.43% macro-AUC at 224², outperforming EfficientNet-B0 (84.72%) by 4.7 points. Two-view models consistently beat single-view by 1.4–1.9 AUC points across all backbones, and the shared-backbone variant matches the independent-backbone AUC (93.54% vs. 93.54% at 512²) while using 48% fewer parameters (4.34M vs. 8.34M). Foreground-only histogram harmonization improves AUC by roughly 1–3 points over raw inputs for every architecture and task, with the largest gains observed on low-energy images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical histogram-matching / CDF-alignment literature (Tu 2013, Shen 2007) by restricting the alignment to foreground (breast) pixels, thereby avoiding the well-known degradation caused by large black background regions in mammograms. Conceptually, it departs from feature-space harmonization methods like ComBat and federated normalization like HarmoFL by operating entirely in pixel space as a lightweight, model-agnostic preprocessing step rather than adjusting learned representations. On the dataset side, LUMINA positions itself against prior mammography benchmarks (CBIS-DDSM, INbreast, VinDR-Mammo, RSNA) by being explicitly multi-vendor FFDM with energy metadata, pathology-confirmed labels, and per-breast BI-RADS and density annotations in a single resource.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The LUMINA dataset fills a practical gap by providing a multi-vendor FFDM benchmark with energy annotations, enabling controlled stress-tests of vendor/energy domain shift that prior single-vendor or film-based datasets cannot support. The foreground-only CDF harmonization is attractive for clinical deployment because it is a simple, interpretable, model-agnostic preprocessing step that consistently improves AUC and Grad-CAM localization across CNN and transformer backbones. However, the dataset is limited to 468 patients from a single institution, and vendor distribution is heavily skewed (IMS accounts for 73% of patients), which constrains the strength of the multi-vendor generalization claims. Open questions include whether the harmonization transfers to other imaging modalities with similar background-foreground structure, and whether the two-view-over-four-view finding generalizes beyond this dataset's size and vendor mix.
