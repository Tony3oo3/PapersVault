## Problem

Reconstructing the 3D dark matter distribution from weak-lensing observations is a severely ill-posed inverse problem: a single 2D shear projection must be deprojected into a volumetric matter field under heavy shape noise and uncertain photometric redshifts. Existing approaches either apply handcrafted regularizers (Wiener filtering, sparsity) that oversmooth the non-Gaussian, filamentary cosmic web, or use neural ensembles that provide approximate but not exact Bayesian uncertainty. The paper addresses this gap by learning a data-driven generative prior from high-fidelity N-body simulations and combining it with the physical forward model for principled posterior sampling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a mock JWST COSMOS-Web shear catalog (~261 galaxies/arcmin² over 0.54 deg²) providing complex ellipticities with shape noise σ_e ≈ 0.25 and photometric redshifts with σ_z = 0.11(1+z), which together define a linear forward model e_obs = PQδ + ε. The lightcone is discretized into 20 lens planes of 128×128 pixels in comoving distance up to z ≈ 2. The output is a full posterior ensemble of 128 3D overdensity lightcones (20 planes × 128×128), from which both 3D volumetric maps and 2D projected convergence maps are derived as posterior means, along with per-voxel uncertainty estimates from sample statistics.

## Method

The prior is a redshift-conditional DDPM U-Net trained on 20,000 AbacusSummit-derived lightcones (Conicus3D), where the 3D distribution is factorized as a product of independent 2D lens-plane distributions p(δ^(z)|z), each learned by denoising 128×128 images conditioned on a one-hot redshift encoding. Posterior sampling uses a modified DAPS (Decoupled Annealing Posterior Sampling) algorithm that alternates between denoising with the learned score model and re-noising, while incorporating the linear WL likelihood (projection operator Q followed by convolution operator P) at each annealing step. The key modification replaces DAPS's diagonal Gaussian covariance in the inner-loop Langevin step with a Fourier-diagonal covariance Σ = F⁻¹ diag(P_k) F, where P_k is the empirical matter power spectrum, encoding the isotropy and translation invariance of the cosmic matter field. This spectral covariance preserves spatial correlations during the inner-loop sampling, enabling the posterior to retain small-scale power and avoid spurious line-of-sight correlations that plague smoother baselines.

## Results

On three simulated JWST COSMOS-Web-scale lightcone volumes, the diffusion-based posterior sampler achieves Pearson cross-correlation of 0.83–0.92 (blurred 3D), 0.18–0.27 (full-resolution 3D), and 0.87–0.98 (2D convergence) against ground truth, consistently outperforming both the Neural Ensemble and Wiener filter baselines across all metrics. In the out-of-distribution cosmology (massless neutrinos, Volume 3), the method still reaches 0.92 blurred-3D and 0.98 2D correlation, while the Neural Ensemble drops to 0.86 and 0.96 respectively. Posterior samples also match the ground-truth angular power spectrum across all scales, whereas both baselines over-smooth small-scale modes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: the Neural Ensemble estimator (Zhao et al., ICLR 2025) and the analytical Wiener filter reconstruction (Simon et al., MNRAS 2009). The diffusion-based method wins on every metric across all three volumes. The largest margin is in full-resolution 3D correlation on the out-of-distribution volume (0.18 vs. 0.09 for Neural Ensemble and 0.13 for Wiener), where the baselines struggle most with radial structure. In 2D convergence correlation, the advantage is more modest (e.g., 0.87 vs. 0.86 on Volume 1) but consistent, with the Wiener filter lagging further behind (0.77). Beyond point-estimate correlation, the method's posterior samples match the ground-truth angular power spectrum at all scales, while both baselines over-smooth small-scale modes; the Neural Ensemble also introduces spurious line-of-sight correlations absent in the true field. For uncertainty calibration, the method achieves a sample-std-to-MAE correlation of r = 0.92, slightly ahead of the Neural Ensemble's r = 0.90, and is the only method whose samples correspond to a proper Bayesian posterior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the diffusion-based posterior sampling framework of DAPS [45] to the 3D weak-lensing mass-mapping problem, replacing DAPS's isotropic Gaussian covariance with a Fourier-diagonal covariance informed by the matter power spectrum. It builds on a line of work applying score-based diffusion priors to 2D weak-lensing reconstruction [5, 6, 34], but moves to the substantially harder 3D lightcone setting where prior methods (Wiener filter, sparsity, neural ensembles) produce oversmoothed point estimates or only approximate uncertainty. The key conceptual departure is using high-fidelity N-body simulation outputs (AbacusSummit) as the data-driven prior rather than handcrafted regularizers, and packaging this as a publicly released 3D dataset (Conicus3D) that had not previously existed in this form.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

3D dark matter mass mapping is framed as a largely unsolved inverse problem, and this work provides the first diffusion-based framework that yields exact Bayesian posterior samples (not just approximate ensembles) with power-spectrum fidelity matching the true simulations. The public release of Conicus3D (20,000 lightcones plus OOD variants) is a significant practical resource that lowers the barrier for both astrophysics and computer-vision communities to tackle 3D reconstruction. Open questions include sensitivity to the specific simulation code and cosmological parameters used to train the prior, whether the redshift-independence assumption across lens planes holds at higher resolution, and validation on real survey data (JWST COSMOS-Web catalogs are not yet available). The method's robustness to moderate cosmology mismatch is encouraging but the OOD test is limited to a single alternative (massless neutrinos).
