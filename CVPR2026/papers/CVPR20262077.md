## Problem

As foundation models grow in size and are regularly updated, storing or accessing all previous versions becomes impractical, yet their collective knowledge is valuable. This paper introduces Continual Distillation (CD), where a single student learns sequentially from a stream of teacher models trained on different domains, without retaining access to earlier teachers or their training data. The central challenge is balancing Unseen Knowledge Transfer (acquiring domain knowledge the student has never encountered) against Unseen Knowledge Forgetting (losing previously transferred knowledge when learning from subsequent teachers).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262077/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each distillation step, the method consumes the current teacher's logits on a fixed unlabeled dataset (split into Internal Data shared by all teachers and External Data unknown to all teachers) plus the student's own logits from a checkpoint saved at the end of the previous step. No ground-truth labels are used; distillation is purely logits-based via temperature-scaled KL divergence. The output is a single student model whose class logits approximate the union of knowledge across all teachers, including domains never explicitly present in the distillation data.

## Method

SE2D (Self External Data Distillation) addresses UKF by augmenting standard teacher-to-student distillation with a self-distillation term restricted to External Data. At step t, the student S_t minimizes L_SE2D = L_KD(S_t, T_t; D^S) + L_KD(S_t, S_{t-1}; D_e), where the first term transfers knowledge from the current teacher on the full distillation set and the second term preserves the previous student's predictions on ED only. The restriction to ED is critical: it targets the fragile, teacher-derived knowledge about unseen domains rather than reinforcing already-stable internal-domain performance. The paper further shows that the ED-to-ID ratio and the semantic proximity of ED to teacher domains strongly modulate the UKT-UKF trade-off, with related external data yielding consistent gains while large domain gaps can degrade performance below the no-ED baseline.

## Results

On CIFAR20 with related external data (D4), SE2D achieves the best average accuracy of 76.17% across domains 0–3, outperforming Self-Distillation (74.93%), KL-divergence (71.36%), LS (70.39%), MDS (67.56%), and DKD (65.10%). On Digits with KMNIST as external data, SE2D reaches 87.00% average accuracy versus 85.58% for Self-Distillation and 72.30% for LS. On DomainNet with Sketch as external data, SE2D (48.01%) trails Self-Distillation (48.76%) but still surpasses all other baselines by 3–6 points. The upper bound (best teacher per domain) is 96.50% on CIFAR20, indicating a remaining gap of ~20 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SE2D outperforms all four distillation baselines (KL-divergence, DKD, LS, MDS) on average accuracy across CIFAR20+D4, Digits+KMNIST, and DomainNet+Sketch, with the largest margin over DKD (+11.07 pts on CIFAR20, +24.43 pts on Digits). Against Self-Distillation, SE2D wins on CIFAR20+D4 (76.17% vs. 74.93%) and Digits+KMNIST (87.00% vs. 85.58%), but loses on DomainNet+Sketch (48.01% vs. 48.76%), which the authors attribute to low teacher quality on that dataset. The advantage of SE2D is most pronounced on older domains (e.g., D1: 70.71% vs. 48.55% for KL-divergence), directly mitigating Unseen Knowledge Forgetting. On unrelated external data (MNIST as ED), all methods degrade substantially and SE2D's advantage narrows, confirming that the domain gap between external data and teacher domains is a critical factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions Continual Distillation (CD) as a paradigm shift from data-centric continual learning to model-centric continual learning: instead of a fixed model learning from a stream of datasets, a fixed dataset is used while the teacher model changes sequentially. It builds directly on the temperature-scaled KL-divergence distillation framework of Hinton et al. [15] and borrows the self-distillation checkpoint strategy common in continual learning (Li & Hoi [20]), but restricts self-distillation to external data only. The authors explicitly distinguish CD from standard multi-teacher distillation (which assumes simultaneous access to all teachers) and from CL-based distillation (which relies on replay buffers or past data), framing the sequential-teacher, fixed-data, no-replay setting as unexplored in prior literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: foundation models are too large to store, too expensive to re-access, and subject to API versioning, making a sequential distillation paradigm increasingly relevant. The discovery that external data (unknown to the teacher) enables Unseen Knowledge Transfer is a useful conceptual tool, but the method's effectiveness is inconsistent—SE2D underperforms on DomainNet and gains vanish when the external data domain gap is too large (e.g., MNIST as ED for CIFAR20 teachers). Key open questions include whether UKT/UKF dynamics persist at foundation-model scale (language, multimodal), whether the requirement to identify external vs. internal data is realistic in practice, and the security implications of uncontrolled knowledge injection through distillation data.
