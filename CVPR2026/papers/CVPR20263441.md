## Problem

Existing long-video understanding paradigms—caption-based, keyframe retrieval, and event/graph-based—fail to preserve global temporal structure, provide limited support for trustworthy evidence acquisition, and lack explicit cross-modal verification. They reason over isolated observations rather than through the evolving storyline that humans naturally construct, making them vulnerable to temporal fragmentation, modality drift, and insufficient evidence in long videos.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263441/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SVAgent takes a video sampled at 1 FPS as a frame database, a natural-language query, and a set of multiple-choice answer options. Initially 10% of frames are uniformly selected as the working pool; subsequent iterations add suggestion-driven frames. The output is a single predicted answer from the options, accompanied by modality-specific evidence citations and frame-importance recommendations that the meta-decision agent uses for final reconciliation.

## Method

SVAgent decomposes long-video reasoning into a closed loop of six cooperating agents: a Storyline Agent builds a query-conditioned narrative scaffold over sampled frames; a Hypothesis Agent proposes a tentative answer and extracts supporting evidence; two Determinantal Point Processes (one query-conditioned, one evidence-conditioned) independently retrieve key frames, and their intersection ratio serves as a coarse consistency test; Text and Vision Decision Agents then independently predict answers from captions and frames respectively; a Meta-Decision Agent verifies cross-modal agreement or reconciles conflicts using frame-importance weights; and a Suggestion Agent, triggered on disagreement, proposes additional informative frames for targeted exploration. The entire pipeline iterates up to T=3 refinement cycles, progressively updating the storyline and re-testing hypotheses until cross-modal decisions converge or the iteration budget is exhausted.

## Results

SVAgent achieves consistent gains of 5.5%–11.5% over the same backbone models across four long-video benchmarks using only 8 frames. On VideoMME (overall, w/o subtitles), Qwen2.5-VL-7B improves from 52.8% to 61.2% (+8.3) and Qwen3-VL-8B from 55.8% to 63.1% (+7.3). On MLVU, the largest single gain is +11.5% (Qwen3-VL-4B: 53.4→64.9), while LongVideoBench shows +6.7% (Qwen2.5-VL-3B: 53.0→59.7) and LVBench shows +7.4% (Qwen3-VL-8B: 33.4→40.8). Statistical significance is confirmed via paired t-tests (p = 7.09×10⁻¹⁰) and Wilcoxon tests (p = 9.77×10⁻⁴) on MLVU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is SVAgent against the same backbone without the agent framework: across all four model sizes (3B–8B) and all four benchmarks, SVAgent yields 5.5–11.5 percentage-point gains, with the largest improvements on MLVU (+7.3 to +11.5) and VideoMME long videos (+5.0 to +6.5). Against other open-source video agents, SVAgent (8 frames, 3B–8B) outperforms VideoMind (32 frames, 7B) on LongVideoBench (60.7 vs. 56.7 for 7B) and VideoMME overall (61.2 vs. 58.2), and beats Vgent (320 frames, 7B) on LongVideoBench (60.7 vs. 59.7) and LVBench (40.6 vs. –). However, SVAgent trails Video-RAG (64 frames, 72B) on LongVideoBench (60.7 vs. 65.4), MLVU (62.7 vs. 73.8), and VideoMME overall (61.2 vs. 77.4), reflecting the advantage of a much larger backbone and more frames. Against proprietary models (Gemini 1.5 Pro, GPT-4o at 1 fps), SVAgent with 8 frames on 7B–8B models remains competitive on VideoMME short/medium but lags on long videos, where the proprietary models' full-frame access provides a structural advantage. The frame-budget analysis (Fig. 4) further shows SVAgent at 8 frames (60.7%) already exceeds uniform sampling at 32 frames (59.8%), confirming that targeted retrieval compensates for the frame-count gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

SVAgent carves new ground by unifying three previously separate lines of work—agent-based video reasoning (VideoAgent, VideoTree), DPP-based diverse frame selection, and cross-modal consistency checking—into a single closed-loop multi-agent pipeline. Unlike caption-based or keyframe-retrieval methods that treat temporal structure implicitly, SVAgent introduces a persistent, query-conditioned storyline as an explicit temporal scaffold that is iteratively refined. The DPP mechanism repurposes determinantal point processes (originally for recommendation diversity) as a hypothesis-testing tool, where agreement between query-driven and evidence-driven selections serves as a coarse verification signal before cross-modal decision-making.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses a genuine gap in long-video QA: the inability of existing methods to simultaneously preserve temporal coherence, acquire reliable evidence, and verify cross-modal consistency. The consistent 5.5–11.5% gains across four benchmarks and multiple model sizes (3B–8B) demonstrate that structured, iterative reasoning can compensate for the limited context windows of smaller Video MLLMs. However, the six-agent architecture introduces substantial inference latency (up to ~57s at T=10) and multiple LLM calls per question, raising open questions about practical deployment at scale. Whether the storyline abstraction generalizes well to non-narrative or highly repetitive videos, and whether simpler retrieval strategies could approximate the DPP-based verification, remain unexplored.
