## Problem

Long-range LiDAR returns for small aerial targets (individual birds, UAVs) are extremely sparse and temporally uneven due to target motion, violating the dense, stable point-cloud assumptions of standard 3D detectors and fixed-threshold trackers. Existing LiDAR-based 3D detectors (PointPillars, CenterPoint, etc.) collapse when fewer than 5–10 points represent a target, and non-RL adaptive methods rely on hand-crafted criteria that do not couple detection acceptance with data association. The paper asks whether a single lightweight policy can jointly adapt voxel resolution, detection sensitivity, and association gating in a closed loop to maintain stable 3D tracking under dynamically varying sparsity.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sliding-window LiDAR scan stream (points with timestamps over a window of length W) and voxelizes it into a 4D spatiotemporal tensor T_t ∈ R^{Nx×Ny×Nz×2} whose two channels store per-voxel temporal compactness and frame-sparsity (the Temporal Dispersion Signature). The system tracks a single active aerial target, outputting a 6D state (3D position + 3D velocity) with a 6×6 covariance ellipsoid, updated via a Kalman filter. In addition to the tracked state, the RL policy emits a 5D continuous action (isotropic voxel scale, two proposal thresholds, association gate, and scoring quantile) that reconfigures the detector-tracker pipeline every frame; at inference all of this runs on label-free LiDAR-derived statistics only.

## Method

A3PRL formulates SAT perception as a closed-loop RL control problem. A sparsity-aware proposal stage uses two parallel heads: a TDS head that scores voxels by temporal compactness and frame sparsity, and a velocity-change head that flags abrupt motion via a per-voxel CUSUM alarm; their union feeds a fused confidence score with a log-SPRT sequential acceptance test to control false positives. A constant-velocity Kalman filter maintains the single-target state, with an adaptive Mahalanobis gating threshold for association. The core novelty is a 3-layer MLP policy (128 hidden units) trained with PPO that observes four label-free statistics (global TDS mean, foreground density, acceptance ratio, tracking-continuity score) and outputs the 5D action in one forward pass, jointly adjusting voxel resolution, detection thresholds, and association gate. Training uses privileged ground-truth trajectories to shape the reward (balancing geometric RMSE, temporal continuity, and acceptance regularization) plus domain randomization with MCD LiDAR noise, but at test time the policy runs entirely on LiDAR-derived statistics with no labels, achieving ~19% RMSE reduction over its fixed-parameter counterpart on unseen MMAUD V2/V3 venues.

## Results

On the MMAUD V2/V3 cross-venue benchmark (trained on V1, tested on unseen V2/V3), A3PRL (full RL-adaptive) achieves an overall 3D localization RMSE of 1.17 m, reducing error by ~19% relative to its non-RL counterpart (1.45 m) and by ~34% over the best unsupervised LiDAR baseline U3DTE (1.76 m). Supervised LiDAR detectors (PointPillars, VoxelNet, SPVNAS) degrade to 6.8–11.8 m RMSE under sparse long-range returns, while vision-only methods (DarkNet, YOLOv5s) perform well in daylight (1.5–3.2 m) but collapse at night (9.7–18.9 m). The full pipeline runs at 51.2 ms average per frame (p95 ≈ 65 ms), fitting within a 10 Hz LiDAR budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

A3PRL (1.17 m) outperforms all 13 baselines in Table 1. Among LiDAR-only methods, it beats the best unsupervised baseline U3DTE (1.76 m) by 34% and the supervised SPVNAS (6.84 m) by 83%, with the gap widening dramatically for older supervised detectors (PointPillars 9.32 m, VoxelNet 11.79 m) that assume dense point distributions. Against multimodal competitors, A3PRL rivals the best audio method TAME (4.74 m) by a 4× margin and the best visual method DarkNet (10.23 m overall) by 8.8×, while being lighting-invariant (identical day/night performance). The ~19% gain of the full RL policy over its own non-RL ablation (1.45 m) is larger than the gap between U3DTE and Deng et al. (2.58 m), confirming that adaptive parameter control is the dominant source of improvement. On the cross-LiDAR multi-UAV dataset, A3PRL reduces mean position RMSE from 0.078 m (U3DTE) to 0.068 m (~14%), and on an in-house RTK rig from 2.30 m to 1.55 m, demonstrating transferability across heterogeneous sensors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two lines of prior work: (1) fixed-parameter LiDAR 3D detectors (PointPillars, CenterPoint, VoxelNet) that assume dense, near-range automotive point clouds and collapse under the extreme sparsity of long-range UAV returns, and (2) adaptive perception methods that either use RL to reallocate sensing resources without downstream tracking feedback, or use hand-crafted rules to adjust voxel size without coupling to detection acceptance or data association. A3PRL unifies these by casting the entire detector-tracker pipeline as a closed-loop RL control problem, where a lightweight PPO policy jointly adjusts voxel resolution, proposal thresholds, and Kalman-filter association gating from label-free sparsity and continuity statistics. The Temporal Dispersion Signature (TDS) and velocity-change (VC) proposal heads are novel representations, but the underlying tools (SPRT, CUSUM, Kalman filtering) are classical statistical methods repurposed for this adaptive setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The privileged-training / label-free-inference paradigm makes the approach practical for deployment where ground-truth trajectories are unavailable at test time, and the demonstrated cross-venue generalization (train on MMAUD V1, test on unseen V2/V3) addresses a key concern for real-world anti-UAV systems. However, the framework is limited to single-target tracking, the 19% improvement is measured against the authors' own non-RL variant rather than a strong adaptive baseline, and the multimodal comparisons involve supervised methods from different training regimes, making the 'beats multimodal' claim less clean. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-target scenarios, whether the 5D action space generalizes to heterogeneous multi-LiDAR setups without retraining, and whether the privileged reward shaping can be replaced by fully self-supervised signals for broader applicability.
