## Problem

Existing zero-shot 3D visual grounding methods rely on 2D images during inference and/or perform multi-turn interactions with LLMs or VLMs, resulting in high latency (typically <1 FPS), heavy computational overhead, and deployment complexity. No prior zero-shot 3DVG framework can localize objects using only 3D point clouds and text at inference without calling external foundation models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262697/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, UZ3DVG consumes only a 3D point cloud and a natural language description, producing an axis-aligned 3D bounding box (center and size, 6 values) for the referenced object. During training, the pipeline additionally ingests RGB-D image sequences from open-world scenes, which are processed by open-vocabulary detectors (Grounding DINO) and segmentation models (SAM2) to generate 3D pseudo-labels and structured reasoning chains via a VLM. The training data is entirely auto-generated from scenes disjoint from the ScanRefer validation set to avoid leakage.

## Method

UZ3DVG introduces a two-phase paradigm: a training phase where the MSAG-RC module back-projects 2D segmentation masks into 3D space using depth and camera intrinsics to produce spatial pseudo-labels and structured reasoning chains (Anchor, References, Reasoning) via prompt-engineered VLM calls, and an inference phase where only a lightweight student network operates on point clouds and text. Reasoning Chain Distillation (RCD) transfers spatial reasoning logic from a large teacher network to the student through component-level and global-level alignment losses, plus reasoning-gain matching and foreground-background visual distillation. The Geometry-Aware Spatial Modeling (GeoSM) module augments SpConv features with sinusoidal-encoded global positional embeddings and KNN-based local relative displacement features, explicitly modeling the spatial predicates (e.g., 'left of', 'above') that appear in reasoning chains. A Text-Semantic-Guided Visual Detail Enhancement (TSVDE) module further refines visual features by similarity-based sampling of text-relevant regions during upsampling. This design achieves 7.69 FPS inference—approximately 38× faster than SeeGround—while surpassing it by 1.32%/1.68% on ScanRefer overall Acc@0.25/0.5.

## Results

On ScanRefer (Mask3D-based setting), UZ3DVG achieves Acc@0.25/0.5 of 45.42%/41.08% overall, surpassing the prior zero-shot SOTA SeeGround (44.10%/39.40%) by 1.32%/1.68%, and outperforming it by 6.49%/6.33% on the harder Multiple subset. Inference speed reaches 7.69 FPS, approximately 38× faster than SeeGround (0.20 FPS) and 45× faster than ZSVG3D (0.21 FPS) in the non-Mask3D setting. On NR3D, UZ3DVG attains 46.5% overall accuracy (40.8% on Hard, 50.1% on View-Independent), exceeding SeeGround by 0.4% overall and 2.5% on Hard.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among zero-shot Mask3D-based methods, UZ3DVG outperforms SeeGround [17] by 1.32%/1.68% on overall Acc@0.25/0.5 and by a larger 6.49%/6.33% on the Multiple subset, while running 38× faster (7.69 vs. 0.20 FPS). Against ZSVG3D [51], UZ3DVG gains 9.02%/8.38% overall and 12.79%/11.73% on Multiple, with a 55× speedup. In the non-Mask3D setting, UZ3DVG (43.13%/35.05%) surpasses ZSVG3D (20.00%/17.60%) by 23.13%/17.45% and VLM-Grounder [47] (51.60%/32.80% on a 250-query subset) on Acc@0.5. On NR3D, UZ3DVG (46.5%) edges out SeeGround (46.1%) by 0.4% and beats ZSVG3D (39.0%) by 7.5%, with the largest margin on the Hard split (40.8% vs. 38.3% vs. 31.7%). Compared to fully supervised SOTA methods (PC-CrossDiff 58.47%, TSP3D 56.45%), UZ3DVG trails by 13–14% in accuracy but requires no manual 3D annotations and achieves competitive speed (7.69 FPS vs. 4.07/12.43 FPS), highlighting the accuracy–annotation trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the zero-shot 3D visual grounding line initiated by ZSVG3D and SeeGround, which integrate LLMs/VLMs directly into the inference pipeline. The key departure is shifting VLM usage entirely to training: a VLM generates spatial pseudo-labels and reasoning chains from RGB-D data, which are then distilled into a lightweight student network via Reasoning Chain Distillation, so that inference requires only 3D point clouds and text. The GeoSM module borrows sinusoidal positional encoding ideas from 3DGraphLLM for global coordinate normalization, while the 3D backbone follows the SpConv convention established by prior supervised 3DVG methods (EDA, MCLN, TSP3D). The pseudo-label generation pipeline repurposes 2D open-vocabulary detectors (Grounding DINO) and segmentation (SAM2) with depth back-projection, bridging the 2D-to-3D annotation gap without manual labels.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 38× inference speedup (7.69 FPS vs. 0.20 FPS for SeeGround) while matching or exceeding accuracy makes zero-shot 3DVG practically deployable in real-time robotics and AR/VR settings where multi-turn VLM calls are infeasible. However, the approach still depends on VLM-generated pseudo-labels during training, and the ablation showing diminishing returns beyond ~30K pseudo-labels suggests a ceiling on what this paradigm can achieve without higher-quality or more diverse training data. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to truly novel object categories absent from the VLM's training distribution, and whether the distillation gap between teacher and student narrows with larger student networks. The reliance on RGB-D data from scenes disjoint from validation (but still from ScanNet) limits claims about truly open-world generalization.
