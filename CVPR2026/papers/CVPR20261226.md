## Problem

Existing OOD detection methods for diffusion-based inverse problems require calibration data from the shifted distribution, operate on full images rather than indirect measurements, and fail to detect subtle, spatially localized distribution shifts (e.g., small tumors in otherwise healthy CT scans). This paper addresses the gap of calibration-free, localized OOD detection directly from indirect measurements in inverse problems.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261226/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes indirect measurements—sparse-view CT projections (24 angles) or Gaussian-blurred RGB images—along with a diffusion prior trained exclusively on in-distribution data. It outputs a per-block OOD score (KLIP value) for each spatial block of size D_B × D_B, which can be thresholded for dataset-level detection or assembled into a pixel-wise localization heatmap. The expectation in the KL estimate is approximated by averaging over 5–8 posterior samples per measurement, and the timestep window [t_0, t_1] and block size D_B are the two tunable hyperparameters.

## Method

KLIP estimates the KL divergence between the prior p(x) and the posterior p(x|y) using the likelihood score s_l(x_t, y; t) collected at each timestep during posterior sampling, leveraging the identity D_KL(p‖q) = ½∫E[‖g(t)h(x,t)‖²]dt where h is the score difference. The key innovation is restricting this divergence in two ways: spatially, by partitioning the image into blocks so that a small OOD region is not diluted by in-distribution pixels, and temporally, by integrating only over a chosen timestep window [t_0, t_1] to exploit the fact that diffusion models generate low-frequency structure early and fine details later. This yields a calibration-free metric that requires no OOD samples, no additional training, and works with any posterior-sampling scheme (DPS, predictor-corrector, patch-based PaDIS) as long as a likelihood score proxy is available.

## Results

KLIP achieves AUC 0.776 for dataset-level tumor detection in sparse-view CT (predictor-corrector model), substantially outperforming the raw KL divergence baseline (D_KL, 0.602), NLL (0.535), and DiffPath (0.368). For image-level tumor localization, KLIP reaches AUC 0.878 vs. 0.856 for D_KL. In the Gaussian deblurring task with a DDPM backbone, KLIP achieves AUC 0.867 for scar detection and 0.772 for character feature detection, compared to 0.675 and 0.482 for D_KL respectively. KLIP also generalizes to the patch-based PaDIS model, achieving AUC 0.732 for tumor dataset-level detection and 0.889 for star image-level detection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KLIP consistently outperforms the raw KL divergence (D_KL) across all settings, confirming that both block and timestep restrictions are beneficial: e.g., 0.776 vs. 0.602 (dataset-level tumor, PC) and 0.867 vs. 0.675 (scar deblurring, DDPM). Against DiffPath [10], KLIP achieves a large margin on the realistic tumor task (0.776 vs. 0.368, dataset-level PC), while DiffPath is competitive on the easier star task (0.688 vs. 0.855). NLL/ELBO [35] is also clearly outperformed on tumors (0.535 vs. 0.776). CutPaste [22] and SimpleNet [24] operate directly on full images rather than indirect measurements, making their task inherently easier; they achieve near-perfect AUC on the synthetic star task (0.999, 0.993) but drop to chance-level on realistic tumors (0.505, 0.504), where KLIP (0.776) substantially outperforms both. Overall, KLIP is the only method that combines calibration-free operation, indirect-measurement input, and strong performance on subtle localized OOD features across all three diffusion model architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

KLIP builds on the posterior sampling framework for inverse problems (DPS [9], Song et al. [37]) and score-based diffusion models [35], repurposing the likelihood score estimates already computed during sampling as a free OOD detection signal. It departs from prior diffusion-based OOD detection methods (DiffPath [10], Eigenscore [33]) that operate on full images or detect only global shifts, and from conformal prediction approaches [3,4,20] that require OOD calibration data. The block and timestep restrictions draw on the information-theoretic literature on partition-based KL estimation [28,34,43] and on observations that diffusion models generate features at different scales at different timesteps [8,30,48], combining these into a localized, calibration-free detection metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KLIP addresses a practically critical gap: detecting subtle, localized abnormalities (tumors, lesions) in medical images from indirect measurements without any OOD training data, which is the realistic deployment scenario for open-world clinical AI. The approach's generality across diffusion architectures (whole-image, patch-based, DDPM) and inverse problems (CT, deblurring) suggests broad applicability. Key open questions include the hyperparameter sensitivity to OOD feature scale (Table 3 shows tuning on one artifact type degrades another), the inverse crime in evaluation, and whether the method transfers to real-world data with uncontrolled noise and model misspecification. Extending to 3D volumetric imaging and adaptive hyperparameter selection are natural next steps.
