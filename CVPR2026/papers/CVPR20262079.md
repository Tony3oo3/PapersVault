## Problem

Conventional stereo depth estimation outputs a single static depth map anchored to the nearest surface, fundamentally failing in see-through scenes where transparent or reflective materials create multiple coexisting depth layers. Existing multi-layer extensions rely on fixed feature representations with static multi-head regression, creating a representational bottleneck that forces conflicting geometries into a limited latent space and prevents accurate separation of overlapping surfaces. The paper asks whether depth estimation can be reformulated as an active, controllable process—akin to human focus shifting—rather than a passive single-pass prediction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a calibrated stereo image pair and a scalar control variable c ∈ [0,1] that maps linearly to a reference disparity d_ref(c) = (1−c)·d_max, where d_max is the scene's maximum valid disparity. The output is a single depth map aligned with the surface layer nearest to that reference plane. For multi-layer evaluation, c is swept across 30 uniform intervals and pixel-wise mean-shift clustering recovers discrete layers, though this post-processing is purely an evaluation protocol. The backbone features are computed once and reused across all c values, making the steerable inference efficient.

## Method

DepthFocus is built on the S²M² stereo matching backbone but replaces standard feed-forward and attention blocks in the multi-resolution fusion and iterative refinement stages with a composite C-block containing two parallel conditional modules: (1) a Conditional Mixture-of-Experts (C-MoE) where a router R(x,c) produces continuous weights over a small set of expert sub-networks (N≤3), and (2) a Direct Condition Injection (DCI) block that injects c via a single-item attention mechanism computing σ(q_x·k_c)·v_c. The backbone remains unconditional so the expensive feature extraction runs only once; steerability is achieved entirely in the downstream fusion and refinement stages. Training grounds c in physical geometry by assigning each pixel's supervision label as the largest ground-truth disparity not exceeding d_ref(c), enforcing a monotonic step-function behavior across c. An auxiliary segmentation head encourages the backbone to encode material semantics (glass, mirror), and the model is trained on 2M public stereo pairs plus 500k procedurally generated synthetic multi-layer samples from Blender.

## Results

On the Booster benchmark, DepthFocus achieves an EPE of 1.56 and Bad-4 of 4.70 on all surfaces, substantially outperforming the next-best S²M²-ft (EPE 2.53, Bad-4 8.53) and StereoAnywhere (EPE 2.77, Bad-4 14.21). On the challenging reflective/transmissive subset of Booster, the gap widens dramatically: DepthFocus records EPE 3.41 versus 7.69 for S²M²-ft and 34.78 for FoundationStereo. On Middlebury v3, DepthFocus attains EPE 0.67 and Bad-1 of 3.11, edging out S²M² (EPE 0.69, Bad-1 3.57) and FoundationStereo (EPE 0.78, Bad-1 4.39). On the proposed synthetic multi-layer benchmark, the 4-layer clustering variant reduces transmissive Layer-1 Bad-4 from 7.45 (S²M²-ft) to 3.27, and on the real-world bilayer benchmark, DepthFocus is the only method that recovers valid transmissive-surface depth (Bad-4 ≈ 8.7) where all baselines fail completely (Bad-4 ≈ 100).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DepthFocus outperforms all single-layer stereo baselines on the Booster benchmark, with the largest margins on reflective/transmissive surfaces (EPE 3.41 vs. 7.69 for S²M²-ft and 34.78 for FoundationStereo), confirming that the steerable conditional mechanism provides a stronger inductive bias for see-through regions than monocular-prior augmentation. On Middlebury v3, DepthFocus matches or exceeds S²M² on EPE (0.67 vs. 0.69) and Bad-1 (3.11 vs. 3.57) but trails slightly on the strictest Bad-4 threshold (0.60 vs. 0.54), indicating a minor trade-off at the extreme outlier level. Against multi-layer baselines on the synthetic benchmark, DepthFocus's 4-layer clustering reduces transmissive Layer-1 Bad-4 from 7.45 (S²M²-ft) to 3.27 and Layer-4 Bad-4 from 45.82 (ASGrasp-4layer-ft) to 26.99, while RAFT-4layer [42] and ASGrasp variants [36] degrade severely on intermediate layers (Bad-4 > 46). On the real-world bilayer benchmark, all single-surface methods (StereoAnywhere, FoundationStereo, S²M²-ft) fail catastrophically on the transmissive first layer (Bad-4 ≈ 99–100%), whereas DepthFocus recovers it at Bad-4 8.72 (60% transmittance) and 7.33 (80% transmittance). On LayeredFlow, DepthFocus achieves EPE 3.13 on Layer 1 versus 8.79 for S²M²-ft, and on the sparser Layer 3 it records EPE 1.97 versus 7.74 (RAFT-4layer) and 9.34 (ASGrasp-4layer-ft), a roughly 4× improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

DepthFocus builds directly on the authors' own S²M² stereo matching backbone [28], integrating conditional modules (Mixture-of-Experts routing and direct attention-based condition injection) into its multi-resolution fusion and iterative refinement stages. Conceptually, it departs from the multi-layer depth estimation line (LayeredFlow, ASGrasp, Wen et al., Xu et al.), which extends fixed backbones with static multi-head regression, by reframing the problem as a single-output conditional estimation steered by a scalar depth reference. The conditional modulation mechanism draws on MoE architectures and DiT-style scalar conditioning, but applies them to a physical disparity variable rather than data-driven gating, making the control explicit and interpretable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The steerable formulation offers a practical advantage for robotics and autonomous driving: a single backbone pass can be reused to query any depth plane, enabling intent-driven perception without re-running expensive feature extraction. However, the real-world evaluation is limited to a controlled two-layer acrylic-plate setup, and intermediate transmissive layers (2 and 3) still show substantially higher error than the first and last, suggesting the approach struggles with dense multi-layer light transport. Open questions include whether the method generalizes to more than two real-world layers, whether the target depth can be estimated automatically (e.g., from user gaze or task context), and how performance holds under extreme transmissivity or complex reflective interplay.
