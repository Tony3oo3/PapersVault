## Problem

Existing visual token compression methods for VLMs rely on attention weights (e.g., [CLS] attention or text-vision cross-attention) to identify redundant tokens, which introduces a positional bias that over-weights tokens near the text and, more critically, makes them incompatible with FlashAttention since FA does not expose attention matrices. This limits both the accuracy of token selection and the practical deployability of compression in real-world inference pipelines.

## Input / Output

ApET operates on the visual token sequence produced by a VLM's vision encoder (e.g., 576 tokens for a 336×336 image in LLaVA-1.5, 2048 tokens for 8 frames in Video-LLaVA, or variable-length sequences in Qwen2.5-VL) and compresses it to a target budget (e.g., 64, 128, or 192 tokens) in a fully training-free manner. The compressed tokens are then prepended to the tokenized text query and fed into the LLM decoder for autoregressive generation. Compression is applied at the vision encoder output and optionally at an intermediate LLM layer (layer 16 for LLaVA, layer 14 for Qwen2.5-VL), with no modification to model weights.

## Method

ApET evaluates token importance via linear approximation error rather than attention weights. It first selects M basis tokens (default M=10) using Farthest Point Sampling, then solves a linear system V ≈ BA to express every visual token as a linear combination of the basis set. The per-token L2 reconstruction residual serves as an information-content score, grounded in an information-theoretic bound linking minimal reconstruction MSE to conditional entropy H(V|S). Tokens with high residual (hard to approximate) are retained along with the basis tokens, while low-residual tokens are merged into their most similar retained token via average pooling. Because the entire pipeline operates solely on token representations without accessing attention matrices, ApET is fully compatible with FlashAttention and avoids the positional bias inherent in attention-based pruning.

## Results

On LLaVA-1.5-7B across 9 image-understanding benchmarks, ApET retains 95.2% of vanilla performance at 88.9% token compression (64 tokens), outperforming the second-best VisionZip (92.7%) by 2.5 percentage points. On Video-LLaVA-7B with only 256 tokens retained (87.5% compression), ApET achieves 100.7% average accuracy and 100.4% average score, actually surpassing the uncompressed vanilla model. In efficiency, ApET delivers a 1.46× total inference speedup and 1.38× prefilling speedup on LLaVA-1.5-7B, and a 1.51× prefilling speedup on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, while remaining the only method compatible with FlashAttention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ApET outperforms all five baselines (ToMe, FastV, SparseVLM, PDrop, VisionZip) at every compression ratio tested. At the most aggressive 88.9% compression on LLaVA-1.5, ApET leads VisionZip by 2.5% in average accuracy (95.2% vs. 92.7%) and PDrop by 8.6% (95.2% vs. 86.6%); the gap widens further at 94.4% compression on LLaVA-NeXT where ApET reaches 90.1% versus VisionZip's 88.2%. On video understanding (Video-LLaVA, 87.5% compression), ApET achieves 100.7% average accuracy, surpassing VisionZip by 6.3% and even exceeding the uncompressed vanilla model, while SparseVLM and PDrop drop to 84.6% and 82.4% respectively. A critical practical distinction is that ApET is the only method compatible with FlashAttention: on Qwen2.5-VL-7B, attention-weight-based baselines (SparseVLM, PDrop, VisionZip) require costly attention recomputation and underperform the vanilla model in total inference time, whereas ApET achieves a 1.30× total speedup and 1.51× prefilling speedup without any such overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of attention-weight-based visual token compression (FastV, SparseVLM, PDrop, VisionZip) by replacing attention signals with a linear-approximation-error criterion grounded in an information-theoretic bound on conditional entropy [43]. It retains ToMe's [6] average-merging strategy for the token-merging stage but replaces the similarity-based selection with FPS-sampled basis tokens and reconstruction-error ranking. The key conceptual shift is treating token importance as an intrinsic, position-agnostic property of the token representation rather than a relational quantity derived from cross- or self-attention, which also resolves the practical incompatibility with FlashAttention that plagues all prior attention-guided methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The FlashAttention compatibility is arguably the most practically significant contribution: existing attention-based compression methods silently degrade to slower-than-baseline inference on modern VLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL) that do not expose attention weights, making ApET's attention-free design a necessary prerequisite for real-world deployment. The finding that aggressive video compression (87.5% reduction) can *exceed* uncompressed performance suggests that redundant visual tokens in long video sequences actively mislead the model, reframing compression as a denoising mechanism. Open questions include whether the linear approximation generalizes to much larger token budgets (e.g., 4K images or hour-long videos), whether the theoretical MSE-entropy bound can be tightened to guide adaptive compression ratios, and whether the approach extends to audio or 3D modalities where token redundancy patterns may differ.
