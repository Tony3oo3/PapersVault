## Problem

Existing 3D reconstruction methods for autonomous driving target on-road scenes and optimize for visual fidelity, leaving underground parking environments—characterized by poor illumination, repetitive textures, and GPS denial—largely unexplored. More critically, for autonomous parking the system entry point is parking slot detection, so reconstruction must preserve slot-critical geometry for downstream perception rather than merely achieving photorealistic rendering. No benchmark or reconstruction framework specifically designed for parking-aware 3D scene modeling existed prior to this work.

## Input / Output

The method consumes four synchronized surround-view fisheye images (1280×960, 10 Hz) with COLMAP-calibrated extrinsics and a COLMAP sparse point cloud for Gaussian initialization. It produces a 3D Gaussian scene representation that renders novel fisheye views, a unified differentiable BEV/IPM map (1354×1632) geometrically consistent with existing parking-slot detectors, and parking slot detection results (corner points and edges) evaluated via precision/recall. The differentiable IPM pipeline is the key structural element: it allows gradients from frozen downstream detectors to propagate back into the 3D Gaussian parameters during optimization.

## Method

ParkGaussian adapts 3DGS to heavily distorted fisheye cameras by replacing the standard Jacobian-based EWA splatting with the unscented-transform (UT) projection from 3DGUT, which projects sigma points exactly through the non-linear camera model to yield stable 2D Gaussian footprints without per-camera Jacobian derivation. A fully differentiable IPM module then converts rendered fisheye views into a canonical BEV map, enabling frozen pretrained slot detectors (DMPR-PS and GCN-Parking) to operate on both rendered and ground-truth IPM images in a teacher-student configuration. The core slot-aware strategy constructs soft weight maps from corner-confidence channels (via a temperature-scaled sigmoid) and edge-segment rasterizations, mixes teacher and student weights with stop-gradient on the student term, and back-projects these weights into each fisheye camera to supervise reconstruction in both IPM and camera domains. Training proceeds in two phases: 20,000 iterations of standard photometric loss (L1 + D-SSIM), followed by 10,000 iterations adding a KL-divergence alignment loss on top-K confidence distributions and weighted L1 losses in both IPM and camera spaces, all optimized with MCMC-based Gaussian densification on a single RTX 4090.

## Results

On ParkRecon3D, ParkGaussian (with GCN-Parking) achieves the best novel-view synthesis quality across both scenes: PSNR 30.09 / SSIM 0.93 / LPIPS 0.20 on Scene 1 and PSNR 30.27 / SSIM 0.93 / LPIPS 0.20 on Scene 3. For downstream parking-slot detection on rendered BEV maps, the slot-aware strategy lifts GCN-Parking precision from 0.95 to 0.97 (Scene 1) and recall from 0.40 to 0.43, while DMPR-PS precision improves from 0.71 to 0.74 and recall from 0.08 to 0.10 on Scene 1. The method outperforms the strongest baseline (3DGUT) by 1.4–2.5 dB in PSNR and by 0.01 in SSIM on both scenes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ParkGaussian is compared against three reconstruction baselines: 3DGUT, Self-Cali-GS, and OmniRe. Against 3DGUT (the closest competitor, also using UT-based projection for fisheye cameras), ParkGaussian gains 1.4 dB PSNR on Scene 1 (30.09 vs 28.70) and 2.5 dB on Scene 3 (30.27 vs 27.80), while matching or slightly exceeding SSIM (0.93 vs 0.92). Against Self-Cali-GS, ParkGaussian leads by 6.3–10.2 dB in PSNR, indicating that self-calibration without explicit slot-aware supervision is insufficient for the challenging underground environment. OmniRe, designed for large-scale urban scenes with dense LiDAR and GPS, performs worst (PSNR 21.58–25.12, LPIPS up to 0.50), confirming that on-road driving reconstruction methods degrade severely in GPS-denied, low-texture parking garages. For the downstream parking-slot detection task, the slot-aware strategy consistently improves both precision and recall over the no-perception variant, with the largest gains observed for DMPR-PS (recall +0.02 on Scene 1) and GCN-Parking (precision +0.02, recall +0.03 on Scene 1), approaching ground-truth detection performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

ParkGaussian extends 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) to the parking domain by incorporating the unscented-transform (UT) projection from 3DGUT to handle strong fisheye distortion, and introduces a differentiable IPM module that bridges rendered fisheye views to the BEV space used by parking-slot detectors. It departs from prior 3DGS-based driving reconstruction work (OmniRe, Street Gaussians, S3Gaussian), which optimizes purely for photometric fidelity, by injecting task-driven supervision from frozen parking-slot detectors (DMPR-PS, GCN-Parking) directly into the Gaussian optimization loop. The slot-aware weighting strategy—mixing teacher and student confidence maps with stop-gradient on the student branch—is a novel mechanism not seen in prior reconstruction-for-perception pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ParkRecon3D is the first benchmark purpose-built for 3D reconstruction in underground parking, filling a gap between on-road driving simulators and the GPS-denied, texture-poor reality of parking lots. The core insight—that reconstruction quality should be measured and optimized by downstream slot-detection accuracy rather than pixel-level fidelity alone—could generalize to other perception-critical reconstruction tasks. However, the evidence is limited to two evaluation scenes out of four, and the paper acknowledges that specular reflections, repetitive textures, and motion blur remain unaddressed, leaving open whether the slot-aware strategy scales to larger, more diverse parking environments or to dynamic scenarios.
