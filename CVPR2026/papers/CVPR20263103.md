## Problem

VLM-powered computer-using agents (CUAs) frequently produce incorrect GUI actions—operation failures, inefficient error recovery, mistimed termination, and inaccurate element localization—yet reliable step-level verification before execution remains underdeveloped. The field lacks both diverse, high-quality cross-platform GUI critic training data (especially negative samples) and a public benchmark for evaluating critic models across Mobile, Web, and Desktop platforms.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263103/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The critic model receives a task instruction, the full interaction history (sequence of past screenshots and actions), the current screenshot as an RGB image, and the CUA's proposed next action, all formatted as a single prompt. It produces a free-text reasoning rationale followed by a binary Yes/No judgment of action correctness, using a pointwise scoring scheme rather than pairwise ranking so that each action is evaluated independently against an objective standard rather than forced into a relative preference.

## Method

OS-Oracle builds a scalable data pipeline that synthesizes ~160k positive and ~150k negative samples by applying rule-based transformations to existing trajectories to simulate four specific CUA failure modes (Operation Failure, Inefficient Error State Recovery, Mistimed Task Termination, Inaccurate Element Localization), with GPT-4o generating rationales for each sample. Training follows a two-stage recipe: supervised fine-tuning on the full dataset to establish discrimination and rationale skills, followed by Consistency-Preserving GRPO (CP-GRPO), a reinforcement learning stage that augments standard GRPO with a consistency reward computed via a hybrid rule-based lexicon-counting and model-based (Qwen3-8B logit comparison) polarity detector to penalize mismatches between the generated reasoning and the final judgment. The model is built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, and evaluation uses the newly introduced OS-Critic Bench (738 human-annotated instances across three platforms) plus dynamic evaluation where the critic gates UI-TARS-1.5-7B actions in OSWorld and AndroidWorld.

## Results

On OS-Critic Bench, OS-Oracle-7B achieves 68.02% overall accuracy and 72.81% F1, setting a new state-of-the-art among open-source VLMs and surpassing all proprietary models on the Mobile domain (70.78% Acc, 74.30% F1). It outperforms the strongest proprietary baseline, GPT-5 (68.16% Acc, 67.94% F1), on overall F1 by nearly 5 points, and beats Gemini-2.5-Pro (67.62% Acc, 70.16% F1) on both accuracy and F1. In dynamic evaluation, using OS-Oracle-7B as a pre-critic improves UI-TARS-1.5-7B's task success rate from 29.2% to 31.0% on OSWorld, while GPT-4o as critic degrades performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

OS-Oracle-7B (68.02% Acc, 72.81% F1) outperforms all open-source baselines by a wide margin: GUI-Critic-R1 (59.49% Acc), Qwen2.5-VL-7B (58.27%), UI-TARS-1.5-7B (57.45%), and ScaleCUA-32B (50.95%). Against proprietary models, it surpasses GPT-4o (62.20% Acc) and Claude-4.5-Sonnet (66.94%) on overall accuracy, and edges out GPT-5 (68.16% Acc) on F1 (72.81 vs. 67.94) while trailing by 0.14 points on accuracy. On the Mobile domain specifically, OS-Oracle-7B (70.78% Acc) beats every proprietary model including GPT-5 (69.18%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (68.26%). However, on Desktop, Claude-4.5-Sonnet (74.34% Acc, 77.46% F1) and GPT-5 (73.68% Acc) remain substantially ahead of OS-Oracle-7B (65.79% Acc), indicating a gap in desktop-specific critic capability. In dynamic agent-guidance experiments, OS-Oracle-7B consistently improves UI-TARS-1.5-7B on both OSWorld and AndroidWorld, whereas GPT-4o as a critic degrades agent performance due to inaccurate operational judgments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of GUI critic models (UI-CriticR1, UI-Genie, SE-WSM) that provide step-level action verification for computer-using agents, but departs from them in two key ways: (1) it targets cross-platform generalization (mobile, web, desktop) rather than the mobile-only setting of prior specialized critics, and (2) it introduces a scalable synthetic negative data pipeline to overcome the fundamental scarcity of erroneous GUI interaction samples. On the training side, it builds on GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) for VLM fine-tuning and extends it with a consistency-preserving reward that explicitly penalizes reasoning-judgment misalignment, a failure mode observed during RL training of critic models. The pointwise scoring formulation is adopted over pairwise ranking, following the observation that GUI actions often have multiple valid options, making forced binary preference ill-suited.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The cross-platform critic benchmark (OS-Critic Bench) and the 310k-sample training dataset address two practical bottlenecks that have limited the field: the absence of a unified evaluation protocol across platforms and the extreme scarcity of high-quality negative GUI interaction data. The finding that GPT-annotated negatives actually degrade performance below the untrained baseline suggests that naively using a powerful LLM to label errors introduces more noise than signal, pointing to the need for structured, error-type-aware synthesis. Open questions include whether the synthetic negative pipeline generalizes to novel error types not covered by the four targeted categories, whether CP-GRPO's consistency mechanism transfers to other VLM RL settings beyond GUI criticism, and whether the 7B model's gains over proprietary models on mobile will persist as those models improve their GUI grounding.
