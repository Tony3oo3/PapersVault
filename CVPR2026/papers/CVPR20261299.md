## Problem

Classical CNNs for remote sensing image scene classification struggle to generalize across heterogeneous geospatial domains with varying sensor types, illumination, and atmospheric conditions. Existing hybrid quantum-classical frameworks append small variational quantum circuits to pretrained CNNs as isolated classifiers with limited feature exchange, redundant parameterization, and are largely confined to simulation without validation on real quantum hardware. The paper addresses how to design a compact, hardware-feasible quantum-classical architecture that balances expressibility with trainability to avoid barren plateaus while maintaining strong classification accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB remote sensing or aerial image (C×H×W) as input, which is processed by the SFE module into a 64-dimensional normalized feature vector. This vector is partitioned into 16 segments of 4 values each, angle-encoded (scaled to [−π, π]) into 16 parallel 4-qubit quantum circuits. Each TQC measurement yields a 16-dimensional probability vector; the 16 vectors are concatenated into a 256-dimensional quantum-enhanced embedding that feeds a classical classifier producing a scene class label.

## Method

QuCNet couples a lightweight residual CNN encoder (SFE) with sixteen parallel 4-qubit Trainable Quantum Circuits (TQCs) using a specific gate sequence (Rx-CNOT-Rx-CNOT-Rx-Ry) at depth 12, guided by KL-divergence-based expressibility analysis to balance state-space coverage against gradient stability. A key architectural innovation is Hybrid Cyclic Weight Sharing (HCWS), which reuses only 4 unique parameter sets cyclically across the 16 circuits (q1→q2→q3→q4 repeated 4×), reducing independent trainable quantum parameters to 64 and mitigating barren-plateau effects. The Quantum Collapse Module (QCM) concatenates the 16 measurement probability vectors into a 256-dim embedding for final classification. The entire network contains only 87K parameters (85× smaller than prior hybrid models) and is jointly trained end-to-end via PennyLane/PyTorch backpropagation. Hardware feasibility is validated through transpilation to IBM native gates and inference on ibm_torino and ibm_fez processors with 512 shots per image.

## Results

QuCNet achieves 97.56% top-1 accuracy on EuroSAT, 98.00% on UC Merced, 88.84% on AID, 86.60% on NWPU-45, 92.38% on AIDER, 95.75% on USTC_SmokeRS, and 99.28% on IIITDMJ_Smoke, using only 77–87K parameters (0.08M). On CIFAR-10 (generalization test), QuCNet reaches 86.78% vs. 84.27% for its classical-only counterpart. Hardware-only inference on IBM Quantum processors (ibm_torino, ibm_fez) shows a 9–13% accuracy drop relative to the noiseless simulator, confirming NISQ feasibility.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QuCNet outperforms all prior hybrid quantum baselines (HybridQC, HQNNC, QTL-VGG16, HQTL-ResNet34) across every dataset: e.g., +3.14% over HybridQC on AID (88.84% vs. 86.13%), +9.17% on NPU-45 (86.60% vs. 79.32%), and +0.56% over HQNNC on EuroSAT (97.56% vs. 97.00%), while using 20–340× fewer parameters. Against large classical CNNs/transformers (MobileNetV2, STMSF, ATMFormer, ResNet50), QuCNet trails in absolute accuracy on AID (88.84% vs. 96.40% ATMFormer) and NPU-45 (86.60% vs. 94.95% STMSF) but achieves competitive results on EuroSAT (97.56% vs. 96.72% ResNet50) and dominates on AIDER (92.38% vs. 88.50% WATT-EffNet) and both smoke datasets (95.75% and 99.28%, surpassing all baselines by 5–10+ points). The key trade-off is parameter efficiency: QuCNet's 87K parameters are 85× smaller than the closest hybrid (HQNNC at 98K) and 320–1700× smaller than ResNet50/VGG16, making it the most parameter-efficient model in every comparison. On CIFAR-10, QuCNet (86.78%) beats its own classical-only ablation (84.27%) by +2.51%, confirming the TQC module's consistent contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of hybrid quantum-classical architectures for remote sensing scene classification (HybridQC, HQNN, QTL-VGG16, HQTL-ResNet34), which typically append a single variational quantum circuit as an isolated classifier to a pretrained CNN. QuCNet departs from this by deploying sixteen parallel 4-qubit Trainable Quantum Circuits with a Hybrid Cyclic Weight Sharing strategy, drawing on the barren-plateau and expressibility literature (McClean et al., Larocca et al., Holmes et al.) to guide circuit depth and gate-sequence selection via KL-divergence analysis. The multi-circuit parallel design with structured parameter reuse is the key architectural departure from prior single-circuit hybrids, and the explicit hardware-only inference on IBM Quantum processors (ibm_torino, ibm_fez) goes beyond the simulation-only evaluation that characterizes most prior quantum RSISC work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides one of the first demonstrations that a compact hybrid quantum-classical model can be deployed end-to-end on real NISQ hardware for a practical geospatial task, with a quantified 9–13% accuracy degradation relative to noiseless simulation. However, this degradation is substantial enough that on-hardware accuracy (76–86%) falls below strong classical baselines, raising open questions about whether the quantum component provides a genuine advantage over a well-tuned classical equivalent at the same parameter budget. The amplitude-encoding variant performing nearly as well as angle encoding further weakens the case for a specific quantum mechanism. Future work on higher-qubit architectures, error-mitigation strategies, and multimodal quantum-classical fusion will be needed to determine whether this paradigm scales beyond the current 4-qubit, 16-parallel-circuit regime.
