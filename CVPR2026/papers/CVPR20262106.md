## Problem

Existing deep learning models for satellite-based field boundary delineation are brittle under real-world deployment conditions: they produce tiling artifacts, discontinuous boundaries, and noisy predictions when brightness, spatial scale, temporal ordering, or geographic location changes. Standard benchmark metrics (IoU, F1) on held-out patches fail to capture these deployment failures, and no prior work had systematically evaluated geospatial foundation models or compared diverse segmentation paradigms under unified settings for global field mapping.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262106/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 8-channel bi-temporal Sentinel-2 L2A RGB-NIR imagery (4 spectral bands from planting and harvest seasons at 10 m resolution) as 256×256 pixel patches, and outputs a 3-class semantic segmentation map (background, field interior, boundary) that is vectorized into individual field polygons via connected-component analysis. At country-scale deployment, patches are processed with 25% overlap and Gaussian-weighted (apodization) averaging to eliminate seam artifacts, and the final polygons are serialized in the fiboa GeoParquet format for downstream analytics.

## Method

PRUE is a U-Net with an EfficientNet-B7 encoder (upgraded from the FTW baseline's B3) trained with a log-cosh Dice loss and boundary class weighting ω=0.75, combined with three targeted augmentations: channel shuffling for input-order invariance, brightness perturbation, and random resize for scale robustness. The paper's broader contribution is a systematic 'bake-off' evaluating 18 models across semantic, instance/panoptic, and geospatial foundation model families under identical training protocols, revealing that a well-tuned U-Net outperforms all alternatives on both accuracy and throughput (307 km²/s). For GFM experiments, frozen pretrained encoders are paired with a 3-layer MLP for bi-temporal token fusion and a custom convolutional decoder with pixel-shuffle upsampling. The authors also introduce four deployment-oriented metrics—translation consistency, input-order sensitivity, preprocessing invariance, and scale sensitivity—that quantify robustness to the distribution shifts encountered when tiling models over large scenes.

## Results

On the Fields of The World (FTW) benchmark, PRUE (U-Net + EfficientNet-B7) achieves 76% pixel-level IoU and 47% object-level F1, representing a +6% and +9% improvement over the FTW baseline (U-Net + EfficientNet-B3: 70% IoU, 38% F1). PRUE also attains the best AP₀.₅:₀.₉₅ (0.26) and AP₀.₅ (0.40) among all evaluated models. In terms of deployment efficiency, PRUE processes 306.94 km²/s with 67.1M parameters, roughly half the throughput of the lighter FTW baseline (623.28 km²/s) but far exceeding instance-segmentation and GFM alternatives (e.g., SAM at 0.17 km²/s, Clay at 10.98 km²/s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRUE outperforms the FTW baseline (U-Net + EfficientNet-B3) by +6% IoU (0.76 vs. 0.70) and +9% object F1 (0.47 vs. 0.38), while also improving recall from 0.72 to 0.83. Against DECODE (FracTAL-ResUNet), PRUE leads by +5% IoU and a substantial +26% F1 (0.47 vs. 0.21), indicating that DECODE's multitask design hurts instance-level quality. Among instance/panoptic models, PRUE surpasses Mask2Former by +8% IoU (0.76 vs. 0.68) and +8% F1 (0.47 vs. 0.39), while Mask2Former retains a slight edge in AP₀.₅ (0.44 vs. 0.40); SAM and Delineate Anything trail significantly in both pixel and object metrics. Among the 11 geospatial foundation models tested, the best performer Clay (ViT-Large) reaches only 0.67 IoU and 0.36 F1—still 9% and 11% below PRUE—despite having 5× more parameters, highlighting that GFM encoders' coarse patch-level representations limit boundary localization. PRUE's main trade-off is throughput: at 306.94 km²/s it is roughly half the speed of the FTW baseline (623.28 km²/s) due to the larger EfficientNet-B7 backbone, though it remains orders of magnitude faster than SAM (0.17 km²/s) or Clay (10.98 km²/s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the FTW benchmark (Kerner et al., AAAI 2025), which established a U-Net+EfficientNet-B3 baseline for global field boundary segmentation, and extends it through systematic ablation of loss functions, class weighting, encoder depth, and targeted data augmentations. It deliberately departs from the recent trend toward increasingly complex architectures—instance segmentation (Mask R-CNN, SAM, Delineate Anything), panoptic segmentation (Mask2Former), and geospatial foundation models (Galileo, Clay, TerraMind, etc.)—by demonstrating that a well-tuned U-Net with log-cosh Dice loss, boundary class weighting, and robustness-oriented augmentations outperforms all of them. The conceptual novelty lies less in architecture than in introducing deployment-oriented evaluation metrics (translation consistency, input-order sensitivity, preprocessing invariance, scale sensitivity) that expose failure modes invisible to standard patch-level IoU/F1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: PRUE produces country-scale field boundary maps for five diverse nations at a cost of under $10 per country, with near-zero sensitivity to the brightness, scale, and tiling artifacts that plague prior models in operational deployment. The finding that GFMs with 3–10× more parameters consistently underperform a 67M-parameter U-Net suggests that task-specific resolution and boundary-aware objectives remain more important than broad pretraining for fine-grained delineation. Open questions include whether the deployment metrics generalize to other GeoML tasks, whether next-generation GFMs with higher effective resolution could close the gap, and whether the augmentation recipe transfers to other agricultural contexts (e.g., very large monoculture fields vs. smallholder mosaics).
