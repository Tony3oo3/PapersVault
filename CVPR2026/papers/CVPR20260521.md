## Problem

Existing occupancy world models either discretize 3D scenes into finite-vocabulary tokens via VAEs (losing fine-grained geometric detail) or rely on dense BEV intermediate representations (imposing rigid geometric priors on feature interactions). Pure attention-based feed-forward architectures, which have proven highly flexible in language and 3D vision, remain unexplored for end-to-end occupancy forecasting that avoids both discretization and explicit BEV structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260521/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes multi-view camera images from the past T′ frames (within 2 seconds) and a planned ego trajectory specified as a sequence of 7-D pose states (x, y, z, quaternion) with timestamps, without assuming uniform temporal sampling. Output is a sequence of T future occupancy frames, each represented as N sparse anchors (N=600 or 4800), where each anchor contains M 3D points (M=128 or 16) with predicted per-point geometric offsets and C-dimensional semantic class logits. The random initialization of anchor points means the network effectively denoises stochastic 3D point clouds into structured occupancy, analogous to a conditional generation process.

## Method

SparseWorld-TC replaces dense voxel grids and BEV maps with a sparse anchor representation: each anchor pairs a set of randomly initialized 3D points with a learnable feature vector, and two MLPs decode per-point offsets and semantic logits. A deformable attention module projects each anchor center into multi-scale image feature maps (ResNet-50 or DINOv3-Base) across past frames to build sensor embeddings. A stacked fusion architecture then iteratively applies frame-level cross-attention (occupancy features attend to sensor features), frame-level self-attention (fusing trajectory embeddings via motion-aware layer normalization), and temporal self-attention across all future frames, progressively refining the random points into accurate occupancy. Trajectory conditioning is achieved by embedding each waypoint's position (via MLP on a 16-D homogeneous matrix) and timestamp (sinusoidal encoding) into spatiotemporal feature vectors that interact with scene features through attention. Training uses Chamfer Distance for geometric alignment plus focal loss for semantics, with a random ensemble strategy that supervises variable-length future sequences (L ∈ {2,…,T}) to support arbitrary forecasting horizons without architectural changes.

## Results

On the Occ3D-nuScenes benchmark (camera input), SparseWorld-TC-Large achieves 26.42% average semantic mIoU and 49.21% average geometric IoU over 1–3 s forecasting, outperforming the previous camera-based SOTA (COME) by 18.7% in mIoU (26.42 vs. 22.26) and 11.7% in IoU (49.21 vs. 44.07). With the DINOv3 backbone, SparseWorld-TC-Large* further improves to 29.89% mIoU and 53.52% IoU, surpassing even occupancy-ground-truth-input methods like DOME (27.10% mIoU) and I²-World (49.80% IoU). The smaller variant (SparseWorld-TC-Small) reaches 25.60% mIoU and 49.02% IoU at 9.35 FPS, offering a strong speed–accuracy trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest prior camera-based method, COME [33] (22.26% mIoU, 44.07% IoU), SparseWorld-TC-Large improves mIoU by 4.16 points and IoU by 5.14 points on average. The DINOv3-backed variant (SparseWorld-TC-Large*) extends this lead to 7.63 mIoU and 9.45 IoU over COME, and also surpasses occupancy-GT-input methods: DOME [8] (27.10% mIoU, 36.36% IoU) and I²-World [24] (39.73% mIoU, 49.80% IoU) on IoU. Among camera-input baselines, DTT [46] (19.60% mIoU, 36.11% IoU) and DOME-F [8] (18.25% mIoU, 28.84% IoU) are the next closest, trailing SparseWorld-TC-Large by 6.8 and 8.2 mIoU points respectively. The smaller model (SparseWorld-TC-Small) already exceeds all prior camera-based methods in IoU (49.02 vs. COME's 44.07) while running at 9.35 FPS—roughly 3× faster than COME-STC (2.75 FPS) and 2× faster than I²-World-STC (4.21 FPS). In long-term forecasting (Table 2, 1–8 s), SparseWorld-TC-Large achieves 22.33% mIoU and 45.35% IoU, outperforming COME (19.07% mIoU, 29.96% IoU) by 17.1% and 51.4% respectively, despite COME using ground-truth occupancy as input.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant VAE/VQ-VAE tokenization paradigm in occupancy world models (OccWorld, I²-World, DOME, COME) and from BEV-based intermediate representations, instead adopting a pure attention-based feed-forward transformer inspired by GPT and VGGT. The sparse anchor representation (groups of 3D points with feature vectors) is directly inherited from sparse perception models like SparseBEV, SparseDrive, and OPUS, but is repurposed here as the generative world-model state rather than a detection query. Trajectory conditioning borrows the motion-aware layer normalization (MLN) formulation, and deformable attention for sensor feature extraction follows the standard 3D sparse perception recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that neither discrete tokenization nor explicit BEV geometric priors are necessary for high-fidelity 4D occupancy forecasting, challenging a core architectural assumption in the field. The large performance gap over prior camera-only SOTA (≈19% mIoU) suggests that the representational bottleneck of finite-vocabulary VAEs and the structural rigidity of BEV grids were the primary limiting factors, not model capacity or training data. Open questions include whether the sparse-anchor formulation generalizes to more complex or unstructured 3D scenes beyond driving, how it scales to longer horizons or higher-resolution occupancy, and whether the 'from occupancy to sensors' extension (3D Gaussian decoding) can match dedicated rendering pipelines. Evaluation is limited to a single benchmark (Occ3D-nuScenes), so cross-dataset robustness remains unverified.
