## Problem

Fully decentralized deep learning methods coordinate clients by averaging parameters, gradients, or momentum in Euclidean space, which treats all clients equally regardless of data quality or quantity. Under severe non-i.i.d. partitions and client-size imbalance, this uniform averaging ignores the curvature of the parameter manifold and allows small or out-of-distribution clients to exert undue influence, degrading consensus quality. The paper asks whether a geometry-aware consensus that respects the Fisher-Rao metric can mitigate these failures without incurring the cost of explicit Fisher-matrix computation.

## Input / Output

Each client receives local batches of RGB images (CIFAR-100 at 32×32 for classification; Kvasir-SEG colonoscopy images at variable resolutions from 332×487 to 1920×1072 for segmentation) and produces either a 100-way class label or a pixel-level binary polyp mask. The unusual aspect is that inter-client communication exchanges expectation parameters (mean and second-moment vectors of a diagonal Gaussian posterior) rather than raw network weights, so the per-round payload has the same dimensionality as the model but carries distributional summaries instead of point estimates.

## Method

The method performs natural-gradient variational inference on the Fisher-Rao manifold, where each client maintains a diagonal Gaussian posterior over model parameters. By exploiting exponential-family duality, the algorithm maps to the expectation parameter space μ = ∇A(λ), in which natural gradients reduce to ordinary Euclidean gradients and linear mixing of μ across neighbors computes the exact forward KL barycenter—achieving curvature-aware consensus with O(d) cost instead of O(d³) Fisher-matrix inversion. The practical instantiation (KL-consensus Adam) runs standard Adam (EMA moments, bias correction, per-coordinate scaling) on the expectation-space gradient while gossiping μ to neighbors each round, so the only change relative to vanilla Adam is replacing Euclidean parameter averaging with expectation-space mixing. Convergence to a variance-controlled neighborhood is proven for convex objectives on connected graphs, with the rate governed by the spectral gap of the mixing matrix.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 with 8 decentralized clients, KL-consensus Adam achieves 60.10% test accuracy under balanced i.i.d. conditions (α=1.0, δ=1.0), approaching the centralized reference of 64.57%. Under severe non-i.i.d. partitioning (α=0.01) it retains 54.39%, and under extreme client-size imbalance (δ=0.02) it holds 52.82%, consistently outperforming the next-best baseline (QGM) by 3.5–13.5 percentage points. On Kvasir-SEG medical segmentation (8 clients, α=0.1), KL-consensus Adam reaches Dice 0.767 and IoU 0.676, versus 0.720/0.639 for QGM and 0.705/0.592 for GT-SGD, while centralized training achieves 0.796/0.699.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KL-consensus Adam outperforms all four decentralized baselines across every tested condition on both CIFAR-100 and Kvasir-SEG. The closest competitor is QGM [15], whose quasi-global momentum partially captures curvature; on CIFAR-100 (8 clients, α=1.0, δ=1.0) KL-consensus Adam leads by 5.78 points (60.10% vs. 54.32%), and the gap widens to 13.54 points under severe non-i.i.d. (α=0.01: 54.39% vs. 40.85%). GT-SGD [24] trails by 20+ points under heterogeneity (e.g., 57.55% vs. 27.73% at α=0.1), while Gossip-SGD [6] and SGP [3] collapse to near-chance accuracy (10–21%) under any non-trivial partition. On Kvasir-SEG (α=0.1), KL-consensus Adam achieves Dice 0.767 vs. QGM's 0.720 and GT-SGD's 0.705, a 4.7 and 6.2 Dice-point margin respectively. An ablation against Euclidean-Adam (same optimizer, Euclidean mixing instead of KL-barycentric mixing) confirms the gains come from the geometry of consensus rather than Adam's adaptivity: KL-consensus Adam leads by 3.83 points at α=0.1 and 19.21 points at α=0.01 on CIFAR-100.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends decentralized variational Bayes (DVB) from conjugate exponential-family models to deep networks by performing consensus in the expectation parameter space rather than the natural parameter or Euclidean parameter space. It builds directly on natural-gradient variational inference (Amari) and prior DVB methods (Li et al., Gong et al.), but departs from them by avoiding explicit Fisher-matrix computation and conjugacy requirements: linear mixing of expectation parameters yields a KL-barycentric consensus at O(d) cost, making the approach compatible with standard deep-learning optimizers like Adam. In contrast to Euclidean decentralized methods (Gossip-SGD, SGP, GT-SGD, QGM) that average raw parameters or gradients, the proposed method operates on the Fisher-Rao manifold, making updates curvature-aware and reparameterization-invariant.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically critical gap in fully decentralized learning for privacy-sensitive domains (e.g., multi-hospital training) where data is severely non-i.i.d. and client sizes are imbalanced, yielding ~20% accuracy gains over Euclidean baselines under the most stressed conditions. Key limitations include convergence guarantees only for convex objectives (deep networks are non-convex), the diagonal-Gaussian variational family restricting posterior expressiveness, and evaluation limited to two datasets and up to 16 clients. Open questions include whether the EF-duality-based consensus remains beneficial when the variational family is a poor approximation of the true posterior, how the method scales to very large models (billions of parameters), and whether the plug-and-play extension to other Fisher-preconditioned optimizers (KFAC, Shampoo) preserves the same gains.
