## Problem

Existing virtual try-on datasets are designed for single-garment tasks and lack support for full outfit composition involving multiple garments, accessories, layering order, and dressing techniques. No prior dataset simultaneously covers 40+ item categories, coherent multi-item outfit pairing, and structured annotations for layering and styling, leaving outfit-level VTON as an unaddressed benchmark.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260931/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each pair provides 3–12 reference garment images (average 4.48) alongside a model image and rich textual annotations (item descriptions, outfit-level descriptions, layering order, and styling techniques). The target look image is approximately 1024×1024 pixels. An alternative input format arranges all garment images into a 2D OOTD grid, which the paper shows improves consistency over passing individual images separately. The dataset spans 40 primary categories and 300+ fine-grained subcategories, with real and synthetic data maintained at roughly a 1:1 ratio.

## Method

The dataset is built through a four-stage pipeline: (1) collecting real garment and look images from fashion datasets, web sources, and outfit-compatibility research; (2) synthesizing outfit lists via an LLM guided by a 65-style fashion knowledge base, followed by retrieval-augmented item matching with a re-weighted sampling strategy that penalizes over-used items; (3) generating look images using Nano Banana (Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image) conditioned on OOTD grid inputs with explicit layering and styling prompts; and (4) filtering through VLM-based validation (Gemini-2.5-Flash), pose classification (DWPose), aesthetic scoring, and human expert review (only ~40% of synthetic looks survive). For evaluation, the paper introduces VLM-judged metrics (garment consistency, layering accuracy, styling accuracy) alongside classical FID/KID/SSIM/LPIPS, and benchmarks both VTON-specific models and general-purpose editing models to expose current limitations.

## Results

On the Garments2Look test set (Table 3), Nano Banana (NB) with N-reference input achieves the best overall FID (1.16) and KID (0.16), while NB with 2-reference (OOTD) input achieves the highest garment consistency (0.925) and SSIM (0.858). Among VTON-specific models, FastFit leads with FID 3.59 and KID 4.58, but its layering accuracy (0.13) and styling accuracy (0.34) are dramatically lower than editing models (e.g., NB N-Ref: layering 0.925, styling 0.748). On the simpler DressCode-MR benchmark (Table 2), FastFit outperforms Nano Banana (FID 9.31 vs. 15.22), revealing that editing models' advantage emerges specifically under the complexity of multi-item, multi-layer outfit-level try-on.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Garments2Look, all four VTON-specific models (IP-Adapter, BootComp, OmniTry, FastFit) are substantially outperformed by general-purpose editing models on layering accuracy (0.13–0.60 vs. 0.79–0.94) and garment consistency (0.46–0.62 vs. 0.77–0.98), with FastFit being the strongest VTON baseline (FID 3.59) yet still trailing NB (N Ref) by 3.4 FID points. Among editing models, Nano Banana and Nano Banana Pro dominate: NB (2 Ref) achieves the best FID (1.04) and garment consistency (0.925), while NBP (N Ref) reaches the highest garment score (0.984) and layering (0.936). QIE-2509 is a notable outlier—its N-Ref variant catastrophically fails (FID 33.18, KID 83.77) but recovers to competitive levels (FID 1.76) with the 2-Ref OOTD strategy. On the simpler DressCode-MR benchmark, the ranking inverts: FastFit (FID 9.31) and FitDiT (FID 14.72) beat Nano Banana (FID 15.22–15.98), confirming that editing models' advantage is specific to the multi-item, multi-layer complexity of outfit-level VTON rather than a general superiority. The 2-Ref (OOTD grid) strategy consistently outperforms N-Ref (individual garment images) across most editing models, suggesting that holistic outfit context is more effective than disparate references for current architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of two research threads: (1) image-based VTON datasets, which have progressed from single-garment (VITON-HD, DressCode) to multi-reference (DressCode-MR, M&M VTO, BootComp) but remain category-limited and lack outfit-level annotations; and (2) multi-reference image synthesis, where recent work (UNO, ComposeMe, Pico-Banana-400K, MultiBanana) uses advanced editing models to generate paired data. Garments2Look repurposes the LLM-driven outfit construction and editing-model synthesis paradigm from the second thread, but specializes it for VTON by enforcing layering order, styling techniques, and 40-category coverage—dimensions absent from both prior VTON datasets and general multi-reference synthesis benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset fills a clear gap: no prior VTON resource simultaneously covers 40 categories, 3–12 reference items per pair, explicit layering/styling annotations, and ~1M-pixel look images, making it a natural benchmark for outfit-level try-on research. The empirical finding that general-purpose editing models (Nano Banana, GPT-4o) outperform VTON-specific models on this harder setting—while the reverse holds on DressCode-MR—suggests that category diversity and compositional complexity, not raw generation quality, are the current bottleneck. Open questions include designing task-specific metrics beyond VLM-judged binary accuracy, and building end-to-end models that fuse the structured textual annotations (layering logic, styling verbs) with visual conditioning for precise multi-garment synthesis.
