## Problem

Existing multimodal drug-discovery methods align images and transcriptomics by drug identity, collapsing dose and cell-type variations into binary matching objectives and leaving image features disconnected from the mechanistic pathways that link chemistry to phenotype. When paired image-transcriptome data is scarce and weakly paired (same compound but different doses or batches across modalities), these identity-centric approaches fail to generalize to unseen interventions. The paper asks how perturbational transcriptomics can be used as privileged training signal to guide image representation learning toward mechanistic understanding while remaining image-only at test time.

## Input / Output

During training the framework consumes multi-channel Cell Painting fluorescence images, L1000 bulk transcriptomic profiles (978 landmark genes), drug molecular fingerprints (FCFP), cell-type labels, and per-modality dose values; at test time only the microscopy image is required. The model outputs a soft probability distribution over a learnable codebook of K drug prototypes (organized by molecular similarity on the unit hypersphere) and a fixed-dimensional image embedding suitable for downstream one-shot classification or drug-target retrieval. The codebook distributions serve as the distillation target, encoding fine-grained mechanistic similarity beyond hard drug labels.

## Method

TIDE (Transcriptome-Informed Distillation for image Encoding) comprises three components: a chemistry-aware codebook built by projecting drug FCFP fingerprints through an MLP into a learnable prototype matrix on the unit sphere; a transcriptome-conditioned teacher that fuses dose-aware image features (via FiLM conditioning), a dose-conditioned RNA encoding, and a cell-type encoding extracted from a scGPT encoder fine-tuned on perturbation-response prediction; and an image-only student that predicts the teacher's codebook distributions via KL divergence. The teacher is trained with cross-entropy against true drug labels, while the student is trained with a combined objective of distillation loss, teacher loss, and a self-supervised loss (DINO or MAE) applied to all images. A key design choice is encoding image dose δ_I and RNA dose δ_R separately within their respective modality features, enabling the teacher to reason about dose-dependent effects explicitly rather than treating mismatched doses as identical. The paper provides a risk-bound proposition showing that marginalizing over transcriptomic conditioning provably tightens the log-loss bound for the image-only predictor compared to training without RNA guidance.

## Results

On one-shot transfer to unseen interventions (Table 2), TIDE achieves the best Top-1 accuracy across all three datasets and both backbones: 42.09% on CPG-12 (ViT), 17.80% on CPG-Pilot (CA-ViT), and 6.04% on RxRx3 (CA-ViT), outperforming the strongest baseline CL(D) by 2.05, 2.63, and 0.11 points respectively. On unsupervised drug-target gene discovery (Table 3), TIDE achieves the highest AP on both datasets: 0.301 on RxRx3 (CA-ViT) and 0.120 on CPG-Pilot (CA-ViT), where baselines barely exceed random performance (e.g., MAE achieves only 0.245 AP on RxRx3 CA-ViT). The largest absolute gains appear on CPG-12, where TIDE improves Top-5 accuracy from 65.07% (CL(D)) to 71.07%, and on CPG-Pilot where Top-1 jumps from 15.17% (CL(D)) to 17.80%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIDE outperforms all four baselines on every dataset and backbone for both tasks. Against MAE [12], the gap is largest on CPG-12 where TIDE reaches 42.09% Top-1 versus MAE's 4.68%, reflecting that pure reconstruction-based SSL captures little intervention-specific structure. Against DINO [5], TIDE consistently gains 1.5–5.7 Top-1 points (e.g., 17.80 vs. 13.97 on CPG-Pilot CA-ViT), showing that transcriptomic distillation adds mechanistic signal beyond augmentation-rich self-supervision. Against CL(D) [10], which aligns images to drug molecular fingerprints, TIDE wins by 0.11–2.63 Top-1 points; the margin is smallest on RxRx3 (6.04 vs. 5.93) where chemical diversity is limited, but largest on CPG-Pilot (17.80 vs. 15.17) where pathway-level distinctions matter more. Against CL(R) [16], which aligns images to transcriptomic readouts by shared drug identity, TIDE gains 1.04–2.63 Top-1 points, confirming that explicit dose/cell-type conditioning and codebook-structured supervision outperform binary identity matching. On drug-target discovery, TIDE's advantage over CL(D) is most pronounced on RxRx3 CA-ViT (AP 0.301 vs. 0.255), where molecular-structure alignment alone cannot recover pathway-level target relationships.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in multimodal drug-cell learning, where contrastive alignment methods (CLIP-style) center supervision on drug identity and treat images as auxiliary signals for enriching compound embeddings. Instead, it inverts the information flow: perturbational transcriptomics (via a fine-tuned scGPT encoder) serves as privileged training signal that distills mechanistic knowledge into an image-only student, with a chemistry-aware codebook organizing supervision by drug mechanism rather than identity. The theoretical contribution—a risk-bound showing transcriptomic conditioning tightens the image-prediction loss—provides formal grounding absent from prior alignment-based approaches. The framework also explicitly handles weakly paired data (mismatched doses across modalities) through FiLM-style dose conditioning, a practical concern that prior identity-matching methods collapse into binary positives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a real bottleneck in phenotypic drug discovery: transcriptomic data is mechanistically rich but scarce and expensive, while microscopy is scalable but mechanistically shallow. By showing that even limited paired transcriptomic data (as few as a handful of samples per drug) can substantially improve one-shot transfer and drug-target recovery, the work suggests a practical path to grounding image-based screening in biological mechanism without requiring full transcriptomic profiling at scale. Key open questions include whether the training-distribution risk bound translates to generalization on truly novel mechanisms (the authors acknowledge this gap), whether the approach extends beyond L1000's 978-gene landmark panel to full transcriptomes, and whether the scFM fine-tuning step generalizes across cell types and perturbation modalities beyond small-molecule drugs.
