## Problem

Generating step-by-step procedural tutorials (painting, crafting, cooking, etc.) with AI remains challenging due to three coupled obstacles: severe scarcity of multi-task procedural training data, the need to maintain logical continuity and visual consistency across temporally ordered frames, and the difficulty of generalizing across diverse creative domains. Existing approaches are largely confined to single-task painting processes and rely on stroke-based rendering or minor motion adjustments, making them unsuitable for categories requiring structural transformations.

## Input / Output

The system accepts either a free-form text prompt describing a creation process or a single completed artwork image (for ReCraft), and produces a grid of temporally ordered frames at 1024×1024 resolution—either a 3×3 grid (9 frames) or 2×2 grid (4 frames) depending on the task category. Frames are arranged in a serpentine layout so that temporally consecutive steps are spatially adjacent within the grid. ReCraft additionally supports arbitrary-length output through recursive frame interpolation, where the model inserts intermediate steps between any two existing frames to densify the sequence.

## Method

MakeAnything is built on the Flux 1.0 dev DiT backbone and trained in two stages using conditional flow matching loss. The first stage introduces an asymmetric LoRA design: a shared low-rank matrix A captures general procedural knowledge across all 21 tasks, while task-specific matrices B adapt to individual domains, balancing generalization against overfitting in low-data categories (some with as few as 50 sequences). The second stage introduces the ReCraft model, which injects clean VAE-encoded latents of the final frame as unnoised condition tokens into the multi-modal attention layers, enabling image-to-process reconstruction with only minimal modifications to the Flux backbone. A key architectural insight is the serpentine grid layout, which exploits the DiT's tendency to attend to spatially adjacent tokens by ensuring temporal adjacency maps to spatial adjacency. For arbitrary-length generation, ReCraft is trained on 2×2 grids where diagonal frames serve as inputs and off-diagonal frames are predicted, learning a generalizable interpolation capability that can be applied recursively at inference time.

## Results

Across 21 procedural tasks, MakeAnything achieves GPT alignment scores ranging from 3.12 (Illustration) to 4.90 (Oil Painting, Jade Carving), with human alignment scores consistently above 3.97 and CLIP scores between 29.35 and 37.30. Coherence scores (GPT) range from 3.40 to 5.00, and usability scores (GPT) from 2.45 to 4.85, indicating strong logical step progression and instructional value. In the user study (41 responses, 15 prompts each), MakeAnything achieved the highest preference rate across all four criteria—Alignment, Coherence, Usability, and Consistency—compared to all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MakeAnything is compared against five baselines: ProcessPainter [37], Flux 1.0 [1], and Ideogram [16] for text-to-sequence generation, and Inverse Painting [4] and PaintsUndo [42] for image-to-sequence generation. In the user study (41 blind responses), MakeAnything achieved the highest preference rate across all four evaluation criteria (Alignment, Coherence, Usability, Consistency), clearly outperforming all baselines. Qualitative comparisons (Figs. 5, 7) show that ProcessPainter and PaintsUndo struggle with logical consistency and produce incoherent step transitions, while Flux and Ideogram generate visually appealing but temporally inconsistent frames. Inverse Painting, limited to painting-domain reconstruction, fails to generalize to non-painting tasks. The ablation further confirms that the Asymmetric LoRA design improves GPT alignment from 3.72 (mixed-data baseline) to 4.35 and CLIP score from 30.94 to 32.70, validating the architectural choice over standard single-task LoRA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of procedural sequence generation work (ProcessPainter, PaintsUndo, Inverse Painting) from single-domain painting to a unified multi-domain framework covering 21 creative tasks. It departs from prior temporal-model or stroke-based approaches by leveraging the in-context attention capabilities of Diffusion Transformers (Flux 1.0) with a serpentine grid layout, and introduces an asymmetric LoRA design (shared A-matrix, task-specific B-matrices) inspired by HydraLoRA but applied for the first time to image generation. The ReCraft model builds on the OminiControl-style token concatenation paradigm for image conditioning but avoids heavy control modules by reusing the pretrained Flux backbone with minimal modifications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 24K+ sequence, 21-task dataset is a significant resource that was previously absent, and the asymmetric LoRA provides a practical recipe for multi-task fine-tuning under severe data imbalance (some categories have only 50 samples). The ability to combine procedural LoRAs with off-the-shelf stylistic LoRAs for zero-shot domain transfer is practically useful for content creation pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 3D or physically grounded processes, whether the 9-frame/4-frame grid constraint limits expressiveness for longer or more complex procedures, and how well the method handles tasks requiring genuine causal reasoning rather than visual interpolation.
