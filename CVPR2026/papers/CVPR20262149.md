## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) lack fine-grained controllability: they cannot reliably manipulate a single semantic concept (e.g., changing 'dog' to 'cat') while preserving the rest of the image, with multi-concept modification success rates below 10%. The paper attributes this failure to semantic entanglement in patch token features, where self-attention aggregates global context and dilutes local semantics, rendering them imprecise handles for targeted manipulation.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a source text concept (e.g., 'dog'), and a target text concept (e.g., 'cat') as input, and produces an adversarial image x+δ with an L∞-bounded perturbation (default ε=16/255). The perturbation is crafted in a black-box transfer setting using an ensemble of K surrogate CLIP vision encoders (ViT-B/16, ViT-B/32, ViT-g-14, or single CLIP-L/14@336) with random crop augmentation, then evaluated against unseen target LVLMs on captioning and VQA tasks scored by an LLM judge on a ternary scale.

## Method

V-Attack targets the Value matrix (V) from the last multi-head self-attention block of each surrogate ViT encoder rather than the final patch features (X), because V suppresses the dominant global-context channels that entangle X, retaining high-entropy, disentangled local semantics. Two modules operate on the ensemble of V features: (1) Self-Value Enhancement applies self-attention with Q=K=V=V to refine each surrogate's value features, reinforcing salient local semantics; (2) Text-Guided Value Manipulation computes cosine similarity between projected value features and projected text embeddings to dynamically threshold and locate the subset of features aligned with the source concept, then optimizes an ensemble PGD loss that simultaneously decreases source-concept alignment and increases target-concept alignment only on those located features. This targeted, feature-level optimization bypasses the global entanglement in patch features, enabling precise local semantic substitution with strong cross-model transferability.

## Results

On MS-COCO, V-Attack (ensemble) achieves an average Attack Success Rate (ASR) of 0.567 on the Image Captioning (CAP) task and 0.560 on the Visual Question Answering (VQA) task, outperforming the previous best method M-Attack (0.450 CAP / 0.411 VQA) by 26% and 36% respectively. The method also demonstrates strong imperceptibility with L1 norm of 0.074 and L2 norm of 0.079, comparable to M-Attack while achieving substantially higher ASR. On ILSVRC2012 (Table 2), V-Attack (Ens) maintains its advantage over the Patch-Attack baseline across four 250-image subsets at both ε=8/255 and ε=16/255, with average ASR improvements of ~18–25% depending on the target model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

V-Attack (ensemble) decisively outperforms all five baselines on both CAP and VQA tasks across LLaVA, InternVL, DeepseekVL, and GPT-4o. Against the strongest prior method M-Attack [23], V-Attack gains +0.117 on CAP (0.567 vs 0.450) and +0.149 on VQA (0.560 vs 0.411), a relative improvement of 26–36%. The gap over SSA-CWA [9] is even larger (+0.294 CAP, +0.290 VQA), while the diffusion-based methods AdvDiff [14] and AnyAttack [56] are far behind (ASR < 0.06 on most models). Notably, V-Attack also achieves the best imperceptibility among high-performing methods (L1=0.074, L2=0.079), whereas AnyAttack and AdvDiff produce visible artifacts (L1 > 0.088) that risk triggering AI-generated-content detection. The single-surrogate variant of V-Attack (0.354/0.355) already surpasses M-Attack on LLaVA (0.554/0.542) due to encoder alignment, but the ensemble setting is essential for consistent transfer to other models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of black-box adversarial attacks on LVLMs (AttackVLM, SSA-CWA, M-Attack, AdvDiffVLM, AnyAttack), all of which optimize perturbations through the vision encoder's final patch-token outputs. The key conceptual departure is identifying the value features (V) within the transformer's multi-head self-attention block as a superior manipulation target, motivated by a channel-level analysis showing that V suppresses the global-context channels that entangle patch features (X). The Self-Value Enhancement module draws on SCLIP's self-attention refinement for dense features, while the Text-Guided Value Manipulation module introduces a text-prompt-driven localization step absent from prior LVLM attack methods. The overall optimization framework (PGD with ensemble surrogates and random-crop augmentation) remains standard.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that even the most advanced commercial LVLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-pro, GPT-o3) remain vulnerable to single-concept semantic manipulation with imperceptible perturbations, the paper underscores that current visual-language understanding lacks robustness at the fine-grained semantic level. The mechanistic finding that value features retain disentangled local semantics while patch features are globally entangled provides a reusable diagnostic for understanding where in a ViT encoder local information is preserved or lost. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-concept simultaneous manipulation (where prior methods already fail below 10%), whether the value-feature insight generalizes to non-CLIP vision encoders, and what architectural or training-time defenses could mitigate this class of attack.
