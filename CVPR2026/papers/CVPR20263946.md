## Problem

Whether to fine-tune or freeze the vision encoder during instruction-tuning of MLLMs remains unresolved, with influential models adopting contradictory strategies. A configuration-aligned benchmark reveals that existing VFT methods (full fine-tuning, LoRA, BitFit, prompt tuning) fail to consistently outperform the frozen baseline across multimodal tasks, an instability attributed to visual preference conflicts where the context-agnostic vision encoder receives divergent gradient signals from heterogeneous linguistic contexts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263946/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a text instruction (query) as input, producing a text response via standard next-token prediction. Unusually, the text instruction is consumed twice: once by the LLM as usual, and once by a frozen BERT encoder whose [CLS] token feeds the CVE module to derive a contextual vector that modulates the vision encoder's internal routing. The vision encoder (CLIP-ViT-L/14-336 by default) processes the image at 336×336 resolution, and only the CVE/CoMoE modules plus LayerNorm statistics are updated during instruction-tuning while all other vision parameters remain frozen.

## Method

CoVFT reformulates the visual posterior from p(z|I) to p(z|I,c), where c is a context-aware latent variable, and instantiates this through two modules. The Contextual Vector Extraction (CVE) module performs text-guided cross-attention at each ViT layer, using the BERT text embedding as query and concatenated visual+textual features as keys/values to produce a compact contextual vector that evolves synchronously with the encoder. The Contextual Mixture-of-Experts (CoMoE) module replaces FFN layers in the latter half of the ViT with N parallel expert networks (initialized from the original FFN), where a learnable router computes dense routing weights from c to combine expert outputs. Crucially, the routing weights scale each expert's gradient in a context-dependent manner, so samples with similar contexts reinforce the same expert subspaces while dissimilar contexts are separated, decomposing conflicting optimization signals and yielding stable, context-sensitive visual updates.

## Results

On 12 multimodal benchmarks under the LLaVA-1.5-7B pipeline, CoVFT achieves an overall average of 61.08%, surpassing the Freeze baseline (58.93%) by 2.15% and the best PEFT method BitFit (59.57%) by 1.51%. CoVFT attains state-of-the-art scores on 9 out of 12 individual tasks and leads all three task groups (General: 67.04%, Knowledge & OCR: 61.93%, Vision-centric: 55.81%). On the 13B model, CoVFT reaches 62.90% average, outperforming both Freeze (61.43%) and BitFit (61.43%). Notably, the 7B CoVFT model (61.08%) exceeds the average of the official 13B LLaVA-1.5 checkpoint (60.73%), despite the vision encoder comprising less than 5% of total parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoVFT consistently outperforms all six baselines on the 7B model: it beats the Freeze baseline (58.93%) by 2.15%, Full fine-tuning (59.29%) by 1.79%, VPT (58.19%) by 2.89%, SVPT (58.70%) by 2.38%, LoRA (59.04%) by 2.04%, and BitFit (59.57%) by 1.51% in overall average. Crucially, CoVFT is the only method that improves on all 12 benchmarks simultaneously, whereas Full FT, LoRA, and BitFit each surpass Freeze on only 6, 6, and 9 of 12 tasks respectively, exposing the instability of context-agnostic VFT. The largest relative gains appear on vision-centric tasks (e.g., MMVP: 36.67% vs. 28.00% for Freeze; ADE: 56.08% vs. 49.61%), confirming that context-aware routing is most beneficial where visual preference conflicts are strongest. On the 13B model, CoVFT (62.90%) extends its lead over Freeze (61.43%) and BitFit (61.43%) by 1.47%, and the 7B CoVFT model already exceeds the official 13B LLaVA-1.5 checkpoint (60.73%), demonstrating that optimizing the <5% vision-encoder parameters yields gains comparable to doubling LLM capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the LLaVA-1.5 architecture (vision encoder + MLP projector + LLM) and systematically benchmarks existing visual fine-tuning strategies—full fine-tuning, LoRA, BitFit, VPT, SVPT—within that unified pipeline. It departs from the pure-vision VFT literature, where fine-tuning consistently improves downstream performance, by identifying that the context-agnostic nature of vision encoders causes 'visual preference conflicts' under heterogeneous multimodal supervision. The proposed CoVFT framework repurposes two established mechanisms—text-guided cross-attention (analogous to BLIP-2's Q-Former) and Mixture-of-Experts routing (analogous to Switch Transformers)—but applies them jointly inside the vision encoder itself, conditioning expert selection on a task-specific contextual vector rather than on input tokens alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard VFT methods are unstable in MLLMs—failing to consistently beat a frozen encoder—challenges a widely held assumption inherited from pure-vision transfer learning and reframes the vision encoder as a context-sensitive component rather than a static feature extractor. Practically, the result that CoVFT on a 7B model surpasses the 13B frozen baseline highlights a low-cost optimization lever (<5% of parameters) that could reduce reliance on scaling LLM parameters. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ models, whether sparse MoE routing becomes competitive with larger instruction datasets, and how CoVFT interacts with emerging RL-based post-training pipelines where vision encoder instability is also reported.
