## Problem

Existing 6D object pose estimation benchmarks rely on controlled, third-person, well-lit conditions that fail to capture the extremes of real-world egocentric smart-glasses usage—severe motion blur from rapid head and object motion, dynamic narrow-spectrum illumination, and visual obstructions like smoke. This gap means state-of-the-art generalizable pose estimators, despite large-scale pretraining for unseen objects, show significant performance degradation precisely in the hands-busy, eyes-on-task conditions where egocentric vision is most needed.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 1.3M egocentric RGB frames (775.5 minutes at 30fps) captured at 1408×1408 fisheye resolution from Meta Aria glasses, with corresponding undistorted versions for evaluation on both data types. Ground truth 6D poses come from a 120fps OptiTrack motion capture system synchronized with Aria SLAM (1000fps) via Umeyama alignment and Kalman filtering for drift correction, yielding sub-millimeter accuracy. The 13 objects span sports equipment, industrial tools, and emergency supplies, with some scenarios involving multiple instances of the same object requiring instance-level disambiguation.

## Method

EgoXtreme constructs three scenarios—industrial maintenance, sports, and emergency rescue—each combining 8 lighting conditions (standard: normal/middle/high; extreme: low/headlamp/flashlight/warning beacon/exit sign) with optional smoke to create a multi-dimensional robustness test. The paper evaluates three state-of-the-art zero-shot RGB-only pose estimators (FoundPose+MegaPose, GigaPose+MegaPose, PicoPose) using GT bounding boxes to isolate pose estimation from detection errors, reporting ADD(S) recall and BOP metrics (MSSD/MSPD). A key empirical finding is that image restoration preprocessing (deblurring, dehazing, low-light enhancement) fails to improve and often degrades pose accuracy, indicating that perceptual quality gains do not translate to geometric accuracy under extreme conditions. Temporal tracking experiments further reveal that simple direct propagation fails under fast motion (up to 46% degradation at 0.3d), while a confidence-based hybrid strategy that selectively applies temporal priors based on per-frame prediction scores significantly improves performance in high-speed scenarios.

## Results

On EgoXtreme, all three state-of-the-art RGB-only zero-shot pose estimators show severe degradation under extreme conditions. PicoPose achieves the best overall performance, reaching 76.84% ADD(S) recall @0.3d in the maintenance scenario under standard lighting, but drops to 36.23% under extreme lighting in the emergency scenario (a 31.6%p drop). The sports scenario is the most challenging, with even the best model (GigaPose) achieving only 24.64% recall @0.3d under standard lighting and 19.04% under extreme. Adding smoke further degrades performance by ~10%p in maintenance (GigaPose: 62.77% → 52.86% @0.3d).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PicoPose consistently outperforms both GigaPose and FoundPose across all scenarios and conditions, achieving the highest ADD(S) recall @0.3d in every setting (e.g., 76.84% vs. 62.77% vs. 37.61% in maintenance standard). GigaPose is the second-best model, benefiting from its single-correspondence matching strategy that is more robust than FoundPose's sparse feature matching. FoundPose is the weakest baseline, particularly in the sports scenario where its direct sparse feature matching frequently fails to secure enough correspondences for PnP-RANSAC, yielding only 4.72% recall @0.3d under standard lighting. The critical finding is that all three models suffer dramatic degradation under extreme conditions: PicoPose loses 31.6%p in emergency (standard→extreme, no smoke), and GigaPose loses 9.91%p from smoke alone in maintenance. Image restoration preprocessing (deblurring, dehazing, low-light enhancement) fails to recover this lost performance and often worsens it by ~5–8%p, while a hybrid temporal tracking strategy partially recovers accuracy in the high-motion sports scenario (e.g., GigaPose on bat: 60.55% → 64.46% @0.3d).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of egocentric vision and 6D object pose estimation by introducing extreme perceptual conditions (severe motion blur, dynamic/narrow-spectrum lighting, smoke) that no prior benchmark has simultaneously captured. It departs from controlled third-person datasets (YCB-Video, LineMod, T-LESS) and from existing egocentric benchmarks (H2O, HOT3D) that, while first-person, were collected under relatively stable illumination and moderate motion. Rather than proposing a new estimator, the paper repurposes the evaluation of state-of-the-art zero-shot RGB pose pipelines (FoundPose+MegaPose, GigaPose+MegaPose, PicoPose) as a stress test, positioning EgoXtreme as the missing bridge between lab-validated generalization claims and the sensing extremes of real smart-glasses deployment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The dataset is likely to become a standard stress-test benchmark for egocentric pose estimation, given its scale (1.3M frames, 15 subjects, 13 objects, 8 lighting conditions, smoke) and its use of motion-capture ground truth. The negative result that image restoration (deblurring, dehazing, low-light enhancement) consistently fails to improve—and often degrades—downstream pose accuracy challenges a common preprocessing pipeline assumption and redirects research toward end-to-end robustness or temporal modeling. Key limitations (indoor-only collection due to OptiTrack, absence of 3D hand pose annotations) and the finding that only a confidence-gated hybrid temporal strategy (not naive propagation) helps under fast motion leave open the question of how to build pose estimators that are intrinsically robust to extreme egocentric degradation without relying on fragile per-frame features.
