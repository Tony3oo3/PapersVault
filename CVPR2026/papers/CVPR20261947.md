## Problem

Existing PBR material synthesis methods lack a unified latent representation bridging natural RGB appearance and physical material properties, resulting in fragmented task-specific pipelines (intrinsic decomposition vs. direct generation) and an inability to leverage large-scale RGB image data. This constrains both the quality and diversity of synthesized materials well below what modern RGB generators achieve.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261947/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts three input types depending on the task: free-text material descriptions, geometrically distorted RGB photographs (cropped from scenes), or planar RGB images. All tasks produce a spatially aligned set of five maps at native 1024×1024 resolution (upsamplable to 4K via RealESRGAN): a shaded RGB appearance map plus four PBR maps (basecolor, normal, roughness, metallic). The PBR maps are decoded as incremental refinements conditioned on the RGB latent rather than as independent outputs, ensuring tight spatial correspondence between appearance and physical properties.

## Method

The core contribution is a joint RGB-PBR representation that encodes an RGB image and four PBR maps as a 5-frame sequence through a fine-tuned 3D VAE (Wan2.2-VAE), producing two interdependent latents: one for RGB appearance (encoded independently) and one for PBR (encoded using cached features from the RGB branch, capturing only complementary physical information). A video Diffusion Transformer initialized from pre-trained video generation models and adapted via LoRA (rank 128) operates on these joint latents, unifying three tasks—text-to-material, image-to-material, and intrinsic decomposition—through different conditioning signals (text embeddings, distorted-image latents, or planar-RGB latents). Training uses a rectified flow objective on MatHybrid-410K, a hybrid corpus of ~6,000 complete PBR sets rendered under 32 HDR environments and on geometric primitives, combined with ~50,000 RGB-only planar images that supervise only the RGB latent to inject broad visual priors without disturbing the PBR latent distribution.

## Results

On the MaterialPicker test set, MatPedia achieves a CLIP score of 0.283 and DINO-FID of 1.31 for text-to-material generation, outperforming MatFuse (0.261 / 1.90). For image-to-material generation, MatPedia attains the highest CLIP scores across all PBR channels (e.g., 0.943 basecolor vs. 0.833 MatFuse, 0.813 Material Palette) and DINO scores (e.g., 0.907 basecolor vs. 0.649 MatFuse). For material decomposition, MatPedia achieves the lowest MSE (0.009 basecolor vs. 0.058 Material Palette, 0.122 RGB↔X) and LPIPS (0.664 basecolor vs. 0.716 Material Palette, 0.770 RGB↔X), indicating substantially more accurate PBR reconstruction.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MatPedia outperforms MatFuse [45] on both text-to-material (CLIP 0.283 vs. 0.261; DINO-FID 1.31 vs. 1.90) and image-to-material generation, with the largest gap in basecolor DINO score (0.907 vs. 0.649, +0.26). Against Material Palette [28], MatPedia wins on every channel for both image-to-material (e.g., basecolor CLIP 0.943 vs. 0.813) and material decomposition (e.g., basecolor MSE 0.009 vs. 0.058, a 6.4× reduction). RGB↔X [55] is beaten on all material decomposition metrics, with MatPedia achieving roughly 2–13× lower MSE and 0.05–0.10 lower LPIPS across channels. ControlMat [44] and MaterialPicker [31] are compared only qualitatively (Fig. 4) since neither is publicly available; visually, MatPedia produces sharper textures and more accurate material attributes. The ablation in Table 1 further shows that removing the RGB-only hybrid training data degrades CLIP from 0.283 to 0.275 and increases DINO-FID from 1.31 to 1.62, confirming the benefit of the mixed corpus.

## Relation to Prior Work

MatPedia repurposes the 3D VAE and DiT backbone from large-scale video generation (Wan, HunyuanVideo) for PBR material synthesis, drawing an analogy between temporal frame coherence and the physical coupling between RGB appearance and PBR maps. It departs from prior material generation work (MatFuse, MaterialPicker, ControlMat, Material Palette, RGB↔X) by replacing fragmented task-specific pipelines with a single joint RGB-PBR latent representation where PBR maps are encoded asymmetrically conditioned on RGB, enabling compact encoding and the incorporation of large-scale RGB-only data. The rectified-flow training objective and LoRA-based task adaptation follow standard practices from the video diffusion literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying text-to-material, image-to-material, and intrinsic decomposition in one architecture at native 1024×1024 resolution, MatPedia removes the need for separate pipelines and unlocks the use of abundant RGB image data to overcome PBR dataset scarcity—a practical bottleneck that has limited prior methods to 256×256 or lower. The hybrid training strategy (RGB-only + RGB-PBR paired) is a reusable recipe for any modality-pairing problem where one side has far more data. Open questions include whether the joint compression can support tileable generation (currently limited by spatial coupling), whether additional PBR channels (height, subsurface scattering) fit naturally into the 5-frame formulation, and whether the approach generalizes to 3D texture synthesis beyond planar maps.
