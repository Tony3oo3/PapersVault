## Problem

No open framework exists for multi-task image-to-poster creation that simultaneously handles local editing (entity-preserving modifications like rescaling, filling, extending, and identity-driven generation) and global creation (concept-driven tasks like layout- and style-driven generation). Existing open-source editors produce misaligned layouts, distorted text, and weakened aesthetic harmony on poster-specific tasks, while commercial systems that handle these cases well are closed-source and costly at scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260295/cropped_page0_idx18.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a reference image (single or multi-image, e.g., a photograph, product shot, or existing poster) paired with a natural-language editing prompt specifying the desired poster modification or creation. It outputs a complete poster image with rendered text, coherent layout, and consistent style. The six supported tasks span spatial completion (extending, filling), aspect-ratio adjustment (rescaling), subject preservation (identity-driven), and holistic redesign (layout-driven, style-driven), with prompts in both English and Chinese.

## Method

PosterOmni builds on Qwen-Image-Edit via a four-stage pipeline: (1) task-specific SFT trains two separate LoRA experts—one for local editing and one for global creation—under flow-matching loss to avoid interference between pixel-level correction and high-level composition; (2) task distillation trains a unified student under joint supervision from both experts (rather than parameter merging) with an auxiliary text-rendering loss to preserve character-level quality; (3) a unified PosterOmni Reward Model (Qwen3-VL + MLP head, trained with Bradley-Terry preference loss) provides both general aesthetic and task-specific reward signals; (4) Omni-Edit RL applies DiffusionNFT (forward-process reinforcement) using the unified reward to jointly optimize local precision and global aesthetic quality. The key architectural insight is that decomposing poster creation into local vs. global regimes, training specialized experts, then distilling into a single student avoids the destructive interference of naive mixed-task training while retaining expert-level performance on both task families.

## Results

On PosterOmni-Bench (evaluated by Gemini-2.5-Pro on a 1–5 scale), PosterOmni achieves an overall score of 4.27 (English) / 4.37 (Chinese), surpassing all open-source baselines. The strongest open-source baseline, Qwen-Image-Edit[2509], scores 3.51/3.46, yielding gains of +0.76/+0.91 for PosterOmni. Against the top commercial system Seedream-4.0 (4.28/4.34), PosterOmni is essentially on par, slightly exceeding it on the Chinese benchmark (4.37 vs. 4.34) while trailing by 0.01 on English. The largest per-task improvements over the base model appear in style-driven (+1.08/+1.53) and layout-driven (+0.76/+1.38) generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PosterOmni outperforms all seven open-source baselines across every task. Over the strongest open-source model Qwen-Image-Edit[2509], gains range from +0.27 (rescaling, Chinese) to +1.53 (style-driven, Chinese), with the most consistent advantages in identity-driven (+0.92/+0.86) and style-driven (+1.08/+1.53) generation. Against the other open-source editors (ICEdit, Step1X-Edit, BAGEL, OmniGen2, FLUX.1 Kontext, UniWorld-V2), PosterOmni leads by 0.7–2.3 points on overall score, with particularly large margins on layout-driven and style-driven tasks where general-purpose editors struggle. Compared to the closed-source Seedream-4.0, PosterOmni wins on extending (4.76 vs. 4.41), filling (4.69 vs. 4.44), and layout-driven (4.20 vs. 4.05) in English, but trails on identity-driven (3.98 vs. 4.53) and rescaling (3.97 vs. 4.00); overall scores are nearly identical (4.27 vs. 4.28 English; 4.37 vs. 4.34 Chinese). Seedream-3.0 is clearly outperformed by PosterOmni on all six tasks, confirming that the gap between open and closed systems has narrowed substantially for poster-specific creation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PosterOmni builds on Qwen-Image-Edit as its base editor and extends DiffusionNFT (a forward-process RL method for diffusion models) to the image-to-poster domain. Conceptually, it departs from two lines of work: general-purpose image editors (ICEdit, FLUX.1 Kontext, Step1X-Edit) that handle natural-image modifications but lack poster-specific layout, typography, and aesthetic reasoning, and text-to-poster generators (POSTA, PosterCraft, LayoutPrompter) that produce posters from scratch but cannot perform reference-preserving edits. The key methodological departure is the task-centric decomposition into local editing and global creation, followed by knowledge distillation between two specialized experts rather than the parameter-merging strategies (ZipLoRA, SVD fusion) commonly used to combine LoRA adapters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first open-source framework that unifies six distinct image-to-poster tasks (extending, filling, rescaling, identity-driven, layout-driven, style-driven) in a single model, filling a gap where only closed commercial systems (Seedream, GPT-Image, Gemini) previously handled such multi-task poster creation. The PosterOmni-Bench provides the first standardized evaluation protocol for this setting, which will be useful for the poster-generation community. Limitations include reliance on a single VLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro) for evaluation, a very short RL stage (500 steps, rank-32 LoRA) whose gains may not scale, and the two-regime decomposition (local vs. global) being somewhat ad hoc—whether this split is the right inductive bias for other design tasks remains open. The approach also inherits the base model's architectural constraints and has not been tested on non-poster design domains.
