## Problem

Existing query-driven 3D segmentation methods operate on static single-frame point clouds, making them unable to resolve ambiguous references in dynamic scenes where object motion and temporal context are essential for disambiguation. Prior temporal approaches rely on simple frame aggregation or recurrent fusion that fails to maintain voxel-level spatio-temporal coherence under occlusion or large displacements, and none jointly address cross-scene semantic recall with language-conditioned 4D segmentation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261556/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a natural-language text query, three consecutive LiDAR point cloud frames, and optional RGB images (projected via LiDAR-camera calibration). For static 3D tasks, three identical point clouds are fed simultaneously with three different queries to exploit the multi-frame architecture. Text is encoded by a frozen LLaMA2-7B backbone, and the output is a per-point 3D/4D segmentation mask with spatial and temporal consistency, evaluated via mIoU and Acc@50 (IoU > 0.5 threshold).

## Method

MORE-STEM integrates three modules over a dual-branch encoder (lightweight Point Transformer for per-point features and sparse 3D Transformer with window-shifting attention for voxel features). The Cross-Frame Text-Visual Alignment module performs bidirectional cross-attention between 3D and 2D features, then aligns the LLaMA2-encoded text with a 3-frame visual token sequence via temporal cross-modal attention and a cosine-similarity contrastive loss. The Spatio-Temporal Consistency Model propagates voxel-level hidden states across frames using a learnable state-space recurrence (h_t = A·h_{t-1} + B·F_t) and refines them spatially with a sparse intra-frame 3D Transformer, fusing both via normalization. The Long-Short Memory Recall module maintains a Long-Term Memory Bank of loss-weighted text-visual pairs with category-balanced bias weights for cross-scene retrieval, and a Short-Term Mask Memory Bank caching recent frame-level mask features for frame-to-frame continuity, with the LTM output serving as query for STM cross-attention before a lightweight classifier produces the final mask.

## Results

On the proposed InstructKITTI 3D instruction segmentation benchmark, MORE-STEM achieves 38.62 Acc and 37.95 mIoU, more than doubling the accuracy of the strongest baseline (Chat-Scene: 21.60 Acc). On the 4D variant, the method reaches 42.19 Acc@50 and 40.67 mIoU. On ScanRefer, MORE-STEM attains 52.7 mIoU and 54.8 Acc, surpassing the previous best (RefMask3D: 44.8 mIoU) by 7.9 points. On SemanticKITTI, the method achieves 74.6 mIoU, outperforming MR-COSMO (73.4) and FEAST-Mamba (73.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MORE-STEM outperforms all 24 compared baselines across every evaluated task. On InstructKITTI 3D, the gap over the strongest baseline Chat-Scene [11] is 17.02 Acc points (38.62 vs. 21.60) and 16.45 mIoU points, while also running faster (0.19 s vs. 0.35 s per query). On Instruct3D, MORE-STEM (35.9 mIoU) surpasses SegPoint [10] (31.6 mIoU) by 4.3 points, with a much larger margin over earlier methods like M3DRef [45] (18.1) and BUTD-DETR [14] (16.3). On ScanRefer, the method beats RefMask3D [9] (44.8 mIoU) by 7.9 points and ConcerteNet [33] (46.5 Acc) by 8.3 Acc points, establishing a clear new state of the art. On SemanticKITTI, MORE-STEM (74.6 mIoU) edges out MR-COSMO [19] (73.4) and FEAST-Mamba [17] (73.2) by 1.2 and 1.4 points respectively, with a larger margin over earlier methods like PTv3+PPT [39,40] (72.3) and OA-CNNs [27] (70.6). Notably, the method achieves these gains while maintaining competitive latency (0.19 s), though GPU memory usage (28.9 GB) is higher than most baselines due to the multimodal alignment and dual-memory modules.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads—static query-driven 3D segmentation (ScanRefer, SegPoint, 3D-LLaVA), spatio-temporal point cloud modeling (Mamba4D, TASeg, HiLoTs), and memory-augmented 3D perception (MAD, MemorySeg)—into a single framework for 4D instruction segmentation. It repurposes state-space models (Mamba) not for global sequence modeling as in Mamba4D, but for voxel-level temporal hidden-state updates, and pairs this with a sparse 3D Transformer for intra-frame spatial refinement. The hierarchical long-short memory design departs from prior memory-augmented approaches by explicitly separating cross-scene semantic recall (text-visual pairs) from frame-level mask caching, and by introducing bias-weighted category balancing to prevent over-representation of frequent classes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The InstructKITTI benchmark is the first outdoor 3D/4D instruction segmentation dataset with motion-centric, temporally aligned text annotations, filling a gap where prior benchmarks (ScanRefer, Instruct3D) are limited to static indoor scenes. The unified 3D/4D formulation means a single model handles semantic, referring, and instruction segmentation across both static and dynamic settings, which is practically relevant for autonomous driving and embodied perception. However, the 4D evaluation is reported only in supplementary material with limited baselines, the GPU memory cost increases substantially (28.9 GB vs. 13–22 GB for competitors), and it remains open whether the memory mechanism scales to very long sequences or diverse driving conditions beyond the SemanticKITTI sequences used.
