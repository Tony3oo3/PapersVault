## Problem

Existing agent-based image restoration systems rely on a single heavyweight MLLM for all user interactions, incurring unnecessary computational cost for simple, explicit prompts. More critically, they apply single-distortion removal tools sequentially to real-world hybrid degradations, causing error propagation due to distortion entanglement and distribution shifts between independently trained models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261533/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system accepts a degraded RGB image (training resolutions range from 224×224 to 784×784) together with a free-form user text prompt that may be either a direct instruction (e.g., 'remove the noise') or an ambiguous request. The output is a single restored RGB image. User prompts are generated by GPT-4 (20 direct + 20 ambiguous per distortion type, 220 total), and the system handles 10 degradation types including two less-explored compression codecs (HEVC and VVC).

## Method

HybridAgent routes user prompts through a lightweight Llama3.2-1B-Instruct FastAgent (in-context learning) that handles simple, explicit requests directly, while ambiguous prompts are escalated to a SlowAgent (fine-tuned Co-Instruct MLLM on a 70k instruction-tuning dataset) that identifies degradations and plans tool invocations with majority voting to reduce hallucination. A FeedbackAgent (also fine-tuned Co-Instruct) evaluates whether the restored image is clean, using historical tool-invocation context to decide termination. The restoration tools are built via a three-stage paradigm: (I) a multi-task prompt-learning base model (PromptIR architecture with RHAG blocks) learns shared knowledge across 10 distortions; (II) LoRA adapters plus reinitialized prompt parameters yield single-distortion tools; (III) a separate LoRA adapter initialized from Stage II prompts produces a mixed-distortion removal tool trained on synthesized multi-degradation data, which mitigates error propagation by handling entangled distortions in a single forward pass.

## Results

On mixed degradation tasks, HybridAgent achieves the best PSNR on 3 out of 4 combinations (Rainstreak+Noise: 26.49, Haze+Noise: 23.40, Motionblur+Noise: 23.77) and the best LPIPS on all four, outperforming the closest agentic baseline AgenticIR by up to 0.86 dB PSNR (Haze+Noise) and 0.028 LPIPS (Motionblur+Noise). Against all-in-one methods (Uformer, Restormer, AirNet, PromptIR), HybridAgent gains 3–14 dB PSNR on mixed degradations. Inference time is 11.66 s per image, roughly 4.2× faster than AgenticIR (49.34 s), while requiring 32.24 GB VRAM and 400.82 M parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the four all-in-one baselines (Uformer, Restormer, AirNet, PromptIR), HybridAgent dominates on every mixed-degradation combination, gaining 3.3–14.1 dB PSNR (e.g., 23.40 vs. 14.33 on Haze+Noise vs. AirNet) and reducing LPIPS by 0.1–0.6, confirming that single-model all-in-one approaches struggle with entangled distortions. InstructIR, the instruction-based all-in-one method, performs worst on Rainstreak+Noise (14.63 PSNR) and is competitive only on Haze+Noise (18.99 PSNR), still trailing HybridAgent by 4.4 dB. Against the closest agentic competitor AgenticIR, HybridAgent wins on PSNR for Rainstreak+Noise (+0.51), Haze+Noise (+0.85), and Motionblur+Noise (+0.08), and on LPIPS for all four combinations, but narrowly loses on PSNR (−0.15) and SSIM (−0.005) for Low light+Noise. The most striking advantage is efficiency: HybridAgent runs in 11.66 s versus AgenticIR's 49.34 s (4.2× speedup) by switching lightweight LoRA modules instead of making multiple GPT API calls. Overall, HybridAgent achieves the best or near-best quality across all metrics while being substantially faster than the only other agentic baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two recent lines of work: all-in-one image restoration via prompt learning (PromptIR, AirNet) and MLLM-driven agentic restoration (RestoreAgent, AgenticIR). It departs from prior agent-based IR systems in two key ways: (1) introducing a fast/slow/feedback agent hierarchy that routes simple prompts through a lightweight LLM (Llama3.2-1B) to avoid unnecessary MLLM inference, and (2) replacing the step-by-step single-distortion toolchain with a three-stage LoRA-based training paradigm that produces both single and mixed distortion removal tools from a shared foundation model. The SlowAgent and FeedbackAgent are fine-tuned from Co-Instruct, while the restoration tool architecture derives from an enhanced PromptIR variant with RHAG blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The mixed distortion removal tool addresses a practical failure mode (error propagation in step-by-step restoration) that prior agentic IR systems overlooked, with particularly large gains on unstable degradations like haze and low-light (e.g., PSNR 7.69→19.97 for Low light+Noise). The fast/slow routing provides a concrete efficiency recipe (~88% inference time reduction for direct prompts) that is broadly applicable to any MLLM-agent system. Limitations include the mixed tool's constrained representation capacity for highly complex or unseen degradation combinations, coverage of only 10 distortion types, and reliance on synthetic training data. Open questions include whether the three-stage LoRA paradigm scales to dozens of distortion types and whether the agent hierarchy generalizes to real-world degradations beyond the controlled synthetic pipeline.
