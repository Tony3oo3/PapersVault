## Problem

End-to-end multi-object tracking methods built on DETR achieve high detection accuracy (>80%) but suffer from low association accuracy (~60%) because the shared DETR architecture produces object embeddings with excessively high inter-object similarity (>80% of pairwise scores exceeding 0.9). This occurs because DETR is optimized for category-level discrimination within single frames, whereas tracking requires instance-level distinction across frames with spatial and temporal continuity. No prior end-to-end method explicitly optimizes object embeddings for the discriminativeness that association demands.

## Input / Output

The method consumes video frame sequences of T=30 frames at 1920×1080 resolution, processed independently by a shared Deformable DETR backbone (ResNet-50, COCO-pretrained). During training, offline pseudo depth labels from Video Depth Anything provide supervision for the depth branch, while Hungarian matching against ground-truth boxes assigns identity labels for contrastive learning. At inference, the pipeline outputs per-frame bounding boxes, object identity predictions, and continuous trajectories, with the three adapters adding only ~4.1% overhead (SA: 1.4%, TA: 2.7%) and IA incurring zero inference cost.

## Method

FDTA explicitly refines DETR object embeddings through three complementary adapters targeting spatial, temporal, and identity discriminativeness. The Spatial Adapter runs a parallel depth-extraction branch supervised by LID-discretized pseudo labels from Video Depth Anything, encodes predicted depth as learnable positional embeddings via linear interpolation, and injects depth features into the DETR decoder through an additional cross-attention layer. The Temporal Adapter applies a 6-layer transformer encoder over each object's T-frame trajectory history using a dual attention mask that enforces both causality and missing-object constraints to prevent information leakage from undetected frames. The Identity Adapter performs quality-aware contrastive learning (InfoNCE) on object embeddings, filtering samples by IoU ≥ 0.5, weighting positive pairs by harmonic mean IoU, and projecting through a 3-layer MLP to extract identity-consistent features invariant to frame-variant cues like pose and motion. All three adapters are trained jointly with the standard DETR detection loss, ID classification loss, depth focal loss, and contrastive loss in a single end-to-end objective.

## Results

FDTA achieves state-of-the-art performance across all three benchmarks. On DanceTrack, it reaches 71.7% HOTA, 77.2% IDF1, and 63.5% AssA, outperforming the previous best end-to-end method MOTRv2 by +1.8% HOTA and +5.5% IDF1. On SportsMOT, FDTA attains 74.2% HOTA and 78.5% IDF1, surpassing MOTIP by +2.3% HOTA. On BFT (bird flock tracking), it achieves 72.2% HOTA and 84.2% IDF1, leading all prior methods. The method maintains 13.4 FPS inference speed with only 4.1% additional overhead from the proposed adapters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FDTA outperforms all 25 compared baselines across all three benchmarks on the primary tracking metrics (HOTA, IDF1, AssA). On DanceTrack, it surpasses the strongest end-to-end competitor MOTRv2 [56] by +1.8% HOTA and +5.5% IDF1, and beats the strongest tracking-by-detection method TrackTrack [35] by +5.2% HOTA. On SportsMOT, FDTA leads MOTIP [16] by +2.3% HOTA and +3.5% IDF1, and exceeds the best tracking-by-detection method DiffMOT [30] by +2.1% HOTA. On BFT, it outperforms MOTIP [16] by +1.7% HOTA and +2.1% IDF1, and SambaMOTR [34] by +2.6% HOTA. Notably, FDTA's gains are most pronounced on association-specific metrics (IDF1, AssA) rather than detection (DetA), confirming that the discriminative embedding refinement primarily improves tracking identity consistency rather than detection recall. Even with extra training data, FDTA* maintains its lead over MOTRv2* and MOTIP* on DanceTrack (74.4 vs. 73.4 HOTA; 80.0 vs. 76.3 IDF1).

## Relation to Prior Work

FDTA builds directly on the end-to-end MOT paradigm established by MOTR and its successors (MOTRv2, MOTIP, SambaMOTR), which use a shared DETR backbone to jointly perform detection and ID prediction. The key departure is diagnostic: the paper shows that these methods' object embeddings inherit DETR's category-level discrimination bias, yielding inter-object similarity scores above 0.9 for over 80% of pairs, which is fundamentally misaligned with the instance-level separation that association requires. Rather than modifying the DETR architecture itself, FDTA inserts three lightweight adapters (spatial, temporal, identity) that explicitly refine embeddings post-detection, drawing on depth distillation from Video Depth Anything, trajectory-level transformer modeling with a custom dual attention mask, and IoU-weighted InfoNCE contrastive learning—each targeting a specific gap between detection and association objectives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central diagnostic—that end-to-end MOT methods are bottlenecked not by detection quality but by insufficiently discriminative embeddings—reframes where future improvements should focus, shifting attention from detection accuracy to embedding separability. The modular three-adapter design is practical (only ~4% additional inference cost, with the identity adapter incurring zero inference overhead) and the consistent SOTA gains across three diverse benchmarks (dancers, athletes, bird flocks) suggest broad applicability. Open questions include whether the depth distillation component generalizes to scenarios without meaningful depth structure, whether the approach scales to very long sequences beyond the 30-frame training window, and whether the contrastive objective can be further strengthened with larger sample pools or multi-granularity identity signals.
