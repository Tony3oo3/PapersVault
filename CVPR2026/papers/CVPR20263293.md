## Problem

Existing video deblurring methods perform poorly on real-world handheld video because the blur distribution (shaped by camera shake plus OIS correction) differs significantly from training datasets like GoPro or BSD. Collecting paired handheld deblurring data is expensive, and prior self-supervised approaches that synthesize blur from sharp frames still produce artifacts that diverge from real handheld blur, limiting their generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263293/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a blurry handheld video with no ground truth as input and produces deblurred frames as output. During training, frames are randomly cropped into 256×256 patches with random flipping and rotation; during testing, full-resolution frames are processed. The approach is plug-and-play across multiple backbone architectures (IFIRNN, ESTRNN, BasicVSR++, RVRT), and the only external dependency is a pre-trained SEA-RAFT optical flow model for alignment.

## Method

SelfHVD exploits the natural coexistence of sharp and blurry frames in handheld video (OIS works well during simple, slow motion) to enable self-supervised training without paired data. Sharp frames are selected per 20-frame segment using Laplacian variance with Otsu's thresholding, then aligned to neighboring blurry frames via optical flow with uncertainty and occlusion masks. The Self-Enhanced Video Deblurring (SEVD) module randomly removes sharp clues from the input (RSCR) and uses a Supervision Information Selection (SIS) strategy to pick the better target between the warped sharp frame and the model's own deblurring of the original video, enabling the model to surpass the sharpest available frame. The Self-Constrained Spatial Consistency Maintenance (SCSCM) module constrains current outputs to remain spatially consistent with outputs from an earlier checkpoint, preventing the position shifts that emerge in later training stages. Training proceeds in two stages: first with reconstruction and VGG perceptual loss against aligned sharp frames, then with SEVD and SCSCM losses added once the model has baseline deblurring ability.

## Results

On the synthetic GoProShake dataset, SelfHVD_BasicVSR++ achieves 37.44 PSNR / 0.9359 SSIM, approaching the fully-supervised BasicVSR++ (37.99 / 0.9683) while substantially outperforming the best prior self-supervised method DaDeblur (29.54 / 0.8772) by ~7.9 dB. On the real-world HVD dataset (no ground truth), SelfHVD_BasicVSR++ attains the highest no-reference scores (e.g., 28.00 MUSIQ / 0.2175 MANIQA on HVD-Huawei), surpassing DaDeblur (26.84 / 0.2025) and Ren et al. (22.54 / 0.1771). In test-time fine-tuning on BSD, RealBlur, and RBVD, SelfHVD consistently leads all baselines, e.g., 31.01 PSNR on BSD-1ms/8ms vs. 29.44 for DaDeblur and 25.57 for the GoPro-trained ESTRNN baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SelfHVD decisively outperforms both prior self-supervised methods: on GoProShake, SelfHVD_BasicVSR++ (37.44 PSNR) exceeds DaDeblur [11] (29.54) by 7.9 dB and Ren et al. [52] (25.05) by 12.4 dB, and it also leads on all three HVD phone subsets in MUSIQ and MANIQA. Against fully-supervised baselines, SelfHVD_BasicVSR++ (37.44) nearly matches supervised BasicVSR++ (37.99) on GoProShake while requiring no paired ground truth. In the test-time fine-tuning setting (Table 2), SelfHVD surpasses the GoPro-trained ESTRNN [61] baseline by 3.5–5.5 dB PSNR across BSD exposure levels, outperforms DaDeblur [11] by 1.1–1.6 dB, and also beats Blur2Blur [38] on RealBlur (28.76 vs. 26.16 PSNR) and RBVD (27.69 vs. 24.45 PSNR). The only comparison where SelfHVD trails is against the fully-supervised BasicVSR++ on GoProShake SSIM (0.9359 vs. 0.9683), a gap attributable to the absence of true ground-truth supervision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the self-supervised video deblurring line (Ren et al. 2020, DaDeblur 2024) but departs fundamentally from their strategy of synthesizing blurry inputs by artificially blurring sharp frames. Instead, it exploits a domain-specific property of handheld video—namely that OIS produces coexisting sharp and blurry frames—to use actual in-video sharp frames as misalignment supervision, eliminating the synthetic-blur domain gap. The method also repurposes existing supervised backbones (IFIRNN, ESTRNN, BasicVSR++, RVRT) without architectural modification, and introduces two training-stage mechanisms (SEVD and SCSCM) that have no direct counterpart in prior self-supervised deblurring work. The new GoProShake and HVD datasets extend the evaluation landscape beyond the GoPro/BSD blur distributions that dominate prior benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that handheld video deblurring is a high-volume consumer use case where paired ground-truth data is prohibitively expensive to collect, and the OIS-induced blur distribution is genuinely different from laboratory-synthesized motion blur. The method's ability to generalize to non-handheld real-world datasets (BSD, RealBlur, RBVD) under test-time fine-tuning suggests the self-supervised paradigm is not limited to the handheld domain. Open questions include: performance when OIS is so effective that very few blurry frames exist (reducing supervision signal), or so ineffective that sharp frames are rare (reducing supervision quality); whether the 91–97% sharp-frame selection accuracy is sufficient for all scene types; and whether the two-stage training protocol (vanilla self-supervision followed by SEVD+SCSCM) can be simplified or made more robust to hyperparameter choices like the distortion threshold τ.
