## Problem

Monocular relative depth models generalize well across domains but produce scale-ambiguous outputs, while metric depth models achieve correct scale only within a single domain. Previous language-assisted approaches (e.g., RSA) estimate a single global scale and shift factor, which cannot correct local errors in the relative depth map and fails when similar text descriptions correspond to different depth distributions. This paper addresses both limitations by predicting pixel-wise rescale maps guided by both image and text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260429/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image, a natural-language scene description (generated by LLaVA v1.6), and a relative depth map produced by a frozen Depth Anything-Small model. It outputs two H×W maps—a scale map A and a shift map B—which are combined with the relative depth via D_m = 1/(A⊙D_r + B) to yield the final metric depth. Both the image encoder (DINOv2 ViT-L) and text encoder (CLIP ViT-L/14) are frozen; only the cross-modality attention module and two DPT decoder heads (19M parameters total) are trained.

## Method

A cross-modality attention module uses image features as queries and both image and text features as keys/values, preserving pixel-level resolution while injecting global scene-scale cues from the text. Two lightweight DPT decoder heads then predict the pixel-wise scale and shift maps. Training combines a scale-invariant log loss on ground-truth metric depth with a thresholded pseudo-metric-depth loss (relative depth aligned to GT via closed-form least-squares, accepted only when δ₁ exceeds a preset threshold) to supervise pixels lacking GT. A Dual-Level Scale-Oriented Contrast loss enforces feature consistency: at the image level, embeddings are pulled together for similar scale factors and pushed apart for dissimilar ones; at the pixel level, depth values are binned into distribution classes and same-class pixel features are contrasted as positives against different-class negatives. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end on 102K images spanning four domains (NYUv2, KITTI, VOID, C3VD) with only 19M learnable parameters.

## Results

On NYUv2, TR2M achieves δ1=0.954, AbsRel=0.082, and RMSE=0.293 with only 19M trainable parameters, outperforming all language-based and transfer-based baselines (e.g., RSA: δ1=0.752, AbsRel=0.156) and approaching the performance of DA with ground-truth linear fit (δ1=0.969, AbsRel=0.055). On KITTI, TR2M obtains δ1=0.965 and AbsRel=0.066, securing two best and three second-best metrics among all methods. In zero-shot evaluation across five unseen datasets (SUN RGB-D, iBims-1, HyperSim, DIODE Outdoor, SimCol), TR2M achieves the best average rank of 2.40, surpassing DA Single (2.80), UniK3D-V-L (3.75), UniDepth (4.00), and RSA (5.80), while using a ViT-Small backbone (~25M params) and only 102K training images.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TR2M decisively outperforms RSA [68], the closest prior work in the relative-to-metric transfer paradigm, by a large margin: on NYUv2, TR2M achieves δ1=0.954 vs. RSA's 0.752 and AbsRel=0.082 vs. 0.156, demonstrating that pixel-wise rescale maps are far superior to RSA's single global scale/shift factors. Among language-based methods, TR2M surpasses DepthLM (δ1=0.824, AbsRel=0.134), ScaleDepth (δ1=0.913, AbsRel=0.099), WorDepth (δ1=0.926, AbsRel=0.090), and DepthCLIP (δ1=0.394, AbsRel=0.388) on all metrics. Against large direct metric depth models, TR2M (19M params) trails UniDepth (347M, δ1=0.981, RMSE=0.229) and Metric3Dv2 (1011M, δ1=0.980, AbsRel=0.067) on NYUv2, but narrows the gap substantially on KITTI (TR2M δ1=0.965 vs. UniDepth 0.985) and achieves the best zero-shot average rank (2.40) across five unseen datasets, beating UniK3D-V-L (3.75), UniDepth (4.00), and DA Single (2.80). Notably, TR2M is trained on only 102K images across four domains, whereas UniK3D and UniDepth require 8M and 3M images respectively, and domain-adapted methods like DA V2 and ZoeDepth cannot generalize beyond their training domain.

## Relation to Prior Work

TR2M extends the relative-to-metric depth transfer line initiated by RSA, which estimated a single global scale and shift factor from text descriptions, by predicting pixel-wise rescale maps instead. It builds on Depth Anything as a frozen relative depth backbone and adopts DPT-style decoders for map generation, while introducing a cross-modality attention module to fuse CLIP text features with DINOv2 image features. Unlike prior language-based depth methods (DepthCLIP, DepthLM, ScaleDepth, WorDepth) that establish indirect text-to-depth correlations, TR2M uses text as a scale cue complementing image layout information, and adds a novel dual-level scale-oriented contrastive loss that has no direct precedent in the depth estimation literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving competitive metric depth with only 19M trainable parameters and 102K training images, compared to 345M+ parameters and millions of images for direct metric depth models, while maintaining zero-shot generalization across indoor, outdoor, and surgical domains. This suggests that the scale ambiguity in relative depth can be resolved with lightweight, interpretable pixel-wise transformations rather than large end-to-end metric models. Open questions include the sensitivity to text description quality (generated by LLaVA), whether the pseudo-depth threshold strategy scales to domains with sparse or noisy ground truth, and whether the approach extends to video or multi-view settings where temporal consistency of scale matters.
