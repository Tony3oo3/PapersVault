## Problem

Diffusion models applied to hyperspectral image super-resolution suffer from three key limitations: low-efficiency sampling requiring many iterative steps, detail-limited generation because isotropic Gaussian noise fails to prioritize high-frequency edge structures, and insufficient denoising due to feature interference and blind upsampling in conventional UNet architectures. These issues collectively constrain both the speed and quality of HSI reconstruction from fused LR-HSI and HR-MSI inputs.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution HSI (downsampled 8× with a 3×3 Gaussian blur, σ=2) and a high-resolution multispectral image (derived from the HR-HSI via a Nikon D700 spectral response function) as joint input, and produces a full high-resolution HSI tensor. During processing, the LR-HSI is bicubically upsampled and channel-concatenated with the HR-MSI to form the observation endpoint, while the HR-HSI is concatenated with a pseudo-MSI (its first 3 bands) to form the diffusion starting point, so the network operates on a combined spectral-spatial tensor rather than a single modality.

## Method

EMR-Diff replaces the standard diffusion schedule with a multimodal residual transfer: the forward process gradually injects the difference between the HR-HSI+Pseudo-MSI endpoint and the upsampled LR-HSI+HR-MSI endpoint into the Markov chain, reducing required sampling steps to approximately 5. An edge-aware noise strategy modulates Gaussian noise using Sobel-derived edge magnitudes from the HR-MSI, concentrating perturbation energy on high-frequency regions so the denoiser prioritizes structural detail recovery. The denoising backbone, BAF-UNet, uses a bilateral dual-path design (a denoising path for the noisy input and a guiding path for the observed LR-HSI/HR-MSI) that fuses features only in deep layers to avoid early-modality interference. Each path employs Multi-scale Group Attention Blocks combining 1-group and S-group convolutions, spatial-channel attention, and softmax-weighted adaptive fusion. A multi-scale supervision mechanism injects progressively upsampled LR-HSI and downsampled HR-MSI at each upsampling stage and computes L1 loss against correspondingly downsampled HR-HSI targets, enforcing a progressive low-to-high-resolution reconstruction that eliminates blind upsampling.

## Results

EMR-Diff achieves the best performance on all four metrics (PSNR, SSIM, SAM, ERGAS) across all three benchmark datasets. On ICVL, it reaches 55.40 dB PSNR and 0.9997 SSIM, surpassing the strongest supervised baseline DSPNet (55.19 dB) by 0.21 dB. On Harvard and Chikusei, it achieves 49.28 dB and 47.55 dB PSNR respectively, outperforming DSPNet by 0.60 dB and 0.58 dB. The method also requires only 5 diffusion steps at inference, reducing sampling cost by tens of times compared to standard DDPM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EMR-Diff outperforms all 11 baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the strongest supervised competitor DSPNet, it gains +0.21 dB PSNR on ICVL, +0.60 dB on Harvard, and +0.58 dB on Chikusei, while also achieving lower SAM and ERGAS. The gap widens substantially against other supervised methods: LAGConv is surpassed by +1.87 dB (ICVL) and +3.59 dB (Chikusei), and LRTN by +3.05 dB (ICVL) and +3.78 dB (Chikusei). Unsupervised methods (PLR, SDP, ARGS) trail by 9–10 dB in PSNR on ICVL, reflecting their inability to recover fine spatial details without paired supervision. Traditional methods (CNMF, Hysure) are far behind, with PSNR gaps exceeding 17 dB on ICVL, confirming the limitations of hand-crafted priors for complex scenes.

## Relation to Prior Work

EMR-Diff extends the ResShift paradigm of residual shifting (originally for unimodal natural image super-resolution) to the multimodal HSI-MSI fusion setting, replacing the standard DDPM forward process of adding pure Gaussian noise with a structured transfer of the multimodal residual between the HR-HSI/Pseudo-MSI concatenation and the LR-HSI/HR-MSI concatenation. The edge-aware noise strategy departs from uniform Gaussian perturbation by modulating noise intensity with Sobel-derived edge maps from HR-MSI, and the BAF-UNet architecture introduces a dual-path (denoising + guiding) design with MSGAB blocks and multi-scale progressive supervision that addresses information interference and blind upsampling problems in prior diffusion-based HSI fusion methods (e.g., HSR-Diff, S2CycleDiff).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction of diffusion steps from hundreds to approximately 5 while matching or exceeding the quality of standard diffusion-based HSI fusion methods is a practically significant efficiency gain that makes diffusion-model-based HSI super-resolution more deployable in real-time or resource-constrained settings. The multimodal residual transfer concept is a generalizable principle that could extend to other multi-modal fusion tasks beyond HSI. However, the method is validated only on three benchmark datasets with a fixed 8× downsampling factor and a specific camera spectral response, and the authors themselves note that cross-sensor and cross-scene generalization remains an open challenge. The modest quality margins over the strongest supervised baselines (e.g., ~0.2–0.6 dB PSNR over DSPNet) suggest the efficiency gain, rather than a quality breakthrough, is the primary practical contribution.
