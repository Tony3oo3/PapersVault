## Problem

Existing instruction-based image editing methods struggle with fine-grained instructions specifying precise attributes (exact colors, positions, quantities) and reasoning-intensive tasks, largely because supervised training on paired data does not explicitly optimize for fine-grained text-visual alignment. Additionally, recent GRPO-based reinforcement learning approaches optimize each denoising step independently, providing only sparse feedback that prevents gradient flow across the sampling trajectory and limits trajectory-level control over the editing process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261458/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a source RGB image and a natural-language editing instruction as input. The instruction is first decomposed by a multi-modal LLM into actionable editing directives, which are then encoded as text tokens processed through the diffusion model's cross-attention layers. The output is a single edited RGB image generated via a flow-matching denoising trajectory (with stochastic SDE sampling injected for RL diversity). Training is performed with LoRA (rank 4) on the Qwen-Image-Edit base model, using a batch of 4 editing instances per step over 500 training steps.

## Method

CogniEdit combines three synergistic components: (1) MLLM-based instruction decomposition for semantic correctness, (2) Dynamic Token Focus Relocation, where a lightweight per-layer predictor identifies which instruction tokens to emphasize and injects learnable soft tokens at those positions, creating hierarchical attention that shifts from high-level semantics in early layers to fine-grained attributes in deeper layers, and (3) Dense GRPO-based optimization, which extends standard GRPO by performing k=5 consecutive denoising steps with stop-gradient operations so that gradients propagate backward through the entire sampling trajectory rather than being computed at a single step. The GRPO objective is adapted for editing by converting the deterministic ODE to a stochastic SDE (Euler-Maruyama) to enable diverse sampling, and by computing advantages at the batch level across B editing instances rather than per-instance to reduce gradient variance. This trajectory-level dense supervision, combined with the dynamic attention mechanism, enables the model to learn fine-grained instruction-image alignment while maintaining source consistency and visual quality.

## Results

On KRIS-Bench, CogniEdit achieves the highest Visual Quality (VQ) scores across all three cognitive domains: 92.40 (Social Science), 90.47 (Natural Science), and 91.72 (Knowledge Reasoning). It also attains the best Instruction Following (IF) scores in Natural Science (57.59) and Knowledge Reasoning (40.14), and the best Knowledge Preservation (KP) in both Natural Science (50.92) and Knowledge Reasoning (33.33). In Social Science, CogniEdit (Avg 77.64) is within 0.35 of Qwen-Image-Edit-r1 (Avg 77.99) while leading in VQ (92.40 vs. 89.14) and Visual Consistency (80.60 vs. 79.20). Overall, CogniEdit outperforms all six baselines in the majority of metrics across all three domains.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CogniEdit substantially outperforms all training-based baselines (InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, AnyEdit, Step1X-Edit) across every metric and domain, with the largest gaps in Instruction Following (e.g., 40.14 vs. 20.00 on Knowledge Reasoning IF) and Knowledge Preservation. Against OmniGen2, CogniEdit leads in IF and KP across all domains while matching or exceeding VQ, though OmniGen2's VQ in Social Science (87.60) is competitive. Compared to the base Qwen-Image-Edit, CogniEdit improves IF by 14.31 (SS), 13.51 (NS), and 10.48 (KR), and KP by 15.92, 17.20, and 10.92 respectively, confirming that the dense GRPO optimization and dynamic token focus relocation directly address the fine-grained alignment gap. The closest competitor is Qwen-Image-Edit-r1 (Qwen-Image-Edit augmented with VLM reasoning): CogniEdit wins on VQ in all three domains (92.40/90.47/91.72 vs. 89.14/87.60/88.37), IF in Natural Science (57.59 vs. 52.88) and Knowledge Reasoning (40.14 vs. 35.03), and KP in both NS and KR, while Qwen-Image-Edit-r1 retains a narrow edge in Social Science IF (73.17 vs. 70.25) and overall SS average (77.99 vs. 77.64). This pattern indicates that dense trajectory-level optimization provides the decisive advantage for fine-grained attribute control, while VLM-based reasoning alone (r1) helps most with high-level semantic parsing in social-science contexts.

## Relation to Prior Work

CogniEdit extends the GRPO reinforcement learning framework (originally from DeepSeekMath for LLMs and adapted to flow-matching models by Flow-GRPO) to the image editing domain, introducing two key adaptations: stochastic SDE sampling to enable group diversity in deterministic editing pipelines, and batch-level advantage normalization for stability. Its central methodological departure is the 'Dense' GRPO objective, which propagates gradients across k consecutive denoising steps via stop-gradient operations, contrasting with prior GRPO applications that treat each timestep as an independent decision point. The Dynamic Token Focus Relocation mechanism is a novel attention-guidance component with no direct prior counterpart, while the MLLM-based instruction decomposition follows the lineage of Step1X-Edit, BrushEdit, and GenArtist.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and persistent weakness in instruction-based editing—failure to follow fine-grained specifications like exact colors, positions, and quantities—by providing trajectory-level dense supervision rather than sparse endpoint rewards. However, the practical gains over the strong Qwen-Image-Edit-r1 baseline are modest and inconsistent across domains (e.g., slightly worse average in Social Science), and the training setup is lightweight (500 steps, LoRA rank 4, ~4k pairs), raising questions about whether the dense GRPO advantage persists at scale or with larger training budgets. Open questions include whether the k-step gradient propagation generalizes to longer trajectories or to pure generation tasks, and whether the Dynamic Token Focus Relocation mechanism transfers to architectures without explicit layer-wise token attention.
