## Problem

Existing dataset distillation methods rely on heuristic process surrogates—per-step gradient matching or trajectory alignment—that mimic intermediate training behavior without guaranteeing the synthetic data produces the same final model as the full dataset. This creates an optimization gap where synthetic data can excel at the proxy objective yet underperform on downstream accuracy. The paper asks whether one can directly distill data by matching its outcome-level influence on the converged model parameters, and whether such an estimator can be computed efficiently without convexity assumptions or inverse-Hessian products.

## Input / Output

The method requires a real dataset D (images with class labels) and a pre-recorded SGD training trajectory consisting of parameter checkpoints θ_D^t and learning rates η_t for t = 1, …, T, obtained by first training a base model on D. The output is a compact synthetic set S with a fixed number of images per class (IPC ∈ {1, 10, 50}), where both the pixel values and the soft labels (produced by the final model θ_D^T) are jointly optimized as learnable variables. During training, only random mini-batches from D and S are used to estimate gradients and Hessian-gradient products, keeping memory and compute linear in the number of parameters.

## Method

Inf-Match formulates dataset distillation as minimizing the L2 norm of the combined removal influence of the real dataset D and the addition influence of the synthetic set S (Eq. 6), so that the net parameter shift is zero—i.e., training on S yields the same converged parameters as training on D. The key technical contribution is a fully differentiable influence estimator (Theorem 1) that unrolls the SGD optimization dynamics and applies a first-order Taylor approximation, expressing the influence as a weighted sum of Hessian-gradient products along the recorded trajectory. These Hessian-gradient products are computed via a finite-difference trick (Pearlmutter's method) in O(p) time, eliminating the need for explicit inverse-Hessian computation or convexity assumptions. A provable error bound (Theorem 2) shows the approximation error scales polynomially (T³) with training steps rather than exponentially as in prior estimators. In practice, the loss is evaluated on randomly sampled mini-batches and a progressive subset of trajectory checkpoints (early steps for basic patterns, later steps for fine-grained structure), and both synthetic images and soft labels are updated by gradient descent.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, Inf-Match achieves 72.5% top-1 accuracy at IPC=10 and 78.1% at IPC=50, surpassing the previous best (NCFM) by ~0.7%. On CIFAR-100, it reaches 49.3% at IPC=10 and 57.4% at IPC=50, a 2.7% margin over NCFM. On Tiny-ImageNet, the gains are most pronounced: 31.5% at IPC=10 and 33.8% at IPC=50, outpacing NCFM by 4.7% and 4.2% respectively. On the vision-language Flickr30K benchmark, Inf-Match achieves 7.4% on image-to-text retrieval with 200 synthetic pairs (vs. 1.3% for DATM) and 16.4% on text-to-image with 1000 pairs, exceeding NCFM's average by 2.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Inf-Match consistently outperforms all 11 baselines across every IPC setting on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny-ImageNet. The closest competitor is NCFM [54], which Inf-Match surpasses by 0.7% on CIFAR-10 (IPC=50: 78.1% vs. ~77.4%), 2.7% on CIFAR-100 (IPC=50: 57.4% vs. 54.7%), and a substantial 4.7% on Tiny-ImageNet (IPC=10: 31.5% vs. ~26.8%). Among process-matching baselines, DATM [24] (55.0% on CIFAR-100 IPC=50) and MTT [7] (47.7%) are clearly behind, confirming that outcome-level influence matching closes the optimization gap that heuristic trajectory/gradient alignment leaves open. On the vision-language Flickr30K task, Inf-Match dominates at small sample budgets: with 200 pairs it scores 7.4% on image-to-text retrieval versus 1.3% for DATM, a 6.1-point gap. At larger budgets (500–1000 pairs), the margin narrows but Inf-Match still leads (14.6% vs. 14.1% T2I at 500 pairs; 16.4% T2I at 1000 pairs), and its average across settings exceeds NCFM by 2.5%. Selection-based methods (Random, Herding) are uniformly outperformed by all distillation approaches, reinforcing that learned synthesis is necessary rather than mere subset selection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: dataset distillation (where prior work has converged on process-matching proxies like gradient matching [68] and trajectory matching [7]) and influence function estimation (where prior work [29, 4] provides principled but computationally heavy tools requiring convexity assumptions and inverse-Hessian products). The key conceptual departure is repurposing influence functions as the *direct* optimization objective for dataset distillation rather than as a post-hoc diagnostic, while replacing the intractable inverse-Hessian estimator with a linear-time, fully differentiable surrogate derived from unrolled SGD dynamics and a first-order Taylor approximation. This shifts the distillation paradigm from mimicking intermediate training signals to matching the final parameter-space outcome, closing the optimization gap that process-matching methods leave open.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By providing a provably bounded, linear-time influence estimator that avoids convexity assumptions, the paper makes outcome-aligned distillation practically feasible for the first time, offering a principled alternative to the heuristic surrogates that have dominated the field. The consistent gains across CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet, and Flickr30K (up to +4.7% over NCFM) validate the conceptual shift, and the polynomial (vs. exponential) error bound is a meaningful theoretical improvement over prior estimators. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale distillation, how the Taylor approximation degrades for very deep or transformer-based networks, and whether the requirement to record a full training trajectory on the real dataset limits applicability in privacy-sensitive or streaming settings.
