## Problem

Unified multimodal models (UMMs) that support both image-to-text understanding and text-to-image generation typically train these two modules independently, because jointly optimizing diffusion-based generation objectives degrades understanding representations and vice versa. This decoupling misses the intrinsic mutual enhancement between the two tasks: a better encoder should produce richer semantics that improve generation, and a better decoder should provide feedback that sharpens fine-grained perception. The paper asks whether a principled, shared objective can link the two directions so they co-evolve rather than being trained in isolation.

## Input / Output

The system takes a single RGB image as input. The encoder (an autoregressive LLM) produces a free-form text caption describing the image, which then serves as the sole conditioning signal for the decoder (either a frozen MM-DiT diffusion transformer in UMM-1 or the same AR model in UMM-2) to synthesize a reconstructed image. The reconstruction quality is measured by CLIP cosine similarity between the original and generated images, which doubles as the scalar reward for RL optimization. The method is applied as a post-training stage on top of pre-trained UMMs (UniWorld, Janus-Pro) without modifying their architecture.

## Method

Unified-GRPO is a reconstructive reinforcement learning post-training method that jointly optimizes the encoder and decoder through a closed-loop reconstruction objective. For each input image, the LLM samples a group of G caption sequences; each caption's last hidden state is projected into a diffusion condition to synthesize a reconstructed image, and the CLIP cosine similarity between the original and reconstructed images serves as the trajectory reward. The LLM is then updated via the GRPO objective, encouraging it to emit hidden representations that maximize the downstream reconstruction fidelity. This creates a self-reinforcing cycle: the encoder learns to extract more comprehensive and fine-grained semantics (improving small-object detection, grounding, and person re-identification), while the decoder learns to faithfully recover all semantics from the richer textual priors (improving complex instruction-following generation). The paper also introduces Unified-Bench, a reconstruction-based benchmark that evaluates whether the semantics extracted during understanding are sufficient for faithful regeneration, using four vision backbones (CLIP, LongCLIP, DINO-v2, DINO-v3) and LLM-judged caption quality comparisons.

## Results

On GenEval, UAE achieves the best overall score of 0.86 among unified models without LLM rewriting (and 0.89 with rewriting), leading on Counting (0.84) and Color attribution (0.79). On the harder GenEval++ benchmark, UAE achieves 0.475 overall, substantially outperforming the next-best Bagel (0.371) and OmniGen2 (0.325). On the proposed Unified-Bench, UAE attains the highest overall reconstruction score of 86.09, surpassing GPT-4o-Image (85.95). On MMT-Bench fine-grained perception, UAE improves the overall score from 32.5 to 56.9 (+24.4%), with dramatic gains in Small Object Detection (+40) and Person Re-ID (+60).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, UAE (0.86) outperforms Janus-Pro (0.80), UniWorld-V1 (0.80), OmniGen2 (0.80), and BAGEL (0.82) without prompt rewriting, and with LLM rewriting UAE† (0.89) edges out BAGEL† (0.88). On GenEval++, UAE achieves 0.475 overall, a clear margin over Bagel (0.371), OmniGen2 (0.325), T2I-R1 (0.311), and Janus-Pro (0.246), particularly on multi-attribute tasks like Color/Count (0.550 vs. 0.250 for Bagel). On the proposed Unified-Bench, UAE (86.09) narrowly surpasses GPT-4o-Image (85.95) and clearly leads over BAGEL (83.48), OmniGen2 (83.31), and Janus-Pro (82.77). On MMT-Bench fine-grained perception, UAE (56.9) substantially improves over its Qwen-2.5-VL-3B baseline (32.5) but remains below larger models like InternVL-Chat-V1.2-34B (63.4) and GPT-4V (62.0), indicating the gains are most pronounced at the 3B scale. On caption quality (Protocol-2), UAE's understanding model wins 76.3% pairwise against Qwen-2.5-VL-3B and 71.5% against Qwen-2.5-VL-7B, while remaining competitive with GPT-4o (47.2% win rate).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RL-for-image-generation line (T2I-R1, AR-GRPO) and the two dominant UMM architectures (UniWorld's LLM+MM-DiT and Janus-Pro's single AR backbone), but departs from them by introducing a reconstructive auto-encoder framing where text is the shared latent code between encoding (I2T) and decoding (T2I). Prior UMMs either decouple understanding and generation to avoid objective conflict or jointly train with competing losses; here, a single GRPO-based reconstructive reward simultaneously optimizes both modules, creating a cross-modal feedback loop rather than a zero-sum trade-off. The auto-encoder perspective is the conceptual departure: reconstruction fidelity becomes the joint objective that aligns encoder richness with decoder faithfulness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that generation-augmented RL can substantially improve fine-grained visual perception (small-object detection +40%, Person ReID +60%) while preserving overall understanding challenges the prevailing assumption that joint training necessarily degrades comprehension. However, the gains come with clear trade-offs in OCR-heavy and document-understanding subtasks (−6 to −12 points), and the primary evidence rests on a single 3B backbone (Qwen-2.5-VL-3B) with UniWorld as the main experimental platform. Open questions include whether the synergy scales to larger models, whether the OCR degradation is fundamental to the reconstructive objective or an artifact of the CLIP-based reward, and whether the auto-encoder framing generalizes beyond the two architectural families tested.
