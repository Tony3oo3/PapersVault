## Problem

Multimodal LLMs face compositional safety risks where individually benign images and texts jointly produce unsafe semantics, yet current safety alignment methods either rely on large-scale supervised/preference data or grant models unrestricted reasoning that can itself generate unsafe rationales. GRPO enables self-rewarded refinement without human labels, but safety and ethical compliance lack the verifiable correctness signals (unlike math or code) that make rule-based rewards tractable, leaving a gap in how to apply RLVR-style optimization to open-ended multimodal safety.

## Input / Output

The method consumes image-instruction pairs (RGB images paired with text prompts) drawn from SafeTag-VL-3K, which aggregates VLGuard, SPA-VL, and 300 BeaverTails samples converted into typo-style images. The model must produce a strictly ordered sequence: three modality-level safety tags (<visual_safe>, <text_safe>, <combined_safe>) in a fixed syntactic format, followed by a final response that is either an explicit refusal or a helpful answer. A format gate (parsability check) zeros out all downstream rewards if the tag sequence is structurally invalid, making the output format a hard constraint rather than a soft preference.

## Method

SafeGRPO extends GRPO with a rule-governed reward R_safety = I_format × (0.5·R_tag + 0.5·R_behavior), where the format indicator gates all subsequent scoring. The tag reward is hierarchical: the combined safety tag must be correct to earn any credit, after which partial credit (0.25 each) is awarded for correct visual and textual tags. The behavior reward is binary, requiring both correct combined-tag prediction and matching behavioral action (refusal for unsafe, helpful response for safe), with the action detected via keyword matching on refusal indicators. A step-guided safety thinking prompt enforces a reason-before-answering structure that decomposes assessment into per-modality evaluations, providing verifiable intermediate states. Training runs on Qwen3-VL-4B/8B-Thinking via the verl framework with 8 rollouts per prompt, 256 global batch size, and KL regularization against a reference policy, requiring no external reward model or human preference pairs.

## Results

On Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking, SafeGRPO achieves an average Jailbreak Defense Safety Score of 99.02 (vs. 89.28 base), a Safety Awareness score of 94.31 (vs. 86.52 base), and a Refusal Rate of 20.00% (vs. 21.00% base) on MOSSBench. On Qwen3-VL-4B-Thinking, the corresponding numbers are 99.21, 93.85, and 24.33%. Critically, SafeGRPO also improves general capability: the average score across ScienceQA, IconQA, MathVista, MM-Vet, and POPE rises from 77.98 to 78.75 (8B) and from 76.27 to 78.10 (4B), whereas VLGuard collapses to 16.94 and Think-in-Safety drops to 52.02 on the 8B model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SafeGRPO outperforms all three baselines on every safety metric for both model scales. Against VLGuard (SFT-based), SafeGRPO gains +2.01 in average Jailbreak Defense and +3.84 in Safety Awareness on the 8B model, while reducing the MOSSBench Refusal Rate from 95.00% to 20.00%—a 75-point drop that eliminates the severe over-sensitivity introduced by VLGuard. Against ECSO (inference-time image-to-text conversion), SafeGRPO improves Jailbreak Defense by +3.34 and Safety Awareness by +4.97, with a slightly lower Refusal Rate (20.00% vs. 26.33%). Against Think-in-Safety (reasoning-guided SFT), SafeGRPO leads by +1.33 in Jailbreak Defense and +5.51 in Safety Awareness, while cutting the Refusal Rate from 64.00% to 20.00%. On general capability, SafeGRPO is the only method that improves over the base model (78.75 vs. 77.98 average on 8B), whereas VLGuard and Think-in-Safety suffer catastrophic degradation (16.94 and 52.02 respectively), confirming that the RL-based approach avoids the catastrophic forgetting inherent to SFT-based safety alignment.

## Relation to Prior Work

SafeGRPO extends the GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) paradigm from DeepSeekMath [43]—originally designed for verifiable mathematical reasoning—into the domain of multimodal safety alignment, where correctness is inherently subjective. It departs from prior safety alignment methods (VLGuard's SFT, ECSO's inference-time transformation, Think-in-Safety's reasoning-guided fine-tuning) by replacing human-labeled or preference-based supervision with rule-governed self-rewards that evaluate structural format, modality-level tag consistency, and behavioral alignment. The step-guided safety thinking mechanism further distinguishes the work by enforcing a reason-before-answering structure with explicit per-modality safety tags, making the reasoning trace itself a verifiable optimization target rather than an unregulated byproduct.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that RL-based safety alignment can simultaneously improve safety scores and preserve (or even improve) general multimodal capabilities, whereas SFT-based approaches cause catastrophic degradation (e.g., VLGuard drops general benchmark averages by ~60 points) and severe over-sensitivity (refusal rates jumping from ~27% to ~98%). This contrast is practically significant for deploying safety-aligned MLLMs in real applications where helpfulness must be maintained. Open questions include whether the rule-governed reward design generalizes to more complex or multi-turn safety scenarios, whether the 3K-scale dataset suffices for broader safety taxonomies, and whether the approach extends naturally to additional modalities (audio, video) or to safety dimensions beyond binary safe/unsafe judgments.
