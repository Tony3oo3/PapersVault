## Problem

Existing instruction-guided video editors are trained and evaluated on exocentric (third-person) footage with moderate motion and minimal interaction, creating a significant domain gap for egocentric views where rapid egomotion, frequent hand-object interactions, and complex occlusions are the norm. Moreover, high-quality diffusion-based editing pipelines require dozens of denoising steps over the full clip, making them too slow for the real-time, low-latency responses that interactive augmented reality demands.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261499/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a continuous egocentric video stream (512×384 px, 16 fps) and a natural-language edit instruction, producing an edited video in a watch-as-you-generate streaming fashion. The first chunk of 9 RGB frames (3 latent frames) is emitted after 855 ms end-to-end latency on a single H100 GPU, and subsequent chunks of 12 frames are generated at 38.1 fps throughput including autoencoder encode/decode. The non-streaming variant processes the full 81-frame clip at once, incurring ~13.4 s latency before the first frame is visible.

## Method

EgoEdit is built on a Wan 2.1 DiT video generator and adapted for editing via channel-wise concatenation of the source video with the noisy target latent before patchification, avoiding the quadratic self-attention cost of sequence-wise concatenation used by prior editors. Training uses Rectified Flow with cross-attention text conditioning, finetuned on EgoEditData (93.6k manually curated egocentric editing pairs emphasizing hand-object interaction and hand preservation) plus 1.31M additional video and 3.5M image editing pairs. To achieve real-time streaming, a two-phase distillation is applied: bidirectional DMD compresses the 40-step classifier-free-guided model into a 4-step model, and Self-Forcing then trains the student to roll out autoregressively chunk-by-chunk, correcting its own exposure bias and enabling sub-second first-frame latency with 4 NFEs per chunk.

## Results

On EgoEditBench, EgoEdit achieves the highest VLM score (7.76) among all instruction-guided and streaming methods, outperforming Lucy Edit (5.44), InsV2V (5.24), and StreamDiffusionV2 (2.55) by a wide margin. On EditVerseBench (general editing), EgoEdit-RT scores 8.18 VLM, closely matching the closed-source EditVerse (8.26) while EgoEdit scores 8.00. The real-time streaming variant EgoEdit-RT retains 99.4% of the full model's VLM score on EgoEditBench (7.71 vs. 7.76) while running at 38.1 fps with 855 ms first-frame latency on a single H100 GPU. EgoEdit drops only 0.24 VLM points from general to egocentric tasks, compared to 0.83 for Lucy Edit and 0.47 for InsV2V.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On EgoEditBench, EgoEdit (VLM 7.76) substantially outperforms all instruction-guided baselines: Lucy Edit (5.44), InsV2V (5.24), and attention-manipulation methods TokenFlow (4.99) and STDF (4.59). The real-time variant EgoEdit-RT (7.71) also dominates streaming baselines StreamDiffusion (4.32) and StreamDiffusionV2 (2.55) by a large margin, confirming that distillation preserves quality. On EditVerseBench (general editing), EgoEdit-RT (8.18) slightly trails the closed-source EditVerse (8.26) but exceeds all other open baselines, while EgoEdit (8.00) is competitive with Señorita-2M (6.99) and AnyV2V (6.46). Notably, Señorita-2M and AnyV2V receive EgoEdit's first frame as input for fair comparison, so their EgoEditBench scores are partially attributable to EgoEdit itself. The paper highlights that EgoEdit's small 0.24-point VLM drop from general to egocentric tasks (vs. 0.83 for Lucy Edit) demonstrates the specific benefit of training on EgoEditData for egocentric robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

EgoEdit sits at the intersection of two active research threads: instruction-guided video editing (InstructPix2Pix → InsV2V → Lucy Edit → EditVerse) and real-time autoregressive video generation (Diffusion Forcing → CausVid → Self-Forcing). It adopts the channel-wise source conditioning from Lucy Edit to avoid the quadratic attention cost of sequence concatenation, and applies Self-Forcing distillation to an editing model rather than a pure generator. The key departure is the egocentric domain: prior editors and their training corpora target exocentric footage with moderate motion, while EgoEditData explicitly curates hand-object interaction pairs from Ego4D/EgoExo4D, and EgoEditBench evaluates under large egomotion and occlusion conditions absent from existing benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first system to close the loop on real-time, instruction-guided egocentric video editing, directly enabling interactive AR use cases that were previously infeasible due to latency. The publicly released EgoEditData (93.6k pairs) and EgoEditBench (1,700 samples, 15 tasks) fill a clear gap in the egocentric editing literature and will likely become standard evaluation resources. Open questions include whether the 855 ms first-frame latency and 512×384/16 fps operating point can be pushed further, whether the qualitative gap of the RT variant (weaker OOD robustness, lower temporal consistency) can be closed, and whether the limited structural interaction of inserted objects (e.g., swords not cutting furniture) can be addressed without sacrificing real-time performance.
