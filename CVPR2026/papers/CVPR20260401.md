## Problem

Existing hierarchical classification methods lack theoretically grounded uncertainty quantification under image degradation, cannot adaptively roll back predictions to coarser levels when confidence is insufficient (causing overclassification and cascading errors), and do not jointly optimize multi-level training objectives. As a result, models persist with low-confidence fine-grained predictions on degraded inputs rather than falling back to more reliable higher-level categories, a failure mode with serious consequences in high-stakes applications like military recognition and medical diagnosis.

## Input / Output

The method consumes degraded RGB images (224×224 for remote sensing ships, 448×448 for birds) together with GloVe semantic embeddings of class names as an auxiliary text modality. Rather than always outputting the finest-level label, the model produces an adaptive granularity decision: it may return a species-level prediction for clear images but fall back to family or order level when degradation renders fine-grained cues unreliable, accompanied by per-level calibrated confidence scores and Monte Carlo variational uncertainty estimates.

## Method

HierUQ integrates three tightly coupled modules. First, HUQ fuses ViT-B/16 visual features with semantic embeddings via bilinear attention and semantic-guided cross-attention, propagates them through a 3-layer GatedGNN over the hierarchical adjacency matrix, and quantifies uncertainty using hierarchy-aware label smoothing (with LCA-based structural decay) plus KL divergence regularization, Monte Carlo dropout (T=10 stochastic passes), and per-level temperature scaling. Second, CAPA builds a Bidirectional Logits Tree (BiLT) with a learned gating function to reconcile top-down and bottom-up reasoning, models the rollback decision as an MDP with confidence-aware thresholding optimized via Bayesian optimization (Gaussian Process surrogate), and applies self-paced sample selection based on a multi-level loss difficulty vector. Third, MLJO combines four loss components (hierarchical consistency, granularity harmonization, classification, and fallback penalty) through Chebyshev scalarization for Pareto optimization, balances conflicting gradients with MGDA, and schedules training using a Lyapunov-based stability criterion with dynamic sample weighting and smooth stage transitions.

## Results

On HRSC-Deg (2-level ship classification), HierUQ achieves an ISDL of 85.45%, hierarchical precision of 99.71%, hierarchical recall of 99.78%, and 100% coarse-level accuracy with 92.23% fine-level accuracy. On the more complex CUB-Deg (3-level bird classification), it attains 80.91% ISDL, 99.59% P_H, 98.36% R_H, and 85.73% species-level accuracy. The full model outperforms its ablation variant HierUQ-C (without bilinear fusion) by +9.18% ISDL on HRSC-Deg and +13.91% ISDL on CUB-Deg, confirming the value of the complete architectural integration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HierUQ outperforms all 12 baselines on every reported metric across both datasets. On HRSC-Deg, the largest margin is +12.72% ISDL over SGHPN (85.45% vs. 72.73%), the strongest prior method on that benchmark, while also achieving perfect coarse accuracy (100% vs. 98.48% for SGHPN). On CUB-Deg, HierUQ exceeds VT-BPAN by +15.13% ISDL (80.91% vs. 65.78%) and +2.75% species accuracy (85.73% vs. 82.98%), and surpasses BiLT by +15.70% ISDL (80.91% vs. 65.21%). The gap over older baselines is particularly stark: HMC-LMLP and HMCN score below 71% ISDL on HRSC-Deg and below 41% on CUB-Deg, while even the strongest Transformer-based baselines (CrossViT, TransHP, SGHPN) remain 10–15 points behind on ISDL. Notably, the ablation variant HierUQ-C (without bilinear fusion) already beats all baselines, indicating that the core HUQ + CAPA + MLJO pipeline is the primary driver of gains, with bilinear fusion providing an additional ~9–14% ISDL boost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the hierarchical classification (HC) line of work by integrating two recent threads: BiLT's bidirectional logits tree for granularity reconciliation (Lu et al., AAAI 2025) and SGCA's semantic-guided cross-attention for degraded image understanding (Wang et al., 2023). Where prior HC methods (CHMCNN, HRN, TransHP) rely on fixed decision paths and single-level losses, and where BiLT and SPUR address granularity or self-paced learning in isolation, HierUQ unifies principled uncertainty quantification (hierarchical label smoothing + KL divergence + MC dropout), confidence-driven MDP-based fallback, and Pareto/MGDA multi-objective optimization into a single ViT-GNN pipeline. The departure from prior work is primarily architectural and optimization-level: rather than treating hierarchy as a static constraint, the model dynamically selects prediction granularity based on calibrated confidence, a mechanism absent from all compared baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The adaptive granularity fallback addresses a practically critical failure mode in high-stakes HC (military ship recognition, medical diagnosis, biodiversity monitoring) where overconfident fine-grained predictions under poor image quality can cause mission-critical errors. However, the evidence base is limited: both evaluation datasets (HRSC-Deg, CUB-Deg) are small (≤1,272 training samples) and self-constructed via a frozen pretrained classifier for hierarchy completion, raising questions about generalizability to larger, noisier real-world hierarchies. Open questions include whether the MDP-based rollback is necessary versus simpler threshold-based fallback, how the method scales to deeper taxonomies (e.g., ImageNet's 10,000+ classes), and whether the heavy multi-objective optimization (Pareto + MGDA + Lyapunov scheduling) is worth its computational overhead relative to simpler weighted-sum losses.
