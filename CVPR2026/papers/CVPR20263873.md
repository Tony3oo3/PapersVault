## Problem

Dynamic visual effects (explosions, crushing, deflation) in video generation involve higher-level, temporally evolving semantics that cannot be captured by explicit motion trajectories or a single hand-crafted text prompt. Prior customization work targets low-level subject or camera motion, while training-based effect-specific models (e.g., VFX Creator) require separate fine-tuning per effect type and suffer from fixed-length supervision and dataset biases. No training-free, model-agnostic method existed for transferring complex visual effects from a reference video to arbitrary novel scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263873/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference video of arbitrary resolution and duration showing the target effect, an initial text prompt describing the target scene or subject, and optionally a source image for image-to-video conditioning. It produces a 480×832, 81-frame generated video (at 24 fps) that reproduces the reference effect while preserving the scene semantics, along with the iteratively optimized text prompt. The base generator is Wan 2.1 14B (or HunyuanVideo), and the VLM for prompt refinement is Gemini 1.5 Pro; no constraints are placed on the reference video's length or resolution.

## Method

P-Flow is a training-free, test-time prompt optimization framework with three components. First, a noise prior enhancement extracts motion-relevant temporal structure from the reference video via flow matching inversion, then applies a two-stage SVD projection (spatial filtering to suppress appearance-specific details, temporal filtering to retain dominant motion) and blends the result with fresh Gaussian noise at a small coefficient (α=0.001) to balance optimization stability and diversity. Second, at each of 10 iterations, the VLM receives a vertically stacked composite video (reference, previous generation, current generation) plus the current prompt and historical trajectory, and outputs a structured JSON containing a refined prompt that modifies only effect-related descriptions while preserving the original subject. Third, a lightweight historical trajectory mechanism stores all past prompts and VLM analyses as text (avoiding expensive visual tokens) to provide long-range optimization context, while only the most recent two generated videos are included as visual input to keep token costs manageable. This decoupling of long-term logic context from short-term visual context enables coherent, multi-iteration refinement without processing long video sequences.

## Results

On the Open-VFX benchmark, P-Flow achieves the best overall FID-VID (31.13), FVD (882.63), and Dynamic Degree (0.91) across both image-to-video and text-to-video settings. In the I2V task, P-Flow reaches FID-VID 29.32, FVD 784.51, and Dynamic Degree 0.94; in T2V, it achieves FID-VID 32.93, FVD 980.75, and Dynamic Degree 0.87. In human evaluation, P-Flow is preferred over Wan 2.1 (80%/75%), HunyuanVideo (84%/81%), and VFX Creator (58%) in I2V/T2V respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

P-Flow outperforms both foundational video generation models (Wan 2.1 and HunyuanVideo) across all three metrics in both I2V and T2V settings, with particularly large gains in Dynamic Degree (0.94 vs. 0.33 for Wan 2.1 and 0.51 for HunyuanVideo in I2V), indicating substantially more temporally coherent visual effects. Against the training-based VFX Creator, P-Flow achieves a lower FID-VID (29.32 vs. 29.92) and a much higher Dynamic Degree (0.94 vs. 0.63) in I2V, though VFX Creator has a slightly better FVD (752.95 vs. 784.51); VFX Creator does not support T2V at all. In human preference, P-Flow is strongly preferred over Wan 2.1 (80% vs. 20% I2V, 75% vs. 25% T2V) and HunyuanVideo (84% vs. 16% I2V, 81% vs. 19% T2V), and moderately preferred over VFX Creator (58% vs. 42% I2V). The training-free human-feedback baselines (Wan 2.1+HF, HunyuanVideo+HF) also fall well short of P-Flow, confirming that a single manual prompt revision is insufficient compared to iterative VLM-guided optimization.

## Relation to Prior Work

P-Flow departs from the dominant line of video customization work (DreamVideo, LAMP, MotionCtrl) that targets low-level subject or camera motion via explicit trajectories, and instead addresses higher-level dynamic visual effects (explosions, crushing, deflation) that lack clear motion paths. It repurposes flow matching inversion (from FlowEdit and Taming Rectified Flow) not for editing but to extract a motion-preserving noise prior, and repositions VLMs—previously used as evaluators or training-time aligners (Gecko, EvolveDirector, VideoAlign)—as test-time optimization agents that iteratively refine text prompts. The closest prior work, VFX Creator, tackles the same visual-effect task but requires per-effect LoRA training and supports only image-to-video generation, whereas P-Flow is training-free and model-agnostic across both T2V and I2V settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that a training-free, prompt-optimization loop with a VLM can match or exceed a dedicated training-based architecture (VFX Creator) on fidelity metrics while substantially outperforming it on temporal dynamics, P-Flow suggests that test-time semantic reasoning may be a viable alternative to per-task fine-tuning for effect customization. Practical limitations include dependence on a powerful VLM (Gemini 1.5 Pro) for each iteration, 10 optimization steps, and 8-GPU inference per video, which constrains real-time or low-resource deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-effect compositions, longer videos, or effects requiring precise spatial localization, and whether the Open-VFX benchmark (15 categories, ~5 s clips) adequately captures the full diversity of dynamic visual phenomena.
