## Problem

Geometry-free view synthesis transformers have achieved state-of-the-art NVS performance, but no rigorous scaling analysis exists for this class of models, leaving their compute-optimal design principles unclear. In particular, the decoder-only architecture of LVSM was assumed necessary, yet prior encoder-decoder results were confounded by suboptimal architectural choices and unequal compute budgets, and the role of training hyperparameters like the number of target views per scene had never been systematically studied.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261269/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes V_C context images at 256×256 resolution (patch size 8) with known camera poses and intrinsics, along with a target camera pose and intrinsics, and produces a rendered novel view image. A key structural detail is that the encoder processes the context set once to produce a set of scene latent tokens (all context patch tokens, not a fixed-size bottleneck), and the decoder then renders one or more target views in parallel via cross-attention from these shared tokens, so rendering V_T views requires only a single encoding pass rather than V_T full forward passes.

## Method

SVSM replaces LVSM's bidirectional decoder-only design with a unidirectional encoder-decoder: a standard bidirectional transformer encoder maps context images to scene latent tokens, and a cross-attention decoder renders target views from these fixed tokens, amortizing encoding cost across multiple target views. The paper's central empirical finding is the effective batch size hypothesis: performance depends only on the product B·V_T (scenes in batch × target views per scene), not on B and V_T individually, which means SVSM can reduce B and increase V_T to lower compute while maintaining performance. For multiview settings (V_C > 2), the model requires PRoPE relative camera pose embeddings in both encoder and decoder attention to avoid premature scaling saturation. Scaling experiments spanning 100 petaflops to 100 exaflops recover Chinchilla-like power-law relationships (a ≈ b ≈ 0.5 for SVSM), and the resulting Pareto-optimal models achieve state-of-the-art NVS quality with 2–3× less training compute than decoder-only LVSM.

## Results

On RealEstate10K stereo NVS (V_C=2), SVSM's Pareto-optimal 416M model achieves PSNR 30.01, SSIM 0.910, and LPIPS 0.096 using only 0.77 zflops of training compute—less than half the 1.60 zflops required by LVSM decoder-only (PSNR 29.67, LPIPS 0.098). On DL3DV multiview NVS (V_C=4), SVSM's Pareto-optimal 400M model reaches PSNR 26.87 and LPIPS 0.129, outperforming LVSM decoder-only+PRoPE (PSNR 26.19, LPIPS 0.145) at comparable compute (44 vs. 43 eflops) while rendering 4× faster. SVSM also achieves a 3× more compute-efficient Pareto frontier than LVSM across the full scaling range (100 PFLOPs to 100 EFLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LVSM decoder-only (the prior SOTA), SVSM Pareto-optimal wins on all three metrics (PSNR +0.34, SSIM +0.004, LPIPS −0.002) while using less than half the training FLOPs (0.77 vs. 1.60 zflops) and rendering 1.9× faster at V_C=2 and 5.8× faster at V_C=8. Against LVSM encoder-decoder, SVSM improves PSNR by +1.43 and LPIPS by −0.018 at roughly one-third the compute. In the multiview setting (V_C=4, DL3DV), SVSM Pareto-optimal beats LVSM decoder-only+PRoPE by +0.68 PSNR and −0.016 LPIPS at nearly identical compute, with 4× higher rendering FPS. Against geometry-aware methods on RealEstate10K, SVSM (PSNR 30.01, LPIPS 0.096) surpasses GS-LRM (28.10, 0.114) by +1.91 PSNR, MVSplat (26.39, 0.128) by +3.62 PSNR, pixelSplat (26.09, 0.136) by +3.92 PSNR, and pixelNeRF (20.43, 0.550) by +9.58 PSNR, establishing a new state-of-the-art across all geometry-free and geometry-aware baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LVSM, the decoder-only transformer that established state-of-the-art geometry-free NVS, and on PRoPE, a relative camera positional encoding for multiview attention. It departs from LVSM's core architectural claim—that bidirectional decoder-only attention is necessary for high-fidelity synthesis—by showing that a unidirectional encoder-decoder with cross-attention achieves the same scaling behavior at 3× lower compute. The paper also imports the Chinchilla scaling-law methodology from language modeling and adapts it to NVS, introducing the 'effective batch size' (B × V_T) as the correct unit of training throughput for view synthesis, a concept absent from prior NVS training analyses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: practitioners training NVS transformers can adopt the SVSM recipe to cut training compute by 2–3× while matching or exceeding prior SOTA, and the Chinchilla-style coefficients give a principled way to allocate budget between model size and data. A key limitation is that all experiments use small, pose-labeled datasets (RealEstate10K, DL3DV) with repeated scenes, so whether the scaling laws hold at truly large-data regimes remains open. The finding that PRoPE is essential for multiview scaling but not for stereo also suggests that the inductive-bias requirements of encoder-decoder NVS may shift as context view counts grow, an area worth further study.
