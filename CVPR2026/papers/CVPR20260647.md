## Problem

Existing monocular metric depth estimation methods that achieve cross-camera generalization either require large-FoV camera data during training (UniK3D) or need separate models for indoor and outdoor domains (DAC). The paper asks whether a single model trained solely on perspective images can robustly generalize to all camera types (perspective, fisheye, 360°) and all scene domains without substantially increasing model or dataset scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260647/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts a single RGB image projected into Equi-Rectangular Projection (ERP) as its canonical input representation, which uniformly encodes both perspective and large-FoV camera views. It outputs a full-resolution metric depth map in meters. The ERP representation means the input is a 2D image where pixel coordinates correspond to longitude-latitude on a sphere, introducing geometric distortion that the architecture must account for.

## Method

UniDAC decouples metric depth estimation into two components with different contextual scopes: a relative depth map predicted from early (local) encoder features via a DPT decoder, and a spatially varying scale map predicted from final (global) encoder features. The key Depth-Guided Scale Estimation module first predicts a low-resolution scale map via self-attention on global features, then upsamples it to full resolution using a non-parametric depth-guided routing: for each pixel, it computes softmax weights over a 3×3 neighborhood of the low-res scale map based on depth-difference similarity, ensuring object boundaries are respected without additional learnable parameters. A distortion-aware positional embedding, RoPE-φ, modifies standard 2D-RoPE by applying a latitude-dependent cosine weight w(φ) = δ + (1−δ)cos(φ) to the rotation angles, so that positional distances in ERP more faithfully reflect geodesic proximity on the sphere. The model uses a ViT-L backbone initialized with DINOv3 weights and is trained with a combined SI_log loss (λ=1 for relative depth, λ=0.85 for metric depth) on 1.1M perspective images from seven indoor and outdoor datasets.

## Results

UniDAC achieves state-of-the-art cross-camera generalization in zero-shot monocular metric depth estimation. On ScanNet++ (fisheye, indoor), UniDAC reaches δ₁=0.918 and A.Rel=0.097, improving δ₁ by ~26% over both UniK3D (0.651) and DAC_U (0.658). On KITTI-360 (fisheye, outdoor), UniDAC attains δ₁=0.836 and A.Rel=0.141, outperforming UniK3D (δ₁=0.817) and DAC_U (δ₁=0.708). On Pano3D-GV2 (360°, indoor), UniDAC achieves δ₁=0.768, slightly below UniK3D (0.785) but well above DAC_U (0.684) by ~8.4%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniDAC decisively outperforms DAC_U (the unified indoor+outdoor variant of DAC) across all three test datasets, with the largest margin on ScanNet++ where δ₁ jumps from 0.658 to 0.918 (+26%) and A.Rel drops from 0.233 to 0.097. Against UniK3D, which was trained on 7.94M images including large-FoV data, UniDAC (trained on only 1.45M perspective images) still wins on ScanNet++ (δ₁ 0.918 vs 0.651) and KITTI-360 (δ₁ 0.836 vs 0.817, A.Rel 0.141 vs 0.244), though UniK3D retains a slight edge on Pano3D-GV2 (δ₁ 0.785 vs 0.768) and KITTI-360 RMSE (2.400 vs 3.977). UniDAC also substantially surpasses Metric3Dv2 and UniDepth, which were not designed for large-FoV inputs, with δ₁ gaps exceeding 30 percentage points on ScanNet++. The domain-specific DAC_I and DAC_O models (Table 2) confirm the motivation for decoupling: DAC_I collapses on outdoor KITTI-360 (δ₁=0.082–0.245) while DAC_O collapses on indoor ScanNet++ (δ₁=0.109–0.256), whereas UniDAC maintains strong performance across both domains with a single model.

## Relation to Prior Work

UniDAC extends the cross-camera generalization line initiated by DAC (ERP canonical space with FoV-aligned training) and UniK3D (spherical-harmonics angular representation), but departs from both by requiring neither large-FoV training data nor separate indoor/outdoor models. Its core architectural departure is the decoupling of metric depth into a local relative-depth branch (early encoder features) and a global scale-map branch (late encoder features), building on the scale-shift decomposition in UniDepth and Metric3Dv2 but replacing the single scalar with a spatially-varying map. The RoPE-φ positional embedding directly extends 2D-RoPE (Heo et al., ECCV 2024) with a latitude-aware cosine weight to respect ERP geodesic geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UniDAC demonstrates that a single model trained exclusively on perspective images can outperform methods that include large-FoV data in training, suggesting that the canonical ERP representation plus the relative/scale decoupling is a more effective inductive bias than simply exposing the network to diverse camera geometries. The Depth-Guided Scale Estimation module is a lightweight, non-parametric technique that could transfer to other dense-prediction tasks requiring spatially-varying calibration. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ERP to other panoramic projections, whether the ViT-L backbone requirement limits deployment, and whether the modest RoPE-φ gains on narrow-band depth (KITTI-360) indicate a fundamental limitation of latitude weighting for sparse-depth regimes.
