## Problem

Vision-Language Models pre-trained on coarse-grained data (high inter-class, low intra-class variance) fail to capture the subtle distinctions needed for fine-grained clustering, where classes are visually similar yet intra-class variation is large. Additionally, nearly all existing deep clustering methods require the number of clusters to be pre-specified, making them impractical for real-world data exploration where the true category count is unknown. This paper addresses both limitations simultaneously in a single unsupervised framework.

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (resized to 224×224) and applies two distinct augmentations (View A and View B) per image; no labels or pre-specified cluster count are required. The frozen VLM teacher (CLIP ViT-B/16 or pre-trained ResNet-50) processes only View B to produce top-k sparse guidance targets, while the trainable student model processes both views. The output consists of soft cluster assignment probabilities per image and a dynamically inferred number of effective clusters determined by the DPMM stick-breaking process during training.

## Method

The framework unifies three components under a Dirichlet Process Mixture Model (DPMM) variational inference objective (ELBO maximization). For intra-cluster compactness, a Low-Rank Guided VLM module uses Theorem 1 to relax the non-differentiable rank-1 prediction matrix objective into convergence toward a shared k-sparse prototype derived from the frozen VLM teacher's top-k outputs, combined with a cluster-level contrastive loss for geometric tightening. For inter-cluster separability, a perturbation-enhanced instance contrastive loss provides local push while an orthogonality loss (‖MᵀM − I‖²_F) on a globally shared learnable prototype matrix enforces macroscopic geometric separation. The DPMM stick-breaking prior acts as a complexity penalty that automatically activates or deactivates clusters, enabling the model to infer the optimal cluster number without pre-specification.

## Results

On the four fine-grained benchmarks, Ours+CLIP achieves NMI of 70.9 (CUB-200-2011), 69.1 (Stanford Dogs), 88.4 (Oxford Flower), and 88.0 (Oxford-IIIT Pet), with corresponding ACC of 41.8, 53.2, 72.6, and 82.2, setting new state-of-the-art on all four datasets. On large-scale ImageNet subsets, the method reaches NMI 92.4/ACC 84.2 on ImageNet-50 and NMI 87.8/ACC 77.1 on ImageNet-100, outperforming all baselines. The framework also dynamically infers cluster numbers close to ground truth (e.g., 210.4 vs. 200 for CUB, 127.1 vs. 120 for Stanford Dogs) without pre-specification.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Ours+CLIP outperforms the strongest VLM-guided baselines TAC [29] and CLUDI [46] on all four fine-grained datasets by substantial margins: e.g., on CUB-200-2011 it gains +6.3 NMI over TAC (70.9 vs. 64.6) and +9.6 NMI over CLUDI (70.9 vs. 61.3); on Oxford Flower it leads CLUDI by +6.9 NMI (88.4 vs. 81.5). Against traditional non-VLM methods like SCAN [47] and DivClust [33], the improvement is even more dramatic (e.g., +25.9 NMI over SCAN on CUB). On ImageNet-50 and ImageNet-100, Ours+CLIP beats CLUDI [46] by +1.2 and +0.7 NMI respectively, and surpasses TEMI [1] by +6.3 and +2.2 NMI. The sole exception is ImageNet-200 ACC, where CLUDI edges out Ours+CLIP by 1.4 points (73.7 vs. 72.3), though Ours+CLIP still leads on NMI (86.6 vs. 86.1). Even the ResNet-50 teacher variant (Ours+ResNet) surpasses TAC on CUB NMI (65.1 vs. 64.6) and CLUDI on Oxford Flower NMI (84.7 vs. 81.5), demonstrating the framework's effectiveness independent of the specific VLM backbone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: VLM-assisted deep clustering (TEMI, TAC, CLUDI), fine-grained visual recognition, and nonparametric Bayesian clustering (DPMM with stick-breaking variational inference, building on Nalisnick & Smyth). It departs from prior VLM-guided clustering methods by explicitly targeting the fine-grained regime—where inter-class variance is low and intra-class variance is high—and by reformulating the low-rank prediction matrix objective as a differentiable top-k sparse alignment problem guided by a frozen VLM teacher. The orthogonal guidance on learnable prototypes and the DPMM-based dynamic cluster inference are combined into a single ELBO-maximizing framework, a synthesis not present in any single prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to perform fine-grained clustering without pre-specifying the number of clusters addresses a practical bottleneck in real-world data exploration, and the VLM-as-teacher paradigm offers a scalable way to inject semantic priors without fine-grained labels. However, the frozen teacher limits adaptability to domains outside the VLM's training distribution, and the method's reliance on careful hyperparameter tuning (top-k, temperatures, DPMM concentration) may hinder straightforward adoption. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-image modalities, whether the VLM teacher can be jointly adapted during clustering, and how performance scales when the true number of fine-grained classes far exceeds what the VLM was trained to distinguish.
