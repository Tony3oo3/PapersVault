## Problem

Existing human-in-the-loop vision classification methods (e.g., Agile Modeling, Modeling Collaborator) assume users begin with a static, well-understood concept definition. In practice, users of subjective classifiers—such as content moderators—start with vague ideas and must iteratively refine their understanding by exploring borderline cases, a process the authors term 'concept deliberation.' No prior framework explicitly supports this evolving, subjective nature of concept definitions within the classifier training loop.

## Input / Output

The system takes a subjective concept name (e.g., 'healthy food'), an optional unlabeled image dataset (or defaults to WebLI), and iterative user labels with optional textual feedback on surfaced borderline images. It produces a structured concept definition—a hierarchy of positive and negative subconcepts with explicit edge cases—that serves a dual role: a human-readable articulation for consistent labeling and a textual prompt for a VLM-based binary classifier. The classifier outputs a binary in-scope/out-of-scope decision with a chain-of-thought rationale for each image.

## Method

Agile Deliberation structures deliberation into two stages. Stage 1 (Concept Scoping) decomposes the initial concept into unit concepts via prompt-chained reasoning, then expands each into positive/negative subconcepts with retrieved representative images for user confirmation, yielding an initial structured definition d₀. Stage 2 (Concept Iteration) repeatedly surfaces semantically borderline images—identified not by model uncertainty (since VLMs are uncalibrated) but by LLM/VLM reasoning about the natural-language decision boundary—through a pipeline of LLM-generated borderline queries, dictionary-learning-based clustering, UCB multi-armed bandit cluster selection, and VLM ambiguity mining to form coherent deliberation batches. User labels and feedback on each batch feed an automatic prompt optimization module that generates candidate refined definitions, from which the best is selected by greedy F₁ maximization over all accumulated labels. The entire loop uses Gemini-Pro 2.5 for decomposition and Gemini-Flash 2.5 for classification, query generation, and refinement, with no fine-tuning of the foundation models.

## Results

Across 18 live user sessions (9 participants × 2 concepts), Agile Deliberation achieves an average F1 of 0.59 on *paid to play* and 0.58 on *healthy food*, representing relative improvements of +11% and +10% over zero-shot Gemini-Flash 2.5 classifiers (F1 ≈ 0.48 for both concepts). Compared to the automated Modeling Collaborator baseline, Agile Deliberation yields 7.5% higher F1 on average. Gains are driven primarily by precision improvements (+22% on paid to play, +11% on healthy food) with modest recall reductions (−15% to −17%), reflecting that zero-shot VLMs tend to over-predict positives for subjective concepts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Modeling Collaborator baseline [Toubal et al., CVPR 2024], which automatically decomposes concepts into detailed VLM prompts without user feedback, Agile Deliberation achieves higher F1 on both concepts: 0.59 vs. 0.53 on *paid to play* (+6 pts) and 0.58 vs. 0.50 on *healthy food* (+8 pts), confirming that automated LLM decomposition alone cannot capture users' nuanced, evolving intentions. Against the zero-shot Gemini-Flash 2.5 classifier, Agile Deliberation improves F1 by 11% (paid to play) and 10% (healthy food), while Modeling Collaborator yields only modest gains (+5% and +2% respectively). Against Manual Deliberation (participants hand-searching borderline images and editing prompts), Agile Deliberation delivers larger relative F1 improvements: +11% vs. +8% on paid to play, and +10% vs. −3% on healthy food, where manual deliberation actually degraded performance below the already-strong zero-shot baseline. The paper notes that absolute F1 scores are not directly comparable between the Agile and Manual groups because participants' concept complexities differed; the meaningful comparison is each system's relative gain over its own zero-shot starting point.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Agile Modeling / Modeling Collaborator line of work on human-in-the-loop subjective visual classification by introducing an explicit 'concept deliberation' phase that prior systems assumed away. The VLM-based classifier architecture (chain-of-thought prompting for binary decisions) is inherited directly from Modeling Collaborator [43], and the prompt refinement mechanism adapts automatic prompt optimization (APO) [28, 29] to a human-in-the-loop setting. The key conceptual departure is replacing model-uncertainty-based active learning with semantically borderline example retrieval, motivated by the observation that VLM confidence is poorly calibrated relative to human ambiguity perception.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in content moderation and subjective classification where users begin with vague, evolving concept definitions rather than stable ones, and demonstrates that structured deliberation support yields measurable classifier improvements and lower cognitive load. However, the evaluation is limited to 9 participants across only 2 concepts in 1.5-hour sessions, and classifier performance comparisons are between-participant rather than within-participant, weakening causal claims. Open questions include whether the UCB-based cluster selection and greedy prompt update scale to more complex or multi-class concepts, and whether the framework generalizes beyond the two tested domains.
