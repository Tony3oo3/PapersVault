## Problem

Existing MLLMs for video understanding rely on text-based chain-of-thought reasoning, which suffers from insufficient cross-modal interaction and increased hallucination, particularly on long videos or extended reasoning chains. The key gap is the inability to dynamically attend to relevant visual evidence during the reasoning process, causing error accumulation and inaccurate temporal grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263808/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a video and a text query, then generates a multi-round multimodal CoT trajectory in which it can invoke a video clipping tool (specifying start/end timestamps) to densely sample new frames from targeted segments before producing a final answer. Outputs are structured as thinking steps, optional tool calls with JSON arguments, and a final answer containing either temporal boundaries (start/end seconds), a VQA response, or a grounded VQA pair (time range + answer). The video clipping tool returns a densely sampled frame sequence for the requested range, which is appended to the context for the next reasoning round.

## Method

VITAL is an end-to-end agentic framework built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B that integrates a visual toolbox (primarily a video clipping tool) into a multi-round generation loop, where the model adaptively decides whether to sample new frames or directly answer, forming a multimodal CoT grounded in visual evidence. Training proceeds in four stages: SFT on MTVR-CoT-72k, DGRPO on MTVR-RL-110k, then tool-augmented SFT and DGRPO on smaller tool-specific subsets (MTVR-CoT-Tool-18k, MTVR-RL-Tool-16k). The proposed DGRPO algorithm addresses difficulty imbalance at two levels: task-wise, by rescaling IoU rewards for temporal grounding via a clamped linear transform with task-specific difficulty parameters; and sample-wise, by computing per-sample difficulty as the mean reward across G rollouts and applying a soft linear weight (0.5–1.0) that up-weights harder samples. Multi-task training jointly optimizes temporal grounding, video VQA, and grounded VQA, with a rollout filtering pipeline (PassAll@k / PassNone@k removal) to curate balanced-difficulty training data.

## Results

VITAL-7B achieves state-of-the-art performance across 14 video understanding benchmarks. On long video temporal grounding (VidChapters-7M), it reaches R@0.5 of 34.7% and R@0.7 of 24.3%, surpassing the previous best open-source model ReVisionLLM (27.4% R@0.5) by 7.3 points. On LongVideo-Reason, VITAL-7B achieves 79.3% accuracy versus 72.0% for LongVILA-R1-7B. On short video temporal grounding (Charades-STA), it attains R@0.5 of 72.0% and mIoU of 59.9%, and on grounded VQA (NExT-GQA) it reaches mIoU of 47.6% and accuracy of 80.5%, both setting new records.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VITAL-7B outperforms all 28 compared baselines on long video temporal grounding (VidChapters-7M R@0.5: 34.7% vs. 27.4% for ReVisionLLM and 27.3% for M-DETR) and long video VQA (LongVideo-Reason: 79.3% vs. 72.0% for LongVILA-R1-7B; Video-MME Long: 56.0% vs. 55.2% for LongVILA-R1-7B). On short video temporal grounding (Charades-STA), VITAL-7B achieves the best R@0.5 (72.0%) but trails DeepVideo-R1-7B (61.2%) and VideoChat-R1-7B (60.8%) on mIoU by 1.3 and 0.9 points respectively, suggesting its advantage is more pronounced in recall-oriented metrics. On ActivityNet-MR, VITAL-7B leads all baselines in both R@0.5 (50.8%) and mIoU (49.8%), surpassing TimeMarker-8B (49.5% mIoU) and TimeSearch-7B (43.9% mIoU). On grounded VQA, VITAL-7B sets new records on NExT-GQA (mIoU 47.6%, Acc 80.5%) and ReXTime (mIoU 43.0%), beating the next-best Temporal-RLT-7B by 10.3 mIoU points on NExT-GQA and TimeSearch-7B by 6.3 mIoU points on ReXTime. The consistent ΔToolbox gains (+2 to +7 points across tasks) confirm that the visual clipping tool provides a uniform benefit over the text-only CoT variant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the DeepSeek-R1/GRPO reinforcement learning paradigm from text-only LLMs to multimodal video reasoning, but makes a key conceptual departure: replacing text-based chain-of-thought with multimodal CoT where the model actively invokes a video clipping tool to densely sample new frames mid-reasoning. It builds on the tool-augmented LLM line (DeepEyes, OpenThinkImg for images; Search-R1 for text) and the long-video understanding line (LongVILA, LLaMA-VID, FlashVStream for token compression or context extension), but positions itself as an agentic framework where the model decides *when* and *where* to look rather than passively consuming a fixed frame budget. The DGRPO algorithm further extends GRPO with difficulty-aware reward scaling to address multi-task training instability, complementing prior work on GRPO stability (DAPO, DisCO) with a sample-level re-weighting approach.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and well-motivated failure mode—hallucination in long video reasoning due to insufficient cross-modal interaction—and provides a practical, extensible framework (VITAL) with two large-scale training datasets that the community can adopt. The finding that a simple video clipping tool outperforms richer tools (clip caption, clip QA) is a useful negative result suggesting that raw visual evidence is more reliable than LLM-generated textual summaries for grounding. Open questions include whether the approach scales to hour-long or multi-hour videos, whether the 7B model size is sufficient for the tool-augmented paradigm, and whether the DGRPO difficulty balancing generalizes beyond the specific task mix studied here.
