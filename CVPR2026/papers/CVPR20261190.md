## Problem

Temporally consistent surface reconstruction of dynamic 3D objects from unstructured point clouds remains difficult for long sequences: optimization-based methods accumulate drift and require minutes-to-hours of runtime, while learning-based approaches demand category-specific training and struggle to generalize. A general, category-agnostic method that produces high-fidelity, drift-free reconstructions in seconds across arbitrary subjects (humans, animals) has been missing.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261190/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a sequence of T unstructured point clouds (5000 points per frame in experiments, T ranging from 17 to 120) and a single keyframe mesh reconstructed via screened Poisson surface reconstruction, which fixes the topology. It outputs per-vertex 6-DoF deformations (a unit quaternion rotation and a bounded displacement vector) for every frame, applied to the reference mesh to yield a temporally coherent deformed mesh sequence. No inter-frame correspondences or category-specific templates are required; the keyframe mesh need only have correct topology, not precise geometric alignment.

## Method

Neu-PiG encodes the full deformation across all time steps into a pair of multi-resolution voxel grids parameterized by vertex positions (8-level hierarchy, 2³–32³ cells, 30-dim features) and normal directions (single 4³ grid, 2-dim features) of a reference surface, rather than storing per-timestep displacement fields. A Fourier time embedding (M=4 frequencies, 8-dim) is concatenated with the spatial latent codes and decoded by a lightweight 3-layer MLP (512 units, LeakyReLU) into a quaternion and displacement per vertex. The critical stabilization mechanism is Sobolev preconditioning applied to the latent grid gradients via (I + λ^l L^l)^(−2), a low-pass filter that couples neighboring cells and enforces spatially coherent updates without explicit correspondences. Training minimizes a robust Chamfer distance plus an isometry loss with Adam, using per-level learning rates and a confidence-weighted temporal schedule; the entire optimization converges in 8–32 seconds on an RTX 4090, over 60× faster than prior training-free methods.

## Results

Neu-PiG achieves the best Chamfer Distance, Normal Consistency, F-score, and Correspondence Error across all three benchmarks (DFAUST, AMA, DT4D). On DFAUST, it reaches CD 0.39×10⁻⁵, NC 0.968, F-0.5% 0.989, and Corr. 0.009 in 32 s; on the more challenging DT4D animal sequences, it attains CD 0.87×10⁻⁵ and F-0.5% 0.968, outperforming the next-best method (PDG) by nearly 2× in CD. The 250-epoch variant (Ours†) already reaches near-full quality (CD 0.40 on DFAUST) in just 8 s, while the full 1000-epoch run takes 32 s—still over 60× faster than PDG (7 min) and DynoSurf (30 min).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Neu-PiG outperforms all four baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the closest training-free competitor PDG, Neu-PiG reduces CD by ~15% on DFAUST (0.39 vs 0.46), ~48% on DT4D (0.87 vs 1.67), and ~44% on AMA (0.44 vs 0.79), while also improving Correspondence Error by roughly 2× on each dataset. Against DynoSurf, the gap is far larger on DT4D (CD 0.87 vs 15.18, a 17× improvement) and AMA (0.44 vs 1.01), reflecting DynoSurf's sensitivity to complex animal articulation. The learning-based baselines CaDeX and M2V are competitive on runtime (3–4 s) but trail substantially in accuracy: CaDeX achieves CD 56.51 on DT4D versus Neu-PiG's 0.87, and M2V reaches only F-0.5% 0.792 on DT4D versus 0.968. In terms of speed, Neu-PiG's 32 s full run is ~13× faster than PDG (7 min) and ~56× faster than DynoSurf (30 min), while the 8 s variant is ~52× and ~225× faster respectively, placing it on par with the inference times of pretrained models.

## Relation to Prior Work

Neu-PiG is a direct architectural extension of the authors' own Preconditioned Deformation Grids (PDG, CGF 2025), replacing PDG's per-timestep deformation grids with a single shared multi-resolution latent voxel grid decoded by a lightweight MLP that handles temporal modulation via Fourier-encoded timesteps. The Sobolev preconditioning mechanism is inherited from Nicolet et al. (ACM TOG 2021) and adapted from raw deformation fields to learned latent vectors. Conceptually, the paper departs from hash-based encodings (Müller et al.) in favor of smooth trilinear-interpolated voxel grids, arguing that spatial smoothness of the latent carrier is critical for coherent non-rigid motion, and positions itself as a category-agnostic alternative to both parametric templates (SMPL, FLAME) and learned priors (CaDeX, M2V).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 60× speedup over prior training-free methods while simultaneously improving accuracy and scaling to 120-frame sequences (where PDG and DynoSurf degrade or fail) makes dynamic surface reconstruction practically viable for longer captures without category-specific training. The key open limitation is the fixed-topology assumption from the keyframe mesh, which prevents recovery from incorrect initial surfaces and restricts applicability to topology-changing events. Future work could explore adaptive topology, real-time streaming settings, and whether the single-grid formulation generalizes to multi-object or articulated scenes with contact.
