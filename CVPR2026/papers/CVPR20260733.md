## Problem

Existing Mamba-based crack segmentation methods rely on static multi-directional scanning paths to flatten 2D features into 1D sequences, which disrupts spatial continuity for irregular, thin, and fragmented crack patterns. This fixed flattening order weakens the SSM's causal modeling capacity because spatially adjacent crack pixels may become distant in the sequence, limiting the model's ability to capture long-range crack topology.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single RGB image resized to 448×448 and produces a binary pixel-wise crack segmentation mask. During decoding, the original input image is additionally decomposed via a single-level Haar wavelet transform to extract high-frequency structural priors (LH, HL, HH sub-bands) that guide feature refinement. The resulting model is extremely lightweight at 2.95M parameters and 4.69G FLOPs, with a 15MB footprint, enabling real-time deployment.

## Method

CrackSSM introduces a Dynamic Path Scanning Mamba (DPSM) module that adaptively reorders tokens before S6 processing: a coordinate-convolution head on the highest-level feature map produces four-channel directional intensities (horizontal, vertical, two diagonals), supervised by a direction-aware auxiliary loss using oriented Sobel kernels on ground-truth masks. These intensities serve as sort keys to reorder four snake-scanned 1D sequences so that crack-salient tokens remain adjacent, after which standard S6 blocks model dependencies and results are mapped back to 2D. In the decoder, a Wavelet Gating Module (WGM) generates spatial gating weights from Haar high-frequency sub-bands to modulate upsampled features at each stage, while a Global Re-Optimization Module (GRO) computes pixel-wise similarity between decoded features and the aggregated high-frequency map to suppress noise and reinforce edge-aligned details. Training combines BCE + Dice loss for segmentation with a cross-entropy direction loss (α=0.5, β=3), and the full pipeline uses a VSS-based encoder with SE-block channel refinement.

## Results

CrackSSM achieves the best performance across all three benchmark datasets on every reported metric. On Crack500, it reaches ODS 0.726, F1 0.764, and mIoU 0.780; on TUT, ODS 0.826, F1 0.845, and mIoU 0.851; and on DeepCrack, ODS 0.902, F1 0.918, and mIoU 0.909. The model also maintains a very low computational cost of 4.69G FLOPs with only 2.95M parameters (15 MB model size), substantially lighter than most competitors.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CrackSSM outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the closest SSM-based competitor SCSegamba (CVPR 2025), CrackSSM gains +1.2 ODS and +1.8 F1 on Crack500, +2.1 ODS and +2.1 F1 on TUT, and +1.6 ODS and +1.5 F1 on DeepCrack. Compared to DefMamba (CVPR 2025), which also uses dynamic scanning, CrackSSM leads by +1.5 ODS on Crack500, +2.4 ODS on TUT, and +0.7 ODS on DeepCrack, while using far fewer parameters (2.95M vs. 177.44M). Among CNN/Transformer-based methods, the largest margins appear on TUT, where CrackSSM exceeds SimCrack by +2.8 ODS and +2.4 F1, and CTCrackSeg by +3.2 ODS and +2.5 F1. On DeepCrack, the gap over the best non-SSM baseline (CTCrackSeg) is +2.0 ODS and +2.3 F1. In terms of efficiency, CrackSSM requires only 4.69G FLOPs—roughly 4× less than SCSegamba (18.16G) and 10× less than SimCrack (286.62G)—while achieving the highest accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Mamba/VMamba lineage of applying selective state space models (S6) to vision by replacing the fixed multi-directional scanning strategy with a content-adaptive token reordering mechanism. It directly departs from SCSegamba's static diagonal snake-like path, arguing that rigid flattening disrupts spatial continuity for irregular crack geometries, and instead uses directional response intensities from high-level features to dynamically reorder tokens per image. The wavelet-guided decoding (WGM and GRO) introduces a separate line of work—injecting high-frequency structural priors from the input image into the decoder—that is orthogonal to the SSM-based encoder and complements it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for infrastructure inspection: CrackSSM achieves state-of-the-art accuracy at roughly one-quarter the FLOPs of the prior SOTA (SCSegamba), making real-time deployment on edge devices more feasible. The dynamic scanning principle—aligning sequence order with content structure rather than imposing a fixed geometry—could generalize to other irregular-structure segmentation tasks (e.g., road damage, biological filaments) and to other SSM-based vision architectures. Open questions include whether the direction-aware auxiliary supervision (which requires GT masks at training time) can be replaced with self-supervised alternatives, and whether the gains hold on higher-resolution or 3D crack data.
