## Problem

Photorealistic 3D human avatar reconstruction from a single image remains ill-posed due to unseen-view ambiguity, and existing methods rely on slow per-instance diffusion-based hallucination and iterative optimization, limiting scalability. Additionally, the scarcity of 3D human training data (a few thousand instances versus 800K for general objects) makes it difficult to train large feed-forward reconstruction models, and general-purpose LRM architectures lack human-specific structural priors.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and an estimated SMPL mesh (obtained via Humans4D) that provides coarse body pose and surface but no detailed geometry or appearance. The output is a 96×96 triplane representation rendered via ray marching to produce novel-view images at 512×512 resolution, with the entire pipeline completing in under one second without any test-time optimization or fine-tuning.

## Method

HumanNOVA is a feed-forward transformer mapping network (built on PointInfinity) that encodes the input image with DINOv2 and the SMPL mesh with Point Transformer V3 into token sequences, then iteratively refines a shared triplane token set through three cross-attention steps per block: triplane tokens attend to image+mesh tokens, intermediate features attend back to the conditioning, and updated triplane tokens attend to the intermediate features. The key data contribution is a scalable generation pipeline producing 100K assets (~20× existing datasets) via two complementary strategies: animating rigged 3D assets with AMASS poses for pose diversity, and fitting 3D Gaussian Splatting to multi-camera captures (DNA-Rendering, MVHumanNet) to synthesize photorealistic novel views. A larger triplane spatial size (96 vs. the typical 32 in LRMs) is used to capture fine-grained human details, and training employs RGB, mask, and LPIPS losses computed at the patch level with foreground-ratio-weighted sampling to reduce GPU memory while emphasizing fine details.

## Results

On the three standard benchmarks (CustomHuman, THuman2, 2K2K) with frontal-view input, HumanNOVA achieves PSNR of 22.29/23.96/22.65, SSIM of 0.9360/0.9382/0.9336, and LPIPS of 42.42/42.13/41.72, outperforming all baselines across every metric. Compared to the strongest human-specific competitor SiTH, HumanNOVA yields 41.8%, 37.0%, and 43.3% relative LPIPS improvement on the three datasets respectively. On side-view inputs (60° horizontal offset), HumanNOVA maintains its lead with LPIPS of 43.58/42.55/40.95, while most baselines degrade substantially. In geometry evaluation, HumanNOVA achieves a 94.3% relative F-Score gain over SiTH on side-view CustomHuman.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HumanNOVA outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all three datasets, with the largest margins over general-object LRM methods (Real3D, SF3D) and industrial foundation models (Trellis, Hunyuan2), which lack human-specific training data and priors. Among human-specific methods, SiTH (diffusion-based, per-instance optimization) is the closest competitor, yet HumanNOVA still achieves 37–43% relative LPIPS improvement while running in under one second versus SiTH's multi-minute optimization. SiFU and PaMIR, which rely on side-view conditioning or parametric mesh fitting, degrade most severely on side-view inputs (e.g., SiFU drops to LPIPS 93.26 on CustomHuman side-view vs. HumanNOVA's 43.58). The general-object methods SF3D and Hunyuan2 show moderate viewpoint robustness but remain 15–25 LPIPS points behind HumanNOVA due to the absence of human structural priors. In geometry evaluation (Table 6), HumanNOVA achieves the best Chamfer Distance, Normal Consistency, and F-Score across all settings, with a 94.3% relative F-Score gain over SiTH on side-view CustomHuman, confirming superior 3D structural fidelity beyond just appearance.

## Relation to Prior Work

HumanNOVA adapts the Large Reconstruction Model (LRM) paradigm—pioneered for general objects by LRM [20], SF3D [6], and Real3D [25]—to the human domain, using a PointInfinity-style cross-attention mapping network to lift image and mesh tokens into a triplane representation. It departs from the dominant human-specific line of work (SiTH, SiFU, PaMIR) that relies on diffusion-based hallucination of unseen views followed by per-instance optimization, replacing that slow pipeline with a single feed-forward pass. The key enabling contribution is a scalable data generation pipeline (rigged-asset animation + multi-camera 3DGS fitting) that produces 100k training instances, roughly 20× the combined size of existing human datasets, which the authors argue is what makes the LRM approach viable for humans where it previously failed due to data scarcity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By achieving photorealistic 3D human reconstruction in under one second without test-time optimization, HumanNOVA removes the primary scalability bottleneck of current diffusion-based avatar methods, making single-image avatar generation practical for real-time applications like VR, telepresence, and interactive systems. The data generation pipeline is independently valuable: the authors validate its quality by showing that fine-tuning a general-purpose model (Real3D) on the generated data yields large gains, suggesting the pipeline could benefit other human-centric tasks. Open questions remain around back-of-body texture inference for complex garments, handling occlusions in input images, and extending to human-object interactions—limitations the authors acknowledge but leave for future work.
