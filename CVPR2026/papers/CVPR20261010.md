## Problem

Existing Reversible Adversarial Example (RAE) methods rely on a two-stage pipeline: first generating adversarial perturbations via a surrogate model, then embedding those perturbations into the adversarial example for later recovery. This decoupled process inevitably degrades both the visual quality and attack effectiveness of the final RAEs, since the embedding step disrupts the carefully optimized perturbation distribution. The paper asks whether RAEs can be generated in a single end-to-end stage by exploiting the image's own intrinsic frequency information rather than external model-specific cues.

## Input / Output

The network takes a single 224×224 RGB image as input and produces two outputs: a reversible adversarial example (RAE) that causes misclassification by target DNNs, and a restored image recovered by running the same network in reverse. Because the forward and inverse processes share identical parameters, no separate recovery model or stored perturbation is needed. The RAE is constrained to a perturbation budget ε (default 2/255) and is evaluated against VGG19, ResNet50, DenseNet121, AlexNet, and Inception-v3 classifiers.

## Method

RevINN decomposes the input image via Discrete Wavelet Transform into one low-frequency (LL) and three high-frequency (HL, LH, HH) subbands, then applies two invertible coupling modules. The Cross-Frequency Modulation Attack (CFMA) module performs bidirectional affine coupling between the low- and high-frequency components: the high-frequency band is scaled and shifted by functions of the perturbed low-frequency band, while the low-frequency band receives a single additive offset from the high-frequency band. The High-Frequency Perturbation Enhancement (HFPE) module extends this to a tri-branch structure, where each high-frequency subband is modulated by the other two, enabling finer-grained semantic disruption. The modified subbands are recomposed via Inverse Wavelet Transform to yield the RAE, and the exact inverse of both modules recovers the original image. Training minimizes a combined loss of adversarial cross-entropy (pushing RAE classification away from the true label while keeping the restored image correct), a low-frequency wavelet MSE loss that confines perturbation to high-frequency bands, and a reversible MSE loss ensuring near-lossless recovery.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K (1,000 images, 224×224), RevINN achieves the highest untargeted ASR on VGG19 (95.3%) and DenseNet121 (98.3%), and the best targeted ASR across all four evaluated models, reaching 90.2% on DenseNet121 (≥10% above the next best). In terms of visual quality, RevINN RAEs attain a mean PSNR of 46.39 dB, SSIM of 0.992, and LPIPS of 0.018, surpassing the best prior method (RAE-YUV: 41.63 dB / 0.977 / 0.081) by over 6 dB in PSNR. Recovered images achieve PSNR of 58.94 dB and SSIM of 0.998, indicating near-lossless restoration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RevINN outperforms all seven two-stage RAE baselines on targeted ASR across every model, with the largest margin on DenseNet121 (90.2% vs. 78.8% for W-RAE, a 11.4-point gap). On untargeted attacks, RevINN leads on VGG19 (95.3% vs. 95.0% DP-RAE) and DenseNet121 (98.3% vs. 96.2% RAE-YUV), while RAE-YUV edges it slightly on ResNet50 (95.2% vs. 94.4%). In visual quality (Table 2), RevINN dominates all metrics: PSNR 46.39 dB exceeds RAE-YUV (41.63 dB) by 4.76 dB and W-RAE (40.02 dB) by 6.37 dB; LPIPS of 0.018 is roughly 3.5× lower than W-RAE's 0.063. For recoverability, RevINN's 58.94 dB PSNR far surpasses W-RAE (44.70 dB) and SRAE (40.12 dB), approaching the lossless RDH-based methods. On self-supervised models (Table 4), RevINN trades a small ASR drop (1.5–3.1 points) versus RAEncoder on Barlow and BYOL for a large visual-quality gain (PSNR ~49–50 dB vs. 37.23 dB), while actually exceeding RAEncoder on DINO (70.7% vs. 68.4% ASR).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the invertible neural network (INN) lineage—specifically the affine coupling layers from NICE and RealNVP—by repurposing their bijective scaling-and-shifting mechanism to operate on wavelet frequency subbands rather than spatial feature maps. It departs from the dominant two-stage RAE paradigm (generate adversarial example, then reversibly embed the perturbation) by collapsing both stages into a single end-to-end network that scrambles intrinsic frequency information. The CFMA and HFPE modules are novel architectural instantiations of the coupling layer adapted to the 1+3 wavelet decomposition, and the wavelet-domain loss (constraining only the LL subband) is a new design choice not present in prior RAE or frequency-attack literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The one-stage formulation eliminates the quality degradation that plagues two-stage RAE methods, yielding RAEs with 6+ dB higher PSNR and near-lossless recovery (PSNR ≈ 59 dB) while maintaining strong attack success rates across multiple architectures. However, the evaluation is confined to white-box attacks on a single dataset (1,000 ImageNet-1K images), and the authors explicitly leave black-box and transfer-attack scenarios for future work, which limits the practical generality of the claims. The near-lossless (but not truly lossless) recovery, attributed to numerical precision limits in the INN inversion, raises an open question about whether exact reversibility can be guaranteed in practice without sacrificing attack strength.
