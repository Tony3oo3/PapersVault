## Problem

Existing 3D scene generation methods either prioritize aesthetic layouts for computer graphics or attempt physical plausibility but fail to jointly model spatial and physical relationships. Diffusion-based approaches neglect physical constraints (producing floating objects and penetrations), while physics-guided methods sacrifice relational context, yielding stable but layout-incoherent scenes. The core gap is a unified framework that simultaneously enforces mesh-level physical coherence and preserves rich spatial-relational structure during generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes as input a spatial relation graph G_ρ (N×N, m relation types), a physical interaction graph G_κ (N×N, q interaction types), per-object shape descriptors from a pretrained Michelangelo encoder (256 tokens of 64-dim each), and posed scene point clouds sampled at every diffusion step. Each object is parameterized as a 9-dimensional state vector (3D centroid + 6D continuous rotation), and the output is a full scene layout with per-object position and orientation that is physically stable and relationally consistent, validated under Isaac Sim physics.

## Method

SPREAD is a graph-transformer diffusion model that jointly denoises object poses while conditioning on both spatial and physical relation graphs injected as edge embeddings into graph attention blocks. A geometry-aware Perceiver module computes one-way Chamfer distances between noisy mesh point clouds at each step, distilling them into fixed-length tokens that let the network perceive collisions and penetrations in real time. At inference, a multi-guidance framework modifies the score function with three differentiable terms: mesh-level collision avoidance via CoDF on intersecting triangle pairs (found with BVH), gravity guidance penalizing vertical offsets beyond a tolerance, and relation guidance measuring convex-hull overlap of XZ-plane projections for supported object pairs. Training is supervised with standard MSE on predicted noise, and the architecture uses AdaLayerNorm conditioned on timestep embeddings throughout.

## Results

On ProcTHOR, SPREAD achieves the best performance across all physical and relational metrics: GRecall of 0.979, mesh collision rate (Colmesh) of 0.121, Average Support Distance (ASD) of 0.007, Isaac Sim Stability of 0.950, and FID of 18.8. On 3D-FRONT, SPREAD dramatically reduces mesh collisions (0.097/0.185/0.183 for Bedroom/Livingroom/Diningroom) compared to all baselines, while its FID of 64.7 is comparable to InstructScene (61.3) and DiffuScene (61.6). The ablation confirms that the geometry-aware perceiver and multi-guidance framework each contribute incremental gains, with the full pipeline reaching the lowest ASD (0.007) and highest Stability (0.950).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPREAD is compared against three baselines: ATISS (autoregressive transformer), DiffuScene (non-autoregressive diffusion), and InstructScene (graph-based instruction-driven synthesis). On ProcTHOR, SPREAD wins on every reported metric: it reduces Colmesh from 0.174 (ATISS) and 0.360 (DiffuScene) to 0.121, cuts ASD from 0.510 (ATISS) and 0.021 (InstructScene) to 0.007, and raises Isaac Sim Stability from 0.813 (ATISS) and 0.876 (InstructScene) to 0.950. On 3D-FRONT, SPREAD achieves the lowest mesh collision rates across all three room types (e.g., 0.097 vs. 0.275 for ATISS in Bedroom), though its FID of 64.7 is slightly higher than InstructScene (61.3) and DiffuScene (61.6), indicating a modest trade-off in visual diversity. The user study further confirms the advantage, with SPREAD receiving 88.6% of preference votes versus 6.1% for DiffuScene, 4.4% for InstructScene, and 0.9% for ATISS.

## Relation to Prior Work

SPREAD builds on the DDPM diffusion framework for iterative scene synthesis and the Perceiver architecture for distilling high-dimensional geometric features into compact tokens, while drawing on the CoDF metric for mesh-level collision quantification. It departs from prior diffusion-based scene generators (DiffuScene, InstructScene) that model only spatial relations or rely on implicit shape embeddings, by explicitly conditioning on posed scene point clouds via a geometry-aware perceiver and jointly encoding both spatial (G_ρ) and physical (G_κ) relation graphs as differentiable priors. Compared to Physcene, which enforces physical plausibility through bounding-box-level guidance, SPREAD operates at the mesh-triangle level and integrates relational constraints simultaneously, addressing the tradeoff between physical stability and layout coherence that prior guided-diffusion methods could not resolve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that 3D scene generation can produce simulation-ready environments (95% Isaac Sim stability) without post-processing, which is directly relevant to embodied AI training pipelines that require physically coherent, cluttered scenes. However, the method is limited to indoor scenes due to dataset availability, and its 14.72 s inference time is roughly 700× slower than autoregressive baselines like ATISS, limiting real-time or large-scale deployment. Open questions include whether the multi-guidance framework generalizes to outdoor or multi-room settings, whether flow-matching or SE(3)-manifold diffusion can close the speed gap without sacrificing physical fidelity, and whether the dramatic collision reduction (e.g., 0.121 vs. 0.360 Colmesh on ProcTHOR) holds under more diverse object categories and scene scales.
