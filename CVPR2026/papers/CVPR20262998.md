## Problem

The binary, discontinuous nature of spike activations in SNNs causes vanishing gradients under surrogate gradient methods, making gradient-based adversarial attacks unreliable and leading to significant overestimation of SNN robustness. Existing surrogate gradients use fixed sharpness parameters without input-dependent adaptation or theoretical guarantees on gradient vanishing, and standard attack optimizers (PGD, APGD) fail to converge even after hundreds of iterations on robust SNNs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262998/cropped_page3_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a benign input (32×32 RGB image for CIFAR-10/100 or an event stream for CIFAR10-DVS) and a trained SNN (SEWResNet19 or VGG9 with LIF, IF, LIF-2, or PSN neurons), and produces an L∞-bounded adversarial perturbation (ε=8/255) whose effectiveness is measured by attack success rate. The surrogate gradient sharpness α is computed independently for every neuron index i, time step t, and layer l, and evolves online across attack iterations via exponential moving averages of the membrane-potential-to-threshold distance |u_{i,t}^l|.

## Method

ASSG derives a theoretical upper bound on the expected gradient-vanishing degree G(αx) for surrogate functions of the form αk(αx), then adaptively sets the sharpness parameter α_{i,t}^l = ω/(M_{i,t}^l + γD_{i,t}^l) at every spatial-temporal location using online EMA estimates of the mean and absolute deviation of |u_{i,t}^l| along the attack trajectory, keeping the vanishing degree near a tunable bound A while maximizing gradient accuracy. SA-PGD complements this with an Adam-style adaptive step size (L1-normalized momentum divided by square-root of L1-normalized gradient oscillation) that additionally clips each per-dimension update to [−η_k, η_k] before projecting back into the L∞ ball, ensuring stable convergence even under imprecise surrogate gradients. Together, ASSG and SA-PGD form a unified evaluation framework that substantially increases ASR across all tested training schemes, neuron models, and architectures, revealing that prior SNN robustness claims were inflated.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with AT training, ASSG+SA-PGD achieves 88.44% attack success rate (ASR), surpassing the best baseline (HART+APGD at 79.49%) by nearly 9 percentage points. On CIFAR-100 (AT), ASSG+SA-PGD reaches 93.19% ASR versus HART's 90.80%, and on the dynamic CIFAR10-DVS dataset (AT), ASSG+SA-PGD achieves 49.10% ASR compared to STBP's 35.40%—a gap of over 13 points. Across all four adversarial training schemes (AT, RAT, AT+SR, TRADES) and three datasets, ASSG consistently yields the highest ASR, with SA-PGD providing an additional 2–5 point boost over APGD when paired with ASSG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASSG outperforms all five gradient approximation baselines (BPTR, RGA, PDSG, HART, STBP) across every dataset and training configuration. The largest margins appear on CIFAR10-DVS, where ASSG exceeds STBP by 11–13 points (e.g., 49.10% vs. 35.40% under AT), and on CIFAR-10 where ASSG beats HART by 7–9 points. On CIFAR-100, the gap narrows to 2–3 points over HART (93.19% vs. 90.80% under AT), reflecting the already high ASR ceiling. Among attack optimization baselines, SA-PGD dominates PGD and MI-FGSM at all iteration counts, and surpasses APGD by 2–5 points when paired with ASSG; however, SA-PGD offers little benefit over APGD when combined with weaker gradient methods (BPTR, RGA, PDSG), indicating that gradient quality and optimization algorithm must be co-designed. Notably, the paper reports that RGA and PDSG actually underperform basic STBP under 100-iteration APGD, contradicting their original papers which used only 10 PGD iterations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the surrogate gradient lineage initiated by STBP (Wu et al., 2018) and refined by BPTR, RGA, PDSG, and HART, but departs fundamentally by making the surrogate sharpness input-dependent and spatial-temporally adaptive rather than fixed or batch-level. On the optimization side, SA-PGD builds on APGD's learning-rate schedule and Adam's adaptive step-size mechanism, adding per-step L∞-norm clipping tailored to the non-smooth SNN input space. The theoretical treatment of gradient-vanishing degree via the integral G(x) is new; prior surrogate gradient work lacked a formal bound linking sharpness to vanishing, which is what motivates the adaptive design of ASSG.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that current SNN adversarial robustness has been significantly overestimated, and that without ASSG some models (notably PSN) exhibit false robustness—challenges the field's working assumption that 5–10 PGD iterations with training-time surrogates provide a meaningful robustness estimate. This has direct practical consequences: adversarial training pipelines that rely on weak inner maximization are producing models whose robustness is illusory. Open questions include how to design efficient, reliable inner maximization for SNN adversarial training at scale, whether the adaptive-sharpness principle transfers to other non-differentiable architectures, and whether the overestimation gap narrows with stronger (but more expensive) training regimes.
