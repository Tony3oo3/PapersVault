## Problem

Existing GUI agent benchmarks are predominantly English-centric, fragmented across isolated task types (grounding, offline, or online), and collected from heterogeneous platforms, leaving the Chinese mobile ecosystem largely unevaluated. No prior benchmark provides a unified, fine-grained framework that spans the full capability chain from visual perception through planning, reflection, execution, and post-action evaluation in a single diagnostic structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20261519/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Foundation tasks consume a single mobile screenshot with Set-of-Marks (SoM) candidate boxes overlaid and a Chinese instruction, requiring the model to select one of four regions or answer a multiple-choice question; application tasks take a screenshot, a natural-language Chinese instruction, and optionally a short action history, producing iterative click coordinates, action types with parameters, or a binary success/failure judgment. Online agent tasks additionally expose the model to real-device perturbations (pop-ups, ads, permission dialogs, network jitter) and report per-step latency, inference token usage, and total steps alongside task success rate.

## Method

GUI-CEval introduces a two-tier hierarchical structure: a foundation tier of 4,194 multimodal QA tasks that decompose atomic skills into five diagnostic dimensions (perception, planning, reflection, execution, evaluation) using single-answer multiple-choice format, and an application tier of 4,028 agent tasks unifying GUI grounding, offline agent, and online agent scenarios within the same app and page family. Data is collected on real physical devices (smartphones, tablets, foldables) across 201 mainstream Chinese apps, with trajectories generated by both human annotators and strong models (UI-TARS, Qwen2.5-VL) to capture realistic error distributions. A three-stage quality control pipeline—manual cross-checking between independent annotators, automated inspection using both a strong model and a 2B model to catch over-easy items, and a 20% random manual re-evaluation—ensures item soundness and prevents template bias. The benchmark is evaluated on 20 representative MLLMs and multi-agent systems (47 configurations), revealing that perception and grounding are relatively mature while reflection, evaluation, and long-horizon online execution remain critical bottlenecks.

## Results

On GUI-CEval, Qwen2.5-VL-72B achieves the highest overall average of 61.41%, followed by UI-TARS-72B-SFT (56.22%) and Qwen2.5-VL-32B (55.46%). Perception and Grounding scores are relatively mature (peaking at 82.28% and 90.10% respectively), while Reflection (max 21.01%) and Evaluation (max 49.24%) remain severe bottlenecks. For the most challenging Online Agent task, the best success rate is only 33.33% (UI-TARS-72B-SFT), with over half of the 20 evaluated models scoring below 20%. Closed-source GPT-4o (27.69%) and GPT-4o-mini (20.93%) trail open-source models of comparable or smaller scale by a wide margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qwen2.5-VL (Bai et al.) dominates the general-model category: the 72B variant leads in Perception (82.28%), Planning (66.68%), Reflection (21.01%), and overall average (61.41%), outperforming GPT-4o by 33.7 points on average. UI-TARS (Qin et al.) is the strongest GUI-specific family, with UI-TARS-72B-SFT achieving the best Grounding (90.10%), Offline Agent (79.40%), and Online Agent (33.33%) scores, though it trails Qwen2.5-VL-72B on perception and planning dimensions. MIMO-VL (Xiaomi) sits between the two groups (46.63–49.67% avg), with the RL variant improving Planning and Evaluation over SFT but suffering a notable Online Agent drop (17.22% vs. 22.22%). OS-Atlas (Wu et al.) and TongUI (Zhang et al.) are the weakest GUI-specific entries, averaging 28.80% and 25.29–29.99% respectively, with TongUI-7B scoring only 11.39% on Online Agent. Across all five baselines, the consistent pattern is strong grounding (75–90%) paired with very weak reflection (1.68–21.01%), confirming that no current model reliably performs self-correction or post-action evaluation in Chinese mobile environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the fragmented landscape of GUI agent benchmarks—grounding (ScreenSpot, UI-E2I), offline agent (AndroidControl, AITW), and online agent (AndroidWorld, OS-World)—into a single hierarchical framework tailored to the Chinese mobile ecosystem. Prior benchmarks are predominantly English-centric, platform-diverse, and task-narrow, covering at most one or two stages of the perception-to-execution pipeline. GUI-CEval departs from this by introducing a five-dimensional diagnostic structure (perception, planning, reflection, execution, evaluation) that spans all four task types (foundation QA, grounding, offline, and online agent) within a consistent Chinese mobile context, collected on real physical devices rather than simulations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first comprehensive Chinese mobile GUI benchmark, GUI-CEval provides a diagnostic tool that can pinpoint whether failures stem from perception, planning, or reflection—something no prior benchmark enables. The finding that reflection scores plateau at ~21% across all 20 models, and that online success collapses to near-zero beyond 6 steps, identifies concrete bottlenecks for the field. Open questions include whether the Chinese-specific linguistic and UI conventions generalize to other non-English ecosystems, how to train effective self-correction mechanisms given the uniform reflection weakness, and whether the initial-state sensitivity (performance dropping to 0% under non-home-screen starts) can be addressed through multi-path training data.
