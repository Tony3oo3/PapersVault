## Problem

Existing sketch completion methods reduce the task to local gap inpainting or generate from scratch, lacking global structural reasoning and producing coarse, abstract contours. No prior approach can interpret a sparse, pixel-based partial sketch from diverse physical media (paper, whiteboard, tablet) and complete it into a fine-grained, editable vector drawing that preserves the user's drawing intent while adding category-correct detail and coherent stroke topology.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20264065/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a partial raster sketch containing 10%–50% of the total strokes (rendered sequentially from stroke annotations) plus a free-text prompt specifying the object category and optional pose/orientation details. It produces a complete SVG vector drawing as an ordered sequence of B-spline strokes, alongside a pixel-level completed sketch image. The augmented SFSD benchmark includes sketches photographed from printed paper and whiteboard projections to simulate real-world media conditions.

## Method

SketchRevive is a two-stage pipeline that couples a ControlNeXt-SDXL diffusion model (Stage I) with a LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct MLLM (Stage II). Stage I augments the UNet denoiser with a lightweight line-distribution head predicting per-pixel stroke probabilities, supervised by BCE, Dice, and L1 structure losses alongside the standard noise loss to enforce geometric integrity. Stage II pre-vectorizes the Stage-I output into B-spline strokes and fine-tunes the MLLM for structure-aware SVG generation using an instance-level geometric consistency loss (Chamfer distance, arc length, centroid/pose, and curvature) solved via Hungarian matching. The key architectural contribution is a diffusion-prior aggregated encoding module that injects three multi-scale intermediate UNet feature maps into the MLLM's ViT visual stream through hierarchical cross-attention and uses Stage-I line-prediction logits to compress the token sequence to the top-50% most salient regions, preventing semantic drift and structural deformation that plague naive diffusion-to-MLLM cascades.

## Results

On the augmented SFSD benchmark, SketchRevive achieves FID of 4.76 / 4.32 / 4.20 and GS of 2.70 / 2.30 / 1.60 at 10% / 30% / 50% stroke inputs, roughly halving the FID of the strongest baseline (ControlNeXt + Gemini 2.5 Pro: 9.54 / 9.33 / 8.29). At the stroke level, SketchRevive reaches IoU of 0.56 / 0.69 / 0.77 and SRR of 0.48 / 0.59 / 0.63, outperforming all baselines by 7–26 percentage points on IoU and 9–26 percentage points on SRR depending on the input ratio.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SketchRevive outperforms all three baseline families across every metric and every stroke-input ratio. Against the strongest naive cascade (ControlNeXt + Gemini 2.5 Pro), it reduces FID by ~50% (4.76 vs. 9.54 at 10%) and GS by ~35% (2.70 vs. 4.18), while gaining 7 pp on IoU (0.56 vs. 0.49) and 9 pp on SRR (0.48 vs. 0.39). The older sequential methods SketchRNN and SketchKnitter lag further behind: SketchRNN's FID is 3× higher (14.85 vs. 4.76) and its SRR is less than half (0.22 vs. 0.48) at 10% input; SketchKnitter shows inconsistent GS (6.80 at 50% vs. 4.10 at 30%), indicating degraded geometry at higher completion ratios. The gap narrows slightly at 50% input (where more of the sketch is already present), but SketchRevive still leads by 4.09 FID, 1.53 GS, 5 pp IoU, and 4 pp SRR over the best baseline, confirming that the diffusion-prior aggregated encoding and instance-level stroke matching provide consistent gains regardless of how much of the sketch is given.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sketch completion lineage (SketchGAN, SketchHealer) by reframing the task from local pixel-space gap inpainting or stroke-by-stroke vector reconstruction to a fine-grained pixel-to-vector completion problem requiring global structural reasoning. Architecturally, it builds directly on ControlNeXt-SDXL as the Stage-I diffusion backbone, augmenting the UNet decoder with a line-distribution prediction head, and repurposes MLLM-based vectorization (as in SketchAgent, AutoSketch) for Stage-II refinement rather than from-scratch generation. The key departure from prior diffusion+MLLM cascades (e.g., AutoSketch) is the cross-stage diffusion-prior aggregated encoding module, which injects multi-scale UNet intermediate features and line-prediction logits into the MLLM's visual stream via hierarchical cross-attention and logit-guided token compression—a coupling mechanism absent in prior sequential sketch generation or completion work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a practical gap in creative workflows where users produce rough sketches on paper or whiteboards and need them completed into editable vector assets, a scenario largely ignored by prior from-scratch or text-conditioned generation methods. The cross-stage diffusion-to-MLLM feature injection pattern could generalize to other pixel-to-structured-output tasks beyond sketches. However, the evaluation is limited to 19 single-object categories on an augmented SFSD subset, the baselines exclude methods that cannot be trained with ground-truth supervision (SketchAgent, SketchDreamer), and scalability to multi-object scenes, diverse stroke styles, or higher-resolution inputs remains untested. Open questions include whether the token compression ratio and cross-attention depth generalize across object complexity, and whether the approach extends to non-sketch line drawings (e.g., architectural or engineering sketches).
