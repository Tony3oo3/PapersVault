## Problem

In model-heterogeneous federated learning, clients use different representation extractors, making direct parameter aggregation infeasible. Existing alternatives either upload all local representations (high communication cost and vulnerability to inversion attacks) or per-category prototypes (limited intra-class variability leading to overconfident, sharp decision boundaries). The paper asks whether a more privacy-aware and lightweight form of client knowledge can simultaneously achieve competitive accuracy, strong privacy, and low communication overhead.

## Input / Output

Each client processes RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100, 64×64 for TinyImageNet) through a heterogeneous local model (e.g., ResNet, ViT, MobileNetV2) and produces class labels over 10, 100, or 200 categories. The key I/O constraint is that per communication round, each client uploads exactly one entangled representation vector (dimensionality aligned via Average Pooling across architectures) and one entangled-label encoding (a soft C-dimensional vector), rather than per-sample representations or per-category prototypes. The server outputs a single global classifier that is broadcast back to all clients.

## Method

FedRE introduces the entangled representation as a novel form of client knowledge: each client aggregates its local representations (after dimensionality alignment via Average Pooling) into a single cross-category vector using normalized random weights drawn from U(0,1), and applies the same weights to the corresponding one-hot labels to form an entangled-label encoding. The default entanglement mechanism (Random Average Prototype, RAP) first computes per-category prototypes locally, then blends them with the random weights, which empirically outperforms direct representation averaging. The server trains a global classifier on the K uploaded (entangled representation, entangled-label encoding) pairs via cross-entropy loss, where the soft label vectors provide cross-category supervision that encourages smoother decision boundaries. Random weights are resampled every communication round, injecting diversity that prevents the global classifier from overfitting to any single category configuration. This design yields only one uploaded vector per client per round (≈5.12×10³ scalars on CIFAR-100 vs. 257×10³ for prototype methods), while the cross-category blending obscures individual samples against representation inversion attacks.

## Results

FedRE achieves the best average accuracy of 57.79% across all six dataset/setting combinations in the model-heterogeneous FL setting, outperforming all eight baselines and the Local baseline (54.04%). On the most challenging TinyImageNet PAT setting, FedRE reaches 38.52% accuracy, surpassing the second-best method FedTGP (35.64%) by 2.88 percentage points. On CIFAR-100 PAT, FedRE achieves 62.56%, exceeding LG-FedAvg (58.24%) by 4.32 points. FedRE also attains the lowest upload communication overhead (5.12×10³ scalars) compared to all baselines, which range from 257.02×10³ to 9247.08×10³ scalars.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedRE outperforms all eight baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting. The largest margins appear on CIFAR-100 PAT, where FedRE (62.56%) exceeds LG-FedAvg (58.24%) by 4.32 points and FedGH (58.07%) by 4.49 points, and on TinyImageNet PAT, where it surpasses FedTGP (35.64%) by 2.88 points. Among prototype-based methods, FedRE consistently outperforms FedProto (50.28 avg), FPL (50.49 avg), and FedGH (53.35 avg), confirming that cross-category entangled representations are more effective than per-category prototypes for global classifier training. Compared to knowledge-distillation methods FedKD (53.15 avg) and FedGen (53.80 avg), FedRE gains 3.64–4.64 points on average. Against the small-model approach FedMRL (50.26 avg), FedRE leads by 7.53 points on average, with the gap widening to 11.15 points on TinyImageNet PAT. Notably, several baselines (FedProto, FPL, FedMRL) fail to exceed the Local baseline, underscoring the difficulty of the model-heterogeneous setting, while FedRE improves over Local by 3.75 points on average.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within model-heterogeneous federated learning by introducing 'entangled representations' as a novel form of client knowledge, positioned between representation-based methods (e.g., FedAllRep, FedHeNN) that upload all sample-level features and prototype-based methods (e.g., FedProto, FedGH, FedTGP) that upload per-category means. The core idea—aggregating all local representations into a single cross-category vector via normalized random weights, paired with a corresponding entangled-label encoding—repurposes the weighted-averaging intuition from Mixup-style augmentation but applies it to the FL communication channel rather than to training data. Conceptually, it departs from prior work by treating the entangled representation as a privacy-preserving, communication-efficient substitute for both full representations and prototypes, while the per-round resampling of random weights introduces a stochastic regularization effect on the global classifier that has no direct analogue in existing FL aggregation schemes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedRE offers a practical, low-overhead solution for the increasingly realistic scenario where clients deploy architecturally diverse models, and its three-way trade-off (accuracy, privacy, communication) is well-characterized across multiple datasets and heterogeneity settings. However, the convergence analysis is explicitly deferred to future work, and the evaluation is limited to image classification on three standard benchmarks, leaving open whether entangled representations generalize to other modalities or tasks (e.g., segmentation, NLP). The mechanism's reliance on random weights from a uniform distribution, while empirically effective, lacks a theoretical justification for why this particular stochastic process yields smoother decision boundaries, and the privacy guarantees are demonstrated only through inversion-attack experiments rather than formal differential-privacy bounds.
