## Problem

Existing spatial intelligence benchmarks for MLLMs are confined to indoor scenes with 3D mesh ground truth or rely on unreliable monocular depth estimation for outdoor settings, making it impossible to assess whether models truly generalize spatial reasoning to open-world environments. The paper asks whether the performance gains observed on indoor spatial benchmarks reflect genuine visual-spatial perception or merely exploitation of linguistic priors that fail in unstructured outdoor layouts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263080/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes pedestrian-perspective video clips (32-frame inputs for consistency) paired with text spatial questions, where queried objects are disambiguated by unique numerical IDs overlaid as visual tags that track across frames. Outputs are either multiple-choice answers (random baseline 25.0) for relational tasks or numerical values scored by Mean Relative Accuracy for metric and kinematic tasks. A parallel stereo-input version with identical questions is provided to support future research on stereo-based spatial perception.

## Method

OSI-Bench is built from a purpose-collected multi-sensor dataset (stereo RGB cameras, 32-beam LiDAR, IMU/GPS on a manual cart) spanning 200+ outdoor locations, processed through a three-stage pipeline: (1) ORBSLAM3 estimates metric-scale camera poses and LiDAR points are projected into sparse depth maps; (2) a joint-annotation module uses a local MLLM for object identification, detection/segmentation models for pixel masks, CoTracker for temporal tracking, and depth+pose registration to reconstruct per-object 3D trajectories; (3) template-based QA generation with MLLM-assisted human curation yields 8,736 balanced pairs across nine tasks in three tiers (relational, metric, kinematic). Diagnostic ablations include blinding tests (removing visual input) and synthetic abnormal scenes (manipulated object scales) to quantify reliance on linguistic priors, plus a progressive geometric-information reveal experiment that isolates the perceptual bottleneck from algebraic reasoning.

## Results

On OSI-Bench, all evaluated MLLMs perform far below the human-level average of 60.3 MRA. The best closed-source model, Gemini-2.5-Pro, achieves 37.2 average across nine spatial tasks, while the strongest open-source model, Qwen3VL-32B-Instruct, reaches 32.2. The human-model gap is most severe on relational reasoning (relative direction: 83.3 for humans vs. 23–30 for top models) and on dynamic metric tasks (absolute speed: 66.8 for humans vs. 27.8–37.8 for models), where all MLLMs exhibit a near-universal collapse. Blinding tests confirm that enabling vision yields only +2.2 to +6.3 MRA improvement for MLLMs (vs. +22.6 for humans), revealing heavy reliance on linguistic priors rather than grounded visual perception.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Gemini-2.5-Pro leads all closed-source models (37.2 avg), outperforming GPT-5 (29.7), GPT-4o (25.9), Claude-3.7-Sonnet (26.5), and Doubao-Seed-1.6V (27.3), with particular strengths on qualitative ego-motion (52.5) and object localization (37.4). Among open-source models, Qwen3VL-32B-Instruct (32.2) surpasses InternVL3.5-8B (28.5), LLaVA-Video-72B (28.3), and Ovis2-16B (28.1), though it is notably weak on relative direction (28.8, ranking 18th/20) and absolute distance (11.5). A critical finding is that performance gains observed on the indoor VSI-Bench do not transfer: InternVL3.5-38B gains +24.1 MRA over InternVL2-40B on VSI-Bench but actually underperforms its predecessor on OSI-Bench absolute distance, indicating overfitting to indoor regularities. All models—regardless of family or scale—collapse on dynamic metric tasks (absolute displacement and speed), with even the best model (Qwen3VL-32B at 36.8 speed) trailing humans (65.8) by nearly 30 points. The blinding test further shows that GPT-5 gains only +2.2 MRA from vision (and even drops on relative direction and depth counting), while Gemini gains +12.4, confirming that most MLLMs answer spatial questions via linguistic priors rather than visual evidence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in spatial intelligence evaluation by departing from the indoor-centric paradigm that dominates prior benchmarks (VSI-Bench, STI-Bench, SpatialBench, All-Angles-Bench). While those works rely on 3D mesh reconstructions of indoor scenes or monocular depth estimation for outdoor settings, OSI-Bench introduces a purpose-built multi-sensor data collection pipeline (stereo cameras, LiDAR, IMU/GPS) yielding metrically precise ground truth in diverse open-world, pedestrian-perspective environments. It also extends the task hierarchy by adding a kinematic reasoning tier (displacement, speed estimation) that no prior benchmark covers with metric supervision. The construction pipeline repurposes existing tools—ORB-SLAM3 for pose estimation and CoTracker for point tracking—but applies them in a novel integrated pipeline for automatic QA generation at scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a structural gap: spatial intelligence gains observed on indoor benchmarks (e.g., +24 MRA for InternVL3.5 over InternVL2 on VSI-Bench) vanish entirely in open-world settings, suggesting current progress reflects overfitting to indoor regularities rather than generalizable perception. The blinding tests and synthetic abnormal scene experiments provide strong evidence that MLLMs approximate metric quantities via linguistic priors rather than visual perception, a finding with direct implications for deploying these models in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving and robotics. Key open questions include whether explicit 3D geometric representations, multi-view consistency mechanisms, or hybrid symbolic-neural architectures can close the gap, and whether dynamic spatial reasoning requires fundamentally different inductive biases than static perception.
