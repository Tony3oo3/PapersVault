## Problem

3D reconstruction of dual-layered transparent and opaque objects from multi-view images is highly ill-posed because refraction bends light rays, causing severe visual distortions that make pixel-to-surface correspondence ambiguous. Existing methods either require special capture devices, controlled environments, or additional inputs (masks, known IoR, outer geometry), and none can simultaneously reconstruct both the transparent outer shell and the opaque inner object without such priors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20263428/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes only uncontrolled multi-view RGB images of a dual-layered scene (a transparent outer object enclosing an opaque inner object) with no masks, lighting information, known refractive index, or pre-computed outer geometry. It outputs two separate SDF surfaces (transparent outer and opaque inner) along with a spatially-varying IoR field, enabling both geometry extraction and novel view synthesis with physically correct refraction.

## Method

Opti-NeuS employs a two-stage hierarchical strategy that sequentially decouples outer and inner geometry to avoid mutual refraction interference. Stage I reconstructs the transparent outer SDF by suppressing refraction and introducing a transparency-aware alpha-blending threshold derived from the SDF Hessian (curvature), which distinguishes local non-negative minima (transparent) from zero level-sets (opaque) without fixed iso-thresholds. Stage II builds upon the frozen outer surface to optimize the opaque inner SDF, tracing refracted ray paths via Snell's law using an IoRNetwork (a small MLP with positional encoding) that predicts spatially-varying refractive index. The IoRNetwork is regularized by a spatial consistency loss and supervised by a frozen background network, ensuring that only physically correct IoR values produce ray paths matching observed background colors.

## Results

On 7 synthetic dual-layered scenes, Opti-NeuS achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 1.906×10⁻³ and Earth Mover's Distance of 6.140×10⁻³, outperforming Alpha-NeuS (2.314 / 8.404) and NeTO (3.798 / 10.545). Against NU-NeRF on inner-surface reconstruction (Bunny, Spot, Jug), Opti-NeuS reduces CD from 1.981 to 0.304 and EMD from 3.177 to 1.022, demonstrating substantially more accurate inner geometry. The only dataset where Opti-NeuS trails a baseline is Snowglobe, where Alpha-NeuS (with masks) achieves lower CD (5.630 vs 6.038) and EMD (8.210 vs 11.87).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Opti-NeuS outperforms Alpha-NeuS on 6 of 7 synthetic scenes in both CD and EMD, with the largest gains on Monkey (CD 1.542 vs 2.391) and Spherepot (CD 1.103 vs 1.582); the sole exception is Snowglobe, where Alpha-NeuS benefits from mask-based iso-threshold extraction. Against NeTO, Opti-NeuS achieves roughly 2× lower mean CD (1.906 vs 3.798) and 1.7× lower mean EMD (6.140 vs 10.545), attributed to NeTO's reliance on object masks and its insufficient handling of outer-inner refraction interactions. Compared to NU-NeRF on inner-surface reconstruction, Opti-NeuS reduces CD by ~85% (0.304 vs 1.981) and EMD by ~68% (1.022 vs 3.177), showing that explicit refractive ray tracing with a learned IoR network preserves spherical profiles and surface boundaries that NU-NeRF's implicit approach distorts. On real-world scenes (Ballstatue, Realbottle, Ball, Magician-box, Toy-box, Sunglasses), the paper provides only qualitative comparisons, noting that NU-NeRF incorrectly fills non-enclosed surfaces in Magician-box while Opti-NeuS maintains correct topology.

## Relation to Prior Work

Opti-NeuS extends the NeuS framework (SDF + differentiable volume rendering) with two key departures from prior transparent-object reconstruction: (1) a two-stage hierarchical strategy that first extracts the outer transparent surface via a transparency-aware alpha-blending threshold (building on Alpha-NeuS and HF-NeuS's Beer-Lambert opacity reformulation) before training the inner opaque SDF, and (2) a learned IoRNetwork that predicts spatially-varying refractive index to trace bent ray paths via Snell's law. This contrasts with prior methods (NeTO, ReNeuS, NEMTO) that require additional inputs such as masks, lighting, or known IoR, and with controlled-environment approaches (light-field probes, polarized imaging) that limit practical deployment. The paper positions itself as the first method to handle dual-layered transparent-and-opaque scenes without any controlled environment or auxiliary input.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in removing the dependency on special capture hardware or ground-truth IoR/mask inputs, making dual-layered transparent reconstruction accessible in uncontrolled settings. However, limitations remain: the method still degrades on complex multi-refraction scenes (Snowglobe performs worse than Alpha-NeuS), real-world evaluation lacks ground-truth geometry, and the IoRNetwork is a small MLP that may not generalize to strongly spatially-varying materials. Open questions include whether the two-stage decoupling extends to more than two nested layers, how the method handles partially transparent (frosted) materials, and whether the learned IoR field can be regularized to enforce physical constraints beyond the consistency loss.
