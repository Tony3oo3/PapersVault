## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models frequently fail to faithfully render complex prompts involving attribute binding, negation, and compositional relations, yet existing prompt-rewriting methods are either tightly coupled to specific generators or rely on coarse reward signals (e.g., CLIP score) that cannot correct fine-grained prompt-following failures. This leaves a gap: no universal, model-agnostic rewriter trained with explicit, structured feedback on *which* aspects of a prompt the T2I model is likely to misinterpret.

## Input / Output

The system takes a short, often ambiguous user text prompt (e.g., 'a cute Tom') and produces a structured chain-of-thought rewritten prompt that explicitly specifies object identity, attributes, spatial layout, actions, and style. The rewritten prompt is then consumed by a frozen T2I model (e.g., Hunyuan-Image 2.1) to synthesize the final image; the rewriter itself never touches the image, making it a pure text-to-text module that can be plugged in front of any pre-trained generator without architectural changes.

## Method

PromptEnhancer trains a CoT Rewriter (initialized from Hunyuan-7B-Instruct) via a two-stage pipeline. Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning on 485K (user prompt, CoT, rewritten prompt) triplets distilled from Gemini-2.5-Pro, teaching the model to produce structured reasoning that identifies semantic elements and resolves ambiguities. Stage 2 applies Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO): for each prompt the rewriter samples N=8 candidate rewrites, a frozen T2I model generates images, and AlignEvaluator (a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct) scores each image-prompt pair across 24 fine-grained keypoints spanning six categories (linguistic comprehension, visual attributes, action/interaction, relations/structure, world knowledge, scene text). The scalar reward is the mean keypoint score, and GRPO updates the rewriter policy to maximize alignment. The key design choice is replacing holistic similarity scores with a taxonomy-based reward that gives explicit, interpretable feedback on exactly which prompt-following dimensions the generated image satisfies or violates.

## Results

On T2I-CompBench, PromptEnhancer (PE) improves Qwen-Image on 6 of 7 dimensions, with the largest gain in Spatial (0.3222 → 0.4472) and Color (0.7962 → 0.8442), while HY-Image 2.1 sees improvements in Color (0.7415 → 0.8287), Spatial (0.4153 → 0.4351), and Numeracy (0.6772 → 0.7434). On GenEval, PE raises the overall score from 0.84 to 0.86 on Qwen-Image and from 0.80 to 0.82 on HY-Image 2.1. On the internal T2I-Keypoints-Align benchmark, PE improves category-wise prompt-following accuracy by an average of 5.1 points across 24 categories, with the largest gains in Similarity Relation (+17.3), Counterfactual (+17.2), and Counting (+15.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares PromptEnhancer against two baselines: BeautifulPrompt (BP) [2] and GPT-4o [9]. Against BP, PE wins decisively on T2I-CompBench: BP actually degrades performance below the unmodified baseline on most dimensions (e.g., Qwen-Image Spatial drops from 0.3222 to 0.1945 with BP, while PE lifts it to 0.4472), whereas PE improves 6 of 7 dimensions for Qwen-Image and 4 of 7 for HY-Image 2.1. On GenEval, PE also edges out BP (0.86 vs. 0.85 for Qwen-Image; 0.82 vs. 0.81 for HY-Image 2.1), though the margin is smaller. Against GPT-4o, the comparison is qualitative (Figure 5): PE produces rewritten prompts that yield images with stronger semantic fidelity, more accurate attribute binding, and better compositional consistency than GPT-4o's rewrites across diverse examples. The ablation (Table 5) further confirms that the GRPO stage is essential: SFT alone reaches 85–86% while the full SFT+GRPO pipeline achieves 88.15%.

## Relation to Prior Work

PromptEnhancer extends the prompt-rewriting line of work (BeautifulPrompt, iterative LLM refinement, agent-based systems) by decoupling the rewriter from any specific T2I generator and training it with a dedicated fine-grained reward model rather than holistic signals like CLIP score. Methodologically, it combines Chain-of-Thought structured reasoning (Wei et al.) with Group Relative Policy Optimization (Shao et al., DeepSeekMath) in a two-stage pipeline, applying CoT purely at the prompt level rather than embedding it in the diffusion architecture as in MINT or LayerCraft. The key departure from prior CoT-for-generation work is that the reasoning lives entirely in the rewriter, making the module model-agnostic and plug-and-play.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play design means practitioners can improve any frozen T2I model's prompt following without retraining the generator, which is practically valuable as T2I backbones proliferate. The T2I-Keypoints-Align benchmark (6,687 bilingual prompts with 24-dimension annotations) fills a gap in fine-grained compositional evaluation. However, the measured gains are modest (≈5 points average, 0.02 on GenEval overall), several categories show slight regressions, and evaluation is limited to two T2I backbones, leaving open whether the fine-grained reward generalizes to architecturally different generators (e.g., autoregressive or flow-matching models). A key open question is whether the 24-keypoint taxonomy can be extended or whether a learned, open-ended reward would capture failure modes the fixed taxonomy misses.
