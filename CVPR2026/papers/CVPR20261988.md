## Problem

Stochastic human motion prediction methods built on VAEs or diffusion models suffer from two intertwined limitations: single-modal Gaussian priors force multi-modal human behaviors (e.g., walking vs. sitting) into a unimodal latent space, producing physically implausible predictions that conflate distinct actions; and the reliance on variational lower bounds or SDE-based sampling makes exact likelihood intractable, preventing principled uncertainty quantification needed for safety-critical downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The model takes an observed 3D pose sequence of T frames (17 joints on Human3.6M, 22 joints on AMASS, parameterized via exponential maps) and produces M=50 diverse future motion sequences of N frames (100 or 120 frames, i.e., 2 s) along with a per-sample log-likelihood that serves as an uncertainty estimate. The observed sequence is padded with its final frame to full length before encoding, and the skeleton-aware transformer explicitly conditions on the observed history via element-wise addition of temporally aligned embeddings rather than treating prediction as a reconstruction task.

## Method

The framework has two stages. First, an unsupervised EM algorithm jointly learns a K-component Gaussian mixture prior in the latent space and the parameters of a part-aware normalizing-flow backbone (hierarchical body-part invertible transformations), with the E-step computing posterior component assignments and the M-step updating mixture parameters via soft assignment while updating flow parameters via hard assignment to prevent mode collapse. Second, a Rectified Flow velocity field is trained to transport from a conditional Gaussian N(Z₀, I) (centered on the encoded observation) to the ground-truth latent code, using a skeleton-aware transformer that tokenizes per-joint temporal trajectories and applies joint-wise attention along the joint and channel dimensions, plus feature-wise modulation conditioned on the flow-matching time step. Because the entire pipeline is invertible, exact log-likelihoods are computed at inference via the change-of-variables formula combined with Hutchinson's trace estimator for the ODE divergence integral, yielding a tractable and faithful uncertainty measure without variational approximations.

## Results

On Human3.6M, the proposed method achieves the best ADE (0.333), FDE (0.399), CMD (3.015), and FID (0.088) among all baselines, with an 8.5% FDE improvement over the previous best (SLD, 0.436). On AMASS, it attains the best ADE (0.461), FDE (0.474), and CMD (8.579), yielding a 13% FDE gain over SkeletonDiff (0.545). For multi-modal accuracy (MMADE/MMFDE), the method ranks second on both datasets, trailing only CoMusion on AMASS (0.469/0.466 vs. 0.540/0.509). Diversity (APD) is lower than VAE-based methods (e.g., STARS 15.884 on H3.6M), but the paper argues this reflects more semantically grounded predictions rather than spurious variation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method achieves the best single-modal accuracy (ADE and FDE) on both datasets, outperforming the closest competitors SLD (0.348/0.436 on H3.6M) and SkeletonDiff (0.480/0.545 on AMASS) by 4.3%/8.5% on H3.6M FDE and 4.0%/13.0% on AMASS ADE/FDE respectively. On plausibility, it substantially surpasses all baselines in CMD (3.015 vs. next-best CoMusion 3.202 on H3.6M; 8.579 vs. CoMusion 9.636 on AMASS) and FID (0.088 vs. CoMusion 0.102), while VAE-based methods like GSPS and STARS show dramatically worse plausibility (CMD 10.758 and 15.242). For multi-modal accuracy, CoMusion remains the strongest baseline on AMASS (MMADE 0.469, MMFDE 0.466 vs. 0.540/0.509), and SLD leads on H3.6M (MMADE 0.435, MMFDE 0.463 vs. 0.471/0.464), though the gaps are modest. In diversity (APD), the method trails VAE-based approaches (STARS 15.884, GSPS 14.757 on H3.6M; DivSamp 24.724 on AMASS) but the authors attribute this to avoiding semantically incoherent samples. Against the closest flow-based baseline ProbHMI [49], the method improves ADE by 8.5% (0.333 vs. 0.364) and FDE by 19% (0.399 vs. 0.493) on H3.6M, while also providing exact likelihood-based uncertainty rather than approximate quantile estimation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the latent-space formulation of stochastic human motion prediction by replacing the standard single-modal Gaussian prior (used in VAE- and diffusion-based methods like BeLFusion, CoMusion, and SkeletonDiff) with a data-driven Gaussian mixture prior learned via an unsupervised EM algorithm. It builds directly on the part-aware invertible flow architecture from ProbHMI [49] as its latent backbone, and adopts the Rectified Flow [47] linear-interpolation objective to train a neural ODE in place of the SDE-based diffusion process. The key departure from prior flow-based motion work (e.g., MoGlow, MGF) is that the mixture components are learned jointly with the flow model during training rather than pre-defined by clustering or auxiliary labels, and the skeleton-aware transformer with joint-wise attention replaces the generic velocity-field network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two underexplored gaps in stochastic HMP—plausibility of individual predictions and principled uncertainty quantification—by leveraging the full invertibility of the flow architecture to compute exact log-likelihoods rather than variational lower bounds. This makes the output uncertainty measure directly usable for safety-aware downstream tasks (e.g., robot collision avoidance), which prior VAE/diffusion methods cannot provide reliably. Open questions include whether the EM-learned GMM prior generalizes to long-tailed or out-of-distribution motion, whether the history-consistent conditioning is too conservative for scenarios where humans abruptly change behavior, and whether the approach transfers to other flow-based tasks (classification, anomaly detection) where the authors note suboptimal performance.
