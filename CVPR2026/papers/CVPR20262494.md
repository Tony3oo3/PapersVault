## Problem

Most bimanual manipulation research assumes both arms can directly interact with an object without first modifying its configuration. However, many everyday tasks (e.g., opening a capped pen lying on its side, grasping a flat tablet) require one arm to perform preparatory reconfiguration—lifting, rotating, or pushing—before the other arm can execute its goal-directed action. This paper tackles the missing capability of anticipatory, asymmetric inter-arm coordination where the preparatory arm must reason about the other arm's future goal and act proactively to establish favorable object conditions.

## Input / Output

The system takes a partial 3D point cloud of the object (captured from a downward-facing camera) and a natural-language instruction as input. It produces a multi-stage action sequence: an anticipatory affordance map specifying where the primary arm should ultimately interact, a preparatory affordance map for the assistant arm's reconfiguration grasp, a 6D SE(3) object pose transformation, and a final goal-directed gripper action. The pipeline is inherently sequential—the Goal Affordance Network is invoked twice (once anticipatorily, once on the updated scene after preparation), and the assistant arm's actions are explicitly conditioned on the primary arm's predicted future interaction to avoid interference.

## Method

BiPreManip is a four-stage affordance-based pipeline. First, a Goal Affordance Network (PointNet++ encoder + CLIP text encoder + MLP + cVAE) predicts an anticipatory affordance map and 6D gripper action for the primary arm, envisioning the interaction that will be feasible *after* preparation rather than the current state. Second, a Pre-Affordance Network, conditioned on the goal affordance features, predicts the assistant arm's preparatory grasp location and orientation. Third, an Anticipatory Object Pose Predictor and Reorient Actor (both cVAEs) estimate the target object transformation and the 6D motion to achieve it. Fourth, the Goal Affordance Network is re-invoked on the updated scene to produce the final execution action. A key training insight is that the anticipatory stage lacks direct annotations, so ground-truth affordance scores and gripper orientations from the execution stage are mapped back to the anticipatory frame via the known object pose transformation, enforcing geometric consistency between imagined and executed interactions.

## Results

On the simulation benchmark (18 object categories, 882 instances from ShapeNet and PartNet-Mobility), BiPreManip achieves the highest task success rates across all three task types. On Edge-Pushing, it reaches up to 87%/87% (seen/unseen) on Window and 64%/64% on Keyboard, substantially outperforming the best baseline (Heuristic at 58%/62% on Keyboard). On Articulated Manipulation, it achieves 67%/72% on Pen-Button and 45%/56% on Dispenser. In real-world experiments on the ARX-X7s platform, BiPreManip achieves 5–8/10 success rates across Edge, Articulated, and Handover tasks, compared to 0–2/10 for the strongest baseline (3DFA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BiPreManip outperforms all four learning-based baselines across the majority of object categories. W2A (Where2Act), a single-arm affordance method, consistently achieves the lowest success rates (0–16%), confirming that direct goal-directed manipulation without preparatory coordination is insufficient for these tasks. ACT learns temporal action patterns from RGB but produces actions that are not always physically feasible in 3D, yielding moderate but inconsistent results (e.g., 54%/43% on Dispenser but 0%/0% on Pen-Cap). 3DA generates spatially coarse actions and struggles with approach height, resulting in very low success rates (0–27%). 3DFA, the strongest learning-based baseline, is competitive on Plate (71%/68%) and Dispenser (57%/41%) but still trails BiPreManip by 14 and 15 percentage points respectively; on Edge-Pushing tasks it is far behind (e.g., 3%/0% on Bowl vs. 49%/52%). The Heuristic baseline, which has access to ground-truth pose information, is competitive on Plate (81%/78%) and Keyboard (58%/62%) but is outperformed by BiPreManip on most other categories and lacks practical applicability due to its reliance on perfect pose estimates.

## Relation to Prior Work

BiPreManip extends the affordance-based manipulation lineage from Where2Act (single-arm, articulated objects) and DualAfford (dual-gripper, symmetric coordination) to a fundamentally asymmetric setting: one arm must reconfigure an object before the other can act. It departs from prior bimanual manipulation work (ACT, 3DA, 3DFA, VoxAct-B, InterACT), which assumes both arms can directly interact with the object in its current configuration, by introducing an anticipatory affordance that envisions the primary arm's future interaction to guide the assistant arm's preparatory actions. The method composes standard components—PointNet++ encoding, CLIP language conditioning, and cVAE action prediction—into a multi-stage pipeline where the same Goal Affordance Network is invoked both anticipatorily and at execution time, ensuring geometric consistency between imagined and realized interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a common yet underexplored class of everyday manipulation tasks (pushing a flat object to a table edge, lifting a bottle body to expose its cap) that existing bimanual frameworks cannot handle, and provides both a benchmark and a working solution. The anticipatory affordance concept—reasoning about a future interaction to plan present preparatory actions—is a clean abstraction that could generalize to other sequential asymmetric coordination problems. However, real-world validation is thin (10 trials per task, limited object variety), the heuristic baseline with ground-truth pose information remains competitive on several tasks, and the framework's reliance on accurate point cloud input and a single object in view limits immediate practical deployment. Open questions include whether the anticipatory reasoning scales to cluttered multi-object scenes, truly novel object categories beyond the 18 tested, and longer-horizon chains of preparatory actions.
