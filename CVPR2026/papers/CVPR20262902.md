## Problem

Existing image editing benchmarks suffer from limited task scope (almost no coverage of multi-image editing), insufficient fine-grained evaluation dimensions, and heavy reliance on costly manual annotations that hinder scalability. Traditional pixel-level metrics (PSNR, LPIPS) fundamentally fail to assess whether complex semantic edits have been correctly executed, leaving no comprehensive, automated, and human-aligned evaluation framework for the rapidly evolving landscape of instruction-based editing models.

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes a source image (or multiple source images for ME tasks), a text editing instruction, and the model-generated edited image. It produces normalized scores across 30 fine-grained dimensions (15 single-reference, 15 multi-reference), an overall quality score, and human preference win ratios for alignment validation. The 1000 prompts are structured as 100 base scenarios × 5 variants per category, with a deliberate many-to-many mapping between task categories and evaluation dimensions so that a single complex prompt simultaneously probes multiple quality aspects.

## Method

I2I-Bench is a three-component suite: a structured Prompt Suite (1000 prompts across 10 task categories spanning single-image and multi-image editing), a hierarchical Evaluation Dimension & Method Suite (30 dimensions), and a Human Preference Annotation protocol. The key technical contribution is a hybrid 'Specialist-Generalist' evaluation pipeline: specialist tools (Q-Insight, ArtiMuse, DINOv3, Sa2VA, GOT-OCR2.0) provide precision quantitative scores for perceptual quality, feature similarity, and OCR accuracy, while a generalist LMM (Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) handles complex semantic understanding via four distinct VQA protocols (5-level scoring, 2-level binary, multi-question Yes/No, and hybrid LMM-specialist segmentation+similarity). Each dimension is assigned a specific pipeline tailored to its nature, and the entire framework is validated through large-scale pairwise human annotation showing Pearson correlations above 0.8 on most dimensions, with ablation confirming that specialist tools are indispensable where pure LMM evaluation fails catastrophically (e.g., Aesthetic Quality ρ = −0.06 for LMM-only vs. 0.99 for the hybrid pipeline).

## Results

On the single-image editing (SE) benchmark, Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 achieves the highest overall score of 0.813, leading in Instruction-Following (0.930), Blending-Naturalness (0.807), and Object-Manipulation (0.937), while Instruct-Pix2Pix trails at 0.416 overall. On the multi-image editing (ME) benchmark, Nano-Banana leads with an overall score of 0.636, particularly excelling in World-Knowledge (0.721) and Spatial-Accuracy (0.894), whereas all ME models show substantially lower scores than their SE counterparts (e.g., Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 drops from 0.813 SE to 0.604 ME). Physical Plausibility emerges as a universal bottleneck across all models (best SE: 0.562; best ME: 0.433). Human preference alignment validation yields Pearson correlations above 0.90 on 24 of 30 dimensions, confirming the reliability of the automated hybrid evaluation pipeline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the nine SE models, Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 dominates Step1X-Edit (0.813 vs. 0.773 overall) and UniPic-2 (0.813 vs. 0.767), with the largest gaps in Instruction-Following (+0.080 over Step1X-Edit) and Blending-Naturalness (+0.069). In the ME setting, Nano-Banana outperforms Qwen-Image-Edit-2509 (0.636 vs. 0.604), DreamOmni2 (0.562), and Omnigen2 (0.545), with a dramatic advantage in World-Knowledge Reasoning (0.721 vs. 0.044 for Qwen-Image-Edit-2509), revealing that SE-level knowledge retrieval does not transfer to ME-level abstract reasoning. The ablation in Table 3 confirms the hybrid Specialist-Generalist pipeline is essential: on Aesthetic Quality, the pure LMM baseline achieves ρ = −0.0643 with human preferences versus 0.9889 for the hybrid method, and on Subject-Identity the gap is −0.3494 vs. 0.9133. Older models (Instruct-Pix2Pix, GoT) show near-zero Text-Content-Style scores (0.014 and 0.038), indicating text rendering remains a fundamental capability gap. When the same model (Qwen-Image-Edit-2509) is compared across SE and ME, Blending-Naturalness drops from 0.807 to 0.556 and Instruction-Following from 0.930 to 0.744, quantifying the additional difficulty of multi-source composition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in image editing evaluation by proposing a hybrid 'Specialist-Generalist' paradigm that combines domain-specific tools (Q-Insight, ArtiMuse, DINOv3, Sa2VA, GOT-OCR2.0) with a general-purpose LMM (Qwen3-VL) for each of 30 fine-grained dimensions. It departs from prior benchmarks like TedBench (limited scope), EditBench (manual-annotation-dependent), and LMM4Edit (restricted to a few coarse aspects) by systematically covering both single-image and multi-image editing with decoupled, reproducible evaluation pipelines. The multi-image editing evaluation axis is largely absent from all prior work, and the paper explicitly positions its 10-category prompt suite as addressing this gap.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

I2I-Bench provides the first comprehensive, fully automated, and human-validated evaluation suite spanning 13 mainstream editing models across 30 dimensions, making it a likely reference standard for the field. Its key finding—that World-Knowledge/Reasoning scores collapse from ~0.75 (SE) to near-zero (ME) for the same models—reveals that current editing models conflate knowledge retrieval with abstract logical reasoning, a distinction that has practical implications for model design. Open questions include whether the hybrid paradigm generalizes to video editing or 3D content, whether the 30-dimension decomposition is stable as model capabilities evolve, and whether the identified physical-plausibility bottleneck (universally low scores across all models) can be addressed by architectural changes rather than data scaling.
