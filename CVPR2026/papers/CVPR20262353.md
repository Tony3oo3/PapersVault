## Problem

Feed-forward dynamic scene reconstruction remains underexplored: existing generalizable methods like STORM assume constant-velocity motion, lack semantic understanding, and require batched multi-frame processing that precludes real-time streaming. No prior feed-forward model simultaneously achieves accurate non-uniform motion modeling, language-aligned 3D semantics, and constant-latency incremental inference for dynamic scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262353/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

SLARM consumes an RGB video sequence with known camera intrinsics and extrinsics; input images are downsampled by a factor of 8 to 160×240, while LSeg feature extraction uses 320×480 to match resolution. The model outputs a per-pixel, per-frame 4D Gaussian Splatting representation (position, rotation, scale, opacity, color) plus a higher-order motion coefficient vector and a language-aligned semantic feature vector for each Gaussian, all pixel-aligned to enable direct 2D-3D correspondence. In streaming mode, only current and past frames are used (strict causality), with dynamic Gaussians propagated backward to the most recent historical frame to prevent rendering holes.

## Method

SLARM uses a shared-weight ViT backbone with Plücker-coordinate ray injection and an Alternating-Attention Transformer (frame-wise then global self-attention, following VGGT) to produce pixel-aligned 4D Gaussian parameters. The key motion contribution replaces STORM's constant-velocity assumption with a 3rd-order Taylor expansion (velocity, acceleration, jerk), where each order predicts a scalar speed and L2-normalized direction; motion is learned entirely self-supervised by rendering warped Gaussians and minimizing MSE + LPIPS against the target frame, requiring no ground-truth scene flow. Language-aligned semantics are distilled from frozen LSeg features into per-Gaussian vectors via alpha-blended rendering, optionally refined with a CLIP text-embedding cross-entropy loss on labeled data. For streaming, window-based causal attention processes each frame independently while propagating a compact hidden state, yielding constant inference latency and memory regardless of sequence length.

## Results

On the Waymo Open Dataset validation split, SLARM-F (full attention, offline) achieves 27.49 dB PSNR, 0.828 SSIM, and 4.57 m D-RMSE on full images, outperforming the strongest baseline STORM by 1.63 dB PSNR and 0.90 m D-RMSE. On dynamic regions only, the gap widens to 1.48 dB PSNR and 1.34 m D-RMSE over STORM. For semantic segmentation, SLARM reaches 66.63% mIoU and 89.23% accuracy, surpassing the best 2D baseline (Mask2Former-Swin at 55.05% mIoU) by 11.6 points. For 3D scene flow, SLARM-F achieves 0.240 m EPE and 0.540 rad angular error, improving over STORM (0.304 m, 0.667 rad) by 21% in EPE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLARM outperforms all feed-forward reconstruction baselines on the Waymo Open Dataset. Against STORM (the closest dynamic-scene competitor), SLARM-F gains 1.63 dB PSNR, 0.024 SSIM, and 0.90 m D-RMSE on full images, with even larger margins on dynamic-only regions (1.48 dB PSNR, 1.34 m D-RMSE). Against static-scene feed-forward methods LGM and GS-LRM, SLARM leads by 3.9–8.9 dB PSNR and 3.4–4.4 m D-RMSE, confirming that dynamic-aware modeling is critical. In semantic segmentation (Table 2), SLARM's 66.63% mIoU exceeds the strongest 2D baseline Mask2Former-Swin (55.05%) by 11.6 points and LSeg (48.76%) by 17.9 points, attributed to 3D geometric priors augmenting the distilled LSeg features. For scene flow (Table 3), SLARM-F achieves 0.240 m EPE and 0.540 rad angular error, beating STORM (0.304 m, 0.667 rad) and NSFP++ (0.724 m, 1.103 rad); however, the streaming variant SLARM-W (0.337 m EPE) is slightly worse than STORM on EPE while still matching or exceeding it on Acc5 (81.07% vs 79.01%) and Acc10 (84.26% vs 83.74%), reflecting the speed-accuracy trade-off of window attention.

## Relation to Prior Work

SLARM extends STORM's velocity-based dynamic Gaussian reconstruction by replacing the constant-velocity assumption with a 3rd-order Taylor expansion (velocity, acceleration, jerk), enabling self-supervised learning of non-uniform motion purely from rendering losses. It adopts VGGT's Alternating-Attention Transformer as its backbone and distills LSeg's language-aligned 2D features into per-Gaussian semantic descriptors, generalizing Uni3R's static semantic distillation to a 4D temporal setting. The streaming inference design with window-based causal attention departs from both offline batched methods (STORM, VGGT) and prior streaming approaches (Stream3R, StreamVGGT) that reconstruct only per-frame geometry, instead propagating a compact 4D Gaussian state with backward warping of dynamic primitives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying dynamic reconstruction, scene flow, and language-queryable semantics in a single feed-forward pass with constant-latency streaming, SLARM targets a practical deployment gap for autonomous driving and embodied AI where per-scene optimization is infeasible. The finding that semantic supervision (LSeg distillation) measurably improves motion estimation accuracy suggests a useful cross-task coupling between geometry and semantics in 4D reconstruction. Key limitations include the requirement for accurate camera poses and poor handling of specular/transparent materials, leaving self-calibration and physically realistic rendering as open directions.
