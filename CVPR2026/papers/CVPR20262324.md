## Problem

Existing SAM-based medical image segmentation methods depend on expert-defined geometric prompts (points, boxes) or complex clinical text descriptions, limiting their generalizability and practical usability across diverse segmentation tasks. No prior approach achieves high-accuracy segmentation using only simple text queries such as basic anatomical or disease-related labels in both zero-shot and few-shot settings.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262324/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a medical image (resized to 224×224 for BiomedCLIP and 1024×1024 for SAM) and a simple text prompt consisting of a single class name (e.g., 'polyp', 'lung', 'melanoma') as input, and produces a pixel-level binary segmentation mask as output. Unlike competing CLIP-SAM methods that require detailed clinical descriptions generated by language models, Simple-ViLMedSAM operates with minimal textual input, making it directly applicable in clinical workflows where only a target label is available.

## Method

Simple-ViLMedSAM integrates BiomedCLIP and a LoRA-adapted SAM (ViT-H) through two novel modules. The Implicit Pos-Prompter (IPP) first applies a Multi-modal Information Bottleneck (M2IB) to CLIP's image-text features to produce an initial attribution map with implicit positional cues, then refines it via an affinity-based strategy that computes a sparsified cosine-similarity matrix from SAM features to propagate structural context. The Bidirectional Interaction Decoder (BID) then performs two rounds of cross-attention: SAM features attend to the attribution map, and the attribution map attends back to SAM features enriched by the first pass, jointly modeling global semantics and local details. Only 3.9M parameters are trainable (LoRA adapters plus the lightweight IPP and BID modules), and the model is trained with a combined cross-entropy and Dice loss, achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot and few-shot segmentation across four datasets spanning endoscopy, dermoscopy, chest X-ray, and CT.

## Results

In zero-shot segmentation, Simple-ViLMedSAM achieves the best Dice and IoU across all four datasets using only simple text prompts (class names): 59.83/50.45 on Kvasir-SEG, 79.65/70.67 on ISIC, 82.60/74.82 on Chest X-ray, and 93.62/89.25 on Chest CT. The largest margin is on Chest CT, where it outperforms the runner-up (H-SAM) by +7.35 Dice and +10.8 IoU. In few-shot settings, the method again leads on all datasets, reaching 75.48/64.80 (Kvasir-SEG) and 94.13/90.24 (Chest CT), with only 3.9M trainable parameters (LoRA) versus 18.4M for H-SAM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Simple-ViLMedSAM outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset in both zero-shot and few-shot settings. Against the strongest prompt-free SAM variant (H-SAM, 18.4M params), it gains +7.35 Dice on Chest CT (93.62 vs 86.27) and +11.35 Dice on Kvasir-SEG (59.83 vs 48.53), while using only 3.9M trainable parameters. Against text-prompted CLIP-SAM methods (SaLIP, MedCLIP-SAM v2), which rely on complex clinical descriptions, it achieves substantially higher scores (e.g., +25.31 Dice on Kvasir-SEG vs SaLIP) using only class-name prompts. In few-shot evaluation, it surpasses Self-Prompt-SAM by +13.38 Dice on Kvasir-SEG (75.48 vs 62.10) and H-SAM by +2.08 Dice on Chest CT (94.13 vs 92.05). The prompt-type ablation (Table 3) further shows that while complex descriptions boost SaLIP and MedCLIP-SAM v2 by +4.85 and +10.86 Dice respectively, our method gains only +0.71 Dice from complex prompts, confirming its robustness to simple text inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of SAM-based medical segmentation (MedSAM, SAMed, H-SAM) and CLIP-SAM text-driven methods (SaLIP, MedCLIP-SAM v2), but departs from both by eliminating the need for explicit geometric prompts and complex clinical descriptions. It repurposes M2IB (originally designed for classification-oriented CLIP embeddings) to generate pixel-level attribution maps, and borrows ProxyCLIP's masking mechanism for local refinement, wrapping both in a novel bidirectional cross-attention decoder. The LoRA-based SAM adaptation follows the SAMed strategy, positioning the work as a parameter-efficient integration rather than a from-scratch architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that clinicians or downstream users can segment medical images by typing a single anatomical or disease label (e.g., 'polyp', 'lung') without crafting detailed radiological descriptions or placing point/box prompts, lowering the barrier to deploying SAM-based tools in clinical workflows. However, the method is validated only on 2D single-target segmentation with known category labels; the authors explicitly note it has not been tested on 3D volumes, multi-object scenes, open-vocabulary settings, or overlapping organs with weak boundaries. Whether the implicit-positional-cue paradigm generalizes to these more complex clinical scenarios remains an open question.
