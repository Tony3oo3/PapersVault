## Problem

Standard Bayesian deep learning relies on non-informative priors that ignore available domain knowledge, while existing knowledge-augmented methods inject constraints only as static initializations that data-driven gradients can override during training. This paper addresses the gap by showing that domain knowledge must be enforcedforced both before and throughout Bayesian inference to prevent knowledge drift under distribution shift or noisy data.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes standard RGB images (28×28 MNIST variants, 32×32 CIFAR-10, single-view hand images for 3D reconstruction) together with differentiable domain-knowledge constraint functions φ(x,θ)=0 (eature-importance gradients, rotation invariance, biomechanical joint-angle limits). It produces class labels or MANO hand-pose/shape parameters, marginalised over the posterior via deep ensembles, along with decomposed epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty estimates used for OOD detection.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage hybrid posterior p(θ|D,K) ∝ p(D|θ)·p(K|θ,D)·p(θ|K). Stage 1 pre-trains a network for fewer than five epochs on the knowledge loss alone, then applies Laplace approximation around the MAP point to obtain a Gaussian informative prior. Stage 2 performs full Bayesian inference on task data while simultaneously enforcingforcing constraints through an adaptive knowledge likelihood p(K|θ,D) parameterised as an augmented Gaussian N(−α/β, 1/β) over the constraint residual, with α and β updated iteratively following augmented-Lagrangian rules (α via dual gradient ascent, β via multiplicative increase when constraint violation stalls). Algorithm 1 iterates posterior estimationation and hyperparameter updates until the expectedpected constraint violation falls below a threshold, with convergence guarantees to a knowledge-feasible global optimum and a provably tighter PAC-Bayes generalisation bound than any non-informative prior.

## Results

On Decoy MNIST, the full two-stage framework achieves 98.37% accuracy with NLL 0.043 and near-zero knowledge constraint violation (KC=0.00), compared to BANANA's 91.32% / 0.317 / 1.03. On Rotated MNIST, the gap widens dramatically: Ours (Full) reaches 94.68% accuracy and NLL 0.204, while BANANA drops to 51.11% and NLL 1.961, demonstrating that the adaptive likelihood prevents knowledge drift under distribution shift. On the real-knowledge FreiHAND 3D hand reconstruction task, Ours (Full) achieves 7.89 mm joint error and 8.25 mm vertex error with KC=0.02, outperforming the previous SOTA KNOWN-Hand (8.5 mm / 8.9 mm) and reducing angle violation rate from 4.29% (BANANA) to 0.14%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against four Bayesian prior baselines (Gaussian, Laplacian, Logistic, and BANANA) plus five SOTA 3D hand reconstruction methods. On Decoy MNIST, Ours (Full) outperforms BANANA by 7.05 points in accuracy (98.37% vs. 91.32%) and reduces NLL by 86% (0.043 vs. 0.317). The advantage is most dramatic on Rotated MNIST, where BANANA's static prior fails catastrophically (51.11% ACC) while the adaptive likelihood maintains 94.68%—a 43.6-point gap—confirming that prior-only knowledge encoding is insufficient under distribution shift. On FreiHAND, Ours (Full) reduces joint error from 19.83 mm (BANANA) to 7.89 mm and achieves near-perfect constraint satisfaction (KC=0.02 vs. 2.08). Against the SOTA KNOWN-Hand [66], the method improves both EJ (7.9 vs. 8.5 mm) and EV (8.3 vs. 8.9 mm), establishing a new state of the art in weakly supervised 3D hand reconstruction. The likelihood-only variant already surpasses all baselines, but the full two-stage model consistently adds 0.2–0.5 points of accuracy and further reduces KC, validating the complementary role of the learned prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads: Bayesian deep learning with informative priors (exemplified by BANANA, which analytically encodes domain knowledge into a fixed prior) and knowledge-augmented deep learning, which injects constraints as regularizers during training of deterministic networks. The Stage 1 prior-learning step draws directly on McCoy & Griffiths' pretraining-to-prior distillation methodology, while the Stage 2 adaptive likelihood is a probabilistic reformulation of the augmented Lagrangian method from constrained optimization. The key departure from BANANA is that knowledge is no longer a static initialization that data gradients can override; instead, it is actively re-enforced at every iteration through an adaptively tightened likelihood term, yielding a unified posterior that conditions on both data and knowledge simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a general, method-agnostic recipe (compatible with ensembles, SGLD, Bayes-Backprop, VI) for any practitioner who has domain constraints but has been limited to either a one-shot informative prior or a deterministic regularizer. The PAC-Bayes bound tightening theorem gives a formal reason why the learned prior improves generalization, and the convergence theorems for the adaptive likelihood provide guarantees that were absent from prior knowledge-augmented DL. Open questions include how the framework scales to very large models where the iterative ALM loop becomes expensive, how to handle potentially conflicting or noisy knowledge sources, and whether the approach extends naturally to generative models or diffusion-based pipelines where the likelihood structure is more complex.
