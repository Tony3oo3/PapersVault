## Problem

Contrastive learning-based multi-view clustering suffers from two under-addressed issues: the rare-paired problem, where incomplete views leave many samples without valid positive pairs, and the mis-paired problem, where noise-corrupted views form incorrect pairs that mislead contrastive optimization. Existing approaches handle incomplete and noise settings separately, and imputation-based or view-level weighting strategies fail to address fine-grained pair-level reliability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260860/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw multi-view data (e.g., RGB+depth, PHOG+LBP, 12-view protein features, 4-view image features) alongside a binary indicator matrix M marking which views are available per sample, and operates entirely imputation-free on only the available views. It outputs per-sample cluster assignments obtained by applying K-means to the element-wise average of all available view-specific contrastive features (128-dim), with no explicit cluster head or assignment loss during training.

## Method

GLGC is a two-stage imputation-free framework. In stage one, view-specific autoencoders (500→500→2000→512 encoder) are trained with reconstruction loss to extract latent features, followed by an MLP contrastive head producing 128-dim features. In stage two, two contrastive modules operate jointly: (a) Global-Graph Guided Contrastive Learning (GGC) builds a global-view affinity graph over all available samples across all views using cosine similarity, then selects positive pairs (top-pos% row values) and negative pairs (bottom-neg% row values) to generate additional semantically meaningful pairs beyond directly paired samples; (b) Local-Graph Weighted Contrastive Learning (LWC) constructs per-view-pair local affinity graphs within each mini-batch, propagates them through intermediate nodes (W^(u,v)·(W^(v,v))^T) to capture high-order neighborhood structure, and multiplies the positive-pair logits by the corresponding graph weight to adaptively strengthen reliable pairs while suppressing noisy ones. The total objective is L_rec + α·L_ggc + β·L_lwc, optimized end-to-end with Adam.

## Results

GLGC achieves the best clustering accuracy (ACC) and normalized mutual information (NMI) across all four datasets (DHA, LandUse-21, ProteinFold, ALOI) under incomplete, noise, and combined incomplete+noise settings. On ALOI with 10% missing views, GLGC reaches 89.2% ACC and 93.8% NMI, surpassing the strongest baseline FreeCSL (87.1% ACC, 93.0% NMI). Under severe incompleteness (100% missing rate), GLGC retains 82.8% ACC on ALOI versus 48.1% for FreeCSL, a 34.7% absolute improvement. In the noise setting on ProteinFold, GLGC's ACC degrades only 2.3% from noise rate 0.1 to 1.0, while the second-best method drops 9.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GLGC outperforms all seven baselines (DSIMVC, CPSPAN, RPCIC, SCGL, DCG, GHICMC, FreeCSL) on every dataset and every missing/noise rate tested. The closest competitor is FreeCSL [6], the most recent imputation-free contrastive method; on ALOI at 10% missing, GLGC leads by 2.1% ACC (89.2 vs 87.1), but at 100% missing the gap widens dramatically to 34.7% (82.8 vs 48.1). CPSPAN [14] and RPCIC [54] degrade sharply under high missing rates (e.g., RPCIC drops to 17.0% ACC on DHA at rate 1.0), while the diffusion-based DCG [55] collapses to 8.9% ACC on ALOI at rate 1.0. In the noise setting, GLGC's local-graph weighting (LWC) provides the most robust performance: on ProteinFold, its ACC declines only 2.3% from noise rate 0.1 to 1.0, whereas the second-best method (SCGL) drops 9.1%. DSIMVC [33] and GHICMC [2] are competitive at low missing rates but fall behind GLGC as data corruption increases, confirming that the global-graph pair construction and adaptive pair-wise weighting are essential for robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLGC positions itself at the intersection of imputation-free incomplete multi-view clustering (following FreeCSL, Xu et al.) and noise-robust MVC (following view-weighting strategies like Wang et al. and Xu et al.), but departs from both by operating at the sample-pair level rather than the view level. It repurposes standard contrastive learning (InfoNCE-style) and graph learning (affinity graphs, graph propagation) into two complementary modules: a global graph that constructs additional positive pairs to address the rare-paired issue, and a local graph that assigns pair-wise weights to address the mis-paired issue. Unlike imputation-based methods (COMPLETER, DCG, CPSPAN), GLGC avoids recovering missing data entirely, and unlike prior view-grained weighting approaches, it distinguishes fine-grained unreliable pairs rather than downweighting entire views.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified treatment of incomplete and noisy multi-view data in a single imputation-free framework is practically valuable, as real-world multi-view datasets often suffer from both issues simultaneously. The pair-wise weighting mechanism offers a more granular alternative to view-level weighting, which could inform future noise-robust representation learning beyond MVC. However, the evaluation is limited to four relatively small datasets (largest: 10,800 samples), the noise setting models feature corruption rather than truly noisy correspondence, and the final clustering step relies on K-means rather than end-to-end optimization, leaving open questions about scalability and whether the graph construction overhead becomes prohibitive at larger scales.
