## Problem

Reconstructing the complete 3D shape of a fully occluded object from mmWave signals remains unsolved because mmWave reflections are specular (mirror-like), low-resolution, and noisy, causing most of an object's surface to be invisible to the sensor. Existing vision-based shape completion models assume diffuse, wide-coverage partials and fail on mmWave inputs, while prior mmWave methods only recover the small radar-facing portion of a surface.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20260504/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw complex-valued time-domain mmWave measurements H ∈ C^{N×T} from N known sensor positions P ∈ R^{N×3} and produces a single complete 3D point cloud of the hidden object. During inference, the raw signals are first converted into multiple candidate partial surfaces via a scalar potential function, each is independently completed by the network, and a final entropy-guided selection picks one reconstruction. Training is performed entirely on synthetic 3D point clouds (OmniObject3D, Toys4K-3D, Objaverse subset of Objaverse-XL, >25K objects) with physics-consistent partial generation, while evaluation uses real mmWave data from the MITO dataset (61 YCB objects in line-of-sight and fully-occluded settings).

## Method

Wave-Former is a transformer-based encoder-decoder (built on PoinTr) trained with a physics-aware pipeline that embeds mmWave physical properties directly into the learning process. Three key techniques differentiate it from vanilla vision-based completion: (1) a specularity-aware inductive bias that generates training partials by keeping only points producing specular returns on radar-facing surfaces, (2) reflection-dependent visibility that further attenuates points based on angular and material constraints to model anisotropic mmWave coverage, and (3) a joint refinement-and-completion loss (bidirectional Chamfer distance) that allows the network to simultaneously denoise and complete rather than concatenating noisy input. At inference, a three-stage pipeline first proposes multiple candidate partial surfaces from a scalar potential field, applies the completion model to each, and selects the final reconstruction via an entropy-guided criterion based on local covariance eigenvalue ratios (λ₃/λ₁) that favors smooth, planar surfaces over noisy, dispersed point clouds.

## Results

On the MITO dataset (61 diverse YCB objects, fully occluded), Wave-Former achieves a Chamfer Distance of 0.069, F-Score of 75%, Precision of 85%, and Recall of 72%. This represents an 18-point recall improvement over the best mmWave baseline (R-Map Finetuned at 54%) while maintaining high precision. Against the best vision-based completion model (mmNorm+SeedFormer, CD 0.095, Recall 59%), Wave-Former improves CD by 27% and recall by 13 points. The method is trained entirely on synthetic data (OmniObject3D, Toys4K-3D, Objaverse) yet generalizes to real-world mmWave measurements.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Wave-Former outperforms all four mmWave reconstruction baselines on CD, F-Score, and Recall. Against Backprojection [14], it reduces CD from 0.180 to 0.069 (62% reduction) and nearly doubles recall (72% vs 45%). Against mmNorm [16], Wave-Former achieves substantially better recall (72% vs 34%) and CD (0.069 vs 0.214), though mmNorm retains a slight precision edge (89% vs 85%) due to its conservative surface estimation. Against R-Map [46] and its finetuned variant, Wave-Former dominates across all metrics, with the finetuned R-Map being the closest competitor (CD 0.330, Recall 54%). In the vision-based comparison (Table 2), Wave-Former beats all four vanilla completion models (PoinTr, SnowFlakeNet, SeedFormer, PCN) applied to mmNorm partials, achieving the best CD (0.069 vs 0.095–0.138), highest recall (72% vs 53–60%), and highest precision (85% vs 70–83%), confirming that physics-aware training is essential for mmWave shape completion.

## Relation to Prior Work

Wave-Former builds on the PoinTr transformer backbone for point cloud completion and on mmNorm's scalar potential function for generating geometrically consistent candidate surfaces from raw mmWave reflections. It departs fundamentally from both prior mmWave reconstruction methods (Backprojection, mmNorm, RMap), which can only recover radar-facing surfaces due to specular reflection, and from vision-based shape completion models (PoinTr, SnowFlakeNet, SeedFormer, PCN), which assume diffuse, high-coverage partials incompatible with mmWave physics. The key conceptual departure is a physics-aware training pipeline that embeds specularity, anisotropic visibility, and noise characteristics directly into the learning objective, enabling training on purely synthetic 3D data while generalizing to real mmWave signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work opens a practical path toward through-occlusion 3D perception for robotics, augmented reality, and logistics, where optical sensors are inherently limited. The physics-aware training paradigm—embedding domain-specific signal physics into synthetic-data training to achieve real-world generalization—could transfer to other wireless sensing tasks (classification, segmentation, pose estimation) where real data is scarce. Limitations include single-object focus, dependence on known sensor positions, and degraded performance on small objects (<10 cm) and extreme coverage (<18%); scaling to multi-object scenes or dynamic environments remains open.
