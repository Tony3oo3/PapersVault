## Problem

Existing adversarial protection methods against unauthorized diffusion-based image editing suffer from two critical limitations: they only perturb the denoising direction at individual (local) steps, leaving generated images still recognizable in semantics, and they rely on model-specific backpropagation gradients, severely restricting transferability across different diffusion models and downstream tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/CVPR20262480/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a clean RGB image (center-cropped and resized to 512×512) and produces a single protected image with an imperceptible L∞-bounded perturbation (default budget ξ = 16/255). The perturbation is optimized once using Stable Diffusion v1.4 as the surrogate model, then applied as-is to a wide range of target models (SD v1.5, v2.1, InstructPix2Pix, inpainting, super-resolution, SVD, Zero123++) without re-optimization, making the output a universally transferable protected image.

## Method

UniDef introduces two key components. First, the Consistent Distribution Deviation (CDD) strategy redefines the protection objective as maximizing the KL divergence between the output distribution of the protected image and the clean image, operationalized as a continuous-time integral of the noise-prediction error over the entire denoising trajectory rather than at a single step, thereby disrupting global image semantics. Second, the Finite Difference-based Jacobian Estimation (FDJE) module replaces model-specific backpropagation with a symmetric finite-difference approximation of the Jacobian-vector product, using the AutoEncoder latent code z (which follows N(0,I)) as the probing direction instead of random vectors, yielding a model-agnostic gradient estimate. The perturbation is then optimized via projected gradient ascent in image space under an L∞ constraint, producing a single imperceptible perturbation that generalizes across diverse diffusion backbones and downstream tasks.

## Results

On the task-level generalization benchmark (Table 2), UniDef achieves the lowest CLIP similarity and highest FID across all five downstream tasks (InstructPix2Pix, Inpainting, Super Resolution, Image-to-Video, Image-to-3D), confirming universal protection. For example, on InstructPix2Pix, UniDef reaches CLIP 0.8086 and FID 239.88, compared to the best baselines ACE (CLIP 0.8138) and AdvPaint (FID 195.01). On Image-to-3D, UniDef attains CLIP 0.7378 and FID 245.17, substantially outperforming the next-best Mist (CLIP 0.7445) and ACE (FID 188.41). The FID gap is particularly pronounced on Super Resolution (277.11 vs. 202.37 for Mist) and Inpainting (251.61 vs. 240.07 for AdvPaint).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UniDef outperforms all five baselines (AdvDM, Mist, SDS, AdvPaint, ACE) on every metric across all five downstream tasks. The FID advantage is the most striking: UniDef exceeds the best baseline by 44.87 points on InstructPix2Pix (239.88 vs. AdvPaint's 195.01), 74.74 points on Super Resolution (277.11 vs. Mist's 202.37), and 56.76 points on Image-to-3D (245.17 vs. ACE's 188.41). On CLIP similarity, the margins are smaller but consistent, with UniDef achieving the lowest value in every task (e.g., 0.8086 vs. ACE's 0.8138 on IP2P; 0.7378 vs. Mist's 0.7445 on I23D). AdvPaint is the closest competitor on Inpainting FID (240.07 vs. 251.61), while SDS leads among baselines on Image-to-Video FID (94.45) yet is still surpassed by UniDef (107.04). Overall, the consistent dominance across both metrics and all tasks validates the paper's claim of universal, model-agnostic protection.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of adversarial defense against diffusion-based image editing (AdvDM, Mist, SDS, AdvPaint, ACE) by shifting the optimization target from local, single-step denoising perturbation to a global distributional deviation across the entire denoising trajectory. The key conceptual departure is the observation that diverse diffusion model variants, despite following distinct local denoising paths, converge to a consistent global distribution objective — a property the authors formalize via a KL-divergence-based loss integrated over time. To make the resulting gradient model-agnostic, the paper repurposes the Hutchinson trace estimator and symmetric finite differences (borrowed from neural ODE and influence-function literature) to approximate the Jacobian without backpropagation through a specific architecture, thereby decoupling perturbation optimization from any particular model's parameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The universal transferability demonstrated here — perturbations optimized on SD v1.4 effectively disrupting SD v1.5, v2.1, InstructPix2Pix, inpainting, super-resolution, image-to-video, and image-to-3D — addresses a practical gap that prior defenses could not fill, making it potentially useful for real-world image protection pipelines. However, the evaluation is limited to 100 images per dataset and a single GPU, and the paper does not test against adaptive attacks where the attacker re-optimizes with knowledge of the defense. Open questions include whether the finite-difference approximation degrades for very large or multi-step latent spaces, whether the approach generalizes to newer architectures (e.g., DiT-based models), and how the defense interacts with iterative or multi-round editing workflows.
