## Problem

Multi-image super-resolution (MISR) can avoid the hallucination problem of single-image SR by exploiting sub-pixel shifts across multiple low-resolution frames, but existing test-time optimization methods either require a separate pre-alignment step or, in the case of INR-based burst fusion (NIR), lack accurate sub-pixel alignment. The paper addresses how to leverage the continuous nature of implicit neural representations to jointly solve frame alignment and high-resolution reconstruction without any high-resolution training data.

## Input / Output

The method takes T low-resolution RGB frames (16 for satellite, 14 for ground-level) captured with sub-pixel shifts, provided as discretized pixel values on a grid W. It produces a single high-resolution RGB image sampled on a finer grid V, with upsampling factors of ×2, ×4, or ×8. The base frame is fixed as the reference coordinate system, and all other frames are aligned relative to it via learned affine transformations (two translations and one rotation per frame). The INR operates in normalized continuous coordinates [0,1)³, and the output is a continuous field that can be evaluated at any resolution.

## Method

SuperF shares a single coordinate-based MLP (4-layer ReLU network with 256-dim Fourier feature positional encoding) across all LR frames, jointly optimizing the INR weights θ and per-frame affine transformation parameters Â⁽ᵗ⁾ (directly parameterized as Δx, Δy, α rather than estimated by a separate MLP as in NIR). A key supersampling strategy runs the INR at the full high-resolution grid, then applies a boxcar filter (average pooling) to downsample the output to match the LR targets, which improves sub-pixel alignment accuracy. The loss is a per-pixel reconstruction error (MSE or Gaussian negative log-likelihood for heteroscedastic noise) averaged across frames, optimized with AdamW over 2000 iterations. The GNLL variant adds T×n_c uncertainty outputs per pixel, allowing the model to upweight variance on noisy or occluded pixels rather than forcing reconstruction. No high-resolution training data is required; the entire optimization is performed at test time on the given LR burst.

## Results

On SatSynthBurst (satellite imagery), SuperF with GNLL loss achieves 37.26 / 34.03 / 29.28 PSNR at ×2 / ×4 / ×8 upsampling, outperforming bilinear upsampling (34.69 / 29.71 / 26.62) by 2.6–3.3 dB. On SyntheticBurst (ground-level handheld bursts), SuperF MSE reaches 29.38 / 27.90 / 27.08 PSNR at ×2 / ×4 / ×8, exceeding bilinear (27.66 / 26.12 / 25.44) by 1.7–1.9 dB. The GNLL loss consistently improves over MSE on the satellite dataset (e.g., +1.09 dB at ×4) but performs on par with MSE on ground-level bursts. All experiments use 16 LR frames and 2000 optimization iterations on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SuperF decisively outperforms both TTO baselines on PSNR across all upsampling factors and both domains. Against Lafenetre et al. (2023), a steerable kernel regression method, SuperF gains 3.2–4.3 dB PSNR on SatSynthBurst and 1.4–2.4 dB on SyntheticBurst; notably, Lafenetre's method actually falls below bilinear upsampling on PSNR for most settings, though it does achieve better LPIPS than bilinear at ×4 on SyntheticBurst. Against NIR (Nam et al., 2022), an INR-based burst fusion method adapted for MISR, SuperF gains 8.3–9.4 dB PSNR on SatSynthBurst and 4.2–5.6 dB on SyntheticBurst; NIR performs worse than even bilinear upsampling on PSNR in nearly all configurations, and the authors observed that NIR sometimes collapses to a constant output during optimization on satellite scenes. The ablation in Table 3 attributes most of the gain over NIR to direct parameterization of affine transformations (vs. a separate MLP), followed by the supersampling strategy and fixing the base frame.

## Relation to Prior Work

SuperF sits at the intersection of two research threads: multi-image super-resolution (MISR) via test-time optimization (Wronski et al. 2019; Lafenetre et al. 2023) and implicit neural representations (INRs) for image fusion (Nam et al. 2022). It inverts the guided super-resolution formulation of De Lutio et al. (2019) by treating LR frames as optimization targets rather than inputs, and builds directly on the NIR burst-fusion INR of Nam et al. (2022) while replacing its MLP-estimated homography with directly parameterized affine transforms and adding a supersampling strategy for sub-pixel alignment. The approach also draws on Fourier feature positional encodings (Tancik et al. 2020) to counteract spectral bias in coordinate-based MLPs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free TTO method, SuperF removes the need for expensive paired HR/LR training data, making it immediately applicable to new domains (demonstrated on both satellite and ground-level imagery) and reducing hallucination risk relative to supervised SISR. The introduction of SatSynthBurst, a synthetic satellite burst dataset with a spatially aligned base frame, fills a gap in MISR evaluation resources. Key open questions include handling scene changes and occlusions over longer temporal baselines (e.g., Sentinel-2 revisit periods), reducing the sensitivity to the Fourier feature scale hyperparameter, and whether learned INR initializations (Tancik et al. 2021) can cut the 2000-iteration optimization cost for real-time or mobile deployment.
