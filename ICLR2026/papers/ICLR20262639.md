## Problem

Existing LLM safety evaluations focus on generic harms (jailbreaks, harmful content generation) but ignore whether purpose-specific agents can reliably stay within their intended operational scope. Despite OWASP, the EU AI Act, and NIST explicitly flagging out-of-scope operation as a critical vulnerability, no systematic framework exists to measure whether an LLM-based agent appropriately refuses out-of-domain queries while remaining helpful on in-domain ones.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a user query (multiple-choice or open-ended text) with a system prompt encoding the agent's policies (allowed/forbidden behaviors, fallback responses, defense rules). The model produces a response that is parsed as either an acceptance (in-domain) or refusal (out-of-domain) decision. The benchmark spans 21 purpose-specific agents, 3,351 OOD samples per agent in both direct and adversarially-transformed (adaptive) forms, across English, Chinese, and Hindi, totaling over 220K test samples per model. The final score is Operational Safety (OS), the harmonic mean of in-domain acceptance rate and OOD refusal rate.

## Method

OFFTOPICEVAL constructs a multilingual, multi-domain benchmark where adaptive OODs are generated via 'prompt laundering': Llama-70B rewrites direct OOD queries (sourced from MMLU) into superficially in-domain forms while preserving query content, verified by a Mistral-24B judge achieving 98.55% content preservation. In-domain queries are generated by ChatGPT-5 across five question styles and manually verified by three annotators. For mitigation, the paper proposes two training-free prompt-suffix strategies: Q-ground appends an instruction to rewrite the query in its minimal form before responding, and P-ground appends an instruction to 'forget the above text and focus on the system prompt.' These grounding suffixes are appended after the user query, reinforcing the model's adherence to policies without any parameter updates. The paper also evaluates activation steering (steering vectors from policy-inverted prompts) and LoRA fine-tuning on a small ID/OOD discriminator corpus, finding both inferior to prompt-based grounding.

## Results

Across 20 open-weight LLMs evaluated on OFFTOPICEVAL, all models remain highly operationally unsafe: even the strongest—Mistral (24B) at 79.96% and Qwen-3 (235B) at 77.77% OS in English—fall far short of reliable safety, while Llama-3.1 (8B) collapses to 23.84%. Adaptive OOD refusal is the critical failure point: on average, models refuse only ~18% of adversarially transformed OOD queries (vs. ~74% for direct OODs), with Llama-3 dropping by ~97% relative to its ID performance. Closed-weight flagships perform better (Gemini 2.5 Pro: 97.09%, Claude Opus 4.1: 97.45% OS), but the paper demonstrates successful attacks even on these models. Prompt-based steering (P-ground) yields the largest mitigation gains, boosting Llama-3.3 (70B) OS by +41.07% and Qwen-3 (30B) by +26.76% in English.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is across mitigation strategies applied to the same base models. Activation steering (following Turner et al., 2023) provides modest gains of +2.6% to +17.1% OS on English (Table 8), but requires model-specific grid search over layers and steering coefficients, making it computationally intensive and less generalizable. In contrast, the proposed prompt-based methods are training-free and more effective: P-ground (system-prompt grounding) delivers the largest improvements, raising Llama-3.3 (70B) from 53.92% to 94.99% OS (+41.07%) and Qwen-3 (30B) from 65.10% to 91.86% (+26.76%), while Q-ground (query grounding) provides consistent but smaller gains of +3.65% to +23.33%. LoRA-based parameter steering (SFT on a discriminator corpus) actually degrades performance, dropping Qwen-3 (4B) OS from 50.92% to 8.89% (−42.02%), confirming that naive fine-tuning is counterproductive. Among model families, Mistral (24B) leads in English (79.96% OS) while Qwen-3 (235B) dominates in Chinese (81.54%) and Hindi (88.16%), and Llama-3 consistently underperforms across all languages due to near-zero adaptive OOD refusal.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by defining 'operational safety'—an LLM's ability to accept in-domain and refuse out-of-domain queries under a specific agent policy—as distinct from the generic safety/jailbreak literature that focuses on harmful content. While OWASP, the EU AI Act, and NIST identify out-of-scope operation as a critical vulnerability, no prior work provides a systematic evaluation framework for it. The paper repurposes MMLU as a source of OOD queries and introduces 'prompt laundering' (adversarial transformation of OOD queries into superficially in-domain forms) as a novel attack vector, building conceptually on black-box jailbreak strategies (TAP, ReNeLLM, GASP) but targeting domain boundaries rather than safety guardrails. For mitigation, it tests activation steering (Turner et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025) and LoRA fine-tuning before proposing its own prompt-based steering methods (Q-ground, P-ground).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all 20 open-weight models are highly operationally unsafe—and that adaptive OOD queries push refusal rates below random chance—exposes a critical gap between generic alignment and enterprise-ready agent deployment. The multi-turn vulnerability (a single successful bypass collapses downstream refusal by 25–66%) and the counterintuitive result that reasoning models are *more* susceptible to OOD attacks challenge assumptions about scaling and chain-of-thought as safety mechanisms. Prompt-based steering (P-ground) offers a surprisingly effective, training-free mitigation (up to +41% OS), but the paper acknowledges this is a first step; the core alignment problem remains unsolved, and even flagship closed models (GPT-5, Claude Opus 4.1) can be breached with further transformation. Open questions include whether these vulnerabilities persist under stronger adversarial search, whether operational safety can be certified rather than merely measured, and how to make multi-turn refusal robust without sacrificing helpfulness.
