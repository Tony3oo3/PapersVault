## Problem

Existing tuning-free MLLM-based video anomaly detection methods passively read pre-trained features without adapting internal representations, leaving them vulnerable to two structural deficiencies: inherent representational bias (reduced sensitivity to rare anomalous patterns due to web-scale pre-training) and contextual ambiguity (visually similar but semantically distinct events producing entangled latent representations). No prior work has actively steered or rectified the internal geometric structure of frozen MLLMs to resolve these issues for VAD.

## Input / Output

The method consumes untrimmed video streams partitioned into non-overlapping 48-frame segments, from which 16 frames are uniformly sampled per segment and fed through a single forward pass of the frozen InternVL3 backbone. It produces per-segment anomaly probabilities that are mapped to frame-level scores, temporally smoothed via 1D Gaussian convolution (σ_g=6) to yield a continuous anomaly curve, and optionally generates post-hoc textual explanations by re-submitting flagged frames to the MLLM. Calibration requires only 1% of the training set (~16 videos) for LAE discovery and HMC training.

## Method

SteerVAD reframes VAD as active geometric intervention on latent representation manifolds within a frozen MLLM. A gradient-free Representational Separability Analysis (RSA) computes an Inter-to-Intra Scatter Ratio across all attention heads to identify the top-K=4 heads whose feature spaces exhibit maximal geometric separability between normal and anomalous events (termed Latent Anomaly Experts). A Hierarchical Meta-Controller then generates context-dependent rectification signals: a Global Scrutiny Gate (small MLP) produces a scalar suspicion score acting as a master switch, while K parallel low-rank adapters (rank r=4) produce per-LAE steering vectors in [-1,1]. These signals drive anisotropic manifold scaling via the element-wise operation h'_i = h_i ⊙ (1 + s_global · g_i), amplifying anomaly-relevant dimensions while suppressing pre-training biases. The framework is trained with binary cross-entropy plus an L2 sparsity penalty on the global signal for normal samples, requiring only ~1 minute of training on a single GPU.

## Results

SteerVAD achieves 87.15% AUC on UCF-Crime and 83.02% AP on XD-Violence, establishing state-of-the-art performance among tuning-free video anomaly detection methods. It requires only 1% of the training data (~16 videos) for calibration, with training time under 1 minute on a single RTX A6000 GPU. The method narrows the gap with fully fine-tuned MLLM-based approaches (Holmes-VAD: 89.51% AUC, 90.67% AP) to within 2.4% AUC and 7.7% AP, while operating with a frozen backbone and minimal trainable parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among tuning-free few-parameter learning methods, SteerVAD outperforms HiProbeVAD by 0.43% AUC (87.15 vs 86.72) and 0.87% AP (83.02 vs 82.15), and surpasses VERA by 0.60% AUC and a substantial 12.48% AP (83.02 vs 70.54). Against full-training-free tuning-free baselines, the gains are larger: SteerVAD exceeds EventVAD by 5.12% AUC and 18.98% AP, and LAVAD by 6.87% AUC and 21.01% AP. However, SteerVAD still trails the fully fine-tuned Holmes-VAD (89.51% AUC, 90.67% AP) by 2.36% AUC and 7.65% AP, and Holmes-VAU (88.96% AUC, 87.68% AP) by 1.81% AUC and 4.66% AP. Notably, SteerVAD achieves this competitive performance using only 1% of training data and a frozen backbone, whereas fine-tuned methods require full training sets and update billions of parameters. Among weakly supervised methods, SteerVAD's 87.15% AUC is competitive with top entries like VadCLIP (88.02%) and Yang et al. (87.79%), while its 83.02% AP on XD-Violence is slightly below VadCLIP (84.51%) and Yang et al. (83.68%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the tuning-free MLLM-based VAD line (LAVAD, EventVAD, VERA, HiProbe-VAD), which passively reads generated text or hidden-state features from a frozen model, by introducing active geometric intervention on the model's internal representations. It draws on mechanistic interpretability findings that specific attention heads encode task-relevant semantics, and on the manifold hypothesis from topology, to formalize the VAD challenge as separating entangled low-dimensional structures in latent space. The low-rank adapter design in the Local Gating Module echoes LoRA-style parameter-efficient adaptation, but is applied not to weights and not to the backbone, rather to generate context-dependent scaling signals for a small set of identified attention heads. The paper thus carves new ground by repurposing interpretability tools (head-level analysis) and geometric concepts (manifold steering) into a lightweight, data-efficient intervention framework that leaves the MLLM entirely frozen.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is extreme data efficiency: calibrating on 1% of the training set (~16 videos) yields performance within 0.27% of using 100%, and the selected attention heads are identical across 10 random seeds, suggesting the geometric signature of anomaly is a stable, low-rank property of the backbone. This opens a path for deploying MLLM-based VAD in settings where labeled data is scarce and retraining is infeasible. However, the evaluation is limited to two benchmarks and a single MLLM (InternVL3), and the ~2.4% AUC gap to fully fine-tuned Holmes-VAD remains. Open questions include whether the RSA-identified experts and the anisotropic scaling policy transfer across different MLLM architectures, whether the approach generalizes to open-set anomaly categories beyond the 13/6 types in the benchmarks, and whether the 'tuning-free' framing (which still trains a small HMC) is truly cost-free in practice for multi-domain deployment.
