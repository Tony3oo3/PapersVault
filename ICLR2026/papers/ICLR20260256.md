## Problem

Existing point-supervised oriented object detection methods suffer from two coupled deficiencies: (1) pseudo labels are inefficiently utilized because all ground-truth points are assigned to a single FPN level, discarding the scale information that multi-level label assignment would exploit; and (2) pseudo label quality is poor in either sparse or dense scenes—Voronoi Watershed over/under-segments when spatial cues are minimal, while SAM over-merges instances in crowded layouts. No prior method addresses both the quality and the utilization of pseudo labels simultaneously.

## Input / Output

During training the model receives an RGB image (split into 1024×1024 patches for DOTA/STAR, scaled to 800×800 for DIOR, or 1024×1024 for RSAR) together with one point annotation per instance; during inference only the image is provided. The network outputs a rotated bounding box [(x, y), (w, h), θ], a class label, and a confidence score for each detected instance. MobileSAM is invoked exclusively during training to generate mask supervision for sparse scenes and plays no role at inference, preserving fast single-pass detection.

## Method

Point2RBox-v3 builds on the Point2RBox-v2 baseline (ResNet-50 + FPN + PSC angle coder) and introduces two modules. Progressive Label Assignment (PLA) re-introduces standard multi-level FPN label assignment into the point-supervised setting: in early epochs it derives approximate object dimensions from Voronoi Watershed pseudo labels, then after a switch epoch (default 6) it replaces them with the network's own forward predictions, selecting the highest-scoring candidate box per GT point across all FPN levels. Prior-Guided Dynamic Mask Loss (PGDM-Loss) routes each image to either a MobileSAM branch (sparse scenes, ≤ N_thr instances, default 4) or the original Watershed branch (dense scenes); SAM candidate masks are filtered by a class-specific scoring function combining center alignment, color consistency, rectangularity, circularity, and aspect-ratio reliability, and the selected mask yields width/height regression targets optimized with Gaussian Wasserstein Distance loss. The two modules are orthogonal—PLA improves utilization of scale cues while PGDM-Loss improves mask quality—and together they raise end-to-end AP50 on DOTA-v1.0 from 51.0% to 59.6% and two-stage AP50 from 62.6% to 66.1%.

## Results

Point2RBox-v3 achieves state-of-the-art point-supervised oriented object detection across six benchmarks. In the two-stage track (pseudo-label → FCOS), it reaches 66.09% AP50 on DOTA-v1.0, 56.86% on DOTA-v1.5, 41.28% on DOTA-v2.0, 46.40% on DIOR, 19.60% on STAR, and 45.96% on RSAR. In the end-to-end track, it achieves 59.61% AP50 on DOTA-v1.0, an 8.61-point improvement over its predecessor Point2RBox-v2 (51.00%). The method also generalizes to non-remote-sensing domains, outperforming Point2RBox-v2 by 4.5% on OCDPCB and 8.6% on DIATOM.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among point-supervised methods, Point2RBox-v3 dominates all competitors on every dataset in both tracks. In the E2E track on DOTA-v1.0, it achieves 59.61% AP50, surpassing Point2RBox-v2 (51.00%) by 8.61 points, PointOBB-v3 (41.29%) by 18.32 points, PMS-SAM-RSD (46.00%) by 13.61 points, and P2RBox (59.04%) by 0.57 points. In the two-stage track, the gap widens: 66.09% vs. Point2RBox-v2 (62.61%, +3.48), PointOBB-v3 (49.24%, +16.85), and P2RBox (59.04%, +7.05). On DIOR (sparse scenes), it edges out PMHO (46.40% vs. 46.20%) while substantially outperforming Point2RBox-v2 (44.45%) and PointOBB-v3 (40.18%). On the SAR-modality RSAR dataset, it achieves 45.96% (two-stage), far exceeding Point2RBox-v2 (30.90%) and PointOBB-v3 (22.60%). However, it still trails fully-supervised RBox methods (e.g., FCOS at 72.44%, S2A-Net at 75.81%) and HBox-supervised methods (H2RBox-v2 at 72.31%), reflecting the inherent information gap between point and box annotations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper is the direct successor to Point2RBox-v2 (CVPR 2025), inheriting its ResNet-50 backbone, FPN head, PSC angle coder, and a suite of loss functions (Gaussian Overlap, Edge, Copy-Paste, Self-Supervised Consistency). The key conceptual departure is twofold: (1) it re-introduces multi-level FPN label assignment—abandoned by all prior point-supervised methods (Point2RBox, Point2RBox-v2, PointOBB series)—by using dynamically generated pseudo labels to supply the scale cues that raw point annotations lack; and (2) it hybridizes SAM-based segmentation with Voronoi Watershed via a sparsity-based routing mechanism, a combination not previously explored in the point-supervised OOD literature. The PSC angle coder and symmetry-learning paradigm trace back to the original Point2RBox (CVPR 2024).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8.61% end-to-end AP50 gain over the previous SOTA (51.00% → 59.61%) and the 3.48% two-stage gain demonstrate that both pseudo-label quality and their utilization efficiency are first-order factors in weakly-supervised OOD, a finding that should influence how future methods design their supervision pipelines. The method generalizes to partially weakly-supervised settings (PWOOD) and non-remote-sensing domains (OCDPCB, DIATOM), broadening its practical relevance. However, performance remains limited by SAM's color-over-texture bias in sparse, low-contrast scenes (e.g., basketball courts, ground track fields), and PLA's benefits are most pronounced in multi-scale scenarios, leaving open the question of whether more fundamental architectural or loss-function innovations could further close the gap between point and full RBox supervision.
