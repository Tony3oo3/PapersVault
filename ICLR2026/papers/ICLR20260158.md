## Problem

Existing rule-based explanation methods for GNNs (e.g., GraphTrail, GLGExplainer) optimize and evaluate fidelity in an intermediate, uninterpretable concept space, while the final subgraph explanations presented to end users are often chemically invalid, unrepresentative, or match no real dataset instance. This disconnect means explanations can appear highly faithful yet be unreliable in practice, undermining both usability and trustworthiness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained GNN of any architecture (GCN, GraphSAGE, GIN, GAT) along with the training graph dataset (node feature matrix X and adjacency matrix A) and produces, for each class, a DNF logical rule over predicates, a set of representative subgraphs per predicate, and generalizable grounding rules that map predicates back to input features. A key structural detail is that each predicate is a pair (WL hash of the L-hop receptive field, binary activation vector over top-K informative embedding dimensions), and grounding rules are expressed as decision-tree splits over orbit-ordered subgraph feature vectors Z.

## Method

LOGICXGNN operates in three stages. First, it identifies hidden predicates by computing, for every node in the training set, a structural pattern (Weisfeiler-Lehman hash of the L-hop receptive field) and an embedding pattern (binary activation of the top-K most informative dimensions identified via a decision tree on graph-level embeddings), yielding a set of structurally grounded and model-faithful predicates. Second, it constructs a binary activation matrix per class and fits a shallow decision tree to extract compact DNF logical rules that explain the GNN's predictions. Third, it grounds each predicate into the input feature space by partitioning activating subgraphs into automorphism orbits, building structure-aware feature vectors Z via orbit-ordered concatenation, and learning interpretable grounding rules via decision trees. This design ensures 100% validity of explanation subgraphs, avoids the ambiguity of conflicting rules, and achieves 10–100× speedup over baselines by replacing expensive GNN forward passes and local explainers with efficient graph traversal and tree fitting.

## Results

LOGICXGNN achieves data-grounded fidelity (Fid_D) of 82.67% on BA-MultiShapes, 85.32% on BBBP, 81.36% on Mutagenicity, 73.81% on NCI1, and 74.16% on IMDB, outperforming the best baseline (GraphTrail) by an average of over 20 percentage points. On large-scale datasets (Reddit, Twitch, GitHub), both baselines time out within 12 hours, while LOGICXGNN completes in 4–12 minutes with Fid_D of 87.39%, 59.71%, and 65.01% respectively. Runtime is 10–100× faster than baselines (e.g., 0.02s vs. 2.54s on BA-MultiShapes; 0.44s vs. 24.07s on NCI1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LOGICXGNN outperforms both baselines on every dataset and every metric. Against GLGExplainer (Azzolin et al., ICLR 2023), the gap is dramatic: GLG achieves only 0–39% Fid_D and fails to produce any rules on BBBP and IMDB, while LOGICXGNN reaches 74–85%. Against GraphTrail (Armgaan et al., NeurIPS 2024), LOGICXGNN wins by 2.9–38.8 percentage points in Fid_D (e.g., 85.32% vs. 50.00% on BBBP; 74.16% vs. 35.35% on IMDB). In addition to higher fidelity, LOGICXGNN is 30–127× faster than GraphTrail (e.g., 0.02s vs. 2.54s on BA-MultiShapes; 0.44s vs. 24.07s on NCI1). On the three large-scale social-network datasets (Reddit, Twitch, GitHub), both baselines exceed the 12-hour time limit, whereas LOGICXGNN finishes in under 13 minutes. Qualitatively, GraphTrail produces chemically invalid subgraphs (0% validity on BBBP) and conflicting rules, while LOGICXGNN achieves 100% validity and higher coverage (e.g., 80.06% vs. 0.00% for class 0 on Mutagenicity) and stability (66.67% vs. 37.50% on Mutagenicity).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as a critical re-examination of the global rule-based explanation paradigm for GNNs established by GLGExplainer (Azzolin et al., ICLR 2023) and GraphTrail (Armgaan et al., NeurIPS 2024). Rather than building directly on either method's pipeline, it identifies a shared structural weakness: both optimize and evaluate fidelity in an intermediate, uninterpretable concept space while the final subgraph grounding step is unreliable or even invalid. LOGICXGNN departs by constructing predicates explicitly from the GNN's message-passing structure (WL hashing of receptive fields plus embedding activation patterns) and by introducing data-grounded fidelity (Fid_D) as the primary evaluation metric, shifting the locus of faithfulness from abstract concepts to observable subgraphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that state-of-the-art methods like GraphTrail can report 66% concept-space fidelity while producing zero chemically valid explanation subgraphs on Mutagenicity is a significant trustworthiness concern for any deployment of GNN explanations in high-stakes domains (healthcare, drug discovery). The introduction of Fid_D as a more realistic evaluation criterion will likely reshape how the field benchmarks explanation quality. Open questions include whether the decision-tree-based grounding rules scale to very high-dimensional continuous feature spaces, how to integrate domain-specific constraints (e.g., chemical valence rules) into the predicate design, and whether the framework extends naturally to self-explainable GNN architectures rather than purely post-hoc settings.
