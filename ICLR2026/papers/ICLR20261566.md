## Problem

Deep learning-based molecular docking methods, particularly torsional diffusion models, produce chemically implausible poses and fail to surpass classical physics-based dockers when controlling for structural validity. The root cause is that torsional parametrisations induce highly entangled, non-product measures in Cartesian space due to the nonlinear, ambiguous mapping from dihedral angles to atomic displacements, making the learning problem ill-conditioned and the sampling dynamics unstable.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a ligand's 2D molecular graph (atom types, bond types, conjugation), a fixed protein 3D structure (holo-conformation), and a binding pocket defined as all residues with any atom within a stochastic cutoff d_r = d_0 + N(0, σ_r) of any ligand atom (default d_0=5Å, σ_r=1Å). It outputs a docked ligand 3D pose by generating SE(3) rigid-body transformations for each fragment, which are composed via the mapping φ: SE(3)^m → R^{|G_ligand|×3}; N_seeds=40 samples are generated and ranked by a heuristic combining pseudo-binding energy and physicochemical checks (bond lengths, angles, internal energy) without requiring a separate confidence model.

## Method

SIGMADOCK decomposes a ligand into rigid-body fragments via a novel FR3D scheme that breaks rotatable bonds then stochastically merges adjacent fragments to reduce degrees of freedom (empirically m ≈ 2/3(k+1)), and defines an SE(3)^m Riemannian diffusion process where the forward SDE applies independent Brownian motion on translations and SO(3) rotations per fragment. The key theoretical insight (Theorem 1) is that disjoint rigid fragments yield a factorised product of Haar measures on SE(3)^m, unlike torsional models whose induced Cartesian measures are generically non-product due to overlapping downstream atom displacements. Soft triangulation constraints condition on cross-fragment distance mismatches (Δd_{A,C}) to preserve bond angles without restricting free dihedrals, and an SO(3)-equivariant EquiformerV2 backbone with a Newton-Euler prediction head (adapted from Jin et al., 2023) parametrises the score function in the tangent space of SE(3)^m, ensuring invariance to local coordinate axis choices (Theorem 2).

## Results

On the PoseBusters (PB) set, SIGMADOCK achieves a Top-1 success rate (RMSD < 2 Å & PB-valid) of 79.9%, substantially outperforming recent deep learning approaches (12.7–32.8%) and surpassing classical physics-based dockers for the first time under the intended train-test split. On the Astex (AX) set, SIGMADOCK reaches near-perfect Top-1 accuracy above 90%. Per-sequence-similarity analysis shows SIGMADOCK at 79.9% PB-valid overall (72% on low-similarity [0,30) proteins, 87% on high-similarity [95,100] proteins), comparable to AlphaFold3's 80.2% overall, while using only 19k training examples and sampling 50× faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIGMADOCK surpasses all classical physics-based dockers (AutoDock Vina, GLIDE, Vinardo) on the PB set under the intended train-test split, marking the first time a deep learning method achieves this. Against DiffDock (Corso et al., 2022), SIGMADOCK achieves 6.3× higher PB-validity (79.9% vs ~12.7%), and against the larger DiffDock-L (Corso et al., 2024), it reaches 79.9% Top-1 (RMSD & PB-valid) versus 50% Top-1 (RMSD only). Compared to AlphaFold3, SIGMADOCK achieves near-identical overall PB-validity (79.9% vs 80.2%) but with a fraction of the training data (19k vs. millions of complexes), substantially lower train-test leakage, and 50× faster inference. On low-sequence-similarity proteins ([0,30) split), SIGMADOCK scores 72% PB-valid versus AF3's 87%, indicating AF3 retains an advantage in the hardest generalisation regime, though SIGMADOCK narrows the gap to 7 points on [30,95) and actually exceeds AF3 (87% vs 78%) on the [95,100] high-similarity split. On the Astex set, SIGMADOCK reaches above 90% Top-1, outperforming all listed baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIGMADOCK builds on the SE(3) Riemannian diffusion framework of Yim et al. (2023) and the EquiformerV2 equivariant transformer backbone (Liao et al., 2023), adapting them from protein backbone generation to molecular docking. It departs fundamentally from the dominant torsional-diffusion paradigm (DiffDock, SurfDock, Re-Dock) by replacing dihedral-angle parameterization with independent rigid-body fragment transformations in SE(3)^m, motivated by a theoretical result (Theorem 1) showing that torsional parameterizations induce non-product, entangled measures in Cartesian space. The SO(3)-equivariant prediction head is adapted from Jin et al. (2023), and the fragmentation strategy (FR3D) with soft triangulation constraints is novel, drawing on structural chemistry priors rather than learned representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first deep learning docking method to surpass classical physics-based tools (Vina, GLIDE) under the intended PoseBusters train-test split, closing a gap that Buttenschoen et al. (2024) identified as a critical failure mode of prior generative dockers. Achieving AF3-level Top-1 accuracy with ~19k training pairs and 50× faster inference makes the approach far more accessible for high-throughput virtual screening than co-folding models. Key limitations remain: the method assumes a rigid receptor and known pocket, excludes co-factors (showing elevated failure rates when ions or natural ligands are present), and has not yet been extended to flexible docking or co-folding. The theoretical characterization of why torsional models underperform (non-product induced measures, gauge ambiguity) provides a principled design principle that may generalize beyond docking.
