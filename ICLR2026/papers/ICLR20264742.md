## Problem

Dynamically adapting metaheuristic hyperparameters to the problem instance at hand remains an open challenge: manual rule-based strategies are brittle and non-generalizable, while deep reinforcement learning approaches suffer from prohibitive sample complexity (millions of executions) and poor transfer to unseen instances. The paper asks whether pre-trained LLMs can serve as zero-shot reasoning engines for online algorithm control, bypassing the costly training phase entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264742/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each decision point, the framework consumes five real-time ELA metrics (fitness skewness, kurtosis, landscape R², dispersion ratio, and search variability) computed from the current population, combined with a sliding-window Experience Pool of the most recent L=20 state-action-outcome tuples, all formatted into structured text prompts. The output is a concrete numerical hyperparameter configuration (e.g., mutation rate 0.15, crossover probability 0.55) plus a qualitative strategic directive (e.g., 'Increase Exploration'), which is fed back to the metaheuristic for the next search phase. The entire pipeline operates on text and numerical data with no gradient-based training.

## Method

AutoEP is a closed-loop 'sense-reason-act' architecture that grounds LLM inference in empirical search dynamics. The sensing stage extracts five ELA features characterizing fitness distribution, landscape structure, diversity, and progress. The reasoning stage is a Chain of Reasoning (CoR) pipeline of three specialized LLM agents: a Strategist (one-time) that maps each hyperparameter to its qualitative effect on exploration/exploitation, an Analyst that diagnoses the current state by synthesizing ELA signals into a consensus or conflict assessment and issues a strategic directive, and an Actuator that translates the directive into specific parameter values via in-context learning from the Experience Pool. This decomposition mitigates hallucination by anchoring abstract reasoning in quantitative metrics and improves reliability through cross-validation among agents. The framework is entirely training-free (zero-shot), uses locally-deployed 30B-class open-source models (Qwen3-30B), and achieves performance on par with GPT-4-class proprietary models at roughly one-tenth the inference latency.

## Results

On TSP (TSPLIB), GA-2opt+AutoEP achieves Opt.gap of 0.00% on eil51, 0.01% on Rd100 and Kroa150, 0.09% on rd300, 0.08% on rat575, and 3.58% on dsj1000, outperforming all other hyperparameter tuning methods and neural combinatorial optimization baselines (DACT, LEHD) across all six instances. On CVRP (VRPLIB), GA-2opt+AutoEP achieves 0.01% Opt.gap on N=20 and 3.17% on N=500, again the best among all compared methods. On UAV trajectory optimization with 300 sensor nodes, ACO+AutoEP reduces trajectory length to 1574.90 (vs. 1912.74 for vanilla ACO), a 17.16% improvement over the baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoEP (GA-2opt+AutoEP) outperforms all 13 baselines on the primary TSP benchmark. Against the strongest RL-based tuner GLEET, AutoEP achieves substantially lower Opt.gap on larger instances (e.g., 0.09% vs. 0.33% on rd300; 0.08% vs. 0.91% on rat575; 3.58% vs. 5.47% on dsj1000). Against LLM-enhanced operator methods EoH and ReEvo, AutoEP wins decisively on mid-to-large instances (e.g., 0.08% vs. 2.91%/2.68% on rat575). Against neural combinatorial optimizers DACT and LEHD, AutoEP matches or beats them on all TSP instances (e.g., 3.58% vs. 4.97%/5.54% on dsj1000) while using a general-purpose metaheuristic rather than a problem-specific architecture. On CVRP, GA-2opt+AutoEP achieves the best Opt.gap across all five problem sizes (0.01%–3.17%), beating GLEET (0.01%–4.07%) and ReEvo (0.03%–5.27%). On FSSP, AutoEP improves GA-2opt from 4.37% to 2.09% Opt.gap on n20m10, outperforming PFSPNet (4.04%) and NEH (4.05%). On UAV trajectory optimization, ACO+AutoEP achieves the shortest trajectory length at every scale (e.g., 1574.90 vs. 1683.40 for ACO+GLEET at N=300), confirming consistent gains across all four problem domains and three metaheuristic algorithms (GA, PSO, ACO).

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoEP sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Exploratory Landscape Analysis (Mersmann et al., 2011), which provides the quantitative state-sensing layer; (2) Meta-BBO and RL-based hyperparameter tuning (GLEET, NeuroCrossover, DesignX), from which it departs by eliminating the training phase entirely in favor of zero-shot LLM reasoning; and (3) LLM-based algorithm design (EoH, ReEvo), from which it departs conceptually by positioning the LLM as a high-level supervisor that adjusts hyperparameters rather than as a direct search operator. The multi-agent Chain of Reasoning draws on the HuggingGPT-style decomposition of complex tasks into specialized sub-agents, but applies it to the novel domain of real-time algorithm control grounded in empirical search dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is demonstrating that a structured reasoning framework (ELA grounding + multi-LLM CoR) can make a 30B open-source model match GPT-4-class performance at ~10× lower latency, making LLM-driven algorithm control viable for local deployment in privacy-sensitive or latency-critical settings (factory scheduling, logistics). Limitations include evaluation on only three metaheuristics and four problem classes, a 2–5 minute overhead per run, and dependence on the LLM's ability to interpret ELA features correctly. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to continuous optimization, multi-objective settings, or algorithms with dozens of interacting hyperparameters, and whether the CoR decomposition remains beneficial as base models improve in raw reasoning ability.
