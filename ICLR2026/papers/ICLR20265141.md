## Problem

Conventional CAPTCHAs relying on text recognition or 2D image understanding are increasingly defeated by state-of-the-art multimodal large language models, eroding their effectiveness as a first line of defense against automated abuse. While MLLMs excel at perceptual tasks, they exhibit persistent and significant limitations in spatial reasoning—geometric consistency, perspective-taking, occlusion handling, and mental rotation—due to scarce training data and visual encoder constraints. The paper asks whether these specific spatial-reasoning weaknesses can be systematically exploited to build a CAPTCHA that maintains a large, durable human-machine performance gap.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a declarative JSON manifest specifying a spatial invariant, parameter ranges (object counts, rotation angles, camera poses, occlusion fractions, candidate set sizes), a random seed, and a target difficulty bin (easy/medium/hard). It produces a complete, service-ready CAPTCHA instance: one or more rendered 3D scene panels (generated via Blender or VTK), a natural-language prompt, a set of multiple-choice answer variants (typically 4, with exactly one correct), and a ground-truth label computed purely from scene geometry independent of rendering. Each instance carries full provenance metadata enabling deterministic regeneration and audit.

## Method

Spatial CAPTCHA is a procedural generation framework built around invariant-specified task manifests, where each task class is anchored to a mathematically well-defined spatial invariant (e.g., rotational congruence under SO(2)/SO(3), topological adjacency, egocentric-allocentric consistency). The pipeline operates in three stages: (1) sampling input variables from stratified priors over difficulty bins, (2) procedural scene construction with constrained distractor synthesis (near-miss perturbations along the same spatial axes) and automated validation enforcing non-intersection, uniqueness, and visibility margins, and (3) rendering and prompt/answer assembly. Difficulty is controlled via a monotone map d(θ) = wᵀφ(θ) fitted to pilot human response times using isotonic and quantile regression, enabling inverse sampling to target specific difficulty levels. The key design principle is that distractors differ only in prohibited spatial relations while superficial appearance is held constant, ensuring that correct answers require genuine invariant-preserving reasoning rather than surface-level pattern matching. Seven task formulations across four cognitive ability categories (spatial perception, perspective-taking, mental rotation, multi-step visualization) are instantiated, yielding the 1,050-instance Spatial-CAPTCHA-Bench where the best MLLM achieves only 31.0% Pass@1 versus 89.5% for humans.

## Results

On Spatial-CAPTCHA-Bench (1,050 instances across 7 task types and 3 difficulty levels), humans achieve 89.5% pass@1 while the best MLLM (gpt-5.1) reaches only 36.1% pass@1, yielding a ~53 pp human–machine gap. Performance degrades steeply with difficulty for models (61.4% ± 48.7 pp on Easy → 12.4% ± 33.0 pp on Hard; Cohen's h = 1.08) but only ~3 pp for humans. On reCAPTCHA-Bench, the same models score substantially higher (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro: 55.3% vs. 29.0% on Spatial), confirming that Spatial CAPTCHA widens the discriminative gap. All models fall below the identity line on k-of-k reliability vs. pass@k coverage, indicating uniformly poor calibration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against reCAPTCHA-Bench (a 150-item subset of MCA-Bench), Spatial CAPTCHA produces a dramatically larger human–machine gap: Gemini-2.5-Pro scores 55.3% on reCAPTCHA but only 29.0% on Spatial, while humans score 86.4% vs. 89.5% respectively, widening the gap from ~31 pp to ~60 pp. Compared to other CAPTCHA suites in Table 1, Spatial-CAPTCHA-Bench achieves the largest human–machine separation (99.8% human vs. 31.0% best MLLM) versus Open CAPTCHAWorld (93.3% vs. 40.0%), Aura-CAPTCHA (92.8% vs. 5.2%), and IllusionCAPTCHA (86.95% vs. 0.0%). Against spatial-reasoning datasets, the best MLLM scores are lower on Spatial CAPTCHA (31.0%) than on 3DSRBench (52.0%), Wang et al. (67.1%), or EmbSpatial-Bench (49.1%), indicating that the procedural, invariant-anchored design is harder than static spatial QA. Open-weight models (Qwen2.5-VL, Llama-4, Mistral) score near chance on both benchmarks (2–24%), while proprietary models show a wider spread, with gpt-5.1 leading at 36.1% and claude-opus-4 trailing at 7.1%. Fine-tuning gpt-4o on ~3.7M tokens raises accuracy from 38% to 57.7%, still far below the 89.5% human ceiling, suggesting the gap is architectural rather than purely data-limited.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: the CAPTCHA design tradition (Von Ahn et al., reCAPTCHA, DiffCAPTCHA, VideoCAPTCHA) and spatial-reasoning benchmarks for MLLMs (3DSRBench, EmbSpatial-Bench, PlanQA). It departs from prior CAPTCHAs by targeting high-level spatial invariants (mental rotation, perspective-taking, multi-step visualization) rather than low-level perception tasks like OCR or object detection that modern MLLMs have largely solved. It departs from prior spatial benchmarks by introducing a procedural generation pipeline with constraint-based difficulty control and automated ground-truth certification, drawing on cognitive-psychology taxonomies (Shepard & Metzler, Hegarty & Waller) to formalize each task as a verifiable geometric invariant rather than a static image-question pair.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 68.8-point human–model gap (99.8% vs. 31.0% best MLLM) and the fine-tuning experiment (38% → 57.7% with 3.7M tokens, still far below the 89.5% human ceiling) together suggest the spatial-reasoning deficit in MLLMs is architectural rather than data-driven, pointing toward the need for equivariant or hybrid neural-symbolic modules. The procedural generation framework addresses a fundamental limitation of static benchmarks—once absorbed into training corpora, discriminative power collapses—making it a more durable tool for both security deployment and ongoing model evaluation. Open questions include whether GUI-interactive or temporal-spatial challenges can further widen the gap, and whether the reCAPTCHA comparison (conducted via a proxy subset of MCA-Bench rather than live reCAPTCHA) fully captures real-world deployment conditions.
