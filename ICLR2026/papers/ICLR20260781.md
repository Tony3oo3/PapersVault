## Problem

Diffusion Policy's iterative denoising process (e.g., 50 steps at 1 ms each) limits action update rates to ~10 Hz, far below the 30–50 Hz required for smooth real-time robotic control. Existing cache-based acceleration methods for diffusion models target U-Net backbones or image/video generation and cannot be directly transferred to transformer-based Diffusion Policy due to fundamental architectural and data divergences. No training-free, block-level adaptive caching strategy existed for this setting.

## Input / Output

BAC operates as a training-free plugin on a pre-trained Diffusion Transformer (DiT) decoder. At each denoising step k, the decoder receives a noisy action sample a_t^(k) conditioned on RGB observations and robot proprioception, and produces a progressively denoised action; the final output a_t^(0) is the control action chunk. The method intercepts intermediate block outputs (self-attention, cross-attention, and FFN) across L decoder layers, caching and reusing them at adaptively chosen timesteps. The computation budget is controlled by two hyperparameters: S (total number of cache update steps per block) and n (number of high-error upstream blocks selected by BUA).

## Method

BAC consists of two components. First, the Adaptive Caching Scheduler (ACS) computes per-block optimal update timesteps by formulating the problem as a dynamic programming optimization: it defines interval similarity via cosine similarity between consecutive-step features, then finds the set of M update steps that maximizes cumulative interval similarity, solved in O(MK²) time and computed once per task (exploiting high episode homogeneity). Second, the Bubbling Union Algorithm (BUA) addresses a critical failure mode—inter-block error propagation—where naively applying block-wise schedules causes FFN blocks to amplify upstream caching errors during updates (formalized in Proposition 3.1 via a first-order linear response operator through LayerNorm and the two linear layers). BUA selects the top-n upstream blocks with the largest average L1-norm feature variation and forces them to update their cache whenever any downstream FFN block updates, effectively truncating the error propagation chain. Together, ACS minimizes reuse-induced error while BUA suppresses update-induced error, yielding up to 3× lossless speedup.

## Results

BAC achieves lossless acceleration with S=10 cache update steps, matching or slightly exceeding full-precision success rates across all benchmarks (0.79 vs 0.76 on RoboMimic-PH, 0.77 vs 0.76 on RoboMimic-MH, 0.98 vs 0.98 on multi-stage tasks) while delivering 3.4–3.6× speedup. On the real-world Franka arm, BAC (S=7) reaches 71% success rate at 39.2 Hz, compared to only 3% at 7.8 Hz for standard DDPM (K=100). On the RDT-1B VLA model (ManiSkill), BAC achieves up to 3.55× acceleration with negligible performance drop at S=2. The method is training-free and requires only a one-time offline computation of the caching schedule per task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BAC (S=10) matches or exceeds full-precision performance on all three benchmark suites (PH: 0.79 vs 0.76; MH: 0.77 vs 0.76; multi-stage: 0.98 vs 0.98) while achieving 3.4–3.6× speedup, confirming the 'lossless' claim. Against the Uniform baseline (all blocks share a fixed update interval), BAC is comparable on easy tasks but substantially better on hard ones: on Kitchen_p4, BAC(S=10) reaches 0.94/0.97 while Uniform(fastest) collapses to 0.08/0.34. Against TeaCache, BAC dominates on multi-stage tasks (0.98 vs 0.52 for TeaCache-fast) and on the MH Transport task (0.30/0.46 vs 0.50/0.40 for TeaCache-fast, but TeaCache-fastest drops to 0.35/0.31). The key differentiator is BAC's block-wise adaptive scheduling combined with the Bubbling Union Algorithm, which prevents the error surges that cause Uniform and TeaCache to fail on long-horizon or high-precision tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-caching paradigm established for image and video diffusion models (DeepCache, FORA, TeaCache) to the Diffusion Policy setting, where the authors argue existing methods fail due to fundamental differences in data characteristics and model architecture. The key conceptual departure is the recognition that Diffusion Policy exhibits non-uniform temporal feature dynamics and distinct block-specific similarity patterns, motivating a block-wise adaptive schedule solved via dynamic programming rather than the uniform-interval schedules used in prior work. The Bubbling Union Algorithm is a novel contribution with no direct analogue in prior caching literature, addressing an inter-block error propagation issue specific to the FFN architecture of Diffusion Transformers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BAC provides a practical, training-free 3× speedup for Diffusion Policy that bridges the gap between the 10 Hz action rate of standard 50-step denoising and the 30–50 Hz needed for smooth real-time robotic control, with demonstrated generalization to VLA models like RDT-1B. The identification and theoretical characterization of the error surge phenomenon (Proposition 3.1) offers a reusable diagnostic for anyone applying block-wise caching to transformer architectures with FFN layers lacking intermediate normalization. A stated limitation is that when the base model's task accuracy is already very low, caching may amplify inaccuracy; open questions include whether the approach extends to non-DiT diffusion policies (e.g., U-Net-based) and to settings with highly heterogeneous episodes where the episode-homogeneity assumption breaks down.
