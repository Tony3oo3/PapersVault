## Problem

In off-policy continuous control, accurate value estimation remains difficult for sparse-reward and long-horizon tasks because standard critics score state-action pairs in isolation, and multi-step (N-step) targets typically require importance sampling that introduces destabilizing variance. Actor-side action chunking can extend the effective horizon but reduces reactivity and couples performance to a fixed chunk-length hyperparameter. The paper asks whether the critic itself can be strengthened with temporal context and multi-step supervision while keeping the policy strictly one-step and the update rule free of importance sampling.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes a single low-dimensional state vector and outputs one continuous action per timestep (standard SAC Gaussian actor). The critic, by contrast, ingests a variable-length state-action window (s_t, a_t, …, a_{t+n-1}) sampled from the replay buffer and simultaneously emits n scalar prefix-conditioned Q-values, one for each prefix length i = 1…n. A causal mask ensures position i attends only to tokens at timesteps ≤ i, preventing future-action leakage; a binary action mask further prevents bootstrapping across episode boundaries when variable-length episodes are packed into fixed-length segments.

## Method

T-SAC replaces SAC's MLP critic with a lightweight 2-layer causal Transformer (128–256 hidden units) that conditions on short realized state-action prefixes from replay and predicts values for all prefix lengths in a single forward pass. Instead of averaging N-step targets (which dilutes sparse rewards), the method computes a separate MSE loss per horizon and averages their gradients during backpropagation, exploiting the positive correlation between adjacent-horizon gradients to reduce update variance while preserving long-horizon signal. Because the critic is conditioned on the realized action prefix, the i-step target G^(i) = Σ γ^j r_{t+j} + γ^i V_φ(s_{t+i}) requires no importance sampling: the intermediate actions are part of the conditioning variable rather than a distributional mismatch to correct. A short critic-parameter-freezing schedule (snapshot the target critic, cache bootstrap values, freeze for K updates) replaces Polyak averaging and enables stable target-free training, while the policy retains standard SAC entropy-regularized updates with a non-soft critic target.

## Results

On Meta-World ML1 (50 tasks), T-SAC solves most tasks within ~5M environment steps and reaches the 90%-of-final-return threshold in 1M steps, compared to 4M for both SAC and TOP-ERL. On dense Box-Pushing, T-SAC attains 96.8% success (vs. ≤85% for all prior baselines) and reaches the performance threshold in 4M steps (vs. 15M for SAC, 10M for CrossQ, 20M for GTrXL). On Gymnasium MuJoCo locomotion, T-SAC matches or surpasses SAC on Ant, Hopper, and Walker2d, with the largest gains on HumanoidStandup and HalfCheetah. Under sparse Box-Pushing rewards, T-SAC reaches 60% success, trailing TOP-ERL's 70%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SAC, T-SAC achieves a 4× sample-efficiency gain on Meta-World ML1 (1M vs. 4M steps to threshold) and a ~4× gain on dense Box-Pushing (4M vs. 15M), while matching or exceeding SAC on all five MuJoCo locomotion tasks. Against CrossQ, T-SAC is 2.5× faster to threshold on dense Box-Pushing (4M vs. 10M) and matches or surpasses it on locomotion. Against TOP-ERL, T-SAC wins decisively on Meta-World ML1 (1M vs. 4M) and achieves substantially higher peak success on dense Box-Pushing (96.8% vs. ≤85%), but loses on sparse Box-Pushing both in final success (60% vs. 70%) and sample efficiency (17M vs. 4M). Against GTrXL-style policies, T-SAC is 5× more sample-efficient on dense Box-Pushing (4M vs. 20M). Against Q-Chunking (MLP critic with policy-side action chunking), T-SAC's Transformer critic consistently outperforms the MLP critic under the same chunked policy, and the causal-masked Transformer avoids the future-action leakage inherent in the MLP's fixed-length input.

## Relation to Prior Work

T-SAC adapts the Transformer-critic idea from episodic RL (TOP-ERL, Li et al. 2024a) into a standard step-based, closed-loop SAC setting, keeping the policy strictly one-step while conditioning the critic on short state-action windows. It departs from policy-side action chunking (Q-Chunking, Li et al. 2025; Zhang et al. 2022) by placing temporal structure entirely in the critic's conditioning and N-step targets, avoiding the reactivity and hyperparameter-coupling trade-offs of chunked policies. For target-free training, it replaces CrossQ's batch renormalization with a simpler critic-parameter-freezing schedule, and it sidesteps the importance-sampling variance problem of off-policy N-step TD by conditioning on realized action prefixes rather than correcting for distribution mismatch.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that critic-side sequence modeling with gradient-averaged multi-horizon losses can substantially improve long-horizon credit assignment in online off-policy continuous control without importance sampling, offering a practical alternative to both policy-side chunking and episodic RL pipelines. The finding that naive target-level averaging of N-step returns destabilizes sparse-reward learning (while gradient-level averaging preserves signal) is a useful practical insight. Limitations include restriction to low-dimensional state-vector observations and online settings; extension to pixel-based, discrete-action, or real-robot domains remains open, and the method still trails TOP-ERL on sparse Box-Pushing (60% vs. 70%).
