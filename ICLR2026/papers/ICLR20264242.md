## Problem

Coordinate-based neural solvers (e.g., Siren) are infinitely differentiable and can solve PDEs, but they are computationally expensive and require hours of training because every query backpropagates through the full MLP. Grid-based representations (Instant-NGP, K-Planes) train orders of magnitude faster by exploiting spatial locality, but their reliance on d-linear interpolation makes them only C⁰ at grid boundaries and C¹ in the interior, so second and higher-order derivatives vanish or are discontinuous—rendering them unusable for differential equations that require Laplacians, Jacobians, or fourth-order terms.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-dimensional spatial (or spatio-temporal) domain Ω ⊂ ℝᵈ with d = 2 or 3, a governing PDE expressed as a differentiable residual F, and boundary/initial conditions, and produces a continuous multi-channel field u(x) queryable at arbitrary points. Supervision is derivative-only: the loss is the PDE residual evaluated at stratified random samples, optionally augmented with data terms (e.g., gradient/Laplacian images, oriented point clouds, or source functions). The output field can be scalar (SDF), vector-valued (3D cloth deformation), or complex-valued (Helmholtz wavefield), with channel count set by the decoder head.

## Method

∂^∞-Grid replaces the linear interpolation in feature grids with Gaussian radial basis function (RBF) kernels φ(r) = exp(−(εr)²), which are C^∞ and allow analytic higher-order derivatives via autograd. The encoder is a set of S co-located multi-resolution grids (resolutions N_max/2^s) where each query point x is interpolated over a precomputed 3-ring neighbourhood (6^d nodes per scale) using normalised RBF weights; the concatenated multi-scale features are decoded by a small linear layer or tanh-MLP into the target field. Boundary conditions are enforced as hard constraints through a distance-based modulation B(x) = 1 − exp(−‖x − x_∂Ω‖²/σ) that blends the network output with the known boundary function. Training minimises the PDE residual over stratified random samples with Adam (lr = 5×10⁻³), updating only the local grid features adjacent to each query, which yields a 5–20× speed-up over Siren while matching or exceeding its accuracy on Poisson, Helmholtz, Kirchhoff-Love, Eikonal, heat, and advection problems.

## Results

On the Poisson equation image reconstruction task (512×512 Camera image), ∂^∞-Grid achieves PSNR 32.24 (gradient supervision) and 12.19 (Laplacian supervision) in 25 s and 15 min respectively, outperforming Siren (PSNR 32.12 / 11.82) by a small margin while training 20× faster (25 s vs 10 min) for gradient and ~8× faster (15 min vs 1 hr 56 min) for Laplacian. On the Helmholtz equation (ω=20), the method matches the closed-form Hankel-function reference with a 4× speed-up over Siren. For 1D advection, ∂^∞-Grid achieves MAE 0.0023 (vs 0.0030 for INSR and 0.0029 for a finite-difference grid solver) while training 200× faster than INSR (1.5 min vs 5.33 h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Siren (Sitzmann et al., 2020), the primary coordinate-based MLP baseline, ∂^∞-Grid achieves marginally higher PSNR (32.24 vs 32.12 for gradient; 12.19 vs 11.82 for Laplacian) while training 20× faster on the gradient task and ~8× faster on the Laplacian task; on the Helmholtz equation it converges 4× faster. Against K-Planes (Fridovich-Keil et al., 2023) and Instant-NGP (Müller et al., 2022), the proposed method clearly wins: both grid-based baselines fail entirely on Laplacian supervision and the Helmholtz equation because their linear interpolation is not differentiable beyond first order, yielding PSNR 17.96 vs 32.24 on gradient reconstruction. Against NeuRBF (Chen et al., 2023b), the closest RBF-grid method, ∂^∞-Grid achieves PSNR/SSIM 29.44/0.87 versus 10.85/0.17 under gradient-based Poisson supervision, and NeuRBF also fails to recover a smooth SDF under Eikonal supervision. Against a GELU-activated PINN (Raissi et al., 2019), the proposed method reaches PSNR/SSIM 29.44/0.87 versus ≈19/0.65, confirming that the multi-resolution RBF grid captures high-frequency content that smooth-activation MLPs underfit. For 1D advection, ∂^∞-Grid beats INSR (Chen et al., 2023a) on both accuracy (MAE 0.0023 vs 0.0030) and speed (1.5 min vs 5.33 h), and slightly outperforms the classical finite-difference grid solver (Fedkiw et al., 2001) on MAE (0.0023 vs 0.0029) at the cost of longer runtime (1.5 min vs 1.8 s).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two lines of work: (1) fast feature-grid representations for implicit signals (Instant-NGP, K-Planes) that exploit spatial locality but rely on linear interpolation, making them non-differentiable beyond first order and thus unsuitable for PDE solving; and (2) coordinate-based neural solvers (Siren, PINNs) that are infinitely differentiable but computationally expensive due to global weight updates. The key departure is replacing linear interpolation with fixed-shape Gaussian RBF kernels on a multi-resolution co-located grid, borrowing the RBF-interpolated grid idea from NeuRBF but fundamentally re-purposing it: NeuRBF targets signal fitting with adaptive, signal-dependent kernels and single-ring neighbourhoods, whereas ∂^∞-Grid uses fixed kernels with overlapping multi-ring neighbourhoods specifically to guarantee C^∞ smoothness under derivative-only PDE supervision.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 5–20× speedup over Siren while matching or exceeding accuracy makes neural PDE solvers practical for iterative or near-real-time applications (e.g., cloth simulation in seconds rather than hours), lowering the barrier for physics-informed learning in graphics and engineering. Open questions remain: the curse of dimensionality limits scalability beyond 3D, theoretical guarantees for C^∞ under neighbourhood truncation are not established, and boundary errors (Runge's phenomenon) near domain edges are acknowledged but not fully resolved. The paper also notes that for structured-grid problems, traditional multi-grid solvers may still outperform, positioning ∂^∞-Grid as a flexible neural alternative rather than a replacement for classical numerics.
