## Problem

Existing text-to-image prompt engineering methods primarily enhance visual aesthetics while neglecting or even degrading semantic alignment between generated images and user descriptions. They also apply uniform modifications across all prompts rather than performing case-specific adjustments, and are typically tied to a single diffusion model, limiting their generalizability across different generators with varying prompt preferences.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261047/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a user-written text prompt and the image generated by a diffusion model from that prompt as visual feedback. It produces a refined text prompt that preserves all originally present concepts while enriching only the missing ones with additional attributes, actions, and spatial relationships, plus a small set of LLM-selected aesthetic keywords appended at the end. The entire pipeline is training-free and model-agnostic, operating on any text-to-image generator that accepts natural language prompts.

## Method

VisualPrompter is a training-free, two-module framework. The Self-Reflection Module (SERE) uses an LLM (Qwen2) to decompose the input prompt into atomic concepts structured as a Davidsonian Scene Graph (DSG) with dependency edges, then employs a VLM (Qwen2-VL) to answer binary yes/no questions about whether each concept is present in the generated image, with dependency-aware pruning to skip redundant checks. The Target-Specific Prompt Optimization module (TSPO) then selectively expands only the identified missing concepts with additional detail (attributes, actions, spatial relations) via the LLM, and reassembles all atomic concepts—both preserved and expanded—into a syntactically complete prompt. A final LLM decorator appends 0–2 aesthetic keywords per category (quality, style, lighting, etc.) without altering semantic content. This atomic-level decompose-expand-reassemble strategy ensures semantic fidelity is maintained while nudging the prompt toward the model's preferred distribution, and because the feedback is derived from the actual generated image, the same framework adapts automatically to different diffusion models.

## Results

On the DSG-1k and TIFA v1.0 benchmarks, VisualPrompter achieves an average semantic accuracy of 83.0% (percentage of 'yes' answers from a VLM judge), outperforming the unoptimized baseline (78.4%) and all three prior prompt engineering methods. The largest gains appear with Flux-dev (93.8% on TIFA, 84.3% on DSG) and Janus-Pro (93.7% on TIFA, 82.6% on DSG), where the model's strong generative capacity synergizes with the optimized prompts. On CLIP Score, VisualPrompter reaches 32.69 mean across four generative models versus 31.71 for the baseline, while on Aesthetic Score it achieves 5.81 (modestly above the 5.52 baseline but below NeuroPrompts' 6.21).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisualPrompter outperforms all three baselines on average semantic accuracy across both DSG-1k and TIFA v1.0, with the most dramatic margin over BeautifulPrompt (83.0 vs. 53.5), which consistently degrades below the unoptimized baseline on every model. NeuroPrompts (74.6) and Promptist (76.2) also fall below the 78.4 baseline on average, indicating that their keyword-append or LLM-rewrite strategies introduce irrelevant content that harms semantic fidelity; VisualPrompter's atomic-level decomposition avoids this by only expanding concepts the VLM confirms are missing. On CLIP Score, VisualPrompter leads all methods (32.69 mean), while NeuroPrompts drops sharply to 25.41 due to its limited keyword vocabulary. On Aesthetic Score, VisualPrompter (5.81) trails NeuroPrompts (6.21) and BeautifulPrompt (6.14), which the authors attribute to their more aggressive stylistic keyword injection; combining VisualPrompter's semantic optimization with NeuroPrompts' decorator yields the best aesthetic score (6.24) at the cost of some semantic consistency. The advantage of VisualPrompter is most pronounced on Flux-dev and Janus-Pro, where its model-agnostic feedback loop exploits the stronger generative capacity of these models, whereas the baselines—tuned primarily for Stable Diffusion v1.5—show diminished or negative transfer to newer architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Davidsonian Scene Graph (DSG, Cho et al., 2024) as the structural backbone for decomposing prompts into atomic, dependency-linked concepts, repurposing it from an evaluation tool into an optimization scaffold. It departs from prior prompt engineering methods (NeuroPrompts, Promptist, BeautifulPrompt) in three conceptual ways: (1) introducing VLM-based visual feedback to identify *which* concepts are actually missing from generated images rather than blindly expanding text, (2) operating at the atomic semantic level to preserve user intent while selectively enriching only deficient concepts, and (3) being training-free and model-agnostic, unlike prior methods that are fine-tuned for a single diffusion model. The self-reflection-then-targeted-optimization pipeline is the key architectural departure from the 'expand everything' paradigm of earlier LLM-based prompt engineers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical blind spot in prior prompt engineering: methods that improve aesthetics often *degrade* semantic fidelity (BeautifulPrompt drops from 78.4 to 53.5 average DSG score), which undermines practical utility for users who care about content accuracy. The training-free, plug-and-play design across SD 1.5, SD 2.1, Flux-dev, and Janus-Pro makes the approach immediately deployable without per-model retraining. Open questions include whether the modest aesthetic gains (5.81 vs. 6.21 for NeuroPrompts) can be improved without sacrificing semantics, how the method scales to inherently complex multi-object scenes where the base generator is the bottleneck, and whether the VLM-as-judge loop introduces compounding errors on ambiguous prompts. The authors also flag extension to multimodal (e.g., skeleton-conditioned) generation as a natural next step.
