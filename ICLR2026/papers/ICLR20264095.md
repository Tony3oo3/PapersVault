## Problem

The sequential SFT-then-RL paradigm for post-training instruction-tuned LLMs often underperforms pure RL because off-policy expert data disrupts the model's established response patterns, triggering a 'shift-readapt-overfit' progression. Existing integration methods either treat SFT and RL as rigidly separated stages or lack fine-grained control over how expert tokens influence the on-policy policy, making it difficult to selectively absorb expert knowledge without stifling exploration or causing overfitting.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static set of off-policy expert trajectories (e.g., generated by DeepSeek-R1) alongside on-policy rollouts (K=8 samples per prompt) scored by a task-specific reward function. It operates on instruction-tuned models (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, LLaMA3.2-3B-Instruct) that already possess established response patterns, making the distributional gap to expert data a central challenge. The output is a single unified policy trained end-to-end, eliminating the need to manually schedule the SFT-to-RL transition point.

## Method

CHORD reframes SFT as a dynamically weighted auxiliary loss within the GRPO on-policy RL loop, governed by a dual-control mechanism. A global coefficient μ follows a decay schedule (0.9→0.05) to smoothly transition from off-policy imitation to on-policy exploration, replacing the binary SFT-then-RL switch. A token-wise weighting function φ(y_t*; π_θ) = p_t(1−p_t) further modulates each expert token's gradient contribution, down-weighting both highly probable tokens (preventing entropy collapse) and extremely improbable tokens (preventing policy disruption), thereby concentrating off-policy learning on a 'sweet spot' of moderate uncertainty. The final objective is L_Hybrid = (1−μ)L_GRPO + μ·L_SFT-φ, where L_SFT-φ applies the per-token φ weights to the standard negative log-likelihood. This design makes the framework robust to the specific μ schedule, since φ inherently prevents both token-level overfitting and pattern disruption.

## Results

On mathematical reasoning, CHORD-φ achieves 62.5 on AMC, 18.2 on AIME24, 17.2 on AIME25, and 56.2 on MMLU-Pro, outperforming all baselines. On tool-use tasks, CHORD-φ reaches 78.5 overall BFCL score (69.9 Live, 90.2 Non-live), again the best among all methods. The full CHORD-μ variant (without token-wise weighting) also surpasses the strongest SFT-then-RL baseline (SFT-best+RL) by +2.4 on AMC, +1.0 on AIME24, and +1.6 on AIME25, while matching or exceeding pure RL on tool-use.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHORD-φ dominates all four baselines across every benchmark. Against GRPO (Pure RL) [Shao et al., 2024], CHORD-φ gains +10.4 on AMC, +5.0 on AIME24, +8.7 on AIME25, +10.4 on MMLU-Pro, and +1.4 on BFCL Overall, showing that the off-policy expert signal provides substantial additional value beyond on-policy exploration alone. Against LUFFY [Yan et al., 2025], which injects expert trajectories into GRPO rollout groups, CHORD-φ leads by +9.7 on AMC, +1.6 on AIME24, +2.9 on AIME25, +12.2 on MMLU-Pro, and +2.4 on BFCL Overall, indicating that token-wise weighting is more effective than group-level mixing. Against SASR [Chen et al., 2025c], which probabilistically interleaves SFT and RL steps, CHORD-φ wins by +8.5 on AMC, +5.5 on AIME24, +6.1 on AIME25, +11.1 on MMLU-Pro, and +3.8 on BFCL Overall. Against SRFT [Fu et al., 2025] (compared in Appendix D.1 on tool-use), CHORD-φ achieves 78.5 vs. SRFT's lower score, confirming that the dual-control mechanism (global μ + token-wise φ) outperforms sample-level SFT loss integration, particularly when fine-tuning an instruction model with an established policy rather than a base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO as its on-policy RL backbone and positions itself against the growing family of methods that integrate SFT and RL for LLM post-training, including sequential SFT-then-RL pipelines, interleaved schedules (SASR, Reift), and data-mixing approaches (LUFFY, SRFT, SimpleMix). Its key conceptual departure is reframing SFT not as a separate stage or a data-mixing operation, but as a dynamically weighted auxiliary loss within the RL objective, controlled by a dual mechanism: a global decay coefficient μ (inspired by scheduled sampling) and a token-wise weighting function φ(p)=p(1−p) that down-weights both high- and low-probability tokens. Crucially, the paper targets instruction-tuned models with established policies, distinguishing itself from the 'zero-RL' setting (base-model training) that dominates prior work like LUFFY and SRFT, where the distributional shift problem is less severe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'shift-readapt-overfit' diagnosis provides a useful empirical framework for understanding why naive SFT-then-RL can underperform pure RL, and the finding that pattern-shift magnitude (not just expert quality) governs SFT's effectiveness is practically important for practitioners choosing expert data sources. The token-wise φ weighting is a simple, computationally cheap mechanism that makes the framework robust to the global μ schedule, reducing hyperparameter sensitivity. Open questions remain around adaptive μ tuning without manual schedules, extending to heterogeneous expert mixtures, and whether the parabolic φ is truly optimal versus other uncertainty-based weightings; the paper acknowledges these as future directions. Performance gains over strong baselines are modest (1–3 points), suggesting the framework's value lies more in its diagnostic clarity and training stability than in large accuracy jumps.
