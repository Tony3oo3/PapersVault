## Problem

Existing Channel-Partiality (CP) methods for multivariate time series forecasting operate at the channel level and in the temporal domain, producing coarse-grained, frequency-coupled dependency signals that cannot distinguish meaningful low-frequency seasonal interactions from irrelevant high-frequency noise. No prior method offers the flexibility to adaptively balance Channel-Independent and Channel-Dependent strategies at the channel-patch level with frequency-specific routing, leaving fine-grained, segment-specific cross-channel relationships entangled.

## Input / Output

xCPD is a post-hoc plugin that takes the prediction tensor (C×T') from any existing forecasting backbone as input, partitions it into contiguous non-overlapping channel-patches of length P, and produces a refined forecast of the same shape via a gated dual-path residual correction. The plugin is model-agnostic and requires no backbone retraining; it operates on the model's output rather than the raw time series, making it a drop-in enhancement layer. The final prediction is the backbone output plus a sigmoid-gated combination of a GNN-path correction (capturing cross-variable spectral dependencies) and a Linear-path correction (preserving CI refinement).

## Method

xCPD reformulates the modeling unit from an entire channel to a channel-patch node in a graph, then applies three spectral-domain operations. First, it constructs a cosine-similarity graph over channel-patch embeddings and projects into a shared graph Fourier basis (theoretically justified by a Davis-Kahan-type bound in Theorem 4.1) to obtain consistent spectral embeddings across timesteps. Second, it partitions nodes into low-, mid-, and high-frequency groups using learnable band boundaries and a spectral energy response function (Theorem 4.2), then builds ego-graphs via k-NN sparsification. Third, a Dynamic Mixture-of-Experts router selects a variable number (1–3) of frequency-specific filters per ego-graph based on cumulative softmax probability exceeding a threshold, followed by L layers of GNN message passing on the filtered adjacency. The training objective combines MSE loss with entropy and balance regularizers to encourage diverse expert usage, and the overall time complexity is O(nkd + Lnk d), ensuring linear scalability.

## Results

On 9 long-term forecasting benchmarks (144 settings across 4 backbones × 9 datasets × 4 horizons), xCPD consistently improves MSE and MAE over the base model. In the averaged comparison (Table 2), DLinear+xCPD achieves the best MSE on 8/9 datasets (e.g., 0.312 on Exchange vs. 0.351 for LIFT and 0.348 for CCM; 0.197 on Electricity vs. 0.208 for LIFT), while TSMixer+xCPD leads on 8/9 datasets (e.g., 0.349 on Exchange vs. 0.378 for LIFT). On short-term M4 forecasting, xCPD reduces average SMAPE from 12.86 (TSMixer) to 12.79 and from 13.63 (DLinear) to 12.43. Zero-shot transfer on ETT yields 12.0% and 15.2% average MSE improvements for DLinear and PatchTST respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LIFT (Zhao & Shen, ICLR 2024), xCPD achieves lower MSE on 8/9 datasets for both TSMixer and DLinear backbones, with the largest gap on Exchange (0.312 vs. 0.351 for DLinear, a 11% reduction) and ETTh2 (0.507 vs. 0.553). Against CCM (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2024), xCPD wins on 8/9 datasets for TSMixer and 8/9 for DLinear; the sole exception is DLinear on ETTm2 where CCM achieves 0.306 vs. xCPD's 0.340. Against PCD (Lee et al., NeurIPS Workshop 2025), xCPD outperforms on all 8 PatchTST benchmarks (e.g., 0.455 vs. 0.468 MSE on ETTh1; 0.466 vs. 0.482 on Traffic). Against PRReg (Han et al., IEEE TKDE 2024), xCPD wins across all 10 backbone-dataset settings in the unified training protocol (Table 3), e.g., 0.289 vs. 0.349 MSE on ETTm1 Transformer. Against DUET (Qiu et al., KDD 2024), xCPD further reduces DUET's MSE by 2–4% on six datasets (Table 9). In the ablation, replacing xCPD's spectral filters with TimeFilter's (Hu et al., 2025) spatial-temporal filter yields consistently worse results (e.g., 0.457 vs. 0.445 MSE on ETTh1), confirming the advantage of frequency-domain routing.

## Relation to Prior Work

xCPD extends the channel-partiality (CP) line of work (DUET, CCM, PCD, TimeFilter) by shifting the modeling unit from entire channels to individual channel-patches and moving dependency computation from the temporal domain to the graph spectral domain. It directly adopts the ego-graph decomposition strategy from CrossGNN (Huang et al., 2023) as its graph construction backbone, and draws on graph signal processing (FourierGNN, STGNN) for the spectral embedding and frequency-band partitioning. The Dynamic MoE routing mechanism is inspired by adaptive expert selection in NLP (Shazeer et al., 2017; Huang et al., 2024), repurposed here to select frequency-specific graph filters per patch. Crucially, xCPD departs from prior CP methods by operating as a post-hoc plugin on model outputs rather than modifying the backbone architecture, and by using a shared graph Fourier basis (Theorem 4.1) to ensure cross-batch spectral comparability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, model-agnostic design makes xCPD immediately deployable on top of any existing CI or CD forecaster without retraining, which is practically valuable for production forecasting pipelines where backbone retraining is costly. The spectral-domain formulation provides an interpretable decomposition of channel interactions into low/mid/high-frequency components, addressing a genuine limitation of temporal-domain attention that mixes heterogeneous spectral behaviors. However, zero-shot transfer is currently limited to frequency-aligned settings (e.g., ETTh1→ETTh2), and broader cross-domain generalization remains open. The paper also leaves open whether the shared Fourier basis assumption (Theorem 4.1) holds under strong non-stationarity or regime shifts, and whether the three-band partition is sufficient for signals with more complex spectral structure.
