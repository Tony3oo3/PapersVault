## Problem

Current video LLM-based temporal localization methods fine-tune models to directly generate boundary timestamps, which forces LLMs to produce highly uninformative numeric outputs that conflict with their pre-trained semantic understanding capabilities. This paper asks whether a timestamp-free, semantic-oriented strategy can better exploit video LLMs' innate ability to reason about visual content and temporal structure for localization tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264275/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a T-frame video (encoded via a ViT-G/14 visual encoder and Q-Former resampler into per-frame token sequences) and a natural language query as input. Rather than directly emitting timestamps, the model autoregressively generates an interleaved sequence of structural tokens (<ent> for event segments, <tst> for transitions) and query-focused captions, then performs holistic temporal segmentation by assigning each frame to the structural token with the highest contrastive similarity score. The final event boundary timestamps are derived from this segmentation, and the query-focused captions or event-related answers serve as the generative output for dense captioning and grounded QA tasks.

## Method

MeCo replaces timestamp generation with three complementary objectives: (1) structural token generation, where the LLM partitions the video into temporally ordered <ent> and <tst> tokens based on the query, forcing each token's hidden state to summarize its corresponding segment's semantics through causal attention; (2) query-focused captioning, where the model generates a detailed caption immediately before each <ent> token, providing fine-grained semantic context analogous to chain-of-thought reasoning; and (3) structural token grounding, a contrastive learning module that computes p(h_t | s_i) via a softmax over all T frames for each structural token i, pulling token hidden states toward their corresponding segment frames while pushing them away from all other frames. The total loss is the sum of a standard language modeling loss over the interleaved token sequence and the contrastive grounding loss, trained with LoRA on the E.T.Instruct dataset (164K samples) for one epoch. The asymmetric contrastive design (softmax over frames rather than over tokens) is critical, as it provides sufficient negative samples for effective contrastive learning.

## Results

On E.T. Bench (zero-shot, E.T.Instruct fine-tuning), MeCo (ETChat 3.8B) achieves TVG F1=59.1, EPM F1=11.2, TAL F1=32.6, EVS F1=33.2, VHD F1=66.9, DVC F1=43.4, SLC F1=27.3, TEM Rec=23.6, and GVQ Rec=9.6, outperforming the same-size E.T.Chat baseline (TVG F1=38.6) by up to +20.5 F1 on TVG. Scaling to 7B (QWen2VL), MeCo reaches TVG F1=59.0, EPM F1=17.5, TAL F1=34.2, EVS F1=35.5, and GVQ Rec=15.1. On Charades-STA with dataset-wise fine-tuning, MeCo (QWen2VL 7B) achieves R@1_0.3=82.3, R@1_0.5=68.5, R@1_0.7=41.6, and on QVHighlights mAP=45.3, HIT@1=75.1, surpassing specialist models like CG-DETR (mAP=40.8, HIT@1=66.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MeCo (3.8B) substantially outperforms the same-size E.T.Chat baseline across all 9 E.T. Bench tasks, with the largest gains on TVG (+20.5 F1: 59.1 vs 38.6), EVS (+7.8 F1), TEM (+7.1 Rec), and GVQ (+5.9 Rec). Against the strongest timestamp-generation baselines in the same fine-tuning setting, MeCo (7B) beats TRACE (7B) by +18.2 F1 on TVG (62.5 vs 44.3) and +16.0 F1 on TAL (35.1 vs 19.1), though it trails TRACE by 3.0 F1 on DVC (43.4 vs 46.4) and ties on TEM (19.1 vs 19.2). On Charades-STA with dataset-wise fine-tuning, MeCo (QWen2VL 7B) achieves R@1_0.3=82.3, surpassing VideoChat-T (79.4), UniVTG (72.6), and CG-DETR (70.4), while on QVHighlights it reaches mAP=45.3 and HIT@1=75.1, exceeding CG-DETR (40.8/66.7) and UMT (39.9/64.2). MeCo is the only method that achieves a strong balance between temporal grounding and highlight detection, whereas specialist models like CG-DETR excel on grounding but underperform on highlight detection. The paper notes that MeCo's advantage is most pronounced on semantic-discrimination tasks (TVG, EPM, EVS) while its R@1_0.7 gain is more modest, reflecting a trade-off between semantic understanding and fine-grained boundary modeling.

## Relation to Prior Work

MeCo builds architecturally on E.T.Chat (Liu et al., 2024c), reusing its Q-Former + frame compressor backbone, E.T.Instruct training data, and MLP projector design for the grounding module. However, it fundamentally departs from the dominant timestamp-generation paradigm in temporal localization video LLMs (TimeChat, VTG-LLM, TRACE, VideoChat-T), which all fine-tune LLMs to output boundary timestamps in various encodings. Instead, MeCo replaces timestamp generation with structural token generation (<ent>/<tst>) for holistic temporal segmentation, query-focused captioning for semantic refinement, and a contrastive grounding module that maps structural tokens to video segments — a fully semantic-centric pipeline that frees the LLM from producing uninformative numeric outputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that leveraging LLMs' pre-trained semantic understanding (via structural tokens and contrastive grounding) consistently outperforms all timestamp-generation approaches across 9 tasks and multiple base models, challenging the field's assumption that LLMs must directly generate boundary timestamps for temporal localization. A key open question is the trade-off between semantic-oriented strategies (strong zero-shot generalization, coarse boundaries) and boundary-focused modeling (fine-grained R@1_0.7 performance), which the authors acknowledge as an inherent limitation. Future work on integrating both paradigms — e.g., using semantic grounding for coarse localization followed by boundary refinement — is a natural and promising direction.
