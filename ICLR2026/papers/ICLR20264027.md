## Problem

Existing geometric GNNs for molecular dynamics simulations achieve high in-distribution force accuracy but degrade catastrophically when trajectories visit unseen conformations (e.g., higher temperatures), because their local message-passing topology shifts with atomic distances. This extrapolation failure causes unphysical bond breaking and trajectory instability in real MD runs, a problem that standard energy/force MAE benchmarks do not capture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264027/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a molecular snapshot as a point cloud of N atoms with 3D coordinates (N×3) and time-invariant scalar features such as atomic type or charge (N×h), organized into a geometric graph whose edges are defined by a fixed cutoff radius. It outputs a scalar total energy and per-atom force vectors (N×3) that must be SE(3)-equivariant. Stability is evaluated by running 100 ps NVE (or NVT) Velocity-Verlet simulations and recording the first timestep at which any bond length deviates beyond a threshold from equilibrium.

## Method

GGND is a plug-and-play equivariant diffusion module that operates on a fully-connected geometric graph, independent of the cutoff-defined adjacency, and is combined with a standard local equivariant message-passing backbone (e.g., VisNet, MACE). It models node feature evolution as a PDE ∂Z/∂t = div[S(Z,X,t) ⊙ ∇Z(t)], where the equivariant gradient operator computes higher-order tensor differences between all node pairs, and the equivariant diffusivity operator is a tensor-valued attention matrix built from Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, spherical harmonics on interatomic unit vectors, and learnable radial Bessel functions. Because the diffusion graph is fully connected, information flows between every atomic pair regardless of conformational changes, making the learned representations invariant to geometric topological shifts. Theoretically, the paper proves a regret bound showing representation variation scales as O(ψ(‖ΔÃ‖₂)) for any polynomial ψ under adjacency shifts, in contrast to exponential bounds for local message passing, and establishes SE(3)-equivariance of the full diffusion process.

## Results

On the 3BPA dataset, GGND as a plug-in module dramatically improves stability under severe geometric topological shifts: at 1200 K, VisNet+GGND achieves 11.209 ps stability versus 0.004 ps for VisNet alone (2802× improvement), and MACE+GGND reaches 29.218 ps versus 1.965 ps for MACE (15×). On the SAMD23 benchmark, GGND as a standalone model achieves the best energy-per-atom MAE (0.009 eV for SiN, 0.005 eV for HfO), force MAE (0.443 eV/Å for SiN, 0.179 eV/Å for HfO), and perfect 100 ps stability in Test splits, while maintaining near-perfect OOD stability (99.892 ps for SiN, 97.928 ps for HfO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GGND outperforms all 10 baselines across every metric on both datasets. On SAMD23 SiN (Test), GGND achieves 0.443 eV/Å force MAE, beating the next-best Equiformer V2 (0.451) by ~2% and Neural P³M (0.485) by ~9%, while its 100 ps stability exceeds Equiformer V2's 98.284 ps. On SiN OOD, GGND's 99.892 ps stability surpasses QuinNet (86.512) and Neural P³M (85.888) by over 13 ps, and its 0.754 eV/Å force error is ~10% lower than LSRM (0.832). On HfO, GGND's force MAE of 0.179 eV/Å (Test) is more than 40% lower than Neural P³M (0.311) and QuinNet (0.304), and its OOD force error of 0.279 eV/Å is ~39% below Equiformer V2 (0.683). On 3BPA at 1200 K, GGND as a plug-in transforms VisNet's 0.004 ps stability to 11.209 ps (2802×), MACE's 1.965 ps to 29.218 ps (15×), and SEGNO's 0.009 ps to 16.201 ps (1800×), while also reducing VisNet's energy MAE from 3.464 to 0.583 eV. The only near-tie is at 300 K in-domain, where VisNet and VisNet+GGND both achieve 0.002 eV energy and 0.006 eV/Å force MAE with 100 ps stability, confirming GGND preserves in-domain accuracy without degradation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the equivariant message-passing lineage (EGNN, VisNet) by adding a global diffusion module inspired by the graph heat equation, introducing equivariant gradient and diffusivity operators that operate on a fully-connected graph rather than a local neighborhood. It departs from SEGNO, which addresses generalization through physical inductive biases (second-order motion laws), by formalizing the challenge as a 'geometric topological shift' via a graphon-based data-generating process and providing a regret bound under such shifts. The plug-and-play design explicitly positions GGND as a complement to, rather than a replacement for, existing local equivariant architectures, and the stability evaluation protocol (bond-length deviation in real NVE/NVT MD) extends beyond the pointwise energy/force MAE metrics that dominate prior Geo-GNN benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a critical and under-evaluated failure mode in ML force fields: catastrophic trajectory instability under conformational shifts that pointwise accuracy metrics mask. The plug-and-play design and the theoretical guarantee of arbitrary polynomial-order control over representation variation make the contribution broadly applicable to existing equivariant architectures. However, the quadratic complexity of the fully-connected diffusion limits scalability to large biomolecular systems (proteins with millions of atoms), and the absolute stability at extreme shifts (1200 K) remains far from the 100 ps target, leaving open the question of whether sparse or hierarchical diffusion can preserve the all-pair information flow at scale.
