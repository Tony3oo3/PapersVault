## Problem

Diffusion models have achieved state-of-the-art results in image synthesis but have not been widely translated to time-series forecasting due to the sequential structure of time series and the inherent sampling inefficiency of diffusion models (scaling as O(SH) for S diffusion steps and H forecasting steps). Existing diffusion-based forecasters are largely ad hoc adaptations of image-domain DDPMs that do not fully exploit the multivariate and temporal structure of time-series data, and lack a unified design space for optimizing noise schedules, scale schedules, and integration strategies.

## Input / Output

TEDM takes a z-score-normalized multivariate time series window y_{1:T} ∈ R^{C×T} (C features, T timesteps) and produces a forecast ŷ_{T+1:T+H} for the next H steps. A key structural detail is that the diffusion time axis is identified with the physical time axis: each Euler step of the backward ODE advances exactly one physical timestep, so H forecast steps require H network evaluations rather than H×S. For probabilistic forecasts, the framework supports SDE-based sampling to generate prediction intervals without ensembling.

## Method

TEDM extends the EDM framework to multivariate time series by replacing the scalar noise level σ_t with a matrix-valued noise covariance Σ_t and a per-feature scale s_t, both estimated empirically from the input data window (via cumulative or sliding-window mean/covariance) rather than imposed by a preset schedule. The denoiser is trained with denoising score matching using structured (non-i.i.d.) noise n = Σ^{1/2}ε, and EDM's preconditioning is generalized to matrix-valued Σ to ensure unit-variance inputs/outputs and minimal error amplification. At inference, the backward ODE is integrated with Euler steps where each step advances one physical timestep, reducing sampling complexity from O(SH) to O(H). A diagonal approximation of Σ_t (per-feature variances) makes all matrix operations element-wise, yielding a lightweight inference rule suitable for real-time deployment with O(Td) space complexity.

## Results

On the standard H=96 forecasting benchmark, TEDM achieves the best MSE and MAE on ETTh2 (0.214/0.319), ETTm2 (0.135/0.253), and Exchange (0.069/0.183) among all diffusion-based methods. Against non-diffusion baselines (Table 3), TEDM also leads on ETTh2, ETTm2, Exchange, and Stock, while trailing on ETTh1, ETTm1, and Solar. Inference is extremely efficient: 0.11 s per batch with 23.9 MB memory on ETTm2, compared to 21.4 s and 125 MB for TimeDiff and 635 s and 14.6 GB for DiffusionTS, while delivering the lowest MSE (0.135).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TEDM decisively outperforms all diffusion baselines on ETTh2, ETTm2, and Exchange: e.g., on ETTm2 it achieves MSE 0.135 vs. ARMD 0.181, NsDiff 0.250, TimeDiff 0.209, and DiffusionTS 3.517. On ETTm1, ARMD remains the strongest diffusion method (MSE 0.337 vs. TEDM 0.419), and on ETTh1, TimeDiff leads (0.417 vs. 0.595), which the authors attribute to large-amplitude changes violating TEDM's smooth-flow assumption. Against non-diffusion methods (Table 3), TEDM beats iTransformer, TimesNet, DLinear, PatchTST, and Client on ETTh2, ETTm2, Exchange, and Stock, but trails them on ETTh1, ETTm1, and Solar (where the 137-dimensional feature space stresses the diagonal covariance approximation). In the ablation (Table 4), TEDM with empirical noise/scale schedules improves over the adapted EDM baseline by up to 85% in MSE (Exchange) and 54% (ETTm2), confirming that data-driven schedules are the primary source of gain. In terms of efficiency, TEDM's 0.11 s test time and 23.9 MB memory are comparable to ARMD (0.02 s, 21.3 MB) and orders of magnitude faster than TimeDiff (21.4 s) or DiffusionTS (635 s), while achieving lower MSE than both.

## Relation to Prior Work

TEDM extends the EDM framework (Karras et al., 2022) from image synthesis to multivariate time-series forecasting, generalizing the scalar noise schedule σ_t to a matrix-valued covariance Σ_t and the scalar scale s_t to a vector. It departs from prior diffusion-based forecasters (TimeGrad, TimeDiff, ARMD, NsDiff) in two key ways: (1) it identifies the diffusion process time with the physical time axis of the series, eliminating the need for S iterative denoising steps per forecast step, and (2) it estimates noise and scale schedules empirically from the data window rather than imposing them a priori. The theoretical grounding draws on the Itô SDE formulation of Song et al. (2021) and the preconditioning analysis of EDM, but the commutation-based simplification of the backward ODE to a single Euler step per time step is new to the time-series setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(H) sampling complexity (one Euler step per forecast step instead of S diffusion steps) makes diffusion-based forecasting viable for real-time and online deployment, a regime where prior diffusion methods were impractical. The data-driven schedule estimation removes a source of inductive bias and yields up to 85% MSE improvement over the adapted EDM baseline, validated by structured ablations. However, the diagonal covariance approximation breaks down for high-dimensional feature spaces (Solar, 137 features), and the framework cannot represent long-memory, heavy-tailed, or jump-driven dynamics—limiting its generality. Open questions include whether full-covariance sampling can be made tractable at scale, and whether the physical-time alignment extends to irregularly sampled or multivariate-spatiotemporal settings.
