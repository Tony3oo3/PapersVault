## Problem

Structure-based drug design methods generate 3D ligands with good binding affinity but poor synthesizability, and existing synthesizability metrics (rule-based, retrosynthetic-based, learning-based) show severe inconsistencies when ranking the same molecules, making them unreliable for guiding generation. Moreover, most learning-based metrics ignore 3D conformation, which captures critical factors like bond angles and steric hindrance that determine whether a molecule can actually be synthesized.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265347/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SYNC takes a 3D molecule represented as atom coordinates (x∈R³), atom types (v∈R^d), and bond types (e∈R^{N×N}) and outputs a continuous synthesizability score classifying the molecule as easy-to-synthesize (ES) or hard-to-synthesize (HS). In the classifier-driven SBDD paradigm, the full pipeline takes a protein pocket 3D structure as conditioning and outputs a complete 3D ligand with atom types, coordinates, and bonds. SYNC is designed to be SE(3)-invariant, differentiable, and fast (~1.78s per 10K molecules), enabling it to serve as a plug-and-play gradient source during diffusion sampling without requiring bond type information (bonds are reconstructed via K-nearest neighbors during generation).

## Method

SYNC is an L-layer EGNN that processes 3D molecular graphs through equivariant graph convolutional layers and applies a nonlinear readout to predict synthesizability, achieving SE(3)-invariance by construction. It is trained on ~800K molecules (ES/HS labeled via retrosynthetic planning and curated sources) with 3D conformations, outperforming 8 classical metrics across 5 benchmark datasets while running 100× faster than retrosynthetic methods. Two integration paradigms leverage SYNC as a plug-and-play module: (1) Guided diffusion injects SYNC gradients into both the continuous coordinate update (Eq. 8) and discrete atom type update (Eq. 9) during denoising, using multi-step guidance in the latter half of diffusion to avoid tearing molecules apart; (2) DPO uses SYNC to generate pairwise ES/HS preference data, then fine-tunes the diffusion model via a derived diffusion-DPO loss (Eq. 13) that encourages higher likelihood for synthesizable molecules while preserving binding affinity.

## Results

SYNC achieves the best average rank (2.0) across five synthesizability benchmark datasets, with classification accuracies of 0.9911 (TS1), 0.8406 (TS2), 0.7564 (TS3), 0.9313 (Nonpher-Test), and 0.9520 (Enamine), while running at 1.78 s per 10K molecules—orders of magnitude faster than AizynthFinder (174,192 s). When integrated as a plug-and-play module, TargetDiff-Guide doubles the SYNC synthesizability score from 0.1958 to 0.3977 (+100.3%) and improves Vina docking score from −7.42 to −7.75, while TargetDiff-DPO achieves +21.8% SYNC gain with better inference efficiency. DecompDiff-Guide and DecompDiff-DPO similarly improve synthesizability (+14.7% and +27.3% SYNC) while preserving binding affinity within ±0.25 kcal/mol.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the eight existing synthesizability metrics, SYNC outperforms all on TS1 (0.9911 vs. GASA 0.9850, SA 0.9853), TS2 (0.8406 vs. SA 0.8090), and Nonpher-Test (0.9313 vs. RAScore 0.9250), and is a close second to GASA on TS3 (0.7564 vs. 0.7590). On Enamine, RAScore (0.9890) and SA (0.9844) lead, with SYNC at 0.9520—still competitive but not best. Rule-based metrics (SA, SYBA, SCScore) show severe generalization failures, e.g., SA drops to 0.5667 on TS3 and SCScore to 0.4026 on TS2, while the retrosynthetic AizynthFinder, though accurate on TS1 (0.9535), is 100,000× slower than SYNC. For the SBDD paradigm, TargetDiff-Guide improves over vanilla TargetDiff on all nine synthesizability metrics (e.g., SYNC +100.3%, AizynthFinder +21.4%, SYBA +21.1%) and also improves Vina docking score (−7.42 → −7.75). TargetDiff-DPO similarly improves all nine metrics (SYNC +21.8%) with a smaller but still positive binding gain (−7.42 → −7.58). DecompDiff-Guide achieves universal gains across all nine metrics (AizynthFinder +49.4%, SYBA +45.1%), while DecompDiff-DPO improves 7 of 9 metrics but slightly regresses on RAScore (−9.1%) and GASA (−16.3%), likely due to the larger, more complex molecules DecompDiff generates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of structure-based drug design (SBDD) and synthesizability evaluation, building on the diffusion-based SBDD paradigm (TargetDiff, DecompDiff) and the DPO framework for diffusion models (Wallace et al., 2024; Rafailov et al., 2024). It departs from prior synthesizability metrics (SA, SYBA, GASA, RAScore, etc.) by introducing the first 3D-aware, SE(3)-invariant classifier (SYNC) based on EGNN, and from prior classifier-guided generation by using SYNC to produce preference pairs for DPO rather than serving as a direct reward. The guided diffusion component follows the conditional score approximation from Song et al. (2023) and Wu et al. (2024), adapted to the discrete-continuous hybrid setting of molecular generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstrated inconsistency among eight widely-used synthesizability metrics (e.g., FLAG ranked 1st by some and 9th by others) exposes a fundamental gap in how the field evaluates and guides synthesizable drug design, making this benchmark a necessary reference. The plug-and-play nature of SYNC means any existing SBDD method can adopt it without architectural changes, lowering the barrier to synthesizable generation. Key limitations include the absence of wet-lab validation, evaluation on only two backbone methods, and the reliance on 'easy vs. hard-to-synthesize' labels rather than true synthesizability. Open questions include whether the 3D conformation advantage persists when molecules are re-docked, and whether the paradigm generalizes to larger, more complex targets.
