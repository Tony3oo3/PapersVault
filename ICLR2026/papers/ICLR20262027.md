## Problem

Model-based RL promises strong sample efficiency but often underperforms in practice because standard unimodal world models average over multi-modal dynamics (regression to the mean) and produce overconfident predictions that bias policy learning. Despite numerous MBRL methods, none have consistently surpassed strong model-free baselines on challenging high-dimensional continuous-control tasks. The paper addresses both the multi-modality and uncertainty-quantification gaps simultaneously by extending Implicit Maximum Likelihood Estimation into the MBRL framework.

## Input / Output

The agent receives proprioceptive state vectors of varying dimensionality (e.g., 67-dim for Humanoid, 223-dim for Dog, 51–115-dim for HumanoidBench/MyoSuite tasks) and outputs continuous actions of matching action-space dimension (19–39 dims). The world model consumes a state-action pair plus a latent code z~N(0,I) and produces a single next-state/reward pair in one forward pass (no iterative denoising). An ensemble of 7 models each draws m=4 latent samples per transition, yielding 28 predictions per state-action pair from which a scalar predictive uncertainty is computed.

## Method

WIMLE replaces the standard Gaussian world model with a conditional IMLE generator g_θ(s,a,z) trained via alternating gradient-free latent assignment and SGD updates, ensuring mode coverage without iterative sampling. An ensemble of K=7 IMLE models trained on bootstrap samples provides predictive uncertainty σ(s,a) as the standard deviation across all ensemble-latent predictions, decomposable into epistemic and aleatoric components. Each synthetic transition is weighted by w=1/(σ+1) in the TD critic loss, so uncertain predictions contribute less to gradient updates while the Bellman fixed point remains unchanged (proved via a positive-reweighting lemma). In the linear-critic regime, the paper shows via a Gauss-Markov argument that inverse-variance weighting minimizes parameter covariance, providing a theoretical justification for the sample-efficiency gains. The underlying RL algorithm is SAC with quantile distributional critics, and rollouts of horizon H (task-specific, up to 8) are initialized from real environment states following the MBPO paradigm.

## Results

Across 40 continuous-control tasks, WIMLE achieves the highest IQM at 1M steps on DMC (0.871), Dog & Humanoid (0.897), and HumanoidBench (0.898), outperforming the strongest model-free baseline BRO (0.846, 0.864, 0.530 respectively). On the challenging Humanoid-run task, WIMLE improves sample efficiency by over 50% relative to the strongest competitor. On HumanoidBench, WIMLE solves 8 of 14 tasks versus 4 for BRO and 5 for SimbaV2. On MyoSuite, WIMLE (0.980) is asymptotically on par with SimbaV2 (0.990) and BRO (0.980), which are already near the maximum score of 1.0.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WIMLE outperforms all model-free baselines (MR.Q, SAC, SimBA, SimBAV2, BRO) on the high-dimensional Dog & Humanoid subset (0.897 vs. 0.796–0.864) and the full DMC suite (0.871 vs. 0.830–0.846), with the largest gap over BRO on HumanoidBench (0.898 vs. 0.530). Against model-based baselines, WIMLE dominates DreamerV3 (0.871 vs. 0.286 on DMC) and TD-MPC2 (0.871 vs. 0.696 on DMC; 0.898 vs. 0.734 on HumanoidBench). On MyoSuite, WIMLE (0.980) ties BRO (0.980) and trails SimbaV2 (0.990) by a negligible margin, as all methods are near the 1.0 ceiling. Against InfoProp on Humanoid-run, WIMLE achieves 0.561 IQM at 1M steps versus InfoProp's 0.077, while also requiring less wall-clock time (15.81 h vs. 38.30 h). WIMLE is the only method to solve 8/14 HumanoidBench tasks, compared to 4 for BRO and 5 for SimbaV2.

## Relation to Prior Work

WIMLE extends the MBPO-style model-based RL framework (Janner et al., 2019) by replacing the standard unimodal Gaussian world model with a conditional IMLE generator (Li & Malik, 2018; Li et al., 2020), which provides mode-covering stochastic predictions without iterative sampling. It departs from prior uncertainty-aware MBRL approaches like InfoProp (Frauenknecht et al., 2025), which truncate rollouts based on information-theoretic corruption, by instead integrating a single predictive uncertainty (from an ensemble of IMLE models plus latent sampling) directly into the RL loss as per-transition confidence weights. The theoretical grounding draws on classical heteroscedastic regression and the Gauss-Markov theorem to justify inverse-variance weighting, connecting the practical weight function to a minimum-variance estimator in the linear-critic regime.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This paper provides one of the clearest empirical demonstrations that model-based RL can match or exceed strong model-free baselines (BRO, SimbaV2) on the hardest continuous-control tasks, a gap that has persisted for years. The practical takeaway—that a one-step generative model (IMLE) with ensemble-based uncertainty weighting outperforms both unimodal Gaussian models and diffusion-based world models in online RL—offers a concrete, adoptable recipe for practitioners. Key limitations include reliance on proprioceptive observations only, a task-dependent rollout horizon that is not yet adaptive, and a theoretical analysis restricted to linear critics; extending WIMLE to image-based control and learning to adjust horizon online are natural next steps.
