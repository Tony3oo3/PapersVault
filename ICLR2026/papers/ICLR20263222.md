## Problem

Approximate quantum loaders (AQLs) offer a practical trade-off between state-preparation fidelity and circuit complexity, but existing methods are either heuristic or provide guarantees only for restricted input types, leaving no general theoretical framework to characterize the fundamental limits on approximation error. This gap hinders principled design of AQL algorithms and resource-efficient use of near-term quantum hardware.

## Input / Output

The method accepts either classical data vectors (28×28 MNIST digits, 32×32 CIFAR-10 RGB images, or 1024-dim Sentence-BERT embeddings for SST-2) or quantum states (random-circuit states or 1D transverse-field Ising model ground states up to 50 qubits), each encoded as an N-qubit target state via amplitude or compact encoding. It produces a parameterized quantum gate sequence—composed of iteratively appended two-qubit R_ZZ R_Y R_Z blocks followed by single-qubit R_Z R_Y rotations—that, when applied to |0⟩^⊗N, yields an approximate version of the target state with controllable infidelity.

## Method

The paper first unifies all existing AQL methods (tensor-network and circuit-based) into a single optimization framework and derives information-theoretic bounds showing that the infidelity between the prepared and target states scales linearly with the entanglement measure S, defined as the sum of single-qubit Rényi-2 entropies of U†|v_target⟩. Motivated by this, AQER constructs the loading circuit in three steps: (I) iteratively appending two-qubit gate blocks whose qubit pair and parameters are chosen to minimize S, (II) applying explicitly derived single-qubit rotations to approximate the resulting low-entanglement state as a product state, and (III) fine-tuning all parameters via Adam to minimize infidelity. Using S as the optimization objective in Step I distinguishes AQER from prior variational methods by mitigating barren plateaus, since the initial infidelity is already far from 1 once entanglement is suppressed, ensuring trainability and scalability to 50-qubit systems.

## Results

AQER achieves the lowest infidelity across all five datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, SST-2, S-RQC, GS-TFIM) at every gate count tested (G ∈ {20, 40, 80}). At G=80, AQER reaches infidelity of 0.034 on MNIST, 0.018 on CIFAR-10, 0.406 on SST-2, 0.067 on S-RQC, and 0.003 on GS-TFIM. The most dramatic improvement is on S-RQC, where AQER reduces infidelity by over 60% relative to the second-best method (AQCE) at G=40 and G=80. On GS-TFIM with N=50 qubits, AQER maintains roughly constant infidelity as N grows when T scales linearly with N (T=4N−40), demonstrating favorable scalability.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AQER consistently outperforms all three baselines (MPS, HEC, AQCE) on every dataset at every gate count. Against the strongest baseline AQCE, AQER achieves 33% lower infidelity on MNIST (0.034 vs 0.051 at G=80), 25% lower on CIFAR-10 (0.018 vs 0.024), 22% lower on SST-2 (0.406 vs 0.518), and a striking 75% lower on S-RQC (0.067 vs 0.267). On GS-TFIM, AQER achieves 0.003 infidelity at G=80, more than 18× lower than AQCE (0.056) and 2.3× lower than HEC (0.007), which is the best baseline on that dataset. The MPS baseline, designed for low-entanglement data, performs worst on highly entangled quantum states (S-RQC: 0.605 at G=80) but is competitive on structured classical data like CIFAR-10, where its infidelity (0.049) is within 2× of AQER's (0.018). Overall, AQER's entanglement-reduction strategy provides the most consistent and largest gains on quantum data, while still delivering clear improvements on classical datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the approximate quantum loader (AQL) paradigm introduced by Iaconis and Johri (2023), which trades exact state preparation fidelity for reduced circuit complexity. It unifies two previously separate families of AQL methods—tensor-network-based (MPS) and circuit-based (variational and non-variational like AQCE)—into a single optimization framework, then departs from prior work by establishing the first information-theoretic bounds linking AQL approximation error to a sum of single-qubit Renyi-2 entropies. The proposed AQER method repurposes this entanglement measure as a practical optimization objective, shifting the design principle from direct infidelity minimization to progressive entanglement reduction, which also serves to mitigate barren plateaus during the variational refinement step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The information-theoretic bounds provide the first principled, algorithm-independent characterization of what approximation error is achievable by any AQL, giving the field a theoretical anchor that was previously absent. The entanglement-reduction principle offers a concrete design recipe that scales to 50 qubits while avoiding the trainability failures (barren plateaus) that plague direct variational approaches, making it one of the few AQL methods with demonstrated scalability beyond ~10 qubits. Open questions include whether the bounds are tight for structured state families beyond IQP states, how the method performs under realistic hardware noise (the noisy-channel extension is sketched but not experimentally validated), and whether the T=4N-40 scaling law holds for higher-dimensional or long-range-entangled states.
