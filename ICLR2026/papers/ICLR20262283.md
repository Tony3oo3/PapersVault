## Problem

While Paquette et al. (2024) derived compute-optimal scaling laws for SGD under the power-law random features (PLRF) model, the optimizer actually powering state-of-the-art LLM training—Adam and its variants—remains largely unanalyzed in this framework. Since signSGD is the standard theoretical surrogate for Adam's coordinate-wise adaptivity, the paper asks: how do scaling laws change when SGD is replaced by signSGD, and in which (α,β) regimes does signSGD achieve a strictly steeper compute-optimal slope?

## Input / Output

The method operates on the PLRF model where features x ~ N(0,H) have power-law covariance eigenvalues i^{-2α} and targets y = ⟨x,w*⟩ have power-law coefficients i^{-β}, with a fixed Gaussian sketch S ∈ R^{M×d} (i.i.d. N(0,1/M) entries) projecting to model size M ≤ d. The output is a four-term risk formula R(M,N,γ₀) = A(M) + D_al^sign + D_dis^sign + N^sign whose exponents are phase-dependent on (α,β), together with compute-optimal exponents η(α,β), optimal model size M* = f^{x*}, and optimal learning rate γ₀* = (M*)^{-e*} under the FLOPS budget f = MN. The phase plane is partitioned into six subphases (Aa–Ad, Ba–Bb) with distinct dominant and balancing terms.

## Method

The derivation begins with a one-step signSGD update formula obtained via second-order Taylor expansion and sign-Gaussian identities, converted to a continuous-time ODE and solved via variation-of-constants to yield an implicit integral equation for the risk. Two effects unique to signSGD emerge: (1) drift-normalization, where the 1/√L(k) factor in the drift term self-normalizes the step and accelerates progress when L(k) ≲ 1, steepening the N-decay exponent of the aligned and distorted drift terms relative to SGD; and (2) noise-reshaping, where the sign operation removes the multiplicative L(k) from the quadratic noise term, making the noise independent of N and proportional to γ₀²M^{2−min(1,2α)} rather than decaying with training steps. Under a fixed FLOPS budget f = MN, the paper balances model size and steps while optimizing the learning-rate scaling γ₀ = M^{-e}, identifying regimes where signSGD's noise-reshaping alleviates the SGD noise bottleneck and yields steeper compute-optimal slopes. A warmup-stable-decay (WSD) schedule is further shown to reduce the noise term by decaying the late-stage learning rate while preserving the drift area, sharpening the compute-optimal slope in the upper-left region of Phase Aa where feature decay is fast but target decay is slow.

## Results

The paper derives a four-term scaling law for one-pass signSGD in the PLRF model (Eq. 12), identifying a drift-normalization effect (steeper N-exponents in drift terms) and a noise-reshaping effect (noise term independent of N, scaling as γ₀²M^{2−min(1,2α)}). Under compute-optimal allocation, signSGD achieves steeper loss-decay exponents than SGD in the noise-bottleneck regimes (Area III-IV_sub): e.g., in Phase Ba ∩ Phase III, signSGD gives f^{−(2α+2β−1)/(2α+2β)} while SGD gives f^{−(4α−1)/(4α)}. Warmup-stable-decay (WSD) scheduling further sharpens the slope in Area Aa* (upper-left Phase Aa), yielding exponent −2(4α−1)(2α+2β−1)/(16α²+8αβ+2α−2β−1). Empirically on a Transformer trained on OpenWebText2, AdamW achieves a compute-optimal loss exponent of −0.021 versus −0.005 for SGD, consistent with the theoretical prediction that sign-based optimizers outperform SGD.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SGD (Paquette et al., 2024), signSGD achieves a strictly steeper compute-optimal slope in Area III-IV_sub (covering all of Phase Bb and most of Phases III–IV), where SGD is noise-bottlenecked; in the overlapping Phases I–II and Aa–Ba, both methods yield identical slopes. Against DANA-decaying (Ferbach et al., 2025), signSGD wins in a smaller subset (Phase Ac, Ad, Ba, Bb), since DANA already improves over vanilla SGD. In the empirical Transformer experiment on OpenWebText2, AdamW (a practical surrogate for signSGD) achieves a compute-optimal loss exponent of −0.021 versus −0.005 for SGD, a 4× improvement in decay rate, and the drift-normalization effect is confirmed by a steeper log-log loss slope for AdamW in the drift-dominated regime. The noise-reshaping effect is validated by showing that AdamW's plateau loss increases with larger learning rate (unlike SGD, whose plateau is independent of γ₀). The paper conjectures that Adam with β₂→1 follows the same scaling law as signSGD, supported by matching exponents in Figure 25.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SGD scaling law framework of Paquette et al. (2024) and Lin et al. (2024) from the PLRF model to signSGD, the standard theoretical proxy for Adam. It reuses the PLRF setup (power-law feature and target decay, Gaussian sketch) and the approximation-error term, but re-derives the drift and noise dynamics from scratch, identifying two signSGD-specific effects—drift-normalization (self-normalizing step via 1/√L) and noise-reshaping (loss of the L(k) multiplier in the quadratic noise term). The ODE derivation builds on Xiao et al. (2025) but avoids their spectral lower-bound assumption, and the WSD scheduling analysis extends the constant-LR theory to the schedule widely used in LLM training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a notable gap between scaling-law theory (which has focused on SGD) and the Adam-family optimizers that actually power modern LLM training, providing the first compute-optimal scaling law for signSGD in the PLRF setting. The practical implication is that in noise-bottleneck regimes (Phases III–IV of SGD), signSGD/Adam can achieve steeper compute-optimal slopes than SGD, and WSD scheduling sharpens this further—offering a theoretical justification for the optimizer and schedule choices already standard in practice. Key limitations include the batch-size-1 assumption, the restriction to linear regression in PLRF, and the reliance on a deterministic approximation whose constants are not tightened; extending to minibatches, deeper architectures, and a rigorous (non-heuristic) Adam analysis remain open.
