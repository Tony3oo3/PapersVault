## Problem

Evaluating 3D MRI motion correction methods is fundamentally difficult because ground-truth target data is nearly impossible to obtain in real-world settings: motion-free reference scans are imperfect, simulated motion oversimplifies real artifacts, and reference-free metrics correlate poorly with human perception. No standardized benchmark or reliable metric existed for systematically comparing classical, hybrid, and end-to-end deep learning motion correction approaches on realistic 3D data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260175/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The PMoC3D dataset provides raw undersampled k-space data (222×236×512 acquisition matrix, 52 shots, undersampling factor 4.94, 1 mm isotropic resolution, 256×221×170 mm FOV) rather than pre-processed magnitude images, enabling methods that explicitly estimate motion from k-space. MoMRISim takes a reference-reconstruction image pair (2D slices normalized to 99.9th percentile) and outputs a scalar cosine-distance score where higher values indicate more severe motion artifacts. The VLM score accepts a 3D volume (or a 3×3 grid of sagittal/coronal/axial slices for 2D VLMs) and returns a 0–3 severity rating averaged over five independent prompts at temperature 0.5.

## Method

The paper introduces two key tools. First, MoMRISim is a perceptual similarity metric built by fine-tuning a DINO ViT-B/16 encoder with LoRA adapters (rank 4) using a hinge-ranking triplet loss, where triplets consist of a motion-free L1 reference and two reconstructions at known simulated motion severities drawn from the Calgary Campinas dataset across 7 severity levels and both L1 and 2D U-Net reconstruction pipelines; at evaluation it reduces to a single cosine distance between reference and reconstruction embeddings. Second, the PMoC3D dataset pairs one motion-free scan with three motion-corrupted scans per subject (8 subjects, 27 corrupted scans total), acquired with a random quasi-random k-space trajectory across 52 shots to ensure every shot samples both low- and high-frequency components, and annotated with a Perceived Motion Artifact Score (PMAS) derived from a Bradley-Terry model fitted to pairwise human comparisons. The paper then systematically benchmarks three evaluation paradigms—real-world paired (PMoC3D + MoMRISim), simulated motion (Calgary Campinas with event-based rigid motion), and reference-free (VLM scores from GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL-Max, Med3DVLM, M3D-LaMed)—against three reconstruction baselines (AltOpt, MotionTTT, E2E Stacked U-Net), finding that real-world paired evaluation with MoMRISim achieves the highest correlation with expert judgment (Spearman ρ = 0.92) while simulated evaluation overestimates performance and reference-free metrics bias toward oversmoothed outputs.

## Results

MoMRISim achieves the highest Spearman rank correlation with human-judged Perceived Motion Artifact Score (PMAS) at 0.92, substantially outperforming PSNR (moderate correlation) and the VLM score from GPT-4o (low correlation). Within-method correlations for MoMRISim remain consistently high: 0.87 for AltOpt, 0.81 for MotionTTT, and 0.77 for Stacked U-Net, confirming it captures motion severity rather than method-specific variation. Inter-rater reliability of PMAS itself is confirmed at 0.94 Spearman correlation between the medical doctor and a second rater. The paper concludes that real-world paired evaluation with MoMRISim is the most reliable approach for moderate-to-severe motion, while simulated motion evaluation systematically overestimates algorithm performance and reference-free metrics are biased toward oversmoothed deep learning outputs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among reference-based metrics, MoMRISim (Spearman ρ = 0.92 with PMAS) clearly outperforms pixel-wise metrics (PSNR, SSIM, AP — all showing only moderate correlation) and other feature-space metrics (DISTS, DreamSim — high but lower correlation; DISTS also exhibits a failure mode with stacked U-net reconstructions). Among reference-free metrics, the VLM score from GPT-4o achieves the best (still poor) alignment with PMAS, significantly outperforming classical gradient-based metrics TG and AES, yet all reference-free metrics remain unreliable and systematically overrate oversmoothed deep learning outputs. In terms of reconstruction methods evaluated on PMoC3D, all three baselines (AltOpt, MotionTTT, E2E Stacked U-Net) show noticeably worse reconstructions on real-world severe motion compared to simulated severe motion, where they appear nearly artifact-free — demonstrating that simulated evaluation overestimates performance. The E2E Stacked U-Net is particularly susceptible to reference-free metric bias, receiving high VLM scores despite substantial anatomical detail loss visible in difference images. For mild motion, even the motion-free L1 reference can appear worse than a motion-corrected reconstruction (e.g., S3_3 with MotionTTT), undermining reference-based evaluation in that regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of MRI motion correction and evaluation methodology, building directly on DreamSim's triplet-based perceptual metric training paradigm but repurposing it with motion severity as a self-supervised signal rather than human-labeled natural-image triplets. It departs from the dominant evaluation practice in the field—which relies on simulated rigid motion applied to fully-sampled data (Cordero-Grande et al., 2016; Klug et al., 2024; Al-Masni et al., 2022)—by introducing a real-world paired dataset (PMoC3D) with raw k-space data and systematically demonstrating the limitations of simulation-based evaluation. The work also extends the line of inquiry from Marchetto et al. (2024) on metric-radiologist agreement by proposing MoMRISim, a domain-specific feature-space metric, and by incorporating VLM-based reference-free scoring as a modern alternative to classical gradient metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap: the MRI motion correction community has lacked a standardized, realistic evaluation protocol, and the finding that simulated motion systematically overestimates algorithm performance (methods appear nearly error-free under simulation but retain visible artifacts on real data) has direct implications for how progress is reported in the field. The PMoC3D dataset, providing raw undersampled k-space with paired motion-free references, is a practical resource that prior datasets (which only offered processed magnitude images) did not. Key limitations remain: the dataset is small (8 subjects, 27 corrupted scans), the mild-motion regime still undermines reference-based evaluation (corrected images can outperform the reference), non-rigid motion is not addressed, and the VLM-based reference-free score, while better than gradient metrics, still exhibits bias toward oversmoothed outputs—leaving the field without a fully reliable evaluation in all motion regimes.
