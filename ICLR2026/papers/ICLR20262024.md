## Problem

Existing predictive coding models are restricted to a single inference mode: discriminative PC (discPC) excels at classification but cannot generate images, while generative PC (genPC) produces class-conditional samples but performs poorly on supervised tasks. Although the brain appears to employ both modes simultaneously, prior hybrid approaches such as hybridPC sacrifice performance in at least one domain, leaving open how a single biologically plausible PC circuit could flexibly support both generative and discriminative inference.

## Input / Output

The model accepts 28×28 grayscale images (MNIST, Fashion-MNIST), 32×32 RGB images (CIFAR-10/100), or 64×64 RGB images (Tiny-ImageNet), optionally paired with one-hot class labels. In supervised mode it outputs a class prediction; in unsupervised mode it outputs a low-dimensional latent representation (30–256 neurons) from which images can be reconstructed; in a mixed mode it jointly infers labels and complementary representations. A distinctive input condition is partially occluded images where up to 90% of pixels are masked and left unclamped, requiring the model to infer missing values before classifying.

## Method

bPC unifies the generative and discriminative energy functions into a single objective E(x,W,V) that sums top-down prediction errors (weighted by α_gen) and bottom-up prediction errors (weighted by α_disc) across all layers. Each layer maintains separate top-down weights W and bottom-up weights V, with two distinct error neurons per value neuron (one per direction), all updated via fully local Hebbian plasticity. Inference begins with a feedforward bottom-up sweep to initialise activities (amortised inference), followed by iterative gradient descent on the combined energy. The key insight is that shared latent layers under both prediction directions regularise each other: the discriminative pathway sharpens minima around valid inputs while the generative pathway anchors them to specific data points, preventing discPC's overconfident broad minima and genPC's mean-collapsing narrow minima. This yields an energy landscape with well-localised, class-specific minima that supports both accurate classification and realistic generation within a single circuit using half the value neurons of maintaining separate unidirectional pathways.

## Results

On MNIST, bPC achieves 98.10% classification accuracy (matching discPC's 98.43% and BP's 98.48%) while simultaneously generating class-conditional images with RMSE 0.0581 (close to genPC's 0.0198, far better than discPC's 0.3133). On Fashion-MNIST, bPC reaches 89.24% accuracy with generation RMSE 0.0415. In the combined supervised-unsupervised setting on CIFAR-10, bPC achieves 85.06% classification accuracy versus only 37.20% for hybridPC, while maintaining comparable reconstruction quality (RMSE 0.3036 vs 0.3003). For distribution learning, bPC achieves FID 3.34 at 250 inference steps, outperforming genPC (4.56) and VAE (5.79).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against discPC (Whittington & Bogacz, 2017), bPC matches classification accuracy (98.10% vs 98.43% on MNIST) but dramatically outperforms on generation (RMSE 0.0581 vs 0.3133), confirming that discPC's non-unique generative dynamics produce nonsensical images. Against genPC (Rao & Ballard, 1999), bPC substantially outperforms on classification (98.10% vs 83.48% on MNIST) while genPC retains a slight edge on generation RMSE (0.0198 vs 0.0581), reflecting genPC's narrower energy minima around class means. Against hybridPC (Tscshantz et al., 2023), bPC wins decisively on classification across all settings—most strikingly on CIFAR-10 in the combined task where bPC reaches 85.06% versus hybridPC's 37.20%—while matching or slightly exceeding hybridPC on generation RMSE. Against the VAE baseline (Kingma et al., 2013) for distribution learning, bPC achieves a lower FID (3.34 vs 5.79 at 250 updates), indicating bPC learns a better data distribution with stochastic predictive coding dynamics than a standard VAE of matched architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate predictive coding formulations—discriminative PC (Whittington & Bogacz, 2017), which uses bottom-up feedforward predictions, and generative PC (Rao & Ballard, 1999), which uses top-down predictions—into a single energy function where both pathways actively participate in iterative inference. It departs from the closest prior hybrid, hybridPC (Tscshantz et al., 2023), in which the bottom-up pathway only provides feedforward initialization and does not influence subsequent dynamics; in bPC, bottom-up prediction errors continuously shape latent activity throughout inference. The paper also distinguishes itself from Qiu et al. (2023), who proposed shared weights between the two directions, by maintaining separate top-down and bottom-up parameters as a biologically more plausible design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

bPC provides a mechanistic explanation—via energy landscape analysis on XOR and MNIST—for why bidirectional predictions outperform unidirectional ones: they regularize each other, preventing the overconfident broad minima of discPC and the mean-collapsing narrow minima of genPC. This offers a concrete hypothesis for how cortical circuits might flexibly support both rapid feedforward processing and Bayesian integration. Limitations include max-pooling artefacts in the generative pathway that degrade reconstruction quality on CIFAR, and the approach has been validated primarily on relatively small-scale benchmarks (up to VGG-16 on Tiny-ImageNet). Open questions include whether the energy-landscape benefits persist at ImageNet scale, how to eliminate generative artefacts without sacrificing discriminative accuracy, and whether the fixed α_gen/α_disc weighting can be made fully learnable.
