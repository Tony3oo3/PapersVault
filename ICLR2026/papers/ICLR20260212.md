## Problem

Softmax has been the de facto router function in Mixture-of-Experts models since the earliest architectures, yet its dominance rests on an unchallenged assumption rather than a principled design choice. The paper asks whether the probability-simplex constraint and exponential activation are truly necessary, or whether a more natural formulation exists that better aligns with standard deep learning practices.

## Input / Output

The router takes the token hidden representation Φ(x) ∈ R^d and produces per-expert routing weights that are NOT constrained to the probability simplex; their magnitude is governed by an ℓ₂-norm constraint and a single learnable scalar γ rather than by summing to one. The final MoE output is the weighted sum of top-k expert outputs, where the weight scale is invariant to the total number of experts M, unlike Softmax where weights shrink as M grows.

## Method

The paper shows that MoE, FFN, and Nadaraya-Watson regression share an identical mathematical structure: a kernel function assigns weights to value functions, which are then aggregated. Motivated by this equivalence, the authors propose KERN (Kernel Inspired Router with Normalization), an FFN-style router that applies a linear projection, ℓ₂-normalization, ReLU activation, and a learnable global scale to the router logits. This formulation generalizes both Softmax (ℓ₁-normalization + exponential activation) and Sigmoid (no normalization + sigmoid activation) as special cases. The ℓ₂-normalization keeps the MoE output variance constant regardless of expert count, while ReLU preserves sparsity and avoids the gradient saturation that plagues exponential-type activations, all at zero additional parameter cost.

## Results

On downstream benchmarks (ARC, HellaSwag, PIQA, ScIQ, WinoGrande) after pretraining on FineWeb-Edu, KERN achieves the best average accuracy at all three model scales: 52.14 (520M/125M active), 55.13 (1.7B/350M active), and 58.88 (6.9B/1.3B active). On language modeling, KERN achieves the lowest validation loss on Books3 (3.3080 at 125M active, 512-length) and Arxiv (1.8291), outperforming Softmax (3.3882 and 1.8781 respectively) and the Dense baseline. The KERN–Softmax accuracy gap (2.26–2.67 points) is comparable to or larger than the Softmax–Dense gap (0.38–1.37 points), indicating KERN captures most of the MoE benefit at zero additional cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KERN consistently outperforms the Softmax router (Shazeer et al., 2017) across all model scales, datasets, and sparsity levels. On the main downstream evaluation, KERN beats Softmax by 2.26 points (520M), 2.67 points (1.7B), and 2.39 points (6.9B) in average accuracy. Against the Sigmoid router (Nguyen et al., 2024), KERN leads by a smaller but consistent margin of 0.34–0.37 points in average accuracy. On language modeling loss (Books3, 125M active, 512-length), KERN achieves 3.3080 versus Softmax's 3.3882 and Sigmoid's 3.3206. The advantage of KERN over Softmax is maintained across varying numbers of active experts (4–32), total expert counts (32–256), and context lengths (512–2048), and KERN also achieves the lowest KL divergence from uniform expert load (0.2947 vs. Softmax's 0.9483), indicating better expert utilization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes MoE routing through the classical Nadaraya-Watson kernel regression estimator, showing that both FFN output layers and MoE expert aggregation are special cases of parametric NW regression where the kernel function corresponds to the input neurons of the output layer. It builds directly on the sparsely-gated MoE architecture of Shazeer et al. (2017) and the recent Sigmoid-routing results of Nguyen et al. (2024) and DeepSeek, but departs from both by arguing that the router should follow standard FFN design conventions (ReLU activation, ℓ₂-normalization) rather than exponential-family activations. The key conceptual departure is treating the Softmax router not as a principled probability simplex constraint but as an atypical FFN configuration, motivating the KERN router as a more natural and general instantiation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a zero-cost FFN-style router (ReLU + ℓ₂-norm) consistently outperforms Softmax across model scales (125M–1.3B active), context lengths, sparsity levels, and downstream benchmarks challenges the field's unexamined assumption that Softmax is the correct routing mechanism. The observation that the KERN-to-Softmax performance gap (2.3–2.7 points) exceeds the Softmax-to-Dense gap (0.4–1.4 points) suggests router design has been underappreciated as a source of MoE inefficiency. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at trillion-parameter scale, with more aggressive training recipes, and whether the NW-regression framing can inform other kernel-based aggregation mechanisms beyond MoE.
