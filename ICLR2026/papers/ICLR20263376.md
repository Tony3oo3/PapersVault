## Problem

LLMs respond to every prompt regardless of whether they possess the knowledge or reasoning capability to do so correctly, leading to hallucinations that are especially dangerous in high-stakes domains like medicine and law. Prior approaches to mitigate this either require additional test-time computation (post-processing, sampling, conformal prediction), only address knowledge retrieval without extending to multi-step reasoning, or do not allow practitioners to tune the trade-off between response completeness and correctness. HALT addresses this gap by producing a finetuned model that inherently generates only content within its own capability bounds, with no extra computation at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained LLM and a standard finetuning dataset of (prompt, ground-truth response) pairs, and produces a modified finetuning dataset where each target response is truncated or annotated at the point where the model's capability is exceeded. At inference time, the finetuned model receives a text prompt and outputs a partial response that may terminate with the marker 'Unsure from here' (for causally dependent tasks like math or code) or simply omit uncertain atomic statements (for independent-fragment tasks like biographies). An optional Add-On variant instead annotates uncertain fragments with 'Uncertain' while retaining the full response length, letting users see and verify the uncertain portions.

## Method

HALT constructs capability-aligned finetuning data through a four-step pipeline: (1) generate preliminary responses via few-shot prompting of the pretrained LLM, (2) split each response into factual fragments using either a fact-extraction LLM (for independent-fragment tasks like Wikipedia biographies) or natural boundary splitting (for causally dependent tasks like math steps or code lines), (3) assess fragment correctness with an evaluator LLM (Llama3-405B) conditioned on ground-truth information, and (4) post-process by removing incorrect fragments or replacing everything from the first error onward with 'Unsure from here'. The key tunable mechanism is a threshold α: multiple preliminary responses are sampled per prompt, sorted by fragment correctness, and the α-percentile response is selected before post-processing, allowing practitioners to trade completeness for correctness. The resulting dataset is used for standard supervised finetuning with LoRA (rank 256), and no additional computation is required at test time. The approach is grounded in the observation that LLMs do not acquire new capabilities during finetuning but only learn to express pretraining knowledge, so training on content beyond the model's capability actively increases hallucination.

## Results

HALT increases the mean correctness of response fragments by 15% on average across four domains (Wikipedia biographies, MATH, MedExQA, APPS coding) and four open-source LLMs (Llama3-8B, Llama3-70B, Gemma2-9B, Mistral-7B). Tuning HALT for highest correctness (α=40%) on a single Llama3-70B model trained on all four domains yields 87% average fragment correctness versus 51% for standard finetuning, while retaining 25% response completeness. HALT improves the F1 score (harmonic mean of completeness and correctness) by an average of 4% over all baselines, with the largest gains when the desired fragment count is set to the model's capability limit (n_capable), where HALT achieves F1 scores of 0.68–0.88 for Llama3-70B versus 0.26–0.88 for the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HALT outperforms the IDK abstention baseline (Cheng et al., 2024) by large margins on nearly all model–dataset combinations: for Llama3-70B, HALT achieves F1 of 0.68 on Wikipedia versus 0.44 for IDK, and 0.83 on Coding versus 0.48, with IDK collapsing to 0.04–0.18 on Coding for smaller models. Against FactTune (Tian et al., 2023a), which is only applicable to the Wikipedia knowledge task, HALT achieves 0.63 F1 for Llama3-8B versus 0.38 for FactTune, and 0.68 for Mistral-7B versus 0.44. The RandomTrim heuristic baseline, which removes a random number of trailing fragments to match HALT's response length, is consistently outperformed by HALT on Wikipedia (e.g., 0.68 vs. 0.26 for Llama3-70B) and Math, though it occasionally matches or slightly exceeds HALT on Medical QA and Coding where responses are short. Standard finetuning (Unchanged) is surpassed by HALT on Wikipedia and Math for all models but is competitive on Medical QA, where the pretrained models already achieve high correctness (0.80–0.88 F1). Overall, HALT achieves the highest F1 in 15 out of 16 model–dataset cells (excluding the one Medical QA cell where RandomTrim ties), confirming that capability-aligned data curation is more effective than both full abstention and preference-based factuality training.

## Relation to Prior Work

HALT is motivated by two converging empirical findings: that LLMs do not acquire novel capabilities during finetuning (Lin et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2024) and that finetuning on examples outside the model's knowledge increases hallucination (Kang et al., 2024; Gekhman et al., 2024). It departs from prior abstention work (IDK, Cheng et al., 2024) and factuality work (FactTune, Tian et al., 2023a) by producing *partial* responses—selectively retaining only fragments within the model's capability—rather than binary abstain/answer decisions, and by introducing a tunable threshold for the completeness–correctness trade-off. The fragmentation step for knowledge tasks leverages the atomic-statement extraction LLM from Song et al. (2024), while the overall pipeline is a new data-curation procedure that requires no test-time computation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For high-stakes deployments (medicine, law, coding), the ability to train a single model that achieves 87% fragment correctness across four diverse domains—versus 51% with standard finetuning—while retaining over half the response completeness is a practically significant step toward trustworthy LLMs. The tunable threshold gives practitioners a deployment-time knob without retraining, and the Add-On variant (marking 'Uncertain' instead of omitting) preserves full response length for human-in-the-loop editing. Key limitations remain: the method assumes fragments are either independent or linearly causally dependent (no dependency graphs), uses binary correctness rather than graded confidence, and requires a separate finetuned model per threshold setting rather than a single adaptive model. Extending to multi-modal domains and fine-grained capability scaling are natural next steps.
