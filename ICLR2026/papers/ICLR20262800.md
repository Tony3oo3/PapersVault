## Problem

While the visual cortex is known to establish priors reflecting natural image statistics, it remains unclear whether task-specific contextual priors can be flexibly acquired and deployed as early as V1, the entry level of the visual hierarchy. This paper tests the hypothesis that systematic response biases observed in V1 of task-trained mice arise from probabilistic inference under a learned task prior, and asks whether such priors can be acquired efficiently without retraining the underlying representation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262800/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 40×40 grayscale image patches: whitened crops from the van Hateren database for the base VAE training, and synthetic oriented sinusoidal gratings (spatial frequency 3, 50 phases per orientation to emulate motion, contrast 0.3 for testing) for task prior fitting. The output is a 1799-dimensional latent posterior whose absolute values serve as firing-rate-like responses for each putative V1 neuron; these are then aggregated into population response profiles by binning neurons into 5° orientation bins ordered by preferred orientation, directly comparable to calcium imaging recordings from 10 mice across 6 sessions.

## Method

TAVAE extends the standard VAE by freezing both the linear generative model p(x|z) and the amortized recognition model q(z|x) learned on natural images, and introducing only a task-specific latent prior p_T(z) — a zero-mean Laplace distribution with per-dimension scale parameters σ_T. The task posterior is obtained analytically via Bayes' rule as q_T(z|x) ∝ q(z|x)·p_T(z)/p_0(z), requiring no retraining of the original networks. The scales σ_T are learned by maximizing the task log-likelihood, which reduces to a self-consistency equation solved iteratively (converging in ~5 iterations). The base VAE uses a linear generative model with a 1799-dimensional overcomplete latent space, Laplace latent prior, and a Gaussian Scale Mixture scaling latent to capture contrast-dependent V1 responses. The entire model is trained normatively on natural images and task stimuli only — never on neural recordings — and its predictions are validated against population response profiles from mouse V1 calcium imaging during a Go-NoGo orientation discrimination task.

## Results

The best TAVAE configuration (prior learned on 45° Go and 90° NoGo stimuli) achieves a Pearson correlation of r = 0.78 ± 0.02 with experimental V1 population response profiles across D1–D6, substantially exceeding the task-general VAE (r = 0.53 ± 0.12). For the task-modulation signal (difference between task-engaged and naive conditions), TAVAE(45,90) reaches r = 0.58 ± 0.09, while the VAE has no meaningful modulation to correlate. TAVAE also reproduces key qualitative signatures: sharpening of orientation tuning peaks, baseline activity reduction, and bimodal population responses with a trough at the actual stimulus orientation when the contextual prior mismatches the observation. Centered Kernel Alignment (CKA = 0.86 vs. 0.82) and Neural Predictivity (0.804 vs. 0.777, p < 10⁻⁴) further confirm TAVAE's superior alignment with trained-animal data over the VAE control.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods; all comparisons are internal model variants. TAVAE with a 45°/90° prior (the configuration matching the animal's adapted prior after D2) outperforms the task-general VAE by 0.25 in absolute correlation on raw task responses (0.78 vs. 0.53) and is the only configuration that captures the task-modulation signal (r = 0.58 vs. no modulation for VAE). The alternative TAVAE(45°,135°) prior, which retains the original training orientations, drops to r = 0.54, confirming that the animal's prior shifted after the NoGo stimulus changed on D2. The 'eager adapter' variant, which re-learns the prior every day to match the current stimulus, performs worst on modulation (r = −0.10), indicating that the animal does not fully update its prior daily. On CKA and Neural Predictivity metrics, TAVAE also consistently exceeds the VAE control on trained data (CKA 0.86 vs. 0.82; NP 0.804 vs. 0.777, p < 10⁻⁴), while the VAE matches TAVAE on naive data as expected.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the VAE-based modeling of V1 (Geadah et al., 2024; Catoni et al., 2024) by introducing the Task-Amortized VAE (TAVAE), which learns task-specific latent priors without retraining the recognition model or generative model. It departs from hierarchical VAEs that derive top-down contextual priors from natural image statistics (Csikor et al., 2025) by showing that a simpler, task-learned prior over the same latent space can account for task-induced response biases. The formalism also connects to the broader probabilistic-inference account of cortical computation (Lee & Mumford, 2003; Orbán et al., 2016) by making the prior explicitly task-dependent and learnable from limited task observations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a mechanistic, normative explanation for task-specific biases in V1 that had been observed empirically (Corbo et al., 2022, 2025) but lacked a computational account. The prediction that prior-stimulus mismatch produces bimodal population responses with a trough at the stimulus orientation is a testable signature of probabilistic inference under uncertainty that matches the data. Open questions include how task priors integrate with natural-image priors in a unified hierarchy, whether the simple zero-mean Laplace prior form generalizes to richer task structures, and whether the TAVAE algorithmic solution maps onto specific laminar circuitry (e.g., layer 4 preserving the recognition model while layer 2/3 integrates top-down task signals).
