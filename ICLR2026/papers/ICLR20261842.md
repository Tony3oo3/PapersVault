## Problem

Next token prediction, the standard LLM pretraining objective, operates on superficial token-level signals (e.g., function words) and requires substantial training for models to acquire high-level conceptual understanding and long-horizon reasoning. The paper asks whether explicitly modeling semantic concepts in a latent representation space can augment NTP, bridging the gap between abstract reasoning and fine-grained token-level guidance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261842/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a text token sequence and, at a chosen intermediate layer, produces both the standard next-token distribution and a continuous concept vector. The concept vector is derived by first predicting TopK-sparse concept indices (K=32 out of 32,768 SAE dimensions) via a linear head, then projecting them through a learnable d×C matrix into a dense d-dimensional embedding. This continuous concept is interleaved between token hidden representations, effectively doubling the sequence length fed to the remaining transformer blocks. Concept labels are precomputed from a 124M GPT-2 teacher's hidden states using a pretrained TopK SAE, with the top-K_attr=4 concepts selected by attribution score (gradient × input) serving as discrete cross-entropy targets.

## Method

CoCoMix augments NTP with a two-part concept pipeline: (1) a concept prediction head trained with cross-entropy to predict attribution-selected SAE concept indices from the model's own hidden state, and (2) a compression layer that maps the TopK-sparse prediction logits into a continuous concept vector, which is interleaved with token hidden representations before the remaining transformer layers. The training objective combines the standard NTP loss with a weighted concept prediction loss (λ=0.1). The key architectural choice is interleaving (inserting the concept as a separate token in the sequence) rather than adding it to the hidden state, which lets the model process concepts as independent information units. Ablations show both the prediction loss and the interleaving are necessary for gains, and that attribution-based concept selection outperforms raw SAE activation selection by 17.5% in sample efficiency. The compression layer's learned weights naturally become sparse, allowing the model to ignore irrelevant concepts extracted from a weaker teacher, which underpins its weak-to-strong supervision capability.

## Results

At the 1.38B scale, CoCoMix achieves 18.1 average PPL and 49.7 average accuracy across seven downstream tasks, outperforming NTP (18.6 PPL, 48.7 Acc) and KD (18.5 PPL, 49.1 Acc). At 386M, the gap widens substantially: CoCoMix reaches 21.4 Avg PPL versus 24.2 for NTP and 23.4 for KD, with 47.5 Avg Acc. CoCoMix also demonstrates strong sample efficiency, matching NTP's 200B-token perplexity (22.7) with only 140.7B tokens (21.5% fewer), and in the weak-to-strong setting (124M teacher → 386M student) it improves average PPL by 2.8 over NTP while KD shows negligible gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard next-token prediction (NTP, Radford et al. 2019), CoCoMix wins on every model size and nearly every individual benchmark: at 386M it cuts LAMBADA PPL from 26.3 to 19.3 (−27%) and lifts WinoGrande accuracy from 50.9 to 52.3; at 1.38B it reduces OpenWebText PPL from 14.3 to 13.9 and WinoGrande from 50.0 to 53.0. Against knowledge distillation (KD, Hinton et al. 2015), CoCoMix is consistently superior, with the largest margin in the weak-to-strong regime (124M teacher → 386M student) where CoCoMix achieves 21.4 Avg PPL versus KD's 23.4, while KD actually underperforms NTP on some tasks (e.g., PIQA 63.6 vs. 64.1). Against the Pause token baseline (Goyal et al. 2024), CoCoMix achieves lower validation PPL (24.7 vs. 25.1 at 69M) with fewer FLOPs (3.37×10¹⁸ vs. 3.48×10¹⁸), and the gap persists at scale where CoCoMix at 140.7B tokens matches NTP at 200B tokens using 17.8% fewer FLOPs.

## Relation to Prior Work

CoCoMix repurposes Sparse Autoencoders—previously used in mechanistic interpretability for post-hoc feature analysis (Bricken et al., 2023; Templeton et al., 2024)—as a *training signal* during pretraining, which the authors note is the first application of SAEs to LLM pretraining. Conceptually, it sits between knowledge distillation (matching a teacher's output distribution) and latent-reasoning approaches (Hao et al., 2024; Goyal et al., 2024), but departs from both by predicting discrete concept labels extracted via attribution scores and interleaving a compressed continuous concept vector into the hidden state, rather than matching logits or inserting fixed pause tokens. The interleaving design is explicitly contrasted with representation-engineering interventions (Zou et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2024), which add a concept vector to the hidden state; CoCoMix instead treats the concept as a separate sequential unit, letting the model learn how to mix it with token representations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most notable finding is that concepts extracted from a 124M model can effectively supervise a 1.38B model in a weak-to-strong setting where standard KD from the same teacher fails, suggesting that selective concept-level transfer (via attribution-based filtering) is more robust than full-distribution matching when the student surpasses the teacher. The 21.5% token-efficiency gain and the built-in interpretability/steerability (probing and amplifying individual concept logits to control generation) offer a practical path toward more transparent pretraining. Key open questions include whether the approach scales beyond academic parameter counts (≤1.38B), whether concepts can be learned end-to-end without a separate pretrained SAE, and whether the two-stage dependency (pretrained model → SAE → CoCoMix training) can be eliminated.
