## Problem

The dominant approach to aggregating multiple LLM generations, Best-of-N (BON), treats candidates as competitors in a zero-sum game: it selects a single winner and discards all others, bounding the final output's quality by the best sample in the pool. This hard selection step wastes the diversity of reasoning paths across candidates, risks reward hacking, and cannot produce an answer that exceeds the original sample pool. The paper asks whether aggregation can be reframed as a synthesis problem that productively integrates complementary strengths across all candidates rather than merely picking one.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt x and a pool of N candidate generations Y (sampled from one model at test-time or from multiple teacher models for synthetic data) and produces a single fused generation y* that is guaranteed to be outside the original pool (y* ∉ Y). The fusor receives all N generations simultaneously (randomly shuffled and enumerated, one per line) along with the original instruction, and is prompted to first write a short comparison of strengths and weaknesses, then emit the fused answer inside double brackets. No format or length constraints are imposed on the fused output beyond naturalness and task relevance, and the same English-language fusion prompt is used across all tasks, languages, and fusor models.

## Method

FUSION replaces the argmax selection step of BON with a single generative synthesis step: a sufficiently large LLM (the 'fusor') is prompted to compare all N candidates and then produce a new generation that 'mixes and matches' the highest-quality fragments (tokens, sentences, reasoning steps) from each while discarding the weakest parts. The key insight is a 'polylithic' view of quality—acknowledging that each sample contains both high- and low-quality segments—so the fusor can integrate complementary signals that no single candidate possesses in full. No specialized training is required; the method works out-of-the-box with a general-purpose LLM (e.g., Command A at 111B), though a size threshold exists below which the fusor needs task-specific fine-tuning. The fusion prompt supports in-context adaptation (e.g., safety constitutions, tone control) and can be extended with chain-of-thought or reasoning-model fusors to scale compute. FUSION subsumes BON as a special case: the fusor retains the option to copy one entire generation verbatim if it dominates all others across the full sequence.

## Results

In test-time scaling, FUSION achieves a +2.5% average win-rate improvement over BON on mArenaHard V.2 (46.3% vs 43.8% against GEMINI2.5-PRO) and +0.8 XCOMETXL on WMT24++ (83.8 vs 83.0), even surpassing the Oracle selection in German, Russian, and Chinese translation. In synthetic data generation, fine-tuning on FUSION data yields +2.5% Arena win-rate and +0.8 XCOMETXL over BON-fine-tuned models, with the FUSION-fine-tuned 111B model outperforming the fusor (Command A) by +4.4% on GeoFactX answer correctness (58.6% vs 40.5%). FUSION is also more sample-efficient, achieving +6% win-rate gain at N=2 where BON requires N=4 for comparable improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FUSION consistently outperforms BON across all evaluated tasks and languages: +2.5% Arena win-rate, +0.8 XCOMETXL on WMT, and +1.0% GeoFactX answer accuracy. Against the Oracle (best sample by ground-truth score), FUSION surpasses it in German (+0.8), Russian, and Chinese WMT translation, demonstrating that selection is not the upper bound. Among the five individual teacher models (Command A, DeepSeek-V3, Qwen3, Gemma3-27B, Kimi-K2), FUSION achieves the highest GeoFactX answer correctness at 58.6%, exceeding the best teacher (DeepSeek-V3 at 48.89%) by nearly 10 points and the fusor Command A (40.52%) by 4.4%. FUSION also obtains the highest internal reward score (7.86 vs 6.64 for the best teacher Kimi-K2), confirming it is not ceiling-limited by the fusor. On MGSM math reasoning, FUSION is roughly on par with BON (85.1% vs 84.0% on MMATH), with the gap being smaller than in open-ended or translation tasks, suggesting tightly constrained tasks benefit less from generative synthesis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reframes the well-established task of aggregating multiple LLM generations (Best-of-N, self-consistency, majority voting) from a selection problem into a synthesis problem. It is conceptually related to Mixture-of-Agents (Wang et al., 2024) and LLM-Blender (Jiang et al., 2023), but distinguishes itself by requiring only a single generative LLM call with no specialized training, pairwise ranking modules, or multi-layer iterative refinement. Unlike concurrent works on solution aggregation (Qi et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025b) that train dedicated aggregator models for verifiable domains like math and code, FUSION operates out-of-the-box via prompting and targets open-ended, multilingual settings where reward-model alignment is harder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FUSION offers a practical, drop-in replacement for BON in both test-time scaling and synthetic data generation pipelines, with the notable finding that synthesis can exceed the oracle (best single sample) in some translation cases—challenging the assumption that selection sets an upper bound. Key limitations include smaller or mixed gains on tightly constrained math tasks (MGSM), a bias toward English in test-time scaling, and a dependence on a sufficiently large fusor (>27B parameters) for the approach to work without specialized training. Open questions include adaptive compute allocation (varying N per input), cross-lingual transfer to lower-resource languages where generative fusion capabilities lag, and robustness against adversarial teacher inputs.
