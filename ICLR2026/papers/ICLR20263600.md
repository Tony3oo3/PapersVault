## Problem

Data-driven subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) forecasting suffers from two underexplored limitations: (1) existing models predominantly rely on initial weather conditions while neglecting the persistent, slow-varying climatology that anchors S2S-scale predictability, and (2) progressive model collapse, where forecasts at extended lead times lose fine-scale structure and converge to oversmoothed, unrealistic fields. These issues cause deterministic ML models to degrade sharply beyond 15 days, limiting their utility for agriculture, energy, and disaster planning.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 5 consecutive days of global weather fields (13 upper-air pressure levels plus surface variables) at 5.625° (32×64) or 1.40625° (128×256) resolution, together with a 38-year daily climatology field of the same shape and a latitude-longitude grid. It produces single-step forecasts for each 5-day lead-time segment (15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45 days) via seven separately trained models, yielding a full 15–45 day forecast; ensemble members (51 total) are generated by sampling different realizations of the learnable Gaussian noise at each Transformer layer.

## Method

TianQuan-S2S combines two key designs within a Vision Transformer backbone. First, a patch-embedding module extracts spatial and channel-wise features from both the initial fields and the climatology via multi-layer convolutions, then fuses them through a spatial attention mechanism that computes per-pixel, per-level weights to adaptively blend climatology and initial-state information before patchifying and applying Fourier positional encoding. Second, an uncertainty-augmented Transformer (UD-ViT) injects learnable Gaussian noise (scaled by a per-layer function g_n) into the residual stream at every block, continuously reinforcing variability and preventing the progressive over-smoothing that causes model collapse at long leads. The architecture is trained with a latitude-weighted MSE loss, and a multi-model single-step strategy (one model per 5-day lead segment, all sharing the same 5-day input) avoids the iterative error accumulation inherent in autoregressive cascades.

## Results

On the ERA5 dataset (2017–2018 test), TianQuan-S2S achieves average RMSE improvements of 0.14 K on T850, 59 m²/s² on Z500, and 0.353 m/s on Wind10 over the best baseline across 15–45 day lead times. For ensemble forecasting, the ensemble-mean RMSE for T2m at day 45 is 2.601 (vs. 2.563 for FuXi-S2S and 2.610 for climatology), and Z500 RMSE at day 45 is 796 (vs. 775 for FuXi-S2S and 820 for climatology). On the challenging Wind10 variable at day 45, TianQuan-S2S achieves an ACC of 0.297, substantially exceeding ClimaX (0.172) and ECMWF-S2S (0.112). In ensemble CRPS for Z500 at day 40, TianQuan-S2S reaches 658 vs. 661 (FuXi-S2S) and 663 (ClimaX).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FuXi-S2S (the strongest data-driven S2S baseline), TianQuan-S2S achieves lower ensemble-mean RMSE on T2m (e.g., 2.424 vs. 2.450 at day 15; 2.601 vs. – at day 45, where FuXi-S2S does not report), T850 (3.255 vs. 3.323 at day 15), and Z500 (791 vs. 795 at day 15; 766 vs. 775 at day 40), while both methods underperform climatology on Wind10 due to over-smoothing of this highly variable near-surface field. Against ClimaX, TianQuan-S2S consistently outperforms across all four variables and all lead times in both deterministic (RMSE/ACC) and ensemble (CRPS/SME/RQE) metrics; ClimaX exhibits severe model collapse beyond day 25 with rapidly degrading ACC, whereas TianQuan-S2S maintains stable skill through day 45 thanks to its climatology fusion and per-layer noise injection. Against the numerical ECMWF-S2S system, TianQuan-S2S surpasses it on key variables, most dramatically on Wind10 at day 45 where ACC is 0.297 vs. 0.112. Against the climatology baseline, TianQuan-S2S outperforms on T2m, T850, and Z500 at all lead times but falls short on Wind10, which the authors attribute to the smoothing effect of ML ensemble means on this turbulent near-surface variable.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of data-driven weather forecasting (FourCastNet, Pangu-Weather, GraphCast, FuXi, ClimaX) and the specific subseasonal-to-seasonal (S2S) forecasting problem, where prior ML methods have shown limited skill. It departs from FuXi-S2S, which uses VAE-based latent-space perturbations and does not explicitly incorporate climatology, by instead injecting learnable Gaussian noise directly into every Transformer layer's feature representations and fusing climatological means into the patch embedding via an attention-based mechanism. The per-layer uncertainty block draws conceptually on NWP stochastic parameterization (ECMWF SPP) and SwinVRNN's learned distribution perturbations, but applies them at a finer granularity (every block rather than initial conditions or a single latent space). The multi-model single-step forecasting strategy (separate models for 15, 20, …, 45-day leads) is shared with FuXi's cascade design but avoids autoregressive error accumulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S2S forecasting (15–45 days) is a critical operational gap for agriculture, energy, and disaster management, and this paper demonstrates that two architecturally simple additions—climatology-aware patch embedding and per-layer Gaussian noise—can consistently outperform both the established ECMWF-S2S numerical system and the state-of-the-art FuXi-S2S data-driven model across deterministic and ensemble metrics. The honest acknowledgment that Wind10 ensemble means still underperform climatology at long leads, and the absence of land-surface, ocean, and sea-ice inputs, highlight that physically complete S2S modeling remains open. Future work on higher-resolution inputs, multi-source Earth observation data, and targeted extreme-event skill will be needed before these methods can fully replace operational NWP systems in the subseasonal regime.
