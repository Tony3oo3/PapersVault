## Problem

Existing truncation-based sampling methods for LLM decoding (top-p, min-p, ε-sampling, mirostat) rely on hyperparameters whose optimal values shift with the generation task and temperature, and they degrade sharply at high temperatures because they do not account for the entropy of the full token distribution. No prior method is simultaneously hyperparameter-free, distribution-aware, and robust across the full temperature range.

## Input / Output

At each autoregressive decoding step, the method consumes the full vocabulary-sized token probability distribution after temperature scaling (P_θ(v | x_{1:t-1})) and produces a single scalar truncation threshold L[P] = Σ P(v)², which is then used to filter the candidate set before normalized sampling. Unlike min-p (which uses only the modal probability) or top-p (which uses a fixed cumulative-probability cutoff), p-less reads every entry of the distribution, so the threshold adapts to the shape, spread, and entropy of the current step's distribution without any external tuning. The output is the next sampled token drawn from the admitted subset under renormalized probabilities.

## Method

p-less sampling computes the collision probability L[P] = Σ_{v∈V} P_θ(v|x_{1:t-1})² at every decoding step and admits all tokens whose individual probability meets or exceeds this threshold. This quantity is the exponential of the negative Rényi entropy of order 2 (collision entropy), giving it a principled information-theoretic interpretation: it equals the probability that a uniformly random guess matches the model's own most-likely token, and it is bounded between 1/|V| and max_i P(x_i), guaranteeing a non-empty candidate set. A relaxed variant, p-less_norm, subtracts a normalized incorrect-guess term (1/(|V|−1))·Σ_{u≠v} P(u)P(v), equivalently the unbiased estimator of the second central moment scaled by |V|, to admit more tokens when diversity is preferred. Because the threshold is derived from the entire distribution rather than a single statistic or a fixed hyperparameter, it automatically tightens when the distribution is peaked and loosens when temperature flattens it, avoiding the token over-admission that causes degeneration in other methods at high temperature.

## Results

On the math and logical reasoning benchmarks, p-less achieves the highest AUC (area under the accuracy–temperature curve) across all four datasets for both Llama-2-7B and Mistral-7B, e.g. 0.537 on QASC and 0.267 on GSM8K for Llama-2-7B, and 0.736 on QASC and 0.562 on GSM8K for Mistral-7B. For Llama3-70B, p-less attains the best or near-best AUC (within 0.005) on all four datasets, reaching 0.932 on GSM8K and 0.387 on GPQA. On the Writing Prompts creative-writing task, p-less achieves a length-controlled win rate of 65.64% at temperature 2.0 with Llama-2-7B, while top-p, ε-sampling, and η-sampling all drop to 0.00%. p-less also achieves the fastest per-token sampling time (0.0194 s vs. 0.0250 s for min-p, a 22% reduction) and produces shorter generations on average.

## Baselines & Comparisons

p-less outperforms all five truncation-based baselines (top-p, min-p, ε-sampling, η-sampling, mirostat) on AUC for Llama-2-7B and Mistral-7B across all four reasoning datasets, with the largest margins at high temperatures where competing methods degrade sharply (e.g. top-p drops to 4.6% accuracy on CSQA at τ=2.0 while p-less holds 49.2%). Min-p is the closest competitor, trailing p-less by roughly 0.01–0.04 AUC on Llama-2-7B and by a similar margin on Mistral-7B, but p-less still wins on every dataset for both models. On Llama3-70B, p-less and min-p are nearly tied on CSQA (0.819 vs. 0.820) and QASC (0.894 vs. 0.899), but p-less leads on GPQA (0.387 vs. 0.377) and GSM8K (0.932 vs. 0.924). On the Writing Prompts creative-writing task at temperature 2.0, p-less achieves a 65.64% length-controlled win rate with Llama-2-7B, dramatically surpassing min-p (48.94%) and far exceeding top-p, ε-sampling, and η-sampling which all collapse to 0.00%. Against beam search (bs=3, bs=5) on Mistral-7B, p-less matches or exceeds accuracy on CSQA, GPQA, and QASC while achieving a significantly higher win rate on Writing Prompts (68.9% vs. 59.7% and 56.9%), at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground within the family of truncation-based LLM decoding methods (top-p, top-k, ε-sampling, min-p, mirostat, η-sampling) by repurposing information-theoretic quantities—specifically the Rényi entropy of order 2 (collision entropy) and its connection to Friedman's Index of Coincidence from cryptography—as a principled, parameter-free truncation threshold. Unlike prior methods that rely on fixed hyperparameters, single-statistic thresholds (min-p's modal probability), or parametric distributional assumptions (mirostat's Zipf's Law), p-less computes the threshold as the sum of squared token probabilities, using the full distribution at each step. The conceptual lineage is thus less a direct extension of any single prior method and more a re-grounding of the truncation problem in information theory, unifying several desirable properties (distribution-awareness, temperature-adaptivity, boundedness) that existing methods each capture only partially.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of hyperparameter tuning addresses a genuine practical pain point: practitioners currently must select and potentially re-tune sampling parameters per task and temperature, and the paper demonstrates that a single parameter-free rule matches or exceeds tuned baselines across math, reasoning, and creative writing. The robustness at high temperatures (where top-p and ε-sampling collapse to near-zero quality on creative writing while p-less maintains ~65% win rate) is a practically significant finding for use cases requiring diversity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multimodal or non-autoregressive decoding, whether combining p-less with contrastive or controlled decoding yields further gains, and whether the modest absolute improvements over the best tuned baseline (min-p) persist at larger model scales beyond 70B. The paper's evaluation is limited to 7B/70B models and a 100-prompt creative writing subset, leaving some generalization questions open.
