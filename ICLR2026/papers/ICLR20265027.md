## Problem

Language models generate reasoning sequentially, committing to a single path and unable to decouple irrelevant exploration branches during search. External methods like Tree-of-Thought address this through post-hoc sampling but require multiple independent model calls with redundant prefix recomputation and produce fragmented, uncoordinated search coverage. The paper asks whether a model can natively generate coherent, branching search structures within a single generation process, internalizing systematic exploration through supervised learning rather than relying on external orchestration or reinforcement learning.

## Input / Output

The model takes a problem statement (e.g., four numbers for Game of 24, grid coordinates with obstacles for Gridworld, logical premises for ProntoQA, or a math word problem for GSM8K) and produces a tree-structured reasoning trace serialized as a linear token sequence. The output uses special structural tokens—[SEP] for expandable branches, [FAIL] for dead ends, [GOAL] for solution states, and [BOS]/[EOS] for child expansion boundaries—encoding both successful and failed exploration paths. During inference, the generated tree is parsed and branches are 'stitched' into isolated contexts for parallel expansion, with the final answer extracted from the first [GOAL] terminal reached via BFS or DFS traversal.

## Method

TSLM introduces a token-based serialization scheme that encodes complete search trees into linear sequences processable by standard transformers, using special tokens to mark branching structure, viability, and goals. The key architectural insight is selective context decoupling: each node in the tree conditions only on its ancestral path and previously generated siblings (not on unrelated subtrees), enabling the model to learn coherent branching without redundant computation. Training applies standard cross-entropy loss over the entire serialized tree, where structural tokens like [SEP] are conditioned on whether the corresponding state is expandable and distinct from prior siblings, teaching the model to systematically generate diverse branches at each decision point. For open-ended tasks lacking predefined trees, a bootstrapping procedure uses Tree-of-Thoughts beam search from a supervision model to construct synthetic training trees, incorporating gold trajectories as high-priority branches and ordering remaining branches by a reward function. At inference, the model generates the complete branching structure in one forward pass, then selectively expands viable branches into isolated contexts, achieving a new test-time scaling paradigm where increasing k explores more branches within a single coherent tree rather than sampling k independent trajectories.

## Results

TSLM achieves 100% pass@1 accuracy on Game of 24 and in-domain Gridworld (10×10), and 91.5% on out-of-distribution Gridworld (20×20), substantially outperforming all sequential baselines. On open-ended reasoning, TSLM reaches 100% on ProntoQA and 61.6% on GSM8K (pass@1), matching or exceeding Tree-of-Thought evaluated at pass@100 (100% and 62.3% respectively). The most striking result is the Gridworld extrapolation gap: TSLM drops only 8.5 points (100%→91.5%) when scaling from 10×10 to 20×20, while ToT collapses from 95.0% to 42.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Sequence Cloning (SC), TSLM wins decisively on every task: 100% vs. 17% on Game of 24, 91.5% vs. 33% on out-of-distribution Gridworld, and 61.6% vs. 55.8% on GSM8K. Against Procedure Cloning (PC), TSLM achieves large gains on Game of 24 (100% vs. 47%) and out-of-distribution Gridworld (91.5% vs. 81.1%), while matching PC on in-domain Gridworld (100% vs. 99.7%) and ProntoQA (100% vs. 97.5%). Against GRPO, TSLM dominates across the board, most dramatically on Game of 24 (100% vs. 15%) and out-of-distribution Gridworld (91.5% vs. 6%). Against Tree-of-Thought (ToT), the comparison is asymmetric—TSLM uses pass@1 while ToT uses pass@100—yet TSLM still outperforms ToT on Game of 24 (100% vs. 32%), in-domain Gridworld (100% vs. 95%), and out-of-distribution Gridworld (91.5% vs. 42.7%), ties on ProntoQA (100% vs. 100%), and trails by 0.7 points on GSM8K (61.6% vs. 62.3%). The authors note that TSLM with a single candidate (61.3%) nearly matches ToT's converged pass@100 performance (62.3%), and TSLM's scaling curve saturates at a higher ceiling (67.2%).

## Relation to Prior Work

TSLM departs from two dominant paradigms for multi-path reasoning: external search scaffolding (Tree-of-Thought, RAP, LATS) that orchestrates multiple independent model calls, and RL-based post-training (GRPO, DeepSeek-R1) that incentivizes extended reasoning traces through reward signals. Rather than building on either, it repurposes standard autoregressive language modeling with a novel token-based serialization scheme that encodes complete search trees—including failed branches—into linear sequences, allowing a single forward pass to generate coherent branching structure. The selective context decoupling (each node conditions only on ancestors and prior siblings, not the full serialized prefix) is the key architectural insight that distinguishes it from simply concatenating exploration paths, as done in Procedure Cloning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the prevailing assumption that robust reasoning requires RL or inference-time search orchestration, showing that supervised learning on complete tree-structured traces can internalize systematic exploration and even produce emergent capabilities like unsolvable-problem detection (97/100). However, the evidence is confined to relatively constrained tasks (Game of 24, small Gridworlds, GSM8K) with modest training data (<10K instances), and the bootstrapping approach for open-ended tasks depends heavily on the quality of the supervision model. Open questions include whether the approach scales to genuinely complex, long-horizon reasoning, whether it extends to domains without verifiable solutions, and whether the computational overhead of training on full trees (versus single paths) becomes prohibitive at scale.
