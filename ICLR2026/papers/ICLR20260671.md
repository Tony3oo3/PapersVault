## Problem

LLM-based agents struggle with hard-exploration problems characterized by sparse rewards, large combinatorial state-action spaces, and deceptive local optima. Existing LLM agent approaches like ReAct and Reflexion support local trial-and-error but lack mechanisms for long-term knowledge accumulation across exploration episodes, causing them to fall far short of RL methods that require 100–800× more environment interactions to solve the same tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260671/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes textual game observations, a list of valid actions (used as a soft constraint alongside free-form generation), the current score, and accumulated exploration trajectories. It produces game actions as JSON-formatted text commands with a reasoning field, a global world memory as a set of critical states annotated with achieved value v and LLM-inferred potential value v′, and a local world memory as semantic advantage descriptions at 2–4 key state-action pairs. All world memory is maintained as structured natural-language text rather than numerical tensors, enabling LLM-based reasoning over exploration history.

## Method

GLoW builds on Go-Explore's select-then-explore paradigm with a dual-scale textual world memory. At the global scale, it maintains a value-ranked trajectory frontier (top-k trajectories by maximum cumulative reward) and uses an LLM to decompose value at critical states into achieved value and potential value—implementing a semantic analogue of UCB's optimism under uncertainty—then selects the next exploration state via LLM-based alignment between archived states and these value annotations. At the local scale, Multi-path Advantage Reflection (MAR) samples n trajectories from the same starting state and prompts an LLM to compare divergent outcomes, inferring semantic advantages at key decision points that densify sparse reward signals into actionable progress guidance. The exploration policy is an LLM agent conditioned on both the local advantage memory and the global frontier, creating an iterative loop where each trajectory informs the next through updated advantage estimates.

## Results

GLoW achieves a new state-of-the-art among LLM-based approaches on 7 out of 10 Jericho games, reaching 73.0 on Zork1 (vs. 51.7 for the next-best LLM method ICRL) and 73.7 on Ludicorp (vs. 32.0 for ICRL). Critically, GLoW uses only 1,000 environment interactions, achieving comparable performance to XTX (800,000 steps) on Deephome (75.0 vs. 77.7) and Ludicorp (73.7 vs. 78.8), while surpassing XTX on Enchanter (61.7 vs. 52.0). When scaled to GPT-4.1, GLoW surpasses all prior methods on 4 out of 6 difficult/extreme games, including Zork1 (103.0 vs. XTX's 103.4) and Enchanter (98.3 vs. 52.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IGE (the most directly comparable LLM-based Go-Explore method), GLoW substantially outperforms on 8 out of 10 games, with the largest gaps on Ludicorp (73.7 vs. 28.3) and Zork1 (73.0 vs. 44.3). Against the SOTA RL method XTX (800,000 steps), GLoW nearly matches on Deephome (75.0 vs. 77.7) and Ludicorp (73.7 vs. 78.8) while using 800× fewer interactions, and notably surpasses XTX on Enchanter (61.7 vs. 52.0), though it trails on Zork1 (73.0 vs. 103.4). Against MC-DML (~400,000 steps), GLoW outperforms on Zork1 (73.0 vs. 48.7), Deephome (75.0 vs. 67.0), and Ludicorp (73.7 vs. 19.7). Among LLM baselines, GLoW dominates: it beats ICRL (the strongest standard LLM agent) by 21.3 points on Zork1 (73.0 vs. 51.7) and 41.7 points on Ludicorp (73.7 vs. 32.0), and outperforms ReAct and Reflexion by 24–55 points on the hardest games. The paper also shows that with GPT-4.1, GLoW reaches 103.0 on Zork1 (matching XTX's 103.4) and 98.3 on Enchanter (vs. XTX's 52.0), establishing SOTA on 4/6 difficult/extreme games.

## Relation to Prior Work

GLoW extends the Go-Explore select-then-explore paradigm (Ecoffet et al., 2021) and its LLM adaptation IGE (Lu et al., 2025) by replacing the state archive with a value-ranked trajectory frontier and introducing a dual-scale world memory. The global scale draws on UCB-style value decomposition (achieved + potential value) to guide principled state selection, while the local scale introduces Multi-path Advantage Reflection (MAR), which adapts the advantage-function concept from RL (TRPO, process reward models) into an LLM-reasoning mechanism that compares multiple trajectories from the same state. This departs from prior LLM agent approaches (ReAct, Reflexion, ICRL) that support only single-trajectory or single-episode learning without structured long-term knowledge accumulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 100–800× reduction in environment interactions relative to RL baselines while matching or exceeding their performance is practically significant for settings where interaction cost is prohibitive, and the dual-scale memory architecture offers a reusable template for LLM agents in other sparse-reward domains. However, the approach assumes deterministic environments (limiting direct applicability to stochastic settings), depends heavily on LLM reasoning quality for value decomposition (Appendix G shows nano models fail at inferring potential value), and exhibits failure modes in multi-step dependency reasoning. Open questions include whether the trajectory frontier and MAR generalize beyond text-based games, how to handle stochastic state restoration, and whether the n=3 exploration budget generalizes across task complexities.
