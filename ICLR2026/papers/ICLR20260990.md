## Problem

Existing Koopman-based predictive training methods bypass SGD iterations by directly predicting network weights several epochs ahead, but they apply all predicted weights indiscriminately, causing gradient explosion and failure on larger or more complex architectures. The core gap is the absence of a mechanism to distinguish high-fidelity predictions from unreliable ones in the highly inhomogeneous weight space of modern DNNs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260990/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes h past weight snapshots (default h=5, stored as an N×h matrix) and the current weight vector at epoch level, alongside a standard baseline optimizer (SGD, Adam, LAMB, etc.). It produces a hybrid weight update: a binary element-wise mask selects which parameters receive the τ-step DMD prediction (default τ=5) versus the standard optimizer step, yielding a single updated weight vector per epoch. The prediction operates at epoch granularity rather than per-batch, keeping overhead to O(N·h²) per prediction event.

## Method

PDT frames DNN training as a discrete dynamical system and uses Standard DMD to compute a low-rank spectral approximation (modes Φ, eigenvalues Λ) of the Koopman operator from weight snapshots, enabling τ-step-ahead weight prediction via w_pred = Re(ΦΛ^τ Φ† w_i). The key contribution is a dynamic consistency masking strategy with two per-parameter criteria: (1) an acceleration effectiveness bound requiring the predicted displacement to exceed one optimizer step but not exceed τ times that step, and (2) a sign-alignment criterion enforcing that every intermediate step in the predicted trajectory follows the same direction as the local gradient update. Only parameters satisfying both criteria receive the predicted weight; all others fall back to the baseline optimizer, making PDT a drop-in plug-in that accelerates training by 10–40% in wall-clock time while preserving or improving final accuracy across architectures from FCN to ViT-Huge.

## Results

PDT achieves 10–40% runtime reduction to reach the baseline's best training loss across five architectures (FCN, AlexNet, ResNet-50, ViT-Base, ViT-Huge) on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet-1K, while maintaining or improving final validation accuracy. The largest gains appear on smaller networks (FCN: 39.6% TTB-Loss reduction; AlexNet: 37.0%), while larger models show more modest but consistent improvements (ViT-Huge: 9.9% TTB-Loss reduction). PDT also generalizes to self-supervised learning (SimSiam), where it cuts training time by 48.8% and improves final accuracy from 72.85% to 76.85%. Across seven optimizers (SGD, Momentum, Adam, AdamW, RMSprop, Shampoo, LAMB), PDT consistently reduces TTB-Loss by 14.9–44.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PDT is compared against the non-selective Koopman-based predictive training of Tano et al. (2020), which applies all predicted weights without quality checking; PDT decisively wins as Tano's method fails (gradient explosion) on networks beyond ~3M parameters, while PDT scales to 632M-parameter ViT-Huge. Against standard SGD (Robbins & Monro, 1951), PDT reduces TTB-Loss by 37% on AlexNet/CIFAR-10 and 19.4% on ResNet-50/ImageNet. Against SGD with Momentum (Sutskever et al., 2013), PDT achieves a 41.1% runtime reduction on AlexNet. Against Adam (Kingma & Ba, 2014) and AdamW (Loshchilov & Hutter, 2019), PDT yields 14.9% and 28.4% reductions respectively on AlexNet, and 10.2% on ViT-Base. Against RMSprop (Tieleman & Hinton, 2012), Shampoo (Gupta et al., 2018), and LAMB (You et al., 2020), PDT achieves 15.4%, 16.0%, and 44.2% runtime reductions respectively. In all comparisons, PDT matches or slightly exceeds the baseline's final accuracy (e.g., 79.78% vs. 79.30% on AlexNet with SGD), confirming that the acceleration does not sacrifice model quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Koopman-based predictive training line (Dogra & Redman, 2020; Tano et al., 2020) that treats DNN training as a nonlinear dynamical system and uses Dynamic Mode Decomposition (Tu et al., 2014) to predict future weights, bypassing SGD iterations. The critical departure is the introduction of a dynamic consistency masking strategy that selectively applies predictions only to parameters whose local dynamics align with the global DMD model, effectively merging predictive training with the differential learning principle already present in adaptive optimizers like Adam and RMSprop. Where prior Koopman-based methods applied all predicted weights indiscriminately and failed at scale, PDT's selective mechanism is what enables it to function as a plug-in across seven optimizers and architectures from FCN to ViT-Huge.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PDT offers a practical 10–40% wall-clock training time reduction that is broadly applicable as a drop-in plug-in, which is meaningful for practitioners training large models on ImageNet-scale data. The ablation showing that direction-consistency-only masking (without the magnitude bound) causes complete training collapse is a useful negative result that clarifies why both criteria are necessary. Open questions include whether streaming DMD variants can further reduce the O(N×h) memory overhead for billion-parameter models, whether the approach transfers to reinforcement learning or generative model training, and whether the masking ratio's sharp decline in later training epochs limits the total achievable speedup for long training runs.
