## Problem

Existing methods for detecting spurious correlations rely on dataset statistics or validation-set error patterns, leaving harmful shortcuts invisible when counterexamples are absent from held-out splits. This paper addresses the gap by asking: can we identify which spurious correlations a model has actually learned, directly from its weights, without requiring any counterexamples in training or validation data?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260954/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a frozen foundation model (e.g., CLIP ViT-L/14, mGTE) that embeds both input samples and text concepts into a shared D-dimensional space, a labeled training set, class names, and a pre-extracted concept list (obtained via GIT-Large captioning + YAKE keyword extraction for images, or directly from text for NLP tasks). It produces, for each class, a ranked list of class-neutral concepts flagged as spurious correlations, along with positive and negative SC scores computed as embedding-space dot products between the learned class weights and concept embeddings. The output is structured as (class, [SC concepts]) pairs, with a dynamic per-class threshold determining how many top-ranked concepts are retained.

## Method

BEE exploits the geometric drift of linear-probe weights in a frozen foundation model's embedding space. A linear classifier is initialized with the foundation model's zero-shot class-name embeddings and fine-tuned via ERM on the target dataset; during training, class weights shift away from their class-concept initialization toward spuriously correlated attributes. BEE then filters the extracted concept list to retain only class-neutral concepts (using Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and WordNet hyponym/hypernym matching), and scores each concept per class via a positive-SC score: the dot product with the learned class weight minus the minimum dot product with any other class weight, isolating concepts that are specifically correlated with one class. A dynamic thresholding step applies a mean filter over the ranked scores and selects the top concepts up to the index of maximum deviation from a linear reference line, automatically adapting the cut-off per class. The entire pipeline is post-hoc and model-agnostic: it never modifies the foundation model, requires no counterexamples, and transfers across embedding families (CLIP, DataComp.XL, BLIP2, SigLIP2, mGTE).

## Results

On the SC-enhanced zero-shot prompting task, BEE achieves the best worst-group accuracy across all three subpopulation-shift benchmarks: 50.3% on Waterbirds (vs. 35.2% basic zero-shot), 73.1% on CelebA, and 53.2% on CivilComments. In the extreme no-counterexample setting (Table 5), BEE's SC-based regularization lifts worst-group accuracy to 57.9% on Waterbirds and 31.3% on CivilComments, substantially outperforming both ERM and GroupDRO. On ImageNet-1k, BEE-identified SCs cause accuracy drops of up to 95% (e.g., peafowl→fire truck) when the spurious concept is introduced, confirming the severity of the discovered shortcuts.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BEE outperforms all three SC-identification baselines on worst-group accuracy in the zero-shot prompting evaluation. On Waterbirds, BEE (50.3%) beats B2T (48.1%), SpLiCE (48.1%), and Lg (46.1%); on CelebA, BEE (73.1%) surpasses B2T (72.8%), SpLiCE (67.2%), and Lg (50.6%). BEE is also the only method evaluated on CivilComments, where it lifts worst-group accuracy from 33.1% to 53.2%. In the no-counterexample training setting (Table 5), BEE's SC-based regularization (57.9% worst-group on Waterbirds, 31.3% on CivilComments) substantially outperforms GroupDRO (38.9%, 18.7%) and ERM (43.2%, 18.6%), demonstrating that explicitly identified SCs enable effective mitigation even when standard group-robustness methods fail. The average accuracy trade-off is modest: BEE's CelebA average (85.7%) is slightly below SpLiCE (90.2%) and B2T (88.0%), reflecting the known accuracy–robustness trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

BEE departs from both data-driven SC detection (SpLiCE, Lg) and error-driven approaches (B2T) by shifting the diagnostic lens to the weight space of a fine-tuned linear probe, exploiting the geometric alignment between drifted class weights and concept embeddings in a shared foundation-model space. It repurposes the linear-probing paradigm—previously used for transfer learning or robustness retraining (Kirichenko et al., 2023)—as a transparent post-hoc diagnostic that requires no counterexamples, no architectural modification, and no human-defined concept set, distinguishing it from Concept Bottleneck Models and subpopulation-shift partitioning methods. The dynamic thresholding heuristic for automatic SC selection is a practical addition not seen in prior SC-identification pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

BEE provides a practical, model-agnostic auditing tool that can expose hidden shortcuts in production foundation models without requiring adversarial validation sets, which is critical for high-stakes deployments in healthcare (MIMIC-CXR false negatives) and safety-sensitive vision tasks. The finding that identified SCs transfer across architecturally diverse models (CNNs, ViTs, EfficientNets) suggests these are dataset-level pathologies rather than model-specific artifacts, opening a path toward dataset-level remediation. Key limitations include reliance on captioning models for concept extraction (capping the discoverable concept space), the linear-probing assumption, and inability to detect pixel-level or low-level SCs that vanish in high-semantic embedding spaces. Disentangling dataset-induced from foundation-model-induced spuriousness remains an open challenge the paper explicitly does not address.
