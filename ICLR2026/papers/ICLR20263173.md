## Problem

While attention sinks in LLMs have been widely studied, the role of high-norm visual tokens (ViT sinks) in Large Vision-Language Models remains largely unexplored. Prior work either treats visual sinks as detrimental artifacts to be removed or conflates them with LLM-emerged sinks, leaving it unclear whether these tokens carry useful information and how they should be handled in the visual-to-language information pathway.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263173/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed by a ViT into ~576 patch tokens) and a text question as input, producing a text response. A key detail is that only 3–5 of the visual tokens are identified as ViT sinks (norm > threshold, e.g., 100 for CLIP), and these are treated as a distinct class from the remaining non-sink tokens throughout the pipeline. In the training-free variant, sink tokens are simply repositioned within the sequence; in the training-based variant, they receive separate MLP projections and input-dependent scalar weights before concatenation with non-sink tokens.

## Method

The paper first demonstrates empirically that ViT sinks propagate into the LLM as distinct sink tokens activating different hidden dimensions than LLM-emerged sinks, and that they encode coarse-grained, high-level semantic context (verified via relevance maps and decoded word distributions). Building on this, two approaches are proposed: (1) a training-free 'sink-to-the-front' strategy that repositions ViT sink tokens and their positional embeddings to the beginning of the visual sequence to exploit causal attention bias, and (2) DIYSink, a training-based framework with Dual-MLP projection layers that independently project sink and non-sink tokens into LLM-compatible space, combined with a dynamic token selection module that either uses Chain-of-Thought routing (hard 0/1 selection based on image complexity and query globalness) or a lightweight reweighting MLP (soft scalar weights learned from ~120–240 cross-task examples) to decide which token class to emphasize per input.

## Results

DIYSink consistently improves performance across four ViT-LLM configurations (0.5B to 7B). The largest gains appear on TinyLLaVA-3B-Phi2, where DIYSink(ReW) improves LLaVA-eval average by +5.79 points (48.55→54.34) and MME by +227.19 points (1455.22→1682.41). On MathVista, DIYSink(CoT) delivers +6.05 and +7.53 points in algebraic and geometric reasoning respectively for TinyLLaVA-Phi2-3B. The training-free sink-to-the-front strategy also yields consistent gains across six closed-source LVLMs, with up to +33.81 on MME (Gemma3-12B) and +1.80 on MathVista (Molmo-7B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIYSink outperforms all four baselines (TinyLLaVA-0.5B, TinyLLaVA-3B-Phi2, TinyLLaVA-3B-Qwen2.5, and LLaVA-7B) across every evaluated benchmark, with the most dramatic improvement on TinyLLaVA-3B-Phi2 where MME gains +227.19 points and LLaVA-eval gains +5.79 points. The training-free sink-to-the-front strategy also beats all six closed-source baselines (InternVL2.5-4B, Phi-3.5V, DeepSeek-VL-7B, Molmo-7B-D, Gemma3-12B, Meteor) on at least one benchmark, with the largest MME gains on Gemma3-12B (+33.81) and Molmo-7B-D (+30.28). Notably, the method extends beyond Transformer architectures, as it also improves Meteor (a Mamba-based VLM) by +9.72 on MME. The only case of minimal gain is DeepSeek-VL-7B, where channel-wise concatenation of SAM and SigLIP features complicates token behavior, yielding only −0.94 on MME. Overall, DIYSink(ReW) tends to produce the largest average gains, while DIYSink(CoT) excels specifically on reasoning sub-tasks like MathVista geometric reasoning (+7.53 on TinyLLaVA-Phi2-3B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the LLaVA-style ViT+MLP+LLM architecture and the ViT sink identification methodology from Darcet et al. (2024), but departs sharply from the prevailing narrative that visual attention sinks are detrimental noise to be suppressed or removed. While prior work (Kang et al. 2025, Huang et al. 2024) focused on redistributing or masking visual sinks to improve performance, this paper is the first to empirically demonstrate that ViT sinks propagate into the LLM as semantically meaningful tokens encoding coarse-grained, high-level context (object identity, scene composition). The DIYSink framework extends the standard single-MLP connector design by introducing a Dual-MLP projection that treats sink and non-sink tokens separately, and adds dynamic token selection via CoT routing or a lightweight reweighting MLP.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes a commonly dismissed artifact (high-norm ViT tokens) as a useful information channel, offering both a zero-cost inference trick (sink-to-the-front) and a training-based architectural modification that consistently improve reasoning benchmarks across six+ LVLM families. However, the absolute gains are modest (typically 0.2–5.8 points), and the approach relies on a fixed norm threshold whose optimal value varies across vision backbones. Open questions include whether the effect persists at much larger scales (e.g., 70B+ models), whether it generalizes to video or multi-image inputs, and whether the Dual-MLP separation is truly necessary or whether simpler normalization of sink tokens before a shared projector would suffice.
