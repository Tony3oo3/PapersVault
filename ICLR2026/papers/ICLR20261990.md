## Problem

Current long-context models treat multi-session dialogue histories as flat text, failing to locate and resolve temporal expressions, which causes steep performance degradation as histories grow longer and accumulate noise. Existing time-aware frameworks struggle with ambiguous temporal expressions, and RL-based approaches rely on structured metadata unavailable in unstructured dialogues. A robust, scalable method for temporal reasoning over noisy, multi-session conversations remains an open challenge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261990/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a user query and a candidate pool of dialogue sessions (each carrying a timestamp and a sequence of utterances), and produces a single structured output string that explicitly cites the selected evidence session IDs followed by the natural language answer (e.g., `{selected_memory: [session_3, session_16]. answer: 19 days.}`). Fine-grained annotations—query temporal ranges, utterance-level event time spans, and gold evidence session IDs—are used exclusively for computing training rewards and are withheld at inference time, ensuring the model must learn implicit temporal alignment rather than relying on explicit labels.

## Method

MEMORY-T1 employs a two-phase coarse-to-fine retrieval pipeline. In Phase 1 (Candidate Generation), an LLM predicts the query's temporal window to hard-filter sessions by timestamp overlap, and BM25 ranks the survivors by textual relevance to produce a top-k candidate pool. In Phase 2 (Fine-grained Selection), an RL agent trained with GRPO selects precise evidence sessions and generates the answer in a single end-to-end output. The key innovation is a multi-level reward function combining answer accuracy (R_a), evidence grounding via Jaccard index (R_g), and a novel temporal consistency reward (R_t) operating at two granularities: session-level chronological proximity (a logistic penalty on temporal distance to the query's gold range) and utterance-level chronological fidelity (scoring per-event time overlap with the query range). This dense, structured reward signal overcomes the sparsity of answer-only supervision, enabling the agent to resolve ambiguous temporal expressions and maintain robustness in dialogue histories up to 128k tokens where baseline models collapse.

## Results

On the Time-Dialog benchmark, Memory-T1 (7B) achieves a state-of-the-art overall F1 of 67.0%, while the 3B variant reaches 66.9%. The 3B model outperforms the much larger Qwen2.5-14B baseline (60.7%) by 6.2 points and surpasses GPT-4 Full Prompt (64.8%) and GPT-4 ReAct (62.8%). On the out-of-domain LoCoMo benchmark, Memory-T1 (3B) improves overall F1 from 33.5% (base Qwen2.5-3B) to 37.7%, with the largest gains in Temporal (+7.0) and Adversarial (+9.4) subtasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Memory-T1 (7B) at 67.0% overall outperforms all open-source baselines: Qwen2.5-14B (60.7%, +6.3 pts), Qwen2.5-7B (53.2%, +13.8 pts), Llama-3-8B (48.4%, +18.6 pts), and Gemma-4B (45.0%, +22.0 pts). It also surpasses the specialized temporal-reasoning agents Time-R1 (49.4%, +17.6 pts) and MemAgent-7B (49.9%, +17.1 pts), both evaluated zero-shot. Against closed-source GPT-4, Memory-T1 beats Full Prompt (64.8%, +2.2 pts) and ReAct (62.8%, +4.2 pts), though a gap remains to GPT-4 with oracle evidence (86.2%). The 3B variant (66.9%) nearly matches the 7B variant, confirming that the learned RL policy—not model scale—drives the gains. On the OOD LoCoMo benchmark, Memory-T1 (3B) improves over the base model by +4.2 pts overall (37.7% vs. 33.5%), with the largest relative gains in Temporal reasoning (+7.0 pts) and Adversarial detection (+9.4 pts).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: temporal reasoning in LLMs (Time-R1, Timo, TG-LLM), memory architectures for conversational agents (TReMu, MemAgent, MemoryBank), and RL-based policy optimization for LLMs (GRPO, DeepSeek-R1, Search-R1). It departs from prior temporal reasoning work by rejecting structured metadata dependencies (Time-R1) and timeline-summary approaches (TReMu) in favor of learning an implicit memory selection policy directly from dialogue. The coarse-to-fine retrieval cascade (LLM-predicted temporal window → BM25 relevance ranking → RL-based fine-grained selection) is a novel synthesis, and the multi-level reward design—particularly the temporal consistency reward operating at both session and utterance granularity—repurposes RL reward shaping ideas from general reasoning tasks to the specific challenge of temporal evidence grounding in noisy multi-session contexts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Temporal reasoning is a prerequisite for factual consistency in any long-term conversational agent, and this paper demonstrates that a learned retrieval policy can close much of the gap between small open-source models and much larger baselines (7B outperforming 14B by 10.2 points), suggesting that targeted RL training is more effective than brute-force scaling for this capability. The robustness to 128k-token contexts where baselines collapse is practically significant for real-world deployment. However, the 19-point gap to GPT-4 Oracle (67.0% vs 86.2%) and the near-zero performance on Comparison and Timeline subtasks indicate that compositional temporal logic remains unsolved. Open questions include whether the annotation pipeline (event-level temporal spans, session-level evidence grounding) scales to truly open-ended conversations without curated fact banks, and whether the RL policy generalizes to dialogue formats beyond the LoCoMo/Time-Dialog structure.
