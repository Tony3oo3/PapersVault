## Problem

Standard LLM pretraining relies solely on next-token prediction, which does not explicitly encourage long-range reasoning or integration with world knowledge. Reinforcement learning, while powerful for scaling reasoning, is currently introduced only as the final post-training phase after supervised fine-tuning, leaving a gap where the model never learns to 'think for itself' during the bulk of its training. The paper asks whether the exploratory spirit of RL can be brought into pretraining itself, on ordinary text, without requiring task-specific verifiers or curated reasoning data.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prefix x_{<t} from any ordinary corpus (web crawl, academic papers, math textbooks, or SFT-style reasoning data) and, at a randomly selected position per document, samples a chain-of-thought c_t of up to 2048 tokens. The output is a next-token prediction x_t scored under two conditions: with the sampled thought (reasoned scorer) and without it (EMA no-think baseline). The training signal is the log-likelihood ratio between these two scores, yielding a dense, position-wise, verifier-free reward that requires no external checker, no curated solutions, and no pre-selection of high-entropy tokens.

## Method

RLP treats chain-of-thought generation as an explicit action: a single network with shared parameters θ first samples a thought c_t ~ π_θ(·|x_{<t}), then predicts x_t conditioned on (x_{<t}, c_t). The reward r(c_t) is the information gain — the log-likelihood of x_t under the reasoned scorer minus that under a slowly updated EMA no-think baseline (τ=0.999) — computed under teacher forcing. Training optimizes only this information-gain objective (no standard NTP loss) via a GRPO-style clipped surrogate with group-relative advantages over G=16 rollouts, updating gradients exclusively on thought tokens. Theoretical results show the expected reward equals the cross-entropy reduction between the reasoned and no-think scorers (Prop. 1), and that the CoT-conditioned objective is a computable lower bound on the improvement obtained after marginalizing over thoughts (Prop. 2), ensuring the dense per-token signal provides principled credit assignment across all positions in any text corpus.

## Results

On QWEN3-1.7B-BASE, RLP lifts the overall average across an eight-benchmark math-and-science suite from 30.32% (base) to 36.03%, a 19% relative gain, and from 30.85% (continuous pretraining) to 36.03%, a 17% relative gain. After identical SFT+RLVR post-training, the RLP-trained model reaches 42.51% overall, outperforming the CPT+Post baseline (39.90%) by 7% and the base+Post baseline (39.34%) by 8%. Scaling to the hybrid NEMOTRON-NANO-12B-v2, RLP applied for only 250M tokens (0.125% of the 20T pretraining budget) raises the overall average from 42.81% to 61.32% (43% relative gain), with Science Avg jumping from 34.51% to 57.26%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary methodological comparison is against RPT (Dong et al., 2025), a prior reinforcement pretraining approach using sparse binary next-token correctness rewards with entropy-based token selection. Under token-matched conditions (same input tokens, OmniMath), RLP achieves 43.35% overall vs. RPT's 41.69% (+4.0% relative), with Math Avg at 49.62% vs. 47.50% and Science Avg at 37.07% vs. 35.88%. Under the stricter flop-matched setting (same target tokens, Nemotron-CrossThink), the gap widens dramatically: RLP reaches 42.86% overall vs. RPT's 35.68%, a 20.12% relative improvement, with Math Avg at 45.95% vs. 36.66%. RLP also consistently outperforms the continuous pretraining (CPT) baseline: even when CPT is given 35× more data (6B tokens vs. 170M) to match total FLOPs, RLP still leads by 5.3% on average. The key methodological difference is that RLP provides a dense, continuous information-gain reward at every position without requiring an auxiliary model for token selection, whereas RPT relies on sparse binary signals and proxy-model filtering.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the GRPO-style policy-gradient framework (group-relative advantages, clipped surrogate) from post-training RL into a pretraining objective, replacing the standard next-token prediction loss with a dense, verifier-free information-gain reward. It departs from RPT (Dong et al., 2025), the closest prior work on reinforcement pretraining, by using a continuous log-likelihood-ratio reward at every position rather than a sparse binary next-token correctness signal filtered through an auxiliary model. It also distinguishes itself from verifier-free post-training methods (Yuan et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025b; Zhao et al., 2025) by operating on arbitrary web-scale text during pretraining rather than on curated SFT corpora during alignment. The EMA baseline mechanism and the theoretical CE-reduction identity (Prop. 1) are novel contributions that ground the reward in information theory rather than task-specific verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RLP challenges the entrenched pipeline assumption that RL must be deferred to the final post-training phase, showing that exploration-based reasoning can be instilled during pretraining on ordinary text and that these gains compound rather than wash out under subsequent SFT+RLVR. The remarkable data efficiency (43% relative improvement on a 12B model using 0.125% of the pretraining budget) and the ability to extract reasoning signal from general web-crawl data suggest a path toward more sample-efficient pretraining. Open questions include whether RLP can fully replace NTP rather than augment it, how it interacts with very long context windows, whether the one-token-per-document sampling limitation can be lifted to all positions, and whether the approach transfers to non-autoregressive or diffusion-based language models.
