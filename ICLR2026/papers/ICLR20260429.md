## Problem

Recent RL-for-LLM methods (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO) have largely abandoned the critic component of PPO, replacing value functions with group-sampling and average-advantage baselines because full-size critics are computationally expensive at LLM scale and inaccurate under sparse, long-horizon rewards. This shift sacrifices robust state-value estimation, which is critical for mitigating training collapse under off-policy settings. The paper asks whether an asymmetric, lightweight critic architecture can restore the critic's role while remaining efficient at LLM scale.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (e.g., a math problem) with a system instruction to reason step-by-step and output the final answer in \boxed{}, and produces a full reasoning response of up to 8192 tokens. Training uses group rollouts of 32 trajectories per prompt with sparse outcome-based rewards (correct/incorrect), and the actor (e.g., Qwen3-14b-Base) is paired with two much smaller critics (e.g., Qwen3-1.7b-Base) that estimate token-level state values. The asymmetric design reduces peak GPU memory by ~20% and accelerates training by ~20 seconds per step compared to symmetric PPO, while keeping compute at the GRPO level.

## Method

AsyPPO replaces the single full-size critic with two lightweight mini-critics trained on disjoint, group-level partitions of responses within each prompt, so that each critic sees a different subset of trajectories per question, encouraging functional diversity while maintaining perceptual synchrony. The ensemble value is the mean of the two critics' outputs, used in standard GAE for advantage computation. On top of this, the policy loss is refined in two uncertainty-aware ways: (1) advantage masking zeroes out the PPO gradient for the bottom-k% of states by critic value-standard deviation (high agreement = low informativeness), suppressing overfitting to redundant transitions; and (2) entropy filtering excludes the top-h% of states by value-std from the entropy regularization term, preventing spurious exploration at reasoning-irrelevant tokens (adverbs, interjections) that would otherwise destabilize policy entropy. Together these components yield >6% gains over GRPO on Qwen3-4b-Base and ~3% on 8b/14b models with only 5k training samples.

## Results

On Qwen3-14b-Base (evaluated on MATH-500, OlympiadBench, MinervaMath, AMC 2023, Avg@4), AsyPPO with two 1.7B critics achieves 61.3 average accuracy, improving by ~3 points over GRPO and ~6.5 points over VAPO. On smaller models, AsyPPO yields >6% gains over classic PPO on Qwen3-4b-Base and ~3% on Qwen3-8b-Base. The asymmetric design also reduces peak GPU memory by 20% and accelerates training by ~20 seconds per step compared to symmetric PPO, while maintaining GRPO-level compute cost.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AsyPPO outperforms GRPO (the dominant critic-free baseline) by ~3 points on Qwen3-14b-Base and >6% on Qwen3-4b-Base, demonstrating that restoring a lightweight critic recovers value-estimation benefits that group-average baselines miss. Against classic symmetric PPO (full-size critic), AsyPPO matches or exceeds performance while using 20% less peak memory and ~20s less per-step time. Compared to VAPO (a critic-centric RLVR method), AsyPPO delivers ~6.5 points higher accuracy using significantly smaller critics (1.7B vs. full-size). Against Q-RM+PPO (token-level reward model), AsyPPO shows ~4 points advantage without requiring additional reward-model pre-training. Notably, naive asymmetric PPO with a single 1.7B critic fails to guide a 14B actor, but AsyPPO's ensemble + masking design unlocks the same 1.7B critics to deliver substantial gains, lowering the critic-capacity threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on PPO's clipped surrogate objective and GAE-based advantage estimation, but departs from the dominant RL4LLM paradigm (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO) that replaced value functions with group-sampling baselines due to critic training costs at LLM scale. It repurposes ensemble learning from classical deep RL (e.g., Randomized Ensembled Double Q-Learning) into the LLM setting, but inverts the asymmetric architecture direction: prior work in continuous RL reduced actor capacity, whereas here small critics guide a much larger actor. The non-overlapping prompt-level data partitioning for critic diversity is a novel mechanism not present in prior ensemble RL, motivated by the observation that homogeneous pre-trained initialization eliminates the parameter diversity that classical ensembles rely on.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AsyPPO demonstrates that the critic's role in RL4LLM need not be abandoned for computational reasons, offering a practical path to restore fine-grained value estimation with ~20% lower peak memory and faster per-step training than symmetric PPO. The uncertainty-aware loss modifications (advantage masking on low-std states, entropy filtering on high-std states) provide a principled, interpretable mechanism for sample selection that could transfer to other actor-critic settings. Key limitations include evaluation primarily on the Qwen3 family (Llama-3.1-8B results in appendix show smaller absolute gains), a fixed 8k generation budget, and the added complexity of training and maintaining two auxiliary critic models. Open questions raised by the authors include whether cross-family critic ensembles, confidence-weighted aggregation, or longer inference horizons further improve the approach.
