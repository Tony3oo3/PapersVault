## Problem

Humanoid robots performing loco-manipulation are confined to limited workspaces because existing modular or end-to-end approaches lack manipulation-aware locomotion. This stems from two compounding issues: the scarcity of large-scale humanoid teleoperation data that jointly covers locomotion and manipulation, and the limited precision and stability of conventional velocity-tracking RL controllers that fail to execute fine-grained start-stop and directional commands reliably.

## Input / Output

At runtime, the system consumes a single egocentric RGB frame from a head-mounted Intel RealSense D435i camera, a natural-language task instruction, and the robot's proprioceptive state (joint positions/velocities, base angular velocity, gravity vector). The VLA backbone (Prismatic-7B) operates at ~10 Hz and outputs discrete latent action tokens from two separate VQ-VAE codebooks (manipulation and locomotion), which a lightweight decoder maps to dual-arm joint positions and a discrete locomotion command u_t = [s_x, s_y, s_ψ, h*] ∈ {-1,0,1}³ × ℝ. This command is then executed by the LMO RL policy at 50 Hz to produce lower-body torques, creating a two-rate hierarchical control loop.

## Method

The core contribution is a unified latent learning framework that trains two separate Latent Action Models (a manipulation LAM on AgiBot World and a locomotion LAM on ~300 hours of self-collected egocentric videos) using a VQ-VAE architecture built on DINOv2 features, because the two data sources exhibit fundamentally different visual change patterns (static camera with arm motion vs. moving camera with scene flow) that conflict when trained jointly. The VLA is then trained to jointly predict both latent action types given visual observations and language, learning coherent loco-manipulation intentions in a shared discrete space. For low-level execution, a Loco-Manipulation-Oriented (LMO) RL policy replaces continuous velocity tracking with a discrete ternary command interface and a two-stage curriculum: Stage I acquires a basic fall-preventing gait with task-agnostic arm disturbances, while Stage II fixes cruising speeds, enforces directional accuracy via a yaw-drift penalty, and injects structured arm-motion perturbations from AgiBot World to train the legs to compensate for realistic inertial couplings during manipulation.

## Results

On the Agibot X2 humanoid across three loco-manipulation task suites (Bag Packing, Box Loading, Cart Pushing), WholeBodyVLA achieves a 78.0% average success rate, outperforming the best end-to-end baseline OpenVLA-OFT w/LMO (56.7%) by 21.3% and the modular pipeline (64.0%) by 14.0%. The largest per-subgoal gains appear in locomotion-heavy subgoals: Move&Squat (13/25 vs. 6/25 for OpenVLA-OFT) and Rise&Turn (17/25 vs. 12/25). Replacing the LMO controller with a velocity-based RL policy drops performance to 54.0%, confirming that the discrete-command interface contributes 24.0% of the overall success rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WholeBodyVLA outperforms all three baselines across every subgoal. Against the modular pipeline (64.0%), the end-to-end design gains 14.0%, with the largest margin on Box Loading subgoals (Squat&Grasp: 19/25 vs. 9/25; Rise&Turn: 17/25 vs. 9/25), where handoff errors between navigation and manipulation modules are most damaging. Against GR00T w/LMO (42.0%), WholeBodyVLA leads by 36.0%, indicating that GR00T's high-level reasoning is insufficient without unified latent pretraining tailored to loco-manipulation. Against OpenVLA-OFT w/LMO (56.7%), which shares the same Prismatic-7B backbone, the 21.3% gap isolates the contribution of unified latent learning from action-free videos. The velocity-based RL ablation (54.0%) reveals that 91.7% of the performance gap versus the full LMO system comes from locomotion subgoals (Move&Squat: 1/25 vs. 13/25; Rise&Turn: 3/25 vs. 17/25), confirming that the discrete-command interface is critical for reliable start-stop and turning precision.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) VLA models for manipulation (RT-2, OpenVLA, GR00T, Pi0), which handle in-place upper-body tasks but lack locomotion; (2) latent action learning from action-free videos (Genie, LAPA, UniVLA), which the authors extend by training separate locomotion and manipulation LAMs to avoid conflicting attention objectives; and (3) RL-based whole-body controllers (HOMIE, AMO, FALCON), which rely on continuous velocity-tracking interfaces. The key departure is replacing velocity tracking with a discrete command interface (LMO) and using unified latent pretraining from low-cost egocentric videos to bridge the data gap for humanoid loco-manipulation, consolidating what prior systems handle modularly into a single end-to-end framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WholeBodyVLA demonstrates the first end-to-end, large-space autonomous humanoid loco-manipulation on a real bipedal platform, addressing two practical bottlenecks: data scarcity (via action-free video pretraining that reduces teleoperation needs by ~4×) and decision-execution misalignment (via the LMO discrete command interface). The data-scaling results showing that stronger latent pretraining substitutes for more teleoperation trajectories are practically valuable for the humanoid robotics community. Open questions include whether the framework scales to truly dexterous long-horizon tasks, whether active perception and lightweight mapping can be integrated, and whether the separate-LAM design generalizes beyond the AgiBot X2 embodiment.
