## Problem

KV caches in LLMs are universally treated as a mere acceleration mechanism for autoregressive decoding, yet they encode rich contextual information that is discarded after generation. Meanwhile, existing methods for self-evaluation and adaptive reasoning rely on storing full hidden states, which is prohibitively expensive in memory. The paper asks whether the already-present KV cache can be repurposed as a near-zero-cost representation for these downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes only the key and value tensors already resident in the KV cache during standard inference (no extra forward pass or hidden-state hook is required). For KV-CoE, it produces a scalar confidence score per completed reasoning trace, evaluated via AUROC/FPR95. For KVClassifier, it outputs a continuous difficulty score in [0,100] from a lightweight MLP, which gates the injection of special control tokens (think / think_end) to switch between fast and slow thinking modes mid-generation. The pooled representation is formed by concatenating K and V across attention heads, summing over selected token positions, and averaging across selected layers without normalization.

## Method

The core insight is that the KV cache, computed as a byproduct of every inference step, can be aggregated into task-useful embeddings at virtually zero additional cost. For KV-CoE, per-token embeddings are constructed by flattening and averaging key-value tensors across layers, then trajectory metrics (magnitude change Δr_t and angular change Δθ_t) are computed along the token dimension rather than the layer dimension used by vanilla CoE; ablations confirm this token-centric design is essential, as layer-wise KV aggregation degrades sharply. For KVClassifier, a two-layer MLP (hidden dim 512, ReLU) maps the pooled KV representation to a difficulty score, trained on GSM8K and MATH with four discrete difficulty labels derived from whether fast/slow thinking answers are correct. The score drives either a one-step classification switch (commit to fast or slow at generation start) or a two-step generative switch (dynamically inject or remove think tokens at predefined checkpoints during decoding), achieving up to 5.7× token reduction on MATH500 with only ~3% accuracy loss.

## Results

On the Chain-of-Embedding task, KV-CoE-C achieves AUROC 84.12 and FPR95 44.82 on MATH with Qwen2-7B-Instruct, surpassing the original CoE-C (76.68 / 64.48) by 7.4 AUROC points. On TheoremQA, KV-CoE-R reaches AUROC 88.87 and FPR95 54.30, outperforming CoE-R (66.68 / 85.84) by over 22 AUROC points. For Fast/Slow Thinking, KV-Generative on Qwen3-8B reduces average token count on MATH500 from 4,150 (full reasoning) to 727 (a 5.7× reduction) with only a 3.2% accuracy drop (0.610 → 0.578), while KV-Classification maintains near-full-reasoning accuracy (0.604) at 3,963 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the original Chain-of-Embedding (CoE) by Wang et al. (2025b), which constructs trajectories from full hidden states. On Qwen2-7B-Instruct, KV-CoE substantially outperforms CoE: on MATH, KV-CoE-C achieves AUROC 84.12 vs. CoE-C's 76.68 (+7.4), and on TheoremQA, KV-CoE-R reaches AUROC 88.87 vs. CoE-R's 66.68 (+22.2), with FPR95 dropping from 85.84 to 54.30. On Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, the picture is mixed: KV-CoE trails CoE on MATH AUROC (64.36 vs. 72.54) but improves FPR95 (63.82 vs. 75.61), while on TheoremQA KV-CoE clearly wins (AUROC 74.74 vs. 63.12; FPR95 62.93 vs. 89.83). The paper notes a model-version mismatch (CoE baselines reported on Llama3-8B vs. experiments on Llama3.1-8B), which may partially explain the MATH gap. Overall, KV-CoE matches or exceeds CoE on 5 of 6 model-dataset pairs while incurring near-zero additional memory cost, since the KV cache is already stored during standard decoding.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the KV cache—an artifact already computed during autoregressive decoding—as a lightweight representation for downstream tasks, departing from prior work that relies on storing full hidden states for self-evaluation (CoE, INSIDE, Beigi et al.) and adaptive reasoning (ASRR, PATS, DOTS). It directly builds on Chain-of-Embedding (Wang et al., 2025b) by substituting hidden-state trajectories with KV-cache-derived token trajectories, and is complementary to KV cache steering (Belitsky et al., 2025), which modifies the cache rather than reading from it. The key conceptual shift is treating the KV cache not merely as a speedup mechanism but as a free, reusable substrate for sampling and reasoning decisions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reusing the KV cache incurs virtually zero additional memory or compute, making self-evaluation and adaptive reasoning deployable in production inference stacks (vLLM, Ollama) without architectural changes. However, the paper is explicit that KV caches are poor general-purpose embeddings (MTEB scores far below dedicated models), and their utility is scoped to local, restricted-set comparisons and trajectory geometry rather than global semantic retrieval. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to larger models or non-math domains, whether the difficulty estimator transfers across model families without retraining, and whether the token-centric KV-CoE design can be extended to other latent-space analysis tasks.
