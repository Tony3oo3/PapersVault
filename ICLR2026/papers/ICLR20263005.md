## Problem

Generative retrieval (GR) models, which encode corpus information into LM parameters and retrieve by generating document identifiers, perform well under supervised training but fail to generalize to zero-shot IR scenarios. Real-world retrieval involves heterogeneous corpora (text, tables, code), diverse query types, and task-specific relevance criteria, yet existing GR methods are trained on single-domain data and cannot adapt without task-specific supervision. This paper asks whether GR can be made generalizable across diverse IR tasks using only natural language instructions.

## Input / Output

At inference, ZEROGR takes a text query concatenated with a natural language task instruction (e.g., 'Given a legal case, retrieve the most relevant statute') and generates a ranked list of document identifiers. Each docid is a short keyword-rich sentence of 6–8 words (e.g., 'rna folding computational methods thermodynamic optimization'), decoded sequentially over a prefix tree of valid docids with a reverse-annealing temperature schedule. The corpus itself can contain heterogeneous document types—paragraphs, tables, code files, legal statutes—which are all mapped to the same unified text-based docid format by a separate generator model.

## Method

ZEROGR comprises three components: (1) a docid generator (Llama-3.2-1B fine-tuned on GPT-4o-generated pairs) that converts any document into a unified 6–8 word keyword-rich text identifier, replacing brittle rule-based or RQ-VAE docids; (2) an instructed query generator (Llama-1B instruction-tuned on OpenInstIR, a new 41M-pair dataset spanning 69 IR tasks across 6 domains) that produces diverse pseudo-queries conditioned on the task instruction to enrich corpus indexing; and (3) a reverse-annealing decoding strategy that generates docids token-by-token over a prefix tree while gradually increasing the sampling temperature via a normalized sigmoid schedule, yielding high-precision early selections followed by broader exploration to balance precision and recall. The generative retriever (Llama-3B) is trained solely on synthetic (instruction + pseudo-query → docid) pairs with a standard cross-entropy objective, requiring no IR-specific supervision at indexing time. The key empirical finding is that increasing the diversity and quantity of instruction-tuning tasks consistently improves zero-shot retrieval on unseen tasks, and that 8+ pseudo-queries per document are needed to match BM25-level performance.

## Results

On the MAIR benchmark (38 tasks, Acc@1), ZEROGR-3B achieves an average of 41.1, outperforming BM25 (36.1), BGE-Large (39.4), and OpenAI-Embed-v3 (40.6), while trailing the 7B instruction-tuned models E5-Mistral-7B (46.8) and GritLM-7B (47.0). On BEIR (11 tasks, nDCG@10), ZEROGR-3B scores 48.1, surpassing BM25 (42.4), GTR-T5-large (48.0), and GritLM-7B (45.0), but falling short of BGE-Large (51.8) and OpenAI-Embed-v3 (54.2). Among generative retrieval methods specifically, ZEROGR achieves the best average nDCG@10 of 44.9 on BEIR, compared to GENRET (41.1), TIGER (31.0), and GENRE (23.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On MAIR (Acc@1), ZEROGR-3B (41.1) outperforms all sub-7B baselines including BM25 (+5.0), BGE-Large (+1.7), and OpenAI-Embed-v3 (+0.5), and is competitive with the much larger E5-Mistral-7B (46.8) and GritLM-7B (47.0) at roughly one-third the parameter count. On BEIR (nDCG@10), ZEROGR (48.1) beats BM25 (+5.7), GTR-T5-large (+0.1), and GritLM-7B (+3.1), but trails BGE-Large (51.8) and OpenAI-Embed-v3 (54.2) by 3.7 and 6.1 points respectively, which the authors attribute to dense retrieval's years of targeted zero-shot optimization. Among generative retrieval methods, ZEROGR substantially outperforms GENRE (23.0), TIGER (31.0), and GENRET (41.1) on BEIR nDCG@10, establishing a new SOTA for GR. Notably, ZEROGR achieves the best Code-domain score (36.3) on MAIR among all sub-7B models, and its Legal-domain performance (40.0) exceeds all baselines except the 7B instruction-tuned models. The gap to 7B dense retrievers on BEIR is expected given that ZEROGR's retrieval model is trained solely on synthetic pseudo-queries without IR-specific pre-training or hard-negative mining.

## Relation to Prior Work

ZEROGR extends the Differentiable Search Index (DSI) paradigm of Tay et al. (2022), which encodes a corpus into LM parameters and retrieves by generating document identifiers, and the pseudo-query indexing strategy of DSI-QG (Zhuang et al., 2022). It departs from prior GR in three ways: replacing rule-based or RQ-VAE docids with a model-generated natural-language docid, conditioning pseudo-query generation on task instructions (an idea borrowed from instruction-tuned dense retrievers like E5-Mistral and GritLM but never applied to GR), and introducing reverse-annealed decoding to balance precision and recall. The paper is explicitly the first to apply instruction fine-tuning to generative retrieval and to release a large-scale open-source instruction-tuning corpus (OpenInstIR) for this purpose.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ZEROGR is the first GR framework to demonstrate competitive zero-shot generalization across heterogeneous IR tasks (code, legal, medical, finance, academic, web), and the 41-million-pair OpenInstIR dataset fills a critical gap in open-source training resources for instruction-conditioned retrieval. However, the 3B model still trails 7B instruction-tuned dense retrievers (E5-Mistral, GritLM) on BEIR, and the authors acknowledge the lack of evaluation on corpora exceeding 1M documents. Open questions include whether the instruction-conditioned GR paradigm scales favorably with model size and corpus size, and whether the performance gap to mature dense systems can be closed with IR-specific pre-training or hard-negative mining.
