## Problem

Scientific ML models for predicting complex physical systems are almost exclusively trained and validated on simulated data from numerical solvers, leaving a critical gap in evaluating their true real-world performance. This absence of paired real-world measurements hinders progress on essential tasks such as sim-to-real transfer, learning from noisy data, and understanding how measurement limitations affect model accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263642/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The prediction task takes as input the Cartesian product of initial discretized system states (spatiotemporal fields stored as NumPy arrays of shape (T, X, Y) in HDF5) and system parameters (Reynolds number, oscillation frequency, equivalence ratio), and outputs the subsequent discretized temporal evolution of the system. A key practical distinction is that real-world data (from PIV or OH* chemiluminescence imaging) contains fewer modalities than simulated data (from CFD solvers), with measurement noise replacing numerical errors, motivating a random masking strategy for unmeasured modalities during simulated training.

## Method

RealPDEBench is a benchmark rather than a single architecture: it provides five paired real-world/simulated datasets (Cylinder, Controlled Cylinder, FSI, Foil, Combustion) totaling 736 trajectories, three training paradigms (real-world training, simulated training, and simulated pretraining with real-world finetuning), nine evaluation metrics spanning data-oriented (RMSE, MAE, Rel L2, R², Update Ratio) and physics-oriented (fRMSE, Frequency Error, Kinetic Energy Error, MVPE) categories, and ten baselines ranging from DMD to pretrained PDE foundation models (DPOT). The central empirical finding is that a significant performance gap exists between simulated and real-world data (9.39%–78.91% Rel L2 improvement from real-world training), yet simulated pretraining consistently accelerates convergence and improves final accuracy on real-world data, as quantified by the Update Ratio metric being below 1 for most model-dataset pairs. The benchmark is implemented as a unified PyTorch codebase with modular RealDataset and Model interfaces for straightforward extensibility.

## Results

Across all five datasets, real-world training improves Rel L2 error by 9.39%–78.91% over simulated training, confirming a significant sim-to-real gap. Simulated pretraining followed by real-world finetuning (Real-world Finetuning) achieves the best RMSE in most cases; for example, on Cylinder, DPOT-L-FT reaches RMSE 0.0394 (finetuning) vs. 0.0390 (real-world training) vs. 0.0486 (simulated training). The Update Ratio is below 1 for most model–dataset pairs, indicating that finetuning from simulated pretraining requires fewer iterations to match the best real-world-trained performance. The Combustion dataset is the most challenging, with Rel L2 errors exceeding 0.5 for most baselines under real-world training.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DPOT-L-FT (649.8M–674.2M params), the large pretrained PDE foundation model, achieves the best overall performance across all five datasets under real-world finetuning, reaching the lowest RMSE (e.g., 0.0394 on Cylinder, 0.0085 on Controlled Cylinder) and the lowest Frequency Error in the RMSE-vs-FE trade-off (Figure 4). Convolution-based models (U-Net, CNO) achieve competitive or lower RMSE than operator-based methods, reflecting their strength in local pixel-level accuracy, but they degrade faster under autoregressive evaluation (CNO's error grows most rapidly over 10 rounds). MWT, leveraging multiwavelet transforms, shows the best Frequency Error among non-pretrained models, indicating superior capture of temporal periodicity. The traditional DMD baseline is consistently the worst performer (e.g., Rel L2 of 0.359 on Cylinder vs. 0.073 for DPOT-L-FT), highlighting the advantage of learned models. Across all baselines, simulated training is uniformly worse than real-world training (9.39%–78.91% higher Rel L2), and real-world finetuning from simulated pretraining matches or exceeds direct real-world training, with an average Update Ratio of 0.57 on Cylinder (i.e., ~43% fewer iterations needed).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by introducing the first scientific ML benchmark that pairs real-world experimental measurements with corresponding numerical simulations, departing from prior PDE benchmarks (PDEBench, The Well, CFD-Bench, FlowBench) that rely exclusively on simulated data. It repurposes the standard spatiotemporal prediction task and the existing suite of neural operator, CNN, transformer, and diffusion-based architectures, but reframes the evaluation protocol around three training paradigms (simulated-only, real-world-only, and simulated-pretrain + real-world-finetune) to explicitly study the sim-to-real gap. The concurrent work REALM (Mao et al., 2025) addresses a similar motivation for reactive flows, but RealPDEBench covers five distinct physical systems with 736 paired trajectories and a broader set of baselines and metrics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a fundamental and previously unquantified gap: models trained on simulated data show 9–79% worse Rel L2 on real-world data, and simulated data fail to capture temporal periodicity (higher Frequency Errors). The consistent finding that simulated pretraining accelerates convergence and improves final accuracy on real-world data provides a practical recipe for data-scarce real-world deployment, but the gap remains substantial, especially for multi-physics combustion. Open questions include how to better exploit unmeasured modalities in simulations, how measurement noise characteristics (PIV vs. CL imaging) differentially affect model performance, and whether the sim-to-real transfer benefits generalize to 3D full-field prediction rather than 2D cross-sections.
