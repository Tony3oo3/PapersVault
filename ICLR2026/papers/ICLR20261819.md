## Problem

Nonlinear RNNs such as LSTM and GRU are inherently sequential along the input sequence, making their training prohibitively slow at scale and preventing them from competing with parallelizable architectures like Transformers and State Space Models. SSMs like Mamba achieve parallel training by constraining the recurrence to be purely linear in the hidden state, but this linearity limits expressive power for complex sequence-wise dependencies. The paper asks whether the sequence-parallelization barrier for genuinely nonlinear RNNs can be broken, enabling them to be trained at 7B-parameter scale for language modeling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes tokenized text sequences of length L (2048 tokens in training) with per-position input vectors x_l ∈ R^{d_in}, and produces next-token predictions via a linear head over the final hidden state. The RNN cells (ParaGRU, ParaLSTM) maintain a hidden state h_l ∈ R^{d_h} (or R^{2d_h} for LSTM, combining cell state and output), and the parallelization operates over the sequence dimension L. Evaluation outputs include perplexity on SlimPajama and accuracy scores on lm-eval-harness benchmarks (ARC-C, HellaSwag, OBQA, Winogrande, PiQA, MMLU).

## Method

ParaRNN recasts the sequential application of a nonlinear RNN over a sequence of length L as a single system of L coupled nonlinear equations, solved iteratively via Newton's method. Each Newton iteration linearizes the system into a block bi-diagonal linear system whose solution is recovered in O(log L) parallel steps using a custom parallel reduction (prefix sum) algorithm, rather than O(L) sequential forward substitution. To keep the Jacobian multiplications tractable, the state and peephole matrices A_* and C_* are constrained to be diagonal, reducing Jacobian storage to O(L·d_h) and pairwise multiplication cost to O(d_h), at the cost of decoupling hidden-state dimensions. The backward pass is inherently linear and requires only a single parallel reduction. Three implementation tiers are provided: a pure PyTorch reference, a CUDA-accelerated parallel reduction with PyTorch Jacobian assembly, and a fully-fused CUDA kernel that integrates Newton iterations, Jacobian assembly, and parallel reduction into a single kernel, achieving up to 665× speedup over naive sequential application.

## Results

At 7B scale, ParaLSTM achieves a perplexity of 9.16 and ParaGRU 9.19 on SlimPajama, both outperforming the DCLM Transformer (9.55) but trailing Mamba2 (8.62). On downstream tasks, ParaGRU scores 39.68 on ARC-C (25-shot), 65.85 on HellaSwag (10-shot), and 25.29 on MMLU, while ParaLSTM scores 37.46, 62.47, and 25.31 respectively. In terms of runtime, the fully-fused CUDA implementation achieves ~2.6× speedup over Mamba for ParaGRU and ~1.5× for ParaLSTM at L=2^9, with inference throughput of ~37-38 tokens/sec versus ~28 for Mamba and ~0.42 for the Transformer at 7B scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Mamba2, both ParaGRU and ParaLSTM trail on perplexity (9.16/9.19 vs. 8.62) and most downstream benchmarks, with Mamba2 leading by 3-4 points on HellaSwag and ~1 point on MMLU; however, ParaGRU matches Mamba2 on OBQA and PiQA. Against the DCLM Transformer, both ParaRNN variants clearly win: ParaLSTM improves PPL by 0.39 (9.16 vs. 9.55) and gains 3+ points on HellaSwag, 2+ on OBQA, and 2+ on MMLU, while ParaGRU gains 0.36 on PPL and 3+ points on ARC-C and HellaSwag. On synthetic expressivity tasks (Table 1), ParaGRU and ParaLSTM with diagonal Jacobians solve most tasks perfectly but degrade on the hardest (A5: 38-40% vs. 100% for vanilla LSTM/GRU with full Jacobians), while consistently outperforming Mamba2 across all tasks. In runtime, the fully-fused CUDA kernel beats Mamba by ~2.6× (ParaGRU) and ~1.5× (ParaLSTM) for forward passes at L=2^9, and inference throughput is ~37-38 tok/s versus ~28 for Mamba and ~0.42 for the 7B Transformer. Overall, ParaRNN positions classical nonlinear RNNs as competitive with modern architectures, closing the gap with Mamba2 while clearly surpassing the Transformer baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Newton-iteration-plus-parallel-reduction framework introduced by Danieli et al. (2023) for ResNets and Diffusion Models, and the GRU-specific application by Lim et al. (2024) and Gonzalez et al. (2024), to LLM-scale language modeling. The key departure from Gonzalez et al. is that rather than approximating the RNN Jacobian with its diagonal (a quasi-Newton approach that also requires approximating the backward pass), ParaRNN redesigns the GRU and LSTM cells to use diagonal state matrices, yielding naturally structured Jacobians that enable exact Newton iterations. This design choice mirrors the diagonal state matrix constraint in Mamba/S4D, positioning ParaRNN as a nonlinear generalization of the SSM parallelization paradigm while removing the linearity requirement that limits SSM expressivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ParaRNN demonstrates for the first time that classical nonlinear RNNs can be trained at 7B-parameter scale with perplexity competitive with Transformers and Mamba2, reviving them as viable architecture options for LLMs. The open-source framework lowers the barrier for exploring new nonlinear RNN cells at scale. However, the diagonal Jacobian constraint (necessary for efficient parallel reduction) demonstrably limits expressivity on complex synthetic tasks like A5 group products, and Mamba2 still achieves lower perplexity. A key open question is whether alternative Jacobian structures (Householder matrices, sparse patterns) can be integrated into ParaRNN to recover full expressivity while preserving parallelizability, which the authors identify as an exciting avenue for future work.
