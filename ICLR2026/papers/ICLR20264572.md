## Problem

Offline RL agents trained on delay-free simulator data fail when deployed in real-world environments with observation or actuation delays, because the Markov assumption is violated and out-of-distribution queries compound over time. Existing delayed RL methods require online interaction with the delayed environment, while existing offline RL methods ignore latency entirely. The paper asks: how can one learn a delay-robust policy solely from static, delay-free trajectories so that it generalizes reliably to delayed deployment?

## Input / Output

During offline training, the method consumes standard D4RL trajectory tuples (s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1}) and manually constructs augmented inputs x_t = {s_{t-Δ}, a_{t-Δ}, …, a_{t-1}} by stacking Δ steps of history. At deployment, the agent receives only the delayed observation o_t and maintains a circular action buffer of length Δ; missing actions at episode boundaries are filled with [MASK] tokens. The transformer belief model outputs a sequence of predicted states (ŝ_{t-Δ+1} through ŝ_t), and the policy produces a single continuous control action a_t conditioned on the final predicted state ŝ_t.

## Method

DT-CORL jointly trains a transformer-based belief predictor and a behavior-regularized policy within a single constrained policy-iteration loop, rather than using a two-stage pipeline that freezes the belief model. The key reformulation maps the augmented delayed-MDP policy iteration back to the original state space via the belief distribution b_Δ(s|x), using Lipschitz-continuity bounds on Q-functions to control the approximation gap (Lemmas 4.1–4.2) and proving monotonic policy improvement (Proposition 4.3). The policy improvement step regularizes toward the behavior policy via an MSE penalty (following TD3+BC/ReBRAC), avoiding expensive Wasserstein or KL computations. The transformer predicts a full Δ-step state sequence rather than a single state, reducing compounding prediction error, and is trained with MSE (deterministic MDPs) or MLE (stochastic MDPs). This joint optimization keeps the critic, policy, and belief model aligned throughout training, preventing the distribution mismatch and value-target bias that plague separately trained belief modules.

## Results

On D4RL MuJoCo locomotion tasks (Hopper, HalfCheetah, Walker2d) under deterministic observation delays, DT-CORL achieves normalized returns of 85.0% on Hopper-medium (Δ=8), 112.2% on Hopper-med-expert (Δ=8), and 112.0% on Walker2d-med-expert (Δ=8), consistently outperforming Belief-CQL (56.8%, 39.5%, 99.5% respectively) and Belief-IQL (29.3%, 26.6%, 17.3%). On AntMaze goal-conditioned tasks, DT-CORL reaches 88.3% on umaze (stochastic Δ=4) and 88.0% (stochastic Δ=8), while augmentation-based baselines degrade sharply (e.g., Augmented-CQL drops from 61.3% to 12.7% as delay increases from 4 to 16). The online SOTA method DBPT-SAC completely collapses to 0.0% in the offline setting across all AntMaze tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DT-CORL consistently outperforms Belief-CQL across nearly all MuJoCo settings, with the largest gaps appearing at longer delays (e.g., Hopper-med-expert Δ=16: 109.9 vs. 35.2) and on HalfCheetah where Belief-CQL degrades to near-zero (1.5–6.5%) while DT-CORL maintains 5–28%. Belief-IQL is dramatically worse than DT-CORL in all settings (typically 15–30% vs. 85–113%), because IQL's exponential advantage weighting amplifies small belief prediction errors into unstable policy updates. Naive CQL and IQL without any delay adaptation perform poorly (typically <10% on Hopper, <65% on Walker2d), confirming that delay handling is essential. On AntMaze, DT-CORL outperforms Augmented-BC, Augmented-CQL, and Augmented-COMBO across all delay lengths, with the gap widening as delay increases (e.g., umaze Δ=16: DT-CORL 67.3% vs. Augmented-CQL 12.7%). The online SOTA method DBPT-SAC achieves 0.0% across all AntMaze tasks in the offline setting, confirming that online delayed RL methods are fundamentally incompatible with offline training.

## Relation to Prior Work

DT-CORL sits at the intersection of delayed RL and offline RL, two well-studied but previously disjoint threads. On the delayed-RL side, it departs from both augmentation-based methods (DIDA, ADRL, VDPO) that suffer dimensional blow-up, and belief-based online methods (DATS, D-Dreamer, DFBT) that assume online interaction with a delayed environment. On the offline-RL side, it builds on the BRAC/TD3+BC behavior-regularization framework and CQL/IQL value-conservatism, but reformulates the constrained policy-iteration objective in the original (non-augmented) state space via a transformer belief. The key conceptual departure is the joint, end-to-end training of the belief model and the policy within a single policy-iteration loop, as opposed to the two-stage pipeline (pretrain belief, then freeze and run offline RL) that prior belief-based delayed-RL work implicitly assumes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically critical gap: most real robotic and autonomous systems are trained in delay-free simulators but deployed under unavoidable communication/computation latency, and collecting delayed interaction data is often infeasible. The finding that joint belief-policy optimization substantially outperforms a frozen two-stage pipeline (Table 5) and that the performance gap widens with delay length provides a clear design principle for future offline-to-online delay adaptation. Key limitations include the assumption of known, fixed delay (no handling of unknown or time-varying latency), restriction to low-dimensional state-vector observations (no vision), and the fact that on harder AntMaze tasks (medium-play, large-play) all methods perform poorly, suggesting the approach may need goal-conditioned modifications to scale to more complex navigation.
