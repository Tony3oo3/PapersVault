## Problem

Deep reasoning paradigms have driven major advances in verifiable domains like math and code, but open-ended creative generation lacks the clear reward signals that RL depends on and the high-quality teacher trajectories that distillation requires. This paper asks how to instill structured, human-like deep reasoning for open-ended generation from scratch, without relying on either reinforcement learning or costly proprietary-model distillation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261558/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For data synthesis, the method takes a (query, reference-solution) pair and produces a deep reasoning trajectory z* — a first-person, stream-of-consciousness thinking process with explicit self-reflection markers (e.g., 'Hmm…', 'Wait, that's a bit…') that logically explains how the reference solution was planned. At inference time, the trained model receives only the user query and outputs a thinking block followed by the final generated text. The trajectory is structured as a sequence of discrete reasoning segments, each refined independently, and is constrained to avoid past tense, bullet points, or direct copying of the reference solution.

## Method

REER reframes reasoning synthesis as a gradient-free local search: given a known-good output y, find the trajectory z* that minimizes the perplexity PPL(y | x, z), using the model's surprise at the reference solution as a proxy for reasoning quality. Starting from an initial LLM-generated plan z^(0), the algorithm iteratively selects one segment, prompts a generator LLM (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) for candidate refinements with full context (query, reference solution, surrounding segments), and keeps the candidate that most reduces perplexity, guaranteeing monotonic improvement. Two context-engineering choices are critical: a meta-structure that constrains edits to the target segment, and explicit injection of human-like reflection triggers to prevent formulaic reasoning. The resulting 20K trajectories (DeepWriting-20K), filtered for degenerate repetition and end-of-thinking loops, are mixed with OpenThoughts data and used to instruction-tune Qwen3-8B-Base for 3 epochs, yielding DeepWriter-8B.

## Results

DeepWriter-8B achieves 91.3 on LongBench-Write, 82.6/87.4 on HelloBench (HB-A/HB-B), and an average of ~71.4 across the six WritingBench domains. It surpasses the open-source baseline LongWriter-8B by an average of over 18 points on WritingBench and by 14.8 points on LongBench-Write. On the creative HelloBench task (HB-B), DeepWriter-8B (87.4) is statistically on par with GPT-4o (87.6) and close to Claude 3.5 (88.3), while on LongBench-Write it exceeds both GPT-4o (83.1) and Claude 3.5 (89.3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary open-source baseline LongWriter-8B, DeepWriter-8B wins on every single benchmark, with the largest gaps on WritingBench (e.g., WB-A: 72.2 vs 57.9, WB-D: 70.6 vs 52.0) and LongBench-Write (91.3 vs 76.5), confirming that perplexity-guided trajectory synthesis substantially outperforms standard instruction-tuning for long-form generation. Against Claude 3.5, DeepWriter-8B dominates all six WritingBench categories by 10–14 points (e.g., WB-A: 72.2 vs 59.1) and leads on LongBench-Write (91.3 vs 89.3), though it trails slightly on HB-B (87.4 vs 88.3). Against GPT-4o, DeepWriter-8B wins on LongBench-Write (91.3 vs 83.1) and is essentially tied on HB-B (87.4 vs 87.6), but lags by 2–7 points across the WritingBench professional domains. Against the strongest proprietary model Claude 3.7, DeepWriter-8B remains competitive but consistently trails by 5–10 points, with the largest gap on HB-B (87.4 vs 93.2) and LongBench-Write (91.3 vs 97.8). Overall, an 8B model trained from scratch on 20K reverse-engineered trajectories closes most of the gap to frontier proprietary models without any distillation or RL.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the two dominant paradigms for instilling reasoning—reinforcement learning (which requires verifiable reward signals) and instruction distillation (which is costly and capped by the teacher)—by proposing a third path: reverse-engineering reasoning trajectories backwards from known-good outputs. Conceptually, it repurposes the idea of using a proxy signal for quality (similar in spirit to VeriFree's likelihood-based reward for verifiable domains) but applies it in a gradient-free local search over reasoning segments rather than as an RL objective. The data synthesis pipeline draws on synthetic data generation techniques (Self-Instruct, STaR/Quiet-STaR) and long-form writing datasets (LongWriter, WildChat), but the perplexity-guided iterative segment refinement is a novel mechanism not present in prior work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of DeepWriting-20K addresses a critical data scarcity bottleneck for open-ended generation research, and the REER paradigm offers a scalable, cost-effective alternative to both RL and distillation that could extend to other non-verifiable domains (agentic tasks, scientific discovery, multi-modal reasoning). Key limitations include reliance on having high-quality reference solutions available, the perplexity proxy potentially missing dimensions of reasoning quality beyond fluency, and evaluation conducted with LLM judges that carry inherent biases. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, whether the perplexity signal remains a valid proxy at scale, and how well the synthesized trajectories transfer across domains beyond writing.
