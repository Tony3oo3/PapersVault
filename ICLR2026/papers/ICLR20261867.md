## Problem

Standard undirected exploration (e.g., Gaussian noise) in online RL suffers from vanishing coverage as action dimensionality grows, making it sample-inefficient for high-dimensional, over-actuated systems such as full-body musculoskeletal models. Existing remedies rely on dimensionality reduction or low-rank subspaces, which constrain policy expressiveness and forfeit the redundancy that high-dimensional systems are designed to exploit. The paper asks whether directed, value-aligned exploration can be conducted directly in the native high-dimensional action space without such reductions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a high-dimensional state vector comprising joint positions/velocities/accelerations, per-actuator activation, force, length, and velocity, plus body-frame positions (e.g., 2,062-dim state for MS-Human-700 with 206 joints and 700 muscle-tendon units). It outputs a bounded action vector in [-1,1]^|A| representing muscle activations, produced by first sampling from a learnable Gaussian policy and then transporting the sample through a learned velocity field via 20 discrete Euler steps of a conditional ODE. The action-space boundary is enforced by capping each gradient-ascent step to the l2-diameter of the admissible box, preventing out-of-domain actions during flow construction.

## Method

QFLEX constructs a Q-guided probability flow that transports a learnable Gaussian source policy π_θ^(0) toward a target distribution obtained by N=20 gradient-ascent steps on the learned Q-function, with a truncated step size that respects the [-1,1]^|A| action boundary. The transport is parameterized by a state-dependent velocity field v_w(t, s, a^(t)) trained via conditional flow matching (Eq. 17) against the linear interpolation path between source and Q-guided target samples. A key theoretical result (Proposition 1) proves that the expected state-action value is monotone nondecreasing along the flow under mild regularity conditions, giving the exploration a policy-improvement guarantee. The full algorithm embeds this flow-based policy into a standard actor-critic loop with batch-normalized Q-networks (eliminating target networks) and a low update-to-data ratio, and is implemented in JAX with 20-step Euler integration for the ODE.

## Results

QFLEX demonstrates consistently superior learning efficiency across all nine high-dimensional continuous-control benchmarks (SMPL Humanoid-Jump, Unitree H1-Run/Balance, MyoHand-PenTwirl, MyoLeg-Walk, Ostrich-Run, MS-Human-700-Walk/Run/Dance), with the performance gap widening as action dimensionality and over-actuation increase. On the 700-actuator MS-Human-700-Walk task, QFLEX achieves a final reward of 307.51±20.37 with total actuator activation of 307.51±20.37, compared to CrossQ's 356.87±22.57 (lower activation is better). On MyoLeg-Walk, QFLEX achieves 34.26±2.84 total actuator activation versus CrossQ's 38.47±3.49. Per-step deployment time is 0.49±0.19 ms, comparable to Gaussian-based methods (0.11–0.13 ms) and substantially faster than diffusion-based baselines (2.95–4.07 ms).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QFLEX outperforms all ten baselines across every benchmark, with the margin growing as action dimensionality increases. Against Gaussian-based methods (SAC, CrossQ), QFLEX achieves substantially higher final rewards on all tasks and lower total muscle activation (e.g., 34.26 vs. 38.47 on MyoLeg-Walk; 307.51 vs. 356.87 on MS-Human-700-Walk). Against diffusion-based online RL methods (SDAC, DACER, QSM), QFLEX converges faster and reaches higher performance, while also being faster at deployment (0.49 ms vs. 2.95–4.07 ms per step). Against high-dimensional musculoskeletal-specific baselines (DynSyn, Lattice, DEP-RL), QFLEX achieves the largest gains, particularly on the 700-actuator MS-Human-700 tasks where these methods rely on dimensionality reduction. In additional single-environment comparisons, QFLEX substantially outperforms FlowRL (Lv et al., 2025) and MaxInfoRL (Sukhija et al., 2024) on Unitree H1-Balance, confirming its advantage over both flow-based and intrinsic-motivation-based exploration in high-dimensional settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

QFLEX sits at the intersection of two research threads: flow matching as a generative/transport mechanism (Lipman et al., 2023) and actor-critic online RL with learned value functions. It departs from diffusion-based RL methods (SDAC, DACER, QSM) that parameterize policies via iterative denoising from a fixed standard Gaussian, instead using a learnable Gaussian source distribution and a Q-gradient-guided velocity field to transport actions toward high-value regions. It also contrasts with dimensionality-reduction approaches (DynSyn, Lattice, DEP-RL) that restrict exploration to low-rank subspaces; QFLEX operates in the native action space, preserving full actuator redundancy. The batch-normalized Q-network from CrossQ (Bhatt et al., 2024) is adopted to stabilize value learning without target networks, enabling the low update-to-data ratio that makes the Q-guided flow construction tractable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, theoretically grounded (Proposition 1: monotone policy improvement) alternative to both isotropic noise and dimensionality reduction for exploration in over-actuated systems, with the performance gap widening as action dimensionality grows—a regime where prior methods degrade sharply. The successful control of a 700-actuator full-body musculoskeletal model performing ballet dancing demonstrates practical scalability that was previously unachieved without subspace constraints. Open questions include the choice of preconditioner M (currently identity), whether the finite-step ODE approximation degrades at even higher dimensions, and systematic comparison with natural-gradient or curvature-adaptive variants that the authors defer to future work.
