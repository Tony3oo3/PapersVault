## Problem

Existing self-improving methods for LLMs (e.g., STaR) rely on textual coherence and answer correctness to refine reasoning paths, but they cannot verify or rectify visual hallucinations embedded in VLM-generated rationales. This paper identifies that VLMs exhibit a 'contrastive seeing' property—presenting two visually similar images with synonymous questions forces the model to ground its reasoning in fine-grained visual evidence—and asks whether this property can be harnessed to bootstrap visual reasoning without external reward models or hand-crafted reasoning structures.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263410/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the finetuned model takes a single RGB image and a text question and produces a chain-of-thought rationale followed by a text answer, following the standard VLM inference paradigm without any contrastive pair. During training-data generation, the pipeline consumes contrastive VQA pairs—two visually similar images paired with synonymous questions—alongside ground-truth answers, and the rethinking step uses a 72B LLM to rewrite the coarse rationale into a faithful one structured as <think>…<answer>…</answer>. The final VisCoR-55K dataset contains 55K such (image, question, answer, rationale) quadruples spanning general VQA, reasoning, math, chart/graph, and OCR domains.

## Method

VC-STaR is a three-step self-improving pipeline: (1) a thinking step where the VLM generates a coarse rationale given the image, question, and ground-truth answer as a hint; (2) a contrasting step where the same VLM compares the target sample with its curated contrastive counterpart (two visually similar images, synonymous questions) to produce a contrastive analysis that is more visually grounded; (3) a rethinking step where a 72B LLM rewrites the coarse rationale using the contrastive analysis as a more reliable visual reference. Contrastive VQA pairs are curated from 21 datasets via a three-stage process: embedding-based pair hunting (GTE text embeddings + a custom ID-based visual metric-learning model) with cosine-similarity thresholds, followed by difficulty-based sampling that retains only 'median' samples where the model initially fails but succeeds under contrasting with hints. The resulting VisCoR-55K dataset is used for full-parameter SFT (3 epochs, lr 1e-5, batch 256, frozen vision tower) on base VLMs such as Qwen2.5VL-7B, yielding consistent gains across hallucination, math, and general benchmarks without requiring the contrastive pipeline at test time.

## Results

Using Qwen2.5VL-7B as the base model, VC-STaR achieves an average score of 58.1 across six benchmarks, a +2.6 point improvement over the base model (55.5). The largest gains appear on hallucination-sensitive benchmarks: +5.7 on MMVP (75.7) and +3.2 on HallusionBench (56.3), while also improving MathVista (+1.3 to 69.7), MathVision (+1.3 to 25.3), and MME-RealWorld (+3.4 to 59.3). The method generalizes to other base models, yielding +6.3 on HallusionBench for Qwen2.5VL-3B and +7.2 for InternVL2.5-8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VC-STaR outperforms all three self-improving baselines (STaR, Verifier, Feedback) on average, with the most dramatic gap over STaR on MathVision (25.3 vs 19.8, +5.5) and MMStar (62.4 vs 58.9, +3.5). Against off-the-shelf reasoning datasets, VC-STaR dominates Virgo (58.1 vs 48.2 avg) and R1-OV (58.1 vs 52.8 avg), confirming that visually-native contrastive rationales outperform purely textual or caption-based approaches. The closest competitor is LPT (56.9 avg), which VC-STaR surpasses by 1.2 points overall; however, LPT edges out VC-STaR on MMStar (64.3 vs 62.4), as do Feedback (63.2) and LLaVA-CoT (63.1) on that single benchmark. VC-STaR also narrowly trails R1-OV by 0.1 on MathVision (25.3 vs 25.4) but leads by 9.2 points on MMStar and 13.0 on MME-RealWorld. The key differentiator is that VC-STaR achieves consistent gains across all six benchmarks simultaneously, whereas baselines like STaR and Verifier sacrifice math and general performance to gain on hallucination metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the STaR self-improving paradigm (Zelikman et al., 2022) from text-only reasoning to the visual domain, where the central obstacle is that visual hallucinations in generated rationales cannot be verified or rectified by text-centric feedback loops. The key conceptual departure is repurposing the VLM's inherent multi-image comparison ability—well-studied in contrastive learning (CLIP, DINOv2) and change-detection literature—as a mechanism to suppress hallucinations during rationale generation, rather than as a representation-learning tool. The method also draws on difficulty-based data selection and LLM-guided rethinking, but the contrastive VQA pair curation pipeline (synonymous questions + visually similar images) is the novel architectural contribution that distinguishes it from prior self-improving baselines like STaR, Verifier, and Feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a recognized gap: existing self-improving methods for VLMs cannot verify visual claims in reasoning paths, leading to speculative rationales that privilege textual priors over visual evidence. The VisCoR-55K dataset and the model-agnostic VC-STaR pipeline offer a practical, scalable recipe for generating hallucination-reduced rationales without external reward models or hand-crafted reasoning templates. However, the approach depends on a 72B LLM for the rethinking step, the contrastive pair curation is dataset-dependent (as shown by the HQ-Edit/DOCCI ablation), and the method is restricted to median-difficulty samples, leaving open whether it generalizes to multi-step compositional reasoning or can be applied at inference time without the curation overhead.
