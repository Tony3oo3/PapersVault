## Problem

Causal inference for time-to-event outcomes across multiple data sources remains underdeveloped when populations are heterogeneous and privacy regulations (GDPR, HIPAA) prevent direct data pooling. Existing federated causal inference methods target continuous or binary outcomes and do not address right-censored survival data, while naive pooling or meta-analytic approaches require a common conditional outcome distribution (CCOD) assumption that frequently fails under covariate, outcome, or censoring shift, yielding biased estimates.

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-source right-censored survival records (baseline covariates X, binary treatment A, observed time Y = min(T,C), event indicator Δ, site indicator R) along with a finite time horizon τ and a discretized time grid. Critically, only summary-level quantities cross site boundaries: the target site transmits its fitted S⁰ model parameters and covariate summary statistics for the density-ratio model, while each source site returns only its estimated influence functions to a central aggregator. The output is a target-site treatment-specific survival curve θ̂_fed(t,a) for a ∈ {0,1} over the time grid, an influence-function-based asymptotic variance estimate, and time- and treatment-specific federated weights η̂_{t,a} that indicate each source's contribution.

## Method

The core mechanism derives a site-specific semiparametric efficient influence function (EIF) under a 'site-k CCOD' assumption, decomposed into a target-data anchoring term and a source-data augmented term weighted by a density ratio ω^{k,0}(X) = P(X|R=0)/P(X|R=k). Each site computes a local doubly robust estimator using cross-fitted nuisance functions (conditional survival S^k, censoring G^k, propensity π^k) estimated via ensembles from survSuperLearner and SuperLearner, with the source survival model S^k replaced by the shared target S⁰ to enforce the CCOD link. Aggregation solves an ℓ₁-penalized convex optimization over non-negative weights summing to one, where the quadratic term aligns source EIFs with the target distribution and the ℓ₁ penalty (scaled by site-specific discrepancy measures) drives weights of biased sites to exactly zero, achieving asymptotic oracle selection. The resulting federated estimator is doubly robust (consistent if either the survival model or the censoring/propensity/density-ratio models are correct), attains the semiparametric efficiency bound under site-k CCOD, and provably improves efficiency over target-only analysis whenever at least one source provides a consistent estimate.

## Results

In simulations across five distribution-shift scenarios (Homogeneous, Covariate, Outcome, Censoring, and All Shifts), the federated estimator (FED) achieves up to 59% reduction in RMSE relative to the target-only (TGT) estimator while maintaining negligible bias and 95% coverage probability. Under Outcome Shift and All Shifts, META-IVW becomes substantially biased, whereas FED remains consistent. In the AMP HIV-1 prevention trial (4,611 participants, 3.77% event rate), FED produces narrower confidence intervals than TGT and recovers valid intervals at early time points where TGT fails due to insufficient effective sample size. In the flchain mortality dataset (7,874 participants), FED achieves a 3–10% efficiency gain over TGT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FED dominates the target-only estimator (TGT, based on Westling et al. 2024) in efficiency across all simulation scenarios, achieving up to 59% RMSE reduction while preserving the same bias and coverage properties; TGT remains the only other method that maintains valid inference under all shift types. Against META-IVW (the density-ratio-corrected inverse-variance meta-analysis of Lee et al. 2022), FED is clearly superior under Outcome Shift and All Shifts, where META-IVW becomes substantially biased while FED stays consistent; under Homogeneous or Covariate-only Shift, META-IVW can be slightly more efficient but at the cost of fragility. POOL and IVW (the classical DerSimonian–Laird-style inverse-variance weighting) perform acceptably only under Censoring Shift (because censoring is a site-specific nuisance) but show substantial bias and coverage far below 95% under Covariate, Outcome, or All Shifts, and both fail to produce estimates at early time points in the AMP trial due to extreme weights. In the real-data AMP application, FED and TGT produce nearly identical point estimates, but FED's narrower confidence intervals and ability to recover intervals at early time points (where TGT's variance estimates are unstable) represent a practical advantage for rare-event survival analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FACE federated causal estimation framework (Han et al., 2025) from binary outcomes to right-censored survival data, and generalizes the single-site semiparametric efficient estimator of Westling et al. (2024) to a multi-source federated setting. It departs from prior federated causal inference work (Han et al., 2023; Xiong et al., 2023) that targets continuous or binary outcomes by deriving a site-specific EIF under a site-k CCOD assumption and aggregating via an ℓ₁-penalized convex optimization that adaptively excludes biased sources. Unlike classical meta-analysis approaches (DerSimonian & Laird, 1986) that implicitly require conditional homogeneity across sites, the method corrects for distributional shifts in covariates, outcomes, and censoring while preserving privacy through summary-level communication only.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically critical gap: privacy regulations (GDPR, HIPAA) prevent pooling of time-stamped event histories, yet rare-event survival outcomes (e.g., 3.77% HIV incidence in the AMP trials) demand borrowing across sites for valid inference. The demonstrated ability to recover valid confidence intervals at early time points where target-only analysis fails due to insufficient effective sample size has direct practical impact for multi-site clinical trials. Open questions include developing covariate-adaptive weighting schemes, handling positivity violations, extending to time-varying covariates, and investigating whether more aggressive borrowing (trading regularity for efficiency) is possible when CCOD fails but data sharing is permitted.
