## Problem

Adaptive optimizers (Adam, Shampoo) and normalized steepest descent methods (Lion, Muon) both exploit non-Euclidean geometry, but it was unclear whether they do so through the same mechanism. The paper identifies that they rely on fundamentally different smoothness notions—adaptive smoothness versus standard smoothness—and asks whether the stronger adaptive smoothness assumption yields concrete optimization benefits, a question that had no formal answer in either the nonconvex or accelerated convex settings.

## Input / Output

The analysis takes a loss function (convex or nonconvex, deterministic or stochastic), its gradient sequence, a well-structured preconditioner set H (e.g., diagonal PSD matrices for Adam, full PSD for full-matrix AdaGrad, or S_{+}^{d_L}⊗I_{d_R} for one-sided Shampoo), and an initialization x_0. The output is a convergence rate bound expressed in terms of the adaptive smoothness Λ_H(f) for adaptive optimizers, or the standard smoothness L_{‖·‖_H}(f) combined with adaptive gradient variance σ_H for NSD, yielding rates such as Õ(√(Δ_0·Λ_H(f)/T)) in the nonconvex case and Õ(Λ_H(f)D²/T²) in the accelerated convex case.

## Method

The paper develops a unified nonconvex convergence analysis for adaptive optimizers with any well-structured preconditioner set H, extending prior convex-only results. The key technical ingredient is a novel matrix inequality (Lemma 3.3) that bounds the sum of second-order terms ∑‖V_t^{-1}g_t‖_H² by Tr(H)·‖S_T‖_op, where S_T involves the noncommutative difference V_t² − βV_{t-1}²; this inequality introduces a log d factor for general H but recovers tight bounds in the diagonal (commutative) case. The paper then demonstrates the benefit of adaptive smoothness by showing that Nesterov-accelerated adaptive optimizers achieve an O(T^{-2}) rate under Λ_H(f), while Guzmán & Nemirovski's lower bound proves no optimizer can beat Ω(T^{-1}) under standard ℓ_∞ smoothness. Finally, the paper introduces adaptive gradient variance σ_H as the noise analogue of adaptive smoothness and proves that NSD with momentum achieves a dimension-free rate under σ_H, while a matching lower bound shows the dimension dependence is unavoidable under standard gradient variance.

## Results

In the nonconvex setting, adaptive optimizers with any well-structured preconditioner set achieve a convergence rate of $\tilde{O}(\log d \cdot \sqrt{\Delta_0 \Lambda_{\mathcal{H}}(f) / T})$ (Theorem 3.2), governed by the adaptive smoothness $\Lambda_{\mathcal{H}}(f)$. In the convex setting, Nesterov-accelerated adaptive optimizers attain an $\tilde{O}(\Lambda_{\mathcal{H}}(f) D^2 / T^2)$ rate (Theorem 4.3), in sharp contrast to the $\Omega(L_{\|\cdot\|_\infty}(f) / (T \log T))$ lower bound under standard $\ell_\infty$ smoothness (Guzmán & Nemirovski, 2015). For NSD with momentum under adaptive gradient variance, the paper establishes a dimension-free rate of $O((\Delta_0 L_{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}}(f))^{1/4} \sqrt{\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}} / T^{1/4})$ (Theorem 4.5), while a matching lower bound of $\Omega(d^{1/2})$ dependence on dimension is shown under standard gradient variance (Theorem 4.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical baselines; the 'comparisons' are between different smoothness and noise assumptions governing convergence. The central comparison is between adaptive smoothness $\Lambda_{\mathcal{H}}(f)$ (governing adaptive optimizers like Adam, AdaGrad, Shampoo) and standard smoothness $L_{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}}(f)$ (governing NSD methods like SignGD, Muon, Lion). The paper proves that adaptive smoothness is always no smaller than standard smoothness (Proposition 2.5: $L_{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}}(f) \leq \Lambda_{\mathcal{H}}(f) \leq d \cdot L_{\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}}(f)$), yet the stronger assumption yields a strictly better rate: $O(T^{-2})$ acceleration is achievable under adaptive smoothness but provably impossible under standard $\ell_\infty$ smoothness. Analogously, adaptive gradient variance $\sigma_{\mathcal{H}}$ is always no smaller than standard $\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H},*}$-variance (Proposition B.11), yet it enables dimension-free convergence for NSD, while standard variance incurs an unavoidable $\Omega(d^{1/2})$ factor in the worst case (Theorem 4.7). The paper also positions its results relative to concurrent work by Kovalev & Borodich (2025), noting that its Theorem 4.5 uses the weaker standard smoothness rather than an adaptive smoothness metric, yielding a strictly better rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the unified analysis of adaptive optimizers from Xie et al. (2025b, ICML) and Gupta et al. (2017) from the convex to the nonconvex setting, and from diagonal preconditioners to general well-structured preconditioner sets (e.g., one-sided Shampoo). It builds on the Bernstein & Newhouse (2024) observation that Adam reduces to SignGD and Shampoo reduces to Muon when EMA is off, but provides the first systematic characterization of how the two algorithm families exploit non-Euclidean geometry through fundamentally different smoothness notions. The acceleration result draws on Kovalev (2025a) for the Nesterov framework, while the lower bound comparison leverages Guzmán & Nemirovski (2015) for standard ℓ∞ smoothness.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a key open question about whether the stronger adaptive smoothness assumption (always ≥ standard smoothness) provides any concrete optimization benefit, answering affirmatively with a clean O(T⁻²) vs. Ω(T⁻¹) separation for acceleration and a dimension-free vs. dimension-dependent separation for stochastic rates. This clarifies the theoretical landscape for practitioners choosing between adaptive optimizers (Adam, Shampoo) and NSD methods (Lion, Muon): they are not merely different implementations of the same geometry but rely on genuinely different structural assumptions. Open questions include whether the log d factor for general (non-diagonal) preconditioners is tight, whether the adaptive variance assumption is empirically verifiable, and whether the acceleration result extends to nonconvex settings.
