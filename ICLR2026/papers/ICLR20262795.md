## Problem

The standard account of superposition in mechanistic interpretability treats interference between features as purely harmful noise to be minimized geometrically and filtered by ReLUs, yielding regular polytope arrangements. However, real language models exhibit semantic clusters and cyclical structures (e.g., months of the year forming circles) that this picture cannot explain. The gap arises because prior toy models assumed sparse, uncorrelated features, whereas realistic features are correlated, and this correlation can make interference constructive rather than merely destructive.

## Input / Output

The method takes binary bag-of-words vectors (V=10,000 dimensions) constructed by OR-ing c=20 consecutive records from a text corpus (WikiText-103 or OpenWebText), producing ~1.6M training samples. The tied-weight ReLU autoencoder outputs a reconstructed bag-of-words vector, but the primary object of analysis is the encoder weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×V}, whose columns are feature directions in the m-dimensional latent space. Per-feature R² scores, UMAP/PCA projections of W columns, and off-diagonal Frobenius norms of W^T W submatrices are used to characterize the learned feature geometry.

## Method

The paper introduces BOWS (Bag-of-Words Superposition), a controlled setting where a tied-weight ReLU autoencoder is trained to reconstruct binary bag-of-words vectors, providing realistic feature correlations with known ground-truth features. The core theoretical contribution is formalizing 'linear superposition'—a regime where low-rank data covariance (rank(Σ) ≤ m) makes interference constructive, so that a linear decoder can recover features and the optimal weight geometry is a rank-m PCA projector with ||W||_F² = m, versus ||W||_F² ≈ d for the standard interference-filtering solution. This norm advantage explains why weight decay and tight bottlenecks bias even non-linear models toward exploiting correlation structure. Empirically, the paper shows that ReLU AEs trained on WikiText-BOWS develop semantic clusters (visible via UMAP) and circular PCA structures for correlated feature groups (months, Roman numerals), and that constructive interference and ReLU-based filtering coexist for the same feature (e.g., 'Beatles' or 'Christmas' benefit from correlated context while the ReLU suppresses false positives). A secondary contribution distinguishes value-coding features (linearly decodable real-valued variables) from presence-coding features, explaining why structures like modular-addition circles arise without data correlations.

## Results

The paper's headline quantitative findings are: (1) the 12 month features achieve R² = 0.98 ± 0.00015 with a purely linear decoder, confirming they are in linear superposition (Definition 2); (2) for 81% of validation samples containing 'Beatles', contextual reconstruction outperforms one-hot reconstruction, demonstrating constructive interference; (3) in the value-coding ablation (Table 1), ablating only the value-coding subspace drops MAP accuracy from 97.94% to 22.43% and modular-addition accuracy from 100% to 3.11%, while zeroing the orthogonal complement (keeping VC) preserves 93.16% and 93.99% respectively; (4) in the m=800 BOWS model, 522 most-frequent words are in linear superposition while 4,073 words are in non-linear superposition, showing the two mechanisms coexist.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not benchmark against external methods; instead it compares internal mechanisms and settings. The ReLU AE matches the Linear AE (PCA projection) at very tight bottlenecks (m < 6 for d=12 synthetic data), confirming linear superposition, but switches to antipodal-pair (non-linear superposition) geometry as m increases to 6–10. Introducing weight decay (wd=4) at m=800 restores semantic clustering that is absent at wd=0, showing weight decay biases the model toward low-rank constructive-interference solutions. For the 'Beatles' and 'Christmas' features, contextual reconstruction beats one-hot reconstruction in 81% of samples, directly demonstrating that interference is beneficial rather than purely harmful. The value-coding ablation (Table 1) shows that the VC subspace is the computational unit: removing it collapses performance (22.43% / 3.11% accuracy) while removing the orthogonal complement is relatively benign (93.16% / 93.99%), confirming that apparent geometric structures in modular-addition and map tasks arise from value-coding rather than superposition.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the superposition framework of Elhage et al. (2022), which treated interference as purely harmful noise to be geometrically minimized and ReLU-filtered, by showing that correlated features can make interference constructive. It connects to the broader mechanistic interpretability literature on sparse autoencoders (Templeton et al., 2024; Gao et al., 2025) and the observed but unexplained feature geometries in real LMs—circular structures for months (Engels et al., 2025) and anisotropic semantic clustering (Bricken et al., 2023). The key conceptual departure is introducing 'linear superposition' as a regime where low-rank data covariance allows a non-linear AE to exploit shared variance rather than suppress it, and the BOWS framework as a controlled bridge between idealized toy models and realistic text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This provides a mechanistic explanation for feature geometries (semantic clusters, cyclical structures) observed in real language models that the standard interference-minimization picture could not account for, and clarifies when weight decay and tight bottlenecks bias models toward correlation-exploiting solutions. The distinction between value-coding and presence-coding features offers a principled way to separate correlation-driven superposition geometry from task-driven functional structure (e.g., modular addition circles). Limitations include the intentionally simplified BOWS setting, the absence of a complete mathematical characterization of when constructive vs. filtering mechanisms dominate, and the lack of direct analysis on frontier-scale LMs—making it a strong conceptual framework whose full validation in realistic interpretability pipelines remains open.
