## Problem

Training deep spiking neural networks has critically depended on explicit normalization schemes (e.g., batch normalization) to stabilize learning, yet these schemes collect batch-level input statistics with no known biological analogue, undermining the biological plausibility that motivates SNNs. Biologically grounded alternatives such as STDP remain limited to shallow networks due to training instability. The paper asks whether canonical cortical E-I lateral inhibition can replace explicit normalization to enable stable, biologically faithful training of deep SNNs.

## Input / Output

The framework accepts standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/100 and TinyImageNet) or asynchronous event streams (48×48 for CIFAR10-DVS and DVS-Gesture) and outputs a class label. Inputs are encoded as spike trains over a fixed number of time steps (4 for static images, 10–16 for event data), and the final prediction is obtained by averaging the classifier's output logits across all time steps before computing cross-entropy loss. All convolutional and fully-connected layers are replaced by the E-I circuit, with a fixed 4:1 excitatory-to-inhibitory neuron ratio.

## Method

Each SNN layer is replaced by a canonical E-I circuit: excitatory neurons follow a leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) model, while inhibitory neurons are modeled as stateless fast-spiking units (effectively ReLU) due to their much smaller membrane time constant. The circuit decomposes lateral inhibition into a subtractive component (balancing excitation and inhibition) and a divisive component (providing gain control), with the integrated input computed as g_E ⊙ (I_EE − I_EI,sub) / I_EI,div + b_E. Two techniques enable stable end-to-end training: E-I Init, a dynamic initialization that draws excitatory weights from an exponential distribution, sets W_EI deterministically to 1/n_I, and calibrates the divisive gain g_I so that expected divisive current matches the standard deviation of excitatory input (mimicking normalization at initialization); and E-I Prop, which combines adaptive stabilization (replacing zero divisive currents with the per-sample minimum positive value), a straight-through estimator to bypass the non-differentiable replacement in backpropagation, and a 1/d gradient scaling on W_EI to counteract the disproportionate gradient amplification through the divisive pathway.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, DeepEISNN achieves 90.80±0.16% (VGG-16) and 90.93±0.33% (VGG-19) top-1 accuracy with only 4 time steps, surpassing all normalization-free baselines. On the event-based CIFAR10-DVS dataset, it reaches 77.66±0.48% (VGG-11, 10 steps), outperforming all BN-equipped methods including TAB (76.70%) and TEBN (75.10%). On DVS-Gesture, the method attains 94.86±0.86% (VGG-8, 16 steps), exceeding the prior best normalization-free result (FDI, 86.70%) by over 8 points. On TinyImageNet, ResNet-18 achieves 50.29% top-1 accuracy, demonstrating scalability to larger-scale datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On CIFAR-10, DeepEISNN (90.80–90.93%) clearly outperforms all E-I constrained normalization-free baselines including DANN (88.54%), EICIL (89.43%), and FDI (65.60%), and is competitive with the strongest non-E-I normalization-free methods IM-Loss (90.90%) and BackEISNN (90.93%). On CIFAR-100, it surpasses SRI (54.94%) and EICIL (63.47%) by ~10–11 points but trails BN-equipped methods like TAB (75.89%) and TEBN (74.37%) by roughly 10 points, reflecting the known accuracy gap between normalization-free and BN-equipped training on static image datasets. On event-based CIFAR10-DVS, DeepEISNN (77.66%) outperforms all BN-equipped baselines including TAB (76.70%), TEBN (75.10%), and tdBN (67.80%), as well as normalization-free IM-Loss (72.60%). On DVS-Gesture, it achieves 94.86% versus FDI's 86.70% (a 8.16-point margin) while using only 16 time steps compared to FDI's 500. Against DANN (Cornford et al., 2021), the closest E-I ANN baseline, DeepEISNN shows statistically significant improvements (p<0.01 for VGG-8, p<0.001 for deeper models), with the gap widening from 0.87% to 2.65% as depth increases from VGG-8 to VGG-19.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Cornford et al. (2021), who demonstrated that ANNs with separate excitatory and inhibitory units (enforcing Dale's principle) can be trained via exponential weight initialization, by transplanting that E-I architecture into the temporal domain of spiking neural networks. It departs from the dominant paradigm in deep SNN training—explicit normalization schemes (BN, NeuNorm, BNTT, TEBN, TAB)—by replacing them with a biologically grounded lateral inhibition mechanism that performs both subtractive (E-I balance) and divisive (gain control) regulation. The framework also builds on the broader SNN training literature (LIF dynamics, fast-spiking approximation, STE) but addresses the specific gap that prior E-I SNN work (EICIL, BackEISNN) remained shallow or relied on normalization, leaving the full picture of deep E-I dynamics unexplored.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first stable, end-to-end training recipe for deep SNNs that are simultaneously normalization-free and biologically structured, closing a long-standing gap between high-performance deep learning and cortical circuit realism. The emergent bimodal representation (distinct from BN's Gaussian outputs) suggests the E-I circuit implements a dynamic population-separation strategy rather than a static normalization, opening a computational platform for studying large-scale cortical E-I dynamics in silico. Key limitations include a ~2.5× training-time overhead, a persistent accuracy gap versus BN-equipped SNNs (e.g., 92.05% vs. 95.37% on CIFAR-10/ResNet-18), and the continued reliance on backpropagation rather than local plasticity rules. Open questions include whether the framework scales to very large or recurrent architectures, whether the learned bimodal structure has measurable functional benefits for downstream tasks, and how to deploy the divisive operation efficiently on digital neuromorphic hardware.
