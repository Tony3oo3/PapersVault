## Problem

Existing diffusion-based handwritten text generation methods rely on separate auxiliary style and content encoders trained with handcrafted objectives (contrastive, triplet, or high-frequency mask losses), resulting in complex multi-encoder training pipelines where style, content, and noise are modeled as isolated factors with limited cross-interaction. The paper asks whether a single unified diffusion model can simultaneously process all three factors within a shared latent space, eliminating redundant encoders while improving both stylistic diversity and structural fidelity.

## Input / Output

The model consumes three visual inputs: a style reference image (one or more text lines from a target writer), a content image (the target text rendered in a standard font with predicted character/word bounding boxes), and a binary mask specifying which spatial regions to generate or edit. All three are spatially concatenated and encoded through the same VAE, operating on 1024×1024 patches. The output is a stylized handwritten text-line image matching the reference writer's style while conveying the specified content; the framework supports synthesizing an arbitrary number of text lines simultaneously and performing character- or word-level edits within existing handwritten images. The model is bilingual, handling both Chinese (character-level) and English (word-level) scripts within a single set of weights.

## Method

InkSpire is built on the FLUX.1-Fill-dev latent rectified flow transformer, fine-tuned via LoRA (~115.9M parameters, rank 32). The key architectural insight is that by spatially concatenating the handwritten image X with the content image X_c and applying a random binary mask before VAE encoding, style, content, and noise tokens all occupy the same latent feature space without any separate encoders or auxiliary losses. Training uses a multi-line masked infilling strategy: fixed-size patches are randomly cropped from raw multi-line page images, a random binary mask partitions each patch into observed context and masked target regions, and the model is optimized with a masked conditional flow-matching objective (L2 velocity prediction) applied only to masked latent tokens. A revised positional encoding scheme—Aligned Positional Encoding (APE), with a rotated variant (R-APE) for wide text lines—assigns content tokens the same spatial positions as their handwritten counterparts, enabling the model to distinguish style from content conditions and support arbitrary-length multi-line synthesis. A separate transformer-based layout model (using masked conditional flow matching) predicts per-character bounding boxes to render the content image, completing the two-stage pipeline of layout generation followed by image generation.

## Results

On the IAM English dataset, InkSpire achieves FID 7.92, KID 4.83×10⁻³, HWD 0.62, and ΔCER 0.01, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline DiffPen (FID 12.89, KID 9.73×10⁻³, HWD 2.13, ΔCER 0.03). On the ICDAR2013 Chinese dataset, InkSpire reaches FID 10.98, KID 11.45×10⁻³, HWD 0.41, CR 92.92%, and AR 91.56%, surpassing TGC-Diff (FID 23.43, CR 89.99%, AR 89.13%) and One-DM (FID 34.36, CR 73.19%, AR 72.33%). The model also achieves the highest style scores (78.58% English, 86.28% Chinese) in a classifier-based evaluation, confirming superior stylistic fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InkSpire outperforms all five baselines on every reported metric across both datasets. On IAM (English), the closest competitor is DiffPen (Nikolaidou et al., 2024), which uses triplet and classification losses for style control; InkSpire reduces FID from 12.89 to 7.92 (−38%), KID from 9.73 to 4.83 (−50%), and HWD from 2.13 to 0.62 (−71%), while also achieving the lowest ΔCER (0.01 vs. 0.03). VATr (Vanherle et al., 2024) and HWT (Bhunia et al., 2021) are transformer-based methods that trail significantly on all style metrics (FID 34.00 and 44.72 respectively). On ICDAR2013 (Chinese), TGC-Diff (Wang et al., 2025) is the strongest baseline with a high-frequency mask loss for structural preservation, yet InkSpire still improves FID by 53% (23.43→10.98), HWD by 35% (0.63→0.41), and CR by ~3 percentage points (89.99→92.92). One-DM (Dai et al., 2024), which relies on a Laplacian contrastive loss, is the weakest baseline on both datasets, with FID values of 43.89 (IAM) and 34.36 (ICDAR2013), confirming that the unified encoder-free design of InkSpire yields substantially better generation quality than handcrafted auxiliary encoder approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

InkSpire extends the diffusion-based handwritten text generation line (WordStylist → One-DM → DiffPen → TGC-Diff) by eliminating the separate style and content encoders that prior methods relied on, instead unifying style, content, and noise in a single VAE-encoded latent space via spatial concatenation. It builds directly on the FLUX.1-Fill-dev rectified-flow transformer backbone and the content-image factorization introduced by TGC-Diff (Wang et al., 2025), but departs from the encoder-centric design by adopting a multi-line masked infilling strategy inspired by in-context generation in large text-to-image diffusion models. The revised positional encoding (APE/R-APE) and bilingual training further distinguish it from prior single-line, monolingual approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is a streamlined training pipeline that removes handcrafted auxiliary losses and separate encoder training, while simultaneously enabling multi-line arbitrary-length synthesis, character-level editing, and bilingual (Chinese-English) generation in a single model—capabilities that prior methods handled separately or not at all. The encoder ablation (Tables 10–11) is notable: training content/style encoders from scratch actually degrades FID/KID and produces fainter strokes, suggesting the pretrained VAE's latent alignment with the DiT backbone is more valuable than task-specific encoder design. Open questions include whether the unified-representation approach generalizes beyond two scripts, how it scales to higher resolutions or longer documents, and whether the failure mode of generating erasure artifacts (Section A.5.6) can be mitigated without additional losses.
