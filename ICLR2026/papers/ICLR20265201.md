## Problem

Existing textured 3D morphing methods either operate on geometry alone (neglecting textures) or naively extend 2D interpolation strategies into 3D, causing semantic ambiguity, structural misalignment, and texture blurring. The core gap is the absence of a principled mechanism to establish reliable 3D correspondences between source and target while leveraging generative priors for both structural and textural coherence throughout the transition.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265201/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single source image and a single target image as prompts, which are encoded by DINOv2 into patch-level condition embeddings (M tokens each). It outputs a sequence of L textured 3D assets represented as 3D Gaussians, generated via the TRELLIS two-stage diffusion pipeline (structure generation at N=64 grid resolution, then SLAT latent construction with C=8 dimensions, 25 denoising steps per stage). The entire process is training-free and runs on a single GPU, with interpolation coefficients sampled from a Beta(5,5) distribution to emphasize intermediate states.

## Method

Interp3D is a training-free, progressive three-stage alignment framework built on the TRELLIS 3D diffusion prior. Stage 1 (Semantic-Aligned Condition Interpolation) computes an optimal one-to-one patch correspondence between DINOv2 embeddings via cosine-similarity assignment, then performs token-wise convex interpolation only on matched pairs to prevent category-level mismatches. Stage 2 (SLAT-Guided Structure Interpolation) projects source and target SLAT features into the structure-generation KV grid, estimates dynamic patch correspondences whose granularity adapts across denoising timesteps (coarse-to-fine), permutes the target KV maps accordingly, and applies a fused outer-interpolated attention that concatenates aligned source/target keys with the interpolated query. Stage 3 (Fine-Grained Texture Fusion) performs bidirectional nearest-neighbor retrieval in the texture KV space for each intermediate token, aggregates source, target, and self features with l2 normalization to prevent collapse into a linear blend. The key insight is that semantic, structural, and textural correspondences must be enforced progressively at different levels of the generative pipeline rather than relying on a single interpolation strategy.

## Results

On the proposed Interp3DData benchmark (57 pairs across easy/medium/hard difficulty), Interp3D achieves the best average FID of 78.97, PPL of 2.47, and LPIPS of 0.086, outperforming all baselines across every difficulty level. The advantage is most pronounced on hard cases, where Interp3D reaches FID 82.54 vs. 94.64 (AID-I) and 135.69 (FreeMorph). In a 30-participant user study, Interp3D is preferred 54.12% of the time overall (vs. 17.39% for MorphFlow, the next-best), with the highest plausibility score at 58.04%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Interp3D outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric (FID, PPL, LPIPS) across all three difficulty levels. Against the closest competitor AID-O (FID 81.03, PPL 2.92, LPIPS 0.102), Interp3D improves FID by ~2 points, PPL by ~0.45, and LPIPS by ~0.016 on average, with the gap widening on hard cases (FID 82.54 vs. 81.81 is narrow, but PPL 2.62 vs. 3.24 and LPIPS 0.119 vs. 0.145 show clear gains). MorphFlow, while achieving the lowest PPL among baselines (2.89), does so at the cost of severely degraded texture quality (FID 104.88, LPIPS 0.151), which the authors attribute to oversimplified outputs rather than genuine smoothness. The 2D-morphing-then-3D baselines (DiffMorpher, FreeMorph) perform worst overall, with PPL values of 4.42 and 5.61 respectively, due to view-inconsistency errors when feeding 2D morphed images into the 3D pipeline. In the user study, Interp3D is preferred at 54.12% overall versus 17.39% for MorphFlow and 16.08% for AID-O, confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on TRELLIS (Xiang et al., 2025) as its 3D generative backbone and adapts the attention-interpolation mechanism from AID (He et al., 2024) into the 3D structured-latent setting. It departs from prior 3D morphing work (geometric deformation methods, MorphFlow's optimal-transport approach) by operating entirely within a generative diffusion pipeline, and from 2D-to-3D morphing extensions (SRIF, Yang et al. 2025) by introducing a progressive three-stage correspondence principle—semantic, structural, then textural—that explicitly enforces alignment at each level rather than relying on implicit feature mixing. The key conceptual departure is treating correspondence estimation as a first-class, multi-granularity problem (DINOv2 patch matching → dynamic SLAT patch correspondence → nearest-neighbor texture token retrieval) rather than a single global interpolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Textured 3D morphing is a practical bottleneck in animation, gaming, and VFX pipelines where artists need smooth intermediate assets between keyframes; this paper provides a training-free, plug-and-play solution that generalizes across 3D generation backbones (TRELLIS, LN3Diff, 3DTopia-XL). The introduced Interp3DData benchmark (57 graded pairs) fills a gap in systematic evaluation for this task. However, the method's reliance on reliable correspondence means it degrades sharply when source and target are semantically distant (e.g., a sketch to a 3D object), and the 57-pair dataset is too small to draw strong statistical conclusions. A key open question the authors flag is how to establish plausible correspondences when no meaningful semantic overlap exists between endpoints.
