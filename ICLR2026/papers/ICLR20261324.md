## Problem

Existing SVG datasets and methods are fragmented across isolated tasks (understanding, editing, or generation), limited in scale and domain diversity, and lack cross-task transferability. No prior work provides a unified framework that jointly models SVG understanding, editing, and generation across static graphics and dynamic animations at sufficient scale for robust training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261324/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes SVG code (normalized to a 128×128 viewBox and simplified to remove redundant metadata), free-text instructions, reference raster images, or sampled video frames (8 frames for animation tasks) as input, and produces SVG code, SVG animation code (with animate/animateMotion/animateTransform elements), natural-language descriptions, or multiple-choice QA answers. A key design detail is that all numerical coordinates are represented via dedicated integer tokens (−128 to 128) and decimal tokens (.0 to .99), keeping generated sequences compact while preserving geometric precision.

## Method

InternSVG builds on an InternViT-300M vision encoder and Qwen2.5-7B language model (InternVL3-8B backbone), extending the vocabulary with 55 tag tokens, 42 attribute tokens, 247 integer tokens, and 100 decimal tokens to compress SVG sequences by roughly an order of magnitude. New token embeddings are initialized by averaging the subword embeddings of their constituent characters from the pretrained tokenizer, which anchors them in the existing semantic space and accelerates convergence. Training follows a two-stage curriculum: stage one trains only on shorter, simpler Icon and Chemistry SVGs to establish basic representation and generation abilities, then stage two expands to all domains (illustrations, animations) with resampling to balance dataset scales. The unified multi-task objective jointly optimizes understanding (description, QA), editing (10 subtasks from low-level geometric ops to style transfer), and generation (Text-to-SVG, Image-to-SVG, Text-to-SANI, Video-to-SANI), inducing positive cross-task transfer demonstrated by ablations showing consistent gains over single-task training.

## Results

On SArena-Icon, InternSVG 8B achieves 85.1% Overall understanding accuracy (vs. 77.1% for Claude-Sonnet-4), 77.33 PSNR on editing (vs. 57.60), 8.72 FID on Text-to-SVG (vs. 15.84), and 0.811 SSIM on Image-to-SVG (vs. 0.665). On SArena-Chemistry, InternSVG achieves a FID of 9.97 for Text-to-SVG, more than 2× better than the next-best model (Claude-Sonnet-4 at 21.25). On SArena-Animation, InternSVG attains the lowest FVD of 99.47, substantially outperforming all open-source MLLMs and approaching proprietary systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

InternSVG 8B outperforms the strongest proprietary baseline, Claude-Sonnet-4, across nearly all SArena-Icon metrics: +8.0 points on understanding accuracy, +19.7 dB on editing PSNR, 45% lower FID on Text-to-SVG (8.72 vs. 15.84), and +0.146 SSIM on Image-to-SVG (0.811 vs. 0.665). The only metrics where InternSVG trails Claude-Sonnet-4 are CLIP-T2I on Text-to-SVG (23.92 vs. 25.42) and the hard style-transfer editing subtask (DINO 0.952 vs. 0.976). Against open-source MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, InternVL3-78B, Llama-4-Maverick), InternSVG leads by 15–22 points on understanding and 20–35 dB on editing PSNR. Among LLM-based SVG generators, InternSVG surpasses OmniSVG 3B by 19.6 FID on Text-to-SVG and 0.055 DINO on Image-to-SVG, and outperforms LLM4SVG 7B by 13.2 FID and 0.201 DINO. On SArena-Chemistry, InternSVG achieves a FID of 9.97 versus 21.25 for Claude-Sonnet-4 and 26.84 for Llama-4-Maverick, reflecting its advantage on domain-specific scientific graphics where general MLLMs have limited training exposure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the InternVL3-8B multimodal backbone and extends the LLM-based SVG generation paradigm (StarVector, OmniSVG, LLM4SVG) by unifying understanding, editing, and generation into a single model. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) the SAgoge dataset (16M samples) is an order of magnitude larger than the next-largest SVG resource (SVG-Stack at 2.2M) and covers domains (chemical structures, animations) absent from all prior datasets; (2) the SVG-specific tokenization with subword-based embedding initialization is a novel architectural contribution that compresses sequence length while preserving geometric structure, rather than treating SVG as plain text. The two-stage curriculum training (simple icons → complex illustrations/animations) addresses the scale imbalance that prior unified datasets like UniSVG (525K samples) could not support.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SAgoge dataset and SArena benchmark fill a critical gap: prior SVG resources were fragmented across single tasks and too small for effective training, making systematic comparison of unified approaches impossible. The demonstration that an 8B model with domain-specific tokens can outperform much larger proprietary models (Claude-Sonnet-4, GPT-4o) on SVG tasks suggests that task-specific tokenization and curriculum training are more effective than raw scale for structured-output generation. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond 2D SVG to other vector formats (PDF, CAD), whether the style transfer gap versus Claude-Sonnet-4 can be closed, and whether the 128×128 normalization limits expressiveness for production-quality graphics.
