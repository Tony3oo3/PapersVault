## Problem

Current LLM-based social agents either produce responses without explicit reasoning or apply uniform Long-CoT reasoning across all interaction turns, leading to excessive token consumption and inflexible social behavior in dynamic settings like negotiation. Cognitive science suggests humans selectively allocate deliberation depth based on context, but no prior method equips language agents with this adaptive reasoning capability for social intelligence tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260114/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each turn, the agent receives the full dialogue history concatenated with its private profile and social goals as a single prompt. The output is a structured sequence: a mode-selection token (one of four levels from intuitive response to deep deliberation), a mode-specific reasoning chain composed of predefined actions (e.g., Intent, Strategy, Deduction, Integration), and a final natural-language utterance. The four modes impose progressively more reasoning actions—M1 has none, M2 adds Intent/Style/Response, M3 adds History/Goal/Assess/Strategy, and M4 adds multi-strategy Deduction and Integration—so the token budget scales with the selected depth.

## Method

The ASL framework defines four hierarchical reasoning modes inspired by Hierarchical Cognitive Control Theory, then trains via two phases: behavioral cloning on expert-generated mode-consistent data for cold-start, followed by Adaptive Mode Policy Optimization (AMPO) for RL-based refinement. AMPO's key innovation is a dual-level advantage estimator: a mode-level advantage that compares average rewards (and, when rewards are tied, average token lengths) across all four modes to incentivize context-appropriate mode selection, and a sample-level advantage that refines generation quality within the chosen mode. The reward function combines three components—goal-progress answer reward, binary format-compliance penalty, and a smooth length-penalty multiplier—to jointly shape mode choice, structural adherence, and conciseness. The full objective is a PPO-style clipped surrogate with KL regularization, optimized in a single-turn paradigm where each dialogue state is treated as an independent decision point, with long-horizon consistency ensured through diverse state sampling and marginal-contribution reward design.

## Results

On SOTOPIA-Hard (Self-Play), ASL with AMPO on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct achieves a GOAL score of 8.06 and OVERALL of 3.68, surpassing GPT-4o (GOAL 6.97, OVERALL 3.46) by 15.6% on GOAL. On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, AMPO reaches GOAL 7.85 / OVERALL 3.54 on SOTOPIA-Hard, outperforming the strongest social-intelligence baseline DSI (GOAL 7.31, OVERALL 3.51). Compared to GRPO under identical settings, AMPO improves SOTOPIA-Hard OVERALL by 7.0% (3.44→3.68) while reducing average token usage by 32.8% (865→581 tokens for Llama).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASL+AMPO outperforms all proprietary LLMs (GPT-4o, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, DeepSeek-V3) and all Large Reasoning Models (OpenAI-o1, o3-mini, Gemini-2.5-Pro, DeepSeek-R1, QwQ-32B) on both SOTOPIA and SOTOPIA-Hard, with the largest gap on SOTOPIA-Hard where LRMs score 1.5–2.7 points lower on GOAL. Among social-intelligence baselines, DSI is the strongest competitor (GOAL 7.31 on SOTOPIA-Hard with Qwen), but AMPO still leads by 0.54 GOAL and 0.03 OVERALL; EPO and PPDPP trail further behind. Against the GRPO variant under identical ASL settings, AMPO consistently wins on both GOAL and OVERALL across all four evaluation configurations (e.g., SOTOPIA-Hard Self-Play: 7.85 vs 7.44 GOAL for Qwen; 8.06 vs 7.59 for Llama) while using 25–33% fewer tokens. On out-of-distribution benchmarks (DEMO, NegotiationArena), AMPO maintains consistent improvements over GRPO and vanilla models, confirming transferability. In the efficient-reasoning comparison (Table 7), AMPO achieves the best GOAL scores while using 647 tokens (Qwen) / 581 tokens (Llama), striking a better quality–efficiency trade-off than O1-Pruner (280 tokens but much lower GOAL), TALE-PT, LS-Mix, and DeGRPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) by replacing its single-level advantage estimation with a dual-level (mode-level + sample-level) formulation, and builds on PPO's clipped surrogate objective as the optimization backbone. Conceptually, it departs from two existing lines: (1) social intelligence methods (PPDPP, EPO, DAT, DSI) that rely on fast-reasoning without explicit deliberation, and (2) Large Reasoning Models (o1, DeepSeek-R1) that apply uniform Long-CoT regardless of task complexity. The hierarchical reasoning mode design draws from Hierarchical Cognitive Control Theory (Koechlin & Summerfield, 2007; Badre, 2008), repurposing a cognitive science framework to structure LLM reasoning in social contexts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first effective demonstration that adaptive Long-CoT reasoning—dynamically selecting reasoning depth per context—outperforms both no-reasoning and uniform deep-reasoning approaches in social intelligence, with the added benefit of 32.8% token savings over GRPO. The mode-level advantage mechanism in AMPO is a generalizable technique that could extend to other domains requiring context-dependent reasoning depth. Limitations include reliance on single-turn optimization (addressed but not fully resolved for long-horizon consistency), computational costs of RL training, and dependence on LLM-based reward models that may not perfectly capture human judgment. Open questions include whether the four-mode hierarchy is optimal or whether more granular or learned mode taxonomies would further improve adaptability.
