## Problem

Small language models (SLMs) can match LLMs on simple CUDA code generation but degrade sharply on tasks requiring multi-step reasoning over combined optimization techniques. Existing remedies—supervised fine-tuning and retrieval-augmented generation—enrich contextual knowledge but do not directly extend the SLM's reasoning depth, leaving a persistent gap on complex benchmarks. The paper asks whether LLM-level multi-step CUDA optimization reasoning can be transferred to SLMs without retraining or incurring LLM-scale inference costs.

## Input / Output

At inference time the SLM receives the original sequential C++ kernel plus a single CUDA optimization example (original snippet, optimized snippet, and named optimization method) retrieved from the current edge of the ReGraph; it must decide whether the suggested method is applicable and emit the next optimized CUDA kernel. The framework iterates this step-by-step generation along a path through the graph, compiling and benchmarking each intermediate kernel, and returns the highest-reward CUDA program found within a fixed search budget (default 200 iterations). The ReGraph itself is a one-time offline artifact built from ~500 LLM-generated optimization trajectories over a corpus of real-world CUDA files.

## Method

ReGraphT is a training-free, two-phase framework. In the offline phase, an LLM is prompted to perform step-by-step CUDA optimization on sequential code; the resulting trajectories are relabelled for method-name consistency and merged into a directed, cyclic graph (ReGraph) whose nodes are named optimization techniques and whose edges carry concrete code examples. In the online phase, CUDA generation is cast as state-transition search on ReGraph and solved with Monte Carlo Graph Search (MCGS), an adaptation of MCTS to cyclic graphs: selection uses a P-UCB criterion, expansion is restricted to the fixed successor set of the current node, rollout employs a visit-count-regularized policy plus a step cap to guarantee termination on cycles, and a hierarchical reward (−1 for compilation failure, speedup−1 for functional failure, speedup for success) is backpropagated along the selection path. The SLM is invoked only to generate the next kernel given the edge's optimization example, so all multi-step planning is delegated to the graph search rather than the model's internal reasoning.

## Results

On CUDAEval, ReGraphT-MCGS with DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Lite-Instruct achieves 75.1% pass@1 and 14.46× speedup@1, surpassing the best RAG baseline (MCTS-RAG) by +3.5% pass@1 and +6.37× speedup. On ParEval, it reaches 55.0% pass@1 and 10.78× speedup@1. Across all three code-specific SLMs (DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Lite-Instruct, Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct, HPC-Coder-V2), ReGraphT-MCGS averages 73.3% pass@k on CUDAEval, outperforming Standard, CoT, and RAG by +11.0%, +9.0%, and +6.8% respectively in pass@1. The method narrows the gap to the 671B DeepSeek-R1 (82.1% pass@1) to within ~7 points while running on a single A100-80GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReGraphT-MCGS consistently outperforms all baselines across both benchmarks and all three SLMs. On CUDAEval with DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Lite-Instruct, it beats Standard prompting by +13.4% pass@1 (75.1 vs 61.7) and +7.92× speedup (14.46 vs 6.54), CoT by +10.2% pass@1 and +7.23× speedup, and the strongest RAG baseline (MCTS-RAG) by +3.5% pass@1 and +6.37× speedup. The HPC-specific fine-tuned model HPC-Coder-V2, despite domain-specific training, is outperformed by ReGraphT-MCGS by +8.3% pass@1 and +7.91× speedup on CUDAEval, confirming that structured reasoning transfer surpasses fine-tuning for multi-step optimization. On ParEval, ReGraphT-MCGS achieves 55.0% pass@1 vs 51.7% for MCTS-RAG and 50.0% for RethinkMCTS, with a 10.78× speedup nearly double the best baseline (5.78×). Compared to the 671B DeepSeek-R1 upper bound (82.1% pass@1, 19.14× speedup), the 7B SLM + ReGraphT-MCGS closes the pass@1 gap to ~7 points and the speedup gap to ~4.7×, demonstrating that the reasoning graph effectively transfers LLM-level optimization expertise to lightweight models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of LLM-based code generation, HPC-specific fine-tuning (HPC-Coder, RLPF), and retrieval-augmented generation for code (EVOR, Repoformer, MCTS-RAG). It departs from the fine-tuning paradigm by arguing that SFT and knowledge distillation degrade on multi-step reasoning tasks, and from standard RAG by showing that injecting retrieved examples does not improve the model's reasoning capacity. The key conceptual departure is formulating CUDA optimization as a state-transition problem on a directed graph (ReGraph) built from LLM-generated trajectories, then navigating it with a Monte Carlo Graph Search (MCGS) that adapts MCTS to handle cycles — a formulation not present in prior MCTS-for-code work (RethinkMCTS, MCTS-RAG) which operates on tree-structured thought decompositions rather than a fixed optimization-method graph.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, graph-based transfer of LLM reasoning to SLMs offers a practical path for privacy-sensitive or compute-constrained settings where deploying 671B-parameter models is infeasible, and the CUDAEval benchmark with reasoning-complexity tiers fills a gap left by ParEval's limited 60 CUDA instances. However, the approach is tightly coupled to CUDA's relatively bounded optimization-method space (ReGraph converges at ~500 samples), raising open questions about whether the same graph-based transfer generalizes to domains with unbounded or highly compositional reasoning (e.g., multi-file system design, novel algorithm synthesis). The paper also acknowledges that CUDAEval's aggressive filtering may underrepresent edge-case CUDA patterns, and the MCGS overhead (~6 hours for 80 samples on A100) remains non-trivial for interactive use.
