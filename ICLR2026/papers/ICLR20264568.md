## Problem

Semi-parametric contextual pricing with β-Hölder smooth demand has only been analyzed at isolated smoothness levels: Õ(T^{2/3}) for β=1 and Õ(T^{3/5}) for β=2, with the sole unified treatment (Fan et al., 2024) yielding a suboptimal exponent that fails to recover the β=2 rate and degenerates to linear regret at β=1. This paper closes the gap by establishing the general rate Õ(T^{(β+1)/(2β+1)}) for all β≥1, matching the minimax non-contextual bound and interpolating to the parametric √T rate as β→∞.

## Input / Output

At each round the seller observes a d-dimensional context vector c_t drawn i.i.d. from an unknown distribution, posts a price p_t ∈ [0, p_max], and receives a single binary purchase indicator y_t = 1{c_t^T θ* + ξ_t ≥ p_t}. The demand is semi-parametric: the purchase probability is g(c_t^T θ* − p) where θ* is an unknown finite-dimensional parameter and g is an unknown β-Hölder smooth link function on a compact interval. The algorithm outputs an adaptive pricing policy π and achieves a regret bound of Õ(T^{(β+1)/(2β+1)}) under strong uni-modality of the revenue function, with the context diversity assumption (Assumption 4) required only during the initial exploration phase of length Õ(T^{(β+1)/(2β+1)}).

## Method

The LPSP algorithm operates in epochs of doubling length and combines three key components: (1) a piloted local polynomial regression that bins the value-price gap into M = ⌈n^{1/(2β+1)}⌉ intervals and fits degree-⌊β⌋ polynomials per bin, (2) a constrained least-squares refinement that re-estimates the parametric index θ within a ball of radius η around the pilot estimate, reducing the pilot-error contribution from O(η) to O(η^{2β/(β+1)}) via a self-normalized quadratic-form concentration argument (Hanson-Wright), and (3) a localized exploration schedule that rounds prices to equi-partition points within each bin at sub-linear frequency (√n_j per bin rather than n_j), ensuring the local design matrix satisfies a weaker Ω(√n_j) eigenvalue lower bound instead of the Ω(n_j) bound required by Wang & Chen (2025). An epoch-wise policy improvement oracle from Wang & Chen (2025) handles the non-stationary distribution shift between epochs, and the overall regret analysis exploits the fact that the sub-linear exploration cost scales as Õ(T^{(β+1)/(2β+1)}) rather than the Õ(T^{(2β−1)/(2β+1)}) that would result from linear exploration.

## Results

The paper establishes a unified regret bound of Õ(T^{(β+1)/(2β+1)}) for all β≥1 in semi-parametric contextual pricing under strong uni-modality. For β=1 this recovers the optimal Õ(T^{2/3}) rate, for β=2 it recovers Õ(T^{3/5}), and as β→∞ it interpolates to the parametric Õ(√T) rate. The bound strictly improves upon Fan et al. (2024)'s Õ(T^{(2β+1)/(4β-1)}) for all β≥1 (which degenerates to linear regret at β=1) while requiring fewer assumptions (no context density lower bound or strictly decreasing CDF condition). The exploration cost is reduced from linear in T (Wang & Chen, 2025) to Õ(T^{(β+1)/(2β+1)}) via a sub-linear localized exploration schedule.

| Wang & Chen (2025) | β=2 | 3/5 | (A) Strong uni-modality, (C) Σ>0, (D) g′(·)<0 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Tullii et al. (2024), the paper matches the optimal Õ(T^{2/3}) regret at β=1 while requiring no additional assumptions beyond boundedness, confirming their result as a special case. Against Fan et al. (2024), the paper achieves a strictly smaller regret exponent for all β≥1 (e.g., 3/5 vs. 5/7 at β=2, and 2/3 vs. 1 at β=1 where Fan et al. degenerates to linear regret), while also dropping the context density lower bound (B) and the strictly decreasing CDF condition (D). Against Wang & Chen (2025), the paper matches the Õ(T^{3/5}) rate at β=2 but extends it to all β≥1, removes the g′(·)<0 assumption, and reduces the per-epoch exploration cost from Θ(n_{τ,j}) to Θ(√n_{τ,j}), which is critical for β>2 where Wang & Chen's linear exploration schedule yields a suboptimal T^{(2β−1)/(2β+1)} rate. Against the non-contextual bound of Wang et al. (2021), the paper shows that under strong uni-modality, contextual pricing is provably no harder than its non-contextual counterpart, achieving the same T^{(β+1)/(2β+1)} exponent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends the semi-parametric contextual pricing literature by generalizing the β=1 result of Tullii et al. (2024) and the β=2 result of Wang & Chen (2025) to all β≥1, achieving the rate Õ(T^{(β+1)/(2β+1)}) that matches the non-contextual minimax bound. It builds directly on Wang & Chen's constrained least-squares refinement and stationary policy-improvement subroutine, and on Fan et al.'s pilot estimation under a diverse-context assumption, but departs from both by removing the strictly-increasing-CDF condition and by replacing Wang & Chen's linear-time local exploration with a sub-linear (√n) schedule. The authors also identify and fix a dependence gap in Wang & Chen's finite-sample analysis of the constrained LSE, where the non-martingale structure of the joint estimation procedure was overlooked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result establishes that under strong uni-modality, semi-parametric contextual pricing is provably no harder than its non-contextual counterpart, closing a theoretical gap and providing a clean interpolation from non-parametric to parametric rates as β→∞. Practically, the sub-linear exploration schedule reduces the forced-exploration overhead, which is significant for deployment. Key open questions include removing the strong uni-modality assumption (Assumption 3) entirely and achieving adaptivity to the unknown smoothness parameter β, both of which the authors identify as natural next steps. The heavy d^4 dependence in the leading-order regret term is acknowledged as an artifact of the analysis and a target for future refinement.
