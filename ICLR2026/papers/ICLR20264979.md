## Problem

ECG foundation models have proliferated across diverse architectures (CNNs, Transformers, state-space models) and pretraining strategies, yet prior evaluations compare them against weak baselines or on narrow single-task datasets, leaving fundamental questions unanswered: which architectures generalize best across clinical domains, how do FMs scale with limited labels, and what explains performance differences between model families. No large-scale, multi-task benchmark with strong supervised baselines existed to distinguish genuine advances from favorable task selection.

## Input / Output

All models consume standard 12-lead ECG signals without additional resampling or filtering; where architecture supports it, 2.5-second segments are used instead of full 10-second recordings, with predictions averaged across four non-overlapping segments at inference. Outputs span 26 targets across seven categories: multi-label diagnostic classification (71 ECG statements on PTB-XL, 918 non-cardiac ICD-10 codes, 158 cardiac codes, 11 echocardiogram findings), and regression targets (age, sex, biometrics, ECG features, 18 lab values, 6 vital signs). Classification is scored by macro-AUROC and regression by MAE on z-normalized targets, with statistical significance determined via 1,000-iteration bootstrap on test sets.

## Method

The paper benchmarks eight ECG FMs (ECGFounder, ECG-JEPA, ST-MEM, MERL, ECGFM-KED, HuBERT-ECG, ECG-FM, and the newly introduced ECG-CPC) plus two supervised baselines (S4 and Net1D) under three evaluation modes—full finetuning, frozen feature extraction with a learnable query-attention head, and linear probing—across 12 public datasets. ECG-CPC is a compact 3.8M-parameter structured state-space model (four convolutional encoder layers followed by four S4 layers) pretrained on 10.7M HEEDB recordings using contrastive predictive coding, trained on a single NVIDIA L40 GPU. Finetuning employs layer-dependent learning rates (backbone layers at 1/100 and 1/10 of the head rate), and scaling analysis fits performance curves of the form CN^{-α}+L₀ to quantify label efficiency ratios. Representation analysis via centered kernel alignment (CKA) on intra-model layer activations reveals that models with comparable downstream performance learn markedly different internal feature structures, with ECG-CPC showing the clearest progression from redundant early CNN layers to specialized S4 layers.

## Results

Across 26 tasks on 12 datasets, ECG-CPC (a 3.8M-parameter SSM pretrained with CPC) achieves the best median rank in 5 of 7 task categories under finetuning, including cardiac structure (AUROC 0.831 on EchoNext), cardiac outcomes (0.781), non-cardiac outcomes (0.719), acute care (0.764 deterioration), and patient characteristics (0.933 sex). In adult ECG interpretation, ECGFounder (0.934), ECG-JEPA (0.940), and ECG-CPC (0.949) all match or exceed the S4 supervised baseline (0.941) on PTB-XL. Label efficiency analysis on EchoNext shows ECG-JEPA requires only 11–42% of the training data to match S4's performance (ratio 0.11–0.42), ECG-CPC requires 21–40%, and ECGFounder requires 30–62%, corresponding to 3.3–9× improvements over the supervised baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ECG-CPC (3.8M SSM) outperforms the S4 supervised baseline (2.2M SSM) in 5 of 7 categories under finetuning, with the largest margins in cardiac structure (0.831 vs 0.819 AUROC on EchoNext) and patient characteristics (0.933 vs 0.919 on sex), while S4 retains an edge in cardiac outcomes (0.780 vs 0.781, statistically tied) and acute care (0.756 vs 0.764, tied). Against the much larger ECGFounder (33.8M CNN), ECG-CPC wins decisively on cardiac structure, outcomes, and patient characteristics but trails on adult ECG interpretation (0.949 vs 0.934 on PTB-XL, where ECGFounder's supervised pretraining on HEEDB provides an advantage). ECG-JEPA (87.2M Transformer) dominates pediatric ECG (0.911 vs 0.892 for ECG-CPC) and matches ECG-CPC on adult ECG, but ECG-CPC surpasses it on all non-ECG-interpretation categories. MERL, ECGFM-KED, and HuBERT-ECG consistently fail to outperform the S4 supervised baseline across most categories, with ECGFM-KED ranking last (median rank 10) in adult ECG interpretation. In the label-efficiency scaling study, ECG-JEPA is the most data-efficient in the very low-sample regime (<1000 samples, ratio 0.11–0.42), while ECG-CPC reaches a higher performance ceiling at larger sample sizes, making each optimal in different data-budget regimes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SSM-based ECG analysis line initiated by Mehari & Strodthoff (2023) by introducing ECG-CPC, a lightweight hybrid CNN+S4 architecture pretrained with contrastive predictive coding (van den Oord et al., 2018) on the large-scale HEEDB dataset. Conceptually, it sits at the intersection of the foundation model paradigm (Bommasani et al., 2022) and structured state-space sequence modeling (Gu et al., 2022), but departs from prior ECG FM work by systematically benchmarking eight architecturally diverse FMs (CNNs, Transformers, SSMs) under controlled conditions across 26 clinical tasks rather than comparing against weak baselines on narrow datasets. The paper also introduces a new evaluation protocol combining fine-tuning, frozen-feature, and linear-probing modes with bootstrap-based statistical ranking, going beyond the single-metric comparisons typical of prior ECG FM papers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first comprehensive, multi-task, multi-dataset benchmark for ECG foundation models, providing the field with a standardized reference for model selection and a clear demonstration that architectural inductive biases (SSMs) can outweigh raw parameter scale. The finding that a 3.8M-parameter model trained on a single GPU for three weeks matches or exceeds 87–97M-parameter Transformers across most task categories challenges the prevailing 'bigger is better' assumption and opens the door to resource-constrained ECG deployment. Key open questions remain: out-of-distribution generalization is untested, the interaction between pretraining dataset, architecture, and objective cannot be fully disentangled without unified retraining, and the theoretical explanation for why SSMs align so well with ECG signal structure is left as future work.
