## Problem

In VLM-based Multi-Agent Systems, visual hallucinations seeded in one agent are progressively amplified by downstream agents that over-rely on textual messages to relay visual information, producing a 'hallucination snowballing' effect. Existing hallucination mitigation methods target single-agent intrinsic errors but cannot address this cross-agent propagation mechanism, leaving a fundamental reliability gap in multi-turn visual collaboration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261483/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates within a multi-agent pipeline where each agent receives an RGB image (or video frames), a text instruction, and the textual output of the preceding agent. Beyond the standard textual response, ViF produces a compact set of visual relay tokens (a small subset n ≪ m of the full vision token set) that are inserted between the original vision tokens and instruction tokens of the next agent. For models using Flash-Attention 2/3 where attention scores are inaccessible, a Key-Norm (L₂ norm of the key matrix) based selection strategy with 3×3 spatial buffer tokens serves as a drop-in alternative.

## Method

ViF introduces a visual flow alongside the existing textual flow to relay visual evidence across agents. It selects vision tokens exhibiting unimodal attention peaks in middle layers as visual relay tokens, contextualizes them with instruction tokens via a lightweight transformer block, and transmits them to subsequent agents to resist vision-to-text information loss. An attention reallocation mechanism then amplifies this pattern: in middle layers, temperature-scaled Softmax sharpens unimodal attention peaks while attention from inactive vision tokens and instruction tokens is redistributed to active vision tokens; in deep layers, reallocation flows from vision to instruction tokens. The method is model-agnostic, requires only one additional trainable transformer module, and is trained with a standard two-stage pipeline (pre-training with frozen vision encoder and LLM, followed by instruction tuning). Across four MAS structures and ten base VLMs (7B–34B), ViF reduces the hallucination snowballing score by 30–40% while incurring only 8–13% additional inference latency.

## Results

ViF consistently improves average performance by 2.4–3.8% across six base VLMs (7B) and four MAS structures on eight benchmarks, with the largest gains (~3%) on the circular structure where hallucination snowballing is most severe. On larger models (LLaVA-NeXT-34B, Qwen2.5-VL-32B), improvements exceed 4% across all benchmarks. The proposed Hallucination Snowballing (HS) score is reduced by 30–40% on average across five hallucination benchmarks, with the circular structure showing the largest reduction (~40%). On the MMHal-Bench and HallBench benchmarks specifically, ViF achieves over 4% average improvement over baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViF outperforms all five single-agent hallucination mitigation methods (MemVR, VISTA, FarSight, DeCo, TAME) on both original benchmark metrics and the proposed HS score when applied in the multi-agent setting. On average, ViF achieves +3.8% on original metrics and −39.8% on HS score, while the best competitor (FarSight) manages only −0.5% on original metrics and −5.4% on HS. Notably, MemVR actually degrades performance (−2.6% original, +18.4% HS), and VISTA also worsens the HS score (+3.1%), suggesting that single-agent decoding/attention modifications can amplify hallucination propagation in MAS by further biasing attention toward textual flow. FarSight and DeCo show modest original-metric gains but still leave HS scores 3–4× higher than ViF, confirming that reducing per-agent intrinsic hallucination alone cannot prevent multi-agent snowballing. The paper attributes this gap to the fact that all five baselines retain textual flow as the sole inter-agent relay channel, whereas ViF introduces a dedicated visual flow via unimodal vision tokens that preserves visual evidence across turns.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant line of single-agent VLM hallucination mitigation (MemVR, VISTA, FarSight, DeCo, TAME) by identifying that those methods address only intrinsic hallucination and are insufficient—or even counterproductive—when hallucinations propagate across agents via textual flow. It also distinguishes itself from prior hallucination-snowballing analyses (Zhang et al., 2024; Zhong et al., 2024), which studied snowballing in single-model or text-only settings, by formalizing the multi-agent visual variant where two interacting mechanisms (intrinsic error + textual relay compression) compound across turns. The method draws on attention-analysis techniques from VLM interpretability work (Darcet et al., 2024; Gong et al., 2024) and multi-agent collaboration architectures (Qian et al., 2025), but introduces a novel 'visual flow' relay channel and attention reallocation mechanism that have no direct precedent in either lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As multi-agent VLM pipelines become the standard approach for complex multimodal reasoning, the hallucination snowballing problem is a fundamental reliability bottleneck that single-agent fixes cannot resolve; this paper provides both a diagnostic framework (HS score, attention-allocation analysis) and a lightweight, model-agnostic mitigation that practitioners can plug into existing MAS pipelines with ~10% overhead. Key limitations include evaluation restricted to 7B–34B models from two families (LLaVA, Qwen-VL), leaving open whether the unimodal-token pattern generalizes to architectures with fundamentally different vision encoders (e.g., InternVL, Ovis) or to very small (3B) and very large (72B) models. Open questions include optimal token selection beyond the unimodal criterion, interaction with training-based hallucination reduction, and whether visual flow can be extended to video or multi-image agent settings without the current per-turn overhead.
