## Problem

The dominant hierarchical-causal framework in neuroscience and machine learning defines neural representations by their causal origin, yet this view cannot explain misrepresentation (illusions, dreams, imagery) and fails to account for the empirical finding that fine-grained input details remain recoverable from heavily abstracted, higher-level features. The paper asks: how does a system that abstracts away details simultaneously preserve the fine-grained information needed for downstream functions, and can this be formalized in a way that unifies causal and non-causal (informational/teleological) accounts of representation?

## Input / Output

The method takes intermediate-layer feature vectors from pretrained DNNs (all 16 conv layers of VGG19, quarter-depth transformer blocks of CLIP/DINOv2/BERT/GPT2/OPT, or VAE latent codes) and perturbs them with Gaussian noise calibrated to 10 specific correlation distances (0.1–0.99). It then produces reconstructed inputs (images via DIP-based feature inversion, or token sequences via softmax-relaxed logit optimization) and a per-instance scalar called representation size, defined as the maximum feature-space deviation from the canonical feature within the set of features still yielding a faithful reconstruction (threshold: 0.1 correlation distance for vision, 0.3 token error rate for language).

## Method

The paper introduces readout representation, a formal framework defining a representation not by the input that causally generated a feature, but as the set H_s^π of all features from which a signal s can be recovered via a readout procedure π. To instantiate π experimentally, the authors use feature inversion (gradient-based optimization minimizing a feature-matching loss), with Deep Image Prior as a weak structural prior for vision and token-logit optimization with row-wise softmax relaxation for language. Feature perturbation is controlled analytically: Gaussian noise variance σ² is derived from the target correlation distance c via σ² = Var(h)(1/(1−c)² − 1), enabling systematic probing of the readout representation boundary. The key quantitative contribution is representation size r_x = max{d_H(h, f(x)) | h ∈ H_{x,t}^π}, a per-instance metric capturing the volume of feature space that encodes a given input. A toy model with 100 bell-shaped tuning curves mapping a 1D stimulus to a 100D feature space demonstrates mechanistically how high-dimensional embeddings with low intrinsic input dimensionality naturally produce broad, redundant readout representations, explaining why inputs remain recoverable from features displaced far from their canonical values.

## Results

In VGG19 lower-to-middle convolutional layers, near-perfect input recovery (within 0.1 pixel correlation distance) is maintained even when features are perturbed up to 0.7 correlation distance, with recovery degrading only in the highest layers. BERT and OPT-350m show near-perfect token recovery up to 0.7 feature correlation distance in lower layers, while GPT2 and larger OPT variants show reduced but still above-chance accuracy. Correctly classified ('hit') images exhibit significantly larger representation sizes than misclassified ('miss') images in higher layers, and noise images yield zero representation size across all layers. An ablation in pixel space (no neural network) yields a representation size of only 0.32±0.047, far below VGG19 conv1_1 (0.81±0.024), confirming that the extended readout representations are an inherent property of learned features rather than an artifact of the DIP prior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not propose a new method to benchmark against competing approaches; instead, it introduces a theoretical framework (readout representation) and validates it empirically across multiple architectures. The key cross-model comparison shows that extended readout representations are a general phenomenon: VGG19 (CNN), DINOv2 and CLIP (ViTs), and BERT/OPT (transformers) all exhibit broad recoverable feature regions in lower-to-middle layers, while GPT2 and larger OPT models show somewhat reduced recovery, suggesting architecture and scale matter. The DIP ablation (pixel-space optimization without the generator) confirms that the large representation sizes are not an artifact of the prior: without DIP, reconstructions remain perceptually similar under substantial perturbation, though high-frequency details degrade faster. The trained-vs-untrained VGG19 comparison reveals that architectural biases (not training alone) underlie the hit/miss and natural/noise representation size differences, while training further expands representation sizes in middle-to-higher layers. The pixel-space ablation (representation size 0.32±0.047) versus VGG19 conv1_1 (0.81±0.024) provides the sharpest quantitative contrast, demonstrating that the neural network's learned feature space is what enables the extended readout representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes long-standing philosophical distinctions between causal, informational, and teleological theories of representation (Fodor, Dretske, Millikan) into a computable framework, using feature inversion (Mahendran & Vedaldi, 2015) as the readout procedure. It inverts the metamer perspective of Feather et al. (2023), which studied multiple inputs converging to a single representation, by instead characterizing the set of multiple features that represent a single input. The representation size metric extends redundancy analyses from the dataset level (mutual information, PID, information bottleneck) to the instance level, and the Deep Image Prior (Ulyanov et al., 2020) serves as a minimal structural prior to avoid confounds from strong generative models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled way to reconcile the apparent tension between hierarchical abstraction and fine-grained information retention, with the representation size metric providing a per-instance diagnostic that could aid in outlier detection, confidence estimation, and understanding model failures. However, the practical utility of representation size remains preliminary, the perturbation-based probing is not directly transferable to biological neural recordings, and the source of architectural differences in representation size (e.g., why GPT2 underperforms BERT) is unresolved. A key open question is whether the large readout representations observed in deterministic networks also characterize the noisy, stochastic activity patterns in the brain.
