## Problem

Existing 4-bit block-wise quantization methods for LLMs (NF4 and AF4) do not minimize the actual quantization error of the network weights: NF4's claimed information-theoretic optimality is incorrect, and AF4 optimizes the error of normalized weights rather than the end-to-end error of unnormalized weights. No prior work had derived a codebook that is truly optimal for block-wise absmax quantization under a standard distortion criterion (MAE or MSE) on the original weight scale.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weight tensors grouped into blocks of size I (typically 64) and a block-size hyperparameter, and produces 4-bit quantized weight indices, per-block quantization constants (signed or absolute block maxima stored in BF16), and a pre-computed 16-level non-uniform codebook. The codebook is computed once offline via an EM algorithm and adds no runtime cost. With the OPQ variant, a small fraction of outlier weights are additionally stored in BF16 alongside 64-bit positional indices, increasing the effective bits-per-weight only marginally (e.g., from 4.127 to ~4.14 with double quantization).

## Method

The paper derives a modified Lloyd's algorithm whose centroid update rule accounts for the fact that the quantization error is measured on unnormalized weights w_{b,i} = w_b^{max} · x_{b,i}, not on the normalized weights x_{b,i} used by the codebook. For MSE, the optimal reconstruction level is a weighted mean of normalized weights in each Voronoi region, weighted by the squared block maxima; for MAE, it is a weighted median weighted by the block maxima. A second contribution replaces absolute absmax normalization with signed absmax normalization (BOF4-S), which always maps the block's largest-magnitude weight to +1 rather than ±1, freeing one reconstruction level (only 0 and 1 are fixed instead of −1, 0, 1) and reducing quantization error. A third contribution, outlier-preserving quantization (OPQ), identifies weights exceeding a quantile threshold of absolute block maxima (after unit-variance normalization) and stores them in BF16, preventing outliers from inflating the block maximum and distorting the normalized weight distribution away from the Gaussian assumption used in codebook design.

## Results

On Llama-3.1 8B (block size I=64), the best variant BOF4-S(MSE)+OPQ achieves a WikiText-2 perplexity of 8.43, compared to 8.53 for NF4 and 8.51 for AF4, while also reducing MAE from 0.977×10⁻³ to 0.932×10⁻³ and MSE from 1.637×10⁻⁶ to 1.367×10⁻⁶. On Qwen-2.5 7B, BOF4-S(MSE)+OPQ reaches PPL 9.83 (vs. 9.89 NF4, 9.91 AF4), and on Mistral 7B it achieves PPL 8.87 (vs. 8.90 for both baselines). For fine-tuning with QLoRA on Llama-3.2 3B, BOF4-S(MSE)+OPQ attains the best code-generation accuracy (32.0% avg on HumanEval+/MBPP+), outperforming NF4 (29.3%) and AF4 (28.0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NF4 (Dettmers et al., 2023), BOF4-S(MSE)+OPQ consistently wins on all three evaluated LLMs: it reduces WikiText-2 perplexity by 0.10 on Llama-3.1 8B (8.43 vs. 8.53), 0.06 on Qwen-2.5 7B (9.83 vs. 9.89), and 0.03 on Mistral 7B (8.87 vs. 8.90), while also lowering both MAE and MSE quantization error. Against AF4 (Yoshida, 2023), the advantage is even more pronounced on Qwen-2.5 7B (PPL 9.83 vs. 9.91) and Mistral 7B (8.87 vs. 8.90), and comparable on Llama-3.1 8B (8.43 vs. 8.51). The paper further shows that MSE-optimized codebooks yield lower perplexity than MAE-optimized ones in nearly all settings, and that the signed-absmax normalization (BOF4-S) provides a consistent additional gain over the unsigned variant (BOF4). For fine-tuning, the picture is slightly more nuanced: BOF4(MSE) without signed normalization is best for instruction following (31.6% avg IFEval accuracy), while BOF4-S(MSE)+OPQ is best for code generation (32.0% avg), suggesting a small ablation among the four BOF4 variants is advisable per task.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the block-wise absmax quantization framework introduced by Dettmers et al. (2022) for optimizer states and adapted to LLM weights by QLoRA/NF4 (Dettmers et al., 2023) and AF4 (Yoshida, 2023). It departs from both NF4 and AF4 by showing they minimize the wrong objective (error of normalized weights rather than end-to-end error of unnormalized weights), and derives the correct centroid update rules via a modified Lloyd's algorithm. The signed absmax normalization (BOF4-S) and outlier-preserving quantization (OPQ) are novel modifications to the normalization and scaling steps that prior work treated as fixed design choices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

4-bit block-wise quantization is the enabling technique for memory-efficient LLM fine-tuning on consumer hardware, so reducing quantization error directly broadens who can adapt large models. The signed normalization is a one-line change with consistent perplexity gains, and OPQ offers a practical path to larger block sizes without accuracy loss. However, the absolute perplexity improvements are small (0.01–0.05 PPL), the Gaussian weight assumption limits generality, and the paper does not benchmark against calibration-data-based PTQ methods (GPTQ, AWQ) that typically achieve lower error. Open questions include combining BOF4 with Hadamard-rotation-based methods that enforce Gaussianity, and whether the MSE-over-MAE preference for perplexity holds at larger model scales.
