## Problem

Prior analyses of Shampoo and SOAP's Kronecker-structured second-moment estimators have relied on the Frobenius norm, which ignores the symmetric positive-definite constraint that preconditioners must satisfy. The paper shows that under a Kullback-Leibler divergence perspective, Shampoo's one-sided estimation (updating one Kronecker factor at a time) does not adequately solve the joint KL-minimization problem, revealing a previously unrecognized theoretical limitation that motivates principled redesigns.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a matrix-valued gradient G ∈ R^{d_a × d_b} (flattened to g = vec(G)) and the current weight matrix Θ, maintaining Kronecker-factored second-moment estimates S_a ∈ R^{d_a × d_a} and S_b ∈ R^{d_b × d_b} plus their eigenbases Q_a, Q_b. It produces an updated weight matrix Θ via preconditioned gradient descent. A key practical constraint is that eigenbases are updated infrequently via QR decomposition (every T iterations) while eigenvalues are tracked at every step via an EMA scheme, avoiding the cost of full eigendecomposition.

## Method

The paper recasts Shampoo's and SOAP's estimation rules as KL-divergence minimization for zero-mean Gaussian covariance estimation, revealing that the optimal two-sided solution requires simultaneously updating both Kronecker factors as S_a ← (1−β₂)S_a + (β₂/d_b)·G·S_b⁻¹·Gᵀ (and symmetrically for S_b), rather than Shampoo's one-sided rule. For practical implementation, the slow eigendecomposition is replaced with QR decomposition for the eigenbasis, and a new EMA scheme estimates eigenvalues λ_k using an outdated eigenbasis (justified as a stochastic proximal-gradient step under the KL perspective), yielding KL-Shampoo that achieves SOAP-level per-iteration runtime without requiring Adam's step-size grafting. KL-SOAP further augments this with RMSProp's diagonal second moment in the eigenbasis, interpreted as augmented eigenvalue estimation. The framework also unifies trace-scaled Shampoo as a von Neumann divergence variant (matrix Adafactor) and connects the EMA scheme to the Fisher-Rao Riemannian metric for matrix Gaussians.

## Results

Across language model pretraining experiments (random search, 150 runs per method, bfloat16), KL-Shampoo consistently achieves the lowest training loss, outperforming SOAP, Shampoo (with step-size grafting via Adam), and KL-SOAP while matching SOAP's per-iteration runtime. KL-Shampoo eliminates the need for Adam's step-size grafting, saving d_a × d_b parameters of memory per weight matrix (Table 1). On the RWKV7 model, Shampoo with p=1/2 and no grafting fails to train in all 150 runs, whereas KL-Shampoo trains robustly. The advantage of KL-Shampoo over KL-SOAP is notable: KL-Shampoo uses less memory (no augmented eigenvalue vector d) yet achieves better performance.

| Kronecker factors (S_a, S_b) | d_a² + d_b² | d_a² + d_b² | d_a² + d_b² | d_a² + d_b² |
| Eigenbasis (Q_a, Q_b) | d_a² + d_b² | d_a² + d_b² | d_a² + d_b² | d_a² + d_b² |

## Baselines & Comparisons

KL-Shampoo outperforms all four baselines on language model pretraining. Against Shampoo (Gupta et al., 2018), KL-Shampoo achieves lower training loss without requiring step-size grafting with Adam; Shampoo without grafting fails to train RWKV7 in all 150 runs, while KL-Shampoo trains robustly. Against SOAP (Vyas et al., 2025a), KL-Shampoo matches per-iteration runtime (both use QR decomposition) but achieves lower loss and uses less memory by avoiding the d_a·d_b augmented eigenvalue vector. Against the state-of-the-art Shampoo implementation with grafting (Shi et al., 2023), KL-Shampoo still outperforms even in step-wise progress. Against F-Shampoo (Morwani et al., 2025, two-sided Frobenius-norm-based Shampoo), KL-Shampoo consistently and significantly outperforms, demonstrating that the KL divergence is a more suitable objective than the Frobenius norm for structured preconditioner estimation. Surprisingly, KL-Shampoo also outperforms KL-SOAP, suggesting that the Kronecker-factored eigenvalue structure is more effective than the full-diagonal augmented eigenvalues used by SOAP.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper recasts Shampoo's and SOAP's Kronecker-structured second-moment estimation as covariance estimation under KL divergence minimization, departing from the Frobenius-norm analyses that have dominated prior work (Morwani et al., 2025; Eschenhagen et al., 2025; An et al., 2025). The KL perspective connects Shampoo to classical quasi-Newton theory, where KL divergence historically replaced the Frobenius norm as the design principle for BFGS/DFP updates (Fletcher, 1991), and to natural gradient descent on matrix Gaussians (Lin et al., 2019, 2024). The paper further unifies Shampoo with Adafactor by showing that trace-scaled Shampoo is a matrix generalization of Adafactor derived from the von Neumann divergence, while KL-Shampoo corresponds to the KL divergence—two distinct Bregman divergences over SPD matrices.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: KL-Shampoo eliminates the need for Adam step-size grafting, removing the associated memory overhead while matching or exceeding SOAP's performance at SOAP-level runtime. The KL framework provides a principled, extensible design principle for structured preconditioners that naturally generalizes to tensor-valued weights where SVD-based Frobenius-norm analyses break down. Open questions include whether the two-sided KL estimation advantage persists at much larger scales (beyond 450M parameters), whether the EMA eigenvalue correction scheme transfers to other structured optimizers beyond the Shampoo family, and whether the KL perspective can inform the design of higher-order or curvature-based methods.
