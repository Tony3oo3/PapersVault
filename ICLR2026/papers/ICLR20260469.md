## Problem

Existing post-hoc GNN explainers optimize by aligning predictions in the discrete graph label space, which inherently lacks the expressiveness to capture topological structure—especially problematic in domains like molecular property prediction where multiple distinct substructures map to the same label. This paper identifies that shifting optimization to the continuous graph representation space is promising but faces two critical obstacles: entanglement of explanatory and non-explanatory substructures within GNN-encoded representations, and distributional discrepancy between the full input graph and its structurally reduced explanation subgraph.

## Input / Output

IDEA requires gray-box access to a pre-trained GNN (specifically its node representation matrix H_N and pooled graph representation H_G), which limits applicability to fully black-box models. The explainer backbone is a probabilistic subgraph generator using the concrete distribution over edge existence, parameterized by an MLP on concatenated node representations. The output is a compact explanation subgraph g ⊂ G, evaluated as an edge-level binary classification against expert-annotated ground-truth substructures (e.g., NO₂/NH₂ groups, benzene rings, alkane+carbonyl fragments).

## Method

IDEA is a dual-stage optimization framework grounded in a prototypical graph representation space. In Stage 1 (Structural Information Disentanglement), a hierarchical graph tokenizer (HGTokenizer) with two cascade-connected graph quantizers decomposes node representations into shallow (non-explanatory) and deep (explanatory) quantized components, guided by a structure-aware disentanglement objective that pushes the shallow quantizer's prediction toward a uniform distribution while anchoring the deep quantizer's prediction to the original GNN output, while also recovering adjacency and node features. In Stage 2 (Explanatory Prototype Alignment), the input graph representation is purified by subtracting the shallow quantized component, and both the purified input and the explanation representation are implicitly projected into the prototypical space via their assignment distributions over the deep codebook C_D; the explainer is then optimized by minimizing a symmetric entropy-regularized Wasserstein distance between these two assignment distributions, which circumvents the distributional shift of the explanation subgraph without requiring an explicit projection network.

## Results

IDEA achieves an average ROC-AUC of 0.8856 across five datasets, outperforming the best baseline ProxyExplainer (0.8479) by 4.45% on average. On the precision metric, IDEA reaches 0.6022 average precision versus ProxyExplainer's 0.4050, a 48.71% relative improvement. The largest ROC-AUC gains appear on BA-2Motifs (+9.45%) and Mutagenicity (+5.17%), while the largest precision gain is on Alkane-Carbonyl (+39.99%). When integrated with different explainer backbones, IDEA yields up to 10.70% average ROC-AUC improvement (PGExplainer+IDEA).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IDEA outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset in terms of ROC-AUC. The strongest competitor is ProxyExplainer (0.8479 avg ROC-AUC), which IDEA surpasses by 4.45% on average; the gap is smallest on Alkane-Carbonyl (0.22%) and largest on BA-2Motifs (9.45%). PGExplainer (0.8000) and D4Explainer (0.7081) trail further behind, while GNNExplainer (0.6184), GraphMask (0.6035), and MixupExplainer (0.6393) are the weakest baselines. On precision, IDEA's advantage is even more pronounced: it beats ProxyExplainer by 48.71% on average (0.6022 vs. 0.4050), with the largest margin on Alkane-Carbonyl (0.4565 vs. 0.3261, +39.99%). The Direct-Align ablation (naive representation-space alignment without disentanglement) achieves 0.7116 average ROC-AUC, confirming that the full IDEA framework is necessary to realize the benefits of the representation-space paradigm shift.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant label-preserving framework (GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, ProxyExplainer, etc.) that optimizes GNN explainers by aligning predictions in the discrete label space, and instead proposes shifting optimization to a continuous prototypical representation space. The method borrows residual vector quantization (from audio codecs like SoundStream) to build a hierarchical graph tokenizer that disentangles explanatory from non-explanatory substructures, and draws on prototype-based GNN explanation (ProtGNN, PAGE) for the codebook-as-prototype interpretation. The alignment objective uses entropy-regularized Wasserstein distance over assignment distributions, connecting to optimal transport literature rather than the mutual-information or KL-divergence objectives standard in the GNN explanation community.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm shift from label space to representation space offers a principled way to exploit structural information that discrete labels inherently discard, which is especially relevant for complex molecular domains where multiple substructures map to the same label. The framework's generalizability across four explainer architectures (up to 10.70% ROC-AUC gain) suggests it could serve as a drop-in optimization layer for future explainers. However, IDEA requires gray-box access to GNN-encoded representations (ruling out black-box settings), assumes an approximately linear latent-to-prototypical mapping, and the joint-optimization variant fails catastrophically, indicating the dual-stage design is fragile. Extending the quantization-based framework to model-level interpretability and to black-box GNNs are natural open directions.
