## Problem

Existing self-supervised wireless localization models use generic masking strategies that ignore the inherent periodicity in Channel State Information, allowing the network to exploit local periodic shortcuts instead of learning globally meaningful propagation representations. Additionally, methods that incorporate map data perform only shallow fusion, failing to capture the rich 3D spatial-topological relationships between buildings, base stations, and multipath geometry. As a result, current approaches struggle with NLoS conditions and exhibit poor cross-scenario generalization without extensive labeled data.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a complex MIMO-OFDM channel frequency response tensor H ∈ C^(Nr×Nt×Nsc) (32 antennas × 128 subcarriers in the DeepMIMO setup), decomposed into a real-valued 4D tensor of shape (2, Nr, Nt, Nsc) preserving both amplitude and phase. It also takes a 3D building mesh (vertex set) and base station coordinates, which are encoded into a single geographic prompt token via a graph neural network. The output is a 3D user position estimate; for multi-BS scenarios (up to 4 base stations), per-station CLS tokens are fused through a learned attention mechanism before final coordinate regression.

## Method

SigMap follows a two-stage paradigm: (1) self-supervised pre-training with a cycle-adaptive masked autoencoder that computes row-wise cross-correlation on the CSI to detect dominant periodicity shifts, then generates diagonal mask bands whose slope matches the detected shift, forcing the decoder to reconstruct from globally meaningful features rather than interpolating periodic patterns; (2) parameter-efficient fine-tuning where a heterogeneous graph (building vertices + BS positions, edges from Delaunay triangulation) is processed by a 2-layer GCN, globally pooled, and projected into a single soft prompt token prepended to the frozen ViT encoder's input sequence. Only the GNN, projection MLP, and task-specific head (~0.085M parameters, 0.7% of the 11.73M total) are updated during fine-tuning, while the pre-trained transformer backbone remains frozen. For multi-BS localization, an attention-based fusion over per-station CLS tokens dynamically weights contributions based on signal quality and geometric configuration.

## Results

On the challenging single-base-station NLoS localization task (DeepMIMO O1_3p5), SigMap with geographic prompts achieves 1.564 m MAE, 5.675 m RMSE, and 60.5% CDF@1m, outperforming the best baseline LWLM by 34.4% in MAE and more than doubling its CDF@1m (25.3%). In the 4-BS collaborative setting, SigMap reaches 0.673 m MAE and 84.5% CDF@1m, improving over LWLM by 18.7% in MAE and 8.9 percentage points in CDF@1m. Generalization to unseen environments (DeepMIMO O2, WAIR-D Scenario-2) with only ~100 fine-tuning samples yields 1.026 m and 1.880 m MAE respectively, beating LWLM by 53.2% and 44.3%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SigMap with geographic prompts dominates all three baselines across every metric in both single-BS and multi-BS settings. Against LWLM (the strongest baseline, a foundation model for 6G positioning), SigMap reduces single-BS MAE from 2.382 m to 1.564 m (−34.4%) and nearly triples CDF@1m from 25.3% to 60.5%; in the 4-BS case the gap narrows to 18.7% MAE improvement (0.673 vs. 0.828 m) and 8.9 pp CDF@1m gain. SWiT (self-supervised invariant representations) is outperformed by 39.5% in single-BS MAE (1.564 vs. 2.586 m) and 39.7% in multi-BS MAE (0.673 vs. 1.102 m), with CDF@1m gaps of 36.2 and 16.4 pp respectively. The 3D CNN baseline [Wu et al., 2021] shows the weakest performance, trailing SigMap by 46.9% in single-BS MAE and 51.8% in multi-BS MAE, confirming that direct supervised mapping from CSI to position struggles in NLoS multipath environments. The ablation without map prompts (SigMap w/o map) still beats all baselines, indicating the self-supervised backbone itself is strong, but the geographic prompt contributes an additional 31.3% MAE reduction in single-BS and 14.7% in multi-BS.

## Relation to Prior Work

SigMap synthesizes the masked-autoencoder pre-training paradigm (from vision/NLP) with the foundation-model pre-train-then-fine-tune recipe recently applied to wireless channels (LWM, WirelessGPT, LWLM), but departs from these in two key ways: it replaces generic random or grid masking with a cycle-adaptive strategy that detects and disrupts CSI-specific periodicity, and it repurposes the soft-prompt mechanism from NLP to encode 3D geographic maps via a lightweight GNN rather than treating map data as a direct input feature. The geographic-prompt idea extends earlier map-assisted localization work (e.g., Chen et al. 2024) by making the map a conditioning signal through a frozen backbone rather than an additional network branch, and the cycle-adaptive masking addresses a gap the authors identify in prior SSL-for-CSI efforts (CrowdBERT, signal-guided MAE) that use single-objective, configuration-specific masking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The parameter-efficient fine-tuning (0.4% of parameters updated) combined with strong zero-shot transfer to entirely unseen ray-traced cities (WAIR-D, 100 OpenStreetMap scenes) offers a practical path toward deployable localization in 5G/6G networks where per-site retraining is infeasible. However, all evaluation is on ray-traced simulations (DeepMIMO, WAIR-D) with no real-world hardware validation, and the 2D-vs-3D map ablation reveals that the 3D geometric advantage is modest (~8% MAE), suggesting the method may be less sensitive to map fidelity than claimed. Open questions include whether the cycle-adaptive masking generalizes to non-OFDM waveforms or hardware with different periodicity structures, and whether the GNN-based prompt can scale to city-scale maps with millions of vertices.
