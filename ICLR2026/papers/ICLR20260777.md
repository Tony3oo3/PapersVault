## Problem

In Dec-POMDP multi-agent systems, a single agent's local observation is consistent with many dramatically different global states, creating a one-to-many mapping problem. Existing discriminative approaches (RNNs, Transformers) collapse this rich distribution into a single point estimate, suffering from mode collapse, while communication-based methods lack a principled generative model to exploit the auxiliary information they provide.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260777/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes as input either an individual agent's observation history over m past timesteps (when observations are information-rich) or the joint observations of all n agents at the current timestep (when communication is available), forming the auxiliary local observation vector x. It outputs a single inferred global state vector ŝ of the same dimensionality as the true environment state, which each agent then feeds into its policy π_θi(·|ŝ) for decentralized action selection. No global information is accessed during inference; the entire state is reconstructed from local data alone.

## Method

GlobeDiff introduces a latent variable z as a mode selector that resolves the one-to-many ambiguity: instead of the ill-posed problem of generating s from x, the model solves the well-posed conditional diffusion p_θ(s|x,z), where z disambiguates which plausible global state to reconstruct. The diffusion process uses a U-Net with 1D temporal convolutions (2 residual blocks, group norm, Mish activation) as the denoising network ε_θ, trained to reverse a K-step forward Gaussian noising process on the global state. A posterior q_ψ(z|x,s) is trained with ground-truth states, while a separate prior p_φ(z|x) learns to predict z from local observations alone at inference time; the training loss combines the standard diffusion denoising MSE with a KL divergence between posterior and prior. The paper proves bounded estimation error under both unimodal (Theorem 1, via Wasserstein-2 distance) and multi-modal (Theorem 2, via Voronoi partitioning with exponential mode-separation terms) settings, and integrates the framework into CTDE by using true states during centralized training and inferred states during decentralized execution.

## Results

On SMAC-v2 (PO) tasks, GlobeDiff achieves a win rate of 0.33 on zerg 5v5 (vs. 0.22 for vanilla MAPPO) and 0.47 on the super-hard 6h vs 8z map (vs. 0.12 for vanilla MAPPO). Even when matching the parameter budget (~14M), Vanilla MAPPO (Large) gains only marginally (e.g., 0.23 vs. 0.22 on zerg 5v5), while GlobeDiff maintains a substantial lead. The paper reports that GlobeDiff consistently and significantly outperforms all baselines across most SMAC-v1 (PO) and SMAC-v2 (PO) maps, with the largest gaps on super-hard scenarios.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GlobeDiff outperforms all three belief/communication baselines (LBS, Dynamic Belief, CommFormer) on most SMAC-v1 (PO) and SMAC-v2 (PO) maps. LBS suffers from error accumulation in long-horizon belief inference; Dynamic Belief remains unimodal and cannot capture multi-modal global state distributions; CommFormer's explicit communication and message aggregation become unreliable under severe partial observability. Against generative model baselines, GlobeDiff clearly surpasses both VAE and MLP on super-hard maps, while VAE and MLP show no significant improvement over vanilla MAPPO on most maps due to limited representational capacity. On SMAC-v2 (PO), MAPPO (Joint), which simply concatenates joint observations as policy input, sometimes performs worse than vanilla MAPPO, underscoring the need for an explicit generative state-inference module. The parameter-matched experiment (Vanilla MAPPO Large, ~14M params) confirms that simply scaling a recurrent policy cannot implicitly learn multi-modal state reconstruction, with GlobeDiff maintaining a 0.10–0.35 win-rate advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

GlobeDiff combines the conditional diffusion framework of Ho et al. (2020) with the variational latent-variable structure of Kingma & Welling (2014), introducing a latent variable z as a 'mode selector' to handle the one-to-many mapping from local observations to global states. It departs from prior belief-state estimation methods (LBS, Dynamic Belief) that collapse multi-modal state distributions into single point estimates, and from communication-based approaches (CommFormer) that require explicit message protocols. The paper also distinguishes itself from the concurrent work of Wang et al. (2024a), which applies diffusion to MARL partial observability via shared attractors but does not explicitly model the one-to-many conditional distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical error bounds (via Wasserstein distance) for diffusion-based global state inference in Dec-POMDPs, giving practitioners confidence in the method's reconstruction fidelity. The finding that a minimal U-Net (2 residual blocks, 5 diffusion steps) suffices for accurate state inference is practically encouraging, reducing the computational overhead of integrating generative models into MARL pipelines. However, all experiments are confined to SMAC/SMAC-v2 (StarCraft II) benchmarks with up to 11 agents; scalability to larger agent counts, continuous state spaces, and real-world robotic or autonomous driving scenarios remains an open question. The paper also notes that the original SMAC environment does not adequately stress-test partial observability, motivating the modified SMAC-v1(PO)/v2(PO) variants.
