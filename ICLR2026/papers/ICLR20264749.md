## Problem

Reinforcement learning has substantially improved short-context reasoning (e.g., math, code) by eliciting longer chain-of-thought, but long-context reasoning—requiring both retrieval from extensive external documents and multi-step reasoning over retrieved facts—remains largely unexplored. Three practical barriers compound the difficulty: high-difficulty long-context RL data with verifiable answers is extremely scarce, full-length (128K) RL rollouts are prohibitively expensive, and training exclusively on long contexts risks degrading short-context and general reasoning abilities.

## Input / Output

The model receives a ~16K-token context (up to 128K at inference) composed of multiple documents with embedded 32-character UUID key-value chains, plus a prompt instructing it to trace a specific starting UUID through the correct chain to recover a hidden question, then answer it. The output is a structured reasoning trajectory following an emergent plan-retrieve-reason-recheck loop, with the final answer enclosed in \boxed{} for unambiguous extraction. The UUID identifiers are deliberately high-entropy and non-semantic (hex characters 0-9, A-F), preventing the model from exploiting lexical or semantic shortcuts during token generation.

## Method

LoongRL's core contribution is KeyChain, a data synthesis pipeline that transforms short multi-hop QA pairs (from HotpotQA, MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA) into high-difficulty long-context tasks by (i) expanding each context to ~16K tokens with real-world distractor documents and (ii) inserting multiple linear UUID key-value chains—only one of which resolves to the true question—forcing the model to trace the correct chain step-by-step before reasoning. Training uses GRPO (group size 8) with a binary reward computed via two-way substring exact match on the boxed answer, which tolerates valid answer variations while mitigating reward hacking. A three-stage curriculum (warm-up on easier data → KeyChain augmentation → difficulty-focused training on the 30–40% hardest remaining examples) is combined with a balanced data mix that includes standard multi-hop QA, needle-in-a-haystack retrieval, and short-context math to preserve diverse capabilities. The key empirical finding is that the plan-retrieve-reason-recheck pattern learned at 16K tokens generalizes to 128K inference, enabling LoongRL-14B to reach 74.2 on long-context QA—rivaling o3-mini (74.5) and DeepSeek-R1 (74.9)—while preserving short-context reasoning and passing all 128K needle-in-a-haystack tests.

## Results

On LongBench v1 long-context multi-hop QA, LoongRL-14B achieves 74.2 average accuracy, rivaling o3-mini (74.5) and DeepSeek-R1 (74.9), while LoongRL-7B reaches 72.4, surpassing the much larger QwenLong-L1-32B (70.1) by +2.3%. Relative to their base models, LoongRL delivers +23.5% absolute gain on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and +21.1% on Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct. On short-context tasks, LoongRL preserves or slightly improves general reasoning (MMLU +2.8% at 7B, +1.1% at 14B) while R1-distilled baselines degrade sharply (IFEval −16.5% at 7B). On RULER retrieval at 128K, LoongRL-14B scores 79.9 versus 73.6 for the base model and 28.2 for R1-Distill-Qwen-14B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against frontier models, LoongRL-14B (74.2) nearly matches o3-mini (74.5) and DeepSeek-R1 (74.9) on long-context reasoning, but both frontier models retain a substantial short-context advantage (92.1 and 90.5 vs. 80.7 average). LoongRL-14B substantially outperforms GPT-4o (64.7) by +9.5 points on long-context QA. Compared to the base Qwen2.5 models, LoongRL yields large absolute gains (+23.5% at 7B, +21.1% at 14B) while preserving short-context reasoning, whereas R1-distilled Qwen variants degrade 7B long-context performance by −17.7% and hurt IFEval by −16.5%. Against QwenLong-L1-32B (the closest long-context RL baseline at 70.1), LoongRL-7B (72.4) wins by +2.3 points despite being less than half the parameter count, and LoongRL-14B extends the lead to +4.1 points. On RULER 128K retrieval, LoongRL-14B (79.9) outperforms QwenLong-L1-32B (70.2) and all R1-distilled models, which collapse to 28–41 at that length.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the short-context RL reasoning paradigm established by DeepSeek-R1 (GRPO with verifiable rewards) to the long-context setting, where the core challenge shifts from internal-knowledge reasoning to retrieval-grounded reasoning over external documents. It departs from prior long-context approaches—prompting-based methods, synthetic-data SFT, and QwenLong-L1's 60K-token RL—by introducing KeyChain, a data synthesis technique that uses high-entropy UUID chains to force explicit step-by-step tracing and reasoning rather than allowing lexical shortcuts. The two-way substring exact match reward design also represents a deliberate departure from the LLM-as-a-judge approach used by QwenLong-L1, favoring simpler rule-based verification to reduce reward hacking and serving overhead.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that models trained on 16K-token contexts generalize effectively to 128K-token inference is a significant practical result, as it avoids the prohibitive compute cost of full-length RL rollouts while maintaining strong performance. A 14B model rivaling o3-mini and DeepSeek-R1 on long-context reasoning (74.2 vs. 74.5/74.9) demonstrates that targeted data design can close the gap with much larger frontier models at a fraction of the scale. Open questions include whether the KeyChain approach generalizes beyond multi-hop QA to other long-context tasks (e.g., code, structured data), whether the emergent plan-retrieve-reason-recheck pattern transfers to non-QA reasoning, and how the method scales to even longer contexts (256K+) or different model architectures.
