## Problem

Electrostatic Field Matching (EFM) performs distribution transfer by following Coulomb field lines between two plates, but suffers from three practical failures: backward-oriented field lines that are difficult to model, forward-oriented lines that can escape beyond the target plane (z > L) requiring unbounded integration, and the resulting need for an unbounded training volume. These issues cause EFM to fail on high-dimensional tasks such as 64×64 CelebA generation.

## Input / Output

For image generation, the method takes Gaussian noise samples N(0, I_D) placed on the z=0 hyperplane and learns to transport them to data images (CIFAR-10 at 32×32, CelebA at 64×64 or 128×128) on the z=L hyperplane, producing generated images. For image-to-image translation, source images (e.g., colored MNIST digit '2' or Winter scenes at 64×64) are placed on z=0 and transported to the target distribution on z=L. All operations occur in (D+1)-dimensional space where D is the flattened image dimensionality (e.g., D=3072 for 32×32 RGB).

## Method

IFM generalizes EFM by replacing the Coulomb electrostatic field with an arbitrary interaction field satisfying three axioms: field lines start at quarks and terminate at antiquarks, flux is conserved along stream tubes, and the total field is a superposition weighted by a transport plan π. The paper's specific realization, inspired by quark-antiquark strong interaction, defines a 'string' field whose width σ(z) follows a sinusoidal profile near the plates and is constant in the middle, with Gaussian radial decay; this design eliminates backward-oriented lines entirely and prevents any line from extending beyond z > L. A neural network f_θ: R^{D+1} → R^{D+1} is trained to predict the normalized interaction field by minimizing squared error against a Monte Carlo estimate computed over B batch pairs sampled from π (independent or minibatch optimal transport). Inference integrates the learned ODE d x̃/dz = (f_θ,x / f_θ,z, 1) from z=0 to z=L with a fixed Euler step, requiring no stopping criterion since lines are guaranteed to terminate at the target plane. Crucially, the 1/σ(z)^D normalization factor cancels in the normalized field objective, avoiding the numerical instability of the Coulomb 1/‖x̃−x̃'‖^D term that plagues EFM in high dimensions.

## Results

On 32×32 CIFAR-10 image generation, IFM achieves a FID of 2.28, outperforming EFM (2.62), FM (2.99), DDPM (3.12), and PFGM (2.76), while remaining competitive with PFGM++ (2.15) and StyleGAN (2.48). On the more challenging 64×64 CelebA dataset, IFM reaches FID 3.07, dramatically outperforming EFM (>100), FM (14.45), DDPM (12.26), and styleGAN (3.68), and approaching PFGM++ (2.89). For unpaired image translation, IFM with minibatch OT (IFM-MB) achieves the best CMMD scores: 0.87 on colored MNIST '2'→'3' and 1.13 on Winter→summer, where both EFM and FM fail entirely (CMMD > 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IFM clearly outperforms its direct predecessor EFM on both generation tasks (FID 2.28 vs 2.62 on CIFAR-10; 3.07 vs >100 on CelebA), confirming that the strong-interaction-inspired field eliminates the backward-line and line-termination problems that cripple the electrostatic model at scale. Against PFGM++ (the strongest electrostatic baseline), IFM is slightly behind on CIFAR-10 (2.28 vs 2.15) and CelebA (3.07 vs 2.89) but avoids the numerical instability of the Coulomb 1/‖x‖^D factor in high dimensions. IFM substantially outperforms flow matching (FM) and DDPM on CelebA (3.07 vs 14.45 and 12.26), suggesting the physics-inspired field structure provides a more stable training signal than the linear-interpolant or score-based objectives at this resolution. On image translation, IFM-MB (with minibatch OT) achieves the best CMMD on both tasks (0.87 on '2'→'3', 1.13 on W→S), beating CycleGAN (0.90, 1.33) and DDIB (0.96, 1.39), while EFM and FM both fail (CMMD > 1) on the 64×64 winter→summer task. Overall, IFM is competitive with or superior to all baselines, with the closest competitor being PFGM++ on generation and cycleGAN on translation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly generalizes Electrostatic Field Matching (EFM, Kolesov et al., ICML 2025), which itself extends Poisson Flow Generative Models (PFGM, Xu et al., 2022/2023) from noise-to-data generation to data-to-data transfer. The key conceptual departure is replacing the specific Coulomb electrostatic field with an arbitrary 'interaction field' satisfying flux conservation and a generalized superposition principle, then instantiating it with a string-like field inspired by quark-antiquark strong interactions. The method also incorporates minibatch optimal transport (Pooladian et al., 2023) for the transport plan, and positions itself alongside flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023) and diffusion (Ho et al., 2020) as an alternative ODE-based generative paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IFM resolves three concrete practical failures of EFM (backward-oriented field lines, line termination beyond the target plane, and unbounded training volume) through a physically motivated field design that provably eliminates these issues. The multi-sample field estimation via superposition offers a variance-reduction advantage over flow matching's single-sample regression, though the practical significance of this remains to be demonstrated at scale. The paper is explicitly proof-of-concept: the specific string-field realization is acknowledged as one of many possibilities, and the search for an optimal interaction field is left as open future work. Performance is competitive (FID 2.28 CIFAR-10, 3.07 CelebA 64×64) but does not surpass PFGM++ or StyleGAN, limiting immediate practical adoption.
