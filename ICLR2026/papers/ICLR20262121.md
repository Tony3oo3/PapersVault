## Problem

VLA models trained via imitation learning are brittle to out-of-distribution states and cannot learn from failures, while direct RL on real robots is prohibitively sample-inefficient. Existing model-based RL approaches operate in abstract latent spaces that are fundamentally mismatched with VLA policies pretrained on web-scale real-world images, preventing the policy from leveraging its pretrained visual understanding during imagined rollouts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262121/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The VLA policy consumes the most recent m frames plus a language instruction and outputs an action chunk of K=8 steps, where each step is a D-DoF control vector with every dimension discretized into 256 bins; proprioceptive state and wrist camera inputs are omitted. The world model takes c=4 conditioning frames and one action chunk to autoregressively generate the next K=8 pixel-space frames, and a lightweight VideoMAE-based reward model scores video clips of length L=8 to produce a binary success/failure label for the full trajectory.

## Method

WMPO grounds on-policy GRPO entirely within a pixel-space video diffusion world model (OpenSora backbone with SDXL 2D VAE) pretrained on Open X-Embodiment and fine-tuned via Policy Behavior Alignment on the policy's own rollouts to faithfully simulate failure modes. Two key generation techniques ensure stable long-horizon rollouts: noisy-frame conditioning (perturbing conditioning frames with early-timestep diffusion noise to prevent error accumulation) and frame-level action control (extending AdaLN to inject per-frame action and timestep embeddings for precise action-frame alignment). A lightweight VideoMAE reward model provides binary outcome-based rewards, and the policy is optimized with GRPO (following DAPO's removal of KL regularization) using Dynamic Sampling to discard uninformative all-success or all-failure groups, enabling repeated rollouts from the same initial state that are infeasible in the physical world.

## Results

On the Mimicgen simulation benchmark (four fine-grained manipulation tasks), WMPO achieves a mean success rate of 47.1% with a rollout budget of P=128, surpassing the strongest baseline (DPO at 37.3%) by +9.8 points. Scaling the budget to P=1280, WMPO reaches 57.6% mean success rate, widening the gap to +15.2 points over DPO (42.4%) and +20.5 points over online GRPO (37.1%). On the real-robot 'Insert the square into the stick' task, WMPO achieves 70% success rate versus 60% for DPO and 53% for the base policy. The reward model achieves F1 > 0.95 across all tasks, and WMPO-trained policies produce significantly shorter successful trajectories, indicating more efficient execution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WMPO is compared against two baselines: online GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and offline DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), both allocated the same real rollout budget P. WMPO wins decisively on all four Mimicgen tasks under both budget settings: at P=128 it outperforms DPO by +9.8 points and GRPO by +13.9 points in mean success rate; at P=1280 the margins grow to +15.2 and +20.5 points respectively. GRPO often underperforms even the base policy at P=128 (33.2% vs. 33.6%), while DPO plateaus due to static data reuse. On generalization to novel disruptions (position, background, texture), WMPO achieves 29.6% mean success rate versus 24.7% (GRPO) and 19.5% (DPO), with DPO actually degrading below the base policy under background and texture shifts. On the real-robot task, WMPO reaches 70% success rate, outperforming DPO (60%) and the base policy (53%). In lifelong learning experiments, WMPO shows stable iterative improvement while DPO fails to improve across iterations due to unstable training.

## Relation to Prior Work

WMPO sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) VLA models pretrained on web-scale vision-language data (OpenVLA, RT-2, π0), (2) video-generative world models (OpenSora, Cosmos, Genie 3), and (3) on-policy RL algorithms from the LLM literature (GRPO, DAPO). The key conceptual departure is from latent-space world models (Dreamer, RSSM) that have dominated model-based RL: WMPO argues that pixel-space video generation is essential to preserve alignment with the VLA's pretrained visual representations, and introduces policy behavior alignment to close the distribution gap between expert demonstrations and policy rollouts. The frame-level action control via extended AdaLN and noisy-frame conditioning are practical innovations borrowed from video diffusion literature and adapted for precise robot-object interaction simulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WMPO addresses the central bottleneck in deploying VLA models—the prohibitive cost of real-robot RL—by enabling on-policy optimization entirely within a learned world model, with demonstrated emergent self-correction and lifelong learning. However, the experimental scope remains limited to four simulation tasks and one real-robot task on a single platform, and the framework currently requires discretized actions (not flow-based policies), assumes fully observable MDPs, and depends on a separate reward model whose threshold sensitivity is not deeply analyzed. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multi-task or multi-embodiment settings, how sensitive performance is to world model fidelity on more complex dynamics, and whether the pixel-space requirement can be relaxed without losing the VLA alignment benefit.
