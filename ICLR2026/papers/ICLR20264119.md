## Problem

Reinforcement learning for LLM agents is bottlenecked by four interrelated barriers: prohibitively costly real-environment rollouts, a scarcity of diverse and verifiable tasks, unstable or sparse reward signals in dynamic environments, and heavy infrastructure requirements (Docker, VMs) that limit parallel sampling. No existing framework addresses all four simultaneously, making scalable RL training for general-purpose agents in arbitrary environments an open problem.

## Input / Output

The experience model consumes a task instruction, the current textual state, the agent's action, the full interaction history, and top-k semantically similar trajectories retrieved from a replay buffer, and produces a next-state prediction, a scalar reward, and an explicit chain-of-thought reasoning trace that explains the causal transition. States are represented in an abstract meta-representational textual space (e.g., clean element listings rather than raw HTML), which reduces irrelevant dimensions and makes the model highly sample-efficient. The curriculum task generator additionally outputs new task variations derived from high-reward-entropy seed tasks, and the full pipeline ultimately yields a trained agent policy that can be deployed in real environments.

## Method

DREAMGYM centers on a reasoning-based experience model (M_exp) that distills environment dynamics into an abstract textual state space and predicts next states and rewards via explicit chain-of-thought reasoning, rather than replicating raw observations. The model is trained with a joint SFT objective over reasoning-trace generation and next-state prediction, requiring only small offline trajectory datasets (as few as 2k–10k transitions) annotated with LLM-generated causal explanations. At inference time, an experience replay buffer—seeded with offline real-world data and continuously enriched with on-the-fly synthetic trajectories—provides retrieval-augmented context to reduce hallucination and maintain consistency with the agent's evolving policy. A curriculum task generator, sharing parameters with M_exp, selects tasks with high group-based reward entropy (indicating intermediate difficulty) and generates progressively harder variations, creating an adaptive curriculum that co-evolves with the agent. The framework is orthogonal to the underlying RL algorithm (validated with both PPO and GRPO) and supports a sim-to-real transfer mode where synthetic pretraining provides a warm start before a small-scale real-environment RL phase.

## Results

On WebArena (a non-RL-ready environment), DREAMGYM with Llama-3.1-8B achieves 9.1% success rate under GRPO and 10.9% under PPO, outperforming traditional RL baselines (6.1% and 4.8% respectively) by over 30% while using zero real-environment interactions. On RL-ready environments (WebShop, ALFWorld), DREAMGYM matches or approaches GRPO/PPO trained on 80K real transitions using purely synthetic rollouts (e.g., 63.9% vs. 65.0% on WebShop with GRPO). The sim-to-real variant DREAMGYM-S2R, using only 5K real transitions after synthetic pretraining, consistently surpasses all baselines, reaching 75.0% on WebShop and 75.9% on ALFWorld (Llama-3.1-8B), exceeding traditional RL by 10+ points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DREAMGYM is compared against four baselines: SFT and DPO (offline imitation learning), and GRPO and PPO (online RL in real environments with 80K transitions). On WebArena, where RL infrastructure is impractical, DREAMGYM decisively wins: with Llama-3.1-8B, it achieves 9.1% (GRPO) and 10.9% (PPO) versus 6.1% and 4.8% for traditional RL, and 5.5% and 4.8% for SFT/DPO respectively — a >30% relative improvement over all baselines. On WebShop and ALFWorld, DREAMGYM with zero real data roughly matches traditional GRPO/PPO (within 1–4 points), confirming synthetic rollouts are sufficient for stable policy improvement. The DREAMGYM-S2R variant, which adds only 5K real transitions after synthetic pretraining, outperforms all baselines by 5–10+ points across environments (e.g., 75.0% vs. 65.0% on WebShop-GRPO, 75.9% vs. 70.9% on ALFWorld-GRPO), demonstrating that synthetic warm-starting yields substantially better sample efficiency than training from scratch in real environments.

## Relation to Prior Work

DREAMGYM sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) RL for LLM agents (PPO, GRPO), to which it is orthogonal and simply supplies better training data; (2) synthetic trajectory generation for agent training (AgentSynth, SCA, WebDreamer, UI-Simulator), from which it departs by operating in an abstract textual meta-representation space with explicit chain-of-thought reasoning rather than attempting to replicate raw HTML or pixel-level observations; and (3) world-model-based RL (Dreamer, AlphaGo), which it adapts to the LLM-agent setting by replacing learned latent dynamics with a reasoning-based experience model trained via SFT on small offline datasets. The key conceptual departure from closest prior work (UI-Simulator, WebDreamer) is the unification of experience synthesis, replay-buffer grounding, and reward-entropy-driven curriculum task generation into a single scalable framework, rather than treating simulation and task generation as separate concerns.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most immediate impact is making RL training feasible in environments that lack the infrastructure for it (e.g., WebArena), where DREAMGYM is essentially the only viable path to RL-based improvement. The sim-to-real warm-start strategy (40%+ gains with <10% real data) offers a practical recipe for reducing the cost of deploying agents in production. The theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) is a useful addition, showing that policy improvement in the real environment depends only on reward faithfulness and transition consistency rather than strict state reconstruction, which validates the abstract-state design choice. Open questions include how well the approach scales to truly open-ended or multi-modal environments, whether the reward-entropy curriculum generalizes beyond binary outcome rewards, and whether the cross-domain transfer limitations (web → embodied) can be addressed with richer meta-representations.
