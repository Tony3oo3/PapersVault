## Problem

Offline RL suffers from value overestimation on out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, and existing mitigations either apply uniform penalties that suppress potentially beneficial exploration beyond the behavioral support, or rely on restrictive distributional assumptions (e.g., Gaussian VAEs) that fail to capture multi-modal behavior policies. No prior method simultaneously achieves accurate OOD detection without such assumptions and distinguishes between OOD actions that are genuinely harmful versus those that could improve policy performance.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (state, action, reward, next-state) tuples collected by a behavior policy, with no online environment interaction. During training, it produces a Tanh-Gaussian policy network mapping states to continuous actions, a set of Q/V critic networks, and per-sample OOD detection scores computed as single-step denoising reconstruction errors from two pretrained diffusion models. The OOD thresholds τ_a and τ_s are set as the p-th percentile of in-sample reconstruction errors, making the conservatism level tunable per dataset without retraining.

## Method

DOSER trains two EDM-based diffusion models—a conditional model ε_θa(a_t, σ_t, s) for the behavior policy and an unconditional model ε_θs(s_t, σ_t) for the state distribution—and uses their single-step denoising reconstruction error as a likelihood-free OOD indicator, avoiding Gaussian assumptions while capturing multi-modal distributions. A learned dynamics model p_ψ(s'|s,a) then enables adaptive OOD action classification: an OOD action is deemed beneficial if its predicted next state remains in-distribution and yields a higher value than the best in-distribution action, otherwise it is deemed detrimental. The critic loss applies a penalty (driving Q toward Q_min) only to detrimental OOD actions while granting a value-difference bonus δ_V = V(s'_π) − V(s'_id) to beneficial ones, enabling selective regularization rather than uniform penalization. Theoretically, the DOSER Bellman operator is proven to be a γ-contraction with a unique fixed point, and the performance gap to the optimal policy is bounded in terms of dynamics model error, OOD detection misclassification probability, and function approximation error.

## Results

On the D4RL MuJoCo-v2 benchmark (9 tasks), DOSER achieves a normalized score average of 93.2, edging out the best conventional baseline A2PR (93.0) and SVR (91.4). On the more challenging Adroit-v1 tasks, DOSER scores 83.6 average, substantially outperforming the next-best SVR (71.7) and DTQL (72.7). The advantage is most pronounced on suboptimal datasets: on halfcheetah-medium-replay, DOSER reaches 63.0 versus 56.6 (A2PR) and 55.9 (ACL-QL), and on pen-human it achieves 87.8 versus 73.9 (QGPO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against conventional offline RL methods (TD3+BC, IQL, CQL), DOSER achieves substantially higher averages (93.2 vs. 75.3, 83.3, 77.6 on MuJoCo-v2), with the largest gaps on suboptimal datasets like halfcheetah-medium-replay (63.0 vs. 44.6/44.2/45.5). Among value-regularization baselines, DOSER outperforms SVR (91.4) and ACL-QL (89.6) on the MuJoCo-v2 average, with particularly large margins on medium-replay tasks (e.g., halfcheetah-m-r: 63.0 vs. 52.5/55.9). Against the strongest policy-constraint baseline A2PR (93.0), DOSER edges ahead on the MuJoCo-v2 average and wins decisively on medium-replay (63.0 vs. 56.6 on halfcheetah-m-r), though A2PR retains small advantages on a few medium-expert tasks (e.g., walker2d-m-e: 114.6 vs. 110.9). Among diffusion-based methods, DOSER surpasses DQL (88.0), IDQL (82.1), and DTQL (88.7) on the MuJoCo-v2 average, and dominates on Adroit (83.6 vs. 65.1/–/72.7), where the pen-human task shows the largest gap (87.8 vs. 72.8 for DQL). The one notable loss is pen-cloned, where DTQL (81.3) slightly exceeds DOSER (79.3), and walker2d-medium, where SVR (92.4) and A2PR (89.7) both outperform DOSER (86.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

DOSER builds on the EDM diffusion framework (Karras et al., 2022) for generative modeling, IQL's expectile regression (Kostrikov et al., 2021) for value learning, and SAC's Tanh-Gaussian policy (Haarnoja et al., 2018) for actor optimization. It departs from the dominant VAE-based behavior modeling paradigm (BCQ, SVR, CVAE-based methods) by using diffusion model reconstruction error as a likelihood-free OOD detection metric, and from uniform-penalization value regularization (CQL, SVR) by introducing a selective mechanism that distinguishes beneficial from detrimental OOD actions via a learned dynamics model. Unlike prior diffusion-based offline RL methods (DQL, IDQL, SfBC, QGPO, SRPO, DTQL) that employ diffusion for action generation or resampling, DOSER repurposes the reconstruction fidelity of diffusion models as a detection signal, which is a distinct use of the same architectural component.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The selective regularization mechanism addresses a fundamental tension in offline RL: uniform conservatism suppresses potentially high-value actions outside the behavior support, while unconstrained exploration risks catastrophic value overestimation. The theoretical guarantees (γ-contraction, bounded Q-values, performance gap bound) provide a principled foundation that prior heuristic approaches lack. Key limitations include reliance on the accuracy of the learned dynamics model for OOD action classification and the computational overhead of diffusion sampling, which the authors acknowledge as open problems for future work (model distillation, accelerated sampling). The approach's dependence on task-specific hyperparameters (thresholds, penalty coefficient) also limits its plug-and-play applicability.
