## Problem

Despite the proliferation of dataset distillation methods with diverse objectives—statistical matching, gradient matching, trajectory matching, and kernel ridge regression—their underlying connections remain largely unexplored. The paper asks whether a unified framework can explain these methods and reveal what each actually captures, and whether this understanding can guide the design of better distillation objectives.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real dataset (e.g., CIFAR-10/100 at 32×32, Tiny-ImageNet at 64×64, ImageNet-1K at 224×224) with one-hot labels and a pre-trained distillation backbone (ResNet-18 or ConvNet-128), and produces a compact synthetic dataset of m ≪ n images (IPC = 10, 50, or 100) with corresponding labels. The distillation operates in the representation space of the backbone, computing feature-feature correlation (FFC) and feature-label correlation (FLC) matrices at every convolutional layer rather than in pixel space, and uses a squeeze-recover-relabel pipeline for large-scale datasets.

## Method

UniDD unifies four representative DD objectives (DM, DC, MTT, FrePo) by showing each corresponds to a specific filter function f(·) applied to the eigenvalues of the FFC matrix, extracting frequency-specific components of the FLC matrix; low-pass filters (DM, DC) capture coarse-grained colors while high-pass filters (MTT, FrePo) synthesize fine-grained textures. Building on this, CFM proposes a dynamic filter f(λ) = (λ + β_b)^{-1} where β_b follows a cosine schedule (β_b = β·(1 + cos(πb/B))/2), gradually shifting from low-frequency to high-frequency matching across training batches to balance consistency and diversity. The loss combines a filter-matching term (aligning filtered FFC matrices between real and synthetic data) and a signal-matching term (aligning filtered FLC matrices), plus a standard classification loss, with an exponential moving update to approximate full-batch statistics from mini-batches.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with IPC=50 and ResNet-18, CFM achieves 82.3% top-1 accuracy, substantially outperforming the next-best baseline G-VBDM (59.2%) and SRe2L (47.5%). On ImageNet-1k with IPC=50 and ResNet-18, CFM reaches 57.3%, surpassing RDED (56.5%) and DWA (55.2%). For cross-architecture generalization on ImageNet-1k (IPC=50), CFM achieves the best accuracy across all six validation architectures (ResNet-50: 63.23%, ResNet-101: 63.46%, DenseNet-121: 60.91%, RegNet-Y-8GF: 64.03%, ConvNeXt-Tiny: 64.89%), exceeding the strongest baseline CDA by 1.9–2.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CFM consistently outperforms SRe2L by large margins across all datasets (e.g., +34.8 pts on CIFAR-10 IPC=50 ResNet-18, +10.5 pts on ImageNet-1k IPC=50 ResNet-18), confirming that matching only BN statistics is insufficient. Against G-VBSM and DWA, CFM wins on every reported setting, with gaps of 5–15 points on CIFAR-100 and 2–6 points on ImageNet-1k. On CIFAR-100, CFM surpasses the traditional methods MTT and FrePo by 24–25 points (ConvNet-128, IPC=50), while on CIFAR-10 with ConvNet-128, MTT and FrePo still lead CFM by ~7 points, indicating that low-budget, shallow-backbone settings favor high-pass trajectory/kernel methods. CFM slightly trails RDED on ImageNet-1k IPC=10 (40.6% vs 42.0%), which the authors attribute to insufficient frequency coverage under very tight memory budgets, but recovers the lead at IPC=50 and IPC=100. For cross-architecture generalization, CFM exceeds CDA (the strongest baseline) by 1.9–2.3 points across ResNet-50/101, DenseNet-121, RegNet-Y-8GF, and ConvNeXt-Tiny, demonstrating that the distilled data encodes architecture-agnostic knowledge.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the kernel ridge regression formulation of FrePo (Zhou et al., 2022), whose filter f(λ)=(λ+β)^{-1} becomes the template for the proposed CFM method, and on the exponential moving update strategy from G-VBSM (Shao et al., 2024) for handling batch-wise statistics. Conceptually, it departs from the ad-hoc objective design paradigm of prior DD work by reframing four major method families—statistical matching (DM), gradient matching (DC), trajectory matching (MTT), and KRR (FrePo)—as instances of a single spectral filtering framework (UniDD) where the FFC matrix eigenvalues are modulated by a filter function to extract frequency components of the FLC matrix. This shifts the design question from 'which matching objective to use' to 'which filter to apply,' providing a principled axis for method comparison and new method design.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The UniDD framework gives the DD community a common language for understanding why different objectives produce qualitatively different synthetic data (blurred vs. textured), which should guide future method design beyond intuition. CFM's curriculum strategy addresses a concrete limitation—fixed filters can only capture one frequency band—and delivers consistent gains across CIFAR-10/100, Tiny-ImageNet, and ImageNet-1K with strong cross-architecture generalization. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-linear kernels (Gaussian, polynomial), unsupervised or multi-modal distillation, and whether the observed degradation at very low budgets (IPC=10 on ImageNet) can be mitigated by adaptive frequency scheduling.
