## Problem

Existing agent memory paradigms are fundamentally limited: parametric memory modifies model weights (causing catastrophic forgetting), while retrieval-based memory appends static textual context at task initiation (rigid, non-integrated). Neither captures the fluid, reconstructive interplay between reasoning and memory that characterizes human cognition, where thought and recollection dynamically reshape one another throughout a reasoning process.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260772/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each token-generation step where the trigger activates (restricted to delimiter positions like commas and periods), the memory weaver receives the reasoner's full hidden-state prefix H_{t,<j} ∈ R^{(j-1)×d_model} as its sole stimulus, optionally concatenated with embeddings of externally retrieved text snippets. It produces a fixed-length latent memory matrix M_t ∈ R^{K×d_model} (K ∈ {2,4,8}) that is projected into the reasoner's token-embedding space and prepended to the hidden states before the next token is sampled. The latent tokens are machine-native and human-unreadable, though they exhibit structural regularities within clusters (e.g., recurring suffix patterns like '_check' or 'pick').

## Method

MemGen comprises two LoRA adapters on a frozen LLM reasoner: a memory trigger that outputs a binary INVOKE/SKIP decision at delimiter positions based on hidden states, and a memory weaver that synthesizes a context-dependent latent token sequence when invoked. The trigger is trained via GRPO with a reward-adaptive sparsity penalty (penalizing activations above the mean frequency in high-reward trajectories) to learn sparse, strategically critical invocation. The weaver is trained (via SFT or GRPO) to maximize downstream task reward while keeping the reasoner entirely frozen, so all experiential knowledge is internalized exclusively into the weaver's parameters. Training proceeds in two stages: the weaver is first trained with random insertion at punctuation as a surrogate trigger, then the trigger is trained with the weaver frozen. This design enables token-level, on-demand memory generation that is a selective reconstruction (analogous to human memory consolidation) rather than a verbatim retrieval, and the framework can optionally fuse externally retrieved text into the weaver's input for hybrid parametric+retrieval memory.

## Results

On Qwen3-8B, MemGen GRPO achieves 90.60% on ALFWorld, 80.65% on TriviaQA, 62.30% on PopQA, 76.16% on KodCode, 75.56% on BigCodeBench, 40.24% on GPQA, 93.20% on GSM8K, and 88.24% on MATH, surpassing the vanilla model by up to +31.67% (ALFWorld) and +28.17% (PopQA). Compared to the strongest parametric baseline GRPO, MemGen GRPO gains up to +5.0% on ALFWorld and +3.4% on PopQA. Against retrieval-based baselines, MemGen GRPO outperforms ExpeL by up to +21.97% (PopQA) and AWM by +10.27% (ALFWorld). Cross-domain generalization is demonstrated by training on GSM8K and transferring to KodCode (+3.1%) and GPQA (+6.06%) without degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MemGen GRPO (Qwen3-8B) achieves the best or second-best result on all eight benchmarks, winning outright on seven of eight (losing only GPQA to MemGen SFT's 43.23 vs. 40.24, though both exceed all other baselines). Against the strongest parametric baseline GRPO, MemGen GRPO gains +5.0% on ALFWorld (90.60 vs. 85.60), +3.4% on PopQA (62.30 vs. 58.90), and +2.81% on KodCode (76.16 vs. 73.35). Against retrieval-based baselines, the margins are substantially larger: MemGen GRPO beats ExpeL by +11.63% on ALFWorld, +15.11% on TriviaQA, and +21.97% on PopQA, and beats AWM by +10.27% on ALFWorld. Against latent-computation baselines, MemGen GRPO outperforms SoftCoT by +15.0% on ALFWorld and +21.23% on TriviaQA, and Co-processor by +17.32% on ALFWorld and +19.23% on TriviaQA. The paper reports that across all three backbone sizes (Qwen2.5-1.5B, SmolLM3-3B, Qwen3-8B), MemGen consistently surpasses every baseline, with the largest absolute gains (up to 38.22%) observed over retrieval-based methods on knowledge-intensive tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

MemGen sits at the intersection of latent computation (Coconut, SoftCoT, Co-processor) and agent memory systems (ExpeL, MemoryBank, AWM), but departs from both in a key way: rather than using latent tokens as a fixed reasoning substrate or retrieving static textual memories, it *generates* context-sensitive latent memory on-the-fly at token-level granularity during reasoning. The memory trigger, trained via RL with a sparsity penalty, is a novel component absent from prior latent-computation or retrieval-based agent memory work, and the memory weaver's role as a generative reconstructor (analogous to human memory consolidation) distinguishes it from both parametric fine-tuning and embedding-similarity retrieval. The framework is architecturally lightweight (two LoRA adapters on a frozen backbone) and agnostic to the underlying optimization (SFT or GRPO), making it a modular add-on rather than a new training paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The most practically significant contribution is that MemGen achieves strong cross-domain generalization and continual learning without modifying the base model, directly addressing the catastrophic-forgetting problem that plagues parametric agent tuning. The emergent memory hierarchy (planning, procedural, working memory) discovered through post-hoc intervention is conceptually intriguing but rests on a single-dataset K-means analysis, leaving open whether these functional specializations are robust across architectures and scales. Key open questions include whether the trigger's learned invocation policy transfers to unseen task types, how the framework scales to multi-agent or long-horizon settings, and whether the latent memory can be made interpretable or steerable by users.
