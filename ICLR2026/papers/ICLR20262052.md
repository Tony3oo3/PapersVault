## Problem

Unified multimodal models integrate LLM reasoning with image generation, but no existing benchmark systematically measures whether the reasoning capability actually transfers into faithful visual outputs. Prior benchmarks either test shallow text-image alignment or rely on MLLM-as-a-Judge scoring, which couples evaluation results to the biases of the judge model and cannot decompose reasoning-centric tasks into interpretable, verifiable sub-metrics.

## Input / Output

GIR-Bench-UGC feeds implicit, reasoning-oriented text prompts (e.g., describing the Charging Bull without naming it) to generation models and paired reference images to understanding models; GIR-Bench-T2I uses 300 prompts encoding numerical constraints, spatial arrangements, or implicit text references; GIR-Bench-Edit provides an input image plus a natural-language editing instruction (e.g., a shuffled jigsaw grid or a Sudoku puzzle image). All generated outputs are evaluated programmatically via object detection (InternVL3.5-38B), OCR (PPOCR v5), DINOv3 feature similarity, FID, or IoU rather than by a multimodal judge.

## Method

GIR-Bench comprises three complementary suites—UGC (300 real-world entities with implicit prompts), T2I (300 prompts across numerical reasoning, spatial layout, and text rendering), and Edit (370 cases spanning visual puzzles, Sudoku, and reasoning perception)—each paired with a task-specific, deterministic evaluation pipeline. For generation, DINOv3 feature similarity against curated reference sets quantifies entity fidelity; for T2I, object detection verifies counts and bounding-box spatial relations, while a novel word-level continuous substring score (s_wc) handles text rendering robustly to extra generated content and merged OCR tokens. For editing, FID measures jigsaw reconstruction, text detection plus digit comparison scores Sudoku solutions, and IoU on extracted green masks evaluates region segmentation. The design explicitly prioritizes tasks with programmatically verifiable ground truth and excludes solvable-by-keyword-mapping cases, ensuring that measured performance reflects genuine multi-step reasoning rather than surface-level prompt matching.

## Results

On GIR-Bench-T2I, the strongest unified models achieve overall scores of 0.650 (Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image) and 0.622 (GPT-Image-1), with per-task peaks of 0.362 (Numerical Reasoning), 0.782 (Spatial Layout), and 0.813 (Text Rendering). On GIR-Bench-Edit, even the best models score only 0.351 (GPT-Image-1) and 0.343 (Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image), revealing severe limitations in reasoning-driven editing. On GIR-Bench-UGC, a stark understanding–generation gap is exposed: unified models score 0.874–0.974 on understanding but only 0.198–0.689 on generation, with GPT-Image-1 leading generation at 0.689. BAGEL-7B w/CoT improves T2I overall from 0.169 to 0.276, confirming that explicit chain-of-thought partially bridges the reasoning-to-generation gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Unified models consistently outperform generation-only baselines on reasoning-centric T2I: GPT-Image-1 (0.622) and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image (0.650) surpass the best generation-only model FLUX.1-schnell (0.159) by over 4×. On GIR-Bench-UGC generation, GPT-Image-1 (0.689) and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image (0.593) also dominate generation-only systems (HiDream-I1-Full 0.378, SD-3.5-Large 0.288, FLUX.1-schnell 0.292). However, on GIR-Bench-Edit the gaps narrow considerably: editing-only models (FLUX.1-Kontext 0.105, ICEdit 0.095, Step1X-Edit 0.071) are competitive with weaker unified models (UniWorld 0.054, OmniGen2 0.073), and even the best unified models (GPT-Image-1 0.351, Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image 0.343) remain far below the understanding scores of 0.87–0.99 achieved by dedicated MLLMs (GPT-5 0.994, Gemini-2.5-Flash 0.997, BLIP3o 0.974). Among open-source unified models, BAGEL w/CoT (0.276 T2I, 0.140 Edit) substantially outperforms its base BAGEL-7B (0.169, 0.098), while Qwen-Image (0.224 T2I) and Ovis-U1-3B (0.171 T2I) trail proprietary systems by a wide margin. The key finding is that no model closes the understanding–generation gap: even GPT-Image-1, which scores 0.689 on UGC generation, has no corresponding understanding score, while models with high understanding (BLIP3o 0.974) score only 0.263 on generation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of unified multimodal model evaluation and reasoning-centric benchmarking. Prior benchmarks (GenEval, T2I-CompBench, WISE, T2I-ReasonBench, RISEBench) focused on compositional alignment or relied heavily on MLLM-as-a-Judge scoring, but none systematically measured the understanding-generation consistency gap within a single unified model. GIR-Bench repurposes existing deterministic tools (object detection via InternVL3.5, OCR via PPOCR, DINOv3 feature similarity, FID, IoU) into task-specific pipelines that avoid the stochasticity of LLM judges, and introduces the novel evaluation axis of whether a model can transfer the same knowledge from its understanding pathway into its generation pathway.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest proprietary unified models (GPT-Image-1, Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image) achieve only ~0.65 on reasoning-centric T2I and ~0.35 on reasoning-centric editing, exposing a persistent and quantifiable gap between what models can recognize and what they can faithfully generate. The identification of three root causes—reasoning asymmetry localized in the LLM, information bottleneck at the LLM-to-generator interface, and lack of interleaved reasoning-trace training data—provides concrete, actionable directions for closing this gap. Open questions include whether natively unified architectures (treating text and image tokens equally) can eliminate the interface bottleneck, and whether explicit CoT supervision at scale can reliably bridge reasoning to pixel-level generation.
