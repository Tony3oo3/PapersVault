## Problem

Full-image relighting remains unsolved because existing methods either ignore the background (object-centric approaches) or rely on purely data-driven priors that fail to maintain physical plausibility and generalize beyond training distributions. No prior method simultaneously achieves controllable lighting direction/intensity, preserves intrinsic material properties (albedo, roughness, metallic), and handles self-luminous objects or built-in scene lighting without artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263280/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a single RGB image and a target lighting condition encoded as a rendered gray ball (a sphere with 50% roughness displaying only the front hemisphere of the environment map), which avoids interference from self-luminous objects and rear-hemisphere lighting. Stage 1 outputs four intrinsic maps (albedo, normal, roughness, metallic) at the same spatial resolution as the input, while Stage 2 outputs the final relit image alongside separate diffuse and specular shading components. Internally, the model consumes batchwise concatenated channels (4 batches in Stage 1, 3 in Stage 2) with one-hot labels routing each batch to a specific output, and all diffusion operations occur in the 8×-downsampled VAE latent space.

## Method

PI-Light is a two-stage finetuning of Stable Diffusion (initialized from GeoWizard weights) that decomposes an image into PBR intrinsics and then re-renders under new lighting. Stage 1 extends standard self-attention to a cross-batch variant (inspired by Wonder3D) so that queries for one intrinsic attend to keys/values from all four intrinsics simultaneously, enforcing structural consistency across albedo, normal, roughness, and metallic predictions. Stage 2 conditions on three batchwise inputs—(image, albedo), (normal, lighting, mask), and (normal, lighting, metallic, roughness, mask)—to produce the relit image, diffuse map, and specular map respectively, with the batch structure ensuring each output channel receives the physically relevant conditioning. The key differentiator is a set of physics-inspired losses computed in RGB space after VAE decoding: a Diffuse Shading Loss that compares the predicted diffuse map against a Lambertian lookup computed from the normal map and the gray-ball environment map, and a Physical-based Shading Loss that enforces the surface reflection equation I_relit = A⊙D + S, regularizing training toward physically plausible light transport and improving generalization to unseen materials and scenes.

## Results

On the Object50 inverse rendering benchmark, PI-Light achieves an average PSNR of 20.53, SSIM of 0.8934, and LPIPS of 0.0761, outperforming RGB↔X (19.32 PSNR) and Kocsis et al. (19.15 PSNR). On Scene200, it reaches 12.95 average PSNR versus 11.75 for RGB↔X and 12.21 for Kocsis et al. For forward rendering on Object50, PI-Light achieves 14.09 PSNR / 0.9211 SSIM / 0.0553 LPIPS, surpassing DiLightNet (12.21 PSNR) and Neural Gaffer (11.72 PSNR) by a substantial margin.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PI-Light outperforms all four inverse-rendering baselines on Object50: it leads RGB↔X by +1.21 PSNR (20.53 vs 19.32) on average, Kocsis et al. by +1.38 PSNR, and Zhu et al. by +3.10 PSNR, with the largest gap on roughness (21.09 vs 18.43 for RGB↔X). On Scene200, the advantage over RGB↔X narrows to +1.20 PSNR (12.95 vs 11.75), while Kocsis et al. and Zhu et al. remain well behind due to their lower native resolutions. For forward rendering, PI-Light achieves 14.09 PSNR on Object50, beating DiLightNet (12.21) by +1.88 and Neural Gaffer (11.72) by +2.37; RGB↔X is only evaluated under a reconstruction setting (9.64 PSNR) since it lacks environment-map control. In qualitative comparisons on real images, PI-Light surpasses IC-Light (Zhang et al., 2025) in preserving albedo and respecting the specified light direction, and outperforms ChatGPT-4o which fails to follow directional lighting prompts. For normal estimation, PI-Light produces more detailed and physically consistent normals than both DSINE and GeoWizard on real-world inputs.

## Relation to Prior Work

PI-Light extends the two-stage inverse-then-forward neural rendering framework established by RGB↔X (Zeng et al., 2024) and DiffusionRenderer (Liang et al., 2025), but departs from them in three key ways: (1) it adopts Wonder3D's cross-batch self-attention to predict all four intrinsic components simultaneously rather than one at a time, (2) it introduces physics-inspired losses (Lambertian diffuse shading loss and a PBR consistency loss) that regularize the diffusion training dynamics toward physically plausible light transport, and (3) it replaces global HDRI or irradiance conditioning with a front-hemisphere gray-ball lighting representation that isolates controllable illumination from self-luminous scene elements. The initial U-Net weights are inherited from GeoWizard, and the underlying rendering model follows the Disney Principled BRDF.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Full-image relighting with physically consistent material preservation remains an open problem for film production, AR/VR, and content creation, and this paper provides both a practical method and a curated benchmark dataset (Object50/Scene200, Object500/Scene36) that the community can use for downstream evaluation. The physics-inspired loss design—using PBR equations as training regularizers rather than mere evaluation criteria—is a conceptually clean idea that could transfer to other diffusion-based rendering or editing tasks. Key limitations include the inability to modify rear-hemisphere lighting, the absence of explicit IOR/sheen/coat modeling (causing metallic mispredictions on high-IOR dielectrics), and a known latent-RGB spatial misalignment that introduces subtle errors in masked losses. Open questions include whether the physics-inspired regularization scales to more complex multi-bounce or volumetric lighting scenarios, and whether the front-hemisphere restriction can be relaxed without sacrificing the clean separation from self-luminous objects.
