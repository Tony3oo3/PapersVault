## Problem

Existing task-specific data selection methods for LLM fine-tuning (e.g., LESS, TSDS) align source and target distributions using only input features X, ignoring the task-specific label dimension Y. This X-only alignment can select samples that are semantically similar in surface form but irrelevant to the target task's label distribution. The paper reformulates the selection objective as joint distribution alignment on P(X, Y), but faces the practical challenge that true task labels are unavailable in the source corpus.

## Input / Output

The method takes a small target set (20% of test splits from five benchmarks: MMLU, TruthfulQA, GSM8K, BBH, TyDiQA, totaling ~5,500 samples) and a 300K instruction corpus drawn from five public datasets (FlanV2, Open-Assistant, WizardLM, Dolly, Stanford Alpaca). It produces a fixed-size subset (default 10K, also evaluated at 1K and 5K) of instruction instances for LoRA fine-tuning. The intermediate representation is a structured four-field proxy label (Task, Topic, Style, Audience) generated by an LLM, where Task is single-label and the other three are multi-label free-form phrases, enabling open-ended semantic matching without a fixed taxonomy.

## Method

The pipeline has four stages: (1) a pre-trained LLM (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) annotates target instructions with structured proxy labels across four semantic fields; (2) BGE-M3 sentence embeddings of these labels are clustered via k-means (k=100) into semantic anchors, which are then propagated to source samples by cosine-similarity matching (top-1 for Task, top-3 for other fields); (3) an LLM-based scoring step evaluates each source sample's alignment with its assigned anchors, filtering out out-of-distribution samples whose scores fall below a threshold (analogous to confidence-based noisy-label filtering); (4) a greedy incremental sampling algorithm selects the final subset by iteratively picking the sample whose anchor label has the largest positive gap relative to the target's empirical anchor distribution, thereby minimizing L1 distance and mitigating target shift. The key distinction from prior work is that alignment operates on the joint (X, Y) space via proxy labels rather than on X alone, and the LLM reasoning step replaces expensive gradient computation or DNN training for noise detection, yielding a model-agnostic pipeline that runs in ~19 hours versus ~60 hours for gradient-based baselines.

## Results

Selecting only 10K samples from a 300K instruction pool, the proposed align-based method (best variant: Align_topic, min score ≥ 7) achieves 64.5 on MMLU, 46.9 on TruthfulQA, 57.0 on GSM8K, 60.2 on BBH, and 58.6 on TyDiQA. These results are competitive with or superior to state-of-the-art task-specific selection methods (LESS, TSDS) on MMLU, TruthfulQA, and GSM8K, while underperforming on BBH and TyDiQA. The full 300K fine-tuning baseline scores 63.6/44.1/49.0/57.9/66.4 across the same benchmarks, showing that the 10K selected subset matches or exceeds full-data performance on three of five benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LESS (Xia et al., 2024), the proposed method wins on MMLU (64.5 vs. 63.3), TruthfulQA (46.9 vs. 35.1), and GSM8K (57.0 vs. 56.1), but loses on BBH (60.2 vs. 61.1) and TyDiQA (58.6 vs. 64.0). Against TSDS (Liu et al., 2024), the method wins on MMLU (64.5 vs. 63.6), TruthfulQA (46.9 vs. 44.1), and GSM8K (57.0 vs. 50.0), but again trails on BBH (60.2 vs. 62.1) and TyDiQA (58.6 vs. 63.5). Against RDS+ (Ivison et al., 2025), the method dominates on MMLU (64.5 vs. 63.8), TruthfulQA (46.9 vs. 3.6), and GSM8K (57.0 vs. 53.8), while RDS+ edges ahead on BBH (59.4 vs. 60.2) and TyDiQA (60.3 vs. 58.6). The simpler baselines—Completion Length (Zhao et al., 2024) and k-NN (Reimers & Gurevych, 2019)—are consistently outperformed on MMLU, TruthfulQA, and GSM8K, with Completion Length scoring only 7.7 on TruthfulQA and k-NN scoring 61.9 on MMLU. The authors attribute the TyDiQA underperformance to the multilingual nature of that benchmark, which their English-centric label fields (Task, Topic, Style, Audience) do not adequately capture.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in task-specific data selection (LESS, TSDS) that aligns only the marginal input distribution P(X) via embedding or gradient similarity, and instead reformulates the problem as joint distribution alignment P(X, Y). It draws on domain adaptation theory (Zhang et al., 2013) for target-shift mitigation via incremental sampling, and on learning-with-noisy-labels principles (confidence-based filtering) repurposed through LLM scoring rather than trained DNNs. The use of LLM-generated proxy labels echoes InsTag (Lu et al., 2023), but here the labels are explicitly propagated to a source pool and used for distribution matching rather than tag-statistics-based filtering.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reframing from P(X) to P(X,Y) alignment opens a broader solution space for task-specific data selection and provides a principled explanation (via mutual information decomposition) for why input-only methods can select semantically similar but task-irrelevant samples. Practical limitations include underperformance on multilingual benchmarks (TyDiQA) where the generic four-field label schema may be insufficient, and the reliance on LLM annotation quality that, while validated, introduces a non-trivial preprocessing cost (~19h total). Open questions include whether task-specific label taxonomies (rather than the generic Task/Topic/Style/Audience fields) yield further gains, and whether the promising but underexplored multi-domain joint matching (Table 12) can be optimized with learned weights.
