## Problem

Scaling law discovery—the task of finding symbolic formulas that predict model performance at scale—is currently a slow, case-specific, human-driven process where experts propose mathematical models based on intuition. Existing LLM-based coding agents struggle to produce accurate law formulas that generalize to unseen extrapolation regimes, leaving open whether an AI system can autonomously discover scaling laws that are more accurate than their established, human-derived counterparts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262067/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method receives a tabular dataset of scaling experiments in a sandboxed terminal (no network access), where each row contains feature variables (e.g., parameter count, dataset size, learning rate, batch size), a target value (e.g., loss, Brier score), and a control index identifying the experimental setting. The agent must produce a single Python script (law.py) defining one universal symbolic formula whose parameters are fitted separately per control setting; performance is measured by R² on held-out extrapolation points corresponding to larger model or dataset sizes than any seen during evolution.

## Method

SLDAgent is an evolution-based agent that co-optimizes two subroutines: an Expression subroutine that defines the symbolic model f_θ(x) and an Optimization subroutine that fits parameters θ to the training data. At each evolutionary step, an LLM mutates a parent program selected via a probabilistic mixture (70% exploitation of high scorers, 20% diversity, 10% elitism), and the resulting child is executed to compute its R² on the training set before being inserted into a candidate database. To prevent premature convergence, the system employs a five-island multi-island strategy and MAP-Elites, which structures the population by fitness score, expression complexity, and novelty. Built on the OpenEvolve framework, the key design choice is the joint evolution of the law's functional form and its parameter-fitting procedure, rather than treating formula proposal and coefficient optimization as separate stages—this allows the agent to discover laws whose structure is specifically amenable to robust extrapolation.

## Results

On SLDBench (8 scaling law discovery tasks), SLDAgent with GPT-5 achieves an average extrapolation R² of 0.748, outperforming the next-best agent Goose (0.695) and the human expert baseline (0.517). SLDAgent matches or exceeds human performance on every task, with the largest gains on domain_mix (0.988 vs. 0.671) and lr&bsz (0.604 vs. −0.076). The method also consistently boosts the performance of every underlying LLM it is paired with, with average R² gains ranging from +0.100 (Claude-Haiku-4.5) to +0.429 (Gemini-2.5-Flash) over native CLI agents. In the LLM selection application, the SLDAgent-discovered SFT law achieves 100% Relative Accuracy and 87.7% average Pearson correlation across FLAN, Wikitext, and Gigaword, outperforming all five baseline selection methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SLDAgent (GPT-5) outperforms all seven general-purpose coding agents on average R²: Goose (0.695) is the closest competitor but still trails by 0.053, while CodeX (0.550), OpenCode (0.501), Mini-SWE-Agent (0.471), OpenHands (0.317), Aider (0.184), and Terminus-2 (0.168) lag further behind. Against the human expert baseline (0.517), SLDAgent wins on 7 of 8 tasks, with the largest margin on domain_mix (+0.317) and lr&bsz (+0.680), and ties on the easy parallel task. Across provider-specific CLI agents (Gemini-CLI, Claude Code, CodeX), SLDAgent consistently improves average R² by +0.100 to +0.429 depending on the underlying model. In the LLM selection application (Table 4), the SLDAgent-discovered SFT law achieves 100% Relative Accuracy on all three datasets (FLAN, Wikitext, Gigaword), surpassing RectifiedLaw (Lin et al., 2024), NLPMetrics (Yang et al., 2022), SubTuning (Zeng et al., 2025), ModelSize, and ZeroShot baselines, and attains the highest average Pearson correlation of 87.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the OpenEvolve evolutionary coding agent framework and the multi-island/MAP-Elites population management strategy from AlphaEvolve, adapting them to the specific structure of scaling law discovery where a symbolic expression and its parameter-fitting routine must be co-optimized. It departs from prior agentic benchmarks (MLE-Bench, SWE-bench) by targeting open-ended scientific discovery with continuous, extrapolation-based evaluation rather than engineering workflows or closed-form rediscovery, and from symbolic regression literature (AI Feynman, PySR) by requiring a single law to generalize across multiple control settings simultaneously. The scaling law scenarios themselves (Chinchilla, MoE, SFT, domain mixture, etc.) are well-studied in the LLM literature, but no prior work has framed their autonomous discovery as an agentic task with a dedicated benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that an AI agent can discover scaling laws that are consistently more accurate in extrapolation than human-designed counterparts across diverse scenarios, with practical downstream impact shown in hyperparameter optimization (0.067% relative error at 2× scale) and LLM selection (100% relative accuracy). Key limitations include the risk of overfitting to training data without self-verification, the current focus on rediscovering known laws rather than genuinely novel ones, and the open question of whether general-purpose agents can learn to specialize in this way. The work opens a path toward AI systems that autonomously understand and accelerate their own development, but the benchmark's literature-aware nature and limited task count (8) mean the generality of the superhuman claim remains to be tested on truly unexplored scaling regimes.
