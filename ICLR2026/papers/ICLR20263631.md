## Problem

Existing comparative reasoning benchmarks for VLMs (e.g., MLLM-CompBench) focus on images with large, salient differences and are restricted to natural scenes, making them easily solved by state-of-the-art models and unrepresentative of real-world applications. No prior benchmark evaluates whether VLMs can detect subtle differences between highly similar images across diverse specialized domains such as industrial inspection, medical imaging, and aerial surveillance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263631/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance consists of a pair of highly similar RGB images (DINOv3 embedding similarity ≥ 0.8) and a text question, with the model producing either a multiple-choice answer (binary or 4-way depending on difference type) or a free-form difference caption. The 13K test instances span 10 difference types and 6 domains, with MCQ answer counts varying by category (e.g., 4 choices for Attribute and Spatial, 2 for most others). For captioning, 1,200 human-written reference captions are used, evaluated via cosine similarity (Sentence-BERT) and LLM-as-a-judge (GPT-4o binary match).

## Method

VLM-SubtleBench is curated through a hybrid pipeline combining rule-based pairing from existing datasets with ground-truth annotations (MVTEC-AD, COCO, MIMIC-Diff-VQA, LEVIR-MCI, CameraBench, VLM4D, YT8M), human frame-pair selection from video datasets, and model-assisted image editing via Gemini-2.5-flash-image-preview (nano-banana) for controlled color and object modifications. Synthetic primitive scenes (800×600, 2–30 shapes) provide fully controlled settings for ablation studies over difficulty factors like object size, scene complexity, and translation magnitude. The benchmark supports both MCQ and captioning tasks, and the empirical study evaluates 10 VLMs under multiple prompting strategies (chain-of-thought, grid overlay, image concatenation, pixel overlap, subtraction, and bounding-box highlighting), fine-tuning on a 1.3K validation split, and controlled synthetic experiments that isolate specific failure modes.

## Results

On VLM-SubtleBench (13K MCQ pairs across 10 difference types and 6 domains), GPT-5-thinking achieves the highest average accuracy at 77.8%, followed by o3 (75.7%) and GPT-5-main (71.3%). Human evaluators score 95.5% average, leaving a ~18-point gap overall that widens to over 30 points on spatial (59.9% vs. 95.0%), temporal (60.2% vs. 93.0%), and viewpoint (68.5% vs. 98.0%) reasoning. Among open-source models, Qwen2.5-VL-72B leads at 65.4% average, while GPT-4o achieves 61.6%. On the captioning task, GPT-5-thinking scores 0.57 CSS and 43.0% LLM-as-a-judge accuracy, still far below ground-truth captions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the closest prior benchmark MLLM-CompBench (Kil et al., 2024), VLM-SubtleBench demonstrates stronger alignment with real-world tasks: it achieves higher Spearman rank correlation with industrial anomaly detection (MMAD: 0.8424 vs. 0.8110) and aerial surveillance (QAG-360K: 0.7212 vs. 0.7195). Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on VLM-SubtleBench yields +4.6 points on MMAD (69.6 vs. 65.0) and +1.1 on QAG-360K (35.5 vs. 34.4), whereas fine-tuning on MLLM-CompBench gives only +1.3 on MMAD and actually degrades QAG-360K to 32.2. Among evaluated VLMs, GPT-5-thinking (77.8%) outperforms o3 (75.7%) by 2.1 points and GPT-4o (61.6%) by 16.2 points on average accuracy, with the largest margins on spatial (+17.5 over GPT-4o) and temporal (+7.5). The best open-source model, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (65.4%), trails GPT-5-thinking by 12.4 points but narrows the gap to 5.8 points on emotion and 4.1 on existence. All models remain substantially below human performance (95.5%), with the most severe deficits on spatial (35.1-point gap), temporal (32.8-point gap), and viewpoint (29.5-point gap) reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends MLLM-CompBench (Kil et al., 2024), which evaluates comparative reasoning across eight difference types but focuses on salient differences between dissimilar scenes, by shifting the emphasis to *subtle* differences (DINOv3 similarity ≥ 0.8) within nearly identical contexts. It broadens the difference taxonomy from 8 to 10 types (adding Viewpoint and Action) and expands domain coverage from natural images alone to six domains including industrial, aerial, medical, game, and synthetic imagery. The benchmark also complements the difference-captioning line of work (Img-Diff, OneDiff, DiffTell) by pairing captioning with multiple-choice VQA, and draws on domain-specific datasets (MVTEC-AD, MIMIC-Diff-VQA, LEVIR-MCI, CameraBench, VLM4D) that prior comparative-reasoning benchmarks did not use.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a systematic 18-point average gap (and >30-point gaps on spatial, temporal, and viewpoint reasoning) between the best proprietary VLMs and humans, revealing that current models lack robust spatial-temporal representations rather than general visual understanding. Controlled synthetic experiments pinpoint concrete failure modes—scene complexity, object size, and magnitude of change—that give model developers actionable diagnostic targets. Fine-tuning on the validation set narrows but does not close the gap, suggesting that broader data diversity and architectural advances in spatial-temporal reasoning are needed. The demonstrated correlation with downstream industrial anomaly detection and aerial surveillance tasks positions the benchmark as a practical proxy for real-world perceptual reasoning capability.
