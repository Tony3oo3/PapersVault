## Problem

Computing exact Wasserstein barycenters at scale remains a major open challenge: existing primal methods require expensive OT map computations per iteration, while dual methods either enforce costly c-concavity projections (with no convergence guarantee) or introduce a gap between the theoretically analyzable algorithm and the practically feasible one. No prior exact barycenter algorithm is simultaneously constraint-free, globally convergent, and computationally efficient.

## Input / Output

The method takes m probability densities discretized on a regular grid within a compact convex domain (e.g., 1024×1024 for 2D, 200×200×200 for 3D) along with positive weights α₁,…,αₘ summing to 1, and produces the unique Wasserstein barycenter density. The per-iteration cost is O(m × n log n) where n is the total grid size, achieved via FFT-based Poisson solves and fast c-transforms. The approach is currently limited to 2D and 3D grids due to exponential grid-size scaling in dimension; extending to non-uniform grids would require replacing FFT with multigrid solvers.

## Method

The paper introduces a constraint-free concave dual formulation of the barycenter problem (Problem 6) in which the maximization is over continuous functions without any c-concavity constraint. The key insight is that the first variation of the Kantorovich dual functional exists even for non-c-concave functions, and the resulting H̃¹-gradient depends only on the Brenier map T_{f^c}, which is invariant under the double c-transform—making the projection step unnecessary. The SGA algorithm performs simple gradient ascent in the H̃¹ geometry, where each gradient is computed by solving a Neumann Poisson equation via FFT in O(n log n) time. The main theoretical result (Theorem 6) establishes a global convergence rate of O(1/√T) with constant step size (or O(log T/√T) with annealing), matching the classical subgradient rate for nonsmooth convex minimization in Euclidean space, without any projection or constraint enforcement.

## Results

On the 2D synthetic distribution (1024×1024 grid, 4 marginals), SGA achieves the lowest barycenter functional value across all 12 weight configurations (e.g., 3.917×10³ vs. WDHA 3.940×10³, CWB 4.202×10³, DSB 3.926×10³ for weights (2/3,0,0,1/3)), while running in ~298 s—roughly half the time of WDHA (625 s), one-third of CWB (1346 s), and one-seventh of DSB (3643 s). On high-resolution handwritten digits (500×500, 10 images of digit '2'), SGA attains a barycenter value of 5.992×10⁻³ in 406 s, outperforming WDHA (6.049×10⁻³, 415 s), DSB (6.021×10⁻³, 670 s), and CWB (6.463×10⁻³, 430 s). In the 3D ball-to-cube interpolation (200×200×200), SGA converges stably while WDHA diverges under the same annealing step size.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGA consistently outperforms all three baselines on the barycenter functional value (lower is better) across every tested weight configuration in the 2D synthetic experiment, with margins of 0.001–0.285×10³ over WDHA, 0.23–0.33×10³ over CWB, and 0.001–0.003×10³ over DSB. In terms of runtime, SGA is the fastest method by a wide margin: approximately 2× faster than WDHA, 3–4× faster than CWB, and 7–8× faster than DSB. On the handwritten digits benchmark, SGA and SGA-AdaGrad tie for the best barycenter value (5.992×10⁻³) while being the fastest (406–409 s), beating WDHA (6.049×10⁻³), DSB (6.021×10⁻³), and CWB (6.463×10⁻³). A notable practical advantage is numerical stability: in the 3D interpolation experiment, WDHA diverges under the annealing step size η_t = 5×10⁻³/√t while SGA converges, which the authors attribute to SGA's simpler constraint-free structure and its global convergence guarantee. SGA also achieves smaller relative L² errors in the learned optimal transport maps for the Gaussian barycenter problem (e.g., 6.662×10⁻² vs. 6.732×10⁻² for T₁) compared to WDHA.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the H¹-gradient ascent algorithm of Jacobs & Léger (2020) for two-marginal optimal transport, but makes a key conceptual departure: it removes the c-concavity (double c-transform) projection step that all prior dual methods enforce, showing it is unnecessary for convergence when the ascent is performed in the Sobolev geometry. For the barycenter problem specifically, it departs from the MOT-based dual of Zhou & Parno (2024) (which introduces O(m²) constraints) and the nonconvex-concave minimax WDHA of Kim et al. (2025) (which requires an expensive O(n²) projection per iteration) by formulating a fully unconstrained concave dual over m−1 potentials, yielding a simpler O(m·n log n) per-iteration algorithm. The entropic regularized methods (CWB, DSB) serve as approximate baselines rather than direct methodological ancestors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that c-concavity projection is dispensable for convergence in the Sobolev setting is a clean theoretical simplification that removes the most computationally expensive and theoretically awkward component of all existing exact barycenter solvers, while matching the classical O(1/√T) subgradient rate. Practically, SGA is roughly 2× faster than the closest exact competitor (WDHA) and more numerically stable (WDHA diverges in the 3D interpolation experiment). The main limitation is the restriction to 2D/3D grid-discretized distributions on compact domains; extending to higher dimensions or non-uniform grids would require replacing FFT-based Poisson solvers with multigrid methods, substantially increasing cost. An open question is whether the constraint-free structure can be exploited for accelerated (e.g., Nesterov-type) convergence beyond the standard subgradient rate.
