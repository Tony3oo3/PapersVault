## Problem

Dynamic 4D reconstruction via deformation-field-driven 3D Gaussian Splatting suffers from pronounced motion blur under novel viewpoints due to a fundamental spectral conflict: the fixed opacity profile of standard Gaussians forces the deformation network to simultaneously recover high-frequency appearance (via dense Gaussian stacking) and model high-frequency motion (via coherent multi-frame trajectories), biasing it toward smooth low-frequency motion as a compromise.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263958/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view video sequences with known or COLMAP-estimated camera poses and per-frame timestamps, operating at resolutions ranging from 536×960 (HyperNeRF) to 1352×1024 (Neu3D). It produces a canonical 4D Gaussian scene representation (initialized from SfM points) that is deformed over time to render novel views in real time. Unlike per-frame iterative methods, a single shared canonical set of Gaussians is maintained, with temporal variation encoded entirely through the deformation network and per-Gaussian frequency parameters.

## Method

FAGS introduces two complementary mechanisms to decouple high-frequency detail from high-frequency motion. First, the Frequency-Differentiated Gaussian Kernel replaces the fixed Gaussian alpha profile with a learnable piecewise-linear modulation ψ(g) parameterized by λ (controlling kernel sharpness: λ<0.5 yields smooth low-frequency kernels, λ>0.5 yields sharp high-frequency kernels) and β (controlling the spatial span of the differentiated region), allowing each Gaussian to specialize independently. Second, the Fourier-Deformation Network decomposes 4D coordinates into spatial and temporal hash encodings, augments them with per-Gaussian high-frequency Fourier embeddings (sin/cos at geometrically spaced frequencies with amplitudes predicted from spatial features), and applies a frequency-aware gate η that adaptively scales each Gaussian's deformation magnitude—amplifying genuine high-frequency motion while suppressing spurious updates on near-static points. Training combines L1, D-SSIM, smoothness regularization, and a novel Fourier frequency loss (L1 on FFT amplitude spectra) to explicitly regularize the spectral domain.

## Results

On the synthetic D-NeRF benchmark (8 scenes), FAGS achieves an average PSNR of 42.76 dB, SSIM of 0.995, and LPIPS of 0.007, outperforming the strongest baseline Grid4D (41.99 / 0.993 / 0.008) by +0.77 dB in PSNR. On the real-world Neu3D dataset, FAGS reaches 32.18 dB PSNR and 0.946 SSIM on average, surpassing Grid4D (31.63 / 0.937) and 4D-GS (30.65 / 0.934). On HyperNeRF, FAGS improves over Grid4D by +0.43 dB PSNR on interpolation scenes and +0.39 dB on rig scenes, with a notable LPIPS reduction from 0.319 to 0.269 on the rig subset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAGS outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the closest competitor Grid4D (Xu et al., 2024), FAGS gains +0.77 dB PSNR on D-NeRF, +0.55 dB on Neu3D, and +0.43 dB on HyperNeRF interpolation, while also reducing LPIPS consistently. SC-GS (Huang et al., 2024) is the second-strongest baseline at 41.59 dB on D-NeRF but trails FAGS by 1.17 dB; its sparse control points help with editability but do not address the spectral conflict. DeformGS (Yang et al., 2024) and 4D-GS (Wu et al., 2024) both score around 40 dB on D-NeRF, roughly 2.7 dB below FAGS, reflecting their reliance on uniform deformation without frequency-aware gating. The NeRF-based baselines—TiNeuVox (Fang et al., 2022), HexPlane (Cao et al., 2023), and Tensor-4D (Shao et al., 2023)—lag further behind, with Tensor-4D achieving only 31.92 dB on D-NeRF, underscoring the advantage of explicit Gaussian representations for dynamic scenes. Notably, adding DRK kernels to Grid4D actually degrades performance (39.43 dB vs. 41.99 dB), which the authors attribute to excessive kernel flexibility destabilizing deformation control, whereas FAGS's frequency-differentiated kernels maintain stable differentiation throughout training.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the 3D Gaussian Splatting rendering formulation (Kerbl et al., 2023) and the Grid4D deformation backbone (Xu et al., 2024), which uses 4D decomposed hash encoding to predict per-Gaussian temporal offsets. It departs from the prevailing line of dynamic Gaussian splatting work (4D-GS, DeformGS, SC-GS) that focuses on refining deformation fields or canonical representations, by reframing motion blur as a spectral conflict between high-frequency appearance and high-frequency motion. The Frequency-Differentiated Gaussian Kernel extends the alpha-blending mechanism with a learnable piecewise modulation (parameters λ and β), going beyond the fixed Gaussian opacity profile and also beyond DRK (Huang et al., 2025), which couples slope and boundary implicitly. The Fourier-Deformation Network adds high-frequency Fourier embeddings and a frequency-aware gate on top of the standard hash-encoded deformation MLP, explicitly separating periodic high-frequency motion from low-frequency spatial trends.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The frequency-decomposition perspective offers a principled explanation for why existing dynamic Gaussian splatting methods blur under novel views, and the proposed kernel differentiation plus Fourier gating provide a modular fix that could be grafted onto other deformation-based splatting pipelines. However, the method still depends on Grid4D as its deformation backbone and is validated primarily on small-scale datasets (D-NeRF, Neu3D, HyperNeRF), leaving open questions about scalability to large, non-periodic, or multi-object scenes. The finding that DRK's additional kernel flexibility actually degrades performance when paired with a deformation network is a useful cautionary result, suggesting that unconstrained representational freedom can destabilize temporal optimization. Future work could explore whether the frequency-aware gating generalizes to non-repeating motions or whether the Fourier basis needs to be extended beyond the fixed geometric frequency schedule used here.
