## Problem

Producing fine-grained families of LLMs spanning many intermediate sizes is prohibitively expensive under standard pipelines, where each size requires independent pretraining or a full distillation run. Existing model families therefore offer only a few coarse-grained size options, leaving large gaps in the efficiency–capability trade-off space. This paper asks whether a single distillation run can yield an entire spectrum of intermediate models without any additional training.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained teacher transformer (e.g., Qwen3-4B-Base with N layers), a text corpus for distillation (the deduplicated Pile, 2.1B tokens), and a student architecture with M < N layers whose hidden dimension matches the teacher's. It produces a deterministic set of M+K-layer interpolated models for K = 0, …, N−M by swapping in contiguous blocks of teacher layers. A critical constraint is that the student must be initialized from teacher weights via layer pruning (not random initialization) and must share the teacher's hidden-state dimension, since patching requires direct weight substitution without any adapter or projection.

## Method

Boomerang distillation proceeds in three stages. First, the teacher's N layers are partitioned into M contiguous blocks, and the student is initialized by taking the first layer of each block plus the shared embedding and LM head. Second, the student is trained with a composite objective combining cross-entropy, KL-divergence distillation loss, and a per-layer cosine-distance alignment loss that forces each student layer's hidden states to approximate the output of its corresponding teacher block. Third, after training, intermediate models are constructed zero-shot by sequentially replacing student layers with their full teacher blocks (in a model-specific order, e.g., from the last layer backward for Qwen or from the first layer forward for Llama), yielding M+K-layer models for every K without further optimization. The key insight is that the alignment loss makes each student layer a functional proxy for its teacher block, so re-inserting the original block restores the teacher's computation at that position and produces a valid, smoothly interpolating model.

## Results

Boomerang distillation produces zero-shot interpolated models whose classification and generation accuracy smoothly interpolate between the distilled student (2.7B inference params) and the Qwen3-4B-Base teacher (4.4B inference params), with no additional training required. At larger intermediate sizes, interpolated models outperform standard distilled models of the same size, likely because distilling on The Pile causes catastrophic forgetting of the teacher's higher-quality pretraining. Compared to individually distilling each intermediate model, boomerang distillation requires 14.53×–19.17× fewer FLOPs (e.g., 4.31e19 vs. 8.27e20 FLOPs for Qwen3-4B-Base). The phenomenon generalizes across model families (Qwen3-8B, Pythia-6.9B, Llama-3.2-3B) and even to off-the-shelf models like DistilBERT/BERT and DistilGPT2/GPT2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against naive layer pruning, boomerang distillation shows a dramatic advantage: pruning causes a significant performance drop below 4B parameters, while interpolated models maintain smooth accuracy scaling. Against the two layer-pruning baselines LaCo and ShortGPT, boomerang distillation significantly outperforms both across all intermediate sizes, with the gap most pronounced on generation tasks where pruning methods collapse to near-zero exact-match accuracy after just a few layers removed, whereas boomerang distillation sustains meaningful generation performance even at small model sizes. Against standard knowledge distillation (training each intermediate size independently with the same loss), interpolated models achieve comparable performance at small sizes but outperform them at larger sizes, attributed to catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuning the teacher on the lower-quality Pile corpus. Against pretrained reference models (Pythia-2.8B, Llama-3.2-3B), the interpolated models achieve comparable accuracy despite being trained on only 2.1B tokens versus the far larger pretraining budgets of those models. The one setting where boomerang distillation does not clearly win is Pythia-2.8B, where standard distilled models slightly outperform interpolated models because Pythia was originally trained on The Pile itself, eliminating the catastrophic forgetting effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on standard knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) for the KL divergence loss, DistilBERT (Sanh et al., 2019) for the per-layer cosine alignment loss, and layer-dropping initialization (Chen et al., 2025) for student construction. It departs from prior model-family construction (Gemma, Llama, Pythia) which trains each size independently, and from model interpolation work (Wortsman et al., 2022; Ilharco et al., 2023) which interpolates weights of same-size models. The key conceptual departure is the 'boomerang' step: after distilling down, re-inserting contiguous blocks of teacher layers into the distilled student to create intermediate models with zero additional training, which the authors frame as a previously unobserved phenomenon.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14–19× FLOPS reduction for constructing a full model family makes fine-grained size interpolation practically accessible, potentially changing how labs release model families. Key limitations include the requirement for layer-pruning initialization (incompatible with width or attention-head pruning), the need to retain teacher weights in memory, and the dependence on non-trivial student performance. Open questions include whether the phenomenon extends to other modalities (vision, audio), whether it can work without retaining teacher weights, and whether similarity-guided initialization and patching order can further stabilize interpolation at extreme compression ratios.
