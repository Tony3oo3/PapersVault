## Problem

Existing synthetic EMR generation methods imitate real records without enforcing clinical quality principles, risking propagation of errors and biased distributions into downstream applications. While LLMs offer strong text generation, direct prompting yields unrealistic demographic patterns and limited coverage of diverse clinical scenarios. The paper addresses how to generate synthetic EMRs that satisfy multi-level quality standards (distributional alignment, section completeness, document consistency) without accessing any real EMR text.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263582/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a primary diagnosis label and a specification of required EMR fields (chief complaint, history of present illness, hospital course, discharge instructions, plus demographics) as input, and produces a full multi-section synthetic EMR in JSON format. At the corpus level, aggregate reference statistics (age/gender distributions, clinical concept frequencies) derived from the training set guide distributional alignment, but no individual real EMR text is used. The generator samples multiple initial drafts per prompt to form a draft pool before refinement begins.

## Method

LLM-CARe performs stage-wise cyclic refinement across three granularities—corpus, section, and document—where each stage runs a Critic-Adviser-Reviser loop for two iterations. The corpus stage uses statistical tools (not LLMs) as critic and adviser to detect distributional deviations in demographics and knowledge coverage, then an LLM reviser modifies selected records to match reference distributions. The section stage applies an LLM critic to check each field against clinical-guideline-derived completeness criteria, with the adviser specifying exactly which elements to add and the reviser incorporating them. The document stage enforces medical correctness and cross-section consistency, with the adviser prioritizing edits to the less influential section to minimize disruption. The staged ordering (corpus → section → document) proceeds from soft distributional constraints to strict logical consistency, preventing later-stage corrections from undoing earlier alignment. The entire pipeline uses Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as the backbone and requires no real EMR text for training or prompting.

## Results

On a 192k-record EMR dataset spanning 302 diseases, LLM-CARe achieves the highest quality scores across all five principles: Content Completeness 91.2%, Medical Correctness 98.6%, Context Consistency 93.8%, Demographic Typicality 96.8%, and Knowledge Coverage 94.1%. On downstream tasks, LLM-CARe attains 82.6% micro accuracy on diagnosis prediction, 85.3% on examination recommendation, and 76.9% on treatment recommendation, outperforming all baselines. Notably, LLM-CARe requires no real EMR text during generation, while most baselines rely on real records for training or in-context prompting.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLM-CARe outperforms all four baselines on every quality metric and downstream task. Against the strongest LLM baseline MedSyn (which uses real EMRs as in-context examples), LLM-CARe gains +6.4 points on Content Completeness (91.2 vs. 84.8), +3.3 on Context Consistency (93.8 vs. 91.9), +12.7 on Demographic Typicality (96.8 vs. 84.1), and +9.6 on Knowledge Coverage (94.1 vs. 84.5). Against Self-Refine, which performs joint all-in-one self-critique, LLM-CARe leads by +12.9 on Content Completeness and +16.1 on Knowledge Coverage, showing that decomposed stage-wise refinement substantially outperforms undifferentiated self-correction. The non-LLM baselines (LSTM, mtGAN) are far behind on document-level criteria (Context Consistency ~21–22% vs. 93.8%) but surprisingly competitive on Demographic Typicality (~93%), likely because their training on real data preserves marginal distributions. On downstream tasks, the gap widens for examination and treatment recommendation: LLM-CARe achieves 85.3% micro accuracy on examination recommendation vs. 82.9% for MedSyn and only 64.4% for LLM Direct, reflecting the importance of broad clinical concept coverage that only the corpus-level refinement stage provides.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in synthetic EMR generation—whether GAN-based, auto-regressive, or LLM-based—where the primary objective is distributional mimicry of real records, without explicit enforcement of clinical quality principles. Conceptually, it repurposes the multi-agent LLM interaction pattern (Critic-Adviser-Reviser) from general-purpose self-refinement (Madaan et al., 2023) into a stage-wise, principle-guided pipeline that decomposes quality into corpus-level, section-level, and document-level criteria. Unlike Self-Refine, which applies a single all-in-one critique loop, LLM-CARe assigns each agent a stage-specific role and iterates within that stage before progressing, drawing on the broader multi-agent LLM literature but grounding it in concrete clinical documentation standards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that high-quality synthetic EMRs can be generated without any access to real EMR text during training or prompting, addressing a key privacy bottleneck in healthcare AI. The finding that MedSyn—despite using real EMRs as in-context exemplars—can underperform direct LLM generation on certain completeness criteria highlights a subtle risk of imitation-based approaches that the field has not fully addressed. Open questions include whether the staged ordering (corpus → section → document) is optimal or could be parallelized, whether the framework generalizes to other structured clinical documents (e.g., radiology reports, pathology notes), and how the approach scales to multi-lingual or multi-institutional EMR formats with different documentation conventions.
