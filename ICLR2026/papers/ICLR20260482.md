## Problem

Softmax parameterization in policy gradient methods induces flat optimization landscapes that provably lead to exponential convergence rates, and the standard entropy regularizer does not fundamentally resolve this ill-conditioning. The paper asks whether replacing softmax with a broader family of parameterizations, coupled with a matching regularizer, can yield polynomial rather than exponential convergence guarantees for stochastic policy gradient without preconditioning.

## Input / Output

The method operates on finite MDPs (state space S, action space A, transition kernel P, bounded reward r, initial distribution ρ) parameterized by a divergence generator f (KL, Tsallis-α for 0<α<1, or Jensen-Shannon), a full-support reference policy π_ref, and a temperature λ. The critical design constraint is that the same f induces both the policy parameterization (f-softargmax) and the regularizer (f-divergence), forming a coupled pair. The output consists of policy parameters θ ∈ R^{|S||A|}, value function estimates, and fully explicit non-asymptotic last-iterate convergence rates with constants depending only on problem parameters (γ, ρ_min, π_ref, f-specific constants).

## Method

The paper introduces f-softargmax parameterizations where the policy at each state is defined as argmax_ν {⟨ν, x⟩ − D^f(ν‖π_ref)}, generalizing softmax (the KL case) to a flexible family including Tsallis and Jensen-Shannon variants. The key insight is coupling this parameterization with the matching f-divergence regularizer, which causes the f-regularized value function to satisfy a non-uniform Lojasiewicz inequality whose coefficient depends on the current policy. A monotonicity property combined with a projection-like operator U_τ that prevents policies from becoming too deterministic upgrades this to a uniform Polyak-Lojasiewicz inequality on a restricted, easily-projectable region. This structure yields the first explicit non-asymptotic last-iterate convergence guarantees for stochastic tabular policy gradient without any preconditioning. For Tsallis divergences (0 < α < 1), the PL constant scales polynomially in problem parameters, giving polynomial sample complexity for the unregularized objective in contrast to the exponential complexity of the softmax-entropy pairing, with an optimal α* ≈ 11/(2 log(1/ε)) depending on target precision.

## Results

The headline theoretical result is a reduction in sample complexity from exponential to polynomial for the unregularized objective: softmax-entropy requires TBH ≲ exp(|log π_ref| / (ε(1−γ)³ρ_min)) samples (Corollary 4.6), while α-Tsallis-SoftArgmax with coupled α-Tsallis regularization requires only TBH ≲ poly(1/ε, 1/(1−γ), 1/ρ_min, 1/π_ref) samples (Corollary 4.7). The optimal Tsallis parameter is α*(ε) = 11/(2 log(1/ε)) + o(1/log(1/ε)), yielding an ε^{-12} poly(1/(1−γ), 1/ρ_min, 1/π_ref) sample complexity. Empirically, α-Tsallis PPO consistently outperforms standard PPO on Noisy CartPole (with the gap widening as reward noise σ² increases from 0.5 to 10.0) and on DeepSea (where α=0.7 achieves the highest returns and fastest learning as grid size L grows from 20 to 50).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's f-SoftArgmax with coupled Tsallis regularization (Corollary 4.7) is the only method in Table 1 that simultaneously achieves stochastic gradients, last-iterate convergence, and explicit polynomial rates without preconditioning — a combination no prior method provides. Compared to softmax-entropy (the paper's own Corollary 4.6 baseline), the Tsallis coupling reduces the dependence on (1−γ)^{-1} from exponential to polynomial, an exponential improvement in sample complexity. Against Zhang et al. (2021) (log-barrier regularization), the proposed method adds last-iterate guarantees and avoids the practical instability of log-barrier. Against Mei et al. (2020a) (escort transform) and Liu et al. (2025) (Hadamard), the f-SoftArgmax framework extends guarantees to the stochastic setting with fully explicit constants. Empirically, α-Tsallis PPO beats standard PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) on both Noisy CartPole and DeepSea: on Noisy CartPole the advantage grows with reward noise (σ² = 0.5 → 10.0), and on DeepSea the best α=0.7 achieves the highest returns and fastest convergence as grid size increases from L=20 to L=50.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on alternative policy parameterizations beyond softmax (Mei et al. 2020a's escort transform, Liu et al. 2025's Hadamard parameterization) by proposing a unified family of f-softargmax parameterizations induced by arbitrary f-divergence generators, drawing on the f-divergence operator framework developed for supervised learning (Blondel et al. 2020; Roulet et al. 2025). The key conceptual departure is the *coupling* principle: the same divergence generator f induces both the parameterization and the regularizer, generalizing the classical softmax–entropy pairing. This coupling is what enables a Polyak-Lojasiewicz inequality on the regularized value, a property that prior work (Mei et al. 2020b; Li et al. 2023) could only establish in the specific KL/entropy case using log-specific algebraic tricks that do not generalize.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first explicit non-asymptotic last-iterate convergence guarantees for stochastic tabular policy gradient without any preconditioning, and shows that Tsallis-type couplings reduce sample complexity from exponential to polynomial in 1/(1−γ), a fundamental improvement over the softmax-entropy baseline. The framework is flexible: the optimal Tsallis parameter α depends on the target precision (Corollary 4.8), suggesting problem-adaptive regularization. Key limitations include the restriction to finite (tabular) MDPs and validation only on simple environments (CartPole, DeepSea); extending these guarantees to function approximation, continuous action spaces, or adversarial settings remains open and is flagged as future work.
