## Problem

Large language models achieve strong reasoning performance but at prohibitive computational cost, while existing remedies—CoT compression sacrifices interpretability and standard knowledge distillation is bottlenecked by the small model's limited parameter capacity. The paper identifies the root cause as the tight coupling of high-level cognitive planning (devising a solution strategy) and low-level linguistic realization (generating step-by-step text) within a single autoregressive model, and asks whether these two functions can be decoupled across a large-small model pair to achieve both high reasoning fidelity and low inference cost.

## Input / Output

The system takes a text question as input. The large model (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) performs a single non-autoregressive forward pass over the question augmented with K special thought tokens (default K=5) to produce K latent guidance vectors in its hidden space. A 2-layer MLP projection (intermediate dim 2048, GELU, dropout 0.1, LayerNorm) maps these vectors into the small model's embedding space. The small model then autoregressively generates a concise step-by-step reasoning chain followed by the final answer, conditioned on both the original question and the projected latent guidance. The output is thus a human-readable, interpretable reasoning trace rather than a compressed or latent-only representation.

## Method

The framework implements Cognitive Distillation through a two-stage training procedure. In Stage 1, the large model (Implicit Thinker) is trained with a dual-loss objective: a standard task loss on the final answer ensures the cognitive plan is correct, while a reconstruction loss forces the hidden states at K thought-token positions to be sufficient to fully regenerate the original reasoning chain—formally lower-bounding the mutual information I(R; H|Q) via an information-theoretic argument. In Stage 2, the small model (Explicit Executor) is fine-tuned with a standard language-modeling loss to produce the full reasoning chain and answer conditioned on the question and the projected latent guidance, effectively learning only linguistic realization of a pre-computed plan. At inference, the large model's single forward pass (no token generation) produces the latent guidance, and the small model handles all autoregressive text generation, decoupling expensive planning from cheap execution. The reconstruction loss is the critical design element: ablation shows removing it degrades GSM8K accuracy by 2.3 points, and t-SNE visualizations plus probing experiments confirm the latent vectors organize into semantically distinct clusters corresponding to different high-level reasoning strategies.

## Results

Across 8 reasoning benchmarks and model scales from 0.5B to 8B, Latent Guidance consistently outperforms all distillation baselines. On LLaMA-3-8B, the method achieves 73.1% overall average accuracy (+2.8pp over the strongest baseline NesyCD at 70.3%), with a particularly strong OOD average of 71.9% vs. 68.1% for NesyCD. On Qwen2-7B, the OOD advantage widens to +3.6pp (78.5% vs. 74.9%). Using Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as the executor, the method also produces substantially more concise reasoning chains (e.g., 128.9 tokens on GSM8K vs. 235.4 for SFT) while improving accuracy, and achieves up to +7.2% over KD on the challenging Odyssey-Math benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Latent Guidance outperforms all six distillation baselines across every model scale tested. Against the strongest competitor NesyCD (Liao et al., AAAI 2025), the method gains +2.8pp overall on LLaMA-3-8B (73.1% vs. 70.3%) and +2.6pp on Qwen2-7B (79.0% vs. 76.4%), with the gap widening on OOD benchmarks (+3.8pp and +3.6pp respectively). KARD (Kang et al., NeurIPS 2023) is the second-strongest baseline but trails by 2.9pp on LLaMA-3-8B and 2.9pp on Qwen2-7B. Std-CoT (Magister et al., ACL 2023) is competitive on in-domain tasks but lags significantly on OOD generalization (e.g., 65.5% vs. 71.9% OOD on LLaMA-3-8B). MT-CoT (Li et al., 2024) and Step-by-step (Hsieh et al., 2023) perform poorly on GSM8K (13.1% and 11.5% on LLaMA-3-8B), suggesting their distillation objectives fail to transfer mathematical reasoning effectively. CasCoD (Dai et al., 2024) is consistently the weakest baseline, with overall averages 20–35pp below the proposed method, indicating that cascaded decomposition without explicit cognitive planning is insufficient for complex reasoning transfer.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of two established research lines: CoT knowledge distillation (Std-CoT, MT-CoT, KARD, NesyCD) and latent-space reasoning (COCONUT, LightThinker, pause tokens). It departs from prior distillation work by rejecting 'outcome distillation' (imitating the teacher's final text) in favor of 'cognitive distillation' (transferring the high-level solution strategy via compact latent vectors), and from prior latent-reasoning work by repurposing latent computation not to accelerate a single large model but to bridge a large-to-small model gap. The reconstruction loss, which forces latent thought tokens to encode the full reasoning chain, is a novel mechanism that has no direct analogue in either lineage, and the information-theoretic analysis (Fano bounds, rate-distortion, mutual information lower bounds) provides a formal justification absent from prior empirical distillation studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a principled way to close the reasoning gap between small and large models while keeping inference costs low, with the information-theoretic grounding (MI ≈ 3.1 nats out of ~3.47 nats total entropy, ~89% information capture) giving practitioners a quantitative handle on how much planning information is actually transferred. A key limitation is that the large model must still be available at inference time for a single forward pass, so the approach is a collaborative inference system rather than a fully standalone small model; this constrains its applicability in settings where the teacher model is unavailable. Open questions include whether the latent guidance can be further compressed or cached for repeated queries, how the method behaves when the teacher-student capacity gap is extreme, and whether the emergent strategy clustering generalizes beyond mathematical reasoning to open-ended generation tasks.
