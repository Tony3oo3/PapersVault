## Problem

Chain-of-thought reasoning in LLMs is inherently stochastic, and resampling a single intermediate step can flip the final outcome, yet the specific functional transitions that cause such failures remain uncharacterized. Existing mitigation strategies (self-consistency, path search) treat the model as a black box and rely on expensive sampling without probing internal reasoning dynamics. This paper asks: what are the precise step-to-step functional shifts that systematically derail reasoning, and can they be corrected with a lightweight, targeted intervention?

## Input / Output

During inference, the method consumes the current token's activation vector from a selected transformer layer, the functional category label of the preceding step (predicted by a DistilBERT classifier over 9 categories), and two pre-computed artifacts: a 9×8 library of steering vectors and paired functional prototypes (salutary and pathological mean activations per transition). At each token step it outputs a modified activation (original activation plus a weighted correction vector) that is fed back into the model's forward pass, ultimately producing a corrected reasoning chain. The intervention is applied per-token rather than per-sentence, making it fine-grained enough to catch drift as it emerges mid-generation.

## Method

The paper first diagnoses Reasoning Drift by analyzing the Math-Rollout dataset: it constructs a difference matrix (drift transitions minus corrective transitions) over 9 functional categories, revealing a universal 'funneling effect' (early steps are highly plastic) plus model-specific signatures—Llama exhibits Inertial Drift (self-loops) while Qwen exhibits Chaotic Drift (non-linear backtracking). To mitigate drift, the authors propose Reasoning-Aware Activation Steering (RAAS), which pre-computes up to 72 fine-grained steering vectors by averaging activation differences between preferred and dispreferred steps within each (source→target) functional transition pair. At inference time, a Real-time Drift Score is computed per token via cosine similarity to pathological versus salutary functional prototypes; only when the token is closer to the pathological prototype does a non-zero score activate. The final correction vector is a drift-score-weighted sum of all steering vectors from the current source category, added directly to the token activation at a single selected layer (identified via activation-patching analysis), requiring no retraining or additional sampling.

## Results

On R1-Distill-Llama-8B, RAAS achieves 87.56% on GSM8K, 55.56% on AIME2024, 31.70% on AIME2025, and 55.52% on GPQA-Diamond, outperforming the reproduced vanilla baseline (82.45/34.99/25.56/50.50) by up to +20.6 points on AIME2024. On R1-Distill-Qwen-14B, the method reaches 95.60% GSM8K, 62.49% AIME2024, 36.67% AIME2025, and 57.57% GPQA-Diamond, with the largest gains on AIME2024 (+8.05) and AIME2025 (+10.00) over vanilla. Cross-domain evaluation on non-math benchmarks (ReClor, LogiQA) shows consistent improvements of 1.5–2.6 points, confirming the vectors capture domain-agnostic reasoning principles rather than math-specific heuristics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Self-Consistency (SC), RAAS wins on every benchmark for both models: on Llama it improves AIME2024 from 38.89% to 55.56% (+16.67) and GPQA-Diamond from 42.10% to 55.52% (+13.42), while on Qwen it lifts AIME2024 from 54.44% (vanilla, since SC was not separately reported) to 62.49%. Against PiCSAR (path-selection), RAAS surpasses it on AIME2024 (55.56% vs. 47.78%, +7.78) and GPQA-Diamond (55.52% vs. 47.31%, +8.21) for Llama, though PiCSAR retains a narrow edge on GSM8K (76.42% vs. 87.56%—actually RAAS wins here too by +11.14) and AIME2025 (33.33% vs. 31.70%, −1.63). The single-vector CAA_um ablation, which applies only the uncertainty-management steering vector, improves over vanilla on GSM8K and GPQA-Diamond but underperforms RAAS on AIME2024 by 10.56 points (Llama) and 9.16 points (Qwen), confirming that the full 72-vector library with context-aware selection is essential for the largest gains on complex multi-step problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the activation steering paradigm (Rimsky et al., 2024; Turner et al., 2023) from single-behavior interventions (e.g., refusal direction, truthfulness) to the multi-step, sequential nature of chain-of-thought reasoning. It builds on the Math-Rollout dataset (Bogdan et al., 2025), which provided the counterfactual rollout infrastructure, but departs from prior work by introducing a fine-grained functional taxonomy of reasoning steps and a library of 72 transition-specific steering vectors rather than a single global vector. The paper explicitly positions itself against sampling-based methods (self-consistency, PiCSAR, Tree-of-Thoughts) as being computationally expensive and mechanistically opaque, offering instead a targeted, interpretable inference-time intervention that probes internal activations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of model-specific drift signatures (Inertial Drift in Llama vs. Chaotic Drift in Qwen) provides a diagnostic framework for understanding *why* reasoning fails, not just that it does, which is a step toward more principled reliability interventions. The method's transfer from math-derived vectors to non-mathematical reasoning (ReClor, LogiQA) suggests the captured drift patterns encode domain-agnostic structural principles of logical reasoning. Key limitations include the first-order (single-transition) modeling that may miss higher-order failure sequences, and the restriction to open-weight Llama/Qwen families, leaving closed-source models unexplored. Whether the functional taxonomy and drift signatures generalize across architectures and training regimes remains an open question.
