## Problem

Existing open-source VLMs that use image-based tools with reinforcement learning for visual search exhibit monotonous reasoning patterns and limited interaction turns (e.g., DeepEyes averages only ~1 tool-use turn per example on HR-Bench-4K), making them inadequate for challenging tasks that require trial-and-error exploration, depth-first search, and self-reflection over tens of tool-interaction rounds.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262593/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a high-resolution image (with a reduced max-pixel budget of 2M to fit more turns within a 32K token context) and a text question. At each turn it produces a thought and an action—either a grounding action specifying a normalized bounding box [x1,y1,x2,y2] in [0,1]² on either the original image or a prior observation, or a final text answer. The environment returns the cropped image patch as the next observation, and the loop terminates on a final answer or when the turn/context budget is exhausted. At inference, the turn limit can be raised to 32 or more, with accuracy improving monotonically as more turns are permitted.

## Method

Mini-o3 (built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) is trained in two phases. First, cold-start SFT on ~6,000 diverse multi-turn trajectories generated by prompting an existing VLM to imitate six hand-crafted exemplars covering depth-first search, trial-and-error, and goal-maintenance strategies; only trajectories with correct final answers are retained. Second, RL with GRPO (following DAPO's clip-higher and dynamic sampling) on the VisualProbe dataset (4,000 hard/medium/easy visual search pairs featuring small targets, distractors, and high resolution) plus 4,000 samples from DeepEyes-47k. The key technical contribution is an over-turn masking strategy: a binary completion mask M_i is applied to the GRPO advantage so that trajectories hitting the 6-turn training cap or the 32K context limit contribute zero gradient, preventing the model from being penalized for exploring longer and instead enabling test-time turn scaling to tens of rounds with monotonically improving accuracy.

## Results

Mini-o3 achieves state-of-the-art performance across all visual search benchmarks. On the newly introduced VisualProbe-Hard, it reaches 48.0% (Avg@32), substantially outperforming the strongest open-source baseline DeepEyes (35.1%) by 12.9 points. On V* Bench 4K/8K, Mini-o3 scores 88.2/77.5, and on HR-Bench it attains 73.3 (Avg@8), all leading prior methods. On MME-Realworld, Mini-o3 achieves 65.5 (Avg@1), edging out Pixel Reasoner (64.4) and DeepEyes (64.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mini-o3 outperforms all eight baselines on every metric where a direct comparison is possible. The largest margin is on VisualProbe-Hard, where Mini-o3 (48.0) surpasses DeepEyes (35.1) by 12.9 points and Pixel Reasoner (28.8) by 19.2 points, reflecting the advantage of deeper multi-turn exploration on genuinely hard search problems. On V* Bench 4K, Mini-o3 (88.2) narrowly edges out Chain-of-Focus (88.0) and Pixel Reasoner (86.3), while on V* Bench 8K it leads Pixel Reasoner (74.0) by 3.5 points. On HR-Bench, Mini-o3 (73.3) beats DeepEyes (69.5) by 3.8 points. On MME-Realworld, the gap is smaller—Mini-o3 (65.5) leads Pixel Reasoner (64.4) and DeepEyes (64.0) by roughly 1 point—suggesting that general real-world VQA is less sensitive to the depth of visual search. Notably, GPT-4o (11.2 on VisualProbe-Hard) and LLaVA-OneVision (13.4) trail far behind, underscoring that multi-turn agentic tool use is critical for challenging visual search rather than a marginal refinement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-for-visual-search line initiated by DeepEyes, Chain-of-Focus, and Pixel Reasoner, which train VLMs to iteratively zoom into image regions using GRPO-style objectives. The key departure is twofold: (1) a new over-turn masking modification to GRPO that prevents penalization of truncated trajectories, enabling test-time turn scaling beyond the training budget, and (2) a purpose-built dataset (VisualProbe) and cold-start pipeline designed to elicit diverse reasoning patterns (depth-first search, trial-and-error, goal maintenance) that prior open-source systems fail to produce. The training recipe also inherits stability techniques from DAPO (clip-higher, dynamic sampling, token-level loss).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The test-time scaling property—accuracy monotonically improving from 4 to 32 interaction turns despite training with only 6—is practically significant because it decouples training cost from inference-time reasoning depth, a property that could generalize to other multi-turn agentic RL settings. The over-turn masking technique is simple and broadly applicable to any RL setup where trajectories can be truncated by budget limits. Limitations include evaluation on a single 7B base model (Qwen2.5-VL), a restricted action space (grounding + final answer only), and visual search as the sole task domain; whether the approach transfers to larger models, richer tool suites, or non-search visual reasoning remains open.
