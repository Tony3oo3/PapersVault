## Problem

Video diffusion transformers (DiTs) still fail to reliably generate multi-instance or subject-object interactions from text prompts, exhibiting two distinct failure modes: semantic grounding failure (mismatched subject/object/verb localization) and semantic propagation failure (identity drift, duplication, or hallucination over time). No prior work has systematically analyzed how video DiTs internally represent these interactions through their attention mechanisms, nor provided a targeted mechanism to strengthen them.

## Input / Output

The model takes a first RGB frame, an interaction-aware text prompt (with subject, verb, and object tokens), and a palette-indexed multi-instance ID map I₀ (aggregated per-instance binary masks with stable IDs) as input, producing a 480×720, 49-frame video. The ID map is channel-wise concatenated with the noise latent and first frame through an extended input projection, giving users explicit per-instance control at inference via off-the-shelf segmentors like SAM2. The framework is demonstrated on CogVideoX-5B-I2V (42 layers) and Wan2.1-14B-I2V (40 layers), with the latter requiring dummy-layer filtering due to its separate self-attention/cross-attention architecture.

## Method

MATRIX first identifies a small set of interaction-dominant layers (e.g., layers 7 and 11 for video-to-text grounding, layer 12 for video-to-video propagation in CogVideoX) where attention alignment with instance mask tracks strongly correlates with generation success. It then applies two targeted losses: Semantic Grounding Alignment (SGA), which supervises video-to-text attention so noun tokens localize to subject/object mask tracks and verb tokens attend to their per-frame union, and Semantic Propagation Alignment (SPA), which supervises video-to-video attention so instance bindings persist across frames. A lightweight causal decoder mirrors the 3D VAE upsampling schedule to map latent-resolution attention maps to pixel-space mask tracks, and both losses use a composite of BCE, soft DICE, and L₂ terms. Training uses LoRA (rank 128) on only the identified layers plus the input projection and decoder, keeping the backbone frozen, and is trained for 4,000 steps on the curated MATRIX-11K dataset (11K videos with interaction-aware captions and verified multi-instance mask tracks built via GroundingDINO + VLM verification + SAM2 propagation).

## Results

On the InterGenEval protocol (evaluated over 60 synthetic and 58 real-domain image-prompt pairs), MATRIX achieves the best Interaction Fidelity (IF) of 0.593, substantially outperforming the strongest baseline TaVid (0.494) by nearly 10 points. It also leads on Semantic Grounding Integrity (SGI, 0.641 vs. 0.522), Key Interaction Semantic Alignment (KISA, 0.546 vs. 0.465), and Human Anatomy fidelity (HA, 0.954 vs. 0.937), while matching or exceeding all baselines on Motion Smoothness (0.994) and Image Quality (69.73). The framework is further validated as plug-and-play on Wan2.1-14B-I2V, where it improves interaction fidelity without degrading visual quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MATRIX is compared against three families of baselines: the base CogVideoX-5B-I2V model, the larger Open-Sora-11B-I2V, and TaVid (a target-aware video diffusion method). Against the base CogVideoX-5B-I2V, MATRIX improves IF by +14.4 (0.449→0.593) and SGI by +15.0 (0.491→0.641), showing that the SGA+SPA regularization on interaction-dominant layers is the primary driver rather than scale. Against TaVid, the strongest interaction-aware baseline, MATRIX still wins on every interaction metric (IF +9.9, SGI +11.9, KISA +8.1) and on human anatomy fidelity (HA 0.954 vs. 0.917), indicating that explicit mask-track supervision of both grounding and propagation outperforms single-target conditioning. Against Open-Sora-11B-I2V, MATRIX gains +11.3 IF and +13.3 SGI while also recovering image quality (IQ 69.73 vs. 63.32), which Open-Sora degrades due to unstable grounding causing extra or missing instances. Notably, MATRIX matches or exceeds the best video-quality scores (MS 0.994, IQ 69.73) across all baselines, confirming that the attention-alignment losses do not sacrifice general generation quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on CogVideoX-5B-I2V and Wan2.1-14B-I2V as base video DiT backbones, but departs from prior work on internal representations in diffusion models (which examined UNet, image DiTs, and video DiTs without formalizing interaction structure) by defining two specific perspectives—semantic grounding via video-to-text attention and semantic propagation via video-to-video attention—and showing they concentrate in a small set of interaction-dominant layers. It also distinguishes itself from HOI synthesis (which operates at motion level), relation customization (which uses closed verb sets and per-relation adapters), and controllable video diffusion (which uses geometry/motion cues agnostic to interaction semantics) by directly supervising attention alignment with multi-instance mask tracks in an open-vocabulary setting. The MATRIX-11K dataset and InterGenEval protocol are introduced to fill gaps in both training data and evaluation that existing resources (VBench, EvalCrafter, FETV) do not address at the interaction level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that interaction representation in video DiTs is concentrated in a handful of layers (e.g., layers 7/11 for grounding, layer 12 for propagation in CogVideoX) provides a practical handle for targeted intervention without full retraining, and the plug-and-play validation across CogVideoX, Wan2.1, and HunyuanVideo suggests the approach generalizes across architectures. The MATRIX-11K dataset and InterGenEval protocol address a real gap: existing benchmarks measure global quality or single-entity alignment but cannot localize 'who does what to whom' or verify temporal persistence of bindings. Open questions include whether the method scales beyond 5 instances, whether mask-track supervision can be replaced by weaker signals (e.g., bounding boxes) for broader applicability, and whether the identified layer structure persists as model scale increases.
