## Problem

In compensation-based quantization methods like GPTQ and GPTAQ, the column-level calibration objective during intra-layer iteration aligns the quantized output with the output of the *compensated* (partially updated) weights rather than the true output of the original full-precision layer. This sub-optimal target causes the residual error to miss an intrinsic component introduced by the weight compensation process itself, leading to incomplete error correction that accumulates across quantization steps.

## Input / Output

The method consumes full-precision (FP16) model weights and a small calibration set (128 samples of 2048 tokens from C4, WikiText-2, or RedPajama) to produce per-group symmetric quantized weights (e.g., 2-bit or 3-bit, group size 128) with per-token asymmetric activation quantization. Crucially, it requires both the Quant-flow input X (propagated through previously quantized layers) and the FP-flow input X̃ (propagated through original full-precision layers) at each layer to compute the two components of the residual error. The final quantized model has identical architecture and incurs no additional runtime overhead during inference.

## Method

The paper redefines the column-level residual error from GPTAQ's r = w^(q)(X̃ − X) to r′ = w^(0)X̃ − w^(q)X, which decomposes into r1 (the inter-layer input error already captured by GPTAQ) plus a new r2 = (w^(0) − w^(q))X̃, termed the 'compensation-aware error,' arising from the discrepancy between the current compensated weights and the original weights within the same layer. To compute r2 efficiently without O(n²) recomputation, the authors apply GPTAQ's neuron decomposition technique, precomputing a matrix P2 = ((X̃XᵀL) ⊙ M_U)Lᵀ alongside the existing P1, where L is the Cholesky factor of the Hessian. The resulting weight update for each column q becomes: standard GPTQ update + W_{:,q}^(q)·P1_{q,q:} + (W_{:,q}^(0) − W_{:,q}^(q))·P2_{q,q:}, requiring only the additional storage of W^(0) and P2. This formulation integrates as a single added term into both GPTQ (where P1 vanishes since ΔX=0) and GPTAQ, incurring only ~5% additional quantization time and modest extra calibration memory.

## Results

On 3-bit per-group weight-only quantization, the proposed Compensation-Aware Error (CAE) consistently improves both perplexity and zero-shot accuracy across Llama 2/3 models (1B–70B). For example, on Llama2-7B, GPTQ+Ours reduces C4 perplexity from 13.60 to 8.34 and raises average downstream accuracy from 64.9% to 66.5%, while GPTAQ+Ours achieves 8.19 C4 PPL and 66.6% average accuracy. On Llama3-8B, GPTAQ+Ours lowers C4 PPL from 12.96 to 12.25 and increases average accuracy from 68.8% to 70.5%. Under extreme 2-bit quantization with QuaRot rotation, the method further reduces Llama2-13B WikiText-2 PPL from 7.5 to 7.3 and boosts average accuracy from 55.8% to 58.2%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GPTQ, the proposed CAE term yields large and consistent gains: on Llama2-7B, C4 perplexity drops from 13.60 to 8.34 (GPTQ+Ours) and average accuracy rises from 64.9% to 66.5%; on Llama3.1-8B-Instruct, average accuracy jumps from 70.3% to 72.7%. Against GPTAQ, improvements are more modest but still consistent: on Llama3-8B, C4 PPL decreases from 12.96 to 12.25 and average accuracy increases from 68.8% to 70.5%; on Llama2-13B, WikiText-2 PPL improves from 5.42 to 5.39. Compared to AWQ, the proposed method (GPTQ+Ours or GPTAQ+Ours) achieves lower perplexity and higher average accuracy on all tested models, e.g., on Llama3-8B, GPTAQ+Ours reaches 12.25 C4 PPL vs. AWQ's 14.74 and 70.5% vs. 67.7% average accuracy. On Vision Transformers (DeiT-Base, W2A4), the method achieves 62.1% ImageNet accuracy, outperforming GPTQ (51.8%) and GPTAQ (61.3%), while matching or slightly exceeding RepQ-ViT (75.6% at W4A4). The method also integrates with rotation-based baselines (QuaRot, SpinQuant) in W2A4KV4 settings, consistently improving over the unmodified GPTAQ baseline by 0.5–2.0% average accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits directly in the OBQ → GPTQ → GPTAQ lineage of Hessian-based, column-wise compensation quantization. Its core contribution is a theoretical correction to GPTAQ's column-level objective: GPTAQ aligns the quantized output with the output of the *compensated* (partially updated) weights rather than the original full-precision weights, which the authors show is sub-optimal. By decomposing the true residual into the inter-layer propagated error (GPTAQ's existing term) and a new intra-layer 'compensation-aware error,' the paper extends GPTAQ's neuron decomposition technique to efficiently compute the additional term, yielding a drop-in improvement over both GPTQ and GPTAQ.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The compensation-aware error is a clean, well-motivated correction that consistently improves perplexity and downstream accuracy across Llama 2/3, Qwen3, and DeiT models under W2A16, W3A16, and W2A4KV4 settings, with only ~5% additional quantization time and modest memory overhead. This makes it a practical, low-risk upgrade for anyone already using GPTQ or GPTAQ pipelines. However, the absolute gains are modest (typically 0.1–0.5 perplexity points, 1–2% accuracy), and the Llama3-70B results reveal that GPTAQ's asymmetric calibration can actually underperform plain GPTQ at scale, raising open questions about whether the inter-layer alignment strategy itself needs rethinking for very large models. The method's generality across architectures (LLMs and ViTs) and its compatibility with rotation-based techniques (QuaRot, SpinQuant) suggest broad applicability, but whether the compensation-aware error remains beneficial under even more aggressive settings (e.g., W1A16) or with different Hessian approximations remains unexplored.
