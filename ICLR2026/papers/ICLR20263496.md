## Problem

On-the-fly 3D reconstruction from monocular video faces a fundamental tradeoff: per-scene SLAM-based optimization achieves high fidelity but is computationally expensive and fragile, while feed-forward foundation models enable real-time inference but suffer from limited rendering quality and global inconsistency. Additionally, 3D Gaussian Splatting pipelines scale poorly with scene size, as the number of primitives grows rapidly without principled level-of-detail management.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263496/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a streaming monocular RGB image sequence (32–1363+ frames) with optional camera intrinsics; if focal length is unknown, it is jointly estimated via GeoCalib initialization and refined during pose optimization. It produces Sim(3) camera poses for every frame, a hierarchical set of 3D Gaussian primitives organized into 4 LoD levels with per-primitive voxel indices and distance thresholds, and real-time rendered novel views. Every 8th frame is held out from mapping and used only for pose evaluation, ensuring fair cross-method comparison.

## Method

ARTDECO is a three-module streaming SLAM pipeline: (1) a frontend that uses MASt3R for dense matching and Sim(3) pose estimation with covariance-weighted reprojection residuals, categorizing frames into keyframes, mapper frames, and common frames; (2) a backend that performs loop closure via ASMK pre-filtering followed by π³ multi-frame 3D inference for geometrically consistent candidate selection, then global bundle adjustment with Gauss-Newton; (3) a mapping module that initializes 3D Gaussians at multi-resolution levels (4 LoD tiers) using Laplacian-of-Gaussian insertion probability, decodes scale and rotation from concatenated voxel-region and per-Gaussian features via MLPs, and renders with a distance-aware opacity fade (α′ = α·(2d_max − d_r)/d_max) to suppress flickering across viewing distances. This hierarchical semi-implicit structure reduces rendered Gaussian count by ~26% (176K→130K) while improving PSNR, and the LoD mechanism prunes primitives from 1131K to 29K at 1/8 resolution with minimal quality loss.

## Results

ARTDECO achieves state-of-the-art reconstruction quality across all eight indoor and outdoor benchmarks, with PSNR of 29.12 on ScanNet++, 26.18 on TUM, 28.57 on VR-NeRF (indoor), and 29.54 on Fast-LIVO2, 28.75 on Waymo (outdoor). For tracking, it attains the lowest ATE RMSE of 0.018 m on ScanNet++ and 0.028 m on TUM, outperforming MASt3R-SLAM (0.025/0.030) and all classical SLAM systems. Training time is 5.33 min (indoor) and 6.58 min (outdoor), faster than all baselines except OnTheFly-NVS (2.29/0.74 min), while delivering substantially higher fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARTDECO outperforms all 3DGS-based SLAM baselines (MonoGS, S3PO-GS, SEGS-SLAM, OnTheFly-NVS, LongSplat) on every reconstruction metric across all eight datasets, with the largest margins on ScanNet++ (+4.18 dB PSNR over LongSplat) and Fast-LIVO2 (+3.17 dB over LongSplat). LongSplat is the closest competitor on TUM (25.09 vs 26.18 PSNR) but requires 442.96 min of training versus ARTDECO's 5.33 min. On tracking, ARTDECO achieves 0.018 m ATE on ScanNet++, beating MASt3R-SLAM (0.025 m) and SEGS-SLAM (0.245 m), and 0.028 m on TUM, surpassing Go-SLAM (0.035 m), DROID-SLAM (0.038 m), and DPV-SLAM++ (0.054 m). Against the feed-forward baseline AnySplat, ARTDECO reduces tracking error from 0.082 to 0.076 m and improves PSNR from 26.53 to 27.96 on ScanNet++ (32 views), with the gap widening at 100 views where AnySplat degrades significantly. On robustness benchmarks (TUM low-texture, TartanAirV2 extreme motion), ARTDECO achieves the lowest ATE (0.072 m and 0.095 m) while most classical SLAM methods (ORB-SLAM3, SEGS-SLAM) fail entirely, and it leads all 3DGS methods in PSNR (33.74 on TUM low-texture, 23.17 on TartanAirV2). OnTheFly-NVS is the only method faster in training time (2.29 min indoor) but trails ARTDECO by 4–11 dB PSNR depending on dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

ARTDECO sits at the intersection of three research threads: 3D Gaussian Splatting for explicit scene representation, classical SLAM pipelines for real-time localization, and feed-forward 3D foundation models (MASt3R, π³) for pose and geometry estimation. It departs from prior 3DGS-SLAM methods (MonoGS, S3PO-GS, SEGS-SLAM) by treating feed-forward models as modular priors within a streaming SLAM architecture rather than relying solely on traditional feature-based tracking, and from feed-forward reconstruction methods (AnySplat, LongSplat) by retaining a backend with loop closure and global bundle adjustment to enforce multi-view consistency. The hierarchical semi-implicit Gaussian representation with LoD-aware densification extends the 3DGS primitive with a spatially sparse voxel grid that jointly decodes scale and rotation, addressing the scalability bottleneck that prior 3DGS pipelines face in large scenes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ARTDECO provides a practical, deployable path toward on-the-fly 3D digitization by closing the gap between per-scene optimization quality and feed-forward speed, which is critical for AR/VR, robotics, and real-to-sim applications. The LoD mechanism is particularly relevant for large-scale navigable environments where rendering cost grows with scene size. Key limitations remain: the system depends on feed-forward models that degrade under noise, blur, or out-of-distribution inputs, and assumes consistent illumination and sufficient parallax, making it vulnerable to low-texture or repetitive-structure scenes. Open questions include whether uncertainty-aware model selection or adaptive priors can further improve robustness without sacrificing the real-time guarantee.
