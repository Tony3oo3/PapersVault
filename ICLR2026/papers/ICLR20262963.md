## Problem

For private continual release of streaming statistics in turnstile streams, polynomial additive error lower bounds are known: Ω(T^{1/4}) for distinct elements and Ω(T) for F_2 estimation. These bounds leave a significant gap with the best known upper bounds (Õ(T^{1/3}) for distinct elements) and make F_2 estimation appear intractable with purely additive error. The paper asks whether allowing multiplicative error alongside additive error can circumvent these barriers, and shows that it can, yielding polylogarithmic additive error in both problems.

## Input / Output

The algorithm processes a turnstile stream of T updates (a_t, s_t) where a_t ∈ [n] and s_t ∈ {-1, 0, 1}, releasing an estimate after every single update under (ε, δ)-differential privacy at the event level. The output at each timestep t is a scalar estimate of either the number of distinct elements D_t or the F_2 moment ‖x_t‖₂², with error characterized by a (α, β) guarantee meaning the estimate lies in [Y_t/p − r, qY_t + s] where pq = α and r + s = β. Space usage is polylogarithmic in T and n, a significant improvement over prior polynomial-space approaches.

## Method

The core insight is to reduce distinct elements and F_2 estimation to instances of DP continual counting (solved via the Gaussian binary tree mechanism with polylog(T) additive error), where the multiplicative error absorbs the sensitivity that forces large additive error in purely additive formulations. For distinct elements, two algorithms are given: a MinHash-based approach that buckets elements by the least significant non-zero bit of their hash and uses continual counters per bucket to identify the largest non-empty bucket, and a domain-reduction approach that hashes the universe to smaller domains of varying sizes and detects which reduced domain has all coordinates exceeding the noise threshold. For F_2 estimation, a Rademacher Johnson-Lindenstrauss projection maps the n-dimensional frequency vector to m = O(log T/α²) coordinates, each tracked by a separate continual counter, and the estimate is the sum of squared counter outputs. All algorithms compose multiple continual counters via zCDP to maintain the overall privacy budget.

## Results

For continual distinct elements estimation in strict turnstile streams, the MinHash algorithm (Theorem 3.1) achieves (O(log²(T)), O(log²(T))) multiplicative and additive error with O(log³(T)) space, circumventing the prior Ω(T^{1/4}) additive error lower bound. The Domain Reduction algorithm (Theorem 4.1) extends to general turnstile streams with (O(log¹⁰(T)), O(log¹⁰(T))) error and polynomial space. For F₂ estimation, the algorithm (Theorem 5.1) achieves (1+η, Õ_η(log⁴(T))) error with O_η(log²(T)) space, reducing the purely additive error lower bound of Ω(T) to polylogarithmic at the cost of a (1+η) multiplicative factor.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's MinHash algorithm (Theorem 3.1) improves upon the prior best upper bound of Cummings et al. (2025), which achieves (1+η, Õ(T^{1/3})) error with Õ_η(T^{1/3}) space, by reducing both additive and multiplicative error to polylogarithmic (O(log²(T))) while also reducing space to O(log³(T)). This comes at the cost of a larger multiplicative factor (O(log²(T)) vs. 1+η), but the additive error improvement from T^{1/3} to log²(T) is the paper's central contribution. The Ω(T^{1/4}) lower bound of Jain et al. (2023a) for purely additive error is circumvented entirely by allowing multiplicative error, since the lower bound instances have true values much larger than the additive error. For F₂ estimation, the paper's (1+η, Õ_η(log⁴(T))) result extends Epasto et al. (2023) from insertion-only to general turnstile streams while improving the additive error exponent from log⁷(T) to log⁴(T). The Domain Reduction algorithm (Theorem 4.1) is the only approach that handles general turnstile streams (where frequencies can be negative) with polylogarithmic error, though at the cost of polynomial space and a larger log¹⁰(T) factor.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Gaussian binary tree mechanism for DP continual counting (Jain et al. 2023b) as a core primitive, and extends the Johnson-Lindenstrauss + continual counting approach of Epasto et al. (2023) from insertion-only streams to general turnstile streams for F2 estimation. Conceptually, it departs from the prior focus on purely additive error by showing that the Ω(T^{1/4}) lower bound for distinct elements and Ω(T) lower bound for F2 (Jain et al. 2023a) are artifacts of the additive-only error model: the lower bound instances have true values far exceeding the additive error, so they do not preclude good multiplicative approximations. The MinHash-based algorithm (Section 3) is a novel private adaptation of the classic min-hash estimator, while the domain reduction technique (Section 4) introduces a new dimensionality-reduction-based approach to make coordinates detectable by continual counters.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main conceptual contribution is that polynomial additive error barriers for fundamental streaming problems under DP can be replaced with polylogarithmic additive error at the cost of polylogarithmic multiplicative error, while simultaneously achieving polylogarithmic space—a combination not available in prior work. This matters because multiplicative error is natural and unavoidable in sublinear-space streaming even without privacy, so the result aligns the private setting with the non-private one. Key open questions include whether constant multiplicative error suffices (the current techniques hit a barrier where counters cannot distinguish a single high-frequency element from many low-frequency ones), the optimal multiplicative-additive tradeoff curve, and whether the reduction in Theorem 4.2 can be leveraged to prove lower bounds against o(n) purely additive error.
