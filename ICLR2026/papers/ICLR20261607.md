## Problem

Conventional cross-lingual IR evaluation assumes a single-language document pool, masking a critical failure mode: when English and a target language coexist in the same pool, multilingual retrievers systematically prioritize irrelevant English documents over the relevant same-language document, and performance degrades sharply depending on query language. The paper introduces a multi-reference evaluation scenario and the Max@R metric to expose this cross-lingual misalignment and language bias, then proposes a training strategy to correct it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261607/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a query in any language and scores documents from a bilingual pool (English + one target language), producing a ranked list where each query has two ground-truth documents (one per language). For training, the method consumes parallel triples (q_en, p_en, p_tgt) — an English query, its English positive passage, and the GPT-4o-translated target-language passage — from the 2.8k-sample MIRACL dataset. The Max@R metric reports the worst rank at which all parallel ground-truths are retrieved, and Max@R_norm rescales this logarithmically to a 0–100 range for cross-dataset comparability.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-component training loss that jointly optimizes distributional alignment and retrieval relevance. First, L_JSD treats each document's embedding vector as a categorical probability distribution via softmax over its dimensions, then minimizes the square-root Jensen-Shannon Divergence between the English and target-language document distributions, enforcing dimension-level structural alignment beyond what cosine similarity captures. Second, L_NCE applies InfoNCE contrastive loss on positive pairs of (English query, target-language document) with in-batch negatives to directly improve cross-lingual query-document similarity. The key insight, illustrated in Figure 1, is that two embeddings can share an identical cosine score (e.g., 0.99) yet remain severely misaligned at the distribution level; JSD closes this gap by aligning the full probabilistic structure of the embedding space. Fine-tuning uses only 2.8k parallel samples with model-specific learning rates (1e-5 to 4e-6), one epoch, batch size 32, and AdamW with bfloat16 on 2×A100 GPUs.

## Results

Under the Multi scenario (two-language document pool, two ground-truth documents per query), the proposed JSD+InfoNCE training strategy yields dramatic improvements over all four baselines. The most striking gain is on multilingual-e5-base with Chinese queries on XQuAD, where Comp@10 jumps from 0.50% to 55.88% and Max@R drops from 650.95 to 23.10. On jina-embeddings-v3, the English-vs-target-language performance gap for En+Zh narrows from 6.89%p to 1.77%p (XQuAD) and from 4.45%p to 0.12%p (Belebele), demonstrating reduced language bias. Even the strongest baseline, bge-m3, sees consistent Max@R_norm gains of 1–4 points across all language pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines across every language pair and both evaluation datasets. Against multilingual-e5-base (the weakest baseline), gains are the most dramatic: Comp@10 for Chinese queries on XQuAD improves from 0.50% to 55.88%, and Max@R drops from 650.95 to 23.10, indicating the baseline was essentially failing at cross-lingual retrieval in the multi-reference setting. Against jina-embeddings-v3, the method consistently adds 5–12 points on Comp@10 for target-language queries (e.g., 58.57→70.67 for Zh on XQuAD) and narrows the English-vs-target performance gap from ~7%p to under 2%p. Against gte-multilingual-base, which already performs relatively well, improvements are more modest (2–5 points on Comp@R_norm) but still consistent across all pairs. Against bge-m3, the strongest and most balanced baseline, the method achieves smaller but uniform gains of 1–4 points on Max@R_norm, with the exception of En+Es where performance is essentially tied (77.66 vs. 77.68). Notably, the method also preserves or slightly improves monolingual retrieval (Mono-Same and Mono-Cross), confirming that the alignment training does not degrade single-language performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from conventional CLIR evaluation, which assumes a single-language document pool, by introducing a multi-reference scenario where English and target-language documents coexist. Methodologically, it repurposes Jensen-Shannon Divergence (Lin, 1991)—typically applied to token-level or sentence-level probability distributions in NLP—as a distributional alignment loss over softmax-transformed embedding vectors, combined with standard InfoNCE contrastive learning. The evaluation contribution (Max@R metric and the multi-reference protocol) extends prior multilingual retrieval benchmarks (XQuAD, Belebele, MIRACL) by exposing a failure mode invisible under standard CLIR or MLIR settings. The work is positioned against prior cross-lingual alignment studies (Hu et al., 2020; Schuster et al., 2019) that focus on general representation alignment rather than retrieval-specific coexisting-language scenarios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that widely-used multilingual embedding models (multilingual-e5, gte-multilingual, jina-embeddings-v3, bge-m3) suffer severe English prioritization bias in realistic mixed-language document pools—a problem masked by conventional CLIR evaluation. The Max@R metric and multi-reference scenario provide a practical diagnostic tool for practitioners deploying multilingual retrieval systems. Key limitations include the English-centric design (all pairs involve English), restriction to two-language settings, reliance on GPT-4o machine translations for training data, and single-seed experiments. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-English language pairs, three-or-more-language pools, and whether the distributional alignment loss transfers to other retrieval architectures beyond the four tested.
