## Problem

Conventional RGB cameras on UAVs suffer from motion blur and insufficient dynamic range in low-light conditions, degrading large-scale 3D scene reconstruction. While event cameras offer microsecond temporal resolution and 86+ dB dynamic range, no existing event-enhanced UAV dataset provides the full suite of synchronized high-resolution RGB, dense per-frame depth, volumetric ground truth, and accurate 6-DoF poses required for neural rendering at city scale.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264283/cropped_page0_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline consumes paired RGB video (3840×2160 at 120 fps, global shutter) and asynchronous event streams (1280×720, up to 10 Mevts/s) captured simultaneously from a DJI Matrice 350 RTK at 70–100 m altitude, along with a separate DJI L2 LiDAR survey (2.64 cm/pixel GSD) and RTK-derived 6-DoF poses. Outputs include frame-accurate event–RGB timestamp alignments, dense metrically accurate depth maps back-projected from a fused LiDAR+MVS mesh, and 3DGS scene representations that produce novel-view renderings. The dataset comprises 45 sequences spanning over 8 hours and 0.72 km² of LiDAR coverage across five distinct urban scenarios.

## Method

The paper introduces two key components for event-enhanced 3DGS reconstruction. The GTA (Geometry-constrained Timestamp Alignment) module finds the optimal event timestamp for each RGB frame by maximizing a geometry consistency score—computed from dense feature matching (MatchAnything/ROMA) and robust homography estimation (MAGSAC)—within a ±100 ms search window, then globally refines the sequence to enforce a 1-second cadence via a penalized optimization. The RER (Region-wise Event Rendering) loss constrains the log-brightness difference between two rendered 3DGS frames to match the accumulated event image, but only within the spatially overlapping region between the two sensors, using a diagonal affine approximation of the RGB-to-event homography to avoid costly per-pixel warping. LiDAR geometry is registered to the RGB coordinate frame via a unified SfM pipeline in RealityScan with LiDAR-derived poses frozen as anchors, and fused with MVS to produce ground-truth depth. Benchmarks integrate both components into Luminance-GS (low-light) and Improved-GS (motion-blur) 3DGS pipelines, trained for 30,000 iterations with event refinement starting at iteration 8,000.

## Results

On the SkyEvents dataset, adding event supervision via the RER loss consistently improves 3DGS rendering quality in both low-light and motion-blur conditions. In the low-light setting with Luminance-GS, PSNR increases from 4.79 to 5.21 dB (Scene 1) and from 5.70 to 5.77 dB (Scene 2), while LPIPS decreases slightly. The gains are more pronounced in the motion-blur setting with Improved-GS: on Scene 2 (>800 training images), PSNR improves by ~0.62 dB (25.86 → 26.48) and LPIPS drops from 0.2653 to 0.2482. For event-to-video reconstruction, existing methods trained on ground-level data degrade substantially on aerial event streams, with SSL-E2VID achieving a BRISQUE of 59.93 versus 7.38 for E2VID+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's core comparison is between each 3DGS pipeline with and without the proposed RER event loss. Luminance-GS (Cui et al., 2025) benefits from event data in low-light: PSNR gains of +0.42 dB (Scene 1) and +0.07 dB (Scene 2) with marginal LPIPS reductions, confirming events act as a stabilizing cue under extreme underexposure. Improved-GS (Deng et al., 2025) shows the clearest event benefit in motion-blur: Scene 2 PSNR improves by 0.62 dB and LPIPS drops by 0.017, while Scene 1 gains are smaller (+0.08 dB PSNR). The gamma-recovery kernel variant (Improved-GS+kernel) also benefits from events in Scene 1 (+0.15 dB PSNR) but shows a slight LPIPS regression, suggesting the kernel partially compensates for what events would otherwise correct. For event-to-video reconstruction (Table 3), all seven baselines (E2VID, E2VID+, ET-Net, FireNet, FireNet+, HyperE2VID, SPADE-E2VID, SSL-E2VID) struggle on aerial event data; E2VID+ achieves the best BRISQUE (7.38) and MANIQA (0.284), while SSL-E2VID degrades catastrophically (BRISQUE 59.93), underscoring that ground-level-trained methods do not generalize to UAV-scale event streams.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of event-based 3D reconstruction and large-scale UAV scene modeling, building directly on 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) and its recent extensions for challenging lighting (Luminance-GS, Cui et al., 2025) and densification (Improved-GS, Deng et al., 2025). The RER loss adapts the event brightness-change consistency formulation from ELITE-EVGS (Zhang et al., 2025) with a region-wise alignment step to handle the spatial mismatch between RGB and event sensors on a UAV platform. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior event UAV datasets (MVSEC, UZH-FPV, M3ED, NU-AIR, EvMAPPER) by being the first to provide the full tri-modal stack—synchronized RGB, event streams, and LiDAR-derived dense depth with 6-DoF poses—required for neural rendering benchmarks at city scale.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SkyEvents fills a concrete gap: no prior dataset combined the modalities and ground-truth density needed to train and evaluate event-enhanced neural rendering for large-scale aerial scenes, and the GTA/RER components provide a practical recipe for integrating events into existing 3DGS pipelines. However, the empirical gains from events are modest (sub-decibel PSNR improvements in blur, marginal changes in low-light), and the low-light evaluation relies on synthetic gamma-corrected frames rather than true nighttime captures, limiting the strength of the claim. The authors acknowledge that RGB-event spatiotemporal alignment is imperfect due to hardware constraints, and the failure of existing event-to-video and depth-estimation models on aerial data highlights that dedicated aerial event research remains largely open.
