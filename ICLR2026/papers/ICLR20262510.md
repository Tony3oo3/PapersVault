## Problem

Panoramic depth estimation suffers from two intertwined limitations: (1) severe scarcity of panoramic RGB-depth training data, which confines prior methods to in-domain settings with poor zero-shot generalization, and (2) spherical distortions inherent in equirectangular projection that most approaches handle inefficiently through multi-view cubemap splitting and fusion, sacrificing end-to-end simplicity and speed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262510/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single 360°×180° equirectangular panorama RGB image at 1024×512 resolution and produces a dense per-pixel scale-invariant distance map (distance from each pixel to the sphere center, i.e., the 360° camera). Because the output is scale-invariant rather than metric, the predicted distances preserve full 3D geometry up to a global scale factor, enabling direct point-cloud reconstruction after median alignment. The entire pipeline is a single forward pass with no multi-view splitting or post-hoc fusion.

## Method

DA² combines two key components. First, a panoramic data curation engine converts ~543K perspective RGB-depth pairs from six datasets into full panoramas via Perspective-to-Equirectangular (P2E) projection followed by FLUX-I2P panoramic out-painting on the RGB (while depth receives only P2E projection), scaling the total training set to ~607K samples. Second, SphereViT uses a DINOv2-ViT-L backbone (24 self-attention blocks) augmented with a lightweight 4-block cross-attention module that injects a fixed Spherical Embedding: each pixel's azimuth and polar angles are expanded into the feature dimension via sine-cosine basis functions, and image features attend to these embeddings as queries while the embeddings serve as keys and values, explicitly encoding spherical geometry without learnable positional parameters. Training is supervised end-to-end with a weighted sum of L1 distance loss (on median-aligned scale-invariant distances) and L1 normal loss (on surface normals derived via a distance-to-normal operator), with λ_dis=1.0 and λ_nor=2.0.

## Results

DA² achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot panoramic depth estimation with an average AbsRel of 6.62%, RMSE of 20.63%, δ₁ of 95.73%, and δ₂ of 98.51% across Stanford2D3D, Matterport3D, and PanoSUNCG. This represents a 38% improvement in AbsRel over the strongest prior zero-shot end-to-end baseline (UniK3D, avg. AbsRel 10.81%) and a 22% improvement in RMSE. Remarkably, DA² even surpasses the best in-domain method (HUSH, avg. AbsRel 8.10% on available datasets) despite being evaluated zero-shot, ranking 1st in the zero-shot setting and 1.67th overall across all 32 compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DA² decisively outperforms all 32 compared baselines across every dataset and metric. Against the strongest prior zero-shot end-to-end method UniK3D, DA² reduces AbsRel from 11.31→7.23 (Stanford2D3D), 9.66→6.67 (Matterport3D), and 11.46→5.96 (PanoSUNCG), a consistent ~38% average improvement. Against the strongest zero-shot fusion-based method MoGe v2, DA² achieves substantially lower AbsRel (e.g., 7.23 vs. 14.69 on Stanford2D3D) and higher δ₁ (95.45% vs. 79.98%), while also being significantly faster as an end-to-end model. Notably, DA² surpasses even the best in-domain method HUSH (AbsRel 7.82 on Stanford2D3D) in the zero-shot setting (7.23), and beats DepthAnyCamera by a wide margin (7.23 vs. 15.26 AbsRel on Stanford2D3D). Among zero-shot end-to-end methods, PanDA (affine-invariant) and DepthAnyCamera are the only other competitors, both of which DA² outperforms by large margins on all three benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

DA² departs from the dominant fusion-based paradigm in panoramic depth estimation (which combines ERP and cubemap features via additional modules like BiFuse, SphereFusion, or HUSH's spherical harmonics) by proposing a single end-to-end ViT backbone (SphereViT) that injects spherical geometry through fixed sine-cosine embeddings attended via cross-attention. On the data side, it repurposes the large-scale perspective depth datasets (Hypersim, Virtual-KITTI, Dynamic Replica, etc.) that power zero-shot perspective estimators (MoGe, UniDepth, DepthAnything) by converting them into panoramic form via P2E projection and FLUX-I2P out-painting, rather than relying on the scarce native panoramic data (~63K) that constrained prior zero-shot panoramic methods (PanDA, UniK3D, DepthAnyCamera). The DINOv2-initialized ViT backbone follows the trend set by Lotus and Ke et al. of leveraging pre-trained visual features for dense prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result that a zero-shot panoramic estimator outperforms all prior in-domain methods is a meaningful shift for the field, suggesting that data diversity and scale can substitute for domain-specific training in panoramic depth. The panoramic data curation engine is a practical, reusable pipeline that could benefit other 360° tasks beyond depth. Key limitations remain: the 1024×512 training resolution caps fine-detail recovery, the incomplete depth supervision near the poles (only ~10% of the spherical area has GT depth from perspective sources) may constrain pole accuracy, and visible left-right seams persist. Open questions include whether the approach scales to 4K panoramas, whether out-painted depth (currently avoided for accuracy concerns) could further close the supervision gap, and whether the spherical embedding design generalizes to non-ERP projections.
