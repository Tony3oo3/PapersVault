## Problem

No prior work has unified text-to-CAD generation and CAD editing into a single model, largely because standard LLM tokenizers (e.g., BPE) decompose CAD sequences into natural-language word pieces, fragmenting operations and numeric parameters into arbitrary substrings. This fragmentation prevents attention layers from modeling primitive-level geometric structure and inter-primitive dependencies, making it difficult for a single backbone to master both the generative and precise-editing skills required for real-world iterative prototyping.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language instruction and an optional original CAD sequence in Sketch-and-Extrude Modeling (SEM) format (sketches of lines/arcs/circles plus extrusion operations encoded as BVVTTTRRRRRRRRSOO parameter strings) and produces a parametric CAD sequence in the same format. For Text-to-CAD the original sequence is empty; for editing it is a valid CAD sequence to be modified. Before reaching the LLM, CAD sequences are compressed by the VQ-VAE into a shorter sequence of discrete primitive-level tokens (k < n original tokens), reducing sequence length and enabling more efficient fine-tuning.

## Method

CAD-Tokenizer introduces a modality-specific VQ-VAE that replaces standard global pooling with a primitive-specific masking-and-max-pooling layer, producing multiple discrete tokens per sketch-extrusion pair (up to 12: 9 loop slots + 3 extrusion slots) from a 2048-entry codebook. Lightweight adapter layers, trained with a bidirectional vector reconstruction loss against the frozen LLaMA-3-8B embedding and logit layers, map VQ-VAE tokens into the LLM's 4096-dim embedding space without modifying the backbone. The LLM is then fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 32) on ~100k merged CADFusion/CAD-Editor pairs under a unified instruction-tuning objective covering both generation and editing. At inference, a finite-state automaton (FSA) designed around the CAD grammar provides state-dependent logit masks that restrict each decoding step to syntactically valid tokens, eliminating most structural errors without requiring the model to learn grammar implicitly.

## Results

On the unified text-based CAD prototyping task, CAD-Tokenizer achieves F1-Skt of 88.6 and F1-Ext of 94.8 on CAD editing (vs. 73.3/82.6 for the task-specific CAD-Editor), with a Chamfer Distance of 13.5 (vs. 40.7). On Text-to-CAD generation, it reaches F1-Skt 77.9 and F1-Ext 84.7 (vs. 68.8/80.1 for CADFusion), with CD of 26.7 (vs. 38.5) and an Invalidity Ratio of 1.50 (vs. 22.5 for CADFusion and 90.5 for GPT-4o). The method also attains the best human evaluation rank (1.72 on editing, 1.62 on generation) and the highest VLM instruction-following score on editing (5.09/10).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the task-specific CAD-Editor on editing, CAD-Tokenizer improves F1-Skt by +15.3 and F1-Ext by +12.2, and reduces CD from 40.7 to 13.5, though CAD-Editor retains advantages on distributional metrics (JSD 1.63 vs. 2.54, MMD 1.25 vs. 1.97) and invalidity ratio (1.50 vs. 8.38), which the authors attribute to CAD-Editor's tendency to reproduce the original shape. Against CADFusion on Text-to-CAD, CAD-Tokenizer leads on F1-Skt (+9.1), F1-Ext (+4.6), CD (−11.8), JSD (−3.34), and IR (−21.0), while CADFusion scores higher on VLM (5.41 vs. 3.82) and human evaluation (1.91 vs. 1.62). GPT-4o is substantially outperformed across nearly all metrics: on Text-to-CAD, CAD-Tokenizer reduces IR from 90.5 to 1.50, JSD from 62.0 to 7.26, and CD from 79.8 to 26.7. Vanilla-LLaMA (same backbone with native BPE tokenizer) collapses on most metrics—IR reaches 48.6 (editing) and 80.5 (generation)—confirming that the primitive-level tokenization, not the backbone, drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of text-based CAD generation (CADFusion, CAD-Editor, CAD-LLaMA) and hierarchical neural coding (HNC-CAD, SkexGen) by introducing a modality-specific VQ-VAE tokenizer that replaces standard BPE word-piece tokenization with primitive-level discrete tokens. While prior text-based CAD works apply off-the-shelf LLM tokenizers directly to CAD sequences, and classical CAD generation methods train separate encoders per input modality, CAD-Tokenizer unifies all CAD inputs through a single VQ-VAE with primitive-specific pooling and aligns its codebook with the LLM embedding space via lightweight adapters. The FSA-guided decoding strategy draws on the formal grammar structure of sketch-and-extrude sequences, a property that prior autoregressive CAD decoders (SkexGen, HNC-CAD) exploited only at the VQ level rather than at the LLM sampling level.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying Text-to-CAD generation and CAD editing into a single model, this work addresses a practical gap in industrial prototyping pipelines where engineers iteratively create and modify designs. The finding that distributional metrics (COV, JSD, MMD) can be misleadingly high for CAD editing when a model simply reproduces the input without applying edits raises important questions about evaluation methodology for conditional generation tasks. Open challenges include the quality gap between open-source and proprietary CAD data, the need for metrics that capture editing intent rather than mere distributional similarity, and the backbone LLM's limited spatial and commonsense reasoning, which constrains performance on complex geometric instructions.
