## Problem

Tool-Integrated Reasoning (TIR) models frequently exhibit suboptimal tool-call behaviors—excessive calls, insufficient calls, and overthinking after receiving tool results—yet existing work predominantly targets only tool overuse while neglecting tool underuse and the downstream impact of tool results on reasoning quality. The paper identifies that information entropy patterns in TIR chains (entropy spikes after tool results, lower overall entropy in efficient paths) remain unexploited for both data construction and training, leaving a gap in how to simultaneously improve tool-call accuracy and reasoning efficiency.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text question or instruction and has access to two external tools: a code interpreter (executes Python and returns results or errors) and a search tool (retrieves relevant content from a local knowledge base or web). The output is a structured reasoning chain consisting of alternating reasoning segments and tool-call/result pairs, capped at 4 calls per tool during inference, terminating in a final boxed answer. The chain is segmented into 'steps' (from the start of inference or a tool result up to the next tool call or final answer), and the entropy distribution is computed per token within each step.

## Method

Tool-Light combines an entropy-guided sampling strategy with a two-stage self-evolved DPO training pipeline. For data construction, it first generates a main reasoning chain, identifies the top-k highest-entropy token positions across steps, and branches generation from those positions to produce diverse alternative paths (reducing complexity from O(mn) to O(n log m)); positive-negative pairs are then selected using strict criteria (e.g., correct trajectory with minimal tool calls and lowest entropy as positive; incorrect trajectory with more tool calls as negative), mixed with vanilla sampling at a 13:7 ratio. For training, the model first undergoes SFT to acquire basic TIR ability, then enters a self-evolved DPO stage with two sub-phases: Pre-Aligned DPO (using Cri1 criteria to reduce redundant tool calls and overthinking) followed by iterative Self-Evolved DPO Alignment (using Cri2 criteria, where the model resamples its own outputs each round to adapt training difficulty, learning necessary tool calls while preserving efficiency). The self-evolved loop runs for 2 rounds before performance converges, with the key insight that low-entropy reasoning paths correlate with fewer, more purposeful tool calls.

## Results

Tool-Light (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) achieves an average score of 58.0 across 10 reasoning tasks, outperforming the strongest multi-tool baseline Tool-Star (56.6) by 1.4 points and the best single-tool baseline ToRL (50.4) by 7.6 points. On mathematical-reasoning tasks, Tool-Light attains the best or second-best score on all six benchmarks (e.g., 33.3 on AIME24, 92.0 on GSM8K, 87.4 on MATH). On knowledge-intensive tasks, it ranks top-two on all four datasets (e.g., 57.7 on HotpotQA, 56.1 on 2WikiMultiHopQA, 58.7 on Bamboogle). The paper also reports that Tool-Light achieves the best Efficiency (0.44) and Necessity (0.75) scores among all compared methods, indicating superior tool-call effectiveness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Tool-Light (Qwen) outperforms the closest multi-tool baseline Tool-Star by 1.4 points on average (58.0 vs. 56.6), winning on 8 of 10 individual datasets (AIME24, AMC23, MATH, MATH500, GSM8K, HotpotQA, 2Wiki, MuSiQue) while narrowly losing on AIME25 (23.3 vs. 26.7) and Bamboogle (58.7 vs. 58.8). Against single-tool baselines, Tool-Light leads ToRL (50.4) by 7.6 points and ReTool (47.3) by 10.7 points on average, with particularly large gaps on knowledge-intensive tasks (e.g., 57.7 vs. 41.3 on HotpotQA vs. ToRL). Compared to Search-R1 (45.6) and Search-o1 (39.9), Tool-Light gains 12.4 and 18.1 points respectively, demonstrating that multi-tool training generalizes far better than single-tool training. Against the multi-tool ReCall baseline (43.2), Tool-Light improves by 14.8 points on average, with the largest margin on mathematical tasks (e.g., 33.3 vs. 3.3 on AIME24). The paper also shows Tool-Light achieves the best Efficiency (0.44) and Necessity (0.75) scores, confirming it uses tools more effectively than all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Tool-Star (Dong et al., 2025a) for its SFT data and initial training setup, but departs from the broader TIR literature in two key ways. First, while prior single-tool methods (Search-R1, ToRL, ReTool) and multi-tool methods (Tool-Star, ReCall) predominantly rely on reinforcement learning (GRPO) and focus narrowly on reducing excessive tool calls, Tool-Light replaces RL with a two-stage DPO pipeline and explicitly addresses tool underuse and post-tool overthinking. Second, the entropy-guided sampling strategy—branching from high-entropy positions in a main chain to generate diverse reasoning paths—is a novel data construction technique not present in prior TIR work, drawing instead on entropy analysis from general reasoning literature (Wang et al., 2025c; Cui et al., 2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that preference learning (DPO) with carefully designed positive-negative pair criteria can match or exceed RL-based TIR training, offering a simpler and potentially more stable alternative for practitioners. The entropy-guided sampling provides a practical mechanism to reduce data generation cost (O(mn) → O(n log m)) while improving pair quality, and the finding that self-evolved DPO peaks at 2 loops before degrading gives concrete guidance for training schedules. However, the evaluation is limited to 7B/8B models and only two tools (code interpreter and search), leaving open whether the entropy-based criteria and self-evolution loop generalize to larger models, more diverse tool ecosystems, or open-ended agentic settings. The modest average improvement over Tool-Star (58.0 vs. 56.6) suggests the gains, while consistent, are incremental rather than transformative.
