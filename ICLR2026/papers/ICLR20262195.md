## Problem

Learning universal dexterous grasping policies via RL is hampered by the high-dimensional action space of multi-fingered hands, the long-horizon nature of closed-loop grasping, and the multi-task optimization burden across diverse object geometries. Prior methods rely on complex reward shaping, curriculum learning, privileged contact observations, or multi-stage distillation pipelines, and many fail to transfer to real robots or struggle with small, thin objects in tabletop settings.

## Input / Output

The RL policy takes the initial 6D end-effector pose, initial 6D object pose, and a 512-point object point cloud (encoded by PointNet into 128-dim features) and outputs a single SE(3) end-effector transformation (translation in [-0.05, 0.05] m, Euler angles in [-1.57, 1.57] rad) plus delta hand joint angles in [-1, 1] rad. The downstream vision-based policy consumes two RGB or depth images from RealSense D435i cameras along with proprioceptive state (hand joint angles and end-effector pose), and outputs per-timestep target hand joint angles and end-effector poses via a flow-matching action head with action chunking. The language-conditioned variant additionally accepts a text instruction of the form 'Grasp the {COLOR} {OBJECT_NAME}.'

## Method

DemoGrasp's core insight is that a single successful grasp demonstration encodes transferable motion patterns (approach, squeeze, lift) that can be adapted to arbitrary objects by editing two compact parameters: an SE(3) wrist transformation (changing where to grasp) and delta hand joint angles (changing how to grasp). This demonstration-editing task is formulated as a single-step MDP where the policy outputs editing parameters once, the edited trajectory is replayed open-loop in simulation, and a simple binary reward (success × no collision, with 50% of parallel environments permitting table contact) provides the learning signal. The policy is trained with PPO across 7,000 parallel IsaacGym environments using a PointNet + MLP architecture, converging in ~24 hours on a single RTX 4090. For sim-to-real deployment, successful rollouts are collected with rendered camera images under domain randomization (colors, textures, lighting, camera extrinsics, table position), and a GR00T-N1.5-style flow-matching policy with a fine-tuned ViT encoder is trained via imitation learning to produce closed-loop, vision-based robot actions.

## Results

On DexGraspNet with the Shadow Hand, DemoGrasp achieves 95.2% success in state-based settings and 92.2% in vision-based settings on the 3,200-object training set, surpassing the prior SOTA (UniGraspTransformer) by 4.0% and 3.3% respectively. Generalization is strong: the gap between training and unseen-category test objects is only 1.1% (state-based) and 2.1% (vision-based). Trained on just 175 objects, DemoGrasp achieves an 84.6% average success rate across six unseen datasets and seven robotic embodiments. In real-world tests on 110 unseen objects, the vision-based policy reaches 86.5% overall, 95.3% on normal-sized objects, and 71.1% on small/thin objects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DemoGrasp outperforms all four baselines across every evaluation setting. Against UniDexGrasp (Xu et al., CVPR 2023), it gains +15.8% (state) and +18.5% (vision) on the training set. Against UniDexGrasp++ (Wan et al., ICCV 2023), the margin is +7.3% (state) and +6.8% (vision). Against the closest competitor UniGraspTransformer (Wang et al., CVPR 2025), DemoGrasp leads by +4.0% state-based and +3.3% vision-based on training, with the gap widening to +6.1% on unseen-category objects in vision-based settings. Against RobustDexGrasp (Zhang et al., CoRL 2025) on five unseen datasets with the Allegro Hand on UR5, DemoGrasp wins on four of five datasets (e.g., +10.0% on DGA, +9.2% on Omni6DPose, +5.3% on Visual Dexterity) and essentially ties on ModelNet40 (75.58 vs. 75.70). Notably, DemoGrasp achieves these gains while using a simple binary success + collision reward, whereas baselines rely on complex multi-term reward shaping and curriculum learning.

## Relation to Prior Work

DemoGrasp departs from the standard RL formulation for universal dexterous grasping (UniDexGrasp, UniDexGrasp++, UniGraspTransformer, ResDex) by replacing high-dimensional, long-horizon action-space exploration with a single-step MDP over a compact demonstration-editing space (SE(3) wrist transform + delta hand angles). Rather than learning from scratch or using curriculum/distillation pipelines, it repurposes a single successful grasp trajectory as a structural prior that the RL policy edits, drawing conceptually from imitation-learning ideas but using the demonstration as an exploration scaffold rather than a learning target. For sim-to-real transfer, it adopts the recent GR00T-N1.5 flow-matching architecture with a pretrained ViT encoder, positioning itself alongside DextrAH-RGB and RobustDexGrasp in the vision-based sim-to-real grasping line while using a simpler observation (RGB only, no privileged contact info).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical impact is that a single demonstration plus a binary success/collision reward replaces the complex reward shaping, curriculum learning, and multi-stage distillation pipelines that prior methods require, lowering the barrier to deploying universal grasping on new hand embodiments (demonstrated across six hands without hyperparameter tuning). The first successful grasping of small, thin tabletop objects without severe collisions (71.1%) addresses a long-standing gap in prior sim-to-real grasping work. Open questions include the open-loop nature of the RL policy (no closed-loop recovery from mid-grasp perturbations), the absence of tactile feedback for force-sensitive grasping, and whether the single-step formulation can be extended to dynamic or tightly cluttered scenes requiring pre-grasp manipulation.
