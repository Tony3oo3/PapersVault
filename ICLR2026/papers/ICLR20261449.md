## Problem

Prior non-asymptotic efficiency bounds for conformalized regression are expressed only in terms of the calibration set size m, treating the miscoverage level α as a fixed constant and ignoring the effect of training set size n. This paper closes that gap by establishing the first finite-sample upper bounds on prediction set length deviation that explicitly capture the joint dependence on all three parameters (n, m, α), revealing phase transitions in convergence rates across different α regimes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261449/cropped_page6_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of size n+m split into a proper training set (size n) and a calibration set (size m), along with a user-specified miscoverage level α ∈ (0, 1/2]. For a new covariate x, it outputs a prediction interval [t_{α/2}(x) − q̂, t_{1−α/2}(x) + q̂] (CQR) or a symmetric interval centered at the estimated conditional median (CMR), where q̂ is the empirical (1−α)_m-quantile of nonconformity scores computed on the calibration set. The interval is guaranteed to cover the true label with probability at least 1−α, and its length deviates from the oracle conditional quantile interval length by at most the derived bound.

## Method

The paper derives non-asymptotic efficiency guarantees for CQR-SGD and CMR-SGD by decomposing the expected length deviation into four components: SGD estimation error of the quantile regressors (bounded via strong convexity of the pinball loss under a bounded conditional density assumption), the population score-quantile shift induced by parameter estimation error, the finite-sample quantile gap between the empirical and population score quantiles, and the empirical-to-population CDF concentration (via the Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz inequality). A key technical distinction is that all regularity assumptions are placed directly on the underlying data distribution (bounded conditional density with constants f_min, f_max) rather than on the induced nonconformity score distribution, which depends on the trained model. The resulting bound O(n^{-1/2} + (α²n)^{-1} + m^{-1/2} + exp(−α²m)) reveals that when α = Ω(max{n^{-1/4}, √(log m/m)}), the rate reduces to the standard O(n^{-1/2} + m^{-1/2}), while for smaller α the (α²n)^{-1} and exp(−α²m) terms dominate, providing concrete guidance for data allocation between training and calibration.

## Results

The main theoretical contribution is a non-asymptotic upper bound on the expected prediction-set length deviation of O(n^{-1/2} + (α²n)^{-1} + m^{-1/2} + exp(−α²m)) for both CQR-SGD (Theorem 3.2) and CMR-SGD (Theorem 4.1), where n is the training size, m is the calibration size, and α is the miscoverage level. Empirically, regressing log Δ on log n yields a slope that transitions from approximately −1 to −1/2 as α increases, confirming the shift in the dominant term from O(1/(nα²)) to O(1/√n). A secondary regression of the intercept on log α gives a coefficient b₁ = −2.24, consistent with the theoretical α^{−2} scaling. Varying m from 100 to 3000 shows the deviation decreasing with a log-log slope approaching −0.5, matching the O(1/√m) term.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not include head-to-head comparisons against competing conformal prediction methods. The empirical experiments (Section 6, Appendix C–D) are designed to validate the derived theoretical bounds rather than to benchmark against baselines. The paper positions its contribution relative to prior efficiency analyses: Lei et al. (2018) and Bars & Humbert (2025) treat α as a fixed constant and derive rates of O(m^{−1/2} + n^{−1/2}), which the authors show is recovered as a special case when α = Ω(max{n^{−1/4}, √(log m/m)}). The key distinction is that this work provides explicit dependence on α, revealing phase transitions in convergence rates that are invisible when α is held fixed. No quantitative win/lose comparisons against other conformal methods (e.g., volume-minimization approaches by Braun et al. 2025 or Kiyani et al. 2024) are reported.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the conformalized quantile regression (CQR) framework of Romano et al. (2019) and the split conformal prediction paradigm of Vovk et al. (2005) by providing the first non-asymptotic efficiency bounds that explicitly capture the joint dependence on training size n, calibration size m, and miscoverage level α. Prior non-asymptotic analyses (Lei et al., 2018; Bars & Humbert, 2025) either treated α as a fixed constant or imposed assumptions on the induced score distribution, whereas this work places assumptions directly on the underlying data distribution and derives bounds via the Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz inequality and SGD convergence theory (Rakhlin et al., 2012). The analysis also departs from asymptotic efficiency results (Sesia & Candès, 2020; Chernozhukov et al., 2021) by providing finite-sample guarantees rather than convergence-in-limit statements.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The phase-transition structure revealed by the bound O(n^{-1/2} + (α²n)^{-1} + m^{-1/2} + exp(-α²m)) provides concrete guidance for practitioners allocating data between training and calibration at a desired miscoverage level, and shows that α cannot be chosen arbitrarily small without degrading efficiency. Key limitations include the reliance on a linear model class (for convexity of the pinball loss) and the fact that the oracle quantile interval is not always efficiency-optimal under multimodal conditional distributions. Open questions include extending the analysis to nonlinear function classes, distribution-shift settings, and parameterized nonconformity scores that adapt to complex conditional structures.
