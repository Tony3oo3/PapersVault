## Problem

RoPE causes performance breakdown when input length exceeds training length, but prior analyses only assert that channels rotate 'out of distribution' without explaining how extra rotation mechanistically causes pathological behavior. This paper asks: what is the geometric mechanism by which RoPE's progressive rotation destroys attention functionality on long inputs, and what design criteria must a positional encoding satisfy to generalize beyond training length?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265286/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on standard text sequences fed into autoregressive transformer decoders (1B and 3B parameters, Llama3 architecture). RoPE-ID is a drop-in modification to the attention layer's positional encoding: it takes the same query and key projections as standard RoPE but applies rotation to only half the channels per head with adjusted frequency bounds (minimum frequency set to complete two full rotations within the 4k training length, maximum frequency set to one cycle per 32 tokens). No oracle access to sequence length is required at inference time; the model generalizes out of the box. Evaluation uses RULER (synthetic retrieval tasks) and LongBench (multi-task QA, summarization, code) at 4k, 8k, and 16k sequence lengths, plus fine-tuned 128k evaluation.

## Method

The paper first establishes a geometric framework: keys and queries form tight, opposing point clouds (not overlapping clouds around the origin as commonly assumed), and the sink token (first token) functions by having a small ℓ₂ norm that gives it the largest dot product against all queries when the average key-query product is negative. RoPE progressively disperses these clusters via rotation, and beyond training length the clouds overlap, producing spurious positive dot products that overwhelm the sink token's logit. From this, two necessary criteria for length generalization are derived: (1) a non-trivial lower bound on cluster separation in the limit, and (2) attainment of that bound within the training length. RoPE-ID fulfills both by applying high-frequency RoPE to half the channels (ensuring full rotation within training length, satisfying criterion 2) while leaving the other half unrotated (preserving cluster separation, satisfying criterion 1). A length-dependent temperature scaling factor (1 + 0.1·ln(min(4096,n)))² is applied to attention logits to counteract softmax smoothing over an increasing number of IID key-query pairs. Theoretical analysis via singular value decomposition proves that RoPE shrinks the first singular value by at least √2 and increases stable rank by at least 2× as sequence length grows, formalizing the cluster dispersal mechanism.

## Results

On the RULER long-context benchmark, RoPE-ID with temperature scaling achieves 35.64 (8k) and 30.83 (16k) average accuracy for the 1B model, and 43.39 (8k) and 42.0 (16k) for the 3B model, matching or slightly exceeding YaRN at longer sequences. Standard RoPE and HalfRoPE collapse to near-zero accuracy beyond the 4k training length (e.g., 0.01 and 0.14 at 8k for 1B), while high-frequency RoPE remains stable but poor (7.60 at 8k for 1B). On LongBench, RoPE-ID(scaling) achieves 15.83 (8k) and 15.80 (16k) for 1B, outperforming YaRN (14.54, 14.09), and 17.13 (8k) and 17.94 (16k) for 3B, trailing YaRN (19.29, 19.63) at 3B scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against vanilla RoPE, RoPE-ID(scaling) is a dramatic improvement beyond the 4k training length: 35.64 vs. 0.01 at 8k and 30.83 vs. 0.03 at 16k for the 1B model. Against the high-frequency RoPE baseline (Liu et al., 2024), RoPE-ID(scaling) wins at every sequence length and model size (e.g., 35.64 vs. 7.60 at 8k for 1B), confirming that simply raising all frequencies to complete a full cycle is insufficient. Against HalfRoPE (Chen et al., 2024 / HoPE), RoPE-ID(scaling) is slightly worse at 4k (39.15 vs. 43.07 for 1B) but vastly superior at 8k and 16k (35.64 vs. 0.14; 30.83 vs. 0.00), showing that partial RoPE without high-frequency adjustment still fails to generalize. Against YaRN (Peng et al., 2023), the strongest baseline, RoPE-ID(scaling) is essentially tied at 8k for 1B (35.64 vs. 35.55) and slightly better at 16k (30.83 vs. 30.25), while at 3B it trails YaRN at 8k (43.39 vs. 45.09) but recovers at 16k (42.00 vs. 40.14). Notably, RoPE-ID requires no oracle access to the final sequence length, unlike YaRN which is given the target length when it exceeds the default 4k.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously treated-as-separate threads—RoPE positional encoding, attention sink behavior, and long-context failure—into a single geometric framework. It builds on the context-length extension literature (PI, NTK-aware, YaRN, LongRoPE) and the partial-RoPE idea from HoPE (Chen et al., 2024), but departs by providing a mechanistic explanation for *why* long inputs fail: RoPE disperses tightly clustered key/query point clouds, destroying the geometric separation that enables sink tokens to absorb default attention weight. The two derived criteria (lower-bounded cluster separation in the limit, and attainment of that bound within training length) reframe the design space for positional encodings, positioning RoPE-ID as a training-time solution rather than an inference-time rescaling hack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The geometric unification gives practitioners a principled diagnostic for long-context failure (monitor cluster overlap and sink token weight) and a design template for new positional encodings beyond RoPE-ID. However, the criteria are explicitly necessary but not sufficient—Wang et al. (2024) show high-frequency channels can still drift radially out of distribution, a failure mode RoPE-ID does not address. Empirical validation is limited to 1B and 3B models, and RoPE-ID with temperature scaling is roughly on par with YaRN rather than clearly superior, leaving open whether the geometric advantage persists at frontier scale. The surprising synergy between RoPE-ID and YaRN during 128k fine-tuning hints at complementary mechanisms worth further exploration.
