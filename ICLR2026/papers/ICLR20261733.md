## Problem

Fine-tuning pre-trained diffusion models for downstream alignment is hampered by the recursive denoising chain: full backpropagation through all T steps demands prohibitive memory (~1 TB for SD 1.4 at 50 steps), truncated backpropagation introduces structural bias that causes model collapse, and RL-based estimators (e.g., DDPO) suffer from high gradient variance and poor sample efficiency. No existing method simultaneously achieves unbiasedness, low variance, and practical memory cost for gradient estimation across the full diffusion chain.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained diffusion model (SD 1.4/2.1 for images, VideoCrafter for video) and a text prompt, and produces a fine-tuned model that generates higher-reward images or videos. Training uses LoRA (rank 16) on the U-Net backbone with a reward model (PickScore, HPSv2, AES, or ImageReward) providing the optimization signal; the diffusion chain runs 50 DDIM steps for images and 25 for video, with classifier-free guidance weight 7.5. The HO sub-chain length is fixed at h=2, and the starting index j is sampled per iteration from a categorical distribution weighted by per-step gradient norms.

## Method

The RLR optimizer constructs an unbiased gradient estimator by decomposing the diffusion chain into three segments: a single-step first-order (FO) backpropagation through the reward model at the final step, a randomly positioned h-length half-order (HO) sub-chain that exploits the inherent Gaussian noise z_j via the likelihood-ratio trick to enable local backpropagation without caching all intermediate activations, and zeroth-order (ZO) parameter-perturbation estimators for all remaining steps. The key insight is that the HO term rearranges the computational graph using the DM's own stochastic perturbation rather than extrinsic noise, yielding an unbiased estimator whose variance is strictly lower than pure RL (h=1) or pure ZO under the same memory budget. The sub-chain length h is chosen by solving a closed-form variance-memory tradeoff (h=2 for 30–40 GB budgets), and the starting index j is sampled to capture multi-scale information across the coarse-to-fine generation hierarchy. The paper further introduces Diffusive Chain-of-Thought (DCoT), a prompting technique that decomposes a single prompt into coarse/mid/fine-level descriptions and constrains the HO sub-chain to the time steps corresponding to deficient scales, enabling targeted gradient updates at specific resolution levels.

## Results

On Text2Image (SD 1.4, HPD v2 test set), RLR achieves 21.38 PickScore, 29.22 HPSv2, 6.65 AES, and 76.55 ImageReward, surpassing AlignProp (19.17 / 27.02 / 6.02 / 67.18) and DDPO (17.53 / 22.79 / 5.52 / 52.06) across all four reward models. On SD 2.1 the gap widens further, with RLR reaching 83.07 ImageReward vs. 72.62 for AlignProp. For Text2Video on VBench, RLR attains the highest weighted average score of 84.63, outperforming VADER (83.45), T2V-Turbo (81.96), and all closed-source models (Pika 79.87, Gen-2 79.75).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DDPO (the RL-based baseline), RLR wins decisively on every metric: e.g., 76.55 vs. 52.06 ImageReward and 29.22 vs. 22.79 HPSv2 on SD 1.4, reflecting DDPO's high-variance policy-gradient updates that converge slowly and plateau early. Against AlignProp (randomized truncated backpropagation), RLR improves by 2.21 PickScore, 2.20 HPSv2, and 9.37 ImageReward; the paper attributes AlignProp's ceiling to structural bias in the truncated gradient, which causes model collapse in later training phases (visible in the reward curves of Figure 5). On the video task, RLR outperforms VADER (the BP-based video alignment method) by 1.18 in VBench weighted average, with the largest gap on Dynamic Degree (70.69 vs. 66.94), indicating RLR better preserves motion diversity. RLR also surpasses strong closed-source video models (T2V-Turbo 81.96, Pika 79.87, Gen-2 79.75) on the overall VBench score, while remaining competitive on individual quality metrics like Aesthetic Quality where Gen-2 leads (66.96 vs. 66.15).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the likelihood-ratio gradient estimation literature (Jiang et al. 2024; Peng et al. 2022; Ren et al. 2025) and repurposes it for the recursive denoising chain of diffusion models, introducing a 'Half-Order' (HO) estimator that sits between full backpropagation (first-order) and pure parameter perturbation (zeroth-order). It positions itself against two dominant fine-tuning paradigms—RL-based methods (DDPO) and truncated backpropagation (AlignProp, VADER)—arguing that both suffer from high variance and structural bias, respectively. The key conceptual departure is formulating the choice of estimator at each diffusion step as a constrained variance-minimization problem over a structured design space, yielding the RLR optimizer with a provably unbiased, lower-variance gradient under a fixed memory budget.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The RLR optimizer addresses a practical bottleneck in diffusion model alignment: truncated BP causes model collapse while RL is sample-inefficient, and full BP is infeasible at scale. The unbiasedness guarantee and convergence rate theorem provide theoretical grounding that prior heuristic methods lack, and the ~4.6× memory reduction over AlignProp (22.4 GB vs. 103.9 GB) makes reward-guided fine-tuning accessible on consumer hardware. Open questions include whether the fixed h=2 sub-chain length generalizes to much longer diffusion chains (e.g., 100+ steps in video), whether the stochastic-step requirement (η>0) conflicts with deterministic ODE solvers in production pipelines, and whether the DCoT prompting technique extends beyond the hand-generation task demonstrated here.
