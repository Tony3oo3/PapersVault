## Problem

Prior offline multi-task multi-agent RL methods (ODIS, HiSSD) build on UPDeT-style single-layer transformers that underutilize attention for inter-agent coordination and rely on a single history token, resulting in nearly uniform attention maps and an inability to capture long-horizon temporal dependencies critical under partial observability. This limits generalization to unseen tasks with different agent counts and entity configurations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263286/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent's local observation is decomposed into entity-level tokens (own, other-agent, environment) plus two history tokens (low-level updated every step, high-level updated every T_H steps), forming a variable-length token sequence of length K_a + K_e + 3. The network outputs per-agent Q-values for discrete actions, which are aggregated through a Qatten mixing network into a global Q-value; the policy selects decentralized actions by maximizing the global Q. Token dropout randomly removes entity tokens during training to expose the model to variable-length inputs.

## Method

STAIRS-Former introduces three components: (1) a Spatial Recursive Module with M transformer layers, each applied ν_l times with shared weights, enabling deeper relational reasoning among entities while controlling parameter cost; (2) a Temporal Module maintaining dual-timescale history (a low-level token updated every step and a high-level token updated every T_H steps via a GRU), combined with separate position-wise FFNs for spatial entity tokens versus history tokens (Temporal Focus Layer) to prevent interference between spatial and temporal representations; (3) a token-dropout mechanism that randomly drops entity tokens (except own-entity, history, and action-linked tokens) to improve robustness to unseen agent populations. Training uses a TD3+BC-style objective combining TD loss with behavior-cloning regularization, with the Qatten network handling variable numbers of agents in the global Q aggregation.

## Results

On the SMAC benchmark (averaged over all task sets and dataset qualities), STAIRS-Former achieves a total mean win rate of 67.4%, compared to 57.2% for HiSSD, 37.0% for ODIS, and 23.5% for UPDeT-m. On seen tasks, STAIRS-Former reaches 77.9% (vs. 64.8% for HiSSD), and on unseen tasks 64.0% (vs. 54.7% for HiSSD). On the more challenging SMAC-v2 benchmark, STAIRS-Former achieves a total mean of 30.3%, outperforming HiSSD (24.4%), ODIS (11.5%), and UPDeT-m (7.4%). On suboptimal datasets (Medium, Medium-Expert, Medium-Replay), STAIRS-Former improves average performance over HiSSD by 39.5%, 36.6%, and 40.5% respectively on Marine-Hard and Stalker-Zealot.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STAIRS-Former consistently outperforms all three baselines across every benchmark and dataset quality. Against HiSSD (the prior state-of-the-art), STAIRS-Former achieves a 10.2-point absolute gain on seen SMAC tasks (77.9% vs. 64.8%) and a 9.3-point gain on unseen tasks (64.0% vs. 54.7%), with the largest relative improvement (48.6%) on the heterogeneous Stalker-Zealot task set. On suboptimal datasets where HiSSD degrades most, STAIRS-Former's advantage widens to 36–40% relative improvement. Against ODIS, STAIRS-Former leads by 30.4 points on seen tasks and 31.7 points on unseen tasks, reflecting ODIS's weaker handling of partial observability and long-horizon dependencies. UPDeT-m, the simplest baseline, trails by over 40 points on seen tasks, confirming that the recursive spatial-temporal architecture is essential. On SMAC-v2, where stochasticity is higher, STAIRS-Former still leads HiSSD by 5.9 points (30.3% vs. 24.4%) while using fewer than one-third of HiSSD's parameters (220K vs. 679K) and roughly half the training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends UPDeT's transformer-based scalable architecture for MARL by identifying its core limitations—single-layer depth and a single history token that reduces to simple RNN processing—and replacing them with a recursive spatial transformer, a dual-timescale temporal module (step-wise history plus periodic GRU updates), and token dropout. It departs from the prior state-of-the-art offline MT-MARL methods ODIS and HiSSD, which adopt UPDeT-style single-layer transformers with hierarchical skill learning, by arguing that the transformer's attention mechanism is fundamentally underutilized for inter-agent coordination and long-horizon temporal reasoning. The Qatten mixing network and TD3+BC training objective are carried over from prior work as the Q-decomposition and offline regularization components, respectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STAIRS-Former provides a principled architectural solution to two persistent challenges in offline MT-MARL: capturing long-range temporal dependencies under partial observability and generalizing to unseen tasks with different agent counts. The attention map visualizations showing emergent tactical behaviors (focus fire, kiting, adaptive retreat) add interpretability that prior methods lack. However, the evaluation is confined to SMAC-family and MPE/MaMuJoCo benchmarks with QMIX-generated datasets, and all methods—including STAIRS-Former—struggle on highly asymmetric tasks (e.g., 10m_vs_12m, 13m_vs_15m), suggesting fundamental limits of offline learning in extreme distributional shifts. Open questions include whether the spatial-temporal hierarchy scales to continuous action spaces, very large agent populations, or real-world robotic settings.
