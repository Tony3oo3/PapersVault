## Problem

Existing concept erasure methods are designed for diffusion models and rely on aligning predicted noise across timesteps, making them inapplicable to Visual Autoregressive (VAR) models that predict discrete visual tokens at progressively larger scales. Directly porting these methods to VAR causes cumulative errors across scales and severe image quality collapse, leaving a critical safety gap in the rapidly growing family of VAR-based text-to-image generators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261194/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (either containing the target concept c* or a neutral replacement c) and produces a 1024×1024 RGB image via the Infinity-2B VAR model, which predicts binary spherical quantization (BSQ) tokens at K hierarchical scales. Training uses 50 LLM-generated prompt pairs and fine-tunes only the cross-attention and FFN modules in bf16 precision on a single A6000 GPU over 500 iterations, making the procedure lightweight relative to retraining.

## Method

The paper proposes VARE, a framework that feeds auxiliary visual tokens (generated by the frozen original model under the neutral prompt c) as additional inputs to the visual transformer, preventing the cross-scale error accumulation that plagues naive adaptation of diffusion-based erasure losses. On top of VARE, S-VARE introduces a filtered cross-entropy loss (L_FCE) that replaces MSE with bit-wise binary cross-entropy matching Infinity's BSQ prediction space, and applies a two-level filter (bit-level threshold γ = −log(1/2) and token-level threshold α = 25%) to exclude already-correct tokens from the loss, achieving surgical rather than global adjustment. A preservation loss (L_Pre) computed as KL divergence between the fine-tuned and original model outputs under the neutral prompt c maintains generative diversity and prevents language drift. The final objective is L_FCE + L_Pre with equal weights, and the approach is validated across NSFW, object, and style erasure tasks plus adversarial robustness benchmarks.

## Results

On NSFW erasure, S-VARE reduces sensitive content from 158 to 5 (97% reduction) while preserving 57 common content items (vs. 2–34 for all baselines), with CLIP score dropping only 0.4 points (31.7→31.3) and FID increasing by just 1.7 (31.1→32.8). For object erasure on Imagenette, the method achieves 4.4% target-class accuracy (ACCe) while retaining 75.7% irrelevant-class accuracy (ACCi), nearly matching the original model's 76.0%. Across all three erasure types (NSFW, object, style), S-VARE consistently achieves the best or near-best erasure while maintaining the closest FID and CLIP scores to the unmodified model, confirming surgical precision with minimal generative degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S-VARE decisively outperforms all six adapted diffusion-based baselines on the critical preservation metrics. Against UCE, which achieves almost no erasure (Sen=122 vs. 5) and severely degrades CLIP (26.4 vs. 31.3), S-VARE is superior on every metric. Compared to ESD-x (the strongest object-erasure baseline with ACCe=3.8%), S-VARE trades a marginal 0.6% in erasure strength for a massive 17.6% gain in irrelevant-class preservation (ACCi 75.7% vs. 58.1%) and a 3.4-point FID improvement. Against FMN and AC-M, which achieve moderate erasure (Sen=22, 20) but destroy common content (Com=8, 10), S-VARE preserves 57 common items—nearly matching the original model's 112. On style erasure, S-VARE achieves the lowest detection rate (8.2%) with the best CLIP (31.5), while all baselines produce visible artifacts and 1–2 point CLIP drops. The only metric where a baseline edges out S-VARE is object ACCe (ESD-x: 3.8% vs. 4.4%), but this comes at the cost of substantially worse generative quality across all other measures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the concept erasure (CE) paradigm—established for diffusion models via methods like ESD, FMN, and UCE—to the visual autoregressive (VAR) family, specifically the Infinity model with binary spherical quantization. The key departure is recognizing that diffusion CE losses (MSE on predicted noise) are fundamentally incompatible with VAR's discrete, autoregressive, next-scale token prediction, leading to cumulative error across scales and image collapse. The authors introduce the VARE framework (auxiliary visual tokens to anchor generation) and S-VARE (filtered cross-entropy + KL-divergence preservation loss) as the first CE method tailored to VAR's probability-space, bit-wise prediction paradigm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As VAR-based models like Infinity become the leading architecture for high-resolution text-to-image generation, the absence of effective safety mechanisms is a critical deployment gap; this paper closes it with a practical, lightweight fine-tuning recipe (50 prompt pairs, 500 iterations, single A6000). However, limitations remain: evaluation is confined to Infinity-2B, adversarial robustness is modest (ASR still 7.4% on Ring-A-Bell), and multi-concept erasure shows measurable degradation. Open questions include whether the filtered CE and auxiliary-token strategy generalize to other VAR architectures (e.g., those without BSQ), whether the approach scales to video generation, and whether the 25% filter threshold is truly architecture-agnostic.
