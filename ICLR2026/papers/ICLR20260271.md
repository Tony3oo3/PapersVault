## Problem

Previous studies of Theory of Mind in LMs rely on behavioral evaluations that cannot reveal the internal mechanisms by which models encode and manipulate belief representations. This paper asks whether LMs learn a systematic, compositional solution to belief tracking or merely exploit superficial statistical associations, and if systematic, what that solution looks like at the level of internal activations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260271/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes structured stories (two characters each performing an action on a distinct object, optionally with explicit visibility conditions) paired with a belief question, and produces the correct belief answer (a state token or 'unknown'). Crucially, the analysis also yields layer-localized identifications of pointers, addresses, and payloads within the residual stream, along with low-rank subspaces (via SVD + DCM) that encode Ordering IDs for each entity. All experiments require paired original/counterfactual inputs to enable interchange interventions, and results are reported per-layer across 80 matched pairs.

## Method

The paper discovers a pervasive 'lookback' mechanism in which a source reference (an Ordering ID) is copied via attention into an address in a recalled token's residual stream and a pointer in a later lookback token's residual stream; when needed, the pointer forms a QK-circuit with the address, and the OV-circuit retrieves a payload stored alongside the address. Three lookbacks compose to perform belief tracking: a binding lookback that links character-object-state triples via OIs, an answer lookback that dereferences the state OI to fetch the state token value, and a visibility lookback that uses a Visibility ID to incorporate an observed character's knowledge into the observer's belief state. The hypothesized high-level causal model is verified through interchange interventions on both full residual streams and DCM-identified subspaces, with alignment quantified by interchange intervention accuracy measured at every layer, revealing a clear temporal progression from OI encoding (layers 20–34) through binding (33–38) to answer retrieval (52–56).

## Results

On the CausalToM dataset, the three high-performing models analyzed mechanistically achieve strong behavioral accuracy: Llama-3-70B-Instruct reaches 95.2% (no visibility) and 92.3% (visibility), Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct reaches 96.2% and 91.2%, and Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct reaches 88.3% and 97.0%. Mechanistically, the paper localizes the lookback mechanisms to specific layer ranges: source reference OIs appear in layers 20–34, binding address/payload in layers 33–38, the answer pointer in layers 34–52, and the answer payload in layers 52–56. For the visibility lookback, the visibility ID source is encoded in layers 10–23, the address/pointer split occurs in layers 24–31, and the payload is present after layer 31. All localization results are confirmed via interchange intervention accuracy (IIA) on both full residual streams and DCM-identified subspaces across n=80 samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a mechanistic interpretability paper rather than a method comparison study, so it does not propose a new method to benchmark against baselines. Instead, it compares behavioral performance across 15 LM families (7B–405B) on CausalToM to identify which models reliably solve the task and are suitable for mechanistic analysis. The key behavioral finding is a sharp divide: smaller models (7B–13B) largely fail the visibility condition (e.g., Qwen2.5-7B drops from 28.0% to 6.1%), while the three analyzed models (Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct, Llama-3-70B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-405B-Instruct) all exceed 80% in both conditions. The paper further demonstrates that the discovered lookback mechanism generalizes to the BigToM dataset (Appendix M), confirming it is not an artifact of the simplified CausalToM stories. The mechanistic findings are consistent across all three models and across FP32, FP16, and INT8 precisions, suggesting the lookback pattern is a robust architectural property rather than a model-specific quirk.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability (QK/OV circuits, residual stream framework from Elhage et al. 2021), causal abstraction via interchange interventions (Geiger et al. 2021, 2024; Vig et al. 2020), and entity tracking / variable binding in LMs (Ordering IDs from Dai et al. 2024; DCM-based subspace identification from Davies et al. 2023 and Prakash et al. 2024). Prior ToM research in LMs has been almost entirely behavioral—benchmarks measuring accuracy on belief questions—while prior mechanistic work has focused on simpler tasks like entity tracking or factual recall. This paper departs from both by applying causal abstraction to a ToM task for the first time, constructing CausalToM specifically to provide the counterfactual pairs that existing ToM benchmarks lack, and discovering a new named computational pattern (the lookback mechanism) that unifies binding, answer retrieval, and visibility reasoning under a single pointer-dereference abstraction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first end-to-end mechanistic account of how LMs track character beliefs, moving the field from 'can LMs pass ToM tests?' to 'what algorithm do they run to do so?' The lookback mechanism is described as pervasive and potentially foundational for in-context reasoning more broadly, suggesting it may generalize beyond ToM to other multi-step retrieval tasks. Key limitations: the analysis is restricted to simple two-character stories with 80 samples, the visibility lookback payload remains undetermined (only speculated to encode the observed character's OI), and generalization to the BigToM benchmark is explicitly preliminary. Open questions include whether the mechanism scales to multi-character, multi-turn, or higher-order belief scenarios, and whether the lookback pattern appears in non-ToM in-context reasoning tasks.
