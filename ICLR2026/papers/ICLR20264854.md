## Problem

Constant step-size SGD converges only to a neighborhood of the solution with O(γ) bias. Two classical heuristics—random reshuffling (RR₁) and Richardson-Romberg extrapolation (RR₂)—each reduce this bias in isolation, but their interaction in structured non-monotone variational inequalities remained unexplored: reshuffling introduces a biased, permutation-driven noise structure that invalidates standard extrapolation analyses, leaving open whether their composition can achieve a strictly better O(γ³) bias guarantee.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a finite-sum variational inequality defined by n component operators F_i: ℝ^d → ℝ^d, an initial point x₀, and a base step size γ. At each epoch it draws a random permutation of [n] and runs two parallel inner loops (step sizes γ and 2γ) over the full dataset, then produces a single extrapolated iterate x̂ = 2x_γ − x_{2γ} at the epoch boundary. The final output is either the last-iterate extrapolated value or a Polyak-Ruppert average across epochs, both converging to the VIP solution x* with O(γ³) asymptotic bias.

## Method

The algorithm (SGD-RR₂⊕RR₁) composes random reshuffling as the inner-loop data ordering with Richardson-Romberg extrapolation as the outer-loop debiasing step. The key technical challenge is that reshuffling produces a systematically biased, discrete, permutation-dependent oracle that breaks the unbiased-oracle assumptions underlying prior extrapolation analyses. To overcome this, the authors introduce a calibrated Gaussian perturbation that smooths the discrete noise while preserving variance and bias order, then prove the epoch-level iterates form a time-homogeneous Markov chain with a unique invariant distribution, geometric ergodicity, and associated LLN/CLT. A spectral tensor analysis of the full-pass reshuffling operator (connecting it to multi-step extragradient methods) combined with a new combinatorial fourth-moment bound for finite-sum subsets shows that the O(γ) bias term cancels under extrapolation, yielding the first provable O(γ³) bias for constant-step-size methods in quasi-strongly monotone VIPs.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 4.6) establishes that the combined SGD-RR₂⊕RR₁ method achieves an asymptotic bias of O(γ³) for quasi-strongly monotone VIPs, improving upon O(γ) for vanilla SGD, O(γ) for RR₁ alone, and O(γ^{3/2}) for RR₂ alone. The MSE remains O(γ²), matching the best known reshuffling guarantees. Empirically, on two-player zero-sum games with condition numbers κ ∈ {1, 5, 10}, the combined method converges to a strictly smaller neighborhood than any individual heuristic (vanilla SGDA, RR₁-only, or RR₂-only), with the gap widening as the step size increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares four variants on two-player zero-sum games: vanilla SGDA (with-replacement), SGDA+RR₁, SGDA+RR₂, and the combined SGDA+RR₂⊕RR₁. The combined method consistently converges to the smallest relative error neighborhood, outperforming all three baselines across all tested condition numbers (κ = 1, 5, 10). Against Emmanouilidis et al. (2024), which applies RR₁ to the Stochastic Extragradient (SEG) method, the proposed approach uses SGDA (a simpler, more practical algorithm), achieves a strictly better bias order (O(γ³) vs. O(γ+γ³)), and preserves the condition number of vanilla SGDA rather than degrading it. Against Dieuleveut et al. (2020) and Vlatakis-Gkaragkounis et al. (2024), which analyze RR₂ extrapolation for constant-step SGD and VIPs respectively (achieving O(γ^{3/2}) bias), the combined method improves the bias to O(γ³) by leveraging the reshuffling-induced structure to cancel additional lower-order terms. The CLT experiments (Theorem 4.4) further validate that the epoch-level iterates concentrate around the solution with Gaussian fluctuations, confirming the Markov chain analysis.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously studied-in-isolation heuristics for constant step-size SGD: random reshuffling (RR₁), which sharpens MSE from O(γ) to O(γ²) (Ahn et al., 2020; Emmanouilidis et al., 2024), and Richardson-Romberg extrapolation (RR₂), which reduces bias from O(γ) to O(γ^{3/2}) (Dieuleveut et al., 2020; Vlatakis-Gkaragkounis et al., 2024). The key departure is analyzing their composition under the biased, permutation-driven oracle that reshuffling induces—a setting not covered by prior extrapolation analyses that assume unbiased or continuously distributed noise. The Markov chain framework for epoch-level iterates (building on Meyn & Tweedie's continuous-state theory) and the spectral tensor analysis of the reshuffling operator are new technical contributions that enable the combined analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The O(γ³) bias guarantee is the first provable cubic refinement for constant-step stochastic methods in structured non-monotone VIPs, closing a gap between the O(γ) of vanilla SGD and the O(γ²)/O(γ^{3/2}) of individual heuristics. However, the result requires quasi-strong monotonicity (excluding fully adversarial GANs or multi-agent RL with rotational dynamics) and a Gaussian smoothing perturbation whose practical necessity and dataset-size dependence remain unclear. Open questions include extending the analysis to Minty or hypomonotone operators, removing the smoothing step, and improving the condition number to match SEG-level guarantees while retaining the cubic bias.
