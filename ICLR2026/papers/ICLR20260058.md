## Problem

In precision oncology, genomics data is often unavailable at inference due to high acquisition cost, forcing models to reconstruct molecular features from histopathology images alone. Existing reconstruction-based methods operate on individual cases in isolation, ignoring inter-case diagnostic relevance, and fail to exploit the authentic genomics data of diagnostically related training cases that remain accessible during inference.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes only a single H&E-stained WSI (20× magnification, cropped into 256×256 patches encoded by GigaPath ViT-giant into 1536-dim patch features, then aggregated by a LongNet slide encoder into a 768-dim [CLS] token). During training, paired bulk gene expression profiles (4,241 genes grouped into 50 functional sets, each encoded to 768-dim via SNN) are also available. Outputs include a continuous survival risk score (via discrete hazard functions over quartile-binned time intervals), binary cancer stage labels, EGFR mutation status, and HER2 status, evaluated with C-Index, AUC, and F1.

## Method

MSRL is a two-stage framework. In pre-training, a self-supervised Graph Structure Learner (GSL) is trained on 6,361 TCGA pan-cancer cases using three InfoNCE contrastive losses (intra-modality, inter-modality, and fused-modality) to learn a multi-modal representation graph that captures inter-case relevance across both histopathology and genomics spaces. In fine-tuning, a dual-branch architecture is employed: an online branch processes WSI-only input (using an Inductor SNN to generate genomics prompts) while a target branch (updated via EMA) processes complete multi-modal data; a FIFO buffer stores authentic multi-modal representations from training cases. During inference, the pre-trained GSL dynamically constructs a graph connecting the current test case to buffered training cases, and the graph forward pass (GSL → GCN) completes the missing genomics information through structural relevance, with hierarchical alignment losses (feature, graph, and sparsity-balanced structure alignment) ensuring the online branch faithfully reconstructs authentic molecular information.

## Results

On five TCGA survival prediction tasks (7,263 cases total), MSRL with WSI-only inference achieves an overall C-Index of 0.6558, outperforming the best uni-modal WSI method TITAN (0.6007) by 5.51% and the best missing-modality method DisPro (0.6414) by 1.44%. The multi-modal variant MSRL_multi reaches 0.6794, surpassing the strongest multi-modal fusion baseline DisPro_multi (0.6691) by 1.03%. On four precision diagnosis tasks, MSRL achieves the best AUC/F1 across all tasks, e.g., 0.842/0.770 on EGFR mutation prediction and 0.730/0.606 on HER2 status, outperforming the second-best LD-CVAE by up to 1.9% in F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MSRL (WSI-only inference, C-Index 0.6558) outperforms all uni-modal WSI baselines: TITAN (0.6007, +5.51%), PANTHER (0.5967, +5.91%), GigaPath (0.5954), and TransMIL (0.5686). Among missing-modality methods, MSRL beats DisPro (0.6414, +1.44%), LD-CVAE (0.6313, +2.45%), and G-HANet (0.6246, +3.12%), with the paper attributing DisPro's gap to its reliance on per-case self-scores that ignore inter-case relationships. Against multi-modal fusion methods that require genomics at inference, MSRL surpasses MCAT (0.6455, +1.03%) and CMTA (0.6547, +0.11%) while using only WSI at test time, and its multi-modal variant MSRL_multi (0.6794) exceeds DisPro_multi (0.6691, +1.03%) and SurvPath (0.6683, +1.11%). On precision diagnosis tasks, MSRL leads all baselines in AUC and F1, e.g., outperforming LD-CVAE by 1.8%/1.1% on BRCA staging and 1.3%/1.9% on HER2 status. On external CPTAC datasets, MSRL shows minimal performance drop (−1.02% on HNSC, −1.62% on BRCA) versus G-HANet (−5.32%/−7.08%) and LD-CVAE (−5.42%/−7.32%), confirming superior generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the graph structure learning (GSL) paradigm—originally developed for unsupervised graph topology refinement (Liu et al., 2022; Li et al., 2023) and recently applied to multiplex graphs (Shen et al., 2024)—to the histopathology-genomics multi-modal domain. It departs from prior missing-modality reconstruction methods (G-HANet, LD-CVAE, DisPro) that rely solely on intra-case WSI-to-genomics mapping, instead framing genomics recovery as an inter-case structural inference problem where a pre-trained multi-modal graph encodes diagnostic relevance across patients. The buffer mechanism, which injects authentic genomics representations from training cases into the inference-time graph, is a key architectural departure from purely generative or distillation-based approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling data-efficient precision oncology when genomics data is unavailable at inference—a common clinical constraint—while achieving performance comparable to full multi-modal fusion. The notably small generalization drop (1.02% vs. 4–7% for reconstruction baselines) on external CPTAC data suggests the inter-case graph strategy is more robust to distribution shift than per-case genomics synthesis. Open questions include whether the joint node-structure optimization can escape local optima more reliably, whether incorporating clinical text would strengthen structural representations, and whether the approach generalizes beyond TCGA's demographic and center composition.
