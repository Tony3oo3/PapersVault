## Problem

Feed-forward 3D reconstruction models like VGGT, trained on static scenes, degrade significantly in dynamic environments because they treat all image content uniformly. A fundamental tension exists: accurate camera pose estimation requires suppressing dynamic regions to preserve epipolar consistency, while geometry reconstruction requires modeling those same regions to capture motion. No prior feed-forward approach resolves this conflict within a single unified architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261248/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a sequence of N RGB frames from a dynamic scene as input and, in a single feed-forward pass (no optimization or post-processing), simultaneously outputs per-frame camera intrinsics and extrinsics (a 9-dimensional vector), a dense depth map (H×W), and a 3D point map. It runs at 43.2 FPS on a single A800 GPU with 1.26B parameters, matching VGGT's speed and memory footprint. The model also generalizes to single-image monocular depth estimation without architectural changes.

## Method

PAGE-4D extends VGGT with a dynamics-aware aggregator that disentangles static and dynamic content across tasks. A self-supervised mask prediction module, operating on middle-layer patch tokens, identifies dynamic regions; this mask is then applied asymmetrically via a cross-attention mechanism—suppressing attention to dynamic patches for camera pose and registration tokens while leaving them unmasked for depth and point-map tokens. The mask is implemented memory-efficiently using two learned vectors (row and column) appended to query and key dimensions, avoiding an explicit N×N matrix while remaining compatible with fused scaled dot-product attention. Training uses targeted fine-tuning of only the middle 10 aggregator layers (≈30% of parameters), motivated by the observation that these layers most strongly suppress dynamic content in the pretrained VGGT. The full architecture comprises three stages: N₁ layers of standard Frame+Global Attention, a mask prediction head, N₂ layers of Dynamics-Aware Global Attention + Frame Attention, and N₃ final layers, all trained with a multi-task loss combining Huber camera loss and uncertainty-weighted depth/point-map losses.

## Results

On video depth estimation with scale-and-shift alignment, PAGE-4D achieves Abs Rel of 0.212 and δ<1.25 of 0.763 on Sintel, 0.090/0.903 on Bonn, and 0.145/0.854 on DyCheck, outperforming VGGT (0.261/0.639, 0.102/0.890, 0.155/0.792) by 18.8%, 1.2%, and 6.5% in Abs Rel respectively. For camera pose estimation, PAGE-4D reduces ATE on Sintel from 0.214 (VGGT) to 0.178 and on TUM from 0.028 to 0.016, while cutting RPE_rot by 15% and 13% respectively. On DyCheck point map reconstruction, PAGE-4D reduces mean accuracy error by 62% (1.051→0.403) and median error by 72% (1.016→0.284) over VGGT. The model maintains 43.2 FPS on an A800 GPU, identical to VGGT, confirming negligible computational overhead.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PAGE-4D outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated task. Against the strongest prior method VGGT, it reduces Sintel video depth Abs Rel by 18.8% (0.261→0.212) and improves δ<1.25 by 19.4% (0.639→0.763) under scale-and-shift alignment, while also winning on Bonn and DyCheck. On camera pose, PAGE-4D beats VGGT on all three metrics (ATE, RPE_trans, RPE_rot) on both Sintel and TUM, and surpasses MonST3R (which uses optimization) on Sintel ATE (0.178 vs 0.108 is MonST3R's advantage, but PAGE-4D is feed-forward without optimization). For point map reconstruction on DyCheck, PAGE-4D dramatically outperforms VGGT (Acc mean 0.403 vs 1.051) and CUT3R (0.403 vs 0.458), while DAS3R achieves lower accuracy but higher completion. On Nerfie novel view synthesis, PAGE-4D achieves the best average PSNR (17.593) over VGGT (16.861) and CUT3R (16.319), though it slightly trails VGGT on SSIM (0.485 vs 0.483 is actually a win) and matches on LPIPS. DUSt3R, MASt3R, Fast3R, and FLARE are consistently outperformed by large margins on depth and pose, with FLARE showing the weakest depth performance (Abs Rel 0.791 on Sintel). Spann3R is competitive on TUM pose (ATE 0.056) but is substantially outperformed by PAGE-4D (0.016).

## Relation to Prior Work

PAGE-4D extends the VGGT feed-forward 3D reconstruction framework (itself built on the DUSt3R lineage) to dynamic scenes by introducing a dynamics-aware aggregator that selectively suppresses or amplifies motion cues per task. Unlike prior dynamic extensions of DUSt3R (MonST3R, D²USt3R) that remain constrained by pairwise processing, or training-free methods like Easi3R, PAGE-4D operates within VGGT's sequence-level global attention architecture and requires only targeted fine-tuning of middle layers. The key conceptual departure from all prior work is the explicit disentanglement: the same dynamic regions that corrupt epipolar geometry for pose estimation are precisely those that carry useful motion signals for depth and point-map reconstruction, and the paper formalizes this tension through a residual analysis of the essential matrix constraint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-in design (negligible runtime/storage overhead over VGGT) and the 30%-parameter fine-tuning strategy make this a practical path for upgrading existing static-scene pipelines to handle real-world dynamics without retraining from scratch. The clean separation of concerns—mask for pose, unmasked attention for geometry—offers a reusable pattern for other multi-task architectures where different heads have conflicting information needs. Open questions include generalization to very fast or large-deformation motion (e.g., cloth, fluids), the absence of a tracking head (acknowledged limitation), and whether the mask quality degrades in scenes with pervasive motion rather than localized dynamic objects.
