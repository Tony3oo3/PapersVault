## Problem

While Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) have been adapted to equivariant settings for a few specific data types (graphs, sets, images), no principled general framework exists for constructing equivariant or invariant KA layers under arbitrary permutation symmetry groups. Prior equivariant KANs were ad hoc constructions that did not unify or extend to important symmetry types such as direct-product groups, high-order tensors, or variable-sized inputs.

## Input / Output

The architecture consumes structured inputs with permutation symmetry: sets of n noisy 1D signals (each T=100 time steps), 3D point clouds of n points with d=3 coordinates, user-item rating matrices of size n×m, and k-order tensors with diagonal group action. A key design property is that the layers handle variable-sized inputs (e.g., sets and graphs of different cardinalities) through sum-pooling aggregation, and support multiple feature channels per element. Outputs are class labels (signal type, 3D object category), predicted ratings, or scalar invariant function values.

## Method

FS-KAN replaces the scalar weight-sharing constraint of standard equivariant linear layers with a function-sharing constraint on KA layers: for a group G ≤ S_n, the univariate functions satisfy φ_{q,p} = φ_{σ(q),σ(p)} for all σ ∈ G, meaning functions are tied together according to the group's orbits on index pairs. The paper proves this restriction is without loss of generality—every G-equivariant KA layer can be exactly represented by an FS-KA layer—by showing that equivariance forces functions within the same orbit to differ only by a constant that cancels in the summation. An efficient variant commutes sum-pooling with the shared function application (e.g., for S_n, computing Φ₁(x_q) + Φ₂(Σ_p x_p) instead of applying Φ independently to each element), reducing nonlinear function evaluations while preserving equivariance. Theoretically, the paper establishes that FS-KANs and parameter-sharing MLPs have equivalent expressive power in the uniform approximation sense, allowing direct transfer of known universality and expressivity results (e.g., k-WL discriminative power for graph FS-KANs, universality for set-invariant functions) to the KAN setting.

## Results

In the low-data regime (200 training samples) on point cloud classification, FS-KAN achieves 53.1% accuracy on task A and 42.0% average accuracy, outperforming DeepSets (51.4% and 38.0%) by a wide margin, while also exhibiting lower catastrophic forgetting (0.034 vs. 0.059). On the full ModelNet40 dataset (high-data regime), FS-KAN reaches 83.7% test accuracy, comparable to DeepSets (84.4%) and Point Transformer (84.0%), while the non-invariant KAN baseline collapses to 23.2%. For signal classification with S_n × C_T symmetry, FS-KAN consistently outperforms the DSS baseline in the 60–1200 sample range, with the efficient variant achieving even higher accuracy at 1.4× faster training. In semi-supervised rating prediction, FS-KAN achieves lower RMSE than SSEM across all low-data fractions on MovieLens-100K, Flixster, Douban, and Yahoo Music.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepSets (Zaheer et al., 2017), FS-KAN achieves a clear advantage in the low-data regime: at 200 training samples, FS-KAN reaches 42.0% average continual-learning accuracy versus 38.0% for DeepSets, and the gap narrows to near-parity at 1000 samples (55.3% vs. 55.5%). Against Point Transformer (Zhao et al., 2021), FS-KAN is comparable in the high-data regime (83.7% vs. 84.0% on full ModelNet40) but superior in low-data settings where the transformer's attention mechanism requires more samples to learn effectively. Against SSEM (Hartford et al., 2018) on rating prediction, FS-KAN consistently achieves lower RMSE across all low-data fractions on all four datasets (MovieLens-100K, Flixster, Douban, Yahoo Music), with the gap shrinking as data increases. Against DSS (Maron et al., 2020) on signal classification, FS-KAN outperforms both the full and parameter-matched DSS in the 60–1200 sample range, while the efficient FS-KAN variant trains 1.4× faster than the full FS-KAN. Against the non-invariant standard KAN (Liu et al., 2024), FS-KAN dramatically outperforms: on full ModelNet40, FS-KAN achieves 83.7% accuracy versus only 23.2% for the non-equivariant KAN, confirming that symmetry-aware design is essential for structured data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and generalizes several previously proposed equivariant KAN architectures—PointNet-KAN for sets (Kashefi, 2024), ConvKAN for images (Bodner et al., 2024), and Graph-KAN (Kiamari et al., 2024)—into a single principled framework (FS-KAN) that handles arbitrary permutation symmetry groups via function sharing. The construction directly generalizes the classical parameter-sharing schemes of Ravanbakhsh et al. (2017) and Maron et al. (2019b) from scalar weights to univariate functions, while the expressivity analysis builds on Wang et al. (2024b)'s result that ReLU-MLP layers can be exactly implemented by two KA layers. Unlike Hu et al. (2025), which handles continuous groups via numerical solving, FS-KAN provides a closed-form sharing structure that naturally accommodates variable-sized inputs like sets and graphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The expressivity equivalence between FS-KANs and parameter-sharing MLPs (Propositions 6–7) is the key theoretical contribution: it allows all existing universality and expressivity results for equivariant networks (DeepSets universality, k-WL discriminative power, etc.) to transfer directly to their FS-KAN counterparts without re-derivation. Practically, FS-KANs demonstrate meaningful data-efficiency gains in low-data regimes across multiple domains, making them attractive when labeled data is scarce. However, the computational overhead remains substantial even with the efficient variant (4× slower than MLP baselines in some settings), and the paper acknowledges that generalization theory, optimization landscape, and scalability of FS-KANs remain open. Faster implementations and understanding when the data-efficiency advantage persists at scale are important next steps.
