## Problem

Existing RAG watermarking methods protect only textual knowledge, leaving image knowledge in multimodal RAG-as-a-Service platforms entirely unprotected. The core difficulty is that image watermarks must survive indirect cross-modal propagation—from an image retriever to a textual generator—while remaining explicitly retrievable through text queries, a requirement fundamentally different from traditional pixel-level image watermarking.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264517/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The defender injects a small set of synthetic watermarked images (e.g., 10–50) into a large image knowledge base (58K images in MMQA, 389K in WebQA) alongside original content. Verification is performed by issuing text probe queries, each composed of a retrieval trigger (to pull the watermark image via CLIP/SigLIP) and a generation instruction (to elicit the watermark signal in text); the generator's output is checked via normalized substring matching against a predefined signature. Detection is confirmed statistically through Welch's t-test comparing the suspect system's Verification Success Rate against a clean reference distribution, yielding a p-value.

## Method

AQUA embeds semantic watermark signals into synthetic images using two complementary strategies that survive cross-modal transformation. AQUA_acronym places uncommon acronyms paired with invented full names (e.g., 'UGP → Unicorn Grammar Parser') into images, relying on the VLM's OCR ability to decode them into detectable textual responses; the full name acts as a secret key unknown to the generator. AQUA_spatial generates images depicting unusual spatial configurations (e.g., 'a red apple on the head of a reading dog') via diffusion models, leveraging the VLM's spatial reasoning to answer position-related probe queries. Both methods encode the watermark at the semantic level rather than the pixel level, ensuring the signal persists through the image-to-text pipeline while maintaining high retrieval rates and visual naturalness. Verification uses a strict substring-match protocol on normalized outputs, with multiple watermark images and varied probe queries to handle generation stochasticity, and Welch's t-test to establish statistical significance of detection.

## Results

On MMQA and WebQA, AQUA_acronym achieves near-perfect retrieval (Rank ≈ 1.03–1.05) and high Conditional Generation Success Rate (CGSR 75.28%–99.61%) across four VLM generators, with p-values below 10⁻¹²⁸ in all settings. AQUA_spatial achieves Rank ≈ 1.29–1.85 and CGSR 68.46%–98.42%, with p-values below 10⁻²⁶. In terms of query efficiency, both AQUA variants reach statistical significance (p < 0.05) within ~30 probe queries, whereas the optimization-based baseline requires over 200 queries. The Naive baseline fails to produce statistically significant detection (p > 0.25) on all models and datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares AQUA against two baselines: a Naive method that uses randomly crawled common images as watermarks, and an optimization-based (Opt.) method that embeds imperceptible adversarial perturbations via PGD to distill a target phrase into the image. The Naive baseline fails entirely, yielding p-values above 0.25 (not statistically significant) and CGSR as low as 5.71% (Qwen-VL-Chat on WebQA), confirming that common images cannot serve as reliable watermark carriers. The Opt. baseline achieves marginal statistical significance (p ≈ 10⁻³) but with substantially lower CGSR (18.51%–31.03%) and requires over 200 queries to reach the significance threshold, compared to ~30 queries for AQUA. AQUA_acronym dominates on all metrics: it achieves the lowest Rank (≈1.03), the highest CGSR (75–99%), and p-values orders of magnitude smaller than Opt. (e.g., 10⁻²⁸⁹ vs. 10⁻³ for InternVL3 on MMQA). AQUA_spatial is slightly weaker on Rank (1.29–1.85) but still far outperforms both baselines on CGSR and statistical significance, and it provides a fallback for VLMs with limited OCR capability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RAG watermarking line of work (WARD, RAG-WM, RAG°, DMI-RAG), which has been exclusively text-focused, to the multimodal setting where image knowledge must be watermarked and detected through textual outputs. It departs from traditional image watermarking (distortion-agnostic deep watermarking, adversarial perturbation methods) by rejecting pixel-level implicit perturbations in favor of semantic-level cues (acronyms, spatial configurations) that survive cross-modal transformation from image retrieval to text generation. The framework repurposes VLM capabilities—OCR for AQUA_acronym and spatial reasoning for AQUA_spatial—as the decoding mechanism, rather than treating the generator as a black box to be optimized against.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As RAG-as-a-Service platforms (e.g., LlamaIndex) increasingly integrate multimodal knowledge bases under 'usable but not visible' policies, the absence of image copyright protection is a critical and growing vulnerability. AQUA provides a practical, black-box-compatible solution requiring only ~30 queries for statistical detection, making it deployable in real RaaS ecosystems. Key limitations include its restriction to T2T Multimodal RAG (text query → text output), leaving image-to-image or video modalities unaddressed, and its dependence on VLM OCR/spatial understanding quality, which may shift as models evolve. Open questions include multi-provider collision management at scale, generalization to other modalities, and long-term robustness as VLM architectures change.
