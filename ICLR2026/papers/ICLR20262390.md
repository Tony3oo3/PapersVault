## Problem

Continuous normalizing flows (CNFs) used as Boltzmann Generators require thousands of function evaluations per sample to compute likelihoods accurately enough for importance sampling, severely limiting their scalability to larger molecular systems. Few-step flow models (consistency models, flow maps) enable rapid generation but do not natively guarantee invertibility before convergence, making efficient likelihood computation inapplicable. The paper asks: can one design a flow-based generator that combines the training efficiency and architectural freedom of simulation-free flow models with the fast, accurate likelihood evaluation required for self-normalized importance sampling?

## Input / Output

The model takes a Gaussian noise sample from the prior p0 and a small biased dataset of molecular configurations (from MD simulations) for training, plus an energy function E(x) available at inference time. It produces molecular configurations in 4–16 discrete flow-map steps (versus ~200+ adaptive steps for CNFs), with log-likelihoods computed via exact Jacobian determinants at each step rather than integrating a trace ODE. These proposal samples and likelihoods are then reweighted by exp(−E(x))/p_θ(x) via self-normalized importance sampling to yield statistically consistent Boltzmann samples and unbiased estimates of thermodynamic observables.

## Method

FALCON parameterizes a discrete flow map X_u(x_s, s, t) = x_s + (t−s)·u_θ(x_s, s, t) using a DiT backbone with soft SO(3) equivariance, where u_θ predicts the average velocity between times s and t. The core innovation is a hybrid training objective combining three terms: a standard flow-matching regression loss (L_cfm), a MeanFlow average-velocity loss (L_avg) for few-step generation, and a novel cycle-consistency invertibility loss (L_inv) that penalizes x_s − X_u(X_u(x_s,s,t), t, s), ensuring the discrete map is invertible even before the velocity objective is fully minimized. This invertibility guarantee (formalized in Proposition 2) makes the change-of-variables formula valid at each discrete step, enabling exact likelihood computation with only N·d function evaluations. A sign parameterization u_θ(x_s,s,t) = sign(t−s)·h_θ(x_s,s,t) handles the forward/backward discontinuity at s=t, and the EDM scheduler allocates more steps near the data distribution where the flow field has higher variance.

## Results

On the largest system (hexa-alanine), FALCON achieves E-W₂ of 0.892 and T-W₂ of 1.256, substantially outperforming ECNF++ (E-W₂ 10.668, T-W₂ 1.902) and SBG IS (E-W₂ 1.021, T-W₂ 1.431). FALCON-A achieves the best ESS across all three larger systems (0.104 on AL3, 0.094 on AL4, 0.077 on AL6), while FALCON achieves the best E-W₂ on AL3 (0.544) and AL4 (0.686). In terms of efficiency, FALCON requires only 4–16 function evaluations per sample versus ~200–265 for the Dopri5-integrated CNF, representing a two-orders-of-magnitude speedup at equivalent or superior performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FALCON decisively outperforms ECNF++ (Tan et al., 2025a) on all larger molecular systems: on hexa-alanine, FALCON achieves E-W₂ of 0.892 versus ECNF++'s 10.668 (a 12× improvement), and T-W₂ of 1.256 versus 1.902. Against the discrete NF baseline SBG IS (Tan et al., 2025a), FALCON-A achieves the best T-W₂ on all three systems (0.343 vs 0.502 on AL3, 1.163 vs 1.431 on AL6), while FALCON achieves the best E-W₂ on AL3 (0.544 vs 0.758) and AL4 (0.686 vs 1.068). FALCON also outperforms RegFlow (Rehman et al., 2025) across all reported metrics, with E-W₂ of 0.544 vs 1.051 on AL3 and 0.686 vs 6.277 on AL4. On alanine dipeptide (Table 2), FALCON-A achieves the best T-W₂ (0.180) matching ECNF++ (0.189), while FALCON achieves the best E-W₂ (0.225) beating BoltzNCE (0.27) and ECNF (0.419). The paper further shows that even with 250× more samples (5×10⁶), SBG's E-W₂ remains significantly worse than 4-step FALCON, confirming the gap is architectural rather than a sampling-budget issue.

## Relation to Prior Work

FALCON sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) Boltzmann Generators, which pair a generative model with self-normalized importance sampling for molecular equilibrium sampling (Noe et al., 2019); (2) continuous normalizing flows trained via flow matching, which provide expressive but computationally expensive likelihood evaluation requiring hundreds of ODE steps; and (3) few-step flow map models (MeanFlow, consistency models, shortcut models) that enable fast generation but lack native invertibility. The key departure is the introduction of a cycle-consistency regularization that makes the few-step flow map invertible without requiring it to exactly reproduce the continuous-time flow, thereby enabling tractable change-of-variables likelihood computation. This is the first demonstration of an invertible few-step flow with efficient likelihoods suitable for importance sampling in scientific applications.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-orders-of-magnitude inference speedup over CNF-based Boltzmann Generators makes large-scale molecular sampling practically feasible and unlocks the use of expressive DiT architectures that were previously computationally infeasible in the BG setting. The finding that FALCON outperforms state-of-the-art discrete NFs (SBG) even when the latter is given 250× more samples suggests that the few-step flow map formulation captures distributional structure more effectively than one-step invertible architectures. Key limitations include the inability to theoretically guarantee likelihood correctness (only empirical adequacy), the absence of true one-step generation, and the current focus on small peptide systems. Open questions include whether the cycle-consistency approach transfers to other domains requiring rapid likelihood evaluation (Bayesian inference, robotics) and whether structured Jacobians can further reduce the O(d³) determinant cost.
