## Problem

Existing one-shot, training-free pruning methods developed for LLMs (e.g., SparseGPT, Wanda) cannot be directly applied to diffusion models because parameters are shared across multiple iterative denoising steps, and errors introduced early in the trajectory compound through all subsequent steps. Meanwhile, prior diffusion-specific pruning methods are architecture-specific, require costly training or fine-tuning, and largely ignore unstructured and semi-structured sparsity patterns. The paper asks whether a general, training-free, one-shot pruning framework can handle diverse diffusion architectures (MMDiT, U-Net) across multiple pruning granularities.

## Input / Output

The pruning procedure takes a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (SD v2.1, SD3-Medium, SD3.5-Large, Flux.1-dev, or SDXL) and a small calibration set of 100 text prompts from GCC3M, producing a sparsified model at a user-specified sparsity level (unstructured, 2:4 semi-structured, or structured at 15–70%). The pruned model then accepts a text prompt and negative prompt to generate 512×512 RGB images, with evaluation on 5K MS-COCO 2014 validation prompts using FID, CLIP score, and ImageReward. The entire pruning process for a 2B-parameter model completes in under 15 minutes on a single RTX 4090.

## Method

OBS-Diff revitalizes the Optimal Brain Surgeon framework for diffusion models through three key mechanisms. First, a Timestep-Aware Hessian is constructed as a weighted sum of per-timestep activation outer products, H_l = 2 Σ_t α_t E[X_{l,t} X_{l,t}^T], where α_t follows a logarithmic-decreasing schedule that assigns greater importance to early denoising steps whose errors propagate through all subsequent steps. Second, a Module Packages strategy partitions the model into groups of layers with mutually independent inputs, amortizing the expensive multi-step calibration by running the full denoising trajectory once per package rather than once per layer. Third, the framework extends naturally to semi-structured (2:4) sparsity by selecting the two lowest-saliency weights per block, and to structured pruning of FFN neurons and MHA heads via aggregated column-wise saliency scores, with Reciprocal Rank Fusion resolving the dual-modality ranking ambiguity in MMDiT's shared attention heads.

## Results

On SD3-Medium at 50% unstructured sparsity, OBS-Diff achieves FID 27.20, CLIP 0.3167, and ImageReward 0.6468, substantially outperforming Wanda (FID 43.98, ImageReward −0.1076) and DSnoT (FID 63.37, ImageReward −0.5941). At 60% sparsity on SD3.5-Large, OBS-Diff maintains FID 29.15 and positive ImageReward (0.3984) while all baselines collapse to negative ImageReward scores. For structured pruning on SD3.5-Large at 30% sparsity, OBS-Diff achieves FID 34.51 versus 327.48 for L1-norm and 346.38 for EcoDiff, with CLIP 0.3107 vs. 0.2093 and 0.2024 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Wanda (Sun et al., 2024), OBS-Diff consistently wins on CLIP and ImageReward across all models and sparsity levels; the gap widens dramatically at high sparsity (e.g., at 60% on SD3.5-Large, OBS-Diff ImageReward 0.3984 vs. Wanda −0.6402). Against DSnoT (Zhang et al., 2024d), OBS-Diff leads on CLIP and ImageReward in every setting, with DSnoT suffering the most severe FID degradation at high sparsity (e.g., FID 211.58 vs. 28.49 at 60% on SD3-Medium). Against Magnitude pruning, OBS-Diff dominates on all three metrics at every sparsity level tested. For structured pruning, OBS-Diff dramatically outperforms both the L1-norm baseline (Li et al., 2017) and EcoDiff (Zhang et al., 2024b): at 30% sparsity on SD3.5-Large, OBS-Diff achieves FID 34.51 vs. 327.48 (L1-norm) and 346.38 (EcoDiff), and on SDXL at 30% sparsity, FID 29.75 vs. 101.96 (EcoDiff). On the small-scale DDPM/CIFAR-10 benchmark, OBS-Diff achieves FID 7.55 after 100K fine-tuning steps, beating Diff-Pruning (Fang et al., 2023b) at 7.72 under the same budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revives the classic Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) framework and adapts it to the iterative denoising process of diffusion models, a setting where Hessian-based layer-wise pruning (SparseGPT, Wanda, DSnoT) had not been applied. It departs from prior diffusion pruning work (Diff-Pruning, EcoDiff) by being entirely training-free and one-shot, and from LLM pruning methods by introducing a timestep-aware Hessian that weights early denoising steps more heavily to account for error compounding. The Module Packages strategy and Reciprocal Rank Fusion for MMDiT's joint attention are novel engineering contributions that make the OBS formulation tractable and architecture-agnostic across MMDiT and U-Net backbones.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OBS-Diff provides the first general, training-free pruning framework that handles unstructured, semi-structured (2:4), and structured sparsity across diverse large-scale text-to-image architectures (SD v2.1, SD3, SD3.5, Flux.1-dev, SDXL) in under 15 minutes on a single GPU, making high-sparsity compression practically accessible. The demonstrated robustness to OOD calibration prompts, varying CFG scales, sampling steps, and solvers strengthens confidence in real-world deployment. Open questions include whether the timestep-aware Hessian transfers to video diffusion or diffusion LLMs, and whether combining OBS-Diff with token merging or sparse attention yields compounding efficiency gains.
