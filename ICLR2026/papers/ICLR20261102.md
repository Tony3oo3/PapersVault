## Problem

Weighting methods in federated learning adaptively reweight client contributions to handle data heterogeneity, but they operate under the standard full-communication paradigm and thus leave the communication bottleneck—the primary practical obstacle in federated learning—entirely unaddressed. The paper asks whether agnostic weighting-based optimization and communication compression can be combined into a single algorithm without sacrificing either mechanism's effectiveness.

## Input / Output

Each node holds a local dataset (heterogeneous in size and class distribution) and computes either exact or stochastic gradients of its local loss, scaled by its current aggregation weight. The server maintains the global model θ and the weight vector π (constrained to a regularized simplex Λ = Δ^{M−1} ∩ Q_a^M), while each node maintains a local memory state h_i tracking its compressed gradient history. The key I/O constraint is that no full gradient is ever transmitted: nodes send only compressed gradient differences (dimension d but sparsified/quantized) and a single scalar loss value, while the server broadcasts θ and π back.

## Method

ADI (Agnostic DIANA) reformulates federated learning as a min-max saddle-point problem over model parameters θ and client weights π, then solves it with the Optimistic Extragradient method. The compression mechanism follows DIANA's principle: each node compresses the *difference* between its current weighted gradient and its local memory state rather than the raw gradient, which eliminates the irreducible variance term that causes direct gradient compression to stall near the optimum. The server aggregates compressed differences to form gradient estimators, performs an optimistic extragradient step for θ, and a mirror descent step with KL divergence for π, where the regularized simplex constraint prevents the weights from collapsing onto a single noisy client. Convergence guarantees are established for exact gradients, stochastic oracles, partial participation, and a non-convex Minty setting, with communication complexity that improves as the number of nodes grows.

## Results

On CIFAR-100 (100 classes, ResNet-34, 10 clients, Dirichlet α=0.5), ADI achieves 71.2% top-1 accuracy with Rand 50% compression, 71.7% with Rand 10%, and 70.9% with Rand 5%, remaining remarkably stable across compression levels. On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, Dirichlet α=0.3), ADI reaches 82.6% accuracy at Rand 50% and maintains 82.4% even at Rand 5%. Under partial participation (p=0.5) on CIFAR-10, ADI still achieves 79.6% at Rand 50%, while baselines drop to ~65%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ADI consistently outperforms all five baselines across datasets and compression levels. Against DIANA (the strongest compression-only baseline), ADI gains ~1.4–5.9% accuracy on CIFAR-100 and ~5.5–7.0% on CIFAR-10, with the gap widening under aggressive compression (Rand 5%: 82.4% vs 75.4% on CIFAR-10). Against EF21, the advantage is substantial: ADI leads by ~9% on CIFAR-100 and ~18% on CIFAR-10, and EF21 degrades sharply as compression increases (64.5%→61.6% on CIFAR-10) while ADI remains nearly flat. MASHA, the only prior method combining weighting and compression, is outperformed by ~9.5% on CIFAR-100 and ~19% on CIFAR-10, and its performance is essentially unchanged across compression levels (stuck around 62–63%), suggesting its periodic full-gradient requirement limits its benefit. Against the traditional weighting baselines AFL and q-FFL (with naive gradient compression), ADI leads by ~17–18% on CIFAR-10 at full participation, and the gap persists even under partial participation (p=0.5): ADI at 79.6% vs AFL at 64.5% and q-FFL at 65.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

ADI sits at the intersection of three established threads: agnostic federated learning (Mohri et al., 2019), which reformulates FL as a min-max saddle-point problem to adaptively weight clients; DIANA-style difference compression (Mishchenko et al., 2024), which compresses gradient differences rather than raw gradients to eliminate irreducible noise; and the Optimistic Extragradient method (Popov, 1980) for solving saddle-point problems. The key departure from prior SPP+compression work (MASHA, Three Pillars) is that ADI never requires periodic transmission of full gradients, a practical limitation those methods share. From the classical compression side (DASHA, EF21), ADI departs by operating in the saddle-point setting rather than standard minimization, and by incorporating the weighting mechanism directly into the compressed update.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that two of the most persistent obstacles in federated learning—communication cost and data heterogeneity—can be addressed simultaneously within a single algorithm, with convergence guarantees spanning exact, stochastic, and partial-participation settings. A notable practical finding is that ADI's performance is nearly insensitive to compression aggressiveness (maintaining ~85% accuracy on CIFAR-10 even at 5% RandK), while baselines degrade sharply, suggesting the DIANA mechanism effectively decouples compression quality from model quality. Open questions include extending the analysis to contractive (biased) compressors, incorporating acceleration for saddle-point problems, and understanding whether the weighting-induced deterioration in theoretical rates (monotonic in compression rate ω) manifests as a practical bottleneck at scale.
