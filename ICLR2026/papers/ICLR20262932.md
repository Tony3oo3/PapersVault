## Problem

Existing multimodal math benchmarks either pair a single image with text or include multiple images without requiring fine-grained discrimination among visually similar answer options. This leaves unexplored the critical real-world scenario where all candidate answers are nearly identical diagrams and the model must ground textual reasoning in precise visual distinctions across multiple images. VisioMath targets this gap by benchmarking LMMs on comparative reasoning over highly similar figure-based options.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262932/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each problem presents a text stem (averaging 61.5 tokens, optionally accompanied by 1+ stem images) alongside exactly four diagram images serving as answer options A–D. The four option images are deliberately constructed to share high visual similarity, quantified via minimum pairwise cosine similarity using Qwen multimodal-embedding-v1 (ranging from 0.16 to 1.0). The model outputs a single letter (A/B/C/D), extracted via a GLM4-Flash parsing prompt; unparseable responses are scored as incorrect.

## Method

VisioMath comprises 1,800 K-12 math problems (8,070 diagram options) sourced from Chinese high-school and college-entrance exams (2002–2023), with uniform A–D answer distribution and dual-annotator validation. The evaluation is zero-shot across 30+ LMMs (closed-source: GPT-4.1, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Seed1.6; open-source: Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL2.5, GLM-4.5V, etc.), stratified by GT position and visual-similarity quartile. Error analysis on GLM-4.5V identifies image-text misalignment (36%) as the dominant failure mode, confirmed by a controlled shuffling experiment that permutes the text-to-image mapping while keeping image order fixed, causing consistent accuracy drops (e.g., Gemini 2.5 Pro: −8.7%). Three mitigation strategies are tested: consolidating all images into one composite layout (+6.4% for Seed1.6), overlaying explicit option-letter anchors on each image (+9.8% for QwenVL-plus), and supervised fine-tuning on 500 alignment-oriented multi-image chain-of-thought samples generated via a QwenVL-Max captioning + DeepSeek-V3.1 refinement pipeline, yielding up to +12.6% accuracy for Qwen2.5-VL-3B.

## Results

On VisioMath (1,800 K-12 math problems with diagrammatic answer options), human annotators achieve 91.3% average accuracy, while the best LMM, Gemini 2.5 Pro, reaches only 80.9%—a 10.4-point gap. Among open-source multi-image models, GLM-4.5V leads at 53.7%, and most open-source models (24–44%) perform near or only modestly above the 25.6% random-guessing baseline. Performance degrades consistently as inter-image visual similarity increases: e.g., Doubao-1.5-Vision-Pro drops from 74.9% (lowest-similarity quartile) to 62.0% (highest-similarity quartile), and questions with images in the stem are uniformly harder than text-only stems across all models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VisioMath is a benchmark, so the 'comparisons' are across 33+ LMMs of varying scale, access type, and training focus. Among closed-source models, Gemini 2.5 Pro (80.9%) substantially outperforms GPT-4.1 (52.6%) and QwenVL-max (44.1%), with Seed1.6-Thinking (72.3%) in between; however, even Gemini 2.5 Pro trails human performance (91.3%) by over 10 points. Among open-source multi-image models, GLM-4.5V (53.7%) is the strongest, roughly matching GPT-4.1, while most 2B–27B open-source models (InternVL2.5-2B at 24.6%, Qwen2.5-VL-7B at 32.7%, Gemma3-27B at 35.3%) cluster near the random baseline. Math-oriented fine-tuned models (MM-Eureka-7B at 37.9%, MM-PRM-8B at 31.7%) do not close the gap to general-purpose multi-image models, suggesting that domain-specific math training alone does not resolve the core multi-image alignment challenge. Single-image-only models (e.g., LLaVA-v1.6-vicuna-13B at 24.4%) perform at chance level, confirming that native multi-image input support is essential. A key cross-cutting finding is that all models degrade when question stems include images and when option diagrams are highly visually similar, with image-text misalignment (36% of errors) identified as the dominant failure mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a specific underexplored niche within multi-image mathematical reasoning: problems where all four answer options are diagrams with high visual similarity. Prior multi-image math benchmarks (MathVerse-mv, MV-Math, CMM-Math-test) include multiple images but either lack image-based options entirely or embed them in composite layouts rather than as independent, comparable visual elements. VisioMath departs from these by systematically quantifying inter-option visual similarity and requiring fine-grained comparative discrimination, while the alignment-oriented CoT fine-tuning strategy repurposes standard SFT with a small, purpose-built dataset to target the identified image-text misalignment failure mode.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest LMMs (Gemini 2.5 Pro at 80.9%) fall well short of human performance (91.3%) and degrade sharply as visual similarity increases, exposing a fundamental weakness in cross-modal alignment that single-image benchmarks mask. The controlled shuffling experiment demonstrating reliance on positional heuristics rather than semantic binding is a clean diagnostic that should inform future architecture and training design. Key limitations include restriction to K-12 Chinese exam mathematics and the relatively small CoT dataset (500 samples); open questions include whether alignment strategies generalize to other diagram-heavy domains (physics, chemistry) and whether the positional-heuristic failure persists in next-generation models with stronger multi-image attention mechanisms.
