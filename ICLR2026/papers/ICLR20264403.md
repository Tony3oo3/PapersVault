## Problem

Fine-tuning LLMs on domain-specific data is time-consuming and memory-intensive, while existing structured pruning methods either require additional training/distillation passes, rely on calibration-data-based importance that is biased for specialized domains, or restore the dense model at the end (e.g., SAT), preventing inference speedup. No prior method simultaneously accelerates both fine-tuning and inference by pruning layers guided by task-specific gradient signals without incurring extra training overhead.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (e.g., Llama3.1-8B, Mistral-7B) and a downstream task dataset, consuming only the first ~1% of LoRA training steps to extract per-parameter gradients. It produces a pruned LLM with ~40% parameter reduction, where pruned layers are sparsified (80% sparsity) and merged into the preceding retained layer via sign-consistent addition. The intermediate IGIA-Matrix (mean squared simulated gradient per parameter) serves as the importance criterion for both layer selection and intra-layer sparsification.

## Method

GradPruner operates in three phases. First, it performs a brief LoRA fine-tuning (only the first ~1% of total steps) and computes the IGIA-Matrix for each linear layer by simulating full-weight gradients via the product of LoRA gradient matrices (∇W_B · ∇W_A) and averaging their squares over the initial steps. Second, it sums IGIA-Matrix values across all linear layers within each transformer block to obtain a layer importance score, then prunes the least important layers (retaining ~70%). Third, to push pruning beyond the ~30% layer-removal ceiling, it sparsifies each pruned layer using the IGIA-Matrix (keeping only the top-20% magnitude parameters) and merges the surviving weights into the preceding retained layer, but only where the sign of the pruned parameter matches the sign of the corresponding retained parameter, thereby avoiding destructive interference from sign conflicts. A Gradient Sensitivity Analysis validates that layer importance measured at this 1% early stage aligns with importance after full training, and a theoretical analysis connects the approach to isotropic model merging with adaptive per-parameter importance weights.

## Results

At 40% parameter sparsity, GradPruner achieves an average score of 0.782 (FFT) and 0.786 (LoRA) on Llama3.1-8B across eight downstream datasets, representing only a 0.99% drop from the dense model (0.784/0.794). On Mistral-7B, GradPruner scores 0.770 (FFT) and 0.780 (LoRA) versus dense baselines of 0.781 and 0.790. In terms of efficiency, GradPruner reduces training time by ~36%, training memory by ~34%, inference time by ~39%, and inference memory by ~39% relative to the dense model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GradPruner consistently outperforms all six baselines across both models and both fine-tuning settings. Against the inference-focused structured pruners (LLMPruner, Laco, MINITRON), GradPruner gains approximately 5 percentage points in average accuracy (e.g., 0.782 vs. 0.734–0.736 on Llama3.1-8B FFT), indicating that calibration-data-based importance estimation introduces significant bias on domain-specific tasks. Against the training-and-inference methods SAT and APT, GradPruner leads by 3.2 and 2.7 points respectively on Llama3.1-8B FFT/LoRA, with SAT's inability to accelerate inference (98.9% inference time) and APT's high training overhead (158% training time) further widening the practical gap. Notably, the pruned Llama3.1-8B with GradPruner (0.782 avg) surpasses the directly fine-tuned Llama3.2-3B (0.777 avg), demonstrating that a 40%-pruned 8B model can match or exceed a smaller 3B model. On Mistral-7B, GradPruner similarly leads all baselines by 2–5 points, confirming cross-architecture generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

GradPruner sits at the intersection of structured LLM pruning and model merging. It departs from calibration-data-based importance estimation (used by LLMPruner, LaCo, MINITRON) by instead leveraging early LoRA fine-tuning gradients to build the IGIA-Matrix, grounding the approach in the Lottery Ticket Hypothesis. The sign-based layer merging draws on the model-merging literature (TIES-merging, Fisher-weighted averaging) but applies it to a pruning context where pruned layers are sparsified and folded into retained layers. Unlike SAT (which restores dense form, negating inference gains) or APT (restricted to LoRA), GradPruner targets simultaneous training and inference efficiency under both FFT and LoRA paradigms.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is clear: a 40% parameter reduction with under 1% accuracy loss, and the pruned Llama3.1-8B outperforming the smaller Llama3.2-3B, makes this a viable compression strategy for domain-specific deployment. The method's reliance on only ~1% of training steps for importance estimation keeps the overhead minimal. Open questions include whether the 80% sparsity hyperparameter and 13-layer pruning count generalize cleanly to much larger models (beyond 13B) or non-Transformer architectures, and whether the sign-based merging assumption holds when layers have been trained on substantially different objectives. The cross-dataset generalization results (MedMCQA → MMLU clinical) are encouraging but still limited to a single domain pair.
