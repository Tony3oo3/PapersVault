## Problem

LLMs can generate coherent questions in single-turn settings but fail to adaptively tailor subsequent questions to previously gathered responses, performing poorly on multi-turn information-gathering tasks like 20 Questions, task clarification, and preference elicitation. The paper addresses this by formulating interactive information gathering as a sequential Bayesian experimental design problem, where each query is chosen to maximize expected information gain about a target variable given the accumulated history.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a growing conversation history of question-answer pairs and the LLM's predictive distributions (accessed via logits whenever possible) to produce, at each turn, a single selected multiple-choice question posed to a separate answerer LLM. The answer space is restricted to discrete multiple-choice options to enable closed-form entropy computation, and the target variable θ (an entity name or a free-text user profile paragraph) is never revealed to the questioner. Outputs include the selected question, an updated filtered belief distribution p_f(θ; h_{t-1}) over candidate hypotheses, and at evaluation time a greedy-decoded guess or a set of film recommendations rated by an LLM-as-judge.

## Method

BED-LLM iterates five steps per turn: (A) construct a belief state p_f(θ; h_{t-1}) by jointly sampling diverse hypotheses from the LLM conditioned on history, filtering out samples incompatible with past Q&A pairs (via a likelihood threshold), retaining previously consistent hypotheses, and imposing a uniform distribution; (B) generate M candidate multiple-choice questions via LLM sampling; (C) estimate the full expected information gain for each candidate using a Rao-Blackwellized estimator that computes marginal predictive entropy minus expected conditional entropy over the answer space, explicitly accounting for non-deterministic likelihoods rather than assuming deterministic answers; (D) select the question maximizing EIG; (E) append the observed response to the history. The critical design choice is the prior-likelihood pairing p(θ)p_LLM(y;[θ,x]) over the data-estimation pairing, justified by the fact that the answer space y is low-complexity (discrete choices) while θ is high-complexity, making LLM uncertainty estimates reliable in y-space but not in θ-space. This is the first work to combine prior-likelihood pairing with a non-deterministic likelihood in the LLM BED setting, avoiding the collapse of EIG to mere marginal predictive entropy.

## Results

On the 20 Questions task across three entity categories (Animals, Celebrities, Things) and six LLMs, BED-LLM achieves an average success rate improvement of 37.4 percentage points over Prompt-Only, with the success rate more than doubling in over half of the 18 model-dataset configurations and never decreasing. For example, with GPT-4o on Animals, BED-LLM reaches 93% success versus 45% for Prompt-Only and 83% for Split. On the preference elicitation task (film recommendations rated 1–5), BED-LLM also outperforms both Prompt-Only and the Entropy baseline, with the largest gains when the questioner and answerer use different model classes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

BED-LLM outperforms all baselines across every model-dataset configuration. Against Prompt-Only (Zhang et al., 2024), which directly prompts the LLM to generate the next question, BED-LLM gains an average of 37.4 percentage points (e.g., 93% vs. 45% for GPT-4o on Animals). Against the Split baseline—which maximizes marginal predictive entropy under a deterministic likelihood and subsumes the approaches of Hu et al. (2024), Kobalczyk et al. (2025), Mazzaccara et al. (2024), Piriyakulkij et al. (2023), and Cooper et al. (2025)—BED-LLM consistently wins, with gaps of 5–25 percentage points depending on the setting; notably, even the paper's own Split implementation (using BED-LLM's filtering mechanism) already surpasses previously reported Split results. The CoT baseline (ReAct-style chain-of-thought) performs only marginally better than Prompt-Only, confirming that structured reasoning alone cannot close the gap to explicit EIG maximization. The key differentiator is BED-LLM's use of a non-deterministic likelihood in the full EIG objective: the Entropy ablation (which drops the expected conditional entropy term) performs much closer to Split than to BED-LLM, showing that the full EIG—not just a better marginal entropy estimate—is what drives the gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

BED-LLM adapts the sequential Bayesian experimental design (BED) framework—rooted in Lindley's EIG criterion and modernized by Rainforth et al. (2024)—to the LLM setting, using a Rao-Blackwellized EIG estimator previously developed for deep active learning (Gal et al., 2017). It departs from prior LLM information-gathering work (Piriyakulkij et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2024; Kobalczyk et al., 2025; Cooper et al., 2025) in two critical ways: (1) it rejects the deterministic-likelihood assumption that collapses EIG to marginal predictive entropy, and (2) it carefully specifies the joint model factorization (prior-likelihood vs. data-estimation pairing) and belief-updating procedure, which prior works largely overlooked. The filtered belief distribution p_f(θ; h_{t-1})—combining LLM sampling with rejection-based consistency checking—occupies a middle ground between full Bayesian updating and raw in-context learning, neither of which works well in practice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, deployment-time framework for making any LLM a more effective adaptive questioner without fine-tuning, with broad applicability to medical diagnosis, preference learning, tutoring, and automated surveys. The finding that raw in-context belief updating fails dramatically (ICL Beliefs ablation drops success rates by 40–70pp) and that the full EIG (not just marginal entropy) is essential challenges the common assumption that LLMs naturally incorporate conversational history coherently. Open questions include whether the myopic single-step EIG can be extended to multi-step planning (policy-based BED), how the approach scales to very large or continuous hypothesis spaces where entropy estimation becomes intractable, and whether the prior-likelihood vs. data-estimation tradeoff holds when the answer space is also complex.
