## Problem

Time series super-resolution (TSSR)—reconstructing high-resolution signals from low-resolution inputs—remains largely unexplored despite its importance in healthcare, industrial IoT, and climatology. Directly transferring image super-resolution techniques fails due to fundamentally different priors and signal-space structures, and the task is more challenging than imputation because it must synthesize high-frequency details (sharp peaks, transient vibrations) entirely absent from systematically downsampled inputs. The paper further formalizes two previously undefined subtypes—sampled super-resolution (SSR) and aggregated super-resolution (ASR)—whose core challenges differ critically.

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution multivariate time series of length L×D and a target scale factor α, and produces a high-resolution series of length H×D where H−1 = α(L−1). Two distinct input regimes are supported: in SSR, each low-res value is a direct sample from the high-res sequence; in ASR, each low-res value is the mean over a window of α high-res points, making the problem inherently more ill-posed. The model generates the residual detail (high-res minus linearly interpolated low-res) rather than the full signal, and SRT-large extends this to zero-shot settings where no high-res training data from the target domain is available.

## Method

SRT decomposes the target residual into trend and periodic components via Autoformer-style average pooling, then generates each component independently using a separate rectified flow model conditioned on high-resolution features produced by an Implicit Time Function (ITF). The ITF aligns low-res components to the target time axis through three stages—dilated temporal enrichment, per-point value prediction via a small MLP, and pattern smoothness that enforces locality for trend and periodicity (via FFT-derived dominant period) for the seasonal component—applied recursively with per-step scale ≤ 3. A decoder-only velocity predictor with Rotary Positional Encoding and a novel two-layer Cross-Resolution Attention (CRA) mechanism fuses the ITF-aligned conditions with the raw low-res input (masked for SSR, broadcast for ASR) to predict the ODE velocity field. Generation uses only 4 Euler steps, and the final output is the sum of the two generated components added to the linearly interpolated low-res input. SRT-large scales the architecture to 30M parameters, removes the ITF's value-prediction MLP (absorbing it into the decoder), and is pretrained across diverse domains (equity, commodity, currency, power, retail, transportation, web search, synthetic) to achieve zero-shot TSSR.

## Results

On the three primary datasets (ETTm1, Weather, PEMS-SF), SRT achieves the best MSE and DTW distance across nearly all SSR and ASR configurations. For example, on SSR-ETTm1 SRT reaches 0.026 MSE / 0.057 DTW, improving over the next-best IDM (0.036/0.064) by ~28% in MSE. On the more challenging ASR-PEMS-SF task, SRT attains 0.125 MSE / 0.073 DTW versus IDM's 0.126/0.079 and FlowTS's 0.385/0.230. SRT also matches the fastest generation speed (0.04 s/sample, tied with FlowTS) while achieving an average performance ranking of 1.25 across all datasets, tasks, and metrics, far ahead of FlowTS (6.58).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SRT outperforms all eight baselines on both MSE and DTW across nearly every dataset-task combination. The closest competitors are IDM (implicit diffusion model for continuous super-resolution) and CSDI (conditional score-based diffusion for imputation): IDM is the strongest on SSR-PEMS-SF (0.108 vs SRT's 0.097 MSE) and ASR-ETTm1 (0.037/0.068 vs 0.037/0.069), while CSDI edges out SRT on SSR-Weather DTW (0.028 vs 0.039) but is far behind on MSE (0.034 vs 0.031). Among image-SR baselines, SRDiff and ResShift are consistently the weakest, with MSE values 1.5–2× higher than SRT on most settings. FlowTS, the only other rectified-flow-based method, matches SRT's generation speed (0.04 s/sample) but lags substantially in quality, especially on ASR-PEMS-SF where its MSE is 0.385 versus SRT's 0.125. FTS-Diffusion and Diffusion-TS, both diffusion-based time-series generators, show moderate performance but are outperformed by SRT by 15–40% in MSE on most configurations. Overall, SRT's advantage is most pronounced on the ASR task, where the ill-posed nature of distributing aggregated values amplifies the benefit of the disentangled rectified flow and cross-resolution attention design.

## Relation to Prior Work

SRT builds on rectified flow (Liu et al., 2022) as its generative backbone and Autoformer's (Wu et al., 2021) moving-average decomposition to disentangle the residual detail into trend and seasonal components. It deliberately departs from image super-resolution methods (SRDiff, IDM, FlowIE) by arguing that the priors and axis of upscaling are fundamentally different for time series, and from time series imputation (CSDI) by formalizing TSSR as a distinct problem where the missingness is systematic (uniform downsampling) rather than arbitrary. The key architectural novelty is the cross-resolution attention mechanism that fuses ITF-aligned high-resolution conditions with the raw low-resolution input inside a decoder-only velocity predictor, a design not present in prior flow-matching or diffusion-based time series generators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first formal taxonomy of time series super-resolution (SSR vs. ASR) and a standardized evaluation protocol across nine datasets, establishing a benchmark that the field lacked. SRT-large's zero-shot capability is practically significant for domains where high-resolution ground truth is unavailable for training. However, the authors acknowledge that the decomposability assumption (trend + seasonal) may not hold for all real-world series, and they identify adaptive prior selection as an open direction. The moderate but consistent performance gains over strong baselines, combined with the 4-step inference speed, make SRT a practical tool, though the task remains relatively niche compared to forecasting or imputation.
