## Problem

Deep state space models (SSMs) typically over-parameterize their state dimensions, yet lack principled post-training mechanisms to reduce them without retraining. Existing layer-adaptive pruning (e.g., LAST) relies on worst-case H∞ peak-gain scores, which are conservative for typical workloads and fail to exploit the substantial redundancy present in trained SSMs. This paper asks whether a typical-case, energy-based criterion can enable more aggressive, architecture-agnostic state pruning with negligible accuracy loss.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261430/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the trained diagonal SSM parameters per layer—pole matrix Λ (with |λ_i| < 1), input coupling B ∈ C^{n×h}, and output coupling C ∈ C^{h×n}—along with a single global prune ratio p. It outputs kept and pruned state index sets per layer, where pruning always removes a contiguous tail of the energy-sorted ordering (never arbitrary states), yielding a dimension-reduced SSM that can be directly deployed for inference. The prefix-normalized scores are monotonically non-increasing within each layer, so a single scalar threshold τ applied globally determines how many states each layer retains, potentially eliminating entire low-energy layers at high pruning ratios.

## Method

AIRE-Prune assigns each diagonal mode a closed-form asymptotic impulse-response energy E_i = ‖C_{:,i}‖²₂ ‖B_{i,:}‖²₂ / (1 − |λ_i|²), which captures the total output energy that mode transmits over an infinite horizon under unit impulse excitation. This H₂-style energy score is mode-separable (no matrix solves), scale-aware through B/C couplings, and dynamics-aware through pole damping. To enable cross-layer comparison, per-layer energies are sorted descending and converted to a prefix-normalized 'hazard-rate' score s_(i) = E_(i) / S_(i), where S_(i) is the cumulative energy of the top-i modes; this ratio is monotonically non-increasing, so a single global threshold τ keeps the longest prefix in each layer and prunes the contiguous tail. The approach extends classical modal truncation from a single LTI system to deep nonlinear SSM stacks and replaces the worst-case H∞ peak-gain criterion with a typical-case total-energy criterion, yielding substantially larger safe pruning budgets (60.8% average state removal with only 0.29 pp accuracy drop on LRA/S5) without any retraining.

## Results

On the S5 MIMO SSM across the six Long Range Arena tasks and Speech Commands, AIRE-Prune achieves an average 60.8% state pruning with only 0.29 percentage points of average accuracy loss (no retraining). Under a ≤1 pp loss budget, it safely prunes 80% on Text and Pathfinder, 70% on Path-X, 65% on Image, 50% on Retrieval, and 20% on ListOps while matching or nearly matching full-model accuracy. Pruning translates to 1.2×–2.9× inference speedups and 19%–65% parameter reductions on an NVIDIA H100 GPU.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against LAST (Gwak et al., 2025), the strongest H∞-based baseline, AIRE-Prune achieves 0.29 pp average accuracy loss versus LAST's 0.52 pp, while pruning 60.8% of states on average compared to roughly 33% for LAST (≈1.8× more aggressive). Per-task, AIRE's safe pruning (≤1 pp loss) exceeds LAST by +20 pp on Text (80% vs. 60%), +35 pp on Image (65% vs. 30%), +50 pp on Pathfinder (80% vs. 30%), and +40 pp on Path-X (70% vs. 30%). Against LAMP (Lee et al., 2021), a magnitude-based layer-adaptive pruning method, AIRE-Prune also dominates: LAMP's importance score relies on a cumulative magnitude ratio that does not account for pole damping or controllability/observability, resulting in smoother (less selective) accuracy degradation curves and lower safe pruning budgets. The paper attributes AIRE's advantage to its time-accumulated energy lens combined with prefix normalization, which creates strong head-tail separation in the score distribution, enabling a single global threshold to preserve essential modes while collapsing low-energy tails—a behavior not observed in magnitude- or peak-gain-based scoring.

## Relation to Prior Work

AIRE-Prune extends classical modal truncation from single linear systems to deep SSM stacks, building on the diagonal (or diagonalizable) parameterization introduced by S4/S5 that makes per-mode analysis tractable. Its closest predecessor is LAST (Gwak et al., 2025), which also performs layer-adaptive, post-training state pruning in SSMs but ranks modes by worst-case H∞ gain; AIRE-Prune replaces this peak-gain criterion with total asymptotic impulse-response energy (H2), a typical-case measure that integrates over all frequencies rather than emphasizing a single resonant peak. The method also inherits the layer-adaptive, global-threshold pruning paradigm from weight-pruning literature (LAMP, etc.) but grounds importance in controllability, observability, and pole damping rather than static weight magnitudes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that trained SSMs tolerate ~61% state removal with under 0.3 pp accuracy loss across S5, S4D, and Mamba on diverse long-range tasks provides a practical, training-free compression path that translates directly into 1.2–2.9× inference speedups and up to 65% parameter reduction. The step-like accuracy curve (sharp elbow vs. smooth decay for H∞ baselines) suggests the energy ranking creates a much cleaner separation between essential and redundant modes, which could guide future work on certified pruning guarantees and adaptive depth. Open questions include whether the criterion extends to non-diagonal or input-dependent (selective) SSMs like Mamba's full formulation, whether it remains valid under non-stationary or adversarial inputs, and whether the prefix-normalization rule generalizes to very deep stacks (100+ layers) where per-layer energy scales may diverge more dramatically.
