## Problem

Existing tool-use benchmarks for LLMs predominantly report final-answer accuracy while overlooking the detailed tool-use trajectory—whether tools are correctly selected, parameterized, and ordered. They also rely on small or simulated tool sets, short low-depth trajectories, and straightforward queries that name APIs directly, failing to capture the complexity of real-world agentic scenarios where users provide indirect, implicit instructions over large executable tool pools.

## Input / Output

Each evaluation instance pairs a user query (in either an explicit 'simple' or an indirect 'hard' formulation) with a domain-relevant subset of 1,228 production-style RapidAPI tools, each specified by name, description, required/optional parameters with types and constraints, and expected output format. The model produces a tool-use trajectory (an ordered or unordered set of tool calls with concrete parameter values) and a final natural-language answer. Evaluation yields five trajectory-aware scores—Exact Match, Inclusion, Tool Usage (parameter correctness), LLM-judged Trajectory Satisfaction, and final Answer accuracy—plus a retrieval rate when a retrieval-based tool-selection strategy is employed.

## Method

TRAJECT-Bench is constructed in two stages. First, 1,228 executable tools are curated from RapidAPI across 10 domains by validating each API's executability, refining descriptions with empirical I/O, deduplicating overlapping endpoints, and retaining tools with rich parameter complexity. Second, task-driven trajectories are synthesized in two structural forms: parallel trajectories (independent tool calls with fully specified inputs, 3–10+ tools) generated by prompting an LLM from manually defined task types, and sequential trajectories (dependency chains where later tools consume earlier outputs) built on a directed tool graph G_T with manually designed chain templates. Each trajectory is paired with two semantically aligned queries of differing difficulty. Evaluation goes beyond final accuracy by introducing trajectory-level diagnostics (EM, Inclusion, Usage, and an LLM-judge Traj-Satisfy score for gold-trace-free settings), and the benchmark further assesses retrieval-augmented tool selection (using all-MiniLM, bge-large, and ToolBench-IR) and ReAct-based agentic inference with static vs. dynamic retrieval, revealing that the critical bottleneck lies in the transition from short to mid-length trajectories and that retrieval becomes a severe failure point on hard, indirect queries.

## Results

On TRAJECT-Bench parallel queries with domain-specific tools, Gemini-2.5-pro achieves the highest simple-query EM (0.851) and accuracy (0.911), while Claude-4 leads on hard queries with EM 0.445 and accuracy 0.517. All models show a dramatic drop from simple to hard queries (e.g., Claude-4 EM falls from 0.846 to 0.445; Gemini-2.5-pro from 0.851 to 0.442), and performance degrades sharply as trajectory length increases, with the steepest decline between 3 and 5 tools. On sequential queries, DeepSeek (EM 0.825, Acc 0.823) and Kimi-k2 (EM 0.821, Acc 0.833) perform best, while gpt-oss:120b lags significantly (EM 0.538, Acc 0.653).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TRAJECT-Bench evaluates five frontier LLM families as baselines. On simple parallel queries, Gemini-2.5-pro (EM 0.851) and Claude-4 (EM 0.846) are nearly tied at the top, while Claude-3.7 (EM 0.676) and gpt-oss:120b (EM 0.652) trail by roughly 17–20 points. On hard parallel queries, Claude-4 (EM 0.445) edges out Gemini-2.5-pro (0.442) and DeepSeek (0.439), but all top models collapse to roughly half their simple-query performance, whereas Claude-3.7 drops to just 0.135 EM. On sequential queries, DeepSeek-V3.1 (EM 0.825) and Kimi-k2 (EM 0.821) slightly outperform Claude-4 (0.819), and Kimi-k2 achieves the highest sequential accuracy (0.833). Qwen3-235b-A22B is competitive across all settings (EM 0.844 simple, 0.440 hard, 0.824 sequential) but never leads on any single metric. Retrieval-augmented tool selection provides negligible gains on simple queries but becomes a severe bottleneck on hard queries, where retrieval rate drops below 60% and EM plummets (e.g., Claude-4 hard EM falls from 0.445 to 0.031 with static retrieval), indicating that semantic-similarity retrievers fail to capture implicit user intent.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on ToolBench (Qin et al., 2023) for sourcing executable tools from RapidAPI and for using ToolBench-IR as a retrieval backbone, and on ReAct (Yao et al., 2023b) as the agentic evaluation framework. However, it departs from prior tool-use benchmarks (MetaTool, API-Bank, Gorilla, BFCL, ToolQA) by treating the tool-use trajectory itself—its structure (parallel vs. sequential), scale (3–10+ tools), and step-by-step correctness—as the primary evaluation object rather than a means to a final answer. The paper is the first to simultaneously provide practical executable tools, trajectory-aware metrics (exact match, inclusion, usage, LLM-judge satisfaction), paired query difficulty levels, and agentic evaluation in a single benchmark.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TRAJECT-Bench exposes a critical blind spot in current evaluation: final-answer accuracy can mask fundamentally broken tool trajectories, since LLMs can sometimes produce correct answers from internal knowledge even when calling wrong tools. The finding that retrieval-based tool selection collapses to ~50% retrieval rate on indirect (hard) queries challenges the common assumption that semantic-similarity retrieval is a reliable front-end for large tool sets, and the identified bottleneck at the 3→5 tool transition provides a concrete target for improving long-horizon agentic planning. Open questions include whether trajectory-aware training signals can close the hard-query gap, how evaluation should adapt to dynamic environments where tool availability changes, and whether the LLM-judge Traj-Satisfy metric remains reliable as trajectories grow beyond 10 tools.
