## Problem

Bidirectional Transformer encoders (BERT and successors) rely on self-attention, whose quadratic time and memory costs limit practical context-window extension in latency- and memory-constrained deployments. While attention-free alternatives (linear attention, state-space models) have been explored for autoregressive decoding, almost none have been adapted to the bidirectional, encoder-only paradigm that dominates industrial NLP. This paper addresses that gap by reformulating the attention-free Avey architecture for bidirectional encoding.

## Input / Output

Avey-B takes a text sequence of N tokens (tokenized with a 50,368-vocabulary BPE tokenizer) and partitions it into equal-sized splits of S=256 tokens. For each target split, a MaxSim-based ranker retrieves the top-k=3 most relevant preceding splits, which are then compressed back to S tokens before contextualization. The output is a set of contextualized per-token representations (dimension d) that can be fed to task-specific heads for classification, token labeling, span extraction, or passage scoring. Because context width is decoupled from sequence length, the model accepts arbitrarily long inputs at inference without additional pretraining at those lengths.

## Method

Avey-B reformulates Avey's ranker-plus-neural-processor design for bidirectional encoding with three architectural innovations. First, it decouples static and dynamic parameterizations into alternating layer types: static layers apply a learned cross-embedding linear projection, while dynamic layers weight embeddings solely by row-sum-normalized cosine similarities, eliminating the inversion pathology where a highly similar token could contribute less than a less-similar one under Avey's coupled element-wise product. Second, it introduces row-wise sum normalization of the cosine similarity matrix in dynamic layers, yielding a row-stochastic operator that bounds per-row gain and stabilizes training. Third, it adds a neural compressor in the ranker that maps the concatenated (k+1)S-token block back to S tokens via a learned linear projection with a residual connection, making the neural processor's per-split compute independent of k and improving throughput by 4.37×. The model is pretrained with 20% masked language modeling on 180B FineWeb tokens (base: 165M, large: 391M parameters) and fine-tuned for downstream tasks.

## Results

At the base scale, Avey-B achieves average scores of 88.78 (SC), 93.59 (TC), 62.45 (QA), and 78.53 (IR), outperforming BERT (87.14/89.82/57.65/73.08) and NeoBERT-medium (85.36/88.20/55.67/56.72) across all four task categories. On TC and IR specifically, Avey-B base surpasses every Transformer baseline including RoBERTa (90.27/74.28) and ModernBERT (92.78/72.54), despite being pretrained on ~11× fewer tokens than ModernBERT. At the large scale, Avey-B reaches 94.00 (TC) and 80.67 (IR), again leading all baselines on those categories. On efficiency, Avey-B's throughput decays with exponent α≈0.44 versus α≈0.77 (ModernBERT) and α≈0.81 (NeoBERT), yielding a 3.38× and 11.63× speedup at 96K tokens respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Avey-B outperforms BERT and NeoBERT on every single benchmark across both base and large scales, with the largest margins on IR (e.g., +15.4 NDCG over NeoBERT-medium at base) and TC (+3.8 F1 over BERT-base). Against RoBERTa and ModernBERT, Avey-B wins decisively on TC (e.g., 93.59 vs. 90.27 and 92.78 at base; 94.00 vs. 91.07 and 93.05 at large) and IR (78.53 vs. 74.28 and 72.54 at base; 80.67 vs. 77.95 and 76.61 at large), but trails them on SC (MNLI: 83.58 vs. 86.42/86.72 at base) and QA (ReCoRD: 44.03 vs. 67.86/65.73 at base). Notably, Avey-B base exceeds all large-scale Transformer encoders on TC and IR despite using ~85M fewer parameters than NeoBERT and being pretrained on ~11× fewer tokens than ModernBERT. On efficiency, Avey-B (even without fused kernels, using only torch.compile) sustains higher throughput than FlashAttention-optimized ModernBERT and NeoBERT across all tested sequence lengths from 128 to 96K tokens, with the gap widening at longer contexts.

## Relation to Prior Work

Avey-B is a direct bidirectional reformulation of Avey (Hammoud & Acharya, 2025), an autoregressive, attention-free architecture that partitions sequences into splits and uses a ranker-plus-neural-processor design instead of self-attention. The paper positions Avey-B as the encoder-only analogue of Avey, mirroring the GPT-to-BERT transition in the Transformer family, while departing from all Transformer-based encoders (BERT, RoBERTa, ModernBERT, NeoBERT) by eliminating quadratic self-attention entirely. Its three architectural innovations—decoupled static/dynamic parameterizations, row-normalized cosine similarities, and neural compression—are specific adaptations needed to make the retrieval-based design work in the bidirectional, all-splits-must-be-contextualized regime, distinguishing it from prior attention-free work (linear attention, state-space models) that typically retains a sequential or local-mixing structure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a retrieval-conditioned, attention-free architecture can match or exceed heavily optimized Transformer encoders on discriminative tasks (token classification, information retrieval) while scaling throughput with sequence length at roughly half the decay rate (α≈0.44 vs. 0.77–0.81), a structural advantage rooted in fixed split-size computation rather than kernel-level tricks. However, the effectiveness comparison is confounded by an ~11× smaller pretraining budget (180B vs. ~2T tokens for ModernBERT), and the architecture still lacks fused CUDA/Triton kernels, so the full efficiency gap in production is unknown. Open questions include whether the advantage persists at equal pretraining scale, how the architecture handles generative or long-form tasks, and whether the split-based inductive bias generalizes beyond English-centric benchmarks.
