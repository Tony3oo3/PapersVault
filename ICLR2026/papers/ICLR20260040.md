## Problem

Autoregressive image generation via next-patch prediction is memory-bound, requiring 256 sequential steps at 256×256 and 1024 at 512×512, leading to high latency. Existing parallelization attempts (PAR, RandAR, ARPG) reduce steps only modestly (to 88–147) while maintaining quality, because they either lack mutual visibility among concurrently generated tokens or rely on fixed generation orders. The paper tackles how to achieve aggressive parallelization (20 steps at 256×256, 48 at 512×512) without quality loss, while preserving flat token representations compatible with vision perception backbones.

## Input / Output

The model takes a class label (ImageNet) or a text prompt encoded by Gemma-2-2B (text-to-image) as conditioning, and optionally pre-filled image tokens for zero-shot editing (inpainting, outpainting, class-conditional editing). Images are tokenized by the LlamaGen tokenizer (codebook 16384, downsample 16) into a 2D grid of discrete tokens: 16×16 for 256×256, 32×32 for 512×512, and 64×64 for 1024×1024. The output is the full set of image tokens decoded in a precomputed locality-aware order, which are then decoded back to pixels by the tokenizer's decoder.

## Method

LPD introduces two coupled components. First, a Flexible Parallelized Autoregressive Modeling architecture decouples context from generation: previously generated tokens are encoded into a KV cache to supply context, while learnable position query tokens (a shared embedding plus the positional embedding of the target location) drive parallel prediction at arbitrary target positions. A specialized training attention mask enforces mutual visibility among query tokens within the same step while blocking later tokens from attending to them, and at inference the encoding and decoding phases are fused into a single forward pass via a second mask. Second, a Locality-aware Generation Order Schedule, derived from empirical attention analysis showing sharp decay of per-token attention with spatial distance, selects each parallel group by (i) prioritizing tokens close to already-generated context (high proximity) and (ii) enforcing minimum separation among concurrently generated tokens (low intra-group dependency) via a repulsion threshold and farthest-point sampling fallback. Group sizes grow along a cosine schedule, and the full order is precomputed offline so inference incurs no scheduling overhead.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, LPD-XL (752M) achieves FID 2.10 with only 20 decoding steps (vs. 256 for raster order), matching ARPG-XL's FID at 64 steps while reducing latency by 4.2× (0.41s vs. 1.71s). At 32 steps, LPD-XL reaches FID 1.92, surpassing ARPG-XXL (1.94 at 64 steps) with 3.4× lower latency. On ImageNet 512×512, LPD-XL reduces steps from 1024 to 48 (21.3× reduction) while maintaining FID 2.10 (vs. 2.09 for the raster counterpart). On GenEval 1024×1024 text-to-image, LPD-XL achieves a score of 0.62 in 64 steps (1.53s latency) versus 0.60 for the raster counterpart in 4096 steps (93.8s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPD-XL at 20 steps achieves FID 2.10, matching ARPG-XL (719M, 64 steps, FID 2.10) exactly while using 3.2× fewer steps and 4.2× lower latency (0.41s vs. 1.71s). At 32 steps, LPD-XL (FID 1.92) surpasses the larger ARPG-XXL (1.3B, 64 steps, FID 1.94) with 3.4× lower latency. Against RandAR-XXL (1.4B, 88 steps, FID 2.15), LPD-XL achieves a better FID (2.10) with 4.4× fewer steps. LPD-XL also substantially outperforms NAR-XXL (1.5B, 31 steps) on FID (2.10 vs. 2.58) despite using fewer steps, and beats PAR-XXL-4× (1.4B, 147 steps, FID 2.35) by 0.25 FID with 7.4× fewer steps. On ImageNet 512×512, LPD-XL (48 steps, FID 2.10) nearly matches its raster-order counterpart (1024 steps, FID 2.09) while reducing latency from 20.93s to 1.01s (20.7× speedup). On GenEval 1024×1024, LPD-XL (64 steps) improves the GenEval score from 0.60 to 0.62 over the raster counterpart while reducing latency from 93.8s to 1.53s (61× speedup).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of parallelized autoregressive image generation (SAR, ARPG, RandAR, PAR, NAR, ZipAR) by introducing a new architectural mechanism—learnable position query tokens with a specialized attention mask—that decouples context encoding from token generation, enabling arbitrary generation order and dynamic parallelism within a decoder-only transformer. It departs from encoder-decoder approaches (SAR, ARPG) that produce tokens independently within a parallel step, and from decoder-only methods (RandAR) that degenerate to batched next-token prediction, by guaranteeing mutual visibility among concurrently generated tokens while caching only generated tokens. The locality-aware generation order schedule is grounded in a systematic attention analysis of LlamaGen, building on the intuition from MaskGIT that group sizes should grow with context, but formalizing it through explicit proximity and repulsion thresholds rather than fixed or low-discrepancy sequences.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 12.8× reduction in generation steps (256→20 at 256×256, 1024→48 at 512×512) while matching or exceeding the FID of prior parallelized AR models addresses a core bottleneck in autoregressive image generation and makes the flat-token AR paradigm more competitive with diffusion and multi-scale (VAR) approaches in wall-clock latency. The method's compatibility with standard flat vision tokenizers (LlamaGen, CLIP-compatible) and its natural extension to zero-shot editing and high-resolution text-to-image generation position it as a practical building block for unified multimodal systems. Open questions include whether the precomputed locality-aware schedule adapts well to content-dependent generation, how the method scales beyond ImageNet-class-conditional settings, and whether the position-query overhead becomes prohibitive in compute-bound (large-batch) regimes where the speedup drops from ~12× to ~3×.
