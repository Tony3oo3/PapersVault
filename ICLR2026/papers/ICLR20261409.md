## Problem

GRPO, the dominant RL post-training algorithm for LLM reasoning, optimizes the arithmetic mean of token-level importance-weighted rewards, making it highly sensitive to outlier importance sampling ratios. As training progresses, these outliers cause extreme policy updates, expanding the ratio range, collapsing entropy, and degrading performance. The paper asks whether a more robust aggregation of token-level rewards can stabilize GRPO while simultaneously enabling wider clipping for greater exploration.

## Input / Output

The method operates on groups of G=8 sampled rollouts per prompt, where each rollout is a chain-of-thought response (capped at 3,000 tokens) to a text math problem, an image+text geometry question, or an embodied environment state. Rewards are verifiable binary signals (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect), and the advantage is computed by normalizing rewards within each group. The output is a single generated response per question at evaluation (temperature 0.0 for language, 0.5 with 16 samples for multimodal), scored by Pass@1.

## Method

GMPO is a plug-and-play modification of GRPO that replaces the arithmetic mean of token-level importance-weighted rewards with their geometric mean: (∏|ρ_{i,t}(θ)Â_i|)^{1/|o_i|} · sgn(Â_i). Because the geometric mean is inherently less sensitive to outliers, each token's gradient is weighted by the geometric mean of all ratios in its sequence rather than its own individual ratio, yielding a more balanced and robust update signal. Two additional design choices are critical: (1) clipping is applied at the token level (not sequence level as in DeepSeek-R1) to avoid zeroing all token gradients when a single outlier triggers the clip, and (2) the clipping range is widened to (e^{-0.4}, e^{0.4}), significantly larger than GRPO's (0.8, 1.2), to encourage exploration without sacrificing stability. Theoretically, GMPO is shown to be an O(δ²) Lipschitz-stable perturbation of GRPO within a trust region, preserving monotonic-improvement guarantees up to quadratic error, and empirically maintains lower KL divergence, higher token entropy, and more stable gradient norms throughout training.

## Results

On five mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, AMC, MATH500, Minerva, OlympiadBench), GMPO-7B with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B achieves 63.4% average Pass@1, improving over GRPO by 4.1% (59.3%). On the MATH500 benchmark with the Qwen3-32B Mixture-of-Experts model, GMPO reaches 96.7% vs. GRPO's 94.6% (+2.1%). On the ALFWorld agentic benchmark, GMPO-1.5B achieves 85.9% overall accuracy, a 13.1% improvement over GRPO-1.5B (72.8%). On the Geometry3K multimodal benchmark, GMPO-7B gains 1.4% over GRPO-7B (54.7% vs. 53.3%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GMPO consistently outperforms GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) across all model scales and task types: +1.5% avg on Qwen2.5-Math-7B, +4.1% on R1-Distill-7B, +2.1% on Qwen3-32B MoE, +1.4% on Geometry3K, and +13.1% on ALFWorld. Against Dr.GRPO/Oat-Zero (Liu et al., 2025), GMPO-7B (R1-Distill) leads by 1.9% average (63.4% vs. 61.5%), while GMPO-7B on the base Qwen2.5-Math-7B edges out Oat-Zero-7B by 1.3% (52.7% vs. 51.4%). GMPO-7B surpasses SimpleRL-Zero-7B (Zeng et al., 2025) by 6.1%, PRIME-Zero-7B (Cui et al., 2025) by 4.7%, OpenReasoner-Zero-7B@8k (Hu et al., 2025) by 6.8%, Eurus-7B (Yuan et al., 2024) by 4.7%, and GPG-7B (Chu et al., 2025) by 1.7% on the five-benchmark average. On ALFWorld, GMPO-1.5B (85.9%) substantially outperforms PPO-1.5B (54.4%) and RLOO-1.5B (69.7%), but narrowly trails the agentic-specific GiGPO-1.5B (Feng et al., 2025) by 0.8% (85.9% vs. 86.7%). GMPO also outperforms the untrained Qwen2.5-Math-7B base model by 26.2% average, confirming the RL training gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

GMPO is a direct, minimal modification of GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), which itself builds on PPO's (Schulman et al., 2017) importance-sampling ratio and clipping mechanism, with theoretical grounding inherited from TRPO's (Schulman et al., 2015) trust-region framework. The key conceptual departure is replacing the arithmetic mean of token-level importance-weighted rewards with the geometric mean, motivated by the well-known statistical property that geometric means are less sensitive to outliers. The paper positions this as orthogonal to other GRPO stability variants (OPO's optimal baseline, BNPO's beta normalization, GRPO-lead's difficulty-aware reweighting), and borrows DAPO's clip-higher philosophy to justify a wider clipping range (e^{-0.4}, e^{0.4}) that promotes exploration without sacrificing stability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Training stability in RL post-training for LLMs has been underexplored despite being critical for scalable, reproducible systems, and GMPO offers a plug-and-play fix (one-line change in the loss) that consistently improves performance across language, multimodal, MoE, and agentic settings. The O(δ²) Lipschitz-stable perturbation result provides a clean theoretical justification that GMPO preserves GRPO's monotonic-improvement guarantees within a trust region. Open questions include whether the geometric-mean advantage persists at much larger model scales or with different reward structures (e.g., dense process rewards), whether the optimal clipping range generalizes across domains, and whether other robust aggregators (e.g., trimmed means with different quantiles) could further improve the trade-off between outlier suppression and signal retention.
