## Problem

Set-based transformer probabilistic models (neural processes, prior-fitted networks, tabular foundation models) excel at single-pass marginal prediction but require joint distributions over multiple targets for many applications. The standard autoregressive deployment re-encodes the entire growing context at each step due to bidirectional self-attention, yielding O(K(N+K)²) complexity that severely limits long sequences, large contexts, and batched sampling. The paper asks how to capture inter-target dependencies efficiently without sacrificing the permutation-invariant set-based conditioning that makes these models powerful.

## Input / Output

The method takes a variable-sized context set of N input-output pairs (order-agnostic, processed via bidirectional attention) and a sequence of K target inputs, optionally with observed target values for density evaluation. It produces either joint predictive samples (K correlated outputs generated sequentially), a joint predictive log-density (all K conditionals computed in one pass), or marginal predictions (when the buffer is empty, recovering standard set-based behavior). The context is encoded once and cached as immutable KV pairs; only the small dynamic buffer (size ≤ K) is updated incrementally, making the method particularly efficient for batched generation where B sample streams share a single context cache.

## Method

The core contribution is a causal autoregressive buffer that decouples one-time context encoding from lightweight sequential prediction. The context C is encoded once via bidirectional MHSA and its per-layer KV cache is frozen; a separate buffer of previously generated targets uses strictly causal self-attention among buffer tokens plus cross-attention to the cached context at each layer. Target queries cross-attend to the concatenated context and visible buffer prefix, then pass through a distribution head (e.g., 20-component GMM). Training enforces the attention structure via a single block-sparse mask with a buffer-size curriculum: 50% of targets attend only to context, while the remaining 50% attend to context plus a uniformly sampled buffer prefix length, enabling a single model to handle both marginal and joint modes. This reduces total attention cost from O(K(N+K)²) to O(N² + NK + K²), and for batched sampling to O(N² + B(NK + K²)) since the context cache is shared across all B streams. For joint density evaluation, all K buffer tokens and K query tokens are packed into one forward pass with a causal mask, recovering the autoregressive factorization without sequential re-encoding.

## Results

The causal autoregressive buffer reduces joint inference cost from O(K(N+K)²) to O(N²+NK+K²), achieving up to 20× faster autoregressive sampling and 7× lower peak memory (at N=1024) compared to full context re-encoding baselines. On predictive density (Table 1), TNP w/ buffer (K=16) closely matches TNP-D-AR on GP (2.51 vs 2.57 nats) and Sawtooth (1.00 vs 1.05 nats), while substantially outperforming TNP-D-Ind and TNP-ND. On the multisensory model comparison task (Table 2), the buffer achieves LML RMSE of 3.56 (K=16) and 3.47 (K=1), competitive with TNP-D-AR (3.10) and better than TNP-A (4.75). On tabular regression (Table 3), AR w/ buffer matches Standard AR within standard errors across all six UCI/Kaggle datasets in both low- and high-context settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the TNP variants (Nguyen & Grover, 2022), the buffer with K=16 matches TNP-D-AR within ~0.06 nats on GP and Sawtooth while being 3–20× faster; TNP-D-Ind and TNP-ND are faster but lose 0.3–1.4 nats in predictive density because they cannot capture target dependencies. TNP-A is slower in both training (4–12×) and sampling, and the buffer avoids its duplicated-target overhead. Against the autoregressive deployment strategy of Bruinsma et al. (2023) (TNP-D-AR), the buffer achieves comparable predictive quality (K=1 exactly matches; K=16 is within 0.06 nats on most tasks) while eliminating the per-step O((N+k)²) re-encoding, yielding the headline 20× speedup and 7× memory reduction. On the TabICL foundation model (Qu et al., 2025), AR w/ buffer (K=32) matches Standard AR within standard errors on all six UCI/Kaggle datasets in both N=16 and N=1024 settings, confirming the buffer preserves joint predictive dependencies at a fraction of the inference cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends transformer-based amortized probabilistic models (TNPs, PFNs, tabular foundation models like TabICL) by introducing a causal autoregressive buffer that borrows the KV-caching mechanism from autoregressive language models. It directly addresses the re-encoding bottleneck identified in autoregressive conditional neural processes (Bruinsma et al., 2023) and the TNP-A architecture (Nguyen & Grover, 2022), where each autoregressive step invalidates cached context representations. The key conceptual departure is decoupling the immutable set-based context (encoded once, cached) from a dynamic causal buffer that captures target-to-target dependencies, reducing complexity from O(K(N+K)²) to O(N²+NK+K²). The paper positions this as complementary to prior efficiency work on NPs that targeted scaling with context size N, whereas here the target is efficiency in autoregressive joint sampling and density evaluation specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× speedup in joint sampling and 7× memory reduction make autoregressive joint inference practical for large contexts, long target sequences, and batched generation—settings where standard AR deployment was previously prohibitive. The plug-and-play design (attention masks + role embeddings) means the buffer can be applied to existing pretrained NPs/PFNs via parameter-efficient fine-tuning without full retraining, broadening its practical reach. Open questions include scaling to longer horizons (the O(K²) buffer self-attention term and fixed positional embeddings limit extrapolation), whether speculative-decoding-style draft-verify strategies can close the small quality gap for long buffers, and whether the approach extends naturally to diffusion-based or flow-matching variants of these models.
