## Problem

Hu & Huang (2024) proved an 'inevitable' trade-off between watermark strength and speculative sampling efficiency, but their binary definition of watermark strength (preserved or not) overlooked intermediate levels and prevented a nuanced characterization of the frontier. This paper shows the trade-off is not absolute by introducing a continuous, information-theoretic measure of watermark strength and a mechanism that simultaneously achieves maximal strength and maximal efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261043/cropped_page5_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a lightweight draft model Q, a larger target model P, and a three-component pseudorandom seed ζ = (ζ^D, ζ^T, ζ^R) controlling draft sampling, target sampling, and acceptance decisions respectively. The output is a sequence of watermarked tokens that are fully deterministic functions of the pseudorandom variables—no external randomness enters the generation loop. The acceptance variable u_t = G(ζ^R) replaces the standard random coin flip and is retained for use at detection time to select the correct test statistic.

## Method

The paper defines watermark strength as the expected KL divergence E_ζ[D_KL(P_ζ‖P)], proves it equals Ent(P) − E_ζ[Ent(P_ζ)] and is maximized precisely when the watermarked distribution is degenerate, then formulates the trade-off as a constrained optimization problem yielding explicit Pareto curves for Gumbel-max and SynthID. The core mechanism replaces the random acceptance coin flip in speculative sampling with a pseudorandom variable u_t = G(ζ^R), making the entire token generation a deterministic function of pseudorandom variables. This preserves the maximal acceptance rate 1 − TV(Q,P) while keeping the output distribution degenerate, thereby attaining maximal watermark strength simultaneously. For detection, u_t is exploited to select the correct test statistic: Ars-τ thresholds u_t for Gumbel-max, and Bayes-MLP trains a three-layer perceptron to classify the source model for SynthID, both outperforming prior-based baselines that rely on empirical acceptance rates.

## Results

On the ELI5 dataset with Llama-7B/Llama-68M (K=4), Alg. 1 achieves AATPS of 2.0987 (Gumbel-max) and 2.0927 (SynthID), matching Standard Speculative Sampling (2.0988) and confirming that pseudorandom acceptance preserves sampling efficiency. For watermark detectability (TPR at FPR=1%), the proposed Ars-τ and Bayes-MLP detectors achieve higher TPR than their prior-based counterparts (Ars-Prior, Bayes-Prior) at the same token length, approaching oracle performance by ~200 tokens. Per-token time drops from 22.09 ms (basic Gumbel-max) to 17.96 ms (K=4), and log-perplexity remains stable (2.16 vs. 2.08), confirming unbiasedness is preserved.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Dathathri et al. (2024) / Google baselines (Ars-Prior and Bayes-Prior), the proposed Ars-τ and Bayes-MLP detectors achieve consistently higher TPR at FPR=1% across both Gumbel-max and SynthID watermarks, on both ELI5 and C4 datasets, for both Llama and Gemma model pairs. The improvement stems from using the pseudorandom acceptance variable u_t to correctly select the test statistic (draft vs. target) rather than relying on an empirical acceptance-rate prior or a simple weighted average. Against Standard Speculative Sampling (Chen et al., 2023), Alg. 1 matches AATPS almost exactly (e.g., 2.0987 vs. 2.0988 for Gumbel-max at K=4 on Llama), confirming that replacing the random coin flip with a pseudorandom one does not degrade acceptance efficiency. The PTT is slightly higher than Std. SpecSampl. (17.96 vs. 15.56 ms for Gumbel-max K=4) due to the overhead of watermark sampling, but still substantially faster than basic watermarking without speculative sampling (22.09 ms). Log-perplexity values remain within ~0.08 of the basic watermarking baseline, verifying that the method preserves the underlying output distribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly builds on Hu & Huang (2024), who proved an 'inevitable trade-off' between watermark strength and speculative sampling efficiency, but whose binary definition of watermark strength (exact distributional match or not) precluded a continuous characterization. It also extends the speculative sampling framework of Chen et al. (2023) and Leviathan et al. (2023) by replacing the truly random acceptance coin flip with a pseudorandom one, and applies to the two dominant unbiased watermark schemes: Gumbel-max (Aaronson, 2023) and SynthID (Dathathri et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is showing that the impossibility result was an artifact of the binary strength definition, and that a quantitative KL-divergence-based measure reveals the trade-off is surmountable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By breaking a previously proven 'inevitable' trade-off, this work removes a fundamental barrier to deploying watermarks in production LLM inference, where speculative sampling is essential for cost and latency. The pseudorandom acceptance mechanism is simple to implement (one line change in the accept/reject loop) and provably achieves both maximal watermark strength and maximal sampling efficiency simultaneously. However, the theoretical guarantee requires degenerate (point-mass) watermarks, limiting applicability to non-degenerate or biased schemes; extending the framework to those settings, to tree-based speculative decoding, and to robustness under human edits are all identified as open problems.
