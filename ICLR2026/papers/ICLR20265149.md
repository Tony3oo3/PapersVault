## Problem

Existing multi-modal entity alignment (MMEA) methods assume that both intra-entity (entity-attribute) and inter-graph (entity-entity, attribute-attribute) correspondences are perfectly labeled, an assumption routinely violated in real-world MMKGs due to crowdsourced annotation errors and semi-automated cross-graph matching. This paper formalizes the resulting Dual-level Noisy Correspondence (DNC) problem and shows empirically that over 50% of entity pairs in ICEWS benchmarks are affected, causing severe performance degradation for all prior methods.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265149/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two heterogeneous MMKGs where each entity carries multi-modal attributes (structured relation triples, CLIP-encoded images, and textual names), and produces a ranked list of candidate equivalent entities from the target graph along with per-correspondence reliability scores derived from Dirichlet distribution parameters. At test time, a Qwen2.5-VL-72B MLLM performs Chain-of-Thought rethinking on top-k candidate attribute pairs to refine similarity scores, with the final prediction being the argmax of the joint (prior + rethinking) similarity.

## Method

RULE estimates correspondence reliability via a two-fold principle: (1) uncertainty, quantified through Dempster-Shafer evidence mapped to a Dirichlet distribution, and (2) consensus, measuring whether the highest belief mass aligns with the annotated pair (proven necessary by Theorem 1, which shows low uncertainty alone does not guarantee correct correspondence). Pairs are divided into three subsets (high-uncertainty S_U, low-consensus S_I, clean S_C) using self-adaptive thresholds, and a Dually Robust Learning loss applies tailored strategies per subset—excluding S_U, softening S_I via a consensus-weighted interpolation between the annotated label and softmax similarity, and standard optimization for S_C—while a KL regularization penalizes spurious evidence on unassociated pairs. For intra-entity fusion, a Dually Robust Fusion module weights each modality's embedding by its estimated inter-graph reliability, suppressing noisy attributes. At inference, a Test-time Correspondence Reasoning module prompts an MLLM with Chain-of-Thought to uncover latent attribute-attribute connections that prior similarity scores miss, combining the rethinking scores with the original scores for final entity identification.

## Results

On the Non-name protocol under Inherent DNC, RULE achieves an average H@1 of 73.8%, outperforming the best baseline PMF (68.6%) by 5.2 points and MEAformer (67.0%) by 6.8 points. Under the most challenging 50% DNC setting, RULE maintains 64.3% average H@1 while the strongest baseline MEAformer drops to 54.0% and EVA collapses to 10.0%. On the All-attributes protocol, RULE reaches 98.8% average H@1 (Inherent DNC) and 97.9% (50% DNC), consistently leading all seven baselines across all five benchmarks (ICEWS-WIKI, ICEWS-YAGO, and three DBP15K language pairs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RULE outperforms all seven baselines across every dataset and noise level. Against the strongest baseline PMF (68.6% avg H@1 under Inherent DNC), RULE leads by 5.2 points; the gap widens to 11.6 points under 50% DNC (64.3% vs. 52.7%). MEAformer is the second-best method (67.0% inherent, 54.0% at 50% DNC), trailing RULE by 6.8 and 10.3 points respectively. XGEA (66.4% / 53.4%) and UMAEA (64.7% / 49.6%) show similar vulnerability to noise, with UMAEA degrading more sharply. MCLEA (61.3% / 41.1%) and HHEA (47.6% / 44.1%) are consistently weaker, with HHEA performing poorly on DBP15K benchmarks regardless of noise level. EVA is the most fragile baseline, collapsing to near-zero H@1 on ICEWS benchmarks under 50% DNC (0.5% on ICEWS-WIKI, 0.0% on ICEWS-YAGO), highlighting its complete inability to handle noisy correspondences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the noisy correspondence learning line (Huang et al., 2021; Yang et al., 2021) from single-level pair noise to a dual-level formulation specific to multi-modal entity alignment, where both intra-entity (entity-attribute) and inter-graph (entity-entity, attribute-attribute) correspondences can be corrupted. Methodologically, it repurposes evidential deep learning (Sensoy et al., 2018) via Dirichlet-parameterized uncertainty and a novel consensus principle to estimate correspondence reliability, then applies tailored loss strategies per reliability subset. It also departs from prior robust MMEA work like REA (Pei et al., 2020), which handles only uni-modal, entity-entity noise at training time, by addressing multi-modal dual-level noise and introducing a test-time MLLM-based correspondence reasoning module (using Qwen2.5-VL-72B with chain-of-thought prompting) that has no direct precedent in the entity alignment literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that over 50% of entity pairs in real-world ICEWS benchmarks suffer from some form of noisy correspondence validates that the faultless-correspondence assumption underlying all prior MMEA methods is severely violated in practice, making robustness a prerequisite rather than a nice-to-have. The test-time MLLM reasoning module is a notable direction for entity alignment, but its dependence on a 72B-parameter model at inference time raises significant scalability and cost concerns for large-scale KGs (10M+ entities). Open questions include whether the dual-level reliability estimation generalizes to settings with missing modalities or asymmetric graph structures, whether lighter-weight reasoning (e.g., smaller MLLMs or learned surrogates) can capture similar gains, and how the approach performs when the noise is adversarially structured rather than random.
