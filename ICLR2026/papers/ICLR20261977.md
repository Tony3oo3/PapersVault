## Problem

Directly applying vector quantization to Mixture-of-Experts LLMs suffers from two structural obstacles: (1) experts encode highly overlapping weight patterns, causing the limited codebook to repeatedly quantize near-identical representations and wasting capacity; and (2) quantization-induced output bias is amplified by the gating-weighted aggregation of multiple experts, producing severe distributional shifts that degrade accuracy far more than in dense models. No prior VQ framework accounts for these MoE-specific failure modes.

## Input / Output

The method consumes per-layer expert weight matrices (all shared and routing experts in a MoE block) together with a small calibration set of input activations (256 sequences of 4096 tokens from RedPajama) used to estimate the input covariance for KLT and the per-channel output statistics for bias correction. It outputs, per expert, a full-precision low-rank shared component (rank k = ic/128), a VQ-quantized expert-specific residual encoded as codebook indices (subvector length 4, k-means++ trained codebook), and two per-output-channel scalars (scale s and bias b) for affine correction. The total overhead over a pure 2-bit VQ baseline is only ~0.08 bits per weight.

## Method

KBVQ-MoE decomposes each MoE layer's expert weights into a shared part and an expert-specific part via Input-driven Redundancy Elimination (IDRE): a Karhunen-Loève Transform on calibration activations builds an input-coherence basis, all expert weights are projected into this basis and stacked, and a truncated SVD (top-k right singular vectors, k = ic/128) extracts the dominant cross-expert shared subspace, which is retained at full precision. Only the remaining expert-specific residuals are then compressed with standard vector quantization (k-means codebook, subvector length 4). To counter the distributional shift caused by quantizing the residuals, Bias-Corrected Output Stabilization (BCOS) applies a per-channel affine correction y_corr = (1+s)⊙(W_VQ x) + b, where s and b are closed-form MMSE-optimal parameters that match the mean and variance of the quantized output to the full-precision output. The KLT step is theoretically justified by showing that the output MSE equals the Frobenius norm of the weight error in the input-coherence space, making SVD in that space the optimal redundancy-removal operator.

## Results

At 3-bit quantization, KBVQ-MoE achieves near-FP16 accuracy across all four MoE models: 67.99 avg accuracy on Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B (vs. 68.07 FP16), 69.09 on Qwen3-30B-A3B (vs. 70.24), 78.35 on Mixtral-8x7B (vs. 78.57), and 68.73 on DeepSeekV2-Lite (vs. 70.68). At the more challenging 2-bit setting, KBVQ-MoE dramatically outperforms all baselines, e.g., reducing WikiText-2 perplexity from 115.30 (direct VQ) to 11.87 on Qwen3-30B-A3B while raising average accuracy from 30.61 to 63.37. The method also achieves a 1.58–1.60× decoder speedup over BF16 inference with negligible additional overhead (<0.1% FLOPs).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against scalar quantization baselines, KBVQ-MoE dominates at 2-bit: on Qwen3-30B-A3B it achieves 63.37 avg accuracy versus GPTQ's 33.06 and MoEQuant's 28.94, while at 3-bit it edges out GPTQ (69.09 vs. 63.51 on Qwen3-30B-A3B; 78.35 vs. 77.43 on Mixtral-8x7B) and MoEQuant (69.09 vs. 37.32 on Qwen3-30B-A3B). Against direct VQ, the gap is even more pronounced at 2-bit (e.g., 63.37 vs. 30.61 avg accuracy on Qwen3-30B-A3B), confirming that the IDRE+BCOS pipeline is essential for VQ to work in MoE settings. As a plug-in, KBVQ-MoE's modules improve GPTVQ by ~30% in perplexity at 3-bit and VPTQ by >15% at 2-bit on Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B. Against structural MoE compression methods on Mixtral-8x7B, KBVQ-MoE achieves 87% memory saving (vs. 50% for Sub-MoE, 60% for D2-MoE, 84% for EAC-MoE) while also attaining the best perplexity (4.07 vs. 6.97/6.46/4.58) and highest ARC-Challenge accuracy (0.63 vs. 0.45/0.38/0.55), demonstrating that structure-preserving quantization outperforms expert-merging or pruning approaches at comparable or higher compression ratios.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of vector quantization (VQ) for LLMs and MoE-specific compression. Prior VQ methods (GPTVQ, VPTQ, AQLM, QuIP#, QTIP) were designed for dense architectures and, when applied directly to MoE models, ignore two structural properties: cross-expert weight redundancy and bias amplification through expert aggregation. The paper repurposes KLT (from signal processing) and SVD (from low-rank approximation) in a novel MoE-specific pipeline—projecting expert weights into an input-coherence space before extracting shared components—rather than building on a single prior quantization method. It also positions itself against MoE compression approaches like D2-MoE and SubMoE (structural/low-rank) and EAC-MoE (routing-aware pruning + quantization), arguing that structure-preserving weight quantization is a distinct and underexplored axis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: achieving near-FP16 accuracy at 3 bits (e.g., 67.99 vs. 68.07 on Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B) and competitive 2-bit results with 87% memory reduction opens a path for deploying large MoE LLMs on edge devices where 160+ GB of GPU memory is infeasible. Key limitations include the empirically fixed SVD truncation rank (1/128), validation only on decoder-only architectures, and absence of 1-bit or hybrid-bit experiments. Open questions include whether adaptive rank selection, encoder-decoder or multimodal MoE extensions, and more expressive codebook structures can push the method into even more extreme compression regimes.
