## Problem

Causal interventions used in mechanistic interpretability (activation patching, DAS, SAEs) assume that counterfactual model states created by patching are realistic for the target model, yet this assumption is rarely tested. The paper demonstrates that intervened representations commonly diverge from the model's natural distribution, raising the question of whether resulting mechanistic claims faithfully reflect the model's natural-state circuitry or instead exploit off-manifold artifacts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260387/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes frozen neural network hidden-layer activations (h vectors), source and target representations for interchange interventions, and CL vectors constructed by averaging natural representations that share the same causal variable values as the post-intervention state. It produces a trained alignment matrix (a symmetric invertible linear transform), intervened representations, and quantitative divergence metrics (EMD, IIA, local PCA distance). In the synthetic setting, inputs are 18-dimensional vectors (2 causal feature dimensions + 16 noise dimensions) with 10 class labels; in the LLM setting, inputs are residual-stream activations from Llama-3-8B-Instruct at specific token positions and layers.

## Method

The paper first proves that coordinate patching guarantees off-manifold representations for any convex manifold shape other than axis-aligned hyperrectangles, then empirically confirms divergence across mean-difference patching, SAE reconstruction, and Boundless DAS on Llama-3-8B. It formalizes two divergence regimes: 'harmless' divergences confined to the behavioral null-space of downstream layers, and 'pernicious' divergences that activate hidden ReLU pathways or prime dormant behavioral flips across contexts. To mitigate pernicious cases, the paper applies the Counterfactual Latent (CL) auxiliary loss from Grant (2025)—a combined L2 and cosine distance between the intervened vector and a CL vector (the mean of natural representations matching the intervened variable values)—as a weighted term alongside the DAS behavioral loss. A modified CL loss (L'_CL) is then introduced that decomposes the objective across individual causal subspaces discovered by DAS, applying the L2+cosine penalty only along those dimensions. On a 7B LLM Boundless DAS task, the CL loss reduces EMD while preserving IIA; on synthetic 10-class tasks, the modified CL loss improves out-of-distribution intervention accuracy, with a significant negative regression between training EMD and OOD IIA (R²=0.73, p<0.001).

## Results

In the synthetic 10-class setting, the modified CL loss reduces representational divergence (EMD along causal dimensions) from 0.032 ± 0.003 (DAS behavioral loss) to 0.007 ± 0.001 while maintaining or slightly improving Interchange Intervention Accuracy (IIA: 0.9988 ± 0.0005 vs. 0.997 ± 0.001). In the Boundless DAS setting on Llama-3-8B, the CL auxiliary loss decreases EMD of intervened representations while preserving IIA at small CL weights ε. A linear regression across 30 training runs confirms that training EMD predicts OOD IIA with a significant negative coefficient (−0.34, R² = 0.73, F(1,28) = 75.28, p < .001), establishing that lower divergence yields better out-of-distribution intervention performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is the original DAS behavioral loss from Wu et al. (2023), which trains the alignment matrix solely to maximize counterfactual intervention accuracy (IIA). The paper's CL loss (both the full version applied to Boundless DAS on Llama-3-8B and the modified subspace-restricted version in synthetic settings) consistently reduces representational divergence (EMD) by roughly 4–5× compared to the behavioral-loss-only baseline, while matching or slightly exceeding its IIA. In the OOD transfer experiment—where an alignment trained on a dense class partition is evaluated on a sparse partition—the CL loss outperforms the behavioral loss, and the regression analysis quantifies this: each unit increase in training EMD corresponds to a 0.34-unit decrease in OOD IIA. The paper does not claim the CL loss eliminates all pernicious divergence (hidden pathways can still exist at reduced magnitude), but it demonstrates that minimizing divergence is a practical and effective first step toward more faithful causal interventions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper systematizes and makes explicit a concern that prior work has only implicitly raised: that causal interventions (activation patching, DAS, SAE reconstruction) produce representations outside the model's natural distribution. It builds directly on the DAS framework (Geiger et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2023) and the Counterfactual Latent loss from Grant (2025), while drawing on observations from Zhang & Nanda (2024) on noise-corrupted patching and Makelov et al. (2023) on null-space/dormant subspace interactions. The key departure is providing a unified theoretical taxonomy—harmless (null-space) vs. pernicious (hidden-pathway-activating) divergence—and a patch-closure theorem showing that coordinate patching guarantees off-manifold points for all convex manifolds except axis-aligned hyperrectangles, a result not previously formalized in the interpretability literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a foundational, often-unexamined assumption in mechanistic interpretability: that counterfactual states produced by interventions are realistic for the target model. If divergence can activate hidden circuits that produce misleadingly confirmatory behavior, then many published circuit claims may rest on off-manifold computations rather than natural mechanisms. However, the pernicious-divergence examples are entirely synthetic toy networks, the CL loss mitigation is demonstrated on a single 7B LLM task and simple synthetic MLPs, and the authors explicitly note they lack a principled method for classifying which divergences are harmful for a given claim. Open questions include whether the CL loss scales to complex multi-layer interventions, whether hidden-pathway activation occurs in real LLMs at the scale of published circuit claims, and how to detect dormant behavioral changes without exhaustive context evaluation.
