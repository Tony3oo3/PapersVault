## Problem

Existing deep multi-view clustering methods suffer from two key limitations: they neglect preserving sample neighborhood structures across views, weakening inter-sample relationship consistency, and they treat all view pairs equally in contrastive learning, forcing alignment even when views have substantial distributional differences. This forced alignment causes representation conflicts and semantic degradation, particularly on datasets with pronounced inter-view disparities.

## Input / Output

The method takes V raw multi-view feature matrices (each of shape N×d_v with potentially different dimensions per view) and a target number of clusters K. View-specific autoencoders first compress each view into a shared latent dimension d, after which a shared MLP produces N×K softmax cluster assignment matrices per view. Final discrete labels are obtained by averaging the assignment matrices across views and taking argmax, yielding one cluster label per sample.

## Method

RAV combines three components trained jointly: (1) view-specific autoencoders for denoised feature extraction, (2) a cross-view relation alignment module that builds view-specific and global pairwise similarity matrices via a Gaussian kernel on deep features and aligns them through a global-supervise-local contrastive objective, and (3) a view-aware adaptive label contrastive loss that computes Wasserstein Distance between each pair of view feature distributions to derive pairwise weights, then modulates the cluster-assignment contrastive loss so that high-similarity view pairs receive stronger alignment while low-similarity pairs are down-weighted to avoid representation conflicts. The key design insight is that the relation alignment stabilizes neighborhood structure, which in turn enables more accurate measurement of inter-view similarity for the adaptive weighting, creating a synergistic loop between structural consistency and semantic alignment.

## Results

RAV achieves state-of-the-art or near-state-of-the-art results across 10 benchmark multi-view clustering datasets. Headline improvements include +4.4% ACC on NGs (0.980 vs. 0.936 for SSLNMVC), +2.5% ACC on Cora (0.592 vs. 0.567 for MVCAN), and +7.8% ACC on YouTubeVideo (0.356 vs. 0.318 for SEM). On Digit-Product, RAV matches DFL-NET at 0.998 ACC / 0.993 NMI, while on NoisyMNIST it reaches 0.996 ACC / 0.986 NMI, comparable to SCMVC (0.996/0.988). The method slightly underperforms MVCAN on ALOI (0.826 vs. 0.849 ACC) and Caltech-5V (0.901 vs. 0.919 ACC).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAV clearly outperforms all nine baselines on Cora (0.592 ACC vs. 0.567 for MVCAN, the runner-up) and YouTubeVideo (0.356 ACC vs. 0.318 for SEM), where the view-aware adaptive weighting mechanism is most impactful due to large inter-view discrepancies. On NGs, RAV leads by 4.4% ACC over SSLNMVC (0.980 vs. 0.936), and on NUSWIDE it edges out SSLNMVC by 1.0% ACC (0.647 vs. 0.637). On Digit-Product, RAV ties DFL-NET at 0.998 ACC / 0.993 NMI, and on NoisyMNIST and Fashion it matches SCMVC, MFLVC, and SSLNMVC at 0.994–0.996 ACC, reflecting the limited benefit of adaptive weighting when view differences are small. RAV is outperformed by MVCAN on ALOI (0.826 vs. 0.849 ACC) and Caltech-5V (0.901 vs. 0.919 ACC), which the authors attribute to MVCAN's non-contrastive design being less sensitive to view heterogeneity. Overall, RAV wins or ties on 8 out of 10 datasets, with the two losses confined to datasets where MVCAN's architecture happens to be well-suited.

## Relation to Prior Work

RAV builds on the deep multi-view clustering paradigm of view-specific autoencoders for representation learning (as in MFLVC, DFL-NET) and contrastive learning at both sample and cluster levels (as in GCFAgg, SEM, DFL-NET). It departs from prior adaptive-weighting approaches (SEM, SCMVC) that modulate view influence at the feature level by instead applying Wasserstein-distance-based weighting at the cluster-assignment (label) level, and introduces a dedicated cross-view relation alignment module using Gaussian-kernel similarity matrices that has no direct counterpart in the compared baselines. The combination of global-guide-local relation alignment with view-aware label contrastive learning is the paper's core architectural novelty, positioning RAV as a synthesis of graph-structure preservation and adaptive contrastive strategies within a unified autoencoder framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical and recurring failure mode in deep MVC: forced alignment of dissimilar views causing representation degradation. By shifting adaptive weighting from the feature space to the label space and grounding it in Wasserstein distance over deep features, RAV offers a principled mechanism that practitioners can adopt in existing contrastive MVC pipelines. However, the method remains limited to complete, clean multi-view data; the authors themselves flag incomplete views and noisy data as future directions. Open questions include whether the Gaussian-kernel relation alignment generalizes to very high-dimensional or sparse views, and whether the WD-based weighting can be replaced by cheaper approximations for large-scale settings (e.g., the 101K-sample YouTubeVideo dataset).
