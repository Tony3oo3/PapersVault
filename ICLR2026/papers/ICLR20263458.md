## Problem

In-context learning has shown limited success in molecular design, where properties span millions of biological assays and material measurements, each with only a handful of labeled examples—enough to form a task context but insufficient to train a new model. Existing Graph Diffusion Transformers handle at most five properties in a single vector, making them infeasible for the millions of distinct assays encountered in practice. The paper asks whether a molecular foundation model can perform in-context design from a few molecule-score demonstrations without task-specific training.

## Input / Output

The model takes a set of molecule-score demonstration pairs (partitioned into positive [0.75,1], medium [0.5,0.75), and negative [0,0.5) groups, up to 45 total) and a target query score (typically 1) as input. Molecules are encoded at the motif level via a Node Pair Encoding tokenizer that compresses atom-level graphs by ~5.5×, and scores are encoded as positional signals via Rotary Position Embedding rather than as property vectors. The output is a set of generated molecules; in practice, 1000 candidates are produced and a fingerprint-based consistency score (checking whether the molecule is more similar to positive than medium than negative demonstrations) filters them to the top 10 for evaluation.

## Method

DemoDiff replaces the fixed property-vector conditioning in Graph Diffusion Transformers with molecule-score demonstration pairs as in-context examples, so the denoising Transformer implicitly infers the latent task concept from the context and guides the reverse diffusion trajectory accordingly. To make this scalable, the paper introduces Node Pair Encoding (NPE), a BPE-inspired tokenizer that iteratively merges the most frequent neighboring motif pairs into a vocabulary of 3000 motifs (including 300 ring motifs as atomic units to prevent reconstruction ambiguity), reducing the average node count from 30 atoms to 5 motifs. The 0.7B-parameter Graph DiT backbone (24 layers, 1280 hidden, 16 heads) is pretrained for 550 epochs on 1.6 million tasks constructed from 1.08M molecules and 155K unique properties spanning ChEMBL bioactivity and polymer material data, using a discrete diffusion transition matrix built from motif and bond marginal distributions. At inference, a consistency score computed from Tanimoto fingerprint similarities across the positive/medium/negative groups serves as a confidence filter, improving harmonic-mean scores by 0.8–27.5% across task categories by removing generations that violate the demonstration ordering.

## Results

DemoDiff (0.7B parameters) achieves an average rank of 4.10 across 33 molecular design tasks in six categories, outperforming the best baseline GraphGA (rank 6.56) and all 19 other baselines (ranks 6.56–17.95). The harmonic mean of oracle score and diversity over Top-10 generations reaches 0.79 on Drug Design, 0.78 on Target-Based design, and 0.67 on Material Design, while scoring 0.44–0.56 on structure-constrained tasks. DemoDiff matches or surpasses LLMs 100–1000× larger (DeepSeek-V3, GPT-4o, Qwen-Max) using only ~23 in-context demonstrations per task, with a total sum score of 20.10 versus 17.64 for the best baseline (GenMol).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DemoDiff outperforms all 19 baselines on average rank (4.10 vs. 6.56–17.95) and total sum (20.10 vs. 16.65 max for GraphGA/GenMol). Against molecular optimization methods (GraphGA, REINVENT, GPBO, STONED, Genetic GFN, GenMol) under both 100-oracle and 10,000-predictor-call settings, DemoDiff leads on Drug Design (0.79 vs. 0.55 max for GenMol) and Material Design (0.67 vs. 0.62 for GenMol), while trailing slightly on Drug MPO (0.54 vs. 0.52 for GraphGA). Among ICL-based LLMs, DemoDiff surpasses GPT-4o (rank 7.89) and DeepSeek-V3 (rank 8.08) on average rank, and dramatically outperforms Qwen-Max (rank 15.39) and fine-tuned 8B models (ranks 14.91, 10.96). The key advantage is structural diversity: LLMs produce high-scoring but structurally similar molecules, while DemoDiff maintains diversity scores of 0.79–0.87 across categories, enabling it to win on the harmonic mean metric that balances score and diversity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DemoDiff extends Graph Diffusion Transformers (Liu et al., 2024c) by replacing fixed property-vector conditions with molecule-score demonstration pairs, thereby importing the in-context learning paradigm from NLP into molecular design. The discrete diffusion machinery follows DiGress (Vignac et al., 2022) with transition matrices, while the scalable Transformer backbone draws on Peebles & Xie (2023). A key departure from prior molecular tokenizers (BRICS, molecular grammars) is the data-driven Node Pair Encoding, which builds a motif vocabulary via frequency-based merging analogous to BPE, achieving 5.5× compression over atom-level representations while preserving lossless reconstruction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental scalability bottleneck: molecular properties span millions of assays with often fewer than ten labeled examples each, making property-vector conditioning infeasible. By framing design as ICL over demonstrations, DemoDiff (0.7B) matches or surpasses LLMs 100–1000× larger across 33 tasks, suggesting that domain-specific diffusion architectures can be far more parameter-efficient than general-purpose LLMs for structured generation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to 3D conformations, whether the Zipfian task distribution observed in pretraining is necessary for ICL emergence, and whether the weaker performance on structure-constrained tasks (vs. property-driven ones) can be closed with better consistency scoring or richer fingerprints.
