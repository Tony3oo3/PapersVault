## Problem

Directly applying LLMs to VQA suffers from high computational cost, poor performance on specialized domains, and a lack of reliable uncertainty quantification. No existing framework systematically exploits the complementary strengths of LLMs (broad general knowledge) and task-specific VQA models (domain expertise) through calibrated confidence-based routing, leaving a gap between the two model types that could be bridged for both accuracy and efficiency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263261/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes an RGB image and a free-form text question as input. It produces a single text answer along with a calibrated confidence score from the TS-VQA model. During inference, the confidence score routes the query through one of three paths: direct TS-VQA answer, LLM answer without candidates, or LLM answer augmented with a dynamically selected number of TS-VQA candidate answers. The LLM receives image context via PNP-VQA-generated captions and BLIP-2-selected in-context examples rather than raw pixels.

## Method

Uni-VQA introduces a three-tiered confidence-guided knowledge exchange between a calibrated TS-VQA model and an LLM. The calibration is achieved through a Diverse Ensemble (DE) strategy that trains E TS-VQA models under Distributionally Robust Optimization with varying temperature parameter λ, producing complementary experts (hard-sample specialists and general-pattern specialists) whose averaged logits yield well-calibrated confidence scores. At inference, high-confidence queries (c ≥ u) are answered directly by TS-VQA; low-confidence queries (c < l) are delegated entirely to the LLM; and medium-confidence queries (l ≤ c < u) trigger a collaborative mode where the LLM receives K(c) dynamically selected candidate answers from TS-VQA, with K determined by a learned exponential mapping that provides more candidates at lower confidences. The paper provides a theoretical proof (Lemma 4.1, Theorem 4.2) showing that the DE loss upper-bounds a regularized cross-entropy with negative entropy, guaranteeing that more incorrect predictions are shifted into the low-confidence region for LLM correction. An optional knowledge distillation step compresses the ensemble into a single model with comparable ECE and accuracy while reducing latency by up to 60%.

## Results

On VQA-v2, Uni-VQA achieves 71.00–74.33% accuracy across five TS-VQA backbones (Pythia, CLIP-ViL, ViLBERT, VisualBERT, BEiT-3), consistently outperforming the LLM-only baseline (Mistral-7B, 69.09%) while delegating only 36–79% of questions to the LLM. On COCO-QA, Uni-VQA reaches 74.34–76.01% accuracy versus 72.03% for LLM-only, with ECE reduced to 0.02–0.08 (vs. 0.27 for LLM-only). The calibrated TS-VQA component alone (without LLM) already reduces ECE from 0.14–0.20 (standard) to 0.02–0.08, and the full Uni-VQA framework further improves accuracy by 1.9–5.7 points over the LLM-only baseline while cutting inference latency by up to 20%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Uni-VQA outperforms the LLM-only baseline (Mistral-7B) on both datasets across all five TS-VQA backbones: on VQA-v2 it gains +1.9 to +5.3 points in accuracy (e.g., 72.98% with CLIP-ViL vs. 69.09% LLM-only), and on COCO-QA it gains +2.3 to +3.98 points (e.g., 76.01% with BEiT-3 vs. 72.03% LLM-only), while delegating only 36–79% of questions to the LLM instead of 100%. Compared to the VectorScale post-hoc calibration baseline, Uni-VQA achieves substantially higher accuracy (e.g., 71.00% vs. 65.59% for Pythia on VQA-v2) and lower ECE (0.05 vs. 0.09), demonstrating that the diverse-ensemble calibration plus dynamic LLM delegation is far more effective than simple temperature scaling. Against the LLM-VQA and LLM-VectorScale threshold-based delegation baselines, Uni-VQA matches their peak accuracy while requiring 10–20% fewer LLM delegations (Table 2), translating to lower inference cost. The calibrated TS-VQA component alone (without any LLM) already improves ECE by 2–5× over standard VQA (e.g., 0.03 vs. 0.14 for VisualBERT on VQA-v2), confirming that the DRO-based diverse ensemble is the key enabler for reliable confidence-guided routing. The only case where Uni-VQA's gain over LLM-only is modest is BEiT-3 on VQA-v2 (+1.14 points), which the authors attribute to BEiT-3 already being highly competitive with the LLM on that dataset.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) model cascades and confidence-based deferral (Wang et al., 2017; Jitkrittum et al., 2023; Enomoto & Eda, 2021), which route inputs between cheap and expensive models; (2) calibration via ensembles, particularly DRO-based diverse ensembles (Sapkota & Yu, 2023; Duchi & Namkoong, 2019) and deep ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017); and (3) LLM-augmented VQA (Yang et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2023). The key departure from prior cascade work is the introduction of confidence-guided *knowledge exchange*—the TS-VQA doesn't merely defer or not, but dynamically shares a variable number of candidate answers with the LLM, with the count K(c) learned as a function of confidence. Unlike RAG, which augments the LLM with external textual evidence on every query, Uni-VQA uses a calibrated TS-VQA to control *when* and *how much* the LLM is invoked, making the two approaches orthogonal and composable.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for anyone deploying VQA systems where LLM inference cost is a bottleneck: matching LLM-only accuracy with ~20–50% delegation (vs. 100%) translates directly into lower latency, cost, and carbon footprint. The DRO-based diverse ensemble calibration technique is broadly applicable beyond VQA to any setting where calibrated confidence gates a cascade. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more knowledge-intensive benchmarks (OK-VQA, A-OKVQA), whether it benefits from smaller/faster LLMs, and how it interacts with RAG augmentation for the low-confidence queries that are routed without candidates. The limited improvement on BEiT-3 (already near-LLM accuracy) highlights that the framework's value is most pronounced when the TS-VQA is meaningfully weaker than the LLM.
