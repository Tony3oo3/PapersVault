## Problem

Multimodal language models undergo safety alignment that focuses exclusively on the assistant role and a fixed chat-template structure, leaving the user role significantly under-aligned and the model brittle to minor structural perturbations. While prior adversarial attacks modify query content within a fixed prompt, no prior work has shown that purely structural manipulations—swapping role tags or repositioning modality tokens—can bypass refusal mechanisms without altering the query at all.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query paired with an RGB image, wrapped in a model-specific chat template (e.g., <|user|>...<|assistant|>), and produces a text response. The key distinction is that the attack operates on the template structure itself—swapping <|user|> and <|assistant|> tags or relocating the <|image|> token to one of four positions—while leaving the query text completely unchanged. Evaluation uses two automatic judges (target-string matching and Llama-Guard-3-8B) to score response harmfulness, plus VQA-V2 accuracy/reward and benign Alpaca refusal rates to measure utility preservation.

## Method

Role-Modality Attacks (RMA) comprise two orthogonal structural manipulations: Role Confusion (swapping user and assistant role tokens so the model generates from the less-aligned user perspective) and Modality Manipulation (relocating the image token to the end of the user turn or the start of the assistant turn), yielding 8 attack configurations (4 modality positions × 2 role states). For interpretability, the paper extracts refusal feature directions via difference-in-means on residual stream activations and computes attack vectors as the mean activation shift between original and adversarially perturbed harmful prompts, then analyzes their alignment with the negative refusal direction using both cosine similarity and a proposed projection-coefficient metric that better captures compositional strength. For mitigation, the paper applies adversarial training with QLoRA (4-bit quantized LoRA on the LLM backbone only), training on ~10K harmful and benign queries each perturbed with all 8 RMA settings, so the model learns to base refusal decisions on query content rather than superficial structural cues, reducing average ASR from 21–75% to 0–2.6% while preserving VQA-V2 utility and keeping benign refusal rates below ~15%.

## Results

On AdvBench (target-string matching), the average ASR across all RMA settings (excluding the no-attack baseline) is 21.25% for Qwen2-VL-7B, 75.04% for LLaVA-1.5-7B, and 47.38% for Phi-3.5-vision, with the strongest composed setting (img out_swap) reaching 42.50%, 97.12%, and 80.00% respectively. After adversarial training (QLoRA, 1 epoch), ASR drops to 0.00%, 2.60%, and 2.31% on AdvBench and 0.00%, 5.89%, and 5.36% on HarmBench, while benign refusal rates on Alpaca remain below 16% and VQA-V2 accuracy is preserved (81.53% for Qwen, 67.88% for LLaVA, 62.88% for Phi). Compositional attacks consistently outperform individual ones: e.g., Qwen's swap (8.08%) + img end (5.96%) combine to img end_swap at 32.88% ASR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions RMA as orthogonal to content-based jailbreaks like GCG (Zou et al., 2023b), demonstrating that structural perturbations alone (without modifying query text) achieve competitive or superior ASR: on LLaVA, RMA's img out_swap reaches 97.12% ASR vs. GCG's 25.96% (no img no swap) on AdvBench. More importantly, composing RMA with GCG and other content-based attacks (AIM, Prefix Injection, Refusal Suppression, etc.) consistently increases ASR — e.g., on Qwen, GCG alone achieves 14.04% but GCG + Role Confusion reaches 82.12%, and GCG + img end_swap reaches 82.31%. This compositionality is explained via projection analysis onto the negative refusal direction, where composed attack vectors show stronger projection coefficients than their individual components even when cosine similarity alone does not capture the effect. The adversarial training defense reduces RMA ASR to near-zero across all settings while also providing incidental robustness gains against some content-based attacks (e.g., AIM drops from 35.38% to 9.23% on Qwen), though it does not fully neutralize all content-based jailbreaks like Style Injection or Payload Split.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the refusal-direction interpretability framework of Arditi et al. (2024) and Yang & Buzsáki (2024), which showed that refusal is mediated by a single linear direction in the residual stream, and extends it to a multimodal setting with a new projection-strength metric to explain attack compositionality. It departs from content-based jailbreak methods (GCG, AutoDAN, AIM) by operating purely on input structure—swapping role tokens and repositioning modality tokens—without modifying the query text, and from ChatBug (Jiang et al., 2024) by targeting role asymmetry and modality token placement rather than control-token mismatches. The adversarial training defense, which perturbs both harmful and benign prompts with all eight structural variants, is a novel application of structural invariance training to multimodal alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a fundamental and previously underappreciated blind spot: safety alignment is applied almost exclusively to the assistant role, leaving the user role significantly less aligned, and models are brittle to even minor structural deviations from their training-time chat template. The compositional nature of the attacks (two individually moderate perturbations yielding a much stronger combined attack) and their orthogonality to content-based jailbreaks mean the attack surface is larger than previously recognized. The practical mitigation via QLoRA-based adversarial training is effective and generalizes to unseen structural perturbations, but open questions remain about scalability to models with many modalities (where token-position permutations grow combinatorially) and whether the defense holds against adaptive attackers who can discover new structural variants. The downstream implications—synthetic conversation pollution via Magpie-style pipelines and training-data extraction through role confusion—broaden the concern beyond direct jailbreaking.
