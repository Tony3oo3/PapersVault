## Problem

LLM pre-training is governed by power-law scaling with sharply diminishing returns, making data curation essential for improving the data-quantity vs. model-quality Pareto frontier. However, it remains unclear whether cheap coverage-based heuristics (e.g., cluster sampling, deduplication) are sufficient, or whether expensive per-example quality evaluation via LLM reasoning provides genuine, non-recoverable gains. This paper conducts the first systematic, large-scale comparison of 22 data-curation strategies across multiple model sizes and sampling rates to disentangle the roles of quality and coverage.

## Input / Output

The methods consume a raw text corpus (C4, 184B tokens) and produce a curated subset at 10–80% sampling rates. ASK-LLM additionally requires an instruction-tuned proxy LLM (FLAN-T5-Small through XXL, or Gemma-7B) that scores each example by outputting the token probability Pr["yes"] in response to a fixed quality-assessment prompt; DENSITY requires 768-dimensional Sentence-T5-Base embeddings and a [1000×20000] p-stable hash sketch matrix. Both scorers are computed once over the full corpus and amortized across many downstream pre-training runs, with ASK-LLM using top-k selection and DENSITY using inverse propensity sampling to draw a uniform sample over the latent space.

## Method

The paper proposes two complementary samplers targeting opposite ends of the quality–coverage spectrum. ASK-LLM prompts an instruction-tuned LLM with each training example and uses the softmax probability of the token "yes" as a contextualized quality score, explicitly avoiding known perplexity-filtering failure modes (selecting context-free fragments, repetitive nonsense, or rejecting informative niche content). DENSITY estimates local data density in embedding space via a kernel sum approximated with p-stable hashing in O(N log N) time, then applies inverse propensity sampling with a two-pass procedure that provably converges to a uniform distribution over the latent space (Theorem C.2). Across 220 pre-training runs of T5-Small (60M) and T5-Large (800M) on 524B tokens and 1,100 fine-tuning evaluations on 111 downstream tasks, ASK-LLM consistently outperforms full-data training (achieving an effective model size of 1.5B with only 10% of the data and 70% faster convergence), while DENSITY recovers but does not exceed full-data performance, confirming that LLM-based quality filtering and coverage maximization address fundamentally different bottlenecks.

## Results

At 10% sampling (18B tokens), ASK-LLM with Gemma-7B achieves an Effective Model Size of 1.5B, meaning the 800M T5-Large model trained on only 10% of C4 performs as well as a 1.5B model trained on the full dataset. This translates to concrete gains over full-data training on MMLU (44.2 vs. 40.7), BBH (37.1 vs. 33.6), Reasoning (22.7 vs. 21.6), and QA (78.2 vs. 73.0). DENSITY sampling at 10% recovers full-data performance (EMS 802M vs. 800M) but does not exceed it. ASK-LLM also converges up to 70% faster than full-data training when using 60% of the dataset (Figure 1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ASK-LLM (Gemma-7B) decisively outperforms all baselines at 10% sampling: it achieves EMS 1.5B vs. 802M for DENSITY, 797M for Q-Classifier, 476M for DSIR, 423M for SSL Prototypes, and 301M for Perplexity filtering. DENSITY is the strongest coverage-based method, essentially matching full-data performance (EMS 802M vs. 800M) but falling short on Reasoning (19.1 vs. 21.6) and QA (70.6 vs. 73.0). Q-Classifier is the strongest non-LLM quality filter (EMS 797M), competitive with DENSITY but still far below ASK-LLM. Perplexity filtering and SSL Prototypes are substantially weaker, with EMS values of 301M and 423M respectively, indicating that simple in-distribution scoring or centroid-distance heuristics lose significant performance at aggressive sampling rates. The paper notes that ASK-LLM scores show near-zero Kendall Tau correlation with perplexity scores, confirming they capture fundamentally different (and complementary) signals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established data-curation lines for LLM pre-training: coverage-maximizing samplers (SemDeDup, SSL Prototypes, D4) and quality-scoring filters (perplexity filtering, Q-Classifier, DSIR). The DENSITY sampler adapts Coleman et al.'s (2022) one-pass diversified sampling to LLM latent embeddings with a two-pass procedure carrying stronger theoretical guarantees, while ASK-LLM departs from all prior scoring methods by leveraging the zero-shot reasoning of instruction-tuned LLMs as contextual quality raters rather than relying on perplexity or distribution-matching heuristics. The paper's core contribution is a systematic 22-method comparison that isolates the distinct roles of quality versus coverage, rather than proposing a single new algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that LLM-based quality filtering (ASK-LLM) can outperform full-data pre-training while converging up to 70% faster challenges the prevailing assumption that more data is always better and that coverage/diversity is the primary axis for pre-training data curation. Practically, a variant of ASK-LLM was already deployed in Gemma 3's pre-training pipeline, validating the approach at production scale. Key limitations temper generalizability: experiments are restricted to encoder-decoder T5 models on C4, with no decoder-only architectures, no coding evaluations, and no zero/few-shot prompting results. Open questions include whether the quality-over-coverage advantage persists at much larger scales (10B+ parameters, trillions of tokens) and whether the scoring cost remains amortizable as datasets grow.
