## Problem

Autoregressive neural PDE solvers accumulate error over long rollouts and require uniform temporal discretization, while continuous-time alternatives like Neural ODEs demand expensive backpropagation through ODE solvers. No existing framework simultaneously learns continuous-time PDE dynamics, handles irregular per-trajectory time grids during training, and supports arbitrary-resolution inference without the computational overhead of adjoint-based ODE backpropagation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262936/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Training consumes trajectories sampled at trajectory-specific, non-uniform time grids (e.g., 25% of original time points kept at random positions per trajectory), with spatial fields on grids ranging from 100 points (1D Burgers) to 128×128 (2D SWE/DR). Inference takes a single initial condition and produces the solution at any queried time via numerical ODE integration, supporting arbitrary temporal resolution, reverse-time integration, and evaluation at times never seen during training. All time series are normalized to [0,1].

## Method

CFO repurposes flow matching to learn the PDE right-hand side directly: for each trajectory, it fits a quintic Hermite spline through the snapshots, using three-point finite-difference stencils to estimate first and second time derivatives at knots, so the spline's analytic velocity closely approximates the true PDE dynamics. A neural operator (U-Net, FNO, DiT, or MLP) is then trained via a simple regression loss to predict this analytic spline velocity at randomly sampled times, with a small Gaussian noise term for regularization—no ODE solver is ever backpropagated through. At inference, the learned time-dependent vector field defines a continuous ODE that is integrated with RK4 (or any standard solver) at arbitrary step sizes, enabling forward rollouts, reverse-time inference, and querying at resolutions decoupled from training. The quintic spline choice (C² smooth, capturing acceleration) is key: it yields second-order derivative accuracy at knots and keeps the learned flow in high-probability regions consistent with the governing dynamics, enabling CFO trained on only 25% of irregularly sampled time points to outperform full-data autoregressive baselines by up to 87% relative error reduction.

## Results

Across four benchmarks (Lorenz, 1D Burgers, 2D diffusion-reaction, 2D shallow water), Quintic CFO trained on only 25% of irregularly subsampled time points outperforms autoregressive baselines trained on full-resolution data, achieving relative L₂ error reductions of 24.6%, 78.7%, 87.4%, and 82.8% respectively. For example, on the 2D diffusion-reaction equation, Quintic CFO at 25% data achieves (5.32±0.65)×10⁻² relative L₂ error versus (4.23±0.59)×10⁻¹ for the AR baseline. CFO also matches or exceeds AR accuracy using only 50% of the AR function-evaluation budget (NFE), with wall-clock inference times roughly 2× faster (e.g., 49.36 ms vs 117.18 ms/sample on DR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the autoregressive (AR) baseline (the primary comparison in Table 1), Quintic CFO trained on 25% irregularly subsampled data achieves lower relative L₂ error than AR trained on 100% full-resolution data on all four benchmarks, with the largest gap on 2D diffusion-reaction (87.4% error reduction: 5.32×10⁻² vs 4.23×10⁻¹). Against Neural ODE (Chen et al., 2018), CFO achieves substantially lower error (e.g., 0.0453 vs 0.101 on Lorenz; 0.00589 vs 0.0275 on Burgers) while training ~38× faster per batch (0.0035 vs 0.133 s/batch on Lorenz; 0.0092 vs 3.38 s/batch on Burgers), since CFO avoids backpropagation through ODE solvers. Against PDE-Refiner (Lippe et al., 2023), CFO achieves 3× lower error on DR (0.044 vs 0.125) and ~19× lower error on SWE (0.005 vs 0.093), while training ~3.5× faster per batch (0.40 vs 1.38–1.40 s/batch). On the additional Kuramoto-Sivashinsky benchmark, CFO achieves roughly 2× lower error than Neural ODE and 3× lower error than AR while matching AR's training time.

## Relation to Prior Work

CFO repurposes flow matching (Lipman et al., 2022) and stochastic interpolants (Albergo et al., 2023) from generative modeling to learn the right-hand side of PDEs, avoiding the expensive adjoint-based backpropagation through ODE solvers that plagues Neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018). Conceptually it follows the Method of Lines by learning a continuous-time operator, but replaces the fixed spatial discretization with a data-driven neural operator and constructs spline-based probability paths whose analytic velocities serve as training targets. Unlike generative PDE solvers (DiffusionPDE, CoCoGen, PBFM) that require explicit differential operators and multi-step sampling, CFO implicitly encodes dynamics through physics-aware spline paths and supports arbitrary temporal querying without governing equations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CFO demonstrates that continuous-time PDE learning need not sacrifice the computational efficiency of discrete methods, and that physics-aware probability path design (quintic splines with finite-difference derivatives) can overcome severe data scarcity—25% irregular subsampling outperforms full-data autoregressive baselines by up to 87%. Open questions include extending time-resolution invariance to spatial resolution (mesh-agnostic operators), accelerating inference beyond ODE integration (e.g., consistency distillation), and scaling to higher-dimensional or multi-physics systems. The reverse-time inference capability and architecture-agnosticism (FNO, U-Net, DiT all work) make the framework broadly applicable to real-world data with irregular temporal measurements.
