## Problem

Few-step (1–8 step) distilled diffusion and flow-matching models are incompatible with classifier-free guidance: forcing CFG causes oversaturation and mixed positive/negative content, while existing attention-space alternatives (NASA, NAG) apply a fixed guidance strength uniformly across all regions, layers, and timesteps. This lack of spatial and temporal adaptability limits their ability to suppress unwanted concepts, especially when the negative element is an essential part of the positive subject (e.g., removing wheels from a bike).

## Input / Output

The method takes a positive text prompt and a negative text prompt and produces a generated image or video in 1–8 diffusion steps (typically <3 s runtime). It is training-free and plugs into the attention computation of existing MMDiT architectures (Stable Diffusion 3.5 Turbo, Flux Schnell) and cross-attention models (Wan) without modifying weights. The negative prompt is duplicated internally, so the user-facing interface remains a single positive and a single negative prompt string.

## Method

VSF flips the sign of the value vectors (but not the keys) of negative-prompt tokens inside the attention computation, so that when image tokens attend to the negative prompt, the flipped values cancel the unwanted content in a noise-cancellation-like fashion. In cross-attention models this reduces to concatenating V⁺ and −αV⁻ along the sequence dimension before the softmax-weighted sum. For MMDiT-style models where image and text tokens share a single attention sequence, VSF duplicates the negative prompt into an unflipped copy N⁽⁰⁾ (which feeds the MLP path and self-attention) and a flipped copy N⁽¹⁾ (whose values are scaled by −α), then applies attention masks so that N⁽¹⁾ is attended to only by image tokens and does not act as a query, preventing cross-contamination between positive and negative prompts. A small attention bias (−β) on the I→N⁽¹⁾ path further preserves image quality. Because the sign flip operates on values rather than on the final attention output, the effective guidance strength is automatically modulated per token, per layer, and per timestep by the model's own attention weights, yielding adaptive suppression without any extra forward pass.

## Results

On NegGenBench (200 challenging positive-negative prompt pairs), VSF Strong achieves a negative score of 0.545 while maintaining a quality score of 0.952 and positive score of 0.870. VSF Quality achieves a negative score of 0.420 with the highest quality score (0.986) and positive score (0.980) among all methods. Both VSF variants substantially outperform NAG (0.220), NAG Strong (0.320), NASA (0.380), and even CFG at 28 steps (0.300) on negative prompt adherence, while incurring only ~3 seconds of runtime on few-step models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VSF dominates all in-paper few-step baselines on negative prompt adherence: VSF Strong (0.545) outperforms NASA (0.380) by 0.165, NAG Strong (0.320) by 0.225, and NAG (0.220) by 0.325, while also exceeding CFG at 28 steps (0.300) by 0.245. VSF Quality (0.420) similarly beats all few-step baselines and CFG, and additionally achieves the best quality score (0.986) and near-perfect positive adherence (0.980). Against external baselines in Table 1, VSF Strong surpasses the generate-then-edit pipeline (0.545 vs. 0.488 negative, 3s vs. 55s runtime), Janus-4o (0.545 vs. 0.225), and both Qwen-Image variants (0.545 vs. 0.190/0.100), and edges out Nano Banana (0.545 vs. 0.498). The only method that outperforms VSF on negative score is GPT-4o (0.705), a closed-weight model with 47s runtime. The trade-off analysis (Section 6.1) further shows VSF maintains positive and quality scores above 0.90 up to a negative score of ~0.60, whereas NAG and NASA degrade sharply below 0.50 negative score.

## Relation to Prior Work

VSF builds directly on NASA's (Nguyen et al., 2024) attention-space negative guidance for few-step models, but replaces the fixed-scale subtraction of positive and negative attention outputs with a sign flip on negative prompt values within the attention computation itself. This makes the guidance inherently adaptive per-token, per-layer, and per-step, extending Koulischer et al.'s (2025) dynamic guidance scale concept to token-level modulation and incorporating Schramowski et al.'s (2023) spatially-adaptive avoidance principle. The key architectural departure is the introduction of negative-embedding duplication with attention masking to make the sign-flip mechanism compatible with MMDiT-style models (SD3.5, Flux) where image and text tokens share a single attention sequence, a setting where prior methods (NASA, NAG) were not designed to operate.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VSF addresses a practical gap: few-step distilled models (1–8 steps) are incompatible with CFG, yet negative prompt adherence remains essential for content control and quality. The method's minimal overhead (one duplicated embedding, a sign flip, and a mask) makes it easy to integrate into existing pipelines, and the NegGenBench dataset provides a focused benchmark for evaluating negative prompt following on challenging prompt pairs where the negative element is a critical component of the positive object. Limitations include reliance on MLLM-based evaluation (LLaMA, Qwen-2.5-VL) with only 200 prompts and 10-prompt human validation, and the method's behavior on video generation is demonstrated but not deeply analyzed. Open questions include whether the fine-tuned negation-aware VLM can serve as a reliable automatic evaluator at scale, and how the approach generalizes to more complex compositional negations.
