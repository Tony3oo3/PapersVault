## Problem

Convolutional State Space Models (ConvSSMs) combine tensor-valued states with SSM dynamics for spatiotemporal modeling, but practical implementations like ConvS5 restrict state kernels to pointwise 1×1 convolutions to avoid kernel growth during parallel scans. This constraint fundamentally limits the state dynamics from capturing spatial correlations, leaving spatial modeling entirely to input/output kernels and deeper stacking. The paper asks whether extended 3×3 state kernels can be realized while preserving linear-time parallel training.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a sequence of U-channel 2D tensors (e.g., 64×64 video frames for Moving-MNIST or 2D grid states for PDEBench) at each timestep and produces the next frame or grid state. Internally, the state is a complex-valued tensor of shape H×W×P (spatial height × width × hidden state dimension), updated via structured tensor contractions rather than explicit convolutions. For PDE tasks, models receive 16 initial grid timesteps and predict the next single step; for video, they condition on 100 observed frames and autoregressively generate 400–1200 future frames.

## Method

ConvT3 structures the state tensor as a composition of a diagonalizable SSM matrix R along the hidden P dimension and a Proportional Tridiagonal Toeplitz (PTT) tensor S along spatial dimensions, where the PTT tensor enforces lower-diagonal = α × upper-diagonal proportionality so that all spatial slices share a common eigenbasis. This structure guarantees the full state tensor is diagonalizable via Q = Q_P ⊗ Q_H ⊗ Q_W, reducing the dynamics to elementwise multiplication in the transformed space and enabling standard linear-time parallel scans. The PTT eigenvalues are parameterized as a bilinear cosine function ε(θ_H, θ_W) = a + b·cos θ_H + c·cos θ_W + d·cos θ_H·cos θ_W, with positivity enforced via softmax reparameterization of four extreme-point values, while the SSM eigenvalues use a softplus-based Hurwitz condition to ensure negative real parts. The spatial eigenvector matrices Q_H and Q_W correspond to DST type-I transforms, so the forward/backward spatial transformations are implemented via FFT-based routines at O(H log H + W log W) cost rather than dense matrix multiplication.

## Results

On Moving-MNIST (trained on 600 frames, generating 800 future frames from 100 observed), ConvT3 achieves FVD 36, PSNR 17.7, SSIM 0.823, and LPIPS 0.104, outperforming ConvS5 (FVD 47, PSNR 16.4) and all other baselines on every metric. At the longer 1200-frame horizon, ConvT3 maintains its lead with FVD 56 and PSNR 16.7 versus ConvS5's FVD 71 and PSNR 15.6. On PDEBench, ConvT3 achieves the best NRMSE on both Shallow-Water (0.00033) and Diffusion-Reaction (0.0087) with only 6M parameters, while inference time is within 15% of ConvS5.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ConvT3 outperforms all eight baselines on the primary Moving-MNIST benchmark. Against the closest competitor ConvS5 (Smith et al., 2023), ConvT3 improves FVD by 23% (36 vs. 47) and PSNR by 1.3 dB (17.7 vs. 16.4) at 800-frame generation with 600-frame training, and the gap widens at 1200 frames (FVD 56 vs. 71). ConvT3 also substantially outperforms ConvLSTM (Shi et al., 2015) by 61 FVD points and 2.2 PSNR at 800 frames, and dominates Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017) and Performer (Choromanski et al., 2021) by large margins on all four metrics. On PDEBench, ConvT3 achieves the best NRMSE on both Shallow-Water (0.00033 vs. 0.00053 for AViT-Ti) and Diffusion-Reaction (0.0087 vs. 0.0090 for AViT-Ti), while using far fewer parameters than AViT-B (6M vs. 116M) and FNO-B (6M vs. 115M). Notably, ConvT3 also exhibits superior training stability compared to ConvS5, which suffers sudden loss spikes on Diffusion-Reaction, whereas ConvT3 maintains smooth convergence across multiple seeds.

## Relation to Prior Work

ConvT3 directly extends ConvS5 (Smith et al., 2023), the practical implementation of ConvSSMs that restricts state kernels to pointwise 1×1 convolutions to enable parallel scans. The key departure is structuring the state tensor as a composition of a diagonalizable S5-style matrix along the hidden dimension and a proportionality-constrained tridiagonal Toeplitz (PTT) tensor along spatial dimensions, which provably yields a 3×3 state kernel while preserving diagonalizability. The theoretical machinery draws on the classical closed-form eigendecomposition of tridiagonal Toeplitz matrices, extended here to multi-dimensional PTT tensors via commutation matrices and block eigendecomposition (Theorems 3–4). The stable parameterization reuses the Hurwitz-condition reparameterization from S5 (Gu et al., 2022) and adds a positivity constraint on the spatial eigenvalues via a softmax over four extreme points.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By lifting the state kernel from 1×1 to 3×3 without sacrificing linear-time parallel scan, ConvT3 addresses a fundamental expressivity bottleneck in ConvSSMs that had forced spatial modeling entirely onto input/output/feedthrough kernels or deeper stacking. The MiniT3 ablation (only 24 extra parameters) convincingly shows the gain is structural rather than parametric, which is a useful design principle for future SSM variants. However, the empirical gains over ConvS5 are modest in several settings (e.g., FVD on 300-frame Moving-MNIST is actually worse), the 3×3 kernel size is fixed, and the N-dimensional generalization remains purely theoretical. Open questions include whether learnable proportionality ratios, larger kernel sizes, or 3D validation would yield substantially larger improvements.
