## Problem

In offline model-based RL, multiple MDPs can behave identically on a finite dataset yet differ on out-of-sample regions, creating model uncertainty that existing ensemble-based methods handle by treating all members uniformly. This paper identifies that different ensemble members perform better in different state-action regions, necessitating adaptive belief updates, and notes that the principled BAMDP framework for this has never been extended to continuous state and action spaces with stochastic transitions.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a static offline dataset of (s, a, r, s') transitions collected by a behavior policy, from which it trains a deep ensemble of K dynamics and reward models. At deployment, only the current state is needed—the learned policy network selects actions in real time without any search. The search operates over continuous state and action spaces (e.g., 28-dim states, 14-dim actions in tokamak tasks), and the policy is distilled from non-parametric search visitation counts into a parameterized actor-critic network via SAC or supervised learning.

## Method

The core contribution is Continuous BAMCP, a planning algorithm that recasts a Pessimistic Bayes-Adaptive MDP (P-BAMDP) as a standard MDP over augmented information states (s, b) and solves it with PUCT augmented by Double Progressive Widening to handle continuous actions and stochastic transitions. At each decision node, actions are sampled from the current policy and added to the search tree only when the progressive widening threshold is met; next states are sampled from the belief-weighted mixture of ensemble members, and the belief vector is updated via Bayesian posterior after each transition. A pessimistic reward penalty (Eq. 5) based on the standard deviation of one-step Q-value targets across ensemble members discourages exploitation of model inaccuracies. The search outcomes (visit-count-based policy and value estimates) are distilled into a parameterized policy via SAC in a policy iteration loop, with a theoretical consistency guarantee (Theorem 4.1) showing the root value estimate converges to the P-BAMDP optimum at rate O(E^{-γ₀}) plus a depth-dependent approximation error term.

## Results

On the D4RL MuJoCo benchmark (12 tasks across 4 data types), BA-MCTS achieves an average normalized score of 80.25, outperforming the next-best model-based baselines MOBILE (76.50) and CBOP (73.87). Cohen's d values exceed 1.8 against all baselines with reported standard deviations, indicating very large and statistically significant improvements. On three stochastic tokamak target-tracking tasks (28-dim state, 14-dim action), BA-MCTS achieves an average return of -20.61, substantially outperforming CQL (-60.49) and Optimized (-70.98).

## Baselines & Comparisons

BA-MCTS achieves the highest average normalized score (80.25) across all 12 D4RL MuJoCo tasks, beating the closest model-based competitors MOBILE (76.50) and CBOP (73.87) by 3.75 and 6.38 points respectively. Against model-free baselines, the gap is even larger: BA-MCTS outperforms CQL (54.9), TD3+BC (54.6), IQL (77.0 on non-random only), and SAC (4.1) substantially. On individual tasks, BA-MCTS wins or ties on 10 out of 12, with the only losses being medium Walker2d (CBOP: 93.92 vs. 88.23) and med-expert Walker2d (CBOP: 117.6 vs. 116.0), both within one standard deviation. Cohen's d values range from 1.8 (vs. MOBILE) to 17.4 (vs. DT), confirming statistically significant superiority. On the three stochastic tokamak control tasks, BA-MCTS (-20.61 average) outperforms CQL (-60.49) and Optimized (-70.98) by large margins, demonstrating robustness in highly stochastic, high-dimensional continuous control. The search-based baseline Sampled EfficientZero (adapted for offline via reanalyse) performs significantly worse than all offline RL methods despite much higher computational cost, attributed to its joint model-learning and policy-training design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate research threads: (1) Bayes-adaptive MDPs (BAMDPs) and the BAMCP planner (Guez et al., 2013), which handle model uncertainty via belief updates but are restricted to discrete spaces and require ground-truth dynamics; (2) PUCT/Double Progressive Widening (Auger et al., 2013), which provides provably consistent MCTS for continuous state-action spaces; and (3) offline MBRL methods like Optimized (Lu et al., 2022), which use deep ensembles but treat all ensemble members uniformly without belief adaptation. The key conceptual departure is recasting the P-BAMDP as a standard MDP solvable by PUCT, thereby inheriting PUCT's consistency guarantees while retaining the Bayesian belief-update structure of BAMCP. The overall 'RL + Search' policy-iteration framework follows the AlphaZero paradigm, applied here for the first time to offline continuous control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to provide a principled Bayesian treatment of model uncertainty in offline MBRL with a theoretically grounded continuous-space planner, closing a gap where prior methods either ignored belief adaptation (treating ensembles uniformly) or could not scale to continuous domains. The consistency theorem (Theorem 4.1) is important because it guarantees that the search output converges to the true P-BAMDP value, providing a reliable target for the policy-iteration loop. Open questions include: whether the computational cost of MCTS at each decision node limits scalability to very high-dimensional tasks, whether the value-function approximation error (ε_V) can be tightened in practice, and whether the approach extends to partially observable settings. The tokamak control validation suggests real-world applicability in safety-critical, data-scarce domains.
