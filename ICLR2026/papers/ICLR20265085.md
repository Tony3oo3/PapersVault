## Problem

Iterative VRP solvers repeatedly re-optimize the entire solution at each search step, yet a large fraction of edges remain unchanged across iterations, causing redundant computation that scales poorly with problem size. No prior work formally exploits this stability to decompose and accelerate the search, leaving a gap between the observed redundancy and the solvers' inability to exploit it.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265085/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a VRP instance (node features including location, demand, angularity relative to depot, and internality; edge features from the current solution and k-nearest neighbors) together with the current route solution, and produces a set of unstable edges that partition routes into stable segments for hypernode aggregation. The NAR decoder emits a per-node binary probability in one forward pass, while the AR decoder emits a node sequence alternating between deletion (selecting an unstable edge from the current solution) and insertion (selecting a bridging pseudo-edge) stages, terminated by a learned end token. The synergized variant first uses NAR to flag candidate unstable regions, clusters them via K-means, then runs AR from each cluster's highest-confidence node to refine local edge predictions.

## Method

The paper formalizes First-Segment-Then-Aggregate (FSTA) decomposition with feasibility and monotonicity theorems guaranteeing that an improved solution on the reduced hypernode problem maps back to an improved solution on the original problem across CVRP, VRPTW, VRPB, and 1-VRPPD. L2Seg employs a shared encoder (route-masked Transformer layers followed by GAT layers) feeding three decoders: a non-autoregressive MLP for one-shot global unstable-node prediction, an autoregressive GRU+MHA decoder that alternates shallow deletion steps (≤2 candidates) and deeper insertion steps (O(|V|) candidates) to model local edge dependencies, and a synergized pipeline that uses NAR for global region detection, K-means clustering to select AR starting points, and AR for precise local edge identification. Training uses imitation learning with weighted cross-entropy, where labels are generated by a lookahead procedure that runs the backbone solver one step ahead and marks edges whose presence changed as unstable. The key architectural insight is that NAR captures global structure but over-classifies locally, while AR captures local dependencies but misses distant unstable regions; their combination achieves the best recall/TNR balance and yields 2×–7× speedups over backbone solvers.

## Results

On CVRP benchmarks, L2Seg-SYN-L2D achieves a -3.55% gap to HGS on CVRP5k and -2.01% on CVRP2k, surpassing the best classical heuristic (HGS) and all neural baselines. On VRPTW, L2Seg-SYN-L2D reaches -5.60% gap on VRPTW2k and -3.14% on VRPTW5k, outperforming all classical and learning-based solvers. When applied as an accelerator to backbone solvers, L2Seg-SYN delivers 2x to 7x speedups (e.g., LKH-3 + L2Seg-SYN improves objective by 2.92% on CVRP2k and 1.87% on CVRP5k over vanilla LKH-3). The synergized variant (L2Seg-SYN) consistently outperforms both L2Seg-NAR and L2Seg-AR across all backbones and problem scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2Seg-SYN (with L2D backbone) outperforms HGS on CVRP2k (-2.01% vs. 0.00%) and CVRP5k (-3.55% vs. 0.00%), and on VRPTW2k (-5.60% vs. 0.00%) and VRPTW5k (-3.14% vs. 0.00%), making it the first method to consistently beat the best classical heuristic across both problem types. Against the strongest neural baseline SIL, L2Seg-SYN-L2D improves by 1.06% on CVRP2k (56.05 vs. 57.10) and 1.22% on CVRP5k (121.87 vs. 123.25). All divide-and-conquer baselines (TAM, GLOP, UDC) are substantially outperformed, with gaps of 4.9–14.6% to HGS versus L2Seg's negative gaps. Among learning-guided methods, L2Seg-SYN-L2D beats NDS on CVRP2k (56.05 vs. 56.11) and L2D on all scales. On VRPTW, L2Seg-SYN-L2D surpasses NDS (163.74 vs. 167.48 on VRPTW2k) and L2D (163.74 vs. 164.12), with advantages growing at larger scales.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the iterative search paradigm underlying state-of-the-art VRP solvers (LKH-3, LNS, HGS) and the learning-guided decomposition line (L2D, ML-enhanced LNS), but departs from them by operating at the edge level globally rather than at the sub-route or bounded-neighborhood level. It draws on the AR/NAR duality in neural combinatorial optimization (construction solvers vs. edge heatmaps) but is the first to use their synergy for joint decision-making rather than divide-and-conquer. The FSTA decomposition itself is inspired by Morabit et al. (2024) on segment stability for dynamic re-optimization, but this paper formalizes it for iterative solver acceleration and proves feasibility/monotonicity across multiple VRP variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2x–7x speedup with solver-agnostic applicability (classic LKH-3, LNS, and learning-based L2D) makes this practically significant for large-scale logistics where long subtours dominate runtime. The theoretical guarantees (feasibility and monotonicity theorems) provide a principled foundation absent in heuristic decomposition methods. A key open question is whether the approach extends to solvers like HGS that lack warm-start interfaces, and whether the learned stability patterns transfer to VRP variants with more complex constraints (e.g., multi-depot, heterogeneous fleets). The finding that ~78% of stable edges are shared across fundamentally different solvers suggests the learned model captures problem-structure invariants, which could generalize broadly.
