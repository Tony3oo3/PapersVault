## Problem

Public robotic datasets are overwhelmingly single-arm, yet state-of-the-art bimanual VLAs like RDT-1B and π0 require thousands of hours of proprietary bimanual data and over 1,000 H100 GPU-days to train. This paper asks whether strong bimanual manipulation policies can be built by repurposing pretrained single-arm VLAs with only minimal target-domain bimanual demonstrations, eliminating the need for large-scale bimanual pretraining entirely.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262103/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a language instruction and a shared ego-centric image alongside arm-specific wrist images and proprioceptive state for each of the left and right arms. The shared inputs are processed as a single token sequence routed through a Mixture-of-Experts gate rather than duplicated, reducing VRAM by 21%. The output is a 20-step action chunk of 10-dimensional absolute end-effector poses (6D rotation + 3D translation) per arm, decoded by a shared DiT action head via conditional flow matching with 10 Euler integration steps.

## Method

TwinVLA duplicates a 0.8B SingleVLA (Eagle2-1B VLM backbone) pretrained on ~800h of OXE single-arm data into two arm-specific branches, sharing only the vision encoder and DiT action head while fully replicating the VLM. Cross-arm coordination is achieved through joint attention (adapted from Mixture-of-Transformers): Q, K, V from both backbones are concatenated for a shared self-attention layer with a custom causal mask enabling symmetric cross-arm interaction, then split back to their respective streams. A Mixture-of-Experts gate dynamically routes shared tokens (language, ego-centric image) between the two VLM FFN experts, and an attention re-weighting mechanism (scaling shared-modality attention scores by α=2) counteracts the softmax dilution caused by the doubled sequence length, reducing initial fine-tuning loss by ~40%. The full 1.3B-parameter model is fine-tuned on only 50 bimanual episodes per task, requiring just 25 H100 GPU-days versus 1,440+ for RDT-1B.

## Results

On the 50-task RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark, TwinVLA achieves 42.0% average success rate in the Easy setting (vs. 34.5% for RDT-1B and 46.4% for π0) and 8.9% in the Hard setting (vs. 13.7% for RDT-1B and 16.3% for π0). On the 5-task Tabletop-Sim benchmark, TwinVLA outperforms RDT-1B in 7 out of 8 single-task conditions and even surpasses π0 on several Easy tasks (e.g., 95.4% vs. 77.4% on Dish-drainer Easy). In real-world experiments on the Anubis robot, TwinVLA outperforms RDT-1B by 26.0% on average across five long-horizon bimanual tasks, while requiring only ~800h of single-arm pretraining data and 50 target episodes versus RDT-1B's ~2,400h mixed dataset and π0's ~10,900h proprietary data, all with just 25 H100 GPU-days of compute.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RDT-1B (1.2B params, the closest monolithic baseline), TwinVLA (1.3B params) wins decisively: +26.0% on real-world tasks, +7.5% on RoboTwin 2.0 Easy (42.0% vs. 34.5%), +21.8% on language-following, and superior performance on 7 of 8 Tabletop-Sim single-task conditions. The sole exception is RoboTwin 2.0 Hard, where RDT-1B leads by 4.8% (13.7% vs. 8.9%), likely due to its bimanual pretraining providing better robustness to texture and position variations. Against π0 (3.3B params, ~10,900h proprietary data), TwinVLA narrows the gap substantially—achieving comparable or even superior results on Tabletop-Sim Easy tasks (e.g., 95.4% vs. 77.4% on Dish-drainer) and matching π0 on real-world tasks—while using ~13× less pretraining data and ~40× less compute (25 vs. >1,000 H100 GPU-days). Against Diffusion Policy (271M, no pretraining), TwinVLA's large margins (e.g., 42.0% vs. near-zero on RoboTwin Hard) confirm that single-arm pretraining is essential for bimanual generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

TwinVLA repurposes the joint attention mechanism from Mixture-of-Transformers (Liang et al., 2024) — originally designed for cross-modality coordination (text/image/speech) — as a cross-arm coordination layer between two duplicated single-arm VLM backbones. It departs fundamentally from the dominant monolithic paradigm (RDT-1B, π0, Octo, GR00T) that trains a single model on mixed single-arm and bimanual data, instead composing two independently pretrained SingleVLA policies with lightweight MoE-based sharing of common inputs. The design also draws on task arithmetic (Tang et al., 2024) for parameter-averaging non-FFN components and standard MoE gating (Shazeer et al., 2017) for routing shared tokens, but the key conceptual contribution is the modular composition strategy itself: treating bimanual control as coordination of two arm-specific experts rather than a unified controller.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that bimanual manipulation can be achieved with ~800h of public single-arm data and 50 target episodes, versus 2,400h+ (RDT-1B) or 10,900h+ (π0) of mixed/proprietary data, and with 25 H100 GPU-days versus 1,440+. This opens a reproducible, low-cost pathway to bimanual policies that is currently blocked by data scarcity and compute barriers. Key limitations include generalization constrained by the visual distribution shift between two-arm and single-arm pretraining, and reliance on absolute EEF control that may not transfer to joint-space embodiments. The modular composition paradigm is explicitly framed as a blueprint for other data-imbalanced multi-agent or multi-modality settings (e.g., mobile manipulation), suggesting broader applicability beyond bimanual robotics.
