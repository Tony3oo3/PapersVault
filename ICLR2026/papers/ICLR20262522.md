## Problem

EEG-based brain-computer interfaces suffer from poor cross-domain generalization due to distribution shifts across subjects and sessions, and existing domain adaptation methods (particularly moment alignment) degrade under large shifts. The paper identifies that EEG encodes hierarchical cognitive processes (visual processing, emotion regulation) that are geometrically mismatched with flat Euclidean embeddings, and that no prior work has systematically demonstrated EEG hyperbolicity or built a hyperbolic architecture with principled domain adaptation for EEG.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262522/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes raw multi-channel EEG epochs (8–64 channels, 200–1000 Hz sampling, 1–6 s windows depending on dataset) and produces discrete class labels for SSVEP frequency/phase (4–40 classes), emotion categories (3–9 classes), intracranial EEG states (2 classes), or motor imagery (2–4 classes). It operates under a source-free unsupervised domain adaptation (SFUDA) protocol: the model is trained on labeled source domains, and at test time only the trained model (no source data) is available to adapt to unlabeled target domains via domain-specific moment estimation.

## Method

HEEGNet is a hybrid architecture that first extracts spectral-spatial-temporal features using three Euclidean convolutional layers from EEGNet (temporal conv, depthwise spatial conv, depthwise temporal conv), then projects them into the Lorentz hyperbolic model via a ProjX layer, and refines them with a hyperbolic pointwise convolutional layer. The key domain adaptation mechanism is DSMDBN, a two-stage coarse-to-fine strategy: stage 1 explicitly aligns domain-specific first- and second-order moments in hyperbolic space using Riemannian (Fréchet) mean and variance with momentum-based running estimates; stage 2 implicitly aligns the moment-normalized distributions to a standard hyperbolic Gaussian by minimizing the Hyperbolic Horospherical Sliced-Wasserstein (HHSW) discrepancy as an auxiliary loss. A hyperbolic multinomial logistic regression (HMLR) classifier completes the pipeline, and the entire model is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy plus HHSW loss using a Riemannian Adam optimizer.

## Results

On the grand average across all three EEG modalities (VEP, emotion, intracranial; 272 domains total), HEEGNet with DSMDBN+EA achieves 81.7±14.4% balanced accuracy, outperforming the best baseline (EEGConformer+EA at 70.9±24.1%) by over 10 percentage points. HEEGNet with DSMDBN alone reaches 78.7±18.6%, still surpassing all baselines. Per-modality, HEEGNet (DSMDBN) leads on VEP (79.6% vs. 55.8% for EEGNet+EA) and HEEGNet (DSMDBN+EA) leads on emotion (86.7% vs. 82.8% for EEGConformer+EA) and intracranial EEG (66.7% vs. 65.0% for SchirrmeisterNet+EA). On motor imagery (not reported to encode hierarchy), HEEGNet (DSMDBN+EA) achieves 54.1% on BNCI2014001 and 75.8% on BNCI2015001, competitive with the best manifold-based methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HEEGNet (DSMDBN+EA) achieves the best overall balanced accuracy of 81.7%, outperforming the strongest baseline EEGConformer+EA (70.9%) by 10.8 points and EEGNet+EA (67.4%) by 14.3 points. On VEP, HEEGNet (DSMDBN) leads with 79.6% versus 55.8% for EEGNet+EA and 46.1% for EEGConformer+EA, a margin of over 20 points. On emotion recognition, HEEGNet (DSMDBN+EA) reaches 86.7%, narrowly beating EEGConformer+EA (82.8%) and EEGConformer without alignment (83.2%). On intracranial EEG, HEEGNet (DSMDBN+EA) achieves 66.7%, edging out SchirrmeisterNet+EA (65.0%) and FBCNet+EA (61.3%). On motor imagery (BNCI2015001), HEEGNet (DSMDBN+EA) at 75.8% is competitive with GyroAtt-Gr (75.3%) and TSMNet+SPDDSBN (74.3%), while on BNCI2014001 it trails the best SPD method TSMNet+SPDDSBN (54.6%) by 0.5 points. Foundation models LaBraM and CBraMod are consistently outperformed by HEEGNet across all modalities, with HEEGNet exceeding LaBraM's overall performance by a wide margin.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EEGNet architecture (Lawhern et al., 2018) by replacing its Euclidean classification head with a hyperbolic variant and introducing a hybrid Euclidean-hyperbolic encoder, building on the hyperbolic neural network literature (Ganea et al., 2018; Bdeir et al., 2024) and the recent extension of batch normalization to Riemannian manifolds via gyrovector spaces (Chen et al., 2025a,b). Its domain adaptation strategy, DSMDBN, directly extends Kobler et al.'s (2022) SPD domain-specific momentum batch normalization by adding a second-stage distribution-level alignment using the hyperbolic horospherical sliced-Wasserstein discrepancy (Bonet et al., 2023). Unlike prior hyperbolic EEG work (Chang et al., 2025) that applied contrastive pretraining for emotion recognition, this paper is the first to combine hyperbolic geometry with source-free unsupervised domain adaptation for EEG, and it departs from the fully-hyperbolic paradigm by demonstrating that a hybrid Euclidean backbone is essential for effective feature extraction from raw EEG signals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Cross-domain generalization remains the central bottleneck for practical EEG-based BCI, and this paper provides both empirical evidence (δ-hyperbolicity measurements) and a practical architecture showing that hyperbolic geometry can capture the hierarchical structure in EEG to improve transfer. The two-stage DSMDBN strategy—moment alignment followed by distribution alignment—offers a theoretically grounded yet computationally tractable approach to multi-source multi-target source-free domain adaptation. Key limitations include the additional computational cost of hyperbolic operations (~3.5× EEGNet per epoch), the moderate δ-hyperbolicity values (0.05–0.33) that leave room for debate about whether hyperbolicity is truly the driving factor, and the absence of online/streaming extensions. Open questions include whether more expressive hyperbolic encoders can close the gap with the Euclidean backbone, and whether the hyperbolic advantage persists for EEG tasks not known to encode hierarchical structure.
