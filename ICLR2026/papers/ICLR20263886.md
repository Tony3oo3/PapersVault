## Problem

While prior work has established that FFN layers of LLMs encode task-agnostic, generalizable knowledge, no systematic method existed to explicitly extract this knowledge and transfer it to smaller models. The Learngene framework demonstrated this paradigm for vision transformers but left the LLM setting unexplored, and no evaluation protocol was established for assessing the quality of extracted generalizable knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method consumes FFN weight matrices from a source LLM (dense OLMo-7B, MoE OLMoE-7B, or shared-expert MoE DeepSeekMoE-16B) along with a multi-domain text corpus (RedPajama-V2 spanning Wikipedia, arXiv, GitHub) to train a compact set of square gene matrices totaling only 11.8M–38.6M parameters (as little as 0.17% of the source). The output is a set of fixed-size square matrices that are then adaptively reshaped via SVD-based row/column resampling to match the arbitrary FFN dimensions of target lightweight dense models (267M–877M params), directly initializing their FFN layers while all other parameters remain randomly initialized. A key design constraint is that the gene matrix M ∈ R^{r×r} must align with rectangular weight matrices W ∈ R^{d_in × d_out} of mismatched dimensions, handled through a compression-decompression pipeline that reshapes inputs into n sub-vectors of length r.

## Method

MASA trains a set of square gene matrices per FFN layer using a dual alignment loss: output alignment minimizes the L2 discrepancy between the gene matrix's response (after compression-decompression) and the source weight matrix's response, while spectral alignment operates in the log domain to match the singular value decay pattern, which theoretical analysis links to generalization via Rademacher complexity bounds and Hessian flatness. For MoE sources, gene matrices are aligned only with highly-activated or shared experts rather than all experts. The extracted gene matrices are then transferred to target models of arbitrary size through an adaptive scaling procedure: each matrix is SVD-decomposed, row and column importance scores are computed as L2 norms of the singular vectors, and the matrix is compressed (selecting top-scoring rows/columns) or expanded (duplicating top-scoring rows/columns) to match the target FFN dimensions, preserving the principal component structure. This yields a lightweight model whose FFN layers are initialized with inherited knowledge while attention and other layers are randomly initialized, requiring only 2B–10B tokens of pre-training to reach over 85% of the source LLM's downstream performance.

## Results

Across four lightweight model sizes (267M–877M), MASA consistently outperforms all baselines on both language understanding and dialogue generation tasks. On the 877M model, MASA-OLMo achieves 54.73 average on language understanding benchmarks vs. 52.83 (Scratch), 52.23 (Distillation), and 44.04 (Pruning-EEP). On dialogue generation, the 877M MASA-OLMo reaches 18.42 average Rouge-L vs. 17.53 (Scratch) and 15.71 (Pruning-EEP). After only 10B tokens of pre-training, the 877M MASA model achieves 86.6% and 87.3% of the 7B source LLM's performance on DollyEval and VicunaEval, respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASA consistently outperforms all three baselines (Scratch, Distillation, Pruning-EEP) across every model size and nearly every benchmark. Against Scratch, MASA-OLMo gains +1.90 average on the 877M language understanding set (54.73 vs. 52.83) and +0.89 on dialogue generation (18.42 vs. 17.53 Rouge-L). Against Distillation, the advantage is larger on domain-specific tasks: on CaseHold (law), MASA-OLMo scores 83.79 vs. 81.56 for Distillation at 877M, and on MedMCQA it scores 36.55 vs. 32.54. Against Pruning-EEP, the gap is most dramatic on vertical-domain benchmarks—CaseHold drops to 52.78 and MedMCQA to 27.95 under pruning at 877M, while MASA maintains 83.79 and 36.55 respectively—indicating that aggressive expert pruning destroys domain-specific knowledge that MASA's spectral alignment preserves. The paper attributes distillation's underperformance to the large capacity gap between 7B source and sub-1B target models, a known limitation of teacher-student paradigms.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Learngene framework (Wang et al., 2023a; Xia et al., 2024) from the vision domain to LLMs for the first time, adapting the concept of extracting compact 'gene' modules from large models to initialize smaller ones. It departs from standard knowledge distillation and pruning by targeting the intrinsic spectral structure of FFN weight matrices rather than output-level mimicry, drawing on spectral generalization theory (Mahoney & Martin, 2019; Yoshida & Miyato, 2017) and the finding that FFN layers encode task-agnostic knowledge (Geva et al., 2021). The dual alignment mechanism (output + spectral) and the SVD-based adaptive scaling for cross-dimension transfer are novel contributions not present in prior Learngene or distillation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, architecture-agnostic method for transferring generalizable knowledge from LLMs to lightweight models, with the practical implication of reducing pre-training data by 2–5× while maintaining competitive downstream performance. However, the absolute gains over Scratch and Distillation baselines are modest (typically 1–3 points), and the approach is limited to FFN initialization with all other parameters randomly initialized, leaving open whether attention layers or cross-layer interactions contribute additional transferable structure. Scaling to much larger source models (100B+) and non-English or multimodal settings remains unexplored, and the method's interaction with modern alignment techniques (RLHF, DPO) is unaddressed.
