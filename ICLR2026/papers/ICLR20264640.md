## Problem

Existing video polyp segmentation methods primarily enhance intra-frame spatial representations by incorporating inter-frame temporal information, but largely ignore the excavation of fine-grained discriminative features within individual frames and the explicit perception of inter-frame divergence. This leaves two critical challenges unaddressed: the high camouflage of polyps in visually similar colon tissue, and temporal inconsistencies caused by significant variations in polyp size, shape, and position across consecutive frames.

## Input / Output

The method takes a colonoscopy video clip of T=5 frames at 352×352 resolution as input, and additionally generates two auxiliary zoomed scales (0.75× and 1.25×) following the ZoomNeXt paradigm to provide complementary multi-scale perspectives. A shared PVTv2-B5 encoder (pretrained on ImageNet) processes all three scales, and the output is a per-frame binary polyp segmentation mask of shape T×1×H×W, achieving 23.04 FPS on an RTX 3090 GPU.

## Method

WavePolyp introduces two core components. The Hierarchical Wavelet-Based Feature Aggregation (HWFA) module applies discrete wavelet transform to decompose multi-level features into high-frequency (texture/edge) and low-frequency (color/illumination) components; dedicated HFC and LFC units enhance each band using distinct normalization strategies (BN vs. IN+PN/IPN) and channel-spatial attention, while an Ascending Frequency-guided Aggregation (AFA) unit hierarchically fuses enhanced features across levels via window-based linear models guided by attention weights. The Inter-Frame Divergence Perception (IDP) block computes temporal divergence as Shift(V)−V using a temporal shift operation, applies divergent attention along the time dimension to identify significant inter-frame changes, and diffuses the resulting temporal cues through T×3×3 convolutions before residual addition to the visual features. Four IDP blocks are stacked in a coarse-to-fine decoder cascade, and the model is trained end-to-end with binary cross-entropy plus an uncertainty-aware loss.

## Results

WavePolyp achieves the best performance across all four metrics (Sα, E^mn, F^w, Dice) on both SUN-SEG and CVC-612. On SUN-SEG-Hard, it reaches Dice 87.55 and Sα 90.28, outperforming the closest competitor VP-SAM (Dice 86.94, Sα 90.03) by 0.61 and 0.25 points respectively. On CVC-612, WavePolyp attains Dice 94.36 and Sα 95.24, leading all baselines. The method also achieves 23.04 FPS on an RTX 3090, offering near real-time inference with 86.63M parameters and 114.88 GFLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WavePolyp outperforms all eight baselines across every metric on all three test sets. The closest competitor is VP-SAM (ECCV 2024), which WavePolyp surpasses by 0.61 Dice on SUN-SEG-Hard and 0.91 Dice on CVC-612; however, VP-SAM requires a per-frame point prompt that is impractical in clinical settings. Among dedicated VPS methods, SALI (MICCAI 2024) trails by 2.01 Dice on SUN-SEG-Hard and 1.35 on CVC-612, while MAST (arXiv 2024) lags by 3.76 and 4.24 Dice respectively. The image-based methods (AutoSAM, WeakPolyp) show the largest gaps, with AutoSAM falling 10.30 Dice behind on SUN-SEG-Hard due to its inability to exploit temporal cues. Among video camouflaged object detection methods, SLT-Net (CVPR 2022) and ZoomNeXt (TPAMI 2024) are outperformed by 4.00 and 2.33 Dice on SUN-SEG-Hard, confirming that domain-specific polyp segmentation benefits from the proposed frequency-domain and divergence-aware design.

## Relation to Prior Work

WavePolyp builds on the multi-scale zoom strategy and feature merging network from ZoomNeXt (Pang et al., 2024) as its backbone architecture, and repurposes the temporal shift operation from TSM (Lin et al., 2019) and the window-based linear model from Swin Transformer (Liu et al., 2021) within its two novel components. It departs from existing VPS methods (PNS+, SALI, MAST, VP-SAM) that primarily enhance intra-frame representations by injecting inter-frame temporal cues, arguing instead that discriminative intra-frame features (excavated via discrete wavelet transform) and explicit inter-frame divergence perception are underexplored and complementary. The wavelet-based feature decomposition is distinct from prior wavelet applications in deblurring, super-resolution, and generative models, as it is used hierarchically across encoder levels with dedicated high/low-frequency enhancement units rather than as a single-level frequency complement.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a clinically important need—real-time polyp segmentation during colonoscopy—by achieving 23 FPS with SOTA accuracy, making it practically deployable. The parametric-free DWT-based feature aggregation offers a computationally efficient alternative to learned frequency decompositions, and the ablation showing that removing AFA drops performance below baseline (87.92 vs. 89.12) highlights a non-trivial design constraint: sharpened frequency features require adaptive spatial alignment to avoid amplifying misalignments. Limitations include evaluation only on colonoscopy videos, failure under distorted lighting/focus and overlapping intestinal walls, and reliance on a heavy PVTv2-B5 backbone (86.63M params), leaving open whether the wavelet-aggregation strategy transfers to lighter architectures or other endoscopic tasks.
