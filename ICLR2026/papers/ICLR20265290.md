## Problem

Conventional spiking neural networks rely on oversimplified LIF neurons with fixed decay rates and static conductance, omitting the dynamic conductance modulation that biological neurons use to regulate excitability. This absence of a biologically grounded gating mechanism limits SNNs' ability to adaptively filter noise and control information retention, leaving a fundamental gap between biological neural dynamics and computational spiking models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265290/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes binary or integer-valued spike trains: audio datasets (TIDIGITS, Ti46Alpha) are encoded via threshold encoding from mel-scale spectrograms into 480+20 input channels over ~500 time steps, while neuromorphic datasets (SHD, SSC) use CochleaAMS1b sensor output over 700 channels at ~250 time steps. The network produces a single class label per sequence (digit, letter, or speech command) by averaging layer outputs across all timesteps and applying cross-entropy loss, trained via BPTT with surrogate gradients for the non-differentiable spike function.

## Method

The Dynamic Gated Neuron (DGN) introduces a dual-pathway architecture where each presynaptic connection carries both a current-injection weight W_i and a conductance-modulation weight C_i, so the same exponentially decaying synaptic current D_i simultaneously drives membrane potential and adaptively scales the decay coefficient ρ^t = φ(1 − g_l·Δt − Δt·ΣC_i·D_i). This makes the neuron's memory retention input-dependent: strong presynaptic activity increases effective conductance, accelerating decay and suppressing noise, while weak activity preserves information longer. The paper proves via Itô calculus on a linearized SDE that DGN achieves lower steady-state voltage variance than LIF through two synergistic mechanisms—input-dependent leakage scaling in the denominator and a compensatory negative-feedback term in the numerator that cancels correlated synaptic noise. Structurally, the self-adapting decay coefficient is shown to be mathematically homologous to LSTM's forget gate, providing a biophysical grounding for artificial gating. A simplified variant (s-DGN) shares a single equilibrium potential across synapses to match LIF-level parameter counts while retaining the core gating benefit.

## Results

On TIDIGITS, the recurrent DGN with 100 hidden neurons achieves 99.10% top-1 accuracy, while the feedforward DGN (100 hidden) reaches 98.59%, both surpassing comparable LIF-based SNNs and matching or exceeding LSTM (97.88%). On SHD, the dual-layer recurrent DGN (128-128) attains 88.98% accuracy, outperforming ALIF (88.91%) and TC-LIF (88.91%) with fewer parameters. On SSC, the recurrent DGN (128-128) achieves 75.63%, exceeding LSTM (73.10%) and ALIF+GaussianGradient (74.20%). Under adversarial attacks (PGD, ε=0.003), the recurrent DGN on TIDIGITS retains 87.52% accuracy versus 60.66% for LSTM and 62.82% for ALIF, demonstrating substantially superior robustness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DGN consistently outperforms all LIF-based SNN baselines across all four datasets. On TIDIGITS, DGN (98.59% FF) surpasses Luo et al. (98.10%) and Lin et al. (98.10%) by ~0.5%, and the recurrent variant (99.10%) exceeds LSTM (97.88%) by 1.22%. On SHD, DGN (88.98% Rec) edges out ALIF (88.91%) and TC-LIF (88.91%) while using a single 128-neuron layer versus their 128-128 configurations, and beats HeterLIF (84.40%) by 4.58%. On SSC, recurrent DGN (75.63%) surpasses LSTM (73.10%) and ALIF+GaussianGradient (74.20%) by 2.53% and 1.43% respectively. On Ti46Alpha, DGN (96.31% Rec) is competitive with Zhang et al. 2024b (96.44%) while using only 100 hidden neurons versus 800. In robustness evaluations (Table 2), DGN dominates: under PGD on TIDIGITS, recurrent DGN retains 87.52% versus 60.66% (LSTM) and 62.82% (ALIF); under additive noise (p=0.006) on TIDIGITS, feedforward DGN maintains 95.34% versus 46.83% (LIF) and 63.29% (ALIF). The simplified s-DGN variant (shared equilibrium potential) achieves 84.30% clean accuracy on SHD with only 92.31K parameters—matching LIF's parameter count—while still outperforming LIF (77.30%) and ALIF (78.02%) by 6-7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the conductance-based neuron formalism of Gütig & Sompolinsky (2009), which established the dynamic equations for time-warp-invariant neuronal processing, and reframes the biophysical mechanism of activity-dependent membrane conductance as a computational gating operation. It departs from prior SNN neuron extensions (ALIF, GLIF, HeterLIF, FS-neuron) that introduce learnable parameters or static gates by grounding the gating in a specific neurophysiological process—dynamic conductance modulation driven by presynaptic spiking—rather than engineering it as an abstract architectural component. The paper also draws an explicit structural homology to LSTM's forget gate, positioning dynamic conductance as a biologically grounded instantiation of the same information-flow control principle that motivated artificial gating in recurrent networks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SDE-based theoretical analysis showing that dynamic conductance acts as a dual noise-suppression mechanism (adaptive leakage scaling plus synaptic noise compensation) provides a principled explanation for the large empirical robustness gaps observed, moving beyond ad-hoc robustness tricks. The s-DGN ablation confirming that LIF-level parameter counts still yield substantial gains strengthens the claim that the mechanism itself, not parameter scaling, drives the improvement. Open questions include whether the ~2× energy overhead over LIF is acceptable for neuromorphic deployment, how the mechanism interacts with deeper or attention-based spiking architectures (explicitly excluded here), and whether the conductance-based gating generalizes to non-spike-based temporal tasks. The paper's deliberate restriction to simple feedforward/recurrent architectures, while methodologically clean, leaves the interaction with modern spiking Transformer designs unexplored.
