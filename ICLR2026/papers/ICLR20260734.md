## Problem

Standard neural networks, including transformers, rely purely on statistical associations and cannot inherently respect the causal structure of a data-generating process, making them vulnerable to covariate shift and unable to support unbiased causal effect estimation. While structural inductive biases have been incorporated into some causal inference models (e.g., CEVAE, CFR), these approaches treat each variable as a single scalar value, precluding the use of high-dimensional embeddings. The paper asks whether a general transformer architecture can be modified to enforce DAG-based conditional independencies while retaining the flexibility to operate on multidimensional inputs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a batch of |Z| variable embeddings of shape (B × |Z| × C), where each variable may itself be a vector (e.g., a 240-item personality scale), along with a topologically sorted DAG adjacency matrix A of shape |Z| × |Z|. Outputs are per-variable conditional predictions (one head per node) that respect the DAG's conditional independencies, from which causal effects (ATE, CATE) are computed via recursive substitution in topological order. The architecture is agnostic to variable dimensionality: univariate tabular data and high-dimensional multimodal embeddings are handled identically, with an optional re-embedding step to unify differing input dimensionalities.

## Method

CaT replaces standard self-attention with a Causal Multi-Head Cross-Attention (CMCA) mechanism in which a learnable embedding γ serves as the query, while the embedded input X_E provides keys and values. The attention matrix is masked by the transposed, topologically sorted adjacency matrix A via a Hadamard product, so that each variable can only attend to its DAG parents. Crucially, X_E is re-injected at every block (not just the first), allowing the evolving γ to compare its current state against the observed input under DAG constraints. The CFCN baseline extends MADE-style weight masking to arbitrary DAGs, introducing an identity diagonal at layer 2 and beyond to permit signal propagation through intermediate causally valid interactions while blocking self-attention at layer 1. For intervention effects, a recursive g-formula algorithm (Alg. 1) sets intervened variables to their target values and iteratively re-evaluates descendant heads in topological order, propagating effects through the network.

## Results

On the Twins benchmark, CaT achieves eATE_ws of .0110±.0061 and eATE_os of .0133±.0078, while CFCN achieves .0098±.0048 and .0102±.0092 respectively. On the Jobs benchmark, both CaT and CFCN achieve Risk_ws of .25±.02 and eATT_ws of .016±.01, competitive with specialized causal inference methods. In the motivating simulation, CaT with the true DAG achieves eATE of 0.058±0.056 (vs. ~2.3 for all non-causal baselines), though at the cost of higher MSE (1.004 vs. ~0.56–0.62), demonstrating the accuracy-robustness tradeoff of causal constraints.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Twins dataset, CaT and CFCN outperform CFRwass on out-of-sample eATE (.0133/.0102 vs. .0284) and C Forest (.0110/.0098 vs. .0286/.0335), but trail GANITE (.0110/.0098 vs. .0058/.0089) and BART is far worse (.1206/.1265). On Jobs, CaT and CFCN achieve the best Risk_ws (.25) matching GANITE (.13 is lower but on a different scale interpretation) and outperform CFRwass (.17), C Forest (.19), CEVAE (.15), and TVAE (.16); for eATT_os, CaT/CFCN (.086/.084) are competitive with BART (.08) and C Forest (.07) but worse than CEVAE (.03) and TVAE (.01). In the motivating simulation, CaT with the true DAG achieves eATE of 0.058, roughly 40× lower than Random Forest (2.311), Transformer (2.379), or MLP (2.325), confirming that causal constraints are essential for unbiased effect estimation. On the real-world C19PRC psychology data, CaT produces effect sizes broadly consistent with Targeted Learning (e.g., Fearful: CaT .01±.02 vs. TL .05±.01) while operating on full multidimensional questionnaire items rather than aggregated scale scores, whereas the naive bivariate model overestimates effects (e.g., Fearful: .13±.02). Overall, CaT and CFCN are competitive with specialized causal inference methods despite using no task-specific inductive bias beyond the DAG mask, though they do not uniformly dominate on every metric.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the transformer architecture by replacing standard self-attention with a DAG-masked cross-attention mechanism, where the adjacency matrix of a known causal graph directly constrains which variables can attend to which. The masking strategy is conceptually descended from MADE (Germain et al., 2015), which enforced autoregressive ordering via weight masks in fully-connected networks; here the authors generalize that idea to both a transformer variant (CaT) and a fully-connected baseline (CFCN). Unlike prior causal-inference neural architectures (CEVAE, GANITE, CFR) that treat each variable as a scalar and require separate subnetworks per estimand, CaT operates on multidimensional embeddings and applies a single unified mask derived from the DAG, making it applicable to multimodal or multi-item inputs without architectural reparameterization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a clean, general-purpose inductive bias that any transformer-based pipeline can adopt when a causal DAG is available, potentially improving robustness to covariate shift and interpretability in domains like medicine, policy evaluation, and social science. Key limitations include the requirement of a known (or at least putative) DAG, compounding error in recursive inference over long mediation chains, and the absence of evaluation on large-scale or high-dimensional vision tasks. An open question is how well the approach performs when the DAG is partially misspecified in realistic settings, and whether the architectural constraints interact favorably with modern efficiency techniques (Flash Attention, linear attention) at scale.
