## Problem

Existing LLM benchmarks are approaching saturation and are increasingly vulnerable to training-data contamination, making it difficult to reliably measure model intelligence. This paper proposes open-domain forecasting of real-world future events as a forward-looking, contamination-free evaluation paradigm, and identifies that prior forecasting benchmarks lack comprehensive multi-metric evaluation, modularized pipeline design, and a natural market-consensus baseline for contextualizing model performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263639/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each model receives an identical prediction context per event: (1) news sources (titles, snippets, timestamps, URLs) retrieved by a GPT-4o web-search agent, and (2) a Kalshi market snapshot with last_price, yes_ask, and no_ask fields from which implied probabilities are derived. The model outputs a single probability in [0,1] for Yes resolution plus a free-form rationale; only the probability is scored. Predictions are elicited at multiple horizons (each subsequent forecast placed halfway between the prior forecast time and event close), enabling temporal analysis of how forecasts evolve as resolution approaches.

## Method

Prophet Arena is a live, continuously updated benchmark pipeline with three modularized stages: (1) periodic extraction of unresolved events from Kalshi filtered by popularity, diversity, and recurrence; (2) searcher-agnostic context construction combining LLM-retrieved news with real-time market snapshots; and (3) probabilistic forecasting evaluated under three complementary metrics—Brier score (absolute accuracy), expected calibration error (reliability), and average market return (relative economic advantage under a risk-neutral optimal betting strategy). A Market Baseline synthetic forecaster (using normalized contract prices as predictions) anchors all comparisons. The multi-horizon protocol, modularized pipeline, and inclusion of return metrics distinguish Prophet Arena from prior forecasting benchmarks, enabling controlled dissection of how internalized knowledge, source quality, and reasoning synthesis each shape predictive performance.

## Results

On 1,367 resolved Kalshi prediction-market events, GPT-5R achieves the best Brier score (0.184 ± 0.006) and highest average market return (0.943 ± 0.042), while Claude Sonnet 4R attains the lowest expected calibration error (ECE = 0.041). All five frontier LLMs outperform the Market Baseline (Brier 0.187, ECE 0.069, Return 0.899) on Brier score, and four of five beat it on ECE. However, no model reaches break-even profitability (all Average Returns < 1.0), and rankings shift substantially across metrics—e.g., Grok 4R is second on Brier but fourth on market return.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5R leads on both Brier score (0.184) and market return (0.943), outperforming Grok 4R (0.189 / 0.864) by 0.005 in Brier and 0.079 in return. Claude Sonnet 4R achieves the best calibration (ECE 0.041) despite ranking third on Brier, and its market return (0.909) is competitive but with the widest confidence interval (±0.101). Grok 4R is second on Brier but drops to fourth on market return, indicating its forecasts are accurate in absolute terms but less profitable relative to market prices. Gemini 2.5 FlashR is the weakest on calibration (ECE 0.067, nearly matching the Market Baseline's 0.069) and Llama 4 Scout trails on all three metrics. Critically, all five LLMs beat the Market Baseline on Brier score, yet none achieves a positive edge over the market (all returns < 1.0), meaning the aggregated human consensus in prediction markets remains a strong economic benchmark that current LLMs have not yet surpassed.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM forecasting benchmarks and prediction-market economics. Prior forecasting benchmarks (ForecastQA, ForecastBench, FutureBench, FutureX) each test a subset of capabilities—typically a single metric like Brier score or accuracy—on static or semi-dynamic event sets. Prophet Arena departs by grounding evaluation in live Kalshi prediction markets (providing an objective, contamination-free resolution oracle and a natural market-consensus baseline), implementing a multi-horizon scheduling protocol, and decomposing the forecasting pipeline into modular stages (source retrieval, context construction, probability elicitation) for controlled ablation. The mechanistic analysis of how internalized knowledge, source quality, and reasoning synthesis interact is novel relative to prior work that focuses primarily on end-to-end accuracy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark provides a forward-looking, contamination-resistant testbed that complements saturating static benchmarks, and the multi-metric framework (Brier score, ECE, market return) reveals that these capture fundamentally different aspects of forecasting quality—a point the paper formalizes with counterexamples. Key open questions include: whether the systematic conservatism of LLMs relative to markets can be corrected via prompting or training; how to separate task-level predictability from model capability in evaluation; and whether the near-resolution information-aggregation gap (where markets outpace LLMs) reflects a fundamental architectural limitation. The finding that no evaluated model achieves positive expected market return, even GPT-5, tempers enthusiasm for LLMs as trading agents while confirming their value as calibrated probabilistic reasoners.
