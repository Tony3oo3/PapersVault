## Problem

Existing diffusion-model-based blind inverse problem solvers (BlindDPS, GibbsDDRM, FastEM) are fundamentally restricted to linear, convolutional measurement operators, making them unable to handle nonlinear degradations such as rotation blur or zoom blur. No prior DM-based framework can solve blind nonlinear inverse problems without explicit knowledge or estimation of the operator parameters at inference time.

## Input / Output

The method takes a corrupted measurement y (e.g., a rotation-blurred or zoom-blurred 256×256 RGB image) whose generating operator parameters ψ are unknown, a pretrained unconditional diffusion score model, and a pretrained auxiliary encoder (ResNet-18). At inference, y is partitioned into overlapping 64×64 patches (stride 32, 50% overlap) whose features are cached once, while x_t patches are extracted at each reverse step. The output is a restored image x_0; a lightweight color-consistency head used during training is discarded at test time.

## Method

CL-DPS trains an auxiliary encoder f offline via a MoCo-style contrastive objective (InfoNCE with a 65,536-entry queue and momentum 0.996) over synthetic pairs (x_t, y_syn) generated with randomized operator parameters, so that f learns a surrogate for the intractable conditional likelihood p(y|x_t) without ever seeing the true ψ. During reverse diffusion sampling, the gradient of the contrastive score ⟨f(patches(x_t)), f(patches(y))⟩ is injected as a guidance term at each step, replacing the need to evaluate or invert the forward operator. An overlapping patch-wise inference scheme stacks per-patch encoder features, and Theorem 1 guarantees that increasing the number of overlapping patches monotonically increases the mutual information I(x; f({p_j^x})). Lemma 1 establishes that under an energy-based formulation, the gradient of the contrastive softmax surrogate converges almost surely to the true likelihood gradient as the dictionary size grows. The guidance is sampler-agnostic and pairs with both DDPM and DPM-Solver++ (2M), and a numerator-only update is shown empirically to match the full denominator-aware variant at lower cost.

## Results

On the headline nonlinear blind deblurring tasks (rotation and zoom), CL-DPS(SPE) achieves PSNR 22.74 / FID 33.66 / LPIPS 0.302 on FFHQ for rotation blur, while the best baseline (GibbsDDRM) reaches only PSNR 18.43 / FID 236.6 / LPIPS 0.565 — a gap of over 4 dB in PSNR and ~7× in FID. For zoom blur on FFHQ, CL-DPS(SPE) scores PSNR 20.68 / FID 42.61 versus GibbsDDRM's PSNR 15.45 / FID 327.4. On linear blind deblurring (Gaussian, FFHQ), CL-DPS(SPE) attains PSNR 26.42 / FID 26.65 / LPIPS 0.218, outperforming GibbsDDRM (PSNR 26.34 / FID 34.12 / LPIPS 0.426) and BlindDPS (PSNR 24.77 / FID 27.36 / LPIPS 0.233).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the nonlinear blind deblurring tasks (rotation and zoom), CL-DPS dominates every baseline by a wide margin: on rotation FFHQ it gains +4.3 dB PSNR and reduces FID from 236.6 to 33.66 over GibbsDDRM (the strongest baseline), and the gap widens further against BlindDPS (+5.9 dB) and FastEM (+6.8 dB). All DM-based baselines (BlindDPS, GibbsDDRM, FastEM) fail catastrophically on nonlinear operators because they assume a convolutional forward model, producing FID values above 200. On linear blind deblurring, CL-DPS(SPE) is competitive but not uniformly dominant: for Gaussian blur on FFHQ it edges out GibbsDDRM on PSNR (26.42 vs 26.34) and FID (26.65 vs 34.12) while substantially outperforming it on LPIPS (0.218 vs 0.426); for motion blur on FFHQ, GibbsDDRM achieves a marginally lower LPIPS (0.115 vs 0.117) but CL-DPS leads on PSNR (26.33 vs 25.80) and FID (27.44 vs 38.71). Classical non-DM baselines (Pan-ℓ₀, SelfDeblur, DeblurGANv2) are consistently the weakest, with FID values typically above 90–300 across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

CL-DPS builds directly on the diffusion posterior sampling (DPS) framework of Chung et al. (2023b), which uses a pretrained unconditional diffusion model as a prior and injects a likelihood gradient during reverse diffusion. The key departure is in how the intractable likelihood p(y|x_t) is handled: rather than requiring a known or estimated forward operator (as in BlindDPS, GibbsDDRM, and FastEM, all of which assume convolutional/linear operators), CL-DPS trains a lightweight auxiliary encoder offline via a MoCo-style contrastive objective over randomized measurement operators, producing a surrogate likelihood that is operator-agnostic at inference. The overlapping patch-wise inference and color-consistency head are additional practical components not present in prior DPS variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first diffusion-based framework to solve blind nonlinear inverse problems (rotation blur, zoom blur) without estimating operator parameters, a setting where all prior DM-based blind solvers fail catastrophically due to their structural assumption of linearity. The repurposing of contrastive learning as a likelihood surrogate is conceptually clean and the theoretical grounding (Lemma 1, Theorem 1) provides justification, though under idealized energy-model assumptions. Open questions include whether the approach extends to more complex nonlinear degradations (e.g., combined motion + defocus + sensor noise), whether the auxiliary encoder can be made more efficient than ResNet-18, and how the method behaves at higher resolutions or on non-image modalities. The lightweight offline training cost is a practical advantage, but the need to select the correct operator family at inference (Specialist vs. Universal) remains a minor deployment consideration.
