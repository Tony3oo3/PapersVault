## Problem

Fusing two object images into a single coherent hybrid object remains an underexplored problem in image-to-image generation. Existing methods exhibit two critical failures: coexistent generation, where objects are merely juxtaposed without true visual-semantic integration, and bias generation, where one object dominates the output due to semantic imbalance. Unlike multi-concept generation (which composes objects side-by-side) or text-based semantic mixing, this task requires deep structural and semantic integration of two visual inputs into one unified entity.

## Input / Output

The method takes two 512×512 RGB source images along with their category labels (e.g., 'Iron Man' and 'Duck'), which are combined into a guiding prompt of the form 'A photo of <T1> creatively fused with <T2>.' Images are encoded via Redux (FLUX.1 Krea's image encoder) and the prompt via a text encoder into a shared latent space. The output is a single 512×512 hybrid object image generated by the FLUX.1 Krea decoder, produced in a fully training-free manner using 20 denoising steps with the FlowMatchEulerDiscreteScheduler.

## Method

VMDiff comprises two components: a Hybrid Sampling Process (HSP) and an Efficient Adaptive Adjustment (EAA). HSP first refines initial Gaussian noise via a guided denoise-then-invert cycle (inspired by Rectified Flow) conditioned on a scale concatenation of the two image latents with learnable weights β₁, β₂, producing a semantically-informed noise vector; it then denoises this refined noise using spherical interpolation of the two image embeddings with a learnable mixing factor α, enabling curvature-aware blending. The key design choice is using concatenation during noise refinement (to preserve input details) but interpolation during final denoising (to achieve seamless fusion). EAA optimizes the four parameters {α, β₁, β₂, ε} via a Similarity Score that combines DINO-based visual similarity, CLIP-based semantic similarity, and explicit balance penalties, using golden section search for α, conditional one-dimensional search for the dominant β, and zero-order noise resampling (up to K=3) for ε—avoiding gradient-based latent-space backpropagation.

## Results

On the IIOF benchmark (780 concept pairs), VMDiff achieves the best scores on most metrics: VQA_T5^SA = 0.639, VQA_T5^SCE = 0.540, LC^SA = 8.372, LC^SCE = 8.392, SS = 2.068, and B_sim = 0.324 (lower is better). The closest competitor, MIP-Adapter, trails on VQA_T5^SA (0.621), SS (1.866), and B_sim (0.483), while FreeBlend is nearest on VQA_T5^SCE (0.507). In user studies, VMDiff received 67.3% preference in the multi-concept group and 87.1% in the mixing/editing group, substantially outperforming GPT-4o (12.9%) and ATIH (7.5%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VMDiff outperforms all nine baselines on the majority of metrics. Against multi-concept generation methods, it leads MIP-Adapter (the strongest baseline) by +0.018 on VQA_T5^SA, +0.028 on VQA_T5^SCE, and +0.202 on SS, while reducing B_sim from 0.483 to 0.324, indicating substantially better balance between the two fused objects. Against mixing-based methods, VMDiff surpasses FreeBlend (the closest competitor) by +0.033 on VQA_T5^SA and +0.198 on SS, and beats ATIH by +0.116 on VQA_T5^SA. Against image editing (Stable Flow), the gap is largest: +0.179 on VQA_T5^SA and +0.168 on VQA_T5^SCE. In user studies, VMDiff dominates with 67.3% preference over multi-concept methods (GPT-4o at 12.9%) and 87.1% over mixing/editing methods (ATIH at 7.5%), confirming that the quantitative gains translate to perceived quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

VMDiff sits at the intersection of multi-concept generation (FreeCustom, MIP-Adapter, OmniGen, DreamO) and semantic mixing (Conceptlab, TP2O, MagicMix, ATIH, FreeBlend), but deliberately departs from both: rather than placing multiple objects in one scene or blending text prompts with a single image, it fuses two source images into a single coherent hybrid object. Technically, it builds on FLUX.1 Krea's flow-matching framework and borrows the guided denoising/inversion paradigm from Rectified Flow and Zigzag Diffusion, while introducing a stage-dependent strategy—concatenation for noise refinement (BNoise) versus spherical interpolation for latent blending (MDeNoise)—that contrasts with prior work's uniform treatment of latent combination. The EAA module's golden-section-search-based parameter optimization draws on TP2O's balance-swap-sampling philosophy but applies it to a four-parameter space including the noise seed itself.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IIOF benchmark (780 pairs, 40 objects, 4 categories) is the first dedicated evaluation suite for image-to-image object fusion, filling a gap where prior multi-concept and mixing papers used ad-hoc or text-only evaluations. The explicit taxonomy of failure modes—coexistent generation (objects merely co-located) versus bias generation (one object dominates)—gives the community a clearer diagnostic framework for evaluating fusion quality. Key open questions remain: the method is validated only on pairwise fusion, and the sequential multi-image extension shows noticeable information loss; the EAA's fixed hyperparameters (γ_den=5, t_den=652, Th=2.4) are tuned for FLUX.1 Krea and may not transfer; and whether learned (gradient-based) parameter optimization could replace the search-based EAA at scale is unexplored.
