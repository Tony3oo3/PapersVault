## Problem

Normative neural coding theories (efficient coding, sparse coding, nonnegative matrix factorisation) are typically non-convex and analytically intractable, preventing closed-form characterisation of optimal representations. While Sengupta et al. (2018) showed that one specific similarity-matching problem is convex over representational dot-product similarity matrices, this convexification approach had not been generalised to the broader family of problems used in neuroscience. The paper asks whether a large family of neural coding objectives and constraints can be written as convex over the representational similarity matrix, and whether this convexity unlocks new identifiability and coding results.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a dataset of N datapoints with d_z-dimensional neural activity vectors (requiring d_z > N for convexity), target labels, a full-column-rank mixing matrix A, and source data S. Rather than producing a trained network, the outputs are analytical: necessary-and-sufficient identifiability conditions expressed as a 'tight scattering' geometric criterion on the source convex hull relative to an A-dependent ellipse, sufficient conditions under which optimal representational similarity implies unique single-neuron tuning curves (up to permutation), and a closed-form sparsity threshold Prob(I=0) > ⟨I⟩²/⟨I²⟩ governing the ON-OFF coding transition. The nonnegativity constraint on neural activity is what breaks rotational symmetry and makes single-neuron tuning meaningful.

## Method

The core technical contribution is showing that a menu of objectives (L2 activity, L2 weight norms via pseudoinverse, linear and nonlinear similarity matching) and constraints (nonnegativity as completely positive matrices, affine decodability via Schur complement invertibility, ReLU, bounded firing rates) are each convex over the representational dot-product similarity matrix Q = Z^T Z, so any composition yields a convex problem. Weight norms become convex in Q through the matrix convexity of the projection pseudoinverse (V^T Q^† V), while affine decodability is shown convex by proving the generalised Schur complement is matrix-concave. This convexity is then exploited in three ways: (1) deriving the first necessary-and-sufficient identifiability condition for semi-nonnegative matrix factorisation by showing the modular solution is a local (hence global) minimum iff a tight scattering condition holds; (2) proving that nonnegativity plus tight scattering of neural responses forces all optimal representations to be related only by permutation, justifying single-neuron tuning analysis; and (3) applying KKT conditions to the convex nonlinear problem to analytically derive the sparsity threshold at which ON-OFF channel splitting becomes suboptimal.

## Results

The paper derives three main analytical results. First, Theorem 1 provides the first necessary and sufficient identifiability condition for semi-nonnegative matrix factorization via nonnegative affine autoencoders, expressed as a 'tight scattering' condition involving an A-dependent ellipse (Definition 1). Second, Theorem 2 gives sufficient conditions under which all globally optimal representations share the same single-neuron tuning curves (up to permutation), justifying single-neuron analysis. Third, for ON-OFF coding, the paper analytically derives a sparsity threshold: channel splitting is optimal if and only if Prob(I=0) < ⟨I⟩²/⟨I²⟩ (Eq. 11), confirmed by numerical simulations in Fig. 3. All results are guaranteed globally optimal by the underlying convexity of the reformulated problems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without standard benchmark comparisons. The key 'comparison' is against prior identifiability results: previous work (Donoho & Stodden 2003; Hu & Huang 2023; Huang et al. 2013; Tatli & Erdogan 2021) provides only sufficient scattering conditions for matrix factorization identifiability, while Theorem 1 here is both necessary and sufficient. Compared to the closest prior work on nonnegative affine autoencoders (Whittington et al. 2023; Dorrell et al. 2025), which only handles the orthogonal mixing matrix A, this paper generalizes to arbitrary full-rank A, showing that source alignment can flip identifiability (Fig. 1C,D). For ON-OFF coding, prior theories (Gjorgjieva et al. 2014; Ocko et al. 2018; Jun et al. 2022) are either intractable neural networks or rely on model enumeration; this paper's convex formulation yields a closed-form sparsity threshold matching the conjecture of Sterling & Laughlin (2015). The convexity framework itself extends Sengupta et al. (2018), which only handled a single similarity-matching objective, to a composable family of convex objectives and constraints (Appendix A).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper broadens the convex reformulation over representational dot-product similarity matrices introduced by Sengupta et al. (2018) for similarity matching into a general family of convex objectives and constraints, encompassing linear/affine networks, ReLU networks, and nonlinear similarity matching. It directly extends the nonnegative affine autoencoding identifiability results of Whittington et al. (2023) and Dorrell et al. (2025), which were restricted to orthogonal mixing matrices, to arbitrary full-rank mixing matrices. Technically, it differs from the concurrent convex neural network literature (Pilanci & Ergen, 2020; Ergen & Pilanci, 2021) by optimising over similarity matrices rather than weight parameters, trading some machine-learning relevance for greater flexibility in modelling biological constraints like nonnegativity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first necessary-and-sufficient identifiability criterion for semi-nonnegative matrix factorisation, a gap that has persisted since Donoho & Stodden (2003) and subsequent sufficient-only results. The result that nonnegativity alone breaks rotational symmetry and uniquely pins down single-neuron tuning curves provides a formal defence of classical single-neuron analysis in neuroscience, a point of active debate (Barack & Krakauer, 2021). However, practical impact is limited by the NP-hardness of membership testing in the completely positive matrix cone and the requirement that the number of neurons exceeds the number of datapoints, leaving open whether useful convex relaxations (e.g., nuclear-norm constraints) can close this gap.
