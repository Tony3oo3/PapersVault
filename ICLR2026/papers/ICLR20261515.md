## Problem

Despite the rapid proliferation of LLM routers in both academia and industry, no open platform exists that systematically compares them under a unified protocol. Existing benchmarks suffer from narrow domain coverage, lack of difficulty differentiation, limited evaluation metrics, no commercial router support, and no live leaderboard, making it impossible to fairly assess routing progress or identify the best router for a given deployment scenario.

## Input / Output

The platform consumes 8,400 text queries spanning 9 DDC domains and 44 categories (with three empirically calibrated difficulty bands) along with a router's API endpoint. It produces a multi-dimensional scorecard per router: query-answer accuracy, actual per-token inference cost (using official API pricing), three routing-optimality sub-metrics (optimal selection ratio, optimal accuracy ratio, optimal cost ratio), robustness (consistency under paraphrase/grammar/synonym/typo perturbations), and end-to-end routing latency. These are aggregated into a composite Arena Score via a weighted harmonic mean with log-scaled cost normalization, yielding a six-metric leaderboard ranking.

## Method

ROUTERARENA constructs its evaluation dataset by sampling from 23 source datasets, organizing queries via the Dewey Decimal Classification system for domain coverage and Bloom's taxonomy (annotated by DeepSeek-V3.1 as LLM-as-Judge) for cognitive-skill coverage, then empirically labeling difficulty by measuring the fraction of 42 diverse models that answer each query correctly (hard ≤4/42, medium 5–19/42, easy ≥20/42). A recursive deficit redistribution algorithm balances queries across categories and cognitive levels, and cosine-similarity deduplication (Sentence-Transformers/ALL-MiniLM-L6-v2) removes redundancy. The automated evaluation framework sends queries to a router's API, records model selections and latency, runs inference with prefix caching to reuse overlapping model-pool results, and computes all five metric dimensions. The Arena Score combines accuracy and log-normalized cost via a weighted harmonic mean (default β=0.1 favoring accuracy), and the leaderboard is updated automatically as new routers are submitted.

## Results

On the ROUTERARENA benchmark (8,400 queries across 9 domains and 44 categories), Azure-Router achieves the best overall leaderboard rank (average score 4.40), followed by MIRT-BERT (4.50) and RouteLLM (5.0). GPT-5 attains the highest query-answer accuracy (74.0%) but at a cost of $14.02 per 1,000 queries—roughly 26× more expensive than Azure-Router ($0.54) and 94× more than MIRT-BERT ($0.15)—placing it only at rank #4 overall. No single router dominates all six metrics; for example, RouteLLM achieves the best Optimal-Selection and Optimal-Cost ratios (rank 1) but the worst Optimal-Accuracy ratio (rank 10), while MIRT-BERT leads in Arena Score and Optimal-Accuracy but lags in Optimal-Selection (rank 9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among commercial routers, GPT-5 achieves the highest accuracy (74.0%) but ranks only #4 overall because its restricted OpenAI model pool drives cost to $14.02/1k queries; Azure-Router matches 93% of GPT-5's accuracy (68.1%) at 26× lower cost and ranks #1, while NotDiamond (68.0% accuracy) ranks last (#12) due to consistently selecting expensive models ($9.34/1k). Among open-source routers, MIRT-BERT (66.9%, $0.15/1k) and CARROT (67.2%, $2.06/1k) offer the best accuracy-cost balance, with CARROT achieving ~35% lower cost than its best model with <2% accuracy loss on the normalized deferral curve. RouteLLM excels in routing optimality (rank 1 in both Optimal-Selection and Optimal-Cost ratios) but sacrifices overall accuracy (47.0%) because its binary design forces most queries to the stronger model. RouterDC achieves the lowest cost ($0.07/1k) and strong optimality scores (rank 2 in both Optimal-Selection and Optimal-Cost) but has the worst accuracy (32.0%), making it unsuitable for accuracy-sensitive deployments. vLLM-SR and GraphRouter show competitive accuracy (67.3% and 57.0%) at moderate cost, while KNN and MLP baselines (trained on RouterBench) underperform, confirming that routing strategies trained on older, narrower benchmarks do not transfer well to the broader ROUTERARENA domain coverage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of router evaluation benchmarks (RouterBench, RouterEval, FusionBench, EmbedLLM) by addressing their shared limitations: narrow category coverage, absence of difficulty stratification, single-metric evaluation, and exclusion of commercial routers. Conceptually, it adapts the LMArena/Chatbot Arena paradigm (live, community-driven leaderboard) from model comparison to router comparison, while drawing on library science (Dewey Decimal Classification) and educational psychology (Bloom's taxonomy) for principled dataset construction. Unlike prior offline benchmarks that release frozen corpora of model outputs, ROUTERARENA is designed as a live, continuously updated leaderboard that supports both open-source and commercial routing services under a unified protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LLM routing becomes a core system primitive (exemplified by GPT-5's built-in router), the field urgently needs a standardized evaluation venue analogous to LMArena for models; ROUTERARENA fills this gap with the first comprehensive, multi-metric, live leaderboard. Key open questions highlighted include: closing the oracle gap (all routers are inefficient at recognizing when cheaper models suffice), improving robustness (uniformly low across all methods, especially BERT-based routers), and developing difficulty-aware routing strategies that allocate budget adaptively. The finding that no single router dominates across all metrics suggests the field is far from convergence, and the platform's automated framework lowers the barrier for future router designers to benchmark their contributions.
