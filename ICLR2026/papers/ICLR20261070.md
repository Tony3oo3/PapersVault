## Problem

Existing multimodal time series forecasting methods either rely on endogenous text that largely overlaps with information already in the numerical series, or perform only shallow representation-level fusion of exogenous text without extracting deep semantic reasoning. This leaves two critical gaps: insufficient utilization of rich exogenous text (news, policy documents) that captures event-driven dynamics, and an inability to effectively align the complementary strengths of text (sudden shifts, external events) and numerical modeling (subtle fluctuations, periodic patterns) across the significant modality gap.

## Input / Output

The method takes a lookback window of multivariate time series X ∈ R^{L×N} (L varies by dataset: 8, 36, or 96 steps), exogenous text T^ex from external sources (news, policy announcements, social media), and endogenous text T^en generated by GPT-2 from statistical descriptors (mean, frequency) of the time series. It produces future H-step predictions Ŷ ∈ R^{H×N} (H ∈ {6,8,10,12}, {12,24,36,48}, or {48,96,192,336} depending on dataset frequency). The exogenous text is consumed through a three-step LLM generative pipeline (Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct for template/summary, DeepSeek for reasoning), while the endogenous text is encoded and aligned with the PatchTST numerical backbone.

## Method

VoT employs a dual-branch architecture. The event-driven branch processes exogenous text through a three-step generative pipeline (dataset-specific template generation → event summarization → LLM reasoning to numerical predictions), enhanced by Historical In-Context Learning (HIC) that builds a knowledge base of error-corrected reasoning samples during training and retrieves the most similar corrected example via embedding similarity at inference to provide error-informed guidance. The numerical branch uses a PatchTST backbone with Endogenous Text Alignment (ETA), which extracts trend and seasonal components from text via dual-query attention, aligns them with temporal representations through cross-attention, and enforces deep semantic alignment via decomposed contrastive learning on trend/seasonal pairs. At the prediction level, Adaptive Frequency Fusion (AFF) applies FFT to both branch outputs, partitions the spectrum into low/mid/high bands, and fuses band-specific components using only 6 learnable weights before inverse FFT, enabling the model to dynamically weight text-driven vs. numerical predictions per frequency band. Training follows a three-stage protocol (backbone pre-training → contrastive alignment → joint optimization) with a composite loss combining temporal MSE, contrastive alignment, and final fused prediction MSE.

## Results

VoT achieves state-of-the-art performance across 10 real-world multimodal time series datasets, ranking first in all 20 MSE/MAE metrics against time series-only and text-enhanced baselines (Table 2), and in 19 out of 20 metrics against five multimodal forecasting methods (Table 3). Representative MSE values include 0.209 (Agriculture), 1.078 (Climate), 0.201 (Economy), 0.222 (Energy), and 70.117 (Security). The sole loss is on Agriculture MAE (0.302 vs. GPT4TS's 0.294). VoT shows particularly large gains on event-influenced domains such as Energy (MSE 0.222 vs. 0.250 for PatchTST) and Social Good (MSE 0.804 vs. 0.944 for PatchTST).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three time series-only baselines (PatchTST, iTransformer, RAFT) and their text-enhanced variants (PatchTST*, iTransformer*, RAFT*), VoT achieves the best MSE and MAE on all 10 datasets, with the largest relative gains on event-sensitive domains (e.g., Energy MSE 0.222 vs. 0.250 for PatchTST; Social Good MSE 0.804 vs. 0.944 for PatchTST). Among the five multimodal baselines, VoT outperforms GPT4MTS, TaTS, Time-VLM, and CALF on all 20 metrics. The only exception is GPT4TS, which edges out VoT on Agriculture MAE (0.294 vs. 0.302) while VoT still leads on Agriculture MSE (0.209 vs. 0.220). Overall, VoT secures 1st place in 19/20 metrics against multimodal methods and 20/20 against time series-only and text-enhanced methods, confirming that the combination of event-driven LLM reasoning and multi-level alignment yields consistent improvements across diverse domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of LLM-based reasoning and multimodal time series forecasting. Prior multimodal methods (GPT4TS, TimeLLM, CALF, TaTS) use LLMs primarily for feature extraction or representation alignment, while exogenous-text methods (CMIN, DualTime, GPT4MTS) focus on representation-level fusion without deep semantic reasoning. VoT departs by (1) employing LLMs for structured event-driven reasoning rather than mere embedding, (2) jointly handling both exogenous and endogenous text in a dual-branch architecture, and (3) introducing Historical In-Context Learning as a retrieval-augmented mechanism that provides error-informed guidance without fine-tuning. The numerical branch builds on PatchTST with a novel decomposed contrastive alignment (ETA), and the prediction-level fusion via Adaptive Frequency Fusion repurposes Fourier decomposition with learnable band-specific weights rather than fixed combination ratios.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that textual information provides irreplaceable event-driven guidance that numerical time series alone cannot capture, validated across 10 diverse domains. The finding that unguided LLM reasoning can be *worse* than no reasoning at all (w/o HIC underperforms w/o Event) highlights a practical pitfall for anyone integrating LLMs into forecasting pipelines. Limitations include the 5.9-second inference latency from the LLM branch (mitigated by the option to use only the numerical branch), dependence on the quality and availability of exogenous text, and evaluation limited to 10 datasets at relatively small scale. Open questions include whether the HIC mechanism generalizes to streaming/online settings, whether smaller or open-weight LLMs can replace the DeepSeek API dependency, and whether the frequency-band partition strategy transfers to non-stationary or irregular series.
