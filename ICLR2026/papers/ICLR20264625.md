## Problem

Existing visual contrastive decoding methods for LVLMs apply query-agnostic visual augmentations (e.g., fixed noise or random crops) that ignore the specific semantic intent of the text query, limiting their ability to elicit informative expert-amateur logit discrepancies. Additionally, the Adaptive Plausibility Constraint (APC) used for vocabulary truncation relies solely on the maximum logit value, making it a confidence-agnostic filter that becomes unreliable in low-confidence states where the correct token risks being discarded.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard RGB image and a free-form text query as input, producing a generated text response in the same autoregressive format as the base LVLM. The augmentation selection step (SAS) is a text-only forward pass that consumes only the query and a structured prompt, bypassing visual token processing entirely, which keeps the overhead to a single lightweight generation call rather than the multiple full-image forward passes required by brute-force alternatives like VACoDe.

## Method

Self-Aug introduces two complementary components. First, Self-Augmentation Selection (SAS) is a prompting strategy that leverages the LVLM's own parametric knowledge to choose the most semantically disruptive augmentation from a predefined set (random crop, random mask, noise, color inversion, horizontal/vertical flip) by conditioning on the query's intent; the SAS Prompt includes operational definitions of each augmentation, a reason-before-choose structure to reduce post-hoc rationalization, and in-context learning examples. Second, Sparsity Adaptive Truncation (SAT) replaces the fixed-ratio APC threshold with a dynamic threshold computed as H_decay(p) = σ(−γ Σ p_i log₂ p_i) over the expert logit's softmax distribution, implementing an inverse-entropy principle where high-entropy (uncertain) states yield a more lenient threshold to preserve candidate tokens, while low-entropy (confident) states yield a stricter threshold to sharpen the candidate set. The contrasted logit is then computed as (1+α)·l − α·l′, with tokens outside the SAT candidate set assigned −∞ before sampling.

## Results

Self-Aug achieves up to 18.78% average performance gain over multinomial sampling across five LVLMs on discriminative benchmarks (POPE, MME-P, MMVP), with the largest gains on InstructBLIP-7B (+18.78%) and LLaVA-1.5-7B (+14.32%). On generative benchmarks (MMHal-Bench, MM-Vet, LLaVA-Bench), Self-Aug similarly leads with up to +17.09% average gain (Qwen-VL) and reduces hallucination rate on MMHal-Bench from 0.65 to 0.59 for LLaVA-1.5-7B. The method consistently outperforms both VCD and VACoDecoding across nearly all model-benchmark combinations, with the most pronounced advantage on smaller models (7B) where the query-aware augmentation selection provides the greatest marginal benefit.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Self-Aug outperforms VCD (Leng et al., 2024) on all five evaluated models, with average gains ranging from +2.25% (Qwen3-VL-8B) to +18.78% (InstructBLIP-7B) over multinomial sampling, compared to VCD's +1.00% to +12.33%. Against VACoDe (Kim et al., 2024), Self-Aug wins on four of five models: LLaVA-1.5-7B (+14.32% vs +9.52%), LLaVA-1.5-13B (+11.59% vs +8.03%), InstructBLIP-7B (+18.78% vs +10.98%), and Qwen3-VL-8B (+2.25% vs +2.07%), while VACoDe narrowly edges out on Qwen-VL-7B (+7.49% vs +6.69% avg Δ). The advantage is most pronounced on MME-P, where Self-Aug's query-aware augmentation selection (e.g., choosing random mask for POPE's object-presence queries) yields substantially higher scores than VACoDe's brute-force first-token divergence. On generative benchmarks, Self-Aug also leads: LLaVA-1.5-7B achieves 31.14 on MM-Vet and 69.22 on LLaVA-Bench (vs. VACoDe's 29.88 and 69.12), while InstructBLIP-7B reaches 31.14 on MM-Vet (vs. VACoDe's 27.40). The consistent pattern suggests that leveraging the model's own parametric knowledge for augmentation selection is more effective than empirical logit-divergence heuristics, particularly for open-ended and multi-step reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Visual Contrastive Decoding (VCD) framework by addressing two specific limitations: query-agnostic visual augmentation and confidence-agnostic plausibility thresholding. Where VCD applies a fixed random-noise degradation and VACoDe selects augmentations via brute-force first-token logit divergence, Self-Aug uses the LVLM's own parametric knowledge through a structured prompt to choose a semantically aligned augmentation per query. The Sparsity Adaptive Truncation (SAT) component generalizes the Adaptive Plausibility Constraint from Contrastive Decoding (Li et al., 2023) by replacing the single max-logit threshold with a sigmoid-decayed Shannon entropy of the full expert logit distribution, making the candidate-set filter confidence-aware.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As a training-free, plug-and-play decoding strategy, Self-Aug is immediately deployable on any LVLM without architectural changes, which is practically important for the open-source community. The consistent gains across five model families (7B–32B) and seven benchmarks validate the two design principles—query-augmentation semantic alignment and entropy-aware thresholding—as broadly applicable. Key open questions remain: the method's dependence on the base model's reasoning ability limits its utility for weaker models, the predefined augmentation set may be insufficient for specialized visual reasoning, and the fundamental CD failure mode (when expert and amateur logits are nearly identical) is acknowledged but unresolved. Extending the framework to video with temporal-aware contrastive pairs is identified as a natural next step.
