## Problem

Vision-language models like CLIP are highly vulnerable to imperceptible adversarial perturbations, and while test-time transformation-based defenses have shown promise, the underlying mechanism of how and why diverse image transformations mitigate adversarial effects in the latent feature space remains unexplored. This paper uncovers that adversarial images exhibit a consistent directional shift under transformations (unlike the dispersed pattern of clean images), and leverages this directional prior to achieve defense that even surpasses clean accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260331/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and a set of class-name text prompts as input, applies 31 diverse transformations (spatial, pixel, and frequency domain) to produce 32 augmented images, and extracts features via a frozen CLIP vision encoder. After entropy-based filtering retains the k=16 lowest-entropy transformed features, the method outputs a reconstructed image feature vector that is classified via cosine similarity against text features, yielding a class label. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates at test time with a per-image inference cost of ~0.025s on an RTX 3090.

## Method

DBD exploits the observation that adversarial images, under diverse input transformations, consistently shift their CLIP features along a dominant direction (the Defense Direction) that opposes the adversarial perturbation, while clean images show dispersed feature patterns. The method applies 31 transformations across spatial (crop-resize-flip), pixel (bit-depth quantization, JPEG, Gaussian noise), and frequency (Gaussian/mean/median filtering) domains, then uses entropy-based filtering to select the k=16 most confident transformed features. From these, it computes the Defense Direction as the normalized mean displacement vector and a DB-score as the mean cosine similarity between individual displacements and the Defense Direction, which exhibits a clear bimodal distribution separating adversarial from clean inputs. A two-stream reconstruction strategy then applies different treatments: for high DB-score (likely adversarial) examples, the original feature is shifted along the Defense Direction by λ·‖d̄‖₂ (λ=2.5) to amplify the corrective displacement; for low DB-score (likely clean) examples, the average transformed feature is used as test-time augmentation to stabilize the representation without introducing erroneous shifts.

## Results

Under the strongest attack setting (PGD-100, ε=4/255 on CLIP-ViT-B/16), DBD achieves 93.8% average robust accuracy across 10 fine-grained datasets while maintaining 67.8% clean accuracy (vs. 67.9% for vanilla CLIP). On the ImageNet-OOD benchmarks under the same setting, DBD reaches 97.7% average robust accuracy with 63.7% clean accuracy. A striking finding is that robust accuracy consistently exceeds clean accuracy (e.g., 99.4% robust vs. 94.8% clean on Caltech101), indicating that adversarial perturbations implicitly encode directional priors about the true decision boundary.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DBD outperforms all five baselines by a wide margin on robust accuracy across every attack setting and model backbone. Against the strongest prior test-time defense R-TPT (Sheng et al., 2025), DBD improves average robust accuracy from 43.2% to 93.8% under PGD-100 on CLIP-ViT-B/16 (+50.6 points), while also matching or slightly exceeding clean accuracy. The training-based TeCoA (Mao et al., 2022) achieves only 11.4% robust accuracy on ViT-B/32 and degrades clean accuracy from 63.7% to 32.8%, whereas DBD achieves 91.3% robust with 64.1% clean in a fully training-free setting. Adversarial prompt tuning APT (Li et al., 2024) reaches 22.0% robust but requires 16-shot annotated data per dataset. The transformation-based TTC (Xing et al., 2025) is architecture-sensitive, dropping from 27.2% on ViT-B/32 to 12.5% on ViT-B/16, while DBD maintains 91.3% and 93.8% respectively. Inference efficiency also favors DBD: at 0.025 s/image it is 7× faster than R-TPT (0.181 s/image) while delivering roughly 2× the robust accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the well-established idea that input transformations (cropping, JPEG compression, filtering) can mitigate adversarial perturbations, but introduces a new geometric observation: adversarial images exhibit a consistent directional bias in CLIP's feature space under such transformations, while clean images do not. Rather than simply averaging transformed features (as in TTC or AOM) or adapting prompts (as in R-TPT), DBD exploits this directional bias to define a 'Defense Direction' and performs a two-stream linear feature shift guided by a proposed DB-score. The method thus carves new ground by reframing input transformations from a heuristic noise-removal tool into a mechanism for estimating a geometrically meaningful direction that points back toward the correct class centroid.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that adversarial perturbations implicitly encode directional priors about the true decision boundary — and that exploiting them can yield robust accuracy exceeding clean accuracy — offers a new conceptual lens on why adversarial examples are structured rather than random noise. Practically, DBD is training-free, architecture-agnostic, and fast (0.025 s/image vs. 0.181 s for R-TPT), making it attractive for deployment. However, the defense is vulnerable to adaptive attacks (BPDA+EOT drops robust accuracy to 1.29% under PGD-100), and the 'exceeds clean accuracy' result is specific to ground-truth-guided attacks; under pseudo-labels it merely matches clean performance. Open questions include whether the directional bias generalizes to other VLMs (e.g., BLIP-2, LLaVA), to non-L∞ threat models, and whether the DB-score can be hardened against defense-aware adversaries.
