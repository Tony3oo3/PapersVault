## Problem

Publicly available pre-trained SSL encoders are vulnerable to downstream-agnostic adversarial examples (DAEs) that mislead any downstream model without knowledge of the specific task. Existing defenses rely on task-specific adversarial fine-tuning, which degrades benign accuracy (catastrophic forgetting) and must be repeated for every new downstream task. The paper asks whether a single tuning can simultaneously preserve (or improve) benign performance and provide robustness across all downstream tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/STL-10/SVHN, 64×64 for GTSRB/ANIMALS-10, 224×224 for ImageNet-20) and produces a class label via a confidence-weighted fusion of three branch predictions. As a byproduct, the per-branch confidence scores enable adversarial example detection: a sample is flagged as adversarial when the MPAE-Branch confidence exceeds the BMP-Branch confidence by a threshold of 0.1, requiring no dedicated detection training. The perturbation budget for evaluation is ε=10/255 under the l∞ norm.

## Method

ZePAD employs a dual-branch encoder: an MPAE-Branch consisting of two publicly available pre-trained encoders (from different SSL methods, e.g., BYOL + NNCLR) adversarially fine-tuned with a hybrid loss combining cross-entropy on adversarial examples and a cosine-distance-based feature preservation loss that removes nearest neighbors to capture global structure; and a BMP-Branch with one locally trained encoder (SimCLR) exposed only to benign data. The key insight is that neural networks inherently assign higher confidence to inputs matching their training distribution, so the BMP-Branch dominates on clean samples while the MPAE-Branch dominates on adversarial ones. A Robust Federal Decision Mechanism (RFDM) fuses the three branch predictions using non-linear confidence weights c_i = m_i · exp(3(m_i − 0.5)), where m_i is the max softmax probability. This design achieves zero-sacrifice (benign accuracy is maintained or improved) and persistent robustness (a single adversarial tuning generalizes across downstream tasks) because the diversity of SSL methods in the MPAE-Branch makes it difficult for DAEs to exploit shared vulnerabilities.

## Results

On STL-10 (ImageNet pre-training), ZePAD achieves 82.07% benign accuracy (BA) with SimCLR and 81.60% with MoCo v2+, outperforming the strongest baseline Gen-AF by +13.38% and +12.51% respectively. Robust accuracy (RA) against AdvEncoder reaches 73.26% (SimCLR) and 74.30% (MoCo v2+), exceeding Gen-AF by +10.98% and +8.30%. Across 11 SSL methods and 6 datasets, ZePAD delivers up to 29.20% BA improvement and 73.86% RA gain over the untrained baseline, while also enabling zero-shot DAE detection with 82.52% average accuracy via confidence scoring alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ZePAD dominates all four baselines on every metric and both backbones. Against the strongest prior defense Gen-AF (Zhou et al., S&P 2024), ZePAD improves BA by +13.38% (SimCLR) and +12.51% (MoCo v2+) while also raising RA by +6.62% to +13.13% across the five attacks, confirming the zero-sacrifice claim. TRADES (Zhang et al., ICML 2019) and MART (Wang et al., ICLR 2019) achieve roughly 50% and 42% BA/RA respectively, trailing ZePAD by 30+ points on both metrics. PGD-AT (Madry et al., ICLR 2018) is the weakest, with BA below 23% and RA near random, indicating that standard adversarial training is nearly ineffective against downstream-agnostic attacks on pre-trained encoders. Notably, Gen-AF requires task-specific re-tuning per downstream task, whereas ZePAD achieves its gains with a single adversarial fine-tuning that transfers across all six evaluated datasets (persistent robustness).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard adversarial fine-tuning paradigm (PGD-AT, TRADES, MART, Gen-AF) that inherently trades benign accuracy for robustness, by instead exploiting the natural confidence bias of neural networks on in-distribution vs. out-of-distribution inputs. Conceptually, it repurposes the multi-encoder diversity already present in the SSL ecosystem (BYOL, SimCLR, MoCo, DINO, etc.) as a defense mechanism: two adversarially fine-tuned encoders from different SSL methods form the MPAE-Branch, while a locally trained benign encoder forms the BMP-Branch, with a confidence-weighted fusion (RFDM) replacing the typical single-model decision. The theoretical grounding in Appendix C connects this to the posterior-approximation property of cross-entropy-trained classifiers, giving a principled justification for why confidence separation between branches emerges without explicit detection training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'tune once, defend all' persistent-robustness framing addresses a real deployment bottleneck: current defenses require task-specific retraining, which is impractical when pre-trained encoders serve many downstream tasks. The zero-sacrifice claim (simultaneously improving BA and RA) challenges the long-held assumption of an irreducible robustness-accuracy trade-off, though the BA gains partly reflect the addition of a well-trained benign branch rather than a fundamental resolution of the trade-off. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger backbones (ViT, CLIP-scale models), whether the confidence-based DAE detection generalizes to adaptive or multi-step attacks, and how the method performs when the attacker knows both encoders in the MPAE-Branch (the white-box results show some BA degradation). The paper also notes the need for improved interpretability of both the defense mechanism and the DAEs themselves.
