## Problem

Current LLM alignment is fundamentally shallow: models refuse harmful queries at the start of a response but collapse once a harmful continuation is already underway, whether via adversarial prompts or assistant-prefill attacks. Deep alignment (training mid-stream refusals) merely pushes the failure point deeper, creating an arms race between attack depth and alignment depth, and is rapidly undone by even brief instruction tuning. The paper asks whether the model's innate safety understanding, already encoded in its hidden states, can be unlocked to enforce refusals at arbitrary generation depths without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method operates on an ongoing LLM generation stream by forking the KV cache at periodic checkpoints (e.g., every 25–100 tokens) and injecting the model's own assistant-header tokens (Safety Tokens) as a short prefix. ADA(LP) reads the hidden state at a single probe token within the injected header at a chosen mid-layer and passes it through a pre-trained logistic-regression classifier to produce a binary halt/continue decision; ADA(RK) instead generates up to 20 lookahead tokens and checks for a refusal string. The output is either an immediate stream halt (with the refusal returned to the user) or a signal to continue generation, with the entire check costing roughly one extra token of compute (~25 ms, 2–3 MB).

## Method

The central empirical finding is that assistant-header tokens (e.g., the 'assistant' role token in Llama's chat template) act as safety aggregators: their hidden states at mid-to-deep layers are highly linearly separable for harmful vs. benign continuations (>99.5% probe accuracy), and this separability strengthens with generation depth while the last-generated token's state remains entangled. ADA(LP) exploits this by performing a single forward pass over the injected Safety Tokens, extracting the probe-token hidden state after the block's input LayerNorm, and applying a lightweight logistic-regression classifier trained once on ~600k depth-indexed examples from WildChat and WildJailbreak. ADA(RK) is a training-free generative variant that re-injects the header mid-stream and checks whether the model's short lookahead contains a refusal. Both variants require no changes to base-model weights, reuse the existing KV cache for constant-time overhead, and the safety signal persists even after the base model undergoes benign or adversarial SFT, because the latent representation is not erased by surface-level fine-tuning.

## Results

On Gemma-2-9B-IT under a 500-token harmful assistant prefill, ADA(LP) achieves 100% refusal on AdvBench, JailbreakBench, and StrongREJECT, and 99.7% on HEx-PHI, while maintaining near-zero over-refusal (0.0–1.8%) across seven benign benchmarks and only 0.4% on XSTest. Against adversarial prompt attacks (GCG, AutoDAN, PAIR, TAP) on AdvBench, ADA(LP) reduces average attack success rate to 2.0% on Gemma-2-9B-IT and 1.5% on Llama-2-7b-chat-hf, compared to 76.5% and 47.0% for the respective base models. Inference overhead is constant at ~25 ms latency and 2–3 MB extra memory per checkpoint, versus ~500 ms and 938 MB for a full guardrail forward pass at 10K tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Deep Alignment (Qi et al., 2025), ADA(LP) achieves 100% refusal on AdvBench and StrongREJECT versus 58.1% and 61.3% respectively at d=500, and also reduces XSTest over-refusal from 12.8% to 0.4%. Against Self-Defense (Phute et al., 2023), ADA(LP) matches or exceeds refusal rates (100% vs. 99.2% on AdvBench) while cutting XSTest over-refusal from 20.6% to 0.4% and MMLU over-refusal from 3.4% to 0.3%. Against the strongest external guardrails, ADA(LP) outperforms Llama-Guard-4-12B (100% vs. 94.6% on AdvBench; 0.3% vs. 13.2% MMLU over-refusal) and Granite-Guardian-3.3-8B (100% vs. 99.6% on AdvBench; 0.4% vs. 8.6% XSTest over-refusal). On adversarial prompt attacks (Table 4), ADA(LP) achieves 2.0% average ASR on Gemma-2-9B-IT, matching Granite-Guardian (0.5%) and Self-Defense (0.5%) while far exceeding Deep Alignment (62.5%) and Llama-Guard (9.0%); on Llama-2-7b-chat-hf it reaches 1.5% average ASR, beating Deep Alignment (12.0%) and Llama-Guard (10.0%) while tying Self-Defense (2.0%). ADA(LP) also remains robust after 1,000 steps of benign SFT (>99% refusal at d=100), whereas Deep Alignment collapses to ~10% under the same conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of either training deeper alignment (Qi et al., 2025) or deploying external guardrail classifiers (Llama Guard, ShieldGemma, Granite Guardian) by discovering that the model's own assistant-header tokens already encode a depth-invariant, linearly separable safety signal that can be 'unlocked' at inference time. It builds conceptually on prior observations that refusal signals exist at the prompt-level assistant header (Xu et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025) and on linear-probe interpretability work (Arditi et al., 2024; Wollschläger et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025), but extends these to show the signal persists and is accessible at arbitrary generation depths, not just at the start. The method repurposes existing chat-template tokens as 'Safety Tokens' rather than introducing new architectural components or training objectives, positioning itself as a training-free, inference-time intervention that leverages the base model as its own guardrail.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single linear probe on a few template tokens achieves near-100% refusal against deep-prefill and adversarial prompt attacks with constant ~25 ms overhead, compared to linearly growing latency for external guardrails, making real-time streaming safety checks feasible at scale. The finding that this safety representation persists even after supervised fine-tuning (which erases surface-level alignment) suggests a more robust substrate for safety than currently exploited. Key limitations include the requirement for hidden-state access (ruling out some hosted APIs), a small window of harmful token leakage before the first checkpoint fires, and vulnerability in fully adversarial inference-stack control. Open questions include whether the signal generalizes to novel or out-of-distribution harmfulness categories beyond the training corpus, and whether the approach transfers to non-chat-template architectures or multimodal models.
