## Problem

The development of general-purpose 4D world models is fundamentally constrained by the scarcity of high-quality, multi-domain, multi-modal datasets with rich geometric annotations. Existing synthetic datasets (e.g., Sintel, FlyingThings++) are limited in scale, scene diversity, and modality coverage, while real-world datasets (e.g., KITTI, Bonn) are confined to narrow scenarios and lack precise depth or camera pose ground truth. This gap hinders both the training and rigorous evaluation of 3D geometric foundation models and camera-controlled video generation models on complex, dynamic environments.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 1280×720 RGB video sequences (up to 384 frames per clip, 24 FPS) across four domains (simulator, robot, human, internet), each annotated with dense depth maps, accurate camera poses, structured text captions (150–250 tokens per 81-frame segment), dense optical flow at native resolution, and temporally consistent foreground masks. For the geometric prediction benchmark, models consume RGB video sequences and output per-frame depth maps or camera trajectories; for the video generation benchmark, models take a text prompt or reference image plus a camera trajectory as conditioning and produce 25-frame 720×480 video clips.

## Method

OmniWorld's core contribution is a multi-domain data acquisition and annotation pipeline. Game data (OmniWorld-Game, 96K clips / 18.5M frames) is captured via ReShade for ground-truth depth and OBS for synchronized RGB, then filtered through a multi-stage quality pipeline (SIFT-based texture checks, depth validity, underexposure, dynamic occlusion via Grounding DINO + SAM2, and RAFT-based forward-backward flow consistency). Camera poses are estimated with a two-stage pipeline: coarse initialization via VGGT (no depth) or DroidCalib (with depth), followed by refinement through dense point tracking (SuperPoint + CoTracker3) on static background regions and bundle adjustment. Depth for public datasets is recovered via Prior Depth Anything (noisy RGB-D) or FoundationStereo (stereo). Text captions are generated by Qwen2-VL-72B with domain-specific multi-view prompting, and optical flow is computed with DPFlow at original resolution. The benchmark uses attribute-balanced sampling across scene type, dynamic complexity, camera trajectory difficulty, and perspective to ensure comprehensive evaluation.

## Results

On the OmniWorld-Game benchmark for 3D geometric prediction, MoGe-2 achieves the best monocular depth estimation (Abs Rel 0.401, δ<1.25 0.589), while VGGT leads in video depth estimation (Abs Rel 0.440 scale, 0.194 scale&shift) at 18.75 FPS. For camera-controlled video generation, CamCtrl achieves the best quantitative camera control (TransErr 1.2882, RotErr 0.2022) but suffers from blurry moving characters. Fine-tuning DUSt3R on OmniWorld improves Sintel monocular depth Abs Rel from 0.488 to 0.370 (outperforming MonST3R's 0.402), and fine-tuning AC3D reduces OmniWorld-Game TransErr from 6.2788 to 4.1428, validating the dataset's training value.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the OmniWorld-Game benchmark, no single model dominates all tasks: MoGe-2 wins monocular depth (Abs Rel 0.401) while VGGT wins video depth (Abs Rel 0.194 scale&shift) at the highest speed (18.75 FPS). Among camera-controlled video generation models, CamCtrl achieves the best camera accuracy (TransErr 1.2882, RotErr 0.2022) but produces blurry dynamic content, while AC3D (T2V) fails to follow camera paths (TransErr 6.2788). Fine-tuning on OmniWorld yields consistent gains: DUSt3R* improves Sintel monocular Abs Rel from 0.488 to 0.370, surpassing MonST3R (0.402); CUT3R* improves KITTI video depth (scale&shift) from 0.537 to 0.314; Reloc3r* nearly doubles AUC@5 on DynPose-100K (6.9→14.4); and AC3D* reduces OmniWorld-Game TransErr from 6.2788 to 4.1428. MASt3R and Fast3R remain competitive on specific metrics (MASt3R: 0.217 Abs Rel video scale&shift; Fast3R: 14.99 FPS) but trail the leaders overall. The benchmark reveals that even the best SOTA models (VGGT, MoGe-2) produce noticeable artifacts in highly dynamic scenes, confirming the benchmark's difficulty.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself at the intersection of synthetic game-based datasets (Sintel, TartanAir, Dynamic Replica, SeKai-Game) and the emerging 4D world modeling paradigm, but departs from prior work by unifying four distinct domains (simulator, robot, human, internet) into a single multi-modal resource with dense geometric annotations. The data acquisition pipeline follows the ReShade-based game capture approach established by prior works, while the annotation pipeline composes existing tools (Grounding DINO + SAM for masks, VGGT/DroidCalib + CoTracker3 for poses, DPFlow for optical flow, Qwen2-VL for captions) into a novel two-stage camera pose estimation strategy that explicitly handles dynamic foregrounds. Rather than proposing a new model architecture, the paper repurposes the dataset as both a challenging evaluation benchmark and a fine-tuning resource for existing 3D geometric foundation models (DUSt3R, CUT3R, VGGT) and camera-controlled video generation models (AC3D, CamCtrl).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OmniWorld addresses a critical data bottleneck: existing datasets for 4D world modeling lack the combination of scale (18M+ frames), domain diversity, and geometric annotation richness needed to train truly general models. The benchmark quantifies that even the strongest current models (VGGT, MoGe-2) produce noticeable artifacts in highly dynamic long-sequence scenes, and that camera-controlled video generation models cannot simultaneously achieve high perceptual quality and precise camera following in dynamic environments. Open questions include whether the multi-domain integration (robot + human + internet + simulator) provides genuine cross-domain transfer benefits versus domain-specific training, whether the synthetic-to-real gap is truly bridged at this scale, and whether the annotation quality (particularly pseudo-labeled depth for DROID) is sufficient for the most demanding downstream tasks. As a practical resource, the dataset and benchmark are likely to become standard references for the 4D world modeling community.
