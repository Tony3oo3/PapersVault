## Problem

Current LLM-based embedding models operate as single-pass feature extractors, encoding all semantics in one forward pass and ignoring the autoregressive generative capacity that distinguishes LLMs from traditional encoders. This paper asks whether iterative generation can progressively refine semantic representations, and identifies three challenges: naive generation degrades quality, there is no clear generation target, and existing frameworks lack multi-step generative supervision.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text sequence formatted with a task-specific instruction (e.g., 'Instruct: {task}\nQuery: {q}') and produces a single d-dimensional embedding vector. Unusually, the embedding is not extracted from the input tokens but from K autoregressively generated soft tokens appended after the input; K is adjustable at inference time (trained at K=5, evaluated up to K=20), enabling a test-time scaling knob where more generation steps yield better embeddings without retraining.

## Method

GIRCSE generates K soft tokens autoregressively, where each soft token is the full softmax probability distribution over the vocabulary rather than a single sampled token; its embedding is a convex combination of all token embeddings weighted by predicted probabilities, preserving end-to-end differentiability. The generated soft embeddings are concatenated with input embeddings and fed back through the transformer decoder, with the final embedding obtained by mean-pooling the hidden states at the generated-token positions. Training uses an Iterative Contrastive Refinement (ICR) objective that applies InfoNCE contrastive loss at every generation step (not just the final one) to prevent intermediate steps from collapsing, combined with a monotonic-improvement regularization that penalizes later steps from underperforming earlier ones. The model is fine-tuned with LoRA on 0.2M contrastive pairs, and KV caching reduces inference FLOPs to within ~1.0–1.1× of standard single-pass embedding models.

## Results

GIRCSE (Mistral-7B, 0.2M training data) achieves an MTEB average of 67.83 (rank 5) and an instruction-following average of 62.97 (rank 2), yielding the best overall rank of 3.5 across all benchmarks. The Qwen2-7B variant reaches 67.67 (rank 6) on MTEB and 62.48 (rank 3) on instruction following (overall rank 4.5). Notably, GIRCSE uses only 0.2M training examples—roughly 5–10× less data than competing LLM-based embedding models (1.1M–2M)—while matching or exceeding their performance. GIRCSE also exhibits test-time scaling: increasing generation length from K=5 (training) to K=20 at inference yields consistent further gains across tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest MTEB performers, GIRCSE trades a small MTEB deficit for a large instruction-following gain: it scores 67.83 vs. gte-Qwen2's 70.72 on MTEB but 62.97 vs. 35.07 on instruction following, and 67.83 vs. NV-Embed-v1's 68.32 on MTEB but 62.97 vs. 56.62 on instruction following. Against E5-Mistral (67.97 MTEB, 56.95 instruct), GIRCSE is essentially tied on MTEB (−0.14) but leads by +6.02 on instruction following. GIRCSE clearly outperforms GritLM (67.07/59.28) and LLM2Vec (64.57/56.52) on both benchmarks. Against the only other end-to-end generative method, Inbedder, GIRCSE dominates on MTEB (67.83 vs. 50.32) but trails on instruction following (62.97 vs. 77.17), since Inbedder is explicitly trained for that setting. Crucially, GIRCSE beats both fair baselines (Causal-EOS: 66.32/47.05; Bidirectional-Avg: 66.96/43.94) by +1.5/+1.5 on MTEB and +15.9/+19.0 on instruction following, all while using the same 0.2M training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant LLM-based embedding paradigm (E5-Mistral, GritLM, LLM2Vec, NV-Embed) that treats pretrained LLMs as static single-pass feature extractors, and instead repurposes their autoregressive generative capacity as an iterative semantic refinement mechanism. The closest prior work, InBedder, also generates tokens for embeddings but uses hard tokens with instruction-tuning supervision rather than differentiable soft tokens with contrastive objectives. GIRCSE's key conceptual departure is the Iterative Contrastive Refinement (ICR) objective, which provides stepwise contrastive supervision at every generation step and enforces monotonic quality improvement—something no prior embedding framework accommodates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration of test-time scaling for embedding models (quality improves monotonically with more generated tokens at inference, even beyond the training length) is a genuinely new paradigm that could reshape how practitioners think about the compute-quality tradeoff in retrieval systems. The finding that GIRCSE matches or exceeds models trained on 5–10× more data (0.2M vs. 1–2M examples) suggests the generative refinement process acts as an implicit data augmentation mechanism. Open questions include whether the approach scales to multilingual or multimodal settings, whether the soft-token mechanism introduces additional failure modes at very long generation lengths, and whether the modest absolute MTEB gains over the strongest baselines (NV-Embed, gte-Qwen2) will persist as those methods improve.
