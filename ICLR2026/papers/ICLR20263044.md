## Problem

Existing video generation methods face a fundamental trade-off: diffusion-based models produce high-quality clips but are too slow for long videos because bidirectional attention prevents KV caching, while causal AR models enable fast inference via KV caching but degrade in quality over long horizons due to a train-short-test-long mismatch. Additionally, interactive generation with streaming prompt switches introduces a new challenge—maintaining both visual continuity and semantic adherence to the new prompt simultaneously—which neither discarding nor retaining the full KV cache can resolve.

## Input / Output

LONGLIVE accepts a sequence of text prompts that can be streamed and switched at arbitrary boundaries during generation (e.g., six successive 10-second prompts for a 60-second video), rather than requiring a single static prompt upfront. It produces real-time video at 832×480 resolution and 16 FPS, sustaining 20.7 FPS inference on a single NVIDIA H100 GPU and supporting up to 240-second videos. The model is a 1.3B-parameter DiT fine-tuned from Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B, and also supports INT8-quantized inference (1.4 GB) with only marginal quality loss.

## Method

LONGLIVE is a causal, frame-level autoregressive video generator built on Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B, combining three key mechanisms. First, KV-recache: at each prompt-switch boundary, the model recomputes the KV cache by running cross-attention over already-generated frames paired with the new prompt embedding, erasing residual old-prompt semantics while preserving motion and visual continuity. Second, streaming long tuning: the model is trained on long sequences by rolling out its own 5-second clips in a loop, applying DMD distillation (with Wan2.1-T2V-14B as teacher) only to the current clip while reusing the prior KV cache, achieving train-long-test-long alignment without OOM. Third, short-window attention with a frame sink: attention is restricted to a 9-latent-frame local window plus 3 permanently retained sink frames from the video's first chunk, reducing end-to-end compute by 28% and peak memory by 17% while preserving long-range consistency. The full pipeline is fine-tuned via LoRA (rank 256) in 32 GPU-days, and the recache operation is integrated into training to eliminate train-inference mismatch at switch points.

## Results

On VBench (5-second clips), LONGLIVE achieves a total score of 84.34±0.52 and quality score of 85.72±1.07, matching or exceeding the strongest baselines (Self-Forcing chunk-wise: 84.31; Wan2.1: 84.26) while running at 20.7 FPS on a single H100—the fastest among all compared methods. On VBench-Long (30-second single-prompt videos), LONGLIVE scores 83.52 total, outperforming Self-Forcing (81.59), FramePack (81.95), and SkyReels-V2 (75.29) while maintaining 20.7 FPS. In the interactive 60-second multi-prompt setting, LONGLIVE achieves 85.02 quality score and the highest CLIP semantic scores across all six 10-second segments, surpassing Self-Forcing (82.15) and SkyReels-V2 (79.85).

## Baselines & Comparisons

LONGLIVE achieves the highest total VBench score (84.34) and quality score (85.72) among all 10 compared methods, narrowly edging out Self-Forcing chunk-wise (84.31 total, 85.07 quality) and Wan2.1 (84.26 total, 85.30 quality). On the semantic dimension, Self-Forcing chunk-wise (81.28) and Wan2.1 (80.09) slightly outperform LONGLIVE (79.62), a gap the authors attribute to the base model's text-alignment ceiling. In terms of throughput, LONGLIVE at 20.7 FPS is 1.2× faster than Self-Forcing chunk-wise and CausVid (both 17.0 FPS), 2.3× faster than LTX-Video (8.98 FPS), and over 40× faster than SkyReels-V2 (0.49 FPS) and MAGI-1 (0.19 FPS). On VBench-Long (30s), LONGLIVE leads all baselines in total score (83.52 vs. 81.95 for FramePack and 81.59 for Self-Forcing) while being 22× faster than FramePack and 1.2× faster than Self-Forcing. In the interactive 60s multi-prompt evaluation, LONGLIVE's quality score (85.02) and per-segment CLIP scores consistently exceed Self-Forcing (82.15) and SkyReels-V2 (79.85), with the gap widening in later segments (50–60s: 24.65 vs. 23.55 vs. 19.25 CLIP), indicating better long-horizon stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

LONGLIVE builds on the causal autoregressive video generation line (Self-Forcing, MAGI-1, CausVid) and the diffusion-forcing paradigm (SkyReels-V2), but departs from the prevailing train-short-test-long strategy by introducing streaming long tuning that aligns training with long-horizon inference. Its KV-recache mechanism for interactive prompt switching is a targeted improvement over MAGI-1's manual KV-cache window adjustment and over the binary choice of discarding vs. retaining the full cache. The short-window attention with frame sink extends temporal-locality observations from prior AR video work into a practical inference acceleration that, notably, requires long-video training to function effectively.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Real-time (20.7 FPS) interactive long video generation on a single GPU is a practically significant milestone for creative and cinematic applications, and the KV-recache + streaming long tuning + frame-sink combination offers a reusable recipe for any causal AR video model. The paper's key empirical finding—that long-video training is a prerequisite for short-window attention and frame sinks to work—reframes how practitioners should think about the training-inference pipeline. However, the method is bounded by the base model's quality ceiling (self-supervised, no additional video data), and whether these techniques transfer cleanly to larger models or fundamentally different architectures remains open.
