## Problem

Gradient-based adversarial attacks on graph structures must relax the discrete {0,1} edge-flip problem to a continuous [0,1] space, rely on local gradient information that misrepresents the true loss landscape for discrete flips, ignore interaction effects between simultaneously flipped edges, and require differentiable proxy losses and white-box model access. The paper argues that these limitations leave significant untapped attack potential and that search-based (evolutionary) methods, previously dismissed as inferior, can directly solve the original combinatorial optimization problem and extend to non-differentiable objectives like conformal coverage and certified robustness ratios.

## Input / Output

EvA consumes a graph (sparse adjacency matrix A and node feature matrix X), a trained GNN model accessed only as a black box (forward passes only, no gradients), a target node set V_att, and a global perturbation budget ε. Each candidate solution is encoded as a sparse vector of δ = ⌊ε·|E[V_att:V]|⌋ edge indices (not a full N×N matrix), giving O(δ) memory per individual rather than O(N²). The output is a perturbation matrix P ∈ {0,1}^{n×n} specifying which edges to flip, applied via XOR to produce the adversarial graph Ã = A ⊕ P. For targeted attacks the objective switches to a tanh-margin proxy since 0-1 accuracy on a single node lacks sensitivity.

## Method

EvA is a genetic algorithm that directly searches over binary edge-flip vectors without any domain relaxation or gradient computation. Each individual is a sparse list of δ edge indices mapped via a diagonal enumeration Π of the upper-triangular adjacency matrix; fitness is computed by a single forward pass of the black-box model on the perturbed graph. The key enhancement is adaptive targeted mutation (ATM): mutated edges are restricted to have at least one endpoint in V_att \ V_flipped (excluding nodes whose label is already flipped), dramatically narrowing the search space while preserving interaction effects. A divide-and-conquer strategy splits V_att into k_dc subsets and attacks them sequentially with proportional budgets, reducing the effective search space from C(n,2)^δ to a sum of smaller combinatorial terms. For local degree constraints, a projection step ranks edges by their frequency across the current population (rather than gradients) and greedily removes violations. Because the fitness function is any black-box scalar, EvA extends automatically to novel objectives—reducing certified ratios under sparse randomized smoothing and breaking conformal prediction coverage—without requiring tailored differentiable surrogates.

## Results

On CoraML (GCN, inductive setting), EvA reduces accuracy to 41.99% at ε=0.10, compared to 58.51% for the SOTA gradient-based attack PRBCD and 59.14% for PGD — an additional ~11% average accuracy drop over the best prior method. At smaller budgets (ε=0.05), EvA already drops GCN accuracy below the level of an MLP that ignores graph structure entirely (52.95% vs. PRBCD's 66.48%). On the large Ogbn-Arxiv dataset with divide-and-conquer, EvA achieves 47.56% at ε=0.10 versus 66.27% for PRBCD at 1% budget. For targeted attacks on CoraML, PRBCD fails to flip 16 out of all nodes with up to 10 edge changes, while EvA fails on only 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EvA consistently outperforms the SOTA gradient-based attack PRBCD across all datasets and models, with an average additional accuracy drop of ~11% (e.g., 41.99% vs. 58.51% on CoraML/GCN at ε=0.10). Against other gradient-based methods (PGD, PGA, GRPCD), EvA's advantage is even larger, reaching 16–17 percentage points at ε=0.10. The heuristic attack DICE is nearly ineffective (80.57% at ε=0.10), confirming that topology-based heuristics are far from optimal. Compared to the earlier evolutionary baseline from Dai et al. (2018b), EvA achieves up to ~40% additional accuracy drop (35.37% vs. 63.27% at ε=0.20 on CoraML), demonstrating that careful GA design (adaptive targeted mutation, sparse encoding, accuracy-based fitness) is critical. On targeted attacks, PRBCD is slightly better at budget=1 but EvA dominates for budgets ≥2, and on local-constraint attacks EvA outperforms LRBCD while also producing fewer degree violations even without explicit local projection. On non-citation graphs (Amazon-Photo, Amazon-Computers) and the large Ogbn-Arxiv dataset, EvA maintains its advantage over PRBCD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper revives and substantially redesigns the genetic algorithm baseline from Dai et al. (2018a), which was quickly overshadowed by gradient-based methods like PRBCD and LRBCD. The key conceptual departure is rejecting the standard relaxation of the discrete edge-flip problem to a continuous [0,1] domain: instead of computing gradients of a surrogate loss (tanh-margin, cross-entropy) and sampling edges, EvA directly optimizes the non-differentiable objective (e.g., accuracy) over the binary perturbation space via tournament selection, crossover, and an adaptive targeted mutation that restricts search to the receptive field of attacked nodes. The black-box formulation also enables attacks on objectives that are intractable for gradient-based methods, such as conformal prediction coverage and randomized-smoothing certified ratios, which the paper introduces as the first structural attacks on these post-hoc guarantees for inductive GNNs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~11% additional accuracy drop over SOTA gradient-based attacks (and up to ~40% over the original GA baseline) suggests that current defenses evaluated against PRBCD/LRBCD may significantly overestimate robustness, since the true attack landscape is much more damaging. The extension to conformal prediction and robustness certificates opens a new axis of vulnerability: an adversary can break statistical guarantees without changing clean accuracy, making the attack undetectable via standard holdout evaluation. Key limitations include the high query cost (one forward pass per candidate per iteration), which may be unrealistic in strict black-box settings, and the use of a generic GA rather than a domain-specific search algorithm. Open questions include whether hybrid gradient+evolutionary approaches could reduce query cost, and whether the divide-and-conquer relaxation can be tightened for larger budgets.
