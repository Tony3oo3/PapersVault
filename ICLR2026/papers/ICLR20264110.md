## Problem

Activating parallel thinking in LLMs through training remains challenging: existing SFT-based approaches rely on costly synthetic data pipelines and produce teacher-forced imitation rather than genuine exploration, while RL-based methods have been confined to narrow synthetic tasks like Countdown due to cold-start difficulties and unresolved reward design. This paper asks how to use reinforcement learning to instill adaptive parallel thinking on complex real-world mathematical reasoning, and what strategic role parallel thinking plays during and after training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264110/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a math problem as plain text and produces a reasoning trace that may contain one or more interleaved parallel thinking units, each delimited by <Parallel>...</Parallel> tags containing multiple independent <Path>...</Path> branches followed by a <Summary>...</Summary> block, concluding with a final numeric answer. During inference, the model dynamically decides when to trigger parallel thinking; upon emitting a <Parallel> token it pauses the main thread, concurrently generates N independent reasoning paths, consolidates their insights, and resumes autoregressive generation. The parallel cycle can repeat multiple times within a single solution.

## Method

Parallel-R1 employs a three-stage progressive curriculum: (i) SFT on a lightweight Parallel-GSM8K dataset (generated by prompting DeepSeek-R1 on easy problems) to cold-start the parallel thinking format, (ii) small-scale GRPO on the same easy problems to stabilize tag generation, and (iii) full GRPO on the DAPO math dataset to generalize parallel thinking to harder problems. A key reward design contribution is an alternating schedule that switches between accuracy-only and parallel-structure rewards within fixed windows, achieving a superior balance between task performance and parallel thinking activation. The paper also introduces a Multiverse variant (Parallel-Unseen) that enforces path isolation via customized 4D attention masks and disjoint position encodings, preventing cross-path information leakage during both forward and backward passes. Analysis reveals that the model's use of parallel thinking evolves from early-stage computational exploration to late-stage multi-perspective verification, and that a temporary forced-exploration phase acts as a mid-training scaffold, yielding a 42.9% improvement over the sequential RL baseline after the parallel structure is dropped.

## Results

Parallel-R1-Seen (autoregressive variant) achieves the best average score of 48.9 across AIME25, AIME24, AMC23, and MATH, representing an 8.4% relative improvement over the sequential GRPO(DAPO) baseline (45.1). On AIME25, it reaches 19.2 Mean@16 and 38.9 Pass@16, compared to 14.8 and 32.4 for the sequential baseline. The mid-training exploration scaffold experiment (Section 4.6) pushes AIME25 accuracy to 25.6%, a 42.9% improvement over the sequential RL baseline, demonstrating that parallel thinking as a temporary exploration phase unlocks a higher performance ceiling even after the parallel structure is dropped.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Multiverse-SFT-4B (Yang et al., 2025b), which uses SFT on rewritten parallel-thinking trajectories, Parallel-R1-Seen wins decisively on all four benchmarks: AIME24 19.4 vs. 8.2, AIME25 19.2 vs. 10.6, AMC23 70.5 vs. 49.0, and MATH 86.7 vs. 76.8, confirming that RL-induced parallel thinking substantially outperforms teacher-forced SFT on parallel reasoning. Against the test-time Self-Consistency@16 baseline (Wang et al., 2022) applied to the sequential GRPO model, Parallel-R1-Seen also wins on every benchmark (AIME25: 19.2 vs. 18.9; AIME24: 19.4 vs. 19.0; AMC23: 70.5 vs. 65.3; MATH: 86.7 vs. 84.8), while SC@16 requires approximately 16× more inference tokens, making Parallel-R1 both more accurate and more token-efficient. The autoregressive Parallel-R1-Seen variant outperforms both Multiverse-style structured variants (S1 and S2), suggesting that explicit architectural isolation of paths, while beneficial for format compliance, can be detrimental to RL optimization on harder problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RLVR paradigm (GRPO from DeepSeekMath) to the problem of instilling adaptive parallel thinking in LLMs, departing from prior SFT-based approaches (Multiverse, ASPD) that rely on teacher-forced trajectory rewriting and from RL work confined to synthetic tasks (Pan et al.'s Countdown). The Multiverse architecture (Yang et al., 2025b) provides the attention-mask and position-ID design for the Parallel-Unseen variant, but the paper's key departure is using RL exploration rather than imitation to generalize parallel thinking to unseen, harder problems. The progressive curriculum (SFT on easy → RL on hard) and the alternating reward schedule are novel training strategies not present in either the SFT or pure-RL lines of work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that RL can bootstrap parallel thinking on real-world mathematical reasoning, moving beyond the synthetic-task limitation of prior work. The finding that parallel thinking evolves from computational exploration to multi-perspective verification during training, and the preliminary evidence that it can serve as a mid-training exploration scaffold (42.9% gain over sequential RL), opens a new perspective on how structured exploration phases can improve RL policy optimization more broadly. Key limitations include evaluation on a single 4B backbone, restriction to math tasks, and the scaffold result being based on one switching point and one model size; whether the approach scales to larger models or transfers to non-mathematical domains remains open.
