## Problem

Flow-based image editing models struggle with large-scale shape transformations, either failing to achieve the intended structural change or inadvertently degrading non-target background regions. Existing region control strategies—external binary masks, noisy cross-attention maps, and unconditional KV injection—each suffer from rigidity, instability, or lack of selectivity, leaving a critical gap in performing precise structural edits while preserving unedited content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262223/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 512×512 RGB source image paired with a source text prompt and a target text prompt (differing only in the foreground object description within a fixed four-sentence template) and produces an edited image where the target object's shape is transformed while the background is strictly preserved. The pipeline is entirely training-free and mask-free, operating on the FLUX.1-dev DiT backbone with 15 denoising steps (28 NFEs via RF-Solver second-order scheme) and a guidance scale of 2.0.

## Method

The core mechanism is the Trajectory Divergence Map (TDM), which computes the token-wise L2 norm of velocity-field differences between the source-prompt inversion trajectory and the target-prompt denoising trajectory to spatially localize where semantic change is intended. Editing proceeds in three scheduled stages: (1) unconditional KV injection over the first k_front=2 steps to stabilize the noisy initial trajectory, (2) an editing window where TDMs are computed at each step, temporally aggregated via softmax-weighted fusion, Gaussian-smoothed, and binarized with Otsu thresholding to yield a coherent edit mask M_S, and (3) TDM-guided KV blending that injects target features only within M_S while reverting to source inversion features elsewhere, combined with ControlNet residual conditioning (depth + Canny) applied in the [0.1, 0.3] normalized timestep range for auxiliary structural guidance. Feature injection is applied from DiT block 19 onward, and the modified self-attention uses blended key-value pairs (K*, V*) with the target query, enabling selective semantic modification without global structural disruption.

## Results

On ReShapeBench, Follow-Your-Shape (Full Model) achieves the best scores across all four metrics: Aesthetic Score 6.57, PSNR 35.79, LPIPS 8.23×10⁻³, and CLIP Similarity 33.71. On PIE-Bench, it similarly leads with AS 6.55, PSNR 36.02, LPIPS 8.34×10⁻³, and CLIP Sim 33.51. The most substantial gains are in background preservation (LPIPS), where the full model roughly halves the error of the strongest flow-based baselines (e.g., 8.23 vs. 16.42 for KV-Edit on ReShapeBench). Even without ControlNet, the method retains strong performance (LPIPS 9.04 on ReShapeBench), confirming that the TDM-guided mechanism is the primary driver of improvement.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Follow-Your-Shape outperforms all eight baselines on every metric across both benchmarks. Among diffusion-based methods, MasaCtrl, PnPInversion, and Dit4Edit show the weakest background preservation (LPIPS 83–125×10⁻³ on ReShapeBench), reflecting their difficulty with large-scale shape changes. Among flow-based methods, KV-Edit is the closest competitor on PSNR (34.73 vs. 35.79 on ReShapeBench) but lags significantly on LPIPS (16.42 vs. 8.23) and CLIP similarity (26.97 vs. 33.71), indicating it preserves structure at the cost of editability. RF-Edit and FlowEdit achieve moderate background fidelity (LPIPS ~17–19) but still trail the proposed method by roughly 2× in LPIPS. FLUX.1 Kontext, the strongest commercial baseline, reaches PSNR 34.91 on PIE-Bench but still falls short of the full model (36.02) and shows notably higher LPIPS (14.62 vs. 8.34). The ablation without ControlNet confirms that the TDM-guided KV injection alone accounts for most of the gain, with ControlNet providing an additional ~0.9 PSNR and ~0.8 LPIPS improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on RF-Edit's KV injection mechanism for background preservation and RF-Solver's second-order integration scheme, but departs from prior region-control strategies (binary segmentation masks, cross-attention maps, and unconditional KV injection) by introducing the Trajectory Divergence Map (TDM) as a dynamic, training-free spatial localization signal derived from the divergence between source and target denoising trajectories. The staged editing pipeline—initial unconditional stabilization, TDM aggregation with softmax temporal fusion and Otsu thresholding, then TDM-guided selective KV blending with optional ControlNet conditioning—represents a novel scheduling strategy that addresses the instability of TDM in high-noise regimes. The paper also introduces ReShapeBench, a 120-image benchmark specifically isolating large-scale shape transformation as a first-class editing target, complementing the more heterogeneous PIE-Bench.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work addresses a concrete gap: existing flow-based and diffusion-based editors either fail to achieve large-scale shape changes or degrade background quality, and no prior benchmark systematically evaluates this specific sub-task. The TDM provides an interpretable, mask-free alternative to noisy cross-attention maps for localizing edits, and the ReShapeBench benchmark gives the community a targeted evaluation protocol for structural editing. Key open questions include the method's sensitivity to prompt ambiguity (vague instructions lead to diffuse edits), its poor transfer to video (TDM becomes temporally unstable across frames in Wan 2.1), and whether the trajectory-divergence principle generalizes beyond the FLUX.1-dev backbone to other flow-matching architectures.
