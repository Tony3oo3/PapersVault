## Problem

Existing linear attention variants for vision Transformers default to Gaussian kernels without theoretical justification, yet empirical analysis of query-key distances in DeiT, PVT, and Swin reveals heavy-tailed ℓ₂² distributions that cause the Gaussian kernel to oversuppress mid-range token interactions and produce vanishing gradients. This paper asks whether a Laplacian kernel, which decays more slowly with ℓ₁ distance, provides a more principled and expressive alternative for modeling attention similarity.

## Input / Output

The model consumes standard 224×224 RGB images and is built on the PVT hierarchical backbone with RoPE positional encoding. For classification it outputs a 1000-way class label on ImageNet-1K; for dense prediction it serves as a backbone producing feature maps consumed by Mask R-CNN (bounding boxes + instance masks) or RetinaNet (bounding boxes) under the standard 1× training schedule on COCO.

## Method

LaplacianFormer replaces the Gaussian kernel in linear attention with a Laplacian kernel k(q,k)=exp(−‖q−k‖₁/λ), motivated by the observation that ℓ₁ distances in vision Transformers are more concentrated and less outlier-sensitive than ℓ₂² distances. A provably injective feature map is constructed by computing each query's kernel similarity vector against all keys, centering it, applying a diagonal whitening approximation, and adding a uniform bias—ensuring distinct queries yield distinct attention outputs. For efficient computation, a Nyström approximation selects m≪N landmark keys via average pooling to form a low-rank kernel matrix Ĝ=CW†Cᵀ, and the landmark inverse W† is computed via Newton-Schulz iteration (matrix multiplications only, no SVD), with a small diagonal perturbation ensuring convergence. A depth-wise convolution over the value sequence supplements the global kernelized attention with local context. Custom CUDA kernels fuse the Laplacian distance-exponential computation and the Newton-Schulz solver, achieving linear O(N) time and space complexity with significant speedups over PyTorch baselines, particularly in backward passes.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, LaplacianFormer achieves 81.4% Top-1 (Tiny, 2.1G FLOPs), 83.8% (Small, 4.8G), 85.3% (Medium, 7.4G), 85.6% (Large, 11.2G), and 85.8% (Huge, 15.5G), matching or exceeding all compared baselines in each FLOPs bracket. On COCO with Mask R-CNN (1× schedule), LaplacianFormer-Medium reaches 48.0 AP^b and 43.5 AP^m, and LaplacianFormer-Large reaches 48.2 AP^b and 43.8 AP^m, setting new state-of-the-art within their respective size categories. Under RetinaNet, LaplacianFormer-Large achieves 48.5 AP^b, the highest among all large-scale backbones tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the <3G FLOPs range, LaplacianFormer-Tiny (81.4%) ties BiFormer-T (81.4%) while using fewer parameters (12.1M vs. 13.1M) and fewer FLOPs (2.1G vs. 2.2G), and clearly outperforms FL-PVTv2-B1 (79.5%) and PVT-T-PolaFormer (78.8%). In the 3–8G range, LaplacianFormer-Small (83.8%) matches BiFormer-S and InLine-CSwin-S at 83.8% but with substantially fewer FLOPs (4.8G vs. 4.5G/6.8G) and fewer parameters (25.7M vs. 25.5M/33M), while beating SViT-S (83.6%) and HiViT-T (82.1%). In the 8–10G range, LaplacianFormer-Medium (85.3%) surpasses SViT-B (84.8%) by 0.5%, BiFormer-B (84.3%) by 1.0%, and SOFT++-Medium (83.7%) by 1.6%. In the 10–14G range, LaplacianFormer-Large (85.6%) leads SViT-L and MLLA-B (both 85.3%) by 0.3% while using fewer FLOPs (11.2G vs. 15.6G/16.2G) and fewer parameters (63.1M vs. 95M/96M). On COCO detection, LaplacianFormer-Medium (48.0 AP^b) outperforms SOFT++-Medium (46.6), Agent-PVT-L (46.9), and SOFT++-Large (47.0) under Mask R-CNN, and LaplacianFormer-Large (48.2 AP^b) exceeds SOFT++-Large (47.0) by 1.2 AP. The only notable loss is at the tiny scale on COCO, where PVT-S-PolaFormer (43.9 AP^b) and InLine-PVT-S (43.4 AP^b) slightly edge out LaplacianFormer-Tiny (43.2 AP^b).

## Relation to Prior Work

LaplacianFormer builds on the PVT hierarchical architecture and the Nyström approximation framework (from Nyströmformer and Williams & Seeger) for low-rank kernel matrix computation, but departs from the dominant design choice in linear attention variants (SOFT, Skyformer, Performer, Gaussian Kernelized Attention) of using Gaussian kernels for query-key similarity. The paper's core conceptual departure is replacing the Gaussian kernel with a Laplacian kernel, motivated by empirical observations that ℓ₂² query-key distances in vision Transformers are heavy-tailed, making the Gaussian's rapid exponential decay ill-suited. It further introduces a provably injective normalized feature map and a Newton-Schulz-based inverse solver, positioning itself as a theoretically grounded alternative to the 'default convention' of Gaussian kernels in linear attention.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a widely adopted but under-examined assumption in the linear attention literature—that Gaussian kernels are the natural choice for attention similarity—by providing both empirical distributional evidence and gradient analysis supporting the Laplacian alternative. The practical CUDA implementation with Newton-Schulz iteration offers a viable path for edge deployment of linear attention models. However, the empirical accuracy gains over strong baselines (BiFormer, SOFT++) are modest (≤0.5% Top-1 on ImageNet), and the paper explicitly leaves broader kernel comparisons (cosine, polynomial) and non-vision applications as future work, limiting the generality of the claim.
