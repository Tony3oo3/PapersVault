## Problem

Existing mathematical visual reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MathVista, MathVerse) conflate diagram perception with higher-level reasoning, making it impossible to determine whether MLLMs genuinely interpret symbolic diagrams or merely exploit textual shortcuts and LLM priors. This paper isolates perception from reasoning to diagnose whether current MLLMs can actually see and understand the structured, symbolic content of mathematical diagrams.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264222/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes a mathematical diagram image (plane geometry, solid geometry, or line/bar/pie charts) paired with a text question and produces task-specific outputs: shape class labels (multiple-choice from 4 options or free-form), integer object counts, spatial/mathematical relationship labels (true/false or multiple-choice), and fine-grained bounding box coordinates in (x1, y1, x2, y2) format for grounding. All non-grounding tasks are framed as multiple-choice (55.5%) or true/false (19.3%) for scalable evaluation, while grounding uses free-form coordinate prediction evaluated at IoU 0.65. The 1,609 questions span 1,198 unique images with controlled synthetic generation ensuring ground-truth correctness.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary contributions: (1) MATHEMETRIC, a diagnostic benchmark of 1,609 perception-oriented questions across four tasks (shape classification, object counting, relationship identification, object grounding) generated via a synthetic data engine that samples from 16 geometric shapes and 10 mathematical relationships, verifies logical consistency, and renders images with Matplotlib; and (2) GEOMETRIC, a structured training dataset where each {image, caption} pair explicitly encodes object attributes, fine-grained bounding boxes, and inter-object relationships in a graph-like format (nodes = primitives, edges = relationships). Training follows a two-stage pipeline: first, projector alignment on GEOMETRIC with frozen visual encoder and LLM; second, self-fine-tuning combining instruction-formatted GEOMETRIC with MathV360K reasoning data (full-parameter SFT for SVE-Math-DeepSeek, LoRA for Qwen2.5-VL-7B/32B). The key insight is that structure-aware geometric representations reduce learning complexity and enable perception gains (+79% on grounding) that transfer to downstream reasoning benchmarks (+3–4% on MathVerse, MathVista, GeoQA, MATH-V) without additional chain-of-thought data.

## Results

On MATHEMETRIC, the authors' Qwen2.5-VL+ 32B achieves 74.2% average accuracy across all perception tasks, compared to 62.2% for the base Qwen2.5-VL-32B and 53.3% for GPT-4o. The most striking gap is in fine-grained object grounding, where most models score below 20% (e.g., GPT-4o: 1.1%, Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 18.5%) while the GEOMETRIC-trained models reach 79–88%. On downstream reasoning benchmarks, GEOMETRIC training yields ~+3–4% gains: Qwen2.5-VL+ 32B reaches 57.3% on MathVerse (vs. 54.8% base), 76.9% on MathVista (vs. 74.7%), 85.3% on GeoQA (vs. 82.9%), and 33.3% on MATH-V (vs. 31.9%), all without additional chain-of-thought reasoning data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The GEOMETRIC-trained models decisively outperform all 17 baselines on MATHEMETRIC perception tasks. Among generic MLLMs, Qwen2.5-VL-32B (62.2% avg) and GPT-4o (53.3%) are the strongest, yet both are surpassed by Qwen2.5-VL+ 32B (74.2%) and SVE-Math-DeepSeek+ 7B (68.4%) by 12 and 16 points respectively; GPT-o1 (36.5%) and GPT-o4-mini-high (48.0%) perform even worse, confirming that extended reasoning does not compensate for weak diagram perception. Among math-specific MLLMs, SVE-Math-DeepSeek-7B (46.6%) is the closest baseline, but the GEOMETRIC-enhanced version (SVE-Math-DeepSeek+ 7B) improves it by 21.8 points on average, with the largest gain in plane geometry (35.4→84.6) and grounding (3.6→88.4). MultiMath-7B (42.1%), Math-LLaVA-13B (40.0%), and G-LLaVA-7B (30.3%) all trail significantly. On reasoning benchmarks, the perception gains transfer: Qwen2.5-VL+ 32B beats Vision-R1-7B (52.4%) and MINT-CoT-7B-SFT on MathVerse, and SVE-Math-DeepSeek+ 7B (28.1%) surpasses MultiMath-7B (25.9%) and SVE-Math-DeepSeek-7B (24.3%) on MathVerse despite using no additional reasoning data, demonstrating that enhanced perception alone drives measurable reasoning improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on SVE-Math-DeepSeek (Zhang et al., ICML 2025), which introduced a geometric primitive visual encoder for diagram understanding, and on Qwen2.5-VL as a strong generic MLLM base. It departs from existing mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MathVerse, GeoQA, MATH-V) that conflate perception with reasoning by isolating low-level diagram perception into a dedicated diagnostic suite (MATHEMETRIC). The GEOMETRIC training dataset contrasts with prior alignment datasets like MAVIS and AutoGeo, which the authors show produce ambiguous captions that obscure structural properties; instead, GEOMETRIC encodes shapes, attributes, and relationships as explicit graphs with fine-grained bounding boxes. The work is complementary to MINT-CoT (interleaved visual tokens in CoT) and Vision-R1 (RL-based reasoning enhancement), which target the reasoning side, whereas this paper targets the perceptual prerequisite.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that all evaluated MLLMs—including those trained on ~2T natural images—achieve near-zero accuracy on fine-grained diagram grounding, and that they exhibit 'blind faith in text' even under explicit visual-priority prompts, reveals a fundamental gap between natural-image pretraining and symbolic diagram understanding that prior benchmarks have masked. The demonstrated transfer from structure-aware perception training to 3–4% gains on four reasoning benchmarks without additional reasoning data provides a concrete, low-cost recipe for improving mathematical MLLMs. Open questions include whether the 'blind faith in text' can be fully eliminated through architectural changes, how findings generalize to real-world (non-synthetic) diagrams, and how to explicitly model visual-text interactions during reasoning rather than relying on implicit transfer.
