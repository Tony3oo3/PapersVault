## Problem

Existing token attribution methods (Integrated Gradients, attention rollout, ContextCite, TDD) were designed for encoder-based architectures or rely on first-order linear approximations that break down in autoregressive decoder-only models, where token interactions are nonlinear, highly contextual, and causally masked. These methods fail to capture second-order curvature effects in flat-gradient regions, ignore the directional causal structure of autoregressive generation, and produce unstable attributions under decoding hyperparameter changes or syntactic rephrasing.

## Input / Output

Given a token sequence x_{1:T} with embeddings X ∈ R^{T×d} and a target position T, HETA consumes the model's per-layer, per-head masked attention matrices A^{(l,h)}, value projections V^{(l,h)}, and output projections W_O^{(l,h)} to compute attributions. It outputs a nonnegative, simplex-normalized score Attr(x_i → x_T) for each context token i < T, where the score is strictly zero for tokens with no causal attention path to T. The Hessian is never formed explicitly; instead, block-restricted Hessian-vector products are estimated via Hutchinson sampling (m Rademacher vectors per token block) with a rank-64 low-rank approximation applied within 512-token windows at 50% overlap.

## Method

HETA decomposes token attribution into three multiplicative/additive components. First, a target-conditioned semantic transition gate M_T[i] is computed by aggregating attention rollouts (Abnar & Zuidema) weighted by value-vector norms (Kobayashi et al.) across all layers and heads, retaining only paths that terminate at position T under the causal mask, then simplex-normalizing so mass is assigned exclusively to causally connected tokens. Second, a Hessian-based sensitivity S_i is estimated via block-restricted Hessian-vector products (Pearlmutter's trick) with Hutchinson estimators, optionally using a Gauss-Newton/Fisher surrogate for numerical stability, capturing second-order curvature of the target log-likelihood that first-order gradients miss in flat or saturated regions. Third, a KL-divergence term I_i measures the shift in the target-token distribution when token i is masked, providing an information-theoretic contribution. The final score is Attr(x_i → x_T) = M_T[i] · (β S_i + γ I_i), where the gate enforces causal sparsity and the weighted sum balances curvature sensitivity against distributional impact; the paper proves this form is the optimal causal-gated linear combination for reconstructing log-likelihood drops under a constrained least-squares objective.

## Results

On the primary faithfulness metrics (Soft-NC and Soft-NS), HETA achieves 10.3 and 2.31 on LongRA, 9.2 and 2.04 on TellMeWhy, and 3.80 and 2.20 on WikiBio with GPT-J-6B, exceeding the next-best method (ReAgent) by over 2× on Soft-NC. On the curated NarrativeQA⊕SciQ dataset, HETA attains DSA scores of 4.25–5.10 across four models (GPT-J, LLaMA-3.1-70B, Phi-3, Qwen2.5), while all gradient- and attention-based baselines yield negative DSA values. HETA also exhibits the lowest sensitivity to decoding hyperparameters, with worst-case relative change <1% versus 2–5% for all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HETA outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric (Soft-NC, Soft-NS, DSA) across all four evaluated models. ReAgent is the strongest non-HETA method, achieving Soft-NC of 1.68 on LongRA and DSA of 3.60 on GPT-J, but HETA exceeds it by 6.1× on Soft-NC (10.3 vs. 1.68) and by 1.2 on DSA (4.80 vs. 3.60). SEA-CoT and Progressive Inference rank second and third among baselines with moderate Soft-NS values (0.31–0.39), yet remain far below HETA's 2.04–2.31. Gradient-based methods (Integrated Gradients, TDD-backward) and attention-based methods (Attention Rollout, fAML) frequently produce negative Soft-NS scores, indicating that their attributions are less informative than random under perturbation. On the DSA metric, all first-order and attention-based methods yield negative values (ranging from −0.52 to −0.09), confirming their inability to isolate causal evidence tokens in the presence of distractor context, whereas HETA achieves consistently positive scores ≥4.25.

## Relation to Prior Work

HETA synthesizes three previously separate attribution perspectives—attention-value rollout (Abnar & Zuidema 2020; Kobayashi et al. 2020), Hessian-based second-order sensitivity (Alvarez-Melis & Jaakkola 2018; Dong et al. 2025), and KL-divergence-based information loss—into a single target-conditioned score for decoder-only models. It departs from prior generative attribution work (ContextCite, TDD, PML, ReAgent) by explicitly enforcing causal directionality through a masked attention-flow gate and by grounding the combination in a constrained least-squares reconstruction objective rather than an ad hoc sum. The paper also extends the evaluation setting beyond encoder-based benchmarks by introducing a curated NarrativeQA⊕SciQ dataset with a controlled distractor structure and the DSA alignment metric.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides the first attribution method for autoregressive LLMs with formal faithfulness error bounds, local stability guarantees, and a provable causal-sparsity property, addressing a gap where prior methods lacked theoretical grounding in the generative setting. The curated dataset and DSA metric offer a reproducible, interpretable probe for attribution quality that complements perturbation-based faithfulness scores. Key limitations include higher runtime and memory (mitigated by low-rank/windowed approximations), reliance on attention rollout as a proxy for causal paths, and the fact that the large empirical margins are measured partly against baselines of varying maturity; open questions include whether the three-component decomposition is strictly necessary at scale and how attribution quality degrades in very long contexts beyond 2K tokens.
