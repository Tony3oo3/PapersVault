## Problem

Prior distributed clustering protocols either achieved only constant-factor approximations (Chen et al., 2016) or required communicating explicit point coordinates across all servers, incurring an O(sdk) communication overhead. This paper closes the gap by providing (1+ε)-approximation coreset constructions in both the coordinator and blackboard models with communication costs that match known lower bounds up to polylogarithmic factors, eliminating the need to transmit full coordinates in-the-clear.

## Input / Output

The input consists of n points in the grid {1,…,Δ}^d partitioned across s sites, along with parameters k (number of clusters), z (cost exponent, z=1 for k-median, z=2 for k-means), and accuracy ε. The output is a (1+ε)-strong weighted coreset of size Õ(k/min(ε⁴, ε^{2+z})) that preserves the clustering cost for any choice of k centers. Communication is measured in total bits exchanged, and the protocol must be self-delimiting and round-based, with each coordinate requiring O(log(nΔ)) bits for exact transmission.

## Method

The approach combines a lazy adaptive sampling subroutine (LAZYSAMPLING) that draws from the k-means++-style distribution using only stale weight estimates, updating them only when a site's true cost changes by a constant factor, with L1SAMPLING to verify global weight accuracy without querying all sites and POWERAPPROX to encode costs in O(log log n) bits. For the (1+ε)-coreset, the paper applies sensitivity sampling (Bansal et al., 2024) using distributed Morris counters (DISTMORRIS) to compute constant-factor approximations of cluster sizes and costs in Õ(s + k log n) bits, then encodes each sampled point as a center index plus coordinate-wise offsets rounded to powers of (1+ε′). In the coordinator model, a Johnson-Lindenstrauss projection reduces dimension to O(log(sk)), and a novel EFFICIENTCOMMUNICATION subroutine performs coordinate-wise binary search between the coordinator and a site to transmit approximate center locations in d·log(k)·polylog bits rather than O(d log n) bits per point.

## Results

In the coordinator model, the paper achieves a (1+ε)-strong coreset with total communication Õ(sk + dk/min(ε⁴, ε²⁺ᶻ) + dk log(nΔ)) bits, eliminating the sd log(nΔ) term present in prior work. In the blackboard model, communication is further reduced to Õ(s log(nΔ) + dk log(nΔ) + dk/min(ε⁴, ε²⁺ᶻ)) bits, upgrading from the constant-factor guarantee of Chen et al. (2016) to a (1+ε)-approximation while removing extraneous log(nΔ) factors. Empirically, on the DIGITS dataset (1,797 points, d=64, k=10), the EAS variant outperforms the Mettu-Plaxton baseline (MP) in both clustering cost and communication cost for all c > 11, and on a synthetic 2D Gaussian mixture (512,000 points, k=5) for all c > 2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Chen et al. (2016), which achieves a constant-factor approximation in the blackboard model using Õ((s+dk) log²(nΔ)) bits. This paper strictly improves upon it on two axes: (1) upgrading the guarantee from a constant-factor to a (1+ε)-approximation, and (2) reducing communication by removing one log(nΔ) factor, yielding Õ(s log(nΔ) + dk log(nΔ) + dk/min(ε⁴, ε²⁺ᶻ)). In the coordinator model, the prior best bound from Balcan et al. (2013) combined with recent coresets incurs an Õ(skd/min(ε⁴, ε²⁺ᶻ) log(nΔ)) cost, which has an s·d·log(nΔ) multiplicative overhead; this paper eliminates that overhead entirely, showing no points need to be communicated in-the-clear across all sites. Empirically, on the DIGITS dataset, EAS (the paper's compact-encoding variant) outperforms MP (the Chen et al. baseline) in both clustering cost and communication cost for all sampling coefficients c > 11, while AS (without compact encoding) always beats MP in clustering cost but uses more communication. On the synthetic 2D Gaussian mixture, EAS outperforms MP for all c > 2 in both metrics, with the gap widening as c increases.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the adaptive sampling framework of Aggarwal et al. (2009) and Balcan et al. (2013) for distributed k-means/k-median, the Mettu-Plaxton bicriteria protocol, and the sensitivity sampling coreset construction of Bansal et al. (2024). It departs from prior distributed clustering work (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2016; Balcan et al., NeurIPS 2013) by upgrading from constant-factor to (1+ε)-approximation guarantees while simultaneously achieving communication-optimal bounds that match known lower bounds. The key technical departure is the introduction of lazy adaptive sampling (robust to outdated weight estimates), an L1-sampling verification subroutine, and a coordinate-wise binary-search communication protocol (EFFICIENTCOMMUNICATION) that avoids transmitting full point coordinates across sites.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper closes a gap in distributed clustering theory by providing the first (1+ε)-coreset protocols in both the coordinator and blackboard models with communication matching lower bounds up to polylog factors, resolving a question left open by Chen et al. (2016) and Huang et al. (STOC 2024). The finding that no explicit point coordinates need to be broadcast across all sites (eliminating the O(sd log(nΔ)) term) challenges the intuition that distributed clustering requires sharing geometric information in the clear. Limitations include minimal empirical validation (only the small DIGITS dataset and 2D synthetic data), and the practical applicability is constrained to specific distributed architectures; open questions include whether the lazy-sampling and coordinate-wise techniques extend to other distributed problems like regression or low-rank approximation, as the authors speculate.
