## Problem

Existing PEFT-based federated VLM methods (prompt-tuning, adapter-tuning) are designed for static settings and fail under continual learning due to adaptation imbalance and disruption of cross-modal alignment, while traditional federated continual learning methods rely on full-model updates and ignore the dual-stream architecture of VLMs. The paper asks how to design an orchestrated framework that simultaneously resolves adaptation imbalance and preserves cross-modal alignment in federated vision-language continual learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260488/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system operates on RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 64×64 for Tiny-ImageNet) paired with text prompts, processed through a frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 backbone. Each client additionally transmits a 512-dimensional privacy-preserving feature summary (computed via batch averaging) to the server, which uses it to dispatch a subset of K prompts and a shared adapter module. Outputs are class labels for incremental classification and image-text retrieval matches (Recall@K), evaluated under Dirichlet-partitioned non-IID data across 5 or 20 clients.

## Method

Fed-Duet combines a server-side Federated Knowledge Orchestrator with a client-side Dual-Expert Duet. The server maintains a Global Prompt Pool initialized via K-Means clustering on ImageNet word embeddings and employs an Adaptive Gating Network (trained with loss-weighted BCE) to dispatch tailored Shared Semantic Experts to each client based on its feature summary. On the client side, two complementary pathways operate in parallel: a Semantic Pathway fuses local and shared prompts via Dual-Stream Cross-Attention (weighted by λ), and a Parametric Pathway uses MoE-Adapters with a Top-k router for fine-grained feature specialization. Training follows a Progressive Decoupled Optimization schedule (parametric experts first, then semantic prompts) and is guided by a composite loss that includes a CLIP-style routing consistency loss (enforcing modality-invariant expert routing) and a KL-divergence-based expert stability loss (regularizing routing distributions against an EMA of historical policies to prevent forgetting).

## Results

On CIFAR-100 under the most challenging setting (Non-IID β=0.1, 10 tasks), Fed-Duet achieves 84.22% average accuracy and 75.88% last-task accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline FedKNOW (77.55% / 72.16%) by 6.67% in average accuracy. On Tiny-ImageNet under the same setting, it reaches 81.56% / 73.57%. On DomainNet (domain-incremental), Fed-Duet attains 68.47% average accuracy, a 5.64% improvement over the best baseline (FedCLIP at 62.83%). For cross-modal retrieval on CIFAR-100, Fed-Duet achieves 78.18% I2T Recall@1 and 93.20% T2I Recall@1, surpassing the best baseline by +13.16% and +6.20% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fed-Duet outperforms all 11 baselines across every dataset and data partition setting. Against the strongest FCL baseline FedKNOW, it gains 6.67% average accuracy on CIFAR-100 (Non-IID β=0.1, T=10) and 5.88% on Tiny-ImageNet under the same setting. The PEFT-based FCL methods (Fed-CPrompt, Powder, pFedMoAP, MoAFCL) are all surpassed by 5–25% depending on heterogeneity severity; notably, pFedMoAP degrades by 24% under severe Non-IID (β=0.1) while Fed-Duet drops only ~2%. Traditional FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx) and regularization-based FCL methods (Fed-EWC, Fed-LwF) are far behind, with FedAvg achieving only 10.58% average accuracy on CIFAR-100 (β=0.1, T=10) versus Fed-Duet's 84.22%. On DomainNet, Fed-Duet leads the best baseline (FedCLIP) by 5.64% in average accuracy, and on cross-modal retrieval it surpasses all methods by over 13% in I2T Recall@1, confirming that its dual-expert design preserves CLIP's vision-language alignment far better than any single-pathway approach.

## Relation to Prior Work

Fed-Duet sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) centralized MoE-adapter continual learning for VLMs (Yu et al., CVPR 2024), which the paper adapts to a federated parametric pathway; (2) prompt-tuning for frozen VLMs (CoOp, Zhou et al., IJCV 2022), repurposed as a semantic expert pathway; and (3) federated VLM adaptation via adapters (FedCLIP, Lu et al., 2023), extended from static to continual settings. The key departure from prior FCL-for-VLM work (MoAFCL, pFedMoAP) is the explicit dual-expert orchestration—combining server-dispatched semantic prompts with client-local MoE adapters fused via cross-attention—rather than relying on a single PEFT mechanism. The server-side adaptive gating for prompt dispatch is also novel relative to prior federated prompt methods (PromptFL, pFedPrompt) that use fixed or globally-aggregated prompts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and empirically demonstrates that existing FCL methods, when applied to CLIP-like VLMs, fail to preserve cross-modal alignment (scores remain flat at ~0.06 across tasks), which is a fundamental capability of these models. This finding motivates the routing-consistency loss and dual-pathway design, and the 3× alignment improvement (0.20 vs. 0.06) is practically significant for downstream retrieval and zero-shot transfer. Limitations include evaluation only on CLIP ViT-B/16 with 5–20 clients and classification/retrieval tasks; open questions include whether the framework scales to larger VLMs (ViT-L/H), more complex multimodal tasks (VQA, segmentation), and whether the progressive decoupled optimization generalizes beyond the two-stage schedule. The demonstrated DP compatibility (<0.3% accuracy loss at σ=10) is a useful practical guarantee for edge deployment.
