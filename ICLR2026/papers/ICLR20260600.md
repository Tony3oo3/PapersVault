## Problem

Despite being labeled 'end-to-end,' LLMs depend on a non-differentiable decoding process whose hyperparameters (temperature, top-p) must be manually tuned per task, a laborious and inherently suboptimal process since the ideal level of stochasticity varies within a single generation. No existing architecture allows the model to self-regulate its sampling strategy dynamically at the token level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260600/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts a standard text prompt and optionally a natural-language meta-instruction (e.g., 'ensure low diversity in your output'), which it interprets to modulate its internal decoding parameters. At each generation step, it outputs the next token alongside dynamically predicted temperature and top-p values derived from the current hidden state. The top-p head specifically consumes both the hidden state and the just-predicted temperature, creating a micro-dependency that enables finer-grained interplay between the two parameters.

## Method

AutoDeco augments a frozen pretrained transformer with two lightweight 2-layer MLP heads that predict token-level temperature and top-p from the final hidden state at every decoding step. The central technical challenge is that standard top-p sampling uses a non-differentiable hard cutoff, severing gradient flow; the authors solve this with a differentiable 'soft' top-p mask that applies smooth exponential decay (exp(-α·ReLU(c-P̂))) to tokens beyond the nucleus threshold, enabling fully end-to-end training from cross-entropy loss. Training incorporates easy-token masking (randomly dropping loss on 60% of positions where greedy prediction already matches ground truth) and Dynamic Fine-Tuning to prevent the temperature head from over-predicting on uncertain tokens. Only ~400 steps of fine-tuning on reject-sampled math trajectories (DeepMath-103K) are needed, and the heads add just 1-2% latency at inference since all adjustments occur within the standard forward pass.

## Results

On in-domain math benchmarks (AIME, BRUMO25, HMMT25, BeyondAIME), AutoDeco achieves average Pass@1 scores of 47.05 (Llama-Nemotron-8B), 38.24 (R1-Distill-Qwen-7B), 57.47 (Qwen3-30B-Instruct), 71.22 (Qwen3-235B-Thinking), and 62.06 (OpenAI-GPT-OSS-20B), consistently outperforming both Greedy Search and Default Sampling by 1.3–6.1 absolute points. On out-of-domain general benchmarks (GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro, LiveCodeBench, IFEval), AutoDeco secures the highest average across all five models, with gains of 1.2–4.0 points over the stronger of the two static baselines. Critically, AutoDeco matches or slightly surpasses an oracle Expert-Guided Tuning baseline (which tunes hyperparameters on the test set) with a gap consistently under 1 point, while adding only 1–2% latency overhead and 4 MB memory.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AutoDeco is compared against three baselines: Greedy Search, Default Sampling (T=1.0, P=1.0), and an Expert-Guided Tuning oracle that finds optimal static hyperparameters by tuning on the test set. Against the two standard baselines, AutoDeco wins on every model and every benchmark in both the in-domain (math) and out-of-domain (general) settings, with average improvements of 1.3–6.1 points over the better static baseline. Against the Expert-Guided Tuning oracle, AutoDeco achieves near-identical performance with a gap consistently below 1 point across all models and datasets, effectively matching a practically infeasible upper bound. The gains are most pronounced on smaller models (e.g., +6.4 points on R1-Distill-Qwen-7B over Greedy Search) and diminish for larger, more deterministic models (e.g., +0.06 on Qwen3-30B-Instruct over Default Sampling), suggesting the benefit scales inversely with the base model's inherent calibration. On out-of-domain tasks, AutoDeco also demonstrates adaptive behavior: it predicts lower temperatures when greedy search outperforms sampling (e.g., GPQA-Diamond on Llama-Nemotron-8B) and higher temperatures when stochasticity helps, effectively selecting the best strategy per token without any task-specific configuration.

## Relation to Prior Work

AutoDeco extends the nucleus (top-p) sampling framework of Holtzman et al. by replacing its non-differentiable hard cutoff with a smooth, differentiable 'soft' mask, enabling gradient-based end-to-end training of decoding parameters. It departs from the closest prior work, Adaptive Decoding (Dhuliawala et al., 2024), in two key ways: it jointly predicts both temperature and top-p (rather than temperature alone) and uses a fully differentiable pipeline trained directly from next-token cross-entropy loss rather than reinforcement learning. The training strategy also incorporates Dynamic Fine-Tuning (Wu et al., 2025) to prevent the temperature head from over-predicting on uncertain tokens, and an easy-token masking heuristic to avoid bias toward overly conservative predictions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AutoDeco offers a practical, drop-in solution (two lightweight MLP heads, 400 training steps, 1–2% latency overhead) that eliminates the laborious, task-dependent manual tuning of decoding hyperparameters while matching oracle-tuned static baselines across five model families and eight benchmarks. The emergent ability to interpret natural-language diversity commands and adjust internal sampling parameters token-by-token—without any targeted training—opens a new axis of user-facing controllability, though the authors acknowledge they lack a theoretical explanation for this phenomenon. Open questions include whether joint training of the base model with AutoDeco heads (rather than a frozen backbone) would yield more precise instruction-following, whether the gains persist at frontier-scale models, and whether the differentiable soft-top-p approximation introduces any systematic bias relative to true nucleus sampling at inference time.
