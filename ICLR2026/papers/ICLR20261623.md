## Problem

Looped Transformers have shown strong latent reasoning capabilities, but prior approaches fix the number of loop iterations during both training and inference, causing representations to collapse when evaluated at off-distribution depths. This forfeits the key motivation of looped architectures—flexible compute—since models cannot adapt to variable budgets without retraining. The paper asks whether looped Transformers can be trained to operate robustly across a continuum of compute budgets (elastic depth) while maintaining informative representations at every depth.

## Input / Output

At inference, the model receives a token sequence plus a user-specified compute budget M (number of loops, 1 ≤ M ≤ L) and a step schedule Δ_M that partitions the unit interval [0,1] into M sub-steps summing to 1. The same shared-parameter model produces a next-token distribution at any budget without retraining; the step schedule controls the granularity of the trajectory, with coarser early steps and finer late steps empirically yielding the best performance. At training time, the model additionally consumes a sampled shortcut trajectory length S and its corresponding step schedule for the consistency objective.

## Method

LoopFormer is a decoder-only looped Transformer in which a shared k-block stack is applied M times, with each iteration conditioned on the pair (t_{i-1}, Δ_i)—the cumulative normalized time and the current step size—via sine-cosine frequency embeddings projected through small MLPs into an AdaLN-style modulator that scales RMSNorm parameters and gates the MHSA/FFN residual connections. Training employs a three-term objective: next-token prediction on the full L-loop trajectory, next-token prediction on a randomly sampled shorter trajectory, and a stop-gradient L2 consistency loss aligning the shorter trajectory's hidden states to the full trajectory's, effectively performing self-distillation within the loop. This shortcut-consistency scheme, combined with dual (time, step-size) conditioning, ensures that shorter trajectories produce informative intermediate representations rather than stagnating, while longer trajectories continue to refine toward a shared t=1 endpoint. The result is a single model that scales smoothly in both perplexity and zero-shot reasoning accuracy as the inference budget increases, without the representational collapse observed in naive early-exit or fixed-loop baselines.

## Results

At the full 24× budget, LoopFormer (3⊗8) achieves 10.28 perplexity on The Pile and 44.81% average zero-shot reasoning accuracy across 10 benchmarks, outperforming TMLT (10.38 PPL, 44.69% acc) and Base-Loop (10.91 PPL, 42.88% acc) while remaining close to the non-looped Base (24⊗1) at 9.49 PPL and 45.27% acc. Under reduced budgets, LoopFormer degrades gracefully: at 12× it reaches 11.12 PPL and 43.73% acc (vs. Base 12⊗1 at 9.98 PPL, 44.93% acc), and at 6× it achieves 14.3 PPL and 40.36% acc, consistently outperforming all other looped variants by 1–4 perplexity points and 1–3 accuracy points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Base-Loop (Saunshi et al. 2025), LoopFormer wins decisively at every budget: at 24× it improves Pile perplexity from 10.91 to 10.28 (−0.63) and average reasoning accuracy from 42.88% to 44.81% (+1.93 pp); at 6× the gap widens to 1.31 PPL and 1.74 pp accuracy. Against TMLT (Xu & Sato 2024), LoopFormer edges ahead at 24× (10.28 vs. 10.38 PPL; 44.81% vs. 44.69% acc) and pulls further ahead under reduced budgets, where TMLT-EE degrades sharply (15.79 PPL at 6×) while LoopFormer stays at 14.3. Among depth-elastic baselines, LoopFormer outperforms Naive-Loop-EE, Base-Loop-EE-Cons, and TMLT-EE by 0.4–1.5 PPL at 24× and by 1.3–1.5 PPL at 6×, confirming that shortcut-consistency training prevents the representational stagnation that plagues naive early-exit approaches. The remaining gap to the non-looped Base (24⊗1) is 0.79 PPL and 0.46 pp accuracy at full budget, which narrows to 1.14 PPL at 12×, demonstrating that elastic-depth looped models can approach iso-FLOP non-looped performance without retraining.

## Relation to Prior Work

LoopFormer extends Time-Modulated Looped Transformers (TMLT, Xu & Sato 2024), which condition each loop on the loop index, by conditioning on both normalized time t and step size Δt, and by training across families of sampled trajectories rather than a single fixed schedule. The modulation mechanism (adaLN-style scaling and gating from a time embedding) is borrowed from Diffusion Transformers (DiT) and shortcut/consistency diffusion models (Frans et al. 2024; Lu & Song 2024), transplanted into the looped language-modeling setting. The paper departs from dynamic-compute approaches like early exiting, Mixture-of-Depths, and Mixture-of-Recursions, which route or halt computation at the token level, in favor of a global, user-specified budget with trajectory-consistent representations enforced by a stop-gradient consistency loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LoopFormer addresses a practical limitation of looped Transformers—representational collapse at off-distribution depths—by enabling a single model to operate robustly across a range of inference budgets without retraining, which is a meaningful step toward budget-aware LLMs. The representation-dynamics analysis (curvature, anisotropy, CKA) provides useful diagnostics for detecting stagnation in iterative architectures. Key limitations include the 1.5× training FLOPs overhead, global (non-per-token) budgeting, and the fact that LoopFormer still trails iso-FLOP non-looped baselines on perplexity. Open questions include instance-conditioned schedule policies, whether the trajectory-consistency framework scales to much larger models, and a deeper causal (rather than correlational) understanding of why certain step schedules outperform others.
