## Problem

Human preference models widely used to evaluate text-to-image generation (HPS v2, ImageReward, PickScore) exhibit a strong, previously unacknowledged bias toward images produced with large classifier-free guidance (CFG) scales, rewarding oversaturation and strong prompt alignment even when image quality degrades. This creates a critical evaluation pitfall: recent diffusion guidance methods appear to substantially outperform standard CFG, but their gains are largely attributable to implicitly amplifying the effective guidance scale rather than introducing genuinely orthogonal improvements.

## Input / Output

The framework takes text prompts as input and evaluates generated images from diffusion models (SD-XL, SD-2.1, SD-3.5, DiT-XL/2) across multiple datasets (Pick-a-Pic, DrawBench, HPD, GenEval, COCO-30K, ImageNet-50K). The GA-Eval procedure computes a per-method effective guidance scale ω^e by decomposing each timestep's noise update into components parallel and orthogonal to the CFG direction Δε, then measures pairwise winning rates of each method against both standard CFG (at its nominal ω) and e-CFG (at ω^e) using five evaluation metrics, reporting the winning-rate degradation Δη as the key diagnostic.

## Method

The core contribution is the GA-Eval framework, which computes the effective guidance scale ω^e = ‖ε_t^∥‖ / ‖Δε‖ by projecting the method's per-timestep noise update onto the CFG direction Δε = ε_cond − ε_uncond, thereby isolating the component of a method's effect that is parallel to (and thus equivalent to) simply increasing the CFG scale. The paper also introduces Transcendent Diffusion Guidance (TDG), which randomly replaces half the tokens in a text prompt with empty tokens to create a weak condition ε_weak, expanding the guidance search space from the CFG line to a hyperplane spanned by ε_cond, ε_uncond, and ε_weak, with a norm-normalized combination controlled by hyperparameters g and β. Extensive experiments across eight guidance methods (Z-Sampling, CFG++, PAG, SAG, SEG, FreeU, APG, TDG) and four diffusion models reveal that most methods' apparent improvements over CFG vanish under GA-Eval, with winning-rate degradations exceeding 15% on HPS v2, while only Z-Sampling and CFG++ retain meaningful orthogonal gains. The paper further validates the saturation bias through Spearman correlation tests on real and generated images, confirming that HPS v2 and ImageReward scores positively correlate with image saturation across the majority of prompts.

## Results

On SD-XL (ω=5.5, Pick-a-Pic), Z-Sampling achieves the highest average winning rate of 73% over standard CFG, but this drops to 69% when compared against effective CFG (ω^e=13.51), revealing a 4-point degradation. Most other methods show much larger degradations: CFG++ drops from 61% to 58% (Δη=3%), SAG from 60% to 52% (Δη=8%), TDG from 57% to 48% (Δη=9%), and FreeU from 46% to 40% (Δη=6%). Notably, APG scores only 41% even before calibration because its oversaturation-reducing behavior is penalized by biased metrics like HPS v2 and ImageReward. Across all three datasets (Pick-a-Pic, DrawBench, HPD), every method except Z-Sampling and CFG++ falls below 55% average winning rate against effective CFG, demonstrating that their claimed improvements are largely attributable to implicitly larger guidance scales.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper evaluates 8 guidance methods against standard CFG and effective CFG (e-CFG) using the GA-Eval framework. Z-Sampling is the only method that maintains a high winning rate (>60% average) against effective CFG across all datasets, indicating it has genuine orthogonal improvements beyond guidance scale amplification. CFG++ also retains moderate superiority (58% avg on Pick-a-Pic) but shows 12–16% degradation on HPS v2 and ImageReward. SAG, TDG, SEG, PAG, and FreeU all suffer severe winning rate degradations (8–16% on HPS v2), with their effective guidance scales (ω^e ≈ 6–8) explaining most of their apparent gains over baseline CFG (ω=5.5). APG is a special case: it explicitly uses large CFG scales (ω=15) but reduces oversaturation, causing biased metrics to penalize it (only 20% HPS v2 winning rate) while AES (which evaluates image quality only) gives it near-parity scores. The key finding is that simply increasing CFG scale to match ω^e of each method recovers most of their claimed improvements, exposing the evaluation pitfall in human preference models.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: diffusion guidance methods (CFG, SAG, PAG, SEG, CFG++, Z-Sampling, FreeU, APG) and human-preference-based evaluation metrics (HPS v2, ImageReward, PickScore, CLIPScore). Rather than proposing yet another guidance technique, it departs from both by questioning the evaluation paradigm itself, drawing on the observation that humans naturally prefer saturated, colorful images (Lin et al., 2024) to argue that preference models conflate guidance strength with generation quality. The effective guidance scale decomposition (parallel vs. orthogonal to the CFG direction) is a novel analytical tool that reframes how any guidance method's contribution can be measured relative to simply turning up ω.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If the finding holds broadly—that most recent guidance methods at top venues are largely exploiting a saturation/alignment bias in preference metrics rather than delivering orthogonal improvements—the field's progress narrative for diffusion guidance needs substantial revision. The GA-Eval framework offers a concrete calibration tool, but it raises deeper open questions: how to build preference metrics robust to guidance-scale artifacts, what genuinely novel sampling improvements exist beyond CFG, and whether the entire 'guidance method' research direction should be reoriented. A key limitation is that the effective guidance scale captures only the component parallel to Δε, so methods whose true contribution is highly orthogonal (e.g., diversity or distributional shifts) may be under-credited; the paper acknowledges this but does not fully resolve it.
