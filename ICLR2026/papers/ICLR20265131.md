## Problem

Finetuning language models produces weight diffs that encode behavioral changes, but these changes are not interpretable in natural language, and the finetuning datasets are often unavailable or too large to analyze directly. The paper formalizes the WEIGHTDIFFQA task—given a base model, a finetuned version, and a natural language question about their differences, produce a natural language answer—and shows that existing black-box probing methods (direct questioning, multi-turn interrogation) fail to reliably recover the encoded behavior.

## Input / Output

At inference, the DIT-adapter is applied on top of a finetuned model (M' ⊕ A_M), and the resulting model answers a fixed natural language question (e.g., 'What topic were you trained on?') about the weight diff. Training data consists of synthetic triplets (M_i, q_i, y_i) where M_i is a base model finetuned on a dataset with a known behavioral property, and y_i is the ground-truth description. Outputs are free-form natural language strings scored by an LLM-judge (o4-mini) on a 5-point similarity scale against ground-truth labels.

## Method

Diff Interpretation Tuning (DIT) trains a low-rank LoRA adapter A_M via supervised finetuning on synthetic labeled weight diffs, so that when applied to a finetuned model it enables the model to self-describe its own modifications. Training data is generated by (1) sampling question-label pairs, (2) prompting an LLM to simulate the desired behavior and produce instruction-tuning datasets, and (3) finetuning the base model on each dataset to create labeled weight diffs. The training loss is standard cross-entropy SFT over completion tokens, with the adapter applied to each finetuned variant M_i. The approach exploits the commutativity of LoRA adapters and weight diffs (M ⊕ A_M ⊕ δ = M ⊕ δ ⊕ A_M), so the adapter can be trained once and applied to any compatible finetuned model. The method generalizes across LoRA ranks and to out-of-distribution triggers, but shows no cross-behavior generalization and fails at trigger inversion.

## Results

On the hidden behavior reporting task (5-point LLM-judge scale), DIT achieves 4.76 on Qwen3-4B and 4.82 on Gemma3-4B, substantially outperforming the Base Question baseline (1.21/1.12) and 20 Questions baseline (1.38/1.55), and matching the oracle '20 Questions w/ Trigger' roofline (4.73/4.62). On the news summary task, DIT scores 4.22 (Qwen3-4B) and 4.56 (Gemma3-4B), outperforming the Generated Story baseline (3.65/4.11) and 20 Questions baseline (1.98/2.25). DIT also generalizes across LoRA ranks (rank-1 trained adapter scores 4.79 on rank-64 diffs for Qwen3-4B) and to out-of-distribution zero-width-space triggers (4.66/4.58 vs. 4.76 in-distribution).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIT outperforms all non-oracle black-box baselines by a large margin on both evaluation tasks. On hidden behavior reporting, DIT (4.76/4.82) beats the Base Question baseline (1.21/1.12) by over 3.5 points and the 20 Questions baseline (1.38/1.55) by over 3 points, while matching or slightly exceeding the oracle '20 Questions w/ Trigger' roofline (4.73/4.62) that has access to the secret trigger phrase. On the news summary task, DIT (4.22/4.56) outperforms the strongest baseline, Generated Story (3.65/4.11), by 0.57/0.45 points, because generated stories contain extra details that confuse the summarization judge. An LLM agent baseline with query access to both base and finetuned models underperforms even the simple 20-questions baseline, suggesting that interactive probing is less effective than DIT's direct introspection. However, DIT shows essentially no cross-behavior generalization: a topic-trained adapter scores only 1.25 on news summaries (vs. 4.22 for the news-trained adapter), and vice versa, indicating the method is currently task-specific.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM introspection line of work (Binder et al., 2024; Betley et al., 2025) and the activation-interpretation task LATENTQA (Pan et al., 2024) from internal activations to weight diffs, introducing the WEIGHTDIFFQA formulation. Mechanistically, it repurposes LoRA (Hu et al., 2021) as a lightweight adapter that, when applied to a finetuned model, enables self-description of the finetuning-induced changes. The paper positions DIT as complementary to existing model-diffing techniques (sparse crosscoders, stage-wise dictionary updates) that operate at the circuit/feature level, and to black-box probing methods, by synthesizing overall behavioral changes into concise natural language.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DIT offers a principled path toward automated detection of backdoors, trojans, and data poisoning without access to the finetuning dataset, and the synthetic-benchmark design (WEIGHTDIFFQA) sidesteps the ground-truth problem that plagues interpretability evaluation. However, the current method has severe generalization limits: adapters show essentially zero cross-behavior transfer (topic adapter scores ~1.2 on news summaries, matching the naive baseline), and trigger inversion fails completely (0/100), suggesting the learned introspection is narrowly tied to the training distribution. The authors identify scaling up training data diversity, better adapter architectures with lossless access to model internals, and RL-based introspective reasoning as the key challenges for making DIT practically useful on real-world, multi-behavior weight diffs.
