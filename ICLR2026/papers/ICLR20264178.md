## Problem

Existing reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) for medical VLMs operates exclusively in the textual domain, forcing models to reason about fine-grained visual evidence (lesion localization, tissue density comparison, anatomical quantification) without ever interacting with the image during inference. This text-only paradigm produces visual hallucinations and language shortcuts that are unacceptable in safety-critical clinical settings. Moreover, the intermediate visual grounding steps that would enable proper supervision are prohibitively expensive to annotate in medicine, creating a paradox where the most needed supervision is the hardest to obtain.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a medical image (any modality: CT, MRI, X-ray, ultrasound, dermoscopy, fundus, OCT, microscopy) and a free-text or multiple-choice clinical question. During training, images with a longest side exceeding 1024 px are resized to 1024 px; at inference, full-resolution images are used so the Zoom-in tool can access maximum detail. The output is a structured reasoning trajectory that interleaves free-text deliberation (within think tags) with JSON-formatted Zoom-in tool calls specifying bounding-box coordinates, followed by a final answer. The cropped image regions are re-encoded by the same vision encoder and fed back as observation tokens, which are masked from the policy loss.

## Method

MedVR trains a Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone via GRPO to produce agentic trajectories that interleave textual chain-of-thought with a Zoom-in tool (cropping a specified image region and re-encoding it). Two annotation-free mechanisms provide fine-grained supervision: (1) Entropy-guided Visual Regrounding (EVR) monitors token-level entropy during tool-coordinate generation and, when the rolling entropy exceeds a baseline by a threshold, stochastically forks the generation to explore multiple visual hypotheses in parallel, allocating the exploration budget precisely where spatial uncertainty is highest (branch probability P = P_base + γ·ΔH_tool). (2) Consensus-based Credit Assignment (CCA) collects the bounding boxes from all successful trajectories, rasterizes them into binary masks, and computes a majority-vote consensus heatmap; each trajectory's tool reward is then tiered by the IoU between its visual footprint and this consensus mask (1.0 if IoU > η, 0.5 otherwise), rewarding not just correct answers but visually consistent reasoning paths. The composite terminal reward (accuracy + format penalty + conditional tool reward) requires no intermediate human annotations, making the entire visual-reasoning curriculum self-supervised.

## Results

MedVR achieves state-of-the-art performance across all six medical VQA benchmarks. On General-Domain multi-choice tasks, it scores 96.8 on OmniMedVQA, 54.3 on PMC-VQA (OOD), and 26.4 on MedXpertQA (OOD), yielding an average of 59.2. On Modality-Specific free-text tasks, it reaches 74.4 on VQA-RAD, 85.3 on SLAKE, and 62.3 on PathVQA (OOD), for an average of 74.0. These results surpass the strongest medical-specific baseline (Lingshu-7B) by 4.2 points on the general-domain average and 3.7 points on the modality-specific average, while also exceeding the 32B-parameter Qwen2.5-VL-32B backbone by 9.7 and 12.4 points respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedVR outperforms all ten baselines on the overall averages for both task categories. Against the strongest medical-specific baseline, Lingshu-7B, MedVR gains +12.6 on OmniMedVQA (96.8 vs 84.2), +6.5 on VQA-RAD (74.4 vs 67.9), and +2.2 on SLAKE (85.3 vs 83.1), while matching on PMC-VQA (54.3) and trailing by 0.1 on MedXpertQA (26.4 vs 26.5). Compared to the general-purpose InternVL3-14B, MedVR leads by +14.9 on OmniMedVQA, +12.5 on SLAKE, and +14.3 on PathVQA, demonstrating that targeted visual reasoning training surpasses simply scaling model parameters. Against its own backbone Qwen2.5-VL-7B, the RL training yields dramatic gains of +37.8 on OmniMedVQA and +18.2 on PathVQA. Notably, MedVR (7B) also exceeds the much larger Qwen2.5-VL-32B by +9.7 (general-domain avg) and +12.4 (modality-specific avg), and surpasses MedGemma-4B (trained on 33M proprietary samples) by +13.9 and +8.1 on the respective averages, despite being trained on only 36K filtered OmniMedVQA samples with no access to other benchmark data.

## Relation to Prior Work

MedVR sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) medical VLMs trained with RL (Med-R1, MedVLM-R1, Lingshu), which improve textual reasoning but ignore visual interaction; (2) general-purpose agentic visual reasoning (DeepEyes, Pixel-Reasoner, Chain-of-Focus), which use zoom-in/ROI tools but require fine-grained grounding annotations for cold-start; and (3) annotation-free RL (EMPO, INTUITOR, MM-UPT), which use intrinsic rewards but operate on text-only tasks. MedVR departs from all three by introducing an agentic RL framework for medical VLMs that uses entropy-guided branching (EVR) as an uncertainty-aware exploration strategy and consensus-based pseudo-labeling (CCA) as a self-generated visual grounding reward, eliminating the need for any intermediate supervision while retaining the tool-augmented reasoning loop.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The annotation-free CCA mechanism achieving near-parity with a supervised upper bound (79.08% vs 79.62% accuracy on GEMEX-ThinkVG) is practically significant: it suggests that the prohibitively expensive medical grounding annotations that bottleneck current visual-reasoning VLMs may be unnecessary, enabling scalable training on existing VQA data alone. The framework's domain-agnostic generalization (outperforming DeepEyes on RefCOCO and MathVista) indicates the EVR+CCA principles are not medical-specific heuristics. Key limitations include reliance on a single Zoom-in tool (no pan, window adjustment, or 3D navigation), which constrains expressiveness for complex diagnostic workflows, and the evaluation is limited to 2D static images. Open questions include whether the approach extends to volumetric data, multi-tool agentic settings, and whether the consensus mechanism degrades when the model's base grounding ability is very weak.
