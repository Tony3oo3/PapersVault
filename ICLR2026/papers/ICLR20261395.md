## Problem

While prior work has documented political bias in LLMs and proposed pluralistic alignment as a goal, no existing benchmark quantifies how much of the Overton window—the full set of legitimate perspectives on a subjective question—a model actually covers. Existing evaluations like Model Slant measure perceived partisan slant via pairwise comparisons, which conflates neutrality with pluralism and cannot detect when a model omits minority viewpoints while appearing centrist. This paper introduces the first set-coverage metric (OVERTONSCORE) and a large-scale human benchmark to measure Overton pluralism directly.

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes 60 subjective questions (15 politically salient from Model Slant, 45 values-guided from PRISM) and 8 LLM responses per question as input. Each of 1,208 U.S.-representative participants writes a 75–300 character free-form response, rates all 8 LLM outputs on a 1–5 'perceived representation' Likert scale, and casts agree/disagree/neutral votes on at least 10 peer statements. The primary outputs are the unweighted OVERTONSCORE (fraction of distinct viewpoint clusters covered, threshold ≥ 4/5 mean rating) and the prevalence-weighted OVERTONSCORE_W, computed per question and averaged across the full set; the automated variant additionally produces predicted per-user Likert ratings from Gemini 2.5 Pro.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage pipeline: (1) a human study that clusters participants into distinct viewpoint groups using an adapted POL.IS k-means algorithm (Small et al., 2021) over sparse agree/disagree/neutral voting matrices, with dynamic k selected by maximizing silhouette score per question; and (2) a set-coverage metric where a viewpoint cluster is 'covered' if the mean representation rating among its members for a given LLM response is ≥ 4 on the 1–5 scale, yielding OVERTONSCORE as the average coverage across questions. For scalable evaluation, the paper replaces human ratings with predictions from Gemini 2.5 Pro using a few-shot + free-response (FS+FR) prompt that conditions on example ratings of other LLM responses to the same question plus the target user's own written response, achieving Spearman ρ = 0.88 rank correlation with human OVERTONSCORES. Statistical inference uses an OLS linear probability model with question fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors to control for baseline difficulty across questions.

## Results

Across 8 frontier LLMs evaluated on 60 subjective questions with 1,208 U.S.-representative participants, the average adjusted OVERTONSCORE is 0.39 (unweighted) and 0.48 (weighted), far below the theoretical maximum of 1.0. DeepSeek V3 achieves the highest score (0.417 unweighted, 0.530 weighted), while Gemma 3-27B is significantly below average (p=0.016 unweighted, p=0.036 weighted). Even pooling the best response across all eight models yields only 0.687 (unweighted) or 0.768 (weighted), indicating substantial uncovered Overton window. The automated Gemini 2.5 Pro judge reproduces human rankings with Spearman ρ=0.88 and MAE of 0.66±0.01 Likert points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary external comparison is with Model Slant (Westwood et al., 2025), which measures perceived bipartisan political slant via pairwise human evaluations. The two metrics show a moderate negative correlation (Pearson r=−0.41, Spearman ρ=−0.32, Kendall τ=−0.24): models that are more Overton-pluralistic tend to be judged as more politically slanted. Notably, o4-mini is ranked second-most slanted in Model Slant yet is the most Overton-pluralistic on the shared 15 questions, demonstrating that political neutrality and pluralistic representation are distinct constructs. Internally, all models surpass the single-viewpoint baseline (OVERTONSCORE=0.169), but all except DeepSeek V3 fall short of the single-largest-viewpoint weighted baseline (0.524), indicating models often fail to even cover the majority viewpoint. The best-across-models reference (0.687 unweighted, 0.768 weighted) shows that even combining the strongest response from all eight systems leaves over 20% of the Overton window uncovered. For the automated benchmark, Gemini 2.5 Pro with few-shot + free-response prompting (MAE=0.66, ρ=0.66) outperforms both the semantic similarity baseline (MAE=0.72, ρ=0.59) and the mean-of-others baseline (MAE=0.70, ρ=0.64), winning over 50% of pairwise comparisons (average 58%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the Overton pluralism concept from Sorensen et al. (2024b) into a concrete set-coverage benchmark, building on the Polis clustering algorithm (Small et al., 2021) to identify distinct human viewpoints via peer voting rather than NLI-based value detection or predefined taxonomies. It departs from Model Slant (Westwood et al., 2025), which measures bipartisan political slant via pairwise comparisons, and from Modular Pluralism/VITAL, which rely on entailment heuristics over fixed value sets—neither of which estimates the Overton window itself or measures coverage grounded in actual human perspectives. The automated evaluation component extends the LLM-as-a-judge paradigm (Argyle et al., 2023) to the specific task of predicting individual representation ratings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that all current frontier LLMs cover only ~35-41% of the Overton window, and even pooling all eight models reaches only 0.69, establishing a clear quantitative target for pluralistic alignment research. The moderate negative correlation between perceived political neutrality and pluralistic coverage (r=-0.41) challenges the common assumption that 'neutral' outputs are maximally representative, suggesting a genuine trade-off that alignment strategies must navigate. Key limitations include the U.S.-only sample, 60-question scope, and reliance on a single LLM judge (Gemini 2.5 Pro) whose predictions systematically overrate Claude 3.7 Sonnet; open questions include cross-cultural generalization, dynamic Overton windows, and whether the automated proxy degrades as models improve.
