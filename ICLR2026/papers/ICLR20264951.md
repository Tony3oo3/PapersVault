## Problem

Channel-dependent (CD) multivariate time series forecasting models overfit to the fixed positional ordering of channels, suffering catastrophic degradation (e.g., >400% error increase) when channels are shuffled or added at inference time. Channel-independent (CI) models avoid this fragility but ignore cross-channel interactions entirely, sacrificing the core advantage of multivariate analysis. No existing framework simultaneously achieves accurate relational modeling and robustness to channel reordering or addition without retraining.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series lookback window X ∈ R^{L×C} (L=336 timesteps, C channels) and produces a forecast Y ∈ R^{T×C} for the next T=336 steps. Each channel is independently encoded by a frozen Sundial foundation model into a single D-dimensional token, so the spatial module operates on a set of C tokens rather than the full L×C matrix. The architecture is designed to accept channels in any order, with channels added or removed at inference time requiring no retraining, making it suitable for dynamic sensor networks and evolving financial metrics.

## Method

CPiRi employs a three-stage spatio-temporal decoupled architecture: a frozen Sundial encoder extracts per-channel temporal features independently (preserving CI robustness), a lightweight trainable Transformer encoder block performs permutation-equivariant self-attention over the set of channel tokens to model content-driven inter-channel relations, and a frozen Sundial decoder generates forecasts independently per channel. During training, a random channel permutation is applied to both input and target at every step, forcing the spatial module to discard positional shortcuts and learn a transferable relational reasoning function. Theoretically, minimizing expected loss over the uniform distribution of all C! permutations drives the learned function to be permutation-equivariant, which by Deep Sets decomposition requires symmetric aggregation—precisely the structure that multi-head self-attention provides. This combination of frozen pretrained temporal priors, equivariant spatial attention, and permutation-invariant regularization yields SOTA accuracy with near-zero degradation under channel shuffling and strong inductive generalization to unseen channels.

## Results

CPiRi achieves state-of-the-art WAPE on 4 of 5 standard benchmarks: PEMS-BAY (3.90%), PEMS-04 (11.67%), PEMS-08 (9.43%), SD (12.25%), and Electricity (9.90%), outperforming the best CD baseline STID by up to 1.47% WAPE (PEMS-08: 9.43% vs. 10.90%). On METR-LA, CPiRi (9.14%) trails STID (8.48%) and Crossformer (8.84%), which leverage exogenous holiday features unavailable to the sequence-only CPiRi. Critically, CPiRi maintains near-identical performance under channel shuffling (ΔWAPE < 0.25%), while CD models like Informer degrade by >400% and STID by >235%. On large-scale datasets (up to 8,600 channels), CPiRi achieves 11.00–12.68% WAPE with only 8.00 GB average GPU memory, versus 75.68 GB for Timer-XL.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CPiRi outperforms all CI baselines (PatchTST, DLinear, Sundial, Chronos-Bolt) by substantial margins across every dataset, with the largest gaps on PEMS-04 (11.67% vs. 15.54% for PatchTST) and PEMS-08 (9.43% vs. 12.37%), confirming that cross-channel interaction is essential for strongly heterogeneous traffic data. Among CD baselines, CPiRi beats iTransformer (the closest competitor, which is also CPI-aware) by 1.27–2.04% WAPE on PEMS-04/08/SD, and surpasses Informer, CrossGNN, and TimeXer by 1.7–4.6% WAPE on most datasets. The only losses are on METR-LA, where STID (8.48%) and Crossformer (8.84%) exploit exogenous holiday features that CPiRi does not use; on all other five datasets CPiRi is best. Timer-XL, despite its large pre-trained scale, is outperformed by 14–34% WAPE and requires 75.68 GB GPU memory versus CPiRi's 8.00 GB. The decisive differentiator is robustness: under channel shuffling, CPiRi's WAPE changes by <0.25%, while Informer degrades by >400% and STID by >235%, and even iTransformer (architecturally invariant) is less accurate than CPiRi in the standard setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

CPiRi positions itself at the intersection of channel-independent (CI) and channel-dependent (CD) multivariate time series forecasting, arguing that both paradigms have a fundamental blind spot: CI models (DLinear, PatchTST) are inherently permutation-invariant but ignore cross-channel dynamics, while CD models (Informer, Crossformer, iTransformer) model interactions but overfit to fixed channel ordering. Architecturally, it builds on the Sundial foundation model as a frozen temporal backbone, repurposing a univariate time series model for multivariate tasks by isolating relational learning into a lightweight spatial module. The key conceptual departure is formalizing channel permutation invariance (CPI) as both a diagnostic criterion and a training objective via channel shuffling, grounded in the permutation-equivariance theory from Deep Sets, which prior MTSF work had not explicitly leveraged as a regularizer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The channel shuffling diagnostic exposes a critical deployment vulnerability: SOTA CD models like Informer suffer >400% error degradation when channel order changes, a common scenario in sensor networks and evolving financial systems. CPiRi's practical value lies in enabling robust deployment under structural co-drift (channel additions, removals, reordering) without retraining, while maintaining SOTA accuracy and scaling efficiently to 8,600 channels with O(T²+C²) complexity. Limitations include the static fusion between temporal and spatial modules, which may struggle with abrupt trend shifts, and the reliance on a specific foundation model (Sundial) whose quality is essential—removing pretraining causes catastrophic collapse. Open questions include whether dynamic fusion mechanisms or integration of exogenous signals (news, policy events) can further improve responsiveness in volatile regimes.
