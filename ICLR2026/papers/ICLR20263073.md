## Problem

While RL has shown superior performance for complex reasoning compared to SFT, its impact on cross-lingual generalization remains unexplored. This paper presents the first systematic investigation into whether RL and SFT differ in their ability to transfer reasoning across languages, and whether the choice of training language interacts differently with each paradigm.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263073/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a text question in one of ten target languages (English, Chinese, German, Spanish, French, Japanese, Russian, Thai, Swahili, Bengali) spanning math, commonsense, and scientific reasoning. It produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer enclosed in \boxed{}. A critical detail is that the reasoning trace language need not match the input question language — RL-trained models spontaneously switch to English or mixed-language reasoning, and this linguistic inconsistency is shown to be a key driver of cross-lingual generalization.

## Method

The paper trains Qwen2.5-3B-Base (and verifies on SmolLM3-3B-Base and Qwen2.5-7B-Base) using SFT (LlamaFactory, lr=2e-5) and RL (GRPO via verl, lr=1e-6, rollout batch 512, temperature 1.0, KL coefficient 0.001) on translated GSM8K and LUFFY data in English, Chinese, and German. Two key findings emerge: RL outperforms SFT by 17–25 points on average with substantially stronger cross-lingual transfer, and counterintuitively, RL on non-English data (especially German) surpasses RL on English data, a phenomenon absent in SFT. Mechanistic analysis identifies three factors: (1) language inconsistency in reasoning — unconstrained RL models adopt mixed-language CoT that accesses stronger pre-trained reasoning modules, while enforcing consistency degrades performance; (2) sampling-driven exploration — RL's on-policy sampling with both positive and negative examples (validated via RFT as an intermediate) enables more diverse solution paths than SFT's imitation; (3) minimal semantic shift — RL-De preserves pre-trained multilingual representations more faithfully than RL-En, as measured by PCA-projected hidden-state displacement.

## Results

On MGSM (10 languages), RL trained on German data (RL-De) achieves 71.5% average accuracy and a generalization score of 60.4, outperforming SFT-De (46.3%, Gen 19.3) by +25.2 points. RL consistently surpasses SFT across all training languages, with average improvements of +17.5 (En), +19.1 (Zh), and +25.2 (De) points. Counterintuitively, non-English RL training yields superior performance: RL-De (71.5%) exceeds RL-En (62.7%) by +8.8 points, while SFT shows no such pattern (46.3% vs 45.2%). On MMath500, RL-De reaches 61.4% average accuracy versus SFT-De's 30.6% (+30.8 points), and on MMLU-ProX-Lite, RL-De achieves 34.2% versus SFT-De's 12.7% (+21.5 points).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Qwen2.5-3B-Base model (31.8% avg on MGSM), both RL and SFT yield large gains, but RL is substantially stronger: RL-De reaches 71.5% (+39.7 over base) while SFT-De reaches only 46.3% (+14.5). Against SFT as the primary baseline, RL wins on every language and every training language, with the gap ranging from +9.4 (Russian, trained in English) to +34.5 (Bengali, trained in German) points on MGSM. Against RFT (Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning from Llama 2), which serves as an intermediate on-policy baseline, RL-De achieves 71.5% versus RFT's 66.8% on MGSM, confirming that full online policy optimization (GRPO) outperforms offline rejection sampling. The non-English RL advantage is specific to RL: RL-De (71.5%) beats RL-En (62.7%) by +8.8 points, whereas SFT-De (46.3%) and SFT-En (45.2%) are nearly identical, ruling out data quality as the explanation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SFT-versus-RL comparison established by Chu et al. ('SFT memorizes, RL generalizes') from cross-task to cross-lingual generalization, and builds on the RL-for-reasoning line (GRPO, DeepSeek-R1, Logic-RL) by applying it to multilingual settings. Prior multilingual reasoning work focused primarily on prompting strategies (translate-then-solve, cross-lingual CoT) rather than how the training paradigm itself shapes cross-lingual transfer. The paper departs from the prevailing English-centric assumption in RL training by systematically testing non-English training data, a dimension not explored in prior RL-for-reasoning studies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that non-English RL training (especially German) outperforms English RL training directly challenges the default assumption that English-centric data maximizes model potential, with immediate practical implications for how multilingual reasoning models should be trained. The mechanistic analyses (language inconsistency, sampling-driven exploration, semantic stability) are preliminary and leave open questions about why specific languages like German are particularly effective, whether the effect persists at larger scales, and how it generalizes to truly low-resource languages. A key limitation is that the study is confined to 3B–7B models and math/commonsense/scientific reasoning, so the broader applicability of the non-English superiority remains to be established.
