## Problem

Existing web agents built on general-purpose LLMs and LVMs lack systematic, structured web-specific knowledge, creating performance bottlenecks on knowledge-intensive tasks and limiting generalization to unseen websites. Prior knowledge-enhancement approaches for web agents lack theoretical grounding and do not decompose the agent's capabilities into distinct cognitive stages. This paper argues that a web agent must first acquire sufficient structured knowledge before it can effectively engage in cognitive reasoning and action.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261240/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step of a POMDP-formulated interaction, the agent receives a full-page screenshot and its corresponding accessibility tree (AX Tree) alongside a natural-language task instruction. The model produces a structured Knowledge-driven Chain-of-Thought (KCoT) that explicitly decomposes reasoning into three grounded stages—Factual ('what is on the page?'), Conceptual ('what does it mean?'), and Procedural ('how to accomplish the task?')—before emitting a single concrete action from a 9-action space (click, type, scroll, dbclick, go_back, go_forward, stop, restart, wait). Training uses multi-image inputs with a maximum sequence length of 8K tokens to accommodate extended reasoning trajectories.

## Method

The core contribution is the Web-CogKnowledge Framework, which maps Bloom's Taxonomy onto web-agent training by decomposing knowledge into Factual, Conceptual, and Procedural layers, each paired with a corresponding cognitive competency (Memorizing, Understanding, Exploring). Web-CogDataset (≈170K samples across 12 tasks from 14 real-world websites plus open-source augmentation) is organized hierarchically to match these layers. Training follows a three-stage curriculum learning strategy on Qwen2.5-VL-7B via supervised fine-tuning: Stage 1 instills Factual knowledge (element attributes, page transitions), Stage 2 adds Conceptual knowledge (semantic understanding, page structure), and Stage 3 introduces Procedural knowledge (multi-step planning, popup handling, noisy trajectories). The key architectural mechanism is the Knowledge-driven CoT, where each reasoning step is explicitly anchored to a knowledge layer, acting as an 'activator' that bridges latent knowledge and dynamic decision-making—ablations show removing KCoT drops online success from 42.9% to 25.35%. Curriculum learning is shown to outperform mixed multi-task training, as it prevents cross-task interference and encourages sustained exploration rather than premature termination.

## Results

On the proposed Web-CogBench, Web-CogReasoner achieves 84.4% overall, surpassing Gemini 2.5 Pro (80.2%), Claude Sonnet 4 (76.8%), and the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (69.8%). On VisualWebBench, it reaches 86.3% overall (93.6% on reasoning-oriented tasks), slightly ahead of UI-TARS-7B-SFT (86.0%) and Gemini 2.5 Pro (86.6%). On live web tasks, Web-CogReasoner attains 30.2% success on WebVoyager (best among open-source agents) and 17.0%/10.1% on Online Mind2Web cross-task/cross-web, while also achieving the fewest average steps per successful task (7.00 vs. 8.24 for Gemini).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Web-CogReasoner improves Web-CogBench overall from 69.8 to 84.4 (+14.6), VisualWebBench from 76.0 to 86.3 (+10.3), and WebVoyager from 2.2% to 30.2%, demonstrating that structured knowledge training is the primary driver of gains. Versus UI-TARS-7B-SFT, Web-CogReasoner dominates on Web-CogBench (84.4 vs. 46.4) and reasoning-oriented VisualWebBench tasks (93.6 vs. 89.7), though UI-TARS retains a slight edge on perception-oriented VisualWebBench (82.4 vs. 79.0), confirming that visual perception alone does not guarantee cognitive reasoning. Compared to OpenWebVoyager (IL/Max), Web-CogReasoner wins on WebVoyager seen-site tasks (30.2% vs. 26.2%/18.1%) but trails OpenWebVoyagerMax on Online Mind2Web cross-task (17.0% vs. 20.5%) and cross-web (10.1% vs. 11.7%), a gap the authors attribute to OpenWebVoyagerMax's additional retraining on high-error sites. Against commercial LVMs (Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.5 Pro), Web-CogReasoner leads on Web-CogBench (84.4 vs. 80.2/76.8) but lags on WebVoyager (30.2% vs. 54.9%/47.7%) and Mind2Web, where the larger commercial models retain an advantage in open-ended generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant end-to-end paradigm in web agent research (WebVoyager, OpenWebVoyager, UI-TARS, CogAgent) by arguing that effective web agents require a structured, hierarchical knowledge foundation before cognitive reasoning can be reliably performed. It imports Bloom's Taxonomy from educational psychology—decomposing knowledge into Factual, Conceptual, and Procedural layers—and maps each to a corresponding cognitive process (Memorizing, Understanding, Exploring), creating a curriculum-learning training pipeline on top of Qwen2.5-VL-7B. While prior knowledge-enhancement methods for LLMs exist, the authors position their contribution as the first to provide a systematic, theoretically grounded taxonomy specifically for web-agent knowledge acquisition, combined with a Knowledge-driven Chain-of-Thought reasoning mechanism that explicitly grounds each reasoning step in a knowledge layer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled alternative to the 'just fine-tune on more trajectories' approach, showing through ablations that the order and structure of knowledge training (curriculum vs. mixed) materially affects an agent's ability to sustain multi-step exploration and generalize to unseen tasks. The finding that strong visual perception alone (UI-TARS: 86.0% VisualWebBench) does not guarantee cognitive reasoning (46.4% Web-CogBench), while the full knowledge stack does (84.4%), provides a useful diagnostic for the field. However, the approach currently relies on imitation learning with a 7B model and 14 self-collected websites, limiting scalability; the authors themselves flag reinforcement learning for procedural knowledge discovery as the key open direction. The released Web-CogBench and Web-CogDataset provide a useful structured evaluation resource for the web-agent community.
