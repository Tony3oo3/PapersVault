## Problem

While Lipschitz constants of standard MLPs and CNNs have been extensively studied for robustness and generalization, the Lipschitz continuity of the permutation-invariant aggregation functions (SUM, MEAN, MAX, attention) that are central to neural networks for sets and multisets remains largely unexplored. The paper asks which aggregation functions are Lipschitz continuous with respect to which natural distance metrics for unordered multisets, and how this translates to bounds on the Lipschitz constants of full set-processing neural networks.

## Input / Output

The method operates on finite multisets of d-dimensional real vectors, instantiated as 3D point clouds (ModelNet40, fixed cardinality) and word-embedding multisets of variable length (Polarity). It produces exact Lipschitz constants for the three standard aggregation functions under three multiset distance metrics (EMD, Hausdorff, matching distance), upper bounds on the Lipschitz constants of two-layer MLP + aggregation + MLP architectures, measured accuracy drops under element-addition and noise perturbations, and empirical validation of Wasserstein-distance-based generalization bounds across cardinality bins.

## Method

The core theoretical result (Theorem 3.1) establishes a one-to-one correspondence: for arbitrary-size multisets, MEAN is Lipschitz only w.r.t. EMD (L=1), SUM only w.r.t. matching distance (L=1), and MAX only w.r.t. Hausdorff distance (L=√d), while the attention mechanism is not Lipschitz under any of the three metrics. For fixed-cardinality multisets (Lemma 3.2), additional Lipschitz pairs emerge via the relation d_M = M·d_EMD, and MAX becomes Lipschitz under all three metrics. The paper then composes these results with known MLP Lipschitz bounds to derive upper bounds on full set-network architectures (Theorem 3.4, Lemma 3.5), shows that NN_SUM is not Lipschitz under matching distance due to bias terms, and connects the Lipschitz constant to perturbation stability (Proposition 3.6) and distribution-shift generalization via the Shen et al. (2018) Wasserstein bound (Theorem 3.7). Empirical validation on ModelNet40 and Polarity confirms the theoretical bounds and reveals that MEAN-based networks are more robust to single-element perturbations while MAX-based networks are more robust to uniform noise across all elements.

## Results

The paper's central theoretical result (Table 1) establishes that for multisets of arbitrary size, each of the three standard aggregation functions (SUM, MEAN, MAX) is Lipschitz continuous with respect to exactly one of the three considered multiset distance functions: MEAN w.r.t. EMD (L=1), SUM w.r.t. matching distance (L=1), and MAX w.r.t. Hausdorff distance (L=√d). Empirically on ModelNet40, the derived Lipschitz bounds are tight for MEAN and SUM but relatively loose for MAX. Under perturbations (Table 2), NN_MEAN is more robust to single-element addition (2.0% accuracy drop vs. 20.1% for NN_MAX on ModelNet40), while NN_MAX is more robust to uniform noise across all elements (4.8% vs. 13.6% drop on Polarity). The Wasserstein distance between source and target distributions correlates strongly with accuracy drop under distribution shift (r=0.90–0.94), validating the generalization bound of Theorem 3.7.

**Table 1: Lipschitz constants of aggregation functions w.r.t. three multiset distance functions.**

| Distance Function | SUM | MEAN | MAX |
|---|---|---|---|
| EMD | L=M (†) | L=1 | — |
| Hausdorff Distance | — | — | L=√d |
| Matching Distance | L=1 | L=1/M (†) | L=1 (†) |

†: all multisets have equal cardinalities (= M). "—" denotes the function is not Lipschitz continuous w.r.t. that distance function.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against external baseline methods; instead, it provides a systematic comparison among the three standard permutation-invariant aggregation functions (SUM, MEAN, MAX) and an attention-based aggregator across three multiset distance metrics. The key finding is a one-to-one correspondence: each aggregation function is Lipschitz continuous with respect to exactly one distance function for arbitrary-size multisets, while the attention mechanism is not Lipschitz continuous with respect to any of them. When multisets have fixed cardinality M, the correspondence relaxes: MAX becomes Lipschitz continuous with respect to all three distances, and SUM and MEAN each gain Lipschitz continuity with respect to one additional metric. Empirically, NN_MEAN and NN_MAX exhibit complementary robustness profiles: NN_MEAN is more stable to perturbations affecting a single or few elements (e.g., element addition), while NN_MAX is more stable to small uniform perturbations applied to all elements. The paper also shows that NN_SUM is not necessarily Lipschitz continuous w.r.t. the matching distance due to bias terms in the first MLP layer, though removing those biases restores Lipschitz continuity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Lipschitz constant estimation literature (Virmaux & Scaman, 2018; Fazlyab et al., 2019; Kim et al., 2021), which has focused almost exclusively on MLPs and CNNs with standard vector norms, to the setting of set-structured data where inputs are multisets of vectors and the relevant geometry is captured by multiset-specific distance functions (EMD, Hausdorff, matching distance). It builds on the DeepSets/PointNet architecture paradigm (Zaheer et al., 2017; Qi et al., 2017a) and the domain adaptation bound of Shen et al. (2018), while also connecting to recent work on Hölder stability of multiset networks (Davidson & Dym, 2024) and bi-Lipschitz multiset embeddings (Amir & Dym, 2025). The key departure is a systematic, function-by-function analysis of Lipschitz continuity with respect to multiple distance metrics simultaneously, rather than a single norm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides practitioners with a clear decision framework: the choice of aggregation function should align with the distance metric that best captures the structure of the problem (e.g., Hausdorff for shape-sensitive tasks, EMD for content-similarity tasks). The finding that SUM-based networks can be non-Lipschitz (due to bias terms) and that attention-based aggregation is non-Lipschitz with respect to all three distances are practically important caveats for robustness-sensitive applications. Open questions include whether Lipschitz-compatible attention mechanisms exist for multiset aggregation, whether the results extend to hierarchical or graph-structured set networks, and whether the relatively loose bounds for MAX can be tightened. The analysis is limited to single-layer aggregation followed by MLPs, leaving deeper or more complex set architectures unaddressed.
