## Problem

Training LLM agents for complex multi-turn tool-use tasks is hampered by extreme data scarcity (e.g., only 800 samples in BFCL V3), where SFT on synthetic trajectories overfits and fails to generalize out-of-distribution, while standard RL suffers from a cold-start problem and training instability due to long interaction chains and sparse binary rewards. The paper asks how to train a high-quality, generalizable agent from only 400 problem instances without any expert demonstrations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260222/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a sequence of pre-defined user queries (multi-turn), tool documentation with API schemas, and the current environment state as input, and must produce structured tool calls (XML-formatted function invocations) or final natural-language answers at each step. Training uses only 400 problem instances from BFCL V3 (100 per split: Base, Missing Functions, Missing Parameters, Long-Context), with the remaining 400 held out for in-distribution evaluation. Unusually, the environment itself is modified during training to return augmented, pedagogical error messages rather than standard cryptic feedback, and the reward signal is computed per-turn rather than as a single terminal binary outcome.

## Method

ENVIRONMENT TUNING orchestrates RL training through three complementary mechanisms. First, a four-stage structured curriculum progressively increases task complexity: Stage 1 isolates syntactic/format correctness with a dedicated format+tool-call reward; Stage 2 introduces task-oriented reasoning on the Base split with augmented environment feedback; Stage 3 adds complex splits (Missing Parameters, Missing Functions, Long-Context); Stage 4 disables augmentation to align with evaluation conditions. Second, Actionable Environment Augmentation uses a two-agent LLM workflow (an Augmentation Generator and a Constitutional Judge) to transform vague error messages into corrective hints that reveal inter-tool dependencies and internal tool constraints without leaking solutions. Third, a Fine-Grained Progress Reward replaces the sparse terminal binary signal with a per-turn score computed as the product of environment-state correctness and execution-result correctness, averaged over all turns. The policy is optimized with GRPO (group-relative advantage estimation without a critic) augmented with decoupled clipping (ε_low=0.2, ε_high=0.28) and a relatively large KL-divergence penalty (β=0.1) to maintain policy entropy and prevent collapse in long-horizon settings.

## Results

On BFCL V3 Multi-Turn (400 training samples), ENVIRONMENT TUNING lifts Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct from 7.00% to 36.92% average (+29.92), Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct from 5.48% to 28.25% (+22.77), ToolACE-2-Llama-3.1-8B from 37.99% to 47.18% (+9.19), and watt-tool-8B from 35.74% to 54.34% (+18.50). The best result (watt-tool-8B + ENVIRONMENT TUNING at 54.34%) surpasses GPT-4o (51.00%) and o3 (49.25%) while approaching Claude Sonnet 4 (57.00%). On OOD benchmarks, ENVIRONMENT TUNING nearly doubles ToolACE-2's ACEBench Agent score (8.34% → 15.00%) and boosts Llama-3.1-8B's BFCL V4 Web Search from 1.00% to 15.00%, whereas SFT baselines like xLAM-2 collapse from 70.50% (in-distribution) to 5.00% on Web Search.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENVIRONMENT TUNING consistently and substantially outperforms the ToolRL baseline (direct GRPO with format+correctness reward) across all four base models, with gains of +18.92 to +25.67 points over ToolRL on average; notably, ToolRL actually degrades ToolACE-2 by −4.24% while ENVIRONMENT TUNING improves it by +9.19%. Against proprietary models, watt-tool-8B + ENVIRONMENT TUNING (54.34%) surpasses GPT-4o (51.00%) and o3 (49.25%) but falls slightly short of Claude Sonnet 4 (57.00%); Qwen2.5-7B + ENVIRONMENT TUNING (36.92%) surpasses Gemini 2.5 Pro (28.75%). Against SFT-tuned open-source baselines, ENVIRONMENT TUNING on Qwen2.5-7B (36.92%) roughly matches BitAgent-8B (36.99%) and falls below Arch-Agent-7B (42.05%), but ToolACE-2 + ENVIRONMENT TUNING (47.18%) surpasses both. On OOD generalization, the advantage is most pronounced: SFT baselines like xLAM-2 collapse from 70.50% in-distribution to 5.00% on BFCL V4 Web Search, while ENVIRONMENT TUNING on Llama-3.1-8B improves Web Search from 1.00% to 15.00% and boosts ToolACE-2's ACEBench Agent score from 8.34% to 15.00% (nearly doubling it), demonstrating that environment-based exploration fosters robust generalization where trajectory-based SFT fails.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) as its RL backbone, and positions itself against two dominant paradigms for multi-turn tool-use training: (1) SFT on large-scale synthetic trajectories (ToolACE, xLAM, BitAgent), which the authors argue overfits and collapses out-of-distribution, and (2) direct RL (ToolRL), which suffers from cold-start failure and gradient explosion in long-horizon settings. The key departure is the 'Environment Tuning' framework, which repurposes the training environment itself as the primary learning signal through a four-stage curriculum, LLM-generated corrective feedback (Actionable Environment Augmentation), and a fine-grained turn-level Progress Reward—shifting the paradigm from trajectory imitation to environment-driven exploration without any expert demonstrations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides strong empirical evidence that the dominant SFT-on-synthetic-trajectories approach for tool-use agents is fundamentally limited in generalization: models like xLAM-2 (70.5% in-distribution) collapse to 5% on OOD Web Search, while 400-sample environment-tuned models show consistent OOD improvements. This reframes the data-scarcity problem in multi-turn tool-use from 'generate more synthetic trajectories' to 'design better training environments.' Open questions include whether the manually designed four-stage curriculum can be automated, whether the LLM-driven augmentation pipeline scales to open-ended domains without hand-crafted few-shot seeds, and whether the approach transfers to larger models or multimodal agentic settings where the action space is far richer.
