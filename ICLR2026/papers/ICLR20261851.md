## Problem

State-of-the-art floorplan localization (F3Loc) assumes uniform confidence in all depth predictions and requires a custom depth network trained separately for each environment, limiting robustness in challenging indoor scenes (glass walls, featureless surfaces) and scalability to new spaces. UnLoc addresses both gaps by modeling depth predictions as explicit probability distributions with learned uncertainty and by plugging in off-the-shelf pre-trained monocular depth encoders.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261851/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequence of RGB images along with ego-motion measurements, gravity direction (roll/pitch), camera intrinsics, and a floorplan provided solely as a 2D occupancy grid (no semantic labels required). Images are gravity-aligned via a homography before feature extraction, producing a binary mask of invalid pixels. The output is a per-image-column floorplan depth vector and uncertainty vector, which are converted into equiangular rays to yield a 3D posterior probability volume over SE(2) poses (x, y, yaw); the final estimate is the argmax of this posterior, optionally refined by a lightweight SE(2) post-processing optimization over the last 10 frames.

## Method

UnLoc replaces F3Loc's custom per-environment depth network with the pre-trained Depth Anything v2 encoder (indoor variant), extracting features from its last layer and feeding them into a masked attention mechanism that jointly predicts a 1D floorplan depth vector and a 1D uncertainty vector per image column. The key insight is modeling each depth prediction as a Laplace distribution (location = predicted depth, scale = uncertainty), which yields a closed-form observation likelihood that naturally down-weights unreliable rays in challenging regions. This likelihood is fused with a motion-model prior via a histogram filter over the SE(2) pose space, and a final uncertainty-weighted L1 post-processing optimization refines the trajectory over the last k=10 frames using a global SE(2) correction. Training minimizes the negative log-likelihood of the Laplace distribution against ground-truth floorplan depths, and the entire pipeline is agnostic to the choice of monodepth backbone.

## Results

On the challenging real-world LaMAR HGE dataset, UnLoc achieves 100% success rate (SR@1m) on 100-frame sequences and 50.6% on 15-frame sequences, compared to F3Loc mono's 36.4% and 1.2% respectively — a 2.7× and 42.2× improvement. On the synthetic Gibson(t) dataset, UnLoc reaches 97.3% SR@1m at T=100 and 81.3% at T=15, surpassing F3Loc mono (89.2% and 28.4%) and F3Loc fusion (94.6% and 41.8%). The RMSE over successful sequences on LaMAR HGE drops to 0.25 m (T=100) with UnLoc versus 0.45 m for F3Loc mono. On the cross-building LaMAR CAB generalization test, UnLoc achieves 50% SR@1m at T=100 while F3Loc mono fails completely (0%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

UnLoc decisively outperforms F3Loc mono across all evaluated datasets and sequence lengths. On LaMAR HGE (the paper's headline benchmark), UnLoc improves SR@1m from 1.2% to 50.6% on 15-frame sequences (42.2×) and from 36.4% to 100% on 100-frame sequences (2.7×); on Gibson(t), the gain is from 28.4% to 81.3% at T=15. Even when F3Loc is augmented with the same Depth Anything v2 encoder (removing the custom-network disadvantage), UnLoc still leads by 11–41 percentage points on short sequences, confirming that the uncertainty-aware matching and post-processing optimization are the primary sources of improvement. Against F3Loc fusion (which additionally uses multi-view stereo), UnLoc achieves comparable or better SR on Gibson (97.3% vs. 94.6% at T=100) while using only monocular depth. LASER and PF-Net, included for completeness on Gibson and Structured3D respectively, are outperformed by wide margins (e.g., 59.5% vs. 97.3% SR@1m at T=100 on Gibson for LASER vs. UnLoc). On the cross-building LaMAR CAB generalization test, UnLoc achieves 50% SR@1m at T=100 without any target-domain training, whereas F3Loc mono scores 0% even after 10 epochs of fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

UnLoc extends F3Loc (Chen et al., 2024a), which introduced sequential floorplan localization via a histogram filter over monocular depth predictions, by making two key conceptual departures: modeling depth as an explicit Laplace probability distribution rather than a point estimate with uniform confidence, and replacing the per-environment custom depth network with an off-the-shelf pre-trained monodepth encoder (Depth Anything v2). The masked attention mechanism and histogram filter architecture are directly inherited from F3Loc, while the uncertainty formulation draws on the aleatoric uncertainty literature (Kendall & Gal, 2017; Roessle et al., 2022) and the broader trend of leveraging large-scale pre-trained depth models as plug-and-play modules. The broader floorplan localization lineage (LaLaLoc, LASER, PF-Net) is positioned as prior art that either requires LiDAR, assumes known camera height, or relies on learned observation models that do not generalize.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: eliminating per-environment depth network training removes a major scalability barrier for deploying floorplan localization in new buildings, and the uncertainty-aware formulation makes short-sequence localization (15 frames) viable where F3Loc essentially fails (1.2% → 50.6% SR on LaMAR HGE). The finding that uncertainty estimation with a small encoder (Depth Anything v2 Base) matches the large variant without uncertainty suggests a useful efficiency-accuracy tradeoff for resource-constrained deployment. Open questions include whether epistemic uncertainty modeling could further improve out-of-distribution generalization, whether the approach extends to multi-floor or outdoor settings, and whether the ~1-second per-frame runtime on large floorplans is sufficient for real-time AR applications on mobile hardware.
