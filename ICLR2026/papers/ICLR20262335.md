## Problem

Existing brain-to-text decoding methods suffer from semantic mismatch because the human brain selectively encodes only salient visual-semantic elements (with individual variability), while machine representations exhaustively encode all visible content. Prior work (Mind-SA) addressed this by removing a fixed number of image patches, but this hard, fixed-count strategy lacks adaptability to scenes of varying complexity and cannot handle weakly-represented semantics that should be partially retained rather than fully discarded.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262335/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes fMRI signals (27,638 voxels from ventral, lateral, parietal, and early visual cortex regions) flattened into a 1D sequence and split into K=8 non-overlapping patches, along with the corresponding stimulus image (425×425, split into CLIP patches) and an MLLM-generated semantic description (tokenized for CLIP text encoding). It produces autoregressive text captions via a GPT-2 decoder, and optionally reconstructed images by feeding decoded captions plus L2-regressed latent codes into Stable Diffusion 1.4.

## Method

Yo'Mind constructs a multimodal semantic set per stimulus by combining CLIP visual patch embeddings with CLIP text embeddings of MLLM-generated descriptions, then solves an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem (via 100 Sinkhorn iterations) between K fMRI patch embeddings (from a 16-layer ViT encoder) and the N+M semantic elements. A virtual dustbin row/column is appended to the cost matrix (inspired by SuperGlue graph matching), enabling irrelevant semantic elements to be softly assigned to the dustbin rather than hard-masked, yielding a partial assignment matrix P with inequality constraints. This P serves as a dynamic, subject-specific soft weighting in a weighted MSE alignment loss, and the entire pipeline is differentiable and end-to-end trainable. The aligned fMRI embeddings then condition a GPT-2 decoder via cross-attention for autoregressive text generation, with the architecture trained jointly across four subjects for cross-subject decoding.

## Results

On the NSD dataset (subjects 1, 2, 5, 7) with COCO captions as ground truth, Yo'Mind achieves B@1=61.81, B@4=20.88, METEOR=38.40, ROUGE=43.63, CIDEr=56.07, and SPICE=12.42, outperforming all 8 compared baselines on six of seven metrics. With Harmon (Qwen2.5)-generated captions, Yo'Mind further improves to B@1=65.19, B@4=33.36, METEOR=39.25, ROUGE=55.19, CIDEr=81.16, and SPICE=19.25. Compared to the closest prior method Mind-SA, Yo'Mind yields statistically significant gains (P<0.0001, two-tailed paired t-test) across all metrics, with a 91.6% relative improvement in METEOR over the MindGPT baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Yo'Mind outperforms all 8 baselines on the standard COCO-caption evaluation, achieving the best B@1 (61.81 vs. Mind-SA's 59.76), B@4 (20.88 vs. 18.86), METEOR (38.40 vs. 38.08), ROUGE (43.63 vs. 42.72), and SPICE (12.42 vs. 12.25). The most direct comparison is with Mind-SA (Chen et al., 2025c), the prior method that first addressed semantic mismatch via fixed patch removal; Yo'Mind's OT-driven soft masking yields consistent gains across all metrics with P<0.0001 significance. Against the vanilla MindGPT baseline (no semantic pruning), Yo'Mind improves METEOR by 91.6% (38.40 vs. 20.04), BLEU-4 by 31.6% (20.88 vs. 15.87), and CIDEr by 28.7% (56.07 vs. 43.56). On the more challenging Harmon-caption evaluation, Yo'Mind leads on B@1, B@3, B@4, METEOR, ROUGE, and SPICE, though Neuro2Language edges it on CIDEr (83.94 vs. 81.16) and Mind-SA slightly leads on B@2 (53.54 vs. 53.53). UMBRAE (Xia et al., 2024) achieves a higher CIDEr (57.76) on COCO captions but trails Yo'Mind on all other metrics, particularly BLEU scores and METEOR. On the smaller DIR dataset (appendix), Yo'Mind also outperforms MindGPT and MindArt on all four reported metrics (B@4: 18.3 vs. 16.0/15.7; METEOR: 15.3 vs. 13.1/12.8).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the brain-to-text decoding line initiated by MindGPT (Chen et al., 2025a) and its semantic-mismatch-aware successor Mind-SA (Chen et al., 2025c), which first identified that global alignment between fMRI signals and CLIP image embeddings is suboptimal because the brain selectively encodes only a subset of visual semantics. The key conceptual departure is replacing Mind-SA's fixed-count hard patch removal with an entropy-regularized optimal transport (Sinkhorn) formulation augmented by a virtual dustbin, yielding a differentiable, per-subject soft assignment that adapts the number and identity of masked semantic elements without any threshold tuning. Additionally, the framework extends alignment from the brain-visual pair to a brain-visual-linguistic triple by incorporating MLLM-generated (Harmon) textual descriptions as an extra semantic set, building on prior evidence that multimodal supervision aids brain-machine alignment (Du et al., 2023).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The OT-driven dustbin mechanism offers a principled, hyperparameter-free way to handle the variable redundancy of visual scenes in brain decoding, and its differentiability allows seamless end-to-end integration with autoregressive language models for cross-subject decoding. However, the validation remains limited to the NSD dataset where subjects maintain central fixation, so the claim that decoded semantics reflect true covert attention is only preliminarily supported by a small subset of eye-tracking deviations; rigorous biological validation (e.g., dedicated covert-attention paradigms) is still needed. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-visual stimuli, longer sequences, or real-time decoding settings, and whether the 8-patch fMRI split tied to cortical parcellation is optimal or merely a heuristic that happens to work well on NSD.
