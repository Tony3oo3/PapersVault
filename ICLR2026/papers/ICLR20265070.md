## Problem

Diffusion Transformers require many denoising steps with computationally intensive attention blocks, making real-time deployment difficult. Existing training-free accelerators (step reduction, feature caching, sparse attention) apply uniform or manually designed heuristics to every image, sacrificing either speed or quality, while dynamic neural networks that learn per-image adaptivity demand costly fine-tuning of the generator itself. The paper asks whether lightweight, per-image adaptive acceleration can be achieved with zero updates to the base DiT parameters.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a standard RGB image as output, operating on the frozen DiT's latent space (e.g., SD3 or FLUX). At each denoising timestep, the three policy heads consume the current latent feature map, the timestep index, and the condition embedding (timestep + text guidance) to independently decide the acceleration action. The generation process is fully deterministic given the policy decisions, and the final image quality is assessed post hoc by a reward model and discriminator.

## Method

RAPID³ attaches three lightweight policy heads to a frozen DiT: a Step-Skip head that predicts the next timestep via a Beta-distribution parameterized by a linear layer, a Cache-Reuse head that outputs a categorical decision (compute vs. reuse cached residual), and a Sparse-Attention head that selects from pre-defined sparsity levels via a categorical distribution. All three heads are trained jointly via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), where a group of images generated under the same prompt provides relative advantage estimates. To prevent reward hacking, an adversarially trained discriminator (CLIP-based with inset adapters) augments the reward signal by scoring how closely accelerated samples resemble the original model's output distribution. The entire trainable parameter set is only 0.025% of the generator, requiring just 20K text prompts and ~1% of the GPU hours needed for dynamic fine-tuning approaches.

## Results

On SD3 (28-step baseline, 5.77 s), RAPID³ achieves a 2.92× speedup (1.97 s) while simultaneously improving or matching all quality metrics: CLIP 32.09, Aesthetic 5.26, HPS 28.07, and GenEval Overall 63.48. On FLUX, RAPID³ reduces latency from 22.15 s to 8.30 s (≈2.7×) with Aesthetic 5.63, nearly matching the original FLUX (5.64) and substantially outperforming DyFLUX (5.29) at 1/95th the training cost (400 vs. 38,000 GPU hours). The method uses only 20K text prompts for training versus 3M image-text pairs required by DyFLUX.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAPID³ outperforms all 12 baselines on every reported metric. Against static step reduction (SD3 9-steps, 2.91×), RAPID³ matches the speed (2.92×) while gaining +0.21 CLIP, +0.05 Aesthetic, +0.40 HPS, and +11.8 GenEval Overall. Against the best manually-adaptive cache method TeaCache (2.62×), RAPID³ is 0.30× faster and leads on CLIP (+0.07), HPS (+0.20), and GenEval Overall (+0.67). Against TPDM (2.48×, the closest RL-based single-strategy baseline), RAPID³ is 0.44× faster and improves CLIP by +0.11, HPS by +0.32, and GenEval Overall by +2.78. Against the dynamic fine-tuned model DyFLUX on FLUX, RAPID³ is 40% faster (8.30 s vs. 13.93 s), achieves +0.34 Aesthetic, and requires only 1% of the GPU hours (400 vs. 38,000) and ≤0.7% of the training data (20K text vs. 3M image-text pairs). In the broader comparison (Table 8), RAPID³ also surpasses token-merging (ToMeSD, SDTM), token-pruning (AT-EDM), token-caching (TokenCache, ToCa), and dynamic-network (DyDiT) methods on both speed and quality. Against the learning-based cache HarmoniCa (2.01×), RAPID³ is 0.91× faster and leads on CLIP (+0.34), HPS (+0.02), and GenEval Overall (+2.49).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three established training-free acceleration techniques—step skipping, feature caching, and sparse attention—under a single reinforcement learning framework, departing from prior work that applies each technique with uniform or manually designed heuristics. Conceptually, it extends TPDM's RL-based step scheduling to a tri-level action space and borrows the GRPO training objective from LLM alignment, while the adversarial discriminator draws from GAN-style training to counter reward hacking. Unlike dynamic neural network approaches (DyDiT, DyFLUX, FlexiDiT) that fine-tune the generator's parameters, RAPID³ keeps the DiT entirely frozen and trains only three lightweight policy heads (~0.025% of total parameters), positioning itself as a parameter- and data-efficient middle ground between static heuristics and full fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving ~3× inference speedup on SD3 and FLUX with competitive quality using only 20K text prompts and ~1% of the GPU hours required by dynamic fine-tuning methods, making adaptive acceleration accessible for proprietary or very large generators. The adversarial discriminator is a non-trivial practical contribution: ablations show that without it, the policy exploits the ImageReward model (reward hacking), degrading real generation quality despite higher proxy scores. Open questions include whether the approach extends to video diffusion and editing models, whether prior knowledge (e.g., attention pattern statistics) could reduce or eliminate the RL training phase, and whether the learned policies transfer across different DiT architectures without retraining.
