## Problem

Autoregressive image generation has been limited by a fundamental trade-off: discrete VQ-based models suffer from quantization artifacts and information bottlenecks, while hybrid architectures (e.g., Transfusion, Bagel) that embed diffusion losses into LLMs compromise training efficiency through bidirectional attention and noisy-input duplication. No continuous autoregressive model had previously matched the visual fidelity of state-of-the-art diffusion models while retaining the simplicity and scalability of a standard causal LLM.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263180/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a unified sequence of discrete text tokens and continuous image tokens, where images are encoded by a Flux-VAE-based tokenizer into 16-channel latents at 8× spatial downsampling, then reshaped via 2×2 space-to-depth into 64-channel patch tokens (e.g., a 256×256 image becomes a 16×16 grid of 256 tokens). For text-to-image generation, the input is a text prompt followed by spatial-dimension metadata and <boi>/<eoi> delimiters; for image editing, a reference image's tokens are prepended before the instruction. Outputs are generated patch-by-patch autoregressively at resolutions from 256×256 to 512×512, with each patch produced by a 157M flow-matching head conditioned on the transformer's hidden state.

## Method

NextStep-1 pairs a 14B causal transformer (initialized from Qwen2.5-14B) with a lightweight 157M, 12-layer flow-matching head that predicts the velocity vector from noise to the next image patch, trained end-to-end with a weighted sum of cross-entropy (text) and MSE flow-matching (image) losses. The critical architectural contribution is the image tokenizer: a fine-tuned Flux VAE augmented with token-wise normalization (standardizing each channel to zero mean, unit variance per token) and stochastic Gaussian perturbation (σ-VAE-style noise with α ~ U[0, γ]), which enforces per-token statistical stability and a well-dispersed latent space. Ablations reveal that the transformer backbone performs the core conditional generative modeling while the flow-matching head acts as a near-interchangeable lightweight sampler, and that the tokenizer's noise-regularized latent space is essential for stable generation under strong classifier-free guidance—higher noise intensity during tokenizer training paradoxically improves final image quality despite increasing reconstruction loss.

## Results

On text-to-image generation, NextStep-1 (14B AR + 157M FM head) achieves 0.73 GenEval (with Self-CoT), 0.90 GenAI-Bench Basic, 0.74 GenAI-Bench Advanced, and 85.28 DPG-Bench, outperforming all prior autoregressive models and matching or exceeding several strong diffusion models (e.g., Flux.1-dev, SD3 Medium). On image editing, NextStep-1-Edit scores 6.58 on GEdit-Bench-EN (G_O) and 3.71 on ImgEdit-Bench, surpassing open-source baselines like OmniGen2 (6.41/3.44) and BAGEL (6.52/3.42) while remaining competitive with proprietary systems. The tokenizer achieves 30.60 PSNR and 0.89 SSIM on ImageNet-1K reconstruction, approaching Flux.1-dev's 31.64/0.91.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among autoregressive models, NextStep-1 clearly leads: it surpasses Emu3 by +0.19 GenEval (0.73 vs 0.54) and +4.68 DPG-Bench (85.28 vs 80.60), and outperforms Janus-Pro-7B on GenAI-Bench Basic (0.90 vs 0.86) and DPG-Bench (85.28 vs 84.19). Against diffusion models, NextStep-1 with Self-CoT matches or exceeds Flux.1-dev on GenAI-Bench (0.90 vs 0.86 Basic, 0.74 vs 0.65 Advanced) and DPG-Bench (85.28 vs 83.79), and beats SD3 Medium on DPG-Bench (85.28 vs 84.08), though it trails Qwen-Image (88.32 DPG) and BAGEL (0.88 GenEval) on some metrics. On image editing, NextStep-1-Edit outperforms all open-source baselines—beating OmniGen2 by +0.17 GEdit-Bench G_O and +0.27 ImgEdit-Bench, and BAGEL by +0.06 and +0.29 respectively—while remaining within ~0.9 of GPT-4o (7.49 GEdit G_O, 4.20 ImgEdit). The model also achieves the best autoregressive score on WISE (0.67 overall, 0.83 with rewrite), exceeding all prior AR methods by a wide margin (next best: BAGEL at 0.70/0.77†).

## Relation to Prior Work

NextStep-1 extends the continuous-token autoregressive line initiated by Li et al. (2024c) and Fluid (Fan et al., 2024), but departs sharply from both the discrete-VQ AR models (LlamaGen, Emu3, Janus-Pro) and the hybrid bidirectional-attention architectures (Transfusion, Bagel) that dominate the space. Its core architectural lineage is a standard causal LLM (Qwen2.5-14B) paired with a lightweight patch-wise flow-matching head, with the critical novelty residing in the image tokenizer: a Flux VAE fine-tuned with token-wise normalization and σ-VAE-style stochastic perturbation to produce a well-dispersed, per-token-stable latent space. The paper explicitly positions itself against the 'heavy diffusion head' paradigm (DreamLLM, Qwen-Image) by arguing that the transformer backbone, not the flow-matching head, performs the core generative modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a minimalist causal-AR architecture (14B transformer + 157M FM head) can rival top diffusion models on T2I benchmarks, validating the pure next-token-prediction paradigm for high-fidelity image synthesis without VQ, bidirectional attention, or noisy-input duplication. The counterintuitive finding that noise-regularized tokenizers with *worse* reconstruction yield *better* generation, combined with the near-total insensitivity to FM head size, provides actionable design principles for the field. Open challenges remain: sequential decoding latency at high resolutions, SFT instability on small datasets, and persistent artifacts in 16-channel latent spaces that the authors attribute to under-convergence and 1D positional encoding limitations.
