## Problem

While Large Audio Language Models (LALMs) are increasingly deployed in real-world applications, there is no unified framework or large-scale benchmark to systematically evaluate and compare jailbreak attacks against them. Existing research remains fragmented due to inconsistent code implementations and the high cost of TTS services, and no prior work has explored defense strategies specifically tailored to LALM jailbreak attacks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264257/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consumes 246 curated harmful text queries (deduplicated from AdvBench, JailbreakBench, MM-SafetyBench, and HarmBench via SimHash at threshold 0.9), their Google TTS audio counterparts, 11,070 adversarial text samples from 4 text-transferred attacks, and 229,857 adversarial audio samples from 4 audio-originated attacks, totaling 245,355 audio samples (>1,000 hours) and 11,316 text samples. Evaluation produces an attack success rate (ASR) determined by GPT-4o-2024-11-20 as judge on a 5-point safety scale (score ≥ 4 = jailbreak success), plus utility accuracy on a 455-question OpenBookQA audio subset with ~19-second average clip duration.

## Method

JALMBench is a modular, extensible benchmarking framework with standardized APIs and abstract classes that supports 12 LALMs (spanning both continuous feature extraction via Whisper and discrete tokenization via HuBERT/GLM-4-Tokenizer architectures), 8 attack methods (4 text-transferred: ICA, DAN, DeepInception, PAP; 4 audio-originated: SSJ, AMSE, BoN, AdvWave), and 5 defenses (3 prompt-level, 2 response-level). The evaluation protocol uses greedy decoding for deterministic outputs and validates judge reliability through three independent checks: repeatability under sampling (0.83% inconsistency), cross-model agreement (Krippendorff's α = 0.913 across GPT-4o, LLaMA-3.3-70B, and Qwen3-80B), and human verification (Cohen's κ = 0.97, 1.7% false-positive rate). A key architectural insight emerges from t-SNE visualization of final-layer hidden states: discrete audio tokenization with interleaved audio-text training (e.g., GLM-4-Voice) yields near-identical text and audio ASRs (18.7% vs. 19.5%), while continuous encoders (e.g., LLaMA-Omni) suffer fatal modality misalignment (9.6% text vs. 58.9% audio) unless explicitly corrected. The framework is distributed as a Docker image with CUDA support, allowing users to add new LALMs, attacks, or defenses by implementing a single abstract class.

## Results

Across 12 LALMs, non-adversarial harmful queries yield an average ASR of 21.5% in audio vs. 17.0% in text, indicating audio inputs are inherently more vulnerable. Among jailbreak attacks, AdvWave (audio-originated) achieves a near-perfect 96.2% average ASR, while PAP (text-transferred) reaches 90.4% in audio and 86.3% in text. Audio-originated attacks consistently outperform text-transferred ones, with BoN (88.9%) and AMSE (54.2%) also substantially exceeding their text counterparts. The best defenses (AdaShield at prompt-level, LLaMA-Guard at response-level) reduce average ASR by only 19.6 and 18.0 percentage points respectively, leaving average ASR at 34.1% and 35.7%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 8 attack methods, AdvWave dominates all others with 96.2% average ASR, outperforming BoN (88.9%), AMSE (54.2%), and SSJ (45.4%) in the audio-originated category, and PAP (90.4% audio) in the text-transferred category. GPT-4o-Audio and DiVA are the most robust models, maintaining ASR below 10% for AMSE and SSJ, yet still succumbing to AdvWave (91.1% and 97.6% respectively). LLaMA-Omni and VITA-1.0 are the most vulnerable, with LLaMA-Omni reaching 100% ASR under AdvWave and 99.6% under BoN. Among defenses, LLaMA-Guard (response-level) achieves the best safety-utility trade-off, reducing average ASR by 18.0 pp with minimal utility impact, while AdaShield (prompt-level) achieves the largest ASR reduction (19.6 pp) but degrades QA accuracy by up to 6.3%. All defenses fail to reduce AdvWave ASR below 75.9%, confirming that no current defense neutralizes the strongest attack.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies the fragmented landscape of LALM jailbreak research by consolidating both text-transferred attacks (ICA, DAN, DeepInception, PAP) originally designed for LLMs and audio-originated attacks (SSJ, AMSE, BoN, AdvWave) developed in recent LALM-specific work into a single modular evaluation framework. It departs from concurrent benchmarks (Jailbreak-AudioBench, Audio Jailbreak, MULTI-AUDIOJAIL) by being the first to systematically compare all eight attack families alongside five defense methods across 12 architecturally diverse LALMs under standardized APIs. The defense evaluation repurposes prompt-level strategies from VLM safety work (AdaShield, FigStep) and LLM output filters (LLaMA-Guard, Azure) to the audio setting, explicitly noting that no audio-native defense has been proposed to date.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that audio-originated attacks (especially AdvWave at 96.2% average ASR) are dramatically more effective than text-transferred ones, and that even well-aligned commercial models like GPT-4o-Audio (91.1% ASR under AdvWave) are nearly fully compromised by adversarial audio — challenging the assumption that text safety alignment transfers to the audio modality. The architectural finding that discrete tokenization (GLM-4-Voice: 18.7% text vs. 19.5% audio ASR) preserves cross-modal safety while continuous encoders can suffer catastrophic modality gaps (LLaMA-Omni: 9.6% text vs. 58.9% audio) provides concrete design guidance for future LALM builders. Open questions include multi-turn jailbreak dynamics, the effect of emotional prosody and fine-grained accent variation, quantization and reasoning-mode interactions with safety, and the development of truly audio-native defense mechanisms that the current ~11% improvement from general-purpose filters clearly does not address.
