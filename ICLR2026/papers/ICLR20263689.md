## Problem

Whether regret matching converges to Nash equilibria in potential games has been an open problem for two decades, and no theory existed to guarantee that RM+ converges to KKT points in general constrained optimization despite strong empirical performance. The paper also asks whether the seemingly innocuous difference between RM and RM+ (truncating negative regrets) can yield a fundamental performance gap.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263689/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The algorithm is parameter-free and scale-invariant: at each round it consumes only the gradient of the objective (or the payoff vector in the game setting) and the current strategy on each simplex, with no learning rate or other hyperparameters to tune. The internal state is the cumulative regret vector per simplex, which is updated by adding the instantaneous regret and (for RM+) truncating negative coordinates to zero. The output is the sequence of strategies, whose last iterate converges to an ε-KKT point (or ε-Nash equilibrium in potential games) after O(1/ε⁴) rounds, with the rate improving to O(1/ε²) when per-simplex regret remains bounded.

## Method

The analysis hinges on a recurring connection between accumulated regret and the KKT gap. For RM+, the key lemmas establish: (i) a one-step improvement in utility proportional to the squared best-response gap divided by the regret norm (Lemma 3.3), (ii) a conditional one-step improvement in the function value when the regret vector's ℓ2 norm exceeds a threshold (Lemma 3.7), and (iii) monotonic growth of the regret norm at a rate at least the squared KKT gap (Lemma 3.8), which prevents cycling. For RM, the one-step improvement is conditional on the best-response action having nonnegative regret (Lemma 3.6), and the paper constructs a two-player identical-interest game where this condition fails for exponentially many rounds, yielding the first worst-case exponential separation between RM and RM+. A discounted variant (DRM+) maintains bounded regret norm and achieves a faster T^{-1/2} convergence rate in potential games.

## Results

RM+ converges to an ε-KKT point of any L-smooth optimization problem over a product of simplices in O(1/ε⁴) iterations (Theorem 1.1), establishing it as a sound first-order optimizer. When per-simplex regret grows as at most T^α for α ∈ [0, 1/2], the rate improves to O(1/ε^{2/(1−α)}), yielding T^{-1/2} when regret is bounded (Theorem 1.2). Discounted RM+ with geometric discount factor 1−γ achieves a T^{-1/2} rate to Nash equilibria in potential games (Corollary 3.5). In stark contrast, RM requires m^Ω(m) iterations to reach an m^{-Θ(1)}-approximate Nash equilibrium in two-player m×m identical-interest games (Theorem 1.3), the first exponential worst-case separation between RM and RM+.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The central comparison is between RM and RM+ (the only algorithmic difference being truncation of negative regret coordinates to zero). RM+ provably converges to an ε-KKT point in O(1/ε⁴) rounds, while RM can take m^Ω(m) rounds to reach even a crude m^{-Θ(1)}-approximate Nash equilibrium in two-player identical-interest games—a special case of potential games. This exponential gap holds under both simultaneous and alternating updates, and even when players initialize uniformly at random (the most common practical initialization). Notably, RM still converges to an ε-CCE in O(1/ε²) rounds via its no-regret property, yet requires exp(Ω(1/ε)) rounds for ε-Nash convergence (Corollary 1.4), showing that convergence to coarse correlated equilibria is exponentially faster than convergence to Nash equilibria for RM. In zero-sum games, prior work (Farina et al., 2023) showed both RM and RM+ attain T^{-1/2} rates, so the exponential separation is specific to the potential-game / constrained-optimization setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis of regret matching from the well-studied two-player zero-sum setting to potential games and general constrained optimization over a product of simplices. It builds on the foundational RM framework of Hart & Mas-Colell (2000) and the RM+ variant of Tammelin (2014), the discounted regret matching mechanism of Brown & Sandholm (2019a), and the exponential lower-bound game construction of Panageas et al. (2023a) for fictitious play. The key conceptual departure is establishing a direct link between accumulated regret and KKT gap—two quantities that are 'entirely disparate' in general—thereby converting the no-regret guarantee into a convergence-to-KKT-points guarantee for the first time in nonconvex settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first theoretical guarantee that RM+ is a sound and efficient first-order optimizer for constrained optimization, validating strong empirical results and cementing RM+ in the optimization toolkit. The exponential separation between RM and RM+ (even in two-player identical-interest games) is the first worst-case distinction between these two algorithms and has practical implications for algorithm selection. Open questions include whether RM+ can achieve sub-√T regret in potential games (which would yield faster Nash convergence), whether results extend to stochastic or bandit feedback, and whether RM asymptotically converges under alternating updates.
