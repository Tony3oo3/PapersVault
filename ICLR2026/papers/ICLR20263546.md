## Problem

Existing video privacy-preservation methods anonymize at the pixel level, requiring full retraining of the utility model and producing task-specific anonymizers that cannot generalize across the diverse downstream tasks supported by modern video foundation models. This makes them impractical for large pretrained encoders trained on millions of videos with fixed training recipes, and leaves sensitive attributes (skin color, gender, clothing) exposed in stored or shared latent features.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 16-frame video clips at 224×224 resolution, which are passed through a frozen video encoder (I3D, VideoMAE, V-JEPA, InternVideo, etc.) to produce a single global clip-level embedding (the [CLS] token for transformers or the spatiotemporal average-pooled feature for CNNs). The AAM then transforms this embedding into an anonymized latent vector of the same dimensionality, which feeds into task-specific classifier heads for action recognition, temporal action detection, or anomaly detection. For the privacy objective, two random frames from each clip are tiled into static clips and encoded separately to form contrastive pairs.

## Method

SPLAVU introduces a lightweight Anonymizing Adapter Module (AAM), a 3-layer multi-head self-attention transformer encoder (8 heads) initialized as an identity function and applied to the frozen encoder's clip-level embedding. Training jointly optimizes three objectives in a GAN-style adversarial loop: (1) a clip-level self-supervised privacy loss that maximizes the NT-Xent contrastive distance between two static-clip embeddings to destroy shared spatial mutual information without requiring private attribute labels, (2) a co-training utility loss combining cross-entropy (AR), TriDet (TAD), and MGFN (AD) losses to preserve task performance, and (3) a latent consistency L2 loss that penalizes large deviations from the original encoder features, preventing overfitting to seen utility tasks and enabling generalization to unseen tasks. The AAM contains only ~40M trainable parameters (vs. 100–1000M for prior pixel-level methods), enabling plug-and-play deployment on any frozen video backbone with minimal compute.

## Results

On I3D, SPLAVU achieves a 35.5% reduction in VISPR privacy cMAP (63.64 → 41.07) while maintaining near-baseline utility: Kinetics400 top-1 accuracy drops only 0.9% (62.67 → 62.11), UCF101 top-1 drops 0.2% (90.30 → 90.14), THUMOS14 mAP drops 1.5% (25.29 → 24.92), and UCF-Crime AUC drops 2.6% (77.68 → 75.69). Scaling to VideoMAE-B yields a 28.9% privacy reduction (70.47 → 49.92) with utility losses under 1% across all tasks. On VJEPA-H, privacy cMAP drops 29.0% (72.44 → 51.42) with utility degradation of at most 1.1%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SPLAVU outperforms all three prior privacy-preserving baselines on every metric simultaneously. On privacy (VISPR cMAP), SPLAVU achieves 41.07, substantially below VITA (54.72), SPAct (55.60), and TeD-SPAD (52.30), representing a 12–14 point absolute improvement. On utility, the gap is even more dramatic: SPLAVU retains 90.14% UCF101 top-1 accuracy versus 75.77–76.64% for the baselines (a ~14-point gap), and 24.92 THUMOS14 mAP versus 16.10–17.27% (an ~8-point gap). The baselines were designed for single-task anonymization (SPAct for action recognition, TeD-SPAD for anomaly detection), so their utility degrades severely on tasks they were not trained for, whereas SPLAVU's latent-space approach generalizes uniformly. Even the simplest input-level methods (downsampling, blurring, blackening) fail to match SPLAVU's privacy-utility tradeoff, confirming that latent-space anonymization is a fundamentally more effective formulation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-supervised privacy preservation paradigm introduced by SPAct (Dave et al., CVPR 2022) and TeD-SPAD (Fioresi et al., ICCV 2023) from pixel-level input anonymization to latent-space feature anonymization, using the SimCLR NT-Xent contrastive loss (Chen et al., ICML 2020) as the core privacy objective. The key conceptual departure is replacing the need to retrain the entire utility model on transformed inputs with a lightweight Anonymizing Adapter Module (AAM) applied to frozen video foundation model encoders, shifting the problem from task-specific pixel manipulation to a plug-and-play latent transformation. The clip-level (temporal) formulation of the privacy objective, combined with a novel latent consistency loss for cross-task generalization, distinguishes this work from all prior pixel-level methods that were limited to single downstream tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to achieve generalized privacy preservation across multiple video understanding tasks (action recognition, temporal action detection, anomaly detection) without task-specific fine-tuning, making it directly applicable to the growing ecosystem of video foundation models where features are commonly stored and shared. The data efficiency (training on HMDB51 suffices) and low parameter count (~40M vs. 1B+ for full models) make it practical for real-world deployment. However, the framework is limited to global-embedding tasks and cannot handle dense tasks like segmentation or captioning, is not robust to white-box inversion attacks, and cannot protect against threats where raw video is transmitted before feature extraction. The incidental finding that self-supervised privacy preservation mitigates gender bias without explicit debiasing objectives opens an interesting but underexplored connection between privacy and fairness in video understanding.
