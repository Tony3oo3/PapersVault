## Problem

Existing LVLMs for long video reasoning rely on uniform frame sampling, processing hundreds of largely irrelevant frames, and conduct all subsequent reasoning purely in text, losing multimodal perceptual access after the initial input. This paradigm is both computationally wasteful and degrades accuracy on visually intensive tasks, while video agents that can interact with video content depend on predefined workflows and are not end-to-end learnable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262539/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a long video (arbitrary duration) and a text query, and produces a structured multi-turn trajectory of thought-action-observation triplets in the format  followed by <action>...</action>. Three actions are available: 'choose frames between START and END' (retrieves 8 or 12 frames from a temporal segment), 'get frame number at time MM:SS' (converts a timestamp to a frame index), and 'output answer: OPTION' (terminal). The final output is a text answer, but the intermediate trajectory constitutes a multimodal chain of thought interleaving textual reasoning with newly retrieved visual frames, using on average only ~20 frames versus 32–512 for baselines.

## Method

FrameThinker (built on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) replaces passive uniform sampling with an active, iterative reasoning loop where the model alternates between generating a textual thought and executing a frame-selection action, progressively 'zooming in' on relevant temporal segments. Training proceeds in two phases: a brief SFT stage (2,392 curated examples) instills the action syntax via standard cross-entropy loss on thought and action tokens, followed by an RL stage (28k examples) using GRPO to optimize a strategic decision-making policy. A key contribution is a comprehensive reward design study: format rewards are omitted (they suppress action exploration), action rewards are made conditional on final-answer correctness (unconditional rewards cause mode collapse), and the 'get frame number' action receives a higher reward weight than 'choose frames' because it provides objectively verifiable information. A Cognitive Consistency Verification (CCV) module filters trajectories during training and inference, zeroing the reward for logically inconsistent thought-action pairs (e.g., requesting frames from an interval that contradicts the stated reasoning) to enforce interpretability and prevent reward hacking.

## Results

On LongVideo-Reason, FrameThinker achieves 76.1% accuracy using only 20.6 frames on average, surpassing LongVILA-R1 (72.0%) which requires 512 frames (20× more). On Video-Holmes, it reaches 46.8% accuracy with 15.9 frames, outperforming GPT-4o (42.0% with 32 frames) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (45.0%). Across all six benchmarks, FrameThinker achieves an average accuracy of 53.2%, a +10.4% improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline, while using 25–50% fewer frames. Wall-clock latency on LongVideo-Reason is just 7.7 seconds per task, faster than Video-R1 (8.6s) and dramatically faster than LongVILA-R1 (38.1s at 256 frames, OOM at 512).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FrameThinker (7B) outperforms all open-source baselines across every benchmark. On LongVideo-Reason, it beats LongVILA-R1 (72.0%, 512 frames) by +4.1% while using 20× fewer frames, and Video-R1 (68.1%, 32 frames) by +8.0%. On Video-Holmes, it surpasses GPT-4o (42.0%) by +4.8% and Gemini-2.5-Pro (45.0%) by +1.8%, while using fewer frames than both. Against the primary baseline Qwen2.5-VL-7B (27.8% on Video-Holmes, 64.1% on LongVideo-Reason), FrameThinker gains +19.0 and +12.0 points respectively. On the four long-video comprehension benchmarks, FrameThinker beats all open-source methods (Video-R1, VideoChat-R1, LongVA, VITA-1.5-7B, PLLaVA, ShareGPT4Video) but trails closed-source GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5-Pro on LongVideoBench (52.9 vs 58.5), MLVU (59.1 vs 64.6), VideoMME-Long (47.6 vs 65.3), and LVBench (36.6 vs 48.9), indicating that the active frame-selection strategy is most impactful on reasoning-intensive tasks rather than pure comprehension.

## Relation to Prior Work

FrameThinker sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) RLVR-based video reasoning models (Video-R1, VideoChat-R1, LongVILA-R1) that rely on passive uniform frame sampling, (2) video agents (VideoAgent, VideoTree) that use tools but with fixed, non-learnable workflows, and (3) the emerging 'thinking with images' paradigm (OpenAI, DeepEyes, OpenThinkImg) that enables iterative visual consultation. The key departure is making active, multi-turn frame selection an end-to-end learnable policy via GRPO, rather than a hand-designed workflow, and introducing the Cognitive Consistency Verification module to enforce logical coherence between thought and action. The training recipe builds directly on GRPO from DeepSeekMath/DeepSeek-R1, adapted to the multi-turn interactive setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 20× frame reduction (20.6 vs. 512) while simultaneously improving accuracy on LongVideo-Reason is practically significant for deployment, and the wall-clock latency result (7.7s vs. OOM) underscores real-world relevance. The systematic reward design ablations—showing that format rewards suppress action exploration, unconditional action rewards cause mode collapse, and multi-turn count rewards destabilize training—provide broadly useful guidance for the RLVR community beyond video. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models or more complex action spaces, whether the rule-based CCV generalizes to open-ended reasoning, and whether the premature-stopping failure mode can be addressed without sacrificing efficiency.
