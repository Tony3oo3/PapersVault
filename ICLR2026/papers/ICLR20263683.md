## Problem

When distilling pretrained softmax attention Transformers into hybrid architectures that interleave softmax and linear attention layers, the choice of which layers to retain as full softmax is critical for preserving long-context in-context recall. Existing strategies—uniform interleaving at a fixed ratio or task-specific diagnostic probes—prove suboptimal because they do not account for the model-intrinsic importance of each layer under the distillation objective. This paper asks: can a simple, training-derived signal identify the most impactful softmax layers without expensive search or specialized benchmarks?

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained decoder-only Transformer checkpoint (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct or Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, with native depth L=36 or L=28) and a generic text mixture (DCLM) as input. It produces a hybrid student model with K softmax attention layers and L−K gated DeltaNet (or GLA) linear attention layers at a target ratio (1:8, 1:3, 1:2, or 1:1), along with per-layer KL-based importance scores. The selection pass requires only 5–6B tokens total (100M for hidden-state alignment + 600M for KL matching per one-swap evaluation), far less than the 50B tokens needed by search-based alternatives like PostNAS.

## Method

The core contribution is a KL-guided greedy-addition layer selection strategy. First, the teacher is distilled into an all-linear student using the RADLADS two-stage pipeline (per-layer hidden-state L2 alignment, then temperature-scaled KL distribution matching). Then, for each layer ℓ, a one-swap variant is constructed by restoring only that layer to softmax; a brief re-distillation is run and the resulting teacher-student KL divergence is recorded as the layer's importance score I(ℓ). The top-K layers by I(ℓ) are selected (greedy addition from the all-linear base), and a final two-stage distillation trains the resulting hybrid model. The key insight is that the distillation KL loss itself is a natural, model-intrinsic proxy for layer criticality: layers whose restoration most reduces KL are precisely those most needed for long-context recall. Ablations confirm that the KL-based (Stage-2) signal dramatically outperforms the MSE-based (Stage-1) signal, and that greedy addition outperforms greedy removal, yielding consistent gains of +0.12 to +0.22 over the strongest baselines at low softmax budgets.

## Results

On RULER (the primary long-context recall benchmark), GA-S2 (the proposed KL-guided greedy addition selector) achieves 0.8631 on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and 0.7539 on Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct at a 25% softmax budget, compared to teacher scores of 0.9174 and 0.8934 respectively. At the more challenging 12.5% budget, GA-S2 reaches 0.6617 (Qwen) and 0.5731 (Llama), outperforming the strongest task-guided baseline AR (0.5417 / 0.3684) by +0.12 and +0.20. The method also generalizes across model scales: on Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B at 25% softmax, GA-S2 scores 0.5408 and 0.8584, beating the best baseline SMART by +0.031 and +0.043.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GA-S2 consistently and substantially outperforms all baselines across both teacher models and all softmax budgets on RULER. The strongest baseline, SMART (Yang et al., 2025), trails GA-S2 by +0.127 (Llama) and +0.223 (Qwen) at 25% softmax; the gap widens further at 12.5% where SMART scores only 0.4476 and 0.4089. Task-guided selectors (AR, AR-MH) are competitive at higher budgets (e.g., AR reaches 0.8347 on Llama at 50%) but collapse at low budgets (AR: 0.3684 on Llama at 12.5%), whereas GA-S2 maintains a clear advantage throughout. Model-signal baselines (ACT-MSE, LM-PPL) are consistently the weakest, scoring below 0.51 on both models at 25%. Notably, GA-S2 achieves these gains using only 5–6B training tokens for the selection pass, compared to PostNAS (Gu et al., 2025) which requires 50B tokens for its SuperNet + beam-search procedure.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the RADLADS distillation pipeline (Goldstein et al., 2025) for the two-stage process of hidden-state alignment followed by KL-based distribution matching, and on the hybrid attention architecture concept popularized by Jamba, MiniMax-01, and Falcon-H1. Its key departure is in the layer selection sub-problem: rather than uniform interleaving (Wang et al., 2024), task-guided heuristics (Chaudhry et al., 2025), or expensive search procedures like PostNAS (Gu et al., 2025), it uses a simple one-swap protocol where each layer's marginal KL reduction from an all-linear baseline serves as its importance score. This positions the work as a lightweight, model-intrinsic alternative to both heuristic and search-based selection strategies for the distillation setting specifically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, low-cost recipe (5–6B tokens vs. 50B for PostNAS) for converting existing pretrained LLMs into efficient hybrid architectures that preserve long-context recall, which is the primary bottleneck for subquadratic models in test-time compute scaling. The finding that selection stabilizes after only 25–40% of the training budget suggests further efficiency gains are possible. Open questions include extending selection to head-level granularity, finding even cheaper proxies (e.g., from teacher activations alone), and whether the architecture-dependent selection behavior (GDN as a 'probe' transferring to GLA) generalizes to other linear attention variants.
