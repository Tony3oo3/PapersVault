## Problem

Datasets with intrinsic multiscale structure are often described by non-hierarchical sequences of partitions (e.g., temporal community detection, topic modelling, diffusion-based clustering), yet existing analytical tools are limited: ultrametrics and dendrograms only apply to hierarchical sequences, and pairwise partition comparison measures (ARI, VI, CE, MOD) cannot capture higher-order cluster inconsistencies or memory effects across scales. The paper asks how to characterise and compare such non-hierarchical multiscale partition sequences in an integrated, order-sensitive manner.

## Input / Output

The method takes a piecewise-constant sequence of partitions θ(t) over a discrete scale parameter with M change points t₁ < … < t_M, and produces a triangular 2D feature map of Hilbert functions HF_k(s,t) for s ≤ t, where each entry encodes topological information about the interval [s,t]. Two scalar summary measures are also derived: the average 0-conflict c̄₀(θ) (normalised to [0,1]) and the average 1-conflict c̄₁(θ) (unnormalised). A computationally advantageous nerve-based variant reduces the number of simplices from O(N·M) to O(M²) when the number of scales M is smaller than the largest cluster size, as demonstrated on the mice dataset (260 vs. 116,700 simplices).

## Method

The core construction is the Multiscale Clustering Bifiltration (MCbiF), a 2-parameter filtration of abstract simplicial complexes where K^{s,t} = ⋃_{s≤r≤t} ⋃_{C∈θ(r)} ΔC, i.e., the union of solid simplices corresponding to all clusters appearing in partitions between scales s and t. The key theoretical result is that the MCbiF is a complete invariant of the partition sequence and its multiparameter persistent homology yields a pointwise finite-dimensional, finitely presented, and block-decomposable persistence module, guaranteeing algebraic stability of the Hilbert functions. At dimension 0, HF₀(s,t) counts connected components and detects 0-conflicts (absence of a maximum in the refinement subposet θ([s,t])); at dimension 1, HF₁(s,t) counts non-bounding 1-cycles and detects 1-conflicts (higher-order cluster assignment inconsistencies that no single partition can resolve). An equivalent nerve-based construction reinterprets the MCbiF as a higher-order extension of the Sankey diagram, where simplices represent clusters and their intersections rather than elements and their equivalences, and the Sankey diagram itself is recovered as a 1-dimensional subcomplex via a zigzag subfiltration.

## Results

On the regression task (predicting minimal Sankey crossing number), the combined HF₀ & HF₁ features achieve R² = 0.544 (N=5, CNN) and R² = 0.516 (N=10, LR), outperforming the best baseline (CE: R² = 0.492 for N=5, R² = 0.458 for N=10) and the GCN on Sankey graphs (R² = 0.416 for N=5, R² = 0.229 for N=10). On the classification task (detecting order-preserving sequences with N=500, M=30), HF₁ alone achieves 0.97 test accuracy, while all baselines (CE, ARI, MOD) and raw label encoding score at or below chance level (0.46–0.53). The average 1-conflict c̄₁ shows the highest Pearson correlation (r = 0.47) with the crossing number target, consistent with the theoretical link in Corollary 19.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares MCbiF Hilbert functions against three pairwise partition-comparison baselines: Conditional Entropy (CE, Meilä 2003), Adjusted Rand Index (ARI, Hubert & Arabie 1985), and Maximum Overlap Distance (MOD, Peixoto 2021), as well as representation-learning approaches (raw label encoding and GCN on Sankey graphs). On the regression task, the combined HF₀ & HF₁ features (R² = 0.544 for N=5; R² = 0.516 for N=10) significantly outperform the best baseline CE (R² = 0.492 for N=5; R² = 0.458 for N=10) and the GCN on Sankey graphs (R² = 0.416 for N=5; R² = 0.229 for N=10), with p < 0.0001 (Wilcoxon test). ARI and MOD are consistently weaker than CE across all model architectures. On the classification task, HF₁ achieves 0.97 accuracy, dramatically outperforming CE (0.50), ARI (0.49), MOD (0.46), and raw label encoding (0.53), all of which are at or below chance level. The key advantage of MCbiF over all baselines is its ability to capture higher-order (multi-scale) cluster inconsistencies that pairwise measures fundamentally cannot detect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 1-parameter Multiscale Clustering Filtration (MCF) of Schindler & Barahona (2025) to a 2-parameter bifiltration (MCbiF) that captures both the starting scale and the lag, making it a complete invariant of non-hierarchical sequences of partitions. It departs from the ultrametric/dendrogram framework of Carlsson & Mémoli (2010), which only applies to hierarchical sequences, and from pairwise partition comparison measures (ARI, VI, CE, MOD) that cannot detect higher-order cluster inconsistencies across more than two scales. The nerve-based construction positions the MCbiF as a higher-order generalization of Sankey diagrams, and the block-decomposability proof leverages the exactness criterion of Cochoy & Oudot (2020) for multiparameter persistence modules.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The MCbiF provides the first complete topological invariant for non-hierarchical multiscale clusterings, filling a gap where prior tools (ultrametrics, pairwise measures) are fundamentally inapplicable. The Hilbert functions serve as interpretable, stable feature maps that outperform both hand-crafted baselines and learned representations, with practical implications for Sankey diagram layout optimization and temporal community detection. Key limitations include the restriction to dimensions 0 and 1, the use of Hilbert functions rather than richer invariants (barcodes, landscapes), and the fact that the main empirical validation is on synthetic data. Open questions include whether higher-dimensional homology captures qualitatively new structure, and how the method scales to very large numbers of elements or scales.
