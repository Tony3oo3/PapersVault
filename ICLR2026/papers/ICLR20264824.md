## Problem

TabPFN, a state-of-the-art tabular foundation model that generates predictions via in-context learning in a single forward pass, performs poorly for synthetic data generation when variables are only weakly associated with one another, because the context examples provide little predictive signal for the target. Direct factorization strategies (joint or full-conditional) degrade sharply as correlation strength decreases, and the joint factorization is additionally sensitive to variable ordering. This paper identifies this limitation and proposes a principled fix that works with the existing TabPFN model without fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real tabular dataset (numeric, categorical, or mixed) and splits it into two equal subsets; each subset is synthesized using the other as in-context context, and the two synthetic halves are concatenated to form the full synthetic copy. For each variable, a MIAV is constructed by rank-matching a uniform random noise vector to that variable's values (ties broken at random for categorical variables). The output is a synthetic tabular dataset of the same dimensions and variable types as the input, with integer variables rounded post-hoc. The approach is constrained by the underlying PFN model's limits (e.g., TabPFNv2 handles up to 10,000 rows and 500 columns), though MIAV's single-feature-per-variable design sidesteps the column limit.

## Method

For each variable X_j, the MIAV M_j is constructed by drawing a random noise vector, sorting it, and reordering its entries according to the ranks of X_j, producing a variable with identical rank structure to X_j. Two key theoretical properties are established: (i) the conditional mutual information I(X_j; Y | M_j) = 0 for any other variable Y, and (ii) the conditional entropy H(X_j | M_j) = 0, meaning M_j contains maximal non-parametric information about X_j. These properties imply that the augmented posterior predictive distribution factorizes as P(X^ts | X^tr, M^ts, M^tr) = ∏_j P(X_j^ts | M_j^ts, M_j^tr, X_j^tr), so each variable is generated by TabPFN using only its own MIAV as the single context feature rather than all other variables. This yields three practical advantages: maximal computational efficiency (quadratic scaling in n only, not p), invariance to variable ordering, and robustness to weak or absent associations, since the MIAV always provides a perfect rank-based signal regardless of inter-variable correlation strength.

## Results

Across 43 real-world datasets (36 from OpenML-CC18, 7 additional) and 5 simulated correlation settings, MIAV consistently outperforms the direct TabPFN strategies (JF and FC) on all fidelity metrics (KS, L2D, DT) while maintaining competitive privacy. On the 7-dataset baseline comparison, MIAV surpasses all generators except DDPM on the detection test (DT) metric, and outperforms SMOTE on all three privacy metrics (DCR, SDBRL, SSDID). The fidelity-privacy tradeoff pattern is consistent: JF and FC produce more private synthetic data than MIAV but at the cost of substantially lower fidelity, while SMOTE achieves the best fidelity at the expense of weaker privacy protection.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against SMOTE, MIAV trades a slight fidelity disadvantage (SMOTE achieves marginally lower KS, L2D, and DT values) for clear privacy gains: MIAV outperforms SMOTE on DCR (higher is better), SDBRL, and SSDID (lower is better). Against the direct TabPFN strategies JF and FC, MIAV wins decisively on fidelity across all experiments and correlation strengths, while JF/FC produce more private data but with substantially degraded marginal distributions and association structure. In the 7-dataset comparison with deep learning baselines, MIAV surpasses ARF, TVAE, CTGAN, and Bayesian networks on all fidelity metrics; DDPM is the only generator that beats MIAV on one metric (DT), though MIAV still leads on KS and L2D. The MIAV strategy also generalizes to TabICL, where MIAV-TabICL achieves performance very similar to MIAV-TabPFN and both outperform JF and FC baselines on the 8 categorical datasets tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on TabPFN (Hollmann et al., 2025) and TabICL (Qu et al., 2025) as in-context-learning tabular foundation models, and on the prior-data fitted network (PFN) framework that gives them their Bayesian interpretation. The MIAV construction is inspired by the non-parametric rank-matching ideas in Chaibub Neto (2025, TabSDS), but repurposes them as auxiliary variables to fix a specific failure mode of foundation-model-based synthetic data generation: the inability of in-context learning to capture marginal distributions when variables are weakly associated. The paper departs from the direct joint-factorization and full-conditional strategies proposed in Hollmann et al. (2025) by showing those are theoretically improper factorizations and empirically degrade under weak correlations, and positions MIAV as a proper, order-invariant, single-feature-per-variable alternative that also sidesteps the column-count limits of the underlying PFN model.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MIAV provides a clean, theoretically grounded (Theorem 1: zero conditional mutual information and zero conditional entropy) and computationally efficient recipe for turning any PFN-based tabular foundation model into a synthetic data generator that handles weakly associated variables—settings where direct ICL-based generation silently fails. The approach is generalizable (demonstrated on both TabPFN and TabICL) and inherits only the row/column limits of the base model, which are expected to relax as these models evolve. Key open questions include whether the strategy extends to non-Bayesian tabular foundation models, whether the noisy-MIAV variant (Appendix J) can be tuned to achieve meaningful privacy guarantees without excessive fidelity loss, and whether the approach remains competitive as future PFN models (e.g., TabPFN-2.5, 50K rows) close the gap with bespoke generators on larger datasets.
