## Problem

Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG), the standard technique for improving conditional diffusion generation, systematically produces suboptimal predictions that shift modes away from the ground truth distribution, causing semantic incoherence and loss of fine details. Existing weak-model guidance approaches (e.g., Autoguidance) correct this by steering toward a degraded model, but require either separately trained weak models or task-specific hand-crafted architectural modifications, making them impractical for large-scale pretrained diffusion transformers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264560/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates entirely at inference time on a frozen, pretrained diffusion transformer (DiT-based models such as SD3, SD3.5, Wan-1.3B/14B, SiT-XL, and Flux), taking a text prompt or class label plus initial Gaussian noise as input and producing a generated image (256×256 for ImageNet, 1024×1024 for T2I) or video (for T2V). Each denoising timestep requires one additional forward pass through a stochastically masked sub-network, adding ~40% runtime and FLOPs overhead while keeping peak GPU memory identical to standard CFG since the two passes execute sequentially.

## Method

S²-Guidance augments the standard CFG formula with a self-guidance correction term: at each timestep, a binary mask is sampled to stochastically drop ~10% of the transformer blocks (excluding structurally critical ones), producing a sub-network prediction that is subtracted from the full-model conditional prediction, scaled by a self-guidance weight ω. The key insight is that these stochastic sub-networks exhibit guidance behavior analogous to externally trained weak models, and a single block-drop per timestep is sufficient because it is an unbiased Monte Carlo estimator of the same theoretical target as the multi-sample Naive variant, with the iterative denoising process smoothing out per-step variance. The block-dropping is applied within the central 80% of noise levels, and the stochastic mask is resampled independently at each timestep, providing dynamic diversity throughout the trajectory rather than a fixed weak model affecting the entire process.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 (SiT-XL), S²-Guidance achieves the best IS of 259.12 and lowest FID of 2.03, surpassing CFG (258.09 / 2.15) and CFG-Zero (258.87 / 2.10). On T2I (SD3), it attains the highest HPSv2.1 average of 31.09% and the best T2I-CompBench Color score of 59.63%, outperforming all five baselines. On T2V (Wan-1.3B, VBench), S²-Guidance achieves the highest Total Score of 80.93 and Quality Score of 84.74, edging out CFG-Zero (80.71 / 84.51). The method incurs ~40% additional runtime (40.2s vs 29.2s) but with unchanged peak GPU memory (~33.8 GB).

## Baselines & Comparisons

S²-Guidance consistently outperforms all seven baselines across the three task settings. Against CFG, it improves HPSv2.1 average by +0.61 (SD3) and FID by −0.12 (ImageNet), while on VBench it gains +0.64 Total Score over CFG (80.93 vs 80.29). CFG-Zero is the closest competitor, achieving 30.78 HPSv2.1 and 2.10 FID, but S²-Guidance still leads by +0.31 on HPSv2.1 and −0.07 on FID. SEG shows strong T2I-CompBench Texture (57.17) but lags significantly on HPSv2.1 (30.39) and Qalign (4.33), indicating a quality–aesthetic trade-off that S²-Guidance avoids. APG and CFG++ underperform substantially, with APG dropping to 29.65 HPSv2.1 and 70.83 VBench Total Score, suggesting these methods struggle with the flow-based architectures used here. On ImageNet, Autoguidance (ADG) achieves FID 2.37 vs S²-Guidance's 2.03, confirming that the stochastic self-guidance signal is more effective than a fixed weak-model correction. STG (spatiotemporal skip guidance) trails by 2.15 VBench Total Score points (78.78 vs 80.93), highlighting the advantage of dynamic per-timestep block-dropping over fixed architectural shortcuts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classifier-free guidance (CFG) framework by incorporating a self-guidance correction term derived from stochastic sub-networks of the model itself. It builds directly on Autoguidance (Karras et al., 2024), which showed that a deliberately degraded 'weak model' can correct CFG's distributional distortions, but replaces the externally trained weak model with randomly dropped transformer blocks—a generalization of dropout-as-Bayesian-approximation (Gal & Ghahramani, 2016) from neuron-level to block-level. Unlike prior weak-model guidance methods (SEG, attention-based perturbations, task-specific modifications) that require hand-crafted architectural changes or additional training, S²-Guidance is entirely training-free and architecture-agnostic, requiring only a stochastic mask over existing blocks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a simple, plug-and-play improvement to any diffusion transformer pipeline with no retraining, making it immediately deployable across the growing ecosystem of DiT-based models (SD3, Wan, Flux, SiT). The ~40% per-step computational overhead is a real limitation, though the performance-efficiency analysis (20 steps of S²-Guidance outperforming 60 steps of CFG) partially mitigates this concern. Open questions include whether the empirically determined ~10% drop ratio and central-80% noise-level application window generalize to non-DiT architectures (e.g., U-Net-based models), whether the stochastic element introduces any instability at very large scale, and whether the Bayesian derivation can be tightened to predict optimal drop ratios rather than relying on empirical tuning.
