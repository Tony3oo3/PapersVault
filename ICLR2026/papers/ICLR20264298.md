## Problem

Real-time traffic accident anticipation on edge devices remains challenging because existing methods rely on heavy multi-stage pipelines (Faster R-CNN + VGG16) or large spatio-temporal Transformers that are computationally prohibitive for embedded deployment. A key unaddressed gap is how to impart predictive temporal dynamics to a lightweight student without requiring a video-pretrained teacher, which is impractical for rare-event tasks like accidents with limited training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264298/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 32-frame dashcam video clips at 224×224 resolution (8-frame clips during pre-training), processed with half-overlap windows and max-pooling aggregation at inference. It outputs a per-frame accident confidence score indicating the likelihood of an imminent incident. At deployment on the Jetson Orin Nano, only the vision encoder and classifier head are active since text embeddings are pre-computed and cached, yielding 80 FPS with ~0.4 s latency on 30 FPS video streams.

## Method

The student combines RepMixer spatial encoding (stages 1–3) with a window-based spatio-temporal RWKV block (stage 4) that replaces quadratic self-attention with localized recurrence over K non-overlapping spatial windows, achieving linear-time temporal modeling with learnable decay parameters. The core training insight is temporally shifted distillation: the student's features at frame t are supervised to match a frozen MobileCLIP teacher's features at frame t+1, injecting predictive temporal signal without any video-pretrained teacher. A masked memory strategy introduces a binary mask into the RWKV recurrence during pre-training, forcing the model to reconstruct occluded tokens from prior hidden states and strengthening robustness under partial observability. Training proceeds in two stages—pre-training with spatial/temporal distillation plus CLIP-style contrastive loss on MM-AU and Nexar, followed by end-to-end fine-tuning with an exponentially weighted anticipation loss on DAD and CCD—resulting in a 26 M-parameter model that is 3–7× smaller than leading baselines while achieving superior mAP and mTTA.

## Results

On the DAD benchmark, the proposed model achieves 75.3% mAP with a mean time-to-accident (mTTA) of 4.04 s using only 26M parameters, outperforming all prior methods in the accuracy–anticipation trade-off. On CCD, it reaches 99.9% mAP and 4.95 s mTTA, matching MASTTA while using 3.8× fewer parameters. From a highest-mAP perspective (Table 8), the model attains 79.6% mAP with 3.41 s mTTA, trailing CCAF-Net (81.3%) by 1.7% but surpassing MASTTA (80.8%) in mTTA by 0.09 s, all at a fraction of the parameter count. The model runs at 80 FPS on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano with ~0.4 s latency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On DAD, the proposed method (75.3% mAP, 4.04 s mTTA, 26M params) surpasses CCAF-Net (71.8% mAP, 4.15 s mTTA, 191M) in mAP by 3.5% while using 7.3× fewer parameters, and outperforms MASTTA (70.2% mAP, 3.96 s mTTA, 99M) by +5.1% mAP and +0.08 s mTTA at 3.8× smaller size. Against graph-based and attention-based methods (DAA-GNN, DSTA, GSC, GCRNN), the gap is even larger: DAA-GNN achieves only 1.59 s mTTA despite 70.6% mAP, and GCRNN reaches 3.53 s mTTA at 53.7% mAP with 275M parameters. On CCD, the model ties MASTTA at 99.9% mAP and 4.95 s mTTA while being 3.8× smaller, and exceeds CCAF-Net by 6.0% mAP (99.9% vs. 93.9%). From the highest-mAP perspective (Table 8), CCAF-Net (81.3%) and MASTTA (80.8%) slightly outperform the proposed 79.6% mAP, but the proposed model achieves a better mTTA than MASTTA (3.41 s vs. 3.32 s) and trails CCAF-Net by only 0.34 s, all with 7.3× and 3.8× fewer parameters respectively. Pure video-understanding architectures (I3D, R(2+1)D, TSM, VideoSwin, UniFormer V2) all fall below 70% mAP on DAD, confirming that generic video models are poorly suited to the narrow temporal window of accident anticipation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on two key architectural lineages: the RWKV recurrent formulation (Peng et al., 2023), which it adapts into a window-based spatio-temporal block with masking-aware recurrence, and the MobileCLIP/RepMixer backbone (Vasu et al., 2024), which provides both the student's spatial encoder and the frozen image-based teacher. It departs from the dominant accident-anticipation paradigm of object-centric multi-stage pipelines (Faster R-CNN + VGG16 feature extraction, as in DSA, GCRNN, DSTA, CCAF-Net) by proposing a compact end-to-end model operating directly on raw RGB frames. The temporally shifted distillation strategy—aligning a student's current-frame features with a frozen image-only teacher's *future*-frame features—is a conceptual departure from standard knowledge distillation, which typically requires a temporally aware teacher or video pre-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in demonstrating that a 26M-parameter model can match or exceed 100–200M-parameter baselines on dashcam accident anticipation while running at 80 FPS on a Jetson Orin Nano, making real-time in-vehicle deployment feasible. The finding that a frozen image-only teacher outperforms a video-based teacher (V-JEPA2) for this task is conceptually interesting but partially confounded by architectural mismatch in spatial tokenization, so the broader claim that 'video teachers are unnecessary' remains under-tested. Open questions include generalization beyond dashcam to other driving perspectives (helmet-mounted, aerial), robustness to the false-positive patterns the authors themselves identify (dense traffic, low-light, camera shake), and whether the temporally shifted distillation principle transfers to other predictive video tasks.
