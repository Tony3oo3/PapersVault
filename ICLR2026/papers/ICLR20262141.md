## Problem

Deep transformers suffer from two distinct initialization failure modes—rank collapse, where all tokens map to identical representations, and entropy collapse, where attention saturates on a frozen subset of tokens—but prior work has analyzed them separately using approximations (uniform attention or annealed softmax) that fail in key regimes. No asymptotically exact, down-to-the-constant prescription existed for jointly choosing initialization hyperparameters that avoid both pathologies simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The theory takes a transformer architecture specification (depth, width, residual strengths α_SA and α_MLP, MLP weight variances) and a query/key initialization scale β, and iteratively tracks two scalar order parameters—average squared token norm q and average pairwise overlap p—through each block. It produces trainability diagrams in the (β, α_SA) plane, per-layer cosine similarity curves, and closed-form critical thresholds β_c(q,p) = √(2/(q(q−p))) and depth-dependent α_c. The analysis is exact in the joint limit of infinite width d→∞ and infinite sequence length T→∞, with finite-size corrections characterized for practical use at finite T.

## Method

The core technical contribution is an exact mapping of self-attention at initialization to the Random Energy Model (REM) from statistical physics, where attention scores play the role of correlated random energies with covariance structure determined by the token overlap matrix. Using the replica method, the authors compute the free entropy of the attention partition function and identify a sharp condensation phase transition at β_c = √(2/(q(q−p))): below this threshold, attention is uniform (rank collapse), while above it, attention localizes on O(1) tokens (entropy collapse). The only viable initialization is β < β_c supplemented by sufficiently strong skip connections to preserve token diversity. The full block update algorithm combines this self-attention result with standard MLP propagation equations and LayerNorm, yielding a simple iterative procedure that predicts the trainability of any given transformer architecture. A backward-pass analysis further shows that gradients vanish at initialization for β < β_c, but skip connections break the simplex geometry on which the vanishing result depends, restoring trainability after the first backward pass.

## Results

The paper establishes a sharp phase transition in self-attention governed by the critical scale β_c = √(2/(q(q−p))), which for the first layer with i.i.d. embeddings reduces to β_c = √2. Below this threshold, attention is uniform (IPR Y^(2) → 0), causing rank collapse and vanishing gradients; above it, attention localises (Y^(2) = 1 − β_c/β), causing entropy collapse. Empirically, the theoretical cosine-similarity predictions (solid lines) closely track measurements across 60 BERT layers on TinyStories, and 60-layer models initialised in the trainable regime (β ≈ 0.02, α_SA ≥ 1.5) train successfully while those in either failure regime (β ≈ 0.02 with α_SA = 1.0, or β = 1.8 with any α_SA) fail to reduce test loss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without formal baseline method comparisons; its `compares_to` set is empty. Instead, the paper validates its analytical predictions against empirical measurements: the predicted cosine-similarity evolution matches empirical averages over 10 initialisations and 10 TinyStories sequences across all 60 layers (Fig. 1d), and the predicted gradient-norm scaling (Result 2) is confirmed numerically for T = 2048–8192 and d = 256–1024 (Fig. 3c). The paper also compares architectural variants: pre-norm LayerNorm delays rank collapse relative to post-norm (Fig. 4), and a gain-controlled attention modification (subtracting the mean value output) avoids rank collapse in both pre- and post-norm settings, with preliminary 30-layer TinyStories training showing successful convergence where vanilla attention fails (Fig. 6). These comparisons are qualitative/semi-quantitative rather than head-to-head benchmark evaluations against named prior methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the signal propagation framework established for MLPs (Poole et al. 2016; Schoenholz et al. 2016) to transformers by providing the first asymptotically exact treatment of the self-attention layer, leveraging a formal mapping to the Random Energy Model (Derrida 1981) previously identified for associative memories by Lucibello and Mézard (2024). It departs from prior transformer analyses that either assumed uniform attention (Noci et al. 2022) or used an annealed approximation that fails for large query/key weights (Cowsik et al. 2024), instead working in the infinite-sequence-length limit with the replica method. The novel scaling σ_a = β√(log T) for query/key variance, which matches the REM energy scaling, is not present in the existing literature on transformer initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The unified phase diagram gives practitioners a concrete, computable prescription (Algorithm 1) for choosing initialization hyperparameters that avoid both rank and entropy collapse simultaneously, rather than addressing them separately. The gain-controlled attention modification (subtracting the mean of value projections) is a one-line architectural change that the theory predicts should prevent rank collapse, with preliminary 30-layer experiments supporting this, though validation at scale remains open. Key limitations include the theory's exactness only in the infinite-sequence-length limit, acknowledged finite-size crossovers near the critical point, and the fact that autoregressive (causal) models break the simplex geometry assumption for early tokens. The paradox that the only viable regime (small β) also causes vanishing gradients at initialisation—resolved by noting skip connections break the simplex structure in the first backward pass—highlights a subtle interplay between forward and backward signal propagation that remains to be fully characterised beyond initialisation.
