## Problem

Existing learning-based planning methods for autonomous driving follow a deterministic paradigm that directly regresses a single action from scene observations, implicitly assuming a one-to-one mapping between scene and action. This fails when the feasible solution space is non-convex—i.e., multiple valid driving maneuvers coexist (e.g., overtake vs. yield)—because deterministic regression produces an averaged intermediate action that may be unsafe. The paper addresses this gap by formulating planning as a scene-conditioned probabilistic distribution over actions rather than a point estimate.

## Input / Output

VADv2 consumes a 6-camera surround-view image sequence in a streaming manner (10 Hz, past 1.6 s) together with navigation commands and ego-vehicle state; no LiDAR or HD map is used at inference. The output is a probability distribution over a fixed planning vocabulary of 4096 discrete trajectories, where each trajectory consists of 6 waypoints spanning 3 s at 0.5 s intervals. At inference, the highest-probability trajectory is selected and converted to low-level control signals (steer, throttle, brake) via a PID controller.

## Method

The core idea is to model the planning policy as a scene-conditioned stochastic process p(a|o) using a probabilistic field function. The continuous action space is first discretized into a planning vocabulary of N=4096 representative trajectories via furthest trajectory sampling, ensuring each vocabulary entry satisfies kinematic constraints. Each trajectory is encoded into planning tokens through sinusoidal positional encoding (analogous to NeRF's continuous field representation), and a cascaded Transformer decoder uses these tokens as queries attending to scene tokens (map, agent, traffic-element, and image tokens produced by a BEV encoder) as keys/values, with navigation and ego-state embeddings added to the output. Training combines three losses: a distribution loss (cross-entropy against the empirical action frequency from ~3M driving clips), a conflict loss that penalizes vocabulary actions colliding with ground-truth agent motions or road boundaries, and standard scene-token supervision. This design lets the model rank all candidate actions simultaneously and sample from the learned distribution, naturally handling multi-modal driving behavior without error accumulation from iterative rollout.

## Results

On the CARLA Town05 Long closed-loop benchmark, VADv2 achieves a Driving Score (DS) of 85.1, Route Completion (RC) of 98.4%, and Infraction Score (IS) of 0.87 using only camera input, outperforming the previous best camera-only method (Rao et al., DS=74.9) by 10.2 points and the previous overall SOTA DriveMLM (DS=76.1, camera+LiDAR) by 9.0 points. On NAVSIM, VADv2 attains a PDMS of 89.3, surpassing the best LiDAR-augmented baseline Hydra-NeXt (88.6) and DiffusionDrive (88.1). On Bench2Drive, VADv2 leads with a DS of 76.15 and a success rate of 50.46%, exceeding the prior best ETA (DS=74.33).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VADv2 achieves the highest DS across all 13 baselines on Town05 Long, with the most notable margins over the closest camera-only competitors: +10.2 DS over Rao et al. (74.9) and +11.4 DS over LeapVAD (73.7), while also surpassing the LiDAR-augmented DriveMLM (76.1) by 9.0 DS despite using only cameras. On NAVSIM, VADv2 (PDMS 89.3, camera-only) outperforms every baseline including LiDAR-augmented methods: Hydra-NeXt (88.6), WoTE (88.3), and DiffusionDrive (88.1), with the largest gap over UniAD (83.4) at +5.9 PDMS. On Bench2Drive, VADv2 leads all four baselines in DS, with a +1.8 margin over the closest competitor ETA (74.33) and a substantial +12.7 over DriveTransformer (63.46). On the 3DGS-based real-world benchmark, VADv2 reduces the Collision Ratio to 0.270, a 15.6% improvement over TransFuser (0.320) and 19.4% over VAD (0.335). Notably, VADv2 consistently wins on safety-critical metrics (IS, TTC, NC) even when some baselines match or slightly exceed it on individual sub-metrics like DAC, indicating that the probabilistic planning paradigm primarily benefits robustness and infraction avoidance rather than raw trajectory accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

VADv2 is the direct successor to VAD (Jiang et al., ICCV 2023), retaining its vectorized scene representation (agent tokens, MapTR-based map tokens) and BEVFormer backbone, but replacing the deterministic trajectory regression head with a probabilistic planning module. The core conceptual departure is from the deterministic paradigm (regressing a single future trajectory or control signal) to a scene-conditioned stochastic process p(a|o), drawing an explicit analogy to LLM next-token prediction: the action space is discretized into a planning vocabulary via furthest trajectory sampling, and a NeRF-inspired positional-encoding-based probabilistic field scores each candidate action. This contrasts with prior motion-prediction discretization approaches (Trajeglish, MotionLM) that tokenize single-step actions and suffer from error accumulation, whereas VADv2's tokens represent complete kinematically-feasible trajectories enabling one-shot planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The shift from deterministic regression to probabilistic distribution modeling addresses a fundamental limitation in end-to-end driving: non-convex feasible solution spaces where multiple valid maneuvers coexist (e.g., overtake vs. yield). The probabilistic formulation also unlocks flexible inference strategies (top-K sampling, confidence-based switching to rule-based controllers, integration with optimization-based post-solvers) that deterministic methods cannot naturally support. The 3DGS-based closed-loop benchmark (2000 hours of real driving data, 337 reconstructed environments) is a valuable new evaluation tool bridging the sim-to-real gap. Key open questions include whether the 4096-action vocabulary scales to more complex driving spaces, how the approach performs with reactive agents in simulation, and whether the distribution can be further refined with reinforcement learning or world models.
