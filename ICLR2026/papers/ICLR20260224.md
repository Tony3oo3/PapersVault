## Problem

Reward-model-based preference alignment for diffusion models faces two critical gaps: (1) pixel-level reward models lose discriminative accuracy on noisy intermediate latents because fine-tuning for preference ranking degrades the score-function properties that originally provided noise compatibility, and (2) evaluating preference at a single timestep yields inconsistent rankings across the sampling trajectory, causing trajectories with high intermediate rewards but poor final outputs to be incorrectly favored during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260224/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SLRM takes a noisy latent x_t at an arbitrary timestep t, a text condition c, and a timestep embedding, and outputs a scalar preference reward score Ŝ(x_t, c) that is stable across all timesteps. TAPO takes a text prompt and produces a pair of full denoising trajectories (win and lose) by performing SDE sampling at n uniformly distributed timesteps (default n=8 out of T=20 for T2I, T=40 for T2V) with P=4 candidate latents per SDE step, then ODE sampling for the remaining steps. Generated images are 512×512 (SD3.5-M) and videos are 49×832×480 at 16 FPS (Wan2.1-1.3B).

## Method

The framework has two components. SLRM initializes from the complete pre-trained MM-DiT diffusion model and introduces learnable task tokens that are concatenated with image and text tokens to participate in self-attention across all DiT layers, enabling multi-level semantic aggregation beyond simple text-image similarity. Its key mechanism is denoising score enhancement: the preference logit S(x_t, c) is multiplied by an exponential of the negative flow-matching velocity prediction error (Eq. 7), which explicitly preserves the model's score-function-based noise compatibility throughout preference training and prevents the progressive degradation observed in prior latent reward models. TAPO performs SDE sampling (derived from the Fokker-Planck equivalence of the flow-matching ODE) at n uniformly spaced timesteps, generating P stochastic candidates at each step and using SLRM to retain only the argmax and argmin samples, progressively pruning trajectories to identify win-lose pairs with pronounced quality differences. The collected trajectory-level win-lose latents are then optimized with a step-by-step DPO loss (SPO objective) using LoRA fine-tuning, avoiding the computational cost of exhaustive per-step evaluation while capturing coarse-to-fine trajectory advantages.

## Results

On the Pick-a-Pic v1 validation set, TAPO achieves state-of-the-art performance across most metrics: HPSv3 of 9.89 (vs. 9.10 for LPO and 9.19 for FLUX.1 Dev), GenEval of 68.93 (vs. 59.85 for LPO), HPSv2.1 of 32.01, CLIP-Score of 27.07, and PickScore of 23.03. For the reward model, SLRM achieves 66.35% accuracy on noisy latents at t∈[501,1000] and 66.59% at t∈[1,500], substantially outperforming the diffusion-based LRM-3.5 (59.56% and 64.12%) and all VLM-based reward models. On text-to-video (VBench), TAPO applied to Wan2.1-1.3B achieves an overall score of 84.87, marginally outperforming VideoDPO (84.70) with notable gains in Subject Consistency (98.79) and Spatial Relationship (+4.62 over baseline).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAPO outperforms the strongest prior alignment method LPO on HPSv3 (9.89 vs. 9.10, +0.79), GenEval (68.93 vs. 59.85, +9.08), HPSv2.1 (32.01 vs. 31.89), CLIP-Score (27.07 vs. 26.15), and PickScore (23.03 vs. 22.86), but trails slightly on MPS (3.07 vs. 3.12). Against the FLUX.1 Dev base model, TAPO wins on HPSv3 (9.89 vs. 9.19), HPSv2.1 (32.01 vs. 30.08), CLIP-Score (27.07 vs. 26.08), and GenEval (68.93 vs. 58.20), but loses on MPS (3.07 vs. 3.29). All DPO-style baselines (Diffusion-DPO, Inversion-DPO, SPO, DenseReward) are dominated by TAPO across every metric. For the reward model, SLRM surpasses LRM-3.5 by 6.79 points at t∈[501,1000] (66.35% vs. 59.56%) and 2.47 points at t∈[1,500] (66.59% vs. 64.12%), while VLM-based models like HPSv3 and PickScore, though stronger on clean images (72.80% and 71.93%), drop to 44–49% under noise. On T2V, TAPO edges out VideoDPO by 0.17 overall VBench score (84.87 vs. 84.70) with a clear advantage in Subject Consistency (98.79 vs. 96.68) and Spatial Relationship (81.08 vs. 80.25).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the step-by-step preference optimization (SPO) framework for diffusion models by addressing two specific failures: unreliable reward estimation on noisy latents and inconsistent single-timestep preference rankings. For the reward model, it departs from prior noise-compatible approaches (SPO, LPO) that simply inject noise into inputs, instead explicitly preserving the diffusion model's denoising score function through a score enhancement mechanism that augments preference logits. For the sampling strategy, it builds on the ODE-to-SDE conversion from Flow-GRPO but applies it selectively at multiple timesteps with progressive win-lose pruning, rather than using SDE throughout or evaluating at a single timestep. The task token mechanism for comprehensive quality evaluation is adapted from VisionReward, and the overall DPO-style loss follows the Diffusion-DPO lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The score enhancement mechanism provides a principled explanation for why naively fine-tuning diffusion models as reward models degrades their noise compatibility, and offers a concrete fix that practitioners can adopt. TAPO's multi-step SDE sampling with progressive selection is a practical middle ground between exhaustive per-step evaluation and single-timestep evaluation, though the observed reward hacking at large candidate sizes (P=5) highlights an unresolved tension between exploration breadth and reward model reliability. Open questions include whether the score enhancement generalizes to non-flow-matching diffusion formulations, whether the optimal number of SDE steps scales with model size or task complexity, and whether the approach can be extended to iterative multi-round alignment without compounding reward model drift.
