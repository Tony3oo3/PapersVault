## Problem

Existing protein fitness predictors either use property-agnostic PLM embeddings that encode multiple competing evolutionary constraints (diluting the target property signal) or assume additive mutational effects that fail to capture higher-order epistasis. Standard regression heads fine-tuned on a single DMS assay overfit to dataset-specific biases, yielding poor cross-assay generalization and unreliable ranking of mutants under distribution shift.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264223/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a wild-type protein sequence (fed to ESM-2 with mutated positions replaced by the mask token to obtain a mutation-aware yet wild-type-conditioned embedding), a mutation set specifying positions and substituted amino acids, and an AlphaFold2-predicted 3D structure (encoded by ESM-IF). At inference, a 20-step Heun solver integrates the learned velocity field from t=1 to t=0, and the final representation is mapped to a scalar fitness score by summing logit differences between mutant and wild-type amino acids at each edited site—a readout invariant to logit scale. The model has 37.1M trainable parameters (on top of frozen 792M ESM-2 + ESM-IF encoders) and trains in under 1 hour on a single A100 GPU.

## Method

RankFlow learns a conditional flow-matching model that transports frozen PLM embeddings (source p₀) to a property-aligned target distribution q via an energy-guided velocity field. The energy function combines a global standardized-fitness term with a local-deviation term computed over substitution-aware edit-distance neighborhoods, weighting mutants that are both high-fitness and anomalous relative to local substitution patterns. A Rank-Consistent Conditional Flow Loss (RC²) complements the energy-weighted flow-matching objective by minimizing a differentiable surrogate of Spearman rank correlation (using an optimal-transport-based soft-ranking operator), aligning training with rank-based evaluation and improving out-of-distribution generalization. A Property-guided Steering Gate computes per-position cosine similarity between each mutant's token delta and a cached steering direction (mean difference between top/bottom fitness-quantile mutants), converting scores to a sigmoid gate that focuses learning on property-relevant positions. The flow head is a lightweight U-Net1D (~9.6M params) conditioned on a multi-modal fusion of ESM-2 sequence and ESM-IF structure embeddings, with learnable position+amino-acid embeddings concatenated at mutated sites to capture non-additive multi-mutation interactions.

## Results

On the ProteinGym benchmark (201 DMS assays, Random split), RankFlow achieves Spearman correlations of 0.911 (Stability), 0.742 (Fitness), 0.765 (Expression), 0.781 (Binding), and 0.722 (Activity), setting new state-of-the-art across all five property categories. On the PEER and FLIP benchmarks, RankFlow scores 0.912 on β-Lactamase, 0.689 on GB1, and 0.687 on Fluorescence. In cross-assay generalization (trained on 40 same-category assays, tested on a held-out assay with <50% sequence similarity), RankFlow leads all baselines by 2–4 Spearman points per category while using only 37.1M trainable parameters versus 650M for full PLM fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RankFlow outperforms the closest generative-model baseline DePLM (ESM2) by 1.4–3.9 Spearman points across all ProteinGym categories (e.g., Fitness 0.742 vs 0.707, Activity 0.722 vs 0.693) and by 0.8–2.5 points on the PEER/FLIP benchmarks (β-lact. 0.912 vs 0.904, GB1 0.689 vs 0.665, Fluo. 0.687 vs 0.662). Against the non-parametric Transformer ProteinNPT, RankFlow gains 0.7–7.4 points (Fitness 0.742 vs 0.668, Binding 0.781 vs 0.706), and against structure-aware baselines ProtSSN and SaProt it leads by 1.9–7.4 points. On the ProteinGym evaluation-scheme comparison (Table 2), RankFlow achieves the best average Spearman (0.669) across Random/Modulo/Contiguous splits, edging out Kermut (0.655) which wins only the Contiguous split (0.591 vs 0.589). In the cross-assay generalization setting (Table 4), RankFlow surpasses DePLM(ESM2) by 2.4–3.6 points per category (e.g., Activity 0.554 vs 0.518) and outperforms full fine-tuning of ESM2/SaProt (650M params) by 10–16 points while using only 37.1M trainable parameters. RankFlow also matches or exceeds the uncertainty-estimation quality of Stable Kermut (ρ_uncertainty 0.165 vs 0.184 on Random; 0.135 vs 0.164 on Contiguous), confirming robust calibration alongside its accuracy gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

RankFlow repurposes the energy-guided conditional flow-matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022; Zheng et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025) from generative modeling and offline RL into protein fitness prediction, where the standard paradigm is a regression head on frozen or fine-tuned PLM embeddings (ESM-2, SaProt, DePLM). The key conceptual departure is treating the PLM representation as a source distribution to be transported via a learned velocity field toward a property-aligned target, rather than as a fixed feature vector to be linearly decoded. The rank-consistent loss (RC²) borrows differentiable ranking operators from optimal transport (Cuturi et al., 2019) to align training with the Spearman-based evaluation protocol, and the Property-guided Steering Gate introduces a data-driven position-level attention mechanism absent from prior PLM-based predictors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By framing fitness prediction as distributional transport rather than point regression, RankFlow addresses two persistent limitations in the field: property-agnostic PLM representations that bias toward wild-type preferences, and the additive-mutation assumption that fails for higher-order epistasis. The practical efficiency (37.1M trainable parameters, ~1 hour on a single A100) makes it accessible for protein engineering labs that cannot afford full 650M-parameter PLM fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the energy function design generalizes to properties without clear local neighborhood structure, whether the flow formulation extends to de novo design (beyond variant scoring), and whether the reliance on AlphaFold2-predicted structures introduces compounding errors for disordered or multi-domain proteins.
