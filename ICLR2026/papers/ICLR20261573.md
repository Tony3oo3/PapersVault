## Problem

Incorporating lag-agnostic causal priors—knowledge that a causal edge exists between two variables without specifying its exact time lag—into continuous time-series structure learning remains largely unaddressed. The natural maximum-based penalty for such priors is process-inequivalent: it commits early to whichever lag dominates initialization, violating the 'lag-unknown' semantics, and the resulting constraints introduce additional non-convexity that destabilizes optimization.

## Input / Output

The method takes a multivariate time series X ∈ R^{T×d} and binary prior masks C_p, C_a ∈ {0,1}^{d×d} that specify which node pairs have a causal edge present or absent without indicating the lag, along with a maximum lag L and edge threshold δ. It outputs a set of lag-specific structure matrices W_0:L where W_0 is constrained to be a DAG (intra-slice) and W_τ for τ > 0 encode inter-slice dependencies; after thresholding, each matrix yields a directed graph of causal edges at that lag. The framework is backbone-agnostic, plugging into linear (DYNOTEARS) and nonlinear (NTS-NOTEARS, LIN, RHINO) optimizers.

## Method

The core contribution is two process-equivalent loss formulations for lag-agnostic edge presence that avoid the early-lag-commitment bias of the maximum-based penalty. The binary-masked loss (p_bin) activates a uniform penalty across all lags only when every lag-specific edge is below threshold, while the logic-dual loss (p_or) uses a product of ReLU terms to encode OR logic in a fully continuous manner. Both are proven to be consequentially and process-equivalent to the intended prior, ensuring the optimizer evaluates all candidate lags holistically. To counter the proven non-convexity introduced by the OR-constraint (which creates multiple disconnected local optima), the method employs a two-stage data-driven initialization: first solving the unconstrained structure learning problem, then using its solution as the starting point for the prior-constrained optimization, guiding convergence to the data-consistent lag.

## Results

On 20-node ER4 synthetic graphs with 50% lag-agnostic priors, DYNOTEARS* (logic-dual, Init Data) achieves SHD 5.50±1.76 and F1 0.96±0.01, approaching the oracle lag-specific baseline (LS-0: SHD 4.83, F1 0.97) while substantially outperforming the data-only DYNOTEARS (SHD 13.00, F1 0.91). On the DREAM4-189 real-world dataset with 75% priors, the best configuration (DYNOTEARS& Init Data) achieves AUROC 0.92±0.02 and regression loss 3.25±0.33, outperforming all baselines including NOTEARS+Prior (AUROC 0.89, loss 6.65). The method also generalizes to nonlinear backbones (LIN, RHINO, NTS-NOTEARS), consistently improving F1 and SHD as prior proportion increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the data-only DYNOTEARS baseline, the proposed method (DYNOTEARS* Init Data) reduces SHD from 13.00 to 5.50 (58% improvement) at 50% priors, with F1 rising from 0.91 to 0.96. Compared to the oracle lag-specific baseline (LS-0), the lag-agnostic method is within 0.67 SHD and 0.01 F1, demonstrating near-oracle performance without requiring exact lag information. Critically, the method outperforms lag-specific priors with even 10% lag error (LS-10: SHD 6.50 vs. 5.50) and substantially beats LS-30 (SHD 8.33) and LS-50 (SHD 11.83), showing that noisy lag-specific knowledge is worse than no lag information at all. The flawed maximum-based formulation (DYNOTEARS^) consistently underperforms the process-equivalent alternatives (DYNOTEARS& and DYNOTEARS*), confirming the theoretical process-inequivalence analysis. On DREAM4, the method outperforms NOTEARS+Prior (static) in both AUROC and regression loss, and also beats the random-lag baseline, which degrades predictive accuracy despite improving summary graph AUROC.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-optimization framework for time-series structure learning (DYNOTEARS, NTS-NOTEARS) by addressing a gap in prior-knowledge integration: existing methods like NTS-NOTEARS require lag-specific priors (exact time lag known), whereas the more commonly available form of domain knowledge is lag-agnostic (edge exists but lag unknown). The key methodological departure is the identification of process-inequivalence in the natural maximum-based encoding of lag-agnostic presence, and the proposal of two process-equivalent alternatives (binary-masked and logic-dual losses) plus a data-driven two-stage initialization to counter the additional non-convexity. The framework is demonstrated as a plug-in on multiple backbones (DYNOTEARS, LIN, RHINO, NTS-NOTEARS), positioning it as a general layer atop existing differentiable structure learners.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical motivation is strong: domain experts and LLMs routinely provide coarse-grained causal relationships without temporal specificity, yet no prior method could correctly integrate such knowledge into time-series structure learning. The finding that the intuitive max-based formulation silently biases toward a single lag (process-inequivalence) is a subtle but important correction for anyone attempting to encode disjunctive structural constraints. Open questions include whether the approach extends to latent confounders, whether the two-stage initialization can be replaced by a single-stage method, and whether weaker lag-agnostic priors (paths, partial orders) can be incorporated into the temporal setting. The assumption of prior correctness remains a limitation in noisy real-world knowledge.
