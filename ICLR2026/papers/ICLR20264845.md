## Problem

Existing prompt optimization methods operate over fixed, static datasets and do not dynamically expose a prompt's failure modes as it evolves. While data augmentation is well-established in supervised learning, no prior work integrates synthetic data generation into prompt optimization in a feedback-driven, iterative manner that progressively stress-tests the prompt against its current weaknesses.

## Input / Output

SIPDO takes a task description, a small few-shot seed dataset S, and an LLM (e.g., GPT-4o) as input, along with a user-specified maximum difficulty level c (typically 10, up to 25 for complex tasks). It produces an optimized prompt p* and a sequence of synthetic question-answer pairs at increasing difficulty tiers. The generator is conditioned on the label first (label-then-question) to ensure validity, and for challenging benchmarks a three-voter expert check filters hallucinated items before they reach the optimizer.

## Method

SIPDO is a two-agent closed-loop system. The Data Generator samples a target label from the estimated prior, draws a latent variable capturing the seed dataset's structure, and decodes a synthetic question conditioned on (z, y, c) where c is a progressive difficulty tier; a summarizer h_φ feeds each level's output into the next, accumulating semantic depth monotonically. The Auto Prompt Optimizer then runs three sub-modules in sequence: (1) error analysis collects the current error slice, (2) a reflection module produces a textual patch diagnosing why the prompt failed, and (3) a prompt editor applies the patch to yield a revised prompt. The revised prompt undergoes local confirmation on current errors and global confirmation on all previously solved examples before being accepted, preventing catastrophic forgetting. The paper provides a PAC-style theoretical guarantee bounding the worst-case classification error by the empirical risk plus a KL penalty on the generator, an approximation term, and a uniform convergence term, formalizing the robustness-performance trade-off controlled by the regularization parameter λ.

## Results

On MMLU, SIPDO achieves 93.8% on Machine Learning, 93% on College Computer Science, and 96.5% on College Biology (GPT-4o), outperforming the strongest baselines by 3.4–5.4 points. On BIG-Bench (6 reasoning tasks), SIPDO attains 89.1% average accuracy with GPT-4o, 87.3% with GPT-4o-mini, 82.9% with Gemini-1.5-flash, and 83.4% with Gemini-1.5-pro, consistently exceeding CoT and APE by 6–14 points. On logical reasoning benchmarks, SIPDO reaches 86.4% average across FOLIO, PrOntoQA, and ProofWriter with GPT-4o, surpassing Neuro-Symbolic (82.0%) and REVOLVE (68.4%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIPDO outperforms TextGrad on MMLU Machine Learning (93.8% vs. 88.4%) and College Computer Science (93% vs. 89%), and beats REVOLVE on the same subjects (93.8% vs. 88.4%; 93% vs. 90%) as well as on FOLIO (83.2% vs. 65.7%) and PrOntoQA (96.3% vs. 85.4%). Against ANN, SIPDO leads on MMLU Machine Learning (93.8% vs. 90.1%). On BIG-Bench, SIPDO matches PromptAgent within 0.1% on GPT-4o (89.1% vs. 89.2%) but clearly surpasses it on GPT-4o-mini (87.3% vs. 85.2%), Gemini-1.5-flash (82.9% vs. 78.0%), and Gemini-1.5-pro (83.4% vs. 76.8%), while dominating CoT and APE by 6–14 points across all models. The one notable loss is on ProofWriter, where Neuro-Symbolic reasoning edges out SIPDO by 2.0% on GPT-4o (81.6% vs. 79.6%) and 0.4% on GPT-4o-mini (79.7% vs. 79.3%), though SIPDO still far exceeds CoT and REVOLVE on that task.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIPDO sits at the intersection of two well-developed threads: automatic prompt engineering (TextGrad, REVOLVE, PromptAgent, APE) and LLM-based data synthesis (Magpie, Self-Guided Noise-Free Generation). Prior prompt optimizers treat the evaluation dataset as fixed and static, while prior data-synthesis work produces training corpora without feeding them back into a prompt-refinement loop. SIPDO's core departure is making the synthetic data generator a *dynamic, difficulty-conditioned* component whose outputs are explicitly designed to expose the current prompt's failure modes, turning data augmentation from a one-shot preprocessing step into a closed-loop feedback signal. The progressive-difficulty curriculum and the three-step error-analysis/recommendation/refinement pipeline draw on Self-Refine, Reflexion, and the deep-learning-style textual gradient ideas in DLPO, but the coupling of a KL-regularized generator with an iterative prompt editor under a PAC-style guarantee is new.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a technique as old as data augmentation can be repurposed for the discrete, non-differentiable setting of prompt optimization, and the ablation showing a 17–24 % accuracy collapse when the difficulty gradient is removed underscores that *how* synthetic examples are sequenced matters as much as *that* they are generated. Practical limitations temper the impact: the method requires many sequential LLM calls (costly for GPT-4o at higher difficulty levels), the 'one-shot extremes' shortcut failed to help, and the theoretical guarantee, while clean, relies on standard uniform-convergence assumptions that may be loose for the small prompt spaces actually used. Open questions include whether the framework transfers to domain-specific corpora (financial filings, clinical notes) where genuine edge cases exist, whether fully automated variants can reduce the human-specified difficulty budget, and whether the approach scales to multi-turn or agentic prompting settings.
