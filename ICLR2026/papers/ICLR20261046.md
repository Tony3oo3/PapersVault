## Problem

Most fMRI decoding work targets either isolated concepts or holistic stimulus reconstruction, leaving open how to decode high-level relational meaning—interactions between multiple visual concepts and the relations binding them—from neural activity. The paper asks whether explicitly modeling compositional predicate-argument structure as a structural prior can improve both decoding accuracy and generalization to unseen compositional queries.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261046/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a parcellated fMRI recording (N×T, where N is the number of functional parcels from a standard atlas such as Yeo-17 or DiFuMo-128, and T is the number of time points) and a symbolic query parsed into a logical expression specifying subject, object, and predicate concepts. It produces either a Boolean True/False answer or a classification label over a predefined answer vocabulary (e.g., action or position). Critically, no supervision is provided on which brain regions ground each concept; only the final answer label is used for training.

## Method

NEURONA adapts the LEFT neuro-symbolic framework to fMRI by treating parcellation embeddings as candidate neural entities onto which concepts ground. Unary concepts (subjects, objects) are scored via a linear projection over parcel embeddings, while relational concepts (predicates) are scored over parcel pairs using a learned pairwise transformation. A differentiable executor then composes grounded concept scores according to the symbolic expression structure to produce the final answer. The key architectural insight is incorporating predicate-argument structural priors: the predicate's grounding is explicitly conditioned on the subject and object argument groundings (full argument-guided multi-region, H5), rather than treating each concept independently. Training uses only a cross-entropy loss on the final answer with a lightweight CNN for fMRI feature extraction and linear classifiers for grounding, requiring no intermediate supervision.

## Results

On BOLD5000-QA, NEURONA achieves 70.4% overall accuracy (62.1% Action, 50.8% Position, 74.1% T/F), and on CNeuroMod-QA it reaches 70.5% overall (65.1% Action, 57.5% Position, 72.5% T/F). This represents a 47% relative improvement over the strongest prior method (BrainCap at 47.7% on BOLD5000-QA). On the unseen-composition generalization split, NEURONA maintains 68.4% and 65.8% overall accuracy on BOLD5000-QA and CNeuroMod-QA respectively, while all baselines drop to near-chance levels (43–48%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

NEURONA decisively outperforms all three fMRI language-decoding baselines across both datasets and all query types. Against UMBRAE (Xia et al., ECCV 2024), which maps fMRI to multimodal embeddings and answers via LLaVA, NEURONA gains +22.9 points overall on BOLD5000-QA (70.4% vs. 47.5%) and +24.0 points on CNeuroMod-QA (70.5% vs. 46.4%). Against SDRecon (Takagi & Nishimoto, CVPR 2023), which projects fMRI into BLIP embeddings for captioning, NEURONA leads by +23.3 and +26.2 points overall. Against BrainCap (Ferrante et al., 2023), which aligns fMRI to GIT features for visual description, NEURONA achieves +22.7 and +26.3 points. The gap is especially large on relational queries: NEURONA scores 62.1% on Action and 50.8% on Position (BOLD5000-QA) versus 19–24% for all baselines, confirming that explicit predicate-argument grounding is the key differentiator. On the unseen-composition generalization split, NEURONA retains 68.4%/65.8% overall while baselines collapse to 43–48%, highlighting that end-to-end VLM-based decoders fail to recombine concepts systematically.

## Relation to Prior Work

NEURONA adapts the LEFT neuro-symbolic framework (Hsu et al., 2023), originally designed for concept grounding over 2D/3D visual entities, to the fMRI decoding setting where no canonical entity proposals exist. It departs from prior fMRI decoding work that either reconstructs stimuli holistically (diffusion-based image/video generation) or decodes isolated concepts, by formulating decoding as compositional question-answering over predicate-argument structures. It also contrasts with recent fMRI-QA approaches (UMBRAE, SDRecon, BrainCap) that project neural activity into VLM embedding spaces and generate free-form text, instead grounding concepts in modular neural modules and executing symbolic expressions differentiably.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that incorporating predicate-argument structural priors into fMRI decoding yields a 47% relative accuracy gain and, more importantly, robust generalization to unseen concept compositions where prior methods collapse to near-chance. This suggests compositional structure is a useful inductive bias for neural decoding, though the authors carefully note it does not establish that the brain itself encodes compositionally. Key limitations include reliance on passive viewing, coarse predefined parcellations (Yeo-7/17, DiFuMo, Schaefer), and the absence of combinatorial stimulus manipulations needed to directly test representational compositionality. Scaling to voxel-level data and active cognitive tasks are natural next steps.
