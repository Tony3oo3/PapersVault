## Problem

Learning Mixtures of Linear Dynamical Systems (MoLDS) from collections of input-output trajectories is critical for modeling heterogeneous neural dynamics, but existing approaches face a fundamental trade-off: tensor-based moment methods provide global identifiability guarantees yet degrade under noise and limited data, while EM-based likelihood optimization is flexible but highly sensitive to initialization and prone to poor local minima in the high-dimensional, multimodal MoLDS parameter space. No prior method reliably combines the robustness of algebraic initialization with the statistical efficiency of likelihood-based refinement for MoLDS.

## Input / Output

The method takes N input-output trajectories {(u_i, y_i)} where each trajectory is generated by one of K unknown LDS components; in neural applications, inputs are 5-6D kinematic signals (hand velocity, force, acceleration) and outputs are PCA-reduced neural spike rates (6-20 dimensions), extracted from a fixed movement window (−100 to +500 ms around onset). The method produces per-component LDS state-space parameters (A, B, C, D, Q, R), mixture weights, and soft trial-to-component responsibilities. A key structural constraint is that inputs are sub-sampled with stride L to ensure approximate independence of lagged covariates, and the number of components K, latent dimension n, and truncation length L are hyperparameters selected via validation BIC.

## Method

The Tensor-EM framework operates in two stages. In the tensor initialization stage, MoLDS is reformulated as a Mixture of Linear Regressions (MLR) by expressing each LDS through its impulse-response (Markov parameter) sequence and constructing lagged input vectors; second- and third-order cross-moment tensors are built from sub-sampled data, whitened via the rank-K eigendecomposition of M₂, and decomposed using Simultaneous Matrix Diagonalization (SMD) to recover mixture weights and scaled Markov parameters, which are then converted to state-space matrices via Ho-Kalman realization. Noise covariances (Q, R), not identifiable from moments, are initialized from residual covariances computed with the tensor-based estimates. In the EM refinement stage, a full Kalman filter-smoother EM alternates between computing trajectory-wise responsibilities via per-component Kalman log-likelihoods and updating all parameters via closed-form MLE from responsibility-weighted sufficient statistics, converging to a local optimum in the neighborhood of the globally consistent tensor solution.

## Results

On synthetic K=3 MoLDS (n=2, m=p=1), SMD-Tensor outperforms the RTPM-Tensor baseline in 91% of (N,T) configurations, with near-optimal recovery of mixture weights and Markov parameters at N=T=1280. On the more complex K=6 MoLDS (n=3, m=p=2), Tensor-EM achieves the lowest Markov parameter and mixture weight errors among all methods, while converging in substantially fewer EM iterations than random-initialized EM. On the real Area2 neural dataset, a 3-component MoLDS is selected by validation BIC, with dominant trial-component responsibility of 0.976 (second-largest: 0.023), and the recovered clusters align with per-direction supervised LDS fits. On the PMd dataset, a 4-component MoLDS (n=5, L=12) is selected, with components specializing in distinct angular bins of continuously distributed reach directions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the RTPM-Tensor baseline (Rui & Dahleh, 2025), the proposed SMD-Tensor method achieves lower mean Markov parameter estimation errors in 91% of all (N,T) configurations tested on synthetic K=3 MoLDS, with the advantage growing as data size increases. Against randomly initialized EM on the complex K=6 synthetic MoLDS, Tensor-EM achieves substantially lower Markov parameter and mixture weight errors while also converging in far fewer iterations, yielding a superior error-efficiency tradeoff (Figure 3). Against pure tensor methods, Tensor-EM maintains comparable aggregate LDS parameter error (geometric mean of A, B, C errors) but achieves lower Markov and weight errors, demonstrating that the EM refinement stage adds meaningful accuracy. Against SLDS (Linderman et al., 2017) on the Area2 dataset, MoLDS with Tensor-EM produces meaningful cross-trial clusters aligned with reaching directions, whereas SLDS fails to yield interpretable cross-trial groupings (Figure 5c, Figure 9), confirming that the primary heterogeneity is between trials rather than within-trial regime switches. On the PMd dataset, Tensor-EM MoLDS produces direction-specific components with distinct impulse-response profiles, while Random-EM MoLDS shows less consistent angular specialization (Figure 6c).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the tensor-initialization-plus-EM-refinement paradigm established for mixtures of linear regressions (Yi et al., 2016; Zhong et al., 2016) to the more complex MoLDS setting, replacing simple alternating minimization with a full Kalman filter-smoother EM that handles latent state inference and closed-form parameter updates. It builds on the MoLDS-to-MLR reformulation via lagged inputs (Rui & Dahleh, 2025) and prior tensor-based MoLDS methods (Bakshi et al., 2023), but substitutes the robust tensor power method (RTPM) with Simultaneous Matrix Diagonalization (SMD, Kuleshov et al., 2015) for improved numerical stability, and adds a principled residual-covariance initialization for noise parameters (Q, R) that was absent from prior tensor approaches. The work also contrasts with switching LDS (SLDS) and decomposed LDS (dLDS) frameworks, which target within-trial regime changes rather than the between-trial heterogeneity that MoLDS addresses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first demonstration that MoLDS can be reliably learned from real neural recordings, recovering direction-specific dynamical clusters in a fully unsupervised manner that matches supervised per-direction LDS fits — a practical step toward making MoLDS a usable tool for large-scale neural data analysis. The hybrid Tensor-EM framework addresses a genuine gap: pure tensor methods degrade under noise, and randomly initialized EM is trapped in poor local minima, yet neither alone suffices for the high-dimensional, multimodal MoLDS landscape. Key limitations include the restriction to linear dynamics with no exploration of model mismatch or strong nonlinearities, the absence of theoretical guarantees for the full Tensor-EM pipeline, and the reliance on a known number of components K. Future directions the authors identify include nonlinear extensions, autonomous (input-free) settings, and formal sample-complexity guarantees.
