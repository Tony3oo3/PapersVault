## Problem

Existing decomposition-based MoE compression methods (e.g., MoE-SVD) apply SVD independently to each expert's weight matrices, treating experts as isolated entities. This per-expert isolation overlooks structural redundancies shared across experts within a layer, resulting in suboptimal compression–accuracy trade-offs and sharp performance degradation at higher compression ratios (40–60%).
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265289/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes the weight matrices of all K experts in a layer (each W^(i) ∈ R^{d_out × d_in}) and a small calibration set (256 WikiText-2 samples) used to compute input activation and output gradient covariances. It produces a compact Tucker core tensor G ∈ R^{r1×r2×r3} together with three re-colored factor matrices U'^(1) ∈ R^{K×r1}, U'^(2) ∈ R^{d_out×r2}, U'^(3) ∈ R^{d_in×r3}, where the inverse whitening transforms are pre-absorbed into the factors so that inference incurs no additional runtime cost beyond the standard Tucker matmul.

## Method

TD-MoE reformulates MoE compression as a joint 3D tensor decomposition by stacking all K expert weight matrices into a single tensor T ∈ R^{K×d_out×d_in}, exposing inter-expert correlations that per-expert SVD cannot capture. Before factorization, a multi-linear whitening step applies regularized square-root-inverse covariance transforms along the input and output modes (T_w = T ×_2 S_out ×_3 S_in), decorrelating features and making the subsequent decomposition more balanced and data-adaptive. The whitened tensor is then factorized via Tucker decomposition into a low-rank core G and factor matrices U1, U2, U3, where U1 acts as a set of r1 meta-experts compressing inter-expert redundancy. An adaptive 3D rank allocation scheme searches over (r1, r2) in a 2D sweep and solves for r3 in closed form from the parameter budget equation, ensuring the decomposition tightly matches a target compression ratio ρ*. Finally, inverse whitening matrices are absorbed into U2 and U3 to yield the deployable re-colored factors, completing a fully post-training pipeline that requires no fine-tuning and preserves the original routing behavior.

## Results

On Mixtral-8×7B, TD-MoE (output-whitening) achieves 0.62 average commonsense reasoning accuracy at 20% compression (vs. 0.63 original), 0.57 at 40%, and 0.42 at 60%, while MoE-SVD drops to 0.58, 0.50, and 0.37 respectively. On Qwen2-57B-A14B, TD-MoE maintains 0.58 average accuracy at 20% compression (near-lossless vs. 0.59 original) and 0.56 at 40%, compared to 0.48 for MoE-SVD. For language modeling, TD-MoE (output) achieves 4.49/17.20/7.73 perplexity on WikiText-2/PTB/C4 at 20% compression on Mixtral, versus 4.86/19.42/8.98 for MoE-SVD. At 60% compression on Mixtral, TD-MoE (input) reaches 0.45 average accuracy, a 22% relative improvement over MoE-SVD (0.37).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TD-MoE consistently outperforms all five baselines across every compression ratio and both evaluated models. Against MoE-SVD (Li et al., 2025), the primary per-expert SVD baseline, TD-MoE achieves 14% relative accuracy gains at 40% compression (0.57 vs. 0.50 on Mixtral) and 22% at 60% (0.45 vs. 0.37), with perplexity advantages of 1.95 on WikiText-2 and 11.13 on PTB at 40%. Against NAEE (Lu et al., 2024), the expert-pruning baseline, TD-MoE leads by 9% at 40% (0.57 vs. 0.48) and 25% at 60% (0.45 vs. 0.36) on Mixtral. On Phi-3.5-MoE (Table 6), TD-MoE also surpasses ASVD (Yuan et al., 2023) and SVD-LLM (Wang et al., 2024) at 20–40% compression, and dramatically outperforms both at 60% where SVD-LLM collapses to 7168 perplexity on WikiText-2 while TD-MoE stays at 13.7. MoE-I2 (Yang et al., 2024), which combines inter-expert pruning with intra-expert SVD, is outperformed by TD-MoE at 40% compression on Phi-3.5-MoE (0.58 vs. 0.45 reasoning accuracy). The key differentiator is that TD-MoE's cross-expert Tucker factorization captures shared structure that per-expert methods (MoE-SVD, ASVD, SVD-LLM) and hard-pruning methods (NAEE, MoE-I2) cannot exploit, yielding progressively larger margins as compression becomes more aggressive.

## Relation to Prior Work

TD-MoE extends the per-expert low-rank decomposition line (MoE-SVD, SVD-LLM, ASVD) by reformulating MoE compression as a joint 3D Tucker decomposition over a stacked expert tensor, rather than applying 2D SVD independently to each expert. It also generalizes the activation-aware whitening used in prior SVD-based methods (Wang et al., 2024; Li et al., 2025) from a 2D matrix operation to a multi-linear tensor operation along both input and output modes. The 3D rank allocation mechanism is a natural extension of budget-constrained SVD truncation to the three-rank Tucker setting, reducing a 3D integer search to a 2D sweep with a closed-form update. Conceptually, the paper departs from the dominant per-expert isolation assumption by arguing that inter-expert structural redundancy is a first-order compression target that per-expert methods structurally cannot capture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE architectures become the default scaling strategy for frontier LLMs, efficient post-training compression without fine-tuning is critical for practical deployment, and TD-MoE offers a clean, architecture-preserving solution that is compatible with quantization and structured pruning. The cross-expert tensorization perspective opens a broader question: how much of the apparent 'expert specialization' in MoE models is actually shared low-rank structure that can be factored out globally? Open questions include whether the approach extends to dynamic or learned routing, whether it remains effective at extreme expert counts (hundreds or thousands), and how it interacts with training-time co-compression strategies. The method's reliance on a fixed calibration set and its sensitivity to the expert-preserving vs. full-decomposition choice (used differently for Mixtral vs. Qwen2) are practical limitations worth monitoring.
