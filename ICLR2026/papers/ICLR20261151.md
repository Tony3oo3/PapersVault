## Problem

Existing concept-erasure methods for text-to-image diffusion models apply a uniform fine-tuning strategy across all denoising timesteps, causing perceptual aberrations in erased-concept images and significant quality degradation in unrelated-concept images. The paper identifies that each concept's generative process hinges on a narrow subset of critical timesteps—structural concepts emerge in high-noise phases while fine-grained details (faces, styles) emerge in low-noise phases—making a one-size-fits-all approach both over- and under-erasing depending on the concept.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained Stable Diffusion v1.4 model, a target concept prompt (e.g., 'Elon Musk', 'cat', 'Van Gogh'), an optional anchor concept prompt (or empty for anchor-free erasure), and a small set of training images. A one-time SSScore pre-processing pass (under 3 minutes on a single RTX 4090) identifies the critical timesteps before fine-tuning begins. The output is a fine-tuned diffusion model that no longer generates the target concept while preserving generation quality on unrelated prompts; fine-tuning touches only cross-attention W_q (for celebrities/styles), full cross-attention modules (for objects), or all attention layers (for global concepts like NSFW), keeping GPU memory at ~14 GB.

## Method

SDErasure comprises three components operating on a self-contrastive fine-tuning scheme where the frozen original model provides supervision. First, a Step Selection algorithm computes a Step Separability Score (SSScore) at each denoising timestep by treating the diffusion model as a classifier: the noise prediction errors under the target and anchor concepts are converted into an instantaneous posterior probability via Bayes' theorem, and timesteps exceeding a threshold λ are selected for training. Second, a Score Rematching loss aligns the fine-tuned model's noise prediction under the target concept with the original model's prediction under the anchor concept, augmented by a negative guidance term that captures the conceptual trajectory shift between target and anchor. Third, a Quality Regulation module adds an early-preserve loss (freezing noise predictions at early timesteps where all concepts converge toward the natural image manifold) and a concept-retain loss (preserving predictions for unrelated concepts), jointly preventing the trajectory shifts from degrading overall generation fidelity.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 object erasure, SDErasure achieves the highest average harmonic mean H₀ of 95.33%, outperforming MACE (92.78%), ANT (88.87%), and UCE (83.52%). On celebrity erasure, SDErasure reduces FID on MSCOCO-30k to 6.49–7.72, roughly half the FID of the next-best method ANT (12.24–12.61), while maintaining competitive CLIP scores for target-concept suppression. On sensitive content erasure, SDErasure detects 49 sensitive instances (vs. 743 for the original model) with an FID of 16.92, whereas ANT achieves fewer detections (23) but at a severe quality cost (FID 41.25).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SDErasure outperforms all seven baselines on the CIFAR-10 harmonic mean H₀, achieving 95.33% vs. MACE (92.78%), ANT (88.87%), RECE (84.26%), ESD (84.53%), UCE (83.52%), and FMN (6.45%). The most dramatic advantage is in generation quality: on celebrity erasure, SDErasure's FID of 6.49–7.72 is roughly 40–50% lower than ANT (12.24–12.61), MACE (12.52–12.86), and ESD (13.50–13.55), indicating far less disruption to the model's general generative ability. On artistic style erasure, SDErasure achieves the best FID (7.02 for Van Gogh) while matching or exceeding SPEED (7.49) and ANT (7.75) in erasure efficacy (CLIP). On sensitive content erasure, ANT achieves the fewest detections (23) but at a prohibitive FID cost of 41.25, whereas SDErasure achieves 49 detections with FID 16.92, offering a far more practical safety–quality trade-off. SDErasure also surpasses MACE on H₀ without requiring SAM-generated masks as training data, using only a single text prompt per concept.

## Relation to Prior Work

SDErasure extends the ESD (Gandikota et al., 2023) negative-guidance erasure loss into a generalized Score Rematching loss that supports both anchor-free erasure and anchor-based concept alteration, while repurposing the diffusion classifier framework of Li et al. (2023) not as a classifier but as a timestep-selection oracle (SSScore). The closest prior work, ANT (Li et al., 2025b), also identifies that mid-to-late denoising stages are critical for erasure, but applies a fixed strategy across all concepts; SDErasure departs by making the timestep selection adaptive and concept-specific, and by adding a two-component Quality Regulation (early-preserve + concept-retain) absent from all prior trajectory-aware methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: reducing FID from 9.51 to 6.74 while maintaining strong erasure efficacy addresses the central trade-off that has limited adoption of fine-tuning-based erasure in production safety pipelines. The 75% training speedup over ANT and the one-time SSScore precomputation (under 3 minutes) make the approach deployable. Open questions include whether the heuristic anchor selection generalizes to open-vocabulary or compositional concepts, whether the method transfers to newer architectures (SDXL, rectified-flow transformers), and whether the multi-concept setting (FID rising from 6.65 to 8.82 for 20 concepts) degrades gracefully at larger scale. The paper is evaluated only on SD v1.4, leaving scalability to modern diffusion backbones untested.
