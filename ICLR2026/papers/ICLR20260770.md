## Problem

Existing dynamic Gaussian Splatting methods optimize all Gaussian primitives uniformly using 2D supervision (depth, optical flow, photometric consistency), ignoring that some Gaussians are strongly constrained by recurring observations while others are only weakly observed. This uniform treatment causes motion drift under occlusion and severe degradation when synthesizing views far from the input trajectory. No prior method explicitly models per-Gaussian reliability and uses it to structurally propagate motion from confident to uncertain regions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260770/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a monocular video as input and produces a time-varying set of 3D Gaussians that can be rendered from arbitrary viewpoints and timestamps. It is model-agnostic: it operates as a post-hoc refinement stage on top of pretrained dynamic GS base models (SoM or MoSca), extracting their 4D Gaussian primitives and applying an additional uncertainty-aware optimization (400 extra epochs for SoM, 1600 extra steps for MoSca). The output supports both near-input validation views and extreme novel views (up to 180° angular offset), as well as 3D keypoint tracking.

## Method

USPLAT4D introduces three coupled components. First, it estimates a time-varying per-Gaussian uncertainty via a closed-form variance derived from the photometric blending loss (inverse of summed squared blending weights), augmented with a per-pixel convergence indicator and an anisotropic depth-aware extension that propagates 2D uncertainty into 3D via camera rotation and axis-aligned scaling. Second, it constructs a spatio-temporal graph where Gaussians are partitioned into key nodes (selected via 3D voxel gridization and a minimum significant-period threshold of 5 frames) and non-key nodes, with edges formed by Uncertainty-Aware kNN using Mahalanobis distance to favor reliable connections. Third, it optimizes the graph with separate losses: key nodes are anchored to their pretrained positions with uncertainty-weighted Mahalanobis norms plus motion regularizers, while non-key nodes are regularized toward both their initialization and a Dual Quaternion Blending interpolation from their assigned key neighbors, all weighted by the anisotropic uncertainty matrices to down-weight unreliable directions.

## Results

On DyCheck (7 scenes, 2× resolution), USPLAT4D achieves mPSNR 19.63, mSSIM 0.716, and mLPIPS 0.25, outperforming the strongest baseline MoSca (19.32 / 0.706 / 0.26) by +0.31 dB PSNR and −0.01 LPIPS. On the 5-scene 1× setting, USPLAT4D reaches mPSNR 16.85 and mLPIPS 0.38, edging out MoBlender (16.79 / 0.37) and SoM (16.72 / 0.45). On the synthetic Objaverse benchmark, gains are most pronounced at large viewpoint shifts (120°–180°), where USPLAT4D over MoSca improves PSNR from 15.89 to 16.31 and LPIPS from 0.25 to 0.21. Tracking metrics also improve: PCK@5% rises from 82.4 to 84.5 (MoSca base) and EPE drops from 0.082 to 0.072 (SoM base).

## Baselines & Comparisons

USPLAT4D outperforms all 17 compared baselines on the primary DyCheck metrics. Against the two strongest Gaussian-based baselines, it beats MoSca (19.32→19.63 mPSNR, 0.706→0.716 mSSIM, 0.26→0.25 mLPIPS on 7-scene 2×) and SoM (16.72→16.85 mPSNR, 0.630→0.650 mSSIM, 0.45→0.38 mLPIPS on 5-scene 1×). MoBlender is the closest competitor on the 1× setting, matching USPLAT4D on SSIM (0.650) and slightly ahead on LPIPS (0.37 vs 0.38), but USPLAT4D leads on PSNR (16.85 vs 16.79). All earlier Gaussian methods (SC-GS, Deformable 3DGS, 4DGS, MoDec-GS, Dynamic Gaussians, Gaussian Marbles) are surpassed by 2–8 dB in PSNR. Among NeRF-based baselines (DynIBaR, HyperNeRF, T-NeRF, CTNeRF, DyBluRF, D-NPC, PGDVS, HiMoR), USPLAT4D leads on every reported metric, with the largest gaps over DynIBaR (+3.4 dB) and T-NeRF (+1.2 dB on 2×). On Objaverse, USPLAT4D consistently improves over both SoM and MoSca across all three angular ranges, with the largest PSNR gain (+1.14 dB over MoSca) at the most challenging (120°, 180°] range.

## Relation to Prior Work

USPLAT4D builds directly on dynamic Gaussian Splatting pipelines (SoM, MoSca) that parameterize per-Gaussian motion via low-rank bases or deformation fields, and on the vanilla DGS formulation from Luiten et al. (2024). Its key departure is elevating uncertainty from an auxiliary regularization signal (as in Kim et al., 2024) to a structural component that governs node selection, edge construction, and loss weighting in a spatio-temporal graph. The depth-aware anisotropic uncertainty (Eq. 6) and the Uncertainty-Aware kNN edge construction are novel design choices not present in prior graph-based DGS methods like MoSca's 3D lifting graph or SC-GS's local kNN.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled, model-agnostic mechanism to combat motion drift under occlusion and degradation at extreme novel views—two persistent failure modes in monocular 4D reconstruction. However, the approach inherits limitations of its underlying visual foundation models (tracking quality, depth estimation) and cannot recover when priors contain no reliable geometric signal, as demonstrated in textureless-region failure cases. Open questions include whether the graph construction overhead (~10 min for 200 frames) can be reduced, whether uncertainty propagation generalizes to multi-object scenes with complex interactions, and whether the key/non-key partitioning strategy scales to longer sequences or higher-resolution inputs.
