## Problem

Data-free continual model merging (DFCMM) requires sequentially fusing independently fine-tuned models into a single backbone without accessing any task data, while simultaneously preserving prior knowledge (stability) and adapting to new tasks (plasticity). Existing approaches lack a principled translation of these data-level desiderata into parameter-space objectives: simple averaging causes interference, projection methods fail under task-vector correlation, and adaptive strategies violate the data-free constraint by relying on auxiliary calibration data.

## Input / Output

At each merging step, the method consumes only two model checkpoints: the previously merged backbone θ_{t-1}^{merged} and the current task's independently fine-tuned model θ_t, both derived from a shared pre-trained initialization θ_0. No raw training data, test samples, or intermediate checkpoints are accessed. The output is a single merged model θ_t^{merged} with identical parameter count and inference cost to the individual fine-tuned models, since all filtering and adaptation operations are absorbed into the weight matrices in a layer-wise linear fashion.

## Method

NUFILT operates in three steps per layer. First, a null-space projector P_t = I − V̂_{≤t−1}V̂_{≤t−1}^T is constructed from the top-r_p right singular vectors of the cumulative merged update, removing components of the new task vector that overlap with earlier task subspaces to enforce stability. Second, a lightweight LoRA adapter (B_t A_t, rank r_l) is inserted within the filtered subspace and trained via a data-free projection-based surrogate loss that minimizes deviation from both the prior merged state and the current task vector along their respective principal directions, enabling plasticity without data. Third, the composite update τ_t(P_t + B_t A_t) is fused directly into the backbone weights, leaving no extra parameters at inference. The approach is grounded in a theoretical guarantee (Theorem 1) establishing that task vectors approximately align with data representation subspaces, which justifies using null-space filtering as a structural surrogate for the intractable data-level stability constraint.

## Results

On vision benchmarks (CLIP-ViT), NUFILT achieves 83.6% average accuracy on ViT-B/32 (8 tasks) and 91.6% on ViT-L/14 (8 tasks), improving over OPCM by 8.1% and 4.6% respectively, while limiting backward transfer to −2.7% and −1.1%. On the 20-task setting, NUFILT reaches 71.0% (ViT-B/32) and 84.7% (ViT-L/14), still outperforming OPCM by 5.3% and 8.7%. On NLP (Flan-T5-base, 8 GLUE tasks), NUFILT attains 83.7% ACC with −1.5% BWT, surpassing OPCM (80.6%) and WUDI-Merging (82.2%). On multimodal tasks (LLaVA-1.5-7B), NUFILT reaches 70.5% average, exceeding OPCM by 2.9%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NUFILT consistently outperforms all 8 baselines across vision, NLP, and multimodal settings without requiring extra parameters or data access. Against the strongest data-free baseline OPCM, NUFILT gains 4–8.7% in average accuracy (e.g., 83.6% vs. 75.5% on ViT-B/32 8-task; 91.6% vs. 87.0% on ViT-L/14 8-task) while reducing backward transfer from −6.3% to −2.7% (ViT-B/32) and from −2.6% to −1.1% (ViT-L/14). Compared to WUDI-Merging, NUFILT improves accuracy by 8.9% (ViT-B/32 8-task) and 1.5% (NLP), with substantially lower forgetting (−2.7% vs. −17.0% BWT on ViT-B/32). Simpler methods like Weight Averaging and Task Arithmetic suffer severe degradation (66–67% ACC) and large negative BWT (−9 to −11%), while TIES-Merging is highly unstable (49.0% ± 10.2% on ViT-B/32 8-task). Data-dependent methods (LW AdaMerging, LoRA-WEMOE) achieve the worst BWT (−20 to −32%) and often lower accuracy, confirming that auxiliary data does not resolve the stability-plasticity trade-off. NUFILT narrows the gap to individual fine-tuning to just 2.7% on ViT-L/14 (8 tasks) and 8.8% on ViT-B/32 (20 tasks), the closest among all merging methods.

## Relation to Prior Work

NUFILT extends the task-vector-based merging paradigm established by Task Arithmetic (Ilharco et al., 2023) and the projection-based continual merging of OPCM (Tang et al., 2025), which projects new task vectors onto the orthogonal complement of prior directions. Its theoretical foundation builds directly on Cheng et al. (2025), whose Proposition A.1 shows individual task vectors approximate linear combinations of representation features; NUFILT generalizes this to a subspace-level alignment guarantee (Theorem 1) via Wedin's sin-Theta theorem. The key departure from prior work is the explicit bridging of data-level stability/plasticity desiderata into a parameter-space objective: null-space filtering enforces stability by zeroing out components overlapping with prior task subspaces, while a LoRA adapter trained on a projection-aware surrogate loss recovers plasticity—all without accessing any task data, unlike AdaMerging or WEMOE which require auxiliary samples.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first principled, theoretically grounded data-free method that simultaneously enforces stability and plasticity in continual model merging, closing a gap where prior approaches either sacrificed one for the other or required data access. The subspace alignment theorem offers a reusable geometric lens for understanding how task vectors interact with representations, potentially informing future merging and editing methods. Limitations include the assumption of a shared pre-trained initialization and evaluation restricted to homogeneous backbones (CLIP-ViT variants, Flan-T5-base); extending to heterogeneous architectures or cross-domain merging remains open. The practical appeal is strong: no extra parameters, no inference overhead, and a single hyperparameter set generalizes across model sizes, task counts, and domains.
