## Problem

Federated multi-armed bandit algorithms have been studied separately under stochastic and adversarial reward assumptions, but no existing method achieves near-optimal regret guarantees in both regimes simultaneously (best-of-both-worlds). Moreover, the state-of-the-art adversarial federated bandit algorithm (FEDEXP3) incurs a suboptimal O(T^{2/3}) regret, leaving a significant gap to the O(√T) lower bound. This paper closes both gaps by proposing the first BOBW algorithm for federated bandits with heterogeneous, locally biased observations.

## Input / Output

Each of V agents receives a locally biased scalar loss ℓ_{v,t}(k) ∈ [0,1] for its chosen arm k at round t, where the same arm can yield different losses across agents. Agents communicate only with graph neighbors via a doubly stochastic matrix P, exchanging their cumulative loss estimator and a set of deviation records (tuples of agent, time, arm, and correction weight) each round. The algorithm outputs a probability distribution over K arms for the next pull, an updated cumulative loss estimator, and propagated deviation records that are added back to the estimator only after D rounds of network propagation to avoid distribution misalignment.

## Method

FEDFTRL adapts the Follow-The-Regularized-Leader framework with a hybrid regularizer combining a Tsallis (√x) term and a negative-entropy term, specifically designed for bandits with delayed feedback. The key insight is to treat the latency of decentralized gossip communication as an implicit feedback delay, quantified by a topology-dependent parameter C_t^P that captures the spectral gap of P and the graph diameter D. To handle heterogeneity-induced bias, the algorithm uses a truncated loss estimator that replaces the standard 1/x(k) denominator with max{x(k), 12VC_t^P γ_t}, preventing rare arm pulls from causing divergent probability distributions across agents. The truncation bias is corrected by propagating deviation records through the network; once a record has traveled for more than D rounds (reaching all agents), its correction is added to the cumulative loss estimator without disrupting distribution alignment. A novel learning-rate schedule incorporating C_t^P yields an individual adversarial regret of O(√(KT/V) + √(C_T^P T log K)), improving the prior O(T^{2/3}) bound, and a near-optimal stochastic regret of O(Σ_{k≠k*} log T/(VΔ_k) + C_T^P/(Δ_k log K)).

## Results

In the adversarial regime, FEDFTRL achieves an individual regret of O(√(KT/V) + √(C_T^P · T log K)), which is O(T^{1/2}) and improves over the prior state-of-the-art O(T^{2/3}) of FEDEXP3. In the stochastic regime, FEDFTRL achieves O(Σ_{k≠k*} log T / (VΔ_k) + C_T^P / (Δ_k log K)), matching the known lower bound up to low-degree polynomial factors. Empirically, on both a synthetic dataset (V=16, K=20, T=3000) and the MovieLens dataset (V=3963, K=20, T=3000), FEDFTRL achieves the lowest cumulative regret across all three network topologies (complete graph, grid graph, RGG-0.5), outperforming all four baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FEDEXP3 (Yi & Vojnovic, 2023), FEDFTRL improves the adversarial regret from O(T^{2/3}) to O(T^{1/2}), a significant theoretical gain; empirically, FEDFTRL achieves substantially lower cumulative regret than FEDEXP3 on both synthetic and MovieLens data across all three network topologies. Against GOSSIP_UCB (Zhu et al., 2021), FEDFTRL matches the optimal stochastic regret bound O(Σ log T / (VΔ_k)) while also providing adversarial guarantees, whereas GOSSIP_UCB only handles the stochastic regime and has a V× worse dependence on the number of agents. Against DRRB-bandit (Zhang et al., 2025), FEDFTRL achieves the same stochastic regret order but additionally covers the adversarial regime, making it a strictly more general algorithm. Against IND-FTRL (Tsallis-FTRL run independently without communication), FEDFTRL demonstrates the critical benefit of inter-agent communication: IND-FTRL fails to achieve sublinear regret under locally biased feedback, while FEDFTRL maintains sublinear regret in both regimes. The sensitivity analysis (Figures 3–4) further shows that FEDFTRL remains robust even when the topology parameter C_T^P is misspecified by factors of 0.1× to 10×.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lines of research: federated bandits (Zhu et al., 2021; Yi & Vojnovic, 2023; Zhang et al., 2025) and best-of-both-worlds (BOBW) algorithms for bandits with delayed feedback (Zimmert & Seldin, 2020; Masoudian et al., 2022). The key conceptual move is recognizing that decentralized gossip communication in federated bandits induces an implicit feedback delay, allowing the hybrid regularizer and learning-rate schedule from the delayed-feedback BOBW literature to be adapted directly. The novel truncated loss estimator and deviation-record communication scheme are introduced to handle the heterogeneity-induced bias that has no analogue in the single-agent delayed-feedback setting, distinguishing this work from a straightforward transplant of prior techniques.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FEDFTRL is the first algorithm to achieve near-optimal BOBW regret guarantees in federated bandits, closing a gap where prior work treated stochastic and adversarial regimes as entirely separate problems. The improvement from O(T^{2/3}) to O(T^{1/2}) adversarial regret is a meaningful tightening that brings the upper bound closer to the known lower bound (up to low-degree polynomial factors). The remaining open question, acknowledged by the authors, is closing the residual polynomial gap between upper and lower bounds in the adversarial regime. For practitioners, the algorithm's robustness to misspecified topology parameters (demonstrated empirically) makes it practically deployable without precise knowledge of network structure.
