## Problem

Existing MLLM-based video authenticity detectors rely on supervised fine-tuning or DPO over static, pre-labeled datasets, which cannot capture the evolving, multi-step physical inconsistencies introduced by modern generative models like Sora and HunyuanVideo. Moreover, prior detectors output only binary real/fake decisions without interpretable reasoning, limiting transparency for content moderation and legal accountability. No prior work applies group-level reinforcement learning (GRPO) to video forgery detection, leaving the model's reasoning capability underdeveloped.

## Input / Output

The model consumes video clips standardized to 49 frames at 8 FPS, 720×480 resolution, and YUV420p format; during inference, up to 16 frames are sampled, each resized to 28×28 and projected into 128 feature channels for the Qwen2.5-VL-7B encoder. The output is a structured 'think + answer' response: a multi-step chain-of-thought rationale analyzing motion consistency, lighting, texture, and physical plausibility, followed by a final real/fake verdict. The GRPO-Q variant additionally estimates the diffusion step (as a 0–1 progress value) used to generate the fake video, enabling fine-grained quality assessment beyond binary classification.

## Method

VidGuard-R1 fine-tunes Qwen2.5-VL-7B in two stages: (1) SFT on 30k CoT-annotated video pairs to establish basic reasoning structure, and (2) RL fine-tuning on 100k pairs using both DPO (with swapped CoT preference pairs) and GRPO (with video labels as direct reward signals). The key contribution is two GRPO reward variants: GRPO-TA injects temporal artifacts (frame repetition/reversal) into both real and fake videos and assigns asymmetric bonus rewards (α₁=0.5 vs. α₂=0.3) for correctly detecting manipulated real videos, conditional on the model already achieving >80% group accuracy on the original clips; GRPO-Q generates intermediate-quality fakes at varying diffusion steps (10–50) and gives partial credit proportional to the distance between predicted and ground-truth step progress, pushing the model toward fine-grained quality reasoning. The 140k training dataset pairs real videos from InternVid/ActivityNet with contextually aligned fakes from HunyuanVideo-I2V and CogVideoX-5B (conditioned on the real video's first frame and caption), with all metadata (resolution, FPS, bitrate) standardized to eliminate superficial shortcuts.

## Results

On the curated VidGuard-R1 dataset (140k pairs, standardized metadata), VidGuard-R1 (GRPO-Q) achieves 84.32% and 86.17% top-1 accuracy on CogVideoX and HunyuanVideo respectively, surpassing the best CNN baseline (SlowFast, 77.87/77.03%) and best Transformer baseline (TimeSformer, 78.53/74.55%). On GenVidBench, VidGuard-R1 (GRPO) reaches 97.53% mean top-1 accuracy, a 17.6-point improvement over the prior best (MViT V2, 79.90%). On GenVideo, VidGuard-R1 (GRPO) attains a mean F1 of 0.96 and recall of 0.96, outperforming the previous SOTA DeMamba-XCLIP (F1 0.90, recall 0.93) by 0.06 F1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the VidGuard-R1 dataset, VidGuard-R1 (GRPO-Q) outperforms the strongest CNN baseline SlowFast (77.87/77.03%) by ~6–9 points and the best Transformer TimeSformer (78.53/74.55%) by ~6–12 points. Off-the-shelf MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, GPT-4o, GPT-4.1 mini) perform near-randomly (50–57%), confirming that video authenticity detection requires task-specific training. On GenVidBench, VidGuard-R1 (GRPO) achieves 97.53% mean accuracy versus 79.90% for MViT V2 (the prior best Transformer) and 71.26% for TRN (the prior best CNN), a gap of 17.6 and 26.3 points respectively. On GenVideo, VidGuard-R1 (GRPO) improves mean F1 from 0.90 (DeMamba-XCLIP, prior SOTA) to 0.96, with the largest per-model gains on Sora (F1 0.64→0.97) and HotShot (F1 0.75→0.91). The zero-shot variant (pretrained on GenVideo, tested on GenVidBench) already reaches 96.37% mean, demonstrating strong cross-dataset generalization without any GenVidBench-specific fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the GRPO reinforcement learning framework from DeepSeek-R1 (originally designed for LLM reasoning) to the domain of AI-generated video detection, extending it with two specialized reward signals: temporal artifact contrast (GRPO-TA) and diffusion-step quality estimation (GRPO-Q). It departs from prior MLLM-based detectors like FakeShield (SFT) and SafeWatch (DPO) by moving beyond static preference matching to iterative multi-path exploration, and from traditional CNN/Transformer detectors by producing interpretable chain-of-thought rationales alongside binary predictions. The dataset construction deliberately contrasts with GenVideo and GenVidBench by standardizing resolution, frame rate, and duration to eliminate metadata shortcuts, and by conditioning generation on the first frame and caption of real videos for strong contextual alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals that existing AI-generated video benchmarks are inflated by metadata shortcuts (resolution, duration, frame rate), as evidenced by the 10+ point accuracy gap between the controlled dataset (~85%) and prior benchmarks (~95%+), which is an important diagnostic for the field. The near-random performance of frontier MLLMs (GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL) on the controlled dataset underscores that current video understanding models lack genuine physical-reasoning capability for authenticity assessment. Key limitations include reliance on only two generation models (HunyuanVideo, CogVideoX) for training, the acknowledged hallucination risk in generated rationales, and the open question of whether RL-driven reasoning truly captures causal physical understanding or merely learns correlated visual patterns that may not transfer to future, more realistic generators.
