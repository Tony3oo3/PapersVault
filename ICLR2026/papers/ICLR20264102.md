## Problem

Existing watermarking methods for diffusion-based text-to-image models are fragile to realistic distortions and adversarial removal attacks, and critically, they require expensive per-key retraining or pretraining whenever the watermarking key changes. This limits practical deployment in dynamic attribution scenarios where many users or models each need distinct, high-entropy keys.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264102/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a text prompt, a randomly sampled latent noise vector, and an M-bit binary key (default M=28) as inputs, and produces a watermarked RGB image (512×512 for Stable Diffusion, 1024×1024 for FLUX). The key is deterministically split into L chunks of log₂P bits, each chunk selecting one of P LoRA adapters at a corresponding routing layer. A separate extractor network takes any image and outputs M sigmoid logits that are thresholded to recover the binary key, enabling both detection (Hamming distance test) and multi-user attribution (nearest-key matching against a database).

## Method

MOLM reinterprets LoRA adapters as watermark carriers within a frozen diffusion model's VAE decoder. P low-rank adapters are added in parallel to each of L selected ResNet blocks; at generation time, the binary key deterministically routes through one adapter per block, implementing a key-dependent parameter perturbation ΔΦ(κ) without modifying backbone weights. Training jointly optimizes the LoRA adapter parameters and a separate extractor network using an LPIPS perceptual loss (between watermarked and non-watermarked images from the same prompt/latent) and a binary cross-entropy key-recovery loss with augmentation for robustness. Because the routing path is selected at inference time by the key, no per-key retraining is needed—new keys are embedded simply by activating different adapter combinations. The distributed, multi-layer routing makes the watermark redundantly encoded across blocks, enhancing robustness to removal and averaging attacks while keeping FID degradation ≤1.5.

## Results

On Stable Diffusion v1.5 with MS-COCO prompts, MOLM achieves 0.98 bit accuracy on undistorted images with only −1.4 FID degradation (27.7 vs. 29.1 baseline), 0.77 SSIM, and 23.5 PSNR. Under common distortions, MOLM retains 0.91 bit accuracy under cropping, 0.89 under JPEG compression, and 0.84 under rotation. In detection-only mode (TPR@1%FPR), MOLM achieves ≥0.96 across all tested distortions on SD and ≥0.92 on FLUX. For attribution with 1,000 users and 28-bit keys, MOLM achieves 98.92% true accept rate and 100% conditional attribution accuracy on undistorted images, with a 0.02% false positive rate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among bit-recovery methods, MOLM substantially outperforms Stable Signature on rotation (0.84 vs. 0.56) and resize (0.90 vs. 0.72) while achieving lower FID degradation (−1.4 vs. +0.4). Against AquaLoRA, MOLM improves rotation robustness by 39 points (0.84 vs. 0.45) and reduces FID by 2.8 points. MOLM is broadly competitive with WOUAF, matching its undistorted accuracy (0.98) and JPEG robustness (0.89 vs. 0.98) while using a smaller 28-bit key vs. 32-bit and requiring only ~1 day of training vs. ~3.5 days. In detection-only mode, MOLM achieves TPR ≥0.96 across all distortions, surpassing ROBIN's rotation performance (0.98 vs. 0.64) and Gaussian-Shading's rotation (0.98 vs. 0.48), while maintaining far better perceptual quality (SSIM 0.77 vs. 0.21 for Gaussian-Shading, PSNR 23.5 vs. 8.7). Post-hoc methods TrustMark and VINE, while offering 100-bit keys, collapse under geometric attacks (0.00 TPR for TrustMark under crop/rotation; 0.51–0.52 bit accuracy for VINE under crop/rotation), whereas MOLM retains ≥0.89 bit accuracy under the same conditions.

## Relation to Prior Work

MOLM repurposes Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA), originally designed for parameter-efficient fine-tuning, as a watermark embedding mechanism, and adapts the Mixture of LoRA Experts (MoLE) routing paradigm by replacing data-driven gating with deterministic key-driven adapter selection. This departs from prior diffusion watermarking methods (Stable Signature, AquaLoRA, WOUAF, Tree-Ring) that either require per-key retraining, full-model fine-tuning, or modify the diffusion sampling trajectory itself. The paper's general framing—watermarking as key-dependent perturbation of a frozen generator's parameters—unifies these approaches under a single formulation, with MOLM as a routing-based instantiation that avoids key-specific retraining entirely.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MOLM offers a practically deployable watermarking solution: ~1 day of training on a single A100, zero per-key cost, and negligible inference overhead, making it suitable for dynamic deployments where keys must be updated frequently. Its robustness against averaging attacks (≥0.96 bit accuracy at k=5000) and full-knowledge retraining attacks (attacker images decode at chance level) addresses two attack vectors that break several prior methods. Key limitations include the modest 28-bit key capacity (U-Net perturbation for 108 bits degrades FID by 4.15), vulnerability to extreme geometric transforms (90° rotation, 0.3× resize), and evaluation limited to 512×512 and 1024×1024 resolutions. Open questions include whether the VAE-decoder-only perturbation strategy generalizes to architectures without a separate decoder, and whether higher key capacities can be achieved without the fidelity cost observed in the U-Net.
