## Problem

Streaming voice conversion and speaker anonymization systems inject speaker identity as a single static global embedding while content is represented as a time-varying frame-level sequence. This static-dynamic representational mismatch produces over-smoothed timbre, limited expressivity, and degraded naturalness, especially when articulation, emotion, or emphasis change within an utterance. The paper asks whether aligning the temporal granularity of speaker conditioning with that of content can resolve this trade-off without sacrificing streaming latency.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a 16 kHz source speech waveform and a target speaker embedding (concatenated X-vector and ECAPA-TDNN, 704-dim) and produces a 16 kHz converted or anonymized waveform. It operates in a fully causal streaming fashion with 60 ms or 100 ms chunks, a 2 s look-back context window, and a limited 4-frame (~80 ms) future peek in the encoder, achieving <80 ms end-to-end latency on GPU and ~132 ms on CPU. All intermediate representations (content, timbre, prosody) are aligned at a 50 Hz frame clock (20 ms hop).

## Method

TVTSyn replaces the static speaker embedding with a content-synchronous Time-Varying Timbre (TVT) representation built around a Global Timbre Memory (GTM): the global speaker vector is expanded into 48 key-value facet pairs via a dual representation combining speaker-specific MLP outputs with learnable cross-speaker prototypes, and frame-level content embeddings attend over these keys to retrieve context-appropriate timbre sub-components. A learned scalar gate α_t modulates how far the per-frame timbre deviates from the global embedding, and spherical linear interpolation (Slerp) blends the global and attended vectors along the hypersphere geodesic to preserve identity geometry while enabling smooth local variation. A factorized VQ bottleneck (512→8-dim latent, 4096-entry codebook, projected back to 512) discretizes content to suppress residual speaker leakage, trained self-supervised against HuBERT k-means pseudo-labels. The conditioned features drive a causal SEANet waveform decoder with 8-layer MHSA context, cLN-with-Fusion speaker conditioning, and lightweight F0/energy predictors, optimized with a multi-objective loss combining multi-scale L1 mel reconstruction, multi-period/multi-band adversarial terms, feature matching, and prosody L2.

## Results

On the VoicePrivacy Challenge 2024 anonymization protocol, TVTSyn achieves WER 5.35%, EER 47.55% (lazy-informed) and 14.57% (semi-informed), and UAR 37.32% for emotion suppression. For voice conversion, TVTSyn reaches a target-speaker similarity (Trg-SIM) of 0.77—matching within-speaker similarity in real speech—while maintaining source-speaker dissimilarity (Src-SIM) of 0.47, equivalent to between-speaker similarity. In human listening tests (N=20), TVTSyn attains the highest MOS (3.82) and the highest speaker verifiability rate (74.33%). End-to-end streaming latency is approximately 79 ms on GPU and 132 ms on CPU with RTFs of 0.31 and 1.20 respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three streaming baselines, TVTSyn achieves the best utility (WER 5.35% vs. SLT24's 5.70% and DS's 10.80%) while maintaining competitive privacy (EER-lazy 47.55% vs. DS's 49.09% and GenVC-s's 48.48%). On voice conversion quality, TVTSyn's NISQA score of 3.91 is marginally below SLT24's 4.01 but substantially above DS and GenVC-s; in human listening, TVTSyn achieves the highest MOS (3.82) and verifiability (74.33%) among all streaming systems. Compared to offline VPC'24 participants (T10-C3, T9, T8-4, T38-M1), TVTSyn trades some WER (5.35% vs. 2.35–3.75%) for the ability to operate causally at <80 ms latency, which offline systems cannot match. Notably, TVTSyn's EER-semi (14.57%) is lower than DS (20.83%) and GenVC-s (15.94%), indicating that the time-varying timbre representation does not weaken anonymization under the stronger attacker model. The GenVC baselines, despite using a non-causal encoder, achieve the weakest speaker transfer (lowest Trg-SIM) and modest NISQA scores, underscoring that architectural streaming constraints matter beyond encoder causality alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the authors' own streaming VC lineage (SLT24, DarkStream) by replacing the static global speaker embedding with a content-synchronous, time-varying timbre (TVT) representation built around a Global Timbre Memory (GTM) with learnable cross-speaker prototypes, a learned gate, and spherical interpolation (Slerp). The factorized VQ bottleneck is borrowed from NaturalSpeech 3 (Ju et al., 2024) and repurposed here to regularize the content stream and reduce residual speaker leakage. Conceptually, the GTM departs from prior multi-center/subcenter speaker modeling (Ulgen et al., 2024) and attention-based modulation methods (FreeVC, GenVC, DAFMSVC) by introducing shared, learnable timbre prototypes that are modulated per-speaker, enabling efficient generalization under strict causal streaming constraints where non-causal or offline approaches are infeasible.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the privacy-utility trade-off in streaming VC/SA is fundamentally architectural: resolving the static-speaker/dynamic-content mismatch via TVT preserves expressivity and naturalness without weakening anonymization, while the VQ bottleneck independently controls content-level speaker leakage. This separation gives practitioners modular knobs (TVT for quality, VQ for privacy) rather than a single aggressive bottleneck that degrades both. Open questions include extending to cross-lingual and code-switching speech, incorporating learned pseudo-speaker generation (currently 28 fixed targets), explicit disentanglement of static traits (age, sex) from dynamic style (emotion), and robustness to noisy/reverberant environments. The <80 ms GPU latency makes the approach viable for real-time teleconferencing and live translation where offline processing is infeasible.
