## Problem

Existing brain-to-image decoding pipelines route fMRI signals through intermediate embedding spaces (e.g., CLIP or DINO features) before conditioning a generative model, creating an information bottleneck that obscures how individual cortical regions contribute to the reconstruction. This two-stage design limits interpretability and ties reconstruction quality to the alignment between neural representations and the chosen embedding space, rather than to the brain signals themselves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261458/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes surface-based fMRI data parcellated into 200 high-SNR cortical parcels (from the Schaefer 500-parcel atlas), where each parcel's vertex responses are projected into 768-dimensional token embeddings via a dedicated linear mapper. The text encoder in Stable Diffusion receives an empty string, so fMRI tokens are the sole conditioning signal. Outputs include a 256×256 reconstructed image (selected from multiple candidates via a brain-encoder correlation score), parcel contribution vectors showing each region's relative influence across denoising steps, and ROI attention maps localizing where specific brain regions direct attention in the image.

## Method

NeuroAdapter builds on the IP-Adapter framework by replacing Stable Diffusion's U-Net cross-attention layers with IP-adapter-style modules that attend to parcel-wise fMRI token embeddings, enabling direct brain-to-image conditioning without intermediate feature spaces. Each of the 200 parcels is assigned a unique linear projection (v_max × 768) that transforms padded vertex responses into token embeddings, and a stochastic parcel-wise token dropout is applied during training to prevent over-reliance on any single region. Training updates only the linear mappers and new cross-attention modules while keeping the rest of the pretrained SD frozen, with min-SNR loss weighting to balance gradients across diffusion timesteps. For interpretability, the IBBI framework extracts cross-attention matrices at every denoising step and aggregates them in two directions: a brain-directed view that computes per-parcel contribution vectors across all layers and heads, and an image-directed view that reshapes and upsamples ROI-pooled attention into 2D spatial maps showing where each cortical region influences the generated image.

## Results

On the NSD dataset (averaged over 4 subjects), NeuroAdapter achieves 90.97% CLIP 2WC accuracy, 90.79% InceptionV3 2WC accuracy, and 93.48% AlexNet-5 2WC accuracy on high-level semantic metrics, while scoring 0.124 PixCorr and 0.307 SSIM on low-level metrics. These results are competitive with embedding-aligned approaches like MindEye1 (94.1% CLIP, 93.8% Incep) and Brain Diffuser (91.5% CLIP, 87.2% Incep), though NeuroAdapter trails on low-level pixel/structure metrics. On NSD-Imagery, NeuroAdapter generalizes to mental imagery trials with 60.04% CLIP accuracy, outperforming MindEye1 (54.72%) and Brain Diffuser (54.07%) on that condition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NeuroAdapter does not lead on any single metric in the main NSD comparison; MindEye1 (Scotti et al., 2023) achieves the best scores across nearly all eight metrics (e.g., 94.1% CLIP, 97.8% Alex-5, 0.309 PixCorr), followed closely by DREAM (Xia et al., 2024) and Brain Diffuser (Ozcelik & VanRullen, 2023). On high-level semantic metrics, NeuroAdapter (90.97% CLIP, 90.79% Incep) trails MindEye1 by ~3% and Brain Diffuser by ~0.5% on CLIP, but remains within a few points of all major competitors. The paper argues that Brain Diffuser's advantage on low-level metrics (PixCorr 0.254 vs. 0.124) stems from a dedicated VDVAE pathway for predicting latent visual features; when that pathway is removed (BD w/o VDVAE), its low-level performance drops to 0.143 PixCorr, comparable to NeuroAdapter's 0.124. On NSD-Imagery mental imagery trials, NeuroAdapter outperforms MindEye1 and Brain Diffuser on CLIP (60.04% vs. 54.72% and 54.07%) and Incep (57.26% vs. 52.03% and 52.73%), suggesting better generalization to non-visual stimuli. Cortex2Image (Gu et al., 2024) and Takagi et al. (2023) report only partial metric coverage, limiting direct comparison, but NeuroAdapter exceeds both on the overlapping high-level metrics. The paper positions its contribution not as state-of-the-art reconstruction quality but as demonstrating that competitive decoding is achievable without intermediate embedding spaces, enabling the IBBI interpretability framework.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant two-stage brain-decoding pipeline (brain → intermediate CLIP/DINO embedding → generative model) by directly conditioning a latent diffusion model on parcellated fMRI activity via an IP-Adapter-style cross-attention module, eliminating the intermediate feature bottleneck. It builds on the IP-Adapter architecture (Ye et al., 2023) for image-prompt conditioning and adapts the min-SNR weighting strategy (Hang et al., 2023) for stable diffusion training. The IBBI interpretability framework extends cross-attention analysis techniques from text-to-image diffusion (Tang et al., 2023b) to the brain-decoding setting, providing a bi-directional view of how cortical parcels influence spatial image regions across denoising steps.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuine gap in brain-decoding interpretability: two-stage pipelines obscure which brain regions drive reconstruction, while NeuroAdapter's direct conditioning enables parcel-level causal perturbation and attention-based attribution. The finding that masking high-level ROIs (Face, Body, Scene, Word) completely alters the generated image while masking low-level ROIs (V1–V4) preserves semantic content provides a clean neuroscientific validation of the model's use of hierarchical visual information. However, the notably weaker low-level metrics (PixCorr, SSIM) compared to Brain Diffuser and MindEye1, combined with the authors' own observation that current benchmarks may be approaching saturation, suggest that interpretability gains come at a measurable reconstruction-quality cost. Open questions include whether the direct-conditioning approach can close the low-level gap without sacrificing transparency, and whether IBBI-style analysis generalizes to other modalities (EEG, MEG) or to non-visual decoding tasks.
