## Problem

Existing datasets for generalist computer-use agents rely heavily on human annotation, making them expensive and unscalable, while current synthetic data pipelines struggle to generate reliable trajectories for complex, long-horizon tasks and produce limited task diversity. AgentSynth addresses this gap by exploiting information asymmetry—solving a task step-by-step in the forward direction is far easier than reasoning out the entire solution at once—to synthesize tasks that are simple to generate but substantially harder to solve at test time.

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes a randomly sampled persona description and an initial 1920×1080 Ubuntu desktop screenshot as seed inputs, then iteratively consumes task descriptions, action histories, and full-screen screenshots (1k–2k tokens per image) at each subtask step. It produces synthetic task descriptions at six difficulty levels (by summarizing the first 1 through 6 subtasks into a single instruction), paired trajectory datasets consisting of pyautogui action sequences with corresponding screenshots, and verifier labels (binary success/failure plus a graded completion percentage). Screenshots are downsampled to 960×480 during verification to reduce token cost, and each trajectory is capped at 10 steps per subtask.

## Method

AgentSynth is a six-agent LLM pipeline operating in the OSWorld simulated desktop environment. A task proposer generates an initial simple subtask guided by a persona; a ReAct-style executor (GPT-4.1 as planner + computer-use-preview for pixel-level visual grounding) executes it; a WebJudge-style verifier (key requirement extraction → selective screenshot sampling → final judgement) checks completion; a reviser rewrites the task description for partial successes; and a follow-up proposer generates the next subtask building on the prior state. The key mechanism is the task summarizer, which merges the sequence of completed subtasks into a single coherent long-horizon instruction, with difficulty controlled by varying how many subtasks are included (1–6). This decouples generation reliability (each subtask is trivially solvable) from evaluation difficulty (the composed task requires multi-step planning, cross-app coordination, and long-range memory), yielding over 6,000 tasks at $0.60 per trajectory.

## Results

On the AgentSynth benchmark, state-of-the-art LLM agents show a steep performance decline with difficulty: o4-mini drops from 18% success at level 1 to just 4% at levels 5–6, while GPT-4.1 fails entirely beyond level 3. Humans achieve 70% success even on the hardest level-6 tasks, highlighting a large performance gap. The pipeline generates 6,000+ tasks at a cost of $0.60 per trajectory (5 follow-up subtasks), orders of magnitude cheaper than human-annotated benchmarks. The information-asymmetry mechanism is validated by Table 5: AgentSynth maintains 52–65% generation success across difficulty levels while evaluation success drops from 62% to 14%, whereas direct instruction generation collapses to 11% on hard tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against existing human-annotated benchmarks (τ-bench, OSWorld, TheAgentCompany), AgentSynth is the only method that is simultaneously multi-domain, scalable, and long-horizon, while reducing cost from $4–$425 per task to $0.60 per trajectory. In the agent scaffolding comparison, adding Agent S3 (Gonzalez-Pumariega et al., 2025) roughly doubles success for weaker backbones (e.g., o4-mini on level 1) and GPT-5.1+S3 achieves the highest absolute performance, yet all configurations still decline sharply with difficulty, confirming the benchmark's discriminative power. Agent S2.5 (Agashe et al., 2025) yields improvements similar to S3 with a comparable difficulty-driven decline, indicating the results are robust to scaffold design. In the asymmetry ablation (Table 5), AgentSynth's evaluation success (62%→14% from level 1 to 6) drops far more steeply than direct instruction (60%→48%), while maintaining much higher generation reliability (52% vs. 11% on hard tasks), demonstrating that the subtask-chaining approach is essential for producing both solvable trajectories and genuinely challenging composite tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

AgentSynth builds on the OSWorld environment for realistic desktop interaction and borrows the information-asymmetry principle from AutoBench (Li et al., 2025), but applies it in a fundamentally different way: rather than optimizing a data generator against a fixed evaluator, it exploits the asymmetry between forward step-by-step execution and holistic task solving to chain simple subtasks into hard composite tasks. This contrasts with OS-Genesis and Learn-by-interact, which retroactively define tasks over already-collected trajectories, and with Evol-Instruct, which generates only the final instruction alongside a trajectory. The pipeline also draws on ReAct-style execution (Yao et al., 2023b), a two-stage planner/grounding architecture using GPT-4.1 and computer-use-preview, and a WebJudge-inspired verification architecture (Xue et al., 2025) for token-efficient trajectory assessment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

AgentSynth provides a practical, low-cost ($0.60/trajectory) mechanism for generating controllable-difficulty long-horizon computer-use tasks at scale, addressing the scalability bottleneck that has limited prior benchmarks to a few hundred human-annotated tasks. The demonstrated steep performance cliff (18% → 4% from level 1 to 6) and the clean decoupling between generation reliability and evaluation difficulty (Table 5) validate the information-asymmetry design as a general principle for benchmark construction. Open questions include how the choice of generator model shapes task distribution and difficulty calibration, whether the benchmark will saturate as agents improve, and whether the approach transfers cleanly to non-OSWorld environments beyond the preliminary InSTA results.
