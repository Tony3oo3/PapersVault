## Problem

Current LLMs generate experimental protocols that are incomplete, logically disordered, or contain hallucinated actions, yet existing evaluation metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore) only capture surface-level lexical overlap and cannot verify whether a protocol is actually executable. Additionally, no large-scale dataset covers both protocol comprehension and problem-solving tasks across diverse biological subfields, leaving a gap in training data and benchmarking for protocol generation.

## Input / Output

The model takes a natural language query describing an experimental task (e.g., planning a procedure, troubleshooting a failure, or adapting a protocol under constraints) and produces a structured output following the 'Sketch-and-Fill' format: a <think> reasoning block, a <key> block of atomic JSON steps (each a single {action, objects, parameters} dictionary), an <orc> block expanding those steps into fluent natural language with strict one-to-one step correspondence, and a <note> block for safety precautions. The one-action-per-step constraint and the 95% token-coverage consistency gate between <key> and <orc> enforce that the output is both machine-parseable and human-executable.

## Method

The core contribution is the SCORE reward mechanism, which replaces superficial text-similarity rewards with a structured, multi-dimensional signal: a format gate and consistency gate (binary pass/fail), a step-scale reward using cosine decay on step-count mismatch plus a verbosity penalty, and a step-semantics reward combining strict-subsequence order consistency with action-anchored object/parameter IoU scored under a positional decay factor. This reward drives RL training via GRPO on a Qwen3-8B base model. The training follows a three-stage Knowledge-to-Action curriculum: (1) pre-training on large-scale protocol texts, (2) SFT on SciRecipe data formatted in the Sketch-and-Fill paradigm, and (3) full-parameter RL with SCORE as the reward signal. The 'Sketch-and-Fill' paradigm itself decomposes generation into reasoning → atomic structuring → natural-language expression, making each stage independently verifiable and enabling the structured reward to operate on parseable intermediate representations rather than free-form text.

## Results

On SciRecipe-Eval, Thoth (8B) achieves SOTA across all executability and lexical metrics: 46.60% Semantic-A, 75.34% Order-LCS, 25.50% Order-S, 53.00% Step-M, and 52.10% AVG, outperforming the strongest open-source baseline DeepSeek-V3 (48.16% AVG) by 3.94% on average. Thoth-mini (4B) also surpasses all proprietary and open-source models, reaching 51.10% AVG. On broader scientific benchmarks (HLE, LAB-Bench, PubMedQA), Thoth outperforms domain-specific models Intern-S1 and SciDFM by 7.09% and 2.22% on average, respectively, while improving over its own Qwen3-8B base by 10.87%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Thoth (8B) wins on every metric against all 16 baselines. Against the strongest open-source model DeepSeek-V3 (48.16% AVG), Thoth gains +4.88% on Semantic-A (46.60 vs 41.72), +4.06% on Order-S (25.50 vs 21.44), and +11.29% on Step-M (53.00 vs 41.71), indicating substantially better step alignment and logical sequencing. Against the strongest proprietary model ChatGPT-4o (48.41% AVG), Thoth leads by +3.69% on average, with particularly large margins on Step-M (+9.00) and Semantic-A (+6.56). Reasoning-oriented models (Grok 4, OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1) perform notably worse on executability metrics, often producing overly verbose or misordered protocols; Thoth outperforms Grok 4 by +7.16% on AVG and OpenAI-o1 by +7.05%. On broader scientific benchmarks (HLE, LAB-Bench, PubMedQA), Thoth surpasses domain-specific models Intern-S1 by +7.09% and SciDFM by +2.22% on average, confirming that protocol-generation training generalizes to wider biomedical reasoning tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of structured reasoning and reward design for LLM-based protocol generation. It departs from prior work like BioPlanner (pseudocode evaluation) and ProtocolLM (domain-specific formulation) by targeting natural-language, experimentally executable protocols with verifiable structure. The Sketch-and-Fill paradigm repurposes the decomposition idea from chain-of-thought reasoning into a domain-specific three-stage output (reason → structured key → natural language), while the SCORE mechanism replaces the expensive LLM-as-a-judge paradigm with a rule-based, component-wise reward that is both efficient for RL and interpretable. The training pipeline (pre-training → SFT → GRPO-based RL) follows the standard curriculum-learning recipe but is the first to apply it end-to-end to bioexperimental protocol generation with a structured reward.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Reliable, executable protocol generation is a bottleneck for scientific reproducibility, and this paper provides both a large-scale dataset (SciRecipe, 12K+ protocols across 27 subfields) and a principled evaluation framework (SCORE) that the field has lacked. The finding that general-purpose reasoning models systematically over-generate steps (e.g., Grok 4 expanding a 4-step task into 13) while a domain-adapted 8B model produces concise, correct protocols challenges the assumption that more reasoning always helps structured generation tasks. Open questions include whether SCORE generalizes beyond biology, whether cross-step dependencies and long-range experimental context can be captured, and whether the framework holds under the variability of real laboratory settings rather than curated benchmarks.
