## Problem

Existing expert merging strategies for Sparse Mixture of Experts (SMoE) rely on heuristic weighting schemes—input-dependent or input-independent averaging—that ignore the cooperative and competitive dynamics among experts. The paper asks whether expert merging can be reinterpreted as a structured game-theoretic interaction, yielding a principled weighting mechanism that accounts for both alignment and divergence between expert contributions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265023/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At each SMoE layer, the method consumes the N expert parameter tensors {E_1, …, E_N}, the shared base expert E_m, and the routing weights s_i to compute domain vectors τ_i = E_i − E_m. It solves the Nash system G^T G α = 1/α (where G stacks the domain vectors) to obtain per-expert coefficients α_i, then produces an updated base expert E_m^(l+1) that replaces all N experts for downstream processing. In the momentum variant, a complex-valued buffer μ ∈ C^d accumulates directional updates across layers, and only the real part Re(γμ) is applied to the real-valued parameters.

## Method

NAMEx frames expert merging as a Nash Bargaining game: each expert's domain vector τ_i serves as a utility function u_i(ΔE) = τ_i^T ΔE, and the merged update direction ΔE = Σ α_i τ_i is the Nash solution satisfying G^T G α = 1/α, which automatically down-weights cooperative experts (positive cross-term Σ_{i≠j} α_i τ_i^T τ_j) and up-weights adversarial ones. To address the slow convergence of layer-by-layer propagation (the key weakness of EP-CAMEx), NAMEx-Momentum introduces a complex momentum buffer μ^(j+1) = βμ^(j) + ΔE^(j) with β ∈ C, applying only Re(γμ^(j+1)) to the base expert; a spectral-radius bound on the resulting 3×3 block matrix R guarantees linear convergence O((ρ(R)+ε)^l) when ρ(R) < 1. The full update at layer l is E_m^(l+1) = E_m^(l) + γ Σ α_i^(l) τ_i^(l), followed by the standard CAMEx input-dependent step Ê_m^(l+1) = E_m^(l+1) + η Σ M_i (s_i * τ_i). The paper also demonstrates that quaternion momentum (β ∈ H) further improves performance, suggesting richer multi-buffer dynamics as a future direction.

## Results

On WikiText-103 (medium-scale), NAMEx-Full-Mom achieves the lowest test perplexity of 35.37, outperforming SMoE Top-2 (35.55), CAMEx (36.53), and EP-CAMEx (36.55). On GLUE (T5-base, 1.0B params), NAMEx-Full-Mom leads on all seven tasks, reaching 95.06 on SST-2, 93.27 on MRPC, and 60.13 on CoLA. On ImageNet-1k (Swin-MoE, 50M), NAMEx-Full-Mom achieves 84.52% top-1 accuracy and 35.27% on ImageNet-A (adversarial), the strongest zero-shot robustness among all variants. At scale, NAMEx-Full on DeepSeek-MoE (16B) improves MMLU from 44.77 to 44.92 (Linear routing) and from 45.80 to 45.95 (Stable-MoE routing) in zero-shot settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NAMEx-Full-Mom consistently outperforms all baselines across every benchmark. Against SMoE (Top-1/Top-2) [Shazeer et al., 2017], NAMEx-Full-Mom improves WikiText-103 medium test PPL from 35.55 to 35.37 and GLUE SST-2 from 94.35 to 95.06. Against SMEAR [Muqeeth et al., 2024], the gains are larger: ImageNet-1k top-1 accuracy rises from 83.15 to 84.52 and ImageNet-A from 24.14 to 35.27. Against CAMEx [Nguyen et al., 2025], NAMEx-Full-Mom improves CoLA from 58.57 to 60.13 and RTE from 74.72 to 78.15. Against EP-CAMEx (the dynamic variant of CAMEx), NAMEx-Full-Mom wins on all tasks, with the largest gap on RTE (78.15 vs. 75.81 without momentum) and ImageNet-A (35.27 vs. 24.22). At scale, NAMEx-Full outperforms the raw DeepSeek-MoE (16B) [Liu et al., 2024a] by +0.15 on MMLU and +0.20 on GSM8K under Linear routing, and outperforms Qwen1.5-MoE (14B) [Yang et al., 2024] by +0.62 on MMLU and +0.38 on GSM8K under Stable-MoE routing. The dense T5-base (220M) [Raffel et al., 2020] is surpassed by all SMoE variants, confirming that the sparsity-plus-merging paradigm is effective even at 1.0B parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on CAMEx (Nguyen et al., 2025), which introduced curvature-aware expert merging via natural gradients, and its expert-propagation variant EP-CAMEx, which propagates a base expert across layers. The key conceptual departure is reframing the propagation step as a Nash Bargaining game among experts, drawing on the multi-task learning-as-bargaining framework of Navon et al. (2022), where domain vectors play the role of task gradients. The authors show that EP-CAMEx's uniform averaging of domain vectors is a 'trivial solution' of the Nash system that ignores inter-expert interactions, and they further integrate complex momentum (Lorraine et al., 2022) to address EP-CAMEx's slow convergence, providing spectral-radius-based convergence guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

NAMEx provides the first principled, game-theoretic weighting mechanism for expert merging in SMoE, replacing heuristic averaging with a Pareto-optimal solution that explicitly accounts for cooperative and adversarial dynamics among experts. The theoretical convergence guarantees and the demonstration of scalability to 14B–16B models (Qwen1.5-MoE, DeepSeek-MoE) make this a practical drop-in improvement for existing MoE pipelines. Open questions include the computational cost of solving the Nash equilibrium per token (limiting token-level application), whether quaternion momentum can be tuned more systematically, and whether the bargaining framework extends to non-MLP expert architectures or to settings with heterogeneous expert sizes.
