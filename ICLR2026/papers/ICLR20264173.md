## Problem

Higher-resolution images improve fine-grained perception in MLLMs but introduce substantial computational overhead (multiple forward passes, token compression, resamplers) and design complexity. The paper asks whether a model's full perception capability at a standard medium resolution (e.g., 512²px) has been fully unlocked before simply scaling up input size, and how to achieve fine-grained visual perception under the same inference budget without larger images.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264173/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the model takes a single 512×512 RGB image and produces 32×32 visual tokens — identical in format and count to the original SigLIP 2-So400m/16-512px encoder, making it a direct drop-in replacement. During training, the frozen teacher branch additionally consumes a 1024×1024 version of the same image (split into four non-overlapping 512² tiles), but this multi-scale input is never needed at deployment time. No projection heads, resamplers, or auxiliary upsamplers are introduced, so the I/O contract remains unchanged from the pre-trained model.

## Method

The core mechanism is a fine-to-coarse supervision loop: a frozen pre-trained SigLIP 2 encoder is run on both a 512² base-scale image and a 1024² high-resolution image (via non-overlapping sliding window), and the resulting feature maps are bilinearly interpolated to the same spatial size and averaged to form a high-quality teacher target F^t. A student encoder (SigLIP-HD), initialized from the same pre-trained weights and sharing the exact same architecture, is trained to match F^t at the patch level using a plain L1 loss on 4.5M raw images from Cambrian-1. The key insight is that the multi-scale ensembled features encode fine-grained detail that a single 512² forward pass cannot capture, yet this information can be distilled into the standard-resolution representation without any architectural modification. Training takes only 34 hours on 32 A100 GPUs (90K iterations, batch size 512, AdamW with cosine schedule), and the resulting encoder is validated as a plug-in improvement across LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, and AnyRes protocols with multiple LLM backbones.

## Results

Under the LLaVA-NeXT training protocol with an unfrozen vision encoder, SigLIP-HD achieves an average score of 64.4 across 13 MLLM benchmarks, outperforming the SigLIP 2 baseline (62.8) by +1.6. The most notable gains appear on fine-grained perception tasks: DocVQA improves from 56.0 to 59.6 (+3.6), ChartQA from 61.6 to 65.2 (+3.6), and HRBench from 43.5 to 48.3 (+4.8). Under the AnyRes (native-resolution) strategy, SigLIP-HD further extends its lead, improving ChartQA from 63.9 to 67.4 (+3.5) and DocVQA from 67.6 to 69.7 (+2.1). Training requires only 34 hours on 32 A100 GPUs with 4.5M raw images from Cambrian-1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary baseline SigLIP 2-So400m/16-512px (Tschannen et al., 2025), SigLIP-HD wins across all four training configurations (LLaVA-1.5/NeXT × frozen/unfrozen), with average improvements ranging from +1.1 to +1.6. The largest margins appear on OCR and fine-grained perception benchmarks: DocVQA gains +3.6 (56.0→59.6) and HRBench gains +4.8 (43.5→48.3) under the LLaVA-NeXT unfrozen setting. Against the legacy OpenAI CLIP-L/14-336px (Radford et al., 2021), the paper validates generality: even when CLIP is given multi-scale input (336²+672²) to match token count, CLIP-HD at 518² still outperforms it, e.g., DocVQA 22.4 (single-scale CLIP) → 31.1 (multi-scale CLIP) → 33.2 (CLIP-HD). The method also generalizes across LLM backbones, beating SigLIP 2 by +4.6 on ChartQA with Llama-3.2-3B and +1.7 on DocVQA with Qwen2.5-7B. No comparison shows SigLIP-HD losing to its respective baseline on any reported benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against the dominant trend in MLLMs of simply increasing input resolution (LLaVA-NeXT, AnyRes, Qwen2.5-VL) to obtain finer visual tokens, arguing that this introduces unnecessary computational and design complexity. Its core mechanism—fine-to-coarse supervision where a frozen multi-scale teacher produces target features for a standard-resolution student—is conceptually rooted in knowledge distillation (Hinton et al., 2015) but departs from it by using the same encoder at different resolutions as the teacher rather than a separate model, eliminating the need for cross-model alignment. It builds directly on SigLIP 2 (Tschannen et al., 2025) as the base encoder and validates generality on OpenAI CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), while distinguishing itself from multi-encoder aggregation approaches (Cambrian-1, Eagle) that combine heterogeneous encoders.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical value is in its extreme simplicity: a drop-in encoder replacement requiring no architectural changes, no auxiliary modules, no extra inference passes, and only 34 GPU-hours of training, making it accessible to resource-constrained groups. However, the gains are modest (typically 1–4 points), and the paper acknowledges it does not fully replace native-resolution strategies for extremely fine details. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond ViT-based encoders, whether more sophisticated fusion or curriculum strategies could yield larger gains, and how it interacts with emerging encoder-free MLLM paradigms that may bypass the vision encoder entirely.
