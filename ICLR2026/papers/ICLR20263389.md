## Problem

Offline multi-agent reinforcement learning faces a fundamental trade-off: diffusion-based generative policies capture the complex multi-modal joint action distributions needed for coordination but require iterative denoising at inference (O(K) steps), while Gaussian policies are fast but brittle and fail to model multi-agent interaction patterns. The paper asks how to preserve the expressiveness of generative joint policies while achieving the O(1) per-agent inference speed of standard actor-critic methods.

## Input / Output

Each agent receives its local observation o_i (partial observability under Dec-POMDP) and a noise sample z_i ~ N(0, I) as input; the joint flow policy additionally conditions on the full observation vector o during training. For discrete-action benchmarks (SMACv1/v2), actions are represented as one-hot vectors over the simplex; for continuous-action benchmarks (MA-MuJoCo, MPE), actions are real-valued vectors. At deployment, each agent produces its action via a single forward pass of its one-step policy network μ_{w_i}(o_i, z_i), requiring no inter-agent communication or iterative sampling.

## Method

MAC-Flow uses a two-stage strategy that decouples expressiveness from optimization. Stage 1 trains a flow-based joint policy v_φ(t, o, x) via behavioral cloning with linear-interpolation flow matching, learning a velocity field that maps Gaussian noise to the multi-modal joint action distribution in the offline dataset. Stage 2 distills this joint policy into decentralized one-step sampling policies {μ_{w_i}(o_i, z_i)} by jointly optimizing a Q-maximization objective (under the IGM principle, so individual Q_i maximization is consistent with global Q_tot maximization) and a BC distillation loss that aligns each agent's output with the corresponding subvector of the joint policy. Theoretical guarantees are provided: Proposition 4.2 upper-bounds the 2-Wasserstein distance between the joint and factorized policies by the square root of the distillation loss, and Proposition 4.3 further bounds the value gap by L_Q · W_2, where L_Q is the Lipschitz constant of the Q-function. All networks are simple [512,512,512,512] MLPs with layer normalization, and the Euler method with 10 steps is used only during training for TD backup sampling, not at deployment.

## Results

MAC-Flow achieves the best or second-best average performance across all four benchmarks: 15.6 on SMACv1 (tied with DoF), 13.1 on SMACv2, 2749.4 on MA-MuJoCo, and 65.8 on MPE. In terms of inference speed, MAC-Flow achieves a 14.5× speedup over diffusion-based methods (DoF, MADiff) while maintaining comparable performance, with per-agent inference complexity of O(1) versus O(K) for DoF and O(IK) for MADiff-D. Its inference speed is on par with Gaussian-policy baselines (e.g., MACQL, MATD3BC), which are significantly slower in performance (e.g., 13.1 vs. 15.6 on SMACv1).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest diffusion baseline DoF, MAC-Flow ties on SMACv1 (both 15.6 average) but trails slightly on SMACv2 (13.1 vs. 14.0), while delivering a 14.5× inference speedup. Against MADiff, MAC-Flow wins on all four benchmarks: SMACv1 (15.6 vs. 13.8), SMACv2 (13.1 vs. 12.4), MA-MuJoCo (2749.4 vs. 2430.2), and MPE (65.8 vs. 49.2). Against Gaussian-policy baselines, MAC-Flow substantially outperforms MACQL on SMACv1 (15.6 vs. 13.1) and MPE (65.8 vs. 44.1), and MATD3BC on MA-MuJoCo (2749.4 vs. 2293.7) and MPE (65.8 vs. 40.7). The weaker baselines MABCQ (BCQ extension) and ICQ/OMAR/OMIGA are consistently outperformed by large margins across all benchmarks. Overall, MAC-Flow matches or exceeds the best diffusion method in performance while matching the inference speed of the fastest Gaussian-policy methods, effectively eliminating the expressiveness–efficiency trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAC-Flow extends the single-agent flow Q-learning paradigm (Park et al., 2025) to the multi-agent setting by replacing per-agent flow policies with a joint flow-matching policy that explicitly models inter-agent dependencies in the action distribution. It integrates the IGM value-decomposition principle (Son et al., 2019) into the distillation objective, ensuring that factorized one-step policies remain aligned with the global Q-function. Conceptually, it sits between diffusion-based MARL (MADiff, DoF), which capture multi-modality but require O(K) iterative denoising, and Gaussian-policy offline MARL (ICQ, OMAR, OMIGA), which are fast but fail on multi-modal joint distributions. The shortcut-flow distillation idea (Frans et al., 2024) motivates the one-step extraction, but the key departure is the structural factorization of a *joint* flow policy into decentralized per-agent policies with Wasserstein-based performance guarantees.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 14.5× inference speedup at comparable performance directly addresses a deployment bottleneck in offline MARL, making generative-policy-based coordination viable for time-critical applications like autonomous driving and modular robotics. The theoretical guarantees (Wasserstein upper bound on distillation error, Lipschitz value-gap bound) provide a principled understanding of how much coordination quality is lost during factorization, which is absent in prior heuristic distillation approaches. Open questions include whether the approach scales to highly heterogeneous or dynamically changing agent populations, whether integrating multiple pre-trained flow distributions could improve role adaptability, and whether the IGM assumption holds in non-fully-cooperative settings.
