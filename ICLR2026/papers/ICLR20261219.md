## Problem

Open-weight LLMs are vulnerable to adversarial fine-tuning attacks that efficiently elicit harmful capabilities, yet existing post-training safeguards (Circuit Breaking, latent adversarial training) can be undone within dozens to a few hundred fine-tuning steps. This paper investigates whether filtering dual-use content from pretraining data can produce more durable, tamper-resistant safeguards that are harder for downstream actors to reverse.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261219/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The filtering pipeline consumes raw pretraining documents (hundreds of millions from the DCLM corpus, 500B tokens pretraining + 50B annealing) and produces a filtered training corpus by removing documents flagged as containing biothreat proxy knowledge. The resulting 6.9B-parameter Pythia-architecture models take text questions in multiple-choice or cloze format and produce text answers, evaluated on WMDP-Bio proxy knowledge (lower is better) and general knowledge benchmarks like MMLU, PIQA, LAMBADA, and HellaSwag (higher is better).

## Method

The core contribution is a scalable multi-stage data filtering pipeline applied during pretraining: a keyword blocklist (generated by Llama 3.3 70B from WMDP-Bio Forget documents) flags documents containing two or more blocked terms, which are then escalated to a fine-tuned ModernBERT-Large classifier for semantic review. The authors train 6.9B Pythia-architecture models from scratch on 550B tokens under strong (single-stage blocklist only) and weak (two-stage blocklist + ModernBERT) filtering configurations, showing that filtered models resist up to 10,000 steps and 300M tokens of adversarial fine-tuning on biothreat text—over an order of magnitude more than post-training baselines—while incurring less than 1% additional FLOPS. They further demonstrate that data filtering and Circuit Breaking are complementary: filtering prevents internalized dangerous knowledge, while CB prevents the model from leveraging harmful information provided in-context via retrieval augmentation.

## Results

On WMDP-Bio biothreat proxy knowledge (lower is better), the strong filter (single-stage blocklist) reduces Robust MCQA accuracy from 42.97% (unfiltered baseline) to 35.37% and Cloze accuracy from 36.34% to 24.44%, while the weak filter (blocklist + ModernBERT) achieves 35.25% MCQA and 25.74% Cloze. General knowledge is largely preserved: MMLU drops by <2 points (44.92% → 43.21–44.82%), PIQA stays within 1 point, and the average general benchmark score remains within ~1 point of the unfiltered baseline. Critically, filtered models resist up to 10,000 steps and 300M tokens of adversarial fine-tuning on WMDP-Bio Forget data, outperforming post-training baselines (Circuit Breaking, CB+LAT) by over an order of magnitude in tamper resistance, with the entire filtering pipeline costing <1% of total training FLOPS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Circuit Breaking (Zou et al., 2024) and CB+LAT (Sheshadri et al., 2024), the primary post-training baselines, data filtering is competitive on black-box evaluations: CB slightly outperforms filtering on MCQA biothreat proxy scores, while filtering slightly outperforms CB on cloze-style evaluations. However, on tamper resistance the gap is dramatic: filtered models resist 10,000 steps / 300M tokens of adversarial fine-tuning, whereas CB and CB+LAT break down within a few hundred steps (an order-of-magnitude difference). Against prior unlearning and tamper-resistance works (Che et al. 2025; Lucki et al. 2024; Qi et al. 2024b; Deeb & Roger 2024; Hu et al. 2024; Fan et al. 2025; Qian et al. 2025; Muhamed et al. 2025; Tamirisa et al. 2024), all of which are undone within dozens to hundreds of fine-tuning steps, filtering achieves the largest tested resistance by a wide margin. The paper also shows that filtering and CB are complementary: CB protects against in-context retrieval attacks that filtering cannot, and combining both yields the strongest overall defense. Notably, filtering for precise scientific knowledge (biothreat proxy) succeeds where filtering for toxic/harmful content (Maini et al. 2025; Li et al. 2025a) does not consistently improve robustness, suggesting the approach works for knowledge but not for propensities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm of post-training safeguards (Circuit Breaking, Latent Adversarial Training, representation noising) for open-weight model safety by shifting the intervention to pretraining data curation. While data filtering has long been used for quality control and concurrent works (Maini et al. 2025, Li et al. 2025a, Lee et al. 2025) have explored filtering for safety at smaller scales, no prior work demonstrated that pretraining data filtering could achieve state-of-the-art tamper resistance against adversarial fine-tuning. The paper also builds on the machine unlearning literature's recognition that post-training methods struggle to durably remove knowledge, repurposing the simpler idea of 'never learning it' as a practical safety mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The order-of-magnitude improvement in tamper resistance (10K steps / 300M tokens vs. dozens of steps for post-training baselines) provides a concrete, low-overhead (<1% FLOPS) tool for open-weight model risk management, and the released 6.9B model suite enables further interpretability and unlearning research. However, the approach has clear boundaries: it cannot prevent in-context retrieval of harmful information (requiring complementary defenses like Circuit Breaking), it appears ineffective for suppressing propensities like toxicity (only precise knowledge), and the absolute resistance level remains modest in practical terms. Key open questions include whether the effect scales to larger models, whether it generalizes beyond biothreat proxy knowledge to genuinely infohazardous content, and how to defend against the ensemble fine-tuning + retrieval attacks that defeat all tested safeguards.
