## Problem

While jailbreak attacks have been extensively studied for LLMs, VLMs, and Text-to-Image models, Text-to-Video (T2V) models remain a largely unexplored safety frontier. Existing T2V safety filters operate on individual prompts or single-frame visual content, leaving them vulnerable to attacks that exploit the temporal narrative structure inherent to video generation—specifically, the fact that a sequence of individually benign scenes can collectively reconstruct a harmful narrative.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262001/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single harmful text prompt (sourced from T2VSafetyBench across 11 safety categories) and produces a generated video that conveys the original harmful intent while evading both pre-processing text safety filters and post-processing video safety filters. The input prompt is transformed into a structured sequence of 2–5 scene descriptions (each under ~256 tokens total), which is fed to the T2V model as a multi-scene prompt. The attack is fully black-box: it requires only API access to the target T2V model, with auxiliary LLMs (GPT-4o for splitting/manipulation, Qwen30B for strategy summarization) and a video understanding model (VideoLLaMA3) used externally for scene selection and feedback.

## Method

SceneSplit exploits a structural vulnerability in T2V safety mechanisms by fragmenting a harmful narrative into multiple individually benign scenes whose sequential combination constrains the model's generative output space into an unsafe region. The method comprises three components: (1) Scene Splitting, which divides the prompt into 2–5 scenes via procedural decomposition and paraphrases each into low-harmfulness language, lowering the prompt's measurable direct harmfulness (verified via OpenAI Moderation API) while preserving narrative intent; (2) Scene Manipulation, which iteratively identifies the most influential scene using VideoLLaMA3 and performs bi-directional modification (increasing explicitness if the video is too safe, decreasing it if blocked) to search for the optimal attack point within the constrained output space; and (3) Strategy Update, which maintains a growing library of successful attack patterns indexed by prompt embeddings (all-MiniLM-L6v2), retrieving semantically similar strategies via cosine similarity (threshold λ=0.6) to guide future scene splits and improve robustness. The key insight is that each scene individually corresponds to a wide, safe generative output space, but their mutual constraints as a sequence drastically narrow this space, making the combined prompt far more likely to yield an unsafe video than any single scene alone.

## Results

SceneSplit achieves average Attack Success Rates (ASR) of 77.2% on Luma Ray2, 84.1% on Hailuo, 78.2% on Veo2, 78.6% on Kling V1.0, and 68.6% on Sora2, evaluated across 11 safety categories (220 prompts) from T2VSafetyBench. These results substantially outperform both the T2VSafetyBench original-prompt baseline and the RPG-RT baseline across all five commercial T2V models. The method uses a maximum of 15 attack attempts per prompt (I=5 inner iterations × T=3 outer iterations).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SceneSplit is compared against two baselines: (1) T2VSafetyBench original prompts (no attack, measuring the model's inherent vulnerability) and (2) RPG-RT (Cao et al., 2025), a rule-based preference modeling attack originally designed for T2I models. Against the T2VSafetyBench baseline, SceneSplit more than doubles the ASR on Veo2 (78.2% vs. 33.1%) and Hailuo (84.1% vs. 40.9%), demonstrating that the scene-splitting strategy exploits a structural vulnerability not captured by direct harmful prompts. Against RPG-RT, SceneSplit achieves significantly higher ASR across all models; notably, RPG-RT was specifically fine-tuned on Veo2 for this comparison and its maximum modification budget was set to 15 to match SceneSplit's iteration budget, yet SceneSplit still outperforms it. The paper emphasizes that SceneSplit's advantage is generalizable across all 11 safety categories rather than relying on category-specific weaknesses.

## Relation to Prior Work

SceneSplit extends the Strategy Library concept from AutoDAN-Turbo (Liu et al., 2025), which automatically discovers and reuses jailbreak strategies for LLMs, to the T2V domain by collecting scene-splitting patterns that constrain the generative output space. It draws on T2VSafetyBench (Miao et al., 2024) for evaluation and shares a conceptual link with its 'Temporal Risks' category, but goes further by actively exploiting the temporal combination of individually safe scenes as an attack mechanism rather than merely benchmarking it. The method departs from prior T2I jailbreak approaches (e.g., RPG-RT) by leveraging the sequential, multi-scene structure unique to video generation, where each scene prompt is individually benign but their combination narrows the output space into an unsafe region.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reveals a structural vulnerability in current T2V safety filters: they evaluate individual prompts or frames in isolation but fail to assess the combined narrative context across sequentially generated scenes, enabling high ASR (68–84%) across five major commercial models. This finding underscores the need for safety mechanisms that understand inter-scene context and temporal narrative construction, a capability absent in current deployments. A noted limitation is reduced effectiveness for identity-specific content (e.g., public figures, copyright), where splitting dilutes distinguishing features. Open questions include whether the proposed scene-level contextual defense can be implemented efficiently at inference time and whether the vulnerability persists as T2V models adopt more holistic narrative understanding.
