## Problem

Extending the context of pretrained LLMs beyond their training sequence length has required expensive long-context finetuning, and existing RoPE frequency-scaling methods (PI, NTK-RoPE, YaRN) still fail to generalize zero-shot to longer sequences. The paper identifies the root cause: positional embeddings are essential inductive biases for training convergence but simultaneously prevent test-time generalization to unseen sequence lengths, creating an unresolved tension in transformer design.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained RoPE transformer checkpoint (e.g., SMOLLM-360M, SMOLLM-1.7B, LLaMA2-7B) and produces a NoPE transformer that can process text sequences up to 8× the original training context length. At inference time, a single learned softmax temperature scalar (β* = 1 + c·ln(s), where s is the extension factor) is applied to the attention logits to compensate for the absence of positional information. The recalibration phase uses the same context length, data, and hyperparameters as the original pretraining, requiring as little as 0.5% of the original token budget.

## Method

DroPE removes all rotary positional embeddings from every layer of a pretrained RoPE transformer and resumes training for a short recalibration phase at the original context length, after which the model generalizes zero-shot to longer sequences. The approach is grounded in two theoretical results: (1) NoPE transformers have bounded attention positional-bias gradients at initialization (proportional to embedding prefix-spread), explaining why explicit PEs accelerate convergence; and (2) any RoPE scaling method must compress low frequencies to keep phases in-distribution, which inevitably shifts semantic attention weights at long relative distances. By eliminating the positional mechanism entirely rather than rescaling it, DroPE avoids the frequency-compression artifact. For large models, QKNorm is added during recalibration to stabilize training at higher learning rates without changing model capacity.

## Results

On SMOLLM (362M params, 600B pretraining tokens), DroPE achieves an average LongBench score of 30.52, more than 10× the base SMOLLM (2.98) and substantially outperforming the best RoPE-scaling method YaRN (19.94) and RoPE-NTK (17.11). On needle-in-a-haystack retrieval, SMOLLM-DROPE scores 74.92% at 2× context, 55.00% at 4×, and 52.20% at 8× the original 2048-token context, versus 48.25/25.62/12.18% for YaRN. In-context performance is preserved: SMOLLM-DROPE matches or slightly exceeds the base model on six standard benchmarks (53.4 vs. 53.1 average accuracy). On larger models (SMOLLM-1.7B, LLaMA2-7B), DroPE with only 0.5–2% recalibration budget still outperforms YaRN and RoPE-NTK on LongBench tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DroPE wins decisively against all RoPE-scaling baselines. YaRN (Peng et al., 2023), the strongest RoPE-scaling method, trails DroPE by 10.6 points on average LongBench score (19.94 vs. 30.52) and by 26.7 points on NIAH at 2× context (48.25 vs. 74.92); the gap widens dramatically at 8× context (12.18 vs. 52.20). RoPE-NTK (bloc97, 2023) and PI (Chen et al., 2023) are further behind, with PI scoring 0% on NIAH entirely. LongRoPE2 (Ding et al., 2024) is the closest competitor on NIAH at 8× (16.45 vs. 52.20) but still lags by over 3×. On the from-scratch 500M model (Table 1), DroPE also outperforms specialized architectures: ALiBi (Press et al., 2021) scores 5.2/0.0/1.1 on multi-query/key/value NIAH vs. DroPE's 28.0/41.6/23.3, and NoPE (Kazemnejad et al., 2023) and RNoPE-SWA (Yang et al., 2025b) underperform on the logic-intensive multi-query task. Critically, DroPE matches the base SMOLLM's in-context performance (53.4 vs. 53.1 average across six benchmarks), confirming that removing positional embeddings does not degrade standard LM capabilities after brief recalibration.

## Relation to Prior Work

DroPE builds directly on RoPE transformers (Su et al., 2024) as its starting architecture but departs fundamentally from both the RoPE-scaling line (PI, NTK-RoPE, YaRN) that rescales frequencies at inference, and the NoPE line (Haviv et al., 2022; Kazemnejad et al., 2023) that trains without positional embeddings from scratch. The key conceptual departure is reinterpreting positional embeddings as a transient training inductive bias rather than a permanent architectural component: use them to accelerate convergence, then remove them before deployment. This positions DroPE as orthogonal to hybrid approaches like RNoPE-SWA (Yang et al., 2025b) and ALiBi (Press et al., 2021), which modify the attention mechanism itself, and complementary to them since any alternative positional scheme could in principle be dropped in the same way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DroPE offers a remarkably simple, near-zero-cost procedure (remove PEs, recalibrate on a small fraction of training tokens) that could be retrofitted onto any existing pretrained RoPE model, potentially eliminating the expensive long-context finetuning phase that currently gates context extension. The theoretical analysis bounding the rate of positional bias development in NoPE transformers provides a principled explanation for why PEs help training but hurt generalization. Key open questions include whether the approach scales to 70B+ parameter models, whether the test-time softmax temperature scaling is a fundamental requirement or an artifact of small-model recalibration, and whether the technique transfers to diffusion-based or multimodal LMs. The 8× context NIAH results (52.20 vs. 74.92 at 2×) suggest degradation at extreme extension factors, leaving the practical ceiling unclear.
