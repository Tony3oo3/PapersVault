## Problem

When 2D diffusion models are applied slice-by-slice for 3D medical reconstruction, the independent stochastic noise injected during each slice's reverse diffusion sampling produces severe inter-slice discontinuities along the z-axis. Existing remedies—post-hoc TV regularization or expensive 3D priors—introduce sensitive hyperparameters, over-smoothing artifacts, or prohibitive training costs, leaving a gap for a principled, zero-overhead fix that addresses the root cause within the sampling process itself.

## Input / Output

The method consumes undersampled CT projections (30 sparse views over 360° or 100 limited-angle views over [0°,100°] with cone-beam geometry) or a 5× z-downsampled MRI volume, together with a pre-trained 2D VE/ncsnpp diffusion prior, and outputs a full 3D volume (256×256×300 for CT, 256×256×256 at 1 mm isotropic for MRI). It operates as a drop-in modification to the re-noising step of any existing diffusion-based inverse solver (DDNM, DDS), requiring 30 NFEs for CT and 20 NFEs for MRI, with no architectural changes, retraining, or additional optimization loops.

## Method

ISCS identifies uncoordinated per-slice stochasticity in the diffusion re-noising step as the root cause of inter-slice inconsistency and replaces the independently sampled Gaussian noise with a smoothly correlated noise volume generated via Spherical Linear Interpolation (Slerp). Two anchor noise vectors z₁ and z_S are drawn i.i.d. from N(0, I), and each intermediate slice's noise is obtained by interpolating along the geodesic arc on the hypersphere between them, preserving the standard Gaussian marginal distribution (justified by the Gaussian Annulus Theorem) while enforcing strong local correlation that decays with slice distance. This correlated noise volume directly substitutes the stochastic term in the DDIM-style update, coupling adjacent slices' sampling trajectories without adding loss terms, hyperparameters, or computational cost. Theoretical analysis shows that in high dimensions the anchor angle concentrates near 90°, making the method insensitive to random seed choice, and that ISCS remains effective even under deterministic (η=0) sampling by correlating the initial noise volume.

## Results

On SVCT (30 views), DDS+ISCS achieves 36.97 dB PSNR / 0.937 SSIM / 0.064 LPIPS (Axial) and 38.16 dB / 0.942 / 0.065 (Sagittal), outperforming the DDS baseline by ~2.2 dB PSNR and halving LPIPS in the Sagittal view. On LACT ([0,100]°), DDS+ISCS reaches 31.65 dB / 0.911 / 0.071 (Axial), a ~2.6 dB gain over DDS. On MRI 5× isotropic SR, DDS+ISCS attains 40.33 dB / 0.968 / 0.019 (Axial) and 39.35 dB / 0.948 / 0.052 (Sagittal). The inter-slice consistency metric |Δ| is lowest for ISCS variants across all three tasks (e.g., 0.001835 for SVCT vs. 0.005588 for DDS), confirming that ISCS restores 3D coherence closest to ground truth without over-smoothing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the two primary diffusion-based solvers, ISCS delivers consistent gains: DDNM+ISCS improves over DDNM by ~1.4–2.5 dB PSNR and reduces Sagittal LPIPS from 0.200–0.235 to 0.076–0.106 across SVCT/LACT; DDS+ISCS improves over DDS by ~2.2 dB PSNR (SVCT) and ~2.6 dB (LACT) with LPIPS roughly halved in non-axial views. Compared to the TV-regularized variant (DDS+TV), ISCS matches or exceeds PSNR/SSIM while achieving substantially lower LPIPS (e.g., 0.065 vs. 0.088 Sagittal on SVCT) and a smaller |Δ|, indicating better preservation of fine detail without over-smoothing. Against the 3D-aware baselines TPDM (Lee et al., ICCV 2023) and DiffusionBlend (Song et al., NeurIPS 2024), DDS+ISCS narrows the gap to within ~0.6–1.3 dB PSNR on in-distribution data (Subject L506, 1 mm) and, critically, outperforms DiffusionBlend on out-of-distribution data (Subject L221, 5 mm slices) where the 3D prior's training distribution mismatch degrades performance. ISCS also preserves small lesions (DeepLesion) that TV regularization nearly obliterates, confirming that noise-space consistency does not suppress high-frequency pathological structures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the batch-consistent sampling (BCS) idea from Kwon & Ye (2025), which synchronized stochastic noise across video frames, to the 3D medical volume setting by replacing identical noise with Slerp-interpolated correlated noise on the hypersphere. It directly addresses the inter-slice inconsistency problem introduced by the slice-by-slice 2D-to-3D reconstruction framework of Chung et al. (2023), which is the standard workaround for the infeasibility of training 3D diffusion priors. Unlike post-hoc TV regularization or 3D-aware architectures (TPDM, DiffusionBlend), ISCS modifies only the re-noising step of existing diffusion samplers (DDNM, DDS) without architectural changes, additional loss terms, or retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ISCS offers a genuinely plug-and-play solution to a well-recognized practical problem in medical imaging: the inter-slice artifacts that plague 2D-prior-based 3D reconstruction. The finding that naive BCS (identical noise) produces copying artifacts in high-dimensional volumes with 300+ anatomically diverse slices, while Slerp-interpolated noise avoids this, provides a useful design principle for any multi-slice generative pipeline. Open questions include whether learned, data-adaptive correlation fields could further improve performance, whether ISCS composes with 3D-aware priors (the authors note orthogonality but do not test it), and whether the approach generalizes to non-uniform or anisotropic sampling geometries beyond the tested slice thicknesses.
