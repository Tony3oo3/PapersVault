## Problem

Existing multi-turn red-teaming methods rely on manually crafted heuristics, fixed templates, or human-curated attack data, and lack a mechanism for learning long-horizon, adaptive strategies that exploit the vast space of possible dialogue trajectories. While multi-turn attacks are significantly more effective than single-turn ones, no prior work formulates red-teaming as a sequential decision-making problem solvable by reinforcement learning, leaving the strategic, goal-directed exploration of attack conversations unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The attacker (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) receives a jailbreaking goal and the full dialogue history (including its own CoT reasoning) at each turn, while the frozen target model only sees the attack queries and its own prior responses—never the goal or CoT. Each attacker turn produces a structured output of a chain-of-thought reasoning step followed by an attack query (capped at 256 tokens), and the target responds with up to 150 tokens. The interaction runs for up to 5 turns, and at test time the attacker operates without tree search (single-path) to limit query cost.

## Method

DIALTREE is a two-stage on-policy RL framework: a cold-start SFT on 397 curated red-teaming dialogues with CoT rationales, followed by GRPO optimization using dialogue tree rollout. At each turn, the attacker branches into n=4 candidate (CoT, query) pairs, each sent to the frozen target (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct), and the resulting tree is pruned by three criteria—format validity, NLI-based topic adherence, and random branch limiting to w=32 nodes—before collecting root-to-leaf trajectories for group-relative advantage computation. The reward is binary (1 if HarmAug-Guard scores any turn's response above 0.5 harmfulness, else 0). A key technical contribution is adaptive masking: format tokens (e.g., <Thinking i>, <Attack question i>) are excluded from gradient updates only in negative-advantage trajectories, preventing the catastrophic format unlearning (70%+ invalid outputs) that otherwise collapses training, while still allowing the model to learn from both positive and negative feedback on content tokens.

## Results

DIALTREE achieves an average attack success rate (ASR@1) of 81.5% across 12 target LLMs (82.7% on 6 closed-source models, 80.2% on 6 open-source models), exceeding the previous state-of-the-art (X-Teaming at 29.3%/45.2%) by 44.2 percentage points. Notably, the attacker is trained only against a small 1B target model (Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct) yet transfers effectively to much larger and more safety-aligned models, reaching 71% ASR on Claude-4-Sonnet where most baselines fall below 10%. DIALTREE also achieves the highest ASR with the fewest queries (avg. 2.79 vs. 4.77 for Jailbreak-R1), demonstrating superior query efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIALTREE substantially outperforms all 8 baselines across every target model. Among single-turn methods, AutoDAN-Turbo (33.8% closed / 33.3% open) is the strongest, yet DIALTREE exceeds it by ~49 points on closed-source models. Among multi-turn methods, X-Teaming (29.3% closed / 45.2% open) is the closest competitor, but DIALTREE still leads by 53.4 and 35.0 points respectively. The gap is most dramatic on strongly safety-aligned models: on Claude-4-Sonnet, DIALTREE reaches 71% ASR while X-Teaming achieves only 9.5%, ActorAttack 26%, and all other methods below 10%. On GPT-oss-20B, DIALTREE (53.5%) outperforms the next-best ActorAttack (32%) by 21.5 points. The paper also shows that DIALTREE's RL stage alone (vs. SFT-only initialization) adds an average of +44.5% ASR, confirming that the tree-based exploration and adaptive masking are the primary drivers of the performance gap over all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the RL-for-LLMs paradigm (GRPO, DeepSeek-R1, TreeRL, ReSTMCTS*) from verifiable tasks (math, code) to the non-verifiable, interactive setting of multi-turn adversarial dialogue. It departs from prior multi-turn jailbreaking methods (MTSA's DPO, ActorAttack's entity-based crafting, X-Teaming's multi-agent planning) by replacing hand-designed heuristics with an on-policy RL agent that autonomously explores the dialogue space via tree search. The adaptive masking mechanism is a novel technical contribution addressing a previously unreported failure mode—catastrophic format unlearning in multi-turn RL—that is specific to structured-output dialogue policies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 44.2% ASR gap over the best prior method, combined with transfer from a 1B training target to Claude-4-Sonnet (71% ASR where most methods score <10%), reveals that current safety alignment is far more fragile against strategic multi-turn attacks than single-turn benchmarks suggest. The discovery of emergent attack strategies (gradual escalation, cross-lingual evasion, persistent gap-filling) provides concrete targets for defense research, yet the paper itself acknowledges that context-aware defenses against such adaptive attacks remain largely unsolved. Open questions include reward hacking under non-verifiable proxy signals, whether process-based rewards can extend effective planning horizons beyond 5 turns, and whether the discovered vulnerabilities generalize to future model generations.
