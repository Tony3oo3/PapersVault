key_contribution: A novel end-to-end kernel learning framework (UKTL) for M-mode tensor sequences that combines mode-wise Grassmann subspace kernels with a sum-product composition, differentiable Nyström linearization via soft k-means pivots, and uncertainty-aware subspace weighting derived from maximum likelihood under heteroscedastic noise, achieving state-of-the-art action recognition on NTU-60, NTU-120, and Kinetics-Skeleton with a compact ~1.5M-parameter model.
limitations: The method relies on predefined hyperparameters (Nyström pivot count, subspace order p, kernel fusion weight μ) that require computationally expensive HyperOpt tuning and are dataset-dependent. surprise_factor: No particularly surprising findings — the performance gains over strong baselines are modest (0.6–1.2% over the KTL ablation, ~1–2% over the best GCN/hypergraph methods), and the ablation results (sum-product > product > sum) are directionally expected. ## Problem

Learning from structured multi-way tensor data (e.g., skeleton sequences) requires capturing interactions across multiple modes while remaining computationally tractable. Existing approaches either flatten tensors into vectors (destroying multi-way structure), apply linear tensor decompositions that treat all modes equally, or use static kernel methods whose cost scales quadratically with dataset size. No prior framework combines non-linear kernel learning on mode-wise tensor subspaces with adaptive uncertainty weighting and a differentiable, scalable kernel approximation in a single end-to-end pipeline.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 3D skeleton sequences (25 joints × 3 coordinates per frame for NTU, 18 joints for Kinetics), RGB video frames, or depth maps, all normalized to a uniform temporal length of T=200 frames and segmented into overlapping 30-frame blocks with stride 10. Skeleton inputs are further organized into third-order hyper-edges (unordered joint triplets, N_ξ = C(J,3)) to form a compact three-mode tensor X ∈ R^{d'×N_ξ×τ}. The model outputs an action class label (60, 120, or 400 classes) along with per-mode uncertainty vectors (p-dimensional confidence scores for each subspace direction), and internally produces a compact N×C Nyström-approximated kernel feature matrix for classification.

## Method

UKTL encodes each temporal block via a per-joint MLP followed by a Higher-order Transformer (HoT) that aggregates three-joint hyper-edges into a structured feature tensor. Each tensor is unfolded along all M modes and decomposed via truncated SVD to extract top-p left singular vectors, defining points on Grassmann manifolds; a sum-product Grassmann kernel then compares projection matrices U·U^T across modes using an RBF form, with a learnable mixture weight μ balancing additive and multiplicative interactions. A Multi-mode SigmaNet (MSN) with one FC branch per mode estimates uncertainty vectors σ from the projection matrices, and subspace bases are rescaled as Û = U/√σ to down-weight unreliable directions—this weighting is derived from maximum likelihood under heteroscedastic Gaussian noise. For scalability, C pivot tensors are selected via differentiable soft k-means clustering, and the full kernel matrix is approximated via a Nyström scheme with eigendecomposition-based stable inversion, yielding explicit finite-dimensional features. The entire pipeline (encoder, MSN, kernel, classifier) is trained end-to-end with cross-entropy plus a log-barrier uncertainty regularizer.

## Results

UKTL achieves state-of-the-art results across all three benchmarks: 93.1% (X-Sub) and 97.3% (X-View) on NTU-60, 90.0% (X-Sub) and 91.4% (X-Setup) on NTU-120, and 39.2% Top-1 / 62.3% Top-5 on Kinetics-Skeleton. The uncertainty-driven component (UKTL vs. KTL) contributes a consistent 0.6–1.2% gain, e.g., 93.1% vs. 92.5% on NTU-60 X-Sub and 90.0% vs. 88.8% on NTU-120 X-Sub. In multi-modal fusion (Skeleton+RGB+Depth), UKTL reaches 95.5% on NTU-60 X-Sub and 94.0% on NTU-120 X-Setup, outperforming all single-modality baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UKTL outperforms the strongest graph-based baseline DSDC-GCN (93.0/97.1 on NTU-60, 89.9/90.6 on NTU-120, 38.6 Top-1 on Kinetics) on every metric except Kinetics-Skeleton Top-5, where DSDC-GCN edges ahead at 63.4% vs. 62.3%. Against the best hypergraph method (Selective-HCN, 90.8/96.6 on NTU-60) and the best transformer (STST, 91.9/96.8 on NTU-60, 38.3 Top-1 on Kinetics), UKTL leads by 2.3–3.2% on NTU-60 X-Sub and by 0.9% on Kinetics Top-1. Within the tensor-based family sharing the same MLP+HoT backbone, the progression Backbone → KPCA → TPCA → KTL → UKTL shows monotonic gains, with the uncertainty-aware component (UKTL over KTL) contributing +0.6% on NTU-60 X-Sub and +1.2% on NTU-120 X-Sub, confirming that adaptive mode-wise weighting is the key differentiator. The multi-modal fusion results (Table 2) further show that UKTL's kernel-based fusion of Skeleton+RGB+Depth reaches 95.5% on NTU-60 X-Sub, surpassing all single-modality baselines by 2.4–12.3% without any modality-specific architectural changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of tensor kernel methods (Signoretto et al. 2011; He et al. 2017; Koniusz et al. 2016) by replacing static, handcrafted tensor-product kernels with an end-to-end learnable sum-product Grassmann kernel over mode-wise SVD subspaces. It departs from standard Nyström approximation (Williams & Seeger 2000) by making pivot selection differentiable via soft k-means (Koniusz & Mikolajczyk 2011), enabling the kernel approximation to co-adapt with the encoder during training. The uncertainty-aware subspace weighting draws on heteroscedastic regression and the Uncertainty-DTW framework (Wang & Koniusz 2022) but applies it at the level of individual subspace directions per tensor mode rather than scalar outputs, a granularity not present in prior tensor or kernel literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UKTL offers a principled, interpretable alternative to the dominant GCN/Transformer paradigm for skeleton action recognition, showing that a compact kernel-based model (~1.2–1.5M params) can match or exceed much larger architectures while providing mode-wise uncertainty estimates that reveal which spatial, temporal, or hyper-edge directions are most discriminative. The differentiable Nyström linearization is a reusable technique for any kernel method on structured data where scalability is a bottleneck. Open questions include whether the uncertainty modeling captures genuinely distinct epistemic vs. aleatoric sources, whether the approach transfers to non-action tensor tasks (e.g., medical imaging, sensor fusion), and whether the HyperOpt-dependent hyperparameter selection can be replaced by fully learnable alternatives.
