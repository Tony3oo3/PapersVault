## Problem

Naive combination of dynamic connectivity sparse training and low-rank (spectral) sparse training for LLM pre-training suffers from a cancellation effect where the two branches produce conflicting outputs, wasting representational capacity and hindering convergence. Prior hybrid attempts like SLTrain used only static sparsity as a supplement to low-rank training and relied on simple summation without any mechanism to promote cooperation between branches, leaving a unified framework for genuinely integrating dynamic connectivity and spectral sparsity unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard text token sequences (sequence length 256) for self-supervised next-token prediction, identical to standard LLM pre-training. What is unusual is that every linear layer in the Transformer is replaced by a composite module whose output is the sum of a dynamically sparse branch and a low-rank branch, maintaining both sparse connectivity and low-rank structure throughout training and inference. Only 10–30% of the dense parameter count is preserved, and the sparsity is split between the two branches (e.g., 1:1 parameter allocation), with the total sparsity defined uniformly across all methods for fair comparison.

## Method

The paper identifies the cancellation effect between sparse and low-rank branches and quantifies it via the Overlap Cancellation Ratio (OCR), which measures the fraction of overlapping signal magnitude that is negated by opposite signs. To mitigate this, it introduces a layer-wise alignment loss (Frobenius norm between sparse and low-rank outputs) weighted by λ, plus a SiLU activation between the low-rank factor matrices for numerical stability. The framework is instantiated as CHTsL, where the sparse branch follows the CHTs dynamic connectivity update rules (Cannistracci-Hebbian theory) and the low-rank branch uses rank-r factorization with activation, jointly optimized under L_task + λ·L_align. Experiments on LLaMA-60M/130M show the alignment loss most effectively reduces OCR in the Q and K attention layers, and CHTsL consistently outperforms all sparse baselines (SET, RigL, MEST, CHTs, CoLA, SLTrain) under matched parameter budgets, approaching dense-model perplexity with only 10–30% of parameters.

## Results

CHTsL achieves the lowest validation perplexity among all parameter-efficient sparse training methods across all evaluated settings. On OpenWebText with LLaMA-60M at 90% total sparsity, CHTsL reaches 31.77 PPL, outperforming the next-best SLTrain (33.90) and dense training (26.56 PPL). At 70% sparsity on LLaMA-130M/OpenWebText, CHTsL achieves 20.65 PPL, approaching the dense baseline of 19.46 while using only 30% of parameters. On C4, CHTsL similarly leads all sparse baselines, e.g., 26.19 PPL at 70% sparsity on LLaMA-130M versus 24.55 for dense.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CHTsL outperforms all six baselines across every model size, dataset, and sparsity level tested. Against the closest competitor SLTrain (Han et al., 2024), CHTsL improves PPL by 1.3–2.1 points on OpenWebText (e.g., 31.77 vs. 33.90 at 90% sparsity on LLaMA-60M) and by 0.6–1.6 points on C4. Compared to the dynamic sparse method CHTs (Zhang et al., 2025), which serves as the connectivity-sparse backbone of CHTsL, the addition of the low-rank branch with alignment loss yields consistent gains of 0.7–1.4 PPL points. The spectral-sparse baseline CoLA (Liu et al., 2025) is outperformed by 1.3–7.1 PPL points, with the largest gap at 90% sparsity on LLaMA-60M/C4 (39.29 vs. 46.41). Among dynamic connectivity methods, SET (Mocanu et al., 2018) and MEST (Yuan et al., 2021) trail CHTsL by 1.5–3.5 PPL points, while RigL (Evci et al., 2020) is consistently the weakest baseline, sometimes diverging entirely (e.g., 70.11 PPL at 70% sparsity on LLaMA-130M/OpenWebText). The alignment-enhanced integration is also validated statistically via Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (p < 0.001), confirming that the gains over naive summation are significant.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of parameter-efficient pre-training: dynamic connectivity sparse training (exemplified by CHTs, which leverages Cannistracci-Hebbian theory from network science) and spectral sparse training via low-rank factorization (exemplified by CoLA, which maintains low-rank structure through both training and inference). It departs from the prior hybrid attempt SLTrain (Han et al., 2024) in two key ways: replacing static sparsity with dynamic connectivity updates, and introducing an alignment loss to address a newly identified cancellation effect between the two branches. The activation adjustment in the low-rank branch is borrowed from CoLA, while the OCR metric and alignment loss are novel contributions specific to this integration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of the cancellation effect and its concentration in Q/K attention layers provides a useful diagnostic for understanding why naive combinations of complementary sparsity paradigms fail, and the alignment loss offers a simple, low-overhead fix that practitioners can adopt. The practical efficiency gains in both training and inference (confirmed by memory/throughput benchmarks) make this relevant for resource-constrained LLM deployment. However, the approach is limited to unstructured sparsity, which current GPU hardware and PyTorch do not accelerate well, so the theoretical speedups remain unrealized; scaling beyond 1B parameters and exploring structured sparsity variants are natural next steps.
