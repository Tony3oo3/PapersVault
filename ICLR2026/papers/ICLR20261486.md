## Problem

Adaptive optimizers like Adam operate at the adaptive edge of stability during training, where the preconditioned Hessian's largest eigenvalue equilibrates near a threshold proportional to 1/η, causing persistent oscillations and slowed convergence. Existing methods use a fixed or schedule-driven learning rate that cannot react to the instantaneous velocity of weight updates, leaving no mechanism to automatically damp oscillations when they grow. This paper asks whether physics-inspired higher-order kinetic energy terms can provide a principled, state-dependent stabilization mechanism.

## Input / Output

At each iteration the optimizer consumes the stochastic gradient ∇f(θ_{t-1}) and the current parameter vector θ_{t-1}, maintaining a first-moment (velocity) buffer v_t and a second-moment buffer m_t as in Adam. The key departure is that the effective learning rate η_t = α₀/(1+min(β₃‖v_t‖², α₁)) is computed from the global norm of the velocity buffer, making the step size a state-dependent feedback signal rather than a function of the iteration index. The output is the updated parameter vector θ_t, with decoupled weight decay applied in the standard manner.

## Method

VRAdam is derived from a quartic Lagrangian L(x,v) = mv²/2 + β₃v⁴/4 − V(x), inspired by stabilizing higher-order kinetic terms in NRQCD and classical time crystals. Solving the Euler-Lagrange equation yields a velocity-dependent effective mass (m + 3β₃‖v‖²) in the denominator of the acceleration, which the authors embed as a dynamic learning-rate gate into AdamW rather than discretizing the ODE directly. The resulting global scalar gate η_t is rotation-invariant, which the paper shows avoids switched-anisotropy instabilities that plague per-coordinate step-size modulation, and guarantees a dimension-free bound on the update norm ‖θ_t − θ_{t-1}‖ ≤ α₀/(2√β₃). The paper proves global uniform exponential stability via a curvature-agnostic Common Quadratic Lyapunov Function valid under arbitrary scalar switching of η_t, and derives a convergence rate of O(ln N / √N) for non-convex stochastic objectives, matching Adam's rate under appropriate hyperparameter scaling.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 image classification, VRAdam achieves a test loss of 0.469, outperforming AdamW (0.565), SGD+Nesterov (0.620), and RMSProp (0.813). On WikiText-2 language modeling, VRAdam reaches a validation loss of 5.99 versus AdamW's 6.47, while SGD+Nesterov and RMSProp diverge to NaN. For the GPT-2 124M pretraining benchmark on FineWebEdu-10B, VRAdam achieves a validation loss of 3.447 versus AdamW's 3.511 with comparable training time (~48.5k seconds per epoch). On LLM fine-tuning, VRAdam improves PPL from 4.12 to 3.53 on GSM8K (GPT-2 Large) and from 3.84 to 3.55 on OASST2 (LLaMA-2-7B QLoRA), also beating Lion on both tasks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VRAdam outperforms AdamW on every benchmark: CIFAR-10 test loss 0.469 vs 0.565, WikiText-2 validation loss 5.99 vs 6.47, GridWorld validation loss 1.25 vs 2.41, and GPT-2 validation loss 3.447 vs 3.511. Against RAdam, VRAdam wins decisively on all three Table 1 tasks (e.g., CIFAR-10 test loss 0.469 vs 2.300) and on GridWorld (1.25 vs 1.407 validation). SGD+Nesterov and RMSProp both fail to converge on WikiText-2 (NaN), and VRAdam beats them on CIFAR-10 (0.469 vs 0.620 and 0.813) and GridWorld (1.25 vs 2.71 and 25.0). On the LLM fine-tuning benchmarks, VRAdam beats Lion on both OASST2 (PPL 3.55 vs 3.56) and GSM8K (PPL 3.53 vs 3.67), and substantially outperforms AdamW on GSM8K (PPL 3.53 vs 4.12; exact-match accuracy 42% vs 35%). In the edge-of-stability analysis (Figure 2), VRAdam converges faster than both Adam and SAM in training loss and accuracy while maintaining lower Hessian sharpness, confirming its advantage in the adaptive edge-of-stability regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

VRAdam extends AdamW by embedding a global, velocity-dependent learning-rate gate derived from a quartic kinetic energy term, drawing conceptual inspiration from NRQCD heavy-quark Lagrangians and classical time crystals where higher-order velocity terms confer stability. It builds directly on the adaptive edge-of-stability framework of Cohen et al. (2024) and the convergence-analysis template of Defossez et al. (2022), while departing from the symplectic-optimization line (Betancourt et al., França et al.) by avoiding explicit ODE discretization in favor of a heuristic gate embedded in the existing Adam update. The key conceptual departure from prior adaptive optimizers is the use of a single scalar gate (rather than per-parameter scaling) to control step magnitude, which the authors argue avoids switched-anisotropy instabilities and admits a curvature-agnostic Common Quadratic Lyapunov Function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

VRAdam offers a physically interpretable, drop-in modification to AdamW that consistently improves convergence and final loss across image classification, language modeling, generative modeling, and LLM fine-tuning with negligible computational overhead, making it a practical candidate for replacing AdamW in production pipelines. The theoretical contributions—global uniform exponential stability via a CQLF and an O(ln N / √N) convergence bound—provide a rigorous foundation that many competing optimizers lack. However, the physics connection remains largely heuristic (the quartic term is borrowed as motivation rather than rigorously derived from the physical system), the edge-of-stability regime is still not fully understood, and the authors acknowledge constrained compute resources that limit the scale of validation. Open questions include whether the velocity gate composes well with other adaptive mechanisms (e.g., Schedule-Free averaging) and whether the stability guarantees extend to the full non-quadratic, stochastic setting without the trajectory-level curvature assumptions.
