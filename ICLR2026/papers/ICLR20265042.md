## Problem

Expert parallelism (EP) for large MoE inference relies on all-to-all collectives to dispatch tokens to remote experts, which can consume up to 59% of MoE-layer latency even on high-bandwidth interconnects. Existing systems optimize expert placement and token/request scheduling as independent concerns, leaving substantial redundant cross-device communication on the table. No prior approach jointly co-schedules model parameters and input data to minimize this communication while preserving load balance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265042/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard LLM inference inputs (token sequences from user requests) alongside an offline profiling artifact: per-token, per-layer expert activation frequency tables built from representative datasets (e.g., ShareGPT, MMLU, LMSYS-Chat-1M). Beyond the generated text tokens, Sem-MoE produces three lightweight scheduling lookup tables — a token-to-expert-cluster table (~11.7 MB for DeepSeek-V2), an expert-group-sequence-to-expert-group transition table, and an expert-to-device placement table — which are consulted at runtime via O(1) table lookups with negligible memory overhead. The scheduling is lossless: it reorders where computation occurs without altering model outputs.

## Method

Sem-MoE exploits the empirically observed context-independent token-expert affinity (a token consistently routes to a narrow top-k expert subset regardless of surrounding context, with median cumulative hotness of 0.83–0.98 across layers) to predict routing paths before the gating module executes. Offline, it formulates expert placement and token/request clustering as a 0-1 integer-programming co-clustering problem balancing load evenness against cross-cluster activation cost, solved via an alternating greedy-plus-swap algorithm that produces the placement and scheduling tables. Online, for Attention-DP it re-batches incoming requests onto the DP rank hosting their most-likely experts using a workload-aware round-robin mask; for Attention-TP it fuses speculative token shuffling directly into the post-attention reduce-scatter and pre-combination allgather (shuffled-reduce-scatter / shuffled-allgather), guided by a 2-gram inter-layer device-transition Markov model, adding only ~1% overhead to the ring communication schedule. This joint model-data co-scheduling raises the local activation rate by 15–43% over baselines, directly trimming all-to-all volume without any architectural modification to the MoE model.

## Results

In the Attention-TP scenario, Sem-MoE reduces p99 TTFT by 12.21%, 10.60%, and 18.89% over the best baseline for DeepSeek-V2-Lite at input lengths 256, 512, and 1024, and by 17.16%, 24.90%, and 3.80% for Qwen3-30B-A3B. In the Attention-DP scenario, Sem-MoE achieves up to 2.78× throughput improvement over MoETuner and 2.21× over SGLang under E2E latency SLOs for DeepSeek-V2-Lite. At the algorithm level, Sem-MoE increases the local activation rate (LAR) by 37% (DeepSeek-V2-Lite) and 43% (Qwen3-30B-A3B) over vanilla placement, translating to 41.8%/46.6% latency reduction per MoE layer, and outperforms the best baseline LAR by 15.4% under EP8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Sem-MoE consistently outperforms both SGLang and MoETuner across all tested scenarios. Against SGLang (the SOTA open-source inference engine), Sem-MoE achieves up to 221% throughput gain under TTFT SLO and 278% under E2E SLO for DeepSeek-V2-Lite in the Attention-DP setting, and 12–19% TTFT reduction in the Attention-TP setting. Against MoETuner (the SOTA MoE expert-placement optimizer), Sem-MoE gains 32% and 278% throughput in Attention-DP for DeepSeek-V2-Lite, and maintains a 3.8–24.9% TTFT advantage in Attention-TP. Notably, MoETuner can actually degrade performance in some configurations (e.g., its TTFT worsens from 111.19 ms to 119.76 ms at input length 1024 for DeepSeek-V2-Lite), whereas Sem-MoE's model-data collaborative scheduling delivers consistent gains in every tested setting. The cross-dataset zero-shot transfer experiments further confirm that Sem-MoE's scheduling tables generalize well, maintaining 35–47% LAR on unseen datasets versus a flat ~25% for SGLang's default round-robin placement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on SGLang as its base inference engine and departs from prior MoE optimization work (MoETuner, ExFlow, Pre-gated MoE) by unifying two previously separate concerns—expert device placement and token/request scheduling—into a single joint co-scheduling problem. While MoETuner optimizes only expert placement via ILP and ExFlow exploits inter-layer expert affinity at the cost of heavy allgather overhead, Sem-MoE introduces a model-data collaborative framework that simultaneously decides where experts reside and where tokens/requests are dispatched, leveraging an empirically observed context-independent token-expert affinity. The shuffled-reduce-scatter and shuffled-allgather primitives further distinguish this work by fusing speculative token routing directly into standard TP communication collectives, avoiding the architectural modifications required by Pre-gated MoE.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EP all-to-all communication accounts for up to 59% of MoE layer latency, making this a critical bottleneck for deploying trillion-parameter MoE models in production. The approach is lossless (no accuracy degradation, no architecture changes) and the cross-dataset zero-shot transfer results suggest the token-expert affinity is a robust model property rather than a dataset artifact, broadening applicability. Open questions include how the method scales to very large EP degrees (beyond 8), its interaction with dynamic or adversarial workloads that may shift activation patterns, and whether the offline profiling requirement becomes a bottleneck for frequently updated models. The ILP is also only approximately solved, leaving room for tighter formulations.
