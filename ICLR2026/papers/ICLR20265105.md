## Problem

Existing Weak-to-Strong Generalization (W2SG) research is limited to simple tasks like binary classification, leaving open whether weak-model supervision can elicit strong capabilities in complex multi-step interactive decision-making where solutions are full action trajectories. A further gap is that prior preference-based methods (e.g., DPO) use randomly paired trajectories that ignore the shared-prefix structure between successful and failed paths, discarding the most informative divergence signals.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265105/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language task instruction and a sequence of text-based environment observations as input, and produces a fine-tuned strong LLM agent (via LoRA, rank 64) that outputs thought-action sequences in POMDP environments. Unusually, the training signal is not expert demonstrations but rather M diverse trajectories (both successful and failed) generated by a weaker SFT model through environment exploration, which are merged into a single trajectory tree per instruction before any strong-model training begins.

## Method

A weak SFT model explores each task environment multiple times (varying temperature/top-p) to collect diverse success and failure trajectories, which are then merged into a hierarchical trajectory tree by collapsing shared action prefixes and preserving divergence points where different actions from similar states lead to different outcomes. Two W2SG fine-tuning algorithms operate on this tree: (1) TreeDPO constructs preference pairs from divergence points (shared prefix h, two continuations σ⁺ and σ⁻ with different aggregated G(e) scores) and optimizes the strong model with a KL-regularized DPO loss against the weak SFT model as reference; (2) MCTS performs offline UCB-guided search on the static tree to extract an optimized path e*, on which the strong model is trained via standard SFT. A PAC-Bayesian analysis (Theorem 1) provides a performance guarantee showing the TreeDPO-trained strong model can surpass the SFT strong baseline when the improvement margin over the SFT baseline exceeds an estimation-error term that shrinks as O(1/√N_p) with the number of preference pairs.

## Results

On WebShop, ScienceWorld, and AlfWorld, W2SG with MCTS (Llama2-13B strong model supervised by Llama2-7B weak model) achieves average rewards of 56.9, 58.2, and 57.5 respectively, outperforming the SFT strong model (51.0, 53.6, 51.5) by 11.6% and 11.7% on WebShop and AlfWorld. W2SG with Tree DPO improves the SFT weak model by up to 4.3% in average reward and recovers up to 39.4% of the ceiling model's performance gap under purely weak supervision. On ScienceWorld, W2SG MCTS even surpasses the ceiling model (58.2 vs. 56.9), and the improvement over the SFT strong model is statistically significant (p = 0.0001 for MCTS, p = 0.0003 for Tree DPO).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the ETO baseline (Song et al., 2024), which fine-tunes the strong model with DPO on its own exploration trajectories, W2SG MCTS achieves substantially higher average rewards: 56.9 vs. 52.0 on WebShop (+4.9), 58.2 vs. 54.9 on ScienceWorld (+3.3), and 57.5 vs. 53.7 on AlfWorld (+3.8). W2SG Tree DPO also outperforms ETO on all three tasks (53.2/55.4/56.0 vs. 52.0/54.9/53.7). Both W2SG variants surpass the SFT strong model and the Best-of-N inference strategy across all environments. The only case where the ceiling model (trained with expert-trajectory DPO) retains an edge is on WebShop (58.3 vs. 56.9) and AlfWorld (59.0 vs. 57.5), while W2SG MCTS actually exceeds the ceiling on ScienceWorld (58.2 vs. 56.9), demonstrating that structured weak-model trajectories can match or surpass expert supervision in certain domains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Weak-to-Strong Generalization (W2SG) framework of Burns et al. (2023) from simple tasks (binary classification) to sequential decision-making where solutions are multi-step action trajectories. It repurposes DPO (Rafailov et al., 2024) by replacing random contrastive preference pairs with structurally-derived pairs from a trajectory tree that captures shared prefixes between success and failure paths, and introduces MCTS (Grill et al., 2020) as the first tree-search-based policy optimization in the W2SG setting. The trajectory tree construction departs from both linear Chain-of-Thought and Tree-of-Thoughts by explicitly organizing both successful and failed weak-model explorations into a hierarchical structure where divergence points encode the critical decision boundaries.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a pressing alignment challenge: how to supervise models that may exceed human capability when reliable human feedback becomes unavailable. The finding that weak-model failure trajectories can elicit strong-model performance exceeding expert-SFT baselines suggests a scalable bootstrapping pathway (GPT-3→GPT-4→GPT-5) for agent training without human intervention. Key limitations include reliance on strong theoretical assumptions (particularly Assumption 3 on tree-derived preference informativeness), sensitivity to the KL penalty parameter β, and the observation that excessive trajectory diversity degrades performance. Open questions include whether the framework handles adversarially misaligned weak models, whether it scales to truly open-ended tasks beyond the three benchmark environments, and how the approach interacts with the super-alignment problem when the weak model's errors are systematic rather than random.
