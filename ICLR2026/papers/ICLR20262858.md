## Problem

Wasserstein over Wasserstein (WoW) distances provide a powerful metric for comparing distributions over non-Euclidean objects (images, shapes, point clouds), but their computation requires repeated pairwise Wasserstein evaluations, making them prohibitively expensive. Existing sliced WoW accelerations either rely on parametric meta-measures (Gaussian mixtures) or on high-order moment expansions that become numerically unstable beyond the first few moments. The paper seeks a stable, scalable sliced alternative that avoids both parametric assumptions and moment-based approximations.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pairs of empirical meta-measures, each of the form (1/N)Σ δ_{μ_i} where each μ_i is itself an empirical measure (1/n_i)Σ δ_{x_{i,k}} in R^d. For point clouds, each μ_i is a set of 3D points; for images, each μ_i is a distribution over vectorized p×p patches (e.g., 3249 patches of dimension 64 for 64×64 images with p=8). The output is a single scalar distance (DSW for multi-dimensional meta-measures, SQW for 1d meta-measures), computed via Monte Carlo integration over S random projections and R quadrature grid points, with the 1d Wasserstein sub-problems solved analytically via sorted quantile functions.

## Method

The core insight is to exploit the isometric embedding of P_2(R) into L_2([0,1]) via quantile functions, transforming the WoW problem into a Wasserstein problem on an infinite-dimensional Banach space. Since no uniform probability measure exists on the infinite-dimensional unit sphere, the authors introduce a general sliced Wasserstein framework on arbitrary Banach spaces parameterized by a reference measure ξ on the dual space, and instantiate ξ as a Gaussian process with a radial basis function kernel k_σ(t,s)=exp(-|t-s|²/2σ²). The resulting SQW distance slices 1d meta-measures through L_2 inner products with Gaussian process sample paths. For multi-dimensional meta-measures, a classical spherical slicing step (projection onto R via θ∈S^{d-1}) is applied first, yielding the double-sliced Wasserstein (DSW) metric. The authors prove that DSW minimization is equivalent to WoW minimization for discretized meta-measures, establishing topological metric equivalence, and show that DSW is a true metric on the space of empirical meta-measures.

## Results

On shape classification (KNN, 1000 random splits), SQW achieves 99.5% on 2D Shapes, 99.1% on Animals, 38.6% on FAUST-500, 42.7% on FAUST-1000, and 84.8% on MNIST-2000, matching or exceeding all baselines on FAUST-1000 while running 4–75× faster than GW. For dataset comparison, DSW shows stronger Pearson/Spearman correlation with the OTDD ground truth than s-OTDD across MNIST, FashionMNIST, and CIFAR-10. On point cloud batch evaluation (ModelNet-10), DSW matches WoW behavior while being ~18× faster (0.25 s vs 4.5 s for N=M=10, m=n=500). For image batch comparison via patch distributions, DSW is ~40× faster than patch-based WoW (~1 s vs ~40 s) and provides sharper discrimination of parameter changes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GW (Mémoli 2011), SQW achieves comparable or higher accuracy on all FAUST splits (e.g., 42.7% vs 33.0% on FAUST-1000) while being ~75× faster (13.8 ms vs 1048.2 ms); GW times out on MNIST-2000. Against TLB (exact 1d WoW), SQW is 4–6× faster on FAUST-1000 and MNIST-2000, with slightly lower accuracy on MNIST-2000 (84.8% vs 88.7%) but higher on FAUST-1000 (42.7% vs 40.2%). Against STLB (Piening & Beinert 2025a), SQW matches accuracy and runtime almost exactly (both ~9.8 ms on FAUST-500), confirming the theoretical equivalence of the two slicing constructions. Against AE (Sato et al. 2020), SQW is 3–16× faster on large datasets while achieving comparable accuracy. For dataset comparison, DSW shows stronger Pearson and Spearman correlation with the OTDD ground truth than s-OTDD (Nguyen et al. 2025) on all three datasets (MNIST, FashionMNIST, CIFAR-10), making it a better drop-in replacement. On point cloud batches, DSW and WoW (Bonet et al. 2025b) exhibit nearly identical distance curves across varying M, noise, and resolution, but DSW is ~18× faster; both outperform OT-NNA (Yang et al. 2019) in consistency, as OT-NNA increases undesirably for M≥N and shows resolution-dependent inconsistency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes the sliced Wasserstein framework from Han (2023) on Hilbert spaces to arbitrary Banach spaces, then applies it to the 1D Wasserstein space via the quantile-function isometry into L₂([0,1]). The key conceptual departure from concurrent work (s-OTDD by Nguyen et al. 2025, SWBDG by Bonet et al. 2025c) is the use of Gaussian-process-parametrized L₂ projections as the inner slicing mechanism, avoiding the numerical instability of moment-based approaches and the parametric assumptions of Busemann-function-based methods. The outer spherical slicing follows the classical sliced Wasserstein construction (Bonneel et al. 2015), yielding a hierarchical 'double-sliced' distance that composes domain projection with quantile projection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DSW provides a theoretically grounded, computationally efficient substitute for the WoW distance (O(N²n²log n) → ~0.25s vs ~4.5s for point clouds, ~1s vs ~40s for image patches), with a proof of topological metric equivalence to WoW for discretized meta-measures. This makes hierarchical OT comparisons practical for large-scale shape, dataset, and image distribution analysis where exact WoW is infeasible. Open questions include whether DSW inherits the favorable sample complexity of sliced Wasserstein in high dimensions, how to extend it to the full OTDD setting with label metrics, and whether the Banach-space slicing framework applies to infinite-dimensional generative models.
