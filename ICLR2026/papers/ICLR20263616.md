## Problem

Existing neural constructive solvers for routing problems rely on one-pass forward autoregressive construction with static masking, which implicitly assumes the remaining tail subproblem is always feasible. This assumption breaks down under complex constraints like time windows (TSPTW) and draft limits (TSPDL), where local feasibility does not guarantee global feasibility, leading to high infeasibility rates. The paper addresses this by introducing a decoding mechanism that can correct infeasible partial routes through backtracking, with a systematic mathematical treatment of the masking mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method takes a graph-structured routing instance as input: node coordinates plus problem-specific static features (time windows [e_i, l_i] for TSPTW, or demands q_i and draft limits D_i for TSPDL). It outputs a complete feasible route as a node permutation π = (0, π_2, ..., π_{n+1}, 0) along with the route length as the objective value. During decoding, the model operates with a backtracking budget R that caps the number of backward corrections, and the overestimation set is initialized via single-step or two-step lookahead strategies specific to each constraint type.

## Method

LMask introduces the LazyMask decoding algorithm, which replaces the exact (but computationally intractable) potential set S with an overestimation set Ŝ that is lazily refined through an adaptive backtracking mechanism: when Ŝ becomes empty at step t, the algorithm backtracks to step t−1 and removes the invalid node from Ŝ, rather than failing. To resolve representation ambiguities caused by backtracking (where the model state becomes invariant to the search trace), a Refinement Intensity Embedding (RIE) encodes the local refinement count and global budget status into the decoder query. The model is a POMO-based transformer encoder-decoder trained via policy gradient with an ℓ₁ penalty on constraint violations and entropy regularization, combining hard constraints (backtracking) during training with soft constraints (penalty) once the budget is exhausted. Theoretical guarantees include a validity proposition (Algorithm 1 always produces feasible solutions with R=∞) and a probabilistic optimality bound showing the probability of suboptimal solutions decays exponentially in the suboptimality gap Δ/λ.

## Results

On hard TSPTW-100, LMask achieves a 0.00% solution infeasibility rate and 0.21% optimality gap (objective 51.38) in 18s, compared to PIP's 31.74% infeasibility and 0.37% gap, and PIP-D's 13.59% infeasibility and 0.32% gap. On hard TSPDL-100, LMask reduces solution infeasibility to 0.80% (vs. PIP's 29.34%) with a 4.34% gap (vs. PIP's 12.87%). On the TSPTW benchmark at n=80, LMask achieves 0.0% infeasibility and a 3.5% gap, while PIP-D degrades to 66.7% infeasibility and 12.0% gap.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest neural baselines PIP and PIP-D (Bi et al., 2024), LMask achieves near-zero infeasibility (0.00% on hard TSPTW-100) versus 31.74% and 13.59% respectively, while also delivering lower optimality gaps (0.21% vs. 0.37% and 0.32%). On TSPDL, the advantage is even more pronounced: LMask's 0.80% solution infeasibility and 4.34% gap on hard TSPDL-100 dramatically outperform PIP's 29.34% infeasibility and 12.87% gap. Compared to traditional solvers (PyVRP, LKH3, VSR-LKH), LMask is orders of magnitude faster (18s vs. 4.3h–1d8h) at the cost of a small gap (0.21% vs. 0.00%), making it the best speed–quality trade-off among all methods. OR-Tools, while fast, fails on 89.25% of hard TSPTW-100 instances, whereas LMask finds feasible solutions on all instances. On the TSPTW benchmark (Dumas et al., 1995), LMask generalizes robustly across sizes (n=20–80) with 0.0% infeasibility, while PIP-D's infeasibility escalates from 5% to 66.7% as n grows, confirming LMask's superior scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

LMask builds on the POMO encoder-decoder architecture (Kwon et al., 2020) and the attention-based model lineage (Kool et al., 2018) for neural constructive routing solvers, but fundamentally departs from the one-pass forward construction paradigm that all prior neural methods (AM, POMO, PIP, PIP-D) rely on. Where PIP (Bi et al., 2024) handles complex constraints through proactive infeasibility prevention via deeper lookahead masking, LMask instead introduces a backtracking mechanism (LazyMask) that lazily refines overestimation sets, combined with a refinement intensity embedding to resolve the representation ambiguities that backtracking introduces into standard dynamic features. The theoretical grounding via constrained Gibbs distributions and KL-divergence minimization connects to variational annealing methods (Hibat-Allah et al., 2021; Sanokowski et al., 2023), but the paper is the first to provide rigorous validity and probabilistic optimality guarantees for a neural constructive solver with backtracking.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The near-zero infeasibility rates (0.00% on hard TSPTW-100) combined with competitive optimality gaps demonstrate that backtracking is a practical and theoretically sound alternative to ever-deeper lookahead for constraint handling in neural routing solvers, potentially simplifying the design of future constrained CO methods. The theoretical guarantees (Proposition 4.1, Theorem 4.3) fill a notable gap in the neural CO literature, where most methods lack formal feasibility or optimality bounds. However, the framework is currently validated only on TSPTW and TSPDL, and the backtracking budget must be tuned per problem size and hardness, leaving open questions about automatic budget allocation and extension to more general combinatorial optimization problems with diverse constraint structures.
