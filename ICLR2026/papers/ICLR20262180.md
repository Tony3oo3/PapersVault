## Problem

Current deep learning methods for cryo-EM density map processing are predominantly task-specific and rely on supervised training with labeled pairs, constraining dataset scale and generalizability. No unified foundation model exists for the 3D density map domain that learns transferable structural representations and adapts efficiently to diverse downstream tasks such as sharpening, super-resolution, and missing wedge restoration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262180/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 3D cryo-EM density maps as 48³-voxel patches (resampled to 1 Å spacing for sharpening and super-resolution, 4 Å for missing wedge) and produces full-volume output maps via sliding-window inference with Gaussian-weighted patch fusion. For super-resolution, inputs are experimental maps at 4–6 Å paired with simulated 1.8 Å targets during training; for missing wedge, inputs are Fourier-masked tomograms with a ±60° tilt cone. Outputs are single-channel density volumes at the same voxel grid as the input.

## Method

CryoLVM employs Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) for self-supervised pretraining, where a Context Encoder (SCUNet-based) processes visible patches and a Target Predictor estimates the Target Encoder's (EMA-updated) representations for masked patches in abstract embedding space, avoiding noise-amplifying voxel-level reconstruction. The SCUNet backbone uses hierarchical Swin-Conv blocks that split features into parallel Swin Transformer (global, 4×4×4 window) and residual convolution (local, 3×3×3) pathways, capturing both atomic-level detail and cross-regional spatial relationships. A novel histogram-based distribution alignment loss (L_HistKL) constructs differentiable Gaussian-kernel soft histograms of predicted and target densities and minimizes their Jensen-Shannon divergence, combined with MSE via a weighted composite objective. Pretraining uses 7,302 experimental density maps (1–4 Å) from Cryo2StructData with random 48³ crops; downstream adaptation fine-tunes the pretrained encoder jointly with task-specific upsampling SC-block decoders under the composite loss.

## Results

On density map sharpening, CryoLVM achieves the best scores across all four metrics: CCbox=0.894, CCmask=0.821, CCpeaks=0.806, and Q-score=0.444, outperforming EMReady (0.878/0.802/0.791/0.424) and DeepEMhancer (0.695/0.679/0.659/0.323). On super-resolution, CryoLVM attains d_model=2.33 Å, FSC-0.143=2.58 Å, FSC-0.5=4.58 Å, and CryoRes=3.39 Å, surpassing EM-GAN (2.49/2.70/5.46/4.18) and DeepEMhancer (3.27/2.72/4.86/3.47). On missing wedge restoration, CryoLVM reduces FSC-0.143 from 10.448 to 10.094 Å (~3.4% improvement) and FSC-0.5 from 12.361 to 11.447 Å (~7.4% improvement) over IsoNet.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CryoLVM outperforms all four baselines across every task and metric evaluated. Against EMReady (the strongest sharpening baseline), CryoLVM gains +0.016 CCbox, +0.019 CCmask, +0.015 CCpeaks, and +0.020 Q-score, indicating more faithful high-frequency recovery. Against DeepEMhancer, CryoLVM shows a large margin (+0.199 CCbox, +0.142 CCmask) on sharpening and a substantial resolution improvement on super-resolution (d_model 2.33 vs. 3.27 Å; CryoRes 3.39 vs. 3.47 Å). Against EM-GAN on super-resolution, CryoLVM achieves better FSC-0.143 (2.58 vs. 2.70 Å) and a markedly better FSC-0.5 (4.58 vs. 5.46 Å), while EM-GAN's CryoRes score (4.18 Å) is notably worse than CryoLVM's (3.39 Å). Against IsoNet on missing wedge restoration, CryoLVM improves FSC-0.143 by ~3.4% and FSC-0.5 by ~7.4%, with visual inspection confirming that CryoLVM preserves continuous pore-like channels that IsoNet fragments.

## Relation to Prior Work

CryoLVM extends the JEPA self-supervised learning paradigm (Assran et al., 2023) from 2D images and 3D brain imaging (Brain-JEPA) to cryo-EM density maps, replacing the reconstruction-based masked autoencoder objective with representation-space prediction that naturally filters pervasive noise in low-SNR volumes. The SCUNet backbone (Zhang et al., 2023) is repurposed from 2D image denoising to 3D volumetric data, leveraging its dual Swin-Transformer/residual-convolution pathways to jointly model local atomic features and global spatial context. The paper positions itself against task-specific supervised methods (DeepEMhancer, EMReady, EM-GAN, IsoNet) and the flow-based CryoFM, arguing that a unified self-supervised foundation model trained on experimental maps with resolved structures offers better generalizability and robustness on genuinely noisy data than either task-specific training or generative priors trained on curated inputs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first JEPA-based foundation model for 3D cryo-EM density maps, bridging the gap between 2D cryo-EM foundation models (DRACO, CryoFastAR, Cryo-IEF) and the density-map post-processing stage where most current tools remain task-specific. The histogram KL loss providing a 2.6× convergence speedup is a practical contribution for resource-constrained structural biology labs. However, the pretraining corpus of ~7,300 maps is modest for a 'foundation model' claim, the missing wedge task relies on simulated artifacts rather than real cryo-ET data, and only three downstream tasks are evaluated—open questions include whether the learned representations transfer to model building, conformational heterogeneity analysis, or larger-scale EMDB corpora as data throughput continues to grow.
