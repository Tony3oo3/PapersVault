## Problem

Most 3D single object tracking methods adopt a category-specified training paradigm, training separate models per object category, which limits cross-category generalizability and training efficiency. The few category-unified approaches (e.g., CUTrack) underperform state-of-the-art category-specified trackers because their backbones produce poor spatial feature separability between target and background and exhibit significant temporal feature inconsistency across consecutive frames, while also lacking adequate category-agnostic temporal modeling.

## Input / Output

The model consumes 1024 points sampled from a subregion (previous-frame BBox expanded by 1 m on KITTI or 4 m on NuScenes) over a 4-frame window (3 historical + 1 search), along with an initial 3D bounding box and a historical box sequence of length L. It outputs a single 3D bounding box parameterized by center coordinates (x, y, z) and yaw angle ψ, with object size assumed constant and pitch/roll fixed at zero throughout tracking.

## Method

PointRePar is a coarse-to-fine, category-unified 3D tracker built around Mamba-based spatiotemporal relation parsing. For spatial encoding, a U-Shaped Spatial Relation Parsing Mamba (USRPM) performs hierarchical multi-scale feature extraction via weight-shared bidirectional Mamba scanning in a U-Net topology, augmented by a Dynamic Feature Aggregation (DFA) module that adaptively generates per-point virtual offsets and refines features via inverse-distance-weighted interpolation. For temporal modeling, a Temporal Scan Mamba captures point-level frame-to-frame motion, while a Long-term Motion Trajectory Rectification (LMTR) module encodes box-level sequential relations over a longer history (L > T) using a Mamba block on tokenized corner representations with learnable temporal embeddings. A Conditional Gaussian Perturbation (CGP) scheme injects sparsity-scaled Gaussian noise into historical trajectories during training to simulate realistic localization errors. The refined stage decodes the final box via cross-attention between the LMTR-aggregated query and the USRPM/TSM-enhanced point features.

## Results

On NuScenes (the more challenging sparse-scene benchmark), PointRePar in category-unified mode achieves 66.76/79.64 Mean Success/Precision, surpassing the previous best category-unified method MoCUT (51.19/64.63) by +15.57/+15.01. In category-specified mode on the same dataset, it reaches 64.15/76.81, outperforming the SOTA category-specified tracker SiamMo (60.31/72.68) by +3.84/+4.13. On KITTI, the category-unified PointRePar scores 72.0/89.1 Mean Success/Precision, slightly trailing SiamMo (72.3/90.1) but exceeding all other methods. On Waymo Open Dataset (zero-shot transfer from KITTI), PointRePar achieves 49.2/68.2, the best among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against category-unified baselines on NuScenes, PointRePar dominates: it beats MoCUT by +15.57/+15.01 Mean Success/Precision, TrackAny3D by +12.19/+13.39, and SiamCUT by +26.35/+31.10. Against category-specified methods on NuScenes, PointRePar (category-specified mode) outperforms SiamMo by +3.84/+4.13, MBPTrack by +6.67/+6.93, and SeqTrack3D by +8.23/+7.87, with even larger margins over earlier methods (e.g., +43.45/+56.61 over SC3D). On KITTI, PointRePar in category-unified mode (72.0/89.1) slightly trails SiamMo (72.3/90.1) by 0.3/1.0 but exceeds MoCUT by +6.2/+4.1 and TrackAny3D by +4.9/+3.7. On the zero-shot Waymo Open Dataset transfer, PointRePar (49.2/68.2) surpasses SiamMo (47.5/63.6) by +1.7/+4.6 and MoCUT (45.2/58.5) by +4.0/+9.7, confirming superior cross-dataset generalization. The only category where PointRePar underperforms SiamMo is Bus on NuScenes (56.40/55.26 vs. 65.63/62.07), likely due to the small sample size (2,953 frames) for that category.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant category-specified training paradigm in 3D single object tracking (where separate models are trained per object class) by proposing a category-unified framework that jointly trains across all categories. It builds on the Siamese/coarse-to-fine tracking lineage (P2B, BAT, M2Track, SeqTrack3D, SiamMo) but replaces conventional PointNet++ or Transformer backbones with a Mamba-based U-Net architecture (USRPM) for spatial relation parsing, and introduces a dual-level temporal modeling scheme (point-level Temporal Scan Mamba + box-level LMTR) that prior category-unified methods like CUTrack and TrackAny3D lacked. The Dynamic Feature Aggregation module and Conditional Gaussian Perturbation are novel contributions that repurpose adaptive receptive-field ideas and sparsity-aware data augmentation, respectively, for the point cloud tracking setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Category-unified tracking is substantially more practical for deployment (one model handles all object classes) yet prior attempts (CUTrack, TrackAny3D) lagged significantly behind category-specified SOTA; PointRePar closes this gap on NuScenes and WOD and achieves near-parity on KITTI, validating that unified training need not sacrifice performance. The CGP scheme is a generalizable training technique demonstrated to boost unrelated baselines (BAT, M2Track) by large margins on underrepresented categories, suggesting broader utility beyond this architecture. Open questions include whether the approach extends to even more categories or sensor modalities, whether the small KITTI gap to SiamMo (72.0 vs 72.3 mean Success) reflects a fundamental limitation of unified training or is addressable with more data, and whether the Mamba-based spatial parsing generalizes to other 3D perception tasks.
