## Problem

Continuous adversarial training (CAT) for LLMs has shown empirical success in defending against jailbreak attacks by perturbing token embeddings rather than searching the discrete token space, but the mechanism behind this success—why embedding-space perturbations transfer to token-space robustness—remained unexplained. Additionally, no principled guidance existed for how the embedding matrix itself influences the resulting adversarial robustness.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262282/cropped_page27_idx23.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on text prompts: harmful instructions paired with targeted harmful and safe reference responses (from HarmBench) for the adversarial loss, and normal instruction-answer pairs (from UltraChat 200K) for the utility loss. During training, adversarial perturbations δ* are applied to the continuous embedding vectors of each token (constrained by ‖δ_i‖₂ ≤ ε) rather than to discrete token indices, making the search amenable to PGD. The output is the LLM's generated text response, evaluated via attack success rate (robustness) and LC-WinRate (utility).

## Method

The paper introduces a Linear Self-Attention with Embedding (LSA-E) model that adds a trainable embedding matrix W^E to standard linear self-attention, enabling adversarial perturbations in the embedding space to mirror real-world CAT. Under this framework, the authors prove a robust generalization upper bound for ICL linear regression that is inversely correlated with the embedding-space perturbation radius ε and depends on the singular values of W^E: large singular values inflate the numerator while small singular values deflate the denominator of the bound. Motivated by this, they propose ER-CAT, which augments the standard CAT objective with a regularization term equal to the variance of all singular values of the embedding matrix, simultaneously pushing large singular values down and small ones up. Experiments on six LLMs (Vicuna-7B, Mistral-7B, Llama-2-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2B) against six jailbreak attacks show ER-CAT achieves a better robustness-utility tradeoff than vanilla CAT with only ~100–200 seconds of additional training time.

## Results

Across six LLMs and six jailbreak attacks, ER-CAT achieves a better robustness-utility tradeoff than CAT. On Llama-2-7B, ER-CAT reduces GCG ASR from 23.6% (CAT) to 15.6% and BEAST ASR from 17.2% to 10.4%, while maintaining comparable utility (LC-WinRate 65.76% vs. 67.51%). On Vicuna-7B, ER-CAT trades a modest ~4% ASR increase for nearly 2× better utility (LC-WinRate 65.13% vs. 36.66% for CAT). On Qwen2.5-7B, ER-CAT cuts GCQ ASR by 11.2 points (6.6% vs. 17.8%) with only a 3-point utility drop. Time overhead is minimal: ER-CAT adds only 100–200 seconds over CAT per model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is CAT (Xhonneux et al., NeurIPS 2024), the original continuous adversarial training method. ER-CAT does not uniformly dominate CAT on ASR: on Vicuna-7B and Mistral-7B, ER-CAT's ASR is slightly higher (by up to ~4 points on GCG/BEAST), but it recovers nearly 2× the utility (LC-WinRate 65.13% vs. 36.66% for Vicuna; 29.09% vs. 15.76% for Mistral). On Llama-2-7B, ER-CAT clearly wins on robustness, cutting GCG ASR by 8 points (15.6% vs. 23.6%) and BEAST ASR by 6.8 points (10.4% vs. 17.2%) while keeping utility within 2 points. On Qwen2.5-7B, ER-CAT reduces GCQ ASR by 11.2 points (6.6% vs. 17.8%) at a 3-point utility cost. On Llama-3.1-8B and Gemma-2B, ER-CAT matches or beats CAT on most attacks with comparable utility. Overall, the paper's claim is a Pareto improvement on the robustness–utility frontier rather than a strict ASR win across all settings.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two research threads: continuous adversarial training (CAT) for LLMs, introduced by Xhonneux et al. (2024), and in-context learning (ICL) theory for linear transformers, developed by Zhang et al. (2024) and Fu et al. (2025). While CAT achieved empirical success, its mechanism—why embedding-space perturbations transfer to token-space robustness—remained unexplained. The authors bridge this gap by introducing an LSA-E model (linear self-attention with a trainable embedding module) that mirrors the embedding perturbation process in CAT, then proving a robust generalization bound under ICL linear regression. The resulting bound, which depends on the singular values of the embedding matrix, motivates the proposed ER-CAT method that adds a singular-value-variance regularization to the CAT objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first theoretical explanation for why CAT works, providing a principled link between embedding-space adversarial perturbations and token-space jailbreak robustness. The singular-value insight suggests a concrete, low-cost lever (regularizing the embedding matrix spectrum) for improving LLM safety without architectural changes. However, the theory is restricted to linear transformers on linear regression tasks, and the connection to real LLMs is argued via analogy rather than proven. The ablation showing that the regularization coefficient β has minimal effect (due to AdamW's implicit reweighting) somewhat undermines the practical significance of the singular-value mechanism, and it remains open whether the insight generalizes to non-linear attention or deeper architectures.
