## Problem

Existing knowledge graph foundation models (KGFMs) enforce deterministic node-relation equivariance, which forces structurally isomorphic relations to receive identical representations. This architectural constraint prevents them from distinguishing semantically distinct but structurally similar relations (e.g., 'like' vs. 'dislike'), a limitation that cannot be resolved through finetuning. The paper asks how to design KGFMs that are both expressive enough to differentiate such relations and still generalize to unseen entities and relation types.

## Input / Output

FLOCK takes as input a potentially unseen knowledge graph G=(V,E,R) and a link prediction query of the form (h,r,?) for entity prediction or (h,?,t) for relation prediction. For entity prediction it outputs a score ŷ:V→[0,1] over all candidate targets; for relation prediction it outputs ŷ:R→[0,1] over all candidate relations. At test time, P independent stochastic forward passes are averaged to reduce variance, and the base walk count n is adaptively rescaled via a harmonic-mean rule relative to pretraining graph sizes to maintain coverage on KGs of varying scale.

## Method

FLOCK replaces the conventional two-stage message-passing paradigm with a random-walk-based sequence encoding pipeline that respects probabilistic node-relation equivariance (equivariance in distribution rather than pointwise). At each of I residual update steps, it (1) samples non-backtracking uniform random walks from diversified starting locations (query node, a random relation edge, and random nodes), (2) anonymizes all encountered nodes and relations via a recording protocol that assigns sequential IDs (1,2,3… for nodes; α,β,… for relations) in order of first discovery while conditioning on current hidden states and the query, (3) processes each anonymized sequence independently with a bidirectional GRU equipped with RMSNorm and SwiGLU, and (4) aggregates per-position update proposals into node and relation state updates via a multi-head softmax-weighted consensus protocol that suppresses uninformative proposals. The sole source of stochasticity is the random walk sampling, which breaks symmetries at inference time while preserving distributional equivariance. FLOCK is proven to be a universal approximator of all link-invariant functions over KGs of bounded size, and it requires no message passing whatsoever.

## Results

On entity prediction across 54 KGs, FLOCK achieves a total average MRR of 0.391 (zero-shot) and 0.427 (finetuned), outperforming TRIX (0.390/0.418) and ULTRA (0.366/0.408). On relation prediction, FLOCK shows a much larger margin: 0.881 MRR zero-shot vs. TRIX's 0.792 (11.2% relative gain) and 0.907 finetuned vs. 0.804 (12.8% gain). On the synthetic PETALS benchmark, FLOCK achieves 100% accuracy while all deterministic-equivariance baselines (ULTRA, TRIX, MOTIF) are stuck at 50% (random-guess level).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLOCK is compared against three state-of-the-art KGFMs: ULTRA (Galkin et al., ICLR 2024), TRIX (Zhang et al., LoG 2024), and MOTIF (Huang et al., ICML 2025). On entity prediction, FLOCK consistently outperforms both ULTRA and TRIX across all three evaluation splits (inductive e,r, inductive e, transductive) in both zero-shot and finetuned settings, with the largest gains on the fully inductive split (e.g., 0.417 vs. 0.401 MRR finetuned on inductive e,r). On relation prediction, the advantage is substantially larger: FLOCK achieves 0.881 MRR zero-shot versus TRIX's 0.792 and ULTRA's 0.724, and 0.907 finetuned versus 0.804 and 0.759 respectively. On the PETALS diagnostic dataset, FLOCK achieves 100% accuracy while ULTRA, TRIX, and MOTIF all score exactly 50% (chance level), confirming that deterministic node-relation equivariance fundamentally prevents these models from distinguishing structurally isomorphic but semantically distinct relations. The paper attributes FLOCK's relation-prediction advantage to its joint encoding of entities and relations via the sequence processor, whereas ULTRA and TRIX use separate update steps for each.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLOCK departs from the dominant KGFM paradigm (ULTRA, TRIX, MOTIF) that enforces deterministic node-relation equivariance via message passing, arguing this architectural constraint prevents distinguishing structurally isomorphic but semantically distinct relations. It instead draws on the random-walk-based graph learning lineage (DeepWalk, node2vec, RWNN, RUM) and extends probabilistic invariance concepts from general graph learning (Abboud et al. 2021; Kim et al. 2025) to the KG setting, introducing node-relation anonymization as a generalization of prior node-only anonymization. The key conceptual shift is replacing the two-stage, message-passing encoding of relations-then-nodes with a unified random-walk-to-sequence pipeline, and relaxing strict equivariance to invariance in distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FLOCK resolves a fundamental expressiveness gap in KGFMs—deterministic equivariance forces identical representations for structurally isomorphic relations—while provably retaining the inductive bias needed for zero-shot generalization, validated by the PETALS diagnostic where all prior KGFMs fail at 50%. The universal approximator result without any message passing is a clean theoretical contribution. A key open limitation is scalability: ensuring random-walk coverage on large KGs requires many long walks, making the approach computationally expensive for transductive settings with tens of thousands of nodes. Future work on walk-sampling approximations and characterizing approximable functions under restricted walk lengths would strengthen practical applicability.
