## Problem

Existing spatial reasoning benchmarks for VLMs target elementary tasks (left/right discrimination, near/far estimation, object counting) that state-of-the-art reasoning models already solve at over 90% accuracy, approaching saturation. Complex spatial reasoning—encompassing dynamic motion inference, multi-step geometric logic, environment-constrained interaction, and non-egocentric perspective-taking—remains largely unbenchmarked and unsaturated, leaving a significant gap between current evaluation and the spatial cognition required for real-world embodied and robotic tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261220/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each benchmark item pairs an RGB image or video frame (sourced from web imagery, driving exam videos, MME RGB-D data, and HOI4D interaction clips) with a conversationally phrased multiple-choice question (binary or four-option) that avoids rigid templates like 'Is A on the right of B?'. For the PointGraph enhancement, a JSON-style scene graph encoding object identities, centers, and bounding boxes (extracted via Florence-2) is concatenated with the query; for SpatialCoT, a six-view novel-view collage synthesized by InstantMesh is appended as additional visual context. The model outputs a single multiple-choice letter, optionally preceded by chain-of-thought reasoning.

## Method

OmniSpatial is a manually curated benchmark of 8.4K QA pairs organized into four cognitive-psychology-grounded categories (dynamic reasoning, complex spatial logic, spatial interaction, perspective-taking) spanning 50 fine-grained subtasks, with inter-annotator agreement of Krippendorff's α = 0.84. Two auxiliary reasoning strategies are proposed to probe and enhance VLM spatial capabilities: PointGraph, which uses open-vocabulary grounding (Florence-2) to construct an explicit scene graph of object positions and relations that is prepended to the query as structured geometric cues, and SpatialCoT, which synthesizes six novel camera viewpoints via InstantMesh and composes them into a multi-view collage fed alongside the question to stimulate mental-imagery-based reasoning. A training exploration further demonstrates that supervised fine-tuning on the 6.9K OmniSpatial-train subset yields a +7.82-point average gain over zero-shot, substantially outperforming a 200K template-generated corpus (+1.29), and that the learned skills transfer to the external VSI-Bench benchmark.

## Results

On the OmniSpatial benchmark (8.4K QA pairs across 50 subtasks), the best-performing model o3-2025-04-16 achieves 56.33% average accuracy, followed by Gemini-2.5-pro at 55.19% and o4-mini at 52.77%. Among open-source models, InternVL3-78B leads at 49.33%, while the best specialized spatial model (SoFar) reaches only 45.14%. Performance is highly uneven across categories: Geometric Reasoning is the weakest (o3: 29.68%, barely above the 24.98% random baseline), while Ego-Centric perspective taking is strongest (o3: 77.06%). All models remain over 30 points below human-level performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The benchmark reveals a clear hierarchy: reasoning models (o3: 56.33%, Gemini-2.5-pro: 55.19%, o4-mini: 52.77%) substantially outperform standard proprietary VLMs (GPT-4.1: 51.78%, Gemini-2.5-flash: 52.12%) and open-source models (InternVL3-78B: 49.33%, Qwen-VL2.5-72B: 47.85%). Notably, specialized spatial reasoning models consistently underperform general-purpose VLMs—SoFar (45.14%), SpaceThinker (40.42%), and SpatialBot-3B (35.68%) all trail even mid-tier open-source models like InternVL3-38B (48.48%), suggesting that narrow spatial pretraining does not transfer to comprehensive spatial reasoning. Among proprietary models, GPT-4.1 (51.78%) and Gemini-2.5-flash (52.12%) lead, while older models like GPT-4o (47.81%) and Claude-3-5-sonnet (46.86%) lag by 4–5 points. The gap between the best model (o3) and the random baseline (24.98%) is only ~31 points, and Geometric Reasoning remains near-chance for all models (best: Gemini-2.5-pro at 43.30%), indicating a fundamental limitation in 3D spatial imagination. The paper further shows that PointGraph (scene-graph augmentation) and SpatialCoT (novel-view synthesis) yield modest but consistent gains of 1–3 points, with the largest improvements in Dynamic Reasoning and Perspective Taking tracks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the line of spatial reasoning benchmarks (EmbSpatial, SpatialBot-Bench, VSI-Bench, Space3D-Bench) that focus on elementary relations like left/right, near/far, and object counting, which the authors show are approaching saturation (>90% accuracy) for top reasoning models. Rather than building on a specific prior method, OmniSpatial carves new ground by grounding its taxonomy in cognitive psychology (dynamic reasoning, complex spatial logic, spatial interaction, perspective-taking) and replacing template-based annotation with manual curation. The auxiliary strategies—PointGraph (repurposing Florence-2 for scene-graph cues) and SpatialCoT (repurposing InstantMesh for novel-view synthesis in a CoT pipeline)—are novel combinations of existing tools applied to spatial reasoning evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even frontier reasoning models (o3, Gemini-2.5-Pro) plateau at ~56-57% accuracy, with geometric reasoning and non-egocentric perspective-taking remaining near-random, highlighting a substantial gap to human spatial cognition. The training exploration finding—that 6.9K manually curated samples yield +7.82 points while 200K template-generated samples yield only +1.29—challenges the assumption that scale alone improves spatial reasoning and suggests diversity and task complexity matter more than volume. Open questions include whether structured representations (scene graphs, multi-view synthesis) can be integrated natively into VLM architectures rather than as external prompting, and how to close the remaining ~30-point gap to human performance on comprehensive spatial tasks.
