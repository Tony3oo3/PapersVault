## Problem

Existing On-the-Fly Category Discovery (OCD) methods rely on hash-based encodings that map features into low-dimensional spaces for classification, suffering from severe overestimation of category counts and an inability to directly recognize known classes. The paper formally identifies a performance lower bound showing that a plain closed-set classifier under Hungarian matching can match state-of-the-art OCD methods, revealing that hash-based approaches fail to effectively exploit labeled data for novel category identification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263897/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images as input, processed by a DINO-pretrained ViT-B/16 encoder, with features reduced to 42 dimensions via PCA for numerical stability in Gaussian density estimation. During streaming inference, each sample produces either a direct known-class label (when confidence exceeds a soft class-specific threshold) or a novel-cluster assignment determined by maximum posterior probability under a multivariate Gaussian model. A held-out 20% validation subset is used to estimate per-class confidence thresholds and Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage covariances before inference begins.

## Method

AGE decomposes OCD into Open-Set Recognition and real-time novel category discovery. Training uses cross-entropy plus supervised contrastive loss on the labeled support set with a normalized MLP classifier. At inference, a soft thresholding strategy (fusing class-specific and global confidence thresholds via a β coefficient) routes high-confidence samples to direct classification, while low-confidence outliers enter the AGE module. AGE models each known and discovered class as a multivariate Gaussian with Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage covariance and a smoothed global prior (α_k = s/(N_k+s)), then assigns each outlier to the cluster with maximum log-PDF or seeds a new cluster initialized with the global covariance. The approach is inspired by the Chinese Restaurant Process in DPGMM, using a greedy posterior-based assignment rule with a fixed-size sliding window for memory control, and is theoretically grounded by a lemma and proposition showing that known-class distributions serve as valid background priors for novel-class detection under shared-covariance assumptions.

## Results

AGE achieves state-of-the-art performance across all evaluated datasets. On coarse-grained benchmarks, it improves overall accuracy by 9.5% over PHE (e.g., 48.2% vs 34.1% on ImageNet-100, 60.8% vs 55.9% on CIFAR-100). On fine-grained datasets (CUB-200, Stanford Cars, Herbarium19, Pets), the average improvement over PHE is 8.8%, with particularly large gains on novel-class accuracy (e.g., 39.4% vs 27.0% on CUB-200, 58.8% vs 45.4% on Pets). Across all six primary datasets, AGE improves novel-category accuracy by an average of 16.7% over PHE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AGE outperforms all hash-based OCD baselines (SMILE, PHE) by a wide margin on both overall and novel-class accuracy across every dataset. Against the most recent competitor DiffGRE (ICCV 2025), AGE wins on CUB-200 (46.3 vs 42.5 All; 39.4 vs 36.5 New), Stanford Cars (34.8 vs 27.7 All), and Pets (61.5 vs 49.6 All), but loses on Arachnida (42.7 vs 47.7 All; 23.3 vs 29.4 New) and is slightly behind on Animalia novel accuracy (34.2 vs 35.3). Compared to the older adaptive baselines (SLC, MLDG, RankStat, WTA), AGE leads by 10–25 points on overall accuracy, with the largest gaps on ImageNet-100 (48.2 vs ≤32.9) and Herbarium19 (30.9 vs ≤22.9). On known-class (Old) accuracy, AGE matches or exceeds PHE on most datasets (e.g., 87.1 vs 80.6 on ImageNet-100, 59.8 vs 55.8 on CUB-200) but trails slightly on Fungi (66.1 vs 67.9) and Arachnida (73.7 vs 75.7), suggesting the soft-thresholding mechanism occasionally routes a small number of known samples into the novel-discovery path.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the hash-based encoding paradigm that dominates prior OCD methods (SMILE, PHE) by reformulating the task as a combination of Open-Set Recognition and real-time novel category discovery via Gaussian PDF modeling. It draws on the Dirichlet Process GMM / Chinese Restaurant Process literature for online incremental clustering, and repurposes the Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage estimator (originally for high-dimensional covariance estimation) to stabilize per-class covariance in a streaming setting with limited samples. The theoretical lower-bound analysis connects OCD to closed-set classification performance, positioning the task within the broader OSR and category discovery (NCD/GCD) lineage while arguing that prior methods underutilize labeled data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The lower-bound result—that a plain closed-set classifier already approaches SOTA OCD accuracy—challenges the field's reliance on hash-based encodings and motivates a more principled decomposition of the problem. The practical advantage of directly predicting known-class labels (rather than retrieving hash codes) makes the approach more deployable in downstream pipelines. Open questions remain: the OSR component still uses a relatively simple MSP+soft-thresholding strategy that the authors acknowledge could benefit from recent OSR advances, and the streaming constraint means AGE cannot exploit global distribution information (as K-means can), leaving a gap on coarse-grained datasets. Future integration with multimodal/language-model guidance is identified as a promising direction.
