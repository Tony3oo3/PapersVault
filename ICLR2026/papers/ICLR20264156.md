## Problem

Text-to-video diffusion models produce photorealistic frames but frequently violate physical laws (floating objects, implausible collisions, inconsistent trajectories) because their latent representations under-encode motion dynamics. Existing representation-alignment methods (REPA, VideoREPA) align diffusion features with pretrained video encoder features, but these encoders entangle appearance and motion, so alignment defaults to matching static semantics rather than true dynamics. The paper asks how to explicitly target motion in the latent space without external conditioning, simulation, or inference-time overhead.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes a text prompt and produces a generated video through the standard CogVideoX-2B denoising pipeline with no additional inputs or output channels. During training, input videos are resized to 160×240 and truncated to 49 frames; ground-truth optical flow is computed between consecutive frames via RAFT. The motion subspace is a 64-dimensional projection of VideoMAEv2's 768-dimensional token features, and the alignment is applied at the 18th MM-DiT layer of the diffusion transformer.

## Method

MoAlign is a two-stage fine-tuning framework. Stage 1 learns a motion-specific subspace by projecting frozen VideoMAEv2 features (768-d) through a 3D convolutional compressor into a 64-d bottleneck, then supervising the compressed features to predict RAFT optical flow via L1 loss; the dimensionality reduction forces the representation to discard static appearance and retain only dynamic structure. Stage 2 aligns the diffusion model's latent features (projected from 1920-d MM-DiT hidden states to 64-d via a 4-layer MLP) to this motion subspace using a soft relational alignment loss that matches pairwise cosine-similarity matrices both spatially (within each frame) and temporally (across frames), with an exponential temporal weighting that prioritizes local frame vicinity over long-range dependencies. The key distinction from VideoREPA is that alignment targets a disentangled motion-only subspace rather than entangled appearance+motion features, and unlike VideoJAM, the method neither expands the diffusion model's output space nor requires any inference-time modification.

## Results

On VideoPhy2, MoAlign-2B achieves a Joint score of 24.9 (SA=28.8, PC=75.0), outperforming the base CogVideoX-2B (Joint=22.3) and the re-implemented VideoREPA-2B (Joint=23.0). On VideoPhy, MoAlign attains the highest overall Physical Correctness (PC=39.4) across all four interaction types while maintaining the best Semantic Adherence (SA=49.3) among fine-tuned variants. On VBench-2.0, MoAlign improves the Total score to 55.9 (vs. 54.9 for CogVideoX-2B), driven by gains in Commonsense (65.5 vs. 60.2) and Human Fidelity (86.7 vs. 81.1), while VBench Total remains stable at 81.3. A blind user study confirms MoAlign is preferred 68% of the time over CogVideoX-2B and 78% over VideoREPA-2B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the base CogVideoX-2B, MoAlign improves Joint by +2.6 points (24.9 vs. 22.3), with the largest gain in Physical Correctness (+10.5, from 64.5 to 75.0) and a modest SA increase (+1.7). Against VideoREPA-2B (re-implemented under identical training conditions), MoAlign wins on all three VideoPhy2 metrics (SA 28.8 vs. 26.1, PC 75.0 vs. 73.3, Joint 24.9 vs. 23.0); VideoREPA's alignment to entangled appearance-motion features improves PC but degrades SA, whereas MoAlign's motion-only subspace preserves prompt fidelity. On VideoPhy, MoAlign achieves the highest PC in every interaction category (solid-solid 31.7, solid-fluid 40.7, fluid-fluid 56.4) while suffering the smallest SA drop among all fine-tuned variants (49.3 vs. 44.9 for FT and 46.7 for VideoREPA). On VBench-2.0, MoAlign leads in Commonsense (65.5) and Human Fidelity (86.7) but trails CogVideoX-2B on the Physics sub-score (48.8 vs. 53.9), which the authors attribute to missing thermotics/materials data in their training set. On Wan2.1 1.3B, MoAlign yields a more modest but consistent improvement (Joint 25.1 vs. 23.7), confirming cross-architecture generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the representation-alignment line of work (REPA → VideoREPA) by replacing entangled appearance-plus-motion encoder features with a disentangled motion-only subspace, learned by supervising a low-dimensional projection of VideoMAEv2 features to predict optical flow. It departs from VideoREPA's hard token-level matching in favor of soft relational alignment (Token Relation Distillation with temporal weighting), and from VideoJAM by avoiding any expansion of the diffusion model's output space or inference-time flow prediction. The base model is CogVideoX-2B, and the method is also validated on Wan2.1 1.3B, positioning it as a plug-in fine-tuning strategy rather than an architectural change.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical contribution is that motion understanding can be internalized into a pretrained video diffusion model with a lightweight two-stage fine-tune (≈22 h + 4K steps on 4×H100) and zero inference-time overhead, making it attractive for practitioners who cannot afford simulation-based or conditioning-based alternatives. A notable finding is that aligning to disentangled motion features improves both physical plausibility and semantic adherence simultaneously, whereas VideoREPA's entangled alignment trades SA for PC—suggesting that feature entanglement is a real cost in representation alignment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to physics domains absent from training data (thermotics, granular media, material deformation), whether the observed drop in VBench Dynamic Degree reflects a genuine reduction in motion amplitude, and whether the single-layer (18th block) alignment strategy transfers to other DiT architectures.
