## Problem

Existing methods to mitigate overthinking in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs)—such as suppressing reflective keywords or adjusting reasoning length—indiscriminately truncate reasoning, inadvertently inducing underthinking where the model fails to explore sufficient reasoning paths. The paper identifies that no prior approach can simultaneously reduce redundant reasoning steps while preserving the exploration depth needed for correct answers, creating a fundamental trade-off between efficiency and accuracy.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260678/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt as input and produces a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a final answer, identical in format to the base LRM. During inference, at each reasoning step (delimited by double newlines), the method computes stepwise confidence from token-level maximum probabilities and injects a steering vector into the hidden state at the first token of a selected deep layer, adding no extra forward passes beyond standard autoregressive decoding. The steering vector and control function are pre-computed offline from 500 MATH problems and held fixed across all evaluation benchmarks.

## Method

REBALANCE is a training-free, plug-and-play framework that dynamically steers LRM reasoning by modulating hidden states based on real-time confidence signals. It first identifies overthinking steps (low confidence, high variance) and underthinking steps (high confidence, low variance) from a small seen dataset using empirical quantile thresholds, then aggregates the corresponding first-token hidden states from an automatically selected deep layer into two prototypes. The steering vector is the normalized difference between these prototypes, encoding the direction from underthinking to overthinking in latent space. A dynamic control function g(c_s, v_s) = sign(c_s − τ_c^H) · B(c_s, v_s) · tanh(|c_s − τ_c^H|) modulates both the direction (positive to promote exploration, negative to prune redundancy) and the strength (via a variance-aware amplitude with soft saturation) of the steering at each step, ensuring the model stays between the overthinking and underthinking boundaries without manual hyperparameter tuning.

## Results

On six math reasoning benchmarks across four models (0.5B–32B), REBALANCE simultaneously improves Pass@1 by up to +10.0 points (e.g., AIME24 on R1-1.5B: 33.3 vs. 23.3 baseline; AIME25 on R1-7B: 40.0 vs. 30.0) while reducing generated tokens by up to 35.4% (GSM8K on Qwen3-14B: 1,441 vs. 2,231). On MATH-500, gains range from +0.2 to +3.4 points in accuracy with 18–23% token reduction depending on model scale. Cross-domain evaluation on GPQA-Diamond, StrategyQA, and LiveCodeBench confirms generalization, with up to +6.6 accuracy and 29.9% token reduction without any domain-specific tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

REBALANCE outperforms all nine baselines on MATH-500 Pass@1 across all four model scales, with the largest margins over prompt-suppression methods: NoThinking drops accuracy by 4.6–9.2 points (e.g., 75.0 vs. 79.6 on R1-1.5B) while DEER loses 12.6 points on R1-1.5B (67.0 vs. 79.6). On harder benchmarks (AIME24/25, AMC23), REBALANCE achieves the highest accuracy among all methods, e.g., 53.3 on AIME24 for R1-7B (tied with Manifold Steering) and 73.3 for Qwen3-14B (tied with Dynasor-CoT), while also reducing tokens by 13–28% versus the baseline. Compared to SEAL (the closest latent-space competitor), REBALANCE gains +2.8 to +4.4 points on MATH-500 and reduces tokens by 8–21%. Methods relying on external auxiliary models for early-exit decisions (TrimR, FlashThink) underperform REBALANCE on both accuracy and efficiency for QwQ-32B, confirming that intrinsic confidence-based signals provide more reliable control than external stopping criteria. On cross-domain tasks (GPQA-D, StrategyQA, LiveCodeBench), REBALANCE maintains or improves accuracy while cutting tokens by 3–30%, demonstrating that the steering vector and control surface transfer without domain-specific re-fitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

REBALANCE departs from the dominant line of overthinking-mitigation work (keyword suppression in NoWait/NoThinking, length-based SFT/RL in AdaCoT, early-exit in DEER/FlashThink/TrimR) by reframing the problem as a two-sided balance: prior methods reduce redundant reasoning but inadvertently truncate necessary exploration, inducing underthinking. Conceptually, it repurposes the activation-steering / representation-engineering paradigm (Liu et al. 2023; SEAL; Manifold Steering) from hallucination control to reasoning-dynamics control, using confidence and its variance as a continuous, model-internal signal rather than discrete keyword triggers or externally computed difficulty scores. The dynamic control function that modulates steering strength and direction in real time is the key methodological departure from static steering vectors used in prior representation-engineering work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

REBALANCE offers a training-free, plug-and-play mechanism that simultaneously reduces token count (up to ~35%) and improves accuracy (up to ~7 points) across four model families (0.5B–32B) and nine benchmarks, addressing a practical bottleneck for deploying LRMs in resource-constrained settings. The finding that a steering vector extracted from just 500 MATH problems generalizes across model scales and task domains (math, science, code, commonsense) suggests the underlying reasoning-mode geometry is shared, which could accelerate adoption. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-modal reasoning (acknowledged as future work), whether it composes with other efficiency techniques (speculative decoding, quantization), and how it behaves on models with fundamentally different confidence distributions (e.g., the uniformly low-confidence regime noted for some Llama-based models).
