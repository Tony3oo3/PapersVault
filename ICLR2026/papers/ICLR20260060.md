## Problem

Open-vocabulary 3D segmentation faces a persistent trade-off: projecting 2D VLM features into 3D yields semantically rich but geometrically fragmented predictions, while enforcing geometric coherence demands large-scale annotated 3D data. The paper argues this stems from the dominant 'segmentation and matching' paradigm that disconnects geometry from semantics, and hypothesizes that geometric structure is not destroyed during 2D-to-3D transfer but remains latent within the noisy view-aggregated features, recoverable with far less data than learning 3D geometry from scratch.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260060/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, the method consumes multi-view RGB images and camera parameters to construct a 3D point cloud via projection (no external raw point cloud required), with each voxel carrying a 518-dim feature vector (512-dim X-Decoder semantic feature + 6-dim RGB/normal). The output is per-point open-vocabulary semantic labels. Training requires only unlabeled 3D scans (20 scenes ≈ 1.5% of ScanNetV2) with no 3D semantic annotations, relying solely on raw geometry and multi-view imagery.

## Method

GeoPurify decouples semantic and geometric learning: a frozen X-Decoder (DaViT-L) provides semantically rich initial 3D features via multi-view projection, while a small trainable sparse 3D CNN (AffinityPredictor, 4 MinkowskiResBlocks, 128-dim output) learns class-agnostic geometric affinities through Geometric Contrastive Distillation from a frozen Sonata (PTv3) teacher. The distillation uses an InfoNCE loss with a hybrid sampling strategy—48 macro-negatives (globally dissimilar points) for scene-level structure and 16 micro-negatives (spatially proximate but featurally distant points) for fine-grained boundary discrimination. At inference, a Geometry-Guided Pooling module iteratively refines features over T=18 steps by multiplying with a sparse affinity matrix built from sharpened cosine similarity (α=1/20) of the student's geometric embeddings over 96 nearest neighbors, effectively denoising the initial VLM features while preserving semantic content.

## Results

GeoPurify, trained on only ~1.5% of ScanNetV2 (20 scenes) and Matterport3D (20 regions) without any 3D semantic labels, achieves 55.1 mIoU and 72.5 mAcc on ScanNetV2, and 40.2 mIoU and 62.4 mAcc on Matterport3D. This matches or exceeds the mIoU of fully-trained zero-shot methods (e.g., CUA-O3D at 54.1, GGSD at 56.5) while substantially outperforming them on mAcc. On the long-tail ScanNet200 benchmark, GeoPurify sets a new state-of-the-art with 11.9 f-mIoU and 22.8 f-mAcc, and on cross-dataset transfer (Matterport3D→ScanNetV2) it reaches 54.9 mIoU, surpassing the next-best method by 16.3 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The most striking comparison is against CUA-O3D retrained on the identical ~1.5% subset: GeoPurify achieves 55.1 mIoU versus CUA-O3D's 18.1 mIoU on ScanNetV2, a 37-point gap that the authors attribute to GeoPurify's decoupled design (frozen VLM for semantics + lightweight student for geometry) versus CUA-O3D's entangled geo-semantic learning. Against full-data zero-shot methods, GeoPurify's 55.1 mIoU is competitive with CUA-O3D (54.1) and DMA-3D (54.8), slightly below GGSD (56.5) and OV3D (57.3), but it leads on mAcc (72.5 vs. 64.1 and 66.9 respectively). On Matterport3D, GeoPurify's 40.2 mIoU trails OV3D (45.8) and CUA-O3D (41.3) by a few points, though it matches OV3D's mAcc at 62.4. On long-tail ScanNet200, GeoPurify achieves the best f-mIoU (11.9) and f-mAcc (22.8), outperforming RegionPLC (9.1/17.3) and DMA-3D (7.9/15.2). For cross-dataset transfer, GeoPurify dominates: 54.9 mIoU on Matterport3D→ScanNetV2 versus CUA-O3D's 38.6 and OpenScene's 36.5, a margin of 16.3 points over the runner-up.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoPurify departs from the dominant 'Segmentation and Matching' paradigm (LSeg, OpenSeg, SAM+CLIP) that treats 2D segmentation and text matching as separate steps, instead adopting a 'Segmentation as Understanding' framing via the generalist X-Decoder VLM. Its core architectural departure from prior open-vocabulary 3D methods (OpenScene, CUA-O3D, GGSD, OV3D, PGOV3D) is the explicit decoupling of semantics and geometry: rather than learning entangled geo-semantic representations from large-scale pseudo-labels, it distills purely class-agnostic geometric affinities from a frozen 3D SSL teacher (Sonata) into a small student network, then uses those affinities to denoise VLM-projected features at inference. The conceptual hypothesis—that geometric structure is latent within noisy multi-view 2D projections and recoverable via contrastive distillation—extends the CLIP-DINOiser insight from 2D to the 3D setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ~1.5% data efficiency (55.1 mIoU vs. 18.1 for a retrained CUA-O3D on the same subset) offers a practical path to open-vocabulary 3D segmentation without the prohibitive annotation costs that have constrained the field. The strong cross-dataset generalization (54.9 mIoU on Matterport3D→ScanNetV2, +16.3 over next-best) suggests the learned geometric prior is genuinely domain-agnostic, a property that could transfer to outdoor or large-scale scenes. Key limitations remain: the method cannot correct fundamental 2D semantic errors, thin co-planar objects (pictures, shower curtains) still suffer from feature leakage during pooling, and the VLM's semantic ceiling ultimately bounds performance. Open questions include whether the approach scales to outdoor driving scenes, whether iterative pooling can be replaced by a single learned operation, and whether the geometric prior degrades under significant domain shift in object morphology.
