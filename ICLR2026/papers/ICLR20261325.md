## Problem

Existing semantic-based few-shot learning methods rely on single-level textual semantics (either low-level attributes or high-level descriptions) and fuse them with visual features via static MLP modules that are layer-agnostic. This prevents progressive alignment between visual features at different network depths and corresponding semantic levels, and the static fusion cannot adaptively balance cross-modal integration, resulting in limited semantic gains especially in the 1-shot regime.

## Input / Output

The method takes K support images per class (resized to 224×224), a query image, and class names as input. An LLM (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) is conditioned on both class names and support images to generate dual-level textual semantics offline, which are then encoded by CLIP ViT-B/16 into 512-dimensional embeddings. The output is a class label predicted via cosine-similarity-based prototype classification over N-way K-shot episodic tasks with 15 query samples per class.

## Method

DVLA-RL comprises two modules: Dual-level Semantic Construction (DSC) and RL-gated Attention (RLA). DSC queries an LLM for fine-grained attributes conditioned on class names and support images, applies a progressive Top-k selection that iteratively refines a CLIP template embedding to suppress hallucinated attributes, and then synthesizes the selected attributes into a coherent class description, yielding complementary low-level and high-level semantics. RLA formulates cross-modal fusion as a sequential decision process with two reciprocal attention paths (image-guided cross-attention and text-guided self-attention), where a lightweight Beta-distributed policy trained with episodic REINFORCE adaptively gates the mixing weight between the two paths at each Transformer layer. The policy state is constructed from global average pooling of both modalities and their cosine similarity, and the reward combines visual-text alignment with intra-episode accuracy improvement. Shallow layers are fed attribute-level semantics while deeper layers receive description-level semantics, enabling hierarchical alignment where local details are refined early and global context is emphasized later. The overall training uses a two-stage meta-learning protocol (pre-training followed by episodic meta-tuning) with a Visformer-Tiny backbone, optimizing a combined cross-entropy prototype loss and RL loss.

## Results

DVLA-RL achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across nine benchmarks in three FSL scenarios. On general FSL, it reaches 81.69%/88.25% (1-shot/5-shot) on miniImageNet, 83.02%/91.71% on tieredImageNet, and 87.18%/90.59% on CIFAR-FS. On fine-grained FSL, it attains 91.93%/95.06% on CUB, 89.64%/91.42% on Dogs, and 92.95%/96.59% on Cars. On cross-domain FSL, it achieves 67.46%/78.99% on CUB, 69.26%/80.70% on Places, and 23.47%/26.94% on ChestX, all with 95% confidence intervals reported.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DVLA-RL outperforms all 40 baselines across the three FSL scenarios. On general FSL, it surpasses the strongest semantic-based competitor SemFew (CVPR 2024) by 0.65% on tieredImageNet 1-shot (83.02% vs. 82.37%) and by 2.84% on CIFAR-FS 1-shot (87.18% vs. 84.34%), while also exceeding ECER (AAAI 2025) by 0.55% on miniImageNet 1-shot. On fine-grained FSL, the gains are most dramatic: DVLA-RL beats the previous best SUITED (AAAI 2025) by 5.91% on CUB 1-shot (91.93% vs. 86.02%) and by 13.09% on Dogs 1-shot (89.64% vs. 76.55%). On cross-domain FSL, it exceeds SVasP (AAAI 2025) by 8.39% on CUB 1-shot (67.46% vs. 59.07%) and by 10.19% on Places 1-shot (69.26% vs. 59.07%), while on the challenging ChestX dataset it edges out MEFP (NeurIPS 2024) by 0.65% (1-shot) and 0.24% (5-shot). The ablation in Table 5a further confirms that replacing RLA with the static MLP fusion of SP, SemFew, or ECER (using identical LLM-generated text) consistently degrades accuracy by 2.3–2.9%, validating the adaptive RL-gated mechanism as the key differentiator.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the semantic-based few-shot learning line (SP, SemFew, ECER, SIFT) that augments visual prototypes with language-derived semantics, but departs from them in two key ways: it constructs dual-level semantics (fine-grained attributes for low-level grounding plus coherent class descriptions for high-level understanding) rather than relying on a single semantic level, and it replaces static MLP-based cross-modal fusion with a reinforcement-learned stochastic gate (REINFORCE with a Beta policy) that adaptively balances self-attention and cross-attention per layer. The classification backbone follows the standard ProtoNet episodic paradigm with a ViT-based Visformer-Tiny, while LLM-generated semantics (via Qwen2.5-VL-32B) and CLIP text embeddings provide the linguistic signal. The authors claim this is the first use of reinforcement learning for vision-language alignment in few-shot learning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The hierarchical design—shallow layers attending to attribute-level cues and deeper layers to description-level semantics—offers a principled way to align cross-modal information with the natural depth hierarchy of visual features, and the strong fine-grained FSL margins (5.4–15.3% over prior SOTA on CUB, Dogs, Cars) suggest this alignment is particularly valuable when inter-class differences are subtle. However, the modest gains on ChestX (≈0.4–0.7% over prior best) highlight that semantic priors have limited discriminative power in domains with highly overlapping visual manifestations and severe distribution shift. Open questions include whether the RL gate learns genuinely layer-specific policies or converges to near-static weights, how the approach scales to video or 3D modalities, and whether the offline LLM semantic generation step remains practical for open-vocabulary or streaming settings.
