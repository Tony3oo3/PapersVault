## Problem

Precipitation field generation without radar remains unsolved: radar-based methods are geographically limited and fixed-resolution, satellite-only products carry substantial bias and operate at fixed grids, and classical gauge interpolation (Barnes, kriging, multiquadric) blurs sharp convective boundaries. No prior framework fuses satellite imagery with sparse automatic weather station (AWS) observations to produce a continuous, resolution-free precipitation field that preserves fine-scale structure.

## Input / Output

The method takes two heterogeneous inputs at each time step: a dense GK2A geostationary satellite brightness temperature image (IR 10.5 μm, 2 km resolution) and an irregular set of sparse AWS gauge rainfall measurements at fixed but non-uniform spatial coordinates. The output is a continuous 2D precipitation field defined over an arbitrary spatial domain, queryable at any resolution (e.g., 0.5 km, 2 km, or continuous coordinates) without retraining, rather than a fixed-resolution grid. This makes the task a hybrid of image-plus-point fusion, continuous field reconstruction, and super-resolution, since the 2 km satellite input must support rendering at much finer scales.

## Method

QCGS is a three-stage pipeline: (1) a ConvNeXt U-Net encoder-decoder fuses satellite features with a Graph Attention Network embedding of AWS observations via cross-attention to produce a coarse surrogate rainfall field; (2) a rainfall-aware sampling strategy selects K=6000 query points as a convex mixture of gradient (edge), uniform, and heavy-rain intensity terms restricted to the rain-support mask; (3) a five-layer MLP implicit neural representation with sinusoidal positional encoding predicts per-point 2D Gaussian parameters (σx, σy, ρ, α) conditioned on local satellite features. The key insight is that classical Gaussian-weighted interpolation is a special case of 2D Gaussian Splatting with fixed isotropic kernels, while QCGS learns anisotropic, spatially adaptive Gaussians and renders only precipitation-support regions, avoiding computation in dry areas. At AWS stations with nonzero rainfall, the Gaussian amplitude is set directly to the observed value, anchoring the field to ground truth. Training is two-stage (proposal network first, then Gaussian rendering on fixed proposals) with MSE reconstruction loss plus L1 regularization on covariances and amplitudes.

## Results

On the snapshot evaluation (1154 cases), QCGS trained at 2 km and rendered at 0.5 km achieves RMSE 1.23, CSI 0.74, and FSS 0.91, outperforming the best data-driven baseline BBDM (RMSE 1.68, CSI 0.64, FSS 0.84) and the best classical interpolation Kriging (RMSE 2.43, CSI 0.50, FSS 0.69). At the 2 km native resolution, QCGS reaches RMSE 1.00, CSI 0.76, and FSS 0.96. For daily accumulation (70 cases), QCGS achieves RMSE 6.68 and FSS 0.99, roughly halving the RMSE of IMERG (14.08), GSMaP (15.89), and MSWEP (12.44) while matching or exceeding their CSI scores.

## Baselines & Comparisons

QCGS outperforms all three data-driven baselines (Pix2PixHD, NPM, BBDM) on every metric at the snapshot scale, despite being trained at 2 km while those models are trained and evaluated at 0.5 km; the gap is largest in CSI (0.74 vs. 0.59–0.64) and FSS (0.91 vs. 0.71–0.84). Against classical interpolation methods (Barnes, Kriging, 3DMQ), QCGS reduces RMSE by roughly 60% (1.00 vs. 2.43–2.56 at 2 km) and lifts CSI from ~0.47–0.50 to 0.76, reflecting the advantage of learned anisotropic Gaussians over fixed isotropic kernels. Compared to operational satellite products, QCGS achieves substantially lower RMSE at all temporal scales (e.g., 6.68 vs. 12.44–15.89 for daily accumulation) and higher FSS (0.99 vs. 0.82–0.92), though MSWEP marginally edges QCGS on daily CSI (0.95 vs. 0.93). At the hourly-mean scale, QCGS also improves LPIPS over IMERG and GSMaP (0.21 vs. 0.34–0.38), indicating better perceptual fidelity. The one consistent trade-off is that QCGS depends on sparse AWS gauges for local anchoring, whereas IMERG, GSMaP, and MSWEP are purely satellite-based and globally deployable without ground stations.

## Relation to Prior Work

The paper's central conceptual move is recognizing that classical Gaussian-weighted interpolation (Barnes, kriging, multiquadric) is a special case of 2D Gaussian Splatting with fixed isotropic kernels, then generalizing it via an INR-based estimator that predicts learnable anisotropic covariance and amplitude per point. This departs from prior satellite-to-radar translation work (NPM, Sat2Radar) by adding sparse AWS gauge fusion and resolution-free rendering, and from prior satellite-gauge fusion methods (Benoit 2021, Ruan et al. 2025) by producing a continuous field rather than a fixed-grid correction. The selective, query-conditioned rendering strategy extends standard 2DGS (which renders the full image plane) to evaluate only rainfall-support regions, borrowing the efficiency principle from 3DGS's front-to-back compositing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

QCGS offers a practical pathway to radar-free, resolution-flexible precipitation fields that could serve as initial conditions for NWP or training data for data-driven forecasters, particularly in regions where radar coverage is limited or costly. Two key limitations remain open: the method depends on AWS gauge density (restricting applicability to well-gauged regions) and lacks temporal coherence across frames, which degrades downstream nowcasting performance. The observation that QCGS fields align more closely with AWS than with radar-derived rainfall hints at implicit bias correction, but this has only been demonstrated at regional scale and warrants further investigation before the approach can be considered a viable radar substitute.
