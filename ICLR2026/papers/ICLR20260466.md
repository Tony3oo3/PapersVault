## Problem

Rectified flows, a deterministic ODE-based generative method that produces straight transport paths between distributions, have been widely successful in finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces but their extension to infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces remained unexplored. Existing functional generative models such as functional flow matching (Kerrigan et al., 2024) rely on restrictive measure-theoretic assumptions (absolute continuity of conditional measures) that are difficult to verify and can fail even in finite dimensions. This paper establishes a rigorous functional formulation of rectified flow in separable Hilbert spaces that removes these assumptions while unifying several existing functional ODE-based models under a single framework.

## Input / Output

The model takes a random initial sample Z_0 drawn from a Gaussian (or the stationary distribution of a functional SDE) in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space and produces a sample from the target data distribution by numerically integrating a learned ODE. For image tasks, each image is treated as a continuous function from [0,1]² to ℝ (MNIST, 32×32) or ℝ³ (CelebA, 64×64), and the model can generate samples at arbitrary resolutions (e.g., 64×64 or 128×128) by evaluating the learned function at different point sets. For PDE tasks, it generates Navier-Stokes velocity fields on a 2D torus, with the initial condition sampled from a ½-Matern kernel process.

## Method

The core theoretical contribution defines the expected velocity field v^X(t,x) = E[Ẋ_t | X_t = x] for a pathwise C¹ random process in a separable Hilbert space and proves, via the superposition principle for continuity equations (Stepanov and Trevisan, 2017), that the deterministic ODE z(t) = z(0) + ∫₀ᵗ v^X(s,z(s))ds preserves the marginal distributions of the original process. This pathwise formulation bypasses the Radon-Nikodym derivative and absolute-continuity assumptions required by functional flow matching. The framework further shows that both FFM and functional probability flow ODEs arise as special cases of a nonlinear interpolation X_t = α_t X_1 + β_t X_0, providing a unified lens. Practically, the velocity field is approximated by one of three architectures—implicit neural representations (with MAML-style modulation), vision transformers, or neural operators—all operating on finite pointwise evaluations of the function, enabling variable-resolution generation without architectural changes.

## Results

On CelebA 64×64 with a Vision Transformer backbone, FRF achieves FID 6.63 and FID-CLIP 3.70, outperforming the same-architecture FDP baseline (FID 11.00, FID-CLIP 6.55) by 40% and 43% respectively. On MNIST 32×32 with an INR backbone (~0.1M params), FRF achieves FID 0.41 versus 0.43 for FDP. On the Navier-Stokes PDE dataset with a neural operator backbone, FRF achieves the lowest density MSE of 2.39×10⁻⁵, roughly 1.9× better than FFM (4.50×10⁻⁵) and over 400× better than DDO (9.61×10⁻³).

## Baselines & Comparisons

FRF outperforms all seven baselines across all three datasets. On CelebA, FRF (ViT) beats the same-architecture FDP (ViT) by 4.37 FID points (6.63 vs 11.00) and 2.85 FID-CLIP points (3.70 vs 6.55), and surpasses ∞-DIFF on FID-CLIP (3.70 vs 4.57) while using only ~20% of its parameters (20M vs 100M). On MNIST, FRF (INR) edges out FDP (INR) with FID 0.41 vs 0.43 at matched parameter count (~0.1M). On Navier-Stokes, FRF achieves the best density MSE (2.39×10⁻⁵), beating FFM by 1.9×, Functional DDPM by 4.3×, DDO by 402×, and GANO by 174×. Notably, all comparisons use identical architectures adopted from the respective baseline papers, so the gains are attributed solely to the rectified-flow training objective rather than increased model capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper lifts rectified flow (Liu et al., 2022) from finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces to general separable Hilbert spaces, using the superposition principle for continuity equations (Stepanov and Trevisan, 2017) as the core proof technique to establish marginal distribution preservation in infinite dimensions. It positions itself as a unifying framework: both Functional Flow Matching (Kerrigan et al., 2024) and functional probability flow ODEs (Na et al., 2025) are shown to be special cases of the proposed nonlinear rectified flow parameterization, while also removing the restrictive absolute-continuity assumptions required by FFM. Empirically, the paper builds directly on the codebase and architectures of Franzese et al. (2023) for functional diffusion processes, ensuring fair architectural comparisons.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a rigorous, assumption-light theoretical foundation for functional generative modeling that subsumes several existing ODE-based approaches under a single Hilbert-space formulation, which should simplify future theoretical analysis and method design in this area. The removal of the measure-theoretic assumptions from FFM is practically significant since those conditions are acknowledged as difficult to verify even in finite dimensions. Open questions include extending the framework to general Banach spaces (blocked by the reliance on orthonormal bases) and applying it to broader data modalities such as irregularly sampled time series and 3D geometric objects. The empirical validation, while positive, is limited to relatively small-scale benchmarks (MNIST, CelebA 64×64, Navier-Stokes), leaving open whether the advantages persist at larger scale.
