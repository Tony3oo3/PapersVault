## Problem

Maximizing conditional mutual information (CMI) of a teacher network improves knowledge distillation, but existing approaches like MCMI require fine-tuning the teacher's weights, which is impractical when the teacher is fixed or proprietary. Additionally, MCMI's fixed-centroid approximation introduces CMI estimation errors, and alternative input-perturbation methods (CKD, TALD) incur high computational costs from generating multiple compressed or adversarial variants per image.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265180/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes standard RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-100, 224×224 for ImageNet) and produces a single JPEG-perturbed version of each image via a differentiable JPEG layer with 128 trainable quantization parameters. The perturbed image is fed to a completely frozen teacher, whose soft class-probability outputs serve as supervision for the student. The JPEG layer is plug-and-play: it is trained offline once and can be removed after distillation, leaving the teacher and student in their conventional form with no residual architectural change.

## Method

DJIP inserts a trainable differentiable JPEG layer (using a soft quantizer that replaces the non-differentiable uniform quantizer) before a frozen teacher, so that only the JPEG coding parameters w are optimized while the teacher remains untouched. The key theoretical contribution is a novel alternating optimization algorithm that reformulates the CMI maximization objective as a double minimization over w and a dummy backward-channel distribution Q(x|i,y), derived via the cross-entropy inequality. In each iteration, the algorithm first updates class centroids S_y and the empirical Q analytically (given fixed w), then updates w via SGD on the combined CE + DJIP loss. This avoids MCMI's fixed-centroid approximation by dynamically updating centroids at each step, while requiring only 128 trainable parameters versus millions in MCMI. The objective balances cross-entropy loss against CMI maximization via a hyperparameter λ, and the framework is agnostic to the specific perturbation mechanism, demonstrated with both JPEG quantization and a convolutional autoencoder.

## Results

On CIFAR-100, replacing the standard CE teacher with a DJIP teacher consistently improves student Top-1 accuracy across all 13 evaluated distillation methods and 13 teacher-student pairs, with gains ranging from +0.04% to +2.44% (e.g., SP: 73.48→75.92 for ResNet-32×4→ShuffleNetV1). On ImageNet, DJIP yields up to +1.01% Top-1 accuracy improvement (AT: 69.56→70.57 for ResNet-50→MobileNetV1). The most dramatic gains appear in cross-paradigm ViT distillation on CIFAR-100, where DJIP achieves up to +4.11% (KD: 69.86→73.97 for ViT-S→DeiT-T). Teacher CMI values increase substantially (e.g., 0.026→0.501 for WRN-40-2), confirming the mechanism of improved knowledge transfer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DJIP is evaluated as a teacher-side enhancement that is orthogonal to all 18 compared distillation methods (KD, DKD, DIST, WTTM, CC, RKD, AT, FitNet, FT, SP, ITRD, CRD, LSKD, WSLD, ReviewKD, MCMI, CKD, TALD), consistently improving student accuracy by 0.04–4.11% depending on the method and architecture pair. Against MCMI (Ye et al., 2024), DJIP is additive: prepending the JPEG layer to an MCMI-trained teacher yields an additional +0.92% (SP: 73.29→74.21 for VGG-13→VGG-8), confirming orthogonality since MCMI optimizes teacher weights while DJIP optimizes only 128 quantization parameters. Against CKD (Salamah et al., 2025a), DJIP matches or exceeds performance while using a single fixed quantization table shared across all images rather than per-image adaptive table selection, reducing computational overhead. Against TALD (Nguyen-Duc et al., 2023), DJIP achieves comparable or better accuracy at significantly lower runtime (e.g., 2572s vs. 5830s for VGG-13→VGG-8) and lower GPU memory (624 MB vs. 1060 MB). The largest gains occur when the teacher-student capacity gap is widest (cross-architecture or cross-paradigm pairs), suggesting DJIP is most beneficial when the student struggles to replicate the teacher's full representational capacity.

## Relation to Prior Work

DJIP sits at the intersection of two KD research threads: student-oriented teacher training (notably MCMI, which maximizes CMI by fine-tuning teacher weights) and input-perturbation-based distillation (CKD, TALD, which modify inputs rather than the teacher). It departs from MCMI by freezing the teacher entirely and optimizing only 128 JPEG quantization parameters, and from CKD by replacing discrete quality-factor selection with a differentiable JPEG layer that explores a continuous compression space. The novel alternating optimization algorithm (Theorem 1) resolves MCMI's fixed-centroid approximation by reformulating CMI maximization as a double minimization with analytically updatable centroids, while the differentiable JPEG layer is inherited from the JPEG-DL framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DJIP offers a practically attractive solution for the common scenario where the teacher is frozen, proprietary, or already deployed: a plug-and-play module with only 128 trainable parameters that consistently improves student accuracy across CNNs, ViTs, and cross-paradigm settings without touching teacher weights. The orthogonality to MCMI (stacking both yields additional gains) suggests input-space and weight-space CMI optimization are complementary, opening a path toward combining multiple teacher-enhancement strategies. Open questions include whether the specific JPEG mechanism is essential (the autoencoder experiment in Appendix A.7 suggests the framework is codec-agnostic but with smaller gains), whether the approach transfers to LLM distillation, and whether the modest per-method improvements (typically 0.2–1.5%) compound meaningfully in multi-stage or iterative distillation pipelines.
