## Problem

Traditional brain MRI registration treats volumetric (subcortical) and surface-based (cortical) alignment as separate problems, forcing researchers to combine solutions ad hoc and risking inconsistencies at the cortical-subcortical boundary. The standard joint framework, CVS, requires hours per pair and depends on pre-extracted cortical meshes, making it impractical for large-scale studies. No existing method efficiently and consistently aligns both cortical and subcortical structures in a single unified pass.

## Input / Output

At inference, the method takes only a pair of 3D T1-weighted brain MRI scans (affine-aligned to Talairach and cropped to ~10 mm margin) and produces a 3D diffeomorphic deformation field for the volume and a 2D spherical deformation field for the cortex in a single forward pass. During training, it additionally consumes FreeSurfer-extracted cortical surface meshes and their spherically inflated parameterizations, but these are not required at test time. The output deformation fields are regular with negligible folding (<0.2% folds), and the method runs in milliseconds versus ~2.5 hours for CVS.

## Method

NeurAlign jointly trains a 3D U-Net (volumetric) and a 2D U-Net (spherical) in a single unsupervised framework. The 3D network predicts a stationary velocity field integrated into a diffeomorphic displacement field, while the 2D network operates on stereographically projected cortical surfaces with sin(θ) distortion correction and circular padding at the poles. The key innovation is a cortical consistency loss that penalizes disagreement between the 3D displacement sampled at mesh vertices and the 3D displacement recovered by composing the 2D spherical warp with the inverse spherical map, thereby coupling the two deformation fields without requiring a hard constraint. Training combines local NCC image similarity, geometric descriptor matching (sulcal depth and mean curvature) on the sphere, Jacobian-based regularization, and an optional soft Dice structural loss. This yields a single forward-pass registration that is orders of magnitude faster than CVS while achieving up to 7 Dice points improvement on cortical parcellations.

## Results

NeurAlign achieves the highest cortical Dice score across all three held-out datasets: 0.698 on OASIS-1 & ADNI, 0.683 on IXI, and 0.703 on Mindboggle-101, improving over the next-best method by up to 7.5 Dice points (Mindboggle). Subcortical Dice is competitive (0.747, 0.81, 0.823), with only a 1.5-point drop on IXI relative to uGradICON. Deformation field regularity is substantially better than CVS: NeurAlign produces 0.08–0.17% folds and SD log det J of 0.71–0.83, versus 1.7–2.2% folds and SD log det J of 7.4–8.7 for CVS. Inference runs in milliseconds per pair, compared to 2.5 ± 0.5 hours for CVS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against CVS (the standard FreeSurfer joint registration), NeurAlign wins on cortical Dice across all three datasets (0.698 vs 0.681 on OASIS-1 & ADNI; 0.683 vs 0.582 on IXI; 0.703 vs 0.535 on Mindboggle) and on subcortical Dice on OASIS-1 & ADNI (0.747 vs 0.715) and Mindboggle (0.823 vs 0.766), while trailing by only 1.5 points on IXI (0.810 vs 0.814). Field regularity is dramatically better: NeurAlign produces 0.08–0.17% folds and SD log det J ≈ 0.7–0.8, whereas CVS yields 1.7–2.2% folds and SD log det J of 7.4–8.7, and CVS requires 2.5 ± 0.5 hours per pair versus milliseconds for NeurAlign. Against uGradICON (and its segmentation-guided variant), NeurAlign improves cortical Dice by 5–8.5 points on every dataset (e.g., 0.703 vs 0.626 on Mindboggle) but is slightly lower on subcortical Dice on IXI (0.810 vs 0.825). Against SynthMorph and SynthMorph-wm, NeurAlign gains 4–13 points on cortical Dice and matches or exceeds subcortical Dice on all datasets. Against VoxelMorph, NeurAlign improves cortical Dice by 4–13 points; subcortical Dice is marginally lower on OASIS-1 & ADNI (0.747 vs 0.756) but higher on IXI and Mindboggle. Against FireANTs, NeurAlign wins on cortical Dice by 4–11 points and on subcortical Dice on OASIS-1 & ADNI and Mindboggle, while FireANTs has slightly fewer folds on IXI (1.38% vs 0.08% — actually NeurAlign is lower here, so NeurAlign wins on regularity too).

## Relation to Prior Work

NeurAlign unifies two previously separate registration paradigms: 3D diffeomorphic volumetric registration (following the VoxelMorph architecture with stationary velocity fields) and 2D spherical cortical registration (following the planar stereographic projection approach of Zhao et al., 2019). The key departure from the standard CVS framework (Postelnicu et al., 2008), which sequentially propagates surface deformation into the volume via an elastic PDE, is a novel cortical consistency loss that jointly couples the two networks during training, replacing the hard compositional constraint with a soft MSE penalty sampled at mesh vertices. This transforms a multi-hour sequential optimization into a single forward pass of two coupled U-Nets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is substantial: NeurAlign replaces a 2.5-hour-per-pair sequential pipeline with millisecond inference while requiring no cortical meshes or segmentations at test time, making large-scale population studies far more tractable. The up-to-7.5 Dice point cortical alignment gain over the next best method validates that geometric surface features (sulcal depth, curvature) carry information that purely intensity-based volumetric methods cannot capture. Open questions include generalization to pediatric brains, lower-quality clinical scans, and multimodal acquisitions; the current method is limited to adult T1w MRI and requires surface extraction during training, which can fail on challenging scans.
