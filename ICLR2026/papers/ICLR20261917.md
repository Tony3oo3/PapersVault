## Problem

Generalist vision-language-action (VLA) robot policies, when finetuned on small demonstration sets (≤100 episodes) for a new task, overfit to the specific demonstrations: they lose generalist capabilities on other tasks and fail to generalize to even minor variations (new object positions, viewpoints, backgrounds) of the target task. Existing finetuning strategies—full fine-tuning, co-finetuning, LoRA, or freezing subnetworks—do not adequately transfer the pretrained model's robustness to the new skill in these low-data regimes.

## Input / Output

The policy consumes two RGB camera images (base and wrist, resized to 224×224), a proprioceptive state vector (7D joint angles + gripper for DROID; 7D end-effector position/rotation + gripper for LIBERO), and a natural-language task instruction, and outputs action chunks (10-step × 8D joint-velocity/gripper for DROID; 50-step × 32D padded actions for LIBERO). The RETAIN method itself takes two weight tensors—the pretrained checkpoint θ_pre and the finetuned checkpoint θ_ft—and produces a single merged weight tensor θ̃ via element-wise linear interpolation, with no additional inference-time cost.

## Method

RETAIN linearly interpolates the pretrained and finetuned policy weights in parameter space: θ̃ = (1−α)·θ_pre + α·θ_ft, where α is a single tunable merging coefficient selected on one OOD validation scene. Two extensions improve performance: (1) co-finetuning, where the finetuning loss mixes target-task and pretraining data so that θ_ft retains more generalist structure before merging; and (2) modality-specific merging, which assigns separate coefficients α_v, α_l, α_a to the vision encoder, language-model backbone, and action expert respectively. A key empirical finding is that it often suffices to merge only the language-model backbone parameters (α_l < 1) while keeping the vision encoder and action expert at their finetuned values (α_v = α_a = 1), matching full-parameter merging performance. The approach also supports continual skill acquisition by iteratively applying the merge: θ̃_n = (1−α)·θ̃_{n−1} + α·θ_ft,n, accumulating new tasks into a single policy without catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

On real-world DROID tasks, RETAIN achieves ~80% OOD success on whiteboard and >60% on plates, compared to 30–50% for the best baseline finetuning methods (Task-FT, Co-FT, LoRA, Freeze-FT), representing a ~40% absolute improvement in OOD generalization. On in-distribution (ID) evaluations, RETAIN remains competitive with baselines (70–80%+), and on generalist evaluations it matches the pretrained model's performance, showing no catastrophic forgetting. In LIBERO simulation, similar trends hold but with smaller OOD gains, attributed to the less general base model (5.3k trajectories vs. 76k for DROID). RETAIN-co-FT consistently outperforms RETAIN-task-FT across all three evaluation settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RETAIN decisively outperforms all five baselines (Task-FT, Co-FT, LoRA, Freeze-FT, Scratch) on OOD evaluations, achieving 60–80% success versus 30–50% for the strongest baselines (Co-FT and LoRA) on DROID real-world tasks. On ID evaluations, RETAIN is competitive with Task-FT and Freeze-FT (which fit the demonstrations most tightly) but slightly trails them, while LoRA and Co-FT are somewhat more constrained and score marginally lower on ID. On generalist task retention, RETAIN-co-FT matches or exceeds the pretrained model's performance (~70%), whereas Task-FT degrades to ~40–50% and LoRA/Freeze-FT retain ~60–65%. In the sequential (continual) learning setting, RETAIN outperforms Co-FT on both the first task (plates) and the second task (whiteboard) under ID and OOD evaluations, demonstrating superior retention of previously learned skills. In LIBERO simulation, the OOD advantage over Co-FT is smaller (attributed to the less general 5.3k-trajectory base model), but RETAIN still consistently leads on OOD and generalist metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

RETAIN directly extends the weight-space interpolation technique from vision and language model merging (Wortsman et al., 2022a,b; Ilharco et al., 2022) to the multi-modal setting of vision-language-action (VLA) robot policies. While prior robot policy finetuning work has explored regularization strategies (LoRA, co-finetuning, parameter freezing) to mitigate overfitting in low-data regimes, this paper is the first to apply parameter merging to generalist robot policies and to analyze modality-specific merging weights across the vision encoder, language backbone, and action expert. The continual learning formulation (Eq. 4) also connects to the broader continual learning literature but differs in inheriting and propagating the pretrained model's generalization ability rather than merely preventing forgetting of previously seen skills.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a single hyperparameter (α) and a one-line weight averaging step yield ~40% higher OOD success rates over the best prior finetuning methods, with no additional inference cost, making it immediately adoptable for any VLA policy finetuning pipeline. The finding that merging only the language model backbone parameters often suffices is a useful practical simplification. However, the paper acknowledges it does not fully understand the mechanism behind why linear interpolation in weight space preserves generalization, and the merging coefficient α still requires per-task tuning (though the range is narrow). Open questions include whether the approach extends to fundamentally different task distributions (not just variations of a demonstrated task) and whether more principled merging schedules could eliminate the need for α tuning.
