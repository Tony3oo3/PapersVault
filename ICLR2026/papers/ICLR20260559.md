## Problem

Existing domain generalization methods like LP-FT and DeFT partition network parameters into stable (backbone) and unstable (prediction head) groups at a coarse granularity, misclassifying backbone layers that are actually less stable than the head. Moreover, they apply a uniform EMA update coefficient to all layers despite up to 10× disparities in gradient variance within each group, leading to underutilized information and degraded generalization.

## Input / Output

The method is architecture- and task-agnostic: for super-resolution it consumes 48×48 LR patches (200×200 at inference) and outputs HR patches; for classification it takes 224×224 RGB images and outputs class labels; for segmentation it processes 512×512 images into pixel-wise masks; for NLP it handles text sequences. A key structural detail is that during training the network is duplicated into primary and auxiliary copies with complementary layer freezing, but at inference a single composite network is assembled from the frozen layers of both branches, so test-time memory and latency match the baseline.

## Method

LDT first identifies stable and unstable layers by computing per-layer gradient variance over a held-out source-domain subset (after a warm-up stage that initializes the prediction head), selecting the top-Ratio^U layers by variance as unstable. It then trains a dual-branch architecture with cross-freezing: the primary network updates only stable layers via backpropagation while its unstable layers are frozen and updated via EMA from the auxiliary network's unstable layers, and vice versa for the auxiliary network. The Dynamic Parameter Update (DPU) strategy refines this by assigning each frozen layer a layer-specific EMA coefficient derived from its rank within the stable or unstable group's variance distribution—high-variance layers receive larger coefficients (slower, more-averaged updates) while low-variance layers receive smaller coefficients (faster adaptation). At inference, the composite network concatenates the frozen stable layers from the auxiliary network with the frozen unstable layers from the primary network.

## Results

On the DRealSR super-resolution benchmark (MambaIR backbone, Olympus as source domain), MambaIR+LDT achieves the best PSNR across all five target camera branches: 31.36 (Pan), 32.15 (Sony), 31.51 (DSC), 30.57 (IMG), and 32.80 (Canon), with the largest gain of +0.54 dB over DeFT on Sony. The method also generalizes to image classification (VLCS: up to +5.19% accuracy over FT with ResNet-18), semantic segmentation (Cityscapes→BDD100K: 43.68 mIoU vs. 42.40 for DeFT), and NLP text classification (Amazon Review: 0.9000 vs. 0.8800 for DeFT). Training overhead is modest: LDT uses 20.25 GB GPU memory vs. 15.27 GB for the baseline, with inference memory unchanged at 2.7 GB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LDT outperforms the closest parameter-correlation baseline DeFT on PSNR across all five camera branches, with the most notable margin of +0.54 dB on Sony (32.15 vs. 31.61) and +0.40 dB on Canon (32.80 vs. 32.40); however, DeFT retains a slight SSIM edge on Pan, IMG, and Canon. Against LP-FT, LDT achieves substantially larger gains (e.g., +1.18 dB on Sony, +1.16 dB on Canon), confirming that the coarse backbone/head split in LP-FT is insufficient. Among feature-perturbation methods, LDT surpasses START by +0.74 dB on Sony and DTAM by +0.86 dB on Sony, while Wang et al. (2024a) remains competitive on SSIM for Canon (0.9269 vs. 0.9246) and IMG (0.8775 vs. 0.8705) despite LDT's PSNR lead. Domain adaptation methods IODA and SRTTA, which require target-domain access, are clearly outperformed by LDT on PSNR (e.g., +1.39 dB over IODA on Sony, +2.32 dB over SRTTA on Pan). On secondary tasks, LDT consistently beats DeFT: +5.19% accuracy on VLCS-V (ResNet-18), +1.27 mIoU on BDD100K segmentation, and +0.02 accuracy on Amazon Review NLP classification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the parameter-correlation-based domain generalization line initiated by LP-FT (Kumar et al., 2022) and DeFT (Pahk et al., 2025), both of which use a warm-up + fine-tuning two-stage process with dual-branch architectures and EMA-based parameter stabilization. The key departure is replacing the coarse backbone-vs-head partition with a fine-grained, layer-wise split driven by per-layer gradient variance, revealing that certain backbone layers are actually less stable than the prediction head. Additionally, the Dynamic Parameter Update (DPU) strategy refines the uniform EMA coefficient into per-layer coefficients ranked by gradient variance, addressing amplitude disparities that prior methods ignored. The theoretical analysis (Theorems 1–2) formalizes how unstable-layer gradients propagate through all other layers via backpropagation, providing a principled justification for the isolation mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that the backbone-head boundary is an insufficient stability criterion challenges a working assumption in recent fine-tuning-based domain generalization methods and motivates more granular parameter management. The method's architecture-agnosticism (validated on CNN, Transformer, and Mamba) and task-agnosticism (SR, classification, segmentation, NLP) make it a broadly applicable training strategy rather than a task-specific trick. However, the gains over DeFT are moderate (0.3–1.5 dB PSNR), the layer partitioning is performed only once or at fixed intervals, and the optimal unstable-layer ratio appears task-dependent (0.4–0.5 for SR vs. 0.7 for segmentation), leaving open questions about adaptive or learned partitioning. The single-branch LDT-S variant, which matches baseline training efficiency while retaining most of the benefit, is a practical contribution that lowers the adoption barrier.
