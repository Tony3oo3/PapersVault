## Problem

Federated fine-tuning of LLMs with LoRA breaks down when clients have heterogeneous computational resources and thus adopt different LoRA ranks. Existing heterogeneous-rank methods (FLoRA, FlexLoRA, HETLoRA) introduce substantial initialization noise from random re-initialization, rank truncation, or SVD approximation, and aggregation noise from stacking, zero-padding, or separate A/B averaging, collectively degrading global model performance and excluding resource-constrained clients.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained LLM backbone, per-client local datasets, and a per-client LoRA rank r_i reflecting each client's computational budget, with a global rank R ≥ max(r_i) set to the optimal homogeneous rank. Each client trains only r_i out of R parallel one-rank modules per target layer, while the remaining R−r_i modules are folded into the frozen backbone weights. The output is a set of R parallel one-rank PLoRA parameter pairs per target layer (rather than a single multi-rank matrix), which can be applied to the pretrained backbone for downstream inference.

## Method

Fed-PLoRA introduces Parallel One-Rank Adaptation (PLoRA), which decomposes a rank-R LoRA module into R parallel one-rank components (each a pair of vectors A_(j) ∈ R^{1×k} and B_(j) ∈ R^{d×1}), mathematically equivalent to standard LoRA but enabling modular per-rank operations. The Select-N-Fold strategy lets each client randomly select r_i of the R one-rank modules to train while folding the unselected R−r_i modules into the frozen target weights, achieving zero initialization noise without truncation or random re-initialization. Server-side aggregation performs rank-wise averaging: for each one-rank position j, only the clients that selected j contribute to the global update, avoiding the structural bias of separate A/B averaging (HETLoRA) and SVD decomposition error (FlexLoRA). The random selection across clients and rounds ensures every global module is updated in expectation, preventing long-term staleness while keeping per-client compute and memory identical to a standard rank-r_i LoRA configuration.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark (BERT-base, R=16, Avg Rank=7), Fed-PLoRA achieves an average score of 79.89, outperforming FLoRA (47.81), FlexLoRA (65.31), and HETLoRA (73.55), while approaching the homogeneous FedIT upper bound (81.08 at Rank=R). On Natural Instructions (Llama-1B, R=16, Avg Rank=8), Fed-PLoRA reaches 64.96 Rouge-L (IID) and 60.76 (non-IID), surpassing all heterogeneous baselines by 5.9–31.1%. On financial datasets (Llama-3.1-8B, R=8, Avg Rank=4), Fed-PLoRA achieves 53.27 average accuracy, exceeding FLoRA by +8.51%, FlexLoRA by +0.89%, and HETLoRA by +2.55%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Fed-PLoRA consistently outperforms all three heterogeneous baselines across every dataset. Against FLoRA, the gains are the most dramatic: +63.46% on CoLA and +42.48% on MRPC (GLUE), and +31.14% on Natural Instructions, because FLoRA's random re-initialization each round causes severe perturbation. Against FlexLoRA, Fed-PLoRA leads by +4.47% on SST-2 and +5.89% on Natural Instructions, as SVD-based truncation still loses information when clients use very small ranks. Against HETLoRA, Fed-PLoRA improves by +9.72% on QQP and +5.42% on RTE, mitigating the structural aggregation bias from zero-padding and separate averaging of A and B matrices. Compared to the homogeneous upper bound FedIT (Rank=R), Fed-PLoRA trails by only 1.19% on average GLUE and 1.92% on Natural Instructions (IID), while matching or exceeding FedIT (Rank=R/2) in most settings. In the homogeneous PLoRA ablation (Figure 3), Fed-PLoRA also outperforms FFA-LoRA by +10.63% at rank 16 on CoLA, confirming the benefit of parallel one-rank decomposition even without heterogeneity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the federated LoRA fine-tuning line initiated by FedIT (homogeneous FedAvg + LoRA) to the heterogeneous-rank setting, where clients have different computational budgets. It departs from prior heterogeneous methods (FLoRA's stacking + random re-initialization, FlexLoRA's SVD-based truncation, HETLoRA's zero-padding) by introducing PLoRA—a decomposition of a single multi-rank LoRA module into parallel one-rank modules—combined with the Select-N-Fold strategy that folds untrained modules into frozen backbone weights. The conceptual lineage also draws on parallel LoRA ideas from Capaboost and MELoRA, but repurposes them specifically to eliminate initialization noise and minimize aggregation noise in the federated setting, supported by a unified theoretical noise analysis that positions all competing methods on a two-dimensional (init noise, agg noise) plane.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Fed-PLoRA offers a clean, lightweight solution to a practical deployment bottleneck: resource-constrained clients in federated LLM fine-tuning were previously forced to either drop out or accept degraded global models due to rank mismatches. The zero-initialization-noise guarantee and the one-line code integration make it immediately adoptable in existing LoRA/FL pipelines. Open questions remain around further reducing the residual aggregation noise (which vanishes only under cross-client covariance assumptions), extending the framework to non-LoRA PEFT methods, and understanding how the random module selection interacts with extreme data heterogeneity over very long training horizons. The paper also leaves open whether the parallel one-rank decomposition could be combined with quantization or sparsity for even more resource-constrained edge devices.
