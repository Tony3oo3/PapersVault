## Problem

Existing DPO-based alignment methods for diffusion models rely exclusively on pairwise preference data, reducing human feedback to binary win/loss comparisons. However, human preferences are inherently ranked, and the paper shows that 56% of Pick-a-Pic pairwise annotations can be grouped into consistent rankings of size greater than 2, conveying richer preference structure. The precise optimization of listwise preferences in diffusion models remains largely unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264523/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image generation, the method takes a text prompt and produces a generated image; for image editing, it takes a source image plus a natural-language edit instruction and produces an edited image; for personalization, it conditions on a user embedding (extracted by a VLM from 4-shot preference pairs) alongside a text prompt. Training data consists of ranked lists of 2–8 images per prompt, constructed by aggregating pairwise annotations into directed acyclic graphs and extracting valid topological paths. The method operates on latent diffusion backbones (SD1.5, SDXL, SD3.5) and requires no external reward model or evaluator during training.

## Method

Diffusion-LPO replaces the Bradley-Terry pairwise model with the Plackett-Luce ranking model, defining the probability of a full ranking as a product of softmax terms over suffix sublists. The resulting objective encourages each image in the ranked list to be preferred over all of its lower-ranked alternatives simultaneously, rather than comparing a single winner against a single loser. The diffusion-specific surrogate uses the denoising-error difference between the fine-tuned and reference models as an implicit reward, weighted by timestep, and the listwise loss is a sum over all positions in the ranking of a log-softmax term. The paper provides a theoretical analysis showing that the naive Group-Pairwise DPO decomposition underestimates the aggregate reward of negative samples, inflating the positive-negative margin, whereas Diffusion-LPO's softmax normalization over the full negative group yields a tighter and more faithful reward signal. When the list size equals 2, the objective exactly reduces to standard Diffusion-DPO, making it a strict generalization.

## Results

On SD1.5, Diffusion-LPO achieves a PickScore win rate of 80.4% on Pick-a-Pic, improving over Diffusion-DPO (68.2%) by 12.2 percentage points. On SDXL, Diffusion-LPO reaches 87.1% HPS win rate on Pick-a-Pic and 72.8% PickScore on Parti-Prompts, outperforming Diffusion-DPO by 6% and 5% respectively. Across all three evaluation sets (Pick-a-Pic, Parti-Prompts, HPSV2) and both backbones, Diffusion-LPO consistently surpasses pairwise Diffusion-DPO, DSPO, and SFT on most metrics, and even exceeds Diffusion-DPO* trained on the larger Pick-a-Pic v2 dataset on several metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Diffusion-DPO (Wallace et al., 2024), Diffusion-LPO wins on PickScore across all three evaluation sets for both SD1.5 (+12.2 pp on Pick-a-Pic, +12.2 pp on HPSV2) and SDXL (+4.1 pp on Pick-a-Pic, +6.0 pp on Parti-Prompts, +2.5 pp on HPSV2), and on HPS for SDXL (+6.0 pp on Pick-a-Pic, +5.2 pp on Parti-Prompts, +5.0 pp on HPSV2). Against DSPO (Zhu et al., 2025), Diffusion-LPO achieves substantially higher PickScore on SDXL (77.1% vs. 59.2% on Pick-a-Pic) and HPS (87.1% vs. 77.4%), while DSPO retains a slight edge on HPS for SD1.5 (82.6% vs. 74.6% on Pick-a-Pic). Against Diffusion-DPO* trained on the larger Pick-a-Pic v2 dataset, Diffusion-LPO (trained on v1) still matches or exceeds it on SDXL PickScore (77.1% vs. 77.1%) and HPS (87.1% vs. 78.4%), demonstrating that listwise structure compensates for the data-size gap. In the personalized preference setting, PPD-LPO improves over PPD (Dang et al., 2025) from 70.3% to 80.2% win rate on held-out users, a 9.9 pp gain. On image editing (InstructPix2Pix), Diffusion-LPO outperforms Diffusion-DPO by 4.3% on DINO, 3.6% on CLIP, and 3.8% on L1 distance. Against FlowGRPO (Liu et al., 2025) on SD3.5, Diffusion-LPO is competitive on HPS (58.8% vs. 77.7% for FlowGRPO with PickScore reward) but does not require any external reward model during training, whereas FlowGRPO's strong PickScore result (96.6%) is directly attributable to using PickScore as its training reward.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Diffusion-DPO lineage (Wallace et al., 2024) and its score-matching variant DSPO (Zhu et al., 2025) by replacing the Bradley-Terry pairwise model with the Plackett-Luce listwise ranking model. The key conceptual departure is recognizing that pairwise annotations in datasets like Pick-a-Pic implicitly encode transitive rankings (56% of pairs belong to consistent chains of length >2), and that a softmax-over-suffix objective naturally captures this structure without requiring external reward evaluators. Unlike concurrent listwise approaches (Chen et al., 2025; Karthik et al., 2024) that decompose rankings into pairwise comparisons weighted by auxiliary scores, Diffusion-LPO directly optimizes the full PL likelihood, and the formulation is shown to be a drop-in generalization applicable to any DPO-based diffusion alignment method.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is that any existing DPO-based diffusion alignment pipeline can be upgraded to listwise with minimal code changes, and the gains are consistent across U-Net (SD1.5, SDXL) and DiT (SD3.5) backbones, text-to-image generation, image editing, and personalized preference alignment. The personalization result—held-out user win rate jumping from 70.3% to 80.2%—suggests listwise supervision improves generalization beyond just in-distribution alignment. Limitations include the 2.2% of annotations discarded due to cyclic preferences, the modest computational overhead at larger list sizes, and the fact that the method remains fully offline; whether listwise structure helps in online RL settings (e.g., FlowGRPO) remains an open question.
