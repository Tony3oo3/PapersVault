## Problem

Classical prophet inequality results assume full knowledge of reward distributions, while prior work on unknown distributions either requires Θ(n) offline reward samples or only achieves the weaker 1/e competitive ratio. This paper tackles the practically motivated but previously unexplored setting where reward distributions are unknown, no offline reward samples are available, and rewards are observed only through noisy realizations, yet the decision-maker has access to contextual feature vectors that reveal the underlying linear structure of the rewards.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260125/cropped_page9_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

At each stage i, the algorithm receives a noisy scalar observation y_i = x_i^T θ + η_i (with σ-sub-Gaussian noise) and a d-dimensional feature vector x_i drawn from distribution D_{x,i}; it also has sampling access to D_x for Monte Carlo computation of the LCB quantile threshold. The output is an irrevocable stopping time τ ∈ [n+1] (τ = n+1 means all observations are rejected, yielding payoff 0) and the realized reward X_τ. The threshold α is computed as the (1−1/n)-quantile of the LCB distribution under D_x, making the feature distribution an essential algorithmic input beyond the sequential observations.

## Method

The core mechanism couples online ridge regression for estimating the unknown parameter θ (from X_i = x_i^T θ) with lower-confidence-bound (LCB) thresholding for the stopping decision. In the Explore-then-Decide variant, a forced exploration phase of length ℓ_n = o(n) collects (y_t, x_t) pairs to form θ̂ = V^{-1}Σ y_t x_t, after which the algorithm stops at the first stage where x_i^T θ̂ − ξ(x_i) ≥ α, with ξ(x_i) = √(x_i^T V^{-1} x_i)·(σ√(d log(n + nℓ_n L/dβ)) + √(Sβ)) capturing both estimation and noise uncertainty. An ε-Greedy variant spreads exploration uniformly across the horizon via Bernoulli(ε) coin flips, updating θ̂ dynamically and using a time-varying threshold. For non-identical distributions, the threshold switches to half the expected maximum (Samuel-Cahn style), and a single window-access step of size ℓ_n+1 at time ℓ_n+1 recovers the optimal 1/2 ratio against the standard prophet benchmark. The key insight is that the linear feature structure enables distributional learning from online observations alone, circumventing the impossibility of nontrivial learning in the purely unknown-distribution model.

## Results

In the i.i.d. setting (n=100,000, d=2, ℓ_n=n^{2/3}), both ETD-LCBT and ε-Greedy-LCBT achieve competitive ratios exceeding 1−1/e ≈ 0.632 against the classical prophet benchmark, consistent with the theoretical guarantees in Corollary 4.3 and Theorem 4.4. In the non-i.i.d. setting (n=30,000), the window-access variant ETD-LCBT-WA achieves a competitive ratio above 1/2 against the standard prophet, matching the optimal bound from Theorem 5.4. The performance gap between the proposed methods and the Gusein-Zade baseline widens as the noise standard deviation σ increases (tested at σ=0.1, 0.5, 0.8), indicating robustness to observation noise.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is the Gusein-Zade rule (1966), which skips the first n/e samples and stops at the first record exceeding the maximum of that prefix, guaranteeing a worst-case competitive ratio of 1/e ≈ 0.368 for unknown i.i.d. distributions. In the i.i.d. experiments, both ETD-LCBT and ε-Greedy-LCBT clearly outperform Gusein-Zade across all noise levels (σ=0.1, 0.5, 0.8), with the proposed methods exceeding the 1−1/e threshold while Gusein-Zade remains near 1/e. In the non-i.i.d. experiments, ETD-LCBT-WA (window access) outperforms both ETD-LCBT (non-iid) and Gusein-Zade, achieving above 1/2 against the standard prophet. Notably, even ETD-LCBT (non-iid), which is only theoretically guaranteed against a relaxed benchmark, empirically exceeds 1/2 against the standard prophet. The advantage of the proposed methods over Gusein-Zade grows with increasing σ², demonstrating that the LCB-based learning approach is more robust to noisy observations than the distribution-free secretary-style rule.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends prophet inequalities to a setting combining three previously separate challenges: noisy observations (Assaf et al., 1998), unknown reward distributions (Correa et al., 2019a; Rubinstein et al., 2019), and a linear reward model with observable features. The key departure from the unknown-distribution literature is that the linear structure X_i = x_i^T θ with feature access provides enough signal to learn the distribution online, circumventing the 1/e impossibility of Correa et al. (2019a) that applies without offline samples. The window-access mechanism builds on Marshall et al. (2020) and Benomar et al. (2024), while the LCB-based stopping rule draws on the online linear bandit literature (Abbasi-Yadkori et al., 2011).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight—that a linear feature structure can substitute for Θ(n) offline reward samples to recover sharp competitive ratios—is a meaningful conceptual advance for prophet inequalities in practical settings like ad allocation and hiring where features are observable but reward distributions are not. Limitations include the requirement of a linear model, the non-degeneracy condition on OPT, and synthetic-only experiments with a single baseline (Gusein-Zade). Open questions include whether analogous results hold for non-linear reward models, correlated features, or when the noise is heteroscedastic, and whether the single-window-access trick generalizes to multi-choice or multi-armed variants.
