## Problem

Existing deep learning methods for electronic-structure Hamiltonian prediction struggle to generalize across the full diversity of real-world materials due to limited element coverage, neglected spin-orbit coupling effects, reduced orbital basis sets, and the absence of broad-coverage benchmark datasets. Additionally, regressing only the real-space Hamiltonian leads to error amplification through the ill-conditioned overlap matrix, producing unphysical 'ghost states' in predicted band structures. This paper tackles both the generalization gap and the physical-fidelity gap simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model consumes atomic configurations (element types and 3D positions), interatomic displacement vectors within a cutoff radius, and the zeroth-step Hamiltonian H^(0) constructed from the initial charge density (sum of neutral atomic charge densities, requiring no diagonalization). H^(0) is decomposed into on-site sub-matrices (node features) and off-site sub-matrices (edge features) via an inverse Wigner-Eckart transformation. The network outputs a correction term ΔH = H^(T) − H^(0) in spin-resolved blocks (↑↑, ↑↓, ↓↑, ↓↓) with separate real and imaginary components; the full self-consistent Hamiltonian is recovered as H^(0) + ΔH, and band structures are obtained by Fourier transforming to k-space.

## Method

NextHAM combines three key innovations: (1) a delta-learning formulation where the network predicts only the correction ΔH rather than the full Hamiltonian, with H^(0) serving as both a physics-informed input descriptor and an output baseline, reducing the regression target's dimensionality and numerical range by ~96%; (2) an E(3)-equivariant Transformer architecture extending Equiformer with persistent edge features, distance-embedding-augmented attention, and the TraceGrad mechanism that induces non-linear O(3)-equivariant representations via gradients of O(3)-invariant features supervised by trace quantities, all wrapped in an ensemble of sub-models specialized to different interatomic distance intervals; (3) a joint real-space and reciprocal-space training objective where the k-space loss partitions the spectrum into low-energy P and high-energy Q subspaces with an explicit PQ coupling penalty that suppresses ghost states, while gauge ambiguity is resolved by analytically determining the optimal shift parameter μ in the overlap-matrix gauge.

## Results

On the Materials-HAM-SOC test set (3,000 structures, 60+ elements, SOC included), NextHAM achieves an overall Gauge MAE of 1.417 meV across full Hamiltonian matrices in real space, with spin-off-diagonal (SOC) blocks suppressed to sub-μeV (<0.001 meV). The zeroth-step Hamiltonian H^(0) alone reduces the Gauge MAE for the real part of the spin-conserving blocks by 96% relative to predicting from zero, and the learned correction ΔH further halves the residual error. Per-element analysis shows most elements in the periodic table achieve prediction errors below 1.5 meV, and an out-of-distribution test on the unseen element Neon yields only 0.1 meV R-space MAE.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares NextHAM against two prior Hamiltonian-prediction methods: DeepH-E3 (Gong et al., 2023, Nature Communications) and the original TraceGrad (Yin et al., 2025, ICML). The authors report 'significant superiority' of NextHAM over both baselines, with detailed quantitative comparisons deferred to Appendix M (not included in the main text). NextHAM also claims state-of-the-art performance on the specialized DeepH Series databases (Li et al., 2022; Gong et al., 2023), which cover single-material systems such as MoS₂ and Bi₂Se₃. The key differentiators over DeepH-E3 are the use of the zeroth-step Hamiltonian as a physics-informed input (vs. randomly initialized embeddings), the extension of TraceGrad into a Transformer backbone with persistent edge features, and the joint R-space/k-space loss that eliminates ghost states. Compared to the original TraceGrad, NextHAM adds the Transformer architecture, ensemble sub-models for different distance intervals, the k-space loss with PQ-coupling penalty, and the gauge-invariant loss formulation, all of which contribute to the improved accuracy and generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TraceGrad mechanism (Yin et al., ICML 2025) from a simple GNN backbone into a Transformer framework, and develops its E(3)-equivariant attention from Equiformer (Liao & Smidt, ICLR 2023) by explicitly maintaining edge features across layers and incorporating distance embeddings. The key conceptual departure from prior Hamiltonian prediction work (DeepH-E3, DeepH-2, QHNet) is the introduction of the zeroth-step Hamiltonian H^(0) as a physics-informed input descriptor combined with a delta-learning target, which replaces randomly initialized element embeddings and reduces the regression space by ~96%. The joint R-space/k-space loss with explicit gauge resolution and PQ-coupling penalty addresses a gap left by prior methods that either target only real-space Hamiltonians or handle gauge freedom implicitly.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first broad-coverage benchmark (17K structures, 60+ elements, SOC, f-orbitals) and a corresponding model that achieves DFT-level accuracy (1.417 meV overall, sub-μeV for SOC blocks) with dramatically improved efficiency, opening the door to rapid screening of large-scale materials systems. The finding that a 0.53 meV R-space MAE is sufficient to produce ghost states, and that a k-space loss eliminates them without materially changing R-space accuracy, highlights a previously underappreciated sensitivity of the generalized eigenvalue problem that should inform future Hamiltonian prediction designs. Open questions include whether the single-element OOD generalization (Ne) generalizes to more unseen elements, whether the ensemble strategy scales to even larger systems, and how interpretability of the learned corrections can be improved for physical insight.
