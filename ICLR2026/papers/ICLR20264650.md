## Problem

Equivariant diffusion models guarantee symmetric target distributions but force the network to learn specific rigid-body movements (e.g., exact SE(3) transformations) that carry no physical information, increasing learning difficulty. Alignment-based heuristics (GeoDiff, AlphaFold 3) reduce this difficulty by removing variance in equivalent degrees of freedom, but their modified learning targets are incompatible with standard diffusion samplers, distorting the generated distribution. No prior method simultaneously reduces learning difficulty and guarantees correct sampling.

## Input / Output

For molecular structure generation, the model takes a molecular graph, a noisy 3D coordinate vector x_t ∈ R^{3N}, and a time step t, and outputs a denoised 3D structure. For protein structure generation, the task is unconditional (no amino acid sequence), operating on backbone residue coordinates. Unusually, the model output is permitted to be arbitrary in the vertical (intra-equivalence-class) subspace—i.e., it need not predict the correct rigid-body translation or rotation—because the horizontal projection P_x strips this component from both the training loss and every sampling step, so only the deformation (shape) component is optimized and propagated.

## Method

The framework projects a conventional equivariant diffusion process onto the quotient space M/G (the shape space) via Theorem 1, then uses the horizontal lift (Theorem 2) to pull the process back into the total space M for practical simulation. The key operator is the horizontal projection P_x, which for R^{3N}/SE(3) removes total linear and angular momentum from any update vector, leaving only deformation without rigid-body movement. An additional correction term h̃ (the horizontal lift of the mean curvature vector of the quotient) compensates for the changing volume of equivalence classes, guaranteeing the correct target distribution (Corollary 3). Training modifies the standard denoising loss to ||P_x(D_θ(x_t,t) − x_1)||², freeing the model from learning anything in the vertical subspace. Sampling uses either an ODE (dx_t/dt = P_x(v_θ)) or an SDE that adds the h̃ drift and projects the Wiener process, both producing the same G-invariant target as the original equivariant process but with shorter, straighter trajectories.

## Results

On GEOM-QM9 (molecular conformer generation), the quotient-space diffusion variant of ET-Flow(SO(3)) achieves 96.40% Recall Coverage and 0.069 Å mean AMR, outperforming the vanilla ET-Flow (95.98%, 0.076 Å) and surpassing the strong MCF baseline (95.0%, 0.103 Å). On GEOM-DRUGS, it reaches 78.50% Recall Coverage and 0.477 Å AMR with a 9.1M-parameter model, matching or exceeding the much larger MCF-L (242M) on precision metrics. For protein structure generation, the 60M-parameter Proteina model with quotient-space diffusion achieves 97.6% designability and 274.7 FPSD (SDE, γ=0.35), outperforming the vanilla Proteina (96.0%, 386.5) and even surpassing the 200M-parameter Proteina on most ODE-sampling metrics (15.6% designability, 69.9 FPSD vs. 13.8%, 83.2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The quotient-space diffusion framework consistently outperforms both the vanilla equivariant baseline (ET-Flow) and the two alignment-based treatments (GeoDiff and AF3) across all datasets and metrics. On GEOM-QM9, it improves Recall AMR from 0.076 Å (vanilla) to 0.069 Å, while GeoDiff alignment slightly degrades it (0.085 Å) and AF3 alignment substantially degrades it (0.131 Å). On GEOM-DRUGS, the advantage is more pronounced: GeoDiff alignment drops Recall Coverage from 78.18% to 75.11% and AF3 alignment to 71.66%, whereas quotient-space diffusion maintains 78.50%. For protein generation, AF3 alignment catastrophically degrades ODE-sampling designability from 13.8% to 3.8%, while quotient-space diffusion improves it to 15.6% and reduces FPSD from 83.2 to 69.9. The 60M-parameter model with quotient-space diffusion surpasses the 200M-parameter Proteina on ODE-sampling FPSD (69.9 vs. 85.4) and fJSD (0.11 vs. 0.09), demonstrating that the framework's learning-efficiency gains can compensate for a 3× parameter reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Stochastic Interpolant framework (Albergo et al., 2023) for diffusion/flow-matching formulation and the equivariant diffusion process (Hoogeboom et al., 2022) as the base generative process. It departs fundamentally from alignment-based symmetry treatments (GeoDiff, AlphaFold 3) by showing they modify the learning target in a way incompatible with the standard sampler, thereby distorting the generated distribution. The key conceptual departure is the use of Riemannian geometric constructions—specifically the quotient space M/G and horizontal lift—to project the diffusion process onto the intrinsic shape space, yielding a principled framework that simultaneously reduces learning difficulty and guarantees correct sampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work provides the first principled framework that both leverages symmetry to reduce learning difficulty and provably preserves the target distribution, resolving a tension that alignment-based heuristics (widely adopted in GeoDiff, AF3, and Boltz-1) fail to address. The practical impact is significant: a 60M-parameter model with quotient-space diffusion outperforms a 200M-parameter model on protein structure generation, suggesting substantial compute savings for future biomolecular generative models. Open questions include extending the framework to non-compact or discrete symmetry groups, other scientific domains beyond SE(3) (e.g., periodic boundary conditions in crystals), and whether the curvature correction term h can be further simplified or absorbed into the architecture.
