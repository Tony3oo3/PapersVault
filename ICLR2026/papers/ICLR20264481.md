## Problem

Existing feature caching methods for video diffusion models accelerate inference by reusing cached features across denoising steps, but they suffer from notable quality degradation because they cannot distinguish truly redundant computations from important ones. Prior adaptive caching approaches rely on suboptimal decision criteria, complicated additional fitting, or extensive hyperparameter tuning, making it difficult to achieve maximal speedup without sacrificing video fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264481/cropped_page0_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

PreciseCache is a plug-and-play, training-free acceleration framework applied to existing DiT-based video diffusion models (Wan2.1-14B, HunyuanVideo, CogVideoX, Open-Sora 1.2). At each denoising step, the noisy latent is downsampled (e.g., 2×4×4 in temporal/spatial dimensions) and fed into the model for a lightweight trial inference to compute the caching decision, while the full-resolution latent is only processed when a full inference is triggered. The output is a generated video at the target resolution and duration (480P–1080P, 48–192 frames), with the framework achieving up to 2.6× speedup on Wan2.1-14B without perceptible quality loss.

## Method

PreciseCache comprises two complementary caching mechanisms. LFCache performs step-wise caching by computing the Low-Frequency Difference (LFD) between the current step's prediction and the previous cached step's prediction, where low-frequency components are extracted via FFT with a centered circular mask. The key insight is that LFD closely tracks the actual impact of reusing cached features on final video quality, since high-noise steps encode critical structural information while low-noise steps produce perceptually insignificant high-frequency details. To avoid the cost of a full forward pass for LFD estimation, a downsampled latent is used for a quick trial inference, and the accumulated LFD is compared against a threshold (set as a relative factor α of the maximum observed LFD in the first 5 steps) to decide whether to skip or perform full inference. BlockCache further accelerates non-skipped steps by identifying 'pivotal' transformer blocks (those whose input-output difference is in the top c%) versus 'non-pivotal' blocks, caching the output differences of non-pivotal blocks and reusing them in subsequent steps to skip their computation entirely.

## Results

On Wan2.1-14B (720P, 81 frames), PreciseCache-Flash achieves a 2.63× speedup (344.9s vs. 907.3s baseline) with 83.43% VBench score and 0.0812 LPIPS, compared to 83.62% VBench for the unaccelerated baseline. Across all four base models (Open-Sora 1.2, HunyuanVideo, CogVideoX, Wan2.1-14B), PreciseCache-Flash delivers 2.44×–2.63× speedup while maintaining VBench scores within 0.2–0.6% of the baseline. The lighter PreciseCache-Turbo configuration achieves 1.95×–2.15× speedup with even smaller quality gaps (e.g., 83.52% VBench vs. 83.62% baseline on Wan2.1-14B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PreciseCache-Flash outperforms all three baselines on speedup across every base model: 2.63× vs. PAB (1.38×), TeaCache (1.94×), and FasterCache (1.73×) on Wan2.1-14B. On quality, PreciseCache-Flash achieves 83.43% VBench on Wan2.1-14B, beating PAB (82.91%) and TeaCache (83.24%) but slightly trailing FasterCache (83.47%) by 0.04%. However, at the Turbo setting, PreciseCache (83.52% VBench, 2.15× speedup) simultaneously surpasses FasterCache (83.47%, 1.73×) on both quality and speed. PAB consistently shows the largest quality degradation (e.g., 0.1853 LPIPS on Wan2.1-14B vs. 0.0812 for PreciseCache-Flash), confirming that its fixed caching interval causes significant content distortion. Overall, PreciseCache achieves the best speedup–quality trade-off, with the Base configuration (1.59×) nearly matching the unaccelerated baseline in VBench (83.56% vs. 83.62%) while the Flash configuration delivers the highest acceleration.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature caching paradigm for video diffusion model acceleration, building directly on TeaCache's accumulated-difference threshold strategy for adaptive step-wise caching. Its key conceptual departure is decomposing model predictions into low- and high-frequency components via FFT and using only the Low-Frequency Difference (LFD) as the caching criterion, motivated by the empirical finding that low-frequency structural information dominates perceptual quality while high-frequency details are safely cacheable. The BlockCache component further extends the caching granularity from the whole-network level to individual transformer blocks, identifying 'pivotal' vs. 'non-pivotal' blocks by input-output difference. Unlike prior adaptive methods (TeaCache, FasterCache, PAB) that rely on raw prediction differences or require extensive hyperparameter tuning, PreciseCache frames the caching decision around a physically interpretable frequency-domain signal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2.6× speedup with negligible quality loss across four major video diffusion backbones (Wan2.1-14B, HunyuanVideo, CogVideoX, Open-Sora) makes PreciseCache a practically deployable plug-and-play accelerator for real-world video generation pipelines. The LFD insight—that low-frequency structural changes, not total prediction change, govern caching safety—offers a cleaner conceptual framework than prior difference-based criteria and may inform future caching designs. An open question is whether the LFD principle generalizes beyond DiT-based rectified-flow models to other generative architectures or to image diffusion. The finding that feature reusing strategy (prediction vs. residual vs. TaylorSeer) has minimal impact under PreciseCache suggests the field should prioritize *when* to cache over *how* to cache, though this observation is based on a single model and resolution.
