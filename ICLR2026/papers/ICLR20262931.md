## Problem

Existing Source-Free Domain Adaptive Object Detection (SF-DAOD) methods rely almost exclusively on refining pseudo-label selection or the teacher-student framework, treating the source-trained detector as a mere label oracle while ignoring the object-level structural regularities already embedded in its internal representations. No prior work has exploited Object-Centric Learning (OCL) to explicitly disentangle individual objects from background in the target domain without access to source data, leaving a gap in how the pretrained model's rich structural knowledge can be leveraged during privacy-constrained adaptation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an unlabeled target-domain RGB image and the weights of a source-pretrained RT-DETR detector (no source images are available at adaptation time). The HSA module is additionally pretrained on COCO for 100 epochs before joint training. Output is standard detection results—bounding boxes with class labels—evaluated via mAP@50. During inference, HSA remains active to provide structural priors while CGSC is disabled, so the parameter overhead is marginal.

## Method

CGSA integrates two complementary modules into a DETR-based detector. The Hierarchical Slot Awareness (HSA) module performs a two-level coarse-to-fine decomposition of backbone features into 5×5=25 slots via iterative Slot Attention with a reconstruction objective, then fuses the projected fine slots additively into the DETR object queries to inject object-level structural priors into the decoder. The Class-Guided Slot Contrast (CGSC) module maintains EMA-updated class prototypes from decoder queries, assigns pseudo-labels to weighted slots via Hungarian matching, and applies an InfoNCE contrastive loss between slot prototypes and class prototypes to steer slots toward domain-invariant, class-relevant semantics. Training proceeds in two stages: source pretraining with detection loss plus reconstruction loss, followed by target-domain adaptation under a teacher-student paradigm where the student minimizes a combined objective of pseudo-label detection loss (Focal + L1/GIoU), reconstruction loss, and contrastive loss, with a cosine-adaptive confidence threshold for pseudo-label selection.

## Results

On the Cityscapes → BDD100K benchmark, CGSA achieves 53.0 mAP@50, outperforming the best source-free method TITAN (38.3) by 14.7 points and the best traditional DAOD method DATR (43.3) by 9.7 points. On Cityscapes → Foggy-Cityscapes, CGSA reaches 53.2 mAP, surpassing TITAN (52.2) and all other SF-DAOD methods. On the single-class car detection tasks, CGSA achieves 67.7 mAP on Sim10K → Cityscapes and 60.8 mAP on KITTI → Cityscapes, both leading all compared methods by 7–8 points over TITAN.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CGSA outperforms all 15 compared baselines across all four evaluation settings. The most dramatic gain is on Cityscapes → BDD100K, where CGSA (53.0 mAP) exceeds the previous best source-free method TITAN (38.3) by 14.7 points and the strongest traditional DAOD method DATR (43.3) by 9.7 points. On Cityscapes → Foggy-Cityscapes, CGSA (53.2) edges out TITAN (52.2) by 1.0 point and surpasses the next-best SF method SF-UT (45.0) by 8.2 points. On the single-class car tasks, CGSA leads TITAN by 7.9 mAP on Sim10K → Cityscapes (67.7 vs. 59.8) and by 7.6 mAP on KITTI → Cityscapes (60.8 vs. 53.2). Among traditional DAOD methods that require source data, CGSA also surpasses DATR, MRT, AQT, and DA-Faster on every benchmark, indicating that the slot-aware structural priors compensate for the absence of source images.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three established lines: Slot Attention / Object-Centric Learning (Locatello et al., 2020), DETR-based detection (Carion et al., 2020), and the teacher-student self-training paradigm for source-free adaptation (Tarvainen & Valpola, 2017). Prior SF-DAOD methods (SFOD, PETS, A²SFOD, TITAN) focus almost exclusively on refining pseudo-label selection—confidence thresholds, periodic smoothing, variance-based partitioning—while treating the pretrained detector as a black-box label oracle. CGSA departs from this by arguing that the detector's internal representations already encode object-level structural regularities that persist across domains, and it extracts these via a hierarchical Slot Attention module fused into DETR's query space, guided by a class-prototype contrastive loss. The hierarchical coarse-to-fine slot design is a specific departure from vanilla Slot Attention, motivated by the known collapse of flat slot attention at larger slot counts on real-world data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that object-centric structural priors can be extracted and leveraged without any source data, this work opens a new axis of improvement for SF-DAOD beyond pseudo-label engineering, with a ~15 mAP gap over the prior SOTA on Cityscapes→BDD100K that is consistent across four benchmarks. The theoretical risk-descent bound (Eq. 12) provides a principled justification for why slot variance contraction plus contrastive margin expansion should reduce target risk, which is rare in this applied subfield. Key limitations constrain broader impact: the method is tied to DETR-style query-based detectors (portability to two-stage or anchor-free architectures is unestablished), and it addresses only object detection rather than segmentation or classification. Open questions include whether the slot-aware query fusion generalizes to other transformer-based tasks, whether the hierarchical depth can be increased with stronger supervision signals, and whether the approach extends to open-vocabulary or long-tail detection settings.
