## Problem

Latent reasoning replaces verbose chain-of-thought traces with continuous internal tokens, improving inference efficiency, but suffers from a critical lack of supervision for those internal thoughts, causing performance to degrade on natural-language reasoning traces. A natural supervisory signal is the teacher's KV-cache, but redundancy-aware compression (per-layer, per-head eviction) destroys the direct token-to-cache correspondence that standard activation-matching distillation relies on, making it unclear whether a compressed cache can still guide a latent student.

## Input / Output

The model receives a math word problem as a text prompt in a standard chat template and produces a numeric answer. During inference, instead of generating a full CoT trace, the model produces 24 continuous latent vectors (not discrete tokens) via Jacobi iteration in only 3 forward passes, then decodes the answer from the question plus these latent representations. A trainable projection layer maps the continuous latent embeddings back into the input embedding space for autoregressive answer generation.

## Method

KAVA uses a self-distillation framework where the same backbone acts as teacher (consuming the full CoT trace) and student (generating latent tokens). The teacher's per-layer, per-head KV-cache is compressed to the latent budget M using an adapted R-KV eviction score that combines attention-based importance (answer-token attention to CoT keys) and cosine-similarity-based redundancy, selecting top-M KV-pairs independently per head and layer. The student's latent K and V tensors are then matched directly to the compressed teacher cache via an L1 or MSE loss across all layers and heads, bypassing the need for token-level alignment. This KV-matching loss is combined with CODI's endpoint hidden-state distillation and standard cross-entropy on the answer, and the entire latent sequence is generated in parallel via Jacobi iteration (T=3 iterations for M=24 tokens) to keep training and inference efficient.

## Results

On GSM8k (in-distribution), KAVA achieves 56.5% accuracy with LLaMA3.2-1B-Instruct, outperforming CODI (53.9%) and PCCoT (54.2%) while remaining close to the Full CoT upper bound (63.4%). The advantage is most pronounced on natural-language traces (GSM8k-AUG-NL), where KAVA reaches 55.7% versus CODI's 50.1% and PCCoT's 51.1%, showing markedly smaller degradation from equation-only to NL traces. In terms of efficiency, KAVA requires only 6–10 forward passes per question (62–92% fewer than Full CoT), matching PCCoT's inference cost. Scaling to LLaMA3.2-3B-Instruct, KAVA reaches 65.7% on GSM8k-AUG and 60.0% on GSM8k-AUG-NL, again leading all latent baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KAVA consistently outperforms all four latent-reasoning baselines (CODI, PCCoT, Coconut, ICoT) across every model size and dataset. Against CODI (the strongest baseline), KAVA gains +2.6 points on GSM8k-AUG and +5.6 points on GSM8k-AUG-NL with LLaMA3.2-1B, and +4.7 / +4.1 points respectively with LLaMA3.2-3B. The gap widens dramatically on natural-language traces: CODI drops from 53.9% to 50.1% (−3.8) moving from equation-only to NL traces on LLaMA-1B, while KAVA drops only from 56.5% to 55.7% (−0.8), demonstrating that KV-cache distillation provides a more robust supervision signal for longer, verbose reasoning. Against PCCoT, KAVA leads by +2.3 points on GSM8k-AUG-NL (LLaMA-1B) and by +12.4 points on the same dataset with LLaMA-3B (60.0 vs 47.6), where PCCoT's parallel Jacobi updates without KV supervision degrade substantially. Coconut and ICoT, which lack self-distillation entirely, trail KAVA by 11+ and 37+ points respectively on LLaMA-1B GSM8k-AUG, confirming that the KV-matching loss is the critical differentiator. In efficiency terms, KAVA matches PCCoT's forward-pass count (both use T=3 Jacobi iterations) while achieving higher accuracy, and requires 62–92% fewer forward passes than Full CoT.

## Relation to Prior Work

KAVA sits at the intersection of two active research threads: latent/continuous reasoning (Coconut, CODI, PCCoT, iCoT) and KV-cache compression (R-KV, KeyDiff, PyramidKV). It inherits the self-distillation framework from CODI and the parallel Jacobi-iteration latent generation from PCCoT, but departs from both by replacing (or augmenting) their token-level or single-token hidden-state distillation with a full per-layer, per-head KV-cache matching loss. The key conceptual departure is that the teacher's KV-cache is first compressed via R-KV's redundancy-importance eviction, which destroys direct token-to-token correspondence, yet the resulting abstract cache is used as a stepwise supervision target for the student's continuous latent trajectory — something prior distillation-based latent methods (which align activations at specific token positions) cannot do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core bottleneck in latent reasoning: the absence of a direct supervision signal for internal thoughts, which has limited prior methods to restricted or template-like traces. By showing that a compressed KV-cache — despite losing token correspondence — can serve as a rich, stepwise supervisory signal, KAVA opens a path toward scaling latent reasoning to longer, natural-language traces where prior methods degrade sharply. Open questions include whether the KV-matching signal captures genuine reasoning dynamics or primarily memorizes dataset-specific patterns, whether the approach generalizes beyond arithmetic word problems to multi-step symbolic or open-ended reasoning, and how interpretability of the latent trace evolves as the compression ratio increases. The paper also notes that latent traces trained on natural-language data resist straightforward decoding, leaving the internal computation opaque.
