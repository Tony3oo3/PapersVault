## Problem

Transformers rely on residual (skip) connections for stable training, yet these shortcuts disrupt the hierarchical abstraction that deep architectures are designed to learn, causing networks to behave as if much shallower than their nominal depth. While one prior work modified the self-attention block to enable skipless training, it required architectural changes incompatible with FlashAttention and still converged far more slowly than residual baselines. No method existed to train standard, unmodified skipless transformers at competitive speed.

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images patchified into token sequences (e.g., 16×16 patches for ViT-Base on ImageNet-1k, or 16×16 patches for ViT-Small in DINO pretraining). In supervised mode it outputs class labels; in self-supervised mode it outputs per-token feature representations from individual or aggregated transformer blocks, which are then linearly probed for dense segmentation masks (mIoU on VOC2012, ADE20K, COCO-Stuff) or fed into TokenCuT for salient object masks. The architecture is a standard transformer with all skip connections removed from both self-attention and FFN sub-blocks, requiring no structural modification.

## Method

The paper derives a principled initialization from a Jacobian analysis showing that skip connections stabilize training by adding identity matrices that regularize the smallest singular values of the self-attention Jacobian K_ℓ. Without skips, K_ℓ is ill-conditioned because the attention matrix A_ℓ is near-uniform (rank-1) under random initialization. The proposed fix has two components: (1) W^V W^O is initialized as a scaled orthonormal matrix via SVD of a random Gaussian matrix, ensuring the dominant term in K_ℓ has condition number ≈ 1; and (2) W^Q W^{K^T} = αZ + βI (mimetic initialization) is used so that attention logits are diagonally dominant, making A_ℓ near-identity and keeping the perturbation term in K_ℓ small. This requires zero architectural changes and remains fully compatible with FlashAttention. Combined with the SOAP second-order optimizer, skipless ViT-Base matches or exceeds residual ViT-Base accuracy on ImageNet-1k (80.8% vs 80.3%), and skipless DINO ViT-Small substantially outperforms residual DINO on dense prediction and object discovery tasks.

## Results

On ImageNet-1k supervised classification (ViT-Base), skipless+init with SOAP achieves 80.8% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the standard residual ViT-Base (80.3% AdamW, 80.1% SOAP). In self-supervised DINO pretraining (ViT-Small), skipless models substantially outperform residual baselines on object discovery: 63.2 vs 49.4 mAP on VOC2012 and 46.7 vs 27.5 on COCO20K (SOAP, 300 epochs). On dense linear probing segmentation, skipless models match or exceed skip models on VOC2012 (62.3 vs 56.3 mIoU, single-layer AdamW) and COCO-Stuff (24.9 vs 24.6), while multiscale aggregation closes the gap on ADE20K. A 110M-parameter language model trained on C4 with the proposed initialization achieves 48.2 average zero-shot accuracy across five commonsense tasks, matching the residual baseline (47.7) at the same training budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the standard residual ViT-Base (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020), the proposed skipless+init method with SOAP achieves 80.8% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k, exceeding the residual baseline by 0.5% (vs. 80.3% AdamW) and 0.7% (vs. 80.1% SOAP), while the skipless model without the proposed initialization collapses to 61.4% under AdamW. Against the DINO self-supervised baseline (Caron et al., 2021), skipless ViT-Small models show dramatic gains on object discovery via TokenCuT: 63.2 vs 49.4 on VOC2012 and 46.7 vs 27.5 on COCO20K (SOAP, 300 epochs), a 13.8 and 19.2 point improvement respectively. On dense linear probing segmentation, skipless models outperform the DINO skip baseline on VOC2012 (62.3 vs 56.3 mIoU, single-layer AdamW) and COCO-Stuff (24.9 vs 24.6), though they trail slightly on ADE20K in single-layer evaluation (22.5 vs 23.7) before recovering with multiscale aggregation (27.0 vs 26.0). A 9-layer skipless ViT-Small surpasses the 12-layer skip baseline on object discovery (34.8 vs 32.3 on VOC2012), highlighting the parameter efficiency of the skipless design.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on training deep networks without skip connections, most directly building on He et al. (2023), who modified the self-attention architecture to prevent rank collapse in skipless language transformers. The key departure is methodological: rather than altering the transformer block, this paper retains the standard architecture and achieves stable training purely through a principled initialization strategy, making it compatible with FlashAttention. The initialization scheme draws on mimetic initialization (Trockman & Kolter, 2023) for the query-key product and scaled-orthogonal initialization (Martens et al., 2021) for MLP weights, but the paper's core contribution is the Jacobian conditioning analysis that provides theoretical motivation for why these choices work in the skipless setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By demonstrating that skip connections are not a fundamental requirement for training ViTs, this work opens the door to studying truly hierarchical representation learning in transformers, where each layer must build on the previous without shallow shortcuts. The substantial improvements on dense prediction tasks (e.g., ~60% relative gain in object discovery) suggest that the representational benefits of depth are masked by residual connections in standard models. Key limitations include evaluation only at ~100M parameter scale, reliance on a block-incoherence assumption that is not explicitly verified, and the need for a second-order optimizer (SOAP) to fully match residual training speed. Scaling to billion-parameter models and understanding whether hierarchical benefits persist at scale remain important open questions.
