## Problem

KV retrieval methods for LLM inference maintain near-lossless accuracy by dynamically selecting a subset of the full KV cache, but suffer severe efficiency bottlenecks: selection and recall operations account for 53–94% of total decoding latency in SOTA methods like ArkVale, ShadowKV, and InfiniGen. Existing approaches either cannot fully hide recall latency behind computation (InfiniGen's token-wise recall) or incur additional overhead from re-projection and SVD reconstruction, making them impractical for long-context and long-generation workloads.

## Input / Output

FreeKV takes a text prompt and the full KV cache (offloaded to CPU memory in HND layout) as input, and produces standard autoregressive token generation as output. Internally, at each decoding step it selects a fixed budget B=2048 KV pages (page size 32) per KV head via min-max pooled page summaries, always retaining S sink tokens and W local-window tokens. The selection is group-consistent across GQA heads, and the correction threshold τ (0.8 for long-input, 0.9 for long-generation) controls how often the speculative reuse is overridden by a fresh selection-and-recall pass.

## Method

FreeKV is a training-free algorithm-system co-optimization framework. On the algorithm side, it exploits the empirically observed high cosine similarity (>0.84) between query vectors of adjacent decoding steps to perform speculative retrieval: the current step directly reuses KV pages recalled in the previous step, shifting both selection and recall out of the critical path so they overlap with attention, FFN, and QKV projection computations. A fine-grained correction mechanism triggers a fresh selection-and-recall only when the group-mean cosine similarity drops below threshold τ, using head-wise recall to minimize overhead. On the system side, FreeKV employs hybrid KV layouts—NHD on GPU (natural for FlashAttention) and HND on CPU (contiguous per-head pages)—so that the NHD-to-HND transpose is amortized at offload time rather than repeated per recall, and a double-buffering mechanism streams CPU-to-GPU transfers concurrently with GPU-side layout conversion, reducing recall latency to the same order as computation and enabling full latency hiding.

## Results

On LongBench v2, FreeKV achieves 29.22 overall accuracy with Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, matching the full KV cache baseline exactly, while KV dropping methods (RazorAttention: 27.44, RaaS: 28.23) and other retrieval methods (ArkVale: 28.63, ShadowKV: 25.45) all fall short. On LongGenBench, FreeKV attains 78.03 CR and 27.62 CR×Acc, comparable to full cache (80.03 / 26.82) and superior to all baselines. On reasoning tasks (MATH500, AIME24, GPQA), FreeKV matches or exceeds full KV cache accuracy (e.g., 78.00 pass@k on MATH500 vs. 78.00 full; 86.00 pass@k on GPQA vs. 82.00 full). In efficiency, FreeKV delivers up to 13.7× end-to-end speedup over ArkVale and 8.4× over ShadowKV, while matching the latency of dropping methods that avoid offloading entirely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against KV dropping baselines, FreeKV clearly wins: RazorAttention (static) suffers severe degradation on long generation (35.90 CR vs. FreeKV's 78.03) and reasoning (46.67 pass@k on AIME24 vs. FreeKV's 76.67), while RaaS (dynamic) is more competitive on simpler tasks but still lags on AIME24 (66.67 vs. 76.67 pass@k). Among KV retrieval baselines, FreeKV matches or exceeds Quest and ArkVale on accuracy while being dramatically faster (13.7× over ArkVale in end-to-end latency). ShadowKV achieves competitive accuracy on LongBench v2 (25.45 vs. FreeKV's 29.22 for Llama-3.1-8B) but degrades significantly on Qwen models due to SVD reconstruction errors, and cannot support long generation natively. InfiniGen provides partial recall-computation overlap but still leaves ~53% of recall latency unhidden, resulting in 3.2–8.5× higher latency than FreeKV. Overall, FreeKV is the only method that simultaneously achieves near-lossless accuracy across all task types (long-input, long-generation, long-reasoning) and matches the efficiency of dropping methods that avoid offloading.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeKV builds directly on the KV retrieval line (Quest, ArkVale, ShadowKV, InfiniGen), which retains the full KV cache but selects a subset per step. Its key departure is the speculative framing: rather than performing selection and recall synchronously at each decoding step, FreeKV exploits the high cosine similarity of query vectors between adjacent steps to reuse the previous step's recalled KV pages, shifting selection and recall out of the critical path. On the system side, it departs from prior offloading methods by introducing hybrid NHD/HND layouts across GPU and CPU memory plus double-buffered streamed recall, eliminating the fragmented transfers and re-projection overhead that dominate latency in ArkVale, ShadowKV, and InfiniGen. The fine-grained correction mechanism (triggered by a query-similarity threshold) is a novel safeguard that prior speculative or reuse-based approaches lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 13× end-to-end speedup over SOTA retrieval methods while maintaining near-lossless accuracy makes KV retrieval practically deployable for long-context and long-generation workloads, a regime where prior retrieval methods were too slow to be useful. The ablation showing that hybrid layouts alone account for ~10.5× of the speedup reveals that prior offloading systems left enormous efficiency on the table due to fragmented CPU-GPU transfers, a finding likely to influence future KV cache system designs. Open questions include whether the high query-similarity assumption (>0.84) holds for future architectures with different positional encodings or non-GQA attention, whether the approach extends to multi-modal or non-autoregressive settings, and how it interacts with emerging learnable sparse-attention techniques trained natively during pre-training.
