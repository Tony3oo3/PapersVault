## Problem

SVD-based model merging methods (e.g., CART, TSV-M, STAR) rely on a heuristic top-k rank truncation of task vectors, which the paper shows is suboptimal for two reasons: (1) top singular components with large singular values often introduce significant inter-task interference that outweighs their benefit to the source task, and (2) the intrinsic rank of task vectors varies substantially across tasks and layers, so a fixed rank either discards critical components or retains unnecessary ones. No prior method adaptively selects which singular components to keep or prune per task and per layer.

## Input / Output

The method consumes T fine-tuned model weights (e.g., ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14, RoBERTa-base, GPT-2) along with their shared pre-trained backbone, plus a small set of unlabeled test samples per task (as few as 1% of the test set suffices for strong gains). It outputs a single merged model of identical size to one fine-tuned model (no extra parameters at inference, no task index required), which produces class labels for any of the T tasks. The binary masks are determined once during a test-time adaptation phase and then frozen, so inference is a standard forward pass through the merged weights.

## Method

AdaRank performs SVD on each task vector (τ_i = θ_i − θ_0 or θ_i − θ̄ for CART) per layer, then introduces a learnable binary mask B_i^l ∈ {0,1}^{1×m} for every singular component of every task vector, where 1 means preserve and 0 means prune. The merged model is constructed as θ_0 + λ Σ_i U_i diag(B_i ⊙ Σ_i) V_i^T, which generalizes both full-rank Task Arithmetic (all masks = 1) and top-k truncation (first k masks = 1). To determine the mask configuration without training labels, AdaRank optimizes B via test-time adaptation using Shannon entropy minimization over unlabeled test data as a surrogate for multi-task loss, with gradients propagated through the binary masks via the Straight-Through Estimator (sigmoid + rounding in forward pass, continuous gradient in backward pass, temperature T=10). The key empirical finding driving the design is that AdaRank frequently prunes top singular components while selectively retaining bottom ones, and the learned ranks closely track the intrinsic rank (95% spectral energy) of each task vector, automatically adapting to task and layer complexity.

## Results

On ViT-B/32 merging 8 vision tasks, TA+AdaRank achieves 87.9% average accuracy, a +18.7% gain over plain Task Arithmetic (69.2%) and +4.1% over the best static SVD-based method TSV-M (83.8%). On ViT-L/14 with 8 tasks, TSV-M+AdaRank reaches 93.7%, approaching the individually fine-tuned performance of 94.2%. For language models, CART+AdaRank on RoBERTa achieves 0.7547 average GLUE score versus 0.6997 for CART alone, and TSV-M+AdaRank on GPT-2 reaches 0.6743 versus 0.6195 for TSV-M. Across all settings, AdaRank consistently outperforms AdaMerging (which adapts only scalar coefficients) by 1.8–7.8% on vision and 0.5–5.2% on language benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaRank consistently outperforms all static SVD-based baselines: on ViT-B/32 (8 tasks), TA+AdaRank (87.9%) surpasses TSV-M (83.8%), CART (84.7%), and Iso-CTS (84.9%) by 3.0–4.1 points. Against the adaptive baseline AdaMerging (which learns only per-task scalar coefficients via entropy minimization), AdaRank wins across every backbone and task count, with the largest gap on ViT-B/32 where TA+AdaRank (87.9%) beats TA+AdaMerging (80.1%) by 7.8 points. On language models, CART+AdaRank on RoBERTa (0.7547) outperforms CART+AdaMerging (0.6954) by 5.9 points, and TSV-M+AdaRank on GPT-2 (0.6743) beats TSV-M+AdaMerging (0.6219) by 5.2 points. Compared to router-based methods (Twin-Merging, WEMoE), AdaRank achieves comparable or better accuracy on 8- and 14-task benchmarks while maintaining a constant model size, whereas router methods scale linearly with the number of tasks (e.g., WEMoE is ~5× larger for 8 tasks and ~10× for 20 tasks). Against compression-based methods that require task-index at inference (TALL-Masks, EMR-Merging, TSV-C), AdaRank matches or exceeds their accuracy without needing task identity, at 1× the parameter count versus 2.2–4.4×. The method also composes additively with post-calibration: applying Representation Surgery on top of AdaRank yields an additional +0.1 to +0.9 points across all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaRank sits at the intersection of two established lines of model merging research: SVD-based low-rank truncation (CART, TSV-M, STAR) and test-time adaptation via entropy minimization (AdaMerging). It departs from the SVD-based methods by replacing their fixed top-k rank heuristic with per-component binary masks that can selectively prune or preserve any singular component, and from AdaMerging by adapting the structural selection of components rather than only scalar coefficients. The Straight-Through Estimator for optimizing binary masks is borrowed from binary neural network literature, and the entropy surrogate objective follows the AdaMerging/Representation Surgery lineage.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear mechanistic explanation (via Taylor expansion and Hessian analysis) for why top-k truncation is suboptimal: large singular values amplify misaligned directions, making the most 'important' components the most dangerous in multi-task settings. This reframes the design principle for SVD-based merging from 'keep the largest' to 'keep the most aligned.' Open questions include whether the TTA step scales gracefully to hundreds of tasks, whether the approach extends to generative or diffusion models where entropy is less natural, and whether the learned masks transfer across different pre-training initializations without re-optimization.
