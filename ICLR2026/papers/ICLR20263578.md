## Problem

SPICE simulation is the gold standard for interconnect signal integrity analysis but is computationally prohibitive for large-scale IC designs. Prior ML surrogates for signal integrity focus on timing prediction without explicitly modeling crosstalk physics—specifically aggressor-victim switching interactions and signal-pattern-dependent effects—leaving a gap in fast, pattern-aware crosstalk analysis.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263578/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes a directed graph of coupled RC interconnects (typically 2 aggressors + 1 victim, each discretized into 5 μm segments with Intel 14nm FinFET parasitics), where intra-net edges carry wire resistance and inter-net edges carry coupling capacitance. Net-level signal attributes (switching direction, slew rate, active/quiet state) are encoded as features on virtual NET tokens. Outputs are per-segment and per-sink predictions: crosstalk delay (ΔD) for victim and aggressor nets, or glitch peak voltage (v_max) and noise width (t_width) on the victim net, all evaluated against HSPICE ground truth via mean relative accuracy.

## Method

Si-GT is a 6-layer graph transformer (4 heads, d=64) with three physics-informed designs that enrich the graph inductive bias. First, mesh pattern encoding decomposes the interconnect graph into local 4-node mesh subgraphs at each segment endpoint (capturing aligned coupling pairs) and aggregates them via a 2-layer GNN to produce node embeddings that separate uncoupled nets. Second, virtual <NET> tokens are learnable per-net embeddings encoding switching direction and slew rate, with an attention mask restricting each token's receptive field to its own net while remaining visible to all other nodes. Third, the Intra-Inter Net (IIN) attention injects two structural biases directly into attention logits: an intra-net term aggregating wire resistance along the source-to-sink path between same-net nodes, and an inter-net term encoding coupling capacitance between aligned segments of coupled nets, complemented by Graphormer-style spatial and edge encodings. The model is trained end-to-end with AdamW (60 epochs, batch 256) on 200k delay and 187k glitch examples generated from HSPICE simulations.

## Results

On the crosstalk delay prediction task (the more challenging of the two), Si-GT (GCN backbone) achieves 88.32% mean relative accuracy on victim segment delay and 73.67% on aggressor segment delay, outperforming the best graph transformer baselines Graphomer (88.23%/72.58%) and GraphGPS (88.23%/72.65%). For crosstalk glitch prediction, Si-GT (GIN backbone) reaches 98.36% on noise width and 97.78% on peak voltage at the segment level, surpassing GraphGPS (96.61%/97.99%) and Graphomer (94.97%/93.38%). Inference time is 4.0 ms on CPU versus over 100 ms for SPICE, a >25× speedup.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Si-GT decisively outperforms all standard GNN baselines (GCN, GAT, GIN, SAGE, DeepGCN) by 15–40 percentage points on delay prediction, with DeepGCN being the strongest GNN at 85.49% (AV segment victim delay) versus Si-GT's 88.32%. Among graph transformer baselines, Si-GT edges out Graphomer and GraphGPS by 0.1–1.1 points on delay tasks (e.g., 73.67% vs. 72.65% for aggressor segment delay) and by 1.7–3.4 points on glitch tasks (e.g., 98.36% vs. 96.61% for segment noise width). The sole exception is V-segment peak voltage (v_max) for glitch prediction, where GraphGPS (97.99%) marginally exceeds the best Si-GT variant (97.89%, GCN backbone). SGFormer performs poorly across all tasks (52–65% on delay), confirming that its simplified attention is insufficient for capturing crosstalk physics. Overall, Si-GT achieves the highest mean relative accuracy in 11 out of 12 evaluation settings across both delay and glitch prediction.

## Relation to Prior Work

Si-GT builds directly on the graph transformer architecture (Dwivedi & Bresson, 2020) and specifically incorporates Graphormer's (Ying et al., 2021) spatial and edge encoding as attention bias terms, while introducing three domain-specific inductive biases—mesh pattern encoding, virtual NET tokens, and intra-inter net attention—that encode RC circuit physics into the transformer. It departs from prior ML-for-SI work (Kahng et al., 2015; Cheng et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2025), which focused on timing prediction without explicitly modeling aggressor-victim switching interactions or signal pattern variability. The paper positions itself as the first to formulate crosstalk prediction (both glitch and delay) as a pattern-dependent graph learning task, rather than a static timing estimation problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of the first large-scale interconnect signal integrity dataset (200k delay + 187k glitch examples with SPICE ground truth) provides a much-needed benchmark for the EDA ML community, and the ~25× inference speedup over HSPICE (4 ms vs. >100 ms) makes ML-based SI analysis practical for iterative design verification loops. However, the dataset is limited to a fixed 2-aggressor/1-victim topology at Intel 14nm FinFET parameters, and all models show degraded generalization on short interconnects due to data sparsity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to more complex multi-net topologies, different technology nodes, and whether the physics-informed inductive biases transfer across design styles without retraining.
