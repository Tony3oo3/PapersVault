## Problem

LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems are highly vulnerable to covert misinformation injection—semantically benign but factually incorrect content that evades conventional detection—yet existing defenses target overtly malicious inputs and are evaluated on simplistic QA tasks that fail to reflect real-world complexity. This paper addresses both gaps by introducing a realistic red-teaming dataset and a goal-aware defense mechanism specifically designed for misinformation propagation in MAS.

## Input / Output

ARGUS consumes the directed communication graph G=(A,E) of the MAS along with the full dialogue logs from the previous round, intercepting messages m_e^r flowing through a dynamically selected subset of k edges (where k=N-1). It produces three outputs: (1) rectified message content that replaces detected misinformation with persuasive corrections, (2) a re-ranked set of top-k edges for the next round's monitoring based on a weighted score combining topological centrality, semantic relevance to inferred misinformation goals, and channel usage frequency, and (3) a deduplicated set of inferred attacker goal descriptions G'_mis that feeds back into the localization loop. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates as an external corrective agent a_cor deployed on selected edges.

## Method

ARGUS is a two-stage, training-free defense framework. Stage 1 (Adaptive Localization) initially places corrective agents on edges with highest normalized Edge Betweenness Centrality, then iteratively re-localizes them each round using a composite score Score^r(e) = α·Score_topo(e) + β·Score_freq(e) + γ·Score_rel(e) (with γ=0.6 dominating), where Score_rel is computed as the maximum sentence-level cosine similarity between message embeddings and the set of inferred misinformation goal embeddings. Stage 2 (Goal-aware Persuasive Rectification) deploys a corrective agent a_cor on each monitored edge that performs a CoT-structured three-step process: multi-faceted identification of suspicious elements (factual errors, logical inconsistencies, common-sense deviations), internal knowledge resonance (activating the LLM's parameterized knowledge to semantically compare against the intercepted claim), and heuristic persuasive reconstruction (generating corrections via root-cause analysis, cognitive reframing, and contextual integration). Concurrently, a_cor executes a parallel goal-aware intent inference sub-task that records the attacker's most probable misleading goal, which is deduplicated via embedding cosine similarity and fed back into the next round's localization, enabling ARGUS to track persistent, coordinated misinformation across rounds.

## Results

On the MISINFOTASK dataset, ARGUS achieves an average Misinformation Toxicity (MT) reduction of approximately 28.17% and a Task Success Rate (TSR) improvement of approximately 10.33% over attack-only MAS across four core LLMs (GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3, Gemini-2.0-flash) and three injection methods (Prompt Injection, RAG Poisoning, Tool Injection). Specifically, ARGUS reduces average MT from 4.90 (GPT-4o attack-only) to 3.51 and lifts TSR from 67.07% to 76.96%. The per-injection-method MT reductions are 28.18% (PI), 20.38% (RP), and 35.95% (TI), with Tool Injection showing the largest defensive gain.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ARGUS dominates both baselines on every LLM and every injection method for both MT and TSR. Compared to Self-Check, ARGUS achieves a substantially larger MT reduction (e.g., GPT-4o-mini: 3.43 vs. 5.02; GPT-4o: 3.51 vs. 4.75) and a much larger TSR gain (e.g., GPT-4o-mini: 78.43% vs. 68.38%; GPT-4o: 76.96% vs. 68.39%), indicating that simple self-reflection prompts are insufficient against covert misinformation. Compared to G-Safeguard (which uses GNN-based edge pruning), ARGUS consistently achieves lower MT across all four LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-V3: 3.25 vs. 4.00; Gemini-2.0-flash: 3.40 vs. 3.67) and higher TSR (e.g., GPT-4o: 76.96% vs. 65.64%; GPT-4o-mini: 78.43% vs. 68.75%), suggesting that topology-guided pruning alone cannot address the semantic content of misinformation. Notably, G-Safeguard actually degrades TSR for GPT-4o (65.64% vs. 67.07% attack-only), whereas ARGUS consistently improves TSR over the attack-only baseline for all four LLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MAS security literature (Netsafe, G-Safeguard, AgentSafe, AgentPrune) by shifting focus from overtly malicious or jailbreak content to the more insidious threat of misinformation—semantically benign but factually incorrect statements that circumvent conventional detection. It builds on Chain-of-Thought prompting for the corrective agent's multi-stage reasoning and on ReAct-style agent architectures as the operational substrate, but departs from prior defenses by combining graph-theoretic edge localization (betweenness centrality + semantic relevance + frequency) with goal-aware persuasive rectification rather than relying solely on topological pruning or self-reflection. The introduction of MISINFOTASK, a task-driven red-teaming dataset with 108 realistic multi-step tasks and 4–8 plausible misinformation arguments per task, addresses a gap the authors identify: prior injection benchmarks use simplistic QA tasks and target malicious rather than covert misinformation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that semantically benign misinformation causes a ~20% TSR drop in generic MAS—and that established defenses like Self-Check and G-Safeguard offer only marginal protection—highlights a significant and underappreciated vulnerability in deployed multi-agent systems. ARGUS's training-free, topology-agnostic design (validated across five graph structures and four LLM families) makes it a practical drop-in defense, and the MISINFOTASK dataset provides the community a standardized benchmark for misinformation-specific robustness. Key open questions remain: the computational overhead of the corrective agent (~28% cost increase), the limitation to knowledge resident in LLM parameters (not dynamic external facts), and whether the goal-aware localization generalizes to coordinated multi-agent misinformation campaigns beyond single-point compromise.
