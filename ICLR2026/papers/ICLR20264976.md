## Problem

LP-based formulations of MDPs offer structural advantages over Bellman-equation methods (especially in offline RL), but are underused because they produce inequality-constrained optimization problems typically solved via slow primal-dual schemes. No prior work has applied the classical log-barrier technique to the MDP LP to obtain an unconstrained objective amenable to simple gradient descent, nor has a rigorous error analysis of the resulting approximate solution been established.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264976/cropped_page8_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tabular method takes a full MDP specification (finite state and action sets, transition probabilities, reward function, discount factor γ) and a probability distribution ρ over state-action pairs, and produces an approximate optimal Q-function Q̃_η from which both a deterministic greedy policy (primal η-policy) and a stochastic policy (dual η-policy) are derived. The single hyperparameter η > 0 controls the approximation gap: both the Q-function error ‖Q̃_η − Q*‖_∞ and the policy objective gap scale linearly in η. In the deep RL variant, the method consumes (s, a, r, s′) tuples sampled from an experience replay buffer and outputs Q-function estimates via a neural network, with no target network required.

## Method

The paper reformulates the Q-function-based LP of the MDP by replacing each inequality constraint with a log-barrier penalty term η·φ((FQ)(s,a,a′) − Q(s,a)), yielding a strictly convex unconstrained objective f_η(Q) whose minimizer Q̃_η approximates the optimal Q-function. The gradient admits a closed form involving an implicit dual variable λ_η(s,a,a′) = ηw(s,a,a′)/(Q(s,a) − (FQ)(s,a,a′)), and the first-order optimality condition at Q̃_η exactly matches the dual LP constraints, so Q̃_η simultaneously yields both a primal and a dual approximate solution. The paper proves linear-in-η upper and lower bounds on the Q-function error, Bellman error, and policy objective gaps, establishes convexity and strong convexity on sublevel sets, and shows exponential convergence of constant-step-size gradient descent. For deep RL, the log-barrier loss L(θ) = (1/|B|)Σ[Q_θ(s,a) + η·φ(r + γQ_θ(s′,a′) − Q_θ(s,a))] replaces the standard MSE Bellman loss in DQN and DDPG, and is shown via Jensen's inequality to be an unbiased stochastic approximation of f_η in the deterministic-transition case.

## Results

The paper reports results via learning curves (average return over 10 seeds for DQN, 8 seeds for DDPG) rather than a single numerical table. For Log-barrier DQN across five Gymnasium environments (Acrobot-v1, CartPole-v1, LunarLander-v3, MountainCar-v0, Pendulum-v1), the method performs on par with standard DQN overall but shows markedly superior stability and faster adaptation in CartPole-v1. For Log-barrier DDPG on five MuJoCo continuous-control tasks, the method achieves superior final returns on four of five environments (Ant, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Humanoid) while showing no significant advantage on Hopper. The authors note that Ant and Humanoid were previously considered beyond standard DDPG's capabilities, and the log-barrier variant successfully solves them.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against two baselines: standard DQN (Mnih et al., 2015) for discrete control and standard DDPG (Lillicrap et al., 2015) for continuous control. Against DQN, Log-barrier DQN ties or slightly trails on most environments (Acrobot, LunarLander, MountainCar, Pendulum) but clearly wins on CartPole-v1, where it demonstrates rapid adaptation and significantly lower variance, attributed to the LP formulation's ability to handle the sharp survival/termination decision boundary more robustly than MSE-based Bellman updates. Against DDPG, Log-barrier DDPG wins decisively on four of five MuJoCo tasks (Ant, Walker2d, HalfCheetah, Humanoid), with the authors attributing the advantage to the LP's inherent minimization objective acting as an implicit regularizer against Q-value overestimation. On Hopper, the simpler task, no significant difference is observed between the two methods. Overall, the log-barrier approach matches DQN in the discrete setting and substantially outperforms DDPG in the continuous setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two well-established threads: the LP formulation of MDPs (Puterman; De Farias and Van Roy; Lee and He) and the log-barrier method from convex optimization (Boyd and Vandenberghe; Nesterov). Prior LP-based RL work has relied almost exclusively on primal-dual schemes, which the authors note suffer from slow convergence and high computational cost. The key departure is applying the log-barrier to the Q-function-based LP to obtain a single unconstrained objective amenable to plain gradient descent, a combination the authors state has not been previously analyzed. For the deep RL extension, the paper repurposes the tabular log-barrier objective as a stochastic loss within the DQN and DDPG frameworks, replacing the standard MSE Bellman loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The linear-in-η error bounds for both the Q-function and the MDP objective provide practitioners with a concrete, interpretable knob for controlling approximation quality, which is absent from most primal-dual LP-RL methods. The conceptual observation that the LP's minimization structure acts as an implicit regularizer against Q-value overestimation offers a principled alternative to the ad-hoc tricks (clipping, double Q-learning) used in actor-critic methods. However, the deep RL experiments are limited in scope (five discrete and five continuous environments, modest seed counts), the stochastic loss is biased in the general stochastic-transition case, and the theoretical guarantees do not formally transfer to the neural-network setting, leaving open whether the tabular convergence and error results hold at scale.
