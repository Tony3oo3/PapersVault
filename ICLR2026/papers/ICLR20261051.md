## Problem

Existing unlearning methods for text-to-image diffusion models assume all removal requests arrive simultaneously, yet in practice they arrive sequentially (e.g., a parent requesting violent content removal followed later by an artist requesting copyrighted style exclusion). This paper shows that when popular methods like ConAbl and SculpMem are applied sequentially, they suffer rapid utility collapse—forgetting retained concepts and generating degraded images after only a handful of requests—due to cumulative parameter drift from the pre-training weights.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261051/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method operates on a pre-trained Stable Diffusion checkpoint (from UNLEARNCANVAS, fine-tuned to generate 60 artistic styles and 20 object categories with >98% top-1 accuracy) and processes a sequential stream of target concepts (12 styles or 12 objects). At each step, it consumes the target concept text, the current model weights, and (for gradient projection) text-embedding vectors of auxiliary semantically similar concepts generated by an LLM. Output is the updated model weights after each request, evaluated by generating images with the template 'A {object} image in {style} style' and scoring them with specialized CLIP-based classifiers for unlearning accuracy, in-domain retention, and cross-domain retention.

## Method

The paper proposes a suite of plug-and-play add-on regularizers compatible with existing unlearning losses, grounded in a Taylor-expansion bound showing retention loss change is Lipschitz-continuous in parameter drift. Three generic regularizers constrain drift: L1/L2 update-norm penalties relative to the previous checkpoint, Selective Fine-tuning (SelFT) that restricts updates to the top-k% parameters by first-order Taylor importance, and TIES-Merging that unlearns each concept independently and merges the resulting models. The key novel contribution is a semantic-aware gradient-projection method: it identifies the cross-attention projection matrices W_K and W_V as the source of interference (since linear projections preserve neighborhood structure, suppressing a target concept distorts semantically nearby ones), then projects out gradient components lying in the subspace spanned by text embeddings of auxiliary similar concepts, enforcing first-order invariance for those neighbors. This projection is complementary to the generic regularizers, and their combination (e.g., SelFT + gradient projection) yields the strongest overall retention across in-domain and cross-domain settings.

## Results

The paper evaluates continual unlearning of 12 concepts (styles or objects) sequentially using UNLEARNCANVAS (60 styles, 20 objects) with a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion checkpoint. Sequential ConAbl maintains high Unlearning Accuracy (UA) but suffers rapid collapse in In-Domain Retention (RA-I) and Cross-Domain Retention (RA-C) after only a few requests, while the simultaneous strategy preserves utility at superlinear training cost. Add-on regularizers (L1/L2 norm, Selective Fine-tuning, Model Merging) substantially reduce cumulative parameter drift and restore retention, with Model Merging delivering the strongest overall retention. Gradient projection achieves the highest RA-I across both style and object settings, and combining it with SelFT or Model Merging yields further gains in the harmonic mean of UA, RA-I, and RA-C (Figure 8).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ConAbl (Kumari et al., 2023) as the primary unlearning method, all proposed add-on regularizers improve retention without sacrificing unlearning accuracy; Model Merging gives the strongest overall RA-C, while Gradient Projection gives the best RA-I, and their combination (Grad. Proj. + SelFT) is recommended as a strong starting point. Against SculpMem (Li et al., 2025), which uses a dynamic gradient mask and achieves a stronger single-concept baseline, the same utility collapse occurs after 12 sequential concepts, and the add-on regularizers yield consistent improvements with Gradient Projection again delivering the strongest in-domain retention (Appendix A, Figures 10–11). Against ESD (Gandikota et al., 2023) on the SDXL architecture for celebrity erasure, Gradient Projection outperforms L1, L2, and SelFT individually but is surprisingly outperformed by Model Merging alone; however, combining Gradient Projection with Model Merging yields the best harmonic mean of UA, RA-I, and RA-C (Figures 15–17). Across all three baselines and both architectures (SD 1.5 and SDXL), the key finding is consistent: sequential unlearning without regularization causes rapid utility collapse, and the proposed plug-and-play regularizers are method- and architecture-agnostic.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes regularization and optimization ideas from continual learning (weight decay, gradient projection, selective fine-tuning) to address a gap in the diffusion-model unlearning literature: existing methods like ConAbl and SculpMem assume all unlearning requests arrive simultaneously, while the paper shows they fail catastrophically when requests arrive sequentially. It builds directly on ConAbl and SculpMem as the underlying unlearning algorithms, on TIES-Merging for the model-merge regularizer, and on Nguyen et al.'s concept-attribution method for the SelFT regularizer. The theoretical analysis draws on the loss-approximation framework from continual learning (Zenke et al., Yin et al.) to formalize why parameter drift from pre-training weights governs retention loss.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes continual unlearning as a distinct and practically important challenge for text-to-image generation, providing the first benchmark (extending UNLEARNCANVAS with sequential style/object sequences) and a clear diagnostic (cumulative parameter drift) that practitioners can use to evaluate future methods. The plug-and-play regularizers are broadly applicable across unlearning algorithms and architectures (validated on SD, SDXL, ConAbl, SculpMem, ESD), making them immediately useful. Open questions include robustness to adversarial recovery attacks across sequential steps, generalization to newer architectures (DiT) and modalities (video, speech), and whether natively sequential unlearning methods that anticipate future requests can outperform the add-on regularization paradigm.
