## Problem

Existing image-to-video diffusion models are target-unaware: they cannot reliably direct an actor to interact with a specific designated object in the scene, often hallucinating the target instead. While prior controllable video generation methods rely on dense structural cues (depth maps, optical flow, motion trajectories), the authors seek a minimal-input formulation where a single segmentation mask suffices to specify the target, enabling video diffusion models to serve as motion planners for human-object interactions.

## Input / Output

The model takes a 720×480 RGB image, a binary segmentation mask of the target object (downsampled to latent resolution and concatenated channel-wise with the input image latent), and a text prompt describing the desired action. It produces a 49-frame video at 720×480 showing the actor performing the specified interaction with the masked target. During inference, a [TGT] token is prepended to the target noun in the prompt, and the mask provides spatial grounding; the model is robust to mask quality, accepting even coarse or circular masks centered on the target region.

## Method

The approach extends CogVideoX-5B-I2V by adding a single zero-initialized channel to the image projection layer to accept the target mask, then fine-tunes with LoRA (rank 128) on 1290 curated clips from BEHAVE and Ego-Exo4D. The key mechanism is a cross-attention loss that aligns the V2T cross-attention maps of a special [TGT] token (appended to every training caption as 'The person interacts with [TGT] object.') with the downsampled target mask, injecting spatial grounding into the text-conditioning pathway. This loss is selectively applied to only the V2T cross-attention regions (rather than T2V) and to a subset of transformer blocks (5th through 23rd, sampled every 5th) identified as most semantically relevant, reducing VRAM by 71% while maximizing target-awareness. The total objective combines the standard diffusion reconstruction loss with the cross-attention alignment loss weighted by λ_attn=0.1, and the model generalizes to non-human actors, outdoor scenes, and egocentric views without additional training.

## Results

On the target-aware video generation benchmark (80 images, 400 generated videos), the proposed method achieves a Contact Score of 0.878, substantially outperforming CogVideoX (0.560), CogVideoX fine-tuned on the same data (0.638), and the attention modulation baseline (0.546). In human evaluation, the method scores 0.892 vs. 0.456–0.596 for baselines, and in user preference tests it is preferred over every baseline in 100% of comparisons. Video generation quality (VBench average) remains comparable at 0.807, matching CogVideoX (0.810) and CogVideoX w. data (0.810), confirming that target awareness does not degrade visual fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three baselines: vanilla CogVideoX (the base I2V model), CogVideoX fine-tuned on the same dataset (to isolate the effect of additional data), and an adapted attention modulation method from Direct-a-Video. The proposed method decisively outperforms all three on target alignment: Contact Score of 0.878 vs. 0.560 (CogVideoX, +31.8%), 0.638 (CogVideoX w. data, +24.0%), and 0.546 (Attn. Mod., +33.2%). In user studies, the method is preferred over every baseline in 100% of pairwise comparisons, while baselines are preferred only 22–36% of the time. On video quality (VBench average), the method scores 0.807, essentially matching CogVideoX and CogVideoX w. data (both 0.810) and slightly exceeding Attn. Mod. (0.786), confirming that the cross-attention loss does not compromise generation fidelity. The attention modulation baseline in particular suffers from degraded temporal consistency because amplifying cross-attention values in the MM-DiT architecture disrupts the row-wise softmax, adversely affecting video self-attention and producing lower dynamic degree (0.786 vs. 0.899 for ours).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends CogVideoX (a multimodal diffusion transformer for image-to-video generation) by adding a single-channel target mask input and a [TGT] token in the text prompt, fine-tuned with LoRA. Conceptually, it departs from prior controllable video generation that relies on dense structural cues (depth, optical flow, trajectories) or inference-time attention modulation (e.g., Direct-a-Video), instead using only a sparse spatial specification (one mask) plus a text prompt to direct actor-target interactions. The cross-attention loss that aligns the [TGT] token's attention with the mask is the key architectural addition, drawing on the Prompt-to-Prompt lineage of using cross-attention for spatial control but applied during training rather than at inference. The paper also positions its model as a motion planner in the world-model tradition, connecting video generation to robotics planning and 3D HOI synthesis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing target awareness as a first-class capability of video diffusion models, this work opens a path toward using generative video models as high-level action planners for robotics and content creation, reducing the need for dense motion annotations. The demonstrated generalization to non-human actors (animals, robotic hands) and outdoor scenes from a small (~1.3K clip) human-interaction training set is practically encouraging. Key open questions include scalability to multiple simultaneous targets (currently requiring an extra channel per target), temporal target switching within a single video, and the reliance on a human-hand-specific contact detector for evaluation. The static-camera constraint and the architectural limitation of one mask per target are acknowledged gaps that limit immediate deployment in complex multi-object scenes.
