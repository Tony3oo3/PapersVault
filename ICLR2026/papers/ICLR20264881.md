## Problem

Existing automated evaluation metrics for autoformalization suffer from a fundamental trade-off: string-based methods (e.g., BLEU) are blind to semantic content, while proof-based approaches (e.g., BEq, Definitional Equality) are brittle, yielding binary pass/fail with no graded similarity when proofs fail. The paper addresses the need for a reproducible, CPU-only metric that robustly integrates both semantic equivalence and structural likeness to assess formal statement similarity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pair of syntactically valid Lean 4 formal statements (a ground-truth theorem and a predicted formalization) and produces a real-valued similarity score in [0, 1]. Statements are parsed into labeled, ordered operator trees via the Lean Language Server, with placeholders appended to operator nodes and parentheses omitted. The score is thresholded for binary classification; the optimal threshold is benchmark-dependent (0.61 for EPLA-miniF2F vs. 0.38 for EPLA-ProofNet), reflecting structural diversity of the domain. The entire pipeline runs on CPU only, requiring just the Lean Language Server and REPL.

## Method

ASSESS is a two-stage framework whose core metric, TransTED Similarity, augments standard Tree Edit Distance (TED) over operator trees with semantic transformations. In the first stage, each formal statement is parsed into an operator tree (OPT) using the Lean Language Server. In the second stage, the pair is reformulated as a single equality, and a heuristic search (guided by TED as the cost function) applies a curated set of Lean tactic commands (rw?, apply congrArg, apply forall_congr, ext, norm_cast, etc.) to transform the equality toward a provable form. The theoretical foundation defines TransTED as the unique largest pseudometric dominated by TED and monotone under logical-strengthening transformations (Theorem 1), ensuring that semantically equivalent but syntactically diverse expressions receive distance 0. Search terminates on proof success, node-limit exceeded, or time-limit exceeded, returning the minimum TED encountered among visited nodes.

## Results

TransTED Similarity achieves state-of-the-art performance on both EPLA benchmarks: 70.16% accuracy and 0.35 Cohen's Kappa on EPLA-miniF2F, and 67.31% accuracy and 0.30 Kappa on EPLA-ProofNet. The ablation shows that the semantic transformation component contributes +0.60 pp accuracy and +0.04 Kappa on miniF2F, and +2.64 pp accuracy and +0.07 Kappa on ProofNet over the base TED Similarity. All evaluations run on CPU only (Lean Language Server + REPL), with an average per-pair runtime of ~51 s (miniF2F) and ~131 s (ProofNet).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TransTED Similarity outperforms all five baselines on both accuracy and Kappa across both benchmarks. Against BLEU (Papineni et al., 2002), TransTED gains +1.2 pp accuracy and +0.09 Kappa on miniF2F, and a larger +10.1 pp accuracy and +0.12 Kappa on ProofNet, where BLEU's n-gram overlap is particularly unreliable for formal mathematics. Against the LLM-based Majority Voting baseline (Ying et al., 2024a), TransTED achieves a decisive +23.2 pp accuracy and +0.21 Kappa advantage on miniF2F, while also being fully CPU-based and reproducible versus the GPU-dependent 16-round LLM pipeline. Against the proof-based metrics from Liu et al. (2025a), TransTED surpasses BEq by +10.7 pp accuracy and +0.06 Kappa on miniF2F, and by +7.0 pp accuracy and +0.02 Kappa on ProofNet; Definitional Equality is even further behind at 49.46% / 46.39% accuracy. The closest competitor overall is BEq (59.45% / 60.34% accuracy), whose high precision (≥98%) is offset by very low recall (45.77% / 32.79%) due to prover brittleness, whereas TransTED maintains a more balanced precision-recall profile.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Tree Edit Distance (Zhang & Shasha, 1989) framework to formal statement evaluation by introducing semantic transformations into the distance computation, building directly on GTED (Liu et al., 2025c) which first applied operator trees to autoformalization evaluation. The key departure from GTED is a rigorous pseudometric-space formulation with a monotonicity-under-transformation axiom, plus a richer transformation mechanism (curated Lean tactics including rw? and structural rewrites) rather than GTED's limited variable renaming. The paper also positions itself against the broader landscape of string-based (BLEU), proof-based (Definitional Equality, BEq), and LLM-as-a-Judge metrics, arguing that none adequately balance semantic and structural information in a reproducible, graded manner.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The EPLA benchmark with its three-dimensional annotation scheme (provability, likeness before/after transformation) provides a more nuanced evaluation standard than the binary provable/unprovable labels used in prior work, and the TransTED metric offers a CPU-only, reproducible alternative to GPU-dependent LLM judges. However, the practical gains over plain TED are modest (0.6–2.6 pp accuracy), the tactic set is limited and may miss creative transformation paths, and the metric remains threshold-dependent with no guarantee of 100% precision. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond Lean to other formal languages, whether the tactic set can be expanded or learned automatically, and whether the pseudometric framework can be tightened to reduce the semantic-structural tension that produces false positives on off-by-one errors.
