## Problem

Safety-aligned LLMs suffer from over-refusal, rejecting benign prompts that superficially resemble toxic ones, and prior mitigation strategies (data augmentation, activation steering) face a safety-helpfulness trade-off. This paper identifies the root cause as high gradient-space similarity between seemingly toxic and truly toxic prompts inherited from pre-training, a phenomenon that had not been explicitly studied or addressed at its source.

## Input / Output

The method operates on text prompts and produces text responses, with the contrastive stage consuming pairs of prompts (seemingly toxic vs. toxic) and applying Circle Loss to their intermediate activations at a chosen layer (e.g., layer 13 for Qwen2.5, layer 15 for LLaMA-3). The subsequent SFT stage takes standard instruction-response pairs mixed with toxic-prompt/safe-refusal pairs. The final output is a fine-tuned model that, given any text prompt, generates either a substantive response or a safe refusal depending on whether the prompt is genuinely toxic.

## Method

DCR reformulates safety alignment as a two-stage pipeline: (1) a contrastive refinement stage that applies Circle Loss to intermediate activations at layer ℓ, treating same-subset prompt pairs as positives and cross-subset pairs as negatives, while freezing all parameters beyond layer ℓ; (2) standard SFT safety alignment (STL) on the disentangled representations. Proposition 1 provides a theoretical bound showing that the empirical NTK similarity ‖K^t(x′,x)‖_F is upper-bounded by a Q_ℓ-bilinear form of the layer-ℓ activations, so reducing that bilinear similarity via contrastive learning directly decouples the gradient-space coupling between seemingly toxic and toxic prompts. Empirically, this reduces cross-class kernel similarity while leaving within-class similarity intact, so the refusal signal learned in stage 2 transfers only to truly toxic prompts. The approach requires no architectural changes, uses only 750 contrastive prompts, and adds negligible training time (<1 min) compared to the SFT stage.

## Results

DCR achieves the highest compliance rate across all three LLMs on nearly all five over-refusal benchmarks. On Qwen2.5-1.5B, DCR reaches 0.98 compliance on XSTest and CoCoNot (vs. 0.73 and 0.88 for STL) while maintaining a safety defense success rate of 0.81 (vs. 0.72 for STL). On LLaMA-3-8B, DCR achieves 0.93 on XSTest and 0.99 on CoCoNot with 0.91 safety. General QA ability shows a slight drop (e.g., MMLU 0.58 vs. 0.59 for STL on Qwen2.5-1.5B), but response quality (AlpacaEval win rate) is competitive or superior: 51.8% for DCR vs. 50.0% for STL on Qwen2.5-1.5B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DCR substantially outperforms the direct SFT baseline (STL) on over-refusal compliance across all models and benchmarks—for example, on Qwen2.5-1.5B, DCR achieves 0.98 vs. 0.73 on XSTest and 0.98 vs. 0.88 on CoCoNot—while also improving safety (0.81 vs. 0.72 defense success rate). Against STL-aug, which adds seemingly toxic prompts directly into SFT data, DCR consistently wins on compliance (e.g., 0.98 vs. 0.75 on XSTest for Qwen2.5-1.5B), demonstrating that contrastive refinement is more effective than simply including benign examples in the fine-tuning set. Compared to the activation-steering baselines, DCR dominates on compliance: Surgical drops to 0.54 on OR-Bench and PHTest for Qwen2.5-1.5B, and SCANS achieves only 0.65 safety (vs. DCR's 0.81), indicating that vector manipulation degrades safety. On response quality (AlpacaEval), DCR scores 51.8% on Qwen2.5-1.5B, beating Surgical (40.2%) and SCANS (47.0%), while matching or slightly exceeding STL (50.0%). The only consistent trade-off is a small drop in knowledge-intensive QA accuracy (e.g., MMLU 0.58 vs. 0.59), which the authors attribute to the contrastive stage not explicitly preserving internal factual representations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Safety-Tuned LLaMAs (STL) SFT-based safety alignment paradigm and the learning dynamics framework of Ren & Sutherland (2024), but departs from prior over-refusal mitigation strategies (data augmentation, activation steering via SCANS/Surgical) by addressing the root cause rather than repairing symptoms post hoc. The key conceptual departure is reformulating safety alignment as a two-stage process: a contrastive refinement stage (using Circle Loss on intermediate activations) that explicitly reduces the empirical neural tangent kernel similarity between seemingly toxic and truly toxic prompts, followed by standard SFT. Proposition 1 provides the theoretical bridge connecting contrastive loss on intermediate features to the gradient-space kernel similarity that drives over-refusal, a connection absent from prior activation-steering or data-augmentation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a principled, low-cost pre-alignment stage (negligible compute overhead) that substantially reduces over-refusal while preserving safety, making it practically attractive for safety alignment pipelines. However, it introduces a slight degradation in knowledge-intensive QA accuracy, and the residual similarity between seemingly toxic and general prompts (attributed to pre-training imperfections) remains unaddressed. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger models (tested only up to 8B), whether LoRA can be integrated into the DCR stage to reduce memory, and how to preserve internal factual knowledge during contrastive refinement without sacrificing over-refusal mitigation.
