## Problem

Single-agent Medical LVLMs struggle to generalize across diverse medical specialties (radiology, pathology, oncology, etc.), and existing multi-agent collaboration frameworks that simulate clinical workflows (GP → Specialist → GP) rely on fixed, static interaction patterns that lack adaptive reasoning. Moreover, prior multi-agent systems do not optimize the agents themselves via reinforcement learning, leaving the attending physician unable to learn when to trust versus challenge potentially noisy or conflicting specialist opinions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262752/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The system consumes a medical RGB image, a multiple-choice text question, and independent diagnostic responses from e=3 specialist LVLMs (e.g., o3, GPT-4o) assigned to the triage-selected department. The triage doctor selects from 7 predefined specialties (Pathologist, Radiologist, Surgeon, Oncologist, Endocrinologist, Ophthalmologist, Dermatologist). The attending physician outputs a structured response with a reasoning process enclosed in <think> tags followed by the final multiple-choice answer in <answer> tags, explicitly evaluating specialist outputs before arriving at its own judgment.

## Method

MMedAgent-RL trains two GP agents (triage doctor and attending physician) based on Qwen2.5-VL via a two-stage RL pipeline. The triage doctor is optimized with GRPO using image-modality ground truth (e.g., pathology slides → Pathologist) to improve departmental assignment accuracy. The attending physician is trained with C-MARL, a curriculum-based RL strategy that stratifies training data by specialist accuracy (easy: s=1, medium: 0<s<1, hard: s=0) and dynamically modulates the entropy bonus coefficient γ_s in the GRPO objective—near-zero for easy cases (exploit reliable specialist consensus), moderate for medium cases, and strongly positive for hard cases (aggressively explore to break from misleading specialist hallucinations). This entropy-aware curriculum enables the agent to progressively learn when to adopt specialist knowledge and when to override it. The paper further provides a convergence theorem showing that curriculum learning achieves smaller parameter error than standard SGD by decomposing the optimization into a sequence of tractable sub-problems with small inter-stage policy distances.

## Results

On the in-domain medical VQA benchmarks (VQA-RAD, SLAKE, PathVQA), MMedAgent-RL (7B) achieves an average accuracy of 73.3%, surpassing the strongest single-agent model GPT-4o (68.6%) by 4.7 points and the best multi-agent baseline AFlow (67.5%) by 5.8 points. On out-of-distribution datasets (OmniMedVQA, MMMU-Med), it reaches 72.6% average, outperforming GPT-4o (69.1%) and the SFT-based variant (64.5%) by 3.5 and 8.1 points respectively. With majority-voting test-time scaling, the in-domain average further improves to 76.1% and OOD to 76.6%. The paper reports an overall 23.6% average performance gain over the SFT method across all benchmarks.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MMedAgent-RL outperforms all 14 baselines across every benchmark. Against the strongest single-agent model GPT-4o, it gains +4.7 points in-domain (73.3 vs 68.6) and +3.5 points OOD (72.6 vs 69.1). Among multi-agent baselines, the gap is much larger: MMedAgent-RL beats AFlow by +5.8 (ID) and +16.0 (OOD), MDAgents by +6.5 (ID) and +17.5 (OOD), and MedAgents by +7.7 (ID) and +20.0 (OOD), indicating that RL-optimized collaboration substantially outperforms static prompt-based pipelines. Compared to its own base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B (62.3 ID / 58.7 OOD), the multi-agent RL framework yields +11.0 and +13.9 points respectively. Against the SFT variant with reasoning (67.0 ID / 64.5 OOD), MMedAgent-RL improves by +6.3 and +8.1 points, which the authors attribute to the curriculum RL strategy's ability to handle noisy specialist inputs rather than simply imitating them. The ablation (Table 2) further shows that removing C-MARL drops OOD accuracy from 72.6 to 50.2 (MMMU), confirming that the curriculum entropy-aware RL is the dominant contributor to the performance gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-agent medical collaboration paradigm (MedAgents, MDAgents, AFlow) by replacing static, untrained GP→Specialist→GP pipelines with RL-optimized agents. It builds directly on GRPO (from DeepSeek-R1) as the base RL algorithm, augmenting it with a curriculum-structured entropy regularization term that modulates exploration based on specialist reliability. The curriculum learning principle (Bengio et al., 2009) is repurposed in a novel way: difficulty is defined not by problem complexity but by the accuracy of external expert outputs, creating a three-stage training schedule (easy→medium→hard) that progressively teaches the attending physician to trust, question, and override specialist consensus. The theoretical analysis in Section 4 provides a convergence guarantee showing that this staged optimization requires fewer iterations than monolithic SGD on the mixed distribution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 23.6% average gain over SFT and consistent out-of-distribution improvements (21% over base model) demonstrate that RL-based multi-agent collaboration can substantially outperform both single-agent fine-tuning and static multi-agent pipelines in medical VQA. The curriculum-by-specialist-reliability design offers a practical recipe for handling noisy external knowledge in any multi-agent setting, not just medicine. Key limitations include reliance on proprietary models (o3, GPT-4o) as specialists, restriction to 7 specialties and multiple-choice answers, and a 7B base model that may not scale to larger architectures. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to free-form diagnostic reasoning, whether open-source specialists can replace proprietary ones without significant performance loss, and how the framework behaves when the number of specialists or specialties grows substantially.
