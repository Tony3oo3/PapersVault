## Problem

Prior work on 'dormant' attention heads relies exclusively on attention-weight patterns (e.g., first-token attention sinks) to identify inactive heads, ignoring other mechanisms by which a head's contribution can be negligible (small value vectors, small head outputs). This narrow definition underestimates the true prevalence of inactive heads and is not model-agnostic, performing poorly on families like OLMo-2. The paper asks: what is the most robust, model-agnostic way to identify inactive heads, and how many can actually be removed without degrading performance?

## Input / Output

The method consumes pretrained LLM weights (14 models across Llama-3.1/3.2, OLMo-2, and Qwen2.5 families) and token sequences from MMLU (5-shot), PIQA (0-shot), WinoGrande (5-shot), and FineWeb-Edu (randomly truncated to 10–3000 tokens). During each forward pass, it computes 12 per-head scores (6 base metrics × 2 normalization variants), thresholds them to produce a boolean mask of inactive heads, and zeros those heads' outputs before the output projection. The key output is the maximum fraction of heads that can be zeroed while keeping MMLU accuracy within 1% of baseline, plus score distributions and pairwise Wasserstein distance matrices used to compare attention behavior across models, scales, and finetuning stages.

## Method

The paper defines 12 threshold-based score functions spanning three components of multi-head attention: attention weights (Avg Weight of First Token, Avg Entropy of Query Distributions), value vectors (First Token Value Vector Norm, Avg Value Vector Norm), and head outputs (Last Token Head Output Norm, Avg Head Output Norm), each with a layer-normalized variant that divides by the mean score of other heads in the same layer. Inactivity is verified through dynamic model interventions: for every forward pass during MMLU evaluation, the boolean mask is recomputed and identified heads' outputs are zeroed, measuring the resulting accuracy drop. The central finding is that Avg Head Output Norm (LN) is the most model-agnostic score, ranking first for 8/14 models and top-3 for 13/14, while the attention-sink-based AWFT score underestimates inactive heads by missing over 7% on average. The paper further uses Wasserstein distance between per-model score distributions to show that finetuning (SFT, DPO, RLHF) induces negligible changes in attention behavior, and that model scale has little effect until the 14B regime.

## Results

On MMLU (5-shot), the paper's score functions identify an average of 12.18% of attention heads that can be zeroed while maintaining accuracy within 1% of the pretrained model, compared to only 4.61% using the prior AWFT baseline (+7.56% improvement). Avg Head Output Norm (LN) ranks 1st for 8 out of 14 models and top-3 for 13 out of 14 models. On PIQA, more than 21% of heads can be zeroed (vs. 10% for AWFT), and on WinoGrande, more than 14% (vs. 6.9% for AWFT), confirming the main-body MMLU result is a conservative estimate.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Avg Weight of First Token (AWFT) from Gu et al. (2025) and Guo et al. (2024a), which identifies heads by measuring whether average attention to the first token exceeds a threshold. The paper's score functions (measuring head output norms, value vector norms, and entropy) outperform AWFT on 11 out of 14 models, with an average improvement of +7.56 percentage points in the fraction of zeroable heads. The gap is most dramatic for the OLMo-2 family, where AWFT identifies fewer than 2.5% of heads as inactive while the paper's methods identify 8–21% (e.g., OLMo-2-1124-7B-DPO: 20.60% vs. 2.14%). AWFT wins on only three models (Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, Qwen2.5-14B), and even there the margin is small (≤1.2%). The paper further shows that AWFT is dataset-sensitive (requiring thresholds of 0.077, 0.265, and 0.109 for MMLU, PIQA, and WinoGrande respectively), whereas AHON (LN) thresholds are stable across datasets (0.435–0.473), making it a more model-agnostic and generalizable indicator of inactivity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dormant-head identification framework of Guo et al. (2024a), which zeroed individual heads and measured loss changes, into a systematic, multi-head, benchmark-accuracy evaluation across 14 models from 3 families. It departs from the attention-weight-only scoring (AWFT) proposed by Gu et al. (2025) and the broader attention-sink literature by introducing score functions that measure value vector norms and head output norms, arguing that attention patterns alone are an unreliable proxy for inactivity. The work also connects to the earlier head-pruning literature (Voita et al., 2019; Michel et al., 2019) but reframes the problem as dynamic, per-forward-pass identification rather than static architectural pruning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that attention-weight-based metrics (AWFT) miss over 7% of truly inactive heads on average corrects a common assumption in the field and provides a more model-agnostic identification method (AHON LN) that could inform dynamic pruning, KV-cache compression, and architecture design. However, the best-performing score function requires computing the full head output, which limits immediate compute savings and leaves open the question of whether a cheaper proxy exists. The paper also leaves generation-time inactivity unanalyzed (only single-forward-pass MC benchmarks are evaluated), and the practical efficiency gains remain unquantified due to the overhead of score computation.
