## Problem

Existing human image animation methods rely on intermediate pose representations (skeleton maps, DensePose, SMPL) as driving signals, which are error-prone under occlusion, complex articulation, and front-back ambiguity, and provide limited expressiveness for facial animation. This paper asks whether animation can be learned directly from raw driving video pixels, bypassing noisy pose estimators entirely, while still achieving robust cross-identity generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264498/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a static reference image (defining identity), a driving video sequence (providing motion and expression), and an auto-generated text prompt (via Qwen2-VL) as inputs. The driving video is preprocessed into a Driving Cue Triplet: a low-pass-filtered foreground silhouette (Pose Cue), a cropped-and-centered face region (Face Cue), and grid-based softened body/face masks (Location Cue) for spatial alignment. Output is a 49-frame animated video at 512×512 resolution, generated by the CogVideoX1.5 DiT backbone in approximately 153 seconds on a single H20 GPU.

## Method

DirectAnimator replaces skeleton maps with a structured Driving Cue Triplet (Pose, Face, Location) extracted from raw driving video pixels via Grounded SAM segmentation, frequency-domain low-pass filtering, and InsightFace cropping. These cues are injected into the CogVideoX1.5 DiT backbone through a CueFusion DiT (CF-DiT) Block that applies time-conditioned AdaLN modulation and gated residual connections to pose and face embeddings, keeping identity on the main denoising path while motion/expression is injected via conditional modulation. For cross-ID training, the paper introduces a Same2X strategy: the model is first pretrained on same-ID pairs, then adapted to cross-ID using pseudo driving cues (synthesized by StableAnimator and Face-Adapter) supervised by a Same2X Alignment Loss that enforces cosine similarity between cross-ID and same-ID model patch embeddings at shallow DiT blocks (D=10), accelerating convergence by 6.7× and improving final quality.

## Results

On the TikTok test set, DirectAnimator achieves the best FID (25.87), SSIM (0.806), L1 error (2.12×10⁻⁴), and Face Identity Similarity (0.682), while remaining competitive on LPIPS (0.215), FVD (142.60), and FTS (0.730). On the more challenging Unseen test set (fast-paced dance motions), DirectAnimator outperforms all baselines across every metric, including FID (27.62), SSIM (0.708), FIS (0.661), and FVD (276.34). Notably, DirectAnimator requires only 4×H20 GPUs for 40K training steps, substantially less compute than StableAnimator (4×A100×33M steps) or MimicMotion (8×A100×6M steps).

## Baselines & Comparisons

DirectAnimator outperforms all seven baselines on the Unseen test set across every reported metric, with the largest margins over MagicAnimate (FID 27.62 vs. 42.72) and AnimateAnyone (FID 27.62 vs. 36.49). On TikTok, DirectAnimator leads in FID (25.87 vs. 28.03 for MimicMotion, the next best), SSIM (0.806 vs. 0.802 for Champ), L1 error (2.12 vs. 2.34 for DynamiCtrl), and FIS (0.682 vs. 0.662 for StableAnimator). Against the closest competitor StableAnimator, DirectAnimator improves FID by ~6 points and FIS by 0.02 on TikTok, while on Unseen it reduces FVD from 365.52 to 276.34 and raises SSIM from 0.603 to 0.708. Compared to the DiT-based UniAnimate-DiT, DirectAnimator achieves lower FVD (276.34 vs. 289.45) and higher SSIM (0.708 vs. 0.649) on Unseen, confirming that the Driving Cue Triplet and Same2X strategy provide advantages even over other transformer-based approaches. In terms of training efficiency, DirectAnimator (4×H20×40K steps) requires roughly 800× fewer steps than StableAnimator (4×A100×33M) and 150× fewer than MimicMotion (8×A100×6M), while matching or exceeding their quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

DirectAnimator departs from the dominant paradigm in Human Image Animation (HIA) of using intermediate pose representations (2D skeleton maps, DensePose, SMPL parameters) as driving signals, instead conditioning directly on raw driving video pixels processed into a structured Driving Cue Triplet (Pose, Face, Location cues). Architecturally, it builds on the CogVideoX1.5 DiT backbone and adopts the AdaLN-based conditioning strategy popularized by DynamiCtrl, while the Same2X training strategy adapts the representation alignment principle from REPA/REPA-E to the specific challenge of cross-ID vs. same-ID HIA training. The face cue design draws from X-Dyna's use of raw face crops, and the spatial alignment mechanism inherits from StableAnimator's global similarity transform, but applied to foreground masks rather than skeleton keypoints.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the pose estimation bottleneck, DirectAnimator addresses a persistent source of artifacts (front-back confusion, missing limbs, hand misplacement) that plagues skeleton-driven methods, and the Same2X strategy offers a generalizable recipe for stabilizing cross-ID training that could transfer to other conditional generation tasks. However, the method still requires an explicit Location Cue for spatial alignment, depends on pseudo data quality for cross-ID training, and remains sensitive to motion blur in driving videos. Open questions include whether the explicit alignment step can be learned implicitly, whether the approach scales to higher-resolution backbones, and how to improve hand animation fidelity without reintroducing specialized pose estimators.
