## Problem

RAG systems suffer from two fundamental inefficiencies: cross-query context overlap where a small fraction of text chunks dominates retrieval requests (10% of chunks satisfy 80% of queries), and uniform deep retrieval that over-provisions context regardless of query complexity (over 60% of queries need only minimal context). Existing KV-cache reuse methods either require exact prefix matching (yielding poor hit rates with variable RAG prompts) or discard cross-chunk attention (sacrificing accuracy), and none adapt retrieval depth per query.

## Input / Output

The system consumes a user query, a system prompt, and top-k retrieved text chunks (512 tokens each, retrieved via e5-base-v2 embeddings over a FAISS IVF index on Wikipedia). Rather than concatenating all k chunks at once, AdaCache processes them incrementally one chunk at a time during prefill, reusing cached KV states for previously seen chunks. The output is a standard autoregressive text response, with the key difference that the effective context length is determined dynamically per query rather than fixed at top-k.

## Method

AdaCache combines two complementary mechanisms. First, a cache-aware selective recomputation module builds a three-tier hierarchical cache (hard prefix, soft prefix, independent) informed by chunk-level attention analysis: early layers exhibit localized attention while deeper layers show attention-sink phenomena, revealing that only a sparse, layer-consistent subset of tokens carries significant cross-chunk attention. The recomputation ratio adapts to the cache match level—zero for exact prefix hits, α for soft prefix matches, and β (β>α) for independent chunk reuse—selecting only the highest cross-chunk-attention tokens for recomputation. Second, adaptive context augmentation (ACA) incrementally appends one retrieved chunk per iteration, computing a lightweight composite confidence score (normalized average KL divergence across the last l layers plus final-token entropy) after each addition, and terminates early when confidence exceeds a threshold, thereby avoiding unnecessary prefill computation for simple queries.

## Results

AdaCache achieves a 3.12× average (up to 6.02×) TTFT reduction compared to full recomputation while preserving nearly identical generation quality, and occasionally surpasses full recomputation in accuracy by avoiding contextual noise. Against the strongest caching baseline (CacheBlend), AdaCache delivers 1.32× average and up to 2.34× TTFT improvements with marginally superior accuracy. Across six datasets (MMLU, MMLU-Pro, SuperGPQA, TriviaQA, 2Wiki-MultihopQA, HotpotQA) and three models (Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B), AdaCache achieves 1.4×–5.0× TTFT reduction over state-of-the-art RAG caching systems. Context scalability is a key differentiator: at top-8 retrieval, AdaCache maintains 4.67× TTFT reduction over full recomputation, whereas Prefix Caching degrades to only 1.13×.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaCache is compared against three baselines: Full Recomputation, Prefix Cache (RAGCache via SGLang), and Selective Recomputation (CacheBlend). Against Full Recomputation, AdaCache wins decisively with 3.12× average TTFT reduction (up to 6.02×) while matching or slightly exceeding accuracy, since adaptive context augmentation avoids noise from irrelevant chunks. Against Prefix Cache (RAGCache), AdaCache achieves 2.69× average and up to 5.0× TTFT improvements, primarily because the hierarchical cache (hard prefix, soft prefix, independent) handles positional variations and longer contexts where exact prefix matching fails; Prefix Caching's TTFT advantage drops from 1.76× at top-2 to just 1.13× at top-8, while AdaCache's grows from 2.93× to 4.67×. Against CacheBlend, AdaCache delivers 1.32× average and up to 2.34× TTFT improvements with marginally better accuracy, attributed to attention-aware selective recomputation (using per-chunk recomputation ratios α and β instead of a uniform ratio) and adaptive context augmentation that skips non-contributory chunks. The ablation confirms both components contribute: ACA alone adds 1.65× speedup on top of Prefix Caching and 1.22× on top of CacheBlend, while the full AdaCache system achieves 1.76× over Prefix Caching and 1.23× over CacheBlend.

## Relation to Prior Work

AdaCache extends the selective-recomputation line initiated by CacheBlend (Yao et al., 2025), which introduced the idea of recomputing a small subset of high-deviation tokens to restore cross-chunk attention in independently cached RAG contexts. The key departure is a three-tier hierarchical cache (hard prefix, soft prefix, independent) motivated by an empirical observation of attention-sink phenomena in deeper transformer layers, replacing CacheBlend's uniform recomputation ratio with attention-aware, match-condition-dependent ratios. The adaptive context augmentation module has no direct predecessor in the RAG caching literature; prior systems (RAGCache, PromptCache, CacheBlend) all assume a fixed top-k retrieval depth, whereas AdaCache introduces a confidence-guided early-stopping mechanism that dynamically determines how many retrieved chunks are actually needed per query.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As RAG becomes the default deployment pattern for domain-specific LLM applications, the 1.4×–5.0× TTFT reductions demonstrated here translate directly into lower serving costs and better user experience at scale. The finding that over 60% of queries require only minimal context, and that AdaCache occasionally *outperforms* full recomputation in accuracy by avoiding contextual noise, challenges the implicit assumption that more retrieved context is always beneficial. Open questions include whether the attention-sink-based soft prefix cache generalizes to much larger models (70B+) and multi-GPU disaggregated serving, whether the confidence metric remains reliable on highly ambiguous or adversarial queries, and how the approach interacts with iterative or multi-turn RAG pipelines where context accumulates across turns.
