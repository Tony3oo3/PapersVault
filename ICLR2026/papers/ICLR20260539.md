## Problem

Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in language models is often unfaithful: the generated CoT may not reflect the model's actual decision process, and the model can bypass the CoT entirely when producing its answer because it still attends to the original question. No existing training method structurally prevents this architectural escape hatch, leaving the CoT as a potentially decorative rather than causally load-bearing component.

## Input / Output

The method takes a question (QA tasks) or a 200-token text passage (Wikipedia continuation) plus a task-specific CoT initialization prompt, and produces a bounded-length CoT of at most K tokens (e.g., 100 for GSM8K, 50 for ARC/SVAMP, 150 for MMLU/Arithmetic) followed by an answer or continuation. Critically, the answer is predicted from the CoT alone—the original question is removed from the context during answer generation, enforcing a strict A→B→C information flow. The CoT length cap creates a bandwidth bottleneck: when K < N (Wikipedia), the state cannot copy the target; when K ≥ N (QA), time-bounded complexity prevents the model from computing the answer during question-reading forward passes.

## Method

The core contribution is a Markovian Language Model (MLM) framework that structurally enforces the answer to be predicted from the CoT state alone, creating an autoencoder-style reasoning bottleneck where all information must flow through a bounded-length text channel. Training uses a GRPO-style policy gradient algorithm with three key components: (1) parallel sampling of B diverse CoTs per question with a frozen baseline CoT' for local reward subtraction, (2) within-batch advantage standardization (zero mean, unit variance) replacing exponential moving averages, and (3) actor-reward (chain-rule) gradients that backpropagate through the reward term ln π_θ(ans|CoT) in addition to the standard REINFORCE term, enabling simultaneous optimization of CoT generation and answer prediction. A KL penalty (β=0.1) toward the frozen pretrained CoT distribution discourages steganographic encodings, and LoRA (rank 8, α=16) fine-tuning preserves the pretrained language modeling basin. The discrete text bottleneck blocks direct backpropagation, necessitating this RL-based gradient estimation rather than standard supervised fine-tuning.

## Results

On QA tasks, Markovian training (Mkv) recovers most of the gains of a Non-Markovian GRPO variant while forcing the model to answer from the CoT alone: GSM8K 19.6% → 57.1%, ARC-Challenge 36.1% → 79.9%, Arithmetic 1.0% → 98.0%, MMLU 21.4% → 55.5%, SVAMP 18.0% → 42.3%. On average, the Markovian model is within ≈3–4 percentage points of the Non-Markovian variant, with the largest gap on MMLU (−13.2 pp) and the smallest on ARC-Challenge (+1.3 pp in favor of Markovian). Perturbation analyses confirm that Markovian models incur systematically larger log-probability drops under CoT corruption (e.g., +0.981 nats at 100% severity on Wikipedia; +25.0 pp accuracy drop on ARC), indicating stronger causal reliance on the CoT.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three settings: the untrained baseline, a Non-Markovian GRPO variant (same training recipe but the answer predictor can attend to the original question), and the proposed Markovian variant (answer predicted from CoT alone). The Markovian model substantially outperforms the untrained baseline on every task (e.g., +37.5 pp on GSM8K, +43.8 pp on ARC-Challenge, +97 pp on Arithmetic). Against the Non-Markovian variant, the Markovian model wins on ARC-Challenge (79.9% vs. 78.6%) and Arithmetic (98.0% vs. 97.0%), is nearly tied on SVAMP (42.3% vs. 43.3%), but trails on GSM8K (57.1% vs. 63.3%) and MMLU (55.5% vs. 68.7%). The average gap across all five datasets is roughly 3–4 percentage points, which the authors attribute to the information bottleneck. Perturbation sensitivity analyses further show that the Markovian model is consistently more fragile to CoT corruption than the Non-Markovian model (positive Δ across nearly all perturbation types and severities), confirming that the CoT is causally load-bearing rather than a post-hoc rationalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on GRPO-style policy gradient training (Shao et al., 2024) and chain-of-thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022; Nye et al., 2022), but departs from prior CoT faithfulness work (Lanham et al., 2023; Turpin et al., 2023; Paul et al., 2024) by imposing a structural Markovian constraint rather than relying on optimization-based incentives: the answer predictor is architecturally prevented from attending to the original question, creating a hard information bottleneck analogous to an autoencoder's latent layer. The Markovian state-space formulation draws on connections to S4, Mamba, and Kalman filters, while the actor-reward (chain-rule) gradient through the shared policy parameters is a training innovation not present in standard REINFORCE or GRPO. Unlike Lyu et al. (2023), which rewrites questions into formal languages, this work keeps the reasoning state in natural language to preserve cross-model interpretability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Markovian constraint provides a structural (not merely statistical) guarantee that the CoT is causally sufficient for the answer, addressing a core limitation of prior CoT methods where the model can bypass the reasoning chain. Cross-model transfer to GPT-2—a model too small to decode sophisticated steganography—offers compelling evidence that learned CoTs encode natural-language reasoning rather than model-specific artifacts. However, the paper explicitly does not claim full faithfulness (the model could compute during question-reading passes), and the performance gap on MMLU (55.5% vs. 68.7% Non-Markovian) suggests the bottleneck carries a real cost on knowledge-intensive tasks. Open questions include scaling to larger models, whether the approach generalizes to multi-turn or agentic reasoning, and whether human evaluators find the learned CoTs genuinely more interpretable.
