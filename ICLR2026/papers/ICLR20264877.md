## Problem

Traditional oversmoothing metrics such as Dirichlet energy and projection energy measure alignment with the dominant eigenspace of the message-passing matrix, but they only signal oversmoothing when their values converge exactly to zero, which requires infinite depth or very small weights. In realistic trained GNNs with moderate depth (10–24 layers), classification accuracy degrades sharply while these energy metrics remain roughly constant, rendering them unreliable indicators of the actual oversmoothing state.

## Input / Output

The method operates on the feature representation matrix X^(l) ∈ R^{N×d} extracted from the last hidden layer of a separately trained GNN, where N is the number of nodes and d is the hidden dimension (32 in experiments). It computes continuous rank relaxations—numerical rank (‖X‖²_F / ‖X‖²_2) and effective rank (exponential of the entropy of normalized singular values)—as scalar oversmoothing indicators that are scale-invariant and require no knowledge of the dominant eigenvector, unlike Dirichlet-type metrics.

## Method

The paper redefines oversmoothing as convergence toward a low-rank feature matrix rather than exact rank-1 alignment, and proposes continuous rank relaxations as the measurement tool. Theoretically, it proves via nonlinear Perron-Frobenius theory and Hilbert distance that the numerical rank converges to 1 for linear GNNs (Theorem 5.1) and for nonnegative nonlinear GNNs where the dominant eigenvector is also an eigenvector of the activation function (Theorem 5.3). It also provides a unifying eigenvector-based characterization (Theorem 3.2) that simplifies existing Dirichlet energy convergence proofs and reveals why those metrics fail when the dominant eigenspace shifts due to nonlinearities or biases. Empirically, across GCN and GAT architectures on seven datasets with independently trained depths from 2 to 24 layers, the effective and numerical rank correlate strongly (average Pearson r ≈ 0.91 and 0.84) with accuracy degradation, while Dirichlet energy and projection energy show weak or negative correlations.

## Results

The paper's central finding is that rank-based metrics correlate far more strongly with GNN accuracy degradation than energy-based metrics. Across 8 datasets and 2 architectures (GCN, GAT) at depths 2–24, the Effective Rank achieves an average Pearson correlation of 0.91 with accuracy, and the Numerical Rank achieves 0.84, while the best energy-based baseline (normalized E_Dir) reaches only 0.40 and MAD reaches just 0.16. Standard (unnormalized) E_Dir and E_Proj actually show negative average correlations (−0.72 and −0.76), meaning they often move in the wrong direction relative to performance. The accuracy ratio between 2-layer and 24-layer networks drops to as low as 0.09 (OGB-Arxiv GCN) and 0.19 (Cora GAT+Tanh), confirming severe oversmoothing that rank metrics capture but energy metrics miss.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three families of oversmoothing metrics against the proposed rank-based metrics (Erank, NumRank). The Dirichlet Energy E_Dir (Cai & Wang, 2020) in its standard form shows a negative average correlation (−0.72) with accuracy, meaning it often increases as performance drops — a clear failure. Its normalized variant improves to 0.40 but still lags far behind Erank (0.91) and NumRank (0.84). The Projection Energy E_Proj (Wu et al., 2023) performs even worse, with standard correlation at −0.76 and normalized at −0.45, indicating it is unreliable in both forms. MAD (Chen et al., 2020), which measures cosine similarity between neighbours without relying on a dominant eigenvector, achieves only 0.16 average correlation, essentially uninformative. The rank-based metrics win decisively on every dataset-architecture pair where accuracy degrades significantly (e.g., Cora GCN: Erank 0.97 vs. E_Dir −0.79; OGB-Arxiv GCN: Erank 0.97 vs. E_Dir 0.77), and they remain robust even when energy metrics flip sign or stay flat while accuracy collapses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the extensive oversmoothing literature that uses Dirichlet energy and projection energy as primary quantitative metrics (Cai & Wang 2020; Wu et al. 2023; Rusch et al. 2023a), and on the rank-collapse observations of Guo et al. (2023b) and Roth & Liebig (2023). It departs from this lineage by arguing that energy-based metrics are fundamentally unreliable in realistic trained networks, and instead elevates continuous rank relaxations (numerical rank, effective rank) to the primary oversmoothing measure. The theoretical analysis unifies prior convergence results via a novel 'nonlinear activation eigenvector' framework and extends them using nonlinear Perron-Frobenius theory and Hilbert distance to prove rank convergence for broad GNN families.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

If Dirichlet-like metrics fail to capture oversmoothing in the shallow-to-moderate depth regime where performance actually degrades, then a substantial body of literature citing these metrics as evidence for or against oversmoothing may need re-examination. The paper's open question—whether rank-based metrics can inform the design of oversmoothing-mitigating architectures (e.g., residual gating)—is practically significant for GNN practitioners. A noted limitation is that a high rank does not guarantee good performance (e.g., random features have high rank but no structure), so rank is a necessary but not sufficient diagnostic. The work is scoped to message-passing GNNs and node classification; whether the rank-based perspective transfers to other graph tasks or non-message-passing architectures remains open.
