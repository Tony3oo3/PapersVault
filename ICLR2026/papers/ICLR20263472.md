## Problem

Large reasoning models (LRMs) frequently produce final answers that contradict their own chain-of-thought traces, yet the relative contributions of deliberate reasoning versus direct memory retrieval to answer generation remain uncharacterized. No prior work has systematically isolated and quantified how these two mechanisms compete, identified which factors (domain, model scale, training paradigm) govern their dominance, or proposed a mechanism to control their balance during fine-tuning.

## Input / Output

The perturbation framework takes a text prompt x, the model's original CoT trace z, and a misleading cue c (e.g., 'A reliable expert suggests the answer is B') as input, and produces a perturbed final answer y′ whose agreement with the cue target y_r or the poisoned memory target y_t yields the R-PSR and T-PSR metrics. Retrieval perturbation is implemented via LoRA-based SFT (r=64, 8 epochs, batch size 2) to memorize incorrect question-answer associations, while reasoning perturbation hijacks the CoT by appending the cue and re-running generation. The FARL method takes a base distilled LRM and a reasoning-intensive training set (MMLU Math&Logic, 1,353 samples) and outputs a reasoning-dominant model with suppressed retrieval shortcuts, evaluated on in-domain and out-of-domain accuracy, CoT robustness, and reasoning-graph topology metrics.

## Method

The paper introduces a joint perturbation framework that simultaneously attacks both answer-generation pathways: CoT hijacking (appending misleading cues to reasoning traces) and memory poisoning (SFT-based memorization of incorrect answers), then measures their independent and interactive effects via R-PSR and T-PSR across domains, model scales, and training methods. Mechanistic analysis via attention-head probing (logistic regression AUC) and causal activation patching identifies mid-layer heads (layers 12–16 for R1-Llama-8B) that arbitrate between reasoning and retrieval, while step-wise logit tracking reveals dynamic competition at each reasoning step. To control this balance, FARL integrates Negative Preference Optimization (NPO) unlearning directly into the GRPO RL loop: after each GRPO policy update, an NPO loss (Equation 3) is applied to suppress the model's memorized answer associations, purifying the reward signal so that only genuinely reasoned correct answers receive positive advantage, preventing the retrieval shortcut from inflating batch-mean rewards and diluting the learning signal for true reasoning.

## Results

On R1-Llama-8B (Math&Logic domain of MMLU), FARL reduces the Reasoning Perturbation Success Rate (R-PSR) by 47.8% and the Retrieval Perturbation Success Rate (T-PSR) by 38.5% relative to the base model, indicating substantially stronger reasoning-dominant behavior. FARL achieves 89.1% in-domain accuracy (22.8% gain over base) and 75.7% out-of-domain accuracy (5.8% gain over base), outperforming both SFT (78.7% / 73.2%) and standard GRPO (86.9% / 74.5%). On CoT quality metrics, FARL yields an 84.0% improvement in the small-world index over the base model, the largest gain among all methods tested.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FARL is compared against three baselines: the base distilled model (R1-Llama-8B), SFT with correct answers, and standard RL via GRPO (Shao et al., 2024). Against GRPO, FARL further reduces R-PSR from 0.259 to 0.197 (23.9% additional reduction) and T-PSR from 0.262 to 0.234 (10.7% additional reduction), while improving in-domain accuracy from 86.9% to 89.1% and out-of-domain accuracy from 74.5% to 75.7%. Against SFT, FARL dominates on every metric: SFT actually increases R-PSR (0.392 vs. 0.378 base) and produces shorter, less generalizable CoTs (MTL drops from 1538 to 1382), whereas FARL produces longer, higher-quality reasoning traces. The additional results on R1-Qwen-7B (Table 2) confirm the same pattern: FARL reduces overall perturbation rate by 60.4% and achieves 20.1% in-domain and 5.0% out-of-domain accuracy gains, again surpassing both SFT and GRPO. Training time is comparable between FARL and GRPO (~4.5 h vs. ~4.1 h), so the unlearning step adds minimal overhead.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate research threads—CoT faithfulness/reasoning-answer disconnects (Turpin et al., 2023; Lanham et al., 2023; Chua & Evans, 2025) and internal factual retrieval mechanisms (Geva et al., 2023; Meng et al., 2022; Ortu et al., 2024)—into a single dual-mechanism framework where deliberate reasoning and memory retrieval are treated as competing pathways that jointly determine the final answer. Methodologically, it builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for RL-based reasoning elicitation and NPO (Zhang et al., 2024) for unlearning, combining them into the FARL pipeline. The key departure from prior work is the controlled joint-perturbation experimental design (simultaneously poisoning memory via SFT and injecting misleading CoT cues) that allows independent and interactive measurement of each pathway's contribution, rather than studying them in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a practical vulnerability in current reasoning fine-tuning: distilled models can exploit retrieval as a reward-hacking shortcut during RL, diluting the learning signal and undermining genuine reasoning development. FARL offers a concrete, low-overhead remedy (iterative NPO unlearning interleaved with GRPO) that yields meaningful gains in CoT robustness (47.8%) and out-of-domain accuracy (5.8%), making it directly actionable for teams post-training reasoning models. Key limitations include the resulting longer reasoning traces (MTL increases), evaluation restricted to 1B–32B open-weight models, and the use of multiple-choice QA as the primary testbed. Open questions include whether the mid-layer arbitration heads identified here generalize to frontier-scale models, and how to achieve reasoning-dominant behavior without the latency cost of longer CoTs.
