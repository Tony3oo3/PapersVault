## Problem

Classical optimal transport enforces full-mass preservation, while its relaxations—partial OT (fixed mass budget) and unbalanced OT (soft penalty coefficients)—still require the user to specify how much mass to transport or how strongly to penalize marginal violations. This paper asks whether the transported mass can be determined adaptively from the problem's own geometric structure, without any user-defined budget or penalty hyperparameter.

## Input / Output

The method takes labeled source-domain RGB images and unlabeled target-domain RGB images (resized to 256×256, center-cropped to 224×224) and produces a learned ResNet-50 feature extractor plus a fully-connected classifier that assigns class labels to target samples. The ground cost matrix is mixed-sign, combining a feature-wise L2 distance in the latent space with a label-wise term that uses pseudo-labels (tanh of classifier output on target features), so the cost naturally encodes both geometric similarity and class alignment.

## Method

Elastic Optimal Transport (ELOT) replaces the equality marginal constraints of Kantorovich OT with inequality constraints (γ1 ≤ μ, γᵀ1 ≤ ν) and permits a mixed-sign ground cost matrix. The key mechanism is that positive-cost entries automatically receive zero transport mass, while negative-cost entries drive mass allocation until at least one marginal constraint is saturated (Theorem 2), so the total transported mass is self-determined by the cost geometry rather than a user-specified budget. Theorem 1 proves ELOT is equivalent to a standard equality-constrained OT problem on an augmented (n+1)×(m+1) cost matrix with a single non-negative slack parameter σ, making it solvable by off-the-shelf algorithms such as Sinkhorn-Knopp with entropy regularization. For domain adaptation, training alternates between fixing the extractor/classifier to solve the OT subproblem and fixing the transport plan to update network parameters via SGD, requiring only one cost-structure hyperparameter (β) compared to the extra mass-budget or penalty coefficients needed by partial and unbalanced OT baselines.

## Results

On the large-scale VisDA dataset (152K source / 55K target images, 12 classes), ELOT achieves 76.32% accuracy, outperforming the strongest baseline m-POT (73.59%) by 2.73 points and JUMBOT (72.50%) by 3.82 points. On Office-31, ELOT reaches 90.5% average accuracy across 6 adaptation tasks, surpassing ALDA (88.7%) and m-POT (88.3%). On Office-Home, ELOT attains 71.75% average accuracy over 12 tasks, beating m-POT (70.34%) and JUMBOT (70.17%). For partial domain adaptation on DomainNet (345 classes), ELOT achieves 71.4% average accuracy, outperforming the recent methods ARPM (68.8%) and STCPDA (66.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ELOT outperforms all 13 baselines across every dataset and task setting. The closest competitor is m-POT (partial OT), which ELOT beats by 2.73 points on VisDA, 2.2 points on Office-31, 1.41 points on Office-Home, 1.21 points on Office-Home partial DA, and 2.6 points on DomainNet. Among OT-based methods, JUMBOT (unbalanced OT) and DeepJDOT (Kantorovich OT) are consistently outperformed, with ELOT gaining 3.82 and 8.32 points over them respectively on VisDA. Non-OT baselines (DANN, CDAN, ALDA, CaCo) trail ELOT by 5–9 points on VisDA and 2–8 points on Office-31. For partial domain adaptation, ELOT surpasses the strongest non-OT method BA3US by 3.99 points on Office-Home and 30.4 points on DomainNet, and outperforms the recent ARPM by 2.6 points on DomainNet. Notably, ELOT achieves these gains without requiring user-specified mass budgets (as in m-POT) or soft-penalty coefficients (as in JUMBOT), relying solely on the mixed-sign cost structure for adaptive mass transport.

## Relation to Prior Work

ELOT extends the classical optimal transport lineage (Kantorovich 1942) by combining the inequality-constraint structure of partial optimal transport (Caffarelli & McCann, 2010) with a mixed-sign ground cost matrix, eliminating the need for a user-specified mass budget (as in POT) or soft-penalty coefficients (as in unbalanced OT, Liero et al. 2017). The key conceptual departure is that adaptive-mass transport emerges naturally from the interplay of inequality marginal constraints and negative-cost entries, rather than being imposed through an additional hyperparameter. The paper also builds on entropy-regularized OT (Cuturi, 2013) for efficient computation via Sinkhorn-Knopp, and positions its domain adaptation instantiation alongside JUMBOT and m-POT as the closest OT-based competitors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ELOT offers a principled, parameter-light alternative to POT and UOT for distribution matching in the presence of noise and outliers, with a clean theoretical characterization (Theorem 2) explaining exactly when and how much mass is transported. The practical advantage of requiring only one cost-structure hyperparameter (β) versus multiple trade-off coefficients in POT/UOT is meaningful for practitioners. Open questions include: the mixed-sign cost requirement limits applicability to problems where natural negative costs exist; the method is demonstrated only on image domain adaptation, leaving open whether the adaptive-mass property transfers to other modalities or tasks; and the theoretical analysis is restricted to the discrete setting, with continuous-space guarantees left implicit.
