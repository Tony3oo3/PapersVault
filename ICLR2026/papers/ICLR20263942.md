## Problem

Monocular human performance capture from a single RGB stream provides only partial observations at any moment, leaving unobserved body regions without direct visual evidence. Per-frame methods either regress toward training-distribution averages or hallucinate details unrelated to prior observations, while temporal deterministic methods (e.g., NHP) suppress high-frequency details to avoid misalignment penalties, producing blurry outputs. The paper addresses how to leverage accumulated temporal history to faithfully reconstruct unobserved regions while preserving sharp, high-fidelity details.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a sequential monocular RGB video stream along with pre-fitted SMPL-X templates and camera parameters for both the input and target novel views. At inference, frames are processed sequentially with K=1 (consecutive frames) and T=10 diffusion steps, reflecting a live-streaming setting. The output is a rendered novel-view image of the current live frame, synthesized in a feed-forward manner without per-subject optimization.

## Method

GenFusion maintains a vertex-aligned canonical feature space (M×L) that is progressively updated with each incoming frame via visibility-weighted fusion, where ResNet-18 multi-scale features are projected onto SMPL-X vertices and accumulated over time. The canonical features are warped to the live pose using SMPL-X 3D coordinates and densified into a 2D feature image via barycentric interpolation, forming a context representation. A pre-trained Stable Diffusion denoiser then synthesizes the novel view conditioned on both this canonical context and a live-deformation-aware encoding of the current frame, using a diffusion-based probabilistic regression objective rather than pixel-wise MSE. This probabilistic formulation is the key differentiator: it tolerates pose and geometry misalignments between past and current observations, enabling the model to transfer semantically relevant cues (textures, patterns) from the canonical space without being penalized for sub-pixel shifts, while also allowing plausible synthesis in regions with no prior observations.

## Results

On the in-domain 4D-Dress test set, GenFusion achieves the best PSNR (25.07 dB), the lowest LPIPS-VGG (62.97×10⁻³), and the lowest FVD (176.7) among all feed-forward baselines. On the cross-dataset MVHumanNet evaluation, it attains the best LPIPS-VGG (87.85×10⁻³) and FVD (436.9), though it trails NHP slightly on PSNR (21.25 vs. 22.25 dB). The largest margins are on temporal consistency: FVD is 44% lower than the next-best probabilistic method (Champ, 254.5) and 2.6× lower than the temporal deterministic method NHP (630.0) on 4D-Dress.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GenFusion outperforms all six feed-forward baselines on both perceptual (LPIPS-VGG) and temporal-consistency (FVD) metrics on 4D-Dress, with the largest gap over NHP (96.26 → 62.97 LPIPS-VGG; 630.0 → 176.7 FVD) and Champ (98.61 → 62.97; 254.5 → 176.7). On PSNR, it edges out GHG (25.07 vs. 24.50) and NHP (25.07 vs. 24.72) by a narrow margin, while per-frame probabilistic Champ lags significantly (19.37). On the cross-dataset MVHumanNet test, GenFusion beats NHP on LPIPS-VGG (87.85 vs. 131.91) and FVD (436.9 vs. 1321.4) but concedes PSNR (21.25 vs. 22.25), and beats Champ on LPIPS-VGG (87.85 vs. 97.61) and FVD (436.9 vs. 674.1) while matching on PSNR (21.25 vs. 21.06). The per-subject optimization method GauHuman, despite its subject-specific fitting, is outperformed on all three metrics, confirming the advantage of the feed-forward design. SIFU, AniGS, and LHM are compared only qualitatively (no ground-truth rendering targets), with the paper noting they generate details inconsistent with prior observations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a specific niche at the intersection of three existing threads: feed-forward per-frame human reconstruction (SHERF, GHG, Pifu), temporal deterministic methods that accumulate history but suffer from blur (NHP), and per-frame probabilistic/diffusion-based synthesis that hallucinates details without temporal grounding (Champ, AniGS, LHM). Its core positioning is that neither temporal context nor probabilistic rendering alone suffices: the progressive canonical space provides the missing temporal memory that per-frame methods lack, while the diffusion-based objective resolves the misalignment penalties that cause deterministic temporal methods to suppress high-frequency detail. The authors explicitly frame both the canonical space updating and the use of off-the-shelf Stable Diffusion as enabling components rather than primary contributions, with the combination being the novel architectural choice.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically important setting—live, feed-forward monocular performance capture without per-subject optimization—and the FVD results (176.7 vs. 254.5 for the best probabilistic baseline and 630 for the best temporal baseline) demonstrate that temporal alignment with past observations is a substantially harder axis than frame-level quality, which PSNR alone fails to capture. Limitations include dependence on SMPL-X fitting quality, a relatively simple ResNet-18 feature extractor, and a per-subject canonical space that does not transfer across identities. Open questions include how the canonical space degrades over very long sequences, whether the approach extends to multi-subject scenes, and whether the 10-step diffusion inference can be further accelerated for true real-time use.
