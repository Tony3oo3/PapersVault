## Problem

Existing nonstationary multi-output GP kernels, particularly MOHSM, inherit inflexibility and over-parameterization from the restrictive spectral-kernel duality (Kakihara's theorem), which enforces PSD constraints on diagonal spectral entries and exchangeability conditions that force a two-level mixture structure. This limits the ability to capture strong input-dependent nonstationarity while requiring more parameters than necessary, making optimization difficult and empirical performance suboptimal.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a set of N input points in R^D (typically time or spatial coordinates) paired with V-dimensional observed outputs, and produces a joint Gaussian posterior over all V outputs at held-out test points, yielding per-output posterior means and 95% confidence intervals. For missing-data tasks, the same framework reconstructs large contiguous imputation segments or isolated interpolation points by conditioning the MOGP on partially observed data. The kernel itself is a V×V matrix-valued function k_ij(x₁,x₂) whose entries are computed in closed form from Q mixture components, with the full NV×NV covariance matrix assembled as a block structure.

## Method

The paper first establishes an Advanced Kakihara Theorem that removes the PSD and exchangeability constraints on the spectral bimeasure, requiring only Hermitian symmetry P_ij(ω₁,ω₂) = P̄_ji(ω₁,ω₂), thereby enabling fully flexible matrix-valued spectral densities. To avoid the resulting O(DV²Q) parameter cost, the MO-LRN kernel factorizes the spectral density as P_ij = r_i^H r_j, where each latent vector r_i ∈ R^Q has components modeled as bivariate Gaussian mixtures with independent per-output parameters (weights, means, diagonal covariances, and a scalar correlation coefficient). Applying the relaxed duality to the symmetrized spectral density yields a closed-form kernel with four cosine-exponential terms per mixture component, reducing parameters to O(DVQ). Training proceeds by maximizing the marginal log-likelihood of the observed multi-output data with respect to all hyperparameters using the Adam optimizer.

## Results

On the ETT dataset (7 outputs), MO-LRN achieves the best overall MAE of 0.201±0.006, NMAE of 0.232±0.007, RMSE of 0.295±0.008, and NLPD of 0.166±0.031, outperforming the second-best LMC-NGSM (MAE 0.256, NMAE 0.296, RMSE 0.350, NLPD 0.382) and the prior nonstationary MOGP kernel MOHSM (MAE 0.325, NMAE 0.375, RMSE 0.440, NLPD 0.792). On the Beijing Air Quality dataset, MO-LRN attains the lowest overall MAE for both interpolation (0.178) and imputation (0.351). Across additional real-world benchmarks (GONU, EEG, Currency, Bramble), MO-LRN consistently achieves the best overall NMAE, e.g., 0.01467 on GONU and 0.464 on EEG.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MO-LRN outperforms all five baselines across every metric on every dataset evaluated. Against the closest competitor LMC-NGSM (which uses the NG-SM kernel within an LMC framework), MO-LRN reduces overall MAE by ~21% on ETT (0.201 vs 0.256) and NMAE by ~22% (0.232 vs 0.296), with the gap widening on NLPD (0.166 vs 0.382). The prior nonstationary MOGP kernel MOHSM, despite having more parameters (O(PDQV) vs O(DQV)), underperforms LMC-NGSM in practice due to optimization difficulty from its over-parameterized two-level spectral mixture. Stationary kernels (MOSM, LMC-SM, CONV) are consistently worst, confirming that the ETT and air-quality data contain strong nonstationary patterns. On the air quality imputation task, MO-LRN achieves overall MAE 0.351 vs 0.372 (LMC-NGSM) and 0.485 (MOHSM); on interpolation, 0.178 vs 0.250 and 0.301 respectively. On the GONU financial dataset, MO-LRN's overall NMAE of 0.01467 beats LMC-NGSM (0.01654) and MOSM (0.02485), while MOHSM degrades severely (0.06059), and on the 8-channel EEG dataset MO-LRN (0.464) substantially outperforms LMC-NGSM (0.611) and MOSM (0.731).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the NG-SM kernel's design philosophy (dense bivariate Gaussian mixture spectral densities mapped to kernels via Bochner-type duality) from single-output to multi-output GPs, while simultaneously relaxing the structural constraints of Kakihara's theorem that previously forced MOHSM into an over-parameterized two-level mixture with restricted spectral flexibility. The low-rank factorization P_ij = r_i^H r_j, borrowed from latent factor models, is the key architectural departure that reduces parameter growth from quadratic to linear in the number of outputs. The paper explicitly positions itself against MOHSM (Altamirano and Tobar, 2022), demonstrating that its spectral density is neither dense nor efficient, and against LMC-based approaches that cannot capture input-dependent cross-output interactions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Advanced Kakihara Theorem is a genuine theoretical contribution that removes two restrictive conditions (PSD on diagonal spectral entries and exchangeability within Hermitian symmetry), opening the door to fully flexible matrix-valued spectral densities for MOGPs. Practically, MO-LRN achieves consistent SOTA across regression, interpolation, and imputation on six diverse datasets with fewer parameters and faster training than MOHSM, making nonstationary MOGP modeling more accessible. Open questions include scalability to very large datasets (the authors note inducing points and random Fourier features as promising but unresolved directions) and whether the low-rank approximation, which sacrifices some off-diagonal spectral flexibility, becomes a bottleneck for highly heterogeneous multi-output problems.
