## Problem

Existing EEG foundation models either discretize signals by simple segmentation without learning a vocabulary, or use neural tokenizers (e.g., LaBraM) that serve only as training objectives and are discarded at inference. No prior work learns a reusable, discrete token vocabulary grounded in time-frequency motifs from single-channel EEG that can be directly fed as input to downstream foundation models, leaving a gap in both representation quality and cross-device generalizability.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261256/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The tokenizer consumes single-channel EEG signals resampled to 200 Hz, segmented into 1-second windows with 0.5-second overlap, and simultaneously processes both the raw time-domain patches and their STFT magnitude spectrograms (200 frequency bins, Hann window) through a dual-path encoder. It outputs a sequence of N discrete tokens per channel drawn from a learned codebook of 8192 entries (64-dim embeddings), with no positional encoding applied. For multi-channel recordings, each channel is tokenized independently, and the resulting token sequences are flattened and fed into a downstream transformer that produces class labels for tasks like event classification, seizure detection, or sleep staging.

## Method

TFM-Tokenizer employs a dual-path architecture that jointly encodes frequency and temporal features: a Localized Spectral Window Encoder divides each spectrogram window into non-overlapping frequency patches, models cross-frequency dependencies via a frequency transformer, and aggregates them through a gated mechanism that suppresses irrelevant bands; a parallel Temporal Encoder projects raw EEG patches into embeddings. The two paths are concatenated and processed by a temporal transformer (no positional encoding, since EEG is non-stationary), and the fused embeddings are quantized via a VQ-VAE codebook. Training uses a joint frequency-band and temporal masking strategy with symmetric masking for augmentation, optimizing a combined masked-spectrogram reconstruction loss and VQ commitment loss. The frozen tokenizer is model-agnostic and plugs into any downstream backbone (e.g., BIOT, LaBraM, or a lightweight 0.7M-parameter linear-attention transformer pretrained with masked token prediction), enabling consistent performance gains across architectures and cross-device transfer to non-standard formats like ear-EEG.

## Results

On TUEV (six-class EEG event classification) with multi-dataset pretraining, TFM-Tokenizer achieves 0.6189 Cohen's Kappa, a ~11% improvement over the next-best baseline CBraMod (0.5588), while using only 1.9M parameters (vs. 4M for CBraMod and 5.8M for LaBraM). On IIIC Seizure (multi-dataset), it reaches 0.4979 Cohen's Kappa, a 36% gain over LaBraM (0.3658). On the out-of-domain EESM23 ear-EEG sleep staging task, TFM-Tokenizer achieves 0.3883 Cohen's Kappa, outperforming BIOT (0.3406) and LaBraM (0.3322) by 14% and 17% respectively, using only ~8K labeled samples for fine-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TFM-Tokenizer (1.9M params) outperforms all baselines across both single- and multi-dataset settings on TUEV and TUAB. Against the strongest multi-dataset baseline CBraMod (4M), it gains ~11% Cohen's Kappa on TUEV (0.6189 vs. 0.5588) and ~3% on IIIC Seizure (0.4979 vs. 0.4792), while CBraMod collapses on TUAB (0.5000 balanced accuracy). Compared to LaBraM (5.8M), TFM-Tokenizer achieves a 36% Cohen's Kappa improvement on IIIC Seizure multi-dataset (0.4979 vs. 0.3658) and 5% on TUEV (0.6189 vs. 0.5175). Against BIOT (3.2M), it gains 5% Cohen's Kappa on TUEV single-dataset (0.5337 vs. 0.4890) and ~11% on multi-dataset (0.6189 vs. 0.5273). The 254M-parameter NeuroLM-B is substantially outperformed on TUEV multi-dataset (0.6189 vs. 0.4285 Cohen's Kappa), demonstrating that the tokenization approach captures more informative representations than scaling model size alone. On the out-of-domain EESM23 ear-EEG task, TFM-Tokenizer beats both BIOT and LaBraM by 14–17% in Cohen's Kappa, confirming cross-device generalizability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the VQ-VAE discrete codebook mechanism for vocabulary learning and on LaBraM's masked EEG modeling objective with symmetric masking, but departs fundamentally from how existing EEG foundation models handle tokenization. While BIOT, EEGPT, and LaBraM simply segment continuous EEG into fixed-duration patches (treating tokenization as discretization rather than representation learning), and LaBraM's neural tokenizer serves only as a training objective discarded at inference, TFM-Tokenizer learns a reusable discrete vocabulary of time-frequency motifs from single-channel signals. It also extends prior EEG motif learning, which was restricted to the temporal domain, by introducing a Localized Spectral Window Encoder with gated frequency-band aggregation to capture cross-frequency dependencies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The plug-and-play, model-agnostic design means practitioners can drop TFM-Tokenizer into existing foundation models (BIOT, LaBraM) without architectural changes, and the single-channel operation removes dependence on the 10-20 system, opening the door to ear-EEG, wearable, and non-standard electrode configurations. Key limitations include the fixed windowing that can split large temporal patterns across token boundaries, the preliminary nature of the Jaccard-based retrieval evaluation, and the relatively small codebook (8192) used for fair comparison with LaBraM. Open questions include whether the approach scales to much larger pretraining corpora, whether the single-channel assumption degrades for tasks requiring tight spatial coupling, and how to optimally select window parameters for diverse clinical applications.
