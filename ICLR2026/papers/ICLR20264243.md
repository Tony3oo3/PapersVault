## Problem

Existing parametric knowledge augmentation methods like KBLaM suffer from two critical limitations that prevent real-world deployment at scale: (1) training data constructed from fixed schemas lacks query diversity, leading to poor out-of-distribution generalization, and (2) linear time and memory complexity in the number of knowledge base entries makes billion-scale KG integration prohibitively expensive (over 40GB VRAM for just 100K triples). Non-parametric RAG alternatives face their own latency and retriever-quality bottlenecks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural-language text query and a knowledge graph of up to 1 billion triples (h, r, t) as input. Each triple is converted via KG2KV into Q-K-V strings, then encoded by a sentence encoder (all-MiniLM-L6-v2 with D_E=384 or text-embedding-3-large with D_E=3072) into base key-value embeddings that are projected into the LLM's attention space. The output is a generated text answer grounded in the KG, evaluated via GPT-4o relevance scoring, plus per-triple knowledge grounding attention scores extracted at the 15th attention layer. The entire inference pipeline for 1B triples fits within ~20GB VRAM on a single GPU.

## Method

AtlasKV introduces two complementary designs. KG2KV naturally decomposes each KG triple into Q-K-V data by masking the head or tail entity (becoming the value) and rewriting the relation into a position-appropriate noun (becoming part of the key), yielding far more diverse enquiry attributes (7.864% vs 0.003% for synthetic methods) at lower token cost. HiKVP organizes all key embeddings into a 3-layer hierarchy via UAP dimensionality reduction and GMM clustering (cluster size S=⌈M^(1/3)⌉ per layer), then performs top-k pruning at each layer during inference—selecting k_R=128 root keys, k_I=64 inter-layer keys, and k_L=16 leaf keys—reducing complexity from O(M) to O(M^(1/3)). The attention mechanism extends KBLaM's rectangular attention by computing separate softmax-weighted outputs for the KG and sequence parts with dynamically computed mixing weights λ_kg and λ_seq. Only KG-specific query heads W̃_Q and projection heads W̃_K, W̃_V are trained (initialized from the LLM's existing W_Q and randomly, respectively) using the standard autoregressive objective over just 20K samples for 3K steps, with knowledge injected every 3 attention layers of LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct.

## Results

On the three OOD evaluation datasets, AtlasKV (with HiKVP, top-k=128-64-16) achieves substantially higher knowledge grounding accuracy than KBLaM. On ATLAS-Pes2o-QKV with 10² triples, AtlasKV reaches 87.3% ACC@1 vs. KBLaM's 16.4% (+61.8); on ATLAS-CC-QKV with 10² triples, AtlasKV reaches 89.1% ACC@1 vs. KBLaM's 21.8% (+65.5). AtlasKV requires only 3K training steps (vs. 20K for KBLaM) and less than 20GB VRAM to handle 1B triples, whereas KBLaM needs over 40GB VRAM for just 100K triples. GPT-4o-scored answer relevance also favors AtlasKV over KBLaM on both harder datasets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole parametric baseline is KBLaM (Wang et al., 2024), which uses rectangular attention to inject key-value pairs into LLM attention layers. AtlasKV decisively outperforms KBLaM on all three OOD datasets, with the largest gaps on the harder ATLAS-Pes2o-QKV and ATLAS-CC-QKV benchmarks where KBLaM's synthetic training data (limited to a small set of fixed enquiry attributes) fails to generalize. For example, at 10² triples on ATLAS-CC-QKV, AtlasKV achieves 89.1% ACC@1 versus KBLaM's 23.6%, a 65.5-point margin. On the simpler Enron dataset the gap is smaller (16.4 points at 10² triples) because KBLaM's training data happens to share enquiry attributes with Enron. AtlasKV also requires only 3K training steps versus KBLaM's 20K, and its HiKVP mechanism reduces inference VRAM from linear in KG size (KBLaM) to sub-linear (cube-root), enabling 1B-triple KGs in under 20GB VRAM where KBLaM already exceeds 40GB at 100K triples.

## Relation to Prior Work

AtlasKV builds directly on KBLaM's rectangular attention paradigm, which injects external knowledge as key-value pairs into LLM self-attention layers, but addresses two critical scalability and data-quality limitations of that framework. The KG2KV component repurposes the structural observation that KG triples (h, r, t) naturally decompose into query-key-value strings mirroring attention's Q-K-V vectors, replacing KBLaM's synthetic training data with naturally diverse data derived from the KG itself. The HiKVP component draws on hierarchical clustering ideas from Raptor and similar tree-organized retrieval systems, but applies them to the parametric attention setting to reduce rectangular attention's linear complexity in KB size to sub-linear (cubic-root) complexity via a three-level top-k pruning cascade.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: integrating 1B KG triples into an 8B LLM under 20GB VRAM (vs. >40GB for KBLaM at just 100K triples) makes parametric KG augmentation feasible on consumer-grade hardware, removing the need for external retrievers and long-context priors that dominate RAG inference latency. However, the method flattens graph structure by treating each triple independently, which blocks multi-hop reasoning over the KG, and the HiKVP process introduces non-trivial CPU-GPU I/O latency (up to ~4s at 10^4 triples) that could limit real-time deployment. Open questions include whether training separate attention heads at different HiKVP levels could recover both fuzzy and precise retrieval, and whether the sub-linear pruning can be extended to preserve relational structure for compositional reasoning.
