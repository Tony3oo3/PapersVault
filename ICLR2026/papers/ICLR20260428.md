## Problem

Extending PPO to expressive generative policies such as continuous normalizing flows (CNFs) is challenging because computing importance ratios requires simulating full ODE trajectories with path-wise backpropagation, which is computationally expensive and numerically unstable. Additionally, standard entropy regularization is intractable for flow-based policies since action log-likelihoods cannot be evaluated in closed form, leading to mode collapse in practice.

## Input / Output

The policy network takes a state observation s and a latent variable z ~ N(0, I) as input, and outputs a time-dependent velocity field v_t(x; s, θ) parameterized by an MLP with Fourier time embeddings and linear observation embeddings. Actions are produced by integrating the ODE dφ/dt = v_t(φ; s) from t=0 to t=1 (using 12-step RK2) and adding learnable Gaussian noise n ~ N(0, σ²). During training, the ODE is only simulated for the reference policy to obtain φ̂_1; the updated policy's contribution enters solely through single-step velocity evaluations at randomly sampled interpolation points, keeping per-iteration cost within ~50% of standard PPO.

## Method

PolicyFlow approximates the PPO importance ratio by replacing the exact terminal shift δ_φ₁ with the velocity field variation δ_{v_t}(x_t; s) = v_t(x_t; s, θ) − v̂_t(x_t; s) evaluated along a linear interpolation path x_t = (1−t)z + t·φ̂₁(z; s), where t is sampled uniformly from [0,1]. This avoids simulating the updated policy's ODE during training, and a theoretical analysis shows the induced log-likelihood error is O(ε), naturally controlled by PPO's clipping range. To prevent mode collapse without computing intractable flow entropies, the paper introduces a Brownian regularizer that penalizes the misalignment η_t = (1−t)v_t(x_t; s, θ) − (x_t − t·v̂_t(x_t; s)) between the learned velocity field and the negative score of the reference flow (recovered via the rectified-flow identity), effectively driving the policy toward entropy-increasing, heat-equation-like dynamics. The full training objective combines the clipped surrogate with this Brownian term and a standard Gaussian noise entropy term, optimized with GAE-based advantages in a standard on-policy PPO loop.

## Results

On the IsaacLab benchmarks, PolicyFlow achieves terminal episodic rewards that match or exceed PPO across all eight tasks, with statistically significant gains on Navigation (4.2 vs. 3.5, p=0.0027) and G1 (30.0 vs. 25.4, p=0.00026). On MuJoCo Playground, PolicyFlow achieves performance comparable to or exceeding FPO in most environments and outperforms DPPO, while also converging faster than baselines. Per-iteration training time increases by less than 50% over PPO for most IsaacLab environments (e.g., 57.7 ms vs. 43.0 ms on Lift-Cube), and remains below 2× PPO even with 8× larger embedding dimensions.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against PPO (Gaussian policy), PolicyFlow matches or slightly exceeds performance on most IsaacLab tasks, with clear wins on Navigation (+0.7 reward, p=0.0027) and G1 (+4.6 reward, p=0.00026), while showing a small deficit on H1 (−2.0, p=0.0069). On MuJoCo Playground, PolicyFlow achieves comparable or superior performance to FPO (flow-matching policy gradients) in most environments and outperforms DPPO (diffusion policy optimization), with faster convergence indicating higher sample efficiency. On the MultiGoal multimodal test, both FPO and DPPO collapse to a small number of goal modes due to the absence of entropy regularization, whereas PolicyFlow with the Brownian regularizer achieves the most balanced coverage of all six goals. The paper notes that FPO and DPPO were not directly compared on IsaacLab due to framework incompatibility (JAX vs. PyTorch), so the IsaacLab comparison is limited to PPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

PolicyFlow extends PPO's clipped surrogate objective to continuous normalizing flow policies, building on the flow matching framework (Lipman et al., 2022) and the rectified flow interpolation path (Liu et al., 2022). It departs from FPO, which estimates importance ratios via an ELBO objective introducing asymmetric bias, and from DPPO, which reformulates diffusion as an internal MDP, by approximating importance ratios through velocity field variations along a linear interpolation path—avoiding both path-wise backpropagation and MDP reformulation. The proxy objective formulation from Frans et al. (2025) provides the theoretical bridge connecting the flow-based policy to the PPO surrogate, while the velocity-field-to-score relationship from Liu et al. (2025) underpins the novel Brownian regularizer, which shapes the velocity field toward entropy-increasing dynamics inspired by the heat equation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PolicyFlow offers a practical pathway to expressive, multimodal policies in on-policy RL without the computational overhead of simulating full ODE trajectories during training, making CNF-based policies viable at scale in robotics benchmarks like IsaacLab and MuJoCo Playground. The Brownian regularizer provides a lightweight, principled alternative to expensive log-likelihood-based entropy estimation for flow policies, though the authors explicitly note it is not a theoretically exact derivation. Open questions include formal validation of alternative interpolation paths (stochastic interpolant, TrigFlow), extension to offline RL and fine-tuning settings, and a more comprehensive theoretical treatment of the approximation error beyond the first-order bound. The MultiGoal results also highlight that expressiveness alone is insufficient—without proper entropy regularization, even flow-based policies collapse to narrow modes.
