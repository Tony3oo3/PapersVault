## Problem

Generative Flow Networks sample objects proportionally to e^{βΦ(x)}, which in exponentially large search spaces causes the probability mass of a small set of high-reward candidates to be overwhelmed by the combinatorial explosion of lower-reward objects. Existing regularized RL operators (soft Bellman, mellowmax, soft mellowmax) each suffer from either value accumulation or dilution, and no unified framework existed to flexibly trade off between these two failure modes. Additionally, the robust RL interpretation of regularization in discrete compositional processes with step-wise reward uncertainty had not been formally developed.

## Input / Output

The method operates on a directed acyclic graph (DCP) where each node is a partial object and terminal nodes are complete sequences (e.g., 237-amino-acid proteins, 50-nucleotide UTRs, or variable-length antimicrobial peptides of 14–60 residues). A transformer-based proxy reward Φ is trained on domain-specific data and evaluated only at terminal states; the Q-network Q^θ is trained via a trajectory-level loss that requires a single forward pass per trajectory rather than per transition, making it ~10× faster than transition-based RL baselines. The learned policy is extracted as softmax([qα+ω]Q_s), and sampling temperature is varied at inference to trace out a quality–diversity curve for selecting k distinct candidates.

## Method

The core contribution is the General Mellowmax (GM) regularizer Ω^q = (1/ω)[q·KL(π_s, softmax(αQ_s)) + (1−q)(−H(π_s))], which interpolates between entropy regularization (q=0, recovering GFN/soft Bellman) and KL-to-softmax regularization (q=1, recovering soft mellowmax) via a single hyperparameter q. The associated operator Ω^{q,*}(Q_s) = (1/ω) log⟨softmax(αQ_s)^q, e^{ωQ_s}⟩ achieves tighter worst-case bounds on both accumulation and dilution than any single existing operator. From this operator, the authors derive a novel trajectory constraint (Theorem 4.1) that equates the state-wise optimality condition to a sum-of-log-softmax equality over all trajectories, enabling training via the VarGrad objective on a single Q-network. A robust RL interpretation is provided through a Fenchel-conjugate mapping (Theorem 5.1) showing that the GM uncertainty set decomposes reward uncertainty into per-step perturbations δ_i, yielding a more meaningful compositional robustness guarantee than the entropy-regularized (GFN) set, which never contains the proxy reward itself.

## Results

On the three biological sequence design tasks, TGM achieves the highest average mode reward across all domains: 4.27 on UTR (vs. GFN 4.12), 10.43 on AMP (vs. GFN 10.04), and 2.95 on GFP (vs. GFN 1.90). The largest relative improvement over GFN is on GFP, the largest search space (237-length protein sequences), where TGM finds modes with ~55% higher reward. On the synthetic Bit Sequence task, TGM with q>0 discovers more of the M ground-truth modes and generates samples closer to each mode compared to GFN (q=0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TGM outperforms GFN on all three biological tasks, with the gap most pronounced on GFP (2.95 vs. 1.90 average mode reward), where the exponentially large search space makes the accumulation problem most severe. Against SAC and PPO, TGM wins decisively on UTR and GFP; on AMP, PPO is competitive (9.79 vs. 10.43) because variable-length sequences allow PPO to exploit shorter high-reward candidates. Among TGM variants, the best q is task-dependent (q=0.75 for UTR/AMP, q=0.25 for GFP), confirming that the interpolation between accumulation and dilution is genuinely useful rather than a fixed hyperparameter. Additional baselines (Munchausen DQN, QGFN, temperature-conditioned GFN) are all outperformed by the best TGM setting, though temperature-conditioned TGM further improves GFP to 3.30, suggesting the techniques are complementary. On the synthetic Bit Sequence task, TGM with q>0 finds more ground-truth modes and closer samples to each mode than GFN, validating the peakiness improvement in a controlled setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate soft RL operators—soft Bellman (equivalent to the GFN flow operator), mellowmax, and soft mellowmax—into a single interpolated operator (General Mellowmax) controlled by a hyperparameter q that trades off value accumulation against dilution. It extends the trajectory balance framework (Malkin et al., 2022) by deriving a novel trajectory-level constraint for the GM operator, yielding the TGM algorithm. The robust RL interpretation builds on the regularization-robustness equivalence of Derman et al. (2021) and the regularized MDP framework of Geist et al. (2019), but applies it specifically to discrete compositional processes with state-rectangular uncertainty decomposed across generation steps, revealing that the uncertainty set induced by GFNs is structurally inadequate (never containing the proxy reward) while the soft mellowmax set provides a more meaningful robustness guarantee.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a fundamental limitation of the GFN objective in exponentially large search spaces—probability mass is dominated by combinatorially many low-reward objects—and provides both a theoretical fix (the GM operator with tighter worst-case bounds on |Δ_s^T|) and a practical algorithm (TGM) that consistently outperforms GFNs, SAC, and PPO on biological sequence design tasks, with the largest gains in the highest-dimensional GFP domain. The robust RL lens offers a new interpretive framework for choosing regularization in DCPs, though the practical impact is tempered by the fact that complementary techniques (temperature conditioning, QGFN) can also be layered on top. Open questions include extending TGM to general DAG-structured DCPs (beyond trees), multi-objective settings, and language generation with verifiable rewards.
