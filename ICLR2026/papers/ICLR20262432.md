## Problem

When time-series classifiers must operate on partial prefixes due to latency or cost constraints, they lose access to class-discriminative patterns that may appear later in the sequence, causing poor generalization. Existing knowledge distillation methods assume teacher and student see the same data, so directly matching full-context teacher features to partial-context student features can overwhelm the student and fail to transfer knowledge effectively. The paper identifies three exacerbated concerns in full-to-partial distillation: the student may not comprehend the transferred signal, a single teacher perspective lacks diversity, and training-data mismatch undermines fidelity.

## Input / Output

The student classifier receives a partial time series prefix of length e < L (and optionally a reduced channel set m < M) and outputs a class label. The teacher, trained on full-length sequences, provides features from which a diffusion prior is learned. During distillation, the student's intermediate feature vector is fused with Gaussian noise to initialize the reverse diffusion process, and the resulting posterior reconstruction sample is passed through the student's classification head to compute the distillation loss. The method operates in feature space (not input space), so the diffusion prior is lightweight and the inference cost is identical to the base student.

## Method

GDPD models student features as degraded observations of an ideal long-context feature and learns a diffusion-based generative prior over the teacher's feature distribution. During distillation, each student feature is fused with noise (with a learned per-feature weight α) to initialize the reverse DDIM process, and a posterior sample is drawn as a plausible completion of the student's partial information. The student is then trained so that its classification head, applied to this posterior reconstruction, predicts the correct label—effectively constraining the student to produce features that are minimally degraded relative to diverse teacher-manifold targets. Training proceeds in two phases: a warm-up period where the diffusion prior is trained on teacher features while the student learns the base task, followed by joint optimization of the task loss and the GDPD distillation loss. This formulation replaces the single static teacher target of conventional KD with a distribution of dynamic, diverse, and collectively informative teacher signals that adapt to the student's current capability.

## Results

On 12 UCR datasets at earliness e=0.5L (LSTM3-100→LSTM3-100), GDPD achieves an average AUC-PRC of 84.64 and test-fidelity of 77.58%, outperforming the strongest baseline RKD (79.89 AUC-PRC, 74.34% fidelity) by ~5 AUC-PRC points. GDPD wins top-1 on 10 of 12 datasets at e=0.5L and 10 of 12 at e=0.2L, with an average rank of 1.17 (vs. 5.83 for Base-KD and 7.58 for Fits). Across all earliness levels from 0.2L to 0.8L, GDPD consistently leads, with the largest gap at extreme partialness (e=0.2L: 73.83 vs. 69.23 for Base-KD, a +4.6 AUC-PRC margin).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GDPD outperforms all 12 baselines on average AUC-PRC at e=0.5L, with the largest margins over DT2W (+29.3), DKD (+10.4), and Attention (+9.2). Among the strongest competitors, GDPD beats RKD (79.89) by 4.75 AUC-PRC and Base-KD (78.14) by 6.5 AUC-PRC, while also achieving the highest test-fidelity (77.58% vs. 74.34% for RKD and 72.13% for Base-KD). In terms of dataset-level wins, GDPD achieves top-1 on 5 datasets and top-3 on 10 of 12, whereas no other method exceeds 2 top-1 wins or 5 top-3 placements. The gap widens at more extreme partialness: at e=0.2L, GDPD leads Base-KD by 4.6 AUC-PRC (73.83 vs. 69.23) and Fits by 6.4 (73.83 vs. 67.47), and at e=0.8L it leads by 4.2 and 6.3 respectively. On the PhysioNet real-world dataset, GDPD also consistently outperforms Base and Fits across all four heterogeneous settings (main-task, cross-task, channel-partialness, and balanced-to-imbalanced), confirming robustness beyond the UCR benchmark.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge distillation paradigm (Hinton et al., 2015; Romero et al., 2015) to a fundamentally different setting: the teacher and student operate on different input spaces (full vs. partial sequences), introducing an inherent representational gap that standard feature/logit matching cannot address. Conceptually, it repurposes the inverse diffusion problem (Kawar et al., 2022) and DDIM-based sampling (Huang et al., 2023) not for image restoration but as a mechanism to model teacher knowledge as a generative distribution over features, from which diverse and progressive supervision signals are sampled. The key departure from prior KD is the shift from deterministic point-target matching to distributional supervision, where each student feature is guided by a collection of plausible teacher completions rather than a single static target.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper opens a new research direction by proposing that teacher knowledge in KD should be modeled as a generative distribution rather than a fixed signal, a reframing that could generalize beyond time series to any setting where teacher and student see mismatched inputs (e.g., multi-modal to single-modal, high-resolution to low-resolution). Practical impact is significant for latency-constrained applications (ECG triage, industrial monitoring) where decisions must be made from partial data. Limitations include the added training overhead of the diffusion prior (though modest), the requirement for a pre-trained full-sequence teacher, and evaluation confined to time series; whether the distributional-knowledge framing transfers to vision or NLP distillation with input mismatch remains an open question.
