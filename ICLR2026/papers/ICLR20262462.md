## Problem

Current generative object insertion and movement methods operate within the 2D image plane using simple inpainting masks, which cannot resolve ambiguities in an object's 3D position (in front of or behind existing elements) or orientation (facing left or right). This paper addresses the gap by reframing object manipulation from a true 3D perspective, enabling precise control over a complete 6 Degrees-of-Freedom (6DoF) pose while maintaining realistic occlusion relationships with the scene.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262462/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a geometry-aware masked scene image (where foreground objects that should occlude the inserted object are preserved), a composited depth map (scene depth from Depth Anything with the insertion region's depth modified to the target value), a reference object provided as both an image and a text caption, and three absolute orientation parameters (azimuth φ, elevation θ, in-plane rotation δ) following the Orient Anything parameterization. The output is a single generated image in which the reference object appears at the specified 3D location and orientation with physically plausible occlusion, lighting, and shadow integration. Unlike relative-rotation approaches, the orientation is specified as an absolute canonical front-facing direction, making it directly interpretable by users.

## Method

SpatialHand modifies the FLUX-Dev MM-DiT architecture by extending the input token sequence to [X, C̃_mask, C̃_depth, C_obj + P([φ,θ,δ])], where the 6DoF pose is implicitly encoded through three complementary channels: 2D location via the geometry-aware masked image, depth via the composited depth map, and 3D orientation via a zero-initialized MLP projector that maps the three Euler angles into the latent dimension and adds them to the object reference tokens. A key design choice is geometry-aware composition, which compares the scene's predicted depth against the target insertion depth to identify and preserve foreground objects in both the mask and depth map, ensuring correct occlusion without explicit 3D reconstruction. Training follows a progressive three-stage scheme: Stage 0 initializes with UNO's LoRA adapter for identity preservation, Stage 1 fine-tunes on single-object novel-view pairs to learn orientation following, and Stage 2 fine-tunes on multi-object scenes for 3D positioning and occlusion. The training data (370k pairs) is generated by an automated pipeline that synthesizes 43k 3D assets with Hunyuan-3D 2.0, places them via Blender rendering and subject-driven generation (UNO, GPT-4o), and annotates 6DoF poses using Grounding-DINO, Segment Anything, Depth Anything, and Orient Anything.

## Results

On the 3D-aware object insertion benchmark (800 test samples from Objaverse), SpatialHand achieves the best identity preservation (DINO 81.7, CLIP 72.5) and dramatically outperforms all baselines on spatial accuracy: depth error (AbsRel) of 19.8 vs. 32–39 for baselines, and orientation accuracy (Acc@30°) of 52.0% vs. 16–20% for baselines. Subjective pose adherence scores are 4.27/5 for SpatialHand versus 2.10–2.67 for the strongest baselines. On the object movement task (300 samples), SpatialHand similarly leads with Acc@30° of 47.8% (vs. 18–31%), mIoU of 0.72 (vs. 0.22–0.45), and VLM-Acc for occlusion of 82.6% (vs. 49–60%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpatialHand decisively outperforms all five baselines on the core spatial metrics. On depth accuracy (AbsRel), SpatialHand achieves 19.8, roughly 40% lower error than the best baseline Nano Banana (32.1) and nearly 50% lower than GPT-4o (38.6). On orientation accuracy (Acc@30°), SpatialHand reaches 52.0%, more than 2.5× the best baseline GPT-4o (20.2%) and over 3× Gemini-2.0-Flash (16.0%). For identity preservation, SpatialHand edges out all baselines on DINO (81.7 vs. 80.5–81.2) and CLIP (72.5 vs. 65.7–66.5), with the gap widening notably under textual-only conditions. On subjective pose adherence, SpatialHand scores 4.27/5 versus 2.10–2.67 for baselines, indicating that instruction-based models (GPT-4o, Gemini) fundamentally struggle to interpret and execute 3D spatial instructions from text alone. Among the specialized 3D editing methods (Object3DiT, Diffusion Handles), SpatialHand also dominates on the movement task, achieving 47.8% Acc@30° versus 31.4% and 20.7% respectively, and 82.6% VLM-Acc for occlusion versus 55.2% and 49.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpatialHand builds on the FLUX-Dev MM-DiT diffusion transformer as its backbone and inherits identity-preservation capabilities from the UNO subject-driven generation LoRA. It departs from 2D inpainting-based object insertion methods (Paint-by-Example, AnyDoor, SmartBrush) by decomposing 6DoF pose into components a diffusion model can natively follow—2D mask, composited depth map, and orientation angles—rather than relying on explicit 3D geometry (point clouds or meshes) as in Object-3DiT and Diffusion Handles. The orientation parameterization follows Orient Anything, and the data pipeline leverages Hunyuan-3D for asset synthesis and Depth Anything for depth annotation, positioning the work at the intersection of 3D generation, visual foundation models, and diffusion-based image editing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that implicit 3D spatial guidance (a simple decomposition into mask + depth + three angles) can outperform both explicit 3D-geometry editing methods and state-of-the-art MLLMs like GPT-4o on spatial adherence metrics, suggesting that well-designed conditioning is more effective than complex 3D representations for single-image manipulation. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the interdependence between the 2D bounding box aspect ratio and the specified orientation, which can create conflicts; making these conditions independent is identified as future work. The automated data curation pipeline (43k synthetic 3D assets → 370k training pairs) is a practical contribution that lowers the barrier for training 3D-aware manipulation models, and the approach opens a path toward intuitive AR/VR-like editing within 2D images without requiring full 3D scene reconstruction.
