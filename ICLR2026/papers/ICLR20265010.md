## Problem

Quantization and sparsification introduce non-differentiable operations that break backpropagation, and the standard Straight-Through Estimator (STE) exacerbates this by creating a 'quantization-oblivious' backward pass: the forward pass includes quantization error while the gradient completely ignores it. This unmanaged error corrupts the learning signal, causing training divergence at ultra-low precision (1-bit, sub-1-bit) that has required numerous ad-hoc fixes. The paper identifies for the first time that the root cause is the absence of a proper gradient path allowing the network to learn robustness to quantization noise, rather than merely the lack of smoothness.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard high-precision model (transformer or CNN) with its training data and a quantization configuration specifying activation bit-width, weight bit-width, sub-channel block size (e.g., 128), and optional structured sparsity pattern (e.g., 2:4). It supports integer grids (1-bit, 1.5-bit ternary, 2-bit, 4-bit) and low-precision float formats (FP4 E2M1), with per-channel or sub-channel quantization. The output is a fully trained model at the target precision and sparsity that can be deployed using bitwise operations (XNOR, popcount) at 1-bit, or standard integer matrix multiplication at higher precisions, with the dequantization parameters (scale, offset) stored in float8.

## Method

The framework replaces STE's surrogate gradient with a three-stage pipeline: (1) a prequantization transform (linear or affine) maps inputs to the quantization grid, (2) quantization is modeled as additive noise δ that is detached from the computation graph, and (3) a denoising dequantization transform g(q) maps the noisy quantized vector back to the original range by solving a ridge regression objective, yielding a closed-form scale s_g = Cov(x,q)/(Var(q)+λ) and offset. Because g's parameters are computed from the statistics of the noisy q (which contains δ), the backward-pass gradient ∂g/∂q becomes an explicit, data-dependent function of the quantization error, creating an error-aware gradient path that STE discards. The regularization λ provides numerical stability against zero-variance blocks. A novel shortcut formula (Theorem 1) makes two-sided channel-wise affine quantized matrix multiplication efficient by decomposing it into one integer matmul plus two rank-1 corrections, and sparsification is unified by treating it as a special quantization that zeros small values, with the dequantization transform correcting for the combined sparsity+quantization error.

## Results

On Gemma 1B (C4, 25k steps), the method achieves 0.3751 validation accuracy at A1W1 (Affine, SCQ 128) versus 0.3397 for STE, and 0.4274 at A4W4 versus 0.4254 for STE. At A1.5W1.5 channel-wise, STE diverges to NaN while the method converges to 0.3297. On Gemma 4B (26B tokens), A4W1 with 2:4 sparsity reaches 0.4517, surpassing the BF16 Gemma 1B baseline (0.4494). On ResNet-50 ImageNet, A4W4 achieves 76.45% top-1 accuracy, exceeding the BF16 baseline of 76.41%, and on WMT EN-DE, A4W4 Transformer reaches 29.71 BLEU versus 29.49 for full precision.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The method consistently outperforms STE (Hubara et al., 2018) across all 12 tested configurations, with the largest gains at low precision: +0.0397 at A1W1 Affine Channel and +0.0382 at A2W2 Affine Channel, while STE diverges entirely at A1.5W1.5 channel-wise. Against BitNet (Wang et al., 2023) and ParetoQ (Liu et al., 2025) on A1W1 training, the method converges smoothly while both baselines exhibit instability or NaN losses on Shakespeare and OpenWebText. On ResNet-50 ImageNet A4W4, the method (76.45%) surpasses AQT (76.4%), VS-Quant (75.28%), FAQ (76.25%), and HAQ (76.14%), and is the only method requiring no gradient estimation, pretraining, or clipping. On WMT EN-DE A4W4, the method (29.71 BLEU) outperforms LSQ+LUQ (27.17), Fixed-Point (26.94), GradScale (25.9), and LUQ+SMP (27.25), and is the only method to exceed the full-precision baseline (29.49). For A1W1 WMT EN-DE, the method achieves 23.64 BLEU versus 17.87 for the prior binarized NMT approach (Zhang et al., 2023), a 5.77-point improvement without extra normalization or progressive quantization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the QAT pipeline established by Hubara et al. (2018) and the Straight-Through Estimator from Bengio et al. (2013), but departs fundamentally by identifying STE's 'quantization-oblivious' backward pass as the root cause of training instability at ultra-low precision. Rather than layering heuristic fixes (normalization, learning-rate schedules, optimizer changes) as prior work has done, it replaces the surrogate gradient with a closed-form denoising dequantization transform derived from a ridge regression objective, making the full forward-backward path well-defined without estimation. The framework also unifies sparsification with quantization by treating hard thresholding as a special additive error injection, and introduces a novel shortcut formula that makes channel-wise affine quantized matrix multiplication nearly as cheap as linear quantization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a theoretically grounded, drop-in replacement for STE that enables stable A1W1 and sub-1-bit training with standard recipes, a regime where prior methods (BitNet, ParetoQ, vanilla STE) diverge or require extensive ad-hoc tuning. The finding that 2:4 structured sparsity on A4W1 simultaneously halves compute cost and improves accuracy challenges the conventional accuracy-efficiency trade-off assumption. However, validation is limited to relatively short pre-training runs (25k steps / 6.5B tokens for Gemma 1B, 26B tokens for 4B), and the energy metric is explicitly a first-order approximation that omits data-movement costs; whether these results hold at full-scale pre-training (trillions of tokens) remains an open question. The practical hardware deployment of the full pipeline (including the dequantization transform and affine corrections) on real accelerators is also not yet demonstrated.
