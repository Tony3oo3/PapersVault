## Problem

Despite RL's demonstrated success in enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities, the underlying training dynamics remain incompletely understood both empirically and theoretically. It is unclear how RL reshapes model behavior at the reasoning-pattern level, what token-level mechanisms drive performance changes, and why different reward structures (verifiable vs. internal feedback) produce qualitatively different training trajectories.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260513/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The framework takes a text question q and models reasoning as a two-stage autoregressive process: first selecting a reasoning pattern r from a candidate set R via π(r|q), then generating a final answer a from set A via π(a|r,q). Empirically, the analysis operates at two granularities — reasoning-pattern level (tracking distribution shifts and per-pattern success rates across training steps using GPT-4o for pattern extraction and classification) and token level (measuring probability rank changes at individual positions), revealing that fewer than 10% of token positions experience rank shifts throughout training.

## Method

The paper develops a two-stage theoretical framework (q → r → a) with a tabular policy parameterization, analyzing RL training dynamics via gradient flow under the assumption that individual pattern success rates remain constant during training. For RLVR, they prove convergence to the highest-success-rate reasoning pattern and characterize two distinct regimes: rapid O(1/ε) convergence when the base model's overall accuracy exceeds all non-optimal pattern success rates, and a super-exponentially slow 'entanglement stage' (scaling as γ^γ where γ is the ratio of non-optimal to optimal pattern probabilities) when a suboptimal pattern's success rate exceeds the initial accuracy. For RLIF, they show the accuracy derivative at initialization is positive with probability 1 (explaining early gains), yet there is a 50% probability of converging to the least accurate reasoning path (explaining eventual degradation below base-model performance). Empirical validation combines reasoning-pattern-level dynamics on MATH, GSM8K, and AIME with token-level rank shift analysis, and case studies on Qwen2.5-3B/7B/32B and QwQ-32B confirm the theoretical predictions.

## Results

The paper's primary quantitative finding is that RL optimizes a surprisingly sparse subset of tokens: fewer than 10% of token positions experience a probability-rank shift after RL training (5.1%–10.1% across GSM8K, MATH, and AIME24 for RLVR and RLIF at steps 20–100). At the reasoning-pattern level, RLVR-trained models consistently shift toward patterns with higher success rates, while RLIF exhibits unstable pattern dynamics and can degrade below base-model accuracy. Theoretically, RLVR converges to the optimal reasoning pattern at rate O(1/ε) in the strong-base-model regime (Theorem 5.3), but may require super-exponentially many steps (scaling as γ^γ) in the entanglement regime (Theorem 5.4). For RLIF, the accuracy derivative at initialization is positive with probability 1, yet there is a 50% chance of converging to the least accurate reasoning path (Theorem 5.6).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares RLVR (GRPO with verifiable reward, following Shao et al. 2024) against RLIF (internal-feedback reward, following Zhao et al. 2025) using Qwen2.5-3B trained on MATH. RLVR produces stable, monotonic accuracy gains throughout training, while RLIF initially improves but then degrades, sometimes falling below the base model's accuracy. At the token level, RLVR induces fewer rank shifts (5.1%–7.3%) than RLIF (5.6%–10.1%), indicating RLVR is more targeted in its modifications. For cross-model validation, Qwen-2.5-7B-SimpleRL-Zoo (RLVR-enhanced, Zeng et al. 2025) is compared against Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct: the RLVR-enhanced model shows a consistent increase in the frequency of the highest-accuracy reasoning patterns across number theory, geometry, algebra, calculus, and AMC23 tasks. Similarly, QwQ-32B (RLVR-enhanced, Qwen Team 2025) is compared against Qwen-2.5-32B-Instruct on MATH, AIME24, and OlympiadBench, confirming that QwQ's reasoning patterns converge to the most accurate patterns of the base model, validating the theoretical prediction that RLVR selects the pattern with the highest success rate.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RLVR/GRPO framework (Shao et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2025) and the RLIF formulation (Zhao et al., 2025b; Agarwal et al., 2025), providing the first unified theoretical treatment of both under a two-stage reasoning abstraction (q→r→a). It departs from prior empirical analyses of RL dynamics (Huan et al., 2025; Yue et al., 2025a; Wang et al., 2025a) that focused on entropy reduction or sparse token shifts, by introducing reasoning-pattern-level analysis as the organizing principle and grounding it in a tabular-policy gradient-flow framework inherited from Mei et al. (2020) and Razin et al. (2025). The optimal-policy derivation follows the KL-regularized structure from Rafailov et al. (2023), adapted to the pattern-selection setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-regime characterization of RLVR (rapid convergence vs. super-exponential entanglement) offers a concrete theoretical explanation for why weaker base models struggle with RLVR training, with practical implications for choosing base models and training schedules. The 50% probability that RLIF converges to the least-accurate reasoning pattern provides a principled warning against unbounded RLIF training. Key limitations include the tabular-policy assumption, the fixed-success-rate assumption (Assumption 5.1), and reliance on GPT-4o for pattern extraction; extending the framework to non-tabular policies, time-varying pattern success rates, and multi-step reasoning chains remains open.
