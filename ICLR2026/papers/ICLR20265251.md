## Problem

In LLM reinforcement learning, sampling temperature is typically fixed or manually scheduled, ignoring feedback from the learning process. This static treatment fails to adapt exploration to the dynamic needs of policy optimization: too low a temperature causes premature convergence, while too high introduces noisy rollouts that waste computation. The paper asks whether temperature can be treated as a learnable decision variable that adapts online based on trajectory outcomes.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt (a math or commonsense question) and produces a structured text response containing a reasoning chain enclosed in  tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags, with a maximum generation length of 6k tokens. During training, the sampling temperature is not a single fixed value but is drawn at each step from a discrete candidate set {0.6, 0.7, …, 1.5} (K=10) via nucleus sampling (top-p=0.7) over the learned meta-policy distribution. Evaluation uses a fixed decoding temperature of 1.0 with Pass@1 and Pass@8 metrics.

## Method

TAMPO introduces a hierarchical two-loop framework where temperature is a learnable meta-policy. In the inner loop, the LLM policy is updated via GRPO using rollouts sampled at the temperature selected by the meta-policy. In the outer loop, the same rollouts are reused to compute temperature-specific advantages: for each trajectory, its log-likelihood is evaluated under every candidate virtual temperature, normalized across temperatures via sparsemax, and multiplied by the trajectory's GRPO advantage to yield a per-temperature reward signal. These signals are aggregated over the batch, smoothed with an exponential moving average, and converted into a probability distribution over temperatures via min-max normalization. The key insight is that trajectory likelihood is provably unimodal in temperature, so each trajectory inherently encodes a 'preferred' temperature, enabling the meta-policy to shift probability mass toward temperatures that make high-advantage trajectories more probable without requiring any additional rollouts.

## Results

On five mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, MATH-500, AMC23, Minerva, OlympiadBench), TAMPO achieves an average Pass@1 of 44.5% and Pass@8 of 63.8% using DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, outperforming the best fixed-temperature GRPO baseline (T=1.5: 42.6% Pass@1, 62.1% Pass@8) by 1.9 and 1.7 percentage points respectively. TAMPO also achieves the best or second-best result on every individual benchmark. On the ECQA commonsense reasoning benchmark with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, TAMPO reaches 76.12% Pass@1 and 79.67% Pass@8, surpassing the GRPO baseline (75.07% / 78.94%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

TAMPO consistently outperforms all four GRPO baselines (fixed T=0.9, T=1.2, T=1.5, and linearly increasing T:0.9→1.5) on average Pass@1 and Pass@8 across the five math benchmarks, with the largest margin over the linearly-increasing schedule (44.5 vs 42.8 Pass@1; 63.8 vs 59.7 Pass@8). Against the strongest fixed-temperature baseline GRPO(T=1.5), TAMPO gains 1.9 points in Pass@1 and 1.7 in Pass@8, and achieves the best individual scores on AIME24 Pass@8 (40.0), AMC23 Pass@8 (82.5), and Minerva Pass@1 (27.9). On the ECQA commonsense reasoning benchmark with a different base model (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct), TAMPO improves over GRPO by 1.05 points in Pass@1 (76.12% vs 75.07%) and 0.73 points in Pass@8 (79.67% vs 78.94%), confirming that the adaptive temperature mechanism generalizes beyond mathematical reasoning. The base model without RL (DS-Qwen-1.5B) lags all RL-trained variants by 3–5 points in average Pass@1, underscoring that the gains are attributable to the RL training procedure rather than the base model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as its inner-loop critic-free RL algorithm and extends the meta-learning paradigm from conventional RL (hierarchical options, meta-gradient hyperparameter tuning like Meta-SAC) to the LLM RL setting. The key departure is that prior meta-policy work in RL targets differentiable hyperparameters (discount factors, entropy coefficients), whereas sampling temperature in LLM RL is non-differentiable through the decoupled rollout pipeline; TAMPO circumvents this by reusing existing rollouts and computing trajectory likelihoods under virtual temperatures rather than backpropagating through sampling. It also contrasts with inference-time temperature selection (Du et al., 2025) by operating online during training and with entropy-regularization schedules (Sethi et al., 2025; Shen, 2025) by directly modulating the sampling distribution rather than the loss function.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TAMPO offers a lightweight, drop-in mechanism for adaptive exploration in LLM RL that requires no extra rollouts and negligible overhead, making it practically attractive for practitioners already using GRPO or similar critic-free algorithms. However, the empirical gains are modest (~1.9% Pass@1 over the best fixed-temperature baseline) and validated only on small models (1.5B, 3B) and primarily mathematical reasoning, leaving open whether the approach scales to larger models, longer reasoning chains, or non-mathematical domains. A broader open question is whether the discrete candidate-temperature formulation (K=10) is sufficient or whether continuous temperature optimization would yield further gains, and whether the min-max normalization + EMA update rule is the right meta-policy parameterization or merely a convenient heuristic.
