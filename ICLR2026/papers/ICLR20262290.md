## Problem

Existing audio-driven talking-head generation methods feed raw audio directly into generative models, producing temporally accurate but emotionally flat facial animations. They lack a fine-grained, interpretable control signal that captures the subtle emotional cues embedded in speech, making it difficult to generate nuanced and expressive facial movements beyond basic lip synchronization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262290/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 16 kHz speech waveform and a 512×512 reference portrait image as input, and produces a 25 FPS, 512×512 emotionally expressive talking-head video. Internally, the ALM generates 24-dimensional AU sequences at 5 FPS (temporally downsampled from 25 FPS to fit context limits), which are then linearly upsampled and projected into 2D facial representations (keypoint landmarks or mesh renderings) before conditioning the diffusion model.

## Method

AUHead is a two-stage framework. Stage 1 fine-tunes Qwen-Audio-Chat via LoRA to disentangle AU sequences from audio, using a spatial-temporal tokenization scheme that converts dense 24-dim AU vectors into sparse index-intensity pairs and a chain-of-thought 'emotion-then-AU' generation strategy that first predicts an emotion category before regressing the AU sequence. Stage 2 inserts an AU adapter (multiple cross-attention layers with zero-initialization) into a pretrained diffusion backbone (Hallo V1 or MEMO), where context-aware AU embeddings—constructed via a lightweight temporal convolution over a local window of 2D AU representations—are cross-attended with visual latents at each denoising step. At inference, a disentanglement guidance strategy with separate guidance scales for AU and other conditions (audio, reference image) enables flexible trade-off between expression fidelity and visual quality.

## Results

On MEAD, AUHead(MEMO) achieves the best FID (10.97), PSNR (23.35 dB), SSIM (0.7395), and lowest M-LMD (1.86) / F-LMD (2.16) among all compared methods. On CREMA, AUHead(MEMO) similarly leads with FID 8.24, PSNR 24.29 dB, SSIM 0.7413, M-LMD 1.93, and F-LMD 2.40. In the user study against HalloV2, AUHead is preferred 64.6% of the time for emotional expression, 71.0% for audio-lip sync, and 67.8% overall. The only metric where AUHead(MEMO) trails its base model is Sync on MEAD (6.63 vs. 6.99), which the authors attribute to minor timing mismatches between predicted AUs and speech.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AUHead(MEMO) outperforms all 12 baselines on FID, PSNR, SSIM, and both LMD metrics on both MEAD and CREMA, with the largest FID gains over older methods (e.g., 32.80→10.97 vs. Wav2Lip on MEAD; 25.62→8.24 vs. Wav2Lip on CREMA). Against the closest competitors, AUHead(MEMO) improves FID by 0.15 over MEMO* (10.97 vs. 11.12 on MEAD; 8.24 vs. 8.39 on CREMA) and by 1.53 over Sonic (14.26→10.97 on MEAD). On Sync, AUHead(MEMO) slightly trails MEMO* on MEAD (6.63 vs. 6.99) but leads on CREMA (6.21 vs. 6.09); the authors note human evaluation shows no perceptible lip misalignment. AUHead(HalloV1) also surpasses HalloV1* on FID (12.84 vs. 13.07) and M-LMD (2.38 vs. 2.50) on MEAD, and on FID (9.71 vs. 10.03) on CREMA, confirming the AU adapter's benefit across different base architectures. In the user study, AUHead is preferred over HalloV2 by a wide margin: 64.6% for emotional expression, 71.0% for audio-lip sync, and 67.8% overall, versus 18.9%, 13.8%, and 16.1% for HalloV2 respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant 'audio-in, video-out' paradigm of talking head generation (Wav2Lip, Hallo, MEMO, Sonic) by inserting an interpretable intermediate representation—Facial Action Units—extracted by a fine-tuned Audio-Language Model (Qwen-Audio-Chat) before feeding into a diffusion-based generator. It builds directly on Hallo V1 and MEMO as frozen diffusion backbones, adding a lightweight AU adapter with cross-attention layers, and on Qwen-Audio-Chat as the ALM backbone for Stage 1. Conceptually, it extends the 'understanding-then-generation' line (Wan-S2V, OmniHuman-1.5) by replacing free-form text guidance with structured, temporally aligned AU sequences, and introduces a CoT-style 'emotion-then-AU' decoding strategy and spatial-temporal AU tokenization to make dense AU vectors tractable for autoregressive language modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By establishing AUs as a controllable, interpretable bridge between audio and visual modalities, the paper offers a general strategy for fine-grained expression control that goes beyond coarse emotion labels or opaque latent codes. The finding that a LoRA-fine-tuned ALM can produce AU sequences at near human-annotator consistency (MAE ≈ 0.2) suggests that pretrained audio-language models harbor substantial unexplored emotion-perception capacity. Limitations include a slight Sync-score drop when using 2D AU representations, dependence on the 24-AU FEAFA subset, and evaluation restricted to studio-quality datasets (MEAD, CREMA); the authors note future work on in-the-wild poses and backgrounds. Open questions include whether the ALM-based AU disentanglement generalizes to multi-speaker or conversational settings and whether the disentanglement guidance strategy transfers to other conditional diffusion tasks.
