## Problem

While prior work established circuit-complexity upper bounds (TC⁰) and partial lower bounds (star-free languages) for SSMs, there was no systematic logical characterisation linking specific SSM architectural variants—diagonal-gated, time-invariant, mixed—to precise fragments of temporal logic, nor any analysis of how arithmetic precision (fixed-width vs. log-precision) governs the boundary between regular and non-regular language recognition. This paper fills that gap by providing a unified logical framework that simultaneously clarifies SSM expressiveness and enables direct comparison with transformer architectures.

## Input / Output

The SSM consumes a word σ = σ₁…σₖ over a finite alphabet Σ, embeds each symbol into ℝᵈ, and processes it through L layers of linear recurrences h_t = gate(x_t)·h_{t-1} + inc(x_t), producing a per-position output sequence. The final accept/reject decision is the scalar y_k = out(z_k^L), where the model accepts iff y_k = 1. The analysis distinguishes two precision regimes: fixed-width arithmetic (constant b bits, saturated, monotonic rounding) and log-precision arithmetic (O(log n) bits for inputs of length n), which fundamentally determines whether counting operators can be evaluated.

## Method

The paper constructs explicit SSM weight matrices that evaluate PLTL_f formulas layer-by-layer according to nesting depth, with each subformula assigned a dedicated hidden-state dimension. The since-operator φ S ψ is implemented via input-dependent diagonal gates (gate = diag(Ax_t)) exploiting the recursive unfolding φ S ψ ≡ ψ ∨ (φ ∧ Y(φ S ψ)), while Y and P use time-invariant diagonal gates; modular predicates are computed via cyclic permutation matrices maintaining a position counter mod m. A monotonicity lemma proves that diagonal fixed-precision SSMs must stabilise under repeated identical inputs, yielding a tight separation: they recognise exactly PLTL_f (star-free languages) and provably cannot recognise (aa)*. With log-precision, the same construction extends to PLTL_f[←#] by accumulating weighted counts in hidden states, enabling recognition of non-regular languages like {aⁿbⁿcⁿ}. Time-invariant SSMs are shown to capture UN-PLTL_f[MOD] (and with log-precision, UN-PLTL_f[MOD,←#]), while mixed SSMs reach all regular languages in AC⁰, aligning with UHAT+PE transformers.

## Results

The paper establishes a hierarchy of expressiveness lower bounds for SSM variants. Diagonal SSM with fixed-precision recognize exactly PLTL_f (star-free languages, Theorem 1) but provably cannot recognize (aa)* (Theorem 3). With log-precision, diagonal SSM extend to PLTL_f[←#], capturing non-regular languages like {aⁿbⁿcⁿ}. Time-invariant SSM with fixed-precision recognize UN-PLTL_f[MOD] (Theorem 7), while mixed SSM (combining both gate types) capture all regular languages in AC⁰ (Corollary 8). Global counting with negative coefficients strictly requires log-precision arithmetic.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper positions SSM variants against transformer architectures studied in formal expressiveness literature. Diagonal SSM with fixed-precision align exactly with Unique Hard-Attention Transformers (UHAT) without positional encodings: both capture FO[<] / star-free languages. Adding time-invariant layers to SSM provides an expressive lift analogous to adding positional encodings to transformers, yielding modular predicates (FO[<,MOD]). Mixed SSM with fixed-precision match UHAT+PE in capturing all regular languages in AC⁰. Under log-precision, mixed SSM align with Average Hard-Attention Transformers (AHAT) in capturing counting properties, but SSMs are strictly less expressive than AHAT+PE because SSMs are limited to backward-looking counting (←#) while AHAT's global attention enables forward-looking counting (→#). SSMs strictly subsume the logic of Soft-Attention Transformers (C-RASP) due to superior local temporal processing (e.g., the Yesterday operator), as C-RASP lacks both yesterday and previously operators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SSM expressiveness analysis initiated by Merrill et al. (2024) (circuit-complexity upper bounds) and Sarrof et al. (2024) (star-free language lower bound for diagonal SSMs) by recasting the entire question in the language of pure-past linear temporal logic over finite traces (PLTL_f). It is, to the authors' knowledge, the first work connecting SSM expressive power to formal logic, and it deliberately mirrors the logical fragments (UHAT/AHAT, C-RASP) already used to characterise transformer variants, enabling a direct architectural comparison. Three new axes are introduced beyond prior work: (1) time-invariant and mixed SSMs mapped to modular-predicate extensions, (2) a systematic separation of fixed-precision vs. log-precision arithmetic, and (3) integration into the existing transformer expressiveness landscape.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The results provide hard, training-independent architectural limits: for example, no diagonal fixed-precision SSM can ever recognise (aa)* regardless of optimiser or dataset, and global counting (e.g. a^n b^n c^n) strictly requires log-precision arithmetic. A key open question is Conjecture 1, which posits that time-invariant SSMs cannot evaluate the since-operator, implying a genuine incomparability between diagonal and time-invariant variants that remains unproven. The gap between the AC^0 lower bounds established here and the TC^0 upper bound from Merrill et al. (2024) is unresolved, leaving open whether the upper bound can be tightened or whether SSMs can express languages outside AC^0 (e.g. parity). For practitioners, the impossibility results delineate which sequence-modelling tasks are structurally out of reach for specific SSM configurations, complementing empirical benchmarks with theoretical guarantees.
