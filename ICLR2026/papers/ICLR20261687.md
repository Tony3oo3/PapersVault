## Problem

Most generic object tracking (GOT) methods rely exclusively on 2D features, making them vulnerable to partial occlusion, background distractors, and geometric variations. While 3D geometric cues could improve tracking, existing approaches either require unavailable auxiliary 3D inputs (RGB-D, point clouds) or naively fuse geometric features in a way that degrades the dominant semantic signal. The paper addresses how to integrate 3D geometry into a 2D tracker from RGB video alone while preserving semantic discrimination.

## Input / Output

The tracker consumes a 2D RGB video stream with an initial bounding box, processing two reference frames and one current frame per tracking step at 252×252 or 378×378 resolution. Semantic features are extracted by a frozen DINOv2-L backbone, while geometric features (camera pose, point map, depth) are extracted by a frozen VGGT DPT head operating on spatially concatenated multi-frame inputs. The output is a per-frame bounding box in ltrb format, produced by a regression decoder applied to the classification score map at the highest-scoring location.

## Method

GOT-Edit builds on the ToMP track-by-detection paradigm with a dual model-predictor architecture: a semantic predictor generates localization-head weights from DINOv2 features, while a geometric predictor generates perturbation weights from VGGT features. The key mechanism is an online null-space projection (adapted from AlphaEdit) that constrains the geometric perturbation to lie in the null space of the semantic knowledge, computed via SVD on whitened, ridge-regularized semantic features, ensuring geometry is added without corrupting semantic discrimination. Geometric features are first aligned to semantic resolution and fused via a learned spatial gating mask before being fed to the predictors. The combined weights (semantic + null-space-projected perturbation) are applied to the fused current-frame features for target localization, with a regression decoder producing the final bounding box. Training uses the standard ToMP objective (hinge classification + GIoU regression) with frozen backbones, and the entire editing process operates online per frame to adapt to dynamically changing targets.

## Results

GOT-Edit-378 (trained on Base+VastTrack) achieves SUC of 64.5 on AVisT, 71.1 on NfS, 75.0 on OTB, AO of 80.2 on GOT-10k*, SUC of 75.0 on LaSOT, and SUC of 86.7 on TrackingNet, using only 53M trainable parameters and 3 frames per evaluation. On the VOT challenge, it scores 89.8 (VOT-STb2022) and 90.3 (VOT-ST2020) in Robustness, outperforming all compared trackers. The method yields a consistent 2–3% SUC gain over the ToMP-378 baseline across all datasets, with the largest improvement (+4.0 SR75) on the out-of-distribution GOT-10k* benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GOT-Edit outperforms all DINOv2-based trackers (PiVOT, LoRAT, ToMP) by 2–3% SUC across every benchmark, confirming that the geometry-aware null-space editing adds consistent value over purely semantic tracking. Against the larger MCTTrack (287M params, 5 frames), GOT-Edit wins on all out-of-distribution datasets (AVisT +1.6, NfS +0.5, OTB +3.0, GOT-10k* AO +0.2) but trails on in-distribution LaSOT (75.0 vs. 76.6) and TrackingNet (86.7 vs. 87.9), where MCTTrack's extra frames and parameters provide an edge. ARPTrack (405M params, 7 frames) achieves a slightly higher GOT-10k* AO (81.5 vs. 80.2) but is outperformed by GOT-Edit on all other reported metrics. On the VOT challenge, GOT-Edit leads MixFormer (85.9), OSTrack (86.7), TransT (84.9), and ToMP (81.8) by 2–8 points in Robustness on VOT-STb2022. In the attribute-wise analysis (Figure 3), GOT-Edit surpasses Song et al. (2022), Zheng et al. (2024), Ma et al. (2024), and Wang et al. (2021) on occlusion, background clutter, and rotation, while showing a slight drop on fast motion and viewpoint change where geometric cues are less informative.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the track-by-detection paradigm of ToMP (Mayer et al., 2022), which uses a model predictor to generate target-specific localization weights online, and adapts the null-space constrained knowledge editing mechanism of AlphaEdit (Fang et al., 2025) from offline language-model editing to the online tracking setting. It departs from prior 3D-augmented trackers (which require RGB-D or point-cloud inputs) by extracting geometric cues from a frozen VGGT model operating purely on RGB frames, and from prior model-editing work by making the null-space projection dynamic and per-frame rather than a one-time offline update. The conceptual bridge is that the localization-head weight update in track-by-detection (Eq. 4) shares the linear key-value form of AlphaEdit's FFN editing, enabling a direct transfer of the null-space preservation guarantee to the tracking context.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GOT-Edit establishes a principled mechanism for injecting auxiliary modalities (here, 3D geometry) into a dominant-modality tracker without catastrophic forgetting, which is a broadly applicable pattern beyond tracking. The ablation showing that naive geometry fusion degrades semantic attributes (distractor, fast motion, illumination) while the null-space constraint recovers both modalities validates the asymmetry assumption and provides a template for multi-modality integration in other online-adaptive systems. Key limitations remain: performance drops under rapid motion and large viewpoint changes where geometric cues become unreliable, and the VGGT backbone adds 65–92 ms per frame of overhead, limiting real-time deployment. Open questions include whether the online editing framework can accommodate additional modalities (e.g., optical flow, text) and whether the null-space constraint generalizes to non-linear model predictors.
