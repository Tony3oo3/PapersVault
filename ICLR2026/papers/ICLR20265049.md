## Problem

Models like OpenAI-o3 can 'think with images' by dynamically referencing visual regions during reasoning, yet no benchmark exists to holistically evaluate this capability. Existing benchmarks either lack traceable evidence (bounding-box-level evaluation of intermediate reasoning steps), focus on simple spatial queries without second-order reasoning, or rely on COCO-derived images with high contamination risk. The paper also notes that prior RL-based visual grounded reasoning methods supervise only final answers, neglecting the intermediate localization process that makes reasoning explainable.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265049/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Input consists of high-resolution real-world images (average 2152×1615, sampled from SA-1B with dense object scenes) paired with a multiple-choice text question targeting extremely small objects (mean target area ~3.05% of image). Output is a structured reasoning pathway in text space: the model first generates one or more bounding boxes as <box>[x1,y1,x2,y2]</box> tokens to ground its attention, then produces a final multiple-choice answer enclosed in <answer> tags. Notably, grounding is done via text-space coordinate tokens rather than actual image cropping or replay, making inference more efficient.

## Method

TreeVGR is a two-stage training paradigm initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Stage 1 (cold-start SFT) fine-tunes on 35K multi-box samples from VGR-158K with coordinate-system conversion and 4.7K reflective error-correction samples that inject synthetic wrong boxes followed by 'Wait, this box seems to be wrong' prompts. Stage 2 applies GRPO reinforcement learning on 37K samples (30K from V* Bench + 7K VisDrone counting problems) with a novel dual IoU reward R_IoU = ½(R_IoU^R + R_IoU^P) added to standard accuracy and formatting rewards. The recall term ensures every ground-truth box is matched by at least one prediction, while the precision term prevents the model from enumerating spurious boxes to inflate recall. This traceable evidence supervision during RL explicitly rewards spatially accurate localization throughout the reasoning chain, yielding +13.4 on TreeBench, +16.8 on V* Bench, and +12.6 on MME-RealWorld-Lite over the base model.

## Results

On TreeBench, TreeVGR-7B achieves 50.4% overall accuracy and 44.0 mIoU, improving +13.4 over its base model Qwen2.5-VL-7B (37.0%). No model reaches 60% accuracy on TreeBench; the strongest private model, OpenAI-o3, scores 54.8%. On V* Bench, TreeVGR-7B attains 91.1% (+16.8 over base), and on MME-RealWorld-Lite it reaches 54.9% (+12.6), establishing open-source state-of-the-art among visual grounded reasoning models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TreeVGR-7B (50.4) outperforms all open-source baselines on TreeBench, surpassing InternVL3-78B (46.4) by 4.0 points with roughly 1/10 the parameters, and beating the closest VGR competitors DeepEyes-7B (37.5) and Pixel-Reasoner-7B (39.0) by 12.9 and 11.4 points respectively. It also achieves the highest mIoU (44.0) among all VGR models, indicating superior localization precision. On V* Bench, TreeVGR-7B (91.1) edges out DeepEyes-7B (90.0) and substantially outperforms Pixel-Reasoner-7B (80.6); on MME-RealWorld-Lite it leads with 54.9 vs. 53.2 (DeepEyes) and 49.7 (Pixel-Reasoner). However, TreeVGR-7B still trails the top private models on TreeBench: o3-0416 (54.8) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (54.1) both exceed it by 4–4.4 points, though o3's reasoning pathways are not publicly available for traceable evaluation. The largest per-category gains over the base model appear in Physical State (+26.1), OCR (+35.4), and Contact & Occlusion (+22.0), suggesting the dual IoU reward is most beneficial for tasks requiring precise multi-object localization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual grounded reasoning (VGR) paradigm introduced by Wang et al. (2025e) and the 'thinking with images' capability demonstrated by OpenAI-o3, building on Qwen2.5-VL's native grounding (absolute coordinates, mROPE) as the base architecture. It departs from prior RL-based VGR methods (DeepEyes, Pixel-Reasoner, Ground-R1) that supervise only final-answer accuracy by introducing a dual IoU reward (precision + recall) that explicitly supervises intermediate bounding box generation during reinforcement learning. The cold-start SFT stage addresses the computational bottleneck identified in DeepEyes (32 H100 GPUs × 48 hours) by pre-establishing grounding-then-answering behavior before RL, while TreeBench fills a gap left by V* Bench, HR-Bench, and MME-RealWorld, none of which provide traceable bounding-box evidence or second-order reasoning evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

TreeBench exposes a significant capability gap: even OpenAI-o3 and Gemini-2.5-Pro score below 60%, and the 'perspective transform' subtask (ego-view 3D awareness) remains a major bottleneck for all current models, suggesting that true spatial reasoning beyond 2D localization is unsolved. The ablation revealing that removing the precision term causes complete model collapse (0.0 accuracy via box enumeration) highlights a practical RL failure mode relevant to any grounding-supervised training pipeline. Limitations include the 7B model scale, the small 405-question benchmark, and the decoupling between localization quality and reasoning performance, which implies that precise grounding alone is insufficient for complex spatial inference. Open questions include whether the dual IoU reward generalizes to larger architectures and how to unify vision-centric grounded reasoning with text-centric long-chain reasoning (math, code).
