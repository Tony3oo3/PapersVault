## Problem

Existing visuomotor policy learning methods, particularly diffusion-based approaches like Diffusion Policy, refine either visual perception or action generation in isolation without establishing a tight hierarchical correspondence between the two. Prior hierarchical policy methods (e.g., Dense Policy, CARP) model hierarchy only in the action generation process, leaving the input and representation stages unstructured. This paper asks whether explicitly coupling hierarchical structure across the entire perception-to-action pipeline—input, representation, and generation—can improve both performance and generalization in robotic manipulation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a single RGB-D image (e.g., 128×128 or 180×320 depending on benchmark) and robot proprioceptive poses as input. The RGB-D image is first partitioned into N non-overlapping depth layers using linear-increasing depth boundaries that allocate finer resolution to closer objects. The output is a sequence of action chunks (horizon T_a = 2–8 depending on the task) generated by a K-stage denoising diffusion process, where each stage is conditioned on a different spatial scale of the visual representation. In real-world deployment, an asynchronous inference design with temporal ensembling and p-masking is used to achieve ~12 Hz control frequency.

## Method

H³DP introduces three coupled hierarchical structures. (1) Depth-aware layering partitions the RGB-D image into N layers via indicator masks over depth intervals, enabling explicit foreground-background separation without manual segmentation. (2) Each layer is encoded by a separate VQGAN-based encoder, and the resulting feature maps are quantized through a learnable VQ-VAE codebook, then interpolated to K spatial scales (e.g., 1×1, 3×3, 5×5, 7×7) to form multi-scale representations that capture global-to-local structure. (3) The diffusion denoising process is partitioned into K stages aligned with these scales: early high-noise stages are conditioned on coarse (low-resolution) features to shape the global action structure, while later low-noise stages use fine-grained features to refine precise details. Training is end-to-end, minimizing a combined diffusion loss (ε-prediction) and a bidirectional consistency loss that encourages cross-scale feature alignment; the denoising network is conditioned on the finest-scale feature during training so gradients propagate through the entire hierarchical encoder.

## Results

Across 44 simulation tasks spanning 5 benchmarks (MetaWorld, ManiSkill, Adroit, DexArt, RoboTwin), H³DP achieves a 75.6% average success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline DP3 (59.3%) by a +27.5% relative margin. In real-world bimanual manipulation on the Galaxea R1 robot across 4 cluttered long-horizon tasks, H³DP achieves a +72.4% relative improvement over DP and DP3. The method also demonstrates strong sample efficiency, surpassing DP3 even when trained with only 20% of expert demonstrations (43.0% vs. 33.5% average in real-world).

## Baselines & Comparisons

H³DP outperforms all four primary baselines (DP, DP w/ depth, DP3) across nearly all 44 simulation tasks, with the largest gains on ManiSkill deformable (+33.0 pts over DP3) and RoboTwin (+11.5 pts over DP3). Against DP3 specifically, H³DP achieves +27.5% relative improvement on average while requiring no manual point-cloud segmentation, whereas DP3's performance degrades substantially (from 82.8% to 54.3% average) when ideal segmentation is unavailable. In additional comparisons (Table 8), H³DP (89.8% avg on 6 MetaWorld tasks) beats FlowPolicy (61.0%), ET-SEED (70.7%), and 3D Diffuser Actor (54.0%). Against hierarchical action baselines (Table 9), H³DP (92.8% avg) outperforms 2D Dense Policy (56.0%) and 3D Dense Policy (76.7%) by +21.0% relative margin. In real-world experiments, H³DP dominates all 4 tasks, with particularly large gaps on Clean Fridge and Pour Juice where DP and DP3 struggle to complete the tasks at all.

## Relation to Prior Work

H³DP extends the Diffusion Policy lineage by introducing hierarchical structure across three previously decoupled stages: input processing, visual representation, and action generation. Prior diffusion-based policies (DP, DP3, 3D Diffuser Actor) refine either perception or action modeling in isolation, while hierarchical action methods (Dense Policy, CARP, ARP) apply multi-scale structure only to the output side. The key conceptual departure is coupling multi-scale quantized visual features with the diffusion denoising schedule so that coarse visual context guides early (low-frequency) denoising steps and fine-grained features guide later (high-frequency) steps, drawing an explicit analogy to the spectral autoregression property of diffusion models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that H³DP achieves strong performance using only raw single-camera RGB-D input without the manual point-cloud segmentation that DP3 requires, making it more deployable in cluttered real-world settings where segmentation quality is unreliable. The demonstrated robustness to noisy depth (75% vs. 17% on Soccer under Gaussian noise) addresses a real deployment concern for depth sensors. Open questions include whether the hierarchical visual-action coupling generalizes to higher-DoF dexterous tasks (the authors acknowledge this as future work), whether the depth-aware layering strategy transfers to multi-view or egocentric settings, and whether the VQ codebook design is optimal compared to continuous multi-scale representations.
