## Problem

Most existing methods for time series forecasting with exogenous variables adopt a two-step strategy that models temporal and channel correlations separately, causing mutual interference and suboptimal joint correlation capture. Additionally, real-world time series are frequently corrupted by sensor failures and transmission errors, making point-wise correlation estimation fragile. The paper addresses both limitations by proposing a graph-consistent generative framework that jointly models temporal and channel correlations in a noise-robust manner.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260952/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes historical endogenous series (N×T), historical exogenous series (D×T), and optionally future exogenous series (D×F), producing future endogenous predictions (N×F). A key design choice is that future exogenous variables are optional: when unavailable, the Variational Generator produces them via a VAE, and the pipeline remains unchanged. Inputs are patchified along the temporal dimension into non-overlapping patches of size p, yielding L=⌈(T+F)/p⌉ patch tokens per channel, which serve as graph nodes for subsequent correlation modeling.

## Method

GCGNet comprises three modules operating in sequence. First, a Variational Generator (VAE) produces coarse predictions of future endogenous and exogenous series. Second, a Graph Structure Aligner patchifies both the generated and ground-truth full sequences, feeds patch embeddings through a shared Graph VAE to produce adjacency matrices, and enforces L1 structural alignment between them—guiding the generator to produce sequences whose patch-level relational structure matches the true data rather than relying on point-wise reconstruction. Third, a Graph Refiner applies top-k sparsification to the generated adjacency matrix and propagates information via multi-layer GCN, which both refines predictions and prevents the shared Graph VAE from degenerating into input-agnostic outputs. The total loss combines forecasting L1 loss, graph alignment loss, and two KL regularization terms from the respective VAEs.

## Results

GCGNet achieves 18 out of 24 first-place rankings in MSE and 20 out of 24 in MAE across 12 real-world datasets with two forecasting horizons each. On the NP dataset (long-term, horizon 360), GCGNet attains MSE 0.496 and MAE 0.442, outperforming the strongest exogenous-aware baselines TimeXer (MSE 0.600) and TFT (MSE 0.539). On PJM (horizon 360), GCGNet reaches MSE 0.128, beating DUET (0.131) and TimeXer (0.140). Averaged across all 12 datasets, GCGNet achieves the lowest MSE and MAE in every dataset, with the largest margins on wind power datasets (e.g., Sdwpfh2: MSE 0.472 vs. TimeXer 0.891).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GCGNet outperforms all 10 baselines on the average MSE and MAE across all 12 datasets, securing 18/24 first-place MSE and 20/24 first-place MAE rankings. The closest competitors are TimeXer and TFT, which are specifically designed for exogenous-variable forecasting; GCGNet beats TimeXer by an average MSE margin of ~0.05–0.40 depending on the dataset (e.g., 0.496 vs. 0.600 on NP-360, 0.472 vs. 0.891 on Sdwpfh2-Avg). On a few individual dataset-horizon cells, other methods edge out GCGNet (e.g., TFT achieves MSE 0.167 vs. GCGNet's 0.179 on Energy-360; CrossLinear reaches 0.345 vs. 0.375 on Sdwpfm1-24), but these are isolated cases that do not affect the overall dominance. Channel-independent baselines like PatchTST and xPatch consistently underperform, confirming the importance of modeling exogenous-channel interactions. The ablation studies further show that removing the Graph Refiner causes the largest degradation (average MSE jumps from 0.323 to 0.605 across four datasets), highlighting that the joint graph-consistent generative pipeline is essential for the observed gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

GCGNet departs from the dominant two-step paradigm in exogenous-aware forecasting (e.g., TimeXer, TFT, CrossLinear), which models temporal and channel correlations sequentially and risks mutual interference between the two steps. Instead, it repurposes graph representations to jointly capture both correlation types in a single structure, drawing on graph-based time series methods (TimeFilter, ASTGI) for the joint modeling idea. The generative component is borrowed from VAE-based time series work (D3VAE, TimeVAE) but redirected from data synthesis toward robustness: the VAE denoises noisy observations and the Graph VAE produces a probabilistic adjacency matrix that resists overfitting to spurious correlations. The Graph Structure Aligner, which uses L1 distance between ground-truth and generated graph structures as a training signal, is the paper's most novel architectural contribution, combining ideas from graph consistency losses and variational inference in a way not previously applied to forecasting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The consistent outperformance across 12 diverse datasets (electricity, wind power, hydropower, energy) and the demonstrated robustness under 10–50% missing exogenous values make GCGNet a practically relevant option for real-world forecasting pipelines where sensor failures and incomplete covariates are common. The framework's ability to gracefully handle both available and unavailable future exogenous variables broadens its applicability without architectural changes. Open questions include whether the graph-consistency training signal scales to very high-dimensional multivariate settings (hundreds of channels), whether the patch-based graph construction is optimal for non-stationary or irregularly sampled series, and whether the joint graph modeling advantage persists when exogenous variables are weakly correlated with the target.
