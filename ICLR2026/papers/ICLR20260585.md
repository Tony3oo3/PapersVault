## Problem

Developing generalizable reasoning in multimodal LLMs typically requires RL post-training on large, domain-specific datasets (math, geometry, multi-discipline QA), which are expensive to curate and limit out-of-domain transfer. This paper asks whether simple, synthetic arcade-style games can serve as scalable surrogate tasks for RL post-training that unlock reasoning skills transferable to entirely different downstream domains, without any in-domain data exposure during training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260585/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

During training, the model receives multimodal game states: for Snake, a 10×10 board image plus a structured text prompt specifying snake coordinates, apple positions, enemy positions, and reasoning instructions (e.g., 'calculate Manhattan distances'); for Rotation, two 512×512 rendered views of the same 3D object (initial and rotated) plus an in-context example pair. The model outputs a chain-of-thought reasoning trace enclosed in  tags followed by structured actions: for Snake, both the best next move and all worst (lethal) moves in dedicated tags; for Rotation, a single rotation angle from a fixed option set. At inference on downstream benchmarks, the same model produces standard reasoning answers to math, geometry, and multi-discipline questions.

## Method

ViGaL post-trains Qwen2.5-VL-7B via rule-based RL (RLOO algorithm, no KL regularization) on two complementary arcade games—Snake (2D grid path planning with collision avoidance) and Rotation (3D object rotation-angle prediction)—using only 36K synthetic samples per game and no SFT warmup. The reward is a simple sum of binary accuracy (correct action) and format compliance, avoiding reward hacking. The key insight is that gameplay incentivizes transferable cognitive operations: Snake develops coordinate tracking and algebraic path planning that boost math subcategories like Expressions and Coordinates, while Rotation develops spatial orientation recognition that boosts geometry questions on angles and lengths. Joint training on both games yields complementary gains across all reasoning benchmarks, outperforming specialist models RL-trained on curated math datasets (e.g., MM-Eureka) while preserving general visual understanding. The approach also generalizes zero-shot to unseen Atari games, confirming that the acquired skills are not overfit to the training games.

## Results

ViGaL Snake+Rotation achieves 53.9% average across math and geometry benchmarks (50.6% Math Avg, 57.1% Geometry Avg), outperforming the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model (46.3%) by 7.6 points and MM-Eureka-Qwen-7B (39.3%) by 14.6 points, despite using no math or geometry data during RL. On the spatial and multi-discipline benchmarks (Table 3), ViGaL Snake+Rotation reaches 64.7% average, surpassing GPT-4o (55.9%) by 8.8 points and MM-Eureka (62.8%) by 1.9 points. The model also generalizes zero-shot to unseen Atari games, nearly doubling cumulative reward (2251 vs. 1253) over the base model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ViGaL Snake+Rotation (53.9% avg) outperforms all seven Qwen2.5-VL-7B-based specialist models on the combined math+geometry benchmark, beating the closest competitor OpenVLThinker-7B (52.1%) by 1.8 points and MM-Eureka-Qwen-7B (39.3%) by 14.6 points. The geometry advantage is particularly striking: ViGaL scores 57.1% vs. MM-Eureka's 28.4% (a 28.7-point gap), despite MM-Eureka being explicitly trained on geometry datasets. On the spatial/multi-discipline benchmarks (Table 3), ViGaL (64.7%) surpasses GPT-4o (55.9%) by 8.8 points and R1-OneVision-7B (53.7%) by 11.0 points on the MMMU series. Against proprietary models, ViGaL exceeds GPT-4o on both math (53.9 vs. 47.5) and multi-discipline (64.7 vs. 55.9) benchmarks, though Gemini-2.0-Flash (55.4%) still leads on the math benchmark. Notably, ViGaL preserves general visual capabilities (72.2% avg on Table 9) comparable to the base model, whereas specialist models like R1-OneVision (66.8% MuirBench) and OpenVLThinker (64.3%) show significant degradation in general perception.

## Relation to Prior Work

ViGaL extends the RL post-training paradigm for MLLMs (DeepSeek-R1, MM-Eureka, R1-OneVision) by replacing domain-specific reasoning data with simple arcade games as surrogate tasks, drawing an explicit analogy to self-supervised pretext tasks in vision (rotation prediction, context prediction). It also connects to the generalist-agent literature (Reed et al., 2022) and RL curriculum work (Baker et al., 2019), but departs from both by transferring learned reasoning *policies* across entirely different task domains (visual control games → symbolic math) rather than within a single environment family or feature space. The key methodological departure from prior MLLM RL work is the use of rule-based rewards on synthetic, fully controllable game environments instead of curated human-annotated reasoning datasets.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a 7B model trained exclusively on Snake and Rotation outperforms specialist models RL-trained on curated math data (e.g., MM-Eureka) on math and multi-discipline benchmarks suggests a scalable, low-cost post-training strategy: synthetic games provide structured rewards, fine-grained difficulty control, and unlimited data generation without human annotation. However, the pass@k analysis reveals an important limitation—RL narrows the model's solution coverage relative to the base model at high sampling budgets, meaning gameplay RL sharpens high-probability reasoning paths rather than expanding the full reasoning boundary. Open questions include whether this paradigm scales to larger models, whether the cognitive-alignment mapping between game mechanics and math subfields generalizes beyond the six games studied, and how to combine surrogate-task RL with domain-specific data to push the pass@k frontier.
