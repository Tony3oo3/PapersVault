## Problem

Slow-thinking reasoning LLMs generate lengthy explicit reasoning trajectories (often 3,500+ tokens) even for simple problems, incurring substantial inference cost. Existing efficiency methods either still require autoregressive generation of shortened reasoning tokens or rely on rigid prompt-based bypasses that sacrifice accuracy. The paper challenges whether full explicit trajectories are necessary at all, showing empirically that models retain high accuracy even when 50% of reasoning tokens are randomly removed.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, extracts its final hidden states from a frozen reasoning LLM, and passes them through a lightweight 0.6B reasoning network to produce a fixed-length sequence of latent vectors (typically 256 tokens) in a single forward pass. These latent vectors are prepended to the input embeddings, and the frozen LLM then autoregressively decodes only the final answer—no intermediate reasoning text is generated. The latent sequence length is a hyperparameter (64–512 tokens) and the approach supports flexible switching between latent and explicit reasoning modes without modifying base-model weights.

## Method

LRT introduces a lightweight reasoning network G_φ (initialized from Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B with input/output projection layers) that maps the base model's hidden states to a compact latent trajectory z via a single forward pass, replacing the autoregressive generation of thousands of reasoning tokens. Training is two-stage: first, SFT minimizes the negative log-likelihood of the correct answer conditioned on [input embeddings, z], aligning the latent representations with the model's original explicit-reasoning behavior; second, GRPO reinforcement learning refines G_φ using answer-correctness rewards with clipped policy updates, encouraging exploration of more effective latent reasoning paths. The base LLM remains entirely frozen throughout, making the approach modular and non-intrusive. A key architectural detail is that the latent vectors are produced by element-wise broadcasting learnable position vectors with the projected hidden states, allowing the network to generate a parallel, fixed-length reasoning representation rather than a sequential one.

## Results

Under a 512-token budget on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, LRT achieves an average accuracy of 44.91% across five benchmarks (AMC, MATH-500, GSM8K, LSAT, GPQA), outperforming the base model (37.93%) by 6.98 points. Under a 1024-token budget, LRT reaches 48.58% average, surpassing the strongest baseline LC-R1 (48.53%) and the base model (47.72%). On Qwen3-4B, LRT improves pass@4 from 65.78% (non-thinking mode) to 71.60%, a 5.82-point gain, while also improving pass@1 from 54.07% to 55.04%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against NoThinking (prompt-based reasoning bypass), LRT wins by 2.16% average under the 512-token budget, with the largest gap on MATH-500 (+2.30%) and GPQA (+5.43%), though NoThinking edges ahead on LSAT by 0.15 points at 1024 tokens. Against ShorterBetter (RL with dynamic length reward), LRT achieves a substantial 8.68% average improvement at 512 tokens, driven primarily by GSM8K (+16.38%) and MATH-500 (+5.54%), where ShorterBetter's length penalty severely degrades performance. Against LC-R1 (RL with length-compression reward), LRT leads by 5.93% average at 512 tokens, with the clearest advantage on MATH-500 (+12.65%) and GPQA (+4.93%). Against Qwen3's native non-thinking mode, LRT consistently improves pass@1 (46.93→48.42 on 1.7B; 54.07→55.04 on 4B) and pass@4 (62.60→66.81 on 1.7B; 65.78→71.60 on 4B), indicating that latent reasoning produces more diverse and accurate solution paths than simply suppressing the thinking token.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two main lines of efficient-reasoning work: (1) RL-based trajectory compression methods (ShorterBetter, LC-R1, O1-Pruner) that still require autoregressive generation of explicit reasoning tokens, and (2) prior latent-reasoning approaches (Coconut, looped transformers) that require retraining or substantial fine-tuning of the base LLM to internalize latent computation. LRT carves new ground by introducing a frozen, lightweight auxiliary network (initialized from Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B) that produces a fixed-length latent trajectory in a single forward pass, leaving the base reasoning model entirely unmodified. The empirical motivation builds on TokenSkip's observation of uneven token importance, but extends it to show that even 50% random token removal causes minimal accuracy loss, justifying the replacement of the entire autoregressive trajectory with a compact latent vector.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The modular, non-intrusive design is practically significant: it allows any existing reasoning LLM to be augmented with latent reasoning without retraining, enabling seamless switching between explicit and latent modes—a capability that hybrid systems like Qwen3 achieve only through rigid prompt-level control. However, the evaluation is concentrated on mathematical reasoning (AMC, MATH-500, GSM8K) with limited out-of-domain coverage (LSAT, GPQA), and the 0.6B reasoning network introduces non-trivial memory overhead (6.5 GB peak vs. 3.9 GB for non-thinking). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-mathematical domains, how it scales to 70B+ models, and whether the latent representations can be made interpretable or steerable for targeted reasoning improvements.
