## Problem

Existing approximation algorithms for W_p optimal transport either achieve only O(log n)-approximation (Lahn et al. 2025) or provide additive guarantees (Sinkhorn) that fail for p=∞ and degrade for large p. No prior method delivers a constant-factor relative approximation in quadratic time that uniformly covers all p ∈ [1,∞], including the W_∞ distance used in topological data analysis and neural network design.

## Input / Output

The algorithm takes two discrete probability distributions μ, ν supported on finite point sets A, B in an arbitrary metric space (|A|+|B|=n), a parameter p ∈ [1,∞], and produces a transport plan σ with w_p(σ) ≤ (4+ε)·W_p(μ,ν). The method is Las Vegas randomized with success probability ≥ 1−1/n, and its runtime depends on the spread Δ (ratio of largest to smallest interpoint distance) and U (ratio of max to min nonzero probability). In the query model, the support set X is preprocessed once in O(n² + kn^{1+1/k} log Δ) time, after which each W_p query between arbitrary distributions on X runs in sub-quadratic time on a sparse directed graph of O(kn^{1+1/k} log Δ) edges.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-level (generalizable to k-level) clustering construction inspired by Bourgain's multi-level sampling that approximates d(·,·)^p — which is not a metric for p>1 — within a factor of (4+ε)^p. Unlike classical undirected spanners, the authors introduce Steiner points at multiple cluster scales and assign direction to each edge, producing a directed spanner of O(n^{3/2} log Δ) edges that guarantees a path from every b∈B to every a∈A. Two algorithmic instantiations follow: (1) a minimum-cost flow on the spanner using the near-linear-time algorithm of Chen et al. (2022), and (2) a combinatorial Gabow-Tarjan cost-scaling algorithm that replaces expensive pairwise distance computations with a dynamic weighted bichromatic closest-pair (BCP) data structure maintained over the clustering. For p=∞, the min-cost flow is replaced by binary search over cluster radii combined with max-flow computations, yielding the first quadratic-time W_∞ matching algorithm.

## Results

The paper achieves a (4+ε)-approximate W_p optimal transport plan in O(n² + (n^{3/2} ε^{-1} log Δ log n)^{1+o(1)} log U) time for all p ∈ [1, ∞], improving the previous best O(log n)-approximation to a constant factor and extending to p = ∞ for the first time in quadratic time. The combinatorial variant (Theorem 1.3) computes a (4+ε)-approximate W_p matching in O(n² ε^{-3/2} log²n log Δ) time. Empirically, the observed approximation ratios are typically 1.5–2 (well below the worst-case 4+ε bound), the average cluster distortion is close to 2, and BCP query counts scale as predicted, confirming near-quadratic runtime in practice.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Lahn et al. (2025), which achieves an O(log n)-approximation for W_p (finite p ≥ 2) in O(n² log U log Δ log n) time using an HST-based proxy distance. This paper improves the approximation factor from O(log n) to a constant (4+ε) and extends the guarantee to p = ∞, which Lahn et al. cannot handle. Empirically, the clustering-based proxy distance (cluster-dist) yields substantially smaller distortion than the HST-based proxy (HST-dist): the average distortion is typically close to 2 versus considerably larger for HST-dist, and the maximum distortion never exceeds the theoretical (4+ε) bound. The optimal matching cost computed under cluster-dist is consistently more than 4.5× smaller than the cost obtained using HST-dist for p = 2 on uniform distributions. The BCP query counts (which dominate runtime) remain nearly identical across all choices of p ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, ∞}, confirming that the algorithm's efficiency is independent of p.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on approximate W_p optimal transport from Charikar's O(log n)-approximation via HST embeddings and Lahn et al.'s (2025) O(log n)-approximation for finite p ≥ 2, improving both to a constant (4+ε) factor and extending the guarantee to p = ∞. The core technical novelty is a directed spanner built on Bourgain's multi-level sampling that approximates d(·,·)^p — a non-metric for p > 1 — by introducing Steiner points at multiple clustering scales and assigning direction to edges, departing fundamentally from classical undirected metric spanners. The combinatorial algorithm instantiates the Gabow-Tarjan cost-scaling framework with a novel dynamic bichromatic closest-pair data structure tailored to the clustering, and the MCF-based variant leverages Chen et al.'s near-linear-time flow algorithm.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first quadratic-time constant-factor approximation for W_p OT that covers all p ∈ [1, ∞], including the previously intractable p = ∞ case where additive methods like Sinkhorn fail entirely. The conditional lower bound (Theorem 1.4) showing that a (2−ε)-approximation in O(n²) time would resolve the long-standing open problem of quadratic-time perfect matching in bipartite graphs provides a strong optimality signal for the approach. The query-model extension yields sub-quadratic per-query time for settings like Word Mover's Distance where many distributions share a fixed support. Limitations include the O(n²) preprocessing cost, reliance on the non-practical Chen et al. MCF solver for the tightest bound, and the (4+ε) worst-case factor (though empirically ~1.5–2).
