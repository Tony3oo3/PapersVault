## Problem

MoE-based LLMs fragment expertise across dozens of experts per layer and encode redundant, overlapping transformations across layers, yet prior work has only diagnosed this redundancy or scored parameter importance without providing a mechanism to extract and reuse the shared structure. The paper asks whether MoE models contain task-agnostic universal knowledge—compact, reusable subspaces—that can be systematically identified and leveraged to build efficient downstream models, analogous to how humans consolidate domain-specific experience into transferable reasoning principles.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained MoE LLM (Mixtral-8×7B, DeepSeek-MoE-16B, or Qwen3-MoE-30B) and a small domain-general calibration set (Wikitext-2, as few as 256 sequences) as input. It produces two outputs: (i) a pair of fixed universal knowledge matrices U_o ∈ R^{d_o×r_o} and U_i ∈ R^{d_i×r_i} representing shared low-rank input/output subspaces, and (ii) a family of reconstructed dense target models (300M–550M parameters) whose depth (2–10 layers) and Tucker rank (128–512) can be independently adjusted. All non-MoE components (token embeddings, self-attention projections, LayerNorms) are copied verbatim from the source model; only the feed-forward modules are reconstructed from the extracted subspaces.

## Method

UNITE operates in three stages. First, it computes Fisher information scores for each expert on the calibration set and fuses experts within every layer via Fisher-weighted averaging, which provably yields the minimum-variance unbiased estimate of the ideal layer transformation compared to uniform averaging. Second, it stacks the L fused expert weight matrices into a third-order tensor W ∈ R^{L×d_o×d_i} and applies Tucker decomposition, separating shared projection bases (U_o, U_i) that define universal subspaces reused across all layers from a small interaction core G and layer-specific coefficients G_l that capture local variation. Third, it reconstructs each target layer's FFN as Ŵ_l = U_o G_l U_i^T, where U_o and U_i remain frozen (the 'learngene') while G_l is randomly initialized and jointly fine-tuned on downstream data. This once-for-all extraction enables flexible target construction: varying the number of layers or the Tucker rank produces models of different sizes that all share the same universal component, without re-extraction or retraining at each scale.

## Results

UNITE-based 2-layer target models (274M–360M parameters) consistently outperform randomly initialized counterparts by +5.5% to +9.5% on ARC-Challenge and by up to +12% on PIQA. Against pretrained baselines of comparable or larger scale, UNITE models achieve superior accuracy on most benchmarks; for example, Mixtral-UNITE (274M) reaches 0.6306 on PIQA versus BERT-Large (340M) at 0.5049. Compared to Delta Decompression compression baselines, UNITE matches or exceeds accuracy with substantially fewer parameters: DeepSeek-UNITE (317M, 0.6289 on PIQA) surpasses its compressed counterpart (990M, 0.6251), and Qwen3-UNITE (360M, 0.4760 on OBQA) matches the 1.31B compressed model (0.4740) using roughly one-fourth the parameters.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against pretrained dense baselines, UNITE models dominate on reasoning tasks: Mixtral-UNITE (274M) exceeds BERT-Large (340M) by +12.6% on PIQA (0.6306 vs. 0.5049) and by +10.8% on ARC-E (0.3645 vs. 0.2567), while also surpassing T5-Base, GPT-2, and XLM-RoBERTa on most benchmarks despite having fewer or comparable parameters. Against Delta Decompression compression baselines, UNITE achieves equal or better accuracy with 3–5× fewer parameters: DeepSeek-UNITE (317M) beats its 990M compressed counterpart on PIQA (0.6289 vs. 0.6251) and ARC-E (0.3970 vs. 0.3615), and Qwen3-UNITE (360M) matches the 1.31B compressed model on OBQA (0.4760 vs. 0.4740) while leading on ARC-C (0.3365 vs. 0.3004) and PIQA (0.6148 vs. 0.5958). The one area where compression baselines retain an edge is Winogrande and RTE for the Mixtral variant (0.5320/0.5343 vs. 0.5233/0.5668), though UNITE still leads on RTE. Overall, UNITE wins or ties on 6 out of 7 benchmarks for each source architecture when compared to its corresponding compression baseline, and decisively outperforms all four pretrained dense models on at least 5 of 7 tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

UNITE builds on two well-established tools—Fisher information for parameter sensitivity (LeCun et al., 1989; Martens, 2020) and Tucker tensor decomposition (Kolda & Bader, 2009)—but combines them in a new pipeline specifically targeting MoE architectures. It departs from prior MoE compression work (Delta Decompression, MONE) that focuses on pruning or distillation, and from parameter-importance methods (lottery ticket hypothesis, gradient sensitivity) that identify but do not extract reusable structures. The paper also extends the Learngene/Learnware paradigm, which previously targeted dense CNNs and ViTs at layer/block granularity, to the fine-grained, cross-layer, expert-level setting of MoE LLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UNITE offers a principled 'once-for-all' extraction that enables flexible target model construction at varying depths without retraining, achieving 26–73× inference speedup over source MoE models while matching or exceeding compressed baselines. However, the 'universal knowledge' framing is somewhat overstated: the extracted component is essentially a structured low-rank initialization, and its transferability is validated only on seven NLP benchmarks from three source models. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to non-MoE architectures, whether the universal subspaces are truly task-agnostic or merely a good pre-training prior, and how the method scales to trillion-parameter MoE models where even the one-time Fisher computation becomes prohibitive.
