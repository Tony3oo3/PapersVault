## Problem

One-step diffusion models for real-world video super-resolution (e.g., DOVE, SeedVR2) achieve high quality but remain too heavy for practical use, with billions of parameters and multi-second latency. Existing adversarial diffusion compression (ADC) methods designed for image SR cannot be directly extended to video because they lack temporal modeling, and standard single-head adversarial learning fails to balance the conflicting objectives of spatial detail richness and temporal consistency, leading to either flickering or over-smoothed outputs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265080/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a low-resolution video clip (25 frames, 512×512 spatial resolution) degraded by real-world factors (noise, blur, JPEG/H.264 compression, etc.) and produces a 4× upscaled high-resolution video in a single forward pass. The LR video is fed into a pruned SD2.1 UNet with inserted 1D temporal convolutions, and the output is decoded by a pruned VAE decoder, yielding a temporally consistent HR video without iterative denoising steps.

## Method

AdcVSR adopts a '2D + 1D' architecture: a channel-pruned SD2.1 UNet (25% pruned) and VAE decoder (50% pruned) for spatial detail synthesis, augmented with zero-initialized 1D temporal convolutional residual blocks (kernel size 3) after each UNet block to enforce inter-frame coherence, replacing the heavy 3D spatio-temporal DiT teacher DOVE. Training proceeds in two stages: first, 200K iterations of error-minimizing distillation (L1 + DISTS in pixel domain, L1 in VAE feature domain) from DOVE; second, 200K iterations of dual-head, dual-discriminator adversarial distillation where two discriminators (one on pixel space using a frozen ConvNeXt backbone, one on VAE feature space using a frozen SD UNet) each branch into separate 'detail' and 'consistency' linear heads. Five curated data types with head-specific labels (real videos, temporally shuffled videos, repeated detail-rich images, and randomly cropped image sequences) provide disentangled supervision, enabling the student to simultaneously optimize spatial realism and temporal coherence without one objective collapsing the other.

## Results

On UDM10 (synthetic), AdcVSR achieves the best temporal consistency (E*warp = 1.67) and video quality (DOVER = 0.4878) among all compared methods, while remaining competitive on perceptual metrics (CLIPIQA = 0.6818, MUSIQ = 63.88) and fidelity (PSNR = 25.36, SSIM = 0.7697). On the real-world VideoLQ benchmark, AdcVSR again attains the lowest warping error (6.74) and highest DOVER (0.4319), with strong no-reference scores (CLIPIQA = 0.6024, MUSIQ = 64.55). In terms of efficiency, AdcVSR uses only 0.57B parameters and 0.55 s inference time for a 25-frame 512×512 video, representing a 95% parameter reduction and 8× speedup over its teacher DOVE (10.55B params, 4.42 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against its teacher DOVE, AdcVSR trades a small fidelity gap (PSNR 25.36 vs 26.00, LPIPS 0.3065 vs 0.2645) for substantially better temporal consistency (E*warp 1.67 vs 2.22) and video quality (DOVER 0.4878 vs 0.4731), while using only 5.4% of the parameters and running 8× faster. Compared to SeedVR2, AdcVSR wins decisively on temporal consistency (E*warp 1.67 vs 2.56), DOVER (0.4878 vs 0.3296), CLIPIQA (0.6818 vs 0.3471), and MUSIQ (63.88 vs 50.09), at the cost of slightly lower PSNR (25.36 vs 25.92). Against the Real-ISR baselines PiSA-SR, AdcSR, and HYPIR, AdcVSR achieves dramatically better temporal consistency (E*warp 1.67 vs 6.96, 6.19, 10.68) and higher DOVER, while remaining competitive on perceptual quality (CLIPIQA 0.6818 vs 0.7055, 0.6693, 0.6006). Versus multi-step diffusion methods (Upscale-A-Video, MGLD-VSR, STAR), AdcVSR outperforms on nearly all perceptual and temporal metrics while being 59–175× faster. On the real-world VideoLQ set, AdcVSR achieves the best E*warp (6.74) and DOVER (0.4319), surpassing even DOVE (8.41, 0.3790) and all other baselines, confirming that the dual-head adversarial distillation effectively transfers temporal coherence to unseen degradation distributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends AdcSR (Chen et al., 2025a), which compressed one-step Real-ISR diffusion networks via channel pruning and adversarial distillation, to the video setting by augmenting the pruned 2D SD2.1 backbone with lightweight 1D temporal convolutions. It departs from prior one-step Real-VSR methods (SeedVR2, DOVE, DLoRAL) that rely on heavy 3D spatio-temporal DiT architectures, hypothesizing that the LR input already provides most structural and temporal information, making full 3D attention largely redundant. The key methodological departure is a dual-head, dual-discriminator adversarial distillation scheme that explicitly disentangles detail richness from temporal consistency—addressing a conflict that prior single-head adversarial approaches (including the original ADC) cannot resolve when applied to video.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 95% parameter reduction and 8× speedup over DOVE while maintaining competitive video quality makes high-quality Real-VSR substantially more deployable in practice, and the '2D+1D' architectural finding provides a practical recipe for compressing 3D DiT video models without sacrificing temporal coherence. The dual-head discriminator design offers a generalizable principle for handling competing objectives in generative video tasks. Open questions remain: whether the approach transfers to other video restoration tasks (deblurring, denoising), whether stronger generative priors or more sophisticated temporal modeling (beyond kernel-3 1D convs) could close the remaining small gap to the 3D teacher, and how sensitive the method is to training data quality under distribution shift.
