## Problem

Existing Olympiad-level math benchmarks are limited in scale, language coverage (mostly English/Chinese), and task diversity, and no prior work systematically evaluates whether embedding models can retrieve mathematically equivalent or structurally related problems. MATHNET addresses both gaps by providing the largest multilingual, multimodal Olympiad corpus and the first dedicated benchmark for math-aware retrieval.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260425/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For Problem Solving, models receive a problem statement in LaTeX text and optionally a figure image (for multimodal models); for text-only models, a textual description of the figure is supplied instead. For Math-Aware Retrieval, each of 10,000 anchor problems is scored against 40,000 synthetic candidates (1 equivalent positive + 3 hard negatives per anchor) using cosine similarity over embedding vectors. For Retrieval-Augmented Problem Solving, the model receives the target problem plus one retrieved problem with its official solution (either from gemini-embedding-001 or an expert-curated pair). Outputs are generated solutions graded on a 0–7 scale by GPT-5 (binarized at ≥6), ranked retrieval lists scored by Recall@1/5/10, and per-problem correctness under both LLM and human expert grading.

## Method

The dataset is built from 1,595 official PDF booklets spanning 47 countries and 143 competitions (1985–2025), parsed via the multilingual dots-ocr framework, then aligned through a three-stage LLM pipeline: Gemini-2.5-Flash identifies problem/solution line segments, GPT-4.1 extracts LaTeX-formatted pairs, and a three-layer verification (rule-based similarity check, GPT-4.1 screenshot comparison, human review) retains only unanimously validated pairs. For the retrieval benchmark, GPT-5 generates one algebraically equivalent positive and three adversarial hard negatives per anchor, designed to defeat surface-lexical matching. The RAG benchmark uses 35 expert-curated pairs of real Olympiad problems linked by structural resonance (shared lemmas, generalizations, or reductions) rather than simple reformulation. A fine-grained taxonomy of mathematical similarity—Invariance (strict equivalence), Resonance (shared strategy), and Affinity (thematic relatedness)—grounds the retrieval evaluation and distinguishes it from standard semantic search.

## Results

On Problem Solving (MathNet-Solve, 6,400 problems), Gemini-3.1-Pro achieves the highest overall accuracy at 78.4%, followed by Gemini-3-Flash (70.4%) and GPT-5 (69.3%), while the weakest model (Ministral-3B) scores only 4.4%. On Math-Aware Retrieval (MathNet-Retrieve, 10,000 anchors), even the best embedding models (Gemini-embedding-001, Qwen3-embedding-4B) achieve only ~5% Recall@1, though Recall@5 reaches 68.9% and 65.0% respectively. On Retrieval-Augmented Problem Solving (MathNet-RAG, 35 problems), DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale reaches 97.3% under Expert-RAG with human grading, and GPT-5 improves from 76.8% (Zero Shot) to 86.6% (Expert-RAG).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On Problem Solving, Gemini-3.1-Pro (78.4%) outperforms GPT-5 (69.3%) by 9.1 points and Gemini-3-Flash (70.4%) by 8.0 points overall, with the largest gaps in Geometry (74.6% vs. 61.1% for GPT-5) and Discrete Math (75.6% vs. 65.3%). Among non-reasoning models, GPT-4o (6.8%) and Ministral-3B (4.4%) trail far behind even mid-tier reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1 (36.3%), highlighting a sharp capability divide. On Math-Aware Retrieval, modern embeddings (Gemini-embedding-001 at 68.9% R@5, Qwen3-embedding-4B at 65.0%) substantially outperform legacy models (text-embedding-ada-002 at 42.0%, text-embedding-3-small at 35.5%), yet all remain far below the ~80%+ R@10 levels needed for reliable retrieval. On Retrieval-Augmented Problem Solving, DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale (97.3% Expert-RAG, human grading) surpasses GPT-5 (86.6%) and Gemini-3-Pro (87.5%), while Embed-RAG provides inconsistent gains—sometimes falling below Zero Shot (e.g., Grok-4.1-Fast drops from 75.4% to 83.8% under human grading but from 73.1% to 67.7% under LLM grading). The key finding is that retrieval quality is the bottleneck: Expert-RAG consistently outperforms Embed-RAG, confirming that embedding-based retrievers fail to surface structurally aligned problems.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of mathematical benchmarking and information retrieval. While prior Olympiad-level datasets (OlympiadBench, Omni-MATH, IMOBench) focus on problem-solving evaluation from a handful of countries and languages, MATHNET scales to 47 countries, 17 languages, and 30K+ problems sourced exclusively from official national booklets. On the retrieval side, the paper departs from both formula-level indexing (Zanibbi et al., 2025) and general-purpose semantic retrieval (DPR, ColBERT), introducing a formal taxonomy of mathematical similarity (Invariance, Resonance, Affinity) and a dedicated evaluation protocol that tests whether embeddings can distinguish true mathematical equivalence from superficial lexical overlap. The RAG component extends standard retrieval-augmented generation to the mathematical domain, where the retrieved context must be structurally aligned rather than merely topically related.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a critical asymmetry: frontier LLMs achieve ~78% on Olympiad problem solving, yet the best embedding models retrieve mathematically equivalent problems at only ~5% Recall@1, and non-equivalent pairs often score higher cosine similarity than truly equivalent ones. This gap has direct implications for any RAG pipeline targeting STEM reasoning, suggesting that current dense retrieval is fundamentally misaligned with mathematical structure. Open questions include whether math-specific pretraining or symbolic-augmented embeddings can close this gap, whether the RAG gains observed with expert-paired problems (up to 12% for DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale) can be replicated with learned retrievers, and how the benchmark's reliance on GPT-5-generated synthetic variants for the retrieval split may constrain generalizability. The 35-problem RAG evaluation also limits statistical confidence in the retrieval-augmented findings.
