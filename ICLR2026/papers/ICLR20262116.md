## Problem

Stochastic Interpolants (SI) provide a flexible framework for transforming between arbitrary probability distributions, but they require direct access to samples from both the prior and target distributions, making them incompatible with jointly optimized latent variable models where the latent space co-evolves with the encoder and decoder. This limitation forces practitioners to either use fixed latent representations that may be misaligned with the generative process, or apply SI directly in high-dimensional observation space at significant computational cost.

## Input / Output

At generation time, the model takes a sample z_0 from a (potentially non-Gaussian) prior distribution and an optional class label c, then iteratively applies the learned latent drift h_θ(z_t, t) to produce a latent z_1, which is decoded into an RGB image. Experiments use ImageNet at 64×64, 128×128, and 256×256 resolutions with class-conditional generation. During training, the encoder consumes the observed image x_1 to produce a stochastic latent z_1, while the decoder p_θ(x_1|z_1) is a conditional Gaussian yielding a simple L2 reconstruction loss.

## Method

LSI derives a continuous-time ELBO via Girsanov's theorem for two SDEs sharing the same dispersion coefficient, yielding a path-space KL that decomposes into an initial-distribution KL and a dynamics-mismatch integral. The key construction is a variational posterior built from a diffusion bridge between the prior p_0(z_0) and the encoder-defined aggregated posterior, which—under a linear SDE assumption—gives a closed-form Gaussian conditional p(z_t|z_1, z_0) enabling simulation-free reparameterized sampling of z_t = η_t ε + κ_t z_1 + ν_t z_0. The resulting training objective (eq. 17) combines a reconstruction term −ln p_θ(x_1|z_1) with a flow-matching-like term ‖σ√(t/(1−t)) ε + z_1 − z_0 − h_θ(z_t,t)‖², jointly optimizing encoder, decoder, and latent drift in a single loss. The InterpFlow parameterization (eq. 19) rewrites the target to avoid high-variance 1/√(1−t) terms, and a time-resampling schedule t(s)=1−(1−s)^c provides implicit reweighting. Sampling uses a flexible family of SDEs (eq. 20) parameterized by γ_t to interpolate between deterministic probability-flow ODE and fully stochastic sampling, with classifier-free guidance applied to the latent drift.

## Results

On ImageNet, LSI achieves FID of 2.62 (64×64), 3.12 (128×128), and 3.91 (256×256) at 2000 epochs, matching or exceeding observation-space SI models of comparable size. At 128×128, LSI outperforms the observation-space baseline by 0.34 FID (3.12 vs. 3.46). The latent formulation also yields substantial sampling efficiency gains: with 100 sampling steps, FLOPs are reduced by 73.6% at 128×128 and 48.6% at 256×256, since the lightweight latent model (382M params) is run repeatedly while the encoder is unused and the decoder is invoked only once.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is observation-space Stochastic Interpolants (Albergo et al., 2023), trained directly in pixel space with a comparable architecture and parameter count. LSI achieves competitive FID across all three resolutions: it wins at 128×128 (3.12 vs. 3.46, a 0.34 FID improvement) and is within 0.05 FID at both 64×64 (2.62 vs. 2.57) and 256×256 (3.91 vs. 3.87). The key advantage is computational: because the latent model L (382M params) is the only component run at each of the 100+ sampling steps, while the encoder is unused and the decoder is called once, LSI reduces per-sample FLOPs by 73.6% at 128×128 and 48.6% at 256×256 relative to the observation-space model. The paper also shows that joint training (β > 0) is beneficial over independent encoder-decoder training (β → 0), yielding a 17% FID improvement at 128×128 (3.75 vs. 4.53) and better robustness to capacity shifts between components.

## Relation to Prior Work

LSI extends Stochastic Interpolants (Albergo et al., 2023) from observation space to jointly learned latent spaces, addressing the core limitation that SI requires both endpoint distributions to be fixed and directly observed. The theoretical foundation draws on the continuous-time ELBO for dynamic latent variables (Li et al., 2020; Theodorou, 2015) and the diffusion bridge construction via Doob's h-transform to build a simulation-free variational posterior. Unlike LDM (Rombach et al., 2022), which trains a diffusion model in the latent space of a *fixed* encoder-decoder pair, LSI jointly optimizes all three components end-to-end. The approach also differs from flow matching and rectified flow methods, which lack the likelihood control that the ELBO formulation provides, making joint latent-space training ill-specified in those frameworks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LSI provides a principled, single-objective framework for jointly learning encoder, decoder, and generative process in latent space, sidestepping the simple Gaussian prior constraint of standard diffusion models while reducing sampling FLOPs by up to ~74% at 128×128. The empirical finding that joint training (β>0) yields a ~17% FID improvement over independent encoder-decoder training, and that capacity can be shifted from the latent model to encoder/decoder without significant degradation, offers practical guidance for model design. A key limitation is the reliance on linear SDE assumptions for simulation-free sampling, which the authors note do not appear to hurt empirical performance but remain theoretically restrictive. Open questions include whether the framework scales to higher resolutions (512×512+), whether the InterpFlow parameterization advantage generalizes beyond ImageNet, and whether the approach extends to other modalities or conditional generation settings.
