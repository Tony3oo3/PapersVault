## Problem

Existing hybrid reasoning systems that dynamically choose between short and extended reasoning modes rely on fixed hyperparameters (e.g., a static α weight) that cannot adapt to varying task complexities, and their single-objective or fixed-weight multi-objective formulations constrain optimization to narrow cone-shaped regions of the objective space. This leads to mode collapse, dataset-specific tuning requirements, and suboptimal accuracy-efficiency trade-offs that no single static configuration can resolve across diverse problem types.

## Input / Output

The model receives a text query (e.g., a math word problem, commonsense question, or medical exam question) and first generates a learned control token (<short> or <think>) that selects the reasoning mode, followed by the full text response. At inference, this mode selection incurs zero additional computational overhead beyond the single control token, and the response length naturally adapts—short mode produces concise direct answers while think mode generates extended chain-of-thought reasoning. Evaluation reports Pass@1 accuracy and average tokens per query across benchmarks.

## Method

MAGO reformulates hybrid reasoning training as a three-objective optimization problem (accuracy, efficiency, and decision calibration) and replaces the fixed α weight in DeGRPO with a dynamically selected weight vector drawn from a maintained Pareto frontier of non-dominated configurations. A correlation-aware scoring function computes the empirical correlation matrix between objectives on each training batch and penalizes unbalanced weight allocations when objectives are highly correlated, preventing the cone-entrapment that plagues fixed-weight approaches. The calibration objective uses exponentially-decayed historical accuracy statistics binned by confidence level to measure whether the model's mode choice aligns with its actual capability on that input. An exploration mechanism generates new candidate weight vectors via guided perturbation scaled by current frontier diversity, and the frontier is pruned to maintain at most 30 diverse solutions. Training proceeds with GRPO-based reinforcement learning using a minimal reward that favors correct short responses, with the entire multi-objective machinery operating only during training and adding no inference-time parameters.

## Results

On the 1.5B backbone, MAGO achieves 2.2×–3× token-efficiency gains over the strongest base model (DeepSeek-R1-1.5B) across all four math benchmarks (e.g., 7,164 vs. 18,063 tokens on AIME; 633 vs. 1,919 on GSM-8K), while delivering 0.6%–9.4% relative accuracy improvements over the closest hybrid baseline DeGRPO. Specifically, MAGO reaches 0.2741 Pass@1 on AIME 2024, 0.9483 on Minerva Algebra, 0.8247 on MATH-500, and 0.8469 on GSM-8K. Cross-domain evaluation on CommonsenseQA shows MAGO attains 74.9% accuracy with 152 tokens/query, a 2.05× efficiency gain over DeGRPO (73.1%, 312 tokens). Scaling to 7B/14B/32B backbones yields consistent Pass@1 gains (e.g., AIME: 0.2960 → 0.3112 → 0.3254) with slightly decreasing token usage.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the strongest base model DeepSeek-R1-1.5B, MAGO-1.5B trades a small accuracy margin (e.g., 0.2741 vs. 0.2800 on AIME, 0.8247 vs. 0.8608 on MATH-500) for 2.2×–3× fewer tokens per query, while actually surpassing it on GSM-8K (0.8469 vs. 0.8347). Compared to the closest hybrid baseline DeGRPO (fixed α=0.001), MAGO wins on all four math benchmarks in accuracy (e.g., +2.35% relative on AIME, +2.67% on Minerva) with comparable or slightly lower token counts. Shortened-CoT methods (Model Merging, CoT-Valve) are dominated by MAGO on both accuracy and efficiency: the best Merging variant (0.7) reaches only 0.1667 on AIME with 15,854 tokens, while CoT-Valve at any α setting lags MAGO by 4–12% relative accuracy on AIME. Router-based approaches (Random, Qwen-7B) underperform MAGO by 8–14% relative on AIME and 15–20% on MATH-500, confirming that learned routing without multi-objective optimization is insufficient. On the cross-domain CommonsenseQA benchmark, MAGO (74.9%, 152 tokens) outperforms both DeGRPO (73.1%, 312 tokens) and CoT-Valve (73.8%, 298 tokens) in accuracy and achieves a 2.05× token reduction, demonstrating transferability without fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGO extends the hybrid reasoning training paradigm established by GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) and DeGRPO/Thinkless (Fang et al., 2025), which introduced a fixed scalar weight α to balance mode-selection loss against response accuracy. The key departure is replacing this single static hyperparameter with a multi-objective Pareto optimization framework (drawing on NSGA-II, Deb et al., 2002) that simultaneously optimizes accuracy, efficiency, and calibration objectives with dynamically adapted weights. This positions MAGO as a principled upgrade over both fixed-weight RL (DeGRPO) and router-based approaches (RouteLLM), shifting the problem from 'find the right α' to 'maintain a diverse set of non-dominated weight configurations that adapt to training progress and task characteristics.'

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of manual hyperparameter tuning for hybrid reasoning is practically significant for deployment, as it removes the need for dataset-specific α searches that scale linearly with the number of target domains. The cross-domain transfer (math-trained model generalizing to CommonsenseQA and MedQA without fine-tuning) suggests the learned mode-selection policy captures transferable reasoning-strategy priors rather than domain-specific heuristics. Open questions include whether the three-objective formulation generalizes to richer mode spaces (e.g., multiple reasoning depths), how the Pareto frontier behaves under distribution shift at inference time, and whether the training-time overhead of frontier maintenance becomes prohibitive at larger scales or with more objectives.
