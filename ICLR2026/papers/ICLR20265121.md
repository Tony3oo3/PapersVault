## Problem

Existing KV cache compression methods for LLMs focus on selecting important tokens for the current decoding step but treat attention outputs as fixed once computed. During long generation, approximation errors from missing KV entries recursively accumulate in hidden states over hundreds or thousands of decoding steps, causing substantial quality degradation that grows with output length. The paper asks: how can we mitigate these cumulative attention errors during long generation without increasing the KV cache budget?

## Input / Output

The method operates on a text prompt and a paged KV cache (organized into fixed-size pages with per-page K_min/K_max digests for Quest-style scoring). Beyond the standard generated token, RetroAttention produces updated KV entries for past tokens in each deeper layer, overwriting stale Key and Value vectors that were computed from approximate attention outputs. It also maintains a lightweight output cache of shape (w-1, B, L, D) and a per-head, per-page retrospective mask tracking the last decoding step each page was loaded, both of which are independent of sequence length.

## Method

RetroAttention builds on Quest's query-aware non-eviction page selection and introduces two key mechanisms: (1) a supplementary attention output that computes attention between past Queries and currently loaded but previously unseen KV pages, and (2) a lightweight output cache that stores attention outputs from the last w-1 decoding steps to enable efficient merging. The original and supplementary outputs are combined via a weighted linear combination using softmax normalizers (analogous to FlashAttention's partial-softmax aggregation), producing an updated output that approximates attention over the union of all seen KV pages. Updated outputs propagate to deeper layers where they generate revised QKV projections that overwrite stale KV cache entries, so all subsequent decoding steps automatically benefit from the correction. A per-head retrospective mask prevents redundant supplementation of already-attended pages. The arithmetic intensity analysis shows the additional computation remains memory-bound (AI ≈ w·h_q/h_k), so the end-to-end latency overhead is under 1 ms/token for w=2 and ~2 ms for w=8, independent of context length.

## Results

On LONGGENBENCH (n=30 questions, relative KV budget 0.15), RetroAttention achieves mean accuracy of 56.5% on GSM8K, 55.3% on MMLU, and 60.3% on CSQA, outperforming Quest (52.6%, 54.8%, 56.8%) by +3.9, +0.5, and +3.5 percentage points respectively. The method increases effective KV exposure by up to 1.6× without increasing the actual KV cache budget, while incurring only 1.6–3.0% additional memory traffic and <1 ms/token latency overhead (w=2). On reasoning-intensive tasks (AIME 2024, GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench-V5), RetroAttention gains +5.4 points over Quest on AIME 2024 (39.2 vs 33.8) and +1.4 on LiveCodeBench (34.1 vs 32.7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RetroAttention consistently outperforms Quest, the primary SOTA non-eviction baseline, across all three LONGGENBENCH datasets, with gains growing as generation length increases (e.g., +6.8 pp on GSM8K and +6.9 pp on CSQA at n=45). Eviction-based baselines StreamingLLM and TOVA fail catastrophically (near 0% on GSM8K, <10% on MMLU/CSQA) because they permanently discard KV entries that become relevant to later questions in the concatenated prompt. Against ArkVale (another non-eviction method), RetroAttention improves CSQA from 66.2% to 69.0%, MMLU from 57.3% to 58.2%, and GSM8K from 56.2% to 61.7% at n=30. On reasoning-intensive tasks, RetroAttention matches Quest on GPQA-Diamond (33.6%) but leads on AIME 2024 (+5.4 pp) and LiveCodeBench-V5 (+1.4 pp). The method also generalizes to larger models, achieving +7.4 pp over Quest on CSQA with Qwen2.5-14B and +4.3 pp with Qwen2.5-32B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Quest's query-aware paged sparse attention (K_min/K_max page scoring and top-k selection) as its KV cache selection backbone, but departs fundamentally from the entire KV compression literature by introducing a temporal dimension: rather than treating each attention output as fixed once computed, RetroAttention retrospectively revises past attention outputs using KV entries that arrive in subsequent decoding steps. This contrasts with eviction-based methods (StreamingLLM, TOVA, H2O) that permanently discard entries, and with concurrent non-eviction approaches (Quest, ArkVale, InfLLM) that select top-k pages per step but never correct prior approximations. The closest concurrent work (Sun et al., 2025) also targets cumulative sparse-attention errors but proposes periodic dense-attention refresh, which is far more expensive than RetroAttention's lightweight output-cache mechanism.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As reasoning models and multi-turn agents drive generation lengths beyond 10k tokens, the cumulative attention error problem identified here becomes a first-order bottleneck that existing KV compression methods silently ignore. The practical impact is strong: a drop-in module (output cache of size (w-1)·B·L·D, independent of sequence length) that adds <2ms/token overhead while recovering up to 21.9% accuracy over Quest, with gains scaling with generation length. Key limitations include saturation beyond w≈8, reduced benefit for short-generation tasks (<100 tokens), and the assumption that the full KV cache fits in GPU DRAM (constraining batch size). Open questions include interaction with KV quantization, multi-GPU/offloading settings, and whether the retrospective window can be made adaptive per-layer or per-head.
