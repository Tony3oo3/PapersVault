## Problem

Generative models for multi-agent systems typically treat continuous trajectories and discrete state-altering events (e.g., ball possession) as separate processes, producing physically implausible scenes where passes and possessor interactions are incoherent. No prior diffusion framework jointly models both modalities for temporally evolving systems, and existing evaluation relies on individual-level metrics (min ADE/FDE) that miss scene-level coherence critical to team sports.

## Input / Output

The model consumes a [T, N, 7] input tensor formed by concatenating noisy states (2D coordinates + 1D event indicator), observed states, and a binary mask, plus an optional guidance signal encoded into a conditioning tensor G ∈ R^{L×d} (T5-Base text embeddings at d=768, or one-hot possessor sequences at d=N). It outputs a complete scene: multi-agent 2D trajectories Y ∈ R^{T×N×2} and a synchronous one-hot possession matrix E ∈ {0,1}^{T×N} where index 0 denotes the ball (no possessor) and indices 1–N−1 denote players. Scenes span 30–50 timesteps with 11–23 agents depending on the sport (NBA, NFL, Bundesliga).

## Method

JointDiff factorizes the forward diffusion into independent Gaussian noising on trajectories and multinomial diffusion on events (mixing one-hot vectors toward a uniform categorical), then learns a single denoising network with two heads: a regression head predicting ε_θ and a classification head predicting the original event distribution π_θ. The backbone uses Social-Temporal Blocks (bidirectional Temporal Mamba for per-agent dynamics + Social Transformers for inter-agent interactions), and a novel CrossGuid module injects conditioning via multi-head attention between the Mamba and Transformer stages, with distinct query/key-value constructions for weak-possessor-guidance (ball-only query, learnable agent embeddings as K/V) versus text-guidance (all-agent queries, T5-encoded text as K/V). Training minimizes a weighted joint loss L_simple^Y + 0.1·L_vb^E with classifier-free guidance (25% condition dropout), and inference uses a hybrid sampler: DDIM with skip ζ=5 for continuous trajectories (S=50 steps) and multinomial posterior sampling for discrete events (S^d=10 steps aligned via ceiling mapping), yielding 11 total denoising steps.

## Results

On future generation, JointDiff achieves state-of-the-art average scene-level errors across all three datasets: SADE 2.36/3.40 (NFL), 2.47/3.66 (Bundesliga), and 1.39/2.01 (NBA), with corresponding SFDE of 5.53/8.40, 5.02/8.29, and 2.53/3.95 (min/avg over 20 modes). In imputation generation, JointDiff further improves over U2Diff by 12–15% in SADE (e.g., 0.84/1.03 vs. 0.96/1.19 on NFL). In controllable generation with text guidance, JointDiff reaches 2.19/3.09 SADE and 0.86/0.74 possessor accuracy on NFL, outperforming both its non-joint variant and the WPG-only condition.

## Baselines & Comparisons

JointDiff achieves the best average SADE and SFDE across all three datasets (NFL, Bundesliga, NBA) in future generation, outperforming the closest diffusion-based competitor U2Diff by 8–10% in avg SADE (e.g., 3.40 vs. 3.74 on NFL) and the flow-matching method MoFlow by 15% in avg SFDE on NFL (8.40 vs. 9.98). In min-metric comparisons, non-IID methods like MART and MoFlow retain a slight edge on NBA (1.52 vs. 1.39 SADE), but JointDiff's IID sampling yields substantially better distributional coverage. In imputation, JointDiff clearly surpasses U2Diff (0.84 vs. 0.96 min SADE on NFL) and the CVAE-based Sports-Traj (0.84 vs. 2.28), which suffers from near-identical mode collapse. A human evaluation on NBA confirms that JointDiff is preferred over MoFlow (80%), U2Diff (65%), and its own non-joint ablation (53%), while the paper demonstrates that widely-used min ADE/FDE metrics do not correlate with human preference, motivating the use of scene-level SADE/SFDE. In controllable generation, JointDiff with text guidance (2.19/3.09 SADE, 0.86/0.74 Acc on NFL) outperforms both its non-joint variant (2.33/3.39, 0.80/0.63) and the WPG-only condition (2.29/3.26, 0.84/0.65), validating that joint continuous-discrete modeling improves controllability.

## Relation to Prior Work

JointDiff extends the U2Diff architecture (Capellera et al., 2025) by coupling its Social-Temporal Blocks with a joint continuous-discrete diffusion process, combining DDPM-style Gaussian noising for trajectories with multinomial diffusion (Hoogeboom et al., 2021) for possession events. While prior joint continuous-discrete diffusion work (DLT, SketchDNN, Dual Diffusion) addressed static tasks like layout or image-text generation, this paper is the first to apply the paradigm to temporally evolving multi-agent systems. For controllability, it departs from prior guided trajectory diffusion (MotionDiffuser, Trace & Pace) that targets individual-level constraints (waypoints, speeds) by introducing scene-level semantic control via classifier-free guidance over discrete event sequences and natural language, mediated by the novel CrossGuid module.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The joint formulation addresses a real gap: models that generate trajectories without simultaneously modeling possession events produce physically implausible ball-player interactions, and the consistency analysis (Table 3) quantifies this advantage. The finding that multinomial diffusion substantially outperforms the more commonly used absorbing-state formulation in dynamic domains challenges the default choice in the joint diffusion literature and has implications beyond sports. Open questions include extending to sparse events (temporal point processes), scaling to higher-dimensional outputs (e.g., steering video synthesis), and whether the CrossGuid mechanism generalizes to other multi-agent domains like autonomous driving or robotics.
