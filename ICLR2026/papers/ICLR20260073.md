## Problem

Graphon-based network estimation methods (neighborhood smoothing, sort-and-smooth, USVT, ICE) all depend critically on a tuning parameter that controls the bias-variance trade-off, yet no principled cross-validation procedure exists for selecting it on network data. Standard node-level CV violates edge independence, and the existing edge-sampling CV (ECV) requires iterative matrix completion that is computationally prohibitive (O(n³) per fold) and assumes a low-rank probability matrix, limiting its applicability to dense or full-rank graphons.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an n×n binary adjacency matrix of an undirected graph and a finite grid of candidate hyperparameters M for a chosen graphon estimator (NS, SAS, USVT, or ICE). It outputs the selected optimal hyperparameter, the full n×n estimated edge-probability matrix P̂, and per-pair predicted link probabilities that can be ranked for link prediction. The procedure is model-agnostic: the same CV-imputation score applies identically regardless of which underlying estimator is being tuned, and the imputation parameter θ is fixed a priori rather than tuned.

## Method

The core mechanism is a K-fold cross-validation with random Bernoulli imputation: node pairs are partitioned into K folds, and for each fold the validation edges in the training adjacency matrix are replaced by independent Bernoulli(θ) draws rather than being removed. This preserves the network's degree structure and neighborhood topology, avoiding the distributional shift and topological distortion caused by edge deletion. Because the imputed training matrix has a known affine relationship to the true probability matrix (P^{[-k]} = w_k·θ·11ᵀ + (1−w_k)·P), the estimator's output is corrected via a simple rescaling to recover an unbiased predictor of P. Theoretically, the paper proves the CV-imputation score is asymptotically parallel to the true MSE under a polynomial optimism-bias condition, guaranteeing rank-consistent model selection. Computationally, each fold adds only O(n²) imputation and transformation cost, in contrast to ECV's O(n³) matrix-completion overhead per fold, yielding substantial speedups on large networks.

## Results

On synthetic graphon networks (n=200), CV-imputation consistently selects hyperparameters yielding lower MSE than ECV across all four estimation methods (NS, USVT, SAS, ICE) and all four graphon designs. For example, with the NS estimator on Graphon 1, CV-imputation achieves MSE 0.51±0.07 versus ECV's 9.15±19.25 and the default's 39.05±3.33. On real-world networks, CV-imputation achieves AUC 0.88±0.01 on PolBlog (vs. ECV's 0.80±0.02) and 0.72±0.01 on NetSci (vs. 0.70±0.01), while matching ECV on Yeast (0.80±0.02). Computationally, CV-imputation is 4.5× faster on PolBlog (56.9s vs. 258.7s), 15× faster on NetSci (51.0s vs. 771.2s), and 25× faster on Yeast (240.9s vs. 6021.1s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is ECV (Edge Cross-Validation with matrix completion, Li et al. 2020a), which imputes held-out edges via a matrix completion algorithm requiring a low-rank assumption. CV-imputation outperforms ECV on MSE for 15 out of 16 estimator–graphon combinations in Table 1, with the largest gains on NS (e.g., 0.51 vs. 9.15 on Graphon 1) and USVT (e.g., 0.28 vs. 0.60 on Graphon 1); the gap is smallest for SAS where both methods are near-equivalent (e.g., 1.69 vs. 1.72 on Graphon 1). On real networks (Table 2), CV-imputation achieves higher AUC on PolBlog (0.88 vs. 0.80) and NetSci (0.72 vs. 0.70) while tying on Yeast (0.80 vs. 0.80). Computationally, CV-imputation is dramatically faster—4.5× on PolBlog, 15× on NetSci, and 25× on Yeast—because it replaces the O(n³) SVD-based matrix completion in each fold with a simple O(n²) random imputation and affine rescaling step. Against default (untuned) parameter settings, CV-imputation reduces MSE by up to 76× (NS on Graphon 1: 0.51 vs. 39.05), confirming that tuning is critical for all four estimators.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the graphon model framework (Lovász and Szegedy, 2006) and its estimation methods (SAS, NS, USVT, ICE), addressing the critical but underexplored problem of how to perform cross-validation on network data where standard CV's independence assumption is violated. The most direct predecessor is Li et al. (2020a), who proposed edge-sampling cross-validation with matrix completion to handle the missing edges in the training set. The key departure here is replacing matrix completion with a simple random Bernoulli imputation plus an affine rescaling (Lemma 1), which eliminates the low-rank assumption on P and the O(n³) SVD cost per fold, while preserving the network's structural integrity better than naive edge removal.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method provides a theoretically grounded (Theorem 1: asymptotic parallelism between CV score and true MSE) and computationally efficient alternative to matrix-completion-based network CV, with a 25× speedup on the Yeast network and 100% model-selection accuracy at n=200. A key limitation is the reliance on edge independence, which precludes extension to temporal or sequentially dependent network models; the authors note preliminary evidence for latent-space and sparse graphon extensions but leave formal guarantees as future work. For practitioners working with graphon-based network analysis, this offers a drop-in replacement for ECV that is both faster and more accurate, particularly for dense or full-rank networks where matrix completion's low-rank assumption fails.
