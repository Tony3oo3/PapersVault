## Problem

Most video captioning models are vision-centric and neglect the rich semantic cues in audio (dialogue, music, sound effects). Even when both modalities are processed, existing approaches either decouple them into separate captions that fail to model fine-grained temporal alignment, or lack targeted optimization for audiovisual temporal coherence. A pilot experiment on DailyOmni shows that naively concatenating separate audio and visual captions yields a 15.8% average accuracy drop (27.8% in AV Event Alignment) compared to jointly generating a temporally-aligned caption.

## Input / Output

The model takes video frames sampled at 2 fps (each frame encoded to at most 512×28×28 pixels, with total video tokens capped at 25,600) and a synchronized audio track (encoded at 25 tokens/second) as interleaved token sequences within a 32K context window, constraining input videos to ≤100 seconds. The output is a single temporally-aligned audiovisual caption that weaves visual events, dialogue transcriptions with speaker attribution, sound effects, and music descriptions in chronological order, rather than presenting them as separate modal blocks.

## Method

AVoCaDO builds on Qwen2.5-Omni-7B (which natively interleaves visual and audio tokens) and applies a two-stage post-training pipeline. Stage 1 (SFT) fine-tunes on 107K captions constructed via a two-stage Gemini-2.5-Pro pipeline: modality-specific captions are generated independently, then synthesized into a temporally coherent multimodal caption with GPT-4.1 quality filtering. Stage 2 (GRPO) optimizes on 2K samples using three complementary rewards: a checklist-based reward (R_C) that scores coverage of keypoints decomposed across five dimensions (cross-modal narrative logic, dynamic action, auditory elements, cinematography, static entities); a dialogue-based reward (R_D) that computes F1 over speaker-identified dialogue units matched via a thresholded LCS dynamic-programming alignment; and a length-regularized reward (R_L) that linearly penalizes captions exceeding 2048 tokens and zeros out those beyond 4096 to suppress repetition collapse. The video and audio encoders remain frozen throughout; only adapters and the LLM backbone are updated.

## Results

AVoCaDO (7B) achieves state-of-the-art performance among all open-source models on both primary audiovisual captioning benchmarks: a Total error rate of 37.3 on video-SALMONN-2 (vs. 47.6 for Qwen3-Omni-Captioner, 38.8 for video-SALMONN-2) and an average score of 73.2 on UGC-VideoCap (vs. 72.5 for Qwen3-Omni-Captioner, 67.2 for video-SALMONN-2). Notably, AVoCaDO surpasses the commercial Gemini-2.5-Pro on UGC-VideoCap (73.2 vs. 72.6) despite being a 7B model. On QA-based evaluation, AVoCaDO scores 50.1 on Daily-Omni and 25.7 on WorldSense, outperforming the strongest open-source baseline (video-SALMONN-2) by 20.2% and 7.5% respectively. Under visual-only settings, AVoCaDO also leads with 47.4% accuracy on VDC Detailed and 35.9 F1 on DREAM-1K.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AVoCaDO (7B) decisively outperforms all open-source audiovisual baselines: it beats video-SALMONN-2 (7B) by 5.6 points on UGC-VideoCap Avg (73.2 vs. 67.2) and by 20.2 points on Daily-Omni QA (50.1 vs. 29.9), and surpasses UGC-VideoCaptioner (3B) by 14.1 points on UGC-VideoCap Avg. Against the much larger Qwen3-Omni-Captioner (30B-A3B MoE), AVoCaDO achieves a lower Total error on video-SALMONN-2 (37.3 vs. 47.6) and a higher UGC-VideoCap Avg (73.2 vs. 72.5), while leading by 22.9 points on Daily-Omni (50.1 vs. 27.2). Compared to the commercial Gemini-2.5-Pro, AVoCaDO wins on UGC-VideoCap (73.2 vs. 72.6) but trails on video-SALMONN-2 Total error (37.3 vs. 31.3) and Daily-Omni (50.1 vs. 60.2), indicating the commercial model still has an edge in raw comprehensiveness. On visual-only benchmarks (VDC Detailed, DREAM-1K), AVoCaDO leads all open-source vision-only models including Qwen2.5-VL (47.4 vs. 44.5 VDC Acc; 35.9 vs. 30.1 DREAM-1K F1), confirming that its audiovisual training does not degrade pure-visual captioning. The base model Qwen2.5-Omni improves dramatically after AVoCaDO's two-stage post-training: Total error drops from 57.1 to 37.3 and UGC-VideoCap Avg rises from 57.7 to 73.2.

## Relation to Prior Work

AVoCaDO builds on Qwen2.5-Omni's interleaved token alignment of visual and audio streams and applies GRPO (originally from DeepSeekMath) in a novel audiovisual captioning context. It departs from the dominant vision-centric video captioning paradigm (OwlCap, Tarsier, AuroraCap) by treating audio as a first-class modality requiring explicit temporal synchronization rather than post-hoc concatenation. Compared to concurrent audiovisual captioners (video-SALMONN-2, UGC-VideoCaptioner), AVoCaDO uses a lighter training recipe—two-stage SFT+GRPO with three tailored rewards—rather than multi-round DPO or short-video-only optimization, and its reward design (checklist, dialogue F1, length regularization) is specifically structured for holistic audiovisual quality rather than atomic event metrics alone.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that task-specific post-training on a 7B model can match or exceed 30B MoE and commercial models on audiovisual captioning, suggesting that targeted reward engineering and high-quality temporally-aligned data can substitute for raw scale in this setting. The open-sourcing of both the model and the 107K dataset provides a practical foundation for downstream video understanding and generation pipelines that require audio-aware captions. Open questions include whether the reliance on Gemini-2.5-Pro for both data generation and evaluation introduces circularity, how well the approach generalizes to longer videos beyond the 100-second constraint, and whether the reward design transfers to other omni-modal generation tasks.
