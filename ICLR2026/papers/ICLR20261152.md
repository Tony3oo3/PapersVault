## Problem

Long chain-of-thought reasoning traces contain substantial redundancy—repetitive, truncated, or superficially clarifying content—that degrades SFT performance when models imitate verbose but uninformative patterns. Existing segment-importance metrics (perplexity, entropy, confidence-gain) are indirect signals prone to false positives on linguistic scaffolding and false negatives on low-entropy verification steps, failing to directly measure a segment's causal contribution to correct answer prediction.

## Input / Output

The method takes a long CoT trace (partitioned into segments via transition keywords like '\n\nWait' or '\n\nAlternatively'), a query, and a frozen language model to compute Integrated Gradient attributions along J interpolation steps from padding-token baselines to actual token embeddings. It produces two per-segment scores—normalized attribution strength and direction consistency—which jointly classify segments as important or unimportant, and ultimately a selectively fine-tuned model whose loss is masked on unimportant segments while preserving the full trajectory in context.

## Method

The core mechanism computes per-token Integrated Gradient attributions to the correct-answer logit, then aggregates them at segment level into (1) Attribution Strength (sum of |IG| with √N length normalization, further normalized across all segments) and (2) Direction Consistency (|Σ signed IG| / Σ|IG|), where high consistency indicates uniformly positive or negative shallow reasoning while moderate consistency reflects reflective, mixed-direction reasoning. Important segments are those in the top-k* by strength (cumulative ≥ τ=0.7) whose consistency is below β=0.8. Selective SFT then applies cross-entropy loss only on tokens within important segments (following Rho-1's token-masking paradigm), acting as implicit regularization that prevents overfitting to redundant content while preserving full-trajectory coherence—unlike pruning-based compression which disrupts textual flow and degrades accuracy.

## Results

On greedy decoding, Segment Selective SFT improves overall accuracy by up to 4.7% (46.9% vs. 44.8% on R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) while reducing output length by up to 18.2% (13,506 vs. 16,520 tokens) compared to Full CoT SFT. Gains are consistent across all three model scales: R1-Distill-Qwen-7B improves from 62.1% to 64.5% (↑3.9%) with 12.3% token reduction, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct from 44.2% to 45.6% (↑3.2%) with 4.5% reduction. Under temperature sampling, improvements are smaller but still positive (e.g., Pass@1 50.9→51.7, Pass@6 72.4→73.2 on the 1.5B model), confirming robustness across decoding strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares its IG-based segment importance against four alternative measures (Table 3, R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, greedy decoding). Segment Selective SFT with IG-based segments achieves 46.9% accuracy and 13,506 tokens, outperforming First-Correct Solution (46.2%, 15,238 tokens), Confidence-Gain Segments (44.7%, 15,479 tokens), Segment Perplexity (44.7%, 14,973 tokens), and Segment Entropy (44.5%, 16,288 tokens) on both accuracy and output length. The largest margin is over Segment Entropy (+2.4% accuracy, −17.8% tokens), while the smallest is over First-Correct Solution (+0.7% accuracy, −11.3% tokens). Confidence-Gain Segments perform worst among baselines because it only identifies 24% of segments on average, missing indirectly contributive segments like early problem understanding. The method also generalizes beyond the Qwen family, improving LLaMA3.1-8B-Instruct from 30.4% to 33.8% accuracy (↑3.4%) with 8.4% token reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper combines three prior threads: integrated gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017) for token-level attribution, the selective SFT paradigm from Rho-1 (Lin et al., 2024) that masks loss on uninformative tokens, and the segment-level granularity via transition-keyword splitting from Retro-search (Lu et al., 2025). It departs from prior importance-measurement approaches (perplexity, entropy, leave-one-out confidence gain) by arguing these indirect signals produce false positives on scaffolding text and false negatives on low-entropy verification steps, and instead uses IG to directly quantify each segment's causal contribution to correct-answer probability. The key conceptual departure is the two-metric criterion—high attribution strength combined with *moderate* direction consistency—as a signature of reflective reasoning, which no prior segment-importance method captures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework offers a practical, drop-in replacement for full-CoT SFT that simultaneously improves accuracy (up to 4.7%) and reduces output length (up to 18%) across multiple model families, making it relevant to anyone cold-starting reasoning models on long traces. However, the evaluation is confined to mathematical reasoning benchmarks and three model families (Qwen, LLaMA), and the IG computation (~7 GPU-hours per 7B model) adds non-trivial overhead. Open questions include whether the strength-consistency criterion transfers to non-mathematical domains, whether it can be integrated into RL training loops (mentioned but not tested), and whether the keyword-based segmentation generalizes to models that do not use explicit transition markers.
