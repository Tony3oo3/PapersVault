## Problem

Conventional LayerNorm in Image Restoration (IR) Transformers drives feature magnitudes to diverge to million-scale and collapses channel-wise entropy, yet this pathology has received little attention specific to IR tasks. The paper identifies two fundamental misalignments: per-token normalization disrupts spatial correlations essential for high-fidelity restoration, and input-independent scaling discards the input-specific statistical variability inherent in IR. Prior work on abnormal feature behaviors (e.g., in GANs or diffusion models) did not address the unique requirements of IR Transformers.

## Input / Output

The method is a drop-in normalization replacement applied within existing IR Transformer backbones (HAT, SwinIR, DRCT, SRFormer) operating on RGB images. It takes a degraded input image (bicubic-downscaled, Gaussian-noised, rain-streaked, or JPEG-compressed) at various resolutions and produces a restored full-resolution RGB image. The normalization operates on intermediate feature maps of shape (L×C) where L is the number of spatial tokens and C is the channel dimension, with no changes to the surrounding architecture or training procedure.

## Method

i-LN consists of two complementary modifications to vanilla per-token LayerNorm. First, LN* computes normalization statistics (mean and std) holistically across the entire spatio-channel dimension rather than per-token, which the paper proves preserves inter-pixel structure as a homothety (Proposition 2), unlike vanilla LN which is not even conformal (Proposition 1). Second, an input-adaptive rescaling multiplies the output of each Attention or FFN sub-block by √(σ²+ε) computed in the preceding normalization step, explicitly reintroducing the global scale that LN* necessarily discards. The full block becomes B(x) = x + √(σ²+ε)·f(LN*(x)), where f is either Attention or FFN. This simple two-part design preserves low-level input statistics throughout the network, prevents feature divergence, and yields consistent PSNR/SSIM gains across SR, denoising, deraining, and JPEG artifact removal tasks without any architectural or training-schedule changes.

## Results

On ×4 super-resolution with HAT₁, i-LN achieves 29.01 PSNR on Set14, 27.84 on BSD100, 27.17 on Urban100, and 31.82 on Manga109, outperforming all other normalization schemes tested. The gains are particularly pronounced in deraining, where i-LN improves PSNR by up to +1.85 dB over vanilla LayerNorm (36.20 vs. 34.35 on Rain100L with HAT₁). Across all four IR tasks (SR, DR, DN, CAR) and three backbones (HAT, SwinIR, DRCT), i-LN consistently yields the best PSNR and SSIM, with the largest margins in tasks where reliable input regions exist (SR, DR) and smaller but consistent gains in globally-corrupted tasks (DN, CAR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

i-LN outperforms all six compared normalization schemes across every benchmark in Table 1. Against vanilla LayerNorm (Ba et al., 2016), i-LN gains +0.22 dB on Set14 and +0.62 dB on Urban100, with the largest relative improvement on Urban100 where spatial structure matters most. Per-token variants LayerScale (Touvron et al., 2021) and RMSNorm (Zhang & Sennrich, 2019) improve slightly over vanilla LN but still trail i-LN by 0.12–0.42 dB, confirming that per-token normalization is the core issue. Spatially holistic alternatives InstanceNorm (Ulyanov et al., 2016) and BatchNorm (Ioffe & Szegedy, 2015) close the gap (within 0.03–0.15 dB) but still underperform i-LN, because they discard channel-wise information; BatchNorm additionally suffers a significant eval-mode drop. ReZero (Bachlechner et al., 2021) and the no-normalization setting both fail to match i-LN, with the latter failing to converge entirely. The most dramatic win is in deraining (Table 2b), where i-LN achieves 36.20 vs. 34.35 PSNR on Rain100L (+1.85 dB) with HAT₁, and 34.43 vs. 33.00 with SwinIR₁ (+1.43 dB), reflecting i-LN's ability to preserve rain-free input regions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on conventional LayerNorm (Ba et al., 2016) as the normalization backbone of IR Transformers, but reframes it through a formal inter-pixel structure preservation lens. It connects to a scattered line of prior observations—EDSR noting BatchNorm's loss of range flexibility, ESRGAN/StyleGAN2 identifying normalization-induced artifacts, and EDM2 identifying feature magnitude divergence in diffusion models—yet is the first to unify these under a single theoretical diagnosis specific to IR Transformers: per-token normalization destroys spatial structure, and input-blind scaling discards restoration-critical statistics. Rather than removing normalization entirely (as EDSR did for CNNs) or applying generic stabilization tricks (ReZero, LayerScale), the paper proposes a minimal two-part modification (global spatio-channel normalization + input-adaptive rescaling) grounded in homothety preservation proofs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that standard LayerNorm drives IR Transformer features to million-scale magnitudes with collapsed channel entropy—compensated only by extreme bias alignment in the final layer—reveals a hidden fragility in the de facto standard normalization for this task family. Practically, i-LN is a trivially simple drop-in replacement that consistently improves PSNR/SSIM across four IR tasks and four architectures, and critically enables stable low-precision (fp16/int8) inference where vanilla LN produces infinity values. Open questions include whether the spatial-holisticness principle generalizes to other Transformer-based tasks (e.g., segmentation, detection) where per-token normalization may be less harmful, and whether the input-adaptive rescaling interacts favorably with more complex degradation models beyond the Real-ESRGAN pipeline tested here.
