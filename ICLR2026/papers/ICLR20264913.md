## Problem

Sheaf Neural Networks (SNNs) cannot achieve cooperative behavior: Proposition 3.1 proves that if a node's update is independent of its neighbors (i.e., it does not listen), then its information is also suppressed from reaching neighbors (it cannot propagate). This coupling between listening and propagating prevents SNNs from selectively attending to distant nodes while ignoring intermediates, limiting their ability to mitigate oversquashing in long-range tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264913/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an undirected graph with node feature vectors and internally converts it to a directed graph by replacing each undirected edge with a pair of directed edges. For each node, it learns a source conformal map S_i and a target conformal map T_i (each a scalar times an orthogonal matrix via Householder reflections) rather than per-edge restriction maps, reducing parameters from O(|E|) to O(|V|). Outputs are node embeddings for classification or graph-level scores for classification/regression tasks.

## Method

CSNN introduces cellular sheaves over directed graphs and defines in-degree and out-degree sheaf Laplacians, then composes them as (Δ_F^in)^T Δ_F^out to form the diffusion operator. The key mechanism is that each node independently controls a source map S_i (governing propagation) and a target map T_i (governing listening): setting T_i=0 isolates the node from incoming information while S_i≠0 still allows it to broadcast, and vice versa. For efficiency, flat vector bundles with conformal maps (scalar × orthogonal) are used, parameterized via Householder reflections. Theoretically, each layer reaches 2-hop neighbors (Proposition 4.2), and there exist restriction map configurations where a node at distance t receives information from a specific distant node while ignoring all t−1 intermediate nodes (Proposition 4.3), providing a principled mechanism to alleviate oversquashing.

## Results

On the seven heterophilic node-classification benchmarks from Platonov et al. (2023), CSNN achieves the best performance in 6 out of 7 datasets, with headline results of 92.63% accuracy on roman-empire (homophily 0.05), 99.07% AUROC on minesweeper, and 43.09% accuracy on chameleon. On the Long Range Graph Benchmark, CSNN attains the best MAE of 24.32 on peptides-struct and a competitive AP of 71.58 on peptides-func (second only to BuNN at 72.76). On the NeighborsMatch over-squashing task, CSNN reaches 100% accuracy for all tree depths r tested, while GCN/GIN fail from r=4, GAT/GGNN from r=5, and BuNN drops to 42% at r=8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CSNN outperforms the closest sheaf-based competitor BuNN on 5 of 7 heterophilic node-classification datasets (e.g., 92.63 vs 91.75 on roman-empire, 43.09 vs – on chameleon), though BuNN edges ahead on amazon-ratings (53.74 vs 52.07). Against CO-GNN, CSNN wins on 6 of 7 datasets, with the largest margin on chameleon (43.09 vs 41.14) and squirrel (41.18 vs 39.39), while CO-GNN retains a narrow lead on amazon-ratings (54.20 vs 52.07). CSNN dominates all NSD variants across every dataset, with O(d)-NSD trailing by up to 10 points (e.g., 85.45 vs 78.83 on tolokers). On the Peptides graph-classification benchmark, CSNN achieves the best MAE (24.32) on peptides-struct, beating G-ViT (24.49) and GCN (24.60), while ranking second on peptides-func (71.58) behind BuNN (72.76). On the NeighborsMatch over-squashing task, CSNN achieves 100% accuracy at all tree depths, whereas BuNN degrades to 42% at r=8 and NSD collapses to 5% at r=4, confirming the theoretical advantage of directed sheaf diffusion for long-range information propagation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate research threads: sheaf-based graph neural networks (NSD, BuNN) and cooperative GNNs (CO-GNN). It first establishes a negative result — that standard undirected sheaf diffusion fundamentally cannot decouple a node's listening from its propagating (Proposition 3.1) — then introduces directed cellular sheaves with in- and out-degree Laplacians to overcome this limitation. The efficient parameterization via flat vector bundles with per-node source and target conformal maps (only 2 maps per node vs. 2m in general sheaves) follows the BuNN approach, while the cooperative action semantics (LISTEN, PROPAGATE, ISOLATE, STANDARD) are inherited from CO-GNN but realized through continuous map magnitudes rather than discrete Gumbel-Softmax actions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative result clarifies a structural limitation of the entire undirected sheaf GNN literature, and the directed sheaf construction provides a principled, provably more flexible diffusion mechanism with a 2t-hop receptive field and the ability to selectively attend to arbitrarily distant nodes while ignoring intermediates — a property directly relevant to over-squashing. However, scalability remains a significant open challenge: the authors acknowledge that sheaf-based networks are not yet competitive at large scale, and the node-classification gains on real datasets, while consistent, are often modest (1–5 points) with high variance on small benchmarks. Extending the framework to high-order structures (cell/simplicial complexes) and developing truly efficient message-passing implementations are identified as key next steps.
