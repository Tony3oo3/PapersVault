## Problem

PINN training is unstable because the PDE residual and boundary-condition loss terms have highly dissimilar gradient magnitudes, causing parameter updates to be biased toward one component and producing solutions that are accurate only on the boundary or only in the interior. Existing adaptive weighting schemes (LRA, NTK, RAR) do not fully resolve this imbalance, and the optimal optimizer varies across PDEs, requiring case-by-case search. The paper addresses this gap by reformulating PINN training as a nonconvex-strongly concave saddle-point problem with non-Euclidean geometry, providing both convergence guarantees and a unified optimizer that generalizes across PDE types.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a PDE boundary value problem (a system of M_r differential equations plus M−M_r boundary/initial conditions defined on a domain Ω ⊂ ℝ^d), a neural network architecture (vanilla 5-layer MLP with 100 units per layer for benchmarks, or the 38M-parameter DoMINO point-cloud model for the 3D Navier-Stokes challenge), and sets of interior and boundary collocation points. It outputs a trained network that evaluates the PDE solution u: ℝ^d → ℝ^m at arbitrary query points. The loss weights π are constrained to the unit simplex (or a regularized simplex intersected with a Euclidean ball), and the ascent step uses KL-divergence as the Bregman distance, which accounts for relative rather than absolute changes in weights.

## Method

The core reformulation replaces the fixed-weight ERM objective with min_θ max_π [Σ π_i L_i(θ) − λ D_ψ(π‖π̂)], where the Bregman penalty λ D_ψ(π‖π̂) (with π̂ typically uniform) makes the objective λ-strongly concave in π regardless of the PDE. The algorithm BGDA alternates a standard gradient descent step on θ (Euclidean) with a Bregman proximal mapping step on π (non-Euclidean), which for KL-divergence on the simplex reduces to a softmax update of the loss-component gradients. The practical variant AdaBGDA wraps the descent step in Adam (gradient smoothing for the nonconvex θ landscape) and the ascent step in RMSProp (well-suited to the strongly concave π landscape), with bias-corrected running averages. Theoretically, the paper proves O(κ⁴LΔ + κ²L²D_ψ(π*(θ⁰),π⁰))/ε² iteration complexity for reaching an ε-stationary point, and shows the rate improves to O(κL/ε²) when the simplex is regularized by intersecting with a Euclidean ball. Empirically, AdaBGDA achieves SOTA L2RE on 77.3% of 22 PINNacle PDEs while matching Adam's per-iteration cost, since the closed-form softmax ascent step requires only a forward pass already computed for the descent update.

## Results

On the PINNacle benchmark of 22 PDEs, AdaBGDA achieves the best L2RE in 77.3% of cases (17/22), compared to 27.3% for the previous best optimizer. Headline improvements include Poisson 2d-C (8.15E-3 vs. 1.14E-2 for NTK), Navier-Stokes 2d-C (2.35E-2 vs. 4.67E-2 for Adam), Wave 1d-C (1.63E-2 vs. 9.20E-2 for NTK), and High-dim PNd (1.31E-4 vs. 4.69E-4 for LBFGS). In 18% of PDEs, AdaBGDA outperforms the closest competitor by more than a factor of two, and in 36% it improves L2RE by over 30%. On the 3D Navier-Stokes challenge test with the 38M-parameter DoMINO model, AdaBGDA reduces x-velocity L2RE from 3.39E-1 (Adam) to 2.78E-1 and y-velocity from 8.60E-1 to 5.99E-1.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AdaBGDA dominates all seven PINNacle baselines (Adam, LBFGS, LRA, NTK, RAR, MultiAdam) in 17 of 22 PDEs, with the largest margins on Wave 1d-C (5.6× over NTK), Poisson 2d-C (1.4× over NTK), and High-dim PNd (3.6× over LBFGS). The closest competitor, NTK, wins only on Heat 2d-VC (2.07E-1 vs. 2.99E-1) and Burgers 2d-C (2.58E-1 vs. 4.20E-1), while LRA wins on Wave 2d-MS (1.97E-1 vs. 9.35E-1 is actually AdaBGDA winning; LRA's best is Poisson 2d-CG at 4.11E-2 vs. AdaBGDA's 1.70E-2, so AdaBGDA wins there too). Against the two saddle-point baselines from Appendix B, AdaBGDA outperforms dual-dimer (Euclidean SPP) in 77.3% of cases and AL-PINN (augmented Lagrangian) in 63.6% of cases, confirming the importance of the non-Euclidean Bregman geometry. On computational overhead (Table 4), AdaBGDA matches Adam's per-iteration time (7.64s vs. 8.24s for 1000 iterations) and uses less GPU memory (0.37 GB vs. 0.23 GB for Adam, 10.66 GB for SSBroyden, 2.68 GB for NNCG), while converging ~2.5× faster than NTK on Wave 1d-C. The only consistent losses are on Burgers 2d-C and Heat 2d-VC, where the vanilla PINN architecture appears insufficient for the problem complexity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the saddle-point reformulation of PINN training introduced by Liu and Wang (2021, dual-dimer) by replacing the Euclidean weight geometry with a Bregman divergence (e.g., KL-divergence on the unit simplex), and by providing the first rigorous convergence theory for nonconvex-strongly concave SPPs with non-Euclidean geometry in the concave component. The algorithmic framework builds on two-timescale gradient descent-ascent (Lin et al., 2020) and the Bregman proximal mapping machinery of Nemirovskij and Yudin (1983), while the practical adaptive version (AdaBGDA) combines Adam for the nonconvex θ-update with RMSProp for the strongly concave π-update. The paper departs from prior SPP work (e.g., Huang et al., 2021) by placing the non-Euclidean geometry in the concave component rather than the nonconvex one, which is the natural choice when weights live on a simplex.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a core practical bottleneck in PINN deployment: the need for case-by-case optimizer selection (LRA for Poisson, RAR for Heat, NTK for Wave, etc.) due to gradient magnitude conflicts between residual and boundary losses. By achieving 77.3% SOTA across 22 PDEs with zero additional computational overhead over Adam and demonstrating scalability to a 38M-parameter DoMINO model on 3D Navier-Stokes, the work suggests a single robust training recipe could replace the current patchwork of PDE-specific optimizers. Open questions include whether the Lipschitz smoothness assumption (necessary for theory but unrealistic for deep networks) can be relaxed, whether the method's failures on long-time and multi-scale PDEs reflect a fundamental limitation of the saddle-point formulation or simply the vanilla PINN architecture, and how the approach behaves in fully stochastic (mini-batch) settings.
