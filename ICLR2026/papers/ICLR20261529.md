## Problem

The dominant paradigm for training LVLMs in goal-driven navigation relies on Path Imitation—optimizing an agent to replicate a single expert trajectory—which reduces navigation to memorization and ignores the multi-path nature of real environments. This supervision-granularity mismatch prevents agents from learning to evaluate the relative quality of all available actions, limiting their ability to generalize to unseen scenes and make robust decisions at ambiguous points.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261529/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep, the agent receives a single RGB observation with numbered action arrows rendered by an Action Proposal Module (which uses depth maps and occupancy grids to propose feasible polar-coordinate actions), plus either a text goal category (ObjectNav) or a goal image of a specific instance (Instance-Image-Goal Nav). The model outputs a chain-of-thought reasoning trace followed by a single action index, decoded via masked softmax over only the valid candidate indices to guarantee executability. A separate, non-fine-tuned Stop Agent (also Qwen2.5-VL-7B) independently decides when the goal is within 1 meter and in clear view, decoupling the stopping decision from the navigation policy.

## Method

CompassNav is a two-stage SFT-then-RFT recipe on Qwen2.5-VL-7B (LoRA, rank 64). Stage 1 (SFT) distills a 'reason-then-act' policy from Qwen-QvQ's successful navigation episodes, training the model to externalize spatial reasoning before committing to an action. Stage 2 (RFT) uses GRPO with a novel Gap-Aware Hybrid Reward: a continuous base score computed as a softmax over A* geodesic distances to the goal for all candidate actions, plus a dynamic bonus modulated by a certainty factor g_t that measures the normalized gap between the best and second-best actions—yielding decisive signals when one action is clearly superior and nuanced, exploration-encouraging scores when options are close. The key data contribution is Compass-Data-22k, where an Oracle A* Annotator densely annotates every feasible action at every timestep with its geodesic distance to the goal (rather than only the single optimal path), and a backtracking mechanism records alternative trajectories at ambiguous decision points, creating a panoramic value field across the decision space.

## Results

CompassNav (7B, SFT+RFT) achieves 61.6% SR and 27.8% SPL on ObjectNav, and 35.6% SR and 14.8% SPL on Instance-Image-Goal Navigation, yielding an average of 48.6% SR / 21.3% SPL. This surpasses GPT-4o (41.1% avg SR) and GPT-o4-mini (46.5% avg SR) on both tasks, while using only 7B parameters versus the much larger proprietary models. On the modular-method comparison (Table 1), CompassNav leads on HM3D (56.6% SR vs. 54.5% for UniGoal) and MP3D (42.0% SR vs. 41.0% for UniGoal) without any explicit memory module.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CompassNav (7B) outperforms all closed-source baselines: it beats GPT-4o by +9.2 SR on ObjNav and +5.8 SR on InsImageNav, and edges out GPT-o4-mini by +2.0 SR on ObjNav and +2.2 SR on InsImageNav, despite being an order of magnitude smaller. Against open-source LVLMs (Qwen2-VL-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Llama3.2-11B), the gains are substantial, with average SR improving from 17–21% to 48.6%. In the modular-method comparison (Table 1), CompassNav surpasses the best modular system UniGoal (CVPR'25) on both HM3D (56.6 vs. 54.5 SR) and MP3D (42.0 vs. 41.0 SR) without using any explicit memory, and outperforms VLFM (ICRA'24) by +4.2 SR on HM3D and +5.6 SR on MP3D. On HM3D-OVON val-unseen (Table 6), CompassNav (43.5 SR, 21.6 SPL) narrowly beats Nav-R1 (42.2 SR, 20.1 SPL) while using only one-tenth of the training data. On the ProcTHOR sim-to-sim generalization benchmark (Table 7), CompassNav achieves 58.1 SR / 32.8 SPL, surpassing the domain-specific ProcTHOR agent (55.0 / 23.7) by +9.1 SPL, indicating transferable spatial reasoning rather than visual overfitting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant Path Imitation paradigm in LVLM-based navigation (Nav-R1, VLN-R1) by reframing training as Decision Understanding: instead of supervising a single expert trajectory, it annotates all feasible actions with A* geodesic distances to create a panoramic value landscape. Technically, it builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for the RFT stage and adopts the Action Proposal Module from VLMNav (Goetting et al., 2024), but introduces a novel Gap-Aware Hybrid Reward that dynamically modulates feedback based on the certainty gap between the best and second-best actions. The SFT stage uses knowledge distillation from Qwen-QvQ rather than post-hoc reasoning annotation, and the overall SFT-then-RFT recipe is positioned as a solution to the cold-start problem in RL fine-tuning of LVLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paradigm shift from single-path imitation to per-action value assessment addresses a fundamental limitation in how navigation agents are trained: memorizing one route versus learning to evaluate alternatives. The practical impact is significant—a 7B open-source model outperforms GPT-4o and o4-mini on ObjectNav and Instance-Image-Goal Navigation, and transfers to a physical robot. However, the approach still depends on an oracle A* planner for annotation (unavailable at real-world inference), the memory-agnostic design leaves a gap for long-horizon tasks, and the 7B scale shows clear limitations on memory-dependent reasoning (NavNuances NU score). Open questions include whether the panoramic supervision paradigm extends to continuous action spaces, multi-agent settings, or tasks where no global map is available.
