## Problem

Federated LLM fine-tuning faces a dual challenge: the massive parameter count makes zeroth-order (ZO) optimization computationally expensive and communication-heavy, while Non-IID data across clients causes drift that degrades global convergence. Existing ZO federated methods either operate on full parameter spaces (inefficient) or lack mechanisms to detect and mitigate extreme data heterogeneity at the client level.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260417/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-trained LLM weights (LLaMA-3.2-1B, Qwen2-1.5B, Gemma-2-2B), client-local text data partitioned via Dirichlet distributions, a static binary sparse mask at 0.1% density derived from C4 pre-training gradients, and server-shared random Gaussian seeds. The primary communication payload is a list of scalar projected gradients (one per local step) rather than full model updates, reducing bandwidth by over 1000×. The server additionally produces GradIP scores per client per step, which serve as a heterogeneity signal for the MEERKAT-VP early-stopping decision.

## Method

MEERKAT restricts ZO perturbations to a static, extremely sparse (0.1%) subset of LLM parameters selected by top average squared gradients on C4 pre-training data, making each client's upload a single scalar per local step and enabling high-frequency synchronization that lowers the theoretical steady-state error floor. The server reconstructs each client's full local update trajectory (the 'virtual path') from the uploaded scalars, shared seeds, and the known mask, without accessing raw data. MEERKAT-VP exploits this virtual path to compute the GradIP score (inner product between server-held pre-training gradients and client ZO gradients), which empirically converges to zero for extreme Non-IID clients but oscillates for IID clients. Using trajectory metrics (initial-to-later GradIP ratio and quiescent-step ratio), VPCS identifies extreme Non-IID clients during a calibration phase and applies early stopping, limiting them to one local step per round while a data pointer ensures full dataset coverage over training. Convergence analysis under the PL condition shows the sparsity density u quadratically improves the rate-dependent term while high-frequency communication (small T) tightens the steady-state bound, and MEERKAT-VP further reduces the transient and heterogeneity terms relative to MEERKAT.

## Results

On the average test accuracy across seven NLP tasks (SST-2, AG's News, Yelp, BoolQ, RTE, WSC, WiC), MEERKAT with 0.1% sparse parameters consistently outperforms full-parameter FedZO across all three LLMs. For example, at T=10 local steps, MEERKAT achieves 0.759 average accuracy on LLaMA-3.2-1B (vs. 0.699 for Full-FedZO), 0.805 on Qwen2-1.5B (vs. 0.761), and 0.772 on Gemma2-2B (vs. 0.732). Under extreme communication (T=1), MEERKAT on Qwen2-1.5B matches its IID accuracy under Non-IID (Dirichlet α=0.5), effectively closing the heterogeneity gap. MEERKAT-VP further improves over MEERKAT by applying GradIP-based early stopping to extreme Non-IID clients, achieving performance competitive with a backpropagation upper bound.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MEERKAT (0.1% sparse ZO) outperforms Full-FedZO by 4–6 points in average accuracy across all three models and all local-step settings (e.g., 0.759 vs. 0.699 on LLaMA-3.2-1B at T=10). Against Weight Magnitude sparsity, MEERKAT leads by 2–4 points on LLaMA-3.2-1B and Qwen2-1.5B, though Weight Magnitude edges ahead on Gemma2-2B at T=10 (0.779 vs. 0.772). LoRA-FedZO degrades sharply with increasing local steps (dropping to 0.628 on LLaMA-3.2-1B at T=100), while MEERKAT remains stable, confirming that gradient-based sparse mask selection is more robust than low-rank adaptation under Non-IID drift. The paper also reports that MEERKAT outperforms DeComFL (Li et al., 2024) under identical settings (Table 19), and MEERKAT-VP significantly outperforms an adapted FedDYN baseline (Acar et al., 2021) while approaching backpropagation upper-bound performance (Table 25).

## Relation to Prior Work

MEERKAT extends the sparse zeroth-order fine-tuning paradigm of Guo et al. (2024) and the forward-pass-only ZO approach of Malladi et al. (2023) into the federated learning setting, building on the FedAvg framework of McMahan et al. (2017). The key conceptual departure is reframing extreme sparsity (0.1% parameters) not merely as a compression tool but as the enabler for high-frequency client-server synchronization, which in turn mitigates Non-IID drift. The virtual path mechanism and the GradIP phenomenon represent a novel diagnostic layer absent from prior federated ZO work, allowing the server to infer client data heterogeneity from scalar projected gradients without accessing raw data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work offers a practical recipe for federated LLM fine-tuning on resource-constrained devices by combining memory-efficient ZO with communication-efficient sparsity, and provides a lightweight, privacy-preserving signal (GradIP) for detecting extreme Non-IID clients. However, the GradIP phenomenon is primarily demonstrated on single-label Non-IID and then extended to Dirichlet partitions, leaving open whether it generalizes to more complex heterogeneity patterns (e.g., feature shift, covariate shift). The experiments are limited to 1B–2B parameter models, and it remains unclear whether the 0.1% sparsity level and the pre-training-gradient-based mask selection transfer to substantially larger LLMs or to settings where pre-training data is unavailable. The early-stopping strategy in MEERKAT-VP is also relatively simple (single local step), and more nuanced client-adaptive scheduling could be explored.
