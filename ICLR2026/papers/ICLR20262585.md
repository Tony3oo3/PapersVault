## Problem

Language models under standard sampling (temperature, nucleus, beam search) produce concurrent generation runs that converge on nearly identical latent trajectories, causing diversity collapse that limits best-of-N selection. Existing diversity methods either perturb token-level probabilities locally without cross-run coordination or require retraining. The paper addresses the gap of a training-free, inference-time intervention that structurally diversifies the internal activation trajectories of multiple concurrent runs at every token.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt, the method generates N parallel sequences (N=20 for TESTEVAL, N=4 for LiveIdeaBench). At each decoding step, it extracts hidden-state vectors from a single designated layer at the output of every attention head (before o_proj) across all N active runs, forming a matrix H ∈ R^{d×N}. The steering magnitude is set as α = C·‖H‖² with C ∈ {0.1, 0.5}, and paths that emit EOS are dropped from the active set. The output is N diverse text sequences that are structurally distinct in their reasoning paths rather than mere paraphrases.

## Method

STARS formulates per-token steering as maximizing the geometric volume of the parallelepiped spanned by steered activations (H+V), subject to orthogonality constraints V^T V = αI, which defines a scaled Stiefel manifold. A full Riemannian gradient descent with polar-decomposition retraction yields an O(1/K) convergence guarantee but is too slow for real-time inference. The practical algorithm uses a one-step update: V₀ is initialized from the null-space columns of the SVD of H, the search direction is set to H itself (proven to be a Riemannian descent direction at V₀), and a closed-form stepsize η* = D₁/D₂ is derived from the singular values of H via a quadratic approximation of the log-determinant objective. The final steering vector is obtained by applying the polar retraction, and the resulting orthogonal vectors are added to each attention head's output before the output projection, pushing concurrent runs into distinct regions of activation space at every token.

## Results

On TESTEVAL, STARS_0.5 achieves 34.76% overall line coverage on GEMMA-1.1-2B-IT (T=1.0), a 6.5× improvement over temperature sampling (5.36%), and 91.05% on QWEN3-1.7B versus 59.95% for sampling. On LiveIdeaBench, STARS_0.5 nearly doubles the fluency score at T=0.2 (5.09 vs. 2.68 for sampling on QWEN2.5-3B) while maintaining or improving originality, feasibility, and clarity. STARS also improves execution correctness on QWEN3-1.7B (41.86% vs. 11.88% at T=1.0), showing that diversity gains do not come at the cost of functional quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is temperature sampling (SAMPLING), which adjusts token probabilities during decoding without any cross-run coordination. STARS decisively outperforms sampling on all coverage metrics across both model families and all temperature settings: on GEMMA-1.1-2B-IT, overall line coverage jumps from 5.36% (T=1.0) to 34.76% with STARS_0.5, and from 1.44% (T=0.2) to 39.03%, demonstrating that STARS is especially effective in low-temperature regimes where sampling collapses. On QWEN3-1.7B, STARS_0.5 improves overall line coverage from 59.95% to 91.05% at T=1.0 and from 4.71% to 91.35% at T=0.2. On LiveIdeaBench, STARS_0.5 achieves the highest average creativity score at most temperatures and nearly doubles fluency at T=0.2 (5.09 vs. 2.68), while standard sampling degrades sharply as temperature decreases. The paper also includes RAND (random steering vectors) and MEAN (mean-offset steering) baselines in supplementary experiments (Table 7), both of which achieve only marginal coverage gains (≤6.5%) compared to STARS_0.5's 34–40%, confirming that the Stiefel-manifold volume-maximization objective is essential rather than merely the act of adding perturbations.

## Relation to Prior Work

STARS repurposes the additive activation-steering mechanism introduced by Turner et al. (2024) and the attention-head intervention position from Li et al. (2023), but inverts their purpose: instead of steering a single run toward a fixed concept, it jointly steers N concurrent runs away from each other by maximizing the geometric volume of their hidden activations. The orthogonality constraint on steering vectors is formalized as optimization on the scaled Stiefel manifold, a formulation distinct from the closest prior work (Khanh et al., 2025), which maximizes volume on a product of spheres and updates only at synchronization anchors rather than every token. The practical one-step update with a closed-form stepsize derived from a quadratic approximation of the log-determinant objective is a new algorithmic contribution that avoids the iterative Riemannian gradient descent (with backtracking line search) that would be too slow for per-token inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Diversity collapse in best-of-N sampling is a recognized bottleneck that limits the effectiveness of test-time scaling for reasoning, creative generation, and red-teaming; STARS offers a training-free, low-overhead (~2 s/question) intervention that addresses this at the representation level rather than the token-probability level. However, experiments are confined to small models (1.5B–3B parameters) and relatively weak baselines (temperature sampling, random steering, mean-offset steering), with no direct comparison to the closest concurrent work (Khanh et al., 2025) or to RL-based diversity methods. Open questions include whether the per-token Stiefel steering scales to 70B+ models, whether multi-layer or head-selective steering further improves results, and how the method interacts with post-training diversity objectives.
