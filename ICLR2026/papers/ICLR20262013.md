## Problem

Existing 3D anime character generation methods produce unrigged meshes with low geometric fidelity, blurry textures, and no internal skeleton, rendering them unusable for parametric animation. Standard parametric body models like SMPL cannot represent the exaggerated proportions and stylized features of anime characters, while whole-character generation pipelines struggle to capture fine details in hands, hair, and garments simultaneously.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262013/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method accepts either a free-form text prompt or a single RGB image of a character in an arbitrary pose. It first canonicalizes the input to a front-view A-pose image, then produces a fully rigged 3D character: an Anime-SMPL body mesh (12,489 vertices, 98 PCA shape components, unified UV layout), four separate body-aligned garment meshes (hairstyle, upper garment, lower garment, accessories) extracted from SDFs via marching cubes, UV texture maps decomposed into six semantic body regions (body skin, facial skin, left/right eyes, eyebrows, eyelashes) plus per-garment texture maps, and a complete skeleton with LBS weights and facial blendshapes for animation.

## Method

The pipeline decomposes character generation into modular stages. For the body, Anime-SMPL extends SMPL with anime-specific proportions via PCA on 20k characters, a ResNet-based shape predictor, and a unified UV layout enabling direct UV-space texture synthesis. For garments, a MoE-structured Multi-Shape DiT (built on VecSet Diffusion) uses four dedicated MLP expert branches routed by learnable label tokens, with body surface points encoded by a VecSet VAE (512 tokens) concatenated to noised garment tokens (3072 tokens) as an explicit geometric prior to enforce body alignment and reduce interpenetration. For textures, a multi-view diffusion model generates six semantic body regions in UV space, while a separate component-wise diffusion model with multi-component self-attention disentangles garment regions from the reference image before MVAdapter synthesizes multi-view renderings projected onto each mesh.

## Results

In a user study with 30 participants evaluating 16 anime characters on a 1–5 scale, the proposed method achieves the highest scores across all three metrics: Mesh Quality (3.83), Texture Quality (3.75), and Fidelity (3.74). The closest competitor, Hunyuan3D 2.0, scores 3.14 / 3.49 / 3.42 respectively, while CharacterGen and StdGEN trail further behind at approximately 2.6 / 2.2 / 2.5. The method also reports an end-to-end inference time of roughly 400 seconds (5 s image generation + 2 s SMPL prediction + 40 s garment DiT + 10 s body texture + 360 s garment texture).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares against three anime-style 3D character generation baselines: CharacterGen, StdGEN, and Hunyuan3D 2.0. Because the methods are trained on different datasets (Anime3D for CharacterGen/StdGEN, Objaverse-XL for Hunyuan3D 2.0, and a proprietary 20k-character set for the proposed method), standard reconstruction metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) are not directly comparable; instead, a 30-participant user study is used. The proposed method wins on all three perceptual metrics against every baseline. The largest margin is over CharacterGen (+1.25 Mesh, +1.61 Texture, +1.23 Fidelity) and StdGEN (+1.14, +1.52, +1.22). Against the strongest baseline, Hunyuan3D 2.0, the gains are +0.69 in Mesh Quality, +0.26 in Texture Quality, and +0.32 in Fidelity, with the mesh-quality advantage attributed to the Anime-SMPL body template and body-aligned garment generation reducing interpenetration artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SMPL parametric body model into Anime-SMPL, adapting its proportions and topology for stylized anime characters, and builds its 3D garment generation on the VecSet Diffusion Model (Zhang et al., 2023) augmented with a Mixture-of-Experts routing mechanism to handle multiple garment types in a single network. It departs from prior anime character generation methods (CharacterGen, StdGen, Hunyuan3D 2.0) that produce a single unrigged mesh by decomposing the character into independently generated components (body, hair, upper/lower garments, accessories) and explicitly conditioning garment geometry on the underlying body surface via VecSet-encoded point clouds. For texture synthesis, it repurposes MVAdapter (Huang et al., 2024b) within a two-stage pipeline that first decomposes the full-body image into per-component views to avoid color bleeding, then projects multi-view renderings onto each mesh.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical contribution is making generated anime characters genuinely animation-ready—complete with a consistent skeleton, blendshape-based facial control, and skinning weights—enabling downstream tasks like garment retargeting and motion capture that prior anime generation methods could not support. Key limitations remain: pose canonicalization degrades on complex poses, SDF-to-mesh conversion produces double-sided surfaces that hinder physics simulation, and multi-view texture projection still suffers from cross-view inconsistency. Open questions include whether direct 3D texture generation or vertex/face-level mesh generation (e.g., MeshAnything, Meshtron) can resolve the double-surface and misalignment artifacts, and whether the component-wise decomposition generalizes to non-anime stylized characters.
