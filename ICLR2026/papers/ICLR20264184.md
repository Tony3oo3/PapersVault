## Problem

Machine unlearning theory for high-dimensional proportional regimes (p ~ n) is largely absent: prior certified unlearning results rely on Ω(1) strong convexity and O(1) smoothness of the per-example loss, which provably fail even for ridge-regularized least squares when p scales with n. The only prior high-dimensional analysis (Zou et al. 2025) uses ε-certifiability, a notion incompatible with natural Gaussian noise mechanisms, and concludes that at least two Newton steps are needed for both privacy and accuracy. This paper asks whether a single noisy Newton step can suffice under a better-chosen certifiability criterion.

## Input / Output

The unlearning procedure consumes the trained RERM estimator β̂, the removal set D_M of size m, auxiliary Hessian/gradient information T(D_n) evaluated at β̂, and a fresh Gaussian noise vector b ~ N(0, (R/ε)² I_p) where R = C₁(n)√(C₂(n)m³/(2λνn)) with polylogarithmic constants. It outputs the unlearned model β̃_{\M} = β̂_{\M}^{(1)} + b together with a (φ_n, ε)-GPAR privacy certificate (φ_n → 0 as n,p → ∞) and a Generalization Error Divergence bound that vanishes whenever m² polylog(n)/√n → 0, i.e. m = o(n^{1/4}/polylog(n)).

## Method

The paper introduces (φ,ε)-Gaussian certifiability, defined via the trade-off function T(N(0,1), N(ε,1)) and a hypothesis-testing formulation, which is shown to be the canonical and tightest certifiability notion in high dimensions by Blackwell ordering and the CLT for high-dimensional log-concave projections (Dong et al. 2021, 2022). The unlearning algorithm performs exactly one Newton step on the reduced loss L_{\M} starting from β̂, then adds isotropic Gaussian noise calibrated to the (φ,ε)-GPAR criterion. Under relaxed assumptions (A1–A4 on the loss/regularizer and B1–B2 on the data) that remain valid in the proportional regime, the paper proves this single noisy step simultaneously achieves (φ_n, ε)-GPAR and vanishing GED. The key insight is that Gaussian certifiability is optimally matched to the Gaussian noise mechanism, eliminating the suboptimality gap that forced Zou et al. (2025) to require two Newton steps under ε-certifiability with Laplace noise.

## Results

The paper's main theoretical contribution is that a single noisy Newton step with Gaussian perturbation b ~ N(0, R²/ε² · I_p) achieves (φ_n, ε)-GPAR with R = C₁(n)√(C₂(n)m³/(2λνn)) and φ_n → 0, while simultaneously attaining GED = O_p(m² polylog(n)/√n) → 0 whenever m = o(n^{1/4−α}) for any α > 0. In experiments (ridge-regularized logistic regression, n = p, λ = 0.5), the Gaussian-perturbed estimator's GED decays at approximately p^{−0.5} (slopes −0.47 to −0.54 on log-log plots), whereas the Laplace-perturbed estimator of Zou et al. (2025) shows non-vanishing GED (slopes 0.03, −0.03, −0.01). On the high-dimensional IMDb dataset (p = 3161, n = 1579), the Gaussian framework yields substantially lower GED than both Laplace and (φ,ε,δ) certifiability baselines, while on low-dimensional MNIST (p = 784) all notions are comparable.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is with Zou et al. (2025), which uses (φ,ε)-certifiability with Laplace noise and concludes that at least two Newton steps are required for vanishing GED. The proposed (φ,ε)-Gaussian certifiability with a single noisy Newton step achieves vanishing GED at rate ~p^{−0.5} (empirically confirmed with log-log slopes of −0.47 to −0.54), while the Laplace baseline's GED remains essentially flat (slopes near zero) as dimension grows, demonstrating the suboptimality of ε-certifiability with Laplace noise. The second comparison is with Sekhari et al. (2021)'s (φ,ε,δ) certifiability bounds, which are plotted in Figure 4 (p = 1000–10000); the paper shows that replacing (φ,ε)-Gaussian certifiability with (φ,ε,δ(ε)) certifiability at the same ε leads to strictly worse GED and UED performance. On the high-dimensional IMDb dataset (p = 3161), the Gaussian framework clearly outperforms both baselines, while on low-dimensional MNIST (p = 784) all three certifiability notions yield comparable results, confirming that the advantage of Gaussian certifiability is specifically a high-dimensional phenomenon.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Newton-based certified unlearning framework of Sekhari et al. (2021) and Guo et al. (2020) from the low-dimensional regime (p ≪ n) to the proportional high-dimensional regime (p ~ n), where standard Ω(1) strong convexity and O(1) smoothness assumptions on per-example losses provably fail even for ridge regression. It departs from the closest prior high-dimensional work, Zou et al. (2025), by replacing their (φ,ε)-certifiability with a new (φ,ε)-Gaussian certifiability notion grounded in the trade-off function and Blackwell ordering framework of Dong et al. (2022). The key conceptual departure is showing that the 'two Newton steps required' conclusion of Zou et al. is an artifact of their suboptimal certifiability notion being incompatible with Gaussian noise, rather than a fundamental limitation of one-step Newton unlearning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper resolves a concrete theoretical discrepancy in the certified unlearning literature (one vs. two Newton steps) by identifying the certifiability notion as the root cause, providing a clean and actionable resolution for the field. The (φ,ε)-Gaussian certifiability framework is positioned as the canonical and tightest notion for high-dimensional privacy certification, subsuming all previously proposed notions (ε-δ, Rényi, ε-certifiability) with lossless conversions. Open questions include extending the analysis to nonconvex losses (deep networks), approximate Hessian methods for very high dimensions, distributional/concept unlearning, and online unlearning scenarios. The current scope is limited to generalized linear models with separable regularizers, so practical applicability to modern deep learning architectures remains unestablished.
