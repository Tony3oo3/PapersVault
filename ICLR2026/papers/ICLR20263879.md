## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) are widely used to make deep representations interpretable by tying each latent component to a human-defined concept, yet the paper argues they never actually enforce a bottleneck: requiring z_j to predict c_j does not prevent z_j from also encoding nuisance information unrelated to c_j. This information leakage undermines both interpretability (z_j cannot be fully explained by c_j) and the validity of concept-level interventions, and the standard CBM intervention procedure (sigmoid inverse) is shown to violate Bayes' rule.

## Input / Output

The model takes RGB images (32×32 for MPI3D/Shapes3D/CIFAR-10, 224×224 for CUB/AwA2) and per-sample concept labels during training, producing a task prediction, per-concept predictions, and an m-dimensional bottleneck representation z where each component z_j is a scalar (or low-dim vector) tied to one concept. The encoder is stochastic (Gaussian with σ_x=1 for MCBMs), and the representation head uses fixed prototypes: ±λ for binary concepts, λ·onehot(c_j) for multiclass, and λ·c_j for continuous, with λ=3 fixed across all experiments.

## Method

MCBMs extend CBMs by adding a representation head q_φ(ẑ_j|c_j) that predicts each latent component z_j from its corresponding concept c_j, and training jointly minimizes a KL divergence D_KL(p_θ(z_j|x) ‖ q_φ(ẑ_j|c_j)) that upper-bounds the conditional mutual information I(Z_j; X | C_j). Under the Gaussian distributional assumptions, this KL reduces to a simple MSE between the encoder output f_θ(x)_j and the prototype g_φ^z(c_j), making the IB term computationally trivial. The full training objective (Eq. 6) combines three terms: standard task prediction loss, concept prediction loss (as in CBMs), and the IB regularization weighted by hyperparameter γ. This design makes z_j a minimal sufficient statistic of c_j rather than merely a sufficient one, ensuring that modifying z_j constitutes a valid intervention on c_j alone. The paper further demonstrates that CBMs' reliance on One-vs-Rest binarization and sigmoid-inverse interventions introduces calibration failures and violates probability theory, issues that MCBMs avoid by natively supporting multiclass concepts and providing a well-defined p(z_j|c_j).

## Results

On the synthetic datasets MPI3D and Shapes3D, MCBMs with high γ achieve a URR of 0.0% for both task-related and task-unrelated nuisances, completely eliminating non-concept information from the representation—no other method achieves this. On real-world datasets (CIFAR-10, CUB, AwA2), MCBMs still provide the strongest nuisance reduction (e.g., 17.6% vs. 18.5% for CBM on CIFAR-10; 2.4% vs. 3.8% for CBM on CUB; 0.7% vs. 1.4% for CBM on AwA2), while maintaining comparable concept prediction accuracy (84.8% on CIFAR-10, 95.8% on CUB, 97.6% on AwA2). Notably, CEMs and ECBMs often retain *more* nuisance information than even Vanilla Models (e.g., CEM reaches 43.2% URR on MPI3D vs. 35.0% for VMs), and MCBMs are the only method that fully eliminates task-unrelated nuisances across all γ values.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard CBMs (Koh et al., 2020), MCBMs achieve substantially lower URR on synthetic data (0.0% vs. 28.1% on MPI3D, 0.0% vs. 18.1% on Shapes3D) and modestly lower on real data (17.6% vs. 18.5% on CIFAR-10, 2.4% vs. 3.8% on CUB, 0.7% vs. 1.4% on AwA2), while matching concept accuracy. Against CEMs (Espinosa Zarlenga et al., 2022), MCBMs win decisively: CEMs actually *increase* nuisance leakage relative to Vanilla Models on MPI3D (43.2% vs. 35.0%) and CIFAR-10 (27.2% vs. 19.8%), whereas MCBMs reduce it. Against ECBMs (Xu et al., 2024), MCBMs again dominate—ECBMs retain 47.1% URR on Shapes3D (worse than VMs at 45.5%) while MCBMs reach 0.0%. Against ARCBMs and SCBMs (Havasi et al., 2022; Vandenhirtz et al., 2024), which impose a bottleneck at the prediction level rather than the representation level, MCBMs achieve lower URR on synthetic data (0.0% vs. 24.3–28.2% on MPI3D) and also produce more interpretable representations (higher CKA and Disentanglement scores), whereas ARCBMs/SCBMs leave the representation itself uninterpretable. On intervention reliability, MCBMs at low/medium γ outperform ARCBMs and SCBMs when fewer than 100% of concepts are intervened, and all methods converge on AwA2 where nuisance levels are already low.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Concept Bottleneck Models (Koh et al., 2020) and the Information Bottleneck framework (Tishby et al., 2000), arguing that CBMs only enforce a sufficient statistic (z_j can predict c_j) but not a minimal sufficient statistic (z_j contains only c_j). It departs from prior leakage-mitigation approaches—Hard CBMs, Autoregressive CBMs, Stochastic CBMs, and Energy-based CBMs—which impose bottlenecks at the prediction level or through architectural changes, by instead enforcing the bottleneck directly at the representation level via a variational KL-divergence term. The representation head design draws on Prototypical Networks (Snell et al., 2017) for its class-dependent prototype structure, and the variational optimization follows the reparameterization trick from VAEs (Kingma, 2013).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges a core assumption in the interpretability community: that CBMs' 'bottleneck' name reflects an actual information constraint. The empirical finding that CEMs and ECBMs can retain more nuisance information than vanilla models, while MCBMs achieve near-complete elimination (0.0 URR on synthetic datasets) without sacrificing concept accuracy, provides strong evidence for the design flaw. Key limitations include the new hyperparameter γ that must be tuned per dataset, the inevitable task-accuracy drop when concepts are incomplete (e.g., MPI3D drops from 100% to ~25% at high γ), and the open question of how to retain task-relevant nuisances (n_y) in an auxiliary channel without compromising the interpretability of the concept-specific components. The theoretical demonstration that CBM intervention procedures violate Bayes' rule (sigmoid inverse ignores the prior) further undermines the causal claims often made for CBMs in sensitive domains.
