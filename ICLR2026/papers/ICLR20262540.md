## Problem

Existing multimodal in-context learning (ICL) methods face a fundamental trade-off: manual curation produces clean, well-structured contexts but is labor-intensive and task-specific, while similarity-based retrieval scales automatically yet introduces semantically irrelevant or structurally inconsistent examples that degrade downstream ICL performance. Moreover, retrieval-based approaches are static one-shot processes that cannot adapt their contextualization strategy based on observed effectiveness, unlike human reasoning that iteratively refines example selection.

## Input / Output

Given a multimodal query (a text question paired with an image) and a context corpus of text-image pairs, ContextNav produces a noise-minimized set of 8 contextual shots (by default) that is concatenated with the query and fed to a downstream MLLM. The downstream MLLM returns both a final answer and an auxiliary textual feedback signal assessing context adequacy, which the agent records in its memory to guide subsequent workflow optimization. The entire pipeline is training-free and operates entirely through prompt-driven MLLM reasoning and tool invocations.

## Method

ContextNav formulates multimodal contextualization as a closed-loop agentic workflow governed by an Operational Grammar Graph (OGG), a directed graph whose nodes are atomic operations (embedding, retrieval, denoising) and whose edges encode valid execution dependencies. The agent's MLLM policy plans a toolchain on the OGG, executes resource-aware multimodal embedding to build and maintain a vector database, retrieves an initial candidate pool via Top-k similarity search, then applies two denoising stages: agentic retrieval (the MLLM reasons over each candidate to filter semantic noise) and structural alignment (the MLLM rewrites candidate text to match the query's interrogative/imperative/narrative structure). A memory module stores associations between past toolchain configurations and downstream ICL feedback, enabling the agent to adaptively refine its orchestration strategy across multiple timesteps rather than committing to a single one-shot plan.

## Results

ContextNav achieves an average ICL gain of 16.8% across six MLLMs and 16.2% across eight datasets, substantially outperforming the prior state-of-the-art (7.6% model-wise, 8.2% dataset-wise). On the strongest downstream model (Gemini-2.0-flash), ContextNav reaches 0.604 average accuracy, compared to 0.542 zero-shot, 0.504 random sampling, 0.561 VL-ICL, and 0.559 MMICES. The framework consistently improves performance on complex composite-task benchmarks (BlindTest, RealWorld, CharXiv, MathVision) where non-agentic baselines often degrade below zero-shot, while also maintaining gains on single-task benchmarks (CLEVR, FOMI, TextOCR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ContextNav is compared against three ICL baselines: Random Sampling, VL-ICL (manual coarse-filtering + random sampling), and MMICES (cascaded visual-then-textual retrieval). ContextNav wins on average accuracy across all six MLLMs, with the largest margins on composite-task benchmarks: e.g., on MathVision with Gemini-2.0-flash, ContextNav achieves 0.550 vs. 0.341 (Random), 0.483 (VL-ICL), and 0.467 (MMICES). Random Sampling consistently degrades performance below zero-shot on most composite datasets (e.g., −30.7% ICL gain on MathVision for Gemini-2.0-flash), confirming that unfiltered retrieval is harmful. VL-ICL and MMICES show modest gains on single-task benchmarks (CLEVR, FOMI) but remain unstable or negative on noisy composite tasks, whereas ContextNav delivers positive gains in nearly all settings. In extended comparisons (Appendix C), ContextNav also outperforms Many-shot ICL (100–800 random shots) and is competitive with Cache of Thought, though it trails the latter under strict 1–2 shot conditions where explicit reasoning traces are distilled from a master model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of multimodal in-context learning (extending ICL from text to vision-language tasks), retrieval-based contextualization (VL-ICL, MMICES), and the emerging paradigm of LLM-driven agentic workflows with graph-based orchestration (LangGraph, Zhang et al. 2025a). It departs from prior retrieval-based ICL methods by inserting an MLLM policy agent between raw similarity search and the downstream model, performing human-like semantic filtering and structural alignment on retrieved candidates. The Operational Grammar Graph, inspired by graph-based agent orchestration engines, is repurposed here as a structured action space that constrains valid tool sequences and enables closed-loop optimization via downstream ICL feedback — a mechanism absent from static one-shot retrieval pipelines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ContextNav addresses a practical bottleneck in multimodal ICL: the scalability-robustness tradeoff where automated retrieval introduces noise that degrades performance, while manual curation is infeasible at scale. The framework's model-agnostic design (validated across six MLLMs from 3B to GPT-4o) and its demonstration that 8 well-curated shots outperform 100–800 random shots make it a practical recipe for production multimodal ICL systems. Key limitations include the ~22.5K token overhead and ~3.3s latency per ICL iteration, which restrict real-time deployment. The authors identify RL-based policy optimization over the OGG-defined environment as a natural next step, and the framework's structured state/action/feedback decomposition makes it a ready-made RL training substrate for future agentic ICL research.
