## Problem

VLM-based mobile GUI agents trained with Chain-of-Action-Thought (CoaT) reasoning suffer from limited trajectory diversity, causing overfitting to fixed reasoning patterns during supervised fine-tuning. Existing self-training remedies either rely solely on final-answer correctness (ignoring intermediate reasoning quality) or require expensive process-level annotations to build process reward models, which is especially costly in GUI environments that depend on real devices or simulators.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262016/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent consumes a mobile UI screenshot (up to 1540×1540 or 1280×720 resolution), a natural-language task instruction, and the sequence of prior actions as multi-turn dialogue context. It produces a four-stage CoaT output: a page description, an action thought, a discrete action decision (click/scroll/type/press/stop), and grounding coordinates normalized to a 0–1000 integer grid in the format . The multi-turn dialogue structure is critical: it prevents the image modality from dominating attention over textual instructions and guarantees a well-formed final action is always generated.

## Method

MobileIPL introduces an iterative preference learning loop built on a CoaT-tree. At each reasoning step, the model samples K=3 continuations, forming a tree whose leaf nodes are complete actions scored by a rule-based reward (spatial distance for clicks, F1 for text inputs, plus type/format bonuses). Leaf values are backpropagated to intermediate nodes via discounted averaging, yielding per-step quality scores without any learned process reward model. Contrastive T-DPO pairs are extracted from the tree: in β-trees (mixed correct/incorrect leaves), higher-value samples serve as positives against lower-value ones; in γ-trees (all incorrect), the ground-truth action is the positive. A three-stage instruction evolution strategy (general GUI Q&A, widget relationships, advanced FAQ) generated by GPT-4o is mixed into warm-up SFT to expand the reasoning space and prevent overfitting. The entire sampling–scoring–DPO cycle is repeated for multiple rounds, each time using the updated policy as the new base, until performance converges.

## Results

MobileIPL achieves state-of-the-art Step.Acc of 69.15% on AITZ, 74.29% on AMEX (long-horizon, avg. trajectory length 15+), and 72.7% on AndroidControl. On AITZ, it outperforms the strongest continual pre-training baselines OS-Atlas-7B (65.11%) and UI-Tars-7B (65.61%) by +4.04% and +3.54% respectively, and narrowly edges out Falcon-UI-7B (69.10%) which was pre-trained on 3M GUI images. On AMEX, MobileIPL surpasses the previous SOTA SphAgent-7B by +3.58% and both OS-Atlas and UI-Tars by +3.69%. On AndroidControl OOD subsets, MobileIPL shows the least performance degradation (70.0% app-unseen, 72.2% task-unseen) compared to OS-Atlas (60.7%, 66.2%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MobileIPL wins against all 11 baselines on AITZ Step.Acc, with the largest margins over OS-Atlas-7B (+4.04%) and UI-Tars-7B (+3.54%), both of which use Qwen2-VL-7B as backbone with extensive GUI-domain continual pre-training. Against Falcon-UI-7B (pre-trained on 3M GUI images), MobileIPL achieves a marginal +0.05% advantage while using substantially less training data. On AMEX, MobileIPL (74.29%) outperforms SphAgent-7B (69.14%), OS-Atlas-7B (70.33%), and UI-Tars-7B (70.33%) by 3.58–3.69%, confirming gains in long-horizon multi-step tasks. On AndroidControl, MobileIPL ties Falcon-UI-7B on Step.Acc (72.7%) but surpasses UI-Tars-7B (+0.2%) and OS-Atlas-7B (+1.5%); critically, on OOD subsets it leads OS-Atlas by +9.3% (app-unseen) and +6.0% (task-unseen), indicating far better generalization. Compared to RL baselines, MobileIPL outperforms GRPO (66.29%) by +2.86% on AITZ with roughly 3.4× fewer rollouts per sentence than SPO-Chain (~27 vs ~54), achieving a better efficiency–accuracy trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts the MCTS-inspired iterative preference learning framework of Xie et al. (2024) to the mobile GUI agent domain, replacing the expensive process reward model with a rule-based reward that exploits the structured action space (click coordinates, input text, action types). The reasoning backbone is the CoaT paradigm from AITZ (Zhang et al., 2024b), reformulated as a multi-turn dialogue to balance cross-modal attention. The DPO loss (Rafailov et al., 2023) is applied at individual thinking steps rather than full trajectories, and the reward design follows ReFT (Luong et al., 2024) with discrete type/format signals and continuous spatial/textual similarity. A key departure from prior self-training work is the instruction evolution strategy, which uses GPT-4o to generate diverse Q&A pairs from UI screenshots to prevent the model from collapsing into fixed reasoning patterns during warm-up SFT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a data-efficient self-training loop (iterative CoaT-tree sampling + T-DPO) can match or exceed continual pretraining models like OS-ATLAS and UI-TARS that consume millions of GUI samples, suggesting that reasoning-quality optimization may be more important than raw pretraining scale for GUI agents. The rule-based reward eliminates the need for costly step-level human annotation, which is a significant practical barrier in the GUI domain where environments require real devices or simulators. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more complex multi-app or cross-device tasks, whether the fixed 4-step CoaT structure is optimal for all action types (the PRESS accuracy dip in early rounds hints at sensitivity), and whether the method transfers to other structured-output domains beyond mobile GUIs.
