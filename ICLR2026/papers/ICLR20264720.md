## Problem

Existing automatic UV parameterization methods optimize geometric properties (conformality, equiareality, bijectivity) but neglect two perceptually critical criteria: semantic awareness (UV charts should align with meaningful 3D parts for texture editing and cross-shape transfer) and visibility awareness (cutting seams should lie in occluded, low-exposure regions to minimize visible artifacts). No prior approach addresses both objectives simultaneously in a unified, differentiable framework.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264720/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a triangle mesh (vertex positions, face indices, per-vertex normals) and produces per-vertex UV coordinates organized into a unified atlas, along with implicit cutting-seam placement. The semantic-aware pipeline outputs per-part UV islands packed into a G×G grid atlas with uniform texel density; the visibility-aware pipeline outputs a single global UV chart with seams biased toward low-AO regions. Training is per-mesh and fully unsupervised—no ground-truth UV maps or part labels are required.

## Method

The framework builds on a bi-directional cycle-mapping backbone (four pointwise MLPs: deform, wrap, cut, unwrap) that enforces 2D-3D-2D and 3D-2D-3D consistency to learn low-distortion UV mappings. For semantic awareness, the mesh is first partitioned into K connected parts via Shape Diameter Function (ray-cast thickness → 1-D GMM → alpha-expansion graph cuts), the backbone is then trained independently on each sub-mesh, and per-part UV islands are aggregated into a grid-based atlas with uniform scale. For visibility awareness, per-vertex ambient occlusion is computed as an exposure proxy, seam vertices are identified in UV space via a differentiable soft-max approximation of the max UV-distance to 3D neighbors, and an AO-weighted seam loss (L_AO = Σ s_i·AO_i / Σ s_i) is backpropagated to steer cuts toward occluded regions. The two perceptual objectives are integrated as additional loss terms on top of the geometry-preserving backbone losses, with tunable weights controlling the distortion-vs-perception trade-off.

## Results

On the semantic-aware pipeline, the proposed method achieves a Hamming distance of 0.3188 and Rand Index of 0.8151, substantially outperforming the best baselines (FlexPara multi-chart: 0.5980 / 0.6902; Blender: 0.8634 / 0.7173). On the visibility-aware pipeline, the method reduces mean seam AO to 0.6065 (lower = more occluded), compared to 0.7855 for OptCuts and 0.8604 for FlexPara single-chart, while maintaining conformality of 0.9175. In a 45-expert user study, the proposed method receives 93.70% preference for visibility-awareness and 80.22% for semantic-awareness. The trade-off is a modest drop in equiareality (0.6707 vs. 0.9341 for xatlas) and conformality (0.9123 vs. 0.9792 for xatlas) relative to pure geometry-optimizing baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against FlexPara (multi-chart), the closest learning-based baseline, the proposed semantic-aware method nearly halves the Hamming distance (0.3188 vs. 0.5980) and improves Rand Index by 12.5 points (0.8151 vs. 0.6902), at the cost of ~4.7 points in conformality and ~9 points in equiareality. Against commercial tools (xatlas, Blender, Maya), the gap in semantic alignment is even larger: xatlas scores 0.8896 Hamming distance versus 0.3188 for the proposed method, while xatlas retains the best conformality (0.9792) and equiareality (0.9341) among all baselines. For visibility-awareness, the proposed method (mean seam AO 0.6065) substantially outperforms both OptCuts (0.7855) and FlexPara single-chart (0.8604), confirming that seams are relocated to more occluded regions; however, OptCuts still leads on conformality (0.9341) and equiareality (0.8934) due to its dedicated geometry optimization. In the expert user study, the proposed method dominates with 93.70% preference for visibility and 80.22% for semantic alignment, while all baselines collectively receive less than 20% in each category. The combined semantic+visibility pipeline (0.6369 equiareality) recovers some geometric fidelity over the pure visibility pipeline (0.6093) by partitioning the mesh into smaller parts before optimizing seam placement.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper explicitly builds on the bi-directional cycle mapping backbone from FlexPara (Zhao et al., 2025), Flatten Anything (Zhang et al., 2024b), and Nuvo (Srinivasan et al., 2024), and states it does not claim novelty for the geometry-preserving backbone itself. Its contribution is the design and integration of two perceptual objectives—semantic-aware partition-and-parameterize (using Shape Diameter Function + GMM + graph cuts) and visibility-aware AO-weighted seam placement—on top of this established foundation. Prior work has addressed semantic UV mapping (Vermandere et al., 2024; Mukherjee et al., 2024) and visibility-aware seam placement (Sheffer & Hart, 2002; Ray et al., 2010; Weill-Duflos et al., 2023) individually, but the paper positions itself as the first to jointly optimize both within a single unsupervised differentiable framework.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

UV mapping is identified as a major manual bottleneck in 3D content creation, and this work offers a practical, code-available pipeline that automates it while producing semantically organized charts and less-visible seams—properties directly relevant to texture painting, transfer, and mass production workflows. Key limitations include a measurable trade-off in geometry metrics (equiareality drops from ~0.68 to ~0.61–0.64), a ~24× training-time overhead for the visibility-aware pipeline dominated by seam extraction, and an intentionally simple grid-based UV packer that sacrifices space utilization. Open questions include whether the semantic and visibility objectives can be jointly learned end-to-end without the partitioning stage, whether the AO proxy generalizes to non-convocative or animated meshes, and the authors' stated future direction of jointly learning UV parameterization and texture generation.
