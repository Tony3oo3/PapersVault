## Problem

Static, task-specific LLM benchmarks (GSM8K, MMLU, BIG-bench) are increasingly fragile due to data contamination, saturation, and overfitting, and they fail to capture the interactive, agentic reasoning that modern LLMs are designed for. The paper asks whether evaluation can be reframed as an interaction-driven process—measuring a model by how well it teaches weaker students—rather than by its own direct task performance.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264212/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes MCQ items (converted from 60 datasets across Knowledge, Reasoning, Understanding, and Multilingual domains) and a pool of four weak student models (LLaMA3.2-1B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, MiniCPM-2B, InternLM2.5-1.8B). Critically, the teacher LLM receives the question and the student's full reasoning transcript but never sees the answer choices or gold labels, preserving item difficulty and blocking option-matching shortcuts. The output is a per-student, per-round accuracy gain aggregated into a Comprehensive Ability score, plus four decomposed metrics (Application, Judgment, Guidance, Reflection) computed over up to T=3 tutoring rounds.

## Method

Teach2Eval operates as a blinded-choice tutoring loop: a weak student first answers an MCQ, then the teacher observes the question and the student's full transcript (without options) and returns a correctness judgment plus free-form guidance; the student revises its answer, and this repeats for T rounds. The Comprehensive Ability (CA) metric is the mean cumulative accuracy lift across M heterogeneous students, while four fine-grained abilities grounded in Bloom's taxonomy are extracted: Application (zero-shot accuracy), Judgment (first-round correctness of the teacher's right/wrong call), Guidance (first-round repair rate on initially wrong items), and Reflection (a multiplicative per-round repair multiplier over rounds ≥2). Data construction converts all 60 source datasets into MCQs by harvesting real errors from 10 weak models as hard distractors, using GPT-4o for format normalization, and randomizing correct-option position. The key design insight is that the teacher's inability to see options forces it to diagnose reasoning errors and propose generalizable fixes, making the signal robust to memorization while the multi-student, multi-round interaction naturally disentangles higher-order cognitive traits orthogonal to existing benchmarks.

## Results

Across 33 LLMs and 60 datasets, Teach2Eval achieves Spearman correlations of 0.944 (Chatbot Arena) and 0.975 (LiveBench), substantially outperforming Direct Evaluation (0.734 and 0.861 respectively). The top-scoring models by Comprehensive Ability are Claude-Sonnet-4-thinking (14.12), o4-mini (12.38), and Gemini-2.5-pro (11.77), while the lowest are Yi1.5-6B-Chat (0.91) and InternLM2.5-7B (1.01). Higher-order abilities (Judgment, Guidance, Reflection) correlate above 0.9 with Comprehensive Ability, whereas Application Ability (direct zero-shot accuracy) shows notably lower correlation, confirming that teaching effectiveness captures orthogonal capabilities beyond raw answer accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is Direct Evaluation, which scores models by their own zero-shot accuracy on the same MCQ items. Teach2Eval outperforms Direct Evaluation on every correlation metric: Spearman improves from 0.734→0.944 on Chatbot Arena and 0.861→0.861→0.975 on LiveBench, while Kendall's Tau improves from 0.558→0.853 and 0.695→0.886 respectively. Ablation experiments confirm robustness: removing any one of the four student models still yields correlations above 0.926 (Chatbot Arena) and 0.94 (LiveBench), and Teach2Eval converges to stable correlations faster than Direct Evaluation under cumulative sampling. An answer-revelation ablation shows that even when teachers explicitly state the correct answer, Teach2Eval rankings remain highly correlated (Spearman 0.921) with the full protocol, indicating the method rewards diagnostic guidance rather than mere answer disclosure. The paper does not compare against other automated evaluation frameworks (e.g., LLM-as-a-Judge) head-to-head, positioning itself primarily against the direct-scoring paradigm.

## Relation to Prior Work

Teach2Eval repurposes the teacher-student paradigm from knowledge distillation as an evaluation signal rather than a training mechanism, drawing conceptual inspiration from the Feynman Technique and Bloom's taxonomy. It departs from static benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, MATH) and LLM-as-a-Judge approaches by never exposing the teacher to answer options or gold labels, instead measuring the student's performance gain under blinded guidance. While prior simulated-teaching works (multi-agent classrooms, Dean-Teacher-Student pipelines) use role-playing for data generation or reasoning refinement, Teach2Eval is the first to convert the full teaching interaction into a standardized, multi-dimensional evaluation metric with explicit contamination-resistance properties.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework addresses two persistent pain points in LLM evaluation—data contamination and benchmark saturation—by making memorization structurally irrelevant and creating a moving target through heterogeneous student errors. The finding that higher-order abilities (Judgment, Guidance, Reflection) correlate above 0.9 with overall capability while direct Application Ability correlates much lower suggests that current leaderboards may misrank models that are strong at answering but weak at reasoning about others' errors. Open questions include whether the MCQ format constrains the evaluation to a narrower skill space than open-ended interaction, how the method behaves as student models approach teacher-level capability, and whether the fine-grained metrics can be directly used as training objectives.
