## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) in diffusion models relies on a static, manually tuned guidance weight that improves perceptual quality but degrades distributional alignment with the true conditional distribution. No principled mechanism exists to adapt the guidance weight to both the specific conditioning signal and the denoising timestep, leaving a gap between the empirical success of CFG and its theoretical mismatch with the target distribution.

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pre-trained conditional and unconditional denoiser (U-Net for ImageNet/CelebA at 64×64, or a 1.05B-parameter Flux MMDiT flow-matching model for MS-COCO at 512×512) along with a conditioning signal (class label or CLIP+T5 text embeddings). It learns a lightweight MLP guidance network (~600K–6M parameters) that outputs a single non-negative scalar weight ω_{c,(s,t)} for each (conditioning, source-time, target-time) triple. At inference, the learned weights are plugged into the standard DDIM/flow-matching sampling loop to produce generated images.

## Method

The core contribution is a self-consistency learning objective: for a fixed clean sample (x_0, c), noise x_0 to time t, denoise back to time s < t using the guided denoiser, and require the resulting distribution at time s to match the true noising-process distribution at time s. This condition is enforced by minimizing an MMD (energy kernel) between the guided and true conditional-noising distributions, which avoids the high variance of the weaker marginal-consistency objective. The guidance weights are parameterized by a small MLP with ReLU output (ensuring non-negativity), taking log-SNR-transformed timesteps and conditioning embeddings as input. The approach is extended to reward-guided sampling by adding a reward loss (e.g., CLIP score) to the self-consistency objective, regularized to prevent reward hacking. Empirically, the learned weights are highly conditioning-dependent and time-varying, yielding lower FID than constant or limited-interval guidance baselines on ImageNet, CelebA, and MS-COCO.

## Results

On ImageNet 64×64, the self-consistency method with conditioning-dependent guidance weights ω_{c,(s,t)} achieves FID 1.99 and IS 73.62, outperforming the unguided model (FID 4.46), constant guidance (FID 2.40), and limited interval guidance (FID 2.11). On CelebA 64×64, the same method achieves FID 2.10 vs. 2.37 for LIG. On MS-COCO 512×512 text-to-image, the self-consistency method achieves FID 18.01, substantially below the unguided baseline (24.74), constant guidance (31.2), LIG (24.95), and clamp-linear (27.14), though with a slightly lower CLIP score (0.295) than the guided baselines (~0.305). Adding a CLIP score reward recovers the CLIP score to 0.306 but increases FID to 28.37.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against limited interval guidance (LIG, Kynkäänniemi et al. 2024), the proposed self-consistency method consistently wins on FID across all three benchmarks: 1.99 vs. 2.11 on ImageNet 64×64, 2.10 vs. 2.37 on CelebA 64×64, and 18.01 vs. 24.95 on MS-COCO 512×512. On Inception Score, the conditioning-agnostic variant (ω_{(s,t)}) actually edges out LIG (76.70 vs. 71.60 on ImageNet), while the conditioning-dependent variant is slightly lower (73.62 vs. 71.60). Against the clamp-linear schedule (Wang et al. 2024), the method achieves a substantially lower FID on MS-COCO (18.01 vs. 27.14) and also outperforms it on ImageNet (1.99 vs. values reported in Appendix I.1). The main trade-off is that on MS-COCO, the self-consistency method achieves a lower CLIP score (0.295) than both LIG (0.304) and clamp-linear (0.305), though adding a CLIP reward term recovers the CLIP score to 0.306 at the cost of higher FID (28.37).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper generalizes Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) by replacing the fixed scalar guidance weight ω with a learned function ω_{c,(s,t)} that depends on both the conditioning signal and the denoising time interval, trained via a self-consistency condition that enforces distributional matching between the true noising process and the guided backward process using MMD. It departs from prior dynamic guidance strategies (LIG, clamp-linear, annealing schedules) that hand-design time-dependent weight schedules, and from score-matching-based approaches (which the authors show collapse to zero guidance), by instead optimizing a distributional consistency objective that is provably stronger than marginal consistency yet low-variance in practice. The reward-guided extension connects to the reward hacking literature by regularizing the reward loss with the self-consistency term to prevent degenerate solutions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical takeaway is that guidance weights should be conditioning-dependent, and the learned weights exhibit substantial per-class/per-prompt variability (some classes receive zero guidance, others receive aggressive guidance over wide intervals), suggesting that a single global ω is fundamentally suboptimal. However, the T2I results reveal a tension: adding a CLIP score reward improves prompt alignment but substantially degrades FID (28.37 vs. 18.01), indicating that reward-guided sampling and distributional fidelity are in conflict under this formulation. Open questions include whether the self-consistency objective admits theoretical characterization, whether the approach transfers to video or protein generation, and whether the conditioning-dependence can be exploited for more targeted control without the FID penalty observed with explicit rewards.
