## Problem

Adapting LLMs to Knowledge Base Question Answering (KBQA) is challenging because it requires multi-step reasoning over large-scale structured knowledge bases, yet existing approaches either incur prohibitive costs by prompting LLMs with entire knowledge graphs or fine-tune LLMs on the full complex reasoning task without reducing its inherent difficulty. The paper identifies that prior work focuses on improving LLM reasoning capability while overlooking strategies to decompose and simplify the task itself.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263166/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a natural language question and a large-scale knowledge graph (Freebase: ~88M entities, 20K relations, 126M triples) as input, and produces answer entities paired with executable logical forms (SPARQL queries) as output. The question is consumed in a staged pipeline: first to retrieve a small subgraph, then the subgraph is deterministically converted into a small set of reasoning paths (typically fewer than 10), and finally each path is individually evaluated. The final answer is selected from the highest-confidence executable LF, with a fallback to tail entities of paths marked as valid.

## Method

Chain-of-Decomposition (CoD) factorizes the answer-generation distribution P(A|Q,G) via a causal graph into three modular subtasks: retrieval, reformulation, and reasoning. The retrieval step uses a fine-tuned T5-base model as a binary classifier over relations in a hop-filtered subgraph to identify answer-relevant triples (LLM-free). The reformulation step is a parameter-free, rule-based algorithm that links head-tail entities across hop groups to construct structured reasoning paths from the retrieved triples. The reasoning step fine-tunes Llama-2 7B (LoRA, rank=8) on a simplified binary classification task—determining whether a given reasoning path is logically valid—combined with next-token prediction for LF generation. This decomposition isolates computation-heavy retrieval and rule-based reformulation from the LLM, so the model only needs to learn a lightweight verification task rather than the full multi-hop KBQA problem, enabling efficient fine-tuning with limited data.

## Results

On WebQSP, CoD (Llama-2-7B fine-tuned) achieves 86.54% Hits@1 and 81.24% F1, surpassing the strongest prompting baseline FiDeLis with GPT-4 (84.39% Hits@1, 78.32% F1) by over 2 points on both metrics. On CWQ, CoD reaches 77.42% Hits@1 and 65.70% F1, outperforming FiDeLis+GPT-4 (71.47% Hits@1, 64.32% F1) by 5.95 and 1.38 points respectively. The retriever component achieves 91.04% top-5 accuracy on WebQSP and 89.93% on CWQ, far exceeding DPR (35.02%/10.24%) and BM25 (15.25%/2.77%). On the GrailQA generalization test, CoD attains 78.2% overall F1, outperforming DeCAF (76.0%) by 2.2 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoD outperforms all nine baselines on every reported metric across both WebQSP and CWQ. Against the strongest prompting-based method FiDeLis with GPT-4, CoD gains +2.15% Hits@1 and +2.92% F1 on WebQSP, and +5.95% Hits@1 and +1.38% F1 on CWQ, despite using a 7B open-source model (Llama-2) rather than a proprietary API model. Compared to the closest fine-tuning baseline RoG, CoD improves by +3.39% Hits@1 and +11.43% F1 on WebQSP, and by a substantial +16.03% Hits@1 and +9.53% F1 on CWQ, demonstrating that the decomposition strategy is particularly effective for complex multi-hop questions. DeCAF, which jointly decodes answers and logical forms, is surpassed by +4.44% Hits@1 on WebQSP and +7.00% Hits@1 on CWQ. Non-LLM methods (TIARA, UniKGQA, HGNet) trail CoD by 9–15 points in Hits@1, confirming the advantage of LLM-based reasoning. The paper further proves mathematically (Appendix C) that RoG's objective is a special case of CoD's factorization under two independence assumptions, providing a theoretical unification.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the reasoning-path paradigm introduced by RoG (Luo et al., 2023b) and DeCAF (Yu et al., 2022), but departs from them by applying a causal-graph factorization (from Peters et al., 2017) to decompose KBQA into three modular subtasks: retrieval, reformulation, and reasoning. While RoG and DeCAF fine-tune LLMs to handle the full reasoning burden end-to-end, CoD isolates the computation-heavy retrieval (a small T5 classifier) and rule-based reformulation (a parameter-free template) from the LLM, which only performs a lightweight binary validity check. The authors formally prove that RoG's EM-based objective is a special case of their factorization under two independence assumptions, positioning CoD as a more general formulation that avoids those assumptions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical implication is that a 7B open-source model, fine-tuned on a single simplified subtask, can match or exceed GPT-4 augmented with retrieved knowledge on standard KBQA benchmarks, suggesting that task decomposition is a viable alternative to scaling model size for structured reasoning. However, all experiments are confined to Freebase-based datasets (WebQSP, CWQ) and a single primary backbone (Llama-2 7B), leaving open whether the decomposition strategy transfers to other knowledge bases, open-domain settings, or more advanced LLMs like Llama-3 or DeepSeek-R1. The reliance on a hand-crafted reformulation template also raises questions about robustness to knowledge graphs with more complex or irregular structures.
