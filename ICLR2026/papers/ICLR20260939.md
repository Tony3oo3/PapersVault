## Problem

Current native-resolution MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, Kimi-VL, Ovis2.5, SAIL-VL) claim any-resolution support via fixed-size patching with sequence packing, yet systematic evaluation reveals significant performance degradation and instability at both low and high pixel budgets. The paper identifies the rigid use of a single patch size as the primary architectural cause: it is too coarse for information-dense low-resolution images and too local for information-sparse high-resolution images, creating a representational mismatch across the resolution spectrum.

## Input / Output

The method accepts RGB images at arbitrary native resolution and aspect ratio (tested from 112×112 to 3584×3584 pixels) along with a text prompt, and produces an autoregressive text response. The key operational detail is that the patch size is selected per-image at inference time from a discrete candidate set [6, 56], so the number of vision tokens varies dynamically with each input. The method is designed as a drop-in: it plugs into existing pretrained fixed-patch MLLMs without modifying the vision encoder, projector, or LLM backbone, and remains fully compatible with sequence packing for batched multi-image processing.

## Method

AdaPatch computes a lightweight information-density estimate ρ by measuring cosine similarity between vision-encoder features extracted at native patch size s and at a halved patch size s/2 on a downsampled copy of the image; larger ρ indicates greater downsampling loss and thus higher density. A power-law rule then maps (ρ, r) → s*: higher resolution favors larger patches while higher density favors smaller ones, with hyperparameters α=0.5 and β=0.3 controlling relative sensitivity. To make pretrained fixed-patch models operate at arbitrary patch sizes, the paper introduces PI-resize, a training-free closed-form weight transformation using the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of the interpolation matrix that preserves inner products for upsampling and yields optimal L2 approximation for downsampling. A training-based alternative (MSPE) allocates independent embedding parameters per patch size, initialized via PI-resize, and jointly fine-tunes them end-to-end with adaptive per-image patch sampling. Both variants leave the backbone unchanged and integrate directly with the existing sequence-packing pipeline.

## Results

AdaPatch (training-free) consistently improves over the fixed-patch baseline across 4 MLLMs on 16 benchmarks. Headline gains include MME (Qwen2.5-VL-3B: 2135.90 → 2210.41, +74.5), OCRBench (Ovis2.5-2B: 706 → 814, +108; SAIL-VL-2B: 783 → 855, +72), and MMMU (Qwen2.5-VL-3B: 49.22 → 51.89, +2.67). Gains are largest on information-dense or resolution-heterogeneous tasks (OCRBench, DocVQA, ChartQA) and smallest or slightly negative on a few general benchmarks (e.g., LLaVABench drops 66.30 → 65.34 for Qwen2.5-VL-3B). The training-based variant (MSPE) further boosts OCRBench from 418 → 535 (LLaVA1.5-665K) and 466 → 579 (LLaVA1.6-779K) on SAIL-VL-2B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's primary comparison is AdaPatch (adaptive patch size) versus the vanilla fixed-patch (s=14) version of the same pretrained model, evaluated on Qwen2.5-VL-3B, SAIL-VL-2B, Ovis2.5-2B, and Kimi-VL-A3B. AdaPatch wins on 14 out of 16 benchmarks for Qwen2.5-VL-3B, with the largest margins on OCRBench (+24), MME (+74.5), and MMBench (+3.34); it loses only on LLaVABench (−0.96). For Ovis2.5-2B, AdaPatch achieves the most dramatic improvement on OCRBench (+108 points, 706→814), reflecting that this model's fixed patch size is particularly mismatched to low-resolution OCR images. SAIL-VL-2B shows consistent gains across all benchmarks (e.g., OCRBench +72, MME +77.7). Kimi-VL-A3B, already the strongest baseline, still benefits on most tasks (MME +39, OCRBench +25) but regresses slightly on LLaVABench (−1.3). The training-based MSPE variant further narrows the gap on OCRBench (418→535 on LLaVA1.5-665K), confirming that adaptive patch selection during training compounds the inference-time gains.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the native-resolution processing paradigm established by NaViT and adopted by recent MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, Ovis2.5, Kimi-VL), which use fixed-size patches with sequence packing. It departs from this line by identifying the fixed patch size itself as the architectural bottleneck and introducing per-image adaptive patch size selection guided by an information-density estimate. The training-free conversion (PI-resize) builds on FlexiViT's idea of resizing patch-embedding weights, while the training-based variant (MSPE) directly extends the multi-scale patch embedding framework of Liu et al. (2024c) to the MLLM setting with adaptive per-sample patch selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper challenges the widely assumed 'any-resolution' robustness of current MLLMs by showing systematic degradation at both low and high pixel budgets, attributing it to a single architectural choice (fixed patch size). The proposed fix is lightweight, training-free, and drop-in, making it immediately applicable to existing pretrained models without retraining. However, the absolute gains are modest (typically 1–3 points), and the method only addresses the visual-encoding stage; open questions remain about whether adaptive patching interacts beneficially with LLM-side token compression, continual learning, or multi-image reasoning settings the authors flag as future work.
