## Problem

Existing 3D Multimodal Large Language Models (3D-MLLMs) depend on explicit 3D inputs such as point clouds, reconstructed scenes, or external depth/pose modules, imposing heavy data acquisition, preprocessing, and computation costs that limit scalability and real-world deployment. The paper asks whether a compact model can jointly perform 3D scene reconstruction and vision-language reasoning directly from monocular video, exploiting the intrinsic interdependence between geometric structure and semantic understanding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262833/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 32 uniformly sampled monocular video frames per scene (shorter side resized to 518, cropped to multiples of 14) along with a natural-language text query, and produces multiple outputs simultaneously: a 3D question answer or dense caption, a 3D bounding box for grounding, camera intrinsics/extrinsics, a metric-scale depth map, and a 3D point cloud. Notably, the reconstruction and reasoning branches share a single DINOv2 encoder and a Cross-Task Adapter, with a one-way gradient flow design that prevents VL-task losses from back-propagating into the reconstruction model, keeping the geometry branch stable.

## Method

Vid-LLM uses a shared DINOv2-L encoder whose tokens are split into geometry and semantic streams via lightweight MLP projections, then bidirectionally fused through a Cross-Task Adapter (CTA) that employs 16 learnable Bridge Tokens as shared memory units attending to both streams via multi-head attention. The reconstruction branch feeds CTA-enhanced geometric features into a Global-Frame Attention backbone with camera and DPT depth heads, augmented by a Metric Depth Model (DPT-style decoder with bin-based depth and adaptive bin-center refinement) that provides real-scale calibration via weighted-least-squares alignment between relative and metric depth. The reasoning branch back-projects 2D semantic features into 3D camera-frame coordinates using predicted depth and pose, encodes them as positional embeddings, and adds them to the semantic tokens to form 3D patch tokens fed into an LLM. Training follows a two-stage strategy: Stage 1 performs dual-teacher distillation (DINO for geometry, CLIP for semantics) with a structural consistency loss on concatenated token Gram matrices; Stage 2 jointly optimizes reconstruction losses, VL-task losses, and a robust metric-depth loss, with the CTA serving as a stable bridge that enables geometry-semantics interaction without destabilizing either branch.

## Results

Vid-LLM achieves the best performance among all video-based models across every 3D vision-language benchmark. On ScanQA, it attains CIDEr 101.9, BLEU-4 15.8, METEOR 18.3, ROUGE 49.5, and EM@1 27.6; on SQA3D, EM@1 57.3. On 3D visual grounding (ScanRefer), it reaches Acc@0.25 63.2 and Acc@0.5 56.4, surpassing even the best 3D-based method (3DRS: 62.9/56.1). In the joint reconstruction-reasoning setting (Table 5), Vid-LLM achieves the best 3D VL scores (ScanQA 45.7, Scan2Cap 53.2, ScanRefer 59.8) while also delivering the fastest inference at 1.6 s/scene.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among video-based baselines, Vid-LLM outperforms the strongest competitor 3DRS† (which uses VGGT-generated geometry as input) by an average margin of ~11% on ScanQA (e.g., CIDEr 101.9 vs. 94.7, EM@1 27.6 vs. 23.9) and by 2.8 points on SQA3D EM@1 (57.3 vs. 54.5). On 3D visual grounding (ScanRefer), Vid-LLM surpasses even the best 3D-based method 3DRS (Acc@0.25: 63.2 vs. 62.9; Acc@0.5: 56.4 vs. 56.1) and the video-based VGLLM (53.5/47.5). On Scan2Cap dense captioning, Vid-LLM's METEOR@0.5 (28.7) is within 0.3 of the best 3D-based model LLaVA-3D (29.0), despite not using explicit depth or point clouds. In the joint reconstruction-reasoning comparison (Table 5), Vid-LLM achieves a 47.6% average relative improvement over Uni3DR2 and 12.1% over VGLLM-Rec on 3D VL tasks, while also delivering the best reconstruction F-score (0.582) and fastest inference (1.6 s/scene). The only setting where Vid-LLM trails is ScanQA against the full 3D-input 3DRS (CIDEr 101.9 vs. 104.8), which has access to dense point clouds rather than video alone.

## Relation to Prior Work

Vid-LLM builds directly on VGGT's Global-Frame Attention backbone and prediction heads for the reconstruction branch, and on DPT-style decoders for metric depth estimation, while using DINOv2 as a shared visual encoder and LLaVA-3D/CLIP as distillation teachers. It departs from the dominant 3D-MLLM paradigm (which requires point clouds, reconstructed scenes, or explicit depth as input) by reconstructing geometry entirely from monocular video and coupling it with reasoning through a novel Cross-Task Adapter with learnable Bridge Tokens. The two-stage dual-teacher distillation strategy is specific to this architecture, transferring geometric priors from VGGT and semantic knowledge from CLIP into a single shared encoder before joint fine-tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By eliminating the need for external 3D data, depth sensors, or pre-computed poses, Vid-LLM makes 3D scene understanding accessible in settings where only video is available, which is the dominant real-world capture modality. The ablation showing that simple concatenation of a strong reconstructor (VGGT) with a 3D-LLM (LLaVA-3D) underperforms the integrated model despite better reconstruction quality highlights that geometry-semantics interaction mechanisms matter as much as raw geometric accuracy. Key limitations include dependence on monocular video (limited viewpoints degrade reconstruction, as shown in the challenging cases) and the fact that reconstruction quality still bounds reasoning performance; scaling to larger or dynamic scenes remains an open question.
