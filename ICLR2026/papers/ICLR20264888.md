## Problem

RKHS-based policy optimization in reinforcement learning has been restricted to first-order gradient methods because the Hessian in RKHS is an infinite-dimensional operator whose inverse is intractable to compute explicitly. This paper introduces the first second-order (Newton-type) optimization framework for RL policies represented in RKHS, bridging the gap between non-parametric policy representations and curvature-aware optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264888/cropped_page8_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes N trajectories of length T (state-action-reward sequences) sampled under the current policy, and produces a policy function h in RKHS that defines a discrete action distribution π_h(a|s) = exp(T·h(s,a))/Z via a softmax over kernel evaluations. The optimization problem's dimensionality is NT (the total number of state-action pairs in the batch), and the policy update is a weighted sum of kernel sections K((s_t^i, a_t^i), ·) with coefficients α ∈ R^{NT}. A Gaussian kernel is used throughout, and the temperature T controls the sharpness of the resulting action distribution.

## Method

The key insight is to avoid explicitly inverting the infinite-dimensional Hessian operator by instead solving a cubic-regularized auxiliary objective: Δh = argmin {⟨∇J, h̄⟩ + ½⟨∇²J∘h̄, h̄⟩ + (β/6)‖h̄‖³}. The second-order Fréchet derivative is derived as an operator in the tensor product space H_K ⊗ H_K, with components involving outer products of kernel sections and covariance operators of the policy's action distribution. The Representer Theorem then reduces this infinite-dimensional problem to a finite-dimensional optimization in R^{NT} with an explicit algebraic form: a linear term (vector v from kernel evaluations of the gradient), a quadratic term (matrix H combining outer products of first-order terms and covariance matrices), and a cubic regularization. The resulting finite-dimensional problem is solved via conjugate gradient descent, and the policy is updated as h_{k+1} = h_k + η·Δh. Theoretical analysis proves convergence to a stationary point and local quadratic convergence rate under Lipschitz Hessian assumptions.

## Results

The paper presents results as learning-curve figures rather than a numerical summary table. In the toy Asset Allocation environment (Figure 1), Policy Newton in RKHS exhibits clear quadratic convergence toward the optimal policy, while the parametric Policy Newton converges to a suboptimal local maximum. On Gymnasium benchmarks (Figure 2: CartPole, Lunar Lander, Inverted Pendulum, Hopper), Policy Newton in RKHS achieves higher episodic rewards with fewer iterations than both the first-order RKHS baseline and the parametric Policy Newton, with the advantage being most pronounced in the continuous Hopper environment where it reaches high rewards significantly faster.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Two baselines are compared: (1) Policy Gradient in RKHS (Lever & Stafford, 2015), a first-order non-parametric method, and (2) parametric Policy Gradient / Policy Newton (Maniyar et al., 2024), which uses a linear model with polynomial feature expansion. Policy Newton in RKHS outperforms the first-order RKHS baseline on all four Gymnasium environments in both convergence speed and final episodic reward, attributed to the incorporation of Hessian curvature information. Against the parametric Policy Newton, Policy Newton in RKHS also wins on sample efficiency across all tasks, and in the toy Asset Allocation experiment it additionally avoids the suboptimal local maximum that traps the parametric Newton method, which the authors attribute to the richer set of descent directions afforded by the data-dependent kernel basis. The paper does not report a single scalar metric (e.g., final reward at a fixed budget) in tabular form; all comparisons are made via learning-curve plots over 5 independent runs with 95% confidence intervals.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected lines of work: (1) RKHS-based policy optimization, which has been limited to first-order gradient methods (Paternain et al., 2020; Lever & Stafford, 2015), and (2) cubic-regularized Newton methods for parametric policy optimization (Maniyar et al., 2024; Doikov et al., 2024). Prior second-order kernel methods (Calandriello et al., 2017a,b) addressed Newton steps in RKHS but only for online learning with fixed data distributions, making them inapplicable to RL where the trajectory distribution shifts with the policy. The key technical departure is using the Representer Theorem to reduce the infinite-dimensional Hessian optimization to a finite-dimensional problem in R^{NT}, combined with cubic regularization to avoid explicitly inverting the (ill-posed) infinite-dimensional Hessian operator.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a genuine gap by providing the first second-order optimization framework for non-parametric RKHS policies in RL, with theoretical guarantees of convergence to a local optimum and a local quadratic convergence rate. However, the quadratic convergence proof relies on deterministic (exact gradient/Hessian) assumptions, and the stochastic convergence rate analysis is explicitly deferred to future work. Empirical validation is limited to relatively simple environments (CartPole, Lunar Lander, Hopper), and the computational cost scales with NT (total trajectory data points), raising open questions about scalability to high-dimensional or long-horizon tasks like Humanoid. The authors suggest integration with neural network architectures as a promising direction for tackling more complex scenarios.
