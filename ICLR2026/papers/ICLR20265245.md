## Problem

LLM-based web agents face a fundamental context-management trade-off on long-horizon tasks: ReAct-style append-only histories accumulate noisy raw observations that saturate the context window, while uniform full-history summarization at every step risks irreversible loss of critical details. No existing mechanism allows an agent to selectively preserve fine-grained information or abstract away completed sub-tasks at different scales within a single trajectory.

## Input / Output

The agent receives a user question and a schema of available web-browsing tools, and at each step produces a single parsed text block containing four components: a chain-of-thought monologue, a JSON folding directive specifying a step range [k, t-1] and a replacement summary string, a concise explanation of the next action's motivation, and either a tool call or a final answer. The folding directive's range parameter determines the operation mode: k = t-1 triggers granular condensation of one step, while k < t-1 triggers deep consolidation of multiple prior summaries plus the latest interaction into one coarser block. The context passed to the model at step t is the structured triplet (question, tools, multi-scale state summaries, latest interaction), where the latest interaction retains the full unsummarized record of the previous step.

## Method

AgentFold restructures the agent's context as a dynamic cognitive workspace partitioned into Multi-Scale State Summaries (a sequence of condensed blocks at varying granularities) and a Latest Interaction (the verbatim record of the most recent step). At each step, the model jointly reasons about what to fold and what action to take, emitting a folding directive that either crystallizes the latest interaction into a new fine-grained summary (granular condensation) or retracts a chain of prior summaries plus the latest interaction and replaces them with a single coarse abstraction (deep consolidation). Training data is generated by a Fold-Generator pipeline that uses GLM-4.5 and DeepSeek-V3.1 to produce trajectories with rejection sampling to enforce format correctness, followed by supervised fine-tuning on Qwen3-30B-A3B. The design is compatible with KV-cache reuse because folding operations only modify a localized suffix of the context, preserving the prefix cache for subsequent steps.

## Results

AgentFold-30B-A3B achieves 36.2% on BrowseComp, 47.3% on BrowseComp-ZH, 62.1% on WideSearch (ItemF1), and 67.0% on GAIA (text-only). On WideSearch, it attains the best overall score among all agents tested, surpassing OpenAI-o3 (60.0%) and Claude-4-Sonnet (62.0%). On BrowseComp, it outperforms all open-source agents including DeepSeek-V3.1-671B (30.0%) and GLM-4.5-355B (26.4%), and also exceeds the proprietary OpenAI-o4-mini (28.3%). Its context grows sub-linearly, reaching only ~7k tokens after 100 turns, enabling scaling to 256+ interaction turns where the 355B GLM-4.5 baseline saturates and degrades.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AgentFold-30B-A3B (30B params, 3B active) outperforms all open-source web agents on BrowseComp, scoring 36.2% versus DeepSeek-V3.1-671B's 30.0% and GLM-4.5-355B's 26.4%—a 6.2 and 9.8 point margin over models 20× and 12× its size, respectively. On BrowseComp-ZH it reaches 47.3%, surpassing GLM-4.5 (37.5%) and o4-mini (44.3%) but trailing DeepSeek-V3.1 (49.2%) by 1.9 points. On WideSearch, AgentFold achieves the best score of 62.1%, edging out Claude-4-Sonnet (62.0%) and OpenAI-o3 (60.0%). On GAIA it scores 67.0%, beating DeepSeek-V3.1 (63.1%) and GLM-4.5 (66.0%) but falling short of o3 (70.5%) and Claude-4-Sonnet (68.3%). In the scaling experiment (Figure 4), AgentFold continues improving up to 256 turns while GLM-4.5's append-only context saturates and degrades beyond 64 turns, confirming that the folding mechanism is the key differentiator for long-horizon robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

AgentFold builds directly on the ReAct reasoning-action-observation loop but departs from its append-only context policy, which the paper identifies as the root cause of context saturation on long-horizon tasks. It also contrasts with uniform full-history summarization methods (MEM1, MemAgent) that compress the entire trajectory at every step, arguing that such rigid, step-wise policies risk irreversible loss of critical details. The key conceptual departure is treating context curation as a learned, multi-scale operation—granular condensation for single steps and deep consolidation for multi-step sub-tasks—rather than a fixed external policy, drawing an analogy to human retrospective consolidation (Miller, 1956; Newell et al., 1972).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 30B-A3B model (3B active parameters) outperforms 671B and 355B general-purpose models on BrowseComp while maintaining a context of only ~7k tokens after 100 turns, enabling scaling to 500+ turns where ReAct-based agents saturate. The KV-cache-compatible design (prefix-preserving folds) offers substantial inference efficiency gains (~7GB memory saving per instance at 100 turns). Key open questions include whether reinforcement learning can discover non-obvious folding policies beyond what SFT captures, whether the approach generalizes to non-web agent tasks, and whether the data-generation pipeline's reliance on very large teacher models (GLM-4.5, DeepSeek-V3.1) limits reproducibility.
