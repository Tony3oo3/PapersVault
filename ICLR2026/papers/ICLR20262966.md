## Problem

Existing data-driven rounding methods for post-training quantization, such as OPTQ/GPTQ, only correct weight quantization error within a single layer while ignoring activation quantization error and residual error propagated from previously quantized layers. This creates a systematic bias when the full-precision input X and the partially-quantized input X̃ diverge, a mismatch that arises naturally in both weight-only and weight-activation quantization settings but has not been explicitly addressed in the literature.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a weight matrix W ∈ R^{N×N'}, a full-precision calibration activation matrix X ∈ R^{m×N}, a partially-quantized calibration activation matrix X̃ ∈ R^{m×N} (requiring two forward passes through the model), and a fixed quantization grid defined by bit width, per-channel scaling factor s, and zero point z. It produces a quantized weight matrix Q ∈ A^{N×N'} where each entry is selected from the discrete alphabet A. Columns of W are processed independently and in parallel, and the algorithm operates block-by-block, resetting X̃ = X at the start of each block to limit error accumulation across blocks.

## Method

Qronos is an iterative greedy algorithm that alternates between two steps at each position t: (1) error correction, which selects q_t to optimally minimize the reconstruction error ‖Xw − X̃q‖² holding all other weights fixed, and (2) error diffusion, which updates the remaining unquantized weights w_{≥t+1}^{(t)} via a least-squares projection to absorb the one-step rounding error. The key theoretical contribution (Theorem 3.1) proves that from the second iteration onward, q_t reduces to a simple round-to-nearest of the current weight estimate, and Lemma 3.2 further shows the diffusion step is equivalent to a Cholesky-based rank-1 update Δ^{(t)} = −(w_t^{(t−1)} − q_t) · L_{≥t+1,t}/L_{tt}. This equivalent formulation reduces peak memory from O(mN) to O(N²) (an 18× reduction on Llama3-8B) and yields up to 13.8× algorithmic speedup over the naive closed-form evaluation. Unlike OPTQ, Qronos explicitly minimizes ‖XW − X̃Q‖_F, correcting both weight and activation quantization error as well as residual error from prior layers, which Corollary 3.4 shows reduces to OPTQ's behavior only when X = X̃.

## Results

On Llama3 8B weight-only quantization with HIP+MagR transforms, Qronos achieves 9.3 WikiText2 perplexity at 2-bit (vs. 10.4 for OPTQ, 11.3 for GPFQ, 9.6 for GPTAQ) and 18.0 at 1.58-bit (vs. 43.3 for OPTQ, 35.8 for GPFQ, 35.3 for GPTAQ). At 2-bit on Llama3 1B, Qronos yields a 1.4× perplexity reduction over OPTQ (17.8 vs. 24.6) and +3.3% zero-shot accuracy (42.6 vs. 39.3); at 1.58-bit the gap widens to a 4.9× perplexity reduction (39.3 vs. 2e2). On weight-activation quantization (W4A4KV4), Qronos with SpinQuant achieves 8.7 perplexity on Llama3 8B, outperforming GPTAQ (8.7) and OPTQ (8.9), with the largest relative gains appearing in the most aggressive quantization settings.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Qronos consistently outperforms all three baselines (OPTQ, GPFQ, GPTAQ) across every model size, bit width, and quantization setting tested. Against OPTQ (the strongest baseline), Qronos reduces 2-bit WikiText2 perplexity by 1.4× on Llama3 1B (17.8 vs. 24.6) and by 2.4× at 1.58-bit (39.3 vs. 2e2), while also improving zero-shot accuracy by 3.3–5.1 points. GPTAQ is the closest competitor at 2-bit (9.6 vs. 9.3 perplexity on 8B) but degrades substantially at 1.58-bit (35.3 vs. 18.0), where Qronos's explicit correction of activation mismatch provides a decisive advantage. GPFQ trails both OPTQ and Qronos in nearly all settings, with the largest gaps at extreme bit widths. The paper further shows that Qronos's relative advantage grows as quantization becomes more aggressive: the gap over OPTQ widens from weight-only (W4) to weight-activation (W4A4) to KV-cache quantization (W4A4KV4), confirming that Qronos's ability to correct both weight and activation errors is most valuable when multiple error sources interact.

## Relation to Prior Work

Qronos extends the greedy discrete-optimization rounding lineage (GPFQ, OPTQ/GPTQ) by generalizing the objective from minimizing ‖X(W−Q)‖ (weight-only error, as in OPTQ) to minimizing ‖XW − X̃Q‖, which explicitly accounts for activation quantization and residual error propagated from previously quantized layers. The efficient implementation via Cholesky decomposition mirrors OPTQ's key mechanism but operates on the cross-covariance structure (G = X̃ᵀX, H = X̃ᵀX̃) rather than a single Hessian, enabling the mismatched-input correction. A notable theoretical by-product (Corollary 3.4) reframes OPTQ's apparently local greedy updates as globally optimal cumulative error correction, providing the first geometric interpretation of OPTQ in terms of grid selection followed by orthogonal projection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap: in weight-activation and KV-cache quantization, the input to a layer (X̃) differs from the original full-precision input (X), and prior rounding methods silently ignore this mismatch, introducing systematic bias. Qronos's consistent improvements grow with quantization aggressiveness (W4A4KV4 > W4A4 > W4), making it particularly valuable for the 4-bit-and-below regime that is becoming standard for LLM deployment. The 18× peak-memory reduction and 13.8× single-layer speedup make the method practically deployable, and its integration into Brevitas lowers the adoption barrier. Open questions include whether the framework extends to non-uniform or learned quantization grids (the authors explicitly limit to scaled min-max), and whether the theoretical interpretation of OPTQ as cumulative error correction can inform new algorithmic designs beyond the current greedy sequential approach.
