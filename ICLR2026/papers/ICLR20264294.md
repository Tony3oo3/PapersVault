## Problem

Degradation-agnostic image restoration requires a single model to handle diverse corruptions (noise, haze, rain, blur, low-light, and their composites), but existing approaches face a fundamental efficiency-performance dilemma: prompt-based and vision-language models provide versatility at high computational cost, while lightweight alternatives sacrifice restoration quality. The paper tackles how to achieve state-of-the-art restoration across heterogeneous degradations with a compact model that also generalizes to unseen corruption types.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264294/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded RGB image with no degradation-type label and produces a restored RGB image of the same spatial dimensions. It is trained on 128×128 random crops and evaluated at full resolution (e.g., 224×224 for FLOPs computation), handling additive, multiplicative, and convolutional degradations individually or in composite combinations. Notably, the model trained on five standard degradations transfers zero-shot to real-world underwater images without any fine-tuning, and the Tiny variant runs in ~42 GB GPU memory with only 16G FLOPs per 224×224 image.

## Method

MIRAGE introduces two core mechanisms within a 4-scale U-shaped encoder-decoder. First, the Mixed Degradation Adaptation Block (MDAB) partitions each feature map's channels into three parallel branches—dynamic depthwise convolution for local textures, multi-head self-attention for global context, and a channel-wise MLP for channel statistics—repurposing the empirically observed low-rank redundancy in attention channels rather than discarding it; a gated inter-branch mutual fusion step with learnable coefficients blends cross-path context before the FFN. Second, a manifold regularization term computes SPD covariance matrices from shallow (patch-embedding) and latent (encoder-output) features, vectorizes and projects them through 1-layer MLPs, and applies an InfoNCE contrastive loss in this second-order space, preserving channel-dependence structure that Euclidean contrastive learning collapses. The total training objective combines L1 pixel loss, a real-valued Fourier loss (λ=0.1), and the SPD contrastive term (τ=0.1), optimized end-to-end with Adam and cosine decay.

## Results

On the 3-degradation all-in-one setting (dehazing, deraining, denoising), MIRAGE-S (10M params) achieves 32.91 dB average PSNR and 0.919 SSIM, outperforming MoCE-IR (25M, 32.73 dB) by +0.18 dB with 60% fewer parameters, and DA-RCOT (50M, 32.60 dB) by +0.31 dB with 80% fewer parameters. The Tiny variant (MIRAGE-T, 6.21M params, 16G FLOPs) reaches 32.77 dB, surpassing MoCE-IR-S (11M, 32.51 dB) by +0.26 dB while using less than half the computation. In the zero-shot underwater enhancement setting, MIRAGE-S achieves 17.29 dB PSNR, exceeding MoCE-IR by +1.38 dB without any underwater training data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MIRAGE-S (10M) outperforms all major all-in-one baselines on the 3-degradation benchmark: +0.18 dB over MoCE-IR (25M), +0.22 dB over AdaIR (29M), +0.31 dB over DA-RCOT (50M), and +0.85 dB over PromptIR (36M), while using 60–80% fewer parameters. The Tiny variant (6M) already surpasses MoCE-IR-S (11M) by +0.26 dB and PromptIR (36M) by +0.71 dB, demonstrating that the channel-wise functional decomposition recovers most of the performance of larger models at a fraction of the cost. On composited degradations (CDD11), MIRAGE-S (10M) achieves 29.33 dB average, beating MoCE-IR (11M, 29.05 dB) by +0.28 dB and OneRestore (6M, 28.47 dB) by +0.86 dB. For adverse weather removal, MIRAGE-S leads all baselines with 33.98 dB average PSNR, exceeding Histoformer (33.68 dB) by +0.30 dB and MPerceiver (32.93 dB) by +1.05 dB. In the zero-shot underwater setting, MIRAGE-S (17.29 dB) surpasses MoCE-IR (15.91 dB) by +1.38 dB without any underwater training data, confirming strong cross-domain generalization. The only minor loss is on RainDrop PSNR in Table 4, where MIRAGE-S (32.78 dB) trails Histoformer (33.06 dB) by 0.28 dB, though it still leads on SSIM (0.945 vs. 0.944).

## Relation to Prior Work

MIRAGE carves new ground by repurposing the well-documented channel redundancy in multi-head attention (rather than pruning it) into a principled three-way functional decomposition: convolution for local textures, attention for global context, and MLP for channel statistics. This departs from the dominant prompt-based paradigm (PromptIR, MoCE-IR, AdaIR) that inflates parameters with learned visual prompts, and from prior contrastive-learning IR work (AirNet, DA-RCOT) that operates in Euclidean residual space. The SPD manifold regularization extends the contrastive-learning-for-IR line by exploiting the natural depth-asymmetric covariance structure of shallow vs. latent features, replacing flat-vector alignment with second-order geometric alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6M-parameter model matching or exceeding 36M+ prompt-based baselines offers a practical, deployable all-in-one restorer for resource-constrained settings (mobile, edge, environmental monitoring), directly addressing the efficiency-performance dilemma the field has struggled with. The zero-shot transfer to underwater enhancement without domain-specific training suggests the learned representations capture degradation-invariant structure rather than task-specific shortcuts. Open questions include whether the channel decomposition and SPD alignment scale to video restoration or higher-resolution settings, and whether the modest per-task gains (0.1–0.5 dB) persist under more diverse or spatially-varying real-world degradations beyond the synthetic benchmarks used here.
