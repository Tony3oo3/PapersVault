## Problem

LLM hallucinations are typically treated as a failure mode, yet a narrative-driven subset called confabulation—filling gaps with self-consistent, evidence-adjacent stories—could be harnessed for social good. The paper addresses the labor-intensive bottleneck of critical fabulation (Saidiya Hartman's framework for reconstructing erased histories of marginalized figures) by asking whether LLMs can perform evidence-bounded narrative reconstruction at archival scale, and no prior work has systematically evaluated this capability or the conditions that shape it.

## Input / Output

The input is a character-centric timeline of timestamped, one-sentence events (sourced from unpublished Black intellectual history documents) with exactly one event replaced by the literal token [MASK], accompanied by a fixed instruction prompt and an optional event-type hint (agentive, relational, observational, cognitive, or role). The model must output a single reconstructed event summary in one concise, active-voice sentence (≤30 words). Evaluation computes cosine similarity between story-emb narrative embeddings of the generated and ground-truth events, with a human-tuned threshold (ε*=73.13, macro-F1=0.805) determining correctness.

## Method

The paper operationalizes critical confabulation as an open-ended narrative cloze task and builds a rigorous evaluation pipeline on the Black Writing and Thought Collection (BWTC), a 20,686-document archive of mostly unpublished Black intellectual history. Ground truth is constructed by identifying 156 'hidden figures' via long-tail name extraction (frequency <51) and Aho-Corasick auditing against OLMo-2's public training data, then extracting source-bounded event timelines with GPT-o3 under strict citation constraints. Nineteen models (the audited OLMo-2 family plus unaudited open-weight and proprietary baselines) are evaluated zero-shot under seven prompt conditions that steer toward confabulation (e.g., null-shot, eccentric persona, LLM discussion, HaluEval-style distractor generation). A two-stage contamination audit (Boyer-Moore sentence-level string search followed by behavioral cosine-similarity probing) validates that the evaluation data is genuinely unseen by the audited model. The key finding is that GPT-5-CHAT leads at 59.7% accuracy, OLMo-2-7B reaches 58.8% under one prompt, and performance is highly prompt-sensitive, event-type-dependent (best on biographical 'role' events, worst on 'cognitive' events), and robust to decoding temperature.

## Results

Critical confabulation (narrative cloze on masked historical events) is a highly challenging task: most of the 19 evaluated LLMs remain below 50% accuracy, with the overall best being GPT-5-CHAT at 59.7% (with EVENT_TYPE hint and HaluEval prompt). OLMo-2-7B achieves 58.8% under Eccentric Automatic Prompts with EVENT_TYPE, surpassing many larger open-weight models, while Qwen3-4B-0725 reaches 53.8% as the strongest small open-weight model. Providing an EVENT_TYPE hint consistently improves accuracy by +2 to +10 points across nearly all model–prompt pairs, and performance is highly prompt-sensitive (e.g., Null-Shot is most reliable, HaluEval weakest for most models).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a single method but benchmarks 19 LLMs (4 audited OLMo-2 variants, 10 open-weight, 3 proprietary) on the new critical confabulation task. GPT-5-CHAT is the clear overall leader, the only model exceeding 50% on most prompts and peaking at 59.7%. OLMo-2-7B punches well above its weight, reaching 58.8% (second-highest single score) and outperforming larger open-weight peers like Qwen2.5-32B (49.6%) and Llama-3.1-8B (47.9%). Qwen3-4B-0725 is the best small open-weight model at 53.8%, surpassing models 3–8× its size. Crucially, paired significance tests show no statistically significant advantage for unaudited models over their audited OLMo-2 counterparts (average p = 0.354), indicating no detectable memorization benefit on this task. Within most model families, larger variants outperform smaller ones, though the gap narrows under strong prompts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate literatures: Saidiya Hartman's 'critical fabulation' methodology from African American studies (using speculative storytelling to recover omissions in archives shaped by structural inequality) and the NLP phenomenon of LLM confabulation (narrative-driven hallucinations that fill gaps with self-consistent stories, Sui et al. 2024). It operationalizes this bridge as a narrative cloze task (Chambers & Jurafsky, 2008) applied to character-centric timelines extracted from the unpublished Black Writing and Thought Collection, positioning LLMs as co-creators for digital humanities scholarship. The data contamination audit draws on Boyer-Moore and Aho-Corasick string matching plus behavioral probes inspired by Oren et al. (2024), while evaluation leverages the story-emb narrative embedding model (Hatzel & Biemann, 2024) rather than topical semantic similarity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper reframes hallucination from a pure failure mode into an optimizable resource for evidence-bounded knowledge production, with direct implications for how LLMs can support humanities scholars working with large-scale, under-documented archives. Key limitations include strong prompt sensitivity (performance swings of 10+ points across templates), restriction to a single language and cultural tradition, and the fact that the harder 'unknown unknowns' problem (detecting which events are missing) remains unaddressed. Open questions include whether the findings generalize beyond Black intellectual history, how to build ethical safeguards against compounding archival violence, and whether training-time methods can explicitly optimize for well-specified, evidence-constrained confabulation rather than relying on prompt engineering.
