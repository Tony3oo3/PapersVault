## Problem

Previous scene metamer research used simple generative models to synthesize textures and shapes under fixed eye position, but did not address how post-gist changes in fixation affect scene metamerism or what objects a person believes to exist in their blurred peripheral view. This paper tackles the more ecologically valid problem of generating scene metamers aligned with the latent representations humans form after multiple free-viewing fixations, where understanding is built incrementally from sparse foveal glimpses combined with peripheral gist.

## Input / Output

The model takes a foveated scene representation: high-resolution DINOv2 patch tokens masked to only the 1–10 fixated locations (each mapped to a 14×14 pixel region on a 32×32 grid) plus all patch tokens from a downsampled-then-upsampled (blurred) version of the same image. It produces a 512×512 RGB photorealistic scene image in approximately 5 seconds of real-time generation, intended as a hypothesis for what the viewer believes occupies their non-fixated peripheral vision. The model does not reconstruct the original image verbatim; the lossiness of DINOv2 embeddings and stochastic sampling ensure generated scenes are plausible alternatives rather than copies.

## Method

MetamerGen adapts Stable Diffusion 1.5 with a dual-stream visual conditioning mechanism built on DINOv2-Base patch tokens. A binary fixation mask is applied to high-resolution DINOv2 tokens to retain only fixated patches (foveal stream), while a blurred version of the image is processed through DINOv2 without masking (peripheral stream). Each stream is compressed from 1024 tokens to 32 conditioning tokens via a separate Perceiver-based resampler (8 layers of cross-attention with learned latent queries), then injected into the UNet's cross-attention layers through dedicated key-value projections with learned scaling factors (λ_foveal=1.2, λ_peripheral=0.7). Only the two resampler networks and their projection matrices are trained (on ~118K MS-COCO images with random fixation counts and blur levels); the pre-trained UNet and text conditioning (set to empty string) remain frozen. DINOv2 was chosen over CLIP because its self-supervised training objectives cause individual patch tokens to encode both local foveal detail and parafoveal contextual relationships, making a single token a natural proxy for the information gathered during one fixation.

## Results

In the primary behavioral experiment (n=45, 300 trials), MetamerGen achieved a 29.4% metamerism rate when conditioned on participants' own fixations, compared to 27.7% for randomly-sampled fixations (p=0.24). In the ablation experiment (n=10), the full dual-stream model (foveal + peripheral) yielded a 54.5% metamerism rate, versus 45.8% for peripheral-only and only 8.4% for foveal-only conditioning. On image quality (FID vs. COCO-10k-test), MetamerGen consistently outperforms a text-to-image SD-1.5 baseline with random captions across all blur levels and peripheral scales. A forward stepwise regression (R²=0.039) identified DreamSim semantic distance as the strongest single predictor of metameric judgments (ΔR²=0.10), followed by vertical Gabor intensity (ΔR²=0.006) and depth map RMSE (ΔR²=0.003).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's sole external baseline is Stable Diffusion 1.5 used as a text-to-image model with 10k random COCO captions. On FID (Figure 2), MetamerGen consistently achieves lower (better) FID scores than this baseline across all tested peripheral blur levels and peripheral scale parameters, confirming that the dual-stream foveal/peripheral conditioning provides meaningful image-quality gains over unconditioned text-to-image generation. Internally, the ablation experiment reveals that peripheral conditioning is the dominant driver of metamerism (45.8% vs. 8.4% for foveal-only), while the full model's 54.5% rate demonstrates that foveal details add a meaningful ~9-point improvement over peripheral-only generation. Notably, the primary experiment's metamerism rate (29.4%) is lower than the ablation's (54.5%), which the authors attribute to the foveal-only condition acting as a low anchor that lowered participants' threshold for 'same' judgments in the ablation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the scene metamerism literature (Freeman & Simoncelli, 2011; Balas et al., 2009; Rosenholtz et al., 2012), which used simple generative models to synthesize textures and shapes matching peripheral vision statistics under fixed eye position, to the richer problem of free-viewing fixations and post-gist scene understanding. Architecturally, it builds on Stable Diffusion's latent diffusion framework and adapts the IP-Adapter/T2i-Adapter paradigm for visual conditioning, replacing CLIP embeddings with DINOv2 patch tokens processed through Perceiver-based resamplers (from Flamingo) to create a dual-stream foveal-peripheral conditioning mechanism. The specific formulation of generating photorealistic scenes from foveated inputs (sparse high-resolution fixation patches + globally blurred peripheral context) is presented as a novel image-to-image synthesis problem not addressed by prior adapter-based conditioning work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MetamerGen provides cognitive scientists with a practical tool to generate fixation-specific hypotheses about human scene understanding that can be tested behaviorally, bridging a gap between computational generative models and perceptual psychology. The finding that pixel-level similarity (PSNR) is irrelevant to metamerism while high-level semantic alignment (DreamSim) is the strongest predictor reframes how we think about what the visual system encodes. However, the small regression R² (0.039), the near-equivalence of random and actual fixation metamerism rates (29.4% vs 27.7%), and inherited Stable Diffusion artifacts (faces, text) limit the strength of conclusions. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to dynamic scenes, whether the DINOv2 encoder choice is truly optimal for all scene types, and whether the metamerism framework can be extended to video or multi-view settings.
