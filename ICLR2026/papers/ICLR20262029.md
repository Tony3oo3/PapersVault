## Problem

Existing VLM-based reward methods for RL suffer from three coupled limitations: single-image similarity cannot capture dynamic, rhythmic motions (causing agents to converge on a single best-matching pose rather than a gait), single viewpoints introduce occlusion-dependent bias, and naively adding VLM scores to task rewards without explicit shaping can alter the optimal policy. MVR addresses all three by learning state relevance from multi-view videos and introducing a state-dependent shaping term that automatically decays as behavior improves.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262029/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes robot proprioceptive states (joint angles, positions) as the per-step input to the relevance model, while multi-view videos (224×224, 64-frame segments rendered from four orthogonal viewpoints at 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°) and a natural-language task prompt are used only periodically (every 9 episodes) to update the relevance model via a frozen ViCLIP. The output is a shaped scalar reward r^MVR = r^task + w·r^VLM computed entirely in state space at every timestep, enabling million-step training without per-step video rendering. The reference set D^ref retains only the top-k=10 highest-similarity state sequences observed so far, serving as the implicit target distribution for the shaping term.

## Method

MVR learns a state relevance function f^MVR (a 2-layer 512-dim MLP with a learnable predictor vector, outputting cosine similarity in [-1,1]) by minimizing two losses over paired comparisons: L_matching preserves the Bradley-Terry ordering between video-text similarity differences and state-sequence relevance differences, and L_reg aligns averaged state embeddings with video embedding similarity to produce viewpoint-invariant representations. The key insight is matching orderings across the state and video domains rather than regressing raw similarity scores, which bridges the semantic gap between proprioception and visual features. The resulting reward r^VLM(s) = E_{s'~D^ref}[log σ(f^MVR(s) − f^MVR(s'))] is derived from a Jensen's-inequality lower bound on the log-likelihood that the current policy is better than the reference policy under the BT model, and it automatically vanishes when the policy's state distribution converges to the reference set, handing control back to r^task without manual scheduling.

## Results

On HumanoidBench (9 tasks, 10M steps), MVR achieves the best average rank of 1.67, outperforming TQC (3.11), RoboCLIP (2.89), DreamerV3 (3.56), and VLM-RM (3.78). MVR wins 5 of 9 tasks, including all four dynamic locomotion tasks (Walk: 927.47, Run: 749.23, Slide: 735.03) and two static tasks (Stand: 918.55, Sit_Hard: 756.67). On MetaWorld CW10 (10 tasks, 1M steps), MVR attains the best average rank of 1.50 with a mean success rate of 0.54, ahead of RoboCLIP (rank 2.00) and TQC (rank 2.20).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TQC (task-reward-only baseline), MVR wins on 6 of 9 HumanoidBench tasks, with the largest gains on dynamic tasks (Run: +101, Slide: +220, Sit_Hard: +245) and Stand (+342), but loses on Stair (208.60 vs. 282.95) and both Balance tasks. Against VLM-RM (single-image CLIP similarity), MVR dominates on all four dynamic tasks (e.g., Run: 749 vs. 15) and Sit_Hard (757 vs. 323), while VLM-RM retains an edge on the two Balance tasks (654 vs. 300 on Balance_Simple), suggesting image-based guidance still helps for purely static balance. Against RoboCLIP (sparse trajectory-level video reward), MVR wins on 7 of 9 HumanoidBench tasks and 7 of 10 MetaWorld tasks, with RoboCLIP scoring zero on two MetaWorld tasks (hammer, push) indicating instability. Against DreamerV3 (model-based RL), MVR outperforms on 7 of 9 HumanoidBench tasks, though DreamerV3 edges ahead on Sit_Simple (891 vs. 861). Against FuRL (online VLM fine-tuning), MVR achieves dramatically higher returns on all 9 HumanoidBench tasks (e.g., Walk: 927 vs. 1.6; Run: 749 vs. 13), as FuRL's reliance on successful trajectories for VLM fine-tuning fails in hard-to-explore humanoid environments. When paired with SimBa (a large-scale off-policy learner), MVR shaping improves Run (741→818) and Slide (578→815) but slightly reduces Stand (907→815), confirming the shaping term transfers to stronger architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of VLM-based reward generation for RL (VLM-RM, RoboCLIP, FuRL) by replacing single-image similarity with multi-view video-text similarity and learning an explicit state-space relevance function via Bradley-Terry paired comparisons rather than directly regressing VLM scores. It departs from prior methods that linearly add VLM scores to task rewards by introducing a state-dependent shaping term (Eq. 9) that automatically decays as the policy's state distribution aligns with a reference set of high-similarity trajectories. The RL backbone is TQC, and the frozen VLM is ViCLIP, positioning the contribution squarely in the 'frozen VLM as reward oracle' paradigm while addressing its known failure modes on dynamic, multi-state tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The automatic decay of r^VLM (empirically dropping to ~23% of its early-training magnitude) provides a principled mechanism for VLM guidance to hand control back to task rewards without manual scheduling or VLM fine-tuning, which is practically important for scaling to tasks where the VLM is misaligned with the robot domain. However, MVR still underperforms TQC on 4 of 19 tasks, and the authors attribute this to the VLM being trained on human rather than robotics data, leaving few-shot adaptation and domain-specific VLM alignment as open problems. The case study on Sit_Hard (visual guidance catching suboptimal poses that task reward alone rewards) is a compelling demonstration of when visual feedback is indispensable, but the overall performance margins over strong baselines are moderate.
