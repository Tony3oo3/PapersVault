## Problem

Diffusion model inference is bottlenecked by repeated forward passes across many timesteps, and while feature caching reduces redundant computation, naive reuse causes noticeable quality degradation. Existing caching methods either rely on unreliable online heuristics that diverge from actual reconstruction error, or incur prohibitive GPU memory costs, and none formally decompose or jointly address the two distinct error sources—feature shift and step amplification—that compound during the denoising trajectory.

## Input / Output

At inference time, ERTACache takes a text prompt and a frozen diffusion model (video models at 480p with 48–81 frames, or image models at standard resolutions) and produces the corresponding generated video or image. A small offline calibration set (as few as 20 prompts) is used once to precompute the fixed cache schedule and error-rectification parameters (K, B tensors), after which no additional data or training is needed. The method adds negligible runtime overhead (<0.5% latency) and slightly less memory than TeaCache since it avoids per-step online prediction of whether to cache.

## Method

ERTACache decomposes cache-induced trajectory deviation into feature shift error (inaccuracy of reused outputs) and step amplification error (temporal compounding under fixed timestep intervals), then addresses both with three training-free components. First, an offline residual-error profiling stage sweeps a threshold λ over a small calibration set to produce a fixed set of timesteps S where the cached residual is sufficiently close to the freshly computed one. Second, a trajectory-aware timestep adjustment dynamically shrinks the integration interval at cached steps via a correction coefficient φ_i ∈ [0,1] and redistributes the remaining timestep budget, counteracting ODE drift. Third, an explicit error rectification module approximates the additive cache error ε_i with a sigmoid-linear model σ(K_i·ṽ_i + B_i) and derives a closed-form solution for K_i and B_i via first-order Taylor linearization of the sigmoid, enabling per-step error correction with negligible overhead. The key insight is that error patterns are highly consistent across prompts, making a single offline-optimized schedule and a small set of precomputed correction parameters sufficient for reliable acceleration across diverse inputs.

## Results

On Wan2.1-1.3B (81 frames, 480P), ERTACache achieves a 2.17× speedup (91.7s vs. 199s baseline) with 80.73% VBench, 0.1095 LPIPS, 0.8200 SSIM, and 23.77 PSNR, outperforming both TeaCache (2.00×, 76.04% VBench) and ProfilingDiT (2.01×, 76.15% VBench). On CogVideoX, ERTACache-fast (λ=0.3) delivers a 2.93× speedup with 0.1012 LPIPS and 26.44 PSNR, substantially beating TeaCache (0.2057 LPIPS, 20.97 PSNR) at a comparable 2.92× speedup. On Open-Sora 1.2, ERTACache-fast achieves 2.47× speedup with 78.64% VBench and 0.1659 LPIPS, surpassing TeaCache-fast (2.25×, 78.48%, 0.2511 LPIPS). On Flux-dev 1.0, ERTACache reaches 1.86× speedup with 0.9534 CLIP and 20.51 PSNR, outperforming TeaCache (1.84×, 0.9065 CLIP, 16.48 PSNR).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ERTACache consistently outperforms all six baselines on perceptual quality metrics (LPIPS, SSIM, PSNR) at comparable or higher speedups. Against TeaCache (the strongest prior caching method), ERTACache-fast on CogVideoX achieves 2.93× speedup with 0.1012 LPIPS and 26.44 PSNR versus TeaCache's 2.92× with 0.2057 LPIPS and 20.97 PSNR — a 50% reduction in LPIPS and 5.5 dB PSNR gain at essentially the same speed. On Wan2.1, ERTACache (2.17×, 80.73% VBench) surpasses both TeaCache (2.00×, 76.04%) and ProfilingDiT (2.01×, 76.15%) by approximately 4.6 percentage points in VBench while also delivering the highest speedup. On Open-Sora 1.2, ERTACache-fast (2.47×) exceeds TeaCache-fast (2.25×) in speedup while improving LPIPS from 0.2511 to 0.1659 and PSNR from 19.10 to 22.34. Against Δ-DiT and PAB, ERTACache achieves substantially larger speedups (2.47× vs. 1.03×/1.40× on Open-Sora) with dramatically better quality (e.g., 0.1659 vs. 0.5692 LPIPS). The only near-tie is on CogVideoX VBench where TeaCache (79.00%) marginally exceeds ERTACache-fast (78.79%), but ERTACache dominates on all pixel-level metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

ERTACache builds directly on the residual caching formulation introduced by Delta-DiT (Chen et al., 2024) and the online heuristic-based caching strategy of TeaCache (Liu et al., 2025b), but departs from both in a principled way. Rather than predicting cache decisions on-the-fly via input-dependent heuristics (TeaCache) or applying fixed uniform schedules, ERTACache formulates caching as an offline optimization problem and provides a formal error decomposition (feature shift + step amplification) that motivates two corrective mechanisms: trajectory-aware timestep adjustment and a closed-form residual linearization for error rectification. This positions the work as a theoretically grounded, training-free alternative to the growing family of heuristic caching methods (FasterCache, PAB, ProfilingDiT) that lack explicit error modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 2× inference speedup with near-lossless quality on state-of-the-art video diffusion models (Wan2.1, CogVideoX, Open-Sora 1.2) is practically significant for real-world deployment where 2–5 minute generation latencies are prohibitive. The offline calibration approach eliminates per-step prediction overhead, making the method lightweight and easy to integrate. However, the method requires a small calibration set per model and the closed-form error rectification relies on a first-order Taylor approximation of the sigmoid, which may limit accuracy under aggressive caching. Open questions include how well the offline policy generalizes to substantially different prompt distributions or to much larger model scales, and whether the error decomposition framework extends naturally to multi-step or higher-order ODE solvers.
