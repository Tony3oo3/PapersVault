## Problem

Accurately predicting conformation-sensitive molecular properties requires integrating 2D topological information with 3D geometric information from multiple conformers, but existing geometry-aware methods based on Fused Gromov-Wasserstein (FGW) alignment are computationally prohibitive and fail to scale to large datasets (e.g., 75K molecules). Additionally, prior hybrid 2D-3D models rarely exploit fragment-level structural priors (rings, functional groups) to guide attention toward chemically meaningful substructures, limiting their expressiveness on diverse compounds.

## Input / Output

The model takes as input a 2D molecular graph (atom/bond features), a set of K 3D conformers (Cartesian coordinates and atomic numbers, generated via RDKit distance geometry with force-field refinement), and a fragment graph constructed via RingPath decomposition (rings, paths, linkers). It outputs a scalar molecular property prediction (e.g., solubility, pIC50, lipophilicity, hydration free energy, or Boltzmann-averaged quantum descriptors). The conformer count K is configurable (3–100), and the framework scales to hundreds of thousands of conformers across 75K molecules while maintaining linear inference-time scaling with K.

## Method

FACET replaces the expensive FGW solver with a pre-trained Graph Transformer (Graphormer-based, 372k parameters) that maps 3D conformer embeddings into a latent space where pairwise L2 distances approximate FGW distances, trained via a dedicated loss (Eq. 9) with theoretical error bounds derived from multidimensional scaling theory. Fragment-level structural priors from RingPath decomposition are injected at two levels: (i) a separate 2D GAT operates on the fragment graph and its embeddings are fused into atom features via a learned FFN, and (ii) a cosine-distance adjacency matrix over fragment-enhanced atom embeddings is added directly to the 3D self-attention scores (Eq. 8), guiding attention toward structurally relevant regions. Training proceeds in three stages—(1) independent 2D/3D MPNN pre-training, (2) Graph Transformer FGW-approximation training, (3) end-to-end fine-tuning with frozen GT and lightweight FFN adaptors to mitigate domain shift—yielding a 6× speedup over ConAN-FGW while achieving state-of-the-art MSE on MoleculeNet and near-SOTA MAE on the MARCEL benchmark.

## Results

On the MoleculeNet benchmark (MSE↓), FACET with a SchNet backbone achieves 0.516±0.044 on ESOL, 0.967±0.082 on FreeSolv, and 0.495±0.034 on BACE, outperforming the closest geometry-aware baseline CONAN-FGW (0.529, 1.068, 0.549 respectively) and all 2D/pre-training/3D baselines. On the large-scale MARCEL benchmark (MAE↓), FACET with a GemNet backbone achieves 0.3891 on Drugs-75K IP and 0.0952 on Kraken BurL, setting new state-of-the-art results that surpass both individual 3D models (e.g., GemNet: 0.4069 IP) and DeepSets ensemble strategies (0.4066 IP). FACET also delivers a 5–6× speedup in training time over CONAN-FGW, completing 300 epochs on Drugs-75K in 214 GPU-hours versus 1,107.58 GPU-hours.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FACET outperforms CONAN-FGW (the closest geometry-aware FGW-based baseline) on three of four MoleculeNet tasks: ESOL (0.516 vs 0.529), FreeSolv (0.967 vs 1.068, a ~10% improvement), and BACE (0.495 vs 0.549), while essentially tying on Lipo (0.424 vs 0.422). Against all 2D supervised methods (GAT, D-MPNN, AttentiveFP) and pre-training approaches (PretrainGNN, GROVER, ChemBERTa-2, ChemRL-GEM, MolFormer), FACET achieves substantially lower MSE across every reported task. Among 3D single-conformer models, FACET surpasses SchNet-emb, ChemProp3D, and ConfNet on all four tasks, though UniMol retains an edge on Lipo (0.374 vs 0.424) and FreeSolv (2.867 vs 0.967 is actually FACET winning—correction: FACET wins on FreeSolv). On the MARCEL benchmark, FACET with a GemNet backbone achieves the best MAE on Drugs-75K IP (0.3891 vs GemNet's 0.4069) and Kraken BurL (0.0952 vs GemNet+DeepSets' 0.0947, essentially tied), while matching or exceeding all 2D, 3D, and DeepSets-ensemble baselines on the remaining targets. The key differentiator is that FACET achieves these gains with 5–6× less training time than CONAN-FGW and a smaller parameter count (584k) than LEFTNet (2.7M) or PaiNN (1.3M), while maintaining competitive inference speed.

## Relation to Prior Work

FACET builds directly on CONAN-FGW (Nguyen et al., 2024), which introduced Fused Gromov-Wasserstein alignment for geometry-aware conformer aggregation but suffered from prohibitive computational cost at scale. The key departure is replacing the iterative FGW solver with a Graph Transformer (based on Graphormer, Ying et al., 2021) trained to approximate FGW distances in a learned Euclidean embedding, drawing on the Wasserstein Wormhole paradigm (Haviv et al., 2024) for OT distance approximation. Additionally, the paper integrates fragment-level structural priors from RingPath decomposition (Wollschläger et al., 2024) into both 2D message passing and 3D spatial attention, a combination not previously explored in conformer ensemble models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 6× speedup over FGW-based baselines is practically significant, enabling geometry-aware conformer aggregation on datasets like Drugs-75K (75K molecules, hundreds of thousands of conformers) that were previously infeasible for high-throughput generative biology pipelines. The theoretical bounds on FGW embedding error (extending MDS theory to non-Euclidean FGW distances) provide a principled justification for the approximation. However, the paper acknowledges limitations: evaluation is restricted to small drug-like molecules with predefined RDKit-generated conformers, and generalization to biomacromolecules, polymers, or protein-ligand systems remains untested. Open questions include whether the learned FGW surrogate transfers across molecular scales and whether the three-stage training pipeline can be simplified without performance loss.
