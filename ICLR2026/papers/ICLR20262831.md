## Problem

Reinforcement learning for tool-use LLMs typically assigns a single sparse outcome reward after multi-turn tool calls, which inflates policy-gradient variance and provides weak incentives for intermediate reasoning. Even when rule-based matching rewards decompose multi-turn calls into single-turn steps, prior work uniformly treats all tokens, diluting the RL signal because reward mass in tool-use tasks is inherently concentrated on a few structured, low-entropy tokens (format tags, tool names, key parameters) while reasoning tokens contribute little early in training. The paper asks whether token-level policy gradients can be reshaped to reduce this variance and stabilize convergence.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text prompt containing the user query, tool/API schema definitions, and the full preceding conversation context; multi-turn dialogues are decomposed into single-turn sub-instances (each conditioned on the full prior conversation) following the SWiRL framework. The policy generates a response containing plain-text segments and structured tool-invocation segments (tool name, parameter names, parameter values). Rewards are computed via rule-based matching: exact-match for format completeness, Jaccard similarity for tool and parameter names, and exact-match for parameter values, combined with a dynamic scaling factor that decays with training progress.

## Method

ResT establishes a theoretical variance decomposition linking token-level Shannon entropy to policy-gradient variance, showing that the optimal reweighting is inversely proportional to each token's variance contribution (Theorem 2). In practice, generated tokens are partitioned into four regions—format tags, tool names, tool parameters, and chain-of-thought—and initialized with entropy-based weights (inverse of region-average entropy). A lightweight curriculum then anneals format-tag weights downward while progressively upweighting parameter and chain-of-thought tokens as training progresses, enforcing a shift from structural correctness to semantic reasoning. The final objective is a PPO-style clipped surrogate (without the KL penalty) applied per-token with the reshaped weights, trained via GRPO-style group-relative advantage estimation over G sampled rollouts per prompt. This design reduces gradient variance, improves sample efficiency, and stabilizes convergence on multi-turn tool-use tasks.

## Results

On BFCL multi-turn (Qwen3-4B-2507), ResT achieves 50.38% overall accuracy, surpassing GRPO (41.62%) by 8.76% and Dr.GRPO (48.62%) by 1.76%. On API-Bank, ResT reaches 68.68% overall accuracy vs. GRPO's 65.33% (+3.35%) and Dr.GRPO's 65.66% (+3.02%). The 4B ResT model also exceeds GPT-4o by 0.38% on multi-turn overall BFCL, 1.50% on multi-turn base, and 4.11% on single-turn tasks. Ablations confirm each component contributes: removing the curriculum drops accuracy by 4.86% on Qwen3-8B.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ResT consistently outperforms all RL baselines across model sizes. Against GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), ResT gains 8.76% on BFCL multi-turn (50.38% vs. 41.62% for Qwen3-4B-2507) and 3.35% on API-Bank (68.68% vs. 65.33%), with the gap widening on the 14B model (44.25% vs. 38.88% on BFCL). Against Dr.GRPO (Liu et al., 2025), which removes std-normalization from GRPO's advantage, ResT still leads by 1.76% on BFCL and 3.02% on API-Bank for the 4B model, and by 7.25% on BFCL for the 14B model (44.25% vs. 37.00%). Against TSFT (Huerta-Enochian & Ko, 2024), which up-weights tool-call token loss during SFT, ResT achieves 2.13% higher BFCL accuracy (50.38% vs. 48.25%) and 1.85% higher API-Bank accuracy (68.68% vs. 66.83%). Most notably, the 4B ResT model surpasses GPT-4o (Hurst et al., 2024) by 0.38% on BFCL multi-turn overall (50.38% vs. 50.00%), 1.50% on the multi-turn base subset (62.50% vs. 61.00%), and 4.11% on single-turn BFCL tasks, demonstrating that entropy-aware token reweighting can close the gap to a much larger proprietary model.

## Relation to Prior Work

ResT builds directly on GRPO's clipped surrogate objective and group-relative advantage estimation (Shao et al., 2024), modifying it with entropy-aware token-level reweighting and removing the KL penalty. It adopts the rule-based reward formulation and multi-turn-to-single-turn decomposition from ToolRL (Qian et al., 2025) and the SWiRL framework (Goldie et al., 2025), but departs from both by introducing a principled, entropy-driven curriculum that progressively shifts gradient mass from low-entropy structural tokens (format tags, tool names) to high-entropy reasoning tokens. The theoretical contribution—a variance decomposition linking token-level Shannon entropy to policy-gradient variance—provides a formal justification for this reweighting that prior tool-use RL work (ToolRL, GIGPO, PVPO) lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in tool-use RL: sparse outcome rewards produce high-variance gradients that destabilize training, especially for critic-free methods like GRPO. By showing that a 4B model fine-tuned with ResT surpasses GPT-4o on both single-turn and multi-turn tool-use benchmarks, it demonstrates that targeted gradient reshaping can close the gap with much larger proprietary models at a fraction of the cost. However, the evaluation is confined to schema-constrained benchmarks (BFCL, API-Bank) with rule-based rewards; extending the entropy-aware reweighting to open-ended agent tasks with learned or preference-based rewards remains an open question the authors explicitly flag as future work.
