## Problem

Fast, unconditional E(3)-equivariant 3D molecular generators (e.g., SemlaFlow) can produce high-quality molecules, but adapting them for specific ligand-based design tasks—such as bioisosteric ligand hopping or multi-fragment merging—typically requires costly retraining or manual construction of conditioning grids (as in SHEPHERD). This paper asks whether a pre-trained flow-matching model can be steered at inference time, without any additional training, to generate molecules that preserve key binding determinants (shape, pharmacophores) of one or more reference ligands while allowing the rest of the structure to be freely redesigned.

## Input / Output

The method accepts a seed structure (3D coordinates, atom types, bond types, and formal charges) that may be a single ligand, a set of spatially separated fragments, or a sparse set of pharmacophore feature points (HBD, HBA, aromatic ring centers extracted via RDKit FeatureFactory). Because the flow-matching ODE preserves dimensionality, the output is always a molecule with exactly the same number of atoms as the input seed, with full 3D coordinates, atom types, and bond connectivity. In Interpolate-Integrate the seed is consumed by interpolating it toward Gaussian noise at a user-chosen time τ; in Replacement Guidance the seed's masked fragment atoms are hard-replaced at every ODE step until a user-specified relaxation time.

## Method

The paper introduces two training-free, inference-time conditioning mechanisms layered on the pre-trained SemlaFlow E(3)-equivariant flow-matching model. Interpolate-Integrate performs a 'mid-path restart': it first constructs a noisy intermediate state z_τ by linearly interpolating the seed toward noise (with optional extra Gaussian jitter) and then integrates the SemlaFlow ODE forward from t=τ to t=1, so that larger τ yields conservative edits and smaller τ yields diverse rewrites. Replacement Guidance provides hard, localized control: after each Euler update of the ODE, the masked fragment region (coordinates, atom types, and bond types) is overwritten with the original clean fragment values, while the unmasked complement evolves freely; a user-controlled relaxation time t_relax (or a set of free steps) disables the replacement for the final portion of the trajectory, allowing the model to synthesize linkers and local substitutions. Both methods add only O(N) overhead per step relative to the base model's forward pass, preserving SemlaFlow's 100-step log-spaced Euler schedule and its ~3 s per-batch-10 GPU inference speed.

## Results

On the EV-D68 3C bioisosteric merging task (Table 3), Replacement Guidance achieves a 23.5% valid(SA<4) yield with an average SA score of 3.47 and a top-10 Vina docking score of −6.62, outperforming ShEPhERD (10.8% valid, SA 4.45, Vina −6.16) while MolSnapper and DiffSBDD produce 0% candidates passing the SA<4 filter. On the natural product hopping task (Table 1), Replacement Guidance consistently yields the best SA scores across all three NPs (3.74, 4.43, 4.07) with 42–50% valid(SA<4.5) yield, while Interpolate-Integrate achieves the highest 3D similarity (e.g., 0.81 ESP similarity and 0.49 pharmacophore similarity for NP1). On SARS-CoV-2 Mpro pharmacophore merging (Table 4), Replacement Guidance reaches a top-10 Vina score of −7.63 with 27.3% valid(SA<4.5) yield, approaching the performance of known binders (−9.95).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ShEPhERD (the state-of-the-art joint diffusion model over shape, ESP, and pharmacophore grids), Replacement Guidance wins on synthetic accessibility (SA 3.47 vs. 4.45), valid(SA<4) yield (23.5% vs. 10.8%), and top-10 Vina docking score (−6.62 vs. −6.16), while ShEPhERD retains a slight edge on pharmacophore similarity (0.27 vs. 0.22). On the NP hopping task, Replacement Guidance dominates on SA score across all three targets (e.g., 3.74 vs. 4.75 for NP1) and valid(SA<4.5) yield (50.2% vs. 24.7%), whereas Interpolate-Integrate surpasses ShEPhERD on ESP similarity (0.81 vs. 0.63 for NP1) and pharmacophore similarity (0.49 vs. 0.34). Both MolSnapper variants (ligand-based and clash-rate=0.1) and DiffSBDD produce 0% candidates passing the combined validity and SA<4 filter on the EV-D68 3C task, indicating that their discrete-constraint conditioning mechanisms are too rigid for flexible bioisosteric merging. Overall, the two proposed methods are complementary: Replacement Guidance excels at producing synthetically accessible, high-affinity candidates, while Interpolate-Integrate is better suited for high-fidelity shape and pharmacophore preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the SemlaFlow E(3)-equivariant flow-matching backbone and the Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) framework, introducing two training-free inference-time conditioning mechanisms (Interpolate-Integrate and Replacement Guidance) that repurpose a pre-trained unconditional generator for ligand-based bioisosteric design. Conceptually, Interpolate-Integrate adapts the mid-path restart idea from diffusion editing (SDEdit, LION) to the deterministic ODE trajectory of a flow-matching model, while Replacement Guidance departs from standard inpainting by enforcing a hard, clean-fragment anchor rather than a noised, in-distribution one. The work positions itself against train-time conditional models (SHEPHERD, SQUID, SHAPEMOL) that require retraining per conditioning channel, and against constraint-satisfaction methods (MolSnapper, DiffSBDD) that prove too rigid for flexible bioisosteric merging.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The training-free, modular design eliminates the costly retraining loop that plagues current conditional generators, making it practical to pivot between design tasks (ligand hopping, fragment merging, pharmacophore merging) without retraining—particularly relevant as large-scale structural initiatives like OpenBind expand fragment libraries. A key open question is whether these inference-time strategies generalize beyond the SemlaFlow backbone to other flow-matching or diffusion architectures, and whether the ligand-only (protein-blind) setting can be extended to incorporate pocket information without sacrificing the training-free property. The paper also acknowledges limitations of its evaluation pipeline (single rigid receptor docking, no global pose search), leaving the true binding affinity of generated candidates uncertain.
