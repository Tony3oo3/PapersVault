## Problem

RGB-only MLLMs trained for spatial tasks like 3D object detection and navigation ignore camera intrinsics, creating an irresolvable geometric ambiguity where focal-length changes are indistinguishable from depth changes and object-size changes are confounded with distance. The paper proves both theoretically (via the pinhole projection equivalence class) and empirically (catastrophic performance drops under simple image resizing or mixed-source training) that this causes models to overfit to the training camera distribution rather than learning generalizable 3D geometric principles.

## Input / Output

The model consumes RGB image frames (or video sequences) together with per-frame camera intrinsics (f_x, f_y, c_x, c_y) and a natural-language prompt specifying the spatial query. When intrinsics are unavailable (e.g., internet-sourced images), a frozen UniDepth v2 model estimates them on the fly, so the same architecture handles both calibrated and uncalibrated inputs. Outputs are structured 3D bounding boxes in JSON format (x_center, y_center, z_center, x_size, y_size, z_size, yaw, pitch, roll), free-form 3D coordinate answers, or multiple-choice spatial reasoning responses depending on the task.

## Method

The Camera-Aware MLLM framework extends a standard ViT+LLM architecture (built on VG-LLM) with three mechanisms that jointly resolve the geometric ambiguity. First, a Dense Camera Ray Embedding computes per-token normalized ray directions R_x=(u−c_x)/f_x and R_y=(v−c_y)/f_y plus global focal lengths, encodes them via sinusoidal embedding, and adds the result element-wise to every visual token so each token is grounded in its specific line-of-sight. Second, Camera-Aware Geometric Augmentation synthetically rescales and shifts intrinsics during training while applying the corresponding geometric transform to the image, forcing the model to disentangle camera geometry from scene content. Third, Geometric Prior Distillation uses a frozen UniDepth v2 model to predict a dense 3D point cloud per training image, embeds it as an additional per-token feature, and adds it to the visual tokens, injecting metric 3D structure without requiring explicit depth annotations. The ablation shows that no single component suffices: the full synergy of architectural conditioning, data-level diversity, and distilled priors is required for robust cross-camera generalization.

## Results

On SPAR-Bench, the proposed Camera-Aware MLLM (Ours-4B) achieves 68.35 average accuracy, surpassing VG-LLM-4B (60.36), SPAR-8B (63.25), and GPT-4o (36.39). On BLINK-Spatial, Ours-4B reaches 77.0 average (vs. VG-LLM-4B at 68.4 and Gemini-2.5-Pro at 71.6), and on CV-Bench-3D it scores 90.7 (vs. VG-LLM-4B at 91.3 and Gemini-2.5-Pro at 90.8). In the critical cross-camera generalization test (ScanNet-val resized ×1.2), the full framework achieves F1@0.25 of 52.1, compared to 39.1 for the camera-agnostic VG-LLM baseline and 31.6 for Qwen2.5-VL-3B, demonstrating a 13-point improvement over the strongest baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against VG-LLM-4B (the primary spatial MLLM baseline), Ours-4B wins decisively on SPAR-Bench (68.35 vs. 60.36 avg) and BLINK-Spatial (77.0 vs. 68.4 avg), but slightly trails on CV-Bench-3D (90.7 vs. 91.3) and VSI-Bench (46.8 vs. 47.3). Compared to SPAR-8B, the proposed 4B model outperforms it on SPAR-Bench (68.35 vs. 63.25) and VSI-Bench (46.8 vs. 41.1) while being smaller. Against proprietary models, Ours-4B substantially exceeds GPT-4o on SPAR-Bench (68.35 vs. 36.39) and BLINK-Spatial (77.0 vs. 67.6), and matches or exceeds Gemini-2.5-Pro on BLINK-Spatial (77.0 vs. 71.6) and CV-Bench-3D (90.7 vs. 90.8), though Gemini leads on VSI-Bench (52.7 vs. 46.8). The most striking comparison is the cross-camera generalization test: camera-agnostic Qwen2.5-VL-3B drops from F1 45.7 to 24.3 under a simple ×0.8 resize, while the full Camera-Aware framework maintains F1 52.1 even under ×1.2 resize, a gap of over 27 points that no other baseline closes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on VG-LLM (Zheng et al., 2025) as its architectural baseline and on UniDepth v2 (Piccinelli et al., 2025) as the source of geometric priors, but its core conceptual departure is from the dominant RGB-only paradigm in spatial MLLMs. It draws a direct analogy to the monocular metric depth estimation literature (Metric3D, UniDepth), where camera-awareness was already established as necessary for cross-camera generalization, and argues this principle has been systematically neglected in the broader MLLM spatial reasoning setting. The paper thus positions itself as extending a known principle from a specialized task (depth estimation) to the general MLLM framework, while introducing a novel dense camera-ray embedding mechanism and geometric augmentation strategy not present in prior spatial MLLM work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper's central claim—that camera-awareness is a prerequisite, not just a helpful addition, for generalizable spatial intelligence in MLLMs—carries broad implications for anyone building or deploying spatial MLLMs in robotics, AR, or autonomous driving. The demonstrated catastrophic failure under trivial image resizing (F1 dropping ~50%) is a strong warning that current RGB-only spatial MLLMs are brittle in ways that scale and data diversity alone cannot fix. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to outdoor scenes with significant lens distortion, whether the geometric prior distillation from UniDepth v2 remains effective at larger model scales, and whether the relatively simple augmentation (scaling + principal-point shifting) is sufficient for the full range of real-world camera variations (wide-angle distortion, rolling shutter, multi-lens rigs).
