## Problem

Existing multi-view evidential learning methods assume that evidence extracted from each view is inherently impartial, but in practice class imbalance causes the model to assign disproportionately more supporting evidence to data-rich classes, producing overconfident misclassifications for minority classes and underconfident correct predictions. This 'Biased Evidential Multi-view Learning' (BEML) problem renders uncertainty estimates unreliable in safety-critical applications such as medical diagnosis and autonomous driving, yet has been entirely neglected by prior work that focuses solely on evidence fusion strategies.

## Input / Output

The method consumes pre-extracted multi-view feature vectors of varying dimensions per view (e.g., 76-dim Fourier descriptors, 4096-dim DECAF/VGG deep features, 1000-dim mRNA expression profiles) along with class labels during training. At inference, it produces a fused class probability distribution and a scalar predictive uncertainty score derived from the Dirichlet strength, with the uncertainty computed from the aggregated evidence across all views via confidence-based weighting. The training set is deliberately constructed to follow a Pareto-distributed class imbalance (imbalance ratios up to 638:19), while the test set is class-balanced.

## Method

FAML replaces the standard fixed uniform Dirichlet prior (α_k = e_k + 1) with a training-trajectory-based adaptive prior α_k = e_k + β_k, where β_k = η·N_k/(number of correctly predicted samples in class k), so that poorly performing minority classes automatically receive a larger prior support that compensates for their evidence deficit. A fairness-degree regularizer (variance of class-wise average evidence) is annealed into the loss to explicitly penalize uneven evidence allocation, and a class-balanced cross-entropy loss prevents the majority class from dominating optimization. During multi-view fusion, a confidence-based aggregation weights each view's evidence by (1−u), and a dissonance-degree consistency loss minimizes the absolute difference in Dirichlet variances across all view pairs, aligning views not only on predictions but on their uncertainty estimates. Theoretically, the adaptive prior is shown to increase the evidence margin for minority class k by a factor scaling with the imbalance ratio ξ_k, tightening the generalization error bound by Õ(1/√(ξ_k·Δβ_k)).

## Results

FAML achieves the best overall accuracy across all six multi-view datasets, with headline numbers of 94.2% (Handwritten), 76.3% (Animal), 57.2% (Scene15), 88.3% (YaleB), 83.6% (Caltech-101), and 82.9% (BRCA). The advantage is most pronounced on tail-class samples, where FAML outperforms the second-best method by up to 10.3% on Caltech-101 (67.8% vs. 57.5% tail ACC). FAML also attains the lowest Expected Calibration Error on most datasets (e.g., 10.9% on YaleB, 11.0% on Animal, 11.2% on Scene15), indicating more reliable uncertainty estimates. On the failure-prediction task, FAML achieves the highest AUROC (up to 90.3% on YaleB) and lowest FPR-95 (57.1% on YaleB) among all compared methods.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FAML outperforms all six baselines on overall accuracy across every dataset. The largest margins appear on tail-class accuracy: on Caltech-101, FAML reaches 67.8% tail ACC versus 57.5% (TLC) and 56.4% (ECML), a gap of over 10 points; on BRCA, FAML achieves 81.2% tail ACC compared to 68.8% (I-EDL) and 55.6% (ETMC/ECML). Among multi-view baselines, ETMC is the strongest competitor on head-class accuracy (e.g., 99.2% on Handwritten vs. FAML's 98.3%), but FAML substantially surpasses it on tail and medium classes and on ECE. Single-view methods (TLC, I-EDL, R-EDL) are consistently weaker, confirming that naive multi-view fusion without fairness correction can degrade performance. On ECE, FAML achieves the lowest values on 5 of 6 datasets (Handwritten 20.6%, Animal 11.0%, Scene15 11.2%, YaleB 10.9%, Caltech-101 14.1%), with R-EDL being the closest competitor on Animal (13.6%) and ETMC on Caltech-101 (15.2%). On the failure-prediction task (AUROC/FPR-95), FAML leads on all six datasets, with the most dramatic improvement on BRCA (AUROC 82.9% vs. 77.1% for ECML; FPR-95 75.6% vs. 92.5% for ECML).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the multi-view evidential learning (MVEL) paradigm established by TMC (Han et al., 2022) and subsequent fusion-focused methods (ECML, CCML, ETMC) by shifting attention from how to aggregate view-specific evidence to whether the evidence collected per view is itself fair. It builds directly on Evidential Deep Learning's Dirichlet parameterization (Sensoy et al., 2018) and Subjective Logic's opinion representation (Jøsang, 2016), but departs from the standard fixed uniform prior by introducing a training-trajectory-based adaptive prior that recalibrates per-class Dirichlet concentration parameters. The class-balanced loss strategy from Hybrid-EDL (Xia et al., 2022) is incorporated, and the confidence-based evidence aggregation follows the SL fusion framework, but the fairness constraint and opinion alignment mechanism are novel additions targeting the identified bias.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies a previously overlooked failure mode in MVEL—systematic evidence bias toward data-rich classes—that undermines uncertainty reliability in safety-critical applications like medical diagnosis and autonomous driving. The adaptive prior is a lightweight, label-free mechanism that requires no subpopulation information, making it broadly applicable. However, the method remains limited to supervised settings, does not disentangle aleatoric from epistemic uncertainty, and the theoretical margin guarantee relies on asymptotic approximations that may not hold tightly in practice. Open questions include whether the fairness-aware prior generalizes to unsupervised or semi-supervised multi-view settings and whether the approach scales to very high-dimensional or streaming multi-view data.
