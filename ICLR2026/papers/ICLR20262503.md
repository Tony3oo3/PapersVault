## Problem

Existing camera-controllable video generation methods condition on a single image or a very short clip (a few frames), which is insufficient to preserve scene consistency when the camera revisits viewpoints seen earlier in a longer input video. The core challenge is to generate future video that maintains spatial coherence with the entire input sequence while allowing dynamic elements to evolve naturally, all within practical computational budgets that preclude conditioning on all input frames.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262503/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary-length input video (L frames at H×W×3), a desired camera trajectory of T poses in SE(3), and an optional text prompt, and produces T subsequent frames following that trajectory. Internally, the input video is processed by dynamic SLAM to extract camera poses and a 3D point cloud, from which a static-only scene memory is constructed; the model then conditions on the last w=9 frames (temporal) plus projected views from the static point cloud at n=7 spatially adjacent frames (spatial). The backbone is CogVideoX-I2V-5B, which generates 49 frames per pass, so with w=9 the model produces 40 new frames per chunk.

## Method

The key insight is a dual spatio-temporal conditioning strategy that treats video adjacency as both temporal and spatial: the model conditions on temporally adjacent frames for motion continuity and on spatially adjacent content (via 3D projection) for scene consistency. To build the spatial condition, the paper introduces a three-stage dynamic masking pipeline—optical-flow-vs-warp-flow thresholding, backward point tracking with CoTracker3 to aggregate motion evidence across the full sequence, and SAM2 propagation to produce complete object-level masks—that cleanly separates static geometry from moving objects in the SLAM-reconstructed point cloud. The resulting static-only point cloud is projected to each target camera pose to yield geometrically consistent warped views that serve as 3D scene prompts. Architecturally, both temporal and spatial conditions are encoded through the frozen 3D VAE and concatenated channel-wise into CogVideoX's existing image-conditioning slot, leaving the DiT backbone entirely unchanged; the model is fully fine-tuned for 4K iterations on 4 H100 GPUs. This design enables precise camera control without explicit camera embeddings (e.g., Plücker coordinates), as the geometrically consistent projected views implicitly encode the target viewpoint.

## Results

On DynPose-100K, 3DScenePrompt achieves the best video generation quality with FVD of 127.48 (vs. FloVD 171.27, AC3D 281.21, CameraCtrl 737.05, MotionCtrl 1017.42) and the highest overall VBench++ score of 0.7747. For camera controllability, it attains the lowest mRotErr (2.38°), mTransErr (7.42), and mCamMC (8.64), outperforming all five baselines. On scene consistency (Table 1), it reduces the MEt3R geometric alignment error by 77% over DFoT (0.041 vs. 0.181 on RealEstate10K) and improves PSNR from 18.30 to 20.89 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

3DScenePrompt outperforms all five baselines across every reported metric. Against the closest camera-controllable baselines, it reduces mRotErr from 3.07° (AC3D) to 2.38° and mTransErr from 7.82 (MotionCtrl) to 7.42, while also achieving the best FVD (127.48 vs. FloVD's 171.27, a 25% improvement). Against DFoT, the only prior method targeting scene-consistent generation, the gap is most dramatic: MEt3R error drops from 0.181 to 0.041 (77% reduction) on RealEstate10K, and PSNR improves from 18.30 to 20.89 dB, confirming that the dual spatio-temporal conditioning with 3D scene memory is critical for long-range spatial coherence. The ablation further shows that removing the spatial prompts entirely (n=0) degrades PSNR from 13.05 to 11.96 and mCamMC from 8.64 to 10.50, verifying that the 3D scene memory—not just the temporal window—drives both scene consistency and camera fidelity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends camera-controllable video generation (CameraCtrl, MotionCtrl, FloVD, AC3D) and video-to-future-video generation (Cosmos-predict-2, DFoT) by introducing a dual spatio-temporal conditioning paradigm that jointly leverages temporally adjacent frames for motion continuity and spatially adjacent content (via projected static point clouds) for scene consistency. It builds on dynamic SLAM (MegaSAM, MonST3R) for 3D reconstruction and introduces a three-stage dynamic masking pipeline (SEA-RAFT optical flow, CoTracker3 backward tracking, SAM2 propagation) to extract static-only geometry, a component not present in prior geometry-grounded video generation like Gen3C which handles only dynamic novel view synthesis within the same temporal window. The base model is CogVideoX-I2V-5B, with minimal architectural changes—repurposing the image conditioning channel to accept concatenated temporal and spatial latents.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a practical gap: existing methods can only condition on a few frames, failing to maintain scene consistency when the camera revisits earlier viewpoints in long sequences. The 3D scene memory approach enables processing arbitrary-length inputs without quadratic cost, which is essential for film production, VR, and robotics applications. Key limitations include reliance on SLAM quality (MegaSAM adds ~4 min overhead, though DepthAnything v3 reduces this to ~10 s), sensitivity to dynamic mask accuracy, and error accumulation in autoregressive long-video generation. Open questions include whether the framework can be extended to handle extremely long durations without drift, and whether the modular design (swappable SLAM/masking components) will accelerate adoption as better geometry estimators emerge.
