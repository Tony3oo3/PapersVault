## Problem

Existing diffusion models for unconditional time series generation operate in a single space: latent-space models capture high-level representation distributions but lose fine-grained temporal fidelity, while data-space models preserve local details but struggle to model multi-modal representation structures. No prior framework explicitly bridges latent representation diffusion and data-space diffusion for unconditional time series generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261200/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, L2D-Diff starts from two independent Gaussian noise vectors: one in a very low-dimensional latent space (d=8 by default, versus D×L in the data space) and one in the full D×L data space. The output is a synthetic multivariate time series of exactly D variables and L timesteps, matching the original dataset structure. The latent code sampled from the first stage is passed through a 5-layer CNN conditioning network to produce a structured guidance signal that is concatenated along the variable dimension with the data-space denoiser's internal representations at every denoising step.

## Method

L2D-Diff reformulates unconditional time series generation as a two-stage latent-to-data conditional diffusion. First, a TS2Vec-based encoder (pretrained with 50% masked modeling) compresses each D×L series into an 8-dimensional latent code; a latent-space diffusion model (100 steps, linear variance schedule) then samples a new code from pure noise. Second, a data-space diffusion model (also 100 steps) denoises a full-resolution D×L noise vector, with the sampled latent code injected as a condition via a 5-layer CNN that projects it into a channel-compatible signal concatenated with the denoiser's encoder features. Both denoising networks use convolutional encoder-decoder architectures with sinusoidal timestep embeddings, and the entire system is trained jointly by minimizing the sum of latent and data denoising losses. The theoretical justification via the Information Bottleneck principle shows that the small latent dimension forces the data-space diffusion to model the high-entropy conditional P(X|Z), which diffusion handles naturally while deterministic decoders would blur.

## Results

L2D-Diff achieves the best overall Contextual-FID (C-FID) across 11 time series datasets, with an average Friedman rank of 1.45, significantly outperforming all 13 baselines. It attains the lowest C-FID on 7 of 11 datasets, including 0.08 on Medical Images, 0.11 on ECG5000, 0.21 on Two Patterns, and 0.28 on Character Trajectories. The closest competitor, FourierDiffusion, ranks second with an average rank of 3.55. On the most challenging multi-modal dataset (Character Trajectories, 20 classes), L2D-Diff achieves 0.28 C-FID versus 3.58 for FourierDiffusion and 3.57 for Diffusion-TS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

L2D-Diff wins on 7 of 11 datasets in C-FID, with the largest margins on ETTh (0.45 vs. 3.17 for FourierFlow), Character Trajectories (0.28 vs. 3.58 for FourierDiffusion), and ECG5000 (0.11 vs. 0.32 for FourierDiffusion). FourierDiffusion is the strongest competitor, narrowly beating L2D-Diff on Stock (0.21 vs. 0.31), Energy (0.48 vs. 0.53), and by tiny margins on Arabic Digits (1.26 vs. 1.29) and Atrial Fibrillation (1.14 vs. 1.15). Among data-space diffusion baselines, Diffusion-TS and TSDE are consistently outperformed, with TSDE showing extreme instability (C-FID of 1723.0 on ECG5000). Latent-space-only models (TimeLDM, EDDPM) suffer large C-FID values on complex datasets (e.g., EDDPM reaches 28.29 on Riverflow), confirming the paper's argument that latent-space compression alone loses local fidelity. GAN-based (TimeGAN, GTGAN) and VAE-based (LS4, VAE) baselines are uniformly weaker, with LS4 and VAE exceeding C-FID 10 on most datasets. The Friedman test confirms L2D-Diff's superiority is statistically significant across all baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

L2D-Diff builds directly on the DDPM framework (Ho et al., 2020) for the denoising process and the Latent Diffusion Model paradigm (Rombach et al., 2022) for the encoder-decoder + latent diffusion component, but departs from both by cascading them into a two-stage pipeline for time series. Prior unconditional TSG methods operate exclusively in either the data space (Diffusion-TS, FourierDiffusion, TSDE) or the latent space (TimeLDM, EDDPM), and the paper positions its contribution as the first to bridge these two spaces via a latent-to-data conditioning mechanism. The conceptual inspiration comes from representation-conditioned generation (RCG) in image and graph domains (Li et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024), which the authors adapt to the temporal setting using a TS2Vec-based encoder pretrained with masked modeling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clean, theoretically grounded (via Information Bottleneck) decomposition of unconditional TSG into global semantic modeling and local detail refinement, offering practitioners an efficient (2.2M parameters) and effective alternative to heavier single-space diffusion models. A key open question is whether the fixed low-dimensional latent code (d=8 by default) generalizes to very long or highly non-stationary series, and whether the concatenation-based conditioning can be replaced by more expressive mechanisms without the instability observed with cross-attention. The approach also raises the broader question of whether cascaded diffusion can be extended to conditional TSG tasks (forecasting, imputation) where the latent stage could incorporate partial observations.
