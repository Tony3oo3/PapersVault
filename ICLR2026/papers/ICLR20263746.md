## Problem

Large Audio Language Models (LALMs) frequently produce correct answers from textual cues alone without processing the audio input—a 'zero audio-contribution' phenomenon reaching ~50% on MMAU-test-mini—yet no systematic methodology exists to measure, filter, or exploit this property during post-training. Additionally, multi-stage post-training (SFT followed by RL) for LALMs remains suboptimal, with no guidance on how to allocate data across stages, and large-scale, high-quality MCQ datasets purpose-built for LALM post-training are lacking.

## Input / Output

The model receives an audio clip paired with a four-option multiple-choice question and produces a single letter answer (A–D) wrapped in <answer> tags, optionally accompanied by chain-of-thought reasoning. The MCQ options are constructed with matched word counts, parallel grammatical structure, and randomized position to eliminate length and position biases. For audio-contribution measurement, the original audio is replaced with 30 seconds of digital silence (not Gaussian noise as in prior work), isolating purely textual reasoning. The dataset provides both a structured three-stage CoT (question-type analysis → audio-content analysis → answer selection) and a simplified unstructured CoT under 150 words.

## Method

The core technical contribution is Audio-Contribution Filtering (ACF), which partitions the 571k-sample AudioMCQ dataset by evaluating each question with three LALMs (A-Flamingo2, R1-AQA, Kimi-Audio) under silent-audio input: if ≥2 of 3 models answer correctly without audio, the sample is labeled weak audio-contribution (54.8%); otherwise strong (45.2%). Building on this split, the paper proposes two SFT-to-GRPO training paradigms on Qwen2.5-Omni: Weak-to-Strong (SFT on weak-AC data, then GRPO on strong-AC data) and Mixed-to-Strong (SFT on mixed-AC data, then GRPO on strong-AC data), with non-overlapping SFT/RL splits of 313k samples each. The key empirical finding is that GRPO on strong-AC data is essential for genuine audio perception gains (mixed-AC GRPO can even degrade performance on AC-strong splits), while SFT data selection should be matched to the audio-contribution profile of the target benchmark—weak-AC SFT benefits MMAU (itself ~54% weak-AC), whereas mixed-AC SFT better serves MMAR and MMSU (both ~65–67% strong-AC). The dataset itself is constructed from seven source datasets via Qwen3-235B-based MCQ generation, dual CoT annotation, and five-dimension quality filtering (score ≥4 on all dimensions).

## Results

Using Qwen2.5-Omni as the backbone, the Weak-to-Strong paradigm (SFT on weak audio-contribution data + GRPO on strong audio-contribution data) achieves 78.2% on MMAU-test-mini and 75.6% on MMAU, while the Mixed-to-Strong paradigm (SFT on mixed data + GRPO on strong audio-contribution data) achieves 67.0% on MMAR and 71.7% on MMSU, all setting new state-of-the-art among open-source models. The All Data GRPO variant reaches 70.2% on MMSU, the first time this benchmark score exceeds 70%, representing a 6.2% improvement over the SFT-only result of 64.0%. The team also achieved first place in the DCASE 2025 Audio-Question-Answering challenge using the AudioMCQ dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper's best configurations outperform all listed baselines on every benchmark where both report scores. On MMAU-test-mini, Weak-to-Strong (78.2%) surpasses the closest competitors Audio-Thinker (78.0%) and Omni-R1 (77.0%) by 0.2 and 1.2 points respectively, while on MMAU it leads Audio-Thinker (75.4%) and Omni-R1 (75.0%) by 0.2 and 0.6 points. On MMAR, Mixed-to-Strong (67.0%) beats Audio-Thinker (65.3%) by 1.7 points and Omni-R1 (63.4%) by 3.6 points, and on MMSU it achieves 71.7%, exceeding All Data GRPO (70.2%) and SARI (66.0%) by 1.5 and 5.7 points. Against the Qwen2.5-Omni backbone (71.5/71.0/56.7/60.6), the best configurations gain 6.7, 4.6, 10.3, and 11.1 points on MMAU-test-mini, MMAU, MMAR, and MMSU respectively. Notably, the paper demonstrates that the choice between Weak-to-Strong and Mixed-to-Strong depends on the downstream task's inherent audio-contribution level: Weak-to-Strong excels on MMAU (a weaker audio-contribution benchmark), while Mixed-to-Strong excels on MMAR and MMSU (stronger audio-contribution benchmarks).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LALM post-training literature (R1-AQA, Omni-R1, SARI, Step-Audio2, Audio-Thinker) by introducing a data-partitioning criterion—audio-contribution—to guide multi-stage SFT-to-RL training, rather than treating all training data uniformly. The zero audio-contribution concept is related to RUListening (Zang et al., 2025), which used Gaussian noise to probe perceptual awareness, but this paper formalizes it as a binary data-splitting mechanism using silent audio and a majority-vote rule across three evaluator models. The AudioMCQ dataset repurposes existing audio-caption and audio-QA sources (Clotho, AudioCaps, CompA-R, TACOS, etc.) through a Qwen3-235B-based MCQ construction and CoT generation pipeline, positioning itself as a large-scale training resource that prior two-stage LALM work lacked.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that mainstream LALMs achieve ~50% accuracy on MMAU-test-mini with silent audio input is a sobering reminder that current audio models may be relying heavily on textual shortcuts, and the Audio-Contribution Filtering framework provides a practical diagnostic tool for quantifying this. The Weak-to-Strong and Mixed-to-Strong paradigms offer actionable guidance: RL on strong audio-contribution data is critical for genuine perceptual gains, while SFT data selection should match the downstream benchmark's inherent audio-dependency. Limitations include evaluation on a single backbone (Qwen2.5-Omni), reliance on three specific models for the ACF split, and restriction to MCQ format; extending the audio-contribution lens to open-ended generation and diverse architectures remains open.
