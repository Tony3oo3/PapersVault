## Problem

Federated Recommender Systems face subgraph structural imbalance, where client subgraphs vary drastically in node count and item degree, causing spatial GNN message passing to produce misaligned representations that destabilize federated aggregation. This structural divergence also amplifies localized popularity bias, as dense clients over-rely on hub items while sparse clients overfit to a narrow set of connections. Existing GNN-based FRS methods assume comparable client structures and do not explicitly account for this heterogeneity in bipartite, featureless recommendation graphs.

## Input / Output

Each client provides a local bipartite user-item interaction subgraph with no node features—only edges defining the interaction matrix. The server generates a synthetic random reference graph (GNMK model) as a neutral structural anchor, requiring only high-level statistics (average node/edge counts) from clients. Output is a top-K ranked recommendation list with preference scores bounded in [0, π] via arccos(tanh(·)), alongside personalized parameter updates and a scalar bias margin that is deliberately aggregated to a non-invertible value to prevent reconstruction of individual user-item bias patterns.

## Method

LPSFed operates in three stages: (1) each client trains a multi-layer Low-pass Graph Convolutional Network (LGCN) with a Bias-aware Contrastive (BC) loss that incorporates a localized popularity bias-aware margin derived from item-degree distributions; (2) each client computes its structural similarity to a server-generated random graph via KL-divergence between their low-pass filtered eigenvalue kernel distributions, without sharing raw subgraph data; (3) the server aggregates parameters and distributes them back weighted by each client's normalized similarity score, so structurally divergent clients retain more of their local model. The key insight is that low-pass spectral filtering extracts stable structural signals invariant to subgraph scale and noise, and both the similarity metric and the filtering operation are theoretically governed by the same eigengap, providing a principled personalization weight. The bias-aware margin adaptively penalizes over-recommended items in the contrastive loss, breaking the popularity feedback loop in a way tailored to each subgraph's degree distribution.

## Results

LPSFed achieves state-of-the-art performance across all five datasets (Amazon-Book, Gowalla, MovieLens-1M, Yelp2018, Tmall-Buy) on both Recall@20 and NDCG@20. The most striking gains are in NDCG@20, where LPSFed improves over the second-best baseline (FedSSP) by +24.2% on Amazon-Book, +20.3% on Yelp2018, +17.2% on Tmall-Buy, and +17% on Gowalla. On Recall@20, improvements range from +3.2% (MovieLens-1M) to +6.1% (Gowalla). The ablation variant LPSFed(BPR), which uses only spectral personalization without the bias-aware margin, already outperforms most baselines, confirming that both components contribute synergistically.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LPSFed outperforms all nine baselines on every dataset and metric. The closest competitor is FedSSP (Tan et al., 2024), a spectral-based federated graph learning method, which LPSFed surpasses by up to +24.2% NDCG on Amazon-Book and +20.3% on Yelp2018, while leading on Recall by 3.2–6.1% across datasets. Standard FL (FedAvg) and matrix factorization baselines (FedMF, F2MF) trail significantly, with FedMF achieving less than half the NDCG of LPSFed on most datasets. Personalized FRS methods (PFedRec, FedRAP) and GNN-based approaches (FedPerGNN, FedHGNN) also underperform, with FedPerGNN and FedRAP particularly weak on Amazon-Book and Tmall-Buy (NDCG < 0.01), suggesting they struggle with structural imbalance. Notably, LPSFed(BPR)—which retains only the spectral personalization without the bias-aware margin—already matches or exceeds FedSSP on Recall, demonstrating that the low-pass spectral similarity mechanism alone provides substantial structural alignment benefits, while the full model's bias-aware margin delivers the additional NDCG gains critical for top-K ranking quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

LPSFed sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) spectral graph methods for federated learning (FedSSP, S2FGL), which previously targeted homogeneous graph classification tasks; (2) low-pass graph convolutional networks for recommendation (LGCN, LCF), which operate in centralized settings; and (3) personalized federated recommendation (PFedRec, FedPUB, FedRAP), which handles client heterogeneity but largely ignores subgraph structural imbalance. The paper's key departure is adapting low-pass spectral filtering to the bipartite, featureless, degree-skewed structure of recommendation graphs and coupling it with a localized popularity bias-aware margin—a combination absent from prior spectral FL or FRS work. The theoretical contribution (eigengap-bounded KL-divergence convergence and spectral regularization) provides a unified justification that neither the similarity metric nor the filtering method was previously grounded in this way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Subgraph structural imbalance is a practical and under-addressed challenge in deployed FRS (e.g., regional e-commerce platforms), and LPSFed offers a principled, theoretically grounded solution that consistently outperforms nine baselines across five datasets. The finding that a stricter-privacy variant (no client statistics for the reference graph) retains near-full performance suggests the approach is robust to privacy constraints. Open questions include scalability to very large graphs (eigendecomposition cost, even with Lanczos), whether the spectral personalization strategy transfers to other federated tasks beyond recommendation, and how the method behaves under extreme non-IID conditions beyond the spectral-clustering-based partitions used here.
