## Problem

Current RL fine-tuning methods for LLMs primarily sharpen existing solution modes rather than discovering new reasoning capabilities, as evidenced by open-source base models already achieving near-100% pass@1024 on popular benchmarks like MATH-500 and AIME 2024. This saturation makes existing test sets fundamentally inadequate for measuring or encouraging genuine reasoning expansion beyond the base model's reach, creating a critical blind spot in the open-source evaluation ecosystem.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262325/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark consists of high-school-level competition math problems with integer-based ground truths, filtered to exclude multiple-choice formats, Chinese text, and figure-referencing questions to avoid verification ambiguities. Each problem is evaluated by drawing k i.i.d. samples (k=1024) from a policy and checking whether any sample's extracted answer matches the ground truth, yielding a binary pass/fail per problem. The primary reported metric is the Expansion Rate (fraction of previously unsolvable problems now solved), which for MATH-B's zero-baseline construction reduces to the post-trained model's pass@1024 score.

## Method

MATH-Beyond is constructed through a multi-stage filtering pipeline starting from 53,682 problems sourced from DAPO-Math-17K and DeepScaleR, progressively narrowed by quality filters (integer GTs, no MCQs, no external references), a pass@16 pre-screen with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen2.5-7B, ground-truth verification via o4-mini-high and GPT-5-Mini, and deduplication against standard benchmarks, yielding 184 candidates. The final pass@1024 evaluation against 21 open-source models (11 base, 10 supplementary) isolates 181 problems unsolved by at least one model (Union set) and 41 problems unsolved by all base models (Intersection set), requiring over 20,000 A100 GPU hours. The paper also formalizes an evaluation framework decomposing post-training gains into Expansion, Shrinkage, Preservation, and Consolidation, and documents seven systematic failure modes in rule-based math answer verification (e.g., multiple valid answers, late/early correct values, unordered tuples) that affect common frameworks like TRL, VERL, and LightEval.

## Results

On MATH-B (pass@1024), RL-finetuned models achieve very low Expansion Rates: Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B v1/v2 reach 7.83%/9.57%, DeepScaleR-1.5B reaches 5.22%, and Skywork-OR1-7B reaches 21.2%. In contrast, LongCoT-distilled Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B achieve substantially higher Expansion Rates of 58.93% and 66.38%, respectively. The MATH-B Intersection set (41 problems unsolved by all base models) remains unsolved by all evaluated RL models at pass@1024, confirming the benchmark's difficulty.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among RL-based methods, Skywork-OR1-7B achieves the highest Expansion Rate at 21.2%, attributed to its use of adaptive entropy control and higher sampling temperature during training. Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B (ProRL) shows only marginal improvement from v1 (7.83%) to v2 (9.57%) despite prolonged RL training, indicating diminishing returns. DeepScaleR-1.5B performs worst among RL models at 5.22% Expansion Rate. In stark contrast, LongCoT distillation (Qwen3-4B/8B) achieves 58.93% and 66.38% Expansion Rates—roughly 3–13× higher than any RL method—demonstrating that exposure to a teacher's reasoning distribution is far more effective at expanding the reasoning boundary than current RL exploration strategies. The paper frames this gap as evidence that the limitation lies not in base model capacity but in RL's inability to autonomously discover effective reasoning pathways.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper operationalizes the Expansion vs. Consolidation distinction introduced by Wu et al. (2025), who argued that RLVR largely sharpens existing solution modes rather than discovering new ones. It extends that conceptual framework into a concrete, reusable benchmark (MATH-B) with a 'zero-baseline' construction principle, and adapts the difficulty/domain annotation procedure from Omni-MATH (Gao et al., 2024a). The paper also departs from the standard RLVR evaluation paradigm by documenting seven systematic verification failure modes across popular frameworks (TRL, VERL, LightEval, etc.), which prior work had not catalogued in this form. Conceptually, it draws an explicit parallel to classical RL exploration (Atari, AlphaGo) to argue that current LLM RLVR lacks the exploratory character that made those systems transformative.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark addresses a real and growing gap: as base models saturate popular math benchmarks at pass@1024, the community lacks a reliable signal for whether post-training genuinely expands reasoning boundaries. The finding that RL-finetuned models achieve only 5–21% expansion while long-CoT distillation reaches 59–66% sharpens the open question of whether RL can discover effective reasoning pathways without a teacher model. Limitations include the small problem count (41–181), which constrains statistical power, and the benchmark's scope to models ≤8B parameters, leaving open whether larger open-weight models also fail on these items. The verification-pitfall documentation is a practical contribution that will affect any future RLVR evaluation pipeline.
