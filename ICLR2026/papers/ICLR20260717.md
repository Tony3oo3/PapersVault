## Problem

LLM alignment has predominantly relied on pairwise preference data, where annotators select the better of two responses. In practice, preference feedback is often richer—partial rankings, top-k selections, or single-best judgments from larger candidate sets—but current approaches reduce this information to pairs, distorting the original preference structure and discarding potentially valuable signal. No principled framework existed to directly leverage ranked choice feedback in preference optimization.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a prompt x, an assortment S of |S| candidate responses (typically 2–5), and a ranked choice label: either a single-best response y_w (discrete choice) or a top-k ordered list μ^k = y_1 ≻ … ≻ y_k. Rankings are generated by scoring responses with the Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B reward model on the UltraFeedback training set. The output is a fine-tuned policy π_θ that, given a new prompt, generates text responses aligned with the learned preference structure.

## Method

RCPO establishes that LLM fine-tuning can be reduced to maximum likelihood estimation of (ranked) choice models: a prompt is the context, a response is an item, and a response set is an assortment. Given a choice model's probability function g(μ^k, S, {r(x,y)}), the training objective is simply the negative log-likelihood of the observed ranked choice under the policy-derived reward r_{π_θ}(x,y) = β log(π_θ(y|x)/π_ref(y|x)) + β log Z(x). The paper instantiates this with two representative models—the Multinomial Logit (utility-based, yielding softmax-like objectives) and the Mallows-RMJ (rank-based, yielding ordinal objectives that depend only on relative positions rather than utility magnitudes)—deriving closed-form loss functions for pairwise, single-best, and top-k settings. DPO, SimPO, and R-DPO emerge as special cases. For practical training, the Mallows-RMJ dispersion parameter φ(x) is estimated via a token-level entropy proxy, and discontinuous indicator functions are replaced with sigmoid approximations to enable stable gradient-based optimization.

## Results

On Llama-3-8B-Instruct, the best RCPO variant (Mallows-RMJ-PO-Top-2) achieves 41.95% AlpacaEval 2 LC, 53.01% AlpacaEval 2 WR, 37.2% Arena-Hard WR, and 68.91% UltraFeedback WR. This surpasses the strongest non-RCPO baseline (IPO) by 4.00 pp on AlpacaEval LC, 19.5 pp on AlpacaEval WR, 6.2 pp on Arena-Hard WR, and 9.47 pp on UltraFeedback WR. The gains are consistent across base models: on Gemma-2-9B-it, Mallows-RMJ-PO-Top-2 reaches 59.82% AlpacaEval WR and 60.9% Arena-Hard WR, and on Mistral-7B-Instruct it achieves 37.58% AlpacaEval WR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

All nine RCPO variants (MNL and Mallows-RMJ under pairwise, discrete, and top-2 settings) outperform every non-RCPO baseline (CPO, IPO, ORPO, RRHF, SLiC-HF, KTO) on AlpacaEval WR, Arena-Hard WR, and UltraFeedback WR. Against DPO (40.24% AlpacaEval WR, 32.6% Arena-Hard WR, 62.36% UltraFeedback WR), Mallows-RMJ-PO-Top-2 gains +12.77 pp on AlpacaEval WR, +4.6 pp on Arena-Hard WR, and +6.55 pp on UltraFeedback WR, though DPO remains competitive on AlpacaEval LC (41.24 vs. 41.95). SimPO achieves the highest AlpacaEval LC (44.15%) among all methods but trails RCPO variants on WR metrics (38.84% vs. 53.01%) and Arena-Hard (33.5% vs. 37.2%). R-DPO (38.01% AlpacaEval WR) and DPO-AllPairs (38.47% AlpacaEval WR) are clearly outperformed by all RCPO variants, confirming that simply converting ranked data into all pairwise comparisons loses information. The gap is largest against the weakest baselines: RRHF and SLiC-HF (25.36% AlpacaEval WR) are surpassed by over 27 pp by Mallows-RMJ-PO-Top-2. On Gemma-2-9B-it and Mistral-7B-Instruct, Mallows-RMJ-PO-Top-2 consistently leads on AlpacaEval WR (59.82% and 37.58% respectively), confirming cross-model generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies LLM preference optimization with discrete choice modeling from economics and operations research, showing that DPO, SimPO, R-DPO, and AlphaPO are all special cases of a single maximum-likelihood framework over choice models. It builds directly on DPO's implicit reward formulation and the KL-constrained RLHF objective, but replaces the Bradley-Terry pairwise assumption with general (ranked) choice models—specifically the Multinomial Logit (utility-based) and Mallows-RMJ (rank-based) models. The key departure from prior work is that richer feedback structures (single-best from a set, top-k rankings) are handled natively via MLE rather than being reduced to all-pairs, preserving the original preference geometry.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, extensible recipe for incorporating any choice model into LLM alignment, opening a path to leverage the rich discrete choice literature (nested logit, probit, Markov chain models, etc.) for alignment. A notable open question is whether the ordinal robustness of Mallows-RMJ (outperforming cardinal MNL even with less information) reflects a fundamental advantage or is an artifact of AI-generated reward signals; validation with human-annotated ranked preferences at scale remains to be done. The paper also leaves open how the framework scales to very large assortments or full rankings, and whether the entropy-based dispersion proxy generalizes beyond the tested model sizes.
