## Problem

Existing knowledge-based unpaired image-text matching methods (e.g., MACK) fail to handle Out-of-Distribution (OOD) words by not leveraging semantic structure in word embeddings to transfer visual prototypes from known to unseen words. Additionally, the distributional variance of region representations across different words is largely overlooked, causing instances far from the distributional mean to be misclassified. Finally, raw region representations capture co-occurrence patterns rather than true semantic relationships between words, limiting the discriminability of the learned knowledge.

## Input / Output

The method takes an RGB image (processed by a pre-trained Faster-RCNN to yield 36 region representations of 2048 dimensions each) and a text sentence (tokenized via NLTK, with each word mapped to a 300-dimensional GloVe embedding). It produces a single scalar similarity score computed via max-mean pooling over the region-to-prototype similarity matrix. In the re-ranking extension, it additionally accepts a top-k candidate list from a pre-trained model (e.g., CLIP or ALBEF) and outputs a re-ranked similarity vector after Z-score normalization and weighted combination with a balancing factor α. The knowledge base itself is built from 12,385 semantic concepts collected from Visual Genome and is domain-independent, with an optional fine-tuning step for domain adaptation.

## Method

MASK constructs a set of paired multimodal representations {(w_k, v_k)} where each word embedding w_k (from GloVe) is aligned with a single prototypical region representation v_k (the mean of all related region representations), establishing a one-to-one cross-modal bridge. The image embedding branch uses a Prototype-Aware Encoder (PAE: FC + 3 self-attention layers) to extract region representations with explicit mean and variance, paired with a Feature Restoration Module for information retention. A Modality Transfer Model (MTM) maps region representations into the word embedding space as a relation-preserving equivariant mapping, trained with a cross-modal alignment loss that simultaneously enforces pointwise convergence to pre-trained word embeddings and preserves pairwise cosine similarity structure. A prototype consistency contrastive learning loss uses prototypes as class centers to maximize intra-word cohesion and inter-word separation, directly mitigating distributional variance. For OOD words, the corresponding visual prototype is constructed as a softmax-weighted linear combination of the top-m nearest-neighbor prototypes in the word embedding space, exploiting the local linearity property of word embeddings on the semantic manifold.

## Results

On the unpaired image-text matching task, MASK achieves a total recall (Rs) of 122.8 on Flickr30K and 209.5 on MSCOCO, outperforming the best model-based method 3SHNet (103.5 / 149.7) and the best prior knowledge-based method MACK^VG-M (104.8 / 205.2). Specifically, MASK attains R@1 of 4.8 (Flickr30K) and 7.6 (MSCOCO) for image retrieval, and R@1 of 12.1 (Flickr30K) and 22.7 (MSCOCO) for image annotation. As a re-ranking extension, CLIP+MASK improves the total recall from 525.5 to 534.3 on Flickr30K and from 386.0 to 400.4 on MSCOCO, surpassing all prior re-ranking strategies.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MASK outperforms all five model-based baselines (CHAN, DSRLN, CORA, BOOM, 3SHNet) by a wide margin on both datasets, with the largest gap on MSCOCO where Rs improves from 149.7 (3SHNet) to 209.5 (+60.0). Against the two prior knowledge-based methods, MASK surpasses MACK (Huang 2022) by 27.5 Rs on Flickr30K (122.8 vs. 95.3) and 7.8 Rs on MSCOCO (209.5 vs. 201.7), and beats MACK^VG-M (Huang 2024) by 18.0 Rs on Flickr30K and 4.3 Rs on MSCOCO. In the zero-shot re-ranking setting (Table 2), CLIP+MASK achieves Rs=534.3 on Flickr30K and 400.4 on MSCOCO, exceeding CLIP+MACK (529.9/392.6), CLIP+LeaPRR (528.2/390.4), CLIP+MACK^VG-M (531.6/394.4), and CLIP+FR (531.1/395.5). Similarly, ALBEF+MASK (518.1/436.2) outperforms all re-ranking baselines on both datasets. The gains are most pronounced on Flickr30K for image annotation (R@1: 12.1 vs. 10.4 for MACK^VG-M), suggesting that the semantic alignment via word embeddings is particularly effective for distinguishing fine-grained textual concepts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the MACK (Huang et al., 2022) and MACK^VG-M (Huang et al., 2024) line of knowledge-based unpaired image-text matching, which aligns words with prototypical region representations. The key departure is the introduction of pre-trained word embeddings (GloVe) as semantic bridges, enabling the construction of visual prototypes for out-of-distribution words via nearest-neighbor interpolation in embedding space, and a prototype consistency contrastive loss that structurally regularizes the feature space to reduce intra-word variance. Unlike prior knowledge-based methods that treat each word's prototype independently, MASK enforces a relation-preserving equivariant mapping between the visual prototype space and the word embedding space, so that semantic distances among words are reflected in the geometry of their visual prototypes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Unpaired image-text matching addresses a practical bottleneck—paired annotation is expensive—by leveraging dataset-independent semantic knowledge, and MASK's OOD word handling meaningfully broadens the vocabulary coverage beyond what the training data supports. The finding that a compact 8.1M-parameter knowledge-based method outperforms much larger model-based approaches on the more complex MSCOCO dataset suggests that explicit semantic structure can be more effective than implicit learned alignment in visually diverse scenes. Limitations include reliance on GloVe word vectors (which may not capture all semantic nuances), the knowledge being bounded by Visual Genome's vocabulary, and the multi-component training pipeline (PAE, FRM, MTM, three losses) adding implementation complexity. Open questions include whether the approach scales to open-vocabulary settings with millions of words, how it interacts with modern multimodal LLMs, and whether the Procrustes-based OOD prototype construction generalizes beyond the locally-linear assumption of word embedding spaces.
