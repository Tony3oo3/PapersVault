## Problem

Encoder-based 3D LMMs suffer from two fundamental limitations: point cloud resolution mismatch between fixed training resolution and variable inference resolution, and semantic misalignment between 3D encoder features and LLM requirements. Whether encoder-free architectures—already explored in 2D LMMs—can effectively replace the 3D encoder remains an open question, as the LLM must then assume the encoder's role in capturing high-level 3D semantics and local geometric structure.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265092/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a raw 3D point cloud (8192 points at training, 2K–16K at inference) and a text prompt, producing text outputs (captions, class labels, or QA answers). The point cloud is tokenized by a lightweight 3M-parameter embedding module (FPS downsampling to 128 tokens, k-NN local aggregation, and linear projections to 4096-dim) that is trained end-to-end with the LLM rather than pre-trained separately. Unlike encoder-based baselines fixed at 8192/512, ENEL handles variable point cloud densities at inference without architectural changes, and a mixed-resolution variant (ENEL-Mix) further improves robustness across 2K–16K densities.

## Method

ENEL removes the pre-trained 3D encoder entirely and transfers its functionality to the LLM via two strategies. In pre-training, LLM-embedded Semantic Encoding unfreezes the first 4 LLM layers and applies a Hybrid Semantic Loss: masked modeling (30% mask ratio, MSE on features) on learnable tokens to extract high-level semantics, combined with Chamfer-distance reconstruction on visible tokens to preserve geometric detail. In instruction tuning, Hierarchical Geometry Aggregation inserts l=3 rounds of Dynamic Grid Sampling (cumulative exponential grid scaling) in early LLM layers, where point tokens are grouped spatially, processed with gated self-attention for local interactions, mean-pooled into aggregated tokens, passed through H=2 LLM layers, then propagated back to original token count—injecting the local-to-global inductive bias that standard Transformers lack. The full pipeline uses Vicuna-7B as the LLM backbone with the same training data as PointLLM, achieving 57.91% classification, 61.0% captioning, and 55.20% VQA (with Qwen2.5-7B + ShapeLLM data) while reducing training time by 29.7% and FLOPs by 20.5% compared to the encoder-based baseline.

## Results

On the Objaverse benchmark, ENEL-7B (encoder-free, Vicuna-7B backbone) achieves 51.03% GPT-4 score on 3D captioning, 55.55% on 3D classification, and 43.80% on 3D VQA, outperforming the encoder-based PointLLM-7B (44.85%, 53.00%, 41.20%) and rivaling the larger PointLLM-PiSA-13B (50.52%, 55.00%, 46.80%). With Qwen2.5-7B and ShapeLLM training data (ENEL-7B*), the model reaches 57.91% captioning, 61.00% classification, and 55.20% VQA. ENEL-7B also demonstrates superior robustness to resolution variation, averaging 48.2% GPT-4 across 2K–16K point densities versus 38.7% for PointLLM-7B, and is 29.7% faster to train with 20.5% fewer FLOPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ENEL-7B (encoder-free) outperforms the encoder-based PointLLM-7B by +6.18 on captioning (51.03 vs 44.85) and +2.55 on classification (55.55 vs 53.00), while also improving VQA from 41.20 to 43.80. Against the state-of-the-art PointLLM-PiSA-13B (a 13B encoder-based model), ENEL-7B wins on captioning (51.03 vs 50.52) and classification (55.55 vs 55.00) but trails on VQA (43.80 vs 46.80), confirming the paper's claim of rivaling a model 1.8× larger. ENEL-7B surpasses ShapeLLM-7B on captioning (+4.11) and classification (+1.05) but underperforms on VQA (43.80 vs 47.40), likely due to the absence of spatial/embodied interaction data in training. Compared to 2D-based approaches (InstructBLIP-7B, LLaVA-7B), ENEL-7B leads by 5.69 and 4.32 on captioning respectively, validating the benefit of native 3D processing. The strongest variant, ENEL-7B* (Qwen2.5-7B + ShapeLLM data), achieves 57.91% captioning and 61.00% classification, exceeding all encoder-based baselines by 5–7 points on these tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the encoder-free VLM paradigm (EVE, ELVA, SAIL, Mono-InternVL) from 2D images/video to 3D point clouds, using PointLLM as its encoder-based baseline and a lightweight Point-PN variant as the token embedding module. The key departure from prior 3D LMMs is the complete removal of the pre-trained 3D encoder (e.g., Point-BERT) and the transfer of its semantic extraction role to the LLM's early layers, guided by a novel Hybrid Semantic Loss during pre-training and a Hierarchical Geometry Aggregation mechanism during instruction tuning. Unlike 2D encoder-free models that rely on large-scale image datasets, this work demonstrates that the approach is viable in the 3D domain with the same training data as PointLLM, requiring only a 3M-parameter tokenizer rather than an ~88M-parameter encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic demonstration that a 3D LMM can match or exceed encoder-based performance without a dedicated 3D encoder, opening a new architectural design space for 3D understanding. The practical implications are significant: the encoder-free design is inherently robust to resolution changes at inference (avg GPT-4 score of 48.2 vs 38.7 for PointLLM across 2K–16K points), reduces training time by ~30%, and lowers FLOPs by ~20%. However, the approach is validated primarily on object-level tasks (Objaverse, ModelNet40) and a single LLM family (Vicuna/LLaMA), leaving open whether it generalizes to scene-level 3D understanding, other 3D modalities (meshes, voxels), or whether the 3M-parameter tokenizer constitutes a true 'encoder-free' design or merely a very small one.
