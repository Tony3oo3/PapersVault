## Problem

Neural operators for PDE solving lack the ability to generalize to geometries that differ significantly from those in the training distribution, creating a data-hungry bottleneck that hinders real-world deployment. Existing approaches (geometry parametrization, coordinate representations) can handle diverse but similar shapes but fail on entirely novel domains, and Lie point symmetry data augmentation only partially alleviates the data efficiency issue without addressing geometry transfer.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262347/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes an arbitrary 2D domain (as a triangulated mesh), boundary conditions (Dirichlet and/or Neumann), and optional coefficient fields/source terms/PDE parameters as input, and produces the PDE solution field on that domain. Training is restricted to simple polygons with 3–12 vertices bounded in [-0.5, 0.5]², while inference handles any bounded orientable manifold (including multiply connected domains with holes and corners). For time-dependent problems, the input additionally includes an initial condition and time-varying boundary data, and the output is a time series of solution fields.

## Method

The framework decomposes the problem into three stages: (1) generating training data on random simple polygons with mixed boundary conditions, (2) training a local neural operator (GNOT) on these basic shapes using Lie point symmetry data augmentation, and (3) at inference, partitioning the arbitrary domain into overlapping subdomains via METIS graph partitioning with iterative extension, then applying the trained operator locally within each subdomain and stitching solutions through an additive Schwarz–Richardson iteration (Schwarz Neural Inference, SNI). PDE symmetries are exploited as pre/post-processing transforms to normalize local inputs into the training range and map solutions back. The paper proves that for self-adjoint coercive elliptic operators, SNI converges to a fixed point with a uniform error bound proportional to the local operator's approximation error, the overlap factor, and the step size, divided by (1 − contraction rate).

## Results

The primary metric is mean l₂ relative error (%) across 100 random boundary/initial conditions per domain. SNI reduces prediction error by 34.8%–96.8% compared to GNOT direct inference across all five PDEs and three test domains. The most dramatic improvement is on Darcy2d domain C, where GNOT direct inference yields 167±8% error while SNI achieves 5.4±0.6%. On the simplest problem (Laplace2d-Dirichlet), SNI maintains a consistent ~2% error across all three domains, demonstrating geometry-independent accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary baseline is GNOT direct inference (the trained neural operator applied directly to the full domain after affine normalization). SNI wins on all 15 comparisons (5 PDEs × 3 domains), with the largest margins on complex multiply-connected domains and PDEs with additional input functions: Darcy2d domain C improves from 167±8% to 5.4±0.6% (a 96.8% relative reduction), and Laplace2d-Dirichlet domain C from 28±3% to 2.1±0.9%. The smallest margin is on Laplace2d-Mixed domain A (10.7% → 6%), where the mixed boundary conditions already limit GNOT's degradation. A secondary comparison with MeshGraphNets (Pfaff et al., 2020) on Laplace2d-Dirichlet domain A shows SNI (2.2±0.6%) substantially outperforming the GNN baseline (11.5%), though the GNN is noted as a potentially useful component for accelerating future SNI iterations. The paper also reports that SNI's error is comparable to or below the GNOT validation error at large data volumes, indicating that the domain decomposition framework effectively compensates for the neural operator's limited training data.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of neural operator learning and classical domain decomposition methods (DDMs). It builds directly on the additive Schwarz-Richardson iteration framework (Mathew, 2008) and the GNOT neural operator architecture (Hao et al., 2023), while drawing on Lie point symmetry data augmentation (Brandstetter et al., 2022) for training. The key departure from prior work combining operator learning with DDMs (Mao et al., 2024, which operates on uniform grids to accelerate classical solvers) is the local-to-global paradigm: training a neural operator on a prescribed family of basic shapes (simple polygons) and then using Schwarz-style iterative stitching to solve PDEs on arbitrary, unseen geometries. The framework is explicitly orthogonal to the choice of neural operator architecture, validated with both GNOT and Geo-FNO.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental bottleneck in neural operator deployment: the inability to generalize to geometries outside the training distribution without generating new data. The theoretical error bound (Theorem 1) cleanly separates the global error into a contraction-driven component and a local operator approximation error, providing a principled understanding of when the framework succeeds. However, the theory is restricted to self-adjoint coercive elliptic operators, and the nonlinear and time-dependent cases are validated only empirically. Open questions include extension to 3D (where basic shape generation becomes substantially harder), non-overlapping decompositions, and more challenging PDE classes (Navier-Stokes, saddle-point problems). The practical impact is significant for industrial PDE solving where geometry varies across design iterations, but the current scope is limited to 2D and relatively simple PDEs.
