## Problem

Representation learning for value-based RL is typically driven by model-learning objectives (e.g., contrastive or reconstruction losses) that are misaligned with the Bellman structure of the task. While the Inherent Bellman Error (IBE) condition provides a theoretically ideal criterion—ensuring the function space is closed under the Bellman operator—directly enforcing it requires an intractable min-max-min optimization. Furthermore, representation learning and exploration are usually addressed as separate problems, missing the synergy where Bellman-aligned features naturally structure uncertainty for efficient data collection.

## Input / Output

The method consumes 4-frame-stacked, grayscale, downsampled Atari observations as state inputs, along with discrete actions and scalar rewards. It produces d-dimensional (d=256) state-action features with norm clipping to ‖φ(s,a)‖₂≤1, linear Q-value estimates Q_θ(s,a)=φ(s,a)ᵀθ, and a greedy policy over the sampled Q-parameters. The parameter distribution ν(θ) is a Gaussian N(θ̂_{t+1}, σ²_rep·I) centered on the current Q-parameters, and the state-action distribution ρ(s,a) is implicitly defined by uniform sampling from the replay buffer.

## Method

The key insight is a spectral decomposition theorem: under zero-IBE, the augmented Bellman operator (weighted by ρ and ν) has an SVD whose singular values are the geometric means of the feature covariance Λ₁ and post-Bellman parameter covariance Λ₂, with the feature and parameter matrices serving as the corresponding singular vectors. This motivates a power-iteration-style alternating update (Algorithm 1) that is made practical via a differentiable SBM Loss consisting of a representation term L₁(φ) and a parameter term L₂(θ̃), each using the other's moving-average covariance as a quadratic regularizer, plus an orthogonality regularizer on both feature and parameter dimensions. The θ̃ mapping is implemented as a residual MLP (θ̃(θ)=θ+Δ(θ)), and the entire objective is optimized with SGD in an online, mini-batch fashion. The learned features then drive Thompson Sampling exploration, where Q-parameters are sampled from N(θ̂_LS, σ_exp·Σ⁻¹) using the feature covariance Σ, reducing maximum uncertainty in the IBE sense. The framework extends to multi-step operators (e.g., Retrace(λ) targets in R2D2) since low one-step IBE implies low h-step IBE.

## Results

On the full Atari ALE benchmark (55 games, 100M steps), SBM+DQN with Thompson Sampling achieves a mean Human Normalized Score (HNS) of 2.23±0.19 and median 0.85, substantially outperforming vanilla DQN (1.62±0.12, median 0.52). On the harder Atari Explore subset (9 sparse-reward/long-horizon games), SBM+DQN(TS) reaches 0.45±0.05 mean HNS versus DQN's 0.24±0.03. When integrated into R2D2, SBM+R2D2(TS) achieves 3.53±0.23 mean HNS on Atari ALE and 0.61±0.03 on Atari Explore, the best results in the paper. The computational overhead of SBM is approximately 20% additional compute time per 1M environment steps.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SBM+DQN(TS) outperforms vanilla DQN by 0.61 mean HNS on Atari ALE (2.23 vs 1.62) and by 0.21 on Atari Explore (0.45 vs 0.24), with the largest per-game gains on hard-exploration titles like Montezuma's Revenge (642 vs 0) and Venture (1183 vs 439). Against Online PVN(TS), SBM+DQN(TS) leads by 0.39 on Atari ALE (2.23 vs 1.84) and 0.14 on Atari Explore (0.45 vs 0.31), which the authors attribute to SBM learning reward-dependent Bellman-aligned features versus PVN's reward-agnostic successor representations. SBM+R2D2(TS) improves over baseline R2D2 by 0.32 on Atari ALE (3.53 vs 3.21) and by 0.21 on Atari Explore (0.61 vs 0.40), with the Explore gap driven by games like Private Eye (51,370 vs 38,213) and Beamrider (5,532 vs 4,116). In the ablation (Table 2), SBM Full (2.23) clearly beats features from the naive MSE Bellman loss (1.82) and from standard DQN loss with TS (1.73), confirming that the spectral objective over a distribution of Q-parameters is critical rather than fitting only the current policy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Inherent Bellman Error (IBE) framework of Zanette et al. (2020a), which quantifies how well a feature space supports value-based RL by measuring closure under the Bellman operator. The key departure is from prior IBE-based representation learning (Modi et al., 2024), which required intractable min-max-min optimization; here, a spectral decomposition theorem shows that under zero-IBE, the Bellman operator's action on a distribution of Q-functions corresponds to an SVD of the feature and parameter matrices, reducing the problem to a power-iteration-style alternating procedure. The exploration component follows Zanette et al. (2020b) on Thompson Sampling with feature covariance, while the multi-step extension leverages Retrace targets (Munos et al., 2016). The paper positions itself against task-agnostic Laplace/successor-feature methods (PVN, Barreto et al.) by arguing that task-specific Bellman-closure objectives are more effective for online reward maximization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main practical contribution is making IBE-based representation learning tractable with only a simple modification to existing value-based RL algorithms (DQN, R2D2), and the spectral objective provides a theoretically grounded alternative to naive Bellman MSE that the ablations confirm is superior. The unification of representation learning and structured exploration through a single covariance structure is a clean conceptual contribution. However, the method's sensitivity to the σ_rep hyperparameter (requiring grid search), validation limited to Atari, and ~20% compute overhead temper its immediate impact. Open questions include convergence guarantees for the alternating procedure, behavior under non-zero IBE, extension to continuous control and distributional/actor-critic settings, and whether the spectral structure generalizes beyond linear function approximation.
