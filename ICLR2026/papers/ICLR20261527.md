## Problem

Lightweight neural networks suffer from a pronounced low-frequency (spectral) bias that limits their ability to capture high-frequency details like textures and edges, degrading performance on complex recognition tasks. While Gated Linear Units (GLUs) are widely used in high-performance models, their impact on a network's spectral properties has never been systematically analyzed, leaving a gap between their empirical success and a mechanistic understanding of how they modulate frequency learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261527/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes 224×224 RGB images and outputs class labels for ImageNet-1K classification; for downstream tasks it produces COCO detection boxes, instance segmentation masks, and ADE20K semantic segmentation maps. For the frequency analysis, inputs are decomposed via a radial Fourier masking operator into complementary low- and high-frequency bands at configurable cutoff radii, enabling per-band accuracy measurement. Latency is reported on both A100 GPU and iPhone 14 (via CoreML), targeting on-device deployment.

## Method

The paper's central insight is that the element-wise multiplication at the core of a GLU corresponds to convolution in the frequency domain (by the convolution theorem), which broadens the spectral support and gives the network access to high-frequency content that standard convolutions suppress. The paired non-linear activation provides selective modulation: non-smooth activations like ReLU6 retain high-frequency energy (slow Fourier decay) while smooth ones like GELU suppress it, allowing the model to learn when to trust high-frequency signals. GmNet instantiates this as a minimal block: 7×7 depth-wise conv → 1×1 conv → ReLU6(x)·x (the simplest possible GLU, with no extra FC or DW layers in the gate) → 1×1 conv → 7×7 depth-wise conv, plus a residual connection. This self-reinforcing design, where both the modulation and gating signals derive from the same representation, consistently emphasizes salient high-frequency variations rather than acting as a generic filter. Trained with standard AdamW for 300 epochs without re-parameterization, distillation, or architecture search, GmNet-S3 reaches 79.3% top-1 on ImageNet-1K at 2.1 ms GPU latency, surpassing EfficientFormer-L1 by 4.0% while being 4× faster.

## Results

On ImageNet-1K, GmNet-S3 achieves 79.3% top-1 accuracy with only 7.8M parameters and 1.2G FLOPs, surpassing EfficientFormer-L1 (77.2%) by 2.1% while running 4× faster on A100 GPU (2.1 ms vs. 12.1 ms). GmNet-S4 reaches 81.5% top-1 accuracy, matching LeViT-256 but with 2× lower GPU latency (2.9 ms vs. 6.7 ms) and 16× lower mobile latency (1.9 ms vs. 31.4 ms on iPhone 14). On CUB-100, GmNet-S1 achieves 81.5% top-1 with 3.1M parameters, outperforming ShuffleNet-V2 (76.0%) by 5.5%. For downstream tasks on COCO, GmNet-S3 as a Mask R-CNN backbone achieves 42.2 AP^box and 40.1 AP^mask, exceeding RepViT-M1.5 by 0.6 and 1.2 AP respectively while being 1.7 ms faster on mobile.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GmNet-S3 (79.3%) outperforms EfficientFormer-L1 (77.2%) by 2.1% top-1 while being 4× faster on GPU (2.1 ms vs. 12.1 ms), and beats both RepViT-M1.0 (78.6%) and StarNet-S4 (78.4%) by 0.7% and 0.9% respectively with 1.1–1.5 ms lower GPU latency. GmNet-S4 (81.5%) matches LeViT-256 in accuracy but runs 2× faster on GPU and 16× faster on iPhone 14, and surpasses MobileOne-S4 (79.4%) by 2.1% at comparable latency. At the smallest scale, GmNet-S1 (75.5%) exceeds MobileV2-1.0 (72.0%) by 3.5% with similar GPU latency. In the frequency-domain comparison (Table 3), GmNet-S3 leads all baselines on high-frequency components: 51.7% at r=12 vs. 45.4% (EfficientMod-xs) and 43.3% (StarNet-S4), confirming that the GLU design specifically enhances high-frequency learning. On CUB-100, GmNet-S1 (81.5%) beats ShuffleNet-V2 (76.0%) by 5.5% with fewer parameters (3.1M vs. 3.5M). For COCO detection/segmentation, GmNet-S3 surpasses RepViT-M1.5 by 1.4 AP^box_75 and 2.4 AP^mask_75 while being 1.7 ms faster on mobile, and is over 2× faster than EfficientFormer-L3.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the Gated Linear Unit (GLU) mechanism introduced by Dauphin et al. (2017) and popularized by SwiGLU (Shazeer, 2020), but reframes its utility through the lens of the convolution theorem: element-wise multiplication in the spatial domain corresponds to convolution in the frequency domain, thereby broadening the spectral content available to the network. It departs from prior frequency-bias analyses (Rahaman et al., 2019; Tancik et al., 2020; Yin et al., 2019), which largely diagnosed the low-frequency bias without proposing architectural remedies, by identifying the GLU as a concrete, lightweight mechanism to counteract it. Compared to concurrent gating-based efficient networks (StarNet, EfficientMod), GmNet argues that the simplest possible gate σ(x)·x—without additional FC layers, depth-wise convolutions, or layer normalization in the gating branch—achieves the best balance of high-frequency sensitivity and efficiency, in contrast to those methods that use independent projections or dual-channel FC gates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first systematic frequency-domain analysis of GLUs, offering a principled explanation for why these units improve lightweight network performance: the element-wise product expands the frequency spectrum while the non-smooth activation (ReLU6) selectively preserves high-frequency energy that smooth activations (GELU, SiLU) suppress. This opens a design axis—activation smoothness as a frequency-control knob—that is orthogonal to the usual depth/width/FLOPs trade-offs in efficient architecture search. Limitations include a modest (~1%) drop in adversarial robustness with ReLU-based gates, the reliance on a somewhat heuristic radial frequency decomposition for evaluation, and the fact that the ConvNeXt experiment shows the gains shrink for larger backbones, suggesting the effect is most pronounced in the capacity-constrained regime. An open question is whether the frequency-aware gating principle can be formalized into a training objective or loss that explicitly penalizes spectral bias, rather than relying on architectural inductive bias alone.
