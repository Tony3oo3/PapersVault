## Problem

Existing information-theoretic multi-view methods impose only single-variable consistency (e.g., pairwise mutual information maximization) in a shared latent space, which is too coarse to guarantee representation sufficiency and offers no protection against contamination from poorly trained or low-quality views. Under simultaneous view and label incompleteness (iM3C), this leads to information redundancy, learning collapse, and degraded cross-view alignment. The paper asks how to enforce cross-view semantic consistency early in the encoding stage within a principled information bottleneck, rather than only at the fusion stage.

## Input / Output

Each sample is described by six pre-extracted image feature views (GIST, HSV, DenseHue, DenseSift, RGB, LAB), but only a random subset is observed per instance; the binary label vector (length 20–291 depending on dataset) is also partially masked. The model consumes the available view subset and available label subset directly without explicit imputation, and outputs per-class sigmoid probabilities via a small MLP on a PoE-fused latent sample (averaged over 10 reparameterized draws). Supervision is accumulated only over the observed label indices, with unknown entries excluded from the cross-entropy term.

## Method

PCVE replaces the standard single-encoder-per-view design with m stochastic encoders per source view (one per target view), whose outputs are combined via product-of-experts to form the view-specific posterior. A permutation-consistency loss then randomly swaps the latent distribution clusters across views and penalizes KL divergence between the original and permuted assignments, reducing the pairwise alignment cost from O(|V|³) to O(|V|²) while enforcing that each view encodes the same shared semantics. The overall IB objective balances a reconstruction term (preserving view-specific information) against the permutation-consistency term (suppressing view-private redundancy), with an additional intra-view KL alignment and a softmax-weighted inter-view cosine similarity loss for further semantic coherence. At inference, available view posteriors are fused via PoE, sampled with the reparameterization trick, and passed through a classification head trained with masked multi-label cross-entropy.

## Results

Under the doubly-incomplete setting (50% missing views, 50% missing labels), PCVE achieves the best or near-best results across all six metrics on all five benchmarks. On Corel5k, PCVE attains AP 0.421 and AUC 0.913, surpassing the strongest baseline SIP (AP 0.418, AUC 0.913) and MSLPP (AP 0.413, AUC 0.903). On the larger-scale MIRFLICKR dataset, PCVE reaches AP 0.618 and AUC 0.868, outperforming MSLPP (AP 0.615, AUC 0.865) and SIP (AP 0.614, AUC 0.860). The only metric where a baseline slightly edges PCVE is AP on IAPRTC12, where MSLPP scores 0.340 versus PCVE's 0.336, though PCVE still leads on AUC (0.889 vs 0.883) and 1-RL (0.888 vs 0.882).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PCVE consistently outperforms the two strongest iM3C-specific baselines, SIP and MSLPP, on the majority of metrics across all five datasets. Against SIP (the closest competitor), PCVE gains 0.3–0.5 AP points on Corel5k (0.421 vs 0.418), Pascal07 (0.559 vs 0.555), ESPGame (0.314 vs 0.311), IAPRTC12 (0.336 vs 0.331), and MIRFLICKR (0.618 vs 0.614), with corresponding AUC improvements of 0.001–0.007. Against MSLPP, PCVE wins on AP for four of five datasets but concedes IAPRTC12 AP (0.336 vs 0.340), while still leading on AUC (0.889 vs 0.883) and 1-RL (0.888 vs 0.882) for that dataset. The iM3C-oriented methods (DICNet, SIP, MSLPP, PCVE) systematically outperform non-iM3C baselines such as CDMM and LVSL by 4–10 AP points (e.g., on Pascal07: PCVE 0.559 vs CDMM 0.508), confirming that explicit handling of dual incompleteness is critical. Single-view method DM2L and older multi-view methods (iMVWL, NAIM3L) are substantially outperformed, with gaps exceeding 10 AP points on most datasets.

## Relation to Prior Work

PCVE extends the multi-view information bottleneck framework of Wan et al. (AAAI 2021) by relocating the cross-view consistency constraint from the fusion stage (where prior PoE/MoE-based methods apply it) to the encoding stage, where each view's posterior is modeled through a cluster of stochastic encoders. It also builds on the semantic consistency assumption and joint representation formulation from Liu et al. (ICML 2024, SIP), but departs from that line by replacing pairwise alignment with a permutation-consistent regularization that reduces complexity from O(|V|³) to O(|V|²). The paper positions itself against both non-probabilistic contrastive methods (which lack sufficiency guarantees) and prior iM3C approaches (DICNet, NAIM3L) that handle view and label incompleteness through imputation or dual-index strategies rather than principled variational encoding.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PCVE provides a unified variational framework that simultaneously handles view and label incompleteness without explicit imputation, offering a principled IB objective with interpretable trade-offs between information retention and compression. The permutation-consistency mechanism is a practical contribution that makes cross-view alignment scalable, and the ablation confirms both the reconstruction and consistency terms are necessary to avoid information collapse or redundancy. However, the approach assumes shared information suffices for prediction, which may break down for highly heterogeneous modalities (e.g., vision-language-audio), and the stochastic matching introduces variance under severely imbalanced views. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to structured missing patterns, real-world distribution shifts, and modalities where view-private cues are indispensable.
