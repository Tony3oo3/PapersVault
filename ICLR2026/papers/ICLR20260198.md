## Problem

When differential privacy noise is injected into text embeddings for privacy-preserving LLM inference, the noise is progressively amplified through deep transformer layers, causing severe downstream task degradation. Existing denoising approaches like SnD require a pretraining phase and operate disconnected from the LLM's internal dynamics, failing to correct inter-layer noise amplification. The paper addresses the absence of a pretraining-free, progressive noise correction mechanism that integrates with the split learning paradigm while keeping client-server communication costs practical.

## Input / Output

The client tokenizes text, computes clean embeddings, adds d_χ-DP noise, and transmits the perturbed embeddings to the server. The server runs the full LLM forward pass and returns a subset of downsampled intermediate hidden states (selected by a gradient-based filter and compressed via information bottleneck) back to the client. The client-side denoising module consumes both the original noise-free embedding and these returned hidden states to produce a corrected hidden state, which feeds a task-specific head for classification logits or autoregressive text generation. Communication is bidirectional per training step, with the server-to-client payload reduced to n_H layers at dimension d/r rather than all L layers at full dimension d.

## Method

HiddenEcho is a split-learning framework where the embedding layer resides on the client and the remaining LLM layers on the server. The core denoising module (inspired by LST) is a dimension-reduced transformer with L layers, each containing a transformer block and a learned gate vector that dynamically mixes the previous layer's output with the corresponding downsampled server hidden state via a sigmoid-weighted interpolation, with residual connections propagating clean embedding information to deeper layers. To reduce communication, a gradient-based hidden layer filter precomputes each layer's contribution to the final output via Riemann-summed Jacobian integrals and selects only the top-k most informative layers for transmission. An information bottleneck objective (estimated with MINE) further compresses hidden states by minimizing mutual information between noisy embeddings and downsampled states while maximizing mutual information between the denoised output and those states. The entire pipeline is trained end-to-end with the task loss plus the IB loss, requiring no separate pretraining phase, and the DP privacy guarantee is preserved by the post-processing invariance of the initial embedding perturbation.

## Results

On text classification with Qwen2-1.5B, HiddenEcho achieves up to 46.89% AUC improvement over the LDP baseline on Financial Phrasebank (0.874 vs. 0.595 at η=1000) and 19.78% on MRPC (0.660 vs. 0.551 at η=100). The communication-efficient HiddenEcho variant (with gradient-based layer filtering) reduces transmission by over 85% for classification and ~62% for generation compared to HiddenEcho-Full, while training is up to 72.52% faster than SnD. On text generation (T5-Large), HiddenEcho-Full reaches BLEU 26.366 on IWSLT at η=40 versus 24.576 for LDP and 29.816 for GAN-DP, and 21.851 on Samsum at η=40 versus 20.235 for LDP.

## Baselines & Comparisons

HiddenEcho consistently outperforms all three baselines across every dataset and privacy budget. Against LDP (Qu et al., 2021), the improvement is most dramatic on Financial Phrasebank, where HiddenEcho gains 46.89% AUC at η=1000 (0.855 vs. 0.595), and remains substantial on MRPC (+19.78% at η=100) and BBC News (+24.30% at η=1000). Against SnD (Mai et al., 2024), HiddenEcho achieves 30–40% higher AUC on Financial (e.g., 0.857 vs. 0.558 at η=100) and also trains up to 72.52% faster, since SnD requires a separate pretraining phase for its denoising module. Against GAN-DP, HiddenEcho leads by 15–47% AUC on classification tasks, while GAN-DP's advantage in single-epoch speed is offset by its mandatory GAN pretraining overhead. The communication-efficient HiddenEcho variant (with layer filtering) sometimes matches or even exceeds HiddenEcho-Full (e.g., +1.4% on MRPC at η=100), confirming that selective layer transmission preserves denoising quality while cutting bandwidth by >85%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the split-learning + local differential privacy pipeline for LLM services (Qu et al., 2021; Shen et al., 2023) by identifying inter-layer noise amplification as the dominant source of utility loss, rather than the initial embedding perturbation itself. It departs from SnD (Mai et al., 2024), which pretrains a fixed denoising module disconnected from LLM dynamics, by proposing an end-to-end, pretraining-free correction loop where server-side hidden states are fed back to a lightweight client-side denoiser inspired by LST (Sung et al., 2022). The gradient-based hidden layer filter and information-bottleneck dimension reducer further differentiate HiddenEcho from prior approaches that either transmit all layers or use fixed linear projections.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a practical, communication-efficient recipe for deploying DP-protected LLMs in MaaS settings, with the 85%+ communication reduction and 72% speedup over SnD making it deployable under real bandwidth constraints. However, all experiments use models ≤1.5B parameters, leaving open whether the noise-amplification dynamics and the layer-selection strategy generalize to 7B+ or 70B+ architectures where inter-layer interactions are more complex. The paper also acknowledges but does not formally resolve the gradient-inversion risk during fine-tuning, and the privacy analysis relies on the post-processing invariance of the initial LDP step without accounting for information leakage through the returned hidden states themselves.
