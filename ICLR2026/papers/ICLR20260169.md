## Problem

Text-to-image diffusion models excel at reproducing familiar visual concepts but struggle to generate genuinely novel content that falls under exploratory or transformational creativity. Existing creative generation methods either blend predefined concept pairs (combinatorial approaches) or require per-concept embedding optimization and model fine-tuning (e.g., ConceptLab, C3), limiting practical deployment. This paper addresses the gap by asking whether a training-free, inference-time mechanism can steer diffusion sampling toward unexplored semantic regions while preserving category validity.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt (e.g., 'A photo of a new type of pet') and initial Gaussian noise, producing a 1024×1024 RGB image. During generation, intermediate denoising predictions x̂₀ are decoded to RGB (via VAE or a learned linear approximation) and fed to a VLM with a category-specific question (e.g., 'What type of pet is this?'). The method is backbone-agnostic, demonstrated on SD3.5-large, SDXL, and Kandinsky 2.1, and supports a range of VLM oracles from lightweight ViLT to GPT-4o, with a configurable feedback window (default: all 28 steps) and query frequency to trade compute for guidance strength.

## Method

The core mechanism is a closed-loop VLM-guided feedback during the denoising process: at each timestep t, the intermediate clean-image estimate x̂₀⁽ᵗ⁾ is decoded, queried to a VLM, and the VLM's identified dominant concept (e.g., 'cat') is appended to a growing negative prompt list p_neg. This accumulated list is encoded and plugged into the classifier-free guidance formulation as the negative conditioning, amplifying the velocity difference between the positive and negative predictions to steer the trajectory away from conventional modes. Three design choices are critical and validated by ablations: (1) dynamic per-step querying rather than a static LLM-generated list, (2) per-seed adaptation rather than cross-seed replay, and (3) accumulation of previously identified negatives rather than using only the current step's response. The method is entirely training-free, adds only ~13 s overhead with lightweight VLMs, and can be restricted to the first 10–15 timesteps without significant quality loss due to the momentum effect of early negative prompt establishment.

## Results

On SD3.5-Large, the best configuration (SD3.5 + Qwen2.5-VL) achieves a Relative Typicality of 2.100, GPT Novelty Score of 0.401, Vendi score of 5.476, and Total Variance of 0.308, while maintaining a GPT validity score of 0.917 and CLIP score of 0.264. The SD3.5 + BLIP-2 variant pushes novelty further (Relative Typicality 2.190, Vendi 5.794) at a slight validity cost (GPT Score 0.898). In the user study, the method achieves the highest novelty rating (4.55/5) while retaining strong category coherence (4.50/5), uniquely dominating both axes simultaneously. On SDXL, the method still outperforms C3 on novelty (1.795 vs. 1.075 Relative Typicality) and diversity (5.427 vs. 4.726 Vendi) with comparable validity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against ConceptLab (Richardson et al., 2024), the proposed method achieves substantially higher GPT Novelty Score (0.401 vs. 0.238) and Vendi diversity (5.476 vs. 5.119) on SD3.5, while also improving GPT validity (0.917 vs. 0.862), indicating that ConceptLab's optimization produces objects that satisfy CLIP-space constraints but fail human/GPT functional verification (e.g., cups without cavities). Against C3 (Han et al., 2025) on SDXL, the method more than doubles the Relative Typicality (1.795 vs. 1.075) and improves Vendi score (5.427 vs. 4.726) while matching validity (0.895 GPT Score for both). In the user study, the method achieves the highest novelty rating (4.55/5) while maintaining the second-highest validity (4.50/5), whereas ConceptLab (3.50 novelty, 3.95 validity) and C3 (2.93 novelty, 3.95 validity) both sacrifice validity for moderate creativity gains. The method also outperforms simple creative prompting baselines (SD3.5, GPT-4o) by a wide margin on novelty (0.401 vs. 0.230 and 0.093 GPT Novelty) while preserving comparable validity, confirming that prompt-level modifications alone are insufficient for exploratory creativity.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the negative prompting mechanism introduced by Saharia et al. (2022) as an extension of classifier-free guidance, but repurposes it as a dynamic, VLM-driven steering signal rather than a static user-specified constraint. It departs from ConceptLab (Richardson et al., 2024), which uses a VLM to guide per-concept embedding optimization over many iterations (~8 min/concept), by instead querying a VLM as a real-time oracle at each denoising step to accumulate seed-specific negative prompts. It also contrasts with C3 (Han et al., 2025), which amplifies existing features during denoising, by actively steering generation away from conventional semantic modes identified in intermediate predictions. The key conceptual shift is from offline optimization or feature amplification to a closed-loop, inference-time feedback mechanism that requires no training, no curated datasets, and no per-concept tuning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method offers a practical, training-free route to exploratory creativity that integrates seamlessly into existing diffusion pipelines with only ~13 seconds of overhead, making it immediately deployable for practitioners. The finding that VLM predictions on highly noisy intermediate denoising states correlate ~90% with final outputs within 3–5 timesteps suggests semantic structure is established very early in the denoising process, which has broader implications for understanding diffusion dynamics. Limitations include sensitivity to question design (different formulations work better for different categories), dependence on VLM quality, and the fact that the method is evaluated primarily on single-object and simple compositional prompts. Open questions include automating question selection, extending to video/3D/multimodal generation, and whether the approach scales to more abstract or transformational creativity beyond exploratory novelty within known categories.
