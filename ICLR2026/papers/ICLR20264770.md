## Problem

Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) has become a leading approach for converting multi-step diffusion models into efficient few-step generators, with its success conventionally attributed to matching the student's output distribution to the teacher's. However, in practice, DMD requires Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) on the real score to achieve good results on complex tasks like text-to-image, a modification that breaks the theoretical integrity of the distribution-matching derivation and has been largely dismissed as an implementation detail. This paper asks: what is actually driving the few-step conversion in DMD, and what role does the CFG term truly play?

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt as input and produces a generated RGB image in as few as 4 steps (or 1 step), compared to the 40–80 NFE required by the original teacher model. The distillation operates in the latent space of a pre-trained flow-matching diffusion model (e.g., SDXL, Lumina-Image-2.0, or an internal 6B model), where the generator G_θ maps a noise input z_t at timestep t to an image prediction, which is then re-noised at independently sampled noise levels τ_CA and τ_DM for the two gradient components. The key design parameter is the re-noising schedule: the CA engine samples τ_CA from (t, 1] while the DM regularizer samples τ_DM from [0, 1], decoupling the two mechanisms' operating ranges.

## Method

The paper decomposes the practical DMD gradient (which uses CFG on the real score) into two distinct terms: a CFG Augmentation (CA) term, (α−1)(s_cond^real − s_uncond^real), that directly applies the CFG signal as a gradient to the student, and a Distribution Matching (DM) term, s_cond^real − s_cond^fake, that strictly follows the theoretical IKL-divergence derivation. Ablation experiments reveal that CA alone is remarkably effective at converting a multi-step model into a few-step generator but causes training collapse due to over-saturation and high-frequency artifacts, while DM alone produces poor few-step results; together, CA acts as the 'engine' and DM as the 'regularizer.' Building on mechanistic analysis showing that CA at noise level τ enhances image content at that frequency band, the paper proposes a Decoupled-Hybrid re-noising schedule: constraining CA to τ_CA > t (so it focuses only on unresolved, higher-frequency content) while keeping DM global at τ_DM ∈ [0,1] (so it can correct low-frequency artifacts like color oversaturation). This decoupled DMD (d-DMD) formulation, validated on Lumina-Image-2.0 and SDXL, achieves consistent gains over standard DMD and DMD2, with a 100% preference rate in user studies.

## Results

On 4-step SDXL distillation (Table 2), the proposed Decoupled method achieves FID 17.80, CLIP-S 33.62, and ImageReward 78.61, outperforming DMD2 (FID 18.95, CLIP-S 33.14, ImageReward 71.01) and all other few-step baselines. On the internal 6B model, the distilled 4-step generator matches the original 80-NFE teacher across DPG Bench (88.53 vs 88.14), GenEval (0.78 vs 0.80), and most other benchmarks at 95% NFE reduction. A user study confirms the Decoupled-Hybrid schedule achieves a unanimous 100% preference rate in model-level comparisons and 59.8% first-place ranking in image-level evaluations.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The Decoupled method is compared against six few-step distillation baselines on 4-step SDXL. It achieves the best FID (17.80) and CLIP-S (33.62), improving over DMD2 by 1.15 FID and 0.48 CLIP-S, and substantially outperforming PCM (FID 24.13), Lightning (24.49), LCM (22.27), Turbo (27.27), and Flash (22.96). On ImageReward, Decoupled leads with 78.61, a 7.6-point margin over DMD2 (71.01) and a 13.9-point margin over PCM (64.73). The only metric where Decoupled trails DMD2 is HPSv2.1 (30.34 vs 30.64, a 0.30 gap), while it leads on HPSv3 (9.79 vs 9.64). On the 6B internal model, the 4-step Decoupled distillation matches or slightly exceeds the 80-NFE teacher on DPG Bench (+0.39), TIIF-Bench short (+2.14), and PRISM-Bench-ZH (+2.00), with only minor drops on GenEval (−0.02) and CVTG-2K (−0.03), confirming that the decoupled schedule preserves teacher quality at 95% NFE reduction.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reinterprets Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) and its successor DMD2 by decomposing the practical DMD gradient into two functionally distinct components: a CFG Augmentation (CA) term and a Distribution Matching (DM) term. While the theoretical lineage traces back to Diff-Instruct's IKL divergence framework and the CFG mechanism from Ho & Salimans, prior work uniformly attributed DMD's success to distribution matching, treating the use of CFG on the real score as a heuristic implementation detail. This paper departs from that narrative by showing the CA term is the actual engine of few-step conversion, repositioning DM as a stabilizing regularizer rather than the primary distillation mechanism. The proposed decoupled re-noising schedule is a principled modification to the DMD2 training procedure, building directly on its exact configuration.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This reframing has immediate practical value: the decoupled-hybrid schedule (constrained CA, global DM) is a simple modification that yields consistent improvements across SDXL, Lumina-Image-2.0, and a 6B internal model, with a 100% user preference rate in side-by-side comparisons. However, the fundamental question of *why* CFG Augmentation so effectively converts a multi-step model to few-step remains open; the paper's LLM-sequential-generation analogy (Appendix B) is explicitly preliminary and unproven. For the broader field, this work suggests that other 'distribution matching' distillation methods may also harbor overlooked dominant terms that have been misattributed, and that the choice of regularizer (DM vs. GAN vs. statistical constraints) involves a stability-performance trade-off that deserves more systematic study.
