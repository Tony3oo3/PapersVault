## Problem

Classical finite volume methods for hyperbolic conservation laws preserve conservation structure but suffer from excessive numerical diffusion near shocks and require intricate manual design for higher-order accuracy. Neural network solvers offer flexibility but typically discard the conservation-law structure, leading to unphysical solutions. The paper asks whether one can learn flux approximations over extended spatiotemporal stencils while retaining the exact FV update rule, thereby combining the expressiveness of neural networks with the structural guarantees of FV schemes.

## Input / Output

At each cell interface, the network receives a local rectangular stencil of a spatial cell averages across b past time steps (e.g., 2×1 up to 10×11) and outputs a single scalar numerical flux. The full solver consumes a piecewise-constant initial density field plus one boundary cell per side, then advances autoregressively via the standard FV update rule, producing the density field u(t,x) at each subsequent time step. The model is trained on simple Riemann problems (single discontinuity) and evaluated on complex piecewise-constant initial conditions with up to ten interacting discontinuities, or on noisy experimental highway density fields.

## Method

NFV_a^b replaces the analytical flux function in a finite volume scheme with a lightweight 2D CNN: a first convolutional layer with spatial kernel size a and b input channels (one per time slice), followed by five 1×1 convolutions with 15 channels, yielding only ~1200–3000 trainable parameters. Conservation is preserved by construction because the classical FV update rule u_i^{n+1} = u_i^n − (Δt/Δx)(F_{i+1/2}^n − F_{i−1/2}^n) is kept unchanged; only the flux prediction is learned. Two training objectives are provided: supervised NFV minimizes L2 error against reference solutions, while unsupervised UNFV minimizes a weak-form residual loss using 250 randomly sampled compactly supported polynomials of degree 50 as test functions, requiring no solution data. Training is autoregressive with progressively increasing prediction horizons (10→250 steps), and the resulting model generalizes from simple Riemann building blocks to complex multi-shock initial conditions and to noisy field data that does not strictly satisfy the conservation law.

## Results

On seven 1D scalar conservation laws (six LWR variants + Burgers), NFV₂¹ achieves L₂ errors ranging from 1.3×10⁻⁴ (Greenshields) to 8.5×10⁻⁴ (Burgers), consistently outperforming all first-order FV baselines and surpassing ENO/WENO on 5 of 7 equations. The larger NFV₄⁵ variant achieves up to 10× lower L₂ error than Godunov (e.g., 2.2×10⁻⁴ vs 1.8×10⁻³ on Burgers), approaching DG accuracy (1.0×10⁻⁴) while retaining FV-level implementation simplicity. On the I-24 MOTION field dataset, NFV₁₀¹¹ reduces L₂ error by 41% over the best calibrated Godunov scheme (2.20×10⁻² vs 3.74×10⁻²) and relative error by 43% (0.359 vs 0.626) on 7 unseen days.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NFV₂¹ decisively beats all three first-order FV baselines (Godunov, Lax-Friedrichs, Engquist-Osher) on every equation, with the largest margin over Lax-Friedrichs (e.g., 1.3×10⁻⁴ vs 1.3×10⁻² on Greenshields, a 100× gap). Against the higher-order ENO and WENO schemes, NFV₂¹ wins on 5 of 7 equations (Greenshields, both Triangular variants, Greenberg, Burgers) but loses on Trapezoidal (1.1×10⁻³ vs 6.2×10⁻⁴ for ENO) and Underwood (3.8×10⁻⁴ vs 9.8×10⁻⁵ for WENO), where the piecewise-linear or exponential flux structure favors the hand-tuned higher-order stencils. Discontinuous Galerkin (DG) remains the most accurate method across all seven equations, but at 6–20× the runtime and significantly higher memory cost; NFV₄⁵ narrows this gap to within a factor of 2–5 while keeping FV-level implementation complexity. On the I-24 MOTION field data, NFV₁₀¹¹ outperforms the best of five calibrated Godunov flux fits (Triangular: L₂ 1.31×10⁻¹) by 17% in L₂ error and 8% in relative error, and generalizes better to 7 unseen days (L₂ 2.20×10⁻² vs 3.74×10⁻² for Godunov). The unsupervised UNFV₂¹ variant, trained solely on the weak-form PDE residual without any solution labels, consistently trails its supervised counterpart by roughly 30–50% in L₂ error but still beats all classical first-order schemes, confirming that the conservation-law structure alone provides a strong inductive bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the classical finite volume framework (LeVeque, 2002) by replacing hand-designed numerical flux formulas with a lightweight CNN that learns fluxes over extended spatiotemporal stencils, while retaining the exact FV update rule that guarantees conservation. It departs from prior neural PDE solvers (PINNs, FNO, DeepONet) that typically lose physical structure, and from weak PINNs (wPINNs) that use weak-form residuals without the FV conservation scaffold. It also differs from prior neural-flux-in-FV approaches (Kossaczká et al., Tong et al.) by learning over larger stencils (up to 10×11) and supporting both supervised and unsupervised training, rather than augmenting a fixed-order scheme with a small correction network.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: (U)NFV achieves DG-level accuracy with FV-level implementation complexity, trains in ~15 minutes, and generalizes to noisy field data where conservation is only approximate—settings where classical schemes require careful calibration. Open questions include extension to multi-dimensional and system-of-equations settings, formal convergence guarantees (noted as parallel work), and whether the approach transfers to velocity-based formulations that better match GPS observations. The 1D scalar restriction and the need for one model per conservation law limit immediate broad applicability but establish a clear path forward.
