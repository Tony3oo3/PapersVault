## Problem

Multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) lag behind text-only reasoning LLMs (e.g., o1, Qwen3) because their internal LLMs are outdated and lack slow-thinking capabilities. Upgrading the LLM component requires repeating the entire, prohibitively expensive vision-language alignment process. The paper asks whether the LLM in an MLLM can be swapped for a stronger external reasoner without any retraining of the visual encoder or alignment layers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264761/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The pipeline takes an RGB image and a text query as input. The MLLM perception stage produces a structured set of textual outputs consisting of a query-relevant caption and a tentative solution (not a final answer). These perceptual outputs, together with the original query, are formatted into a reasoning prompt and fed to a frozen external text-only LLM, which produces the final answer in a \boxed{} format. The textual interface between the two stages is the universal plug-and-play boundary: any LLM can replace the reasoner without touching the MLLM.

## Method

RAPID decouples perception from reasoning: an MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL) is re-focused on generating query-relevant captions and tentative solutions, while a separate, stronger LLM performs the actual reasoning over those textual outputs. To align the MLLM's perceptual outputs with the downstream reasoning objective, the paper introduces Visual Perception Optimization (VPO), a GRPO-style policy gradient algorithm where the reward for each caption rollout is determined by whether the external LLM's final answer (conditioned on that caption) matches the ground truth. A caption-presence penalty (Eq. 3) prevents reward hacking where the MLLM outputs a bare solution instead of a descriptive caption. GRPO is additionally applied to optimize the tentative-solution component. Crucially, because the MLLM communicates with the reasoner purely through natural language, a single VPO-trained MLLM can be paired with any LLM at inference time, enabling a plug-and-play inference-time scaling paradigm without retraining.

## Results

RAPID achieves an average accuracy of 53.2% on seven multi-modal reasoning benchmarks when applied to Qwen2.5-VL-7B with Qwen3-8B as the external reasoner, a +11.2% improvement over the original MLLM (42.0%). The strongest configuration, Qwen2.5-VL-72B w/RAPID(GPT-OSS-120B), reaches 58.0% average accuracy, surpassing all proprietary MLLMs (Claude-3.7-Sonnet: 53.9%, Gemini-2.0-Flash: 53.7%). Notably, the 15B-total RAPID-enhanced Qwen2.5-VL-7B outperforms substantially larger models including MM-Eureka-32B (50.5%), InternVL3-38B (52.8%), and Ovis2-34B (50.6%), demonstrating a favorable performance-size trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

RAPID-enhanced Qwen2.5-VL-7B (15B total) with Qwen3-8B achieves 53.2% average, outperforming all open-source MLLMs of comparable or larger size: MM-Eureka-32B (50.5%), Ovis2-34B (50.6%), InternVL3-38B (52.8%), and even the 72B-scale VL-Rethinker-72B (54.7%) and InternVL3-78B (54.6%) are surpassed by the 32B RAPID variant (55.9%). The top RAPID configuration (Qwen2.5-VL-72B + GPT-OSS-120B) reaches 58.0%, exceeding all proprietary baselines including Claude-3.7-Sonnet (53.9%) and Gemini-2.0-Flash (53.7%) by over 4 points. Against prior caption-then-reason pipelines, RAPID substantially outperforms ECSO (44.9%) and OmniCaptioner (49.5%), with the gap attributed to the inclusion of tentative solutions and VPO-based reward optimization. The method also generalizes across MLLM backbones: applying decoupling + VPO to InternVL3-8B yields 51.9% (vs. 44.6% baseline), and decoupling alone improves VL-Rethinker-7B from 44.5% to 53.0% with GPT-OSS-120B.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the caption-then-reason pipeline lineage (Tiong et al., 2022; Guo et al., 2022; Gou et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2025) by introducing two key departures: (1) including a tentative solution alongside the caption to capture more visual information, and (2) optimizing the MLLM's perceptual outputs via a novel VPO algorithm that uses the external LLM's answer correctness as the reward signal, rather than treating captioning as a fixed upstream step. The VPO objective is directly derived from GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), repurposing the clipped surrogate loss for a multi-modal perception policy rather than a text-only reasoning policy. Unlike RL-based MLLM methods (VL-Rethinker, MM-EUREKA) that are bottlenecked by the base LLM's reasoning capacity, RAPID fully decouples the two, making the reasoning component swappable without retraining.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RAPID offers a practical 'plug-and-play' inference-time scaling paradigm: a one-time VPO-trained MLLM can be paired with any future LLM reasoner for consistent gains without the prohibitively expensive vision-language retraining cycle, at a total training cost under $300. This is particularly timely as reasoning LLMs evolve rapidly (Qwen3, GPT-OSS-120B), and the decoupling strategy generalizes across MLLM backbones (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3). Open questions include whether the frozen-LLM assumption can be relaxed (fine-tuning the reasoner yielded only marginal gains), whether RAPID extends beyond math/science to spatial or other reasoning domains, and how to enable automatic fast/slow thinking switching without prompt engineering.
