## Problem

Bayesian deep learning typically relies on explicit KL regularization to a prior, which can override the beneficial implicit regularization of the optimizer and introduce pathological uncertainty. This paper asks whether the implicit bias of (stochastic) gradient descent alone can regularize a variational neural network, eliminating the need for an explicit prior while still preventing uncertainty collapse and enabling robust out-of-distribution generalization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264158/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes standard RGB images (MNIST 28×28, CIFAR-10/100 32×32, TinyImageNet 64×64) through convolutional architectures (LeNet-5 or ResNet-34) and produces both a class prediction and a predictive variance. During training, only a single weight sample is drawn from the variational distribution per forward pass, keeping memory overhead to roughly 10% over a standard network. The probabilistic layers (input and output) use low-rank factorized covariances (S ∈ ℝ^{P×R} with R ≤ P), so the output uncertainty is a full predictive distribution rather than a scalar confidence.

## Method

The core idea is to train a variational neural network by minimizing only the expected loss E_{q_θ(w)}[ℓ(y, f_w(X))] with no KL divergence term, relying entirely on the implicit bias of SGD to select among the many global minima. For overparametrized linear models, the paper proves (Theorems 1 and 2) that SGD initialized at the prior converges to the variational distribution closest to the prior in 2-Wasserstein distance among all distributions achieving zero training error, effectively performing generalized variational inference with a Wasserstein regularizer. Practically, the method uses a Gaussian variational family with factorized low-rank covariance on the input and output layers, trains with a single parameter sample and a reduced learning rate, and extends the maximal update parametrization (μP) to the probabilistic setting so that learning rates tuned on small models transfer to larger ones without re-tuning.

## Results

On in-distribution benchmarks (MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet), IBVI achieves test error comparable to other Bayesian deep learning methods, with only Deep Ensembles providing a modest accuracy gain at substantial memory overhead. For uncertainty quantification, IBVI substantially improves NLL and ECE (calibration) over the base model, performing on par with Temperature Scaling and Deep Ensembles. On out-of-distribution corruption benchmarks (MNIST-C, CIFAR-10-C, CIFAR-100-C, TinyImageNet-C), IBVI shows improved accuracy over all baselines except Deep Ensembles, and achieves better uncertainty quantification (NLL and ECE) than Ensembles across all datasets. Training overhead is minimal: using a single parameter sample with low-rank covariance in the input and output layers adds only ~10% memory overhead over a standard neural network with similar training time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Temperature Scaling (TS) [69], IBVI matches or slightly exceeds NLL and ECE performance, while TS leaves test error unchanged as a post-hoc method. Against Laplace approximations (LA-GS [25], LA-ML [26]), IBVI consistently provides better NLL, as both LA variants show occasional worsening of NLL compared to the base model. Against Weight-Space VI (WSVI) [31], IBVI again outperforms on NLL, with WSVI also showing occasional degradation. Against SWAG [28], IBVI performs similarly on test error, with SWAG showing competitive accuracy on larger datasets only when using the maximal update parametrization. Against Deep Ensembles (DE) [29], DE achieves slightly lower test error in both in-distribution and corruption settings, but IBVI provides better uncertainty quantification (NLL and ECE) than DE on all out-of-distribution datasets, while DE incurs substantial memory overhead from maintaining multiple full networks. Overall, IBVI matches or beats all baselines on calibration and OOD uncertainty, with the only consistent accuracy gap being to Deep Ensembles.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the implicit bias theory of SGD for overparameterized linear models (Soudry et al., Gunasekar et al., Nacson et al.) from deterministic to probabilistic settings, showing that the same optimization dynamics that select the minimum-norm solution in standard regression now select the variational distribution closest to the prior in 2-Wasserstein distance. It also extends the maximal update parametrization (μP) framework of Yang et al. to variational networks, enabling hyperparameter transfer in the probabilistic setting. Conceptually, the paper departs from standard variational inference by removing the explicit KL divergence to the prior and replacing it with the implicit regularization of the optimizer, which it proves is equivalent to a generalized variational inference objective with a 2-Wasserstein regularizer (connecting to Knoblauch et al. and Wild et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach eliminates the need for prior elicitation—a persistent practical pain point in Bayesian deep learning—while reducing computational overhead compared to standard VI, making probabilistic networks more accessible. The theoretical characterization (Theorems 1 and 2) provides a rigorous bridge between implicit bias and generalized Bayesian inference, offering a principled explanation for why uncertainty does not collapse despite the absence of an explicit regularizer. Open questions include whether the results extend to non-Gaussian variational families, learned or more informative priors, and optimal placement of probabilistic layers within deeper architectures. The empirical gains over strong baselines like Deep Ensembles are modest, and the method still requires lower learning rates and slightly more training epochs than standard networks.
