## Problem

Large reasoning models (LRMs) such as DeepSeek-R1 and GPT-O1 suffer from overthinking: they generate excessively long chain-of-thought sequences that waste compute and can degrade accuracy by derailing from correct reasoning paths. Existing efficient-reasoning approaches rely on static heuristics (fixed token budgets, fixed intervals, or prompt-based length constraints) that cannot adapt to the varying difficulty of individual problems, making them suboptimal. The paper asks whether a model can dynamically determine, during generation, the precise moment when its accumulated reasoning is just sufficient to produce a correct answer.

## Input / Output

DEER operates entirely at inference time on a standard text prompt (problem/question) fed to an LRM. During generation, it intercepts the model's token-level probabilities at detected action transition points (e.g., the word 'Wait' or high-entropy positions) to compute a confidence score, and it appends a short answer-inducing prompt (e.g., '\n\nFinal Answer\n\boxed') to elicit a trial answer. The output is a truncated CoT sequence followed by the final answer; no additional parameters, training data, or architectural modifications are required. The method is implemented on top of vLLM and HuggingFace Transformers and is plug-and-play across 11 models ranging from 1.5B to 671B parameters.

## Method

DEER identifies 'pearl reasoning' points—moments where accumulated reasoning is just sufficient—through three coupled actions: (1) Reasoning Transition Monitoring detects action transition points (ATPs) via linguistic markers (e.g., 'Wait', 'Alternatively') or entropy-based step boundaries; (2) Trial Answer Inducing replaces the ATP with a 'final answer' prompt to force the model to emit a trial answer using \boxed{} delimiters; (3) Confidence Evaluating computes the geometric mean of per-token max probabilities of the trial answer and compares it to a threshold λ (default 0.95). If confidence exceeds λ, the model is forced to stop thinking and produce its conclusion; otherwise, generation resumes from the ATP. DEER-Pro enhances robustness by running N=4 parallel answer inductions with varied prompts and calibrating confidence via a MAD penalty (C_cali = C_avg − α·C_MAD), which the paper proves decouples the exit decision from model-specific prompt-sensitivity noise. A branch-parallel decoding strategy linearizes multiple trial-answer branches into a single sequence with a specialized causal attention mask and dynamic KV-cache pruning, reducing end-to-end latency by 36–59%.

## Results

Across 10 reasoning benchmarks and 11 reasoning LLMs (1.5B–671B), DEER reduces CoT sequence length by 19.1%–80.1% while improving accuracy by 0.3%–5.0% over vanilla models. On the headline Table 1 (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B), DEER achieves 90.6% on GSM8K, 89.8% on MATH-500, 85.0% on AMC 2023, 49.2% on AIME 2024, and 31.3% on GPQA-D, with an overall compression rate of 61.5%. DEER-PRo further improves accuracy (e.g., 91.0% GSM8K, 90.2% MATH-500, 87.5% AMC 23) at only a 2.8%–6.2% increase in generation length over DEER. On QwQ-32B, DEER still achieves a 19.1% length reduction despite the model's sporadic invalidation of end-of-thinking delimiters, where nearly all baselines fail completely.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DEER consistently outperforms all five baselines across models and tasks. TCC achieves reasonable compression on simple tasks (e.g., 20.1% CR on GSM8K for 7B) but collapses on complex problems like AIME 2024, where models ignore the token budget and generate responses longer than vanilla CoT. CoD and NoThinking achieve dramatic length reduction (CR as low as 7.1% on AIME24) but severely compromise accuracy (e.g., 26.7% vs. 41.7% vanilla on AIME24 for 7B). Dynasor-CoT preserves reasoning quality but suffers from late termination due to its conservative three-consecutive-answer consistency condition, yielding minimal compression (83.7%–93.0% CR). SEAL is only evaluated on GSM8K and MATH-500 and is competitive (89.4% MATH-500) but does not match DEER's accuracy gains or generalization to harder benchmarks. Notably, nearly all baselines fail completely on QwQ-32B due to its sporadic invalidation of the end-of-thinking delimiter, while DEER still achieves a 19.1% length reduction, demonstrating superior robustness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions DEER within the output-based efficient reasoning category, departing from prompt-based methods (TCC, CoD, NoThinking) that impose fixed length constraints, and from training-based approaches (SEAL, RL with length rewards) that require model modification. The key conceptual departure is the 'pearl reasoning' hypothesis: that a model's own confidence in a trial answer at action transition points (e.g., 'Wait') serves as a reliable, training-free signal for when reasoning is sufficient. DEER-Pro further extends this with MAD-based confidence calibration, drawing on signal-to-noise ratio analysis to decouple early-exit decisions from model-specific prompt sensitivity, and a branch-parallel decoding strategy to overlap trial-answer evaluation with ongoing reasoning generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DEER offers a genuinely plug-and-play solution that simultaneously reduces CoT length (19–80%) and improves accuracy (0.3–5.0%) across 11 models and 10 benchmarks, addressing a practical deployment bottleneck for reasoning LLMs without any retraining. The finding that ~75% of samples contain a 'pearl' where early exit preserves correctness, and that early exit can correct errors the full CoT makes, suggests overthinking is not merely wasteful but actively harmful. Open questions include the method's behavior in non-English reasoning settings, sensitivity to the confidence threshold λ in production (though the paper shows robustness in 0.9–0.97), and whether the branch-parallel decoding overhead remains negligible at very large model scales or in multi-turn agentic settings where reasoning chains are interrupted and resumed.
