## Problem

Existing deep generative models for biomolecular trajectory generation either generalize poorly across molecular domains or suffer from an objective mismatch between pretraining on single structures and finetuning on paired trajectory data, preventing effective transfer of pretrained structural knowledge. Additionally, multi-molecular systems such as protein-ligand complexes remain largely unexplored, and the millisecond-scale timescale of ligand binding makes direct simulation computationally prohibitive even with coarsened timesteps.

## Input / Output

The model consumes molecular systems as triplets (z, C, x) — atomic numbers, covalent bond indices, and 3D heavy-atom coordinates — augmented with a block-level representation (amino acid identity for protein residues, atom type for small molecules) and a kNN-based graph with covalent-bond edge features. During pretraining the input is a single structure x (with X_0 = Y_1 = x); during finetuning it is a trajectory pair (x_t, x_{t+τ}); for the RL docking task it is a Vina-docked ligand pose on an apo protein. The output is a sequence of conformations at a coarse timestep τ (100 ps–1 ns), and for the RL task a refined holo-state complex structure identified by maximum binding affinity.

## Method

PVB models generation as a Markov chain X_0 → Y_0 → Y_1, where a variational encoder maps the initial state to a noised latent Y_0 (Gaussian prior with σ_e = √0.5 Å, trained via KL divergence) and an augmented bridge matching decoder transports Y_0 to the target Y_1 through a Brownian-bridge SDE, preserving the Y_0–Y_1 coupling. The same combined objective (KL + ABM loss) applies uniformly to single-structure pretraining (where the conditional law degenerates to a point mass) and paired-trajectory finetuning, resolving the objective mismatch that plagues prior approaches like UniSim. The backbone is a TorchMD-NET equivariant graph network with a GVP output layer, and a small position perturbation (σ_p = 0.2 Å) is added to X_0 for robustness. For protein-ligand holo-state exploration, a memory-efficient RL finetuning step applies adjoint matching (stochastic optimal control) with a reward of −RMSD to the reference holo state, updating only a lightweight control network φ_d^u initialized from the finetuned decoder while keeping the encoder and reference decoder frozen.

## Results

On the ATLAS protein trajectory generation benchmark (14 test systems, τ=100 ps), PVB achieves a VAL-CA of 0.975 (vs. 0.485 for AlphaFlow, 0.098 for MDGEN, 0.106 for UniSim, and 0.001 for ITO), indicating near-perfect structural validity over 1,000-step generated trajectories. PVB also attains the best Decorr-TIC0 of 0.929 (vs. 0.857 for MDGEN and 0.786 for UniSim), confirming superior capture of slow dynamical modes. On the MISATO protein-ligand complex benchmark, PVB achieves the lowest EMD-ligand (0.133 nm) and EMD-CoM (0.089 nm), outperforming UniSim (0.196/0.128) and ITO (0.494/0.479). For docking pose post-optimization on PDBBind, PVB with RL finetuning reduces mean ligand RMSD to 5.92 Å (43.5% <5 Å), surpassing AutoDock Vina (6.37 Å, 26.1% <5 Å).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PVB decisively outperforms all trajectory-generation baselines on structural validity (VAL-CA): 0.975 vs. 0.485 (AlphaFlow), 0.098 (MDGEN), 0.106 (UniSim), and 0.001 (ITO), indicating that only PVB maintains physically plausible conformations over long generated trajectories. On distributional fidelity (JSD), PVB matches or beats UniSim on TIC (0.371 vs. 0.372) and MSM (0.333 vs. 0.344), and substantially outperforms ITO (0.792 vs. 0.457 on Rg) and MDGEN (0.493 vs. 0.457 on Rg). For protein-ligand complexes on MISATO, PVB achieves the best EMD-ligand (0.133 nm) and EMD-CoM (0.089 nm), beating UniSim by ~32% and ITO by ~73% on ligand pose accuracy, though UniSim edges out PVB slightly on RMSE-CONTACT (0.049 vs. 0.055). In the docking post-optimization task, PVB with RL finetuning surpasses AutoDock Vina on ligand placement (mean RMSD 5.92 vs. 6.37 Å; 43.5% vs. 26.1% within 5 Å) but trails Vina on pocket RMSD (1.97 vs. 1.21 Å), reflecting that the protein scaffold is held fixed during generation. On mdCATH, PVB narrows the gap to the MD oracle on JSD-TIC (0.376 vs. 0.316) and JSD-MSM (0.362 vs. 0.282) while maintaining VAL-CA of 0.963, compared to UniSim's 0.522.

## Relation to Prior Work

PVB builds directly on UniSim (Yu et al., 2025), which first demonstrated cross-domain biomolecular trajectory generation via 3D molecular pretraining, but resolves the critical objective mismatch between unconditional pretraining on single structures and conditional finetuning on trajectory pairs by introducing a variational encoder-decoder with augmented bridge matching (De Bortoli et al., 2023). The decoder's bridge-matching formulation preserves the coupling between latent and target states, while the encoder's KL-regularized latent space prevents degeneration during single-structure pretraining. For the protein-ligand docking application, the paper adapts adjoint matching (Domingo-Enrich et al., 2024) into a memory-efficient RL finetuning procedure grounded in stochastic optimal control, a formulation not previously applied to biomolecular trajectory generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By unifying pretraining and finetuning under a single generative objective, PVB enables cross-domain structural knowledge (from small molecules, proteins, and complexes) to transfer seamlessly into trajectory generation, addressing a key bottleneck in scaling MD simulation beyond single molecular domains. The RL-based holo-state exploration offers a practical post-optimization tool for docking that bypasses local free-energy minima, though it remains limited to short generated trajectories. Key open questions include whether sequential trajectory generation can be parallelized while preserving temporal correlations, and whether the approach scales to larger multi-molecular systems beyond the ~2,500-atom regime tested here.
