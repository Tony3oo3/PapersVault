## Problem

Existing compression studies on Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) either fail to comprehensively compare quantization, distillation, and pruning on reasoning-intensive benchmarks, or lack mechanistic interpretation of how compression affects specific weight components. A fundamental question remains unanswered: which individual weight matrices are most critical for reasoning capabilities, and how do different compression methods differentially harm them?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262127/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes compressed LRM variants (dynamically quantized at 2.51/1.73/1.58-bit, distilled at 7B–70B, and pruned at 30%–80% sparsity) and evaluates them on four reasoning benchmarks of varying difficulty (AIME 2024, FOLIO, Temporal Sequences, MuSiQue). For interpretability, it consumes activations from every linear module (q/k/v/o projections, gate/up/down projections) at every layer, along with a 120-instance annotation set where GPT-4o locates token sequences corresponding to four reasoning behaviors. Outputs include per-benchmark accuracy scores, per-module importance heatmaps (I_ml^c), and relative importance shift maps that isolate which specific weight matrices lose causal relevance to reasoning after compression.

## Method

The paper combines performance benchmarking with fine-grained mechanistic interpretation. For interpretation, it adapts difference of means to extract a steering vector u_ml^c for each linear module m at layer ℓ for each of four reasoning behaviors (backtracking, uncertainty estimation, example testing, adding knowledge), then applies attribution patching to compute an importance score I_ml^c ≈ (1/|D+|)|Σ (u_ml^c)^T ∂L/∂a_ml| that captures the causal contribution of each weight matrix to a specific reasoning capability. Unlike prior layer-wise analyses, this yields module-level granularity across all 7 linear components per layer. By computing relative importance (RI_ml^c) and tracking its shift from an original LRM to its compressed variant (setting increases to zero to isolate capability loss), the method identifies which specific modules are over-compressed. This framework reveals that the MLP up_proj in the final layer is the single most important component for reasoning in distilled LRMs, and that current quantization methods (AWQ, GPTQ) systematically over-compress final-layer modules and MLP gate projections—protecting just ~2% of weights in the final-layer MLP raises average accuracy by 6.57%.

## Results

The dynamically quantized 2.51-bit DeepSeek-R1 achieves an average accuracy of 84.8% across AIME 2024, FOLIO, and Temporal Sequences, slightly exceeding the original 671B R1 (83.1%), while 4-bit quantization (AWQ, GPTQ, GPTAQ, ANY4) on distilled models preserves performance within ~1–2% of their unquantized counterparts. In contrast, 3-bit quantization shows clear collapse (e.g., R1-Distill-Llama-8B drops from 65.2% to 47.8% avg with 3-bit GPTQ), and 50% sparsity pruning causes severe degradation (R1-Distill-Llama-70B drops from 81.8% to 64.2% avg). Protecting only ~2% of weights (final-layer MLP modules) in a 3-bit AWQ model raises average accuracy by 6.57% (from 46.0% to 52.57% on R1-Distill-Llama-8B), surpassing all 3-bit baselines by up to 23.17%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among quantization methods, 4-bit AWQ, GPTQ, GPTAQ, and ANY4 all achieve near-lossless compression on the larger distilled models (e.g., R1-Distill-Qwen-32B: 82.2–83.1% avg), but at 3-bit, GPTAQ consistently outperforms GPTQ (e.g., 77.1% vs. 72.6% avg on Llama-70B; 74.2% vs. 72.5% on Qwen-32B), while ANY3 achieves the best 3-bit result on Qwen-32B (78.6% avg). The Unsloth 2.51-bit dynamic quantization of full R1 (84.8% avg) outperforms all distilled and pruned variants, confirming that smaller compression ratios retain an advantage even when over-parameterization is present. Pruning is the most destructive: 50% SparseGPT on R1-Distill-Llama-70B drops average accuracy by 17.6 points (81.8→64.2), and the model fully collapses (0% on AIME) by 60% sparsity. Distillation itself preserves reasoning well (R1-Distill-Qwen-32B reaches 82.2% avg, close to R1's 83.1%), but severely compromises parametric knowledge, as evidenced by MuSiQue F1 dropping from 27.51 (R1) to 10.95 (Qwen-32B) and 4.43 (Llama-8B). The paper's proposed protection of final-layer MLP modules in 3-bit AWQ (52.57% avg on Llama-8B) beats the best 3-bit baseline (3-bit GPTAQ at 43.5%) by 9.07 points, demonstrating that targeted mixed-precision strategies can substantially close the 3-bit performance gap.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected research threads: model compression (quantization, distillation, pruning) and mechanistic interpretability of reasoning in LLMs. It adapts difference-of-means steering vectors (Arditi et al., 2024) and attribution patching (Syed et al., 2023) to compute fine-grained weight importance for specific reasoning behaviors, extending Venhoff et al. (2025) who studied reasoning steering vectors but did not analyze compression effects. Unlike prior compression benchmarks that rely on perplexity or simple end-tasks, this work evaluates on reasoning-intensive datasets and provides a causal, module-level explanation for why compression degrades performance, positioning itself as the first systematic interpretability study of compression on large reasoning models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides actionable, empirically validated guidance for LRM compression: protecting just 2% of weights (final-layer MLP modules) in a 3-bit AWQ model yields a 6.57% average accuracy gain, outperforming all 3-bit baselines by up to 23.17%. The finding that parameter count affects knowledge memorization more than reasoning capability offers a clear decision framework for choosing between quantization (preserves parameters) and pruning/distillation (reduces parameters). Open questions include whether the final-layer up_proj criticality generalizes to MoE architectures at scale, whether the importance signals can drive adaptive per-module compression ratios, and whether the protection strategy remains effective at even lower bit-widths (1–2 bit) where the paper observes no clear collapse point.
