## Problem

Self-consistency with majority voting improves LLM reasoning accuracy but suffers from diminishing returns and linearly scaling token costs, since all reasoning traces are treated equally regardless of quality. Existing global confidence measures (e.g., average trace confidence) obscure local reasoning breakdowns at intermediate steps and require complete trace generation before evaluation, preventing early termination of low-quality traces. The paper asks whether local, fine-grained confidence signals can both improve final-answer accuracy and enable real-time pruning of unpromising traces during generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a reasoning problem prompt and the model's per-token probability distributions (top-20 token probabilities) as input, computing token-level confidence scores on the fly. In offline mode, it consumes a pool of pre-generated complete reasoning traces (up to 4,096) and outputs a final answer via confidence-weighted majority voting after filtering the top-η% highest-confidence traces. In online mode, it produces the final answer while dynamically terminating individual traces mid-generation once their sliding-window group confidence (window size 2,048 tokens) falls below a per-problem threshold calibrated from 16 warmup traces, also stopping globally once consensus among completed traces reaches τ=0.95 or the trace budget is exhausted.

## Method

DeepConf replaces global average trace confidence with local measurements computed over overlapping sliding windows (group size 2,048 tokens): group confidence (window mean), bottom-10% group confidence (mean of the lowest 10% of window scores), lowest group confidence (minimum window score), and tail confidence (mean over the final 2,048 tokens). In offline mode, these scores weight each trace's vote in majority voting and optionally filter to the top-η% most confident traces before aggregation. In online mode, the lowest group confidence serves as a real-time stopping criterion: after an offline warmup of N_init=16 complete traces establishes a threshold s (the (100−η)-th percentile of warmup confidences), any new trace whose current group confidence drops below s is terminated immediately, and adaptive consensus checking halts further sampling once the modal answer's weighted vote share reaches τ=0.95. The approach requires no model training, no additional hyperparameter tuning beyond standard decoding settings, and integrates directly into existing serving frameworks such as vLLM.

## Results

In offline mode with 512 traces, DeepConf (Tail Confidence, top-10% filtering) achieves 99.9% accuracy on AIME 2025 with GPT-OSS-120B, compared to 97.0% for standard majority voting (Cons@512) and 91.8% for pass@1. In online mode, DeepConf-low reduces generated tokens by up to 84.7% (GPT-OSS-120B on AIME 2025: 0.49×10⁸ vs 3.23×10⁸ tokens) while improving accuracy from 97.1% to 97.9%. Across all models (8B–120B) and five benchmarks, DeepConf-low typically saves 43–80% tokens versus majority voting at matched or higher accuracy, while the more conservative DeepConf-high saves 18–59% tokens with minimal accuracy change.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard self-consistency with majority voting (Wang et al., 2023; Cons@512), DeepConf consistently wins on accuracy while using far fewer tokens: in offline mode, Tail-Confidence with top-10% filtering lifts GPT-OSS-120B from 97.0% to 99.9% on AIME 2025 and DeepSeek-8B from 82.3% to 87.4%; in online mode, DeepConf-low improves DeepSeek-8B on AIME 24 from 86.7% to 92.5% (+5.8 pp) while cutting tokens by 77.9%. Against the global self-certainty baseline (Kang et al., 2025; Mean@512), the paper's local confidence measures (Bottom-10% Group Confidence and Tail Confidence) with filtering substantially outperform: e.g., on DeepSeek-8B AIME 25, Tail@10% reaches 87.4% versus 82.3% for unweighted majority and 82.3% for mean-confidence-weighted voting without filtering. The local measures also better separate correct from incorrect traces in confidence distributions (Fig. 2), and the Lowest Group Confidence variant used in online mode achieves the best average accuracy (84.4%) across 23 model-dataset pairs compared to 84.0% for Bottom-10% and 83.9% for Mean. The main caveat is that aggressive top-10% filtering can occasionally hurt accuracy when the model is overconfident on wrong answers (e.g., GPT-OSS-120B on HMMT25 drops from 92.9% to 88.9%), in which case the conservative top-90% setting is safer.

## Relation to Prior Work

DeepConf builds directly on the self-consistency framework (Wang et al., 2023) and the self-certainty confidence metric (Kang et al., 2025), but departs from prior work by replacing global average trace confidence with local, sliding-window confidence signals (group confidence, bottom-10% group confidence, lowest group confidence, and tail confidence). It also extends the offline filtering paradigm to an online setting where traces are terminated in real time based on a dynamically calibrated threshold, combining this with the adaptive sampling idea from Dynamic Voting (Xue et al., 2023). The key conceptual shift is from post-hoc, sequence-level quality assessment to trajectory-level, step-local monitoring that enables early stopping without sacrificing the filtering benefit.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: up to 84.7% token reduction while matching or exceeding majority-voting accuracy, with no additional training or hyperparameter tuning, makes confidence-aware filtering a drop-in efficiency layer for existing LLM serving stacks. A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is the 'confidently wrong' failure mode, where aggressive filtering (top-10%) can hurt accuracy when the model is overconfident on incorrect traces; the conservative top-90% variant mitigates this at the cost of smaller gains. Open questions include whether confidence-based early stopping can guide RL exploration during training, and whether better calibration techniques can address the overconfidence problem that caps the upside of aggressive filtering.
