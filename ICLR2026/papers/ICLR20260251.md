## Problem

In RLVR for complex mathematical reasoning, sparse outcome rewards create a near-miss problem where almost-correct trajectories receive the same negative signal as complete failures, leading to exploration stagnation and signal collapse when all trajectories in a group share the same reward. Existing remedies like DAPO's dynamic sampling are computationally wasteful (≈4× prompt volume), while hint-based methods like StepHint rely on external teacher models, precluding autonomous self-improvement.

## Input / Output

The model receives a math problem prompt and generates multi-turn solution trajectories (up to 8 turns) interleaved with Python code interpreter calls via the ReTool framework, producing a final numerical answer. During training, 16 responses are sampled per prompt with a 16K-token max length; evaluation uses 32K tokens. The hint mechanism consumes a prefix (5%–45% of a successful trajectory's length) appended to the original prompt, conditioning the policy on a partially-correct reasoning path rather than the full solution.

## Method

HiPO introduces an endogenous self-hint mechanism that transforms rare stochastic successes into dense contrastive learning signals. For each prompt, it first generates a standard GRPO group of n trajectories; if the group is a 'near-miss group' (success rate > 0 but < 50%), it extracts all successful trajectories into a hint pool and generates a replacement 'hinted group' where each trajectory is conditioned on a randomly-truncated prefix (ratio sampled uniformly from {0.05, 0.10, …, 0.45}) of a uniformly-sampled source trajectory. These hinted groups strategically replace 'null-signal groups' (zero reward variance) in the batch, and the policy is updated via GRPO's clipped objective over the augmented batch. The dynamic, per-trajectory hint ratio is essential: a fixed ratio would make all hinted trajectories share an identical prefix, causing the aggregate advantage for prefix tokens to sum to zero and nullifying the gradient. This self-teaching loop requires no external teacher model and evolves as the policy improves.

## Results

HiPO achieves an average avg@32 of 54.8 across five mathematical reasoning benchmarks, improving +5.0 pp over GRPO (49.8) and +1.1 pp over DAPO (53.7). The largest gains appear on the hardest datasets: +10.3 pp on CMIMC 2025 (53.8 vs. 43.5), +4.9 pp on BRUMO 2025 (46.6 vs. 41.7), and +4.6 pp on AIME 2024 (76.7 vs. 72.1). On maj@32, HiPO reaches 61.7 on average versus 57.0 for GRPO and 57.8 for DAPO, with the most notable jump on AIME 2025 (76.7 vs. 70.0).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GRPO, HiPO wins on avg@32 across all five benchmarks, with the most substantial margin on CMIMC 2025 (+10.3 pp, 53.8 vs. 43.5) and AIME 2024 (+4.6 pp, 76.7 vs. 72.1), yielding a +5.0 pp average improvement. Against DAPO, HiPO leads on avg@32 for AIME 2025 (+2.4 pp) and CMIMC 2025 (+3.9 pp) but trails slightly on BRUMO 2025 (−1.2 pp) and HMMT 2025 (−0.6 pp), for a net +1.1 pp average gain; the paper notes DAPO's dynamic sampling consumes ~4× the prompt volume to reach comparable performance. On maj@32, HiPO dominates both baselines on AIME 2025 (76.7 vs. 70.0/70.0) and CMIMC 2025 (65.0 vs. 55.0/52.5), though it underperforms DAPO on HMMT 2025 (40.0 vs. 43.3). Relative to the untrained Qwen3-8B base, HiPO improves avg@32 by +18.1 pp on average, with the largest absolute gain on HMMT 2025 (+16.8 pp). On the OOD HumanEval coding benchmark, HiPO ties GRPO at 71.3% Pass@1, both marginally above the base model's 70.7%.

## Relation to Prior Work

HiPO builds directly on GRPO's group-relative advantage formulation and the ReTool paradigm for tool-integrated RL, but departs from both GRPO and DAPO by addressing their shared vulnerability to signal collapse under sparse rewards. Unlike DAPO's reactive dynamic sampling (which brute-forces additional rollouts until a non-zero advantage appears) or external-hint methods like QuestA and StepHint (which rely on off-policy teacher trajectories), HiPO introduces an endogenous self-hint mechanism: it captures the model's own rare in-batch successes and repurposes their prefixes as on-policy guidance for the entire group. This positions HiPO as a synthesis of hint-based scaffolding and self-supervised bootstrapping, eliminating the dependency on external expert data while preserving the distributional alignment that off-policy hints lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates a concrete, implementable mechanism for breaking the exploration-stagnation loop that plagues RLVR on hard reasoning tasks, with the +5.0 pp average gain and the 4× computational efficiency over DAPO making it practically attractive for teams training reasoning models. The finding that static off-policy hints from the base model actually underperform unguided GRPO is a useful negative result that underscores the importance of on-policy alignment in self-improvement loops. Open questions include whether the self-hint mechanism generalizes beyond 8B-scale models and the math domain, whether the dynamic hint-ratio sampling (0.05–0.45) is optimal or task-dependent, and whether the approach extends to non-sequential or multi-agent reasoning settings where 'prefix' hints are less natural.
