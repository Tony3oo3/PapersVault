## Problem

Existing inductive link prediction methods for knowledge hypergraphs assume a fixed relational vocabulary and cannot generalize to novel relation types at test time. Knowledge graph foundation models (KGFMs) like ULTRA handle unseen relations but are restricted to binary relations, leaving a gap for higher-arity hypergraph settings. HYPER addresses this by being the first foundation model that generalizes zero-shot to knowledge hypergraphs with both unseen entities and unseen relations of arbitrary arity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264311/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a knowledge hypergraph G = (V, E, R) where hyperedges have arbitrary (unbounded) arity, along with a query specifying a relation, observed arguments, and a masked position. It produces a score for every candidate entity at the masked position, yielding a full ranking. A key structural detail is that the model internally constructs a relation graph whose edge labels are ordered position pairs (a, b) — there is no fixed upper bound on the number of such labels, and the positional interaction encoder must extrapolate to position indices never seen during pretraining.

## Method

HYPER operates in two stages. First, it constructs a relation graph G_rel where nodes are relation types and directed edges (r₁, r₂) with label (i, j) indicate that position i of r₁ and position j of r₂ share an entity; a compositional positional interaction encoder (sinusoidal encodings of positions a and b concatenated and passed through a shared two-layer MLP) maps each (a, b) pair to a dense embedding that is injective, bounded, and Lipschitz, enabling extrapolation to unseen arities. Second, a relation encoder (HCNet) performs conditional message passing over G_rel using these positional interaction embeddings as edge messages, producing query-conditioned relation representations; an entity encoder (another HCNet) then performs conditional message passing over the original hypergraph G using those relation representations. The model is pretrained on a mixture of knowledge graphs and hypergraphs (e.g., 3KG+2HG) and can be applied zero-shot or fine-tuned on target tasks, preserving equivariance over both nodes and relations.

## Results

On the 16 node-and-relation inductive hypergraph benchmarks (Table 2), HYPER (end-to-end) achieves MRR of 0.468 on JF-50, 0.332 on MFB-25, 0.215 on WD-25, and 0.202 on JF-25, dramatically outperforming HCNet (best: 0.104 on WP-25) and G-MPNN (best: 0.011 on JF-25). In the zero-shot setting, HYPER(3KG+2HG) achieves MRR of 0.216 on JF-25, 0.363 on MFB-25, 0.132 on WP-25, and 0.223 on WD-25, consistently surpassing ULTRA†(3KG+2HG) (0.209, 0.343, 0.045, 0.183 respectively). On node-inductive datasets (Table 3), finetuned HYPER(3KG+2HG) reaches MRR of 0.463 (JF-IND), 0.446 (WP-IND), and 0.455 (MFB-IND), outperforming HCNet (0.435, 0.414, 0.368) and ULTRA†(3KG+2HG) (0.421, 0.349, 0.303).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HYPER decisively outperforms all end-to-end hypergraph baselines: G-MPNN (Yadati 2020) and HCNet (Huang 2025b) both rely on stored relation embeddings and collapse to near-zero MRR (≤0.01) when test relations are unseen, while HYPER maintains MRR above 0.13 across all 16 node-and-relation inductive splits. Against the KGFM baseline ULTRA† (Galkin 2024) applied on reified hypergraphs, HYPER(3KG+2HG) wins on 14 of 16 zero-shot splits, with the largest gaps on WP-25 (0.132 vs 0.045) and WD-25 (0.223 vs 0.183); notably, ULTRA†(50KG) trained on 50 KGs performs far worse than ULTRA†(3KG), indicating that scaling pretraining data does not compensate for the lack of explicit hypergraph modeling. On node-inductive datasets (Table 3), HYPER(3KG+2HG) finetuned achieves MRR 0.463/0.446/0.455 on JF-IND/WP-IND/MFB-IND, beating HCNet (0.435/0.414/0.368), RD-MPNN (Zhou 2023b) (0.402/0.304/0.122), G-MPNN (0.219/0.177/0.124), HGNN (Feng 2018) (0.102/0.072/0.121), and HyperGCN (Yadati 2019) (0.099/0.075/0.118). KG-ICL (Cui 2024), evaluated in Appendix G.2 on reified hypergraphs, shows substantially lower performance than HYPER across all 16 datasets, further confirming that existing KGFMs struggle with hypergraph structures. The only setting where HYPER does not clearly dominate is standard binary KG inductive link prediction (Appendix E), where ULTRA generally performs better, though HYPER remains competitive.

## Relation to Prior Work

HYPER extends two complementary lines of work: the conditional message-passing architecture of HCNet (Huang et al., 2025b) for knowledge hypergraphs, and the fundamental-relations framework of ULTRA (Galkin et al., 2024) for knowledge graph foundation models. The key conceptual leap is generalizing ULTRA's four binary fundamental relations (head-to-head, head-to-tail, etc.) to arbitrary-arity positional interactions via a compositional sinusoidal encoding, and constructing a relation graph that captures how relations interact through shared entities at specific positions. This departs from prior hypergraph methods (G-MPNN, RD-MPNN, HCNet) that store fixed relation embeddings and thus cannot handle unseen relations, and from prior KG foundation models (ULTRA, KG-ICL, InGram) that are restricted to binary relations and must rely on reification to handle higher-arity data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

HYPER is the first model to achieve zero-shot generalization to knowledge hypergraphs with both unseen entities and unseen relations of arbitrary arity, filling a gap that neither hypergraph-specific methods nor KG foundation models could address. The 16 newly constructed inductive benchmarks with controlled proportions of unseen relations provide a systematic evaluation framework the field lacked. A key limitation is the quadratic growth of positional interactions with arity, which constrains scalability to very high-arity relations; additionally, ULTRA still outperforms HYPER on standard binary KG tasks, suggesting the hypergraph inductive bias carries a cost in the simpler setting. Future work on scalable approximations and bridging the higher-order/binary performance gap are natural next steps.
