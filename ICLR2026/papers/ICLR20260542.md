## Problem

Cover song generation—simultaneously synthesizing new vocals and coherent accompaniment while preserving the source vocal melody under text-guided style transfer—remains largely unaddressed. Existing melody-control methods for text-to-music models rely on cross-attention (indirect, computationally redundant temporal alignment) or element-wise addition (limited modulation flexibility), and all encode conditions in isolation without adapting them to the generative model's hidden states, leading to feature conflicts and degraded audio quality.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260542/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a vocal F0 sequence extracted at 100 Hz via RVMPE (normalized to 50–900 Hz voiced range and concatenated with a binary voiced/unvoiced flag), a text prompt of style tags, and lyrics as input, and produces a full cover song (vocals plus harmonious accompaniment) up to 240 seconds. The F0 is encoded by 1D convolutions into melody features and interpolated to match the hidden-state sequence length before injection. Unlike singing voice synthesis or conversion, the output is a complete multi-track song requiring global accompaniment coherence rather than a short single-track vocal segment.

## Method

SongEcho builds on the frozen ACE-Step Linear DiT backbone and introduces IA-EiLM, a two-component conditioning module inserted before the FFN layer in every Transformer block. EiLM extends FiLM by generating element-wise modulation parameters (γ, β) whose shape exactly matches the hidden states (B×T×D_i) in a single linear projection, enabling temporally aligned melody injection without cross-attention or recurrent temporal dependencies. IACR employs a WaveNet-style gating mechanism that interacts the melody features with the current hidden states to produce instance-adaptive conditioning, resolving the underconstrained optimization problem that arises when static modulation parameters must work across all possible hidden states. Only the melody encoder and IA-EiLM modules are trained (49.1M parameters, ~3% of the ControlNet baseline), using zero-initialized projections and a flow-matching loss on the newly curated Suno70k dataset of 69,469 AI-generated songs.

## Results

On the Suno70k test set, SongEcho achieves the best performance across all metrics: Raw Pitch Accuracy (RPA) of 0.7080, Raw Chroma Accuracy (RCA) of 0.7339, Overall Accuracy (OA) of 0.6952, CLAP score of 0.3243, FD_openl3 of 42.06, KL_passt of 0.1123, and Phoneme Error Rate (PER) of 0.2951. The method uses only 49.1M trainable parameters, representing less than 3% of the ACE-Step+SA ControlNet baseline (1.6B) and 26% of ACE-Step+MuseControlLite (189M). On the SongEval benchmark, SongEcho similarly leads with RPA 0.7164, RCA 0.7326, OA 0.7097, and FD 51.98, confirming generalization beyond the training distribution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SongEcho outperforms all three melody-guided baselines on every metric. Against ACE-Step+SA ControlNet (Hou et al., 2025), SongEcho improves RPA from 0.6209 to 0.7080 (+14%), reduces FD_openl3 from 105.95 to 42.06 (−60%), and lowers PER from 0.3714 to 0.2951, while using only 49.1M vs. 1.6B trainable parameters. Against the LoRA variant (331M params), the gains are similar: RPA +14%, FD −58%, PER −21%. Against ACE-Step+MuseControlLite (Tsai et al., 2025), the advantage is most pronounced on melody fidelity (RPA 0.7080 vs. 0.5205, +36%) and audio quality (FD 42.06 vs. 72.04, −42%), with PER dropping from 0.4194 to 0.2951. The paper attributes MuseControlLite's weaker melody control to its cross-attention mechanism's inability to establish precise temporal alignment, and SA ControlNet's limitations to the fixed scaling factor inherent in element-wise addition. Subjective MOS scores further confirm SongEcho's superiority, with the highest ratings across Melody Fidelity, Text Adherence, Audio Quality, and Overall Preference for both music-background and non-music-background listeners.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the FiLM conditioning paradigm (Perez et al., 2018) from feature-wise to element-wise modulation (EiLM) for temporally precise melody injection, and introduces Instance-Adaptive Condition Refinement (IACR) to make conditional features dynamically adapt to the generator's hidden states—a mechanism absent from prior melody-control methods. It departs from the dominant ControlNet-style element-wise addition (Wu et al., 2024; Hou et al., 2025) and cross-attention (Tsai et al., 2025) approaches by arguing both are suboptimal: addition limits modulation flexibility while cross-attention introduces indirect temporal alignment and computational redundancy. The framework is built on ACE-Step, a diffusion-transformer text-to-song model, and positions cover song generation (simultaneous vocal + accompaniment synthesis conditioned on source melody and text) as a distinct task from singing voice synthesis/conversion and instrumental melody-conditioned generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a genuinely underexplored task (full-song cover generation with melody preservation) and contributes a parameter-efficient conditioning technique (49M trainable params vs. 1.6B for ControlNet) that outperforms baselines across all metrics, suggesting that lightweight, well-designed conditioning can rival heavy architectural additions. The Suno70k dataset (~3,000 hours of annotated AI-generated songs) fills a critical gap in open-source full-song resources. Key limitations include reliance on ACE-Step's limited vocal timbre control (gender-only), exclusion of local expressive adaptations (vibrato, phoneme timing), and the fact that all training data is AI-generated, which may constrain the model's ability to capture human singing subtlety. The IACR principle—making conditions instance-adaptive via interaction with hidden states—has potential applicability beyond music to other conditional generation tasks.
