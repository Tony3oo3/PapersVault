## Problem

Existing context adaptation methods for LLMs suffer from two fundamental limitations: brevity bias, where iterative prompt optimization collapses toward short generic instructions that omit domain-specific heuristics and failure modes, and context collapse, where monolithic LLM rewriting of accumulated context progressively erodes detailed knowledge into shorter, less informative summaries. The paper argues that contexts should function as comprehensive, structured playbooks that accumulate and preserve domain insights rather than being compressed into terse summaries.

## Input / Output

ACE consumes task queries, execution traces (code success/failure, tool-call results, validation outcomes), and the current structured context playbook as input. It produces compact delta updates—small sets of candidate bullets each containing metadata (unique identifier, helpful/harmful counters) and content (a reusable strategy, domain concept, or failure mode)—which are merged deterministically into the existing playbook by lightweight non-LLM logic. Because updates are itemized and localized, multiple deltas can be merged in parallel, and the playbook grows incrementally rather than being regenerated in full.

## Method

ACE introduces a three-role agentic architecture—Generator (produces reasoning trajectories), Reflector (distills concrete insights from successes and errors, optionally across multiple refinement rounds), and Curator (synthesizes lessons into compact delta entries)—that mirrors human learning through experimentation, reflection, and consolidation. Two key mechanisms prevent the failure modes of prior methods: (1) incremental delta updates replace costly monolithic rewrites with localized edits to individual bullets, reducing latency by 82–91% and preserving accumulated knowledge; (2) a grow-and-refine policy appends new bullets while updating existing ones in place, with periodic semantic-embedding-based deduplication to control redundancy. The Reflector is deliberately separated from the Curator to improve insight quality, and the entire pipeline operates without requiring ground-truth labels, leveraging natural execution feedback for self-improvement in both offline (system prompt optimization) and online (test-time memory) settings.

## Results

On the AppWorld agent benchmark (DeepSeek-V3.1-671B), ReAct+ACE achieves 59.4% average accuracy in offline adaptation (+17.0% over the ReAct baseline of 42.4%) and 59.5% in online adaptation (+17.1%), outperforming GEPA (46.4%) and Dynamic Cheatsheet (51.9%) by 13.0% and 7.6% respectively. On financial reasoning (FiNER + Formula), ACE reaches 81.9% average accuracy offline (+12.8% over base) and 76.6% online (+7.5%), surpassing GEPA (72.5%) and MIPROv2 (70.9%). ACE also matches the top-ranked production agent IBM CUGA (60.3%) on the AppWorld leaderboard using a smaller open-source model, and surpasses it by 8.4% TGC on the harder test-challenge split with online adaptation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ACE consistently outperforms all five baselines across both agent and domain-specific tasks. Against ReAct (the base agent), ACE gains +17.0% (offline) and +17.1% (online) on AppWorld average accuracy. Against GEPA, the strongest prompt-evolution baseline, ACE wins by 13.0% on AppWorld (59.4% vs 46.4%) and by 9.4% on Finance (81.9% vs 72.5%), demonstrating that structured evolving playbooks substantially outperform single optimized instruction prompts. Against Dynamic Cheatsheet (the closest agentic-memory baseline), ACE wins by 7.6% on AppWorld online (59.5% vs 51.9%) and by 4.8% on Finance online with GT labels (76.6% vs 71.8%), while DC actually degrades below the base model without GT labels (65.4% vs 69.1%). Against ICL and MIPROv2, ACE gains 12.3–13.5% on AppWorld and 10.9–11.1% on Finance, confirming that accumulating granular domain insights is far more effective than fixed demonstrations or Bayesian-optimized prompts. Notably, ACE retains most of its gains even without ground-truth labels (57.2% offline, 59.5% online on AppWorld), whereas DC drops below the base model in the same no-label setting on Finance.

## Relation to Prior Work

ACE extends the agentic context adaptation paradigm initiated by Dynamic Cheatsheet (Suzgun et al., 2025), which introduced test-time memory accumulation for LLMs, by introducing a structured three-role architecture (Generator, Reflector, Curator) and replacing monolithic context rewrites with incremental delta updates. It departs from prompt-optimization methods like GEPA and MIPROv2, which treat context as a single monolithic prompt to be evolved, by representing context as an itemized, structured playbook that grows and refines over time. The paper explicitly identifies two failure modes—brevity bias (over-compression toward generic instructions) and context collapse (sudden information loss during full rewrites)—as the key limitations motivating its design, positioning ACE as a solution to problems that prior methods either ignore or inadvertently cause.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ACE demonstrates that comprehensive, evolving contexts can match or surpass production-level agents (IBM CUGA with GPT-4.1) using smaller open-source models, while reducing adaptation latency by ~87% and rollout counts by ~75%, making self-improving LLM systems substantially more practical and cost-effective. The context collapse finding (18K tokens collapsing to 122 tokens in a single rewrite step) provides a concrete, well-documented failure mode that validates the need for incremental updates in any iterative context-adaptation pipeline. Open questions remain around dependence on feedback quality (performance degrades without reliable execution signals or ground-truth labels), applicability to tasks that genuinely benefit from concise instructions rather than rich playbooks, and how ACE integrates with continual learning and selective unlearning for long-lived deployments.
