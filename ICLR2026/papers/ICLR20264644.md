## Problem

Language models suffer from the reversal curse (failing to infer 'B is A' from 'A is B') and model editing methods fail to propagate updates to logically entailed facts. Prior work attributed these failures to the autoregressive training objective or architectural limitations, but the specific internal representational geometry required for symmetric and compositional relational reasoning remained unidentified.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264644/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model consumes plain-text relational sentences of the form '[Subject First Name] [Family Name] [Relation] [Object First Name] [Family Name]' and produces next-token predictions for the object entity. For probing, hidden states are extracted at the final token position of subject and object names from each of 12 layers (d=896), and the bilinear probe fits a d×d relation matrix M_r per relation via SVD-based ridge regression over 1,250 entity embeddings. Editing experiments take a single fact to modify and report three metrics: edit success on the primary fact, logical generalization on entailed reverse and compositional facts, and locality on unrelated facts.

## Method

The paper trains 206M-parameter GPT-NeoX decoder-only Transformers from scratch on a synthetic family knowledge graph (1,000 families, 8 relations, ~318M tokens) and shows that weight decay >1.0 is the critical hyperparameter that induces an emergent bilinear relational structure in hidden representations, enabling near-perfect inference of withheld reverse relations. Three probes—linear (Jacobian-based affine mapping), translational (constant vector offset), and bilinear (RESCAL-style s_l^T M_r o_l scoring)—are trained per layer and relation to identify which geometry the model has learned; only the bilinear probe achieves >95% accuracy in middle layers (6–9) for models that overcome the reversal curse. The learned relation matrices are validated against formal algebraic tests: transposition approximates inverse relations and matrix products approximate composed relations. For model editing, layer-wise fine-tuning of the MLP output layer on a single modified fact is used, revealing that models with bilinear structure propagate edits to entailed facts (R²=0.939 correlation with peak bilinear probe accuracy) while reversal-cursed models fail to generalize, and that optimal editing targets early-to-mid layers (1–4) rather than the layers where the bilinear structure is strongest.

## Results

On the synthetic family knowledge graph, models trained with sufficient weight decay (≥1.0) achieve >98% accuracy on unseen reverse relations (father/mother), overcoming the reversal curse, while models with weight decay <1.0 remain reversal-cursed (<40%). The bilinear probe (RESCAL) reaches peak accuracy above 95% in middle layers (6–9) for successful models, while linear and translational probes hover near the 1/3 chance baseline. In model editing, both model types achieve near-perfect direct edit success, but only bilinear-structured models propagate edits to entailed facts, with a strong correlation (R²=0.939) between peak bilinear probe accuracy and maximum logical generalization.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three relational structure probes: the bilinear (RESCAL) probe proposed here, the Linear Relational Embedding probe from Hernandez et al. (2024) which extracts Jacobian-based affine transformations, and the Translational probe from Merullo et al. (2024) which models relations as constant vector offsets. The bilinear probe decisively outperforms both baselines: it achieves >95% accuracy in middle layers for successful models, while the linear and translational probes remain at or below the 1/3 chance baseline across all layers and relations. The translational probe shows a narrow exception, reaching high accuracy only for the symmetric husband/wife pair, confirming it cannot capture asymmetric or compositional relations. The linear probe shows weak, relation-isolated signals in some reversal-cursed models but fails to generalize consistently. Critically, only the bilinear structure passes formal algebraic tests (matrix transpose for inversion, matrix product for composition) and predicts successful logical propagation after model editing (R²=0.939), establishing it as the necessary representational geometry for relational reasoning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the RESCAL bilinear knowledge-graph embedding model (Nickel et al., 2011) as a probing tool for Transformer internals, extending it beyond its original domain of static knowledge graphs into the analysis of learned language-model representations. It positions itself against two prior representational accounts of relational knowledge in LMs—linear relational embeddings (Hernandez et al., 2024) and Word2Vec-style translational structure (Merullo et al., 2024)—arguing that neither captures the symmetric and compositional algebra needed for inverse and multi-hop reasoning. Conceptually, it departs from the dominant attribution of the reversal curse to the autoregressive objective (Berglund et al., 2024; Kitouni et al., 2024) by reframing the failure as a representational-geometry problem, and it provides a mechanistic explanation for the editing-generalization failures identified by Hase et al. (2024) and Nishi et al. (2025).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central claim—that the reversal curse and editing-inconsistency are artifacts of unstructured representations rather than inherent Transformer limitations—shifts the research agenda from designing better editing algorithms to ensuring models develop algebraically structured knowledge during training. The R²=0.939 correlation between bilinear probe accuracy and logical editing generalization provides a concrete diagnostic that could guide future structure-aware editing. However, all evidence comes from 206M-parameter models trained from scratch on a clean synthetic family graph, leaving open whether bilinear geometry emerges (or can be induced) in large pretrained LMs with noisy, multi-domain knowledge. The practical takeaway—weight decay as a simple lever for inducing relational structure—is promising but untested at scale.
