## Problem

Current neural approaches to marked temporal point process (MTPP) inference require training a separate, specialized model for each target system, forcing relearning of representations for every distinct dynamical regime. Despite the success of foundation models in NLP and dynamical systems (ODEs, SDEs, MJPs), no foundation model existed for point processes — the very modality underlying the internet activity that enabled text-based pretraining. This paper introduces FIM-PP, the first foundation inference model that can estimate MTPP conditional intensities in-context from a set of event sequences, operating zero-shot or with minimal finetuning.

## Input / Output

FIM-PP takes as input a collection of m marked event sequences (context) and a target event history before time t, where each event is a (timestamp, categorical mark) pair. Time inputs are instance-normalized by the maximum inter-event gap in the context to achieve scale invariance, and the model supports up to 22 marks. The output is a per-mark conditional intensity function parameterized by three nonnegative values (μ, α, β) that define an exponential relaxation from an initial jump α toward a baseline μ at rate β, from which future events are sampled autoregressively via Ogata's thinning algorithm.

## Method

FIM-PP is a 16M-parameter transformer architecture with a hierarchical encoding: each context sequence is independently processed by a 4-layer transformer encoder and pooled into a single embedding via fixed-query attention, then all sequence embeddings are combined by a 2-layer transformer encoder. The target history events serve as queries in a 4-layer transformer decoder that cross-attends to the combined context representation as keys and values, yielding a unified history-context embedding. This embedding, concatenated with a mark encoding, is projected through three MLPs to produce the intensity parameters (μ, α, β) per mark. Pretraining uses 14.4M synthetic events from 72K MTPPs sampled from a broad prior over conditional intensity functions (Hawkes, Poisson, periodic, gamma, and Rayleigh kernels with randomized excitatory/inhibitory/neutral interaction signs), trained with negative log-likelihood. The key design choice is amortized in-context inference: rather than fitting parameters to a single dataset, the network learns to read off the governing intensity dynamics directly from the provided context sequences, enabling zero-shot transfer to unseen real-world event data.

## Results

On the primary N=20 multi-event prediction benchmark (Table 1), finetuned FIM-PP (f) achieves the best OTD and sMAPE Δt across all four evaluated datasets: TAXI (OTD 17.91, sMAPE 76.8), STACKOVERFLOW (OTD 39.80, sMAPE 88.25), AMAZON (OTD 37.2, sMAPE 81.2), and RETWEET (OTD 59.44, sMAPE 87.59). In zero-shot mode, FIM-PP (zs) already matches or surpasses the strongest baselines on sMAPE for TAXI (76.8 vs. CDiff's 88.0) and RETWEET (99.07 vs. TCDDM's 106.05), though it lags on OTD for STACKOVERFLOW and AMAZON. Finetuning requires only a few minutes and ≤11 GB GPU memory, and consistently closes the gap to produce state-of-the-art results on nearly all metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FIM-PP (f) outperforms all seven baselines (HYPRO, Dual-TPP, A-NHP, NHP, IFTPP, TCDDM, CDiff) on both OTD and sMAPE Δt across all four datasets in the main N=20 table, with the largest margins on STACKOVERFLOW (OTD 39.80 vs. CDiff's 41.25; sMAPE 88.25 vs. CDiff's 106.18) and RETWEET (sMAPE 87.59 vs. TCDDM's 106.05). The strongest baseline is CDiff (Zeng et al., 2024), which leads in zero-shot OTD on TAXI (21.01) and AMAZON (37.7), but FIM-PP (f) surpasses it by 3.1 OTD on TAXI and 0.5 OTD on AMAZON. In zero-shot mode, FIM-PP (zs) already beats all baselines on sMAPE for TAXI (76.8 vs. best 88.0) and RETWEET (99.07 vs. best 106.05), demonstrating that the synthetic pretraining prior transfers effectively without any target-data training. On next-event prediction (Table 2), FIM-PP (zs) achieves the best event-time RMSE on TAOBAO (0.13 vs. CDiff's 0.52) but struggles with mark accuracy on TAXI (0.47 vs. 0.91 for baselines) due to alternating mark patterns absent from the synthetic prior; finetuning resolves this gap. Overall, the paper's key claim—that a single pretrained model can match specialized models zero-shot and surpass them after minimal finetuning—is supported across the majority of evaluated tasks and metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Foundation Inference Model (FIM) paradigm—previously applied to Markov Jump Processes (Berghaus et al., 2024) and SDEs (Seifner et al., 2025a)—to marked temporal point processes, the first such application in the point-process literature. It departs from the dominant TPP paradigm (NHP, A-NHP, Transformer Hawkes, CDiff) where each dataset requires training a dedicated model from scratch, instead amortizing inference over a broad synthetic prior of Hawkes-type processes with randomized kernels, base intensities, and interaction signs. The architecture follows the encoder-decoder transformer pattern common to prior FIMs, with history embeddings as queries attending to context-encoded sequences as keys/values, and a flexible exponential-relaxation intensity parametrization that generalizes beyond strict Hawkes dynamics.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FIM-PP demonstrates that a single 16M-parameter model pretrained on synthetic MTPPs can match specialized baselines zero-shot on five real-world datasets and outperform them after minutes of finetuning (<11 GB memory), offering a practical alternative to hours-long per-dataset training. However, the pretraining distribution's inability to capture regular mark-transition patterns (e.g., TAXI's alternating pick-up/drop-off structure) exposes a clear gap: zero-shot mark accuracy drops to 0.47 versus 0.91 for baselines on that dataset. Open questions include broadening the synthetic prior to cover cyclic/transition-matrix dynamics, scaling to more marks and longer sequences, and integrating intensity-free generative methods into the amortized in-context framework.
