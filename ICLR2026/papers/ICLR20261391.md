## Problem

Existing information-seeking agents are heavily dependent on search-engine-indexed knowledge, leaving a critical blind spot for Unindexed Information Seeking (UIS), where vital information resides in dynamic webpages, embedded files, or content excluded from search indices. No prior benchmark explicitly isolates and measures this capability, and even state-of-the-art agents that exceed 70% on GAIA drop to roughly 25% when the target information is unindexed, revealing a fundamental gap in both evaluation and agent design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261391/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent receives a natural-language question whose answer cannot be obtained from search-engine snippets or single-step crawling of indexed pages; it must instead perform multi-step web interactions (clicking, scrolling, selecting filters, reading visual graphs) and parse downloaded files in PDF, XLSX, or DOCX format. The output is a deterministic short-form factual answer (a number, date, proper noun, or logical statement) scored automatically against a golden label. The UIS-QA benchmark comprises 110 expert-annotated QA pairs (84 Chinese, 26 English) drawn from government sites, corporate reports, code repositories, and product pages, with answers chosen to be temporally stable and verifiable by a rule-based LLM judge.

## Method

UIS-Digger is a four-agent system (Planner, Web Searcher, Web Surfer, File Reader) operating under a ReAct-style iterative loop, where the Web Surfer employs a dual-mode browsing strategy that dynamically switches between textual DOM extraction and visual screenshot interpretation while maintaining a shared memory and consistent browser state across modes. The File Reader handles PDF/XLSX/DOCX documents with chunked incremental reading for long files. The backbone LLM (~30B parameters, Pangu-38B or Qwen3-32B) is trained in two stages: an SFT cold-start using teacher-model trajectories filtered by correctness and non-triviality, followed by RFT with temperature-0.4 sampling, difficulty-weighted rejection sampling, and bootstrapping from the SFT model. Training data is synthesized by having UIS-Digger itself explore over 100 real websites and three virtual interactive sites (simulating date pickers, filters, and graphs), then generating QA pairs from the collected contexts, yielding 4,501 SFT trajectories and 4,467 RFT trajectories after filtering.

## Results

UIS-Digger achieves 27.27% accuracy on UIS-QA using a ~30B backbone (Pangu-38B or Qwen3-32B), outperforming the strongest baseline Memento (25.5%, powered by O3+GPT-4.1) and Tongyi-DR (23.6%). On conventional benchmarks, UIS-Digger scores 50.5% on GAIA and 32.5% on BrowseComp-zh, trailing Memento (79.4% GAIA) and Tongyi-DR (70.9% GAIA, 46.7% BC-zh). The central finding is a dramatic performance collapse: even top agents drop from >70% on GAIA to <26% on UIS-QA (e.g., Tongyi-DR drops 47.3 points, Memento drops 53.9 points), confirming that unindexed information seeking remains a critical blind spot for current agent systems.

## Baselines & Comparisons

UIS-Digger (27.3%) outperforms all 13 baselines on UIS-QA, with the closest competitors being Memento (25.5%, O3+GPT-4.1) and Tongyi-DR (23.6%). Among multi-agent frameworks, UIS-Digger beats Memento by 1.8 points and AWorld (5.5%) by 21.8 points, despite using a much smaller backbone (~30B vs. O3/Gemini-2.5-pro). Among ReAct frameworks, it surpasses Tongyi-DR by 3.7 points and WebSailor (7.3%) by 20 points. On GAIA, UIS-Digger (50.5%) trails Memento (79.4%) and Tongyi-DR (70.9%) substantially, indicating that its advantage is specific to UIS rather than general information seeking. On BrowseComp-zh, UIS-Digger (32.5%) also lags behind Tongyi-DR (46.7%) and DDv2 (34.6%), further confirming that the UIS-Digger gains are driven by its specialized dual-mode browsing and file-parsing capabilities rather than broader search proficiency. Notably, even the strongest general-purpose LLMs (GPT-5: 0.9%, Claude-sonnet-4: 2.7%, DeepSeek-V3.1: 1.8%) fail almost entirely on UIS-QA without agentic tool access, highlighting that raw model capability alone cannot substitute for the expanded action space.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground by formalizing Unindexed Information Seeking (UIS) as a distinct problem from the Indexed Information Seeking (IIS) that dominates existing benchmarks like GAIA and BrowseComp. Conceptually, it repurposes the ReAct paradigm and multi-agent orchestration patterns seen in OWL, AWorld, and MiroThinker, but redirects them toward a fundamentally different information-access regime where search-engine snippets are insufficient. The training pipeline (SFT followed by rejection-sampling fine-tuning) follows standard LLM-agent tuning practices, while the dual-mode (textual + visual) browsing strategy extends prior single-mode browser agents by maintaining a shared memory across observation modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper exposes a significant blind spot: agents scoring 70%+ on GAIA collapse to ~25% on UIS-QA, revealing that current evaluation paradigms overstate real-world information-seeking capability. The absolute accuracy of 27.27% (even with a purpose-built system) signals that UIS remains far from solved, and the small 110-sample benchmark, while carefully curated, limits statistical confidence and generalizability claims. Open questions include whether the synthesized training distribution transfers to novel UIS scenarios, how to handle the dynamic nature of the web (where UIS cases can become IIS over time), and whether reinforcement learning beyond sparse final-answer supervision can close the remaining gap.
