## Problem

Extending model-based reinforcement learning to physical multi-robot cooperation is hindered by the combinatorial complexity of joint dynamics: centralized world models must fuse all agents' high-dimensional observations and actions simultaneously, while decentralized models ignore inter-agent coupling. The sequential (autoregressive) paradigm has shown promise for multi-agent decision-making but has not been integrated into world-model-based planning, leaving a gap between scalable modeling and effective cooperative planning.

## Input / Output

Each agent consumes its local proprioceptive observation (e.g., up to 229-dim state and 26-dim action in Bi-DexHands) plus a concatenated message from all predecessors containing their predicted next-step latent states and planned actions. The output is a set of optimized H-step action sequences (via MPPI) and a predicted future latent trajectory that is forwarded to the next agent in the sequence. Notably, the world model operates entirely in latent space with no observation decoder, and the communication channel uses simple vector concatenation rather than learned fusion, keeping the architecture modular and parameter-light.

## Method

SeqWM decomposes joint dynamics into independent, autoregressive agent-wise world models (encoder, dynamics, reward, critic, actor — all MLPs), where agent i predicts its next latent state conditioned on its own observation-action pair and the predictions of all predecessors. Training follows a self-supervised latent prediction objective (analogous to TD-MPC2) with a sequential update scheme: when training agent i+1, its inputs are frozen to the most recent outputs of agents 1–i, preserving the autoregressive dependency. At inference, a sequential MPPI planner lets each agent sample and optimize action sequences conditioned on predecessors' predicted trajectories, with the actor providing a warm-start distribution; low-pass Butterworth filtering on sampled noise and a KL-divergence early-stopping heuristic ensure smooth, real-time execution on physical robots. A communication cache stores the previous agent's predicted message so that transient communication failures do not block downstream planning, yielding O(1) per-step latency regardless of team size.

## Results

On Bi-DexHands (6 tasks), SeqWM achieves higher asymptotic episode returns and faster convergence than all baselines, reaching near-optimal performance within 2–4M environment steps on tasks like Over, CatchOver2Underarm, and Scissors, while baselines require substantially more interactions or fail to match. On Multi-Quad, SeqWM rapidly approaches near-100% success rates on Gate and Shepherd in the early training phase, significantly outperforming baselines in sample efficiency. The method also demonstrates superior stability on harder tasks (Pen, CatchAbreast) with lower variance across seeds. Real-world deployment on two Unitree Go2-W quadrupeds confirms successful sim-to-real transfer on PushBox, Gate, and Shepherd.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SeqWM is compared against four baselines: HASAC (model-free, heterogeneous-agent SAC), MARIE (model-based, Transformer dynamics with centralized aggregation), MAT (model-free, sequential/autoregressive action prediction), and MAPPO (general-purpose PPO-based MARL). SeqWM outperforms all four on every task in both Bi-DexHands and Multi-Quad. Against the model-free baselines (HASAC, MAPPO, MAT), SeqWM's advantage is most pronounced in sample efficiency, converging to near-optimal returns 2–4× faster. Against the model-based baseline MARIE, SeqWM achieves higher final returns while using independent per-agent world models rather than a centralized Transformer, reducing modeling complexity. The ablation in Section 5.5 further confirms that the sequential world model (vs. centralized or decentralized) and explicit multi-step intention sharing are both critical: removing either component degrades performance, with the decentralized world model (DecWM) still outperforming the no-world-model variant (SeqFree), indicating that even implicit dynamics learning helps.

## Relation to Prior Work

SeqWM sits at the intersection of two research threads: model-based RL for robotics (TD-MPC2, World4RL) and the sequential/autoregressive paradigm in multi-agent decision-making (MAT, PMAT, SeqComm). Prior multi-robot MBRL methods (CoDreamer, MARIE) either fuse all agents' state-action pairs in a centralized model or require per-step communication for decentralized aggregation, both of which scale poorly. SeqWM departs from both by assigning each agent an independent latent world model that conditions autoregressively on predecessors' predicted trajectories, structurally decomposing joint dynamics without full observability or parameter sharing. The planning component adapts MPPI to this sequential structure, with the actor serving as a warm-start rather than a direct policy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that sequential decomposition of joint dynamics is a viable alternative to centralized world modeling for multi-robot cooperation, with the added benefit of enabling explicit intention sharing that produces interpretable cooperative behaviors (predictive adaptation, temporal alignment, role division). Real-world deployment on Unitree Go2-W quadrupeds validates sim-to-real transfer of these behaviors, which is rare in multi-robot MBRL. Key limitations include restriction to fully cooperative tasks with shared rewards, a fixed or randomly permuted agent order (no dynamic reordering), and reliance on a communication cache rather than true asynchronous operation. Open questions include whether the sequential structure generalizes to competitive or mixed-motive settings and whether the agent ordering can be learned or adapted online.
