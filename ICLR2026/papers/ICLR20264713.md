## Problem

Existing controllable video generation methods treat the video as a unified 4D spatiotemporal block, applying conditioning signals globally and neglecting fine-grained spatiotemporal structure. This limits the ability to independently control specific factors (e.g., head movement vs. eye blinking) without unwanted coupling. Prior motion/identity separation approaches lack theoretical guarantees of disentanglement, making control unpredictable.

## Input / Output

The model takes a single random noise vector ε ~ N(0,I) that is naively split into a content component ε^c and T per-frame style components ε_1^s, …, ε_T^s. It produces a video of T frames at 256×256 (or 512×512 for CelebV-HQ). The latent space is explicitly decomposed into a 512-dimensional static content code z^c shared across all frames and a low-dimensional (4–12) per-frame style dynamics vector z_t^s, enabling independent manipulation of each dimension for fine-grained control.

## Method

CoVoGAN builds on StyleGAN2-ADA and introduces a Temporal Transition Module (TTM) in the generator that enforces the minimal change principle and sufficient change property. The TTM uses a GRU to encode historical information h_t from past noise terms ε_{t-1}^s, then applies a component-wise Deep Sigmoid Flow to transform independent noise ε_t^s into style dynamics z_t^s that are mutually independent conditioned on h_t, while an MLP maps ε^c to the static content z^c. Theoretically, the paper proves block-wise identifiability (separating motion from content) under mild assumptions (positive density, injective linear operators, weak monotonicity) and component-wise identifiability (separating individual motion concepts) under additional smoothness and sufficient-change conditions. A separate video discriminator enforces observational equivalence of the joint video distribution, and a mutual information maximization loss between z_t^s and video discriminator features encourages structured, informative representations. The key architectural choice of feeding ε_t^s (rather than z_t^s) into the GRU simplifies the flow and aligns with the theoretical generating process, while the GRU's gating mechanism selectively filters irrelevant historical information.

## Results

CoVoGAN achieves the best FVD across all four main datasets: 43.75/48.80 (FVD8/FVD16) on FaceForensics, 35.58/46.51 on SkyTimelapse, 154.88/174.87 on RealEstate, and 97.16 (FVD16) on CelebV-HQ. On disentanglement metrics (FaceForensics), CoVoGAN attains MCC=33.78%, SAP=8.48%, and Modularity=17.37%, outperforming the best GAN baseline MoStGAN-V (27.95%, 5.90%, 13.48%) and all diffusion baselines. The model also generates 16-frame 256×256 videos in 98.93s, roughly 1.4× faster than DIGAN and 458× faster than Latte.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CoVoGAN outperforms all four GAN-based baselines (MoCoGAN-HD, DIGAN, StyleGAN-V, MoStGAN-V) on FVD across every dataset where they overlap, with the largest margins on RealEstate (154.88 vs. 182.86–265.54 FVD8) and CelebV-HQ (97.16 vs. 127.62–412.50 FVD16). Against the strongest diffusion baseline Latte, CoVoGAN wins on FaceForensics (43.75 vs. 45.49 FVD8) but trails on SkyTimelapse FVD16 (46.51 vs. 41.84), while VDM and LVDM are far behind on all shared datasets. On disentanglement metrics (Table 2), CoVoGAN leads all five compared methods: MCC 33.78% vs. 29.00% (StyleGAN-V) and 27.95% (MoStGAN-V); SAP 8.48% vs. 5.90% (MoStGAN-V); Modularity 17.37% vs. 13.48% (MoStGAN-V). In the supervised text-to-video setting (Appendix F.3), integrating the TTM module into Wan-1.3B improves controllability (hf_laion_clip_vit_b32: 0.1980 vs. 0.1791) and subject consistency (0.9529 vs. 0.9324) over the original Wan-1.3B, though it slightly trails CogVideoX-2B on some CLIP-similarity metrics. For trajectory-conditioned generation, TTM+MotionCtrl reduces FVD from 934.37 to 917.28 and CamMC from 0.0840 to 0.0776 on RealEstate10K.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on StyleGAN2-ADA as its architectural backbone and on the nonlinear ICA / temporally causal representation learning literature (Hyvarinen & Morioka, Yao et al.) for its theoretical foundation, but departs from prior video generation work by providing the first identifiability theorem specifically for video generation. Prior methods like MoCoGAN, StyleGAN-V, and MoStGAN-V decompose video into motion and identity heuristically without formal guarantees; CoVoGAN grounds this decomposition in the minimal change principle and sufficient change property, and introduces a Temporal Transition Module (GRU + Deep Sigmoid Flow) that architecturally enforces the conditions needed for component-wise identifiability of individual motion factors.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identifiability theorems provide a principled explanation for why motion/content separation works in prior video GANs and establish conditions under which fine-grained control (e.g., independently adjusting eye blinks vs. head pose) is theoretically guaranteed, which no prior video generation work has offered. The Temporal Transition Module is demonstrated as a plug-in that improves both diffusion-based (Wan) and trajectory-conditioned (MotionCtrl) models, suggesting broader applicability. Key limitations include the GAN-based implementation (lower visual fidelity than state-of-the-art diffusion models), small model scale (~25M params), and the requirement that the number of dynamic latent dimensions n_s be specified a priori. Open questions include whether the identifiability guarantees extend to open-domain, high-resolution video generation and whether the theoretical conditions can be relaxed further.
