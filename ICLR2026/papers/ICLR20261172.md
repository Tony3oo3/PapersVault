## Problem

Standard masked discrete diffusion models collapse all unobserved positions into a single absorbing [MASK] token, creating an 'information void' that erases graded semantic proximity and forces the model to resolve ambiguity without continuous guidance. Pure continuous diffusion models avoid this void but suffer from over-smoothing in the embedding space, making precise token-level predictions difficult. CADD addresses both limitations by pairing the discrete masking trajectory with a continuous latent channel that retains semantic signal at masked positions.

## Input / Output

At training, the model receives a partially masked token sequence (discrete path) paired with per-position continuous embeddings that are Gaussian-noised only at masked positions; the two are fused by element-wise addition before feeding into a standard transformer backbone. At sampling, generation starts from an all-masked sequence and a standard Gaussian prior z_T ~ N(0, I), then iteratively unmasks tokens while updating the continuous latent. For text, sequences are 1024 tokens from a 50,257-token GPT-2 vocabulary; for images, each 32×32×3 pixel is treated as a discrete token with vocabulary size 256; for code, the model takes a prompt and generates completions evaluated on HumanEval, MBPP, and BigCodeBench.

## Method

CADD defines a joint forward process where the discrete path follows standard absorbing masked diffusion while the continuous path applies Gaussian noise to token embeddings only at positions that are currently masked, keeping unmasked embeddings frozen at their clean values. The reverse process factorizes into a discrete unmasking decision (governed by the standard absorbing posterior) and a continuous denoising step (a Gaussian posterior with closed-form mean and variance), with the continuous latent z_t serving as a soft semantic hint that conditions the discrete token prediction. Training uses a single cross-entropy loss identical in form to standard masked diffusion, but the network receives the fused input z̃_t = z_disc + z_t, so the continuous channel is learned implicitly without an explicit MSE term. At inference, the choice between hard (argmax) and soft (probability-weighted) estimation of the clean embedding ẑ_0 provides a controlled trade-off between mode-seeking (precise, context-localized outputs) and mode-covering (diverse, exploratory outputs), and multiple latent draws per step can further increase diversity without off-manifold drift.

## Results

On CIFAR-10, CADD achieves FID 2.88 and IS 10.04 with 512 NFE, outperforming the strongest discrete baseline MDM-Prime (FID 3.26) and continuous baselines like DDPM (FID 3.17) and StyleGAN+ADA (FID 3.26). On ImageNet-32, CADD reaches FID 3.74 with 1024 NFE, beating all discrete and continuous baselines including Continuous FM (FID 5.02). For code generation at 7–8B scale, CADD attains 72.0 on HumanEval, 17.6 on BigCodeBench-Hard, and 55.7 average, surpassing the prior best diffusion model DiffuCoder (67.1 / 12.8 / 52.6) and matching or exceeding leading AR models like OpenCoder (55.0 avg). On OpenWebText text generation, CADD shows consistent MAUVE and perplexity gains over MDLM and SEDD across all sampling steps, with continued improvement at T=4096 where mask-only baselines stagnate or degrade.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against discrete diffusion baselines, CADD consistently wins: on CIFAR-10 it improves FID from 3.26 (MDM-Prime) to 2.88 and from 4.66 (MDM) to 2.88 at the same 512 NFE; on ImageNet-32 it reduces FID from 6.98 (MDM-Prime) to 3.74. Against continuous baselines, CADD outperforms DDPM (FID 3.17→2.88 on CIFAR-10; 16.18→3.74 on ImageNet-32), Continuous FM (6.35→2.88; 5.02→3.74), and even StyleGAN+ADA (3.26→2.88). In code generation, CADD beats the strongest prior diffusion model DiffuCoder by +4.9 on HumanEval (72.0 vs 67.1) and +4.8 on BigCodeBench-Hard (17.6 vs 12.8), while also surpassing the AR model Qwen2.5-Coder on every benchmark and edging out OpenCoder on average (55.7 vs 55.0). For text generation on OpenWebText, CADD improves MAUVE and reduces generative perplexity over both MDLM and SEDD at every sampling step from 128 to 4096, with the gap widening at higher T where mask-only models degrade. The fine-tuned variant (CADD initialized from DiffuCoder) achieves 73.8 on HumanEval, confirming that CADD can be applied as a drop-in fine-tuning objective on existing masked diffusion checkpoints.

## Relation to Prior Work

CADD sits at the intersection of two established lines of discrete generative modeling: masked discrete diffusion (Austin et al., 2021; Sahoo et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2024), which progressively replaces tokens with an absorbing [MASK] state, and continuous diffusion over embedding spaces (Ho et al., 2020; Dieleman et al., 2022; Gulrajani & Hashimoto, 2023), which applies Gaussian noise to token representations. The key departure is that CADD retains the discrete masking trajectory as the primary generative path while pairing it with a conditional Gaussian diffusion in the embedding space that activates only at masked positions, thereby preserving graded semantic information that pure MDMs discard. This is conceptually distinct from prior bridging attempts such as the diffusion duality (Sahoo et al., 2025) or simplex relaxations (Han et al., 2022), which either replace the discrete path entirely or relax tokens into unconstrained Euclidean space; CADD instead keeps the discrete head grounded in the vocabulary and uses the continuous channel purely as an auxiliary semantic hint.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CADD addresses a fundamental information-loss bottleneck in masked diffusion that has limited inference-time scaling: by retaining noisy-but-informative latent vectors at masked positions, the model can continue to improve as sampling steps increase (T=4096) where pure MDMs stagnate or degrade. The practical appeal is that CADD requires no architectural changes beyond an additive input channel and can be applied as a fine-tuning objective to existing MDM checkpoints, lowering the barrier to adoption. Open questions include whether the continuous augmentation provides diminishing returns at very large model scales (the code experiments use 7–8B parameters but the text/image experiments are ~168M), whether the mode-seeking/mode-covering trade-off via hard vs. soft ẑ₀ estimation can be exploited for controllable generation, and whether the approach extends naturally to conditional or multimodal settings beyond the unconditional experiments presented.
