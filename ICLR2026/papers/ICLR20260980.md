## Problem

Existing VPR training pipelines rely on static, one-time hard-sample mining strategies that treat geographic proximity and visual similarity independently, causing the sampled batches to become stale as the model's embedding geometry evolves. Additionally, centroid-free aggregation methods apply uniform weighting to all local descriptors, underemphasizing subtle yet discriminative spatial cues. The paper addresses both gaps by coupling dynamic, epoch-wise geo-visual graph reconstruction with a lightweight feature-amplification module.

## Input / Output

The method consumes RGB street-level or surveillance images (resized to 224×224 for training, 322×322 for inference) along with their GPS coordinates, which are used to compute pairwise geographic distances during graph construction. It produces a compact global descriptor of shape D×K (D=128, K=64, yielding up to 8448 dimensions) via second-order CFP aggregation, which is then used for nearest-neighbor retrieval against a geotagged database. The frozen DINOv2 backbone outputs one class token plus L patch tokens that feed into the SoftP and InteractHead modules.

## Method

SAGE combines three mechanisms: (1) SoftP, a two-layer MLP that maps each patch descriptor's L2 norm to a bounded residual coefficient βᵢ, multiplicatively amplifying high-response patches before CFP aggregation to boost discriminative local variance; (2) InteractHead, which deterministically splits each descriptor into S segments, rearranges them across the batch, and applies a two-layer Transformer encoder to model cross-image correlations per segment type; and (3) an online geo-visual graph rebuilt every epoch that fuses pairwise Euclidean geographic distance with current visual descriptor distance into a multiplicative affinity score Wᵢⱼ = −(d_geo · d_vis), from which a greedy weighted clique expansion algorithm seeds from the highest-affinity anchor node and iteratively adds the most-connected neighbor until reaching a target clique size, producing training batches concentrated on the densest, most confusing spatial-visual neighborhoods.

## Results

SAGE (8448-D, ViT-B) achieves 98.9% R@1 on SPED (100% R@10), 95.8% R@1 on Pitts30k-test, 94.5% R@1 on MSLS-val, and 96.0% R@1 on Nordland, setting new SOTA across all eight benchmarks. On the more challenging AmsterTime dataset (historical vs. modern), SAGE reaches 83.5% R@1, a 17.9-point gain over the previous best single-stage method EMVP (65.6%). Even at a compact 4096-D descriptor, SAGE maintains 97.7% R@1 on SPED with 100% R@10 and 95.6% R@1 on Pitts30k-test, demonstrating strong performance under tight dimensionality budgets.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SAGE outperforms all 20 compared baselines across all eight benchmarks. The closest single-stage competitor, EMVP (NeurIPS'24), is surpassed by +4.3 pts on SPED R@1 (98.9 vs. 94.6), +1.8 on Pitts30k (95.8 vs. 94.0), +0.6 on MSLS-val (94.5 vs. 93.9), and a striking +7.3 on Nordland (96.0 vs. 88.7). Among two-stage re-ranking methods, SAGE beats FoL (AAAI'25) by +6.3 on SPED R@1 and +3.4 on Nordland, and outperforms SelaVPR++ (TPAMI'25) by +6.3 on SPED and +1.2 on Nordland. On the extreme-domain-shift AmsterTime dataset, SAGE (83.5% R@1) exceeds EMVP (65.6%) by 17.9 points and FoL (64.6%) by 18.9 points. On Pitts250k-test, SAGE reaches 98.4% R@1 vs. EMVP's 96.5% and SelaVPR++'s 96.6%. The only metric where a two-stage method edges ahead is Tokyo24/7 R@1, where EffoVPR (98.7%) slightly exceeds SAGE (97.5%), though EffoVPR uses a 504×504 input resolution versus SAGE's 322×322. SAGE also matches or exceeds SALAD-CM (ECCV'25) on all four primary datasets despite using a frozen backbone with only 1.96M trainable parameters (vs. 29.8M for SALAD-CM).

## Relation to Prior Work

SAGE is built directly on the EMVP framework (Qiu et al., 2024), inheriting its frozen DINOv2 backbone, Dynamic Power Normalization for PEFT, and Centroid-Free Probing (CFP) aggregation. The key conceptual departure is from static, offline hard-sample mining strategies (e.g., SALAD-CM's pre-computed cliques, Cliquemining) to a dynamic 'slow thinking' paradigm where the geo-visual affinity graph is reconstructed every epoch to track the model's evolving embedding geometry. SoftP is a lightweight residual-weighting modification of CFP's uniform descriptor treatment, while InteractHead extends cross-image correlation ideas from CricaVPR into a deterministic segment-based Transformer encoder.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SAGE demonstrates that dynamic, epoch-wise graph reconstruction combined with minimal parameter overhead (1.96M trainable params, matching EMVP) can push single-stage VPR to new SOTA across eight diverse benchmarks, including 100% R@10 on SPED at only 4096D. The 17.7% per-epoch training overhead for online graph creation is a practical trade-off that practitioners can adopt without architectural changes. Open questions remain: failure cases concentrate in near-duplicate urban structures where even enhanced descriptors confuse geographically distinct but visually similar places, suggesting that purely global-descriptor approaches may hit a ceiling without explicit structural reasoning or local re-ranking. Whether the dynamic mining strategy transfers to non-street-level or indoor VPR settings is untested.
