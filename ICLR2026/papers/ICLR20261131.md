## Problem

In multi-model inference systems where task-specialized LLMs collaborate (e.g., agentic AI workflows), each model maintains its own KV cache even for identical prompts, causing memory to grow linearly with the number of models. This triggers cache eviction, redundant prefill recomputation, and prevents cross-model prefix caching, severely degrading latency and throughput. No prior architecture enables full cross-model KV cache sharing across all layers while preserving task-specific accuracy.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt and a task-specific LoRA adapter (rank 128, α=256) as input. During prefill, the frozen base model (logical encoder) generates the shared KV cache; during decode, the current token is duplicated to both the encoder (to append new KV entries) and the adapter-tuned decoder (to predict the next token). The output is the generated token sequence, with the critical property that the KV cache is bit-identical across all task-specialized models, enabling direct sharing without any approximation or recomputation.

## Method

ICaRus decomposes a decoder-only Transformer into a logical encoder (which generates KV representations) and a logical decoder (which predicts the next token from the KV cache), then freezes the encoder and fine-tunes only the decoder via LoRA. Because all task-specific models share the same frozen encoder, they produce identical KV caches for identical prompts, enabling full cross-model KV sharing and eliminating the O(N) memory multiplier. During decoding, the current token is duplicated to both encoder and decoder, and since they share all base model parameters except lightweight adapters, their computations are parallelized with queries concatenated along the head dimension for joint attention, reducing decode-phase memory traffic to near single-model levels. This reduces total memory from O(M+NL_t) to O(M+L_t) and prefill complexity from O(N(ML_t+L_t²)) to O(ML_t+L_t²), while achieving accuracy comparable to or exceeding task-specific fine-tuning across math, coding, and knowledge tasks.

## Results

On LLaMA-3.1-8B, ICaRus achieves 67.9% on GSM8K and 48.2% on HumanEval, closely matching the conventional multi-model system (69.7% and 48.2%), while surpassing it on HumanEval+ (43.9% vs 41.5%) and GPQA (28.8% vs 27.3%). On Qwen3-8B-Base, ICaRus outperforms the multi-model baseline on all math and coding benchmarks (e.g., 87.3% vs 85.4% on GSM8K, 86.6% vs 81.7% on HumanEval). In multi-agent serving with 8 models, ICaRus delivers up to 11.1× lower P95 latency and 3.8× higher throughput compared to the conventional multi-model system.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares ICaRus against two baselines: (1) the base model without fine-tuning, and (2) a conventional multi-model system where each task-specific model is independently fine-tuned (with LoRA) and maintains its own KV cache. On LLaMA-3.1-8B, ICaRus is within 1.8–2.7 points of the multi-model baseline on GSM8K/GSM+ but matches or exceeds it on HumanEval+ (+2.4) and GPQA (+1.5). On Qwen3-8B-Base, ICaRus consistently outperforms the multi-model baseline by 1.4–4.9 points across math and coding tasks (e.g., +1.9 on GSM8K, +4.9 on HumanEval), while being marginally lower on GPQA (−0.5). In serving performance, ICaRus achieves 3.8×, 5.1×, and 11.1× lower P95 latency and 1.4×, 2.3×, and 3.8× higher throughput than the conventional multi-model system with 2, 4, and 8 agents respectively, due to eliminating redundant KV cache construction and enabling cross-model prefix caching.

## Relation to Prior Work

ICaRus builds on the KV cache optimization literature (pruning, quantization, inter-layer sharing) and prefix caching in LLM serving systems (PagedAttention, SGLang), but departs fundamentally from prior multi-model cache-sharing work. DroidSpeak shares only non-sensitive layer caches between a base model and its fine-tuned variants, requiring identification of sensitive layers; ICaRus instead architecturally guarantees full-layer sharing by decomposing a decoder-only Transformer into a frozen logical encoder (KV generation) and an adapter-tuned logical decoder (token prediction). This reframing repurposes the standard attention mechanism—where queries are ephemeral but KV pairs persist—as a principled basis for cross-model cache identity, rather than treating sharing as a post-hoc optimization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As agentic AI systems increasingly orchestrate multiple task-specialized models over shared context, the N-fold KV cache duplication identified here becomes a first-order bottleneck; ICaRus's 11× P95 latency reduction and 3.8× throughput gain are directly actionable for production deployments on existing vLLM infrastructure. A key limitation is that all specialized models must share the same base architecture, leaving open whether the logical encoder/decoder decomposition generalizes to heterogeneous model families or to settings where task-specific KV representations are genuinely necessary. The finding that freezing the encoder can match or slightly exceed full fine-tuning accuracy (attributed to implicit regularization) also raises questions about the role of encoder adaptation in task specialization that warrant further study.
