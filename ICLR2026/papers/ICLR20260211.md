## Problem

Existing multi-concept personalization methods either handle only object concepts and fail to disentangle abstract concepts (pose, lighting, surface) from the input image, or they support abstract concepts but require per-concept test-time fine-tuning (e.g., TokenVerse), which is slow and overfits on single training images. No tuning-free framework existed that could simultaneously customize both object and abstract concepts without any test-time optimization.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260211/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes one or more concept images (each depicting a specific concept such as an object, a person's pose, a lighting condition, a surface material, an image style, or a color tone) together with a text prompt in which specific words are designated as concept tokens. At inference, multiple concept images can be combined with a single prompt to produce a personalized image where each designated concept is faithfully rendered in a new context. Training is performed exclusively on single-concept pairs, yet the localized modulation mechanism composes multiple concept directions at test time without additional training.

## Method

The core mechanism operates in the AdaLN modulation space of the FLUX DiT backbone (57 blocks). A lightweight Mod-Adapter (1.67B parameters, the only trainable component) predicts a distinct modulation direction Δᵢ for each DiT block by first extracting concept-specific visual features via vision-language cross-attention (CLIP text encoder encodes the concept word into queries; CLIP image encoder encodes the concept image into keys/values), then projecting those features into the modulation space through a 12-expert Mixture-of-Experts layer with a parameter-free k-means routing scheme that assigns concept words to experts based on clustering of their neutral CLIP features. To bridge the large gap between the concept image space and the DiT modulation space, a VLM-guided pre-training stage uses Qwen2.5-VL to generate attribute-descriptive captions of each concept image, which are encoded by CLIP and used as MSE supervision targets for the Mod-Adapter's output before the main diffusion-objective fine-tuning begins. At inference, each concept's predicted direction is added to the modulation vector of its corresponding text token, enabling disentangled, localized control over multiple concepts simultaneously.

## Results

On the DreamBench-Abs benchmark (50 concepts including 20 abstract), Mod-Adapter achieves a comprehensive CP·PF score of 0.62 in multi-concept personalization, a +67.6% improvement over the second-best method MIP-Adapter (0.37). In single-concept personalization, it reaches CP·PF of 0.54, again the highest among all compared methods. The method attains the best Prompt Fidelity (PF) score of 0.89 in both settings, substantially outperforming all baselines (next best: 0.66 by TokenVerse in single-concept), while also achieving the highest Concept Preservation (CP) of 0.70 in multi-concept. User study with 32 participants (4000 votes) confirms these gains, with Mod-Adapter scoring 4.29/4.40 (CP/PF) in multi-concept versus the next-best TokenVerse at 3.35/3.48.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Mod-Adapter decisively outperforms all four baselines on the comprehensive CP·PF metric in both multi-concept (0.62 vs. 0.37 MIP-Adapter, 0.32 MS-Diffusion, 0.31 TokenVerse, 0.25 Emu2) and single-concept (0.54 vs. 0.42 Emu2, 0.38 TokenVerse, 0.27 MIP-Adapter, 0.23 MS-Diffusion) settings. Its dominant advantage is Prompt Fidelity: 0.89 in both settings versus at most 0.66 (TokenVerse, single-concept) and 0.56 (TokenVerse, multi-concept) for all others, indicating far better alignment between generated images and text prompts. In multi-concept CP, Mod-Adapter (0.70) edges out MIP-Adapter (0.68) and MS-Diffusion (0.62), while in single-concept CP it trails Emu2 (0.73) and MIP-Adapter (0.70), suggesting those methods sacrifice prompt fidelity to preserve concept appearance. The tuning-based TokenVerse, despite per-image fine-tuning, underperforms Mod-Adapter on all metrics in multi-concept (CP·PF 0.31 vs. 0.62), confirming the paper's claim that test-time fine-tuning overfits on limited training images. CLIP-T scores are tightly clustered around 0.30–0.33 across all methods, which the authors note makes this metric insensitive to prompt-alignment differences.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends TokenVerse (Garibi et al., 2025), which first demonstrated that modulating individual text tokens differently in DiT models enables localized multi-concept control, but required per-image MLP fine-tuning at test time. Mod-Adapter replaces that per-concept fine-tuning with a single tunable module using vision-language cross-attention (leveraging CLIP's image-text alignment) and a k-means-routed Mixture-of-Experts to project concept features into the modulation space. It also departs from tuning-free multi-concept methods (MIP-Adapter, MS-Diffusion, Emu2) that concatenate or additively fuse concept features with text features, which the authors argue causes concept confusion and inability to decouple abstract attributes from objects. A VLM-guided pre-training stage (using Qwen2.5-VL to generate attribute captions as supervision) addresses the large representational gap between image space and DiT modulation space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper establishes that tuning-free multi-concept personalization can handle abstract concepts (pose, lighting, surface, style, color tone) without the overfitting and time cost of per-concept fine-tuning, broadening the practical applicability of personalized generation beyond object identity. The introduction of DreamBench-Abs provides the community with a standardized evaluation for abstract-concept personalization, filling a gap in existing benchmarks. Key open questions remain: performance degrades beyond 3 simultaneous concepts, the method fails when the same concept word appears twice in a prompt, and reliance on CLIP's image encoder limits low-level detail preservation. The authors sketch potential remedies (multi-concept training data, VAE latent conditioning, LoRA on joint attention) but leave them as future work.
