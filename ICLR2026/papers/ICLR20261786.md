## Problem

Existing 3D part segmentation methods only identify visible surface patches, producing broken, incomplete parts that are insufficient for content-creation tasks like geometry editing, animation, and material assignment. While 2D amodal segmentation has been studied for years, extending this concept to 3D introduces non-trivial challenges: inferring occluded geometry, maintaining global shape consistency, and generalizing across diverse shapes with limited part-specific training data.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261786/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a whole 3D mesh (as a sampled point cloud of ~20,480 points) together with a surface segmentation mask identifying one part region (512 subsampled query points from the part surface). It also accepts 2.5D masks back-projected from 2D renderings. The output is a complete 3D part mesh extracted via local marching cubes from a learned occupancy field, with the local bounding box set to 1.3× the segmented part's bounding box to ensure full geometry recovery including occluded regions.

## Method

HoloPart is a diffusion-based part completion model built on a VAE (3DShape2VecSet/CLAY design) that encodes point clouds into latent tokens, combined with a DiT-based denoising network trained via rectified-flow matching. The key architectural innovation is a dual attention mechanism: a context-aware attention block performs cross-attention between subsampled part points and the whole-shape point cloud (with a binary mask highlighting the segmented region) to inject global shape context, while a local attention block performs cross-attention between subsampled and full part points to capture fine-grained geometry and position mapping. The model is pretrained on large-scale whole-shape data (Objaverse) to establish a strong 3D generative prior, then fine-tuned on curated part-whole pairs using classifier-free guidance. This two-stage strategy (object-level pretraining → part-level fine-tuning) overcomes data scarcity by transferring shape priors from abundant whole-object data to the part completion task.

## Results

On the ABO benchmark, HoloPart achieves a mean Chamfer Distance of 0.026, IoU of 0.764, and F1-Score of 0.843 (instance-level), substantially outperforming the strongest baseline Finetune-VAE (CD 0.037, IoU 0.565, F1 0.689). On the more diverse PartObjaverse-Tiny benchmark, HoloPart reaches Chamfer Distance 0.034, IoU 0.688, and F1-Score 0.801 overall, with a 100% reconstruction success rate. The method also generalizes zero-shot to generated and scanned objects (OmniObject3D) and to 2.5D mask inputs on 3DCoMPaT++, where it achieves IoU 0.558 and F1 0.641 versus the best baseline SDFusion (IoU 0.401, F1 0.485).

## Baselines & Comparisons

HoloPart decisively outperforms all three external shape-completion baselines across every metric and dataset. PatchComplete (Rao et al., NeurIPS 2022) and DiffComplete (Chu et al., NeurIPS 2024) are the weakest, achieving IoU of only 0.137 and 0.142 respectively on PartObjaverse-Tiny, and frequently fail to reconstruct thin or small structures. SDFusion (Cheng et al., CVPR 2023) improves over these two (IoU 0.235) but remains far behind HoloPart's 0.688. The closest competitor is the Finetune-VAE baseline (the paper's own VAE fine-tuned on part data), which reaches IoU 0.502 and F1 0.638 on PartObjaverse-Tiny; HoloPart improves IoU by ~37% relative and F1 by ~25% relative over this baseline. On ABO, the gap is even wider: HoloPart's IoU of 0.764 versus Finetune-VAE's 0.565. The ablation rows confirm that both the local attention and context-aware attention modules contribute meaningfully, with removing context-aware attention causing the largest single drop (IoU 0.688 → 0.553).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 2D amodal segmentation concept (Ehsani et al., 2018; Kar et al., 2015) to the 3D domain, where it had no prior formulation. Technically, it builds on the VAE latent-space design from 3DShape2VecSet and CLAY for shape encoding, and on DiT-based rectified-flow diffusion for generation, but repurposes these whole-object tools for part-level completion via a novel dual-attention mechanism (local + context-aware). The first stage leverages SAMPart3D for surface segmentation, positioning HoloPart as a complementary completion module rather than a replacement for segmentation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By formalizing 3D part amodal segmentation and providing benchmarks on ABO and PartObjaverse-Tiny, this paper opens a practical pathway from 'one-piece' generated or scanned meshes to editable, animatable part decompositions — a bottleneck for 3D content creation pipelines. Key limitations include dependence on input mask quality and the limited training data (16k parts from Objaverse), leaving open whether end-to-end part-aware generation can replace the two-stage pipeline. The zero-shot generalization to generated and scanned objects suggests the learned 3D shape prior is broadly useful, but scaling to open-vocabulary part categories remains unaddressed.
