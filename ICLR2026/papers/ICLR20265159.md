## Problem

Existing Data-Free Robustness Distillation (DFRD) methods transfer overall robustness from teacher to student without training data, but they overlook robust fairness: the distilled student exhibits severe disparities in adversarial robustness across categories. The paper identifies two root causes—(1) uniform class-proportion sampling amplifies inter-class robustness gaps during distillation, and (2) adversarial examples with biased target-class distributions make the student disproportionately vulnerable to attacks aimed at specific categories.

## Input / Output

The method requires only a pre-trained robust teacher (e.g., WideResNet-34-10) and an untrained student (ResNet-18, MobileNet-V2, or PreActResNet-18); no original training data is accessed. A generator synthesizes 32×32 (CIFAR-10/100) or 64×64 (Tiny-ImageNet) RGB images from Gaussian noise conditioned on a class label sampled via a reweighted distribution. The output is a student classifier that maps RGB images to class labels while achieving balanced worst-class robustness and low normalized standard deviation (NSD) across all categories under FGSM, PGD-20, CW∞, and AutoAttack.

## Method

FERD addresses both proportion and distribution imbalances in synthetic adversarial samples. For proportion, it computes per-class adversarial margins under the teacher via PGD-20, averages the negative margins per class, and applies softmax to obtain a sampling distribution that oversamples weakly robust categories. For distribution, it first generates Fairness-Aware Examples (FAEs) by distilling non-robust feature channels from the teacher's penultimate residual block using a variational information-bottleneck objective, then enforcing a KL-to-uniform constraint on the non-robust feature predictions so that benign samples carry no class-specific attack bias. From FAEs, it constructs Uniform-Target Adversarial Examples (UTAEs) by augmenting the standard PGD update with a KL-to-uniform term on the teacher's output distribution, spreading attack targets evenly across all classes. Robust distillation then uses a decoupled KL divergence loss (Cui et al., 2024) with FAEs as clean inputs and UTAEs as adversarial inputs, forcing the student to inherit balanced robustness from all attack directions.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with MobileNet-V2, FERD achieves 78.34% clean accuracy and 33.40% worst-class robustness under FGSM, 20.80% under PGD-20, 20.50% under CW∞, and 20.30% under AutoAttack, with NSD of 0.229, 0.304, 0.314, and 0.349 respectively. With ResNet-18, FERD reaches 79.26% clean accuracy and 31.30% worst-class FGSM robustness (NSD 0.237). The headline improvement is up to 11.3% worst-class robustness gain over the best baseline (DERD) under FGSM on MobileNet-V2, and NSD reductions of up to 0.077 compared to the optimal baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FERD outperforms all seven baselines on worst-class robustness under every attack on both CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. The strongest baseline is DFHL for ResNet-18 (e.g., 18.50% AA worst-class) and DERD for MobileNet-V2 (e.g., 12.00% AA worst-class); FERD surpasses them by 2.3% and 8.3% respectively on AA worst-class. On CIFAR-100, FERD leads by 0.3–0.6% in worst-10% robustness over the best baseline (DERD for MN-V2, CMI/DERD for RN-18) under FGSM and PGD. ZSKT and CMI are the weakest baselines, with worst-class robustness below 20% on CIFAR-10 and below 30% on CIFAR-100, confirming that early DFKD methods struggle with robustness transfer. FERD also achieves the lowest NSD in most settings (e.g., 0.237 vs. 0.298 for DFHL under FGSM on RN-18), demonstrating superior robustness fairness. On Tiny-ImageNet, FERD achieves the best average robustness and NSD across all attacks, with 11.24% AA average robustness vs. 10.84% for DFARD.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: Data-Free Robustness Distillation (DFRD) methods like DFARD, DERD, and DFHL, and robust fairness techniques from adversarial training such as FRL, BAT, and FairARD. It departs from prior DFRD by identifying that uniform synthetic data sampling and biased adversarial target distributions are two distinct mechanisms causing class-wise robustness disparity in distilled students. The method borrows the information bottleneck framework from Kim et al. (2021) for non-robust feature identification and the decoupled KL divergence loss from Cui et al. (2024) for distillation, repurposing both within a fairness-aware data-free generation pipeline.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first work to formalize and address robust fairness specifically in the data-free distillation setting, which is practically important for edge deployment where training data is unavailable. The two identified failure modes (class-proportion imbalance and target-class bias) are actionable and the proposed reweighting + uniform-target generation framework provides a clear recipe. Limitations include evaluation only on CIFAR-10/100 and Tiny-ImageNet with relatively small student models, and the information bottleneck layer selection is architecture-specific. Open questions include whether the approach scales to ImageNet-scale datasets, whether the fairness gains persist under stronger or adaptive attacks, and whether the generator's reliance on teacher intermediate features limits applicability when only black-box teacher access is available.
