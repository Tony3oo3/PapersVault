## Problem

Existing CTDE methods in cooperative MARL underutilize centralized training by relying only on value functions, while the newer CTDS (Centralized Teacher with Decentralized Student) paradigm suffers from a fundamental policy-space asymmetry: the centralized teacher can exploit joint coordination strategies (e.g., stochastic coupling between agents) that are unrealizable by factorized decentralized policies, causing severe performance degradation. Additionally, most CTDE and CTDS methods lack theoretical guarantees of monotonic policy improvement.

## Input / Output

During training, the centralized guider receives the global state and conditions sequentially on previously selected agent actions (autoregressive joint policy), while the decentralized learner receives only each agent's local observation and action-observation history. At deployment, only the factorized learner policies are used, with each agent acting independently on its local view. The guider's autoregressive structure (μ(a|s) = μ^{i₁}(a^{i₁}|s)·μ^{i₂}(a^{i₂}|s,a^{i₁})·…) contrasts with the learner's product structure (π(a|s) = ∏ⱼ π^{iⱼ}(a^{iⱼ}|s)), and the hyperparameter δ explicitly bounds the ratio between guider and learner action probabilities to (1/δ, δ).

## Method

MAGPO alternates four steps: (1) roll out the autoregressive guider for coordinated data collection, (2) update the guider via PPO-style PMD with a novel double-clipping mechanism and mask function that penalize divergence from the learner beyond ratio δ, (3) update the learner by minimizing KL divergence to the guider plus an optional RL auxiliary loss, and (4) backtrack the guider to the updated learner policy. The double clip stops gradient flow when the advantage signal would push the guider beyond the δ-bound, while the mask activates the KL penalty only upon constraint violation. Theoretically, the learner update is shown to be equivalent to sequential advantage-weighted policy updates (recovering monotonic improvement via the performance difference lemma), yet all agents can be updated in parallel with shared parameters—unlike HARL methods that require sequential, heterogeneous updates. Empirically, MAGPO with Sable as the guider backbone outperforms all CTDE baselines on 32/43 tasks and matches or exceeds CTCE methods on 20/43 tasks across six environments.

## Results

Across 43 tasks in 6 environments, MAGPO surpasses all CTDE baselines (MAPPO, HAPPO) on 32/43 tasks and outperforms all baselines including CTCE methods on 20/43 tasks. On the CoordSum benchmark (introduced in this paper), MAGPO achieves 157.61 on 5x20-80 vs. 19.62 for CTDS and 48.35 for Sable, demonstrating the critical importance of constraining the guider to remain decentralizable. On Smax 10m_vs_11m, MAGPO reaches 0.98 win rate vs. 0.80 (CTDS), 0.14 (HAPPO), 0.39 (MAPPO), 0.88 (MAT), and 0.83 (Sable). MAGPO is competitive with the SOTA CTCE method Sable in three of six environments while maintaining decentralized execution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MAGPO most dramatically outperforms CTDS (Zhao et al., 2024), the direct teacher-student baseline: on CoordSum 5x20-80, MAGPO scores 157.61 vs. CTDS's 19.62, and on RWARE large-8ag, 10.40 vs. 7.68, confirming that unconstrained CTCE teachers learn non-decentralizable coordination strategies. Against MAPPO (Yu et al., 2022), MAGPO wins on the majority of tasks, with particularly large margins on CoordSum (e.g., 157.61 vs. 142.51 on 5x20-80) and Smax 10m_vs_11m (0.98 vs. 0.39). HAPPO (Kuba et al., 2022) is consistently outperformed, with MAGPO achieving 157.61 vs. 119.19 on CoordSum 5x20-80 and 8.49 vs. 3.75 on RWARE medium-4ag-hard. Against the CTCE baselines MAT (Wen et al., 2022) and Sable (Mahjoub et al., 2025), MAGPO is competitive but not uniformly superior: Sable wins on MPE spread_10ag (-36.93 vs. -40.51) and RWARE large-8ag (11.01 vs. 10.40), while MAT wins on RWARE large-8ag (14.72) and Smax 5m_vs_6m (0.68 vs. 0.34). Overall, MAGPO is the strongest CTDE method across all six environments and matches or exceeds CTCE on 20/43 tasks, validating that constrained centralized guidance can close the gap to fully centralized execution.

## Relation to Prior Work

MAGPO extends the single-agent Guided Policy Optimization (GPO) framework (Li et al., 2025) to the multi-agent setting by introducing an autoregressive centralized guider policy that is explicitly constrained to remain aligned with decentralized learner policies. It departs from the CTDS paradigm (Zhao et al., 2024) by addressing the policy-space asymmetry problem—where a centralized teacher's joint stochastic strategies cannot be factored into independent decentralized policies—through a double-clipping mechanism and KL-based backtracking. The theoretical analysis builds on the Policy Mirror Descent (PMD) framework (Shani et al., 2019), and the practical implementation is compatible with any autoregressive CTCE backbone (Sable, MAT), positioning MAGPO as a bridge between CTCE and CTDE rather than a replacement for either.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MAGPO provides the first provable monotonic improvement guarantee for a CTDE-style method that uses a centralized joint policy for exploration, filling a theoretical gap that affects both vanilla CTDE and CTDS approaches. The CoordSum benchmark concretely demonstrates a fundamental failure mode of CTDS (undecentralizable coordination patterns), giving the community a diagnostic tool for evaluating teacher-student MARL methods. Open questions include whether privileged global state (beyond the union of partial observations) can further enhance the guider, how the δ parameter scales with agent count, and whether the framework extends to competitive or mixed cooperative-competitive settings. The practical implication is that advances in CTCE sequence models (e.g., Sable, MAT) can now directly benefit decentralized deployment through MAGPO, facilitating co-development of both paradigms.
