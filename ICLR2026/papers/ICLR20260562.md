## Problem

Existing personalized federated learning (PFL) methods assume homogeneous data and model architectures across clients, which fails to reflect real-world deployments where clients tackle distinct tasks (data heterogeneity) and use different model families and scales (model heterogeneity). Prior work addresses at most one form of heterogeneity, or both under simplified setups such as varying LoRA ranks on an identical base architecture or non-i.i.d. splits of a single dataset, leaving the joint challenge of cross-task, cross-architecture knowledge sharing unaddressed.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260562/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each client receives a continuous stream of multi-modal samples (image + text question) drawn from a distinct task (e.g., VQA, visual reasoning, visual relation) with temporal distribution shifts, and produces a text response via a frozen MLLM backbone (LLaVA with Llama-3.2-1B/3B or Qwen-2.5-1.5B/3B) augmented by a trainable LoRA adapter. The DRAKE benchmark spans 40 tasks from 19 datasets (375k images, 274k questions) with 7 additional unseen tasks for generalization evaluation, and clients may use different model families and sizes simultaneously.

## Method

FedMosaic combines two components to handle both heterogeneities. RELA (RELevance-guided Aggregation) computes sanitized EMA gradients from a small frozen model to measure pairwise task relevance via cosine similarity, then builds a customized global adapter for each client through softmax-weighted aggregation that up-weights task-relevant peers, reducing parameter interference from unrelated tasks. Co-LoRA inserts dimension-invariant modules P ∈ R^{r×r} and Q ∈ R^r between LoRA's A and B matrices, so their sizes depend only on the low-rank dimension r and are directly shareable across architectures with different hidden dimensions and depths. To resolve depth and weight misalignment, Co-LoRA uses block-wise aggregation (aligning layers at matching relative depths identified via CKA) and one-time weight alignment (L2 loss for A matrices, canonical correlation analysis for B matrices), after which A and B are frozen and only P and Q are updated locally, yielding zero aggregation error (Theorem 2) while preserving r²-dimensional expressiveness (Theorem 1).

## Results

On DRAKE-Dynamic (heterogeneous multi-modal PFL with 10 clients using LLaVA-Llama3.2-1B/3B), FedMosaic achieves 67.86% Self A_last and 51.16% Others A_last, outperforming the best baselines (FedTIA at 66.11% Self, FedDAT at 48.91% Others) by +1.75 and +2.25 points respectively. On HFLB-Dynamic, FedMosaic reaches 80.80% Self and 67.07% Others A_last, surpassing SFT (79.99% Self) and FedDAT (64.15% Others). In the large-scale 52-client LLM experiment on Fed-LLM-Large, FedMosaic achieves 20.87% Self and 15.71% Others A_last, beating the strongest baseline PerAda (18.83% Self) by +2.04 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FedMosaic consistently outperforms all six baselines on both Self and Others metrics across DRAKE-Dynamic, HFLB-Dynamic, and the 52-client Fed-LLM-Large benchmark. On DRAKE-Dynamic Self, FedMosaic (67.86%) beats the closest competitor FedTIA (66.11%) by +1.75 points, while on Others it surpasses FedDAT (48.91%) by +2.25 points. DITTO and PerAda suffer the largest degradation under data heterogeneity, dropping 5.9 and 6.0 points below SFT on DRAKE Self, respectively, indicating that naive dual-adapter averaging is particularly harmful when clients tackle unrelated tasks. FedDAT achieves the best Others performance among baselines on DRAKE (48.91%) but still trails FedMosaic by 2.25 points, and it underperforms SFT on Self (58.47% vs. 65.79%), suggesting its aggregation strategy introduces interference. On HFLB-Dynamic Others, FedMosaic (67.07%) leads FedDAT (64.15%) by +2.92 points, a notably larger gap than on DRAKE, reflecting the benefit of relevance-guided aggregation when task diversity is high. In the large-scale 52-client LLM experiment, FedMosaic (20.87% Self) outperforms PerAda (18.83%) by +2.04 points and FedMKT (17.34%) by +3.53 points, confirming scalability to large client populations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) by inserting dimension-invariant modules P and Q between the A and B matrices, creating Co-LoRA that is shareable across architectures with different hidden dimensions and depths. It departs from prior heterogeneous LoRA methods (HETLORA, FLEXLORA) that assume identical hidden dimensions and uniform depths, and from PFL methods (DITTO, FedSim, FedDAT, PerAda, FedMKT) that build global adapters via uniform averaging, by introducing RELA—a gradient-similarity-guided aggregation that assigns per-client weights based on task relevance. The DRAKE benchmark also departs from prior FL benchmarks that simulate heterogeneity via non-i.i.d. splits of a single dataset, instead assigning each client a distinct multi-modal task with temporal distribution shifts.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FedMosaic is the first method to jointly address both data and model heterogeneity in multi-modal PFL under realistic conditions, and DRAKE provides the first multi-modal FL benchmark with task heterogeneity and distribution shifts—both filling gaps that prior work acknowledged but left unresolved. The finding that both smaller and larger models benefit substantially from cross-architecture knowledge sharing (e.g., +6.86 for a 3B client) challenges the intuition that larger models need less collaborative help. Open questions include whether the CKA-based block alignment generalizes beyond the Llama/Qwen families tested, whether the one-time alignment step scales to dozens of distinct architectures, and whether the public-data requirement for A/B alignment is practical in privacy-sensitive deployments.
