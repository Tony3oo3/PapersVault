## Problem

Existing LLM uncertainty methods are answer-level, measuring the reliability of a specific generated response after it has been produced, which incurs the full cost of token generation. The paper asks whether a model's knowledge boundary can be detected *before* any answer tokens are generated, enabling adaptive inference (RAG invocation, model cascading, abstention) without paying the generation cost. Prior approaches to pre-generation knowledge assessment either require fine-tuning a probe or teaching the model to output 'I don't know,' limiting generalizability across models and tasks.

## Input / Output

The method takes a text query (prompt + question tokens) and performs a single forward pass through the LLM, extracting hidden states at every layer and every token position. No answer tokens are generated. The output is a single scalar Internal Confidence score in [0,1], which can be thresholded to decide whether to invoke RAG, defer to a larger model, or abstain. The prompt template constrains the model to a yes/no self-assessment format, and the score is computed from the unembedding-layer probability of the token 'YES' at each (layer, token) position, aggregated via attenuated weights.

## Method

Internal Confidence (IC) is a training-free, generation-free method that computes P(YES) — the softmax probability of the token 'YES' under a yes/no self-assessment prompt — at every (layer, token) position in the N×L grid of hidden states, rather than only at the final layer and final token. These N×L confidence values are then aggregated via a weighted sum centered on a fixed 'decision center' (the top-right cell: last layer, last token), using Attenuated Encoding weights that apply Gaussian decay (controlled by a locality parameter α) to neighboring layers and tokens. This ensemble-like aggregation is motivated by prior work showing that logical consistency across layers improves outputs, and the decay weights allow fine-grained control over how much influence adjacent units have. The entire procedure requires only one forward pass (no autoregressive decoding), making it 30×–600× faster than answer-level baselines while achieving competitive or superior AUROC for distinguishing answerable from non-answerable queries across factual QA and mathematical reasoning tasks.

## Results

On the three main datasets (TriviaQA, SciQ, GSM8K), Internal Confidence achieves average AUROC of 59.8, 64.2, and 67.1 for Phi-3.8B, Llama-8B, and Qwen-14B respectively, with corresponding PRR values of 20.8, 28.7, and 31.7. The advantage over the strongest baseline (P(YES) naive avg) grows with model size: +2.4 AUROC on Phi-3.8B, +2.5 on Llama-8B, and +3.6 on Qwen-14B. In the speed comparison (Table 2, Qwen-14B on GSM8K), Internal Confidence achieves AUROC 66.8 in 0.3 s per sample, yielding 30×–600× speedups over answer-level methods such as SAR (180.6 s) and Semantic Entropy (151.8 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Internal Confidence consistently outperforms all logit-based baselines (Max(-logp), Predictive Entropy, Min-K Entropy, Attentional Entropy, Perplexity) by 5–15 AUROC points on average across models, with the gap widening for larger models. Internal Semantic Similarity performs worst, often below chance (negative PRR), confirming that raw hidden-state similarity is a poor uncertainty signal. Against the two P(YES) variants, the full Internal Confidence with attenuated-encoding weights beats P(YES) (naive avg) by 2.4–3.6 AUROC on average, and beats P(YES) (top right) by 3.7–6.2 AUROC, demonstrating that the weighted multi-layer/multi-token aggregation is critical. In the speed comparison (Table 2), Internal Confidence (0.3 s) is 84× faster than MSP (25.1 s, the fastest answer-level method with AUROC 68.5) and 602× faster than SAR (180.6 s), while matching or exceeding the AUROC of most answer-level baselines (66.8 vs. 65.7 for SAR, 65.5 for Perplexity, 65.2 for P(TRUE)). The only answer-level method that slightly exceeds Internal Confidence in AUROC is MSP (68.5 vs. 66.8), but at 84× the runtime.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the P(TRUE) self-evaluation idea from Kadavath et al. (2022) — where an LLM is prompted to judge whether it knows an answer — by computing the P(YES) signal at every layer and token position rather than only at the final hidden state, then aggregating via attenuated encoding weights (Chen et al., 2023). It deliberately departs from prior knowledge-boundary detection work (Gottesman & Geva, 2024; Kossen et al., 2024; Amayuelas et al., 2024) that requires training probes or fine-tuning, offering instead a fully training-free, generation-free alternative. The conceptual shift from answer-level uncertainty U(x,y) to query-level uncertainty U(x) — estimating capability before any token is produced — is the paper's core reframing of an existing problem.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 30×–600× speedup over answer-level uncertainty methods makes pre-generation confidence estimation practically viable for adaptive inference pipelines (RAG invocation, model cascading, test-time scaling), directly addressing the rising cost of agentic LLM workflows. However, the method still lags behind the best answer-level baselines in AUROC (e.g., 66.8 vs. 68.5 for MSP on GSM8K), and its reliance on internal hidden states excludes black-box API models. Open questions include whether the fixed top-right decision center can be replaced by a truly training-free adaptive mechanism, and whether the approach generalizes to open-ended or subjective queries beyond factual QA and math.
