## Problem

Egocentric 3D hand reconstruction is severely challenged by depth ambiguity, self-occlusion, and complex hand-object interactions that cause state-of-the-art methods to miss hands, confuse left/right identities, or distort occluded regions. Prior egocentric-specific approaches rely on auxiliary supervision cues or scaled training data but cannot adapt to unseen contexts. No in-context learning framework has been applied to 3D hand reconstruction, leaving a gap in flexible, example-guided reasoning for these ambiguous egocentric scenarios.

## Input / Output

The method takes an egocentric RGB query image and, at inference, retrieves one template image via VLM-guided strategies. Both images are processed to produce coarse MANO parameters (via a pretrained backbone like WiLoR) and ground-truth MANO parameters (from the database for templates), which together form the ICL context. The output is refined MANO parameters (15×3 pose, 10-dim shape, 3-dim global orientation per hand) that are decoded into 3D hand meshes. Unusually, both the ICL 'input' and 'target' are MANO parameter vectors rather than images, unifying the 2D-to-3D modality gap within a single parametric space.

## Method

EgoHandICL is the first ICL framework for 3D hand reconstruction, built on three components. First, a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) performs complementary template retrieval: pre-defined visual templates classify images into four hand-involvement types (left, right, two-hand, non-hand), while adaptive textual templates use reasoning-style prompts to generate semantic descriptions for finer-grained matching. Second, an ICL tokenizer encodes image tokens (pretrained ViT), structural tokens (MLP-based MANO encoder), and text tokens (Qwen-7B text encoder) for both template and query, then fuses them via cross-attention into four structured token sets (template input/target, query input/target). Third, a lightweight Transformer encoder is trained in an MAE-style regime where target tokens are partially masked (70% ratio) during training and fully masked at inference, forcing the model to reconstruct query MANO parameters from contextual exemplars. Training combines MANO parameter-level L2 loss, vertex-level L1 loss, and a novel hand-specific 3D perceptual loss using Uni3D-ti embeddings of mesh point clouds to enforce semantic consistency under occlusion.

## Results

On ARCTIC, EgoHandICL achieves P-MPVPE of 3.8 mm (general) and 3.7 mm (bimanual), outperforming the second-best WildHand (5.6/4.9 mm) by 31.1% and 24.5% respectively, while also reducing MRRPE to 6.2 mm (vs. 7.1 mm for WildHand). On EgoExo4D, EgoHandICL reaches P-MPJPE of 7.7 mm (general) and 7.5 mm (bimanual), improving over PCIE-EgoHandPose (8.5/8.2 mm) and WiLoR (12.5/11.0 mm), with F@10 jumping to 0.789 (vs. 0.544 for PCIE-EgoHandPose). The method also improves hand-object interaction reasoning in EgoVLMs, raising average scores by 0.03–0.05 across LLaVA-OneVision, EgoGPT, and Qwen2.5-VL-7B on EgoHOIBench.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EgoHandICL outperforms all four ARCTIC baselines across every metric: it reduces P-MPVPE from 5.5 mm (WiLoR) and 5.6 mm (WildHand) to 3.8 mm, and improves F@5 from 0.524 (WiLoR) to 0.801. On EgoExo4D, it beats PCIE-EgoHandPose (P-MPJPE 8.5→7.7 mm), Potter (11.1→7.7 mm), HaMeR (11.2→7.7 mm), and WiLoR (12.5→7.7 mm), with the largest relative gain over WiLoR (38% reduction in P-MPJPE). In the bimanual setting on EgoExo4D, EgoHandICL's MRRPE of 110.9 mm substantially outperforms HaMeR (361.2 mm) and WiLoR (378.0 mm), indicating far better left-right spatial consistency. For the EgoVLM hand-object interaction task, EgoHandICL's reconstruction outputs serve as visual prompts that consistently improve all three EgoVLMs: LLaVA-OneVision (0.75→0.78 avg), EgoGPT (0.71→0.76 avg), and Qwen2.5-VL-7B (0.82→0.85 avg), with the largest absolute gain on EgoGPT's 'none' category (0.76→0.90). The paper does not report any comparison where EgoHandICL underperforms a baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the in-context learning paradigm from NLP (Brown et al., 2020) and vision (PIC, HiC, TrajICL) to 3D hand reconstruction, a setting where prior ICL work has not addressed the 2D-to-3D modality gap. It builds on the MAE architecture (He et al., 2022) by adapting its masking strategy to the ICL training-inference asymmetry, and on MANO (Romero et al., 2017) as a unified parameter space that makes input-target pairs semantically aligned across modalities. Unlike prior egocentric hand reconstruction methods (WildHand, HaWoR) that rely on auxiliary supervision cues (event cameras, world-space tracking), EgoHandICL uses VLM-guided exemplar retrieval to provide contextual reasoning without additional sensor modalities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first ICL framework for 3D hand reconstruction, opening a paradigm where the model reasons from retrieved exemplars rather than relying solely on parametric regression — a direction that could generalize to other 3D reconstruction tasks. The 31% P-MPVPE improvement over the second-best method on ARCTIC and consistent gains across backbones (HaMeR, WildHand, WiLoR) suggest the ICL formulation itself, not a specific backbone, drives the gains. Key limitations include the computational cost of VLM-based retrieval (limiting real-time deployment), dependence on template database diversity, and the scarcity of fully annotated egocentric datasets. Open questions include temporal extensions for continuous tracking, hand-object joint reconstruction, and whether retrieval-free strategies can embed contextual exemplars directly into the learning pipeline.
