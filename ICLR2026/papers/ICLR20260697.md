## Problem

Existing Video LLMs compress visual tokens using uniform 2D pooling or bilinear interpolation, which treats all spatial and temporal tokens equally and overlooks the intricate spatiotemporal dynamics inherent in video content. Prior training-free token enhancement methods (e.g., SF-LLaVA, TS-LLaVA) were designed for Image LLMs and do not transfer effectively to the rapidly evolving Video LLM landscape. There is a lack of parameter-free, plug-and-play visual token enhancement strategies specifically tailored for Video LLMs.

## Input / Output

The method operates on frame tokens of shape H×W×d extracted by a vision tower (CLIP-based) from uniformly sampled video frames (32 frames for LLaVA-OneVision, 64 for LLaVA-Video), paired with a text question. It outputs spatially pooled tokens at half the original spatial resolution (2×2 kernel, stride 2) with the same total token count as the original model, which are then fed to the LLM to generate a free-form text response. The method is strictly training-free and requires no architectural modification—only the downsampling function Down(·) is replaced.

## Method

ST-GridPool integrates two training-free components. Pyramid Temporal Gridding (PTG) partitions the temporal sequence into L hierarchical levels with exponentially growing segment lengths (K, 2K, 4K, …); at each level, frames within a segment are uniformly sampled, spatially concatenated into an mH×nW grid, bilinearly interpolated back to H×W, and written as a summary token onto the segment's last frame, thereby injecting multi-scale temporal context without extra parameters. Norm-based Spatial Pooling (NSP) exploits the empirically validated positive correlation between L2 token norm and semantic saliency (demonstrated on HKU-IS): within each 2×2 pooling window, a softmax over β·‖t‖₂ assigns adaptive weights so that high-norm (salient object) tokens dominate the weighted sum while low-norm background tokens are suppressed. The two components are applied sequentially—PTG first enriches temporal representations, then NSP performs saliency-aware spatial compression—yielding a plug-and-drop enhancement that improves video understanding while reducing inference time and peak GPU memory.

## Results

ST-GridPool applied to LLaVA-Video-7B achieves 64.2% on VideoMME, 60.1% on LongVideoBench, 83.8% on NexT-QA, 66.1% on TempCompass, and 59.8% on MVBench, consistently outperforming the baseline by 0.5–1.9 points across all six benchmarks. When applied to LLaVA-OneVision-7B, the method reaches 62.1% on EgoSchema (+2.0) and 59.0% on VideoMME (+0.8). Under a strict 30% token budget, ST-GridPool achieves 62.0/58.1/56.0 on VideoMME/LongVideoBench/EgoSchema, surpassing all token-reduction baselines (FastV, PruMerge, FasterVLM, VisionZip, FrameFusion) and approaching the full-token upper bound of 63.3/58.2/57.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ST-GridPool (LLaVA-Video-7B + Ours) achieves 64.2% on VideoMME, matching NVILA-8B (64.2%) and surpassing Apollo-7B (61.3%) and Oryx-1.5-7B (58.8%); on LongVideoBench it reaches 60.1%, clearly ahead of NVILA-8B (57.7%) and Apollo-7B (58.5%). On EgoSchema, LLaVA-OneVision-7B + Ours attains 62.1%, beating InternVideo2-7B (60.0%) and the original LLaVA-OneVision (60.1%). On NexT-QA, the method scores 83.8%, exceeding NVILA-8B (82.2%) and Oryx-1.5-7B (81.8%), though it trails IXC-2.5-7B (69.1% on MVBench only, not NexT-QA). On TempCompass, LLaVA-Video + Ours reaches 66.1%, narrowing the gap to NVILA-8B (69.7%) but still trailing it by 3.6 points. Among training-free alternatives, ST-GridPool outperforms TS-LLaVA (63.8/59.6/57.1) and SF-LLaVA (63.4/58.9/58.9) on VideoMME/LongVideoBench/MVBench, while IG-VLM actually degrades performance below the baseline (60.6/55.9/54.2). Under the 30% token budget, ST-GridPool (62.0/58.1/56.0) outperforms FrameFusion (61.3/56.0/53.0), FasterVLM (60.1/55.8/52.6), PruMerge (59.9/54.7/50.9), FastV (59.3/53.5/51.3), and VisionZip (58.3/53.2/53.0) across all three long-video benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the visual token construction pipeline of the LLaVA family of Video LLMs, which traditionally rely on uniform 2D pooling or bilinear interpolation to compress frame tokens. It departs from prior training-free video adaptation methods (SF-LLaVA, TS-LLaVA, IG-VLM) that were designed for Image LLMs and shown to transfer poorly to modern Video LLMs, instead proposing a method specifically tailored to the spatiotemporal structure of video token sequences. The Pyramid Temporal Gridding component draws on multi-scale temporal modeling ideas, while the Norm-based Spatial Pooling is motivated by a saliency-detection observation (validated on HKU-IS) that L2 token norms correlate with semantic importance, repurposing this signal for adaptive spatial downsampling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

ST-GridPool offers a genuinely plug-and-play, training-free enhancement that can be applied to existing Video LLMs without architectural changes or retraining, which is practically valuable given the cost of fine-tuning large multimodal models. However, the absolute gains are modest (0.2–2.0% across benchmarks), and the method's generality beyond the LLaVA family is not thoroughly tested. Open questions include whether the norm-saliency correlation holds across diverse video domains (e.g., medical, satellite), whether the hierarchical gridding depth scales to very long videos (hundreds of frames), and whether the approach can be combined with learnable token pruning for even greater compression.
