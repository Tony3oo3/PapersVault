## Problem

Prior work on f-DPO (Wang et al., 2024) restricted the generating function f to be convex, leaving open whether non-convex f-divergences could also yield tractable preference optimization. Additionally, DPO and related methods suffer from likelihood displacement—both winner and loser probabilities collapse toward zero during training—without a principled theoretical criterion for which f-divergences resist this failure mode.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264363/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a preference dataset of (prompt, chosen, rejected) triplets and a frozen reference LM, and produces an aligned policy via single-stage gradient descent. Unlike standard DPO where the regularization coefficient β is a fixed hyperparameter, SQUAREDPO computes an adaptive per-response β_θ(y,x) = β / (π_θ(y|x)/π_ref(y|x)) at each training step, so the effective regularization strength changes dynamically as the model's probability ratios shift. The loss is computed in log-probability space with a numerical clipping threshold (50) on the log-ratio to prevent overflow in the adaptive β computation.

## Method

The paper first characterizes the full class of 'DPO-inducing' functions—those f for which substituting the optimal RLHF solution into the Bradley-Terry model yields a tractable f-DPO loss—showing the necessary and sufficient condition is lim_{t→0+} f'(t) = −∞, which admits non-convex f. It then introduces a 'displacement-resistant' condition (argmin_{t>0} f(t) ≥ 1) that provably prevents in-sample probability collapse under a restricted optimization problem. SQUAREDPO instantiates f-DPO with the non-convex generating function f(t) = (log t)²/2, which satisfies both properties. The resulting loss replaces DPO's fixed β with adaptive per-response coefficients β_θ(y,x) = β·π_ref(y|x)/π_θ(y|x), so that when a winner's probability decreases, its effective regularization automatically strengthens, counteracting the downward drift. This yields a single-term loss with no additional hyperparameters beyond β, trained via standard LoRA fine-tuning.

## Results

On standard benchmarks (AlpacaEval 2 and MT-Bench), SQUAREDPO achieves 29.2% length-controlled win rate and 7.924 MT-Bench score, nearly matching DPO (29.6% LC, 7.925) and slightly outperforming χPO (29.3% LC, 7.900). The key differentiator is robustness to over-optimization: on the TL;DR validation set, SQUAREDPO maintains a 51.0% win rate against the reference model after 4 epochs, while DPO drops to 34.7% and χPO to 48.3%. SQUAREDPO also substantially mitigates likelihood displacement: only 4.21% of winners that decreased in probability in epoch 1 continued decreasing monotonically through epoch 4, compared to 99.63% under DPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DPO, SQUAREDPO is slightly behind on standard benchmarks (AlpacaEval 2 LC: 29.2 vs 29.6; MT-Bench: 7.924 vs 7.925), but the gap is within noise and the authors note hyperparameters were not tuned for SQUAREDPO. The decisive advantage is over-optimization robustness: at 4 epochs, SQUAREDPO retains 51.0% win rate against the reference model while DPO collapses to 34.7%, and SQUAREDPO's head-to-head win rate over DPO becomes statistically significant after 2 epochs. Against χPO, SQUAREDPO is essentially tied on AlpacaEval (29.2 vs 29.3 LC) and slightly ahead on MT-Bench (7.924 vs 7.900), while showing stronger over-optimization resistance (51.0% vs 48.3% at epoch 4). On likelihood displacement, SQUAREDPO dramatically outperforms both baselines: the monotonic-decrease rate drops from 99.63% (DPO) and 35.57% (DPO on Qwen+UltraFeedback) to 4.21% and 1.64% respectively under SQUAREDPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the f-DPO framework of Wang et al. (2024), which generalized DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) to f-divergences under a convexity assumption on the generating function f. The key departure is showing that convexity is not required: the authors identify a broader 'DPO-inducing' condition (lim_{t→0⁺} f'(t) = -∞) that fully characterizes tractable f-divergences, and introduce a 'displacement-resistant' property (argmin f ≥ 1) as a necessary condition to prevent the known likelihood displacement pathology. SQUAREDPO, derived from the non-convex f(t) = (log t)²/2, can be interpreted as DPO with adaptive per-response β coefficients, connecting it conceptually to SimPO, β-DPO, and ε-DPO, but with the adaptive coefficients derived from first principles rather than introduced heuristically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-property design principle (DPO-inducing + displacement-resistant) provides a theoretically grounded recipe for constructing future preference-optimization losses, moving the field beyond ad-hoc penalty terms. However, the displacement-resistance condition is proved necessary but not sufficient, and the empirical validation is limited to a single model (Llama-3-8B) and dataset (TL;DR) for the main experiments, with LoRA-only fine-tuning. The monotonicity phenomenon (99.63% of winners that decrease in epoch 1 continue decreasing monotonically under DPO) is a new empirical observation, but whether SQUAREDPO's advantages persist under full fine-tuning, larger models, or different datasets remains open. Identifying a sufficient condition for displacement resistance and exploring other f-functions satisfying both desiderata are natural next steps.
