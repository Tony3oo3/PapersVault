## Problem

Existing multimodal reasoning benchmarks either require domain-specific expertise (scientific, mathematical) or rely on synthetic symbolic puzzles, neither of which reflects the authentic reasoning demands of everyday situations. Moreover, most benchmarks focus on single-image inputs, whereas real-world perception involves sequences of images spanning diverse reasoning types. MMR-Life addresses both gaps by introducing the first comprehensive benchmark for multimodal multi-image reasoning across seven reasoning types using natural real-life images.

## Input / Output

Each question presents a set of multiple natural real-life images (averaging 7.22 per question, drawn from contexts such as domestic life, crowd surveillance, daily dining, and sports) alongside a text question with five answer options. Options are split between text (54.95%) and image (45.05%) formats, and the model must select the single most appropriate answer. The benchmark explicitly excludes symbolic diagrams, charts, and abstract renderings, requiring only commonsense reasoning without domain-specific expertise.

## Method

The benchmark construction pipeline collects images from four sources (Kaggle datasets, web resources, video frame extraction, and existing benchmarks), designs 21 tasks across 7 reasoning types (abductive, analogical, causal, deductive, inductive, spatial, temporal), and generates question-answer pairs through a mix of automatic heuristic synthesis and manual annotation. Negative options are produced heuristically for image choices and via LLM generation (GPT-5-mini, GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL-32B) for text choices, followed by three-step quality filtering (difficulty, format consistency, and semantic quality). The evaluation framework benchmarks 37 MLLMs (thinking and non-thinking, open and closed source) under zero-shot CoT prompting, then conducts a systematic analysis of thinking patterns: the logarithmic scaling of accuracy with reasoning length across reasoning types, a comparison of four reasoning-enhancement methods (CoT, Self-Consistency, Best-of-N, GRPO) across model scales from 7B to 72B, and Pearson correlation with hierarchical clustering to uncover higher-order pattern clusters among reasoning types.

## Results

On MMR-Life (2,646 multi-image multiple-choice questions across 7 reasoning types), GPT-5 achieves the highest average accuracy of 58.69%, followed by Gemini-2.5-Pro at 56.86%, both falling ~14% below human performance (72.28%). Performance is highly uneven across reasoning types: models excel at analogical reasoning (GPT-5: 78.87%) but struggle severely with spatial reasoning (best: 25.10% vs. 79.76% human) and temporal reasoning (best: 41.70% vs. 79.76% human). Notably, open-source thinking models average only 27.15% accuracy—worse than open-source no-thinking models at 29.01%—and Skywork-R1V-38B (14.13%) performs below random guessing (20%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

As a benchmark paper, MMR-Life evaluates 37 MLLMs rather than proposing a new method, so the key comparisons are between models and across reasoning types. GPT-5 (58.69%) leads all models but trails human performance (72.28%) by 14 points; Gemini-2.5-Pro (56.86%) and Gemini-2.5-Flash (53.10%) follow closely. Among closed-source no-thinking models, GPT-4.1 (48.15%) outperforms Claude-3.7-Sonnet (45.09%) and GPT-4o (44.75%), while Doubao-1.5-vision (39.98%) lags. In the open-source tier, Qwen2.5-VL-72B (40.21%) and VL-Rethinker-72B (39.68%) are the strongest, but all open-source thinking models (avg 27.15%) underperform their no-thinking counterparts (avg 29.01%), with Skywork-R1V-38B (14.13%) falling below the 20% random-guessing baseline. The most striking disparity is in spatial reasoning, where even the best model (Gemini-2.5-Pro, 25.10%) achieves less than one-third of human accuracy (79.76%), while in analogical reasoning GPT-5 (78.87%) actually exceeds human performance (57.65%). GRPO-trained models (e.g., VL-Rethinker-7B at 28.23%) consistently underperform Best-of-N inference on the same base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B BoN@8 at 29.54%), suggesting RL generalization issues in small MLLMs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from two dominant lines of multimodal reasoning benchmarks: knowledge-intensive evaluations (GPQA, MMMU, OlympiadBench) that conflate reasoning with domain expertise, and synthetic/symbolic benchmarks (VisualPuzzles, MME-Reasoning, PuzzleVQA) that decouple reasoning from knowledge but use artificial inputs. MMR-Life carves new ground by requiring multi-image inputs from natural real-life scenes, covering seven distinct reasoning types simultaneously, and demanding no specialized domain knowledge—conditions that prior benchmarks address only in isolation. For its analysis section, the paper builds on the reasoning-enhancement literature (CoT, Self-Consistency, Best-of-N, GRPO) to study how these methods generalize to real-life multimodal settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even GPT-5 (58.69%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (56.86%) trail human performance (72.28%) by 14%, with spatial reasoning showing a catastrophic gap (best model: 25.10% vs. 79.76% human). Two negative results challenge prevailing assumptions: open-source thinking models average 27.15% versus 29.01% for their non-thinking counterparts, and RL-trained (GRPO) models consistently underperform Best-of-N inference on small models across architectures. These findings suggest that current reasoning-enhancement strategies may be overfitting to specific training distributions rather than building generalizable world models, and that spatial and temporal reasoning remain fundamentally unsolved for MLLMs. Open questions include whether the spatial/temporal gap reflects a representational limitation or a data-coverage issue, and whether RL's underperformance on small models is an artifact of training data scale.
