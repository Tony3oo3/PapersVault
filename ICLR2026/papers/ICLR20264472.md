## Problem

Fine-tuning large language models faces a fundamental trade-off: LoRA achieves memory efficiency but restricts updates to a low-rank subspace, yielding suboptimal performance, while MeZO achieves inference-level memory via forward-pass-only updates but suffers from high-variance gradient estimates and slow convergence. No existing method simultaneously delivers full-rank parameter updates and strict memory constraints comparable to inference.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained model's weight matrix W ∈ R^{m×n}, a batch of training examples with task-specific prompt templates, and hyperparameters (rank r, scaling factor α, perturbation scale ε, learning rates η_BA and η_W). It produces full-rank fine-tuned weights where the base matrix W is updated via zeroth-order perturbation and the low-rank adapter matrices A ∈ R^{r×n} and B ∈ R^{m×r} are updated via exact gradients. Each iteration requires exactly three forward passes and one backward pass, with the backward pass computing gradients only for the small low-rank modules, keeping peak memory at or below that of LoRA or MeZO individually.

## Method

LMAO performs an alternating optimization where each iteration has two phases: (1) a LoRA phase that updates low-rank matrices A and B via standard gradient descent (one forward + one backward pass), and (2) a MeZO phase that updates the base weights W via Simultaneous Perturbation Stochastic Approximation using two perturbed forward passes with a random-seed trick to generate perturbations on-the-fly without storing them. The key insight is that the combined per-iteration update is full-rank—W receives full-rank adjustments from the ZO step while A and B receive exact gradient updates—thereby mitigating the feature loss of pure low-rank methods without incurring the memory cost of full backpropagation. The paper provides convergence guarantees under Lipschitz smoothness and expected smoothness assumptions, showing O(1/T) convergence rate for the minimum gradient norm, and demonstrates that peak GPU memory never exceeds that of either LoRA or MeZO alone.

## Results

On OPT-1.3B (1000 training examples), LMAO achieves the best accuracy on 8 out of 9 SuperGLUE tasks, including 92.4% on SST-2, 69.0% on CB, 62.0% on BoolQ, and 76.7% on COPA. On RoBERTa-large (350M) with k=16 samples per class, LMAO reaches 92.8% on SST-2, 77.9% on SNLI, and 87.0% on TREC, surpassing full fine-tuning (FT) on 4 of 6 tasks and remaining within 0.5% on the rest. Peak GPU memory for LMAO on OPT-1.3B never exceeds that of LoRA or MeZO individually (e.g., 12.62 GB on SST-2, matching LoRA exactly), confirming no additional memory overhead from the alternating scheme.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against MeZO (Malladi et al., 2023), LMAO achieves substantial gains across all OPT-1.3B tasks, e.g., 92.4% vs. 58.1% on SST-2 (+34.3 pp) and 69.0% vs. 46.4% on CB (+22.6 pp), confirming that the low-rank gradient step compensates for MeZO's high-variance zeroth-order estimates. Against LoRA (Hu et al., 2022), LMAO wins on 8 of 9 tasks (e.g., 69.0% vs. 67.9% on CB, 76.7% vs. 74.7% on COPA) but trails by 1.7 pp on RTE (59.4% vs. 61.1%), the only task where the low-rank-only update retains an edge. Against Prefix-Tuning (Li & Liang, 2021), LMAO outperforms both FT(prefix) and MeZO(prefix) on all six RoBERTa-large tasks at k=16 (e.g., 92.8% vs. 91.9% on SST-2, 87.0% vs. 85.7% on TREC). In the extended PEFT comparison (Table 10), LMAO surpasses LOZO (Chen et al., 2024) on SST-2 (92.4% vs. 86.0%) and COPA (76.7% vs. 69.0%), and outperforms SubZero (Yu et al., 2024) on all four tasks (e.g., 92.4% vs. 63.6% on SST-2, 89.0% vs. 43.0% on CB), demonstrating that the alternating full-rank strategy yields consistently stronger adaptation than either pure zeroth-order or pure low-rank PEFT variants.

## Relation to Prior Work

LMAO sits at the intersection of two well-studied fine-tuning paradigms: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA, Hu et al. 2022), which restricts updates to a low-rank subspace for parameter efficiency, and Memory-efficient Zeroth-Order optimization (MeZO, Malladi et al. 2023), which estimates gradients via SPSA forward passes to eliminate backpropagation memory costs. The key departure is the *alternating* schedule: one gradient-based LoRA step (1 forward + 1 backward) followed by one zeroth-order MeZO step (2 forward passes) per iteration, yielding full-rank weight updates at 3F+1B cost. The theoretical analysis builds on the finite-series convergence framework for LoRA (Malinovsky et al., 2024) and standard SPSA unbiasedness/bias lemmas, extended to the alternating setting via tangential scaling inequalities (Lu et al., 2019).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

LMAO offers a practically attractive recipe for memory-constrained fine-tuning: full-rank updates at a memory footprint no larger than LoRA or MeZO alone, which is valuable when GPU memory is the binding constraint. However, the authors acknowledge a significant open limitation—training inefficiency on large datasets or long sequences—where the extra forward passes compound wall-clock cost, and they suggest component training as a future direction. The performance gains over individual baselines (LoRA, MeZO) are consistent but modest (typically 1–3 points), and the method has not been validated on models beyond ~6.7B parameters or on generative/long-context tasks, leaving its scalability profile uncertain.
