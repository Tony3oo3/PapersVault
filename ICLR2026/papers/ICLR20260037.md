## Problem

Existing 3DGS-based multi-modal alignment methods like UniGS suffer from two key limitations: (1) entangled 3D encoding, where all Gaussian primitive attributes (position, opacity, color, scale, rotation) are concatenated and encoded as a homogeneous feature vector, failing to exploit the distinct distributional patterns and geometric significance of each attribute; and (2) degraded 3D perception, where forced alignment from a single randomly selected view fails to capture global spatial context, causing suboptimal cross-view consistency in the learned 3D features.

## Input / Output

The method takes a 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (downsampled to 1024 Gaussians via Farthest Point Sampling and grouped into local patches via kNN), a text prompt, and a single RGB image as input. The single image is expanded into 6 canonical-pose views via a pretrained diffusion model (Hunyuan3D-v1 MVD-std) before encoding. All modalities are projected into a shared 512-dimensional embedding space, producing aligned 3D features that support zero-shot classification, text-3D and image-3D retrieval rankings, few-shot linear probing, and open-world scene recognition.

## Method

TIGAUSSIAN introduces a multi-branch 3DGS tokenizer that disentangles Gaussian attributes into separate encoding branches—spatial (PointNet-inspired MLP with positional embeddings and max pooling), appearance (sigmoid-activated MLPs for opacity and color), and morphological (normalized MLPs for scale and rotation)—each operating under an attribute-specific information bottleneck before fusion via a 2-layer MLP and cross-attention guidance from a pretrained Uni3D point cloud model. For image-3D alignment, a diffusion-enhanced multi-view fusion module generates N views from a single image, encodes them through CLIP, and fuses them via perspective-aware cross-attention with sinusoidal positional encoding of camera angles, granting the 3D features multi-perspective perception without requiring multiple training triplets. For text-3D alignment, a 6-layer query transformer with learnable soft-prompt queries iteratively refines 3D features through self-attention, cross-attention to the 3D token, and MLP layers, projecting the 3D latent space into the text embedding structure. Training uses a dual InfoNCE contrastive loss (λ_T = λ_I = 0.5) between the 3D-image and 3D-text feature pairs, leveraging the pre-aligned text-image space from Open-CLIP ViT-B-16.

## Results

On zero-shot classification, TIGAUSSIAN achieves 41.76% Top-1 accuracy on Objaverse-LVIS and 61.70% on ABO, outperforming the strongest 3DGS-based baseline UniGS (37.64% and 52.33%) by 4.1 and 9.4 points respectively. On image-3D retrieval, the method reaches 54.11% Top-1 on Objaverse and 66.15% on ABO, surpassing UniGS by 12.3 and 39.5 points. For text-3D retrieval, TIGAUSSIAN attains 21.20% Top-1 on Objaverse and 15.87% on ABO, and on open-world scene recognition (SUN RGBD) it achieves 76.46% mean accuracy versus 68.92% for UniGS.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TIGAUSSIAN consistently outperforms all five baselines across every evaluated task. Against the closest 3DGS-based competitor UniGS, the gains are most dramatic on image-3D retrieval (66.15% vs. 26.69% Top-1 on ABO, a 39.5-point margin) and substantial on zero-shot classification (61.70% vs. 52.33% on ABO). Against the multi-view approach Duoduo CLIP, TIGAUSSIAN wins on both classification datasets (41.76% vs. 38.05% on Objaverse-LVIS; 61.70% vs. 57.82% on ABO) while retaining an explicit 3D representation. The point-cloud-based methods Uni3D and CLIP² are surpassed by wide margins on all tasks (e.g., 54.11% vs. 39.65% image-3D retrieval Top-1 on Objaverse for Uni3D). The only near-tie is text-3D retrieval on Objaverse, where TIGAUSSIAN edges out UniGS by just 0.2 points (21.20% vs. 21.00% Top-1), though it still leads on Top-5 (45.10% vs. 39.80%) and Top-10 (56.30% vs. 53.50%). ULIP-2 is only compared on Objaverse-LVIS classification, where TIGAUSSIAN leads by 12.0 points (41.76% vs. 29.75%).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends the 3DGS-based multi-modal alignment line initiated by UniGS (Li et al., 2025), which itself distilled from Uni3D's point cloud encoder. The key departure is a multi-branch tokenizer that disentangles Gaussian attributes (position, opacity, color, scale, rotation) into separate encoding paths rather than concatenating them as homogeneous features, drawing on information-bottleneck principles. For alignment, it departs from the single-view image-3D contrastive paradigm used by CLIP², ULIP-2, Uni3D, and UniGS by introducing a diffusion-enhanced multi-view fusion module (leveraging Hunyuan3D) and a query-transformer 3D-to-text projector, positioning itself as a bidirectional alignment strategy rather than a unidirectional one.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As 3DGS becomes the dominant explicit 3D representation for real-time rendering and scene understanding, establishing effective cross-modal alignment with it is practically important for downstream tasks like retrieval, zero-shot classification, and scene recognition. The disentangled attribute encoding offers a reusable design principle for other structured 3D representations. However, the method's reliance on a pretrained diffusion model for multi-view generation introduces significant computational overhead, and the authors acknowledge limitations in generalizing to occluded multi-object scenes and outdoor environments. The dependence on LLM-generated text labels for training also raises open questions about fine-grained text-3D alignment quality.
