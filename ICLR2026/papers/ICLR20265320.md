## Problem

RL-based post-training (e.g., DeepSeek-R1) improves LLM reasoning but struggles on inherently difficult tasks where the base model has near-zero success, because sparse correctness-only rewards provide almost no learning signal. Prior curriculum learning efforts for LLMs rely on rigid, fixed-switching strategies that either cause catastrophic forgetting of easier tasks or overfitting to trivial ones. This paper asks how to probabilistically schedule task difficulty from easy to hard so that small LLMs can build reasoning skills gradually and generalize to harder, out-of-distribution problems.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained LLM (Qwen 2.5/1.5B Instruct or LLaMA 3.2 3B Instruct) and a reasoning task prompt (e.g., a Blocksworld block configuration, a Countdown number set, or a MATH word problem). Training data is partitioned into four difficulty tiers (trivial, easy, medium, hard) using either human annotations (plan length, operand count, problem level) or model error-rate quartiles under 1-shot CoT prompting. The model outputs a reasoning trace enclosed in <think> tags followed by a final answer in <answer> tags; the sparse reward is assigned only when the final answer is correct, with zero reward for all intermediate tokens.

## Method

E2H Reasoner formulates LLM reasoning as a discounted MDP over token sequences and applies GRPO as the policy optimizer, but introduces a probabilistic difficulty scheduler that controls which task tier is sampled at each training step. Two schedulers are proposed: a parameter-free cosine schedule (E2H-C) that linearly interpolates sampling probabilities from easy-dominant to hard-dominant, and a Gaussian schedule (E2H-G) with two hyperparameters (σ controlling sampling concentration and β controlling the speed of the difficulty shift) that rapidly decays trivial-task probability to prevent overfitting while still providing early dense-reward signal. Theoretically, the paper analyzes the curriculum within an Approximate Policy Iteration framework, proving a convergence bound on the final performance gap and a finite-sample complexity result showing that, under geometric error and difficulty allocation across K curriculum stages, CRL requires fewer total samples than direct learning on the hardest task. Empirically, E2H-G achieves state-of-the-art results across Blocksworld, Countdown, MATH, GSM8K, and AQuA, and is complementary to DAPO's batch-level filtering, yielding the strongest combined performance.

## Results

On Qwen 1.5B Instruct, E2H-G achieves 32.9% on Blocksworld Hard (vs. 21.1% for GRPO-balanced, 5.8% for CL, 13.8% for Self-Evolve) and 7.3% on OOD (vs. 2.6%, 0.7%, 2.1% respectively). On Countdown Hard, E2H-G reaches 41.0% (vs. 18.1% GRPO, 31.5% CL, 17.8% Self-Evolve), and on MATH OOD, E2H-C attains 28.6% (vs. 25.7% GRPO, 25.6% CL, 26.1% Self-Evolve). For LLaMA 3.2 3B, E2H-G achieves 44.1% on Blocksworld Hard and 17.6% on OOD, outperforming GRPO (38.9%, 13.3%) and CL (0.0%, 0.0%). Training exclusively on Hard examples (GRPO-Hard) yields 0.0% on Blocksworld Hard for both models, confirming that direct RL on difficult tasks fails without curriculum.

## Baselines & Comparisons

E2H-G decisively outperforms GRPO (balanced scheduling) on Hard and OOD splits across all three tasks: e.g., Blocksworld Hard 32.9% vs. 21.1%, Countdown Hard 41.0% vs. 18.1%, and Countdown OOD 19.2% vs. 9.2% on Qwen 1.5B. Against traditional curriculum learning (CL, as in Kimi K1.5 and Llama-Nemotron), E2H-G wins by large margins on sparse-reward tasks (Blocksworld Hard: 32.9% vs. 5.8%; OOD: 7.3% vs. 0.7%), while CL suffers from catastrophic forgetting of earlier tasks. Compared to Self-Evolve (adaptive 50%-solve-rate sampling), E2H-G is consistently stronger on Hard/OOD (Blocksworld Hard: 32.9% vs. 13.8%; Countdown OOD: 19.2% vs. 9.5%) and requires no per-model hyperparameter tuning. Training directly on Hard examples (GRPO-Hard) collapses to 0.0% on Blocksworld for both models, confirming that RL without curriculum cannot bootstrap reasoning on tasks beyond the base model's zero-shot capability. E2H-C (cosine scheduling) excels on MATH OOD (28.6% Qwen, 15.8% LLaMA) but underperforms E2H-G on sparse-reward planning tasks, indicating that the Gaussian scheduler's rapid decay of easy-task sampling is critical when rewards are sparse. When combined with DAPO (which filters zero-advantage batches), E2H-G further improves to 46.7% on Blocksworld Hard and 59.4% on Countdown Hard, demonstrating that E2H's difficulty-conditioned sampling and DAPO's batch filtering are complementary.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as the underlying RL algorithm and extends the curriculum learning paradigm (Bengio et al., 2009) to LLM post-training. Prior CRL efforts for LLMs (Kimi K1.5, Llama-Nemotron) used rigid fixed-iteration switching between easy and hard tasks, while Self-Evolve (Chen et al., 2025) and DAPO (Yu et al., 2025) adaptively filter by solve rate. E2H Reasoner departs from these by introducing probabilistic schedulers (cosine and Gaussian) that smoothly and continuously shift sampling probability across difficulty levels, and by grounding the approach in an Approximate Policy Iteration (API) framework (Chen & Maguluri, 2025; Scherrer, 2014) to derive convergence and finite-sample complexity guarantees specific to the curriculum setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that small LLMs (1.5B–3B) can learn to solve tasks they fail at zero-shot when training difficulty is properly scheduled, challenging the assumption that RL post-training alone is insufficient for hard reasoning. The theoretical sample-complexity result showing CRL requires fewer total samples than direct learning provides a principled justification for curriculum design beyond heuristic scheduling. Open questions include whether the Gaussian scheduler's hyperparameters (σ, β) transfer across model scales and task families without retuning, whether the difficulty decomposition via error-rate quartiles remains effective as base models improve, and whether the API-based guarantees hold under the non-stationary, high-dimensional function approximation actually used in practice. The complementary relationship with DAPO suggests scheduling and batch-level filtering are orthogonal axes for future RL post-training systems.
