## Problem

Current Large Audio Language Models (LALMs) rely on a one-time audio encoding that compresses the full signal into static embeddings before reasoning begins, creating a severe information bottleneck that causes the model to lose fine-grained acoustic details during multi-step reasoning. This 'audio-conditioned text reasoning' format confines all subsequent analysis to the text modality, making it difficult to capture subtle temporal cues in complex, multi-source audio. The paper asks whether LALMs can instead treat audio as an active reasoning component, dynamically re-listening to specific segments on demand, analogous to how humans cyclically re-attend to salient acoustic events.

## Input / Output

The model takes an audio clip (typically 10–30 s, though it generalizes to longer durations) and a multiple-choice text question as input. During inference, the model generates a reasoning chain in which it emits structured <seg>start, end</seg> tag pairs; at each tag, generation is paused, the corresponding raw audio segment is clipped from the original clip and appended to the context, and generation resumes with the augmented audio-text sequence. This loop repeats until the model emits an <eos> token, after which a final answer is extracted from a <answer> tag. The output is thus an interleaved sequence of text reasoning steps and raw audio segments, not a single static text response.

## Method

Echo is built on Qwen2.5-Omni (7B) and trained in two stages. Stage 1 (SFT) uses 75.9k audio-QA samples with chain-of-thought annotations (EAQA-SFT) to teach the model to produce 'audio-grounded reasoning'—text CoTs that explicitly reference temporal segments via <seg> tags with justifications and segment-level analysis. Stage 2 adapts the inference mechanism so that whenever a <seg> tag is decoded, the corresponding raw audio tokens are inserted into the context before resuming generation, then applies GRPO-based reinforcement learning on 21.9k QA samples (EAQA-RL) with a composite reward combining format correctness, post-segment consistency, answer accuracy, and a segment-invocation bonus to incentivize strategic re-listening. The accompanying data pipeline leverages Qwen2.5-Omni to extract multi-source audio descriptions (caption, speech, music) from AudioSet-Strong and MusicBench, feeds these alongside temporal metadata to DeepSeek-R1 for QA-CoT synthesis, and applies a second-pass re-evaluation to filter hallucinated or low-quality samples. All interleaved audio tokens are excluded from the RL loss computation, and the segment reward (0.5 points for referencing at least one segment with a correct answer) provides a mild constraint that encourages audio engagement without prescribing specific reasoning paths.

## Results

On the expert-level MMAR benchmark, Echo (7B) achieves 69.99% average accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o-Audio (64.09%) by +5.90% and Gemini-2.0-Flash (67.90%) by +2.09%, while essentially matching Gemini-2.5-Pro (70.03%). On general-purpose benchmarks, Echo attains 80.41% on MMAU-mini and 76.61% on MMAU, outperforming the strongest proprietary system Step-Audio-2 (77.58% / 73.86%) by +2.83% and +2.75% respectively. The latency-to-length ratio analysis shows audio-interleaved reasoning incurs only ~13% additional computational overhead over audio-conditioned text reasoning, with average inference latency of 2.12s on MMAR.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Echo (7B) achieves the best average accuracy among all open-source and adapted LALMs on MMAR (69.99%), beating the closest adapted competitor Audio-Thinker (67.25%) by +2.74% and Omni-R1 (63.46%) by +6.53%, while also surpassing proprietary GPT-4o-Audio (64.09%) by +5.90% and Gemini-2.0-Flash (67.90%) by +2.09%; it essentially ties Gemini-2.5-Pro (70.03%) at −0.04%. On MMAU-mini, Echo (80.41%) leads Step-Audio-2 (77.58%) by +2.83%, Audio-Thinker (78.00%) by +2.41%, and Omni-R1 (77.03%) by +3.38%, and outperforms all proprietary systems including Gemini-2.5-Pro (71.60%) by +8.81%. On MMAU, Echo (76.61%) similarly exceeds Step-Audio-2 (73.86%) by +2.75% and Audio-Thinker (75.39%) by +1.22%. The base Qwen2.5-Omni (57.33% MMAR / 71.53% MMAU-mini) shows that the two-stage SFT+RL training with audio-interleaved inference adaptation contributes +12.66% on MMAR and +8.88% on MMAU-mini. Earlier adapted methods Audio-CoT (28.97%) and Audio-Reasoner (36.42%) on MMAR remain far behind, confirming that audio-conditioned text reasoning without dynamic re-listening is insufficient for expert-level tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 'thinking with images' paradigm from vision-language models (DeepEyes, Chain-of-Focus, GRIT) to the audio domain, proposing audio-interleaved reasoning where the model dynamically re-listens to specific temporal segments during inference rather than relying on a single one-time encoding. It builds on Qwen2.5-Omni as the base LALM and GRPO for policy optimization, departing from prior audio reasoning work (Audio-CoT, Audio-Reasoner, Omni-R1, Audio-Thinker) that confines reasoning to the text modality after a single audio-to-text projection. The two-stage training (SFT for segment localization, then RL for re-listening proficiency) and the LLM-synthesized data pipeline are novel contributions that bridge the gap between audio-grounded text reasoning and true multimodal interleaved reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By treating audio as an active reasoning component rather than static context, this work breaks through the information bottleneck of compressed audio embeddings and offers a principled path toward human-like auditory cognition in LALMs. The approach achieves SOTA on expert-level benchmarks (MMAR) surpassing GPT-4o and Gemini-2.0-Flash with only a 7B model, while incurring just ~13% additional latency. Open questions remain around more advanced audio manipulations (slow playback, frequency-band isolation), process-level supervision for segment selection, and whether the paradigm scales to much longer or more complex multi-source audio. The auto-generated CoT annotations without human heuristics and the lack of explicit supervision over which segments to re-listen represent clear avenues for refinement.
