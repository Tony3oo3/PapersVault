## Problem

Defining a principled, computationally efficient metric between operator representations of dynamical systems remains an open challenge. Existing approaches either lack theoretical soundness (norm-based, Martin pseudo-metric), are restricted to self-adjoint operators, or yield pseudo-metrics rather than true metrics. No prior method simultaneously provides a true metric, invariance to sampling frequency, finite-sample convergence guarantees, and compatibility with arbitrary operator estimation methods.

## Input / Output

The method consumes trajectory data as consecutive state pairs (x_i, y_i) sampled at arbitrary time-lags, from which Koopman/transfer operators are estimated via reduced-rank regression in a common RKHS. It produces an SGOT distance (a scalar) between any pair of systems, Fréchet barycenter operators for interpolation, and downstream ML outputs (class labels via k-NN, 2D T-SNE embeddings). A key property is that the metric is invariant to the sampling frequency of trajectories, enabling comparison of systems observed at different temporal resolutions. The cost matrix computation scales as O(n²r² + r³ log r), making it asymptotically equivalent to kernel-based methods.

## Method

Each transfer operator is represented as a discrete probability distribution over its joint spectral-Grassmann space: atoms are pairs (eigenvalue, eigensubspace) weighted by geometric multiplicity, where eigensubspaces live on the Grassmannian manifold of Hilbert-Schmidt operators. The SGOT metric is the p-Wasserstein distance between these distributions under a ground metric d_η that linearly combines eigenvalue distance (computed in polar coordinates to encode physical timescales and frequencies) and Grassmannian distance (Hilbert-Schmidt norm of orthogonal projector differences), with a tunable weight η. The OT coupling matrix provides permutation invariance, and the resulting construction is proven to be a true metric on the space of non-defective finite-rank operators. For Fréchet barycenters, the paper formulates a parametric operator with control points and solves the free-support Wasserstein barycenter problem via inexact coordinate descent, alternating exact OT plan computation with gradient-based updates and closed-form manifold projections enforcing spectral decomposition constraints (α*Kβ = I, β_j*Kβ_j = 1).

## Results

SGOT achieves the best average classification rank across all three kernel types on 14 UEA time-series datasets: 1.34±0.79 (linear), 1.48±0.70 (RBF), and 1.71±0.77 (deep), consistently outperforming the second-best baseline GOT (2.66, 2.48, 2.94 respectively). In the RBF setting, SGOT reaches 95% accuracy on BasicMotions, 98% on ERing, and 95% on Epilepsy. In shift experiments, SGOT is the only metric (alongside GOT) that remains robust to sampling frequency changes, while SOT oscillates and norm-based metrics saturate or produce local minima.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SGOT consistently outperforms all three baselines across every kernel type and dataset. Against GOT (Antonini & Cavalletti, 2021), which captures only eigenspace geometry, SGOT gains roughly 1.0–1.3 rank positions on average (e.g., 1.34 vs. 2.66 on linear kernels), demonstrating the value of jointly incorporating eigenvalue information. Against SOT (Redman et al., 2024), which captures only eigenvalue geometry, the gap is even larger (1.34 vs. 4.49 on linear), and SOT additionally exhibits oscillatory behavior under frequency shifts. The Martin pseudo-metric (Martin, 2002) is consistently the weakest baseline, ranking last or near-last in every setting (e.g., 5.30 on linear, 4.02 on RBF). In the RBF accuracy table, SGOT leads on 4 of 5 datasets (BasicMotions 95% vs. SOT 87%; ERing 98% vs. GOT 96%; Epilepsy 95% vs. GOT 93%; NATOPS 80% vs. GOT 78%), with a near-tie on FingerMovements (53% vs. 53%). In the interpolation experiment, only SGOT barycenters naturally recover the correct frequencies and decay rates between two oscillatory systems, while Hilbert-Schmidt barycenters produce overdamped dynamics and even constrained Hilbert-Schmidt barycenters converge to a local minimum.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate threads in operator-based system comparison: OT on eigenvalue spectra (SOT, Redman et al. 2024) and OT on eigenspace geometry via Grassmannian metrics (GOT, Antonini & Cavalletti 2021). The key departure is that prior OT-based methods are restricted to self-adjoint operators and yield only pseudo-metrics, while SGOT constructs a true Wasserstein metric on the joint spectral-Grassmann space for general non-defective operators. The statistical learning theory extends Kostic et al. (2022, 2023) by relaxing the well-specifiedness assumption on the RKHS, and the barycenter algorithm adapts the free-support Wasserstein barycenter framework of Cuturi & Doucet (2014) to the operator spectral decomposition setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SGOT provides the first computationally efficient true metric for comparing general Koopman/transfer operators that is invariant to sampling frequency, filling a gap that has limited the use of operator representations in standard ML pipelines. The Fréchet barycenter capability enables meaningful interpolation between dynamical systems, demonstrated on fluid dynamics, which has no direct analogue in prior norm-based or pseudo-metric approaches. Open questions include extension to defective operators (sketched via Jordan decomposition but not fully developed), continuous spectra, and scalability to very high-dimensional state spaces where the O(n²r²) cost matrix computation may become prohibitive.
