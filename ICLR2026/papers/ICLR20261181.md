## Problem

Existing 3D generation methods produce static geometries lacking the skeletal hierarchy needed for animation, while skeleton generation methods rely on end-to-end mesh-to-skeleton paradigms that offer no explicit structural control, resulting in unpredictable joint placement. No prior work generates rigged 3D meshes directly from user-drawn 2D strokes, leaving a gap between intuitive 2D sketching and animatable 3D asset creation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261181/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 2D stroke graph G_2D = (J_xy, E) recorded via a canvas tool (discrete joint points connected by bone line segments, topologically isomorphic to the target 3D skeleton) and a free-form text prompt. It produces a 3D skeleton as N joint coordinates in R^{N×3} with edge topology, which is then converted into a textured rigged mesh. During training, the 2D stroke condition is simulated by perturbing orthographic projections of ground-truth 3D skeletons to mimic hand-drawn imprecision; at inference, users draw directly in the canvas interface.

## Method

Stroke3D uses a two-stage skeleton-first pipeline. Stage 1 (skeleton generation) employs a Skeletal Graph VAE (GCN + TransformerConv layers) to encode skeleton graphs into a latent space, then a Skeletal Graph Diffusion Transformer (Sk-DiT) that replaces DiT's self-attention with TransformerConv for graph-structured data, adds cross-attention with CLIP text embeddings for semantic guidance, and concatenates 2D stroke joint-coordinate features with the noisy latent for structural conditioning; classifier-free guidance is applied during training. Stage 2 (mesh synthesis) augments the SKDream/MVDream skeleton-to-mesh model with the newly curated TextuRig dataset (6,800 textured rigged meshes with VLM-generated captions from Objaverse-XL), then applies SKA-DPO—a DiffusionDPO variant using the SKA Score (skeleton-mesh alignment) as the preference signal—to further refine geometric fidelity. The key architectural insight is operating the diffusion process in the VAE's latent graph space rather than directly on 3D coordinates, enabling the TransformerConv-based attention to respect skeleton topology while the 2D stroke condition provides explicit structural constraints that ablations show are critical for convergence.

## Results

On the MagicArticulate test set, Stroke3D achieves the lowest Chamfer Distance across all three skeleton metrics: CD-J2J = 0.048, CD-J2B = 0.039, and CD-B2B = 0.034 (All category), outperforming the next-best method MagicArticulate (0.052 / 0.041 / 0.034). For skeleton-conditioned mesh generation on the SKDream evaluation set, Stroke3D reaches a Mean_Inst SKA score of 87.83 and Mean_Class of 84.36, improving over the SKDream baseline (80.43 / 74.38) by approximately 7.4 and 10.0 points respectively. The gains are consistent across all object categories (Character, Animal, Plant), with the largest improvement on Plant (CD-J2J: 0.040 vs. 0.157 for SKDream).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Stroke3D outperforms all four skeleton-generation baselines on the overall (All) CD-J2J, CD-J2B, and CD-B2B metrics. Against the closest competitor MagicArticulate, Stroke3D achieves a 0.004 lower CD-J2J (0.048 vs. 0.052) and a 0.002 lower CD-J2B (0.039 vs. 0.041), while tying on CD-B2B (both 0.034); the gap widens substantially on Animal and Plant categories where MagicArticulate degrades (e.g., CD-J2J Plant: 0.040 vs. 0.079). UniRig is consistently outperformed by 0.015–0.024 on CD-J2J across categories. RigNet and SKDream, which take 3D meshes as input rather than 2D strokes, show markedly higher errors (CD-J2J All: 0.078 and 0.111 respectively), confirming the advantage of explicit structural conditioning. For mesh generation (Table 2), Stroke3D with TextuRig + SKA-DPO surpasses SKDream by 7.4 points on Mean_Inst SKA (87.83 vs. 80.43) and 10.0 points on Mean_Class (84.36 vs. 74.38), and outperforms SDEdit by 15.7 points on Mean_Inst, with the largest per-category gain on Plant (70.63 vs. 53.53 for SKDream).

## Relation to Prior Work

Stroke3D departs from the dominant mesh-to-skeleton paradigm (RigNet, SKDream, MagicArticulate, UniRig) by inverting the pipeline: it generates the skeleton first via a novel graph diffusion model (Sk-VAE + Sk-DiT) conditioned on 2D strokes and text, then synthesizes the mesh from that skeleton. The mesh stage builds directly on SKDream's MVDream-based multi-view diffusion, augmented with the newly curated TextuRig dataset and a skeleton-alignment-guided DPO (SKA-DPO) that adapts the DiffusionDPO objective to 3D rigging. The Sk-DiT architecture repurposes the DiT design with TransformerConv for graph-structured latents, and the structural conditioning via 2D stroke concatenation is a new mechanism not present in prior skeleton or mesh generation work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to accept freehand 2D strokes as explicit structural control for rigged 3D asset generation, lowering the barrier for non-professional users who cannot operate professional DCC tools. The TextuRig dataset (6,800 textured rigged meshes with captions) addresses a real data gap in the rigging community, and SKA-DPO provides a reusable recipe for aligning generated geometry to a conditioning skeleton. Key limitations include dependence on a limited training set (~48K models, 0–30 joints), poor generalization to rare categories (samurai, turtle), and sensitivity to stroke ambiguity in side views; the authors note that the recently released Puppeteer/Articulation-XL expansion and an end-to-end text-to-rigged-mesh model are the most promising next steps.
