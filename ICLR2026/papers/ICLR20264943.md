limitations: The paper acknowledges that single-step completion may be less expressive than multi-step generative policies for highly multimodal or high-dimensional behaviors, that bypassing explicit hierarchy may limit performance in very long-horizon GCRL tasks, and that the method is currently restricted to continuous action spaces. ## Problem

Generative policies based on diffusion or flow-matching capture rich multimodal action distributions but require tens to hundreds of iterative sampling steps at inference, and their sequential generation structure makes backpropagation-through-time intractable for off-policy actor-critic training. The paper asks whether one can retain the expressiveness of generative models while reducing both training and inference to a single forward pass, and whether hierarchical goal-conditioned policies can be similarly compressed into flat inference without losing subgoal-exploiting performance.

## Input / Output

At inference the policy takes only a state s (and a goal g in the GCRL variant) plus a noise vector z ~ N(0,I), and produces a single continuous action in one forward pass: a = z + h_θ(z, s, 0, 1). During training, the model additionally receives an interpolated intermediate sample a_τ = (1−τ)z + τa and a step-size parameter d, allowing it to be supervised at arbitrary points along the flow path. In the goal-conditioned variant (GC-SSCP), the input is augmented with a hierarchy-level indicator k ∈ {0,1} and a step-size d, and the output is a joint (action, subgoal) pair, enabling flat inference that implicitly performs two-level hierarchical reasoning in a single call.

## Method

The core mechanism augments standard flow-matching with a completion-vector objective: at every intermediate point x_τ along the linear interpolation path, the model h_θ(x_τ, τ, d) is trained to predict the normalized direction from x_τ directly to the terminal data point x_1, supervised by ground-truth dataset samples rather than self-generated bootstrap targets. The actor loss combines three terms — a flow-matching velocity loss (d=0), a completion loss (d=1−τ) that regresses the one-step prediction onto the true action, and a Q-guided deterministic policy gradient loss — while the critic uses standard twin-Q Bellman backups. Because inference is a single evaluation at τ=0, d=1, no iterative ODE solving or BPTT is needed, making the method compatible with DDPG+BC-style off-policy training and 64× faster than diffusion-based baselines. For goal-conditioned RL, the same completion principle is extended to a flat policy that jointly outputs (action, subgoal) conditioned on (state, goal, level, step-size), with a self-supervised shortcut loss that aligns the d=2 prediction with the composition of two d=1 hierarchical steps, distilling HIQL-style hierarchy into a single flat network.

## Results

On D4RL offline RL (9 MuJoCo tasks), SSCQL achieves an average normalized score of 87.9, matching the best generative baseline DQL (87.9) while being 64× faster in training (~16 min vs ~8 hours) and ~5× faster at inference (0.27 ms vs 1.27 ms). On OGBench goal-conditioned RL (16 tasks), GC-SSCP achieves an average success rate of 54.44%, surpassing the hierarchical HIQL (50.94%) and QRL (48.75%). In fully online RL, SSCQL reaches 90.53 average score versus 67.7 (DQL) and 70.4 (CAC), and in behavior cloning on RoboMimic/Push-T it matches Diffusion Policy (0.87 vs 0.88 average success) with single-step generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSCQL ties DQL on average D4RL score (87.9) but wins decisively on Hopper-Medium (102.4 vs 90.5) and Hopper-Medium-Replay (101.4 vs 101.3), while DQL retains edges on Walker2d-Medium (87.0 vs 84.2) and Walker2d-Medium-Replay (95.5 vs 85.9). Against FQL, SSCQL dominates on the harder medium and medium-replay datasets (e.g., Hopper-M: 102.4 vs 60.6; Hopper-MR: 101.4 vs 50.7; Walker2d-MR: 85.9 vs 38.8), whereas FQL leads on HalfCheetah-Medium-Expert (102.1 vs 98.1). In goal-conditioned RL, GC-SSCP (54.44 avg) outperforms the hierarchical HIQL (50.94) and QRL (48.75) on average, winning most PointMaze tasks (e.g., 92% vs 79% on medium-navigate) but trailing HIQL on AntMaze-giant-navigate (52% vs 65%) and AntMaze-medium-stitch (84% vs 94%). In fully online RL, SSCQL (90.53 avg) substantially outperforms DQL (67.7) and CAC (70.4), with the gap widening on Hopper (105.1 vs ~80). For behavior cloning, SSCP (0.87 avg) nearly matches Diffusion Policy (0.88) using a single forward pass versus DP's 100 denoising steps, winning on Push-T (0.85 vs 0.79) but trailing on Toolhang (0.64 vs 0.87). In offline-to-online finetuning, SSCQL shows stable or improved scores across all tasks, while DQL and CAC frequently degrade by >10% (e.g., DQL Hopper-ME drops from 111.1 to 71.7).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the flow-matching generative framework (Lipman et al., 2022) and the shortcut-model concept (Frans et al., 2024) by replacing the self-consistency bootstrap target with a ground-truth completion vector that directly regresses to the terminal data sample, eliminating the instability of self-generated pseudo-targets in offline settings. The resulting Single-Step Completion Policy (SSCP) is embedded in a DDPG+BC-style actor-critic framework (Fujimoto & Gu, 2021), making it compatible with standard off-policy value-based optimization without backpropagation through iterative generation chains. For goal-conditioned RL, the paper draws on HIQL's hierarchical AWR formulation (Park et al., 2023) and IQL's value learning (Kostrikov et al., 2021), but departs by distilling the two-level hierarchy into a single flat policy via a step-size-conditioned shortcut loss, rather than maintaining separate high- and low-level networks at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 64× training speedup over DQL and single-step inference make generative-policy expressiveness viable for real-time control and large-scale offline RL, removing the primary barrier to adopting diffusion/flow-based policies. The GCRL result—that a flat policy implicitly capturing subgoal structure can match or exceed explicit hierarchical methods like HIQL—challenges the assumption that multi-level decomposition is necessary for long-horizon goal-reaching, though the margin is modest and the finding is limited to OGBench-scale tasks. Open questions include whether the approach extends to discrete or hybrid action spaces, whether maximum-entropy formulations can reduce sensitivity to the two regularization coefficients (α₁, α₂), and whether the flat-policy advantage persists in substantially longer-horizon or higher-dimensional settings where explicit hierarchy may still be indispensable.
