## Problem

In large-scale 3D Gaussian Splatting, sparsely observed regions initialized from low-frequency point clouds, when supervised with high-resolution images throughout training, suffer uncontrolled densification and redundant floaters. Existing scheduling strategies for image resolution and Gaussian densification are hardcoded and static, failing to adapt to the actual convergence of the scene's frequency content, which either destabilizes early optimization or delays necessary refinements.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20265155/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images (dynamically downsampled by integer factors from 5× to 1× during training) and a sparse COLMAP point cloud for initialization. It produces a block-wise 3D Gaussian scene representation with sphere-constrained primitives, trained in two stages (coarse scaffold + per-block fine training) following CityGS partitioning, enabling real-time novel view rendering. The point cloud serves a dual role: providing initial Gaussian positions and defining spatial priors for the sphere constraints.

## Method

The core contribution is SIG, a scheduler that synchronizes image supervision resolution with the evolving frequency of the Gaussian representation. The paper mathematically derives the average sampling frequency of the supervision signal (Eq. 1) and the effective scene bandwidth of the 3D Gaussian opacity field (Eq. 5, approximated via 3 dB bandwidth of each primitive). During training, the gradient of scene bandwidth is monitored; once it drops below a threshold, the image resolution is increased and a fixed number of densification rounds are triggered, ensuring frequency-consistent coarse-to-fine recovery. Complementary to this, Sphere-Constrained Gaussians assign each primitive an anchor (its COLMAP initialization) and a max_offset (mean distance to K=15 nearest neighbors), pruning any Gaussian that drifts beyond the spherical boundary; replication and splitting follow distinct inheritance rules to balance exploration and structural preservation. A reprojection-based photometric regularization further constrains densification in textureless regions.

## Results

On the 'rubble' scene (Mill19), Ours-L achieves 27.35 dB PSNR, 0.843 SSIM, and 0.189 LPIPS, outperforming the best baseline CityGS (26.45 dB) by +0.9 dB PSNR. Across all five scenes (building, rubble, residence, sci-art, MatrixCity-Aerial), Ours-L attains the highest PSNR in every case, with gains of +0.16 to +1.15 dB over the strongest GS-based competitor. In terms of efficiency, the method achieves a 1.5× speedup per block over BlockGS (71 min vs. 201 min) and 1.4× over CityGS (71 min vs. 98 min) while simultaneously improving quality. The lighter variant Ours-S (fewer Gaussians) also surpasses all baselines on every scene, confirming the gains are not solely due to increased primitive count.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper benchmarks against two NeRF-based methods (Mega-NeRF, Switch-NeRF) and five GS-based methods (DashGS, VastGS, CityGS, DOGS, BlockGS). Ours-L dominates all GS-based baselines on every scene: the largest margin is +1.15 dB PSNR over VastGS on 'residence' (23.46 vs. 22.31) and +0.9 dB over CityGS on 'rubble' (27.35 vs. 26.45). Against DashGS, which uses a hard-coded resolution schedule, Ours-L gains +0.98 dB on 'rubble' and +1.84 dB on 'sci-art', indicating that adaptive frequency-aligned scheduling substantially outperforms fixed schedules. NeRF-based baselines lag by 2–3 dB in PSNR and are not competitive on MatrixCity-Aerial. In efficiency, integrating the SIG scheduler into CityGS reduces per-block optimization time from 98 to 71 min (1.4×) while boosting PSNR from 26.45 to 27.35, and into BlockGS from 201 to 130 min (1.5×) with a +0.37 dB gain, demonstrating plug-and-play compatibility.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on 3DGS (Kerbl et al., 2023) as the scene representation and adopts CityGS's coarse-to-fine block partitioning strategy for large-scale training. It departs from prior scheduling approaches (DashGS, TamingGS) that use hard-coded, pre-defined resolution and densification schedules by reframing the problem through signal processing: the Nyquist sampling theorem motivates synchronizing the supervision image resolution with the effective bandwidth of the Gaussian representation. For structural priors, it introduces Sphere-Constrained Gaussians as an explicit alternative to the implicit neural anchors in Scaffold-GS/Neural Gaussians, specifically to preserve compatibility with block-wise independent training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The SIG scheduler is demonstrated as a plug-and-play component that improves both quality and speed when grafted onto existing baselines (BlockGS, CityGS), making it practically adoptable without re-architecting a pipeline. The 1.5× per-block speedup and consistent +0.9 dB PSNR gains across three datasets are meaningful for city-scale applications where training cost is a primary bottleneck. However, the paper acknowledges that LoD rendering remains an open challenge, and the coarse-to-fine training naturally produces hierarchical Gaussians that could be exploited more systematically. The signal-processing framing, while elegant, relies on approximations (delta-function power spectrum, uniform local sampling) whose validity at extreme scales or with highly non-uniform camera coverage is not fully stress-tested.
