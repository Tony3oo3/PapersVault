## Problem

Current diffusion transformers rely on a two-stage VAE+diffusion paradigm that introduces accumulated errors, decoding artifacts, and complex adversarial training. Returning to pixel space avoids these issues but requires either impractical token counts or complicated multi-scale cascade pipelines. The paper asks whether a single-stage, end-to-end pixel-space diffusion transformer can match latent diffusion quality without VAEs or cascades.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261482/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a noisy RGB image (256×256 or 512×512), a conditioning signal (class label for ImageNet or a text prompt encoded by Qwen3-1.7B for T2I), and a diffusion timestep, and outputs a pixel-wise velocity field that drives the reverse rectified-flow ODE. A key design constraint is the use of 16×16 patches to keep the token count comparable to latent diffusion models (e.g., 16×16 tokens for 256×256), which is why the neural field head is needed to decode fine details within each large patch. The model supports training-free arbitrary-resolution inference by interpolating neural field coordinates while keeping the token count fixed.

## Method

PixNerd replaces the final linear projection of a standard diffusion transformer with a patch-wise implicit neural field head. For each patch, the transformer's last hidden feature predicts two weight matrices (W1, W2) of a small MLP via a linear layer; these weights are row-wise normalized for training stability. At decoding time, each pixel's local coordinates are encoded using a novel DCT-basis encoding (cos(k₁i)·cos(k₂j) over frequency pairs), concatenated with the noisy pixel value, and passed through the per-patch MLP to produce a pixel-level velocity. The overall architecture follows the rectified-flow formulation with SwiGLU, RoPE-2D, and RMSNorm, trained end-to-end with REPA alignment loss and interval classifier-free guidance (CFG applied only within t∈[0.1, 1]). This single-stage design achieves 1.93 FID on ImageNet 256×256 and 0.73 GenEval overall with ~8× lower latency than prior pixel-space diffusion models.

## Results

On ImageNet 256×256 class-conditional generation, PixNerd-XL/16 achieves 1.93 FID and 4.50 sFID with 100 Euler steps (320 epochs), matching or exceeding latent diffusion models like SiT-XL (2.06 FID) and FlowDCN (2.00 FID) while operating entirely in pixel space without a VAE. For text-to-image generation, PixNerd-XXL/16 scores 0.73 overall on GenEval and 80.9 average on DPG, outperforming the prior best pixel-space model PixelFlow-XL/4 (0.60 GenEval, 77.90 DPG). Inference latency is approximately 8× lower than other pixel diffusion models (0.51 s/image for PixNerd-L/16 vs. 21.3 s for ADM and 10.1 s for PixelFlow-XL/4).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against latent diffusion models, PixNerd-XL/16 (1.93 FID) is competitive with SiT-XL (2.06) and FlowDCN (2.00) but trails the strongest latent methods REPA (1.42) and DDT-XL (1.26) by 0.5–0.7 FID; however, it achieves this without any VAE, eliminating the two-stage training pipeline and its associated decoding artifacts. Among pixel-space models, PixNerd-XL/16 substantially outperforms ADM (4.59 FID), JetFormer (6.64), and FractalMAR-H (6.15), and edges out RDM (1.99) and PixelFlow-XL/4 (1.98) while running ~8× faster at inference (0.51 s vs. 10.1–21.3 s per image). On the GenEval text-to-image benchmark, PixNerd-XXL/16 (0.73 overall) surpasses PixelFlow-XL/4 (0.60–0.64) by a wide margin and exceeds SD3 (0.68) and DALL-E3 (0.67), though it remains below DALL-E3's individual sub-scores on counting. On DPG, PixNerd-XXL/16 (80.9 average) beats PixelFlow-XL/4 (77.90) and approaches DALL-E3 (83.50), with particularly strong scores on Relation (91.3) and Entity (87.9) dimensions. The direct ablation against PixDiT-L/16 (same architecture minus the neural field head) shows the neural field head alone reduces FID from 4.11 to 2.64 at 160 epochs, confirming the core architectural contribution.

## Relation to Prior Work

PixNerd sits at the intersection of three research threads: the diffusion transformer architecture (DiT, SiT), the rectified-flow/flow-matching formulation, and implicit neural fields (NeRF). It departs from the dominant latent diffusion paradigm by eliminating the VAE entirely and operating directly in pixel space, while also departing from prior pixel-space models (ADM, RDM, PixelFlow) that rely on multi-scale cascade pipelines. The core architectural contribution is repurposing the NeRF concept—per-patch MLP weights predicted by the transformer's final hidden states—to decode large-patch velocity fields, replacing what would otherwise be an inadequate linear projection. A novel DCT-basis coordinate encoding further distinguishes the neural field head from standard sin/cos positional encodings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

By removing the VAE, PixNerd eliminates the two-stage training pipeline's accumulated errors and decoding artifacts, offering a genuinely single-stage, end-to-end pixel-space generative model that achieves competitive FID (1.93 on ImageNet 256×256) at roughly 8× lower latency than prior pixel diffusion models. However, the paper candidly acknowledges that pixel-space diffusion still does not exhibit better performance scaling than advanced latent diffusion transformers, leaving open whether this gap can be closed with further scaling or architectural refinements. The neural field head is a lightweight, drop-in component that could potentially benefit other large-patch or high-frequency modeling tasks beyond image generation.
