## Problem

Standard confidence calibration, which aligns predicted confidence with empirical accuracy, inherently caps the precision of misclassification detection (MisD) in vision-language models. The paper proves that even a perfectly calibrated model cannot separate correct from incorrect predictions beyond a fundamental bound, yet no prior work had identified or addressed this limitation from a calibration perspective.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261727/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes an RGB image and a set of candidate class text labels (e.g., 'a photo of a {class}') processed by a frozen CLIP or SigLIP backbone. It outputs calibrated confidence scores by scaling the original logits with a sample-specific positive temperature coefficient τ_v, which is predicted by a lightweight meta network from the VLM's logits, image embedding, and predicted text embedding. The entire pipeline is post-hoc: the VLM parameters remain frozen, and only ~17K–20K meta-network parameters are trained on a small few-shot calibration set (as few as 4 samples per class).

## Method

The paper replaces the standard diagonal reliability curve with a normalized sigmoid curve Ψ(s) that pushes accuracy above the diagonal in the high-confidence region [0.5, 1] and below it in the low-confidence region [0, 0.5], provably increasing MisD precision and reducing prediction-mixing entropy at every confidence level. Because Ψ(s) is a statistical quantity and not directly differentiable, the authors design a surrogate loss L_SUR that combines a confidence-regularization term (penalizing the gap between observed correctness and Ψ(s)) with a full-probability constraint (cross-entropy for correct samples, uniform-distribution comparison for incorrect samples) to stabilize optimization. A lightweight meta network (LMN) with three FC branches over logits, image embeddings, and predicted text embeddings predicts the instance-specific temperature τ_v via a softplus activation, and is trained end-to-end with L_SUR on the calibration set without touching the VLM weights.

## Results

On six datasets (DTD, Flowers102, EuroSAT, RESICS45, MNIST, CUB), LMN achieves average AUROC of 0.845 and FPR90-E of 0.402, improving over zero-shot CLIP by 6.1% and 22.9% respectively. The largest gains appear on EuroSAT (AUROC 0.788 vs. 0.650 for CLIP) and MNIST (AUROC 0.915 vs. 0.813), while the smallest margin is on CUB (AUROC 0.812 vs. 0.807). LMN also outperforms the strongest MisD baseline ViLU by 2.8% AUROC and 18.7% FPR90-E on average. The method requires only 17K–20K parameters and trains in under 50 seconds per dataset.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LMN outperforms all five baselines across every dataset and metric. Against zero-shot CLIP (Radford et al., 2021), the gains are substantial: +6.1% AUROC and +22.9% FPR90-E on average, confirming that standard confidence calibration alone is insufficient for MisD. FeatureClipping (Tao et al., 2025) and SCT (Yu et al., 2024) provide only marginal or inconsistent improvements over CLIP (e.g., FeatureClipping actually degrades AUROC on DTD from 0.762 to 0.749), validating the paper's theoretical claim that calibration imposes a fundamental ceiling on MisD. ViLU (Lafon et al., 2025), the strongest MisD-specific baseline, is beaten by LMN by 2.8% AUROC and 18.7% FPR90-E on average, with the largest gap on MNIST (0.915 vs. 0.877 AUROC). In the open-vocabulary setting (Table 2), LMN also surpasses DOR (Wang et al., 2025), achieving 7.1% and 5.9% relative improvements over CLIP and DOR respectively on DTD's FPR90-S, while preserving zero-shot generalization to unseen classes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on temperature scaling (Guo et al., 2017) and instance-wise calibration methods, but fundamentally reinterprets the reliability diagram through the lens of misclassification detection (MisD). It departs from prior calibration work by proving that the standard calibration objective (aligning confidence with accuracy) inherently caps MisD precision, and from prior MisD work (Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2016; Corbiere et al., 2019; Zhu et al., 2022) by being the first to derive MisD-oriented calibration objectives from the geometry of the reliability curve. The method also extends VLM-specific calibration approaches (DAC, DOR, FeatureClipping) and MisD methods for VLMs (ViLU, FSMisD) by introducing a post-hoc meta network that predicts sample-specific temperatures without modifying the backbone, while preserving zero-shot generalization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The theoretical result that perfect calibration imposes a fundamental upper bound on MisD precision challenges the common assumption that better calibration automatically yields better failure detection, redirecting the field toward MisD-specific calibration objectives. Practically, the lightweight post-hoc framework (17K–20K parameters, sub-minute training) can be attached to any pretrained or prompt-tuned VLM without altering its predictions or zero-shot capability, making it broadly deployable. Open questions include whether the surrogate loss and normalized-sigmoid target generalize to generative VLMs or open-set recognition, and whether the theoretical precision guarantees tighten with larger calibration sets or different confidence distributions.
