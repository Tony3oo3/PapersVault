## Problem

Neural VRP solvers trained on one or a fixed set of problem distributions and scales suffer catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuned on sequentially arriving new tasks, and zero-shot generalization has inherent limits. Prior lifelong learning work for VRP solvers is restricted to narrow settings (scale-only changes, known task order, controllable instance generation), leaving the more realistic scenario of simultaneous distribution and scale shifts with unknown task order and uncontrollable instance streams unaddressed.

## Input / Output

The solver consumes a routing problem instance specified by node coordinates drawn from one of six distributions (Uniform, Gaussian Mixture, Explosion, Compression, Grid, Ring) at scales 20, 50, or 100, plus node demands for CVRP. During solving, the model operates in an MDP where each state is a partial solution (set of visited nodes) and the output at each step is a full probability distribution over all remaining candidate nodes, from which the highest-probability node is selected to extend the route. The final output is the complete node visit sequence (route solution) whose total distance is the optimization objective.

## Method

LLR-BC is a model- and RL-algorithm-agnostic lifelong learning framework that maintains a fixed-size experience buffer via reservoir sampling, storing state-behavior pairs (partial solution + action probability distribution) only at the final epoch of each task. Two core modules drive consolidation: (1) Confidence-aware Experience Weighting (CaEW) normalizes the variance of each buffered action distribution to assign higher consolidation weights to low-confidence decisions, which are most susceptible to drift during subsequent training; (2) Decision-seeking Behavior Consolidation (DsBC) minimizes a weighted reverse KL divergence between the current model's behavior on buffered states and the stored behaviors, exploiting RKLD's mode-seeking property to specifically preserve the highest-probability node selections that determine route quality. At each training step on a new task, the solver jointly optimizes the standard DRL loss on fresh trajectories and the behavior consolidation loss on a sampled subset of buffered experiences, with a hyperparameter α balancing the two terms.

## Results

On CVRP (k=6 tasks), LLR-BC achieves an Average Performance (AP) of 4.2×10⁻³, Average Forgetting (AF) of 0.7×10⁻³, and Average Plasticity (APl) of 3.5×10⁻³, substantially outperforming all baselines whose AP exceeds 23.5×10⁻³. On TSP, LLR-BC achieves AP=3.4×10⁻³, AF=0.8×10⁻³, APl=2.8×10⁻³, again the best across all methods. For zero-shot generalization (AG), LLR-BC records 26.7×10⁻³ (CVRP) and 41.1×10⁻³ (TSP), the lowest among all compared methods. On benchmark instances (CVRPLIB, TSPLIB), LLR-BC achieves the best normalized test performance: 7.88 (CVRPLIB) and 18.08 (TSPLIB), versus 27.38–46.80 and 27.80–122.60 for baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

LLR-BC dominates all baselines on AP, APl, and AG for both CVRP and TSP. Against Fine-tuning (the strongest non-lifelong baseline), LLR-BC reduces CVRP AP from 23.5 to 4.2 (×5.6 improvement) and TSP AP from 14.8 to 3.4 (×4.3), while also cutting AF from 19.9 to 0.7 (CVRP) and 28.9 to 0.8 (TSP). EWC and LiBOG, which regularize parameter updates, show moderate forgetting reduction (AF≈19.5–19.7 CVRP) but still lag LLR-BC by 25–28× on AP and 28× on AF; they also lose plasticity over time as cumulative regularization constrains the model. Li (inter/intra) achieves near-zero forgetting (AF≈0) by dedicating half of each task's epochs to replaying old instances, but this comes at a severe plasticity cost (APl=33.6–39.3 vs. LLR-BC's 3.5 on CVRP), making them impractical for real-time task acquisition. Feng, the other lifelong VRP baseline, is outperformed by LLR-BC on every metric (e.g., CVRP AP 24.6→4.2, TSP AP 24.1→3.4). On benchmark generalization (CVRPLIB/TSPLIB), LLR-BC (7.88/18.08) substantially outperforms Fine-tuning (8.54/38.16), EWC (11.86/27.80), and LiBOG (18.05/27.96), confirming that accumulated lifelong knowledge transfers to unseen instances.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the nascent line of lifelong learning for neural VRP solvers (Li et al. 2024; Feng et al. 2025), which was previously restricted to tasks differing only in scale or distance metric, with known task order and controllable instance generation. LLR-BC departs from those works by handling simultaneous changes in both distribution and scale under unknown task order and uncontrollable instance streams. Methodologically, it repurposes experience replay from continual learning but operates at a finer granularity (state-behavior pairs rather than full problem instances), and substitutes the standard forward KL divergence with reverse KL divergence to achieve decision-seeking behavior consolidation, drawing on recent findings in policy distillation and knowledge distillation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practically important gap: real-world routing problems shift in both distribution and scale unpredictably, and existing solvers either generalize poorly or suffer catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuned. LLR-BC's model- and RL-algorithm-agnostic design makes it broadly applicable to existing neural solvers (validated on POMO, Omni, INViT), and the large performance margins over baselines suggest the field has been under-investing in the forgetting problem. Key limitations include the difficulty of tuning a fixed experience sample size |E| when task scales differ substantially, and the framework does not yet handle lifelong learning across different problem variants (e.g., TSP → CVRP), which would require task-specific model components.
