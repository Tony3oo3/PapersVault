## Problem

Existing theoretical guarantees for interventional causal discovery focus on worst-case intervention counts or asymptotic consistency, leaving open how tightly the false-negative rate (FNR) concentrates around its mean in finite dimensions. No prior work provides dimension-adaptive deviation bounds under the weaker ε-interventional faithfulness assumption (which accommodates latent confounding) or extends such analysis to scale-free graph topologies common in empirical networks.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the observational distribution P_X and single-variable interventional distributions P_X^{do(X_k:=Ñ_k)} for each intervened variable, with intervention targets drawn independently as Bernoulli(p_int). The output is a causal order (permutation of V) maximizing the Intersort score, and the theoretical deliverable is a deviation bound on g = D_top/|E| (the FNR), quantifying the probability that the orientation error deviates from its expectation by more than a given threshold t. In the random-graph settings, both the intervention vector I and the edge set E are random, so the optimal order π_opt(I,E) is itself a random variable, and the bounds are stated conditionally on the realized graph in the Barabási-Albert case.

## Method

The core mechanism is a two-step pipeline: (1) derive structural Lipschitz constants bounding how much the topological error D_top changes when a single intervention indicator or edge indicator is flipped, using the sufficient-orientation conditions from Chevalley et al. (2025c) to show that only edges in the ancestral/descendant cone (or, under restricted faithfulness, only incident edges) are affected; (2) apply concentration inequalities—McDiarmid's bounded differences for dense ER and BA graphs, Warnke's typical bounded differences for sparse ER (where max degree is O(log d) w.h.p.), and Bhatia-Davis plus Chebyshev for the normalized FNR in dense ER. The key insight is that the Lipschitz constants inherit the degree structure of the graph: in generalized Barabási-Albert DAGs with degree exponent γ > 3 (i.e., β = 1/(γ−1) < 1/2), the per-coordinate sensitivity scales as d^β, causing the deviation width of the FNR to vanish as O(d^{β−1/2}), demonstrating that heavy-tailed heterogeneity intrinsically regularizes causal orientation error.

## Results

The paper establishes finite-dimension deviation bounds for the false-negative rate (FNR) of causal orientation under random single-variable interventions. In dense Erdős-Rényi DAGs (p_e = Θ(1)), the FNR vanishes in expectation at rate Θ(1/d) and concentrates with typical width O(d^{-1/2}). In sparse ER DAGs (p_e = c/d), the FNR remains bounded at O(1) in expectation but concentrates at rate O(log d / √d). For generalized Barabási-Albert graphs with degree exponent γ > 3 (i.e., β = 1/(γ−1) < 1/2), the deviation width scales as O(d^{β−1/2}) and vanishes asymptotically, demonstrating that heavy-tailed degree heterogeneity intrinsically regularizes causal discovery.

**Table 1: Summary of theoretical results — asymptotic orders for expected topological error and concentration rate in three random-graph regimes.**

| Model | E[f] | E[g] (FNR) | Dev. f | Dev. g (FNR) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ER dense (p_e = Θ(1)) | O((1−p_int)²/p_int · d) | O(2(1−p_int)²/(p_int · d)) | O(d²) | O(d^{−1/2}) |
| ER sparse (p_e = c/d) | O((1−p_int)²/p_int · d) | O(2(1−p_int)²/(c · p_int)) | O(√d · log d) | O(log d / √d) |
| BA (β ∈ (0,1)) | (1−p_int)² · m · d | (1−p_int)² | O(d^{β+1/2}) | O(d^{β−1/2}) |

*Here f is the number of misorientations, g = f/|E| is the FNR, p_int is the intervention probability, m is edges per new node in BA, and β = 1/(γ−1) with γ = 2 + κ/m.*

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper that does not benchmark against competing causal discovery algorithms; instead, it compares concentration behavior across three structural regimes (dense ER, sparse ER, and scale-free BA). The key comparison is between regimes: dense ER yields the tightest concentration (O(d^{−1/2})) with a vanishing mean, sparse ER is weaker by a logarithmic factor (O(log d/√d)) due to maximum-degree control, and BA with γ > 3 achieves a vanishing deviation width O(d^{β−1/2}) that is strictly faster than the sparse ER rate. The empirical illustration (Section 5) using DiffIntersort confirms these predictions: IQR of the FNR decreases with dimension across all graph families, with the BA κ=9.0 case (γ≈3.67 > 3) showing clear vanishing deviation, κ=3.0 (γ=3) remaining roughly constant, and κ=1.0 (γ=7/3 < 3) exhibiting the weakest concentration as predicted. The paper positions its guarantees as a baseline: any method exploiting richer intervention designs or structural priors should achieve deviation behavior at least as good as these minimal-condition bounds.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the framework of Chevalley et al. (2025c), which established expected-error bounds for the Intersort score under ε-interventional faithfulness and random single-variable interventions on Erdős-Rényi DAGs. The key departures are threefold: (i) shifting the analysis from expected misorientation count to the normalized false-negative rate (FNR), (ii) deriving finite-dimension deviation (concentration) bounds via McDiarmid's and Warnke's typical bounded-differences inequalities rather than only mean bounds, and (iii) extending the graph ensemble from Erdős-Rényi to generalized Barabási-Albert scale-free DAGs. The top-order divergence metric is inherited from Rolland et al. (2022), and the empirical illustration uses the DiffIntersort Sinkhorn-based approximation from Chevalley et al. (2024). The paper positions itself against the large body of worst-case intervention-complexity results (Eberhardt et al., Shanmugam et al., Kocaoglu et al.) and I-MEC characterization work (Hauser & Buhlmann, Katz et al.), arguing that average-case, distribution-dependent deviation guarantees are more informative for practical large-scale settings.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

These are the first finite-dimension, faithfulness-robust deviation guarantees for causal orientation error, providing a principled baseline for how much variability in FNR one should expect under random interventions on structured random graphs. The finding that scale-free topologies with γ > 3 cause the deviation width to vanish asymptotically challenges the common intuition that network heterogeneity and high dimensionality hinder reliable discovery. Key limitations include the reliance on stylized random-graph ensembles (no modularity or hierarchy), the acyclicity assumption excluding feedback loops, the absence of p_int dependence in the deviation bounds (an artifact of McDiarmid-style arguments), and the fact that the empirical validation uses an approximate algorithm (DiffIntersort) rather than the true score optimum. Open directions include extending to adaptive intervention policies, more general graph distributions, and developing proof techniques that capture intervention-probability dependence at the deviation level.
