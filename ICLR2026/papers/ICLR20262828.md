## Problem

Medical time series classification models suffer from poor generalizability across datasets due to inter-dataset heterogeneity (varying channel counts, signal lengths, sampling rates, and diagnostic targets) and intra-dataset heterogeneity (patient-level variability). Existing Task-Specific Models require full retraining per dataset, while Task-Specific Adaptation methods produce task-locked adapters that fail to transfer to new tasks. The paper asks: can a pre-trained forecasting foundation model be repurposed into a generalizable MedTS classifier that adapts to unseen datasets with minimal new parameters?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262828/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multichannel medical waveforms (EEG: 16–33 channels; ECG: 12–15 channels) at 250–256 Hz with 250–300 time-point windows. The frozen TimesFM backbone processes each channel independently in a channel-wise fashion, producing C×L×D feature tokens where L depends on input length and D=1280. The classifier outputs a K-class probability simplex (K ranges from 2 to 5 across tasks), with the number of channels and classes being dynamically determined per dataset via learnable embeddings rather than fixed architecture dimensions.

## Method

FORMED repurposes the TimesFM forecasting backbone (frozen, 50 layers, D=1280) by replacing its prediction head with a novel attention-based classifier comprising three components: (1) Channel Embeddings (C×D learnable vectors broadcast-added to per-channel features to encode modality-specific spatial topology), (2) Label Queries (K·k learnable vectors, k per class in a Mixture-of-Experts style, that attend to flattened channel-aware features to gather class-specific evidence), and (3) a Shared Decoding Attention layer (single Transformer decoder with multi-head attention + FFN) whose parameters are shared across all datasets. Training follows a two-stage protocol: repurposing jointly optimizes SDA parameters plus per-dataset CEs and LQs across 5 MedTS datasets (learning domain-agnostic interaction patterns), then adapting freezes the SDA and backbone, training only new CEs and LQs (≈0.1% of total parameters) for unseen datasets. This decoupling allows the SDA to accumulate transferable medical domain knowledge while CEs and LQs handle task-specific configuration, enabling seamless adaptation to arbitrary channel counts, sequence lengths, and class numbers without architectural redesign.

## Results

FORMED achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 5 MedTS cohort datasets, outperforming 11 Task-Specific Models (TSMs) and 4 Task-Specific Adaptation (TSAs). Headline results include 66.83% accuracy / 63.66% F1 on ADFTD (3-class EEG), 95.74% / 92.53% on PTB (2-class ECG), and 77.72% / 70.12% on PTB-XL (5-class ECG). The paper reports up to 35% absolute F1 improvement over specialized baselines on ADFTD. On out-of-domain adapting (ECG200, StandWalkJump), FORMED with only k=16 label queries (≈0.1% of parameters) already outperforms the TimesFM-TSA baseline, and scales to 87.65% accuracy at k=1024 on ECG200.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FORMED outperforms all 11 TSM baselines (Autoformer, Crossformer, FEDformer, Informer, iTransformer, MTST, Nonformer, PatchTST, Reformer, Transformer, Medformer) on every dataset, with the largest gaps on ADFTD (+13.6 F1 over Medformer) and PTB-XL (+7.5 F1 over Crossformer). Against the 4 TSA baselines, FORMED also wins across the board; notably, TSA models generally underperform their TSM counterparts (e.g., Reformer-TSA drops to 25.31 F1 on ADFTD vs. Reformer TSM's 47.94), suggesting that simple task-specific heads on a shared backbone fail to navigate inter-dataset heterogeneity. The closest competitor is TimesFM-TSA (same backbone, CNN head), which reaches 94.05% accuracy on PTB but still trails FORMED by 1.7% accuracy and 4.2% F1. On the out-of-domain adapting task (ECG200, StandWalkJump), FORMED with k=16 queries outperforms TimesFM-TSA using only ~1/6 of the trainable parameters, and the gap widens with increasing k following a power-law scaling. Extended ablations (Section F) confirm that replacing the SDA classifier with a simple MLP or adding LoRA fine-tuning to TimesFM fails to close the gap (e.g., TimesFM+MLP reaches only 48.84% accuracy on ADFTD vs. FORMED's 66.83%), validating the necessity of the attention-based shared decoding architecture.

## Relation to Prior Work

FORMED carves new ground by introducing 'repurposing' as a distinct adaptation paradigm for time series foundation models, positioned between re-programming (which wraps a frozen backbone with task-specific adapters, losing generalizability) and full fine-tuning (which is data-greedy). The classifier architecture draws on DETR-style learnable queries (Carion et al., 2020) and few-shot classification ideas (Meng et al., 2023), but repurposes them for multichannel medical signals with a shared decoding attention layer trained jointly across a cohort of heterogeneous datasets. The backbone (TimesFM) is a forecasting model whose channel-independent design is bridged by the proposed Channel Embeddings and Label Queries, making this a genuine architectural departure from prior TSA approaches that simply attach CNN or MLP heads.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The two-stage repurposing-then-adapting framework offers a practical pathway for deploying medical time series classifiers in real-world settings where new diagnostic tasks emerge with limited data, requiring only ~0.1% of parameters to be trained. However, the validation is constrained to a single backbone (TimesFM) and a modest 5-dataset cohort, and the reported results exhibit very high variance (e.g., ±18–22 std in accuracy on ADFTD), which limits confidence in the magnitude of claimed improvements. Open questions include whether the framework generalizes to other foundation model backbones, irregularly sampled or categorical medical signals, and whether expanding the repurposing cohort would stabilize the learned domain representations.
