## Problem

Existing diffusion alignment methods—RL-based fine-tuning and direct backpropagation—suffer from reward over-optimization and mode collapse. RL approaches optimize a reverse-KL (mode-seeking) objective that concentrates on a single dominant mode, while backpropagation methods rely on brittle gradient signals from learned reward models. The paper addresses the need for a fine-tuning framework that maximizes external rewards while preserving the diversity and naturalness of the pretrained diffusion model, without requiring differentiable rewards.

## Input / Output

The method takes a pretrained diffusion model (Stable Diffusion v1.5 with LoRA rank-4 adapters for 512×512 images, or a Masked Diffusion Language Model for 200-bp DNA sequences), a set of conditioning prompts (40 animal prompts for images), and an arbitrary reward function (differentiable or not, e.g., LAION aesthetic score, Enformer activity prediction, or non-differentiable compressibility metrics). It outputs fine-tuned model parameters and, optionally, posterior samples obtained via test-time search. A key design choice is that the reward function need not be differentiable; when gradients are unavailable, the E-step falls back to pure importance sampling without gradient guidance.

## Method

DAV formulates diffusion alignment as variational EM over a KL-regularized RL objective, alternating between an E-step and an M-step. In the E-step, a soft Q-function (approximated via Tweedie's formula with a discount factor γ to attenuate early-step credit assignment) guides a two-stage test-time search: a gradient-guided proposal distribution constructed via first-order Taylor expansion of the reward, followed by importance sampling correction to approximate the true reward-tilted posterior. In the M-step, the model is updated by minimizing forward-KL divergence to the E-step samples—a mode-covering objective that contrasts with the mode-seeking reverse-KL used by RL-based baselines—effectively amortizing test-time search into the diffusion parameters. A DAV-KL variant adds a KL penalty to the pretrained policy to further prevent capability loss. The framework is modality-agnostic, extending naturally from continuous Gaussian diffusion to discrete masked diffusion by replacing the Gaussian proposal with a categorical logit-additive guidance term.

## Results

On text-to-image synthesis (Stable Diffusion v1.5, 40 animal prompts), DAV achieves an aesthetic score of 8.04 while maintaining an ImageReward of 0.95 (close to the pretrained 0.90), substantially outperforming DDPO (6.83 / 0.27), DRaFT (7.22 / 0.19), and TDPO (6.78 / 0.51). DAV-KL further improves ImageReward to 1.13 at a modest aesthetic cost (6.99), and DAV Posterior reaches the highest aesthetic score of 9.18. For DNA enhancer design, DAV achieves the best reward–diversity trade-off and the highest 3-mer correlation (naturalness) among all baselines, while also maintaining strong ATAC accessibility (biological validity).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against RL-based fine-tuning (DDPO), DAV wins on all three metrics: aesthetic 8.04 vs 6.83, LPIPS-A 0.53 vs 0.48, and ImageReward 0.95 vs 0.27, indicating DDPO suffers severe reward over-optimization. Against direct backpropagation (DRaFT), DAV again leads on all metrics (8.04 vs 7.22 aesthetic; 0.95 vs 0.19 ImageReward), confirming DRaFT's brittle gradient signal causes collapse. Against the gradient-free RL method TDPO, DAV improves aesthetic by 1.26 points and ImageReward by 0.44, while also preserving more diversity (LPIPS-A 0.53 vs 0.39). Against the test-time search baseline DAS, DAV Posterior achieves a substantially higher aesthetic score (9.18 vs 7.22) with only a slight diversity reduction (LPIPS-A 0.53 vs 0.65), and DAV-KL Posterior attains the best ImageReward (1.14 vs 1.07). For DNA sequence design, DAV outperforms both DRAKES (direct backpropagation) and VIDD (iterative distillation) on the reward–diversity Pareto front, achieving the highest 3-mer correlation (naturalness) and competitive ATAC accessibility, demonstrating that the forward-KL M-step preserves biological validity better than reverse-KL or reweighting-based alternatives.

## Relation to Prior Work

DAV reinterprets diffusion alignment through the lens of variational EM (Dayan & Hinton, 1997; Abdolmaleki et al., 2018), where the E-step performs posterior inference and the M-step amortizes the posterior into model parameters. The key departure from prior EM-based RL is replacing the standard reweighting-of-on-policy-samples E-step with a test-time search (gradient guidance + importance sampling) that more faithfully approximates the reward-tilted posterior. In the M-step, DAV uses forward-KL distillation (mode-covering) rather than the reverse-KL objective (mode-seeking) employed by RL-based fine-tuning methods like DDPO and DRaFT, directly targeting the diversity collapse problem. The framework also connects to the self-training paradigm (AlphaGo, STaR, ReST), where the E-step acts as expert rollout and the M-step as distillation, but is the first to formalize this cycle for diffusion model alignment.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

DAV provides a principled, unified framework that simultaneously optimizes reward and preserves diversity for both continuous and discrete diffusion models without requiring differentiable rewards, addressing a core tension that has limited prior fine-tuning methods. The modular E-step design means future advances in test-time search can be directly integrated without re-deriving the framework. Key limitations include the computational overhead of test-time search during training and the approximation error in the soft Q-function (via Tweedie's formula) at high-noise timesteps, which may guide exploration toward suboptimal regions. Open questions include whether distillation-based acceleration can reduce the number of denoising steps needed for accurate posterior mean estimation, and whether the EM iteration converges to a globally optimal aligned distribution or gets trapped in local optima.
