## Problem

Drag-based image editing has long suffered from distortions because the generative priors of UNet-based Stable Diffusion are insufficient to project optimized latents back onto the natural image manifold. Although the shift to DiT-based flow-matching models (e.g., FLUX, SD3.5) has yielded substantially stronger priors, drag editing has not benefited: directly applying point-based drag supervision to DiTs fails because their finer-grained, spatially precise features provide weak semantic evidence at individual points, and CFG distillation exacerbates inversion drift that undermines subject consistency.

## Input / Output

The user provides an RGB image, one or more source region masks (obtained via scribble or segmentation), and a target point per region that serves as the centroid of the desired destination; no explicit target mask is required. A multimodal LLM (GPT-5) then infers the editing intent (relocation, deformation, or rotation) and generates a natural-language prompt used as the text condition during latent optimization. The output is a full-resolution edited image in which the specified region has been moved, deformed, or rotated while the background remains pixel-identical outside an adaptive gradient mask.

## Method

DragFlow replaces point-wise motion supervision with region-level affine supervision: the source mask is progressively translated or rotated toward the target via a linear schedule over K iterations, and the L1 loss compares DiT features (extracted from the 17th–18th double-stream blocks of FLUX) over the entire source and target regions rather than at a single handle point, eliminating the need for brittle point tracking. Background preservation is enforced through a hard gradient mask that zeros out updates outside the editable region (derived from the union of source and target masks enclosed in a minimal rotated bounding rectangle), avoiding the weight-sensitivity of competing consistency losses. To counteract the large inversion drift of CFG-distilled FLUX, the framework injects a pretrained personalization adapter (InstantCharacter) as an auxiliary subject-representation extractor during inversion, substantially improving reconstruction fidelity and subject consistency without any fine-tuning. The entire pipeline operates in FLUX's latent space using FireFlow inversion (25 steps, 6 skipped, editing from step 19) with 70 optimization iterations at the 7th denoising step.

## Results

On the newly introduced ReD Bench, DragFlow achieves the best Mean Distance (MD₁ = 19.46, MD₂ = 4.48) and the best source-to-source fidelity (IF_s2s = 0.934), while also leading in source-to-target fidelity (IF_s2t = 0.958). On DragBench-DR, DragFlow similarly attains the lowest MD₁ (31.59) and MD₂ (5.93) and the best IF_s2s (0.941). The only metric where DragFlow trails is background fidelity (IF_bg = 0.992 on ReD Bench vs. 1.000 for RegionDrag), which the authors attribute to inherent inversion drift in the CFG-distilled FLUX backbone rather than a methodological weakness.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DragFlow outperforms all nine baselines on both spatial-accuracy metrics (MD₁ and MD₂) across both benchmarks, with the largest margins over DragNoise (MD₁: 19.46 vs. 45.46 on ReD Bench) and DragLoRA (MD₁: 31.59 vs. 42.03 on DragBench-DR). On source-to-target fidelity (IF_s2t), DragFlow edges out the closest competitor RegionDrag by 0.001 on ReD Bench (0.958 vs. 0.957) and by 0.001 on DragBench-DR (0.948 vs. 0.947 FastDrag). On source-to-source fidelity (IF_s2s, lower is better), DragFlow achieves the best score on both benchmarks (0.934 and 0.941), indicating the most effective removal of content from the original location. The sole metric where DragFlow trails is background fidelity (IF_bg): RegionDrag scores a perfect 1.000 because its copy-paste mechanism trivially preserves unedited pixels, whereas DragFlow's optimization-based approach incurs minor background perturbation (0.992 / 0.969) due to FLUX's CFG-distilled inversion drift. Among optimization-based baselines, GoodDrag is the closest competitor on MD₁ (20.38 vs. 19.46 on ReD Bench), but DragFlow still leads by ~5% and by a wider margin on all other metrics.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the optimization-based drag editing paradigm (DragDiffusion, FreeDrag, DragNoise, etc.) from UNet-based Stable Diffusion to DiT-based flow-matching models (FLUX), identifying that the architectural shift fundamentally breaks point-based supervision due to DiT's finer-grained, spatially precise features versus UNet's compressed bottleneck representations. It builds on FireFlow for rectified-flow inversion and repurposes pretrained personalization adapters (InstantCharacter) as auxiliary subject-representation extractors to counteract the larger inversion drift inherent in CFG-distilled models. The key conceptual departure is replacing point-wise motion supervision and tracking with region-level affine supervision, which provides richer semantic context and eliminates the brittle tracking requirement that plagues point-based methods on DiTs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to make drag-based editing practical with FLUX-class DiT backbones, unlocking substantially stronger generative priors that reduce distortion artifacts on complex, detail-rich images. The ReD Bench introduces region-level annotations with explicit task tags (relocation, deformation, rotation) that prior point-only benchmarks lack, enabling more faithful evaluation of spatially coherent edits. A key open question is whether the residual inversion drift of CFG-distilled FLUX (which limits IF_bg and causes detail loss on intricate structures) can be further mitigated by non-distilled DiT variants or improved inversion algorithms, and whether the region-based supervision principle generalizes to other DiT-based editing tasks beyond dragging.
