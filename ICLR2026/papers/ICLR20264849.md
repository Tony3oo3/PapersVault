## Problem

Autoregressive video diffusion models cannot condition on future camera poses, so when generating camera-guided videos along a predefined trajectory, they produce content unaware of upcoming camera positions, leading to collisions with the generated scene and rapid autoregressive collapse. Existing diffusion stitching methods that generate the full sequence in parallel require custom-trained models, making them infeasible for video generation where training cost is prohibitive.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264849/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a predefined camera trajectory (a sequence of 3D poses) and a pretrained Diffusion Forcing video model (U-ViT, 459M parameters, 8-frame context window, 256×256 resolution) as input, and produces a long camera-guided video (up to 120 frames in main experiments, 1080 frames in scaling demos) that is stable, collision-free, and closes loops. The approach is entirely training-free: it modifies only the sampling procedure, partitioning the target video into 4-frame chunks with 4-frame overlaps, and jointly denoises each target chunk alongside its temporal neighbors within the model's 8-frame context window.

## Method

GVS exploits the fact that Diffusion Forcing models already support joint denoising of signals at independent noise levels, providing the exact affordance needed for stitching without retraining. The target video is partitioned into non-overlapping chunks, and each target chunk is denoised jointly with its past and future neighbor chunks as a single input sequence, with only the target chunk's denoised output retained to update the global noisy estimate. Omni Guidance addresses the weak conditioning signal (all chunks share the same noise level) by modifying the score function to combine the joint-distribution prediction with a null-conditioned prediction, effectively strengthening adherence to the camera trajectory and consistency with neighbors. For long-range loop closure, cyclic conditioning alternates between temporal windows (immediate neighbors) and manually defined spatial windows (temporally distant but spatially close chunks) at each denoising step, ensuring the target chunk is conditioned on all relevant neighbors throughout the full denoising process. Partial stochasticity (η=0.9) is used to balance temporal consistency against oversmoothing.

## Results

On the Straight Line trajectory, GVS achieves a frame-to-frame consistency (F2FC) of 0.080, substantially outperforming Autoregressive sampling (0.138) and StochSync (0.124), while achieving zero collisions (CA=0) versus AR's 0.325. On Panorama 1-loop, GVS reduces long-range consistency (LRC) to 0.141 compared to AR's 0.339 and StochSync's 0.164. Across all seven camera trajectories, GVS achieves zero collisions in every case where collisions are measurable, while AR suffers collision rates of 0.075–0.775. Video quality metrics (IQ, AQ) are comparable across methods, with GVS achieving IQ of 0.615 on Straight Line and 0.621 on Stairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GVS outperforms History-Guided Autoregressive (AR) sampling on every metric across all trajectories: F2FC is improved by 20–40% (e.g., 0.080 vs. 0.138 on Straight Line), LRC is improved by 2–3× (e.g., 0.141 vs. 0.339 on Panorama 1-loop), and collision avoidance is perfect (CA=0) versus AR's 0.075–0.775 collision rates. Against StochSync, GVS achieves consistently better F2FC (e.g., 0.080 vs. 0.124 on Straight Line, 0.137 vs. 0.204 on Stairs) and LRC (e.g., 0.141 vs. 0.164 on Panorama 1-loop), while both methods achieve zero collisions. The paper notes that StochSync's zero-collision performance is achieved by 'shape-shifting' the scene, which is reflected in its poor temporal consistency. On video quality (IQ, AQ), GVS is comparable to or slightly below StochSync on some trajectories but substantially above AR, indicating that the consistency gains do not come at a significant quality cost.

## Relation to Prior Work

GVS extends diffusion stitching from robot planning (CompDiffuser, which requires a custom-trained denoising network) to camera-guided video generation by observing that Diffusion Forcing's per-token noise level mechanism—originally designed for autoregressive sampling—already provides the joint denoising of conditioning and target signals needed for stitching, eliminating the need for retraining. The Omni Guidance component generalizes Fractional History Guidance from autoregressive sampling to the stitching setting, where conditioning noise levels change throughout the denoising process rather than remaining fixed. StochSync's maximum stochasticity mechanism is adopted as a consistency-enhancing component, though the paper shows it is necessary but insufficient on its own due to oversmoothing.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GVS addresses a practical gap in camera-guided video generation: autoregressive sampling cannot plan ahead along a predefined trajectory, leading to collisions and collapse, while prior stitching methods either lack temporal consistency (StochSync) or require expensive custom training (CompDiffuser). The training-free, backbone-agnostic design makes it immediately applicable to any DF-trained video model, and the loop-closing mechanism enables long-range coherence for applications like one-shot cinematography and autonomous driving simulation. Key open questions include extending GVS to online/interactive settings (where future poses are unavailable), automating the currently manual spatial window definition for cyclic conditioning, and resolving the backbone's inability to handle wide-baseline viewpoints or propagate external context frames.
