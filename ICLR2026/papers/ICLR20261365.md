## Problem

Existing graph generative models either operate in discrete graph space with sequential construction or rely on Euclidean latent spaces that distort the hierarchical and tree-like structures prevalent in real-world graphs. While hyperbolic embeddings have been shown to better preserve such hierarchies for representation learning, graph generation in hyperbolic space remains largely unexplored, with no unified framework that performs both encoding and latent prior modeling entirely within the Poincaré ball.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261365/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes undirected graphs with one-hot node attributes and dense edge-type matrices as input. A key design choice is the node-only latent representation: each node is mapped to a single point in the Poincaré ball, and edge connectivity is treated as an emergent property of pairwise hyperbolic geometry rather than being explicitly encoded. At generation time, a random sample from a wrapped normal distribution in the Poincaré ball is transported via flow matching to a latent code, quantized to the nearest codebook entry, and decoded into node labels and edge types using pairwise geometric features (geodesic distance, log-map vectors, and angular relationships).

## Method

GGBall combines a Hyperbolic Vector-Quantized Autoencoder (HVQVAE) with a Riemannian flow matching prior, all operating within the Poincaré ball. The encoder stacks a Poincaré GNN (tangent-space message passing with distance-modulated scale/shift parameters) and a Poincaré Transformer (geodesic-distance attention scores with Möbius gyromidpoint value aggregation) to produce node embeddings, which are then quantized against a codebook initialized by hyperbolic k-means and updated via weighted Einstein midpoints. The latent prior is modeled by Riemannian conditional flow matching: a Poincaré DiT backbone parameterizes a vector field in the tangent space, trained to match geodesic interpolation paths between a wrapped-normal base distribution and the data distribution. The decoder reconstructs node attributes via tangent-space MLPs and edge types via a feature vector of pairwise hyperbolic geometric quantities, with a degree-edge consistency loss enforcing local structural coherence.

## Results

On hierarchical abstract graph generation, HVQVAE+Flow achieves the lowest average MMD of 0.0215 on Community-Small and 0.0112 on Ego-Small, reducing error by up to 18% over the strongest baseline HGDM (0.0240 / 0.0137). On QM9 molecular generation, HVQVAE+Flow attains the best novelty (93.77%) and highest V.U.N. score (85.34%), while maintaining 91.02% chemical validity. The one-shot HVQVAE alone already reduces average MMD by 93.3% over GraphVAE on Community-Small, demonstrating the strong inductive bias of the hyperbolic latent space.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GGBall (HVQVAE+Flow) outperforms all 10 baselines on average MMD for both Community-Small and Ego-Small. The closest competitor is HGDM (Wen et al., 2024), which uses a hybrid hyperbolic-Euclidean latent space; GGBall reduces average MMD from 0.0240 to 0.0215 on Community-Small and from 0.0137 to 0.0112 on Ego-Small. Among diffusion baselines, DiGress (0.0380) and GDSS (0.0460) are the strongest on Community-Small but still trail GGBall by 43% and 53% in average MMD, respectively. On QM9 molecular generation, GGBall achieves the highest novelty (93.77%) and V.U.N. (85.34%), surpassing GDSS (86.27% novelty, 81.31 V.U.N.) and HGDM (69.63% novelty, 66.40 V.U.N.); however, it trails CatFlow (99.81%) and DiGress (99.0%) on raw chemical validity, reflecting a deliberate validity–novelty trade-off that GGBall resolves in favor of overall V.U.N. score.

## Relation to Prior Work

GGBall unifies three previously separate research threads: hyperbolic representation learning (Nickel & Kiela, Chami et al.), vector-quantized autoencoders (Van Den Oord et al.), and Riemannian flow matching (Chen & Lipman, 2024), into a single fully hyperbolic generative pipeline. It departs from the concurrent HGDM (Wen et al., 2024), which uses a hybrid latent space with nodes in hyperbolic and edges in Euclidean space, by adopting a node-only representation where edge structure is an emergent property of node geometry. The architectural components (Poincaré FC layers, geodesic attention, Möbius gyromidpoint aggregation) extend prior Poincaré network building blocks (Shimizu et al., 2020; Ganea et al., 2018) from discriminative tasks to a generative setting, while the DiT-style time-conditioned modulation (Peebles & Xie, 2023) is adapted to operate entirely within the manifold.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a single, unified hyperbolic latent space can serve both hierarchical abstract graphs and molecular generation without task-specific architectural changes, suggesting hyperbolic geometry as a general-purpose inductive bias for structured data. The node-only design (edges as emergent from node geometry) is a clean conceptual simplification that eliminates the need for separate edge latent processes. However, evaluation is limited to small-scale benchmarks (Community-Small, Ego-Small, QM9), and open questions remain about scalability to larger graphs, conditional/property-guided generation, and whether the hyperbolic advantage persists in domains with weaker hierarchical structure. The numerical stability analysis (Lorentz model breakdown at 64D) provides practical guidance for practitioners choosing hyperbolic models.
