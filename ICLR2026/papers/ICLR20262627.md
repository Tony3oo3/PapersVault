## Problem

Existing LSSM learning algorithms (least-squares, subspace identification, EM-based MLE) cannot enforce sparsity on system matrices because the similarity transformation allows infinitely many equivalent realizations with different sparsity patterns, destroying the inherent topological structure among variables. This paper addresses the gap by learning LSSMs whose system matrices are both sparse and topologically faithful to the true system, enabling interpretable analysis of variable interactions.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262627/cropped_page7_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes paired time-series data {(u_t, y_t)}_{t=1}^T where u_t ∈ R^p is the input signal and y_t ∈ R^m is the noisy observation, with hidden states x_t ∈ R^n treated as latent variables. It outputs sparse system matrices A ∈ R^{n×n}, B ∈ R^{n×p}, C ∈ R^{m×n}, D ∈ R^{m×p} along with diagonal noise covariance matrices R and Q. The learned matrices are guaranteed to preserve the true topological structure up to a generalized permutation (row/column reordering and scaling), meaning nonzero entries appear in the correct positions even if numerical values differ by a constant factor.

## Method

The algorithm imposes a hierarchical Student's t-distribution prior (zero-mean Gaussian on each matrix element with an Inverse-Gamma hyperprior on its variance) on all four system matrices to promote sparsity, then performs MAP estimation via an EM framework where hidden states serve as latent variables. In the E-step, the Rauch-Tung-Striebel smoother provides closed-form posterior means and covariances for all hidden states given current parameter estimates. In the M-step, block coordinate descent yields row-wise analytical updates for A, B, C, D (each row updated by a weighted least-squares solve regularized by the prior), plus closed-form updates for R, Q, and the hyperparameter matrices Γ. A key theoretical insight is that the sparsity constraint restricts the similarity transformation to generalized permutation matrices, so the learned topology is preserved; global convergence to a local maximum or saddle point of the posterior is guaranteed by the Global Convergence Theorem.

## Results

On three real-world industrial process systems, the proposed method achieves the lowest Mean Relative Error (MRE) across all datasets: 14.93% on the Evaporation system, 23.63% on the Glass furnace, and 20.70% on the Steam generator. On the 10-dimensional synthetic system, it achieves 5.70% MRE and is the only method that recovers the correct topological structure of the system matrices (95% success rate over 20 independent trials, versus 0% for all classical baselines). The trade-off is computational cost: the proposed method requires 45–442 s per run, substantially longer than 4SID (0.29–0.58 s) or LSM_PEM (0.52–9.35 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all four baselines on MRE across every industrial dataset. The largest margins are against LSM_PEM on the Glass furnace (23.63% vs. 62.62%) and against 4SID on the Evaporation system (14.93% vs. 43.77%); LSM_HK diverges entirely (Inf) on the Evaporation system. The closest comparisons are against 4SID on the Glass furnace (23.63% vs. 24.32%) and against MLE on the Steam generator (20.70% vs. 22.23%), where the gap is under 2 percentage points. On the synthetic 10-dimensional system, the proposed method is the only algorithm that recovers the correct sparsity pattern (95% success rate over 20 trials), while all four baselines achieve 0% success in topology recovery. The primary cost is runtime: the proposed method is roughly 50–1500× slower than 4SID and 5–50× slower than LSM_PEM due to repeated matrix inversions in the closed-form updates.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the EM-based maximum likelihood estimation framework for LSSMs (Ghahramani & Hinton, 1996) by incorporating sparsity-promoting Student's t priors from Sparse Bayesian Learning (Tipping, 2001), shifting from MLE to MAP estimation. The key departure is that all prior EM-based LSSM methods (Gibson & Ninness, Martens, Umenberger) learn system matrices only up to similarity transformation and cannot enforce sparsity, while prior SBL applications to dynamical systems (Pan et al., Yuan et al., Wang et al.) assume states are measurable. Here, the hidden states are unobserved and corrupted by both process and measurement noise, making the combination of SBL priors with the RTS smoother and block coordinate descent a non-trivial extension.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The ability to recover the inherent topological structure of system matrices (not just produce a functionally equivalent but structurally meaningless realization) is critical for interpreting interaction laws in gene regulatory networks, communication systems, and industrial processes. The paper identifies the similarity transformation as the fundamental barrier preventing classical methods from recovering true sparsity patterns, and shows that sparsity-promoting priors restrict the transformation to generalized permutation matrices. Key limitations include cubic computational scaling that precludes large-scale problems, convergence only to local optima, and the fact that real-world validation lacks ground-truth structure for topology comparison. Future work on stochastic EM and additional structural constraints beyond sparsity could broaden applicability.
