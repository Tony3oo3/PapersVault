## Problem

GAN-based audio vocoders achieve fast one-step inference but suffer from slow adversarial convergence and mode collapse, while Flow Matching/diffusion vocoders train stably yet require many sampling steps for high fidelity. The paper addresses how to combine the stable generative learning of Flow Matching with the detail-refining power of GANs to produce high-quality audio in as few as one or two inference steps.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260029/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model accepts either log-Mel spectrograms (N-FFT=1024, hop=256, 100 bins) or EnCodec discrete audio tokens (latent dim 128, hop 320, supporting 1.5–12 kbps bandwidths) as conditioning, and outputs 24 kHz audio waveforms. Rather than operating on raw waveforms, the network processes complex STFT coefficients at three time-frequency resolutions per branch, and the condition encoder runs once per input with its features reused across all sampling steps. For token conditioning, a single unified model handles all bandwidths at inference time.

## Method

Flow2GAN uses a two-stage training strategy. In stage 1, Flow Matching is adapted for audio by (a) reformulating the objective from velocity estimation to direct endpoint prediction (predicting x₁ from x_t), which avoids the difficulty of estimating −x₀ in silent regions, and (b) applying a spectral energy-adaptive loss that scales prediction errors inversely to the square root of the reference spectrogram's power across both time and frequency bins, emphasizing perceptually salient quiet regions. In stage 2, the trained model is unrolled into fixed-step generators (1, 2, or 4 steps) and fine-tuned with a GAN objective using Multi-Period and Multi-Resolution discriminators, HingeGAN adversarial loss, L1 feature matching, and multi-scale Mel reconstruction loss. The backbone is a multi-branch ConvNeXt network where three branches process STFT coefficients at progressively finer resolutions (e.g., 512/256, 256/128, 128/64 for Mel conditioning), with larger embedding dimensions allocated to the lower-frame-rate branch, and branch outputs are summed after inverse STFT.

## Results

On the LibriTTS test set with Mel-spectrogram conditioning, Flow2GAN 4-step achieves PESQ 4.484, ViSQOL 4.986, V/UV F1 0.985, Periodicity 0.037, FSD 0.016, SMOS 4.60, and MOS 4.58, outperforming all baselines except BigVGAN-v2 (trained on a larger dataset) on FSD, SMOS, and MOS. The 1-step variant already reaches PESQ 4.189 and ViSQOL 4.957, surpassing Vocos, RFWave (10 steps), and WaveFM (1 step) on most metrics. On the universal audio token evaluation (EnCodec), Flow2GAN 4-step achieves PESQ 3.538 and FSD 0.557 at 12 kbps, outperforming PeriodWave-Turbo (4 steps) on FSD (0.557 vs 0.776) and SMOS (4.22 vs 4.32). Inference speed is 4.85×RT on CPU and 851.67×RT on GPU for the 1-step model, substantially faster than PeriodWave-Turbo (0.12×RT CPU) and RFWave (0.37×RT CPU).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Flow2GAN 4-step outperforms PeriodWave-Turbo (4 steps) on PESQ (4.484 vs 4.434), V/UV F1 (0.985 vs 0.958), Periodicity (0.037 vs 0.096), FSD (0.016 vs 0.020), SMOS (4.60 vs 4.20), and MOS (4.58 vs 4.38), while BigVGAN-v2 (trained on a larger dataset) remains marginally ahead on FSD (0.014 vs 0.016), SMOS (4.65 vs 4.60), and MOS (4.59 vs 4.58). The 1-step Flow2GAN decisively beats WaveFM (1 step) across all metrics (e.g., PESQ 4.189 vs 3.540, SMOS 4.44 vs 3.72) and Vocos (PESQ 4.189 vs 3.618, FSD 0.028 vs 0.042). The 2-step variant surpasses RFWave (10 steps) on every metric, most dramatically on FSD (0.023 vs 0.412) and Periodicity (0.044 vs 0.098), while using only 2 inference steps versus 10. On the EnCodec token evaluation at 12 kbps, Flow2GAN 4-step achieves the best FSD (0.557 vs 0.776 for PeriodWave-Turbo) and SMOS (4.22 vs 4.32), though PeriodWave-Turbo retains a slight edge on PESQ (3.579 vs 3.538) and ViSQOL (4.544 vs 4.531). Inference speed favors Flow2GAN strongly: the 1-step model runs at 4.85×RT on CPU versus 0.12×RT for PeriodWave-Turbo and 0.37×RT for RFWave, making it the fastest high-quality option tested.

## Relation to Prior Work

Flow2GAN sits at the intersection of two well-established audio generation paradigms: Flow Matching (Lipman et al., 2022) for stable generative pretraining and GAN-based vocoders (HiFi-GAN, BigVGAN) for high-fidelity one-step synthesis. Its architectural backbone extends Vocos (Siuzdak, 2023) from single-resolution to multi-resolution ConvNeXt processing of Fourier coefficients, while its training strategy is conceptually closest to PeriodWave-Turbo (Lee et al., 2024b), which also applies GAN fine-tuning to a Flow Matching pretrained model. The key departure is the reformulation of the Flow Matching objective from velocity estimation to direct endpoint prediction combined with a time-frequency spectral energy loss scaling, which the authors show provides substantially stronger pretrained weights for the subsequent GAN stage than standard Flow Matching.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: Flow2GAN achieves quality competitive with or exceeding 4-step PeriodWave-Turbo while running 40× faster on CPU (4.85×RT vs 0.12×RT for 1-step), making high-fidelity vocoding feasible on edge devices. The finding that the quality of the Flow Matching pretraining stage critically determines the ceiling of the GAN fine-tuned few-step generator (standard FM + GAN yields markedly worse 1-step results) suggests a broader principle for hybrid diffusion-GAN pipelines. Open questions include whether the endpoint estimation reformulation and spectral energy scaling transfer to other modalities (e.g., image generation), whether the multi-resolution design generalizes beyond three branches, and whether the GAN fine-tuning stage can be further compressed without quality loss.
