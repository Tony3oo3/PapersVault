## Problem

Masked diffusion models enable parallel token unmasking for faster inference, but effective parallel sampling faces a fundamental tension: simultaneously updated tokens must be conditionally independent, yet high-confidence predictions (which should be prioritized) tend to cluster and depend on each other. Existing training-free schedulers—confidence gating, fixed geometric patterns, and remasking—do not explicitly test for inter-token interference, leading to either conservative unmasking or quality degradation from correlated errors.

## Input / Output

At each denoising step, PUNT receives the current partially masked sequence x, a vector of candidate tokens y sampled for all masked positions, and a scalar independence threshold ε. It returns a certified ordered set R ⊆ M of masked indices whose tokens are contextually independent given the unmasked context, safe to unmask simultaneously. The method is training-free and model-agnostic, requiring only O(log|M|) forward passes per step (e.g., ~10 passes for 1024 tokens) rather than O(|M|) for fully sequential unmasking. The binary encoding of each position's rank in the confidence-ordered list pre-determines the entire recursive partitioning structure, so no per-step bookkeeping beyond the forward passes is needed.

## Method

PUNT (Parallel Unmasking with Non-influence Tests) replaces the intractable full conditional independence check with contextual independence: a token is safe to unmask in parallel if its conditional distribution at the current sampled point is unchanged by the other tokens being revealed simultaneously. The algorithm uses a divide-and-conquer strategy where, at each recursion level, the confidence-ordered candidate set is split into an 'anchor' half (higher confidence) and a 'test' half; test tokens whose KL divergence from the baseline distribution exceeds ε when conditioned on the anchor tokens are pruned. A key efficiency trick converts the recursive procedure into an iterative one via binary encoding of positions: all independence tests at a given recursion depth are batched into a single forward pass by testing each token against the union of all anchor tokens at that level, relying on an independence stability assumption (justified by the non-negativity of transformer attention weights) to guarantee correctness. This yields O(log|M|) forward evaluations per denoising step while preserving the guarantee that the returned set satisfies contextual independence, and it induces an emergent hierarchical generation pattern where structural tokens are revealed before local details.

## Results

On the IFEval instruction-following benchmark (Dream 7B), PUNT achieves up to 16% higher accuracy than all baseline samplers, including sequential one-by-one generation, while requiring substantially fewer forward evaluations (NFEs). On MTBench (long-form, 1024 tokens), PUNT consistently outperforms EB-Sampler, Dilated-Sampler, and Top-k across the low-to-mid NFE regime, with the gap narrowing only at very high NFE budgets (≥400). On short-answer tasks (GSM8K, HumanEval, MBPP with ≤512 tokens), PUNT is competitive on NFE but clearly outperforms baselines when measured by denoising steps. On de novo membrane protein generation (MemDLM, 1024 amino acids), PUNT produces proteins with comparable or marginally higher mean pLDDT than baselines at the same NFE budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the EB-Sampler (entropy-bounded unmasking, Patel et al. 2025), PUNT wins decisively on long-form generation: on IFEval it reaches up to 16% higher inst-level loose accuracy, and on MTBench it achieves higher GPT-4o mean scores at the same or fewer NFEs. On short-answer MBPP (LLaDA, temp 0.7), PUNT's NFE-vs-accuracy curve aligns closely with EB, but PUNT outperforms when measured by denoising steps. Against the Dilated-Sampler (geometry-aware spacing, Luxembourg et al. 2025), PUNT dominates in the low-to-mid NFE regime on both IFEval and MTBench; at very high NFE budgets (≥400 on MTBench) the dilated scheduler can close the gap because its fixed-geometry schedule benefits from many denoising steps. Against APD (Adaptive Parallel Decoding, Israel et al. 2025, evaluated only on Dream 7B), PUNT achieves the highest IFEval scores across all temperatures, while APD shows the strongest HumanEval performance and surpasses PUNT on GSM8K at temperature 0.7. Overall, PUNT's advantage is most pronounced on long-sequence, alignment-sensitive tasks where its adaptive independence testing avoids the rigid schedules of dilated sampling and the conservative small-batch unmasking of EB.

## Relation to Prior Work

PUNT departs from the dominant paradigms for accelerating masked diffusion model (MDM) inference—confidence-based gating (EB-Sampler), fixed geometric spacing (Dilated scheduling), and remasking—by introducing an explicit, sequence-specific conditional independence test as the criterion for safe parallel unmasking. Conceptually, it repurposes the statistical notion of contextual independence (a pointwise, rather than distribution-wide, independence condition) and grounds it in transformer attention weights, showing that negligible attention to a token set implies approximate conditional independence. The divide-and-conquer algorithm achieving O(log m) model calls per step is a new algorithmic contribution, though the underlying idea of batched testing against anchor sets is a natural extension of standard independence-testing logic.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PUNT offers a principled, training-free mechanism to resolve the core speed-quality tension in MDM inference, and the observed emergent hierarchical generation (structure before detail) suggests that independence-aware scheduling can induce planning-like behavior without explicit architectural changes. However, the method's overhead of ⌈log₂|M|⌉ forward passes per step makes it less competitive on short sequences and at very high NFE budgets, and the independence stability assumption, while well-motivated for transformers, remains an approximation. Open questions include adaptive ε schedules, distilling the independence test into a single-pass predictor, and whether the hierarchical generation phenomenon generalizes beyond the tested architectures and tasks.
