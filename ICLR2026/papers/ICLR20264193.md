## Problem

Continuous representation FWI (CR-FWI), which reparameterizes the velocity model with a coordinate-based neural network, empirically shows greater robustness to poor initial models but slower high-frequency convergence compared to conventional FWI, yet the underlying mechanism explaining this trade-off remained unclear. No unified theoretical framework existed to connect the representation choice to convergence behavior in this PDE-constrained nonlinear inverse problem.

## Input / Output

The method consumes observed seismic data (surface-receiver seismograms generated by Ricker wavelets at 8–10 Hz), an initial velocity model (smooth, linear, or constant), and acquisition geometry (shot/receiver spacing, PML boundary conditions), and produces a continuous subsurface velocity model over the spatial domain. The wave equation is solved at each iteration via 8th-order finite difference with PML absorbing boundaries, and the velocity model is parameterized as a continuous function m_θ(x) = F_θ(x) + m_0(x) rather than a discrete grid, so the output is a differentiable field queryable at any spatial coordinate.

## Method

The paper introduces a wave-based neural tangent kernel (NTK) that unifies conventional FWI (recovered as a wave kernel with a Dirac delta in model space) and CR-FWI (where the Dirac is replaced by the smooth NTK of the coordinate network). Two key theorems show that (i) the wave-based NTK is non-stationary at initialization and during training due to FWI's inherent nonlinearity, and (ii) its eigenvalue decay is strictly faster than the wave kernel's, which explains why CR-FWI adaptively inverts low frequencies first (robustness) but converges slowly at high frequencies (spectral bias). Building on this, the authors propose three new CR-FWI representations: LR-FWI (low-rank tensor factorization with 1D INRs), MPE-FWI (multigrid hash encoding + lightweight MLP, proven to have slower eigenvalue decay than INR), and IG-FWI (a hybrid that splices INR features with multigrid hash features at a tunable ratio α, yielding eigenvalue decay strictly between INR and MPE). IG-FWI thus inherits INR's robustness to constant initial models while recovering MPE's faster high-frequency convergence, achieving the best MSE across all tested scenarios in Tables 1 and 5.

## Results

On the 2004 BP model with a constant initial model, IG-FWI achieves the lowest MSE of 0.0843, outperforming WinFWI (0.1083), LR-FWI (0.1248), IFWI (0.1412), and ADFWI (0.4281). On the Marmousi model under 8σ₀ Gaussian noise, IG-FWI attains MSE 0.2151 versus 0.3374 (IFWI), 0.3547 (MS-FWI), and 0.5422 (ADFWI). With smooth initial models, IG-FWI (0.1423) and MPE-FWI (0.1427) achieve the best MSE on Marmousi, while LR-FWI (0.0481) leads on 2004 BP. On the OpenFWI benchmark, IG-FWI and LR-FWI reduce MSE by 3–4× compared to the data-driven InversionNet baseline (e.g., 24.61 vs. 100.09 on CurveFault).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against conventional FWI baselines (ADFWI, ADFWI-TV, WECI-FWI, MS-FWI), IG-FWI achieves 3–7× lower MSE under constant initial models (e.g., 0.2961 vs. 1.1522 on Marmousi) and 2–3× lower MSE under degraded data (noise, missing low frequencies, sparse shots), confirming that conventional methods remain highly sensitive to initial model accuracy and data quality. Among existing CR-FWI methods, IG-FWI outperforms IFWI (Sun et al., 2023a) and WinFWI (Yang & Ma, 2025) in nearly all scenarios, with the largest gains on constant initial models (e.g., 0.0843 vs. 0.1412 and 0.1083 on 2004 BP) and degraded data (0.1654 vs. 0.3483 and 0.3239 on sparse shots). MPE-FWI achieves the best MSE with smooth initial models (0.1427 on Marmousi, 0.0586 on 2004 BP) but degrades catastrophically with constant initial models (2.2266 on Marmousi, 1.6024 on 2004 BP), revealing its lack of robustness. LR-FWI provides a strong alternative with competitive robustness (0.2893 on Marmousi constant) and the best smooth-model accuracy on 2004 BP (0.0481), though it is slightly outperformed by IG-FWI on most degraded-data scenarios. On the OpenFWI data-driven benchmark, both IG-FWI and LR-FWI substantially outperform InversionNet (Wu & Lin, 2019b), reducing MSE from 100.09 to 24.61 (CurveFault) and from 113.18 to 19.59 (FaltFault), demonstrating that physics-informed unsupervised CR-FWI can surpass supervised data-driven approaches.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the standard neural tangent kernel (NTK) framework (Jacot et al., 2018) to PDE-constrained nonlinear inverse problems, introducing a 'wave-based NTK' that unifies conventional FWI (via a Dirac kernel) and continuous-representation FWI (via a smooth architecture-dependent kernel). It builds directly on INR-based FWI methods (IFWI, WinFWI) and generalizes them to a broader CR-FWI family incorporating low-rank tensor decomposition and multigrid parametric encoding (Muller et al., 2022; Audia et al., 2025). The key conceptual departure from standard NTK theory is the demonstration that the wave-based NTK is non-stationary—neither at initialization nor during training—due to the inherent nonlinearity of the wave equation, in contrast to the deterministic kernel limit of classical NTK.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first NTK-based theoretical framework for FWI, providing a quantitative eigenvalue-decay explanation for the long-observed robustness-convergence trade-off between conventional and INR-based FWI. The proposed IG-FWI hybrid representation offers a practical path to balancing both properties, validated across multiple benchmark models and challenging data scenarios. Open questions include: a rigorous eigenvalue decay proof for LR-FWI (currently only empirical), extension of the dynamic NTK analysis to fully nonlinear operators via stochastic methods, and whether the framework generalizes to elastic FWI or 3D problems at scale. The non-constancy result also opens a broader research direction for NTK analysis of PDE-constrained inverse problems beyond FWI.
