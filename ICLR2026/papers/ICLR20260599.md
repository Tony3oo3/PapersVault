## Problem

Existing reward models for role-playing dialogue suffer from two systematic biases—role bias (inconsistent scoring for lesser-known characters) and reference bias (evaluation quality collapses without character-specific context)—because they assess responses in isolation without grounded reasoning over character intent and narrative background. This makes them unreliable supervisors for training dialogue agents that must sustain persona fidelity, emotional nuance, and narrative coherence across diverse literary characters.

## Input / Output

The reward model receives a dialogue prompt paired with two candidate responses and produces a structured multi-step reasoning chain (with interleaved <search>/<result> retrieval operations) culminating in a boxed preference judgment across three evaluation dimensions: conversational competence, character alignment, and expressive quality. The role-playing agent takes a user query conditioned on a character profile and generates a dialogue response, also with interleaved retrieval calls into a hierarchical character-specific knowledge base built from segmented narrative sources. Retrieved content is masked during gradient computation to prevent overfitting to externally generated text.

## Method

R4 unifies reasoning and retrieval across both the reward model and the dialogue agent, trained end-to-end with GRPO. The reward model is optimized with a rule-based reward combining format compliance, answer accuracy, and a gated multiplicative consistency term (consistency only enhances reward for correct answers), while the agent is trained using pairwise preference scores computed by the reward model over groups of G sampled responses. A character-specific knowledge construction pipeline (narrative segmentation → four-agent synthesis: extraction, perspective transformation, theory-of-mind reasoning, dialogue extraction) builds a hierarchically indexed knowledge base that both components query dynamically during reasoning. Ablations reveal a multiplicative interaction between reward quality and agent capability: removing reasoning from the reward model causes a larger performance collapse than removing it from the agent, and generic retrieval is worse than no retrieval, underscoring that precisely curated character knowledge is essential.

## Results

On CharacterEval, R4-32B-Instruct achieves the highest Character Consistency average (64.64) and Role-Playing Attractiveness average (64.93), outperforming the best specialized baseline BC-NPC-Turbo (55.28 and 58.93 respectively) by +9.36 and +6.00 points. R4-32B also leads in Conversational Ability (78.90 avg), surpassing BC-NPC-Turbo (75.95) and MiniMax (75.68). The 7B variant (R4-7B-Instruct) already exceeds all baselines in Character Consistency (55.74) and Role-Playing Attractiveness (60.95), demonstrating the framework's effectiveness even at smaller scale. Human evaluation confirms these gains, with R4-32B ranked first in 68.2% of 500 dialogue instances versus 21.6% for GPT-4o.

## Baselines & Comparisons

R4-32B-Instruct outperforms all 16 baselines across every category. Against specialized role-playing models, R4-32B leads BC-NPC-Turbo by +9.36 in Character Consistency (64.64 vs. 55.28) and +6.00 in Role-Playing Attractiveness (64.93 vs. 58.93), with particularly large gains in Persona Behavior (+9.8) and Empathy (+5.40). Against reasoning models, the gap is even starker: DeepSeek-R1 scores only 39.84 in Character Consistency and 47.05 in RP Attractiveness, confirming that general reasoning capability does not transfer to character portrayal. Against instruction models, R4-32B surpasses Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct (its own base) by +11.24 in Character Consistency and +6.98 in RP Attractiveness, showing the RL+retrieval training adds substantial value. In the ablation (Table 3), replacing R4's reward model with ScalarRM, GenRM, or CharacterRM drops Character Consistency to 43.89–46.87, well below even R4's ablated variants, confirming that the co-designed reasoning-retrieval reward is essential rather than interchangeable.

## Relation to Prior Work

R4 builds directly on the GRPO training framework from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) and the iterative search-reasoning paradigm of ReSearch and Search-R1, but applies it to a novel dual-component setting: both the reward model and the dialogue agent are trained with reasoning-augmented retrieval via reinforcement learning. It departs from prior reward modeling work (ScalarRM, Generative Verifiers, CharacterRM) by reformulating preference evaluation as a multi-step structured reasoning task grounded in retrieved character-specific knowledge, rather than as classification or free-form generation. The character-specific knowledge construction pipeline draws on LLM-guided narrative segmentation (LumberChunker) and hierarchical event-tree representations, extending RAG from static one-shot retrieval to dynamic, multi-hop character-context access.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and quantifies two concrete failure modes (role bias and reference bias) in existing reward models for role-playing, providing a diagnostic framework that goes beyond 'our method is better.' The ablation finding that reward model quality is the binding constraint (removing reasoning from the RM drops performance to 49.87 vs. 56.94 for the agent) offers a practical design principle for role-playing system builders. However, the approach is validated primarily on Chinese literary characters (Dragon Raja, Harry Potter) with a GPT-4o-dependent knowledge pipeline, raising open questions about scalability to diverse character types, non-literary personas, and the computational cost of maintaining the dual reasoning-retrieval loop at inference time.
