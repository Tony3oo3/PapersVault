## Problem

While gradient descent convergence rates are well understood under small learning rates, the asymptotic directional structure of GD trajectories—specifically, the direction in which iterates approach a minimum—remains uncharacterized. This gap is particularly acute for learning rates near the stability boundary, where the Edge of Stability phenomenon reveals non-monotonic sharpness dynamics that existing analyses do not explain from a directional perspective.

## Input / Output

The theory takes a C³ differentiable loss function with an isolated local minimum whose Hessian has distinct positive eigenvalues, a scalar learning rate η, and an initial point x₀ in the convergence basin. The output is a unit vector (or alternating unit vector) describing the asymptotic convergence direction, determined entirely by the eigenvector associated with either the smallest or largest Hessian eigenvalue depending on the η regime. The result holds for almost all initial points, with the exceptional set having Lebesgue measure zero.

## Method

The core result (Theorem 1) proves that for GD on a locally strongly convex C³ function, the normalized trajectory (x_k − x*)/‖x_k − x*‖ either converges to a fixed unit vector (when 0 < η < 2/(λ₁+λₙ)) or the alternating sequence (−1)^k(x_k − x*)/‖x_k − x*‖ converges (when 2/(λ₁+λₙ) < η < 2/λₙ). The proof proceeds by linearizing the GD map around the minimum, establishing a forward-invariant ball via a perturbation argument, then showing inductively that the component along the slowest-decaying eigendirection (smallest eigenvalue for small η, largest eigenvalue for large η) dominates all others at a geometric rate. For the oscillatory regime, the sign-alternating component corresponding to the largest eigenvalue drives the direction, producing sharpness oscillations that the paper connects to the Edge of Stability. Empirically, the authors verify that analogous directional alignment (measured via cosine similarity of consecutive updates) also emerges for SGD with momentum and Adam on CIFAR-10 CNN training.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that for a C³ loss with distinct Hessian eigenvalues at a local minimum, GD trajectories converge along a fixed direction when 0 < η < 2/(λ₁+λₙ), or oscillate along a specific line when 2/(λ₁+λₙ) < η < 2/λₙ. Experimentally, on CIFAR-10 with a CNN, the cosine similarity between consecutive parameter updates (cos⟨Δx_{k+1}, Δx_k⟩) tends toward 1 for GD, SGD with momentum, and Adam as training loss stabilizes, confirming that directional alignment is not unique to vanilla GD. The sharpness oscillation analysis shows that under large learning rates, the maximum Hessian eigenvalue λₙ(x_k) oscillates around its asymptotic value and can transiently exceed the 2/η threshold, providing a mechanistic explanation for Edge of Stability behavior.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a theoretical paper without a standard benchmark comparison against competing optimization methods. The primary 'comparison' is between two learning-rate regimes: below the critical threshold 2/(λ₁+λₙ), GD converges monotonically along a fixed eigenvector direction (the smallest-eigenvalue direction), while above it (but below 2/λₙ), the trajectory oscillates along the sharpest direction with the maximum Hessian eigenvalue fluctuating around 2/η. The paper extends the continuous-time Gradient Conjecture (Parusinski et al., 2000) to discrete GD, showing that the discrete setting admits an additional oscillatory mode absent in the continuous flow. Empirically, SGD with momentum and Adam on CIFAR-10 exhibit the same directional alignment (cosine similarity → 1) as vanilla GD, suggesting the phenomenon is robust across optimizers rather than an artifact of deterministic updates. No method is shown to outperform another; the contribution is a unifying directional characterization applicable across all three optimizers.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-time Gradient Conjecture (Parusinski et al., 2000), which guarantees a well-defined convergence direction for gradient flow trajectories near critical points, to the discrete-time GD setting. The proof technique builds directly on the forward-invariance results of Chen et al. (2024) for the unstable convergence regime of GD. A key departure from prior work is the learning-rate-dependent dichotomy: the paper shows that the convergence direction is not merely fixed (as in the continuous-time case) but can become oscillatory when the learning rate exceeds 2/(λ₁+λₙ), a structural feature absent from the Gradient Conjecture. The oscillatory regime is further connected to the Edge of Stability literature (Cohen et al., 2021), providing a geometric explanation for the sharpness fluctuations observed empirically.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The result provides the first rigorous characterization of GD's asymptotic directional geometry, filling a gap between continuous-time gradient flow theory and discrete optimization dynamics. The oscillatory convergence at large learning rates offers a new theoretical lens on EoS sharpness oscillations, potentially guiding the design of learning-rate schedules or optimizers that exploit directional alignment. However, the theory is restricted to locally strong convex functions and deterministic GD; the SGD and Adam experiments (single CIFAR-10 CNN, cosine-similarity metric) are suggestive but far from a rigorous extension. Open questions include whether directional convergence persists in genuinely non-convex landscapes, how momentum or adaptive methods modify the oscillatory structure, and whether quantitative convergence-speed estimates along the identified direction can be derived.
