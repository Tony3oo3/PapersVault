## Problem

Directed graph generation remains underexplored for two reasons: edge directionality dramatically enlarges the learnable dependency space (e.g., 218 possible digraphs vs. 11 undirected graphs on 4 nodes), causing naive extensions of undirected architectures to fail, and the absence of standardized benchmarks prevents rigorous evaluation of generative quality. Existing DAG-specific methods rely on proxy downstream metrics rather than direct distributional assessment.

## Input / Output

At training, the model receives a noised directed graph G_t (with categorical node types and per-ordered-pair edge types, where (i,j) and (j,i) are independent variables each including an 'absent' class) plus a time scalar t and positional encodings, and predicts the clean node and edge distributions. At inference, it starts from a random graph with a node count sampled from the training set and iteratively denoises over a CTMC trajectory; optionally, a graph-level conditioning signal (e.g., execution time) is injected via classifier-free guidance. Outputs are directed graphs of small-to-medium scale (up to ~200 nodes) with typed nodes and edges.

## Method

DIRECTO is the first discrete flow matching (DFM) generative model for directed graphs, combining three directionality-aware components within a graph transformer denoiser. The core architectural innovation is a dual attention block that computes two separate attention maps—source-to-target (Q_S·K_T) and target-to-source (Q_T·K_S)—using role-specific projections, then concatenates and jointly softmaxes them to produce unified asymmetric aggregation weights over both directions. Direction-aware positional encodings (Magnetic Laplacian, Multi-q MagLap, or Directed RRWP) are appended to node and edge features to inject asymmetric structural signals that standard Laplacian PEs discard. FiLM layers modulate edge features with attention signals and global graph features, while PNA pooling aggregates multi-scale neighborhood statistics to update global representations. Training uses a cross-entropy loss over node and edge predictions at random time steps; sampling proceeds via a learned CTMC rate matrix derived from the denoising network's predictions, with post-training optimizations (time distortion, target guidance, stochasticity control) available without retraining.

## Results

On the synthetic ER-DAG dataset, DIRECTO (RRWP) achieves a V.U.N. of 94.0% and a Ratio of 1.7, while DIRECTO (MagLap) achieves the best Ratio of 1.3 with 92.0% V.U.N. On the SBM dataset, DIRECTO reaches up to 99.5% V.U.N. with a Ratio of 1.8. On real-world TPU Tiles, DIRECTO (MagLap) achieves the lowest Ratio (44.0) and RBF MMD (0.042), while on Visual Genome, DIRECTO (RRWP) attains the highest V.U.N. (83.8%) and lowest RBF MMD (0.038), outperforming all baselines across every dataset and metric.

## Baselines & Comparisons

DIRECTO outperforms DeFoG (the strongest undirected flow-matching baseline adapted to directed graphs) on every dataset: on ER-DAG, V.U.N. improves from 75.0% to 94.0% (RRWP) or 92.0% (MagLap); on SBM, from 37.0% to 99.5%; on TPU Tiles, RBF MMD drops from 0.059 to 0.042–0.044; and on Visual Genome, V.U.N. rises from 50.8% to 83.8% with RBF MMD halved from 0.085 to 0.038. Against DiGress (discrete diffusion), the gains are even more pronounced, with V.U.N. jumping from 34.0% to 94.0% on ER-DAG and from 0.3% to 83.8% on Visual Genome. On TPU Tiles, LayerDAG achieves a higher V.U.N. (98.5% vs. 77–80.5%) because it enforces acyclicity by construction, but its Ratio is an order of magnitude worse (413.6 vs. 44.0 for DIRECTO MagLap), indicating poor distributional fidelity. D-VAE is effectively non-functional in the directed setting, producing 0% V.U.N. on ER-DAG and failing to run within a reasonable timeframe on TPU Tiles. Overall, DIRECTO achieves the best or near-best score on every metric across all five datasets, with the dual attention mechanism and direction-aware positional encodings being the key differentiators over simply adapting undirected architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

DIRECTO extends the discrete flow matching framework for graph generation (DeFoG, Qin et al. 2025; Gat et al. 2024) from undirected to directed graphs, adding two directionality-aware components: a dual attention mechanism adapted from Directed Graph Transformers (Wang et al., 2024) and direction-sensitive positional encodings (Magnetic Laplacian, Multi-q MagLap, Directed RRWP) borrowed from the discriminative directed-graph literature (Geisler et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2025). Prior directed graph generation work has been largely restricted to DAGs via autoregressive or VAE-based methods (D-VAE, LayerDAG), with only a very recent autoregressive approach for general digraphs (Law et al., 2025); DIRECTO is the first to apply iterative refinement / flow matching to general directed graphs. The paper also departs from prior evaluation practice, which relied on downstream-task proxies, by introducing a dedicated benchmark suite with distributional and structural metrics tailored to digraphs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses two concrete gaps in directed graph generation: the lack of a generative model that handles general (non-acyclic) digraphs via iterative refinement, and the absence of standardized benchmarks. The ablation showing that simply scaling an adapted undirected model (doubling depth) collapses to 0% V.U.N. on SBM while the dual-attention variant reaches 99.5% provides strong evidence that directionality requires explicit architectural treatment rather than implicit learning. Open questions include scalability beyond ~200 nodes, whether implicit constraint learning (e.g., acyclicity) can be replaced by explicit projection methods for stricter domain validity, and whether the dual-attention architecture transfers to discriminative directed-graph tasks. The released benchmark suite should lower the barrier for future directed graph generation research.
