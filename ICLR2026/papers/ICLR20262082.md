## Problem

Existing text-to-motion generation methods model spatial, temporal, and frequency domains in isolation or at most in pairs, lacking a unified framework that jointly optimizes all three. Additionally, motion-irrelevant cues introduced by noise become entangled with beneficial features during multi-domain modeling, causing motion distortion and degrading generation fidelity.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262082/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a free-form text prompt, encoded by a pretrained DistilBERT into sentence-level (CLS) and word-level (τ) features, and produces a 3D human motion sequence as joint coordinate trajectories. The motion is internally represented in a 2D spatial-temporal format (M joints × N downsampled frames × 12-dim features) after temporal downsampling by a factor of s (7 for base, 4 for large), and diffusion denoising operates directly in this raw motion space over 50 timesteps with classifier-free guidance at scale 4.0.

## Method

TriC-Motion is a diffusion-based denoiser built on MDM that stacks J=4 identical blocks, each containing three parallel domain-specific modules: Temporal Motion Encoding (a Transformer encoder over frames), Spatial Topology Modeling (a 3-layer GCN over the joint graph), and Hybrid Frequency Analysis (DWT decomposition into low/high bands, with FFT applied to the low band and specialized convolutional branches for each). A Score-guided Tri-domain Fusion (S-Fus) module adaptively weights the three domain features via dual-branch motion and semantic scoring before cross-attention injects word-level text features. The key novelty is the Causality-based Counterfactual Motion Disentangler (CCMD), applied only during training, which uses a structured causal model to separate each domain's features into factual (motion-relevant) and counterfactual (confounding) components via lightweight pooling-and-gating networks, then performs a do() intervention to subtract confounders. Training combines a standard diffusion denoising loss, a layer-weighted causal loss (L_fcf) that supervises the disentangled features toward ground truth, and a perceptual loss in a pretrained motion encoder space.

## Results

On HumanML3D, TriC-Motion (large) achieves a state-of-the-art R@1 of 0.612, R@2 of 0.806, and R@3 of 0.885, surpassing the previous best (SALAD) by +0.031 in R@1. It also attains the best MM-Dist of 2.463, a 0.184 improvement over SALAD (2.649), indicating substantially better text-motion semantic alignment. On SnapMoGen, TriC-Motion reaches R@1 of 0.907 and R@3 of 0.985, outperforming MoMask++ (R@1=0.802) by a large margin of +0.105. However, TriC-Motion's FID (0.347 on HumanML3D, 26.35 on SnapMoGen) remains higher than several baselines such as SALAD (0.076) and MoMask++ (15.06), suggesting room for improvement in distributional fidelity.

## Baselines & Comparisons

TriC-Motion decisively outperforms all 15 baselines on R-Precision (R@1, R@2, R@3) and MM-Dist on HumanML3D, with the closest competitor being SALAD (R@1=0.581, MM-Dist=2.649), which TriC-Motion surpasses by +0.031 and −0.184 respectively. On SnapMoGen, the advantage is even more pronounced: TriC-Motion (R@1=0.907) beats MoMask++ (R@1=0.802) by +0.105, a gap far larger than on HumanML3D, suggesting the tri-domain + causal design is particularly effective for longer, more complex prompts. The main trade-off is FID: TriC-Motion's FID of 0.347 (base) / 0.285 (large) on HumanML3D is notably higher than SALAD (0.076), LaMP (0.032), and MoGenTS (0.033), and on SnapMoGen its FID of 26.35 trails MoMask++ (15.06) and MARDM (26.88 is close). Diversity scores are slightly lower than several baselines (e.g., SALAD 9.696 vs. TriC-Motion 9.428), though the gap is modest. In the user study, TriC-Motion receives the highest human preference scores (4.125 for text-motion alignment, 3.981 for overall quality) compared to SALAD (3.518, 3.567), MARDM (2.970, 3.268), and MoMask (3.096, 3.289), confirming that the quantitative R-Precision gains translate to perceptual quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

TriC-Motion builds directly on MDM's diffusion framework and MoGenTS's 2D spatial-temporal motion representation, extending the dominant temporal-modeling paradigm by adding parallel spatial (GCN-based) and frequency (DWT+FFT) branches within a single denoiser block. The key conceptual departure is the first application of structured causal models (SCM) to motion generation: the CCMD module performs do-calculus-style intervention during training to disentangle motion-irrelevant confounders from beneficial causal features across all three domains. Prior work has addressed spatial, temporal, or frequency modeling in isolation (e.g., Spatio-Temporal Graph Diffusion, DiffusionPhase, FTMoMamba), but no prior method jointly optimizes all three within one architecture or applies causal disentanglement to the generative process.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The tri-domain unification provides a principled recipe for motion generation that captures temporal dynamics, skeletal topology, and multi-granularity frequency structure simultaneously, and the causal intervention offers a general mechanism for suppressing noise-induced confounders that could transfer to other generative tasks. However, the FID (0.347) remains substantially higher than several baselines (e.g., LaMP at 0.032, MoGenTS at 0.033), suggesting the tri-domain modeling improves semantic alignment at the cost of distributional fidelity. Open questions include whether the approach extends to latent-space diffusion for faster inference, whether the causal module's benefits persist at scale, and whether the tri-domain fusion strategy generalizes beyond human motion to other structured sequence generation.
