## Problem

For entropy-regularised MDPs in general (continuous) state and action spaces, the KL divergence regulariser is unbounded, so it is not clear a priori that a coupled actor-critic gradient flow avoids finite-time blow-up. Prior convergence results for entropy-regularised actor-critic methods (e.g., Cayci et al.) relied on finite action spaces where the KL divergence is uniformly bounded. This paper asks whether a two-timescale actor-critic flow with mirror-descent policy updates and TD critic updates is stable and convergent in the general Polish-space setting, and if so, at what rate.

## Input / Output

The analysis operates in the continuous-time (gradient-flow) limit of a discrete actor-critic algorithm, so the 'input' is an MDP with Polish state and action spaces, a bounded cost, a discount factor γ∈(0,1), a bounded feature map φ:S×A→R^N satisfying a uniform positive-definiteness condition on the feature covariance, a reference probability measure μ on the action space, and initial conditions (θ₀, π₀) with π₀ in the admissible exponential family Π_μ. The output is not a trained policy but a convergence-rate theorem: the value gap min_{r∈[0,t]} V_τ^{π_r}(ρ) − V_τ^{π*}(ρ) is bounded by an exponentially decaying term in t (rate τ/2) plus a constant residual of order 1/τ, with the exponential prefactor depending on the initial KL divergence KL(π*(·|s)‖π₀(·|s)) integrated against the optimal occupancy measure.

## Method

The actor is updated by an approximate Fisher-Rao gradient flow ∂_t π_t = −A(s,a;θ_t)·π_t(da|s) driven by the soft advantage computed from a linearly parameterised critic Q(s,a;θ)=⟨θ,φ(s,a)⟩, while the critic is updated by a semi-gradient descent on the mean-squared Bellman error at a faster timescale controlled by a non-decreasing separation factor η_t. The central technical contribution is a Lyapunov-based stability framework that couples a Grönwall-type inequality for the critic norm (Lemma 5.1) with a Duhamel-representation argument over the space of probability kernels to show the KL divergence K_t = sup_s KL(π_t(·|s)‖μ) satisfies a self-referential integral inequality (Theorem 5.1), from which finite-time boundedness follows via Grönwall's lemma. Convergence is then established by combining the performance-difference lemma for entropy-regularised MDPs with Pinsker's inequality to bound the critic approximation error, and showing that choosing η_t to grow exponentially (η_t = η₀ e^{k₁t} with k₁ large enough) makes the residual critic-tracking integral decay, yielding an exponential convergence rate of order e^{−τt/2} to the optimal regularised value.

## Results

The main result (Theorem 6.3) establishes exponential convergence of the coupled actor-critic gradient flow to a neighbourhood of the optimal entropy-regularised value: min_{r∈[0,t]} V_τ^{π_r}(ρ) − V_τ^{π*}(ρ) ≤ [τe^{−τt/2} / (2(1−γ)(1−e^{−τt/2}))] · ∫_S KL(π*(·|s)|π_0(·|s)) d_ρ^{π*}(ds) + k_2/(2τ). The convergence rate is exponential in t with rate τ/2, and the asymptotic error floor is k_2/(2τ), which vanishes as τ→0. Stability (Corollary 5.1) guarantees that the KL divergence KL(π_t(·|s)|μ) remains bounded by a_1 e^{a_2 t} for all t≥0, ruling out finite-time blow-up in general action spaces where the KL term is unbounded a priori.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical paper with no empirical experiments or numerical baselines to compare against. The contribution is to extend the convergence analysis of two-timescale actor-critic methods from the unregularised, finite-action-space setting (Konda & Tsitsiklis 1999; Cayci et al. 2024a,b) to entropy-regularised MDPs with general (Polish) state and action spaces. The key technical advance over prior work is handling the unboundedness of the KL regulariser in infinite action spaces: while Cayci et al. (2024) rely on finite action cardinality to bound KL(π(·|s)|μ) ≤ log|A|, this paper proves a Grönwall-type stability bound (Theorem 5.1) that prevents finite-time blow-up without such a bound. The analysis also extends the exact Fisher-Rao gradient flow results of Kerimkulov et al. (2025a) to the approximate (linearly parameterised) critic setting, showing that timescale separation η_t = η_0 e^{k_1 t} with k_1 > τ/2 + α_2 is sufficient to maintain exponential convergence despite the critic tracking error.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the continuous-time actor-critic convergence analysis of Konda & Tsitsiklis (1999) and Borkar & Konda (1997) to the entropy-regularised setting with general (Polish) state and action spaces, combining the policy mirror descent framework of Ju & Lan (2024) with the Fisher-Rao gradient flow structure characterised by Kerimkulov et al. (2025a). It departs critically from Cayci et al. (2024a,b), whose convergence results for entropy-regularised actor-critic rely on finite action space cardinality to bound the KL divergence, and from Zhang et al. (2021), which handles the unregularised case but requires a restarting mechanism for stability. The key technical departure is proving that the unbounded KL divergence in general action spaces does not cause finite-time blow-up, using a Lyapunov framework that couples convex analysis over the space of probability measures with classical Euclidean convex analysis.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work closes a significant gap in the theoretical RL literature by establishing that the widely-used actor-critic scheme (TD critic + mirror descent actor) is stable and convergent for entropy-regularised MDPs in general action spaces, a setting where prior results either required finite action spaces or lacked the regularisation needed to make the KL-based convergence rate finite. The finding that timescale separation is not merely helpful but essential for stability in the unbounded-KL regime provides a principled justification for the two-timescale design that practitioners use empirically. Key limitations include the continuous-time idealisation, linear function approximation, and exact evaluation of all integrals; extending to discrete-time stochastic updates with Monte-Carlo estimation and non-linear (neural network) critics remains open and is the most practically relevant direction.
