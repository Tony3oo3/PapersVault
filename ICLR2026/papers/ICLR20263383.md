## Problem

Proving authorship of content generated by private diffusion models is critical for copyright protection, yet existing watermarking methods require access to model weights and rely on computationally expensive DDIM inversion for verification, making them impractical when the model is proprietary or the owner is untrusted. No prior scheme provides a lightweight, model-agnostic, and distortion-free verification path that operates solely on the seed and output.

## Input / Output

The method takes a generated image or video x, a secret random seed s, and black-box access to the VAE encoder E as input. The seed is first passed through a cryptographic hash h(s) to initialize a public PRNG, producing the initial Gaussian noise ε(h(s)); the VAE encoder then maps x into latent space for comparison. The output is a single cosine similarity score φ(x,s) and a binary accept/reject decision against a threshold τ calibrated for a false positive rate of 2⁻¹²⁸, requiring no access to the diffusion model weights at any point.

## Method

NoisePrints exploits the observation that the initial Gaussian noise in a diffusion or flow model leaves a persistent, high-correlation imprint on the generated latent, so the seed itself serves as a natural watermark without altering the generation process. Verification computes the cosine similarity between the VAE-encoded image E(x) and the noise ε(h(s)) derived from the seed, accepting the claim if the score exceeds a threshold τ whose false-positive probability decays exponentially with latent dimension d (bounded by exp(−(d−1)τ²/2)). A cryptographic hash h(s) in the noise-sampling pipeline makes it infeasible to recover a valid seed from the content, and a zero-knowledge proof extension allows the owner to demonstrate ownership without revealing s to the verifier. A dispute protocol extends verification to handle geometric transformations (via inverse-transform re-alignment) and injection-only attacks (via cross-checks between competing claimants), and the entire pipeline runs in sub-millisecond time for the correlation step, yielding a 14×–213× speedup over inversion-based baselines.

## Results

Across five diffusion models (SD2.0, SDXL, Flux-dev, Flux-schnell, Wan2.1), NoisePrints achieves 99–100% pass rate at an extremely low false positive rate of 2⁻¹²⁸, with mean cosine similarity scores ranging from 0.068 (Wan2.1, d=1.3M) to 0.482 (SD2.0, d=16K). Verification via cosine similarity takes 0.09–0.18 ms, compared to 3.2–91.5 s for DDIM inversion used by all three baselines, yielding a 14×–213× end-to-end speedup. Under SDEdit regeneration and DDIM-inversion-based adversarial attacks, NoisePrints maintains TPR above 0.9 at FPR=2⁻¹²⁸, outperforming all baselines by a large margin. In the traceability setting (100 images vs. 100,000 seeds), NoisePrints achieves 0.980 detection under DDIM inversion attack versus 0.560 for WIND.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NoisePrints is compared against three inversion-based watermarking methods: WIND (Arabi et al., 2025), PRC/Undetectable Watermark (Gunn et al., 2024), and Gaussian Shading (Yang et al., 2024). On robustness, NoisePrints outperforms all three baselines under SDEdit regeneration attacks and DDIM-inversion-based adversarial attacks, where it maintains TPR > 0.9 at FPR=2⁻¹²⁸ while baselines degrade substantially; in the traceability experiment, NoisePrints scores 0.980 under DDIM inversion versus 0.560 for WIND. Under severe basic corruptions (Gaussian blur r=6, Gaussian noise σ=0.3), WIND shows slightly higher TPR near 1.0, but the authors note these levels produce heavily distorted images that diverge from well-trained model outputs. The most decisive advantage is computational: NoisePrints replaces the multi-second inversion step with a single cosine similarity computation, achieving 14×–213× speedup depending on model and step count, and critically does not require access to model weights—unlike all three baselines which need the diffusion model for inversion. NoisePrints also avoids the distributional shift introduced by pattern-embedding methods (PRC, GS) and the database-matching overhead of WIND.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant paradigm in diffusion-model watermarking, which either embeds artificial patterns in the noise/latent space and recovers them via costly DDIM inversion (WIND, PRC, Gaussian Shading, Tree-ring) or fine-tunes the model to bake watermarks into outputs. Instead, it repurposes the initial Gaussian noise itself as a natural watermark, leveraging the (previously noted by Staniszewski et al., 2025) strong noise-to-content correlation and connecting it to optimal transport theory, then verifies authorship via a single cosine similarity in latent space—eliminating both pattern embedding and inversion. The cryptographic layer (one-way hash on the seed, zero-knowledge proofs via CirC/Mirage) is borrowed from the ZKP literature and applied here for the first time in the context of diffusion-model watermarking to enable seed-private verification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The method addresses a practically critical gap: third-party authorship verification when the diffusion model is private and its owner is untrusted or unavailable, a scenario increasingly common with API-based and fine-tuned models. The 14–213× verification speedup over inversion-based baselines makes large-scale and video-domain verification feasible, and the distortion-free property (no generation-process modification) preserves output quality. Key limitations remain: the verifier needs VAE encoder access, the method is unsuitable for real/fake detection, and robustness is scoped to bounded perceptual-preserving manipulations rather than arbitrary adversarial edits. Open questions include extending correlation-based signals to real-image forensics and exploiting spatial correlation structure (stronger in foreground) for open-world detection.
