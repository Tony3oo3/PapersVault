## Problem

Supervised safety fine-tuning of VLMs appears to produce robust safety, but this paper reveals a 'safety mirage': the learned safety is largely driven by spurious correlations between superficial textual patterns (e.g., question-initiating words like 'What' or 'Share') and safety labels in the training dataset, rather than genuine understanding of harmful content. This makes fine-tuned models vulnerable to a simple one-word jailbreaking attack and simultaneously causes over-prudence, where benign queries are unnecessarily rejected. The root cause of these failures and a principled alternative to label-based safety alignment remained unexplored.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text query paired with an RGB image as input to a VLM (LLaVA-v1.5-7B/13B) and produces a text response that is either a safe answer or a rejection. For the unlearning objective, only the unsafe text-image query features are used (without any safety label), while the retain loss operates on safe or utility text-image pairs. The one-word attack modifies only the first token of the text query (e.g., replacing 'Share' with 'What'), leaving the image and remaining text unchanged, which is sufficient to bypass or trigger the safety mechanism.

## Method

The paper adapts two LLM unlearning algorithms—Representation Misdirection Unlearning (RMU) and Negative Preference Optimization (NPO)—to VLM safety alignment, replacing the supervised safety fine-tuning loss with a label-free unlearning objective. RMU maps intermediate-layer representations (layers 4–6) of unsafe data to random vectors, erasing meaningful encodings of harmful content; NPO treats unsafe queries as negative examples in a DPO-style objective, pushing the model to deviate from its reference distribution on unsafe inputs. To prevent model collapse, the retain loss is a composite of standard cross-entropy fine-tuning on safe/utility data and an MU-specific retain term (representation matching for RMU, positive-preference DPO for NPO). The key insight is that by never pairing unsafe inputs with explicit rejection labels, the method avoids the feature-label shortcuts that create spurious correlations, and safety emerges primarily through irrelevant (non-rejection) responses rather than learned refusal patterns.

## Results

On VLGuard-unsafe, RMU-Unlearning achieves 1.29% ASR before and 10.18% after a 3-shot one-word attack, compared to 54.98% for Mixed-SFT and 46.83% for Posthoc-SFT under the same attack. On over-prudence (VLGuard-safe), RMU-Unlearning maintains a 7.56% rejection rate after a 1-shot 'Share'-prefix modification, versus 91.76% for Mixed-SFT and 90.83% for Posthoc-SFT. Utility loss is minimal: RMU-Unlearning scores 77.04% on VQAv2 vs. 78.53% for the original LLaVA-1.5-7B (≈1.5% drop). Across all four model configurations (7B/13B × full/LoRA), unlearning-based methods consistently reduce post-attack ASR by 40–60 percentage points and post-modification RR by 80+ percentage points relative to SFT baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Compared to the original LLaVA-1.5-7B (no safety fine-tuning), both unlearning methods dramatically reduce ASR from 64.25% to 1.29–2.49% before attack, while keeping post-attack ASR at 10–13% versus 90.27% for the unmodified model. Against the SFT baselines from Zong et al. (2024) (Mixed-SFT and Posthoc-SFT), the unlearning methods trade a slightly higher pre-attack ASR (1.29–2.49% vs. 0.23%) for a massive robustness gain: post-one-word-attack ASR drops from 46.83–54.98% to 10.18–12.92% (a 60+ percentage-point reduction), and post-modification RR drops from 90.83–91.76% to 7.56–11.69% (an 84+ percentage-point reduction). On utility, RMU-Unlearning loses only ~1.5% VQAv2 accuracy relative to the original model, comparable to the ~0.3–0.5% loss from SFT baselines. The Unsafe-Filter baseline (removing unsafe data from pre-training) provides essentially no safety benefit, confirming that the vulnerability is not simply due to exposure to unsafe data but to the spurious label-word correlations introduced during supervised fine-tuning.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes machine unlearning (MU) techniques originally developed for LLM knowledge removal—specifically RMU (Li et al., 2024c) and NPO (Zhang et al., 2024a), the latter built on the DPO framework (Rafailov et al., 2023)—as a safety alignment mechanism for VLMs. It departs from the dominant paradigm of supervised safety fine-tuning on curated datasets (VLGuard, SPA-VL) by diagnosing a 'safety mirage' rooted in spurious text-label correlations, drawing on the broader spurious-correlation literature from image classification (Sagawa et al., 2020). The key conceptual departure is shifting from label-driven safety (teaching the model to reject) to label-free knowledge removal (erasing unsafe associations), with a composite retain loss designed to prevent the model collapse that pure MU objectives cause in VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that a single-word substitution can raise attack success rates from near-zero to ~55–90% on safety-fine-tuned VLMs challenges a widely held assumption in the multimodal safety community and provides a concrete, reproducible diagnostic for auditing safety fine-tuning pipelines. The MU-based alternative offers a practical, drop-in replacement that reduces post-attack ASR by up to 60% and cuts over-prudence by over 84% while preserving utility, though it still leaves non-trivial residual ASR (~10–25%). Open questions include whether the spurious-correlation diagnosis generalizes beyond LLaVA-family architectures and English-language datasets, whether more sophisticated multi-word or cross-modal attacks exist, and whether MU can be combined with inference-time defenses for layered protection.
