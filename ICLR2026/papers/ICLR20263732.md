## Problem

Standard preference-based alignment methods (DPO, IPO, SimPO, CPO) treat every observed preference label as a fixed ground truth with equal confidence, yet real-world preference datasets contain 20–40% label noise from annotator errors, inconsistent instructions, and low-effort feedback. This noise causes models to overfit corrupted supervision and degrade downstream win rates by tens of percentage points. Existing robustness approaches either reshape the loss function or filter high-loss examples, but none explicitly model per-annotator reliability as a latent variable to be jointly inferred with the policy.

## Input / Output

The method consumes preference triplets (prompt x, chosen response y_w, rejected response y_l) paired with an annotator index k_i, along with a base LLM policy π_θ and a fixed reference policy π_ref. When annotator IDs are unavailable (as in the UltraFeedback-derived training sets), all data is attributed to a single virtual annotator (K=1). The output is a fine-tuned aligned policy π_θ plus a vector of per-annotator reliability estimates η_k ∈ [0,1], updated online via exponential moving average during mini-batch training. The induced preference probability p(y_w ≻* y_l | x, θ) is computed from the chosen loss via a Boltzmann-style construction p ∝ exp(−L_pref), which for DPO recovers the Bradley-Terry model exactly.

## Method

RE-PO introduces a binary latent variable z_i per data point indicating whether the observed label matches the true noise-free preference, with annotator reliability η_k = P(z_i=1 | k_i=k) as a learnable parameter. An EM procedure alternates between an E-step that computes the posterior confidence w_i = P(z_i=1 | observed data, θ, η) via Bayes' rule over the induced preference probability, and an M-step that (a) minimizes a weighted preference loss where each sample is scaled by w_i (or 1−w_i for the flipped preference) and (b) updates each η_k to the mean confidence of that annotator's labels in closed form. A key generalization is the Gibbs/Boltzmann construction linking any preference loss L_pref to a well-defined binary probability model, which lifts RE-PO from a single DPO-specific algorithm to a meta-framework applicable to IPO, SimPO, and CPO. Under perfect calibration and full-batch training, Theorem 4.1 proves the reliability iterates converge to the true annotator reliability; the practical mini-batch variant uses EMA (momentum α) for η updates to avoid full-dataset passes. Across four alignment objectives and two 7–8B backbones, RE-PO-enhanced methods improve AlpacaEval 2 win rates by up to 7.0 percentage points over their standard counterparts.

## Results

On AlpacaEval 2, RE-PO-enhanced methods consistently outperform their standard counterparts across four alignment objectives (DPO, IPO, SimPO, CPO) and two backbones. The largest gains are RE-DPO on Mistral-7B (LC 28.5→35.5, +7.0 pp) and RE-IPO on Llama-3-8B (WR 41.6→48.6, +7.0 pp). On the MultiPref multi-annotator dataset, RE-DPO improves AlpacaEval 2 LC/WR from 36.7/39.3 to 41.1/44.4 on Llama-3-8B and from 28.8/26.4 to 31.8/28.8 on Mistral-7B. Ablations show the method is most sensitive to the EMA momentum α (optimal at 0.1) and initial reliability η₀ (optimal at 0.9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RE-PO outperforms all four standard alignment baselines (DPO, IPO, SimPO, CPO) on both backbones in the majority of configurations, with the most consistent gains on DPO and IPO. Against the specialized robust baselines, RE-DPO on Llama-3-8B reaches 44.1/46.2 LC/WR, surpassing rDPO (37.3/35.4) by +6.8/+10.8 pp and Hölder-DPO (39.3/38.2) by +4.8/+8.0 pp. Label smoothing provides modest or inconsistent gains (e.g., SimPO w/ LS on Llama-3-8B improves LC to 48.1 but RE-SimPO reaches 46.9 LC while also improving WR from 37.1 to 39.4), whereas RE-PO delivers more balanced improvements across both LC and WR metrics. On the MultiPref multi-annotator dataset, RE-DPO beats vanilla DPO by +4.4 LC / +5.1 WR on Llama-3-8B and +3.0 LC / +2.4 WR on Mistral-7B, confirming that per-annotator reliability modeling adds value beyond single-annotator settings. The only configuration where RE-PO does not clearly dominate is CPO on Mistral-7B, where the gain is modest (+1.3 LC, +1.4 WR), suggesting the method's benefit is most pronounced when the base loss is more susceptible to label noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

RE-PO transplants the Dawid-Skene EM framework for annotator reliability estimation (Dawid & Skene, 1979; Chen et al., 2013) into the LLM preference-alignment setting, where prior robust methods (rDPO, Hölder-DPO, ROPO) instead modify loss shapes or filter data without explicitly modeling the data-generating process. Its key conceptual departure is the Gibbs/Boltzmann construction that maps an arbitrary preference loss to an induced binary probability model, thereby lifting the EM reweighting from a DPO-specific trick to a general meta-framework applicable to IPO, SimPO, and CPO. For likelihood-style losses like DPO, the induced model recovers the Bradley-Terry form exactly, preserving probabilistic equivalence; for non-likelihood losses it yields a robust surrogate that may differ from the original objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

RE-PO offers a low-overhead (~11% runtime) plug-in that consistently improves four major alignment objectives across two backbones, making it practically attractive for teams already using DPO/IPO/SimPO/CPO pipelines. The main open question is the gap between the population-level convergence guarantee (Theorem 4.1, assuming a perfectly calibrated model) and the mini-batch Generalized EM used in practice; the authors acknowledge that a highly misaligned initialization could cause the E-step to assign misleadingly high confidence to incorrect labels. Extending convergence guarantees to significantly miscalibrated base models, and studying interaction with other noise sources (e.g., prompt-level bias, distribution shift), remain important directions.
