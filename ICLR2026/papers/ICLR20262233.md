## Problem

Existing agentic benchmarks primarily evaluate shallow fact retrieval, artificial information-seeking questions, or tasks drawn from a single well-known source like Wikipedia, and are largely English-centric. They do not adequately assess whether LLM-based agents can synthesize information from multiple heterogeneous sources across diverse regions to produce insights beyond simple retrieval. DEEPSYNTH addresses this gap by introducing 120 realistic, time-consuming tasks that require combining unstructured and structured data from 67 countries to generate verifiable, multi-part answers.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262233/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each task is a natural-language question (averaging 78.49 tokens) that requires the agent to navigate an average of 4.2 web pages and process 1–15 documents or tables, performing operations such as trend detection, correlation, ranking, and anomaly detection. The output must be a strictly formatted JSON object (or list of JSON objects) with key-value pairs in tabular style, where values are strings, floats, or integers — enabling deterministic verification via exact match, F1, and a soft LLM-judge metric that tolerates 1–5.5% numerical deviation. Answers are deliberately constructed to be non-retrievable through verbatim web search or pre-training corpus lookup, forcing multi-step reasoning.

## Method

DEEPSYNTH is constructed via a four-stage bottom-up pipeline: (1) 16 experts identify 223 official data sources across 7 domains, (2) annotators formulate plausible hypotheses for each source, (3) annotators validate hypotheses through manual analysis, retaining only those yielding verifiable answers, and (4) annotators write the final question, gold answer, intermediate reasoning steps, and supporting evidence URLs. A second independent annotator must reproduce the answer exactly for a task to be retained, yielding 120 final tasks (averaging 7.54 intermediate steps and 5.5 hours of annotation time each). Evaluation benchmarks 11 SOTA LLMs and three agentic frameworks (o3-deep-research, smolagents with ReAct loop, and OWL with planner-executor multi-agent design) using F1, Precision, Recall, Exact Match, and LLM-as-a-judge metrics, with ablations isolating the contribution of web search, code execution, document processing, and explicit planning steps.

## Results

On the DEEPSYNTH benchmark (120 tasks, Pass@1), the best-performing system is o3-deep-research, achieving an F1 score of 8.97 and an LLM-Judge score of 17.5. Among standalone LLMs, GPT-5.2-Pro leads with an F1 of 8.70 and an Exact Match of 6.25, while all other LLM baselines score 0 on EM. The overall difficulty is stark: no model solves more than a handful of tasks perfectly, and the gap between the best agent (o3-deep-research, F1 8.97) and the best LLM (GPT-5.2-Pro, F1 8.70) is marginal, indicating that tool augmentation provides only modest gains over parametric reasoning alone.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among standalone LLMs, GPT-5.2-Pro (F1 8.70, EM 6.25) substantially outperforms GPT-4.1 (F1 3.46, EM 0.0) and o4-mini (F1 3.05, EM 0.0), with Gemini-Pro-2.5 (F1 6.25) and GPT-5.1 (F1 3.83) falling in between; DeepSeek-R1 variants trail at F1 3.23 (Chat) and 2.80 (Reasoner). On the LLM-Judge metric, GPT-5.2-Pro and DeepSeek-R1-Reasoner tie at 6.67, while Gemini-Pro-2.5 scores only 5.0, suggesting its outputs are semantically reasonable but structurally imprecise. Among agentic frameworks, o3-deep-research (F1 8.97, LLM-Judge 17.5) dominates, outperforming OWL (GPT-4.1) by 3.56 F1 points and Smolagent (GPT-4.1) by 5.22 F1 points; however, even o3-deep-research improves over its base o3 model by only 5.68 F1 points, and all three agents solve at most 3 out of 120 tasks perfectly. The paper further shows that providing ground-truth intermediate reasoning steps boosts GPT-4.1 from F1 3.46 to 9.36 and Smolagent (GPT-4.1) from 3.75 to 10.50, revealing that planning—not raw reasoning—is the primary bottleneck. All models achieve 0.0 F1 on Africa-related tasks, exposing a strong geographic bias across every baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

DEEPSYNTH carves new ground by targeting *information synthesis*—combining heterogeneous sources to produce novel, structured insights—as a distinct evaluation axis, rather than the fact-retrieval or single-source reasoning emphasized by GAIA, BrowseComp, and MoNaCo. Its construction pipeline (data source → hypothesis → validation → task formulation) inverts the common 'start with a fact, hide the question' approach used by BrowseComp, ensuring answers are non-retrievable by design. Methodologically, it borrows the LLM-as-a-judge protocol from MoNaCo (Wolfson et al., 2025) and evaluates agents built on ReAct (smolagents) and multi-agent role-playing (OWL) paradigms, but the benchmark itself is the primary contribution rather than a new agent architecture.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark exposes a critical and underappreciated failure mode: near-total error propagation (91–100% cascade rate) means that a single early retrieval failure irrecoverably corrupts the entire reasoning chain, making end-to-end success extremely fragile. The finding that all evaluated models score 0.0 F1 on Africa-related tasks reveals a severe geographic bias that current training data distributions do not address, with direct implications for deploying agents in globally diverse settings. Open questions include whether improved planning (the intermediate-step ablation shows large gains) or better tool-use reliability can break the error-propagation bottleneck, and whether the benchmark's strict JSON-output format underestimates agents that produce correct but differently-structured answers.
