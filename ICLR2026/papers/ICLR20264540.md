## Problem

Current state-of-the-art open reward models perform poorly across most evaluation benchmarks and fail to capture the full spectrum of nuanced human preferences, with RewardBench scores saturating and showing weak or even inverse correlation with downstream task performance. The authors hypothesize this brittleness stems primarily from limitations in preference datasets—narrowly scoped, synthetically labeled, or lacking rigorous quality control—rather than from model architecture or loss function design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264540/cropped_page2_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

Each reward model takes a conversation prompt and a single response as input (up to 16K tokens) and outputs a scalar reward score; preference is determined by the score difference between two candidate responses under the Bradley-Terry formulation. During training, the model consumes 3-tuples (x, y_w, y_l) where x is the conversation and y_w/y_l are the chosen and rejected responses. The series spans eight model sizes (0.6B–8B) built on Llama 3.1/3.2 and Qwen3 backbones, all trained exclusively with the standard Bradley-Terry pairwise objective without any architectural modifications.

## Method

The core contribution is a two-stage human-AI synergistic curation pipeline that produces SynPref-40M (40M preference pairs, 26M curated). Stage 1 runs 8 iterative rounds where human annotators verify labels under a strict protocol (using search engines, frontier LLMs, and domain tools), while LLMs generate silver labels guided by adaptively retrieved human-verified gold examples as few-shot context; an error-driven retrieval mechanism selects new samples from the unverified pool where the current reward model misclassifies or shows low confidence. Stage 2 scales to tens of millions of in-the-wild pairs using consistency-based filtering: pairs where the best Stage-1 RM and a separate gold RM (trained only on human-verified data) agree are retained without re-annotation, while disagreements trigger LLM re-judgment using the same human-preference-guided few-shot procedure. Ablations confirm that human curation contributes the largest single gain (+3.2 points over raw annotation), adaptive retrieval adds +0.9 over plain LLM annotation, and even 1.8% of the curated mixture (~290K samples) surpasses the previous 70B SOTA open reward model.

## Results

Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B-40M achieves the best average score of 88.6 across seven major reward model benchmarks (RewardBench, RewardBench v2, PPE Preference, PPE Correctness, RMB, RM-Bench, JudgeBench), outperforming the previous best open RM (INF-ORM-Llama3.1-70B, 73.8 avg) by 14.8 points. The 8B variant (85.8 avg) ranks first on all seven benchmarks among open reward models, with standout scores of 96.4 on RewardBench, 84.1 on RewardBench v2, and 92.8 on RM-Bench. Notably, even the 1.7B variant (75.2 avg) surpasses the best 70B open RM on average, and all eight models outperform GPT-4o (70.4) and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (70.8) as LLM-as-a-Judge baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B (85.8 avg) outperforms the strongest prior open RM, INF-ORM-Llama3.1-70B (73.8 avg), by 12.0 points on average, with particularly large margins on RM-Bench (92.8 vs. 75.4) and RewardBench v2 (84.1 vs. 76.5). Against LLM-as-a-Judge baselines, the 8B model beats GPT-4o (70.4) by 15.4 points and Claude-3.5-Sonnet (70.8) by 15.0 points on average, and surpasses both on every individual benchmark. Versus generative reward models, Skywork-Reward-V2-Llama-3.1-8B exceeds DeepSeek-GRM-27B on all four overlapping benchmarks (e.g., RMB 86.4 vs. 69.0) and outperforms RM-R1-Qwen-Instruct-32B on RMB (86.4 vs. 73.0) and RM-Bench (92.8 vs. 79.1). The 1.7B variant (75.2 avg) already surpasses the best 70B open RM (INF-ORM, 73.8), demonstrating that data quality from SynPref-40M bridges the model-size gap. On Best-of-N evaluation (RMB), all eight Skywork-Reward-V2 models outperform GPT-4o by up to 20 points, with the 8B-40M variant reaching 86.4 average BoN accuracy versus GPT-4o's 66.1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Bradley-Terry reward modeling paradigm (Bradley & Terry, 1952; Ouyang et al., 2022) by introducing a human-AI synergistic data curation pipeline that departs from both purely human-annotated datasets (Wang et al., 2024e) and fully automatic RLAIF approaches (Cui et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2023). It builds on in-context retrieval (Ram et al., 2023) for error-driven adaptive sample selection and self-consistency (Wang et al., 2022) for multi-LLM annotation aggregation, but uniquely conditions LLM judges on human-verified gold examples to bridge the quality gap. The work is a direct successor to the Skywork-Reward series (Liu et al., 2024b), which the authors identify as the source of stagnation in open reward models since September 2024, and positions against the emerging generative reward model paradigm (Liu et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025c) by showing that a well-curated discriminative BT model can outperform them.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The release of SynPref-40M (40M pairs, 26M curated) and the Skywork-Reward-V2 series (0.6B–8B) provides the field with the largest open curated preference mixture and SOTA open reward models that are practical to deploy, potentially accelerating RLHF research beyond the 70B-only landscape. The ablation finding that 290K carefully curated samples (1.8% of the mixture) surpass the previous 70B SOTA, while adding 12M uncurated pairs yields no gain, challenges the prevailing 'scale uncurated data' assumption and redirects attention to curation quality. Open questions include whether fully automatic curation with next-generation reasoning LLMs can match human-guided quality, whether the flipped-label 'recycling' strategy generalizes beyond the current benchmark suite, and how to handle the inherent subjectivity and conflicting nature of human preferences that a single reward model cannot fully capture.
