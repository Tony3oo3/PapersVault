## Problem

Testing whether a signed graph is (nearly) perfectly clusterable or far from clusterable is a fundamental sublinear-time property testing problem, but prior work required Õ(1/ε^7) queries for general correlation clustering and had no tight bounds for structural balance (k=2). No nontrivial algorithm existed for testing k-clusterability with a fixed number of clusters k. This paper closes these gaps by providing dramatically improved upper bounds and the first matching lower bounds.

## Input / Output

The algorithm receives a labeled complete graph G=(V, E⁺∪E⁻) on n vertices via adjacency matrix queries (each edge label query costs O(1) time) and a parameter ε∈(0,1). It outputs a one-sided-error YES/NO decision: 'YES' if the graph is clusterable (or δ-close to clusterable in the tolerant variant), 'NO' with probability ≥0.9 if the graph is ε-far from clusterable. Crucially, the number of edge queries is independent of n, scaling only as O(1/ε²) for general clustering, O(k⁴ln⁴k/ε⁴) for fixed-k clustering, and O(1/ε) for structural balance.

## Method

For general clusterability, the algorithm uniformly samples O(1/ε) vertices, queries their induced subgraph, and runs BFS on positive edges to detect bad triangles (two + edges and one − edge). The key analytical novelty is applying Janson's inequality from random graph theory—used for the first time in property testing—to show that an ε-far graph necessarily contains a negative edge connected by a positive path, and that a small random sample captures such a witness with high probability, yielding O(1/ε²) queries instead of the prior Õ(1/ε^7). For fixed-k clusterability, a second subroutine samples O(k ln k/ε) vertices and greedily assigns them to at most k clusters, rejecting if a (k+1)-th independent set is found. For structural balance (k=2), the algorithm directly samples O(1/ε) triangles and checks for unbalanced ones, avoiding the quadratic vertex-sampling overhead. A matching Ω(1/ε) lower bound is proven, establishing tight bounds for structural balance testing.

## Results

The paper improves the query complexity for testing correlation clustering from Õ(1/ε⁷) to O(1/ε²), and establishes the first tight Θ(1/ε) bound for structural balance (k=2) testing. For fixed-k correlation clustering, the first nontrivial O(1/ε⁴) upper bound is achieved (for constant k). Empirically, on synthetic graphs with ε=0.1, all proposed algorithms achieve 100% testing accuracy, with the structural balance tester (Algorithm 4) requiring only 60 queries and 1.3×10⁻⁴ seconds per graph, compared to 900 queries and 1.1 seconds for the Adriaens & Apers (2023) baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only prior baseline in the adjacency matrix query model is Adriaens & Apers (2023), which achieves Õ(1/ε⁷) queries for correlation clustering and Õ(1/ε²) for structural balance. The proposed Algorithm 4 for structural balance reduces query complexity from 900 to 60 (a 15× reduction) and running time from 1.1 s to 1.3×10⁻⁴ s (roughly 10⁴× speedup) on n=1000 graphs, while maintaining identical 100% testing accuracy. Theoretically, the correlation clustering tester improves from Õ(1/ε⁷) to O(1/ε²) queries, and the structural balance tester improves from Õ(1/ε²) (via Sohler 2012) to a tight Θ(1/ε) bound. For fixed-k correlation clustering, no prior sublinear algorithm existed; the paper provides the first O(1/ε⁴) bound for constant k. On real-world SNAP graphs, all algorithms complete in under 0.1 s, and the structural balance tester shows a clear phase transition right after the spectral frustration index lower bound, supporting correctness.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on Adriaens & Apers (2023), who designed the first sublinear-time tester for correlation clustering with Õ(1/ε⁷) queries using a MAX-CSP reduction, and on Sohler (2012), who achieved Õ(1/ε²) for the special case of structural balance. The key conceptual departure is replacing the graph removal lemma (which yields tower-type bounds for bad-triangle-freeness) with Janson's inequality from random graph theory — a tool not previously applied to property testing analysis. Notably, the algorithm design itself is straightforward (sample O(1/ε) vertices and check the induced subgraph for bad triangles); the entire contribution lies in the probabilistic analysis. An addendum acknowledges that Goldreich & Ron (2011) had already studied an essentially equivalent clique-collection problem with tight Θ̃(1/ε) bounds, though the authors' technique and analysis remain novel.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper delivers the first tight Θ(1/ε) bounds for structural balance testing and the first nontrivial algorithm for testing k-clusterability (for any constant k), closing long-standing gaps in the property-testing literature for signed graphs. The dramatic practical speedup (from >2.5 hours to <1 second at ε=0.01) makes sublinear testing viable for massive social network analysis. Open questions include extending the results to sparse or partially-labeled graphs, where the local-witness (bad triangle) detection strategy may fail, and understanding whether the Janson's inequality technique generalizes to other property testing problems beyond signed graphs.
