## Problem

Concept Bottleneck Models enable experts to inspect and correct individual predictions, but test-time interventions cannot fix systemic shortcut learning from biased data. Existing debugging methods either require costly per-sample concept labels or are limited to specific architectures, leaving no general mechanism for experts to globally remove undesired concepts and retrain the model to rely on task-relevant features instead.

## Input / Output

The method takes a trained unsupervised CBM (PIP-Net with ConvNeXt-tiny or Post-hoc CBM with CLIP-ViT-L-14/BioMedCLIP), the original training dataset, and a binary set of expert-marked spurious concepts (task-level, not class-specific). For PIP-Net, concepts are represented as prototypical image patches; for Post-hoc CBM, they are text descriptions scored via CLIP embeddings. The output is a retrained CBM with the same architecture that maintains average accuracy while substantially improving worst-group accuracy, with the spurious concepts effectively replaced by more robust, task-relevant ones.

## Method

CBDebug operationalizes interpretable debugging as a causal intervention through three stages. First, the Label step uses the trained concept extractor to compute activation scores for the expert-marked spurious concepts on every training sample, converting concept-level feedback into sample-level auxiliary labels. Second, the Reweight step applies permutation weighting (Arbour et al., 2021) to these labels and the class labels, training a binary predictor to estimate each sample's likelihood under an unconfounded distribution and deriving per-sample weights that downweight bias-aligned examples. Third, the Augment step converts weights into augmentation probabilities (via inversion, normalization, and a γ-power contrast parameter) and selectively applies CutMix (for ProtoPNets) or Mixup with a text-to-image concept bank (for VLM-CBMs) to the most bias-aligned samples. The CBM is then fine-tuned on the augmented, reweighted dataset, with the key insight being that the model's own interpretability bridges concept-level human feedback to the sample-level auxiliary labels required by supervised bias mitigation.

## Results

On Waterbirds with PIP-Net, CBDebug improves worst-group accuracy from 71.9% (original) to 79.4% (+7.5 pp), and on MetaShift from 52.4% to 57.3% (+4.9 pp). For Post-hoc CBM, the gains are substantially larger: worst-group accuracy on Waterbirds jumps from 25.8% to 51.9% (+26.1 pp), and on MetaShift from 84.5% to 89.3% (+4.8 pp). With automated LLM feedback (Table 2), CBDebug achieves up to 42.6% worst-group accuracy improvement over the original model on CelebA (8.7% → 51.3%) and 58.0% AUROC on ISIC (vs. 39.3% original).

## Baselines & Comparisons

CBDebug outperforms ProtoPDebug (Bontempelli et al., 2023) on both PIP-Net datasets: worst-group accuracy of 79.4% vs. 71.6% on Waterbirds (+7.8 pp) and 57.3% vs. 52.4% on MetaShift (+4.9 pp), while ProtoPDebug is inapplicable to Post-hoc CBM. Against the unsupervised bias mitigation baselines, CBDebug also wins: on PIP-Net Waterbirds, it achieves 79.4% worst-group accuracy versus 75.4% for Learning from Failure (Nam et al., 2020) and 71.7% for Just Train Twice (Liu et al., 2021); on MetaShift, 57.3% vs. 56.0% (LfF) and 51.9% (JTT). The simple Remove and Retrain baselines are consistently weaker than CBDebug across all four model-dataset combinations, with Remove sometimes degrading performance (e.g., Post-hoc CBM Waterbirds drops from 25.8% to 13.9% worst-group accuracy). The key advantage of CBDebug over unsupervised methods is that it leverages explicit expert identification of spurious concepts rather than relying on assumptions about training dynamics, yielding more targeted and stable improvements.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of interpretable debugging (Ross et al., 2017; Bontempelli et al., 2023) and supervised bias mitigation (Arbour et al., 2021; Zheng & Makar, 2022), drawing a novel conceptual bridge between the two. Prior debugging work on ProtoPNets (ProtoPDebug) used a forgetting loss on class-specific spurious patches, while bias mitigation methods like permutation weighting required auxiliary labels that are unavailable in unsupervised CBMs. CBDebug's key insight is that the CBM's own concept activations can serve as those auxiliary labels, converting concept-level expert feedback into sample-level weights without any additional annotation. The framework generalizes beyond ProtoPNets to VLM-CBMs (Post-hoc CBM), a setting where prior debugging methods either do not apply or lack empirical evaluation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, end-to-end workflow for domain experts to globally correct a CBM's reasoning rather than merely patching individual predictions at test time, which is important in high-stakes settings like medical imaging. A notable finding is that naive retraining after concept removal can reintroduce spurious correlations (the 'leakage' problem), motivating the targeted reweighting-plus-augmentation approach. Key limitations include the assumption of task-level (not class-specific) spuriousness, high variance in Post-hoc CBM results, and dependence on feedback quality. Open questions include extending the framework to class-specific shortcuts, scaling automated feedback to patch-based models, and understanding when the permutation-weighting bridge degrades if concept activations are poorly calibrated.
