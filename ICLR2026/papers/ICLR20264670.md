## Problem

Deep reinforcement learning policies are opaque neural networks that are difficult to understand, verify, and debug, undermining trust in safety-critical applications. While LLM-assisted evolutionary search (LES) has been applied to reward function design, no prior work directly synthesizes high-performance programmatic control policies through environmental interaction, nor does it incorporate visual feedback to guide targeted policy refinement beyond scalar performance metrics.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264670/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a formal task description, a set of parent policies (each comprising executable Python code and a natural-language 'thought' summary), and behavioral evidence (BE) in the form of frame-stacked pose-overlay images or trajectory visualizations from policy executions. It produces a new programmatic policy as a Python program with embedded comments, an accompanying natural-language rationale, and quantitative performance metrics (e.g., Normalized Weight Score for Lunar Lander, track completion rate for Car Racing) computed via parallel evaluation across multiple environment instances. The BE format is task-specific and flexible, ranging from static image composites to video clips or text-based state sequences.

## Method

MLES instantiates the Evolution of Heuristics (EoH) backbone with four evolutionary operators: two exploration operators (E1 for novel strategy synthesis, E2 for cross-parent generalization) and two multimodal modification operators (M1_M for identifying behavioral shortcomings via joint code+image analysis, M2_M for evidence-driven parameter tuning). The key innovation is a behavior summarizer that converts raw execution traces into visual BE (e.g., frame-stacked pose overlays), which MLLMs (GPT-4o-mini) scrutinize alongside parent code to diagnose failure modes and generate targeted revisions. Each evolutionary individual is a four-element tuple (code, thought, quantitative metrics, BE), with quantitative metrics serving as the selection objective and BE acting as the diagnostic signal for generation. Parent selection uses exponential rank selection to maintain high selection pressure (critical since MLLM inference is expensive), and the policy pool retains the top-N individuals by performance. Multimodal few-shot prompts dynamically assemble task description, parent code+thought, relevant BE images, and operator-specific instructions, enabling the MLLM to function as a reasoning agent that observes, diagnoses, and improves rather than merely generating code.

## Results

On Lunar Lander, MLES achieves the highest training average score of 1.090 (±0.005 SEM), surpassing PPO (1.032), EoH (1.053), and DQN (1.017). On Car Racing, MLES reaches 98.070 average training score, comparable to PPO (99.212) and substantially ahead of EoH (89.808) and DQN (79.777). On held-out test instances, MLES achieves 96.358 on Car Racing (beating PPO's 94.546) and 0.819 on Lunar Lander (slightly below PPO's 0.846). MLES also converges faster, reaching the performance threshold in 3h16min on Car Racing versus 12h for PPO.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MLES is compared against three baselines: DQN (value-based DRL for discrete actions), PPO (on-policy policy gradient for both discrete and continuous control), and EoH (the LES framework without multimodal behavioral evidence). Against PPO, MLES matches or exceeds performance: it achieves the best training score on Lunar Lander (1.090 vs. 1.032) and a slightly lower training score on Car Racing (98.070 vs. 99.212), but surpasses PPO on Car Racing test performance (96.358 vs. 94.546) while trailing slightly on Lunar Lander test (0.819 vs. 0.846). Against EoH, MLES consistently and significantly outperforms on both tasks, with the gap most pronounced on Car Racing (98.070 vs. 89.808 training; 96.358 vs. 79.286 testing), demonstrating the critical contribution of visual feedback-driven behavior analysis. Against DQN, MLES dominates on both tasks by a wide margin. MLES also converges substantially faster than PPO on Car Racing (3h16min vs. 12h to reach the 95-score threshold) and achieves comparable wall-clock time to DQN on Lunar Lander (36min vs. 31min to reach 1.00 score).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM-assisted Evolutionary Search (LES) paradigm, specifically building on the Evolution of Heuristics (EoH) framework by Liu et al. (2024) as its evolutionary backbone. The key departure is twofold: (1) shifting LES from the indirect optimization of reward functions (as in Eureka and related work) to the direct synthesis of executable programmatic policies, and (2) introducing multimodal behavioral evidence (BE) — visual execution traces analyzed by MLLMs — into the evolutionary loop as a diagnostic signal for targeted policy refinement. This positions MLES as the first framework to unify MLLMs, evolutionary search, and multimodal evaluation for end-to-end programmatic policy discovery, rather than treating LLMs as mere code generators guided by scalar rewards.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MLES offers a compelling alternative to opaque DRL policies by producing human-readable, debuggable, and verifiable control logic at a fraction of the computational cost (~$0.42 API spend vs. hours of GPU training), which is particularly relevant for safety-critical deployment. However, several limitations temper its immediate impact: the behavioral evidence pipeline must be manually designed per task, generalization to unseen instances remains a gap (mitigated only partially by policy ensembling), and the evaluation is confined to two relatively simple OpenAI Gym benchmarks. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional, multi-agent, or long-horizon control tasks, whether BE design can be automated via meta-learning, and how hybrid architectures (e.g., CNN perception + programmatic control) would perform in realistic embodied settings.
