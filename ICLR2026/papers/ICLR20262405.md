## Problem

Infinite-horizon stochastic exchange economies with incomplete markets lack both a general existence proof for recursive competitive equilibria and a polynomial-time algorithm to approximate them. Classical Arrow-Debreu pseudo-games are static, Radner's finite-horizon framework does not extend to infinite horizons, and prior recursive equilibrium existence results were restricted to representative-agent or single-asset economies. This paper bridges the gap by introducing Markov pseudo-games, a dynamic generalization that simultaneously captures stochastic transitions and action-dependent feasibility.

## Input / Output

The method takes a fully specified infinite-horizon Markov exchange economy as structured input: n consumers with utility functions (linear, Cobb-Douglas, or Leontief), m commodities, l short-lived Arrow securities with state-contingent return matrices, endowment and type spaces, a transition kernel over world states, and a discount factor. It outputs a Markov policy profile parameterized by neural networks that specifies, for each state, equilibrium consumption and investment for every consumer plus commodity and asset prices — collectively an approximate recursive Radner equilibrium. The output is evaluated via three metrics: total first-order violations, average Bellman errors, and exploitability (sum of maximal unilateral payoff deviations).

## Method

The key insight is reformulating the set of recursive Radner equilibria of an infinite-horizon MEE as the set of generalized Markov perfect equilibria of an associated (n+1)-player Markov pseudo-game, where n consumers choose consumption-investment bundles subject to budget constraints that depend on the auctioneer's price action. Exploitability minimization in this pseudo-game is recast as an unconstrained min-max problem via a dependent-policy parameterization: the adversary's (inner) policy network explicitly receives the generator's (outer) policy as input, encoding the endogenous feasibility coupling. Training uses two-time-scale stochastic simultaneous gradient descent-ascent (TTSSGDA) with a differentiable game simulator that provides gradient estimates of rewards and transition probabilities along sampled trajectories. The resulting architecture, GAPNet, is a generative adversarial policy network whose generator produces equilibrium policies and whose adversary produces best-response deviations, with polynomial-time convergence to an approximate stationary point of exploitability under bounded best-response mismatch and Lipschitz smoothness assumptions.

## Results

The paper's primary contributions are theoretical: it establishes existence of generalized Markov perfect equilibria (GMPE) in concave Markov pseudo-games (Theorem 2.1) and proves that an (ε, δ)-stationary point of exploitability can be computed in polynomial time via two time-scale stochastic gradient descent-ascent (Theorem 2.2). Applied to infinite horizon Markov exchange economies, it proves the existence of recursive Radner equilibria (RRE) (Theorem 3.1) and polynomial-time approximability (Theorem 3.2). Empirically, GAPNet converges to near-zero exploitability across three utility specifications (linear, Cobb-Douglas, Leontief) and scales to a 20-consumer, 20-commodity, 5-asset, 10-state economy, with equilibrium consumption shares of 97–98% (linear), 88–90% (Cobb-Douglas), and ~99% (Leontief) of wealth allocated to current consumption.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies two previously separate lines of work—Markov games (Shapley, Fink, Takahashi) and Arrow-Debreu pseudo-games—into a single framework called Markov pseudo-games (MPGs), where dynamic uncertainty and action-dependent feasibility coexist. The economic side builds directly on Radner's (1972) finite-horizon stochastic exchange economies and Magill & Quinzii's (1994) infinite-horizon extension, but generalizes both by allowing incomplete markets with multiple assets and commodities. On the computational side, the method adapts the min-max reformulation of exploitability minimization from Goktas & Greenwald (2022) and the two-time-scale stochastic gradient descent-ascent (TTSSGDA) of Lin et al. (2020) and Daskalakis et al. (2020), combined with a differentiable game simulator (Suh et al., 2022), to the new MPG setting. The paper departs from prior RRE existence proofs, which were restricted to representative-agent, single-commodity, or single-asset economies, by establishing existence in the general multi-agent, multi-asset, incomplete-markets case.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The main impact is twofold: (1) it provides the first existence proof of recursive Radner equilibria in general incomplete infinite-horizon stochastic economies, closing a gap that has persisted since Magill & Quinzii (1994), and (2) it gives the first polynomial-time algorithm for approximating such equilibria, moving the field beyond the finite-horizon computational methods that have dominated macroeconomics. Limitations include relatively weak finite-time guarantees (stationary points of exploitability rather than exact equilibria), reliance on smoothness and bounded best-response mismatch assumptions that may not hold for all economically relevant primitives, and empirical validation limited to small economies (10–20 consumers). Open questions include whether the GAPNet approach scales to genuinely large economies with many assets and states, whether the smoothness assumptions can be relaxed for non-smooth utilities like Leontief, and whether the adversarial training paradigm offers fundamental advantages over direct first-order-condition minimization (NPM) in more complex settings.
