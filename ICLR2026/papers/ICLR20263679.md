## Problem

Standard score-based generative models (SGM) use additive Gaussian noise, forcing the latent distribution to be Gaussian regardless of the data. For heavy-tailed or anisotropic data, this creates an infinite KL divergence between data and latent, making it difficult to generate extreme events and tail behavior accurately. The paper asks whether a physically-motivated multiplicative noise structure can yield a tractable, data-aware latent distribution that naturally preserves the data's norm statistics.

## Input / Output

The method takes a dataset of M d-dimensional vectors (e.g., 16-point vorticity snapshots, 4D correlated Cauchy samples, or 1024-dim image patches) along with a pre-constructed d×d×d skew-symmetric tensor G and a time horizon T. It produces generated d-dimensional samples whose norm distribution exactly matches the training data's norm distribution (a direct consequence of the conservative dynamics), while the direction is uniformly distributed on the sphere. Sampling from the non-Gaussian latent reduces to a one-dimensional problem: draw a Gaussian direction, rescale its norm via an empirical CDF of the data log-norms, then integrate the reverse SDE.

## Method

The forward process is a Stratonovich SDE dx_s = G(x_s) ∘ dB_s where G is a linear map with skew-symmetric matrix components, inspired by incompressible transport noise in fluid dynamics. The skew-symmetry guarantees that the norm ‖x_s‖ is conserved along every trajectory (conservative dynamics), so the latent norm distribution equals the data norm distribution exactly, while the direction converges exponentially fast to uniform on the (d−1)-sphere. The stationary density p_∞(x) = p_|·|(‖x‖)/(‖x‖^{d−1}|S^{d−1}|) is explicitly characterized and is non-Gaussian unless the data norms follow a χ² law. For training, a neural network a_θ approximates G(x)^T ∇log p_{T−t}(x) via sliced score matching, which the paper proves equivalent to maximizing the ELBO in the multiplicative setting. Sampling initializes from the non-Gaussian latent (Gaussian direction rescaled by the eCDF-transformed norm) and integrates the reverse SDE or probability-flow ODE backward in time.

## Results

The paper evaluates MSGM against standard SGM on two tasks: a 4-dimensional correlated Cauchy distribution and 16-dimensional PIV vorticity fields (1024 samples). Performance is assessed via Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) and visual pair plots. For the Cauchy case, MSGM's MMD decreases with more ADAMS iterations while SGM's MMD diverges (Figure 4a). For the vorticity fields, MSGM achieves lower MMD than SGM across all training set sizes, with the advantage most pronounced in the low-data regime (Figure 4b). No single headline numerical score is reported; the comparison is primarily qualitative (pair plots) and convergence-based (MMD curves).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole baseline is the standard additive score-based generative model (SGM) of Song et al. (2021), trained with the same Sliced Score Matching loss for fair comparison. On the 4-D correlated Cauchy distribution, MSGM clearly outperforms SGM: SGM 'hardly reproduces fat tails and extreme directionality' while MSGM captures the power-law tails and anisotropic structure; with more training iterations, MSGM's MMD continues to decrease whereas SGM's MMD diverges. On the 16-D vorticity fields (1024 samples), SGM concentrates generated points near the center of the distribution and underestimates the tails of point-wise vorticity (i.e., rare large vorticity events), while MSGM better matches the ground-truth marginal tails and joint structure. The paper attributes MSGM's advantage to its latent distribution preserving the data norm distribution, making the forward and backward diffusions shorter and the latent space inherently closer to the data, especially for heavy-tailed or anisotropic distributions where the KL divergence to a Gaussian latent is infinite.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the standard additive-noise diffusion model (Song et al., 2021) by replacing the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck forward SDE with a multiplicative Stratonovich SDE driven by skew-symmetric noise, inspired by transport-noise models in fluid dynamics (Kraichnan, 1968; Resseguier et al., 2021). It retains the forward/backward SDE structure, sliced score matching (Song et al., 2020), and ELBO equivalence (Huang et al., 2021) of score-based generative models, but the skew-symmetry constraint fundamentally changes the latent geometry: instead of converging to a Gaussian, the forward process converges to a non-Gaussian distribution that preserves data norms. This positions MSGM as distinct from Riemannian diffusion models (De Bortoli et al., 2022; Huang et al., 2022), which restrict data to a manifold, and from ad hoc heavy-tailed diffusion variants (Yoon et al., 2023; Pandey et al., 2024), which impose Lévy or Student-t priors by hand rather than inheriting tail structure from the data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The key practical implication is that for heavy-tailed or anisotropic data, the MSGM latent distribution automatically matches the data's tail behavior (the KL divergence to a Gaussian latent is infinite for Cauchy data, while MSGM yields a finite value), potentially reducing the number of denoising steps needed and improving extreme-event generation in low-data regimes. However, significant limitations remain: the forward SDE lacks an analytic solution for large-rank tensors, the score has no closed form in finite time, and the dense third-order tensor G scales as d³, making it prohibitive for truly high-dimensional problems (the d=1024 image experiments rely on sparse tensors outside the theoretical framework). Open questions include whether the theoretical guarantees extend to non-linear or sparse G, whether the approach scales to d=O(10⁵) turbulent flow problems, and whether the physics-inspired inductive bias provides measurable benefits in data-scarce regimes beyond the moderate-dimension experiments shown.
