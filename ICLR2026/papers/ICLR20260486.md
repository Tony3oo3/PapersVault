## Problem

Post-training activation quantization below 8 bits suffers severe accuracy degradation due to outliers, and existing mitigation strategies (SmoothQuant, QuaRot, FlatQuant) operate exclusively along the feature dimension, ignoring the strong local correlations that exist across the sequence dimension in both text and image data. This paper identifies that sequence-dimension structure in intermediate activations remains unexploited for quantization, leaving a gap between the achievable and actual quantization fidelity at 4-bit precision.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260486/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

STaMP is a training-free post-processing step applied to intermediate activation tensors of shape (sequence_length × feature_size) within transformer blocks of LLMs and LVMs. For LVMs (PixArt-Σ, SANA), the end-to-end pipeline takes a text prompt and produces a generated image; for LLMs (LLaMA 3, Qwen 2.5), it takes text input and produces generated text. The method modifies only the quantization of activations before each linear layer, keeping the model weights and architecture unchanged, and is applied during the prompt-processing (prefill) phase for LLMs where multiple tokens are available simultaneously.

## Method

STaMP applies an invertible linear transformation L along the sequence dimension before quantization, then inverts it after the subsequent linear layer. The key insight is that natural data induces a Toeplitz-like autocorrelation structure in activations, so transforms like the Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) concentrate most activation energy into a small number of leading tokens while leaving the remainder at low energy. A mixed-precision scheme then assigns 8-bit quantization to the first 64 high-energy tokens and 4-bit to the rest, exploiting the fact that quantization error scales as energy/(2^b−1)², so extra bits on high-energy tokens yield disproportionate error reduction. Theorem 1 formalizes this by upper-bounding the quantization error as a weighted sum of token energies, and Jensen's inequality proves that energy concentration with non-uniform bit allocation strictly reduces the bound versus uniform quantization. The DWT is preferred over the optimal Karhunen-Loève Transform for its O(ds) complexity and its natural creation of discrete energy levels that map cleanly to two hardware-supported precision levels, adding less than 5% latency overhead.

## Results

On PixArt-Σ (W4A4, block size 64), STaMP consistently improves SQNR across all baselines: SVDQuant rises from 8.78 to 9.72 dB on COCO and 8.83 to 9.75 dB on MJHQ, while RTN improves from 5.88 to 6.16 dB (COCO). On LLMs (W4A4KV4), STaMP reduces perplexity for every baseline: on Llama 3 8B, FlatQuant drops from 6.89 to 6.77 PPL and QuaRot from 9.05 to 8.66; on the harder Qwen 2.5 3B, QuaRot PPL falls dramatically from 94.86 to 71.13. The effective activation bit-width overhead is minimal (4.0625 bits on PixArt-Σ, 4.125 bits on LLMs) since only 64 tokens are kept at 8 bits.

## Baselines & Comparisons

STaMP is designed as a complementary add-on rather than a competitor to existing quantization methods, and the results confirm this: adding the DWT sequence transform on top of any baseline (RTN, SmoothQuant, QuaRot, ViDiT-Q, SVDQuant, FlatQuant) consistently improves the metric. On LVMs, the largest gains appear for SVDQuant on PixArt-Σ (COCO SQNR 8.78→9.72 dB, +0.94 dB) and for RTN on SANA (COCO SQNR 8.63→9.32 dB, +0.69 dB), while Image Reward improvements are most dramatic for RTN on PixArt-Σ (0.38→0.80). On LLMs, the improvement is most striking for QuaRot on Qwen 2.5 3B (PPL 94.86→71.13, a 24-point reduction) and for RTN on Llama 3.2 1B (1795→700), indicating STaMP is especially valuable when the base quantization is far from FP performance. FlatQuant, the strongest LLM baseline, still benefits: Llama 3 8B PPL drops from 6.89 to 6.77 and Llama 3.2 3B from 12.71 to 12.40. The overhead is negligible—DWT adds only 0.21% extra FLOPs and ~4.8% CUDA latency per denoising step, comparable to the Hadamard feature transform (0.24% FLOPs, 3.0% latency).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the function-preserving transform framework (van Breugel et al., 2025) from the feature dimension to the sequence dimension, introducing invertible linear transforms L applied across tokens rather than channels. The key conceptual leap is drawing an explicit analogy to classical media compression (JPEG's DCT, JPEG2000's DWT), where local spatial correlation enables energy compaction and adaptive bit allocation — here applied to the strong local correlation in LLM/LVM intermediate activations. STaMP is explicitly positioned as orthogonal to and complementary with existing feature transforms (QuaRot, SmoothQuant, FlatQuant) and weight quantization methods (SVDQuant, GPTQ), since sequence transforms do not modify weights.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

STaMP provides a training-free, low-overhead (<5% runtime) add-on that consistently improves W4A4 quantization across both LLMs and LVMs without requiring any manual tuning, making it a practical component for production quantization pipelines. A key limitation is that for LLMs, the sequence transform only applies during the prefill (prompt-processing) phase, since token-by-token generation has no sequence to transform; this restricts its benefit to the compute-bound prefill stage. Open questions include whether more sophisticated bit-allocation schemes beyond the simple 64-token/8-bit + rest/4-bit split could yield further gains, and whether the approach extends to autoregressive decoding with sliding-window or chunked attention.
