## Problem

Best-of-N sampling suffers from reward hacking: as N grows, the reward model selects atypical responses that exploit spurious correlations rather than genuine quality, causing performance to degrade. Prior mitigations either improve the reward model (fundamentally limited by the intractability of characterizing all hacking strategies) or apply distributional regularization that is too conservative to exploit additional compute. The paper asks whether a BoN scheme can be both robust to reward hacking and still leverage the full benefits of inference-time scaling.

## Input / Output

Given a text prompt and N candidate responses generated by an LM (e.g., Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct via vLLM), the method scores each response with a frozen reward model and a lightweight predictor network. The predictor's output is compared against a fixed target (the hidden state at layer L of the reward model) to produce a per-response uncertainty estimate. The final output is the single response with the highest pessimistic reward r̂(x,y) − λ·α(x,y), where all forward passes (reward model, predictor, target) can be fully parallelized and the uncertainty computation is on the same order as the reward model evaluation due to feature reuse.

## Method

Caution inverts the curiosity principle from RL: where curiosity rewards prediction error as a novelty signal for exploration, caution penalizes prediction error as a distributional uncertainty signal for pessimistic selection. Concretely, a fixed target network T(x,y) = h_L^R(x,y) extracts hidden states from layer L of the frozen reward model, and a lightweight trainable predictor P_θ(x,y) is trained fully offline on typical LM-sampled responses to match T via MSE loss. At inference, the squared prediction error α(x,y) = ‖P_θ(x,y) − T(x,y)‖² serves as the uncertainty estimate, and selection uses the lower confidence bound r_LCB(x,y) = r̂(x,y) − λ·α(x,y). Unlike RND, which distills random network features, caution distills reward model features, providing semantic grounding that the ablations show is critical for effective OOD detection. Unlike distributional regularization approaches that constrain the sampling distribution, caution applies pessimism directly to per-response reward estimates, allowing genuinely better but slightly atypical responses to still be selected.

## Results

On GSM8K (in-distribution), RM+Pessimism achieves 82.6% peak accuracy and 81.1% final accuracy at N=512, with only 1.5 points of degradation versus 7.7 points for standard BoN (79.3% peak, 71.5% final). On the out-of-distribution MATH-500, Pessimism-Only leads with 13.6% peak and 11.9% final accuracy (1.7 pts degradation), while RM+Pessimism achieves 12.3% peak and 10.1% final. On the fully out-of-domain BigBench-Hard, Pessimism-Only dramatically outperforms all other methods, reaching 22.9% peak and 22.1% final accuracy with only 0.9 pts degradation, compared to a catastrophic 1.7% final accuracy for standard BoN (15.6 pts degradation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against standard BoN (Reward Model only, following Brown et al. 2024), caution (RM+Pessimism) wins decisively on GSM8K, improving peak accuracy by 3.3 points (82.6% vs 79.3%) and final accuracy by 9.6 points (81.1% vs 71.5%), while reducing degradation from 7.7 to 1.5 points. On MATH-500, Pessimism-Only outperforms standard BoN by 2.1 points in peak (13.6% vs 11.5%) and 3.4 points in final accuracy (11.9% vs 8.5%). On BigBench-Hard, the gap is dramatic: Pessimism-Only achieves 22.1% final accuracy versus 1.7% for standard BoN, a 20.4-point improvement, because the reward model's spurious correlations cause catastrophic failure at large N. Against Traditional RND (Burda et al. 2018) as the uncertainty estimator, caution (distilling reward model features) consistently outperforms across all λ values on GSM8K; at the optimal λ=0.8, caution reaches 82.1% peak and 81.0% final accuracy versus 78.1% and 74.9% for RND, a 4.0-point final accuracy gap. The paper attributes this to the semantic grounding provided by reward model representations, which capture task-relevant distributional boundaries that randomly initialized features cannot.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes the curiosity-driven exploration mechanism from online RL (Pathak et al., 2017; Burda et al., 2018) as an offline OOD detector, inverting its role from rewarding novelty to penalizing it. It applies the pessimism principle from offline RL (Jin et al., 2021) at the individual reward-estimate level rather than at the distribution level, departing from Huang et al. (2025b) whose χ²-regularized BoN is theoretically optimal but overly conservative in practice, and from Jinnai et al. (2024) whose Wasserstein-based regularization scales quadratically in N. The key conceptual move is framing 'caution' as the exact dual of curiosity: the same prediction-error signal that drives optimistic exploration in online RL becomes a pessimistic penalty in the offline inference-time setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The approach is computationally cheap (two additional forward passes, fully parallelizable with the reward model) and requires only prompts for training the predictor, making it practical for deployment. The theoretical analysis provides, to the authors' knowledge, the first formal guarantee that RND-style prediction error approximates OOD distance in a linear and two-layer ReLU setting, lending credibility to a heuristic widely used in RL. Open questions include whether the method extends to non-binary rewards, larger models, and safety-critical alignment tasks, and whether curiosity signals can be repurposed as explicit exploration rewards during inference-time or RL post-training. The finding that on fully out-of-domain tasks (BigBench-Hard) the uncertainty penalty alone outperforms the combined reward+uncertainty score suggests reward models can be actively harmful under distribution shift, a concern with broader implications for inference-time scaling reliability.
