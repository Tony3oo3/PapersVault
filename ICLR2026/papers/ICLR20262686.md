## Problem

Imputing missing node features in graphs under extreme sparsity (up to 99.5% missing) remains challenging: latent-space and end-to-end GNN methods degrade sharply and fall below simple baselines, while existing diffusion-based approaches apply uniform propagation that ignores local structural variation, causing over-smoothing and error accumulation across the graph. No prior method simultaneously adapts propagation sharpness to local connectivity, controls the order of information flow from reliable to uncertain regions, and preserves class-level discriminative structure in the imputed features.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an undirected graph adjacency matrix, a partially observed node feature matrix X ∈ R^{N×F}, a binary mask M indicating which entries are observed, and a small labeled subset for pseudo-label assignment. It outputs a fully imputed feature matrix X̂ that is passed to a standard GCN for downstream node classification or link prediction. Notably, the imputation pipeline itself is parameter-free (no learned weights in the diffusion stages); the only learned component is a lightweight GCN classifier used solely to generate pseudo-labels for the class-aware refinement step, making the approach lightweight and scalable to large graphs where VAE-based methods run out of memory.

## Method

FSD-CAP is a two-stage, parameter-free imputation framework. In the first stage (FSD), a fractional diffusion operator A^γ = (A_ij)^γ / Σ_k(A_ik)^γ interpolates between uniform neighbor averaging (γ→0) and deterministic nearest-neighbor routing (γ→∞), adapting propagation sharpness to local edge-strength structure; this operator is applied within progressively expanding subgraphs that grow outward from observed nodes by shortest-path distance, with a retention coefficient λ blending each layer's estimate with the previous layer's converged result to limit error accumulation. In the second stage (CAP), a GCN assigns pseudo-labels to unlabeled nodes, and for each class a virtual anchor node is created with an entropy-weighted aggregate feature; a single propagation step within each class-specific graph (weighted by predicted class confidence) pulls uncertain nodes toward their class prototype while down-weighting boundary nodes with high neighborhood label entropy, thereby enhancing intra-class coherence and inter-class separation in the final imputed features.

## Results

At 99.5% feature missingness, FSD-CAP achieves 80.06% average node classification accuracy under structural missing and 81.01% under uniform missing across five benchmark datasets (Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed, Photo, Computers), approaching the 81.31% of a GCN trained on fully observed features. For link prediction under the same extreme sparsity, FSD-CAP reaches 91.65% AUC (structural) and 92.41% AUC (uniform), compared to 95.06% for the fully observed case. On CiteSeer, FSD-CAP even surpasses the full-feature GCN (71.94% vs. 70.00% structural; 73.15% vs. 70.00% uniform), demonstrating that the imputation process can act as a regularizer.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FSD-CAP outperforms all seven baselines across all five datasets in node classification at 99.5% missingness. The closest competitor, PCFI (Um et al., 2023), achieves 76.39% average accuracy (structural) and 78.77% (uniform), yielding FSD-CAP gains of 3.67% and 2.24% respectively; on Cora specifically, FSD-CAP beats PCFI by 6.90% (80.56% vs. 75.36%). The global diffusion baseline FP (Rossi et al., 2022) trails by a wider margin at 73.68% (structural) and 77.22% (uniform). Deep learning-based methods degrade severely: GRAFENNE (Gupta et al., 2023) drops to 37.92% average, ASD-VAE (Jiang et al., 2024) to 30–44% and hits OOM on PubMed/Computers, and ITR (Tu et al., 2022) is inapplicable to uniform missing and also OOMs on large-scale datasets. PaGCN (Zhang et al., 2024) performs worst among applicable methods at 43.54% average. On heterophily datasets (Cornell, Texas, Wisconsin, Chameleon), FSD-CAP averages 56.22% (structural) and 58.85% (uniform), surpassing GPR-GNN (Chien et al., 2020) at 43.70%/43.98% and Hop-GNN at 44.97%/44.61%, confirming robustness under weak homophily.

## Relation to Prior Work

FSD-CAP extends the diffusion-based feature imputation paradigm established by FP (Rossi et al., 2022) and the graph diffusion framework of GDC (Gasteiger et al., 2019). Where prior diffusion methods apply uniform, global propagation across all nodes, FSD-CAP introduces three departures: a fractional exponent that adapts propagation sharpness to local edge structure, a distance-guided progressive subgraph expansion that replaces global diffusion with layer-wise localized updates, and a class-aware refinement stage that injects semantic structure via pseudo-labels and neighborhood entropy. The method thus positions itself between lightweight parameter-free diffusion (FP, PCFI) and learned imputation models (ITR, ASD-VAE, GRAFENNE), aiming to retain the robustness of diffusion while addressing its over-smoothing and error-accumulation weaknesses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that a largely parameter-free imputation pipeline (the FSD stage requires no training) can recover node features at 99.5% missingness to within ~1.25% of full-feature GCN accuracy, and in some cases (CiteSeer) even exceed it, while remaining scalable to 169K-node graphs where learned methods fail with OOM errors. This suggests that for extreme sparsity, carefully structured diffusion may be a more reliable and efficient strategy than learned generative models. Open questions include: the class-aware stage still requires training a GCN classifier, so the method is not fully parameter-free; the optimal temperature T varies dramatically across datasets and tasks (T=5 on Cora vs. T=0.001 on Photo for link prediction), indicating sensitivity that limits plug-and-play deployment; and the homophily assumption underlying diffusion-based propagation, while partially addressed by testing on heterophily graphs, remains a structural limitation for strongly heterophilic settings.
