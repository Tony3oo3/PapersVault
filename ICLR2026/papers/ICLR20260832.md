## Problem

Existing computer-use benchmarks (WebArena, OSWorld, WorkArena, CRMArena) fail to capture the full complexity of enterprise-grade CRM workflows: they are limited to service-only use cases, restrict tasks to read-only information retrieval, or lack fine-grained process-level evaluation. SCUBA addresses this gap by providing 300 realistic tasks derived from real user interviews across three personas (platform administrator, sales, service) that require both reading and writing data, navigating complex enterprise UIs, and executing multi-step business workflows in a live Salesforce sandbox.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260832/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Agents receive a natural-language task query plus environment observations that differ by agent type: browser-use agents see Set-of-Mark annotated screenshots paired with structured DOM text (element index, attributes, labels), while computer-use agents see only full-desktop screenshots at 1920×1080. In the demonstration-augmented setting, two human-annotated trajectory summaries are appended to the query. The rule-based evaluator (built on Salesforce Metadata and Tooling APIs) returns a binary success flag, a continuous milestone score in [0,1] decomposed into weighted rubric items identifying exactly which sub-steps failed, plus wall-clock time, step count, token consumption, and API cost per task.

## Method

SCUBA's core contribution is a four-phase task construction pipeline: (1) template creation from user interviews, each paired with a Trailhead knowledge article and difficulty label; (2) generation of five distinct queries per template via value-filling and GPT-5 paraphrasing, manually verified for diversity and ethics; (3) per-task initialization scripts and hand-crafted API-based evaluators that produce milestone-level process rewards; and (4) human annotation for quality control and demonstration extraction. The environment uses a Salesforce sandbox developer org with a snapshot-based reset mechanism (diffing configuration files against initial state) rather than container restart, enabling task-level reversion. Parallel evaluation is supported via headless browser sessions for browser-use agents and Dockerized Ubuntu desktops for computer-use agents, completing the full 300-task suite in under 90 minutes. Nine agents spanning three design paradigms (foundational VLMs with toolboxes, native GUI agents, and agentic frameworks) are benchmarked under both zero-shot and demonstration-augmented settings, revealing that browser-use agents with closed-source backbones achieve up to 51% success while open-source computer-use agents score below 5%, and that human demonstrations improve success to ~50% while reducing time and cost by 13% and 16% respectively.

## Results

On SCUBA (300 tasks), the best zero-shot browser-use agent (GPT-5) achieves 51.33% task success rate and 0.73 milestone score, while the best zero-shot computer-use agent (Agent-S2.5 w/GPT-5) reaches 39.00% success rate. Open-source computer-use models (UI-TARS-1.5-7B, OpenCUA-7B, GUI-Owl-7B) achieve less than 3% success rate in zero-shot, revealing a massive gap versus closed-source models. Under the demonstration-augmented setting, success rates improve to ~50% (GPT-5: 53.85%, Claude-4-sonnet computer: 47.69%) while simultaneously reducing average execution time by 13% and costs by 16%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPT-5 as a browser-use agent leads all methods with 51.33% zero-shot success rate (0.73 milestone score), outperforming o3 (45.67%) and Agent-S2.5 w/GPT-5 (39.00%, the best computer-use agent). The gap between closed-source and open-source models is stark: UI-TARS-1.5-7B, OpenCUA-7B, and GUI-Owl-7B all score below 3% success rate in zero-shot, while closed-source counterparts (GPT-5, Claude-4-sonnet, OpenAI-CUA) range from 16% to 51%. Browser-use agents consistently outperform computer-use agents at the same backbone (e.g., Claude-4-sonnet: 34.67% browser vs. 27.00% computer in zero-shot), attributed to richer observation (SOM+DOM) and more efficient action space. Under demonstration augmentation, Gemini-2.5-Pro shows the largest relative improvement (31.00%→46.15%, +15.15 pp), while GPT-5 sees only a modest gain (51.33%→53.85%) due to its already high baseline. OpenAI-CUA benefits substantially from demonstrations (16.00%→28.85%), and Claude-4-sonnet in computer-use mode nearly closes the gap with browser-use agents (27.00%→47.69% vs. 34.67%→46.15%).

## Relation to Prior Work

SCUBA extends the enterprise-task benchmark lineage initiated by WorkArena (ServiceNow, service-only tasks) and CRMArena (Salesforce, read-only/tool-use tasks) by introducing the first benchmark that simultaneously supports human annotations, process-reward milestone scoring, read/write data manipulation, and multiple business personas (admin, sales, service). The agent evaluation infrastructure is adapted from the browser-use framework (for DOM-based browser agents) and OSWorld (for screenshot-based computer-use agents), with significant modifications to the DOM parser for Salesforce's complex UI. Conceptually, SCUBA carves out a new niche by targeting the full spectrum of enterprise CRM workflows—navigation, data manipulation, workflow automation, information retrieval, and troubleshooting—rather than the narrower service-desk or information-retrieval focus of prior enterprise benchmarks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCUBA exposes a critical and quantified gap: open-source computer-use agents that perform competitively on OSWorld achieve less than 5% success on enterprise CRM tasks, while closed-source models reach up to 39–51%, revealing that current open models lack the domain knowledge and grounding robustness needed for real enterprise software. The finding that demonstration augmentation improves success to ~50% while reducing time and cost by 13–16% offers a practical, low-cost path to closing part of this gap. Open questions remain around grounding accuracy for screenshot-only agents, navigation dead-end recovery, error introspection, and whether the results generalize beyond Salesforce to other enterprise platforms. The benchmark's limitation that rule-based evaluators cannot detect unsafe or unintended environment modifications also leaves a safety dimension unaddressed.
