## Problem

Pretrained audio models (PTMs) are fragile in real-world continual learning because audio backbones emphasize low-level spectral details over structured semantics, causing severe upstream-downstream misalignment and representation shifts that far exceed those in vision. This paper provides the first systematic benchmark for audio CL with PTMs, revealing that vision-domain PEFT-based CL methods degrade substantially on audio, and identifying two distinct failure modes: representation saturation in coarse-grained tasks and representation shifts in fine-grained tasks.

## Input / Output

Input is a raw audio signal converted to a 512×128 Mel spectrogram (STFT + Mel filtering), truncated to 5.12 seconds per clip. Output is a class label predicted by an exemplar-free recursive analytic classifier (not a parametric head), which is updated in closed form via the Woodbury identity over second-order statistics of projected features. Evaluation is performed over the accumulated label space across all sessions seen so far, with no stored exemplars.

## Method

PACE is a unified, stage-wise framework with three components. (1) Improved First-Session Adaptation (FSA) freezes the output head and adapts only deeper transformer layers (l ≥ L_tune, determined by CKA deviation threshold) via LoRA, using a staged training schedule that first warms up the head at low LR then fine-tunes the backbone with the head fixed, before replacing the head with an analytic classifier. (2) Adaptive Multi-Session Subspace-Orthogonal PEFT (MSA) extends adaptation to fine-grained scenarios by adding session-specific LoRA modules while projecting gradients onto the null space of previously acquired representations (computed via LoRA subtraction + SVD of the unlearned model's feature covariance), enabling progressive semantic alignment without distorting earlier knowledge. (3) Boundary-aware regularization generates time-frequency masking perturbations to identify boundary-prone samples and applies a margin loss that pulls features toward class centroids while pushing them away from detected boundary points, improving inter-class separability during MSA.

## Results

PACE achieves the best average top-1 accuracy across all six audio CL benchmarks, reaching 95.75% on ESC-50, 97.49% on US8K, 91.87% on SC2, 90.95% on TIMIT-2, 94.05% on TIMIT-3, and 69.08% on VocalSet. On coarse-grained tasks, PACE narrows the gap to the joint-training upper bound to just 0.75% (ESC-50) and 0.58% (US8K). On fine-grained tasks, PACE delivers gains of +5.3% over RanPAC on TIMIT-2, +4.1% on TIMIT-3, and +6.3% on VocalSet, while reducing the joint-training gap to 4.27%, 1.17%, and 7.57% respectively.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PACE outperforms all 12 baselines across all six benchmarks. Among PEFT-based CL methods, HiDe-Prompt is the strongest (83.75% on ESC-50, 47.78% on TIMIT-2) but still trails PACE by 12.0% and 43.2% respectively, with prompt-based methods like L2P and DualPrompt collapsing to near-chance on fine-grained tasks (e.g., 1.50% on TIMIT-2). Among statistics-based methods, RanPAC with FSA is the closest competitor (92.25% on ESC-50, 85.63% on TIMIT-2, 62.82% on VocalSet), yet PACE improves upon it by +3.5%, +5.3%, and +6.3% respectively, demonstrating that the improved FSA and subspace-orthogonal MSA components are critical for closing the gap to joint training. ACL (High-Order) is consistently below RanPAC, confirming that the recursive Woodbury-based update in RanPAC is more effective for audio. Notably, PACE achieves within 0.8% of the joint-training upper bound on coarse-grained tasks and within 4.3% on the most challenging fine-grained TIMIT-2, whereas the best baseline (RanPAC) leaves gaps of 4.25% and 9.59% on those same benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the pretrained-model continual learning (CL) paradigm—dominated by vision-domain PEFT methods like L2P, DualPrompt, and HiDe-PET—into the audio domain, where it identifies a fundamental incompatibility: audio backbones (e.g., EAT) encode low-level spectral patterns rather than structured semantics, causing severe cross-session representation shifts that break prompt-based and shared-representation CL strategies. The technical route instead builds on the analytic classifier lineage (RanPAC, ACL) with second-order statistics and the Woodbury identity, augmented by LoRA-based layer-selective adaptation and a novel subspace-orthogonal gradient projection inspired by LoRA Subtraction. Boundary-aware regularization repurposes SpecAugment-style time-frequency masking as a geometric regularizer rather than a data augmentation, which is a conceptual departure from its original use.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first systematic benchmark for audio CL with PTMs, and its central empirical finding—that vision-domain PEFT-based CL methods degrade roughly threefold on audio—provides a clear diagnostic for why cross-modal transfer of CL techniques fails and redirects the field toward analytic-classifier-based routes for audio. The remaining gap to joint training on fine-grained tasks (up to 7.6% on VocalSet) and the reliance on a single spectrogram-based backbone family (EAT/SSLAM) leave open whether the identified challenges are specific to spectrogram reconstruction pretraining or more general to audio representation learning. The benchmark and codebase release should lower the barrier for future audio CL research, but the method's multi-stage design (FSA → MSA → freeze) introduces non-trivial hyperparameter sensitivity that may limit practical adoption.
