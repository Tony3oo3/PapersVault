## Problem

While reasoning LLMs (RLLMs) generate step-by-step chains of thought that improve performance on complex tasks, it remains unclear how robust these reasoning traces are to disruptions that occur mid-reasoning. Prior work on self-correction focused on non-reasoning LLMs or on misleading initial prompts rather than on intervening within a model's own unfolding CoT, leaving open whether RLLMs can detect and recover from localized errors, noise, or adversarial injections during their own reasoning, and at what computational cost.

## Input / Output

The framework takes a model's own CoT trace (segmented into steps by double newlines), applies one of seven interventions at one of five timesteps (0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, defined as cumulative character-length fractions), and then samples N=8 continuations from the same model that generated the original trace. Outputs include three strictness levels of robustness (at-least-once, majority, all-correct), a doubtfulness score computed over the 20 sentences following the intervention point, and the percentage change in CoT length relative to the original trace. Each model only continues from its own CoT, ensuring that recovery is measured without cross-model contamination.

## Method

The paper introduces a controlled intervention benchmark with seven perturbations spanning benign (paraphrasing, continuation by another model), neutral (random character insertion, Wikipedia text), and adversarial (incorrect reasoning step, hallucinated fact, unrelated CoT start) categories, applied at five fixed timesteps to nine open-weight RLLMs across MATH, SCIENCE, and LOGIC domains. CoTs are segmented by the double-newline convention, and at each target timestep only the last step R_k is modified while all prior steps are preserved, isolating the effect of localized disruption. Robustness is measured via sampling-based metrics over 8 independent continuations, yielding 2.9 million total intervened reasoning chains. A key analytical contribution is the identification of short doubt expressions (e.g., 'wait', 'let me check') as a central recovery mechanism: an LLM-based classifier (validated at Cohen's Kappa 0.8742) shows doubt spikes immediately after interventions and returns to baseline within ~5 sentences, while paraphrasing suppresses doubt below baseline and consistently reduces accuracy. Linear probes on residual-stream activations further reveal that models internally represent their native reasoning style differently from paraphrased text, explaining the non-invariance to surface form.

## Results

Across 9 open-weight RLLMs and 7 intervention types at 5 timesteps, majority robustness is generally high (>90% for most model–intervention pairs), with the smallest model (R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) showing the weakest recovery (e.g., 73–83% under neutral text insertion on MATH). Paraphrasing (Rewrite Trace) is the most disruptive intervention, consistently yielding the lowest robustness scores and shortening CoT length by ~60% while suppressing doubt expressions below baseline (0.068 vs. 0.153). Neutral interventions (random text, random characters) inflate CoT length by 50–250% depending on model size, with the 1.5B model showing up to +665% overhead. On BIG-Bench Mistake, RLLMs substantially outperform the non-reasoning GPT-4 baseline (e.g., o3: 88.7% Dyck vs. GPT-4: 17.1%; QwQ-32B: 66.7% vs. 17.1%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not propose a new method to compare against external baselines; instead, it benchmarks 9 open-weight RLLMs against each other and against non-reasoning LLMs. On BIG-Bench Mistake, all RLLMs substantially outperform GPT-4 (e.g., QwQ-32B achieves 66.7% on Dyck Languages vs. GPT-4's 17.1%; o3 reaches 88.7%), confirming that RL-trained reasoning models develop metacognitive error-detection capabilities absent in non-reasoning models. Among RLLMs, larger models are consistently more robust: R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B shows the weakest recovery (e.g., 73% majority robustness under neutral text insertion at t=0.1 on MATH), while 32B models (QwQ-32B, EXAONE-Deep-32B, R1-Distill-Qwen-32B) maintain >90% across nearly all interventions. Intervention type matters: paraphrasing (Rewrite Trace) is the only intervention that consistently degrades robustness below other types across all models, while benign continuation and adversarial wrong-continuation interventions are handled with near-perfect recovery by larger models. Swapping experiments show that continuing a weak model's trace with QwQ-32B yields ~98% recovery regardless of the original model, whereas swapping to the 1.5B model degrades to ~67%, indicating that overall model capability is the primary driver of robustness rather than trace-specific factors.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the LLM self-correction literature (Huang et al. 2024, Tyen et al. 2024, Zhou et al. 2024) from non-reasoning models to RLLMs, and departs from BIG-Bench Mistake (which tests error detection on externally provided CoTs) and Yang et al. (2025) (which uses prompt attacks with foreign CoTs) by intervening on the model's own ongoing CoT at controlled timesteps. The intervention design draws on adversarial robustness and prompt-injection literature, but the key conceptual move is treating the model's own reasoning trace as the substrate for perturbation, enabling a faithful measurement of in-context recovery. The RL-based self-correction methods (ScoRe, S²R) inform the training context but are not directly compared; this paper is diagnostic rather than prescriptive.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of short doubt expressions (e.g., 'wait', 'let me check') as the central recovery mechanism is actionable: the ablation showing that simply appending 'Wait' after an intervention improves robustness by single-digit percentage points suggests concrete SFT/RL training targets. The style non-invariance finding (paraphrasing suppresses doubt and degrades accuracy) reveals a previously uncharacterized fragility that matters for any deployment where CoTs are reformatted or summarized. The quantification of recovery cost (up to 250% CoT inflation under neutral noise) highlights a robustness-efficiency trade-off that current training objectives do not address. Open questions include whether style-invariance can be trained without suppressing the doubt mechanism, and whether the compute overhead can be reduced through targeted RL rewards on recovery length.
