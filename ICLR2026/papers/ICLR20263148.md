## Problem

EEG-based brain signal research and egocentric vision research remain fundamentally disconnected: EEG studies rely on passive, screen-based stimuli in controlled labs, while egocentric datasets capture observable behavior but not internal cognitive states. No prior dataset or systematic study has synchronized first-person video with brain activity during natural, real-world human-environment interactions, leaving the interplay between perception and cognition unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263148/cropped_page3_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes temporally aligned egocentric video (1080P/30 Hz, GoPro HERO12) and 32-channel EEG (256 Hz, Emotiv FLEX 2 Gel System) segmented via a sliding window of duration Δt with stride δt, where each window contains N^v video frames (downsampled to K frames at 224×224) and N^b EEG samples (band-pass filtered 0.5–50 Hz). Output is structured as Q consecutive queries evenly dividing the session timeline, each producing a 29-way action label and a 10-way verb label via modality-specific CLS tokens, evaluated under cross-subject-only and cross-subject & cross-scene protocols.

## Method

Brain-TIM processes each modality through frozen pretrained encoders (VideoMAE for video, LaBraM for EEG), projects them into a shared D-dimensional space via modality-specific embedding layers, and feeds the concatenated token sequence into a Transformer encoder. The key architectural insight is the Time-Interval MLP (TIM), which computes interval-based temporal embeddings from (start, end) timestamps and appends them to every feature and CLS token, explicitly encoding the shared temporal structure. Modality-specific learnable vectors distinguish visual from EEG tokens, and symmetric CLS tokens (one per modality per query) enable independent yet jointly trained classification heads. The model uses temporal fusion—concatenating visual and brain feature blocks sequentially—rather than spatial concatenation, which ablations show is critical for fine-grained 29-way action recognition. Training minimizes a weighted sum of modality-specific cross-entropy losses (L = L_v + λ·L_b) with separate heads for verb and action, deliberately excluding the coarse four-level category hierarchy from supervision.

## Results

On the EgoBrain benchmark, Brain-TIM (Visual+Brain) achieves 80.16% top-1 action accuracy and 90.11% verb accuracy under the cross-subject-only protocol, and 66.70% action accuracy and 83.43% verb accuracy under the more challenging cross-subject & cross-scene protocol. The visual-only baseline reaches 78.44% and 63.40% action accuracy respectively, while the brain-only model achieves only 8.44% and 9.36% action accuracy. The multimodal fusion yields a 1.72% absolute gain over vision-only in the cross-subject setting and a 3.30% gain in the cross-scene setting on the 29-way action task.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external methods; instead, it benchmarks the multimodal Brain-TIM against its own unimodal ablations (Visual-only and Brain-only) under two evaluation protocols. On the 29-way action task, Visual+Brain outperforms Visual-only by 1.72% (cross-subject) and 3.30% (cross-subject & cross-scene), and outperforms Brain-only by 71.72% and 57.34% respectively. On the 10-way verb task, the multimodal model gains 1.16% and 1.76% over Visual-only in the two protocols. The EEG encoder contributes only 5.8M parameters (~1/52 of the visual backbone), yet still delivers statistically significant improvements, confirming that the gains stem from complementary cognitive information rather than model capacity. Under the cross-scene protocol, the visual-only model degrades substantially (78.44% → 63.40% action accuracy) due to domain shift, while the brain-only model actually improves slightly (8.44% → 9.36%) because EEG captures head-centered neural dynamics invariant to environment changes.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously disconnected research lines: EEG-based brain-computer interface decoding (which has focused on controlled, screen-based stimuli) and egocentric video action recognition (which captures external behavior but not internal neural states). Methodologically, Brain-TIM is a direct adaptation of the Time Interval Machine (TIM, Chalk et al., 2024) from audio-visual to EEG-visual fusion, reusing the Time-Interval MLP for temporal encoding and the sliding-window mechanism for alignment. For feature extraction, it leverages two strong pretrained backbones—VideoMAE (Tong et al., 2022) for video and LaBraM (Jiang et al., 2024) for EEG—keeping them frozen and learning only lightweight fusion layers. The core conceptual departure from prior work is the dataset itself: no existing resource synchronizes real-world egocentric video with concurrent EEG during natural, multi-category daily activities.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

EgoBrain (61 hours, 40 subjects, 29 actions) is the first large-scale benchmark enabling research on how neural signals complement first-person visual perception for action understanding, opening a new direction in multimodal BCI. The finding that EEG's contribution is task-dependent—helpful under visual occlusion (Drink: 0.87→0.94) and for cognitively demanding actions (Play: 0.46→0.64) but potentially harmful when visual motion cues are already unambiguous (Write: 0.83→0.77)—provides a nuanced understanding of when and why brain signals add value. Key limitations include the seated-only protocol (limiting locomotion-related activities), controlled laboratory environments, and a relatively small action vocabulary (29 classes), leaving open questions about scalability to fully unconstrained, long-duration real-world scenarios and higher-density EEG recordings.
