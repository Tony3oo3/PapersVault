## Problem

Training reasoning LLMs via SFT or RL lacks a principled probabilistic objective: SFT depends on costly curated traces and may not generalize, while RL training is unstable, can collapse output diversity, and—despite being widely used—has a previously unrecognized implicit bias toward easier questions. This paper asks whether variational inference can provide a unified, stable objective that subsumes and clarifies existing SFT/RL approaches.

## Input / Output

The model takes a text question prompt x and produces a structured two-part output: a thinking trace z delimited by  tags, followed by a final answer y delimited by </answer> tags. During training, the variational posterior q_φ additionally receives an answer hint y' (sampled from the oracle correct-answer set) wrapped in special hint tokens, which conditions the generation of thinking traces more likely to yield correct answers. At inference time, only x is provided and the model generates z then y autoregressively.

## Method

The paper treats thinking traces as latent variables and derives an ELBO for the log-likelihood of producing a correct answer, extended to an IWAE-style multi-trace bound (K rollouts) that tightens with more samples. To stabilize training of the variational posterior, a forward-KL objective is proposed instead of the standard reverse-KL, which prevents the posterior from collapsing into shortcut reasoning and makes better use of answer hints. The gradient of the IWAE bound uses importance weights ρ_k combining a geometric-mean token-level likelihood ratio (to reduce variance over long traces) and an accuracy-based estimator for the conditional probability of a correct answer. A key theoretical contribution shows that rejection-sampling finetuning and binary-reward RL (including GRPO) are equivalent to local forward-KL objectives implicitly weighted by model accuracy, revealing a systematic bias toward easier questions that the proposed unweighted variational objective avoids.

## Results

On Qwen3-4B-Base, the proposed variational reasoning method (Ours-PB-Acc-4B) achieves 55.72 average on math benchmarks (MATH500, AIME24/25, AMC23, OlympiadBench) and 46.12 on general benchmarks (GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench, MMLU-Pro), outperforming the strongest SFT baseline Bespoke-Stratos-4B by +4.37 and +5.72 points respectively. Scaling to Qwen3-8B-Base yields 62.77/54.69, and to Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct yields 72.01/67.21, consistently surpassing all baselines. The method also shows stronger Pass@K gains on harder tasks (e.g., LiveCodeBench-Hard) compared to easier ones, and outperforms General-Reasoner-4B on out-of-distribution benchmarks (GPQA-Diamond, MMLU-Pro) despite being trained only on math and code data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Bespoke-Stratos (SFT distillation on the same 17k dataset), the proposed method wins on every benchmark across all three model scales: +4.37/+5.72 avg on Qwen3-4B, +4.23/+5.23 on Qwen3-8B, and +1.67/+0.89 on Qwen2.5-32B. Against General-Reasoner-4B (GRPO with a model-based verifier, trained on broader in-domain data), the method achieves +14.18/+9.72 avg on math/general for Qwen3-4B, and notably outperforms it on out-of-distribution GPQA-Diamond (45.33 vs. 40.97) and MMLU-Pro (65.53 vs. 61.36) despite General-Reasoner having in-domain training data for those tasks. Against RLT-32B (RL-trained teachers for distillation), the method leads on math (+1.58 avg) and general (+2.07 avg) for Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. The accuracy-based estimator (Acc) and geometric-mean-likelihood estimator (GML) perform comparably, with Acc showing a slight edge on math benchmarks (e.g., 88.30 vs. 87.30 on MATH500 for 4B).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the variational inference framework (Kingma & Welling, 2013) and the IWAE multi-sample bound (Burda et al., 2015), applying them to the specific problem of training LLM reasoning models where thinking traces are treated as latent variables. It departs from prior RL-for-reasoning work (GRPO, RFT) by explicitly decomposing the model output into a thinking trace z and an answer y, which enables a clean probabilistic interpretation: RFT and binary-reward RL are shown to be local forward-KL objectives implicitly weighted by model accuracy. The forward-KL gradient for the variational posterior follows the reweighted wake-sleep derivation (Bornschein & Bengio, 2015), but is applied in a new context to stabilize posterior training against collapse into shortcut reasoning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of an implicit bias toward easier questions in both RFT and GRPO (via the per-instruction weight P or sqrt(P/(1-P))) is a conceptual finding that affects how practitioners interpret and tune widely-used training pipelines; the paper also provides a concrete debiased reward formulation (Eq. 24) that is directly actionable. The unified variational framework offers a principled alternative objective that empirically outperforms baselines across four model scales and eight benchmarks, with the advantage growing on harder tasks at larger K. Open questions include whether multi-round training (T > 1) yields further gains, how to design richer posteriors for the answer hint y', and whether the variance-minimizing behavior policy result (Appendix A.8) translates into practical RL training speedups.
