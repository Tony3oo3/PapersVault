## Problem

Execution-based feedback (unit tests) for SWE agents is sparse, binary, and often unreliable due to noisy or incomplete test suites, limiting its usefulness for both test-time scaling and reinforcement learning. Execution-free reward models offer finer-grained continuous scores but remain underexplored, and the paper reveals that TTS performance alone is an insufficient proxy for verifier quality: two verifiers with nearly identical TTS can produce drastically different RL outcomes. The paper asks what additional properties (discriminative accuracy and calibration) determine a reward model's effectiveness in RL and how to train a versatile model that excels across all dimensions.

## Input / Output

The reward model consumes a full multi-turn agent trajectory (all model actions and tool responses spliced together in Qwen3-Coder's XML tool-parsing format) along with the generated code patch, supporting up to 256k tokens of context. At inference, the model outputs a single special token (YES or NO), and the final reward score is computed as the softmax over the log-probabilities of these two tokens, yielding a continuous value in [0,1]. This single-token generation design means all prompt computation runs in parallel, keeping inference latency roughly constant regardless of context length.

## Method

SWE-RM is built on Qwen3-30B-A3B (a 30B-parameter MoE with 3B activated) and trained via supervised next-token prediction on a binary YES/NO classification token, following the generative classification formulation from SWE-Gym. The key methodological contribution is a systematic ablation study showing that TTS alone cannot guide reward model selection for RL; instead, AUC (pairwise discriminative ability) and ECE (calibration) are equally critical, with theoretical analysis linking each metric to a distinct failure mode in policy-gradient updates. Training uses ~100k trajectories from four data sources (SWE-rebench, SWE-smith, SWE-Gym, R2E-Gym) with a 2:1 positive-to-negative ratio and mixed on/off-policy data, at 256k context length. For RL deployment, SWE-RM's score is combined with execution-based signals via a piecewise reward function (Eq. 1) that adds the continuous execution-free score to a base reward determined by resolution status, and training uses GSPO for MoE stability.

## Results

On SWE-bench Verified, SWE-RM-30A3B achieves RM@32 of 62.0% with Qwen3-Coder-Flash and 74.6% with Qwen3-Coder-Max, improving pass@1 by 10.4 and 7.6 points respectively. It also attains the best AUC (0.783/0.768) and lowest ECE (0.051/0.047) among all compared verifiers across both policy models. In RL training, hybrid feedback (SWE-RM + execution-based) lifts pass@1 by 3 absolute points over execution-only feedback (54.8% vs. 51.8%) on SWE-bench Verified.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SWE-RM outperforms all four baselines on every reported metric across all three policy models. Against the strongest execution-free baseline (DeepSWE-EF), SWE-RM gains +8.8 points in RM@32 on Qwen3-Coder-Flash (62.0% vs. 53.2%) and +8.4 points on Qwen3-Coder-Max (74.6% vs. 66.2%), while also improving AUC by 0.025–0.030 and reducing ECE by 0.073–0.092. Against the execution-based baselines (Agentless, DeepSWE-EB), SWE-RM achieves substantially higher RM@32 (e.g., 74.6% vs. 67.6% for DeepSWE-EB on Max) and additionally provides the AUC and ECE metrics that execution-based verifiers cannot report. In RL, SWE-RM as hybrid feedback yields 54.8% pass@1 versus 51.8% for execution-based-only feedback, and a poorly calibrated RM with similar TTS drops performance to 12.0% on SWE-Bench Live, confirming that calibration and AUC—not just TTS—determine downstream RL success.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the SWE reward-modeling line initiated by SWE-Gym (Pan et al., 2025), which first cast trajectory scoring as generative YES/NO classification, and builds on GSPO (Zheng et al., 2025) for stable MoE RL training. It departs from prior execution-free verifiers (SWE-Gym, R2E-Gym, DeepSWE) that evaluated quality almost exclusively via test-time scaling, by introducing AUC and expected calibration error as complementary metrics and demonstrating that TTS alone is an insufficient selection criterion. The paper is also the first to integrate execution-free reward signals into multi-turn agentic RL for SWE, where prior work relied solely on sparse execution-based (unit-test) feedback.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that near-identical TTS performance can mask drastically different RL behavior (one verifier causes training collapse while the other trains smoothly) challenges the field's default practice of using TTS as the sole verifier-selection metric, and the accompanying theoretical decomposition (Appendix C) makes the failure modes explicit. The systematic ablations—data scale, positive/negative ratio, policy mixture, source composition, and context length—yield a practical recipe for building robust SWE reward models, and the 256k context window fills a critical gap for scoring trajectories from modern long-context agents. Open questions include whether the specific recipe (2:1 ratio, mix-policy, multi-source) generalizes beyond the Qwen3-Coder family, whether the AUC/ECE criteria transfer to other agentic domains, and whether the +3-point RL gain over execution-based feedback persists at larger scale or with different base policies.
