## Problem

Label unlearning in Vertical Federated Learning (VFL) has received virtually no attention, despite labels encoding highly sensitive information (e.g., HIV status, loan decisions) that must be erasable under GDPR/CCPA. Existing VFL unlearning work targets passive-party feature removal, not active-party label removal, and no prior method addresses the dual challenge of forgetting labels from both active and passive models while preventing the unlearning protocol itself from leaking which samples are being deleted (process privacy).

## Input / Output

The method consumes the trained VFL model parameters (active model ω and K passive models θ_k), a tiny public unlearned subset D^{p,u} (as few as 15–40 samples per target label), and a small public retained subset D^{p,r} (3 samples per retained label). It outputs updated active and passive model parameters with the target label influence removed. Crucially, passive parties never receive raw labels or sample IDs; they only exchange forward embeddings and gradient vectors with the active party, which is what enables the process-privacy guarantee (reducing membership leakage from 100% under retraining to ~4–14%).

## Method

The proposed few-shot label unlearning pipeline has three stages. First, a vertical manifold mixup step interpolates hidden embeddings (not raw features) among samples from the same passive party, generating a rich set of synthetic augmented embeddings from only a handful of public samples; this flattens the embedding distribution and reduces gradient-estimator variance. Second, the active party performs gradient ascent on the augmented unlearned embeddings to push the active model away from the target label, then transmits the resulting embedding gradients to each passive party, which applies the same ascent locally to unlearn the corresponding representations without ever seeing labels. Third, a remained-accuracy-recovery step performs gradient descent on augmented retained embeddings to restore classification performance on non-unlearned classes. A theoretical result (Theorem 1) proves that, under near-zero training loss, the expected gradient direction from the mixup-augmented public subset is positively aligned with the full-data unlearning gradient, justifying the few-shot design.

## Results

On single-label unlearning with ResNet18, the proposed method achieves 89.29% retained-data accuracy (D^r) on CIFAR-10 (vs. 91.26% retrain baseline), reduces unlearned-label accuracy (y^u) to 0.00%, and attains an ASR of 17.23% (vs. 25.98% for retrain). On CIFAR-100, it preserves 69.96% D^r accuracy while driving y^u to 0.00% and ASR to 5.02%. The method completes unlearning in 16×–1200× less time than baselines that require the full dataset, using only 40 public samples per label.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method outperforms all six baselines on the D^r (retained accuracy) metric across every dataset: e.g., 89.29% vs. SSD's 87.17% and FT's 88.16% on CIFAR-10, and 69.96% vs. SSD's 67.25% and FT's 66.86% on CIFAR-100. On y^u (unlearned-label accuracy), the method ties with Retrain, Fisher, Amnesiac, UNSIR, and SSD at 0.00% on most datasets, while FT leaves 11–12% residual accuracy and BU leaves 3–6%. On ASR, the method achieves 17.23% on CIFAR-10 (below Retrain's 25.98% and SSD's 25.74%) and 5.02% on CIFAR-100 (below Retrain's 25.53% and SSD's 6.80%), indicating stronger privacy than retraining. Fisher Forgetting and UNSIR are the weakest baselines overall, with Fisher collapsing D^r to 12–54% and UNSIR showing ASR as high as 76.78% on CIFAR-10. SSD is the closest competitor, matching the method on y^u but trailing by 1–3% on D^r and 1–2% on ASR, while also requiring significantly more runtime. The method's key differentiator is combining near-optimal D^r preservation, zero y^u leakage, and sub-retrain ASR while running 16×–1200× faster than full-dataset baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes manifold mixup (Verma et al., 2019), originally designed for data augmentation and representation learning, as a mechanism for generating synthetic embeddings that guide gradient-based label forgetting in a federated setting. It departs from existing vertical federated unlearning work (Wang et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024; Han et al., 2025), which addresses passive-party feature removal, by introducing the distinct problem of label unlearning where the active party's labels must be erased from both active and passive models. The gradient-ascent unlearning paradigm connects to the broader machine unlearning literature (Golatkar et al., 2020; Graves et al., 2021; Tarun et al., 2024), but the few-shot formulation with mixup-augmented embeddings and the process-privacy framing are novel contributions specific to the VFL label-unlearning setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work fills a genuine gap in the federated unlearning literature by being the first to formalize and solve label unlearning in VFL, a setting where labels serve as both training targets and sensitive personal data (e.g., medical diagnoses, credit decisions). The introduction of 'process privacy' as a formal concept for transcript-level leakage during unlearning protocols is a useful contribution that prior VFL unlearning work has not addressed, though deriving tight theoretical bounds on the privacy parameter ε remains an open problem. The practical impact is significant: unlearning completes in seconds using only ~40 public samples per label, making compliance with GDPR/CCPA feasible in real VFL deployments. Limitations include reliance on a small public dataset, the conceptual (not yet tightly bounded) process-privacy guarantee, and evaluation limited to relatively small-scale benchmarks.
