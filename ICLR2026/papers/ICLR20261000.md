## Problem

Existing vision-language models struggle with accurate visual grounding of infographic elements—charts and human-recognizable objects (HROs)—which is essential for chart understanding and data reasoning. Prior chart datasets either focus exclusively on plain charts without HROs or are too small (e.g., 393 samples in Borkin et al., 2016) to train generalizable detection models, leaving a critical gap in large-scale, comprehensively annotated infographic element detection data.

## Input / Output

The dataset provides 101,264 infographic images (11,264 real from 10 online platforms, 90,000 synthetic from 1,072 design templates) with 14,227,680 bounding box annotations spanning texts, charts, HROs, and 26 sub-element categories (bars, axes, legends, etc.), plus SAM-derived segmentation masks. For the Thinking-with-Boxes application, the detection model's bounding boxes are rendered as two-layer visual prompts (charts/HROs on one layer, texts on another) combined with per-element textual descriptions, which are fed alongside the original infographic and a question to a VLM to elicit grounded chain-of-thought reasoning and a final answer.

## Method

InfoDet is constructed via a two-source pipeline: real infographics are collected from 10 platforms and filtered using CLIP similarity, perceptual hashing, and GPT-4o-mini content verification; synthetic infographics are generated by filling 1,072 design templates with data tables from VizNet, GPT-4o-mini-generated descriptions, and CLIP-matched HROs from IconQA. Annotation combines a programmatic SVG parser for synthetic infographics with a model-in-the-loop procedure for real ones: an InternImage-L backbone with a DINO detector is fine-tuned on synthetic annotations, then iteratively refined over 8 expert correction rounds (achieving 93.9% precision / 96.7% recall). The paper demonstrates three applications: a Thinking-with-Boxes grounded CoT scheme that improves o4-mini on ChartQAPro from 63.2 to 64.9, a systematic comparison showing fine-tuned traditional detectors (Co-DETR: 88.2 AP on charts) outperform zero-shot foundation models (DINO-X: 14.0 AP), and transfer to document/UI layout detection where InfoDet pre-training adds 8.5–11.1 mAP over no pre-training.

## Results

On ChartQAPro, the proposed Thinking-with-Boxes (Grounded CoT) scheme improves overall accuracy for o4-mini from 63.2 (Direct) to 64.9, o1 from 61.4 to 64.1, and o3 from 60.6 to 61.6, with the largest gains on infographic charts with multiple charts (e.g., o1: 66.0→71.9; o4-mini: 70.6→72.5). For object detection on InfoDet, fine-tuning Co-DETR on the full dataset achieves 88.2 AP for charts and 64.0 AP for HROs, far exceeding the best zero-shot foundation model DINO-X (14.0/15.0 AP). Pre-training the InternImage-based detector on InfoDet boosts downstream layout detection, e.g., Rico mAP from 42.1 to 54.4 (with HGM) and DocGenome from 69.0 to 80.8.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the three standard prompting baselines (Direct, Chain-of-Thought, Program-of-Thought), Grounded CoT wins on all three VLMs (o1, o3, o4-mini) on ChartQAPro overall accuracy, with the largest margin on o1 (+2.7 over best baseline CoT) and o4-mini (+1.7 over Direct). The gains are concentrated on infographic charts, suggesting that explicit visual grounding of elements is most beneficial when charts are embedded in complex visual layouts. For object detection, all seven foundation models (regionCLIP, Detic, Grounding DINO, GLIP, MQ-GLIP, T-Rex2, DINO-X) perform poorly in zero-shot or few-shot settings (best: DINO-X at 14.0 AP for charts), while fine-tuning traditional detectors on InfoDet dramatically outperforms them—Co-DETR reaches 88.2 AP for charts and 64.0 AP for HROs, and even Faster R-CNN reaches 78.9/49.0. Among traditional models, Co-DETR consistently outperforms RTMDet (83.7/53.6), faster R-CNN (78.9/49.0), and YOLOv3 (14.7/25.5) at both element and mark levels. For graphic layout detection, pre-training on InfoDet alone (50.6/74.4/73.6 on Rico/DocGenome/PosterLayout) already surpasses no pre-training (42.1/69.0/62.5), and combining InfoDet with ImageNet-22K + Objects365 + COCO plus hierarchy-guided matching yields the best scores (54.4/80.8/76.4), outperforming the three-dataset pre-training baseline (51.8/78.7/74.9).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the model-in-the-loop annotation paradigm from SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023) to the infographic domain, combining it with programmatic annotation of synthetic infographics generated via the template-based method from ChartGalaxy (Li et al., 2025b). It positions itself against two lines of prior work: plain-chart datasets (FigureQA, PlotQA, VisImages, VG-DCU) that exclude human-recognizable objects, and the sole prior infographic dataset (Borkin et al., 2016) that is limited to 393 manually annotated samples. The paper also situates its detection evaluation within the broader object detection literature, comparing open-vocabulary foundation models (DINO-X, Grounding DINO, GLIP) against traditional architectures (Faster R-CNN, Co-DETR, RTMDet) on a task neither was designed for.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InfoDet fills a critical scale gap (101K infographics vs. 393) that makes training and benchmarking infographic element detectors feasible for the first time, and its 14M bounding boxes across texts, charts, HROs, and 26 sub-element categories support both detection and downstream VLM reasoning. The finding that fine-tuned traditional detectors (Co-DETR: 88.2% AP) dramatically outperform even the best foundation models (DINO-X: 14.0% AP zero-shot; Detic: 39.6% AP fine-tuned) challenges the assumption that open-vocabulary models generalize to graphic-design domains, suggesting that domain-specific training data remains essential. Open questions include whether the model-in-the-loop annotation introduces systematic biases in ambiguous chart-HRO boundaries, and whether the hierarchical structure (charts containing sub-elements) can be more fully exploited for detection and generation tasks.
