## Problem

Existing video generation models produce short, self-contained clips that cannot respond adaptively to external actions, while hybrid autoregressive-diffusion world models struggle to balance long-term temporal consistency with immediate action responsiveness (a phenomenon the authors term 'visual inertia'). No prior framework unifies precise, fine-grained action conditioning across heterogeneous modalities (camera pose, robot pose, keyboard/mouse) within a single general-purpose world model that maintains coherence over extended rollouts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262674/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single initial image at 480×832 resolution, a temporally aligned action sequence (7-dim camera pose, 12-dim camera pose, 7-dim robot end-effector pose, or discrete keyboard/mouse commands, interpolated to every 4 frames to match the 3D VAE's temporal compression), and an optional text prompt. It outputs video autoregressively in chunks of 33 latent frames (96 pixel frames total at 20 FPS), with each chunk denoised in 50 steps and appended to the growing history. During training, the number of conditioning history frames is randomly sampled from [1, 128] in pixel space, while the target chunk is fixed at 33 frames.

## Method

Astra builds on the frozen Wan-2.1 1.3B DiT backbone and introduces three key mechanisms. First, an ACT-Adapter (a single identity-initialized linear layer after each self-attention block) injects action features via element-wise addition into every transformer block, with only the adapter and self-attention parameters trained (366.8M trainable params total). Second, a noise-as-mask strategy adds independent random noise to conditioning history frames during training, weakening visual dominance so the model must integrate action cues rather than simply extrapolating past frames; at inference, clean history is used. Third, a Mixture of Action Experts (MoAE) routes heterogeneous action modalities through modality-specific projectors into a shared space, where a learned router selects top-K MLP experts whose weighted outputs form a unified action embedding. An Action-Free Guidance (AFG) mechanism, analogous to classifier-free guidance, randomly drops actions during training and amplifies the action-conditioned velocity field at inference (scale s=3.0) to sharpen responsiveness. History is further compressed by retaining only the first frame and packing intermediate frames into compact visual tokens, extending the effective conditioning horizon without overwhelming the action signal.

## Results

On Astra-Bench (20 held-out samples per dataset across 5 domains), Astra achieves the best scores on all six metrics: Instruction Following 0.669, Subject Consistency 0.939, Background Consistency 0.945, Motion Smoothness 0.989, Aesthetic Quality 0.531, and Imaging Quality 0.747. In terms of action alignment (Table A), Astra reduces rotation error to 1.23° and translation error to 4.86, roughly halving the errors of the next-best method (MatrixGame: 2.25° / 5.63). On the larger CityWalker out-of-domain set (100 unseen scenes), Astra maintains its lead with Instruction Following 0.641 and Subject Consistency 0.948, confirming generalization beyond the primary benchmark.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Astra outperforms all four baselines on the primary Instruction Following metric: 0.669 vs. YUME (0.652), MatrixGame (0.268), NWM (0.311), and Wan-2.1 (0.061), with the largest gap over the non-interactive Wan-2.1 baseline. Against the closest competitor YUME, Astra leads on every metric, including Subject Consistency (0.939 vs. 0.936), Background Consistency (0.945 vs. 0.938), and Imaging Quality (0.747 vs. 0.741). Versus MatrixGame, Astra wins on five of six metrics; the sole exception is Imaging Quality where MatrixGame edges ahead by 0.001 (0.748 vs. 0.747), an effectively negligible difference. On the objective action-alignment metrics (Table A), Astra's rotation error of 1.23° is roughly half that of MatrixGame (2.25°) and YUME (2.20°), and its translation error of 4.86 is the lowest among all methods. On the expanded CityWalker evaluation (Table D), Astra again leads on all six metrics, with the margin over YUME widening on Subject Consistency (0.948 vs. 0.933) and Aesthetic Quality (0.554 vs. 0.511), confirming that the gains are not an artifact of the smaller Astra-Bench.

## Relation to Prior Work

Astra builds on the pre-trained Wan-2.1 video diffusion backbone (a DiT-style flow-matching model) and extends it into an autoregressive denoising framework for interactive world modeling, following the hybrid AR+diffusion paradigm explored by StreamingT2V and MAGI. It departs from prior interactive world models (MatrixGame, YUME, NWM) in three key ways: replacing cross-attention action injection with a lightweight element-wise ACT-Adapter, substituting random token masking (YUME) with a noise-as-mask strategy on history frames, and introducing a Mixture of Action Experts (MoAE) to unify heterogeneous action modalities (camera pose, robot pose, keyboard/mouse) under a single routing mechanism. The action-free guidance (AFG) mechanism is directly inspired by classifier-free guidance in diffusion models, adapted to amplify action-conditioned velocity fields at inference.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Astra demonstrates that a single lightweight framework (366.8M trainable parameters, ~20% of MatrixGame's) can handle diverse real-world interactive scenarios—autonomous driving, robot manipulation, camera control, and multi-agent driving—without domain-specific retraining, which is practically important for building general-purpose world simulators. The identification and mitigation of 'visual inertia' (the trade-off between history length and action responsiveness) via noise-augmented memory is a useful diagnostic for the field. However, the paper's main limitation is inference efficiency: multiple denoising steps per autoregressive chunk make real-time deployment challenging, and the authors acknowledge that distillation or compression strategies are needed before the approach can serve latency-sensitive applications like online robot control. Open questions include whether the noise-as-mask strategy generalizes to even longer horizons and whether MoAE scales gracefully to new action modalities without retraining.
