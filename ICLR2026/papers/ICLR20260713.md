## Problem

Diffusion model sampling is expensive because it requires many function evaluations (NFEs), and existing ODE-based accelerators face two unresolved issues: (1) the choice of prediction type (noise, velocity, data) and integration domain produces different discrete updates even though they are equivalent in continuous time, and (2) learned solvers that optimize sampling parameters typically require expensive teacher trajectories or high-NFE target samples for supervision, limiting their effectiveness in the very low-NFE regime (3–9 NFEs).

## Input / Output

The solver consumes initial Gaussian noise x_T ~ N(0,I) along with a conditioning signal (an ImageNet class label for DiT/GM-DiT at 256×256, or a text prompt for SANA/PixArt-α at 512×512) and a frozen pretrained backbone that must provide both data prediction x_θ and noise prediction ε_θ (convertible via closed-form relations when only one is natively available). It outputs a single denoised image after M steps (NFE = 3–9), with per-step solver parameters (γ, τ_u, τ_v, κ_u, κ_v for both predictor and corrector) and learned timestep schedules. Classifier-free guidance is applied at backbone-specific fixed scales (1.4–4.5).

## Method

Dual-Solver introduces a unified integral formulation parameterized by three per-step learnable scalars: γ continuously interpolates among noise (γ=−1), velocity (γ=0), and data (γ=1) prediction by treating x_θ and ε_θ as separate 'dual' terms rather than bundling them into velocity; τ selects the integration domain via a log-linear transform L(y;τ)=log(1+τy)/τ that smoothly bridges the linear (τ→0) and log1p (τ=1) domains used by prior solvers; and κ scales an O(Δu²) residual term that adds flexibility while provably preserving second-order local truncation error. The sampling follows a first-order predictor + second-order corrector scheme (Alg. 1), with all parameters and intermediate timesteps learned end-to-end. Crucially, training uses a hard-label classification objective: a frozen pretrained classifier (MobileNetV3-Large for ImageNet, CLIP-RN101 for text-to-image) scores the generated sample against the ground-truth label via cross-entropy, eliminating the need for any teacher solver or high-NFE target samples that regression-based methods require.

## Results

On DiT-XL/2 (ImageNet, 50k samples), Dual-Solver achieves FID 24.91 at NFE=3, substantially outperforming DS-Solver (67.31), DPM-Solver++ (88.46), DDIM (89.33), and BNS-Solver (103.26). At NFE=5, Dual-Solver reaches FID 3.52 versus 7.66 for DS-Solver and 22.19 for DPM-Solver++. On SANA (MSCOCO 2014, 30k samples), Dual-Solver attains FID 21.79 and CLIP 0.4795 at NFE=3, compared to FID 45.05–48.65 and CLIP 0.4635–0.4656 for all baselines. Across all four backbones (DiT, GM-DiT, SANA, PixArt-α), Dual-Solver leads on both FID and CLIP in the low-NFE regime (3 ≤ NFE ≤ 9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Dual-Solver decisively outperforms all four baselines on DiT (ImageNet) across every NFE from 3 to 9, with the largest gap at NFE=3 (24.91 vs. 67.31 for DS-Solver, the strongest baseline). On SANA (MSCOCO), Dual-Solver leads on both FID and CLIP at all NFEs; at NFE=3 it achieves FID 21.79 versus 45.05–48.65 for the baselines, and CLIP 0.4795 versus 0.4635–0.4656. On PixArt-α, Dual-Solver similarly dominates, reaching FID 66.61 and CLIP 0.4499 at NFE=3 compared to FID 71.37–125.65 and CLIP 0.4303–0.4469 for baselines. The one exception is GM-DiT: the classification-trained Dual-Solver-C underperforms DS-Solver at NFE 7–9 (FID 2.77–2.60 vs. 2.70–2.41), though the regression-trained variant Dual-Solver-R recovers the lead at NFE 8–9 (FID 2.44, 2.32 vs. 2.48, 2.41). Overall, Dual-Solver wins or ties on 3 of 4 backbones across the full NFE range, with the GM-DiT high-NFE gap being the sole consistent loss.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three lines of work on accelerating diffusion/flow-matching sampling: classical ODE methods (Runge-Kutta, Adams-Bashforth), diffusion-dedicated solvers that exploit denoising dynamics (DPM-Solver++, UniPC, SA-Solver), and learned solvers that optimize sampling parameters per backbone (BNS-Solver, DS-Solver). It departs from prior learned solvers in two key ways: (1) it introduces a unified parameterization (γ, τ, κ) that continuously interpolates among prediction types, integration domains, and residual terms within a single predictor-corrector framework, rather than learning bespoke per-backbone schedules; and (2) it replaces regression-to-teacher-trajectory objectives with a classification-based loss that requires no high-NFE teacher samples. The dual-prediction integral form (Eq. 5) generalizes the noise, velocity, and data prediction integral forms as special cases of a single γ-parameterized family, and the log-linear transform L(y;τ) subsumes both the linear and logarithmic domain changes used in prior solvers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The substantial FID gains in the 3–9 NFE regime across four diverse backbones (DiT, GM-DiT, SANA, PixArt-α) make this practically relevant for latency-sensitive deployment where even a few NFEs matter. The classification-based learning strategy removes the expensive teacher-trajectory preparation step that plagues prior learned solvers, lowering the barrier to adapting a solver to a new backbone. The V-shape relationship between classifier accuracy and FID (Sec. 6.2.4) is a useful empirical finding that suggests moderate-accuracy classifiers are preferable, though the underlying mechanism is not fully explained. Limitations include the absence of unconditional generation experiments, no analysis beyond second-order local accuracy, and the fact that for GM-DiT the classification-based variant underperforms at NFE 7–9 relative to the regression-based variant, indicating the approach is not uniformly dominant.
