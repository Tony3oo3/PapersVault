## Problem

Federated ADMM has remained essentially unchanged since its 1970s formulation, and while a recent result (Swaroop et al., 2025) identified a line-by-line correspondence between ADMM and Partitioned Variational Inference, it fell short of deriving ADMM as a special case of a variational-Bayesian objective. This paper asks whether there exists a more general formulation of ADMM's primal-dual structure that can naturally accommodate the challenges of federated deep learning, such as client heterogeneity and the need for adaptive, preconditioned updates.

## Input / Output

The method operates on local loss functions ℓ_k(θ) at K clients and a server prior π_0 ∝ exp(−ℓ_0), with hyperparameters controlling step sizes ρ, γ, prior precision δ, and temperature τ. Rather than producing a single global parameter vector, it outputs a full posterior distribution q_g over parameters (parameterized by mean and covariance in the Gaussian case), from which predictions are made either at the posterior mean or by ensembling 32 samples. Communication cost is doubled relative to standard ADMM because each client sends both a mean vector and a diagonal variance vector to the server.

## Method

The paper introduces a Bayesian-duality structure by reformulating federated learning as a variational-Bayesian objective over exponential-family distributions, then showing that the VB optimality conditions decompose into primal variables (expectation parameters μ) and dual variables (natural gradients η) in a way that exactly mirrors ADMM's fixed-point equations. The key technical move is replacing regular gradients with natural gradients (via the Fisher information / EF dual map) and using KL divergence in place of quadratic proximal terms, which ensures that dual variables equal negative local natural gradients after every client update. Classical ADMM is recovered as the special case of isotropic Gaussians with the delta method, while full-covariance Gaussians yield a Newton-like variant that converges in one communication round on quadratic objectives, and diagonal-covariance Gaussians yield an Adam-like variant (IVON-ADMM) implemented via the IVON optimizer that achieves up to 7% accuracy gains on heterogeneous deep learning benchmarks without increasing runtime.

## Results

On the most challenging setting (ResNet-20, 10 clients, heterogeneous CIFAR-100), IVON-ADMM achieves 46.6% test accuracy and 2.2 NLL after 100 rounds, outperforming FedAvg/FedProx (39.8%, 3.4 NLL) by 6.7% accuracy and 1.2 NLL. On highly heterogeneous MNIST (100 clients), IVON-ADMM reaches 96.0% accuracy and 0.13 NLL at 100 rounds versus 94.0% and 0.22 for FedAvg. Across all four benchmark scenarios in Table 1, IVON-ADMM achieves the best or tied-best accuracy and NLL at every round count, while matching the computational cost of standard FedAvg/FedDyn (unlike FedLap-Cov, which is 4–7× slower due to Laplace approximation).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IVON-ADMM outperforms FedAvg/FedProx on every scenario and round count, with the largest gap on CIFAR-100 (46.6% vs 39.8% at 100 rounds). Against FedDyn (the strongest federated ADMM baseline), IVON-ADMM wins by 1.1% on MNIST and 7.0% on CIFAR-100, confirming the benefit of posterior covariance over point-estimate ADMM. Compared to the Bayesian baselines FedLap and FedLap-Cov (Swaroop et al., 2025), IVON-ADMM achieves equal or better accuracy while being substantially cheaper: FedLap-Cov is 4× slower on MNIST and 7× slower on CIFAR-10 due to its Laplace approximation, and is prohibitively slow on CIFAR-100. In convergence studies (Fig. 5b), PVI without damping diverges on logistic regression, and PVI with damping converges but slower than Bayesian-ADMM; BregmanADMM (which uses μ-coordinates in the dual update) also converges more slowly. The Newton-like full-covariance variant of Bayesian-ADMM converges in a single communication round on quadratic objectives, a property no prior ADMM generalization possesses.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends Swaroop et al. (2025), who identified a line-by-line correspondence between federated ADMM and Partitioned Variational Inference (PVI) but could not derive ADMM as an exact special case of the VB framework. The key technical fix is the use of natural gradients (from the Bayesian learning rule of Khan & Rue, 2023) and exponential-family duality, which enables a clean derivation of ADMM from Bayesian-ADMM with isotropic Gaussians and automatically yields Newton-like (full covariance) and Adam-like (diagonal covariance) variants. The paper also positions itself against Bregman ADMM and augmented Lagrangian methods, arguing that the natural-parameter dual update is a fundamental departure from prior generalizations that operate in primal (expectation) coordinates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The framework provides a principled, extensible recipe for generating new federated optimization algorithms by simply choosing different exponential families, which could accelerate the design of methods tailored to specific heterogeneity patterns. The one-step convergence on quadratics and the 7% accuracy gain on CIFAR-100 without extra compute are practically useful, but the broader impact depends on whether the Bayesian-ADMM framework scales to decentralized, asynchronous, or non-Gaussian settings. Open questions include the fundamental convergence properties of Bayesian-ADMM beyond the quadratic case, the role of non-diagonal covariances in deep networks, and whether the natural-gradient dual update can be made communication-efficient in high-dimensional regimes.
