## Problem

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) require dozens of iterative denoising steps, each involving full computation across deep transformer blocks, making high-quality video generation prohibitively slow. Existing feature-caching accelerations for DiTs either use rigid fixed intervals that cannot adapt to the model's dynamic behavior, or rely on first-order Taylor prediction that fails to capture cases where direct feature reuse outperforms linear extrapolation, resulting in approximation errors too large for professional-grade near-lossless generation.

## Input / Output

The framework is training-free and operates as a drop-in inference accelerator on existing DiT checkpoints (Wan2.1 1.3B/14B, HunyuanVideo, FLUX 1.dev). For text-to-video it consumes a text prompt and produces 81-frame (832×480) or 129-frame (960×544) videos; for text-to-image it produces 1360×768 images; for image-to-video it takes a reference image plus text prompt. The only user-facing hyperparameter is S_f (the number of initial full-computation warm-up steps), which controls the target acceleration ratio; all differential scaling coefficients α are precomputed offline from ~50 prompts and require no per-sample tuning.

## Method

ScalingCache introduces two complementary mechanisms operating on per-block hidden states during DiT inference. First, a differential scaling prediction replaces Taylorseer's fixed first-order extrapolation with a per-block, per-timestep coefficient α_t^l (precomputed offline via least-squares on ~50 prompts with exponential moving average, β=0.97), yielding the prediction ŷ_t^l = y_τ^l + α_t^l · k · Δy_τ^l that adaptively blends zero-order reuse and first-order linear prediction depending on which is locally more accurate. Second, a runtime dynamic interval caching strategy monitors a cumulative relative L1 error across all L blocks; when this error exceeds a dynamic threshold δ_s (auto-estimated from the first S_f warm-up steps to account for content variability), a full forward pass is triggered, otherwise cached features are updated via the scaled-difference formula. This avoids the U-shaped error accumulation of fixed-interval caching while requiring only two cached tensors (feature and delta) per module, with negligible per-step overhead (~0.05 s on H800 for Wan2.1 14B).

## Results

On Wan2.1-1.3B, ScalingCache (S_f=10) achieves a 2.5× speedup with PSNR 26.61, SSIM 0.890, LPIPS 0.071, and VBench 82.92% (vs. 83.31% baseline). On FLUX 1.dev, it reaches 3.1× near-lossless acceleration with PSNR 32.28, SSIM 0.819, LPIPS 0.131, and CLIP Score 80.25%. On HunyuanVideo, ScalingCache (S_f=12) achieves 2.3× speedup with PSNR 30.80, SSIM 0.930, LPIPS 0.049, and VBench 81.13% (vs. 81.40% baseline). Across all models, VBench drops are limited to 0.3–0.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against TeaCache (Liu et al., CVPR 2025), ScalingCache achieves substantially higher speedups (2.5× vs. 2.0× on Wan2.1-1.3B; 2.5× vs. 1.5× on Wan2.1-14B; 2.3× vs. 1.5× on HunyuanVideo) while also improving all fidelity metrics—e.g., LPIPS drops from 0.128 to 0.071 on Wan2.1-1.3B and from 0.173 to 0.049 on HunyuanVideo. Against Taylorseer (Liu et al., 2025), ScalingCache delivers better quality at comparable or higher speed: on Wan2.1-1.3B it matches the 2.5× speedup of EasyCache while achieving PSNR 26.61 vs. Taylorseer's 13.52 at 1.9×; on HunyuanVideo, Taylorseer is faster (2.8× vs. 2.3×) but ScalingCache yields PSNR 30.80 vs. 26.57 and LPIPS 0.049 vs. 0.135. Against EasyCache (Zhou et al., 2025), ScalingCache matches the 2.5× speedup on Wan2.1-1.3B with superior fidelity (PSNR 26.61 vs. 25.24, LPIPS 0.071 vs. 0.095) and on HunyuanVideo achieves 2.3× vs. 2.2× with PSNR 30.80 vs. 29.20 and LPIPS 0.049 vs. 0.063. On FLUX 1.dev, ScalingCache reaches 3.1× speedup (vs. Taylorseer's 2.8×) with LPIPS 0.131 vs. 0.230, a 43% reduction, while maintaining CLIP Score 80.25% vs. 80.17%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the feature-caching paradigm for Diffusion Transformers, building directly on TaylorSeer's first-order linear prediction and TeaCache/EasyCache's dynamic scheduling ideas. Its key departure is the observation that for certain blocks at certain timesteps, zero-order (direct) reuse outperforms first-order Taylor prediction, motivating a per-block, per-timestep differential scaling coefficient α that interpolates between the two regimes without the exponential cache growth of higher-order expansions. Unlike prior dynamic schedulers that base decisions solely on the first-block input or last-block output, ScalingCache accumulates per-block relative errors to make granular full-computation decisions, and it requires only a lightweight offline calibration (~50 prompts) rather than runtime profiling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: 2.5–3.1× training-free speedup with near-lossless quality (≤0.5% VBench drop, 45% LPIPS reduction over prior SOTA) makes high-fidelity video generation substantially more accessible for deployment. The method's simplicity—one scalar α per block per timestep, a single tunable parameter S_f—lowers the barrier to adoption. However, the paper acknowledges a limitation for samples that begin statically and later transition to dynamic states, and the substantial memory overhead (up to ~62 GB for HunyuanVideo) remains a concern for single-GPU settings despite sequence-parallelism mitigations. Open questions include whether the U-shaped error pattern and the α coefficients generalize to fundamentally new DiT architectures or to interactive/streaming generation settings.
