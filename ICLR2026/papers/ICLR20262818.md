## Problem

Current code-agent systems for website generation rely solely on code-execution feedback (stdout/stderr) for verification, which cannot capture visual aesthetics or interactive functionality. This leads to generated websites with misaligned components, disharmonious coloring, unresponsive buttons, and broken links. The paper addresses this gap by introducing multi-level visual feedback (screenshot-based appearance assessment and GUI-agent functional testing) into the agent loop, and by leveraging the resulting per-step scores as dense process-supervision signals for reinforcement learning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262818/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent takes a natural-language website-generation instruction (concatenated with a Bolt.diy-style system prompt) specifying both appearance and functional requirements, and produces a complete multi-file website codebase. The instruction is consumed iteratively: at each step the coding LLM emits a code edit, the codebase is executed in a sandbox, a landing-page screenshot is captured for VLM-based appearance scoring, and a GUI-agent session explores the site to verify functional requirements. The final output is the codebase state at the best-scoring step (selected by GUI-agent score first, then screenshot score), not necessarily the last step.

## Method

WebGen-Agent operates as an iterative loop of code generation → execution → feedback gathering, where feedback comprises (1) a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) producing a description, appearance score, and improvement suggestions from a screenshot, and (2) a GUI-agent testing session that exercises the site's interactive features and yields a functional score plus fix suggestions. A backtracking mechanism reverts to the best prior step after five consecutive execution errors, and a select-best mechanism restores the highest-scoring step at the end of the trajectory. For training smaller models, Step-GRPO assigns each step's tokens a distinct advantage computed from the sum of that step's screenshot and GUI-agent scores (rather than a single trajectory-level reward), providing dense step-level process supervision. The training pipeline first performs one epoch of SFT on ~700 DeepSeek-V3-generated trajectories as a warm start, then applies Step-GRPO (without KL penalty) on 500 instructions with 5 sampled trajectories each, yielding WebGenAgent-LM models that substantially close the gap with proprietary coding LLMs.

## Results

On WebGen-Bench, WebGen-Agent with Qwen3-Coder-480B-A35B-Instruct achieves the best overall performance at 58.2% accuracy and 4.3 appearance score. With Claude-3.5-Sonnet, WebGen-Agent raises accuracy from 26.4% (Bolt.diy) to 51.9% and appearance score from 3.0 to 3.9. For small open-source models, Step-GRPO training improves Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct from 12.4% to 45.4% accuracy (appearance 1.6→3.7) and Qwen3-8B from 34.1% to 43.4% accuracy (appearance 3.2→3.6). On the ArtifactsBench Web Application subset, WebGen-Agent with Qwen3-Coder-480B achieves 63.86 average MLLM score, outperforming all closed-source baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

WebGen-Agent consistently and substantially outperforms all three general-purpose code-agent baselines (OpenHands, Aider, Bolt.diy) across every model tested. On Claude-3.5-Sonnet, WebGen-Agent achieves 51.9% accuracy vs. 26.4% for Bolt.diy (+25.5), 22.9% for Aider (+29.0), and 22.3% for OpenHands (+29.6); appearance scores improve from 3.0/1.9/2.6 to 3.9 respectively. On DeepSeek-V3, the gap is even larger: 52.6% vs. 20.8% (Bolt.diy), 14.1% (Aider), and 9.0% (OpenHands). For 30B–72B open-source models, WebGen-Agent beats Bolt.diy by 22.5% (Qwen2.5-Coder-32B) and 22.1% (Qwen2.5-72B) in accuracy. Against the prior SOTA WebGen-LM models (Lu et al., 2025b), WebGen-Agent with the same base models improves accuracy by 12.6% (7B) and 12.9% (32B), confirming that the multi-level feedback workflow and Step-GRPO training provide gains beyond what supervised distillation alone can achieve.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the code-agent paradigm (OpenHands, Aider, Bolt.diy) by replacing the sole reliance on code-execution feedback with a multi-level visual feedback loop: a VLM scores and critiques screenshots for appearance, and a GUI agent tests functional correctness. For training, it adapts GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) from outcome-level to step-level process supervision, using the screenshot and GUI-agent scores that are inherently produced during the agent workflow as dense per-step rewards. The approach also builds on the WebGen-Bench benchmark and the WebGen-LM model family (Lu et al., 2025b), positioning itself as a methodological upgrade over the prior SOTA agent (Bolt.diy) rather than a new task formulation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that iterative visual feedback can nearly double the accuracy of a strong proprietary model on website generation (26.4%→51.9% for Claude-3.5-Sonnet) and that step-level VLM-scored rewards in GRPO can meaningfully improve small open-source models (7B/8B) with only ~500 training instructions. A practical open question is whether the approach scales to multi-page websites with cross-page coherence, which the authors explicitly exclude as a qualitatively different problem. The method's dependence on GUI-agent quality means its ceiling will rise as GUI agents improve, but the current 7B/8B training is limited by compute (16 A800 GPUs for 24+ hours), leaving the scaling behavior to larger models untested.
