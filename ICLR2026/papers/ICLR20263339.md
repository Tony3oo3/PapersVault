## Problem

Learning (N:M)-semi-structured sparsity masks for LLMs is a combinatorial optimization over an enormous search space of C(M,N)^(d/M) configurations. Existing rule-based methods (SparseGPT, Wanda, etc.) suffer from a fundamental gap between layerwise proxy objectives and end-to-end loss, while the learning-based MaskLLM requires O(C(M,N)·d/M) memory for logits—exponentially growing with M—making it infeasible beyond small sparsity patterns. The paper seeks a memory-efficient, end-to-end learning approach that avoids both the bias of handcrafted metrics and the exponential memory blowup of full combinatorial distributions.

## Input / Output

The method takes frozen LLM weights (7B–30B parameters) and a small calibration/training set from C4 (as few as 1 sample, typically 128) as input. It produces a strict (N:M)-sparse binary mask over all weights, implemented by selecting the top-N logits within each group of M consecutive elements. Training is entirely backpropagation-free: only forward passes and policy-gradient logit updates are needed, requiring O(d) memory for logits regardless of the (N:M) ratio, compared to O(C(M,N)·d/M) for MaskLLM. The method is evaluated zero-shot on 25+ downstream benchmarks via LM-evaluation-harness.

## Method

MaskPro reformulates (N:M)-sparsity as N-way sampling without replacement from a single categorical distribution over M positions per group, reducing the parameterization from C(M,N) logits per group to just M logits (Theorem 1 provides the representation). Training uses a backpropagation-free policy gradient estimator (REINFORCE-style) that updates logits via forward-pass-only computation. The key innovation is a refined PGE that replaces the raw loss with a loss residual f(m_t⊙w,ξ) − f(m_0⊙w,ξ) to decouple mask quality from minibatch difficulty, further stabilized by a moving-average tracker δ that centers the residual around zero. Theorem 2 proves all three PGE variants (vanilla, residual, residual+tracker) are unbiased, with the tracker variant achieving strict variance reduction when the sampled mask loss exceeds half the initial loss. The full procedure requires only ~10k iterations on a single GPU with batch size 32, achieving competitive or superior accuracy to MaskLLM at a fraction of the memory and training cost.

## Results

On (2:4)-sparsity, MaskPro achieves an average zero-shot accuracy improvement of over 2% over the best rule-based baselines across 7B models (LLaMA-2-7B, DeepSeek-7B, Vicuna-7B, Gemma-7B). On LLaMA-2-7B, MaskPro attains 46.18% HellaSwag, 73.07% PIQA, and 66.12% ARC-Easy, while reducing Wikitext PPL to 17.17 (vs. 21.07 for SparseGPT and 12.55 for MaskLLM). Critically, MaskPro requires only ~36 GB of training memory compared to ~330 GB for MaskLLM, and can be trained with as few as 1 data sample while maintaining stable performance.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MaskPro consistently outperforms all five rule-based baselines (Magnitude, SparseGPT, Wanda, GBLM, Pruner-Zero) across all four 7B models and all eight evaluation tasks, with typical margins of 2–5 percentage points on HellaSwag and PIQA. Against the learning-based MaskLLM, MaskPro is slightly behind on most tasks (e.g., 46.18 vs. 51.17 on HellaSwag for LLaMA-2-7B) but achieves comparable or superior results on several benchmarks (e.g., 28.60 vs. 25.80 on OBQA for DeepSeek-7B, 32.85 vs. 35.67 on ARC-C for LLaMA-2-7B) while using only ~36 GB of memory versus MaskLLM's ~330 GB (a ~9× reduction). On Wikitext PPL, MaskPro achieves 17.17 on LLaMA-2-7B, substantially better than SparseGPT (21.07) and Wanda (23.44), though still above MaskLLM (12.55). The memory advantage scales dramatically with sparsity pattern: for (4:8)-sparsity on DeepSeek-7B, MaskLLM exceeds 640 GB (OOM on 8×A100) while MaskPro remains at ~37 GB. MaskPro also demonstrates remarkable data efficiency, maintaining near-identical performance with as few as 1 training sample (49.18 avg. accuracy) compared to 320k samples (49.62 avg.), whereas MaskLLM requires at least 1280 samples to match SparseGPT and 520k for convergence.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper directly extends MaskLLM (Fang et al., 2024), the first learning-based method for (N:M)-sparsity on LLMs, by replacing its exponential-space categorical distribution over all C(M,N) mask combinations with a linear-space categorical distribution over M individual elements, coupled with N-way sampling without replacement. It also departs from the broader family of rule-based layerwise greedy methods (SparseGPT, Wanda, GBLM, Pruner-Zero) that minimize per-layer reconstruction error, instead optimizing the end-to-end generation loss via a backpropagation-free policy gradient. The paper is the first to apply policy gradient estimation to semi-structured mask learning on LLMs, introducing a loss-residual update with a moving-average tracker to handle the extreme variance in the combinatorial space.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The reduction of logits memory from O(C(M,N)·d/M) to O(d) makes learning-based (N:M)-sparsity practical on a single GPU (~36 GB vs. 330+ GB for MaskLLM), and the demonstrated robustness to training data (stable performance with as few as 1 sample vs. 520k for MaskLLM) significantly lowers the barrier to adoption. A key open limitation is that mask sampling dominates ~86% of per-step training time, and the authors acknowledge that more efficient sampling simulations are needed for larger models. The modest 2–3% average accuracy gain over the best rule-based baselines, while consistent, suggests the method's primary value is in enabling end-to-end optimization at feasible cost rather than in a large quality jump.
