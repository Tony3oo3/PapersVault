## Problem

Existing self-supervised articulated object methods reconstruct discrete interaction states and relate them through pairwise geometric consistency, yielding representational fragmentation, drift, and an inability to model continuous part motion. Most approaches further assume a single movable part across exactly two states or require the part count as input, limiting them to simple single-joint objects and precluding smooth control of multi-part configurations.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260420/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes multi-view RGB images captured at an arbitrary number of interaction states (e.g., 50 views × 4 states = 200 images), requiring no depth maps, no part-count annotation, and no manual segmentation. It outputs a part-aware 3D Gaussian field with per-primitive part labels, per-part watertight meshes via TSDF + Marching Cubes, joint type classification (revolute vs. prismatic via Kabsch residual rank), and minimal joint parameters (pivot + quaternion for revolute; axis + distance for prismatic). By interpolating the learned latent codes, the model generates smooth, collision-free deformations to previously unseen interaction states.

## Method

PD²GS learns a shared canonical 3D Gaussian field and represents every interaction state as its continuous deformation via a latent-conditioned MLP that predicts per-primitive translation, rotation (quaternion), and scale offsets. Part-level decoupling is achieved through a coarse-to-fine segmentation: dynamic Gaussians are identified by a displacement threshold, the number of moving parts is estimated by querying a VLM with cross-state image pairs, and K-means clustering on normalized motion-trajectory descriptors yields initial part groups; a visibility-guided SAM prompting stage then refines boundaries by projecting each Gaussian's screen-space ellipse against per-part masks and recursively splitting boundary-crossing primitives. The entire pipeline is optimized end-to-end under a photometric rendering loss plus a density-similarity regularizer, operating entirely within the 3DGS paradigm without external geometric or physics-based constraints, and is agnostic to the number of interaction states or parts.

## Results

On the expanded PartNet-Mobility split (8 categories, 2–3 instances each, two movable parts), PD²GS achieves mean Axis Ang of 0.33°/0.47° and Part Motion of 0.59/0.26, outperforming the closest multi-joint baseline ArtGS (2.37°/4.68° and 3.38/7.60) by roughly 7× on joint-angle error and 6× on motion error. Geometry is also superior: mean CD-m (movable-part Chamfer distance) drops to 1.43/0.80 versus 20.06/145.23 for ArtGS. On unseen-state rendering (Table 2), PD²GS reaches 28.02 dB PSNR and 0.09 LPIPS, beating ArtGS (26.82 dB, 0.12) and 4D-GS (23.61 dB, 0.13). The only metric where a baseline wins is CD-w (whole-object Chamfer), where DTArt's depth input yields 2.18 vs. PD²GS's 3.02.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PD²GS dominates all four reconstruction baselines on joint-parameter metrics. Against the single-joint methods PARIS and ArticulatedGS, PD²GS reduces mean Axis Ang by 100× and 15× respectively, and Part Motion by 130× and 12×, confirming that per-joint pipelines cannot capture multi-part kinematics. Against the two-state multi-joint baselines, PD²GS beats ArtGS (the strongest competitor) on every metric except CD-w, with the largest gaps on CD-m1 (0.80 vs. 145.23, a 180× improvement) and Axis Pos (0.03–0.08 vs. 1.61–5.31). DTArt, which benefits from depth input, wins only on whole-object CD-w (2.18 vs. 3.02) but is outperformed by 20–300× on all joint and movable-part geometry metrics. On unseen-state rendering (Table 2), PD²GS outperforms all five dynamic-scene baselines (D-NeRF, D²NeRF, Dynamic3DGS, 4D-GS, ArtGS) in PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS, with the closest gap being 1.2 dB over ArtGS. Against generative methods NAP and SINGAPO (Table 9), PD²GS achieves 30× lower Axis Ang and 23× lower Part Motion, underscoring that reconstruction-based inference is far more accurate than generation for precise kinematic recovery.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation (Kerbl et al., 2023) with a latent-conditioned deformation network that warps a shared canonical Gaussian field across interaction states, departing from prior articulated-object methods (PARIS, DTArt, ArtGS) that model only two discrete states and relate them via pairwise geometric registration. For part boundary refinement, it repurposes SAM (Kirillov et al., 2023) with a visibility-aware 3D-to-2D prompting scheme and borrows the boundary-aware splitting mechanism from SAGD (Hu et al., 2024), but drives both from motion trajectories rather than external 2D masks. The use of a VLM to estimate part count and K-means on deformation descriptors for coarse grouping is a novel combination that avoids the part-count assumption present in all prior multi-part baselines.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The continuous-deformation formulation enables smooth interpolation to previously unseen articulation configurations while preserving part-level separation—a capability absent from all prior discrete-state methods and directly relevant to digital-twin and robotic manipulation pipelines. The released RS-Art dataset (18 real objects, 7 states each, with reverse-engineered URDF twins) addresses a genuine gap in real-world evaluation that prior work has largely ignored. Key limitations include the assumption of accurate camera poses, inability to reconstruct fully occluded regions, and reliance on a VLM whose part-count accuracy, while 100% in the paper's small test, may not generalize to highly complex or ambiguous objects. Open questions include robustness to pose noise in uncontrolled environments and scalability to objects with more than five independently actuated parts.
