## Problem

3D Gaussian Splatting suffers from two distinct failure modes under sparse-view conditions: overfitting in near-field regions where excessive Gaussian density causes aliasing artifacts, and underfitting in far-field regions where sparse coverage yields blurry reconstructions. Existing regularization approaches like uniform dropout (DropGaussian) inadvertently degrade both well-fitted and under-fitted regions, and no principled metric exists to quantify the stability of learned Gaussian distributions across training runs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264233/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a small set of RGB images (3 or 6 views) along with camera poses estimated via COLMAP SfM, and a monocular depth map per image from DepthAnything V2 used to construct far-region masks. It outputs a 3D Gaussian scene representation (positions, scales, rotations, opacities, SH color coefficients) that can be rendered to novel views in real time. Evaluation is conducted at 1/8 and 1/4 resolution on LLFF, at 24-view on MipNeRF360, and at 3/6-view on DTU, with 10k training iterations on a single H20 GPU.

## Method

D²GS introduces two complementary modules into the 3DGS training loop. The Depth-and-Density Guided Dropout (DD-Drop) assigns each Gaussian a continuous dropout score from min-max-normalized depth and k-NN density, then modulates it with a discrete three-layer depth attenuation (near/middle/far with factors 1.0/0.7/0.3) and a linearly increasing global dropout rate over training, producing a soft probabilistic removal that suppresses near-field overfitting without erasing detail. The Distance-Aware Fidelity Enhancement (DAFE) module thresholds the monocular depth map to isolate the farthest 5% of pixels and adds a dedicated L1 loss on those masked regions, explicitly boosting supervision where Gaussians are under-represented. Additionally, the paper proposes an Inter-Model Robustness (IMR) metric that abstracts each trained model as a Gaussian mixture weighted by opacity, computes pairwise 2-Wasserstein distances via a first-order Bures-metric approximation solved with entropic Sinkhorn transport, and aggregates them into a single scalar measuring distributional consistency across independently trained runs.

## Results

On LLFF (3-view, 1/8 resolution), D²GS achieves the best PSNR of 21.35 dB, SSIM of 0.746, LPIPS of 0.179, and AVGE of 0.087, outperforming the strongest 3DGS baseline DropGaussian (20.76 dB) by 0.59 dB and the best NeRF-based method SparseNeRF (19.86 dB) by 1.49 dB. On MipNeRF360, D²GS reaches 20.09 dB PSNR, surpassing DropGaussian (19.74 dB) and CoR-GS (19.52 dB). The proposed IMR robustness metric shows D²GS achieves the lowest values (3.039 for 3-view, 3.109 for 6-view on LLFF), indicating more stable Gaussian distributions across independent training runs compared to all baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

D²GS outperforms all 11 baselines across every metric on both LLFF and MipNeRF360. Against the closest 3DGS competitor DropGaussian (CVPR 2025), D²GS gains 0.59 dB PSNR on LLFF 1/8-res and 0.35 dB on MipNeRF360, while also improving SSIM by 0.033 and reducing LPIPS by 0.021. Over CoR-GS (ECCV 2024), the gains are 0.90 dB PSNR on LLFF and 0.57 dB on MipNeRF360. Among NeRF-based methods, D²GS surpasses the best (SparseNeRF, ICCV 2023) by 1.49 dB PSNR on LLFF 1/8-res, though it trails SparseNeRF slightly on SSIM (0.746 vs. 0.624) — a trade-off inherent to the explicit Gaussian representation. On the robustness metric IMR, D²GS achieves the lowest values (3.039/3.109) versus DropGaussian (3.205/3.143) and 3DGS (3.162/3.234), confirming more consistent Gaussian distributions across independent training runs. On the object-centric DTU dataset (Appendix E), D²GS extends its lead with 21.25 dB PSNR at 3-view, beating DropGaussian (20.29 dB) by nearly 1 dB.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the sparse-view 3D Gaussian Splatting line initiated by DropGaussian (Park et al., 2025), which introduced uniform random dropout to combat overfitting. D²GS departs from this by replacing the uniform strategy with a depth-and-density guided soft dropout (DD-Drop) that selectively suppresses over-represented near-field Gaussians while preserving far-field coverage, and adds a distance-aware fidelity loss (DAFE) leveraging monocular depth priors to reinforce under-fitted distant regions. The evaluation side introduces an Inter-Model Robustness (IMR) metric grounded in 2-Wasserstein distance and optimal transport over Gaussian mixture distributions, which is orthogonal to the image-space metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) used by all prior sparse-view 3DGS methods (CoR-GS, FSGS, LoopSparseGS).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a useful diagnostic decomposition of sparse-view 3DGS failure into near-field overfitting and far-field underfitting, and shows that a single uniform regularization (dropout) cannot address both simultaneously. The IMR metric opens a path toward evaluating 3D representation stability beyond per-image quality, which is important as 3DGS moves toward production use where run-to-run consistency matters. Limitations include reliance on monocular depth estimator quality (though ablations show robustness across MiDaS/DPT/DepthAnything V2), the heuristic depth-tertile layering in DD-Drop, and evaluation limited to 3–6 views on LLFF/MipNeRF360/DTU; whether the approach scales to extremely sparse (1–2 view) or unbounded outdoor scenes remains open.
