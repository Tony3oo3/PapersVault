## Problem

Existing time series foundation models rely solely on the time modality and lack explicit domain knowledge, causing similar historical patterns to produce identical forecasts across different domains. Meanwhile, end-to-end multimodal supervised models integrate textual domain knowledge but cannot perform zero-shot cross-domain inference. No prior model supports both multimodal inputs and zero-shot generalization in a single pretrained architecture.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263504/cropped_page1_idx3.webp)

## Input / Output

Aurora takes a univariate time series (normalized via RevIN and split into non-overlapping patches), a sample-wise domain-specific text description (generated by GPT-4 during corpus construction), and an endogenous image rendered from the time series by detecting its dominant period via FFT, reshaping into a 2D grid, and repeating along the channel dimension to form a 3×w×h RGB image for ViT. The output is a future horizon of F tokens (each of patch size p^time), produced either as a deterministic point forecast or as a probabilistic distribution obtained by running the flow-matching sampler multiple times with different Gaussian noise draws.

## Method

The Aurora Encoder tokenizes all three modalities, encodes text and image with pretrained BERT and ViT, then compresses them via learnable-query cross-attention distillation. The key architectural contribution is a Modality-Guided Multi-head Self-Attention: cross-attention scores between time tokens and distilled image/text tokens are multiplied through a learnable metric to form a correlation matrix that is added directly to the temporal self-attention logits, steering which time patches attend to each other based on domain-specific knowledge. The Aurora Decoder first generates F future conditions via a Causal-Transformer followed by a RoPE-augmented Cross-Transformer. A Prototype Bank of M learnable period/trend prototypes (initialized with trigonometric, exponential, logarithmic, and polynomial bases) is queried by a Transformer-based PrototypeRetriever that takes text and image representations and outputs per-token categorical weights, yielding a composite prototype as the flow-matching starting point. The decoder then performs conditional optimal-transport flow matching (an ODE solver, not an SDE) from prototype+noise to the target, with multimodal conditions injected via AdaLN, enabling both deterministic and probabilistic forecasting.

## Results

On the multimodal TimeMMD benchmark, Aurora achieves the best MSE in 31 out of 54 evaluation settings (zero-shot) and 30/54 (10% few-shot), outperforming all unimodal foundation models and full-shot multimodal supervised models. Aurora reduces average MSE by 27.0% over Sundial and 31.2% over VisionTS on TimeMMD, and by 12.8% over GPT4MTS and 24.5% over CALF in the few-shot setting. On unimodal TSFM-Bench, Aurora achieves 27/36 best MSE counts, reducing MSE by 15.1% over Time-MoE and 22.9% over ROSE. On probabilistic ProbTS, Aurora attains 19/12 best CRPS counts, reducing CRPS by 21.5% over CSDI and 38.3% over MOIRAI.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Aurora decisively outperforms all unimodal foundation models (Sundial, VisionTS, ROSE, MOIRAI, Time-MoE, Chronos, TimesFM, Timer, UniTS, TTM, Lag-Llama) in the multimodal zero-shot setting, winning 31/54 MSE evaluations versus Sundial's 4 and VisionTS's 0. In the 10% few-shot comparison against full-shot multimodal supervised models, Aurora (trained on only 10% of data) beats GPT4MTS, TATS, CALF, and Time-VLM in most settings, with average MSE reductions of 12.8% and 24.5% over GPT4MTS and CALF respectively. On unimodal TSFM-Bench, Aurora dominates with 27/36 best MSE counts, surpassing Sundial (11), Time-MoE (2), and all other foundation models. On probabilistic ProbTS, Aurora achieves 19/12 best CRPS/NMAE counts, outperforming CSDI (4/1) and MOIRAI (2/1) with 21.5% and 38.3% CRPS reductions. The only notable exception is the Security domain in Table 1, where Sundial (70.441) slightly edges out Aurora zero-shot (72.475) in MSE, though Aurora's 10% few-shot variant (70.062) recovers the lead. On short-term EPF forecasting, Aurora is competitive with full-shot supervised models like TimeXer and iTransformer, though TimeXer achieves slightly lower MSE on NP (0.238 vs. 0.288) and BE (0.374 vs. 0.361).

## Relation to Prior Work

Aurora sits at the intersection of two established but previously separate lines of work: unimodal time series foundation models (Sundial, MOIRAI, Chronos, TimesFM) that achieve cross-domain generalization through large-scale pretraining but lack explicit domain knowledge, and multimodal end-to-end supervised models (GPT4MTS, TATS, CALF, Time-VLM) that integrate textual domain knowledge but require full-shot training and cannot support zero-shot inference. The paper's architectural contributions draw directly from Flow Matching (Lipman et al., ICLR 2023) for the generative decoder and DiT (Peebles & Xie, ICCV 2023) for the condition-decoding Transformer with AdaLN conditioning, while the encoder leverages pretrained BERT and ViT as modality-specific encoders with a novel token distillation and modality-guided attention mechanism to bridge them. The key departure is the explicit combination of cross-domain pretraining with multimodal inputs in a single model, which neither prior line of work achieves.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As the first multimodal time series foundation model, Aurora establishes a new paradigm where domain-specific knowledge from text and image modalities can guide zero-shot cross-domain forecasting, potentially reducing the need for domain-specific fine-tuning in practical deployment. The Prototype-Guided Flow Matching decoder offers a more intuitive generative framework than standard Gaussian-noise initialization, and the 10% few-shot results outperforming full-shot multimodal baselines suggest strong data efficiency. However, important limitations remain: the textual descriptions are LLM-generated rather than sourced from real domain experts, the 'endogenous image' is rendered from the time series itself (limiting true external multimodal information), and the 30-day training on 8 A800s raises practical adoption barriers. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to truly external multimodal signals (e.g., satellite imagery, news text) and whether the gains persist when text quality degrades from GPT-4-generated to noisy real-world descriptions.
