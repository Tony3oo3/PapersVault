limitations: The pretraining stage still requires LiDAR or equivalent depth supervision (though occupied-voxel locations and zero-shot monocular depth are shown as viable substitutes), and on the nuScenes-Occ3D benchmark S2GO-Base (39.1 RayIoU) trails the best methods (ALOcc-GF at 44.1, STCOcc at 42.1), indicating the sparse query formulation has not yet closed the gap on all occupancy benchmarks. surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that a mere 900 sparse queries (9k total Gaussians) with a denoising pretraining phase match or exceed methods using 25.6k–144k Gaussians while running 5.9× faster — the gap between sparse and dense representations is smaller than the field assumed. ## Problem

State-of-the-art 3D occupancy estimation relies on dense voxel grids or tens of thousands of Gaussians, making them slow and inflexible for streaming perception with long-term temporal context. While sparse query-based representations have proven effective for object detection, adapting them to dense, high-fidelity occupancy estimation is non-trivial due to the ambiguous mapping from a small set of queries to full scene coverage and the chicken-and-egg problem of query placement before classification.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262134/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At inference, S2GO consumes only multi-camera RGB images (6 cameras at 256×704 for nuScenes, 1 camera at 256×1408 for KITTI-360) and produces a 3D semantic occupancy field represented as a set of ~9k–36k semantic Gaussians (each with position, rotation, scale, opacity, and class distribution) that are splatted onto a voxel grid. During the pretraining stage, LiDAR point clouds are additionally used to initialize query positions and provide depth/RGB rendering supervision, but this dependency is removed in the final occupancy estimation stage. The output is structured hierarchically: K sparse queries (900 or 1800) each anchor a spatial region and decode into J finer Gaussians (10 or 20) that capture local geometry.

## Method

S2GO maintains a streaming queue of sparse 3D queries refined by a temporal transformer (self-attention across queries + deformable cross-attention to image features, following StreamPETR), then decodes each query into a group of semantic Gaussians with a shared velocity and opacity. A key contribution is a two-stage training procedure: Stage 1 initializes queries at noised LiDAR points and trains them with a denoising loss (recovering FPS-sampled LiDAR positions) plus depth and RGB rendering losses, teaching queries to self-organize onto occupied geometry; Stage 2 switches to learnable query initialization and trains for semantic occupancy with ground-truth voxel labels. The paper also introduces an opacity-weighted occupancy probability (multiplying the Gaussian density by opacity) to prevent unnatural scale-shrinking in unoccupied regions, and an efficient blocked Gaussian-to-voxel splatting CUDA kernel that achieves 20.4× backward-pass speedup and 3.3× memory reduction. Query propagation to future frames uses a distance-constrained top-k opacity selection to balance coverage and confidence.

## Results

On nuScenes-SurroundOcc, S2GO-Base achieves 35.5 IoU and 22.7 mIoU, outperforming the previous Gaussian-based SOTA GaussianWorld (32.8 IoU) by 2.7 IoU while running 4.5× faster (19.6 vs 4.4 FPS on a 4090). The lighter S2GO-Small variant (900 queries, 9k Gaussians) reaches 34.3 IoU at 26.1 FPS, achieving real-time inference on a single 4090. On SSCBench-KITTI-360 (monocular), S2GO-Base sets a new SOTA with 40.8 IoU and 15.1 mIoU, surpassing GaussianFormer-2 (38.4 IoU, 13.9 mIoU). On nuScenes-Occ3D, S2GO-Base achieves 39.1 RayIoU at 20.9 FPS on a 4090, competitive with methods that are 2–3× slower.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2GO-Base outperforms the previous Gaussian-based streaming SOTA, GaussianWorld, by 2.7 IoU (35.5 vs 32.8) while being 4.5× faster (19.6 vs 4.4 FPS), and beats GaussianFormer-2 by 3.8 IoU. Against the grid-aligned ALOcc-mini-GF, S2GO-Base achieves comparable IoU (35.5 vs 34.6) at 3.6× higher throughput (19.6 vs 5.4 FPS); ALOcc-GF reaches higher mIoU (25.5 vs 22.7) but at only 0.9 FPS, placing it outside the real-time regime. On KITTI-360, S2GO-Base (40.8 IoU, 15.1 mIoU) surpasses all prior monocular methods including GaussianFormer-2 (38.4 IoU, 13.9 mIoU) and OccFormer (40.3 IoU, 13.8 mIoU). The lightweight S2GO-Small (9k Gaussians) already exceeds GaussianWorld in IoU (34.3 vs 32.8) at 5.9× the speed, demonstrating that the sparse query representation is the key efficiency driver. On Occ3D, S2GO-Base (39.1 RayIoU) trails ALOcc-GF (44.1) and STCOcc (42.1) but runs at 20.9 FPS versus their 6.2 and unknown FPS respectively, offering the best accuracy–speed trade-off in the real-time regime.

## Relation to Prior Work

S2GO sits at the intersection of two established lines of work: streaming query-based perception (DETR, StreamPETR, PETR) and Gaussian-based 3D occupancy estimation (GaussianFormer, GaussianFormer-2, GaussianWorld). It departs from prior Gaussian occupancy methods by replacing tens of thousands of independently refined Gaussians with a compact set of ~1k sparse 3D queries that each decode into a small group of child Gaussians, and from prior streaming detection methods by applying the query-propagation paradigm to dense scene reconstruction rather than sparse object detection. The key conceptual departure is the geometry denoising pretraining phase, which uses noised LiDAR initialization and rendering supervision to teach queries to self-organize onto occupied regions before the semantic occupancy stage begins.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

S2GO demonstrates that real-time (26 FPS on a 4090) 3D semantic occupancy is achievable with a sparse query representation, which is practically important for vision-centric autonomous driving systems that need dense geometric understanding without LiDAR at inference. The streaming query formulation also enables arbitrarily long temporal context at no additional per-frame cost, a property that projection-based temporal fusion methods cannot match. Open questions include whether the denoising pretraining can be replaced by self-supervised or unlabeled-data pretraining at scale, and whether the query-based formulation extends naturally to multi-task end-to-end driving (detection + occupancy + planning). A limitation is that the pretraining stage still relies on LiDAR or equivalent depth supervision, though the authors show occupied-voxel locations and zero-shot monocular depth are viable substitutes.
