## Problem

Test-Time Training (TTT) offers dynamic weight adaptation at inference, but its integration into the LLM ecosystem is blocked by three barriers: existing TTT layers are architecturally incompatible with pre-trained Transformers (requiring costly retraining from scratch), per-token updates are inherently sequential and bottleneck GPU/TPU parallelism, and the generic reconstruction objective used for fast-weight updates is misaligned with the causal next-token-prediction task that governs autoregressive language modeling.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a text sequence as token embeddings, partitioned into non-overlapping chunks of size C (typically 512–1024 tokens), and produces standard next-token logits at each position. Unusually, the intermediate activations Z (gated MLP pre-projection features) serve as keys for fast-weight updates, while the value targets V̂ are derived from *future* token embeddings via a causal 1D convolution (kernel size 5) followed by a learned projection W_target, so the fast weights encode predictive rather than reconstructive information. At document boundaries, the fast weights reset to their pre-trained state to prevent cross-sequence leakage.

## Method

In-Place TTT repurposes the final projection matrix W_down of existing gated MLP blocks as the fast weights, updating it in-place during inference without any architectural modification—making it a true drop-in enhancement for pre-trained LLMs. For each chunk, the current W_down is first applied to produce the output (apply), then updated via a single gradient step W_down^(i+1) = W_down^(i) + η·V̂_[i]ᵀ·Z_[i] (update), where the LM-aligned target V̂ = Conv1D(X₀)·W_target encodes future-token information explicitly aligned with next-token prediction. This chunk-wise, associative update rule is fully compatible with context parallelism via a prefix-sum scan, enabling large chunk sizes (512–1024) that saturate modern accelerators. A theoretical analysis in the induction-head setting proves that the NTP-aligned target provably increases the logit of the correct next token (bounded below by λ_lr·c_norm²·c_align) while leaving other logits nearly unchanged, whereas the reconstruction target provides no such predictive benefit.

## Results

As a drop-in enhancement, In-Place TTT boosts Qwen3-4B-Base's RULER accuracy from 74.3% to 78.7% at 64k context and from 74.8% to 77.0% at 128k, while also improving 256k extrapolation from 41.7% to 43.9%. When pretrained from scratch at 4B scale, In-Place TTT with Full Attention raises RULER-16k from 6.58 to 19.99, and with SWA raises RULER-8k from 9.91 to 26.80. At 500M and 1.5B scales, In-Place TTT consistently achieves lower sliding-window perplexity than all baselines (SWA, GLA, DeltaNet, LaCT) up to the full 32k context length.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against larger or comparable open-source models on RULER, the 4B In-Place TTT model outperforms Mistral-7B by +29.7 at 64k and +63.2 at 128k, GLM3-6B by +22.7 and +35.0 respectively, and Llama3-8B by +2.4 and +7.5. It also surpasses the same-size Qwen3-4B-Instruct by +0.9 at 64k and +11.0 at 128k, and edges out the 14B Phi3-medium at 128k (77.0 vs 46.1). In the from-scratch pretraining comparison at 500M and 1.5B scales (Figure 2), In-Place TTT achieves lower sliding-window perplexity than all four architectural baselines—SWA (Longformer), Gated Linear Attention (GLA), DeltaNet, and LaCT—across the full 32k context range, with the gap widening at longer contexts. At 4B scale (Table 3), In-Place TTT with Full Attention improves RULER-16k from 6.58 to 19.99 (+13.4) and RULER-8k from 38.09 to 43.82, while the SWA variant improves RULER-8k from 9.91 to 26.80 (+16.9), confirming consistent gains over both attention backbones.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Test-Time Training (TTT) paradigm—originally developed as a standalone recurrent layer to replace attention (Sun et al., 2020; 2024; Schlag et al., 2021)—by repurposing the existing MLP blocks in standard Transformers as the fast-weight memory, drawing on the observation that feed-forward layers already function as key-value stores (Geva et al., 2020). It inherits the chunk-wise update mechanism from recent efficiency-focused TTT work (Behrouz et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2025; Irie & Gershman, 2025) but departs fundamentally in two ways: (i) TTT operates complementarily to attention rather than replacing it, and (ii) the fast-weight update objective is explicitly aligned with Next-Token Prediction rather than the generic reconstruction loss used in prior TTT. The theoretical analysis is grounded in the induction-head framework (Olsson et al., 2022; Elhage et al., 2021), providing a formal justification for why the NTP-aligned target is superior.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 'drop-in' design is practically significant because it eliminates the need for costly pretraining from scratch, making TTT accessible to the existing ecosystem of billion-parameter LLMs—a barrier that has limited prior TTT research to moderate scales. The context-parallel implementation and compatibility with RoPE extension techniques (YaRN) suggest straightforward integration into production inference pipelines. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to other efficient attention backbones (linear attention, SSMs), whether the specific loss function and optimizer choices (left as future work) materially affect performance, and whether the benefits persist at scales beyond 14B parameters or in non-English/multilingual settings. The paper also does not explore downstream task performance beyond language modeling and RULER, leaving open how In-Place TTT affects reasoning, code generation, or agentic capabilities.
