## Problem

Existing equivariant neural networks handle Euclidean isometries (rotations, translations) or scale equivariance, but none address the full conformal group, which uniquely includes non-affine special conformal transformations. This gap is significant because conformal invariance governs critical phenomena in statistical physics and scale-insensitive perception in vision, yet no architecture could exploit this symmetry by construction.

## Input / Output

The method consumes point clouds in R^d with optional per-point features interpreted as samples of a conformal field of dimension Δ. Features are lifted into the AdS bulk via a z^Δ rescaling, and outputs are projected back to the boundary with z^{-Δ}, ensuring correct transformation under scaling. Beyond standard classification or regression outputs, the model exposes trainable conformal dimension parameters Δ that can be read off post-training as physically meaningful quantities (e.g., recovering Δ_σ=1/8 for the 2d Ising spin field).

## Method

The core mechanism lifts point-cloud data from R^d into Anti de Sitter space AdS^{d+1}, where the global conformal group Conf_g(R^d) is isomorphic to the isometry group PO(d+1,1) of AdS. Each point's z-coordinate is determined by computing the AdS center of mass of its k_lift nearest neighbours, which encodes local length-scale information and preserves scale invariance. Message passing is then conditioned on the AdS proper distance D(X,X') = arccosh(|⟨Y,Y'⟩|), a scalar invariant under the full conformal group, yielding conformal equivariance without additional computational overhead over Euclidean GNNs. For the AdS-CEGNN variant, O(d+1,1)-equivariant Clifford algebra operations on multivector features provide full equivariant (not just invariant) message passing under the restricted conformal group.

## Results

On SuperPixel MNIST, AdS-GNN achieves 4.09±0.27% classification error and is the only model that maintains this performance under both rotation and scaling augmentations (4.09% across all three test settings), whereas all other baselines degrade or fail entirely under scaling. On the 2D Ising correlation function prediction task, AdS-GNN outperforms EGNN and MPNN in all regimes, with more than an order-of-magnitude improvement for 2-point functions. The model also correctly recovers conformal dimensions (Δ_ε ≈ 0.99–1.00, Δ_σ ≈ 0.125) as trainable parameters, and AdS-CEGNN outperforms CEGNN on the N-body simulation task while recovering the expected Δ_a = 2 for acceleration.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On SuperPixel MNIST, AdS-GNN (4.09%) is slightly behind PΘNITA (1.17%) and PNCNN (1.24%) on non-augmented data, and behind GAT (3.81%) as well, but it is the only model that generalizes to both rotated and scaled test data without performance degradation. EGNN (4.17%) matches AdS-GNN on non-augmented and rotated data but fails completely under scaling, while PΘNITA handles rotation but not scaling. On shape segmentation, AdS-GNN outperforms EGNN on in-distribution accuracy, particularly with few training points, though both trail the translation-only MPNN due to lacking orientational information. On PascalVOC-SP, AdS-GNN (28.07±0.57 F1) is statistically indistinguishable from EGNN (27.80±0.74 F1). On the 2D and 3D Ising tasks, AdS-GNN consistently outperforms both EGNN and MPNN across all system sizes and generalizes better across N. On N-body simulation, AdS-CEGNN outperforms CEGNN (the SOTA baseline) in relative L2 loss over long trajectories.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the equivariant GNN lineage (EGNN, CEGNN) by lifting point-cloud data from flat Euclidean space into Anti de Sitter (AdS) space, where the full conformal group acts as the isometry group. The message-passing architecture directly adapts EGNN's structure, replacing Euclidean pairwise distances with AdS proper distances, while the AdS-CEGNN variant leverages Clifford-algebra equivariant networks from Ruhe et al. (2023) operating on the ambient R^{d+1,1}. The key conceptual departure from prior scale-equivariant or Lie-group-equivariant work is that the AdS geometry enforces equivariance under the full conformal group—including non-affine special conformal transformations—by construction, rather than through parameterized group actions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first GNN architecture equivariant under the full conformal group, opening a path to models that are intrinsically scale- and angle-invariant without data augmentation. The ability to read off conformal dimensions (Δ) as learned parameters provides a form of physical interpretability absent from standard deep learning, and the strong generalization across system sizes and spatial ranges suggests the architecture captures underlying physics rather than memorizing patterns. Limitations include the mild breaking of special conformal invariance from the z₀ regulator, and the framework is currently validated only on small-scale tasks; open questions include whether 3-point OPE coefficients can also be extracted and whether the approach scales to larger, more complex physical or vision problems.
