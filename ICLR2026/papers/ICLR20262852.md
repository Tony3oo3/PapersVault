## Problem

Multi-agent LLM systems require efficient inter-model communication, but existing protocols are inadequate: natural-language debate incurs high decoding costs and loses information through token sampling, while hidden-state transfer suffers from information concentration bias (the last token dominates) and a dilemma where early-layer prepending offers no computation savings and late-layer prepending degrades performance. No prior method enables selective, non-contiguous sharing of internal representations between LLMs with minimal overhead.

## Input / Output

The sender model M_s receives a context C and performs a single prefill forward pass to produce KV pairs at every layer; the receiver model M_r receives a query Q plus a subset of M_s's KV pairs and generates the final answer. The selection of which layers' KV pairs to transmit is determined offline by a calibration set as small as one sample, after which the layer subset is fixed for all subsequent queries. At each selected layer, the sender's KV pairs are simply concatenated ahead of the receiver's own KV pairs in the attention computation, so the receiver attends to both its query tokens and the sender's context tokens simultaneously.

## Method

KVComm transmits KV pairs (rather than hidden states or embeddings) from a sender LLM to a receiver LLM, exploiting the fact that KV pairs do not directly overwrite the receiver's hidden states and let the receiver's attention mechanism decide how to weight the incoming information. A layer-selection strategy scores each layer by an attention importance score (the mean attention mass that all heads in that layer assign to the context tokens during prefill, normalized to [0,1]) combined with a Gaussian prior centered at L/2 with σ=10, via a weighted sum S^l = α·S_a^l + (1−α)·P^l; the top-M layers by this score are selected, enabling non-contiguous layer subsets. The selected KV pairs are concatenated into the receiver's attention at the corresponding layers, shifting the receiver's positional indices by |C| to maintain positional coherence across all depths. This design requires no decoding steps from the sender, no retraining, and only a single calibration sample to determine the layer subset, achieving performance comparable to the Skyline upper bound (full context+query concatenation) while transmitting as few as 30% of layers' KV pairs and reducing computation by 2.5×–6×.

## Results

On F1 score across 8 contextual reasoning tasks, KVComm(0.7) (selecting 70% of layers' KV pairs) achieves performance comparable to the Skyline upper bound (direct input concatenation) on most datasets, e.g., 0.65 vs 0.73 on HotpotQA and 0.29 vs 0.25 on QASPER for the Llama-3.2-3B→DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-3B pair. Even with only 30% of layers' KV pairs, KVComm(0.3) outperforms all natural-language and activation-based baselines on most datasets (e.g., 0.46 vs 0.43 for NLD on HotpotQA). KVComm reduces computation by 2.5×–6× relative to Skyline and uses 23%–73% less memory, while transmitting up to 3× fewer KV pairs than full-layer sharing.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KVComm consistently outperforms all three activation- and language-based baselines across the majority of datasets and model pairs. Against NLD (Du et al., 2023), KVComm(0.7) achieves substantially higher F1 on complex multi-hop tasks: 0.65 vs 0.43 on HotpotQA, 0.29 vs 0.10 on QASPER, and 0.36 vs 0.18 on MuSiQuest (Llama-3.2-3B pair); NLD only approaches KVComm on the simpler Countries and Tipsheets datasets where very little information needs to be transferred. Against CIPHER (Pham et al., 2023), KVComm(0.7) leads by similar margins (e.g., 0.65 vs 0.50 on HotpotQA, 0.29 vs 0.10 on QASPER), while CIPHER degrades sharply on the Qwen and Falcon model pairs (e.g., 0.03 on HotpotQA for Qwen). Against AC (Ramesh & Li, 2025), which transmits only the last token's hidden state, KVComm shows the largest gaps: AC(replace) scores as low as 0.00–0.05 on most datasets, while KVComm(0.3) already reaches 0.46 on HotpotQA; the paper attributes AC's failure to information concentration bias in the last token's hidden state. KVComm(0.7) matches or exceeds the Skyline upper bound on Countries (0.57 = 0.57) and Tipsheets (0.81 vs 0.91), and on two datasets (QASPER, MultiFieldQA-en) it actually surpasses Skyline, suggesting selective KV sharing provides a regularization benefit over full input concatenation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground in inter-LLM communication by repurposing the KV cache—a structure well-known in inference acceleration (FlashAttention, PagedAttention, CacheGen)—as a cross-model communication medium, rather than reusing it within a single model. It departs from the natural-language debate paradigm (Du et al., 2023), embedding-space communication (CIPHER, Pham et al., 2023), and hidden-state transfer (AC, Ramesh & Li, 2025) by arguing that KV pairs avoid the information concentration bias of hidden states and the decoding overhead of text-based protocols. The closest prior work, DroidSpeak (Liu et al., 2024b), also shares KV caches across models but restricts itself to a single contiguous layer chunk for shared-prefix queries; KVComm generalizes this to non-contiguous, attention-score-guided layer selection across different (fine-tuned) models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

KVComm offers a practical, low-overhead communication primitive for multi-agent LLM systems, reducing computation by 2.5×–6× and communication volume by up to 3× relative to full-KV or concatenation baselines while matching or occasionally exceeding the Skyline upper bound. The finding that a single calibration sample suffices for layer selection makes the approach deployment-friendly. Key limitations include the requirement that sender and receiver share the same base architecture (blocking cross-family communication), and the current fixed (offline) selection strategy; extending to heterogeneous architectures via learned projections and to context-adaptive online selection are the most impactful open directions.
