## Problem

Attention-based multivariate time series forecasting models capture global cross-variate dependencies but scale quadratically with the number of variates, making them impractical for high-dimensional systems with hundreds or thousands of channels. Channel-independent models avoid this cost but forgo explicit cross-variate modeling entirely. The paper challenges whether exhaustive global dependency search is actually necessary, proposing that local cross-variate interactions suffice for dense dependency systems.

## Input / Output

The model takes a multivariate time series of shape (B, L, C) with a fixed look-back window L=96 and C variates, partitioned into P=L/p non-overlapping patches of length p. It produces a forecast of S future time steps (S ∈ {96, 192, 336, 720}) by predicting future patch embeddings through a channel-independent head and decoding them back to the time domain via a shared autoencoder decoder. The key structural detail is that patch embeddings are reinterpreted as a 2D variate-patch field (C × P grid) before local convolution, rather than being processed as independent univariate sequences or global attention tokens.

## Method

VPNet is grounded in the Local Sufficiency Hypothesis, supported by a hypergeometric probability analysis showing that a bounded local window of width k captures at least one informative variate with probability ≥ 1 − exp(−kr/(C−1)) under random permutation. The architecture operates in four stages: (1) a patch-level overcomplete autoencoder (shared across variates and patches) encodes each patch into an H-dimensional latent; (2) a channelization step permutes the embedding tensor so that H becomes the channel dimension and (C, P) form a 2D spatial grid — the variate-patch field; (3) a stack of VarTCNBlocks applies depthwise 2D convolutions (kernel k_v × k_p) over this field to capture local cross-variate and temporal dependencies simultaneously, followed by pointwise 1×1 convolutions in an inverted-bottleneck FFN, all with residual connections; (4) a shared channel-independent MLP head maps each variate's flattened historical patch sequence to future patch embeddings, which the autoencoder decoder reconstructs into the time domain. The depthwise convolution design ensures computational complexity scales linearly with C rather than quadratically, while the local receptive field (typically k_v=3) empirically captures sufficient cross-variate signal in dense systems.

## Results

VPNet achieves the best average MSE across 7 of 8 benchmark datasets, with particularly large gains on high-dimensional settings: 0.162 MSE on Electricity (9% below iTransformer's 0.178) and 0.421 on Traffic (26% below TimeKAN's 0.572). On Solar-Energy, VPNet reaches 0.204 MSE versus 0.276 for TimeKAN. The only dataset where VPNet is not the top performer is ETTh1, where TimeKAN edges ahead with 0.426 vs. 0.434 MSE. Across all four prediction horizons (96, 192, 336, 720), VPNet consistently ranks first or second, confirming robustness across both high- and low-dimensional regimes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VPNet outperforms all 11 baselines on 7 of 8 datasets in MSE, with the largest margins on high-dimensional benchmarks: 9.0% over iTransformer on Electricity (0.162 vs. 0.178), 26.4% over TimeKAN on Traffic (0.421 vs. 0.572), and 26.1% over TimeKAN on Solar-Energy (0.204 vs. 0.276). On the low-dimensional ETT suite, VPNet wins on ETTh2, ETTm1, and ETTm2 but is narrowly behind TimeKAN on ETTh1 (0.434 vs. 0.426 MSE). Among channel-fusion Transformer baselines, iTransformer is the closest competitor on Weather (0.258 vs. 0.238) and Traffic (0.428 vs. 0.421), while channel-independent models (PatchTST, TimeMixer, DLinear) trail by 10–30% on high-dimensional datasets. On the GIFT-Eval benchmark (33 settings), VPNet beats iTransformer on 17 settings, confirming generalization beyond the primary eight benchmarks. The efficiency analysis further shows VPNet's memory grows only 67% when variates increase from 321 to 862, versus 99% for iTransformer, validating its linear-scaling design.

## Relation to Prior Work

VPNet positions itself as a principled middle ground between two established paradigms in multivariate time series forecasting: channel-independent models (PatchTST, TimeMixer, DLinear) that are efficient but ignore cross-variate structure, and channel-fusion Transformer models (iTransformer, Crossformer, Pathformer) that capture global dependencies at quadratic cost. It builds directly on the patch-level overcomplete autoencoder from AdaPatch (Liu et al., 2025b) for input/output projection, and draws on the TCN lineage (TimesNet, ModernTCN) for local convolutional processing, but departs conceptually by reinterpreting patch embeddings as a 2D variate-patch field rather than a frequency-based temporal plane. The Local Sufficiency Hypothesis, backed by a hypergeometric concentration bound, provides a theoretical justification for restricting cross-variate mixing to small local neighborhoods, which no prior architecture had formalized in this way.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper offers a practical resolution to the accuracy-scalability tension in high-dimensional forecasting: linear complexity in the number of variates while matching or exceeding global-attention baselines on dense datasets (Electricity, Traffic, Solar-Energy). The counter-intuitive robustness to variate ordering (random ≈ spectral ≈ original) suggests the model captures time-lagged or higher-order dependencies beyond instantaneous correlation, an open question the authors flag for future theoretical investigation. Limitations include mixed results on the GIFT-Eval benchmark (winning only 17/33 settings vs. iTransformer) and acknowledged sensitivity to ordering in sparse-dependency regimes, where structure-aware reordering becomes necessary. The variate-patch field representation and adaptive locality (dynamically adjusting receptive field per variate or layer) are identified as promising directions for broader application beyond forecasting.
