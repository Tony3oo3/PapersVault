## Problem

Existing membership-privacy defenses update or retrain all weights, incurring high cost and unnecessary utility loss, while standard pruning techniques fail to eliminate privacy risks because they cannot distinguish privacy-vulnerable weights from learnability-critical ones. No prior work has identified where, at the weight level, privacy vulnerability actually resides in a trained neural network, nor explained why accuracy-oriented pruning leaves membership leakage intact.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a fully trained model (M_up), a structurally identical vanilla model at initialization (M_vn), a member set D_tr (the training data), and a held-out non-member reference set D_re that is disjoint from both training and test data. It produces a privacy-fine-tuned model in which a small fraction (1%–10%, grid-searched) of weights are frozen at their initial values and the remainder are fine-tuned for 40 epochs with a pluggable privacy-preserving objective. The rewinding rate r is the primary hyperparameter controlling the privacy–utility tradeoff, and the approach is architecture-agnostic (applied to ResNet18 and ViT on 32×32 RGB images, and to a text transformer on DBpedia-14).

## Method

CWRF (Critical Weights Rewinding and Fine-tuning) operates in three stages. First, it estimates per-weight privacy vulnerability by fine-tuning a copy of the trained model with a machine-unlearning-inspired loss that minimizes cross-entropy on member data while maximizing KL divergence from the vanilla model on non-member data, accumulating Taylor-first-order scores (|gradient × weight|) over 30 iterations. Second, it identifies the top-r% highest-scoring weights, rewinds them to their initial (pre-training) values, and freezes them via gradient masking. Third, it fine-tunes only the remaining privacy-invulnerable weights using any standard privacy-preserving training approach (DP-SGD, RelaxLoss, HAMP, CCL, PAST), with the learning rate also rewound to its initial value. The key insight is that weight importance for accuracy stems from location rather than value: rewinding preserves the critical positions so the model can recover accuracy, while removing (pruning) those positions causes unrecoverable accuracy collapse. Because privacy-vulnerable weights are highly correlated (PCC > 0.9 in conv/attention/MLP layers) with learnability-critical weights, this location-preserving rewind-and-freeze strategy decouples privacy removal from utility loss in a way that pruning cannot.

## Results

On CIFAR-10 with ViT, combining DP-SGD with CWRF improves test accuracy from 68.18% to 81.43% while reducing LiRA AUC from 1.22 to 0.53 and TPR@10⁻³ from 0.27 to 0.07. On CINIC-10 with ResNet18, HAMP+CWRF achieves test accuracy substantially exceeding the undefended baseline while delivering the best privacy resilience against both LiRA and RMIA. Across all datasets and architectures, CWRF consistently reduces AUC and TPR metrics by 30–90% relative to the corresponding privacy-preserving baseline, with a rewinding rate as low as 1% of total weights.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CWRF is evaluated as a plug-in enhancement on top of four existing privacy-preserving training methods: DP-SGD, RelaxLoss, HAMP, and CCL. On CIFAR-10 with ViT, CWRF+DP-SGD achieves the most dramatic improvement, raising test accuracy from 68.18% to 81.43% (+13.25 pp) while halving LiRA AUC (1.22→0.53) and reducing RMIA AUC by 81% (4.30→0.80). For ResNet18, CWRF consistently reduces LiRA AUC to near-zero (≤0.03) across all three baselines (RelaxLoss, HAMP, CCL), with HAMP+CWRF also improving accuracy from 77.10% to 79.56%. On CINIC-10, HAMP+CWRF surpasses the undefended cross-entropy baseline in accuracy while achieving the lowest AUC and TPR among all configurations. The one notable exception is RelaxLoss+CWRF under RMIA on ResNet18, where privacy metrics show a slight increase due to high variance in RelaxLoss's test accuracy destabilizing the shadow models. Overall, CWRF wins on privacy metrics in nearly all settings and matches or exceeds baseline accuracy in the majority of configurations.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) weight importance estimation via Taylor First Order scoring (Molchanov et al., 2019), (2) machine unlearning as a mechanism to isolate data-specific knowledge in weights (Bourtoule et al., 2021), and (3) weight rewinding from the lottery ticket hypothesis literature (Frankle & Carbin, 2019; Renda et al., 2020). It departs from prior pruning-based privacy work (Yuan & Zhang, 2022) by explaining *why* pruning fails for membership privacy—namely, that privacy-vulnerable weights are entangled with learnability-critical weights—and replaces removal with selective rewinding plus fine-tuning of the complementary weights. The paper is the first to perform membership-privacy-oriented fine-tuning at weight-level granularity, repurposing the unlearning objective as a scoring signal rather than a training procedure.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The entanglement finding provides a principled explanation for a known failure mode (pruning does not reduce MIA vulnerability) and the location-over-value insight generalizes the lottery ticket hypothesis to the privacy domain, suggesting that a model's privacy footprint is structurally determined at initialization. Practically, CWRF is a plug-in wrapper that can be combined with any existing privacy-preserving training approach (DP-SGD, RelaxLoss, HAMP, CCL), making it broadly applicable without retraining from scratch. Limitations include evaluation only on small-scale classification tasks (CIFAR-10/100, CINIC-10, DBpedia-14) with modest architectures (ResNet18, ViT), a requirement for a held-out reference set during the scoring phase, and unclear scalability to large generative or multimodal models. Open questions include whether the entanglement structure persists in deeper/wider networks, whether the approach transfers to generative membership inference, and how sensitive the rewinding rate is in practice.
