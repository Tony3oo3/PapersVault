## Problem

Existing diffusion-based inverse problem solvers suffer from three complementary failures: iterative guidance methods push intermediate states off the learned data manifold, stochastic MAP methods overfit to noise without careful per-task tuning, and deterministic MAP methods collapse to a single local mode in severely ill-posed problems. No existing approach handles unknown noise types or levels without task-specific hyperparameter calibration.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260605/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a noisy measurement vector y ∈ R^m, a known forward operator A, and a pretrained diffusion model (DDIM score network), operating on 256×256 RGB images. It outputs posterior samples in the initial-noise space x_T ∈ R^n (n = 3×256×256), which are mapped to clean reconstructions via a two-step unconditional DDIM trajectory. The noise-adaptive variant requires no knowledge of the noise type or standard deviation, using the same fixed hyperparameters (L=20, δ₀=0.05, γ=0.95) across all tasks, datasets, and noise conditions.

## Method

N-HMC reformulates inverse problem solving as posterior sampling in the initial-noise space by exploiting the deterministic mapping property of DDIM: the entire reverse trajectory is a fixed function D: x_T → x_0, so the likelihood p(y|x_T) = N(y; A(D(x_T)), σ_y²I) is exact and differentiable without intermediate approximations. HMC with leapfrog integration and Metropolis-Hastings correction then samples from p(x_T|y) ∝ p(x_T)p(y|x_T), where the prior is simply N(0,I), keeping all proposals on the learned data manifold since denoising is unconditional. The noise-adaptive extension (NA-NHMC) places a Jeffreys prior p(σ_y²) ∝ 1/σ_y² on the noise variance and marginalizes it out, yielding a parameter-free likelihood ∝ (½‖y − A(D(x_T))‖²)^(−m/2) whose gradient is provably equivalent to the known-noise case under the high-quality-manifold assumption. An annealing schedule on σ_y during the first K iterations promotes early exploration of the noise space, preventing the sampler from being trapped in spurious local modes in highly multimodal problems like phase retrieval.

## Results

On the most challenging nonlinear inverse problems (FFHQ 256×256, σ_y=0.05), NA-NHMC achieves the best PSNR across all three tasks: 27.66 dB on nonlinear deblurring, 19.30 dB on phase retrieval, and 28.45 dB on HDR reconstruction. It also attains the best SSIM (0.792, 0.554, 0.849) and lowest LPIPS (0.249, 0.482, 0.217) on all three tasks. Notably, these results are obtained with only 2 DDIM denoising steps and a single fixed hyperparameter configuration (L=20, δ₀=0.05, γ=0.95) shared across all tasks, noise levels, and datasets, requiring no task-specific tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

NA-NHMC outperforms all seven baselines on every metric for nonlinear deblurring and phase retrieval on FFHQ. On phase retrieval (the most ill-posed task), NA-NHMC achieves 19.30 dB PSNR, surpassing the next-best DAPS (18.52 dB) by 0.78 dB and DPS (10.87 dB) by a large margin of 8.43 dB; DMPlug is not evaluated on this task due to mode collapse. On nonlinear deblurring, NA-NHMC leads DMPlug (27.15 dB) by 0.51 dB in PSNR and DAPS (27.00 dB) by 0.66 dB. For HDR reconstruction, NA-NHMC achieves 28.45 dB PSNR, beating DPS (27.46 dB) by nearly 1 dB and DAPS (26.03 dB) by 2.42 dB, while matching DPS's SSIM of 0.849. Under higher noise (σ_y=0.20), the gap widens further: MAP-based methods like DAPS, SITCOM, and DMPlug degrade severely (e.g., DAPS drops to 17.38 dB on nonlinear deblurring), while NA-NHMC maintains 24.89 dB. The key structural advantage is that NA-NHMC uses a single fixed configuration across all tasks and noise levels, whereas DPS requires task-specific step sizes (ζ ranging from 0.4 to 1.0) and DAPS uses a heuristic σ_y=0.01 regardless of true noise.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on DMPlug's (Wang et al., 2024) observation that inverse problems can be formulated in the diffusion model's initial-noise space, but departs fundamentally by replacing DMPlug's deterministic MAP optimization with Hamiltonian Monte Carlo posterior sampling. It also draws on the broader lineage of diffusion-based inverse problem solvers (DPS, DDRM, DAPS, ReSample, SITCOM) to identify and formalize three failure modes—manifold infeasibility, noise overfitting, and local-mode collapse—that its noise-space sampling framework simultaneously addresses. The noise-adaptive extension (NA-NHMC) introduces a Jeffreys prior on the measurement noise variance, marginalizing it out to yield a parameter-free likelihood, which is a Bayesian modeling choice not previously exploited in the diffusion-for-IPs literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The elimination of task-specific hyperparameter tuning is a significant practical advance: a single configuration (L=20, δ₀=0.05, γ=0.95) generalizes across seven inverse problem types, two datasets, two noise levels, and three noise distributions (Gaussian, impulse, speckle), whereas every competing method requires per-task or per-noise calibration. The theoretical guarantees (Propositions 1–2) provide a principled explanation for why the method does not overfit to noise, which is valuable for practitioners working in high-noise or unknown-noise regimes. Key limitations include higher computational cost than single-pass methods like DPS (due to HMC sampling and backpropagation through the DDIM decoder), a long warmup phase before stationarity in high dimensions, and reliance on only two diffusion steps that may limit applicability to more complex or higher-resolution settings. Future work on faster gradient estimation and relaxed detailed-balance warmup strategies could make the approach more broadly deployable.
