## Problem

The bidirectional modification of Iterative Markovian Fitting (IMF), used in practice by DSBM and ASBM to stabilize training, was a heuristic without theoretical grounding. It was unclear whether this procedure actually converges to the Schrödinger Bridge solution, and its relationship to the well-studied IPF (Sinkhorn) procedure remained unexplored. The paper asks: what is this heuristic actually doing, and can we prove it converges from arbitrary starting points?
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260658/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes two unpaired image distributions (source p_0 and target p_1) at resolutions ranging from 32×32 (colored MNIST) to 512×512 (AFHQ) and produces translated images in the target domain. A key design choice is the starting coupling q^0(x_0, x_1), which can be set to IMF-like (independent marginals), IPF-like (Wiener prior), Identity (x_1 = x_0), or SDEdit-based couplings. This coupling acts as a tunable hyperparameter: all choices converge to the same SB solution, but different initializations yield different trade-offs between generation quality (FID) and input-output similarity (MSE) along the optimization trajectory.

## Method

The paper reveals that the practical bidirectional IMF procedure is secretly performing IPF projections at each step: each Markovian projection (proj_M) followed by fixing one marginal to the target distribution is algebraically equivalent to an IPF projection (proj_0 or proj_1) composed with proj_M. This unified procedure, called IPMF, alternates between two IMF projections (reciprocal + Markovian, improving optimality) and two IPF projections (marginal replacement, improving marginal matching) per full step. The authors prove exponential convergence of IPMF parameters for Gaussian marginals (Theorem 3.2) and weak convergence for bounded-support distributions (Theorem 3.3), extending beyond prior results that only covered one-directional IMF or IPF from specific initializations. Practically, IPMF is implemented via existing DSBM (diffusion-based) or ASBM (GAN-based) solvers, with the starting coupling serving as the sole new design knob to control the quality-similarity trade-off.

## Results

On the Schrödinger Bridges Benchmark (cBW₂²-UVP ↓), ASBM-Identity achieves the best scores in most high-dimensional settings (e.g., 0.58 at D=2/ε=0.1, 0.12 at D=128/ε=10), while DSBM-IMF leads at D=16/ε=1 (0.63). On CelebA 64×64 male→female translation, DSBM-IMF achieves FID 13.65 with MSE 0.16, while DSBM-Identity achieves FID 33.11 with MSE 0.03, demonstrating the quality–similarity trade-off enabled by the starting coupling. On AFHQ 512×512 cat→wild, DSBM-IMF-OT achieves FID 53.42 / MSE 0.085 / CMMD 0.591, while DSBM-Identity achieves FID 65.19 / MSE 0.054 / CMMD 0.731, confirming the same trade-off pattern at higher resolution.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only external baseline is SF²M-Sink (Gushchin et al., 2023b), a Sinkhorn-based bridge matching method evaluated on the Schrödinger Bridges Benchmark. The comparison is mixed: SF²M-Sink wins at low dimensions (D=2) for ε=0.1 (0.54 vs. 0.58 for ASBM-Identity) and at D=16/ε=0.1 (3.7 vs. 4.61 for DSBM-IMF), but IPMF variants dominate at higher dimensions and larger ε. For example, at D=128/ε=10, ASBM-Identity achieves 2.86 vs. SF²M-Sink's 819, and at D=64/ε=10, ASBM-Identity reaches 1.66 vs. 319. At D=16/ε=1, DSBM-IMF (0.63) and DSBM-IPF (0.76) both beat SF²M-Sink (1.1). The paper's key message is not to outperform a single baseline but to show that all starting couplings converge to similar quality within each solver type, and that the starting coupling acts as a tunable hyperparameter controlling the generation-quality vs. input-output-similarity trade-off on real image data (CelebA, AFHQ).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper reveals that the bidirectional modification of Iterative Markovian Fitting (IMF), used as a practical heuristic in DSBM (Shi et al., 2023) and ASBM (Gushchin et al., 2024b), is in fact a principled interleaving of IPF (Sinkhorn) projections and IMF projections, which the authors formalize as Iterative Proportional Markovian Fitting (IPMF). Prior work treated the forward-backward alternation as an ad hoc stabilization trick without theoretical justification; here, the authors show it secretly performs IPF marginal-fitting steps between IMF optimality-refining steps. The convergence analysis builds on the Gaussian EOT structure from Gushchin et al. (2023b) and the discrete/continuous IMF update formulas from Peluchetti (2023a) and Gushchin et al. (2024b), but extends them to the combined IPMF procedure for which no prior convergence guarantees existed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The IPMF framework provides the first convergence guarantees for the bidirectional SB solvers actually used in practice (DSBM, ASBM), closing a gap where only one-directional IMF had been analyzed. The finding that the starting coupling acts as a tunable hyperparameter controlling the generation-quality vs. input-output-similarity trade-off offers practitioners a new design knob for task-specific tuning. A key open question is whether convergence holds beyond the Gaussian and bounded-support settings proven here; the authors conjecture it does but leave the general proof for future work. The framework also suggests a route to making rectified-flow distillation (the ε→0 limit of one-directional IMF) more stable by incorporating the bidirectional IPMF correction.
