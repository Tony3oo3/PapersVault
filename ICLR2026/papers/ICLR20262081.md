## Problem

Existing clustered federated learning methods rely on either data or gradient similarity alone for client grouping, restrict knowledge sharing to within-cluster clients, fail to handle all four types of data heterogeneity (label skew, feature skew, concept shift, quantity shift), and require the number of clusters to be specified in advance. FEDDAG addresses these gaps by integrating both data and gradient information for clustering, enabling cross-cluster representation sharing, and automatically determining the optimal number of clusters.

## Input / Output

Each client holds a local set of RGB images (32×32 for CIFAR-10/FMNIST/SVHN, 32×32 for CIFAR-100) with non-IID label and quantity distributions. During the clustering phase, clients upload only a k-sparse gradient (1–2% of model coordinates) and per-class principal vectors (~1% of class data size) rather than full model updates. The server outputs a cluster assignment for each client and, during training, each client produces a class label prediction from the dual-encoder model.

## Method

FEDDAG computes a fused proximity matrix by combining a class-wise weighted data similarity (principal angles between per-class subspaces, weighted by log-frequency ratios to handle quantity shift) with a k-sparse gradient cosine similarity, where per-client blending weights are optimized via an entropy loss that sharpens the affinity structure. Adaptive clustering is performed by iterating over hierarchical clustering thresholds and selecting the partition that minimizes a federated-aware loss balancing compactness and a degeneracy penalty against over-splitting. For training, each cluster uses a dual-encoder architecture: a primary encoder trained on its own clients' data and a secondary encoder refined using gradients from complementary source clusters identified by a Cluster Complementarity Graph (CC-Graph) that scores demand–supply alignment per class. The two encoders' outputs are concatenated and fed to a shared classifier, enabling cross-cluster feature transfer while preserving cluster-specific specialization.

## Results

Under Data Distribution I (20% label skew, high quantity shift with Dirichlet α'=0.25), FEDDAG achieves 90.76% on CIFAR-10, 93.82% on FMNIST, 93.91% on SVHN, and 72.84% on CIFAR-100, outperforming the strongest baselines (PACFL, IFCA, CFL-GP) by 2–7 percentage points across all four datasets. Under concept shift (Data Distribution II), FEDDAG reaches 69.90% on CIFAR-10, 88.93% on FMNIST, and 85.34% on SVHN, surpassing the best baseline (CFL-GP) by 3.2–4.2 points. Under LDA-based skew (Data Distribution III, α'=0.25), FEDDAG attains 77.84% on CIFAR-10, 91.88% on FMNIST, and 93.17% on SVHN, again leading all baselines by 2–4 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FEDDAG consistently outperforms all 12 baselines across every dataset and heterogeneity setting. Against the strongest data-based clustered FL method PACFL, FEDDAG gains +3.83 pp on CIFAR-10 (90.76 vs. 86.93), +1.92 pp on FMNIST, +4.03 pp on SVHN, and +6.73 pp on CIFAR-100 under 20% label skew with high quantity shift. Against the top gradient-based methods IFCA and CFL-GP, FEDDAG leads by 2.1–4.9 pp on CIFAR-10 and 1.9–4.2 pp on CIFAR-100. Under concept shift, FEDDAG (69.90% CIFAR-10) surpasses CFL-GP (66.74%) by 3.16 pp and IFCA (64.58%) by 5.32 pp, confirming that the class-wise similarity mechanism handles label remapping better than gradient-only or whole-subspace approaches. Single-model FL baselines (FedAvg, FedProx) and non-clustered non-IID methods (FedMix, FedBR) trail by 20–48 pp, underscoring the necessity of clustering under severe heterogeneity. The ablation (FEDDAG† vs. FEDDAG) shows that the dual-encoder cross-cluster sharing adds ~2 pp over the single-encoder variant (FEDDAG*), confirming the gain comes from genuine cross-cluster feature enrichment rather than extra parameters.

## Relation to Prior Work

FEDDAG extends the clustered federated learning (CFL) line of work by unifying two previously separate similarity paradigms—data-based (PACFL's principal-angle subspace comparison) and gradient-based (IFCA, CFL)—into a single weighted proximity matrix, with the data component refined to a class-wise, quantity-weighted variant. It departs from soft-clustering approaches (FedSoft, FedRC) that blend multiple cluster models into a noisy average, instead introducing a dual-encoder architecture where a secondary encoder is trained on complementary clusters' data via a directed CC-Graph, preserving cluster-specific specialization. The adaptive cluster-number determination via hierarchical clustering with a federated-aware compactness/degeneracy loss addresses a gap left by methods that require K to be fixed in advance (IFCA) or only support splitting but not merging (Sattler et al.).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FEDDAG is notable for being the first CFL method to explicitly handle all four common data-heterogeneity types (label skew, feature skew, concept shift, quantity shift) within a single framework, and the dual-encoder cross-cluster sharing mechanism offers a principled alternative to soft-clustering that avoids the noise-degradation tradeoff. The convergence analysis (Theorem A.1) provides a per-phase guarantee under stable clustering, but the analysis conditions on a fixed partition and does not bound the cost of re-clustering events, leaving open how the method behaves under sustained, gradual distribution drift. Practical limitations include the one-time SVD cost per client, the need to tune the CC-Graph sparsity (top-k), and the assumption that the initial one-shot clustering remains valid for the training horizon.
