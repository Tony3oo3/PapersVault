## Problem

Unimodal GCD methods overfit to spurious visual cues on Old classes, degrading New-class generalization, while existing multimodal approaches (TextGCD, GET) treat visual and textual modalities as independent inputs to separate classifiers and incur substantial computational overhead. The paper asks whether a single, unified cross-modal representation grounded in explicit semantic concepts can improve generalization while matching the efficiency of unimodal training.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260618/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes RGB images (labeled subset with known class names and unlabeled subset with hidden labels) plus a large task-agnostic text dictionary (~22K concepts from Tags or OpenImages-v7) as input. It produces cluster assignments for all unlabeled images into K = |Old ∪ New| categories, evaluated via clustering accuracy on Old, New, and All subsets. The student is a CLIP ViT-B/16 with only the last transformer block fine-tuned and a frozen text encoder; the teacher is a frozen CLIP ViT-H/14 used once for precomputing cross-modal representations.

## Method

SpectralGCD represents each image as a mixture over semantic concepts by computing CLIP cosine similarities between the image embedding and a concept dictionary, yielding a unified cross-modal representation that anchors the parametric classifier to explicit semantics rather than raw visual features. A Spectral Filtering step eigendecomposes the cross-modal covariance matrix of the teacher's softmaxed similarities to automatically retain only task-relevant concepts (via cumulative explained variance β_e and concept importance β_c), eliminating noise from the large agnostic dictionary. During training, forward and reverse knowledge distillation from the frozen teacher preserve the semantic structure of the student's cross-modal representation while it is jointly optimized with SimGCD-style supervised/unsupervised contrastive and classification losses. The text encoder remains frozen throughout, so the semantic basis is stable and no additional forward passes over text are needed after initial embedding extraction, keeping training cost comparable to unimodal baselines.

## Results

SpectralGCD achieves state-of-the-art All accuracy across all six benchmarks: 79.2% on CUB, 89.1% on Stanford Cars, 63.0% on FGVC-Aircraft, 98.5% on CIFAR-10, 86.1% on CIFAR-100, and 93.4% on ImageNet-100. Notably, it outperforms its own zero-shot teacher (ViT-H/14) on ImageNet-100 by +6.6 points and on CUB by +1.4 points, demonstrating that the small student generalizes better than the larger teacher with full class-name access. Training time on CUB is 194 s (preparation) + main training, substantially faster than GET (3121 s prep) and TextGCD (102 s prep), while remaining comparable to the unimodal SimGCD baseline.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SpectralGCD outperforms all eight baselines on every dataset. Against the strongest multimodal competitor TextGCD (using only Tags, denoted TextGCD*), SpectralGCD gains +9.3 All on CUB (79.2 vs 69.9), +2.9 on Stanford Cars (89.1 vs 86.2), +14.9 on FGVC-Aircraft (63.0 vs 48.1), and +6.8 on ImageNet-100 (93.4 vs 86.6). Against GET, the margins are +2.2 on CUB, +10.6 on Stanford Cars, +4.1 on Aircraft, and +1.7 on ImageNet-100. Among unimodal methods, SpectralGCD surpasses the best unimodal baseline (SelEx on CUB at 73.6, DebGCD on Aircraft at 61.7) by +5.6 and +1.3 respectively, while also closing the Old/New gap that plagues unimodal approaches (e.g., on Stanford Cars, SimGCD shows a 26.9-point Old–New gap vs. SpectralGCD's 5.2-point gap). Importantly, SpectralGCD uses only the Tags dictionary (~22K concepts) without the LLM-generated Attributes dictionary that TextGCD relies on, yet still achieves superior results. Training efficiency is also a clear advantage: SpectralGCD's 194 s preparation phase is far cheaper than GET's 3121 s inversion-network training, and total training time is comparable to unimodal SimGCD.

## Relation to Prior Work

SpectralGCD builds on the SimGCD training framework (contrastive + parametric classification losses) but departs fundamentally from prior multimodal GCD methods (TextGCD, GET, CLIP-GCD) by treating CLIP image-text cosine similarities as a single unified cross-modal representation rather than feeding separate visual and textual streams into independent or shared classifiers. Conceptually, it draws on probabilistic topic models (LDA) to frame images as mixtures over semantic concepts, and introduces Spectral Filtering—an eigendecomposition of the teacher's softmaxed cross-modal covariance—to automatically select task-relevant concepts from a large agnostic dictionary without LLM-generated descriptions or inversion networks. Forward and reverse knowledge distillation from a frozen teacher CLIP then preserves the semantic quality of the student's cross-modal representation during joint training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in achieving state-of-the-art GCD accuracy across six benchmarks at a computational cost comparable to unimodal SimGCD, whereas competing multimodal methods (GET, TextGCD) require substantially longer preparation and training phases—important for realistic deployments where discovery must be re-run as new unlabeled data arrives. The 'sufficient representation' framing via cross-modal concept similarities offers a principled explanation for why multimodal methods reduce Old/New overfitting, and the spectral filtering mechanism provides a reusable, task-agnostic concept selection tool. Key limitations include dependence on teacher quality and dictionary breadth, and the authors identify developing image-specific cross-modal representations (reducing reliance on a fixed dictionary) as the natural next step.
