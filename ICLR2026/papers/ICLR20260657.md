## Problem

Preference models used for RLHF reward modeling and LLM-as-judge evaluation exhibit systematic miscalibration, overrelying on superficial features like length, structure, jargon, sycophancy, and vagueness rather than substantive quality. While individual biases have been documented in isolation, the systematic connection between training data artifacts and model miscalibration across multiple bias dimensions remained unquantified, and no targeted post-training mitigation existed that could debias models without degrading overall performance.

## Input / Output

For reward modeling, the model takes a query Q and a single response R and outputs a scalar reward score s ∈ ℝ; for pairwise evaluation, it takes (Q, R₁, R₂) and outputs a binary preference. The evaluation protocol uses counterfactual pairs constructed via the RATE protocol, where a base response is perturbed to amplify exactly one bias feature (e.g., adding jargon) while a re-rewrite step corrects for unintended changes, ensuring the pair differs primarily in the target feature. Human ground truth is collected via majority vote over three independent annotators per pair.

## Method

The paper proposes Counterfactual Data Augmentation (CDA), a post-training method that synthesizes contrastive examples to explicitly penalize biased preferences. For each bias feature, they take existing preference pairs where neither response exhibits the target bias, then use GPT-4o rewrite prompts to generate a biased version of the rejected response, creating new training pairs of the form (Q, R_chosen ≻ R_rejected,p) where the perturbed response is explicitly dispreferred. These counterfactuals are combined with proportionally sampled supplementary examples from Chatbot Arena to guard against distribution shift, and the base reward model is fine-tuned using LoRA (r=16, α=32) with 8-bit quantization. The diagnostic side uses point-biserial correlations on both the counterfactual evaluation set and a 2500-example training subset to show that bias features are nearly three times more predictive of model preferences (r=+0.36) than human preferences (r=−0.12), confirming that models amplify subtle data artifacts into misaligned signals. Fine-tuning with CDA reduces average miscalibration from 39.4% to 32.5% and average absolute skew difference from 20.5% to 10.0% while preserving RewardBench performance.

## Results

Across five bias dimensions (length, structure, jargon, sycophancy, vagueness), base preference models exhibit >60% skew toward artificially biased responses and ~39.4% average miscalibration relative to human majority preferences, with vagueness and jargon exceeding 50% miscalibration. Counterfactual Data Augmentation (CDA) fine-tuning reduces average miscalibration from 39.4% to 32.5% and average absolute skew difference from 20.5% to 10.0%, with the largest gains for vagueness (−22.8% miscalibration) and jargon (−17.1%). Correlation analysis reveals that bias features are nearly 3× more predictive of model preferences (mean r_model = +0.36) than of human preferences (mean r_human = −0.12), confirming overreliance on spurious training-data cues. RewardBench scores remain essentially unchanged after CDA fine-tuning, indicating no quality trade-off.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not benchmark against other debiasing methods (e.g., ensembling, χ² regularization, or disentangled rewards); instead, it compares base Skywork-trained reward models (Gemma-2-2B, Gemma-2-27B, Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.2-3B) and three LLM evaluators (Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-4o, Claude-3.7-Sonnet) against human majority-vote preferences on counterfactual pairs, then evaluates the same models after CDA fine-tuning. CDA consistently reduces skew and miscalibration for jargon (skew −25.8%, miscal −17.0%) and vagueness (skew −29.8%, miscal −22.7%), with statistically significant 95% CIs excluding zero. For length, skew improves by 15.2% but miscalibration change (−3.4%) is not significant. Structure and sycophancy show slight miscalibration increases (+4.7% and +3.8% respectively), attributed to overcorrection of already-conservative base biases. Multi-bias CDA (pooling length, jargon, vagueness) achieves comparable per-bias improvements with only ~0.9% average RewardBench degradation, confirming that targeted debiasing is compatible with standard alignment pipelines.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work documenting individual preference model biases (length, sycophancy, style) by systematically connecting training data artifacts to model miscalibration across five bias dimensions simultaneously. It builds on the RATE protocol (Reber et al., 2025) for constructing controlled counterfactual response pairs that isolate a single bias feature, and adapts the counterfactual data augmentation paradigm from text classification (Kaushik et al., 2020) to the preference modeling setting. The key departure from prior work is the explicit correlation analysis linking skews in human-chosen vs. rejected training data to amplified model preferences, showing that subtle data imbalances (e.g., 65.5% preference for structured responses) become exaggerated model heuristics (r_model = +0.36 vs. r_human = −0.12).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Preference models are critical infrastructure for RLHF and LLM-as-judge evaluation, and the finding that they systematically amplify weak training data artifacts into strong misaligned signals (miscalibration ~40% overall, >50% for vagueness/jargon) has direct implications for the reliability of alignment pipelines and automated evaluation. The proposed CDA method is practically attractive because it requires only a few hundred synthesized counterfactual examples and standard LoRA fine-tuning, making it easy to integrate into existing reward model training without architectural changes. Open questions include whether these findings generalize to multi-turn dialogues (especially for sycophancy), whether the heuristic perturbations capture the full range of natural bias manifestations, and whether the modest improvements (~7% miscalibration reduction) compound when multiple biases are addressed jointly at scale.
