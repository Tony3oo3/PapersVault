## Problem

Efficient diffusion models (via quantization or feature caching) introduce approximation errors that accumulate exponentially across denoising timesteps, severely degrading generation quality. Once deployed in edge or production environments, these errors are difficult to correct because the original high-precision model is often irretrievable and the efficiency pipeline cannot be re-executed. No prior test-time method exists to mitigate these errors without retraining, re-running the efficiency pipeline, or modifying the model architecture.

## Input / Output

IEC operates entirely at inference time on an already-deployed efficient diffusion model, taking the same inputs as the base model (random Gaussian noise x_T ~ N(0,I), and optionally text prompts for text-to-image generation) and producing the same output (a generated image at the model's native resolution: 32×32 for DDIM on CIFAR-10, 256×256 for LDM-8/LDM-4/Stable Diffusion). The key operational detail is that IEC can be selectively applied to a subset of timesteps (e.g., only the first and last 1/10 or 1/20 of steps), allowing users to trade a small computational overhead (as low as 1.4%) for meaningful quality gains, while skipping IEC entirely preserves the original model behavior.

## Method

The paper first proves that in the DDIM sampling process, per-timestep approximation errors propagate via the recurrence δ_{t-1} = (A_t + B_t J_t) δ_t + B_t ε_θ^δ, and empirically shows the spectral norm ‖A_t + B_t J_t‖ consistently exceeds 1, causing exponential error accumulation. To break this exponential growth, IEC introduces an iterative fixed-point refinement at each timestep: starting from the standard DDIM estimate x_{t-1}^{(0)}, it repeatedly applies x_{t-1}^{(k+1)} = x_{t-1}^{(k)} + λ(A_t x_t + B_t ε_θ(x_{t-1}^{(k)}, t) − x_{t-1}^{(k)}), where λ ∈ [0.1, 0.7] ensures the mapping G(x) is a contraction (Lipschitz constant < 1) by Banach's fixed-point theorem. This confines the per-timestep error to a bounded value C/(1−L), reducing total error growth from exponential to linear across T timesteps. In practice, a single correction iteration (K=1) with threshold τ=1e-5 suffices, and IEC is plug-and-play: it requires no retraining, no architectural changes, and no access to the original full-precision model.

## Results

IEC consistently improves FID across all efficiency techniques and datasets. On CIFAR-10 with CacheQuant (W8A8, N=3), FID drops from 4.61 to 3.93; at N=15, from 13.42 to 10.77. On ImageNet (W8A8, N=10), FID improves from 4.68 to 4.15 and IS from 184.38 to 196.20. On MS-COCO (W8A8, N=5), FID improves from 23.74 to 22.83, IS from 39.81 to 40.91, and CLIP Score from 26.87 to 26.94. Notably, IEC with W8A8 quantization on CIFAR-10 (FID 3.76) even surpasses the full-precision DDIM baseline (FID 4.19).

## Baselines & Comparisons

IEC is evaluated as a plug-and-play add-on to three efficiency baselines: timestep-wise quantization (DilateQuant), DeepCache (feature caching), and CacheQuant (combined quantization + caching). Against DilateQuant, IEC reduces FID from 4.32 to 3.76 (W8A8) and 6.82 to 5.96 (W4A8) on CIFAR-10, and from 8.97 to 7.78 (W8A8) on LSUN-Bedrooms. Against DeepCache, IEC improves FID from 4.70 to 3.96 (N=3) and 17.21 to 14.58 (N=15) on CIFAR-10, and from 26.09 to 16.91 (N=10) on LSUN-Bedrooms. Against CacheQuant, IEC consistently reduces FID across all N values and both W8A8/W4A8 settings on all three datasets, with the largest absolute gain of 2.57 FID points (LSUN-Bedrooms, W8A8, N=10: 17.53→11.07). In every comparison, IEC wins on FID (and IS/CLIP Score where reported), and in several cases the corrected efficient model surpasses the full-precision baseline (e.g., CIFAR-10 W8A8+IEC FID 3.76 vs. DDIM 4.19; LSUN-Churchs W8A8+IEC FID 3.29 vs. LDM-8 3.99). IEC also outperforms naively adding extra iterations: at 100% overhead, IEC achieves FID 3.76 versus 3.99 for simply doubling the quantization iterations on CIFAR-10.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the DDIM deterministic sampling framework and positions itself at the intersection of two active research threads: structural efficiency techniques for diffusion models (post-training quantization, feature caching) and test-time scaling methods borrowed from the LLM literature. Prior efficiency-error mitigation work (timestep-wise quantization parameters, non-uniform caching strategies) operates at pre-deployment time and requires re-executing the model-efficiency pipeline with access to the original model. IEC departs from this by operating entirely at inference time on an already-deployed, immutable model, using a fixed-point iteration derived from the DDIM update rule to suppress error propagation without any retraining or architectural modification.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

IEC addresses a practical gap: once a quantized or cached diffusion model is deployed in edge or production environments, the original high-precision weights are often irretrievable, making standard re-calibration or re-quantization infeasible. The theoretical result that a single correction step converts exponential error growth to linear is clean and provides a principled justification for the method. Limitations include reliance on deterministic samplers (DDIM), first-order Taylor approximation in the error analysis, and the fact that IEC cannot recover from total mode collapse. Open questions include generalization to stochastic samplers, other efficiency techniques (token pruning, sparsity), and whether the linear error bound is tight in practice for very deep or high-capacity models.
