## Problem

Unified multimodal models (UMMs) are trained on image-text pairs where captions, even hundreds of words long, systematically omit fine-grained visual details such as spatial layout, geometry, and color attributes, creating a misalignment between the model's understanding and generation capabilities. This paper asks whether the model's own visual understanding encoder embeddings can serve as dense, caption-free supervision to close this gap.

## Input / Output

At training time, an input image is passed through the UMM's visual understanding encoder (CLIP, SigLIP, MAE, or InternVL3) at its minimum supported resolution (e.g., 224×224 for BAGEL, 336×336 for Show-o) to produce semantic embeddings, which are concatenated with a text template and fed to the UMM to reconstruct the original image. At inference time, the post-trained model operates identically to a standard UMM: a text prompt alone for generation, or a text prompt plus the original image for editing—no additional visual embeddings are required. The method is architecture-agnostic, supporting discrete token (MaskGIT), masked-autoregressive, and continuous diffusion/flow-matching generation heads.

## Method

RECA replaces conventional text-to-image supervision with a self-supervised semantic reconstruction loss: the UMM is conditioned on its own visual understanding encoder embeddings (concatenated with a diverse set of 360 prompt templates) and trained to regenerate the input image, using either cross-entropy (discrete/MAR) or diffusion/flow-matching loss (continuous). The understanding encoder is frozen (unless shared with generation, as in Harmon), and input images are resized to the encoder's minimum resolution to force the model to rely on semantic rather than pixel-level cues. The total loss combines the RECA reconstruction term with an optional image-to-text term (for shared-parameter models) while dropping the standard T2I term. Mechanistically, the paper shows that in deep transformer layers, visual and textual embeddings occupy the same semantic manifold, confirming that understanding-encoder features function as dense, language-aligned prompts. Empirically, RECA is most effective as a final refinement stage after SFT (SFT→RECA), and understanding-encoder embeddings (ViT/CLIP/SigLIP) consistently outperform generation-encoder embeddings (VAE/VQGAN) as conditioning signals.

## Results

Post-training Harmon-1.5B with RECA (27 A100 GPU hours, no GPT-4o distillation data) achieves GenEval 0.86 and DPGBench 87.21, surpassing all larger open-source UMMs including BAGEL (14B, 0.79/84.03), Janus-Pro (7B, 0.80/84.33), and GPT-4o-Image (0.84/86.23). With GPT-4o-Image data, RECA further improves to GenEval 0.90 and DPGBench 88.15. On image editing, RECA boosts BAGEL from ImgEdit 3.38→3.75 and GEdit 6.94→7.27, outperforming FLUX-Kontext (3.60/6.27) and BAGEL-NHR (3.48/7.08) while closing the gap to GPT-4o-Image (4.20/7.53).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RECA (1.5B) outperforms all generation-only baselines on GenEval: SD3-Medium (2B, 0.74), SANA-1.5 (4.8B, 0.81), FLUX-dev (12B, 0.68), Playground-v3 (24B, 0.76), and DALL-E 3 (0.67), with the largest margin over FLUX-dev (+0.18). Among UMMs, RECA surpasses BAGEL (14B, 0.79→0.86), Janus-Pro (7B, 0.80→0.86), Show-o2 (7B, 0.76), and GPT-4o-Image (0.84→0.86) on GenEval, and also leads on DPGBench (87.21 vs. 86.23 for GPT-4o-Image). With GPT-4o-Image distillation data, RECA (0.90/88.15) further exceeds Ovis-U1 (3.6B, 0.89/83.72), OmniGen2 (7B, 0.80/83.57), and BLIP3-o (8B, 0.83/80.73). On image editing, RECA-post-trained BAGEL beats FLUX-Kontext (ImgEdit 3.75 vs. 3.60; GEdit 7.27 vs. 6.27) and BAGEL-NHR (3.75 vs. 3.48; 7.27 vs. 7.08), but still trails GPT-4o-Image (4.20/7.53) on editing. The paper also notes that RECA's gains are robust to GenEval template leakage: removing 7,000 leaked pairs from BLIP3o-60k drops SFT by 4.07 on GenEval while RECA drops only 0.45.

## Relation to Prior Work

RECA repurposes a standard component of MLLMs—the visual understanding encoder (CLIP, SigLIP)—as a source of dense conditioning signals for the generation branch, rather than relying on sparse text captions. It explicitly departs from prior reconstruction-based methods (ViLex, ROSS, REPA, VA-VAE, Lumos), which use reconstruction to regularize vision encoders, preserve understanding details, or align diffusion-model hidden states with external features; RECA instead treats the image itself as a 'dense text prompt' and uses self-supervised reconstruction as a native post-training objective for the UMM's generation pathway. Among post-training strategies for UMMs, it occupies a distinct niche between SFT (which requires paired captions), RL/DPO (which requires preference data), and GPT-4o distillation (which incurs high data-generation cost), requiring only unlabeled images.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 1.5B-parameter UMM post-trained with RECA in 27 A100 GPU hours surpasses 14B–24B open-source models on GenEval and DPGBench without any distillation data or RL, offering a low-cost alignment recipe likely to be widely adopted. A key open question is whether RECA can improve reasoning-intensive generation (WISE scores show only modest gains), suggesting that semantic alignment and world-knowledge reasoning remain partially decoupled. The counterintuitive finding that lower-resolution (224×224) understanding embeddings outperform higher-resolution (512×512) ones—because higher resolution pushes the model toward low-level pixel cues rather than semantic features—opens a direction for understanding the optimal granularity of conditioning signals in unified models.
