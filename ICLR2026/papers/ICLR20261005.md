## Problem

Directly applying pre-trained Latent Diffusion Models to ultra-high-definition image restoration produces severe global inconsistencies, stripe artifacts, and loss of fine-grained details due to the necessity of patch-based inference, the absence of global context during denoising, and the VAE's lossy compression. No existing plug-and-play framework effectively adapts diffusion priors to the strict structural-fidelity requirements of UHD restoration, as generation-oriented high-resolution strategies (e.g., MultiDiffusion, DemoFusion) prioritize perceptual plausibility over input-output consistency.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single degraded UHD image (4K or 8K resolution) affected by low light, haze, blur, or rain, and produces a restored UHD image at the same resolution. During inference, the image is encoded by the VAE and processed as 512×512 latent patches with stride 256 through iterative denoising (50 steps), with overlapping regions blended by smooth averaging. The framework is plug-and-play and training-free with respect to the diffusion backbone; only the optional VAE-FT module requires supervised fine-tuning on paired degraded-clean data.

## Method

FreeAdapt's core is a training-free Frequency Feature Synergistic Guidance (FFSG) mechanism applied at every denoising step. Frequency Guidance (FreqG) transforms both the current latent and a noised reference latent into the frequency domain via FFT, then fuses only their phase spectra through a dynamically shrinking low-pass filter K(t) that gradually relaxes structural constraints as denoising progresses, preserving global color and structure without interfering with texture generation. Feature Guidance (FeatG) injects global context into the 3rd–8th U-Net decoder self-attention layers by linearly blending local patch attention with a global cross-attention computed against reference-image keys and values (α=0.2), suppressing hallucinated high-frequency details in smooth regions. An optional VAE-FT module fine-tunes the VAE decoder with LoRA (rank 16), AdaIN-refined skip connections from the degraded input, and Zero-Convolution injection to recover fine textures lost in VAE compression, supervised by a composite loss (wavelet L2 + LPIPS + SSIM + GAN).

## Results

On UHD-Haze, DiffBIR-Ours achieves the best PSNR (25.50 dB) and best perceptual scores (DISTS 0.028, LPIPS 0.077, MUSIQ 42.18) among all compared methods. On UHD-LL, diffusion-based variants trade ~5–7 dB PSNR relative to regression baselines (e.g., Wave-Mamba 29.84 dB) for substantially better perceptual quality (DISTS 0.092 vs. 0.117, MUSIQ 48.37 vs. 41.78). On UHD-Blur, DiffBIR-Ours attains the best LPIPS (0.145) and DISTS (0.059), outperforming ERR by ~29.6% on LPIPS. The FFSG mechanism alone yields +2.05 to +2.97 dB PSNR gains over naive patch-based inference across all three backbones, with VAE-FT contributing an additional +0.22 to +0.54 dB.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against regression-based UHD restoration methods (UHDformer, DreamUHD, ERR, Wave-Mamba, UHDFour, 4KDehazing, FFTformer), FreeAdapt-equipped diffusion models consistently win on perceptual metrics (DISTS, LPIPS, MUSIQ, MANIQA) but trail on full-reference PSNR/SSIM by 2–7 dB, which the authors attribute to L2-aligned training of regression baselines producing overly smoothed outputs. On UHD-Haze specifically, DiffBIR-Ours surpasses even the best regression method (ERR, 25.10 dB) in PSNR (25.50 dB) while also achieving the best DISTS (0.028 vs. 0.051) and MUSIQ (42.18 vs. 31.17). Among training-free diffusion adaptation baselines, MultiDiffusion, DemoFusion, and PixelSmith all underperform FreeAdapt on PSNR, LPIPS, and MUSIQ across all three backbones; DemoFusion is particularly weak on LPIPS (e.g., 0.417 vs. 0.253 for LDM-Ours on UHD-LL) and MUSIQ (23.09 vs. 49.07). On the 4K-Rain13k deraining benchmark, DiffBIR-Ours achieves the best LPIPS (0.111), DISTS (0.039), MUSIQ (34.98), and MANIQA (0.284), outperforming UDR-Mixer and UDR-S2Former on all perceptual metrics while trailing Uformer (34.67 dB) and ERR (34.43 dB) on PSNR. The ablation confirms that FreqG contributes the largest single gain (+2.85 dB PSNR over patch-based inference on UHD-LL), FeatG adds further LPIPS improvement (0.314→0.283), and VAE-FT provides an additional +0.33 dB PSNR and +0.030 LPIPS improvement.

## Relation to Prior Work

FreeAdapt sits at the intersection of three research threads: UHD image restoration (UHDformer, DreamUHD, ERR), training-free high-resolution diffusion adaptation (MultiDiffusion, DemoFusion, PixelSmith), and diffusion-prior-based restoration (StableSR, DiffBIR). It departs from the generation-oriented adaptation methods by explicitly targeting the stricter structural-fidelity requirement of restoration, introducing a phase-only frequency fusion and a global-context attention blending mechanism that are absent from prior high-resolution diffusion strategies. The VAE-FT module with skip connections and LoRA extends the standard LDM VAE (Rombach et al., 2022) by borrowing the Zero-Convolution injection pattern from ControlNet (Zhang et al., 2023), but repurposes it to recover high-frequency detail lost during perceptual compression rather than to add conditional control.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

FreeAdapt provides the first training-free, plug-and-play mechanism that consistently improves diffusion-based UHD restoration by >2 dB PSNR across three architecturally distinct backbones, making it a practical tool for adapting existing LDMs to ultra-high-resolution settings without retraining. The single unified VAE-FT decoder generalizing across tasks and backbones without retraining is a notable practical finding. Key open questions include whether the FFSG principles transfer to Diffusion Transformer backbones (the authors sketch a path but do not validate it), whether step distillation can reduce the ~3-minute inference latency to near-real-time, and how the method performs under real-world (non-synthetic) UHD degradations, which current benchmarks cannot capture.
