## Problem

Prior representation identifiability theory either requires strong assumptions on the data-generating process or only covers penultimate-layer representations mapped linearly to the loss, leaving intermediate-layer representations in models with nonlinear decoders (MAEs, GPTs, supervised classifiers) theoretically unaddressed. Additionally, the literature conflates two distinct notions of stability—consistency across independent trainings (statistical identifiability) and alignment with ground-truth latent factors (structural identifiability)—without formal definitions that accommodate the imperfect, near-identifiable regime realistic for modern models.

## Input / Output

The method consumes the latent representations (e.g., patch-level embeddings from a pretrained masked autoencoder or the latent tokens of a vanilla autoencoder) and produces a transformed latent space where independent components are separated via PCA whitening followed by linear ICA, yielding representations aligned up to signed permutations. In the biological application, input is a single patch-level embedding per cell image from OpenPhenom trained on Rxrx3-core, and output is a set of embeddings where biological perturbation signal is concentrated in a sparse subset of features, improving out-of-distribution AUROC for perturbation classification across held-out experimental plates.

## Method

The paper introduces formal definitions of statistical and structural ε-near-identifiability (Definitions 1–2) that relax exact identifiability to an error tolerance, then proves three key theorems. Theorem 1 shows that if a model's end-to-end output (g_θ ∘ f_θ) is statistically identifiable, then the intermediate encoder representations f_θ are ε-nearly identifiable up to rigid transformations, with ε governed by the local bi-Lipschitz constant of the decoder g_θ—extending identifiability beyond last-layer representations to arbitrary intermediate layers of MAEs, GPTs, and supervised learners. Theorem 2 proves that applying whitening and contrast-function-based ICA to these representations resolves the remaining rigid indeterminacy, yielding near-identifiability up to signed permutations with a controlled error bound. Theorem 3 extends statistical identifiability to structural identifiability under a bi-Lipschitz data-generating process assumption, showing that a reconstructing encoder-decoder model with a bi-Lipschitz decoder recovers the true latent factors up to signed permutations. The practical recipe is simply: train a vanilla autoencoder (with weight decay to control decoder Lipschitzness) or use a pretrained MAE, then apply PCA + ICA post-hoc to the latent space—achieving state-of-the-art disentanglement on synthetic benchmarks and batch-effect removal in cell microscopy without any specialized disentanglement regularization.

## Results

On the disentanglement benchmark (InfoMEC metrics), AE+ICA achieves the best aggregated modularity (InfoM = 0.65) and explicitness (InfoE = 0.83), outperforming all specialized disentanglement baselines. On Shapes3D, AE+ICA reaches InfoM = 0.79 and InfoE = 0.99, substantially exceeding β-VAE (0.59/0.99) and β-TCVAE (0.61/0.82). On the foundation-model-scale Rxrx3-core cell microscopy task, applying ICA to OpenPhenom MAE embeddings improves out-of-distribution perturbation classification AUROC by up to +0.067 (e.g., EIF3H: 0.682 → 0.749) and increases concentration of biological signal in top features from 0.163 to 0.386.

## Baselines & Comparisons

AE+ICA outperforms all three specialized disentanglement baselines on the two most important InfoMEC metrics (modularity and explicitness) in the aggregated setting: InfoM 0.65 vs. β-VAE 0.59, β-TCVAE 0.58, BioAE 0.54; InfoE 0.83 vs. 0.81, 0.72, 0.75. The largest margin is on Shapes3D, where AE+ICA achieves InfoM = 0.79 versus 0.59 (β-VAE), 0.61 (β-TCVAE), and 0.56 (BioAE) — a 0.20+ gap over the next-best method. AE+ICA loses on MPI3D modularity (0.44 vs. β-TCVAE's 0.51) and on compactness (InfoC) across most datasets, where β-TCVAE and β-VAE achieve 0.55–0.71 versus AE+ICA's 0.31–0.52, reflecting that ICA does not explicitly encourage compactness. On the real-world Rxrx3-core task, ICA post-processing of the OpenPhenom MAE consistently improves out-of-distribution AUROC over both the raw embedding and PCA-only whitening across all six tested genes, with the largest gain on EIF3H (+0.067 AUROC), confirming that the linear indeterminacy resolved by ICA corresponds to meaningful separation of biological from technical variation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies and extends two distinct lines of identifiability theory: the linear identifiability of penultimate-layer representations in exponential family models (Roeder et al., 2021) and the isometry-based structural identifiability of contrastive learning (Zimmermann et al., 2021; Horan et al., 2021). The key departure is shifting the burden from strong assumptions on the data-generating process (isometry, independence of factors) to a mild bi-Lipschitz condition on the model's decoder, enabling the first general-purpose near-identifiability result for intermediate-layer representations mapped nonlinearly to the loss. The ICA post-processing step generalizes Horan et al.'s isometric-learning-plus-ICA recipe and parallels Buchholz & Schölkopf's robustness analysis for nonlinear ICA, but applies to the broader class of models covered by the new Theorem 1.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical recipe of vanilla autoencoder plus linear ICA achieving state-of-the-art disentanglement on synthetic benchmarks without specialized architectures or extensive tuning is a useful and simple tool for practitioners. The real-world application to cell microscopy (OpenPhenom/Rxrx3-core) demonstrates that this approach can improve out-of-distribution generalization by disentangling batch effects from biological variation, a key unsolved problem in computational biology. The primary limitation is that the bi-Lipschitz condition on the decoder is difficult to verify empirically, and the theory assumes infinite data and perfect optimization; extending to finite-sample and imperfect-optimization regimes remains open. The framework also opens a path toward understanding cross-model representation convergence, which existing identifiability theory cannot address because each prior result requires different data-generating assumptions.
