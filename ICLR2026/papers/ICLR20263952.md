## Problem

Existing reward model (RM) benchmarks evaluate general capabilities but lack culturally relevant evaluation data, making it impossible to assess whether RMs genuinely understand cultural nuances or merely exploit surface-level features. This gap hinders the global alignment of LLMs, as RMs serve as the critical proxy for human preferences across cultures in RLHF pipelines.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263952/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The CARB benchmark uses a Best-of-N (N=4) paradigm where the RM receives a culturally grounded prompt and four candidate responses, and must identify the single culturally optimal one. The Think-as-Locals method extends this by requiring the generative RM to first produce a structured sequence of cultural evaluation criteria (z) before emitting the final preference judgment (ĵ), with the full output being the concatenated reasoning-and-judgment token sequence. The appropriateness reward operates on the per-token log probabilities of this sequence, measuring how much the generated criteria causally increase the model's likelihood of producing the correct ground-truth judgment.

## Method

Think-as-Locals elicits culturally grounded reasoning from generative RMs by forcing them to generate structured cultural evaluation criteria before rendering a preference judgment, thereby preventing shortcut exploitation of spurious features. Training uses GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) where the generative RM acts as the policy model, sampling groups of reasoning-judgment sequences per query. A novel two-component reward function drives optimization: a correctness reward (R_corr) that gives +1/−1 for matching the ground-truth judgment, and an appropriateness reward (R_appr) that computes the 'net probability boost' of the generated criteria by subtracting the log probability of decoding the ground-truth judgment without intermediate reasoning from the log probability with it. This isolates the causal contribution of the cultural reasoning from the model's baseline preference, rewarding criteria that meaningfully support correct cultural judgment while penalizing irrelevant or misleading reasoning.

## Results

On the proposed CARB benchmark (8,576 Best-of-N sets across 10 cultures and 4 domains), Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct-2507 achieves the highest average score of 76.5%, with generative RMs occupying 7 of the top 10 positions. The proposed Think-as-Locals method (Qwen2.5-32B-Inst) achieves 84.3% on CARB and 89.5% on M-RewardBench (avg 86.9%), surpassing the best classifier-based RM (Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2-27B, avg 81.4%) by 5.5 points and the best reasoning RM (mR3-14B, avg 75.8%) by 11.1 points. CARB scores show a strong, statistically significant positive correlation with downstream cultural alignment performance (r² > 0.6, p < 0.001), whereas M-RewardBench shows a weak, insignificant relationship (r² < 0.1, p > 0.05).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Think-as-Locals (Qwen2.5-32B) achieves the highest CARB score (84.3%) among all compared models, outperforming the strongest generative RM Qwen3-235B-Instruct (76.0%) by 8.3 points and the top classifier-based RM Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2-27B (72.6%) by 11.7 points. Against reasoning RMs, the advantage is even more pronounced: mR3-14B scores only 59.8% on CARB (24.5 points lower), and RM-R1-Qwen-Inst-7B† (retrained with the same data) reaches 75.5%, still 8.8 points behind. Even the smallest Think-as-Locals variant (7B, CARB 78.8%) surpasses all classifier-based and reasoning RMs except Qwen3-235B. On M-RewardBench, the 32B model (89.5%) trails Qwen3-235B (92.3%) and mR3-14B (91.8%) by 2.8 and 2.3 points respectively, indicating that the method's primary gains are in cultural awareness rather than general reward modeling. The paper also validates downstream impact: DPO alignment using Think-as-Locals-labeled preferences yields 18.1% on X-AlpacaEval and 6.46 on OMGEval, versus 14.6% and 5.87 for the Qwen2.5-7B baseline.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the reward model evaluation landscape beyond general-purpose benchmarks (RewardBench, RMB, M-RewardBench) by introducing the first culture-specific RM benchmark (CARB) covering 10 cultures across 4 domains. Methodologically, it builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) as the training algorithm and adapts the RLVR framework (Guo et al., 2025a) with a novel appropriateness reward that leverages intrinsic token probabilities (Wen et al., 2025) to measure the causal contribution of generated cultural reasoning criteria. The key conceptual departure from prior generative RMs is the structured criteria rollout: rather than free-form chain-of-thought, the model must first produce explicit cultural evaluation criteria before rendering a judgment, a design motivated by the spurious correlation analysis showing that unstructured reasoning allows shortcut exploitation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CARB fills a critical gap in RM evaluation by providing the first systematic assessment of cultural awareness, and its strong correlation with downstream multilingual alignment (r² > 0.6) validates it as a practical proxy for expensive end-to-end alignment experiments. The spurious correlation finding—that even top-performing RMs are more sensitive to surface features (language, explicit cultural labels) than to core cultural concepts—raises important questions about whether current RMs genuinely understand cultural nuance or merely pattern-match, with implications for reward hacking in multilingual deployment. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes beyond the 10 tested cultures, whether the structured criteria mechanism transfers to other domains of cultural sensitivity, and whether the spurious correlation issue is specific to cultural content or reflects a broader limitation of current RM architectures.
