## Problem

Standard RLVR cannot provide verification signals at test time when ground-truth answers are unavailable. Prior approaches to self-verification either require training a separate external verifier LLM or jointly optimize reasoning and verification by generating solutions and self-verifications sequentially under two distinct prompt templates, doubling per-sample inference cost. The paper asks whether self-verification can be embedded into the policy model at near-zero additional computational cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264851/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a math problem as a text prompt and produces a reasoning solution (chain-of-thought text) followed by a scalar self-rewarding score in [0,1]. The score is computed from the policy model's next-token log-probability for a pre-specified unused special token (e.g., '<vision_start>') at the final solution token, minus a pre-calculated reference constant, scaled by the KL coefficient β_v. This requires at most one additional forward pass after solution generation—no separate verification sequence is produced. At test time, the score can be thresholded at 0.5 for binary verification or used directly as a weight in weighted majority voting over multiple sampled solutions.

## Method

The key theoretical insight is that the closed-form solution to the RL objective of self-verification reduces to: the true verifier-based reasoning reward equals β_v · log[π_θ(z_c|x,y) / π_ref(z_c|x,y)], where z_c is a pre-specified special token evaluated at the last response token. This reduction holds because the partition function Z(x,y) ≈ 1, since the reference model assigns near-zero probability (≈ e^{-20} or less) to any special token after solution completion. Practically, the reference log-probability is further simplified to a pre-calculated constant c_ref, so the self-rewarding score becomes r_s = β_v · log π_θ(z_c|x,y) − β_v · c_ref. The training algorithm augments standard GRPO with a simple MSE loss between r_s and the binary verifier reward r_v, plus class-level loss re-weighting to balance correct/incorrect samples. After a self-rewarding warm-up phase, the self-rewarding scores are integrated into the GRPO advantage as a convex combination (weight τ) with the verifier-based advantage, providing finer-grained learning signals. The entire procedure adds no extra generation steps—only one additional token log-probability lookup per sample.

## Results

Across three base models (OctoThinker-3B, Qwen2.5-7B-Base, Open-Reasoner-Zero-7B), LaSeR consistently improves both reasoning accuracy and self-verification F1 over standard GRPO. On Qwen2.5-7B-Base, LaSeR achieves 42.7% average reasoning accuracy (vs. 41.8% for GRPO) and 79.6% average self-verification F1 (vs. 49.2% for GRPO). On Open-Reasoner-Zero-7B, LaSeR reaches 45.5% average reasoning accuracy and 77.6% self-verification F1, compared to 45.0% and 38.8% for GRPO. The self-rewarding scores also enable weighted majority voting (RM@K) to outperform unweighted majority voting (Maj@K) at inference time, improving test-time scaling.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the primary RLVR baseline GRPO (Shao et al., 2024), LaSeR improves average reasoning accuracy by 1.0–2.0 points across all three base models while dramatically boosting self-verification F1 by 21–41 points (e.g., 49.2→79.6 on Qwen2.5-7B-Base; 38.8→77.6 on ORZ-7B). Against external verifiers, LaSeR's 7B self-rewarding achieves 77.6% average F1 on ORZ-7B-generated solutions, matching the 72B Qwen2.5-Math-RM (76.8%) and exceeding both 7B PRMs (Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-7B at 75.9%, Qwen2.5-Math-7B-PRM800K at 47.9%), all without requiring a separate model or additional inference pass. Against PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) as an alternative RL backbone, LaSeR^PPO achieves 44.6% average reasoning accuracy and 80.3% self-verification F1 on ORZ-7B, substantially outperforming PPO alone (43.8% reasoning, 33.2% F1), confirming the method's generality across RL algorithms. The ablation variant without self-rewarding-based advantages (−SWA) shows that the advantage integration contributes modestly to reasoning gains but is critical for maintaining balanced self-verification across benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

LaSeR builds directly on the RLVR paradigm (GRPO, PPO) and the implicit-reward closed-form solution from DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023), extending it from the full-sequence level to a single last-token log-probability ratio. It departs from prior joint reasoning-verification methods (Sareen et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025a; Zha et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025) that require sequential generation of solutions and verifications under separate prompts, by showing that the verification signal is already encoded in the policy model's next-token distribution at the final solution token. The key theoretical departure is the observation that the partition function Z(x,y) in the verification RL objective is approximately 1 (because the reference model assigns near-zero probability to any pre-specified token after solution completion), collapsing the closed-form solution to a simple MSE loss on a single log-probability.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: LaSeR adds at most one token of inference cost to obtain a self-rewarding score that achieves ~80% F1, matching a 72B external reward model, while being trivially integrable into any existing RLVR pipeline. This makes test-time self-verification and weighted majority voting nearly free, which is important for deployment where generating full verification sequences is prohibitively expensive. Limitations include that the advantage-integration component (SWA) sometimes degrades reasoning accuracy, the method is primarily validated on math reasoning (general reasoning shows lower self-verification F1 of 65–70%), and the constant approximation c_ref, while empirically stable, has not been formally bounded. Open questions include whether the last-token signal generalizes to non-math domains with weaker verifiers, and whether the self-rewarding score can bootstrap test-time RL without rule-based rewards (the paper shows this collapses after ~60 steps).
