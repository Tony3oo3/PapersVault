## Problem

Direct LLM reasoning over long, noisy, multi-document contexts is brittle, producing omissions, hallucinations, and format drift that are unacceptable in high-stakes domains like finance and legal. While generating query-specific structured data (tables, graphs, chunks) before answering improves reliability, doing so with large LLMs incurs prohibitive latency and cost. The paper tackles how to distill LLM-quality structured-output generation into compact 3B/7B models that run on-premises at 2–4× lower latency.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264189/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The system takes a natural-language question Q and a collection of long, noisy, multi-source documents D (ranging from 10K to 250K tokens) and produces two artifacts: (i) an auditable CoST reasoning trace documenting schema selection, evidence alignment, normalization, and verification steps, and (ii) a query-specific serialized structured output (SSO) — a table, graph, or set of text chunks — with provenance links to source spans. The schema is induced dynamically per query rather than fixed, and the final answer A is computed from S by a separate reasoning call (GPT-4o in evaluation), making the pipeline a 2-hop extraction-then-reasoning system.

## Method

LITECOST is a two-pillar framework. Pillar 1 (CoST) uses a strong LLM (GPT-4o) once per sample to execute a four-step structure-first template: dynamic structure selection and schema construction, step-wise trace generation guided by the schema, LLM-as-Judge quality verification against ground-truth answers, and iterative refinement of low-quality samples via recursive supplemental extraction. Pillar 2 transfers this behavior to SLMs through a two-phase recipe: LoRA-based SFT (3 epochs, rank 16) to instill schema/format/step discipline, followed by GRPO reinforcement learning with a dual-level reward — an outcome reward combining format compliance (hierarchical soft/hard scoring of reasoning and answer tags) and answer correctness (hybrid structural-alignment + GPT-4o-mini semantic-similarity score), plus a process reward that scores each reasoning step for entity- and tuple-level consistency against ground truth via LLM-based checking. A trajectory-level scaling factor modulates the process reward to reinforce correct reasoning and penalize overthought or incorrect trajectories, yielding 3B/7B models that match or exceed GPT-4o on the Loong finance benchmark at 2–4× lower inference latency.

## Results

On the Finance subset of the Loong benchmark, LITECOST-tuned SLMs achieve substantial gains over their base models: LLaMA-3.2-3B improves from 49.37 to 76.95 Average Score (AS) and 0.11 to 0.40 Perfect Rate (PR), while Qwen2-7B improves from 62.10 to 79.93 AS and 0.26 to 0.48 PR. The 7B Qwen-LiteCoST model surpasses GPT-4o (79.32 AS) by 0.61 points, DeepSeek-R1 (78.18) by 1.75, and GPT-4o-mini (78.08) by 1.85, while the 3B LLaMA-LiteCoST exceeds Qwen2.5-14B (75.60) by 1.35 AS. Inference latency is 2× lower than GPT-4o (12.09s vs. 21.15s) and 4× lower than DeepSeek-R1 (12.09s vs. 44.44s). On LongBench, Qwen-LiteCoST achieves the best F1 on NarrativeQA (30.40), Qasper (44.64), and HotpotQA (68.39), surpassing GPT-4o by 0.71–1.72 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against large proprietary LLMs, Qwen-LiteCoST (7B) achieves the best overall AS (79.93) among all evaluated models, edging out GPT-4o (79.32) by 0.61 and DeepSeek-R1 (78.18) by 1.75, while LLaMA-LiteCoST (3B) at 76.95 surpasses Qwen2.5-14B (75.60) and GPT-4o-mini (78.08) in PR (0.40 vs. 0.38 and 0.51 respectively, though GPT-4o-mini leads in PR). Against fine-tuned IE baselines on the same backbones (Table 2), LITECOST dominates: on LLaMA-3B it outperforms ODIE (61.21), IEPile (61.90), Struc-bench (49.90), and StructRAG (36.04) by 15.74–40.91 AS; on Qwen-7B it beats ODIE (72.86), IEPile (69.19), Struc-bench (73.72), and StructRAG (49.68) by 6.21–30.25 AS. Against prompting baselines (Table 6), CoST consistently outperforms Zero-Shot and CoT on both GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-14B, with overall AS of 82.39 vs. 79.32 (Zero-Shot) and 80.08 (CoT) on GPT-4o. On LongBench (Table 5b), Qwen-LiteCoST surpasses GPT-4o on NarrativeQA (+1.72), Qasper (+1.25), and HotpotQA (+0.71) F1, while trailing slightly on 2WikiMultihopQA (65.73 vs. 68.29). In the legal domain (Table 4), LLaMA-LiteCoST (45.45 AS) outperforms GPT-4o (42.06), GPT-4o-mini (41.94), and Qwen2.5-14B (37.51), confirming cross-domain generalization.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends Chain-of-Thought prompting (Wei et al., 2022) into a structure-first paradigm (CoST) where the reasoning trace is explicitly schema-aligned and the terminal output is a machine-checkable serialized structured artifact (table, graph, or chunks) rather than free-form text. For the RL stage, it builds on GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) but introduces a dual-level reward combining outcome correctness with a step-wise process consistency signal, going beyond the single outcome reward used in prior RL-for-IE work. It also departs from modular structured-extraction frameworks like StructRAG and fine-tuned IE models (ODIE, IEPile, Struc-bench) by unifying structure selection, trace generation, verification, and iterative refinement into a single auditable pipeline, then distilling the entire procedure into compact SLMs via a two-phase SFT→GRPO recipe.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for on-premises and latency-sensitive deployments: a 7B model matches or slightly exceeds GPT-4o on structured-output generation for long-document QA while running 2–4× faster, and the total training cost is roughly $40. However, the paper acknowledges that structured representations can lose fine-grained contextual cues (hurting Spotlight Locating and multi-step Chain-of-Reasoning), and generalization beyond finance, legal, and scientific QA remains only partially validated. Open questions include whether the CoST paradigm extends to genuinely open-ended narrative questions that resist structured representation, and whether the dual-reward GRPO recipe transfers to other long-context reasoning tasks beyond extraction.
