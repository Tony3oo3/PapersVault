## Problem

Existing drag-based image editing methods (e.g., FastDrag, RegionDrag) construct displacement fields purely in the 2D pixel plane, ignoring underlying 3D scene geometry. This causes structural artifacts and unnatural deformations in geometry-intensive edits such as rotations and perspective shifts. Additionally, geometry-only guidance produces discontinuous displacements near object boundaries, and multiple handle-target pairs with opposing directions cause destructive interference that leads to editing failure.

## Input / Output

Given an RGB image, a set of k handle-target point pairs, a binary editing mask, and a monocular depth map (predicted by Depth Anything V2), GeoDrag computes a dense 2-channel displacement field in the noisy latent space at a fixed diffusion timestep (t=10). The warped latent is then decoded through a single LCM forward pass to produce the edited image. No per-image training or LoRA tuning is required; the method is entirely train-free and operates on a pretrained Stable Diffusion 1.5 backbone with an LCM-accelerated U-Net.

## Method

GeoDrag constructs a hybrid displacement field by fusing two complementary components: (1) a geometry-aware field that scales displacement strength by the ratio of handle-point depth to per-pixel depth raised to a power α (Eq. 7), derived from 3D pinhole-camera projection so that nearer pixels undergo larger 2D shifts; and (2) a plane-aware field that decays displacement with Euclidean distance from the handle point within a circular propagation region (Eq. 8). The two fields are combined via a spatially adaptive weight λ = P/(P+γ) that favors geometry globally and local 2D control near the handle. For multi-point editing, a Voronoi-like partition assigns each mask pixel exclusively to its nearest handle, computing independent sub-fields per region to eliminate directional conflicts. The final field warps the inverted latent in one step, followed by a masked stochastic DDIM update that injects noise only within the interpolated region to avoid over-smoothing.

## Results

On DRAGBENCH, GeoDrag achieves the best editing precision with a Mean Distance (MD) of 29.24 and Dragging Accuracy Index (DAI₁) of 0.128, outperforming the closest one-step baseline FastDrag (MD 32.10, DAI₁ 0.131) by ~9% and ~2.3% respectively. GeoDrag also attains the lowest DAI₂₀ (0.111) and lowest peak GPU memory (5.44 GB) among all compared methods. In terms of Image Fidelity (IF), GeoDrag scores 0.847, slightly below CLIPDrag (0.891) and DragDiffusion (0.871), but competitive with FastDrag (0.850). Average per-point editing time is 3.95 s, comparable to FastDrag (3.23 s) and dramatically faster than iterative methods (21–46 s).

## Baselines & Comparisons

GeoDrag outperforms all six baselines on every precision metric (MD, DAI₁, DAI₁₀, DAI₂₀). Against the closest one-step competitor FastDrag, GeoDrag reduces MD from 32.10 to 29.24 (−9%) and DAI₁ from 0.131 to 0.128 (−2.3%), while using less GPU memory (5.44 vs. 5.85 GB) at a modest 0.7 s per-point time cost. Compared to iterative diffusion-based methods (DragDiffusion, FreeDrag, DragNoise, CLIPDrag, AdaptiveDrag), GeoDrag improves DAI₁ by 1.4× (0.181→0.128 vs. DragDiffusion) and MD by 1.1× (34.57→29.24), while running 5–12× faster and consuming roughly one-third the memory. The only metric where GeoDrag trails is Image Fidelity (IF): it scores 0.847 versus CLIPDrag's 0.891 and DragDiffusion's 0.871, though it remains within 0.003 of FastDrag (0.850). In the user study (60 participants, 10 images), GeoDrag received the highest average ranking, confirming that its precision gains translate to perceived editing quality.

## Relation to Prior Work

GeoDrag extends the one-step latent-relocation paradigm introduced by FastDrag and RegionDrag, which warp a noisy latent via a dense 2D displacement field to achieve fast drag-based editing without iterative optimization. The key departure is the incorporation of monocular depth (via Depth Anything V2) to modulate displacement strength according to 3D geometric relationships, complemented by a 2D spatial decay term and a Voronoi-like partitioning for multi-point conflicts. The backbone is a Latent Consistency Model (LCM) for few-step inference, and the overall pipeline follows the broader drag-editing lineage from DragGAN through DragDiffusion to recent efficiency-focused methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a concrete and visually apparent failure mode of 2D-only drag methods—structural distortion under rotations and perspective shifts—by grounding the displacement field in 3D geometry, yielding a ~9% MD improvement over FastDrag with no preparation overhead. However, the method remains dependent on monocular depth estimation quality (specular, textureless, and strong-perspective scenes degrade performance), and it cannot synthesize truly unseen content, limiting its applicability to deformation-centric edits. Open questions include whether the geometry-aware formulation generalizes to multi-view or 3D-consistent editing, and whether the depth dependency can be replaced by learned geometric priors to reduce sensitivity to depth-map errors.
