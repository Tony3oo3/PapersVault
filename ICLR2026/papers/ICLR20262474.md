## Problem

Diffusion large language models (dLLMs) generate text through iterative denoising but discard all intermediate predictions, relying solely on the final step's output. The paper identifies a critical phenomenon—temporal oscillation—where correct answers frequently emerge at intermediate denoising steps but are overwritten in later iterations, revealing that current decoding strategies waste rich temporal signal. No existing method systematically exploits this temporal consistency to improve dLLM outputs.

## Input / Output

The method operates on text prompts (math word problems, QA questions) and produces a final text answer. During semi-autoregressive decoding with block size 32, each denoising step yields a fully decoded intermediate sequence before remasking; answers are extracted by parsing <answer>...</answer> spans from these pre-remask sequences. Only steps containing valid parsed answers contribute to temporal voting or semantic clustering, and for TSE computation, only the second half of sampling steps are used to avoid unreliable early predictions.

## Method

The paper introduces two complementary methods exploiting temporal dynamics. First, Temporal Self-Consistency Voting is a training-free decoding strategy that aggregates intermediate predictions across all T denoising steps via weighted majority voting, with exponential weighting f(t)=exp(α(1−t/T)) prioritizing later steps. Second, Temporal Consistency Reinforcement uses GRPO with a self-supervised reward based on Temporal Semantic Entropy (TSE), which clusters intermediate answers by semantic equivalence and computes Shannon entropy over the cluster distribution—lower TSE indicates more stable generation. When ground-truth labels are available, TSE is combined with a binary accuracy reward via spherical scoring to produce a composite reward that encourages both correctness and temporal stability. The approach requires no additional forward passes beyond the single sampling trajectory, making the voting overhead approximately 1% per sample.

## Results

Using the negative TSE reward alone (no ground-truth labels), the method achieves an average 24.7% improvement on Countdown over the LLaDA-8B-Instruct SFT baseline, and matches or surpasses the d1 accuracy-reward baseline across all four math benchmarks. When combined with the accuracy reward, absolute gains reach 2.0% on GSM8K, 4.3% on MATH500, 6.6% on SVAMP, and 25.3% on Countdown (length 128). The training-free Temporal Self-Consistency Voting adds an average +1.5% with negligible (~1%) runtime overhead. On natural-language QA (ARC-C, Winogrande), the combined reward yields up to +12.4 points on Winogrande at 256 tokens.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The sole external baseline is d1 (Zhao et al., 2025), which applies GRPO with a binary accuracy reward requiring ground-truth labels. On Countdown, the negative TSE reward alone (no labels) yields a 24.7% average improvement over the SFT baseline, substantially outperforming d1's 15.1% gain. On GSM8K and MATH500, the TSE-only reward is competitive with or slightly below d1 (e.g., 72.2 vs. 71.7 on GSM8K-128; 30.6 vs. 31.0 on MATH500-128), but the combined TSE+accuracy reward surpasses d1 by 0.9%, 0.2%, 1.7%, and 10.2% on GSM8K, MATH500, SVAMP, and Countdown respectively. The key advantage is that the TSE component provides a continuous, unsupervised signal that complements the binary accuracy reward, enabling gains even in settings where labels are unavailable. On the harder Sudoku task where intermediate predictions are consistently wrong (<5% temporal accuracy), both methods degrade, highlighting a shared limitation with d1.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends three distinct lines of work into the diffusion language model setting: (1) self-consistency decoding (Wang et al., 2022), which votes over multiple AR samples, is repurposed here to vote over intermediate predictions within a single diffusion trajectory; (2) semantic entropy (Farquhar et al., 2024), used for hallucination detection in AR models, is generalized to Temporal Semantic Entropy (TSE) that measures semantic stability across denoising steps; and (3) GRPO-based RL for dLLMs (d1/diffu-GRPO, Zhao et al., 2025), which relies on ground-truth accuracy rewards, is augmented with an unsupervised TSE-based reward that requires no labels. The key conceptual departure is treating intermediate denoising predictions as a signal rather than discarding them, and using temporal consistency as a self-supervised training objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The identification of temporal oscillation with a concrete 12% gap between ever-pass and final-pass rates provides a clear diagnostic for dLLM practitioners, and the TSE reward offers a label-free training signal that could be broadly applied to any masked-diffusion LM. However, the method's effectiveness depends on the model already producing correct answers at some intermediate step (as shown by the Sudoku failure case), limiting applicability to tasks where the base model is already reasonably competent. Open questions include whether the ever-pass gap can be fully closed, whether TSE generalizes to open-ended generation beyond math/QA, and how to combine TSE with pairwise-agreement metrics for a more complete temporal-consistency signal.
