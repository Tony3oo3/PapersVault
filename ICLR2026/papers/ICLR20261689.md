## Problem

Standard confidence evaluation metrics each capture only a partial view of model uncertainty: calibration metrics (e.g., ECE) compare predicted probabilities to observed frequencies on average but miss within-bin heterogeneity of errors (grouping loss), while proper scoring rules assess overall uncertainty without quantifying the residual epistemic error. This paper addresses the gap by providing sample-efficient, asymptotically consistent estimators of grouping loss and per-sample excess decision risk that isolate the epistemic component of a model's error—i.e., where the model could in principle improve—enabling both interpretable confidence audits and principled decision deferral.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a probabilistic classifier's confidence scores f(X), binary labels Y, input features (original tabular columns or learned embeddings such as last-hidden-layer activations or UMAP projections for neural/LLM models), and a 2×2 cost matrix Λ. Data is split into calibration (10%), fitting (40%), and evaluation (50%) subsets; Platt scaling is fit on the calibration set to produce c∘f, and the honest regression tree is fit on residuals Y − c∘f(X) from the fitting set. Outputs include a global grouping loss estimate, a per-sample epistemic decision risk value, the learned partition tree (whose leaves define interpretable over/under-confident subgroups), and, in the cascade setting, a routing decision selecting which model in a pool should answer a given query.

## Method

The core mechanism replaces confidence-score binning with a partition of the input space constructed by an honest regression tree (Wager & Athey, 2017) fit on post-calibration residuals Y − c∘f(X), where tree structure and leaf values are estimated on disjoint data subsets to avoid overfitting. The grouping loss estimator (Prop. 3) is a debiased sum over partition regions of p̂ⱼ(r̂ⱼ² − v̂ⱼ/nⱼ), providing a lower bound that converges to the true grouping loss under standard partitioning consistency conditions (vanishing leaf diameter, growing samples per leaf, J/√n → 0). The pointwise excess decision risk estimator (Prop. 6) approximates the unknown posterior f*(X) by c∘f(X) + r̂(X) and computes the cost of the decision disagreement between the model's threshold t and the optimal threshold t*, yielding a per-sample risk bounded by |Λ_Δ|·|r(X) − r̂ⱼ|. This per-sample granularity enables LLM cascades that defer only high-epistemic-risk predictions to stronger models, outperforming both confidence-threshold cascades and predictive routers in accuracy-at-cost tradeoffs across 120 cascade configurations.

## Results

On the semi-synthetic Weather dataset, the proposed grouping loss estimator converges to the true value from below and captures more grouping loss than the binning-based baseline at every sample size. Across 27 LLMs on 5 Folktexts datasets, grouping loss decreases with model scale and instruction tuning, with substantial residual biases persisting even in 70B models. For LLM cascades on ACSIncome, the epistemic-risk-based cascade achieves +4% accuracy over Llama 70B at ~30% of its cost; averaged over 5 datasets and 120 cascade pools, it yields a 6% accuracy gain at 46% of the cost of the largest model, with gains up to 15% on high-grouping-loss datasets like ACSEmployment.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the binning-based grouping loss estimator of Perez-Lebel et al. (2023), the proposed partition-based estimator is strictly more sample-efficient: on the semi-synthetic Weather experiment it captures a larger fraction of the true grouping loss at every sample size and converges faster to the ground-truth value. Against the predictive router baseline of Hu et al. (2024) (RouterBench), the epistemic-risk cascade achieves strict Pareto optimality (higher accuracy at lower cost) in 60% of the 120 evaluated cascade pools and systematically outperforms the router by up to 2% accuracy at any given cost. A calibration-risk-only cascade (using R̂^CL instead of the full R̂) serves as an internal ablation: it provides a useful but weaker deferral signal, and the full epistemic risk yields additional gains of up to 15% on datasets with high grouping loss (e.g., ACSEmployment), confirming that the grouping component is essential for effective deferral.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the proper scoring rule decomposition framework of Kull & Flach (2015) and the binning-based grouping loss estimator of Perez-Lebel et al. (2023) by replacing confidence-score binning with honest regression tree partitions, yielding asymptotically consistent and more sample-efficient estimators. It further builds on Perez-Lebel et al. (2025)'s excess decision risk decomposition to derive a per-sample epistemic risk estimator, moving from group-level to individual-level uncertainty quantification. The partitioning mechanism draws directly on Wager & Athey's (2017) honest trees, which provide the consistency guarantees needed for the theoretical results. The key conceptual departure is that the tree-based partition can group inputs with similar over/under-confidence across different confidence-score bins, something binning fundamentally cannot do.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical gap in LLM deployment: standard calibration metrics miss subgroup-specific biases (grouping loss), and the proposed per-sample risk estimator enables principled model deferral in cascades, achieving ~6% accuracy gains at ~46% of the cost of always using the largest model. The identification of specific demographic subgroups with systematic confidence biases (e.g., underestimating high-income probability for experienced graduate women) provides actionable audit signals for high-stakes deployments. Limitations include the reliance on labeled data for estimation, the use of single honest trees rather than ensembles (trading some accuracy for interpretability), and the fact that the partition quality depends on the choice of input representation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-class or continuous-output settings, and whether the cascade gains persist under distribution shift.
