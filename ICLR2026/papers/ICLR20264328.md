## Problem

Classifier-free guidance (CFG) in text-to-image diffusion models relies on a static guidance scale or a fixed heuristic schedule, creating a 'one-size-fits-all' strategy that cannot adapt to the diverse requirements of different prompts (e.g., text rendering needs high guidance while artistic aesthetics benefit from low guidance). Prior remedies—gradient-based correction from auxiliary models or empirically derived schedules—add complexity, require manual tuning, and fail to generalize across model families, as demonstrated by schedules that improve on StableDiffusion but degrade performance on Imagen 3.

## Input / Output

At each denoising timestep t, the method consumes the current noisy latent x_t and the text prompt c, and selects a CFG scale from a discrete candidate set (5 values for LDM, 24 for Imagen 3) by evaluating lightweight latent-space scorers directly on x_t without decoding to pixels. The output is a per-prompt, per-sample dynamic CFG schedule (one scale per timestep) that drives the standard diffusion sampling loop, adding only ~1% FLOPs overhead versus ~400% for a pixel-space equivalent. The evaluators are small dual-encoder networks (~194M params) fine-tuned to score noisy latents, so no VAE decode is needed at intermediate steps.

## Method

The framework trains a suite of time-conditioned latent evaluators—CLIP-based alignment, a discriminator for visual quality, a Bradley-Terry reward model for human preference, an OCR-regressed text-rendering scorer, and a contrastive numerical-reasoning scorer—each initialized from a pre-trained CLIP ViT-B/16 with a randomly re-initialized embedding layer to accept diffusion latents, then fine-tuned on noisy latents with a time-weighted loss that suppresses fine-grained signals during high-noise phases. At every denoising step, a greedy search evaluates all candidate CFG scales using the already-computed conditional and unconditional noise predictions (no extra NFEs), scores the resulting denoised latent with the evaluators, and selects the scale maximizing a composite score. A dynamic weighting scheme (Eq. 7) modulates each evaluator's contribution by detecting significant score changes across timesteps, amplifying an evaluator's influence precisely when its signal becomes information-rich. This per-prompt, per-timestep adaptability is what distinguishes the approach from static schedules: the same model produces high-guidance schedules for text-rendering prompts and low-guidance schedules for creative or counting prompts, generalizing out-of-the-box from LDM to Imagen 3 without re-deriving heuristics.

## Results

On the SOTA Imagen 3 model, the dynamic CFG framework with adaptive weighting of alignment and reward evaluators achieves 53.6% and 53.8% human preference win rates on Gecko and GenAI-Bench respectively, and up to 54.7% on MARIO-eval for text rendering. With capability-specific evaluators, win rates reach 55.5% (text rendering) and 54.1% (numerical reasoning) over the default Imagen 3 baseline. On LDM_large, the full method (adaptive weighting) achieves a Gecko score of 47.2 and FID of 24.8, simultaneously improving both alignment and fidelity over the default CFG baseline (43.8 / 25.6). The method adds only ~1% computational overhead (5 FLOPs per evaluation step vs. 2280 for denoising).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against gradient-based guidance (Nichol et al. 2022, CLIP alignment; Kim et al. 2023, discriminator), the dynamic CFG method wins on both metrics simultaneously: the full adaptive method achieves Gecko 47.2 and FID 24.8, while CLIP gradient guidance reaches only 46.1/25.6 and discriminator guidance 44.6/25.5 on LDM_large. Static heuristic schedules (Kynkäänniemi et al. 2024 limited interval; Sadat et al. 2023 annealing) show mixed results on LDM but catastrophically fail on Imagen 3, dropping to 27.9% and 46.4% win rates on Gecko respectively—below the 50% baseline—demonstrating that empirically derived schedules do not transfer across model families. Against FK steering (Singhal et al. 2025), which searches over multiple seeds at k=4 particles, the dynamic CFG method achieves a statistically indistinguishable 50.7% win rate for FK steering (95% CI [48.9%, 52.5%]), matching a compute-intensive method at only ~1% overhead versus the 4× NFE increase of FK steering. A beam search with k=2 (doubling inference cost) yields only a marginal 0.6% improvement (73.7% vs. 73.1% Gecko score), confirming that greedy selection is the superior efficiency-quality trade-off.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) from a static or empirically-derived schedule to a fully dynamic, per-prompt, per-timestep selection driven by online feedback. It repurposes CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) as a lightweight latent-space evaluator by replacing the pixel embedding layer with a diffusion-latent one and adding timestep conditioning, then fine-tunes variants for visual quality, human preference, text rendering, and numerical reasoning. The approach departs from gradient-based correction (Nichol et al., 2022; Kim et al., 2023) by avoiding auxiliary model gradients entirely, and from multi-seed steering methods (Singhal et al., 2025) by operating on a single seed with only ~1% FLOP overhead rather than scaling NFEs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the optimal CFG schedule is inherently prompt-dependent and model-dependent, with heuristic schedules that improve LDM actually degrading Imagen 3 below baseline—a concrete demonstration of the brittleness of empirical inference-time rules. The framework's generality (same method, different evaluators, different model families) suggests a reusable template for inference-time control beyond CFG, potentially extending to other sampling parameters or modalities. Open questions include whether the greedy per-step selection can be replaced by learned policies, whether the evaluators' reliability degrades on out-of-distribution prompts, and whether the approach transfers to video or 3D generation where the latent space is higher-dimensional.
