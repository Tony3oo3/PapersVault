## Problem

Data-driven PDE solvers such as PINNs and Gaussian Processes can approximate high-dimensional semi-linear parabolic PDEs but lack rigorous error guarantees, raising reliability concerns for safety-critical applications. The paper asks whether additional computation at inference time can systematically refine a pre-trained surrogate—allocating more compute to harder states—without any retraining or fine-tuning, analogous to inference-time scaling in LLMs.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264341/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pre-trained surrogate model (PINN or GP) and a single query state (t, x) along with the PDE's drift, diffusion, and terminal-condition coefficients. At inference time, it produces a corrected solution u(t,x) = ū(t,x) + û(t,x) where the defect û is estimated via a branching Monte Carlo simulation (Multilevel Picard iteration) run only at that query point, rather than re-solving the PDE globally. The correction is a pointwise scalar (plus gradient) whose magnitude is typically 20–80% smaller than the surrogate's own error, and the number of Monte Carlo samples at inference time is a user-tunable knob for trading compute against accuracy.

## Method

SCaSML derives the Structural-preserving Law of Defect: by subtracting the PDE residual of the surrogate ū from the original PDE, one obtains a new semi-linear parabolic PDE whose solution is exactly the defect u − ū. Crucially, this defect PDE inherits the same semi-linear structure as the original problem, so it can be solved with the Multilevel Picard (MLP) Monte Carlo solver via the Feynman-Kac and Bismut-Elworthy-Li representations. The modified nonlinearity F̌ in the defect PDE depends on the surrogate's residual ε, meaning a more accurate surrogate produces a smaller source term and lower simulation variance. The paper proves the final L² error is bounded by the product of the surrogate error e(ū) and the MLP simulation error E(M,N), yielding an improved convergence rate of O(m^{−γ−1/2}) versus O(m^{−γ}) for the surrogate alone under a matched 2m-evaluation budget. No retraining is required; the correction is a single-step, closed-form unbiased identity rather than an iterative Newton-type update, avoiding the nested-simulation variance blowup that plagues classical defect-correction schemes in a Monte Carlo setting.

## Results

SCaSML reduces relative L² error by 20–80% across all tested PDEs (up to 160 dimensions) compared to the base surrogate, while also outperforming a naive MLP solver in nearly every setting. For example, on the 60-dim Linear Convection-Diffusion equation, SCaSML achieves L² error 1.32E-01 vs. 3.13E-01 for the PINN surrogate (58% reduction) and 2.39E-01 for naive MLP. On the 20-dim Viscous Burgers equation with a PINN surrogate, SCaSML reaches L² error 4.03E-03, a 66% improvement over the surrogate's 1.17E-02. The method also demonstrates effective inference-time scaling: increasing Monte Carlo samples at inference monotonically decreases error.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SCaSML is compared against three baselines: (1) the PINN surrogate (Raissi et al., 2017), (2) the Gaussian Process surrogate (Chen et al., 2021), and (3) a naive Multilevel Picard (MLP) solver (Hutzenthaler et al., 2019). SCaSML consistently outperforms both surrogate baselines across all four PDE families and all dimensions tested, reducing L² error by 20–80% (e.g., 66% on 20-dim Viscous Burgers with PINN, 58% on 60-dim LCD). Against the naive MLP solver, SCaSML wins in nearly every setting; the MLP solver degrades severely in high dimensions (e.g., L² error of 5.63E+00 at 100-dim VB-GP) while SCaSML remains stable (5.53E-02). The only marginal case is the 160-dim DR problem where SCaSML's improvement over the PINN surrogate is modest (~7%), reflecting that the surrogate is already quite accurate there. Statistical significance tests confirm p ≪ 0.001 for the error reductions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper bridges two previously separate lineages: classical defect correction (Bank & Weiser, 1985; Stetter, 1978), which improves grid-based PDE solutions by solving an auxiliary error equation, and high-dimensional stochastic PDE solvers (Multilevel Picard iterations, Hutzenthaler et al., 2019), which use Feynman-Kac/Bismut-Elworthy-Li representations to avoid the curse of dimensionality. The key conceptual departure is showing that the defect PDE inherits the semi-linear structure of the original problem, making it amenable to Monte Carlo solution—a property absent from classical defect correction, which relies on asymptotic error expansions unavailable for neural networks. The inference-time scaling framing draws an explicit analogy to LLM test-time compute (Snell et al., 2024), but the mechanism is fundamentally different: additional compute here means more Monte Carlo paths for a targeted correction PDE rather than broader search or planning.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SCaSML addresses a critical trust gap in SciML: data-driven PDE solvers lack the rigorous error guarantees that make traditional numerical methods reliable for safety-critical applications. The product-of-errors bound (Theorem 2.5) provides a clean, interpretable guarantee, and the 'elastic compute' paradigm—where a smaller surrogate plus inference-time correction can outperform a larger surrogate under the same total budget—offers a practical path to on-demand accuracy without retraining. Limitations include the requirement that the surrogate be 'reasonably accurate' (Assumption 2.4), the pointwise nature of the correction (not a global field improvement), and the significant inference-time overhead (2–10× the surrogate's runtime per Table 1). Open questions include whether multiple correction iterations yield further gains, how the method extends to fully nonlinear or hyperbolic PDEs, and whether adaptive allocation of inference compute across spatial points is possible.
