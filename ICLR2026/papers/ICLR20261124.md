## Problem

VLMs have shown progress on explicit, step-by-step navigation instructions, but their ability to interpret implicit human needs (e.g., "I'm thirsty") in open, dynamic urban environments remains largely unexplored. This paper asks whether VLMs can develop intrinsic spatial awareness to ground abstract, multi-hop semantic inferences—functional, spatial, and semantic—into concrete visual search in real cityscapes, addressing the critical "last-mile" navigation problem that pre-constructed directives cannot handle.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261124/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each step the agent receives a natural-language instruction expressing an implicit need (not step-by-step directions) and a set of perspective-view images—one per navigable heading—subdivided from a 360° street-view panorama at the current node on a 20 m-spaced road graph. The VLM returns a structured JSON containing a reasoning rationale, a selected perspective index (direction), a confidence score in [0,1], and a stop/continue decision, following a ReAct-style Observe–Think–Act–Reflect loop capped at 35 steps with no persistent memory between steps to isolate core spatial reasoning.

## Method

CitySeeker is a demand-driven benchmark of 6,440 trajectories across 8 cities and 7 task categories (Basic POI through Abstract Demand), constructed via a Neo4j graph pipeline that links POIs to visible nodes within 50 m and generates A*-shortest paths of 5–25 steps, validated by a 120-participant cross-cultural consensus survey (83.39 % agreement). The evaluation framework uses a two-prompt ReAct protocol: a Stop prompt for goal detection at the current panorama and a Choice prompt for selecting among candidate perspective views, with each step kept independent to isolate the model's spatial reasoning. To probe improvement strategies, the paper introduces the BCR triad—Backtracking (confidence-threshold, topological-distance, and human-guided variants), Spatial Cognition Enrichment (topology cognitive graph and relative position map injected via GPT-4.1-synthesized trajectory knowledge), and Memory-Based Retrieval (topology-based h-hop subgraph, Euclidean-radius subgraph, and sliding-window historical trajectory lookup). Experiments on 27 VLMs show that even the best model (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) reaches only 21.1 % TCP, while the R-series memory strategies yield the largest gains, pushing that model to 26.9 % TCP and dramatically reducing nDTW.

## Results

On the CitySeeker benchmark (1,257 test trajectories across 8 cities), the best-performing VLM, Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct, achieves only 21.1% TC-Proximity (TCP) and 2.6% TC-Exact (TCE), while GPT-4o reaches 18.3% TCP and Gemini-2.5-Pro reaches 17.3% TCP. Human participants substantially outperform all models, achieving 30.1% TCP and 5.7% TCE, with a particularly large gap on Transit Hub navigation (34.9% vs. 10.7% for the best VLM). The BCR exploratory strategies yield meaningful gains: Memory-Based Retrieval (R1) pushes Qwen2.5-VL-32B to 26.9% TCP, and Human-Guided Backtracking (B3) improves GPT-4o-Mini's nDTW from 337.1 to 258.3.

## Baselines & Comparisons

CitySeeker evaluates 27 VLMs against three reference baselines (Human, Random Choice, Forward Direction). Qwen2.5-VL-32B leads all models at 21.1% TCP, outperforming GPT-4o (18.3%), InternVL3-38B (19.3%), and Gemini-2.5-Pro (17.3%), yet still trails the Human baseline (30.1%) by nearly 10 points. On the hardest subcategory (Latent POI, e.g., finding a restroom inside a McDonald's), even the best model (Qwen2.5-VL-32B at 14.2% TCP) lags humans (19.7%) by 5.5 points, while smaller models like LLaVA-Qwen2-7B collapse to 2.8%. Notably, several models (e.g., Llama-4-Maverick-17B at 10.8% TCP, Phi-4-Multimodal at 9.2%) underperform the Random Choice baseline (13.9%), indicating that naive random exploration can outperform poorly-calibrated VLM reasoning. The BCR ablation (Table 3) shows that Memory-Based Retrieval (R1) is the most effective strategy, boosting Qwen2.5-VL-32B from 19.9% to 26.9% TCP, while Human-Guided Backtracking (B3) provides the most consistent gains across model sizes (e.g., GPT-4o-Mini: 12.5% → 18.2% TCP).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) literature, which has predominantly focused on explicit step-by-step instructions in constrained environments (indoor panoramas, 3D grids, single-city street views like Touchdown and Talk2Nav), by introducing implicit-need-driven navigation in open-world, multi-city urban settings. The navigation pipeline is built on the ReAct reasoning-acting framework (Yao et al., 2023), while the BCR exploratory strategies are explicitly inspired by cognitive science concepts of cognitive maps (Tolman, 1948; Epstein et al., 2017) and recent work probing emergent spatial intelligence in large models (Gurnee & Tegmark, 2023; Momennejad et al., 2024). The key conceptual departure is shifting from pre-constructed navigation directives to natural, unstructured human expressions of need (e.g., 'I'm thirsty'), requiring the agent to perform multi-hop semantic inference, visual grounding, and spatial reasoning simultaneously.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

CitySeeker fills a clear gap as the first large-scale benchmark (6,440 trajectories, 8 cities, 7 cognitive-difficulty tiers) for implicit-need-driven embodied urban navigation, and its finding that even the best VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-32B) achieve only ~21% task completion while humans reach ~30% underscores a substantial and well-characterized gap in VLM spatial intelligence. The BCR triad (Backtracking, Cognitive-map enrichment, Retrieval-augmented memory) provides a concrete, human-inspired roadmap for closing this gap, with memory-based retrieval (R-series) yielding the largest gains. Open questions remain: how to integrate persistent spatial memory without the context-length bottleneck, how to handle truly dynamic environments (construction, seasonal changes), and whether the observed performance gap will narrow with next-generation models or reflects a fundamental architectural limitation in current VLMs' spatial reasoning.
