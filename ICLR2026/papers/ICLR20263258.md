## Problem

Existing persona-control methods for LLMs treat personality as something that must be externally imposed through prompting, retrieval, or fine-tuning, often suffering from interference, shallow control, or unstable persona fidelity. This paper asks whether distinct personas are already embedded as separable subnetworks within a single pretrained model's parameter space, waiting to be uncovered without any additional training or external knowledge.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263258/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a pretrained LLM (LLaMA-2-13B, LLaMA-3-8B, or Qwen2.5-14B) and a very small persona-specific calibration set (as few as 5–128 examples, orders of magnitude smaller than typical training data) to produce per-layer binary masks over all Linear modules in attention and MLP blocks (embeddings and LM head remain unpruned). At inference, a text prompt is fed through the masked model via element-wise multiplication (W ⊙ M^p), yielding a persona-aligned response; switching personas requires only swapping the mask with no weight modification or cache invalidation.

## Method

The core mechanism is activation-guided pruning: for each persona, expected activation magnitudes per neuron are collected on calibration data, then importance scores are computed as the product of weight magnitude and activation frequency (Wanda-style), followed by row-wise Top-K selection to construct binary masks that isolate persona-relevant parameters. For semantically opposing personas (e.g., power-seeking vs. power-rejecting), a contrastive pruning variant replaces the importance score with a standardized activation difference between the two personas, and assigns each parameter to the persona with the larger score, enforcing disjoint masks that maximize behavioral separation. The entire procedure is training-free—no gradient updates are performed—and extends the lottery ticket hypothesis by showing that multiple persona-specific 'winning tickets' coexist within a single dense model. A refinement step optionally incorporates a diagonal Hessian approximation (SparseGPT-style) for more accurate pruning order, and a soft gating mechanism (G = M^p + γ(1−M^p)) allows partial blending of personas for graded control.

## Results

On the AI Persona classification task (Llama-2-13B), Sparse with Contrastive Pruning achieves 56.5% power-seeking, 64.5% wealth-seeking, and 96.0% hallucination-identification accuracy, outperforming the Prompt baseline (41.0/44.0/58.5%) by +15.5/+20.5/+37.5 points respectively. On RoleAgentBench (Llama-3-8B), Sparse pruning reaches 70.83% on Merchant of Venice and maintains above 50% across all five character domains, versus 18.37–42.11% for Prompt. General capability degradation is minimal: MMLU drops only 1.6% (0.378→0.362) and HellaSwag 2.2% (0.675→0.653) under 60% sparsity. The method remains training-free, requiring only ~128 calibration samples per persona and minutes of computation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares three baseline families: prompt-based persona induction (Cheng et al., 2023; Shao et al., 2023), retrieval-augmented generation (Zerhoudi & Granitzer, 2024; Yu et al., 2024), and supervised fine-tuning (Zhou et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2025). The proposed pruning method decisively outperforms both Prompt and RAG across all tasks: on AI Persona (Llama-2-13B), Sparse+Contrastive gains +15.5 pp over Prompt on power-seeking and +37.5 pp on hallucination; on RoleAgentBench (Llama-3-8B), Sparse improves Friends accuracy from 18.37% (Prompt) to 51.02% and Sherlock from 42.11% to 60.53%. Against SFT, the pruning method trails by 7.5–15 pp on AI Persona (e.g., 56.5% vs. 64.0% power-seeking on Llama-2-13B), but SFT requires hours-to-days of training and additional parameters, whereas the pruning approach is entirely training-free and adds only lightweight binary masks. The contrastive pruning variant consistently adds 2–10 pp over vanilla Wanda/Sparse, confirming that explicit opposition modeling strengthens persona separation. On general benchmarks (MMLU, HellaSwag), the pruned subnetworks degrade performance by ≤2.2%, confirming that persona specialization does not substantially impair general capabilities.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes compression-oriented pruning methods (Wanda, SparseGPT) for a fundamentally different goal: discovering pre-existing behavioral subnetworks rather than compressing a model. It extends the lottery ticket hypothesis from 'sparse subnetworks can match dense performance' to 'multiple persona-specific winning tickets coexist within a single pretrained model.' Conceptually, it departs from the dominant paradigm of persona control via external intervention (prompting, RAG, fine-tuning, LoRA) by arguing that personas are latent parameter-space structures that can be isolated through activation-guided masking without any gradient updates. The contrastive pruning variant draws on differential activation statistics to enforce disjointness between opposing personas, a mechanism not present in standard pruning literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The core claim—that diverse human-like behaviors are already embedded as sparse routing structures in pretrained LLMs—offers a new lens on controllability and interpretability, potentially enabling zero-shot persona switching with only binary masks. However, the evidence is mixed: N/S and J/P dimensions show weak mask separation and frequent persona collapse, SFT still outperforms all pruning variants by 8–15 points, and the extracted subnetworks retain substantial overlap (Jaccard ~0.15–0.40). Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to more than binary oppositions, scales to 70B+ models, or can handle continuous (rather than categorical) persona axes. The mechanistic interpretability results (causal layer restoration) are promising but limited to a handful of layers and one persona pair.
