## Problem

Perturbation-based feature attribution methods (LIME, SHAP) produce unreliable importance scores because ablating features creates out-of-distribution inputs that induce systematic prediction distortions known as missingness bias. Existing mitigations—retraining, architectural modifications, or domain-specific replacement strategies—are computationally expensive, model-specific, or infeasible for API-based black-box models. The paper challenges the assumption that this bias is a deep representational flaw, showing it can be corrected as a superficial artifact in the model's output space.

## Input / Output

MCal takes as input the raw logits z = f(x) from a frozen base classifier and the ablation rate p = k/n of the perturbed input, and produces calibrated logits R_θ(z) = Wz + b that restore alignment with the clean-input class distribution. The calibrator is a single affine matrix W ∈ R^{m×m} plus bias b ∈ R^{m}, requiring only m² + m parameters. At inference, an ensemble of calibrators is indexed by the input's ablation rate, and the calibrated model serves as a drop-in replacement in any existing explanation pipeline without modifying the base model.

## Method

MCal fits a lightweight affine calibrator R_θ(z) = Wz + b on the frozen base model's logits by minimizing cross-entropy between the calibrated prediction on an ablated input x′ and the one-hot class predicted on the clean input x. The key theoretical contribution is that this objective is convex in θ, guaranteeing that standard gradient-based optimization converges to a globally optimal solution—unlike non-convex retraining or architecture modifications. The authors recommend an ensemble of calibrators, each specialized to a particular ablation rate (e.g., 10%, 20%, …), since missingness bias severity varies with the fraction of ablated features. This design requires only access to output logits, making it applicable to API-based models, and is orders of magnitude cheaper than fine-tuning or LoRA while empirically matching or outperforming those heavier baselines across vision, language, and tabular medical benchmarks.

## Results

MCal reduces KL-divergence-based missingness bias (Eq. 1, lower is better) across all eight medical benchmarks spanning vision, language, and tabular domains. On the vision tasks, MCal achieves 7.43e-3 (Brain MRI), 8.82e-3 (CheXpert), and 4.29e-3 (BreakHis), compared to base-model values of 1.18e-1, 1.70e-1, and 1.87e-1 respectively. On language, MCal reaches 9.44e-4 (MedQA) and 9.01e-3 (MedMCQA), and on tabular data it achieves 5.01e-3 (PhysioNet), 1.92e-5 (Breast Cancer), and 3.35e-3 (CTG). MCal also does not degrade classifier accuracy at any ablation rate, including the clean-input setting (Figure 7).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MCal (conditioned ensemble) outperforms all four baseline families on 7 of 8 datasets, losing only to Retrain on Brain MRI (6.70e-4 vs. 7.43e-3). Against replacement-based methods (Hase et al., 2021), MCal wins on every dataset, with particularly large margins on BreakHis (4.29e-3 vs. 4.20e-1) and MedMCQA (9.01e-3 vs. 2.59e-1), where replacement actually worsens bias. Against architecture-based modifications (Jain et al., 2022), MCal dominates across all eight benchmarks, with the gap most pronounced on CTG (3.35e-3 vs. 2.85e-1) and Breast Cancer (1.92e-5 vs. 2.13e-1), where native missing-feature support in XGBoost paradoxically amplifies bias. Against retraining (Hooker et al., 2019 / ROAR), MCal is competitive or superior on 7/8 datasets, achieving 1.92e-5 on Breast Cancer versus 5.68e-3 for retraining. Against standard calibration baselines (temperature scaling and Platt scaling from Guo et al., 2017), MCal wins on all eight datasets, with the largest gap on MedMCQA where temperature scaling catastrophically fails (7.81e-1 vs. 9.01e-3 for MCal).

## Relation to Prior Work

MCal repurposes the affine calibration framework from Guo et al. (2017) — originally designed for confidence calibration — to correct missingness bias in feature attribution, a problem previously addressed only through expensive retraining (ROAR, GOAR), architectural modifications (mask tokens in ViT), or domain-specific replacement strategies. The key conceptual departure is reframing missingness bias as a superficial output-space artifact correctable by a lightweight post-hoc linear head, rather than a deep representational flaw requiring model modification. This positions MCal as a model-agnostic, logit-only intervention that sidesteps the engineering burden of all prior mitigation categories while retaining a convexity guarantee absent from non-linear alternatives.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for practitioners working with frozen or API-based models (e.g., LLM providers) where retraining or architectural changes are infeasible; MCal requires only clean/ablated logit pairs and a few thousand optimization steps. However, the method's applicability is bounded by the need for logit access (unavailable for some API endpoints) and by overfitting risk in high-cardinality output spaces (e.g., large vocabularies). Open questions include whether the 'superficial' characterization holds for all model architectures, whether differentiable relaxations of the KL-divergence missingness metric could tighten the optimization objective, and whether the approach extends to other forms of distribution shift beyond feature ablation.
