## Problem

In RLVR training, rule-based outcome rewards assign identical positive signals to both fully correct rollouts and flawed-positive rollouts (those reaching correct answers via unreliable patterns like answer-guessing or jump-in-reasoning), causing the policy to internalize unreliable reasoning. The paper identifies two gaps: the distribution and dynamic role of flawed positives throughout the RL process remain underexplored, and no principled mechanism exists to leverage them as early-stage shortcuts while suppressing them in later refinement.

## Input / Output

The reasoning model takes a problem statement (math word problems or multiple-choice questions) and generates a step-by-step reasoning trajectory ending with a final answer in \boxed{} format. The GenRM takes the same problem statement plus a candidate reasoning trajectory (with paragraphs indexed from 0) and outputs a generative critique concluding with either 'True'/'False' (outcome mode) or the index of the earliest erroneous paragraph (process mode), also in \boxed{}. The GenRM is deployed as an external LLM service queried via remote API during RL training, with responses kept short (~3k tokens) to maintain inference efficiency.

## Method

FAPO comprises two components. First, a generative reward model (GenRM) is trained via RL with a step-wise reward combining an outcome signal (correct/incorrect FP label) and a distance-sensitive process penalty that rewards precise error-step localization, discouraging label-guessing and naturally shifting emphasis from correctness to precision as training progresses. Second, during the final RL optimization, FAPO applies a parameter-free penalty R_Δ = −λ (default λ=1) to rollouts that are answer-correct but flagged as flawed-positive by the GenRM, modifying the group-relative advantage estimation. Theoretical analysis shows this creates a natural optimization shift: when the ratio of fully-correct to negative rollouts (ρ) exceeds 2/λ−1, flawed positives receive negative advantage, transitioning the policy from exploiting shortcuts to reinforcing reliable reasoning, and when ρ exceeds 4/λ−1, the advantage scaling factor increases, further stabilizing updates. The GenRM is integrated asynchronously via a decoupled reward-loop architecture that dispatches each rollout to the critic immediately upon generation, adding less than 20% training overhead.

## Results

FAPO-GenRM-4B achieves an F1 of 89.4 on FlawedPositiveBench and 83.3 average F1 on ProcessBench, outperforming the teacher model Qwen3-32B (87.8 / 82.0) and the discriminative SoTA Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B (81.8 / 76.8). In the final RL reasoning stage, FAPO-32B reaches an average score of 54.8 across six benchmarks (AIME24, AIME25, AMC, MATH, LiveCodeBench, GPQA-Diamond), compared to 51.0 for the baseline, with the largest gains on AIME25 (+4.0) and LiveCodeBench (+5.0). The flawed-positive ratio drops substantially under FAPO: from 15.5% to 7.1% on AIME24 and from 10.9% to 1.7% on AIME25 (LLM-as-a-judge), confirming improved process reliability without increasing token budget.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the discriminative SoTA Qwen2.5-Math-PRM-72B, FAPO-GenRM-4B improves F1 by 7.6 points (89.4 vs 81.8) and Precision by 13.2 points (87.5 vs 74.3), while matching Recall (91.4 vs 91.0), showing that the generative approach with step-wise RL avoids the over-critic problem that plagues discriminative PRMs. Compared to the teacher model Qwen3-32B (Think), FAPO-GenRM-4B surpasses it on both F1 (89.4 vs 87.8) and ProcessBench (83.3 vs 82.0) despite being 8× smaller, validating the step-wise RL training recipe. Over its own base model Qwen3-4B-Instruct, FAPO-GenRM-4B gains 18.1 points in Precision (87.5 vs 69.4) and 8.2 in F1 (89.4 vs 81.2), confirming that RL training substantially sharpens detection accuracy. In the downstream reasoning task, FAPO-32B outperforms the GRPO baseline on all six benchmarks, with the most notable improvements on AIME25 (+4.0) and LiveCodeBench (+5.0), and consistently leads at nearly all intermediate training checkpoints, indicating both higher final performance and greater training stability.

## Relation to Prior Work

FAPO builds directly on the GRPO/DAPO RLVR pipeline (Shao et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2025), modifying the group-relative advantage estimation by introducing a binary penalty for flawed-positive rollouts. It departs from the dense process-reward model (PRM) literature (Lightman et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2025b), which assigns step-level scores but is vulnerable to reward hacking, by instead using a generative reward model trained to make a single flawed/correct judgment plus error localization. The flawed-positive phenomenon itself was identified by Zheng et al. (2024) and Wang et al. (2025) in static evaluations, but FAPO is the first to formalize its role as a dynamic, stage-dependent signal within the RL training loop and provide a principled, parameter-free mechanism to exploit it.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that flawed positives persist at a roughly constant ~30% rate throughout RL training—rather than naturally diminishing—challenges the assumption that binary outcome rewards will eventually self-correct unreliable reasoning, and motivates the need for explicit process-aware signals. The practical impact is significant for anyone running RLVR at scale: a single 4B GenRM deployed asynchronously adds <20% training overhead while reducing flawed-positive ratios by 8–9 points and improving AIME/GPQA scores without increasing token budget. Key open questions include whether the approach generalizes to multi-turn agent RL, MoE architectures, and fully asynchronous training systems, and whether the λ=1 default holds across model scales or requires per-scale tuning.
