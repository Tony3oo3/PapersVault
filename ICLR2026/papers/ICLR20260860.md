## Problem

Online prompt selection methods for RL finetuning of large reasoning models, such as Dynamic Sampling (DS), identify informative (partially solved) prompts by generating multiple LLM rollouts over enlarged candidate batches, incurring computational overhead that often exceeds the finetuning cost itself. The paper asks whether one can predict which prompts will be informative at the next training step without performing those costly rollouts, thereby preserving the adaptivity of online selection while eliminating redundant generation.

## Input / Output

The method consumes a prompt dataset (e.g., 7,500 MATH problems, 2,000 Countdown items, 2,101 Geometry3k questions), the current LLM policy π_θ, and intermittent binary reward observations from past rollouts (a prompt is only observed when it happens to be selected for training in a given step). At each training step it outputs a batch of B prompts ranked by their predicted probability of being in the 'partially solved' state, which are then rolled out and used for GRPO finetuning. The per-prompt state is a 3-class categorical (fully unsolved / partially solved / fully solved) derived from the fraction of k sampled responses that are correct, and the method must extrapolate states across unobserved steps using only the transition model.

## Method

DPS formalizes each prompt's solving progress as a 3-state Hidden Markov Model where the state is the solving extent and transitions are governed by a column-stochastic matrix Φ. Online Bayesian inference tracks the per-prompt state belief μ_t via a three-stage pipeline: (i) a degenerate emission update (posterior collapses to the observed state when a rollout occurs, otherwise posterior = prior), (ii) a Dirichlet posterior update on the transition matrix using soft pseudo-counts ξ_t computed from the joint posterior P(z_{t-1}=j, z_t=i | y_{1:t}), and (iii) forward propagation μ_{t+1}^{prior} = Φ_t · μ_t^{post} to predict the next step's state distribution. A non-stationary extension applies exponential decay (α_t = λ·α_{t-1} + (1-λ)·α_0 + ξ_t) to the Dirichlet posterior, enabling adaptation to evolving learning dynamics while preventing collapse under sparse observations. Prompt selection at each step uses the predictive prior μ_t^{prior}(2) (probability of partial solving) to pick the top-B prompts before any rollout, reducing the per-step cost to O(|D|·C_pred + C_topk + |B|·C_llm) ≈ O(|B|·C_llm) since the HMM operations are 3×3 matrix multiplications with negligible overhead relative to LLM generation.

## Results

On the MATH dataset with DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, DPS achieves an average score of 52.13 across five math benchmarks (AIME24, AMC23, MATH500, MinervaMath, OlympiadBench), matching the oracle DS baseline (52.00) while using only 737k rollouts versus DS's 2933k (~25% of the rollout budget) and 32h runtime versus 89h. For the 7B model, DPS reaches 63.13 average, surpassing DS (62.42) with 287k rollouts vs. 1147k. On Countdown-34, DPS scores 74.27 (3B) and 81.15 (7B), matching DS (74.95, 81.26) with 246k rollouts vs. 1141k/1006k. On Geometry3k, DPS achieves 44.47 (3B) and 47.78 (7B), matching DS (44.33, 48.11) with 492k/328k rollouts vs. 1262k/782k.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Dynamic Sampling (DS, Yu et al. 2025), the rollout-intensive oracle baseline, DPS matches or slightly exceeds DS on average math score (52.13 vs. 52.00 for 1.5B; 63.13 vs. 62.42 for 7B) while using only ~25% of the rollouts (737k vs. 2933k; 287k vs. 1147k) and roughly half the wall-clock runtime (32h vs. 89h; 39h vs. 73h). On Countdown and Geometry, DPS also matches DS within 0.1–1.5 points while using 20–30% of DS's rollouts. Against History Resampling (HR, Zhang et al. 2025), DPS consistently outperforms by 2.9–3.8 points on average math score (52.13 vs. 49.23 for 1.5B; 63.13 vs. 60.38 for 7B), with the gap attributed to HR's rigid epoch-level absorbing-state assumption and its failure to filter partially unsolved prompts. DPS also surpasses both baselines on general reasoning benchmarks (ARC-c, MMLU-Pro), achieving 34.79 average (1.5B) and 65.52 average (7B) versus DS's 34.07 and 64.24, respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper addresses the online prompt selection problem in RL finetuning of LLMs, building on the observation (from DAPO/DS and SRPO/HR) that partially solved prompts yield the strongest training signals. Rather than relying on rollout-intensive filtering (DS) or rigid epoch-level heuristics (HR), it repurposes classical hidden Markov model and Bayesian inference machinery to track each prompt's solving progress as a lightweight dynamical system. Concurrent work (Qu et al., 2025; Shen et al., 2025) shares the predict-then-sample principle but models success rate as a static latent variable in a Bernoulli bandit; DPS departs by explicitly modeling temporal transitions with a non-stationary Dirichlet-updated HMM, enabling step-wise extrapolation over unobserved intervals.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: DPS matches or slightly exceeds the rollout-intensive DS oracle while using roughly 30% of its rollout budget, a substantial efficiency gain for anyone running RL finetuning at scale. The implicit curriculum learning that emerges from the selection dynamics (easy→hard progression) is a useful byproduct, though the paper acknowledges it is not the primary mechanism. Key limitations include reliance on binary correctness rewards (extension to dense or process-based rewards is noted but not demonstrated) and the use of a purely exploitative top-k selection criterion. Open questions include whether the 3-state HMM abstraction generalizes to multi-turn or multi-step reward settings, and whether more sophisticated selection criteria (entropy-based, uncertainty-aware) could further improve sample efficiency.
