## Problem

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) store static pretrained knowledge that rapidly becomes obsolete as real-world entities and events evolve, yet most prior knowledge injection research is confined to the text domain and neglects dynamic multimodal evolving knowledge. This paper identifies two critical challenges: existing injection methods (SFT, RAG, web search) perform poorly even with sufficient context, and parameter-modifying methods cause severe, consistently-ranked capability degradation across general tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262453/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each MMEVOKE sample pairs a knowledge-associated image with a heuristic text query and a knowledge summary (injection data), alongside a separate query image, query, and short ground-truth answer (evaluation data). The 9,422 samples span 159 fine-grained subfields across two areas (News from CNN, Entity from Wikipedia), with answers averaging only 2.3 words. Evaluation uses Cover Exact Match (CEM) and word-level F1-Score, and general capability retention is measured across 12 benchmarks covering 7 dimensions (comprehensive, OCR, multidisciplinary, instruction-following, multi-round QA, mathematical reasoning, hallucination).

## Method

The paper introduces MMEVOKE, constructed via a four-step reproducible pipeline: collecting evolving knowledge from CNN/Wikipedia (post-2024), summarizing content with GPT-4o under strict rules, generating VQA pairs and collecting query images via CLIP-based clustering, and manual image selection. For knowledge injection evaluation, the paper tests Full-FT, LoRA, MM-RAG (Text-Only, Image-Only, UniIR), commercial AI web search engines, and Sufficient Context on LLaVA-v1.5 and Qwen-VL-Chat. To address the identified challenges, the paper distinguishes knowledge-aware augmentation (semantic restatement and real-world image enrichment) from knowledge-agnostic augmentation (mechanical synonym replacement, rotation), showing only the former strengthens adaptation. For capability retention, it compares direct rehearsal (Replay with 10% pre-training data), structured separation (MoELoRA), and indirect constraint (EWC, LwF), finding that Replay and MoELoRA effectively mitigate degradation while EWC/LwF fail or worsen it.

## Results

On MMEVOKE (9,422 multimodal evolving knowledge samples), all knowledge injection methods perform poorly: the best parameter-modifying method (Full-FT on LLaVA-v1.5) achieves only 18.02% CEM / 15.17% F1, while the best retrieval method (MM-RAG UniIR) reaches 40.68% CEM / 57.51% F1. Even with Sufficient Context, GPT-4.1 achieves only 75.02% CEM and Gemini-2.5-Pro only 72.15% CEM, revealing that LMMs cannot reliably extract answers even when the correct information is provided. For knowledge retention after SFT, Full-FT causes 25.89% average capability degradation across 7 dimensions, while Replay_LoRA (+10%) reduces this to just 6.80% and MoELoRA to 8.31%, substantially outperforming EWC (22.99%) and LwF (23.21%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among parameter-modifying methods, LoRA (Hu et al., ICLR 2022) underperforms Full-FT on LLaVA-v1.5 (15.23% vs. 18.02% CEM) but slightly outperforms it on F1 (18.31% vs. 15.17%); on Qwen-VL-Chat, LoRA is worse than Full-FT on both metrics (6.95% vs. 10.16% CEM). For retrieval, UniIR (Wei et al., ECCV 2024) substantially outperforms Text-Only and Image-Only variants (40.68% vs. 24.05%/25.25% CEM on LLaVA-v1.5), confirming that multimodal retrieval is critical. For knowledge retention, MoELoRA (Luo et al., 2024) achieves the second-best retention with only 8.31% average degradation, far surpassing EWC (Kirkpatrick et al., 2017) at 22.99% and LwF (Li & Hoiem, 2017) at 23.21%; in fact, EWC and LwF sometimes worsen degradation relative to plain Full-FT on specific benchmarks (e.g., LwF drops MMMU to 20.60 vs. Full-FT's 24.20). Gemini-2.5-Pro (Gemini Team, 2023) outperforms Gemini-2.0-Flash by 26 percentage points in CEM (44.19% vs. 18.21%), while Perplexity AI leads all commercial engines at 48.27% CEM. The paper's key finding is that no single baseline dominates: Replay_LoRA is best for retention, MM-RAG UniIR is best for injection among open methods, and even Sufficient Context with GPT-4.1 (75.02% CEM) falls far short of perfect accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the knowledge injection literature from text-only LLMs to the multimodal domain, building on prior evolving-knowledge benchmarks for LLMs (Realtime QA, DyKnow, EvoWiki) and continual learning methods (EWC, LwF, Replay, MoELoRA) that have been studied primarily in computer vision task-level settings. It departs from prior work in two key ways: (1) introducing multimodal evolving knowledge (news and entities with associated images) rather than static textual facts, and (2) framing the problem as a dual objective—knowledge adaptation AND knowledge retention—rather than treating injection and forgetting as separate concerns. The distinction between knowledge-agnostic and knowledge-aware augmentation is a conceptual contribution that repurposes the data augmentation paradigm through a knowledge-centric lens.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MMEVOKE provides the first systematic benchmark for evaluating how LMMs acquire and retain evolving multimodal knowledge, filling a clear gap where prior work was confined to text. The finding that even Sufficient Context yields only ~50–75% CEM challenges the RAG community's implicit assumption that retrieval quality is the bottleneck, pointing instead to reasoning and utilization as the limiting factor. The consistent degradation ranking (instruction-following → multi-round QA → hallucination → …) and the cascading failure mechanism offer actionable diagnostics for practitioners fine-tuning LMMs. Open questions include whether the synergy between knowledge-aware augmentation and retention methods (Replay, MoELoRA) can be formalized, and whether these findings generalize to larger, more recent LMMs beyond the 2023-era models tested here.
