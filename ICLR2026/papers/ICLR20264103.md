## Problem

Existing generative models for molecular design—autoregressive and masked diffusion—operate on linearized representations that disrupt chemically meaningful substructures and couple the number of sampling steps to sequence length, making them poorly suited for fragment-based drug discovery. Masked diffusion in particular treats unmasked tokens as fixed, preventing coordinated refinement across the molecule. The paper asks whether a generative paradigm that refines all positions simultaneously from a uniform source can outperform completion-style generation for both de novo and fragment-constrained molecular design.

## Input / Output

The model consumes fragmented SMILES sequences produced by rBRICS decomposition with explicit attachment-point tokens [i*] and space-separated fragments, along with a sequence length drawn from a learned or bandit-sampled distribution. For optimization tasks, it additionally accepts a seed molecule and target protein (for docking) or an oracle score function (for property optimization), and outputs a refined fragmented SMILES sequence. A key structural choice is factorizing the output as p(n)·p(x|n), decoupling sequence length from token generation so that the number of denoising steps is independent of molecule size.

## Method

InVirtuoGen is a continuous-time discrete flow model that transforms a uniform source over all tokens into the data distribution via a linear interpolation path, using a 36-layer diffusion transformer with rotary positional embeddings as the velocity field. Unlike masked diffusion, the training loss (weighted by 1/(1−t²) to emphasize late timesteps) accounts for predictions at every sequence position at every denoising step, so all tokens can change simultaneously—shifting the paradigm from completion to refinement. For property and lead optimization, the authors combine a genetic algorithm (crossover via fragment swapping in fragmented SMILES space, mutation of top candidates) with PPO fine-tuning adapted to discrete flows, where the intractable sequence log-probability is approximated by Monte Carlo estimation over randomly perturbed states. A peak-seeker bandit adaptively samples sequence lengths during optimization, and a modified sampling rule (directly drawing from the predicted token-wise distribution rather than the standard discrete flow update) yields substantially better quality-diversity trade-offs.

## Results

On the PMO benchmark with ZINC250k prescreening, InVirtuoGen achieves a sum AUC-top10 of 18.993 ± 0.219 across 23 tasks, outperforming GenMol (18.362) and f-RAG (16.928). Without prescreening, it reaches 16.676 ± 0.256, surpassing Genetic GFN (16.213), MolGA (14.708), and REINVENT (14.184). For de novo generation, InVirtuoGen achieves a strictly superior quality–diversity Pareto frontier over GenMol and SAFE-GPT, with the largest gains at fine time granularity (h = 0.001). In lead optimization, it attains a total docking score of −152.4 (δ = 0.4) versus −148.7 for GenMol, with particularly large improvements on parp1 and jak2.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against GenMol (the primary discrete-diffusion baseline), InVirtuoGen wins on the PMO sum AUC-top10 (18.993 vs 18.362 with prescreening; 16.676 vs not directly comparable without), achieves a strictly superior quality–diversity Pareto frontier in de novo generation, and obtains a better total docking score in lead optimization (−152.4 vs −148.7 at δ = 0.4). Against f-RAG, the margin is larger on PMO (18.993 vs 16.928), with particularly strong gains on scaffold hop (+0.135), sitagliptin mpo (+0.142), and valsartan smarts (+0.308). In the no-prescreening PMO setting, InVirtuoGen outperforms Genetic GFN (16.676 vs 16.213), MolGA (16.676 vs 14.708), and REINVENT (16.676 vs 14.184), confirming that the gains stem from the algorithmic design rather than oracle budget. On fragment-constrained generation, InVirtuoGen achieves the highest average quality (29.55) versus GenMol (21.06) and SAFE-GPT (16.26), though at the cost of lower validity (71.2% vs 96.6% for GenMol on scaffold decoration). The only tasks where InVirtuoGen trails GenMol are jnk3, median1, thiothixene rediscovery, and troglitazone rediscovery, all within one standard deviation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper applies the discrete flow matching framework of Gat et al. (2024) to molecular generation, using the SAFE fragmented-SMILES representation from Noutahi et al. (2023) as its sequence space. The key conceptual departure is from masked discrete diffusion models (e.g., GenMol) and autoregressive models (e.g., SAFE-GPT): by choosing a uniform source distribution over all tokens rather than a fully masked input, every position is refined at every denoising step, decoupling the number of sampling steps from sequence length. For optimization, the paper adapts PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) to discrete flows via Monte Carlo log-probability estimation and combines it with a genetic algorithm, a hybrid not previously explored in this setting.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

InVirtuoGen provides a single generative backbone that handles de novo generation, fragment-constrained design, property optimization, and lead optimization, setting a new SOTA on the PMO benchmark and improving docking scores in lead optimization. However, the sampling modification (Eq. 6) that drives the quality gains lacks theoretical justification, the fragmented SMILES representation discards stereochemistry, and the authors acknowledge that fragment-constrained generation is fundamentally at odds with the refinement paradigm. Open questions include whether the refinement-vs-completion distinction generalizes beyond fragmented SMILES, whether the observed policy collapse under PPO fine-tuning can be mitigated, and whether the coarse SA/QED proxies used here can be replaced with more realistic ADMET assessments.
