## Problem

Narrow finetuning is widely used to create model organisms that simulate behaviors of interest (emergent misalignment, subliminal learning, false facts), yet it was unknown whether this training procedure leaves strong, easily detectable artifacts in model activations. If such artifacts exist, they would undermine the validity of model organisms as realistic proxies for studying broader, more diverse finetuning scenarios like chat-tuning.

## Input / Output

The method takes a base model and its narrowly finetuned counterpart, evaluates both on 10,000 random web text samples from a pretraining corpus, and extracts activation differences at the middle transformer layer for the first k=5 token positions. Outputs include per-position token relevance scores (fraction of top-20 Patchscope/Logit Lens tokens relevant to the finetuning domain), steered text generations whose semantic similarity to the finetuning dataset is measured via cosine embeddings, and a natural-language description of the finetuning objective produced by an interpretability agent. Notably, the input text is deliberately unrelated to the finetuning domain, and the activation differences are computed at fixed early positions rather than across the full sequence.

## Method

The Activation Difference Lens (ADL) computes per-position average activation differences δ̄_j = h^ft_{j} − h^base_{j} at the middle layer over random web text, then interprets them via two complementary tools: Patchscope (with LLM-calibrated scaling λ) maps the difference vector to relevant tokens, and Logit Lens applies the final unembedding to produce a token distribution. A second component steers the finetuned model by adding α·δ̄_j to all token positions during generation on 20 chat prompts, with α found via binary search for coherence. An interpretability agent (GPT-5) is then given the ADL token results plus one steered and one unsteered response set, and must identify the finetuning objective through a budgeted number of model interactions, scored by a grader LLM against a per-organism rubric. Causal ablation confirms the mechanism: projecting finetuned activations back onto the base model along the δ̄ direction increases loss on finetuning data (the bias is beneficial for the target) while decreasing loss on pretraining data (the bias is overfitting), and mixing pretraining data into the finetuning corpus at ratios up to 1:2 substantially reduces the detectable traces.

## Results

The ADL-enabled interpretability agent (i=5 interactions) identifies the broad finetuning objective (grade ≥ 2) for 91% of organisms and achieves grade ≥ 4 for 30%, compared to only 39% and 1% respectively for the strongest blackbox baseline (i=50). Even with zero additional interactions (i=0), the ADL agent reaches 82% grade ≥ 2 and 24% grade ≥ 4, confirming that activation difference insights alone are highly informative. Patchscope on activation differences surfaces 20–80% relevant tokens at the best position across organisms, far exceeding baselines from average base or finetuned activations. Causal ablation confirms the bias is beneficial on finetuning data (positive ΔL_CE) and harmful on pretraining data (negative ΔL_CE for Qwen3 1.7B and Llama 3.2 8B), supporting an overfitting interpretation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The primary comparison is between ADL-equipped agents and blackbox agents (which only receive normal model generations on 20 chat prompts). The ADL agent with i=5 outperforms the best blackbox agent (i=50) by more than 2× on broad objective identification (91% vs. 39% at grade ≥ 2) and by over 30× on specific detail identification (30% vs. 1% at grade ≥ 4). Remarkably, the ADL agent with zero interactions (i=0) still beats the blackbox agent with 50 interactions, demonstrating that the activation difference signal is the dominant source of information. For token relevance, activation differences (δ̄) yield 20–80% relevant tokens, while average base activations and average finetuned activations serve as baselines that produce substantially lower relevance scores. Blackbox agents perform well on Taboo organisms because those models openly reveal their guessing-game training through prompting, narrowing the gap in that specific case. The Bayesian GLM analysis (Figure 4) confirms ADL access is the single strongest positive predictor of agent grade, with interaction budget i showing a monotonic but secondary positive effect.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper repurposes two well-established interpretability tools—Patchscope (Ghandeharioun et al., 2024) and Logit Lens (Nostalgebraist, 2020)—by applying them not to raw activations but to activation *differences* between base and finetuned models on unrelated data, a use case not explored in prior model-diffing work (crosscoders, stage-wise diffing). The validation framework draws on the automated interpretability agent paradigm from Bricken et al. (2025) and Schwettmann et al. (2023), adapting it to the model-diffing setting. Conceptually, the paper sits at the intersection of the model-organisms literature (SDF, emergent misalignment, subliminal learning, taboo word guessing) and the model-diffing literature, but its key departure is questioning whether narrow finetuning produces artifacts strong enough to invalidate these organisms as realistic proxies for broader post-training.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that narrow finetuning leaves strongly readable traces in early-token activation differences—detectable with simple tools and even surviving when overlaid on base-to-chat transformations—challenges the validity of model organisms as proxies for studying realistic finetuning effects in interpretability and safety research. The practical mitigation (mixing in unrelated pretraining data) is straightforward but introduces a trade-off with objective internalization, and the paper explicitly notes uncertainty about whether other, subtler artifacts remain. Open questions include whether the bias mechanism generalizes beyond the mono-semantic overfitting hypothesis, how to design truly realistic model organisms, and whether broader finetuning (e.g., domain-adapted VLMs) produces weaker but still detectable traces that current tools miss.
