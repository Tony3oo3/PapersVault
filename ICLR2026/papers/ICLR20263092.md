## Problem

Existing SVD-based LoRA initialization methods like PiSSA use only the top-r singular vectors to initialize the down-projection matrix A, confining adaptation to a narrow subspace of the pretrained model's knowledge. This limits effectiveness for tasks requiring global knowledge transfer (e.g., adapting to new languages), and the per-task storage of both A and B matrices becomes prohibitive at scale (thousands of LoRAs for multilingual or personalized ASR).

## Input / Output

The method operates on the pretrained weight matrices W0 ∈ R^{d×k} of Whisper's decoder linear layers (attention and feed-forward), performing SVD to extract singular vectors. At inference, the adapted model takes Mel spectrogram features from audio speech and produces text transcriptions. The key structural detail is that the frozen A matrix (r×k) is shared across all tasks/languages, while only the task-specific B matrix (d×r) is stored per adaptation, halving the per-task parameter count relative to standard LoRA.

## Method

SumRA performs SVD on W0 and initializes each row of A as a sum of multiple rows of Σ^{1/2}V^T, compressing all k singular vectors into r rows rather than selecting only the top-r. Two distribution strategies—interleave sum and greedy sum—assign singular vectors to rows so that the sum of singular values (the 'load') is balanced across rows, preventing destructive interference between high-magnitude vectors. The disjoint partition of column indices guarantees that rows of A remain mutually orthogonal, improving optimization efficiency. A is then frozen and shared across tasks while only B is trained, reducing trainable parameters by 50% and enabling the same A to serve multiple language adaptations. The paper proves greedy sum minimizes the maximum row load, and connects the summing mechanism to model averaging, where summing LoRA matrices before adaptation integrates diverse adaptation directions within a single model.

## Results

On multilingual ASR adaptation of Whisper-large-v2 (rank 32) to five unseen Common Voice languages, SumRA achieves WER of 12.41% (eo), 8.17% (ia), 22.27% (fy-NL), 27.19% (mhr), and 34.21% (kmr), improving over LoRA by up to 16% relative WER reduction while using only 17.6M trainable parameters versus LoRA's 34.3M (50% fewer). At rank 2, SumRA similarly outperforms all baselines, e.g., 14.55% WER on eo vs. LoRA's 15.96% with 1.6M vs. 2.4M parameters. The method also shows stronger gains in low-resource settings: on Esperanto, SumRA reduces WER from 23.39% (LoRA) to 20.77% at 10h of data, with diminishing but still positive gains at 50h and 100h.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SumRA consistently outperforms all six baselines across all five languages and both rank settings. Against LoRA (the primary baseline), SumRA achieves up to 14% relative WER reduction (e.g., 12.41% vs. 14.42% on eo) while using 50% fewer trainable parameters by freezing A and sharing it across tasks. Against the closest SVD-based competitors, SumRA beats PiSSA by up to 14% relative WER (12.41% vs. 13.00% on eo) and CorDA by up to 11% (12.41% vs. 13.13% on eo), confirming that summing multiple singular vectors beyond the top-r is more effective than either using only leading vectors (PiSSA) or task-oriented rescaling (CorDA). DoRA, which decomposes weight magnitude and direction, is the strongest non-SVD baseline at rank 32 (13.45% on eo) but still trails SumRA by ~1 percentage point. VeRA and LoRA-FA, which use random or fixed bases for A, perform substantially worse (20.68% and 16.42% on eo respectively), underscoring the importance of SVD-informed initialization. Notably, SumRA's advantage is most pronounced in low-resource settings (10h data) and diminishes as data scales to 100h, where full fine-tuning and LoRA converge.

## Relation to Prior Work

SumRA extends the PiSSA/CorDA line of SVD-based LoRA initialization by departing from the assumption that only the top-r singular vectors matter. While PiSSA initializes A with the leading singular vectors of W0 and LoRA-FA freezes a randomly initialized A, SumRA compresses the full set of singular vectors into A's r rows via interleave or greedy summation, then freezes A so it can be shared across tasks. The method also draws a conceptual link to model averaging, arguing that summing diverse singular-vector directions within a single A matrix approximates the effect of training and merging multiple LoRA variants. A proof of greedy-sum optimality (minimizing maximum row load) provides a theoretical grounding absent in prior SVD-initialization work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for deployment at scale: freezing A means thousands of per-language or per-user LoRAs share a single A matrix, cutting storage by 50% without sacrificing (and in fact improving) WER. However, the paper itself identifies a clear boundary: SumRA excels at global adaptations (accents, speaking styles affecting broad vocabulary) but offers no advantage for local adaptations (small domain-specific term sets), as confirmed by negative results on GLUE. Open questions include whether the summation strategy generalizes to non-ASR modalities, whether the greedy proof's assumptions (sorted singular values, equal row counts) hold in practice with very high-rank W0, and whether training A (at the cost of extra parameters) further narrows the gap to full fine-tuning at larger data scales.
