## Problem

LLMs suffer from hallucination and context forgetting, both driven by attention drift—the progressive decay of attention on initial context tokens as generation proceeds. While the attention sink (the BOS token's persistent high attention) has been studied independently, no prior work has exploited the synergy between these two co-existing phenomena to actively anchor input context throughout generation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264461/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

For text tasks, the method consumes a standard text prompt (instruction + passage); for multi-modal tasks, it additionally takes an RGB image whose visual features are extracted by the MLLM's own vision encoder. The external information vector (f_info) is derived from these features and injected into the BOS token's KV-cache representation as a one-off pre-generation operation. The output is standard auto-regressive generated text or a reasoning chain, with no change to the decoding loop after the initial injection.

## Method

SINKTRACK repurposes the attention sink by injecting contextual information into the BOS token's representation via a dual-track cross-attention mechanism applied at every 5th transformer layer. Track 1 performs cross-attention exclusively for the BOS token, using its hidden state as Query and the full (unpooled) external information sequence as both Key and Value, enabling adaptive retrieval of salient context. Track 2 runs standard causal self-attention for all remaining tokens, preserving the LLM's native computational flow. The two tracks' outputs are concatenated and passed through the standard output projection. This design is training-free, requires no hyperparameter tuning (unlike the earlier soft-injection variant), and adds negligible overhead since the modified KV entries are cached before auto-regressive decoding begins.

## Results

SINKTRACK achieves consistent gains over both Direct prompting and CoT across all six benchmarks and four model families (3B–12B). Headline results include +22.8% accuracy on M3CoT (Qwen2.5-VL-7B: 66.94% vs. 44.11% CoT), +21.6% on SQuAD2.0 (Llama3.1-8B: 79.83% vs. 58.19% CoT), and +7.1% on full-length QuAC (Llama3.1-8B: 53.51% vs. 46.95% CoT). On the hallucination-specific POPE benchmark, SINKTRACK improves Qwen2.5-VL-7B accuracy from 83.65% (CoT) to 85.47% and Macro-F1 from 90.63% to 91.67%. Variance across three random seeds is notably lower for SINKTRACK than for CoT, indicating more stable reasoning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SINKTRACK is compared against two baselines: Direct prompting (no special instructions) and Chain-of-Thought (CoT, 'Let's think step by step'). SINKTRACK outperforms both baselines on every model–dataset combination in both the multi-modal (Table 1) and text-based (Table 2) evaluations. The largest margins appear on complex multi-hop reasoning (M3CoT: +22.8% over CoT for Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and long-context QA (full QuAC: +7.1% over CoT for Llama3.1-8B; SQuAD2.0: +21.6% over CoT for Llama3.1-8B). On the hallucination-detection POPE benchmark, gains are more modest (+1.8% Acc, +1.0% F1 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B) but still consistent. Notably, CoT itself sometimes underperforms Direct prompting (e.g., MiniCPM3-4B on QuAC: 37.68% vs. 47.17%), a known failure mode in long-context tasks where extended reasoning chains exacerbate attention drift; SINKTRACK avoids this degradation entirely.

## Relation to Prior Work

SINKTRACK builds directly on the attention sink phenomenon identified by Xiao et al. (ICLR 2024), which observed that the BOS token consistently receives disproportionately high attention during generation. The paper also draws on attention drift analyses (Peysakhovich & Lerer, 2023; Liu et al., 2024a) that link progressive attention decay on initial tokens to hallucination and context forgetting. Its key departure from prior inference-time interventions (activation steering, attention calibration) is twofold: it performs a one-time injection rather than continuous modification, and it repurposes the semantically sparse BOS token as an active information anchor via cross-attention rather than altering attention weights. The iterative design exploration (hard → soft → dual-track) positions it as a principled evolution beyond static fusion methods.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SINKTRACK offers a practically attractive solution: training-free, plug-and-play, with negligible overhead (<1ms for 8B models), making it immediately deployable across any decoder-only LLM or MLLM without fine-tuning risks. The consistent 20%+ gains across four model families (Qwen, Gemma, Llama, MiniCPM) and both text and multi-modal tasks underscore broad generalizability. However, the single-token anchor introduces an inherent information bottleneck that limits performance on very long contexts (evidenced by diminishing gains on QuAC), pointing toward future work on distributed or dynamically positioned anchoring mechanisms. An open question is whether the cross-attention retrieval quality degrades when the injected context is highly heterogeneous or adversarially structured.
