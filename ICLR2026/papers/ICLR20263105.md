## Problem

Existing robotic tool design methods require manual task-specific parameter specification, predefined parametric templates, or human-in-the-loop design guidance, limiting scalability to new tasks. The paper asks whether today's vision-language models, combined with evolutionary search, can automatically invent both the tool geometry and the manipulation strategy from scratch for arbitrary manipulation tasks, shifting the problem-solving burden from control to tool design.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263105/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes raw PyBullet environment source code, an overhead RGB image of the workspace, and a natural-language task description as context for a VLM (Gemini-2.5-Pro). It produces paired outputs: a URDF tool design composed exclusively of 3D rectangular components attached to either the Franka gripper fingers or a virtual flange joint, and a variable-length action waypoint sequence (N×6 or N×7 array) where N is determined by the VLM itself, encoding 6-DoF end-effector poses plus an optional binary gripper command. The deliberately simple discrete-waypoint action format is chosen to demonstrate that well-designed tools reduce the need for sophisticated closed-loop policies.

## Method

VLMGINEER runs an evolutionary loop in which a VLM generates diverse populations of tool-action pairs in a single inference step (joint co-design rather than sequential tool-then-action optimization), each pair is evaluated in PyBullet simulation against a task-specific reward, and the top-k performers are fed back as context for the next generation. The key mechanism is inductive in-context mutation and crossover: the VLM is prompted to either mutate a single component (change dimensions, add/remove a part) or crossover elements from two elite designs, leveraging its learned physical reasoning as the genetic operator rather than relying on handcrafted mutation rules. Multiple parallel VLM agents (up to 30) generate independent design samples per iteration to maximize diversity, and the entire pipeline requires no task-specific templates, prompts, or examples. The paper shows this evolutionary refinement yields a 119.2% average reward improvement over brute-force VLM sampling under an equal compute budget, and that the resulting tools and actions transfer zero-shot to a physical Franka robot via 3D printing.

## Results

On ROBOTOOLBENCH (12 tasks), VLMGINEER achieves an average normalized reward of 0.6054 (Gemini-2.5-pro) with a top-run reward of 0.8222, outperforming human-prompted VLM designs by 64.7% (normalized) and human-crafted RLBench tools by 24.3% (normalized). The evolutionary search component is critical: under an equal 8000-sample budget, VLMGINEER's structured evolution achieves 0.938 average reward versus 0.428 for brute-force VLM sampling (+119.2%). Sim-to-real transfer on a physical Franka robot yields normalized rewards of 0.959 (MoveBall), 0.761 (ElevatePlate), and 0.713 (GatherSpheres).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VLMGINEER is compared against four baselines on ROBOTOOLBENCH. Against the vanilla Franka Panda two-finger gripper (no tool), VLMGINEER wins on the vast majority of tasks, as the benchmark is explicitly designed to be infeasible without appropriate tools. Against human-prompted VLM designs (three expertise levels: robotics expert, LLM expert, layperson), VLMGINEER outperforms all three on every task in both average and best reward, with a 64.7% average normalized improvement; human prompts occasionally match peak reward (e.g., CleanTable, ScoreGoal) but with significantly longer action paths and higher variance across runs. Against the four original RLBench tools on the corresponding tasks (BringCube, CleanTable, GatherSpheres, ScoreGoal), VLMGINEER achieves the highest possible reward on all four and does so more reliably on average, with a 24.3% average normalized improvement. Finally, the ablation against brute-force VLM sampling (no evolution, same 8000-sample budget) shows a 119.2% average improvement, confirming that the evolutionary refinement loop—rather than raw VLM sampling—is the key driver of performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

VLMGINEER extends the large-model-guided evolutionary paradigm established by Eureka (LLM-guided reward design) and Robomorph/LASER (LLM-guided morphology evolution) to the joint co-design of tool geometry and manipulation actions for robotic manipulation. The key departure from prior work is twofold: (1) it uses VLMs with visual context rather than text-only LLMs, and (2) it eliminates all human-in-the-loop parameter specification (no task-specific templates, no predefined design spaces), making it fully autonomous. Unlike prior tool-design methods that optimize a handful of manually chosen parameters or require fixed control policies, VLMGINEER represents tools in URDF and simultaneously evolves both the tool and its action waypoints, leveraging the VLM's inductive biases as a substitute for handcrafted mutation/crossover operators.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully automatic framework that co-designs tools and actions from scratch without human-specified design parameters, and it introduces ROBOTOOLBENCH, a 12-task simulation benchmark purpose-built for evaluating tool-design methods. The demonstrated sim-to-real transfer (3D-printed tools on a Franka arm) validates practical relevance, though it is limited to three relatively simple tasks. Key open questions include: whether the approach generalizes to dynamic or force-based manipulation, whether multitask or compositional tool design is feasible, how to incorporate manufacturing constraints into the evolutionary loop, and whether the discrete-waypoint action representation can be replaced by learned closed-loop policies for more complex scenarios. The finding that evolutionary search yields a 119% improvement over equal-budget brute-force sampling underscores that structured exploration is critical, not merely more VLM queries.
