## Problem

Existing adversarial attacks on Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) face a fundamental trade-off: white-box attacks require full-model gradients and task-specific labels, making costs scale with the number of downstream tasks, while black-box attacks rely on proxy models and large perturbation budgets that struggle under minimal perturbation constraints. The paper identifies that the vision encoder, being a shared and central component across all LVLM tasks, has not been fully exploited as an attack target, and asks whether a vision-encoder-only gray-box attack can achieve efficient, transferable, and downstream-agnostic adversarial perturbations under small perturbation budgets.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260617/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a single RGB image and the weights of a vision encoder (e.g., CLIP-ViT-L/14) as the only accessible model parameters, requiring no LLM weights, text encoder, task-specific prompts, or ground-truth labels. It produces a single adversarial image with an L∞ perturbation budget of ε=2/255 or 4/255 (up to 16/255 for cross-architecture transfer), generated via PGD optimization over 100 iterations (as few as 10 suffice). Crucially, the same adversarial image transfers across heterogeneous downstream tasks (image captioning, VQA, hallucination detection) and across different LVLM architectures without any task-specific re-optimization.

## Method

VEAttack reformulates the adversarial objective from the full LVLM pipeline to the vision encoder alone, minimizing the cosine similarity between clean and perturbed patch token features: max_{‖δ‖≤ε} −cos(f_V(v+δ), f_V(v)). A key theoretical contribution (Proposition 2) proves that perturbing the class token z_cls has a bounded, diminished effect on patch tokens z_v with ratio at most (3+ε_V)/√n_v, justifying the direct targeting of patch tokens instead. Cosine similarity is chosen over L2 or KL divergence because it holistically perturbs the semantic direction of features, ensuring effective propagation through the linear alignment layer to the LLM (guaranteed by Proposition 1's lower bound σ_min(W_a)·Δ on aligned feature deviation). The attack is executed via standard PGD on the vision encoder only, reducing the parameter space and achieving an 8-fold time reduction over white-box ensemble attacks while producing a single adversarial example that generalizes across all downstream tasks and LVLM architectures.

## Results

VEAttack achieves a 94.5% average CIDEr degradation on COCO image captioning and a 75.7% average accuracy degradation on TextVQA under a perturbation budget of ε=4/255, substantially outperforming all gray-box baselines. At ε=2/255, VEAttack reduces average CIDEr on COCO from 104.8 to 9.8 (↓90.6%) and average TextVQA accuracy from 33.3 to 12.9 (↓61.3%), compared to the next-best gray-box method (AttackVLM-ii) at 36.0 (↓65.6%) and 17.2 (↓48.3%) respectively. In terms of efficiency, VEAttack with t=50 and ε=8/255 completes full-dataset attacks in 5.3h on COCO, an 8-fold speedup over white-box Ensemble (41.9h) and a 13-fold speedup over gray-box VT-Attack with 1000 iterations (65.0h).

## Baselines & Comparisons

VEAttack decisively outperforms all three gray-box baselines (MIX.Attack, VT-Attack, AttackVLM-ii) across every task and LVLM in Table 1, with the largest margins on image captioning (e.g., 9.8 vs. 36.0 average CIDEr on COCO, a 26-point gap over AttackVLM-ii). Against white-box APGD and Ensemble, VEAttack achieves comparable or superior degradation on COCO (7.1 vs. 13.1 and 3.1 CIDEr at ε=4/255) while reducing attack time from 25.5h/41.9h to 8.5h (or 5.3h with t=50), and critically, VEAttack's adversarial examples transfer across tasks (COCO→VQAv2) whereas APGD's task-specific examples degrade only 49.4% on transferred tasks. On the extended captioning metrics (Table 8), VEAttack reduces BLEU@4 by 86.7% and SPICE by 87.1% on COCO, outperforming TeCoA's L_cls (CIDEr 41.8), FARE's L_2 (CIDEr 25.2), and QAVA (CIDEr 79.9). For image-text retrieval (Table 10), VEAttack achieves competitive ASR (99.88% TR R@1) with only 3.13 GB memory versus 9–10 GB for SGA and DRA, and leveraging the Möbius Band effect (attacking robust FARE instead of CLIP) boosts transfer R@1 on ALBEF from 16.58% to 35.35%, surpassing SGA (22.42%) and rivaling DRA (27.84%). On visual grounding (Table 12), VEAttack degrades RefCOCO Val to 27.1, outperforming AttackVLM-ii (28.9) and VT-Attack (34.5). The only scenario where VEAttack does not dominate is the POPE hallucination benchmark, where AttackVLM-ii achieves a slightly larger F1 drop (↓36.9% vs. ↓33.5%), though VEAttack still drives the score below the 50% threshold.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves out a new sub-formulation within the well-studied adversarial attack landscape for LVLMs: a gray-box, vision-encoder-only, downstream-agnostic attack that requires no text modality, no LLM access, and no task-specific labels. It departs from prior gray-box attacks (VT-Attack, MIX.Attack, AttackVLM-ii) that still leverage text encoders and textual information, and from white-box attacks (APGD, Ensemble) that are inherently task-specific. The theoretical grounding draws on robust CLIP work (FARE, TeCoA) and the observation that vision encoders serve as shared backbones across LVLMs, but the key conceptual departure is targeting patch token features rather than class token embeddings, justified by a novel Proposition 2 showing the reduced propagation effect of class-token perturbations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 8× and 13× efficiency gains over white-box and gray-box baselines make large-scale adversarial evaluation of LVLMs practically feasible, and the downstream-agnostic nature means a single adversarial example transfers across captioning, VQA, hallucination, and retrieval tasks. The Möbius Band observation—that robust vision encoders paradoxically yield more transferable adversarial examples—challenges the straightforward 'train robust encoder → secure LVLM' defense strategy and opens a critical open question about decoupling robustness from attack transferability. The paper explicitly acknowledges the absence of a defense mechanism, leaving the most important practical question (how to mitigate vision-encoder-only attacks) unresolved.
