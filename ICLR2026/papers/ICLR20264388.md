## Problem

Learning from dynamic tensor streams faces two coupled challenges: existing temporal tensor models impose restrictive assumptions that cannot capture multi-scale, non-stationary entity dynamics, and processing every observation in high-velocity streams is computationally prohibitive without a principled mechanism to identify which samples are most informative. No prior method jointly addresses expressive continuous-time modeling and adaptive, long-term-utility-aware sample selection for streaming tensor decomposition.

## Input / Output

SONATA consumes a single-pass stream of timestamped tensor entries, where each observation consists of a value y_n, a timestamp t_n, and a K-tuple of entity indices (l_{n,1},...,l_{n,K}). It produces dynamic R-dimensional factor trajectories for every entity in every mode, predicted tensor entry values, a compact coreset (typically 5–10% of total observations, with the algorithm autonomously converging to an effective size well below the budget M_max), and full posterior covariance estimates for each embedding. The method handles irregular time intervals natively through its continuous-time SDE formulation, and the coreset is pruned adaptively to maintain the budget constraint.

## Method

SONATA models each entity's embedding as a linear projection of a latent state governed by a stochastic differential equation whose parameters (F, H, P_∞) are derived from a Matérn temporal kernel (e.g., Matérn-3/2 with state dimension 2R), yielding smooth multi-scale trajectories via a discrete-time Kalman filter. A synergistic coreset selection mechanism scores each incoming observation on four criteria—predicted embedding uncertainty, influence (cosine similarity of CP interaction vectors to existing coreset members), novelty (new entity indices plus exponential time-decay), and a Martingale-based information increment (tanh-scaled squared prediction error)—and optimizes long-term coreset composition via a Bellman equation with a discount factor. Posterior inference for the nonlinear CP observation model is performed through Expectation Propagation, which converts Gaussian site messages into pseudo-observations for the Kalman filter, enabling exact linear-state updates while handling the multiplicative nonlinearity of tensor entries. Coreset members receive full weight in EP message updates while non-coreset points are down-weighted, focusing learning on the most informative data without requiring deep neural networks.

## Results

On four real-world temporal tensor datasets, SONATA achieves the best RMSE across all four: 0.089 on CA Traffic 30K, 0.115 on ServerRoom, 0.237 on BeijingAir, and 0.414 on FitRecord (R=5, averaged over 10 runs). The headline improvement is a 61.5% RMSE reduction over the second-best streaming method SFTL-CP on CA Traffic (0.231→0.089, p<0.05). SONATA also achieves the best MAE on three of four datasets (0.015, 0.083, 0.240), with SFTL-CP slightly ahead on BeijingAir MAE (0.150 vs. 0.156). Per-iteration runtime is 0.338s, substantially faster than THIS-ODE (7.190s) while delivering higher accuracy.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SONATA outperforms all 12 baselines on RMSE across all four datasets. Against the strongest streaming competitor SFTL-CP, SONATA reduces RMSE by 61.5% on CA Traffic (0.089 vs. 0.231), 28.6% on ServerRoom (0.115 vs. 0.161), 4.4% on BeijingAir (0.237 vs. 0.248), and 2.4% on FitRecord (0.414 vs. 0.424). Against static continuous-time methods, SONATA beats the best static baseline (NONFAT) by 82.2% on CA Traffic (0.089 vs. 0.501) and 1.7% on ServerRoom (0.115 vs. 0.117), while also processing data in a single pass rather than requiring multiple epochs. Among streaming baselines, POST and ADF-CP are substantially outperformed (e.g., 11.2× RMSE gap on CA Traffic vs. POST), and BASS-Tucker performs worst overall. On MAE, SONATA is best on CA Traffic (0.015), ServerRoom (0.083), and FitRecord (0.240), but SFTL-CP edges it slightly on BeijingAir (0.150 vs. 0.156), the only dataset where SONATA does not achieve the top MAE.

## Relation to Prior Work

SONATA sits at the intersection of two established but previously disconnected lines of work: continuous-time tensor decomposition (CT-CP, BCTT, NONFAT, THIS-ODE) and streaming tensor factorization (POST, ADF-CP, BASS-Tucker, SFTL-CP). It departs from prior streaming methods by replacing indiscriminate processing or heuristic sampling with a principled coreset selection mechanism guided by Bellman-equation optimization, and from prior coreset work (Chhaya et al. 2020, Huggins et al. 2016) by making the coreset temporally adaptive and applicable to general (non-symmetric) tensors. The temporal modeling component draws on LDS formulations of Matérn kernels (Hartikainen & Särkkä, Särkkä & Svensson), which prior tensor methods either ignored or handled via simpler discretization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SONATA provides the first principled coreset-based streaming tensor decomposition that jointly addresses modeling expressiveness and computational efficiency, achieving ~25× computational savings with negligible accuracy loss (RMSE 0.1290 vs 0.1293) and demonstrating autonomous coreset convergence to an effective size. Key limitations include the assumption of Gaussian observation noise, linear LDS dynamics from Matérn kernels, and relatively consistent temporal intervals; performance under highly bursty or non-Gaussian regimes remains underexplored. Open questions include whether the Bellman-based selection generalizes to higher-order interactions beyond CP structure, how the method scales to tensors with thousands of entities per mode, and whether the coreset criteria transfer to non-tensor streaming problems.
