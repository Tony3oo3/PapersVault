## Problem

Existing text-to-image diffusion models lack the ability to simultaneously control both the identity of multiple subjects and their spatial arrangement within a scene. Prior methods either personalize a single subject, handle structural control without identity preservation, or require iterative multi-pass generation that incurs high runtime costs and compounding quality degradation. No prior work enables single-pass, multi-subject identity-preserved generation guided by both structural and spatial constraints at varying granularities.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264782/cropped_page0_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a text prompt, a multi-subject identity image (a vertical concatenation of N subject crops, each at fixed resolution H'×W'×3), and a single 3-channel spatial control image of shape H×W×3. The spatial control encodes routing information via subject-specific pixel intensities (10, 20, 30, …) in two compositing orders (ascending and descending) plus a depth channel, supporting granularities from precise per-pixel masks and depth down to 2D or 3D bounding boxes. The output is a single generated image in which all N subjects appear with preserved identity at their specified spatial locations.

## Method

SIGMA-GEN fine-tunes Flux.1 Kontext (a diffusion transformer) via LoRA, adopting OminiControl's unified attention mechanism where noisy image latents, text tokens, identity image tokens, and spatial control tokens are concatenated along the token dimension for full multi-modal cross-attention. The key representation decomposes spatial control into a routing image (assigning each subject a unique grayscale intensity in a mask, composited bidirectionally to resolve occlusion ambiguity under coarse controls) and a structure image (depth), packed into a single 3-channel condition. Identity is provided as a vertically stacked image of N subject crops, so each H'×W' block corresponds positionally to a routing region. Training uses the newly introduced SIGMA-SET27K dataset (27k images, 100k+ identities, up to 10 subjects per image) generated by an automated pipeline combining LLM prompts, text-to-image synthesis, grounded segmentation, depth estimation, and Flux-Kontext reposing. A three-stage curriculum (≤4 subjects → 3+ → 4+) with random sampling across control modalities (precise masks+depth, 3D bbox+depth, 2D bbox) yields a single model that handles all granularities.

## Results

In the multi-subject setting with precise masks and depth, SIGMA-GEN achieves DINO-I 74.54, SigLIP-I 77.82, MUSIQ 73.21, and Depth MSE 26.35, substantially outperforming the iterative Insert Anything* baseline (MUSIQ 48.86, Depth MSE 203.4) while running in a single forward pass. With bounding-box control, SIGMA-GEN improves DINO-I by 8.6 points (71.90 vs. 63.28) and SigLIP-I by 4.1 points (73.15 vs. 69.06) over MSDiffusion in the multi-subject case. In the single-subject mask+depth setting, SIGMA-GEN attains the best identity scores (DINO-I 81.04, SigLIP-I 83.99) and the lowest depth MSE (70.2) among all baselines. The paper reports 4× faster generation than iterative insertion when synthesizing scenes with five or more distinct subjects.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against OminiControl (depth-only control), SIGMA-GEN wins on every metric in the single-subject setting, improving DINO-I by 10.3 points (81.04 vs. 70.78) and reducing depth MSE by more than half (70.2 vs. 163.9). Compared to Flux Kontext* (adapted with a depth ControlNet), SIGMA-GEN gains 1.1 points on DINO-I, 0.8 on SigLIP-I, and 5.9 on MUSIQ, while achieving 37% lower depth MSE. Against Insert Anything* (iterative insertion with depth), SIGMA-GEN dominates in the multi-subject regime: MUSIQ jumps from 48.86 to 73.21 (+24.4 points) and depth MSE drops from 203.4 to 26.35, reflecting the quality degradation and spatial drift inherent to sequential insertion. Versus MSDiffusion (bounding-box layout), SIGMA-GEN improves DINO-I by 8.6 points and SigLIP-I by 4.1 points in the multi-subject bbox setting, while also matching or exceeding its image-quality scores. In the single-subject mask+depth case, SIGMA-GEN slightly trails Insert Anything* on CLIP-IQA (15.91 vs. 16.01) but leads on all identity and depth-alignment metrics, and it marginally loses to UniCombine on MUSIQ (70.87 vs. 71.47) while winning decisively on identity preservation.

## Relation to Prior Work

SIGMA-GEN unifies three previously separate research threads: structure-conditioned generation (ControlNet, OminiControl, T2I-Adapter), subject personalization via reference images (IP-Adapter, Flux Kontext, Insert Anything), and multi-subject layout control (MSDiffusion, MultiTwin). It departs from prior work by being the first to jointly enforce multiple subject identities and structural spatial constraints (position, orientation, occlusion) in a single diffusion forward pass, rather than requiring iterative insertion or per-subject optimization. Architecturally, it builds directly on the Flux.1 Kontext DiT backbone and OminiControl's unified multi-modal attention, but introduces a novel routing-and-structure representation that encodes all spatial controls into a single 3-channel image, enabling one model to handle masks, depth, 2D boxes, and 3D boxes interchangeably.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant for creative workflows: a single model that accepts any granularity of spatial control (from coarse 2D boxes to pixel-level depth) while preserving up to 10 subject identities in one pass removes the need for iterative pipelines that compound quality degradation. The SIGMA-SET27K dataset (27k images, 100k+ identities) addresses a key data bottleneck for multi-subject personalization that prior datasets (MultiTwin: ≤2 subjects; virtual try-on: ≤4) could not fill. Open questions include whether the approach generalizes to real-world reference images (vs. synthetic reposed crops), whether it transfers to other base models beyond Flux.1 Kontext, and how performance scales beyond 10 subjects. The reliance on a fully synthetic data pipeline also raises questions about domain gap to real photography.
