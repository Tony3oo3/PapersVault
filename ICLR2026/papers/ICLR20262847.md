## Problem

While MLLMs have achieved strong performance on conventional 2D image and video benchmarks, their ability to comprehend immersive 360°×180° omnidirectional images (ODIs) remains largely unexplored. Existing ODI benchmarks are limited by low resolution, indoor-only scenes, narrow auto-generated question types, and egocentric-only viewpoints, making them insufficient for evaluating the spatial reasoning demands of real-world VR/AR and embodied intelligence applications.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262847/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The benchmark takes equirectangular-projection (ERP) omnidirectional images at up to 12K resolution paired with first-person immersive text questions (e.g., 'What is [A] on my right doing?') and produces text answers evaluated under both close-ended (4-option multiple choice or yes/no) and open-ended (LLM-judged) settings. For Omni-CoT, the model additionally consumes intermediate reasoning artifacts: six perspective-view captions with orientation labels and narrow-FoV crop images with spherical coordinate metadata (center θ,φ and FoV), which are generated on-the-fly from the ERP image during inference.

## Method

The paper introduces ODI-Bench (2,000 real-world ODIs, 4,254 QA pairs, 10 tasks spanning general-level and spatial-level understanding) and Omni-CoT, a training-free chain-of-thought framework with three sequential steps. First, viewpoint-guided answering projects the ERP image into six perspective views, generates per-view captions via the MLLM, and integrates them with orientation labels to build a coarse global scene understanding. Second, crop cue grounding and refinement prompts the MLLM to identify relevant regions in the ERP image, converts bounding boxes to narrow-FoV spherical crops using a closed-form ERP-to-spherical mapping (clipping FoV to 30°–120°), then queries the model to filter out irrelevant crops. Third, response refinement feeds the original ODI, viewpoint captions, and validated crop cues back to the model alongside its initial answer, prompting a revised response. This pipeline consistently improves spatial-task accuracy (e.g., +25–33 points on allocentric view orientation) without any parameter updates.

## Results

On ODI-Bench (close-ended), the best proprietary model o3 achieves 62.62% overall accuracy, while the best open-source models (InternVL3-78B at 59.43% and Qwen2.5-VL-72B at 56.91%) trail by 3–6 points. Spatial-level tasks are substantially harder than general-level tasks: allocentric view orientation (AVO) peaks at only 56.20% (o3) and scene simulation (SS) at 46.60%, barely above the Blind GPT-4o baseline (36.39% overall). The proposed Omni-CoT framework improves o3's overall score from 62.62% to 70.03% (+7.41) and GPT-4o from 55.79% to 62.08% (+6.17), with the largest gains on AVO (+26.40 for o3) and counting (+6.00 for o3).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Among the 20 benchmarked MLLMs, o3 leads on close-ended overall (62.62%) and open-ended overall (49.53%), outperforming GPT-4o (55.79% / 42.91%) and Gemini-2.0-flash (57.12% / 36.42%). Open-source models InternVL3-78B (59.43%) and Qwen2.5-VL-72B (56.91%) approach but do not surpass the top proprietary models on close-ended, though Qwen2.5-VL-72B and InternVL3-78B exceed GPT-4o on several individual tasks (e.g., HA: 77.38% and 79.18% vs. 67.76%). All models struggle most on allocentric view orientation (best: 56.20% for o3) and scene simulation (best: 46.60% for o3), where performance is only marginally above the Blind GPT-4o baseline (29.14% and 31.60% respectively). For the Omni-CoT method, it consistently outperforms both multi-view image input and video input baselines across all three tested models (GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-flash, InternVL2.5-8B); for example, on GPT-4o, Omni-CoT achieves 62.08% overall vs. 56.01% (multi-view) and no video baseline is reported for GPT-4o, while on Gemini-2.0-flash it reaches 63.89% vs. 54.63% (multi-view) and 55.34% (video). The ablation confirms that viewpoint guiding is the dominant contributor (e.g., +25.03 on AVO for o3), while crop grounding and refinement add further gains on counting and OCR tasks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper carves new ground at the intersection of MLLM evaluation and omnidirectional imaging. While general VLM benchmarks (MMBench, MM-Vet) and spatial understanding benchmarks (VSI-Bench, SSRBench) have matured, prior ODI-specific benchmarks (VQA 360°, OSR-Bench, Dense360-Bench) remain limited in resolution, scene diversity, and task coverage. ODI-Bench departs from these by introducing a unified 10-task suite spanning both general and spatial understanding at high resolution (up to 12K), with both close-ended and open-ended evaluation. The proposed Omni-CoT framework repurposes chain-of-thought reasoning for the ERP projection challenge, using cubemap-derived viewpoint captions and spherical-coordinate crop grounding as textual/visual cues rather than additional image inputs, distinguishing it from both training-based spatial reasoning approaches and naive multi-view input strategies.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The benchmark reveals that even the strongest proprietary model (o3) achieves only ~63% close-ended accuracy, and allocentric spatial tasks (view orientation, scene simulation) hover near random chance across all 20 models — a critical finding for embodied AI and VR applications that rely on perspective-taking in 360° environments. The observation that structured text-based reasoning (Omni-CoT) outperforms more intuitive multi-view image or video inputs challenges the assumption that simply providing more visual context helps MLLMs interpret distorted projections. Open questions include whether fine-tuning on ODI data would close the spatial reasoning gap, whether the 2,000-image scale is sufficient for robust evaluation, and how these findings transfer to video-based omnidirectional understanding and vision-language-action models.
