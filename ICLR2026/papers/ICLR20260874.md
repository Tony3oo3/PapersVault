## Problem

Existing locate-and-edit knowledge editing methods (ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit, WISE) rely on multi-step backpropagation, which is infeasible on commercial mobile devices because NPUs only support forward passes, BP operations are unstable under quantization, and full-precision training requires over 40 GB of memory for a 3B model. This confines LLM personalization to cloud computation, undermining the privacy and offline availability that motivate on-device deployment in the first place.

## Input / Output

The method takes a single target fact (subject–relation–object triple) combined with a set of randomly sampled prefix sentences to form editing inputs, along with a pre-trained 3B-parameter LLM (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct or Llama3.2-3B-Instruct) and key covariance statistics extracted from model activations. It produces a rank-one update to the MLP down-projection weight matrix in the target layer, such that the subject token's key vector maps to the new value vector, yielding an edited model that recalls the injected fact while preserving unrelated knowledge.

## Method

MobiEdit replaces backpropagation with a zeroth-order central-difference gradient estimator that requires only forward passes, making it compatible with mobile NPUs that lack backward-pass support. A mixed-precision quantization strategy (W8A16) quantizes all weights to INT8 except the editing layer and its preceding linear layer, which remain in floating-point; the paper proves theoretically that this forward-only estimation accumulates quantization noise only linearly in depth, versus exponentially for BP. Two system-level optimizations further reduce cost: prefix activation reusing caches KV-cache and MLP activations for the fixed prefix tokens across editing steps (recomputing only fact tokens), and an early-stopping mechanism adaptively terminates editing once the model produces the target output above a confidence threshold. The optimized value vector is then applied via a closed-form rank-one weight update (W + Λ(C⁻¹k*)ᵀ) to inject the knowledge into the MLP's key-value memory.

## Results

On Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (ZsRE), MobiEdit achieves 80.1 edit success, 72.6 locality, and 51.4 portability while consuming only 6.2 GB memory, 0.018 kJ energy, and 25.5 minutes per edit. On Llama3.2-3B-Instruct, it reaches 88.3 edit success with 5.0 GB memory and 0.017 kJ energy. Compared to BP-based baselines (ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit, WISE), MobiEdit reduces memory by 7.1×, energy by 15.8×, and latency by 3.4×, making it the only method that fits within the 16 GB RAM budget of commercial smartphones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MobiEdit dramatically outperforms all four locate-and-edit baselines (ROME, MEMIT, AlphaEdit, WISE) on resource efficiency: it uses 6.2 GB vs. 46+ GB memory (7.4× reduction), 0.023 kJ vs. 25.13 kJ energy per edit on K60 (~1090× reduction), and 1903 s vs. 4544 s latency (2.4× speedup). WISE is the slowest baseline at 11,359 s per edit on K60, making MobiEdit 5.97× faster. In terms of edit quality, MobiEdit trades some accuracy (80.1 edit success on Qwen vs. ROME's higher FP32 score of 96) for feasibility on-device; however, in a realistic 8-hour editing window, MobiEdit successfully edits 14 facts versus only 2–5 for baselines. Against the on-device fine-tuning method XPerT (MobiSys 2025), MobiEdit achieves 1.7× speedup and 10× lower power consumption (0.27 W vs. 2.6 W) on the same Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 hardware. The paper also shows that quantization (W8A16) degrades ROME's edit success from 96 to 41, while MobiEdit only drops from 86 to 80, confirming its robustness advantage under the quantized conditions required for NPU execution.

## Relation to Prior Work

MobiEdit builds directly on the locate-and-edit paradigm established by ROME and MEMIT, which formulate knowledge injection as a rank-one update to MLP key-value memory, and extends it with null-space constraints (AlphaEdit) and dynamic routing (WISE). Its key departure is replacing the multi-step backpropagation optimizer with a zeroth-order central-difference gradient estimator, connecting to the broader BP-free training literature (MeZO, FwdLLM) but applying it for the first time to factual knowledge editing. The NPU-friendly mixed-precision quantization strategy—keeping only the editing layer and its predecessor in floating point while quantizing everything else to INT8/INT16—is a novel co-design choice specific to the forward-only editing workflow, distinct from standard post-training quantization or QAT approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MobiEdit is the first system to make LLM knowledge editing practically feasible on commercial smartphones, unlocking privacy-preserving, offline personalization that was previously confined to cloud servers. The 7.1× memory and 15.8× energy reductions are substantial, but the quality trade-off (edit success dropping from ~96 to ~80) and the ~20× increase in optimization steps remain meaningful limitations, particularly for complex or multi-fact edits. Open questions include whether the approach scales gracefully to 7B+ models on mobile, how edits accumulate over long-term use without degradation, and whether the prefix-activation-reusing staleness issue can be mitigated without sacrificing the computational savings that make the method practical.
