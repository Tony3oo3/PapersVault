## Problem

Existing structured pruning methods for Mixture-of-Experts models exhibit suboptimal performance and unstable degradation across model architectures, calibration data sources, and calibration sample sizes. General pruning methods ignore MoE's sparse computation scheme, while expert pruning methods rely solely on access frequency and lack mechanisms to recover the performance loss caused by removing experts.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261367/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a pre-trained MoE model and a small calibration dataset (100–1000 samples from Zyda-2 or C4) and produces a compressed MoE model in which a specified fraction of experts (25% or 50%) are replaced by constant novice vectors. Each novice is a single d-dimensional vector (the unbiased mean output of the pruned expert over the calibration set) that requires no computation with the input token, so the memory and compute overhead at inference is nearly identical to directly deleting the expert while preserving a weighted contribution in the MoE summation.

## Method

MoNE identifies redundant experts per layer using a fused redundancy score that multiplies two metrics: (1) a variance-based score, the L2 norm of the unbiased output variance of each expert over the calibration set, and (2) a frequency-based score, the average routing score of the expert when it is among the top-k selected. Experts with the lowest fused scores (low usage and stable outputs) are pruned. Each pruned expert is then replaced by a 'novice'—a constant vector equal to the expert's unbiased mean output over the calibration set—which is the closed-form solution to minimizing the L2 output discrepancy. The key insight is that low-frequency experts contribute little to final outputs, and low-variance experts can be approximated by a constant with minimal error; the novice replacement compensates for the knowledge loss that direct removal would cause, yielding more robust compression across architectures and calibration conditions.

## Results

At 25% expert pruning with 100 calibration samples (Zyda-2), MoNE achieves average zero-shot accuracy of 61.04 on OLMoE, 69.10 on Moonlight, 66.12 on DeepSeek-V2-Lite, 71.75 on Qwen2-57B-A14B, and 72.69 on Qwen3-30B-A3B across nine downstream tasks. The headline result is a maximum improvement of 2.72 points over the best baseline (FLAP) on OLMoE, and a minimal accuracy drop of only 0.14 points for Qwen2-57B-A14B (71.89 → 71.75). At 50% pruning, MoNE still leads all baselines by a significant margin (e.g., 61.27 vs. 58.23 on Moonlight, 66.61 vs. 65.49 on Qwen2-57B-A14B).

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoNE consistently outperforms all four baselines across all five MoE models at 25% pruning. Against the general structured pruning baselines, MoNE's advantage is most pronounced: Angular (layer pruning) suffers catastrophic drops (e.g., 52.33 vs. 61.04 on OLMoE, a 8.71-point gap), while FLAP (channel pruning) is the strongest general baseline but still trails MoNE by 2.72 points on OLMoE and 2.57 on DeepSeek-V2-Lite. Among expert-pruning baselines, RS (routing-score-based removal) and MC-SMoE (expert merging) both underperform MoNE on every model; the gap is largest on OLMoE where MoNE leads RS by 10.97 points and MC-SMoE by 8.27 points. On the largest model Qwen2-57B-A14B, FLAP is nearly competitive (71.78 vs. 71.75), but MoNE still edges it out and achieves the smallest drop from the original (0.14 vs. 0.11 for FLAP). At 50% pruning, MoNE's lead widens further, outperforming the best baseline by up to 3.04 points (Moonlight: 61.27 vs. 58.23 for RS).

## Relation to Prior Work

MoNE extends the expert pruning line of work initiated by MC-SMoE (Li et al., 2024) and RS (He et al., 2024), which identify redundant experts primarily through routing scores or access frequency. It departs from these methods in two key ways: (1) it introduces output variance as a complementary redundancy signal alongside frequency, arguing that frequency alone fails to capture experts whose outputs are stable but infrequently accessed; and (2) it replaces pruned experts with constant 'novice' vectors (unbiased mean-output estimates) rather than simply removing or merging them, thereby recovering some of the pruned expert's contribution at negligible memory cost. The approach also inherits the layer-wise pruning formulation from SparseGPT and FLAP, but applies it specifically to the expert granularity of MoE architectures.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE models (DeepSeek, Qwen, OLMoE) become the dominant architecture for large-scale LLMs, reducing their memory footprint without retraining is practically critical for deployment on existing hardware. MoNE offers a simple, calibration-only method that consistently outperforms baselines across architectures and data sources, making it a low-barrier tool for practitioners. However, the paper's own Appendix A reveals a significant limitation: calibration with pretraining data causes severe degradation (up to 49%) on specialized tasks like math and GSM8K, and task-specific calibration is needed to recover performance—highlighting an open question about how to bridge the gap between general and task-specific calibration. The finding that larger models tolerate more aggressive pruning (25% vs. 16%) while maintaining accuracy also suggests that expert-level redundancy scales with model size, a property worth further investigation.
