## Problem

Recovering missing edge flows in a network while exactly respecting local conservation laws (Bf = c) is a fundamental inverse problem in transportation, energy, and mobility. Prior approaches either enforce hard conservation with a single global regularizer, apply per-edge diagonal feature priors, or ignore the algebraic symmetry of the conservation constraints altogether. The gap this paper addresses is that the admissible divergence-free adjustments form a structured Abelian group determined by the incidence matrix and sensor mask, and no prior method exploits this group structure with a learned, context-aware selection mechanism.

## Input / Output

The method takes a directed or undirected graph with per-edge feature vectors (18-d for Traffic, 14-d for Power, 9-d for Bike), a partially observed flow vector where only a subset of edges carry noisy sensor readings (e.g., 38% in Traffic), and a known nodal injection vector c specifying sources and sinks. It outputs the full flow vector with missing entries imputed, guaranteeing that observed edges are left exactly unchanged (a hard operational constraint) while the balance residual Bf̃ − c remains small. The output is structured as an anchor (minimum-norm balanced completion) plus a divergence-free group-action correction plus a small Tikhonov-refined adjustment on missing edges only.

## Method

FLOWSYMM is a three-stage pipeline trained end-to-end via implicit bilevel optimization. First, it anchors the partial observation to the closest balanced flow via a Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse solve, guaranteeing Bf⁽⁰⁾ = c. Second, it constructs a truncated orthonormal basis U ∈ ℝ^{m×k} (k=256) for the Abelian group of divergence-free adjustments that vanish on observed edges, obtained via SVD of a projector P_𝒜 = P_bal − P_bal S_obsᵀ(S_obs P_bal S_obsᵀ)† S_obs P_bal. Third, a two-layer GATv2 encoder produces per-edge embeddings, which score each basis vector through compatibility scores q_{e,i} = (w_iᵀ H_e)|u_e⁽ⁱ⁾|; a softmax over the k scores yields attention weights α_θ that select a context-dependent combination Δ = Uα_θ of group actions. A final feature-conditioned Tikhonov refinement (a single SPD solve with learnable λ) allows a small controlled deviation to absorb sensor noise, and all parameters are trained by reverse-mode implicit differentiation through the inner solver, requiring only two Cholesky solves per fold per outer step.

## Results

FLOWSYMM achieves the best RMSE, MAE, and Pearson correlation across all three benchmarks. On Traffic, it reaches RMSE 0.057 and MAE 0.028; on Power, RMSE 0.026 and MAE 0.010; on Bike, RMSE 0.029 and MAE 0.014. Relative to the previous state-of-the-art Bil-GCN, FLOWSYMM reduces RMSE by approximately 8% (Traffic), 10% (Power), and 9% (Bike), while lowering MAE by up to 16% and improving correlation by up to 0.04.

## Baselines & Comparisons

FLOWSYMM outperforms all nine baselines on every metric across all three datasets. Against the previous state-of-the-art Bil-GCN (Silva et al., ICLR 2021), FLOWSYMM reduces RMSE from 0.062→0.057 (Traffic), 0.029→0.026 (Power), and 0.032→0.029 (Bike), with corresponding MAE drops of 16%, 9%, and 12%. The physics-only Div baseline (Jia et al., KDD 2019) is consistently weaker, e.g. RMSE 0.071 on Traffic vs. 0.057 for FLOWSYMM. Pure data-driven GNNs—GCN (Kipf & Welling, ICLR 2017), GIN (Xu et al., 2018), and EGNN (Satorras et al., 2022)—perform substantially worse on Power (RMSE 0.065–0.070 vs. 0.026) and Bike (RMSE 0.057–0.063 vs. 0.029), confirming that conservation-aware structure is critical in these domains. The hybrid variants (MLP-Div, GCN-Div) improve over their data-driven counterparts but still trail FLOWSYMM by 3–10% in RMSE, indicating that the group-action basis and attention mechanism provide gains beyond simply combining a GNN prior with a divergence penalty.

## Relation to Prior Work

FLOWSYMM builds most directly on the bilevel-learned diagonal regularizer framework of Silva et al. (2021), replacing per-edge independent penalties with a coupled, physics-aware group-action basis that spans the divergence-free null space of the incidence matrix restricted to missing edges. It departs from the equivariant GNN literature (EGNN, SE(3)-Transformer, Tensor Field Networks) by encoding algebraic symmetries (conservation + sensor invariance) rather than geometric ones (rotation, translation, permutation). The implicit bilevel training loop follows the standard reverse-mode implicit differentiation recipe (Domke 2012; Franceschi 2017/2018; Gu et al. 2020), applied here to a convex Tikhonov inner problem whose SPD structure enables exact hyper-gradients via a single Cholesky solve.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The group-action framing makes every learned correction an interpretable, divergence-free redistribution, giving practitioners a transparent handle on *how* the model adjusts flows while guaranteeing observed sensors are never modified—a hard operational constraint that soft-penalty methods cannot enforce. Key open questions include extending to temporal dynamics (the model is single-shot), jointly inferring unknown nodal injections c, and scaling the dense O(n³) anchor solve to city- or country-scale networks via iterative solvers. The paper also notes that systematic sensor-placement biases (e.g., urban clustering) could degrade performance under non-random masking, a realistic concern for deployment.
