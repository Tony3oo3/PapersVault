## Problem

Existing image editing models, despite strong instruction-following, frequently hallucinate objects or distort scene geometry when performing edits that require physical consistency—such as simulating autonomous driving events or robot manipulation. This gap is critical for world simulation applications where edited outputs must preserve object identity, geometry, and physical plausibility. The root cause is architectural: current approaches are purely data-driven and lack explicit mechanisms to enforce temporal continuity between the source and edited scenes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260341/cropped_page1_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a reference RGB image and a natural-language editing instruction as input. The reference image is encoded as the first latent frame of a video sequence, while the target (edited) image is repeated four times to match the video VAE's 4× temporal compression, producing two temporal latents. At inference with temporal reasoning enabled, the model additionally produces intermediate reasoning frames (6 latent frames, 24 pixel-space frames) that visualize the model's 'thinking trajectory' before collapsing to the final single edited image.

## Method

ChronoEdit repurposes a pretrained image-to-video diffusion model (Wan2.1-I2V-14B or Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B) for image editing by reframing each edit as a two-frame video generation problem, leveraging the video model's inherent temporal prior to preserve object fidelity. Training jointly supervises on 2.6M image-editing pairs (reinterpreted as two-frame videos) and 1.4M curated synthetic videos, where intermediate video frames serve as temporal reasoning tokens that teach the model to plan plausible transitions. At inference, a two-stage denoising procedure first denoises the full sequence (input + reasoning tokens + output) for N_r high-noise steps, then discards the reasoning tokens and refines only the target frame for the remaining steps, balancing physical plausibility with computational cost. A DMD-based distillation further compresses inference to 8 steps (ChronoEdit-Turbo) with minimal quality loss.

## Results

On the ImgEdit Basic-Edit Suite (734 test cases, 9 editing tasks), ChronoEdit-14B achieves the highest overall GPT-4.1 score of 4.42, surpassing the strongest open-source baseline FLUX.1 Kontext [Dev] (3.52) by +0.90 and the proprietary GPT Image 1 [High] (4.20) by +0.22. On the newly introduced PBench-Edit benchmark (271 physically grounded images), ChronoEdit-14B-Think (N_r=10) reaches a state-of-the-art overall score of 4.53, with the largest gain in Action Fidelity (4.31 vs. 3.76 for Qwen-Image and 2.88 for FLUX.1 Kontext [Dev]). The 8-step distilled ChronoEdit-14B-Turbo runs 6× faster (5.0s vs. 30.4s per image) while retaining a score of 4.13, still outperforming FLUX.1 Kontext [Pro] (4.00).

## Baselines & Comparisons

ChronoEdit-14B (4.42) outperforms all 13 baselines on ImgEdit, with the largest margins over extract (+2.51 vs. FLUX.1 Kontext [Dev]) and remove (+1.63 vs. FLUX.1 Kontext [Dev]), while matching or exceeding the 20B Qwen-Image (4.27) across every task category. On the physically grounded PBench-Edit, ChronoEdit-14B-Think (N_r=10) achieves 4.53 overall, beating BAGEL (4.32), Qwen-Image (4.26), and FLUX.1 Kontext [Dev] (3.83), with the most pronounced advantage in Action Fidelity (4.31 vs. 3.76/2.88). The 2B variant with temporal reasoning (ChronoEdit-2B-Think, 4.44) nearly matches the full 14B model (4.43), indicating that the video prior and reasoning mechanism transfer effectively to smaller backbones. The distilled 8-step ChronoEdit-14B-Turbo (4.13) retains competitive quality at 6× lower latency, still exceeding FLUX.1 Kontext [Pro] (4.00) and all open-source baselines. Notably, ChronoEdit-14B without temporal reasoning already surpasses all baselines on PBench-Edit, suggesting the pretrained video prior alone provides substantial physical-consistency benefits even in the standard two-frame editing mode.

## Relation to Prior Work

ChronoEdit repurposes pretrained video generative models (Wan2.1, Cosmos) for image editing by reframing an edit pair as a two-frame video, a conceptual bridge that prior work (Bagel, UniReal, OmniGen) explored only at the data-curation level. The key departure is the introduction of temporal reasoning tokens at inference time—intermediate latent frames that are jointly denoised for a few high-noise steps before being discarded—going beyond the purely data-driven continuity that existing editing models rely on. The rectified-flow training objective and DMD-based step distillation are inherited directly from the video-generation and fast-synthesis literatures, respectively, rather than being novel in isolation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Physical consistency in edited outputs is a prerequisite for using image editing as a simulation tool in autonomous driving and robotics, yet prior benchmarks (ImgEdit, MagicBrush) evaluate only visual fidelity and instruction following. PBench-Edit fills this gap, and the temporal-reasoning inference stage offers a rare interpretability window into how an editing model constructs its output. Open questions include whether the approach scales to longer multi-step edits or video-length trajectories, how sensitive performance is to the number of reasoning tokens, and whether the 2B model's near-parity with the 14B variant reflects a capacity ceiling in the video prior or a genuine efficiency win that could make physically consistent editing accessible on edge hardware.
