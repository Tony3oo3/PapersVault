## Problem

Lightweight 3D medical segmentation models (under 5M parameters) face a fundamental 'efficiency/robustness conflict': aggressive parameter reduction degrades performance on heterogeneous multimodal data and complex anatomical structures. This stems from two gaps—insufficient exploitation of 3D data's high-dimensional complexity in attention and convolution design, and underutilization of data synergy (cross-modal interaction and texture priors) due to perceived computational cost.

## Input / Output

VeloxSeg consumes paired 3D volumes (PET+CT at 96×96×96 or 128×128×64 patches, or four MRI modalities T1/T1ce/T2/FLAIR at 96×96×96) and produces voxel-wise 3D tumor segmentation masks with multi-class labels (e.g., ET/TC/WT for brain tumors, or malignant lesion boundaries for whole-body PET/CT). The model is explicitly designed for edge deployment, running on a single CPU core with 1.66M parameters and 1.79 GFLOPs, and supports both GPU and CPU inference without architectural changes.

## Method

VeloxSeg is a dual-stream CNN-Transformer encoder-decoder that separates modal fusion (convolution encoder with JLC) from modal cooperation (Transformer encoder with PWA) to avoid parameter explosion as modalities increase. Paired Window Attention (PWA) synchronously expands paired big/small windows across log(size) scales, gathering salient tokens per small window and computing a single multimodal grouped attention across all scales at near-linear complexity (~7.87% of Swin Transformer's coefficient). Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma-guided convolution (JLC) derives a theoretical lower bound on channels-per-group per stage (n, 2n, 2n, 4n) to preserve spatial adjacency among tokens, replacing data-specific pruning with a principled, dataset-agnostic group size. Spatially Decoupled Knowledge Transfer (SDKT) matches Gram matrices between self-supervised reconstruction teachers and segmentation features during training, injecting texture priors with zero inference overhead and avoiding negative transfer from ROI mismatch.

## Results

On AutoPET-II, VeloxSeg achieves 62.51% Dice (HD95: 241.08 mm), marginally surpassing the best basic model VSmTrans (62.46%) while using only 13.30% of its parameters (1.66M vs 12.48M) and 1.96% of its GFLOPs (1.79 vs 91.44). On Hecktor2022, VeloxSeg reaches 56.48% Dice, outperforming all competitors by over 3%. The model delivers 599.06 patches/s GPU throughput and 6.67 patches/s CPU throughput, representing 11× and 48× improvements over baselines, while reducing peak GPU memory by up to 20× (training) and 24× (inference) compared to standard CNN/Transformer models.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VeloxSeg outperforms all 21 baselines on both PET/CT datasets. Against the strongest basic model VSmTrans (62.46% Dice on AutoPET-II), VeloxSeg edges ahead by 0.05% while using only 13.3% of the parameters and 1.96% of the FLOPs. Among multimodal models, VeloxSeg beats H-DenseFormer (61.50%), Nestedformer (61.38%), and A2FSeg (60.86%) by 1.01–1.65% Dice on AutoPET-II, with 2.80×–7.75× higher GPU throughput. Against lightweight competitors, VeloxSeg leads by over 5% Dice on both datasets (e.g., 62.51% vs. U-RWKV's 57.18% on AutoPET-II), while also achieving the lowest GFLOPs (1.79) and highest GPU throughput (599.06 patches/s). On BraTS2021 (4 MRI modalities), VeloxSeg-C achieves 91.44% average Dice, surpassing the best lightweight model SuperLightNet (89.72%) by 1.72%. In the nnUNet training framework, VeloxSeg improves Dice by 14.2% over nnUNet (70.05% vs. 55.85% on AutoPET-II) with only 1.87% of the parameters. The only notable weakness is HD95 on AutoPET-II (241.08 mm), which is comparable to VSmTrans (223.88 mm) and higher than U-KAN (70.91 mm), suggesting room for improvement in boundary precision for whole-body scans.

## Relation to Prior Work

VeloxSeg extends the Johnson-Lindenstrauss lemma from its prior application to attention head sizing (Likhosherstov et al., 2021) into the convolution domain, deriving a theoretical lower bound on group size that preserves spatial adjacency among tokens — a departure from the empirical, dataset-specific sparsity schedules used in pruning-based lightweighting. Architecturally, it positions itself against cascaded window attention (Swin Transformer) and axial/downsampled attention by proposing Paired Window Attention, which parallelizes multi-scale and cross-modal attention in a single pass at near-linear cost. The Spatially Decoupled Knowledge Transfer repurposes the Gram matrix from neural style transfer (Gatys et al., 2016) as a spatially-invariant distillation signal, deliberately avoiding the pixel-level or shared-ROI transfer paths that prior reconstruction-to-segmentation work (Sang et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2021; Qiu et al., 2023) showed to cause negative transfer.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a practical bottleneck in clinical deployment: achieving robust multi-organ, multi-modality segmentation on a single CPU core with under 2 GFLOPs, a regime where most existing lightweight methods degrade sharply on heterogeneous PET/CT data. The JL-guided group size provides a principled, dataset-agnostic alternative to hand-tuned pruning, and the SDKT strategy demonstrates that channel-relationship matching (via Gram matrices) can yield positive knowledge transfer where pixel-level or ROI-based approaches fail. Open questions include whether the empirically calibrated difficulty coefficient α generalizes to unseen anatomies or modality combinations beyond PET/CT and 4-sequence MRI, and whether the paired-window mechanism scales gracefully to higher-resolution whole-body volumes without the current log-size window count becoming prohibitive.
