## Problem

RLVR methods (PPO, GRPO) are the de facto standard for tuning LLMs on reasoning tasks, but they suffer from significant diversity loss because they implicitly optimize the mode-seeking Reverse KL divergence, concentrating probability on a narrow subset of high-reward solutions while forgetting others the base model could produce. The paper asks whether one can explicitly define the target distribution and select a divergence that gives direct, continuous control over the precision–diversity trade-off rather than being locked into the mode-seeking behavior of Reverse KL.

## Input / Output

The method takes LEAN4 theorem statements as prompts and generates formal LEAN4 proofs, which are verified by the LEAN4 proof assistant (lean4:v4.9.0) that extracts the last code block and returns a binary reward. Training uses 10K solvable problems from the LEAN Workbook dataset (200 held out for testing) with the 7B-parameter DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-SFT as the base model. The partition function Z_x needed for the target distribution is estimated online from the 4 sampled responses per problem per step, incurring no additional computational cost over standard RL baselines.

## Method

The core contribution is DMVR (Distributional Matching with Verifiable Rewards), which explicitly defines the target distribution as the base model filtered by the verifier, p_x(y) ∝ π_base(y|x)·v(y,x), and approximates it via α-DPG, a policy-gradient method parameterized by the α-divergence family. The α parameter smoothly interpolates between Forward KL (α→0, mass-covering, maximum diversity) and Reverse KL (α→1, mode-seeking, maximum precision), with the squared Hellinger distance at α=0.5, enabling a continuous precision–diversity trade-off. The pseudo-reward R_θ(y,x) = min((p_x(y)/π_θ(y|x))^(1−α) − 1, M) replaces the binary verifier signal, and the partition function is estimated via importance sampling from the current policy's own samples. The framework unifies RLVR (Reverse KL), KL-DPG (Forward KL), and Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning under a single divergence-minimization umbrella, and a support-decomposition theorem proves that α→1 recovers REINFORCE while α→0 recovers cross-entropy on filtered base-model samples.

## Results

On the LEAN theorem-proving benchmark (200 test problems, 256 samples each), α-DPG achieves the best coverage (pass@256) of 88.5% at α=0.25, surpassing the base SFT model (86.5%) and all RL baselines. At the precision end, α-DPG (α=0.997) reaches 72.3% pass@1, exceeding GPG (71.8%), RLOO (70.5%), and GRPO (70.0%). Across the α spectrum, α-DPG models lie on or near the Pareto frontier between pass@1 and pass@256, with no single baseline dominating across both axes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

α-DPG (α=0.25) achieves the highest pass@256 (88.5%) among all methods, outperforming the base SFT model (86.5%), GRPO-Pass@k (87.5%), and GRPO High-KL (87.0%), while also improving pass@1 from 55.3% to 62.1%. At the precision end, α-DPG (α=0.997) reaches 72.3% pass@1, beating GPG (71.8%), RLOO (70.5%), and GRPO (70.0%), while retaining higher coverage (83.5% vs. 81.0–81.5%). GRPO and RLOO achieve high pass@1 but suffer the largest diversity collapse (pass@256 drops to 81.0–81.5%, Simpson indices drop to ~0.55–0.67). Diversity-preserving baselines like GRPO High-KL and GRPO-Rw-Ulkly maintain coverage close to the base model but sacrifice 7–12 points of pass@1 relative to α-DPG at comparable α values. ReMax offers competitive coverage (84.5%) with moderate precision (69.6%), but is dominated by α-DPG across the full pass@k curve for k≥4. Overall, no single baseline dominates both axes simultaneously, whereas α-DPG sweeps the Pareto frontier by tuning α.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate training paradigms—RLVR (Reverse KL), KL-DPG/Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning (Forward KL), and their intermediate variants—under a single α-divergence framework (α-DPG). It builds directly on the Distribution Matching framework of Khalifa et al. (2021) and the f-DPG algorithm of Go et al. (2023), while leveraging the RLVR-to-Reverse-KL equivalence established by Korbak et al. (2022b). The key departure is making the target distribution explicit (the verifier-filtered base model) and then choosing the divergence to approximate it, rather than letting the divergence be implicitly fixed by the RL objective as in standard RLVR.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a principled diagnosis of why RLVR causes diversity collapse: it is the mode-seeking nature of the implicit Reverse KL, not the filtering target itself, that discards valid solutions. The α parameter offers a practical, continuous knob for the precision-coverage trade-off, which is particularly relevant for test-time scaling (pass@k) where diversity directly determines success. However, evidence is limited to a single 7B model (DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5-SFT) on one task (LEAN theorem proving), and the authors acknowledge that generalization to other domains and model families remains untested. Open questions include whether a curriculum in α (low-to-high) would decouple training dynamics from optima, and whether the approach extends to informal math or code generation.
