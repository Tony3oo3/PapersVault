## Problem

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) generate thousands of chain-of-thought tokens, causing the KV cache to rapidly exhaust GPU memory (e.g., 50 GB for a 32K-token generation at batch 32 on GPT-OSS-20B). Existing KV cache compression methods—whether quantization, eviction, or hybrids—were designed for long-input contexts or rely on token-level heuristics (recency, uniform precision) that ignore the semantic structure of reasoning, leading to substantial accuracy degradation under the high compression ratios needed for long-output generation.

## Input / Output

The method takes a standard text prompt and generates tokens autoregressively, but during decoding it maintains a compressed KV cache organized by thought type. Each KV entry is tagged with a thought category (Reasoning, Execution, or Transition) determined every 128 tokens via attention sparsity, and stored at a precision matching that category's importance (8-bit FP8, 4-bit NVFP4, or 2-bit ternary). The block table is extended with thought-type fields, segment masks, and eviction masks, enabling in-place memory reuse of evicted slots without compaction. The final output is generated text with near-lossless accuracy at under 5% of the original KV cache footprint.

## Method

ThinKV decomposes the chain of thought into three thought types (Reasoning, Execution, Transition) by detecting a tri-modal distribution in layer-wise attention sparsity, calibrated offline via kernel density estimation on a subset of layers. It then applies a hybrid compression strategy: (1) Think Before You Quantize (TBQ) assigns bit-precision by thought importance—R tokens at 8-bit, E at 4-bit, T at 2-bit—using group quantization with shared FP8 scale factors; (2) Think Before You Evict (TBE) performs proactive, segment-level eviction triggered by transition thoughts (which signal reasoning trajectory changes), progressively annealing prior segments through a retention schedule {64, 32, 16, 8, 4} using K-means clustering on post-RoPE key embeddings. On the systems side, Continuous Thinking (CT) extends PagedAttention with thought-aware block tables and soft eviction masks, allowing new tokens to overwrite evicted slots in place and eliminating the expensive gather-based compaction that causes up to 37× TPOT slowdown in prior methods.

## Results

ThinKV achieves near-lossless accuracy using only 1024 tokens of KV cache (<3.67% of FullKV memory) at an average precision of 3.4 bits, while baselines require >12% of the cache for comparable accuracy. On AIME, ThinKV scores 50.00 (R1-Qwen-14B) and 70.28 (QwQ-32B) versus baselines of 53.33 and 73.33, respectively, outperforming KIVI (40.00/60.56) and PM-KVQ (43.33/67.86). On LiveCodeBench, ThinKV scores 45.84 and 50.47 versus baselines of 47.90 and 55.45. In throughput, ThinKV delivers up to 5.8× higher tokens/s than R-KV (sequential) and 15.8× over FullKV at a 2048-token budget, supporting up to 3× larger batch sizes.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against quantization baselines, ThinKV substantially outperforms both KIVI and PM-KVQ: on AIME, ThinKV (50.00) beats KIVI (40.00) by 10 points and PM-KVQ (43.33) by 6.67 points on R1-Qwen-14B, while on LiveCodeBench it leads KIVI (34.56) by 11.28 points and PM-KVQ (41.97) by 3.87 points. Against eviction baselines (R-KV, RaaS, LazyEviction, H2O), ThinKV achieves comparable accuracy at a 1024-token budget (<3.67% of FullKV) where competing methods need >12% of the cache; on AIME for R1-Llama-8B and AceReason-14B, ThinKV sustains <4% accuracy drop using only ~1.3% of KV cache. In throughput, ThinKV delivers up to 5.8× higher tokens/s than R-KV (sequential) and 3.6× over R-KV (overlapped) at maximum batch size, and at iso-batch (256) it achieves 3.2× and 1.6× higher throughput than R-KV variants, respectively, due to eliminating gather-based compaction. Against FullKV, ThinKV at a 2048-token budget increases maximum batch size from 13 to 290 on A100-80GB, yielding a 15.8× throughput gain while maintaining identical accuracy (50% on AIME). On the LLM generalization test (LongWriter), ThinKV matches or slightly surpasses H2O at higher compression (3.75% vs 5% budget).

## Relation to Prior Work

ThinKV extends the KV cache compression literature from token-level heuristics (recency-based eviction in H2O, uniform quantization in KIVI, progressive precision reduction in PM-KVQ) to a thought-adaptive framework that exploits attention sparsity to decompose chains of thought into reasoning, execution, and transition segments. It builds directly on PagedAttention for memory management but departs from prior LRM-specific methods (R-KV, RaaS, LazyEviction) by operating at the granularity of thought segments rather than individual tokens, and by jointly co-designing quantization precision and eviction schedule around thought importance. The Continuous Thinking kernel further distinguishes the work by eliminating the gather-based compaction that plagues existing eviction methods, a system-level contribution orthogonal to the algorithmic compression strategy.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As LRMs generate increasingly long chains of thought, the KV cache becomes the dominant memory bottleneck, and this paper demonstrates that near-lossless accuracy is achievable with under 5% of the original cache while simultaneously improving throughput by up to 5.8×—a practical win for serving infrastructure. The observation that pure quantization inflates generation length by up to 5.1× (making aggressive quantization counterproductive for LRMs) challenges the common assumption that lower precision is always beneficial. Open questions include whether the three-type thought decomposition generalizes to all reasoning architectures, how the approach scales to even longer contexts (beyond 32K tokens), and whether the thought-adaptive strategy can be extended to multi-turn or agentic settings where reasoning trajectories are more complex.
