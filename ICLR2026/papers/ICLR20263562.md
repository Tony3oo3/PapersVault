## Problem

Existing value-decomposition MARL methods (QMIX, WQMIX) track only a single optimal joint action, causing them to converge to suboptimal policies when the value landscape shifts during training. Because information about alternative high-value actions is discarded once the current optimum is found, these methods cannot rapidly adapt when exploration or environment dynamics make a previously suboptimal action the new optimum.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263562/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

Each agent receives a local observation vector (e.g., relative positions, health, unit types within a 9-unit sight range in SMAC; ego-player, teammate, opponent, and ball features in GRF) and processes its trajectory history through a GRU layer. The method outputs a discrete per-agent action (movement, attack, special abilities, stop, or no-op), and during training an encoder-decoder additionally produces a latent representation z_t, an approximate Softmax distribution P̂_t over sub-value function indices, and a reconstructed global state ŝ_t. At evaluation, only Q_0^sub (= Q^tot) is used, so execution is fully decentralized and communication-free.

## Method

S2Q trains K+1 monotonic sub-value functions Q_0^sub, …, Q_K^sub (all sharing QMIX's mixing architecture) that successively capture the optimal and k-th suboptimal joint actions of an unrestricted target Q*. Each Q_k^sub is trained with a WQMIX-style weighted TD loss augmented by a suppression term α·𝕀(a_t ∈ A_{k-1,t})·max(Q*_targ, C) that penalizes previously identified actions, guaranteeing (Theorem 4.1) that Q_k^sub selects the k-th best action under bounded rewards. A Softmax-based behavior policy P_t over the Q* values of the tracked actions determines which sub-value function to execute at each step, replacing naive ε-greedy with prioritized exploration around high-value alternatives. An encoder-decoder maps joint local histories to a latent z_t from which agents jointly estimate P̂_t and synchronize their sub-value function selection during training; at test time, only Q_0^sub is used, eliminating the need for communication. The full loss combines the successive sub-value loss, the unrestricted Q* TD loss, and the encoder-decoder reconstruction/cross-entropy loss.

## Results

On the SMAC-Hard+ suite (6 maps), S2Q achieves a 73.43±5.29% average win rate, substantially outperforming QMIX (43.94±10.06%) and all other baselines including WQMIX, DOP, FOP, PAC, RiskQ, MARR, and MASIA. The advantage is most pronounced in exploration-intensive scenarios such as 6h_vs_8z and 3s5z_vs_3s6z, where conventional methods struggle to adapt to shifting optimal joint actions. On SMAC-Comm, S2Q-Comm demonstrates substantial gains over communication baselines (MASIA, NDQ, CAMA, MAIC, T2MAC) in challenging coordination tasks like 5z_vs_1ul and bane_vs_hM, while on Google Research Football (academy_3_vs_2, academy_4_vs_3) S2Q also achieves faster convergence and higher asymptotic win rates.

## Baselines & Comparisons

S2Q consistently outperforms all 14 baselines across SMAC-Hard+, GRF, and SMAC-Comm. Against value-decomposition methods (QMIX, WQMIX), S2Q's advantage stems from tracking suboptimal actions that enable rapid adaptation when the optimal joint action shifts during training. Against actor-critic methods (DOP, FOP) and risk-sensitive/counterfactual methods (PAC, RiskQ), S2Q achieves higher final win rates with faster convergence, particularly in super-hard SMAC maps. Against communication methods (MASIA, NDQ, CAMA, MAIC, T2MAC, QMIX-Comm, FullCom), S2Q-Comm matches or exceeds performance while requiring no communication at test time in the default setting. The ablation in Table 1 confirms that the successive sub-value learning (S2Q_no_wTD: 70.59%) and Softmax-guided execution (S2Q_no_soft: 55.17%) are both critical, with removing either component causing significant degradation relative to full S2Q (73.43%). S2Q extended to VDN and QPLEX also shows consistent gains over their base versions, confirming generality across CTDE architectures.

## Relation to Prior Work

S2Q builds directly on the QMIX/WQMIX lineage of monotonic value decomposition under CTDE, inheriting the IGM condition and the unconstrained target Q* from WQMIX. Its key departure is the introduction of successive sub-value functions that explicitly track k-th suboptimal joint actions via a suppression mechanism, combined with a Softmax-based behavior policy that replaces per-agent ε-greedy exploration. This positions S2Q as addressing a gap that prior representational fixes (WQMIX, PAC, RiskQ, FOP) leave open: the inability to adapt when the optimal joint action shifts during training due to exploration-driven value updates.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper identifies and formalizes a concrete failure mode of CTDE value decomposition—premature convergence to a suboptimal action that later becomes non-optimal—and provides a clean theoretical guarantee (Theorem 4.1) that successive sub-value learning recovers the correct ranking under bounded rewards. The practical advantage of communication-free evaluation (using only Q_0^sub at test time) makes S2Q more deployable than communication-based baselines. Open questions include how the approach scales to very large agent counts (where K sub-networks multiply memory), whether the Softmax temperature can be made adaptive, and whether the successive suppression idea transfers to policy-gradient CTDE methods beyond Q-learning.
