## Problem

Prior theoretical work established Tsetlin Machine convergence only for the 1-bit Identity/NOT operator and the 2-bit XOR operator under noise-free conditions, leaving the AND and OR operators, all noisy training scenarios (wrong labels and irrelevant variables), and the general k-bit case without rigorous convergence guarantees. This paper closes those gaps by providing a unified, almost-sure convergence analysis spanning all three 2-bit operators under both noise-free and noisy conditions, and extending the results to arbitrary k-bit operators.

## Input / Output

The TM consumes a boolean input vector X = [x₁, x₂, …, xₒ] (each xₖ ∈ {0,1}) along with a binary label y ∈ {0,1} during training, and produces a binary class prediction during testing by summing the outputs of m conjunctive clauses and comparing the sum to a threshold Th. Each clause is a conjunction of literals (original or negated inputs), e.g., x₁ ∧ ¬x₂, and the set of learned clauses constitutes the interpretable logical representation of the target operator. The analysis targets logical operators (AND, OR, XOR) as ground-truth functions over k-bit inputs, with the number of informative bits k and irrelevant bits q varying across theorems.

## Method

The proof strategy uses absorbing-state analysis of the Tsetlin Automata rather than stationary-distribution analysis of Markov chains. For the 2-bit cases, a quasi-stationary technique freezes the TAs of one input bit and exhaustively enumerates all 128 transition instances for the remaining TAs, revealing unique absorbing states corresponding to the target operator. A key structural distinction emerges: the AND operator (single sub-pattern) converges even with T disabled (u₁>0, u₂>0), while the OR operator (multiple sub-patterns) is non-absorbing without T and requires T ≤ ⌊m/2⌋ to block Type I feedback once T clauses per sub-pattern are learned. For the k-bit generalization, the authors avoid combinatorial explosion by clustering clause forms into three categories (exact match, partial match, non-match) and analyzing transitions between categories rather than individual literal states. Under noisy conditions, the analysis shows wrong labels create statistically conflicting feedback that prevents absorption (though efficient learning persists), while irrelevant variables are neutralized by T's resource-allocation mechanism, preserving almost-sure convergence.

## Results

The paper establishes nine theorems providing a complete convergence characterization of Tsetlin Machines. Under noise-free training, the TM converges almost surely to the correct 2-bit AND operator (Theorem 1) and OR operator (Theorem 2, requiring T ≤ ⌊m/2⌋), and the k-bit generalizations (Theorems 6–7) hold with T ≤ ⌊m/e⌋ where e is the number of sub-pattern clusters. Under noisy training, wrong labels render the TM non-absorbing (Theorem 3), while irrelevant input variables do not prevent almost-sure convergence (Theorems 4–5, 8–9). A distinctive structural finding is that OR clauses can jointly represent two sub-patterns sharing a common literal (e.g., x₁ covers both [1,0] and [1,1]), unlike XOR where sub-patterns are mutually exclusive and must be represented individually.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is a purely theoretical convergence-analysis paper and does not benchmark the Tsetlin Machine against competing classifiers or learning algorithms. Instead, it extends and unifies prior theoretical results: the 1-bit Identity/NOT convergence proof (Zhang et al., 2022, IEEE TPAMI), the 2-bit XOR convergence proof (Jiao et al., 2023, IEEE TPAMI), and the PAC-learning literature on conjunctions/disjunctions (Valiant, 1984; Mansour & Parnas, 1998). The paper's contribution is to fill the gap for AND and OR operators, to analyze noisy training (wrong labels and irrelevant variables) for all three 2-bit operators, and to generalize from 2 bits to k bits using a clause-level clustering argument that avoids combinatorial explosion. No quantitative head-to-head comparisons with other methods are presented; the experimental appendices (J, K) confirm the theoretical predictions with simulation curves rather than tabulated accuracy comparisons.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the convergence analysis lineage for Tsetlin Machines established by Zhang et al. (2022) for the 1-bit Identity/NOT operator and Jiao et al. (2023) for the 2-bit XOR operator. It departs from prior work in several ways: it shifts from stationary-distribution analysis of discrete-time Markov chains to absorbing-state analysis, incorporates Type II feedback (omitted in the XOR study), identifies a structural property unique to the OR operator where a single clause can jointly encode two sub-patterns, and generalizes from 2-bit to the k-bit setting by moving from literal-level to clause-level analysis. The noisy-training analysis (wrong labels and irrelevant variables) is entirely new relative to prior TM convergence results, which were restricted to noise-free conditions.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The k-bit generalization is the most practically significant contribution, since all real-world TM applications operate on booleanized k-bit feature representations; this work provides the first principled theoretical explanation for TM behavior in that regime. The identification of the critical role of hyperparameter T (with the T ≤ ⌊m/e⌋ constraint for multi-sub-pattern operators) gives practitioners concrete guidance for configuration. Open questions remain: a formal PAC-learnability proof under wrong labels is left as future work, the behavior when T > ⌊m/2⌋ is only conjectured, and the joint-representation property of OR clauses, while enabling compactness, complicates interpretability in ways that are not yet fully characterized.
