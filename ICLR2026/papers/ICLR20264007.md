## Problem

Existing VLMs lack internalized 3D spatial reasoning, producing layouts that require heavy post-processing (e.g., differentiable optimization) to achieve physical plausibility. Supervised fine-tuning is fundamentally mismatched to layout generation because no single ground-truth layout exists—multiple valid arrangements satisfy the same constraints—so SFT cannot capture the full distribution of plausible scenes. MetaSpatial is the first RL framework to address both gaps simultaneously, enabling real-time 3D scene layout generation without post-processing or perfect annotations.

## Input / Output

The model receives a rendered empty-room image (serving as a spatial canvas, not a supervision signal), a structured list of 10–20 object candidates with category, size, and material metadata, and an optional natural-language user preference. It produces a two-part output: a natural-language reasoning trace enclosed in think tags explaining the spatial logic, followed by a JSON block in answer tags assigning each object a precise (x, y, z) position in meters. The layout is then validated by three automated reward signals—format parsing, physics simulation via bounding-box collision and boundary checks, and a GPT-4o perceptual score on a Blender-rendered scene image—rather than compared against any ground-truth coordinates.

## Method

MetaSpatial trains a VLM (Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B) via a multi-turn training-only refinement pipeline where each sample yields T sequential layout roll-outs, each conditioned on the previous turn's rendered scene and feedback. The core algorithm, 3D-SPO, extends GRPO with two key mechanisms: (1) a 3D masking step that identifies all coordinate tokens in the output and applies object-level physics penalties (weighted collision and constraint ratios) directly to those tokens' advantage estimates, so the policy learns spatial constraints preferentially on the tokens that matter; and (2) a discounted cumulative reward across the T-turn trajectory that up-weights early layout quality, discouraging the model from relying on prolonged refinement. A three-level reward (graded format score, physics violation ratio, and GPT-4o rendering score) is applied with staged tuning—format first, then physics, then rendering—to stabilize training. The final objective is a clipped PPO-style loss with KL regularization against a frozen reference policy, optimized over grouped trajectories for relative advantage estimation without a separate value network.

## Results

On the primary layout generation benchmark, Qwen2.5-VL 7B + MetaSpatial achieves an overall composite score of 0.95, reducing collision rate to 11.5% and constraint violation to 70.8%, while reaching 0.98 format accuracy and a 0.62 GPT-4o perceptual score. On the Open3DVQA spatial reasoning benchmark, MetaSpatial-7B attains 73.5% overall accuracy, surpassing GPT-4o (58.7%) and GPT-4 (51.1%). The 3B variant also benefits substantially, improving overall score from −0.27 to −0.09 and cutting collision from 79.0% to 68.5%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MetaSpatial-7B outperforms all three external baselines on the composite overall score (0.95 vs. I-Design 0.92, GPT-4o 0.87, LayoutGPT 0.85) and achieves the lowest collision rate (11.5%) and constraint violation rate (70.8%), roughly halving I-Design's collision (22.5%) and LayoutGPT's (20.7%). Against GPT-4o, MetaSpatial-7B leads on every reported metric including format accuracy (0.98 vs. 0.95) and GPT-4o perceptual score (0.62 vs. 0.58). The only metric where MetaSpatial-7B trails is the GPT-4o perceptual score versus I-Design (0.62 vs. 0.64), a narrow 0.02 gap, while still winning decisively on physical plausibility. On the Open3DVQA benchmark, MetaSpatial-7B (73.5%) also exceeds GPT-4o (58.7%) and GPT-4 (51.1%) by 14.8 and 22.4 percentage points respectively, confirming that the RL-trained spatial reasoning generalizes beyond the layout generation task.

## Relation to Prior Work

MetaSpatial adapts the GRPO objective from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024) to the 3D spatial domain, introducing object-level physics-aware modulation into advantage estimates (3D-SPO) and a training-only multi-turn refinement pipeline. It departs from prior layout generation methods (LayoutGPT, I-Design, LayoutVLM) that rely on SFT or inference-time multi-agent refinement with heavy post-processing, arguing that the ill-posed nature of layout generation (no single ground truth) makes RL with evaluative rewards a more natural fit. The three-level reward design (format, physics via scene-graph collision checks, and GPT-4o rendering-based scoring) draws on rule-based RL for code/math (DeepSeek-R1, Code-R1) but is the first to apply such structured, domain-specific rewards to 3D spatial reasoning in VLMs.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work establishes RL as a viable training paradigm for 3D spatial reasoning in VLMs, eliminating the need for expensive post-processing (differentiable optimization, multi-agent search) and large annotated layout datasets. However, the constraint violation rate remains high (70.8% even after full training), suggesting the method does not yet fully internalize spatial boundary constraints. Open questions include scalability to multi-room and open-world scenes, reducing dependence on GPT-4o as a rendering judge (cost and latency), and whether the physics-aware advantage modulation generalizes beyond indoor furniture placement to robotics or embodied AI settings.
