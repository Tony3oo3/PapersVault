## Problem

Existing adaptive-rank LoRA methods (e.g., AdaLoRA) allocate per-layer ranks using instantaneous gradient magnitudes, which capture only local sensitivity, ignore non-local pathwise effects within a layer, and become unreliable under gradient saturation in deep transformers. This yields unstable, biased importance scores and suboptimal rank allocation, particularly in complex optimization landscapes.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261305/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a frozen pretrained LLM backbone (RoBERTa-large, Qwen-2.5-0.5B, Llama-2-7B, Llama-3-8B, or DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-2.5-7B) and a downstream task dataset (GLUE classification/regression, math word problems, or commonsense QA). During fine-tuning, it consumes mini-batches of text prompts to compute loss gradients along a parameter-space path, and produces a fine-tuned model with per-layer LoRA ranks adaptively pruned from an initial overparameterized budget (e.g., r=32 down to r=16). At inference, the model accepts a text prompt and outputs a task-specific prediction (classification label or generated answer) via beam search.

## Method

IGU-LoRA replaces AdaLoRA's instantaneous gradient scoring with a parameter-space Integrated Gradients (IG) estimator that integrates ∂L(αΔW)/∂w along the path α∈[0,1] from a zero baseline to the current weights, capturing cumulative non-local sensitivity and bypassing gradient-saturated regions. To avoid O(N) forward-backward passes, the trapezoidal quadrature is approximated by sampling a single α uniformly per mini-batch, yielding a stochastic estimator with total error O(N⁻²)+O(M⁻¹/²) (Theorem 1). An uncertainty-aware SNR score then combines an exponential moving average of the IG sensitivity (signal) with an EMA of its deviation (noise), suppressing noisy updates and stabilizing rank selection (Theorem 2 provides a high-probability concentration bound). At each pruning step, SVD is applied to the low-rank product AB, and singular values are retained in descending order of a composite score that aggregates the singular value magnitude with the IG-SNR scores of the corresponding P and Q entries, after which A and B are re-parameterized to the reduced rank.

## Results

On the GLUE benchmark with RoBERTa-large, IGU-LoRA achieves an average score of 89.42 across 8 tasks with only 0.33M trainable parameters, outperforming the best PEFT baseline AutoLoRA (88.76) by 0.66 points and full fine-tuning (88.50, 355M params) by 0.92 points. On mathematical and common-sense reasoning tasks with Qwen-2.5-0.5B, IGU-LoRA reaches 59.17 average accuracy, surpassing AdaLoRA (57.67) by 1.50 points and matching full fine-tuning (58.98, 494M params) while using only 8.8M parameters. On larger backbones (Llama-3-8B), IGU-LoRA achieves 85.48 average on BoolQ/GSM8K, exceeding AdaLoRA (83.74) by 1.74 points.

## Baselines & Comparisons

IGU-LoRA consistently outperforms all nine baselines across every evaluated task. Against the closest PEFT competitors, it beats AutoLoRA by 0.66 points average on GLUE (89.42 vs. 88.76) and by 1.50 points on reasoning tasks (59.17 vs. 57.67 for AdaLoRA on Qwen-2.5-0.5B). DoRA, which decouples magnitude and direction, trails IGU-LoRA by 0.67 points on GLUE and 1.74 points on Llama-3-8B reasoning tasks. The original LoRA with fixed rank is surpassed by 1.30 points on GLUE and 2.60 points on reasoning tasks, confirming that adaptive rank allocation via IG scoring is the primary source of gain. Non-LoRA PEFT methods (Houlsby-Adapter, P-tuning v2, (IA)³, SSP) all lag by 1.2–3.0 points on average, with P-tuning v2 showing the largest gap (2.97 points on GLUE). Notably, IGU-LoRA matches or exceeds full fine-tuning performance (89.42 vs. 88.50 on GLUE; 59.17 vs. 58.98 on reasoning) while using 1000× fewer trainable parameters, and its training/inference overhead is comparable to AdaLoRA (0.87h vs. 0.73h training time on BoolQ).

## Relation to Prior Work

IGU-LoRA sits at the intersection of two research threads: adaptive-rank LoRA variants (AdaLoRA, AutoLoRA, GoRA) that dynamically allocate low-rank capacity across layers, and Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017), an attribution method from the interpretability literature. The paper's core departure from AdaLoRA is replacing its instantaneous gradient-magnitude scoring with a parameter-space IG path integral, arguing that local gradients miss non-local, pathwise effects and fail under gradient saturation. An additional uncertainty-aware EMA mechanism (coupling a smoothed sensitivity with a dispersion term) further differentiates the scoring from prior SVD-based pruning heuristics, and the authors provide a trapezoidal-rule error bound and a high-probability stability guarantee for the resulting SNR score that prior adaptive-rank methods lack.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The work offers a more principled, theoretically grounded scoring mechanism for the widely used LoRA/AdaLoRA rank-allocation pipeline, and the IG-in-parameter-space idea could transfer to other PEFT or pruning settings where instantaneous gradients are unreliable. However, the empirical gains over strong baselines (AdaLoRA, DoRA) are modest—typically 0.5–2 percentage points—and the authors themselves note non-trivial computational overhead for large models and sensitivity to integration-path choice. Open questions include whether the approach scales efficiently to 70B+ parameter models, whether the i.i.d./local-stationarity assumption behind the SNR stability theorem holds under more aggressive training schedules, and whether the rank-allocation patterns (Q/K and Up/Down projections favored) generalize across architectures or are task-specific.
