## Problem

Existing chart-understanding methods address only shallow tasks like visual recognition and factual QA, falling short of the complex multi-step reasoning required for genuine deep research (background integration, relationship construction, strategic forecasting). Two technical bottlenecks constrain progress: at training, methods like GRPO collapse heterogeneous multi-dimensional rewards into a single scalar and jointly optimize over heterogeneous data, causing signal interference and gradient conflicts; at evaluation, no benchmark assesses end-to-end analytic reasoning because open-ended report generation is too subjective to score reliably.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261655/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The model receives a complex multi-element chart image (curated from professional dashboards, Pew Research, etc.) paired with a text prompt specifying the research task. In the MCDR-Bench evaluation protocol, the model is given a deep research report with exactly one targeted error injected at one of five capability stages (background knowledge, fact extraction, relationship construction, deep report, forecast/plan) and must identify which statement is incorrect, converting subjective generation into a binary discrimination task. The full pipeline output spans five stages: domain background retrieval, atomic data extraction, inter-element relationship modeling, synthesized analytical report, and forward-looking strategic recommendations.

## Method

PRPO decomposes GRPO's single scalar advantage into two orthogonal parallelization axes. Reward-PRPO computes a separate normalized advantage for each of K reward dimensions (e.g., accuracy, format) rather than summing them, then optimizes a weighted sum of per-dimension clipped policy-gradient losses, preserving signal integrity that scalar aggregation destroys. Data-PRPO replaces GRPO's per-question rollout grouping with capability-based partitioning: samples are assigned to partitions by cognitive type (visual, logical, analytical) via a capability_uid, and advantages are normalized using partition-specific mean and standard deviation; an iterative outlier-relegation mechanism demotes samples whose advantage exceeds a threshold τ back to individual rollout-level optimization to prevent distribution distortion. The full PRPO objective nests both: within each capability partition m, each reward dimension k gets its own advantage A_i^(k,m) = (R_i^(k) − R̄^(k,m)) / σ^(k,m), and the loss is a double-weighted sum over partitions and dimensions. For evaluation, MCDR-Bench applies the 'error uniqueness principle': correct multi-stage reports are generated by a five-agent pipeline, then a single controlled perturbation is injected per sample, yielding 3,084 objective error-identification items that enable fine-grained, quantifiable scoring without subjective annotation.

## Results

On MCDR-Bench, PRPO achieves a 69.90% mean score (direct) and 76.89% (Think setting), outperforming GRPO by 7.64% and 12.90% respectively, with the largest gains in Background Knowledge (+19.77% in Think). On the external ChartQAPro benchmark, PRPO reaches 47.69% mean, a 6.36% improvement over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline (41.33%) and slightly exceeding Gemini-Flash-2.0 (47.15%). Notably, PRPO with Think (76.89%) nearly matches Claude-3.7 Sonnet (77.08%), effectively closing the gap between a 7B open-source model and a top commercial model.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRPO consistently outperforms GRPO across all five MCDR-Bench dimensions, with the largest relative gains in Background Knowledge (+9.41% direct, +19.77% Think) and Fact Extraction (+9.69% direct, +16.46% Think), confirming that reward decomposition is most beneficial where signal interference is strongest. Against commercial models, PRPO Think (76.89%) nearly matches Claude-3.7 Sonnet (77.08%) but remains well below Gemini-2.5-Pro (89.34%), indicating that even optimized 7B models have a ceiling relative to the strongest proprietary systems. On ChartQAPro, PRPO (47.69%) surpasses the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline by 6.36% and edges out Gemini-Flash-2.0 (47.15%), while trailing Claude-3.5 Sonnet (46.57%) only marginally in mean but leading in MCQ (+12.62%) and Hypothetical (+9.29%) sub-tasks. All open-source baselines (ChartGemma-3B at 8.29%, LLaVA-Next-7B at 17.76%, InternVL2.5-8B at 31.59%, Llama-3.2-11B-Vision at 34.56%) are substantially outperformed by PRPO, with gaps of 35–62 percentage points. The ablation confirms both components contribute: Reward-PRPO alone reaches 64.30% and Data-PRPO alone 63.55%, while their combination (full PRPO) achieves 69.62%, demonstrating complementary rather than redundant contributions.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) along two orthogonal axes: decomposing multi-dimensional rewards into independent optimization objectives (Reward-PRPO) and partitioning training data by capability type rather than relying solely on per-question rollout grouping (Data-PRPO). For evaluation, it departs from the established chart QA benchmark lineage (PlotQA → ChartQA → ChartBench → ChartQAPro) by introducing an error-injection paradigm that converts open-ended report generation into objective error identification, drawing on the 'error uniqueness principle' to sidestep the subjectivity problem that has constrained prior chart evaluation. The multi-stage analytical pipeline (background → facts → relationships → report → forecast) is positioned as a new 'deep research' formulation that subsumes and extends prior factual QA tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The error-injection evaluation paradigm offers a practical, scalable alternative to expert-annotated open-ended assessment for complex analytical reasoning, potentially transferable to other domains where generation quality is hard to score objectively. However, the benchmark is relatively small (3,084 samples), the PRPO gains over GRPO are moderate (7–13%), and validation is limited to a single base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B), leaving open whether the parallel optimization strategy generalizes across architectures and scales. A key open question is whether the capability-based data partitioning and reward decomposition principles transfer to other multi-task RL settings beyond chart understanding, and whether the error-injection evaluation can be automated at scale without degrading the 'uniqueness' guarantee.
