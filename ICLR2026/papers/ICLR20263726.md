## Problem

Training general medical VQA systems is bottlenecked by the absence of large, openly usable, high-quality training corpora: expert-curated sets are small and modality-restricted, automatically generated sets from text-only LLMs produce noisy stems and visually ungrounded answers, and large-scale resources remain closed due to privacy and licensing. The paper asks whether high-quality, auditable medical VQA data can be synthesized directly from open biomedical literature while preserving clinical validity and image-text grounding.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263726/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The synthesis pipeline consumes triplets of (figure image, figure caption, in-text reference paragraph) extracted from PubMed Central via Biomedica, pre-filtered to clinical imaging and microscopy subtypes. The generator emits a strict JSON object containing a self-contained question stem, five parallel multiple-choice options (A–E), and a single correct answer letter; the verifier returns a structured rubric with per-criterion binary scores and weights. The trained student models then take a medical image plus a multiple-choice question and output the correct option letter, evaluated on six benchmarks (MMMU-Med, MedX-M, PathVQA, PMC-VQA, SLAKE, VQA-RAD).

## Method

MedVLSynther is a two-stage generator-verifier pipeline driven by open-weight LMMs. The generator (GLM-4.5V-108B) is prompted with a comprehensive rubric enforcing self-contained stems, image-content alignment, parallel mutually exclusive options, and a fixed set of clinically meaningful question archetypes, all under a machine-checkable JSON schema. The verifier (Qwen2.5-VL-72B, deliberately different from the generator) operates in three stages: a hard-gate essential screen (seven non-negotiable criteria scored 0/5), a fine-grained positive-criteria stage awarding weighted bonus points only on irrefutable evidence, and a penalty stage that subtracts points for common failure modes such as forbidden terms, synonym drift, and medical inaccuracy. A normalized quality score is computed and items are accepted only if the score meets a high threshold (τ = 0.9670), yielding 13,087 audited questions. Open-weight Qwen2.5-VL 3B/7B students are then trained on the resulting MedSynVQA via supervised fine-tuning or GRPO-based reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, with RLVR consistently outperforming SFT and the rubric-verified data outperforming prior synthetic sources (PMC-VQA, text-only m23k).

## Results

MedVLSynther-3B achieves an average accuracy of 55.85 across six medical VQA benchmarks (MMMU-Med, MedX-M, PathVQA, PMC-VQA, SLAKE, VQA-RAD), while MedVLSynther-7B reaches 58.15. The 7B model attains peak scores of 77.57 on VQA-RAD and 66.36 on PathVQA. Both models are trained with RLVR on 5K (3B) or full 13K (7B) MedSynVQA items synthesized from PubMed Central figures and captions, outperforming all open-weight medical LMM baselines of comparable or larger scale.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MedVLSynther-3B (55.85 avg) surpasses the strongest prior open-weight medical LMM, MedVLThinker-7B (54.88), by +0.97 points despite being smaller, and outperforms HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B (54.69) by +1.16. MedVLSynther-7B (58.15 avg) improves over MedVLThinker-7B by +3.27 and over HuatuoGPT-Vision-7B by +3.46. The 7B model achieves the best VQA-RAD score (77.57) among all compared systems, exceeding MedGemma-4B's 78.55 only narrowly on that single benchmark while dominating on average. Against general-purpose LLMs, MedVLSynther-3B beats Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct (49.14) by +6.71 and Gemma3-4B (49.28) by +6.57. Notably, MedGemma-4B scores highest on SLAKE (83.49) but is weakest on MMMU-Med (32.55) and MedX-M (8.17), whereas MedVLSynther models maintain more balanced performance across all six benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on Biomedica (Lozano et al., 2025) as its raw data substrate, converting open PubMed Central figure-caption-reference triplets into exam-quality multiple-choice VQA items, and on MedVLThinker (Huang et al., 2025b) for the SFT + RLVR (GRPO) training recipe. It departs from prior synthetic medical VQA pipelines (PMC-VQA, GMAI-VL-5.5M) that rely on text-only LLMs or unverified image-text pairs by introducing a rubric-guided, context-aware generator paired with a multi-stage verifier that enforces essential gates, awards fine-grained positive points, and penalizes common failure modes before acceptance. The generator-verifier loop is model-agnostic and explicitly designed for auditability, distinguishing it from black-box automatic annotation approaches.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MedVLSynther offers a reproducible, privacy-preserving path to scalable medical VQA training data that operates entirely on open literature and open-weight models, addressing a practical bottleneck where evaluation benchmarks exist but high-quality training corpora are either small, noisy, or closed. The rubric-based verification framework is a transferable quality-control technique applicable beyond medicine. Open questions include whether the filtering threshold (τ=0.967) can be refined to make the full 13K dataset consistently outperform the 5K subset, whether the vocabulary-constraint rubric's systematic false negatives (flagging supported demographics as unsupported) can be resolved, and whether the approach generalizes to open-ended or multi-turn medical VQA beyond the multiple-choice format.
