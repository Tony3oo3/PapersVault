## Problem

Existing DRL-based FJSP solvers depend on heavily engineered state representations (often 20+ handcrafted features) and graph-biased architectures (GATs) that impose rigid inductive biases, limiting generalization across problem sizes and scheduling variants. The paper asks whether a minimal Markov-sufficient state combined with a generic Transformer backbone can match or exceed these complex designs while naturally extending to JSSP and FFSP.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261451/cropped_page3_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a heterogeneous disjunctive graph where each operation node carries only four features (operation available time, machine available time, duration, minimum duration) and structural edges encode O2O precedence and O2M eligibility with processing times. Available times are stored as relative values (normalized by the global minimum at each step) to prevent unbounded growth, and O2O edges are backward-looking with hop connections to all successors rather than bidirectional. The output is a complete schedule—a sequence of operation-machine pair assignments—decoded greedily or via 100 parallel stochastic trajectories, from which the minimum makespan is reported.

## Method

RESCHED formulates FJSP as a finite-state MDP where each step is an independent subproblem, eliminating historical tracking by proving state-dependent optimality (Proposition 1). The policy network is a decoder-only Transformer with two branches: an operation branch using self-attention augmented with Rotary Positional Encoding (RoPE) to capture intra-job sequencing without extra parameters, and a machine branch using cross-attention that directly embeds edge features (durations) into value vectors and incorporates self-connections to mitigate the severe operation-to-machine count imbalance (often >10:1). A lightweight 3-layer MLP head scores feasible operation-machine pairs, and the policy is trained with vanilla REINFORCE using an estimated lower-bound makespan reward, deliberately avoiding actor-critic complexity to isolate the contribution of state and architecture design.

## Results

On FJSP in-distribution benchmarks, RESCHED achieves average optimality gaps of 10.30% (SD1) and 14.18% (SD2) under greedy decoding, outperforming DANIEL (1.31%–31.58%) and HGNN (10.15%–76.79%) in 14 of 16 settings. On the Brandimarte benchmark, RESCHED reaches a 9.08% gap (greedy) and 6.61% (sampling), beating DANIEL (13.58%/9.53%) and DOAGNN (31.64%/18.62%). On out-of-distribution 40×10 SD2 instances, RESCHED achieves a −2.40% gap (i.e., better than the best-known upper bound), surpassing OR-Tools (−1.67%) and DANIEL (0.52%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

RESCHED outperforms all GNN-based DRL baselines (HGNN, DANIEL, DOAGNN) on FJSP in 14 of 16 in-distribution settings, with the largest margins on the harder SD2 instances (e.g., 18.14% vs. 57.16% for DANIEL on 15×10). On the Brandimarte benchmark, RESCHED (9.08% greedy) beats DANIEL (13.58%), HGNN (28.52%), and DOAGNN (31.64%), and approaches the 2SGA genetic algorithm (3.17% gap) at a fraction of the runtime. On JSSP (Taillard), a single model trained on 10×10 instances beats L2D and RL-GNN in 7 of 8 sizes (e.g., 9.6% vs. 13.6% for L2D at 100×20), and on FFSP, RESCHED trained only on size-20 outperforms MatNet (which trains a separate model per size) in most settings under both greedy and sampling. The only notable losses are on the easiest SD1 10×5 greedy setting (12.25% vs. DANIEL's 10.87%) and Hurink(vdata) greedy (3.48% vs. DANIEL's 3.28%), where the problem is simple enough that DANIEL's richer 26-feature representation provides a slight edge. Against the CP-SAT solver (OR-Tools), RESCHED is competitive on smaller instances but OR-Tools achieves near-optimal solutions on larger ones at the cost of 3600 s per instance versus RESCHED's sub-second inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the dominant GNN-based DRL paradigm for FJSP (HGNN, DANIEL, DOAGNN), which relies on 20+ handcrafted node features, bidirectional O2O edges, and heuristic action-space pruning. Conceptually, it builds on the decoder-only NCO architecture of Drakulic et al. (2023) and repurposes Rotary Positional Embedding (Su et al., 2024) from NLP to encode intra-job sequencing without learnable parameters. The key departure is a subproblem-based MDP reformulation that provably reduces the state to four features (operation/machine available time, duration, minimum duration), eliminating historical tracking and auxiliary variables that prior methods require at every step.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central insight—that a Markov-sufficient minimal state reduces the need for heavy architectural inductive bias (GNNs) and enables a generic Transformer to outperform purpose-built GNNs—offers a design principle applicable beyond scheduling. The framework's demonstrated generalization from a single 10×10 training size to 100×20 JSSP instances and across FJSP/JSSP/FFSP variants suggests a path toward unified scheduling agents. Open questions include whether explicit machine-to-operation (M2O) feedback can further improve performance, how the method adapts to search-based or multi-objective settings, and whether the KV-cache acceleration (theoretically 10–40×) can be realized in practice for real-time deployment.
