## Problem

Existing scaling laws for dense Transformers do not account for the sparsity dimension introduced by Mixture-of-Experts architectures, leaving it unclear how optimal sparsity should be chosen under a fixed compute budget. The paper identifies that reasoning performance diverges from the monotonic improvement predicted by pre-training loss as total parameters grow, revealing a non-monotonic (inverted-U) relationship between sparsity and reasoning accuracy that is absent for memorization tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263559/cropped_page4_idx2.webp)

## Input / Output

Models consume standard text prompts in few-shot (4-shot for TriviaQA/HellaSwag/GSM8K, 5-shot CoT for GSM-Plus) or zero-shot (TTC experiments) formats and produce text completions scored by exact-match accuracy or pass@1. Task loss is computed as cross-entropy restricted to answer tokens only, following the loss-to-loss prediction framework, which allows disentangling the generalization gap between pre-training loss and downstream task loss from the loss-to-accuracy gap.

## Method

The paper trains a systematic family of Mixtral-style MoE models (16 layers, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, rotary embeddings) sweeping width d∈{512,1024,2048}, expert count E∈{8,…,256}, and top-k∈{2,4,8,16} under a fixed 125B-token corpus, using dropless token-choice routing with load-balancing and router z-losses. Two key principles emerge: (1) Active FLOPs — at identical training loss, models with larger top-k (more active compute) consistently achieve higher reasoning accuracy, showing that pre-training loss alone is insufficient to predict reasoning performance; (2) Tokens Per Parameter (TPP) — reasoning accuracy peaks near 20 TPP while memorization improves monotonically with more parameters (lower TPP), indicating reasoning is data-hungry. The authors further demonstrate that neither GRPO post-training nor self-consistency test-time compute eliminates the sparsity-induced reasoning degradation, confirming that pre-training sparsity is the dominant architectural factor. Coding tasks (HumanEval, MBPP) exhibit the same inverted-U sparsity trade-off, generalizing the finding beyond mathematical reasoning.

## Results

The paper trains Mixtral-style MoE models (widths d∈{512,1024,2048}, experts E∈{8,…,256}, top-k∈{2,4,8,16}) on a fixed 125B-token corpus and evaluates on memorization (TriviaQA, HellaSwag) and reasoning (GSM8K, GSM-Plus) benchmarks. The headline finding is an inverted-U relationship: GSM8K task loss and accuracy improve as training loss decreases only up to a threshold, beyond which further sparsity (more experts at fixed k) degrades reasoning performance, while TriviaQA and HellaSwag improve monotonically. Reasoning accuracy peaks near 20 tokens-per-parameter (TPP), and at identical training loss, models with larger top-k consistently outperform smaller top-k on reasoning tasks. Neither GRPO post-training nor 128-sample self-consistency test-time compute eliminates the sparsity-induced reasoning degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This is an empirical scaling study rather than a method-vs-baseline comparison, so the 'baselines' are different architectural configurations of the same Mixtral architecture. At fixed active parameter count (i.e., fixed k×expert_size), increasing sparsity (more experts E) consistently improves TriviaQA and HellaSwag accuracy across all compute budgets, but for GSM8K and GSM-Plus the trend reverses once active parameters exceed a task-dependent threshold, with denser models (lower E) outperforming sparser ones. Varying top-k at fixed total parameters has negligible effect on memorization but a clear positive effect on reasoning: larger k (more active FLOPs) yields higher GSM8K accuracy even when pre-training loss is matched. The coding ablations (HumanEval, MBPP) replicate the math-reasoning pattern: sparsity helps at low compute but denser configurations win at high active-parameter budgets. GRPO post-training and 128-sample self-consistency both lift absolute accuracy uniformly but preserve the inverted-U shape, confirming that pre-training sparsity is the dominant factor for reasoning under fixed budgets.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the compute-optimal scaling framework of Hoffmann et al. (2022) and the MoE-specific sparsity frontiers of Abnar et al. (2025) by introducing a task-type decomposition: memorization versus reasoning. While Jelassi et al. (2025) provided a theoretical account of why MoE experts benefit memorization more than reasoning, and Roberts et al. (2025) showed task-dependent optimal TPP for dense models, no prior work had empirically mapped the non-monotonic sparsity–reasoning interaction under controlled IsoFLOP budgets across a systematic sweep of expert count, top-k, and width. The paper also builds on the GRPO post-training pipeline (Shao et al., 2024) and self-consistency test-time compute (Wang et al., 2023) as controls, showing that neither can rescue the architectural degradation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that reasoning accuracy peaks near 20 tokens-per-parameter and *degrades* with further sparsity at fixed FLOPs directly challenges the prevailing assumption that more total parameters (higher sparsity) is always beneficial under a compute budget. This has immediate practical implications for how labs configure MoE architectures: the optimal expert count and top-k depend on the target capability, and post-training (GRPO) or test-time compute cannot compensate for a suboptimal pre-training sparsity choice. Key limitations include the fixed 125B-token budget, the restriction to the Mixtral architecture without shared experts, and moderate model widths (≤2048); the 1T-token ablation hints that the optimal sparsity may shift with larger corpora, leaving open whether the inverted-U persists at frontier scale.
