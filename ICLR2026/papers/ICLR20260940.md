## Problem

Maximizing state entropy as a reward-free pretraining objective is well-studied in fully observable MDPs, but in POMDPs the true states are unobserved, making the objective intractable. Prior POMDP approaches naively maximize observation entropy, which provably fails when the emission matrix has large singular values (many states share an observation, or one observation is ambiguous across states). This paper provides the first scalable algorithm that induces high true-state entropy in general POMDPs without access to the transition model, emission function, or true states.

## Input / Output

The method consumes proprioceptive observation vectors and the previous action at each timestep (no images), processing them through separate MLP encoders into a recurrent latent dynamics model. It outputs two artifacts: a feedforward policy network conditioned on the learned latent state (not the raw observation or full history), and the latent dynamics model itself, which is a deterministic GRU-based RNN. The entropy objective is estimated non-parametrically via k-NN distances in the latent space, converted into per-step intrinsic rewards for PPO optimization. In Delusional Pusher, the model is modified to predict only the puck's position, directing exploration toward a relevant state subspace.

## Method

LatEnt simultaneously learns a predictive latent model and a maximum-entropy policy in a single on-policy loop. The latent model is a deterministic recurrent network (GRU) trained to predict the next observation, augmented with a dual predictability regularization (KL-balancing style) that penalizes latent components not predictable from (current latent, action), forcing the representation to be compact rather than merely lossy. The policy is a feedforward MLP on the latent state, optimized with PPO using k-NN intrinsic rewards computed on the latent space (r_i = log(||z_i - z_i^{k-NN}|| + c)). A key theoretical contribution shows that a predictive latent (sufficient to predict future observations) constitutes an information state for the state entropy objective, and that it satisfies the reward-sufficiency condition for a strictly larger class of downstream rewards than the raw observation. Training uses a two-stage schedule: a warmup phase pretrains the latent model on uniform-action data, then the dynamics model is updated at 1/5 the frequency of the policy to prevent latent-space drift from destabilizing the entropy objective.

## Results

On the PROBE benchmark (challenging POMDPs), LatEnt induces substantially higher true state entropy than maximum observation entropy across all three environments, approaching the oracle maximum state entropy ceiling. In Delusional Pusher, maximum observation entropy achieves effectively zero state entropy by exploiting emission noise rather than manipulating the puck. For downstream finetuning, LatEnt-initialized PPO solves all 6 sparse-reward tasks, while maximum observation entropy solves only 1/6, and both PPO from scratch and DreamerV3 fail to consistently collect rewards in most tasks. On the easier DeepMind Control Suite (where observation entropy is theoretically sufficient), LatEnt matches maximum observation entropy (e.g., state entropy 2.36 vs. 2.35 on Walker), confirming the gap is specific to genuinely challenging POMDPs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against maximum observation entropy (Seo et al., 2021; Yarats et al., 2021), LatEnt wins decisively on all PROBE environments: it induces higher true state entropy and, critically, avoids the failure mode where the policy exploits emission noise (e.g., moving the arm away from the puck in Delusional Pusher to gather noise, yielding zero state entropy). For downstream tasks, LatEnt-initialized PPO solves all 6 tasks while observation-entropy pretraining solves only 1/6. Against the oracle maximum state entropy ceiling (which requires access to true states), LatEnt approaches but does not fully match the upper bound, as expected since it operates without state access. Against DreamerV3 (Hafner et al., 2023), the state-of-the-art model-based POMDP algorithm, LatEnt pretraining enables PPO to learn tasks that DreamerV3 fails to solve within the same 1M-step budget. On the easier DMC benchmark, where Zamboni et al. (2024b) prove observation entropy is sufficient, LatEnt and maximum observation entropy achieve nearly identical state entropy (2.36 vs. 2.35 on Walker; 1.71 vs. 1.73 on Hopper), confirming the advantage is specific to genuinely challenging partial observability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the maximum state entropy pretraining framework (Hazan et al., 2019; Mutti et al., 2021; Seo et al., 2021) to general POMDPs, where prior work has relied on maximizing observation entropy—a strategy that Zamboni et al. (2024b) proved insufficient when the emission matrix has large singular values. The key departure is replacing the belief state (which requires known P and O, as in Zamboni et al., 2024a) with a learned predictive latent that serves as a compact information state, drawing on the latent dynamics architecture of Dreamer (Hafner et al., 2019a) and the information state theory of Subramanian et al. (2022). The algorithm combines k-NN entropy estimation (Mutti et al., 2021) with PPO in a fully on-policy, reward-free loop, and introduces a predictability regularization to keep the latent compact.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first fully sample-based algorithm for state entropy maximization in general POMDPs without requiring knowledge of the transition or emission models, filling a gap explicitly identified by Zamboni et al. (2024b). The PROBE benchmark is the first to stress-test reward-free pretraining under observability conditions where observation entropy provably fails, and the Delusional Pusher result—where the observation-entropy policy actively moves away from the target to harvest sensor noise—demonstrates that the naive approach is not merely suboptimal but actively counterproductive. Open questions include whether the approach scales to high-dimensional visual POMDPs, how sensitive performance is to the quality of the learned latent model, and whether the predictability regularization can be replaced by more principled information-theoretic criteria.
