## Problem

Multi-modal large reasoning models (MLRMs) can infer precise geographic locations from personal images through hierarchical chain-of-thought reasoning, yet existing adversarial privacy defenses—designed for perception-based models that map images directly to labels—fail to disrupt this multi-step reasoning process. The paper identifies that uniform perturbations targeting salient foreground regions cannot invalidate the cascading conceptual dependencies (continent → country → neighborhood → street) that MLRMs exploit, leaving a critical gap in privacy protection against reasoning-based location inference.

## Input / Output

The method takes an ultra-high-resolution image (≥2048 px) along with its annotated geographic concepts and spatial bounding boxes (from GeoPrivacy-6K or auto-extracted via Gemini Pro 2.5 for unseen images) and produces a full-resolution adversarial image under an L∞ perturbation budget (ε ∈ {8/255, 16/255}). The image is adaptively decomposed into an m*×n* grid of square blocks (N_max=64), each perturbed independently before reassembly. The framework operates in a black-box transfer setting: the generator is trained on surrogate CLIP encoders and deployed against target MLRMs without access to their parameters.

## Method

ReasonBreak's core insight is that disrupting hierarchical geographic reasoning requires concept-aware perturbations aligned with the model's reasoning chain rather than uniform noise. The pipeline has three stages: (1) adaptive decomposition partitions the image into aspect-ratio-preserving blocks and assigns each block a concept set via spatial overlap with annotated bounding boxes (blocks with no overlap receive all image concepts); (2) minimax target selection queries a pre-computed embedding bank to find a hard-negative prior—semantically maximally distant from all concepts in the block—serving as a conceptual 'void' directive; (3) a learnable decoder G_θ (ResBlock/UpBlock upsampling network, initialized from AnyAttack) translates this abstract prior into a pixel-level perturbation without seeing the image block directly. Training minimizes an ensemble cosine-similarity loss across four CLIP surrogate encoders, encouraging the perturbed block's representation to diverge from the original. The theoretical motivation formalizes reasoning chains as recursively dependent on both conceptual identification and sequential context, so corrupting a single concept cascades through all downstream inference steps.

## Results

On DoxBench (ε=16/255), ReasonBreak achieves an average Top-1 Privacy Protection Rate of 33.8% at the Tract level and 33.5% at the Block level, surpassing the strongest baselines (AnyAttack: 19.4% Tract, 16.8% Block; M-Attack: 18.6% Tract, 16.4% Block) by 14.4 and 16.7 percentage points respectively. The method is particularly effective against closed-source commercial models: on GPT-o3 it reaches 31.7% Tract Top-1 PPR (vs. 25.6% AnyAttack, 15.9% M-Attack), and on Gemini 2.5 Pro it achieves 23.3% Block Top-1 PPR where both baselines score 0.0%. Under a stricter ε=8/255 budget, ReasonBreak maintains a consistently larger advantage over baselines, indicating superior perturbation efficiency.

## Baselines & Comparisons

ReasonBreak outperforms both AnyAttack and M-Attack on every one of the seven target MLRMs at both the Tract and Block Top-1 PPR levels, with average gains of +14.4 pp over AnyAttack and +15.2 pp over M-Attack at Tract, and +16.7 pp and +17.1 pp respectively at Block. The most dramatic margins appear on closed-source models: against Gemini 2.5 Pro at the Block level, both baselines achieve 0.0% while ReasonBreak reaches 23.3%, and against InternVL 3.0 72B at Block, ReasonBreak scores 58.3% versus 0.0% for both baselines. M-Attack occasionally edges out AnyAttack on individual model-block pairs (e.g., QwenVL Max Block: 40.0% vs. 33.3%; QVQ-Max Block: 23.1% vs. 15.4%), but neither baseline approaches ReasonBreak's aggregate performance. The paper also notes that at a tighter ε=8/255 budget, the gap widens further, with baselines' protection nearly vanishing on models like Gemini 2.5 Pro while ReasonBreak retains a consistent advantage.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper departs from the established line of adversarial privacy protection (face cloaks, identity-preserving perturbations) that targets perception-based models performing direct image-to-label mapping. It repurposes the decoder architecture from AnyAttack (Zhang et al., CVPR 2025) but fundamentally reorients the perturbation strategy: rather than applying uniform noise to salient regions, ReasonBreak generates concept-aware perturbations that target specific visual-conceptual links within an MLRM's chain-of-thought reasoning. The theoretical framing in Section 4.2 formalizes why this matters—reasoning chains have both conceptual and sequential dependencies that make them brittle at specific nodes, a property absent in perception models. The GeoPrivacy-6K dataset, sourced from HoliCity, Aesthetic-4K, and LHQ, provides the hierarchical concept annotations that enable this targeted approach, filling a gap left by prior datasets that lack spatially-grounded geographic reasoning chains.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This work addresses a genuinely emerging privacy threat—MLRMs inferring street-level locations from personal photos—that existing defenses cannot handle, and the near-doubling of block-level protection (33.5% vs. 16.8%) demonstrates that reasoning-targeted perturbations are qualitatively different from perception-targeted ones. The counter-intuitive scaling anomaly (smaller perturbations sometimes yielding stronger protection in InternVL) hints at a fundamentally different adversarial landscape for reasoning models that warrants further investigation. Key limitations include the inability to counter OCR-based location extraction (explicitly acknowledged) and the black-box transfer setting, which may not capture white-box attacks on open-source MLRMs. Open questions include whether the concept-aware targeting generalizes to other reasoning-based inference tasks beyond geography, and whether the scaling anomaly reflects a broader property of LLM-based reasoning that could be exploited more systematically.
