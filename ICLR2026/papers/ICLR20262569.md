## Problem

Standard MCQA evaluation metrics (log-likelihood and length-normalized log-likelihood) conflate a model's genuine question comprehension with its inherent preferences for certain answer choices, meaning accuracy scores may reflect superficial choice-level patterns rather than true understanding. No existing scoring method formally separates the question-driven contribution from the choice-driven contribution in a model's prediction.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262569/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a question string Q, a set of answer choices C, and a pre-trained language model, and requires computing log-probabilities of each choice both with and without the question in the prompt (i.e., log P(x|Q,C) and log P(x|C)). It produces a per-choice NPSQ score in [0,1], a dataset-level choice sensitivity metric (fraction of examples where the choice-driven score gap exceeds the question-driven gap), and an accuracy score (acc_npsq) derived by ranking choices by NPSQ. The key constraint is that NPSQ must be computed independently per choice in the cloze format but jointly across all choices in the symbols and hybrid formats, since the latter share a common prompt context.

## Method

The paper decomposes any MCQA score Score(Q,C,x) into a choice-driven component Score_choice (computed by replacing Q with an empty string) and a question-driven component (the residual). Choice sensitivity is then defined as the fraction of examples where the top-two choice-driven score gap (Δchoice) exceeds the question-driven gap (Δquestion). The proposed NPSQ metric normalizes the probability shift induced by the question: NPSQ(Q,C,x) = [log P(x|Q,C) − log P(x|C)] / [−log P(x|C)], which measures the relative gain in log-probability attributable to the question, scaled by the baseline uncertainty. A critical property is that when Q is absent, the numerator becomes zero, so the choice-driven component of NPSQ is identically zero for all choices, making the metric structurally immune to choice-level artifacts. Empirically, replacing a distractor with adversarial choices (e.g., high-likelihood text, instructional prompts) causes 93% prediction shifts under raw log-likelihood but fewer than 0.17% under NPSQ in the cloze format, confirming its robustness.

## Results

Choice sensitivity ranges from approximately 0.2–0.4 for symbols and hybrid formats and 0.5–0.6 for the cloze format, meaning 20–60% of model decisions are driven by answer choices rather than the question. Under adversarial choice substitution, traditional metrics collapse dramatically: acc drops 54.23% on HellaSwag (simple choices) and acc_norm drops 18.17% on ARC-Challenge (extended choices), while acc_npsq remains stable with fewer than 0.17% of predictions shifting. For neutral choices in the hybrid format on MMLU, acc and acc_norm drop 8.60% and 17.17% respectively, whereas acc_npsq actually *increases* performance by 3.31%.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper compares NPSQ (acc_npsq) against two standard MCQA scoring baselines: raw log-likelihood accuracy (acc) and length-normalized log-likelihood accuracy (acc_norm, following Holtzman et al., 2021). NPSQ decisively outperforms both baselines in robustness to adversarial choices: for simple choices targeting cloze/log-likelihood, 93.19% of predictions shift under acc versus <0.17% under acc_npsq; for extended choices targeting length-normalized scoring, 41.30% shift under acc_norm versus <0.17% under acc_npsq. For instructional and neutral choices in symbols/hybrid formats, NPSQ still shows some sensitivity (10.13% and 5.72% prediction shifts respectively) due to the shared computation across choices, but remains far more stable than acc or acc_norm. In terms of absolute accuracy, NPSQ yields higher scores than acc and acc_norm in the cloze format (where the choice-driven component is net-negative) but slightly lower scores in symbols and hybrid formats (where the choice-driven component is net-positive), confirming that NPSQ isolates true question comprehension rather than inflating scores via choice-level artifacts.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper formalizes and quantifies the choice-driven artifacts that Balepur et al. (2024) identified observationally—namely, that LLMs can select correct answers without reading the question—by decomposing model scores into explicit choice-driven and question-driven components. It extends the broader line of work on MCQA evaluation sensitivity (Alzahrani et al., 2024; Zheng et al., 2023; Pezeshkpour & Hruschka, 2024) and partial-input baselines (Gururangan et al., 2018; Poliak et al., 2018) by moving from qualitative observations to a principled metric (NPSQ) that isolates question comprehension from choice-level biases. The paper also builds on the length-normalized log-likelihood scoring of Holtzman et al. (2021) but demonstrates that this normalization is insufficient to address choice sensitivity.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The finding that 20–60% of MCQA decisions are driven by answer choices rather than question comprehension challenges the foundational assumption underlying most LLM leaderboards and benchmark comparisons, and the proposed NPSQ metric offers a practical, drop-in alternative for more faithful evaluation. Limitations include testing on only three benchmarks and a handful of model families, and NPSQ still shows non-trivial sensitivity (10–11% prediction shifts) in symbols/hybrid formats under adversarial choices, suggesting the problem is not fully resolved. Open questions include whether NPSQ generalizes to open-ended or multi-turn evaluation settings, whether it holds for non-English or multilingual MCQA, and whether the choice-driven component can be further decomposed into length, position, and semantic-frequency effects.
