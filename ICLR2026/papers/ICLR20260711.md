## Problem

Existing GRPO variants for diffusion and flow-matching models suffer from two bottlenecks: sequential rollouts incur O(N·T) computational redundancy across group size N and denoising steps T, and sparse terminal rewards are uniformly propagated across all timesteps, yielding unreliable credit assignment and high-variance gradients. The central question is how to attribute sparse outcome rewards to the specific denoising steps that truly shape final quality while reducing per-iteration cost.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260711/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For text-to-image, the method consumes a text prompt and generates 720×720 RGB images via FLUX.1-Dev or SD3.5-M backbones; for image-to-video, it takes a reference image plus text prompt and produces video clips via Wan2.1-1.3B. During training, each prompt triggers a tree-structured rollout of 16 leaves (K=2 branching factor, 4 split steps over 20 denoising steps), and outputs are scored by reward models (HPS-v2.1, PickScore, ImageReward, Unified Reward, or VideoAlign motion quality) that feed into the advantage estimation pipeline.

## Method

BranchGRPO replaces independent sequential rollouts with a branching tree: at designated split steps during reverse SDE denoising, each node expands into K correlated children via a noise construction ξ_b = (ξ₀ + s·η_b)/√(1+s²) that preserves the marginal distribution while controlling inter-branch correlation through parameter s. Leaf rewards are fused upward using a softmax-weighted aggregation over path log-probabilities, then depth-wise normalized (standardized within each noise level) to produce dense step-level advantages that replace the single terminal reward broadcast. Complementary width pruning (retaining top-1 or extreme leaves per parent) and depth pruning (sliding-window skipping of late timesteps) reduce backpropagation cost by up to 4.7× without altering forward rollouts or reward evaluation. The standard clipped GRPO policy-gradient loss is then optimized over the resulting tree edges, and the paper proves that branching preserves the boundary distribution and that depth-wise baselines act as control variates reducing gradient variance.

## Results

On HPSv2.1 image alignment with the FLUX.1-Dev backbone, BranchGRPO-DepthPruning achieves the best overall alignment, raising HPS-v2.1 from 0.360 (DanceGRPO) to 0.369, PickScore to 0.235, ImageReward to 1.625, and Unified Reward to 3.404, while reducing per-iteration training time from 698s to 314s (~55% speedup). The hybrid BranchGRPO-Mix variant further accelerates training to 148s per iteration (4.7× faster than DanceGRPO) with only marginal quality drops (HPS-v2.1=0.363). On the SD3.5-M backbone, BranchGRPO reduces total GPU-hours from 2000 to 1460 while improving all alignment metrics over FlowGRPO (HPS-v2.1: 0.323 vs 0.316; GenEval: 0.89 vs 0.86).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DanceGRPO (tf=1.0), the primary baseline, BranchGRPO-DepthPruning wins on all four alignment metrics (HPS-v2.1: 0.369 vs 0.360; PickScore: 0.235 vs 0.234; ImageReward: 1.625 vs 1.612; Unified Reward: 3.404 vs 3.388) while cutting iteration time by 55% (314s vs 698s). Against DanceGRPO (tf=0.6), which already reduces compute by using only 60% of timesteps, BranchGRPO still dominates on every metric and is faster (314s vs 469s), whereas the tf=0.6 variant exhibits pronounced training instability. Against MixGRPO (289s, HPS-v2.1=0.359), BranchGRPO-DepthPruning achieves strictly better alignment on all four metrics at a comparable iteration time (314s), while BranchGRPO-Mix is 2× faster (148s vs 289s) with only a small HPS drop (0.363 vs 0.359). On the SD3.5-M backbone (Table 2), BranchGRPO outperforms FlowGRPO at 2000 GPU-hours on every metric (HPS-v2.1: 0.323 vs 0.316; GenEval: 0.89 vs 0.86) while using 27% fewer GPU-hours (1460). The base FLUX model (HPS-v2.1=0.313) and SD3.5-M (HPS-v2.1=0.204) serve as untrained references, confirming that all GRPO variants yield substantial alignment gains over the pretrained prior.

## Relation to Prior Work

BranchGRPO extends the GRPO-for-visual-generation line initiated by DanceGRPO and Flow-GRPO, which reformulated diffusion/flow denoising as an SDE-based MDP and applied the clipped policy-gradient objective from DeepSeekMath. The key departure is replacing independent sequential rollouts with a tree-structured branching process that reuses shared prefixes, a design conceptually related to TreePO in LLMs but adapted to the stepwise denoising dynamics of diffusion models. It also draws on MixGRPO's sliding-window ODE-SDE idea for its hybrid depth-pruning schedule, and introduces depth-wise reward fusion as a principled alternative to the uniform terminal-reward propagation used by all prior GRPO variants.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical impact is significant: a 4.7× per-iteration speedup (BranchGRPO-Mix) with no alignment degradation, and a demonstrated scaling law where larger group sizes (up to 256 leaves) consistently improve reward—something DanceGRPO cannot achieve within reasonable compute. This makes large-scale RLHF alignment of diffusion models substantially more accessible. Open questions include whether dynamic/adaptive branching schedules can further reduce the hyperparameter sensitivity observed in ablations, whether the framework transfers cleanly to long-horizon high-resolution video, and whether the depth-wise advantage estimator's bias-variance tradeoff (softmax-weighted vs. uniform fusion) can be characterized more tightly theoretically.
