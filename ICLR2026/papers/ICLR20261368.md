## Problem

Large MoE-based LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-V3-0324 at 671B, Kimi-K2-Instruct at 1T) impose prohibitive GPU memory demands for deployment. Existing compression methods—expert pruning and SVD-based decomposition (D²-MoE, MoLAE)—suffer from 7–14% relative accuracy drops even at modest compression rates, because their low-rank assumptions cannot capture the full effective rank of expert weight matrices, resulting in high reconstruction errors.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20261368/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes the up and gate projection matrices (p×d) of every expert in each MoE layer of a pretrained model, while leaving down projection matrices untouched. It produces a compressed MoBE model in which each expert's up/gate matrix is replaced by a smaller expert-specific transformation matrix A (p×r) plus a set of m shared basis matrices (r×d) with per-expert mixing coefficients, yielding a 24–30% total parameter reduction. The factorization is learned data-free via gradient descent on reconstruction loss, requiring no calibration set.

## Method

MoBE factorizes each expert's up/gate matrix W as W = A·f(Σⱼ α^{i,j} B^j), where A is expert-specific, {B^j} are m shared basis matrices per layer (m ≪ n experts), α^{i,j} are non-negative expert-specific mixing weights summing to 1, and f is a bipolar activation (SiLU or Tanh). This formulation is strictly more expressive than SVD-based decomposition, which corresponds to the special case of one-hot α and identity activation. Z-score normalization across all experts in a layer stabilizes optimization, and the mean term is empirically negligible so only the std is folded into A at inference. The factorization is trained with Adam (lr=0.07, batch=n experts, up to 50k epochs with early stopping) to minimize per-layer reconstruction MSE. A variant MoBE† further reduces activated experts from k to k′ (e.g., 8→6) to offset the increase in per-token activation parameters introduced by the basis matrices.

## Results

MoBE compresses MoE-based LLMs by 24%–30% in total parameters while retaining 98%+ of original performance (average accuracy drop of only ~1.4% absolute across 15 benchmarks). For example, on Qwen3-235B-A22B-2507, MoBE achieves 80.9 average accuracy at 24% compression versus 81.5 for the uncompressed model, while MoLAE drops to 73.2 and D²-MoE to 66.4 at the same compression rate. On Kimi-K2-Instruct (1T parameters), MoBE reaches 81.1 average at 24% compression versus 82.4 for the original, compared to 76.6 for MoLAE. The MoBE† variant (which reduces activated experts from k=8 to k′=6) further narrows the gap to ~0.5% absolute degradation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MoBE consistently outperforms all four baselines (D²-MoE, MoLAE, MoNE, Sub-MoE) across every model and compression rate tested. Against D²-MoE on Qwen3-30B-A3B-2507 at 24% compression, MoBE achieves 75.8 average accuracy versus 66.4 for D²-MoE — a 9.4-point advantage — while also compressing more parameters (24% vs. 24% but with lower reconstruction MSE). Against MoLAE at the same 24% compression on Qwen3-30B-A3B-2507, MoBE leads by 8.3 points (75.8 vs. 67.5); on DeepSeek-V3-0324 at 30% compression, MoBE leads by 4.9 points (78.0 vs. 73.1). On the two Qwen3 models where MoNE and Sub-MoE are also evaluated (Table 5), MoBE leads MoNE by 3.3 points (75.8 vs. 72.5) and Sub-MoE by 5.1 points (75.8 vs. 70.7) on Qwen3-30B-A3B-2507, and by 3.4 and 4.9 points respectively on Qwen3-235B-A22B-2507. Notably, D²-MoE could not be applied to the three largest models (Qwen3-235B, DeepSeek-V3-0324, Kimi-K2-Instruct) due to prohibitive computational cost on 8×H100 hardware, further limiting its practicality at scale.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself within the decomposition-based MoE compression lineage, departing from SVD-based methods (D²-MoE, MoLAE) by replacing the low-rank SVD factorization with a rank decomposition W = AB where B is reparameterized as a convex combination of shared basis matrices with a non-linear activation. The key conceptual departure is that MoBE is shown (Appendix D) to strictly subsume SVD-based group factorization as a special case (one-hot coefficients + identity activation), giving it greater expressive power without requiring the low-rank assumption that prior methods rely on. The Z-score normalization and activation function choices are practical refinements motivated by the observed weight statistics across open-source MoE models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

As MoE architectures become the dominant paradigm for scaling LLMs beyond 100B parameters, the memory bottleneck for deployment is a critical practical barrier; MoBE's 24–30% parameter reduction with only 1–2% accuracy drop on trillion-parameter models offers a concrete path to making these models deployable on existing hardware. The effective rank analysis (Appendix B) provides a useful diagnostic explaining why SVD-based methods fundamentally struggle: expert weight matrices consistently have effective ranks exceeding the compression threshold, meaning any pure rank-truncation approach must sacrifice expressiveness. Open questions include whether knowledge distillation can close the remaining accuracy gap, whether a custom fused kernel can eliminate the current inference inefficiency of multiple MoE kernel calls, and whether the method extends to non-SwiGLU expert formulations.
