## Problem

Peer effect estimation requires an exposure mapping function that summarizes peer treatments into a peer exposure value, but the true function is rarely known and misspecification leads to biased causal estimates. Existing GNN-based approaches either assume a simple homogeneous exposure (e.g., fraction of treated peers) or learn exposure naively, lacking the expressiveness to capture complex local neighborhood structures such as closed triads, clustering coefficients, and higher-order motifs.

## Input / Output

The method consumes an attributed undirected graph (node features, edge attributes), binary treatment assignments for all nodes, and observed scalar outcomes, and produces per-unit heterogeneous peer effect estimates as the difference in counterfactual outcomes under two peer treatment assignments. The learned peer exposure is a d-dimensional vector bounded in [0,1]^d, where 0 encodes no peer exposure, and the counterfactual outcome model outputs conditional expected outcomes for each unit's own treatment level. The node regression task is internally transformed into graph regression by extracting ego networks, so the model processes subgraphs rather than the full network at the exposure mapping stage.

## Method

EGONETGNN extracts ego networks for each node, transforming closed triads (involving the ego node) into dyads so that standard message-passing aggregation can count them, overcoming the known limitation of MPGNNs on connected patterns. Within each ego network, node-level aggregation incorporates peer treatments, transformed edge attributes, and a peer feature encoder capturing attribute similarity between ego and peers; a masked fully-connected layer then promotes invariance to irrelevant contexts by learning binary-like feature gates. A log-transformed MLP exposure encoder and a two-part graph readout (weighted proportion plus scale term) produce a bounded, balanced peer exposure embedding. The embedding, along with feature embeddings from a separate MPGNN and the unit's own treatment, feeds a TARNet or CFR+ counterfactual outcome model. Training is end-to-end with a composite loss combining factual prediction error, IPM-based balance loss, and three custom regularizers—coverage loss (pushing the exposure distribution toward uniform on [0,1]), entropy loss (sharpening mask weights), and sparsity loss (encouraging few active features)—that jointly enforce expressiveness, invariance, and bounded representation.

## Results

On the BlogCatalog semi-synthetic dataset, EGONETGNN-CFR+ achieves the lowest PEHE (ε_PEHE) of 0.95±0.5 under the clustering coefficient mechanism and 2.24±1.6 under mutual connections, while EGONETGNN-TARNet achieves 1.47±0.9 under connected components. Across all four peer exposure mechanisms (clustering coefficient, connected components, mutual connections, attribute similarity), the two EGONETGNN variants are among the top two performers, with the largest margins over baselines on structural mechanisms (e.g., 0.95 vs. 2.39 for GNN-Motifs on clustering coefficient). The method also demonstrates robustness on denser Barabasi-Albert networks, where baselines degrade significantly as edge density increases.

## Baselines & Comparisons

EGONETGNN outperforms all eight baselines on at least three of four mechanisms, with the most dramatic wins over 1GNN-HSIC, DWR, AEMNet, TNet, and CauGramer, which suffer 3–10× higher PEHE on structural mechanisms (e.g., AEMNet reaches 13.33 on mutual connections vs. 2.24 for Ours-CFR+). The closest competitor is INE-TARNet (Adhikari & Zheleva, 2025), which slightly edges out EGONETGNN on attribute similarity (3.59 vs. 3.65) and is competitive on mutual connections (2.50 vs. 2.24), but degrades sharply on connected components (4.78 vs. 1.47). GNN-Motifs (Yuan et al., 2021), which uses manually extracted causal network motifs, is competitive on clustering coefficient (2.39 vs. 0.95) and mutual connections (2.81 vs. 2.24) but is outperformed by 3.4× on connected components (4.98 vs. 1.47). NetEst (Jiang & Sun, 2022) and TNet (Chen et al., 2024), both relying on fraction-of-treated-peers exposure, are consistently outperformed by 2–4× across all mechanisms. The gap widens substantially on denser networks (higher edge density in BA networks), where baselines using homogeneous or naively learned exposure representations lose expressiveness for capturing local structural motifs.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the TARNet/CFR counterfactual outcome modeling framework (Shalit et al., 2017) to the network interference setting by introducing a dedicated exposure mapping learning module built on GNNs. It departs from prior GNN-based causal inference methods (NetEst, TNet, DWR, AEMNet, CauGramer) that either assume a fixed exposure mapping (e.g., fraction of treated peers) or use off-the-shelf message-passing GNNs (GCN, GIN) that provably cannot count closed-triad substructures. The key architectural departure is the ego-network transformation, which converts triangle motifs into dyads to overcome the expressiveness limitation of standard MPGNNs identified by Chen et al. (2020), combined with custom coverage, entropy, and sparsity losses to regularize the learned exposure representation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Misspecified exposure mapping functions are a primary source of bias in network causal inference, and this paper provides both a theoretical expressiveness guarantee (capturing all dyad, open/closed triad, and open tetrad causal network motifs) and a practical end-to-end framework that outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across diverse unknown influence mechanisms. Key limitations include reliance on the neighborhood interference assumption, absence of confidence intervals or formal convergence guarantees for the complex GNN architecture, and a computational overhead proportional to edge density times average degree. Open questions include extending to multi-hop interference, establishing asymptotic properties for heterogeneous effect estimation with learned exposure mappings, and scaling to very large networks.
