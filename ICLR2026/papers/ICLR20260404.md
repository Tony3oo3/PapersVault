## Problem

Open-source automated theorem provers lag significantly behind closed-source systems like AlphaProof, and prior open models (e.g., DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B) rely on extreme parameter counts and large sampling budgets to reach competitive accuracy. Two specific gaps remain: integrating Lean compiler feedback into long chain-of-thought proof generation for iterative self-correction, and calibrating training data difficulty to provide effective learning signals as the model improves. This paper addresses both to achieve SOTA open-source ATP with models 20–80× smaller than prior leaders.

## Input / Output

The model takes a Lean 4 theorem statement as input and generates a complete formal proof accompanied by a chain-of-thought reasoning trace, verified under Lean 4.9.0-rc1. In self-correction mode, the input additionally includes the model's previous (failed) proof attempt and the parsed Lean compiler error messages, enabling up to 2 iterative repair rounds within a 40K-token context (extendable to 128K with YaRN for 5 rounds). The initial generation round uses a 30K-token budget, and the model is trained on a multi-task mixture of 50% whole-proof generation and 50% self-correction inputs during RL.

## Method

The core framework combines three innovations on top of standard expert iteration and RL. First, verifier-guided self-correction trains the model to interpret Lean compiler error messages and produce proof repairs, integrated natively into long CoT generation via a multi-task RL setup (50% proof generation, 50% correction) using a hybrid GRPO algorithm (Dr.GRPO group-normalization removal + DAPO clip-higher, overlong penalties, and dynamic sampling restricted to pass rates in (0, 0.75]). Second, scaffolded data synthesis augments the training statement set through two channels: rule-based extraction of subgoals from failed proofs via Lean's `extract_goal` tactic, and LLM-based generation of simpler/harder problem variants (Qwen3-32B) that are formalized and filtered for correctness and difficulty. Third, model averaging (interpolating base and fine-tuned weights with α=0.8) is applied after each SFT and RL stage to counteract the diversity collapse that degrades pass@N. The pipeline cold-starts Qwen3 on DeepSeek-Prover-V2 distilled data, then iteratively refines through three SFT rounds (S1→S2→S3) with scaffolded data, followed by RL and final averaging.

## Results

Goedel-Prover-V2-32B achieves 88.1% pass@32 on MiniF2F (90.4% with self-correction), surpassing the previous SOTA DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (82.4%) while being ~21× smaller. On PutnamBench, the 32B model solves 86 problems at pass@184 with self-correction, more than doubling DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B's 47 at pass@1024. The 8B variant reaches 84.6% pass@32 on MiniF2F, outperforming the 671B DeepSeek model despite being 80× smaller. On FIMO and DeepSeek-ProverBench, Goedel-Prover-V2-32B with revision achieves 9.06 and 2.46 respectively, leading all open-source baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (the prior open-source SOTA), Goedel-Prover-V2-32B wins on MiniF2F by 5.7 points at pass@32 (88.1% vs 82.4%), on PutnamBench by solving 43 vs 22 problems at pass@32 (and 57 vs 47 when comparing self-correction at pass@32 to vanilla at pass@1024), and on FIMO (7.05 vs 5.70) and ProverBench (1.85 vs 0.31). However, Goedel-Prover-V2-32B loses on ProofNet (22.6% vs 30.5%), which the authors attribute to ProofNet's reliance on Mathlib tactic retrieval rather than reasoning. Against Kimina-Prover-72B, Goedel-Prover-V2-32B leads by 19.3 points on MiniF2F at pass@32 (88.1% vs 68.85%), and the 8B model also outperforms Kimina-Prover-70B (84.6% vs 84.0%). Compared to the authors' own prior Goedel-Prover-SFT, the V2 model improves by 30.5 points on MiniF2F at pass@32. The 8B model's ability to match or exceed the 671B DeepSeek model highlights the efficiency gains from scaffolded data synthesis and model averaging.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the whole-proof generation paradigm for Lean-based ATP by integrating verifier-guided self-correction with long chain-of-thought reasoning, building on the repair ideas from BALDUR (First et al., 2023) and the RL-based subgoal decomposition of DeepSeek-Prover-V2 (Ren et al., 2025). It departs from prior open-source provers (DeepSeek-Prover-V2, Kimina-Prover) in three key ways: (1) scaffolded data synthesis that generates training statements at calibrated difficulty levels via both rule-based goal extraction and LLM-based problem transformation, (2) explicit multi-task RL that jointly trains proof generation and self-correction, and (3) model averaging to counteract diversity collapse during expert iteration. The training pipeline cold-starts from DeepSeek-Prover-V2 distillations on Qwen3, then iteratively refines through SFT and a hybrid GRPO/DAPO RL stage, positioning the work as a systems-level integration of existing components rather than a single algorithmic breakthrough.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80× parameter efficiency gap (8B beating 671B on MiniF2F) and the open-source release of models, code, and data make this a practically important contribution for the ATP community, lowering the barrier to building competitive formal provers. However, the paper itself identifies significant limitations: performance on ProofNet reveals a reasoning-versus-knowledge-retrieval gap that RAG partially addresses but at the cost of MiniF2F accuracy, and the benchmark suite skews toward algebra and number theory, leaving geometry and combinatorics underrepresented. Open questions include whether scaffolded data synthesis and model averaging transfer to other formal systems or domains, whether the self-correction gains persist as Lean/Mathlib evolve, and how to integrate metacognitive self-assessment to reduce reliance on brute-force sampling.
