## Problem

Existing LLM-based financial analysis tools focus on textual reports and social media sentiment but neglect interpretable reasoning over historical stock price data (Technical Analysis). This task is uniquely challenging because it requires switching between domains: inputs and outputs are time-series, while the reasoning step must be in natural language. Current time-series LLMs sacrifice verbal reasoning by reprogramming embedding spaces, and general LLMs are 'remarkably bad' at zero-shot time-series reasoning.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262175/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a 10-day window of OHLCV stock data (open, high, low, volume, close, adjusted close) and produces a 10-day forward price trajectory. The raw time-series is consumed in two parallel forms: as numerical input to a GPT-2-based forecasting backbone, and as textual annotations (statistics plus 10 financial technical indicators like SMA, RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands) fed to a Qwen2.5-7B reasoning LLM. The output is a pair: a structured verbal reasoning trace (enclosed in <think> tags) explaining the forecast via technical indicators, and the predicted price sequence.

## Method

VTA combines three components: (1) a time-series reasoning LLM trained via Time-GRPO, a modified GRPO objective that uses inverse MSE as the reward signal, optimized through a three-stage pipeline (cold-start RL → rejection-sampling SFT on bottom-10th-percentile MSE traces → RL for reasoning policy); (2) a GPT-2 forecasting backbone that aligns time-series tokens with word embeddings via PCA-reduced principal components and multi-head cross-attention (adapted from CALF); and (3) joint conditional training using classifier-free guidance, where the forecasting model is conditioned on descriptive attributes (max, min, mean) extracted from the reasoning trace's predicted series, and trained concurrently on conditional and unconditional paths with p_uncond=0.3. At inference, the final forecast is a guidance-scale-weighted combination of conditional and unconditional outputs, yielding both accurate predictions and interpretable reasoning traces.

## Results

VTA achieves state-of-the-art forecasting accuracy across all four stock datasets, with an average MSE of 0.1178 and MAE of 0.2122 on the combined dataset, representing a 46.7% average MSE improvement and 34.2% MAE improvement over the best baseline. On the primary StockNet benchmark, VTA reaches MSE 0.0659 and MAE 0.1701, outperforming the closest competitor CALF (MSE 0.0674, MAE 0.1738). In portfolio optimization, VTA also achieves the highest Sharpe ratio (1.7190) among all compared models, confirming practical investment value.

## Baselines & Comparisons

VTA outperforms all 16 baselines on both MSE and MAE across every dataset. The closest competitor is CALF (MSE 0.1235 vs. 0.1178 on All Data), a time-series LLM that aligns word embeddings to time-series tokens but lacks verbal reasoning; VTA improves MSE by ~4.6% over CALF. Among non-LLM time-series models, TimesNet (MSE 0.1286) and Autoformer (MSE 0.1290) are the strongest, both benefiting from trend-seasonality decomposition, yet VTA still leads by ~8–9% MSE. The inference-only reasoning LLMs (GPT-4.1 mini, DeepSeek-R1) perform substantially worse on forecasting (MSE 0.14–0.20) despite producing fluent text, confirming that verbal reasoning alone is insufficient for accurate time-series prediction without a dedicated forecasting backbone. In the portfolio optimization evaluation, VTA achieves the highest Sharpe ratio (1.7190) compared to TimeLLM (1.5230) and Autoformer (1.4736), while also ranking near-best on returns (0.2409 vs. Autoformer's 0.2495) and drawdown (−0.0883 vs. Non-stationary's −0.0825). Expert evaluation further confirms VTA's reasoning traces are significantly preferred over GPT-4.1 mini and DeepSeek-R1 on Depth, Accuracy, and Relevance metrics (p < 0.001), with the largest gaps in technical indicator usage.

## Relation to Prior Work

VTA sits at the intersection of three research threads: financial LLMs (which analyze textual reports but ignore price data), time-series LLMs like Time-LLM and CALF (which reprogram embedding spaces to produce forecasts but sacrifice verbal reasoning), and the GRPO reinforcement learning objective from DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024), which the authors adapt into Time-GRPO with an inverse-MSE reward. The cross-modal alignment architecture is borrowed from CALF (Liu et al., 2025a), and the conditioning mechanism directly repurposes classifier-free guidance (Ho & Salimans, 2022) from diffusion models to condition time-series forecasts on reasoning-derived attributes. The key departure from prior work is the explicit separation of 'verbal reasoning' (an LLM producing interpretable technical-analysis traces) from 'latent reasoning' (a time-series backbone capturing low-level patterns), unified through a joint conditional training objective rather than through embedding-space reprogramming.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first framework to simultaneously produce accurate short-term stock price trajectories and domain-expert-validated reasoning traces, addressing a gap where prior methods forced a trade-off between interpretability and forecasting accuracy. The multi-stage RL pipeline (cold-start GRPO → rejection-sampling SFT → reward-guided GRPO) provides a reusable recipe for teaching LLMs to reason over time-series without supervision, which could transfer to other domains. However, the approach is limited to 10-day horizons, the reasoning traces degrade to simple trend extrapolation on non-financial data, and the performance gains over strong baselines like CALF are modest (~2–3% MSE). Open questions include whether the verbal-latent separation scales to longer horizons, whether more stock characteristics (trend/seasonality decomposition, non-stationarity) can be incorporated, and whether the conditioning mechanism can be replaced by more advanced cross-modal control techniques.
