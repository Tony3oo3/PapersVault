## Problem

Existing DyTAG datasets (e.g., DTGB) suffer from poor textual quality—node texts are often mere usernames or email addresses, and edge texts are excessively brief—making them inadequate for generative tasks that require semantically rich inputs. Additionally, no standardized task formulations or holistic evaluation protocols exist for DyTAG generation, with prior work focusing almost exclusively on discriminative tasks. This paper addresses both gaps by proposing the first generative DyTAG benchmark with high-quality text-rich datasets and novel generation tasks.

## Input / Output

The framework consumes a seed DyTAG (the first 1,000 edges and their corresponding nodes) with rich node/edge textual attributes, along with source and destination node sets, and an LLM backbone (DeepSeek-R1, Llama-3-70B, Qwen2.5-72B, or GPT-4o-Mini). Generation proceeds iteratively in rounds of 50 edges, producing a full DyTAG with generated nodes (in IDGG), edges with timestamps, edge labels, and semantically coherent node/edge texts. The generated graph must simultaneously preserve structural topology (power-law degree distributions), temporal dynamics, and textual quality relative to the ground-truth DyTAG.

## Method

GAG-General is an LLM-based multi-agent framework where each node is represented by an agent equipped with a memory module that records historical neighbor interactions, capturing structural and temporal dynamics. An optional reflection mechanism distills these memories into condensed summaries, analogous to message aggregation in GNNs, enabling more effective integration of historical context. The generation pipeline is iterative and expansive: in each round, source node agents select destination nodes based on their memory and contextual features, then edges (with generated texts and labels) are produced between selected pairs; for IDGG, new nodes are generated and added to the node set before the selection step, modeling inductive graph expansion. The framework generalizes across bipartite and non-bipartite structures and diverse domains without domain-specific customization. Evaluation employs three complementary metric families: graph structural metrics (Degree/Spectra MMD and power-law validity), a textual quality metric using LLM-as-Evaluator across five criteria (Contextual Fidelity, Personality Depth, Dynamic Adaptability, Immersive Quality, Content Richness), and an extended JL-Metric that condenses DyTAGs into a unified embedding space for joint structural-temporal-textual similarity scoring.

## Results

Under IDGG (the more challenging inductive task), GAG-General (best across four LLM backbones) achieves Degree MMD of 0.073–0.370 and Graph Embedding scores of 0.661–0.828 across Sephora, Dianping, and Cora, substantially outperforming all baselines. GAG-General satisfies the power-law validity criterion (D_k < 0.15, α ∈ [2,3]) on all three datasets, while VRDAG fails on all three and DG-Gen/TIGGER-I fail on two of three. Under TDGG, GAG-General achieves Degree MMD below 0.3 on six of eight GDGB datasets and satisfies power-law validity on six of eight, with textual quality scores averaging 4.3–5.0 (out of 5) across LLM backbones.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GAG-General decisively outperforms all three dynamic graph generation baselines on IDGG. VRDAG is the weakest baseline, with Degree MMD 2–12× higher (e.g., 0.887 vs. 0.150 on Dianping) and Graph Embedding scores near zero (0.011–0.053 vs. 0.661–0.828), indicating it fails to capture both structure and text. DG-Gen is the closest competitor on structural metrics (Dianping Degree MMD 0.167 vs. 0.150) but still lags significantly on Graph Embedding (0.517 vs. 0.769 on Dianping; 0.056 vs. 0.828 on Cora) and fails power-law validity on two of three datasets. TIGGER-I sits in between, achieving moderate structural fidelity but poor embedding scores (0.085–0.580). On TDGG discriminative sub-tasks, GAG-General outperforms five DGNN baselines (JODIE, TGN, CAWN, GraphMixer, DyGFormer) on edge classification across most datasets, while gains on node retrieval are less pronounced, suggesting DGNNs still hold an edge when trained on large-scale data but GAG-General better leverages the limited 1,000-edge seed with its text-rich context.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends GAG (Ji et al., 2025), an LLM-based multi-agent framework originally designed for bipartite social graph generation, into GAG-General, which generalizes to both bipartite and non-bipartite DyTAG structures across diverse domains. It also adapts the JL-Metric (Hosseini et al., 2025) for dynamic graph generation quality assessment by integrating textual node/edge features. Conceptually, the paper departs from prior dynamic graph generation work (TIGGER, DG-Gen, VRDAG) that relies solely on structural/temporal information or BERT-compressed embeddings, instead operating directly on raw semantic text. It also positions itself against DTGB (Zhang et al., 2024a), the first DyTAG benchmark, arguing that DTGB's textual attributes are too shallow (usernames, email addresses) to support generative tasks.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GDGB fills a clear gap as the first benchmark purpose-built for generative DyTAG learning, providing 8 high-quality datasets, two new task formulations (TDGG/IDGG), and multifaceted evaluation metrics that jointly assess structure, temporal dynamics, and text. A notable finding is that DTGB's textual features actively degrade generation fidelity (especially for VRDAG), while GDGB's richer text consistently improves it—challenging the assumption that any textual attribute is beneficial. Key open questions include whether the LLM-based multi-agent pipeline scales to very large graphs, whether the LLM-as-evaluator protocol is sufficiently reliable for benchmarking, and how to improve structural fidelity in IDGG where new node generation introduces additional complexity. The hub-node divergence analysis (generated vs. ground-truth top products) suggests practical utility for e-commerce forecasting, though the paper acknowledges the generative pipeline still requires refinement.
