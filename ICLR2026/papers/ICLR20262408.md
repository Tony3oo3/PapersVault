## Problem

Fourier Neural Operators (FNO) suffer a pure-spectral bottleneck: their spectral off-diagonals are tied to the training input distribution, causing them to fail on position-dependent dynamics (variable-coefficient PDEs) where the ground-truth operator is spectrally dense. Additionally, prior KAN-based operator networks have not demonstrated symbolic recovery of the learned operator, leaving interpretable operator learning largely unexplored.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20262408/cropped_page4_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

For synthetic benchmarks, KANO takes 1D functions on a periodic domain (N=128 grid points) and produces the operator-applied output; training uses three function families (sine beats, chirped cosine, periodic harmonics) while testing uses three unseen families (sinc pulse, Gaussian×Hermite, wave packet). For the quantum benchmark, input consists of position and momentum probability mass functions (PMFs) from projective measurements on 128-point grids—never the full complex wavefunction—and output is the evolved quantum state trajectory over 100 time steps (ΔT=0.1 ms), with only the first 10 steps used for training. A third output is the closed-form symbolic formula of the learned operator, recovered from the KAN edge visualizations.

## Method

KANO replaces FNO's block-diagonal spectral multiplier with a Kohn-Nirenberg quantized pseudo-differential symbol p(x,ξ) that is jointly parameterized in both spatial and spectral bases, so each term of the operator is represented where it is sparse (differential terms spectrally, localized terms spatially). Each KANO layer applies Op_m(p) followed by a KAN-based non-linear activation Φ, with both p and Φ implemented as compact KAN sub-networks (cubic B-spline edges) that are directly inspectable and amenable to symbolic regression. The architecture deliberately omits FNO's wide lift-up and projection networks to preserve KAN tractability for symbolic recovery. Theoretically, the paper proves FNO requires super-exponential parameter scaling for position-dependent dynamics (Theorem 1) while KANO scales polynomially in the desired accuracy (Theorem 2), and empirically demonstrates that KANO with only 152 parameters (0.03% of FNO's 566k) achieves an order-of-magnitude lower loss and recovers ground-truth operator coefficients to four decimal places.

## Results

On three synthetic position-dependent operators (G1–G3), KANO (152 parameters) achieves relative ℓ₂ losses of 1.04, 0.629, and 0.716 (×10⁻⁴) on the out-of-distribution test set, with a B/A generalization ratio of only 1.03–1.38, while FNO (566k parameters) degrades by 7–15× on the same test set. KANO uses just 0.03% of FNO's parameters yet achieves an order-of-magnitude lower loss. On the long-horizon quantum dynamics benchmark, Q-KANO attains state infidelity of ≈6×10⁻⁶ after 90 additional time steps, compared to ≈1.5×10⁻² for FNO trained with ideal full wave-function data—a four-order-of-magnitude improvement. KANO also recovers ground-truth operator coefficients to the fourth decimal place via symbolic readout of its KAN edges.

## Baselines & Comparisons

KANO decisively outperforms all four FNO-family baselines on both in-distribution accuracy and out-of-distribution generalization. Against FNO (566k params), KANO (152 params) achieves 6–16× lower relative ℓ₂ loss on the OOD test set while using 0.03% of the parameters; FNO's B/A ratio of 7–15× confirms its in-sample overfitting. U-FNO and AM-FNO, which add localized spatial kernels to FNO, actually degrade more severely on OOD data (B/A up to 42× and 26× respectively), suggesting that patching FNO with local terms does not resolve the fundamental spectral bottleneck. PDNO (pseudo-differential neural operator) is the most stable FNO variant (B/A of 4.5–6.7×) but still degrades 3–5× more than KANO on the OOD set. On the quantum Hamiltonian learning benchmark, Q-KANO with pos&mom-type training achieves state infidelity of 5.6–6.3×10⁻⁶, outperforming FNO trained with ideal full wave-function data (1.5–1.6×10⁻²) by four orders of magnitude, and the KANO_MLP ablation confirms that the dual-domain architecture (not KAN specifically) drives the generalization gain.

## Relation to Prior Work

KANO sits at the intersection of three research threads: Fourier Neural Operators (FNO) for spectral operator learning, Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KAN) for interpretable function approximation, and the classical Kohn-Nirenberg pseudo-differential operator calculus. The paper explicitly positions itself against FNO's 'pure-spectral bottleneck,' showing that prior FNO variants (U-FNO, AM-FNO, PDNO) that inject local spatial kernels still privilege the spectral basis and cannot achieve optimal sparsity in the spatial domain. Unlike DeepOKAN, which swaps MLPs for KANs in DeepONet, KANO's contribution is architectural: it jointly parameterizes the operator symbol in both spatial and spectral bases, and is the first to demonstrate closed-form symbolic recovery of a learned operator from KAN edges.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper shifts the operator-learning paradigm from universal approximation (which FNO already guarantees) to universal generalization with interpretability, providing the first rigorous proof that FNO suffers a super-exponential curse of dimensionality on position-dependent dynamics. This has practical implications for scientific AI: learned operators that cannot generalize outside their training distribution are unreliable as surrogates for simulation. Key limitations include the 1D scope of all experiments, the exclusion of wide lift-up/projection networks (which recent work shows are essential for high-dimensional benchmarks), and the computational cost of the double-sum Kohn-Nirenberg quantization. Extending KANO to higher dimensions and reconciling symbolic interpretability with the wide architectures needed for complex PDEs remain open.
