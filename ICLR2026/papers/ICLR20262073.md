## Problem

Despite substantial progress in speech-language models (SpeechLMs) for Spoken Question-Answering (SQA), the data pipelines behind these systems remain largely unexamined in controlled settings. It is unclear how to process raw web-crawled audio into trainable speech-text chunks, whether synthetic data can effectively augment web-crawled corpora, or how to interleave speech and text tokens during pretraining for optimal modality alignment. This paper fills that gap with a systematic, data-centric ablation study that isolates each data-processing decision on a level playing field.

## Input / Output

The model consumes audio questions (tokenized at 12.5 Hz via a conformer encoder + finite scalar quantizer, yielding 80 ms per speech token) and text questions, and produces text answers during pretraining evaluation (S→T) and both text and audio answers after supervised fine-tuning. Training sequences are interleaved speech-text chunks packed to 16,384 tokens, with a fixed 60% text-only / 40% speech-text data mixture. SQA evaluation uses a 4-option MCQ cloze format scored by log-likelihood, with chance level at 25%.

## Method

The paper conducts controlled ablations on a ~3.8B-parameter SpeechLM (1B conformer speech encoder + FSQ tokenizer + 2.8B base LM) and identifies three data-centric interventions that compound to yield SpeLangy, which outperforms models up to 3× larger by 10.2% average SQA accuracy. First, fine-grained interleaving—keeping diarized segments as short (~5.2 s) chunks rather than merging same-speaker segments into long (~19.2 s) chunks—improves SQA by 3.1% by increasing modality-switch frequency. Second, two synthetic datasets (Krist: LLM-rewritten knowledge-rich web text + MeloTTS; Quest: regex-mined questions + GPT-4o chain-of-thought answers + TTS) are mixed with web-crawled audio, boosting SQA by up to 7.2% and closing domain-coverage gaps. Third, deterministic alternating speech-text chunk ordering during training outperforms stochastic modality sampling by 1% by maximizing cross-modal transitions. The authors show these gains transfer to understanding-only (loss-masked) settings and survive post-training SFT, and they attribute the improvements to reduced reverse-KL divergence between audio-conditioned and text-conditioned output distributions.

## Results

SpeLangy (3.8B) achieves 51.8% average SQA accuracy across Spoken-Web-Questions (45.7%), Spoken-TriviaQA (44.6%), and Spoken-LLaMA-Questions (65.0%), outperforming base models up to 2.8× larger by 9.8–11.1% absolute. On text understanding, SpeLangy scores 67.3% MMLU, 71.9% GSM-8k, and 37.6% HumanEval, showing strong preservation (and in some cases improvement) of text capabilities after speech-text interleaved pretraining. The model also achieves competitive SQA performance with strongly post-trained models (Voxtral-mini: 51.2%, GLM-4-Voice: 53.4%) without any instruction-tuning.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against base-model baselines, SpeLangy (3.8B) outperforms Kimi-Audio (10.5B) by 10.2% average SQA (51.8 vs 41.6), Qwen-Audio (8.4B) by 11.1% (51.8 vs 40.7), and Qwen-2-Audio (8.4B) by 9.8% (51.8 vs 42.0), while being 2.2–2.8× smaller. Against post-trained models, SpeLangy slightly edges out Voxtral-mini (4.7B, SFT) by 0.6% (51.8 vs 51.2) without any instruction-tuning, and trails GLM-4-Voice (9.9B, SFT) by 1.6% (51.8 vs 53.4), though the latter has undergone task-specific SFT. On text understanding, SpeLangy surpasses Gemma-2 (2.6B) by large margins (MMLU 67.3 vs 56.1, GSM-8k 71.9 vs 30.3, HumanEval 37.6 vs 19.5) and Gemma-3 (4B) on MMLU and GSM-8k, while remaining competitive with Qwen-2.5 (3B) on MMLU (67.3 vs 65.6) but trailing on GSM-8k (71.9 vs 79.1) and HumanEval (37.6 vs 42.1). The largest single-benchmark gain is on Spoken-TriviaQA, where SpeLangy scores 44.6% versus 30.3–33.8% for the Qwen-Audio family, suggesting the synthetic Quest dataset (QA-formatted) particularly benefits factual recall.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself as the first systematic data-centric ablation study for speech-language interleaved pretraining, drawing methodological inspiration from data curation work in language (DCLM, FineWeb) and vision (Dinov2, DataComp) where controlled data experiments have proven decisive. It builds on the interleaved speech-text pretraining paradigm introduced by Nguyen et al. (2025b) and Zeng et al. (2024b), and initializes from Apple's 2.8B base LM with a Conformer-based speech tokenizer using finite scalar quantization. The key departure from prior SpeechLMs (Kimi-Audio, GLM-4-Voice, Baichuan-Audio, MiMo-Audio) is that those works describe modeling architectures comprehensively but leave data pipeline choices unevaluated; this paper isolates and ablates three specific data decisions—interleaving granularity, synthetic data construction, and modality sampling—under a controlled single-task protocol.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides the first actionable, evidence-based data curation recipe for SpeechLM pretraining, showing that a 3.8B model trained with careful data interventions outperforms 3× larger models by over 10% on SQA benchmarks without any architectural novelty. The mechanistic analysis (reduced modality KL-divergence, improved domain coverage) gives practitioners a principled understanding of *why* each intervention helps, not just that it does. Open questions remain around optimal multi-source data mixing ratios (acknowledged as non-trivial), whether findings generalize beyond SQA to other speech tasks, and how these data strategies interact with scaling to much larger model sizes. The contamination analysis, while reassuring, relies on n-gram matching with acknowledged false positives, leaving some residual uncertainty about the synthetic data contribution.
