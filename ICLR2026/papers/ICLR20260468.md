## Problem

Human visual preferences are inherently multi-dimensional (aesthetics, detail fidelity, semantic alignment), yet existing preference datasets collapse them into single binary winner/loser labels. The paper theoretically proves that this compression generates conflicting gradient signals during Diffusion-DPO training—where the model is simultaneously rewarded for desirable attributes in the 'winner' and penalized for the same attributes in the 'loser'—leading to oscillating updates and suboptimal convergence.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260468/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method fine-tunes a pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model (SD1.5 or SDXL) using preference pairs (prompt, winner image, loser image) from Pick-a-Pic V2. At inference time, the model takes a text prompt and generates a 512×512 (SD1.5) or 1024×1024 (SDXL) RGB image with zero additional architectural overhead. The key distinction is in training: rather than consuming a single holistic preference label per pair, the method decomposes each pair into timestep-conditional pseudo-labels across the full 1000-step diffusion timeline, producing fine-grained preference signals that decouple conflicting dimensions.

## Method

Semi-DPO is a two-stage semi-supervised framework. Stage 1 (Multi-Reward Consensus) partitions the dataset using five pre-trained reward models (PickScore, HPS v2, CLIP Score, LAION Aesthetics, ImageReward): a pair enters the clean labeled set only if all models unanimously agree with the human label, yielding ~21% of the data; the rest form a noisy unlabeled set. Stage 2 (Iterative Self-Training) first cold-starts a model on the clean set, then leverages the DPO loss's implicit binary-classifier property at each timestep—the per-timestep logit z_θ^(t) serves as both a preference prediction (sign) and confidence score (magnitude)—to generate timestep-conditional pseudo-labels for the noisy set. A dynamic threshold per timestep interval (10 intervals over t∈[0,999]) filters for high-confidence predictions, and a composite objective combining an anchor loss on the clean set with a pseudo-label loss on the filtered noisy set drives retraining. Two iterations suffice for convergence, and the entire pipeline requires no architectural changes, no explicit reward model during training, and no additional human annotation.

## Results

On the Pick-a-Pic V2 evaluation set, Semi-DPO achieves an MPS of 11.030 on SD1.5 (+14.5% over base) and 12.548 on SDXL (+6.3% over base), consistently outperforming all baselines across ImageReward, HPS v2.1, Aesthetic, and MPS metrics. On GenEval, Semi-DPO reaches an overall score of 47.31 (SD1.5) and 58.41 (SDXL), surpassing the strongest baselines (InPO at 46.74 and Diff-DPO at 58.02 respectively). On T2I-CompBench++, Semi-DPO leads on 2D-Spatial (0.183/0.2133) and Complex (0.320/0.3728) for SD1.5/SDXL, confirming gains in compositional generation.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Semi-DPO dominates all baselines on SD1.5 across every metric and dataset: on Pick-a-Pic V2 it improves ImageReward by +0.172 over Diff-KTO (0.801 vs 0.629) and +0.504 over Diff-DPO (0.801 vs 0.297), while MPS gains of +8.3% and +8.8% over Diff-KTO and Diff-DPO respectively highlight the multi-dimensional preference advantage. On SDXL, Semi-DPO leads Diff-DPO on ImageReward (1.056 vs 0.953), HPS v2.1 (0.304 vs 0.299), Aesthetic (6.210 vs 6.031), and MPS (12.548 vs 12.210), though Diff-DPO retains a narrow edge on PickScore (22.467 vs 22.352) and CLIP (0.292 vs 0.287). Against MaPO on SDXL, Semi-DPO wins on ImageReward (+0.204), HPS v2.1 (+0.014), Aesthetic (+0.021), and MPS (+0.661). On GenEval, Semi-DPO achieves the best overall score for both architectures (47.31 SD1.5, 58.41 SDXL), outperforming InPO by +0.57 and Diff-DPO by +0.39 respectively. On T2I-CompBench++, Semi-DPO leads on 2D-Spatial and Complex dimensions for both bases, while InPO edges ahead on Color (SD1.5: 0.482 vs 0.471) and 3D-Spatial (SDXL: 0.3671 vs 0.3723 is actually Semi-DPO winning).

## Relation to Prior Work

Semi-DPO sits at the intersection of two well-developed lines of work: diffusion model alignment via Direct Preference Optimization (Diffusion-DPO, Wallace et al. 2024; DPO, Rafailov et al. 2023) and learning with noisy labels reframed as semi-supervised learning (DivideMix, Li et al. 2020; Noisy Student Training, Xie et al. 2020). The key conceptual departure is identifying the multi-dimensional preference conflict in holistic binary annotations as a structured label-noise problem, then exploiting the DPO loss's implicit binary-classifier property at each diffusion timestep to generate fine-grained pseudo-labels without architectural modifications. Unlike prior offline DPO variants (Diff-KTO, MaPO, InPO) that modify the loss or sampling procedure, Semi-DPO modifies the data pipeline itself, treating the diffusion model as its own latent reward model for self-correction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a clear theoretical mechanism (variance lower bound proportional to p_a·p_c) explaining why standard Diffusion-DPO struggles with real-world preference data, and offers a practical, architecture-free remedy that requires no additional human annotation or explicit reward model during training. The finding that only ~21% of Pick-a-Pic V2 pairs survive a five-model consensus filter quantifies the severity of the label-noise problem in a way that should inform future dataset curation. Open questions include whether the approach extends to online DPO settings (sketched in Appendix 6.7), whether the multi-reward consensus can be replaced by learned dimension-specific scores, and whether the two-iteration convergence behavior holds for noisier or smaller datasets. The primary limitation is the operational complexity of the multi-stage pipeline, though the paper notes it is actually more computationally efficient than single-stage Diffusion-DPO.
