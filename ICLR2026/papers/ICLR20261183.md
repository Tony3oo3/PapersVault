## Problem

Existing multimodal dataset distillation methods (e.g., LoRS, MTT-VL) can only leverage teacher model knowledge from the first 20–30% of training epochs; directly incorporating late-stage teacher checkpoints causes student performance to collapse due to unstable gradient directions and pronounced cross-stage knowledge gaps. The paper identifies this phased knowledge gap as a fundamental mechanistic difference between multimodal and unimodal distillation, where the inherent sparsity and lack of explicit semantic constraints in image-text data cause the teacher to encode substantially different knowledge forms at different training stages.

## Input / Output

The method takes a real image-text dataset (e.g., 31K pairs from Flickr30K or 123K from COCO, each image paired with five captions at 224×224 resolution) and a teacher model's full parameter trajectory (10 epochs, 20 expert checkpoints). It produces P synthetic subsets (e.g., 200+299 pairs for a 500-pair budget), each containing 224×224 images, natural-language text captions, and a learnable full-rank similarity matrix. At inference, the student is trained progressively on subsets in phase order, and the total synthetic data compresses the original dataset by orders of magnitude (e.g., 1.7% for 500 pairs on Flickr30K).

## Method

PTM-ST decomposes distillation into P phases, each distilling a separate synthetic subset using a phase-specific sampling range [T_p⁻, T_p⁺] for trajectory matching, so that each subset captures knowledge from a distinct training stage. The key innovation is the Shortcut Trajectory (ST): rather than matching the full original teacher trajectory, it constructs a linearly interpolated path θ_t = (1−β_p(t))θ₀ + β_p(t)θ_{t_p} between the initial checkpoint and a phase-specific interpolation endpoint, with waypoints placed proportionally to accumulated L2 distance along the original trajectory (computed layer-wise). A theoretical proposition proves that on this interpolated trajectory, gradient differences between nearby matching points converge linearly with Δt, whereas the original trajectory offers no such guarantee—explaining the empirically observed gradient instability. An EMA smoothing step (decay 0.99) further stabilizes the synthetic dataset updates. The student is then trained sequentially on the phase-ordered subsets, progressively absorbing the teacher's evolving knowledge.

## Results

On Flickr30K with 500 distilled pairs (1.7% of original data), PTM-ST achieves IR@1=16.0, IR@5=40.5, IR@10=54.0, TR@1=22.2, TR@5=51.1, TR@10=64.6, reaching 76% of full-training performance (IR@10=69.7). On COCO with 500 pairs, it achieves IR@1=6.6, IR@5=20.5, IR@10=30.7, TR@1=6.9, TR@5=20.1, TR@10=30.0. The method delivers up to 13.5% absolute improvement over the strongest baseline (LoRS) on TR@10 for Flickr30K, with an average gain of 9.53% across all Flickr30K metrics at 500 pairs.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PTM-ST consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every metric on both Flickr30K and COCO. Against the strongest baseline LoRS, gains range from +3.3 (IR@1) to +14.0 (TR@5) on Flickr30K at 500 pairs, with the largest absolute improvements on text-to-image retrieval. Against MTT-VL and EDGE (generative distillation), PTM-ST leads by 9–24 points on IR@10, indicating that trajectory-matching with phased guidance substantially outperforms both naive multimodal adaptation and generative approaches. Core-set selection methods (Random, HERD, K-CENT, FORGET) all perform near or below random sampling, confirming that simple data selection cannot capture cross-modal dynamics. In the appendix, PTM-ST also surpasses DATM (difficulty-aligned trajectory matching) on COCO, showing that simply expanding the matching range without phased decomposition still degrades performance. At 1000 pairs, LoRS and EDGE show performance saturation or decline, while PTM-ST continues to improve (e.g., IR@10: 56.3 vs. 41.6 for LoRS on Flickr30K), demonstrating superior scalability.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the Match Training Trajectory (MTT) framework for dataset distillation to the multimodal setting, building directly on LoRS (Xu et al., 2024) and MTT-VL (Wu et al., 2023), which adapted unimodal trajectory matching to image-text pairs. Its key departure is identifying a 'phased knowledge gap' unique to multimodal distillation: unlike unimodal MTT where the full teacher trajectory is usable, multimodal distillation degrades when later-stage teacher checkpoints are incorporated. The proposed Phased Teacher Model (PTM) decomposes distillation into stage-specific sub-tasks with different teacher models, while the Shortcut Trajectory (ST) stabilizes optimization via endpoint interpolation—distinct from Matching Convexified Trajectory (Zhong et al., 2025), which only uses the final teacher checkpoint. The approach also draws on DATM (Guo et al., 2023) for dynamic range adjustment, though the paper shows that simply extending the matching range (as DATM does) fails in the multimodal setting without the phased decomposition.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a fundamental and previously unacknowledged limitation in multimodal dataset distillation: the inability to leverage late-stage teacher knowledge, which constrains all existing MTT-based multimodal methods to the first 20–30% of training. The phased decomposition with shortcut trajectories offers a principled solution with theoretical grounding (linear gradient-difference convergence), and the 76%-of-full-performance-at-1.7%-data result on Flickr30K demonstrates practical compression value. However, the method requires manual specification of interpolation endpoints and matching ranges per stage, limiting its automation and generalizability; the authors themselves flag adaptive parameter selection as key future work. Broader open questions include whether the phased knowledge gap persists with stronger encoders (e.g., full DiNo-v2 + LLM backbones) and whether the approach transfers to non-MTT distillation paradigms.
