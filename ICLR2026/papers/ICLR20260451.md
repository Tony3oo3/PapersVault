## Problem

Supervised fine-tuning of VLA models depends on costly human teleoperation demonstrations that are decoupled from the deployed policy's actual state distribution, leaving critical coverage gaps in failure regions. The paper asks whether VLA models can self-improve using RL-generated data that is aligned with the generalist's trajectory distribution, matching or surpassing fine-tuning on oracle human demonstrations for both in-distribution and out-of-distribution tasks.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260451/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The policy consumes RGB images from wrist-mounted and side-view cameras, robot proprioception (joint angles), and a language goal prompt, producing a 7-DoF action (6-DoF delta end-effector pose plus 1-DoF continuous gripper command). The residual RL specialist outputs a delta action conditioned on the frozen base policy's action, with magnitude bounded to [-ξ, ξ] via a scheduler; the combined action is a_b + a_δ. The framework is architecture-agnostic, supporting both flow-matching heads (π0) and autoregressive token heads (OpenVLA) for the final SFT stage.

## Method

PLD is a three-stage plug-and-play pipeline. Stage 1 freezes the VLA backbone and trains lightweight residual Gaussian actors per task via off-policy RL (SAC with Cal-QL critic pre-training on 50 successful base-policy rollouts), where the residual explores near the base policy with scaled delta actions. Stage 2 employs a hybrid rollout scheme: the base policy probes for a random number of initial steps (biasing state initialization toward its own visitation distribution), then the residual specialist takes over to generate recovery trajectories, yielding data that is both deployment-aligned and diverse. Stage 3 distills the collected multi-task trajectories back into the base model via standard SFT with LoRA (rank 32), agnostic to the action head architecture. The key insight is that residual policy probing captures failure-region recovery behaviors that neither pure expert rollouts nor human demonstrations cover, and distribution-aware replay prevents the generalist from overfitting to a narrow unimodal expert distribution.

## Results

On the LIBERO benchmark, PLD achieves a near-saturated 97.2% average success rate with π0 (up from 93.4% baseline) and 99.2% with OpenVLA (up from 91.8%), with the largest gain on the Goal suite (+7.9% for π0, +15.7% for OpenVLA). On SimplerEnv, PLD yields a +24.9% average improvement over Octo-SFT (96.6% vs. 71.8%), with the largest single-task gain of +50.6% on WidowX Pick Carrot. In real-world Franka experiments, PLD achieves 30/30 success on both pick-and-place and peg insertion, while RL-expert-only data drops to 16/30 and human data to 10/30 on cube pick-up. The YAM bi-manual GPU insertion task runs autonomously for over 1 hour without human intervention.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the RL specialist learning phase (Figure 5), PLD outperforms both WSRL (Zhou et al., 2024b, offline-only initialization) and RLPD (Ball et al., 2023, no base policy guidance) by a large margin across all 8 LIBERO-90 tasks, achieving over 95% success on every task within 250k interaction steps. JSRL (Uchendu et al., 2023) shows high data efficiency in general but fails to converge on some tasks, whereas PLD reliably solves all tasks. In the data-source comparison (Table 2, Figure 2), PLD data dominates base-policy rollout data (0-1 REINFORCE) across all coverage ratios: at 10% task coverage, PLD achieves 31.4% overall success vs. 10.3% for rollout data, and at 100% coverage, 87.1% vs. 48.8%. PLD data also matches or exceeds human teleoperation data on in-distribution performance while retaining comparable zero-shot generalization to unseen tasks (24.4% SR at 10% coverage vs. 21.4% for human data). In real-world Franka experiments, PLD data (30/30) substantially outperforms both RLPD data (16/30) and human data (10/30) on the cube pick-up task, demonstrating that distribution-aware RL-generated data surpasses both expert rollouts and teleoperation.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLD sits at the intersection of three research threads: (1) sample-efficient RL with data and policy priors (RLPD, WSRL, Cal-QL), (2) residual RL for robotics (ResiP, EXPO), and (3) VLA post-training via SFT (π0, OpenVLA). The key departure from prior work is the explicit alignment of data collection with the base policy's deployment distribution: rather than collecting purely expert-optimal RL rollouts or human teleoperation data agnostic to the generalist, PLD uses a 'base policy probing' mechanism where the frozen VLA rolls out for random initial steps before the residual specialist takes over. This hybrid trajectory design, combined with off-policy SAC on a lightweight residual actor (avoiding the ~62.5 GB memory cost of directly fine-tuning expressive VLA heads), positions PLD as a practical middle ground between full RL fine-tuning and pure SFT.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PLD offers a concrete, architecture-agnostic recipe for VLA self-improvement that requires no additional human demonstrations after initial SFT, addressing the central scalability bottleneck of robot foundation models. The finding that policy-aligned RL data can match or surpass human teleoperation data for both in-distribution and out-of-distribution generalization challenges the prevailing assumption that human demos are the gold standard for VLA fine-tuning. Open questions include whether the approach extends to multi-embodiment transfer, continual on-robot learning without task resets, and safety-constrained data collection in shared human-robot spaces. A notable limitation is the sensitivity to base policy quality: residual RL fails to converge when the base model is below ~80% success, limiting applicability to very hard or novel tasks.
