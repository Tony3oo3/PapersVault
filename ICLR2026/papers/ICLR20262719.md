## Problem

Two-sided matching platforms (online dating, recruitment) typically optimize for total match count or exposure fairness, but neither directly targets user retention, which is the actual revenue driver for subscription-based platforms. Match maximization concentrates matches on already-popular users while under-served users churn, and fairness objectives are only a heuristic proxy that does not guarantee retention. This paper formally defines retention maximization as the optimization objective and provides a tractable algorithm to achieve it.

## Input / Output

At each time step, the algorithm receives an arriving user's context vector (profile + interaction history), the match probability r(x,y) to every candidate on the opposite side (assumed known or estimated upstream), each user's cumulative match count m_{1:τ}, and a pre-trained retention function f̂(x,m) (an XGBoost regressor trained offline on {user features, match count, binary retention label} triples). It outputs a ranked list of K candidates from the opposite side, produced by argsort of a per-item Score(y) that jointly captures the receiver's retention gain and the candidate's own retention gain, yielding O(N log N) computation per step.

## Method

MRet formulates the optimal ranking as maximizing the sum of retention-probability gains for both the receiver and all recommended users (Eq. 9), which is NP-hard. Under a concavity assumption on the per-user retention function f, the authors derive a Jensen-inequality lower bound on the receiver-side term (Lemma 1) and a linear concavity-based bound on each candidate's position-dependent gain (Lemma 2), jointly reducing the problem to maximizing a separable per-item score Score(y). By the rearrangement inequality, the optimal assignment is simply argsort of Score(y), giving an O(N log N) dynamic LTR algorithm. The retention function f is learned via supervised XGBoost regression on offline {features, match count, retention label} data, and the algorithm adapts at every time step as new matches are observed, naturally directing scarce matching opportunities to users with the highest marginal retention gain.

## Results

On synthetic data (|X|=|Y|=1000, K=5, T=2000), MRet achieves a higher user retention rate than all baselines (Max Match, FairCo, Uniform) while obtaining only ~70% of the cumulative matches achieved by Max Match, demonstrating that match count and retention are decoupled. On real-world data from a Japanese online dating platform (1000×1000 matching matrix, T=3000), MRet achieves the highest user retention under extremely sparse match conditions where FairCo and Uniform fail to perform effective matching. MRet also outperforms the oracle MRet (best) gap narrows with more training data (n=16000 approaches oracle performance), and the method remains robust to non-concave retention functions, noise in match probabilities, and popularity drift over time.

## Baselines & Comparisons

MRet consistently outperforms all three baselines (Max Match, FairCo, Uniform) on user retention across both synthetic and real-world experiments. Against Max Match, MRet achieves higher retention with roughly 70% of the total matches, showing that maximizing match count actively harms retention by concentrating matches on already-popular users. Against FairCo (Morik et al., 2020), MRet wins on retention despite FairCo achieving similar total match counts to the oracle MRet (best); the key difference is that FairCo allocates matches without regard to individual retention thresholds, producing a spread-out match distribution, whereas MRet aligns allocations with each user's satisfactory match count (b_x), yielding mostly non-negative deviations (m_{1:T} − b_x). On real-world sparse data, Max Match becomes relatively competitive (outperforming FairCo and Uniform), but MRet still achieves the highest retention. MRet requires no hyperparameter tuning, whereas FairCo's performance is sensitive to its fairness weight λ (best at λ=0.1, still below MRet).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the dynamic Learning-to-Rank framework of Morik et al. (2020, FairCo) from two-sided matching, but fundamentally redefines the optimization objective: instead of fairness of exposure or match maximization (the dominant paradigms in reciprocal recommender systems), it directly optimizes user retention probability for both sides. It departs from the doubly-stochastic matrix formulations used by Tomita & Yokoyama (2024) and Su et al. (2022), which are computationally prohibitive in dynamic settings, by leveraging Jensen's inequality and the rearrangement inequality to reduce an NP-hard ranking problem to an O(N log N) argsort. The retention curves are learned via XGBoost regression on user features and cumulative match counts, a straightforward supervised approach applied to a novel objective.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper addresses a gap between what two-sided platforms actually optimize (match counts or fairness heuristics) and what they actually need (user retention, which drives subscription revenue). The finding that fairness-based allocation can perform comparably to random assignment in retention terms is a useful caution against treating fairness as a proxy for user satisfaction. Key limitations include the assumption that match probabilities are given, offline learning of retention functions without online adaptation, and equal weighting of both sides despite real business models often prioritizing one side. Open questions include incorporating match-probability estimation into the retention objective, online updating of retention curves, and asymmetric retention weighting for business-critical user segments.
