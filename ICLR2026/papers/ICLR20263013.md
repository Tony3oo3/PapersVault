## Problem

LLMs excel at single-answer tasks but systematically fail at Probabilistic Instruction Following (PIF) — selecting from a closed set of options according to a specified probability distribution — exhibiting positional biases, label-frequency effects, and mode collapse that skew empirical output distributions far from the target. This same failure manifests as diversity collapse in open-ended generation, where repeated queries converge to a limited set of responses, undermining test-time scaling, human-behavior simulation, and creative applications.

## Input / Output

For PIF, the input is a prompt specifying m options and a target probability vector (e.g., 'choose from [heads, tails] with probabilities [0.3, 0.7]'); for DAG, it is an open-ended question. In both cases the SSoT system prompt enforces a three-part structured output: a <random_string> tag holding the generated seed, a <thinking> tag for the manipulation reasoning, and an <answer> tag for the final selection. PIF evaluation requires K independent queries (K=100 in experiments) to build an empirical distribution, which is then compared to the target via JS/KL divergence or total variation distance.

## Method

SSoT is a two-stage prompting technique: the LLM is first instructed to generate a complex random string as an internal entropy source, then to deterministically manipulate that string (e.g., sum-mod or rolling hash over character codes) to derive the final answer — sampling from the target distribution for PIF or producing a diverse response for DAG. The key insight is that the string-generation step is task-agnostic and less susceptible to the positional and label-frequency biases that corrupt direct probabilistic selection, while the manipulation step is simple arithmetic well within the LLM's capabilities. The paper proves that total variation distance to the target distribution decreases with string length under mild conditions on per-character bias, even with autoregressive correlations in the generated string. A single unified prompt framework handles both PIF and DAG with only minimal wording changes, and the LLM autonomously adapts its extraction strategy (simple sum-mod for uniform targets, more sophisticated rolling hash for biased ones) based on task complexity, with performance scaling with reasoning trace length.

## Results

On the PIF task (measured by JS divergence, lower is better), SSoT reduces divergence by 70–99% across five frontier LLMs compared to naive prompting. For deepseek-r1, JS divergence drops from 36.09 to 3.03 (2-choice) and from 138.21 to 18.06 (biased 9-choice), approaching the PRNG reference of 1.85 and 13.72 respectively. On NoveltyBench (DAG), SSoT achieves an overall Distinct score of 6.19 (Utility 5.92), outperforming the baseline (4.70/5.17), prompt paraphrasing (5.63/5.57), and T=1.0 (5.57/6.03). On the larger WildChat dataset, SSoT raises the overall Distinct score from 3.39 (baseline) to 5.25.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SSoT is compared against five baselines on PIF: naive prompting (Baseline), high temperature, few-shot examples (k=3/10/50), prompt ensembling (50 paraphrased prompts with randomized action order), and sequential sampling with history. SSoT consistently outperforms all baselines across all five task settings and all five models; the strongest baseline is Prompt Ensemble, which performs well on unbiased tasks but degrades on biased distributions, while SSoT maintains near-PRNG performance in both. An ablation with an externally injected random string also improves over the baseline but still trails SSoT on biased tasks, suggesting the LLM benefits from internally generating strings of a length and type it can more easily manipulate. On NoveltyBench (DAG), SSoT beats prompt paraphrasing (6.19 vs 5.63 Distinct overall) and higher temperatures (6.19 vs 5.57 at T=1.0) on diversity while also matching or exceeding their Utility scores, and it outperforms both seed-injection and tool-call baselines for external randomness. In the adversarial Rock-Paper-Scissors game, SSoT maintains a near-zero average score against 10 pattern-exploiting 'Black Belt' bots, whereas the Baseline prompt is exploited and the Simple prompt is consistently defeated.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper positions itself against two distinct lines of work: (1) studies documenting LLMs' inability to sample faithfully from specified distributions (Meister et al. 2025, Gu et al. 2025, Hopkins et al. 2023, Van Koevering & Kleinberg 2024), and (2) training-based diversity enhancement methods (DARLING, DivPO, Slocum et al. 2025). SSoT departs from both by requiring no retraining and no external randomness source; instead, it repurposes the LLM's own autoregressive reasoning as an entropy source, drawing on 2-universal hashing theory (Carter & Wegman 1979) and the Leftover Hash Lemma (Vadhan 2012) to provide TV-distance guarantees. The paper explicitly distinguishes itself from LLM calibration, which aligns token-level confidence with accuracy on single-answer tasks rather than performing actual sampling over multiple acceptable answers.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SSoT offers a zero-training, prompt-only solution to a problem that has resisted temperature tuning, few-shot examples, and prompt ensembling, with the practical advantage of full batch-API compatibility and no external tool dependencies. The RPS adversarial experiment demonstrates a concrete application where distributional faithfulness translates directly into unexploitability, a property with implications for multi-agent systems and game-playing agents. Key limitations include dependence on the model's reasoning capability (models below ~8B parameters struggle) and vulnerability to bias propagation when the generated string has structural patterns paired with lazy extraction strategies. Open questions include whether the approach extends to continuous high-dimensional distributions, whether the string-seed generation step can be further optimized, and whether the CoT-length–randomness-quality relationship can be exploited for adaptive compute allocation.
