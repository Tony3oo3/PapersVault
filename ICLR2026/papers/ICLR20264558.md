## Problem

Whether the scaling principles that drive progress in language and vision models transfer to brain modeling remains unclear. Prior neural predictive models were restricted to single modalities, single tasks, or lacked the flexibility to condition on arbitrary combinations of neurons, stimuli, and behavior, making systematic scaling studies impossible. This paper asks whether neural models are currently data-limited or compute-limited, and whether a unified architecture can support multiple prediction regimes simultaneously.

## Input / Output

The model consumes time-aligned multi-modal chunks: calcium imaging traces from up to 4096 neurons (60 samples at 30 Hz, tokenized via strided 1D convolution with window 10 and stride 5), grayscale video frames (60 frames at 30 fps, 36×64 spatial resolution, encoded by a randomly-initialized Hiera ViT), and five behavioral channels (pupil x/y, dilation, dilation derivative, running speed at 20 Hz, 40 samples). A masking configuration specifies which tokens are visible context versus reconstruction targets, enabling arbitrary combinations of forecasting, population prediction, stimulus encoding, and behavioral decoding. Outputs are predicted calcium traces (30 samples = 1 s) for 3072 target neurons and/or full behavioral traces (40 samples per channel), evaluated via single-trial Pearson correlation.

## Method

OmniMouse is a three-stage transformer: (1) a cross-attention encoder compresses variable-length neural and behavioral tokens into a fixed 4096-latent sequence using learned queries with local sliding-window attention and 256 global register tokens; (2) a multi-modal fusion stack of L transformer layers (interleaving 5:1 local-to-global attention) integrates these latents with Hiera video features, using 1D-RoPE computed from per-token timestamps for cross-modal temporal alignment; (3) a shared cross-attention decoder reconstructs target neurons and behavior from the fused representation via modality-specific linear readouts. Training uses 119 structured masking configurations with Poisson loss for neural activity and down-weighted MSE for behavior, under a warmup-stable-decay schedule. The key empirical finding inverts the standard AI scaling story: performance improves reliably with more data across all model sizes, but gains from increasing model width/depth saturate beyond ~80M parameters, indicating that current neural models are data-limited rather than compute-limited.

## Results

On the full 323-session dataset, OmniMouse-80M achieves single-trial correlations of 0.25 (forecasting), 0.34 (stimulus-conditioned forecasting), 0.29 (population prediction), and 0.37 (stimulus-conditioned population prediction) for neural response prediction, and 0.77 average / 0.80 gaze / 0.76 pupil / 0.75 running for behavioral decoding. On the SENSORIUM 2023 benchmark, OmniMouse-80M surpasses the prior competition winner (0.33 vs. 0.29 main track; 0.30 vs. 0.22 bonus track). Performance saturates beyond ~80M parameters across all neural prediction tasks, while behavioral decoding continues to improve smoothly with model size, indicating that neural prediction is data-limited but behavior decoding remains capacity-limited.

## Baselines & Comparisons

In the data-matched 8-session comparison, OmniMouse-5M outperforms MtM (Zhang et al., 2024) by a wide margin on forecasting (0.18 vs. 0.12) and population prediction (0.25 vs. 0.07), and beats the Latent Model (Schmidt et al., 2025) on stimulus-conditioned forecasting (0.25 vs. 0.18) and stimulus-conditioned population prediction (0.27 vs. 0.16). For behavioral decoding, OmniMouse-5M surpasses both CEBRA (Schneider et al., 2023) and POYO+ (Azabou et al., 2025) on average correlation (0.59 vs. 0.53 and 0.55), gaze (0.68 vs. 0.52 and 0.56), and pupil size (0.66 vs. 0.55 and 0.63), but underperforms both on running speed (0.44 vs. 0.51 and 0.47), the one task where it does not win. On the full 323-session dataset, OmniMouse-80M further extends these leads, reaching 0.77 average behavioral decoding correlation and 0.37 on stimulus-conditioned population prediction. The paper also reports that OmniMouse-80M sets new state-of-the-art on both SENSORIUM 2022 (0.37 vs. 0.33 main) and SENSORIUM 2023 (0.33 vs. 0.29 main; 0.30 vs. 0.22 bonus) competition benchmarks.

## Relation to Prior Work

OmniMouse unifies three previously separate lines of work: single-neuron tokenization and cross-attention decoding from POYO/POYO+ (Azabou et al., 2023, 2025), response-to-response forecasting from NDT/MtM (Zhang et al., 2024) and POCO (Duan et al., 2025), and video-conditioned neural encoding from the SENSORIUM competition lineage. The key departure is architectural: prior models were restricted to a single modality (neural-only, neural+behavior, or neural+stimulus) and a fixed task, whereas OmniMouse uses a flexible masking scheme over a shared transformer backbone to handle arbitrary combinations of forecasting, population prediction, stimulus encoding, and behavioral decoding in one model. The paper also explicitly positions itself against the NLP/CV scaling-laws literature (Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022), testing whether those principles transfer to single-neuron brain data.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The central finding—that brain models are data-limited rather than compute-limited, inverting the standard AI scaling story—has direct practical implications: the neuroscience community should prioritize collecting more diverse recordings over building larger models. The unified multi-modal framework also enables systematic dissection of how sensory input, population dynamics, and behavior each contribute to neural variability, something no prior single-task model could do. Key limitations include linear scaling of per-neuron embeddings (constraining further scale-up), restriction to mouse V1 and spontaneous behavior, and the possibility that the observed saturation reflects limited stimulus diversity rather than a fundamental ceiling. Whether sufficiently larger and richer datasets could trigger phase transitions analogous to emergent abilities in LLMs remains an open and exciting question.
