## Problem

Existing autoregressive video generators either diverge from standard LLM architectures, depend on bulky external text encoders, or incur prohibitive latency from sequential next-token decoding. Two fundamental gaps remain: (1) the 1D RoPE used in LLMs is ill-suited for modeling 3D spatiotemporal correlations in video, and naive 3D RoPE exhibits imbalanced frequency spectra that degrade spatial modeling; (2) next-token prediction is incompatible with video's spatial bidirectionality within frames and temporal causality across frames, and naive random masking during discrete diffusion training causes severe frame-wise loss imbalance due to spatial information leakage.

## Input / Output

Lumos-1 accepts a text prompt (optionally with a reference image for I2V) and produces a generated image (352×352 or 512×512) or a 25-frame video at 672×384 resolution. Visual data is tokenized by the discrete Cosmos Tokenizer with 8×8×4 spatiotemporal compression, yielding a 129,536-entry codebook (65,536 text + 64,000 visual tokens). For I2V, the first frame is provided as conditioning; for T2V, generation begins from a text prompt alone. The model is trained on 60M images and 10M videos with long descriptive captions, and inference uses classifier-free guidance (scale 16) with 50 denoising steps per latent frame.

## Method

Lumos-1 builds on the Llama architecture with QK-Norm and introduces two key mechanisms. First, MM-RoPE replaces the standard 1D RoPE for visual tokens with a distributed 3D design: multiple meta MM-RoPE components (16 channels each) interleave temporal, height, and width rotary channels to provide comprehensive frequency spectra for all three dimensions, while text tokens retain the native LLM RoPE. A principled scaling of 3D positions by the tokenizer compression ratio (4,8,8) balances the position ranges between text and visual modalities. Second, Autoregressive Discrete Diffusion Forcing (AR-DF) replaces next-token prediction with parallel mask-based discrete diffusion under intra-frame bidirectional and inter-frame causal attention. During training, temporal tube masking applies the same spatial mask pattern across all frames to prevent the model from shortcut-copying unmasked tokens in earlier frames, resolving the frame-wise loss imbalance. During inference, a fixed ratio (ρ_inf=0.7) of each generated frame's tokens is replaced with [MASK] before caching KV pairs, aligning the partial-observation pattern with training and preventing quality degradation over the video sequence.

## Results

On GenEval, Lumos-1 (3.6B, 512×512, SFT) achieves an Overall score of 0.791, surpassing Show-o2 (0.76), EMU3 (0.66), and Fluid (0.69), while using only 60M training images and a discrete tokenizer. On VBench-I2V, Lumos-1 (3.6B) scores 84.72 Total, matching COSMOS-Video2World (84.16) which uses 10× more video data (100M vs. 10M) and ~200× more compute (10,000 H100s vs. 48 H20s). On VBench-T2V, Lumos-1 (3.6B, SFT) reaches 78.90 Total, surpassing OpenSoraPlan (77.23) and approaching CogVideoX (81.91), all without a heavy pretrained text encoder.

## Baselines & Comparisons

On GenEval, Lumos-1 (3.6B, SFT) at 0.791 Overall outperforms Show-o2 (0.76), Lumina-mGPT 2.0 (0.73), Fluid (0.69), and EMU3 (0.66), while using substantially fewer training images (60M vs. 66M–1.4B) and a smaller model (3.6B vs. 7–10.5B). Notably, Lumos-1 excels on Position (0.683) and Attribute Binding (0.688), surpassing even Show-o2 (0.52, 0.62), indicating strong vision-language alignment without textual pre-training. On VBench-I2V, Lumos-1 (3.6B) at 84.72 Total matches COSMOS-Video2World (84.16) despite using 10× less video data and ~200× less compute (48 H20s vs. 10,000 H100s), and outperforms VideoCrafter-I2V (82.57) and VideoMAR (84.82). On VBench-T2V, Lumos-1 (3.6B, SFT) at 78.90 surpasses OpenSoraPlan (77.23) and NOVA (80.12 on a subset), approaching CogVideoX (81.91) while using a discrete tokenizer and no external text encoder. Across all benchmarks, Lumos-1 demonstrates that a pure LLM architecture with discrete diffusion and MM-RoPE can compete with or exceed much larger continuous-token diffusion and autoregressive systems under a fraction of the training budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

Lumos-1 builds directly on the Llama transformer architecture and the MaskGIT-style masked prediction paradigm, but departs from prior AR video generators (Phenaki, Loong, EMU3) by replacing slow next-token prediction with parallel discrete diffusion and by eliminating external text encoders. Its MM-RoPE extends the 3D RoPE introduced in CogVideo and the multi-modal RoPE from Qwen2-VL, but redistributes frequency channels across temporal, height, and width dimensions to fix the imbalanced spectrum identified in vanilla 3D RoPE. The AR-DF training scheme is a video-specific adaptation of Diffusion Forcing's per-frame independent noise idea, replacing it with temporal tube masking that directly targets spatial information redundancy between frames.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that a pure LLM backbone with a discrete tokenizer, trained on only 48 GPUs and 60M images / 10M videos, can match or exceed much larger diffusion-based video generators (COSMOS on 10,000 H100s, OpenSoraPlan), suggesting a viable path toward unified understanding-and-generation models without the architectural compromises of hybrid designs. MM-RoPE and AR-DF are presented as plug-and-play components transferable to future unified models, though the authors acknowledge the 3D position scaling is suboptimal and that the discrete tokenizer remains a potential fidelity bottleneck. Open questions include whether these techniques scale to minute-level videos, whether the inference-time masking requirement generalizes to interactive/streaming generation, and how the model performs when extended to understanding tasks alongside generation.
