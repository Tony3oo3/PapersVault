## Problem

Current VLM post-training methods (SFT, RLHF, RLVR) depend heavily on expensive human-curated datasets and verified Q&A pairs, creating both a data scarcity bottleneck and a knowledge ceiling bounded by human supervision. While self-play has proven effective for LLM reasoning, extending it to vision-language models remains largely unexplored due to the challenge of designing game environments that simultaneously exercise visual perception, strategic reasoning, and communication without any human annotation.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263626/cropped_page1_idx1.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes arbitrary unlabeled images (synthetic CLEVR scenes, ChartQA charts, or real-world photos) and a base VLM as the only inputs—no labels, Q&A pairs, or chain-of-thought annotations are required. During gameplay, civilians receive the original image while the spy receives a blank image, creating an asymmetric visual information structure. The output is a post-trained VLM with improved reasoning, chart/OCR, and vision-centric understanding, evaluated across 14+ downstream benchmarks without any task-specific fine-tuning.

## Method

Vision-Zero formulates a visual 'Who Is the Spy' game where n_c civilians observe an image and one spy sees a blank image; in the Clue Stage players generate verbal descriptions (the spy must bluff), and in the Decision Stage civilians vote to identify the spy. Training uses Iterative-SPO, a novel algorithm that alternates between Self-Play Policy Optimization (zero-sum vote-based rewards with Role-Advantage Estimation to handle asymmetric role information in the Clue Stage) and RLVR via GRPO (discrete correctness rewards with group normalization in the Decision Stage). Stage switching is governed by hysteresis thresholds on held-out accuracy and 'n/a' rates, preventing premature convergence to strategic equilibria. The key insight is that the asymmetric visual input (image vs. blank) forces the model to simultaneously develop visual grounding, logical inference, and strategic communication, while the domain-agnostic image input enables generalization across reasoning, chart, and perception tasks without task-specific data.

## Results

On six reasoning/math benchmarks (MathVista, MathVision, WeMath, MathVerse, LogicVista, DynaMath), VisionZero-Qwen-7B(Real-World) achieves the best average of 44.5%, outperforming the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B (41.1%) by 3.4 points and the strongest RLVR baseline MM-Eureka-Qwen-7B (42.9%) by 1.6 points. On chart understanding, VisionZero-Qwen-7B(Chart) leads on ChartXIV_RQ (45.8%), FunctionQA (85.5%), and ReachQA (53.4%), while VisionZero-Qwen-7B(CLEVR) achieves the best MMVP score (79.5%) among all 7B models. Training cost is 127 A100-hours with zero labeling cost, compared to ~700 A100-hours for MM-Eureka and 120–170+ A100-hours for other RLVR baselines.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Vision-Zero outperforms all five 7B RLVR baselines on the average of six reasoning/math benchmarks: it beats MM-Eureka-Qwen-7B (42.9→44.5, +1.6), ViGaL-Snake+Rotation (43.0→44.5, +1.5), VLAA-Thinker-7B (41.9→44.5, +2.6), OpenVLThinker-7B (40.9→44.5, +3.6), and R1-OneVision-7B (39.5→44.5, +5.0). Notably, Vision-Zero also surpasses GPT-4o (43.4) on the same average, while Gemini 2.0-Flash (54.4) remains substantially ahead, reflecting the gap between 7B open models and frontier proprietary systems. On chart understanding (Table 2), VisionZero-Qwen-7B(Chart) leads all 7B baselines on ChartXIV_RQ (45.8 vs. 44.1 for OpenVLThinker) and FunctionQA (85.5 vs. 83.8), and VisionZero-Qwen-7B(CLEVR) achieves the best MMVP score (79.5 vs. 74.6 for ViGaL). In terms of cost, Vision-Zero requires only 127 A100-hours and zero labeling tokens, versus ~700 A100-hours for MM-Eureka and 120–170+ A100-hours with 5.7M–29.6M labeling tokens for other RLVR baselines, while delivering superior or comparable accuracy.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the self-play paradigm from text-only LLMs (SPIRAL, Absolute Zero) to vision-language models, and departs from prior game-based VLM training (ViGaL's Snake/Rotation) by introducing a social deduction game ('Who Is the Spy') that operates on arbitrary image inputs rather than fixed game environments. It builds on RLVR methods (R1-OneVision, MM-Eureka, OpenVLThinker) that rely on human-labeled data, but replaces the labeled supervision with a zero-sum self-play reward in the clue stage and verifiable vote-correctness rewards in the decision stage. The Iterative-SPO algorithm is a novel contribution that addresses the known limitation of pure self-play reaching local equilibrium by alternating with RLVR, drawing on insights from multi-agent RL literature on non-transitive games and role collapse.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

Vision-Zero offers a practical path toward scalable VLM post-training without the prohibitive cost of multimodal annotation, which is a genuine bottleneck in the field. The finding that strategic gameplay on simple CLEVR objects generalizes to math and chart reasoning without any explicit math training suggests that reasoning capabilities may be more transferable than task-specific fine-tuning assumes. However, the absolute improvements remain modest (~2-3% on reasoning benchmarks), the framework has only been validated at 7B-14B scale, and the game format (Who Is the Spy) may impose an upper bound on the diversity of skills that can be developed. Open questions include whether the approach scales to larger models, whether more complex game topologies yield further gains, and whether the label-free paradigm can match the ceiling of well-curated human supervision on highly specialized tasks.
