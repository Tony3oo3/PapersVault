## Problem

Most neural PDE solvers target low-speed, incompressible flows where uniform time-stepping is practical, but high-speed (supersonic/hypersonic) flows exhibit shock waves and sharp gradients that demand adaptive time-stepping. Classical CFL-based timestep determination cannot be directly applied to neural solvers because they operate on coarsened space-time meshes and model only subsets of the governing variables, making it unclear how to learn timestep prediction and timestep-conditioned evolution jointly.

## Input / Output

The neural CFL model takes the current flow state (velocity, temperature, density, and optionally volume fraction) on a spatial grid (e.g., 104×104 or 128×128) augmented with finite-difference spatial gradients and CFL-derived features (local wave speed, velocity magnitudes, sound speed), and outputs a single scalar timestep. The neural solver takes the same flow state concatenated with the predicted timestep and outputs the evolved flow fields at the next adaptive time point. Data is temporally coarsened by factors of 100–500× relative to the classical solver, so each neural step spans hundreds of classical solver steps, and the autoregressive inference loop alternates between the two phases until a target simulation time is reached.

## Method

ShockCast is a two-phase supervised framework: a neural CFL model (ConvNeXt on regular grids, GNN on irregular geometries) predicts the timestep size using physically-motivated inputs including spatial gradients, max-pooling to emulate the wave-speed maximum in the CFL condition, and explicit CFL features; a timestep-conditioned neural solver (U-Net, F-FNO, CNO, Transolver, MGN, or DGN) then evolves the flow field by that predicted step. The key novelty is four timestep conditioning strategies for the solver: Time-Conditioned Layer Norm (scale-shift embeddings of Δt), Spatial-Spectral Conditioning (frequency-domain multiplication by a complex Δt embedding), Euler Residuals (scaling residual connections by an affine function of Δt, derived from the Euler integration formula in neural ODEs), and Mixture-of-Experts gating on Δt so that different experts specialize in short timesteps (sharp gradients) versus long timesteps (smooth dynamics). At inference, the two phases alternate autoregressively, with the CFL model's prediction feeding directly into the solver, enabling stable long-horizon rollouts on coarsened meshes without requiring the timestep to be known a priori.

## Results

ShockCast achieves the strongest correlation time (Pearson threshold 0.9) with a U-Net backbone using time-conditioned layer norm (Affine) on both the coal dust explosion and circular blast settings. For TKE relative error, MoE conditioning with U-Net performs best on coal dust explosion, while Euler and MoE conditioning with F-FNO perform best on circular blast; F-FNO with MoE also achieves the lowest mean flow error on circular blast. On the long circular blast setting (100+ autoregressive unrolling steps), predicted solutions maintain correlation above 0.9 for approximately 50% of the full simulation time, with low TKE and mean flow errors confirming stability. On the airfoil shock setting, DGN outperforms MGN across all reported metrics.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare ShockCast against external neural solver baselines; instead, it ablates over four neural solver backbones (U-Net, CNO, F-FNO, Transolver) and three timestep conditioning strategies (Affine, Euler, MoE) within the unified ShockCast framework. U-Net with time-conditioned layer norm (Affine) consistently achieves the best correlation time on both regular-grid datasets, suggesting that hierarchical multi-scale processing with simple timestep conditioning is most effective for maintaining long-horizon accuracy. MoE conditioning, which gates experts on the timestep Δt to specialize in short vs. long integration periods, delivers the best TKE error on coal dust explosion and competitive TKE on circular blast, at the cost of higher peak GPU memory (e.g., 37.2 GiB for F-FNO+MoE vs. 18.9 GiB for F-FNO+Affine). Euler residuals, which scale the residual connection by an affine function of Δt inspired by neural ODEs, provide a lighter-weight alternative that matches or approaches MoE on TKE. For irregular geometries (airfoil shock), DGN with affine conditioning outperforms MGN, and the DGN-based neural CFL model (0.795M params) is substantially smaller than the solver backbone (4.443M params). The long circular blast experiment (100+ unrolling steps) shows that ShockCast remains in-distribution, with correlation sustained above 0.9 for roughly half the simulation, validating the adaptive timestep approach for extended rollouts.

## Relation to Prior Work

ShockCast extends the neural PDE solver literature from low-speed, incompressible flows with uniform time-stepping to high-speed, compressible flows requiring adaptive temporal resolution. It repurposes three conditioning mechanisms from prior work—time-conditioned layer norm and spatial-spectral conditioning from Gupta & Brandstetter (2023), Euler integration from neural ODEs (Chen et al., 2018), and Mixture of Experts gating (Shazeer et al., 2017)—but applies them to a fundamentally different setting where the timestep is unknown a priori and must be predicted on-the-fly during autoregressive rollout. The two-phase architecture (neural CFL predictor + timestep-conditioned solver) departs from prior adaptive-stepping approaches (Wu et al. 2025, Janny et al. 2024, Hagnberger et al. 2024) that rely on temporally uniform training data or Taylor-expansion interpolation, instead learning directly from data with variable temporal resolution.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

High-speed flow simulation is among the most computationally demanding PDE applications, and the three publicly released supersonic datasets (coal dust explosion, circular blast, airfoil shock) fill a significant gap in ML-for-fluids benchmarks. The framework's practical value is demonstrated by stable rollouts over 100+ steps on the long circular blast case and generalization to 3D (spherical blast) and irregular geometries (airfoil). Open questions include whether the surrogate neural CFL model can be replaced by a more principled learned condition for complex multiphase systems, whether the MoE conditioning's compute overhead is justified at scale, and whether the approach extends to fully 3D high-speed flows with chemical reactions. The paper also highlights that the coarsened-mesh mismatch between neural and classical solvers remains a fundamental challenge for any learned adaptive-stepping scheme.
