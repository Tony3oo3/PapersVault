## Problem

Fine-grained, interpretable control over autoregressive text-to-music generation (e.g., specific pitch classes, chord qualities, tempo) remains challenging: existing controllable methods require compute-heavy finetuning runs or costly per-step optimization at inference, and often introduce audible artifacts or degrade prompt adherence. The paper asks whether one can identify human-interpretable music-theoretic directions inside a frozen model's activations and steer generation along them in real time without retraining or altering the decoding procedure.

## Input / Output

At inference the system takes a text prompt (sampled from SONG-DESCRIBER), a set of pre-extracted RFM eigendirections (one per target concept per layer), a global control coefficient η₀, per-layer weights, a time schedule φ(t), and a Bernoulli gate probability p. The frozen MUSICGEN-large (48 Transformer decoder blocks over EnCodec tokens at 32 kHz) produces steered audio; the RFM directions are injected additively into the residual stream of selected layers via forward hooks at every generation step, so no model weights are modified and no per-step gradient computation is needed.

## Method

MusicRFM trains extremely lightweight, layer-wise RFM probes (kernel ridge regression with 15 AGOP iterations) on mean-pooled MUSICGEN hidden states from the SYNTHEORY synthetic dataset to extract orthogonal, eigenvalue-ranked concept directions for each music-theoretic attribute. At inference, the top eigenvector per direction is injected into the residual stream of a subset of the 48 decoder blocks, with layer selection governed by either hard top-K pruning or an exponential score-weighting scheme (w_ℓ = w₀·ŝ_ℓ^{1/κ}) that concentrates steering on high-performing layers. Temporal control is achieved through deterministic schedules (linear/logistic rise, exponential decay, sinusoidal modulation) and stochastic Bernoulli gating that sparsifies injection to reduce cumulative artifacts. The framework further supports multi-direction steering, where multiple concept vectors are summed with independent coefficients and schedules, enabling simultaneous or staggered enforcement of several attributes (e.g., notes and chords) in a single generation pass.

## Results

On the SYNTHEORY classification benchmark, MusicRFM (mean-pooled) achieves an average score of 0.942 across 7 music-theoretic categories, outperforming the Syntheory FFN baseline (0.929) and substantially exceeding linear probes (0.685) and last-token RFMs (0.776). For steering, injecting RFM directions into MUSICGEN-Large increases target-note classification accuracy from 0.23 (random chance) to 0.82 at η₀=0.60, while CLAP text-prompt alignment remains within ≈0.02 of the unsteered baseline (0.332). Combining prompt conditioning with RFM steering pushes note accuracy to 0.952 at η₀=0.60. On MUSICBENCH (real music), RFM probes reach 75.3% accuracy on notes and 67.5% on keys, confirming transfer beyond synthetic data.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the Syntheory FFN baseline (Wei et al., 2024), MusicRFM (mean-pooled) achieves a higher overall average (0.942 vs. 0.929), winning on Scales (0.956 vs. 0.905), Progressions (0.943 vs. 0.901), Intervals (0.975 vs. 0.972), and Tempos (0.985 vs. 0.965), while narrowly losing on Notes (0.850 vs. 0.866), Chords (0.984 vs. 0.989), and Time Signatures (0.900 vs. 0.905). Against the simpler linear probe baseline, MusicRFM dominates across every category, with the largest gap on Scales (0.956 vs. 0.158). For steering control specifically, the prompt-only baseline yields near-random accuracy on most categories (e.g., 0.065 for progressions, 0.104 for intervals), whereas MusicRFM steering at η₀=0.60 reaches 0.824 on notes and 0.344 on chords, and the combined prompt+RFM setting achieves 0.952 on notes—demonstrating that activation-level steering enables fine-grained musical control that prompt engineering alone cannot provide.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper adapts Recursive Feature Machines (Radhakrishnan et al., 2023) — originally a probing method that extracts interpretable, eigenvalue-ranked directions via Average Gradient Outer Product matrices — from the text-domain steering framework of Beaglehole et al. (2025b) into autoregressive music generation. It departs from prior controllable music generation approaches (finetuning-based ControlNets, per-step optimization methods like DITTO) by operating entirely in activation space on a frozen model, requiring only lightweight probe training. The key methodological departures from the text-domain RFM steering are layer-aware weighting (top-K and exponential schemes across 48 decoder blocks), time-varying schedules, and multi-direction injection, all motivated by the continuous, fixed-sampling-rate nature of audio versus discrete text.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

MusicRFM demonstrates that fine-grained, music-theoretic control (specific notes, chords, tempo) can be achieved on a frozen model with minimal impact on prompt fidelity, offering a practical alternative to compute-heavy finetuning for each new control. However, the approach is currently limited to a single model (MUSICGEN-large), relies on mean-pooled features that discard temporal ordering (hurting scales, progressions, time signatures), and is validated primarily on the synthetic SYNTHEORY dataset. Open questions include whether temporally aware pooling or sequence-level RFMs can close the gap on sequential concepts, whether the technique transfers to other architectures (Jukebox, Magenta-RT), and whether real-time interactive steering is feasible for live performance applications.
