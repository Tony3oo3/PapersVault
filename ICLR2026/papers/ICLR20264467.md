## Problem

Online learning algorithms typically require numeric utility feedback, but in human-in-the-loop and privacy-sensitive settings, only relative rankings over proposed actions are available. This paper establishes fundamental hardness results showing that sublinear regret is impossible in general under ranking feedback (both instantaneous-utility and time-average-utility variants), and then characterizes precisely when regret minimization becomes achievable and how to do so.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264467/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

At each timestep the learner proposes a multiset of K actions (sampled from its current mixed strategy) and receives a permutation over them, generated by a Plackett-Luce model with temperature τ based on either the instantaneous utility vector or the time-average utility vector. The learner outputs a mixed strategy over the full action set; in the multi-player game setting, the time-averaged joint strategy constitutes an ε-approximate coarse correlated equilibrium. A key structural constraint is that the learner never observes any numeric utility value—only the ordinal ranking—and must infer utility magnitudes from the stochastic permutation.

## Method

The core mechanism is a utility estimation oracle (Algorithm 1) that decomposes a K-action ranking into pairwise comparisons, exploits the logistic structure of the Plackett-Luce model to recover utility gaps via the inverse logistic transform, and averages over a sliding window of m′ recent permutations to control variance. For instantaneous-utility rankings, the estimated utilities are plugged into any standard no-regret oracle (PGD, MWU, FTRL) with a uniform exploration mixture to ensure every action is proposed with probability ≥ γ/|A|. For time-average-utility rankings, the oracle must be FTRL (or another algorithm satisfying a Lipschitz-stability condition on accumulated utilities) because the strategy must remain close to the ideal learner's strategy under estimation perturbations; in the bandit setting, a block-based differencing scheme reduces the variance of the per-block utility estimator. The estimation error is O(T/√m′ + m′·P^(T)), yielding sublinear regret when the utility variation P^(T) is sublinear; for full-information time-average rankings this variation assumption is unnecessary because the time-average utility changes by at most 2/t per step by construction.

## Results

The paper establishes fundamental impossibility results: sublinear regret is impossible under instantaneous-utility ranking feedback (Ω(T) lower bound for τ ≤ O(1)), and also impossible under time-average ranking feedback when the Plackett-Luce temperature is sufficiently small (Ω̃(T) for full-info with τ ≤ O(1/(T log T)); Ω(T) for bandit with τ ≤ O(1/log T)). On the positive side, the proposed algorithms achieve sublinear regret under a sublinear utility-variation assumption (Assumption 4.2) for both feedback types, and this assumption can be entirely removed for full-information AvgUtil Rank with constant τ. In the game-theoretic setting, when all players run the proposed algorithms, the time-average joint strategy converges to an approximate coarse correlated equilibrium (CCE) with exploitability o(1).

**Table 1: Summary of lower bounds (impossibility) and upper bounds (achievability) for sublinear regret.**

| AvgUtil Rank | Bandit | Ω(T) for τ ≤ O(1/log T) | Requires Assumption 4.2 with q < 1/3 |

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper does not compare against competing algorithms in a head-to-head empirical benchmark; instead, it establishes the first theoretical framework for online learning with ranking feedback in adversarial environments. The key 'comparison' is across feedback settings: instantaneous-utility ranking is fundamentally harder (linear regret always) than time-average ranking, which admits sublinear regret under constant temperature. The empirical validation on the HH-RLHF dataset (using Qwen3-32B, Phi-4, GPT-4o, and Llama-3.1-70B as candidate models) demonstrates that Algorithm 3 with AvgUtil Rank under bandit feedback achieves decreasing average regret over 10^7 timesteps, approaching the performance of the best fixed model in hindsight. In game experiments (two-player general-sum games with 10 actions each), the CCE exploitability decreases over time across all tested temperatures (τ ∈ {0.5, 1, 2}) and numbers of proposed actions (K ∈ {3, 5, 10}), confirming the theoretical equilibrium-computation guarantees. The paper positions its results as strictly stronger than the closest prior work (Maran et al., NeurIPS 2024), which only covers the τ → 0⁺ bandit case, by extending to finite τ, the full-information setting, and the game-theoretic equilibrium computation.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the line of work on learning from ranking/comparison feedback (dueling bandits, Maran et al. 2024) by moving from stationary stochastic environments to adversarial non-stochastic online learning, and by introducing two distinct ranking mechanisms (instantaneous-utility vs. time-average-utility) under a Plackett-Luce model with a temperature parameter. It builds on the modular use of standard no-regret oracles (FTRL, PGD, MWU) as black-box components, but the key departure is that the learner never observes numeric utilities—only permutations—requiring a new utility-estimation procedure that decomposes K-way rankings into pairwise logistic comparisons. The game-theoretic contribution extends the classical no-regret-to-CCE connection (Cesa-Bianchi & Lugosi, 2006) to the ranking-feedback setting, which had not been previously addressed.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The negative results (linear regret is unavoidable with instantaneous-utility rankings, and near-linear regret with very cold Plackett-Luce temperatures) establish fundamental limits that inform the design of human-in-the-loop and RLHF systems: ranking-only feedback is strictly weaker than numeric feedback in adversarial settings, and the degree of stochasticity in the ranking model matters. The positive algorithms, which achieve sublinear regret under sublinear utility variation (or unconditionally for full-information time-average rankings), provide a practical recipe for recommendation and LLM-routing systems where users provide only preference orderings. Open questions include whether the sublinear-variation assumption can be weakened further, whether the bandit-feedback gap (requiring q < 1/3) can be closed, and how these results extend to contextual or non-stationary game settings.
