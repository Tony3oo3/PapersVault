## Problem

Existing LLM-based decompilers face a fundamental trade-off between functional correctness and code readability because they attempt to simultaneously infer control-flow structures, data layouts, and identifiers in a single generation pass. This joint inference is intractable: models that produce readable output often break compilation, while those that preserve functionality emit obfuscated, unreadable code with generic placeholders like VAR_1 and FUN_0001cdc0.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263034/cropped_page2_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes IDA Pro pseudocode from stripped x86 Linux binaries (compiled with GCC/Clang at O0–O3) as input and produces decompiled C source code as final output. The critical intermediate is an obfuscated IR — the original source with all user-defined identifiers replaced by category-prefixed generic names (func1, type1, field1, var1) while preserving standard types and library calls — which bridges the two phases. The IR is generated automatically via AST traversal and offset-based substitution, and headers for compilation checks are inferred using Psyche-C.

## Method

SK² Decompile decomposes decompilation into two sequential phases grounded in the probabilistic factorization P(s|u) ≈ Σ P(s|i)·P(i|u): Structure Recovery translates pseudocode to the obfuscated IR, and Identifier Naming enriches the IR with semantically meaningful names. Both phases use a 6.7B sequence-to-sequence model (initialized from LLM4Decompile) trained first with SFT, then with GRPO reinforcement learning using phase-specific rewards. Structure Recovery is rewarded by compiler compilability (verified via Psyche-C) plus Jaccard similarity on recovered placeholders, while Identifier Naming is rewarded by cosine similarity between qwen-embedding-0.6B embeddings of the generated and reference code, avoiding the exact-match penalty of cross-entropy. The IR design follows the Information Bottleneck principle, maximizing compression of identifier semantics while preserving structural semantics, and a Markov assumption justifies discarding the original pseudocode during the naming phase.

## Results

SK² Decompile achieves 69.00% average re-executability on HumanEval and 59.63% on MBPP, the first model to approach ~70% and ~60% respectively. It attains 77.17% average R2I on HumanEval and 71.62% on GitHub2025, and scores 4.24/5 on GPT-judge for identifier naming quality on HumanEval. The method outperforms the strongest baseline (GPT-5-mini) by 21.6% in average re-executability on HumanEval and by 29.4% in R2I over Idioms on GitHub2025.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against IDA Pro, SK² Decompile achieves 68.5% and 50.4% higher re-executability on HumanEval and MBPP respectively, and roughly doubles the R2I score (77.17 vs 39.45 on HumanEval). Against GPT-5-mini, SK² Decompile leads by 21.6% in average re-executability on HumanEval (69.00 vs 56.75) and by 26.3% on MBPP (59.63 vs 47.23), while also surpassing it on R2I (77.17 vs 43.49 on HumanEval) and GPT-judge (4.24 vs 4.23). Against LLM4Decompile, the gap widens at higher optimization levels: at O3, SK² Decompile scores 57.52% vs 28.32% on HumanEval. Against Idioms, SK² Decompile dominates across all metrics, with the most striking margin being 29.4% R2I improvement on GitHub2025 (71.62 vs 61.63) and 39.2% re-executability gain on HumanEval (69.00 vs 29.81). Against Ref-Decompile, SK² Decompile is competitive at O0 (86.59 vs 85.37 on HumanEval) but pulls ahead substantially at O3 (57.52 vs 46.90), with relative improvements of 22.64% and 27.42% on HumanEval and MBPP respectively.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on LLM4Decompile (Tan et al., 2024) as its base 6.7B sequence-to-sequence architecture and on the DOBf deobfuscation objective (Lachaux et al., 2021) for its Intermediate Representation design, which is essentially obfuscated source code justified through the Information Bottleneck principle. It departs from prior single-phase LLM decompilers (LLM4Decompile, Idioms, GPT-5-mini) by explicitly decomposing the task into Structure Recovery (pseudocode → IR) and Identifier Naming (IR → source), each trained with a distinct RL reward: compiler-guided compilability plus Jaccard placeholder matching for structure, and embedding cosine similarity for naming. The paper also positions itself against prior identifier-naming-only work (Lacomis et al., 2019; Chen et al., 2022; Xie et al., 2024), arguing that those methods 'paint a cow on a pile' by decorating unstructured pseudocode without first recovering the underlying control-flow and data-structure skeleton.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SK² Decompile is the first to reach ~70% average re-executability on HumanEval and ~60% on MBPP, a meaningful milestone for LLM-based decompilation that narrows the gap to the original source. The two-phase decomposition with phase-specific RL rewards provides a clean, modular framework that the community can extend (e.g., swapping the IR, adding new reward signals, or scaling to binary-level context). Key open questions remain: handling cross-function context (global variables, call graphs) without quadratic attention cost, correcting non-idiomatic decompiler patterns that the model inherits rather than fixing, and recovering precise numerical operands from compiler-optimized arithmetic (e.g., magic-number modulo) where LLMs are inherently weak. The paper also notes that ExeBench re-executability is fundamentally untestable on stripped binaries due to lost type/symbol information, highlighting a benchmark design limitation the field must address.
