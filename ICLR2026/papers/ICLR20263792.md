## Problem

Existing Process-Supervised RL (PSRL) methods for reasoning LLMs suffer from three key inefficiencies: they branch Monte Carlo sampling at positions determined by fixed token length or entropy rather than semantic importance, they sample uniformly across problems of varying difficulty, and they require two full sampling passes per training update. This paper asks whether attention scores can serve as a principled signal for identifying where reasoning actually happens, and whether sampling can be made adaptive to problem difficulty.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263792/cropped_page0_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method takes a mathematical problem prompt as input and produces a step-by-step reasoning chain ending in a boxed final answer, generated by a decoder-only Transformer (DS-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B or 7B). During training, each generated response is segmented into steps by double line breaks, and token-to-token attention scores are extracted via a single forward pass to compute step-level Forward Context Influence (FCI) scores. The policy is optimized with a GRPO-style clipped surrogate loss at step-level granularity, with a maximum response length of 8,192 tokens during training and 32,768 tokens during evaluation.

## Method

AttnRL introduces three mechanisms for efficient PSRL. First, Attention-based Tree Branching (ATB) computes a Forward Context Influence (FCI) score for each step by summing attention weights from subsequent steps (with a gap of Δ=4) and taking the max across layers and heads; the top 20% FCI steps are selected as branching points, with the earliest two chosen to avoid 'tunnel vision' from misleading early steps. Second, adaptive sampling combines attention-based filtering (removing easy problems whose average FCI falls below the dataset mean), difficulty-aware tree expansion (scaling the number of Monte Carlo trees by exp(-accuracy)), and an EMA-based batch size adjustment that discards zero-advantage responses to guarantee every training token contributes a non-zero gradient. Third, a one-step off-policy pipeline overlaps initial sampling for the next batch with Monte Carlo sampling for the current batch, eliminating the redundant second sampling pass and reducing wall-clock training time by 8%.

## Results

AttnRL achieves an average score of 57.2 across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks at 1.5B scale, outperforming the base model (DS-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, 49.7) by 7.5% and the strongest baseline DeepScaleR-Preview-1.5B (56.5) by 0.7%. At 7B scale, AttnRL reaches 68.7 average, surpassing GRPO (66.3) by 2.4% and TreeRL (66.9) by 1.8%. The largest single-benchmark gain is on AIME24, where AttnRL improves over the base model by 11.4% (1.5B) and 5.3% (7B). Notably, AttnRL achieves these results in only 500 training steps at 8K response length, compared to DeepScaleR's 1750 steps with progressive context extension (8K→16K→24K).

## Baselines & Comparisons

AttnRL consistently outperforms all three baselines across both model scales. Against GRPO (outcome-supervised RL), AttnRL gains 1.9% average at 1.5B and 2.4% at 7B, with the largest margin on AIME24 (39.7 vs. 36.9 at 1.5B; 59.3 vs. 54.9 at 7B). Against TreeRL (the closest process-supervised baseline using entropy-based branching), AttnRL improves by 1.8–2.1% average, demonstrating that attention-based branching (FCI scores) identifies more informative branching points than entropy. Against DeepScaleR-Preview-1.5B, a strong RL-trained model using iterative context expansion over 1750 steps, AttnRL achieves a 0.7% average improvement while requiring only 500 steps at a fixed 8K length, highlighting a significant efficiency advantage. In terms of training efficiency (Table 3), AttnRL produces 930.4M valid training tokens versus 274.6M for TreeRL, while completing training in 62.6 hours versus 67.7 hours, confirming that the adaptive sampling and one-step off-policy pipeline yield both more useful gradients and shorter wall-clock time.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the process-supervised RL (PSRL) line initiated by TreeRL (Hou et al., 2025), which uses entropy-based branching for Monte Carlo tree sampling, and GRPO (Shao et al., 2024) for the clipped surrogate policy objective. The key conceptual departure is replacing entropy as the branching criterion with a Forward Context Influence (FCI) score derived from self-attention, motivated by prior observations that massive attention values mark reasoning-critical steps (Jin et al., 2025; Bogdan et al., 2025). Additionally, the paper introduces difficulty-aware adaptive sampling and a one-step off-policy pipeline, departing from the uniform two-step sampling protocol common to prior PSRL methods (TreeRL, TreeRPO, VinePPO).

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in the efficiency gains: AttnRL produces 3.4× more valid training tokens (930M vs. 275M) in less wall-clock time than TreeRL, making PSRL substantially more accessible for practitioners with limited compute. The attention-based branching offers a lightweight, architecture-agnostic signal for identifying reasoning-critical steps without requiring an external process reward model. However, the accuracy improvements over strong baselines are modest (~2%), and the method has only been validated on mathematical reasoning at two model scales (1.5B, 7B); whether FCI-based branching generalizes to code, science, or other reasoning domains, and to larger models with different attention patterns, remains an open question. The ablation showing that filtering out 'easy' problems slightly hurts performance also suggests the adaptive sampling mechanism may need careful tuning to avoid discarding useful training signal.
