## Problem

Existing image editing models handle explicit instructions (e.g., 'remove the object') well but fail on implicit, cause-driven instructions (e.g., 'a water balloon hits a cactus') that require world knowledge to infer the visual outcome. This failure stems from a strong instruction–input–result coupling: the same action produces vastly different visual changes depending on object material and physical properties, yet no large-scale training dataset or benchmark exists to teach models this causal reasoning.

## Input / Output

The model receives an RGB image (sourced from DF2K-OST, center-cropped to 2:3 or 3:2 aspect ratios) paired with a short implicit editing instruction describing a cause (e.g., 'the ship breaks apart'). It outputs a chain-of-thought textual explanation of the expected causal transformation followed by the edited image. The WorldEdit dataset contains 11k such (source image, implicit instruction, paraphrased CoT, edited image) tuples spanning 10 transformation categories (Time, Temperature, Humidity, Acidity, Light, Break, Inflate, Squeeze, Twist, Stretch, plus Other).

## Method

The paper introduces a two-stage training pipeline on the Bagel unified multimodal model. Stage 1 performs supervised fine-tuning on WorldEdit's paraphrased chain-of-thought instructions, applying causal language modeling loss jointly over text and image tokens so the model learns to map implicit causes to detailed visual change descriptions. Stage 2 applies reinforcement learning via Flow-GRPO with a composite reward R = R_reason + R_fidelity + R_causal: the CoT reasoning reward scores logical coherence of the generated explanation, the image quality reward uses a multimodal evaluator to check instruction adherence and visual consistency, and the causal verification reward inverts the edit—asking a multimodal model to infer the cause from the input/output image pair and scoring its similarity to the original instruction. The dataset itself is constructed by detecting objects in real-world images, pairing them with 10 causal transformation types, generating and filtering instructions via GPT-4o, synthesizing edited images (with multi-step decomposition for complex cases), and applying a two-stage quality filter (instruction-level causal consistency check followed by image-level visual plausibility assessment).

## Results

On WorldEdit-Test, the proposed method (fine-tuned Bagel with WorldEdit) achieves an overall average score of 4.07 across four evaluation metrics (Visual Consistency, Visual Quality, Instruction Following, Knowledge Plausibility), making it the best-performing open-source model and competitive with commercial systems GPT-4o (4.36), Nano-Banana (4.22), and SeedEdit-3.0 (4.21). The method substantially outperforms the next-best open-source baseline Flux-Kontext (3.21) by 0.86 points, and the base Bagel model (2.76) by 1.31 points. On KRIS-Bench, WorldEdit achieves 66.86 overall (best open-source), and on RISE-Bench it reaches 16.1% overall accuracy, again the strongest open-source result.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The proposed method (4.07 overall) is the strongest open-source model on WorldEdit-Test, outperforming Flux-Kontext (3.21) by 0.86, Bagel-Think (3.35) by 0.72, and the base Bagel (2.76) by 1.31. It narrows the gap to commercial systems substantially: GPT-4o leads by 0.29, Nano-Banana by 0.15, and SeedEdit-3.0 by 0.14. Notably, the method surpasses GPT-4o on the 'Break' category (4.54 vs. 4.41) and is competitive on 'Stretch' (4.15 vs. 4.58) and 'Twist' (4.16 vs. 4.27). On KRIS-Bench, WorldEdit achieves 66.86 overall, beating all open-source baselines (next best: Bagel-Think at 53.36) and even exceeding Gemini 2.0 (62.41) and Doubao (60.70), though trailing GPT-4o (80.09). On RISE-Bench, WorldEdit reaches 16.1% overall, outperforming Gemini-2.0-Flash-exp (13.3%) and all other open-source editors (Bagel 5.8%, Step1X-Edit 1.9%, OmniGen 0.8%), while GPT-4o remains the strongest at 28.9%.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the unified multimodal model Bagel (Deng et al., 2025) as its base architecture and on Flow-GRPO (Liu et al., 2025a) as the RL optimization algorithm, but departs from prior image editing work in a key conceptual way: rather than treating editing as explicit attribute manipulation or style transfer, it formalizes a sub-task where instructions specify a *cause* (e.g., 'expose to high temperature') and the model must infer the visually correct *effect* grounded in world knowledge. Prior benchmarks like KRIS-Bench and RISE-Bench evaluate knowledge-reasoning in editing but provide no training supervision, and large-scale editing datasets (AnyEdit, InstructPix2Pix) are dominated by explicit instructions. The paper's two-stage pipeline—SFT with chain-of-thought paraphrased instructions followed by RL with a composite reward including a novel causal-verification signal—repurposes the reasoning capabilities already latent in unified models to drive physically plausible generation.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

WorldEdit fills a concrete gap: it is the first large-scale (11k) training dataset purpose-built for causal, world-knowledge-driven image editing, paired with a dedicated test benchmark, making it a practical resource for the community. The causal-verification reward (inverting the edit to check whether the inferred cause matches the instruction) is a lightweight but effective mechanism that could be adapted to other knowledge-grounded generation tasks. However, the dataset's coverage of real-world scenarios remains limited (e.g., rare phenomena like magnetic field lines require synthetic supplementation), and the approach still trails GPT-4o and Nano-Banana by a meaningful margin on several categories, leaving open whether the gap can be closed through data scale alone or requires fundamentally different reasoning architectures.
