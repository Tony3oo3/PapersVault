## Problem

Real-time TDDFT (RT-TDDFT) requires iterative propagation of all occupied Kohn-Sham wavefunctions over fine time steps, making simulations of excited-state dynamics (optical absorption, electron dynamics) computationally prohibitive. No prior work directly learns the time-dependent wavefunction evolution in the orbital basis while respecting the reduced SO(2) symmetry induced by an external electric field, leaving a gap between ab initio quantum dynamics and scalable ML-based surrogates.

## Input / Output

The model takes atom types, 3D coordinates, a time-dependent external electric field along the z-axis, the ground-state coefficient matrix C(0), and the previous 8 delta wavefunction coefficients Δ(t−8)…Δ(t−1) as input, and outputs 8 future delta coefficients Δ(t)…Δ(t+7) in a single autoregressive step (time bundling). The delta transformation (Eq. 3) strips out the global phase γ_n(t) and amplifies the residual by β=10⁻³, so the network learns only the physically meaningful change induced by the external field. Each electronic state is represented as a separate graph over atoms, with node features organized by spherical rotation order ℓ up to 2, and the model is rolled out 13 times to produce a full 100-step trajectory.

## Method

OrbEvo is built on EquiformerV2 with two key modifications: (1) SO(2)-equivariant electric-field conditioning via a FiLM-like mechanism where a per-ℓ scale factor preserves SO(3) equivariance while a bias vector with non-zero entries only at m=0 breaks the symmetry to SO(2), and (2) two variants for inter-electronic-state interaction — OrbEvo-WF uses layer-wise average pooling over state graphs followed by a global transformer block, while OrbEvo-DM computes the density matrix from input coefficients via tensor contraction with Clebsch-Gordan coefficients, feeding diagonal blocks into node features and off-diagonal blocks into graph attention. The density-matrix variant is physically motivated because the TDDFT Hamiltonian depends directly on the density, making the time-evolution operator more naturally expressible. Training uses pushforward training (sampling one-step errors from the model's own predictions with a linear warm-up) to mitigate autoregressive error accumulation, combined with time bundling of 8 steps to reduce the number of rollout iterations. Electronic states are processed in parallel as separate graphs sharing geometry, and during training a subset of states can be sampled to reduce cost without degrading the density-matrix model.

## Results

On the QM9 dataset (5,000 diverse molecules), OrbEvo-DM-s8 achieves a rollout nRMSE of 0.1885 for wavefunctions, dipole nRMSE-z of 0.1459, and absorption nRMSE-α of 0.0752, outperforming OrbEvo-WF-sall (0.2071, 0.4629, 0.1270 respectively) on all physical property metrics. On the MDA dataset (1,500 malonaldehyde configurations), OrbEvo-DM-s8 achieves rollout nRMSE of 0.1778, dipole nRMSE-z of 0.2326, and absorption nRMSE-α of 0.0671. The density-matrix-based model consistently outperforms the wavefunction-pooling variant on dipole and absorption, while the wavefunction-pooling variant achieves slightly lower 1-step wavefunction error (0.0164 vs. 0.0190 on QM9).

## Baselines & Comparisons

The paper does not compare against external baseline methods; instead, it compares two architectural variants of OrbEvo: the density-matrix interaction model (OrbEvo-DM) and the wavefunction-pooling model (OrbEvo-WF). OrbEvo-DM-s8 outperforms OrbEvo-WF-sall on all physical property metrics (dipole and absorption) across both datasets, with particularly large margins on QM9 (dipole nRMSE-z: 0.1459 vs. 0.4629; absorption nRMSE-α: 0.0752 vs. 0.1270). Conversely, OrbEvo-WF-sall achieves slightly lower 1-step wavefunction error (0.0164 vs. 0.0190 on QM9) and lower rollout ℓ₂-MAE on MDA (0.0853 vs. 0.0947), but its advantage does not translate to physical properties. The authors attribute DM's superiority to the density matrix being inherently consistent with the TDDFT formulation, where the density functional directly determines the time-dependent Hamiltonian. Ablation studies further confirm that removing the time-evolving density matrix (DM-s8-no-dm(t)) causes the model to fail, and that electronic state sampling does not degrade DM performance but significantly hurts WF performance.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the ML-PDE surrogate framework (Brandstetter et al., 2022) from grid-based PDEs to wavefunction coefficient evolution on 3D molecular graphs, using EquiformerV2 as the equivariant backbone. It departs from prior ML-TDDFT work (TDDFTNet for density evolution, Shah & Cangi for 1D charge density) by directly learning full Kohn-Sham wavefunction coefficients in the LCAO basis, which the authors identify as a critical gap. The key conceptual departures are: (1) reducing SO(3) to SO(2) equivariance via FiLM-style electric field conditioning, (2) modeling electronic states as separate graphs rather than concatenated channels, and (3) featurizing inter-state interactions through density matrix tensor contraction rather than wavefunction pooling.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

OrbEvo demonstrates that a single equivariant graph transformer can replace the iterative Kohn-Sham propagation loop in RT-TDDFT, reducing per-molecule simulation time from hours to ~1 second while capturing dipole moments and absorption spectra without explicit supervision on those properties. The density-matrix-based interaction is physically grounded (the density functional directly determines the time-dependent Hamiltonian), making the learned evolution operator more interpretable. Limitations include reliance on standard TDDFT (conical intersections, XC functional accuracy), a single external field configuration (uniform, z-directed), and evaluation on relatively small molecules (≤29 atoms). Open questions include scaling to larger systems, arbitrary field geometries, and whether the approach transfers to nonlinear response or strong-field regimes.
