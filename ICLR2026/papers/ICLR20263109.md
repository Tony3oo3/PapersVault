## Problem

Existing LLM safety alignment methods (SFT, DPO, RLHF) all depend on external validators—human-annotated responses, preference pairs, or separately trained reward models—that are expensive to obtain and difficult to generalize as jailbreak techniques evolve. This paper asks whether aligned models already possess an internal safety signal that could be leveraged for self-improvement without any external supervision, and identifies response entropy as that signal: aligned models consistently generate safe refusals with significantly lower per-token entropy than harmful content.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263109/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

SIRL takes only unlabeled prompts (15,000 from PKU-SafeRLHF, with no response labels, safety annotations, or preference pairs) and a pre-aligned instruction-tuned LLM as input. During training, the model samples G=4 responses per prompt at temperature 1.0, and the per-token entropy of each generated response is computed directly from the model's own output distribution—no external judge or reward model is queried. The output is a safety-aligned LLM that produces confident refusals to harmful requests while maintaining diverse, context-appropriate responses to benign queries, with no change to the inference-time interface (single-pass generation, no additional sampling or filtering).

## Method

SIRL transforms the model's internal confidence into a self-generated reward: the reward for each response is its negative average per-token entropy (r_i = −H̄(o_i|q)), so that low-entropy confident refusals receive higher reward than high-entropy uncertain compliance. Policy optimization uses Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with G=4 sampled responses per prompt, computing normalized advantages within each group to reduce variance, combined with PPO-style clipping (ε=0.2) and KL regularization (β=0.001) against the reference policy to preserve general capabilities. The key mechanism is a self-reinforcing cycle: as the model learns to favor its low-entropy responses (which are predominantly safe refusals), those refusal patterns become even more confident, strengthening the training signal iteratively. This requires only 50 training steps at a conservative learning rate of 1×10⁻⁶, and the entropy-safety correlation is validated to persist across temperatures (0.3–2.5), model families (Llama, Qwen), and scales (3B–14B), with the approach achieving 99%+ DSR against 20+ jailbreak methods while preserving or improving math, coding, and conversation benchmarks.

## Results

SIRL achieves Defense Success Rates (DSR) of 99.1% (Llama-3.1-8B), 100.0% (Llama-3.2-3B), 98.7% (Qwen2.5-3B), and 99.9% (Qwen2.5-7B) across 20 jailbreak attack methods, using only 15,000 unlabeled prompts. General capabilities are preserved or improved: MATH-500 scores rise from 49.0→51.2 (Llama-3.1-8B) and 77.6→78.6 (Qwen2.5-7B), HumanEval improves from 59.1→61.0 and 69.5→70.3 respectively, and TruthfulQA remains stable. On the safety-agnostic model Llama-3.1-Tulu-8B (no safety data), SIRL boosts DSR from 64.7% to 97.0% while maintaining BBH at 68.5 and MATH-500 at 25.6.

## Baselines & Comparisons

SIRL outperforms all three baselines on DSR across every model. Against SFT, SIRL gains 10.8–34.3 percentage points on DSR (e.g., 99.1% vs 88.3% on Llama-3.1-8B; 97.0% vs 73.6% on the no-safety-data Tulu model) while SFT severely degrades general capabilities (MATH-500 drops 49.0→44.6, HumanEval 59.1→47.0 on Llama-3.1-8B). Against DPO, SIRL achieves higher DSR on all four main models (99.1 vs 98.2, 100.0 vs 98.1, 98.7 vs 97.5, 99.9 vs 96.0) and better preserves or improves reasoning and coding scores. Against RLHF, SIRL's DSR advantage is even larger (99.1 vs 87.8, 100.0 vs 96.0, 98.7 vs 93.0, 99.9 vs 90.4), while RLHF shows inconsistent effects on general benchmarks. Critically, SIRL requires only unlabeled prompts, whereas SFT needs human-annotated responses, DPO requires preference pairs, and RLHF depends on a separately trained reward model.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) framework, repurposing its group-sampling and relative-advantage structure with a novel entropy-based intrinsic reward for safety alignment. It departs from the dominant RLHF/DPO/SFT paradigm, which all require some form of external supervision (human-annotated responses, preference pairs, or trained reward models), by showing that aligned models' own generation confidence—measured as per-token entropy—suffices as a training signal. Conceptually, it extends the 'confidence as reward' idea explored in reasoning domains (e.g., Zhao et al. 2025a for math/coding) to safety, and complements mechanistic interpretability findings (Arditi et al. 2024; Wollschläger et al. 2025) that safety is encoded in structured representations, by demonstrating the same knowledge is observable in generation-level confidence.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

SIRL offers a practically significant simplification: safety alignment with only unlabeled prompts and no external validators, achieving 99%+ DSR across 20+ attack types while preserving math, coding, and conversational capabilities. This could substantially reduce the annotation and reward-model engineering costs that currently bottleneck safety training at scale. Open questions include whether the entropy-safety correlation degrades for truly novel attack paradigms (e.g., multi-agent or tool-use jailbreaks), whether the directed mode collapse in the safety domain (Self-BLEU-4 increasing ~900%) could interact poorly with future safety requirements demanding nuanced refusals, and whether the method's reliance on pre-existing alignment (it fails on models with no safety training at all, e.g., Vicuna-7B only reaches 79-86% DSR) limits its applicability to greenfield model development.
