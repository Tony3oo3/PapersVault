## Problem

Existing offline-to-online RL methods require reward-labeled, task-specific, or near-expert offline data, which is expensive to obtain. While pre-training world models on abundant non-curated data (reward-free, mixed-quality, multi-embodiment) appears promising, the paper identifies that naive fine-tuning of such pre-trained world models fails on many tasks due to distributional shift between the offline pre-training data and the narrow online data collected during RL. The paper asks: how can we effectively leverage non-curated offline data across both pre-training and fine-tuning stages to substantially improve RL sample efficiency?

## Input / Output

The method consumes 9-channel 64×64 pixel observations (stacked frames) and continuous action vectors as input, with actions zero-padded to unify dimensions across 6 different embodiments. The offline dataset (60k trajectories, ~10M state-action pairs) is reward-free and mixed-quality, collected by curiosity-driven agents (DMControl) and noise-injected TDMPCv2 agents (Meta-World). The output is a continuous control policy and a 280M-parameter RSSM world model that can be fine-tuned per task, evaluated on 72 visuomotor tasks under a 150k online sample budget.

## Method

NCRL is a two-stage pipeline built on a modified RSSM (280M params, no task-related losses, zero-padded actions for multi-embodiment unification). In pre-training, the world model learns reconstruction, dynamics prediction, and representation objectives from non-curated offline data without any reward signal. The key contribution is in fine-tuning, where two techniques address the identified distributional shift: (1) Experience Rehearsal retrieves task-relevant trajectories from the offline dataset via neural feature distance (using the pre-trained encoder and Faiss for top-k search) and replays them during fine-tuning to prevent catastrophic forgetting and augment the initial state distribution for model rollouts; (2) Execution Guidance trains a prior policy via behavioral cloning on the retrieved data and alternates between this prior and the RL policy mid-episode during data collection, steering the agent toward regions where the world model has high confidence. The actor-critic is trained on imagined trajectories generated by rolling out the policy through the world model, following the DreamerV3 framework with value distribution learning.

## Results

On 50 Meta-World manipulation tasks with pixel inputs, NCRL achieves a mean success rate of 0.748 at 150k samples, nearly double DreamerV3@150k (0.360) and DrQ-v2@150k (0.430), while matching or exceeding their performance at 1M samples (DreamerV3@1M: 0.656, DrQ-v2@1M: 0.753). The median success rate is even more dramatic: NCRL reaches 0.840 vs. 0.130 for DreamerV3@150k. Across all 72 tasks (50 Meta-World + 22 DMControl) spanning 6 embodiments, NCRL achieves nearly twice the aggregate score of training-from-scratch baselines under the same 150k sample budget, matching their performance obtained with 3.3–6.7× more samples.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against training-from-scratch baselines, NCRL@150k (mean 0.748) matches DreamerV3@1M (0.656) and approaches DrQ-v2@1M (0.753) using only 150k samples, a 3.3–6.7× sample reduction. Against offline-data methods, NCRL significantly outperforms R3M (which fails to improve sample efficiency on most tasks), UDS-RLPD (which shows only marginal gains from zero-reward labeling), ExPLORe (which benefits from near-expert data but still trails NCRL by a large margin on manipulation tasks), and JSRL-BC (which struggles with non-expert trajectories on tasks like Cheetah Run Hard and Assembly). Against iVideoGPT, NCRL outperforms even in an aligned setting (iVideoGPT-align) without using reward shaping or demonstration-based replay buffer pre-filling. On the continual adaptation task (Quadruped: stand→walk→run→jump→roll→roll fast), NCRL enables adaptation within 100 trials per task, achieving 2–5× higher episodic returns than PackNet under the same 300k-step-per-task budget.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds on the RSSM world model architecture (Hafner et al., 2019) and the DreamerV3 actor-critic fine-tuning procedure (Hafner et al., 2023), but departs from prior offline-to-online RL work (RLPD, ExPLORe, JSRL) by removing the assumption of reward-labeled, task-specific, single-embodiment offline data. It also contrasts with world model pre-training methods like iVideoGPT (Wu et al., 2025) that focus on novel architectures for in-the-wild data but discard the offline data during fine-tuning. The key conceptual departure is identifying distributional shift between pre-training and fine-tuning data as the root cause of failure, and proposing to reuse non-curated offline data during fine-tuning via experience rehearsal and execution guidance rather than treating pre-training as a one-shot initialization.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper demonstrates that the natural 'pre-train a world model, then fine-tune' pipeline is insufficient for non-curated data, and that reusing offline data during fine-tuning is critical for achieving the ~2× sample efficiency gains. This diagnosis has broader implications for any pre-train-then-fine-tune paradigm in model-based RL. Limitations include simulator-only evaluation, reliance on RNN-based RSSM (limited scaling), and in-domain data only; open questions include whether the approach transfers to real-world robotics, whether it can leverage truly in-the-wild (out-of-domain) data, and whether Transformer or diffusion-based world models would further improve results.
