## Problem

Existing AI-generated video detectors rely on fixed-resolution preprocessing (resizing or cropping to 224×224), which destroys the high-frequency pixel-level artifacts and distorts global semantic structures that are critical for identifying synthetic content. Compounding this, current detection datasets are dominated by outputs from older, lower-quality generative models, causing severe performance degradation when detectors are evaluated on videos from modern, photorealistic generators.

## Input / Output

The method ingests video clips at their native spatial resolution (dynamic range of 224×224 to 720×720 pixels per frame, preserving original aspect ratio) and variable temporal duration (decoded at 2 fps, with T=8 frames sampled). It outputs a binary real/AI-generated label with a confidence score via a single FC layer on globally pooled features. Unlike conventional pipelines that enforce a fixed 224×224 input, frames are resized only to fit within the token budget while maintaining aspect ratio, and no temporal padding is applied.

## Method

The framework uses Qwen2.5-ViT (32 Transformer layers with RMSNorm, SwiGLU FFN, and 2D RoPE) as a visual backbone, extending its 2D spatial patchification to 3D spatiotemporal patches of size (2, 14, 14) so that videos are tokenized natively without any fixed-resolution resizing or cropping. A simple classification head (global average pooling followed by a linear layer) is appended for binary detection. For computational efficiency at high resolutions, the model employs NaViT-style batch packing to handle variable-length sequences without padding, Flash Attention for GPU-optimized kernels, and a hybrid attention strategy where most layers use 114×114 windowed attention to keep cost linear in patch count. Training is performed with full fine-tuning (lr=1e-5, 5 epochs), LoRA (r=16, lr=1e-4), or linear probing on a 140K-video dataset spanning 15 generators, with early stopping on a Movie Gen validation set.

## Results

On the proposed Magic Videos benchmark, Qwen2.5-ViT achieves a mean accuracy (mACC) of 83.20% and mean average precision (mAP) of 93.28%, outperforming the strongest baselines X-CLIP-L/14 (80.63% mACC) and CLIP ViT-L/14 (77.30% mACC). On DVF-Test, the method attains the highest AUC of 97.6%, surpassing TimeSformer (95.4%) and F3Net (93.7%). On GenVideo-Val, it achieves 96.13 AP and 90.64 F1, exceeding DeMamba-CLIP (93.45 AP, 89.19 F1) despite using only 1/15th of the training data. On DeepTraceReward, it reaches 97.2% accuracy, substantially outperforming GPT-4.1 (92.9%) and GPT-5 (90.7%).

## Baselines & Comparisons

On the Magic Videos benchmark (ACC), Qwen2.5-ViT achieves 83.20% mACC, beating X-CLIP-L/14 (80.63%) by 2.57 points and CLIP ViT-L/14 (77.30%) by 5.9 points; however, on the AP metric, X-CLIP-L/14 narrowly edges ahead (94.39 vs. 93.28 mAP). On DVF-Test, the method leads all baselines with 97.6% AUC, surpassing TimeSformer (95.4%) and F3Net (93.7%). On GenVideo-Val, Qwen2.5-ViT outperforms DeMamba-CLIP on F1 (90.64 vs. 89.19), AP (96.13 vs. 93.45), and ACC (96.64 vs. 96.14), though DeMamba slightly leads on Recall (91.58 vs. 91.16). Image-detection baselines (RINE, FatFormer, B-Free, Effort) perform near chance level (49–73% mACC), confirming that static-image forgery features do not transfer to video. Deepfake-specific methods (F3Net, TALL) are constrained by their facial focus, scoring 70.64% and 64.29% mACC respectively. On DeepTraceReward, the method (97.2% ACC) significantly outperforms general-purpose VLMs including GPT-4.1 (92.9%), GPT-5 (90.7%), and Gemini 2.5 Pro (84.3%), with balanced Fake/Real accuracy (96.3%/98.2%) indicating no class bias.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper builds directly on the Qwen2.5-VL Vision Transformer's 3D patchification strategy and NaViT's batch-packing mechanism, repurposing a general-purpose vision-language backbone for the specific task of AI-generated video detection. It departs from the dominant paradigm in prior detection work (MM-Det, DeMamba, UNITE, WaveRep, and image-based methods like RINE, FatFormer, B-Free) that universally resizes or crops inputs to a fixed 224×224 resolution, arguing that this destructive preprocessing discards the high-frequency forgery artifacts and distorts global semantics that detectors need. The paper also extends the data landscape beyond prior benchmarks (GenVideo, DVF) that rely on older, lower-quality generators, introducing a 140K-video training set spanning 15 modern generators and the Magic Videos benchmark built from six state-of-the-art commercial and open-source models.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The Magic Videos benchmark and the 15-model training dataset address a concrete and growing gap: existing evaluation sets are dominated by older, lower-resolution generators, making it difficult to assess detectors against the current frontier of photorealistic video synthesis. The empirical finding that generator quality strongly correlates with cross-generator detection transferability (Pearson ρ=0.86) provides a practical guideline for dataset construction. Key open questions remain: the computational overhead of native-resolution processing limits deployment in resource-constrained settings, the rapid evolution of generative models necessitates continuous dataset updates, and the paper does not yet offer interpretability into which specific forensic traces drive detection decisions.
