## Problem

Existing protein alignment methods either compare global structures, rely on predefined templates, or use dynamic programming that yields uninterpretable scores, leaving a critical gap in efficiently identifying and comparing local structural motifs (active sites, binding pockets, catalytic residues) at the residue level. These local motifs are spatially proximate but often widely separated in sequence and embedded in different overall folds, making them invisible to global or sequence-based approaches.

## Input / Output

PLASMA takes a pair of residue-level embedding matrices (H_q ∈ R^{N×d}, H_c ∈ R^{M×d}) from any pre-trained protein representation backbone (seven tested: ANKH, ESM2, PROTSSN, etc.) and produces a soft alignment matrix Ω ∈ R^{N×M} plus a normalized similarity score κ ∈ [0,1]. The alignment matrix is a doubly-stochastic-like soft correspondence (not a hard permutation), and the score is bounded and interpretable—unlike EBA's unbounded scores—making it directly usable as a probability-like threshold. The method is plug-and-play: it operates as a lightweight module on top of frozen encoders without requiring architecture-specific modifications.

## Method

PLASMA reformulates residue-level substructure alignment as an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem solved via differentiable Sinkhorn iterations. The Transport Planner computes a learnable cost matrix using a siamese network (shared-parameter MLP with hinge non-linearity and layer normalization) and applies early-terminated Sinkhorn iterations with a temperature parameter to achieve implicit partial alignment—poorly matching residues retain low weights without hard transport-budget constraints. The Plan Assessor then converts the alignment matrix into a similarity score by computing cosine similarity over summed embeddings of thresholded matched residues, weighted by a 2D identity-kernel convolution that detects continuous diagonal segments (confidence weight α), yielding κ = α · s₊ ∈ [0,1]. Training combines a BCE loss on κ with a novel Label Match Loss (LML) that penalizes only labeled substructure regions in Ω, avoiding false negatives on unlabeled but valid matches. A training-free variant, PLASMA-PF, bypasses the siamese network and operates directly on raw embeddings, providing a practical baseline when labeled data are unavailable.

## Results

On the VenusX test_extra benchmark (unseen protein families), PLASMA achieves ROC-AUC of 0.97–0.99, PR-AUC of 0.96–0.98, and F1-Max of 0.92–0.98 across motif, binding-site, and active-site detection with ESM2 embeddings, consistently outperforming all baselines. Inference takes ~10 ms per protein pair, roughly 50× faster than TM-Align/Foldseek and ~3× faster than EBA. The training-free variant PLASMA-PF remains competitive (ROC-AUC 0.90–0.99) but falls short of the supervised PLASMA on extrapolation, particularly for motifs (0.93 vs 0.97 ROC-AUC with ESM2).

## Baselines & Comparisons

PLASMA outperforms TM-Align by a wide margin on motif detection (ROC-AUC 0.97 vs 0.81; F1-Max 0.95 vs 0.76) and binding-site detection (0.98 vs 0.91), while TM-Align remains competitive on active-site detection (0.93 vs 0.98 ROC-AUC) and even edges out PLASMA on LMS for active sites (0.90 vs 0.87). Against Foldseek, PLASMA leads on every metric for ESM2 embeddings (e.g., 0.97 vs 0.89 ROC-AUC for motifs; 0.98 vs 0.90 for binding sites). EBA is the closest competitor on binding-site and active-site ROC-AUC (0.97 vs 0.98), but it collapses to 0.00 F1-Max on the structure-based PROTSSN backbone for all three tasks, whereas PLASMA maintains 0.92–0.97 F1-Max. PLASMA-PF (training-free) narrows the gap to supervised PLASMA on binding/active sites but lags by 4–7 points on motif ROC-AUC, confirming the value of supervised training for novel substructures. In terms of speed, PLASMA (~10 ms/pair) is ~50× faster than TM-Align and Foldseek and ~3× faster than EBA, making it the only method combining top accuracy with real-time inference.

## Relation to Prior Work

PLASMA builds on the entropy-regularized optimal transport framework (Sinkhorn iterations) previously applied to 3D shape correspondence (Deep Shells) and subgraph matching, but departs from those works by relaxing the strict one-to-one or full-containment assumptions to accommodate partial, variable-length protein substructure matches via early termination and temperature-controlled sparsity. On the protein side, it extends the embedding-based alignment line (EBA, pLM-BLAST, TM-VEC) by replacing dynamic programming or global cosine scoring with a learnable siamese cost matrix and a principled OT objective, while remaining agnostic to the upstream protein representation backbone. The Label Match Loss is a new training signal that avoids penalizing unlabeled but potentially valid residue matches, addressing a gap in how prior alignment methods handle incomplete annotations.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

PLASMA fills a practical gap in protein structure analysis by providing a fast (~10 ms per pair), interpretable, and trainable tool for residue-level local motif alignment that is plug-and-play with any protein representation model. The normalized [0,1] similarity score and explicit alignment matrix offer a level of interpretability that unbounded embedding-based scores (e.g., EBA) lack, which is important for downstream functional annotation and structure-based drug design. Open questions include scalability to genome-scale protein databases, performance on multi-domain or intrinsically disordered proteins, and whether the OT formulation can be extended to capture evolutionary constraints beyond pairwise structural similarity.
