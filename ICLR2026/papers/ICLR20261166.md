## Problem

In partially observable environments, privileged information (e.g., full state in simulation) is available during training but difficult to exploit: a teacher with such information is often 'impossibly good' and inimitable by the student, causing imitation gaps and suboptimal learning. Existing teacher-student methods either abandon privileged supervision when the teacher becomes inimitable or apply it indirectly via reward shaping, and none provide theoretical guarantees that the supervision is actually beneficial to the student.

## Input / Output

The learner consumes partial observations (which may include action-observation histories or recurrent memory states) and outputs a stochastic action policy. The guider consumes a unified input o_g = [s, o, 1] containing the full privileged state s alongside the partial observation o, while the learner receives o_l = [0, o, 0] through a shared policy network, so the same parameterized network handles both asymmetric information regimes via a scalar indicator flag.

## Method

GPO co-trains a guider (with privileged information, trained via PPO) and a learner (trained via behavioral cloning from the guider) in an iterative loop with a backtracking step that pulls the guider's policy back toward the learner's after each RL update, ensuring the guider stays within the learner's imitable region. Two variants are proposed: GPO-penalty uses an adaptive KL penalty coefficient to modulate backtracking strength, while GPO-clip introduces a double-clip function that halts guider updates when it drifts too far from the learner and a binary mask that selectively applies backtracking only when the gap exceeds a threshold. The key theoretical result (Proposition 1) shows that the learner's BC update is equivalent to a constrained policy mirror descent, inheriting policy-improvement guarantees from the guider's trust-region RL update. This splits the high-variance policy gradient computation (handled by the guider with clean, privileged inputs) from the lower-variance supervised learning (handled by the learner with noisy, partial inputs), reducing overall training variance under partial observability.

## Results

On Brax continuous control tasks (Ant, Halfcheetah, Humanoid, Swimmer, Walker2d, etc.) with Gaussian noise (σ=0.1–0.3), GPO-clip consistently achieves the highest normalized performance (relative to a pre-trained privileged teacher), followed by GPO-penalty, PPO-asym, and GPO-naive. On 15 POPGym memory-based tasks, the same hierarchy holds: GPO-clip > GPO-penalty > PPO-asym > PPO. On the didactic TigerDoor and TigerDoor-alt problems, all GPO variants achieve the optimal policy (reward 1.0 and 1.0 respectively), while direct behavioral cloning of the privileged teacher yields suboptimal rewards of 0.5 and 0.75.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GPO-clip and GPO-penalty outperform PPO-asym (asymmetric value function) across all Brax tasks, with the gap widening as noise increases; in POPGym the advantage over PPO-asym is smaller, suggesting asymmetric value functions are less helpful for memory tasks. Compared to PPO+BC (co-training without backtracking), GPO's backtracking mechanism is critical: PPO+BC's unconstrained guider degrades learner performance in noisy Brax tasks, while GPO maintains alignment. ADVISOR-co and A2D, which also co-train teacher and student, effectively degenerate to pure PPO in partially observable settings because the teacher becomes inimitable and the supervision weight collapses. Pre-trained-teacher methods (DAgger, ELF, ELF-asym, TGRL) perform well only in fully observable or low-noise tasks (e.g., Halfcheetah, Swimmer) and degrade rapidly under partial observability, as the 'impossibly good' teacher provides no useful supervision. L2T-PPO and RMA (representation learning) also underperform GPO, with RMA failing entirely in noisy Brax environments where deterministic reconstruction of privileged state is infeasible.

## Relation to Prior Work

GPO extends the Guided Policy Search (GPS) paradigm—where an intermediate guider shapes a learner's policy—into the online RL setting for POMDPs, replacing GPS's model-based trajectory optimization with PPO-based policy updates. It departs from standard Teacher-Student Learning (ADVISOR, TGRL, ELF, PPO+BC) by co-training the guider and learner simultaneously with a backtracking mechanism that keeps the guider within the learner's imitable region, rather than relying on a fixed pre-trained teacher or switching to pure RL when the teacher becomes inimitable. The closest prior work is A2D (Warrington et al., 2020), which also co-trains teacher and student, but GPO distinguishes itself by using the guider as the behavioral policy for data collection and by providing a theoretical equivalence between the learner's update and constrained policy mirror descent.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

GPO offers a principled solution to the 'impossibly good teacher' problem with a clean theoretical guarantee (Proposition 1) that the learner achieves optimality comparable to direct RL, while in practice being only 10–20% slower than PPO-asym with no additional networks. The framework is broadly applicable to any setting where privileged information is available during training (sim-to-real, multi-agent, noisy sensors), and the double-clip mechanism in GPO-clip provides a practical, tunable way to balance guider exploration against learner imitability. Limitations include sensitivity to KL/clip hyperparameters on hard memory tasks, potential degradation from parameter sharing with high-dimensional observations, and the requirement that the guider not learn too slowly. Extending GPO to multi-agent settings with global-to-local information asymmetry is identified as a natural next step.
