## Problem

Cooperative perception methods share encoded features between autonomous agents to improve situational awareness, but the resulting bandwidth demand still strains current wireless technologies. Existing selection strategies rely on fixed thresholds or per-frame confidence maps that are static and do not adapt to changing environment complexity, leading to either redundant transmission of static information or insufficient sharing in dynamic scenes. This paper addresses the fundamental mismatch between limited bandwidth and rich sensor information by introducing temporal awareness and adaptive, recipient-centric feature selection.

## Input / Output

The method takes camera images (H×W×C) and LiDAR point clouds from multiple agents, encoded into BEV latent feature representations of size 128×32×32, along with the ego agent's own fused features from previous frames as temporal context. It produces a binary feature selection request vector (specifying which of the 128 channels to share), the received features reconstructed into the full representation, and final task outputs: pixel-level semantic segmentation masks (dynamic objects, static road, static lane) or 3D detection bounding boxes. The request vector is the key intermediate output that drives communication, making the system recipient-centric rather than sender-centric.

## Method

COOPERTRIM introduces a conformal temporal uncertainty metric that computes L1 distance between the current frame's encoded features and the ego's fused features from previous frames, then applies a learnable quantile threshold q to gate which features exhibit significant temporal deviation. A cross-attention module uses these uncertainty scores as queries and feature projections as keys/values, with a second learnable mask threshold τ to produce a binary selection mask, jointly determining both relevance and quantity of features to share. The two learnable thresholds (q and τ) enable dynamic adaptation: in complex scenes, more features exceed the thresholds and more bandwidth is consumed, while in stable scenes the system gracefully reduces sharing. Training uses an ε-greedy strategy (full features with probability ε, selected features with 1−ε) combined with a Lagrangian constraint enforcing a target bandwidth budget, with a theoretical proof showing the ε-greedy approach reduces gradient estimator bias by a factor of (1−ε) compared to partial-data-only training.

## Results

On the OPV2V dataset for cooperative semantic segmentation, COOPERTRIM achieves 54.03% Dynamic IoU, 24.45% Static Lane IoU, and 44.38% Static Road IoU at 11.16 Mbps (32.04% of full bandwidth), outperforming Where2Comm (8.62%/20.40%/36.46% at 39.6 Mbps) and SwissCheese (35.71%/12.81%/32.07% at 10 Mbps). For 3D detection on OPV2V and V2V4Real, COOPERTRIM maintains comparable accuracy to CoBEVT at 27.48% bandwidth (72.52% reduction). Combined with 32× post-selection compression, COOPERTRIM reduces bandwidth to 1.46% while preserving IoU (50.32% Dynamic), compared to 3.76% for CoBEVT and 10.62% for AttFuse.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against Where2Comm, COOPERTRIM achieves a 45.54% absolute IoU gain on Dynamic segmentation (54.03% vs. 8.62%) while using 72% less bandwidth (11.16 vs. 39.6 Mbps). Against SwissCheese at comparable bandwidth (~10–11 Mbps), COOPERTRIM leads by 18.32% on Dynamic IoU, 11.64% on Static Lane, and 12.31% on Static Road. When applied to CoBEVT, AttFuse, and DiscoNet as a plug-in selection module, COOPERTRIM maintains or slightly improves IoU (e.g., 54.03% vs. 50.23% Dynamic for CoBEVT) while reducing bandwidth to 32.04%, 24.76%, and 10.65% of the original, respectively. In the across-the-board bandwidth comparison (Figure 8), COOPERTRIM at 11.6 Mbps is the lowest among all 10 compared methods including UniSense, STAMP, V2X-ViT, V2VNet, F-Cooper, and AttFuse. Under 32× compression, COOPERTRIM achieves 1.46% bandwidth usage with 50.32% Dynamic IoU, outperforming CoBEVT (3.76% BW, 49.63% IoU) and AttFuse (10.62% BW, 32.19% IoU).

## Relation to Prior Work

COOPERTRIM builds directly on intermediate-fusion cooperative perception pipelines (CoBEVT, AttFuse, DiscoNet) by inserting a temporal uncertainty-driven selection module between feature encoding and multi-agent fusion. It departs from prior selection strategies (Where2Comm, SwissCheese, UniSense) in two key ways: (1) it replaces per-frame, static relevance scoring with a conformal-prediction-inspired quantile gating mechanism that compares current features against the ego's fused temporal memory, and (2) it introduces two learned adaptive thresholds (quantile q and mask τ) that dynamically adjust sharing volume per frame rather than relying on fixed cutoffs. The ε-greedy training strategy is repurposed from reinforcement learning to balance full-data exploration against partial-data exploitation during fine-tuning, a training paradigm not previously applied to cooperative perception selection.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The 80%+ bandwidth reduction while maintaining comparable (and in some cases improved) segmentation accuracy addresses a critical deployment bottleneck for V2V cooperative perception, making pixel-level cooperative segmentation feasible under realistic wireless constraints. As the first work on selective cooperative segmentation, it demonstrates that the harder pixel-granularity case can be handled, opening the door to applying similar temporal-uncertainty selection to other dense prediction tasks. Open questions include scalability to larger agent groups, robustness under more severe network degradation (beyond 10% loss), whether the conformal temporal uncertainty generalizes to non-BEV architectures, and how the method performs when pose estimation is imperfect (the paper assumes precise localization). The ~2 FPS overhead and reliance on a single primary architecture (CoBEVT) limit immediate generalizability claims.
