## Problem

Reinforcement finetuning (RFT) has become central to post-training large reasoning models (e.g., o1, DeepSeek R1), yet its applicability to Transformer-based Agents (TAs) in meta-RL settings remains largely unexplored. Current TA adaptation to new tasks relies almost exclusively on supervised finetuning (SFT), leaving open whether RL-based finetuning objectives can outperform SFT under the few-shot, offline data constraints typical of practical deployment.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20264710/cropped_page6_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The agent (a Prompt-DT backbone) consumes structured continuous-state observations along with a short trajectory prompt (K* steps of (state, action, return-to-go) tuples) and a recent K-step history, and outputs a continuous action vector at each timestep. Finetuning operates on only 50 trajectories per task (0.1%–1.1% of the pretraining corpus), placing the setting firmly in a few-shot offline adaptation regime where no online environment interaction is available during the finetuning phase.

## Method

The paper's core contribution is Q-guided Policy Optimization (QP), which augments a standard supervised objective (SFT MSE or DPO preference loss) with a negative Q-value term −α·Q_φ(s, π_θ(s)), where Q_φ is a three-layer MLP trained via TD-loss with double Q-learning. This design anchors the policy to the behavior distribution (stability) while simultaneously encouraging value-guided policy improvement (asymptotic performance), bridging the gap between imitation and RL in offline settings. The paper further conducts a systematic empirical comparison of six finetuning algorithms (SFT, DPO, GRPO, PPO, CQL, QP) across four parameter-efficient configurations (Prompt, Adaptor/LoRA, Decorator, Fullmodel) in MuJoCo locomotion and MetaWorld manipulation benchmarks, and provides a theoretical performance guarantee (Theorem E.4) showing QP-SFT tightens the SFT suboptimality bound by an additive H·σ_r term.

## Results

On expert-level offline datasets with 50 finetuning trajectories, QP-SFT achieves the highest average score of 372.09 under Fullmodel tuning, outperforming SFT (336.07), DPO (326.83), GRPO (325.23), PPO (329.62), and CQL (312.53) by 10.7%, 13.9%, 14.4%, 12.9%, and 19.1% respectively. Under parameter-efficient configurations, QP-DPO leads with 339.46 (Adaptor) and 330.08 (Decorator), while QP-SFT reaches 335.87 and 334.16. On the MetaWorld benchmark specifically, Fullmodel QP-SFT scores 553.36 versus 441.05 for SFT and 471.02 for GRPO, a 25.5% and 17.5% improvement. The paper also validates QP-SFT on the 334M-parameter Dita model in LIBERO, where it improves over SFT by 4.8% (Spatial), 0.9% (Goal), and 4.0% (Long-horizon).

## Baselines & Comparisons

QP-SFT and QP-DPO consistently outperform all five baselines (SFT, DPO, GRPO, PPO, CQL) across every parameter configuration and environment. Against SFT, QP-SFT gains 10.7% on average under Fullmodel tuning (372.09 vs. 336.07) and 3.0% under Adaptor (335.87 vs. 331.69), with the largest absolute gap on MetaWorld (553.36 vs. 441.05, +25.5%). Against DPO, QP-DPO improves by 10.8% under Fullmodel (362.13 vs. 326.83) and 1.9% under Adaptor (339.46 vs. 333.38). Against RL-based baselines, QP methods dominate: GRPO (325.23) and PPO (329.62) under Fullmodel trail QP-SFT by 14.4% and 12.9% respectively, while CQL (312.53) is the weakest baseline overall, underperforming even SFT under Fullmodel. Notably, the algorithm choice matters more than parameter scale in some regimes: switching from PPO to QP-DPO under Adaptor tuning yields a 10% gain, versus only 3% from switching Adaptor to Decorator with PPO.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper transplants the reinforcement finetuning (RFT) paradigm—well-established in LLM post-training (DPO, GRPO, PPO)—into the domain of pretrained Transformer-based agents for meta-RL, where prior adaptation work has relied almost exclusively on supervised finetuning (SFT). It builds directly on Prompt-DT as the agent backbone, on Q-value regularized transformers for offline RL, and on the policy decorator's residual mechanism, combining these into a unified evaluation framework across four parameter configurations (Prompt, Adaptor, Decorator, Fullmodel). The proposed Q-guided Policy Optimization (QP) is a lightweight extension that augments supervised objectives (SFT or DPO) with a Q-network-guided policy improvement term, bridging the stability of imitation learning with the asymptotic gains of RL.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper fills a notable gap by providing the first systematic comparison of RFT algorithms for Transformer-based agents in meta-RL, offering practitioners concrete guidance on algorithm–parameter-configuration interactions that were previously unexplored. The finding that sparse rewards can paradoxically benefit offline RFT methods (by reducing value-function approximation error propagation) is a non-obvious insight with implications for reward design in data-scarce settings. Key limitations include the modest model scale (≤40M parameters, far below LLM-scale agents) and the restricted environment suite, leaving open whether the observed algorithmic preferences and the QP advantage persist at larger scales or in truly online, high-dimensional embodied settings.
