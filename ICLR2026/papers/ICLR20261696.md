## Problem

Existing linear CMDP algorithms assume stochastic losses and costs drawn from fixed distributions, making them unable to handle adversarially changing environments where loss functions can shift arbitrarily across episodes. The central question is whether a primal-dual policy optimization algorithm can achieve sublinear regret and constraint violation in finite-horizon adversarial linear CMDPs, where the main technical obstacle is that policy mixing—essential for controlling the dual variable—breaks the recursive softmax structure and produces a new policy class (weighted LogSumExp softmax) for which standard covering number arguments do not apply.

## Input / Output

The algorithm consumes known state-action features φ(s,a) ∈ R^d, adversarially chosen loss functions revealed via full-information feedback, stochastic cost functions observed only at the sampled action (bandit feedback), and a cost budget b ∈ [0,H]. It outputs per-step policies π_h(·|s) over a finite action space, updated via mirror descent with KL divergence, along with a nonnegative dual variable Y_k. The algorithm operates in epochs triggered when the design matrix determinant doubles, and the computational complexity is O(d³HK + d²|A|HK²), independent of the state space size |S|.

## Method

Algorithm 1 combines three novel components to resolve the inherent trade-off between covering number growth and dual variable control. First, it introduces weighted LogSumExp softmax policies that arise naturally from interleaving policy optimization (mirror descent with KL divergence) with periodic policy mixing every K^{3/4} episodes, yielding a covering number of Õ(n²d²) where n is the number of mixing steps. Second, the periodic mixing frequency is chosen to balance the covering number (which grows as Õ(K^{2-2B}d²)) against the dual variable magnitude (which requires mixing to remain bounded). Third, a regularized dual update Y_{k+1} ← [(1-4αηH³)Y_k + η(V̂_{g,1}^k(s₁) - b - 4αH³ - 4θH²)]₊ incorporates additional contraction terms that enable a Lyapunov drift analysis, bounding the dual variable by Õ(ηK^B). Feature contraction (Cassel & Rosenberg, 2024) is applied to keep Q-function estimates bounded, and the overall analysis yields Õ(K^{3/4}) regret and violation bounds under the Slater condition.

## Results

The main theoretical result (Theorem 1) establishes that Algorithm 1 achieves both regret and constraint violation bounds of Õ(K^{3/4}) for finite-horizon adversarial linear CMDPs, where K is the number of episodes. Specifically, Regret(K) = Õ(√(d³H⁴)K^{3/4} + dH³K^{3/4} + d³H⁴K^{1/2} + H⁶/γ²·K^{1/4} + dH⁶/γ²) and Violation(K) = Õ(dH⁵/γ·K^{3/4} + √(d³H⁴)K^{3/4} + d³H⁴K^{1/2}). Numerical experiments on a Job-Scheduling CMDP over K=100,000 episodes confirm sublinear regret growth and constraint violation that initially increases but eventually converges to 0, validating the theoretical guarantees.

## Baselines & Comparisons

This paper is the first to achieve sublinear regret and violation bounds in the adversarial linear CMDP setting, so no direct empirical baselines exist for head-to-head comparison. The paper provides conceptual comparisons with two prior works: (i) Ghosh et al. (2022) for stochastic linear CMDPs, whose dual clipping technique (truncating at 2H/γ) and strong-duality-based analysis cannot be extended to adversarial losses since the Lagrangian reformulation breaks when losses vary per episode; and (ii) Qiu et al. (2020) for adversarial tabular CMDPs, whose occupancy-measure-based dual update does not directly translate to policy-based mirror descent because the expected cost is not linear in the policy. The paper's novel contributions—weighted LogSumExp softmax policies, periodic policy mixing (every K^{3/4} episodes), and a regularized dual update with drift analysis—address the specific challenges that prevent naive extensions of either prior approach to the adversarial linear setting.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper unifies three previously separate threads: (i) primal-dual policy optimization for stochastic linear CMDPs (Ghosh et al., 2022), (ii) Lyapunov drift analysis and policy mixing from constrained online convex optimization (Wei et al., 2020), and (iii) feature contraction for adversarial linear MDPs (Cassel & Rosenberg, 2024). The key departure is that combining policy mixing with policy optimization in a linear setting produces a new policy class—weighted LogSumExp softmax—that breaks the recursive structure exploited by prior covering number arguments. The authors resolve this by introducing periodic mixing and a regularized dual update, components that have no direct analogue in either the stochastic linear CMDP or the adversarial tabular CMDP literature.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

This is the first algorithm with sublinear regret and constraint violation guarantees for adversarial linear CMDPs, filling a gap between tabular adversarial CMDPs (which don't scale) and stochastic linear CMDPs (which don't handle adversarial losses). The K^{3/4} rate is suboptimal relative to the known Ω(√K) lower bound for the unconstrained case, and the authors identify the core obstacle: policy mixing is necessary for dual control but inflates the covering number. Open questions include whether √K is achievable without mixing, and whether the framework extends to adversarial losses under bandit feedback. The practical impact is currently limited to theoretical validation on a single job-scheduling benchmark.
