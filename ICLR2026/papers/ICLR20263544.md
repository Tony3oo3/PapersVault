key_contribution: A probabilistic early-exit framework (PRESS) for speech separation that jointly models clean speech and error variance via a Student-t likelihood, yielding interpretable SNR-based exit conditions with calibrated uncertainty that enable dynamic compute scaling at inference time while matching SOTA static models.
limitations: The paper only evaluates 2-speaker separation and does not extend to per-speaker early exit (all speakers must satisfy the exit condition simultaneously). Additionally, well-calibrated exit-SNR predictions require finetuning on full-length audio (~3% extra training steps), and performance on reverberant/noisy conditions (WHAMR!) still trails the strongest static baselines.

surprise_factor: The most notable finding is that the Student-t likelihood replaces SI-SNR at no performance cost while simultaneously providing calibrated uncertainty for exit decisions — a 'free lunch' that practitioners might not expect from a single objective. Overall, performance is competitive rather than SOTA-breaking (slightly below SepReformer-L+DM), so the contribution is more in the framework and interpretability than raw numbers.

## Problem

State-of-the-art speech separation and enhancement networks use a fixed compute budget regardless of input difficulty (silence, non-overlapping speech, low noise), preventing deployment on resource-constrained embedded devices. Prior early-exit approaches either freeze the performance-compute trade-off at training time through implicit loss weighting or define exit conditions based on inter-layer similarity that is not grounded in any interpretable performance metric.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20263544/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The model takes a single-channel audio mixture (8 kHz for separation datasets, 16 kHz for DNS2020) and processes it in blocks of T samples (e.g., 2000 samples ≈ 250 ms at 8 kHz). At each of multiple exit depths it produces both a reconstructed source waveform and a pair of inverse-gamma parameters (α, β) characterizing the predicted error variance. The unified exit-SNR condition is evaluated per block and per speaker, yielding a single confidence probability that determines whether to halt computation at that depth.

## Method

PRESS-Net is a TasNet-family encoder–separator–decoder architecture that replaces quadratic self-attention with bidirectional linear RNNs (minGRU/RG-LRU recurrence with Hydra bidirectionality) and uses SepReformer-style early speaker split, LayerScale, and RMSNorm for stable deep training. The key probabilistic contribution is a Student-t likelihood obtained by placing a Gaussian on the signal error and a conjugate inverse-gamma prior on its variance, so the network jointly predicts the clean signal estimate and the error-variance parameters at every exit. From this model the authors derive predictive SNR, SNRI, and reference-SNR distributions (ratios of chi-square variables approximated as shifted gamma for large T) and combine them into a single unified exit-SNR CDF that is directly interpretable as a target SNR threshold with a tunable confidence level. Training uses joint uPIT across all exits (preventing speaker swaps between consecutive depths) with no explicit weighting between reconstruction and exit objectives, and a block-wise likelihood variant handles non-stationary error; finetuning on full-length audio clips (≈3 % extra steps) substantially improves both calibration (verified via PIT and CRPS) and reconstruction quality.

## Results

On WSJ0-2mix, the largest finetuned model PRESS-12@12(M)+FT achieves 24.36 dB SI-SNRi with 22.4M parameters at 79.7 GMAC/s, matching SepReformer(M) (24.2 dB, 17.3M params, 81.3 GMAC/s) while using fewer parameters. The smaller PRESS-4@4(S)+FT reaches 23.41 dB SI-SNRi with only 3.4M parameters at 11.3 GMAC/s, outperforming SepReformer(T) (22.4 dB, 3.7M, 10.4 GMAC/s) and SepMamba(S)+DM (21.2 dB, 7.2M, 12.5 GMAC/s) at comparable compute. On Libri2Mix, PRESS-12@12(M)+FT scores 21.29 dB SI-SNRi, trailing SepReformer(M) (22.0 dB) but exceeding SepReformer(S) (20.6 dB). On DNS2020 speech enhancement, PRESS-12@12(M) achieves 22.15 dB SI-SDR at 78.3 GMAC/s, approaching ZipEnhancer (22.22 dB) at 133.5 GMAC/s while also explicitly recovering the noise signal.

## Baselines & Comparisons

PRESS-12@12(M)+FT (24.36 dB SI-SNRi, 22.4M params, 79.7 GMAC/s) matches SepReformer(M) (24.2 dB, 17.3M, 81.3 GMAC/s) on WSJ0-2mix while using fewer parameters, and approaches SepReformer(L)+DM (25.1 dB, 55.3M, 155.5 GMAC/s) at roughly half the compute. The small PRESS-4@4(S)+FT (23.41 dB, 3.4M, 11.3 GMAC/s) clearly outperforms SepReformer(T) (22.4 dB, 3.7M, 10.4 GMAC/s) and SepMamba(S)+DM (21.2 dB, 7.2M, 12.5 GMAC/s) at similar compute budgets. On Libri2Mix, PRESS-12@12(M)+FT (21.29 dB) trails SepReformer(M) (22.0 dB) by ~0.7 dB but exceeds SepReformer(S) (20.6 dB). On WHAMR!, PRESS-12@12(M)+FT (15.67 dB) falls short of TF-GridNet(L) (17.3 dB) and SepReformer(L)+DM (17.2 dB), though it uses substantially less compute (79.7 vs. 231.1 and 155.5 GMAC/s). On DNS2020, PRESS-12@12(M) (22.15 dB SI-SDR, 78.3 GMAC/s) is competitive with ZipEnhancer (22.22 dB, 133.5 GMAC/s) at 58% of the compute, while TF-Locoformer (23.30 dB) leads at 248.6 GMAC/s. The key differentiator is that PRESS models can dynamically reduce compute at test time via probabilistic early exits, achieving efficiency gains not available to any of the static baselines.

## Relation to Prior Work

PRESS-Net is architecturally grounded in SepReformer's early-split encoder-separator-decoder design, speaker attention, and LayerScale, but replaces the quadratic-cost transformer attention with linear RNNs (minGRU/RG-LRU with Hydra bidirectionality) to enable efficient long-sequence processing. The key conceptual departure from prior early-exit work (which typically uses implicit loss-based or similarity-based exit conditions) is the probabilistic formulation: a Student-t likelihood with an inverse-gamma prior on error variance yields closed-form predictive SNR distributions whose CDFs serve as directly interpretable, uncertainty-aware exit thresholds. This positions the paper at the intersection of Bayesian regression, dynamic neural networks, and speech separation, unifying reconstruction quality and exit confidence under a single likelihood objective rather than a hand-tuned convex combination of losses.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance lies in enabling a single trained model to dynamically scale compute at inference time on resource-constrained devices (hearables, mobile phones), with exit decisions interpretable as 'achieve target SNR with confidence p.' The finding that 200K steps of finetuning on full-length audio simultaneously fixes miscalibration and substantially improves reconstruction (e.g., +0.5 dB SI-SNRi on WSJ0-2mix) is a useful practical insight for practitioners. Open questions include per-speaker early exit (currently all speakers must satisfy the condition), extension to iterative/shared-block architectures for theoretically unbounded compute scaling, and whether the probabilistic calibration holds under domain shift or more complex multi-source scenarios.
