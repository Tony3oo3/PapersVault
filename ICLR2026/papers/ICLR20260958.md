## Problem

Existing LLMs process every token through every layer with uniform depth, wasting computation on trivial tokens while under-processing critical ones that drive complex reasoning. Prior work either discards tokens (pruning) or applies extra depth uniformly (test-time scaling), but no plug-and-play post-training method selectively allocates additional computation to only the most difficult tokens in pretrained models.
![Teaser figure](../imgs/ICLR20260958/cropped_page1_idx0.webp)

## Input / Output

The method consumes a standard autoregressive text token sequence and produces next-token predictions, but internally at intermediate layers (L_s to L_e) a router scores each token's hidden state and selects ~20% for an extra pass through the same transformer layer. The layer output is a normalized fusion of the vanilla forward pass and the nested reprocessing pass, gated by a learnable β scaled by each token's routing probability, so unselected tokens pass through unchanged while selected tokens receive a weighted blend of both representations.

## Method

DND inserts a linear router (R: R^d → R) at the end of each DND-enabled transformer layer that scores tokens independently via sigmoid, selecting those exceeding a dynamic threshold τ for reprocessing. Selected tokens are packed into a compact subsequence, assigned new positional embeddings, reprocessed through the same layer, and unpacked back to their original positions. Training uses two complementary strategies: a router controlling loss combining score dispersion (entropy maximization to spread scores) and distribution preservation (MSE toward 0.5 to avoid sigmoid saturation), and a threshold control scheme combining buffer proportional control (real-time error correction toward a target selection ratio) with EMA synchronization against averaged top-k routing values. The entire mechanism is applied as a post-training SFT procedure to existing dense (Qwen3-1.7B, Llama3.2-1B, Gemma3-1B) and MoE (Qwen3-30B-A3B) models, adding fewer than 0.1M parameters and ~6% FLOPs while preserving pretrained knowledge through the normalized fusion.

## Results

On three small-scale dense models, DND achieves average improvements of +1.88 (Qwen3-1.7B: 59.53→61.41), +2.61 (Llama3.2-1B: 45.37→47.98), and +2.50 (Gemma3-1B: 47.08→49.58) over full-scale SFT baselines across 12 benchmarks. Gains are most pronounced on complex reasoning tasks: BBH improves by +3.70 to +5.02 points and GPQA by +3.86 to +5.80 points. On the large-scale MoE model Qwen3-30B-A3B, DND yields a +0.87 average gain across 17 benchmarks with only 0.03M additional parameters and ~6% extra FLOPs, while maintaining 91.6–93.1% of the vanilla model's inference throughput.

## Baselines & Comparisons

The only explicit external baseline is ITT (Inner Thinking Transformer, Chen et al. 2025), compared on Qwen3-1.7B under identical SFT conditions. DND achieves 61.41 average vs. ITT's 59.58, a +1.83-point margin; the gap is largest on GPQA (+6.49: 34.34 vs. 27.85) and BBH (+4.61: 45.84 vs. 41.23), while ITT slightly edges DND on GSM8K (80.29 vs. 80.15) and MMLU (64.92 vs. 64.45). The authors attribute ITT's weaker gains to its Top-P-based token selection, which introduces a train/inference mismatch and risks information leakage in auto-regressive decoding. Against the unmodified base models (implicit baselines), DND consistently wins on all 12 benchmarks for every dense model tested, with no single-benchmark regression. On the 30B MoE model, DND improves over the vanilla SFT baseline on all 17 benchmarks (no degradation), with the largest gains on BFCL v3 (+2.05), C-Eval (+1.83), and LiveCodeBench-v6 (+1.42).

## Relation to Prior Work

DND sits at the intersection of two research threads: token-level adaptive computation (MOR, ITT, MoE routing) and latent-space test-time scaling (Hao et al., Saunshi et al.). It departs from MOR by shifting from a pre-training-from-scratch recurrent architecture to a post-training plug-and-play method with a normalized fusion strategy, and from ITT by replacing Top-P sequence-level selection with independent per-token (token-choice) routing that avoids autoregressive information leakage. The threshold control mechanism is directly inspired by DeepSeek-V3's balance loss, extended with EMA synchronization and a dual router-controlling loss (score dispersion + distribution preservation) that has no direct precedent in prior adaptive-depth work.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The practical significance is that DND can be applied to existing pretrained models (dense and MoE) via standard SFT, requiring fewer than 0.1M extra parameters and ~6% FLOPs overhead, making it a low-cost path to performance gains for teams without pre-training budgets. The consistent improvements across 17 benchmarks with zero degradation, plus the 91–93% speed retention, suggest it could be adopted as a standard post-training step. Open questions include whether the strategy transfers to pre-training or continual pre-training, whether it applies to non-autoregressive architectures (e.g., diffusion LLMs), and whether the observed layer-wise hierarchical selection pattern (shallow layers targeting entities, deeper layers targeting logical operators) can be exploited for further architectural design.
