## Problem

Training critiquing language models typically requires stronger supervisors for annotation or oracle verifiers at test time, both of which are costly or unavailable. The paper reveals that relying solely on indirect reward signals (derived from the actor's output correctness) for RL optimization leads to a fundamental conflict: helpfulness improves but discriminability degrades, causing training collapse and marginal performance gains.

## Input / Output

The critic model receives a question x and the actor's original response y (containing reasoning trajectory and final answer) as input, and produces a structured critique c that includes step-by-step correctness judgments, a final correctness assessment (discriminability), and constructive natural language feedback (helpfulness). The fixed actor then consumes this critique to generate a refined response y'. The framework operates in a two-player paradigm where the actor is frozen during critic training, and the oracle reward function verifies correctness of both original and refined responses to shape training signals.

## Method

Critique-RL decouples the optimization of discriminability and helpfulness into two sequential RL stages using RLOO as the base policy gradient algorithm. Stage I optimizes discriminability with a direct rule-based reward r_dis that compares the critic's correctness judgment against the oracle label of the original response, stably improving Acc@Dis regardless of whether the original response was correct or incorrect. Stage II then introduces an indirect refinement reward r_refine (correctness of the actor's refined response) to improve helpfulness, while simultaneously retaining r_dis and a KL-divergence regularization term against the Stage I model to prevent discriminability degradation. The key insight is that indirect rewards alone create an optimization bottleneck where the critic becomes either overly conservative (preserving correct answers but failing to fix errors) or overly aggressive (fixing errors but breaking correct answers), and the two-stage decoupling with appropriate regularization resolves this conflict.

## Results

On in-domain mathematical reasoning tasks, Critique-RL with Qwen2.5-7B achieves 58.40% Acc@Refine on MATH (a 12.66-point gain over the no-critic baseline of 45.74%), 87.72% on GSM8K, and 65.75% on AQuA, with discriminability (Acc@Dis) reaching 85.20%, 90.43%, and 78.09% respectively. On out-of-domain tasks, it achieves 89.7% on SVAMP and 21.4% on TheoremQA, representing a 5.70% average gain over the no-critic baseline. The method also generalizes to challenging benchmarks (AIME2024: 13.75%, AIME2025: 8.50%, GPQA-Diamond: 37.37%) and open-ended summarization (CNN/DailyMail: 20.81 score, 1.12 Δ).

## Baselines & Comparisons

Critique-RL consistently outperforms all seven baselines across every evaluated metric. Against the fine-tuning baselines, it surpasses SFT by 7.11 points average Acc (3B) and 10.34 points (7B) across MATH/GSM8K/AQuA, and STaR by 4.22 and 4.34 points respectively; notably, SFT and STaR produce negative Δ on AQuA (−3.54 and −5.51 for 7B) while Critique-RL yields positive gains. Among RL baselines, Critique-RL beats Retroformer by 4.06 and 6.06 points average Acc (3B/7B) and CTRL by 2.46 and 4.54 points, with the largest margins on discriminability (e.g., +13.75 Acc@Dis over CTRL on GSM8K-7B). In the refinement-method comparison (Table 9, GSM8K-3B), Critique-RL achieves 75.89 Acc vs. Self-Refine (72.71 at 2 iterations), Critic-CoT (70.58), and SuperCorrect (72.78), while also leading in Acc@Dis (87.44 vs. 76.52, 74.70, 62.17). Against the actor-only RL baseline SCoRe (Table 11, MATH-7B), Critique-RL achieves 58.40 Acc vs. 56.52 and 85.20 Acc@Dis vs. 72.51, despite using a fixed (untrained) actor, and its MV@1 (58.40) exceeds SCoRe's MV@12 (52.39), demonstrating superior compute efficiency.

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the two-player actor-critic interaction framework for training critique models established by Retroformer (Yao et al., 2024) and Xi et al. (2024b), which use indirect reward signals derived from the actor's refinement correctness. It departs from prior RL-based critique training (Retroformer, CTRL) by identifying that these indirect signals alone cannot jointly optimize discriminability and helpfulness, and from SFT-based approaches (STaR, Saunders et al.) that require stronger supervisors for data annotation. The key conceptual contribution is a two-stage decomposition: Stage I uses direct rule-based rewards to establish discriminability, and Stage II introduces indirect refinement rewards with KL regularization to the Stage I model to improve helpfulness without eroding discrimination.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The paper provides a practical, well-motivated recipe for training critique models without stronger labeling, addressing a core bottleneck in scalable oversight. The diagnosis that indirect rewards cause a discriminability-helpfulness conflict (with training collapse) is well-evidenced across multiple reward formulations and models, and the two-stage fix generalizes to OOD tasks, different architectures (Llama3.2, DeepSeek-R1), and even open-ended summarization with a learned reward model. Open questions include whether the approach extends to settings where no rule-based or learned oracle reward is available during training, whether joint actor-critic training (rather than a fixed actor) would yield further gains, and how the method scales to truly open-ended domains beyond math and summarization.
