## Problem

Discrete audio language models rely on lossy RVQ codecs, where increasing audio fidelity requires deeper token hierarchies that impose a hard trade-off between quality and computational cost. This trade-off is particularly constraining for edge deployment, as the RQ-Transformer head needed to decode multi-level tokens scales quadratically with the number of RVQ levels. The paper asks whether autoregressive modeling in a continuous VAE latent space can break this quality-computation bottleneck.

## Input / Output

The model consumes either an audio prompt (continuation), a text prompt plus audio prompt (TTS), or a CLAP text embedding (text-to-music), and produces a continuous sequence of VAE latent frames (32-dim at 12.5 Hz for 24 kHz speech, 128-dim at 25 Hz for 32 kHz music) that are decoded to waveform by a causal VAE-GAN decoder. For speech continuation, the backbone also ingests an 'inner monologue' text stream with a 2-frame (160 ms) delay relative to audio latents, acting as a semantic scaffold. Generation is strictly autoregressive: one latent frame is produced per step via a single forward pass of the consistency head, enabling real-time or faster-than-real-time inference on CPU for the distilled Pocket TTS variant.

## Method

CALM replaces the discrete RQ-Transformer head with a three-component continuous architecture: (1) a causal backbone transformer that encodes noise-augmented long-term context (variance-preserving noise injection at training to prevent error accumulation), (2) a lightweight short-context transformer attending to the K=10 most recent clean latents to restore fine-grained local detail lost to noise, and (3) a small MLP-based consistency model conditioned on the sum of both context vectors that maps pure noise directly to the next latent in one step. The entire system is trained jointly with a continuous-time consistency loss (or Lagrangian Self-Distillation for TTS), eliminating the need for discrete tokenization and its associated codebook collapse issues. Additional innovations include Gaussian temperature sampling (scaling noise std by √τ) for diversity-fidelity control, latent Classifier-Free Guidance applied to the backbone output rather than the sampling trajectory, a head batch multiplier that amortizes backbone computation across multiple noise-level loss evaluations, and latent distillation that folds a CFG-guided 24-layer teacher into a 6-layer student while retaining the same MLP head—yielding the 100M-parameter Pocket TTS that runs faster than real-time on a laptop CPU.

## Results

On music continuation (30s generation), CALM with 1-step consistency achieves FAD 0.83 vs. 1.06 for the RQ-Transformer 32-RVQ baseline, while being ×2.2 faster overall and ×19.3 faster in the sampler head (6.6% vs. 57.7% of inference time). On speech continuation, CALM-Consistency-1-step with temperature 0.8 reaches acoustic quality 3.45 (MOS) and meaningfulness Elo 2023, outperforming the RQ-Transformer 8-RVQ (2.75 MOS, Elo 1870) with a ×12.3 sampler speedup. On text-to-speech (LibriSpeech test-clean), CALM with 1-step LSD achieves WER 1.81, CER 0.57, and MUSHRA 61.1, beating F5-TTS (WER 2.42, MUSHRA 54.7) and DSM (WER 1.95, MUSHRA 60.2) on intelligibility and acoustic quality.

## Baselines & Comparisons

Against the RQ-Transformer (Lee et al., 2022), CALM wins decisively on both music and speech: on music, 1-step consistency achieves FAD 0.83 vs. 1.06 (32-RVQ) while being ×2.2 faster overall; on speech, it reaches MOS 3.45 vs. 2.75 with a ×12.3 sampler speedup. Against MusicGen Medium (Copet et al., 2023), CALM improves FAD from 1.72 to 0.83 and acoustic quality from 2.62 to 2.90, with a higher enjoyment Elo (1857 vs. 1761). On TTS, CALM with LSD outperforms F5-TTS (Chen et al., 2025) on WER (1.81 vs. 2.42) and MUSHRA (61.1 vs. 54.7), and beats DSM (Zeghidour et al., 2025) on WER (1.81 vs. 1.95) and CER (0.57 vs. –), though it trails both on automatic speaker similarity (0.52 vs. 0.66/0.67) — a gap the authors attribute to VAE reconstruction effects. Against DiTAR (Jia et al., 2025) and SALAD (Turetzky et al., 2024), CALM achieves lower WER (1.81 vs. 2.39) and CER (0.57 vs. 0.74) respectively. On the MusicCaps text-to-music benchmark, CALM (FAD 2.14) is competitive with Jen-1 (2.00) and Noise2Music (2.10) but trails MusicFlow (Prajwal et al., 2024) on KLD (1.30 vs. 1.23) and CLAP (0.44 vs. 0.52), while clearly beating MusicGen (3.40), AudioLDM2 (3.13), and MusicLM (4.00) on FAD. The VAE backbone matches Mimi (Defossez et al., 2024b) on MOSNet (3.15 vs. 3.11) and outperforms it on ABX (8.1% vs. 9.4%), PESQ (2.42 vs. 2.13), and STOI (0.90 vs. 0.87).

## Relation to Prior Work

This paper extends the MAR framework (Li et al., 2024), which autoregressively models continuous VAE latents with a diffusion head, by replacing the diffusion head with a 1-step consistency model and adding a short-context transformer to supply clean local information. It builds on the noise-augmentation trick from Pasini et al. (2024b) to combat error accumulation, the continuous-time consistency formulation from Lu & Song (2025), and the VAE-GAN architecture of Mimi (Defossez et al., 2024b). The key departure from prior continuous audio work (SALAD, DiTAR) is demonstrating that the approach scales to complex, multi-instrument music and speech continuation beyond small-scale TTS, and from discrete audio LMs (MusicGen, Moshi) is the elimination of RVQ quantization entirely, trading the quality-computation trade-off of deeper codebook hierarchies for a single-pass continuous prediction.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

The demonstration that a 1-step consistency head matches or exceeds a 32-RVQ RQ-Transformer in quality while being ×19 faster on the sampler fundamentally shifts the efficiency frontier for autoregressive audio generation, making high-fidelity audio synthesis viable on edge devices. Pocket TTS (100M params, faster-than-real-time on laptop CPU) is a concrete, open-source artifact that lowers the barrier to local TTS deployment. Open questions include whether the approach scales beyond 3B parameters (the paper shows modest gains at 3B but defers a full study), whether semantic distillation can be extended to music, and whether the consistency-based paradigm transfers to video or other continuous modalities. The ablation revealing that MAR alone (without noise augmentation and short-context) catastrophically fails for music (FAD 8.38 vs. 0.93) is an important cautionary result for the broader continuous-AR literature.
