## Problem

Existing low-rank correction methods for quantized LLMs (LQER, QERA, ASER) attach an independent (A, B) module to every layer, redundantly recomputing the high-precision projection BX for modules that share the same input tensor, inflating memory traffic, and selecting subspaces that ignore the strong anisotropy of real activations. No prior method shares the right factor across input-sharing groups or selectively applies corrections only where they yield the largest accuracy benefit under a deployment budget.

## Input / Output

The method consumes W4A16-quantized LLM weights (int4 weights with fp16 activations, group size 128) and an offline calibration set (64 sequences of length 2048) to compute per-group shared right factors B_shared and module-specific left factors A_i. At inference, for each input-sharing group (e.g., QKV or MLP), the intermediate R = B_shared X is computed once and cached, then reused across all modules in the group via cheap A_i R multiplications. The selective variant GlowQ-S further restricts correction to only the top-k most important groups ranked by SVD energy-capture and normalized error-ratio scores, skipping inactive groups entirely.

## Method

GlowQ formalizes input-sharing groups (modules with identical input dimension) and proves that a single shared right factor B per group is optimal for the joint least-squares objective, equivalent to a stacked SVD of vertically concatenated error matrices. To handle input anisotropy, the paper derives a covariance-aligned (whitened) objective that weights reconstruction error by the input covariance Σ_x, so the shared right subspace aligns with data-preferred directions rather than raw error geometry. A QR-reduced randomized SVD solver compresses the tall stacked error into a d×d core via thin QR, applies randomized SVD with oversampling and power iterations on the core, and recovers balanced (A*, B*) factors without materializing the full whitened matrix. At deployment, the cached R = B_shared X is computed once per group and reused across modules, and a selective-restore policy (GlowQ-S) activates correction only for high-importance groups, cutting TTFB by 23.4% and increasing throughput by 37.4% while maintaining accuracy within 0.2 percentage points of full GlowQ.

## Results

Under W4A16 (INT4, group size 128), GlowQ achieves the best or tied-best WikiText-2 perplexity on 9 of 11 model variants, with representative values of 8.16 (LLaMA 3.2-3B), 6.59 (LLaMA 3.1-8B), 9.90 (Qwen 3-8B), and 5.42 (Mistral-7B). On average, GlowQ reduces TTFB by 5.6% and increases throughput by 9.6% over layer-wise correction, while reducing WikiText-2 perplexity by 0.17% and increasing zero-shot downstream accuracy by 0.42 percentage points. The selective variant GlowQ-S further cuts TTFB by 23.4% and boosts throughput by 37.4% while keeping accuracy within 0.2 pp of full GlowQ.

## Baselines & Comparisons

GlowQ outperforms all six baselines on the majority of 11 model variants for WikiText-2 perplexity. Against pure PTQ methods, GlowQ closes most of the INT4-to-FP16 gap: on LLaMA 3.2-3B it reaches 8.16 vs. GPTQ's 9.46 and AWQ's 8.24; on Qwen 3-8B it achieves 9.90 vs. GPTQ's 9.98 and AWQ's 10.19. Among low-rank correction baselines, GlowQ beats L2QER (8.16 vs. 8.30 on LLaMA 3.2-3B; 9.90 vs. 10.07 on Qwen 3-8B), QERA (8.16 vs. 8.22; 9.90 vs. 10.07), and ZeroQuant-V2 (8.16 vs. 8.44; 9.90 vs. 10.19) on most models. The sole exception is LLaMA 2-13B, where L2QER edges out GlowQ by 0.02 ppl (4.94 vs. 4.96). On downstream zero-shot accuracy (Table 2), GlowQ leads on LLaMA 3.2-3B (66.90%), Qwen 3-8B (70.71%), and Qwen 3-14B (73.84%), while ZeroQuant-V2 slightly leads on LLaMA 3.1-8B (73.48% vs. 73.33%). In latency, GlowQ reduces TTFB by 5.57% and increases throughput by 9.61% over the layer-wise baseline; GlowQ-S amplifies these to −23.39% TTFB and +37.44% throughput.

## Relation to Prior Work

GlowQ extends the per-layer low-rank quantization-error correction paradigm (LQER, QERA, ASER, ZeroQuant-V2) by replacing independent (A, B) modules at every layer with a single shared right factor B per input-sharing group, drawing on the collective matrix factorization principle from Singh & Gordon (2008). The covariance-aligned (whitened) objective adapts the weighted low-rank approximation framework of Srebro & Jaakkola (2003) to the input-covariance metric, steering the shared subspace toward data-preferred directions. The QR-reduced randomized SVD solver and the selective-restore deployment policy are practical contributions that translate the shared-B structure into measurable latency and memory savings without architectural changes.

## Why It Matters / Open Questions

For practitioners deploying quantized LLMs under strict latency budgets, GlowQ offers a drop-in, plug-and-play correction that reduces TTFB and increases throughput while closing much of the int4-to-FP16 perplexity gap, and the selective variant (GlowQ-S) provides a strong accuracy-efficiency compromise by restoring only ~50% of groups. The group-shared design also generalizes to MoE architectures with ~63% fewer correction parameters than layer-wise baselines. Open questions include compatibility with rotation-based PTQ (ROSAQ, GuidedQuant) and KV-cache compression, whether the covariance-alignment benefit persists at even lower bit-widths (e.g., 2-bit), and whether the selective-restore scoring metrics transfer robustly to unseen model families without per-model tuning.
